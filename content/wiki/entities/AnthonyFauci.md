# Anthony Fauci

## Overview
Anthony Fauci appears in the All-In science-policy corpus as a source-scoped symbol of COVID-era damage to public trust in science. The Kratsios interview records [[MichaelKratsios|Michael Kratsios]] saying Fauci did severe damage to science and using school masking, protest restrictions, hospital-visit restrictions, and anti-science labeling as examples of institutional credibility loss. The Weinstein interview uses willingness to investigate claims about Fauci and COVID origins as a test of scientific freedom rather than predetermined conclusion.

## Current Profile
This page is not a full biography of Fauci. Its current wiki profile is source-scoped: Fauci functions as the named figure through whom All-In episodes discuss [[MedicalDogmaTrustRepair|medical dogma trust repair]], the broader [[ScienceTrustPoliticization|politicization of science trust]], and the boundary between scientific inquiry and partisan accusation.

## Key Characteristics
- Fauci is used as a contested public-health authority figure rather than as a neutral institutional actor in this source.
- The episode associates him with COVID-era certainty, school masking, and perceived inconsistency in public-health restrictions.
- Kratsios's criticism makes Fauci a symbol of why the administration says scientific institutions must protect questioning and dissent.
- Weinstein's interview treats questions about Fauci and COVID origins as examples of topics scientists should be free to investigate without pre-committed answers.
- The page keeps the source's negative judgment bounded because the transcript does not independently adjudicate pandemic-policy evidence.
