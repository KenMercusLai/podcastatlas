# Anthony Fauci

## Overview
Anthony Fauci appears in the All-In science-policy corpus as a contested, source-scoped symbol of COVID-era public trust and origin-investigation disputes. The Kratsios interview criticizes Fauci through school masking and restriction examples; the Weinstein interview treats Fauci-related inquiry as a test of scientific freedom; and the Gurley presentation argues that Fauci's research-policy connections created a conflict requiring independent scrutiny.

## Current Profile
This page is not a full biography of Fauci. Its current wiki profile is source-scoped: Fauci functions as a named figure through whom All-In episodes discuss [[MedicalDogmaTrustRepair|medical dogma trust repair]], [[ScienceTrustPoliticization|science trust politicization]], [[InvestigationConflictOfInterest]], and the boundary between scientific inquiry and partisan accusation. None of the bounded sources independently establishes that Fauci caused COVID-19, concealed its origin, or committed misconduct.

## Key Characteristics
- Fauci is used as a contested public-health authority figure rather than as a neutral institutional actor in this source.
- The episode associates him with COVID-era certainty, school masking, and perceived inconsistency in public-health restrictions.
- Kratsios's criticism makes Fauci a symbol of why the administration says scientific institutions must protect questioning and dissent.
- Weinstein's interview treats questions about Fauci and COVID origins as examples of topics scientists should be free to investigate without pre-committed answers.
- Gurley's presentation casts Fauci as an institutional blocker because of prior support for virology research and dismissal of a possible laboratory accident.
- The blocker and conflict characterizations are the speaker's allegations, not findings independently adjudicated by the source.
- The page keeps the source's negative judgment bounded because the transcript does not independently adjudicate pandemic-policy evidence.
