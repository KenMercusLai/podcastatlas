## Overview

路温舒 / Lu Wenshu enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-421-xihan-xingfa-you-duo-hen-guanli-dui-qiufan-xiangsha-jiunengsha-lie9ru4_faverwxkazxxmsegh7ww|Hanji 421]] as the Western Han Tingwei clerk who submits 《尚德缓刑书》 to [[EmperorXuanOfHan|汉宣帝]] in 67 BCE. The source makes him the voice of judicial restraint after Xuan has begun consolidating power against the Huo family.

## Current Profile

Lu Wenshu's profile is narrow but institutionally important. He does not appear here as a factional actor; he appears as a legal critic who converts Xuan's new-ruler moment into an argument for penal repair. His memorial says disorder can become the occasion for renewal and that Xuan, having just taken supreme authority after the Changyi king's failure, should correct earlier mistakes and simplify burdensome institutions.

His specific legal criticism is that Western Han still preserves one Qin-like failure: cruel judicial officials. Because death and mutilation are irreversible, he argues that criminal administration should prefer caution over wrongful killing. The source emphasizes his incentive diagnosis: officials gain a reputation for fairness by being harsh, fear trouble if they are mild, and therefore push prisoners toward death sentences. Torture then manufactures usable confessions because pain makes prisoners want death, and interrogators can supply the words and structure of the desired confession.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han legal remonstrator attached to Xuan's early reign.
- Uses historical disorder and succession language to legitimate reform after a crisis.
- Treats criminal punishment as irreversible state violence that requires caution.
- Diagnoses cruelty as an institutional incentive problem rather than only individual malice.
- Makes torture and confession-manufacturing central evidence for why penal leniency is necessary.
- Connects humane punishment to the possibility of visible peace and restoration.
