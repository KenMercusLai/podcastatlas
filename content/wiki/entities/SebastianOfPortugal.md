# Sebastian of Portugal

## Overview

Sebastian of Portugal was the young king whose failed 1578 campaign in Morocco caused a dynastic crisis and helped bring Portugal under [[PhilipII|Philip II of Spain]].

## Current Profile

The episode treats Sebastian as the disastrous late expression of the crusading energy that had helped drive Portuguese expansion. Seeking glory in Morocco and expecting Spanish support that did not arrive, he crossed to North Africa, was defeated in his first battle, disappeared, and left no heir.

His death was therefore more than a military failure. After the short reign of Cardinal Henrique, Philip II took Portugal in 1580. Later impostors and survival myths turned Sebastian into a vehicle for hopes that the distinct kingdom and empire might escape Spanish rule.

## Key Characteristics

- Young monarch formed around crusading ambition and personal glory.
- Organizer of the failed 1578 Moroccan expedition.
- King who disappeared in battle without leaving an heir.
- Immediate cause of the succession crisis preceding Spanish rule.
- Posthumous focus of restoration myths and royal imposture.

## Evidence

- Campaign: [[228-portugal-the-golden-age-of-discovery-part-2-glt7949934800]] says Sebastian crossed to Morocco in 1578 after promised Spanish reinforcements failed to appear.
- Defeat and succession: [[228-portugal-the-golden-age-of-discovery-part-2-glt7949934800]] connects his rout and disappearance to Cardinal Henrique's succession and Philip II's takeover in 1580.
- Afterlife: [[228-portugal-the-golden-age-of-discovery-part-2-glt7949934800]] interprets later claimants and survival stories as expressions of restoration hope.

## Qualifications

Sebastian's last charge and the failure of Spanish reinforcement are presented narratively and require specialist corroboration. The source does not reconstruct Moroccan politics, the battle, Portuguese succession law, or Sebastianist belief in depth.

## What Changed

- Established Sebastian as the succession bridge between Portuguese crusading expansion and the Iberian Union.
