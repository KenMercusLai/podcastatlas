# Edgar the Peaceful

## Overview

Edgar the Peaceful appears in the wiki as a tenth-century English king whose delayed 973 coronation, unitary institutions, and readiness to punish disorder reveal that royal peace depended on sacred legitimacy, oath-bound service, and coercive reach.

## Current Profile

The sources present Edgar's Bath coronation in 973 as a display of biblical and imperial kingship over a realm with a unitary church, broadly shared language, and single royal coinage. Its timing several years into his reign suggests a proclamation of consolidated rule rather than a simple accession ceremony. Bath's Roman setting, Edgar's imperial title, and Pentecost joined political unity to Roman inheritance and the Holy Spirit.

[[Dunstan]] likely shaped a rite in which Edgar entered with bishops, placed the crown on the altar, prostrated himself, and swore to preserve peace, justice, mercy, and restraint from violence. The same episode associates his wife Alfrith's anointing and crowning with a durable precedent for queens. His reign represents the mature authority built from the West Saxon sequence associated with [[AlfredTheGreat|Alfred]] and [[Athelstan]].

“Peaceable” does not mean passive. Edgar's punitive ravaging of Kent after York merchants were kidnapped shows royal peace as an enforced order. His death in 975 then opens a dangerous minority succession between Edward and [[AethelredTheUnready|Æthelred]], revealing how much stability still depended on the reigning king.

## Key Characteristics

- Sacred monarch whose delayed coronation projects consolidated, biblical, Roman, and imperial authority.
- Oath-bound ruler whose prostration and promises frame kingship as service beneath God.
- Ruler of a realm joined by church organization, language, and royal coinage.
- Enforcer whose punitive violence demonstrates the coercive basis of political peace.
- Dynastic pivot whose death exposes succession fragility beneath institutional order.

## Qualifications

The sources use selected events to characterize Edgar's monarchy and do not independently settle the coronation ordo's authorship, constitutional meaning, the exact reason for its delayed timing, the punitive expedition's details, or how uniformly church, language, and coinage produced political unity. “Peaceful” names an order that the sources explicitly treat as coercively maintained.
