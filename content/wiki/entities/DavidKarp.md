# David Karp

## Overview

David Karp is identified by [[MarketplaceTech]] as a George Washington University professor who studies Silicon Valley's founder culture, venture-capital networks, and corporate-governance assumptions. This page represents the academic guest in the source and is distinct from [[AlexKarp|Alex Karp]], the chief executive associated with [[Palantir]].

## Current Profile

Karp argues that the exceptional-founder narrative is partly produced by institutions that select, finance, advise, connect, and celebrate founders. In his account, real individual ability can coexist with cumulative network advantage, while mature-company dependence on one leader signals weak succession design and accountability rather than proof that the person should be beyond board constraint.

## Key Characteristics

- Interprets founder worship as an institutional and cultural product, not only a judgment about individual talent.
- Traces Silicon Valley's resistance to external constraint to 1990s techno-optimism and later concentrations of wealth and power.
- Treats venture-capital and accelerator networks as cumulative-advantage systems that can be mistaken for pure founder genius.
- Distinguishes the early-stage value of charismatic founders from the mature-company need for succession and operational depth.
- Connects founder dependence to market fragility, board weakness, and public-company accountability.
