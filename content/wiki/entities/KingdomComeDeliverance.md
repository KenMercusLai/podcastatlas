# Kingdom Come: Deliverance / 《天国：拯救》

## Overview

*Kingdom Come: Deliverance* is a historical role-playing game set in medieval Bohemia that the source treats as a demanding social and material simulation rather than a fantasy adventure.

## Current Profile

The game removes magic, dragons, and elves and instead builds difficulty from ordinary dependence. Saving, sleep, hunger, fatigue, drunkenness, food decay, hygiene, literacy, clothing, noise, reputation, crime, combat, alchemy, and craft all constrain the player. NPC occupations and routines make the world respond socially as well as physically.

The source argues that these frictions are not merely punishment. By making rules produce consequences, the game lets players learn the dependencies of medieval life through action. Its reconstruction also changes travel: Trosky Castle and [[KutnaHora|Kutná Hora]] become places where present ruins, mining history, and game spaces can be compared.

## Key Characteristics

- Historical role-playing game grounded in medieval Bohemian geography and conflict.
- Rejects fantasy creatures and magic in favor of material, bodily, and social constraints.
- Models reputation, dress, noise, crime, literacy, routine, combat, and production as interacting systems.
- Uses historical swordsmanship and procedural craft to make competence learned rather than assumed.
- Connects virtual reconstruction with travel to surviving landscapes and ruins.

## Qualifications

The page records one player's interpretive account, not an independent audit of historical accuracy, system completeness, accessibility, or the distinction between the franchise's installments. “Medieval simulator” describes a design ambition and player effect; it does not mean the game reproduces medieval life without selection, compression, or invention.

## What Changed

- Established the game as a systems-based historical interpretation rather than only a medieval visual setting.
- Connected its simulation design to travel, ruins, mining history, and embodied learning.
