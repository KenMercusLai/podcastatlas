# Medea / 美狄亚

## Overview

Medea is the Colchian princess, magic user, indispensable helper, foreign wife, and catastrophic avenger whose changing literary profile joins the [[Argonautica]] to [[Euripides]]' [[MedeaPlay|Medea]].

## Current Profile

In [[605-greek-myths-jason-the-quest-for-the-golden-fleece-part-4-glt3067620137]], Medea enters as the person who makes [[Jason]]'s quest possible. [[ApolloniusOfRhodes|Apollonius]] gives her a love wound caused by Eros, magical knowledge, divided loyalty, and practical agency: she protects Jason from the bulls and supplies the tactic for defeating the warriors born from dragon's teeth. The episode interprets this version as a deliberate softening because audiences already knew the darker Euripidean future.

[[67-meidiya-gu-xila-qinxianglian-de-fuchou-ji-qi-xiandaixing-662400556]] centers that future. Medea is exiled, abandoned for a Corinthian royal marriage, and deprived of a clean way out of the marital bargain. Yet injury does not make her innocent: poisoned gifts kill the princess and Creon, and Euripides makes Medea knowingly murder her children before escaping in a dragon chariot.

The combined profile resists a single label. Medea's intelligence, erotic compulsion, sacrifice, foreignness, and dependence on hospitality make her vulnerable, while magic, calculation, kin violence, and child murder make her terrifying. Her literary development shows how a heroic helper can become the figure who exposes the unpaid costs of heroic adventure.

## Key Characteristics

- Colchian princess and magic user whose knowledge enables Jason's quest.
- Lover whose attachment is represented as divinely inflicted but enacted through consequential choices.
- Foreigner who sacrifices kinship and homeland before facing abandonment and renewed exile.
- Tragic speaker who articulates women's marital, reproductive, and political vulnerability.
- Avenger whose intelligence and agency culminate in ethically unbearable violence.
- Variable literary figure whose helper and threat profiles are weighted differently by Apollonius and Euripides.

## Evidence

- Quest helper: [[605-greek-myths-jason-the-quest-for-the-golden-fleece-part-4-glt3067620137]] describes the love wound, protective ointment, dragon-teeth counsel, and betrayal of her father.
- Inherited threat: [[605-greek-myths-jason-the-quest-for-the-golden-fleece-part-4-glt3067620137]] notes that Euripides had already made fratricide, Pelias's death, poisoned gifts, and child murder part of Medea's audience horizon.
- Gendered injury: [[67-meidiya-gu-xila-qinxianglian-de-fuchou-ji-qi-xiandaixing-662400556]] emphasizes exile, marriage, childbirth, status loss, and Medea's women's-position speech.
- Chosen revenge: [[67-meidiya-gu-xila-qinxianglian-de-fuchou-ji-qi-xiandaixing-662400556]] follows her hesitation and grief before the child murder, preventing either an impulse-only or innocent-victim reading.

## Qualifications

Ancient versions differ about Medea's deeds, including responsibility for her children's deaths. The sources interpret selected literary works rather than recovering a stable historical belief about her. Calling Apollonius's Medea “softened” is an episode-level comparative judgment, and divine compulsion does not settle the degree of human responsibility within the poem.

## What Changed

- Added Apollonius's helper profile and made the tension between erotic compulsion and practical agency explicit.
- Recast the Euripidean avenger as one later weighting of a variable inherited character.
