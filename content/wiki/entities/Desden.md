# Desden

## Overview

Desden is [[CarolDeshaun]]'s licensed collegiate-accessories company, selling clear stadium bags and game-day products designed for female fans.

## Current Profile

The episode presents Desden as a growing manufacturer with licenses from 90 universities and a purchaser-user split: mothers and grandmothers often pay, while daughters and granddaughters carry the products. Its marketing and product decisions should therefore begin with student relevance even when the transaction comes from an older gift buyer. Campus ambassadors, game-day activations, visible product collaborations, and QR-code offers can help the company learn directly from users, while license-level performance should determine whether it expands into more teams or sports.

## Key Characteristics

- Combines collegiate licensing with clear stadium-bag policy compliance and fashion positioning.
- Uses overseas bag production alongside in-house manufacturing and printing in Houston.
- Operates a long-running campus ambassador program and occasional pop-ups.
- Benefits from direct-to-consumer margins while carrying the complexity of 90 university licenses.
- Can use the transparent bag itself as a visible collaboration and merchandising surface.

## Evidence

- Scale and production: [[advice-line-with-seth-berkowitz-of-insomnia-cookies-vmp8144262595]] reports 90 licenses, mixed overseas and Houston production, and expected fiscal-year sales around $2.5 million.
- Purchaser-user split: [[advice-line-with-seth-berkowitz-of-insomnia-cookies-vmp8144262595]] says mothers and grandmothers often buy for daughters and granddaughters.
- Growth experiments: [[advice-line-with-seth-berkowitz-of-insomnia-cookies-vmp8144262595]] recommends student relevance, collaborations, QR-code learning, campus moments, license analysis, and one- or two-team tests in another sport.

## Qualifications

Revenue, margins, license count, and buyer patterns are company- or episode-reported. The discussion does not quantify license economics, sell-through by team, production capacity, seasonality, customer-acquisition cost, or the results of proposed cross-sport tests.

## What Changed

- Initial profile created around the purchaser-user split and license-level growth discipline.
- Identified the clear bag as both product and potential collaboration surface.
