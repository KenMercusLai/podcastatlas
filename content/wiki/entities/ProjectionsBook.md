# Projections

## Overview
Projections is [[KarlDeisseroth]]'s book, discussed in two Huberman Lab versions and a Chinese-language crossover under the translated title 《照亮破碎之心》 as an accessible account of psychiatry, neuroscience, clinical suffering, and scientific hope.

## Current Profile
The sources present the book as an attempt to make difficult psychiatric and neuroscientific material available to general readers without moving beyond what the science can support. Its role is thematic rather than bibliographic: tragic clinical cases coexist with the claim that psychiatry has progressed, still lacks decisive understanding, and can continue toward more precise relief of suffering. The full Huberman conversation situates that optimism beside the author's path from poetry and neurosurgery toward psychiatry and tool building. The Chinese crossover adds a translator-adjacent reading of the title's multiple meanings: neural projection, psychological projection, and literary movement between physical mechanism and subjective life.

## Key Characteristics
- Combines psychiatric clinical cases with neuroscience.
- Aims for accessibility without sacrificing evidential restraint.
- Keeps unresolved scientific distance visible alongside progress.
- Uses qualified optimism rather than technological certainty.
- Uses scientific and literary language to connect neural projection with psychological interpretation.
