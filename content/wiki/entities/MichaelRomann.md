# Michael Romann

## Overview

Michael Romann is an Australian researcher represented as a co-developer of [[MaturityBasedCorrectiveAdjustment|Maturity-Based Corrective Adjustment Procedures]] for adolescent swimming.

## Current Profile

In [[outliers-revisited-outliers-revisited|Outliers, Revisited]], Romann and [[StephenCopley]] estimate biological maturity from anthropometric and chronological measures, then adjust swimming times to make developmental differences visible in talent identification. The episode presents the procedure as a provisional way to keep late maturers from being overlooked or discouraged.

Romann's role is bounded to the swimming method in the supplied summary. The episode's later proposal for age-adjusted academic scores is Gladwell's cross-domain extension, not evidence that Romann developed an educational algorithm.

## Key Characteristics

- Co-developer of a maturity-based correction procedure for youth swimming.
- Uses developmental estimates to test whether raw rankings hide late-maturing talent.
- Provides sport evidence that the episode treats as suggestive, not dispositive, for education.

## Evidence

### Corrected race ordering

- [[outliers-revisited-outliers-revisited]] reports that applying the co-developed procedure changed the finishing order in every race examined.

### Late-maturer visibility

- [[outliers-revisited-outliers-revisited]] says none of the initially top-performing quarter in a 700-swimmer sample were late maturers before adjustment.

## Qualifications

The source does not provide the underlying study, model specification, uncertainty, external validation, or long-term athlete outcomes. The episode's education analogy should not be attributed to Romann as a validated application.
