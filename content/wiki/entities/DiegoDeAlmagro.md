# Diego de Almagro

## Overview

Diego de Almagro was a Spanish conquistador whose dispute with Francisco Pizarro over Cusco ended in defeat at Las Salinas and execution by Hernando Pizarro in 1538.

## Current Profile

[[649-the-fall-of-the-incas-the-last-emperor-part-6-glt4505534064]] presents Almagro as initially strong but strategically exposed. He controls Cusco and holds Hernando and Gonzalo Pizarro, yet releases or loses both advantages, abandons arbitration, and marches toward a coast whose supply and communication networks favor [[FranciscoPizarro|Pizarro]].

Illness leaves Rodrigo Orgóñez in command at Las Salinas. Defeat makes Almagro a prisoner in the same tower where he had confined Hernando; after roughly ten weeks, Hernando refuses an appeal to Charles V and orders his garroting and beheading. The execution creates a revenge constituency around Almagro's son and extends factional war beyond Almagro's death.

## Key Characteristics

- Conquistador claimant to Cusco within the New Toledo-New Castile jurisdictional dispute.
- Initially advantaged by control of Cusco and Pizarro family prisoners.
- Strategically weakened by prisoner releases, failed arbitration, illness, and coastal isolation.
- Defeated at Las Salinas while Rodrigo Orgóñez commanded his army.
- Executed without receiving the royal appeal he requested.
- Posthumous center of an Almagrist faction led by his son.

## Qualifications

The page uses one episode's compressed causal account. It does not settle the legal merits of the territorial dispute, the arbitrator's independence, Almagro's intentions in releasing Hernando, precise troop strengths, or full responsibility for the feud. Describing tactical errors does not imply that his execution was lawful or politically stabilizing.

## What Changed

- Connected his execution to the next generation's revenge politics.
