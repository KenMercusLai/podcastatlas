# Erich Ludendorff

## Overview

Erich Ludendorff was the former German military leader whom the episode connects to the stab-in-the-back myth, the [[BeerHallPutsch|Beer Hall Putsch]], and the later electoral marginality of direct nationalist military prestige.

## Current Profile

The episode presents Ludendorff as helping displace responsibility for German defeat onto alleged internal betrayal, thereby supplying anti-republican movements with a language of treason and humiliation. His First World War prestige also made him valuable to Hitler's 1923 coup attempt, where he joined the coerced beer-hall alliance and the march confronted by police.

His later presidential result provides a qualification to the power of that prestige: the episode says he received only about one percent of the vote. The same political culture that could elevate him as a nationalist symbol did not automatically make him an electorally viable national leader.

## Key Characteristics

- Former wartime military leader with nationalist prestige.
- Promoter of the stab-in-the-back account of German defeat.
- High-profile participant in the Beer Hall Putsch.
- Figure used by Hitler to lend military-national legitimacy to the coup.
- Later presidential candidate with minimal electoral support.

## Evidence

- Defeat mythology: [[296-the-nazis-the-beer-hall-putsch-part-2-glt4099180386]] links Ludendorff to the claim that Germany had been betrayed from within.
- Putsch role: [[296-the-nazis-the-beer-hall-putsch-part-2-glt4099180386]] places him beside Hitler in the beer-hall action and march.
- Electoral limit: [[296-the-nazis-the-beer-hall-putsch-part-2-glt4099180386]] reports that his presidential candidacy won roughly one percent.

## Qualifications

This page is limited to Ludendorff's role in one episode and is not a full military or political biography. The exact diffusion and causal weight of the stab-in-the-back myth, his intentions during the putsch, and the reported electoral figure remain source-scoped.
