# Charles Sennett

## Overview

Charles Sennett was a Church of Christ minister, husband of [[ElizabethSennett]], and the person Parts 2 and 3 of [[TheAlabamaMurders]] identify as central to arranging her assault and possibly inflicting her fatal wounds.

## Current Profile

Part 2 reports that Charles was having an affair and that investigators quickly suspected him. They interpreted his unusually dense alibi trail, precise recall, apparent knowledge that multiple attackers were involved, failure to aid Elizabeth despite CPR training, and reaction when [[KennethSmith]] was named as evidence of prior knowledge. He admitted the affair, denied involvement in the murder, and died by suicide seven days after Elizabeth was attacked.

Part 3 moves beyond the planned-assault account. It reports evidence that an unrecovered smaller knife caused rapidly fatal chest wounds after [[JohnForrestParker]] and Smith had left, as well as a nurse's recollection that Elizabeth feared Charles would kill her. On that basis, the episode advances Charles as the most plausible later assailant, but this remains a circumstantial reconstruction rather than an adjudicated conclusion.

## Key Characteristics

- Church of Christ minister reported to have been having an affair with a parishioner.
- Husband whose unusually elaborate alibi behavior and statements drew immediate investigator suspicion.
- Person the series presents as arranging a paid attack intended to resemble a burglary.
- Husband reported to have left his critically wounded wife untouched despite CPR training.
- Man whom Part 3 identifies as the most plausible inflictor of the fatal chest wounds, without independent proof.
- Person who admitted the affair, denied murder involvement, and died by suicide one week after Elizabeth's death.

## Evidence

### Planning, suspicion, and aftermath

- [[the-alabama-murders-part-2-coon-dog-cemetery-road]] reports the affair, recruitment chain, staged-burglary plan, dense alibi trail, incriminating-seeming statements, failure to aid Elizabeth, police questioning, denial, and suicide.

### Abuse and disputed fatal conduct

- [[the-alabama-murders-part-3-a-peculiar-institution]] reports Elizabeth's alleged bruising and fear of Charles, the mismatch between the recovered weapon and fatal wounds, the survival timeline, and the episode's alternative-assailant theory.

## Qualifications

The two sources are consecutive episodes from one openly revisionist series, not independent confirmation. Investigator suspicion, witness interpretation of demeanor, the affair, and suicide do not by themselves prove murder. Part 2's religious-shame explanation is speculative, and Part 3's theory that Charles inflicted the fatal wounds depends on circumstantial weapon, timing, and abuse evidence rather than an adjudication. The complete police, medical, confession, trial, and family records are outside the bounded evidence.

## What Changed

- Created a profile separating evidence of attack planning from the later disputed theory of fatal conduct.
- Preserved religious shame as a possible explanation rather than a known motive.
- Joined Part 2's investigator suspicions to Part 3's weapon, timing, and abuse evidence without treating the combined case as conclusive.
