# 法正 / Fa Zheng

## Overview

法正 / Fa Zheng is a Fufeng-born adviser whose current profile moves from long underuse under [[LiuZhang|刘璋]], through a covert alignment with [[ZhangSongLateHan|张松]] and [[LiuBei|刘备]], to later tactical precision and morally troubling use of administrative power.

## Current Profile

Fa Zheng's profile begins with frustrated ability rather than immediate influence. He enters Shu during disorder and famine, remains in minor posts under Liu Zhang, and is reportedly mocked despite his abilities. His friend Zhang Song recommends him as envoy to Liu Bei; Fa Zheng returns persuaded that Liu Bei can become a ruler worth serving, after which the two men begin planning to receive Liu Bei into Yizhou. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1123-ta-bu-shang-zhanchang-ruhe-li-xia-xianhe-zhangong-losloy1puogq4o9azo2x_ombrbyz|Hanji 1123]] supplies this early layer, while [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1126-maizhu-qiurong-de-zhangsong-zenme-si-de-lkfazjqcokpijxykqxagybgewhrfx|Hanji 1126]] carries it through Zhang Song's death, Liu Bei's conquest of Yizhou, and Fa Zheng's later importance in the Hanzhong campaign.

His later profile joins precise tactical value to morally troubling power use. At Dingjun Mountain, he is presented as recognizing how a particular position can be turned against [[XiahouYuan|夏侯渊]], placing him inside [[AdviserStateArchitecture|谋士政权架构]] as a campaign planner alongside, but not identical to, Zhuge Liang's broader state-system role. He is also portrayed as remembering small favors and resentments and using law-like or administrative instruments against private enemies. The current synthesis therefore retains both strategic ability and retaliatory danger rather than converting effectiveness into moral innocence. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-976-sanguo-moushi-dapandian-shui-shi-ni-xinzhong-de-shen-ljgpbosq-9xiowpgaeyossmgst6x|Hanji 976]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-977-yi-zheng-yi-xie-luanshi-dushi-de-guijue-fengyun-lgtpgxl5anxial8ym2nt8dmuc3ee|Hanji 977]] ground these later layers.

## Key Characteristics

- Talented but underused Liu Zhang official whose frustration becomes part of an alignment shift.
- Close Zhang Song associate and envoy whose meeting with Liu Bei precedes a covert Yizhou transfer project.
- Survives the exposure of that project and converts alignment choice into strategic service during Liu Bei's Yizhou and Hanzhong campaigns.
- Tactical planner associated with exploiting terrain and timing at Dingjun Mountain.
- Adviser whose strengths are compared in the source tradition with Cao-side figures such as Cheng Yu and Guo Jia.
- Morally ambiguous officeholder whose formal tools can serve private resentment as well as public strategy.

## Evidence

- Underuse and realignment: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1123-ta-bu-shang-zhanchang-ruhe-li-xia-xianhe-zhangong-losloy1puogq4o9azo2x_ombrbyz|Hanji 1123]] describes Fa Zheng's minor offices, friendship with Zhang Song, mission to Liu Bei, and participation in a plan to bring Liu Bei into Yizhou.
- Consequence of realignment: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1126-maizhu-qiurong-de-zhangsong-zenme-si-de-lkfazjqcokpijxykqxagybgewhrfx|Hanji 1126]] presents Fa Zheng as helping Liu Bei take Yizhou and later contributing important judgment in the Hanzhong campaign.
- Tactical precision: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-976-sanguo-moushi-dapandian-shui-shi-ni-xinzhong-de-shen-ljgpbosq-9xiowpgaeyossmgst6x|Hanji 976]] presents Fa Zheng as identifying a usable position against Xiahou Yuan at Dingjun Mountain.
- Administrative revenge: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-977-yi-zheng-yi-xie-luanshi-dushi-de-guijue-fengyun-lgtpgxl5anxial8ym2nt8dmuc3ee|Hanji 977]] uses Fa Zheng as a case where legal or bureaucratic form can conceal retaliatory purpose.

## Qualifications

The four podcast notes are interpretive summaries rather than a complete biography. Hanji 1123's genealogy, migration motive, degree of underuse, hometown ridicule, feigned reluctance, private conversation, Liu Bei charisma, and Chen Shou comparison require direct textual corroboration. Hanji 1126's account of Fa Zheng's Yizhou and Hanzhong contribution and Zhuge Liang's qualified respect is compressed. Hanji 976 compresses the Dingjun campaign, while Hanji 977's revenge examples and legal mechanics are metaphor-heavy and explicitly source-scoped. The current evidence does not establish that grievance alone caused Fa Zheng's realignment or that later tactical skill validates every political method attributed to him.

## What Changed

- Extended the realignment sequence through Zhang Song's death and Liu Bei's conquest of Yizhou.
- Connected early underuse more explicitly to Fa Zheng's later value in the Hanzhong campaign.
- Preserved the distinction between demonstrated strategic value and moral approval of Fa Zheng's methods.
