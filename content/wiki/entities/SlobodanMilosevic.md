# Slobodan Milošević

## Overview

Slobodan Milošević was a Serbian and Yugoslav political leader whose profile connects the mobilization of pre-existing grievances to the institutional withdrawal that ended his rule.

## Current Profile

[[2-civil-war-glt5809911007]] does not present Milošević as a self-sufficient cause of Yugoslavia's collapse. It uses him as a catalyst: a political actor able to identify, intensify, and mobilize grievances that already existed within a brittle state.

The profile therefore preserves a dual claim. Structural pressures help explain why mobilization was possible, while leadership choices help explain why inherited divisions became politically useful and violent. Treating Milošević only as a symptom would erase agency; treating him as the complete cause would restore the same monocausal logic the episode rejects.

The end of his rule supplies a [[NonviolentResistanceStrategy]] case. After a fraudulent election, [[Otpor]] combined parallel vote verification, mass mobilization, humor, and [[DilemmaActions]]; police eventually ceased defending him, illustrating how a ruler can lose a decisive [[PillarsOfSupport|pillar of support]].

## Key Characteristics

- Serbian and Yugoslav leader associated in the episode with Yugoslavia's breakup.
- Example of a leader who can exploit rather than invent all underlying grievances.
- Catalyst connecting political ambition to nationalist mobilization and institutional brittleness.
- Case used to keep individual agency inside a multicausal account of civil war.
- Autocrat whose removal illustrates the political effect of police withdrawal and mass civil resistance.

## Evidence

- Leader-catalyst framing: [[2-civil-war-glt5809911007]] explicitly asks whether leaders cause civil wars or exploit conditions already present.
- Yugoslav application: [[2-civil-war-glt5809911007]] presents Milošević as a figure able to mobilize existing grievances for political advantage.
- Resistance and removal: [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-5212bd1a2369bdd5087bc9f33d3a033a-49704134-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-5212bd1a2369bdd5087bc9f33d3a033a-49704134]] connects fraudulent election claims, Otpor's tactics, mass protest, police non-defense, and his departure.

## Qualifications

The sources offer bounded comparative references rather than a biography, chronology, policy record, legal history, or differentiated account of the Yugoslav wars and Serbian transition. "Catalyst" is an analytical role, not a full allocation of responsibility, and the civil-resistance episode does not isolate the effect of Otpor from elections, opposition coordination, economic conditions, foreign influence, or other actors.
