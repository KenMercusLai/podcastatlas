# Slobodan Milošević

## Overview

Slobodan Milošević was a Serbian and Yugoslav political leader used in the source to frame the disputed relationship between individual agency and the structural conditions of civil war.

## Current Profile

[[2-civil-war-glt5809911007]] does not present Milošević as a self-sufficient cause of Yugoslavia's collapse. It uses him as a catalyst: a political actor able to identify, intensify, and mobilize grievances that already existed within a brittle state.

The profile therefore preserves a dual claim. Structural pressures help explain why mobilization was possible, while leadership choices help explain why inherited divisions became politically useful and violent. Treating Milošević only as a symptom would erase agency; treating him as the complete cause would restore the same monocausal logic the episode rejects.

## Key Characteristics

- Serbian and Yugoslav leader associated in the episode with Yugoslavia's breakup.
- Example of a leader who can exploit rather than invent all underlying grievances.
- Catalyst connecting political ambition to nationalist mobilization and institutional brittleness.
- Case used to keep individual agency inside a multicausal account of civil war.

## Evidence

- Leader-catalyst framing: [[2-civil-war-glt5809911007]] explicitly asks whether leaders cause civil wars or exploit conditions already present.
- Yugoslav application: [[2-civil-war-glt5809911007]] presents Milošević as a figure able to mobilize existing grievances for political advantage.

## Qualifications

The source offers only a brief comparative reference and does not provide a biography, chronology, policy record, legal history, or differentiated account of the Yugoslav wars. "Catalyst" is an analytical role, not a full allocation of responsibility among Milošević, other political and military leaders, institutions, armed organizations, outside powers, and local actors.
