# Ralph Adolphs

## Overview
Ralph Adolphs is the [[Caltech]] neuroscientist and psychology guest in the [[HubermanLab]] episode on emotions, social perception, and regulation.

## Current Profile
In the current source, Adolphs is the main scientific voice for treating emotion as a functional control state rather than as a single brain structure or merely a conscious feeling. He moves between conceptual criteria, lesion evidence, social-cognition research, body-brain mapping, and practical regulation examples. His profile in the wiki is therefore source-scoped: he is represented here through one long-form interview rather than a complete biography or literature review.

## Key Characteristics
- He defines emotions by what they do for behavior, not by one human brain implementation.
- He separates emotions from conscious feelings so emotion science does not depend entirely on solving consciousness.
- He uses patient, lesion, animal, and social-perception examples to challenge simplified emotion stories.
- He treats regulation problems as problems of monitoring, granularity, timing, environment, and flexibility.
- He keeps practical claims bounded when they come from personal experience rather than published evidence.

## Evidence
- Functional frame - [[neuroscience-of-emotions-tools-for-improving-emotion-regulation-dr-ralph-adolphs-scim1222961716]] has Adolphs define emotions as functional states that prioritize and organize behavior between reflex and planning.
- Consciousness boundary - [[neuroscience-of-emotions-tools-for-improving-emotion-regulation-dr-ralph-adolphs-scim1222961716]] records him distinguishing functional emotions in animals or possible AI systems from conscious emotional experience.
- Lesion and social evidence - [[neuroscience-of-emotions-tools-for-improving-emotion-regulation-dr-ralph-adolphs-scim1222961716]] uses [[PatientSM]], amygdala lesions, facial-expression research, and text inference to complicate fear and social-reading claims.
- Regulation examples - [[neuroscience-of-emotions-tools-for-improving-emotion-regulation-dr-ralph-adolphs-scim1222961716]] discusses reappraisal, environmental design, cold exposure, ultrarunning, solitude, meditation, and task switching while noting limits.
