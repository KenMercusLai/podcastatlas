# Ark of the Covenant

## Overview

The Ark of the Covenant is the sacred chest that biblical tradition associates with the covenant tablets, divine presence, dangerous holiness, Israelite movement and warfare, and eventually Solomon's Temple. Its disappearance from the record made it a powerful object of later theology, national tradition, cinema, and treasure hunting.

## Current Profile

The current evidence does not support a single uncomplicated biography. [[345-raiders-of-the-lost-ark-part-1-glt1189467433]] presents the Bible as the only direct source and emphasizes [[ArkTraditionLayering]]: the Ark can be a law chest, God's meeting place above the cherubim, a leader of the people, and a weapon that defeats enemies or destroys those who approach it wrongly.

After its placement in Solomon's Temple, the Ark vanishes without a clear biblical account of removal or destruction. Competing traditions place it in Egypt, hidden in the Holy Land, on Mount Gerizim, in Rome, Ireland, or [[Abyssinia|Ethiopia]]. The source treats continued belief and reception as historically visible while keeping the object's existence and fate unresolved.

## Key Characteristics

- Gold-overlaid chest associated with the tablets of the law, poles, a mercy seat, and facing cherubim.
- Material focus for [[BiblicalCovenant]], divine presence, and the Holy of Holies.
- Dangerous sacred object whose power cannot be safely controlled by either Israelites or enemies.
- Mobile leader and battlefield symbol in the Jordan, Jericho, and Philistine narratives.
- Missing object whose unexplained disappearance creates many incompatible survival traditions.
- Modern cultural symbol remade as weapon, relic, national inheritance, and treasure-hunt prize.

## Evidence

- Form and divine presence: [[345-raiders-of-the-lost-ark-part-1-glt1189467433]] reports the Exodus description of a gold-overlaid chest with carrying poles, mercy seat, cherubim, and divine manifestation between them.
- Covenant function: [[345-raiders-of-the-lost-ark-part-1-glt1189467433]] connects the tablets inside the Ark to the covenant between God and the children of Israel.
- Dangerous agency: [[345-raiders-of-the-lost-ark-part-1-glt1189467433]] recounts the Jordan and Jericho stories, Philistine capture and plagues, Dagon's fall, and deaths caused by improper encounter.
- Temple and disappearance: [[345-raiders-of-the-lost-ark-part-1-glt1189467433]] places the Ark in Solomon's Holy of Holies, then notes its absence from the Babylonian loot list and later Second Temple traditions.
- Reception history: [[345-raiders-of-the-lost-ark-part-1-glt1189467433]] surveys Holy Land, Egyptian, Roman, Irish, Ethiopian, cinematic, and modern treasure-hunting claims.

## Qualifications

The Bible is the episode's only direct source for the Ark, and its accounts are treated as layered, fragmentary, and sometimes contradictory. Ancient Near Eastern chests and footstool imagery are possible comparisons, not proof of the Ark's origin. The source does not verify any proposed survival location and leaves open both nonexistence and destruction.

## What Changed

- Created a bounded profile separating biblical functions, disappearance, and later reception.
- Preserved the difference between evidence of belief in the Ark and evidence that a physical object survived.
