## Overview

贾谊 / Jia Yi is an early Western Han writer whose current wiki profile centers on [[GuoQinLun|《过秦论》]] as a reusable diagnosis of Qin's collapse. One source uses his praise of the [[WarringStatesFourLords|战国四公子]] to show how early Han historical judgment responds to Qin's recent fall; another uses his account of blocked advice to explain how coercive rulers lose access to corrective information.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-109-2-chunshenjun-bei-huangdi-dajiuge-quanzu-miemen-locma2plu7v-vsssm5dr5frc3szw|Qinji 109-2]] presents Jia Yi as more favorable to the Four Lords than [[YangXiong|扬雄]] or [[SimaGuang|司马光]]. The episode explains the difference through [[HistoriographicalPresentism|史评当代性]]: writing closer to Qin's sudden collapse, Jia Yi can treat aristocratic patrons who gathered talent and resisted Qin as useful counterweights, whereas later writers are more alert to overmighty ministers and weakened ruler control.

A governance layer appears in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-337-gudai-zuiming-keyi-you-duo-huangtang-lqoqdtg35ua6m9y4shcz9u8zygp1|Hanji 337]]. The host says [[SimaQian|司马迁]] quotes Jia Yi at length in the Qin account to show that a ruler who rejects advice makes loyal ministers afraid to remonstrate and capable advisers afraid to plan, so disorder can grow without reaching the center. Placed beside [[YanYiWesternHan|颜异]]'s 腹诽 case, Jia Yi becomes not only an evaluator of historical elites but a source for the feedback-collapse mechanism linking punished dissent to political blindness.

## Key Characteristics

- Early Western Han writer interpreted through the political problem of Qin's rapid collapse.
- Authorial figure attached to 《过秦论》 and its explanation of Qin failure.
- Positive evaluator of the Four Lords in contrast with later centralization-minded critics.
- Diagnostician of rejected advice as a mechanism that silences loyal and capable officials.
- Historiographical bridge through which later writers can criticize present governance by discussing Qin.

## Qualifications

The two sources do not provide a full biography, textual history, or systematic account of Jia Yi's political thought. Qinji 109-2 gives a comparative historiographical reading rather than proving that early-Han context fully explains his praise of the Four Lords. Hanji 337 paraphrases a selected 《过秦论》 argument through Sima Qian and the podcast host; it does not establish the exact quotation boundary, prove that Sima Qian intended a covert criticism of Han Wudi, or show that rejected advice alone explains Qin's fall. This page therefore preserves the episode-level interpretations as bounded uses of Jia Yi rather than exhaustive authorial intent.
