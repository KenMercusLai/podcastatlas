# John Tyler (historian)

## Overview

John Tyler is a historian whose study of eighteenth-century insurance and shipping records supports a commercial reinterpretation of Boston revolutionary politics.

## Current Profile

Tyler examined hundreds of policies written by Ezekiel Price and held by the Boston Athenaeum. He treats insurance records as unusually revealing because merchants who concealed cargoes and risks from customs officials still needed to describe them accurately enough to insure their voyages. Discrepancies among insurance and customs records, undeclared routes, and elevated premiums led him to identify extensive tea smuggling involving prominent patriots, including John Hancock.

His role in the episode is evidentiary and interpretive. The archive supports material incentives behind non-importation and resistance to the Tea Act, while Tyler's emphasis on intimidation and the physical harms of tarring and feathering prevents revolutionary enforcement from appearing harmless.

## Key Characteristics

- Historian of colonial Boston commerce and smuggling.
- Uses insurance records to reconstruct activity hidden from customs enforcement.
- Connects prominent patriot merchants to illicit tea distribution.
- Interprets the Tea Act as a threat to smugglers as well as a constitutional provocation.
- Emphasizes the coercive violence used to enforce non-importation.
