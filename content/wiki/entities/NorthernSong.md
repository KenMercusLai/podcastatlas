# 北宋 / Northern Song

## Overview

北宋 / Northern Song is the dynasty that emerges from the Five Dynasties military world, consolidates through civil-official and military redesign, becomes a major setting for literary-officeholding and factional politics, and now also carries a fiscal-market extraction branch through Hanji 396-4.

## Current Profile

The founding sources describe Northern Song as an institutional answer to late Tang and Five Dynasties instability. [[ZhaoKuangyin|赵匡胤 / 宋太祖]] rises through Later Zhou military politics, the [[ChenQiaoMutiny|陈桥兵变]], and legitimacy performance, then moves toward [[SongMilitaryCentralization|宋初军事集权]], [[CupWineDisarmsGenerals|杯酒释兵权]], and the [[KaifengCapitalTradeoff|开封定都取舍]]. The lower Taizu episode adds that unification and civil-official order were accompanied by conquest violence and unresolved succession legitimacy.

The Su Shi source gives the mature dynasty's scholar-official face: literary talent, exams, memorials, local governance, poetry, and court faction all belong to one career world. [[WangAnshiReforms|王安石变法]] appear there as a response to fiscal and security pressure that could become coercive in implementation, while [[SongFactionalPolitics|宋代党争]] makes both reform and anti-reform dangerous.

Hanji 396-4 adds a harsher economic diagnosis. It argues that official commerce and state credit monopoly enriched the treasury at the expense of merchants and ordinary people, then connects that logic to [[CaiJing|蔡京]], flower-and-rock transport, and a broader claim that fiscal extraction helped accelerate Northern Song collapse.

## Key Characteristics

- Founded from a military-coup and succession-anxiety environment rather than from a purely bureaucratic or literary order.
- Built stability through civil-over-military redesign, disarmament, internal garrisoning, and Kaifeng-centered logistics.
- Unified the south through pragmatic strategy while creating local resentment where conquest turned into plunder and monopoly taxation.
- Became a scholar-official world where literary ability, officeholding, local service, and political danger were intertwined.
- Reform politics combined real fiscal-security pressures with implementation harms, factional retaliation, and later historiographical arguments.
- Hanji 396-4 adds an economic-collapse branch in which state commercial monopoly and fiscal extraction damage markets and public livelihood.

## Evidence

- Predecessor formation: [[ep252-taipingnian-zhiwai-de-wudai-shiguo-shenme-yang-lnxm7tfs3zmve3erm9qlbehlefkh|EP252]] places Northern Song inside Tang-Song transition, southern-regime accumulation, military turnover, and later historical ordering.
- Founding institutions: [[169-songtaizu-zhisi-zhuying-fusheng-yinmoulun-zhong-de-renxing-mituan-shang-957052909|Song Taizu 169]] explains Chenqiao, legitimacy packaging, Kaifeng, cup-wine disarmament, and military centralization.
- Unification and succession pressure: [[170-songtaizu-zhisi-zhuying-fusheng-yinmoulun-zhong-de-renxing-mituan-xia-958158115|Song Taizu 170]] adds southern-first strategy, Sichuan extraction, the suspicious Taizu-Taizong transfer, and succession legitimacy crisis.
- Scholar-official politics: [[105-su-dongpo-yiqiang-haomai-zhiqi-yi-dupi-buhe-shiyi-741072645|Su Dongpo 105]] places Su Shi, Ouyang Xiu, Wang Anshi, Sima Guang, reform, faction, literary speech, and local service inside the same Northern Song field.
- Fiscal-market extraction: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-396-4-wanganshi-bianfa-zenme-daozhi-beisong-miewang-de-lslupw7c76poanj-zzmh59z1iofr|Hanji 396-4]] argues that state commercial and credit monopoly damaged merchants and people while enriching the treasury, then links Cai Jing's later policies to collapse acceleration.

## Qualifications

The Northern Song page now combines sources with different scopes: founding history, transition history, literary biography, and a short polemical reform critique. Hanji 396-4's claim that Wang Anshi's reforms contributed to the dynasty's fall is preserved as a source interpretation, not elevated into the dynasty's sole or settled explanation.

- Reframed Northern Song as combining founding military redesign, scholar-official politics, and fiscal-market stress.
