# Paris Peace Accords

## Overview

The Paris Peace Accords were the January 1973 agreement that enabled U.S. ground-force withdrawal from Vietnam without creating a durable end to the conflict.

## Current Profile

The episode interprets the agreement primarily as an American political exit. Fighting resumed quickly, Communist forces remained in South Vietnam, and the settlement depended on a threat of renewed U.S. action that became less credible after Watergate and congressional restriction.

Its meaning remains contested because Nixon and Kissinger's purpose is unresolved. The agreement may have been designed to create a sufficient interval before defeat, or it may have rested on an overconfident belief that retaliatory force would preserve the settlement.

## Key Characteristics

- Signed in January 1973 by U.S. and North Vietnamese negotiators.
- Enabled the withdrawal of U.S. ground forces.
- Left Communist forces inside South Vietnam.
- Failed to stop continuing military conflict.
- Depended on political and military enforcement that later disappeared.

## Qualifications

The source does not settle the negotiators' private intentions, the agreement's legal interpretation, or the counterfactual effect of continued American enforcement. It is a narrative history account rather than a complete diplomatic archive.

## What Changed

- Established the agreement as a withdrawal mechanism whose peace depended on absent enforcement.
- Preserved competing cynical-exit and sincere-deterrence readings.
