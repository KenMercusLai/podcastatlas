# Merovingian Dynasty

## Overview

The Merovingian Dynasty was the Frankish royal house whose inherited title and long-haired ritual symbolism outlived its effective power before [[PepinTheShort]] deposed its final king in 751.

## Current Profile

The episode introduces the dynasty through [[Einhard]]'s retrospective contrast between royal appearance and political capacity. Chilperic III retained long hair, a beard, a throne, ceremonial audiences, and the royal name, while mayors of the palace controlled wealth, armies, and government. Those symbols had once helped Clovis combine Frankish ancestry with Roman authority in Gaul; in Carolingian narration they became signs of rustic and empty kingship.

The dynasty's removal was therefore more than a seizure of office. [[CharlesMartel]] and his sons had already accumulated practical power, but Pepin sought papal reassurance and then used [[RoyalAnointingTradition|anointing]] to recode rule around effective government and divine consecration. Chilperic's haircut and confinement in a monastery extinguished the visible markers and succession of the old house.

## Key Characteristics

- Frankish dynasty associated with Clovis and long-haired sacred kingship.
- Royal house that preserved ceremony after effective authority shifted to palace mayors.
- Target of Carolingian retrospective portrayals of indolence and rusticity.
- Dynasty ended through deposition, haircut, monastic confinement, and a rival consecration.

## Evidence

- Symbolic kingship - [[523-charlemagne-return-of-the-kings-part-1-glt5104943454|Part 1]] describes the final king's hair, beard, throne, audiences, and formal title alongside his political weakness.
- Earlier legitimacy - [[523-charlemagne-return-of-the-kings-part-1-glt5104943454|Part 1]] links Clovis to Roman authority at Tours and Frankish royal symbolism.
- Dynastic replacement - [[523-charlemagne-return-of-the-kings-part-1-glt5104943454|Part 1]] connects papal approval, Chilperic's deposition, and Pepin's anointing to the dynasty's end.

## Qualifications

The surviving profile here is mediated by Einhard, a Carolingian courtier writing after Charlemagne's death, so the “powerless king” contrast also legitimates the victors. Ritual kingship was not necessarily meaningless merely because executive power had shifted. The episode does not provide a full social, legal, territorial, or dynastic history from Clovis to Chilperic III.
