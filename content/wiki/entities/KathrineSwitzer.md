# Kathrine Switzer

## Overview

Kathrine Switzer is the runner whose numbered entry in the 1967 Boston Marathon and public resistance to an official's attempt to remove her became a visible challenge to women's exclusion from distance running.

## Current Profile

[[pushkin-goes-to-the-olympics-pushkin-goes-to-the-olympics]] traces Switzer from running at Syracuse University, which had no women's sports teams, through training with volunteer coach Arnie Briggs. After completing a 31-mile training run and confirming that the race rules did not explicitly prohibit women, she entered under her customary “K. V. Switzer” signature.

During the race, an official pursued her and tried to tear off number 261 before her boyfriend Tom Miller knocked him aside. The photographed confrontation made exclusion visible, but the source carefully distinguishes visibility and official entry from first participation: [[BobbiGibb]] had run Boston earlier and was already ahead of Switzer in the 1967 race.

## Key Characteristics

- Trained for marathon distance despite the absence of women's varsity sport at Syracuse.
- Completed a 31-mile training run before entering Boston.
- Entered after confirming that the written rules did not explicitly exclude women.
- Continued after an official physically tried to remove her race number.
- Represents a numbered-entry and public-confrontation milestone rather than the first woman marathoner.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to one structured episode summary, not race records, Switzer's full memoir, contemporary reporting, or a complete history of women's distance running. The official-entry milestone should not erase earlier women runners or turn one confrontation into the sole cause of later institutional change.
