# Natalie Hipple

## Overview

Natalie Hipple is a criminologist presented as developing a more direct measure of shootings by counting bullets that strike people rather than relying on homicide or broad aggravated-assault categories.

## Current Profile

Hipple's work in the episode addresses a data problem: fatal shootings receive distinct attention, while nonfatal shootings can be grouped with punches, shoves, and other assaults. Her team reportedly reconstructed Indianapolis cases by manually reviewing police records and combining them with medical-facility reports.

The resulting “bullet-to-skin contact” measure aims to track violent exposure independently of whether treatment prevents death. It is conceptually useful but operationally demanding, and the episode does not provide its coding rules, validation, coverage, or uncertainty.

## Key Characteristics

- Criminologist focused on the measurement of nonfatal shootings.
- Critic of homicide-only and overly broad aggravated-assault indicators.
- Researcher combining police and medical reporting to reconstruct cases.
- Proponent of bullet-to-skin contact as an exposure measure.
- Contributor to the distinction between violence incidence and medical outcome.

## Evidence

### Indianapolis reconstruction

- [[guns-part-4-moral-hazard]] reports that her team manually reviewed thousands of records and found homicide and bullet-to-skin trends moving differently.

## Qualifications

The source does not supply the study, sampling frame, record-linkage method, treatment of multiple wounds, reporting completeness, trend estimates, or replication. “Bullet-to-skin contact” may count exposure more directly than homicide, but it still requires stable definitions and should be interpreted with victim counts, incident counts, injury severity, population rates, and data-quality measures.
