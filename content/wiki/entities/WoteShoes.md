# 沃特鞋企 / Wote Shoes

## Overview

Wote Shoes is the Putian basketball-footwear brand associated in the bounded sources with Huafeng Shoes, early brand ambition, failed expansion, renewed OEM concentration, and a difficult second-generation rebuilding effort.

## Current Profile

[[no-225-putian-xie-wangshi-gkwrijeowkkqa6hffgt1ih2w]] describes Huafeng as a 1993 contractor for brands including Kappa and Fila before it launched Wote, partnered with the Chinese women's basketball team, and used Wang Zhizhi as an endorser. A listing-oriented expansion around 2010 failed amid sunk investment, dealer receivables, and bank credit withdrawal; the firm survived through asset sales, family support, and retained customers, then returned its emphasis to OEM work.

[[ep255-chang-erdai-de-jicheng-zhi-zhan-nianqingren-jieban-hui-chongsu-qiye-ma-likvzmpgwxnetpaezxclsx4s-hkw]] shows the later succession problem. The founder treated the brand as another child, while the returning son faced a stressed company and tested ecommerce, livestreaming, IP, design, foreign trade, and Amazon sales. The case makes succession a rebuilding problem rather than passive inheritance.

## Key Characteristics

- Putian manufacturer that attempted an early domestic basketball-shoe brand route.
- Brand expansion involved sports partnerships, endorsement, dealer channels, capital raising, and listing ambitions.
- Failed expansion combined operating commitments, unpaid dealer balances, and financing withdrawal.
- Recovery depended on manufacturing customers and family resources more than on strong own-brand sales.
- Second-generation work centers on new channels, design, overseas sales, and consumer language.
- A major customer's relocation demand made overseas factory capacity part of retaining OEM business.

## Qualifications

- The sources use Wote and Huafeng at different levels; the page treats Wote as the brand within the broader manufacturer history without assuming every corporate detail belongs to one unchanged legal entity.
- Sales, customer share, timing, and causes of the financial crisis remain episode-scoped rather than independently audited.
- Overseas relocation preserved a customer relationship but may shift employment and tax value away from Putian; the sources do not provide a full net-impact account.

## What Changed

- Added Wote's pre-succession brand expansion, financing crisis, recovery, and return to OEM emphasis.
- Added customer-driven production relocation to Indonesia.
- Reframed the later successor experiments as part of a longer brand-capability and capital-cycle problem.
