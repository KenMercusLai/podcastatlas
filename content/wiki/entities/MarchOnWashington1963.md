# 1963 March on Washington

## Overview

The 1963 March on Washington was a large civil-rights demonstration at the Lincoln Memorial on August 28, 1963, now most closely remembered for [[IHaveADreamSpeech|Martin Luther King Jr.'s final address]].

## Current Profile

The march joined a long-standing proposal by [[APhilipRandolph|A. Philip Randolph]] to [[BayardRustin|Bayard Rustin]]'s detailed organization and a coalition including the NAACP, National Urban League, CORE, SNCC, and King's allies. It took place amid public opposition, segregationist attacks, Kennedy administration caution, and extensive official preparations for feared disorder.

The event instead produced a large, hopeful, and nonviolent gathering. Its later memory is narrowed when treated only as the setting for one speech: organization, transport, Black and white participation, music, celebrity visibility, labor demands, and internal hierarchy all shaped the day. Most notably, women including Rosa Parks were denied speaking roles, exposing unequal authority inside the coalition.

## Key Characteristics

- National civil-rights demonstration held in Washington on August 28, 1963.
- Product of older labor strategy, inter-organizational coalition work, and extensive logistics.
- Event initially viewed negatively by much of the public and feared by officials as a security risk.
- Large, predominantly Black, peaceful gathering that exceeded some organizational expectations.
- Platform whose final speaking slot produced the event's dominant public memory.
- Equality movement that nevertheless excluded women from speaking roles.

The episode gives approximate crowd proportions and attendance, selective organizational detail, and a speech-centered account rather than a complete institutional history. The peaceful outcome should not erase official intimidation, internal conflict, the labor content of the march, or the agency of participants beyond the leaders named here.
