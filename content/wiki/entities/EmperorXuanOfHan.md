## Overview

Emperor Xuan of Han / 汉宣帝 / 刘询 / 刘病已 enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-689-3-wuxing-chu-dongfang-li-zhongguo-yige-gulao-yuyan-de-xiandai-jiedu-lqccxzbvg-jziurfxm5tr5k4jlvq|Hanji 689-3]] as the Western Han ruler whose reign supplies the five-star omen command formula behind [[ZhaoChongguoWesternHan|赵充国]]'s Southern Qiang campaign setting. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-430-chigua-qunzhong-qianren-yu-xianren-de-gongdou-daxi-lkfcjewfhmhfgw39vqfqkpa9umzq|Hanji 430]] adds an earlier gratitude-and-security layer: Xuan rewards old benefactors such as [[ZhangHeWesternHan|张贺]] and [[BingJiWesternHan|丙吉]], but secretly monitors the deposed former emperor [[LiuHeHaihunHou|刘贺]] before reducing him to [[HaihunMarquisate|海昏侯国]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-433-xihan-wangbao-de-shengzhu-de-xianchen-song-shuo-le-sha-lmaah4tjg_gsewzb6izhojtommlx|Hanji 433]] adds the Shenjue 1 / 61 BCE literary-patronage layer: Xuan sacrifices at [[GanquanPalace|甘泉宫]] and Hedong, sends [[WangBaoWesternHan|王褒]] toward Yizhou in the gold-horse and jade-rooster search, values classics, poetry, music, and fu, and prompts [[ShengzhuDeXianchenSong|《圣主得贤臣颂》]] as a court ode on ruler-minister mutual recognition. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-449-yichu-huangdan-naoju-xiongnu-wuda-chanyu-duoquan-huopin-lo39-mygg6xxo8cwcyvpqzcpzhrm|Hanji 449]] adds a Wufeng 1 / 57 BCE court-decision layer: Xuan performs [[HanYuandi|刘奭]]'s capping rite, consults Xiao Wangzhi when officials urge attacking the internally fractured Xiongnu, and accepts Xiao's recommendation to mourn and aid rather than exploit the crisis militarily. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-459-hanxuandi-weishenme-shuo-taizi-liushi-shi-baijiazi-lsnb6mesdpegwhihmxx06fy8fawz|Hanji 459]] adds an internal succession and statecraft layer: Xuan rebukes crown prince Liu Shi for wanting to rely more purely on Confucian scholars and says Han rule requires a 王霸 / moral-force mixture; he considers [[LiuQinHuaiyangKingWesternHan|淮阳王刘钦]] but preserves Liu Shi because of嫡长 status, [[XuPingjunWesternHan|许平君]]'s memory, and the danger of succession struggle. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-462-kanbi-dayuebing-xiongnu-xianghan-changmian-you-duo-niu-lnfkacjnat32wi-akpwdggvljkxk|Hanji 462]] adds his Huhanye reception layer: Xuan accepts [[XiaoWangzhiWesternHan|萧望之]]'s guest-rite protocol, reduces domestic burdens through amnesty and poll-tax relief, and stages [[HuhanyeChanyuWesternHan|呼韩邪单于]]'s audience as a major display of Han power. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-463-caicaikan-zai-hanxuandi-yanzhong-shui-shi-zuidade-gongchen-lqblzdg7sdkzln4vxhtnnye92292|Hanji 463]] adds the late Ganlu 3 high-point layer: Xuan protects and provisions Huhanye, sees broader Western Regions obedience, creates the [[QilinPavilionMeritHonors|麒麟阁功臣荣典]], standardizes parts of Five Classics interpretation, and presides over palace events that introduce [[WangZhengjun|王政君]] and [[HanChengdi|刘骜 / 后来的汉成帝]]. ## Current Profile

The current profile has two source layers. Hanji 689-3 is narrow and artifact-linked: Xuan appears through an omen-worded command context where "five stars rising in the east" signals advantage for China and defeat for non-Han enemies during a crisis threatening the Han-Western-Regions route. That layer makes him a bridge between [[FiveStarsOutOfTheEastBrocade]], astrological-political language, and Zhao Chongguo's frontier campaign.

Hanji 465 broadens the profile from a campaign setting into a reign evaluation. It says Xuan's early life matters to his later rule: rescue by Bing Ji, prison childhood, release under Han Wudi, and time among common people are used to explain why he understands ordinary suffering. After Huo Guang raises him to the throne, Xuan waits cautiously, consolidates after Huo Guang's death, repairs internal administration, disciplines officials, and stabilizes livelihood. The same source credits him with using Xiongnu internal division, supporting the submitted Huhanye side, weakening Zhizhi's position, reducing the Xiongnu threat, incorporating the Western Regions, and establishing the [[WesternRegionsProtectorate|西域都护府]]. His death is therefore framed as a dynastic transition into [[HanYuandi|汉元帝]]'s accession and visible Western Han decline pressure.

Hanji 449, Hanji 462, and Hanji 463 fill the late-reign achievement scene before that death evaluation. Hanji 449 shows Xuan declining the obvious strike-while-weak option after Xiongnu fragments into five chanyus, choosing Xiao Wangzhi's legitimacy-minded aid proposal instead. Hanji 462 shows Xuan accepting Xiao Wangzhi's argument that Huhanye should be received above the princes as a guest rather than simply ranked as an internal subject. The resulting audience joins ritual flexibility with spectacle: cavalry line the route, Huhanye receives lavish gifts, and mass crowds at Wei Bridge make submission publicly visible. Hanji 463 then shows that support is not only ceremonial: Han escorts, protects, and feeds the submitted chanyu, and Western Regions polities become more obedient after Huhanye's court visit. Xuan fixes that success into political memory through the Qilin Pavilion portraits and into scholarly administration through court settlement of selected Five Classics interpretations. The same episode also shows the reign's future cost: the entrance of Wang Zhengjun into the crown-prince household and the birth of Liu Ao make Xuan's late court the origin point for later Chengdi and Wang-family politics.

Hanji 430, Hanji 457, Hanji 451, Hanji 450, and Hanji 452 complicate that strong-ruler profile before the late diplomatic high point. Hanji 430 shows the positive and suspicious sides together: Xuan repays pre-accession kindness to Zhang He, Zhang Pengzu, Zhang Ba, Bing Ji, and other helpers, yet keeps Liu He under secret surveillance and later grants only a reduced marquisate without royal ritual access. In the Yang Yun case, Xuan's court receives a private reply letter as evidence after Yang Yun has already looked unrepentant through banquets, resentment, and refusal to lower his posture. Hanji 451 then gives the procedural Han Yanshou version of the blemish: Xuan orders investigations after Xiao Wangzhi reframes Han's counteraccusation, has Han's East Commandery violations itemized, and approves beheading after ministers treat Han as both guilty on old accounts and guilty of false accusation. Hanji 450 adds the security motive interpretation: after Huo Guang, Xuan is read as treating Huo-era personnel ties, scholar-official networks, and popular affection as potential threats. Hanji 452 is milder but revealing: Xuan's personal gratitude to Bing Ji is protected through formal impeachment of Xiao Wangzhi, and punishment takes the form of removing a powerful office while preserving Xiao's use as crown-prince tutor. The sources do not deny real exposure in these cases, but they foreground Sima Guang's criticism that killing Yang Yun and other capable or upright officials such as Zhao Guanghan, Gai Kuangrao, and Han Yanshou did not persuade public opinion. Xuan therefore remains an effective restorer and frontier strategist in the wiki, but his profile now carries proportional-punishment, political-security, private-gratitude discipline, and residual-legitimacy containment layers.

Hanji 433 adds a softer cultural layer to that profile. Xuan is shown as a ruler who admires Han Wudi, cultivates classical and literary discussion, and uses court access around [[JinmaMenCourtLiteraryAccess|金马门待诏]] to make writers and scholars visible. His interest in [[WangBaoWesternHan|王褒]] and [[ShengzhuDeXianchenSong|《圣主得贤臣颂》]] supports the positive ideal of [[ShengzhuXianchenMutualRecognition|圣主贤臣互相成就]], while the broader page keeps that ideal beside the harsher evidence that real Xuan-era court politics could still punish or constrain capable officials.

Hanji 459 adds the succession-and-ideology version of the same effective-but-hard profile. Xuan's anger at Liu Shi is not treated as anti-learning in general: Sima Guang's comment keeps a distinction between shallow "俗儒" and true Confucian statecraft. But Xuan's own formula makes practical control nonnegotiable. He sees Liu Shi's humane Confucian disposition as politically insufficient without law, timing, and command of ministers. The episode also shows Xuan restraining his own preference. Liu Qin would fit Xuan's temperament better, yet replacing the established嫡长 crown prince would violate the Xu Pingjun debt and risk the kind of succession violence Xuan knew from earlier Han crises.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han emperor attached to the five-star omen formula that links celestial language, the Niya brocade, and Zhao Chongguo's frontier campaign.
- Former witchcraft-disaster survivor whose prison and commoner experience is used by Hanji 465 to explain later attention to livelihood, and whose pre-accession debts shape Hanji 430's benefactor rewards.
- Cautious post-Huo-Guang ruler who consolidates authority before repairing internal governance, but whose security logic and gratitude debts can turn capable officials' speech or posture into punishable threats.
- Reward-punishment and personnel-assessment ruler praised through Ban Gu's evaluation in Hanji 465.
- Frontier strategist credited with exploiting Xiongnu division, forgoing opportunistic attack when restraint better served prestige, receiving Huhanye through guest-rite protocol, and formalizing Western Regions control.
- Literary patron, memory maker, and succession-risk manager who turns writers into court-visible talent, ministerial service into Qilin Pavilion honor, selected classics interpretation into court orthodoxy, and an abandoned Liu Qin replacement impulse into preservation of the established crown prince.
- Deathbed and turning-point ruler who entrusts affairs to Shi Gao, Xiao Wangzhi, and Zhou Kan before Han Yuandi's accession opens the Yuan-era decline branch.

## Qualifications

This page is not a full biography of Emperor Xuan. Hanji 689-3 remains narrow to the brocade, omen formula, and Zhao Chongguo campaign interpretation; the brocade's direct relationship to Xuan's court remains interpretive because the source does not prove production date, sender, or recipient. Hanji 430 supplies gratitude, Liu He surveillance, and hidden-virtue moral interpretation, not the full Changyi deposition record, Liu He biography, or a medical explanation for Bing Ji's recovery. Hanji 433 supplies Xuan's literary-patronage atmosphere, Wang Bao's court visibility, and the political reading of [[ShengzhuDeXianchenSong|《圣主得贤臣颂》]], not a full history of Western Han fu, the daizhao system, or the gold-horse and jade-rooster cult. Hanji 449 supplies a Xiongnu crisis-response and crown-prince capping layer, not a full account of five-chanyu military geography or the complete court deliberation. Hanji 457 supplies the Yang Yun case and Sima Guang's severity critique, not a complete legal history of every official Xuan executed. Hanji 451 supplies the Han Yanshou case procedure through the podcast's retelling, while Hanji 450 supplies an interpretive motive layer around Huo Guang's aftermath; neither is the complete original legal record. Hanji 452 supplies the Bing Ji gratitude and Xiao Wangzhi demotion branch, not the full procedure behind Xiao's household-market accusation or Bing Ji's biography. Hanji 459 supplies the Liu Shi warning and Liu Qin alternative-heir branch, not a full biography of either prince, Zhang Jieyu, or the original Wangdao/Badao debate. Hanji 462 supplies a compact Huhanye audience and domestic-notice layer, not full biographies of Xuan's sons, Zhang Lu, Du Yannian, Yu Dingguo, or Zhao Chongguo. Hanji 463 supplies a compact late-Ganlu scene, not full biographies of all Qilin Pavilion ministers, a full history of Five Classics standardization, or a complete account of Wang Zhengjun's early life. Hanji 465 is a compact reign evaluation and does not reconstruct Xuan's full biography, the Huo-family purge, legal reforms, exact Western Regions administration, or the full original evaluations by Ban Gu and Liu Xiang. Its death-cause claim is deliberately narrow: the episode reports grave illness, entrustment, and death, but not a specific disease or immediate medical cause.
