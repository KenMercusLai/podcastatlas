# The Carrier Bag Theory of Fiction / 《小说的载物袋理论》

## Overview
The Carrier Bag Theory of Fiction is [[UrsulaKLeGuin|Ursula K. Le Guin]]'s essay introduced in [[episode-225-yidai-yilu-8212959555-438866|Episode 225: 一袋一录]] as both a literary theory and an evolutionary metaphor.

## Current Profile
In this source, the essay is a corrective to spear-like accounts of civilization and story. It argues that containers, gathering, storage, maintenance, care, and relation can be more fundamental than weapons, hunting, heroic conflict, and linear victory when explaining both survival and fiction.

## Key Characteristics
- Opposes the assumption that human civilization naturally begins from hunting weapons and heroic conquest.
- Revalues containers, gathering, preservation, maintenance, care, and connection as civilizational technologies.
- Treats the novel as a possible bag rather than only an arrow, spear, or conflict machine.
- Allows conflict to exist inside fiction while refusing to make conflict the only organizing principle.
- Gives later readers a vocabulary for [[CarrierBagNarrative|carrier-bag narrative]] and [[NarrativePluralism|narrative pluralism]].

## Evidence
Evolutionary metaphor:
- [[episode-225-yidai-yilu-8212959555-438866|Episode 225: 一袋一录]] says the host presents the essay as challenging civilization-origin stories built around hunting and weapons, noting the importance of gathering and containers.

Literary theory:
- [[episode-225-yidai-yilu-8212959555-438866|Episode 225: 一袋一录]] summarizes Le Guin's fiction claim as a move from hero conflict toward gathered relations among characters, memories, emotions, and details.

Interpretive afterlife:
- [[episode-225-yidai-yilu-8212959555-438866|Episode 225: 一袋一录]] connects the essay to Chinese literary traditions, [[DonnaHaraway]], Mochila bags, river-rights movements, and [[RobertMacfarlane]].

## Qualifications
The episode presents the essay through the host's interpretation. It does not provide a full textual exegesis, and it keeps the essay's feminist and anthropological claims at the level of conceptual framing rather than independent historical proof.
