# Daisy Christodoulou

## Overview

Daisy Christodoulou is the education and assessment specialist who joins [[TheRestIsHistory|The Rest Is History]] to explain the history, design tradeoffs, and social effects of examinations.

## Current Profile

Christodoulou connects long-run institutional history to practical measurement. She uses Chinese civil-service exams, oral disputation, Prussian reform, Victorian papers, changing curricula, and pandemic assessment to argue that common exams are imperfect but difficult to replace when a system needs comparable judgments at scale.

She also introduces [[NoMoreMarking]], whose [[ComparativeJudgmentAssessment|comparative-judgment]] method ranks extended writing through repeated pairwise choices. Her position is reformist rather than simply pro-exam: content, fairness, public confidence, formulaic rubrics, and the distributional effects of alternatives all need scrutiny.

## Key Characteristics

- Connects assessment practice to the institutional history of education and state selection.
- Defends comparability while acknowledging rigidity, social closure, and curriculum distortion.
- Presents comparative judgment as an alternative to detailed rubric marking for extended work.
- Treats claims about changing exam difficulty as evidence problems requiring candidate responses and context.
- Warns that teacher assessment can distribute disadvantage differently from common exams.
