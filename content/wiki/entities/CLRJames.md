# C. L. R. James

## Overview

C. L. R. James was a Trinidad-born Marxist writer, historian, cricket thinker, and post-colonial intellectual whose transatlantic career ended in a flat above the *Race Today* offices in Brixton.

## Current Profile

[[211-london-people-part-3-glt1562041230]] connects James's Trinidad education and cricket ability to his move to Britain, journalism, Trotskyist politics, and writing. *The Black Jacobins* made the Haitian Revolution central to his historical work, while *Beyond a Boundary* used cricket to examine Caribbean life, British culture, empire, attachment, and criticism together.

After years in Britain, North America, and Trinidad, James returned to London through his nephew Darcus Howe's *Race Today* network. His final home above the magazine's Railton Road offices joined a transnational intellectual biography to [[Brixton]]'s Black British political and cultural geography.

## Key Characteristics

- Trinidad-born intellectual shaped by education, literature, sport, and colonial society.
- Marxist and Trotskyist writer working across history, politics, drama, and journalism.
- Major interpreter of the Haitian Revolution through *The Black Jacobins*.
- Cricket thinker whose *Beyond a Boundary* joins sport to empire and Caribbean identity.
- Transatlantic figure with sustained ties to Britain, the Caribbean, the United States, and Mexico.
- Late-life Brixton resident connected to *Race Today* and local Black British memory.
