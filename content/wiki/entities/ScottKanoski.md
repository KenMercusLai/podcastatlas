# Scott Kanoski

## Overview
Scott Kanoski is presented as a neurobiologist studying how feeding, metabolic signals, the vagus nerve, gut microbes, and the hippocampus interact to shape memory, appetite, and food preference.

## Current Profile
Kanoski's research program treats eating and cognition as bidirectional. Rather than assigning appetite only to the hypothalamus or memory only to the hippocampus, he describes a distributed system in which meal memories, bodily signals, nutrients, hormones, reward pathways, and social context jointly influence behavior. His examples span human behavioral experiments and observational evidence, but much of the causal mechanism comes from rodents.

His public stance in the source is notably evidence-bounded. He treats GLP-1 drugs as effective diabetes and obesity treatments while leaving microdosing, cognitive enhancement, lean-person use, and brain access unresolved. He likewise presents diet, vagus stimulation, exercise, and microbial manipulation as research programs rather than ready-made human protocols.

## Key Characteristics
- Studies the two-way relationship between feeding and hippocampal-dependent memory.
- Uses vagal, hormonal, microbial, and behavioral mechanisms to connect the gut and brain.
- Investigates developmental dietary exposure and later cognitive outcomes in animal models.
- Separates promising mechanisms from established human clinical evidence.
- Frames appetite and food preference as plastic, distributed processes rather than fixed traits.

## Evidence
- Research focus - [[how-food-choices-shape-memory-cognition-cravings-dr-scott-kanoski-scim9087005398]] connects Kanoski's laboratory work to sugar, Western-style and heat-treated diets, hippocampal acetylcholine, vagal pathways, and gut microbes.
- Translational restraint - [[how-food-choices-shape-memory-cognition-cravings-dr-scott-kanoski-scim9087005398]] repeatedly distinguishes rodent causality from human association and declines to treat GLP-1 microdosing or specific probiotics as established interventions.
- Systems framing - [[how-food-choices-shape-memory-cognition-cravings-dr-scott-kanoski-scim9087005398]] presents hypothalamic, brainstem, hippocampal, endocrine, reward, interoceptive, and social influences as complementary layers.

## Qualifications
This profile rests on one public interview summarized without primary-paper references, complete methods, sample sizes, or effect sizes. Research conclusions, institutional details, and clinical implications should therefore remain limited to what the source reports. Animal findings and preliminary or observational human findings are not individualized dietary, neurological, or medication advice.
