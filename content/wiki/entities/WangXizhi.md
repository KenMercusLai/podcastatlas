# 王羲之 / Wang Xizhi

## Overview

王羲之 / Wang Xizhi is the Eastern Jin calligrapher and official whom the source presents as organizer of the 353 [[LantingGathering|兰亭雅集]] and author of [[LantingjiXu|《兰亭集序》]].

## Current Profile

The episode places Wang late in life among more than forty elite participants at Lanting, where drinking, verse composition, landscape, and sociability become the setting for reflection on change and death. His cultural afterlife is inseparable from the later fame of the preface and its calligraphy, especially Tang imperial admiration and transmitted copies. That fame does not settle whether the received long text is wholly his: the source contrasts it with the shorter *Linhe Xu* preserved in a commentary to *Shishuo Xinyu* and retains later addition as a possibility.

## Key Characteristics

- Eastern Jin official and calligrapher associated with the 353 Lanting gathering.
- Organizer whose social circle included relatives and prominent aristocratic participants.
- Author traditionally credited with a preface joining landscape, pleasure, aging, and mortality.
- Central figure in a reception history shaped by transmitted copies and Tang patronage.
- Historically important despite unresolved questions about the received text's formation.

## Evidence

- Gathering and authorship: [[24-dongxifang-de-fanju-zuihou-de-wancan-yu-lanting-yaji-lraekm-bgowhg1wxysfshcwoz5xp]] attributes the gathering and preface to Wang and places them in 353 at Shanyin.
- Cultural afterlife: [[24-dongxifang-de-fanju-zuihou-de-wancan-yu-lanting-yaji-lraekm-bgowhg1wxysfshcwoz5xp]] connects later prestige to calligraphic transmission and Tang imperial admiration.
- Textual qualification: [[24-dongxifang-de-fanju-zuihou-de-wancan-yu-lanting-yaji-lraekm-bgowhg1wxysfshcwoz5xp]] contrasts the received preface with the shorter *Linhe Xu* and leaves later expansion unresolved.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to one podcast account, not a full biography or calligraphic history. Participant count, political context, the relation to the Jingu gathering, textual authenticity, and the precise transmission of the calligraphy require specialist verification.
