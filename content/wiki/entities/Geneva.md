# Geneva

## Overview

Geneva is presented as a sixteenth-century Protestant city-state that combined refuge, preaching, welfare, education, moral oversight, and coercive doctrinal government.

## Current Profile

[[274-switzerland-calvins-cancel-culture-glt2874543265]] treats Geneva as both sanctuary and disciplinary project. Persecuted Protestants, including English and Scottish reformers, came to the city, and John Knox praised its Christian order. Under [[JohnCalvin|Calvin]]'s influence, ministers preached, teachers instructed, deacons cared for vulnerable people, and elders monitored conduct through the consistory.

The [[MichaelServetus|Servetus]] execution exposes the settlement's limit. Civic authorities rather than Calvin alone arrested, tried, sentenced, and burned Servetus, and they consulted other Protestant cities. Geneva's capacity to protect one threatened confession therefore coexisted with punishment of doctrinal dissent inside the resulting [[ConfessionalCommonwealth|confessional commonwealth]].

## Key Characteristics

- Swiss city-state and major refuge for persecuted Protestants.
- Center of Calvin's preaching and institutional influence.
- Christian civic project joining worship, education, poor relief, and elder oversight.
- Consistory-based site of recurrent moral and behavioral scrutiny.
- Polity whose councils retained authority distinct from Calvin.
- Place where Servetus's execution made Protestant coercion internationally visible.

## Evidence

- Refuge and reputation - [[274-switzerland-calvins-cancel-culture-glt2874543265]] describes Protestant migration and Knox's praise.
- Institutional order - [[274-switzerland-calvins-cancel-culture-glt2874543265]] outlines the four offices and weekly consistory.
- Civic autonomy - [[274-switzerland-calvins-cancel-culture-glt2874543265]] distinguishes Calvin's influence from the city authorities' legal power and choice of execution method.
- Coercive boundary - [[274-switzerland-calvins-cancel-culture-glt2874543265]] uses Servetus's trial and burning to test the city's claim to Christian liberty.

## Qualifications

This page represents Geneva through one episode focused on Calvin and Servetus, not a full political, social, demographic, or confessional history. The source's description of welcome extending beyond Protestants, the proportion summoned by the consistory, civic motives, the scale of popular resistance, and the city's direct influence on later Dutch and Anglo-American cultures require further evidence. Refuge and oppression are both source-supported aspects of the profile; neither should erase the other.

## What Changed

- Established Geneva as a dual case of Protestant sanctuary and coercive confessional discipline.
- Distinguished Calvin's informal and institutional power from the councils' formal civic authority.
- Made the Servetus execution the clearest boundary on the city's religious liberty.
