# 宜阳 / Yiyang (Warring States)

宜阳 / Yiyang appears in [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-27-weiguan-weihuiwang-he-qiweiwang-shi-za-shangye-hupeng-lminlxhrm5bethub9kyy-8blimrb]] as the [[HanState|韩国]] place taken by [[QinState|秦国]] in 335 BCE. The episode says Qin had already attacked around Yiyang more than fifty years earlier, but had then taken only several large outlying settlements rather than the city itself.

The source treats Yiyang as a long-coveted strategic target for Qin, comparable in function to [[WeiState|魏国]]'s Hexi lands. On this page, Yiyang is kept as a Warring States place node rather than merged with later or modern Yiyang references.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-28-hanzhaohou-de-kuaile-ni-xiangxiang-budao-ln2tbxniz4kvwogbwp32exmdokz8]] uses the same loss as background for [[HanZhaohou|韩昭侯]]'s high-gate criticism. Because Han has recently suffered Qin's attack and drought, the place becomes part of the circumstance that makes the gate project [[UntimelyExtravagance|时绌举赢]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-54-wo-zun-ni-shi-wang-ni-dang-wo-shi-pi-lvhcz0ban9frlmwshd9vch-q7-ie]] reuses Yiyang as a threat point in [[ZhangYiStrategist|张仪]]'s speech to [[HanXiangwang|韩襄王]]. Zhang Yi warns that if Han does not serve [[QinState|秦国]], Qin can seize Yiyang and control Chenggao, splitting Han and making its palace and parks no longer secure. The place therefore moves from a prior Qin gain into a live piece of [[EncirclementThreatPersuasion|包围威胁式说服]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-57-gudai-fa-dushi-haiyou-zhe-zuoyong-lj6wi61osaytowgmdp8dtkrwb-si]] makes that tension explicit. In 308 BCE, [[GanMao|甘茂]] treats Yiyang as a large [[HanState|韩国]] target that [[QinState|秦国]] cannot take quickly after a long march, and the next year he is still besieging it after five months. This sits uneasily beside the earlier 335 BCE "Qin takes Yiyang" notice, so the page should keep both claims as a source-layer problem until later evidence clarifies whether the earlier taking was partial, temporary, differently scoped, or erroneous.
