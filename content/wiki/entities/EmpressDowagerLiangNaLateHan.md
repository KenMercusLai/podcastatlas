## Overview
梁妠 / Empress Dowager Liang Na is the late-Han empress and dowager whose current profile now begins with her 132 CE selection as [[EmperorShunOfHan|汉顺帝]]'s empress after ministers reject lottery-based selection. Later layers show her authority handling [[EmperorChongOfHan|汉冲帝]]'s death announcement, installing [[EmperorZhiOfHan|汉质帝]] as a child successor, appointing suppression officials in 145 CE, formalizing [[EmperorHuanOfHan]]'s accession in 146 CE, and then only partly restraining [[LiangJiLateHan]]'s later terror.

## Current Profile
[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-811-gudai-huangdi-jing-kao-chouqian-xuan-huanghou-lghfih9xcrjyrvdlb4obdxvwhl1y|Hanji 811]] supplies Liang Na's first current layer. She is introduced as [[LiangShangLateHan|梁商]]'s daughter and a relative of the earlier Liang Guiren, enters the palace, becomes Shun's guiren, and is favored. When Shun considers choosing an empress by lottery or divination, [[HuGuangLateHan|胡广]] and Guo Qian object, and Liang Na is eventually selected after the criteria shift toward virtue, age, and appearance.

The same source makes Liang Na's virtue narrative central to her appointment. She is said to decline repeated summons and urge Shun to share favor among palace women, which Shun interprets as filial and restrained conduct. Her elevation then immediately changes Liang-family status because Liang Shang is made Tejin and later Zhijinwu.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-825-lishi-gaosu-ni-dang-ni-meiyong-shi-renxing-zui-zisi-luv-8z9axuejnfhlkgsuxee3uked|Hanji 825]] supplies Liang Na's first current regency layer. After Emperor Chong dies, she considers delaying the announcement until summoned royal candidates arrive in Luoyang, but [[LiGuLateHan|李固]] warns against concealed death and persuades her to publish the death that night. When [[LiuSuanLateHan|刘蒜]] and Liu Zuan arrive, Liang Na and Liang Ji choose the eight-year-old Liu Zuan, making him Emperor Zhi despite Li Gu's older-successor advice.

The same source also shows bounded reform receptivity. Liang Na accepts Li Gu's modest-burial recommendation for Emperor Chong and then entrusts government to the Three Excellencies and senior ministers, creating a short window in which Li Gu's cleanup removes harmful eunuchs and irregular officials before backlash starts.

The second 145 CE layer shows Liang Na acting as regent before the Huan accession crisis. She seeks commanders for Xu-Yang unrest, appoints [[TengFuLateHan|滕抚]], and later reduces the punishment of [[ZhongHaoLateHan|种暠]] and [[YingChengLateHan|应承]] after Li Gu argues that execution for failed service would discourage future official effort.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-828-da-jiangjun-liangji-shanquan-feili-de-donghan-jianchen-llqnqijagtmdlgitulddxspncu0e|Hanji 828]] supplies the accession layer. Liang Na's earlier plan to marry her younger sister to Liu Zhi brings him toward Luoyang before Emperor Zhi's death. After [[CaoTengLateHan]] persuades Liang Ji to favor Liu Zhi, Liang Na's authority formalizes the outcome: Liang Ji is sent with staff, tally, and princely carriage to receive Liu Zhi into the South Palace, and Liang Na continues hearing government because the new emperor is fifteen and not yet adult by ritual marker.

The same source shows Liang Na acting through the Liang-family power channel against Li Gu. After Li Gu continues resisting Liu Zhi's accession, Liang Ji grows angry and has Liang Na remove him from the taiwei office.

The 147 CE royal-placement layer comes from [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-829-junzi-weishenme-doubuguo-xiaoren-lthvnstkfu8n9thx1sz6yezl4yqq|Hanji 829]]: after Liu Hong dies without a son, Liang Na appoints Huan's younger brother Liu Kui as Bohai king and Liu Hong's successor. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-830-donghan-ligu-wo-sui-si-yourong-lt-j7n9gotiw-negqxojoacf6ji|Hanji 830]] then shows bounded restraint: she initially refuses Du Qiao's arrest, orders Li Gu released after petitions, pardons mourners, and permits return burial, but she does not stop Liang Ji's renewed accusation or the prison deaths.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-832-wei-lingdao-beiguo-ta-shi-sha-haishi-zhi-lmfpuixmi4odrf6lu31rf6-66-lq|Hanji 832]] supplies the transition layer: Liang Na returns governing authority to Huan and dies soon after. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-833-tanguan-neng-you-duo-lipu-kanwan-dadieyanjing-lqudlijjyqmcro-wsmrvwkodgt72|Hanji 833]] shows the downstream field after her exit, with Liang Ji and [[SunShouLateHan]] receiving expanded honors while the Liang household-power sequence continues.

## Key Characteristics
- Empress whose initial legitimacy is narrated through virtue, deference, and ministerial rejection of lottery-based selection.
- Liang-family hinge whose appointment immediately raises Liang Shang's status.
- Dowager who accepts public death announcement after Li Gu rejects concealing Emperor Chong's death, then chooses the child Liu Zuan over Liu Suan.
- Regent who appoints suppression officials and can reduce punishment in the 145 CE unrest field.
- Regent whose authority formalizes Liu Zhi's accession and continues because Huan is still ritually underage.
- Liang-family authority figure who removes Li Gu after his resistance to Liang Ji's succession plan.
- Later dowager whose royal assignment, release orders, pardons, burial permission, return of authority, and death show real but non-durable control over Liang Ji's field.

## Qualifications
The current source set does not provide Liang Na's full reign, personal policy program, or independent relationship with all Liang relatives. Hanji 811 shows the legitimacy narrative around her empress selection, but it does not independently prove her private motives or the fairness of the selection process. Hanji 825 shows regency authority in death-announcement timing, child accession, burial restraint, and ministerial delegation, but it does not fully prove her private reasons for preferring Liu Zuan over Liu Suan. Hanji 826 shows appointment and sentence reduction, but it does not prove her private reasoning in the Zhong Hao and Ying Cheng case. Hanji 828 shows her formal role in accession and removal actions but does not prove her full private reasoning. Hanji 830 shows protective and pardoning authority, but it also shows that her orders do not amount to durable control over Liang Ji. Hanji 832 and Hanji 833 define her mainly through transition effects: returning power, dying, and leaving the Liang family exposed but still materially rewarded.

## What Changed
- Added Hanji 811 as Liang Na's earliest current layer: empress selection after anti-lottery correction, virtue/deference narration, and Liang Shang's immediate status rise.
- Reframed Hanji 825 as the first regency layer rather than the beginning of her profile.
- Preserved Hanji 826 as the second 145 CE layer around command selection and failed-service sentence reduction.
- Preserved Hanji 828 as Liang Na's direct 146 CE accession, continuing-regency, and Li Gu removal layer.
- Preserved her return of power and death as later transition points in the Liang-family sequence.
