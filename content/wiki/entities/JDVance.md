# JD Vance

## Overview

JD Vance is a U.S. vice president and new-right political figure whose wiki record connects [[PeterThiel]]-backed political recruitment, post-Trump movement institutionalization, economic nationalism, immigration restriction, foreign-policy restraint, and a possible future presidential coalition.

## Current Profile

The bounded sources show Vance in three overlapping roles. He is a product of a long political-talent pipeline around [[PeterThiel]] and the technology right; an ideological carrier for [[NewRightPolicyNetwork|new-right]] economic populism, nationalism, immigration rethinking, and selective foreign-policy restraint; and now an administration advocate translating those ideas into claims about border enforcement, reindustrialization, H-1B restrictions, benefit-fraud enforcement, electricity supply, AI governance, and interest-based alliances.

His September 2026 All-In interview sharpens rather than replaces the earlier profile. Vance defends risky, long-horizon executive action and argues that [[DonaldTrump]] tests whether officials are choosing the easy path or the structurally correct one. Yet the source is also campaign communication: many quantitative and causal claims are asserted without independent verification, and its non-entanglement, fiscal, fraud, and industrial-policy positions remain contested.

## Key Characteristics

- Combines economic populism and political nationalism with a selective rather than categorical openness to trade, skilled immigration, technology, and alliances.
- Treats border control, manufacturing capacity, electricity supply, and fiscal credibility as linked foundations of state capacity.
- Prefers administrative action when Congress is unlikely to legislate, including the source's account of a $100,000 H-1B fee.
- Frames frontier AI governance through national capability and defensive access rather than a global approval authority.
- Presents alliances with [[Israel]] and [[NATO]] as useful but subordinate to independently defined U.S. interests.
- Functions as both a governing spokesperson and a plausible post-Trump movement successor.

## Qualifications

The sources mix reporting, ideological interpretation, forecasts, friendly interviews, and campaign messaging. Thiel-network accounts establish influence and support but do not reduce Vance to a proxy. Predictions about 2026 or 2028 are not outcomes. The September interview gives Vance's position clearly but does not independently verify its figures or resolve whether the Iran action fits a restraint doctrine, whether executive H-1B measures will survive legal challenge, or whether claimed fraud savings translate into durable deficit reduction.

## What Changed

- The profile now includes Vance's own integrated governing case rather than only third-party network, ideology, and succession interpretations.
- His foreign-policy posture is more precisely characterized as interest-based alliance management rather than simple isolationism.
- Energy abundance, cyber-defense access, H-1B worker protection, and federal-state benefit verification are now explicit parts of the profile.
