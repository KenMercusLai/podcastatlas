# JD Vance

## Overview

JD Vance is a U.S. vice president and new-right political figure whose record connects [[PeterThiel]]-backed recruitment, post-Trump movement institutionalization, economic nationalism, selective foreign-policy restraint, and a possible 2028 succession coalition.

## Current Profile

The sources place Vance in three overlapping roles: a product of the technology right's political-talent pipeline, an ideological carrier for new-right economic and immigration policy, and an administration advocate translating those commitments into border enforcement, reindustrialization, H-1B restriction, benefit-fraud enforcement, energy supply, AI governance, and interest-based alliances.

His September interview defends the Iran war as protection for shipping and energy while acknowledging conflict with anti-entanglement promises. The post-midterm source then reports a sharper succession posture: Vance avoids attacking Trump personally but blames the war and the U.S. relationship with Israel, appealing to voters alienated from the administration's foreign policy. This makes him both a governing partner and a candidate for selective inheritance rather than simple continuity.

## Key Characteristics

- Combines economic populism and nationalism with selective openness to trade, skilled immigration, technology, and alliances.
- Treats borders, manufacturing, electricity, and fiscal credibility as linked state-capacity foundations.
- Prefers administrative action where Congress is unlikely to legislate.
- Frames AI governance through national capability and defensive access rather than global approval authority.
- Treats alliances as useful but subordinate to independently defined U.S. interests.
- Positions himself as a restraint-oriented MAGA successor without making a clean personal break from Trump.

## Qualifications

The sources mix reporting, ideological interpretation, forecasts, friendly interviews, and campaign communication. Thiel-network accounts do not reduce Vance to a proxy. The post-midterm positioning is an immediate interpretation, not a declared 2028 platform, and it does not establish whether Vance can separate himself from policies he defended in office or build a broader electoral coalition.
