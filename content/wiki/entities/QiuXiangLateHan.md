## Overview

仇香 / Qiu Xiang is the Hanji 862 local-governance figure who transforms an alleged unfiliality case through moral diagnosis and teaching rather than punishment. His current wiki role centers on [[ChenYuanLateHan|陈元]], [[WangHuanLateHan|王涣]], [[GuoTaiLateHan|郭泰]], and [[HumaneLocalGovernance|仁厚地方治理]].

## Current Profile

The source presents Qiu Xiang as quiet, morally serious, and initially little known in his community. At forty, while serving as Puting chief, he receives a complaint from Chen Yuan's mother accusing Chen Yuan of unfilial conduct. Qiu Xiang does not immediately punish. He recalls the orderliness of Chen Yuan's home and fields, infers that the problem is insufficient teaching rather than bad nature, and persuades the mother not to throw away years of care in a moment of anger.

His next step is direct moral instruction. He visits the home, speaks about human relations, filial conduct, and consequences, and Chen Yuan later becomes known as filial. [[WangHuanLateHan|王涣]], despite favoring stern governance, recognizes this as superior transformation and funds Qiu Xiang's study at [[Taixue|太学]]. [[GuoTaiLateHan|郭泰]] later bows to Qiu Xiang as a teacher, while the episode also presents Qiu Xiang's household discipline as self-directed: when his wife or children err, he removes his cap and blames himself before they apologize.

## Key Characteristics

- Quiet local official whose virtue is not initially visible to the village.
- Diagnostic judge who separates bad conduct from bad nature.
- Non-punitive educator who treats unfiliality as a remediable teaching failure.
- Recognized by Wang Huan as more than an ordinary county subordinate.
- Revered by Guo Tai as a teacher rather than merely a friend.
- Household self-discipline figure whose authority begins with self-correction.
- Source case for [[HumaneLocalGovernance|仁厚地方治理]].
