## Overview

田蚡 / Tian Fen is a Western Han chancellor represented in the current bounded evidence through an alleged conflict between public flood repair and the interests of his own fief during the long-running Huzi breach.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-363-hanwudi-jiandao-shenxian-zhihou-de-chuanshuo-ll1skj3ygm2fop-pz1etkn3qexw5|Hanji 363]] backfills the 132 BCE Huzi breach before the repair ordered under [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] in 109 BCE. The episode says Tian Fen advised against timely intervention because eastward flooding benefited his fief, leaving the Liang-Chu region exposed until the later mass repair.

This makes Tian Fen a bounded case of [[PrivateInterestDisasterNeglect|private-interest disaster neglect]] in the current wiki. The source does not supply his broader political biography or the full technical and fiscal arguments available at court, so the page does not reduce his entire career to this one allegation.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han chancellor in the Huzi flood-control backstory.
- Alleged to have opposed repair while the breach benefited his own fief.
- Illustrates how elite property interest can distort public infrastructure priorities.
- Appears here as a decision-incentive case rather than a full biographical subject.

## Evidence

- Non-repair advice: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-363-hanwudi-jiandao-shenxian-zhihou-de-chuanshuo-ll1skj3ygm2fop-pz1etkn3qexw5|Hanji 363]] says Tian Fen advised Han Wudi not to repair the Huzi breach promptly.
- Private-interest interpretation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-363-hanwudi-jiandao-shenxian-zhihou-de-chuanshuo-ll1skj3ygm2fop-pz1etkn3qexw5|Hanji 363]] attributes that advice to the benefit the eastward flood pattern gave Tian Fen's fief.
- Later contrast: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-363-hanwudi-jiandao-shenxian-zhihou-de-chuanshuo-ll1skj3ygm2fop-pz1etkn3qexw5|Hanji 363]] places a large repair in 109 BCE under [[GuoChangWesternHan|郭昌]] and Ji Ren, followed by diversion works and [[XuanfangPalace|宣房宫]].

## Qualifications

The current profile rests on one compact podcast summary rather than a complete primary-source dossier. It does not establish Tian Fen's full reasoning, the hydrology of his fief, the feasibility of earlier repair, the influence of other officials, or his wider career. The private-interest motive is therefore retained as an episode-attributed allegation, not a complete neutral explanation of the twenty-three-year delay.
