## Current Profile

The source set now gives Yu Dingguo as more than a high-status predecessor. Hanji 417 makes his judicial temperament visible before the later chancellorship: as tingwei, he treats doubtful or weakly evidenced cases with restraint, and the episode says both court and common people take reassurance from that fairness. Hanji 524 says he later replaces Huang Ba as chancellor, is enfeoffed as Xiping Hou, remains respected after Han Yuan's accession, and is allowed to retire with carriage, horses, and gold. Hanji 471 gives one concrete policy judgment before that retirement: Yu sides with Jia Juanzhi's capacity-bound argument that Zhuya and Dan'er should be abandoned under famine and campaign-cost pressure. Hanji 476 places his eventual retirement in a disaster-accountability sequence, where resignation absorbs political responsibility after a famine shock. He dies in his seventies, and Hanji 524 treats that end as relatively fortunate.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han senior official and father of [[YuYongWesternHan|于永]].
- Tingwei whose judicial reputation in Hanji 417 rests on caution, fairness, and leniency under evidentiary doubt.
- Replaces Huang Ba as chancellor in the episode's retrospective.
- Receives the Xiping Hou title and retains respect under Han Yuan.
- Supports Jia Juanzhi's Zhuya-abandonment recommendation against a suppression line.
- Resigns during the Yongguang 1 disaster-accountability sequence and retires with honor gifts.
- Dies in old age, treated by Hanji 524 as a relatively fortunate official end.
