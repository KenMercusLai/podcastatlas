# Kongqiang / 空枪

## Overview
Kongqiang / 《空枪》 is a mainland Chinese film discussed here as a Hong Kong crime-film inheritance work built around a [[ZhangZiqiang|张子强]]-style figure, 1997 Hong Kong, capitalism, mainland police order, and the Hong Kong dream.

## Current Profile
The source treats the film as more than a repeat of older Hong Kong gangster or crime films. It follows a mainlander's fascination with Hong Kong consumer modernity, then reorganizes the material through a later mainland political vocabulary: private violence, capital violence, and state legal order form the central power triangle.

The episode's main critique is historical and ideological. The film is read as [[GreaterBayAreaCinema]] because it uses Hong Kong genre memory from a Greater Bay Area-era standpoint, but that standpoint also produces [[HongKongHandoverTeleology]]: 1997 is no longer an uncertain threshold but a known endpoint where mainland-Hong Kong police cooperation and state order are made to resolve disorder. The film's anti-capital feeling is described as affective and populist rather than structurally left-wing, because it personifies Hong Kong capitalism through a bad real-estate tycoon more than it analyzes land finance or financial capital.

## Key Characteristics
- Reworks Zhang Ziqiang-style crime material through a mainland-centered view of Hong Kong.
- Uses Hong Kong consumer symbols, especially food and work imagery, to stage the protagonist's Hong Kong dream.
- Converts old Hong Kong crime-film uncertainty into a state-order narrative around 1997.
- Criticizes capital through real-estate anger, tycoon villainy, public-money spectacle, and audience affect.
- Shifts from gangster/biopic energy toward police-order logic in the second half.
