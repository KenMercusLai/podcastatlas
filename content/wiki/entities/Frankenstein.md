# Frankenstein / 《弗兰肯斯坦》

## Overview

[[Frankenstein|《弗兰肯斯坦》]] is the Mary Shelley novel the wiki uses as a bridge between Romantic-era science, creator responsibility, experimental ethics, and later AI-cinema anxiety.

## Current Profile

The novel's main wiki branch corrects the public shorthand that "Frankenstein" is the monster's name, then reads the unnamed creature and Victor Frankenstein through [[ScienceFictionContemporaryScienceIntegration]], [[ScientificAnxietyLiteraryMaterial]], [[ModernPrometheusReading]], and [[CreatorResponsibilityEthics]]. Its central claim is that the novel is not "unscientific" simply because its science is not modern. It absorbs the live scientific problems of [[MaryShelley|Mary Shelley / 玛丽·雪莱]]'s time: [[AnimalElectricityDebate]], [[VitalismLifePrincipleDebate]], [[SpontaneousGenerationLifeOrigin]], [[AlchemyChemistryContinuity]], [[CadaverTradeAnatomyFear]], [[EarlyTransplantationImaginary]], and [[ElectricTherapySpeculation]].

The experimental-science branch uses the novel as a literary-science bridge. The fantasy of body animation was not pure invention: public and private electrical experiments on frogs, animal parts, and bodies made galvanic reanimation imaginable in the cultural environment around Shelley. [[ScientificPublicSpectacle]] and early electrical demonstrations turn the gothic premise into a memory of actual experimental culture, while [[ExperimentalScienceEthics]] keeps the question of bodily manipulation and curiosity's limits visible.

The AI-cinema branch extends the novel into later artificial-being stories. The source argues that later films often inherit a [[FrankensteinComplexAI|Frankenstein complex]]: human beings desire artificial life or intelligence, then fear the created being's autonomy, rebellion, replacement, or demand for responsibility. This newer branch also qualifies the inheritance by noting that Mary Shelley's original responsibility problem is often simplified into generic fear of machines or forbidden god-playing.

## Key Characteristics

- The novel functions as a science-fiction origin point because it turns contemporary science into the engine of a creation story.
- Its central moral pressure is creator responsibility, not only creature danger.
- Its scientific imagination is historically grounded in galvanism, vitalism, anatomy, corpse supply, and experimental spectacle.
- The unnamed creature challenges appearance-based moral judgment because social rejection helps produce violence.
- Later AI cinema inherits the creation-anxiety pattern while often shifting blame from creator to created being.

## Evidence

- Science-fiction origin: [[141-zhizao-fulankensitan-kehuan-zhimu-he-tade-shenqi-shidai-857923931]] treats the novel as plausibly modern science fiction because frontier science, not magic alone, drives the creation premise.
- Contemporary science: [[141-zhizao-fulankensitan-kehuan-zhimu-he-tade-shenqi-shidai-857923931]] links the novel to galvanism, vitalism, spontaneous generation, anatomy, electric therapy, and Romantic science culture.
- Experimental bridge: [[68-fengkuang-shiyan-shi-ai-zhe-gaisi-de-qiuzhiyu-664384549]] connects the novel's body-animation imagination to electrical experiments on animals and executed bodies.
- Responsibility and social exclusion: [[141-zhizao-fulankensitan-kehuan-zhimu-he-tade-shenqi-shidai-857923931]] reads the creature's suffering against physiognomic moral inference and creator abandonment.
- AI-cinema inheritance: [[dianying-li-de-ai-qingren-juying-he-ducai-zhe-gkwriuen9djgazhkswsh-d-b]] makes Frankenstein the source pattern for later artificial-being stories in which human creators fear their creations.

## Qualifications

The wiki keeps the novel's "first science fiction" and "scientific for its age" claims source-scoped. The AI-cinema source also warns that later robot and AI stories often transform Shelley's responsibility question into a more general machine-fear pattern.
