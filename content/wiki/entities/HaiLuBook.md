# 《海录》 / Hai Lu

## Overview

《海录》 is an overseas travel account based on [[XieQinggao|谢清高]]'s recollections and published in 1820, shortly before the Opium War.

## Current Profile

The source presents *Hai Lu* as an unusually broad civilian record of the late-eighteenth-century maritime world. It covers location, goods, buildings, dress, ritual, religion, language, Chinese migrant communities, European colonial administration, military installations, opium, urban water supply, and steam navigation. Its importance lies in both what it preserved and what it could not accomplish: detailed observation circulated through oral dictation, print, recommendation, and later compilation, yet did not by itself reorganize Qing public knowledge or state strategy.

The work is neither a Humboldt-style scientific survey nor a transparent transcript of direct experience. It mixes observation, moral judgment, phonetic mediation, secondhand information, and error. Its later incorporation into *Haiguo Tuzhi* nonetheless made it part of a longer Chinese and Japanese effort to understand the overseas world.

## Key Characteristics

- **Orally composed travel record:** converted a blind traveler's memories, and possibly earlier notes, into a substantial printed text.
- **Commercial-maritime scope:** records ports, goods, migration, agriculture, mining, and transregional Chinese communities.
- **Colonial and strategic evidence:** notices British territory, forts, soldiers, firearms, opium production, and European maritime power.
- **Technological observation:** describes London water distribution and reports steamships propelled without rowing.
- **Hybrid reliability:** combines useful firsthand detail with hearsay, mistaken geography, uncertain chronology, and culturally hierarchical interpretation.

## Evidence

### Composition and circulation
- [[lue3zoz87pipdfhtc8pmxj1axuyd-lue3zoz87pipdfhtc8pmxj1axuyd]] says the work was dictated after Xie's blindness, printed in 1820, recommended by Lin Zexu, and later largely incorporated into Wei Yuan's *Haiguo Tuzhi*.

### Maritime Chinese world
- [[lue3zoz87pipdfhtc8pmxj1axuyd-lue3zoz87pipdfhtc8pmxj1axuyd]] describes Chinese settlement and economic activity across Southeast and South Asia, including early records of overseas Chinese polities such as Lanfang.

### Empire, opium, and infrastructure
- [[lue3zoz87pipdfhtc8pmxj1axuyd-lue3zoz87pipdfhtc8pmxj1axuyd]] attributes to the book observations of British colonial reach and military order, Bengal opium cultivation and China-bound supply, Lisbon defenses and quarantine, London waterworks, and American steam navigation.

### Limits of conversion into strategy
- [[lue3zoz87pipdfhtc8pmxj1axuyd-lue3zoz87pipdfhtc8pmxj1axuyd]] uses the work to argue that foreign information existed before the Opium War but did not automatically become a shared explanatory framework or bureaucratic response.

## Qualifications

This profile is based on a podcast summary rather than a critical edition. The episode itself flags uncertainty over travel duration, language ability, the directness of American information, and several ethnographic or technological anecdotes. Later inclusion in *Haiguo Tuzhi* demonstrates circulation, not uniform readership, official acceptance, accurate interpretation, or policy effect in China or Japan.

## What Changed

- Added *Hai Lu* as evidence against a binary “closed China suddenly opens its eyes” narrative.
- Preserved the distinction among observation, circulation, interpretation, and institutional action.
- Marked oral transmission, mediation, and hearsay as part of the work's evidentiary profile.
