# Robert F. Kennedy Jr.

## Overview

Robert F. Kennedy Jr. appears in the bounded sources as a US health-policy official and as the proponent of a disputed reinterpretation of the 1918 influenza pandemic.

## Current Profile

[[bobbie-laura-modi-how-a-baby-formula-startup-took-market-share-from-two-industry-giants-4488842b-74d3-40ed-8776-5831c5934e69]] places Kennedy in a source-scoped 2025 meeting as HHS Secretary. [[LauraModi]] attends with leaders from larger formula companies despite customer criticism, arguing that government regulation of [[InfantFormula]] makes policy engagement necessary and that feeding babies should not become partisan.

[[the-joe-rogan-intervention-the-joe-rogan-intervention|The Joe Rogan Intervention]] examines Kennedy's earlier claim to [[JoeRogan]] that an experimental vaccine, not influenza, caused the 1918 pandemic. [[MalcolmGladwell]] argues that Kennedy mistakes fatal secondary bacterial pneumonia for evidence against the initiating viral infection and traces the Kansas vaccine theory to attorney Kevin Barry and chiropractor Sal Martingano rather than the biomedical literature the episode treats as mainstream.

The sources therefore show a consequential asymmetry: Kennedy participates in formal health-policy authority while also being presented, in an openly critical media source, as using weak source evaluation for a major biomedical claim.

## Key Characteristics

- Appears as HHS Secretary in a regulated infant-formula policy setting.
- Holds public influence over health-policy conversations involving regulated companies.
- Advances a source-disputed theory that a vaccine caused the illnesses attributed to the 1918 influenza pandemic.
- In the critical source, treats a secondary lethal mechanism as evidence against the initiating infection.
- Illustrates why public authority increases the importance of transparent source evaluation.
