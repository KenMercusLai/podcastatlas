# James Clear

## Overview
James Clear is an author and creator represented in the wiki through a [[HubermanLab]] interview on building desired habits, weakening unwanted ones, and designing a life that remains workable across changing seasons.

## Current Profile
[[best-ways-to-build-better-habits-break-bad-ones-james-clear-scim2778708637]] presents Clear as a toolkit-oriented behavior-change writer rather than a proponent of one rigid protocol. His approach joins visible cues, low-friction starts, immediate satisfaction, identity evidence, social norms, and quick recovery after lapses. He also uses his writing career, public criticism, sport, rest, and family modeling to show how habits interact with learning and identity transitions.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames habits as learned solutions to recurring environmental problems.
- Organizes behavior change through cues, attractiveness, ease, and satisfaction rather than motivation alone.
- Treats showing up as a trainable entry behavior that can precede higher performance.
- Uses repeated action as evidence for identity while warning against identities that become too rigid.
- Values public feedback, curiosity, reflection, and recovery from failure as learning mechanisms.
- Adapts routines across creative, entrepreneurial, and family seasons instead of equating consistency with sameness.

## Qualifications
The wiki currently has one structured interview source for Clear. It summarizes his public framework and autobiographical examples but does not independently validate every mechanism, comparative effect, or universal application. The advice should not be substituted for condition-specific care in addiction, compulsive behavior, eating disorders, psychiatric illness, rehabilitation, or medically risky behavior.
