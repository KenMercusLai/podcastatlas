# 韩嵩 / Han Song (late Han)

## Overview
韩嵩 / Han Song (late Han) is the Jingzhou official whose warning that court appointment would reorder his duty leads first to imprisonment under [[LiuBiao|刘表]] and later to release and high office under [[CaoCao|曹操]].

## Current Profile
Hanji 1036-1037 present Han Song as a duty-bound envoy rather than a simple defector. Before leaving for [[Xudu|许都]], he warns Liu Biao that an imperial appointment will make it impossible to remain only Liu Biao's private subordinate. After accepting court office, he returns praising the court-Cao order and urges Liu Biao to send a son to court. Liu Biao treats the foretold conflict as betrayal, finds no proof of treason, and imprisons him anyway.

Hanji 1095 closes that arc after Cao Cao takes Jingzhou. Cao Cao orders Han Song released and appoints him grand herald. The transition retrospectively strengthens Han Song's original claim: the loyalty dispute concerned competing public orders, and regime change turns the imprisoned adviser into a useful court official.

## Key Characteristics
- Treats the recognized Han court's title order as weightier than local patronage.
- Warns before departure that court appointment will change his formal obligations.
- Uses “圣达节，次守节” to frame his choice as fixed duty rather than opportunism.
- Presses Liu Biao toward visible court alignment after returning from Xudu.
- Survives an execution threat but is imprisoned despite the absence of proven treason.
- Is released and appointed grand herald after Cao Cao takes Jingzhou.
