# Corps of Colonial Marines

## Overview

The Corps of Colonial Marines appears in the wiki as the British-organized War of 1812 unit of escaped enslaved men that complicates [[StarSpangledBanner|The Star-Spangled Banner]]'s freedom language.

## Current Profile

The episode presents the corps through Chesapeake strategy and American slaveholder fear. British commanders welcomed runaways, used escaped enslaved people for local intelligence, armed some men, gave them red uniforms, and organized them into a fighting force. This makes the anthem's third-verse controversy harder to dismiss, because enslaved African Americans were plausibly among the people Key and other slaveholders imagined as dangerous British collaborators.

## Key Characteristics

- British-organized military unit in the [[WarOf1812]].
- Made up of escaped enslaved men who reached British protection.
- Connected to Chesapeake intelligence, guidance, and local military operations.
- Symbolically threatening to white American slaveholders because armed Black men fought with Britain.
- Central context for the "hireling and slave" dispute in [[StarSpangledBanner|The Star-Spangled Banner]].
- Postwar freedom case because the source says Britain did not return thousands of escaped enslaved people.

## Evidence

- Recruitment and use: [[677-usa-the-star-spangled-banner-part-1-glt7890467734]] says Admiral George Coburn promised runaways welcome and non-return, and British forces relied on escaped enslaved people for local intelligence.
- Military organization: [[677-usa-the-star-spangled-banner-part-1-glt7890467734]] says male runaways were armed, uniformed, and organized into the Corps of Colonial Marines.
- Slaveholder alarm: [[677-usa-the-star-spangled-banner-part-1-glt7890467734]] says white American slaveholders were alarmed by armed Black men fighting with Britain.
- Anthem relevance: [[677-usa-the-star-spangled-banner-part-1-glt7890467734]] treats this context as a reason the third verse may have been at least partly aimed at enslaved people who fled to the British.
- Afterlife: [[677-usa-the-star-spangled-banner-part-1-glt7890467734]] says many escaped enslaved people went to Canada and some Colonial Marines settled in Trinidad.

## Qualifications

The source does not prove that Key had only the Colonial Marines in mind when writing the disputed line. The corps is evidence for plausibility and context, not a complete interpretation of the anthem.
