# Senegal

## Overview

Senegal appears in the bounded sources through two distinct branches: contemporary radio capacity and the colonial and commemorative history of [[Goree|Gorée Island]].

## Current Profile

The media branch presents Senegal alongside [[Kenya]] and [[Ghana]] as a country trialing [[DABRadioCapacityExpansion|DAB radio]] to expand crowded broadcast capacity while preserving low-data access.

The historical-memory branch follows Gorée through European colonial competition, Franco-African trading networks, commercial decline, preserved architecture, independence in 1960, and the rise of the [[HouseOfSlavesGoree|House of Slaves]] as a global slavery memorial. It distinguishes Senegal's real entanglement with Atlantic commerce and slavery from unsupported claims that one museum building was a mass embarkation depot.

## Key Characteristics

- West African country represented by both contemporary media infrastructure and colonial public history.
- Participant in a DAB radio trial intended to expand station capacity.
- National setting for Gorée's layered Portuguese, Dutch, English, and French colonial history.
- Post-independence home of a major but historically contested Atlantic-slavery pilgrimage site.

## Evidence

- Radio capacity: [[in-arms-way-gaza-deal-sticking-points-6a71b49647235c354d3c9b74]] groups Senegal with Kenya and Ghana in DAB trials responding to crowded FM bands.
- Gorée and colonial history: [[272-senegal-the-door-of-no-return-glt8611353465]] describes the island's trading role, European rule, signare networks, French authority, and movement of commerce to the mainland.
- Independence and memory: [[272-senegal-the-door-of-no-return-glt8611353465]] links post-1960 museum development and UNESCO-era tourism to African American pilgrimage while challenging the House of Slaves' promoted export claims.

## Qualifications

Neither source is a general national history. The radio episode gives no broad account of Senegal's media system or DAB outcomes, while the Gorée episode centers an island and an international memory dispute rather than Senegalese society as a whole. Colonial chronology, export estimates, and museum claims remain source-scoped.

- Distinguished Senegal and Gorée's documented historical roles from disputed mass-embarkation claims.
