# Montagnards / The Mountain

## Overview

The Montagnards, or the Mountain, were the radical, Paris-centered camp in the National Convention opposed to the [[Girondins]].

## Current Profile

The episode presents the Mountain as a flexible factional camp rather than a modern party. It drew power from Paris, the Jacobin Club, popular mobilization, and a greater willingness to justify revolutionary violence, but [[MaximilienRobespierre|Robespierre]], [[GeorgesDanton|Danton]], and [[JeanPaulMarat|Marat]] remained distinct figures rather than a coordinated triumvirate.

## Key Characteristics

- Its deputies sat high in the Convention and were associated strongly with Paris.
- It was more accepting than the Girondins of popular pressure and revolutionary violence.
- It held growing influence in the Jacobin Club.
- Its leading personalities differed in temperament, method, and political relationship.
- Girondin attacks on Marat and Robespierre failed to remove them and strengthened the camp.

## Evidence

### Factional position

- [[546-the-french-revolution-the-monarchy-falls-part-3-glt3779628370]] distinguishes the Mountain from the provincial Girondins and the shifting Plain or Marsh.

### Leadership without unity

- [[546-the-french-revolution-the-monarchy-falls-part-3-glt3779628370]] names Robespierre, Danton, and Marat while rejecting the idea that they functioned as one unified triumvirate.

### Conflict and violence

- [[546-the-french-revolution-the-monarchy-falls-part-3-glt3779628370]] links the camp to acceptance of revolutionary violence and to successful resistance against Girondin denunciation.

## Qualifications

- “Montagnard” identifies a changing alignment rather than disciplined party membership.
- Parisian grounding does not mean every Montagnard held the same view of violence, policy, or leadership.
- The episode covers the opening Convention conflict rather than the faction's full later history.

## What Changed

- A canonical profile now separates the Mountain as a political camp from the individual roles of Robespierre, Danton, and Marat.

## Relationships

- [[Girondins]] - principal Convention rivals during the factional struggle.
- [[NationalConvention]] - institutional arena in which the camp formed and fought.
- [[JacobinClub]] - political club increasingly dominated by Montagnard influence.
- [[MaximilienRobespierre]] - leading figure who defeated a Girondin accusation of dictatorship.
- [[RevolutionaryFactionalOutflanking]] - dynamic that strengthened its position against moderates.
