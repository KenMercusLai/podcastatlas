# IREN

## Overview
IREN is an energy and data-center operator that used Bitcoin mining to bootstrap power infrastructure before shifting part of its capacity toward AI compute.

## Current Profile
In the source, IREN's advantage is not a proprietary model but control over the physical path from land and grid connection to commissioned compute. Its CEO says the company develops sites, secures permits and power, builds data centers, and is replacing some Bitcoin-mining equipment with AI chips. The emerging constraint is "time to compute": available megawatts become valuable only after labor, cooling, foundations, equipment, networking, and commissioning are complete.

## Key Characteristics
- Develops power-connected data-center sites rather than only leasing finished cloud capacity.
- Uses Bitcoin mining as an earlier cash-flow and infrastructure-bootstrap workload while expanding into AI compute.
- Treats construction throughput, skilled labor, cooling, equipment, and network latency as first-order AI constraints.
- Locates capacity near hydro, wind, solar, and existing heavy electrical infrastructure.
- Depends on large customer contracts, grid reliability, fast buildout, and sustained compute demand.

## Qualifications
The capacity, contract, renewable-energy, wage, latency, utilization, and demand figures are company claims in a founder-led interview. Available power is not equivalent to completed compute, and grid connection does not eliminate local cost, water, land, transmission, reliability, or lifecycle-emissions questions. Bitcoin and AI workloads also carry different revenue and hardware risks.

## What Changed
- Created IREN as an energy-to-compute infrastructure company profile.
- Distinguished power ownership from the labor and construction path required to commission AI capacity.
- Preserved the company's renewable-energy and demand claims as source-scoped rather than independently verified facts.
