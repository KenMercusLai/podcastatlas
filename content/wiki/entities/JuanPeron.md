# Juan Perón

## Overview

Juan Domingo Perón was the Argentine president and movement leader whose balancing of labor mobilization, military power, and [[EvaPeron|Evita]]'s charisma preceded overthrow, exile, and a divided return.

## Current Profile

In 1951-52, Perón relied on Evita as an emotional bridge to workers and popular supporters while balancing the CGT's vice-presidential campaign against military opposition. The source depicts his hesitation as politically useful but leaves his private intentions uncertain. It also reports skepticism toward doctors and a plan to preserve Evita permanently, while stopping short of proving that he caused her treatment resistance.

After Evita's death, Perón was politically less flexible and emotionally isolated. Economic strain, escalating opposition violence, and church conflict preceded his 1955 overthrow. Exile preserved him as the distant center of [[Peronism]], but his 1973 return reunited the movement electorally without resolving armed conflict between left and right.

## Key Characteristics

- He balanced mass-union demands against an army capable of overthrowing him.
- His authority depended partly on Evita's emotional connection to Peronist constituencies.
- He allowed the vice-presidential nomination struggle to unfold without an immediate public decision.
- He intended Evita's preserved body to remain a permanent political and commemorative presence.
- Exile preserved his symbolic centrality while increasing the importance of intermediaries.
- His return restored electoral power without resolving Peronism's factional violence.

## Qualifications

These episodes focus on the crisis around Evita's death and the later history of her body, not a comprehensive evaluation of Perón's governments. His responsibility for treatment delay, precise motives during the nomination struggle, emotional reaction to Evita's death, and private relationships remain source-attributed rather than settled conclusions.

## Relationships

- [[EvaPeron]] - wife and political partner whose living authority and death reshaped his regime.
- [[PedroAra]] - specialist commissioned for Evita's permanent preservation.
- [[IsabelPeron]] - third wife, exile intermediary, vice-president, and constitutional successor.
- [[JoseLopezRega]] - household intimate and minister whose influence grew during Perón's final years.
- [[Peronism]] - movement Perón led through mass mobilization, proscription, exile, and return.
- [[StateOrchestratedMassMourning]] - funeral system organized under his government after Evita's death.
- [[Argentina]] - country he governed before and after exile.
