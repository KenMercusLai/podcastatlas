# Juan Perón

## Overview

Juan Domingo Perón is the Argentine president and movement leader whose loss of [[EvaPeron|Eva Perón]], 1955 overthrow, exile, and 1973 return structure the episode's political chronology.

## Current Profile

The source portrays Perón after Evita's death as politically less flexible and emotionally isolated. Economic strain, escalating opposition violence, and conflict with the Catholic Church preceded his overthrow; exile then transformed him into the distant center of a banned but persistent [[Peronism]].

In Madrid, [[IsabelPeron|Isabel Perón]] and [[JoseLopezRega|José López Rega]] became increasingly important intermediaries. Perón returned to office in 1973 at the head of a mass movement already divided between left and right, died in 1974, and left Isabel to inherit a violent and unstable political order.

## Key Characteristics

- His political authority depended partly on Evita's emotional connection to Peronist constituencies.
- His fall followed economic pressure, political violence, church conflict, and military revolt.
- Exile preserved his symbolic centrality while forcing him to govern the movement through intermediaries.
- His return reunified Peronism electorally without resolving its armed factional conflict.
- His late household gave Isabel and López Rega unusual access to political power.

## Evidence

- Weakening and overthrow: [[498-evita-the-mystery-of-the-missing-body-part-5-glt5587823308]] links Evita's absence, economic crisis, violence, church conflict, and the 1955 coup.
- Exile politics: [[498-evita-the-mystery-of-the-missing-body-part-5-glt5587823308]] describes Perón using Isabel as a messenger to underground Peronists while López Rega entered the Madrid household.
- Return and succession: [[498-evita-the-mystery-of-the-missing-body-part-5-glt5587823308]] follows the 1973 election sequence, violence at Ezeiza, Perón's landslide victory, illness, death, and Isabel's accession.

## Qualifications

This is an episode-bounded profile of Perón's post-1952 decline, exile, and return, not a comprehensive account of his earlier rule or Peronist policy. The episode's allegation of a sexual relationship with the teenage Nelly Rivas and its reconstructions of private motives remain source-attributed and are not used as defining claims here.

## Relationships
