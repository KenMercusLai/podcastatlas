# Free Companies in the Hundred Years' War

## Overview

The free companies are mobile groups of battle-hardened soldiers who continue organized predation between campaigns, making nominal peace and military demobilization into sources of insecurity.

## Current Profile

The episode presents the companies as an afterlife of campaigning rather than a separate problem. Many fighters had English-war connections, but once outside disciplined royal armies they ravage France and threaten Avignon strongly enough that the pope pays them off.

Their persistence weakens settlements because territory already damaged by war must support rulers, ransoms, courts, and garrisons while armed labor remains difficult to control. They therefore connect battlefield mobilization to rural disorder and the fiscal fragility of [[Aquitaine]].

## Key Characteristics

- Groups of experienced soldiers operating beyond stable royal control.
- Predatory force in damaged French territory.
- Military labor problem persisting across truce and settlement.
- Threat capable of extracting payment from the papacy at Avignon.
- Constraint on the governance of enlarged English Aquitaine.

## Evidence

- Composition and violence: [[321-hundred-years-war-a-storm-of-swords-part-4-glt1570526345]] describes battle-hardened soldiers, many connected to English campaigns, ravaging France.
- Avignon threat: [[321-hundred-years-war-a-storm-of-swords-part-4-glt1570526345]] says the pope pays them to withdraw.
- Aquitanian instability: [[321-hundred-years-war-a-storm-of-swords-part-4-glt1570526345]] keeps company violence present after Brétigny.

## Qualifications

The source does not distinguish individual companies, contracts, leaders, routes, or changing political affiliations. English connections should not be read as proof that all companies were English state instruments.

## What Changed

- Created the free-company profile as the coercive residue of mobilization and failed demobilization.
