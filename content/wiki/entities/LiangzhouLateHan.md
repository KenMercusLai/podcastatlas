# 凉州 / Liangzhou (late Han)

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-897-zhichang-bidu-shenme-cai-jiao-lingdaoli-lvrrmksykazlsb7w8abbdmnqfzqq|Hanji 897]] adds an earlier office-capability warning. [[MengTuoLateHan|孟陀]] becomes Liangzhou inspector through manufactured access to [[ZhangRangLateHan|张让]], but his later failure at [[ShuleKingdom|疏勒]] shows Liangzhou as a military office whose responsibilities can expose appointment-channel corruption.

凉州 / Liangzhou (late Han) enters through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-934-sha-yiren-ze-tianxia-an-ni-sha-busha-ltar8hvdkqct8oizm3k22z38b1v8|Hanji 934]] as the frontier region at the center of [[CuiLieLateHan|崔烈]] and [[FuXie|傅燮]]'s court dispute. Cui Lie treats repeated rebellion and rising military taxation as reasons to abandon the province; Fu Xie treats the same disorder as proof that the court must repair control rather than surrender the western shield.

The source's strategic language makes Liangzhou more than a local trouble spot. Fu Xie calls it a key traffic corridor and national barrier, then ties it to earlier Han frontier policy: [[LiuBang|刘邦]]'s post-founding western arrangements, [[LiShang|郦商]] in Longyou, and [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s Hexi expansion through Wuwei, Zhangye, Jiuquan, and Dunhuang. In this reading, abandoning Liangzhou would give enemy forces horses, space, and a future base from which to threaten the wider realm.

This page is distinct from [[LiangzhouWarlordClique|凉州系军阀]], the later Dong Zhuo / Li Jue / Guo Si military bloc. Hanji 934 concerns the province as a strategic frontier before that later warlord clique controls Chang'an.
