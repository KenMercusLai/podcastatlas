## Overview

凉州 / Liangzhou (late Han) appears in the current wiki as a strategic northwestern frontier province, an office whose military responsibilities expose appointment quality, and a repeated object of abandonment debates. Hanji 784 now supplies the earlier An-era anti-abandonment case through [[YuXuEasternHan|虞诩]], while Hanji 934 supplies the later Ling-era case through [[FuXie|傅燮]] against [[CuiLieLateHan|崔烈]].

## Current Profile

Hanji 784 gives the earliest current Liangzhou layer. [[PangCanLateHan|Pang Can]] proposes abandoning the province, and [[DengZhiLateHan|Deng Zhi]] initially accepts the argument. Yu Xu rejects the policy because abandoning Liangzhou would expose the Sanfu / [[GuanzhongRegion|Guanzhong]] region, [[ChangAnHanCapital|Chang'an]], and imperial tombs, while turning loyal western populations into resentful subjects and weakening the Han court's ability to contain Qiang and Hu pressure. His proposed countermeasure is to bring Liangzhou elite and official sons into capital offices, combining reward with hostage-like restraint.

Hanji 897 adds Liangzhou as an office-capability test. [[MengTuoLateHan|Meng Tuo]] becomes Liangzhou inspector after manufacturing a public access signal through [[ZhangRangLateHan|Zhang Rang]]'s household servant, but his later failure at [[ShuleKingdom|Shule]] shows that access-brokered officeholding does not supply the military competence required by the frontier post.

Hanji 934 returns to Liangzhou as a strategic frontier under later and harsher conditions. Cui Lie treats repeated rebellion and rising military taxation as reasons to abandon the province; Fu Xie treats the same disorder as proof that the court must repair control rather than surrender the western shield. Fu Xie calls Liangzhou a key corridor and national barrier, invokes earlier Han frontier policy, and warns that abandoning it would give enemy forces horses, space, and a future base from which to threaten the wider realm.

This page is distinct from [[LiangzhouWarlordClique|凉州系军阀]], the later Dong Zhuo / Li Jue / Guo Si military bloc. The current Liangzhou page concerns the province as a strategic frontier and appointment arena before that later warlord clique controls Chang'an.

## Key Characteristics

- Northwestern frontier province whose value is repeatedly argued through shield, corridor, horse, population, and core-region protection.
- Abandonment target when rebellion, military spending, and tax pressure make the frontier look too expensive.
- Political-symbol buffer because loss of Liangzhou can expose Chang'an, Guanzhong, and imperial tombs.
- Local elite and population field whose loyalty cannot be assumed if the court abandons or coerces them.
- Inspector office whose responsibilities can expose the difference between access-made appointment and actual command capacity.
- Distinct regional page from the later Liangzhou warlord clique.

## Qualifications

This page remains bounded to three source notes and should not be read as a complete administrative history of Liangzhou. Hanji 784 and Hanji 934 both concern abandonment debates but are separated by decades and by different court conditions. Hanji 897 concerns the Liangzhou inspector office and a later Western Regions command failure, not a province-wide Liangzhou crisis narrative.
