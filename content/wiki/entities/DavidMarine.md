# David Marine

## Overview

David Marine was a physician and thyroid researcher whose school experiment in [[AkronOhio|Akron, Ohio]] is presented by [[way-to-go-ohio-way-to-go-ohio|Way to Go, Ohio]] as decisive evidence that carefully dosed iodine could prevent goiter.

## Current Profile

The episode traces Marine's interest to thyroid enlargement among people and animals in the Cleveland area. His 1909 hatchery observation connected goiter in trout to iodine-poor feed and its disappearance to iodine-rich sea fish, after which he spent roughly a decade building laboratory evidence.

In 1917, Cleveland rejected his proposal to administer iodine to schoolgirls, at a time when high-dose experiments and poison warnings made institutional suspicion understandable. Akron approved the study. The reported prevention result helped establish the medical premise behind later [[IodizedSaltPopulationPrevention]], although the episode assigns the salt-industry campaign itself to David Cowie and participating manufacturers rather than to Marine alone.

## Key Characteristics

- Used animal observation and laboratory work to develop an iodine-deficiency account of thyroid enlargement.
- Treated dose, timing, duration, and delivery as empirical questions after excessive iodine had caused harm.
- Sought a controlled school-based prevention experiment after years of preliminary work.
- Faced understandable resistance because iodine was widely regarded as dangerous and the proposed participants were schoolgirls.
- Became associated with a reported Akron result that helped move iodine prevention toward population implementation.

## Evidence

- Research path: [[way-to-go-ohio-way-to-go-ohio]] describes Marine's Cleveland-area observations, the trout-feed comparison, and about a decade of laboratory work.
- Experimental design: [[way-to-go-ohio-way-to-go-ohio]] says Marine proposed giving iodine to schoolgirls in fifth grade and above to test prevention under specified administration instructions.
- Institutional response: [[way-to-go-ohio-way-to-go-ohio]] contrasts Cleveland's refusal with Akron's approval and notes the contemporary fear created by prior excessive dosing.
- Public-health significance: [[way-to-go-ohio-way-to-go-ohio]] presents the Akron result as definitive prevention evidence and an antecedent to broad salt iodization.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to one structured podcast summary rather than Marine's publications, the original school records, participant-level results, or a full history of iodine science. The source's “definitive” and breakthrough language is the episode's assessment. It does not settle the experiment's consent standards by present-day norms, the precise role of institutional relationships in approval, or how credit should be distributed among Marine, Cowie, manufacturers, schools, and other researchers.

## What Changed

- Created the profile around the experimental bridge from iodine-deficiency research to school-based prevention evidence.
- Preserved the distinction between Marine's prevention experiment and the later salt-industry implementation campaign.
