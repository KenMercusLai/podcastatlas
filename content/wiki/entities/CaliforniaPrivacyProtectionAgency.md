# California Privacy Protection Agency

## Overview
The California Privacy Protection Agency, referenced in the source as CalPrivacy, is the state privacy regulator whose enforcement capacity shapes whether California's data-broker deletion rights become practical.

## Current Profile
In this episode, CalPrivacy is not profiled institutionally; it is the enforcement bottleneck for [[CaliforniaDeleteAct]] implementation. The agency has conducted data-broker sweeps and fined brokers that failed to register, but [[JenniferKing]] says the specific friction and reporting practices highlighted by the Stanford report have not yet been publicly investigated.

## Key Characteristics
- Serves as California's privacy-law enforcement actor in the source.
- Has devoted resources to data-broker sweeps.
- Has fined brokers for failing to register at all.
- Faces scale limits if consumers must rely only on agency complaints.
- Is positioned beside possible private lawsuits as one part of a broader enforcement model.

## Evidence
- Enforcement role: [[tech-20260825-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260825-mp-tech-pod-128]] says consumers currently complain directly to CalPrivacy when they believe a company has not honored data rights.
- Broker sweeps: [[tech-20260825-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260825-mp-tech-pod-128]] says the agency has devoted significant resources to data-broker sweeps.
- Registration fines: [[tech-20260825-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260825-mp-tech-pod-128]] says the agency recently fined two brokers that failed to register.
- Enforcement gap: [[tech-20260825-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260825-mp-tech-pod-128]] says King has not seen investigations into the specific practices described in the Stanford report.
