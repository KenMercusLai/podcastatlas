# Methuen Treaty

## Overview

The Methuen Treaty was a 1703 Anglo-Portuguese agreement represented here as both a wartime realignment and a trade arrangement favoring English cloth and Portuguese wine.

## Current Profile

[[12-days-port-wine-and-darwin-sets-sail-glt6423200478]] situates the treaty during the War of the Spanish Succession, when conflict reduced English reliance on French wine and Portugal shifted away from France. Negotiated by [[JohnMethuen]], the agreement joined alliance politics to reciprocal market access: English wool and cloth entered Portugal, while Portuguese wine received a duty advantage over French wine in England.

The treaty matters in the source less as a legal text than as market infrastructure. Alongside earlier merchant contact, fortification, shipping, and English port houses, it helped turn [[PortWine]] into an eighteenth-century British social institution.

## Key Characteristics

- Concluded on 27 December 1703 during the War of the Spanish Succession.
- Joined Portuguese diplomatic realignment to bilateral commercial privileges.
- Favored English wool and cloth exports to Portugal.
- Gave Portuguese wine a tariff advantage over French wine in England.
- Helped redirect British consumption and support the port trade.

## Evidence

- War and alignment - [[12-days-port-wine-and-darwin-sets-sail-glt6423200478]] places the agreement within the struggle against France and Portugal's change of side.
- Trade terms - [[12-days-port-wine-and-darwin-sets-sail-glt6423200478]] describes the cloth-for-wine structure and preferential wine duty.
- Cultural consequence - [[12-days-port-wine-and-darwin-sets-sail-glt6423200478]] links the treaty regime to port houses, import growth, clubs, and heavy-drinking prestige.

## Qualifications

The source is a brief popular-history treatment. It does not reproduce the treaty text, present Portuguese negotiators in depth, isolate the agreement's effect from war and prior commerce, or test the stated duty and import figures. The claim that the treaty made port culturally British is therefore a market-history synthesis rather than a single-cause finding.

## What Changed

- Created the treaty as a distinct link between alliance policy, tariff design, and drinking culture.
