# Willie Whitelaw

## Overview
Willie Whitelaw appears as a British Conservative crisis politician: first as the minister sent to [[NorthernIreland]] after [[Stormont]] was suspended in March 1972, and later as the establishment alternative defeated by [[MargaretThatcher|Margaret Thatcher]] in the 1975 Conservative leadership contest.

## Current Profile
[[700-the-troubles-bloody-sunday-part-4-glt3909829334]] portrays Whitelaw as shrewd, emotionally forceful, and facing an almost impossible brief after [[EdwardHeath]] suspends Stormont. He engages in early back-channel and London talks with the IRA, but the opening fails because Provisional IRA demands for withdrawal and constitutional change are far beyond what the British side will accept. [[662-britain-in-the-70s-the-rise-of-thatcher-part-1-glt1785737730]] adds his Conservative Party role: after Heath lost the first ballot, Whitelaw entered as the experienced, aristocratic, affable establishment alternative and lost heavily to Thatcher on 11 February 1975.

## Key Characteristics
- British minister associated with the March 1972 shift from Stormont rule to direct rule.
- Crisis manager entering office after [[BloodySunday]] and escalating bombings.
- Negotiator in early contacts with IRA representatives during the June-July 1972 ceasefire.
- Figure used by the episode to show that political contact could coexist with continued violence.
- Example of Westminster inheriting responsibility after devolved Northern Ireland governance failed.
- Establishment Conservative alternative to Thatcher after Heath's first-ballot defeat.
- Representative of the older party style Thatcher overcame in the 1975 second ballot.

## Evidence
- Direct-rule appointment: [[700-the-troubles-bloody-sunday-part-4-glt3909829334]] says Heath announced Stormont's suspension on 24 March 1972 and sent Whitelaw to Belfast as secretary of state for Northern Ireland.
- Brief characterization: [[700-the-troubles-bloody-sunday-part-4-glt3909829334]] portrays Whitelaw as shrewd and emotionally forceful while facing an almost impossible assignment.
- Early contacts: [[700-the-troubles-bloody-sunday-part-4-glt3909829334]] says British officials met IRA representatives near Derry before an IRA delegation flew secretly to London to meet Whitelaw.
- Failed talks: [[700-the-troubles-bloody-sunday-part-4-glt3909829334]] reports that IRA demands included British withdrawal by 1975 and an all-Ireland referendum, which the British side would not accept.
- Leadership contest: [[662-britain-in-the-70s-the-rise-of-thatcher-part-1-glt1785737730]] says Whitelaw entered the second ballot as the obvious establishment alternative and lost to Thatcher 146 to 79.

## Qualifications
This is not a full Whitelaw biography. The current profile is restricted to his role in the immediate post-Stormont, post-Bloody-Sunday phase of the Troubles and his second-ballot role in the 1975 Conservative leadership contest.
