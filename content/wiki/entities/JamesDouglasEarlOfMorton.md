# James Douglas / Earl of Morton

## Overview

James Douglas, Earl of Morton, is the Scottish magnate whose betrayal by [[HenryStuartLordDarnley|Darnley]] supplied a personal motive for joining the conspiracy that killed him.

## Current Profile

Morton participated in David Rizzio's murder and went into exile after Darnley abandoned the conspirators. Pardoned by [[MaryQueenOfScots|Mary]] on Christmas Eve 1566, he returned to Scotland in January 1567 and met [[WilliamMaitlandOfLethington|Maitland]] and [[JamesHepburnEarlOfBothwell|Bothwell]] at Whittingham. The episode treats this reconciliation of former enemies as the point at which vengeance and elite security converged into a murder plan.

Morton's men are presented as helping prepare gunpowder beneath Darnley's lodging, while Morton's council position later helped suppress or obscure evidence. Allowing Bothwell to absorb public blame also protected Morton and Maitland from equivalent scrutiny.

## Key Characteristics

- Rizzio conspirator exiled after Darnley's betrayal.
- Returned magnate with a strong revenge motive.
- Former enemy of Bothwell who joined him against Darnley.
- Likely organizer whose retainers helped prepare the gunpowder plot.
- Privy councillor who benefited from a compromised investigation and concentrated blame on Bothwell.

## Evidence

- Motive and return: [[588-mary-queen-of-scots-the-mystery-of-the-exploding-mansion-part-5-glt2383249995]] links Darnley's betrayal, Mary's pardon, and Morton's January return.
- Coalition: [[588-mary-queen-of-scots-the-mystery-of-the-exploding-mansion-part-5-glt2383249995]] places Morton with Bothwell and Maitland at Whittingham.
- Execution and concealment: [[588-mary-queen-of-scots-the-mystery-of-the-exploding-mansion-part-5-glt2383249995]] attributes cellar preparation to his men and shows implicated councillors controlling the aftermath.

## Qualifications

The episode's conclusion rests on reconstructed intelligence, confessions, and testimony rather than an uncompromised contemporary trial. The exact division of labor among Morton, Bothwell, Maitland, their retainers, and men outside the lodging remains source-scoped.
