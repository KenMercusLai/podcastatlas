# James Douglas / Earl of Morton

## Overview

James Douglas, Earl of Morton, is the Scottish magnate whose betrayal by [[HenryStuartLordDarnley|Darnley]] supplied a personal motive for joining the conspiracy that killed him.

## Current Profile

Morton organized the armed plot to kill [[DavidRizzio]] after [[HenryStuartLordDarnley|Darnley]] agreed to restore the exiled Protestant lords in return for coronation and political support. Men under Morton's leadership entered Mary's Holyrood rooms, helped seize Rizzio, and turned murder into an attempted coup. Darnley's later denial and signed bond exposed the coalition, while [[MaryQueenOfScots|Mary]]'s escape left Morton outlawed and in English exile.

Pardoned by Mary on Christmas Eve 1566, Morton returned to Scotland in January 1567 and met [[WilliamMaitlandOfLethington|Maitland]] and [[JamesHepburnEarlOfBothwell|Bothwell]] at Whittingham. The later episode treats this reconciliation of former enemies as the point at which vengeance and elite security converged into a murder plan against Darnley.

Morton's men are presented as helping prepare gunpowder beneath Darnley's lodging, while Morton's council position later helped suppress or obscure evidence. Allowing Bothwell to absorb public blame also protected Morton and Maitland from equivalent scrutiny.

## Key Characteristics

- Organizer of the Rizzio murder and coup, exiled after Darnley's betrayal.
- Returned magnate with a strong revenge motive.
- Former enemy of Bothwell who joined him against Darnley.
- Likely organizer whose retainers helped prepare the gunpowder plot.
- Privy councillor who benefited from a compromised investigation and concentrated blame on Bothwell.

## Qualifications

The Rizzio source clearly identifies Morton as an organizer but leaves exact actions, dialogue, English knowledge, and responsibility for each wound source-scoped. The Darnley-murder conclusion rests on reconstructed intelligence, confessions, and testimony rather than an uncompromised contemporary trial. The exact division of labor among Morton, Bothwell, Maitland, their retainers, and men outside the lodging remains uncertain.

## What Changed
