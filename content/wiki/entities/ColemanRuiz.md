# Coleman Ruiz

## Overview

Coleman Ruiz is a former Navy SEAL and Tier 1 special operator who describes his path from New Orleans, wrestling, and the Naval Academy through combat service, repeated loss, civilian transition, severe depression, and recovery.

## Current Profile

[[overcoming-physical-emotional-challenges-coleman-ruiz-scim6632700547]] presents Ruiz less as a catalogue of military achievements than as a case in how adaptive traits can change function across a life. Physical intensity, fear-driven effort, team loyalty, improvisation, and toughness helped him enter and operate inside demanding institutions. After service, the same narrow operating style could not by itself process grief, rebuild ordinary identity, or protect him from acute psychological danger. His later model of “range” keeps discipline and protective capacity while adding vulnerability, kindness, professional help, and reliance on trusted people.

## Key Characteristics

- He channels physical intensity through structured discipline, first in wrestling and later in military selection and service.
- He understands team belonging as both a source of purpose and a reason separation from the military felt identity-threatening.
- He describes repeated combat loss and family notification as cumulative burdens that remained partly unprocessed while the operational tempo continued.
- He uses [[HeroReturnWithBoon]] to interpret the difficulty of returning from an extraordinary service world to ordinary civilian life.
- He treats disclosure, therapy, prescribed medication, sobriety, sleep, movement, family, and friendship as complementary parts of recovery.
- His mature toughness model values range: protection and endurance alongside gentleness, openness, and help-seeking.

## Evidence

- Discipline and belonging: [[overcoming-physical-emotional-challenges-coleman-ruiz-scim6632700547]] connects wrestling with improved behavior and grades, and later connects SEAL persistence with team identity after family disruption.
- Loss and transition: [[overcoming-physical-emotional-challenges-coleman-ruiz-scim6632700547]] records Ruiz's accounts of deaths, memorials, casualty notifications, memory gaps, turning in his gear, loss of access, and difficulty managing ordinary civilian life.
- Crisis and recovery: [[overcoming-physical-emotional-challenges-coleman-ruiz-scim6632700547]] describes severe depression, a period of suicide risk, friend and family intervention, therapy, short-term prescribed Wellbutrin, stopping alcohol, sleep stabilization, and exercise.
- Return framework and range: [[overcoming-physical-emotional-challenges-coleman-ruiz-scim6632700547]] has Ruiz apply Campbell's return stages to his transition and redefine toughness as a wider repertoire rather than permanent control.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to one long-form interview and Ruiz's retrospective self-report. It does not independently verify operational history, casualty counts, other people's motives, clinical diagnoses, treatment effects, or causal explanations. His experience with ibogaine and 5-MeO-DMT is not evidence of general safety or efficacy; the episode itself stresses preparation, screening, support, legality, and professional care.

## What Changed

- Created a bounded profile connecting Ruiz's elite performance history to delayed grief recognition, civilian return, and recovery.
- Distinguished his range-based model of toughness from simple endurance or combat readiness.
