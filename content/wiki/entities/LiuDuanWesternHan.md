## Overview

刘端 / Liu Duan was a son of [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]] and the Western Han king of Jiaoxi. The current source profile treats him as a case in which dynastic kinship protected a ruler whose private violence, obstruction of taxation, and hostility toward centrally appointed officials damaged local government.

## Current Profile

Liu Duan's importance in the current wiki is institutional rather than sensational. Both episodes record a bodily illness and sexual disorder inside his household, but the durable judgment concerns power: favored palace attendants and their families can be killed, officials enforcing Han law can be framed or poisoned, and tax collection can be suspended because the king retains room to resist ordinary administration.

[[HanWudi|汉武帝]] repeatedly refuses demands to execute Liu Duan. The episode attributes that restraint to brotherly feeling and to Liu Duan's lack of an evident usurpation threat, creating a sharp contrast between low central-regime risk and high local human cost.

The danger is visible in [[DongZhongshu|董仲舒]]'s appointment as Jiaoxi chancellor. Liu Duan respects Dong's scholarly reputation, yet Dong still leaves by claiming illness because personal favor does not change the post's structural exposure.

Hanji 237 adds the end of the territorial line: Liu Duan dies after a reported forty-seven-year reign without a son to inherit, and Jiaoxi is abolished. The kingdom's eventual reabsorption does not erase the long period in which court restraint shifted risk onto household members and appointed administrators.

## Key Characteristics

- Protected imperial relative whose kinship reduces the likelihood of capital punishment.
- Violent household ruler whose private grievance extends to attendants and relatives.
- Obstructor of centrally supervised administration, taxation, and official accountability.
- Low apparent usurpation risk but high local governance and personnel risk.
- Dangerous patron whose temporary respect cannot guarantee a minister's future safety.
- Dies without an heir, allowing Jiaoxi's eventual abolition only after a long protected reign.

Unreliable patronage:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-367-1-pengnuren-jiu-piruan-de-zhuhouwang-lnc1sg-lkwl9dtnflxxgkieb79hj]] says Liu Duan treats Dong Zhongshu well because of his reputation, while Dong nevertheless exits from fear of eventual punishment.

Dynastic endpoint:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-237-qumu-yinmei-hanjingdi-ersun-you-duo-huangtang-1-lhiknwiq7jj0ysumzxd319s766qf|Hanji 237]] says Liu Duan dies after forty-seven years as king without a son and that Jiaoxi is then abolished.

## Qualifications

The page does not infer that Liu Duan's impotence caused his violence or personality. That causal link belongs to the episodes' interpretation, not to demonstrated medical evidence. Hanji 367-1's modern “male lover” language and positive characterization of Liu Duan's posthumous title remain bounded rather than settled. Hanji 237's exact killings, poisoning method, official count, tax description, reign length, heir status, and conversion of Jiaoxi into a commandery also remain episode-attributed pending direct comparison.
