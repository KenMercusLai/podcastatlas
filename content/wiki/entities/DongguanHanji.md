## Overview

《东观汉记》 / Dongguan Hanji enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-732-donghan-guangwudi-liuxiu-jiabeng-bingsi-de-lnsu1le2x-cj75cjslysvbndeuvr|Hanji 732]] as an Eastern Han source tradition used to supplement the terse [[ZizhiTongjian|《资治通鉴》]] notice of [[LiuXiu|光武帝刘秀]]'s death.

## Current Profile

The source's current function is evidentiary. Hanji 732 uses Dongguan Hanji notices about Liu Xiu's earlier illnesses to ask whether Guangwu's death may have followed a longer health decline. The contrast is important: Zizhi Tongjian records death date, place, and age, while Dongguan Hanji gives health episodes that make a medical inference possible but not settled.

In the wiki, Dongguan Hanji therefore marks a source-comparison layer rather than an independent book history. It helps show how later synthesis can widen a chronicle's sparse death notice while still respecting the gap between symptom record and diagnosis.

## Key Characteristics

- Eastern Han historical source tradition cited in the episode for Guangwu health notices.
- Supplements a terse chronicle death notice with earlier symptom evidence.
- Enables bounded inference without proving a specific disease.
- Functions as a reminder that source selection shapes what can be responsibly concluded.
