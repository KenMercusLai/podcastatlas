## Overview
Tokyo appears in [[the-continent-nobody-owns-everyone-benefits-from-summer-school]] as the city example behind a parking-policy lesson about street space, car ownership, and public transportation.

## Current Profile
The source uses Tokyo narrowly: car ownership is tied to proof of an off-street parking space, and strong public transportation reduces the expectation that daily movement should depend on private cars. The result is a compact example of [[ParkingCostInternalization]].

## Key Characteristics
- Tokyo is presented as a city where on-street parking is mostly unavailable.
- Buyers must show local police proof that they own or pay for an off-street parking space before buying a car.
- Good public transportation makes lower car dependence more plausible in the source's account.
- The parking rule shifts storage costs toward car owners instead of spreading them across public street users.

## Evidence
- Parking rule: [[the-continent-nobody-owns-everyone-benefits-from-summer-school]] says buyers need proof of off-street parking through the local police station.
- Transport context: [[the-continent-nobody-owns-everyone-benefits-from-summer-school]] says people in Tokyo generally do not expect to get around by car because public transit is very good.
- Policy lesson: [[the-continent-nobody-owns-everyone-benefits-from-summer-school]] presents the rule as a way to make car owners pay directly for parking.

## Qualifications
The source gives Tokyo as a listener-selected policy example, not a complete transportation history or full parking-law account.
