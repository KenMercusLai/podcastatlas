# Delta Air Lines

## Overview
Delta Air Lines is a large U.S. carrier used in the wiki for incumbent fare competition, loyalty scale, and a non-Starlink route into low-earth-orbit in-flight connectivity.

## Current Profile
The bounded sources show Delta combining the structural advantages of a legacy carrier with supplier choice in the next connectivity cycle. It is grouped with American and United in the basic-economy response to Spirit, while the latest source says Delta selected [[AmazonLeo]] and viewed it as potentially cheaper than [[Starlink]].

## Key Characteristics
- Legacy carrier able to combine lower upfront fares with a large network and loyalty system.
- Incumbent competitor to ultra-low-cost carriers such as Spirit.
- Airline-connectivity example that chose Amazon Leo rather than Starlink in the latest source.
- Demonstrates that the free-Wi-Fi shift need not produce one uncontested satellite supplier.

## Evidence
- Fare and loyalty competition: [[spirit-airlines-and-the-future-of-cheap-flights]] groups Delta with American and United as incumbents that copied budget pricing while retaining network and loyalty advantages.
- Supplier choice: [[haiwai-hangsi-fenfen-yongbao-xinglian-mianfei-wi-fi-weihe-zhujian-chengwei-hangsi-xin-biaopei-gkwriueoqxaeah7ykgtrgbwv]] says Delta chose Amazon Leo for low-earth-orbit connectivity.
- Cost rationale: [[haiwai-hangsi-fenfen-yongbao-xinglian-mianfei-wi-fi-weihe-zhujian-chengwei-hangsi-xin-biaopei-gkwriueoqxaeah7ykgtrgbwv]] reports Delta's view that Amazon Leo could cost less than Starlink, preserving price competition inside the satellite market.

## Qualifications
The Amazon Leo choice and cost comparison remain source-scoped. The sources do not independently verify contract terms, deployment timing, fleet coverage, service quality, or Delta's full loyalty and connectivity economics.
