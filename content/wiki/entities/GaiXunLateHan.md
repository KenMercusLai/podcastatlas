# 盖勋 / Gai Xun (late Han)

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-930-ruhe-pochu-tanxin-he-buganxin-lkyysh5jbgxwjq8ayrfar4gsjdcd|Hanji 930]] backfills Gai Xun's western frontier reputation before his later capital-side remonstrance and anti-eunuch branch. At Ji County, he rebukes [[BianZhangLateHan|边章]] and helps produce the withdrawal of the siege; later, when his rescue force is defeated and he is badly wounded, [[DianWuQiang|滇吾]] stops other Qiang fighters from killing him because he regards Gai Xun as worthy.

The same source shows reputation converting back into office. [[YangYongLateHan|杨雍]] recommends Gai Xun after his captivity and return, leading to his concurrent [[HanyangCommanderyLateHan|汉阳郡]] post. This makes Gai Xun an early example of [[MoralReputationPoliticalCapital|道德名望政治资本]]: battlefield failure does not erase value when enemy respect, official recommendation, and clean conduct all point in the same direction.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-941-shenchu-guanchang-ta-jing-xiang-ganfan-huangdi-liu-fqiz9pi7u6cltrn-cjmjz-r|Hanji 941]] introduces Gai Xun immediately before his stronger Hanji 942 anti-eunuch role. When [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]] holds a large Pingle Guan military display and calls himself "无上将军," Gai Xun argues that former kings promoted virtue rather than empty force and that drilling in the capital while enemies remain outside only adds a militaristic reputation.

[[GaiXunLateHan|盖勋]] enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-942-yue-youxiu-de-ren-huode-yue-lei-heli-ma-lkwlrcgnt-kxxc2qqz6z6wkg7e5z|Hanji 942]] as an upright late-Han official whose anti-eunuch stance makes him politically useful but also exposed. He treats [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]] as a ruler blocked by attendants rather than simply incapable, and asks [[YuanShao|袁绍]] to discuss removing the eunuchs from power.

The episode makes his transfer to Jingzhao yin a case of [[PalaceAccessMonopoly|palace access monopoly]]: [[JianShuoLateHan|蹇硕]] learns of the plan and turns a respectable appointment into a way to move Gai Xun away from the Luoyang center. This creates a pre-[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-943-quanshi-kaoyan-renxing-beihou-cangzhe-juda-xiongxian-lg7ia5xok-swdmhml35yv9qqkvt|Hanji 943]] example of how direct palace access could neutralize capable outsiders before succession politics fully detonated.
