# National Institutes of Health

## Overview
The National Institutes of Health is a federal research institution in the All-In science and health-policy branch. The bounded sources use NIH in three related ways: as strategic U.S. research capacity, as the target of root-cause public-health priority reform, and as a test case for whether more funding has produced enough scientific output.

## Current Profile
NIH currently sits at the center of a live wiki tension. [[RahmEmanuel|Rahm Emanuel]] treats NIH-style funding as a national advantage that should not be casually cut, [[MartyMakary|Marty Makary]] argues NIH priorities should move toward upstream chronic-disease causes, and [[MichaelKratsios|Michael Kratsios]] argues that rising NIH budgets have not produced proportional breakthroughs, making funding-process reform necessary.

## Key Characteristics
- NIH is part of the U.S. strategic research-capacity system alongside NSF, DARPA, universities, life sciences, and national labs.
- It is a health-research funding institution whose priorities are contested between treatment, genetics, root causes, public health, and scientific productivity.
- The Kratsios source makes NIH a funding-output problem by contrasting budget growth with perceived stagnation and Eroom's law.
- NIH reform is linked to COVID-era trust because Jay Bhattacharya and Marty Makary are used as reform figures in nearby All-In sources.
- The institution is politically exposed: different sources frame cuts or reallocations as either capacity damage or necessary depoliticization and productivity reform.
