## Overview

Shuya Gong is a designer who uses the [[DominosPizzaTracker|Domino's Pizza Tracker]] to explain how interfaces can reduce anxiety and make digital service work feel legible.

## Current Profile

In the episode, Gong connects progress interfaces to the familiar “physics” of offline transactions: people trust a process more easily when they can see recognizable work and movement. She also describes cases where designers deliberately add stages or delay because an instantaneous transaction feels implausible, exposing an ethical boundary between emotional legibility and fabricated process.

## Key Characteristics

- Uses “Pizza Tracker” as shorthand for staged progress design during uncertain waits.
- Frames trackers as a way to restore the visibility that physical transactions naturally provide.
- Connects visible effort to reassurance and perceived value.
- Treats some artificial delay as recreating familiar transactional physics, while acknowledging the truthfulness problem it creates.

### Manufactured stages

- [[the-dominos-pizza-tracker-theory-of-everything]] describes her fintech examples in which instant completion generated distrust and staged delay made the transaction feel more substantial.

## Qualifications

- The episode does not establish that all of Gong's clients or designs use synthetic delay.
- Calling artificial progress familiar “physics” explains its psychological purpose but does not settle whether a particular implementation is ethical.
