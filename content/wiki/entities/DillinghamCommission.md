# Dillingham Commission

The Dillingham Commission is the U.S. immigration investigation invoked in [[168-yin-yu-xin-yibu-riben-laogong-yimin-de-kuayang-huikuan-shi-lmgfmmgkawoa-6xyucnm1ljebzwu]]. The episode describes it as a large early-20th-century inquiry whose reports included material on immigrant banks and remittances, interpreting money sent abroad by southern European, eastern European, and Asian migrants as a drain on U.S. wealth.

The commission matters in the source because it makes [[RacializedRemittancePolitics]] concrete. Remittances were not only financial transfers; in U.S. politics they became evidence used to sort desirable and undesirable immigrants, especially amid anti-Japanese agitation and "yellow peril" narratives on the West Coast.

## Key Claims
- Official social-science-style immigration investigation can embed racial assumptions in research design and interpretation.
- Cross-border remittances can be reframed from family support into national economic threat when migrants are already racialized.
- Japanese migration after the Russo-Japanese War became entangled with U.S. fear of Japanese imperial expansion.
