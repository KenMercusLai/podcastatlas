# Charles Stewart Parnell

## Overview

Charles Stewart Parnell was leader of the Irish Parliamentary Party who joined land agitation, parliamentary leverage, and Home Rule before scandal split his movement and ended his leadership.

## Current Profile

Parnell appears as the pivotal late-nineteenth-century leader of [[IrishConstitutionalNationalism]]. He worked with Gladstone for [[IrishHomeRule|Home Rule]], used land agitation to strengthen national politics, and blurred any simple boundary between constitutional nationalism and Fenian republicanism.

His career also reveals the movement's vulnerability to violence, hostile information, and personal scandal. The Phoenix Park murders damaged the wider cause; forged letters falsely associated him with violence before being exposed; and the public O'Shea divorce case gave opponents leverage to split the Irish Parliamentary Party. His mass funeral nevertheless demonstrated the durability of his nationalist authority.

## Key Characteristics

- Leader who made the Irish Parliamentary Party a disciplined Westminster force.
- Home Rule advocate and political partner of William Gladstone.
- Strategist who connected agrarian agitation to national constitutional leverage.
- Figure who crossed or blurred the boundary between parliamentary and Fenian currents.
- Target of forged allegations linking him to political violence.
- Leader brought down by the O'Shea scandal and the resulting party split.
