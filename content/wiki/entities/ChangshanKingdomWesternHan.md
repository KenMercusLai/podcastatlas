## Overview

西汉常山国 / Changshan Kingdom is the Western Han vassal kingdom and royal title line presented in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-342-1-waihao-bie-luan-jiao-you-de-mingzi-hui-bei-duzhou-yisheng-lpehgfgtkbed4vg3z7yegsqalll1|Hanji 342-1]] through the successive misfortunes of several kings.

## Current Profile

The episode supplies a royal-outcome sequence rather than an administrative history. Liu Buyi dies soon after transfer to the title, Liu Hong is killed amid a disputed-parentage purge, [[LiuShunChangshanKingWesternHan|刘顺]] rules for thirty-two years under paternal favor, and Liu Shun's son Liu Bo is soon deposed. The title is then said to lapse until the Eastern Han.

This sequence makes Changshan the organizing center of a [[CursedTitleNarrative|“cursed title” story]], but the outcomes do not share one demonstrated mechanism. The polity's geography, offices, revenues, local administration, territorial changes, and relations with the central court remain outside the supplied account.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han vassal kingdom represented through a succession of same-surname kings.
- Presented through royal deaths, killing, indulgence, and deposition rather than territorial administration.
- Includes Liu Shun's unusually long thirty-two-year reign as a partial exception to the title's ominous pattern.
- Associated in the episode with [[GaozhuangHanTomb|高庄汉墓]] through the proposed burial of Liu Shun.
- Said to lose its active royal title after Liu Bo's deposition until a later Eastern Han revival.

## Evidence

Royal succession pattern:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-342-1-waihao-bie-luan-jiao-you-de-mingzi-hui-bei-duzhou-yisheng-lpehgfgtkbed4vg3z7yegsqalll1|Hanji 342-1]] names Liu Buyi, Liu Hong, Liu Shun, and Liu Bo as Western Han holders of the Changshan title and summarizes their different outcomes.

Long reign and burial connection:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-342-1-waihao-bie-luan-jiao-you-de-mingzi-hui-bei-duzhou-yisheng-lpehgfgtkbed4vg3z7yegsqalll1|Hanji 342-1]] gives Liu Shun a thirty-two-year reign and reports an archaeological attribution of Gaozhuang to him.

Title discontinuity:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-342-1-waihao-bie-luan-jiao-you-de-mingzi-hui-bei-duzhou-yisheng-lpehgfgtkbed4vg3z7yegsqalll1|Hanji 342-1]] says Liu Bo's deposition is followed by a gap in use of the Changshan title until the Eastern Han.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to one short popular-history episode. It does not reconstruct the kingdom's location, foundation, territorial changes, institutions, population, economic base, complete king list, or the legal and political processes behind each succession. The four outcomes are evidence of a narratively striking sequence, not of supernatural causation or one continuous political mechanism.

## What Changed

- Created a canonical polity page separating Changshan's royal succession sequence from the episode's curse metaphor.
- Recorded Liu Shun's long reign as a qualification to the claim that every holder immediately met disaster.

- [[LiuShunChangshanKingWesternHan]] - long-reigning king whose life partially resists, and whose burial completes, the episode's ominous pattern.
