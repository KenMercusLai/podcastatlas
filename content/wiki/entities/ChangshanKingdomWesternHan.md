## Overview

西汉常山国 / Changshan Kingdom is the Western Han vassal kingdom and royal title line presented through successive royal outcomes and the household-investigation crisis that ends the kingship in 114 BCE.

## Current Profile

The episode supplies a royal-outcome sequence rather than an administrative history. Liu Buyi dies soon after transfer to the title, Liu Hong is killed amid a disputed-parentage purge, [[LiuShunChangshanKingWesternHan|刘顺]] rules for thirty-two years under paternal favor, and Liu Shun's son Liu Bo is soon deposed. The title is then said to lapse until the Eastern Han.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-342-2-gudai-fuqi-tongfang-jingyou-zhezhong-falv-xianzhi-lsnhz4q-oxjpefgkdfxkcal-37iv|Hanji 342-2]] now explains the final removal. Liu Shun's divided household, [[LiuZhuoChangshanWesternHan|刘棁]]'s exclusion, [[LiuBoChangshanKingWesternHan|刘勃]]'s alleged mourning violations, and his interference with [[ZhangQianWesternHan|张骞]]'s investigation turn a family grievance into a central-court case. Deposition ends the royal line and Changshan becomes a commandery, while separate grants to Liu Ping and Liu Shang preserve other branches of Liu Shun's descendants.

The sequence still makes Changshan the organizing center of a [[CursedTitleNarrative|“cursed title” story]], but Hanji 342-2 supplies a political and legal mechanism for the last case rather than supernatural causation. The polity's geography, offices, revenues, and ordinary local administration remain outside the supplied accounts.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han vassal kingdom represented through a succession of same-surname kings.
- Presented through royal deaths, killing, indulgence, and deposition rather than territorial administration.
- Includes Liu Shun's unusually long thirty-two-year reign as a partial exception to the title's ominous pattern.
- Ends through a combined household, mourning, and investigation crisis rather than title fate alone.
- Associated in the episode with [[GaozhuangHanTomb|高庄汉墓]] through the proposed burial of Liu Shun.
- Converted from a vassal kingship into a commandery after Liu Bo's deposition.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to two short popular-history episodes. It does not reconstruct the kingdom's location, foundation, institutions, population, economic base, complete king list, or ordinary government. The received chronicle's conversion of Changshan into a commandery should not be confused with a general end to Liu Shun's descendants: the later Zhengding and Sishui grants relocate dynastic continuity rather than restore the Changshan kingship.

## What Changed

- Added the household accusation, central investigation, and commandery-conversion mechanism behind the final deposition.
- Distinguished abolition of the Changshan kingship from later grants to other sons of Liu Shun.
