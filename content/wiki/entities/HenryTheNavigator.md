# Henry the Navigator / 恩里克王子

## Overview
Henry the Navigator / 恩里克王子 was a son of [[JohnIOfPortugal|João I]] and [[PhilippaOfLancaster|Philippa of Lancaster]] whose role in [[Ceuta]], Tangier, Atlantic navigation, and early captive trading makes him both a central expansion sponsor and a morally difficult figure.

## Current Profile
The sources keep Henry important but not mythically omniscient. He helps hold the Ceuta target when some Portuguese voices suggest attacking [[Gibraltar]] instead, is knighted after the victory, and later fails at Tangier in 1437.

Part 1 adds the project behind his reputation: austere Christian militancy, commerce, land, spices, the search for Prester John, knowledge exchange at Sagres, and experimentation with caravels and African routes. It also makes coercion inseparable from that program by linking Henry's expansion era to papal authorization, African captive raiding and sales, and [[EarlyPortugueseAtlanticPlantationSlavery]].

## Key Characteristics
- Participant in the 1415 Ceuta campaign.
- Sponsor-symbol for navigation, cartography, Atlantic islands, and African coastal exploration.
- Combined commerce and geographic inquiry with crusading anti-Muslim purpose and the search for Christian allies.
- Associated with failed Tangier campaigning as well as successful expansion memory.
- Implicated in the coercive origins of African captive trading and Atlantic plantation labor.

## Evidence
- Campaign evidence: [[496-yiminchao-beihou-de-xiuda-cong-gudian-shidai-manzu-duhai-yibiliya-shoufu-shidi-yundong-dao-putaoya-dahanghai-lu8pawnkiselmmrgubrey4pq-4fw]] says Henry and his brothers participated in Ceuta and were knighted after the battle.
- Target-discipline evidence: [[496-yiminchao-beihou-de-xiuda-cong-gudian-shidai-manzu-duhai-yibiliya-shoufu-shidi-yundong-dao-putaoya-dahanghai-lu8pawnkiselmmrgubrey4pq-4fw]] says Henry was among those insisting on Ceuta rather than switching to Gibraltar.
- Qualification evidence: [[496-yiminchao-beihou-de-xiuda-cong-gudian-shidai-manzu-duhai-yibiliya-shoufu-shidi-yundong-dao-putaoya-dahanghai-lu8pawnkiselmmrgubrey4pq-4fw]] uses the 1437 Tangier failure and unfulfilled ransom promise to complicate heroic memory.
- Motive evidence: [[227-portugal-on-the-edge-of-the-world-part-1-glt1834337589]] joins wealth, spices, land, Prester John, and militant Christianity in Henry's program.
- Maritime evidence: [[227-portugal-on-the-edge-of-the-world-part-1-glt1834337589]] connects Sagres, cross-cultural knowledge, caravels, shallow hulls, islands, and African rivers to exploration.
- Coercion evidence: [[227-portugal-on-the-edge-of-the-world-part-1-glt1834337589]] connects the expansion to papal enslavement language, the 1444 Arguin raid, Lisbon captive sales, and island plantations.

## Qualifications
The sources do not establish a complete institutional history of Sagres or distinguish every voyage Henry personally directed from later memory attached to him. Character judgments, papal intent, captive totals, and the balance among religion, commerce, and knowledge remain source-scoped.

## What Changed
- Expanded Henry from the Ceuta campaign into a qualified profile of maritime sponsorship, crusading-commerce, Tangier failure, and slavery.
