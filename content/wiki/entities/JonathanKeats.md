# Jonathan Keats

## Overview
Jonathan Keats is the artist and self-described experimental philosopher in [[salt-020260914-archive-keats-podcast]]. The source presents him as turning philosophical questions into public situations, artifacts, and long-duration experiments.

## Current Profile
Keats's wiki role is to add an art-and-philosophy route into the [[LongNow]] branch. Where other Long Now sources emphasize institutional design, ecological memory, progress communication, or mathematical models, Keats builds objects that let people inhabit questions about thought, time, identity, cities, ecology, and future accountability.

## Key Characteristics
- Keats treats thought experiments as public situations whose conclusions should remain open.
- His work often uses intentionally absurd or speculative objects to reveal ordinary assumptions about time, labor, identity, and value.
- He moves from short conceptual gestures toward projects that operate across centuries or millennia.
- He relies on institutional hosts such as [[LongNow]] when they provide space for inquiry without prescribing outcomes.
- His deep-time work makes future viewers or living ecological processes part of the artwork's meaning.

## Evidence
### Public Experiment
- [[salt-020260914-archive-keats-podcast]] describes Keats's 2000 gallery project, where he used a factory time clock and time cards to mark thoughts and made the punched card the purchasable trace.
- [[salt-020260914-archive-keats-podcast]] says he became dissatisfied with thought experiments used only as predetermined arguments and began calling himself an experimental philosopher.

### Speculative Objects
- [[salt-020260914-archive-keats-podcast]] links lead time ingots, a time-warp undershirt, neutron-star architecture, rotating cities, and epigenetic "cloning" to Keats's habit of making improbable proposals that expose hidden assumptions.

### Deep-Time Practice
- [[salt-020260914-archive-keats-podcast]] presents Keats's 100-year and 1,000-year cameras as ways to make people imagine future viewers looking back on present choices.
- [[salt-020260914-archive-keats-podcast]] introduces [[CenturiesOfTheBristlecone]] as a 5,000-year calendar proposal rooted in tree growth and climate exposure.

## Qualifications
Keats's projects are not presented as conventional engineering plans or policy instruments. Their practical value is source-scoped to imagination, conversation, ritual, and public accountability rather than guaranteed material effectiveness.
