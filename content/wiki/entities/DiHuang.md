## Overview

翟黄 / Di Huang is a [[WeiState|魏国]] official whose two bounded episodes present complementary political skills: finding capable people for distinct roles and making dangerous criticism acceptable to [[WeiWenhou|魏文侯]].

## Current Profile

Di Huang first appears as the disappointed chancellor candidate who grounds his claim in five consequential recommendations: [[WuQi|吴起]], [[XimenBao|西门豹]], [[LeYang|乐羊]], [[LiKe|李克]], and [[QuHoufu|屈侯鲋]]. Li Ke's comparison favors [[WeiCheng|魏成]] because attracting teacher-level figures is treated as a higher-order contribution, and Di Huang accepts the judgment rather than turning disappointment into factional resistance.

Zhouji 05 part 2 adds a different form of usefulness. After [[RenZuoWarringStates|任作]] angers Wei Wenhou by criticizing a family assignment, Di Huang does not deny the criticism or confront the ruler directly. He argues that the presence of such a direct minister proves the ruler's own humane quality. The formulation preserves both the critic's integrity and the ruler's face, enabling Ren Zuo's recall.

## Key Characteristics

- Personnel broker whose recommendations fill military, local-administration, governance, and educational roles.
- Adviser able to distinguish useful referrals from the higher-order attraction of teacher-level talent.
- Disappointed candidate who accepts a reasoned adverse judgment.
- Intermediary who protects direct criticism by connecting it to the ruler's preferred self-image.
- Court actor whose value lies in both talent placement and feedback repair.

## Qualifications

The current evidence consists of two podcast episodes organized around exemplary dialogue rather than a complete biography. Di Huang's exact office, the historical wording, and his role in the later recall are not independently verified here. His face-saving argument succeeds in restoring access for Ren Zuo, but the source does not say that Wei Wenhou reverses the Zhongshan assignment.
