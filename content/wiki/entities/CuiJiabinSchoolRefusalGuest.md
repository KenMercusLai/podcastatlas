# 崔家斌 / Cui Jiabin (School-Refusal Guest)

## Overview
崔家斌 / Cui Jiabin is the name transcribed for a child mental-health guest in [[vol-141-zhe-xue-fei-shang-buke-ma-zhe-ban-fei-shang-buke-ma-don-t-push-the-river-shijie-ertongri-lnukt9drrpft43iueo4xfpyrkkbm|VOL.141]] of [[ZheBingShuoLaiHuaChang|这病说来话长]]. ## Current Profile
In the episode, Cui frames school refusal through autonomy, family-school ecology, and the child's limited ability to verbalize distress. Children need a “third space” outside home and school for play, peers, rest, exploration, and discovering competence. When feelings remain disorganized, bodily symptoms may communicate what the child cannot yet say; helping connect body, emotion, and context is described as mentalization.

Cui's “Don't push the river” metaphor argues for supportive pacing rather than escalating pressure. The stance does not remove adult responsibility: the episode also retains routines, rules, environmental adjustment, and development of the child's adaptive capacity. Because the corpus contains two other same-pinyin guests with different Chinese spellings, this profile remains source-scoped and disambiguated.

## Key Characteristics
- Interprets school refusal as an interaction among emotion, agency, family, school, peers, and education pressure.
- Uses third-space activity to protect exploration and competence outside performance demands.
- Explains bodily distress as possible communication when a child lacks usable emotional language.
- Uses mentalization to connect physical sensation, emotion, and context without making a bodily symptom automatically psychological.
- Advocates supportive pacing and bounded choice rather than escalating control or passive abandonment.
- Treats environmental change and the child's adaptive capacity as complementary rather than mutually exclusive.
