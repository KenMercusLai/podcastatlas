# Thespis, or The Gods Grown Old

## Overview

*Thespis, or The Gods Grown Old* is the 1871 comic opera that began the Gilbert and Sullivan partnership, placing an acting troupe in charge of Olympus and exposing institutional absurdity through mismatched replacements.

## Current Profile

According to [[12-days-good-king-wenceslas-and-the-first-gilbert-sullivan-glt4255677286]], *Thespis* opened at the Gaiety Theatre on Boxing Day 1871 and was advertised as an original grotesque opera in two acts. The aged and bored Olympian gods exchange places with Thespis's actors, whose personal dispositions make them incompetent substitutes: Mars becomes pacifist, Hymen resists marriage, and Pluto prevents entry to the underworld.

The gods eventually return and condemn the actors to become tragedians ignored by audiences. The source reports 63 performances and popular laughter alongside hostile criticism of the premiere's length, plot, and music. Most of the score's loss makes the work's place in [[GilbertAndSullivan|Gilbert and Sullivan's]] origin easier to recover than its complete musical effect.

## Key Characteristics

- First collaboration between Gilbert and Sullivan.
- Two-act Victorian “grotesque opera” premiered on Boxing Day 1871.
- Mythological role-reversal plot involving gods and an acting troupe.
- Institutional comedy driven by officeholders unsuited to their assigned functions.
- Work with a reportedly successful run but mixed or hostile critical reception.
- Partly lost work whose later fame depends strongly on its creators' subsequent careers.
