## Overview

Dong Xian / 董贤 is synthesized as [[HanAidi|Emperor Ai of Han]]'s highly visible favorite and a weak political operator at the late [[WesternHanDynasty|Western Han]] succession hinge. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-602-tanxun-guoxue-zhihui-pojie-2024nian-fantaisui-zhi-mi-lvrwnpanicgjpf43cbmveigtxqj|Hanji 602]] shows him already prominent enough for a Xiongnu chanyu to ask about him in court, while [[126-wangmang-zhisi-shensheng-diguo-de-huixuanbiao-he-huixuanbiao-de-huixuanbiao-786255780]] uses his later failure to show that imperial proximity and symbols do not automatically become durable power.

## Current Profile

Dong Xian's visibility layer comes from the Yuanshou 2 chanyu visit. The foreign ruler notices a young, handsome high official among the Han court ministers and asks who he is. Aidi has him identified as the grand marshal Dong Xian and explains his high office through youth paired with worthiness. The scene does not prove Dong Xian's competence, but it does show his conspicuous elevation within Aidi's court.

Dong Xian's succession-failure layer turns on symbolic access without organizational control. Emperor Ai entrusted him with the imperial seal before death, but Dong Xian blocked information without taking effective political action. He had palace and military proximity but lacked the judgment needed to convert possession into rule, allowing [[WangZhengjun|王政君]] and [[WangMang|王莽]] to recover control.

Dong Xian's wiki role is therefore diagnostic rather than biographical. He marks the difference between intimacy, status, and authority: a favorite can be visible enough to impress foreign observers and still fail when the court's real succession machinery starts moving.

## Key Characteristics

- Highly visible favorite of Han Aidi whose youth and appearance draw notice in a diplomatic court scene.
- Holder of very high office in Aidi's reign, presented by the court as worthy despite youth.
- Possessor of palace and symbolic access at Aidi's death.
- Failed succession operator whose possession of symbols did not become control.
- Foil for Wang Zhengjun and Wang Mang, whose institutional and seniority advantages beat Dong Xian's proximity.

## Qualifications

The current page is not a full biography of Dong Xian. Hanji 602 supports his public visibility and court rank in one diplomatic scene, while the Xiangrui source supports his late succession failure. His relationship with Aidi, exact offices and dates, wider factional network, later death, and the textual history of his reputation remain source-scoped.
