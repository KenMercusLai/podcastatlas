# Shanna Swan

## Overview
Shanna Swan is the reproductive epidemiologist interviewed in [[how-to-safeguard-your-hormone-health-fertility-dr-shanna-swan-scim6175232531|How to Safeguard Your Hormone Health & Fertility]]. The episode presents her work on endocrine-active environmental chemicals, prenatal development, anogenital distance, semen quality, and population sperm-count trends.

## Current Profile
In the bounded evidence, Swan's distinctive contribution is translating an animal toxicology signal into human epidemiology. After phthalate-exposed rats showed a cluster of incompletely masculinized traits, she used stored maternal urine samples and carefully standardized infant measurements to test whether prenatal phthalate biomarkers were associated with male anogenital distance. She reports a related result in that first study and in TIDES, a later trimester-sampling cohort.

Swan also advocates practical exposure reduction while acknowledging different evidence layers and persistence profiles. Her recommendations focus on repeated sources such as fragranced products, plastic-food contact, can linings, pesticides, and nonstick materials, while her broader research claims remain observational and population-level rather than individualized diagnoses.

## Key Characteristics
- Studies reproductive health through environmental epidemiology and human biomonitoring.
- Connects animal toxicology signals to explicitly designed human measurements.
- Emphasizes fetal developmental timing as a distinct vulnerability window.
- Uses anogenital distance and semen parameters as reproductive-development endpoints.
- Treats dose, timing, species, endpoint, and study design as essential interpretation context.
- Couples policy concern with feasible household exposure-reduction choices.

## Evidence
- Research path - [[how-to-safeguard-your-hormone-health-fertility-dr-shanna-swan-scim6175232531]] traces Swan's move from the rat "phthalate syndrome" literature to maternal urine biomarkers and infant examinations.
- Replication - [[how-to-safeguard-your-hormone-health-fertility-dr-shanna-swan-scim6175232531]] says TIDES collected urine by trimester, examined babies at birth, and reproduced the phthalate-related pattern.
- Fertility research - [[how-to-safeguard-your-hormone-health-fertility-dr-shanna-swan-scim6175232531]] attributes to Swan work on anogenital distance, sperm count, geographically varying semen quality, pesticides, and long-run sperm-count trends.
- Public guidance - [[how-to-safeguard-your-hormone-health-fertility-dr-shanna-swan-scim6175232531]] records her advice on fragrance, food packaging, heating plastic, can linings, cookware, receipts, and persistence differences among chemical classes.

## Qualifications
This profile is bounded to one summarized interview rather than Swan's complete publication record. The episode moves across animal experiments, human associations, intervention ideas, policy comparisons, population trends, and demographic speculation without supplying full citations for every claim. Its reported associations do not establish that a particular exposure caused an individual's anatomy, behavior, fertility status, or pregnancy outcome. The source title spells her name Shanna; repeated "Shauna" in the body is treated as transcription error.
