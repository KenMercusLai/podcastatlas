# Tupia

## Overview

Tupia was a Tahitian navigator, cultural intermediary, and translator who chose to join the [[Endeavour]] and became indispensable during its encounters with Māori in New Zealand.

## Current Profile

The episode restores Tupia's agency against a purely European expedition story. [[JosephBanks]] may describe taking him to Britain “as a curiosity,” but the hosts emphasize that Tupia could equally regard the British ship and its world as objects of curiosity. His ability to communicate with [[Maori|Māori]] reveals the linguistic and navigational reach of Polynesian societies and materially changes what Cook's party can understand.

Tupia's mediation reduces some language barriers but cannot remove the expedition's unequal weapons, coercive threats, or incompatible expectations. Translation becomes both a bridge and part of a violent contact zone.

## Key Characteristics

- Self-directed traveler whose choice to join the expedition should not be reduced to European collection.
- Polynesian navigator carrying geographic and cultural knowledge unavailable to the British.
- Translator whose Tahitian language remained sufficiently connected to Māori for meaningful communication.
- Intermediary who makes Pacific mobility and shared linguistic history visible to Cook.
- Participant whose presence improves diplomacy without neutralizing armed inequality.

## Evidence

- Agency and departure: [[381-captain-cook-to-the-ends-of-the-earth-part-2-glt6950185057]] records Banks's July 1769 note that Tupia wanted to travel with the Endeavour.
- Translation: [[381-captain-cook-to-the-ends-of-the-earth-part-2-glt6950185057]] credits Tupia with enabling communication during Māori encounters in New Zealand.
- Navigational significance: [[381-captain-cook-to-the-ends-of-the-earth-part-2-glt6950185057]] says the linguistic connection helps Cook infer that Polynesians crossed immense ocean distances before Europeans.
- Limits of mediation: [[381-captain-cook-to-the-ends-of-the-earth-part-2-glt6950185057]] shows friendly messages delivered through Tupia coexisting with Cook's warning that British weapons could kill those who insulted them.

## Qualifications

Tupia's own perspective is reconstructed through European records in this source. His motives, exact words, status, navigation knowledge, and interpretation of British intentions cannot be fully recovered from the episode alone.
