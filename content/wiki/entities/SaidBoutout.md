# Saïd Boutout

## Overview

Saïd Boutout was a 14-year-old Algerian shepherd whom French reservist [[StanislasHutin|Stanislas Hutin]] found being tortured with electricity during the Algerian War.

## Current Profile

The source gives Boutout little independent biography, but it preserves his name, age, occupation, and portrait. Hutin photographed him after the torture, and the portrait later appeared on covers of critical histories and became a recognizable image of the conflict. Boutout therefore appears both as an individual victim and as a warning against allowing iconic imagery to erase the person it depicts.

## Key Characteristics

- Fourteen-year-old Algerian shepherd at the time of the reported abuse.
- Victim of electrical torture by French soldiers in north-eastern Algeria.
- Identifiable person preserved in Stanislas Hutin's testimony and photography.
- Portrait subject whose image entered later histories of the Algerian War.

## Evidence

- Identification evidence: [[coffer-stop-ai-may-shrink-tax-base-6abf81fdb06169dc7218d384]] names the victim as Boutou, gives his age and occupation, and places the event in north-eastern Algeria; independent historical references render the surname Boutout.
- Abuse evidence: [[coffer-stop-ai-may-shrink-tax-base-6abf81fdb06169dc7218d384]] says Hutin discovered him being shocked from a field generator.
- Image-afterlife evidence: [[coffer-stop-ai-may-shrink-tax-base-6abf81fdb06169dc7218d384]] says Hutin's portrait of Boutout later appeared on historical book covers and became recognizable.

## Qualifications

The episode does not provide Boutout's later life, family account, own testimony, or a full chain of custody for the photograph. The requested transcript shortens his surname to "Boutou"; historical and archival references use Boutout, with some French sources also using Boutoute. His image should not be treated as a substitute for his agency or as proof of abuses beyond the event Hutin described.

## What Changed

- Corrected the canonical surname from the episode's "Boutou" rendering to the historically attested Boutout.
