# Alexander Hamilton

## Overview

Alexander Hamilton is a founding-era U.S. political figure who appears in the wiki as a theorist of energetic but removable executive power, a Federalist architect of national finance, and the victim of the fatal [[HamiltonBurrDuel|Hamilton-Burr duel]].

## Current Profile

The current synthesis holds Hamilton's institutional ambition and personal volatility together. In the impeachment source, he is the founding voice for a single energetic president constrained by election, separation of powers, and removal. In the Hamilton episode, the same energy becomes biographical and political: Hamilton rises from Caribbean outsider to Washington aide, constitutional advocate, and Federalist state-builder who wants central authority, national banking, manufacturing, and British-leaning policy. His rivalry with [[AaronBurr|Aaron Burr]] then shows how a politician committed to public institutions could still be trapped by [[HonorDuelCulture|honor duel culture]] when reputation threatened future usefulness.

## Key Characteristics

- Advocate for a strong but removable executive inside the [[UnitedStatesConstitution]] debate.
- Federalist state-builder associated with central government, public finance, a national bank, manufacturing, and British alignment.
- Brilliant, ambitious, sharp-tongued political actor whose style created durable enemies.
- Central opponent of Jeffersonian and Burrite power in the [[FirstPartySystem]].
- Participant in a fatal honor duel despite religious objections, family obligations, debt, and [[PhilipHamilton|Philip Hamilton]]'s earlier death.
- Posthumous martyr whose memory was preserved by [[ElizaHamilton|Eliza Hamilton]] and later cultural attention.

## Evidence

- Executive-power theory: [[173-tanhe-ruhe-bamian-yiwei-zongtong-963141809]] uses Hamilton to explain why a single president could offer energy and accountability if constrained by elections and [[PresidentialImpeachment|impeachment]].
- Federalist program: [[685-hamilton-duel-to-the-death-part-3-glt9231431046]] describes Hamilton's support for strong central government, a national bank, manufacturing, and closer relations with Britain.
- Political rivalry: [[685-hamilton-duel-to-the-death-part-3-glt9231431046]] traces Hamilton's conflict with Jefferson and Burr through the 1791 Senate race, the 1800 election, and Burr's 1804 New York governorship campaign.
- Honor-culture case: [[78-xifang-juedou-shi-chujian-ba-weile-rongyu-687731000]] gives Hamilton and Burr as an American political-dueling example, while [[685-hamilton-duel-to-the-death-part-3-glt9231431046]] reconstructs the full challenge, Weehawken encounter, and death.
- Memory aftermath: [[685-hamilton-duel-to-the-death-part-3-glt9231431046]] says Hamilton died on July 12, 1804, left debts, was remembered through Eliza's work, and eventually became a founding martyr figure.

## Qualifications

The duel episode leans toward Hamilton sympathetically, and it acknowledges that Burr's character is filtered through hostile accounts and later reputation. Hamilton's own stated plan to throw away his shot does not remove ambiguity around his visible aiming behavior or the seconds' conflicting accounts.
