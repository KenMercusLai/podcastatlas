## Overview

宋意 / Song Yi enters the wiki as the Eastern Han shangshu who argues against joining [[SouthernXiongnu|Southern Xiongnu]] in an immediate attack on [[NorthernXiongnuLateHan|Northern Xiongnu]]. Hanji 759 makes him an early source for [[ReputationConstrainedWarRestraint|名声约束下的战争节制]] because his memorial links cost, frontier balance, [[Xianbei|Xianbei]] incentives, and Guangwu-era jimi policy.

## Current Profile

Hanji 759 places Song Yi opposite [[GengBingEasternHan|Geng Bing]] in the Northern Xiongnu debate. Geng treats Northern Xiongnu famine and Southern Xiongnu request as an opportunity; Song treats the same moment as a test of frontier balance.

His memorial argues from several directions at once. Previous Xiongnu campaigns did not repay their cost, Guangwu's policy of jimi support had kept the border quiet for more than forty years, and Xianbei pressure had already weakened Northern Xiongnu. If Southern Xiongnu return north and Han then restrains Xianbei on their behalf, Song warns that Han may create a new resentment problem.

Song's preferred alternative is to let Northern Xiongnu submit and become an outer screen rather than destroy the balance through forced attack. His page therefore marks a policy logic: restraint is not passivity when it preserves a cheaper frontier order.

## Key Characteristics

- Eastern Han shangshu speaking in the 88 CE Northern Xiongnu campaign debate.
- Opposes immediate participation in Southern Xiongnu's requested attack.
- Treats prior Xiongnu wars as costly and under-rewarding.
- Uses Guangwu-era jimi as a precedent for lower-cost frontier quiet.
- Reads Xianbei pressure as already doing part of the work against Northern Xiongnu.
- Prefers Northern Xiongnu submission as an outer screen over destabilizing attack.
- Distinct from other Song Yi/Song You route names in the wiki.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 759 and does not reconstruct Song Yi's full office career. The current identity is kept separate from [[SongYouEasternHan|Song You (Eastern Han)]], [[SongYi|宋义]], and [[SongYiLateHan|宋翼]] because the role, period, and characters differ.
