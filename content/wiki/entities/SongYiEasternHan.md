## Overview

宋意 / Song Yi now appears as the Eastern Han shangshu whose current profile has two restraint layers. Hanji 758 shows him warning [[EmperorZhangOfHan|Emperor Zhang]] that royal affection must not override the emperor-subject order: uncles and brothers are still subjects in court protocol, and adult princes should return to their fiefs. Hanji 759 then shows him arguing against joining [[SouthernXiongnu|Southern Xiongnu]] in an immediate attack on [[NorthernXiongnuLateHan|Northern Xiongnu]], linking cost, frontier balance, [[Xianbei|Xianbei]] incentives, and Guangwu-era jimi policy.

## Current Profile

Hanji 758 supplies Song Yi's court-protocol layer. Zhangdi has let royal kin stay in Luoyang and enjoy privileges such as carriage entry through palace gates, sitting without bowing, eating from the imperial kitchen, large gifts, and visible residence construction. Song Yi argues from Spring-and-Autumn-style hierarchy: the emperor may have uncles and brothers as relatives, but in court they are also subjects. His remedy is to restore distance by sending adult princes with wives and children back to their fiefs.

Hanji 759 places Song Yi opposite [[GengBingEasternHan|Geng Bing]] in the Northern Xiongnu debate. Geng treats Northern Xiongnu famine and the Southern Xiongnu request as an opportunity; Song treats the same moment as a test of frontier balance.

His anti-expedition memorial argues from several directions at once. Previous Xiongnu campaigns did not repay their cost, Guangwu's policy of jimi support had kept the border quiet for more than forty years, and Xianbei pressure had already weakened Northern Xiongnu. If Southern Xiongnu return north and Han then restrains Xianbei on their behalf, Song warns that Han may create a new resentment problem.

Song's preferred alternative is to let Northern Xiongnu submit and become an outer screen rather than destroy the balance through forced attack. His page therefore marks a consistent policy logic: restraint is not passivity when it preserves hierarchy, cost discipline, and a cheaper frontier order.

## Key Characteristics

- Eastern Han shangshu who uses court hierarchy to limit royal-kin privilege under Zhangdi.
- Treats royal relatives as subjects in the formal court setting despite family closeness.
- Uses return-to-fief distance as a remedy for overfamiliar capital residence.
- Opposes immediate participation in Southern Xiongnu's requested attack on Northern Xiongnu.
- Treats prior Xiongnu wars as costly and under-rewarding.
- Uses Guangwu-era jimi and Xianbei pressure as arguments for lower-cost frontier quiet.
- Distinct from other Song Yi/Song You route names in the wiki.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 758 and Hanji 759 and does not reconstruct Song Yi's full office career. The page treats the two restraint layers as compatible but distinct: Hanji 758 is about royal-kin protocol inside the capital, while Hanji 759 is about frontier strategy. The current identity is kept separate from [[SongYouEasternHan|Song You (Eastern Han)]], [[SongYi|宋义]], and [[SongYiLateHan|宋翼]] because the role, period, and characters differ.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 758 as Song Yi's earlier court-protocol layer before his Hanji 759 frontier-policy memorial.
- Reframed Song Yi around restraint across two domains: kinship hierarchy in the capital and frontier balance outside it.
