# Nkosi Sikelel' iAfrika

## Overview

Nkosi Sikelel' iAfrika is presented as [[EnochSontonga]]'s Christian hymn that became an ANC anthem, a liberation symbol, and one half of South Africa's post-apartheid multilingual national anthem settlement.

## Current Profile

The episode treats Nkosi as powerful because it carries religious, political, and memorial meaning at the same time. It begins in Methodist school and choir life, enters black South African Christian politics, becomes tied to the [[AfricanNationalCongress|ANC]], and then appears at the [[RugbyWorldCup1995|1995 Rugby World Cup]] as a sign that rugby's old symbolic order is being asked to recognize the new [[SouthAfrica]].

## Key Characteristics

- Christian hymn written by [[EnochSontonga]] in 1897.
- Song associated with black South African Christianity and resistance to white missionary paternalism.
- Sung at the 1912 founding meeting of the South African Native National Congress.
- Adopted as the [[AfricanNationalCongress|ANC]] official anthem in 1925.
- Post-apartheid anthem component fused with [[DieStemVanSuidAfrika|Die Stem]] in a five-language national anthem.

## Evidence

- Hymn origin: [[682-south-africa-mandela-and-the-death-of-apartheid-part-6-glt6535658080]] says [[EnochSontonga]] wrote the song in 1897 while teaching and leading a Methodist school choir.
- Christian-political context: [[682-south-africa-mandela-and-the-death-of-apartheid-part-6-glt6535658080]] places the hymn in black South African Christian resentment of white missionary paternalism and claims about Christianity's African antiquity.
- Congress adoption path: [[682-south-africa-mandela-and-the-death-of-apartheid-part-6-glt6535658080]] says the song was sung at the 1912 Congress meeting and became the ANC anthem in 1925.
- Rugby final ceremony: [[682-south-africa-mandela-and-the-death-of-apartheid-part-6-glt6535658080]] says the Imilonji Kantu Choral Society from Soweto led the anthems at Ellis Park.
- Fused anthem: [[682-south-africa-mandela-and-the-death-of-apartheid-part-6-glt6535658080]] says the later national anthem combined Nkosi and [[DieStemVanSuidAfrika|Die Stem]].
