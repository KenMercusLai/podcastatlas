# Ayanna Thomas

## Overview
Ayanna Thomas is the psychologist guiding the [[HiddenBrain]] episode's account of reconstructive memory, imagination-driven source confusion, eyewitness caution, cognitive aging, and practical learning methods.

## Current Profile
In the bounded episode, Thomas treats all recollection as reconstruction rather than separating a perfectly preserved class of memories from a distorted class. She uses action-and-imagination experiments to show how repeated mental simulation can be misattributed to actual performance, then extends the lesson to photographs, family stories, leading questions, witness discussion, and remote-event disputes.

Her practical account is balanced rather than purely skeptical. The same prior knowledge and imagery that can distort recall can also organize learning through familiar locations, distinctive associations, active retrieval, spacing, and sleep. She also distinguishes possible age-related encoding changes from anxiety and stereotype threat that can depress performance without proving broad cognitive failure.

## Key Characteristics
- Presents memory as reconstructive, source-dependent, and vulnerable to post-event information.
- Separates confidence and vividness from independently established accuracy.
- Uses repeated-imagination experiments to make source confusion experimentally legible.
- Applies memory science cautiously to eyewitness and remote-event disputes without deciding contested facts.
- Connects associative memory mechanisms to retrieval practice, spacing, sleep, and mnemonic imagery.
