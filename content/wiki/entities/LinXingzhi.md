## Overview
林行止 / Lin Xingzhi is presented as the founder-editor of the [[HongKongEconomicJournal|《信报》]], a prolific financial commentator, and a participant in Hong Kong's postwar public sphere.

## Current Profile
The bounded source follows Lin from childhood displacement in Chaoshan and migration to [[HongKong|Hong Kong]] through factory and trading work, English night school, research and editing at Ming Pao, and the 1973 founding of 《信报》 with [[LuoYoumei|骆友梅]]. His distinctive role was not only to discuss stocks, but to translate economics, policy, institutions, and international affairs into readable Chinese for a growing professional and middle-class audience.

His claimed position, “不左不右，只求其是,” is presented as independence from competing political centers rather than neutrality without judgment. The episode also ties that independence to commercial conduct: he resisted advertiser pressure from [[LiKaShing|李嘉诚]] but apologized when a published criticism of Wu Jinglian proved factually inaccurate.

## Key Characteristics
- Combined self-directed economic study with sustained newspaper editing and high-frequency commentary.
- Built financial journalism around institutions and public affairs rather than price listings alone.
- Used accessible, wide-ranging prose to connect specialist knowledge with ordinary readers.
- Accepted political and advertiser conflict as costs of editorial independence.
- Treated factual correction as a necessary companion to independence.
- Worked through collaboration with editors, writers, and administrators rather than as a solitary founder.

## Qualifications
The page is bounded to one admiring retrospective. The source gives limited space to critics of Lin's market liberalism, and several childhood, circulation, advertising, relationship, and motive claims derive from recollection rather than independently checked archival evidence. It also corrects the common Cambridge University claim: the episode places his formal British study at a Cambridgeshire technology college.
