# Tarleton Gillespie

Tarleton Gillespie is the media scholar whose paper [[TheRelevanceOfAlgorithms]] provides the main framework for [[159-suanfa-de-liufu-miankong-ta-shi-ruhe-cong-chuli-shuju-biancheng-dingyi-women-shi-shui-de-lh5ng5mbvkworfxlq9l-coscepc7]]. [[DavidWeng|大卫翁]] uses Gillespie to shift the episode from a technical account of recommendation systems toward algorithm sociology: how algorithms classify, predict, rank, claim objectivity, become entangled with practice, and construct publics.

In the source, Gillespie matters because he names algorithms as social institutions, not only computational methods. His frame makes [[PublicRelevanceAlgorithms]] comparable to older media and knowledge systems: like editors, catalogues, indexes, and search engines, they decide what becomes visible and credible, while presenting that decision as technical order.

## Key Claims
- Gillespie gives the episode its six-part analytical structure.
- His usefulness is that he treats algorithms as arrangements of data, models, institutions, and legitimacy claims rather than as neutral code.
- The source uses him to connect [[PageRankSearchRelevance]], platform feeds, social classification, and identity formation into one media-theory branch.
