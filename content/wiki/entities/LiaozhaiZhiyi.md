## Overview

《聊斋志异》 is [[PuSongling|蒲松龄]]'s strange-tale collection and a literary field in which ghosts, foxes, corpses, marvels, folk skills, institutions, gender, war, and desire become moral and social tests.

## Current Profile

Across the preserved sources, the collection cannot be reduced to horror or fox romance. It stores war shadow, performance culture, exam satire, failed justice, folk religion, nonhuman moral agency, and emotional interiority. Supernatural figures often expose human violence or institutional blindness rather than merely threatening society.

The collection also serves different inquiries. It makes [[UnderworldBureaucracy]] narratable through divine exams and offices; supplies [[FictionAsHistoricalEvidence]] for fears and fantasies omitted by official archives; and provides adaptation material whose screen versions can change romance, domesticity, character, and visual tradition.

## Key Characteristics

- Combines moral instruction with sympathy for unruly human and nonhuman agents.
- Makes war, official failure, examination absurdity, and human cruelty part of supernatural fear.
- Preserves folk performance, social imagination, and institutional satire.
- Turns the afterlife into offices, exams, ledgers, appointments, and relationship-dependent judgment.
- Supports adaptations that may become more familiar than source plots or endings.

## Qualifications

The sources use the collection for literary criticism, folklore, microhistory, and film adaptation; these uses should not be collapsed into direct supernatural or historical fact. Specific tale details and adaptation comparisons remain bounded unless checked against primary texts.
