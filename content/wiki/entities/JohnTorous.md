# John Torous

## Overview
John Torous is the psychiatrist interviewed by [[MarketplaceTech]] about emotional support, mental-health information, long-term attachment, and clinical evidence around general-purpose AI chatbots.

## Current Profile
In the bounded source, Torous's main contribution is a two-level safety model. Some failures are immediately visible in a dangerous answer, while others emerge through a long relationship in which memory, agreeableness, emotional intensity, and perceived authority accumulate. He therefore argues for [[LongitudinalChatbotSafetyEvaluation]] grounded in real user experience and for more precise clinical reporting than the umbrella label [[AIPsychosis]].

His position is neither blanket endorsement nor blanket rejection. General-purpose chatbots are not presented as clinical tools, but basic psychoeducation, wellness information, or emotional support may have value for some users. Safety depends on recognizing warning signs, preserving human relationships, disclosing AI use to treatment providers, and distinguishing conversational reinforcement from the causes and diagnoses of mental illness.

## Key Characteristics
- Distinguishes immediately unsafe output from cumulative relationship-level harm.
- Prioritizes user-created, longitudinal tests over model-only evaluation pipelines.
- Treats agreeableness as a safety tradeoff when correction or escalation is needed.
- Calls for tool identity, logs, psychiatric history, and physical-state evidence in reported crises.
- Preserves a limited wellness role for AI while rejecting general-purpose chatbots as clinical authorities.
