# Dost Muhammad

## Overview
Dost Muhammad was the Afghan ruler displaced by the [[EastIndiaCompany|East India Company]] during the [[FirstAngloAfghanWar|First Anglo-Afghan War]] and restored after the intervention collapsed.

## Current Profile
[[88-the-first-anglo-afghan-war-glt5156371854]] presents Dost Muhammad as available for diplomacy rather than committed to [[Russia]]. [[AlexanderBurnes|Alexander Burnes]] reportedly judged that he had no inherent hostility to Britain and could be brought into alignment without invasion, but that assessment lost the bureaucratic contest.

His return after the war supplies its political verdict. The foreign-backed restoration of [[ShahShuja|Shah Shuja]] failed, while Dost Muhammad came back with added prestige from the defeat of an imperial intervention and an agreement to exclude Russian influence.

## Key Characteristics
- Afghan ruler who had displaced Shah Shuja before the British intervention.
- Presented as diplomatically flexible rather than naturally pro-Russian.
- Removed because British officials chose regime change over Burnes's proposed alignment.
- Held in Mussoorie during part of the conflict.
- Restored after the intervention, with resistance prestige strengthening his position.

## Evidence
- Diplomatic position: [[88-the-first-anglo-afghan-war-glt5156371854]] reports Burnes's view that Dost Muhammad could be aligned without removal.
- Displacement: [[88-the-first-anglo-afghan-war-glt5156371854]] identifies restoration of Shah Shuja as the invasion's political purpose.
- Return: [[88-the-first-anglo-afghan-war-glt5156371854]] says he was allowed back after agreeing not to admit Russian influence.

## Qualifications
The source offers little detail about Dost Muhammad's wider reign, domestic coalition, or independent diplomacy. Its portrayal is designed mainly to expose the intervention's avoidable logic.

## What Changed
- Established Dost Muhammad as the diplomatic alternative the invasion displaced and the ruler restored by its failure.
