## Overview

狄山 / Di Shan is a Western Han court erudite presented by Hanji 329 as a Confucian scholar who argues for renewed [[HanXiongnuHeqinPolicy|Han-Xiongnu heqin]], attacks [[ZhangTangWesternHan|张汤]], and dies after [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] assigns him to guard a northern frontier barrier.

## Current Profile

The current evidence supports a narrow court-and-frontier profile. Di Shan opposes continued force against [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] and cites earlier Han setbacks, but the debate turns personal after Wudi disparages him and Di attacks Zhang Tang as falsely loyal. Wudi then asks whether Di can govern a commandery, a county, or one barrier. Di denies the first two capacities and accepts the last under perceived mortal pressure; the episode says Xiongnu raiders kill him within a month.

The case therefore belongs to [[DirectRemonstranceCriminalization|直谏入罪]] without proving that Di's policy position was empty. His speaking style, administrative capacity, military competence, and the strategic merits of heqin are distinct questions that the punitive assignment collapses into one lethal test.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han erudite and Confucian scholar represented as favoring heqin over renewed anti-Xiongnu war.
- Blunt court critic whose policy disagreement escalates into a personal attack on Zhang Tang.
- Official who openly admits lacking commandery- and county-governance capacity.
- Coercively assigned frontier defender whose rapid death becomes a warning to other court speakers.

## Qualifications

This profile rests on one structured podcast summary. Di Shan's exact words, stutter, literary ability, formal office, the barrier's location, the interval before death, and Wudi's intent remain source-scoped. The episode's “only talks” judgment should not be used to infer that military or administrative skill is a prerequisite for valid policy criticism, nor does Di's death adjudicate the comparative strategic value of heqin and war.
