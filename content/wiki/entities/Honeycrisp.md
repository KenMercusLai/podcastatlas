# Honeycrisp

## Overview
Honeycrisp is the University of Minnesota apple variety presented as the breakout model for modern premium produce branding.

## Current Profile
The episode treats Honeycrisp as the proof that a university-bred fruit variety can become a mass consumer brand and a meaningful licensing asset. Released in 1991, it gave the [[UniversityOfMinnesota|University of Minnesota]] a per-tree licensing stream and more than $20 million in revenue. Honeycrisp therefore becomes the comparison point for [[ScarletSunrise|Scarlet Sunrise]] and the broader "honeycrispification" of the produce aisle.

## Key Characteristics
- Premium apple variety released by [[UniversityOfMinnesota|University of Minnesota]] in 1991.
- Became a consumer-recognized branded produce success.
- Generated more than $20 million for the university through licensing.
- Serves as the episode's benchmark for [[BrandedProduceDifferentiation]].
- Also shows possible market saturation when many apple varieties try to copy the premium-brand model.

## Evidence
- Release and ownership: [[branded-fruit-how-produce-got-honeycrisp-ified|Branded fruit]] identifies Honeycrisp as a University of Minnesota apple released in 1991.
- Licensing economics: [[branded-fruit-how-produce-got-honeycrisp-ified|Branded fruit]] says the university licensed it around a dollar per tree and made more than $20 million.
- Benchmark role: [[branded-fruit-how-produce-got-honeycrisp-ified|Branded fruit]] uses Honeycrisp as the model for why other produce developers seek distinctive names and protected varieties.

## Qualifications
Honeycrisp is used as a strong but not universally repeatable example. The source warns that premium apple choices may have multiplied enough to create consumer search costs.
