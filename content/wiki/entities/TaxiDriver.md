# Taxi Driver

## Overview

*Taxi Driver* is a 1970s New York film used by the bounded sources both as a product of urban-crisis imagery and as a warning portrait of alienation and violence.

## Current Profile

[[360-fear-city-new-york-in-the-1970s-glt7107047696]] places the film within a cluster that reflected and amplified the era's crime, decay, corruption, and civic fear. [[snap-judgement-japan-pms-electoral-landslide-6989c0fc61be18927058bb7d]] narrows that reading by treating the city largely through [[TravisBickle|Travis Bickle]]'s unstable perception rather than as neutral documentary evidence. The combined synthesis is that the film draws power from a real historical atmosphere while organizing that atmosphere through one alienated, racist, misogynistic, and violent subjectivity.

## Key Characteristics

- Its New York setting is grounded in the visual and emotional atmosphere of 1970s urban crisis.
- The city is filtered through Travis Bickle's unstable point of view.
- Travis's loneliness and resentment turn into gun attachment and violent recognition-seeking.
- Later admiration for Travis can invert a warning portrait into masculine iconography.
- The film both participates in and complicates the dystopian image of New York.

## Evidence

- Historical setting: [[360-fear-city-new-york-in-the-1970s-glt7107047696]] groups the film with other works emerging from the city's crisis atmosphere.
- Subjective-city reading: [[snap-judgement-japan-pms-electoral-landslide-6989c0fc61be18927058bb7d]] argues that the film's New York reflects Travis's mental landscape.
- Antihero reception: [[snap-judgement-japan-pms-electoral-landslide-6989c0fc61be18927058bb7d]] uses the mirror line and climactic violence to ground [[AntiheroMisreading]].

## Qualifications

The sources offer historical and cultural interpretation rather than a complete production history or reception study. The film should not be treated as a representative documentary record of all 1970s New York, and later audience admiration is not uniform.
