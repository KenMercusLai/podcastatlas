## Overview

东越 / Dongyue is the southeastern polity recognized under [[YuShanDongyueKing|余善]] after he kills [[MinyueKingYing|闽越王郢]] under Han pressure, then later destroyed when Yu Shan's attempted alignment with both Western Han and Nanyue becomes rebellion, internal defection, elite killing, surrender, and population removal.

## Current Profile

Hanji 256 supplies the current origin layer. After Ying attacks Nanyue, a Han expedition induces Yu Shan and other Minyue elites to remove him. Han Wudi first recognizes [[YaoKingChouMinyue|越繇王丑]] as inherited ritual successor, but Yu Shan's local following prevents Chou from ruling alone. Wudi then recognizes Yu Shan as Dongyue king because renewed war would be disproportionately costly. Dongyue therefore appears here as part of a divided post-crisis settlement rather than as a polity with a fully explained independent founding.

Hanji 355 supplies the transition from recognized rule to rebellion. Yu Shan offers troops for the Nanyue campaign but stops at Jieyang, secretly communicates with Nanyue, and later blocks Han routes after his nonarrival is reported. Dongyue therefore enters open war through a failed attempt to wait between two powers.

Hanji 356-2 presents Dongyue as terrain-favored but internally divided. [[WuYangYueMarquis|吴阳]] moves seven hundred followers to the Han side, while [[JianchengHouAo|建成侯敖]] and [[JuguDongyueKing|繇王居股]] kill Yu Shan and surrender their people.

The aftermath is more than a change of ruler. The source says [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] rewards participants and orders the regional population moved to the Yangtze-Huai area, leaving the old territory empty. The episode also shifts between Dongyue and [[Minyue|闽越]] language, so this page treats the two as historically connected labels without assuming they are interchangeable in every context.

## Key Characteristics

- Southeastern Yue polity recognized around Yu Shan's effective local power after a Minyue succession crisis.
- Dual-settlement participant beside Chou's inherited and ritual kingship.
- Polity whose ruler later promises Han support while retaining a covert Nanyue channel.
- Terrain-favored resistance target of Western Han forces.
- Politically divided community whose insiders defect and kill the ruler.
- Region subjected to post-conquest population removal.

## Qualifications

This profile rests on three short podcast sources with substantial transcription corruption. Exact movements, offices, names, rewards, the Dongyue-Minyue relation, and the scale of population removal require specialist verification. Hanji 256 clarifies a political sequence but does not establish the constitutional or territorial boundary between Chou's and Yu Shan's titles. Hanji 355's gambling diagnosis is interpretation; Hanji 356-2's population description is imperial justification, not a neutral group characteristic.

## What Changed

- Added Dongyue's recognition layer after Ying's death and distinguished Yu Shan's effective authority from Chou's ritual legitimacy.
- Reframed the later rebellion and destruction as the collapse of an earlier divided settlement.
