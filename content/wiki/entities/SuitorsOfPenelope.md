# Suitors of Penelope

## Overview

The Suitors of Penelope are the 108 aristocratic men occupying [[Odysseus]]' household in [[TheOdyssey]] as retold by [[688-the-odyssey-return-of-the-king-part-2-glt2882541084]].

## Current Profile

In the episode, the suitors are the collective pressure that makes homecoming a political and household crisis. They consume the estate, pressure [[Penelope]] to remarry, mock and assault the disguised Odysseus, fail the bow contest, and are killed when Odysseus reveals himself.

## Key Characteristics

- The suitors convert Odysseus' absence into a resource, marriage, and succession crisis.
- Their behavior violates the hospitality and household order that the episode treats as central to the poem.
- They underestimate the beggar disguise and cannot perform the bow test that would legitimate marriage to Penelope.
- Their slaughter is framed by the episode as narratively justified inside the aristocratic world while still brutal in modern reception.

## Evidence

- Household pressure: [[688-the-odyssey-return-of-the-king-part-2-glt2882541084]] says 108 suitors consume Odysseus' house and pressure Penelope to remarry.
- Abuse of the stranger: [[688-the-odyssey-return-of-the-king-part-2-glt2882541084]] describes Antinous throwing a footstool at the disguised Odysseus.
- Failure of legitimacy: [[688-the-odyssey-return-of-the-king-part-2-glt2882541084]] says all 108 suitors fail to string the bow before Odysseus succeeds.
- Violent end: [[688-the-odyssey-return-of-the-king-part-2-glt2882541084]] has Odysseus condemn the suitors and kill them with Telemachus, Athena, and loyal servants supporting the attack.

## Qualifications

This page treats the suitors as a collective actor because the episode emphasizes their shared function. Individual suitors such as Antinous are not separately profiled here unless future sources make them canonical subjects.
