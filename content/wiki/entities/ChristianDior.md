# Christian Dior

## Overview

Christian Dior is the French designer whose 1947 New Look collection serves here as the first major postwar rejection of wartime utility clothing.

## Current Profile

Dior's collection restored Paris as a source of international fashion authority after occupation and austerity. Narrow waists, sweeping skirts, curves, and conspicuous fabric use offered luxury and escapism, but this remained an elite Parisian intervention distinct from the youth-led boutique and mass-consumer system later associated with London.

## Key Characteristics

- Presented his first individual collection on 12 February 1947.
- Became associated with the American-coined label “New Look.”
- Rejected utility austerity through fabric, glamour, and a curving silhouette.
- Reasserted Parisian fashion leadership before London's youth-market rise.

## Qualifications

This profile reflects one conversational history episode and covers Dior only as a hinge between wartime utility and later youth fashion. It does not summarize his wider career, business, collaborators, or the full reception of the New Look.

## What Changed

- Created Dior as the Parisian luxury counterpoint to London's later youth-fashion system.
