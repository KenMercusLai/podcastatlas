# Battle of Bunker Hill

## Overview

The Battle of Bunker Hill was a costly British tactical victory near Boston that demonstrated the difficulty of suppressing the colonial rebellion.

## Current Profile

British forces retook commanding heights, but casualties approached a third of the troops engaged in the episode's account. The battle therefore exposed a mismatch between tactical success and political strategy: Britain could capture ground, yet such victories could consume scarce forces without restoring durable authority.

## Key Characteristics

- British assault on rebel-held commanding terrain near Boston.
- Tactical British success at exceptionally high human cost.
- Evidence that colonial forces could impose serious losses.
- Early demonstration that battlefield victory did not equal political control.

## Evidence

- Objective and outcome: [[348-the-boston-tea-party-part-2-glt5189620267]] says British troops retook the heights.
- Casualty burden: [[348-the-boston-tea-party-part-2-glt5189620267]] reports casualties of roughly one-third among about 3,000 British troops engaged.
- Strategic reading: [[348-the-boston-tea-party-part-2-glt5189620267]] uses the victory to show how difficult and costly the war would be.
