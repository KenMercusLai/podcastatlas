## Overview

赵兴 / Zhao Xing is presented as the young fourth king of [[Nanyue|南越]] who favors closer submission to Han with his mother [[QueenDowagerJiuNanyue|樛太后]] but cannot overcome chancellor [[LyuJiaNanyue|吕嘉]]. His death in Lü Jia's coup turns a contested incorporation policy into dynastic rupture and war.

## Current Profile

Hanji 352-1 places Zhao Xing between royal policy and locally rooted chancellor power. He and the queen dowager have reportedly agreed to attach Nanyue more closely to Han. At a banquet intended to confront Lü Jia, however, the Han envoys hesitate and Zhao Xing stops his mother from attacking the chancellor directly.

After a two-thousand-person Han force enters Nanyue, Lü Jia depicts the young king as captured by his Han-born mother and foreign envoys. Lü Jia's faction kills Zhao Xing, the queen dowager, and the envoys, then replaces him with [[ZhaoJiandeNanyue|赵建德]]. The host interprets Zhao Xing's conduct as fatal indecision; the source establishes the failed confrontation and violent removal, not the counterfactual that one killing would certainly have preserved his rule.

## Key Characteristics

- Fourth Nanyue king and son of Queen Dowager Jiu.
- Young ruler aligned with closer Han incorporation.
- Monarch whose authority does not neutralize Lü Jia's locally rooted power.
- Participant in a failed banquet confrontation with the chancellor.
- King killed and replaced during Lü Jia's coup.

## Evidence

- Incorporation policy and confrontation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-352-1-yi-pao-hong-mie-le-bainian-diguo-lgpuqijq5kyd5ivh8ajlclo8ojex|Hanji 352-1]] says Zhao Xing and his mother favor attachment to Han, but Lü Jia leaves the banquet alive after Zhao Xing intervenes.
- Propaganda vulnerability: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-352-1-yi-pao-hong-mie-le-bainian-diguo-lgpuqijq5kyd5ivh8ajlclo8ojex|Hanji 352-1]] says Lü Jia uses Zhao Xing's youth and his mother's Han origin to challenge the royal party's legitimacy.
- Death and replacement: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-352-1-yi-pao-hong-mie-le-bainian-diguo-lgpuqijq5kyd5ivh8ajlclo8ojex|Hanji 352-1]] says Lü Jia's faction kills Zhao Xing and installs Zhao Jiande.

## Qualifications

The profile is bounded to one popular-history summary. It does not reconstruct Zhao Xing's accession, independent policy formation, court coalition, exact role at the banquet, or wider support. “Indecision” is the host's moral interpretation; the source does not prove that killing Lü Jia was operationally feasible or would have produced peaceful, durable incorporation.

## What Changed

- Created a source-bounded profile separating Zhao Xing's failed authority from the host's counterfactual certainty.
- Located his death as the transition from disputed royal submission policy to replacement kingship and open war.

## Relationships

- [[QueenDowagerJiuNanyue|樛太后]] - mother and ally in the move toward closer Han incorporation.
- [[LyuJiaNanyue|吕嘉]] - chancellor who resists the royal policy, kills Zhao Xing, and replaces him.
- [[ZhaoJiandeNanyue|赵建德]] - successor installed by Lü Jia after the coup.
- [[Nanyue|南越]] - kingdom whose divided authority constrains Zhao Xing's rule.
- [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] - external ruler whose incorporation pressure helps define the court crisis.
