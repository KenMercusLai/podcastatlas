## Overview

赵兴 / Zhao Xing is the son and designated heir of [[ZhaoYingqiNanyue|赵婴齐]] and the Han-born [[QueenDowagerJiuNanyue|樛太后]]. He succeeds as the fourth king of [[Nanyue|南越]], favors closer submission to Han with his mother, but cannot overcome chancellor [[LyuJiaNanyue|吕嘉]]; his death in Lü Jia's coup turns a contested incorporation policy into dynastic rupture and war.

## Current Profile

Hanji 349-2 supplies Zhao Xing's succession setup. Zhao Yingqi asks the Han court to recognize Lady Jiu as queen and Zhao Xing as heir. When Zhao Yingqi dies, Zhao Xing succeeds and his mother becomes queen dowager. The episode treats their position as a possible opening for [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s long-term incorporation plan, not as a completed transfer of authority.

Hanji 352-1 then places Zhao Xing between royal policy and locally rooted chancellor power. He and the queen dowager reportedly agree to attach Nanyue more closely to Han. At a banquet intended to confront Lü Jia, however, the Han envoys hesitate and Zhao Xing stops his mother from attacking the chancellor directly.

After a two-thousand-person Han force enters Nanyue, Lü Jia depicts the young king as captured by his Han-born mother and foreign envoys. Lü Jia's faction kills Zhao Xing, the queen dowager, and the envoys, then replaces him with [[ZhaoJiandeNanyue|赵建德]]. The host interprets Zhao Xing's conduct as fatal indecision; the evidence establishes failed confrontation and violent removal, not the counterfactual that one killing would certainly have preserved his rule.

## Key Characteristics

- Court-recognized heir of Zhao Yingqi and Queen Dowager Jiu.
- Fourth Nanyue king after his father's death.
- Young ruler aligned with closer Han incorporation.
- Monarch whose authority does not neutralize Lü Jia's local power.
- Participant in a failed banquet confrontation with the chancellor.
- King killed and replaced during Lü Jia's coup.

## Qualifications

The profile is bounded to two popular-history summaries. They do not reconstruct Zhao Xing's age, independent policy formation, court coalition, exact role at the banquet, or wider support. Hanji 349-2 previews him as part of a Han-favorable succession arrangement, but later alignment does not prove that his accession was controlled by Han. “Indecision” is the host's moral interpretation; the evidence does not show that killing Lü Jia was operationally feasible or would have produced peaceful, durable incorporation.

## What Changed

- Added Zhao Xing's designation and accession as the planned heir of Zhao Yingqi and Lady Jiu.
- Reframed the later coup as the failure of a succession-based opening rather than an unexplained starting point.

## Relationships

- [[ZhaoYingqiNanyue|赵婴齐]] - father who requests his recognition as heir.
- [[QueenDowagerJiuNanyue|樛太后]] - mother and ally in the move toward closer Han incorporation.
- [[LyuJiaNanyue|吕嘉]] - chancellor who resists the royal policy, kills Zhao Xing, and replaces him.
- [[ZhaoJiandeNanyue|赵建德]] - successor installed by Lü Jia after the coup.
- [[Nanyue|南越]] - kingdom whose divided authority constrains Zhao Xing's rule.
- [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] - external ruler whose recognition and incorporation pressure define the court crisis.
