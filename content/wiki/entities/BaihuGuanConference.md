## Overview
The Baihu Guan Conference is the 79 CE court gathering where [[EmperorZhangOfHan|Emperor Zhang]] convened officials and Confucian scholars to debate differences in the Five Classics. It turned [[YangZhongEasternHan|Yang Zhong]]'s concern about fragmented scholastic commentary into an official state process associated with `白虎议奏` and, in the newer source, the received *Baihu Tongyi*.

## Current Profile
The conference follows [[YangZhongEasternHan|Yang Zhong]]'s recommendation that [[EmperorZhangOfHan|Emperor Zhang]] imitate Emperor Xuan's Shiquge precedent and use court authority to clarify Five Classics disagreements. Zhang orders generals, senior officials, doctors, palace attendants, and Confucian scholars to assemble at Baihu Guan. Wei Yingcheng asks the first questions, Chunyu Gong reports the discussions, and Zhang personally attends and records the proceedings.

Its political significance is not only scholastic. The bounded sources present the gathering as a Zhangdi-era method for binding classical learning to imperial governance: disagreement was routed through a court-managed forum, reporting, imperial review, and recorded conclusions. The newer source attributes compilation of the proceedings to [[BanGuEasternHan|Ban Gu]] and emphasizes doctrines concerning imperial status, relational ethics, and exceptions to universal subordination. Participants named across the sources include Ding Hong, Lou Wang, Cheng Feng, Huan Yu, Ban Gu, Jia Kui, and Guangping King Liu Xian.

## Key Characteristics
- It is a ruler-sponsored court conference, not a private scholarly exchange.
- It addresses disputed interpretations of the Five Classics through formal questioning, reporting, and imperial attendance.
- It uses precedent from Emperor Xuan's Shiquge discussions to legitimate a new Eastern Han classics forum.
- It produces a written outcome, `白虎议奏`, making debate part of institutional memory.
- It brings together officials, Confucian scholars, and royal kin, showing classics learning as a court-wide governance issue.
- Its received doctrinal legacy is presented as extending beyond textual interpretation into political status and social ethics.

- Scholastic problem: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-750-jiemi-donghan-wanmei-nvxing-beihou-manshi-xisheng-lu-96lbdh7r0tsmiopbqqrj0q1gt|Hanji 750]] frames [[YangZhongEasternHan|Yang Zhong]]'s proposal as a response to scholars who over-focused on annotations and lost the larger meaning of the classics.
- Institutional recording: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-750-jiemi-donghan-wanmei-nvxing-beihou-manshi-xisheng-lu-96lbdh7r0tsmiopbqqrj0q1gt|Hanji 750]] links the proceedings to `白虎议奏`, with Wei Yingcheng questioning first and Chunyu Gong reporting.
- Doctrinal scope: [[30-gaibian-lishi-de-guanjian-huiyi-wanhu-huiyi-vs-baihuguan-huiyi-ltuaquuhrwirgv2egihrk4vuo7cm]] connects the meeting to the *Baihu Tongyi* and highlights imperial title, relational norms, and recognized exceptions to subordination.

## Qualifications
The current evidence comes from two podcast summaries rather than the surviving proceedings and specialist textual scholarship. It does not settle the exact date, participant roles, legal force of particular conclusions, textual derivation of the received *Baihu Tongyi*, or the scale and path of its long-run social influence. Comparing the conference with [[WannseeConference|Wannsee]] isolates an institutional mechanism and does not imply moral or political equivalence.
