# Macbeth / 《麦克白》

## Overview

*Macbeth* is Shakespeare's tragedy of regicide, ambition, equivocation, witchcraft, and political fear, whose theatrical power largely displaced the historical Macbeth in public memory.

## Current Profile

[[104-macbeth-glt3313769671]] places the play around 1606 under [[JamesVIAndI|James VI and I]] and after the [[GunpowderPlot]]. Shakespeare inherits a chronicle Macbeth but adapts it to Jacobean concerns: Banquo becomes virtuous in deference to James's claimed ancestry, the witches align with James's demonological interests, and the porter scene's equivocation evokes treason and Henry Garnet.

The play turns [[MacbethHistoricalKing|a long-ruling historical king]] into a murderous usurper and [[Gruoch]] into Lady Macbeth, a durable archetype of the ambitious political wife. Later performance keeps remaking the work—from Orson Welles's 1936 Harlem “Voodoo Macbeth” to the supposed theatrical curse—while the episode qualifies both the surviving text's completeness and the antiquity of the curse tradition.

## Key Characteristics

- Jacobean tragedy shaped by royal patronage, succession, treason, and witchcraft anxiety.
- Major agent in replacing historical Macbeth with a theatrical tyrant.
- Regicide drama whose murder of Duncan remains offstage.
- Work of equivocation in which supernatural prophecy and human ambition reinforce one another.
- Source of a gendered political archetype through Lady Macbeth.
- Performance tradition marked by adaptation, textual uncertainty, and late superstition.

## Evidence

- Court and treason context: [[104-macbeth-glt3313769671]] links the King's Men, Banquo's altered role, the 1605 plot, Garnet, equivocation, and James's witchcraft beliefs.
- Historical transformation: [[104-macbeth-glt3313769671]] contrasts Duncan's battlefield death and Macbeth's long reign with the play's murder and tyranny.
- Performance afterlife: [[104-macbeth-glt3313769671]] discusses Pepys, Orson Welles, Shostakovich, modern casting, textual-shortening theories, and the late curse story.

## Qualifications

The episode does not establish a single cause for the play or prove direct influence from the Oxford nymph ceremony. The date, textual history, reasons for offstage regicide, audience response, and Max Beerbohm origin story remain interpretive or source-scoped.
