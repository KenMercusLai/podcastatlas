# Caroline Hoxby

## Overview

Caroline Hoxby is the economist whose research with [[ChristopherAvery]] is discussed in [[carlos-doesn-t-remember-carlos-doesn-t-remember|Carlos Doesn’t Remember]] to challenge the belief that selective colleges had already found nearly all high-achieving low-income applicants.

## Current Profile

The episode says Hoxby and Avery linked national SAT and ACT performance to information about schools, neighborhoods, and family circumstances. Their analysis is reported to identify roughly 35,000 students per year scoring at or above the 90th percentile while living in families earning less than $40,000, many of whom did not apply to selective institutions.

Within the episode, Hoxby's research changes the diagnosis from a shortage of talent to a failure of discovery and application. It supports [[CollegeApplicationUndermatch]], but Gladwell and Eisner add an upstream qualification: test-based estimates cannot count gifted children who have already left school or never reached the exams.

## Key Characteristics

- Economist associated with research on high-achieving low-income students.
- Uses linked achievement and contextual data to estimate an overlooked applicant pool.
- Challenges selective colleges' assumption that existing outreach had saturated that pool.
- Provides national-scale evidence that complements Carlos's individual case.
