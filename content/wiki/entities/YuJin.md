# 于禁 / Yu Jin

## Overview
于禁 / Yu Jin is a Cao-side commander whose current profile joins battlefield recovery after Wancheng, strict enforcement against the repeatedly rebellious [[ChangXiLateHan|昌豨]], and surrender after environmental catastrophe at Fancheng.

## Current Profile
Hanji 1013 and 1014 establish Yu Jin's crisis discipline. After [[CaoCao|曹操]] escapes Wancheng, Yu Jin defeats [[ZhangXiu|张绣]]'s continued pursuit, keeps his own troops controlled, suppresses plundering by Qingzhou soldiers, and builds defenses before answering their accusations. Cao Cao rewards him as Yishou tinghou because he protects operational order and civilians while trusting that evidence can clear his name.

Hanji 1076 adds a second kind of rule-bound conduct. When Chang Xi surrenders only after encirclement, other officers favor sending him to Cao Cao. Yu Jin instead invokes the stated non-pardon rule, rejects friendship as grounds for exception, bids farewell to Chang Xi, and executes him. Together the cases present discipline as priority ordering: immediate defense over self-vindication at Wancheng, and command law over private relationship in the Chang Xi case.

A reversal comes at the [[FanchengBattle219LateHan|Battle of Fancheng]]. Sustained rain and Han River flooding overwhelm the position of Yu Jin's seven armies; after [[GuanYu|关羽]] surrounds survivors by boat, Yu Jin surrenders. The episode contrasts this with [[PangDe|庞德]]'s continued resistance, but the current synthesis treats the choice under catastrophic defeat as a source-bounded event rather than proof that Yu Jin's earlier discipline was false. ## Key Characteristics
- Stabilizes retreat after Wancheng and helps prevent a local disaster from becoming total collapse.
- Keeps his own command ordered while suppressing looting by allied Qingzhou soldiers.
- Fortifies against continued pursuit before defending his personal reputation.
- Trusts the command system to distinguish evidence from an earlier accusation.
- Enforces the stated rule against post-encirclement surrender even against an old acquaintance.
- Combines visible emotion with refusal to let emotion change the command decision.
- Surrenders after flood destroys his Fancheng field position, creating a sharp late-career contrast with earlier crisis discipline.

## Qualifications
The page remains bounded to four podcast notes and does not reconstruct Yu Jin's full career. Hanji 1076's command wording, reinforcement sequence, farewell, and Cao Cao quotation remain source-scoped. Hanji 1166's weather, field disposition, force totals, surrender circumstances, and contrast with Pang De require broader comparison. Surrender under catastrophic defeat does not by itself invalidate earlier discipline, while the episode's moral ranking is not adopted as a universal rule.

## What Changed
- Added the flood-driven Fancheng defeat and surrender.
- Preserved the tension with earlier crisis discipline without converting one outcome into a total character judgment.
