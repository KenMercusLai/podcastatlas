# 杨晓丹 / Yang Xiaodan

## Overview
杨晓丹 / Yang Xiaodan is presented by the source as a licensed veterinarian focused on canine and feline oncology, with general small-animal clinical training, overseas continuing education, medical-course interpreting experience, and public-facing disease education.

## Current Profile
In the current source set, Yang's role combines clinician, specialty learner, translator between human and veterinary medicine, and public educator. She describes building expertise in a field with limited local mentorship by observing overseas specialists, reading human-oncology material, interpreting continuing-education courses, and adapting techniques cautiously to animal anatomy and evidence.

Her clinical posture is uncertainty-aware. She emphasizes pathology, staging, imaging, sampling, quality of life, owner constraints, and the possibility that a successful intervention may partly reflect luck or an unrecognized variable. She also rejects attention strategies that display suffering for traffic and criticizes unvalidated treatments or pet-communication claims that can replace professional decisions.

## Key Characteristics
- Practices in canine and feline oncology while treating strong general-practice foundations as necessary for tumor care across organ systems.
- Uses [[ComparativeMedicine]] to learn from human oncology without assuming human procedures or thresholds transfer directly to animals.
- Frames [[VeterinaryOncology]] around diagnosis, staging, pathology quality, patient burden, owner resources, and quality of life.
- Uses owner education as an indirect lever for raising pathology, referral, and clinical standards.
- Treats clinical success with epistemic caution, keeping complications, chance, and unknown mechanisms visible.
- Rejects suffering-based self-promotion and [[VeterinaryMedicalMisinformation|unsupported veterinary claims]] as substitutes for evidence or care.
