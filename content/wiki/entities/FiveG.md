# 5G

## Overview
5G is a mobile communications standard whose wiki profile combines standards-and-patent coordination with a broader service design spanning consumer broadband, reliable low-latency industrial communication, and massive machine connectivity.

## Current Profile
The sources position 5G as more than a faster consumer network. Its distinctive targets include industrial production, dense machine deployments, and time-sensitive live events, yet many benefits are less visible to ordinary phone users than download speed. The [[LasVegasGrandPrix]] case adds dense-crowd capacity, dynamic navigation, ticketing, payments, video, and telemetry, while sailing examples add real-time athlete and coaching data. Deployment still depends on spectrum, ownership, device integration, coverage density, power, economics, privacy, and fallback design as well as on the radio standard itself. Like earlier digital standards, 5G also relies on essential-patent and licensing structures that permit many firms to implement a common system.

## Key Characteristics
- Supports high-bandwidth human communication while expanding toward low-latency, high-reliability industrial links and massive machine connectivity.
- Creates user value unevenly because consumer perception does not capture many industrial or city-infrastructure use cases.
- Depends on standards coordination, essential patents, licensing rules, and antitrust guardrails.
- Faces industrial adoption constraints around private-network control, dedicated spectrum, cost, and Wi-Fi substitution.
- Carries network energy and coverage tradeoffs involving bandwidth, frequency, site density, and antenna systems.
- Supports dense live-event services and low-latency telemetry in the sponsored Las Vegas and sailing cases.

## Evidence
- Standards evidence: [[the-invention-invention]] names 5G as a modern communications standard built on patent-pool and essential-patent coordination patterns refined around MPEG.
- Service-profile evidence: [[tefan-cong-fengwo-wangluo-dao-shouji-geming-yangyang-tan-yidong-tongxin-langchao-sanshinian-lhbvza29-24szqust-nwtm0z09-4]] describes enhanced broadband, high-reliability low-latency production links, and massive machine connectivity as 5G's three main scenario groups.
- Deployment evidence: [[tefan-cong-fengwo-wangluo-dao-shouji-geming-yangyang-tan-yidong-tongxin-langchao-sanshinian-lhbvza29-24szqust-nwtm0z09-4]] identifies private-network preference and dedicated-spectrum access as barriers to industrial use.
- Energy evidence: [[tefan-cong-fengwo-wangluo-dao-shouji-geming-yangyang-tan-yidong-tongxin-langchao-sanshinian-lhbvza29-24szqust-nwtm0z09-4]] links 5G power demand to bandwidth, higher-frequency propagation, denser base stations, and multi-antenna systems.
- Live-event evidence: [[a-new-day-at-the-races-a-new-day-at-the-races]] describes a dense-crowd network supporting event applications, navigation, commerce, video, telemetry, and coaching-oriented sensor data.

## Qualifications
Reliability targets, device-density figures, frequency examples, base-station counts, energy claims, and deployment outcomes remain source-scoped. The Las Vegas source is a paid T-Mobile partnership and does not establish that 5G alone caused the described services or outperformed alternatives. The sources do not provide a comprehensive global comparison of standards releases, operator economics, spectrum regimes, privacy, failure modes, or measured industrial and event outcomes.

## What Changed
- Added dense live events, telemetry, and real-time sports participation to the service profile.
- Preserved the distinction between technical capability and independently measured deployment outcomes.
