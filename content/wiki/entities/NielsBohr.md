# Niels Bohr

## Overview

Niels Bohr appears as the physicist who escaped occupied Denmark, joined the Allied atomic effort, warned that Germany was pursuing a bomb, and argued that atomic knowledge ultimately required international openness rather than permanent national monopoly.

## Current Profile

In the episode, Bohr joins technical authority to political foresight. His information about [[NaziGermany|Nazi Germany]] reinforces the wartime race, while his internationalism anticipates the postwar problem: a secret powerful enough to reorder world politics is unlikely to remain contained within one state.

[[JRobertOppenheimer|Oppenheimer]] was receptive to that vision, whereas Churchill is described as hostile to it. Bohr therefore functions as an early bridge between the [[ManhattanProject|Manhattan Project]]'s military necessity and [[AtomicKnowledgeInternationalControl|international control of atomic knowledge]].

## Key Characteristics

- Refugee physicist incorporated into the Allied bomb effort after escaping Denmark.
- Source of warning that Heisenberg and Germany were working on an atomic bomb.
- Advocate of eventual openness and international governance for atomic knowledge.
- Influence on Oppenheimer's belief that scientific power required a wider political settlement.

## Evidence

- Wartime movement: [[343-oppenheimer-the-father-of-the-atom-bomb-part-1-glt9202576882]] traces Bohr from Denmark through Britain to Los Alamos.
- Threat information: [[343-oppenheimer-the-father-of-the-atom-bomb-part-1-glt9202576882]] attributes to him the warning that Heisenberg was working on a Nazi bomb.
- Internationalist proposal: [[343-oppenheimer-the-father-of-the-atom-bomb-part-1-glt9202576882]] presents him arguing that atomic knowledge should ultimately be shared to support international control.

## Qualifications

The episode compresses Bohr's scientific work, escape, wartime diplomacy, and proposals into a short narrative. His exact recommendations, contacts with political leaders, and influence on policy require documentary treatment beyond this source.
