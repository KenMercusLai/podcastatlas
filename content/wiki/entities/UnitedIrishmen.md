# United Irishmen

## Overview

The United Irishmen were an Irish reform and later revolutionary organization that sought parliamentary reform, Catholic emancipation, and an Irish parliament before helping organize the 1798 rebellion.

## Current Profile

The source places the organization within the American and French revolutionary transformation of the 1780s and 1790s. Its initial program crossed confessional lines and aimed to unite Catholics, Protestants, and dissenters around reform rather than reproduce a simple Catholic-nationalist bloc.

Suppression drove the organization underground in 1795 and toward French support. Wolfe Tone sought aid in Paris; weather stopped the 1796 expedition, and the 1798 rebellion mobilized about 50,000 rebels across several regions but ended with roughly 30,000 deaths. The defeat did not erase the movement's significance: it helped define a modern revolutionary nationalism while the British response produced the [[ActOfUnion1801|Act of Union]].

## Key Characteristics

- Began with parliamentary reform, Catholic emancipation, and an Irish legislature as central aims.
- Attempted a cross-confessional alliance of Catholics, Protestants, and dissenters.
- Became a secret revolutionary organization after suppression in 1795.
- Sought French military help through Wolfe Tone and other contacts.
- Helped organize the regionally dispersed and extremely lethal 1798 rebellion.
- Marks the emergence of a modern republican and anti-monarchical nationalism in the source.

## Qualifications

The episode stresses that later Catholic-nationalist and Protestant-unionist identities had not yet become absolute: Protestant dissenters were central to the United Irish project, and later Irish nationalism continued to include Protestant leaders. Mobilization and casualty totals are episode-level estimates rather than independently adjudicated figures here.

## What Changed

- Created a profile joining the movement's reform origins, cross-confessional ambition, revolutionary turn, French strategy, and 1798 defeat.
