## Overview

窦穆 / Dou Mu is the Dou-family son in Hanji 736 whose false use of dowager authority turns marriage alliance into a court-punished abuse of status. His page is bounded to the early Mingdi-era Dou-family branch and is not a full synthesis of later Dou-family power.

## Current Profile

Hanji 736 identifies Dou Mu as [[DouRongEasternHan|Dou Rong]]'s eldest son and the husband of Neihuang Princess. His central act is to falsely invoke [[YinLihua|Yin Taihou]]'s command in order to make Liu Xu of Liu'an divorce his original wife and marry Dou Mu's daughter.

The wife's family reports the coercion to court. [[EmperorMingOfHan|Mingdi]] is angry, removes Dou Mu and his brothers from office, and sends the Dou relatives who hold office back to their original commandery with their households. The source stresses that only Dou Rong remains in the capital, treating Dou Rong's standing as the reason the first punishment is not harsher.

Dou Mu's later fate is worse. After Dou Rong dies, Dou Mu and his sons Dou Xun and Dou Xuan are accused again, imprisoned, and executed. The page therefore treats Dou Mu as an early case of private family ambition borrowing imperial command language before elite protection disappears.

## Key Characteristics

- Dou Rong's eldest son and Neihuang Princess's husband in Hanji 736.
- Uses a false dowager command to coerce a royal-marriage rearrangement.
- Turns family alliance ambition into a public court offense.
- Receives initial office removal and forced return rather than immediate execution.
- Later dies in prison with his sons after renewed accusations.

## Qualifications

This page does not create separate canonical pages for Liu Xu, Neihuang Princess, Dou Xun, or Dou Xuan because Hanji 736 uses them as participants in Dou Mu's case rather than as independently developed subjects. The false-order incident is related to forged-edict abuse but is narrower than public-resource extraction.
