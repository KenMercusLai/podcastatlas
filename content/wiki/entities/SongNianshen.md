## Overview
宋念申 is a historian represented through conversations on Northeast Asian borders, layered urban space, archives, fieldwork, deindustrialization, public writing, and the limits of rigid categories.

## Current Profile
Song's method begins from bounded places such as Xita and the Tumen River, then asks how institutions, movement, memory, and material space changed their meaning. The border conversation connects ordinary crossing to identity documents, colonial partition, international law, capitalism, and disciplinary boundaries. The later [[Mianji|面基]] discussion makes the research practice more explicit: compare archives made by different powers, visit sites, ask concrete oral-history questions, find a line that organizes fragments, and write toward a real public without flattening complexity.

## Key Characteristics
- Historian of borders, cities, and Northeast Asian modernity in the represented sources.
- Reads places and borders as changing relationship networks rather than fixed containers.
- Uses ordinary lives and administrative cases to test abstract geopolitical categories.
- Combines cross-archive comparison, oral history, maps, images, and field observation.
- Historicizes legal, political, and regional concepts instead of treating present categories as timeless.
- Treats reader awareness, teaching, and narrative construction as part of historical responsibility.

- Public method: [[cong-dongbei-dao-dongya-lishi-shi-yige-guocheng-ren-yeshi-lgolueqq4aiwj4bg5jk-im4qhzc5]] links teaching and early editing work to question-led, reader-aware historical narrative.

## Qualifications
This profile is source-bound to two podcast conversations and does not attempt a complete biography, bibliography, or assessment of Song's scholarship. Comparisons across deindustrialized regions, claims about colonial knowledge and international law, and judgments about AI translation remain episode-level interpretations.
