# King Faisal

## Overview

King Faisal appears as the Saudi monarch who connected a generally pro-American and pro-British orientation to an increasingly explicit warning that unconditional U.S. support for Israel endangered access to cheap Arab oil.

## Current Profile

[[120-the-oil-weapon-glt6620754143]] places Faisal at the hinge between alliance and pressure. He initially appears more cautious than smaller producers such as Algeria and Libya, yet before and during the Yom Kippur War he warned U.S. oil executives and the American public that the political relationship could not be separated indefinitely from Palestine, Israel, and petroleum. His role helps explain why the [[OilCrisis1973|1973 oil crisis]] was not simply an economic cartel maneuver but a statecraft problem joining war, alliance, legitimacy, and energy dependence.

## Key Characteristics

- Saudi monarch with strong ties to the United States and Britain.
- Opponent of Israel whose regional and religious commitments constrained those Western relationships.
- Initially cautious relative to more militant oil-producing governments.
- Used warnings about oil access to press the United States over its support for Israel.
- Became a central figure in the episode's account of oil as geopolitical leverage.

## Evidence

- Alliance and caution: [[120-the-oil-weapon-glt6620754143]] describes Faisal as pro-American and pro-British while distinguishing him from earlier Algerian and Libyan pressure for an oil weapon.
- Political warning: [[120-the-oil-weapon-glt6620754143]] attributes to him warnings to American oil executives and television viewers that cheap oil and unconditional support for Israel could not both continue.
- Crisis role: [[120-the-oil-weapon-glt6620754143]] places Saudi action inside the October 1973 price, production, and embargo sequence.

## Qualifications

The bounded source is a short British popular-history account rather than a full biography or Saudi policy history. Its portrayal does not isolate Faisal's personal influence from decisions by other Arab producers, wartime developments, company negotiations, or wider Saudi institutions, and it should not turn distinct price, production, and embargo decisions into a single OPEC-wide act.

## What Changed

- Established Faisal as a cautious ally who nevertheless linked oil access to the Arab-Israeli conflict.
- Separated personal diplomatic warning from a simplistic image of automatic cartel action.
