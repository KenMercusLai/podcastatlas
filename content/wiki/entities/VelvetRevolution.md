# Velvet Revolution / 天鹅绒革命

## Overview

The Velvet Revolution was the 1989 Czechoslovak democratic transition remembered in the source set through institutional restoration, exile's unresolved aftermath, mass demonstration, and peaceful transfer of power.

## Current Profile

In the Kundera source, 1989 is the turning point after which the Czech entomologist in *Slowness* regains academic status while [[MilanKundera|Milan Kundera]] does not simply return to Czech belonging. Political liberalization can restore institutions and reputations without automatically repairing home, privacy, memory, or a writer's public image.

Its public setting is Wenceslas Square in [[Prague]], where mass demonstrations gathered before a peaceful government transfer. Pairing this with the same square's 1968 memory makes the revolution both a democratic break and a later answer to the suppression of the [[PragueSpring|Prague Spring]].

## Key Characteristics

- 1989 transition ending communist rule in Czechoslovakia through mass mobilization and peaceful transfer.
- Public event strongly associated in the source with Prague's Wenceslas Square.
- Institutional restoration that could reverse professional punishment without fully resolving exile or memory.
- Historical counterpart to the suppression of reform in 1968.

## Evidence

- Institutional and personal aftermath: [[12-man-dang-milan-kundela-yi-ju-zhengjing-meiyou-549335675]] contrasts restored academic status with Kundera's unresolved exile and public identity.
- Mass politics and transfer: [[no-234-jieke-youji-shuyu-chengbao-yinkuang-yu-jiuguan-de-boximiya-ditu-gkwrijiovueba7xf6gt0j48r]] locates demonstrations in Wenceslas Square and describes the subsequent peaceful handover.
- Urban historical pairing: [[no-234-jieke-youji-shuyu-chengbao-yinkuang-yu-jiuguan-de-boximiya-ditu-gkwrijiovueba7xf6gt0j48r]] places 1989 beside the square's 1968 invasion memory.

## Qualifications

The sources provide literary and travel-led uses of the revolution rather than a full account of organizations, leaders, negotiations, regional variation, Slovak participation, constitutional change, or subsequent state dissolution. “Peaceful” describes the transition's broad contrast with violent overthrow and should not erase coercion, uncertainty, or longer political conflict.
