# Animal Farm

## Overview

*Animal Farm* is [[GeorgeOrwell]]'s political fable about a farm revolution whose pigs become a new ruling elite.

## Current Profile

[[208-george-orwell-glt5823999588]] places the book in wartime Britain, when the Soviet Union was an ally and publishers were reluctant to accept an openly anti-Stalinist allegory. The episode treats the Soviet reference as central but not exhaustive: the farm also models [[RevolutionaryEliteReplacement]], in which the overthrow of one hierarchy creates a vacuum occupied by another.

The ordinary animals give the fable its populist moral center, while the pigs show how revolutionary language and authority can be captured. The episode presents that clarity, rather than exhaustive historical correspondence, as the source of the book's accessibility and durability.

## Key Characteristics

- Political fable written during Britain's wartime alliance with the Soviet Union.
- Soviet allegory that also supports a broader account of revolutionary replacement elites.
- Populist structure contrasting ordinary animals with pigs who consolidate rule.
- Compact narrative whose political force remains legible to younger readers.
- Publication history shaped by the diplomatic sensitivity of criticizing an ally.

## Evidence

- Wartime setting and publication: [[208-george-orwell-glt5823999588]] says Orwell began the book while Britain was allied with Stalin and that multiple publishers declined it.
- Revolutionary argument: [[208-george-orwell-glt5823999588]] presents the pigs as a new elite filling the vacuum created by revolution.
- Reception and form: [[208-george-orwell-glt5823999588]] reports strong British reviews outside communist outlets and emphasizes the work's populist accessibility.

## Qualifications

The episode is an interpretive discussion, not a textual edition or complete publication history. Reading the book as a general theory of revolution should not erase its specific Soviet and Stalinist targets. The identification of Old Major with Marx is presented as plausible, while a direct identification with Lenin is rejected by the guest.

## What Changed

- Established the book as both Soviet allegory and a broader warning about revolutionary elite replacement.

## Relationships

- [[GeorgeOrwell]] - author whose socialist anti-Stalinism shapes the fable.
- [[RevolutionaryEliteReplacement]] - general political mechanism embodied by the pigs' rise.
- [[SovietUnion]] - specific regime and revolutionary history targeted by the allegory.
- [[NineteenEightyFour]] - later Orwell work extending the warning from elite capture to total control of reality.
