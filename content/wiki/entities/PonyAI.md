# Pony.ai

[[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-why-ai-will-dwarf-every-tech-revolution-before-it-robots-manufacturing-ar-glasses-from-ces-2026-39655790]] adds Pony.ai to the [[CES]] 2026 self-driving discussion as part of the global race in [[PhysicalAI]]. The source names it with [[WeRide]], [[Waymo]], [[Zoox]], [[Tesla]], [[Baidu]], and others, using the cluster to argue that autonomy has become a manufacturing and deployment competition as well as an AI-capability competition.

Pony.ai appears in [[tech-20251229-1229-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20251229-1229-mp-tech-pod-128]] as one of the Chinese robotaxi companies [[KirstenKorosek]] names for 2026. The episode says Pony.ai is aiming for a global fleet of 3,000 robotaxis by the end of 2026.

[[acc532947b65-acc532947b65]] adds a direct operator view through [[ZhangNingPonyAI|张宁]]. The source frames Pony.ai's Robotaxi work as L4 service rather than advanced driver assistance: the company must handle [[AutonomousDrivingResponsibilityBoundary|system responsibility]], [[RobotaxiFleetOperations|fleet operations]], charging, maintenance, passenger incidents, dispatch density, lifecycle amortization, and overseas local regulation, not only autonomous-driving algorithms.

The source also says Pony.ai uses [[Nvidia]] Orin-series car-grade SoCs as a main compute platform and has optimized models enough to run L4 operations on a production automotive platform. It treats Pony.ai's business as moving from heavy self-operation toward exportable technology, regulatory coordination, and operations packages for partners where appropriate.

The source uses Pony.ai as a scale-ambition signal rather than as a technical teardown. In the wiki, it belongs beside [[WeRide]] and [[Momenta]] as evidence that robotaxi competition is not only about [[Waymo]]'s U.S. lead; global fleet growth, local regulation, and [[RobotaxiEconomics]] will decide whether expansion becomes a sustainable service.
