# Marc Breedlove

## Overview
Marc Breedlove is a neuroscientist represented in the wiki through a [[HubermanLab]] interview about how hormones and development relate to brain sexual differentiation, sexual behavior, and sexual orientation.

## Current Profile
Breedlove's profile is defined less by a single causal theory than by comparison across evidence types. He uses human correlational findings, rare developmental conditions, controlled rodent work, and naturally occurring ram partner preference to ask which aspects of later behavior might reflect prenatal organization, adult hormone activation, experience, or their interaction.

His interpretive stance is explicitly population-level and multifactorial. Prenatal testosterone may contribute to some developmental pathways, especially in selected female-orientation findings, but digit ratios, hearing measures, brain anatomy, birth order, and clinical conditions cannot classify individuals. He also separates sexual motor patterns from partner preference and emphasizes that animal findings do not automatically become accounts of human identity.

## Key Characteristics
- Studies hormonal influences on brain development and sex-typical behavior.
- Uses converging human, clinical, and animal evidence while keeping their inferential limits distinct.
- Treats sexual orientation as unchosen and developmentally multifactorial rather than reducible to one marker.
- Repeatedly distinguishes group-average association from individual prediction.
- Separates prenatal organizing effects from adult hormone activation and lifelong plasticity.
