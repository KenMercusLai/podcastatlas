# Shahar Azulay

## Overview
Shahar Azulay is the co-founder and CEO of [[Groundcover]], an observability company built around [[EBPFObservability]] and a bring-your-own-cloud architecture.

## Current Profile
In [[d5efc401]], Shahar appears as a technical founder who learned enterprise selling by doing the complete job himself. His product and go-to-market judgments are closely connected: kernel-level telemetry and customer-hosted data support a different cost model, while early demos, proofs of concept, discounted contracts, and replacement migrations taught the company what buyers needed before it could build a specialized sales organization.

## Key Characteristics
- Combines cybersecurity and engineering-management experience with observability product design.
- Treats [[FounderLedSales]] as a way to learn procurement, security, legal review, pricing, customer value, and product priorities.
- Personally handled prospecting, discovery, demonstrations, proofs of concept, pricing, negotiation, and closing during Groundcover's early growth.
- Remained involved in demonstrations and non-North-American sales after the first sales hires to stay close to customers and competitors.
- Advocates going deeply into any company function important enough to determine the outcome, even when the work resembles an SDR's job.
- Distinguishes early low-leverage deal making from the pricing confidence a more mature product, reference base, and replacement motion can support.

## Evidence
Technical and product judgment:
- [[d5efc401]] describes Shahar's cybersecurity background, early eBPF experiments, and role in developing Groundcover's customer-hosted observability architecture.

Sales learning and leadership:
- [[d5efc401]] says he sourced meetings, ran demos and proofs of concept, negotiated the first contracts, and continued selling while Groundcover learned how to recruit and train a commercial team.

Replacement execution:
- [[d5efc401]] shows him treating migration and verified incumbent removal as part of the customer outcome rather than assuming a signed contract completed the sale.
