# Claude Ullin

## Overview

Claude Ullin was [[HedwigUllmann]]'s grandson and the family researcher who reconstructed her dispersed collection and pursued the return of works lost around the family's flight from Nazi Europe.

## Current Profile

Late in life, Ullin examined family papers to identify works and trace their later locations. [[hedwig-s-lost-van-gogh-hedwig-s-lost-van-gogh|Hedwig’s Lost Van Gogh]] presents that search as a response to family rupture rather than a simple asset-recovery project. It led to claims involving museums in Toledo and Detroit, but courts ruled against the family on statutes of limitations and related legal grounds.

The source contrasts those defeats with the Oetker Group's voluntary return of a Hans Thoma panel once installed in Hedwig's dining room. The return affected Ullin deeply and occurred shortly before his death, making him a central example of how [[HolocaustEraArtRestitution|restitution]] can work as intergenerational acknowledgment even when litigation is unavailable or unsuccessful.

## Key Characteristics

- Descendant who used family papers to reconstruct a dispersed art collection.
- Restitution claimant whose museum cases encountered statutes-of-limitations defenses.
- Family historian motivated by rupture, memory, and healing as well as ownership.
- Recipient of a voluntarily returned Hans Thoma panel shortly before his death.
