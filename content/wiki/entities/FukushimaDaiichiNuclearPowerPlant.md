## Overview

The [[FukushimaDaiichiNuclearPowerPlant|Fukushima Daiichi Nuclear Power Plant]] is the Japan nuclear-accident and wastewater-discharge case added by [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-394-3-shenwa-lishi-heweiji-weihe-dou-fasheng-zai-riben-lspztkfaaq0hcq1no3yum1xdox8a]]. The episode uses it less as a technical reactor case than as an example of nuclear-risk aftermath becoming a regional, oceanic, and intergenerational responsibility problem.

## Current Profile

Hanji 394-3 says the Fukushima case begins with the 2011 earthquake and nuclear accident, then re-enters public argument through the later discharge of contaminated water into the sea. The source emphasizes explosions, raised accident severity, consumer panic, and public fear in neighboring countries before turning to the discharge as a case of [[NuclearRiskExternalization]].

The plant therefore functions in the wiki as a public-risk and responsibility node. It links nuclear infrastructure to [[NuclearDisasterMemory]], because the host narrates Fukushima beside Hiroshima, Nagasaki, and Chernobyl to argue that nuclear harm is remembered through both sudden catastrophe and long, uncertain aftermath.

## Key Characteristics

- Japan-based nuclear infrastructure used by the episode as the opening case for nuclear wastewater discharge controversy.
- Public-risk event whose perceived consequences extend beyond the plant through panic buying, regional fear, and ocean-discharge concerns.
- Source-scoped responsibility case in which local accident aftermath is framed as risk transfer to the world and future generations.
- Comparative node linking civilian nuclear accidents with nuclear-weapons memory and Chernobyl-style long-duration harm.

## Qualifications

This page records one podcast episode's account and rhetorical framing. It does not settle the technical composition of discharged water, the adequacy of monitoring, international regulatory findings, epidemiological effects, or the full Fukushima accident history. The episode is explicitly critical of Japan's discharge decision, so its discharge-volume and risk language should remain source-scoped until other source notes broaden the evidentiary base.
