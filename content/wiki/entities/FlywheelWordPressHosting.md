# Flywheel (WordPress Hosting)

## Overview
Flywheel was a WordPress hosting company co-founded by [[RickKnudtson]]. In the available source, it matters primarily as the prior startup whose scaling challenges, acquisition, and distributed workforce exposed the internal communications pain that later led to [[WorkshopInternalComms|Workshop]].

## Current Profile
The wiki currently knows Flywheel as Rick Knudtson's first major SaaS outcome. Its relevance is not only the exit itself but the operating environment it created: hundreds of employees, tens of thousands of customers, agency-oriented hosting workflows, and communication problems that became seed material for a later company.

## Key Characteristics
- Built as a WordPress hosting provider focused on designers, creative agencies, and freelancers.
- Founded around 2012 and sold in 2019, according to the source episode.
- Reached roughly 30,000 customers and more than 200 employees before or around the sale, as described in the interview.
- Created internal communications strain as the company grew and later joined WP Engine.
- Gave [[RickKnudtson]] credibility and capital access for Workshop, while leaving product-market fit for the second company unresolved.

## Evidence
- Company identity and market: [[rick-knudtson-workshop-the-email-signal-he-ignored-for-9-months]] describes Flywheel as WordPress hosting for creative agencies and adjacent users.
- Scale and exit: [[rick-knudtson-workshop-the-email-signal-he-ignored-for-9-months]] reports a 2019 sale with about 30,000 customers and more than 200 employees.
- Link to Workshop: [[rick-knudtson-workshop-the-email-signal-he-ignored-for-9-months]] traces the internal communications pain at Flywheel and WP Engine into Rick's next company.
- Founder-credibility effect: [[rick-knudtson-workshop-the-email-signal-he-ignored-for-9-months]] notes that the prior outcome helped Workshop raise seed funding but did not solve its market-selection problem.

## Qualifications
Flywheel's profile is source-scoped to Rick Knudtson's retrospective account in a podcast interview. The page does not yet incorporate independent acquisition records, customer metrics, or WP Engine integration details.
