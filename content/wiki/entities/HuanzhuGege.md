## Overview

《还珠格格》 / My Fair Princess is a Qiong Yao novel and television story represented here through a [[ZhiZuiJinMiFM|纸醉金迷FM]] retrospective on its first version's characters, relationships, comedy, and palace order.

## Current Profile

The story begins from [[XiaZiwei|夏紫薇]]'s search for her father but becomes a complementary two-woman narrative after [[Xiaoyanzi|小燕子]] is wounded and mistakenly recognized as [[QianlongEmperor|乾隆]]'s daughter. The mistaken identity persists not because nobody notices problems, but because different actors have reasons to believe, fear, conceal, investigate, or exploit it.

Its comedy and romance are integrated with hierarchy. Xiaoyanzi's language, improvisation, refusal of ritual distance, and treatment of servants as family let everyday conduct challenge court solemnity. Ziwei supplies a different center: she endures violence and displacement, interprets people carefully, and uses precise speech when a crisis cannot be solved by improvisation.

The episode also treats the work as unusually open to adult reinterpretation. The empress's suspicion can become intelligible without excusing torture or attempted killing; imperial consort Ling's help can contain both warmth and factional strategy; and Qianlong can be affectionate, perceptive, guilty, desirous, and coercively sovereign at once.

## Key Characteristics

- Mistaken-identity plot sustained by injury, guilt, expectation, fear, concealment, and palace faction rather than one flawless lie.
- [[DualFemaleProtagonistComplementarity|Complementary dual protagonists]] whose different capacities share the story's work.
- Comedy that makes hierarchy visible by disrupting speech, seating, kneeling, and official dignity.
- Relationships that combine attraction and character fit with practical narrative functions.
- Character construction rich enough to support changed judgments between childhood viewing and adult rewatching.

- [[lmjhsewrqbnqsy8d1ilyp_xo885e]] cites shared seating, resistance to kneeling, irreverent language, and the exposure of official absurdity as enacted rather than merely declared anti-feudal themes.

- [[lmjhsewrqbnqsy8d1ilyp_xo885e]] rereads the empress, Rong Momo, imperial consort Ling, and Qianlong through institutional position and motive while preserving moral distinctions among suspicion, factional maneuver, cruelty, and punishment.

## Qualifications

The current profile is bounded to one retrospective focused mainly on the older television version. Quiz answers are sometimes based on memory or live lookup, and several comparisons with the remake are evaluative rather than systematic. Claims about character motive, authorial design, casting, audience response, historical palace practice, and the work's anti-feudal meaning remain source-scoped. A fictional Qianlong should not be treated as direct evidence about the historical emperor.

## What Changed

- Established the work's mistaken-identity mechanism as motivated belief rather than simple gullibility.
- Defined Xiaoyanzi and Ziwei as complementary rather than rival protagonists.
- Connected comedy and everyday etiquette to the story's critique of hierarchy.

- [[XiaZiwei|夏紫薇]] - original daughter-seeker and stabilizing protagonist.
