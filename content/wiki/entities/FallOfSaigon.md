# Fall of Saigon

## Overview

The Fall of Saigon is the April 1975 collapse of South Vietnam and capture of its capital by North Vietnamese forces, represented in the source through the final U.S. Embassy evacuation and the arrival of troops at the Presidential Palace.

## Current Profile

The episode treats the event as more than a military endpoint. The embassy walls separated people protected by American evacuation capacity from Vietnamese civilians and allies left behind, making the collapse a visible test of promises accumulated during intervention. At the palace, [[DuongVanMinh|Dương Văn Minh]]'s attempt to surrender power was answered with the claim that no power remained to surrender.

Its afterlife extends through postwar repression and displacement and into [[VietnamWarAmericanMemory]], where the final evacuation became an image of American defeat, guilt, and contested national honor.

## Key Characteristics

- It completed the military and political collapse of South Vietnam.
- Its final hours joined organized American extraction to chaotic exclusion of Vietnamese allies.
- The embassy and palace supplied contrasting symbols of abandonment and extinguished sovereignty.
- Immediate participant testimony carried shame as well as relief.
- Its consequences continued through imprisonment, economic breakdown, refugee flight, and political memory.

## Qualifications

The page reflects a Western-hosted narrative that explicitly acknowledges its limited Vietnamese perspective. Exact timings, dialogue, crowd behavior, casualty and detention totals, and individual motives remain source-scoped. The fall ended South Vietnam's state power but did not end violence, displacement, regional conflict, or political argument over the war.
