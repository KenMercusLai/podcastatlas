## Overview

Li Shaojun / 李少君 is the fangshi whom [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-259-hanwudi-wo-ba-ni-dang-dashi-ni-ba-wo-dang-shazi-1-lqczbezelhdjrtxdrgsfcnmpoklx|Hanji 259 part 1]] places at the beginning of [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s sustained court pursuit of immortality. His current profile is source-bounded: the episode presents a deliberately constructed authority persona, but does not independently prove the mechanism or intent behind every reported feat.

## Current Profile

Li Shaojun reportedly claims extreme longevity and supports the claim with knowledge that appears independently checkable. He recalls an elderly man's grandfather hunting at a particular place and later identifies a bronze vessel as an object from Duke Huan of Qi's era before its inscription is examined. These successes turn antiquarian knowledge into evidence for an impossible age, giving him enough authority to supervise medicines at court.

His proposed path to long life joins several dependencies: sacrifice to the stove deity brings spirits; spirits enable cinnabar to become gold; gold vessels prolong life; prolonged life enables contact with [[AnQisheng|安期生]] at [[Penglai|蓬莱]]; and fengshan completes the Huangdi precedent. After Li Shaojun dies, Wudi treats an earlier dragon-ascent dream as evidence that death was transformation, so the claimant's disappearance strengthens rather than closes the search.

## Key Characteristics

- Builds an extraordinary-age persona from apparently verifiable knowledge of old events and objects.
- Claims exclusive personal access to An Qisheng, giant jujubes, Penglai, and a Huangdi route to immortality.
- Packages stove sacrifice, spirit summoning, material transformation, gold vessels, maritime encounter, and fengshan as one ordered program.
- Converts court belief into medicine responsibility, ritual practice, alchemical expenditure, and sea search.
- Remains influential after death because the patron reinterprets mortality as dragon transformation and ascent.

## Qualifications

The source is a popular-history podcast summary, not a complete biography or trial record. It does not establish Li's birth, true age, how he knew the hunt or bronze vessel, whether helpers supplied information, the exact office he held, the mechanics or results of the material work, or whether he consciously designed every step as deception. The episode's fraud framing is retained as an interpretation; the supernatural and longevity claims remain unverified.

## What Changed

- Created a bounded profile for Li Shaojun's credibility strategy and court program.
- Separated observable patronage and search from unverified immortality and material-transformation claims.
- Preserved uncertainty about intent and the mechanism behind his apparently accurate knowledge.
