# 张大白 / Zhang Dabai

## Overview

张大白 appears as a co-host of [[WeirdHistoryPodcast|怪东西 Weird History]] in comparisons of major earthquakes, outsider officials, chivalric traditions, and culturally consequential meals across Chinese and wider world history.

## Current Profile

In the earthquake episode, Zhang opens from personal experience of distant shaking near Beijing and helps move the discussion from immediate fear into historical comparison. In the guest-official episode, Zhang introduces a separate history program and helps frame [[LiSi|李斯]] and [[RobertHart|赫德]] through political opportunity, trust, and ruling interests. Episode 33 adds an intellectual-biographical entry: he recalls resisting the idea of a universal “literati dream of chivalry” after reading Chen Pingyuan, then later recognizing youxia as a historical social phenomenon distinct from fictional martial prowess. Episode 24 has him survey Chinese political and literary banquets before selecting the [[LantingGathering|Lanting Gathering]] as a thematic counterpart to the [[LastSupper]], then move from remembered school recitation toward adult reflection on aging and limited time.

The internal summary of episode 19 gives Zhang a childhood-reading and writing autobiography: access through a teacher's school library, magazines, comics, popular science, classical novels, [[ZhengYuanjie|郑渊洁]], and later used-book recovery leads into imitation, newspaper submission, film criticism, book reviewing, regular commissions, and longer work. Because the episode title instead names [[ZhangXiangrong|张向荣]], this extension remains summary-grounded rather than an identity merger.

## Key Characteristics

- Uses personal earthquake experience as an entry into historical analysis.
- Participates in a comparative rather than nationally isolated disaster narrative.
- Keeps attention on both institutional response and the cultural need to explain suffering.
- Uses paired lives to ask how political systems recruit, trust, and control outside expertise.
- Distinguishes historical social roles from later literary archetypes and tests cross-cultural resemblance against institutional difference.
- Uses Chinese banquet traditions and changing life experience to connect literary interpretation with mortality and present responsibility.
- Treats broad childhood reading, public submission, editorial feedback, and increasing length as a practical route into writing.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to five sources and does not establish Zhang's broader biography, expertise, publication record, or role across the full podcast. The separate program promoted at the start of episode 40 and his changing personal responses to wuxia and *Lantingji Xu* are recorded only as source-level context. Episode 19's title identifies Zhang Xiangrong while its internal summary identifies Zhang Dabai; this page records only the latter attribution and does not merge the entities. Banquet anecdotes, Lanting politics, textual authenticity, book memories, publication history, and market claims remain source-scoped.
