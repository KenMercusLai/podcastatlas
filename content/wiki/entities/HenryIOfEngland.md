# Henry I of England

## Overview

Henry I of England appears as the Anglo-Norman king whose own opportunistic accession, legitimacy construction, and loss of his son William Atheling set up [[EmpressMatilda|Matilda]]'s succession crisis.

## Current Profile

The episode frames Henry as a ruler who understood that kingship had to be actively made. He took power quickly after William Rufus's death, secured coronation and treasury control, and strengthened legitimacy by marrying Matilda of Scotland, who linked him to the Anglo-Saxon royal line.

After the [[WhiteShipDisaster|White Ship disaster]], Henry's problem became dynastic rather than merely personal. He remarried in search of another son, then designated Matilda as heir when no son appeared. His strategy used baronial oaths and Matilda's remarriage to Geoffrey of Anjou, but the episode presents this as only partially successful because it could not by itself overcome doubts about female rule or Stephen's later first-mover advantage.

## Key Characteristics

- Younger Norman king whose own accession shows succession rules were not fixed.
- Legitimacy builder who used coronation, treasury control, birth timing, and marriage.
- Father of many illegitimate children but only two legitimate children in the episode's frame.
- Ruler whose succession plan collapsed after William Atheling's death.
- Attempted to bind nobles to Matilda through oaths.
- Treated Matilda as both heir and bridge to future male descendants.

## Evidence

- Opportunistic accession: [[empress-matilda-civil-war-and-the-fight-for-the-throne-glt3399401666]] says Henry moved quickly after William Rufus died and secured coronation and treasury control.
- Legitimacy construction: [[empress-matilda-civil-war-and-the-fight-for-the-throne-glt3399401666]] links Henry's claim to being born after the Conquest and to marriage with Matilda of Scotland.
- Heir-loss crisis: [[empress-matilda-civil-war-and-the-fight-for-the-throne-glt3399401666]] says William Atheling's death left Henry with Matilda as his only legitimate child.
- Succession planning: [[empress-matilda-civil-war-and-the-fight-for-the-throne-glt3399401666]] says Henry required oaths to Matilda and married her to Geoffrey of Anjou.

## Qualifications

This profile is limited to Henry's succession relevance. It does not reconstruct his wider administrative, legal, Norman, or military reign beyond the episode's legitimacy and heir-management frame.

## What Changed

- Created Henry I as the ruler whose own legitimacy tactics and heir-loss crisis make Matilda's claim possible but fragile.
