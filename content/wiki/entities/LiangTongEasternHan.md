## Overview

梁统 / Liang Tong is currently synthesized as an early Eastern Han official whose profile joins a no-return break from [[WeiXiaoEasternHan|隗嚣]], Hexi incorporation, and postwar legal order. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-703-donghan-kaiguo-hou-zui-xianhe-de-jiazu-pingsha-shi-ta-lgg-fra8ostp2xsuugph6o-ilzac|Hanji 703]] now supplies the earliest current layer: Liang Tong fears hesitation, kills Wei Xiao's envoy, severs the relationship, and discards Wei Xiao's general seal and ribbon. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-714-shi-shui-di-yi-ge-han-chu-nan-er-dang-ma-ge-guo-shi-lpkqxrl3dsok4ui0lij4c3x8zfpi|Hanji 714]] then places him in the [[DouRongEasternHan|Dou Rong]] Luoyang-entry branch as one of the officials reassigned by [[LiuXiu|光武帝刘秀]], while [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-716-luanshi-zhizhong-lvfa-you-duo-zhongyao-lqxuinbrl-rop8d9yxgwx9z4fr4i|Hanji 716]] develops him as Taizhong dafu, a Legalist-minded figure, and the main advocate for selecting stable penal statutes after lenient death-penalty commutations had, in his view, weakened respect for law.

## Current Profile

Hanji 703 supplies Liang Tong's earliest current placement. He fears that hesitation will leave his alignment unclear while Wei Xiao's side is being pressured, so he kills Wei Xiao's envoy Zhang Xue, cuts off the relationship, and discards the general seal and ribbon Wei Xiao had given him. The current synthesis treats this as an [[IrreversibleDefectionBinding|irreversible defection binding]] signal: Liang Tong makes return to Wei Xiao politically impossible before the later Luoyang redistribution.

Hanji 714 supplies Liang Tong's next placement. After Dou Rong and five commandery governors enter Luoyang, Liu Xiu appoints Dou Rong, Liang Tong, [[KongFenEasternHan|Kong Fen]], and [[RenTingEasternHan|Ren Ting]] into new roles. Liang Tong is named as Taizhong dafu in this integration branch before Hanji 716 makes his legal agenda visible.

Liang Tong's profile in the current wiki remains conceptually centered on law. He does not appear as a battlefield actor or factional court broker; he appears through a policy claim about how a newly stabilized state should make violence, murder, and official judgment costly enough to deter disorder.

His argument has two layers. First, he reads late Western Han commutations under Emperors Yuan and Ai as evidence that excessive leniency can teach people to discount punishment. Second, after [[DuLinEasternHan|Du Lin]] objects to dense and harsh law, Liang answers that he is asking for "moderation" rather than arbitrary cruelty. That makes him a source case for [[PenalSeverityExecutionTradeoff|penal severity and execution tradeoff]] rather than a simple caricature of harsh punishment.

## Key Characteristics

- Early Eastern Han official whose current profile begins with an explicit break from Wei Xiao's camp.
- Hexi-linked official placed in Liu Xiu's Dou Rong personnel-redistribution branch.
- Actor who turns wavering affiliation into a public no-return signal by killing an envoy and discarding a seal.
- Taizhong dafu and leading legal voice in the later penal-statute debate.
- Legalist-minded advocate who treats clear, stable punishment as a tool for restoring postwar order.
- Critic of routine death-penalty commutation when it weakens deterrence for murder and serious crime.
- Moderation claimant who says punishment should be neither too light nor too heavy.

## Qualifications

The current page still does not give a full biography. Hanji 703 supplies a forceful break-from-Wei action but does not establish Zhang Xue's broader identity, the exact legal framing of the envoy killing, or Liang Tong's full prior relationship to Wei Xiao. Hanji 714 supplies office-placement context rather than a detailed action by Liang Tong. Hanji 716 reports Liang Tong's legal position and the host's approving interpretation, but it does not prove that Liu Xiu enacted Liang's preferred revision. Because the debate is shelved, Liang should be treated as a durable legal-position source rather than as the maker of a completed Guangwu code.
