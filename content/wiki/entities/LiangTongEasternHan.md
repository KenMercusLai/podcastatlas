## Overview

梁统 / Liang Tong is currently synthesized as an early Eastern Han official whose profile joins Hexi incorporation and postwar legal order. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-714-shi-shui-di-yi-ge-han-chu-nan-er-dang-ma-ge-guo-shi-lpkqxrl3dsok4ui0lij4c3x8zfpi|Hanji 714]] now places him in the [[DouRongEasternHan|Dou Rong]] Luoyang-entry branch as one of the officials reassigned by [[LiuXiu|光武帝刘秀]], while [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-716-luanshi-zhizhong-lvfa-you-duo-zhongyao-lqxuinbrl-rop8d9yxgwx9z4fr4i|Hanji 716]] develops him as Taizhong dafu, a Legalist-minded figure, and the main advocate for selecting stable penal statutes after lenient death-penalty commutations had, in his view, weakened respect for law.

## Current Profile

Hanji 714 supplies Liang Tong's earlier current placement. After Dou Rong and five commandery governors enter Luoyang, Liu Xiu appoints Dou Rong, Liang Tong, [[KongFenEasternHan|Kong Fen]], and [[RenTingEasternHan|Ren Ting]] into new roles. Liang Tong is named as Taizhong dafu in this integration branch before Hanji 716 makes his legal agenda visible.

Liang Tong's profile in the current wiki remains conceptually centered on law. He does not appear as a battlefield actor or factional court broker; he appears through a policy claim about how a newly stabilized state should make violence, murder, and official judgment costly enough to deter disorder.

His argument has two layers. First, he reads late Western Han commutations under Emperors Yuan and Ai as evidence that excessive leniency can teach people to discount punishment. Second, after [[DuLinEasternHan|Du Lin]] objects to dense and harsh law, Liang answers that he is asking for "moderation" rather than arbitrary cruelty. That makes him a source case for [[PenalSeverityExecutionTradeoff|penal severity and execution tradeoff]] rather than a simple caricature of harsh punishment.

## Key Characteristics

- Early Eastern Han official identified in the source as Taizhong dafu and as a leading legal voice.
- Hexi-linked official placed in Liu Xiu's Dou Rong personnel-redistribution branch.
- Legalist-minded advocate who treats clear, stable punishment as a tool for restoring postwar order.
- Critic of routine death-penalty commutation when it weakens deterrence for murder and serious crime.
- Moderation claimant who says punishment should be neither too light nor too heavy.
- Source case for the tension between mercy toward offenders and protection of law-abiding people.

## Qualifications

The current page still does not give a full biography. Hanji 714 supplies office-placement context rather than a detailed action by Liang Tong. Hanji 716 reports Liang Tong's legal position and the host's approving interpretation, but it does not prove that Liu Xiu enacted Liang's preferred revision. Because the debate is shelved, Liang should be treated as a durable legal-position source rather than as the maker of a completed Guangwu code.
