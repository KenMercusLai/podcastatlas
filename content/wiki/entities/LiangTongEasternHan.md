## Overview

梁统 / Liang Tong is currently synthesized as an early Eastern Han legal official whose memorial turns postwar law into a deterrence and order problem. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-716-luanshi-zhizhong-lvfa-you-duo-zhongyao-lqxuinbrl-rop8d9yxgwx9z4fr4i|Hanji 716]] presents him as Taizhong dafu, a Legalist-minded figure, and the main advocate for selecting stable penal statutes after lenient death-penalty commutations had, in his view, weakened respect for law.

## Current Profile

Liang Tong's profile in the current wiki is narrow but conceptually important. He does not appear as a battlefield actor or factional court broker; he appears through a policy claim about how a newly stabilized state should make violence, murder, and official judgment costly enough to deter disorder.

His argument has two layers. First, he reads late Western Han commutations under Emperors Yuan and Ai as evidence that excessive leniency can teach people to discount punishment. Second, after [[DuLinEasternHan|Du Lin]] objects to dense and harsh law, Liang answers that he is asking for "moderation" rather than arbitrary cruelty. That makes him a source case for [[PenalSeverityExecutionTradeoff|penal severity and execution tradeoff]] rather than a simple caricature of harsh punishment.

## Key Characteristics

- Early Eastern Han official identified in the source as Taizhong dafu and as a leading legal voice.
- Legalist-minded advocate who treats clear, stable punishment as a tool for restoring postwar order.
- Critic of routine death-penalty commutation when it weakens deterrence for murder and serious crime.
- Moderation claimant who says punishment should be neither too light nor too heavy.
- Source case for the tension between mercy toward offenders and protection of law-abiding people.

- Stable-code request: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-716-luanshi-zhizhong-lvfa-you-duo-zhongyao-lqxuinbrl-rop8d9yxgwx9z4fr4i|Hanji 716]] says Liang asks Liu Xiu to have officials select legal provisions and fix a code not easily altered.
- Moderation response: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-716-luanshi-zhizhong-lvfa-you-duo-zhongyao-lqxuinbrl-rop8d9yxgwx9z4fr4i|Hanji 716]] says Liang answers Du Lin by stressing "适中" and by denying that his proposal is mere harshness.

## Qualifications

The current page rests on one source note. Hanji 716 reports Liang Tong's legal position and the host's approving interpretation, but it does not give a full biography or prove that Liu Xiu enacted Liang's preferred revision. Because the debate is shelved, Liang should be treated as a durable legal-position source rather than as the maker of a completed Guangwu code.

## What Changed

- Created Liang Tong as a canonical Eastern Han entity from Hanji 716.
- Framed him as a deterrence-and-moderation legal thinker rather than only as an advocate of harsh punishment.
- Kept the unpassed code-revision request distinct from later implemented land-registration punishments.
