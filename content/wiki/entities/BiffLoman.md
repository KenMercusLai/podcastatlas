# Biff Loman / 彼夫·洛曼

## Overview
Biff Loman is [[WillyLoman|Willy Loman]]'s elder son in [[DeathOfASalesman|《推销员之死》]], whose movement from athletic promise and paternal worship toward disillusionment and ordinary self-knowledge supplies the play's main alternative to Willy's success creed.

## Current Profile
The source treats Biff's discovery of Willy's affair in Boston as a collapse of both paternal authority and personal direction. He keeps the event from his mother, drifts among jobs, steals, and repeatedly damages upward paths; the hosts interpret this as possible resistance to becoming like his father, while keeping the motive inferential.

Biff's eventual admission that he is not exceptional is painful but potentially liberating. His preference for open air, nature, and farm work becomes evidence of desire discovered beneath parental and social expectation, not proof that one occupation is universally more authentic than another.

## Key Characteristics
- Former school athlete shaped by his father's promise of exceptional success.
- Son whose discovery of paternal betrayal destroys an idealized model.
- Adult marked by job instability, secrecy, theft, and possible self-sabotage.
- Character who begins separating personal preference from inherited ambition.
- Counterpoint to Happy, who continues rather than questions the father's project.

## Qualifications
The source's account of Biff's silence, theft, and self-sabotage is an interpretation rather than a clinical explanation. His preference for farm work should not be turned into a universal opposition between authentic nature and false city work.
