# Malta

## Overview

Malta appears in the wiki through two narrow but distinct branches: a U.S.-Malta retirement-account tax controversy and a modern national-courage association involving the George Cross.

## Current Profile

[[the-leaked-tapes-that-show-how-the-rich-avoid-taxes]] presents Malta as the treaty counterparty and retirement-account jurisdiction behind the [[MaltaTaxLoophole]]. The source emphasizes the mismatch between U.S. Roth IRA restrictions and Maltese retirement structures that, in its account, permitted much broader contributions and asset types.

The symbolic-history branch centers on the George Cross. Malta's flag bears the cross awarded in 1942 for civilian courage during the Second World War, making the country a modern example of how George-associated imagery can signify collective bravery without reducing the national story to medieval England.

## Key Characteristics

- Small European jurisdiction whose domestic rules can interact consequentially with international treaties.
- Counterparty in the U.S.-Malta treaty controversy over tax-favored retirement accounts.
- National flag bearer of the George Cross awarded for wartime civilian courage.
- Modern case linking St George's name to courage outside an exclusively English setting.

## Evidence

- Tax-jurisdiction role: [[the-leaked-tapes-that-show-how-the-rich-avoid-taxes]] connects Maltese retirement structures and the bilateral treaty to a contested tax-avoidance strategy.
- Treaty clarification: [[the-leaked-tapes-that-show-how-the-rich-avoid-taxes]] reports that U.S. and Maltese officials narrowed the disputed interpretation in late 2021.
- George Cross symbolism: [[462-st-george-dragon-slayer-glt5334319363]] highlights the 1942 award and its place on Malta's flag as an emblem of civilian courage.

## Qualifications

Neither source supplies a general history of Malta. The tax episode treats the country chiefly as a jurisdictional node, while the St George episode uses the flag as one example in a global symbolic afterlife. The sources do not establish that the George Cross exhausts Maltese wartime memory or national identity.
