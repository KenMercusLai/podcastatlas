# Jean de Mailly

## Overview

Jean de Mailly was an early thirteenth-century chronicler who supplied one of the earliest surviving accounts of a woman becoming pope.

## Current Profile

In [[403-the-mystery-of-the-pregnant-pope-glt1981561492]], Jean's version presents a disguised woman rising from secretary to cardinal and pope, then being exposed in childbirth and violently punished. The hosts think he probably believed the tale and understood it as morally meaningful rather than as modern documentary history.

## Key Characteristics

- Early surviving transmitter of the [[PopeJoan]] legend.
- Presented advancement through clerical office before public exposure.
- Used dragging and stoning to turn the story into exemplary punishment.

## Evidence

- Narrative position: [[403-the-mystery-of-the-pregnant-pope-glt1981561492]] identifies Jean's account as early thirteenth century, long after the alleged pontificate.
- Moral structure: [[403-the-mystery-of-the-pregnant-pope-glt1981561492]] reports a public birth followed by violent punishment.

## Qualifications

The source does not establish Jean's sources or exact compositional motives. Its suggestion that he believed the story is interpretive, and his late testimony does not provide evidence that Joan existed.

## What Changed

- Created a source-bounded profile of Jean as an early transmitter of the legend.
