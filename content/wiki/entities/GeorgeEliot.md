# George Eliot / 乔治·艾略特

## Overview

George Eliot is the pen name of the woman writer, translator, and critic discussed as the author of [[Middlemarch|《米德尔马契》]].

## Current Profile

The bounded episode presents Eliot as a linguistically and intellectually connected Victorian writer whose work joins provincial marriage plots to political, medical, industrial, religious, and social change. It treats her translation of David Strauss's historical-critical life of Jesus as useful context for Casaubon's obsolete theological project, while qualifying the familiar claim that a male pen name can be explained only by discrimination against women writers.

## Key Characteristics

- Wrote under a male pen name after establishing work as a translator and critic.
- Uses provincial domestic life to examine public institutions and historical change.
- Places female aspiration and moral agency inside the constraints of marriage, property, religion, and vocation.
- Draws intellectual context from languages, criticism, and controversial German theology.
- Creates characters whose mixed motives resist simple moral sorting.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to one podcast reading rather than a complete biography or study of Eliot's corpus. Claims about her motives for adopting the name, intellectual network, languages, and use of Strauss remain source-attributed; the page does not reduce her fiction to biography.
