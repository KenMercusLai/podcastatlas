# Ray Dalio

## Overview

Ray Dalio is an investor associated in the wiki with [[BridgewaterAssociates]], [[RiskParity]], all-weather allocation, macro cycles, and the translation of monetary-system risk into portfolio design.

## Current Profile

Across the bounded sources, Dalio is useful less as a strategy to copy than as an example of worldview-shaped investing. The Chinese investment sources place his all-weather approach inside [[InvestmentWorldviewFit]], warn through [[InvestorIdolRisk]] that ordinary investors may lack his constraints and holding capacity, and cite his gold guidance as support for a persistent defensive sleeve.

In [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-ray-dalio-our-system-is-in-jeopardy-debt-ai-the-cycle-that-destroyed-rome-40289280]], Dalio links U.S. debt, domestic division, great-power conflict, technology, and natural shocks through [[FiveForcesSystemicCycle]]; favors a three-part fiscal adjustment toward a roughly 3%-of-GDP deficit; treats gold as non-credit money and a diversifier; remains skeptical of Bitcoin as an established safe haven; and separates AI's technological success from company profitability through [[TechnologyCompanyPerformanceGap]].

## Key Characteristics

- Uses recurring historical mechanisms to connect monetary, political, geopolitical, technological, and natural forces.
- Treats diversification and survivability as responses to uncertainty rather than as precise forecasting tools.
- Distinguishes gold's monetary and diversification roles from tactical price chasing.
- Separates technological transformation from company survival and investor return.
- Presents fiscal stabilization as a coordinated problem of spending, taxes, rates, growth, and political legitimacy.
- Is treated by the bounded sources as an analytically useful but non-copyable investment master.

## Qualifications

The bounded sources do not evaluate Dalio's investment record, forecast accuracy, Bridgewater operations, or the empirical performance of all-weather portfolios. Allocation ranges and fiscal targets are source-scoped guidance rather than universal recommendations. His five-force stages and historical analogies are broad interpretive models without weights or falsification criteria in the episode, while budget, asset, central-bank, technology, and political claims are not independently verified by the supplied note.
