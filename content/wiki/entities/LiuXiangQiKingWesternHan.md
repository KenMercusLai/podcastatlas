## Overview

齐王刘襄 / Liu Xiang is the Qi king presented as the first royal mobilizer against the Lü faction after Lü Zhi's death, the preferred imperial candidate of his brother [[LiuZhangChengyangKingWesternHan|刘章]], and the regional ruler who first had to seize control of his own kingdom's command structure.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-213-gei-liubang-dai-lvmao-de-shenyiqi-xiachang-ruhe-2-lntutfvfj3vdirbrjxlg1d5vsiip|Hanji 213]] uses Liu Xiang to explain the political debt behind [[LiuXingjuWesternHan|刘兴居]]'s rebellion. Liu Xiang reportedly raised forces in Qi while Liu Zhang acted in Chang'an, but senior ministers installed [[LiuHeng|刘恒]] instead. The episode interprets the later territorial settlement as offering the Qi branch less than its contribution and expectation seemed to warrant.

Liu Xiang's death is important in the source chiefly because it removes one of Liu Xingju's close political supports. The host speculates that imperial pressure may have contributed, but supplies no evidence of an order, plot, or direct causal mechanism.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-210-lingnan-shishang-diyi-wen-baowendi-shu-1-ltdb8eivlaosovnin7toa18mllso|Hanji 210 part 1]] independently closes by recording Liu Xiang's death and crediting him as the initiator who first set the anti-Lü mobilization in motion. The episode's praise of him as one of Liu Bang's most capable grandsons is an evaluation rather than a comparative finding.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-205-nvhuangdi-lvzhi-jingran-yin-gou-er-si-2-ltgwfdhn9cdwphudy0blyh36fge1|Hanji 205 part 2]] now supplies the mobilization prequel. Liu Zhang secretly asks Liu Xiang to move from Qi while promising inside support with [[LiuXingjuWesternHan|刘兴居]]. Liu Xiang's initial obstacle is internal: chancellor [[ZhaoPingQiChancellor|赵平]] rejects the plan and surrounds the royal palace, but [[WeiBoWesternHan|魏勃]] redirects Zhao's troops, after which Liu Xiang appoints [[SiJunWesternHan|驷钧]], Wei Bo, and [[ZhuWuWesternHan|祝午]] to senior offices and raises forces.

The same source grounds Liu Xiang's capacity in his descent from [[LiuFeiQiKing|刘肥]] and in Qi's early territorial scale, then presents territory removed under the Lü regency as accumulated grievance. These details make the action a combination of dynastic claim, regional coercive capacity, internal office control, and public restoration language; they do not prove every alleged Lü intention or the exact coordination described.

## Key Characteristics

- Qi king in the immediate post-Lü succession crisis.
- Early mobilizer against the Lü faction in the episode's account.
- Preferred throne candidate of Liu Zhang rather than the ministers' eventual choice, Liu Heng.
- Political reference point for the Qi branch's later reward grievance.
- Death becomes part of Liu Xingju's isolation, without an established imperial-causation case.
- Remembered as an initiator of the anti-Lü mobilization rather than only a claimant passed over in the succession.
- Regional ruler who neutralizes internal ministerial resistance before mobilizing outside the capital.

Qi command and territorial base:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-205-nvhuangdi-lvzhi-jingran-yin-gou-er-si-2-ltgwfdhn9cdwphudy0blyh36fge1|Hanji 205 part 2]] says Wei Bo overcomes Zhao Ping's resistance, Liu Xiang reassigns senior offices, and Qi's inherited scale and later territorial losses shape his position.

Afterlife in the Jibei crisis:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-213-gei-liubang-dai-lvmao-de-shenyiqi-xiachang-ruhe-2-lntutfvfj3vdirbrjxlg1d5vsiip|Hanji 213]] treats his death as one condition increasing Liu Xingju's fear and political isolation.

- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-210-lingnan-shishang-diyi-wen-baowendi-shu-1-ltdb8eivlaosovnin7toa18mllso|Hanji 210 part 1]] records his death and attributes the opening move of the anti-Lü struggle to him.

## Qualifications

This remains a source-scoped profile. It does not reconstruct Liu Xiang's full campaign, later negotiations, territorial settlement, date or cause of death. Hanji 205 part 2 announces but does not analyze flaws in its Shiji-derived narrative, so the alleged Lü coup, household intelligence, exact dates, office titles, speeches, and coordination remain provisional. Hanji 213's suggestion that Liu Heng's pressure contributed to Liu Xiang's death is interpretive and does not establish responsibility; Hanji 210 part 1 adds no causal evidence. Its superlative assessment of Liu Xiang's ability also remains the host's judgment.

## Relationships

- [[LiuZhangChengyangKingWesternHan|城阳王刘章]] - brother and capital ally who preferred Liu Xiang for the throne.
- [[LiuXingjuWesternHan|济北王刘兴居]] - royal relative whose later grievance is framed through the Qi branch's settlement.
- [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]] - rival succession outcome chosen by senior ministers.
- [[JibeiRebellionWesternHan|刘兴居济北叛乱]] - later crisis for which Liu Xiang's contribution and death supply background.
- [[WeiBoWesternHan|魏勃]] - adviser and general who neutralizes internal resistance to mobilization.
- [[ZhaoPingQiChancellor|赵平]] - chancellor who opposes the mobilization and loses the Qi command contest.
- [[LiuFeiQiKing|刘肥]] - father whose large Qi grant supplies Liu Xiang's dynastic and territorial base.
- [[PostRegencyInsideOutsideMobilization|摄政终结后的内外合击动员]] - coalition mechanism linking Qi forces to internal capital allies.
