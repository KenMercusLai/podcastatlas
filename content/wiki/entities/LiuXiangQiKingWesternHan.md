## Overview

齐王刘襄 / Liu Xiang is the Qi king presented as the first royal mobilizer against the Lü faction after Lü Zhi's death, the preferred imperial candidate of his brother [[LiuZhangChengyangKingWesternHan|刘章]], and the regional ruler who first had to seize control of his own kingdom's command structure.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-213-gei-liubang-dai-lvmao-de-shenyiqi-xiachang-ruhe-2-lntutfvfj3vdirbrjxlg1d5vsiip|Hanji 213]] uses Liu Xiang to explain the political debt behind [[LiuXingjuWesternHan|刘兴居]]'s rebellion. Liu Xiang reportedly raised forces in Qi while Liu Zhang acted in Chang'an, but senior ministers installed [[LiuHeng|刘恒]] instead. The episode interprets the later territorial settlement as offering the Qi branch less than its contribution and expectation seemed to warrant.

Liu Xiang's death is important in the source chiefly because it removes one of Liu Xingju's close political supports. The host speculates that imperial pressure may have contributed, but supplies no evidence of an order, plot, or direct causal mechanism.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-210-lingnan-shishang-diyi-wen-baowendi-shu-1-ltdb8eivlaosovnin7toa18mllso|Hanji 210 part 1]] independently closes by recording Liu Xiang's death and crediting him as the initiator who first set the anti-Lü mobilization in motion. The episode's praise of him as one of Liu Bang's most capable grandsons is an evaluation rather than a comparative finding.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-205-nvhuangdi-lvzhi-jingran-yin-gou-er-si-2-ltgwfdhn9cdwphudy0blyh36fge1|Hanji 205 part 2]] now supplies the mobilization prequel. Liu Zhang secretly asks Liu Xiang to move from Qi while promising inside support with [[LiuXingjuWesternHan|刘兴居]]. Liu Xiang's initial obstacle is internal: chancellor [[ZhaoPingQiChancellor|赵平]] rejects the plan and surrounds the royal palace, but [[WeiBoWesternHan|魏勃]] redirects Zhao's troops, after which Liu Xiang appoints [[SiJunWesternHan|驷钧]], Wei Bo, and [[ZhuWuWesternHan|祝午]] to senior offices and raises forces.

The same source grounds Liu Xiang's capacity in his descent from [[LiuFeiQiKing|刘肥]] and in Qi's early territorial scale, then presents territory removed under the Lü regency as accumulated grievance. These details make the action a combination of dynastic claim, regional coercive capacity, internal office control, and public restoration language; they do not prove every alleged Lü intention or the exact coordination described.

The expansion and pause between mobilization and the capital coup now have a fuller account. Liu Xiang has [[ZhuWuWesternHan|祝午]] lure [[LiuZeLangyeKingWesternHan|琅邪王刘泽]] to Linzi, detains him, and absorbs Langye's troops before attacking Jinan and issuing a wider anti-Lü call. When [[GuanYing|灌婴]] advances from Chang'an, he stops at [[YingyangWarringStates|荥阳]] rather than defeat Qi for the Lü faction, and Liu Xiang withdraws to await events. The host reads the deception and Liu Zhang's message as evidence of a possible accession project, but this establishes political ambiguity rather than Liu Xiang's private intention. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-206-zhu-lv-an-liu-shishang-diyi-miemen-an-1-liolyhn4i9_nuhxbm_srpomoxaxb|Hanji 206 part 1]]

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-207-liubang-xiaoyizi-lvxu-bei-gunsha-1-lsrs42rb8-qref1eevqdnhe3mufv|Hanji 207 part 1]] supplies both demobilization and rejection. [[LiuZhangChengyangKingWesternHan|刘章]] brings news of the capital victory and asks Liu Xiang to stop the Qi campaign. In the ensuing succession discussion, Liu Xiang's descent and contribution are not enough: ministers reportedly treat his uncle [[SiJunWesternHan|驷钧]] as a renewed outer-relative threat and choose [[LiuHeng|刘恒]] instead. This adds a concrete risk rationale to the existing record of being passed over, but it remains the episode's account of ministerial reasoning.

## Key Characteristics

- Qi king and early outside mobilizer in the immediate post-Lü succession crisis.
- Regional ruler who neutralizes internal resistance and coercively absorbs Langye's troops before moving west.
- Restoration claimant whose methods and reported messages leave accession ambition source-scoped rather than settled.
- Preferred throne candidate of Liu Zhang but rejected because ministers reportedly fear Si Jun's outer-relative power.
- Coalition contributor who pauses at Guan Ying's Xingyang line and later demobilizes after the capital victory.
- Political reference point for the Qi branch's later reward grievance and Liu Xingju's isolation.
- Death remains unexplained; claims of imperial pressure do not establish direct causation.

## Evidence

Anti-Lü role and succession claim:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-213-gei-liubang-dai-lvmao-de-shenyiqi-xiachang-ruhe-2-lntutfvfj3vdirbrjxlg1d5vsiip|Hanji 213]] says Liu Xiang first raised forces in Qi and that Liu Zhang expected him to become emperor.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-205-nvhuangdi-lvzhi-jingran-yin-gou-er-si-2-ltgwfdhn9cdwphudy0blyh36fge1|Hanji 205 part 2]] gives the inside-outside message chain, Liu Xiang's appeal to other princes, and the restoration justification.

Qi command and territorial base:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-205-nvhuangdi-lvzhi-jingran-yin-gou-er-si-2-ltgwfdhn9cdwphudy0blyh36fge1|Hanji 205 part 2]] says Wei Bo overcomes Zhao Ping's resistance, Liu Xiang reassigns senior offices, and Qi's inherited scale and later territorial losses shape his position.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-206-zhu-lv-an-liu-shishang-diyi-miemen-an-1-liolyhn4i9_nuhxbm_srpomoxaxb|Hanji 206 part 1]] says Liu Xiang detains Liu Ze, absorbs Langye's troops, attacks Jinan, and calls for wider action.

Campaign pause and disputed aim:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-206-zhu-lv-an-liu-shishang-diyi-miemen-an-1-liolyhn4i9_nuhxbm_srpomoxaxb|Hanji 206 part 1]] reports Guan Ying's refusal to strengthen the Lü faction through battle, Liu Xiang's withdrawal to await events, and the host's interpretation that the Qi action may have sought accession as well as restoration.

Afterlife in the Jibei crisis:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-213-gei-liubang-dai-lvmao-de-shenyiqi-xiachang-ruhe-2-lntutfvfj3vdirbrjxlg1d5vsiip|Hanji 213]] treats his death as one condition increasing Liu Xingju's fear and political isolation.

Death and retrospective assessment:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-210-lingnan-shishang-diyi-wen-baowendi-shu-1-ltdb8eivlaosovnin7toa18mllso|Hanji 210 part 1]] records his death and attributes the opening move of the anti-Lü struggle to him.

Demobilization and succession rejection:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-207-liubang-xiaoyizi-lvxu-bei-gunsha-1-lsrs42rb8-qref1eevqdnhe3mufv|Hanji 207 part 1]] says Liu Zhang asks Qi to stop its campaign and reports that ministers reject Liu Xiang because Si Jun appears capable of reproducing Lü-style outer-relative domination.

## Qualifications

This remains a source-scoped profile. It does not reconstruct Liu Xiang's full campaign, demobilization mechanics, succession deliberation, territorial settlement, date or cause of death. Hanji 205 part 2 announces but does not analyze flaws in its Shiji-derived narrative, while Hanji 206 part 1 supplies a skeptical interpretation without independently proving accession intent. The alleged Lü coup, household intelligence, detention of Liu Ze, troop absorption, proclamations, exact dates, offices, speeches, and coordination remain provisional. Hanji 207 part 1 does not independently verify Si Jun's conduct or prove that outer-relative risk was the sole reason for rejecting Liu Xiang. Hanji 213's suggestion that Liu Heng's pressure contributed to Liu Xiang's death is interpretive and does not establish responsibility; Hanji 210 part 1 adds no causal evidence. Its superlative assessment of Liu Xiang's ability also remains the host's judgment.

## What Changed

- Added Liu Xiang's deceptive detention of Liu Ze and absorption of Langye's troops as the coercive expansion layer of Qi's mobilization.
- Qualified the restoration account with the host's source-scoped accession-ambition reading.
- Added Guan Ying's Xingyang non-engagement and Liu Xiang's temporary withdrawal before the capital outcome.

## Relationships

- [[LiuZhangChengyangKingWesternHan|城阳王刘章]] - brother and capital ally who preferred Liu Xiang for the throne.
- [[LiuXingjuWesternHan|济北王刘兴居]] - royal relative whose later grievance is framed through the Qi branch's settlement.
- [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]] - rival succession outcome chosen by senior ministers.
- [[JibeiRebellionWesternHan|刘兴居济北叛乱]] - later crisis for which Liu Xiang's contribution and death supply background.
- [[WeiBoWesternHan|魏勃]] - adviser and general who neutralizes internal resistance to mobilization.
- [[ZhaoPingQiChancellor|赵平]] - chancellor who opposes the mobilization and loses the Qi command contest.
- [[LiuFeiQiKing|刘肥]] - father whose large Qi grant supplies Liu Xiang's dynastic and territorial base.
- [[PostRegencyInsideOutsideMobilization|摄政终结后的内外合击动员]] - coalition mechanism linking Qi forces to internal capital allies.
- [[SiJunWesternHan|驷钧]] - maternal uncle whose reported conduct becomes the reason ministers reject Liu Xiang's candidacy.
- [[LiuZeLangyeKingWesternHan|琅邪王刘泽]] - allied prince whom Liu Xiang reportedly detains while absorbing Langye's troops.
- [[GuanYing|灌婴]] - opposing commander whose Xingyang pause prevents the regional campaign from becoming an immediate battle.
