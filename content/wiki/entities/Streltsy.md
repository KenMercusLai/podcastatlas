# Streltsy / 射击军

## Overview

The Streltsy were an old Russian infantry institution and political force whose armed interventions shaped [[PeterTheGreat|Peter the Great]]'s accession and later became the target of torture, public execution, and institutional destruction. The [[MoscowUprising1682|1682 Moscow uprising]] first made them succession brokers; the 1698 revolt then supplied Peter's pretext for abolition.

## Current Profile

The corps began as a hereditary standing force with genuine grievances over abusive officers and uncompensated private labor. In 1682, dynastic actors amplified those grievances with claims that Ivan had been murdered and Orthodoxy threatened. The Streltsy entered the Kremlin, killed Naryshkin-aligned officials and relatives, and forced a settlement making Ivan and Peter joint tsars under [[SophiaAlekseyevna|Sophia]]'s regency.

Peter secured Streltsy loyalty during the 1689 defeat of Sophia, yet his childhood exposure to the 1682 violence made the relationship deeply unstable in the episode's interpretation. Complaints before the [[GreatEmbassy|Great Embassy]] led to exemplary executions, and the 1698 march on Moscow convinced Peter that the Streltsy represented both armed disobedience and a wider conservative conspiracy.

After loyal forces defeated the revolt, Peter reopened the case through dedicated torture chambers. Extracted confessions were used to implicate Sophia and justify executions staged across Moscow and outside her convent. The corps was then disbanded, removing an autonomous military-political constituency and strengthening Peter's personal command.

## Key Characteristics

- The corps combined military function with recurring political leverage in Moscow.
- Real service grievances made it vulnerable to dynastic mobilization and rumor.
- Its relationship with Peter shifted from tactical loyalty to suspected existential threat.
- The 1698 revolt was defeated before Peter returned, but he expanded punishment afterward.
- Torture converted uncertain conspiracy claims into confessions and public spectacle.
- Institutional abolition was part of consolidating autocratic control, not only punishing individual mutineers.

## Qualifications

The two episodes cover separate crises: the 1682 succession uprising and the 1698 revolt. The later source says Sophia almost certainly did not know of the 1698 revolt, so torture-derived claims of a coordinated restoration plot should not be treated as settled fact. Responsibility for the 1682 rumor network, exact numbers, execution details, Peter's personal participation, and the broader institutional history remain source-scoped.
