# Operation Frequent Wind

## Overview

Operation Frequent Wind was the final U.S. helicopter evacuation from Saigon as South Vietnam collapsed in April 1975.

## Current Profile

In the episode, the operation combined detailed contingency signals and repeated helicopter flights with severe scarcity and exclusion. “White Christmas” and a weather announcement activated the plan, but access to the embassy compound, American evacuation priority, limited aircraft space, and the order to remove remaining personnel left many Vietnamese allies outside.

The operation therefore appears as both effective emergency logistics and evidence of failed political obligation. Removing the last Marines ended the American physical presence but did not resolve the dependency and promises produced by the intervention.

## Key Characteristics

- It used a coded radio signal to activate final evacuation movement.
- Helicopter capacity and official priorities imposed a strict rescue hierarchy.
- Embassy crowd control included force against Vietnamese civilians seeking entry.
- Washington ultimately compelled Ambassador [[GrahamMartin|Graham Martin]] to leave.
- The final Marine extraction ended with Vietnamese civilians still without evacuation.
