# Bertrand du Guesclin

## Overview

Bertrand du Guesclin is the French commander whom [[CharlesVOfFrance|Charles V]] appoints constable for ability and uses to shift the [[HundredYearsWar|Hundred Years' War]] from English-favored open battle toward attritional recovery.

## Current Profile

The episode defines du Guesclin chiefly through strategic contrast. English victories at Crécy and Poitiers depended on battles that French armies repeatedly accepted under unfavorable conditions. Du Guesclin instead avoids offering another decisive engagement and helps recover territory through an [[AttritionalReconquest|attritional strategy]].

His appointment is also presented as evidence of Charles V's practical state rebuilding: command ability matters more than pedigree when the object is to reverse territorial loss without repeating earlier battlefield disasters.

## Key Characteristics

- French commander elevated to constable under Charles V.
- Appointment framed as merit over pedigree.
- Avoider of open battle against English strengths.
- Practitioner of attrition and incremental territorial recovery.
- Military counterpart to French fiscal and administrative rebuilding.

## Evidence

- Appointment: [[321-hundred-years-war-a-storm-of-swords-part-4-glt1570526345]] says Charles chooses du Guesclin for ability rather than birth.
- Strategic method: [[321-hundred-years-war-a-storm-of-swords-part-4-glt1570526345]] says he avoids open battle and favors attrition.
- Result: [[321-hundred-years-war-a-storm-of-swords-part-4-glt1570526345]] places that strategy inside the collapse of most English holdings.

## Qualifications

The source provides a strategic summary rather than campaign-by-campaign evidence. Du Guesclin's individual contribution cannot be separated here from Charles V's revenues, local loyalty, English fiscal problems, disease, and treaty politics.
