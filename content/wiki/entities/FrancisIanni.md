# Francis Ianni

## Overview

Francis Ianni was a Columbia University sociologist and anthropologist whose long-term study of a New York Mafia family examined organized crime as an intergenerational family enterprise and possible route toward conventional legitimacy.

## Current Profile

After meeting lawyer Philip Alcamo in 1964, Ianni gained unusual access to a major crime family while also maintaining relationships with police studying organized crime. His 1972 book *A Family Business* used the pseudonym “Lupollo”; the episode identifies the organization as probably the Lucchese family and its patriarch “Giuseppe” as probably Tommy Lucchese.

The study's durable contribution is generational. By 1970, the episode says, only four of 42 fourth-generation family members remained in criminal businesses, while others entered medicine, law, academia, finance, universities, affluent neighborhoods, and respectable institutions. Ianni therefore interpreted crime not simply as rejection of the American Dream but sometimes as an intense, excluded pursuit of it.

## Key Characteristics

- Combined sociology and anthropology in the study of organized-crime families.
- Gained long-term access through lawyer Philip Alcamo.
- Treated criminal organization as a family business rather than only a sequence of violent acts.
- Tracked occupational and status change across generations.
- Interpreted legitimacy as a possible long-term family aspiration.

## Qualifications

The source summarizes one episode's account rather than the full book, field notes, research ethics, later scholarship, or a representative study of organized-crime families. The Lucchese and Tommy Lucchese identifications are presented as Gladwell's interpretation, and one family's generational trajectory cannot establish a universal path from crime to legitimacy.
