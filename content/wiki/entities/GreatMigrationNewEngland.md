# Great Migration to New England

## Overview

The Great Migration to New England was the episode-described movement of roughly 20,000 [[Puritans]] to [[MassachusettsBayColony]] between 1629 and 1643.

## Current Profile

Migration was expensive and consequential: households sold property, financed Atlantic passage, and often expected never to return to England. The movement's family composition, social breadth, and concentration around an existing chartered colony support the interpretation that migrants intended a permanent commonwealth rather than a temporary extractive outpost.

Many migrants came from East Anglia and Kent, including tradespeople, teachers, lawyers, landholders, and other literate professionals. The Winthrop fleet's arrival in 1630 supplied an institutional center that later arrivals could join, helping towns and congregations reproduce rapidly.

## Key Characteristics

- Concentrated between 1629 and 1643 in the episode's chronology.
- Involved costly property liquidation, passage, and likely permanent departure.
- Included whole families rather than primarily male labor contingents.
- Drew many migrants from East Anglia and Kent.
- Included literate professionals, landholders, teachers, lawyers, and tradespeople.
- Fed an existing chartered center capable of authorizing towns and institutions.
- Enabled demographic and cultural continuity for a permanent settler project.

## Qualifications

The migration figure, regional composition, and comparison with extractive colonies come from one structured episode summary. The profile describes settler intention and capacity; it does not legitimate settlement or center the Indigenous peoples whose land made expansion possible.
