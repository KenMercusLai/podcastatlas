[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-397-2-rang-huangdi-dou-wei-zhi-bianse-de-liumin-cong-hechu-laide-ltmoqipqk61_cg8n7w1gwaprtitj|Hanji 397-2]] now backfills the policy-argument layer: before the later purge, Sang defends Han Wudi's salt, iron, junshu, liquor, and foreign-policy system during the [[SaltAndIronConference|Salt and Iron Conference]], where worthy and literary scholars attack those institutions as government competition with the people for profit. ## Current Profile

The sources give Sang Hongyang a compressed but now sharper profile. His earlier achievement is financial: he helped Han Wudi enrich the state and manage fiscal pressure. Hanji 397-2 shows the public-policy cost of that legacy after the [[LateHanWudiDisplacementCrisis|late Han Wudi displacement crisis]]: Sang becomes the defender of the old fiscal and foreign-policy line at the Salt and Iron Conference, while critics accuse salt, iron, junshu, and liquor monopoly of impoverishing commoners and enriching officials. Huo Guang does not abolish the system Sang defends, but the debate becomes a political setback because it lets Huo criticize the Wudi-era fiscal program without losing all fiscal capacity.

Sang's final political profile is darker. He asks unsuccessfully for offices for family members, falls into deep policy conflict with Huo Guang, and participates in the anti-Huo conspiracy that Hanji 400-1 narrates in more detail. By age seventy-four, old fiscal service no longer protects him; Huo Guang has him executed and his kin group destroyed.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han fiscal official remembered in the episode as an important contributor to Han Wudi's state finance.
- Public defender of Han Wudi-era salt, iron, junshu, liquor, and foreign-policy institutions at the Salt and Iron Conference.
- Policy rival whose fiscal program is politically weakened by Huo Guang's selective use of worthy-literary criticism.
- Political opponent of Huo Guang during the Han Zhao regency field.
- Late-life participant in the Shangguan-Gai-Liu Dan anti-Huo conspiracy.
- Merit-holder whose old service does not prevent execution once factional conflict and rebellion charges combine.
- Father of Sang Qian, whose flight and concealment open a second legal-political case after Sang Hongyang's death.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 397-2, Hanji 400-1, and Hanji 402-1. It does not reconstruct Sang Hongyang's full fiscal career, the full text of the Yantielun, all institutional details of salt and iron policy, his complete relationship with Han Wudi, or the complete legal record of the Liu Dan rebellion. Hanji 397-2 supplies a compact conference profile, not a full economic evaluation of every policy Sang defended.
