## Overview

刘向 / Liu Xiang is synthesized here through four source roles. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-119-2-zanmen-dou-yuanwang-le-beifu-2000-duonian-heiguo-de-yegong-haolong-lh-h-gezpqtvnyq40zlqac8s-5e|Qinji 119-2]] presents him as the late Western Han compiler associated with [[XinXu|《新序》]], whose [[YeGongHaolong|叶公好龙]] anecdote shaped the later reputation of [[YeGong|叶公]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-522-shi-shui-rang-liushi-jiangshan-gaixing-le-wang-ll_s8bakpepbp-1w4g9ohzfgksbo|Hanji 522]] backfills his earlier Chengdi-era warning against Wang-family outer-relative power: Liu Xiang privately tells [[ChenTangWesternHan|陈汤]] why he must speak, then urges [[HanChengdi|汉成帝]] to strengthen Liu kin, remove overmighty Wang relatives, and recover state authority. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-546-haoyou-bianwei-choudi-neng-you-duo-kepa-lrtypxhdqvzkme_rgesxwoiv1asv|Hanji 546]] adds the later omen-remonstrance role: as Zhonglei Xiaowei, Liu Xiang uses repeated solar eclipses to tell Chengdi that disasters arise from human and imperial-family faults. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-536-1-renxing-huanghou-zhaofeiyan-ruhe-mingmu-zhangdan-gaowaiyu-llxo0my5ngp-mnbvjbl92yod1x2n|Hanji 536-1]] adds a broader Guanglu Dafu correction role, normalizing the transcript's "刘相" to Liu Xiang: he compiles consort examples from classical texts, repeatedly memorializes Chengdi, and attacks the Changling mausoleum works through frugality and afterlife logic.

## Current Profile

In the Qinji 119-2 branch, Liu Xiang matters less as a simple author-villain than as a reception-history node. The episode says the familiar Ye Gong anecdote comes from Xin Xu and treats it as a later moralizing story that can overwhelm a fuller historical record. Liu Xiang therefore marks how edited classical story collections can turn a political actor into a durable idiom.

Hanji 522 and Hanji 546 give Liu Xiang a different register. Rather than compiling a didactic story after the fact, he appears inside Chengdi's court as a Liu-lineage old minister who treats speech as dynastic duty. In Hanji 522, he warns that Wang-family appointments, guests, retainers, luxury, selective precedent against Liu kin, and omen rhetoric around the Wang ancestral tomb have become signs of possible dynastic transfer. In Hanji 546, he compares Spring and Autumn eclipse counts with the unusually frequent eclipses under Chengdi, argues that all calamities come from human misgovernment, and identifies royal-family faults as the cause. Chengdi is moved or attentive in these accounts but does not adopt the advice, so the episode set records Liu Xiang as a warning voice whose diagnosis enters the court without becoming policy.

Hanji 536-1 adds Liu Xiang as a court moralist and frugality remonstrant. The episode says he treats moral education as beginning inside the ruler's closest relationships, compiles examples of virtuous women and destructive favorites from the `Shijing` and `Shangshu` traditions, and memorializes Chengdi many times. His Changling advice extends the critique from inner-palace disorder to public works: extravagant burial burdens the living and fails under either assumption about whether the dead retain awareness.

## Key Characteristics

- Late Western Han compiler associated in the source with [[XinXu|《新序》]].
- Connected to the reception history of [[YeGongHaolong|叶公好龙]], where a didactic anecdote reshapes Ye Gong's later image.
- Chengdi-era Zhonglei Xiaowei in Hanji 546.
- Uses disasters, omens, classical precedent, and solar-eclipse frequency as evidence of outer-relative danger, political disorder, and imperial-family fault.
- Frames natural calamity as human and moral failure rather than as isolated natural event.
- Uses classical consort examples and mausoleum-frugality reasoning to criticize Chengdi's inner court and Changling works.
- His advice is recorded as respected or moving to Chengdi, but not fully converted into settled policy in these source notes.

## Qualifications

This page is source-scoped to Qinji 119-2, Hanji 546, Hanji 536-1, and Hanji 522. It does not reconstruct Liu Xiang's full bibliography, office history, complete memorial wording, standard-history biography, or the full textual transmission of Xin Xu. The Ye Gong branch records reception effects, while the Chengdi branches record outer-relative, omen, consort-morality, and mausoleum-frugality remonstrance episodes. Hanji 522's Wang ancestral-tomb omen and Chengdi's inner reasons for non-adoption remain source-scoped political rhetoric rather than independently verified causation.
