## Overview

刘向 / Liu Xiang is synthesized here through two source roles. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-119-2-zanmen-dou-yuanwang-le-beifu-2000-duonian-heiguo-de-yegong-haolong-lh-h-gezpqtvnyq40zlqac8s-5e|Qinji 119-2]] presents him as the late Western Han compiler associated with [[XinXu|《新序》]], whose [[YeGongHaolong|叶公好龙]] anecdote shaped the later reputation of [[YeGong|叶公]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-546-haoyou-bianwei-choudi-neng-you-duo-kepa-lrtypxhdqvzkme_rgesxwoiv1asv|Hanji 546]] adds the Chengdi-era remonstrance role: as Zhonglei Xiaowei, Liu Xiang uses repeated solar eclipses to tell [[HanChengdi|汉成帝]] that disasters arise from human and imperial-family faults.

## Current Profile

In the Qinji 119-2 branch, Liu Xiang matters less as a simple author-villain than as a reception-history node. The episode says the familiar Ye Gong anecdote comes from Xin Xu and treats it as a later moralizing story that can overwhelm a fuller historical record. Liu Xiang therefore marks how edited classical story collections can turn a political actor into a durable idiom.

Hanji 546 gives Liu Xiang a different register. Rather than compiling a didactic story after the fact, he appears inside Chengdi's court as an adviser interpreting eclipse frequency. The source says he compares Spring and Autumn eclipse counts with the unusually frequent eclipses under Chengdi, argues that all calamities come from human misgovernment, and identifies royal-family faults as the cause. Chengdi does not adopt the advice, so the episode records Liu Xiang as a warning voice whose diagnosis enters the court but does not become policy.

## Key Characteristics

- Late Western Han compiler associated in the source with [[XinXu|《新序》]].
- Connected to the reception history of [[YeGongHaolong|叶公好龙]], where a didactic anecdote reshapes Ye Gong's later image.
- Chengdi-era Zhonglei Xiaowei in Hanji 546.
- Uses solar-eclipse frequency as evidence of political and imperial-family disorder.
- Frames natural calamity as human and moral failure rather than as isolated natural event.
- His advice is recorded as unaccepted by Chengdi in the episode's account.

## Qualifications

This page is source-scoped to Qinji 119-2 and Hanji 546. It does not reconstruct Liu Xiang's full bibliography, office history, complete memorial wording, standard-history biography, or the full textual transmission of Xin Xu. The Ye Gong branch records reception effects, while the Chengdi branch records one disaster-remonstrance episode.
