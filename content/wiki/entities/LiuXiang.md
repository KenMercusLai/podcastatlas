## Overview

刘向 / Liu Xiang is synthesized here through eight source roles. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-119-2-zanmen-dou-yuanwang-le-beifu-2000-duonian-heiguo-de-yegong-haolong-lh-h-gezpqtvnyq40zlqac8s-5e|Qinji 119-2]] presents him as the late Western Han compiler associated with [[XinXu|《新序》]], whose [[YeGongHaolong|叶公好龙]] anecdote shaped the later reputation of [[YeGong|叶公]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-477-xihan-shouwei-jianchen-da-huanguan-shi-ruhe-raoluan-chaozheng-de-lq_tjdwttakvkciban4sptyyhuhb|Hanji 477]] adds an earlier Yongguang 1 role under [[HanYuandi|汉元帝]] under his prior name 刘更生: he uses court harmony, disaster signs, and classical precedent to warn Yuan that [[ShiXianWesternHan|石显]]-style slander and ruler suspicion are driving out upright ministers. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-496-qiong-pa-le-jiemi-tanguan-zouxiang-tanfu-de-xinli-youyin-lnskbcqvjhtpfi4ikq44kpbbfnol|Hanji 496]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-497-huangniba-diao-kudang-xihan-mingjiang-chentang-dangguan-you-duo-nan-liz2ssrmijhowebl5hhx8-61ybmp|Hanji 497]] backfill a court-defense role under Yuan: Liu Xiang uses classical and military precedent to defend [[ChenTangWesternHan|陈汤]] and [[GanYanshouWesternHan|甘延寿]], arguing that the killing of [[ZhizhiChanyu|郅支单于]] should not be erased by procedural and property faults, leading Yuan to pardon and reward them after the campaign. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-514-da-xiao-wang-duijue-ni-cai-shui-neng-ying-lnlvthkkqbp_zo2lfpp4bqmh5nsu|Hanji 514]] backfills the scholarly base behind his Chengdi-era authority: Liu Xiang comes from Liu De's meritorious learned household, serves as Guanglu Dafu, collates imperial texts, and compiles disaster-anomaly material into `Hongfan Wuxing Zhuanlun` as a warning about Wang-family power. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-522-shi-shui-rang-liushi-jiangshan-gaixing-le-wang-ll_s8bakpepbp-1w4g9ohzfgksbo|Hanji 522]] then shows a sharper secret-memorial version of the same concern: Liu Xiang privately tells [[ChenTangWesternHan|陈汤]] why he must speak, then urges [[HanChengdi|汉成帝]] to strengthen Liu kin, remove overmighty Wang relatives, and recover state authority. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-546-haoyou-bianwei-choudi-neng-you-duo-kepa-lrtypxhdqvzkme_rgesxwoiv1asv|Hanji 546]] adds the later omen-remonstrance role: as Zhonglei Xiaowei, Liu Xiang uses repeated solar eclipses to tell Chengdi that disasters arise from human and imperial-family faults. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-536-1-renxing-huanghou-zhaofeiyan-ruhe-mingmu-zhangdan-gaowaiyu-llxo0my5ngp-mnbvjbl92yod1x2n|Hanji 536-1]] adds a broader Guanglu Dafu correction role, normalizing the transcript's "刘相" to Liu Xiang: he compiles consort examples from classical texts, repeatedly memorializes Chengdi, and attacks the Changling mausoleum works through frugality and afterlife logic.

## Current Profile

In the Qinji 119-2 branch, Liu Xiang matters less as a simple author-villain than as a reception-history node. The episode says the familiar Ye Gong anecdote comes from Xin Xu and treats it as a later moralizing story that can overwhelm a fuller historical record. Liu Xiang therefore marks how edited classical story collections can turn a political actor into a durable idiom.

Hanji 514, Hanji 522, and Hanji 546 give Liu Xiang a different register. Rather than compiling a didactic story after the fact, he appears inside Chengdi's court as a Liu-lineage old minister and scholar-official who treats speech as dynastic duty. Hanji 514 shows the institutional base: Chengdi's search for lost books lets Liu Xiang turn collation of classics, masters, and poetry into summarized knowledge for the throne, while `Hongfan Wuxing Zhuanlun` turns disaster precedent into indirect warning about Wang Feng's brothers. In Hanji 522, he warns more directly that Wang-family appointments, guests, retainers, luxury, selective precedent against Liu kin, and omen rhetoric around the Wang ancestral tomb have become signs of possible dynastic transfer. In Hanji 546, he compares Spring and Autumn eclipse counts with the unusually frequent eclipses under Chengdi, argues that all calamities come from human misgovernment, and identifies royal-family faults as the cause. Chengdi is moved or attentive in these accounts but does not adopt the advice, so the episode set records Liu Xiang as a warning voice whose diagnosis enters the court without becoming policy.

Hanji 536-1 adds Liu Xiang as a court moralist and frugality remonstrant. The episode says he treats moral education as beginning inside the ruler's closest relationships, compiles examples of virtuous women and destructive favorites from the `Shijing` and `Shangshu` traditions, and memorializes Chengdi many times. His Changling advice extends the critique from inner-palace disorder to public works: extravagant burial burdens the living and fails under either assumption about whether the dead retain awareness.

Hanji 477, Hanji 496, and Hanji 497 add earlier Yuan-era roles before his later Chengdi remonstrance profile. Hanji 477 shows Liu Xiang as a disaster-and-harmony remonstrant against slander: his memorial links court disharmony, ruler suspicion, petty men's rumors, and repeated disasters into one warning about whom Yuan trusts. Hanji 496 and Hanji 497 show a proportional-judgment role: his defense of Chen Tang and Gan Yanshou argues that unauthorized action and smaller faults should be judged against necessity, result, public military benefit, frontier deterrence, and diplomatic prestige.

## Key Characteristics

- Late Western Han compiler associated with [[XinXu|《新序》]] and the reception history of [[YeGongHaolong|叶公好龙]], where a didactic anecdote reshapes Ye Gong's later image.
- Chengdi-era Guanglu Dafu and imperial text-collation lead in Hanji 514; Zhonglei Xiaowei in Hanji 546.
- Uses disasters, omens, court harmony, classical precedent, and solar-eclipse frequency as evidence of slander politics, outer-relative danger, political disorder, and imperial-family fault.
- Frames natural calamity as human and moral failure rather than as isolated natural event.
- Uses classical consort examples and mausoleum-frugality reasoning to criticize Chengdi's inner court and Changling works.
- Uses classical and military precedent under Yuan to argue that extraordinary frontier merit can justify pardoning procedural and property-related faults.
- His advice is recorded as respected or moving to Chengdi, but not fully converted into settled policy in these source notes.

## Qualifications

This page is source-scoped to Qinji 119-2, Hanji 477, Hanji 496, Hanji 497, Hanji 514, Hanji 546, Hanji 536-1, and Hanji 522. It does not reconstruct Liu Xiang's full bibliography, office history, complete memorial wording, standard-history biography, or the full textual transmission of Xin Xu. The Ye Gong branch records reception effects; Hanji 477 records a Yuan-era slander-and-harmony memorial under the Liu Gengsheng name; Hanji 496 and Hanji 497 record a Yuan-era proportional-merit defense; the Chengdi branches record book collation, outer-relative warning, omen, consort-morality, and mausoleum-frugality remonstrance episodes. Hanji 514's `Hongfan Wuxing` framing, Hanji 522's Wang ancestral-tomb omen, and Chengdi's inner reasons for non-adoption remain source-scoped political rhetoric rather than independently verified causation.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 477's earlier Yuan-era Liu Gengsheng layer, showing Liu Xiang as a slander-and-court-harmony remonstrant before the Chen Tang/Gan Yanshou defense and Chengdi-era omen memorials.
- Preserved Hanji 496's fuller Yuan-era defense frame: Liu Xiang appears not only in the reward outcome but in the accusation-stage argument over whether major frontier merit outweighs procedural and property faults.
