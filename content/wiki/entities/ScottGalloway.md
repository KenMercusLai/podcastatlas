# Scott Galloway

## Overview
Scott Galloway is the guest in the [[HubermanLab]] source note on masculinity, young men, Big Tech, relationships, health, national service, and intergenerational policy.

## Current Profile
The current evidence presents Galloway as a public educator and professor-style commentator who combines concrete life advice for young men with broader social and economic critique. In this source, he frames masculinity as a code of usefulness, service, protection, provision, courage, risk-taking, and surplus value rather than cruelty or dominance. He also argues that the personal advice only works inside a wider institutional field shaped by social media, education, health care, wealth transfer, national service, mentorship, and economic opportunity.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames positive masculinity through provision, protection, procreation, service, and surplus value.
- Converts advice to struggling young men into concrete behavioral audits around phone use, exercise, work, volunteering, dating, and rejection.
- Treats Big Tech and social-media platforms as structurally misaligned with young men's real-world relationships when attention monetization rewards isolation or outrage.
- Rejects both manosphere blame of women and blanket claims that masculinity is inherently toxic.
- Connects lonely, economically blocked young men to wider social instability, violence risk, and policy failure.
- Favors structured civic service and male mentorship as practical responses to isolation and absent role models.
