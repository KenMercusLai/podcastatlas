# Garmin

[[kafeidou-liangci-zaoyu-pingguo-chongji-yundong-shoubiao-jiaming-weihe-hai-neng-zengzhang-1006272684]] adds Garmin as a full company-history and strategy case. The source says Garmin was founded in 1989 by [[GaryBurrell|Gary Burrell]] and [[MinKao|Min Kao / 高明环]], began with expensive [[GlobalPositioningSystem|GPS]] devices for aviation, marine, surveying, and other professional users, and later carried that navigation capability into consumer car navigation.

The first major shock came from phones. The episode says Garmin's 2007 revenue exceeded $3 billion and automotive products contributed roughly three quarters of it, but the [[IPhone|iPhone]] and [[Google]]'s free Android voice navigation turned a formerly paid navigation device into a default smartphone service. This makes Garmin a case in [[NavigationDeviceCommoditization]] and [[ConsumerElectronicsLifecycle]] rather than only a wearables brand.

The second shock came from [[AppleWatch|Apple Watch]] after Garmin had moved toward sports watches. The source argues that this shock did not have the same result because Garmin competed through [[ProfessionalWearableMoat|professional wearable moats]]: sport-specific data, GPS precision, maps, routes, terrain, battery credibility, and loyal runners, cyclists, triathletes, swimmers, golfers, and outdoor users. Its overall smartwatch share remains small, but the source says Garmin ranks first in the above-$500 watch market.

The source also makes Garmin an operations case. Its [[SpecializedHardwareVerticalIntegration]] includes owned factories, warehouses, and customer service, which the episode says let Garmin adjust production when car-navigation demand fell and support many watch models whose individual volumes may be limited.

Garmin still appears in [[tsr-s4-ericm-v2-tsr-s4-ericm-v2]] as one of the companies whose wearable and fitness positioning pressured [[Pebble]] after Pebble's initial notification-centered smartwatch success. Together with [[Apple]] and [[Fitbit]], it helps explain why Pebble explored health, fitness, color watches, and other product directions under [[ProductVisionDrift]] and [[ConsumerElectronicsLifecycle]] pressure.

## Source Position
- Garmin is not presented as immune to platform pressure; its car-navigation business is the counterexample.
- The source's stronger claim is that platform pressure is survivable when a company moves into use cases where professional reliability, battery, sensors, maps, and sport-specific workflows matter.
- Apple Watch Ultra, screenless fitness bands, and smart rings remain real pressure because they attack Garmin's high-end and health-tracking edges from different directions.
