## Overview

唐蒙 / Tang Meng is a Western Han envoy who, in the current source, turns the presence of Shu sauce in [[Nanyue|南越]] into intelligence about trade through [[YelangStateWesternHan|夜郎]] and then into a proposal for military access and administrative expansion under [[HanWudi|汉武帝]].

## Current Profile

Tang Meng first appears after the Han intervention against Dongyue, when he is sent to persuade Nanyue to maintain or deepen its submission. A meal in Nanyue produces the episode's key intelligence event: he identifies sauce associated with Shu, asks where it came from, and then checks the answer with Shu merchants after returning to Chang'an.

The merchants' account gives him more than commodity provenance. It suggests that goods move from Shu into Ye Lang, that Ye Lang lies near a broad navigable river leading toward Nanyue, and that Nanyue influences but does not fully control the southwestern polity. Tang Meng converts those claims into [[CommodityTraceRouteInference|商品踪迹式路线推断]]: an indirect commercial trace becomes a hypothesis about an alternative attack corridor.

His memorial joins military surprise, local manpower, Ba-Shu resources, diplomacy, and office creation. Wudi approves the proposal and sends him with soldiers and a much larger transport labor force. In Ye Lang, Tang Meng uses valuable gifts and imperial prestige to obtain a provisional agreement from [[YelangKingDuotongWesternHan|多同]] and local chiefs, but the chiefs' expectation that Han cannot sustain control keeps the result short of demonstrated incorporation.

## Key Characteristics

- Envoy sent to Nanyue after Han's intervention against Dongyue.
- Observer who treats a familiar commodity in an unexpected place as a geographic clue.
- Investigator who checks Nanyue testimony against Shu merchant knowledge.
- Strategist who proposes a river approach to Nanyue when eastern routes appear obstructed.
- Planner who combines warfare, transport, diplomacy, local recruitment, and official appointment.
- Negotiator who uses gifts and demonstrations of Han capacity to secure conditional Ye Lang cooperation.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to one short popular-history summary rather than a full biography or the underlying Shiji and Hanshu passages. The sauce's precise identity, route, river name, force and labor totals, Ye Lang troop count, memorial wording, administrative package, dialogue, and the chiefs' private reasoning remain episode-attributed. Traded goods may move through multiple intermediaries, and provisional acceptance does not establish continuous access, voluntary incorporation, or durable Han rule.

## What Changed

- Created a profile centered on Tang Meng's conversion of commodity evidence into a combined route, campaign, and governance proposal.
- Distinguished his successful negotiation from proof of sustained territorial control.

## Relationships

- [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] - ruler who approves Tang Meng's proposal and commissions the Ye Lang mission.
- [[Nanyue|南越]] - destination where the sauce clue appears and target of the proposed river approach.
- [[YelangStateWesternHan|夜郎]] - polity whose trade position, manpower, and river access anchor the proposal.
- [[YelangKingDuotongWesternHan|夜郎王多同]] - ruler from whom Tang Meng obtains provisional agreement.
- [[CommodityTraceRouteInference|商品踪迹式路线推断]] - reasoning method that converts the sauce's displacement into a route hypothesis.
- [[NominalSubmissionLocalAutonomy|名义臣服与地方实权并存]] - boundary on interpreting the chiefs' gift-backed agreement as effective rule.
