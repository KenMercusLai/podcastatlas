## Overview

唐蒙 / Tang Meng is a Western Han envoy who turns the presence of Shu sauce in [[Nanyue|南越]] into intelligence about trade through [[YelangStateWesternHan|夜郎]], then into a proposal for military access and administrative expansion under [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]. His current profile also includes the coercive road-building implementation that reportedly converts conditional cooperation into revolt.

## Current Profile

Tang Meng first appears after the Han intervention against Dongyue, when he is sent to persuade Nanyue to maintain or deepen its submission. A meal in Nanyue produces the episode's key intelligence event: he identifies sauce associated with Shu, asks where it came from, and then checks the answer with Shu merchants after returning to Chang'an.

The merchants' account gives him more than commodity provenance. It suggests that goods move from Shu into Ye Lang, that Ye Lang lies near a broad navigable river leading toward Nanyue, and that Nanyue influences but does not fully control the southwestern polity. Tang Meng converts those claims into [[CommodityTraceRouteInference|商品踪迹式路线推断]]: an indirect commercial trace becomes a hypothesis about an alternative attack corridor.

His memorial joins military surprise, local manpower, Ba-Shu resources, diplomacy, and office creation. Wudi approves the proposal and sends him with soldiers and a much larger transport labor force. In Ye Lang, Tang Meng uses valuable gifts and imperial prestige to obtain a provisional agreement from [[YelangKingDuotongWesternHan|多同]] and local chiefs, but the chiefs' expectation that Han cannot sustain control keeps the result short of demonstrated incorporation.

The sequel supplies the implementation test. After a commandery is reportedly established, Tang Meng mobilizes Ba-Shu labor and tens of thousands of local people for a road toward the Zangke river. Deaths and flight are followed by military-law executions; fear then becomes armed resistance. The result qualifies his reputation as an opener of the southwest: strategic imagination and initial diplomacy do not prevent [[CoerciveFrontierLaborBackfire|强制边疆劳役反噬]] when construction treats people as expendable inputs.

## Key Characteristics

- Envoy sent to Nanyue after Han's intervention against Dongyue.
- Observer who treats a familiar commodity in an unexpected place as a geographic clue.
- Investigator who checks Nanyue testimony against Shu merchant knowledge.
- Strategist who proposes a river approach to Nanyue when eastern routes appear obstructed.
- Planner who combines warfare, transport, diplomacy, local recruitment, and official appointment.
- Negotiator who uses gifts and demonstrations of Han capacity to secure conditional Ye Lang cooperation.
- Implementer whose compulsory labor regime and punishment of flight reportedly turn access-building into revolt.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to two short popular-history summaries rather than a full biography or the underlying Shiji and Hanshu passages. The sauce's precise identity, route, river name, force and labor totals, mortality, executions, Ye Lang troop count, memorial wording, administrative package, dialogue, and the chiefs' private reasoning remain episode-attributed. Traded goods may move through multiple intermediaries, and provisional acceptance does not establish continuous access, voluntary incorporation, or durable Han rule. The sequel attributes the coercive project to Tang Meng's leadership but does not provide a full chain of command or independent casualty evidence.

## What Changed

- Added the road-building implementation and revolt as the decisive limit on Tang Meng's initial negotiation.
- Reframed his southwestern-opening legacy around both route innovation and coercive governance costs.
