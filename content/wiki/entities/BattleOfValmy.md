# Battle of Valmy

## Overview

The Battle of Valmy was the 20 September 1792 confrontation in which French forces halted the Prussian advance during the crisis surrounding the birth of the French Republic.

## Current Profile

The episode treats Valmy as a limited military result with outsized political consequences. French regulars and improved artillery held their position, while rain, hunger, disease, strained supply, and [[CharlesFrancoisDumouriez|Dumouriez]]'s operational threat made Brunswick unwilling to press the attack; survival against expected collapse was then remembered as a republican triumph.

## Key Characteristics

- It occurred one day before the National Convention abolished the monarchy.
- French forces combined Dumouriez's position with Kellermann's reinforcement and artillery.
- The Prussian army faced sickness, hunger, rain, fatigue, and vulnerable supply lines.
- Artillery dominated the encounter before the Prussian infantry advance was abandoned.
- Its political and morale effect was greater than its tactical decisiveness.

- The relative weight of artillery, logistics, command judgment, disease, and political context remains interpretive.
