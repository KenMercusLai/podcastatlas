## Overview

烧当羌 / Shaodang Qiang is a recurring Qiang group in the current Eastern Han source notes. The evidence now begins with [[MiTangQiangLeader|Mi Tang]]'s failed surrender, renewed rebellion, defeat, relocation, and remnant collapse in Hanji 772, then moves into Hanji 773's resource explanation of Shaodang strength through West Sea land, fishery, and salt before later An- and Shun-era campaign notices.

## Current Profile

Hanji 772 presents the Shaodang field through Mi Tang's late Emperor-He crisis. Mi Tang surrenders, resists returning to the old residence, rebels again, is defeated, and leaves a weakened remnant that is relocated and then collapses under later pressure. The episode makes Shaodang instability look like a problem of residence, subsistence, and broken incorporation, not just battlefield defiance.

Hanji 773 presents the Shaodang Qiang as the recurring initiator of western Qiang unrest in [[EmperorHeOfHan|Emperor He]]'s court. [[CaoFengLateHan|Cao Feng]]'s memorial argues that the group's advantage rests on fertile Yu-valley land and access to West Sea fishery and salt, which make local strength politically hard for the Han state to ignore.

The later notes show that the group remains a pressure point after Deng Sui's regency ends. Hanji 796 and Hanji 797 place the Shaodang field around [[MaXianLateHan|Ma Xian]] and the leader Manu: settlement is unstable, appeasement fails, Ma later defeats Shaodang forces, and famine contributes to surrender. Hanji 820 and Hanji 824 then preserve later Shun-era notices in which Shaodang-aligned pressure continues through named leaders such as Na Li and campaigns involving Zhao Chong and Zhang Gong.

## Key Characteristics

- Recurrent Qiang group label across early Eastern Han, An-era, and Shun-era frontier notes.
- Earliest current episode centers on failed surrender and residence management under Mi Tang.
- Strongest current explanation is resource-based: fertile land, fishery, and salt around the West Sea strengthen the group.
- Later evidence is mostly campaign and surrender notices rather than a full internal political history.
- Associated with named leaders including Mi Tang, Manu, and Na Li, though only Mi Tang is currently promoted to a standalone page.
- Useful contrast with [[XianlingQiang|Xianling Qiang]] because both appear repeatedly, yet the current Shaodang page begins from resource geography rather than battlefield recurrence alone.

## Qualifications

The current sources are separated by decades and come through podcast summaries, so the page treats "Shaodang Qiang" as a recurring group label rather than one continuous command structure. Hanji 772 supplies an earlier Mi Tang episode but does not by itself reconstruct the group's full internal politics. Hanji 773's resource explanation remains the strongest synthesis contribution, while later Manu, Na Li, Zhang Gong, and campaign-place details remain source-scoped until additional notes justify separate pages or tighter chronology.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 772 as the earliest current Shaodang layer: Mi Tang's surrender, failed return, renewed rebellion, defeat, relocation, and remnant collapse.
- Reframed Hanji 773's resource policy as a response to an already visible incorporation failure rather than only an abstract frontier diagnosis.
- Created the page from Hanji 773 and folded in later source notes that had mentioned Shaodang Qiang without a standalone canonical page.
