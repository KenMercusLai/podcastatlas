# U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement

## Overview
U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement is the DHS component at the center of the wiki's immigration-enforcement expansion, detention infrastructure, procurement scrutiny, surveillance-as-a-service access, and detainee DNA collection branches.

## Current Profile
ICE is currently profiled as both an expanding enforcement organization and a data-power actor. Planet Money sources connect it to rapid hiring, field-training disputes, detention capacity, private detention economics, procurement-records journalism, and Palantir-enabled data integration. Marketplace Tech sources connect it to government access to private surveillance databases and detainee samples entering the FBI-linked [[CODIS]] database. The recent [[TheIntelligence]] and Planet Money sources add quieter [[TechnologyAssistedInteriorEnforcement|technology-assisted interior enforcement]], overloaded [[ImmigrationCourtThroughputPressure|immigration-court throughput]], ICE access to many datasets, and the [[GovernmentDataSiloCollapse]] question raised by an ICE-IRS data-sharing memorandum.

## Key Characteristics
- Immigration-enforcement agency under [[USDepartmentOfHomelandSecurity|DHS]], kept distinct from the unrelated [[ICEInvestor]] page.
- Rapidly scaling workforce and detention system with disputed training and accountability conditions.
- Procurement and vendor-accountability subject because public records expose ICE-linked technology contracts.
- Surveillance data user in the government data-broker and surveillance-as-a-service branch.
- DNA collection actor whose detainee samples can feed durable criminal-policing databases.
- Interior-enforcement actor whose arrests, target-listing tools, cross-agency data access, and court pipeline can affect long-term residents and people whose legal status is stripped.

## Evidence
- Workforce and detention expansion - [[the-ice-hiring-boom]] says DHS reported 12,000 new ICE agents and officers, record detention levels, and planned detention-capacity spending.
- Procurement scrutiny - [[betty-boop-excel-olympics-penny-isms-our-2026-valentines]] uses 404 Media reporting on ICE technology and Palantir-related claims as a public-records accountability example.
- Private surveillance access - [[tech-20260302-0302-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260302-0302-mp-tech-pod-128]] names ICE among agencies able to search or use private surveillance-as-a-service systems.
- DNA-to-CODIS pathway - [[tech-20260831-0831-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260831-0831-mp-tech-pod-128]] ties ICE detainee DNA collection to [[ImmigrationDNADatabaseExpansion]] and long-term CODIS inclusion.
- Quiet interior enforcement and courts - [[ire-and-ice-the-toll-of-americas-deportations-6a993426809ae7c2f4293df9]] says ICE arrested 50,000 people in July, describes a Palantir-created target-listing app in court testimony, and links arrests to crowded immigration-court hearings.
- Dataset integration and IRS access - [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-8eb9e254581eaa220d9428df389214d5-27844515-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-8eb9e254581eaa220d9428df389214d5-27844515]] says ICE can access 30 to 40 datasets, sought taxpayer information through an IRS memorandum, and can use Palantir software to enrich files on individuals and networks.

## Qualifications
The ICE page preserves source boundaries around disputed claims. The training-hours record remains unresolved, the earlier Palantir app description was disputed, the new target-listing account is based on court testimony as summarized by the episode, removal totals are not official statistics, the DNA episode presents Georgetown's constitutional critique without a detailed ICE or DHS response, and the Palantir-focused Planet Money source does not include direct ICE comment on dataset count, database scale, or operational use.
