# Satchin Panda

## Overview
Satchin Panda is presented in the source as a Salk Institute professor and Regulatory Biology Laboratory director whose research connects cellular and organ clocks, retinal and brain timing circuits, and time-restricted eating.

## Current Profile
In the [[HubermanLab]] interview, Panda treats circadian health as coordination among light, food, sleep, activity, caffeine, alcohol, and social timing rather than as a fasting-only program. His central analytical move is to separate calorie quantity, food quality, eating-window duration, biological timing, and schedule consistency. He uses mouse experiments to explain mechanisms and longevity hypotheses, then narrows human conclusions to feasibility, selected metabolic outcomes, and population-specific caution.

The earlier solo [[scim4349715199-scim4349715199]] episode cites Panda's laboratory for improvements in liver enzymes and insulin sensitivity associated with intermittent fasting while retaining energy balance and denying one generally superior breakfast- or evening-skipping rule. Read alongside the later interview, this makes evidence separation part of the profile: metabolic timing may matter without establishing one healthy schedule for everyone.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames time-restricted eating as distinct from calorie restriction and other intermittent-fasting protocols.
- Explains meal timing through anticipatory clocks distributed across cells, organs, and brain systems.
- Separates strong animal findings from more limited and heterogeneous human evidence.
- Favors feasible, consistent windows over extreme compression and repeatedly foregrounds energy adequacy.
- Studies real-world circadian disruption through occupational settings such as firefighter shift work.
- Is cited for metabolic benefits of time-restricted eating while preserving calorie-balance and universal-schedule limits.

## Qualifications
This profile is based on one interview summary and one earlier solo episode citing Panda's work rather than his complete publication record. Study descriptions, effect sizes, historical interpretations, personal routine, app status, and liver or insulin outcomes remain source-scoped. His broad twelve-hour or adult eight-to-twelve-hour framing is not individualized medical advice, especially for pregnancy, childhood, diabetes, eating disorders, high athletic demand, medication use, or prolonged fasting.
