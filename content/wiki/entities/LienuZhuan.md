## Overview

《列女传》 / Lienu Zhuan is a [[LiuXiang|刘向]]-associated Western Han text represented in the source set both as a carrier of legendary, moralized female biography and as an earlier, broader catalogue of female exemplarity not reducible to chastity alone.

## Current Profile

The text has two distinct roles in the current source set. As a layer of moralized women-and-dynasty storytelling, it carries dragon-saliva and beacon-fire traditions around [[BaoSi|褒姒]], while the retelling warns that supernatural content should not be treated as straightforward history. As a benchmark for plural female exemplarity, it is presented as teaching through maternal example, discernment, benevolence, and wisdom rather than chastity alone.

That second role supports a qualified reception contrast: later exemplary-women histories allegedly narrowed “列女” toward chastity-centered “烈女.” This is useful for resisting the assumption that Chinese women's historical value was always defined by sexual fidelity, but the supplied episodes do not establish the full textual history, original purpose, category structure, or Sui-Tang transition.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han classical text associated in the episode with Liu Xiang.
- Collection presented as preserving multiple kinds of female conduct and capacity rather than chastity alone.
- Source layer for Bao Si's mythic and moralized biography.
- Vehicle for linking women's conduct, ruler failure, and dynastic disaster in later memory.
- Reception benchmark used to criticize a later narrowing from “exemplary women” to “martyr-like chaste women.”
- Requires source-critical handling both when supernatural narratives are reused and when broad claims are made about its original purpose or later transformation.

## Qualifications

This page synthesizes two podcast uses of 《列女传》 rather than a critical textual history. Its authorship and compilation, editions, chapter categories, biographies, intended audience, political purpose, relationship to the Zhao Feiyan sisters, and later reception require primary-text and specialist comparison. The difference between 列女 and 烈女 is interpretively important in Hanji 347-2, but wordplay alone does not prove a uniform historical transition across post-Sui-Tang official histories.
