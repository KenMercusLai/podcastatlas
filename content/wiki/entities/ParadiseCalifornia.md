# Paradise, California

## Overview
Paradise is a northern [[California]] town whose destruction in the 2018 Camp Fire and subsequent rebuilding make it a concentrated case of climate disaster, housing recovery, and property-insurance market stress.

## Current Profile
[[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-043ffe0bc2c7516bac64132229755e93-43026861-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-043ffe0bc2c7516bac64132229755e93-43026861]] reports that the Camp Fire killed 85 people, destroyed more than 11,000 homes, and damaged roughly 90% of town structures. Paradise then adopted stronger fire-resistant building rules and organized neighborhood mitigation, yet private insurance returned only slowly and in limited quantities.

## Key Characteristics
- Was devastated by the 2018 Camp Fire and mass evacuation.
- Adopted rebuilding standards modeled on [[InstituteForBusinessAndHomeSafety|IBHS]] research.
- Became heavily exposed to premium increases, nonrenewals, and residual-market coverage.
- Demonstrates that insurance affects mortgages, home sales, population recovery, business recruitment, and municipal revenue.
- Secured a limited re-entry by private insurers after regulatory change and direct review of local mitigation.

## Qualifications
- Destruction, population, policy, price, and market-return figures remain episode-reported and are not independently verified here.
- Paradise is an informative case, not proof that the same adaptation pathway will work in every hazard-exposed community.

## What Changed
- Established Paradise as a community-scale case linking physical rebuilding to insurance-market recovery.
