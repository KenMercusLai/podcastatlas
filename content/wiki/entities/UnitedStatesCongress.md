# United States Congress

## Overview

The United States Congress is the federal legislature whose powers over statutes, appropriations, confirmations, oversight, and institutional mandates make it both a governing body and a check on executive action.

## Current Profile

The bounded sources show Congress operating through several non-interchangeable channels. It writes the legal authority and mandate for the [[FederalReserve]], confirms senior officials, can clarify removal standards, oversees federal data practices, protects or withdraws project funding, and determines whether temporary tariff authority continues. Budget conflict can also turn Congress into a source of shutdown, missing data, delayed services, and policy uncertainty.

The newest episode adds divided-government detail. House appropriations, Senate confirmation, hearings, document demands, subpoenas, investigations, impeachment, and war powers differ in threshold, timing, enforceability, and political cost. Congressional control can therefore constrain a president without guaranteeing rapid policy reversal or uniform market effects.

## Key Characteristics

- Controls legislation and appropriations while sharing lawmaking power with the president.
- Uses Senate confirmation as a personnel and institutional-governance check.
- Exercises oversight through hearings, document requests, subpoenas, and investigations.
- Creates statutory mandates and time limits that courts and executive agencies later interpret.
- Can preserve selected projects despite broader administrative retrenchment.
- Contains veto points that can generate shutdown and policy uncertainty as well as prevent unilateral action.

## Qualifications

The sources are episode accounts rather than a complete constitutional or legislative history. Chamber control does not erase presidential vetoes, judicial review, agency discretion, enforcement disputes, supermajority thresholds, internal party divisions, or state authority. The newest source's claims about election control, impeachment, subpoenas, and military operations are not treated as current legal advice or independently verified outcomes.
