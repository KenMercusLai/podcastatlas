## Overview

士徽 / Shi Hui is [[ShiXie|士燮]]'s son and the Jiaozhou claimant whose attempt to inherit practical rule over [[JiaozhiCommandery|交趾郡]] ends in induced surrender and execution by [[LyuDai|吕岱]].

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1119-ta-shengqian-guishun-sunquan-sihou-weihe-jiazu-bei-ganjin-shajue-ll4mblhk7ydn3pdxg-5zucstokzz|Hanji 1119]] introduces Shi Hui only after Shi Xie's death in 226 CE. When [[SunQuan|孙权]] divides the southern administration and appoints Chen Shi to Jiaozhi, Shi Hui refuses the replacement, claims the governorship, and mobilizes his kin. The source reads this as a failure to preserve his father's restraint; structurally, it is a direct contest over whether a long local tenure had become hereditary.

Lyu Dai avoids immediate assault and uses Shi Kuang to promise that submission will preserve the family's property and lives. Shi Hui and five brothers surrender, after which Lyu Dai executes them and sends their heads to Wuchang. The case therefore joins a succession error to a credibility failure by the conquering authority: resistance creates the opening for removal, but the removal is completed through an assurance the source says was broken.

## Key Characteristics

- Heir to a deeply rooted but formally non-hereditary local power network.
- Claimant who treats his father's practical command as a basis for office succession.
- Resister who rejects Sun Quan's replacement appointment.
- Kin-network leader whose mobilization turns an appointment dispute into armed conflict.
- Surrenderer induced by a safety assurance transmitted through Shi Kuang.
- Executed figure whose defeat ends the Shi family's principal Jiaozhou power.

## Qualifications

This profile rests on one podcast account and does not independently establish the legal status of Shi Hui's office, the exact scale of resistance, or the wording and authorization of the surrender promise. The host's contrast between a wise father and rash son is useful but incomplete: Sun Quan's administrative redivision and replacement appointment indicate a structural centralization conflict even if Shi Hui had acted more cautiously.

## What Changed

- Created Shi Hui as the failed successor to Shi Xie's negotiated local order.
- Preserved both his active resistance and the alleged breach of surrender assurance in explaining his death.
