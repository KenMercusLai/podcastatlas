# Odin / 奥丁

## Overview

Odin is a central but unstable figure in [[NorseMythology]], associated with knowledge, runes, elite descent, wandering, homelessness, war, mind, magic, sacrifice, support, and betrayal rather than one fixed divine domain.

## Current Profile

[[ep269-cong-manwei-yuzhou-dao-aodesai-weihe-zongyao-fanpai-yingxiong-shishi-ltklotheqpup6oamyflfzpxtipsw]] presents Odin as a knowledge-seeker preparing for [[Ragnarok]] and marks Marvel's heroic family relationships as modern reworking. [[358-viking-sorcery-glt1200485419]] adds a lived Viking Age frame: rulers could claim divine descent or personal relations with Odin, while his dangerous unreliability, self-sacrifice, rune acquisition, war powers, and mastery of the gendered magic of [[Seidr]] make contradiction central to his power.

[[59-the-world-cup-of-gods-part-2-glt6474253297]] extends the reception history. Woden/Wotan naming, West Saxon royal genealogy, the eye and tree sacrifices, ravens, wolves, Sleipnir, Valkyries, and Fenrir make Odin vivid, but the surviving apocalypse is mediated by Christian authors and later layers. Nineteenth-century reinvention and Nazi or neo-Nazi appropriation are changes in political use, not transparent continuations of Viking belief.

## Key Characteristics

- Seeks wisdom and runic knowledge through extreme self-sacrifice.
- Combines elite legitimacy with wandering and social marginality.
- Governs aspects of war through clarity, confusion, hesitation, rage, and altered mental state.
- Masters seiðr despite the practice's strong association with women.
- Can favor warriors and then betray them, making divine support unreliable.
- Changes substantially across Edda materials, Christian-era interpretation, and modern superhero reception.
- Carries a long political afterlife through royal genealogy, romantic revival, and extremist appropriation.

## Qualifications

Odin's profile is assembled from variable mythic, poetic, archaeological, Christian-era, and modern reception layers. The relationship between his tree-hanging and Christian imagery, the historical practice of claimed divine descent, the meaning of gendered magic, and the existence or form of specifically Odinic warrior groups remain qualified. Modern popularity or extremist appropriation cannot establish the content of Viking Age belief.

## What Changed

- Extended Odin's reception profile from medieval Christian mediation and Marvel adaptation to royal genealogy, romantic revival, and extremist misuse.
- Added the Mimir, animal-companion, Fenrir, and Ragnarok cluster without treating it as an unmediated canon.
