## Overview
Teng Fu is presented as a late-Han official whose proven anti-bandit merit does not translate into durable reward because he refuses to attach himself to powerholders. The source uses his dismissal to show how eunuch hostility and senior-official compliance could override merit-based recognition.

## Current Profile
Teng Fu's profile is bounded to the Hanji 828 account. He is described as upright, successful in suppressing bandits, and eligible for enfeoffment because of that military-administrative merit.

The same account emphasizes his refusal to associate with the powerful. That refusal makes him disliked by eunuchs, and the expected reward is replaced by impeachment: [[HuGuangLateHan]] impeaches him, Teng Fu is dismissed, and he later dies at home.

## Key Characteristics
- Upright official with a record of anti-bandit achievement.
- Merit claimant whose expected reward is blocked by political hostility.
- Figure who refuses to cultivate powerholder relationships.
- Target of eunuch resentment despite public-service accomplishments.
- Example of how senior-official procedure can translate palace hostility into formal dismissal.
