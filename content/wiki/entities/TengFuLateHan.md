## Overview
Teng Fu is presented as a late-Han official whose current profile now combines an earlier 145 CE anti-bandit success with a later blocked reward. The same merit record that makes him eligible for recognition in Hanji 828 is grounded by Hanji 826's account of Teng Fu suppressing Xu-Yang and southeast disorder.

## Current Profile
Teng Fu's earlier layer comes from the 145 CE unrest field. [[EmpressDowagerLiangNaLateHan|梁妠]] appoints him as Jiujiang commandery administrator when bandit groups disturb Xu and Yang provinces, and he opens reward channels while campaigning against Ma Mian, Fan Rong, Zhou Sheng, and [[XuFengBanditLateHan|徐凤]]. Local action by [[XieAnXiapiLateHan|下邳谢安]] helps finish the Xu Feng branch, while Teng Fu later defeats [[HuaMengLateHan|华孟]] after Hua attacks Jiujiang and kills Yang Cen.

Hanji 828 gives the later court-political consequence. Teng Fu's anti-bandit merit should have earned enfeoffment, but he refuses to associate with powerholders. Eunuchs dislike him, [[HuGuangLateHan|胡广]] impeaches him, and the expected reward becomes dismissal; he later dies at home.

## Key Characteristics
- Anti-bandit commander whose 145 CE campaign suppresses Xu-Yang and southeast disorder.
- Reward-system operator whose bounties mobilize local actors such as Xie An.
- Upright official with a record of public-service merit.
- Merit claimant whose expected reward is blocked by political hostility.
- Figure who refuses to cultivate powerholder relationships.
- Example of how senior-official procedure can translate palace hostility into formal dismissal.

## Qualifications
The current evidence still does not provide a full biography, but it now explains the merit behind the later reward dispute. Hanji 826 establishes the 145 CE campaign record; Hanji 828 shows how that record is neutralized by eunuch hostility and senior-official compliance. Ma Mian, Fan Rong, Zhou Sheng, Yang Cen, and exact campaign accounting remain source-scoped.

## What Changed
- Added the 145 CE Xu-Yang and southeast pacification layer behind Teng Fu's later merit claim.
- Connected Teng Fu's rewards to Xie An's local action and visible merit conversion.
- Reframed the Hanji 828 dismissal as a later reversal of a now-bounded campaign success.
- Distinguished Teng Fu from [[TengYanLateHan]], whose later case belongs to a different political episode.
