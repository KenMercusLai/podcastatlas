# Roundhay Garden Scene

## Overview

The Roundhay Garden Scene is surviving motion-picture footage recorded by [[LouisLePrince|Louis Le Prince]] at Oakwood Grange in Roundhay, Leeds, in October 1888.

## Current Profile

The source describes the scene as showing Sarah Whitley, Joseph Whitley, Adolphe Le Prince, and Annie Hartley moving in a garden. It belongs to a small surviving group that also includes Adolphe playing accordion and traffic crossing a Leeds bridge.

Its historical importance is evidentiary. The film supports Le Prince's claim to successful motion-picture capture before [[ThomasEdison|Thomas Edison]]'s 1891 demonstration, while not by itself resolving whether Le Prince possessed a working projector or publicly exhibited his films. Later projection made the recorded movement visible, but the episode suggests Le Prince may never have seen that success himself.

## Key Characteristics

- Recorded at Oakwood Grange in Roundhay, Leeds.
- Dated by the source to October 1888.
- Shows four members or associates of the Whitley-Le Prince circle in motion.
- Survives as material evidence of Le Prince's image-capture achievement.
- Does not alone settle the separate question of contemporary projection.

## Evidence

- Date, place, and participants: [[278-france-the-mystery-of-le-prince-glt1101934476]] identifies Oakwood Grange, October 1888, and the four people shown.
- Related footage: [[278-france-the-mystery-of-le-prince-glt1101934476]] groups it with the accordion and Leeds bridge films.
- Historical role: [[278-france-the-mystery-of-le-prince-glt1101934476]] treats the surviving film as a monument of film history while preserving uncertainty about Le Prince's projector.

## Qualifications

The source gives only a concise account of the film and states the recording rate as approximately 12 to 16 pictures per minute, wording that may require technical corroboration. Survival and later projection prove recorded sequential images, not necessarily a complete contemporary camera-projector-exhibition system or an uncontested title of first film.
