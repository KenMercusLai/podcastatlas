# Michel Foucault / 福柯

## Overview
Michel Foucault / 福柯 appears in the wiki as the philosopher used to explain surveillance, visibility, discipline, and self-monitoring in both workplace satire and platform-era youth culture.

## Current Profile
The current sources use Foucault in applied rather than doctrinal form. [[92-bailatu-shangbanji-yong-zhexue-gaoxiao-zhichang-716822350]] turns him into the monitoring officer at [[ThinkReasonLtd|思考理性有限公司]], making [[OfficeSurveillancePrivacy]] concrete through screens, open offices, and restroom visibility. [[episode-227-ershiyishiji-qingchun-wuyu-8211230332-278487]] then uses the panopticon frame to explain [[PlatformSelfDiscipline]]: young people anticipate that any interaction might be preserved, searched, reported, and judged.

## Key Characteristics
- Functions in the wiki as the main philosophical reference for visibility as discipline.
- Is used satirically in a workplace setting where monitoring becomes literal office infrastructure.
- Is used sociologically in the youth-risk episode to explain self-discipline without a fixed watcher.
- Bridges institutional surveillance and platform-native self-regulation.

## Evidence
- Workplace satire: [[92-bailatu-shangbanji-yong-zhexue-gaoxiao-zhichang-716822350]] places Foucault in charge of screens monitoring employee desks and restrooms.
- Office privacy: [[92-bailatu-shangbanji-yong-zhexue-gaoxiao-zhichang-716822350]] uses the Foucault scenes to make [[OfficeSurveillancePrivacy]] legible.
- Platform discipline: [[episode-227-ershiyishiji-qingchun-wuyu-8211230332-278487]] invokes the panopticon to explain why no actual watcher is needed when everyone knows anyone can become an observer.
- Youth-risk culture: [[episode-227-ershiyishiji-qingchun-wuyu-8211230332-278487]] connects this self-discipline to [[YouthPrecautionaryCulture]] and digital records.

## Qualifications
Both sources use Foucault as an interpretive reference. The page does not attempt a full account of his philosophy, historical scholarship, or major works.
