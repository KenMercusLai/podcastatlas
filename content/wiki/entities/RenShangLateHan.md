## Overview

任尚 / Ren Shang appears as a Qiang-front commander whose current profile moves from early defeat and retained command to tactical adaptation, real battlefield merit, proxy assassination, and fatal conflict with Deng-family credit politics.

## Current Profile

Hanji 782 gives the earliest current layer. Ren Shang is one of Deng Zhi's commanders against Dianling's Qiang coalition and is defeated at Pingxiang, with more than 8,000 Han soldiers killed. Pang Can nevertheless recommends leaving him in charge after Deng Zhi's recall, and the court stations him at Hanyang to command forces. This opening matters because Ren's later merit does not erase a record that begins in crisis and institutional desperation.

Hanji 788-789 then show Ren Shang as an adaptable frontier commander. He implements Yu Xu's light-cavalry advice, fights Qiang forces, and becomes part of the campaign field around Lin Chang, Langmo, Deng Zun, and forced relocation. Hanji 790 makes the political danger explicit: Ren has real military achievements, but his attempt to dispute credit with Deng Zun turns merit into punishment risk under Deng-family proximity.

## Key Characteristics

- Qiang-front commander whose current evidence begins with defeat rather than victory.
- Retained field actor whose usefulness survives the recall of Deng Zhi.
- Tactical implementer who benefits from Yu Xu's light-cavalry advice in later operations.
- Real military performer in the later Qiang field, including operations around Lin Chang and Langmo.
- Politically vulnerable commander whose credit dispute with Deng Zun becomes fatal despite battlefield utility.

## Qualifications

Ren Shang should not be read as merely incompetent because Hanji 788-790 preserve later capability and achievement. He should also not be read as simply a victim of court politics because Hanji 782 places heavy defeat at the start of the current evidence. The durable pattern is that military usefulness and political survivability are separate measures.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 782 as the earliest current layer, moving Ren Shang's profile back to Pingxiang defeat and retained Hanyang command.
- Reframed later battlefield merit as recovery and adaptation after an earlier failed campaign.
- Connected Ren's final credit conflict to a longer pattern of commanders operating under Deng-family political constraints.
