## Overview

任尚 / Ren Shang is a late-Han Qiang-front commander whose profile now begins with a successful light-cavalry adaptation advised by [[YuXuEasternHan|虞诩]], then moves through direct fighting, household attack, proxy assassination, and fatal conflict over Deng-family credit.

## Current Profile

Hanji 788 gives Ren Shang's earliest current layer. Yu Xu argues that infantry-heavy Han forces cannot catch mounted Qiang enemies and proposes horse-funded light cavalry as the usable answer. The court accepts the advice, and Ren Shang sends light cavalry into action successfully. In this first layer Ren Shang is not yet a political cautionary tale; he is the commander through whom a cavalry-mobility correction works.

Hanji 789 moves Ren Shang into the wider 116-117 Qiang crisis. He and [[DengZunLateHan|Deng Zun]] are assigned against Qiang forces during [[EmpressDowagerDengLateHan|Empress Dowager Deng]]'s regency. Ren Shang defeats Xianling Qiang forces near Dingxi, attacks [[LinChangQiang|Lin Chang]]'s Beidi position, kills Lin Chang's wife and children, burns his residence, seizes livestock, and later sends the surrendered Qiang Yu Gui to assassinate [[DuJigongLateHan|Du Jigong]].

Hanji 790 then shows the same kind of frontier success becoming fatal. Ren Shang and [[MaXianLateHan|Ma Xian]] pursue Langmo for more than sixty days and win at Fuping, but Langmo escapes. Deng Zun later receives decisive court credit for arranging Langmo's death, and Ren Shang's resentment turns a real merit record into a superior-linked credit fight. The court moves against him for inflated enemy-kill reports and large-scale embezzlement, executes him, exposes the body, and confiscates his household property.

## Key Characteristics

- Early implementer of Yu Xu's light-cavalry answer to mounted Qiang mobility.
- Militarily effective in the Qiang theater across direct fighting, pursuit, and proxy assassination.
- Willing to target enemy households and leadership networks, not only battlefield formations.
- Politically exposed because his successes sit beside Deng Zun's regency-family proximity.
- Vulnerable to the gap between unfinished battlefield victory and court-recognized completion.
- His fall shows how command reports, money, gifts, and credit claims can be bundled into a lethal case.

## Qualifications

The current page does not decide whether every charge against Ren Shang was factually true, politically selected, or both. The bounded synthesis is that his military competence did not protect him once success had to be adjudicated through Deng-family credit, court reporting, and financial accusation. Hanji 788 adds that his competence should not be flattened into failure: before the fatal credit dispute, he appears as the executor of a sensible mounted-force adaptation.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 788's light-cavalry implementation before Ren Shang's Hanji 789-790 Qiang-front and credit-conflict layers.
- Reframed Ren Shang as both a successful mobility-adaptation commander and a later warning about superior-linked credit exposure.
