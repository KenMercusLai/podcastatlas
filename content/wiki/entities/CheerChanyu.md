## Overview

[[CheerChanyu|车儿]] is the Southern Xiongnu chanyu in the 158 CE frontier layer preserved by [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-844-ruhe-liyu-bubaizhidi-lsp49uw8atyiufrf-ryo-9ctos5z|Hanji 844]]. His profile centers on the post-surrender dispute between [[ZhangHuanLateHan|张奂]]'s assessment of ruling capacity and [[EmperorHuanOfHan|汉桓帝]]'s preference for hereditary legitimacy and reassurance of a submitted frontier ruler.

## Current Profile

After Zhang Huan's campaign fractures the [[Wuhuan|Wuhuan]]-[[SouthernXiongnu|Southern Xiongnu]] alignment and the Xiongnu submit, Zhang wants to detain Che'er and replace him with the Left Guli King. Emperor Huan refuses the proposal, cites the principle that succession belongs to the son, and orders Che'er released back to the royal court because a chanyu sincerely facing Han should not be punished by deposition.

## Key Characteristics

- Che'er is a submitted Southern Xiongnu ruler whose treatment becomes a test of frontier legitimacy after Han victory.
- Zhang Huan views him as politically inadequate and prefers replacement with another Xiongnu elite.
- Emperor Huan treats hereditary succession and the credibility of surrender terms as more important than Zhang's capacity assessment.
- The case shows a distinction between defeating a frontier opponent and preserving a usable frontier order afterward.

## Evidence

- Post-surrender dispute: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-844-ruhe-liyu-bubaizhidi-lsp49uw8atyiufrf-ryo-9ctos5z|Hanji 844]] says Zhang Huan wants to detain Che'er after judging him unable to govern the Xiongnu.
- Imperial reversal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-844-ruhe-liyu-bubaizhidi-lsp49uw8atyiufrf-ryo-9ctos5z|Hanji 844]] says Emperor Huan rejects the deposition plan and returns Che'er to the royal court.
- Legitimacy logic: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-844-ruhe-liyu-bubaizhidi-lsp49uw8atyiufrf-ryo-9ctos5z|Hanji 844]] frames the decision through Chunqiu-style hereditary order and the need to avoid punishing a loyal submitter.
