# Maximilien Robespierre / 罗伯斯庇尔

## Overview

Maximilien Robespierre was a leading Montagnard revolutionary whose virtue-centered politics, support for the June 1793 insurrection, and later entry into the [[CommitteeOfPublicSafety|Committee of Public Safety]] made him a defining figure of the Reign of Terror.

## Current Profile

The earlier source emphasizes Robespierre's classical self-fashioning and moral vocabulary; the new episode places those ideas inside a concrete transfer of power. He called for popular insurrection against the Convention in May 1793, benefited from the forced Girondin purge, and joined the Committee of Public Safety seventeen days after Danton's removal, as Marat's assassination strengthened the politics of suspicion and uncompromising virtue.

## Key Characteristics

- He framed revolutionary politics through virtue, sincerity, and moral struggle against enemies.
- Unlike Marat, he initially regarded Marat's bloodthirstiness and personality with distaste.
- He publicly called for popular insurrection when lawful good faith seemed exhausted.
- The Girondin purge left his Montagnard faction dominant.
- His committee ascent followed Marat's death and Danton's loss of influence.
