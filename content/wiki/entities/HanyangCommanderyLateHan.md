## Overview

汉阳郡 / Hanyang Commandery (late Han) is a Liangzhou-region commandery whose current wiki profile now has three bounded roles: an early An-era Shanggui rebellion setting, a later Gai Xun appointment branch after Qiang-front survival, and the besieged commandery where [[FuXie|Fu Xie]] chooses office-duty martyrdom after Geng Bi's army collapses.

## Current Profile

Hanji 786 supplies the earliest current layer. Hanyang men Du Qi, [[DuJigongLateHan|Du Jigong]], Wang Xin, and others join Qiang forces and occupy Shanggui. Hanyang administrator Zhao Bo has Du Qi assassinated, but Du Jigong and Wang Xin continue resistance until Tang Xi defeats and kills Wang Xin, after which Du Jigong flees toward the Qiang leader Dianling.

Hanji 930 adds a later appointment branch. After [[GaiXunLateHan|Gai Xun]] survives defeat and captivity in the Qiang field, [[YangYongLateHan|Yang Yong]] recommends him and Gai Xun concurrently holds Hanyang. This makes the commandery part of Gai Xun's reputation-to-office arc before later anti-eunuch material.

Hanji 937 turns Hanyang into the setting of terminal frontier collapse. After [[GengBiLateHan|Geng Bi]]'s army mutinies at [[DiDaoLateHan|Didao]], rebels surround Hanyang while the city has too few troops and no food. Fu Xie refuses escape, sends [[FuGan|Fu Gan]] away under [[YangHuiLateHan|Yang Hui]]'s protection, rejects [[HuangYanLateHan|Huang Yan]]'s surrender mission, and dies fighting.

## Key Characteristics

- Liangzhou-region commandery repeatedly appearing through Qiang-linked disorder and official-risk episodes.
- Early Shanggui rebellion setting where local Han rebels and Qiang alignment overlap.
- Later office assignment for Gai Xun after his Qiang-front reputation proves politically useful.
- Siege setting where food shortage, troop weakness, and prior command collapse make ordinary administration impossible.
- Source-bounded place page, not a full administrative geography reconstruction.

## Qualifications

The page is bounded to three podcast source notes. Hanji 786's Du Qi, Wang Xin, Zhao Bo, Tang Xi, and Shanggui details remain source-scoped; Hanji 937 supplies the most developed Hanyang narrative, while Hanji 930 is an appointment notice rather than a place-history account.
