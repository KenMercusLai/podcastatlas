# John Milton

## Overview

John Milton appears in the bounded source as an English poet, political pamphleteer, and Commonwealth official whose defense of freer print coexisted with support for the execution of Charles I and service under [[OliverCromwell]].

## Current Profile

[[4-were-all-so-17th-century-glt1251829896]] uses Milton as a bridge between seventeenth-century pamphlet culture and modern arguments over expression and platform governance. The episode identifies him as the author of *Paradise Lost*, a secretary during the [[EnglishCommonwealth]], a defender of regicide, and an advocate of freedom of speech amid debate over whether some publications should be controlled.

The profile is intentionally narrow. It shows that support for freer circulation did not sit outside revolutionary power or moral conflict: Milton’s literary, polemical, and official roles belonged to the same contested information order.

## Key Characteristics

- English poet identified with *Paradise Lost*.
- Political writer who defended the execution of Charles I.
- Commonwealth secretary associated by the episode with Cromwell’s regime.
- Advocate of freer printed argument in an age of licensing breakdown and pamphlet proliferation.

## Qualifications

This is a one-episode, conversational profile rather than a full literary biography or analysis of Milton’s political theology. “Freedom of speech” is the source’s modern shorthand and should not be assumed to map exactly onto contemporary rights, universal toleration, or platform governance. The episode does not examine the chronology, institutional limits, exclusions, or later development of Milton’s views.
