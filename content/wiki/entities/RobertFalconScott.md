# Robert Falcon Scott

## Overview

Robert Falcon Scott was a British naval officer and Antarctic expedition leader whose 1910-1912 South Pole campaign combined national prestige, scientific work, experimental transport, and a fatal race against [[RoaldAmundsen]].

## Current Profile

The bounded episode presents Scott neither as a simple incompetent nor as an explorer defeated only by bad luck. He led a large, well-funded expedition with legitimate scientific ambitions, but its people, cargo, patron expectations, and incompatible transport methods created [[ResourceAbundanceCoordinationBurden]]. The overloaded ship, unsuitable ponies, lost and failed motor sledges, and eventual reliance on human hauling made the expedition's apparent strength operationally fragile.

Scott's relationship with [[ClementsMarkham]] also made institutional support a constraint: a powerful patron hostile to dogs and skis could influence decisions despite lacking Antarctic field experience. Scott's death does not erase courage or scientific value, but his own storytelling helped make catastrophe central to his afterlife in [[HeroicFailureMemory]].

## Key Characteristics

- British naval officer leading a large state-and-patron-backed Antarctic expedition.
- Pursued both the symbolic South Pole prize and a broad scientific program.
- Combined motor sledges, ponies, dogs, and human hauling without a coherent proven transport system.
- Deferred to institutional expectations that competed with field evidence and mission focus.
- Became a tragic national figure whose failure proved more narratively durable than Amundsen's success.
