## Overview

赵弟 / Zhao Di is presented in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-375-1-zhichang-qishi-bie-rang-ziji-biancheng-yi-tou-chunlu-li-za4h0ixnq4jym8algtxzyy6os|Hanji 375-1]] as a rider from Shanggui in [[ShangguanJieWesternHan|上官桀]]'s force during the aftermath of the Dayuan campaign. He becomes memorable through one bounded act: while four riders hesitate over a captive king, Zhao Di kills the prisoner and brings the immediate escort problem to an end.

## Current Profile

The source does not provide a full biography, later office, or independent account of Zhao Di's motives. Its profile is a compact courage-and-decision vignette. Zhao Di is described as the youngest of four escorts, yet the first willing to accept responsibility for irreversible action when the group fears that the [[YuchengWesternHan|郁成]] king may escape or be rescued.

The podcast treats that action as career-making decisiveness and links it to the [[BuridanIndecisionEffect|布利丹效应]]. The wiki preserves a harder qualification: ending hesitation and proving that the killing was necessary or lawful are different judgments. The event supports a claim about taking action under perceived time pressure, not a general rule that violent speed is superior to restraint.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han rider identified with Shanggui and the Dayuan-expedition aftermath.
- Youngest member of a four-rider escort in the episode's account.
- Acts while the group fears captive escape or rescue and cannot agree who should strike first.
- Remembered for killing the captive Yucheng king and completing the delivery.
- Used by the host as a decisiveness example rather than documented through a full career narrative.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to one popular-history source. Zhao Di's exact age, rank beyond “rider,” private reasoning, later career, and the necessity or legality of killing the captive are not established here. The transcript writes his name as “赵帝” and Shanggui as “上规”; the wiki uses the historically legible forms 赵弟 and 上邽 while keeping all biographical conclusions source-scoped.
