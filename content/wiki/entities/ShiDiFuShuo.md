## Current Profile
The current wiki evidence shows the show as a long-form conversational psychology program that moves between interviews, listener letters, and host self-disclosure. Episode 472 uses clinical language, host self-observation, family examples, and intimate-relationship reflection to make anxiety observable and workable for a general audience. Episodes 471 and 468 add counselor-letter formats in which Steve uses listener problems to examine complex trauma, omnipotent self-blame, intrusive thoughts, emotional flashbacks, compulsive reasoning, unsafe therapy experiences, and the need to recover bodily and emotional self-contact. Episode 469 extends the same embodied and relational orientation into sex, treating sexual education and counseling as ways to discuss desire, shame, dysfunction, AI companionship, adult products, and responsible exploration without collapsing the topic into either spectacle or moral judgment. Episode 467 reveals the show-level value system behind these episodes: people need space to tell their story, be seen, contact vulnerability, and rebuild vitality through concrete relationships and practices. Episode 465 adds a parenting branch: the show can use fatherhood not as a lifestyle verdict but as a route into attention, surrender, death-facing intimacy, child development, information overload, and the patience needed to witness growth.

## Key Characteristics
- Long-form interview format centered on psychological self-understanding and relationship repair.
- Uses host self-disclosure to translate clinical concepts into concrete body, family, and everyday interaction examples.
- Frames anxiety as a practical regulation and support problem rather than a moral weakness.
- Uses listener letters to turn everyday relational distress into bounded public psychoeducation.
- Treats psychology as care-oriented language that can become harmful when used for self-surveillance.
- Treats sex and intimacy as psychologically serious subjects shaped by body, relationship, social norms, and responsibility.
- Repeatedly separates situational difficulty, system pressure, and old trauma alarm from the conclusion that the person is defective, while treating self-narration, love, embodied community, and parenting as sources of vitality and development inquiry.
