## Overview

黄生 / Huang Sheng is the Huang-Lao-side disputant in Hanji 392-3 who defends fixed ruler-minister hierarchy against Yuan Gusheng's Confucian defense of Tang-Wu revolution.

## Current Profile

In [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-392-3-daodi-zenme-zuo-caishi-yige-hao-diwang-lgrcpyv1mf14axsq6hnqxaymrpu0|Hanji 392-3]], Huang Sheng argues before [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]] that [[ShangTang|商汤]] overthrowing Xia Jie is rebellion rather than legitimate revolution. His hat-and-shoe analogy treats hierarchy as positionally fixed: the ruler remains above the minister even when the ruler is morally worse, so ministers may remonstrate but may not overturn the ruler. The episode uses his position as a foil for [[EarlyConfucianResistancePolitics|early Confucian resistance politics]].

## Key Characteristics

- Huang-Lao-side disputant in the episode's Tang-Wu revolution debate.
- Defender of positional hierarchy over people-based mandate transfer.
- Foil whose argument lets Yuan Gusheng expose the danger of condemning Han Gaozu's anti-Qin founding.

## Evidence

- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-392-3-daodi-zenme-zuo-caishi-yige-hao-diwang-lgrcpyv1mf14axsq6hnqxaymrpu0|Hanji 392-3]] gives Huang the hat-and-shoe analogy: old cap still belongs on the head, new shoes still belong below.

Ruler-remonstrance boundary:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-392-3-daodi-zenme-zuo-caishi-yige-hao-diwang-lgrcpyv1mf14axsq6hnqxaymrpu0|Hanji 392-3]] presents Huang as saying a ruler may err, but a minister's role is remonstrance rather than overthrow.
