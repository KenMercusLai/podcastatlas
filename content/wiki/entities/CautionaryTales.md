# Cautionary Tales

## Overview

Cautionary Tales is [[TimHarford]]'s narrative podcast about historical mistakes, unstable reputations, and the practical lessons people draw from them, represented here through [[WilliamMcGonagall]] and the South Pole contest between [[RobertFalconScott]] and [[RoaldAmundsen]].

## Current Profile

The bounded episodes combine narrated history with behavioral and organizational interpretation. The McGonagall episode turns confidence in the audience's judgment of failure into the object of scrutiny. The Antarctic episode turns Scott's apparent advantages into [[ResourceAbundanceCoordinationBurden]] and Amundsen's apparent disadvantages into [[OperationalMethodFit]].

The show preserves tension between instruction and uncertainty. It offers usable lessons about [[EverydayCreativeAgency]], focus, method fit, and institutional constraint while refusing to let a practical winner automatically become a moral hero. It also examines how catastrophe and storytelling can generate [[HeroicFailureMemory]] more readily than competent success.

## Key Characteristics

- Narrative history organized around mistakes, judgment, and practical lessons.
- Uses biography and vivid scenes to make abstract behavioral questions concrete.
- Revises its apparent moral as system details and competing interpretations accumulate.
- Connects historical material to current creative, technological, startup, and organizational questions.
- Preserves uncertainty when the available anecdotes cannot establish motive.
- Separates effective method from ethical endorsement and tragic memory from operational merit.
