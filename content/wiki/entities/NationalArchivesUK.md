# The National Archives (United Kingdom)

## Overview

The National Archives is the United Kingdom archival institution that hosted the exhibition *Treason: People, Power and Plot* and the episode's document-based tour of British treason history.

## Current Profile

The episode presents the institution as a public-history setting where legal change becomes visible through material records. Its exhibition places the 1352 Treason Act beside trial volumes, declarations, prosecution papers, proclamations, and court-martial records, allowing visitors to compare how governments documented and enforced changing claims of allegiance.

## Key Characteristics

- Custodian and exhibitor of British state and legal records.
- Host of *Treason: People, Power and Plot*.
- Connects medieval legislation to modern treason cases through archival objects.
- Makes the production, survival, and later reuse of official records part of historical interpretation.

## Evidence

- Exhibition scope: [[249-treason-in-modern-britain-part-2-glt2159531504]] says the exhibition follows treason records from the 1352 act to modern cases.
- Archival method: [[249-treason-in-modern-britain-part-2-glt2159531504]] examines retrospective trial compilations, Restoration papers, annotated prosecution material, proclamations, and courts-martial.
