# John Stuart Mill / 穆勒

## Overview

John Stuart Mill / 穆勒 is used across the wiki as a liberal theorist of individual freedom and as a critic of concentrating society's most capable people inside the state.

## Current Profile

In the law episode, Mill supplies the freedom principle for testing when law may restrict self-regarding conduct. Drug use, self-harm, helmets, seatbelts, and voluntary slavery expose a boundary inside liberalism: present choice cannot straightforwardly justify permanently surrendering future freedom.

In the exams episode, Mill is invoked for an institutional warning. If civil-service selection draws all the “best and brightest” into government, it can weaken competence and independence outside the state while helping an official elite reproduce itself. Together the sources present liberty as requiring both limits on coercion and a social distribution of capable agency.

## Key Characteristics

- Liberal reference point for distinguishing self-regarding conduct from harm to others.
- Recognizes limited boundaries where a choice would extinguish future liberty.
- Warns against concentrating talent and status inside the civil service.
- Connects personal freedom to institutional pluralism rather than anti-state slogans alone.

- State concentration: [[84-exams-glt6507641896]] attributes to Mill a warning that recruiting all leading talent into government could weaken society outside it and create a self-perpetuating elite.

## Qualifications

Both sources use Mill selectively. They do not reconstruct his complete political philosophy, historical context, writings on education or bureaucracy, or the exact textual basis and wording of every attributed warning.

## What Changed

- Added Mill's warning about talent concentration, civil-service dominance, and elite reproduction.
- Reframed the profile around distributed agency across both individual liberty and institutional life.
