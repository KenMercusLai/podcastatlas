# New Balance

## Overview
New Balance is a running and lifestyle footwear brand whose current wiki profile combines fit-led product appeal, performance-footwear competition, resale-market demand, and active protection of its N-shaped brand mark.

## Current Profile
New Balance competes through comfort, lasts, width choices, running credibility, and lifestyle adoption rather than only elite racing technology. Rising demand on [[GOAT]] shows that this mix can carry into sneaker resale, while the reported lawsuit against [[Decathlon]] shows that recognizable visual identity is treated as part of the same consumer-brand asset.

## Key Characteristics
- Combines running-function credibility with lifestyle and sneaker-fashion demand.
- Is associated with fit, comfort, lasts, and width choice in the performance-footwear market.
- Competes under attention pressure from carbon-plate and super-foam racing products.
- Treats its long-used N mark as a protectable source identifier.

## Evidence
- Product position: [[e241-paoxie-jishu-diedaishi-malasong-paojin-2-xiaoshi-kao-ren-haishi-kao-xie]] places New Balance among comfort- and fit-oriented running brands while keeping it inside the broader racing-technology competition.
- Market demand: [[tsr-s3-eddylu-v2-tsr-s3-eddylu-v2]] reports more than 100% year-over-year New Balance growth on GOAT in Eddy Lu's market commentary.
- Brand protection: [[jinghu-gaotie-zhongqiujie-qian-chuxian-jiangjia-xinbailun-qisu-dikanong-qinquan-1017386449]] reports New Balance's allegation that Keep Run's K mark could be confused with its N mark and notes earlier enforcement actions.

## Qualifications
The GOAT growth figure is platform-specific and source-reported. The Decathlon dispute is an allegation rather than a judicial finding; the available source does not include Decathlon's detailed response or consumer-confusion evidence.

## What Changed
- Added trademark enforcement as a second expression of New Balance's consumer-brand moat.
- Distinguished a filed consumer-confusion claim from a proven infringement finding.
