## Current Profile

The source set uses Shangguan An to show how kinship can be converted into power and then discarded under higher stakes. In Hanji 396-1, he treats his young daughter as a route into palace status, bypassing Huo Guang through Ding Wairen. In Hanji 400-1, the pattern becomes harsher: publicly, the conspirators propose killing Huo Guang, deposing [[EmperorZhaoOfHan|汉昭帝刘弗陵]], and installing [[LiuDanYanKingWesternHan|燕王刘旦]]. Privately, Shangguan An plans to lure Liu Dan in, kill him, and enthrone his father [[ShangguanJieWesternHan|上官桀]].

His most revealing moment is the question of what would happen to the young empress, his own daughter. Shangguan An answers through a hunting metaphor that implies the pursuit of the deer matters more than the hare. The episode reads this as the point where kinship, marriage alliance, and office all collapse under the desire for supreme power.

## Key Characteristics

- Young Shangguan-family conspirator in the Han Zhao regency crisis.
- Father who uses his six-year-old daughter's palace entry as a family-advancement instrument.
- Office-seeking outer-relative actor whose resentment grows after Huo Guang refuses private appointments and Ding Wairen advancement.
- Planner who treats Liu Dan as a disposable claimant rather than a trusted ally.
- Son of Shangguan Jie and father-linked beneficiary of the second-stage coup plan.
- Figure used by the episode to illustrate kinship sacrifice under power competition.
- Destroyed after the leaked conspiracy is reported to Han Zhao.
