# Hamilton-Burr Duel

## Overview

The Hamilton-Burr duel was the July 11, 1804 encounter at Weehawken, New Jersey, in which [[AaronBurr|Aaron Burr]] fatally wounded [[AlexanderHamilton|Alexander Hamilton]].

## Current Profile

The episode treats the duel as the point where founding-era party rivalry, personal reputation, and formal honor procedure overwhelmed ordinary political channels. Burr challenged Hamilton after a reported insult during the 1804 New York governorship campaign, while Hamilton accepted despite debts, family obligations, religious objections, and the earlier death of [[PhilipHamilton|Philip Hamilton]] in a duel. The source keeps the shot sequence and Burr's intent ambiguous because Hamilton's and Burr's seconds gave conflicting accounts.

## Key Characteristics

- Fatal American political duel between Hamilton and Burr.
- Escalation point in the [[FirstPartySystem]] after the 1800 Jefferson-Burr tie and Burr's failed 1804 New York campaign.
- Rule-bound but illegal or semi-illegal affair of honor shaped by seconds, witnesses, command procedure, and pistol choice.
- Tragic repetition of family history because Hamilton's son Philip had died in a duel nearby in 1801.
- Evidentiary puzzle because the participants' seconds disagreed over who fired first and whether Hamilton's shot was intentional.
- Reputation-transforming event that made Hamilton a martyr and Burr a ruined political figure.

## Evidence

- Political prehistory: [[685-hamilton-duel-to-the-death-part-3-glt9231431046]] traces Burr's resentment through Hamilton's role in the 1800 election, Hamilton's opposition to Burr's New York governorship bid, and the published "despicable" remark.
- Honor procedure: [[685-hamilton-duel-to-the-death-part-3-glt9231431046]] explains dueling as a formal culture of challenge, seconds, weapon rules, and possible settlement before gunfire.
- Hamilton's acceptance: [[685-hamilton-duel-to-the-death-part-3-glt9231431046]] says Hamilton had moral objections, family obligations, debts, and Philip's death in memory, but still believed political usefulness required honor defense.
- Fatal exchange: [[685-hamilton-duel-to-the-death-part-3-glt9231431046]] reports the Weehawken crossing, the Church pistols, Hamilton's glasses, two shots, Burr unharmed, and Hamilton mortally wounded.
- Aftermath: [[685-hamilton-duel-to-the-death-part-3-glt9231431046]] describes Hamilton's July 12 death, Eliza's memory work, Burr's flight, murder charges that did not go to trial, and later treason prosecution.

## Qualifications

The episode does not resolve the central evidentiary dispute. Hamilton's second said Burr fired first and Hamilton discharged accidentally after being hit; Burr's second said Hamilton fired first and Burr then answered. The page therefore records the duel's political and honor-culture meaning without claiming certainty about intent.
