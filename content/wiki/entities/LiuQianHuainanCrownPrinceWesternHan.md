## Overview

淮南太子刘迁 / Liu Qian is the son and crown prince of [[LiuAn|淮南王刘安]] whose current profile begins with interpreting an accidental sword injury by [[LeiBeiWesternHan|雷被]] as deliberate, obstructing Lei Bei's departure, and proposing an envoy ambush. It continues through favored household status and conflict with the marginalized [[LiuBuhaiWesternHan|刘不亥]] branch, then ends with threatened arrest, failed mobilization, [[WuBeiWesternHan|伍被]]'s confession, and execution after Liu An's death.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-294-2-yi-chang-bijian-yinbao-de-jingtian-daan-lpqd6by5nn9k8p28m30yrzxanxql|Hanji 294-2]] supplies the earliest current conflict. It says Lei Bei accidentally wounds Liu Qian during a sword bout, but Liu Qian treats the injury as intentional. When Lei Bei later seeks to leave for central military service, Liu Qian allegedly denounces him to Liu An and helps block the departure, partly because the household fears disclosure.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-295-doufu-de-famingzhe-jing-shi-zaofan-de-fanwang-libnwsqalvpzymlnbaewua4s6miw|Hanji 295]] supplies the next operational role. After Lei Bei's appeal brings an imperial envoy to Huainan, Liu Qian proposes hiding armed men, killing the envoy if he announces punishment, and rising immediately. The envoy's deferential presentation causes Liu An to dismiss the ambush, so Liu Qian's plan is prepared but not executed.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-302-1-xihan-shuzhi-neidou-jieguo-kengdie-ye-kengye-ln6rfk8nzoyeqxduik_edlqqiyug|Hanji 302-1]] supplies a household prehistory. It says Liu An and the queen favor Liu Qian while Liu Qian fails to treat elder non-heir son Liu Buhai as an elder brother. Liu Buhai's son then discusses removing his crown-prince uncle; after the plan leaks, Liu Qian allegedly has the nephew seized and tortured. The episode establishes hostility and coercive response, but its final segment appears to give the nephew the same name as Liu Qian, so the nephew remains unnamed here.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-302-2-kuang-gei-laoban-po-lengshui-wubei-you-duo-gan-shuo-llp7lwbpkj16w0rme6p-qs9jrjds|Hanji 302-2]] gives that household breach a procedural consequence. A letter attributed to Liu Jian reaches the emperor, and the Henan investigation is said to uncover Liu Qian and his associates' rebellion activity. The note does not independently specify Liu Qian's acts, but it links internal denunciation to the formal case that later makes him the arrest target in Hanji 303-1.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-303-1-huainanwang-ruhe-bei-jiujiexin-bisi-de-ljtqn-nrxyf8yqx-yowsainkzmjc|Hanji 303-1]] places Liu Qian at the later crisis point. After the court learns of his intent and sends officers to Huainan to arrest him, father and son reportedly plan to summon and kill the kingdom's senior officials before rising. Almost no one answers. Liu Qian then judges that the usable officials have already been removed, offers to go to court and take responsibility for Liu An, and attempts suicide but survives within the excerpt.

The adjacent sources supply disclosure and outcome rather than a fuller biography. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-304-1-gudai-ruhe-zuo-shuangmian-jiandie-lu05uaxp5b_buvl5go2bn7iy9q_x|Hanji 304-1]] says [[LeiBeiWesternHan|雷被]] had earlier reported Liu An and Liu Qian and that Liu Qian's vulnerability to arrest made Wu Bei fear losing the value of first disclosure. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-303-2-saiweng-shima-de-zuozhe-shi-zenme-si-de-lp9u6yu1la2w6sfqwqfikcvgq9pt|Hanji 303-2]] places Liu Qian and the queen in the post-confession arrest sweep and says they are killed with the alleged co-conspirators after Liu An's suicide.

## Key Characteristics

- Crown prince and favored son of Liu An, contrasted with the marginalized elder-brother branch of Liu Buhai.
- Royal heir who allegedly turns an accidental sword injury into suspicion, obstructs Lei Bei's departure, becomes a co-subject of his early report, and proposes an ambush keyed to the envoy's apparent intent.
- Uncle reported to have answered a leaked removal plan by arresting and torturing his nephew.
- Alleged conspirator exposed through a household denunciation and formal Henan investigation, then directly targeted by the court mission that forces the project into crisis.
- Participant in a proposed roundup of senior kingdom officials that fails for lack of attendance.
- Heir who reportedly offers to assume responsibility for his father and attempts suicide.
- Arrested heir whose possible testimony changes Wu Bei's incentive to confess and who is later reported killed with the queen and alleged co-conspirators.

## Qualifications

The current sources do not reconstruct Liu Qian's life, independent evidence, or exact legal disposition. Hanji 294-2's injury, intent attribution, resentment, disclosure fear, denunciation, and obstruction remain episode-attributed. Hanji 295's ambush plan, hidden attackers, and intent remain episode-attributed; its “刘谦” is normalized to Liu Qian from the father, title, and continuing case. Hanji 302-1's household hierarchy, motives, leaked plot, and torture claim remain episode-attributed, and its duplicated “刘谦” makes the nephew's identity unsafe to normalize. Hanji 302-2 reports that investigation uncovered Liu Qian and his associates' plot but does not provide the letter, testimony, or acts needed to adjudicate the allegation independently; its “刘建” remains source-scoped and is not merged with the Jiangdu king. Hanji 303-1's dialogue, official-attendance sequence, offer to take responsibility, and suicide attempt likewise remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- The profile now begins with the sword-bout injury and Liu Qian's source-scoped decision to read accident as intent.
- The blocked enlistment now appears as both personal retaliation and an attempt to contain possible household disclosure.
