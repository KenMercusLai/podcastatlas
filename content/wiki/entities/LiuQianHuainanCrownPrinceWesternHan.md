## Overview

淮南太子刘迁 / Liu Qian is the son and crown prince of [[LiuAn|淮南王刘安]] whose alleged involvement and arrest help trigger [[WuBeiWesternHan|伍被]]'s late confession and whose reported execution closes the Huainan royal line in the current source set.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-304-1-gudai-ruhe-zuo-shuangmian-jiandie-lu05uaxp5b_buvl5go2bn7iy9q_x|Hanji 304-1]] supplies only a narrow case role. It says [[LeiBeiWesternHan|雷被]] earlier reported Liu An and Liu Qian, and that Liu Qian's later arrest made Wu Bei fear that another insider would confess first and leave him without bargaining value.

The current evidence therefore supports Liu Qian's place in the disclosure sequence, not a complete biography, independent account of the conspiracy, or adjudication of his precise conduct.

The outcome layer places Liu Qian and the queen under arrest after Wu Bei's disclosure, with officers surrounding the palace and seizing royal guests; following Liu An's suicide, the episode says Liu Qian, the queen, and co-conspirators are killed. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-303-2-saiweng-shima-de-zuozhe-shi-zenme-si-de-lp9u6yu1la2w6sfqwqfikcvgq9pt|Hanji 303-2]]

## Key Characteristics

- Crown prince and son of Liu An in the Huainan royal house.
- Alleged co-subject of Lei Bei's early denunciation.
- Arrested figure whose expected testimony changes Wu Bei's incentive to confess.
- Royal heir reported killed with the queen and alleged co-conspirators after Liu An's death.

## Evidence

Denunciation and arrest:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-304-1-gudai-ruhe-zuo-shuangmian-jiandie-lu05uaxp5b_buvl5go2bn7iy9q_x|Hanji 304-1]] places Liu Qian in Lei Bei's report and says his arrest prompts Wu Bei to disclose the plot before being implicated by someone else.

Final punishment:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-303-2-saiweng-shima-de-zuozhe-shi-zenme-si-de-lp9u6yu1la2w6sfqwqfikcvgq9pt|Hanji 303-2]] places Liu Qian's arrest in the immediate post-confession sweep and reports his later execution with the queen and co-conspirators.

## Qualifications

The current sources do not reconstruct Liu Qian's life, specific acts, arrest procedure, confession, independent evidence, or exact legal disposition. They establish only his role in the Huainan-case disclosure and punishment sequence as presented by two adjacent podcast episodes.

## What Changed

- The profile now includes the reported arrest sweep and execution without inferring a fuller biography or independent guilt finding.
