# Dan Carlin

## Overview

Dan Carlin is the host of [[HardcoreHistory]] and the guest in a [[TheRestIsHistory]] crossover about historical memory, moral judgment, Cold War contingency, propaganda, and whether the past can be known.

## Current Profile

In [[184-historys-biggest-questions-with-dan-carlin-part-2-glt1976082798]], Carlin treats history as useful perspective without denying its capacity to keep grievance politically alive. His approach favors conditional answers: flawed people can produce consequential achievements, the West could have lost the Cold War, propaganda can distort the propagandist, and the past remains knowable even when every surviving account is mediated.

## Key Characteristics

- Uses counterfactual choices to expose hidden assumptions about privilege, danger, technology, and political control.
- Defends historical knowledge while emphasizing uneven evidence, source loss, literary framing, and retrospective manipulation.
- Separates recognition of an achievement from moral endorsement of the person responsible for it.
- Treats political outcomes such as Soviet collapse and the Ukraine invasion as contingent rather than inevitable.

## Evidence

- Historical method and judgment: [[184-historys-biggest-questions-with-dan-carlin-part-2-glt1976082798]] records Carlin's arguments about mediated knowledge, three-dimensional historical figures, and belief as a causal historical reality.
- Contingency and strategy: [[184-historys-biggest-questions-with-dan-carlin-part-2-glt1976082798]] presents his claims that the West could have lost the Cold War and that Putin may have misread Ukraine through propaganda and historical analogy.

## Qualifications

This profile comes from one conversational crossover rather than a systematic account of Carlin's career or body of work. Its Cold War, Soviet technology, and Ukraine claims are arguments made in the episode, not independently verified conclusions.
