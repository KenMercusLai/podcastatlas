## Overview

Hélène Landemore is the political scientist who helped design the [[ConnecticutCitizensAssembly]] and explained its use of random selection, demographic diversity, collective intelligence, and sustained deliberation.

## Current Profile

In the source, Landemore argues that elections select an unusually narrow and power-seeking subset of citizens, while carefully composed random groups can bring broader experience into public judgment. She treats procedural learning, friendship, and civic affection as parts of democratic capacity, but also acknowledges that the publication controversy cost organizers goodwill among participants.

## Key Characteristics

- She draws on ancient Greek selection by lot to challenge election as democracy's only representative mechanism.
- She argues that collective intelligence becomes visible through practice rather than through participants' prior credentials.
- She emphasizes demographic composition and diverse lived experience alongside random selection.
- She describes friendship across political and social difference as a recurring product of well-designed deliberation.
- She defended timely publication as part of the organizers' promise to participants and the public.

## Qualifications

- The page records Landemore through one edited episode summary rather than her publications or a full account of her democratic theory.
- Examples from Ireland, France, and the Netherlands establish precedent but do not by themselves demonstrate comparative effectiveness.
- The source's observations of collective intelligence and friendship do not isolate the effects of selection, facilitation, compensation, instruction, or time.
