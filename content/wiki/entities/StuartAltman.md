# Stuart Altman

## Overview

Stuart Altman appears as the Nixon administration health-policy staffer who helped write the 1974 reform proposal and conducted secret compromise talks with [[TedKennedy]]'s staff.

## Current Profile

In [[from-the-big-dig-mitt-ted]], Altman connects presidential ambition to working-level policy design. He is credited with much of [[RichardNixon]]'s employer-based coverage proposal and later met a senior Kennedy aide beneath St. Mark's Episcopal Church to reconcile government direction with private insurance competition. His later recollection that Kennedy believed they should have completed the deal makes him a witness to the episode's central lost-opportunity judgment.

## Key Characteristics

- Nixon administration health-policy designer associated with the 1974 proposal.
- Working-level negotiator seeking a public-private compromise with Kennedy's staff.
- Later recipient of Kennedy's expressed regret over abandoning the talks.

## Evidence

### Policy design and negotiation

- [[from-the-big-dig-mitt-ted]] credits Altman with largely writing Nixon's proposal and describes his June-July 1974 secret meetings with a Kennedy health aide.

### Retrospective witness

The source is a structured episode summary rather than Altman's papers or a full legislative history. It does not establish his sole authorship, every negotiated term, or the congressional viability of the emerging bargain.
