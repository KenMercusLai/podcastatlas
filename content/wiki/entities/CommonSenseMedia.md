# Common Sense Media

## Overview
Common Sense Media appears in the bounded sources as a child- and teen-safety organization producing or supporting independent assessments of AI chatbot risks. Its work spans companion-style mental-health use and the reliability of teen-focused product safeguards.

## Current Profile
The earlier source pairs Common Sense Media with [[StanfordUniversity]] in recommending that minors not rely on consumer chatbots for mental-health support. The newer [[YouthAISafetyInstitute|Youth AI Safety Institute]] assessment tests whether a teen-focused [[ChatGPT]] experience delivers its promised crisis, parental, learning, and relational protections. Together, the sources place the organization at the boundary between product claims and independently observed behavior, while keeping company methodological objections visible.

## Key Characteristics
- Centers children and teenagers in AI product-risk assessment.
- Examines emotional support, crisis escalation, education, and relationship design together.
- Converts simulated-use findings into guidance for parents, educators, clinicians, and policymakers.
- Treats product safeguards as helpful baselines rather than substitutes for human support.
- Advocates stronger independent access to evidence about company safety systems.

## Evidence
- Mental-health chatbot boundary - [[using-ai-chatbots-for-mental-health-support-poses-serious-risks-for-teens-report-finds]] cites a Common Sense Media and Stanford report arguing that consumer chatbots are unreliable mental-health support for minors.
- Teen-product evaluation - [[tech-20261105-1105-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261105-1105-mp-tech-pod-128]] reports a roughly 4,000-prompt assessment finding missed crisis resources, bypassable study guidance, and continued friend-like personalization.
- Human-support position - [[using-ai-chatbots-for-mental-health-support-poses-serious-risks-for-teens-report-finds]] and [[tech-20261105-1105-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261105-1105-mp-tech-pod-128]] recommend education and adult or professional involvement rather than solitary teen reliance on AI for difficult personal circumstances.

## Qualifications
The source set does not include either underlying assessment, full methodology, prompt corpus, or comparative product results. The newer source also preserves [[OpenAI]]'s claim that the evaluation does not accurately reflect real-world safeguard behavior, leaving methodological validity contested rather than settled.
