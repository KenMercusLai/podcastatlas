## Overview
孙奋 / Sun Fen is the wealthy Fufeng man in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-833-tanguan-neng-you-duo-lipu-kanwan-dadieyanjing-lqudlijjyqmcro-wsmrvwkodgt72|Hanji 833]] whose household becomes a concentrated victim case for [[LiangJiLateHan|梁冀]]'s coercive extraction. The episode says Liang gives Sun Fen a horse, demands a huge loan, retaliates when Sun Fen pays only part, fabricates a charge against Sun Fen's mother, has Sun Fen and his brother tortured to death in prison, and seizes the family's wealth.

## Current Profile
Sun Fen's profile is victim-centered. He is not important because of independent office or factional activity; he matters because the source uses his case to show how Liang Ji's power turns a coerced "loan" into lethal punishment when the target does not satisfy the demand. His wealth makes him visible, but visibility is a liability inside a network that can fabricate criminal identity and use prison violence to transfer property.

The source also makes Sun Fen part of a broader pattern rather than an isolated anecdote. Sun-family relatives and Liang-linked actors allegedly identify rich households, invent charges, and demand payment; Sun Fen's partial compliance still leads to destruction. That makes the case evidence for the limits of appeasement under predatory power.

## Key Characteristics
- Wealthy Fufeng victim targeted because Liang Ji wants access to his money.
- Coerced payment case where a gift and loan request function as extortion rather than ordinary exchange.
- False-accusation victim whose mother is recoded as a stolen Liang-family servant to justify seizure.
- Household-collateral case in which Sun Fen and his brother die in prison.
- Property-transfer case where torture and accusation end in confiscation by Liang Ji.
- Source-bounded figure whose current significance is the coercive-extraction pattern, not a full biography.

## Qualifications
The page is source-bounded to Hanji 833. The exact legal wording of the accusation, the brother's name, and the property amount are preserved as source-note claims rather than independently reconstructed biography. Sun Fen should remain disambiguated by Fufeng because the name alone may collide with other Sun-family figures.
