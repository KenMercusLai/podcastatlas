# Vitellius

## Overview

Vitellius was the Lower German governor elevated by the Rhine armies during the [[YearOfTheFourEmperors|Year of the Four Emperors]], who defeated Otho before forces supporting [[Vespasian]] overthrew and killed him.

## Current Profile

[[12-days-neros-succession-and-the-fall-of-the-byzantine-empire-glt6148714296]] presents Vitellius as a candidate produced by military mutiny more than personal initiative. The Upper German soldiers who rejected [[Galba]] needed someone with sufficient age, pedigree, and rank to make their revolt an imperial project; their own instigator, Aulus Caecina, was too junior, while the senior Upper German governor appeared ineffective.

Vitellius, governor in Lower Germany, supplied the necessary standing. The episode says he hesitated and associated command with a prophecy of disaster, but his commanders moved south as the Roman succession changed again from Galba to Otho. He defeated Otho and became emperor, only to be defeated and killed by Vespasian's supporters before the end of AD 69. His career shows how armies could select a plausible princeps while leaving the wider contest unresolved.

## Key Characteristics

- Lower German governor with the pedigree and seniority needed by mutinous Rhine commanders.
- Reluctant or hesitant candidate in the episode's account rather than the initial architect of rebellion.
- Beneficiary of provincial military kingmaking after Galba's failure.
- Victor over Otho whose own rule remained dependent on civil-war success.
- Emperor defeated by Vespasian's coalition before the end of AD 69.

## Qualifications

The source offers a compressed account of candidacy and succession rather than a full reign. Vitellius's reluctance, prophetic fear, private motives, and degree of control over his commanders remain dependent on hostile or retrospective ancient traditions, while the political and military stages of the wars against Otho and Vespasian are summarized rather than reconstructed.
