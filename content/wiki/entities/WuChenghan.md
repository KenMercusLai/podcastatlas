# 吴承翰 / Wu Chenghan

## Overview
吴承翰 / Wu Chenghan is the neurosurgery-trained guest in [[yong-guang-tiaokong-shenjingxibao-jinnian-nuobeierjiang-ruhe-gaibian-women-dui-qingxu-de-lijie-chuantai-shengdongjixi-s10e32-df4fa6fe-db30-4fb5-809f-67fe0b42ca3b]], discussing optogenetics, functional neurosurgery, psychiatry, and neurotechnology ethics.

## Current Profile
The source presents Wu as a former neurosurgical clinician and researcher whose Stanford period brought him into contact with [[KarlDeisseroth]] and the surrounding laboratory community. His contribution is translational: he explains why causal circuit manipulation is more informative than observation alone while repeatedly separating animal behavior from human subjective experience and experimental precision from routine clinical readiness.

He also connects technical power to clinical obligation. Historical psychosurgery, medication, TMS, deep-brain stimulation, optogenetics, and future diagnostic tools are evaluated through severity, reversibility, target knowledge, individual variability, informed consent, and the possibility that treatment could become control.

## Key Characteristics
- Combines neurosurgical clinical experience with neuroscience research framing.
- Explains optogenetics as a causal experimental method rather than a routine psychiatric cure.
- Preserves the inference boundary between animal behavior and human subjective experience.
- Distinguishes treatment efficacy from proof of disease cause.
- Treats autonomy and personality continuity as part of neurotechnology safety.
