# 1995 Rugby World Cup

## Overview

The 1995 Rugby World Cup is presented as the public event where post-apartheid [[SouthAfrica]] staged reconciliation through rugby, anthem ceremony, and [[NelsonMandela]]'s embrace of the [[Springboks]].

## Current Profile

The episode focuses on the final at [[EllisPark]], where South Africa defeated New Zealand in extra time. The match's historical meaning comes from its symbolic staging: a formerly isolated country hosted the tournament, an apartheid-coded team became a shared object of support, Springbok players participated in [[NkosiSikelelIAfrika|Nkosi Sikelel' iAfrika]], and Mandela wore the captain's number-six shirt before a mostly Afrikaner crowd.

## Key Characteristics

- First major rugby event after South Africa's return from sporting isolation in the episode's account.
- Hosted by post-apartheid South Africa after readmission to international rugby.
- Final staged at [[EllisPark]] against New Zealand, with South Africa winning in extra time.
- Public test of whether [[Springboks|Springbok]] and anthem symbolism could cross apartheid-era boundaries.
- Event where the crowd's chant for Mandela became a visible reconciliation sign.

## Evidence

- Tournament timing: [[682-south-africa-mandela-and-the-death-of-apartheid-part-6-glt6535658080]] says South Africa returned to international rugby in 1992 and hosted the 1995 tournament after being excluded from the 1987 and 1991 Rugby World Cups.
- Final result: [[682-south-africa-mandela-and-the-death-of-apartheid-part-6-glt6535658080]] says the Springboks beat New Zealand in extra time, with Joel Stransky scoring all South Africa's points.
- Mandela's appearance: [[682-south-africa-mandela-and-the-death-of-apartheid-part-6-glt6535658080]] says Mandela wore [[FrancoisPienaar]]'s number-six Springbok shirt on final day.
- Anthem moment: [[682-south-africa-mandela-and-the-death-of-apartheid-part-6-glt6535658080]] says both anthems had to be sung because the committee had not yet produced the fused national anthem.
- Crowd response: [[682-south-africa-mandela-and-the-death-of-apartheid-part-6-glt6535658080]] presents the "Nelson" chant at Ellis Park as a major reconciliation moment.
