# Treaty of Brétigny

## Overview

The Treaty of Brétigny is the 1360 Anglo-French settlement that exchanged [[EdwardIIIOfEngland|Edward III's]] French crown claim for a greatly enlarged sovereign [[Aquitaine]], but did not create a durable political order.

## Current Profile

The treaty follows Edward's failed effort to take Reims and Paris and the Duke of Lancaster's warning against risking decades of gains in one battle. Its terms resemble the earlier Windsor settlement: England receives extensive territory without French overlordship, while Edward renounces the French throne claim.

The episode treats Brétigny as a strong but unstable English result. War damage, free companies, ransom obligations, taxation, and the unresolved ability of Aquitanian subjects to appeal to [[CharlesVOfFrance|Charles V]] undermine implementation. The Black Prince's refusal of a French summons and Edward's renewal of the crown claim reveal that legal text did not settle allegiance and enforcement.

## Key Characteristics

- Negotiated in 1360 after Edward III's final invasion failed to secure coronation or Paris.
- Grants England a greatly expanded sovereign Aquitaine.
- Requires Edward to renounce his claim to the French crown.
- Builds on earlier Windsor terms while reducing maximal English demands.
- Vulnerable to fiscal, territorial, and appellate implementation disputes.
- Eventually repudiated when war resumes.

## Qualifications

The episode summarizes negotiations and implementation rather than parsing every clause, ratification step, territorial transfer, or later legal argument. The exact status of renunciations and appeals remains source-scoped.
