# Battle of Copenhagen (1801)

## Overview

The Battle of Copenhagen was the 2 April 1801 British attack on Danish naval defenses during the Baltic crisis created by the [[LeagueOfArmedNeutrality|League of Armed Neutrality]].

## Current Profile

The source presents Copenhagen as a battle in which strategy, hydrography, close-range fire, command discretion, and negotiation were inseparable. [[HoratioNelson]] shifted to the shallower-draft Elephant, scouted the shoals, consulted captains, and approached from the south through the King's Deep, but Agamemnon, Bellona, and Russell still grounded. Danish ships and floating batteries resisted fiercely before their capital, inflicting serious British losses even as their line was progressively disabled.

At the battle's command crisis, [[HydeParker]] ordered the action discontinued from a more distant position. Nelson refused to repeat the order and kept the close-action signal flying, while [[EdwardRiou]] obeyed with his frigates and was killed during withdrawal. Nelson then used a flag-of-truce note combining conciliation and threat to secure a ceasefire. Denmark's later temporary neutrality mattered, but Tsar Paul I's assassination and Alexander I's policy change also helped collapse the league, so the battle was an important lever rather than a self-sufficient cause.

## Key Characteristics

- Shoal-constrained naval attack dependent on pilots, reconnaissance, draft, wind, and precise approach routes.
- Close-range gunnery contest fought beside a defended capital and watched by civilians ashore.
- Command crisis in which different positions and ranks produced conflicting judgments about withdrawal.
- Tactical victory converted into political leverage through a coercive but face-saving truce proposal.
- British-centered heroic memory qualified by Danish resistance, casualties, and wider Russian political change.

## Qualifications

The bounded evidence is a single British popular-history episode. Its Danish perspective is limited, the blind-eye story may be mythic, and exact timings, losses, captures, ship positions, reported words, diplomatic motives, and counterfactual claims about withdrawal require broader evidence. The league's collapse cannot be attributed to the battle alone.

## What Changed

- Established Copenhagen as a distinct battle page rather than leaving the 1801 attack compressed inside the long Anglo-Danish survey.
- Joined tactical action to command conflict, negotiation, and wider Baltic political change.
