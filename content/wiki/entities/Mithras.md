# Mithras

## Overview

Mithras was the central god of a Roman mystery cult that became prominent from the first century AD and is often invoked in modern claims that Christianity copied pagan beliefs.

## Current Profile

[[402-christmas-pagan-or-christian-glt8329172184]] distinguishes Roman Mithras from the older Persian Mithra and rejects several alleged Christian parallels as unsupported. In the surviving imagery discussed by the episode, Mithras emerges fully formed from a rock; he is not buried there, and the source finds no ancient account of his death and resurrection after three days.

The Walbrook Mithraeum in [[Londinium]], built around AD 240, supplies a local cult setting. Worship is presented as male, initiatory, rank-structured, and socially connective, while the temple's archaeological recovery shows how a mystery cult becomes legible primarily through space, objects, and imagery rather than a full surviving theology. [[59-the-world-cup-of-gods-part-2-glt6474253297]] adds soldiers, officers, bureaucrats, underground temples, bull-killing imagery, and a mysterious lion-headed figure while preserving the possibility that Roman cult makers used eastern names and prestige to construct something substantially Roman.

## Key Characteristics

- Central deity of a later Roman mystery cult rather than an unchanged continuation of Persian worship.
- Commonly represented as rock-born, a motif different from burial in a rock.
- Associated with solar language while remaining distinct from [[SolInvictus]].
- Frequently used in modern pagan-origin arguments that exceed the ancient evidence presented here.
- Practiced in small initiatory groups whose ranks and social ties are partly recoverable from archaeology.
- Socially associated in these sources with men in military, administrative, and official networks rather than a universal mass congregation.

## Evidence

- Cult distinction: [[402-christmas-pagan-or-christian-glt8329172184]] separates Plutarch's earlier reference to Mithraic mysteries from the later Roman officers' cult.
- Rock-birth imagery: [[402-christmas-pagan-or-christian-glt8329172184]] describes Mithras emerging fully formed from rock and finds no three-day resurrection narrative.
- Solar relationship: [[402-christmas-pagan-or-christian-glt8329172184]] notes solar titles and imagery but also scenes presenting Mithras and Sol as separate figures.
- London cult setting: [[209-londinium-part-1-glt8659020002]] describes the Walbrook temple, its approximate AD 240 date, male initiation, ranks, and modern reconstruction.
- Eastern name and Roman construction: [[59-the-world-cup-of-gods-part-2-glt6474253297]] relates Mithras to Mithra and Mitra while presenting a substantially Roman invention as a serious possibility.
- Cult imagery and social base: [[59-the-world-cup-of-gods-part-2-glt6474253297]] connects underground Mithraea, tauroctony, the lion-headed figure, and male military-administrative networks.

## Qualifications

The episodes do not provide a complete study of Mithraic archaeology, theology, membership, or ritual. The Christmas episode's strongest negative conclusion is limited to alleged Christmas and resurrection parallels for which it reports no ancient evidence. Claims about male membership, ranks, networking, exclusivity, and inability to become a mass religion are reconstructions from archaeology and social pattern rather than surviving participant theology.

## What Changed

- Clarified the difference between eastern name-prestige and the possible Roman construction of the cult.
- Added the military-administrative social base and qualified the claim that exclusivity limited mass appeal.
