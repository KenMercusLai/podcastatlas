# John Gentile

## Overview

John Gentile is the [[RAND]] military historian in [[a-serious-game-a-serious-game|A Serious Game]] who interprets the North Korean collapse simulation as evidence that a Korean Peninsula conflict would be operationally more complex than recent United States wars in Iraq and Afghanistan.

## Current Profile

Gentile's source role is compact but important. His comparison follows the game's accumulation of fortified-border losses, negotiated passage, numerous hazardous sites, missing nuclear material, refugees, congestion, chemical protection, casualties, and possible nuclear use. It converts those details into a warning against treating the mission as a short or straightforward extension of familiar expeditionary warfare.

## Key Characteristics

- Provides military-historical interpretation of the Korean war game.
- Emphasizes the scale and complexity hidden by an abstract mission statement.
- Uses comparison with Iraq and Afghanistan to challenge institutional familiarity.
- Appears as a commentator on the exercise rather than as its primary designer.

## Evidence

### Comparative warning

- [[a-serious-game-a-serious-game|A Serious Game]] attributes to Gentile the judgment that Korean Peninsula conflict would make the U.S. wars in Iraq and Afghanistan appear comparatively simple.

## Qualifications

The episode supplies a forceful comparison but not the criteria, historical analysis, or game documentation needed to evaluate it systematically. Different conflicts involve different political objectives, coalitions, adversaries, terrain, civilian conditions, and measures of complexity. The statement should therefore remain an attributed warning rather than a settled ranking.

- Established Gentile's focused wiki profile as the historian interpreting the Korean simulation's operational scale.
