## Overview

Malinche is the Indigenous interpreter and intermediary in the [[SpanishConquestOfMexico|Spanish conquest of Mexico]], represented here at the surrender of Tenochtitlan and through her constrained post-conquest position.

## Current Profile

Malinche's absence from Tenochtitlan while accompanying [[HernanCortes|Hernan Cortes]] to confront Narvaez shows the structural importance of her interpreting work. [[PedroDeAlvarado|Pedro de Alvarado]] and the garrison faced rumor and ritual without their principal translator before the [[ToxcatlMassacre|Toxcatl massacre]]; the episode does not claim her presence would have prevented it, but her absence narrowed communication at a critical moment. She then survives the [[NocheTriste|Noche Triste]], making her continued role dependent on escape from the retreat that destroyed much of Cortes's force.

At the end of the [[SiegeOfTenochtitlan|siege of Tenochtitlan]], she is present when [[Cuauhtemoc]] is brought before Cortes and helps communicate the victors' demand for gold. The next episode finds her pregnant with Cortes's son Martin when Cortes's wife arrives in the conquered capital. Cortes later takes her on the Honduras expedition, grants her native village as an encomienda, and arranges her marriage to Juan Jaramillo, giving her and her child standing within Spanish law. She survives the expedition, later has a daughter named Maria, and then disappears from the surviving record.

The source resists reading this settlement as a simple romantic conclusion. Property, marriage, parenthood, translation, and legal status all operate inside a violent colonial order that gave Malinche agency but sharply constrained its terms.

## Key Characteristics

- Multilingual intermediary central to contact between Cortes's forces and Indigenous polities.
- Absent interpreter whose departure exposes how dependent the Tenochtitlan garrison was on mediated communication.
- Survivor of the Noche Triste retreat and the campaign's transition from defeat to renewed war.
- Interpreter at Cuauhtemoc's surrender and the immediate Spanish demand for gold.
- Indigenous woman navigating conquest through interpretation, kinship, property, and Spanish legal status.
- Mother of Martin Cortes, making her life part of the symbolic and familial aftermath of conquest.
- Encomienda holder whose native village was transferred to her under the new colonial order.

## Qualifications

These four consecutive episodes do not reconstruct Malinche's whole life or interpreting work. Part 5 establishes her absence, not the counterfactual claim that she could have prevented the massacre; Part 6 records her survival but gives little detail about her actions during the retreat. Her birth date, age, later death, degree of choice, and private relationships remain uncertain. Legal recognition and property should not be mistaken for freedom from the coercive structure of conquest.

## What Changed

- Extended the profile backward to her survival of the Noche Triste without inventing an undocumented tactical role.
- Added her absence during the Toxcatl crisis as evidence of the occupation's dependence on interpretation, without asserting a prevention counterfactual.
