# Adidas

Adidas is a global sportswear company discussed in [[e241-paoxie-jishu-diedaishi-malasong-paojin-2-xiaoshi-kao-ren-haishi-kao-xie]] through [[AdidasUltraBoost]], Adizero Evo SL, and [[AdidasAdiosProEvo3]]. The source frames Adidas as the brand that turned both comfort/lifestyle running shoes and elite marathon racing shoes into commercial narratives.

[[vol-264-ba-shijiebei-zuowei-fangfa-999416048]] adds Adidas as a [[FIFAWorldCup]] marketing case in China. The episode says Adidas moved beyond product launches and store dressing into more than 40 offline activities, including a large Shanghai football event, while also showing local marketing autonomy through a meme-responsive T-shirt and Tencent-linked variety content.

[[zhizhuxia-xinpian-naxia-jinban-guonei-piaofang-ai-moxing-baofa-jiagezhan-1004403588]] adds a current operating-pressure note. The source says Adidas' second-quarter revenue reached a record and China had grown for 13 straight quarters, but profit growth lagged because World Cup marketing spending increased; it also connects China fitness-event activity to local brand activation.

[[vol-271-a-di-da-si-ruhe-chudi-fantan-1007333155]] adds Adidas as a full operating-repair case after its 2021 peak and 2022 shock stack. The episode connects [[SportswearInventoryCycle|inventory cyclicality]], Greater China weakness, [[Yeezy]] termination, the [[Russia]] exit, [[BjornGulden|Bjørn Gulden / 古尔登]]'s channel reset, [[AdidasSamba|Samba]]-led [[RetroProductRevival]], local product autonomy, and renewed running-shoe proof into a recovery that restored confidence without proving a permanent growth reversal.

## Key Claims
- [[AdidasUltraBoost]] is treated as an era-defining commercial product because it brought TPU supercritical-style foam into mainstream running and lifestyle use.
- [[AdidasAdiosProEvo3]] is presented as a lightweight, sellable, elite-racing product tied to the episode's reported sub-2 marathon result.
- The episode says Adidas' Pro Evo line may use scarcity and high price not only because of yield and cost, but also to pull attention toward lower-tier mass-market versions.
- The World Cup source adds a brand-activation layer: Adidas uses event space, local memes, and football lifestyle to turn sponsorship into [[OfflineBrandActivation]].
- Adizero Evo SL is used as an example of racing aesthetics moving into broader fashion and lifestyle consumption.
- The 声动早咖啡 source treats Adidas' marketing spend as a profit tradeoff: event-led growth can strengthen the brand while reducing near-term margin.
- Vol.271 adds that Adidas' recovery depended on operating repair as much as demand heat: lower-quality inventory had to be cleared, dealer relationships rebuilt, and local teams given more room to develop market-specific products.
- The same source treats [[Yeezy]] as a concentrated collaboration risk and [[AdidasSamba|Samba]] as a lower-risk archive revival that could offset part, but not all, of the lost Yeezy contribution.
- Adidas' 2025-2026 challenge in the episode is that recovery metrics improved while inventory began rising again and challenger sportswear brands made the global and China competition field less forgiving.
