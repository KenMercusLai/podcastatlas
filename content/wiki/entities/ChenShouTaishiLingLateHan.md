## Overview

陈寿 / Chen Shou is a Huan-era Taishi ling in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-843-gudai-nongmin-pinkun-de-genyuan-shi-shenme-lutep8yyzanhlb4ao6sope-8edvy|Hanji 843]], disambiguated here from [[ChenShou|陈寿]] the Three Kingdoms historian. The source records him blaming a 158 CE solar eclipse on [[LiangJiLateHan|梁冀]], after which Liang has him arrested, tortured, and killed in prison.

## Current Profile

The current wiki profile is source-bounded to one court-persecution scene. Chen Shou uses the Taishi ling's omen-reading function to identify Liang Ji as politically responsible for a solar eclipse and transmits that accusation through [[XuHuangLateHan|徐璜]]. Liang hears of the report, uses the Luoyang magistrate and prison torture channel against him, and [[EmperorHuanOfHan|汉桓帝]] knows without visibly intervening.

## Key Characteristics

- Chen Shou's role depends on omen interpretation: eclipse blame becomes a politically charged diagnosis, not a neutral astronomical note.
- His accusation targets Liang Ji directly, placing disaster responsibility on an overmighty outer relative.
- His information route through Xu Huang shows palace-mediated reporting rather than open safety for correction.
- His death demonstrates that naming responsibility can trigger retaliatory coercion before ordinary adjudication.
- His page is semantically suffixed because he shares a normalized name with the later historian [[ChenShou|陈寿]].

## Evidence

- Omen accusation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-843-gudai-nongmin-pinkun-de-genyuan-shi-shenme-lutep8yyzanhlb4ao6sope-8edvy|Hanji 843]] says Taishi ling Chen Shou reports through Xu Huang that the eclipse's responsibility lies with Liang Ji.
- Retaliation and death: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-843-gudai-nongmin-pinkun-de-genyuan-shi-shenme-lutep8yyzanhlb4ao6sope-8edvy|Hanji 843]] says Liang Ji has the Luoyang magistrate arrest and torture Chen Shou, who dies in prison.
- Ruler non-action: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-843-gudai-nongmin-pinkun-de-genyuan-shi-shenme-lutep8yyzanhlb4ao6sope-8edvy|Hanji 843]] says Emperor Huan knows of the case but does not act against Liang in this layer.

## Qualifications

The page is bounded to one podcast source note and should not be merged with the Three Kingdoms historian. The source gives the office, accusation, mediation route, and death clearly, but exact transcript variants, the Luoyang magistrate's identity, and full legal procedure remain source-scoped.
