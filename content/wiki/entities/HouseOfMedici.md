## Overview

The House of Medici / 美第奇家族 was the Florentine family whose informal dominance, patronage, 1494 expulsion, and 1512 papal-backed restoration frame both Savonarola's rise and [[NiccoloMachiavelli|Machiavelli]]'s republican service and exile.

## Current Profile

The earlier source presents the Medici as Renaissance patrons and hard political actors. Their return ended the republic Machiavelli served, leading to his imprisonment, torture, exile, and composition of [[ThePrince|《君主论》]]. The newer episode adds the collapse sequence between Lorenzo's death and restoration.

Medici predominance remained informal inside a nominal republic. Lorenzo's son [[PieroDeMedici|Piero]] was expected to inherit control, but automatic succession created unease. French invasion exposed the dependence of family authority on competent negotiation and elite acceptance.

Piero's concessions to [[CharlesVIIIOfFrance|Charles VIII]] precipitated revolution. The Signoria banished the family, placed a bounty on Piero, seized bank assets, auctioned treasures, removed Cosimo's “father of his country” inscription, and transferred Donatello bronzes from the family palace to the civic palace. These changes staged an attempted return of legitimacy to the republic.

The family's absence opened space for [[GirolamoSavonarola|Savonarola]] and a wider council. Its 1512 return under Cardinal Giovanni with papal troops ended that settlement. Later dukes and grand dukes converted informal family dominance into dynastic territorial rule.

## Key Characteristics

- Florentine banking and patronage dynasty with extensive political influence.
- Exercised dominance through republican institutions before hereditary ducal rule.
- Depended on succession competence and acceptance rather than legal sovereignty.
- Lost Florence after Piero's unauthorized concessions during invasion.
- Suffered financial seizure and symbolic transfer of art and civic honor.
- Returned through papal-backed force in 1512 and displaced the republic.

## Qualifications

The profile covers the family through episodes focused on Machiavelli and Savonarola, not a complete banking, patronage, ecclesiastical, or dynastic history. Piero's personality, Charles's demands, popular motives, asset values, later decline, and continuity into ducal rule remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Added the distinction between informal republican dominance and hereditary territorial rule.
- Added the 1494 succession, invasion, expulsion, confiscation, and symbolic-legitimacy sequence.
- Connected the 1512 restoration to both Savonarola's settlement and Machiavelli's punishment.
