# Konrad Lorenz

## Overview
Konrad Lorenz is the historical ethology figure used in the [[HubermanLab]] aggression Essentials episode to introduce fixed action patterns, imprinting, and the idea that aggressive behavior can build pressure before being released.

## Current Profile
The current wiki evidence uses Lorenz as a conceptual ancestor for thinking about aggression as a patterned behavioral sequence rather than a vague mood. [[AndrewHuberman]] presents Lorenz's "hydraulic pressure" idea as a way to describe how hormones, neurotransmitters, experience, and context can accumulate toward aggressive action.

This profile is source-limited. It does not evaluate Lorenz's full scientific career, political history, or later debates around his work; it records only the role he plays in the episode's explanation.

## Key Characteristics
- Historical reference point for formal aggression research and ethology.
- Associated in the source with imprinting and fixed action patterns.
- Used to frame aggression as a sequence with a beginning, middle, and end.
- Provides the pressure metaphor that the episode later connects to hormones, stress, and context.
