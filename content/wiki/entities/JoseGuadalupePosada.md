# Jose Guadalupe Posada

## Overview

Jose Guadalupe Posada was a Mexican printmaker and political cartoonist whose calavera imagery supplies the episode's nineteenth-century genealogy for [[LaCatrina]] and modern Day of the Dead skeleton iconography.

## Current Profile

The source treats Posada's skeletons as satire before they became generalized national-festival imagery. His *La Calavera Garbancera* mocked status aspiration and elite imitation through an elegantly hatted skull. [[DiegoRivera]] later renamed and repositioned the figure as La Catrina, helping move a political cartoon into national art and public memory.

## Key Characteristics

- Nineteenth-century Mexican printmaker and cartoonist.
- Used skulls and skeletons for social and political satire.
- Created the figure first known as *La Calavera Garbancera*.
- Critic of the social order associated with Porfirio Diaz in the episode's account.
- Major visual precursor to [[LaCatrina]] and modern festival imagery.

## Evidence

- Satirical practice: [[264-mexico-day-of-the-dead-glt9022174156]] describes Posada as a cartoonist who filled his work with politically charged skulls and skeletons.
- Character genealogy: [[264-mexico-day-of-the-dead-glt9022174156]] identifies *La Calavera Garbancera* as the precursor of [[LaCatrina]].
- Later reuse: [[264-mexico-day-of-the-dead-glt9022174156]] connects Posada's image to [[DiegoRivera]]'s mural and the wider visual language of Dia de Muertos.

## Qualifications

The episode offers a compressed interpretation of Posada's politics and the image's meaning. It supports a satirical genealogy for La Catrina but does not establish that all modern calavera imagery derives from this one print or has one stable political meaning.

## What Changed

- Created a profile separating nineteenth-century political satire from claims of unchanged ancient skeleton ritual.

## Relationships

- [[DiegoRivera]] - artist who recontextualized the figure in a major mural.
- [[DayOfTheDead]] - festival whose modern iconography absorbed Posada's skeleton imagery.
- [[MexicanPostRevolutionaryCulturalNationalism]] - later national frame through which the image circulated.
