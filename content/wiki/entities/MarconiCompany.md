# Marconi Company

## Overview

The Marconi Company employed Titanic wireless operators Jack Phillips and Harold Bride and provided the communication system described in [[430-titanic-the-iceberg-strikes-part-4-glt2129072034]].

## Current Profile

The episode places Marconi wireless at the boundary between passenger service and ship safety. Titanic used it for private messages, news, and ice reports, while its operators worked for Marconi rather than [[WhiteStarLine]]. After equipment failure created a commercial-message backlog, navigational warnings arrived among competing traffic and were not assembled into one clear operational picture. The case therefore shows that connectivity can expand information without ensuring prioritization, synthesis, or command uptake.

## Key Characteristics

- Provider of Titanic's ship-to-shore wireless capability.
- Employer of operators Jack Phillips and Harold Bride.
- Infrastructure for passenger messages, news reports, and navigational warnings.
- Organizational boundary between communications staff and ship command.
- Part of a workload and prioritization problem after equipment failure produced a backlog.

## Evidence

- Employment boundary: [[430-titanic-the-iceberg-strikes-part-4-glt2129072034]] states that Phillips and Bride worked for Marconi rather than White Star.
- Mixed traffic: [[430-titanic-the-iceberg-strikes-part-4-glt2129072034]] describes passenger telegrams, news used for an onboard bulletin, and ice warnings sharing the system.
- Backlog pressure: [[430-titanic-the-iceberg-strikes-part-4-glt2129072034]] links a Friday-night and Saturday-morning breakdown to accumulated passenger messages.
- Warning handling: [[430-titanic-the-iceberg-strikes-part-4-glt2129072034]] says separate ice reports were not collated into a single clear picture.

## Qualifications

The source does not establish that operator fatigue or commercial traffic alone caused the collision, nor does it provide a complete technical or organizational history of Marconi service. The exact routing, priority, receipt, and bridge handling of each warning remain source-scoped.
