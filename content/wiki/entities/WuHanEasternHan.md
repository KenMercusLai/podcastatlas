## Overview

Wu Han / 吴汉 is synthesized as an Eastern Han founding commander whose current profile now spans both early recruitment/campaign work and a late-life model of disciplined service. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-733-2-gengyan-youzhizhe-shijingcheng-jiangmen-huzi-yongguan-sanjun-lnlfumtdzrigby9ox4v6pzbeogeh|Hanji 733-2]] places him with [[GengYanEasternHan|耿弇]] in Liu Xiu's northern military network; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-721-ta-sha-jianu-ma-gongzhu-weihe-fanbei-shang-lryzcejdnrm4-92esnddfvyjkqli|Hanji 721]] adds his death, deathbed advice, battlefield composure, rapid deployment, and family-asset discipline.

## Current Profile

Hanji 733-2 pairs Wu Han with [[GengYanEasternHan|Geng Yan]] in [[LiuXiu|刘秀]]'s effort to secure soldiers and cavalry from the Youzhou/Shanggu military environment. He later appears alongside Geng Yan in Qinghe-related action, where the episode emphasizes battlefield pursuit, ambush, and tactical coordination rather than Wu Han's independent career.

Hanji 721 adds the late-life evaluation. When Wu Han is seriously ill, Liu Xiu visits and asks for final words. Wu Han says only that the ruler should be especially cautious and not lightly pardon criminals. The episode connects this warning to its larger law-and-personnel theme: useful officials and commanders protect public order by resisting easy exemption.

The same source presents Wu Han's military and household discipline as the basis of his durable merit. In battle adversity, he remains composed, prepares arms, and steadies morale; when ordered to campaign, he can depart the same day. After his family buys land and property while army supplies are strained, he rebukes them and distributes the assets to relatives, showing that founding merit should not become unchecked family accumulation.

## Key Characteristics

- Liu Xiu founding general connected to the northern recruitment mission.
- Operational partner to Geng Yan in the episode's Qinghe campaign narrative.
- Calm battlefield commander who prepares and rallies during adverse conditions.
- Rapid executor of campaign orders.
- Deathbed adviser who tells Liu Xiu to avoid easy amnesty for criminals.
- Family-asset disciplinarian who restrains private accumulation during military strain.
- Supporting figure who helps show how Liu Xiu converts regional military resources and personal discipline into durable command.

## Qualifications

This page still does not attempt a complete account of Wu Han's Eastern Han career. Hanji 733-2 gives a supporting founding-war slice, and Hanji 721 gives a deathbed and character-evaluation slice. Later offices, campaigns, and controversies remain outside the current bounded evidence.
