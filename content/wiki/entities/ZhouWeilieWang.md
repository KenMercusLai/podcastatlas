## Overview

周威烈王 / King Weilie of Zhou is the Zhou ruler whose 403 BCE recognition of the Han, Zhao, and Wei leaders opens [[ZizhiTongjian|《资治通鉴》]] and supplies the formal middle threshold in [[PartitionOfJin|三家分晋]].

## Current Profile

King Weilie's material power is weak, but the sources treat his naming authority as consequential. By recognizing [[WeiWenhou|魏文侯]], [[ZhaoLiehou|赵烈侯]], and [[HanJinghou|韩景侯]] as vassals, he catches public rank up to a practical shift that had already hollowed out [[JinState|晋国]].

The new evidence supplies a strongly negative Sima Guang-centered assessment: because the king remains the authority who grants rank, his decision is not passive acknowledgment but official ratification of ministers as peers of their former ruler. A later annalistic notice records his death and succession by [[ZhouAnWang|周安王]] in 402 BCE.

## Key Characteristics

- Retains formal title-making authority despite weak territorial and military capacity.
- Converts the Three Jin houses' practical dominance into recognized vassal rank.
- Bypasses the residual Jin ruler and thereby changes the public hierarchy.
- Serves as the chronicle's opening example of how legitimacy can outlive material power.
- Is remembered through a posthumous name and a short succession notice as well as through the recognition controversy.

- [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-06-weiwenhou-xuan-shui-dang-zaixiang-ne-2-lmfzo4sl9nd5kz3mrxdl1dl9efei|Zhouji 06 part 2]] records his death, posthumous-name context, and succession by Zhou An Wang.

## Qualifications

The sources establish the recognition and succession roles within the podcast's chronicle reading. The claim that King Weilie should have mobilized the lords against the Three Jin, and that his recognition caused the wider collapse of Zhou order, belongs to the episode's presentation of Sima Guang's normative judgment. The notes do not independently establish his motives, alternatives, or actual enforcement capacity.

## What Changed

- Reframed the king from a chronological title-granter into the contested holder of residual legitimating authority.
- Added the distinction between recognition of existing force and active ratification of a hierarchy breach.
