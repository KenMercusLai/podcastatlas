# François Mitterrand

## Overview

François Mitterrand was the first left-wing president of the [[FrenchFifthRepublic|French Fifth Republic]], presented in the source as an ideologically adaptable, secretive, and highly presidential successor to the system he had once opposed.

## Current Profile

[[179-french-presidents-1981-2022-part-2-glt2206497694]] traces Mitterrand from conservative Catholic origins and an ambiguous Vichy-to-Resistance wartime path through opposition to [[CharlesDeGaulle|Charles de Gaulle]], the 1981 socialist victory, and the later turn toward economic rigor. The episode argues that he abandoned much of the original program while mastering the grandeur, secrecy, cultural patronage, and national role expected of the presidency.

## Key Characteristics

- Moved from nationalist Catholic roots toward leadership of the French left.
- Opposed de Gaulle and the constitutional order he later learned to inhabit effectively.
- Began with nationalization, wage, and wealth-tax commitments before turning toward economic rigor.
- Used cultural building projects, diplomacy, and ceremony to project presidential grandeur.
- Combined public authority with secrecy about health, surveillance, and private life.

## Evidence

- Political formation and adaptation: [[179-french-presidents-1981-2022-part-2-glt2206497694]] connects conservative origins, wartime ambiguity, anti-Gaullism, and later socialist leadership.
- Program and reversal: [[179-french-presidents-1981-2022-part-2-glt2206497694]] describes the 110 propositions and the 1983-84 shift toward rigor associated with Jacques Delors.
- Presidential performance and secrecy: [[179-french-presidents-1981-2022-part-2-glt2206497694]] links monument-building and foreign relations to concealed illness, wiretapping, and a hidden family.

## Qualifications

The profile comes from one anecdotal survey rather than a complete account of Mitterrand's economic policy, European role, Cold War diplomacy, Rwanda policy, or domestic legacy. Wartime motives, private conduct, and the causes of the turn to rigor remain compressed and source-scoped.
