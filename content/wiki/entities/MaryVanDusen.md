# Mary Van Dusen

## Overview

Mary Van Dusen is a Livingston descendant and independent researcher who assembled and publicized the modern case that [[HenryLivingstonJr]] wrote [[AVisitFromStNicholas|“A Visit from St. Nicholas”]].

## Current Profile

[[twas-the-night-before-christmas-twas-the-night-before-christmas]] describes Van Dusen building a large research corpus after encountering the family claim online in the late 1990s. She collected poem variants and Livingston writings, searched historical newspapers, installed a microfilm reader at home, and created a website she estimated at more than 15,000 pages.

She brought the material to [[DonFoster]], enabling his stylistic analysis. The episode treats the scale and persistence of her work seriously while concluding that a large archive does not resolve selection, interpretation, chronology, or the absence of a contemporaneous Livingston claim.

## Key Characteristics

- Livingston descendant committed to investigating an inherited authorship claim.
- Built a large archive of poem versions, family material, and historical newspapers.
- Recruited literary-attribution specialist Don Foster in 1999.
- Presents evidence and her belief while inviting readers to decide.
- Preserves a compelling family narrative whose documentary foundation remains disputed.

## Evidence

- Corpus building: [[twas-the-night-before-christmas-twas-the-night-before-christmas]] details her newspaper search, microfilm work, collection of variants, and research website.
- Scholarly collaboration: [[twas-the-night-before-christmas-twas-the-night-before-christmas]] traces her approach through Ian Lancashire to Foster.
- Evidentiary boundary: [[twas-the-night-before-christmas-twas-the-night-before-christmas]] distinguishes the archive's scale from the strength of its authorship conclusion.

## Qualifications

The supplied episode is critical of the conclusion Van Dusen favors and does not independently audit every item in her archive. Her family relationship can motivate unusually sustained research without making the evidence either true or false; individual claims must still be judged by provenance and corroboration.

## What Changed

- Added Van Dusen as the researcher who transformed inherited memory into a public attribution case.
- Distinguished archival effort from the validity of the resulting inference.
