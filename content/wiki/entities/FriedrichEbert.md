# Friedrich Ebert

## Overview

Friedrich Ebert was the Social Democratic leader and early Weimar president who helped construct the republic after imperial collapse, used emergency powers amid disorder, and adopted *Das Lied der Deutschen* as Germany's national anthem in 1922.

## Current Profile

The early-Weimar source places Ebert at the improvised founding of the republic amid mutiny, revolution, the Kaiser's exile, and fear of further upheaval. Although personally attached to monarchy and hostile to revolution, he helped create the new democratic order and repeatedly used Article 48 emergency authority in response to instability.

The anthem source frames his 1922 decision as an attempt to create symbolic common ground after defeat. By emphasizing the third verse rather than the first, he tried to make the anthem speak to unity, rights, and freedom while appealing across liberal and conservative divisions in the [[WeimarRepublic]]. The combined profile is therefore constructive but qualified: Ebert sought democratic unity while also establishing an emergency-power precedent later actors could exploit in a different setting.

## Key Characteristics

- Social Democratic leader who helped build the republic after imperial collapse.
- President who used Article 48 repeatedly amid severe instability.
- Political actor using national music as a unifying postwar symbol.
- Figure whose third-verse emphasis anticipates the later postwar compromise.

## Evidence

- Adoption role: [[679-germany-the-song-hitler-stole-part-3-glt6217148052]] says Ebert adopted *Das Lied der Deutschen* as the national anthem in August 1922.
- Third-verse emphasis: [[679-germany-the-song-hitler-stole-part-3-glt6217148052]] says Ebert emphasized unity, rights, and freedom rather than the first verse.
- Coalition aim: [[679-germany-the-song-hitler-stole-part-3-glt6217148052]] says Ebert hoped the anthem could appeal to both liberals and conservatives.
- Founding role: [[296-the-nazis-the-beer-hall-putsch-part-2-glt4099180386]] places Ebert in the emergency construction of the republic after the Kaiser's fall.
- Emergency authority: [[296-the-nazis-the-beer-hall-putsch-part-2-glt4099180386]] reports extensive Article 48 use under Ebert during disorder and instability.

## Qualifications

This two-source profile remains a narrow account of Ebert's founding, emergency-power, and anthem roles rather than a full presidency or history of Social Democratic politics. His personal monarchism, precise emergency-decree count, necessity judgments, and the later causal significance of his precedent remain episode-attributed.

- Expanded Ebert from the anthem decision to the republic's emergency founding and Article 48 precedent.
