# Darla Buchanan

## Overview

Darla Buchanan was a Black Topeka teacher whose termination letter anchors the bounded episode's account of educator displacement during school integration. The district described her dismissal as a “period of adjustment,” turning a professional loss into an administrative euphemism.

## Current Profile

The superintendent's letter said Buchanan's services would no longer be needed and stated that the board assumed most white families would not want Black teachers teaching their children. The episode therefore presents her exclusion as institutional accommodation of racial prejudice, not as evidence of professional deficiency.

Decades later, [[LindaBrown]] read the letter at a fiftieth-anniversary event for [[BrownVBoardOfEducation]]. Buchanan's case thereby became a compact record of who exercised authority during implementation and who was made to absorb its costs.

## Key Characteristics

- Black educator dismissed during Topeka's integration process.
- Subject of a letter using conciliatory administrative language to describe job loss.
- Excluded through an explicit assumption about white parental prejudice.
- Became a symbol of the professional costs omitted from a student-centered integration story.
