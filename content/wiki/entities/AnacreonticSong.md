# The Anacreontic Song

## Overview

The Anacreontic Song appears in the wiki as the English melody used for [[FrancisScottKey]]'s poem that became [[StarSpangledBanner|The Star-Spangled Banner]].

## Current Profile

The episode treats the melody as an example of how national anthems can depend on borrowed, portable musical material. Written in England in 1775 by John Stafford Smith for the Anacreontic Club, the tune was already familiar in the United States and had been used for political, patriotic, and abolitionist songs before Key's poem.

## Key Characteristics

- English tune written in 1775 by John Stafford Smith.
- Associated with the Anacreontic Club, a London musical society.
- Familiar in the United States before Key used it in 1814.
- Reused for broadside ballads, political songs, and patriotic lyrics.
- Technically difficult melody later inherited by the U.S. anthem.
- Example of national-symbol borrowing across political boundaries.

## Evidence

- Origin: [[677-usa-the-star-spangled-banner-part-1-glt7890467734]] says the tune was written in England in 1775 by John Stafford Smith.
- Social setting: [[677-usa-the-star-spangled-banner-part-1-glt7890467734]] says the tune was associated with the Anacreontic Club rather than simply being a bawdy drinking song.
- American reuse: [[677-usa-the-star-spangled-banner-part-1-glt7890467734]] says versions were already popular in the United States and had been used for abolitionist and political songs.
- Key's use: [[677-usa-the-star-spangled-banner-part-1-glt7890467734]] says Key had previously written another patriotic ballad to the same melody before using it for the Fort McHenry poem.
