# Mumtaz Mahal

## Overview

Mumtaz Mahal, born Arjumand Banu Begum, was the favored wife of the Mughal emperor [[ShahJahan]] and the woman commemorated by the [[TajMahal]]. Her current profile is bounded by court descriptions, marriage politics, repeated childbirth, campaign travel, death, and later monumental memory.

## Current Profile

Mumtaz was a niece of the politically influential empress Nur Jahan and belonged to an important Khorasani court family. Her marriage to Prince Khurram strengthened court ties, while Mughal histories praised her beauty, kindness, loyalty, and mutual affection with the future Shah Jahan. The episode treats her presence on his military campaigns and near-constant companionship as unusual.

She died on 17 June 1631 while giving birth to the couple's fourteenth child during a Deccan campaign. Seven of the fourteen children survived to adulthood. The Taj Mahal made her the center of an exceptionally grand female mausoleum, but the source cautions through its broader analysis that her memorial also communicated Shah Jahan's piety, refinement, lineage, and kingship.

## Key Characteristics

- Mughal imperial consort born into a powerful Persianate court family.
- Favored wife described in court sources as closely and reciprocally attached to Shah Jahan.
- Companion who traveled with the emperor, including on military campaigns.
- Mother of fourteen children who died during the final childbirth in 1631.
- Commemorated through a mausoleum of unusual scale and refinement for a woman.

## Evidence

- Family and marriage: [[362-the-taj-mahal-love-and-death-glt2445608477]] identifies her as Nur Jahan's niece and connects the marriage to court-family alliances.
- Companionship and motherhood: [[362-the-taj-mahal-love-and-death-glt2445608477]] reports campaign travel, fourteen births, seven surviving children, and court praise for the marriage.
- Death and commemoration: [[362-the-taj-mahal-love-and-death-glt2445608477]] dates her death to 17 June 1631 and connects Shah Jahan's grief to the Taj Mahal's construction.

## Qualifications

The surviving portrait in this source depends heavily on court histories written inside the Mughal political world. The monument preserves Mumtaz as an object of commemoration more clearly than it preserves her own voice or independent political agency. The episode does not treat romantic devotion as the complex's only purpose.
