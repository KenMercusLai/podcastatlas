# William the Conqueror

## Overview
William the Conqueror appears in the wiki through the [[BayeuxTapestry|Bayeux Tapestry]] segment as the Norman claimant whose 1066 invasion of England becomes the tapestry's central political narrative.

## Current Profile
The source presents William through an embroidered legitimacy story: [[EdwardTheConfessor]] sends [[HaroldGodwinson]] to Normandy, Harold appears to swear that William should have the crown, Harold later takes the throne, and William invades and wins.

## Key Characteristics
- Norman claimant to the English crown in the source's account of 1066.
- Victorious figure in the Bayeux Tapestry's battle sequence.
- Possible end-point of the missing tapestry section if it once showed his coronation.
- Political-memory figure whose legitimacy is narrated through oath, omen, battle, and visual contrast.

## Evidence
- Claim and invasion sequence - [[ire-and-ice-the-toll-of-americas-deportations-6a993426809ae7c2f4293df9]] says the tapestry narrates Harold's oath and William's invasion.
- Victory and possible coronation - [[ire-and-ice-the-toll-of-americas-deportations-6a993426809ae7c2f4293df9]] describes battle scenes ending in William's victory and notes that the missing ending may have shown his coronation.

## Qualifications
This is a source-bounded historical profile, not a full biography of William or a settled interpretation of all Norman succession claims.
