## Overview

楚王刘戊 / Liu Wu, King of Chu enters the current wiki as the Chu ruler whose declining respect for inherited advisers precedes his participation with [[LiuBiWuKing|吴王刘濞]] in the [[RebellionOfTheSevenStatesWesternHan|Rebellion of the Seven States]].

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-232-wuwang-liubi-pingshenme-gan-faqi-qiguo-zhiluan-lqtwfjr6oknd9pgdtvnnz61rs1f1|Hanji 232]] contrasts Liu Wu with his predecessor Liu Jiao. Liu Jiao's repeated accommodation of [[MuXianshengWesternHan|穆先生]] represents durable respect; Liu Wu first continues and then neglects the practice, which Mu reads as evidence that the inherited moral relationship has ended.

The episode then moves from signal to coercion. Liu Wu becomes increasingly violent, ignores or punishes remonstrance, joins Wu after Chu loses territory, humiliates advisers who oppose rebellion, threatens his uncle Liu Fu, and reportedly kills Chancellor Zhang Shang and Tutor Zhao Yiwu. His profile therefore joins deteriorating treatment of counsel to open princely revolt without claiming that one forgotten courtesy caused the rebellion.

## Key Characteristics

- Successor whose abandonment of an inherited courtesy becomes a small sign of changed court relations.
- Ruler who answers repeated anti-rebellion remonstrance with humiliation, threat, or killing in the episode's account.
- Chu participant whose territorial grievance and alliance with Liu Bi turn internal coercion into open rebellion.

## Qualifications

This profile rests on one popular-history episode. The exact succession, offense and reduction, chronology, courtesy, adviser identities, punishments, threats, deaths, speeches, and division of initiative between Chu and Wu require comparison with transmitted records. The courtesy episode supports a narrative of deteriorating respect; it does not establish a universal predictive rule or a direct causal path from etiquette to rebellion.

## What Changed

- Established Liu Wu as a ruler whose treatment of counsel links the episode's early-warning story to its rebellion narrative.
- Distinguished the missing courtesy as a signal of relationship change rather than the cause of later violence.
