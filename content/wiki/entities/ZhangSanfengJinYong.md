## Overview

张三丰（金庸小说） / Zhang Sanfeng (Jin Yong) is the fictional Wudang founder whom a [[ZhiZuiJinMiFM|纸醉金迷FM]] roundtable presents as its strongest all-round example of a good master.

## Current Profile

The episode's judgment rests on combination rather than one spectacular trait. Zhang Sanfeng is portrayed as an elite martial artist who can also diagnose different learners, adapt instruction, communicate principles quickly under pressure, and retain enough emotional steadiness to protect students without making obedience or lineage purity the measure of worth.

His openness supplies the ethical center of the profile. The roundtable contrasts his willingness to judge conduct and intention with rigid orthodox-versus-heterodox labeling, using his acceptance of Yin Susu and his care for Zhang Wuji to argue that a master's authority is strongest when expertise, pedagogy, trust, and breadth of mind reinforce one another.

The earlier [[HeavenSwordDragonSaber|《倚天屠龙记》]] discussion deepens that openness into an explicit institutional critique. Zhang Sanfeng refuses to treat Yin Susu's Heavenly Eagle Cult origin as a moral verdict, warns Wudang disciples against orthodox pride, and judges people by character and willingness to turn toward good. His calm response when a young Shaolin monk questions his identity also presents humor and freedom from status anxiety as part of authority. Yet his disciples initially interpret his principle as favoritism, showing that exemplary judgment does not automatically become shared organizational culture.

## Key Characteristics

- Combines elite martial ability with teaching skill rather than treating mastery as proof of pedagogy.
- Adapts training to differences in personality, foundation, and circumstance.
- Can communicate principles efficiently, including under urgent conditions.
- Shows emotional steadiness and sustained care toward students and descendants.
- Resists reducing moral judgment to sectarian identity.
- Provides the institutional inheritance and reputation of Wudang without making lineage exclusivity absolute.
- Holds prestige lightly enough to respond to status challenges with humor rather than retaliation.

## Qualifications

This profile records two podcast discussions of Jin Yong's fictional character, not a historical biography, exhaustive textual study, or objective ranking of martial ability and teaching outcomes. The examples are conversationally recalled and sometimes debated; exact dialogue, authorial intent, sect history, and the extent of Zhang's influence on Wudang culture remain source-scoped. Their durable use is to model competence, pedagogy, care, steadiness, and non-sectarian authority rather than to settle every plot detail.

## What Changed

- Made conduct-based moral judgment an explicit part of Zhang Sanfeng's institutional leadership.
- Added humor and low status anxiety as expressions of secure authority.
- Added the boundary that a leader's breadth does not automatically become organizational culture.
