# Karima Sharif-Ali

## Overview
Karima Sharif-Ali is the healthcare media and marketing leader interviewed in [[ep-49-the-human-side-of-ai-in-media-speed-trust-whats-really-changing|Data Science With Sam EP49]]. The source presents her as a practitioner who has worked across more than 100 pharmaceutical and medical-device brands and who connects AI adoption to media workflow speed, healthcare trust, cultural context, and representation in AI decision-making.

## Current Profile
Karima's episode position is pragmatic rather than utopian: she sees AI as an accelerator for planning, RFP comparison, meeting notes, contracts, reporting, creative reformatting, and optimization feedback, while keeping strategy, authenticity, cultural understanding, and healthcare trust on the human side of the boundary. Her risk frame includes deepfakes, hallucination, copyright, environmental costs, algorithmic bias, weak representation, and the loss of human "intellectual flavor."

## Key Characteristics
- Healthcare media practitioner who frames AI adoption through real media operations rather than abstract model capability.
- Treats speed as AI's most visible impact in planning, buying, optimization, and reporting.
- Argues that strategy, creative concepts, authenticity, relationships, and cultural understanding should remain human-led.
- Connects healthcare AI risk to patient trust, false information, deepfakes, hallucinated drug details, and regulated communication.
- Expands responsible AI beyond accuracy to include copyright, environmental impact, bias, platform disclosure, and representation in leadership.

## Evidence
- Media operations: [[ep-49-the-human-side-of-ai-in-media-speed-trust-whats-really-changing]] has Karima describe planning cycles shrinking from months toward weeks, daily optimization feedback, RFP scorecards, insertion orders, contracts, reports, and meeting-note follow-up.
- Human-led strategy: [[ep-49-the-human-side-of-ai-in-media-speed-trust-whats-really-changing]] has her say creative and strategic concepts should begin with people, while AI can adapt and reformat ideas for different channels.
- Healthcare trust: [[ep-49-the-human-side-of-ai-in-media-speed-trust-whats-really-changing]] ties deepfakes, hallucinations, prescription-drug accuracy, authenticity, and relationships to patient-facing media risk.
- Responsible AI scope: [[ep-49-the-human-side-of-ai-in-media-speed-trust-whats-really-changing]] raises copyright, data-center burdens, historical bias, resume-screening effects, platform disclosure, regulation, and representation.
