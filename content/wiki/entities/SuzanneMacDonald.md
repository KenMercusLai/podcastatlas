# Suzanne MacDonald

## Overview

Suzanne MacDonald is the animal-cognition researcher whose Toronto raccoon work supplies the empirical reversal in [[rat-vs-raccoon-rat-vs-raccoon|Rat vs. Raccoon]].

## Current Profile

After [[MichaelPettit]] introduced her to [[LawrenceCole]]'s historical research, MacDonald considered whether North American raccoons might occupy a comparative-cognition role analogous to primates elsewhere. Her later observations complicate that attractive hypothesis: baby and adult raccoons perform poorly on several standard developmental and cognition measures.

MacDonald's “search and destroy” description reframes their urban success. Raccoons may obtain outcomes by touching, overturning, breaking, and persisting rather than by visibly pausing to solve a problem. The source therefore makes her work evidence for species-specific strategy, not proof of either exceptional intelligence or generalized foolishness.

## Key Characteristics

- Studies animal cognition with a focus on Toronto's urban raccoons.
- Reopened a neglected research subject after learning about Cole's historical work.
- Reports weak raccoon performance on several standard cognition measures.
- Characterizes raccoon exploration as impulsive, physical, persistent, and destructive.
- Keeps major knowledge gaps explicit because urban raccoon research remains difficult.

## Qualifications

The current profile comes from one structured podcast summary rather than MacDonald's papers, protocols, datasets, or a full transcript. “Search and destroy” is a compact behavioral description, not a complete theory of raccoon cognition, and performance on selected human-designed tests does not establish low general intelligence.
