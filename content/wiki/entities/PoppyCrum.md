# Poppy Crum

## Overview
Poppy Crum is a neuroscientist and technologist presented in the source as a [[StanfordUniversity]] professor and former Dolby Laboratories chief scientist whose work connects perception, neuroplasticity, human performance, sensing, and adaptive technology.

## Current Profile
In [[enhance-your-learning-speed-health-using-neuroscience-based-protocols-dr-poppy-crum-scim3116912471]], Crum argues that technology is already shaping neural allocation through repeated use, attention, feedback, and incentives. Her practical distinction is between systems that amplify learning and systems that replace the cognitive work a person intended to train. She extends this logic from AI tutoring and game-like feedback to computer-vision coaching, digital twins, environmental sensors, hearables, and possible voice-based health signals.

Her account is grounded partly in auditory experience. Crum describes absolute pitch as learned categorical perception rather than a timeless reference standard, connects altered tuning systems to secondary perceptual maps, and uses cross-species sensory examples to show how organisms become calibrated to consequential environmental data.

## Key Characteristics
- Connects neuroscience, perception, and product design through experience-dependent brain change.
- Treats attention, feedback, incentive, and ecological importance as major conditions for learning.
- Distinguishes cognitive amplification from automation that removes learning-relevant effort.
- Favors integrated, minimally obtrusive sensing over proliferating body-worn devices.
- Uses auditory expertise and comparative sensory biology to explain learned perceptual maps.
