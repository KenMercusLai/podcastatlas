# Michael Pollan

## Overview
Michael Pollan is an author and journalist who uses gardening, food systems, psychoactive substances, and subjective experience to investigate how humans relate to other organisms and to their own minds.

## Current Profile
In the bounded source, Pollan is a synthesis-oriented public thinker rather than a laboratory authority. He separates evidence from experience, treats unusual experiences as hypotheses or prompts for inquiry, and keeps unresolved theories open without declaring them true. His practical method moves from intimate experiments—gardening, psychedelic experience, and caffeine abstinence—to broader questions about consciousness, agriculture, public policy, and social change.

## Key Characteristics
- He defines consciousness provisionally through subjective experience while resisting a human-only standard.
- He distinguishes plant perception and adaptive behavior from demonstrated plant consciousness.
- He treats psychedelic experience as potentially revealing but not self-validating, automatically prosocial, or universally safe.
- He uses gardening as a model of reciprocal intervention in nature rather than either domination or non-interference.
- His food writing combines investigation with explicit advocacy for healthier incentives, prices, and production systems.
- He uses self-experimentation to examine ordinary drug relationships while acknowledging context and dependence.
