## Current Profile

The sources use Lady Gouyi as origin legend, household absence, and succession-risk figure. Hanji 381-2 gives the pre-death aura: her encounter with Han Wudi is framed as the discovery of a strange woman, and Liu Fuling's reported fourteen-month birth lets Wudi compare the child to Yao and rename a palace gate "尧母门." Hanji 391-1 later explains her death before accession through two layers: the public logic that a young son with a strong mother could repeat Lu-family-style harem or outer-relative domination, and the host's source-scoped claim that Han Wudi had discovered her hidden role behind [[JiangChongWesternHan|江充]] and [[SuWenWesternHan|苏文]] in the destruction of Liu Ju. The source says Han Wudi does not publicize that alleged guilt because Liu Fuling's legitimacy must be protected.

Hanji 396-1 then shows the consequence after her death: her absence creates the care gap around an eight-year-old emperor and makes the older princess's palace role politically consequential. That household arrangement later intersects with consort selection, [[DingWairenWesternHan|丁外人]]'s access to the princess, and six-year-old [[ShangguanTaihouWesternHan|上官氏]]'s entry.

## Key Characteristics

- Mother of Han Zhao.
- Origin-legend figure whose clenched-hand discovery story and fourteen-month pregnancy give Liu Fuling an auspicious aura.
- Palace figure whose death is framed by Hanji 391-1 as the inverse of "mother raised by son's status": Liu Fuling's heir value makes her politically disposable.
- Deceased mother whose absence creates a care gap around the child emperor.
- Source-scoped alleged participant in the Liu Ju frame-up branch of the Wugu affair.
- Background figure connecting succession, harem politics, princess household authority, and fear of mother-regent power.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 396-1, Hanji 391-1, and Hanji 381-2. It does not reconstruct Lady Gouyi's full life, death procedure, relationship with Han Wudi, or later historiographical debate. Hanji 381-2's origin and pregnancy details are recorded as transmitted legend and host interpretation, not as medical or archival proof. Hanji 391-1's hidden-guilt explanation is recorded as the episode's interpretation, not as settled proof that Lady Gouyi was the mastermind behind the Wugu affair.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 381-2's origin-legend and "尧母门" layer before Hanji 391-1's death-explanation layer: public fear of young-ruler mother power plus the source-scoped claim of hidden involvement in Liu Ju's destruction.
- Preserved Hanji 396-1's household-background explanation for Han Zhao.
