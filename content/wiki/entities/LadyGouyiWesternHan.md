## Overview

钩弋夫人 / Lady Gouyi enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-396-1-12-sui-huangdi-yu-6-sui-huanghou-shui-ganchu-de-huangtang-shier-lnq-puxt0zdxhfm7pwilxellccdr|Hanji 396-1]] as [[EmperorZhaoOfHan|汉昭帝刘弗陵]]'s mother, whose death before Han Zhao's accession helps explain why [[GaiChanggongzhuWesternHan|鄂邑 / 盖长公主]] is brought into the palace to care for the young ruler. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-391-1-wei-le-li-taizi-hanwudi-weihe-xian-sha-chongfei-gouyi-furen-lhsva8zj7n7mfy6u4vyfhrwmbwzg|Hanji 391-1]] now supplies the death-explanation layer: [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] kills her before making Liu Fuling heir, publicly citing young-ruler mother power and, in the host's source-scoped hidden explanation, allegedly punishing her for involvement in the [[WuguIncidentWesternHan|巫蛊之祸]] against [[LiuJuCrownPrinceWesternHan|卫太子刘据]].

## Current Profile

The sources use Lady Gouyi as both a household absence and a succession-risk figure. Hanji 391-1 explains her death before accession through two layers: the public logic that a young son with a strong mother could repeat Lu-family-style harem or outer-relative domination, and the host's source-scoped claim that Han Wudi had discovered her hidden role behind [[JiangChongWesternHan|江充]] and [[SuWenWesternHan|苏文]] in the destruction of Liu Ju. The source says Han Wudi does not publicize that alleged guilt because Liu Fuling's legitimacy must be protected.

Hanji 396-1 then shows the consequence after her death: her absence creates the care gap around an eight-year-old emperor and makes the older princess's palace role politically consequential. That household arrangement later intersects with consort selection, [[DingWairenWesternHan|丁外人]]'s access to the princess, and six-year-old [[ShangguanTaihouWesternHan|上官氏]]'s entry.

## Key Characteristics

- Mother of Han Zhao.
- Palace figure whose death is framed by Hanji 391-1 as the inverse of "mother raised by son's status": Liu Fuling's heir value makes her politically disposable.
- Deceased mother whose absence creates a care gap around the child emperor.
- Source-scoped alleged participant in the Liu Ju frame-up branch of the Wugu affair.
- Background figure connecting succession, harem politics, princess household authority, and fear of mother-regent power.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 396-1 and Hanji 391-1. It does not reconstruct Lady Gouyi's full life, death procedure, relationship with Han Wudi, or later historiographical debate. Hanji 391-1's hidden-guilt explanation is recorded as the episode's interpretation, not as settled proof that Lady Gouyi was the mastermind behind the Wugu affair.
