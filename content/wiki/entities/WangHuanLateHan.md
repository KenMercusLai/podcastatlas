## Overview

王涣 / Wang Huan appears in the current wiki as a late-Han local official whose severity is repeatedly qualified by discernment and public trust. Hanji 862 uses him as the Kaocheng magistrate who recognizes [[QiuXiangLateHan|仇香]] after the [[ChenYuanLateHan|陈元]] unfiliality case, while Hanji 777 gives his later Luoyang endpoint: strict but compassionate judgments, public mourning, and posthumous court recognition by [[EmpressDowagerDengLateHan|Empress Dowager Deng]].

## Current Profile

Hanji 862 presents Wang Huan as an official whose own governance leans severe. That makes his response to Qiu Xiang significant: he does not dismiss non-punitive correction as weakness. Instead, after hearing that Qiu Xiang transformed Chen Yuan without punishment, he appoints Qiu Xiang as a subordinate and asks whether Qiu lacks the fierce spirit associated with punitive enforcement.

Qiu Xiang answers that he prefers the image of an auspicious bird to the predatory bird, and Wang Huan accepts the distinction. He then funds Qiu Xiang's study at [[Taixue|太学]], treating the county office as too small for Qiu's talent. Wang Huan therefore functions as a recognizing official: his own severity is qualified by the ability to see a better governance style when it appears.

Hanji 777 adds Wang Huan's later Luoyang profile. As Luoyang ling, he is strict, detects hidden wrongdoing, and applies law with judgments that people accept, while the source also says he is inwardly compassionate. When he dies in office, officials, transporters, and capital residents mourn him; as his coffin passes through Hongnong on the westward return, people make roadside sacrifices and credit him with stopping official and transport robbery. Deng then issues an edict praising loyal and good officials and appoints Wang Huan's son Wang Shi as langzhong.

## Key Characteristics

- Local official whose public profile combines severity, discernment, and humane trust.
- Kaocheng magistrate in the Qiu Xiang story who recognizes non-punitive transformation as meaningful rather than weak.
- Patron who funds Qiu Xiang's Taixue study after judging that local office is too small for him.
- Luoyang administrator whose strict judgments are accepted because they are paired with hidden detection and compassion.
- Posthumous good-official exemplar whose public mourning prompts court recognition.
- Case for [[HumaneLocalGovernance|仁厚地方治理]] because trusted enforcement and moral recognition both produce order.

## Qualifications

This page is not a complete biography of Wang Huan. It now records two bounded roles: the Hanji 862 recognition of Qiu Xiang and the Hanji 777 Luoyang death-and-memory notice. The stable route remains `WangHuanLateHan`; the visible Chinese form is corrected to 王涣 because Hanji 777 gives 王煥/王焕, Hanji 823 already compares later Luoyang officials to 王涣, and the older 王奂 display was a normalization error rather than a separate subject.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 777 as Wang Huan's Luoyang endpoint: strict accepted judgments, compassion, public mourning, shrine memory, and court reward to Wang Shi.
- Corrected the visible Chinese form from 王奂 to 王涣 while preserving the stable route.
- Reframed Wang Huan from only a severe magistrate who recognizes Qiu Xiang to a broader strict-but-trusted local-governance exemplar.
