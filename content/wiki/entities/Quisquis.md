# Quisquis

## Overview

Quisquis was a senior northern Inca commander who resisted Pizarro's advance toward Cusco, withdrew toward Quito with a depleted army, and was killed by his own officers after refusing peace.

## Current Profile

[[646-the-fall-of-the-incas-death-to-the-emperor-part-3-glt8131543734]] places Quisquis at Cusco after victory in the Inca civil war. He obeys captive [[Atahualpa]]'s order not to intervene in the ransom and allows three Spaniards to strip gold plates and other sacred wealth from Coricancha. The episode treats that inaction as a product of imperial obedience, uncertainty about Europeans, and continuing hostility toward the defeated southern faction rather than a lack of military capacity.

[[647-the-fall-of-the-incas-the-king-in-the-north-part-4-glt4740382633]] then places Quisquis at the center of the military aftermath. His northern army had devastated Huascar-aligned areas around Jauja, and he continued to contest the Spanish-Cusco-faction advance after Atahualpa's death. Even after [[Chalcuchima]]'s execution, Quisquis nearly stopped the invaders in a mountain-pass battle but withdrew when night ended the opportunity.

He then led a reported force of about twenty thousand battle-hardened but exhausted troops north. Arriving near Quito after Spanish-led forces had taken the capital, he rejected his officers' request to seek peace, declared that he preferred starvation in the wilds to surrender, and was bludgeoned to death by his own commanders. His end makes morale and internal consent, not simply battlefield defeat, part of the northern collapse.

## Key Characteristics

- Senior commander of Atahualpa's northern army.
- Occupier of Cusco who permitted Coricancha's looting while obeying the captive emperor.
- Veteran of the Inca civil war and destruction in Huascar-aligned territory.
- Persistent opponent of the Spanish advance and Manco's Cusco faction.
- Leader of a long northward retreat by an exhausted army.
- Uncompromising resister killed after his officers lost confidence in continued war.

## Evidence

- Captive-command obedience: [[646-the-fall-of-the-incas-death-to-the-emperor-part-3-glt8131543734]] connects Quisquis's inaction to Atahualpa's ransom orders and Coricancha's plunder.
- Civil-war army: [[647-the-fall-of-the-incas-the-king-in-the-north-part-4-glt4740382633]] connects Quisquis's force to killings and retreat around Jauja.
- Mountain resistance: [[647-the-fall-of-the-incas-the-king-in-the-north-part-4-glt4740382633]] describes a battle that failed to destroy the Spanish column before nightfall.
- Morale collapse: [[647-the-fall-of-the-incas-the-king-in-the-north-part-4-glt4740382633]] follows his army to Quito and his death at the hands of officers seeking peace.

## Qualifications

The episodes do not independently verify troop totals, attributed speech, motives for permitting the Coricancha expedition, the precise mountain battle, responsibility for earlier atrocities, or the circumstances of Quisquis's death. His officers' belief in Spanish invincibility is evidence of collapsing morale in this account, not proof that military resistance everywhere had become impossible.

## What Changed

- Added Quisquis's Cusco occupation, obedience during Atahualpa's captivity, and permission for Coricancha's plunder.
- Connected early paralysis to later resistance and command breakdown.

## Relationships

- [[Atahualpa]] - northern ruler whose cause Quisquis's army had served.
- [[Chalcuchima]] - fellow northern commander captured before Quisquis resumed resistance.
- [[Ruminawi]] - northern war leader operating around Quito during Quisquis's retreat.
- [[MancoInca]] - Cusco-faction ruler allied with the Spanish advance Quisquis opposed.
- [[FranciscoPizarro]] - conquistador whose column Quisquis tried to stop before Cusco.
- [[SpanishConquestOfPeru]] - conquest process in which his army represented continued northern resistance.
