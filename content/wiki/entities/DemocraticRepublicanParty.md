# Democratic-Republican Party

## Overview
The Democratic-Republican Party appears as [[ThomasJefferson|Thomas Jefferson]]'s political vehicle in the [[FirstPartySystem|first party system]], opposed to [[AlexanderHamilton|Alexander Hamilton]] and the [[FederalistParty|Federalists]].

## Current Profile
The Jefferson episode presents the Democratic-Republicans through Jefferson's public role and James Madison's organizing work. Jefferson stands as the enlightened philosopher above the fray, while Madison is credited as the practical party organizer. The Hamilton episode adds Burr as a problematic aligned figure: formally on Jefferson's side in 1800, then sidelined and dropped from the 1804 ticket, he shows that party victory did not automatically produce internal trust or stable ambition management.

## Key Characteristics
- Jefferson-led founding-era party faction.
- Organized practically by James Madison in the episode's account.
- Opposed Hamiltonian and Federalist arguments for stronger federal power and British-leaning policy.
- Associated with suspicion of banks, debt, centralized power, and tyranny.
- Winner of the 1800 electoral crisis that produced a peaceful transfer of power.
- Contained Burr as an ambitious figure whose tie with Jefferson and later marginalization exposed internal instability.

## Evidence
- Leadership: [[686-jefferson-the-betrayal-of-liberty-part-4-glt6535571769]] describes Jefferson as standard-bearer of the Democratic-Republicans.
- Organization: [[686-jefferson-the-betrayal-of-liberty-part-4-glt6535571769]] says James Madison was the key organizer of the party machinery.
- Policy conflict: [[686-jefferson-the-betrayal-of-liberty-part-4-glt6535571769]] lists federal power, banks, debt, Britain, France, farmers, and tyranny as issues in Jefferson's conflict with Hamilton.
- Election outcome: [[686-jefferson-the-betrayal-of-liberty-part-4-glt6535571769]] says Jefferson's 1800 victory followed a 36-ballot House deadlock.
- Burr alignment: [[685-hamilton-duel-to-the-death-part-3-glt9231431046]] says Burr aligned with Jefferson rather than Hamilton and tied Jefferson in the 1800 Electoral College.
- Internal marginalization: [[685-hamilton-duel-to-the-death-part-3-glt9231431046]] says Jefferson sidelined Burr and planned to drop him from the 1804 ticket, leading Burr toward the New York governorship race.

## Qualifications
The sources frame the party through Jefferson, Hamilton, and Burr rather than a comprehensive organizational history. The page therefore emphasizes ideology, elections, and elite rivalry, not a complete map of voters, caucuses, press networks, or later party evolution.

## What Changed
- Added Burr's 1800 tie and 1804 marginalization as evidence that party victory did not eliminate internal ambition risk.
