# 诸葛亮 / Zhuge Liang

## Overview
诸葛亮 / Zhuge Liang is Liu Bei's strategist and state-building adviser, while the current source set also uses him as a case in moralized political technique, historically conditioned strategy, and cross-camp alliance networking through his brother [[ZhugeJin|诸葛瑾]].

## Current Profile
Hanji 976 gives the strongest positive synthesis: Zhuge Liang helps convert [[LiuBei|刘备]] from mobile survival politics into a state-level operating system, so advice becomes institutions, legitimacy, and executable strategy. Hanji 977 supplies a darker, explicitly source-scoped counter-reading in which moral language, inherited mission, and selective information can become instruments of political pressure.

The cross-period sources use Zhuge Liang comparatively rather than biographically. Hanji 148-1 contrasts Han Xin's successful return from Hanzhong with Zhuge Liang's later northern expeditions to show that route imitation cannot ignore timing, opponents, force condition, and institutional setting. Zhouji 80 invokes Zhuge Liang as a later admirer of Le Yi, making him a reception marker for the loyal-minister exit tradition.

Hanji 1099 adds a direct 208 CE alliance role. Liu Bei looks to Zhuge Liang after Lu Su proposes cooperation with [[SunQuan|孙权]], and Lu Su answers with the personal signal that he is Zhuge Jin's friend. The episode reads the moment as alignment between the post-Changban survival decision and the [[LongzhongPlan|隆中对]]'s existing cooperation-with-Sun-Quan component, though with alliance moved ahead of territorial control. Hanji 1100 continues that role into direct advocacy before Sun Quan: Zhuge Liang narrows the choice to resistance or surrender, uses dignity to make capitulation costly, answers the capacity objection with coalition forces and Cao-side weaknesses, and closes with the prospect of a tripartite balance.

## Key Characteristics
- Adviser associated with converting Liu Bei's mobile coalition into durable state architecture.
- Strategic planner whose Longzhong framework includes cooperation with Sun Quan in the current source's account.
- Participant in the post-Changban decision who then argues the coalition case directly before Sun Quan.
- Connected to the opposing camp through his elder brother Zhuge Jin.
- Subject of a source-scoped critical reading about moral pressure and information selection.
- Comparative marker for the limits of copying routes or precedents across different strategic conditions.
- Later admirer of Le Yi in the source set's reception history.

## Qualifications
The source set is thematically uneven and does not constitute a complete Zhuge Liang biography. Hanji 976 and 977 use broad interpretive labels, Hanji 148-1 and Zhouji 80 use him mainly as a comparison or reception marker, and Hanji 1099 infers meaning from a brief glance and relationship statement. Hanji 1100 is a popular-history reconstruction whose exact dialogue, emotional effects, force totals, and neat rhetorical sequence require verification against underlying texts.

## What Changed
- Extended Zhuge Liang's post-Changban role from accepting an alliance channel to presenting the direct anti-Cao coalition case.
- Distinguished the negotiation's dignity lever from its operational claims about force, fatigue, water warfare, and cohesion.
- Preserved uncertainty around exact dialogue, quantified strength, and narrated psychological effect.
