# 战国时期 / Warring States Period

The Warring States Period is the historical setting for [[30-mengzi-zhanguo-fenqing-de-chizi-zhixin-592494138]]. The episode contrasts it with the [[SpringAndAutumnPeriod|春秋时期]]: older ritual and lineage orders have weakened, interstate competition is harsher, rulers compete for population and talent, and schools of thought become practically useful in a world of policy, war, and mobility.

This page is source-scoped. Its role is to explain why [[Mengzi|孟子 / Mencius]] can be read as both idealistic and realistic: if people, scholars, and merchants can move, then [[MencianBenevolentGovernment|benevolent government]] can attract resources; if rulers chase only advantage, then [[RighteousnessOverProfit|righteousness over profit]] becomes a political critique, not only a moral slogan.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-04-yurang-wei-zhiyao-fuchou-ln34tydha6iqwlrgmw7iugqfgfik]] adds a periodization guardrail. The episode treats 453 BCE, after Jinyang and the division of [[ZhiYao|智瑶]]'s territory, as the beginning of the Warring States, while the broader wiki keeps that beside 403 BCE [[ThreeJinVassalRecognition|formal recognition]] and 376 BCE Jin extinction inside [[PartitionOfJin|三家分晋]]. The same source adds [[TalentVirtueDistinction|才德之分]], [[RetainerReciprocityEthic|士为知己者死]], and [[ZhaoXiangzi|赵襄子]]'s conquest of [[DaiState|代国]] as early-Warring-States transition material.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-05-guangcai-duomu-de-weiwenhou-1-lmkxzbmwhw3nhurape-eebydqcdl]] adds a non-military explanation for early Wei prominence. [[WeiWenhou|魏文侯]]'s treatment of scholars, promise-keeping, and Han-Zhao diplomacy show that Warring States competition also rewards [[RenhePoliticalPower|人和政治力量]], not only armies, terrain, and formal recognition.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-06-weiwenhou-xuan-shui-dang-zaixiang-ne-1-lk-6ejgmjhvukqgtlz2lowu8qx-v]] adds the personnel side of that competition. [[LiKe|李克]]'s advice to [[WeiWenhou|魏文侯]] turns chancellor selection into [[SituationalCharacterTests|situational character testing]] and [[TalentReferralQuality|referral-quality]] judgment, while the [[WuQi|吴起]] section shows that early Warring States rulers also had to decide how much defect they could tolerate for scarce military capability.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-06-weiwenhou-xuan-shui-dang-zaixiang-ne-2-lmfzo4sl9nd5kz3mrxdl1dl9efei]] adds an earlier military and chronicle branch. [[WuQi|吴起]] and [[WeiState|魏国]] show Warring States competition as a problem of training load, morale, and execution, while [[ZhouWeilieWang|周威烈王]], [[ZhouAnWang|周安王]], [[YanState|燕国]], and [[ChuState|楚国]] keep the episode anchored in annalistic succession notices.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-07-liangqi-cisha-1-lvkp-ju7pf5z5cz3thojv3s2fkyx]] adds the political-order transition more explicitly. Qin-Wei fighting, Han/Zhao/Qin successions, [[ZhengState|郑国]]'s pressure between [[JinState|晋国]] and [[ChuState|楚国]], and [[ZiyangZheng|子阳]]'s killing show the source's claim that older aristocratic constraints were giving way to sharper interstate and factional competition.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-07-liangqi-cisha-2-lhngst3xsnyjrmzh1cvheocvvsqm]] extends that transition through [[NieZheng|聂政]]'s assassination, [[ZhengXugong|郑繻公]]'s killing, [[WeiWuhou|魏武侯]]'s accession, and [[FushuCity|负黍]]'s repeated transfer. The source's strongest period claim is that war tempo and political violence are both accelerating.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-07-liangqi-cisha-3-lsbx8l7wxeioae2lwuwkkkutpdwn]] adds the legitimacy lag behind that violence. [[JinState|晋国]] still has a ruler but little independent meaning, [[TianHe|田和]] can displace [[QiKangGong|齐康公]] before formal recognition catches up, and old labels such as "Jin" can remain in war notices even when the political actor may be [[WeiState|魏国]]. The episode therefore links [[TianFamilyReplacementOfQi|田氏代齐]], [[ThreeJinVassalRecognition|三晋受封]], and [[ChronicleChronologyDrift|编年错位]] as part of the same transition from inherited titles to power-backed legitimacy.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-08-yidai-mingjiang-wuqi-cansi-1-lq5r3xe-3y7al9nkbc8kyhntipll]] adds an internal Wei branch. [[WuQi|吴起]]'s West River warning shows Warring States rulers balancing geography against legitimacy through [[VirtueOverNaturalBarriers]], while the chancellor dispute with [[TianWenWeiChancellor|田文]] shows [[TransitionFitOverMerit]] inside a fragile succession.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-08-yidai-mingjiang-wuqi-cansi-2-lvaqlgyccrugfo0l-wuwf1mt1kcm]] adds the reform-politics sequel. Wu Qi's move to [[ChuState|楚国]] turns military and administrative talent into [[WarringStatesReformBacklash]], while [[HanFei|韩非]]'s use of [[ZhaoJinghou|赵靖侯]] adds [[LegalistRulerTechnique]]. The same source also reinforces the period's compressed chronology through Qin, Zhao, Han, and Qi succession or recognition notices.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-08-yidai-mingjiang-wuqi-cansi-3-ltca-ikuzo9ej7a0lfrgwv2-wulb]] adds the next King An years as a concentrated Warring States transition sample. [[QinState|秦国]] changes rulers through a coup, Tian-family [[QiState|齐国]] consolidates through both violence and [[JixiaAcademy|scholar sponsorship]], [[WeiState|魏国]] and [[ZhaoState|赵国]] fight despite their Jin background, an eclipse notice exposes omen reasoning, and [[WuQi|吴起]] dies when reform backlash breaks open after [[ChuDaoWang|楚悼王]]'s death.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-09-zhanguo-shidai-de-shijie-dazhan-losmum91vjpqfymywelj60o7-vbl]] adds a broad interstate-war frame and two old-order endings. Qi, Yan, Han, Zhao, Wei, and Chu are entangled in [[EarlyWarringStatesInterstateWar]], while [[QiKangGong|齐康公]]'s death and [[JinJinggong|晋静公]]'s deposition show older residual lineages disappearing behind the new power map. [[Zisi|子思]]'s advice adds governance concepts about flawed talent and failed correction inside courts.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-10-yi-jiazu-danwei-you-mayi-chi-daxiang-de-jingshen-1-lq3t7lwpmq1oyb8zmmmn2tywnu8d]] adds a long-horizon statecraft branch. [[HanState|韩国]]'s destruction of [[ZhengState|郑国]] becomes [[GenerationalFamilyStrategy]], while repeated capital moves become [[StrategicCapitalRelocation]]. The same episode adds [[QinState|秦国]] origin diplomacy through [[TaishiDan|太史旦]] and keeps the period's conflict tempo visible through Yan-Qi-Lu-Wey and Zhao-Wei notices.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-10-yi-jiazu-danwei-you-mayi-chi-daxiang-de-jingshen-2-lmnqfjmydygqnfec6xhzpixralvi]] adds the institutional fragility behind that conflict tempo. Han court violence, Wei succession disorder, Qi reputation auditing, and explicit source dating doubts show early Warring States politics as a problem of records, heirs, information channels, and interstate pressure at the same time.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-11-shijie-fengqi-yunyong-1-lt60b2gddiosf5kade35uopwohi5]] opens [[ZhouXianwang|周显王]]'s long reign as a still more crowded conflict field. Qi, Zhao, Wei, Han, Song, and Qin all appear in terse war notices, while [[QinEastwardPressure]] and [[ShimenBattle|石门之战]] show Qin's military advantage becoming visible to the Three Jin states. The source also adds a ritual layer: [[FufuRobe|黻服]] and [[TwelveInsigniaPoliticalSymbolism|十二华章政治象征]] show that warfare and legitimacy are being narrated through material symbols as well as armies.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-11-shijie-fengqi-yunyong-2-llzzalowv5vmbvgd6l-jujltkbyf]] adds 362 BCE as a pre-reform pressure sample. [[WeiState|魏国]] defeats [[HanState|韩国]] and [[ZhaoState|赵国]], then [[QinState|秦国]] defeats Wei at [[Shaoliang|少梁]]. In the same year, [[WeyState|卫国]] title demotion shows small-state status loss, [[YanState|燕国]] changes rulers, and [[QinXiaogong|秦孝公]] inherits a state that needs both internal repair and [[QinDiplomaticExclusion|diplomatic-status reversal]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-14-qiweiwang-buming-zeyi-yiming-jingren-lrs6rbifyiqrojxg0mccpg7sf9rv]] extends that early Zhou Xian Wang cluster through 358-356 BCE. Qin-Han war, Wei's western wall, Qin-Chu marriage, Wei-Han and Zhao-Yan/Qi-Song meetings, and Qi Wei Wang's delayed emergence show the period as a mix of military pressure, infrastructure, alliance ritual, meeting diplomacy, and damaged chronology.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-16-sunbin-pangjuan-enyuan-jiuge-guiling-zhi-zhan-lryhzkc1-xsvfjhvicu8yxa9d2jr]] adds 353 BCE as a strategy and fragmentation case. [[GuilingBattle|桂陵之战]] shows [[QiState|齐国]] using [[EncirclingWeiToRescueZhao|indirect pressure]] against [[WeiState|魏国]], while [[EasternWesternZhouSplit|东周西周分裂]] shows the [[ZhouRoyalHouse|周王室]] itself shrinking and dividing under the same Warring States conditions.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-17-hanzhaohou-he-shenbuhai-lghjmjhq9qs4se96c5ofgz6caqof]] adds the 352-350 BCE link between reform, diplomacy, and ruler discipline. [[QinEastwardPressure|Qin pressure]] makes Wei repair relations with Zhao and Qi, [[ShenBuhai|申不害]] and [[HanZhaohou|韩昭侯]] turn Han governance toward [[MeritBasedRewardPunishment|merit-based reward and punishment]], and [[ShangYangReforms|商鞅变法]] reaches capital geography, households, counties, land, and measurements through [[Xianyang|咸阳]], [[HouseholdSeparationLaw|分居令]], and [[QinAdministrativeStandardization]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-18-zhongguo-lishishang-di-yi-li-rentoushui-lgqnequk0rxejqgihwdqxqyqnsvq]] adds 349-343 BCE as a fiscal, lexical, and legitimacy case. [[PollTax|人头税]] shows Qin turning reform into person-based extraction, [[MilitaryActionTerminology|伐/侵/袭 terminology]] shows how political violence is encoded in chronicle verbs, [[AudienceVsMeetingDiplomacy|诸侯会于京师]] shows Zhou authority being bypassed at [[Luoyi|洛邑]], and [[ZhouHegemonRecognition|王致伯于秦]] shows the same weakened center still elevating [[QinState|秦国]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-19-maling-zhi-zhan-lhbapt09x2ptdqx0uopkp8apbada]] adds 341 BCE as a rescue timing and operational-risk case. [[MalingBattle|马陵之战]] shows [[QiState|齐国]] turning [[HanState|韩国]]'s danger into leverage through [[DelayedRescueStatecraft|delayed rescue]], while [[WeiState|魏国]]'s response brings together [[PangJuan|庞涓]], [[PrinceShenWei|太子申]], [[CrownPrinceCommandRisk|crown-prince command risk]], and [[ForcedMarchOperationalRisk|forced-march risk]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-20-pangjuan-si-ci-shu-xia-ls-8-3jhz5q1c5ca11mhdzmz4wg4]] adds the battle's resolution. [[SunBin|孙膑]] turns false weakness into [[DecreasingStovesDeception|减灶诱敌]], Pang Juan's rapid pursuit ends in death, [[QiState|齐国]] captures [[PrinceShenWei|太子申]], and [[WeiState|魏国]]'s decline becomes a major Warring States power-map consequence.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-22-gongsunyang-zha-wei-gongzi-ang-lss1l7lojfwgwdtcje7m5ijodpxi]] adds 340 BCE as the next consequence of that power-map shift. [[QinState|秦国]] uses [[ShangYang|公孙鞅 / 商鞅]]'s strategic case to strike weakened [[WeiState|魏国]], while [[GongziAng|公子卬 / 公子昂]]'s capture shows how Warring States competition blends interstate timing, reform incentives, aristocratic trust, and deception.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-23-weihuiwang-hui-buyong-gongshu-zhi-yan-lveaqklo7oncmqudgmdk7l03qhsf]] turns that strike into a larger period pattern. Qin's victory forces Wei's cession and capital move, while Shang Yang's reward, reputation damage, and death show how Warring States success could combine military merit, one-shot deception, displaced capital geography, and reform backlash in one career.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-32-shishang-shouwei-lianheguo-mishuzhang-suqin-lgfkk45yq1c0yblequ-d1t7mol3]] adds a later Su Qin perspective on the same period. The episode's [[HezongAlliance|合纵]] tour shows diplomacy adapting to larger armies, city wealth, and ruler face: [[WeiState|魏国]]'s army list points to [[WarringStatesMilitaryTransformation|military transformation]], while [[Linzi|临淄]] turns [[QiState|齐国]]'s urban prosperity into evidence of state capacity and dignity.
