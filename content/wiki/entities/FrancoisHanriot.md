# François Hanriot

## Overview

François Hanriot was the Paris National Guard commander whose forces rescued him during the Thermidorian crisis but whose retreat from the Convention surrendered the Commune's strongest military opportunity.

## Current Profile

The source places Hanriot at the operational hinge of 9 Thermidor. After his arrest, roughly three thousand guardsmen with artillery freed him and faced a poorly defended [[NationalConvention|National Convention]]. He ordered them back toward the Hôtel de Ville instead of attacking, giving the Convention time to outlaw the Robespierrists and organize forces under [[PaulBarras|Paul Barras]]. As support dispersed, Hanriot fell from a window during the final seizure of the Hôtel de Ville and was later executed.

## Key Characteristics

- He commanded the armed Paris force aligned with the Commune and Robespierre's allies.
- His rescue demonstrated that arrest had not yet settled the political struggle.
- He held a temporary numerical and artillery advantage near an exposed Convention.
- His order to regroup transferred initiative to the Convention.
- His failure was operationally decisive in the source's interpretation but remains counterfactually uncertain.

## Qualifications

- The claim that an immediate assault would have saved Robespierre is a plausible counterfactual advanced by the hosts, not a demonstrated outcome.
- Force totals, orders, timing, the circumstances of his fall, and his private reasoning remain source-scoped.
- Operational error does not alone explain weak popular mobilization, Robespierre's indecision, or Convention unity.

## What Changed

- A canonical profile now records Hanriot's armed opportunity and retreat during the Thermidorian crisis.
