# Minamoto no Yoritomo

## Overview
Minamoto no Yoritomo is the spared Minamoto heir who rises from Izu exile into the political victor of the late [[GenpeiWar]], turning military success into a durable warrior government centered on Kamakura.

## Current Profile
The episodes frame Yoritomo's later success as the delayed consequence of Kiyomori's mercy. [[659-dawn-of-the-samurai-bloodbath-at-the-bridge-part-2-glt3420393571]] shows [[TairaNoKiyomori]] sparing the 13-year-old Yoritomo after [[MinamotoNoYoshitomo]]'s defeat, exiling him to Izu, and underestimating the networks Yoritomo later builds through Kanto samurai and marriage to [[HojoMasako]].

The later episodes contrast Yoritomo with [[MinamotoNoYoshitsune]]. [[660-dawn-of-the-samurai-japans-greatest-warrior-part-3-glt3581345898]] presents Yoritomo as patient, ruthless, charismatic, and strategically gifted: he builds power in the Kanto, gains prestige by sending grain toward famine-struck areas around Kyoto, and turns [[KisoYoshinaka]]'s attack on [[GoShirakawa]] into a legitimacy opening. [[661-dawn-of-the-samurai-the-shogun-triumphant-part-4-glt7837412375]] then shows Yoritomo institutionalizing victory.

Yoritomo's political strength is also his ruthlessness. He uses imperial sanction when useful, destroys [[KisoYoshinaka]], kills or marginalizes defeated Taira elites, and refuses to allow Yoshitsune's fame to become an independent power base.

## Key Characteristics
- Yoritomo's career begins as a survival story: spared, exiled, and presumed harmless by the Taira.
- His Izu and Kanto networks turn exile into a power base once anti-Taira revolt becomes possible.
- He is presented as the figure who converts civil-war victory into governing structure.
- He relies on imperial legitimacy while moving real power away from Kyoto toward Kamakura.
- His rivalry management is ruthless, extending from Kiso to Yoshitsune and defeated Taira survivors.
- The source frames him as the greatest political figure in samurai history because his settlement outlives him.

## Evidence
- Exile and network formation: [[659-dawn-of-the-samurai-bloodbath-at-the-bridge-part-2-glt3420393571]] links Yoritomo's survival to Kiyomori's mercy, Izu exile, links with local samurai, and marriage to [[HojoMasako]].
- Prestige and patience: [[660-dawn-of-the-samurai-japans-greatest-warrior-part-3-glt3581345898]] contrasts Yoritomo's eastern base, grain diplomacy, and strategic caution with Yoshitsune's speed and aggression.
- Legitimacy strategy: [[660-dawn-of-the-samurai-japans-greatest-warrior-part-3-glt3581345898]] shows Yoritomo using Kiso's attack on [[GoShirakawa]] to claim the role of imperial defender; [[661-dawn-of-the-samurai-the-shogun-triumphant-part-4-glt7837412375]] emphasizes the continuing need for imperial sanction even as samurai power rises.
- Institutional settlement: [[661-dawn-of-the-samurai-the-shogun-triumphant-part-4-glt7837412375]] describes Yoritomo's 1190 Kyoto visit, 1192 appointment as shogun, and Kamakura-based government.
- Rival removal: [[661-dawn-of-the-samurai-the-shogun-triumphant-part-4-glt7837412375]] traces his attacks on Kiso and Yoshitsune and his harsh treatment of captured Taira leaders.

## Qualifications
The source's judgment that Yoritomo is the greatest political figure in samurai history is an interpretive ranking, not a settled comparative claim across all Japanese historiography. Part 2's account of his childhood survival is also framed through later knowledge that the decision to spare him proved disastrous for the Taira.

## What Changed
- Added the part 2 survival-and-exile layer: Kiyomori's mercy, Izu, Kanto ties, and Hojo Masako as the precondition for later rebellion.

## Relationships
- [[MinamotoNoYoshitsune]] - battlefield-winning half-brother whose fame Yoritomo suppresses.
- [[TairaNoKiyomori]] - enemy who spares Yoritomo and thereby preserves the future Minamoto threat.
- [[HojoMasako]] - Izu alliance connection in Yoritomo's rebuilding of power.
- [[KisoYoshinaka]] - Minamoto rival destroyed after seizing Kyoto.
- [[GoShirakawa]] - imperial legitimacy source and political operator whom Yoritomo eventually outmaneuvers.
- [[KamakuraShogunateFormation]] - institutional result associated with Yoritomo's victory.
- [[SamuraiRule]] - broader regime order Yoritomo inaugurates.
