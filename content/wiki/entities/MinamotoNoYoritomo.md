# Minamoto no Yoritomo

## Overview
Minamoto no Yoritomo is the political victor of the late [[GenpeiWar]], turning Minamoto military success into a durable warrior government centered on Kamakura.

## Current Profile
The episodes contrast Yoritomo with [[MinamotoNoYoshitsune]]. [[660-dawn-of-the-samurai-japans-greatest-warrior-part-3-glt3581345898]] presents Yoritomo as patient, ruthless, charismatic, and strategically gifted: he builds power in the Kanto, gains prestige by sending grain toward famine-struck areas around Kyoto, and turns [[KisoYoshinaka]]'s attack on [[GoShirakawa]] into a legitimacy opening. [[661-dawn-of-the-samurai-the-shogun-triumphant-part-4-glt7837412375]] then shows Yoritomo institutionalizing victory.

Yoritomo's political strength is also his ruthlessness. He uses imperial sanction when useful, destroys [[KisoYoshinaka]], kills or marginalizes defeated Taira elites, and refuses to allow Yoshitsune's fame to become an independent power base.

## Key Characteristics
- Yoritomo is presented as the figure who converts civil-war victory into governing structure.
- He relies on imperial legitimacy while moving real power away from Kyoto toward Kamakura.
- His rivalry management is ruthless, extending from Kiso to Yoshitsune and defeated Taira survivors.
- The source frames him as the greatest political figure in samurai history because his settlement outlives him.

## Evidence
- Prestige and patience: [[660-dawn-of-the-samurai-japans-greatest-warrior-part-3-glt3581345898]] contrasts Yoritomo's eastern base, grain diplomacy, and strategic caution with Yoshitsune's speed and aggression.
- Legitimacy strategy: [[660-dawn-of-the-samurai-japans-greatest-warrior-part-3-glt3581345898]] shows Yoritomo using Kiso's attack on [[GoShirakawa]] to claim the role of imperial defender; [[661-dawn-of-the-samurai-the-shogun-triumphant-part-4-glt7837412375]] emphasizes the continuing need for imperial sanction even as samurai power rises.
- Institutional settlement: [[661-dawn-of-the-samurai-the-shogun-triumphant-part-4-glt7837412375]] describes Yoritomo's 1190 Kyoto visit, 1192 appointment as shogun, and Kamakura-based government.
- Rival removal: [[661-dawn-of-the-samurai-the-shogun-triumphant-part-4-glt7837412375]] traces his attacks on Kiso and Yoshitsune and his harsh treatment of captured Taira leaders.

## Qualifications
The source's judgment that Yoritomo is the greatest political figure in samurai history is an interpretive ranking, not a settled comparative claim across all Japanese historiography.

- [[KamakuraShogunateFormation]] - institutional result associated with Yoritomo's victory.
