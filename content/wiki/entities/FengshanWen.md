## Overview

《封禅文》 / *Fengshan Wen* is the posthumous manuscript that Hanji 359-2 attributes to [[SimaXiangru|司马相如]] and presents as an appeal for [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] to perform [[FengshanRitualLegitimacy|fengshan]] at [[Taishan|泰山]].

## Current Profile

The episode places the work between private literary preparation and state ritual decision. Sima reportedly leaves instructions that the manuscript be given to an imperial messenger; after his death, [[ZhuoWenjun|卓文君]] transmits it when the court comes looking for his writings. Its described argument invokes legendary fengshan rulers, praises Wudi's achievements, interprets auspicious signs, and urges sacrifice to Heaven and Earth.

The work matters in the episode less as a text examined line by line than as political literature. It gives Wudi a prestigious literary warrant at a moment when an auspicious tripod and court interest make fengshan actionable. The source does not establish that the memorial alone caused the ceremony, and its lack of extended quotation prevents a firm judgment about rhetoric, textual history, or precise ritual content.

## Key Characteristics

- Posthumous prose work attributed by the episode to Sima Xiangru.
- Manuscript reportedly reserved for delivery to an imperial envoy.
- Uses legendary precedent, imperial praise, and auspicious signs to advocate fengshan.
- Connects literary persuasion with a later court process of ritual reconstruction.

## Qualifications

The current page is bounded to one short podcast summary. It does not reproduce the complete work or compare received versions, and it does not independently verify Sima's delivery instructions, Zhuo Wenjun's role, Wudi's emotional response, or the manuscript's causal weight. The text should therefore be treated as an episode-attributed political-literary intervention rather than a fully analyzed primary document.

## What Changed

- Created the work as a canonical text entity rather than leaving it inside Sima Xiangru's biography.
