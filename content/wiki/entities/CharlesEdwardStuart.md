# Charles Edward Stuart

## Overview

Charles Edward Stuart, remembered as Bonnie Prince Charlie, appears as the Stuart claimant whose 1745 campaign, defeat at Culloden, and escape through the Highlands and islands became a powerful landscape of memory for [[SamuelJohnson]] and [[JamesBoswell]].

## Current Profile

The episode recounts Charles landing in Scotland, leading a Jacobite army as far as Derby, retreating, and suffering destruction at Culloden before escaping through the Highlands and Hebrides with help that included [[FloraMacDonald]]. By 1773 he was absent from Britain, but his route, veterans, hiding places, former bed, and rescuers made him vividly present to Johnson and Boswell.

Their attachment was political only in a qualified sense. Johnson’s Tory sympathy and Boswell’s childhood romance made the prince an emotional and literary figure, while the tour also exposed the forts, repression, emigration, and social transformation that followed the failed rising.

## Key Characteristics

- Stuart claimant whose 1745 campaign challenged the Hanoverian monarchy.
- Military leader whose advance to Derby ended in retreat and defeat at Culloden.
- Fugitive whose Highland and Hebridean escape gave later travelers a route of political memory.
- Romantic figure for Johnson and especially Boswell after practical Jacobite restoration had failed.
- Historical presence whose afterlife linked personal nostalgia to post-Culloden institutional change.

## Qualifications

This source centers the prince’s afterlife in a 1773 literary tour rather than providing a complete campaign biography. The route-as-escape-route interpretation, private motives, exact remembered dialogue, and emotional meanings remain episode-attributed.
