## Overview

宋昌 / Song Chang is presented as the Dai official who argues that the invitation to [[LiuHeng|刘恒]] is credible, performs a final inspection before the candidate enters Chang'an, protects the public character of the accession, and then receives command of the capital armies.

## Current Profile

Song Chang rejects [[ZhangWuWesternHan|张武]]'s trap hypothesis through a structural assessment rather than simple trust in the messengers. He argues that the realm has settled around the Liu house, royal kingdoms constrain rival ambitions, the population is not easily mobilized against Han, and the Northern Army's response to [[ZhouBo|周勃]] has already demonstrated that armed allegiance favors the Liu line.

He then converts analysis into procedure. Liu Heng takes him toward Chang'an, sends him ahead from Gaoling, and relies on his report that [[ChenPing|陈平]] and the senior ministers are waiting at the bridge. When Zhou Bo requests a private meeting, Song Chang insists that public business be stated publicly and that the prospective ruler has no private business. After accession, Liu Heng appoints him guard general over the northern and southern armies, turning an adviser who verified the transition into a commander responsible for making it operational. ## Key Characteristics

- Adviser who evaluates accession risk through lineage, force structure, popular stability, and observed allegiance.
- Advocate for accepting the invitation when other Dai officials favor delay.
- Advance envoy who checks the reception outside Chang'an before Liu Heng proceeds.
- Procedural gatekeeper who rejects private bargaining at the point of accession.
- Trusted Dai follower appointed to command the northern and southern armies after enthronement.
