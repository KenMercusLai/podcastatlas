# Alexander Sergeyevich Menshikov

## Overview

Prince Alexander Sergeyevich Menshikov was the Russian commander who opposed the Allied invasion of Crimea at the [[BattleOfTheAlma|Battle of the Alma]].

## Current Profile

[[723-the-charge-of-the-light-brigade-crushing-the-russians-part-3-glt9985161615]] presents Menshikov as overconfident in a strong defensive position above the Alma. He assumed the cliffs on his left were impassable, invited spectators to witness an expected Russian victory, and was outmaneuvered when French troops under Pierre Bosquet climbed that flank while British rifle fire pressed the center.

After defeat, Menshikov retreated toward Sevastopol and initially judged the weakly defended city potentially untenable. Allied delay gave Russian defenders time to strengthen its landward works and block the harbour, so his battlefield failure did not produce the immediate strategic collapse that a rapid Allied advance might have caused.

## Key Characteristics

- Selected a naturally strong defensive position above the Alma.
- Treated the position as effectively impregnable.
- Misjudged the accessibility of his left flank.
- Presided over a defense defeated by French maneuver, British firepower, and Russian errors.
- Retreated toward Sevastopol after the battle.
- Initially doubted whether weakly defended Sevastopol could be held.

## Evidence

- Defensive confidence - [[723-the-charge-of-the-light-brigade-crushing-the-russians-part-3-glt9985161615]] describes Menshikov's chosen heights, expected victory, and invited spectators.
- Flank error - [[723-the-charge-of-the-light-brigade-crushing-the-russians-part-3-glt9985161615]] connects his impassable-cliff assumption to Bosquet's successful movement.
- Strategic aftermath - [[723-the-charge-of-the-light-brigade-crushing-the-russians-part-3-glt9985161615]] reports his retreat and initial belief that Sevastopol might require evacuation.

## Qualifications

The source is an Allied-centered popular-history narrative. Menshikov's precise plan, information, confidence, evacuation view, responsibility relative to subordinates, and the relative effects of terrain, weapons, morale, and command require Russian and specialist evidence.

## What Changed

- Established Menshikov's overconfidence and flank misjudgment as central but not exclusive causes of Russian defeat at the Alma.
