# William Hogarth

## Overview

William Hogarth was an English artist and satirist whose beef-themed images made food, national rivalry, religion, and military anxiety legible through crowded visual contrasts.

## Current Profile

In [[286-england-beef-and-liberty-glt5964129806]], Hogarth is the most important visual author of [[RoastBeefEnglishIdentity]]. After being arrested as a suspected spy while sketching in Calais, he produced *The Gate of Calais*, later circulated as *The Roast Beef of Old England*: a large side of beef approaches an English tavern while ragged French soldiers and a well-fed Catholic friar embody deprivation and hierarchy around it.

His later invasion satire extends the same grammar by showing French troops roasting frogs before a threatened crossing. The images do not simply document diet; they stage a political opposition between English abundance and French hunger, Protestant plainness and Catholic privilege, and sturdy liberty and foreign threat.

## Key Characteristics

- Turns food into a visual language of political and national difference.
- Builds satire through bodily contrast, abundance, deprivation, religion, and military setting.
- Connects personal experience in Calais to a reproducible patriotic image.
- Works across painting, print, caption, cantata, and theatrical reuse rather than inside one medium alone.
- Helps make the roast-beef myth memorable while also pushing it toward caricature.

## Evidence

- Calais image: [[286-england-beef-and-liberty-glt5964129806]] describes a large side of beef destined for an English tavern among ragged French soldiers and a Catholic friar.
- Personal and political context: [[286-england-beef-and-liberty-glt5964129806]] connects the work to Hogarth's arrest as a suspected spy while sketching in Calais.
- Cross-media afterlife: [[286-england-beef-and-liberty-glt5964129806]] says Theodosius Forrest wrote a cantata from the image for Haymarket performance.
- Invasion satire: [[286-england-beef-and-liberty-glt5964129806]] describes a later print in which French troops roast frogs while preparing to invade England, paired with David Garrick's beef-and-beer contrast.

## Qualifications

This profile is limited to the episode's beef-and-liberty branch and is not a survey of Hogarth's life or art. The works reveal patriotic and anti-French representation, not direct measurements of French hunger, English plenty, Catholic practice, or popular belief.
