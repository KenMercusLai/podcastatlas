# Haitian Revolution

## Overview

The Haitian Revolution was the multi-sided upheaval in [[SaintDomingue]] that turned a mass slave uprising into emancipation, defeat of renewed French control, and independent [[Haiti]].

## Current Profile

The episode explains the revolution through convergence rather than a single trigger. A rich and violent plantation order contained an enslaved majority, white colonists, free people of color seeking unequal civic rights, maroon communities, and competing imperial interests. The French Revolution introduced a power vacuum and universal equality language that different groups tried to apply or contain.

The 1791 uprising expanded through coordinated action and plantation burning. Revolutionary war then joined emancipatory purpose to shifting alliances, torture, execution, massacre, and refusal to take prisoners. French representatives proclaimed emancipation under military pressure, the revolutionary government widened it, [[ToussaintLouverture]] became a central leader, and Napoleon's attempt to restore control made the possibility of re-enslavement decisive. [[JeanJacquesDessalines]] led the final independence struggle and later governed through retaliatory killing.

## Key Characteristics

- Mass slave uprising beginning in a highly unequal plantation colony.
- Revolution shaped by French universal rights and metropolitan state collapse.
- Multi-sided conflict among enslaved rebels, whites, free people of color, French forces, and foreign powers.
- Emancipation produced through political principle, military necessity, and organized resistance.
- Successful defeat of attempted renewed colonial control and re-enslavement.
- Atlantic precedent combining liberation, fear, and atrocity.

## Qualifications

The source is a thematic podcast summary rather than a specialized history of the revolution. Religious ceremony, coordination, factional goals, alliance changes, French policy, foreign intervention, atrocity reports, casualty scale, and leader motives remain compressed and episode-scoped. Explaining reciprocal violence does not make all actors' power or responsibility equivalent.

## What Changed

- Established a convergence model linking plantation order, racial status, rights language, state crisis, and organized revolt.
- Preserved emancipation and atrocity within one account without treating either as cancellation of the other.
