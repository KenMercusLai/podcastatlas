# Institute for Business and Home Safety

## Overview
The Institute for Business and Home Safety (IBHS) is an insurer-funded research organization that subjects full-scale buildings and components to wind, wildfire, hail, and weathering tests. [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-9de180c7bff8bba31ae5f11fce51f1e1-34185790-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-9de180c7bff8bba31ae5f11fce51f1e1-34185790]] presents its South Carolina facility as a destruction laboratory for finding practical ways to reduce property losses.

## Current Profile
IBHS sits between private risk management and public safety. Member insurers finance shared research and receive fuller findings, while selected results inform builders, homeowners, product choices, insurance decisions, and building codes. Its evidence can improve structural resilience, but the organization does not claim to solve insurance affordability, geographic concentration, or community-wide exposure.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses repeatable, destructive full-scale tests rather than relying only on post-disaster observation.
- Is funded collectively by insurers with different regional hazard portfolios.
- Studies wind, wind-driven rain, hail, roof aging, embers, vegetation, vents, fences, and other failure pathways.
- Translates research into fortification guidance and certifications intended to be legible to insurers and property owners.
- Releases some findings publicly while reserving fuller access for member insurers.

### Limits of structural science
- [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-9de180c7bff8bba31ae5f11fce51f1e1-34185790-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-9de180c7bff8bba31ae5f11fce51f1e1-34185790]] reports that homes following mitigation standards can still face steep premiums or lost coverage because risk extends beyond one structure.

## Qualifications
- The episode does not provide the lab's engineering protocols, independent replication, complete member list, full research archive, or measured causal effect on aggregate insured losses.
- Member-only access means the research is not a fully public good, even when public findings influence codes and household choices.
- Certification or fortification can reduce physical vulnerability without guaranteeing affordable coverage.
