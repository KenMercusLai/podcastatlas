# Institute for Business and Home Safety

## Overview
The Institute for Business and Home Safety (IBHS) is an insurer-funded research organization that subjects full-scale buildings and components to wind, wildfire, hail, and weathering tests and translates results into fortification guidance and certification.

## Current Profile
[[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-9de180c7bff8bba31ae5f11fce51f1e1-34185790-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-9de180c7bff8bba31ae5f11fce51f1e1-34185790]] establishes IBHS as a destruction laboratory between private risk management and public safety. [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-043ffe0bc2c7516bac64132229755e93-43026861-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-043ffe0bc2c7516bac64132229755e93-43026861]] follows its wildfire standards into Paradise building rules and a first Wildfire Prepared home, showing both their physical value and the unresolved problem of converting certification into insurance eligibility and price.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses repeatable destructive tests rather than relying only on post-disaster observation.
- Is funded collectively by insurers with different hazard portfolios.
- Studies wind, rain, hail, roof aging, embers, vegetation, vents, fences, and other failure pathways.
- Translates research into fortification guidance, certifications, product choices, and possible code changes.
- Produces evidence that can improve structural resilience without binding insurers to offer affordable coverage.
- Releases some findings publicly while reserving fuller access for members.

## Qualifications
- The sources do not provide engineering protocols, independent replication, the complete member list, or measured aggregate loss effects.
- Member-only access means the research is not a fully public good.
- Certification can reduce physical vulnerability and improve legibility without guaranteeing coverage, discounts, or community-wide market capacity.
- The reported first certification, prices, and policy outcome remain episode-scoped.

## What Changed
- Followed IBHS research from laboratory testing into a town building code and certified home.
- Added direct evidence of the gap between certification and initial underwriting recognition.
- Preserved the distinction between structural-resilience evidence and insurance-market repair.
