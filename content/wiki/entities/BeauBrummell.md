# Beau Brummell

## Overview

Beau Brummell is the Regency dandy whom the episode presents as a decisive interpreter of British masculine understatement and an influence on the later bespoke suit.

## Current Profile

In [[459-the-suit-savile-row-and-smartly-dressed-men-glt8912379704]], Brummell does not reject elite fashion. He shifts display into exact cut, dark coats, trousers, spotless white linen, and a carefully tied cravat. His importance lies in making restraint itself visibly difficult: the result looks controlled and simple but depends on expense, time, cleanliness, servants, and judgment.

## Key Characteristics

- Regency exemplar of dandyism rather than ornamental foppery.
- Advocate of dark coats, white shirts, trousers, and precise neckwear.
- Figure who made cleanliness and controlled fit part of elite masculine style.
- Practitioner of understatement as an expensive performance.
- Cultural precursor to Savile Row's bespoke ideal.

## Evidence

- Silhouette and garments: [[459-the-suit-savile-row-and-smartly-dressed-men-glt8912379704]] associates Brummell with trousers, dark coats, white shirts, clean linen, and the carefully tied cravat.
- Status performance: [[459-the-suit-savile-row-and-smartly-dressed-men-glt8912379704]] emphasizes that his apparently sober appearance remained costly and labor-intensive.
- Tailoring legacy: [[459-the-suit-savile-row-and-smartly-dressed-men-glt8912379704]] places Brummell near the formation of Savile Row's elite bespoke culture.

## Qualifications

The source offers a cultural-history profile, not a complete biography or a quantified account of Brummell's influence. It uses him as an archetype in a longer movement toward sober male dress rather than as the sole inventor of modern menswear.

## What Changed

- Created a profile of Brummell as the key mediator between dandyism, disciplined understatement, and bespoke tailoring.
