# Lazaro Cardenas

## Overview

Lazaro Cardenas was a post-revolutionary Mexican president whom the episode associates with state promotion of [[DayOfTheDead|Dia de Muertos]] as a distinctively Mexican national tradition.

## Current Profile

Within this source, Cardenas marks the 1930s phase in which public institutions amplified Indigenous-looking, popular, and anti-clerical elements of the festival. His role is therefore cultural as well as political: the state did not create every practice from nothing, but selected and recoded inherited Catholic, local, artistic, and Indigenous materials into a national synthesis.

## Key Characteristics

- Post-revolutionary president associated with the consolidation of a secular national state.
- Linked by the episode to strong official promotion of Dia de Muertos.
- Positioned after the violent church-state struggle associated with [[PlutarcoEliasCalles]].
- Representative of [[MexicanPostRevolutionaryCulturalNationalism]].
- Important to the source's qualified [[InventedTradition|invented-tradition]] argument.

## Evidence

- Festival promotion: [[264-mexico-day-of-the-dead-glt9022174156]] says the state strongly promoted Day of the Dead under Cardenas.
- Cultural recoding: [[264-mexico-day-of-the-dead-glt9022174156]] connects this promotion to an Indigenous-facing and anti-European national image.
- Historical layering: [[264-mexico-day-of-the-dead-glt9022174156]] places the initiative over older Catholic observance rather than describing a wholly new ritual calendar.

## Qualifications

The source gives Cardenas a compact supporting role and does not document the agencies, curricula, museums, artists, regions, or local communities through which promotion occurred. The strongest claim that home altars were essentially invented in the 1930s is attributed to Elsa Malvido and remains source-scoped.
