# Mohenjo-daro

## Overview

Mohenjo-daro is a major urban site of the Indus Valley Civilization in present-day Pakistan, used by the episode to open the subcontinent's architectural history at the civilization's height between about 2600 and 1900 BCE.

## Current Profile

[[110-history-of-india-in-10-buildings-glt7972990176]] centers the Great Bath, a watertight citadel structure with channels, changing spaces, and adjacent bathrooms. The combination of water engineering, urban planning, ritual interpretation, and trade makes the site an early case of [[ArchitectureAsHistoricalEvidence]], but the source preserves uncertainty about both the bath's exact use and the causes of urban decline.

## Key Characteristics

- A large planned city associated with the mature Indus Valley Civilization.
- Home to the Great Bath, interpreted as a carefully engineered ritual or communal water space.
- Part of an urban and commercial civilization with trading outposts and long-distance connections.
- Evidence of sophisticated construction whose script, institutions, and decline remain only partly understood.

## Evidence

- Urban chronology: [[110-history-of-india-in-10-buildings-glt7972990176]] places the site's high point between about 2600 and 1900 BCE.
- Water architecture: [[110-history-of-india-in-10-buildings-glt7972990176]] describes the Great Bath as watertight and connected to channels, changing rooms, and bathrooms.
- Commercial setting: [[110-history-of-india-in-10-buildings-glt7972990176]] characterizes the Harappan world through cities, commerce, trade, and outposts.

## Qualifications

The episode does not establish the Great Bath's precise ritual function or a single explanation for the Indus cities' decline. Climate change and shifting water systems are presented as possibilities, not settled causes, and the episode's brief architectural survey is not a complete archaeological account.

## What Changed

- Established Mohenjo-daro as the earliest urban and water-engineering case in the wiki's India architecture branch.
- Preserved functional and collapse uncertainty rather than turning architectural sophistication into false historical completeness.
