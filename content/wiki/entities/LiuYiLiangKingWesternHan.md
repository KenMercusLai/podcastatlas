## Overview

梁怀王刘揖 / Liu Yi is the Western Han prince whose accidental death without an heir opens the Liang succession and strategic-buffer problem in Hanji 221.

## Current Profile

The episode presents Liu Yi primarily through his relationship with tutor [[JiaYi|贾谊]] and the political consequences of his death. He dies after falling from a horse in 169 BCE, leaving no son; the expected abolition of [[LiangKingdomWesternHan|Liang]] leads Jia Yi to argue that the kingdom should instead remain part of a defensive royal geography. For Jia Yi, the loss is also personal and vocational: he interprets the accident as failure in his duty as tutor and the destruction of four years of preparation.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han royal prince and ruler of Liang.
- Pupil whose education is entrusted to Jia Yi.
- Childless death creates a kingdom-succession question with wider strategic consequences.
- Accidental death becomes the immediate trigger in the episode's account of Jia Yi's final grief.

## Qualifications

The current profile rests on one popular-history episode. The exact date, circumstances of the riding accident, succession custom, length and content of the tutorship, and causal role of the death in Jia Yi's own decline require comparison with transmitted sources.
