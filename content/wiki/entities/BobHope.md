# Bob Hope

## Overview

Bob Hope was a widely popular entertainer and enthusiastic golfer who, in the bounded [[RevisionistHistory]] source, became the public face of California's 1960 campaign for preferential golf-course land valuation.

## Current Profile

The episode presents Hope's everyman image as politically useful to private country clubs seeking a constitutional exception from taxation based on land's “highest and best use.” His role illustrates how a familiar public personality can make a narrow institutional benefit appear to protect a broadly valued public amenity.

The source also stresses the period's exclusion: many Los Angeles clubs then barred Jewish and Black members while asking voters to treat their grounds like publicly beneficial open space. That contrast is central to the episode's interpretation of Hope's campaign role, but the supplied summary does not reconstruct his full argument or personal views.

## Key Characteristics

- Nationally popular entertainer with an everyman public persona.
- Enthusiastic golfer associated with California country-club interests.
- Public campaign figure for the 1960 golf-course valuation amendment.
- Example of celebrity legitimacy applied to a specialized tax policy.
