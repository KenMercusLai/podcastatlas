# Scott Morrison

## Overview

Scott Morrison was the Australian prime minister who succeeded Malcolm Turnbull and whose tenure is presented through bushfire criticism, fortress-style pandemic policy, AUKUS, the Djokovic visa dispute, Pentecostalism, and awkward campaign imagery.

## Current Profile

The source introduces Morrison as the beneficiary of [[MalcolmTurnbull|Turnbull's]] removal and then evaluates him through overlapping crises. It criticizes his Hawaii holiday during the 2019-20 bushfires, credits Australia's border restrictions and vaccination response with comparatively low COVID-19 mortality, describes the AUKUS submarine pact as provoking [[France]] and debate about [[China]], and treats the Novak Djokovic visa affair as a confused federal-state controversy. His Pentecostal faith is also used to distinguish him from the Catholic and mainline Protestant backgrounds of earlier leaders.

## Key Characteristics

- Succeeded Malcolm Turnbull after an internal party removal.
- Public image marked by deliberately ordinary presentation and campaign mishaps.
- Criticized for holidaying during the 2019-20 bushfire crisis.
- Associated with “fortress Australia” pandemic controls and vaccine rollout.
- Signed the AUKUS submarine agreement.
- Presided over the Novak Djokovic visa controversy.
- Pentecostal Christian whose religious language drew public attention.
