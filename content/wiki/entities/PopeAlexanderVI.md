## Overview

Pope Alexander VI is presented as the pontiff who treated [[GirolamoSavonarola|Savonarola]]'s prophecy, anticorruption attacks, and disobedience as threats to papal authority, then used summons, preaching restrictions, jurisdictional pressure, excommunication, and threats against Florence to suppress him.

## Current Profile

After forming the Holy League against France in 1495, Alexander faced a Florence aligned with [[CharlesVIIIOfFrance|Charles VIII]] and a preacher who called the king an instrument of justice. The conflict was geopolitical, ecclesiastical, and personal.

Alexander summoned Savonarola to Rome; Savonarola refused, citing illness and fear of assassination. The pope condemned his teaching, prohibited public preaching, and attempted to reorganize Dominican jurisdictions to remove him from Florence. Savonarola ignored these commands.

Excommunication in 1497 did not end Savonarola's influence, so Alexander increased pressure on the Signoria, including reported threats of interdict and measures against Florentine bankers in Rome. This joined famine, disease, factional hostility, disappointed prophecy, and the failed ordeal rather than acting as a single sufficient cause.

## Key Characteristics

- Pope defending jurisdiction over preaching and Dominican organization.
- Organizer of the Holy League against the French.
- Opponent of Savonarola's prophetic claims and anticorruption attacks.
- Escalated from summons and prohibition to excommunication.
- Used possible collective and financial sanctions to pressure Florence.
- One actor in a multi-causal collapse rather than its sole author.
