# Geoffrey Chaucer

## Overview

Geoffrey Chaucer was a 14th-century English poet, royal servant, diplomat, and customs official whose [[TheCanterburyTales]] gives the wiki a literary window onto plague-era social change.

## Current Profile

The episode places Chaucer between commercial London and royal government. Born around 1342 into a wine-trade family, he survived the Black Death, entered the household of Lionel of Antwerp, campaigned in France, was captured and ransomed, travelled diplomatically, and later served as controller of the wool custom. His career therefore connects [[HundredYearsWar|war]], court patronage, trade, language, and [[PostPlagueSocialMobility]].

His literary importance is presented as both English and cosmopolitan. Exposure to French, Latin, Italian writers, merchants, and diplomacy helped him make English a serious literary medium, while his pilgrims represent a society more varied and unstable than the traditional tripartite hierarchy suggests.

## Key Characteristics

- London wine-trade family background followed by access to royal households and administration.
- Direct exposure to the Black Death, the Hundred Years' War, diplomacy, commerce, and political revolt.
- Multilingual and continentally influenced rather than culturally isolationist.
- Comic and self-parodic writer willing to place a failed version of himself inside his own work.
- Observer of hierarchy who gives distinctive speech and agency to people across social ranks.
- Foundational figure in the long legitimation of English as a literary language.
