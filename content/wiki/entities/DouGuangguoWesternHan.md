## Overview

窦广国 / Dou Guangguo is presented as the long-lost younger brother of [[EmpressDowagerDouWesternHan|窦皇后 / later 窦太后]] who rises from childhood sale and displacement into recognized imperial kinship, receives preventive moral supervision, and is later considered but passed over for the chancellorship because visible consort-family favoritism could damage public trust.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-208-yi-tui-wei-jin-chenping-de-houheixue-2-lnz79ec6cz5s1n90tifnnxmfvi2i|Hanji 208 part 2]] now supplies the earlier family history. Dou Guangguo is reportedly sold away at age four or five, transferred through several households, survives a mountain collapse while producing charcoal, and later reaches Chang'an. Remembered details about home, his sister, mulberry gathering, hair washing, and being fed before her palace entry allow the siblings to recognize one another. These details are episode-attributed and do not independently verify every stage of the reunion narrative.

The same source immediately makes recognition a political problem. [[ZhouBo|周勃]] and [[GuanYing|灌婴]] fear that sudden honor after poverty and displacement could produce arrogance or another Lü-family-style outer-relative crisis, so upright people are chosen as teachers and companions for the Dou siblings. This adds formation before office to Guangguo's profile: he is not accused of abuse, but his new social environment is treated as a matter of state risk.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-227-he-rennai-xuming-baisui-yang-qiqie-bairen-ta-shi-shui-liu5ugz2bds9e0rgl9xytk0a4pvj|Hanji 227]] supplies the later chancellorship transition after [[ZhangCang|张苍]] resigns. The episode says Liu Heng regards Guangguo as virtuous and capable enough for the post but worries that the public will interpret appointment of the empress's brother as partiality toward outer relatives.

The current significance is both formative and procedural. Ministers first try to shape how Guangguo inhabits sudden rank; later, his reported competence still does not produce the highest office because the ruler treats public confidence in impartial selection as an independent constraint. [[ShentuJiaWesternHan|申屠嘉]] becomes chancellor instead. Neither source establishes Guangguo's own view of these interventions or whether appointment would actually have produced improper influence.

## Key Characteristics

- Long-lost younger brother of Empress Dou whose recognition moves him abruptly from displacement into imperial kinship.
- Subject of preventive tutorship intended to make sudden honor compatible with modest conduct.
- Described by the episode as virtuous and capable enough to be considered for the chancellorship.
- Passed over because visible kinship could undermine the perceived impartiality of the appointment.

## Evidence

Displacement, recognition, and preventive formation:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-208-yi-tui-wei-jin-chenping-de-houheixue-2-lnz79ec6cz5s1n90tifnnxmfvi2i|Hanji 208 part 2]] recounts sale in childhood, repeated transfer, survival of a mountain collapse, arrival in Chang'an, and sibling recognition through detailed memories.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-208-yi-tui-wei-jin-chenping-de-houheixue-2-lnz79ec6cz5s1n90tifnnxmfvi2i|Hanji 208 part 2]] says Zhou Bo and Guan Ying arrange upright teachers and companions because the Lü precedent makes sudden outer-relative elevation a court-security concern.

Appointment alternative and impartiality:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-227-he-rennai-xuming-baisui-yang-qiqie-bairen-ta-shi-shui-liu5ugz2bds9e0rgl9xytk0a4pvj|Hanji 227]] says Liu Heng considers Dou Guangguo after Zhang Cang's departure, judges him capable, but abandons the choice because it may look like favoritism toward consort kin.

## Qualifications

This is a two-source bounded profile rather than a complete biography. The age at sale, number of transfers, mountain-collapse toll, route to Chang'an, remembered details, reunion procedure, selected teachers, character formation, qualifications, the emperor's private reasoning, and the political effect of passing him over remain episode-attributed. The ministers' fear does not prove Guangguo would have abused rank, and avoidance of an appearance problem does not prove either that he lacked merit or that all outer-relative appointments are improper.

## What Changed

- Added childhood displacement, survival, and sibling recognition before the later appointment question.
- Reframed the profile around two preventive stages: moral formation after sudden elevation and later denial of the highest office on public-trust grounds.
- Kept feared outer-relative abuse distinct from evidence of Guangguo's own conduct.
