## Overview

窦广国 / Dou Guangguo is presented as the capable younger brother of Empress Dou whom [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]] considers but declines to appoint as chancellor because choosing a consort relative could be read as imperial favoritism.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-227-he-rennai-xuming-baisui-yang-qiqie-bairen-ta-shi-shui-liu5ugz2bds9e0rgl9xytk0a4pvj|Hanji 227]] introduces Dou Guangguo only at the chancellorship transition after [[ZhangCang|张苍]] resigns. The episode says Liu Heng regards him as virtuous and capable enough for the post but worries that the public will interpret appointment of the empress's brother as partiality toward outer relatives.

The current significance is therefore counterfactual and procedural: Dou Guangguo's reported competence does not produce office because the ruler treats public confidence in impartial selection as an independent constraint. [[ShentuJiaWesternHan|申屠嘉]] becomes chancellor instead. The source does not establish Dou Guangguo's wider career, his own view of the decision, or whether the alternative appointment would actually have produced improper influence.

## Key Characteristics

- Younger brother of Empress Dou and thus a high-salience outer-relative candidate.
- Described by the episode as virtuous and capable enough to be considered for the chancellorship.
- Passed over because visible kinship could undermine the perceived impartiality of the appointment.
- Counterfactual candidate whose nonappointment helps explain Shentu Jia's selection.

## Qualifications

This is a one-source, one-decision profile rather than a complete biography. Dou Guangguo's family position, character, qualifications, the emperor's private reasoning, the duration of deliberation, and the political effect of passing him over remain episode-attributed. Avoidance of an appearance problem does not by itself prove either that Dou Guangguo lacked merit or that all outer-relative appointments are improper.

## What Changed

- Created a bounded profile for the chancellorship candidate passed over on perceived-favoritism grounds.
