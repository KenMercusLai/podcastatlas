# Otto II

## Overview

Otto II was the son of [[OttoTheGreat]], husband of [[Theophano]], and western emperor whose drive into southern Italy ended in defeat and a succession crisis.

## Current Profile

The episode depicts Otto II as inheriting a strong but contested Saxon imperial project. His marriage to Theophano supplied Byzantine prestige, and the birth of [[OttoIII]] strengthened the dynasty. He then sought wider control in Italy, entering territory contested by Byzantium and Muslim forces and proclaiming an expansive Roman title after taking Taranto.

His defeat in 982 exposed the limits of that project. Rebellion and external attacks followed, and his death from malaria in 983 left Theophano to preserve their young son's inheritance.

## Key Characteristics

- Dynastic heir crowned emperor during his father's lifetime.
- Husband in a marriage designed to strengthen Roman legitimacy.
- Ruler whose southern Italian campaign joined territorial ambition to universal imperial language.
- Defeated commander whose military failure triggered wider instability.
- Father whose early death made female regency central to dynastic survival.

## Evidence

- Dynastic position - [[460-the-empress-of-the-apocalypse-glt9103397168]] links Otto II's prior coronation, marriage, and son's birth to the consolidation of his rule.
- Italian ambition - [[460-the-empress-of-the-apocalypse-glt9103397168]] follows his advance into Byzantine-held territory, capture of Taranto, and claim to rule the whole Roman world.
- Crisis - [[460-the-empress-of-the-apocalypse-glt9103397168]] describes the July 982 defeat, rebellions, Danish attacks, his death in 983, and the resulting minority succession.

## Qualifications

The source's claim that Theophano raised Otto II's ambitions or encouraged a program of Roman reunification is interpretive. The campaign's political aims, coalition possibilities, and the episode's vivid account of his escape and rebuke remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Created a profile centered on the relationship between dynastic legitimacy, Italian expansion, military defeat, and the regency crisis.
