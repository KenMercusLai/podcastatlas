# Lego / 乐高

## Overview
Lego / 乐高 appears in [[waimai-chengwei-haidilao-zengzhang-zuikuai-de-yewu-legao-biaoshi-buhui-yong-ai-lai-sheji-chanpin-1008870590]] as a toy company growing by broadening its consumer base, increasing digital investment, and keeping AI away from independent product design.

## Current Profile
The source presents Lego as a mature consumer brand still finding growth through range expansion: lower-priced simple products for children, complex high-priced sets for adults, and licensed or sports-linked launches. Its AI stance is bounded: software and AI can support standardized materials such as instructions, but product creativity remains assigned to human designers.

## Key Characteristics
- Lego is extending demand across age groups rather than treating toys as only a children's category.
- The company uses frequent new-product launches and partnerships, including sports tie-ins, to widen consumer occasions.
- Digital investment is increasing, including more software engineers and smart bricks with light or sound.
- Its stated AI boundary preserves human designers as the source of product creativity.

## Evidence
- Revenue and audience expansion: [[waimai-chengwei-haidilao-zengzhang-zuikuai-de-yewu-legao-biaoshi-buhui-yong-ai-lai-sheji-chanpin-1008870590]] says first-half revenue reached a record and describes both lower-priced children's products and more complex adult sets.
- Product cadence: [[waimai-chengwei-haidilao-zengzhang-zuikuai-de-yewu-legao-biaoshi-buhui-yong-ai-lai-sheji-chanpin-1008870590]] says Lego released more than 300 new products and used F1 and World Cup collaborations to attract sports fans.
- Digital and AI boundary: [[waimai-chengwei-haidilao-zengzhang-zuikuai-de-yewu-legao-biaoshi-buhui-yong-ai-lai-sheji-chanpin-1008870590]] says software engineering capacity grew and that AI is positioned for standardized work, not independent product creativity.
