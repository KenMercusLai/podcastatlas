# Marie Antoinette

## Overview

Marie Antoinette was the French queen whose information-sharing with Austria deepened the monarchy's wartime legitimacy crisis and whose later imprisonment and relationship with [[PrincesseDeLamballe|the Princesse de Lamballe]] became targets of revolutionary propaganda.

## Current Profile

The sources present Marie Antoinette as both a participant in royal duplicity and an object of exaggerated, often gendered enemy-making. Her transfer of information to Austria gave substance to suspicions of court betrayal without proving Brissot's wider Austrian-plot allegation. She distrusted [[MarquisDeLafayette|Lafayette]] as a potential captor, urged Louis to show resolve on 10 August, and entered the Temple after the monarchy's collapse. Lamballe's loyalty then became dangerous at La Force, while sexualized pamphlet claims about the two women supplied an existing vocabulary that later atrocity stories reused.

## Key Characteristics

- She shared information with Austria during France's war, intensifying suspicion of the crown.
- She rejected Lafayette's rescue logic because dependence on him could mean captivity and civil war.
- She urged Louis toward visible resolve during the 10 August palace crisis.
- She was imprisoned with the royal family in the Temple after the Tuileries insurrection.
- Lamballe remained personally loyal to her despite revolutionary danger.
- Revolutionary pamphlets sexualized the relationship between the two women.

## Evidence

### Wartime duplicity and royal isolation

- [[506-the-french-revolution-massacre-at-the-palace-part-4-glt9954902163]] states that Marie Antoinette passed information to Austria, distinguishes this from Brissot's unproved wider plot, and describes her rejection of Lafayette's rescue offer.

### Tuileries crisis

- [[506-the-french-revolution-massacre-at-the-palace-part-4-glt9954902163]] describes her conduct during the 20 June and 10 August confrontations and the royal family's move from palace to Assembly and prison.

### Imprisonment and royal collapse

- [[544-the-french-revolution-the-september-massacres-part-1-glt8301042051]] places Marie Antoinette and Louis XVI in the Temple as invasion and political panic intensified.

### Lamballe and gendered propaganda

- [[544-the-french-revolution-the-september-massacres-part-1-glt8301042051]] connects Lamballe's loyalty, pamphlet claims about the women, and the sexualized atrocity narratives following Lamballe's murder.

## Qualifications

- These sources do not provide a full biography or complete assessment of her revolutionary politics.
- Real information-sharing with Austria does not prove every claim about an Austrian Committee or her control of policy.
- The source treats the pamphlet allegations as political pornography, not established evidence about the women's private relationship.

## What Changed

- The profile now includes Marie Antoinette's own role in wartime royal duplicity before imprisonment.
- Evidence-backed conduct is separated from unsupported wider conspiracy claims.
- Her distrust of Lafayette and actions during the Tuileries crisis clarify the royal family's narrowing options.
