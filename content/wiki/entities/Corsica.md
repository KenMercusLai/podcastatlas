# Corsica

## Overview

Corsica is the Mediterranean island where Napoleon was born and where his family's political break with Pasquale Paoli ended his viable local career before his rise in revolutionary France.

## Current Profile

The episode presents Corsica in 1792-93 as a violent, factional setting where French revolutionary conflict overlapped with local allegiance and anti-French politics. Napoleon's failed Sardinian expedition, capture, conflict with [[PasqualePaoli|Pasquale Paoli]], and the Bonaparte family's flight transformed the island from homeland into a lost political future. He returned only once, in 1799, while [[RevolutionaryUniversalismIdentity|revolutionary universalism]] gave his later French allegiance a larger ideological meaning.

## Key Characteristics

- The island's politics combined local factional struggle with the wider French revolutionary crisis.
- It was the Bonaparte family's homeland for roughly two and a half centuries before their 1793 flight.
- The failed Sardinian expedition intensified the conflict between Paoli and the Bonapartes.
- Napoleon's expulsion from Corsican politics became a precondition of his French reinvention.
- His single later visit underscores the durability of the break described by the episode.

## Qualifications

This page represents Corsica only through one Napoleon-centered episode. It does not provide a complete account of Corsican institutions, social groups, Paolist politics, French rule, the Anglo-Corsican period, or how other islanders understood the conflict. The depth of Napoleon's inner identity change remains interpretive.
