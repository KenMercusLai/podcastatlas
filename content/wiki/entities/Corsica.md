# Corsica

## Overview

Corsica is the Mediterranean island whose Genoese inheritance, French conquest, Paolist state-building, and revolutionary factional conflict formed Napoleon's first political identity before exile redirected him toward France.

## Current Profile

The new evidence backfills eighteenth-century [[Corsica]] as mountainous, poor, proud, locally violent, and predominantly Ligurian-speaking, with Genoese control stronger on the coast than inland. [[PasqualePaoli|Pasquale Paoli]] turned resistance into a [[CorsicanNationalism|national project]] of constitutional government, education, and public institutions before Genoa transferred its position to France and French force drove him into exile. [[NapoleonBonaparte|Napoleon]] was born during that conquest and initially understood the island as his mother country. The [[FrenchRevolution]] let him imagine autonomy, integration, and revolutionary liberty together, but local clan politics and street conflict made that combination unstable.

Part 2 follows the collapse in 1792-93. Napoleon's failed Sardinian expedition, conflict with Paoli, arrest, pursuit, and the Bonaparte family's flight transformed homeland into a lost political future. He returned only once, in 1799, while [[RevolutionaryUniversalismIdentity|revolutionary universalism]] gave his French allegiance a larger ideological meaning.

## Key Characteristics

- Mountainous island society with a distinct Ligurian-speaking identity and uneven Genoese control.
- French takeover in 1768-69 turned political difference into conquest and resistance.
- Paoli joined independence to constitutional, educational, and institutional state-building.
- Revolutionary politics overlaid autonomy, integration, class language, clan rivalry, and street violence.
- The Bonaparte family's 1793 flight ended a roughly two-and-a-half-century local residence and Napoleon's viable Corsican path.

## Qualifications

Both sources are Napoleon-centered and do not provide a complete social or institutional history of the island, Genoese rule, French administration, the Anglo-Corsican period, or the views of different Corsican communities. Descriptions of poverty, violence, pride, clan politics, and national feeling are broad narrative characterizations. Napoleon's inner identity change remains interpretive.

## What Changed

- Backfilled the island's language, geography, Genoese setting, French conquest, and Paolist institutional program.
- Reframed the 1793 rupture as the end of a longer attempt to combine Corsican nationalism with French revolutionary opportunity.

## Relationships

- [[PasqualePaoli]] - independence leader and institution-builder whose later break with the Bonapartes drove the family crisis.
- [[CorsicanNationalism]] - political tradition joining island liberty to constitutional state-building.
- [[FrenchRevolution]] - upheaval that reopened autonomy and integration before intensifying factional conflict.
