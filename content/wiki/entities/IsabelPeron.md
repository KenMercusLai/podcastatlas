# Isabel Perón

## Overview

María Estela Martínez de Perón, known as Isabel Perón, is presented as [[JuanPeron|Juan Perón]]'s exile-era partner, political messenger, vice-president, and successor as president of [[Argentina]].

## Current Profile

The episode follows Isabel from her meeting with Perón in Panama into the Madrid exile household, where she became a bridge to underground Peronist groups. Supporters increasingly staged her as a successor to [[EvaPeron|Evita]], although the source stresses her limited political experience and dependence on [[JoseLopezRega|José López Rega]].

After Perón's death in 1974, Isabel inherited the presidency amid inflation, factional violence, and state-linked repression. She was overthrown in 1976, detained, and later returned to Madrid.

## Key Characteristics

- She moved from performer and exile companion into movement intermediary and elected vice-president.
- Her public image borrowed heavily from Evita's charisma and symbolism.
- Her political rise was intertwined with López Rega's household and ministerial influence.
- She inherited rather than resolved Peronism's internal conflict and national instability.
- Her presidency ended with the 1976 military coup.

## Evidence

- Exile role: [[498-evita-the-mystery-of-the-missing-body-part-5-glt5587823308]] says Perón used Isabel as a messenger to Peronist networks in Argentina.
- Symbolic succession: [[498-evita-the-mystery-of-the-missing-body-part-5-glt5587823308]] describes supporters treating her reverently, her appearance with an enlarged Evita photograph, and reported rituals around Evita's body.
- Presidency and fall: [[498-evita-the-mystery-of-the-missing-body-part-5-glt5587823308]] follows her vice-presidential candidacy, succession after Perón's death, López Rega's influence, and overthrow in March 1976.

## Qualifications

The episode emphasizes symbolic politics and López Rega's occult reputation, so its ritual stories are not treated as independently verified. This page does not provide a complete administrative history of Isabel's presidency.
