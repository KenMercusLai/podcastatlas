# Jane Sharp

## Overview

Jane Sharp is the seventeenth-century English midwife and author presented in [[191-childbirth-glt5119375504|191. Childbirth]] as an advocate for combining learned theory with practical birth experience.

## Current Profile

The episode uses Sharp's 1671 writing to challenge any clean split between book knowledge and embodied expertise. Her midwife needs both: inherited medical theory alone cannot substitute for attending births, while experience benefits from anatomical and medical understanding.

Sharp also represents the religious and moral vocabulary of [[EarlyModernMidwifery]]. She frames the work as a godly duty, invokes the biblical midwives of Israel, and writes about infant care such as swaddling. Within [[ChildbirthHistory]], she therefore provides a rare named female voice linking practice, authorship, faith, and household care.

## Key Characteristics

- Seventeenth-century English midwife and author discussed through a 1671 midwifery text.
- Argues that competent care requires theoretical and practical knowledge together.
- Frames midwifery as a godly duty rather than merely a technical occupation.
- Connects childbirth care to infant practices such as swaddling.
- Supplies a female-authored counterweight to the male-dominated printed record.

## Evidence

- Theory and practice: [[191-childbirth-glt5119375504|191. Childbirth]] attributes to Sharp the position that good midwives need both learned and hands-on knowledge.
- Religious vocation: [[191-childbirth-glt5119375504|191. Childbirth]] says Sharp invoked the midwives of Israel and presented midwifery as godly service.
- Infant care: [[191-childbirth-glt5119375504|191. Childbirth]] cites her explanation of swaddling and limb development.
- Historical significance: [[191-childbirth-glt5119375504|191. Childbirth]] places her against a surviving literature disproportionately written by men.

## Qualifications

The episode provides selected teachings rather than a full biography, publication history, or textual analysis. Its account establishes Sharp's interpretive role in the episode but does not resolve how representative her printed prescriptions were of ordinary midwifery practice.
