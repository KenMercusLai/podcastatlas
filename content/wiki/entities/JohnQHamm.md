# John Q. Hamm

## Overview

John Q. Hamm is the Alabama corrections commissioner questioned about the state's nitrogen-hypoxia protocol and responsible for its post-execution success account in the final episode of [[TheAlabamaMurders]].

## Current Profile

Hamm's testimony supplies the episode's principal institutional evidence. He says the second execution would reuse the chamber, gurney, team captain, and most personnel from the failed lethal-injection attempt, with a nitrogen-fed mask replacing the intravenous line. Under cross-examination, he acknowledges limited medical involvement and preparation around vomiting and mask sealing. Afterward, he interprets Smith's movement as expected breath-holding, involuntary action, and agonal breathing and says nothing fell outside ordinary expectations.

## Key Characteristics

- Alabama corrections commissioner overseeing the execution protocol described by the episode.
- Confirmed substantial continuity between the failed first attempt and second execution setting and team.
- Acknowledged gaps in medical consultation, vomiting planning, expert review, and written seal assurance.
- Defended the observed execution as consistent with the protocol's expectations.
- Could not clearly see Smith's face when asked about apparent prolonged consciousness.
