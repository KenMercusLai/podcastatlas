# Carlo Collodi / 卡洛·科洛迪

## Overview

Carlo Collodi / 卡洛·科洛迪 is the Italian writer of [[AdventuresOfPinocchio|《木偶奇遇记》]] whose work supplies both a dark writer-made fairy tale and a sharp piece of evidence about pizza's low status in nineteenth-century Italy.

## Current Profile

The Pinocchio source presents Collodi as an author shaped by Italian unification, political disillusionment, education, translation, and serial publication. His initial 1881 ending, in which the puppet is hanged, and the stranger continuation that followed reader pressure make the work resistant to safe moral paraphrase. The food-history source adds a separate role: Collodi's 1886 description of pizza as greasy filth records Tuscan disgust toward a poor Neapolitan street food before it became a national icon.

## Key Characteristics

- Author of a writer-made fairy tale whose darker original exceeds the familiar lesson about lying.
- Serial storyteller whose first ending killed Pinocchio before audience reaction helped produce the longer work.
- Writer whose hunger, fear, punishment, attachment, and transformation themes make childhood morally and psychologically unsettled.
- Nineteenth-century Tuscan observer whose hostility to pizza documents classed and regional difference inside later national food memory.

## Evidence

- Literary formation: [[122-pinuocao-chengzhang-de-cantong-jiqi-suo-chuangzuo-de-777464172]] links Collodi to Italian independence, later political disillusionment, children's writing, education, and translation.
- Serial and ending evidence: [[122-pinuocao-chengzhang-de-cantong-jiqi-suo-chuangzuo-de-777464172]] says the 1881 serial initially ended with Pinocchio hanged before the story continued.
- Food-status evidence: [[484-the-food-that-changed-the-world-glt2872429496]] uses Collodi's 1886 description of pizza to show that it was not yet a universally celebrated Italian national food.

## Qualifications

The food episode uses one vivid published description as status evidence; it does not establish Collodi as a representative of all Tuscans or all non-Neapolitan Italians. The psychological readings of Pinocchio remain interpretations supplied by the literary source rather than a complete account of Collodi's intent.
