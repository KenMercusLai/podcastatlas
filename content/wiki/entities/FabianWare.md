# Fabian Ware

## Overview

Fabian Ware is the organizer whom the episode credits with driving First World War graves registration toward the institution that became the [[ImperialWarGravesCommission]].

## Current Profile

[[118-end-of-the-first-world-war-remembrance-glt2743125909]] presents Ware as a practical institution-builder behind modern remembrance. His importance lies less in a single ceremony than in converting wartime recordkeeping into a durable system for locating, marking, and maintaining the dead.

The policy associated with that system made a moral claim through administration: soldiers would remain near where they died and receive common commemorative treatment rather than a hierarchy shaped by rank or a family's ability to repatriate a body.

## Key Characteristics

- Organizer associated with wartime graves registration.
- Principal institutional figure behind the Imperial War Graves Commission's emergence.
- Advocate of durable and coordinated commemoration.
- Linked to equal treatment across rank and wealth.

## Evidence

- Institutional role: [[118-end-of-the-first-world-war-remembrance-glt2743125909]] identifies Ware as the person who pushed the registration system that became the Commission in 1917.
- Equality principle: [[118-end-of-the-first-world-war-remembrance-glt2743125909]] connects the Commission's common graves and non-repatriation policy to a democratic memorial landscape.

## Qualifications

This is a narrow profile from one podcast episode, not a full biography or an independent assessment of Ware's administration, imperial assumptions, collaborators, or the Commission's unequal practices and later reforms.
