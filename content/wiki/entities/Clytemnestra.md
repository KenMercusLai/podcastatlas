# Clytemnestra

## Overview

Clytemnestra is the mythic queen of [[Mycenae]], sister of [[HelenOfTroy|Helen]], wife and killer of [[Agamemnon]], and the dominant tragic figure in [[Aeschylus]]' [[TheOresteia|Oresteia]].

## Current Profile

The episode presents Clytemnestra as an agent within a violent family order rather than a one-note villain. Agamemnon deceives her and sacrifices their daughter Iphigenia, returns from Troy with Cassandra as a concubine, and is then murdered by Clytemnestra with her ally and lover Aegisthus. Her revenge is intelligible without becoming morally clean: it continues the house's cycle of kin-killing and produces Orestes' retaliatory matricide.

Her dramatic power also exceeds what the source describes as fifth-century Athenian women's formal political position. She therefore carries both a possible memory of visible Bronze Age queenship and a later Athenian anxiety about female, domestic, sexual, and sovereign authority.

## Key Characteristics

- She acts directly as Agamemnon's killer in the episode's reading of the tragedy.
- Her revenge is grounded in Iphigenia's sacrifice, deception, and Agamemnon's return with Cassandra.
- She belongs to a family system where violence is inherited and repeatedly answered with violence.
- Her forceful agency resists reduction to either powerless victimhood or uncomplicated heroism.
- She connects possible Bronze Age elite female status to classical Athens' staging of female power.

## Evidence

- Revenge and agency: [[458-helen-of-troy-a-family-of-blood-part-2-glt7255356131]] centers Iphigenia's sacrifice, Cassandra's arrival, and Clytemnestra's direct killing of Agamemnon.
- Dynastic violence: [[458-helen-of-troy-a-family-of-blood-part-2-glt7255356131]] places the murder inside the cursed house of Tantalus, Pelops, Atreus, and Thyestes and before Orestes' matricide.
- Gender and authority: [[458-helen-of-troy-a-family-of-blood-part-2-glt7255356131]] compares her tragic dominance with evidence and speculation about elite women in the Mycenaean world.

## Qualifications

- Clytemnestra is not established as a recoverable historical individual.
- Her motives and characterization vary across mythic and dramatic traditions; this profile follows the episode's Aeschylean emphasis.
- Archaeological evidence for elite women cannot identify a grave occupant as Clytemnestra or prove the tragic plot.

## What Changed

- Established Clytemnestra as both a revenge agent and a figure through whom Greek tragedy tests gendered authority and inherited violence.

## Relationships

- [[Agamemnon]] - husband she kills after his sacrifice of Iphigenia and return with Cassandra.
- [[HelenOfTroy]] - sister and parallel figure for female power, sexuality, and Bronze Age memory.
- [[TheOresteia]] - tragic trilogy that makes her revenge central to a wider justice crisis.
- [[Aeschylus]] - playwright whose treatment anchors the current profile.
- [[BloodVengeanceToCivicJustice]] - institutional transition set in motion through the family's retaliatory killings.
- [[Mycenae]] - royal and archaeological setting associated with her story.
