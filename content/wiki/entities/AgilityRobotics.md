# Agility Robotics

## Overview
Agility Robotics is the warehouse-humanoid company represented in the All-In robotics special by Jonathan Hurst. The episode uses Agility's Digit to show a more bounded humanoid commercialization route than open-ended household service.

## Current Profile
Agility is presented as a company turning two decades of humanoid and legged-robot research into useful physical work in human environments. Its strongest source-scoped claim is not that humanoids can do everything, but that a bipedal two-arm system can handle a cluster of warehouse workflows where narrow human spaces, balance, whole-body control, and task versatility matter.

## Key Characteristics
- Agility's near-term wedge is warehouse work such as moving bins and totes, not general household autonomy.
- Hurst frames the current cycle as different because perception and AI context have improved, while control data and useful workflows still matter.
- Digit's value depends on versatility across tasks such as each picking, bin filling, carrying, palletizing, and depalletizing.
- Safety is central to scaling: Digit V5 is described as a redesign aimed at operation around people without a physical barrier.
- Agility offers both CapEx and robot-as-a-service models depending on customer preference.
- The company is also framed as an emerging labor-market creator for operators, assemblers, deployment staff, and maintenance roles.

## Qualifications
The episode does not prove that Digit V5 safety certification, economics, or future market expansion will scale. It keeps Agility's value claim tied to bounded physical workflows where current robots can do useful work without needing full general autonomy.
