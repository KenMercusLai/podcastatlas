# Fugitive Slave Act of 1850

## Overview

The Fugitive Slave Act of 1850 is the federal law presented as a major prewar trigger because it enabled the capture and return of alleged fugitives from slavery within Northern communities.

## Current Profile

[[200-american-civil-war-the-causes-part-1-glt1124511401]] emphasizes the act as a mechanism rather than background legislation. Slaveholding interests demanded federal enforcement of property claims across state borders, while Northern states and communities invoked local authority and liberty against that enforcement.

The [[AnthonyBurns|Anthony Burns]] rendition made the statute's meaning visible in Boston. His chained removal under federal protection showed residents that slavery could command national power even where state law rejected the institution.

## Key Characteristics

- Federal enforcement statute for the recovery of alleged fugitives from slavery.
- Law extending slave-property claims into free-state communities.
- Catalyst for Northern resistance and antislavery radicalization.
- Example of states' rights and federal power reversing around slavery.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to one episode's causal use of the statute, not a full account of its procedure, constitutional litigation, enforcement variation, personal liberty laws, resistance networks, or the wider Compromise of 1850. The claim that it may have been the single most important legislative trigger remains Smith's interpretation.
