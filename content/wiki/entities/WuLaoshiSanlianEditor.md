## Current Profile
In the source, Wu Laoshi grounds AI-era education in journalism, parenting, and contact with the real world. Her profile centers on the argument that young people should not be trained into obedient, emotionless, machine-like performers; they need professional self-knowledge, field experience, resilience, and the social ability to handle real people and concrete difficulties.

## Key Characteristics
- Wu uses journalism as the clearest case for profession-person fit: some reporting work depends on temperament, initiative, scene entry, and willingness to go to the现场.
- She warns that AI can remove junior training rungs in fields such as finance and law, making professional formation harder even when senior judgment remains valuable.
- She distinguishes information access from knowledge ownership, especially in medicine, science, writing, and reporting.
- She treats real-world experience as an education remedy: students need contact with people, institutions, friction, and uncertainty beyond exam practice.
- Her parenting answer is explicitly humanizing: children should develop emotion, resilience, autonomy, and practical problem handling rather than only obedience.
