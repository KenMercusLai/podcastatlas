# Neuralink

## Overview
Neuralink is presented in the bounded April 2023 interview and its later condensation as a neurotechnology company developing implantable interfaces that record from and stimulate neural tissue, with [[MatthewMacDougall]] serving as its head neurosurgeon at that time.

## Current Profile
The overlapping source versions describe Neuralink’s immediate product direction as an assistive [[BrainComputerInterface]] for people with severe paralysis: fine cortical electrodes would capture motor intentions and software would convert them into cursor and keyboard actions. The approach combines a surgeon-led operation with [[RoboticNeuralElectrodeInsertion]] and adaptive decoding. More ambitious ideas—including brain-to-spinal bypass, silent speech, memory functions, multi-brain communication, and AI-linked cognition—remain future-facing in this evidence set.

## Key Characteristics
- Prioritizes assistive digital control before natural-limb restoration or able-bodied augmentation in the source’s roadmap.
- Uses fine implantable cortical electrodes and software decoding rather than treating the implant as a general intelligence or plasticity device.
- Relies on a specialized insertion robot for repeatable placement around surface vasculature.
- Uses animal models for surgical safety and functional signal validation in the development account.
- Faces a recurring tradeoff between high theoretical neural bandwidth, surgery, safety evidence, and user adoption.
