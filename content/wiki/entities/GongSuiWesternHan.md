## Overview

龚遂 / Gong Sui enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-425-huoguang-sihou-canzao-miemen-simaguang-weisha-fenfenbuping-lmz7bdfy8anhkuq24l-f2ulkolbu|Hanji 425]] as a Western Han official whose Bohai commandery governance becomes an exemplar of [[LivelihoodFirstPacification|民生优先安抚治理]] and [[HumaneLocalGovernance|仁厚地方治理]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-405-1-jiunangfandai-wudao-hunjun-haihunhou-liuhe-daodi-duo-huangtang-ljoll1pll7ovr4lnyuzcyykxgbgs|Hanji 405-1]] now strengthens his earlier Changyi profile: before Liu He's accession, Gong Sui is already a forceful remonstrant who tries to separate [[LiuHeHaihunHou|刘贺]] from sycophants and install upright classics-literate companions.

## Current Profile

The source set gives Gong Sui a long remonstrance-to-governance arc. Hanji 405-1 shows him under Liu He as a principled, forceful adviser: he rebukes surrounding officials, cries before the ruler, treats servant-and-sycophant intimacy as a survival danger, cites the Jiaoxi king Liu Duan as a warning case, and tries to surround Liu He with upright readers of the classics. Hanji 425 then summarizes the later consequence of that record: Gong Sui is spared by [[HuoGuangWesternHan|霍光]] when Liu He falls, but still endures shaved-headed convict labor before release. By the time [[EmperorXuanOfHan|汉宣帝]] considers him for Bohai, he is old, thin, and unimpressive in appearance.

His governing value appears in diagnosis and method. Gong Sui tells Xuan that Bohai's "robbers" are fundamentally hungry commoners neglected by officials, then asks whether the emperor wants suppression or pacification. After securing room to act, he removes catch-bandit officials, distinguishes farmers' tools from weapons, opens granaries, models thrift, restores agricultural and livestock production, and personally inspects and persuades households. The episode presents his later Water-Balance Commandant appointment as a central-office honor and retirement-style reward rather than the best use of his local talents.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han clean-official figure whose early service under Liu He included tearful direct remonstrance, companion reform, and later punishment.
- Elderly local-governance specialist whose value is initially hidden by unimpressive appearance.
- Diagnostic official who treats banditry as hunger plus official neglect before treating it as criminal identity.
- Pacification practitioner who asks for operational autonomy and uses classification rules to reduce confrontation.
- Relief-and-production administrator who combines granary release, thrift, agriculture, livestock, inspection, and persuasion.
- Late-recognized official recalled to central service as 水衡都尉 after Bohai's recovery.

## Qualifications

This page is source-scoped to Hanji 405-1 and Hanji 425. It does not reconstruct Gong Sui's full biography, exact Bohai tenure dates, the original Han Shu wording beyond the episode retellings, or quantitative measures of crime reduction. Hanji 405-1's strange-dog warning is treated as omen-coded remonstrance inside the episode. Hanji 425 treats 水衡都尉 as an honor and pension-like central role; that interpretation should remain source-scoped until supported elsewhere.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 405-1's early Changyi remonstrance layer: Gong Sui's tearful warnings, sycophant diagnosis, Liu Duan cautionary example, and failed attempt to place upright companions around Liu He.
- Preserved Hanji 425's later profile as a Western Han livelihood-first local-governance exemplar.
