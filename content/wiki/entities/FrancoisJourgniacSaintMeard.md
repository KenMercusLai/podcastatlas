# François Jourgniac Saint-Méard

## Overview

François Jourgniac Saint-Méard was a royalist journalist and army officer who survived an improvised tribunal at the Abbaye during the [[SeptemberMassacres]].

## Current Profile

Saint-Méard matters chiefly as a survivor-witness. His later account, *My Agony of 38 Hours*, describes prisoners listening to quiet executions, preparing answers, and facing a tribunal that could acquit as well as condemn; the testimony supports a picture of selective, ritualized killing while remaining a retrospective personal narrative.

## Key Characteristics

- He entered the Abbaye as a politically suspect royalist journalist and former officer.
- He heard killings conducted with an unnerving quiet rather than continuous crowd tumult.
- A guard helped him anticipate the tribunal's questions.
- His answers persuaded the killers that he was not a conspirator.
- His survival helps establish that the prison violence was selective rather than indiscriminate.
