# A Star Is Born (1937)

## Overview

*A Star Is Born* (1937) is a [[DavidOSelznick]] production about rising actor Vicki Lester and declining, alcohol-dependent star Norman Maine. [[star-struck]] treats its revised ending as a consequential redistribution of harm, responsibility, and audience sympathy.

## Current Profile

[[DorothyParker]] and Alan Campbell's screenplay had Norman drive drunk, force another vehicle off Malibu Road, and confront the fatal wreck. That version made his addiction dangerous to strangers rather than only self-destructive and gave audiences a concrete image of intoxicated driving as foreseeable lethal wrongdoing.

The released film removes the deaths. Norman hits a tree, receives a jail sentence, returns to Vicki's care, and ultimately dies by suicide; Vicki's public identification as “Mrs. Norman Maine” completes his romantic memorialization. The source therefore uses the film as a counterfactual cultural case, not proof that retaining the collision would have changed public policy.

## Key Characteristics

- 1937 Hollywood drama pairing Vicki Lester's ascent with Norman Maine's decline.
- Addiction story whose original screenplay extended Norman's harm to unknown road users.
- Production case in which junior-reader feedback helped make a fatal collision seem narratively unusable.
- Released film that preserves Norman as a tragic romantic center through a nonfatal crash and suicide.
- Counterfactual example of popular culture potentially supplying moral vocabulary before institutions adopt it.

## Evidence

### Deleted collision

- [[star-struck]] describes Parker and Campbell's scene of Norman crossing into opposing traffic and causing a fatal fiery crash.

### Revised accountability

- [[star-struck]] reports that Selznick replaced the collision with a tree crash, jail, and Vicki's promise to assume responsibility for Norman.

### Final sympathy

- [[star-struck]] connects Norman's suicide and Vicki's final public identification with him to a romantic frame centered on his suffering and memory.
