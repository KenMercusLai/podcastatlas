# Saul Kassin

## Overview
Saul Kassin is a psychologist represented here through research and public explanation on confession evidence, interrogation pressure, juror judgment, and wrongful conviction.

## Current Profile
In [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-7b40091055d1394cc6a3435ffecc95c8-53960014-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-7b40091055d1394cc6a3435ffecc95c8-53960014|Did I Really Do That?]], Kassin connects his early interest in accusation and helplessness to mock-jury findings, interrogation manuals, wrongful-conviction cases, and laboratory experiments. His central position is that confession evidence must be evaluated as the output of a process rather than treated as self-authenticating proof.

He distinguishes voluntary, compliant, and internalized false confessions; emphasizes authority, isolation, duration, misinformation, immediate relief, and suggestibility; and argues that a confession can contaminate later evidence. His reform program favors complete recording, open-ended information-gathering interviews, protection of nonpublic crime details, and active testing of alternative explanations.

## Key Characteristics
- Legal and social psychologist focused on confession evidence and wrongful conviction.
- Treats interrogation as a situation capable of changing incentives, memory confidence, and behavior.
- Combines case analysis with mock-jury, witness, and false-confession experiments.
- Distinguishes the production of a confession from the later interpretation of its reliability.
- Advocates transparent, evidence-preserving, hypothesis-testing investigation.
