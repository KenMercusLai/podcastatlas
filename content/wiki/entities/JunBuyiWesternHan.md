## Current Profile

The sources now present Jun Buyi as a judicial and political official whose later crisis decisiveness rests on earlier evidence, ritual, and moderation habits. Hanji 379-2 says that when Bao Shengzhi summons him in Bohai, Jun refuses to remove his sword, enters as a self-possessed ritual actor, and warns Bao that too much hardness breaks while too much softness abandons government. Bao accepts the counsel and recommends him, leading to Jun's Qingzhou inspector appointment.

Hanji 394-1 says that after taking office as Jingzhao Yin in 86 BCE, Jun reviews county cases and prisoners, then returns home to a mother who asks whether he has vindicated the wronged and saved people from death. Her pleasure or displeasure makes justice a repeated family standard. This is not sentimental background only: the episode links that daily maternal evaluation to Jun's evidence-conscious, fair, and benevolent style.

Hanji 396-2 then shows the political version of the same official temperament. Other senior officials hesitate because the claimant resembles Liu Ju and because a mistaken denial could be dangerous. Jun Buyi instead treats the claim as an order problem: even if Liu Ju had somehow survived the [[WuguIncidentWesternHan|巫蛊之祸]], he had offended the former emperor and fled, so he could not simply reappear as a legitimate claimant. This allows the court to act before rumor, crowd attention, and verification paralysis widen the crisis.

## Key Characteristics

- Jingzhao Yin whose mother repeatedly evaluates his office by whether he redresses wrongful cases and saves lives.
- Scholar-official whose ritual self-possession lets him advise a severe imperial envoy without appearing subordinate.
- Moderation adviser who frames good authority as a balance between威严 and恩惠.
- Judicial official associated with evidence, fairness, benevolence, and caution around life-and-death decisions.
- Western Han Jingzhao Yin who intervenes in the palace-gate impostor crisis.
- Official who prioritizes dynastic order and former-emperor authority over resemblance-based uncertainty.
- Classical-reasoning crisis handler whose Wei crown-prince precedent is praised by Han Zhao and Huo Guang after the arrest.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 396-2, Hanji 394-1, and Hanji 379-2. It does not reconstruct Jun Buyi's complete career, the exact legal status of Liu Ju after the Wugu affair, the primary text of the Wei crown-prince precedent, the full deliberation among all officials at the palace gate, the complete Western Han Jingzhao Yin procedure, or every case Jun reviewed. Hanji 379-2's transcript renders his name as 郡布夷; the wiki normalizes it to 隽不疑 based on the shared Bao Shengzhi, Bohai, Spring and Autumn learning, and Qingzhou inspector narrative. Hanji 394-1's parenting interpretation is recorded as the episode's family-education frame, not as a full causal proof of Jun's personality.
