# Plutarco Elias Calles

## Overview

Plutarco Elias Calles was a post-revolutionary Mexican general and president whose anti-clerical program forms the episode's political bridge between the Mexican Revolution and the modern national presentation of [[DayOfTheDead|Dia de Muertos]].

## Current Profile

The source presents Calles as an aggressive state-builder who reduced Catholic institutional power over education and politics. Those measures helped provoke the [[CristeroWar]], while the wider regime promoted Indigenous symbols as alternatives to Hispanic and Catholic cultural authority. In this account, coercion against the Church and cultural nationalism belong to the same post-revolutionary project, though Calles did not single-handedly create the festival's later form.

## Key Characteristics

- Revolutionary general who became president in the late 1920s.
- Central actor in the post-revolutionary state's anti-clerical campaign.
- Associated with restrictions on clerical education and political activity.
- Political antagonist in the conflict that became the [[CristeroWar]].
- Part of the institutional setting for [[MexicanPostRevolutionaryCulturalNationalism]].

## Evidence

- Anti-clerical state-building: [[264-mexico-day-of-the-dead-glt9022174156]] says Calles stripped the Church of educational power and barred priests from politics.
- Armed backlash: [[264-mexico-day-of-the-dead-glt9022174156]] links his program to the rural Christian uprising known as the [[CristeroWar]].
- Cultural setting: [[264-mexico-day-of-the-dead-glt9022174156]] places the regime's promotion of Indigenous identity alongside its rejection of Catholic and European cultural authority.

## Qualifications

The episode compresses a long presidency, the institutional history of the post-revolutionary regime, and a complex church-state war into background for the festival. Its claim that Calles founded the party that then ruled for 71 years requires chronological nuance about the party's later names and transformations.

## What Changed

- Created a bounded profile connecting anti-clerical state-building to the episode's account of cultural reinvention.

## Relationships

- [[CristeroWar]] - armed conflict intensified by Calles-era anti-clerical enforcement.
- [[LazaroCardenas]] - later post-revolutionary president associated with stronger state promotion of the festival.
- [[MexicanPostRevolutionaryCulturalNationalism]] - cultural program operating beside the regime's institutional secularization.
- [[DayOfTheDead]] - older observance whose modern national framing developed within this political environment.
