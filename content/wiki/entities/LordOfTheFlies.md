# Lord of the Flies / 《蝇王》

## Overview
[[LordOfTheFlies|Lord of the Flies / 《蝇王》]] is the [[WilliamGolding|William Golding / 威廉·戈尔丁]] novel discussed in [[vol-351-yingwang-21shiji-de-huangdao-gushi-haineng-zenme-kai-naodong-1983-nuojiang-dezhu-gkwriueodap1a3taaattkgol]].

## Current Profile
The episode presents the novel as a child-island thought experiment that becomes a severe test of civilization. A group of boys initially build rule, assembly, speech order, signal fire, and shelter around [[RalphLordOfTheFlies|Ralph]] and the conch, but [[JackMerridew|Jack]]'s hunting faction, the rumor of the beast, ritual fear, and escalating violence destroy that order. The final naval rescue does not restore innocence; it exposes the island as a miniature of the adult war world.

## Key Characteristics
- It turns a survival premise into [[CivilizationFragilityAllegory]] rather than a practical castaway adventure.
- It inverts [[TheCoralIsland|The Coral Island]] by replacing colonial adventure optimism with group fracture and moral collapse.
- Its characters carry symbolic pressures around rule, reason, power, truth, and violence.
- Its key objects, especially the conch, Piggy's glasses, masks, and fire, make social order materially visible.
- Its ending is ironic because rescue from children's violence comes from an adult military world also organized around war.

- Inversion: [[vol-351-yingwang-21shiji-de-huangdao-gushi-haineng-zenme-kai-naodong-1983-nuojiang-dezhu-gkwriueodap1a3taaattkgol]] frames the novel as a reversal of the cooperative child-adventure model in The Coral Island.
- Symbolic cast: [[vol-351-yingwang-21shiji-de-huangdao-gushi-haineng-zenme-kai-naodong-1983-nuojiang-dezhu-gkwriueodap1a3taaattkgol]] reads Ralph, Piggy, Jack, Simon, and Roger as carriers of rule, reason, power, truth, and violence.
- Material symbols: [[vol-351-yingwang-21shiji-de-huangdao-gushi-haineng-zenme-kai-naodong-1983-nuojiang-dezhu-gkwriueodap1a3taaattkgol]] tracks the conch, glasses, fire, mask, and pig head as objects through which order or disorder takes form.
