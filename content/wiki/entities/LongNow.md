# Long Now

## Overview
Long Now is the host and institutional context for sources that make distant consequences perceptible through AI critique, temporal politics, planetary coordination, design, ecology, governance, science, public art, and interdependence.

## Current Profile
Across the complete bounded source set, Long Now is less a single doctrine than a venue for practices that make long horizons thinkable and governable. Its sources expose AI's material empire, question modern clock discipline, defend planetary optionality, visualize slow progress, follow memory through oceans, redesign ownership, measure neural geometry, and stage deep-time public experiments. The Thurston–Agüera y Arcas conversation adds a relational layer: long-term thinking also depends on how humans understand themselves as differentiated participants inside biological, technological, political, and ecological networks. The current synthesis is that Long Now assembles multiple tools for seeing dependency, consequence, and responsibility without forcing them into one theory.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames long-term thinking as material, political, ecological, and relational rather than only philosophical.
- Hosts productive disputes over mastery, progress, ownership, measurement, and the proper unit of responsibility.
- Uses design, beauty, participation, and public experiments to make slow or distant change perceivable.
- Connects environmental and multispecies memory to responsibility across generations.
- Treats governance and ownership design as necessary for sustaining missions under financial and technological pressure.
- Extends long-term inquiry into intelligence, consciousness, collective cognition, and human-machine relationships.
- Preserves difference and qualification rather than presenting interdependence or long horizons as automatically beneficial.

## Qualifications
The Long Now branch contains tensions rather than a unified answer. [[BayoAkomolafe]] warns that alternative clocks can reproduce mastery; [[StefanSagmeister]] defends long-run progress evidence without denying present danger; [[JonathanKeats]] offers symbolic experiments rather than policy; [[NinaMiolane]] separates measurable neural structure from consciousness claims; and the interdependence conversation warns that cooperation can produce cartels or cancer-like local success. These differences are part of the profile and should not be flattened into a single institutional position.

## What Changed
- Added relational interdependence as a ninth route for making long-horizon responsibility concrete.
- Extended the profile from distant consequences to the habits, power relations, and boundaries formed inside present relationships.
- Added an explicit warning that interdependence and cooperation require scale-sensitive governance rather than automatic approval.
