## Overview
Netscape was the browser company that packaged access to the early internet for ordinary computer users and became an ecosystem center for the first commercial internet startup wave.

## Current Profile
Across the two bounded sources, Netscape is both a product-interface breakthrough and a platform signal. [[MarkAndreessen|Marc Andreessen]] describes it as bundling the components consumers needed to reach an internet that most incumbents did not yet expect ordinary people to use. [[RonConway]] remembers the company as a gravitational center whose browser, public validation, talent, and open-source practices helped make internet startups legible and cheaper to build. Netscape did not create the underlying internet, and its role belongs inside a wider adoption stack including research networks, [[AOL]], computer integration, telecommunications, and later broadband.

## Key Characteristics
- Consumer browser company that reduced setup and usability friction for internet access.
- Commercialization layer built on existing protocols, research networks, and earlier browser work.
- Platform hub that concentrated engineers, founders, investor attention, and downstream company ideas.
- Category signal that helped make internet software publicly and financially legible.
- Contributor to open-source practices that lowered infrastructure costs for later startups.

## Evidence
- Consumer adoption role: [[93-silicon-valley-part-1-glt5734449332]] has Andreessen describe Netscape as packaging the pieces needed for ordinary consumers to use the internet.
- Ecosystem role: [[tsr-s4-ronconway-v4-tsr-s4-ronconway-v4]] has Conway describe Netscape as the company he wanted proximity to even after its investment round was full.
- Startup-cost role: [[tsr-s4-ronconway-v4-tsr-s4-ronconway-v4]] connects Netscape engineers and open-source software to a sharp decline in the capital needed to start internet companies.

## Qualifications
Both sources are retrospective participant accounts. They foreground browser usability and Silicon Valley investment while compressing earlier browsers, standards work, telecommunications policy, infrastructure, geographic inequality, and the roles of other firms and research institutions. Netscape made the internet easier to reach; it did not invent the network or single-handedly cause mass adoption.
