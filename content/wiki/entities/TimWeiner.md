# Tim Weiner

## Overview

Tim Weiner is an investigative journalist represented in the wiki through his reporting on the [[CIA]] and the fatal disclosure dispute reconstructed in [[the-road-to-damascus-the-road-to-damascus|The Road to Damascus]].

## Current Profile

The episode presents Weiner as treating a secret intelligence service as a public institution that still requires aggressive reporting. After a CIA insider alerted him to an undisclosed relationship with a former terrorist, he says he corroborated the account through multiple independent sources, including a member of Congress and a diplomat. He then negotiated omissions with agency officials but retained the asset's role in finding [[CarlosTheJackal|Carlos the Jackal]], judging it necessary to the public-interest story.

His position in the later dispute is institutional as well as personal. He rejects the claim that the newspaper killed the asset and argues that the CIA created the danger by recruiting him without proper consultation and spreading the information through official disclosures. The episode accepts the democratic value of his scrutiny while leaving open whether the retained operational detail made the source identifiable.

## Key Characteristics

- Investigative reporter focused on secret state power and institutional accountability.
- Uses multi-source corroboration before publishing sensitive allegations.
- Accepts negotiation and selective omission without granting government a publication veto.
- Treats the asset's role in finding Carlos as material to public understanding of the case.
- Disputes a simple causal claim that publication alone caused the asset's death.

## Evidence

### Reporting method

- [[the-road-to-damascus-the-road-to-damascus]] describes Weiner confirming the story through several independent sources before publication.

### Negotiated restraint

- [[the-road-to-damascus-the-road-to-damascus]] says he withheld nationality, affiliation, attack locations, and other details after discussions with CIA officials.

### Accountability judgment

- [[the-road-to-damascus-the-road-to-damascus]] records his refusal to remove the asset entirely and his later argument that CIA procedure and official dissemination created the underlying danger.

## Qualifications

The page reflects one episode's reconstruction rather than Weiner's full career or the complete editorial record. The supplied source does not include his notes, source communications, the full government warning, the newspaper's deliberations, or classified evidence about the killing. Corroboration can establish that a relationship existed without settling whether every identifying detail should be published.
