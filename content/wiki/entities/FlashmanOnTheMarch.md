# Flashman on the March

## Overview

Flashman on the March, George MacDonald Fraser's novel about the 1868 [[AbyssinianExpedition1868|Abyssinian expedition]], enters the wiki through [[706-queen-victorias-revenge-storming-the-citadel-part-2-glt3285474568]] as the episode's opening hook rather than as historical evidence.

## Current Profile

The episode opens with a dramatised reading in which Flashman, in chains, meets a staggeringly drunk [[TeodrosII]]. The hosts then immediately separate the novel from the record: Flashman is a fictional narrator, his secret mission is invented, and the emperor, his mountain lair, the British expedition and the hostage crisis around them are documented history. The book is described as deeply rooted in historical records without being a work of history.

The wiki therefore keeps the novel as a narrative and memorial device attached to the campaign. It explains why listeners arrive at [[Magdala]] through a comic imperial adventure, and it marks the boundary the episode itself polices between a fiction that borrows the past and the sources that reconstruct it.

## Key Characteristics

- It is a historical novel whose narrator is invented but whose setting is documented.
- It gives the episode its melodramatic and comic framing, including the drunk-emperor meeting.
- The source treats it as an entry point that must be explicitly quarantined from evidence.
- It connects the campaign branch to the wider Flashman series and its Victorian-imperial style.

## Qualifications

The page records the novel only as the episode's hook, so it does not cover Fraser's wider Flashman series, the book's plot beyond the opening scene, its reception, or the accuracy of its historical apparatus. Because the wiki's own contract forbids drawing historical claims from fiction, nothing in this page should be used as evidence for the campaign itself.
