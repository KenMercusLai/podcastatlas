# Bangladesh

## Overview

Bangladesh appears as a garment-industrialization case, a fragile democratic-transition case, the neighboring state in a coercive migration and border-enforcement dispute with [[India]], and a warning about politically connected banking losses reversing development gains.

## Current Profile

The development branch shows successful [[TextileKnowledgeTransfer]] from [[SouthKorea|South Korea]] producing employment and export capacity without guaranteeing institutional upgrading or workplace safety. The election branch presents the first genuinely competitive national vote since 2008 after [[SheikhHasina]] and the [[AwamiLeague]] lose power, while keeping party exclusion and constitutional legitimacy unresolved.

The border branch adds external pressure. Indian pushbacks, stronger Bangladeshi patrols, border deaths, hostile political language, and disputed citizenship checks make bilateral respect and lawful return procedures part of Bangladesh's wider relationship with India.

The banking branch adds a domestic transmission mechanism. Connected lending under [[SheikhHasina]], delayed recognition of bad loans, and selective payment holidays weaken trust and bank capital; frozen credit then transmits those governance failures into slower growth and renewed poverty. Recapitalization may restore lending, but an episode-reported cost near 10% of GDP makes loss allocation, external conditions, subsidy reform, and protest inseparable.

## Key Characteristics

- Built a major garment export industry through people-centered knowledge transfer and quota incentives.
- Remains institutionally constrained despite industrial success.
- Is navigating competitive elections after incumbent removal and interim rule.
- Faces a legitimacy problem when a large party and its voters are excluded from the immediate transition.
- Has strained relations with India over migration rhetoric, pushbacks, border force, and citizenship disputes.
- Faces a banking crisis in which connected lending, bad-loan recognition, and weak capital restrict productive credit.
- Must reconcile financial reform and recapitalization with poverty, subsidy politics, protest, and transition legitimacy.

## Evidence

- Industrialization: [[getting-entrepreneurial-in-korea-summer-school]] follows Daewoo training, Desh Garments, export discipline, and the Rana Plaza qualification.
- Democratic transition: [[dhaka-matters-an-election-for-bangladesh-698c5a3afeb59e13a3b8a94d]] describes a competitive vote, constitutional referendum, economic stabilization, party rivalry, and unresolved Awami League inclusion.
- Border relations: [[national-front-runner-who-can-beat-le-pen-6ab3960493749e4c0ba24e60]] reports deaths at the border, informal Indian pushbacks, stronger Bangladeshi patrols, and demands for dignity in bilateral relations.
- Banking governance: [[strait-draw-irans-hot-war-cools-a9cfdd53e637fbdfdb684cf03b8222f4]] attributes a large bad-loan stock to politically connected lending and says newer valuations place non-performing loans near 30% rather than the earlier reported 10%.
- Credit and reform: [[strait-draw-irans-hot-war-cools-a9cfdd53e637fbdfdb684cf03b8222f4]] links impaired bank lending to sharply slower growth and renewed poverty, while presenting external recapitalization as costly and politically difficult.

## Qualifications

The four sources address separate domains and do not form a comprehensive country account. Border death counts, undocumented-migrant estimates, and official motives remain source-scoped; the border episode provides more detail on Indian enforcement than on Bangladesh's own practices. The banking episode does not supply bank-level data, audited loss estimates, or lender terms, so its bad-loan share, growth figures, recapitalization cost, and causal attribution remain episode-reported.

## What Changed

- Added connected lending and delayed bad-loan recognition as a threat to earlier development gains.
- Added the reform tradeoff between recapitalizing banks, restoring credit, imposing external conditions, and sustaining political legitimacy.

## Relationships

- [[India]] - neighboring state and partner-rival across migration, border security, and regional politics.
- [[WestBengal]] - Indian border state sharing Bengali linguistic and historical ties.
- [[IdentityProxiedBorderEnforcement]] - mechanism exposing Bengali-speaking Muslims and citizens to mistaken foreignness.
- [[TextileKnowledgeTransfer]] - development mechanism behind the garment branch.
- [[DemocraticTransitionElection]] - political framework for the competitive-vote branch.
- [[BangladeshNationalistParty]] - leading election actor in the transition source.
- [[AwamiLeague]] - displaced incumbent party whose exclusion creates a legitimacy problem.
- [[PoliticallyConnectedBankingCrisis]] - mechanism linking favored loans and hidden losses to credit scarcity and development reversal.
- [[BankingLossAllocationPolitics]] - framework for deciding who absorbs the cost of recapitalization and reform.
