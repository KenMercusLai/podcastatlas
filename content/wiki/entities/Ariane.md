# Ariane

## Overview
Ariane is Europe's launch program and the episode's principal example of how multinational work-sharing can preserve participation while slowing delivery and increasing cost.

## Current Profile
The source treats Ariane as evidence that European space dependence is organizational as well as technical. Dividing work among participating countries contributed to delay and higher cost, while the reported comparison of four Ariane launches with 165 heavy-lift Falcon launches in the previous year demonstrates a large cadence gap.

## Key Characteristics
- European multinational rocket and launch program.
- Strategic route to less dependence on American launch providers.
- Constrained by delay, cost, and fragmented industrial work-sharing.
- Far behind SpaceX in the episode's source-dated launch-cadence comparison.

## Evidence
- Organizational constraint: [[songs-of-disenchantment-the-sorrow-of-nigeria-6275426999307c5f9d5c7073a5b16e0d]] attributes some delay and cost escalation to division of work among participating countries.
- Cadence gap: [[songs-of-disenchantment-the-sorrow-of-nigeria-6275426999307c5f9d5c7073a5b16e0d]] contrasts four Ariane launches with 165 heavy-lift Falcon launches in the comparison year.

## Qualifications
The launch comparison is episode-reported and does not normalize vehicle class, mission type, payload, reliability, public-service obligations, or accounting. Multinational work-sharing can carry political and industrial benefits even when it reduces speed.

## What Changed
- Created Ariane as the launch-capacity and procurement-fragmentation case in Europe's autonomy problem.
