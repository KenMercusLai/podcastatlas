# 《第二天金钱进化论》 / Second-Day Money Evolution

## Overview
《第二天金钱进化论》 is [[FangYanWealth|方言]]'s book about how people relate to money after getting it, discussed across two bounded podcast interviews.

## Current Profile
The book's wiki profile is source-limited to podcast discussions rather than the text itself. Its central contrast is between earning money and surviving the life system money creates. It treats sudden wealth, entrepreneurship, stock-option gains, real-estate windfalls, family strain, social-circle change, and investment temptation as one linked "second day" problem.

The positive answer is not only frugality. It asks the newly wealthy or temporarily enriched person to define an endpoint, control desire, preserve family and judgment, avoid leverage and social traps, and turn income into assets that can buy time. E251 adds that keeping the first pool of capital requires continuing productive work, honest limits on company understanding, low recurring status costs, and mechanisms strong enough to survive market temptation.

## Key Characteristics
- It focuses on what happens after obtaining money rather than on wealth acquisition tactics.
- It frames money as a tool that can improve life or amplify greed, vanity, comparison, and bad judgment.
- It uses bankruptcy, luxury-property, banking, entrepreneurship, and family cases to make re-poverty risk concrete.
- It treats reading, plain living, and value-investing discipline as ways to survive the "second day."
- It treats capital preservation as a behavior-and-lifestyle system, not merely an asset-allocation choice.
- It defines mature wealth around passive income, controlled spending, family stability, and time freedom.

## Qualifications
The wiki has not read the book directly; this page records it as represented in two podcast episodes. Claims about the book's scope may therefore reflect interview emphasis rather than the complete published argument.

## What Changed
- Extended the book's profile from post-windfall risk to investment readiness, self-binding, and preservation of the first pool of capital.
