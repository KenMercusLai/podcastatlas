## Overview

郅支单于 / Zhizhi Chanyu enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-570-xinyang-buke-queshi-yingxiong-burong-dihui-lrrcbw5rrurytgjxasbp12buf82l|Hanji 570]] as the Xiongnu ruler killed by [[ChenTangWesternHan|陈汤]] and [[GanYanshouWesternHan|甘延寿]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-474-guji-kangkai-fusi-wo-weihe-renwei-ta-yuchun-lp-07zvaxr21dt2mtva2ndp6b9by|Hanji 474]] now supplies the earlier envoy-killing and westward-alliance layer: after Han releases his hostage son, Zhizhi Chanyu kills [[GuJiWesternHan|谷吉]]'s escort party, flees west under Han retaliation risk, accepts [[Kangju|康居]]'s invitation, and uses Kangju ties against [[Wusun|乌孙]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-489-weizao-shengzhi-shadi-hanjiang-chentang-you-duo-kuang-ligjvbvujg4nujinr1k39aiv28lz|Hanji 489]] supplies the pre-battle threat layer: Zhizhi Chanyu has killed Han envoy Gu Ji's party, retreats to Kangju, abuses Kangju royal and commoner allies, coerces city construction, pressures nearby states, and pretends submission to manage Han retaliation risk. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-490-dahan-zhanlang-chentang-weizao-shengzhi-li-qigong-lnmkvvmwppn6w3-prhfi6nyoqina|Hanji 490]] supplies the battle prequel: Zhizhi Chanyu receives Han envoys before the siege, probes their purpose, is pushed by the Han claim that they have come to escort a submitted chanyu to Chang'an, and is then defeated after crossbow pressure, fire, failed breakouts, and fighting near the palace. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-491-zhongguo-waijiao-zui-qiangying-de-shike-jing-shi-zai-xihan-lpgajp_vky9dx7jvnoahaxdfjflk|Hanji 491]] supplies the immediate public aftermath: his head is sent to Chang'an, the commanders ask that it be displayed as a warning, and [[HanYuandi|汉元帝]] orders a ten-day display before burial. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-496-qiong-pa-le-jiemi-tanguan-zouxiang-tanfu-de-xinli-youyin-lnskbcqvjhtpfi4ikq44kpbbfnol|Hanji 496]] adds the immediate reward-dispute layer: [[LiuXiang|刘向]] argues that killing Zhizhi Chanyu repaired Han prestige and affected frontier diplomacy enough that the commanders' faults should not block recognition.

## Current Profile

The sources present Zhizhi Chanyu as a defiant ruler whose death restored Han prestige and ended a humiliation burden in memorial rhetoric. Hanji 474 gives the earliest current causal layer: his demand for the hostage son's return leads to Gu Ji's fatal escort mission, and his subsequent westward move into Kangju makes him both fugitive and dangerous ally. Hanji 489 gives the threat case before battle: envoy-killing, Kangju coercion, surrounding-state pressure, and fake submission make him a target whose danger is both violent and diplomatic. Hanji 490 adds the closest battle view: he is not only a displayed head after the fact, but the target whose fortified position, Xiongnu cavalry, and Kangju support are overcome through Han intelligence, crossbow discipline, fire, and a final palace assault. Hanji 491 adds that the death is turned into state communication: the head display, court announcement, amnesty, and victory banquet make him the object through which Han advertises deterrence across distance. His role is therefore mostly relational: he is the opponent whose defeat made Chen Tang and Gan Yanshou's service extraordinary enough that both the initial reward dispute and later under-reward/disgrace argument became politically meaningful.

The source summary uses the form "置之单于"; this page normalizes the route to the historically standard 郅支单于 while preserving the exact naming issue as a source-scoped qualification.

## Key Characteristics

- Xiongnu ruler used in the source as the defeated opponent in a major Western Han frontier victory.
- Presented as a brutal and evasive threat because his hostage-return demand, killing of Gu Ji's escort party, Kangju alliance, coercion, and feigned submission turn diplomacy into retaliation pressure.
- Dangerous ally for Kangju, welcomed against Wusun but pursuing his own recovery, revenge, and regional pressure.
- Fortified opponent whose defeat depends on intelligence, assault timing, and overcoming Kangju-linked support.
- Defeated opponent whose head display makes the victory a public warning in Chang'an.
- His killing anchors Liu Xiang's and Geng Yu's arguments that large frontier merit should receive proportionate recognition despite later or smaller faults.
- Functions as a prestige and deterrence marker in the source set rather than as a fully developed biography.

## Qualifications

This page is source-bounded and does not reconstruct Zhizhi Chanyu's full career, Xiongnu succession politics, western movement, or the complete campaign record. Hanji 474 and Hanji 489 give podcast-summary threat dossiers rather than full independent accounts of Gu Ji's mission, Kangju politics, or Zhizhi Chanyu's diplomacy. Hanji 490 gives a podcast-summary battle sequence rather than an independently verified military reconstruction. Hanji 491 uses the defeat as victory announcement and deterrence theater; Hanji 496 and Hanji 570 use the defeat primarily for Han court reward and memory arguments. The route normalization should be revisited if a future source preserves a genuinely distinct "置之单于" identity.
