## Overview

Shockley Semiconductor Laboratory was William Shockley's Mountain View semiconductor venture, presented in the episode as a talent-concentration point whose internal breakdown helped generate Silicon Valley's company genealogy.

## Current Profile

[[silicon-valley-on-the-couch]] treats the laboratory's location and organizational dynamics as equally consequential. Shockley's scientific reputation brought strong researchers to a region that was not yet the obvious center of American electronics, and the laboratory pursued silicon devices while germanium remained common. His controlling and paranoid management then drove eight leading employees away together.

The laboratory therefore matters less as a successful standalone business than as an unstable bridge between recruited technical talent and later regional firms. Calling it the birthplace of Silicon Valley is useful for genealogy but should not erase public research, universities, finance, military demand, migration, infrastructure, and other institutions in [[SiliconValleyInnovationSystem]].

## Key Characteristics

- Established in Mountain View in 1956 under William Shockley's leadership.
- Recruited nationally through Shockley's scientific prestige and personal attention.
- Focused on silicon semiconductor devices.
- Used extensive psychological screening for prospective employees.
- Suffered severe trust and management failures.
- Became an origin node for later semiconductor companies through employee departure.

## Evidence

- Location and recruitment - [[silicon-valley-on-the-couch]] contrasts Mountain View with Northeastern electronics centers and says Shockley persuaded promising scientists to relocate before commercial plans were settled.
- Technical focus - [[silicon-valley-on-the-couch]] describes the laboratory's silicon work against a field still using substantial germanium.
- Trust failure - [[silicon-valley-on-the-couch]] recounts psychological examinations, sabotage suspicion after a broken thumbtack, and attempted lie-detector testing.
- Regional diffusion - [[silicon-valley-on-the-couch]] connects the [[TraitorousEight]] departure and their decision to remain locally to later company formation.

## Qualifications

The “birthplace” label compresses a multi-institutional regional history into one site. The source supports the laboratory as an important genealogical node but does not establish that it alone caused Silicon Valley or quantify the relative weight of Stanford, military research, finance, housing decisions, prior California electronics, and national semiconductor demand.
