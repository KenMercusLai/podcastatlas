# George Loewenstein

## Overview
George Loewenstein is a psychologist represented here through a [[HiddenBrain]] episode on state-dependent self-prediction, visceral drives, decision safeguards, and the [[HotColdEmpathyGap]].

## Current Profile
Loewenstein's account treats calm and emotionally or physiologically intense states as partially inaccessible to one another. A person may sincerely form a plan while calm yet underestimate how pain, hunger, sexual arousal, fear, anger, or craving will reorganize attention and motivation; once the state passes, the same person may again underestimate it and judge the earlier behavior as inexplicable.

His practical emphasis is institutional as well as personal. Rehearsal can make a response easier to retrieve, accessible safeguards can reduce reliance on foresight, and delay can interrupt dangerous impulsive action. These interventions aim to help a hot-state person enact priorities endorsed while calm rather than treating cold-state intention as sufficient.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames self-prediction as dependent on current emotional and physiological state.
- Distinguishes prospective forecasting error from retrospective failure to understand past conduct.
- Uses visceral states including pain, hunger, arousal, anger, fear, and addiction craving to explain motivational change.
- Argues that experience alone may not preserve usable access to a past hot state.
- Connects repeated practice and environmental design to safer action under pressure.
- Uses the framework to encourage humility without erasing responsibility.

## Qualifications
This profile is based on one supplied structured episode summary, not a complete biography, bibliography, transcript, or independent review of Loewenstein's studies. Experimental responses and policy examples do not establish that every hot state has the same mechanism or that state dependence determines conduct. Explanation does not erase consent, accountability, individual variation, or structural power.
