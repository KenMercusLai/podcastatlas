# Au Bon Pain

## Overview
Au Bon Pain is a bakery-cafe company that [[RonShaich|Ron Shaich]] first joined as a licensee, then combined with his Cookie Jar business and rebuilt. In the bounded account, it supplied both the operating problems and customer behavior from which an early fast-casual format emerged.

## Current Profile
Shaich added Au Bon Pain baked goods to improve the Cookie Jar's weak morning trade, then proposed combining his profitable store with Au Bon Pain's three struggling locations. The inherited operation lacked reliable delivery, billing, maintenance, equipment, and production processes, making the early company as much a repair project as a product opportunity.

Observation turned bread into a platform rather than an endpoint. After seeing customers add meat and cheese to baguettes, the company opened a Copley Place bakery cafe in 1985 with sandwiches and soups. Au Bon Pain later acquired St. Louis Bread Company, but Shaich ultimately sold the larger legacy chain and other divisions so the corporation could concentrate on what became Panera.

## Key Characteristics
- Bakery business joined with Shaich's profitable cookie shop through a turnaround-oriented combination.
- Early operation constrained by weak equipment, maintenance, production, delivery, and billing systems.
- Platform for developing a bakery-cafe format from observed customer meal assembly.
- Urban counterpart to the suburban St. Louis Bread Company concept.
- Successful legacy division later divested to concentrate resources on Panera.
