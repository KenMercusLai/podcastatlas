# Gyirong Port

## Overview
Gyirong Port is the China-[[Nepal]] road crossing at the center of a long-used trans-Himalayan route through a steep, hazard-prone valley. The source presents it as trade infrastructure, a border-governance instrument, and a concentrated disaster-exposure site rather than as a neutral transport node.

## Current Profile
The route historically linked Tibetan salt, wool, and raw materials with rice and other goods from Nepal and India, alongside close cultural and seasonal family movement. Modern Gyirong became more important after 2014 landslides and the 2015 Nepal earthquake repeatedly cut the Zhangmu corridor. China's subsequent international-port upgrade, third-country access, trade zone, and support for Timure facilities made the crossing part of a wider connectivity strategy. Its strategic value now coexists with recurrent slope, flood, and ice-rock hazards that have destroyed or interrupted infrastructure in the same narrow valley.

## Key Characteristics
- A more-than-millennial Himalayan trade route adapted to modern road freight and border administration.
- A commercial crossing that also supports Chinese control of movement along Tibet's southern border.
- An alternative corridor through which Nepal seeks to reduce transport and fuel dependence on India.
- A node shaped by Chinese-backed port, road, logistics, power, and proposed rail investment.
- A concentrated exposure site where customs buildings, bridges, settlements, hydropower, and roads share one hazard field.
- A corridor whose nominally bilateral location may still contain unequal practical control over opening and passage.

## Qualifications
The current profile comes from one reported episode rather than port records, engineering studies, or a bilateral institutional history. Local control perceptions, construction losses, political motives, and reconstruction expectations remain source-scoped. The episode also distinguishes recurrent corridor exposure from proof that the precise August 2026 ice-rock collapse could have been predicted.
