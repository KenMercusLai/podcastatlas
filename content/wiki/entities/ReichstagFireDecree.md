# Reichstag Fire Decree

## Overview

The Reichstag Fire Decree was the 28 February 1933 emergency measure that suspended core civil liberties and enabled the Nazi-led national government to repress opponents and intervene in Germany's states after the [[ReichstagFire1933|Reichstag fire]].

## Current Profile

The episode presents the decree as the bridge from a crisis narrative to durable coercive power. It removed protections for press, assembly, association, privacy, and freedom from arrest without trial while widening the central government's authority over federal states.

Although issued under [[PaulVonHindenburg|Hindenburg's]] presidential authority, the practical beneficiary was Hitler's cabinet. Police arrests, paramilitary intimidation, and state takeovers show why the decree cannot be understood as a neutral legal response separated from organized coercion.

## Key Characteristics

- It followed the Reichstag fire by one day.
- It suspended press, assembly, association, privacy, and due-process protections.
- It supported mass detention of political opponents.
- It widened national intervention into German state government.
- It transferred practical emergency leverage from presidential restraint toward Hitler's cabinet.
