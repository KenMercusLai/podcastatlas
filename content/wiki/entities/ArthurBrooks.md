# Arthur Brooks

## Overview
Arthur Brooks appears in the wiki as the [[HubermanLab]] guest in [[the-art-of-true-happiness-dr-arthur-brooks-scim2290485637]], where he presents happiness as a practiced and morally oriented life rather than a mood state or escape from suffering.

## Current Profile
In the current source, [[ArthurBrooks]] frames happiness through [[HappinessMacronutrients|enjoyment, satisfaction, and meaning]]. His account repeatedly distinguishes animal impulse from moral aspiration: pleasure alone can narrow into addictive pursuit, while enjoyment includes people, memory, and alignment with the kind of person one wants to become. He treats satisfaction as the reward that follows struggle, and meaning as a lived answer to coherence, purpose, significance, faith, love, and service.

Brooks' profile is also explicitly practical and religious-philosophical. He uses exercise, marriage vows, leisure, retirement, addiction, teaching, the Camino de Santiago, Mass attendance, and the four idols of money, power, pleasure, and honor as diagnostic cases. The source presents him as warning ambitious or successful people that status and measurement can displace the forms of love that actually make a life good.

## Key Characteristics
- Presents happiness as a practice involving enjoyment, satisfaction, and meaning rather than constant positivity.
- Distinguishes pleasure from enjoyment by adding people, memory, conscious management, and moral aspiration.
- Treats suffering, frustration, and struggle as potentially internal to happiness rather than simply opposed to it.
- Uses public commitment, daily practice, faith, service, and teaching as tools for living according to deeper values.
- Diagnoses money, power, pleasure, and honor as tempting idols when they become ultimate goals.
- Places love, faith, family, friends, and work-as-service above external scoreboards and stranger admiration.

## Qualifications

Brooks' religious claims, Vedic life-stage references, right-hemisphere meaning language, addiction analogies, and self-assessment exercises are treated as source-scoped public philosophy and psychology rather than clinical, theological, or universal empirical conclusions.
