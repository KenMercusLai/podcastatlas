## Overview

皇甫规 / Huangfu Gui is a late-Han frontier official whose current wiki profile now has five source-bounded layers: 162 CE Qiang-front stabilization and corrupt-official impeachment, accusation and eunuch reward gatekeeping after that success, public-duty recommendation of [[ZhangHuanLateHan|张奂]], failed exit from a later frontier appointment, and shame at being exempted from Party Prohibition punishment. Across those layers, he is useful to the state but repeatedly exposed when merit, office, and moral reputation pass through hostile court channels.

## Current Profile

The current earliest layer is [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-856-lishi-gaosu-ni-ren-ruo-xinluan-zhenjiao-zi-luan-lo7m-9mz-pvstxtfgavrne4bszks|Hanji 856]]. Huangfu Gui enters a cut-off and epidemic-hit army in the Qiang campaign, visits the camps, and restores soldier morale before eastern Qiang envoys surrender. He then treats rebellion as a governance problem as well as a military problem by impeaching frontier officials accused of corruption, killing surrenderers, and incompetence; wider Qiang submission follows.

The next layer shows the same achievement failing to protect him from enemies who accuse him of bribing rebel groups into false surrender. In [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-857-xuehui-gongying-cai-shi-zhenzheng-de-yingjia-lh3zx6pw-4ib7owiz8c68bv-9htn|Hanji 857]], his memorial answers on two fronts: he denies personal enrichment and points to accounts, while also arguing that even paid pacification can be legitimate if it cheaply settles a large frontier threat. Emperor Huan recalls him to Luoyang as yilang, but the reward channel then becomes a second danger.

The reward-gatekeeping layer shows Huangfu Gui's principled refusal as politically costly. [[XuHuangLateHan|徐璜]] and [[ZuoGuanLateHan|左悺]] send intermediaries around his merit review to solicit gifts. Huangfu refuses to pay or apologize; subordinates temporarily calm the matter by making payments and apologies, but Huangfu himself is jailed, sentenced to hard labor, and only released after elite petitions and a general amnesty. The source therefore makes him an early case of [[MilitaryRewardEunuchGatekeeping|military reward eunuch gatekeeping]] before the later Zhang Huan reward case.

In the 163 CE frontier crisis covered by [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-859-gudai-haoren-weishenme-nan-dang-haoguan-llkyx18ssvserxncvrtkw7xfduad|Hanji 859]], Huan's court recalls Huangfu Gui toward the Duliao general role, but he argues that Zhang Huan is the stronger large-army commander and offers to serve below him. The court accepts that recommendation, making Zhang Huan Duliao general and Huangfu Gui zhonglangjiang. The same source says Zhang had been barred after the Liang Ji case and that Huangfu Gui defended him repeatedly, so the recommendation is part of a longer talent-rescue pattern rather than a one-off courtesy.

The later office-exit layer shows the opposite pressure. In [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-870-gudai-tizhinei-buganle-meiyou-tuilu-lpwbniu9ifpt6cp4ip9czqwsh5xd|Hanji 870]], Huangfu Gui receives a northern frontier office he does not want, submits repeated memorials claiming illness, and still cannot escape the assignment. His profile therefore marks a system where an official can yield rank for competence in one setting yet fail to control his own exit in another.

The Party Prohibition layer turns punishment into moral evidence. In [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-875-li-dapu-weihe-ta-yi-ruyu-wei-rong-lgffkjk-0yu-ehfsw59anoych-ld|Hanji 875]], Huangfu Gui narrowly avoids proscription through eunuch kinship and complains that missing the shared calamity leaves him ashamed beside fellow gentlemen who suffer for principle. His current profile combines capable frontier service, refusal of corrupt reward brokerage, talent-centered recommendation, constrained office duty, and the moral weight attached to persecution.

## Key Characteristics

- Late-Han frontier official who first stabilizes his own diseased and cut-off army before Qiang surrender follows.
- Treats frontier rebellion as a personnel and governance failure by impeaching corrupt, violent, and incompetent officials.
- Has pacification work recoded as bribery or false surrender, then answers with accounts, poverty claims, and frontier precedent.
- Refuses eunuch-linked gift and apology pressure even when the merit-review channel becomes personally dangerous.
- Repeatedly defends Zhang Huan and recommends him as the better commander during a frontier emergency.
- Attempts to avoid a later unwanted frontier posting through illness memorials but cannot secure exit.
- Treats escape from Party Prohibition as a source of shame because it separates him from persecuted upright officials.

## Qualifications

The page remains source-bounded to the podcast summaries in its source list and does not reconstruct Huangfu Gui's full career. Transcript forms including "黄腐归", "黄府规", and "黄福贵" are normalized to 皇甫规 because the office sequence, Zhang Huan connection, frontier role, and Huan-era setting match the established late-Han actor. Hanji 856's exact Qiang subgroup details and Hanji 857's accounting figures are preserved as source claims, not independently audited military or fiscal history.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 856 as the earlier Qiang-front success behind Hanji 857's accusation layer.
- Reframed Huangfu Gui's pacification as camp stabilization plus anti-corruption personnel repair, not only later fiscal self-defense.
- Kept Huangfu Gui as a case where real frontier merit can remain unsafe when reward and accusation channels are eunuch-gated.
- Integrated the earlier reward-gatekeeping case with the later Zhang Huan recommendation, failed-exit, and Party Prohibition shame layers.
