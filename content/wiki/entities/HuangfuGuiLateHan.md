## Overview

皇甫规 / Huangfu Gui is a late-Han frontier official whose current wiki profile has two source-bounded layers. Hanji 870 shows him trying and failing to exit a renewed Duliao general appointment, while Hanji 875 places him in the first Party Prohibition reputation field as a western-region notable who regards exclusion from prison as shameful.

## Current Profile

Huangfu Gui's profile is now more than a Party Prohibition honor case. Hanji 870 presents him as a needed frontier official whom the court reappoints after summoning [[ZhangHuanLateHan|张奂]] back to manage agricultural finance during famine. Huangfu Gui claims illness, then engineers a jurisdictional violation so [[HuFangLateHan|胡方]] might impeach him for leaving camp. Hu Fang sees through the tactic and refuses to help him escape service.

Hanji 875 then shows the same kind of public-role binding from another angle. During the first Party Prohibition, Huangfu Gui treats safety as dishonor because famous worthy men are imprisoned while he remains outside. He self-reports association by saying he recommended Zhang Huan and was defended by Taixue students. The court ignores this attempt too, so both sources show failed self-exposure: one intended to leave office, the other to share punishment.

## Key Characteristics

- Late-Han frontier official identified as Duliao general in the bounded sources.
- Attempts to avoid an unwanted frontier appointment through illness and manufactured impeachment.
- Has enough recognized capacity that another official refuses to validate his exit tactic.
- Treats not being imprisoned in the Party Prohibition case as shame rather than safety.
- Self-reports association with party figures through recommendation and student-defense ties.
- Functions as a bridge between office-duty exit constraint and punishment-as-moral-credential logic.

## Qualifications

The current page is still episode-bounded and should not be treated as a full biography of Huangfu Gui's campaigns or offices. Hanji 870 does not settle whether his attempted exit reflects fatigue, prudence, avoidance, or political self-protection. Hanji 875 renders him through a Party Prohibition reputation frame rather than through operational command. Transcript variants such as "黄福贵" are normalized to 皇甫规 because the Duliao-general and late-Han context match the established historical actor.
