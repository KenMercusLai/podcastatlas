## Overview

皇甫规 / Huangfu Gui is a late-Han frontier official whose current wiki profile is source-bounded to Hanji 875. The episode places him in the first Party Prohibition reputation field as a western-region notable and Duliao general who regards exclusion from imprisonment as shameful because the prisoners are famous worthy men.

## Current Profile

Huangfu Gui's current profile is narrow but useful. He does not appear here as a campaign commander in detail; he appears as an actor who reads Party Prohibition punishment as evidence of moral belonging. In Hanji 875, he self-reports by saying that he had recommended Zhang Huan and that Taixue students had defended him, so if those networks are criminal, he should also be liable.

The court does not pursue him, which matters as much as the self-report. The source shows that repression can invert ordinary incentive: punishment may become a credential for belonging to the moral community, while non-punishment can feel like exclusion from recognized名节.

## Key Characteristics

- Late-Han frontier official identified in the source as Duliao general and western-region notable.
- Treats not being imprisoned in the Party Prohibition case as shame rather than safety.
- Self-reports association with party figures through recommendation and student-defense ties.
- Functions as an early example of punishment becoming moral credential under captured politics.
- Remains source-bounded; the current wiki does not reconstruct his full frontier career.

## Qualifications

Hanji 875 is brief and does not provide Huangfu Gui's complete offices, campaigns, or later career. Earlier wiki source notes mention Huangfu Gui source-scopedly beside Zhang Huan in frontier-policy contrasts, but this page only makes canonical claims from the current source's explicit episode account. The transcript renders him as "黄福贵"; this page normalizes to 皇甫规 because the Duliao-general and late-Han Party Prohibition context match the established historical actor.
