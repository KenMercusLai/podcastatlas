# St Bartholomew's Day Massacre

[[692-elizabeth-i-vs-the-catholics-a-massacre-in-paris-part-2-glt4665799025]] makes the massacre the origin point for the Elizabethan sequence's security logic. The episode begins from [[ChristopherMarlowe]]'s theatrical afterlife, then follows [[GaspardColigny]]'s murder by men tied to [[HenryDukeOfGuise|Henry, Duke of Guise]], the Louvre killings, the Paris bell signal, wider neighborhood violence, and Catholic as well as Protestant testimony about the horror.

The same source stresses both atrocity and complexity. Papal and Spanish celebration help explain why English Protestants see the event as a warning, while some Catholics and monasteries shelter Huguenot fugitives. For [[FrancisWalsingham]], [[RobertBeale]], [[WilliamCecil|William Cecil / Lord Burleigh]], and [[ElizabethI|Elizabeth I]]'s ministers, the massacre becomes [[ConfessionalTraumaMemory|confessional trauma memory]] that makes a London version imaginable.

The St Bartholomew's Day Massacre appears in [[694-elizabeth-i-vs-the-catholics-a-treacherous-conspiracy-part-4-glt1627515087]] as the 1572 French Catholic violence after which [[France]] remained politically unstable. The episode uses the massacre as the background for the three-Henry factional field: [[HenryOfNavarre]] for the Huguenots, [[HenryDukeOfGuise|Henry, Duke of Guise]] for the Catholic side, and [[HenryIIIOfFrance|Henry III]] as king.

[[695-elizabeth-i-vs-the-catholics-the-shadow-war-part-5-glt5637968455]] also uses the massacre to explain [[PhilipSidney|Sir Philip Sidney]]'s militant Protestant formation. Sidney was in Paris with [[FrancisWalsingham]] during the massacre, and the experience helps explain why Sidney later sees the [[LowCountries|Low Countries]] as more than a distant war.

For the wiki, the massacre is a French-confessional trauma node that links court politics, Protestant militancy, Anglo-French diplomacy, and the road toward [[ConfessionalWarEscalation]].
