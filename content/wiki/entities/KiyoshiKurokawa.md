## Overview

Kiyoshi Kurokawa appears in the bounded source as the physician selected to lead an independent parliamentary investigation of the Fukushima nuclear disaster.

## Current Profile

[[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-bill-gurley-searching-for-feynman-42956628|Bill Gurley's presentation]] values Kurokawa less for narrow nuclear specialization than for independence, credibility, a public-safety mandate, and willingness to make findings internationally transparent. Gurley uses that profile as one model for a future COVID-origin commission.

## Key Characteristics

- Leads across specialties without being institutionally tied to the operator or regulator under review.
- Centers human safety and actionable prevention.
- Treats broad authority and transparent reporting as prerequisites for credible inquiry.
- Represents commission design in which independence is a selection criterion, not only a declared value.

## Evidence

- Independence rule: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-bill-gurley-searching-for-feynman-42956628|Bill Gurley's presentation]] says commission members could not have institutional connections to the regulator or power company.
- Public-safety mandate: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-bill-gurley-searching-for-feynman-42956628|Bill Gurley's presentation]] says Kurokawa emphasized human safety and actionable recommendations.
- Transparency: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-bill-gurley-searching-for-feynman-42956628|Bill Gurley's presentation]] presents international openness and the finding that the accident was man-made and preventable as central to the commission's credibility.

## Qualifications

This is Gurley's compressed account of Kurokawa's role, not a comprehensive profile of the commission, Japanese nuclear governance, or the full technical and institutional causes of Fukushima.
