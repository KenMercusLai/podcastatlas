# John Curtin

## Overview

John Curtin was Australia's Labor prime minister and principal Second World War leader, associated above all with redirecting national security toward the United States after Japan entered the war.

## Current Profile

The episode traces Curtin from an Irish Catholic, trade-union, anti-war, and anti-conscription background to wartime decisions that included qualified overseas conscription and the [[AustralianStrategicRealignment|turn toward America]]. Pearl Harbor, the fall of Singapore, attacks on Darwin and Sydney, and perceived British prioritization of India form the strategic setting.

The turn was not a simple cultural break. Curtin retained British racial language and attachment even while declaring that Australia would look to America, and his estrangement from Catholic institutional practice complicates any direct mapping from origin to mature identity.

## Key Characteristics

- Labor and trade-union leader formed in an Irish Catholic milieu.
- Earlier opponent of war and conscription who changed position under existential threat.
- Wartime prime minister during Japanese advance and attacks on Australia.
- Principal political voice of Australia's security turn toward the United States.
- Retained important British identifications despite strategic realignment.
