# 鸭哥

## Overview
鸭哥 is an agent builder and educator whose podcast appearances connect [[OpenClaw]] product analysis with hands-on experiments in personal context, content operations, health tracking, generated software, and AI-mediated organizational work.

## Current Profile
Across the two sources, 鸭哥's stable position is that an AI system should be managed more like fallible staff than treated like a deterministic tool. The user must make goals, context, standards, permissions, and feedback explicit; agents can then execute, inspect outcomes, revise, and preserve what they learn.

His later account names this operating layer [[ContextInfrastructure]]. It extends his earlier emphasis on [[AgentHarness]], [[PersistentAgentMemory]], [[AISkills]], and [[LocalAgentExecution]] into a personal and organizational loop: capture useful files and conversations, retrieve them when acting, turn outputs and corrections into durable context, and keep humans responsible for consequential decisions.

## Key Characteristics
- Treats AI as delegated labor that must receive clear objectives, evidence, success criteria, and review.
- Builds context as files, transcripts, indexes, skills, and feedback rather than relying only on chat history or model memory.
- Prefers executable loops in which agents act, observe results, correct failures, and document reusable procedures.
- Experiments across personal administration, health data, publishing, software, devices, and agent-to-agent collaboration.
- Favors local control where private context, credentials, and broad machine permissions make hosted execution risky.
- Distinguishes automation of execution from discovery of demand, commercial closure, responsibility, and trust.

## Qualifications
The profile is based on two podcast accounts centered on 鸭哥's own practice. Reported costs, benchmark scores, audience growth, health correlations, and automation outcomes are not independent audits. His local-file approach and personalized “老鸭汤” may not transfer directly to users with different workflows, risk tolerances, or judgment standards.
