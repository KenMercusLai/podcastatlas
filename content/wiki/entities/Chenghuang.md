# 城隍

城隍 is the city-god institution discussed in [[136-chunri-mingmei-liaoliao-guishen-827911486]]. The episode describes a movement from city-wall or city-protection deity toward a god imagined as an underworld mayor: a local divine official who protects territory, receives reports, and participates in the administration of the dead.

[[ep249-minnan-gucheng-youshenji-chuantong-yishi-beihou-de-xiangtu-wangluo-lpk1vb9-zmgmuous1nyzefgapgww]] adds a living Minnan temple case through [[YongningAncientCity|永宁古城]]. There, the 城隍 temple is treated as a main deity space and public resource: incense money, heritage status, engineering projects, events, and committee seats can pull different surname groups into competition, while [[JitongSpiritMedium|鸡童/乩童]] ritual makes divine presence bodily and local.

The source also stresses how city-god posts could be filled by historical worthies. Suzhou's Spring Lord, Hangzhou's Wen Tianxiang, Han Yu as a Guozijian city god, and the Ming elevation of city-god ranks all show how the office borrows prestige from officialdom, moral reputation, education, and imperial classification. [[LiaozhaiZhiyi|《聊斋志异》]] then turns the role into story through underworld examinations and delayed appointment.
