# Enzo Ferrari

Enzo Ferrari is the founder figure at the center of [[ferrari-1]]. The episode presents him as a driver who became more important as an organizer, dealer, racing operator, and brand builder than as a pure racing talent.

His path runs from postwar hardship and early racing through [[AlfaRomeo]], the creation of [[ScuderiaFerrari]], wartime machine-tool production, the move to [[Maranello]], and the first [[Ferrari]] road cars. The source treats Enzo's life as the origin of [[RacingHeritageBrandMoat]]: speed, death, grief, independence, and marketing instinct became part of the company's durable mythology.

## Source Position
- Enzo learned that organizing racing could be more powerful than driving.
- He used road-car sales to fund racing, making the commercial business subordinate to the racing mission at first.
- His rejection of [[FordMotorCompany]] protected racing control even though it cost Ferrari a large acquisition deal.
- The later [[Fiat]] deal shows a different priority: continuity for [[Ferrari]], racing, and [[PieroFerrari]] mattered more than total independence.
