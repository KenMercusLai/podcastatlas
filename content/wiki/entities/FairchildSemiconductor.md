# Fairchild Semiconductor

Fairchild Semiconductor appears in [[zhenzheng-gaibian-shijie-de-jishu-weishenme-yikaishi-dou-bu-bei-kanhao-s10e16-8c95b3dc-d75a-4bdd-84d4-2c06fd2d85b1]] in two semiconductor-history roles. First, the source says early integrated circuits were hard to sell because customers could assemble discrete transistors more cheaply and flexibly. Second, it says companies such as Fairchild and [[Intel]] spent years improving [[MOSFET|MOS]] stability until the integration advantage became decisive.

The episode therefore treats Fairchild as part of the bridge from skeptical early markets to [[MooreLaw|Moore's Law]] as an industry rhythm. The commercial difficulty of early chips helps explain why a roadmap could matter as much as a lab result.
