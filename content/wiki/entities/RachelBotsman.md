# Rachel Botsman

## Overview

Rachel Botsman is presented in the bounded source as a trust researcher and the author and narrator of *How to Trust and Be Trusted*.

## Current Profile

Botsman treats trust as a practical relationship with uncertainty rather than as total knowledge, constant visibility, or the absence of risk. Her framework works in both directions: people need to decide whom and what to trust, while leaders and colleagues need to earn trust through clear expectations, honest limits, and behavior that can withstand appropriate verification.

Her decision guidance is calibrated rather than suspicious. Trust remains the ordinary default that permits cooperation, but consequential choices deserve a pause in which familiarity, desirability, convenience, intuition, missing information, and possible harm are examined.

## Key Characteristics

- Defines trust as confident engagement with an outcome that cannot be fully known in advance.
- Separates honest openness and contextual explanation from continuous visibility and surveillance.
- Treats trust-giving and trust-earning as distinct but reciprocal responsibilities.
- Uses familiarity, convenience, intuition, and decision speed as diagnostic checks on judgment.
- Recommends clear expectations, boundaries, and proportionate verification rather than universal monitoring.
