# Mike Pitts

## Overview

Mike Pitts is an archaeologist, writer, journalist, and editor of *British Archaeology* whose work on [[Stonehenge]] emphasizes the stones themselves, changing chronology, collective construction, and disciplined uncertainty.

## Current Profile

In [[199-stonehenge-glt3612348466]], Pitts explains Stonehenge through geology, excavation, radiocarbon dating, burial evidence, isotope analysis, comparative archaeology, and practical transport constraints. His account rejects fantasies such as alien building and avoids turning disputed routes or ritual meanings into facts.

Pitts nevertheless treats interpretation as necessary. He reads stone movement as [[MonumentBuildingAsSocialCoordination|social and political action]], accepts that the monument's meaning changed over generations, and proposes possible links among bluestones, cremated ancestors, the later protective sarsen architecture, and distributed stone fragments. His method pairs concrete evidence with visible labels for uncertainty.

## Key Characteristics

- Centers Stonehenge's stones, geological origins, movement, dressing, arrangement, and later fragmentation.
- Uses improved dating and later fieldwork to replace a compressed three-phase chronology with a longer sequence.
- Interprets monument building through labor, gathering, prestige, authority, and regional networks.
- Accepts multiple functions and historical change rather than one timeless answer to what Stonehenge was for.
- Distinguishes established alignments, burials, and materials from speculative belief, sensory experience, and institutional organization.

## Evidence

- Chronological method: [[199-stonehenge-glt3612348466]] attributes the longer activity sequence to excavation review, fieldwork, and improved radiocarbon dating.
- Material method: [[199-stonehenge-glt3612348466]] follows Welsh bluestone provenances, local sarsens, possible sledges, waterways, track preparation, and stone weights.
- Social interpretation: [[199-stonehenge-glt3612348466]] frames transport and construction as opportunities for gathering, prestige, and authority.
- Epistemic restraint: [[199-stonehenge-glt3612348466]] rejects sacrifice and modern-computer claims while marking decoration, music, access, transport route, and ancestor meanings as uncertain.

## Qualifications

This profile derives from one podcast summary centered on Pitts's Stonehenge interpretation, not a full account of his career or publications. His preferred transport route, burial estimate, protective-sarsen hypothesis, ancient-music inference, and interpretation of later fragments remain source-scoped. His rejection of a single explanation does not establish that every proposed function contributed equally.
