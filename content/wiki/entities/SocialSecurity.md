# Social Security

## Overview
Social Security is the U.S. public retirement and social-insurance program used in this episode as a reform target for broadening citizen ownership of equity upside. The page records the source's policy debate, not a full institutional history.

## Current Profile
In the All-In discussion, [[DavidFriedberg|Friedberg]] contrasts confiscatory public ownership of AI companies with a market-oriented Social Security reform. He argues that the trust fund's Treasury-based structure could be replaced or supplemented by account-based equity ownership, letting citizens share in American company growth, including AI-company growth, without the government directly seizing shares from specific firms.

The current wiki role for Social Security is therefore narrow: it is the retirement-program vehicle inside a debate over [[AIPublicOwnershipProposal]], [[AIAutomationRedistribution]], and [[UniversalEquityOwnership]].

## Key Characteristics
- U.S. public retirement program invoked as an alternative vehicle for broad citizen equity ownership.
- Presented as a non-confiscatory counterproposal to direct state ownership of AI-company shares.
- Used to connect AI productivity upside to retirement-account design, asset allocation, and public legitimacy.
