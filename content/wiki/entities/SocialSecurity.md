# Social Security

## Overview
Social Security is the U.S. public retirement and social-insurance program financed mainly through payroll taxes on current workers and employers. The wiki now covers both its projected financing gap and a separate proposal to use it as a vehicle for broad citizen equity ownership.

## Current Profile
[[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-fa7b2a90f9b1f7efc01397a3ed4ae0bf-30494375-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-fa7b2a90f9b1f7efc01397a3ed4ae0bf-30494375|The Planet Money episode]] presents Social Security as a [[SocialSecurityPayAsYouGoFinancing|pay-as-you-go]] system with a reserve buffer. Current payroll receipts largely fund current beneficiaries; when outlays exceed revenue, the trust fund fills the gap. Aging, longer life, low fertility, employment, immigration, and the distribution of taxable wages therefore affect solvency together.

The source says reserves could become insufficient for full scheduled benefits as soon as 2032. [[SteveGoss]] describes a matching 2.2-percentage-point payroll-tax increase as sufficient to close the modeled 75-year gap by itself; without it, policymakers need a [[SocialSecurityReformPortfolio]] spanning taxes, taxable compensation, benefit growth, retirement age, high-earner treatment, and workforce growth. Distribution is inseparable from arithmetic because each option assigns costs differently.

A distinct asset-allocation proposal appears in [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-anthropics-fable-backlash-nationalizing-ai-inflation-heats-up-californias-broken-elections-41630275]]: [[DavidFriedberg|David Friedberg]] argues that citizens could gain equity exposure through Social Security-style accounts rather than direct government confiscation of AI-company shares. [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-fa7b2a90f9b1f7efc01397a3ed4ae0bf-30494375-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-fa7b2a90f9b1f7efc01397a3ed4ae0bf-30494375]] qualifies the proposal's solvency relevance by saying the trust fund is now too depleted for stock-market investment alone to close the gap.

## Key Characteristics
- Transfers payroll-tax revenue from current workers and employers to current beneficiaries, backed by trust-fund reserves.
- Faces a source-projected reserve shortfall driven by demographics, employment, wages, and the taxable earnings base.
- Can be rebalanced through revenue, benefits, retirement-age rules, immigration, or combinations of those levers.
- Produces unavoidable distributional choices across workers, employers, retirees, high earners, and occupations.
- Also appears in a separate AI-era proposal for broad citizen equity ownership through retirement accounts.

## Qualifications
The 2032 date, 22% payment reduction, reform percentages, poverty impact, wealth shares, and Canadian comparison are episode-reported estimates rather than independently verified current actuarial findings. The two sources also address different questions: one models program financing, while the other advances an AI-distribution and ownership proposal without analyzing transition costs, market risk, benefit guarantees, or solvency.

## What Changed
- Expanded the profile from a narrow AI-equity proposal to the program's financing structure and projected shortfall.
- Added the need for a mixed reform portfolio when a full payroll-tax increase is politically unavailable.
- Made distributional burdens and the limits of late trust-fund equity investment explicit.
