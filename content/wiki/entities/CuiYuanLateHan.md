## Overview

崔瑗 / Cui Yuan appears in Hanji 804 as a source-bounded case of legitimacy reading and post-coup self-protection. He sees the weakness in Yan Xian's succession position before the Yan fall, but after [[EmperorShunOfHan|Liu Bao / Emperor Shun]] is restored he refuses to reopen the risky private counsel that could implicate him.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-804-gei-ni-tang-de-ren-bu-yiding-shi-guiren-lqongdqg-hgetoegpylv-os86buh|Hanji 804]] says Cui Yuan, son of Cui Yin, had accepted an appointment from [[YanXianLateHan|Yan Xian]]. Because Beixiang Hou was not the legitimate son of the prior emperor, Cui Yuan expected the Yan family's route to fail and wanted to advise Yan Xian to depose Beixiang Hou and enthrone Liu Bao as Jiyin Wang.

The source then turns Cui Yuan into a survival case. He cannot reach Yan Xian directly and tells Zhangshi Chen Chan instead. After the Yan collapse, Cui Yuan is implicated and removed. His students want to petition, and Chen Chan is willing to testify as Sili Xiaowei, but Cui Yuan refuses both routes and goes home, never again accepting office in the episode's account. The likely reason is that reopening the private conversation would expose an old high-risk political calculation after the winning side had changed.

## Key Characteristics

- Legitimacy-sensitive court actor who reads Beixiang Hou's succession weakness before the Yan fall.
- Yan-appointed insider who considers advising Yan Xian toward Liu Bao's restoration.
- Risk-aware survivor who refuses post-coup petitions and testimony.
- Example of political foresight that still cannot be safely publicized after regime change.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 804. It does not reconstruct Cui Yuan's full biography, writings, later reputation, or Chen Chan's independent career. The motive for refusing petition and testimony is an inference from the source's risk context rather than a stated confession.
