## Overview

沈万三 / Shen Wansan is a historical wealthy figure whose sparse record was overtaken by stories of fabulous wealth, Nanjing wall construction, imperial punishment, and the political danger faced by merchants under early Ming power.

## Current Profile

The episode separates the person from the legend. It accepts that Shen Wansan existed and that his descendants suffered during early-Ming political purges, but cites scholarship placing his death in the late Yuan or at most the beginning of the Ming. That chronology makes the familiar story in which he funds Nanjing's wall, offers to reward the army, angers [[ZhuYuanzhang]], and is exiled to Yunnan highly doubtful.

His cultural afterlife nevertheless matters. By the middle and late Ming he had become a generic symbol of extreme wealth, attracting treasure-basin, alchemy, wall-building, and state-persecution stories. The episode reads that figure as a container for memories of Jiangnan wealthy-household displacement, political coercion, and the tragic dependence of merchants on court power.

## Key Characteristics

- Historically real wealthy figure with a much thinner evidentiary record than his later fame suggests.
- Chronologically difficult to reconcile with the best-known Zhu Yuanzhang and Nanjing stories.
- Family memory connected to early-Ming political purges and decline.
- Later transformed into a folklore symbol of inexhaustible wealth.
- Functions as a narrative template for the merchant trapped beneath concentrated state power.

## Qualifications

The episode does not settle Shen Wansan's precise name history, birth, death, businesses, fortune, or family chronology. Its strong conclusion is negative: famous Ming-court anecdotes cannot be treated as straightforward biography. The proposed links to Jiangnan displacement, memory of Zhang Shicheng, and later literary figures are interpretations of cultural formation rather than proof about Shen's own actions.
