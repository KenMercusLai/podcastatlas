# Tenerife Expedition (1797)

## Overview

The Tenerife expedition was a failed July 1797 British attempt against Santa Cruz de Tenerife in which [[HoratioNelson]] lost his right arm and the attacking force gained no strategic or financial return.

## Current Profile

Nelson and Thomas Troubridge proposed the expedition after reports that Spanish treasure might be at Tenerife, joining strategic disruption to the prospect of prize money. The force arrived with poor local knowledge and faced steep beaches, cliffs, heat, shore guns, and difficult currents. A first predawn landing failed when the current delayed the boats until defenders were alerted, while troops dragged guns uphill only to find they could not reach the town. Nelson acknowledged that success had become a forlorn hope but argued that national honor required another attempt. During the night assault he stood conspicuously at the front of his boat and was shot through the right elbow; Josiah Nisbet's tourniquet helped keep him alive before amputation aboard *Theseus*. Troubridge's landed force became trapped and surrendered. Spanish commander Antonio Gutierrez then supplied food and medical help, giving the defeat an honorable negotiated ending without changing the expedition's failure.

## Key Characteristics

- Amphibious operation driven by strategic hopes, prize incentives, and inadequate local intelligence.
- First failed landing that exposed current, terrain, artillery-position, and surprise problems.
- Repeated assault justified through national honor despite Nelson's recognition of poor odds.
- Disabling wound and rapid shipboard amputation that reshaped Nelson's body and confidence.
- British defeat moderated by honorable Spanish treatment of the surrendered force.

The profile rests on one British- and Nelson-centered popular-history episode. The treasure intelligence, prize-money motive, force composition, chronology, casualty figure, reported dialogue and letters, surgical detail, individual lifesaving credit, and the exact balance of Nelson's and Troubridge's command responsibility remain source-scoped.
