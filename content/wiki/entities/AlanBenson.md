# Alan Benson

## Overview

Alan Benson is an economist featured in [[hamlet-was-wrong-hamlet-was-wrong|Hamlet Was Wrong]] for research on promotion and managerial performance in sales organizations.

## Current Profile

The episode attributes to Benson a study of roughly 100,000 salespeople at 400 firms. Its central result supports the [[PeterPrinciple]]: top sellers were more likely to be promoted even though individual sales success did not predict management success, while teams led by collaborative sellers reportedly performed better.

Benson also supplies the episode's main qualification. Passing over stars can reduce their subsequent sales, so a firm faces a joint problem of role fit, status, reward, and motivation rather than a simple choice of the best manager.

## Key Characteristics

- Studies promotion and performance inside sales organizations.
- Distinguishes individual-contributor output from managerial capability.
- Makes the incentive cost of non-promotion part of the analysis.
- Supplies the episode's main empirical counterweight to Gladwell's personal anecdotes.

## Evidence

- Promotion mismatch - [[hamlet-was-wrong-hamlet-was-wrong]] reports that star sellers were promoted more often but managed lower-performing teams.
- Collaborative management - [[hamlet-was-wrong-hamlet-was-wrong]] reports better team results under collaborative sellers.
- Motivation response - [[hamlet-was-wrong-hamlet-was-wrong]] reports declining performance among high sellers who were passed over.

## Qualifications

The bounded input is a structured episode summary, not Benson's paper, dataset, methods, or full interview. Sample size, identification strategy, effect magnitude, organizational context, and the reported 30 percent performance difference require the primary research before generalization.

## What Changed

- Established Benson as the empirical source for the episode's promotion trade-off.
