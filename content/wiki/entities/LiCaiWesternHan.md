## Overview

李蔡 / Li Cai is presented as [[LiGuangHanGeneral|李广]]'s younger brother and a three-reign veteran who rises from yushi dafu to chancellor under [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]. His current profile is dominated by a burial-land offense: after receiving an honorable grant near Yangling, he appropriates adjoining land, sells it, is removed and imprisoned, and dies by suicide.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-332-2-dahan-chengxiang-licai-zhisi-jiujing-youmeiyou-heimu-loyre4ghvm3zcp-jxez9k26du8oj|Hanji 332-2]] separates two explanatory layers. Its directly stated case is that Li Cai expands beyond a roughly twenty-mu imperial burial grant by three qing and sells the occupied land for 400,000 cash. Its broader political interpretation is that, after a major flood, he also fails to follow an expected chancellor's illness-and-resignation script for absorbing disaster responsibility. The source does not prove that this omission caused the prosecution or that a hidden plot existed.

The episode's death account is therefore serious but bounded. Han Wudi removes and imprisons Li Cai after the land matter becomes known; Li Cai then kills himself in prison. The host interprets the act as fear of inevitable punishment and moral inferiority to Li Guang, but explicitly reports no evidence of additional corruption.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han official who advances from yushi dafu to chancellor after [[GongsunHong|公孙弘]].
- Three-reign veteran and younger brother of [[LiGuangHanGeneral|李广]].
- Recipient of an honorable burial-land grant near Yangling.
- Officeholder accused of appropriating adjoining land and selling it for 400,000 cash.
- Imprisoned former chancellor whose case ends in suicide rather than a completed trial in the supplied account.

## Qualifications

The current profile rests on one short podcast summary rather than a full primary-source comparison. The episode does not establish the precise land-law classification, buyer identity, trial procedure, likely sentence, method of suicide beyond its own narration, or causal link between the earlier disaster-resignation issue and the land prosecution. Its statements about Li Cai's greed, competence, political understanding, fear, and moral standing are host judgments, and its express claim that no other corruption is recorded is not proof that none occurred.
