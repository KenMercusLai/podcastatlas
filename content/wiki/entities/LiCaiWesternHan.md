## Overview

李蔡 / Li Cai is presented as a three-reign Western Han veteran and kinsman of [[LiGuangHanGeneral|李广]] whose path through palace attendance, military service, a marquisate, and senior civil office ends in the chancellorship under [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]. His final case concerns the sale of land adjoining an imperial burial grant, followed by removal, imprisonment, and suicide; the two current sources disagree about both his exact kinship with Li Guang and the suicide mechanism.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-332-1-gudai-zuigaowei-zhiye-zhiyi-juran-you-ta-lk6luhbh37m9xnachwjp9zbdmcnu|Hanji 332-1]] extends Li Cai's profile backward. It places his entry into service with Li Guang as an attendant under Emperor Wen, gives him senior rank under Emperor Jing, and says he later serves as Dai chancellor and campaigns under Wudi. In its account, service as Light Chariot General under [[WeiQingHanGeneral|卫青]] in 124 BCE produces the Lean marquisate, and Li Cai later follows [[GongsunHong|公孙弘]] as Wudi's seventh chancellor. The episode contrasts this official success with Li Guang's greater fame and interprets “为人在下中” as a low personal rating, but does not supply a full performance record.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-332-2-dahan-chengxiang-licai-zhisi-jiujing-youmeiyou-heimu-loyre4ghvm3zcp-jxez9k26du8oj|Hanji 332-2]] separates the terminal case into a stated offense and a broader political interpretation. It says Li Cai expands beyond a roughly twenty-mu imperial burial grant by three qing, sells the occupied land for 400,000 cash, and is removed and imprisoned. It also argues that he earlier fails to perform an expected illness-and-resignation script after a major flood, but does not prove that omission caused the prosecution or that a hidden plot existed.

Both sources agree that the case ends in suicide, but their mechanics conflict: Hanji 332-1 says Li Cai cuts his own throat, while Hanji 332-2 says he dies by striking the prison's iron gate. The current synthesis therefore treats suicide after his fall as shared ground and leaves the method unresolved. His career also supplies a bounded example of the [[StrongRulerChancellorshipDoubleBind|chancellor's double bind]]: political deference was demanded alongside capacity for war, finance, strategy, and court coordination, without proving that this structure alone caused his individual death.

## Key Characteristics

- Three-reign Western Han veteran whose reported career spans palace attendance, military service, a Dai chancellorship, a marquisate, and the imperial chancellorship.
- Kinsman of [[LiGuangHanGeneral|李广]], called a cousin in Hanji 332-1 and a younger brother in Hanji 332-2.
- Light Chariot General said to receive the Lean marquisate after campaigning with [[WeiQingHanGeneral|卫青]] in 124 BCE.
- Official who advances from yushi dafu to chancellor after [[GongsunHong|公孙弘]].
- Recipient of an honorable burial-land grant near Yangling.
- Officeholder accused of appropriating adjoining land and selling it for 400,000 cash.
- Imprisoned former chancellor whose case ends in suicide, with conflicting accounts of the method.

- Hanji 332-1 and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-332-2-dahan-chengxiang-licai-zhisi-jiujing-youmeiyou-heimu-loyre4ghvm3zcp-jxez9k26du8oj|Hanji 332-2]] both place him after Gongsun Hong in the imperial chancellorship; 332-2 specifies advancement from yushi dafu.

- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-332-2-dahan-chengxiang-licai-zhisi-jiujing-youmeiyou-heimu-loyre4ghvm3zcp-jxez9k26du8oj|Hanji 332-2]] interprets his nonresignation after flood disaster as political-rule insensitivity, while leaving its causal connection to his later prosecution unproved.

- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-332-2-dahan-chengxiang-licai-zhisi-jiujing-youmeiyou-heimu-loyre4ghvm3zcp-jxez9k26du8oj|Hanji 332-2]] says Han Wudi grants roughly twenty mu near Yangling and that Li Cai later occupies three additional qing and sells the land for 400,000 cash.

Death and reputation:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-332-1-gudai-zuigaowei-zhiye-zhiyi-juran-you-ta-lk6luhbh37m9xnachwjp9zbdmcnu|Hanji 332-1]] gives Li Cai the low “为人在下中” judgment and says he dies by cutting his throat; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-332-2-dahan-chengxiang-licai-zhisi-jiujing-youmeiyou-heimu-loyre4ghvm3zcp-jxez9k26du8oj|Hanji 332-2]] says he is removed, imprisoned, and dies by striking the prison's iron gate. Both use Li Guang as a reputational contrast.

## Qualifications

The current profile rests on two short podcast summaries rather than a full primary-source comparison. They disagree on whether Li Cai is Li Guang's cousin or younger brother and whether suicide occurs by throat-cutting or striking the prison gate. The profile does not independently establish the office sequence, campaign details, date conversion, transmitted personal ranking, land-law classification, buyer identity, trial procedure, likely sentence, or causal link between disaster-resignation expectations and the land prosecution. Statements about Li Cai's greed, competence, political understanding, fear, and moral standing are host judgments, and the report that no other corruption is recorded is not proof that none occurred.

## What Changed

- Extended the profile from the terminal land case to Li Cai's three-reign service, military advancement, marquisate, and route to the chancellorship.
- Recast the Li Guang relationship as source-contested rather than preserving one kinship label as settled.
- Recorded the conflicting suicide mechanisms while retaining suicide after political fall as shared ground.
- Connected the case to the chancellorship's compliance-versus-capacity double bind without making it a complete cause of death.
