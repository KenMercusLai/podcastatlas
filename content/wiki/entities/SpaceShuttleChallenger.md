# Space Shuttle Challenger / 挑战者号

## Overview

Space Shuttle Challenger was the NASA orbiter lost shortly after launch on 28 January 1986 with seven crew members aboard. The bounded source treats the disaster as a coupled technical and institutional failure rather than an isolated defective-part story.

## Current Profile

[[502-yuzhou-chaoji-gongcheng-shi-ruhe-kua-diao-de-liuyi-tan-tiaozhanzhehao-shigu-sishi-zhounian-loylfmnxyc-cuajgcn12-v7-ybd]] places the immediate low-temperature sealing failure inside a shuttle program strained by mixed objectives, constrained budgets, difficult reusable-system maintenance, launch-cadence promises, and ineffective escalation of engineering warnings. The accident therefore joins component failure to [[GoFeverOrganizationalRisk]] and [[CatastrophicFailureRootCauseInvestigation]].

## Key Characteristics

- The launch carried seven crew members, including teacher Christa McAuliffe, and had unusually high public visibility.
- The source identifies cold-sensitive solid-rocket-booster joint sealing as the immediate technical mechanism.
- Low temperature, ice, and thermal-protection concerns were raised before launch but did not stop the mission.
- Schedule, publicity, and launch-frequency commitments shaped the organizational decision environment.
- The Rogers Commission and [[RichardFeynman]] made the technical and institutional failures part of the public record.
- The later Columbia disaster shows that shuttle-system risk was not confined to one seal or one mission.
