## Overview

淳于意 / Chunyu Yi is a former Qi official whose sentence to mutilating punishment becomes the case through which [[ChunyuTiying|淳于缇萦]] petitions [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]] and the episode narrates a broader penal reform.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-224-tiying-jiufu-14sui-shaonv-ruhe-gandong-hanwendi-lifxjdmrqoao7b8dq43w-xjh_dhy|Hanji 224]] says Chunyu Yi commits an unspecified punishable offense and, because he had held office, is transported from Qi to Chang'an to receive a mutilating sentence. The episode attributes to Tiying's petition the claim that Qi people had regarded him as honest and fair, but it does not explain the charge well enough to determine guilt, legal procedure, or whether that reputation bears on the offense.

His complaint that five daughters provide no help in an emergency supplies the narrative reversal: the youngest daughter stops crying, follows him to Chang'an, and acts successfully. Liu Heng reportedly pardons Chunyu Yi. His durable place in the current wiki is therefore as the condemned person around whom family assumptions, individual clemency, and [[MutilatingPunishmentAbolition|general penal reform]] intersect, not as a fully reconstructed official or medical biography.

## Key Characteristics

- Former Qi official sentenced to transport and mutilating punishment in Chang'an.
- Father of five daughters whose gendered complaint is contradicted by Tiying's action.
- Beneficiary of an imperial petition and reported pardon.
- Individual case through which the episode introduces a general reform of punishment.

## Qualifications

The profile rests on one popular-history podcast summary. It does not identify the offense, reconstruct the case record, independently verify Chunyu Yi's official reputation, or explain the legal significance of his former office. The sentence, family dialogue, travel, petition, and pardon remain episode-attributed. Broader claims about his medical career are outside this source and are not added here without evidence.

## What Changed
