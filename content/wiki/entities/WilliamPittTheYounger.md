# William Pitt the Younger

## Overview

William Pitt the Younger was a British prime minister remembered in this source for taking office at twenty-four, anti-corruption reform, opposition to Jacobinism, wartime leadership against France, and support for abolition.

## Current Profile

[[35-the-prime-ministers-world-cup-glt6124252189]] presents Pitt the Younger through a set of unusually legible distinctions: he became prime minister at twenty-four, pursued anti-corruption reform, resisted Revolutionary and Napoleonic France, opposed Jacobin politics, and supported abolition. Those associations helped him defeat his father, [[WilliamPittTheElder|William Pitt the Elder]], with 75% of the vote.

The hosts infer that abolitionism and wartime reputation probably helped the younger Pitt with a modern audience. That interpretation makes him a case of policy associations aligning with current moral and patriotic filters, but the source does not reconstruct the limits, timing, or consequences of those positions.

## Key Characteristics

- Became prime minister at twenty-four, the youngest in the episode's account.
- Associated with anti-corruption reform.
- Anti-Jacobin political leader during the revolutionary era.
- Central figure in British resistance to Revolutionary and Napoleonic France.
- Committed abolitionist in the source's compressed characterization.
- Strong modern poll performance against his father.
