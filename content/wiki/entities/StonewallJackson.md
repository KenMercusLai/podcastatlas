# Stonewall Jackson

## Overview

Stonewall Jackson is a Confederate commander presented as a stern, religious military figure whose Bull Run nickname and later legend complicate but do not remove the slavery-centered moral context of Confederate commemoration.

## Current Profile

[[201-american-civil-war-outbreak-part-2-glt4100428000]] distinguishes Jackson from the polished Cavalier stereotype attached to some Confederate commanders. It describes him as Cromwellian in austerity, connects his nickname to standing firm at Bull Run, and notes the episode's account of his teaching Black children in Sunday school.

Those features complicate a flat social portrait but do not detach Jackson from the Confederate cause. The episode uses him to show why personal piety, discipline, and memorable battlefield identity can sustain military legend while leaving the political order being defended open to moral judgment.

## Key Characteristics

- Confederate commander associated with firmness at Bull Run.
- Stern religious figure contrasted with Cavalier imagery.
- Subject of a durable battlefield nickname and military legend.
- Example of how personal traits can complicate without resolving Confederate moral memory.

## Qualifications

This is a narrow memory-and-persona profile, not a full biography or campaign history. The Sunday-school claim, the exact origin and wording of the nickname, Jackson's motives, his views on slavery, and the comparison with Cromwell remain source-scoped. Religious conduct toward individual Black children does not establish opposition to slavery or negate service to the Confederacy.

## What Changed

- Established Jackson as a case where personal piety and military legend coexist with the Confederate cause's slavery context.
