## Overview

《飞狐外传》 / The Young Flying Fox is a [[JinYong|金庸]] wuxia novel represented here through a [[ZhiZuiJinMiFM|纸醉金迷FM]] reading of the mistimed attachments among [[HuFei|胡斐]], [[YuanZiyi|袁紫衣]], and [[ChengLingsu|程灵素]].

## Current Profile

The bounded episode treats the novel's triangle as a conflict between immediate attraction and gradually recognized compatibility. Yuan Ziyi's beauty, energy, martial parity, teasing opposition, and uncertain availability awaken Hu Fei's youthful desire. Cheng Lingsu instead becomes legible through intelligence, shared values, medical and tactical protection, and daily care. The contrast is not that one woman has value and the other does not; it is that desire, admiration, dependence, and suitability for a common life develop at different speeds.

The revised edition complicates the triangle by having Hu Fei imagine marrying Cheng Lingsu and living quietly with her. That moment leaves open a possible movement from pity and respect toward attraction, but her death prevents possibility from becoming a durable decision. Her sacrifice then turns the novel's timing problem into irreversible tragedy: Hu Fei understands more only after he can no longer answer her.

The episode further interprets the ending through publication order. Because the earlier-written 《雪山飞狐》 presents Hu Fei as unattached and available for another romantic line, the discussion sees the prequel's two failed relationships as partly serving continuity. That inference helps explain the outcome but does not by itself prove authorial motive or erase the characters' internal choices.

## Key Characteristics

- Separates immediate erotic attraction from admiration, dependence, value alignment, and long-term compatibility.
- Uses beauty, play, combat, teasing, care, healing, and ordinary domestic imagination as distinct languages of attachment.
- Lets a revised-edition moment reopen a relationship that earlier labels of pity, respect, and siblinghood appear to close.
- Gives objects such as the jade phoenix and orchid different romantic and protective functions.
- Makes delayed recognition tragic by allowing emotional change only as death removes future action.
- Places character choice inside a possible continuity constraint created by an already-written later novel.

## Qualifications

The current profile comes from one multi-host literary discussion rather than a complete textual or edition history. Its claims about desire, insecurity, symbolism, future love, memorial behavior, and publication-order constraint are interpretations. The revised-edition marriage fantasy shows possibility, not a settled relationship; Cheng Lingsu's sacrifice shows profound care but does not create a romantic obligation for Hu Fei or a model for real-world conduct.

## What Changed

- Preserved publication-order pressure as a qualified narrative explanation rather than established authorial intent.
