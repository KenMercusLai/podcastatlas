# Xipe Totec

## Overview

Xipe Totec, “the Flayed One,” is an Aztec deity presented through the joined domains of flaying, fertility, agriculture, spring renewal, illness, and healing.

## Current Profile

[[58-the-world-cup-of-gods-part-1-glt8884399326]] describes Xipe Totec through a renewal logic in which removed skin is compared with a seed's husk or the earth's old covering. The god's self-flaying gives food to humanity, while priests wearing the skins of sacrificed prisoners embody the cycle for an extended ritual period. [[51-aztecs-glt9590586130]] independently places the wearing of flayed skins within a sacred impersonation in which the priest takes on the victim's divine persona.

The same figure is associated with pimples, rashes, and eye complaints. The episode uses that combination to resist dividing gods neatly into beneficent and harmful classes: a power connected to agricultural renewal can also inflict or relieve bodily affliction.

## Key Characteristics

- Deity of agricultural renewal and spring fertility represented through flaying.
- Associated with sacrificial embodiment in which priests wear victims' skins.
- Connected to both causing and relieving skin and eye disease.
- Example of divine power combining nourishment, violence, danger, and cure.
- Case where ritual specialists materially embody a sacrificed person's sacred persona.

## Evidence

- Renewal and self-gift: [[58-the-world-cup-of-gods-part-1-glt8884399326]] connects divine self-flaying to food, seed covering, and agricultural regeneration.
- Ritual embodiment: [[58-the-world-cup-of-gods-part-1-glt8884399326]] describes priests wearing the skins of flayed prisoners for twenty days.
- Harm and healing: [[58-the-world-cup-of-gods-part-1-glt8884399326]] links Xipe Totec to rashes, pimples, eye complaints, and the wider double-sided character of divine power.
- Sacred impersonation: [[51-aztecs-glt9590586130]] explains the wearing of flayed skin as taking on the sacrifice victim's sacred persona and places the rite within an expanded priestly sacrificial system.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to two fast-moving podcast accounts and does not reconstruct regional variation, chronology, ritual participants, captive selection, iconography, Nahuatl terminology, or the evidentiary basis for each reported association. The tournament's judgment of vividness is modern reception, not evidence about historical importance. The claim that a professional priestly cohort helped expand sacrifice is Townsend's episode-level interpretation rather than a settled institutional history.

## What Changed

- Added sacred impersonation as the mechanism connecting priests, skins, victims, and divine persona.
- Situated the rite within the qualified claim that priestly institutions helped sustain expanded sacrifice.
