# Gregor Strasser

## Overview

Gregor Strasser was a Nazi organizer associated with the party's expansion beyond Bavaria, a more socially radical emphasis within National Socialism, [[JosephGoebbels]]'s early movement path, a failed 1932 attempt to split Hitler's coalition, and his own murder during the [[NightOfTheLongKnives|Night of the Long Knives]].

## Current Profile

The 1920s source credits Strasser with helping move the Nazis from a mainly Catholic Bavarian movement into Protestant northern Germany. His emphasis on the “socialist” side of National Socialism helped broaden appeal among farmers, artisans, shopkeepers, the self-employed, and younger Germans; Goebbels entered the movement through his circle before becoming identified with Hitler.

That organizational position later made Strasser an alternative node within the party. In 1932, [[KurtVonSchleicher]] tried and failed to split the movement through him when building an army-centered government. In 1934, the purge widened beyond the SA leadership and killed Strasser, showing that earlier internal difference and potential independence had become lethal under Hitler's consolidated rule.

## Key Characteristics

- Organizer in the Nazi Party's expansion beyond Bavaria.
- Advocate associated with the movement's socially radical language.
- Early political connection in Joseph Goebbels's route into Nazism.
- Possible internal rival whom Schleicher unsuccessfully tried to detach from Hitler.
- Victim of the 1934 purge.

## Qualifications

These sources do not supply a full biography or a complete account of Strasserism. “Socialist” identifies a current and appeal within a violent antisemitic anti-democratic movement; it does not detach Strasser from Nazism or equate his politics with democratic socialism. His precise independence, influence, motives, and threat to Hitler remain source-scoped.
