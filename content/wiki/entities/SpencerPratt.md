# Spencer Pratt

## Overview
Spencer Pratt is presented in the wiki as a [[LosAngeles|Los Angeles]] mayoral candidate whose campaign begins with the [[PacificPalisades|Pacific Palisades]] fire and later becomes part of a disputed election-administration discussion. The sources use him less as an entertainment figure than as a symbol of basic-services frustration, post-fire governance anger, and distrust of California election procedures.

## Current Profile
Pratt's profile has three linked branches. His first interview turns personal fire loss into a platform around public safety, homelessness treatment, nonprofit audits, spending dashboards, faster permitting, and city competence. The June All-In discussion places him inside a Los Angeles mayoral-primary count dispute. The September follow-up lets Pratt state the evidence boundary himself: he objects to ballot harvesting and California election rules but cannot claim fraud caused his loss without proof.

The latest source also extends his fire-recovery profile. Pratt says he is involved in mass-tort litigation, attributes the fire's severity to several alleged prevention and response failures, and says insurance and unresolved liability have blocked rebuilding. These remain allegations without agency responses or judicial findings in the source.

## Key Characteristics
- Los Angeles mayoral candidate whose platform is tied to fire response, rebuilding, public safety, homelessness policy, and fiscal transparency.
- Anti-machine figure in the All-In election discussion, contrasted with [[KarenBass|Karen Bass]] and [[NithyaRaman|Nithya Raman]].
- Candidate whose reported election-day and mail-ballot vote shares are used to question California election rules, while explicitly conceding that suspicion is not proof of fraud.
- Fire survivor and plaintiff using alleged prevention, water-readiness, monitoring, insurance, and rebuilding failures as a state-capacity critique.
- Media entrepreneur who plans to keep producing political content after the campaign.
