# Spencer Pratt

## Overview
Spencer Pratt is presented in the wiki as a [[LosAngeles|Los Angeles]] mayoral candidate whose campaign begins with the [[PacificPalisades|Pacific Palisades]] fire and later becomes part of a disputed election-administration discussion. The sources use him less as an entertainment figure than as a symbol of basic-services frustration, post-fire governance anger, and distrust of California election procedures.

## Current Profile
Pratt's profile now has two linked branches. The first source frames him as a candidate turning personal fire loss into a platform around public safety, homelessness treatment, nonprofit audits, spending dashboards, faster permitting, and city operational competence. The new All-In source places him inside a Los Angeles mayoral-primary count dispute, where the hosts treat his election-day vote share and later mail-ballot decline as suspicious but not independently proven fraud.

The current judgment is source-scoped: Pratt anchors a local anti-establishment campaign and an election-trust controversy, but the wiki does not infer that the election was stolen. The significance is procedural: his case lets the sources connect candidate insurgency, mail voting, ballot harvesting, voter ID, chain of custody, and public confidence in counts.

## Key Characteristics
- Los Angeles mayoral candidate whose platform is tied to fire response, rebuilding, public safety, homelessness policy, and fiscal transparency.
- Anti-machine figure in the All-In election discussion, contrasted with [[KarenBass|Karen Bass]] and [[NithyaRaman|Nithya Raman]].
- Candidate whose reported election-day and mail-ballot vote shares are used by the hosts to argue that California election rules can look manipulable.
- Source-scoped evidence marker for [[ElectionAdministrationTrust]], not proof by itself of fraud or vote manipulation.
