# Leola Brown

## Overview

Leola Brown was [[LindaBrown]]'s mother and a parent in the Topeka challenge that became [[BrownVBoardOfEducation|Brown v. Board of Education]]. Her account is central to the episode's distinction between valuing a Black school and opposing a school board's racial control over family choice.

## Current Profile

Brown described Monroe School as providing a quality education through qualified teachers who took a personal interest in pupils. Her objection concerned Linda's longer journey and the principle that race alone should not let the school board deny the family access to a nearby school.

That testimony complicates the Court-centered psychological framing. It supports equal access without depicting Black-run education as inherently harmful and points toward a substantive account of equality involving family agency, institutional power, and the authority of Black educators.

## Key Characteristics

- Parent participant in the Topeka challenge to segregated school assignment.
- Praised Monroe School and its teachers rather than presenting them as deficient.
- Framed the grievance around distance, choice, and discriminatory public control.
- Supplies a family perspective distinct from the Court's psychological-harm rationale.

## Qualifications

The profile rests on one episode's selected account rather than a complete oral history or case record. It should not be used to infer that every Black family, school, or NAACP litigant framed desegregation identically.

## What Changed

- Established Brown as a source for distinguishing equal freedom from deficit-based accounts of Black schooling.
- Added family choice and teacher care to the case's institutional interpretation.
