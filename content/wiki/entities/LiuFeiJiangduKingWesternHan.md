## Overview

江都王刘非 / Liu Fei enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-382-2-yisheng-zuoguo-sanwei-zhuhou-feizi-gudai-chaomei-nuoji-de-beiqing-rensheng-lt06-as48q9qlpt_-dfdx_jsidjy|Hanji 382-2]] as [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s elder brother, [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]]'s son, Jiangdu king, and [[NuoJiWesternHan|淖姬]]'s first royal partner. The page is disambiguated from [[LiuFeiQiKing|刘肥 / Liu Fei, Qi king]] and [[LiuFei|刘飞]], the modern podcast host.

## Current Profile

The episode uses Liu Fei as the favorable contrast in Nuo Ji's story. At fifteen during the Seven States Rebellion, he is said to request combat service from Han Jingdi, win merit, and receive an exceptional imperial banner. He is also described as physically strong, martial, fond of associating with haoxia figures, and admired as a heroic prince.

His role is also emotional and structural. The source describes him and Nuo Ji as a well-matched pair and points to an excavated lacquer plate bearing Nuo Ji's name in his tomb context as a sign, in the host's interpretation, that he cared deeply for her. His death at forty removes the protective patron under whom Nuo Ji had relative security.

Hanji 237 adds a less flattering qualification to that favorable contrast. Liu Fei is again described as strong and drawn to haoxia, but also as highly extravagant and active in palace construction. His martial image and Nuo Ji attachment therefore do not settle the quality or burden of his rule.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han Jiangdu king, Han Jingdi's son, and Han Wudi's elder brother.
- Remembered in the source for youthful military service during the Seven States Rebellion.
- Presented as strong, martial, haoxia-associated, and admired as a hero-like prince.
- Also described as extravagant and committed to palace construction.
- Nuo Ji's first royal partner in the episode's sequence.
- His early death exposes Nuo Ji to Liu Jian's coercive seizure and later Zhao transfer.

## Qualifications

This page does not reconstruct Liu Fei's full reign, exact Seven States Rebellion command role, palace-building burden, tomb archaeology, or all children. The affectionate interpretation of the lacquer plate is source-scoped to Hanji 382-2. Hanji 237's supplied “刘飞” is normalized to the received royal name 刘非; strength, haoxia recruitment, construction, and extravagance remain compact episode-attributed characterizations.

## What Changed

- Qualified the favorable heroic profile with Hanji 237's palace-building and extravagance claims.
- Added an overlapping account of strength and haoxia association while normalizing “刘飞” to 刘非.
