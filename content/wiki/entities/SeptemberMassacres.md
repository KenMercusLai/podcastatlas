# September Massacres / 九月屠杀

## Overview

The September Massacres were organized killings in Paris prisons from 2 to 6 September 1792, carried out amid Prussian invasion, frontier collapse, rumors of internal conspiracy, and claims that prisoners would join counter-revolution.

## Current Profile

The sources now support a more differentiated judgment than either “spontaneous mob” or “central state plot.” Radical incitement, raids, alarm, and official non-intervention created permission; sections and local networks supplied killing teams and improvised tribunals; questioning and acquittal made the violence appear judicial to its participants; and the victim category widened far beyond proven political enemies. The event became both an early model for [[RevolutionaryTerrorPolitics|revolutionary terror politics]] and a later propaganda battlefield over necessity, depravity, and responsibility.

## Key Characteristics

- Real invasion and the fall of Longwy and Verdun interacted with unsupported prison-breakout and internal-enemy rumors.
- Radical writers including [[JeanPaulMarat|Marat]] discussed or encouraged killing prisoners before and during the event.
- Organized teams used improvised tribunals, killed some prisoners, acquitted others, and left more than half of detainees in the targeted prisons alive.
- Victims included priests and political suspects but also petty criminals, forgers, young detainees, and sex workers.
- Local section networks and identifiable organizers such as [[StanislasMaillard]] are better supported than a direct top-down order from Danton or Robespierre.
- Major leaders knew of the killings and did not stop them; [[GeorgesDanton|Danton]] dismissed prisoner protection and later justified the deaths as necessary.
- Revolutionary necessity narratives and counter-revolutionary atrocity stories each distorted how the killings were understood.

## Evidence

### Fear, incitement, and preventive violence

- [[125-mala-zhisi-beibi-de-ansha-huo-zhengyi-de-chujue-783972516]] links the massacres to fear that enemy forces would release prisoners and identifies Marat's encouragement.
- [[707-the-terror-an-assassination-in-paris-part-1-glt6580043397]] places the event inside Marat's escalating execution rhetoric and reports an approximate toll of 1,500.
- [[544-the-french-revolution-the-september-massacres-part-1-glt8301042051]] reconstructs the Prussian advance, Verdun news, rumors, raids, press incitement, and alarm of 2 September.

### Organized tribunals and distributed responsibility

- [[544-the-french-revolution-the-september-massacres-part-1-glt8301042051]] describes killing teams, local section coordination, named organizer Maillard, prisoner questioning, acquittals, and leadership non-intervention.

### Victims, memory, and political afterlife

- [[544-the-french-revolution-the-september-massacres-part-1-glt8301042051]] widens the victim record, distinguishes established facts about [[PrincesseDeLamballe|Lamballe]] from sensational claims, and records copycat killings and substantial contemporary approval.
- [[125-mala-zhisi-beibi-de-ansha-huo-zhengyi-de-chujue-783972516]] and [[707-the-terror-an-assassination-in-paris-part-1-glt6580043397]] make the massacres central to [[CharlotteCorday|Corday]]'s later judgment of Marat.

## Qualifications

- The sources report approximately one thousand and approximately 1,500 deaths; neither estimate is treated as an audited final total.
- Marat's incitement, Danton's rhetoric and non-intervention, and Robespierre's political position are distinct from proven operational command.
- Selective tribunals did not provide lawful jurisdiction, reliable evidence, counsel, appeal, or protection from ideological classification.
- The pike procession of Lamballe's head is plausible but uncertain; many sexualized mutilation and cannibalism stories are unsupported or contradicted by section records.
- Contemporary acceptance in Paris does not establish unanimous approval or make emergency claims factually true.

## What Changed

- The event is now understood as organized, selective, and quasi-judicial violence rather than an undifferentiated frenzy.
- Responsibility is distributed among inciters, local organizers, participants, approving publics, and non-intervening leaders without inventing a central command.
- The victim profile now includes socially marginal and criminalized people swept into an expanding traitor category.
- Conflicting death estimates and atrocity narratives are preserved explicitly.

## Relationships

- [[JeanPaulMarat]] - radical journalist who incited preventive killing.
- [[GeorgesDanton]] - national leader who mobilized Paris, dismissed prisoner protection, and justified the deaths.
- [[StanislasMaillard]] - identifiable local organizer associated with the killing teams and tribunals.
- [[FrancoisJourgniacSaintMeard]] - survivor whose testimony reveals the tribunals' selective procedure.
- [[PrincesseDeLamballe]] - prominent victim around whom sensational atrocity stories accumulated.
- [[RevolutionaryPopularJustice]] - legitimating logic that turned extra-legal killing into purported judgment.
- [[CrisisValidatedConspiracyPolitics]] - interaction of real danger with widening internal-enemy suspicion.
- [[RevolutionaryAtrocityNarrativeContest]] - conflict between necessity claims and sensational atrocity propaganda.
- [[CharlotteCorday]] - later assassin whose judgment of Marat was shaped by the killings.
