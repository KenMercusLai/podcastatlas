# September Massacres / 九月屠杀

## Overview

The September Massacres were the 1792 killings of prisoners in Paris, driven by fear that enemies would free inmates to join counter-revolution and encouraged in part by [[JeanPaulMarat|Jean-Paul Marat]].

## Current Profile

The sources treat the massacres as an early concentration of the mechanisms later organized in the Terror: foreign-war panic, imagined internal coordination, enemy lists, popular preventive violence, and claims that bloodshed protected the people. They were also central to [[CharlotteCorday|Charlotte Corday]]'s judgment that Marat had become a lethal public threat.

## Key Characteristics

- Militants entered Paris prisons and killed inmates amid fear of counter-revolutionary release.
- The new source reports an approximate death toll of 1,500.
- Marat encouraged the militants according to both sources.
- The killings fused popular action with preventive security logic.
- Their memory shaped Corday's later assassination decision.

## Evidence

### Fear and preventive violence

- [[125-mala-zhisi-beibi-de-ansha-huo-zhengyi-de-chujue-783972516]] links the massacres to fear that enemy forces would release prisoners as internal enemies.
- [[707-the-terror-an-assassination-in-paris-part-1-glt6580043397]] describes Marat's encouragement and gives the episode-level estimate of roughly 1,500 dead.

### Corday's political judgment

- [[125-mala-zhisi-beibi-de-ansha-huo-zhengyi-de-chujue-783972516]] makes the massacres a central reason Corday regarded Marat as deserving punishment.
- [[707-the-terror-an-assassination-in-paris-part-1-glt6580043397]] places them in her broader horror at revolutionary bloodshed and her hope that one killing would prevent more.

## Qualifications

- The approximate toll is source-scoped and should not be treated as a final audited count.
- Marat's encouragement is distinct from sole command or sole responsibility for all killings.
- Fear of foreign invasion and internal revolt was real, but it did not establish the guilt of individual prisoners.

## What Changed

- The massacre now has an approximate source-reported scale and a clearer preventive-security mechanism.
- Its connection to later institutional terror and Corday's strategic error is more explicit.

## Relationships
