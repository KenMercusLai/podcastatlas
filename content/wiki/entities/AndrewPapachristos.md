# Andrew Papachristos

## Overview

Andrew Papachristos is a sociologist and criminologist whose social-network research is used to explain why gun-violence risk can be concentrated within a small connected group rather than spread uniformly across a neighborhood.

## Current Profile

The episode describes Papachristos linking co-arrestees, overlaying shooting and homicide records, and finding that victims clustered within the resulting network. Risk was reportedly highest within roughly one to three connections of a victim and declined with greater network distance.

In one West Chicago neighborhood of about 50,000 residents, the episode says violence concentrated among roughly 400 people. [[MalcolmGladwell]] uses that finding to replace broad suspicion of whole communities with [[ViolenceConcentration]], while the data source and network construction require safeguards against treating association as guilt.

## Key Characteristics

- Uses social-network analysis to study concentrated shooting and victimization risk.
- Shifts the unit of analysis from whole neighborhoods to connected relationships.
- Supports narrower prevention while requiring strong limits on individual inference.

## Qualifications

The episode summary does not reproduce the study, network definition, sample, model, uncertainty, enforcement-bias controls, or causal identification strategy. Co-arrest and social proximity may reflect policing practices as well as relationships and cannot establish an individual's intent, guilt, or future conduct.
