# October Days of 1789

## Overview

The October Days of 1789 were the women's march from Paris to Versailles, the following palace invasion, and the forced transfer of [[LouisXVI|Louis XVI]], [[MarieAntoinette|Marie Antoinette]], and the National Assembly to Paris.

## Current Profile

The event joined subsistence crisis to constitutional politics. Market women demanding bread initiated the march on 5 October; armed men joined, the National Guard followed against [[MarquisDeLafayette|Lafayette]]'s wishes, and the crowd compelled the king to accept revolutionary decrees and return to Paris. The dawn breach of the palace and killing of two bodyguards made the procession both a popular political victory and an act of terror against the royal household. By relocating crown and legislature, the October Days changed where sovereignty could be exercised and made direct Parisian pressure a durable part of national politics.

## Key Characteristics

- Began with women mobilizing around bread scarcity and political suspicion.
- Combined petitioning, armed procession, seizure of cannon, invasion of representative space, and palace violence.
- Forced the king to accept the August decrees and Declaration of Rights without qualification.
- Exposed the National Guard commander's dependence on the political will of his troops.
- Ended with the royal family and Assembly relocated from Versailles to Paris.
- Made the ambiguity between popular liberation and coercive captivity impossible to avoid.

## Qualifications

- "Women's march" names its initiating force but should not erase the men and soldiers who joined it or the changing composition of the crowd.
- The source cannot recover hungry women's voices with the same detail available for court witnesses.
- The event can be understood as effective popular enforcement without treating murder, threat, and humiliation as incidental.
- Crowd totals, exact dialogue, responsibility for the palace breach, and the sequence of particular violent acts remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Created the event profile as the constitutional, subsistence, gender, and coercive bridge between the Declaration of Rights and the later flight to Varennes.
