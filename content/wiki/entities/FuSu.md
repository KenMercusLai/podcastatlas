# 扶苏 / Fu Su

扶苏 / Fu Su enters this wiki branch through [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-125-2-lishishang-qinshihuang-weihe-yao-fenshu-kengru-lulaivzp1xqs71nbaltr-ygpjihw|Qinji 125-2]] as [[QinShiHuang|秦始皇]]'s eldest son and the prince who objects after the [[QinKengruIncident|秦代坑儒事件]]. The source says more than 460 people have been坑杀 at [[Xianyang|咸阳]] when Fusu warns that they are readers who follow [[Confucius|孔子]] and that using extreme law against them may unsettle the realm.

The source's interest in Fusu is narrow but important. He appears as a remonstrating heir inside a court where fear already produces [[CourtFeedbackCollapse|君臣反馈失灵]]. Qin Shi Huang's response is not debate or correction; he angrily sends Fusu to [[Shangjun|上郡]] to serve as [[MengTian|蒙恬]]'s supervising officer.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-127-2-lisi-weisha-bu-bang-yingming-de-fusu-lq-vny5duwwzfubpeijr21oazxbg]] resumes Fusu at Shangjun after Qin Shi Huang's death on tour. [[ZhaoGao|赵高]], [[QinErshi|胡亥]], and [[LiSi|李斯]] forge an order that accuses Fusu of failing the frontier mission and resenting exclusion from the capital, then commands him and Meng Tian to die. Meng Tian advises verification before obeying, but Fusu treats a father's order as binding and kills himself, making his obedience the decisive opening for the [[ForgedEdictSuccessionCoup|伪诏继位政变]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-127-3-haiwai-huigui-yipi-zhujian-jiekai-huhai-lidi-zhi-mi-lja0pankcx4ugtylbxxqkwuavdnw]] keeps Fusu's northern distance central but changes what that distance means in the [[ZhaoZhengShu|《赵正书》]] branch. Instead of only being the target of a post-death forged order, Fusu becomes the absent alternative whose remoteness lets [[LiSi|李斯]] and [[FengQuji|冯去疾]] argue for installing nearby Hu Hai during Qin Shi Huang's final illness.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-128-2-qilai-bei-yapo-de-renmen-lpx9-iicwog4nj-nudaj-i4ajb25]] adds Fusu's posthumous use as a rebel legitimacy name. [[ChenSheng|陈胜]] and [[WuGuang|吴广]] claim Fusu is still alive when they launch the [[DazexiangUprising|大泽乡起义]], because the source says his unjust death, political reputation, and Qin-Chu identity make him attractive to Chu-region followers. This is a later mobilization use of Fusu's image, not a new account of his death.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-128-3-chensheng-wuguang-zudui-chenggong-locnaq-fo1fztyud5nghwkteams|Qinji 128-3]] adds the host's strategic interpretation of that use. Fusu matters not only because he is a plausible grievance symbol, but because Chen Sheng's later kingship shows he is arguing about who should inherit unification and how it should be governed. In that reading, Fusu represents a more acceptable Qin successor than [[QinErshi|胡亥 / 秦二世]], while Chen Sheng's own [[ZhangChuRegime|张楚]] turn shows the same question being answered through Chu.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-138-wudu-qiannian-de-lishi-zhenxiang-zhaogao-bushi-taijian-2-lvtievjrclb1lxxxxr6k-q7kryto|Qinji 138 part 2]] adds a late family-identity branch. The host reads [[ZiyingQin|子婴]] as Fusu's son by interpreting "二世之兄子" and "二世兄子婴" as Hu Hai's elder brother's son. This page records that as the episode's source-scoped position, connecting Fusu's unjust death to the final attempt to install Ziying as Qin king after Hu Hai's death.

## Key Claims
- Fusu's remonstrance frames the victims through scholarship and Confucius rather than through fangshi fraud alone.
- His warning is about public stability: harsh punishment of scholars risks making the empire uneasy.
- Qin Shi Huang's punishment of Fusu shows that even high-status correction becomes dangerous in the late Qin court.
- Qinji 127-2 makes Fusu the displaced heir whose refusal to verify the forged order lets the coup remove its most important rival quickly.
- Qinji 127-3 makes Fusu's distance from the deathbed a practical argument for [[EmergencySuccessionLegitimation|临终就近立储]], while still leaving his later killing as part of Hu Hai's destructive accession.
- Qinji 128-2 makes Fusu a posthumous rebel banner whose remembered injustice can be used against Qin.
- Qinji 128-3 interprets that banner as part of Chen Sheng's argument over the rightful and humane successor to Qin's unified order.
- Qinji 138 part 2 reads Ziying as Fusu's son, making Fusu's line reappear in the final Qin kingship after Hu Hai is killed.
