# Nick Epley

## Overview
Nick Epley is the behavioral scientist interviewed in [[how-to-overcome-social-anxiety-dr-nick-epley-scim7834121132|How to Overcome Social Anxiety | Dr. Nick Epley]]. In the source, [[AndrewHuberman]] introduces him as a University of Chicago researcher whose work centers on social connection, how people infer minds, and why everyday interactions often go better than people predict.

## Current Profile
In this source, Epley is both a social-psychology researcher and a practitioner of the habits he studies. He explains anthropomorphism, stereotypes, gaze, voice, political disagreement, loneliness, strangers, requests, and social anxiety through a recurring frame: people constantly infer what others think, but those inferences are often pessimistic, egocentric, or overconfident. His practical answer is not forced extroversion; it is low-pressure testing through greetings, compliments, questions, and real interactions that let evidence replace mistaken forecasts.

The interview also presents Epley as a parent whose research informs family life. His stories about adoption, his daughter Lindsay, Down syndrome, a stillbirth, and accepting children's paths make the episode's social-connection frame personal: people should not let diagnoses, roles, or imagined futures replace contact with the actual person in front of them.

## Key Characteristics
- Studies social connection, mind perception, and the errors people make when predicting how others will respond.
- Treats everyday interaction as a repeated well-being practice, not only as a path to lasting friendship.
- Emphasizes voice, gaze, behavior, stereotypes, and self-projection as useful but fallible evidence for social inference.
- Recommends safe real-world exposure for misplaced social fears, especially when avoidance is maintained by inaccurate forecasts.
- Connects research to family experience, adoption, disability, parenting expectations, and modeled social habits.
