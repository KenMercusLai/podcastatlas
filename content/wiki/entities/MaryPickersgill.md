# Mary Pickersgill

## Overview

Mary Pickersgill appears in the wiki as the Baltimore flag maker whose large Fort McHenry flag became central to [[StarSpangledBanner|The Star-Spangled Banner]]'s imagery.

## Current Profile

The episode presents Pickersgill through the material production behind a national symbol. She made the large 15-star, 15-stripe flag for [[FortMcHenry]] with help from her daughter, nieces, and a servant. When [[GeorgeArmistead]] ordered it raised after the bombardment, the flag became the visual proof of survival that [[FrancisScottKey]] converted into poetry.

## Key Characteristics

- Baltimore flag maker tied to the Fort McHenry garrison flag.
- Made the large 15-star, 15-stripe flag associated with the anthem.
- Worked with household and workshop helpers rather than alone.
- Material contributor to a national symbol usually remembered through Key's text.
- Connected to the distinction between the storm flag flown during bombardment and the large flag raised afterward.

## Evidence

- Flag production: [[677-usa-the-star-spangled-banner-part-1-glt7890467734]] says Pickersgill made the 42-by-30-foot flag with help from her daughter, nieces, and a servant.
- Symbolic timing: [[677-usa-the-star-spangled-banner-part-1-glt7890467734]] says the large flag was raised at dawn on 14 September after the bombardment.
- Anthem connection: [[677-usa-the-star-spangled-banner-part-1-glt7890467734]] links the flag scene to the sight that inspired Key's poem.
