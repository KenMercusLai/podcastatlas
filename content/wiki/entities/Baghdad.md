# Baghdad

## Overview

Baghdad is represented as the purpose-built political center of the [[AbbasidCaliphate]] and the commercial, legal, intellectual, and imaginative metropolis that grew around it, joining a designed claim to universal rule with a global afterlife in storytelling.

## Current Profile

Part 2 places Baghdad's origin in the aftermath of the [[AbbasidRevolution]]. [[AlMansur]] rejected Damascus, Kufa, and Harran and chose a Mesopotamian site that combined river and trade access with proximity to the dynasty's eastern support. The [[RoundCityOfBaghdad|Round City]] concentrated palace, mosque, gates, walls, and military access around a controlled center, while Persian naming, designers, precedents, and nearby older capitals situated Abbasid authority within deeper regional history.

Part 3 reconstructs the city that grew beyond that plan. Canals and the Tigris supported waterborne movement; Karkh concentrated specialized markets; Rusafa gathered palaces, gardens, mosques, and fountains; and long-distance trade fed merchant wealth, new foods, land speculation, and dense migration. This [[ImperialMetropolisIntegration|metropolitan integration]] depended on Abbasid scale, Arabic communication, legal frameworks, roads, water, and unequal labor.

Part 4 adds jurists, hadith specialists, libraries, translators, multilingual scholars, and the wider [[AbbasidTranslationMovement]]. It also pairs that institutional city with the literary Baghdad of [[ArabianNights|The Arabian Nights]]. The fiction preserves a socially broad urban imagination but cannot directly reconstruct demographic reality.

Grandeur did not erase coercion or danger. Poverty, enslavement, jizya, field labor, fire, flood, plague, gangs, riots, and the al-Amin–al-Ma'mun siege make Baghdad's golden age an account of scale, cultural force, and remembered security rather than universal flourishing.

## Key Characteristics

- Purpose-built Abbasid capital whose location and geometry expressed security, hierarchy, and universal ambition.
- Canal-linked metropolis integrated with trade routes stretching across Africa and Eurasia.
- Commercial city of merchants, specialized markets, land speculation, food culture, and unequal labor.
- Religiously plural but legally stratified home to Muslim, Christian, and Jewish communities.
- Gathering point for Greek, Persian, Christian, Muslim, and other intellectual traditions.
- Literary symbol whose global afterlife exceeds the surviving physical evidence of medieval Baghdad.
- Politically vulnerable city damaged by succession war and later loss of caliphal centrality.

## Qualifications

The current profile rests on three popular-history episodes. The Round City's exact foundation date, design attributions, architectural symbolism, and reported anecdotes remain source-scoped, as do population estimates, “largest” or “most global” comparisons, communal tolerance, crop diffusion, and precise district functions. The episodes range across several centuries, and the Nights accumulated across places and periods; literary setting is suggestive evidence, not a direct civic survey.

## What Changed

- Added al-Mansur's site selection and the Round City as Baghdad's designed political origin.
- Connected the capital's hierarchy and commercial promise to Persian precedents, Khorasani support, and older Mesopotamian urban history.
- Distinguished the controlled circular core from the larger metropolis that grew outside it.

## Relationships

- [[AbbasidCaliphate]] - imperial polity for which Baghdad served as capital and cultural center.
- [[AlMansur]] - founder who selected the site and built its planned political core.
- [[RoundCityOfBaghdad]] - original enclosure that spatialized Abbasid hierarchy and ambition.
- [[AbbasidRevolution]] - dynastic transformation that shifted the caliphate's center eastward.
- [[HarunAlRashid]] - ruler whose reign became the city's remembered benchmark of order.
- [[ImperialMetropolisIntegration]] - systems framework for the city's scale, connectivity, and inequality.
- [[AbbasidSuccessionInstability]] - political pattern that brought civil war into the city.
- [[HouseOfWisdom]] - court-linked library associated with Baghdad's learned reputation.
- [[AbbasidTranslationMovement]] - multilingual movement concentrated around the city's patronage networks.
- [[ArabianNights]] - story corpus that made Baghdad a global imaginative setting.
