# Battle of the Rosebud

## Overview

The Battle of the Rosebud was the 17 June 1876 engagement in which Lakota and Cheyenne forces checked [[GeorgeCrook]]'s northward column shortly before the [[BattleOfLittleBighorn]].

## Current Profile

The episode presents the battle as both a Native operational success and a failure of U.S. situational awareness. Crook initially discounted warnings from Crow scouts and reacted only when the attack began. The fighting lasted roughly six hours, unusually long in the hosts' account of Plains warfare, before Crook stopped advancing.

The consequence exceeded the local result. Crook did not inform [[AlfredTerry]] that his column was no longer moving north, so Terry and [[GeorgeArmstrongCuster]] continued under a three-column concept whose southern arm had already been removed. The victory also strengthened the confidence of fighters associated with [[CrazyHorse]] before Little Bighorn.

## Key Characteristics

- Lakota and Cheyenne forces initiated the engagement.
- Crook discounted scout warnings before contact.
- The battle stopped the southern U.S. column's advance.
- The result was not communicated to the other campaign commanders.
- Its strategic importance lay in both battlefield success and information asymmetry.

## Evidence

- Warning and attack: [[451-custers-last-stand-the-charge-of-the-7th-cavalry-part-6-glt4301705603]] describes Crook's response to Crow scouts and the opening attack.
- Duration and result: [[451-custers-last-stand-the-charge-of-the-7th-cavalry-part-6-glt4301705603]] reports roughly six hours of combat and Crook's decision to stop.
- Campaign consequence: [[451-custers-last-stand-the-charge-of-the-7th-cavalry-part-6-glt4301705603]] states that Terry's officers still did not know Crook had been checked when Custer was released.

## Qualifications

- This profile is based on a compressed popular-history account rather than a detailed battle study.
- Exact force sizes, casualties, tactical movements, and command decisions are not established here.
- The source's characterization of the battle's duration as unusual requires comparison with other Plains engagements.
- The episode does not settle why Crook failed to communicate his changed position.
