# English Defence League

## Overview

The English Defence League, or EDL, is the anti-Islam street movement that [[the-weekend-intelligence-shire-folk-6aa44797a6e9aef4ccfde9bc]] links to [[TommyRobinson]], Kevin Carroll, and the 2009 [[Luton]] confrontation after a Royal Anglian Regiment parade.

## Current Profile

The episode presents the EDL as a movement that tried to distinguish itself from older racist far-right organizations by claiming opposition to radical Islam rather than race. In practice, the source says its early marches used confrontation, black balaclavas, St George's crosses, Facebook organizing, and anti-Muslim chants that often widened the target beyond extremist groups.

Its wiki significance is as an early example of [[BritishMediaExclusionOnlineAmplification]] and [[SelectiveThreatNarrativeEditing]]: local grievances around war, gangs, and Islamist fringe activity were converted into a mass street-protest style that attracted extremists and generated mainstream media exposure.

## Key Characteristics

- Anti-Islam street movement emerging from Luton after the 2009 soldiers' parade confrontation.
- Associated in the episode with Tommy Robinson and Kevin Carroll.
- Claimed to oppose radical Islam rather than race, while often drawing broader anti-Muslim chants and extremist participants.
- Organized early marches through Facebook when major social platforms were still new.
- Described as violent, disruptive, and capable of putting towns into lockdown.

## Evidence

- Origin: [[the-weekend-intelligence-shire-folk-6aa44797a6e9aef4ccfde9bc]] says Robinson and Carroll helped organize the angry response that later formed the EDL.
- Identity claim: [[the-weekend-intelligence-shire-folk-6aa44797a6e9aef4ccfde9bc]] says the EDL tried to distance itself from older far-right movements and present itself as anti-Islam rather than racist.
- Protest style: [[the-weekend-intelligence-shire-folk-6aa44797a6e9aef4ccfde9bc]] describes black balaclavas, black polo shirts, St George's crosses, and Facebook-organized marches.
- Violent and extremist pull: [[the-weekend-intelligence-shire-folk-6aa44797a6e9aef4ccfde9bc]] has Nick Lowles argue that many supporters chanted against Muslims generally, and the episode says EDL events attracted white nationalists, neo-Nazis, and ethno-nationalists.
