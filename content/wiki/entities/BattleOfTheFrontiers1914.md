# Battle of the Frontiers (1914)

## Overview

The Battle of the Frontiers was the failed French opening campaign of August 1914 in Alsace-Lorraine, the Ardennes, and Belgium that produced enormous losses, exposed the Allied left, and forced France to abandon its initial offensive strategy.

## Current Profile

[[595-the-first-world-war-the-battle-of-the-frontiers-part-2-glt5758313909]] presents the campaign as the decisive collapse of France's prewar opening assumptions. Joseph Joffre interpreted German strength on the flanks as evidence of a weak center, then sent the Third, Fourth, and Fifth Armies into attacks whose reconnaissance, clothing, weapons balance, and morale-centered doctrine were poorly matched to German machine guns, artillery, and prepared positions.

The disaster did not end French resistance. Charles Lanrezac's retreat preserved the Fifth Army despite exposing the neighboring [[BritishExpeditionaryForce1914|BEF]], while Joffre accepted that the offensive plan had failed and shifted toward retreat, re-equipment, defense of Paris, and eventual counterattack at the [[FirstBattleOfMarne|Marne]].

## Key Characteristics

- French offensive into Alsace-Lorraine, the Ardennes, and southern Belgium.
- Inadequate reconnaissance, including fog-limited visibility in the Ardennes.
- Severe mismatch between attacking formations and German defensive firepower.
- Exceptional French losses, including a source-reported 27,000 killed on 22 August.
- Command tension between Joffre's assumptions and Lanrezac's warnings about the German move through Belgium.
- Strategic failure followed by retreat and preservation rather than immediate French collapse.

## Evidence

- Offensive design: [[595-the-first-world-war-the-battle-of-the-frontiers-part-2-glt5758313909]] connects Plan XVII to attacks toward Alsace-Lorraine and through the Ardennes.
- Firepower mismatch: [[595-the-first-world-war-the-battle-of-the-frontiers-part-2-glt5758313909]] describes French troops advancing into German machine guns, howitzers, and prepared positions.
- Losses: [[595-the-first-world-war-the-battle-of-the-frontiers-part-2-glt5758313909]] reports 27,000 French dead on 22 August and 75,000 killed across two weeks.
- Strategic transition: [[595-the-first-world-war-the-battle-of-the-frontiers-part-2-glt5758313909]] follows Joffre from failed attack toward retreat, recovery, and defense of Paris.

## Qualifications

This profile follows one narrative podcast episode rather than a complete operational history. Its casualty totals, judgments about doctrine, Joffre and Lanrezac, and claims about English-language neglect of French sacrifice remain source-scoped. Bright uniforms and offensive rhetoric were visible liabilities, but they do not alone explain the outcome without force deployment, artillery, intelligence, terrain, and command.

## What Changed

- Created the opening-campaign bridge between the July Crisis and the existing Marne synthesis.
- Distinguished catastrophic strategic failure from the later preservation and recovery of the French army.
