# Robert-François Damiens

## Overview

Robert-François Damiens attempted to kill Louis XV in 1757 and was subjected to an exceptionally prolonged public execution that became an emblem of Ancien Régime penal spectacle.

## Current Profile

The episode uses Damiens less as a political actor than as a boundary case in punishment history. Burning, pincers, molten substances, emasculation, and attempted dismemberment by horses were meant to communicate sovereign vengeance and moral terror. Their extremity instead strengthened elite unease about spectacular cruelty and helps explain the later appeal of the swift mechanical reform associated with [[JosephIgnaceGuillotin|Guillotin]].

## Key Characteristics

- His crime was an attempted assassination of Louis XV.
- His punishment was deliberately public, exemplary, ritualized, and exceptionally painful.
- The execution exposed practical as well as moral limits of theatrical punishment.
- His case became part of the prehistory of humanitarian penal reform.
