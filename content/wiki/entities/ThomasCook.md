# Thomas Cook

## Overview

Thomas Cook is the organized-travel enterprise used in the episode as the long historical model for guided and intermediated tourism.

## Current Profile

The source follows the enterprise rather than supplying a full biography of its founder. It says Thomas Cook evacuated about 6,000 travelers stranded in Europe when the [[FirstWorldWar]] began while continuing tours to unaffected parts of France. The example shows that organized travel already depended on an intermediary able to coordinate transport, information, destinations, and disruption.

The episode then treats [[VladimirRaitz]]'s charter packages and the later travel-agent era as adaptations of this model. Travelers bought a managed chain of flight, accommodation, food, advice, representatives, and reassurance rather than assembling every component themselves. Thomas Cook Group's 2019 liquidation becomes a symbol of the model's weakening under direct digital comparison and booking, although the brand continued in successor form.

## Key Characteristics

- Long-lived British organizer and intermediary in mass travel.
- Combined itinerary coordination with reassurance in unfamiliar or disrupted conditions.
- Historical model for later package operators, agents, brochures, and destination representatives.
- Enterprise whose 2019 collapse symbolizes pressure on traditional travel intermediation.

## Evidence

- Wartime coordination: [[223-sun-sea-and-sex-glt5217352210]] attributes the 1914 evacuation of stranded travelers and continued Côte d'Azur tours to Thomas Cook.
- Business-model continuity: [[223-sun-sea-and-sex-glt5217352210]] compares postwar charter packages and 1970s travel agents with the earlier organized-tour model.
- Digital-era pressure: [[223-sun-sea-and-sex-glt5217352210]] uses the 2019 liquidation to close its account of the intermediary-dominated travel age.

## Qualifications

This profile covers the enterprise as an episode-level historical symbol, not its complete corporate history or the founder's life. Evacuation totals, operational detail, causation of the collapse, and the legal relationship between the liquidated group and successor brand remain source-scoped.
