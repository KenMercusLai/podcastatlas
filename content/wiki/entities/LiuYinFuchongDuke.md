## Overview

刘音 / Liu Yin enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-636-wangmang-dang-huangdi-hou-shenjing-cuoluan-le-lq-essrurb6jdefzjrfgoujw5uby|Hanji 636]] as the former Jiaodong king later recast by [[WangMang|王莽]] as Fuchong Duke, and as the Liu royal kinsman who refuses to join [[LiuKuaiXinRebel|刘快]]'s failed uprising.

## Current Profile

Liu Yin's current profile is a survival and appeasement case inside early [[XinDynasty|新朝]] rule. The episode says Wang Mang preserves certain privileges for Liu royal kin, and Liu Yin's conduct shows why that policy could matter: a former Han king has enough status to be dangerous, but also enough exposure to choose defensive compliance.

When Liu Kuai attacks Jimo, Liu Yin closes the city gates and imprisons himself. After the disturbance is suppressed, Wang Mang pardons him and enlarges his fief. The episode reads this as Liu Yin's practical wisdom and as evidence that Wang Mang can still reward compliant Liu-line figures.

## Key Characteristics

- Former Jiaodong king reclassified under Xin as Fuchong Duke.
- Liu royal kinsman positioned between old-dynasty identity and new-regime survival.
- Refuses to join Liu Kuai's uprising.
- Uses self-imprisonment as a visible distancing tactic during the attack on Jimo.
- Receives pardon and enlarged fief after the rebellion fails.
