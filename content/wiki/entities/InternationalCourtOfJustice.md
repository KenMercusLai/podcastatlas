# International Court of Justice

## Overview
The International Court of Justice is the United Nations' principal judicial body. This wiki page currently covers it through the episode-reported judgment that [[Myanmar]] committed genocide against the [[Rohingya]].

## Current Profile
The judgment gives survivors an authoritative legal finding, recognizes the community by its chosen name, and may shape later genocide litigation. Its practical reach is narrower: the court can order return and reparation but has no police or military force with which to implement those remedies amid continuing war, destroyed homes, and communal distrust. The source therefore presents the court as consequential for law and recognition without treating judgment as self-executing relief.

## Key Characteristics
- It adjudicates disputes between states rather than conducting criminal trials of individuals.
- Its Myanmar judgment is presented as the first finding by the United Nations' highest court that a state was responsible for genocide.
- Its use of the name “Rohingya” carried recognition value beyond the operative orders.
- Its rulings can influence legal doctrine and later cases even when immediate compliance is weak.
- It lacks an independent enforcement force.

## Qualifications

Legal recognition, doctrinal precedent, state compliance, safe return, and material reparation are separate outcomes.
