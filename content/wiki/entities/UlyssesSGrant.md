# Ulysses S. Grant

## Overview

Ulysses S. Grant is the Union commander whose Vicksburg victory divided the Confederacy along the Mississippi and who later accepted Robert E. Lee's surrender at Appomattox under lenient terms.

## Current Profile

Grant's bounded profile joins strategic victory to military settlement. The July 4, 1863 [[SiegeOfVicksburg|Vicksburg]] surrender gave the Union control of the Mississippi, cut the Confederacy in two, and paired with [[BattleOfGettysburg|Gettysburg]] to narrow Confederate possibilities. At Appomattox, allowing officers to retain sidearms and men to take horses supported immediate demobilization and reconciliation, while leaving open whether honorable treatment without a deeper reckoning eased later preservation of Confederate self-respect. [[202-american-civil-war-gettysburg-part-3-glt5256515778]] grounds the Vicksburg role, and [[203-american-civil-war-aftermath-legacy-part-4-glt3990109453]] grounds the surrender without reducing Reconstruction's failure to its terms.

## Key Characteristics

- Union commander central to the Confederate surrender at Appomattox.
- Campaign commander whose Vicksburg victory secured the Mississippi and divided Confederate territory.
- Figure linking the paired 1863 turning point to the war's later military conclusion.
- Negotiator associated with restrained and practical surrender terms.
- Actor in a settlement designed to end fighting rather than adjudicate the full meaning of rebellion.
- Figure whose military success did not itself determine Reconstruction's political outcome.

## Qualifications

This is a tightly bounded Vicksburg-and-Appomattox profile, not a full account of Grant's campaigns, presidency, Reconstruction enforcement, Native policy, or memoirs. The Vicksburg section does not reconstruct the campaign itself, and the suggestion that Appomattox leniency facilitated Lost Cause memory is interpretive rather than a simple causal chain.
