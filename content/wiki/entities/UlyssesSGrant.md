# Ulysses S. Grant

## Overview

Ulysses S. Grant is the Union commander who accepted Robert E. Lee's surrender at Appomattox under lenient terms intended to end organized fighting and allow Confederate soldiers to return home.

## Current Profile

[[203-american-civil-war-aftermath-legacy-part-4-glt3990109453]] uses Grant primarily as the Union actor in the Appomattox settlement. Allowing officers to retain sidearms and men to take horses supported immediate demobilization and reconciliation, but the episode asks whether honorable treatment without a deeper reckoning also eased the later preservation of Confederate self-respect.

The source does not reduce Reconstruction's failure to Grant's surrender terms. It presents Appomattox as a successful military settlement whose political and racial consequences remained open.

## Key Characteristics

- Union commander central to the Confederate surrender at Appomattox.
- Negotiator associated with restrained and practical surrender terms.
- Actor in a settlement designed to end fighting rather than adjudicate the full meaning of rebellion.
- Figure whose military success did not itself determine Reconstruction's political outcome.

## Qualifications

This is a tightly bounded Appomattox profile, not a full account of Grant's campaigns, presidency, Reconstruction enforcement, Native policy, or memoirs. The suggestion that leniency facilitated Lost Cause memory is interpretive and does not establish a simple causal chain from surrender terms to later rollback.
