# Mary II

## Overview

Mary II appears as the daughter of James II and joint monarch with [[WilliamIII]], whose 1689 coronation converted an unusual marital and dynastic arrangement into a public Protestant succession settlement.

## Current Profile

The source emphasizes the ceremony's constraints rather than Mary's wider reign. The coronation had to be organized quickly after the Glorious Revolution, and Mary reportedly used a less prestigious crown because time and resources were limited. That practical asymmetry sat inside a politically exceptional claim: husband and wife were crowned as joint monarchs rather than Mary serving only as consort.

Her profile therefore illustrates how coronation can absorb constitutional novelty without eliminating hierarchy or improvisation. Shared oath and sacred form presented joint rule as legitimate even though the material arrangements were unequal.

## Key Characteristics

- Protestant daughter of the displaced James II.
- Joint monarch rather than merely royal consort.
- Participant in a rapidly assembled coronation after dynastic replacement.
- Monarch whose less prestigious crown exposed practical inequality inside the shared ceremony.

## Qualifications

The source does not provide a full biography, explain Mary's decision-making during the Glorious Revolution, or assess her independent exercise of rule. The less prestigious crown is a ceremonial detail and should not by itself be treated as a complete measure of her constitutional power.
