## Overview

唐羌 / Tang Qiang enters the wiki as the Linwu county head whose memorial against fresh longan and lychee tribute makes [[PerishableTributeLaborBurden|perishable tribute labor burden]] visible in the [[EmperorHeOfHan|Emperor He]] period. His current role is narrow but important: he turns a court delicacy into a public-cost problem.

## Current Profile

Hanji 776 says Lingnan fresh longan and lychee were moved through dense relay arrangements, with posts at short intervals and day-night transport. Tang Qiang argues that rulers should not treat delicious food as virtue, and lower officials should not treat presenting rare food as merit. He describes dangerous heat, insects, wild terrain, and transport deaths across the Jiaozhi region, then asks the court to save future transporters because the dead cannot be revived.

Emperor He accepts the argument and orders the imperial kitchen not to receive this class of tribute. Tang Qiang's current profile is therefore a source-bounded example of local remonstrance converting luxury logistics into governance restraint.

## Key Characteristics

- Linwu local official whose current evidence is tied to one anti-tribute memorial.
- Remonstrator who evaluates court consumption through labor deaths and route danger.
- Practical burden-reduction actor whose advice leads Emperor He to stop accepting fresh tribute.
- Bridge between local administrative knowledge and central court restraint.

## Qualifications

Tang Qiang's page is source-bounded. The current evidence does not reconstruct his wider career, the full Jiaozhi tribute system, or the exact number of people harmed. It supports the narrower claim that a local official's memorial could turn perishable luxury supply into a central policy restraint.

## What Changed

- Created Tang Qiang as the canonical actor behind the Lingnan fresh-fruit tribute memorial.
- Connected his memorial to perishable tribute logistics and Emperor He's consumption restraint.
