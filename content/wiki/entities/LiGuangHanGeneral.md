## Overview

李广 / Li Guang is synthesized as the famous Western Han "Flying General" whose frontier reputation becomes both inheritance and burden for later family members. [[LiLingWesternHan|李陵]] is measured against his riding, archery, troop care, courage, and ill fortune, while [[LiGanWesternHan|李敢]] continues the family's combat reputation and reacts violently after Li Guang's suicide.

## Current Profile

Hanji 380-1 and 377-1 present Li Guang mainly through Li Ling. His fame and martial model shape the grandson's frontier identity, but the sources do not justify a genetic or fixed-destiny explanation. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-335-dangzhe-wudi-de-mian-huoqubing-weihe-gan-sha-liguang-zhi-zi-lupxhu64fi0d0xl223zq9pwenmy1|Hanji 335]] adds the first direct end-of-life layer: after Li Guang misses a movement during a campaign under [[WeiQingHanGeneral|卫青]], Wei's office asks why he failed to arrive. The episode says Li Guang refuses the humiliation of answering subordinate clerks after a long career and kills himself. Li Gan then blames Wei Qing, making the father's death the grievance behind a second elite conflict.

## Key Characteristics

- Famous Western Han general remembered as the "Flying General."
- Grandfather and martial reputation model of Li Ling.
- Father of Li Gan and source of the grievance that leads Li Gan to assault Wei Qing.
- Long-serving but unenfeoffed commander whose final campaign ends in missed movement, inquiry, and suicide.

## Evidence

Family reputation:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-380-1-liling-touxiang-xiongnu-ruhe-yanbian-cheng-gengdade-beiju-ls70jnzybc7z3of-tpfbdwb9ns89|Hanji 380-1]] identifies Li Ling as Li Guang's grandson and frames Li Guang as a famed general.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-377-1-liling-qingzhan-xiongnu-hanwudi-weihe-buyuan-ta-dang-qianfeng-lttki-nkmyzvt3q1wxomv0-hpmmk|Hanji 377-1]] compares Li Ling's riding, archery, treatment of soldiers, desire for battle, courage, and ill fortune with Li Guang.

Final campaign and death:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-335-dangzhe-wudi-de-mian-huoqubing-weihe-gan-sha-liguang-zhi-zi-lupxhu64fi0d0xl223zq9pwenmy1|Hanji 335]] says Li Guang becomes lost, misses the planned encirclement, and dies by suicide rather than answer an inquiry conducted through junior officials.

Intergenerational consequence:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-335-dangzhe-wudi-de-mian-huoqubing-weihe-gan-sha-liguang-zhi-zi-lupxhu64fi0d0xl223zq9pwenmy1|Hanji 335]] says Li Gan treats Wei Qing as responsible and assaults him.

## Qualifications

These sources still do not reconstruct Li Guang's complete campaigns, Shiji portrayal, reasons for never receiving a marquisate, or wider literary reception. Hanji 335 compresses the missed movement, inquiry, and suicide and does not establish that Wei Qing intended humiliation or bears sole responsibility. Hanji 377-1's genetics-and-fate opening remains a narrative device rather than evidence of biological destiny.

## What Changed

- Added Li Guang's final missed movement, inquiry, and suicide as a source-scoped end-of-life account.
- Connected his death to Li Gan's later assault without treating Wei Qing's culpability as settled.

## Relationships

- [[LiLingWesternHan|李陵]] - grandson whose frontier service is framed against Li Guang's reputation.
- [[LiGanWesternHan|李敢]] - son whose grief turns Li Guang's death into an elite grievance.
- [[WeiQingHanGeneral|卫青]] - campaign commander whose post-movement inquiry Li Gan treats as causally blameworthy.
- [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] - frontier opponent around which the family's military reputation forms.
- [[CommandSynchronizationFailure|军令同步失灵]] - adjacent command problem in the Li Ling branch of the family story.
