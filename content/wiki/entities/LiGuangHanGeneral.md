## Overview

李广 / Li Guang enters the wiki as the famous Western Han "Flying General" whose family reputation frames [[LiLingWesternHan|李陵]]'s later tragedy. The sources do not reconstruct Li Guang's campaigns, but they use him as Li Ling's grandfather and as the inherited model of riding, archery, troop care, repeated requests for battle, courage, and ill fortune behind Li Ling's active anti-[[Xiongnu|匈奴]] service.

## Current Profile

Hanji 380-1 presents Li Guang as a reputational ancestor rather than as a direct actor. Hanji 377-1 strengthens the comparison by saying Li Ling resembles him in military ability, treatment of soldiers and worthy people, desire to fight, courage, and a host-framed pattern of poor fortune. The durable point is lineage pressure and emulation, not the episode's rhetorical suggestion that genes fix personality or destiny.

## Key Characteristics

- Famous Western Han general remembered as "Flying General."
- Grandfather of Li Ling in the episode's account.
- Functions as inherited military reputation and a behavioral model behind Li Ling's frontier service.

## Qualifications

These two sources do not reconstruct Li Guang's full biography, campaigns, death, Shiji portrayal, or later literary reception. Hanji 377-1's genetics-and-fate opening is a narrative device and does not establish biological inheritance of character or destiny.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 377-1's more specific comparison between Li Guang and Li Ling while rejecting fixed genetic destiny as a historical conclusion.
