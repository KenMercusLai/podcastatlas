## Overview
Janet Mertz is presented by the source as the Stanford graduate student whose proposed recombinant-DNA experiment helped trigger an early safety dispute and whose lost research lead exposes the unequal costs of collective restraint.

## Current Profile
Mertz occupies two roles in the episode. As a researcher, she was close to cloning tumor-virus DNA in *E. coli* when concerns about laboratory escape led [[PaulBerg]] to stop the work. As a retrospective witness, she acknowledges that leaks can happen and judges the moratorium worthwhile, while also saying that it damaged her career and cost her a likely scientific first. Her account keeps public benefit and individual sacrifice in view at the same time.

## Key Characteristics
- Early-career molecular biologist working at the frontier of recombinant-DNA research.
- Direct bearer of the opportunity cost created by a field-wide safety pause.
- Source of a qualified pro-moratorium judgment rather than a simple account of scientific consensus.
- Example of how junior researchers can bear decisions made in forums from which they are excluded.
