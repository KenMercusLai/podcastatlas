## Overview

王成 / Wang Cheng enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-419-nongxu-zuojia-de-hanchen-wangcheng-ruhe-ba-hanxuandi-pian-de-tuantuanzhuan-lim7jqyvestgoqb68pxhyuwiuhyd|Hanji 419]] as the Jiaodong xiang whose reported local achievements won [[EmperorXuanOfHan|汉宣帝]]'s praise before later review exposed the report as false. This page uses a Western Han semantic suffix to distinguish him from [[WangChengLiGuStudent|王成]], the later Li Gu student.

## Current Profile

Hanji 419 presents Wang Cheng as a compact case of official-performance fraud. In March 67 BCE, Xuan publicly praises him for local prestige and for supposedly bringing back more than 80,000 displaced people, grants him Guannei Hou rank, and gives the attached stipend. Wang Cheng dies before further reuse, but a later court review of commandery and kingdom administration finds that his reported achievements were inflated to deceive the ruler.

The episode's interpretation makes Wang Cheng important less for a long biography than for the information-system failure around him. Qin-Han assessment through 考课 and 上计 made written performance consequential for promotion and reward. Once the emperor had publicly certified Wang Cheng as a good official, officials who knew more had reason to avoid embarrassing the ruler, allowing false merit to damage bureaucratic norms even after the individual case ended.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han Jiaodong xiang remembered in this source for fabricated local-performance reporting.
- Recipient of an imperial commendation, Guannei Hou rank, and stipend after claiming major return of displaced people.
- Exposed only after later administrative review, not during the initial reward process.
- Case showing how reported achievement can outrun ground truth when central assessment depends on documents.
- Negative example whose false merit is read as damaging official culture beyond his own career.

## Qualifications

This page is source-scoped to Hanji 419. It does not reconstruct Wang Cheng's full biography, the original Jiaodong population records, the precise administrative review document, or the exact knowledge held by court officials before the exposure. The host's claim that officials stayed silent to avoid embarrassing Xuan is preserved as an interpretation of the incentive environment.
