# Erica Chenoweth

## Overview

Erica Chenoweth is the political scientist whose comparative research anchors the episode's case that disciplined nonviolent resistance often outperforms armed struggle.

## Current Profile

Chenoweth entered the research with an interest in military history and terrorism and an expectation that violent movements with sufficient capacity would prove more effective. After [[MariaStephan|Maria Stephan]] challenged her use of selected successes and failures, they assembled comparable campaigns seeking government overthrow or national independence and imposed a demanding success test.

The resulting episode-level profile is methodological as well as substantive. Chenoweth moves from anecdote toward comparison, then explains the finding through participation, connections to [[PillarsOfSupport]], tactical innovation, and organizational discipline. She treats the [[MovementParticipationThreshold|3.5 percent pattern]] as a rule of thumb and uses Sudan to distinguish near-term campaign success from durable democratic consolidation.

## Key Characteristics

- Political scientist studying civil resistance, political violence, and campaign outcomes.
- Revised an initial belief in violent effectiveness after comparative testing.
- Emphasizes mechanisms rather than treating nonviolence as moral persuasion alone.
- Preserves uncertainty around campaign coding, thresholds, and long-term outcomes.
