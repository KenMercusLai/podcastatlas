# YES program

## Overview

YES is the educational program founded by [[EricEisner]] in [[carlos-doesn-t-remember-carlos-doesn-t-remember|Carlos Doesn’t Remember]]. The episode describes it as identifying gifted Los Angeles public-school students, tutoring them, and using continuing advocacy and relationships to place them in private schools.

## Current Profile

The program begins earlier than conventional selective-college recruitment and treats opportunity as a chain rather than a single admission decision. Academic recognition is paired with tutoring, placement, material attention, and long-term adult involvement. [[CarlosStudent|Carlos]] illustrates both the value and fragility of that model: YES helps him enter private education, but family disruption and foster placement can still sever the relationship and close an otherwise funded opportunity.

The episode treats YES as a practical counterexample to the belief that schools need only open their doors. At the same time, the program's reliance on Eisner's judgment, persistence, and social connections raises a scale problem. A fair system cannot assume that every disadvantaged child will encounter an unusually committed and well-connected advocate.

## Key Characteristics

- Identifies academically gifted students in under-resourced public schools.
- Begins support in childhood rather than waiting for college application season.
- Combines tutoring with private-school placement and continuing advocacy.
- Responds to material and family constraints as well as academic preparation.
- Relies substantially on personal relationships and individualized follow-through.

## Qualifications

The source does not expand the YES acronym or provide a formal program history, legal identity, budget, cohort size, admissions criteria, governance, or comparative outcome data. The profile therefore uses the name exactly as supplied and treats claims about effectiveness as episode-scoped.

## What Changed

- Established YES as an early, continuing, whole-context educational intervention.
- Distinguished individualized advocacy from a scalable entitlement to support.
