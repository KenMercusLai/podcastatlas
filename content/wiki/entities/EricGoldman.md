# Eric Goldman

## Overview
Eric Goldman is a technology-law expert associated in the sources with [[SantaClaraUniversity]]'s [[HighTechLawInstitute|High Tech Law Institute]], explaining how older doctrines, statutory categories, and liability theories affect modern internet services and users.

## Current Profile
Across three [[MarketplaceTech]] episodes, Goldman treats internet litigation as a translation problem with product-wide consequences. In social-media cases, plaintiffs must establish [[PlatformLegalCausation]] and defend design-based claims against [[Section230]] and First Amendment arguments. In video-privacy litigation, courts must decide whether the 1988 [[VideoPrivacyProtectionAct]] reaches analytics and targeted advertising on modern sites. His recurring concern is [[InternetLiabilitySpillover]]: uncertain or expansive rules can reshape design, pricing, access, communication, consent, and litigation across services beyond the original defendant or technology.

## Key Characteristics
- Focuses on legal causation and statutory fit rather than treating documented concern as sufficient liability proof.
- Distinguishes jury acceptance or lower-court survival from a final rule after appeal.
- Tracks how design-versus-content theories interact with Section 230 and platform speech defenses.
- Warns that statutory damages and reusable legal theories can spread liability across internet categories.
- Frames compliance consequences from the user's side, including functionality, access, pricing, consent friction, and agent restrictions.

## Qualifications
The pages synthesize Goldman's commentary rather than adopting his predictions as court holdings. The sources do not provide full records, briefing, statutory text, or opposing expert analysis. His warnings identify plausible spillover and compliance effects, not measured forecasts that every adverse ruling will remove services or generate the maximum litigation described.
