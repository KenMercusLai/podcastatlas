# Carl Jung / 荣格

[[CarlJung|Carl Jung / 荣格]] enters the wiki through [[07-yong-rongge-lilun-fenxi-xiaowangzi-yongheng-shaonian-ji-qita-539091165]], where [[MihuanChishu|蜜獾吃书]] uses Jungian analytical psychology as a literary-reading method for [[TheLittlePrince|《小王子》]]. The episode emphasizes collective unconscious, recurring mythic images, child-god figures, and symbolic interpretation rather than presenting a full biography of Jung.

In the source, Jung matters because his framework makes the little prince readable as more than a charming child. Snakes, foxes, roses, baobab trees, sheep, volcanoes, wells, feet, and desert scenes become possible psychic or mythic signs. The hosts also treat this method cautiously: it can open the text, but it can also become [[InterpretationAndOverinterpretation]] if every resemblance is treated as proof.

[[20-demian-gaiyin-de-etou-shang-na-shenmi-de-yinji-567302734]] adds a [[HermannHesse|Hesse]] extension. The episode links Hesse's World War I psychological crisis, analysis by Jung's circle, and [[DemianNovel|《德米安》]]'s figures to collective unconscious, anima-like imagery, and [[SpiritualMentorAsSelfProjection]]. [[MaxDemian|德米安]] and [[FrauEva|夏娃夫人]] therefore become examples of how a character can function as both plot person and inner guide.

## Key Claims
- Jungian reading is useful here because it lets a children's classic be read through recurring image patterns rather than only plot or quotation.
- The source uses Jung primarily through later Jungian analyst [[MarieLouiseVonFranz|Marie-Louise von Franz / 玛丽-路易丝·冯·弗兰兹]] and her account of [[PuerAeternus|永恒少年]].
- The episode keeps Jungian psychology as a heuristic literary lens, not a diagnostic or scientific demonstration.
