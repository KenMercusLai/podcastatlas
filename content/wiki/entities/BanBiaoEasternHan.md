## Overview
班彪 / Ban Biao is synthesized as an Eastern Han historian and frontier-policy adviser whose profile begins with Han-loyalist survival choices during [[WangMang|Wang Mang]]-era collapse and now includes the immediate pre-`王命论` debate with [[WeiXiaoEasternHan|Wei Xiao]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-693-panchu-liuxiu-de-pengchong-ruhe-yin-beipan-er-wang-lqler4fu9wqztsrgzsxhs1519nm]] shows Ban Biao arguing that Han restoration is institutionally different from late Zhou fragmentation because Han inherited Qin commandery-county administration and people still attach authority to the Liu name. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-694-wangminglun-jielu-donghan-shenme-yinmi-lvsrsevrkj9ukcth6qbalnt6buf8|Hanji 694]] then makes `王命论` a direct warning to Wei Xiao: imperial title should not be pursued through local strength, cleverness, or opportunity when mandate, historical basis, and people-support are not present. Later sources extend the same political mind into Dou Rong persuasion, Qiang and Wuhuan institution design, and Northern Xiongnu diplomatic drafting.

## Current Profile
Hanji 693 supplies the pre-text advisory scene. Wei Xiao compares the post-Wang Mang world to late Zhou and asks whether vertical and horizontal alliance politics might return, leaving room for a new recipient of the mandate. Ban Biao answers with institutional history: Zhou's feudal lords had hereditary territorial bases, while Han inherited Qin's commandery-county order, so current strongmen lack six-state hereditary foundations. He also argues that after Wang Mang's top-down usurpation and the ensuing disorder, the people remain habituated to Liu-Han authority. Wei Xiao does not accept the point, prompting Ban Biao to prepare a written argument.

Hanji 694 supplies the direct `王命论` warning layer. Ban Biao writes to Wei Xiao in Jianwu 5 because Wei Xiao wants more than survival and begins imagining kingship or a contest for the realm. The argument joins [[MandateOfHeavenLegitimacy|Mandate of Heaven legitimacy]], Liu-family continuity, Liu Bang's talent-absorbing founding capacity, and failed claimant precedents to say imperial authority is not a prize that any fast or forceful actor can seize. When Wei Xiao does not accept the counsel, Ban Biao leaves him, moves to Hexi, and becomes [[DouRongEasternHan|Dou Rong]]'s adviser.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-705-2-banchao-lishishang-toubicongrong-diyiren-luo6hyqkruwnblmse4ju7cxpb7tb|Hanji 705-2]] sharpens that biography into a sequence of restoration-era judgment. Ban Biao is presented as a Fufeng Anling elite whose family flees Guanzhong disorder, joins Wei Xiao in Tianshui, and tries to prevent Wei Xiao from overreading local strength against the Han restoration. His `王命论` argues that Han legitimacy has not ended; after leaving Wei Xiao for Dou Rong, `北征赋` records the northern journey and Dou Rong's respectful treatment. The source then ties Ban Biao's historical continuations after `Shiji` to [[BanGuEasternHan|Ban Gu]]'s `Han Shu` and [[BanChaoEasternHan|Ban Chao]]'s later non-literary military path.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-708-jizun-bingshi-weihe-yinfa-baiguan-jingkong-lhfuvjyiiirlaf-ugrufkaspuauq|Hanji 708]] supplies an early Ban Biao policy layer. Ban argues that Qiang unrest cannot be solved as a simple military issue because Qiang and Han customs and language differ, dependence on Han authority is weak, and local official abuse can generate grievance. His remedy is institutional and informational: restore a frontier office, inspect commanderies, use interpreters, and gather reliable knowledge before acting.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-726-ma-ge-guo-shi-de-ma-yuan-weihe-mengyuan-buneng-shuo-de-mimi-lpxrkrx2uffdzpyctiwwcaanfzbu|Hanji 726]] gives the broader family and office frame. It identifies him as Ban Gu's father, from a Confucian family, displaced during Wang Mang turmoil, once attached to Wei Xiao, and later useful in persuading Dou Rong and the northwest faction toward Liu Xiu. It also says Ban Biao later enters court, withdraws because of illness, concentrates on history, begins work on Han Shu, and leaves that work to be continued by Ban Gu and [[BanZhaoLateHan|Ban Zhao]]. The same source makes Ban Biao a frontier-institution thinker: after Hao Dan and other [[Wuhuan|Wuhuan]] leaders submit, he warns that weak surrender officials cannot control Wuhuan volatility and recommends restoring the Wuhuan protector office at Ningcheng in Shanggu to manage rewards, hostages, and seasonal border trade involving Wuhuan and [[Xianbei|Xianbei]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-729-bangu-tadie-jing-yeshi-ge-da-wenhao-loadjkevhjie2yur8u23ox2-gmce|Hanji 729]] then shows Ban Biao's diplomatic drafting ability. When [[NorthernXiongnuLateHan|Northern Xiongnu]] sends tribute, requests heqin, asks for music, and wants to bring Western Regions envoys to court, Ban reads the approach as fear after [[SouthernXiongnu|Southern Xiongnu]] submission. He recommends calibrated rewards, careful praise, selective refusal, and noncommittal wording rather than rupture or credulous alliance.

## Key Characteristics
- Ban-family historian whose profile includes Fufeng lineage, Wang Mang-era displacement, Wei Xiao warning, Dou Rong persuasion, and Han Shu succession.
- Institutional-historical analyst who distinguishes late Zhou feudal fragmentation from Han commandery-county continuity.
- Han-loyalist restoration reader who turns mandate, historical precedent, failed claimant cases, and Liu Bang's founding capacity into title-restraint advice for Wei Xiao.
- Qiang-frontier analyst who treats unrest as a communication, custom, language, and official-abuse problem.
- Adviser who treats submitted Wuhuan as an institutional management problem after initial recognition and provisioning.
- Diplomatic drafter who reads Northern Xiongnu tribute as weakness after Southern Xiongnu submission.
- Policy adviser whose proposals are adopted or vindicated across Dou Rong's submission, Wuhuan office restoration, and Northern Xiongnu reply drafting.

## Qualifications
This page remains bounded to the listed source notes. Hanji 693 is a prelude to `王命论`, not the full essay; it is valuable because it preserves the institutional argument that makes the later warning more than generic Han loyalty. Hanji 694 deepens `王命论` as political counsel, but the episode still represents the text through excerpting and host explanation rather than a full independent literary analysis. Hanji 705-2 deepens Ban Biao's restoration-era biography, but `北征赋`, exact family prestige claims, and the count of later biographies remain source-scoped unless fuller literary-history notes are added. Hanji 708 supports Ban Biao as a Qiang-frontier diagnostician, but it does not show whether the proposed office, inspections, interpreters, or reconnaissance fully solved Qiang unrest. Hanji 726 gives a compact biography and Han Shu lineage but not a full literary history. Its Wuhuan institutional branch also does not prove that the office solved all later Wuhuan volatility. Hanji 729 supports Ban Biao as a precise frontier-policy drafter, but it does not by itself reconstruct every Northern Xiongnu negotiation after Liu Xiu.
