# Lauren Colenso-Semple

## Overview
Lauren Colenso-Semple is the exercise scientist interviewed by [[AndrewHuberman]] in a [[HubermanLab]] episode about women's resistance training, cardio, nutrition, reproductive-hormone context, menopause, supplements, and recovery.

## Current Profile
In the current source, Colenso-Semple's defining stance is that women should not be given a separate exercise rulebook unless outcome evidence supports it. She distinguishes average baseline differences, individual symptoms, contraception, and life stage from claims that women universally need different repetitions, intensities, nutrient timing, or menstrual-phase programming.

Her practical approach is deliberately conventional: progressive resistance training, challenging sets, broad workable repetition ranges, sustainable scheduling, adequate nutrition, and recovery. She also applies an evidence-hierarchy lens to creatine, cortisol, fasted training, hormone therapy, and online fitness claims, treating mechanistic plausibility or one study as weaker than convergent longer-term outcomes.

## Key Characteristics
- Emphasizes broad similarity in male and female training adaptation while preserving baseline and individual differences.
- Rejects automatic menstrual-cycle syncing in favor of symptom- and readiness-led adjustment.
- Uses progressive resistance, sufficient effort, and sustainable frequency as the programming foundation.
- Separates acute hormone changes and mechanistic stories from demonstrated long-term outcomes.
- Treats menopause exercise as important for muscle, bone, and function without making hormone status a wholly different training goal.
