# Cato the Elder / 老加图

## Overview

Cato the Elder / 老加图 is the Roman senator who turned Carthage's renewed prosperity into a sustained political argument that [[Carthage]] must be destroyed.

## Current Profile

After visiting Carthage in 152 BC, Cato interpreted its grain, wealth, upgraded harbors, and dockyard capacity as evidence that defeat had not made the city safe. The fresh fig he reportedly displayed in the Senate dramatized geographic proximity, while his recurring destruction formula made annihilation a standing answer to matters beyond Carthaginian policy itself.

The episode places Cato against [[Polybius]] and Scipio Nasica, who represented more sympathetic or preservationist positions. Cato's campaign joins strategic fear to [[RomanEnemyMemory]]: material recovery becomes proof of possible renewed threat because Hannibalic devastation remains politically alive. Calling the policy genocidal may illuminate its annihilatory aim, but the episode treats the modern category as anachronistic and does not reduce Roman motives to Cato alone.

## Key Characteristics

- Senatorial advocate of Carthage's destruction.
- Interprets commercial and infrastructural recovery as renewed military danger.
- Uses vivid repetition and the fresh-fig story to make distance and threat politically immediate.
- Represents the annihilation side of a real Roman elite debate.
