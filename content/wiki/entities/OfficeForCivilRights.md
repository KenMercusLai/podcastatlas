# Office for Civil Rights

## Overview

The Office for Civil Rights is the [[DepartmentOfEducation]] unit discussed as the federal overseer of school and university sexual-harassment and violence cases under [[TitleIX]].

## Current Profile

The episode presents the office as both the source of changing administrative guidance and a constrained enforcement institution. Its interpretation helps determine university procedure, yet a former office lawyer reports a post-2020 fall in completed investigations, and the source says the office reached no resolution agreements in this case category during 2025 despite more than 1,100 pending complaints.

## Key Characteristics

- Enforces federal education civil-rights obligations.
- Shapes campus procedure through administrative interpretation.
- Operates within rules that change across presidential administrations.
- Faces a large unresolved complaint inventory in the episode's account.
- Supplies oversight distinct from university, civil, criminal, and state processes.

## Evidence

- Rule effects: [[gilets-jeunes-frances-school-protests-2959cbdca16de39a43a75666b5bc7057]] cites a former office lawyer saying completed campus-sexual-violence investigations fell sharply after the 2020 rule.
- Enforcement capacity: [[gilets-jeunes-frances-school-protests-2959cbdca16de39a43a75666b5bc7057]] reports more than 1,100 pending harassment and violence complaints and no resolution agreements in 2025.

## Qualifications

Pending volume and absence of resolution agreements do not alone establish the reason for delay or the quality of every investigation. The source does not provide the full administrative record, staffing history, case mix, or outcome definitions needed for a complete performance judgment.
