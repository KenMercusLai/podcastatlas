# White House Plumbers

## Overview

The White House Plumbers were a Nixon-administration leak-control unit whose illegal methods and personnel formed an operational bridge between the [[PentagonPapers]] response and the [[WatergateScandal]].

## Current Profile

The episode presents the unit as a product of genuine leak anxiety intensified by [[RichardNixon|Richard Nixon]]'s defensive political style. Its name referred to plugging leaks, but its work crossed into burglary when operatives entered [[DanielEllsberg|Daniel Ellsberg]]'s psychiatrist's office looking for material that could discredit him. [[HowardHunt]] and [[GordonLiddy]] then carried the same permissive culture into reelection-campaign dirty tricks.

## Key Characteristics

- Created to identify or deter unauthorized disclosures.
- Operated within a White House climate of suspicion and aggressive demands.
- Used illegal entry in an effort to discredit Ellsberg.
- Included Hunt and Liddy, who later designed campaign operations.
- Prefigured Watergate in method, personnel, and disregard for legal boundaries.

The source does not reconstruct the unit's complete authorization chain or establish everything Nixon knew about particular operations. It supports broad awareness of aggressive action while keeping detailed presidential knowledge uncertain.

## What Changed

- Established the plumbers as the organizational bridge between leak retaliation and campaign burglary.
