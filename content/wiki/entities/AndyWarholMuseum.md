# Andy Warhol Museum

## Overview

The Andy Warhol Museum is the institution through which [[dragon-psychology-101-dragon-psychology-101|Dragon Psychology 101]] tests how an artist's broad accumulation can become an equally broad archival preservation practice.

## Current Profile

The episode reports that the museum holds at least 500,000 archival objects, including 610 time capsules assembled and sealed by [[AndyWarhol]]. Staff preserve not only recognizable artworks or records but original boxes, arrangement, paper clips, purchased images, photographs, invitations, scrapbooks, and the clothing and personal effects returned after Warhol's death.

This care makes context and association part of the object. It also creates a selection problem: if an apparently mundane item may illuminate Warhol's life, practice, or memory, discarding it risks destroying evidence before its significance is known. The episode interprets that caution through [[ObjectMediatedMemoryAndIdentity]] and questions whether personal accumulation has been inherited as institutional reluctance to discriminate among holdings.

## Key Characteristics

- Single-artist museum and archive centered on Andy Warhol.
- Holder of a very large archival collection and hundreds of time capsules.
- Preserver of containers, arrangement, ephemera, and personal effects as contextual evidence.
- Case in which association with an artist can confer archival significance on mundane objects.
- Institution used to test the boundary between preservation and indiscriminate retention.

## Evidence

### Archive scale and time capsules

- [[dragon-psychology-101-dragon-psychology-101]] reports at least 500,000 archival objects and 610 time capsules.

### Contextual preservation

- [[dragon-psychology-101-dragon-psychology-101]] describes conservation of original boxes, object arrangement, paper clips, ephemera, and hospital-returned effects.

### Inherited selection problem

- [[dragon-psychology-101-dragon-psychology-101]] argues that Warhol's indiscriminate accumulation became the input to a museum reluctant to discard any associated material.

## Qualifications

The source does not provide the museum's acquisition policy, appraisal criteria, finding aids, research use, public access statistics, conservation priorities, donor agreements, or response. Preserving ordinary material in a single-artist archive can serve legitimate documentary and scholarly purposes; the episode's hoarding comparison does not establish that the collection is unmanaged or valueless.
