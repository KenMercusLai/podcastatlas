## Overview

The Galactic Empire is Star Wars's oppressive political order, represented through both the collapse of republican institutions and the everyday machinery of imperial rule.

## Current Profile

At saga scale, the Empire emerges from an Old Republic corrupted from within. The Senate persists temporarily, then is dissolved as the Death Star lets regional governors rule through fear. Palpatine's emergency powers and claimed defense of democracy connect the transition to Roman dictatorship, Augustus's modest presentation of concentrated power, Nazi destruction of Weimar, and American fears that republics can surrender liberty through corruption, elite weakness, and public consent.

At operational scale, [[Andor|Andor]] makes the Empire concrete through police incentives, intelligence services, prisons, forced labor, surveillance, cultural replacement, public spectacle, and functionaries ranging from lazy to zealous. Aldhani, Narkina Five, and Ferrix show that domination relies on institutions and local compliance while also generating ordinary resistance.

## Key Characteristics

- Successor to a republic hollowed out before representative institutions are formally abolished.
- Ruler-centered order legitimized through emergency, security, and claimed institutional continuity.
- Bureaucratic state operating through surveillance, policing, prisons, labor, spectacle, and regional control.
- Historically composite fictional regime drawing on Roman, Nazi, Soviet, British imperial, and American political anxieties.
- Coercive order whose everyday practices can generate community and prisoner resistance.

## Qualifications

The Empire is not a one-to-one representation of Rome, Nazi Germany, the Soviet Union, British imperialism, or Nixon-era America. Those traditions overlap in the sources' readings. Andor provides a richer bureaucratic profile than the original film, so not every characteristic is equally explicit across the franchise.
