# National Football League

[[the-nfl-1]] makes the National Football League the main case rather than only a comparison. The [[Acquired]] episode explains the NFL as a [[LeagueFirstOperatingModel]] built from [[SportsCompetitiveBalance]], pooled [[SportsMediaRights]], national storytelling, the [[SportsBroadcastingAct]], [[NFLFilms]], the [[SuperBowl]], [[MondayNightFootball]], and owner willingness to share enough economics to grow the total league.

The National Football League appears in [[how-we-got-free-agents-in-baseball]] as a comparison case for the spread and limits of professional-sports free agency. The source says similar challenges were happening in football after [[CurtFlood|Curt Flood]]'s baseball case, but that NFL free agency remained more limited until the 1990s.

The NFL also supplies the episode's salary-cap comparison: unlike [[MajorLeagueBaseball|Major League Baseball]], the NFL uses a salary cap while still allocating around half of revenue to player salaries, according to the source. [[the-nfl-1]] extends that point by tying the 1993 free-agency and salary-cap settlement to the league's broader bargain among players, owners, competitive balance, and media growth.

[[e237-yangshi-he-fifa-tanpan-fenzheng-beihou-tiyu-saishi-zhuanboquan-de-boyi-yu-shengyi-c3607c08-6eb6-48bd-8250-b41b5b8272e6]] adds the NFL as a top U.S. [[SportsMediaRights]] benchmark. The episode contrasts China's post-bubble rights market with the NFL's much larger current rights cycle, using the league to show how mature paid viewing habits and streaming competition can keep elite rights prices rising.

The Acquired source updates the modern side of that rights thesis through [[Amazon]], [[YouTubeTV|YouTube TV]], [[Netflix]], and [[ESPN]], while also identifying long-term pressures from [[FootballPlayerSafetyTrustRisk]], [[ColinKaepernick]], local revenue disparity, college-football disruption, betting, international expansion, and [[PrivateEquityInSportsOwnership]].
