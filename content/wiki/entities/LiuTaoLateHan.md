## Overview

刘陶 / 刘桃 is a late-Han remonstrant whose current wiki profile begins in the [[Taixue|太学]] field under [[EmperorHuanOfHan|汉桓帝]] and ends in the eunuch-filtered prison case under [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]]. Hanji 839 first shows him leading thousands of Taixue students to defend [[ZhuMuLateHan|朱穆]] against punishment after an anti-eunuch-family enforcement case. Hanji 840 then shows him using disasters, famine, Qin precedent, and eunuch harm to warn Huan; Hanji 843 shows him rejecting a coinage explanation for peasant poverty. Later Ling-era notes show him warning about Yellow Turban and western rebellion pressure, being vindicated too late, and dying after crisis reporting is recoded as suspicious knowledge.

## Current Profile

Liu Tao's profile is a repeated-warning arc rather than a stable office biography. The earliest current layer, Hanji 839, makes him a Taixue public-feedback actor. He leads a mass petition for Zhu Mu, argues that Zhu enforced law for commoners against eunuch kinship privilege, and offers to accept punishment in Zhu's place. The petition helps secure Zhu's pardon, but it does not solve the court's eunuch-power problem.

The next Huan-era layers move from collective petition into memorial diagnosis. Hanji 840 has Liu connect disasters, hunger, imperial inexperience, eunuch abuse, Qin's collapse, and the need for upright officials such as Zhu Mu and [[LiYingLateHan|李膺]]. The memorial reaches the throne but does not move Huan. Hanji 843 then gives a more policy-specific version: commoners need grain, clothing, reduced extraction, and recovery time more urgently than heavier coinage.

In the Ling-era layer, Liu Tao and [[YangCiLateHan|杨赐]] warn that [[ZhangJiaoLateHan|张角]]'s followers are secretly linking toward [[LuoyangLateHan|洛阳]]; after the [[YellowTurbansLateHan|Yellow Turbans]] erupt, the court belatedly rewards their accuracy. Hanji 935 then makes the pattern fatal: Liu Tao warns that western rebels under [[BianZhangLateHan|边章]] and [[HanSui|韩遂]] now threaten the state, but the [[TenAttendantsLateHan|十常侍]] turn his knowledge into evidence against him and he dies in prison.

## Key Characteristics

- Liu Tao uses Taixue collectivity to turn one official's punishment into a ruler-facing public-law problem.
- Liu Tao treats disasters and omens as political-responsibility signals rather than isolated natural events.
- He tests policy diagnosis against household subsistence: food and clothing outrank coin design when people are hungry, unclothed, and over-extracted.
- He treats unrest as a root-cause problem, linking poverty, disease, frontier pressure, hidden organization, eunuch abuse, and official concealment before force alone can solve it.
- His warnings reach the throne, but ruler conversion is unstable: Hanji 840 and Hanji 923 show non-action, Hanji 843 shows acceptance, and Hanji 935 shows punitive reinterpretation.
- His delayed vindication after the Yellow Turban outbreak shows the court can recognize accurate warning only after events make denial impossible.

## Qualifications

The wiki preserves both 刘陶 and 刘桃 because the ingested source notes use variant forms for the same remonstrant route. The profile is still bounded to the listed podcast source notes rather than a full official biography. Hanji 839 is earlier than Hanji 840 but appears last in the frontmatter because provenance order is preserved. The ignored Huan-era warnings, the accepted anti-coinage advice, the ignored and belatedly vindicated Ling-era warnings, and the fatal accusation belong to different ruler-side contexts and should be read as a sequence, not as a contradiction.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 839's Taixue collective petition for Zhu Mu as Liu Tao's earliest current evidence.
- Reframed Liu Tao's Huan-era role as collective public-law defense, systemic remonstrance, and practical subsistence diagnosis.
- Preserved the later ignored, belatedly vindicated, and lethal Ling-era warning sequence.
