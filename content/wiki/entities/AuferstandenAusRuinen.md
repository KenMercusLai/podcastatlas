# Auferstanden aus Ruinen

## Overview

Auferstanden aus Ruinen appears as East Germany's anthem and the major rival German anthem tradition in the episode.

## Current Profile

The episode presents the anthem as musically and politically important because it was not an aggressively communist text. Written by Johannes Becher with a tune by Hanns Eisler, it referred to a united German fatherland, fitting East Germany's early claim to represent all Germany. That same unity language later became awkward after German division hardened, leading authorities to discourage singing rather than replace the song.

## Key Characteristics

- Official anthem of [[EastGermany]] after the state's foundation in 1949.
- Song whose lyrics carried all-German unity language rather than explicit class-struggle vocabulary.
- Anthem promoted through schools, ceremonies, banners, and public performance.
- Later silenced because its unity language conflicted with the politics of two German states.
- Reunification alternative whose lyrics were rejected for association with the communist dictatorship.

## Evidence

- Creation and adoption: [[679-germany-the-song-hitler-stole-part-3-glt6217148052]] says East Germany adopted the anthem shortly after its foundation, with text by Johannes Becher and music by Hanns Eisler.
- Political tone: [[679-germany-the-song-hitler-stole-part-3-glt6217148052]] says the lyrics did not mention workers, peasants, socialism, or class struggle.
- Unity problem: [[679-germany-the-song-hitler-stole-part-3-glt6217148052]] says the reference to a united German fatherland became awkward after the Berlin Wall and recognition of two German states.
- Reunification debate: [[679-germany-the-song-hitler-stole-part-3-glt6217148052]] says Lothar de Maiziere proposed using the East German lyrics with Haydn's tune, but Helmut Kohl rejected the association.

## Qualifications

The source reports that East Germans generally liked the anthem, but this page records that claim as part of the episode's account and not as a comprehensive survey of East German public opinion.
