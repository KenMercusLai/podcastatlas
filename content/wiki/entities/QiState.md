# 齐国 / Qi State

齐国 / Qi State appears in [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-06-weiwenhou-xuan-shui-dang-zaixiang-ne-2-lmfzo4sl9nd5kz3mrxdl1dl9efei]] as the state where [[SimaRangju|司马穰苴]] receives command after [[YanYing|晏婴]] recommends him to [[QiJinggong|齐景公]]. The source uses Qi primarily as the setting for talent recognition, status barriers, and military command reform.

The page also gives a home to existing Qi-related pre-Qin nodes such as [[QiHuanGong|齐桓公]] and [[QiXuanWang|齐宣王]], without claiming that this episode discusses those rulers directly.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-07-liangqi-cisha-2-lhngst3xsnyjrmzh1cvheocvvsqm]] adds a later annalistic notice: Qi attacks [[LuState|鲁国]] in 394 BCE and occupies Zui, contributing to the episode's picture of quickening interstate conflict.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-07-liangqi-cisha-3-lsbx8l7wxeioae2lwuwkkkutpdwn]] moves Qi's internal transition earlier than the later death notice. [[TianHe|田和]] displaces [[QiKangGong|齐康公]] to a coastal city, seeks [[WeiWenhou|魏文侯]]'s help for recognition, and makes [[TianFamilyReplacementOfQi|田氏代齐]] a case where practical control precedes formal legitimacy. The same source says Qi's attack on Wei and loss to [[LuState|鲁国]] at Pinglu matter less than Tian-family internal consolidation unless they seriously damage the state.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-08-yidai-mingjiang-wuqi-cansi-2-lvaqlgyccrugfo0l-wuwf1mt1kcm]] adds the Zhou court's formal recognition of [[TianHe|田和]] in 386 BCE. The host stresses that the practical agreement happened earlier and Tian He had already died, making Qi a case where formal recognition and actual political transfer are offset in time.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-08-yidai-mingjiang-wuqi-cansi-3-ltca-ikuzo9ej7a0lfrgwv2-wulb]] continues Qi immediately after that recognition. Qi attacks [[LuState|鲁国]], while the Tian-family succession becomes a source problem: [[TianYan|田衍]] is killed by [[TianQiHuanGong|田武]], [[GongziXiQi|公子喜]] is installed and then killed, and Tian Wu rules as the Tian-family Qi Huan Gong. The same source gives Qi a constructive sequel through [[JixiaAcademy|稷下学宫]] near [[Linzi|临淄]], treating state support for scholars as a strategic talent policy that helps explain later Qi strength.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-09-zhanguo-shidai-de-shijie-dazhan-losmum91vjpqfymywelj60o7-vbl]] makes Qi central to both war and dynastic transition. Qi attacks [[YanState|燕国]], is counterattacked by Han, Zhao, and Wei, later joins Wei against Zhao, and is attacked again by the three Jin states at Lingqiu. The same source says [[QiKangGong|齐康公]] dies without an heir, ending Jiang-line Qi, while Tian-family succession continues through [[TianQiHuanGong|Tian-family Qi Huan Gong]] and [[TianYingqi|田英齐]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-10-yi-jiazu-danwei-you-mayi-chi-daxiang-de-jingshen-1-lq3t7lwpmq1oyb8zmmmn2tywnu8d]] keeps Qi as the target of several 373 BCE attacks. [[YanState|燕国]] defeats Qi at Linghu, while [[LuState|鲁国]] and [[WeyState|卫国]] also attack Qi.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-10-yi-jiazu-danwei-you-mayi-chi-daxiang-de-jingshen-2-lmnqfjmydygqnfec6xhzpixralvi]] adds a contested [[QiWeiwang|齐威王]] branch. The source says [[ZizhiTongjian|《资治通鉴》]] places Qi Wei Wang's Zhou-court visit and the [[JimoDafu|即墨大夫]] / [[AyiDafu|阿邑大夫]] official-audit story under [[ZhouLiewang|周烈王]]'s sixth year, but the host argues the date is probably wrong because the surrounding Qi chronology is not yet ready for Qi Wei Wang. The administrative lesson remains important as [[IndependentOfficialAudit]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-11-shijie-fengqi-yunyong-1-lt60b2gddiosf5kade35uopwohi5]] begins [[ZhouXianwang|周显王]]'s reign with Qi attacking [[WeiState|魏国]] and occupying Guanjing in 368 BCE. The episode then has [[ZhaoState|赵国]] attack Qi and seize a Qi-built wall or fortification line, turning Qi from opportunistic attacker into target within the same year cluster.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-14-qiweiwang-buming-zeyi-yiming-jingren-lrs6rbifyiqrojxg0mccpg7sf9rv]] returns to Qi through [[QiWeiwang|齐威王]] but makes the chronology caution more explicit. The episode argues that Qi Wei Wang's accession likely belongs around 357 BCE rather than the earlier received placement, then narrates how [[ChunyuKun|淳于髡]]'s hidden remonstrance leads to [[QiWeiwangPoliticalTurnaround|齐威王一鸣惊人式转向]], independent official audits, military recovery, and neighboring states returning occupied land.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-15-jiu-bu-chun-yinfa-de-shijie-dazhan-li-iojxthq8komhlhyumho3yvtem]] adds two Qi roles in the next cluster. Qi Wei Wang answers [[LiangHuiWang|魏惠王]] by naming officials such as [[TanZi|檀子]] as treasures, making [[TalentAsStateTreasure|以贤臣为宝]] a Qi governance claim. Qi also joins [[ChuState|楚国]] against [[LuState|鲁国]] after the Lu wine insult, making Qi part of the [[LuWineHandanWarCascade|鲁酒薄而邯郸围]] chain even before it later rescues Zhao.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-16-sunbin-pangjuan-enyuan-jiuge-guiling-zhi-zhan-lryhzkc1-xsvfjhvicu8yxa9d2jr]] adds Qi's Sun Bin branch. A Qi envoy secretly brings [[SunBin|孙膑]] out of [[WeiState|魏国]], [[TianJi|田忌]] shelters him, and [[QiWeiwang|齐威王]] uses Tian Ji and Sun Bin to rescue [[ZhaoState|赵国]] through [[EncirclingWeiToRescueZhao|围魏救赵]] and [[GuilingBattle|桂陵之战]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-17-hanzhaohou-he-shenbuhai-lghjmjhq9qs4se96c5ofgz6caqof]] adds a post-Guiling peace-seeking layer through [[ZhushuJinian|《竹书纪年》]]. After [[LiangHuiWang|魏惠王]] uses Han forces to defeat a coalition at [[XianglingWarringStates|襄陵]], [[QiWeiwang|齐威王]] seeks peace with Wei through [[Jingshe|景舍]]. The episode treats the move as part of a wider rebalancing under [[QinEastwardPressure|Qin pressure]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-18-zhongguo-lishishang-di-yi-li-rentoushui-lgqnequk0rxejqgihwdqxqyqnsvq]] adds a compact internal notice: Qi kills [[MouXin|牟辛]] in 346 BCE. The same source also previews [[MalingBattle|马陵之战]] as the next [[QiState|Qi]]-[[WeiState|Wei]] confrontation involving [[SunBin|孙膑]] and [[PangJuan|庞涓]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-19-maling-zhi-zhan-lhbapt09x2ptdqx0uopkp8apbada]] turns that preview into a policy and campaign case. [[QiWeiwang|齐威王]] hears [[ZouJi|成侯邹忌]], [[TianJi|田忌]], and [[SunBin|孙膑]] debate whether Qi should avoid, hurry, or delay the rescue of [[HanState|韩国]]. Qi secretly promises help, waits while Han and [[WeiState|魏国]] exhaust each other, then attacks toward [[Daliang|大梁]] with Sun Bin advising the campaign that leads toward [[MalingBattle|马陵之战]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-20-pangjuan-si-ci-shu-xia-ls-8-3jhz5q1c5ca11mhdzmz4wg4]] completes Qi's campaign success. Sun Bin's [[DecreasingStovesDeception|reduced-stove deception]] pulls [[PangJuan|庞涓]] into rapid pursuit, the Maling ambush kills him and disorders Wei's army, and Qi follows by breaking Wei and capturing [[PrinceShenWei|太子申]]. The same victory creates an internal Qi problem because [[TianJi|田忌]]'s military standing may threaten [[ZouJi|成侯邹忌]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-21-zouji-xia-yinzhao-tianji-guowai-binan-lkvmjoxolgfgw-ig7ir-ngvjifij]] follows that victory's domestic cost. Zou Ji frames Tian Ji through a staged market divination and possible rebellion accusation, Sun Bin proposes a sudden counterattack at Yongmen, and Tian Ji instead escapes to [[ChuState|楚国]]. The source makes Qi a case where external victory against Wei does not settle internal court alignment.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-27-weiguan-weihuiwang-he-qiweiwang-shi-za-shangye-hupeng-lminlxhrm5bethub9kyy-8blimrb]] gives Qi a diplomatic payoff after that same victory. [[TianYing|田婴]] tries to turn [[MalingBattle|马陵之战]] prestige into a Qi-centered order, and [[XuzhouMutualKingship|徐州相王]] lets [[QiWeiwang|齐威王]] receive formal recognition from [[LiangHuiWang|魏惠王 / 梁惠王]]. The source also says Wei's deference has its own agenda through [[HuiShi|惠施]], so Qi's status gain is real but not uncontested.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-28-hanzhaohou-de-kuaile-ni-xiangxiang-budao-ln2tbxniz4kvwogbwp32exmdokz8]] gives that payoff its cost. [[ZhangChou|张丑]] warns [[TianYing|田婴]] that accepting Wei's title recognition will anger strong states, and the later [[ChuWeiwang|楚威王]] attack at [[Xuzhou|徐州]] proves the danger. Qi also faces pressure from Zhao and Yan, so the source treats the aftermath as [[StatusOrderBacklash|名分秩序反弹]] rather than a simple two-state dispute.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-29-suqin-lizhi-shuangwen-zhishi-gaibian-mingyun-lrf5cqvoriyep2lkbozju-lyjaxv]] extends the same aftermath. Chu demands that [[TianYing|田婴]] be expelled, but [[ZhangChou|张丑]] persuades Chu that leaving him in place better serves Chu's interest. The episode also flags Qi's 333 BCE succession notice as unreliable: the host says [[QiWeiwang|齐威王]] has not yet died and [[QiXuanWang|齐宣王]] should begin only in 319 BCE.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-32-shishang-shouwei-lianheguo-mishuzhang-suqin-lgfkk45yq1c0yblequ-d1t7mol3]] brings Qi into [[SuQin|苏秦]]'s completed hezong tour. The episode emphasizes [[Linzi|临淄]]'s households, adult male population, entertainment, dense traffic, and confident public bearing. Su Qin uses that [[WarringStatesUrbanProsperity|urban prosperity]] as [[DignityBasedPersuasion|dignity pressure]]: a state with such wealth and population should not submit to [[QinState|秦国]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-34-qinguo-zongli-ruhe-wanzhuan-liuguo-lsenzwp0kr5wscb0btvuzmkibriq]] shows Qi on the other side of that alliance story. [[GongsunYan|犀首公孙衍]] helps pull Qi and [[WeiState|魏国]] toward an anti-[[ZhaoState|赵国]] move, breaking the [[HezongAlliance|合纵]] arrangement, but Zhao's flooding of the Yellow River dikes forces the Qi-Wei army to withdraw. Qi later receives [[SongTicheng|宋剔成]] after [[SongJunYan|宋君偃]] overthrows him in [[SongState|宋国]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-37-suqin-liuguo-jituan-ceo-de-bukan-qingshi-fj2kdqlaxzyy2nqtcutktuwrq5fo]] adds Qi as Su Qin's target court and as a meeting participant. In the source's 324 BCE frame, [[QiXuanWang|齐宣王]] appoints [[SuQin|苏秦]] as guest minister after Su Qin leaves [[YanState|燕国]], and Su Qin urges palace and garden expansion as [[StrategicLuxurySabotage|奢侈诱导式削弱]] for Yan's benefit. In 323 BCE, Qi's chief minister meets [[ZhangYiStrategist|张仪]] and the Chu chief minister at [[Niesang|啮桑]]. Because the wiki records the correction that Qi Xuan Wang's first year should be 319 BCE, this remains a [[QiChronologyDispute|齐威王编年争议]] case.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-38-gao-jianzhi-de-zhangyi-bu-yiban-lobhcpz679psy54k0iduccqkfcrn]] gives Qi a different Zhang Yi problem. The source says the Qi ruler, identified in the supplement as [[QiMinWang|齐湣王]], hates Zhang Yi enough that Zhang Yi can predict a Qi attack on whichever state employs him. Qi attacks [[WeiState|魏国]] after Zhang Yi arrives there, but withdraws when [[FengXiWarringStates|冯喜]] reveals Zhang Yi's earlier pitch to [[QinWuwang|秦武王]]. Qi therefore becomes the counterparty that makes [[YinmouYangmouConversion|阴谋阳谋转换]] work: once the plan is public, continuing the campaign looks like being manipulated.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-40-5-yue-chusheng-de-haizi-re-shui-la-ll9e-thidxl5cqoacfrbz-ygw8-t]] brings Qi back through domestic power and household succession. [[TianYing|田婴]] becomes powerful when the Qi ruler delegates state affairs to him, then tries to fortify [[XueWarringStates|薛地]]. The episode's retainer advice says Qi itself is the sea that protects Tian Ying, so Xue's walls would be a [[FiefSecurityIllusion|fief security illusion]] if he lost the state. The same source introduces [[Mengchangjun|孟尝君 / 田文]] as Tian Ying's son, nearly excluded by low maternal status and a May-fifth birth taboo.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-41-mengchangjun-wo-shi-ruhe-dabai-zhe-40-duo-wei-gege-de-ltxgq6oxo1zlkkc6xqjo8-5htns4]] continues the same Qi-linked household branch. Tian Wen wins Tian Ying's recognition, uses guest reception to build reputation, inherits Xue, and becomes a major patron of wandering士人 through [[WarringStatesRetainerPatronage|战国养士]]. For the Qi page, the source shows how state power, fief wealth, and private patronage can sit inside one aristocratic network.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-42-gei-mengchangjun-ti-yijian-de-houguo-lr1jp0tpiaboaca3j5-yvyhyn4vf]] makes that aristocratic network morally contested. [[SimaGuang|司马光]] criticizes Mengchang Jun for using state-derived wealth and status to gather an indiscriminate private retainer network, while the ivory-bed story still presents him as unusually good at accepting useful advice.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-43-mengchangjun-qizi-touqing-bei-faxian-ta-zha-chuli-lr2idcktgfxtdkik1biaj9tiz8ix]] gives that private network a Qi-protective payoff. A retainer sent away from Mengchang Jun's household after an affair gains status in [[WeyState|卫国]] and later persuades the Wey ruler not to join an attack on Qi. The episode flags possible narrative embellishment because Wey was weak, but the story still shows how Mengchang Jun's personal patronage could be narrated as interstate leverage for Qi.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-44-yi-ge-gongsi-weishenme-buneng-you-liang-wei-ceo-lsz0xk6-0wwznkc62k-xkoxgz-r]] uses an older Qi court disaster as one of [[MiuLiu|谬刘]]'s examples. Qi Jian Gong's paired use of Tian Cheng and Kan Zhi is cited as a warning that divided high office can turn into internal violence, making Qi part of the episode's [[DualExecutivePowerRisk|双首长权力风险]] argument even though the source does not develop a full Qi biography here.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-45-yi-shi-wei-jian-junwang-daodi-gai-bu-gai-sharen-chengxing-lo3cok7cfwzrxesp-i8miz1fc1db]] adds a 320 BCE succession endpoint and two later policy roles. [[QiWeiwang|齐威王]] dies and [[QiXuanWang|齐宣王]] succeeds, aligning with the existing correction that Qi Xuan Wang's first year belongs around 319 BCE. In the 318 BCE anti-Qin campaign, Qi promises support but has [[Mengchangjun|孟尝君 / 田文]] move slowly. Later, Qi joins Chu and Wei in destroying [[SongState|宋国]] after [[SongJunYan|宋康王 / 宋君偃]]'s violent expansion.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-46-ma-ni-shi-ge-erbaiwu-de-lishi-laiyuan-lkz5hiamaojtvanllbunecy3g3ff]] makes Qi the place where [[SuQin|苏秦]] dies. The source says a Qi grandee sends an assassin, while the host leaves open whether the motive was court rivalry or discovery of Su Qin's covert [[YanState|燕国]] alignment. The deathbed plan attributed through [[Shiji|《史记》]] has the Qi ruler publicly accuse Su Qin as a Yan agent to draw out the killer, and the episode's rejection of the "二百五" story adds [[IdiomOriginSkepticism|典故来源辨伪]] to Qi's source-critical branch.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-49-mengzi-yue-wo-jiao-ni-zhiguo-ni-que-ba-wo-dang-huaping-lhy14pbf9thcfdiaulcmf8dwcef8]] adds Qi's failed occupation of [[YanState|燕国]] as a Mencian governance test. [[QiXuanWang|齐宣王]] wants to read rapid conquest as [[MandateOfHeavenLegitimacy|天意]], but [[Mengzi|孟子 / Mencius]] says legitimacy depends on whether the conquered people are saved and protected. The episode also keeps Qi's chronology unstable by flagging the received death notice for Qi Xuan Wang and accession notice for [[QiMinWang|田地 / 齐湣王]] as about twelve years too early.
