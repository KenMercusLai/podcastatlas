# Vladimir Uglev

## Overview
Vladimir Uglev was a Soviet chemist described by the episode as a developer of secret nerve agents who later identified alleged uses of [[Novichok]] and spoke publicly about the programme.

## Current Profile
Uglev's biography joins technical authorship, occupational danger, retrospective identification, and later disclosure. The episode says he worked in secret laboratories from 1975 to 1993, synthesized two of a team total of four substances, recognized A234 through molecular and purity signatures, and attributed the Salisbury poisoning to a state-level Russian operation. His later criticism does not erase his role in creating the capability, while his earlier role does not make later disclosure irrelevant.

## Key Characteristics
- He worked on secret Soviet poison development for roughly eighteen years.
- He claimed authorship of two of four agents synthesized by his team.
- He said molecular structure, spectrometry, and purity could identify his products.
- He identified the Salisbury agent as A234 and spoke publicly about the programme.
- His career included direct laboratory danger and knowledge of lethal accidents.
- His reported death from heart-attack complications is not evidence that he was poisoned.

## Qualifications
The episode summary is not a laboratory archive, chemical analysis, official investigation, medical record, or verified stockpile inventory. Authorship, attribution, programme-linked deaths, and state direction remain source-attributed. The circumstances of Uglev's death are unresolved only in the narrative sense; no affirmative poisoning claim is supported here.
