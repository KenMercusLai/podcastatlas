# Perceval

## Overview

Perceval is Chrétien de Troyes's inexperienced knight and original Grail-quest protagonist, whose failure to ask compassionate questions at the Fisher King's castle gives the quest its moral problem.

## Current Profile

In [[346-the-mystery-of-the-holy-grail-part-2-glt9518077570]], Perceval begins outside knightly society, becomes dazzled by mounted warriors, and learns courtly rules too mechanically. At the Grail castle he sees a bleeding lance, a radiant grail, and a wounded king, but obeys advice against asking needless questions.

His silence is later judged not as good etiquette but as failed compassion. Wandering, Good Friday recollection, and instruction from a hermit begin a religious reorientation, yet Chrétien's poem ends before he repairs the failure. Perceval thus embodies a form of [[ChivalricSacralization]] in which technique and restraint are insufficient without mercy and spiritual understanding.

## Key Characteristics

- Forest-raised outsider initially ignorant of knightly society.
- Enthusiastic learner who mistakes a social rule for an absolute moral command.
- Witness to the first Grail procession discussed by the episode.
- Failed questioner whose silence brings ethical and social consequences.
- Unfinished penitent whose quest remains unresolved in Chrétien's text.

## Evidence

- Formation: [[346-the-mystery-of-the-holy-grail-part-2-glt9518077570]] recounts his protected childhood, encounter with knights, departure, and instruction.
- Grail failure: [[346-the-mystery-of-the-holy-grail-part-2-glt9518077570]] says he remains silent before the wounded king, lance, and grail.
- Moral reinterpretation: [[346-the-mystery-of-the-holy-grail-part-2-glt9518077570]] presents later denunciation as identifying a failure of compassion.
- Religious turn: [[346-the-mystery-of-the-holy-grail-part-2-glt9518077570]] links Good Friday and hermit instruction to his recognition of the Grail's holiness.

## Qualifications

This profile is limited to the episode's compressed treatment of Chrétien and later Grail tradition. Different medieval continuations and adaptations do not necessarily preserve one stable Perceval, and the poem's unfinished state prevents a definitive account of Chrétien's intended resolution.

## What Changed

- Created a profile of Perceval as a failed compassionate questioner rather than merely an unsuccessful adventurer.
