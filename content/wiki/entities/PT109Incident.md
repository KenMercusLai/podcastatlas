# PT-109 Incident

## Overview

The PT-109 incident was the August 1943 destruction of [[JohnFKennedy|John F. Kennedy]]'s patrol torpedo boat in the South Pacific and the survival-and-rescue episode that became central to his war-hero reputation.

## Current Profile

After a Japanese destroyer cut PT-109 in half, two crewmen were killed or lost and Kennedy led ten survivors through the ordeal. The episode emphasizes his four-hour swim while towing the injured Patrick McMahon by a life-jacket strap held in his teeth, followed by the coconut message carried through local islanders toward rescuers.

Later retelling romanticized the episode, but the hosts treat Kennedy's bravery as genuine. Reader's Digest publicity converted the rescue into national political capital, while the physical ordeal worsened the serious health problems that remained hidden during his career.

## Key Characteristics

- Wartime collision and survival event involving the loss of two PT-109 crewmen.
- Rescue case in which Kennedy's endurance and responsibility for an injured crewman were central.
- Story mediated by local islanders and a coconut message before rescue.
- Genuine act of courage later amplified and romanticized as political biography.
- Physical ordeal that worsened Kennedy's already poor health.
