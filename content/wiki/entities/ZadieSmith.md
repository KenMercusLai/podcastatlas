# Zadie Smith

## Overview

Zadie Smith is the novelist whose [[TheFraudZadieSmith|The Fraud]] reworks the [[TichborneClaimant]] and foregrounds [[AndrewBogle]]'s movement through slavery, empire, Australia, and Britain.

## Current Profile

In [[400-victorian-britains-maddest-mystery-glt4466006716]], Smith describes the case as already possessing the density of fiction. She treats facts as constraints, uses digitized Jamaican plantation records to recover life details, and locates invention mainly in viewpoint and the expanded role of Mrs. Touchet. Her method also asks what domestic Victorian novels hide when colonial spaces absorb labor, wealth, violence, and troublesome sons.

## Key Characteristics

- Adapts a legal sensation without treating factual manipulation as the main creative task.
- Uses plantation records to connect Bogle's biography to Jamaican slavery.
- Preserves uncertainty around recognition, motive, strategy, and belief.
- Extends Mrs. Touchet to create a sustained fictional viewpoint.
- Reads Victorian domestic fiction against colonial worlds it leaves offstage.

## Evidence

- Factual boundary: [[400-victorian-britains-maddest-mystery-glt4466006716]] records Smith's insistence that the facts require little unreasonable alteration.
- Archival method: [[400-victorian-britains-maddest-mystery-glt4466006716]] describes plantation records supplying names, occupations, children, and locations.
- Literary intervention: [[400-victorian-britains-maddest-mystery-glt4466006716]] identifies Mrs. Touchet's extension and colonial absences as creative choices.

## Qualifications

The episode explains research and intent, not the novel's full form or reception. Plantation records reflect coercive institutions, and fiction cannot eliminate uncertainty about Bogle's private beliefs.
