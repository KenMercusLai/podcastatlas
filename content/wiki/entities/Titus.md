# Titus

## Overview

Titus is the Roman emperor presented in [[369-the-colosseum-romes-arena-of-death-glt7808118779]] as [[Vespasian]]'s successor, a conqueror of Jerusalem, and the ruler who inaugurated the [[Colosseum]] amid disaster and reputational risk.

## Current Profile

The source contrasts Titus's earlier reputation as Praetorian commander and libertine with his self-presentation as a reformed emperor. The eruption that destroyed Pompeii and Herculaneum, a major Roman fire, and plague made the amphitheater's inauguration more than celebration: it became a ritual and political opportunity to address the dead, punish figures associated with control, reorder spectators, and perform guardianship of city and empire.

## Key Characteristics

- Flavian successor associated with victory in Judea and the capture of Jerusalem.
- Emperor who probably inaugurated the Colosseum in AD 80 in the episode's chronology.
- Ruler carrying an earlier reputation for coercive office and libertine conduct.
- Disaster-era emperor presenting reform, generosity, moral order, and public guardianship.
- Patron of spectacles combining punishment, animals, mythic theater, and gladiatorial combat.

## Evidence

- Conquest and succession: [[369-the-colosseum-romes-arena-of-death-glt7808118779]] joins Titus to Jerusalem, Flavian wealth claims, and succession from Vespasian.
- Disaster setting: [[369-the-colosseum-romes-arena-of-death-glt7808118779]] places inauguration after Vesuvius and amid fire and plague.
- Reputational repair: [[369-the-colosseum-romes-arena-of-death-glt7808118779]] contrasts Titus's earlier profile with his conduct as emperor.
- Ordered inauguration: [[369-the-colosseum-romes-arena-of-death-glt7808118779]] connects public punishment and ranked spectatorship to restored moral order.

## Qualifications

The inauguration date is presented as probable, and the source's account of Titus's motives, moral transformation, public punishment, ritual obligations, and audience response remains interpretive. The episode also qualifies a simple claim that Jerusalem loot alone paid for the amphitheater.

## What Changed

- Created a profile connecting the Colosseum's inauguration to disaster response, reputation, conquest, and dynastic order.

## Relationships

- [[Vespasian]] - father, predecessor, and initiator of the Colosseum project.
- [[Colosseum]] - amphitheater whose inauguration became Titus's major public performance.
- [[Nero]] - negative predecessor against whom Flavian public virtue was framed.
- [[GladiatorialSpectacle]] - culminating arena form within Titus's inaugural program.
- [[MonumentalDynasticLegitimation]] - mechanism connecting the building to Flavian authority and moral repair.
