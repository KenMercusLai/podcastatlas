# Langya Mountain / 琅琊山

Langya Mountain / 琅琊山 appears in [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-122-2-ribenren-daodi-shi-bushi-xufu-de-houdai-lhgbbhg3zw8rlx1jjb7lpb5ihihh|Qinji 122-2]] as a sea-facing stop on [[QinShiHuang|秦始皇]]'s 219 BCE eastern tour. After sacrificing to the [[QiEightSpirits|齐地八神]], the emperor travels south, climbs Langya, remains there for more than three months, relocates 30,000 households below Langya Terrace, grants twelve years of household-tax relief, and erects a stone inscription praising Qin's order.

The page's role is narrow: Langya is where [[ImperialTourPoliticalTheater|帝国巡游政治剧场]] becomes settlement policy and written public doctrine. The episode places it after [[Taishan|泰山]] and before the [[XuFu|徐福]] sea-search branch, so it bridges sacred geography, conquered-territory possession, inscription, and the turn toward maritime immortality.

## Key Claims
- Langya extends Qin Shi Huang's eastern tour from mountain sacrifice into coastal residence, migration, and inscription.
- Moving households below Langya Terrace makes the tour materially territorial rather than only symbolic.
- Tax remission frames forced or directed settlement as imperial favor while still serving Qin's control of former Qi space.
- The inscription recorded there anchors [[LangyaInscriptionPoliticalProgram|琅琊刻石政治纲领]].
