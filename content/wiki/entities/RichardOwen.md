# Richard Owen

## Overview

Richard Owen was a British anatomist and paleontologist who coined “Dinosauria,” helped establish the Natural History Museum, and collaborated on the Crystal Palace dinosaur models that turned fossil reconstruction into mass public spectacle.

## Current Profile

In [[109-dinosaurs-glt4328792494]], Owen combines durable scientific classification, institutional power, public communication, and serious personal qualification. Grouping Megalosaurus, Iguanodon, and Hylaeosaurus under Dinosauria gave a common identity to otherwise fragmentary finds. His work with Benjamin Waterhouse Hawkins then gave Victorian audiences monumental bodies through which to imagine those animals.

Those bodies also make scientific revision visible: the Crystal Palace Iguanodon was reconstructed as a heavy quadruped and is now recognized as inaccurate. The episode further portrays Owen as denying Gideon Mantell credit and using institutional standing against him, so Owen's scientific importance cannot be collapsed into an uncomplicated heroic profile.

## Key Characteristics

- Coined Dinosauria in 1842 to group several large fossil reptiles.
- Converted specialist classification into public imagery through the Crystal Palace models.
- Helped found the institutional museum setting in which prehistoric life became a national public object.
- Worked from fragmentary evidence, producing influential reconstructions later revised.
- Exercised scientific authority in ways the episode portrays as damaging to Gideon Mantell.
