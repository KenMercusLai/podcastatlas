# BlackBerry

## Overview

BlackBerry, formerly Research in Motion, was a major smartphone and mobile-messaging company. The bounded sources show it first as an enabling mobile platform for an early connected watch and later as an incumbent caught between a deteriorating handset business and a proposed cross-platform messaging pivot.

## Current Profile

BlackBerry's platform value could create opportunities for other hardware while also exposing partners to platform decline. Its accessory support made [[EricMigicovsky]]'s [[ImpulseWatch|Impulse]] practical when iPhone Bluetooth access was insufficient, but the “BlackBerry watch” label tied demand to a user base already moving toward iPhone.

The later source places that platform transition inside BlackBerry itself. [[JimBalsillie]] proposed shifting from threatened handsets to an open [[BlackBerryMessenger|BBM]] service, while [[MikeLazaridis]] and other leaders remained committed to hardware. The board's rejection shows that recognizing a platform shift, designing a replacement business, and securing institutional authorization are separate capabilities.

## Key Characteristics

- BlackBerry combined mobile devices, an operating platform, accessory APIs, and a large messaging network.
- Its platform enabled third-party products but could also make those products fragile when users migrated elsewhere.
- By 2011, handset competition from [[Apple]] and [[Google]] had created pressure for a strategic reset.
- [[BlackBerryMessenger|BBM]] offered a possible services path built on an existing network and carrier relationships.
- Internal disagreement and board authority prevented the proposed cross-platform pivot from launching.

## Evidence

### Enabling but declining platform

- [[tsr-s4-ericm-v2-tsr-s4-ericm-v2]] says BlackBerry's Bluetooth support enabled the early [[ImpulseWatch|Impulse]], while customer migration toward iPhone weakened demand before the product shipped.

### Hardware-to-services fork

- [[the-blackberry-problem-the-mistakes-series-the-blackberry-problem-the-mistakes-series]] says Balsillie developed an open BBM strategy with roughly 80 million users and carrier commitments, but the board chose continued hardware investment.

### Governance constraint

- [[the-blackberry-problem-the-mistakes-series-the-blackberry-problem-the-mistakes-series]] records Balsillie's retrospective admission that he failed to prepare and build board support for a radical change.

## Qualifications

The early-watch source is a founder retrospective, and the later strategy source is framed mainly through Balsillie and Gladwell. The board's complete analysis, consultant work, carrier agreements, launch readiness, and competing forecasts are not supplied. BBM's network scale and the weakness of the hardware path make the proposed pivot plausible, but they do not prove it would have succeeded against WhatsApp, Apple, Google, or later messaging competitors.

## What Changed

- Reframed BlackBerry from a supporting mobile platform into a company facing its own hardware-to-services decision.
- Added BBM's proposed cross-platform pivot and the board's rejection.
- Made governance and persuasion part of the company's strategic profile without treating the counterfactual as proven.

## Relationships

- [[JimBalsillie]] - co-CEO who advocated the open messaging-services strategy.
- [[MikeLazaridis]] - co-CEO associated with the hardware path.
- [[BlackBerryMessenger]] - messaging asset proposed as the company's post-handset core.
- [[EricMigicovsky]] - founder whose early watch depended on BlackBerry's accessory APIs.
- [[Apple]] - competing mobile platform and destination for migrating users.
- [[FounderBoardRelationshipMaintenance]] - governance concept explaining why strategic conviction did not become authorization.
