# Enoch Sontonga

## Overview

Enoch Sontonga is presented as the black South African Methodist teacher and composer of [[NkosiSikelelIAfrika|Nkosi Sikelel' iAfrika]], whose hymn later became central to ANC and national anthem symbolism.

## Current Profile

The episode uses Sontonga to connect the 1995 rugby anthem ceremony to a much older black Christian history. He is described as a Xhosa-speaking teacher in Johannesburg who wrote the hymn in 1897, died young in 1905, and was buried in a black Christian cemetery section before his grave was rediscovered and memorialized by [[NelsonMandela]] in 1996.

## Key Characteristics

- Xhosa-speaking black South African born in the Eastern Cape Colony in the early 1870s.
- Methodist schoolteacher in Johannesburg who ran a school choir.
- Composer of [[NkosiSikelelIAfrika|Nkosi Sikelel' iAfrika]] in 1897.
- Figure whose work moved from church hymn to ANC and national anthem symbolism.
- Remembered through a grave rediscovery and Mandela memorial after earlier erasure and insult.

## Evidence

- Biographical setting: [[682-south-africa-mandela-and-the-death-of-apartheid-part-6-glt6535658080]] says Sontonga was born in the early 1870s in the Eastern Cape Colony and was a Xhosa speaker.
- School and choir role: [[682-south-africa-mandela-and-the-death-of-apartheid-part-6-glt6535658080]] says he became a teacher at a Methodist school in Johannesburg and ran the school choir.
- Composition: [[682-south-africa-mandela-and-the-death-of-apartheid-part-6-glt6535658080]] says Sontonga wrote [[NkosiSikelelIAfrika|Nkosi]] in 1897.
- Death and burial: [[682-south-africa-mandela-and-the-death-of-apartheid-part-6-glt6535658080]] says he died in 1905 of gastroenteritis at about 32 and was buried in a black Christian section of a Johannesburg cemetery.
- Memorial after erasure: [[682-south-africa-mandela-and-the-death-of-apartheid-part-6-glt6535658080]] says his grave was rediscovered in the early 1990s and Mandela unveiled a memorial there in 1996.
