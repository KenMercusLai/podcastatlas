## Overview
[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-795-dengjia-miewang-hou-weihe-juchao-wei-tamen-mingyuan-lqvmbqrrookphb4becaqbp7h-wi1|Hanji 795]] presents Empress Dowager Deng as the fallen regent figure whose household becomes the focus of a severe post-regency purge under [[EmperorAnOfHan|Emperor An]]. The episode does not build a full biography; it preserves a contested memory in which Zhu Chong defends her earlier statecraft and service while the post-Deng court uses accusations against the [[DengFamilyAnEraLateHan|Deng family]] to justify liquidation.

## Current Profile
The source's Deng profile is mediated through aftermath rather than direct action. [[ChenZhongLateHan|Chen Zhong]] and anti-Deng actors help convert the household into a target after Deng power collapses, while [[ZhuChongLateHan|Zhu Chong]] argues that the dowager and her brothers had protected the dynasty, installed Emperor An, and withdrawn from immediate domination once he came to the throne.

This makes Deng a legitimacy hinge for the early An reign. If Zhu Chong is right, the purge turns a successful regency into a [[ProcedurelessPoliticalPurge|procedureless political purge]] based on untested palace testimony. If the anti-Deng line is right, the new regime is correcting outer-relative domination. The episode's own emphasis falls on the procedural defect: even Emperor An partially retreats by permitting burial and survivor return rather than pursuing total annihilation.

## Key Characteristics
- Fallen empress dowager whose household is punished after the transition to Emperor An's personal rule.
- Remembered by Zhu Chong as a high-merit regent who protected Han institutions and placed Emperor An on the throne.
- Defended indirectly through procedural critique: the source stresses missing proof, interrogation, and charge clarity more than a detailed innocence brief.
- Central figure in a legitimacy tradeoff where removing [[OuterRelativeCourtPower|outer-relative court power]] creates a new favorite-dominated field.
- Distinct from later Deng-named empress figures such as [[EmpressDengHuanLateHan|Empress Deng of Huan's reign]].

## Evidence
- Merit memory and dynastic service: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-795-dengjia-miewang-hou-weihe-juchao-wei-tamen-mingyuan-lqvmbqrrookphb4becaqbp7h-wi1|Hanji 795]] reports Zhu Chong's claim that Empress Dowager Deng and her brothers served the Han state, settled Emperor An's accession, and did not deserve household destruction.
- Procedural vulnerability: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-795-dengjia-miewang-hou-weihe-juchao-wei-tamen-mingyuan-lqvmbqrrookphb4becaqbp7h-wi1|Hanji 795]] says the anti-Deng case rested on one-sided palace testimony without direct confrontation, leaving the purge open to courtwide grievance.
- Partial imperial correction: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-795-dengjia-miewang-hou-weihe-juchao-wei-tamen-mingyuan-lqvmbqrrookphb4becaqbp7h-wi1|Hanji 795]] has Emperor An blame local officials for over-destruction, permit Deng burials, and return some cousins to Luoyang, indicating that Deng's posthumous status remained contested rather than fully criminalized.

## Qualifications
The current profile is single-source and source-bounded. It does not settle the dowager's full regency record, the exact legal charges against every Deng relative, or how much of Zhu Chong's defense should be treated as forensic truth rather than strategic remonstrance.
