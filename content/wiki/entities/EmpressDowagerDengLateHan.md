## Overview
和熹邓皇后 / Empress Dowager Deng now appears as the An-era regent whose delayed return of power, possible replacement politics, death, and household purge become the opening problem for [[EmperorAnOfHan|Emperor An]]'s personal rule. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-794-yidai-nvjun-dengsui-qushi-hou-jing-quanjia-zaoyang-llmlhabdd09opca6-pr80q4mfoxi|Hanji 794]] supplies the pre-purge tension and first liquidation wave; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-795-dengjia-miewang-hou-weihe-juchao-wei-tamen-mingyuan-lqvmbqrrookphb4becaqbp7h-wi1|Hanji 795]] preserves the later contested memory in which Zhu Chong defends her and her family.

## Current Profile
Hanji 794 makes Deng more than a fallen name after the fact. She had installed Emperor An when he was young, later came to dislike his developing character, delayed returning power, and summoned sons from collateral royal houses to Luoyang. [[LiuYiPingyuanWangLateHan|Pingyuan Wang Liu Yi]]'s presence lets Wang Sheng, Li Run, and Jiang Jing convert regency uncertainty into slander that An may be replaced.

After Deng dies, the accusation against her brothers and relatives turns that fear into punishment. The episode does not prove the alleged Liu Yi plan, but it shows why the charge is politically explosive: if true, it would make Deng's regency a threat to An's throne; if untested, it makes her death the moment when palace slander becomes household liquidation.

Hanji 795 then gives Deng's contested public memory. [[ZhuChongLateHan|Zhu Chong]] argues that she and her brothers had protected the dynasty, settled An's accession, and should not be reduced to a criminal household through one-sided palace testimony. Emperor An's later partial retreat, allowing burial and limited survivor return, leaves Deng's posthumous status contested rather than fully criminalized.

## Key Characteristics
- Empress dowager and regent who installed Emperor An but delayed returning governing power.
- Associated with possible replacement politics because she summoned and retained collateral royal sons in Luoyang.
- Posthumous center of a succession-sensitive accusation involving Pingyuan Wang Liu Yi.
- Defended through procedural critique and merit memory rather than a complete innocence brief.
- Legitimacy hinge for the early An reign because her household's destruction tests whether post-regency correction can remain lawful.
- Distinct from later Deng-named empress figures such as [[EmpressDengHuanLateHan|Empress Deng of Huan's reign]].

## Evidence
- Regency tension and replacement fear: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-794-yidai-nvjun-dengsui-qushi-hou-jing-quanjia-zaoyang-llmlhabdd09opca6-pr80q4mfoxi|Hanji 794]] says Deng installed An, delayed returning power, brought royal sons to Luoyang, and became the target of slander from Wang Sheng, Li Run, and Jiang Jing.
- First posthumous purge: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-794-yidai-nvjun-dengsui-qushi-hou-jing-quanjia-zaoyang-llmlhabdd09opca6-pr80q4mfoxi|Hanji 794]] says the alleged Liu Yi plan leads Emperor An to punish Deng relatives through title abolition, office removal, confiscation, exile, and death.
- Merit memory and dynastic service: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-795-dengjia-miewang-hou-weihe-juchao-wei-tamen-mingyuan-lqvmbqrrookphb4becaqbp7h-wi1|Hanji 795]] reports Zhu Chong's claim that Empress Dowager Deng and her brothers served the Han state, settled Emperor An's accession, and did not deserve household destruction.
- Procedural vulnerability: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-795-dengjia-miewang-hou-weihe-juchao-wei-tamen-mingyuan-lqvmbqrrookphb4becaqbp7h-wi1|Hanji 795]] says the anti-Deng case rested on one-sided palace testimony without direct confrontation, leaving the purge open to courtwide grievance.
- Partial imperial correction: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-795-dengjia-miewang-hou-weihe-juchao-wei-tamen-mingyuan-lqvmbqrrookphb4becaqbp7h-wi1|Hanji 795]] has Emperor An blame local officials for over-destruction, permit Deng burials, and return some cousins to Luoyang.

## Qualifications
The current profile is source-bounded. It does not settle Deng's full regency record, whether she truly intended to depose An, the exact legal charges against every Deng relative, or how much of Zhu Chong's defense should be treated as forensic truth rather than strategic remonstrance. The page treats the replacement plot as an accusation with major political consequences, not as a proven fact.
