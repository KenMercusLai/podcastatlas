## Overview

和熹邓皇后 / Empress Dowager Deng now appears as an An-era regent whose record starts before the 118-121 CE episodes previously emphasized. Her profile combines serious domestic crisis governance, frontier and fiscal failure, Western Regions underimplementation, Deng-family reward and transfer risk, accepted and rejected warnings, and a posthumous memory contested by both purge agents and defenders.

## Current Profile

Hanji 789 moves Deng's regency back into the 116-117 CE Qiang crisis. The court sends [[RenShangLateHan|Ren Shang]] and [[DengZunLateHan|Deng Zun]] against Qiang forces, implements forced civilian relocation that helps produce [[DuJigongLateHan|Du Jigong]]'s defection, manages multi-front pressure from Xianbei and Yizhou, accepts [[LiuKaiEasternHan|Liu Kai]]'s three-year mourning proposal, and removes [[YuanChangLateHan|Yuan Chang]] after a court-secret correspondence case. This makes Deng's rule visible as both institutional repair and coercive frontier improvisation.

Hanji 790 and Hanji 791 show the regency under continuing frontier and fiscal strain. Qiang war spending is treated as enormous, while Deng-family proximity shapes reward politics around Deng Zun and Ren Shang. Deng also supervises royal and Deng-family education, which looks like institution-building but can be read as power consolidation. In the Western Regions debate, she summons [[BanYongLateHan|Ban Yong]] and accepts the office-and-Dunhuang portion of his constrained-retention plan, but omits the Shanshan forward post that gave the proposal its leverage.

Hanji 792 makes the transfer risk explicit through [[DengKangLateHan|Deng Kang]]. He argues that court prestige should rise and outer-relative power should be weakened. Deng refuses, punishes him, and leaves the family-power problem unresolved before her death. Hanji 793 then gives the precise death sequence while evaluating her regency through famine, flood, drought, frontier attacks, banditry, fiscal depletion, and gendered criticism alongside austerity, relief, education, ritual repair, pardons, labor and tax reduction, old palace women's release, and Jiangnan development.

The posthumous layer is unstable. Hanji 794 says Deng had installed [[EmperorAnOfHan|Emperor An]], delayed returning power, and summoned collateral royal sons to Luoyang, making Pingyuan Wang Liu Yi usable in replacement rumors after she dies. Hanji 795 shows the purge partially checked when [[ZhuChongLateHan|Zhu Chong]] memorializes in defense of the Deng family, forcing limited correction while not restoring the old power structure.

## Key Characteristics

- Crisis regent judged through frontier war, fiscal strain, disaster relief, ritual policy, education, and transfer risk together.
- Capable of institutional repair, including mourning-policy permission, education, relief measures, and ritual restoration.
- Vulnerable to frontier-policy failure, especially Qiang relocation backfire and Western Regions underimplementation.
- Governs through a powerful Deng-family field whose military rewards and education roles create both capacity and suspicion.
- Receives internal family warning from Deng Kang but responds with punishment rather than risk reduction.
- Dies with no specific medical cause supplied by the current sources, leaving a posthumous struggle over memory and blame.
- Her fall exposes the difference between correcting outer-relative dominance and replacing it with new favorite-household networks.

## Qualifications

The profile remains mixed. The sources do not support a simple hagiography, because Qiang policy, Western Regions implementation, Deng-family concentration, and delayed transfer create real damage. They also do not support reducing Deng to usurpation, because relief, austerity, education, ritual repair, and public defense remain substantial in the bounded evidence.
