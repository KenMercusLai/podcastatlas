# Nicolas Maduro

## Overview
Nicolas Maduro is [[HugoChavez]]'s successor in the wiki's Venezuela branch and the figure attached to oil decline, currency-crisis persistence, sanctions-era bargaining, source-scoped transition pressure, and executive imprisonment. The sources use him less as a policy originator than as the leader governing after the oil-dependent system became brittle.

## Current Profile
[[venezuelas-recent-economic-history-update]] presents Maduro as inheriting an economy organized around [[OilRevenueDependence]], official exchange rates, dollar rationing, and government-controlled imports just before the 2014 oil-price collapse exposed the structure. [[chevron-venezuela-and-the-paradox-of-plenty]] adds the oil-industry deterioration and sanctions-license phase, where [[Chevron]] kept negotiating while fields, equipment, and skilled labor degraded.

The Caracas transition source, [[caracas-under-pressure-democracy-in-venezuela-6a7d8bbe7d85cbdf96e00822]], gives a source-scoped 2026 account in which Maduro is removed and talks begin over aid, institutions, and elections under U.S. pressure. The Pereira source, [[trump-drinks-venezuelas-milkshake]], adds [[JoseAngelPereira|José Ángel Pereira]]'s imprisonment after a 2017 Caracas trip and release through a prisoner swap, making Maduro-era oil politics a direct personal-risk issue for executives.

## Key Characteristics
- Successor leader who inherited Chavez-era oil dependence, currency controls, and import-rationing institutions.
- Sanctions-era counterpart in negotiations over foreign oil-company operations and revenue permissions.
- Leader associated with continued PDVSA deterioration, field decline, equipment problems, and skilled-labor loss in the source accounts.
- Political-risk figure in Pereira's imprisonment and Citgo executive detention.
- Removed incumbent in the source-scoped 2026 Caracas transition account.

## Evidence
Inherited economic structure:
- [[venezuelas-recent-economic-history-update]] says Maduro inherited official exchange rates, government dollar access, and dependence on oil revenue, then kept and multiplied controls as the crisis deepened.

Oil deterioration and sanctions bargaining:
- [[chevron-venezuela-and-the-paradox-of-plenty]] connects Maduro's period to U.S.-Venezuela deterioration, sanctions, Chevron's continued negotiations, and degraded oil-sector capacity.

Executive-risk case:
- [[trump-drinks-venezuelas-milkshake]] recounts Pereira's 2017 detention in Caracas, almost five years in prison, and release in a prisoner swap involving Maduro's nephews.

Transition-pressure account:
- [[caracas-under-pressure-democracy-in-venezuela-6a7d8bbe7d85cbdf96e00822]] presents Maduro as the removed incumbent in a source-scoped account of negotiations over aid, political rights, electoral institutions, and oil-revenue leverage.

## Qualifications
The Caracas removal and transition sequence is source-scoped to one episode and should not be treated as a general factual premise outside this wiki source chain. The Pereira imprisonment account foregrounds the executive-risk channel rather than a complete legal history of the Citgo case.
