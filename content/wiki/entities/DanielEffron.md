# Daniel Effron

## Overview

Daniel Effron is a psychologist presented in [[the-lady-vanishes-the-lady-vanishes|The Lady Vanishes]] as an interpreter of [[MoralLicensing]], especially the way prior moral conduct can protect a positive self-image while later prejudice is expressed.

## Current Profile

Effron defines moral licensing as the possibility that doing something regarded as good gives a person psychological permission to do something they would otherwise see as questionable. The episode reports an experiment in which some participants who publicly supported Barack Obama later became more willing to express racially suspect views, using that result to model how symbolic support for an outsider might be converted into evidence of one's own fairness.

His account is explicitly conditional rather than deterministic. Researchers do not fully understand when good conduct produces further good conduct and when it licenses regression. The episode applies his framework analogically to the Royal Academy's treatment of [[ElizabethThompson|Elizabeth Thompson]] and Australia's treatment of [[JuliaGillard|Julia Gillard]].

## Key Characteristics

- Psychologist working on moral judgment and self-concept.
- Defines moral licensing as moral credit that can permit later questionable conduct.
- Connects prejudice expression to prior evidence of one's own fairness.
- Emphasizes uncertainty about when virtue reinforces itself versus licensing worse behavior.
- Supplies an interpretive mechanism rather than direct historical proof about the Academy or Australian politics.
