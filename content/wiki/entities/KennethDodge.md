## Overview

Kenneth Dodge is the psychologist whose work on [[HostileIntentAttribution]] gives [[memorial-day-2020-part-1-memorial-day-2020-part-1|Memorial Day, 2020 | Part 1]] a developmental account of threat-biased interpretation.

## Current Profile

Dodge describes how a person can learn to interpret ambiguous actions as hostile and answer them with aggression. His examples make the pattern conditional rather than inherently irrational: vigilance can protect a child in a violent home, yet the same learned rule can produce avoidable aggression and isolation when carried into safer settings. He also emphasizes that children ordinarily learn to distinguish accidental, playful, benign, and hostile intentions through social feedback.

## Key Characteristics

- Psychologist studying aggression and the interpretation of ambiguous social behavior.
- Treats hostile-intent attribution partly as a developmental and learned pattern.
- Shows how threat vigilance can be adaptive in danger but maladaptive when generalized.
- Connects intention interpretation with the regulation of aggressive response.

## Evidence

### Ambiguous behavior and aggression

- [[memorial-day-2020-part-1-memorial-day-2020-part-1]] recounts Dodge's example of an aggressive hospitalized teenager who reflexively struck him after unexpected touch and justified the act as vigilance.

### Learned threat detection

- [[memorial-day-2020-part-1-memorial-day-2020-part-1]] reports Dodge's account of a child learning to inspect a violent stepfather's breath, expression, and belt, illustrating how locally protective vigilance can become a generalized social liability.

## Qualifications

The episode does not reproduce Dodge's studies, methods, or effect sizes. Gladwell explicitly lacks evidence about Chauvin's upbringing, so applying Dodge's developmental examples to Chauvin remains a behavioral hypothesis rather than a diagnosis or established causal history.

## What Changed

- Established Dodge's role in distinguishing learned threat vigilance from accurate interpretation across contexts.
