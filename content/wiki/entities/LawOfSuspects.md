# Law of Suspects

## Overview

The Law of Suspects was the 17 September 1793 measure that greatly widened who could be arrested as politically dangerous during the [[FrenchRevolution|French Revolution]].

## Current Profile

The law made suspicion administratively scalable. It covered nobles, émigrés, dismissed officials, people without certificates of good citizenship, and anyone whose conduct, associations, speech, or writing could be read as support for tyranny. Because insufficient enthusiasm and conspicuous revolutionary excess could each become suspicious, the regime shifted repression away from specified acts toward contradictory judgments of status, association, and patriotism.

## Key Characteristics

- It was enacted on 17 September 1793.
- It expanded arrest categories beyond identifiable hostile acts.
- It treated conduct, associations, and speech as evidence of political danger.
- It made certificates of good citizenship part of the boundary between security and suspicion.
- It institutionalized the widening internal-enemy category of the Terror.
- Its open-ended logic made innocence difficult to demonstrate through political behavior.

## Evidence

### Expanded arrest categories

- [[708-the-terror-the-reign-of-robespierre-part-2-glt2099451333]] lists nobles, émigrés, dismissed officials, people lacking civic certificates, and broadly suspicious conduct, association, or speech.

### Place in the terror apparatus

- [[708-the-terror-the-reign-of-robespierre-part-2-glt2099451333]] places the law beside the expanded [[RevolutionaryTribunal]] and centralized committee direction.

### Contradictory suspicion and scale

- [[710-the-terror-death-at-the-guillotine-part-4-glt5038102951]] emphasizes that both too little revolutionary zeal and excessive zeal could become evidence of disloyalty and reports roughly half a million people passing through prisons by summer 1794.

## Qualifications

- The sources summarize the law's reach but do not reconstruct its complete text, regional enforcement, or later amendments.
- Genuine war and revolt explain the security context without establishing the guilt of each person captured by its broad categories.
- The half-million prison figure is episode-level and includes the wider French prison system rather than a demonstrated count attributable only to this law.

## What Changed

- Added speech and writing to the source-backed suspect criteria.
- Added the contradictory-zeal problem that made behavioral proof of innocence unstable.
- Added the source-scoped scale estimate while preserving its attribution limits.
