# Law of Suspects

## Overview

The Law of Suspects was the 17 September 1793 measure that greatly widened who could be arrested as politically dangerous during the [[FrenchRevolution|French Revolution]].

## Current Profile

The law made suspicion administratively scalable. It covered nobles, émigrés, dismissed officials, people without certificates of good citizenship, and anyone whose conduct, associations, or speech could be read as support for tyranny, shifting repression away from clearly specified acts toward broad judgments of status and patriotism.

## Key Characteristics

- It was enacted on 17 September 1793.
- It expanded arrest categories beyond identifiable hostile acts.
- It treated conduct, associations, and speech as evidence of political danger.
- It made certificates of good citizenship part of the boundary between security and suspicion.
- It institutionalized the widening internal-enemy category of the Terror.
