# Romanus IV

## Overview

Romanus IV Diogenes was an eastern Roman general who became emperor through marriage to the regent Eudokia, was captured at the [[BattleOfManzikert|Battle of Manzikert]], and lost the throne during the civil conflict that followed.

## Current Profile

[[12-days-neros-succession-and-the-fall-of-the-byzantine-empire-glt6148714296]] presents Romanus as a successful Anatolian military aristocrat who had served on the Danube frontier. After Constantine X died, his widow Eudokia governed for young sons. Romanus then appears as a suspected or failed conspirator brought to [[Constantinople]] expecting death, blinding, or monastic confinement; Eudokia instead married him, turning a dangerous general into emperor on 1 January 1068.

His reign is organized around military recovery and catastrophic political reversal. At Manzikert in 1071, confused operations and divided forces ended with his capture by the Seljuk ruler Alp Arslan. After symbolic humiliation, Romanus received honorable treatment, negotiated release, and returned to find that court rivals had displaced him. He surrendered after civil conflict under a promise of safety, was blinded in a botched procedure, exiled, and died from infection. The profile therefore distinguishes battlefield defeat from the internal struggle that made it irreparable.

## Key Characteristics

- Anatolian military aristocrat and experienced frontier general.
- Accused rebel transformed into emperor through marriage to the regent Eudokia.
- Ruler who sought military recovery against Seljuk pressure.
- Captured emperor released after negotiation with Alp Arslan.
- Victim of an internal succession struggle, broken safe-conduct promise, blinding, exile, and fatal infection.

## Qualifications

This profile follows one short narrative source. The nature of Romanus's conspiracy, Eudokia's calculations, command decisions at Manzikert, Alp Arslan's conduct and settlement, the responsibility of court factions, and the account of Romanus's blinding and final letter require comparison with the Byzantine and Islamic evidence. The source's hindsight does not prove that his elevation was doomed from the start.

## What Changed

- Created a profile separating Romanus's battlefield capture from the civil conflict that destroyed his rule.
