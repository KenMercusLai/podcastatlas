## Overview
汉桓帝 / Emperor Huan of Han is a late-Han emperor whose current wiki profile now begins with his 146 CE installation as Liu Zhi by [[LiangJiLateHan]] and [[CaoTengLateHan]] after [[EmperorZhiOfHan]]'s death. His reign then moves through accession reward inversion, resentment toward [[LiGuLateHan]] and [[DuQiaoLateHan]], tolerated Liang-family dominance, a secret palace coup that destroys Liang Ji, and a post-Liang order that remains trapped in eunuch reliance, favorite protection, harem disorder, and unstable correction.

## Current Profile
[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-828-da-jiangjun-liangji-shanquan-feili-de-donghan-jianchen-llqnqijagtmdlgitulddxspncu0e|Hanji 828]] supplies the earliest current Huan layer. Liu Zhi is already near Luoyang because [[EmpressDowagerLiangNaLateHan]] had summoned him for a planned Liang marriage alliance. After Emperor Zhi dies, Cao Teng persuades Liang Ji that [[LiuSuanLateHan]] would threaten Liang-family guests and eunuch wealth; Liang Ji pressures the court to accept Liu Zhi, and the fifteen-year-old Liu Zhi enters the South Palace as Huan while Liang Na continues regency because he is not yet capped. The same source records Huan's post-accession ancestral honors for his grandfather Liu Kai, grandmother Lady Zhao, father Liu Yi, and mother Lady Yan.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-829-junzi-weishenme-doubuguo-xiaoren-lthvnstkfu8n9thx1sz6yezl4yqq|Hanji 829]] gives the first full reign-year layer. Huan rewards Liang Ji, Liang relatives, Hu Guang, Zhao Jie, Yuan Tang, and eunuchs including Liu Guang for the accession settlement, but gives no visible answer when Du Qiao argues that worthy appointments and fair rewards and punishments must come before favors to relatives and close attendants. Tang Heng and Zuo Guan later tell Huan that Li Gu and Du Qiao opposed his accession, and the Liu Wen-Liu Wei plot around Liu Suan keeps the unused accession alternative politically alive.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-836-xiongdi-fanmu-beihou-ruzhe-he-jianchen-de-jiaofeng-lq9wjn5c8mwt-onrtslum4fw53eq|Hanji 836]], [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-838-tanlan-de-youhuo-rang-ta-kuangbiao-zai-zuosi-de-lushang-lrydilyni6o6yzhk1-e74m6aigwr|Hanji 838]], [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-844-ruhe-liyu-bubaizhidi-lsp49uw8atyiufrf-ryo-9ctos5z|Hanji 844]], and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-845-cong-dangdai-hunyin-guan-liao-gudai-de-hunjian-zhidu-lr0i25twkoxm2wen7pnfbwt3qdsn|Hanji 845]] show the pre-coup Huan field. He ignores [[YangBingLateHan]]'s ritual-warning critique after a private outing, grants Liang extraordinary privilege even when [[HuangQiongLateHan]] limits part of the reward, uses [[ZhangHuanLateHan]] with some frontier prudence while leaving [[ChenGuiDuliaoGeneralLateHan]]'s anti-Liang memorial unanswered, and remains locked inside a cold Liang-family marriage to [[EmpressLiangYingLateHan]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-847-nvren-biande-haoming-cong-dongde-zhe-liang-zi-kaishi-lrlov43jtz0vq-o-5m8r2cqo0etx|Hanji 847]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-848-shunjian-kuatai-wo-weihe-shuo-ta-si-you-yu-gu-lgqvgmxlwga2fknmtxpj3ya9chk0|Hanji 848]] supply the anti-Liang execution layer. Huan privately consults Tang Heng, selects palace insiders who resent Liang, binds them through an oath, arrests Liang's palace monitor, controls offices, seals, tallies, and troops, removes Liang's general-in-chief seal, forces Liang and [[SunShouLateHan]] into suicide, liquidates the Liang-Sun network, and rewards the five eunuchs as the [[FiveMarquisesHuanEra]].

The post-coup layer is release from Liang without durable institutional recovery. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-850-huandi-weihe-zhiyi-sha-zhongchen-lokxtceq7vv-giec6iufwwk9fwk7|Hanji 850]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-851-hanhuandi-weihe-zhuding-yichou-wannian-ltgm1ix6cgqb-kqo9bztfrzjgeun|Hanji 851]] show [[LiYunLateHan]] and [[DuZhongLateHan]] dying after direct remonstrance is criminalized, Huang Qiong diagnosing eunuch and reward inversion without conversion, and the [[ShanChaoLateHan]] network reversing enforcement against Yang Bing and [[FifthZhongLateHan]]. Later sources show Huan can accept bounded harem, personnel, legal, frontier, or state-capacity arguments, but he preserves palace access structures, favorite power, harem disorder, and party-arrest mechanisms that make durable reform fail.

Huan's final layers turn private preference into public succession damage. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-878-bu-shou-yueshu-de-quanli-hairen-buqian-lvevnahcxshgbz5zyqbaf36pa7nk|Hanji 878]] presents coercive princess-marriage pressure on [[YangQiaoLateHan]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-879-si-zai-nvren-shenshang-de-hunyong-nanren-lqz5-mpi0waa-xmuhjhwkaecjjrc|Hanji 879]] shows affection for [[TianShengLateHan]] losing to [[EmpressDowagerDouMiao]]'s formal status, then Huan's death on 167-12-28 without a son. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-901-jixingzi-de-ren-hui-shi-shenme-xiachang-lh1yfyjzqptxvtnv9taiqms9yjud|Hanji 901]], [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-914-xinren-de-ren-weihe-zong-shang-ni-zui-shen-lvwit-rzrkscn5a3h6wblntxppso|Hanji 914]], and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-929-duishi-buduiren-ni-xin-le-ma-lgaxj0jzopddmt3c5hibnexqlhb3|Hanji 929]] preserve him as a burial-legitimacy object, predecessor comparison, and contested accession memory.

## Key Characteristics
- Accession-choice ruler installed at fifteen because Liang Ji and Cao Teng judge him safer than Liu Suan for incumbent power.
- Early Huan-era ruler whose 147-152 CE profile pairs accession reward inversion, ignored Du Qiao remonstrance, private household outings, ritual-omen warning, overgenerous Liang Ji reward, and bounded restraint after Wang Jing's Khotan failure.
- Anti-Liang ruler whose decisive capacity emerges only when palace access, seals, tallies, guards, and eunuch insiders align.
- Mixed correction recipient who can accept bounded harem, personnel, legal, or state-capacity arguments while refusing deeper palace-access reform.
- Ruler dependent on scarce frontier and court talent, capable of using practical personnel solutions when crisis is obvious.
- Omen-era ruler who solicits or tolerates criticism but rarely converts it into durable institutional reform.
- Private-desire, favorite-protection, and harem-status ruler whose companion choices, marriage pressure, childlessness, and death create public consequences.

## Qualifications
The page remains bounded to the listed podcast source notes, not a full reign biography. Hanji 828 moves the current start point back to 146 CE and explains why Huan's accession is already structured by Liang-family and eunuch self-protection; it also gives ancestral honor details that remain source-scoped until corroborated elsewhere. Hanji 829 explains why Liu Suan's unused candidacy, Du Qiao's accurate memorial, and eunuch slander matter to Huan's early legitimacy environment. Hanji 847 and Hanji 848 preserve a trigger tension: Hanji 847 narrates Xuan surviving a failed assassination attempt and reaching Huan, while Hanji 848 compresses or reports the trigger as her being killed. The current judgment is mixed rather than absolute: Huan can remove an overmighty outer-relative, create public benefit, and be moved by practical, legal, and state-capacity arguments, but those wins do not undo the palace, favorite, harem, and accusation structures that repeatedly defeat public correction.
