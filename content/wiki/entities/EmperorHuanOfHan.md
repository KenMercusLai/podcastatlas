## Overview

汉桓帝 / Emperor Huan of Han is a late-Han emperor whose current wiki profile now begins in 147 CE with accession rewards to [[LiangJiLateHan|梁冀]], Liang relatives, and eunuchs, [[DuQiaoLateHan|杜乔]]'s ignored appointment-and-reward memorial, [[EmpressLiangYingLateHan|梁盈]]'s empress appointment, and the failed [[LiuSuanLateHan|刘蒜]] plot before the later Liang-family marriage and coup sequence. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-836-xiongdi-fanmu-beihou-ruzhe-he-jianchen-de-jiaofeng-lq9wjn5c8mwt-onrtslum4fw53eq|Hanji 836]] shows Huan leaving the palace privately for Liang Ji's son and treating wind, darkness, and uprooted trees as ineffective warning when Yang Bing frames the outing as a ritual, hierarchy, palace-defense, and seal-security breach. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-838-tanlan-de-youhuo-rang-ta-kuangbiao-zai-zuosi-de-lushang-lrydilyni6o6yzhk1-e74m6aigwr|Hanji 838]] shows Huan accepting limits on Liang's territorial reward while still granting extraordinary privilege, then rejecting immediate punitive escalation after [[WangJingXiyuLateHan|王敬]]'s Khotan killing destabilizes [[KhotanKingdomLateHan|于阗国]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-844-ruhe-liyu-bubaizhidi-lsp49uw8atyiufrf-ryo-9ctos5z|Hanji 844]] shows him using [[ZhangHuanLateHan|张奂]] against the northern frontier crisis, rejecting Zhang's proposal to depose [[CheerChanyu|车儿]] after submission, and leaving [[ChenGuiDuliaoGeneralLateHan|陈龟]]'s anti-Liang memorial unanswered. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-845-cong-dangdai-hunyin-guan-liao-gudai-de-hunjian-zhidu-lr0i25twkoxm2wen7pnfbwt3qdsn|Hanji 845]] shows him married to Liang Ying, an empress backed by the Liang clan but not by affection or heirs. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-847-nvren-biande-haoming-cong-dongde-zhe-liang-zi-kaishi-lrlov43jtz0vq-o-5m8r2cqo0etx|Hanji 847]] then shows him still constrained by Liang, responding to [[XuanDengMotherLateHan|Xuan]] after Liang's failed assassination attempt, and secretly selecting palace insiders for an anti-Liang oath. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-848-shunjian-kuatai-wo-weihe-shuo-ta-si-you-yu-gu-lgqvgmxlwga2fknmtxpj3ya9chk0|Hanji 848]] shows him decisively destroying Liang through palace access, seals, tallies, guards, and confiscation. Yet [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-850-huandi-weihe-zhiyi-sha-zhongchen-lokxtceq7vv-giec6iufwwk9fwk7|Hanji 850]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-851-hanhuandi-weihe-zhuding-yichou-wannian-ltgm1ix6cgqb-kqo9bztfrzjgeun|Hanji 851]] show the freed court failing to recover a trustworthy feedback or enforcement system: [[LiYunLateHan|李云]] and [[DuZhongLateHan|杜仲]] die after direct remonstrance is criminalized, Huang Qiong's warning is ignored, and the [[ShanChaoLateHan|单超]] family corruption case punishes Yang Bing and [[FifthZhongLateHan|第五种]] rather than the protected offender. Later sources show Huan can accept bounded, practical, or precedent-backed correction, but he preserves palace access structures, favorite power, harem disorder, and party-arrest mechanisms that make durable reform fail.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-829-junzi-weishenme-doubuguo-xiaoren-lthvnstkfu8n9thx1sz6yezl4yqq|Hanji 829]] supplies the earliest current Huan layer. In 147 CE Huan's court records eclipse, amnesty, dragon, earthquake, and senior-office turnover while Du Qiao becomes taiwei after Hu Guang's removal. Huan rewards Liang Ji, Liang relatives, Hu Guang, Zhao Jie, Yuan Tang, and eunuchs including Liu Guang for the accession settlement, but gives no visible answer when Du Qiao argues that worthy appointments and fair rewards and punishments must come before favors to relatives and close attendants.

The same source shows accession memory turning into resentment. Du Qiao refuses Liang Ji's attempt to enlarge Liang Ying's wedding rites and rejects a corrupt shangshu recommendation. Later Tang Heng and Zuo Guan tell Huan that Du Qiao and Li Gu had opposed his accession and judged him unfit; Huan resents both men. The eleventh-month Liu Suan plot then makes the unused accession alternative politically active again: Liu Wen and Liu Wei attempt to install Liu Suan, Xie Hao is killed after refusing them, and Liu Suan is demoted, exiled, and dies by suicide.

Hanji 836 supplies the next current Huan layer. In 151 CE Huan privately leaves the palace for the home of Liang Ji's sixteen-year-old son Liang Yin. Yang Bing reads the same day's violent wind, darkness, and uprooted trees as Heaven's warning, then argues that imperial movement should be constrained by ritual purposes, hierarchy, palace defense, and the security of imperial seals. Huan gives no effective response, making the source an early [[OmenCodedRemonstrance|omen-coded remonstrance]] and [[ImperialRitualDistance|imperial ritual distance]] failure before the later Liang and eunuch branches.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-838-tanlan-de-youhuo-rang-ta-kuangbiao-zai-zuosi-de-lushang-lrydilyni6o6yzhk1-e74m6aigwr|Hanji 838]] then adds the early reward and frontier layer. In 151 CE Huan asks high officials to discuss rewarding Liang Ji, accepts Huang Qiong's limit on land and households, but still grants Liang extraordinary etiquette, wealth, residence, and Shangshu access; Liang remains dissatisfied. In 152 CE Wang Jing's Khotan banquet killing creates Western Regions disorder. Huan rejects Ma Da's punitive expedition and replaces him with Song Liang, showing bounded restraint after an envoy failure. The same source records Luoyang earthquakes, Xiaochong Empress's death and burial, an eclipse, and Huang Qiong's removal.

Hanji 844 supplies the next current Huan layer. Huan appoints Zhang Huan to answer the Southern Xiongnu and Wuhuan crisis, and after Zhang's victory he refuses to depose Che'er because hereditary order and the credibility of submitted frontier rulers matter alongside military capacity. The same source gives the darker court-side counterpart: Chen Gui memorializes against Liang Ji, Huan receives the warning without visible action, and Chen dies after a hunger strike.

Hanji 845 supplies the next current Huan layer. Before Huan can strike Liang Ji, he is married to Liang Ying, whose empress status depends on Liang-family power. Huan sends a female official to inspect her body and voice before marriage, but the procedure does not create desire, trust, or succession security: Liang Ying becomes jealous and childless, Huan avoids her after the Liang dowager dies, and her 159 death opens space for his anti-Liang timing.

The next current layer is Huan's secret anti-Liang recruitment. In Hanji 847, Xuan reaches the palace after Liang Ji's attempt to kill her, giving Huan a direct household-triggered crisis around Deng Meng. Huan privately consults [[TangHengLateHan|唐衡]], asks which close attendants resent the Liang family, and brings [[ShanChaoLateHan|单超]], [[ZuoGuanLateHan|左悺]], [[XuHuangLateHan|徐璜]], [[JuYuanLateHan|具瑗]], and Tang Heng into an oath-bound conspiracy. The oath scene, including Huan's blood drawn from Shan Chao's arm, frames Huan as frightened but willing to commit when palace insiders make action possible.

Hanji 848 supplies the execution layer. The source note compresses the Xuan trigger as Liang Ji having Deng Meng's mother killed, then shows Huan's side arresting Liang's palace monitor, convening the shangshu channel, guarding palace offices, seizing imperial tallies and seals, sending Ju Yuan and Zhang Biao to surround Liang's mansion, and removing Liang's general-in-chief seal. Liang and [[SunShouLateHan|孙寿]] die the same day. Huan then liquidates the Liang-Sun network, sells Liang property into the treasury, halves the year's land tax, distributes gardens to commoners, restores Deng Meng's Deng surname, and rewards the five eunuchs as the [[FiveMarquisesHuanEra|Five Marquises]].

The immediate post-coup layer is release from Liang without stable self-restraint. In Hanji 850, [[WeiHuanLateHan|魏桓]] refuses office because palace women, imperial horses, and powerful-family corruption look unreformable. Huan rewards Deng and eunuch-favorite networks, accepts [[HouLanLateHan|侯览]]'s claimed merit and gift channel, and grants the Five Marquises large rewards. When Li Yun questions whether the emperor hears truth, Huan arrests him; Du Zhong chooses shared-fate solidarity; intercessors are rebuked or punished; and Li Yun plus Du Zhong die in prison.

Hanji 851 keeps the same 159 CE field in view from a senior-minister and enforcement angle. Huang Qiong warns that Huan's reign has not surpassed the Liang order, eunuchs fill the court, loyal officials have been harmed, and corrupt actors share imperial favor with the loyal. Huan gives no visible answer. Shan Chao is then elevated while sick, Shan Kuang's corruption and attempted killing of Wei Yu are exposed, Yang Bing is punished after asking for confrontation, and Fifth Zhong is exiled after Shan Chao uses Shusun Wuji's disorder as pretext.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-852-dang-meinv-ruyun-shi-fu-haishi-huo-luml30ytbc-awpvk-7-23ehzbqpx|Hanji 852]] presents Huan's favor rewards and harem scale. [[ChenFanLateHan|陈蕃]] gets a partial correction when Huan releases more than five hundred palace women, but key rewards still proceed. [[YuanHongChenliuLateHan|袁闳]] can initially criticize Huan as a middle-grade ruler and be promoted, yet later receives no answer when he uses an omen memorial to criticize [[DengWanshiLateHan|邓万世]]'s excessive intimacy.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-853-jiemi-hanchao-ban-de-zhaoshi-gu-er-lvsa2pby8iyfevfoobobpik52mij|Hanji 853]] inserts a 160 CE repair-and-relapse layer. Huan searches for [[LiGuLateHan|李固]]'s descendants, but also grants Shan Chao lavish burial honors and dismisses [[TengYanLateHan|滕延]] after protected servants and guests tied to Hou Lan and [[DuanGuiLateHan|段珪]] are punished.

The 163-165 CE layers show limited movability. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-858-weishenme-quan-ni-yiding-yao-huanwei-sikao-lobr-xznqo-ctjpc-ytpstubmahl|Hanji 858]] contrasts Chen Fan's unanswered hunting remonstrance with Huan's acceptance of [[ZhouJingLateHan|周景]] and Yang Bing's concrete eunuch-linked officeholder review. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-859-gudai-haoren-weishenme-nan-dang-haoguan-llkyx18ssvserxncvrtkw7xfduad|Hanji 859]] shows Huan using needed frontier personnel such as [[HuangfuGuiLateHan|皇甫规]], [[ZhangHuanLateHan|张奂]], and [[DuanJiongLateHan|段熲]], while rejecting [[ZhuMuLateHan|朱穆]]'s anti-eunuch access reform. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-865-zhichang-boss-ye-rongyi-bei-zhezhong-ren-beici-ljfhfevljqnx6zceakjfbw5gwfma|Hanji 865]] shows Yang Bing forcing Hou Lan's removal through a precedent-backed jurisdiction argument, and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-868-ying-le-ye-shi-shu-jia-donghan-hougong-zhengyan-ji-lmmjgx23nlkeqr3mkzliij-zwe2v|Hanji 868]] places local governance correction and the [[TianShengLateHan|田圣 / 田胜]] versus [[EmpressDowagerDouMiao|窦妙]] empress decision in the same reign.

The 166-167 CE layers explain why corrections do not become recovery. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-869-liying-weihe-bei-chengwei-tianxia-diyi-kaimo-lltckcioe1n9v91yy4c9vvwmz4mo|Hanji 869]] shows Huan pardoning [[LiYingLateHan|李膺]] through [[YingFengLateHan|应奉]]'s state-capacity framing and backing Li Ying against [[ZhangRangLateHan|张让]]'s complaint. But [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-870-gudai-tizhinei-buganle-meiyou-tuilu-lpwbniu9ifpt6cp4ip9czqwsh5xd|Hanji 870]], [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-872-ta-quqie-bucheng-jing-sharen-quanjia-ltbegc7gbet2-hlhwhtevvltgnlc|Hanji 872]], [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-873-ta-ruhe-liyong-tianxiang-tongma-diwang-lqebkodcbcrsvyf80qwxnobydbhc|Hanji 873]], [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-874-zhenjing-muzi-xiangcan-tianli-nanrong-lpr3eglbnwbve1clrttapw6k5zdq|Hanji 874]], and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-876-zhichang-zong-pengbi-shuoming-ni-meiyou-zhaodui-guanxi-lrjob-a07t29hdr0iogrmcsjr7qp|Hanji 876]] show harem-burden criticism, eunuch-linked enforcement reversal, omen-coded remonstrance, party-arrest authorization, and Chen Fan's removal before indirect rescue works through [[JiaBiaoLateHan|贾彪]] and [[DouWuLateHan|窦武]].

Huan's private sphere becomes public damage. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-878-bu-shou-yueshu-de-quanli-hairen-buqian-lvevnahcxshgbz5zyqbaf36pa7nk|Hanji 878]] presents his coercive princess-marriage pressure on [[YangQiaoLateHan|杨桥]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-879-si-zai-nvren-shenshang-de-hunyong-nanren-lqz5-mpi0waa-xmuhjhwkaecjjrc|Hanji 879]] shows affection for Tian Sheng losing to Dou Miao's formal status, then Huan's death on 167-12-28 without a son. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-901-jixingzi-de-ren-hui-shi-shenme-xiachang-lh1yfyjzqptxvtnv9taiqms9yjud|Hanji 901]], [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-914-xinren-de-ren-weihe-zong-shang-ni-zui-shen-lvwit-rzrkscn5a3h6wblntxppso|Hanji 914]], and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-929-duishi-buduiren-ni-xin-le-ma-lgaxj0jzopddmt3c5hibnexqlhb3|Hanji 929]] preserve him as a burial-legitimacy object, predecessor comparison, and contested accession memory.

## Key Characteristics

- Early Huan-era ruler whose 147-152 CE profile pairs accession reward inversion, ignored Du Qiao remonstrance, Liu Suan succession resentment, private household outings, ritual-omen warning, overgenerous Liang Ji reward, and bounded restraint after Wang Jing's Khotan failure.
- Anti-Liang ruler whose sequence now begins with tolerated overreward before unanswered Chen Gui warning, cold Liang-family marriage, Xuan's assassination-trigger branch, secret eunuch oath, palace coup execution, public settlement, eunuch overreward, lethal remonstrance punishment, ignored senior warning, and protected-family enforcement reversal.
- Mixed correction recipient who can accept bounded harem, personnel, legal, or state-capacity arguments while refusing deeper palace-access reform.
- Ruler dependent on scarce frontier and court talent, capable of using practical personnel solutions when crisis is obvious.
- Ruler whose eunuch reliance is framed as a palace-control structure, not merely a personnel mistake.
- Omen-era ruler who solicits or tolerates criticism but rarely converts it into durable institutional reform.
- Private-desire, favorite-protection, and harem-status ruler whose companion choices, marriage pressure, childlessness, and death create public consequences.

## Qualifications

The page remains bounded to the listed podcast source notes, not a full reign biography. Hanji 829 moves the current start point back to 147 CE and explains why Liu Suan's unused candidacy, Du Qiao's accurate memorial, and eunuch slander matter to Huan's early legitimacy environment; it does not reconstruct the whole accession debate. Hanji 836 adds the later ritual-warning failure, but the Liang Yin visit and disaster interpretation are source-scoped and should not be treated as a complete account of Huan's movement practices. Hanji 838 moves the reward and frontier starting point earlier but does not overturn the mixed judgment: Huan can restrain punitive escalation after a frontier failure while still normalizing extreme privilege for Liang Ji. Hanji 844 makes the current judgment more mixed: Huan's Che'er decision can be read as prudent legitimacy management after surrender, while his nonaction after Chen Gui's memorial remains a court-feedback failure. Hanji 845's marriage-inspection details and modern marriage survey are source-scoped and should not be converted into independent medical, demographic, or historiographical claims. Hanji 847 and Hanji 848 preserve a trigger tension: Hanji 847 narrates Xuan surviving a failed assassination attempt and reaching Huan, while the Hanji 848 source note compresses or reports the trigger as her being killed. The current page treats Hanji 847 as the fuller pre-coup setup until a later source resolves the wording. Hanji 848 clarifies that Huan has real capacity for decisive command when palace access, symbols, and armed force are aligned under his will. Hanji 851 strengthens the later feedback-collapse and enforcement-reversal picture, but it also clarifies chronology: Yang Bing's punishment precedes his later restoration and successful anti-Hou Lan case, and Shan Chao's sickbed activity precedes his Hanji 853 death and burial. The current judgment is mixed rather than absolute: Huan can remove an overmighty outer-relative, create public benefit, and be moved by practical, legal, and state-capacity arguments, but those wins do not undo the palace, favorite, harem, and accusation structures that repeatedly defeat public correction.

## What Changed

- Hanji 829 moves the earliest Huan profile back to 147 CE, adding accession reward inversion, ignored Du Qiao remonstrance, Liang Ying's appointment, eunuch slander, and Liu Suan's failed enthronement aftermath.
- Hanji 838 remains the early reward and frontier layer, adding Liang Ji reward excess and restraint after Wang Jing's Khotan failure.
- Hanji 844 adds a frontier legitimacy decision around Che'er and Chen Gui's ignored anti-Liang memorial as a pre-coup feedback-failure signal.
- Hanji 845 keeps the Liang Ying marriage layer: body inspection, cold empress status, childlessness, and her death before anti-Liang action.
- Hanji 847 and Hanji 848 keep the anti-Liang execution layer while preserving the Xuan-trigger wording caution.
