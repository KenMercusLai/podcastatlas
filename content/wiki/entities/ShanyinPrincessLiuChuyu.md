## Overview

山阴公主刘楚玉 / Shanyin Princess Liu Chuyu is represented through a strongly moralized Liu-Song story that joins female desire, imperial privilege, the changing meaning of “面首,” and dynastic violence.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-616-plus-pan-dian-shi-shang-de-hao-se-nv-ren-men-lgsf8_asfrictdmsptsyfizykpon|Hanji 616 plus]] says Liu Chuyu compared an emperor's access to many women with her own single husband and then received thirty young male attendants from [[LiuZiyeFormerDeposedEmperor|刘子业]]. [[28-dongxifang-de-mianshou-cong-xuehuaiyi-dao-bojiangjin-lkuahrvj2vxdgkkf7n0prtfw3pl0|Weird History episode 28]] adds a lexical qualification: “面首” could still mean appearance or handsome attendants in the cited passage, while later retelling of the sensational story may have helped stabilize its specialized meaning as male lover or favorite.

The current judgment therefore separates reception from secure biography. Liu Chuyu's story clearly became a vehicle for debating unequal sexual access and condemning powerful female desire, but the exact status and sexual role of the thirty attendants are not established by the phrase alone.

## Key Characteristics

- Liu-Song princess remembered through intensely moralized accounts of sexuality and palace privilege.
- Voices a comparison between an emperor's many partners and a princess's single husband in the source tradition.
- Is associated with thirty “面首,” whose exact original status and sexual role remain disputed.
- Becomes central to the later specialized meaning of “面首” through reception and repetition.
- Dies in the political reversal following Liu Ziye's assassination in the compressed account.

## Evidence

- Unequal-access complaint: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-616-plus-pan-dian-shi-shang-de-hao-se-nv-ren-men-lgsf8_asfrictdmsptsyfizykpon|Hanji 616 plus]] says Liu Chuyu contrasts imperial sexual abundance with her own marriage.
- Thirty attendants: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-616-plus-pan-dian-shi-shang-de-hao-se-nv-ren-men-lgsf8_asfrictdmsptsyfizykpon|Hanji 616 plus]] treats the `Song Shu` phrase as a grant of thirty male attendants.
- Lexical reception: [[28-dongxifang-de-mianshou-cong-xuehuaiyi-dao-bojiangjin-lkuahrvj2vxdgkkf7n0prtfw3pl0|Episode 28]] says the phrase may originally have referred to handsome attendants and argues that the story's circulation helped produce the later sexual sense.
- Political death: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-616-plus-pan-dian-shi-shang-de-hao-se-nv-ren-men-lgsf8_asfrictdmsptsyfizykpon|Hanji 616 plus]] says she was ordered to die after Liu Ziye was killed.

## Qualifications

The alleged sibling incest, the attendants' exact role, the charges after the coup, and the full textual tradition are not independently adjudicated. The newer source directly qualifies the older page's certainty about “thirty male attendants”: later usage supports that reading, but the original wording may have denoted handsome retainers without specifying sexual relations.

## What Changed

- Reclassified the thirty “面首” from a settled sexual fact to a contested lexical and reception problem.
- Added the possibility that later circulation of Liu Chuyu's story helped fix the term's specialized meaning.
- Retained the durable power-and-memory pattern without treating every sensational detail as verified biography.
