## Overview

山阴公主刘楚玉 / Shanyin Princess Liu Chuyu enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-616-plus-pan-dian-shi-shang-de-hao-se-nv-ren-men-lgsf8_asfrictdmsptsyfizykpon|Hanji 616 plus]] as the Liu-Song princess whose story joins female desire, imperial privilege, moralized sexual memory, and dynastic violence.

## Current Profile

The episode says Liu Chuyu married He Ji, had an incestuous relationship with her brother [[LiuZiyeFormerDeposedEmperor|刘子业]], and later complained that an emperor could possess many women while a princess had only one husband. Liu Ziye then allegedly granted her thirty young male attendants. The source treats this as extreme female sexual privilege and says Liu Chuyu was ordered to die after Liu Ziye's assassination.

The page does not adopt every detail as verified. Its durable profile is that Liu Chuyu's later memory became a vehicle for debating unequal sexual access and for condemning a politically powerful woman's desire, while the same palace structure depended on her brother's coercive and violent rule.

## Key Characteristics

- Liu-Song princess remembered through intensely moralized accounts of sexuality.
- Sister of Liu Ziye and wife of He Ji in the episode's narrative.
- Voices a comparison between an emperor's many partners and a princess's single husband.
- Receives thirty male attendants through imperial authority in the source account.
- Dies in the political reversal following Liu Ziye's assassination.

## Qualifications

The episode is sensational and strongly condemnatory. The alleged sibling incest, the exact meaning and status of the thirty attendants, the charges used after the coup, and the full source tradition are not independently adjudicated here. The page therefore distinguishes a durable power-and-memory pattern from disputed biographical detail.
