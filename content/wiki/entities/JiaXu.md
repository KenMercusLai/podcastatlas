# 贾诩 / Jia Xu

## Overview
贾诩 / Jia Xu is a late-Han adviser whose wiki profile combines survival-first calculation, dangerous tactical craft, host-risk reading, and condition-sensitive patron choice. He can save himself and others through precise advice, but the same advice can empower violent executors when the user of the strategy is unstable.

## Current Profile
Jia Xu's current synthesis is not reducible to the "poison adviser" label. He advises Dong Zhuo's remnant commanders to attack Chang'an because dispersal looks fatal, then later refuses reward and works to reduce harm around Emperor Xian. He mediates among Liangzhou warlords, leaves hosts before suspicion becomes lethal, advises Zhang Xiu's Liu Biao alignment and Cao Cao surrender, helps convert Wancheng exposure into attack, and later judges that Cao Cao is the better patron than Yuan Shao because name, need, and public magnanimity matter more than raw size.

## Key Characteristics
- Survival strategist who treats amnesty refusal, troop dispersal, host suspicion, and court captivity as immediate life-and-death constraints.
- Executor-risk aware adviser whose correct tactical advice can still generate destructive outcomes when violent commanders control implementation.
- Patron-risk reader who compares hosts by suspicion, need for talent, legitimacy, decisiveness, and capacity to tolerate capable outsiders.
- Tactical designer of permission and timing, visible in Zhang Xiu's camp-movement plan before the Wancheng night attack.
- Reputation manager who can appear dangerous when useful, then reduce visibility or refuse credit when survival requires restraint.
- Legitimacy-and-marginal-value analyst whose Cao-over-Yuan advice turns Zhang Xiu's old blood debt into a recoverable surrender opportunity.

## Qualifications
The sources keep Jia Xu's moral status mixed. The Chang'an advice is intelligible as survival but helps violent commanders seize the capital; later restraint does not erase that cost. The "poison adviser" frame is source-scoped and should not flatten all of Jia Xu's conduct into pure malice. The renewed submission branch shows accurate patron analysis, not a general guarantee that Cao Cao would forgive every former enemy.

## What Changed
- Integrated Jia Xu's renewed Cao-over-Yuan advice into his broader host-risk and patron-choice profile.
- Added the emperor-backed legitimacy, marginal-value, and public-magnanimity logic behind Zhang Xiu's second submission to Cao Cao.
- Shifted the page from source-by-source chronology into a synthesis-first profile that preserves both destructive advice risk and protective restraint.
