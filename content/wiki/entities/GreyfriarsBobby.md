# Greyfriars Bobby

## Overview

Greyfriars Bobby is an Edinburgh dog remembered for supposedly remaining beside his owner John Gray's grave for fourteen years, a loyalty story sustained through commemoration, tourism, literature, and repeated embellishment.

## Current Profile

The traditional story identifies Bobby as a Skye terrier belonging to night watchman John Gray. After Gray died, Bobby allegedly remained at the grave in Greyfriars kirkyard, while local supporters fed him and the Lord Provost paid for his dog license.

The episode treats the story as neither pure invention nor transparent biography. A fountain and statue fixed Bobby in Edinburgh's landscape, while later accounts and Eleanor Atkinson's novel added routine and sentiment. The durable historical fact is therefore the public-memory system around the dog, even where the exact grave-side behavior is difficult to recover.

## Key Characteristics

- Edinburgh dog associated with Greyfriars kirkyard and John Gray's grave.
- Publicly remembered as a model of loyalty beyond death.
- Supported in the story by local feeding and civic licensing.
- Materially commemorated through a fountain and statue.
- Biography expanded through tourism, commercial interest, and literary retelling.

## Qualifications

The source emphasizes evidentiary uncertainty and interested retelling. Bobby may have had a real association with the graveyard, but the fourteen-year vigil, daily routine, exact breed, ownership, and motives should not be treated as equally documented.

## What Changed

- Created a profile centered on the distinction between recoverable biography and secure civic afterlife.
- Preserved tourism and literary embellishment as part of the history rather than only as contamination of it.
