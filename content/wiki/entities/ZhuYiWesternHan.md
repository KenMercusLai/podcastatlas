## Overview

朱邑 / Zhu Yi enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-439-gongbi-weiqing-huoqubing-zhichang-guanlizhe-bangyang-zhaochongguo-lirqsoujwewu6efd-shb7mypqvu3|Hanji 439]] as a Western Han senior fiscal official whose clean conduct and local reputation form the episode's non-military counterpart to [[ZhaoChongguoWesternHan|赵充国]].

## Current Profile

The source presents Zhu Yi as 大司农, a high minister responsible for national rent, grain, money, salt, iron, and fiscal revenue. His public image is not built from spectacular reform in this note, but from frugal living, refusal of private accumulation, care for kin and local community, and a death that leaves little money for burial.

Emperor Xuan praises Zhu as clean, principled, public-minded, non-factional, and incorrupt, and grants his son gold for sacrifice. Zhu's own burial wish shifts the measure of reputation from court rank to community memory: he wants to be buried where he once served locally, trusting that the people there will remember him better than descendants performing routine family sacrifice. The source says local people build and maintain worship for him after his death.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han 大司农 whose office tied him to state finance, grain, salt, and iron administration.
- Clean and frugal official whose household did not retain large surplus wealth after death.
- Public-minded figure praised by Emperor Xuan for integrity, non-factional conduct, and refusal of bribery.
- Official whose generosity was directed toward kin and local community rather than private luxury.
- Local-memory figure whose burial wish and later worship make popular gratitude part of his historical profile.
