## Overview
莎车王康 / King Kang of Shache is an early Eastern Han Western Regions ruler whose current wiki profile comes from [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-699-gan-yu-liuxiu-zhengxiong-de-ta-weihe-candan-shouchang-lth-8mndudj3khf9cnsouj86c1-z|Hanji 699]]. He inherits [[ShacheKingYanEasternHan|King Yan]]'s Han-loyal instruction, resists Xiongnu pressure, protects Han officials and families left in the Western Regions, and receives recognition through [[DouRongEasternHan|Dou Rong]].

## Current Profile
Hanji 699 presents Kang as the active successor to King Yan's loyalty. After Wang Mang-era disruption and renewed Xiongnu pressure weaken Han control, Kang keeps Shache oriented toward Han rather than simply submitting to the stronger local pressure.

His most concrete action is protection. The episode says Kang protects former Han Protectorate officials and families, numbering more than a thousand people, and asks Hexi for news of the central realm. Dou Rong follows precedent and gives Kang titles that recognize both Shache kingship and broader Western Regions responsibility.

Kang's profile matters because it backfills a practical proxy phase before Liu Xiu later faces the King Xian overrecognition problem. Shache can be a useful Han-oriented partner, but recognition also creates hierarchy and credibility problems once the court lacks full frontier capacity.

## Key Characteristics
- Han-loyal Shache ruler shaped by King Yan's intergenerational instruction.
- Resists Xiongnu pressure during a period of weakened Han control.
- Protects stranded Han officials and families in the Western Regions.
- Uses Hexi and Dou Rong as the recognition channel back to the restored Han order.
- Pre-Xian proxy figure who complicates Shache's later contested-recognition profile.

## Qualifications
This page is source-bounded to Hanji 699. The exact Chinese title formula, the "fifty-five states" count, and the administrative meaning of Kang's recognized authority remain source-scoped until additional source notes develop them. Kang is kept separate from later King Xian and later Shache succession figures.
