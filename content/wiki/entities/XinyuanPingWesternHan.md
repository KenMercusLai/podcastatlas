## Overview

新垣平 / Xinyuan Ping is a Western Han fangshi whom the current source presents as converting claims of privileged access to qi, omens, and sacred objects into rewards and influence over [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]]'s ritual decisions before an accusation and investigation expose the scheme.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-226-zhuangshen-nonggui-de-xinyuanping-shi-ruhe-bei-hanwendi-shipo-de-ljxpb9_fn-jmatlg621chtclqn3j|Hanji 226]] gives Xinyuan Ping a three-stage authority path. He first claims to see five-colored qi and a sacred object, prompting Liu Heng to build a temple, perform sacrifice, grant office and rewards, and include him in discussions of institutions and major rites. He then arranges a stronger form of apparent verification: after predicting a precious object outside the palace, an associate presents a jade cup inscribed “人主延寿.” Prediction and proof therefore come from the same controlled scheme.

The later claims scale upward. A reported second solar culmination is linked to a restart of the reign count and public feasting, while a supposed Zhou鼎 at Fenyin prompts temple construction in anticipation of an object that has not appeared. The source does not establish the mechanism behind the solar report, so only the staged cup can be described confidently as a concrete prop-based fraud within this bounded evidence.

His fall begins not with independent imperial detection but with an unnamed petitioner's accusation. The episode says a廷尉 investigation confirms fabrication, after which Liu Heng executes Xinyuan Ping and exterminates his three kin groups. The case changes the patron as well as removing the claimant: Liu Heng reportedly loses interest in calendar, color, and spirit projects and stops personally visiting the altars.

## Key Characteristics

- Fangshi who claims exclusive ability to read qi, sacred objects, and auspicious signs.
- Court entrepreneur who converts unverifiable interpretation into office, wealth, ritual construction, and policy access.
- Stager of a prediction-and-confirmation loop through the prearranged jade cup.
- Escalating claimant whose signs move from a temple to chronology, public feasting, and dynastic-symbolic projects.
- Fraud defendant exposed through accusation and investigation rather than through the ruler's unaided skepticism.

## Qualifications

The profile rests on one popular-history podcast note rather than a primary-source dossier. It does not independently establish the signs, dates, rewards, ritual details, solar mechanism, confession, legal procedure, or punishment scope. The title's “被汉文帝识破” compresses a sequence that the episode itself begins with external denunciation. The廷尉 name appears as “张世芝” in the supplied material and is not normalized here. Sharing the uncommon surname with [[XinyuanYan|新垣衍]] does not establish a family relationship.
