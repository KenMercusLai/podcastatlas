# Smithfield

## Overview

Smithfield is a historic open space and market district beside the old City of London where livestock commerce, religion, fairs, tournaments, executions, revolt, and commemoration repeatedly overlapped.

## Current Profile

The episode presents Smithfield less as a single monument than as a layered urban field. Its size outside the medieval walls supported cattle trading, butchery, St Bartholomew's Fair, tournaments, assembly, and exemplary punishment. The neighboring priory and hospital added worship, care, and institutional continuity, while the downhill route toward the [[RiverFleet]] connected the market to waste disposal and urban ecology.

Later memorials convert the same ground into a contest over public memory. William Wallace, [[WatTyler]], the rebels of 1381, and Protestants burned under Mary I are remembered through plaques or monuments created long after the events, so the district preserves both violence and later political interpretations of it.

## Key Characteristics

- Large open space beside medieval London used for livestock, fairs, tournaments, gathering, and punishment.
- Meat-market district whose location and waste were tied to the nearby Fleet valley.
- Setting for William Wallace's execution and the Smithfield confrontation of the [[PeasantsRevolt1381]].
- Site of Protestant burnings under Mary I and later Protestant commemoration.
- Neighbor of St Bartholomew's priory, church, and hospital.
- Memorial landscape in which later national, radical, and confessional narratives reinterpret earlier events.

## Qualifications

The source is a walking-tour synthesis rather than a complete market, archaeological, or topographical history. Etymology, smells, waste practices, fair chronology, casualty and execution details, discovered bones and ashes, and claims about the motives behind later memorials remain source-scoped.
