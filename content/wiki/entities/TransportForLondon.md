# Transport for London

## Overview

Transport for London is the public transport authority used in the episode as a practical test case for reflective bus-roof cooling.

## Current Profile

London buses have reportedly used white roofs since 2004 to reflect sunlight and reduce cabin heat. Record heat complaints in 2025 nevertheless led scientists, engineers, and Transport for London to investigate stronger coatings, including a UCL material reported to reflect about 94% of sunlight.

The source positions TfL as a deployment partner rather than the coating's inventor. Its relevance is operational: bus materials must turn laboratory reflectivity into durable cooling under weather, dirt, maintenance, safety, and passenger-comfort constraints.

## Key Characteristics

- Public transport authority associated with London's bus network.
- Early user of white bus roofs as a passive heat-reduction measure.
- Operator facing increased passenger heat complaints.
- Potential real-world test environment for highly reflective cooling coatings.

## Evidence

- Existing-practice evidence: [[coffer-stop-ai-may-shrink-tax-base-6abf81fdb06169dc7218d384]] says London buses have had white roofs since 2004.
- Operational-pressure evidence: [[coffer-stop-ai-may-shrink-tax-base-6abf81fdb06169dc7218d384]] says bus heat complaints reached a record in 2025.
- Research-connection evidence: [[coffer-stop-ai-may-shrink-tax-base-6abf81fdb06169dc7218d384]] connects TfL with scientists and engineers assessing improved reflective paint.

## Qualifications

The episode does not establish fleet-wide adoption of the UCL coating, measured passenger-temperature reductions, durability, cost, procurement status, or implementation dates. White roofs and advanced radiative-cooling coatings should not be treated as identical technologies.
