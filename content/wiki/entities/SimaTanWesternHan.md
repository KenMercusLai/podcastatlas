## Overview

司马谈 / Sima Tan is the Western Han Grand Historian and father of [[SimaQian|司马迁]]. The current source set connects him to both textual transmission and court ritual: his personal name helps explain a received name variant in [[Shiji|《史记》]], while Hanji 350-2 places him among the officials who recommend regular Taiyi worship to [[HanWudi|汉武帝]].

## Current Profile

The two branches should remain distinct. Zhouji 96 uses Sima Tan indirectly to explain why 李谈 may appear as 李同 under name avoidance, with inconsistent application preventing a mechanical rule. Hanji 350-2 presents him directly as taishi ling and says he and the sacrificial official Kuanshu support making the Ganquan heavenly rite triennial after ritual personnel report light and yellow vapor. That establishes an attributed advisory role, not sole authorship of Wudi's ritual policy or proof that Sima Tan personally manufactured the signs.

## Key Characteristics

- Father of Sima Qian and a biographical reference point for interpreting name avoidance in the received Shiji text.
- Grand Historian associated in Hanji 350-2 with court deliberation over Taiyi worship at Ganquan.
- Participant in an attributed recommendation that converts a reported sign and one ritual occasion into a recurring three-year schedule.

## Evidence

- **Textual afterlife:** [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-96-maosui-zijian-4-lqs3pj2-rebp0uh6q7ayecpju3hk|Zhouji 96 part 4]] explains the 李谈／李同 variation through Sima Qian's avoidance of his father's personal name, while noting that the received text is not fully consistent.
- **Court office and ritual advice:** [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-350-2-weishenme-buneng-re-dongshizhang-mishu-lmxrw9cg4lsqzzbx_628vwqtwfgu|Hanji 350-2]] identifies Sima Tan as Sima Qian's father and taishi ling, then places him with Kuanshu among those proposing a Taiyi rite every three years.
- **Institutional conversion:** [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-350-2-weishenme-buneng-re-dongshizhang-mishu-lmxrw9cg4lsqzzbx_628vwqtwfgu|Hanji 350-2]] says Wudi considers and accepts the proposal, linking technical-ritual personnel to a repeat imperial observance.

## Qualifications

The current sources do not provide Sima Tan's full intellectual biography, authorship, family life, or complete court career. The name-avoidance explanation is a source-critical interpretation rather than a perfectly consistent substitution law. Hanji 350-2 does not establish whether Sima Tan believed the reported light and vapor, originated the proposal, or merely joined an existing recommendation; Kuanshu's full identity and the exact ritual offices also remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Created a canonical profile joining Sima Tan's textual afterlife to his attributed Ganquan ritual advice.
- Kept the triennial-worship recommendation separate from claims about supernatural signs or private belief.
