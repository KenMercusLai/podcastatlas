## Overview

Liu Zhi is the Taiyuan administrator whose source-bounded role parallels [[ChengJinLateHan|成瑨]]: he punishes a protected local offender, [[ZhaoJinLateHan|赵津]], and then becomes a target of eunuch-backed accusation. Hanji 871 supplies the enforcement setup, while Hanji 873 and Hanji 874 preserve the case as a later remonstrance example and prison-death aftermath.

## Current Profile

Liu Zhi's current profile is an enforcement-rebound case. In Hanji 871, Zhao Jin is a small yellow-gate eunuch whose abuse harms a county. Liu Zhi sends [[WangYunLateHan|王允]] to arrest him and kills him after an amnesty. That action is paired with Cheng Jin's execution of Zhang Fan at Wan.

The case then moves from local order to palace retaliation. Hou Lan uses Zhang Fan's wife's petition and eunuch complaint channels to accuse both Cheng Jin and Liu Zhi. Later source notes keep Liu Zhi as one of the capable officials defended by Xiang Kai and as one of the men whose death in prison shapes Jia Biao's refusal to shelter Cen Zhi. His page therefore records a local official whose punitive action is morally defended but procedurally exposed.

## Key Characteristics

- Taiyuan administrator acting against a locally harmful eunuch.
- Superior who sends Wang Yun to arrest Zhao Jin.
- Parallel to Cheng Jin in the post-amnesty enforcement-rebound chain.
- Remonstrance example of an official endangered by eunuch slander.
- Prison-death case that later changes how others assess fugitive shelter.

## Qualifications

The page uses 刘质 / Liu Zhi for the Taiyuan administrator and keeps the `TaiyuanLateHan` suffix to avoid confusion with unrelated modern or late-Han Liu names. The evidence defends his public purpose but also preserves the post-amnesty timing that opponents exploit.
