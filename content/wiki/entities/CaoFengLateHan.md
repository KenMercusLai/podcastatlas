## Overview

曹凤 / Cao Feng is the Emperor-He-era official whose West Sea memorial makes early Eastern Han Qiang policy look like a resource and administration problem rather than a purely military one. In the current wiki he is a focused source-bounded figure: his importance lies in diagnosing why the [[ShaodangQiang|Shaodang Qiang]] could repeatedly challenge Han control and in proposing [[FrontierResourceIntegration|frontier resource integration]] as the answer.

## Current Profile

Hanji 773 presents Cao Feng as a kingdom chancellor who reports to [[EmperorHeOfHan|Emperor He]] after earlier Qiang unrest. He argues that Shaodang Qiang power grows from concrete advantages around the West Sea: fertile land in the Yu valleys and access to fishery and salt revenue. The episode treats this as an economic diagnosis of frontier rebellion.

Cao's proposed repair combines settlement, food supply, and state control. He recommends garrison farming, movement of Han populations into the area, intermarriage or cultural blending, and tighter administration of agriculture and salt. Emperor He accepts the memorial, restores the old West Sea commandery area, moves the Jincheng western commandant office there, appoints Cao Feng to that command, and expands farming before later An-era Qiang rebellion interrupts completion.

## Key Characteristics

- Frontier adviser whose diagnosis starts from land, fishery, salt, and local wealth.
- Policy designer who links garrison farming, migration, intermarriage, and resource administration.
- Official whose memorial Emperor He accepts, giving the proposal an implementation layer rather than leaving it as abstract advice.
- Source-bounded figure whose current wiki role is policy diagnosis rather than a full biography.

## Qualifications

The page rests on one source note and should not be treated as a complete biography of Cao Feng. The exact kingdom office, West Sea administrative geography, and later career remain source-scoped. The policy appears plausible inside the episode's interpretation, but the source also says later rebellion interrupts the work, so acceptance does not prove durable stabilization.
