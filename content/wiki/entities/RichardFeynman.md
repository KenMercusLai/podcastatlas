## Overview

Richard Feynman appears in the bounded source as the model of an independent catastrophic-failure investigator during the Challenger inquiry.

## Current Profile

[[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-bill-gurley-searching-for-feynman-42956628|Bill Gurley's presentation]] emphasizes Feynman's direct interviews, cold-temperature O-ring demonstration, and refusal to let a softened commission report obscure his conclusion. The profile is source-scoped to investigative method rather than a full account of Feynman's physics career or the Challenger investigation.

## Key Characteristics

- Pursues evidence independently of institutional choreography.
- Makes a technical failure publicly legible through a simple demonstration.
- Treats disagreement inside a commission as something to disclose rather than smooth over.
- Represents personal credibility joined to persistence and inspectable evidence.

## Evidence

- Direct inquiry: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-bill-gurley-searching-for-feynman-42956628|Bill Gurley's presentation]] says Feynman visited NASA facilities and interviewed personnel independently.
- Public demonstration: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-bill-gurley-searching-for-feynman-42956628|Bill Gurley's presentation]] recounts his ice-water demonstration of cold-stiffened O-ring material.
- Report independence: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-bill-gurley-searching-for-feynman-42956628|Bill Gurley's presentation]] says he threatened to remove his name when the commission report softened his findings, after which his notes appeared as an appendix.

## Qualifications

The source compresses a complex investigation into an investigator archetype. It does not provide a complete Challenger chronology, assign every causal responsibility, or establish that one individual can substitute for a properly empowered multidisciplinary inquiry.
