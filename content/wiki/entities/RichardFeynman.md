## Overview

Richard Feynman appears in the bounded sources as both a model of independent catastrophic-failure investigation and an example of continuing attachment after bereavement.

## Current Profile

[[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-bill-gurley-searching-for-feynman-42956628|Bill Gurley's presentation]] emphasizes Feynman's direct interviews, cold-temperature O-ring demonstration, and refusal to let a softened commission report obscure his conclusion. [[the-science-process-of-healing-from-grief-scim7724129089|The grief episode]] uses his letter to his deceased wife Arline to show that explicit knowledge of death can coexist with love, address, and an internal relationship. Together these appearances concern legible evidence and lived attachment, not a full account of Feynman's life or physics.

## Key Characteristics

- Pursues evidence independently of institutional choreography.
- Makes a technical failure publicly legible through a simple demonstration.
- Treats disagreement inside a commission as something to disclose rather than smooth over.
- Represents personal credibility joined to persistence and inspectable evidence.
- Illustrates how a continuing bond can remain emotionally active after explicit acceptance of a death.

## Evidence

- Direct inquiry: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-bill-gurley-searching-for-feynman-42956628|Bill Gurley's presentation]] says Feynman visited NASA facilities and interviewed personnel independently.
- Public demonstration: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-bill-gurley-searching-for-feynman-42956628|Bill Gurley's presentation]] recounts his ice-water demonstration of cold-stiffened O-ring material.
- Report independence: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-bill-gurley-searching-for-feynman-42956628|Bill Gurley's presentation]] says he threatened to remove his name when the commission report softened his findings, after which his notes appeared as an appendix.
- Continuing attachment: [[the-science-process-of-healing-from-grief-scim7724129089|The grief episode]] reads Feynman's letter to Arline as an example of knowing a person has died while continuing to address and love them.

## Qualifications

The sources compress distinct parts of Feynman's life into two illustrative examples. They do not provide a complete biography, physics profile, Challenger chronology, or account of his marriage and bereavement. The letter is used interpretively to explain continuing attachment, not to diagnose Feynman's grief.
