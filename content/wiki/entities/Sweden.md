# Sweden

## Overview

Sweden appears as the refuge that made the 1943 [[RescueOfTheDanishJews]] operationally possible and, in a separate contemporary branch, as a country adopting a tougher youth-criminal-justice policy.

## Current Profile

In the rescue case, proximity alone was insufficient. The episode says [[NielsBohr]] sought an assurance of admission and that Swedish radio publicly announced on 2 October 1943 that Danish Jewish refugees would be welcome. This converted the Oresund from nearby water into a credible escape route and gave Danish hiding and transport networks a receiving state.

The contemporary branch shows a different state-capacity problem. Sweden is opening prison-based units for children aged 15 or older convicted of serious crimes and has debated lowering the age of criminal responsibility. The source also warns that lower thresholds may fail to deter gangs and may instead shift recruitment toward younger children.

## Key Characteristics

- Nearby neutral receiving state for thousands escaping occupied Denmark in 1943.
- Public admission commitment that turned geography into usable refuge capacity.
- Contemporary case of prison-based youth custody and debated lower criminal-responsibility ages.

## Evidence

- Refuge commitment: [[284-denmark-the-great-escape-glt4809968647]] links Bohr's advocacy, a Swedish radio announcement, and the Oresund crossings.
- Youth custody: [[the-elon-game-musks-vision-of-the-future-6a633594d19896314260e5c4]] reports child prison units in eight prisons for serious offenders aged 15 or older.
- Responsibility-age debate: [[the-elon-game-musks-vision-of-the-future-6a633594d19896314260e5c4]] follows the failed age-13 proposal, planned age-14 threshold, and gang-recruitment concern.

## Qualifications

The sources cover two narrow episodes rather than Sweden's broader wartime neutrality, refugee policy, or criminal-justice system. The rescue source does not settle Bohr's exact influence on policy, the full admission process, or Sweden's wider wartime conduct. The youth-justice proposals are time-sensitive and the source does not establish their later implementation or effects.

## What Changed

- Added Sweden's public refugee commitment as a necessary receiving-state layer in the Danish rescue.
- Distinguished geographic proximity from an operationally credible right of entry.
