# The Fraud (Zadie Smith novel)

## Overview

*The Fraud* is [[ZadieSmith]]'s historical novel built around the [[TichborneClaimant]], [[AndrewBogle]]'s imperial life, and the limits of Victorian domestic fiction.

## Current Profile

[[400-victorian-britains-maddest-mystery-glt4466006716]] presents the novel as an archive-constrained reworking of a case already resembling melodrama. The inheritance dispute supplies public plot; Bogle's plantation and migration history opens the colonial frame; and the expanded Mrs. Touchet supplies a perspective for examining truth, fraud, spectatorship, class, and literary concealment.

## Key Characteristics

- Uses the Tichborne litigation as its historical structure.
- Connects public scandal to Jamaican slavery and imperial mobility.
- Treats Bogle as morally and epistemically ambiguous.
- Extends Mrs. Touchet beyond ordinary limits of the record.
- Foregrounds colonial spaces and lives often hidden by Victorian fiction.

## Evidence

- Case structure: [[400-victorian-britains-maddest-mystery-glt4466006716]] explains why the trials required little invented complication.
- Colonial archive: [[400-victorian-britains-maddest-mystery-glt4466006716]] connects Bogle and plantation records to the novel's field.
- Fictional boundary: [[400-victorian-britains-maddest-mystery-glt4466006716]] identifies Mrs. Touchet's extension as the principal intervention.
