## Overview
王梁 / Wang Liang is an early Eastern Han official whose current wiki profile comes from [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-699-gan-yu-liuxiu-zhengxiong-de-ta-weihe-candan-shouchang-lth-8mndudj3khf9cnsouj86c1-z|Hanji 699]]. He is not presented as a pure recluse: the episode describes prior high office, illness around recall, a friend's harsh judgment that his rank exceeded his counsel or merit, and later shame-driven withdrawal.

## Current Profile
Hanji 699 places Wang Liang near Liu Xiu's recluse and summons sequence, but his case has a different texture from Zhou Dang and Yan Guang. He has already served as Pei commandery administrator and Da Situ sizhi, and the episode remembers him for modest and frugal conduct.

The turning point is social judgment. When Wang Liang is ill during recall, a friend refuses to see him and argues that Wang Liang has held high rank without giving loyal counsel or producing exceptional merit. Wang Liang is ashamed, stops answering summons, and dies at home.

His profile is therefore a reputation-accountability case. Withdrawal here does not simply protect autonomy; it also reflects the pain of a public mismatch between office status and perceived contribution.

## Key Characteristics
- Former high official rather than lifelong recluse.
- Remembered for modest or frugal conduct but judged by a friend as undercontributing for his rank.
- Withdrawal case shaped by shame and reputation pressure.
- Contrast case beside Zhou Dang and Yan Guang's more straightforward refusal of office.
- Evidence point for how moral reputation can become an accountability mechanism against office status.

## Qualifications
This page is a one-source bounded profile and should not be treated as a full official biography. The transcript alternates 王良/王梁; this page normalizes to 王梁 because the described Guangwu-era official and Da Situ sizhi role fit that route. The friend's critique is a source event, not a final wiki verdict that Wang Liang had no merit.
