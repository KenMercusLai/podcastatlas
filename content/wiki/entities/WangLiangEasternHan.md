## Overview
王梁 / Wang Liang is now synthesized as an early Eastern Han high official whose rank creates two forms of accountability after an earlier Yuyang support role. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-671-nide-lingdao-shifou-zhide-zhuisui-jinlai-duibi-xia-lrjwus-s14y1x2y4r3f-dvri1nv|Hanji 671]] presents him as Hunu magistrate Wang Liang joining [[WuHanEasternHan|吴汉]] and Ge Yan in urging [[PengChongEasternHan|彭宠]] to support [[LiuXiu|刘秀]], then receiving appointment as one of the arriving northern commanders. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-684-zhending-wang-liuyang-weishenme-beipan-guangwudi-liuxiu-lhtdqtfxihyua1nj8ofsdxl8il8d|Hanji 684]] presents him as Da Sikong under Wu Han's unified command, nearly executed after unauthorized action. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-699-gan-yu-liuxiu-zhengxiong-de-ta-weihe-candan-shouchang-lth-8mndudj3khf9cnsouj86c1-z|Hanji 699]] later presents him as a recalled former official whose shame follows a friend's charge that rank exceeded contribution.

## Current Profile
Hanji 671 gives Wang Liang's earliest current layer in Yuyang. As Hunu magistrate, he joins Wu Han and Ge Yan in urging Peng Chong to support Liu Xiu against Wang Lang. After Peng Chong commits troops, Wang Liang goes south with Wu Han and the Yuyang force; Liu Xiu later appoints him as pianjiangjun while allowing the arriving commanders to retain their original forces.

Hanji 684 places Wang Liang in the 26 CE suppression of Tanxiang and Wuxiao-style bandit forces moving through Wei and Qinghe. Liu Xiu appoints Wu Han commander and orders all action to follow Wu Han's dispatch. Wang Liang resents that hierarchy because he and Wu Han both come from Yuyang-related beginnings, while Wang Liang is locally rooted and sees Wu Han as an outsider who should not stand above him. He then moves troops on his own to seize merit despite imperial orders.

That action turns status resentment into a military-law crisis. Liu Xiu sends Zong Guang with authority to execute Wang Liang before the army. Zong Guang instead protects imperial room for judgment by sending Wang Liang back in a prison cart, using the Chen Ping-Fan Kuai precedent. Liu Xiu ultimately pardons Wang Liang from death, demotes him to Zhonglangjiang, and sends him north. The source therefore makes Wang Liang an early case for [[CommandDisciplineAuthority|军法威严]]: even founding grandees cannot ignore unified command without forcing the ruler to display punishment.

Hanji 699 places Wang Liang near Liu Xiu's recluse and summons sequence, but his case has a different texture from Zhou Dang and Yan Guang. He has already served as Pei commandery administrator and Da Situ sizhi, and the episode remembers him for modest and frugal conduct. When Wang Liang is ill during recall, a friend refuses to see him and argues that Wang Liang has held high rank without giving loyal counsel or producing exceptional merit. Wang Liang is ashamed, stops answering summons, and dies at home.

His current profile is therefore not pure reclusion. It is a rank-accountability arc: military hierarchy first disciplines his status grievance, and later moral reputation disciplines the mismatch between high office and perceived public contribution.

## Key Characteristics
- Yuyang-origin supporter who helps urge Peng Chong toward Liu Xiu and receives early command recognition.
- High-status Guangwu official whose later command behavior creates an early military-law problem.
- Da Sikong ordered to obey Wu Han but unwilling to accept Wu Han's superior battlefield command.
- Nearly executed for unauthorized troop movement before imperial judgment converts death into demotion.
- Former high official whose later withdrawal differs from lifelong reclusion.
- Remembered for modest or frugal conduct but judged by a friend as undercontributing for his rank.
- Evidence point for how both military hierarchy and moral reputation can constrain office status.

## Qualifications
This page is bounded to Hanji 671, Hanji 684, and Hanji 699 and should not be treated as a full official biography. Hanji 671 uses 王良 in the Yuyang context and supports the early support-and-reward layer, but it keeps Ge Yan, exact troop sequence, and title mechanics source-scoped. Hanji 684 explains the resentment through Yuyang origins, local standing, and Wu Han's outsider status, but it does not prove Wang Liang's full private motive beyond that source frame. The transcript alternates 王良/王梁 in the later context; this page normalizes to 王梁 because the described Guangwu-era official and Da Situ sizhi role fit that route. The friend's critique in Hanji 699 is a source event, not a final wiki verdict that Wang Liang had no merit.
