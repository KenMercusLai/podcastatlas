# Operation Breakthrough

## Overview
Operation Breakthrough was a U.S. federal effort beginning in the late 1960s to test industrialized and off-site housing systems, as described in [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-76efcd195d1b26a4faab0a64b31c1972-52966951-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-76efcd195d1b26a4faab0a64b31c1972-52966951]].

## Current Profile
The program reportedly produced nearly 3,000 prototype units and attempted to pair technical demonstration with regulatory reform. Funding cuts, the energy crisis, and economic downturn then contributed to participating-company failures, interrupting the accumulation of firms, skills, and institutional momentum that comparable international systems continued to develop.

## Key Characteristics
- Used public procurement and demonstration to test industrialized housing systems.
- Produced prototypes but did not secure a durable domestic production ecosystem.
- Linked technical experimentation to the need for regulatory coordination.
- Illustrates how macroeconomic and funding continuity can determine whether learning persists.
