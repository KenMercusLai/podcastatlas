## Overview

董昭 / Dong Zhao is a late-Han adviser whose bounded profile joins early patron recognition, route-opening persuasion, court relocation, and multi-audience information strategy for [[CaoCao|曹操]].

## Current Profile

Hanji 968 first shows Dong Zhao serving [[ZhangYangLateHan|张杨]] in Henei and persuading him that the Yuan Shao-Cao Cao alliance will not last, that Cao is the stronger future bet, and that Cao's envoy should be allowed through. Dong Zhao also sends calibrated greetings in Cao Cao's name to Chang'an powerholders, lowering the cost of first court contact.

Hanji 1003 makes that initiative more explicit. Dong Zhao identifies [[YangFengLateHan|杨奉]] as the key route obstacle, writes without waiting for direct instruction, and reframes Cao Cao as a grain-rich ally whose interests can complement Yang Feng's troops. Hanji 1004 then shows him turning court access into an executable relocation plan: move [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]] from ruined Luoyang to Xu, use food and settlement needs as the public rationale, and reassure Yang Feng before resistance consolidates.

The same actor-and-incentive method appears in a late military-information case. When [[SunQuan|孙权]] asks Cao Cao to keep his planned attack on [[GuanYu|关羽]] secret, Dong Zhao advises outward agreement but operational disclosure: copy the message into both besieged Fancheng and Guan Yu's camp. The same information is expected to improve defender morale, increase withdrawal pressure on Guan Yu, and keep Sun and Guan consuming one another's attention.

Across the bounded sources, Dong Zhao's recurring capacity is not generic cleverness. He identifies the actor whose decision controls access or movement, then redesigns that actor's perceived interests with praise, supply, timing, or selected information.

## Key Characteristics

- Recognizes a useful future patron before formal hierarchy is settled.
- Opens blocked routes by identifying the actor with practical veto power.
- Uses praise, material need, and safety to reframe interests rather than relying on threat alone.
- Acts proactively when delay would let obstacles consolidate.
- Converts broad political goals into logistical and psychological execution plans.
- Treats information release as an operational tool when audience reaction is more valuable than secrecy.
- Designs for several actors' incentives at once rather than optimizing one bilateral exchange.

## Qualifications

These sources provide selected episodes rather than a full Dong Zhao biography. Claims about exceptional foresight, acting without instruction, the exact letters, offices, dialogue, delivery mechanics, audience reactions, Guan Yu's reasoning, and the longer-term trust cost of violating Sun Quan's secrecy expectation remain episode-attributed. Strategic disclosure may improve the immediate position while weakening later cooperation or exposing sources.
