# Robert Menzies

## Overview

Robert Menzies was an Australian Liberal prime minister whose two periods in office connect wartime imperial commitment to long postwar conservative dominance.

## Current Profile

The source presents Menzies as rural Victorian, cricket-loving, strongly Anglophile, and committed to the Second World War. His long wartime absence in Britain weakened his first premiership, but his return through [[ForgottenPeoplePolitics]] built a coalition around homes, families, suburban middle-class aspiration, anti-communism, and prosperity.

His profile remains mixed. The Petrov affair aided his 1954 victory, he committed Australia to Vietnam, and the era retained censorship and moral conservatism; yet his governments also began dismantling the [[WhiteAustraliaPolicy]].

## Key Characteristics

- Deeply Anglophile political identity and strong imperial-war commitment.
- First premiership weakened by absence abroad and domestic political erosion.
- Architect of a durable suburban and middle-class conservative coalition.
- Strong anti-communist who benefited politically from the Petrov affair.
- Vietnam supporter whose governments also began relaxing racial immigration restriction.
