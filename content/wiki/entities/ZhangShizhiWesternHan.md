## Overview

张释之 / Zhang Shizhi is a Western Han official whose current profile joins slow early advancement, concrete historical counsel, status-neutral palace protocol, skepticism toward eloquence as a promotion signal, root-cause burial restraint, and廷尉 judgments that keep punishment stable under imperial pressure.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-214-tiemian-zhangshizhi-dui-huangdi-lan-taizi-1-lqlbul2drksmqgmligaelawflsvq8|Hanji 214 part 1]] introduces Zhang as a Nanyang man who reportedly obtained a costly 骑郎 position through family resources, remained without promotion for more than a decade, and considered leaving. [[YuanAngWesternHan|袁盎]]'s recommendation makes him a 谒者, but proximity alone does not explain the later rise: [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]] asks for concrete, currently usable discussion, and Zhang answers through lessons drawn from Qin, Chu, and Han.

Three early episodes establish a pattern of separating the governing problem from the ruler's first preference. At the imperial animal park, Zhang refuses to let one fluent answer stand in for a full appointment judgment and uses [[ZhouBo|周勃]] and Zhang Xiangru as counterexamples to the equation of verbal fluency with worth. At Sima Gate, he stops Crown Prince [[HanJingdi|刘启]] and [[LiuWuLiangKingWesternHan|梁王刘武]], accuses them of disrespect, and requires formal pardon before allowing entry. At Baling, he tells Liu Heng that desirable grave goods, rather than insufficient stone sealing, create the basic theft incentive.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-214-tiemian-zhangshizhi-dui-huangdi-lan-taizi-2-lixkic0bkzjc0ehyupeolnqmuxs|Hanji 214 part 2]] extends the same institutional posture into criminal adjudication after Zhang becomes廷尉. He retains the reported four-liang fine for a pedestrian who startled the imperial horses despite Liu Heng's fear of injury, arguing that punishment changed by imperial anger would destroy public trust in law. He later sentences an ancestral-temple thief to death but rejects clan extermination, using a hypothetical attack on the imperial tomb to defend a ranked and proportionate penalty system.

The relationship is reciprocal but not frictionless: Zhang constrains the ruler's convenience, family preference, fear, and filial display, while Liu Heng repeatedly accepts correction. The second episode also says [[ZhouYafu|周亚夫]] respects Zhang's impartiality and seeks friendship with him, but the exact relationship and their joint description as “社稷之臣” remain source-attributed. A much later episode shows the burial advice becoming precedent when [[LiuXiang|刘向]] reportedly invokes Zhang's warning against [[HanChengdi|汉成帝]]'s Changling project.

## Key Characteristics

- Late-recognized palace official whose advancement begins through recommendation but continues through usable historical and institutional judgment.
- Personnel skeptic who distinguishes verbal fluency from administrative fitness and moral reliability.
- Protocol enforcer willing to apply a public gate rule to the crown prince and a powerful king.
-廷尉 who defends predictable and proportionate punishment when imperial fear or honor demands escalation.
- Root-cause remonstrant who shifts tomb security from harder barriers to reduced burial temptation.
- Adviser whose effectiveness depends on a ruler willing to tolerate constraint and convert criticism into accepted decisions.

## Qualifications

This remains a bounded profile rather than a complete biography or legal reconstruction. Hanji 214 part 1's entry mechanism, family financing, tenure, offices, promotion chronology, dialogue, official titles, Sima Gate procedure, royal and dowager actions, and Baling design require comparison with transmitted histories. Part 2's bridge location, fine, temple object, offense classifications, penalty rules, speeches, dowager mediation, Zhou Yafu relationship, and “社稷之臣” attribution are likewise episode-scoped. Its “法律面前人人平等” language usefully names resistance to arbitrary override but should not be projected backward as a modern constitutional regime. Hanji 536-1 establishes later reuse of the burial precedent within that episode's account, not a complete reception history.
