# Roy Baumeister

## Overview
Roy Baumeister is the psychologist whose ego-depletion experiments and later glucose account anchor one side of the episode's debate over whether repeated self-control draws down a limited resource.

## Current Profile
In [[how-to-increase-your-willpower-tenacity-scim7958949675]], [[AndrewHuberman]] presents Baumeister's cookie-and-radish and repeated-challenge studies as evidence that demanding restraint can reduce later persistence. The episode also presents his glucose hypothesis and later multi-challenge qualification, but does not treat either as settled: [[CarolDweck]]'s belief-sensitive findings and broader replication controversy keep [[EgoDepletionDebate]] open.

## Key Characteristics
- Associated with the limited-resource model of willpower.
- Uses sequential self-control tasks to test carryover into later persistence or performance.
- Proposed glucose availability as one physiological contributor to repeated-task decline.
- Later emphasized that multiple back-to-back challenges may differ from simpler two-task designs.

## Evidence
- Sequential-task paradigm - [[how-to-increase-your-willpower-tenacity-scim7958949675]] summarizes cookie-versus-radish restraint followed by an impossible puzzle.
- Glucose account - [[how-to-increase-your-willpower-tenacity-scim7958949675]] reports studies comparing glucose-containing and non-caloric sweet drinks across demanding tasks.
- Qualification - [[how-to-increase-your-willpower-tenacity-scim7958949675]] says Baumeister later stressed repeated real-world challenges rather than a single two-task sequence.

## Qualifications
This profile is based on one podcast summary, not Baumeister's complete publication record or the full replication literature. The experimental effects, glucose mechanism, and interpretation of ego depletion remain contested and should not be converted into nutrition or mental-health advice.
