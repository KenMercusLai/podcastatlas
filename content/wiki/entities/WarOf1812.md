# War of 1812

## Overview

The War of 1812 appears in the wiki as the conflict setting for [[StarSpangledBanner|The Star-Spangled Banner]] and as a North American war whose memory diverges across the United States, Britain, and Canada.

## Current Profile

The episode uses the war mainly to explain the anthem's battlefield scene. The hosts describe causes including British trade restrictions, impressment, American ambitions toward Canada, conflict involving Native American allies of Britain, and American national-unity hopes. After early failed American invasions of Canada and later British attacks around Chesapeake Bay, the war produced the September 1814 attack on [[FortMcHenry]] that [[FrancisScottKey]] turned into poetry.

The source also makes the war part of slavery history. British commanders used escaped enslaved people as guides, informants, and soldiers, while American slaveholders feared the political and military meaning of armed Black men in British service.

## Key Characteristics

- Anglo-American war remembered unevenly in Britain, the United States, and Canada.
- Conflict with causes including trade restrictions, impressment, Canada ambitions, frontier conflict, and national-unity politics.
- Canadian identity case because failed U.S. invasions and defense against the United States became foundational in the source's account.
- Chesapeake campaign setting for the burning of Washington and the attack on Baltimore.
- Immediate historical context for the Fort McHenry flag scene and the anthem poem.
- Slavery and freedom conflict because escaped enslaved people reached British lines and some fought in the [[ColonialMarines]].

## Evidence

- Causes and memory: [[677-usa-the-star-spangled-banner-part-1-glt7890467734]] lists trade restrictions, impressment, Canada, Native American alliances, and U.S. unity as causes while calling the war obscure in Britain and ambiguous in U.S. memory.
- Canada branch: [[677-usa-the-star-spangled-banner-part-1-glt7890467734]] says failed American invasions helped make the war foundational in Canadian identity.
- Chesapeake campaign: [[677-usa-the-star-spangled-banner-part-1-glt7890467734]] follows British action from coastal attacks to Washington and then Baltimore.
- Anthem setting: [[677-usa-the-star-spangled-banner-part-1-glt7890467734]] says the bombardment of [[FortMcHenry]] on 13-14 September 1814 supplied the rockets, bombs, dawn, and flag imagery.
- Slavery branch: [[677-usa-the-star-spangled-banner-part-1-glt7890467734]] says British forces welcomed escaped enslaved people and organized some male runaways into the [[ColonialMarines]].
