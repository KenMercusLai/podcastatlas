## Overview

《仙剑奇侠传四》 is a 2007 Chinese xianxia role-playing game represented here through a [[ZhiZuiJinMiFM|纸醉金迷FM]] retrospective prompted by its television adaptation.

## Current Profile

The bounded episode reconstructs the game as a four-person journey whose early humor, exploration, and youthful rule-breaking gradually open into an inherited conflict among the Qionghua Sect, the Dreamworld, two paired swords, and the previous generation. Yun Tianhe begins outside ordinary society; Han Lingsha seeks longevity for a short-lived tomb-robbing clan; Liu Mengli discovers obligations to her demon people; and Murong Ziying must decide whether loyalty to a sect means obeying its leaders or preserving its stated humane purpose.

The work's interpreted moral center is [[XianxiaHumanCenteredCultivation|“先修人道，再修仙道”]]. Qionghua's orthodox identity cannot justify sacrificing disciples, demons, or civilians for ascension, while demon and ghost identity does not establish wickedness. Xuan Xiao's exploitation and genuine attachment can coexist, and Murong Ziying can oppose his institution without abandoning the values it claims to serve.

The episode also locates the game's durability in emotional and cultural integration. Turn-based combat, 3D presentation, mazes, voice acting, and later screen spectacle shape access, but the hosts argue that friendship, love, responsibility, fate, loss, Chinese mythic materials, and moral choice give those forms their lasting force. The hundred-year ending concentrates that tragic structure: survival, return, death, blindness, memory, and an unexplained grave deny the party a simple reward without reducing their choices to futility.

## Key Characteristics

- Uses a four-person party to join contrasting social innocence, family duty, group belonging, and institutional loyalty.
- Moves from youthful adventure toward a conflict inherited from parents, sect leaders, and earlier violence.
- Rejects orthodox, human, demon, or ghost identity as a sufficient moral verdict.
- Places humane conduct before cultivation power, immortality, institutional ambition, or ascension.
- Combines Chinese mythic and xianxia materials with friendship, romance, sacrifice, grief, and responsibility.
- Ends through irreversible cost and temporal separation rather than uncomplicated heroic reward.

## Qualifications

This profile comes from one retrospective podcast summary rather than direct play, script collation, production records, or a systematic reception study.
