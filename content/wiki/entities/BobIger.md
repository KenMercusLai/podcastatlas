## Overview

Bob Iger is a [[TheWaltDisneyCompany|Disney]] executive whose leadership combines branded content, technology, global reach, major IP acquisitions, and stewardship of [[WaltDisney]]'s institutional legacy.

## Current Profile

Iger succeeded [[MichaelEisner]] and repaired Disney animation by acquiring [[Pixar]] while protecting its creative system, then expanded Disney's portfolio through [[MarvelEntertainment|Marvel]], [[Lucasfilm]], and Fox entertainment assets. That strategy used [[ESPN]]-funded scale and strong IP to widen the [[EntertainmentIPFlywheel]], before cord-cutting and streaming made [[DisneyPlus|Disney+]] strategically necessary but economically harder. [[disney-the-renaissance-and-the-empire-1]]

As a self-described steward, Iger wants Disney to remain beloved and globally entertaining while respecting the values behind its history without letting past practice prevent reinvention. He treats theme parks as story environments, identifies courage and technological experimentation with Walt's example, and frames global expansion as cultural adaptation rather than dilution of Disney identity. [[rihc-disneys-legacy-with-bob-iger-glt6989183827]]

## Key Characteristics

- Organizes strategy around high-quality branded content, technology, and global reach.
- Uses acquisitions to add creative systems and story portfolios, with Pixar presented as the strongest repair case.
- Treats Disney leadership as stewardship of an inherited institution rather than preservation of every inherited practice.
- Connects physical parks, narrative design, and technology through experiences such as [[StarWarsGalaxysEdge|Star Wars: Galaxy's Edge]].
- Presents localization as keeping a recognizable Disney identity while adapting respectfully to local culture.
- Operates across a transition in which legacy cable economics weaken and streaming becomes necessary but less attractive.

## Qualifications

The Acquired source is a business-history interpretation, while the Rest Is History source is a friendly interview centered on Iger's own account. The latter gives limited scrutiny to acquisition failures, labor, park pricing, streaming losses, governance, or controversy. Claims about happiness, admiration, cultural respect, and Walt Disney's hypothetical judgment should therefore be read as leadership framing rather than independent measures of performance.
