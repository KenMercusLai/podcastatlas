# Thomas Ashe

## Overview

Thomas Ashe was an Irish teacher, language activist, Gaelic Athletic Association organizer, volunteer, and Easter Rising participant whose 1917 death after force-feeding during a prison hunger strike became a major republican mobilizing event.

## Current Profile

The source presents Ashe as a link between cultural nationalism, volunteer organization, rebellion, prison politics, and public martyrdom. A farmer's son from County Kerry, he trained as a teacher, promoted the Irish language and Gaelic games, joined the [[IrishVolunteers|Irish Volunteers]], and commanded in north County Dublin during the [[EasterRising|Easter Rising]].

After release from imprisonment, Ashe returned to political activity and was arrested for a seditious speech. He demanded prisoner-of-war status in Mountjoy Jail and joined a hunger strike. He died after force-feeding, turning a dispute over classification and prison authority into a public indictment of British rule. His funeral brought large crowds, organized volunteers, religious ritual, armed display, and a short graveside statement by [[MichaelCollinsIrishRevolutionary|Michael Collins]] into one [[PoliticalFuneral|political funeral]].

## Key Characteristics

- Joined cultural-nationalist work in language, education, and Gaelic sport to armed volunteer activity.
- Easter Rising commander outside the main Dublin-center narrative.
- Prisoner whose demand for prisoner-of-war status made classification part of the sovereignty conflict.
- Hunger striker who died after force-feeding rather than from a long period of starvation.
- Martyr figure whose funeral widened republican visibility and ritual organization.
