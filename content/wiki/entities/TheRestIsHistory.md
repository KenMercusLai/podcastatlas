# The Rest Is History

[[694-elizabeth-i-vs-the-catholics-a-treacherous-conspiracy-part-4-glt1627515087]] adds the earlier Elizabethan conspiracy branch. It connects [[FrancisDrake]]'s return, the [[GoldenHind]] ceremony, [[FrancoisDukeOfAnjou|Anjou]] diplomacy, the [[LowCountries|Low Countries]], French civil war, the [[ThrockmortonPlot]], [[FrancisWalsingham]]'s intelligence network, and the [[BondOfAssociation]] into the prelude before the later Sidney and Mary Stuart episodes.

[[695-elizabeth-i-vs-the-catholics-the-shadow-war-part-5-glt5637968455]] adds the preceding Elizabethan war branch. It uses [[PhilipSidney|Sir Philip Sidney]] to connect courtly ambition, militant Protestantism, the [[DutchRevolt]], [[Antwerp]]'s fall, the [[TreatyOfNonsuch]], [[FrancisDrake]]'s raids, and the [[StrategicInterventionThreshold|threshold]] that moves [[ElizabethI|Elizabeth I]] toward direct action in the [[LowCountries|Low Countries]].

[[696-elizabeth-i-vs-the-catholics-killing-the-queen-of-scots-part-6-glt2334035496]] adds an Elizabethan branch to The Rest Is History in the wiki. The episode follows [[MaryQueenOfScots|Mary, Queen of Scots]] from the [[BabingtonPlot]] and [[FrancisWalsingham]]'s [[SpyNetworkEntrapment|spy-network trap]] through trial, warrant politics, and execution at [[FotheringhayCastle]], then links the aftermath to [[ConfessionalWarEscalation]] and the [[SpanishArmada]].

The Rest Is History appears in [[697-the-troubles-murder-in-belfast-part-1-glt4877520736]] and [[698-the-troubles-the-brink-of-civil-war-part-2-glt2308408988]] as the podcast context for a narrative history of [[TheTroubles]] and [[NorthernIreland]]. Part 1 reconstructs partition, state design, Catholic exclusion, unionist fear, [[IanPaisley|Paisley]]'s rise, and 1966 loyalist killings; Part 2 follows the 1968-1969 civil-rights escalation into street violence and British troop deployment.

For the wiki, the show page anchors selected narrative-history branches rather than a full show catalogue. These branches emphasize chronological decisions by politicians, soldiers, activists, security forces, and symbolic figures instead of reducing large conflicts to inevitability.
