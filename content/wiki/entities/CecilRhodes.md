# Cecil Rhodes

## Overview

Cecil Rhodes was a British imperial politician and mining magnate presented as a central advocate of territorial expansion in southern Africa.

## Current Profile

[[241-young-churchill-prisoner-and-fugitive-part-3-glt9087091726]] places Rhodes at the junction of mineral wealth and imperial geography. Diamond interests associated with De Beers and his Cape-to-Cairo vision made the independent Boer republics appear as obstacles to a continuous British sphere. The failed Jameson Raid then supplied a concrete example of expansionist intervention preceding the [[BoerWar]].

## Key Characteristics

- Mining magnate associated with southern African diamond wealth.
- Advocate of a Cape-to-Cairo British imperial vision.
- Treated the Boer republics as obstacles to territorial continuity.
- Politically implicated in the failed Jameson Raid.

## Evidence

- Mineral and imperial role: [[241-young-churchill-prisoner-and-fugitive-part-3-glt9087091726]] connects Rhodes, De Beers, diamonds, and British expansion.
- Territorial vision: [[241-young-churchill-prisoner-and-fugitive-part-3-glt9087091726]] presents Cape-to-Cairo continuity as a motive for opposing independent Boer republics.
- Prewar intervention: [[241-young-churchill-prisoner-and-fugitive-part-3-glt9087091726]] identifies the 1895-1896 Jameson Raid as a failed attempt against the Transvaal order.

## Qualifications

This source supplies only the prewar imperial role needed for the Churchill narrative. It does not provide a full biography, a detailed business history of De Beers, African perspectives on Rhodes's rule, or a complete reconstruction of the Jameson Raid and its responsibility chain.

## What Changed

- Created Rhodes as the episode's bridge among mineral capital, territorial ambition, and prewar intervention.
