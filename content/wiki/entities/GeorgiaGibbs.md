# Georgia Gibbs

## Overview

Georgia Gibbs is a singer used in [[in-a-metal-mood-in-a-metal-mood]] as the episode's clearest example of close copying that can displace a source performer.

## Current Profile

The episode compares Gibbs's recording of [[LaVernBaker|LaVern Baker]]'s “Tweedle Dee” with Baker's arrangement and treats their similarity as materially different from [[PatBoone]]'s conspicuously transformed versions. Gibbs therefore functions less as a full biographical subject than as a comparison case for [[ImitativeCulturalDisplacement]].

## Key Characteristics

- Recorded a commercially prominent version of LaVern Baker's “Tweedle Dee.”
- Is presented as closely reproducing the source arrangement.
- Serves as the episode's benchmark for copying with insufficient creative distance.

## Evidence

### Arrangement similarity

- [[in-a-metal-mood-in-a-metal-mood]] says the listening panel regarded Gibbs's version as a close reproduction of Baker's arrangement.

### Comparative role

- [[in-a-metal-mood-in-a-metal-mood]] contrasts her recording with Boone's genre and performance transformations.

## Qualifications

The source provides no recordings, contracts, chart data, royalty records, independent musicological analysis, or account of Gibbs's intent. Similarity alone does not measure every form of economic harm, and the episode's listening judgment remains source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Established Gibbs as the episode's comparison case for displacement through close imitation.
