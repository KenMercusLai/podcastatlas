# Charlotte Corday / 夏洛特·科黛

## Overview

Charlotte Corday was a young Norman republican and [[Girondins|Girondin]] sympathizer who assassinated [[JeanPaulMarat|Jean-Paul Marat]] in Paris on 13 July 1793 and was executed four days later.

## Current Profile

The sources agree that Corday was a politically conscious actor rather than a manipulated, lovesick, irrational, or covertly royalist figure. Classical republican reading, horror at revolutionary violence, the Girondin crisis in Caen, and Marat's threats against the city shaped her belief that killing one man would save many; the outcome instead exposed the institutional insufficiency of assassination and accelerated suspicion, repression, and Marat's martyr cult.

## Key Characteristics

- She came from a minor aristocratic Norman family, received a convent education, and read Plutarch, Voltaire, and Rousseau.
- She supported the Revolution before revolutionary violence and the death of a family-connected priest pushed her toward the Girondin cause.
- She conceived the assassination independently and expected to die for it.
- Her manifesto framed Marat as outside the law and the killing as a sacrifice intended to prevent civil war.
- Her calm trial conduct and insistence on acting alone challenged gendered and conspiratorial assumptions among investigators.
- Her act failed strategically: it strengthened the terror politics she meant to stop.

### Republican formation and agency

- [[125-mala-zhisi-beibi-de-ansha-huo-zhengyi-de-chujue-783972516]] presents Corday as a moderate republican and rejects romantic or royalist reductions of her motive.

### Assassination and investigation

- [[707-the-terror-an-assassination-in-paris-part-1-glt6580043397]] reconstructs her journey, purchase of the knife, entry by offering names, single fatal blow, interrogation, six-hour trial, and execution.

### Political outcome

- [[125-mala-zhisi-beibi-de-ansha-huo-zhengyi-de-chujue-783972516]] argues that assassination may preserve refusal without repairing institutions.
- [[707-the-terror-an-assassination-in-paris-part-1-glt6580043397]] supplies the immediate reversal: a martyr cult, harsher treatment of Girondins, increased fear of hidden assassins, and Robespierre's ascent.

## Qualifications

- Reported dialogue in Marat's bath and claims about Corday's inner motives are source-mediated reconstructions.
- Political agency does not by itself make assassination lawful, ethically justified, or strategically effective.
- The comparison with [[MarcusBrutus|Brutus]] concerns republican self-understanding and institutional failure, not identical circumstances.

## What Changed

- Corday's biography now includes the Caen federalist crisis, classical formation, manifesto, and operational planning.
- The current judgment separates intelligible motive from catastrophic strategic effect.
- Officials' refusal to accept lone agency is now part of her profile rather than a peripheral trial detail.
