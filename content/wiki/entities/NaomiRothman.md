# Naomi Rothman

## Overview
Naomi Rothman is a psychologist represented here through a [[HiddenBrain]] episode on the cognitive, social, and organizational consequences of experiencing and expressing mixed emotions.

## Current Profile
Rothman treats [[EmotionalAmbivalence]] as neither simple indecision nor an automatic advantage. Personal cases involving sport, parenting, housing, dating, family illness, and academic leadership show its discomfort and practical cost; experiments and workplace studies examine advice-taking, creativity, negotiation, status judgment, ethics, and team information flow.

Her central contribution is contextual. Visible conflict can invite exploitation or loss of authority where dominance and competition are rewarded, yet cooperative goals can turn it into a cue for inquiry. [[AmbivalentLeadership]] captures the resulting promotion-performance gap, while [[ParadoxMindset]] names a promising but not yet established method for sustaining both-and attention.

## Key Characteristics
- Studies mixed emotions as simultaneous positive and negative experience.
- Separates the internal informational value of ambivalence from its external status cost.
- Connects emotional conflict with advice-taking, creativity, cognitive flexibility, negotiation, and leadership.
- Treats cooperation and competition as moderators of whether ambivalence helps a group.
- Uses personal experience to make mechanisms legible while preserving research uncertainty.

## Qualifications
This profile is based on one structured episode summary rather than Rothman's publications, study materials, datasets, or full interview transcript. The episode reports experimental and field findings without enough methodological detail to establish effect sizes, replication, causal mechanisms, subgroup differences, or universal application. Her personal stories are illustrations rather than independent tests, and the paradox-mindset proposal is explicitly presented as a hypothesis.
