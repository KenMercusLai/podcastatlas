# Philip D. Curtin

## Overview

Philip D. Curtin was a Johns Hopkins historian of the Atlantic slave trade whose quantitative and documentary work is used by the episode to challenge the [[HouseOfSlavesGoree|House of Slaves]] narrative.

## Current Profile

The episode presents Curtin's 1969 *The Atlantic Slave Trade: A Census* as a landmark effort to replace inherited totals with evidence-based estimates. It says he denounced the Gorée museum account as a hoax or scam and that the underlying conclusion—that the building was not what its tourist narrative claimed and Gorée was not statistically central—is now broadly accepted among Western scholars.

Curtin also anchors the episode's dispute over scholarly authority. His status as a white American historian intensified controversy around his criticism, raising a real question about power and voice without, in the hosts' view, resolving the factual issue.

## Key Characteristics

- Historian associated with quantitative reconstruction of the Atlantic slave trade.
- Author of *The Atlantic Slave Trade: A Census*.
- Prominent critic of the House of Slaves' mass-embarkation narrative.
- Case linking evidentiary authority to disputes over race, identity, and public memory.
