# Soweto

## Overview
Soweto is presented as a Johannesburg-area black township whose apartheid history, internal class differences, tourist framing, and 1976 student uprising make it more complex than a simple poverty label.

## Current Profile
The episode treats [[Soweto]] as an apartheid-formed residential and political space. 王妈妈 stresses that calling it only a "slum" misses its internal range: shack areas sit alongside black middle-class neighborhoods and ordinary family life. The traveler also feels the ethical tension of entering such a place through guided tourism while trying not to turn residents into spectacle.

Soweto's strongest historical role in the episode is the 1976 student uprising. The trigger is described as educational inequality and the imposition of Afrikaans in black schools, making Soweto a concrete site where [[Apartheid]] appears through childhood, language, schooling, and state violence rather than abstract law.

## Key Characteristics
- Apartheid-formed black residential area connected to [[Johannesburg]].
- Internally mixed space with shack settlements, black middle-class areas, and ordinary family life.
- Tourist route that raises ethical questions about looking at other people's hardship.
- Site associated in the episode with the 1976 student uprising over education and Afrikaans-language policy.
- Evidence point for [[PostApartheidStructuralInequality]] because historical education inequality remains central to the episode's interpretation.
