# Great Fear of 1789

## Overview

The Great Fear was the summer 1789 wave of rural panic, rumor, and attack on chateaux, abbeys, monasteries, and feudal records during the opening of the [[FrenchRevolution|French Revolution]].

## Current Profile

The episode presents the Great Fear as the interaction of genuine deprivation and uncertain explanation. Crushing dues, taxes, high bread prices, and institutional collapse made rural grievance real, while rumors of aristocrats, foreigners, or brigands plotting starvation and violence gave that distress an immediate enemy. Peasant destruction of registers and charters targeted the documentary machinery of obligation, and the Assembly's 4 August attack on feudal privilege was partly an attempt to answer disorder with national legislation.

## Key Characteristics

- Spread through rumor in a countryside already under severe economic pressure.
- Directed attacks toward elite property and records of feudal obligation.
- Joined local grievance to imagined coordinated plots by aristocrats, foreigners, or brigands.
- Helped accelerate the National Assembly's abolition of feudal privilege.

## Qualifications

- Real poverty and dues do not prove the coordinated aristocratic plots described in contemporary rumor.
- The source does not supply a complete regional chronology or quantify participation and damage.
- National abolition did not instantly settle every local obligation or conflict.
