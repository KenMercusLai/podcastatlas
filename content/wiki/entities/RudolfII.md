# Rudolf II

## Overview

Rudolf II was the Holy Roman Emperor in Prague whom [[JohnDee]] and [[EdwardKelley|Edward Kelley]] approached as a potential patron for their alchemical and angelic project.

## Current Profile

The source uses Rudolf as evidence that occult and alchemical interests reached the highest levels of European patronage. His curiosity makes [[Prague]] a plausible destination for Dee and Kelley after Olbracht Laski's Polish plan fails, but interest does not mean unconditional acceptance.

Their approach is compromised by Kelley's arrest after a fight and by a claimed angelic instruction that Dee rebuke Rudolf for his sins. Kelley later receives employment and a knighthood through alchemical promise, then loses protection when he cannot deliver gold, illustrating the volatile exchange at the center of [[CourtOccultExpertiseRisk]].

## Key Characteristics

- Imperial patron associated by the episode with alchemy and occult inquiry.
- Prague ruler whose court attracts mobile specialists promising rare knowledge or material transformation.
- Politically sensitive sovereign for whom rebuke presented as supernatural authority could be offensive rather than useful.
- Patron who rewards Kelley but later imprisons him after failed alchemical delivery.

- Patronage target: [[542-elizabeth-is-sorcerer-angels-and-demons-in-renaissance-europe-glt4512754016]] says the failed Polish venture redirects Dee and Kelley toward Rudolf in Prague.
- Failed Dee audience: [[542-elizabeth-is-sorcerer-angels-and-demons-in-renaissance-europe-glt4512754016]] links Kelley's brawl and the angelic rebuke to Dee's damaged approach.
- Kelley relationship: [[542-elizabeth-is-sorcerer-angels-and-demons-in-renaissance-europe-glt4512754016]] says Rudolf employs and knights Kelley before imprisoning him for failure to produce gold.

## Qualifications

This source supplies only the Dee-Kelley branch of Rudolf's reign. It does not establish the full structure of his court, the chronology and legal form of Kelley's rewards, or a complete account of imperial scientific, artistic, religious, and political patronage.
