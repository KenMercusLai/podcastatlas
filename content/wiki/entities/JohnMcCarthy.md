# John McCarthy / 约翰·麦卡锡

## Overview
John McCarthy appears in the source as the young Dartmouth assistant professor whose proposal helped name artificial intelligence and convene the 1956 workshop.

## Current Profile
The episode's McCarthy profile centers on field formation. At age 28, he is described as writing a 1955 proposal with collaborators, seeking Rockefeller funding, and using the phrase "artificial intelligence" before the field had a settled identity.

The source also portrays his later interpretation of Dartmouth as possibility-oriented: the meeting did not solve AI, but it showed that artificial intelligence had many possible routes.

## Key Characteristics
- Young Dartmouth mathematics and astronomy assistant professor in the source's account.
- Central proposal author for the 1956 Dartmouth AI workshop.
- Associated with the first proposal-level joining of "artificial" and "intelligence."
- Treated as a convener whose initial work plan gave way to freer discussion.

## Evidence
- Proposal and funding: [[401-bianyu-manju-manzou-women-haineng-yinglai-lingwai-yige-datemao-si-de-xiatian-ma-f7495e05]] says McCarthy wrote the 1955 proposal, requested funding, and received a smaller Rockefeller grant.
- Naming role: [[401-bianyu-manju-manzou-women-haineng-yinglai-lingwai-yige-datemao-si-de-xiatian-ma-f7495e05]] says the proposal first joined "artificial" and "intelligence" into the field name.
- Meeting style: [[401-bianyu-manju-manzou-women-haineng-yinglai-lingwai-yige-datemao-si-de-xiatian-ma-f7495e05]] says McCarthy listed a work plan early but the workshop shifted toward looser discussion.
- Retrospective judgment: [[401-bianyu-manju-manzou-women-haineng-yinglai-lingwai-yige-datemao-si-de-xiatian-ma-f7495e05]] attributes to McCarthy the view that the workshop showed many possibilities for AI.
