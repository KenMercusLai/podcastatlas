# Celestine Porter

## Overview

Celestine Porter was a Black educator whose testimony in the bounded source argues that school integration should have included teachers and administrators from the beginning rather than placing its principal burden on children.

## Current Profile

Porter proposed reciprocal integration of authority: Black children entering white schools should encounter Black teachers, and white children entering Black schools should encounter white teachers. The arrangement would integrate adults and institutions rather than simply relocate pupils.

Her recollection emphasizes knowledge and trust. Black teachers had nurtured and understood their students, while teachers in receiving white schools could be unfamiliar with and afraid of them. This supports the episode's view that educational equality depends on who recognizes potential and exercises discretion inside the school.

## Key Characteristics

- Black educator offering an implementation critique of school desegregation.
- Argued for integrating teachers and administrators alongside students.
- Treated adult authority and institutional knowledge as central to educational equality.
- Emphasized the risks of transferring children without trusted educators.

## Qualifications

The source supplies selected remarks rather than a full interview, biography, or comparative implementation study. Her proposal identifies an omitted institutional dimension but does not by itself establish the feasibility or effects of every teacher-integration model.

## What Changed

- Established Porter as a source for shifting desegregation analysis from student movement to adult authority.
- Added reciprocal teacher integration as an alternative implementation principle.
