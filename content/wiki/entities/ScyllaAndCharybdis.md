# Scylla and Charybdis

## Overview

Scylla and Charybdis are paired sea hazards in [[TheOdyssey]] that force [[Odysseus]] to choose limited loss over total destruction.

## Current Profile

In [[687-the-odyssey-hero-of-the-trojan-horse-part-1-glt2393125423]], Circe tells Odysseus to sail closer to Scylla rather than Charybdis because losing six men is better than losing the whole ship. Odysseus follows the advice: Scylla seizes six men, the ship avoids Charybdis, and the voyage continues toward Helios' island.

## Key Characteristics

- The pair creates a no-clean-win decision rather than a monster that can simply be defeated.
- Scylla represents predictable limited loss; Charybdis represents the risk of whole-ship destruction.
- Circe's advice makes survival depend on accepting tragedy before it happens.
- The scene adds a hard practical edge to Odysseus' leadership because command means choosing who will be lost.

## Evidence

- Forced tradeoff: [[687-the-odyssey-hero-of-the-trojan-horse-part-1-glt2393125423]] says Circe advises sailing nearer Scylla because six deaths are preferable to the whole ship's loss.
- Executed loss: [[687-the-odyssey-hero-of-the-trojan-horse-part-1-glt2393125423]] says Scylla seizes six men while the ship avoids Charybdis.
- Continuation under cost: [[687-the-odyssey-hero-of-the-trojan-horse-part-1-glt2393125423]] places the scene immediately before the Island of the Sun, showing survival without moral or emotional cleanliness.
