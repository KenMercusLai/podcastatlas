# Hogeweyk Dementia Village / 荷兰失智小镇

## Overview
Hogeweyk Dementia Village / 荷兰失智小镇 is the Netherlands dementia-care environment discussed in the source as a contrast to conventional patient-centered institutionalization.

## Current Profile
The episode presents Hogeweyk as a closed but everyday-looking care setting for people with severe dementia, with streets, shops, services, and staff roles designed around recognizable life. Its wiki role is conceptual: it helps define dementia care as environment design and resident life, while Tang Yong cautions that cost, payment systems, long-term-care institutions, and care philosophy are harder to copy than buildings.

## Key Characteristics
- Built environment designed to let residents live in familiar daily scenes while reducing wandering risk.
- Care philosophy treats people with dementia as residents first, not only patients.
- Serves as a comparative case whose replication depends on payment, staffing, and institutional ideas, not only architecture.

## Evidence
- Daily-life setting - [[manchang-de-gaobie-shizhizheng-zhaohuzhe-yu-zhongjiang-laoqu-de-women-lscotoglqyoznytqatlefdwv3jez]] describes streets, supermarket, hair salon, and other everyday spaces inside a protected community.
- Resident-first philosophy - [[manchang-de-gaobie-shizhizheng-zhaohuzhe-yu-zhongjiang-laoqu-de-women-lscotoglqyoznytqatlefdwv3jez]] records Tang Yong's interpretation that the important idea is to see older people as residents rather than patients.
- Replication boundary - [[manchang-de-gaobie-shizhizheng-zhaohuzhe-yu-zhongjiang-laoqu-de-women-lscotoglqyoznytqatlefdwv3jez]] notes Tang's view that buildings are easier to copy than national payment systems, long-term-care systems, and care ideas.

## Qualifications
The episode uses Hogeweyk as a comparative care model but does not provide a full independent evaluation of outcomes, staffing ratios, licensing, or costs.
