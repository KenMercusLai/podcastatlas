## Overview

Jieyang / 揭阳 is represented by two source-bounded layers: an ancient southeastern location where [[YuShanDongyueKing|余善]] halts his promised supporting force during the Western Han campaign against [[Nanyue|南越]], and one of the three modern cities commonly used to define [[Chaoshan]].

## Current Profile

Hanji 355 uses Jieyang as the stopping point that makes Yu Shan's promised support measurable. He reaches the location with a reported eight thousand troops, cites dangerous seas, and does not proceed to Panyu before the city falls. The page does not assume that the ancient administrative unit and modern prefecture-level city had identical boundaries.

The modern layer comes from the Chaoshan business-history episode. It names Jieyang as part of the post-1991 structure created when the old Shantou area was divided into [[Shantou]], [[Chaozhou]], and Jieyang. That source treats administrative division as part of the region's development problem: duplicated infrastructure, local competition, and weaker coordination can make [[MerchantToIndustrialCityUpgrade]] harder when a region already lacks [[Shenzhen]]-level policy scale and external proximity.

## Key Characteristics

- Ancient southeastern place-name in the Nanyue-Dongyue campaign field.
- Location where Yu Shan's promised supporting force stops instead of reaching Panyu.
- Modern prefecture-level city included in the common three-city definition of Chaoshan.
- Post-1991 administrative unit created from the division of the old Shantou region in the business-history source.
- Example of why a stable place-name does not imply stable boundaries or one administrative identity across time.

## Evidence

- Ancient campaign location: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-355-guren-jiao-ni-bie-shua-xinji-buxin-ni-kan-lthxbtmcl8mfpgk2bnb-6pmolgwc|Hanji 355]] says Yu Shan reaches Jieyang, stops on the ground of rough seas, and never joins the Nanyue attack.
- Modern regional identity: [[no-205-chaoshan-wangshi-qiaopi-jingji-tequ-he-meiyou-denglaide-shenzhen-qiji-989721046|Chaoshan past]] names Jieyang with Shantou and Chaozhou as the core modern Chaoshan city set.
- Administrative-fragmentation role: [[no-205-chaoshan-wangshi-qiaopi-jingji-tequ-he-meiyou-denglaide-shenzhen-qiji-989721046|Chaoshan past]] treats the 1991 split as weakening coordination and encouraging duplicated development.

## Qualifications

The sources do not establish continuous boundaries, jurisdiction, or institutional identity between ancient Jieyang and the modern city. Hanji 355 supplies only a campaign waypoint, while the modern episode supplies only a regional-economic role after 1991. Exact ancient geography and the full administrative history require specialist sources.
