## Overview

闽越王郢 / Ying is the Minyue ruler whose unauthorized attack on [[Nanyue|南越]] triggers a Han expedition and whose killing by his brother [[YuShanDongyueKing|余善]] ends the immediate military crisis.

## Current Profile

Hanji 256 presents Ying at the center of a coercive frontier crisis. After attacking Nanyue, he prepares the passes against forces sent by [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]. Yu Shan and other Minyue elites judge continued resistance too costly, assign responsibility to Ying, and decide that surrendering his head may preserve their territory.

Yu Shan kills Ying and sends his head to [[WangHuiWesternHan|王恢]]. The Han army halts and withdraws, so Ying's death substitutes for a full invasion in the episode's sequence. Han Wudi later recognizes Ying's uninvolved descendant [[YaoKingChouMinyue|越繇王丑]] to maintain ancestral sacrifice, preserving part of Ying's lineage without restoring Ying's rule.

## Key Characteristics

- Minyue ruler who attacks Nanyue without prior Han authorization in the episode's account.
- Defensive ruler who prepares difficult passes after learning that Han forces are approaching.
- Political target blamed by local elites for exposing Minyue to invasion.
- Brother killed by Yu Shan as an internal settlement offered to Han.
- Dynastic predecessor whose ritual line continues through Chou.

## Qualifications

The supplied transcript calls Ying “洛影,” and this page normalizes the identity from the event sequence. It does not establish his full reign, the scale or motive of the Nanyue attack, the legality of his relationship to Han, the exact fortifications, or whether all Minyue elites independently supported the killing. The account makes Ying the punitive target but does not supply his perspective on the conflict.
