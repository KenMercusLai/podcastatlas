# Wharton School

## Overview

The Wharton School is a business school represented across the bounded sources through research or teaching on AI-assisted learning, AI-mediated judgment, cryptocurrency confidence, retirement policy, and a [[RevisionistHistory]] exercise about relative-age privilege among seniors.

## Current Profile

Wharton's research role spans several decision environments. [[DaveReibstein]] uses a monthly consumer survey to test whether crypto confidence relates to price and payment behavior, while [[SteveShaw]] studies [[CognitiveSurrender]] when users adopt AI answers—including manipulated wrong answers—instead of reasoning independently.

The education branch distinguishes answer access from learning transfer. A Wharton/Penn high-school math experiment described in [[yong-ai-rang-women-bianbenle-ma-s10e25-a6b360da-07f7-4d9e-ab1b-64a7130e9254]] found that direct GPT-4 access improved supported practice but could weaken closed-book performance, whereas guardrailed help preserved more independent reasoning.

The labor and retirement branch presents [[OliviaSMitchell]] rejecting a fixed number of jobs while preserving the narrower possibility that scarce senior roles can block advancement. A live “birth privilege” exercise in [[outliers-revisited-outliers-revisited|Outliers, Revisited]] uses roughly 75 Wharton seniors: no participant received the youngest scores under the episode's rule, and nearly all reported gifted-program placement. The exercise is illustrative, not a representative study of the school or its admissions.

## Key Characteristics

- Supplies research contexts for consumer confidence, AI judgment, learning transfer, and retirement economics.
- Connects business-school research to practical decisions in markets, education, work, and technology use.
- Serves as the setting for a small live exercise on relative age, gifted placement, and selective educational opportunity.
- Requires domain-specific evidentiary caution because the source summaries often omit full methods, samples, and validation details.

## Qualifications

The sources are podcast summaries rather than complete research reports. The crypto index source omits sample size, weighting, and question wording; the AI studies require their full methods and external validation; the retirement debate does not settle every domain-specific succession problem; and the Wharton birth-privilege exercise is small, non-random, and unable to establish causation or represent the student body. Institutional affiliation does not make every claim a school-wide position.
