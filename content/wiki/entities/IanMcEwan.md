## Overview

Ian McEwan / 伊恩·麦克尤恩 is the British novelist discussed by [[CultureLimited|文化有限]] through [[LessonsNovel|《钢琴课》 / Lessons]], a long novel that combines transformed biographical material with invented relationships and seven decades of public history.

## Current Profile

The episode divides McEwan's career between an early reputation for disturbing subjects and a later expansion toward war, politics, moral conflict, and personal responsibility. It treats *Lessons* as unusually autobiographical in its military-family background, boarding school, birth year, parents' wartime story, and discovery of an adopted-away brother, while preserving fiction as transformation rather than disguised memoir.

## Key Characteristics

- Uses suspense and causal preparation to make initially improbable events feel narratively inevitable.
- Draws on private and family history while refusing a one-to-one identity between author and character.
- Places intimate decisions inside long historical sequences rather than isolating private life from politics.
- Moves between disturbing sexuality, moral ambiguity, family responsibility, and ordinary aging.
- Treats lived experience as material to be selected, rearranged, and invented through fiction.
