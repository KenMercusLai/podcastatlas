## Overview
秉尊 / Bing Zun is a source-scoped late-Han figure identified in Hanji 847 as Deng Meng's brother-in-law. His narrative role is warning and cost: he tells Xuan not to let Liang Ji absorb Deng Meng through adoption or surname change, and Liang Ji kills him before attempting to kill Xuan.

## Current Profile
Hanji 847 makes Bing Zun the clearest internal critic of Liang Ji's harem-family strategy. He reads Liang Ji as unstable and short-lived, warns Xuan against tying Deng Meng's identity to the Liang family, and thereby blocks the smoother conversion of imperial favor into Liang-family capital.

His death marks the shift from manipulation to violence. Once Liang Ji kills Bing Zun and moves against Xuan, the conflict leaves the household sphere and becomes a direct challenge to Emperor Huan's favored woman and her natal family.

## Key Characteristics
- Brother-in-law figure tied to Deng Meng's natal household.
- Warning voice against Liang Ji's attempted adoption or surname absorption of Deng Meng.
- Early reader of Liang Ji's political fragility.
- Victim whose killing exposes the coercive underside of Liang Ji's household strategy.
- Source-scoped actor known mainly through the Hanji 847 episode.
