# Kathy Hochul

## Overview

Kathy Hochul is the New York governor appearing in sources on assisted dying, hyperscale data centers, campus sexual-misconduct oversight, and housing production.

## Current Profile

The four sources present Hochul using state executive authority across difficult risk tradeoffs. She signs a tightly safeguarded assisted-dying law, pauses the largest data-center construction while resource and subsidy concerns are reconsidered, appoints Attorney General [[LetitiaJames]] to investigate the Cornell Seven case, and argues that New York's approval delays raise housing costs and postpone homeownership and family formation. The common feature is intervention under contested medical, infrastructure, institutional, or affordability conditions, not evidence that each policy achieved its intended outcome.

## Key Characteristics

- Signed New York's assisted-dying law with additional procedural safeguards.
- Linked the assisted-dying decision to personal experience of her mother's ALS death.
- Ordered a one-year pause on hyperscale data-center construction.
- Responded to power, water, land, noise, bill, and subsidy concerns around data centers.
- Appointed an external state investigation into the Cornell Seven case.
- Connects housing-review time and per-unit cost to delayed starter-home access and births.
- Supports state and city reforms intended to increase housing production.

## Evidence

- Medical law: [[a-keir-death-experience-britains-pm-clings-on-698b0dc3337b2a3b2ef0aa07]] describes Hochul signing the law and supporting mental-fitness review plus filmed, independently witnessed requests.
- Infrastructure: [[tech-20260717-0717-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260717-0717-mp-tech-pod-128]] links her moratorium to hyperscale facilities' local resource and fiscal burdens.
- Campus oversight: [[gilets-jeunes-frances-school-protests-2959cbdca16de39a43a75666b5bc7057]] says she appointed James to investigate the Cornell case.
- Housing: [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-dde046734f50fc7c6a18ae9c19ada137-55355169-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-dde046734f50fc7c6a18ae9c19ada137-55355169]] attributes to Hochul the comparison of New York review time and cost with peer states and her link between later homebuying and delayed births.

## Qualifications

Each episode supplies a policy snapshot rather than a full assessment of implementation or outcomes. The Cornell allegations remain contested and the investigation's appointment is not a finding. The sources do not settle the effects of the assisted-dying safeguards or data-center pause. Housing review-time, per-unit cost, homebuying-age, production, and fertility claims remain episode-attributed, and the source does not isolate the effects of environmental review or the City of Yes initiative.

## What Changed

- Expanded the profile from risk intervention to housing affordability and production.
- Added the claimed link from approval delay to home cost, homebuying age, and family timing.
