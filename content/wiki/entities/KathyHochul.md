# Kathy Hochul

## Overview

Kathy Hochul is the New York governor appearing in sources on assisted dying, hyperscale data centers, and state oversight of a campus sexual-assault case.

## Current Profile

The three sources present Hochul using state executive authority across difficult risk tradeoffs. She signs a tightly safeguarded assisted-dying law, pauses the largest data-center construction while resource and subsidy concerns are reconsidered, and appoints Attorney General [[LetitiaJames]] to investigate the Cornell Seven case. The common feature is intervention under contested medical, infrastructure, or institutional conditions, not evidence that each policy achieved its intended outcome.

## Key Characteristics

- Signed New York's assisted-dying law with additional procedural safeguards.
- Linked the assisted-dying decision to personal experience of her mother's ALS death.
- Ordered a one-year pause on hyperscale data-center construction.
- Responded to power, water, land, noise, bill, and subsidy concerns around data centers.
- Appointed an external state investigation into the Cornell Seven case.

## Evidence

- Medical law: [[a-keir-death-experience-britains-pm-clings-on-698b0dc3337b2a3b2ef0aa07]] describes Hochul signing the law and supporting mental-fitness review plus filmed, independently witnessed requests.
- Infrastructure: [[tech-20260717-0717-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260717-0717-mp-tech-pod-128]] links her moratorium to hyperscale facilities' local resource and fiscal burdens.
- Campus oversight: [[gilets-jeunes-frances-school-protests-2959cbdca16de39a43a75666b5bc7057]] says she appointed James to investigate the Cornell case.

## Qualifications

Each episode supplies a policy snapshot rather than a full assessment of implementation or outcomes. The Cornell allegations remain contested and the investigation's appointment is not a finding. The sources do not settle the effects of the assisted-dying safeguards or data-center pause.
