## Overview
香港足球总会 / Hong Kong Football Association appears in the source as the governing body that moved Hong Kong league football from nominal amateurism into allowed professionalism after the 1968 Olympic amateur-status dispute.

## Current Profile
The association's role in this wiki branch is institutional rather than administrative. The source says Hong Kong football had long involved paid players despite formal amateur status, partly because amateur classification mattered for Olympic and Asian Games eligibility. When the Hong Kong Olympic Committee refused to approve football participation on the ground that players were effectively professional, the football association amended its rules to allow professional players in the league.

Later, the association also appears as the rule-setting body around foreign-player limits, all-Chinese-squad policy, and commercial-team access through membership borrowing or takeover. Those rules helped shape whether patrons had to build from lower divisions, could borrow an existing club's place, or would withdraw when import-player restrictions made competition less attractive.

## Key Characteristics
- Governing body in the source's explanation of the 1968 professionalization turn.
- Institutional mediator between Olympic amateur eligibility and paid club football reality.
- Rule setter for later foreign-player limits and all-Chinese-squad policy.
- Competition-access gatekeeper whose membership rules affected commercial teams after the 1980s.
