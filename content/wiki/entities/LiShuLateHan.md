# 李术 / Li Shu (late Han)

## Overview
李术 / Li Shu is the Lujiang administrator whose refusal of [[SunQuan|孙权]]'s authority becomes Sun Quan's first major internal-consolidation war in Hanji 1053. His page records a hard-edged case: a Sun Ce-era appointment can become a defection shelter and force a young successor to test whether inherited command is real.

## Current Profile
Hanji 1053 presents Li Shu as the Lujiang official left in place after Sun-family expansion into the [[LujiangCommandery|庐江]] field. When Sun Quan succeeds, Li Shu refuses to accept Sun Quan's control and shelters defectors from the Sun side. Sun Quan then writes [[CaoCao|曹操]] to frame Li Shu as the killer of court official Yan Xiang and asks Cao Cao not to aid him. After Cao Cao ignores Li Shu's appeal, Sun Quan attacks, captures [[WanChengLujiang|皖城]], kills Li Shu, slaughters civilians, and relocates more than twenty thousand people.

## Key Characteristics
- Converts a Sun-side commandery post into resistance against Sun Quan's succession authority.
- Shelters defectors, making Lujiang a leakage point for Sun Quan's fragile regime.
- Tries to use Cao Cao as an outside rescue channel after Sun Quan attacks.
- Becomes the negative case through which Sun Quan turns legal-political framing and military force into a visible authority test.
- Carries a severe civilian-cost qualification because the source records slaughter and forced relocation after Wan City's capture.

- Cao-side cutoff: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1053-genzhe-sunquan-xuexi-ruhe-zhengdun-jituan-gaoguan-lq9hy25xonjzhj1sfisvauu3bwmi|Hanji 1053]] reports Sun Quan's letter to Cao Cao about Yan Xiang and Cao Cao's refusal to save Li Shu.
- Defeat and coercive settlement: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1053-genzhe-sunquan-xuexi-ruhe-zhengdun-jituan-gaoguan-lq9hy25xonjzhj1sfisvauu3bwmi|Hanji 1053]] records Sun Quan's capture of Wan City, Li Shu's death, civilian slaughter, and relocation of more than twenty thousand people.

## Qualifications
The current source gives Li Shu mainly through Sun Quan's consolidation campaign and Sun Quan's letter to Cao Cao. The wiki therefore treats the Yan Xiang accusation and Cao-side non-rescue as source-scoped evidence rather than a full independent biography. The civilian slaughter remains part of the judgment and prevents a purely affirmative "leadership" reading of the campaign.
