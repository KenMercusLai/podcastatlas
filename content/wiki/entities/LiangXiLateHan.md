## Overview

梁习 / Liang Xi is a late-Han official whom Hanji 1073 presents as Cao Cao's military-administrative choice for settling [[BingzhouLateHan|并州]] after [[GaoGanLateHan|高干]]'s defeat.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1073-huguan-zhi-zhan-caocao-de-tucheng-jihua-haican-le-shui-lspli7p63ouktn6k0zbgvggj59di|Hanji 1073]] portrays Liang Xi as a lower-status official who rises through local administrative work before [[CaoCao|曹操]] gives him both civil and military authority in Bingzhou. His settlement method is layered rather than simply conciliatory or punitive. He first treats local strongmen courteously and recommends some for office, then recruits local young men and disperses them among different commands, removing concentrated fighting capacity from the strongmen's home bases.

The next steps are more openly coercive. Liang moves tens of thousands of the soldiers' and officials' relatives to [[YeLateHan|邺城]], attacks those who resist, and accepts large-scale surrender after several thousand deaths in the episode's account. The source credits the resulting order with Xiongnu and tribal submission, regular taxation and service, farming and sericulture, stricter law, and the recommendation of displaced scholars such as [[ChangLinLateHan|常林]]. The durable profile is therefore effective frontier consolidation through combined incorporation, dispersal, relocation, and force—not a purely benevolent local administration.

## Key Characteristics

- Rises from lower-level clerical and county administration in the episode's account.
- Receives combined provincial administrative and military authority from Cao Cao.
- Begins by honoring and appointing influential local strongmen.
- Separates local armed capacity from its home networks by recruiting and dispersing young men among commands.
- Relocates strongmen's families to Ye and uses armed suppression against refusal.
- Is credited with frontier submission, fiscal order, agriculture, sericulture, and stricter law.
- Recommends displaced scholars for office after coercive stabilization.

## Qualifications

This profile rests on one short podcast treatment rather than a full Liang Xi biography or independent audit of the outcome. The episode's praise from local elders coexists with forced relocation, killings, and mass surrender. Exact totals, the degree of Cao Cao's prior design, whether relocated families functioned as hostages, the legal scope of Liang's dual offices, and the later careers of every recommended scholar remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Created the profile from Hanji 1073's Bingzhou settlement account.
- Distinguished administrative effectiveness from the coercive means used to produce it.
