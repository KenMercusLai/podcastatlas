# Alberta

## Overview
Alberta is represented as the Canadian province whose oil wealth creates both national trade leverage and a resource-centered separatist grievance.

## Current Profile
Alberta first appears as a constraint on [[MarkCarney]]’s response to American tariffs: it has low direct tariff exposure in the earlier source while supplying a large share of U.S. imported oil, so energy retaliation could also intensify provincial anger. The oil-sands source adds production concentration, high producer profits, [[TransMountainExpansion]] capacity, and environmental and cost boundaries.

The newest episode makes separatism explicit. It describes a non-binding independence referendum and frames Alberta’s grievance through fiscal contribution, restricted oil development, entrepreneurial self-understanding, and resentment of distant federal control. This differs from Quebec’s language- and culture-centered movement even when both pressure Ottawa at the same time.

## Key Characteristics
- Central oil-sands production setting and source of Canadian energy leverage.
- Lower direct tariff exposure than Quebec in the earlier trade source.
- Political constraint on federal energy retaliation and infrastructure decisions.
- Resource- and fiscal-grievance case within Canadian separatism.
- Environmental and cost boundary because oil-sands production is described as dirtier and more expensive.
