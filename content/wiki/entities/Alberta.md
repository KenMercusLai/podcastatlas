# Alberta

## Overview
Alberta is represented in the wiki as the Canadian province whose oil position makes national trade retaliation and energy infrastructure politically difficult.

## Current Profile
Alberta first appears as a constraint on [[MarkCarney]]'s response to [[DonaldTrump]]'s tariffs. The trade source says Alberta has almost zero tariff exposure on exports to the [[UnitedStates]] while supplying more than half of America's imported oil, making energy retaliation costly for Canadian federal politics and salient to provincial separatist pressure.

The new oil-sands segment makes Alberta more than a leverage node. It says 97% of Canadian oil comes from Alberta's oil sands, describes bitumen extraction as more expensive and dirtier than conventional oil, and links the province to strong producer profits, [[TransMountainExpansion]] capacity, and [[CanadianOilSandsBoom]].

## Key Characteristics
- Provincial energy base that complicates Canada's federal trade response.
- Low direct tariff exposure in the earlier trade source compared with Quebec's higher exposure.
- Central oil-sands production setting in the new energy segment.
- Political constraint because energy leverage against the United States can also anger Alberta.
- Environmental and cost boundary because oil-sands production is described as dirtier and more expensive.
