# Ku Klux Klan

## Overview

The Ku Klux Klan is a U.S. white-supremacist and nativist movement whose second incarnation became a mass social and political organization during the 1920s before scandal, exposure, backlash, and institutional weakness drove rapid decline.

## Current Profile

The bounded source presents the second Klan as more than a clandestine terror group. It joined anti-Black racism, antisemitism, anti-Catholicism, and “100% Americanism” to fraternal business, Protestant respectability, picnics, fairs, newspapers, women's organizations, electoral machinery, and highly visible spectacle. Those ordinary-looking forms widened recruitment while armed raids, beatings, abduction, flogging, and intimidation remained close at hand.

Under [[HiramWesleyEvans]], the national organization sought respectability and mainstream political influence. [[DCStephenson]] built its strongest state machine in Indiana, but his predation against [[MadgeOberholtzer]], conviction, and disclosure of bribery exposed the contradiction between moral guardianship and leadership corruption. The episode distinguishes this 1920s mass formation from the smaller, intensely racist third Klan founded in 1946.

## Key Characteristics

- It defined authentic Americanness through exclusion of African Americans, Jews, Catholics, immigrants, and other constructed outsiders.
- It blended spectacle and family sociability with secrecy, hierarchy, ritual, and vigilante force.
- Recruitment and revenue depended on fraternal organization, paid organizers, newspapers, events, and women's logistical labor.
- It converted membership networks into electoral endorsements, turnout, officeholding, and influence over public institutions.
- Its national unity was fragile because money, prestige, local variation, corruption, and leadership rivalry pulled the organization apart.
- Its resemblance to fascism is substantial but incomplete under a historically specific definition.

## Qualifications

The page is grounded in one synthetic podcast episode, principally about the second Klan's peak and fall. Membership, crowd, officeholder, and present-day estimates are source-attributed. The relative weight of ideology, entertainment, religion, class, gender, violence, policy success, scandal, and economic change remains debated, and local Klans differed. Calling the movement fascist depends on the definition used and should not erase its specifically U.S. histories of race, Protestant nativism, Reconstruction, and vigilantism.

- Created a profile joining the second Klan's social, violent, commercial, and electoral forms.
- Distinguished the 1920s mass organization from the later third Klan.
