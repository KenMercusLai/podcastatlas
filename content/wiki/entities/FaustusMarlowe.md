## Overview

Faustus / 福士德 is the scholar at the center of [[DoctorFaustusMarlowe|Christopher Marlowe's tragedy]], exchanging his soul for twenty-four years of demonic service.

## Current Profile

Faustus begins with genuine intellectual ambition but treats every finite discipline as a failure. He wants knowledge to remove mortality and make nature obey, so he rejects the possibility that learning might instead disclose limits, correct desire, or guide judgment.

His tragedy is a loss of proportion and self-direction. After accepting [[MephistophelesMarlowe|Mephistopheles]] as an apparent servant, he accepts new limits on inquiry, diverts great power into spectacle and petty domination, and repeatedly lets immediate stimulation interrupt reflection on consequences he already understands.

## Key Characteristics

- Intellectually accomplished but intolerant of finite achievement.
- Equates valuable knowledge with unlimited command.
- Trades durable future agency for bounded present capability.
- Retains awareness of consequences without sustained action on that awareness.
- Gains external options while losing reflective control over desire and direction.
