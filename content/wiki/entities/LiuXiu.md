## Overview

Liu Xiu / 刘秀 is synthesized as the Eastern Han founder whose profile spans restoration warfare, founding coalition formation, remote campaign judgment, post-conquest repair, post-unification settlement, domestic pacification, palace-succession management, omen politics, legal discipline, frontier settlement, ritual legitimacy, health vulnerability, and later Guangwu memory. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-711-ta-zhu-liuxiu-da-xia-banbi-jiangshan-weihe-si-fa-biequ-liib38zub0-tgrbtycmlqvohv-w|Hanji 711]] adds the Jianwu 11-12 pre-Chengdu layer: Liu Xiu benefits from Cen Peng and Zang Gong's tempo breakthrough, writes to Gongsun Shu urging surrender, returns to Luoyang when Shu seems close to settled, appoints Wu Han after Cen Peng's assassination, accepts Ma Yuan's Jincheng frontier-resource plan, and receives Guo Ji's warning that official selection should widen beyond Nanyang compatriots. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-712-liuxiu-weihe-bei-yuwei-zui-hui-da-zhang-de-huangdi-lt7rx7tblodjfngqfdq33ikrcmyn|Hanji 712]] then adds the Shu-campaign command layer: Liu Xiu orders Wu Han to hold Guangdu, predicts the danger of Wu Han's separated Chengdu-area camp and Liu Shang's distant southern camp, and reads the later recovery through restored concentration at Guangdu. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-713-liuxiu-nu-le-mingjiang-wuhan-jing-xuexi-chengdu-lv6555l99it33gw34uydsz4tkbje|Hanji 713]] adds the Shu-conquest aftermath: Liu Xiu condemns Wu Han and Liu Shang's violence after Chengdu has already surrendered, then repairs Shu through honors for refusers, repayment, summons, former-opponent appointments, and reward for Wen Qi's local loyalty. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-714-shi-shui-di-yi-ge-han-chu-nan-er-dang-ma-ge-guo-shi-lpkqxrl3dsok4ui0lij4c3x8zfpi|Hanji 714]] follows with the Jianwu 12-13 consolidation layer before [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-715-shishang-zui-zhihui-de-yici-duoquan-loqkawl247maw9fn-nomaw5gzkgu|Hanji 715]] adds the 37 CE post-unification merit settlement and the later episodes develop legal, frontier, ritual, health, and succession layers.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-705-1-diwang-moulue-liuxiu-ruhe-yi-sheling-nizhuan-caiyuan-fengyun-lq2m06p7eczj9ebysxpl84bkzzi|Hanji 705-1]] now adds the still-earlier Jianwu 7-8 layer: Liu Xiu demobilizes local troops back to farming, turns a solar eclipse into sealed-memorial consultation, accepts [[ZhengXingEasternHan|Zheng Xing]] and [[ChenYuanEasternHan|Chen Yuan]]'s corrective advice, issues an amnesty, appoints [[LiTongEasternHan|Li Tong]] as Da Sikong, benefits from [[DuShiEasternHan|Du Shi]]'s Nanyang welfare governance, and uses [[LaiXiEasternHan|Lai Xi]]'s Lüeyang seizure to make [[WeiXiaoEasternHan|Wei Xiao]] exhaust elite troops.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-699-gan-yu-liuxiu-zhengxiong-de-ta-weihe-candan-shouchang-lth-8mndudj3khf9cnsouj86c1-z|Hanji 699]] now pushes the profile one step earlier: Liu Xiu's order is still forming through [[CenPengEasternHan|Cen Peng]]'s southern correspondence, Qu Chong's proclamations, tribute and enfeoffment from Jiaozhi/Jiangnan actors, [[XiGuangEasternHan|Xi Guang]] and [[RenYanEasternHan|Ren Yan]]'s frontier instruction, tolerance of [[ZhouDangEasternHan|Zhou Dang]] and [[YanGuangEasternHan|Yan Guang]] refusing office, and [[ShacheKingKangEasternHan|King Kang of Shache]]'s recognition through [[DouRongEasternHan|Dou Rong]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-708-jizun-bingshi-weihe-yinfa-baiguan-jingkong-lhfuvjyiiirlaf-ugrufkaspuauq|Hanji 708]] now adds the earlier Jianwu 9 layer: Liu Xiu intensely mourns [[JiZunEasternHan|Ji Zun]] and then accepts [[YaoQiEasternHan|Yao Qi]]'s warning that repeated praise is frightening the ministers, restores [[GoguryeoLateHan|Goguryeo]]'s royal title after tribute, faces continuing Wei Xiao/Gongsun Shu and Xiongnu pressure, approves [[LaiXiEasternHan|Lai Xi]]'s supply-backed Longxi/Tianshui plan, and accepts [[BanBiaoEasternHan|Ban Biao]]'s Qiang-governance diagnosis.

## Current Profile

Earlier Wang Mang-focused sources place Liu Xiu as the Liu-house restoration figure whose victory at the [[KunyangBattle|Kunyang Battle]] exposes the fragility of [[WangMang|Wang Mang]]'s [[XinDynasty|Xin dynasty]]. Those sources also show continuity between rebellion-era prophecy and imperial control: Liu Xiu benefits from mandate and chenwei language but later makes [[ChenweiPolitics|chenwei politics]] less freely available to private actors.

Hanji 733-2 supplies the founding coalition layer. Liu Xiu needs northern cavalry and capable commanders, and [[GengYanEasternHan|Geng Yan]] becomes valuable because he can connect family resources from [[GengKuangEasternHan|Geng Kuang]]'s Shanggu/Youzhou environment to Liu Xiu's campaign. Liu Xiu's trust is not automatic: [[PengChongEasternHan|Peng Chong]]'s rebellion forces the Geng family to make loyalty visible through return-to-court and [[GengGuoEasternHan|Geng Guo]]'s service in Luoyang.

Hanji 699 supplies an early consolidation layer before the current Jianwu 7-8 correction sequence. Liu Xiu's side uses Cen Peng's letter to Jiaozhi Mu Deng Rang and Qu Chong's circulation of imperial orders to bring southern and Jiangnan actors into tribute and enfeoffment. The same source shows his order seeking moral talent without forcing every refusal: Zhou Dang returns to the mountains after a public criticism dispute, Yan Guang refuses appointment despite old friendship, and Wang Liang's withdrawal exposes a harsher reputation-accountability branch.

Hanji 705-1 supplies the Jianwu 7-8 consolidation layer. Liu Xiu sends commandery, kingdom, and fief troops back to farming while Gongsun Shu, Wei Xiao, and Lu Fang remain unsettled; after a solar eclipse, he asks for sealed criticism and receives Zheng Xing's warning about public feeling, strict political tempo, and the delayed Da Sikong appointment. The same source shows Liu Xiu answering pressure through amnesty, Li Tong's appointment, acceptance of Chen Yuan's warning against suspicious supervision of the Three Excellencies, recognition of Du Shi's welfare-producing Nanyang administration, and patient use of Lai Xi's Lüeyang holdout to trap Wei Xiao's elite troops.

Hanji 708 supplies the earlier Jianwu 9 pressure layer. Liu Xiu honors [[WenXuEasternHan|Wen Xu]] after suicide under capture, mourns Ji Zun with unusually visible grief, then stops repeating the Ji Zun lament after Yao Qi warns that other ministers feel ashamed and frightened. The same year shows Liu Xiu as a still-pressured frontier ruler: he recognizes Goguryeo after tribute, continues fighting the Wei Xiao/Wei Chun and Gongsun Shu field, assigns northern defensive posts after Xiongnu-backed setbacks, approves Lai Xi's grain-and-inducement plan for Longxi and Tianshui, and accepts Ban Biao's advice that Qiang unrest needs institutional communication and protection against local official abuse.

Hanji 711 supplies the pre-Chengdu Shu layer. Cen Peng's rapid breakthrough from Jiangzhou to Wuyang and Guangdu makes Shu collapse look close enough that Liu Xiu writes to [[GongsunShuEasternHan|Gongsun Shu]] urging surrender and later returns from Chang'an to Luoyang. When Gongsun Shu's assassins kill [[CenPengEasternHan|Cen Peng]], Liu Xiu appoints [[WuHanEasternHan|Wu Han]] as commander with [[LiuShangEasternHan|Liu Shang]] and [[ZangGongEasternHan|Zang Gong]] as deputies. The same source broadens Liu Xiu's statecraft profile: he accepts [[MaYuanEasternHan|Ma Yuan]]'s argument against abandoning Jincheng frontier resources and receives [[GuoJiEasternHan|Guo Ji]]'s warning that appointments should come from national talent rather than mainly from Nanyang networks.

Hanji 712 supplies the active Shu-campaign command layer. Liu Xiu does not simply urge attack after Wu Han's early victories; he reads Chengdu's remaining force, Guangdu's position, river-separated camps, and mutual-support timing as a system. His warning that Gongsun Shu's side could restrain Wu Han while attacking or isolating [[LiuShangEasternHan|Liu Shang]] is confirmed before the letter arrives, making the episode a case of remote operational judgment as well as top-down command discipline.

Hanji 713 supplies the 36 CE Shu-conquest aftermath. After [[YanCenEasternHan|Yan Cen]] offers Chengdu and surrenders following [[GongsunShuEasternHan|Gongsun Shu]]'s fatal wound, [[WuHanEasternHan|Wu Han]] kills Gongsun Shu's household and clan, destroys Yan Cen's clan, and lets troops loot and burn [[Chengdu]]. Liu Xiu rebukes Wu Han and [[LiuShangEasternHan|Liu Shang]] because Chengdu had already surrendered and officials and civilians had submitted. His later settlement praises [[LiNieEasternHan|Li Nie]], sacrifices to and compensates [[QiaoXuanShuScholar|Qiao Xuan]], summons Fei Yi, Ren Yong, and Feng Xin, uses former Gongsun Shu officers Cheng Ru and Li Yu, and rewards [[WenQiYizhouEasternHan|Wen Qi]], making conquest rule depend on publicly rejecting post-surrender plunder and rebuilding local elite confidence.

Hanji 714 supplies an earlier Jianwu 12-13 order-building layer. Liu Xiu's frontier edict tells border officials to defend when troop strength is insufficient and to pursue by actual enemy conditions rather than by fear of harsh delay law. He calls Dou Rong and five commandery governors to Luoyang, returns Dou Rong's marquis seal after ritual submission, appoints Dou Rong, [[LiangTongEasternHan|Liang Tong]], [[KongFenEasternHan|Kong Fen]], and [[RenTingEasternHan|Ren Ting]], refuses ordinary rare-food tribute to the imperial kitchen, rewards Zhi Yun after a gate-law and hunting remonstrance case, and appoints Sui Yu after Lu Fang's force fractures.

Hanji 715 supplies the first current post-unification settlement. With the realm unified in 37 CE, Liu Xiu reduces residual royal titles, expands fiefs for 365 meritorious officials, rewards commanders such as [[WuHanEasternHan|Wu Han]], and turns the founding coalition toward [[HonorsWithoutOfficeSettlement|honors without office]]: [[DengYuEasternHan|Deng Yu]], [[JiaFuEasternHan|Jia Fu]], [[GengYanEasternHan|Geng Yan]], and others surrender military authority while keeping rank, income, honor, and limited access. The episode presents this as a peaceful solution to [[PostRestorationMeritThreat|post-restoration merit threat]], while Dou Rong's rejected resignation shows Liu Xiu also managing the anxiety of powerful new affiliates.

Hanji 716 supplies the current 38-39 CE stabilization prelude. Liu Xiu refuses Western Regions states' request for restored Han management because the center is newly pacified and still unstable; receives Liang Tong and Du Lin's unresolved debate over penal severity, moderation, and enforceability; removes [[HanXinSituEasternHan|Han Xin / 韩歆]] after blunt famine prediction and sends the rebuke that drives Han Xin and his son to suicide; responds to worsening [[Xiongnu|Xiongnu]] raids through Wu Han, Ma Cheng, Ma Wu, border relocation, and stronger garrisons; and orders land checks that some officials use to disturb commoners and protect local magnates.

Hanji 717 then supplies the sharper 39 CE diagnostic layer. Liu Xiu sees a tiny Chenliu note saying Yingchuan and Hongnong can be consulted while Henan and Nanyang cannot, hears twelve-year-old Liu Yang explain that imperial-city ministers and imperial-hometown kin make those two commanderies unreliable benchmarks, and then has Chenliu officials questioned until their confession matches the explanation. The same source says Liu Xiu sends inspectors to examine high-ranking local officials for favoritism in degree-field reporting, jails Ouyang Xi for inaccurate Runan land measurement and embezzlement, lets Ouyang Xi die in prison despite petitions, and handles a parallel frontier branch through Lu Fang's Gaoliu return, Du Mao's removal, Ma Cheng's beacon defense, and Zhang Kan's Gaoliu/Yuyang service.

Hanji 718 supplies the earliest current stabilization layer after founding warfare. Liu Xiu punishes Henan Yin Zhang Ji and more than ten commandery administrators for cheating in land measurement and grain affairs, then accepts Ma Yuan's view that officials who damage the people through fiscal fraud deserved death. The same source gives Liu Xiu's anti-bandit policy: bandits can inform on one another, groups that kill bandits can be pardoned, local officials are judged by captured numbers rather than punished for every delay or fear, sheltering bandits is punished, and leaders are resettled with land and grain. It also adds Lu Fang's rewarded but brittle surrender settlement, Ma Yuan's five-zhu coin recommendation, and the Jiaozhi revolt led by Zheng Ce and [[ZhengErJiaozhi|征贰]].

Hanji 719 adds a palace-and-frontier layer immediately before the previously ingested late Guangwu sequence. It explains Guo Shengtong's marriage as a Hebei political alliance tied to [[LiuYangZhendingKingEasternHan|真定王刘杨]] during the [[WangLangHebeiClaimant|王郎]] crisis; once Liu Xiu consolidates, that alliance loses protective value. Liu Xiu deposes Guo under solar-eclipse and queenly-virtue language, makes Yin Lihua empress, orders no congratulations, makes [[LiuFuPeiKingEasternHan|刘辅]] Zhongshan king with enlarged territory, and preserves Guo as Zhongshan Taihou rather than eliminating her branch. [[ZhiYunEasternHan|郅恽]] warns that the change is not merely household business, which sets up Hanji 720's [[LiuQiangDonghaiKing|刘强]] succession crisis.

The same source gives Liu Xiu an earlier Western Regions recognition problem. He initially grants Shache King Xian a protectorate-level package, then accepts Pei Zun's objection and downgrades the grant to Great General. This makes his 41 CE frontier conduct less a simple withdrawal than a calibration problem: symbolic recognition, capacity, and other frontier audiences have to be kept aligned.

Hanji 721 supplies the 43-44 CE personnel and legal-discipline prelude. Liu Xiu uses [[YinShiEasternHan|Yin Shi]] and [[YinXingGuangwuMaternalKin|Yin Xing]] around Crown Prince Liu Zhuang, while [[HuanRongEasternHan|Huan Rong]] becomes a classics teacher whose Taixue performance receives imperial recognition. The same source tests Liu Xiu's family boundaries: after [[DongXuanEasternHan|董宣]] executes [[HuyangPrincessEasternHan|Princess Huyang]]'s murderer-slave and refuses forced apology, Liu Xiu shifts from anger to reward, making [[RuleOverKinshipPower|law over kinship power]] visible against his own sister's household. The Nandun tax-remission scene adds another restraint branch: Liu Xiu grants favor but refuses to promise ten years of relief on emotional grounds alone.

Hanji 722 supplies the 44-45 CE illness and first frontier-capacity prelude. Liu Xiu's severe headache and dizziness make him think he may die, so he appoints Yin Xing as shizhong and entrusts affairs to him. After recovery, Liu Xiu wants Yin Xing to replace the dead [[WuHanEasternHan|吴汉]] as great marshal, but Yin refuses to protect Liu Xiu's virtue from the appearance of excessive outer-relative appointment. The same source shows Liu Xiu compensating the displaced Guo branch by changing Liu Fu to Pei king and rewarding Guo Kuang, while still handling northern raids and Western Regions requests under limited capacity.

Hanji 723 supplies the 46 CE court-and-frontier prelude. Liu Xiu travels to Chang'an and returns to Luoyang, receives solar-eclipse and earthquake notices, removes Zhu Fu as Grand Minister of Works, appoints Du Lin, and praises Liu Kun's modest response to miracle-like virtue stories. On the frontier, he receives Punu's heqin request after Xiongnu famine and succession churn, learns that Wuhuan attack has driven Xiongnu north, and removes some roadside and border defenses while using money and silk to attract Wuhuan. The same source makes his Western Regions answer a case of [[CapacityBoundFrontierCommitment|capacity-bound frontier commitment]]: Shanshan asks for a restored protectorate, but Liu Xiu says Han lacks practical capacity to send envoys and troops.

Hanji 724 through Hanji 727 supply Liu Xiu's later frontier decision sequence. He accepts [[MaYuanEasternHan|马援]]'s Wuling deployment after Liu Shang's defeat, accepts [[GengGuoEasternHan|耿国]]'s argument for receiving [[SouthernXiongnu|Southern Xiongnu]] as a buffer, rewards [[ZongJunEasternHan|宗均]] after unauthorized but successful Wuling pacification, institutionalizes Wuhuan submission, and protects Southern Xiongnu through ritual, hostages, gifts, grain, livestock, guards, winter protection, patrol duties, and border-commandery restoration. He also refuses destabilizing Northern Xiongnu contact when Southern Xiongnu confidence is newly fragile.

Hanji 729 through Hanji 732 supply the personnel, ritual, illness, death, and succession endgame. Liu Xiu accepts [[BanBiaoEasternHan|Ban Biao]]'s calibrated Northern Xiongnu reply, recognizes quiet merit in [[JiaFuEasternHan|贾复]], tests rumors around [[FifthLunEasternHan|第五伦]], and still moves from earlier Taishan refusal into chenwei-backed fengshan. His reaction to [[HuanTanEasternHan|桓谭]] shows how legitimacy-sensitive prophecy becomes dangerous to contradict. [[ZizhiTongjian|Zizhi Tongjian]] records Liu Xiu's death in 57 CE in terse date/place/age form, while [[DongguanHanji|Dongguan Hanji]] notices from 41 CE and 44 CE suggest earlier illness and heavy workload. Later sources add hostage-ransom precedent and Guangwu tomb memory.

Across those annalistic layers, Liu Xiu's strongest evaluative profile is [[CultivatedImperialCapacity|cultivated imperial capacity]]: [[Taixue|太学]] learning, [[KunyangBattle|Kunyang]] initiative, post-victory trust through the [[WangLangHebeiClaimant|Wang Lang]] letters, non-purge treatment of merit holders, and self-command after Liu Yan's death are read together rather than as isolated talents.

## Key Characteristics

- Learned restoration founder whose fallen-lineage Taixue formation and military-political effectiveness defeat Wang Mang's ritualized regime and later include subordinate tempo exploitation, surrender-letter pressure, command succession, and remote operational risk judgment in the Shu campaign.
- Coalition builder and postwar centralizer who recognizes commanders, uses marriage alliance when weak, later manages suspicion around powerful founding families, and turns founding commanders from military authority into honored but limited merit nobles.
- Domestic-order ruler who combines southern tribute incorporation, noncoercive recluse handling, demobilization, amnesty, senior-minister trust correction, surrendered-city civilian protection norms, post-conquest local-elite repair, tribute restraint, law-bound remonstrance acceptance, welfare-producing local governance, penal-law debate, land-survey disturbance, degree-field diagnostics, harsh fiscal accountability, anti-bandit incentive redesign, surrender incorporation, and currency restoration.
- Palace-succession ruler who can convert a depreciated founding marriage into deposition, compensation, and heir-field instability.
- Personnel judge who can use clean officials, blunt remonstrants, maternal kin, scholars, law-enforcement officials, frontier specialists, reclusive refusals, nationwide appointment advice, and post-victory trust repair while learning when praise of one model official pressures others.
- Ruler vulnerable to severe illness, succession anxiety, remonstrance intolerance, and accusation in Ma Yuan's case, but still reachable through evidence-based corrective memorials.
- Frontier manager who combines southern proclamation, ritual-agricultural instruction, early Shache proxy recognition, key-position attrition against Wei Xiao, royal-title recognition, resource-retention repair, flexible pursuit rules, capacity-bound deferral, forced relocation, recognition downgrading, hostage-retention signals, submitted-buffer acceptance, protected settlement, office design, and restraint against overextended war.

## Qualifications

Hanji 699 PLUS is explicitly evaluative. Its superlatives about Liu Xiu's learning, personnel use, battlefield ability, and "极品皇帝" status should remain source-scoped rankings rather than a completed comparative biography of every emperor it names.

These sources address Liu Xiu through different windows rather than a single biography. Hanji 699 supports an early consolidation reading through southern tribute, reclusive tolerance, and Shache recognition, but its minor southern actors, title formulas, and transcript variants remain source-scoped. Hanji 705-1 supports a Jianwu 7-8 correction-and-consolidation reading, but it should not be overread as proof that every amnesty, appointment, or western campaign move was caused only by Zheng Xing, Chen Yuan, or Lai Xi. Hanji 708's Ji Zun scene supports a specific public-signal correction, not a claim that Liu Xiu intended to threaten all ministers or formally censure them. Hanji 711's Shu success belongs operationally to Cen Peng and Zang Gong; Liu Xiu's own evidence there is surrender-letter pressure, strategic assessment, command replacement after assassination, acceptance of Ma Yuan's frontier plan, and reception of Guo Ji's personnel critique. Hanji 712 shows accurate remote military judgment, but it should not be inflated into omniscience or direct field command over every tactical move; the source's evidence is the specific Wu Han-Liu Shang camp geometry and its rapid confirmation. Hanji 713 reports anger and rebuke over the Chengdu sack but does not establish formal punishment for Wu Han or Liu Shang, so the evidence supports a conquest-discipline boundary more than an institutional sanction. Hanji 714's positive portrait of flexible military rules, tribute restraint, and accepted remonstrance should be held beside Hanji 716's Han Xin case and Hanji 731's Huan Tan case, which show real tolerance limits. Hanji 715's peaceful merit-settlement claim should not be inflated into absence of pressure: it shows reward-backed military authority recovery and a source-scoped claim that the Yuntai commanders avoided political persecution. Hanji 716's legal debate is not an enacted code revision, and Han Xin's death shows a tolerance limit rather than a full formal criminal case in the current evidence. Hanji 717's Henan/Nanyang comparison should be read as a source-bounded diagnosis of protected fiscal environments, not as proof that every elite holding there exceeded legal limits. Hanji 718's bandit-pacification success and law-versus-human-feeling frame remain source-scoped to 40 CE and should not be turned into a universal theory of Liu Xiu's penal style. Hanji 719's "渣男" framing and comments about Guo's resentment are source-scoped interpretation; the durable claim is narrower, concerning political marriage value, palace-succession handling, omen language, and compensation. Hanji 721's Dong Xuan case should not be treated as modern rule of law: Liu Xiu's backing of enforcement remains a ruler decision after anger. Hanji 722 through Hanji 729 form a changed frontier-contact sequence, so each answer has to be read by timing and capacity rather than as one fixed policy. Hanji 732 does not prove Liu Xiu's cause of death; its strongest contribution is long-term health vulnerability under workload.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 699 PLUS as a high-level cultivated-capacity frame tying Liu Xiu's Taixue study, Kunyang initiative, Wang Lang letter-burning trust repair, non-purge reputation, and emotional restraint into one ruler-evaluation layer.
- Preserved Hanji 699 and Hanji 705-1/708 as the early Guangwu consolidation sequence from southern proclamation and reclusive tolerance through demobilization, eclipse correction, Lüeyang attrition, praise-pressure correction, and frontier advice.
- Preserved Hanji 711-715 as the Shu command, post-surrender repair, consolidation, and post-unification merit-settlement sequence.
- Preserved Hanji 716-718 as the subsequent stabilization baseline of penal-law debate, degree-field diagnostics, fiscal accountability, bandit policy, and Jiaozhi revolt.
- Preserved Hanji 719-732 plus later Hanji 899/915 as palace-succession, personnel, legal-discipline, illness, frontier, ritual, death, memory, and precedent evidence.
