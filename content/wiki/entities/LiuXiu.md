## Overview

Liu Xiu / 刘秀 is synthesized as the Eastern Han founder whose profile spans restoration warfare, omen politics, founding coalition formation, personnel judgment, royal-household legal discipline, frontier settlement, ritual legitimacy, health vulnerability, succession, hostage-ransom precedent, and later Guangwu ritual memory. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-721-ta-sha-jianu-ma-gongzhu-weihe-fanbei-shang-lryzcejdnrm4-92esnddfvyjkqli|Hanji 721]] now adds the 43-44 CE prelude to his late-rule profile: Liu Xiu uses cautious maternal kin and classicists around the crown prince, backs [[DongXuanEasternHan|董宣]] after the [[HuyangPrincessEasternHan|Princess Huyang]] enforcement crisis, limits a local tax-remission promise, and honors [[WuHanEasternHan|Wu Han]] after a deathbed warning against easy amnesty.

## Current Profile

Earlier Wang Mang-focused sources place Liu Xiu as the Liu-house restoration figure whose victory at the [[KunyangBattle|Kunyang Battle]] exposes the fragility of [[WangMang|Wang Mang]]'s [[XinDynasty|Xin dynasty]]. Those sources also show continuity between rebellion-era prophecy and imperial control: Liu Xiu benefits from mandate and chenwei language but later makes [[ChenweiPolitics|chenwei politics]] less freely available to private actors.

Hanji 733-2 supplies the founding coalition layer. Liu Xiu needs northern cavalry and capable commanders, and [[GengYanEasternHan|Geng Yan]] becomes valuable because he can connect family resources from [[GengKuangEasternHan|Geng Kuang]]'s Shanggu/Youzhou environment to Liu Xiu's campaign. Liu Xiu's trust is not automatic: [[PengChongEasternHan|Peng Chong]]'s rebellion forces the Geng family to make loyalty visible through return-to-court and [[GengGuoEasternHan|Geng Guo]]'s service in Luoyang.

Hanji 721 supplies the 43-44 CE personnel and legal-discipline prelude. Liu Xiu uses [[YinShiEasternHan|Yin Shi]] and [[YinXingGuangwuMaternalKin|Yin Xing]] around Crown Prince Liu Zhuang, while [[HuanRongEasternHan|Huan Rong]] becomes a classics teacher whose Taixue performance receives imperial recognition. The same source tests Liu Xiu's family boundaries: after Dong Xuan executes Princess Huyang's murderer-slave and refuses forced apology, Liu Xiu shifts from anger to reward, making [[RuleOverKinshipPower|law over kinship power]] visible against his own sister's household. The Nandun tax-remission scene adds another restraint branch: Liu Xiu grants favor but refuses to promise ten years of relief on emotional grounds alone.

Hanji 722 supplies the 44-45 CE illness and first frontier-capacity prelude. Liu Xiu's severe headache and dizziness make him think he may die, so he appoints Yin Xing as shizhong and entrusts affairs to him. After recovery, Liu Xiu wants Yin Xing to replace the dead Wu Han as great marshal, but Yin refuses to protect Liu Xiu's virtue from the appearance of excessive outer-relative appointment. The same source shows Liu Xiu compensating the displaced Guo branch by changing [[LiuFuPeiKingEasternHan|Liu Fu]] to Pei king and rewarding Guo Kuang, while still handling northern raids and Western Regions requests under limited capacity.

Hanji 723 supplies the 46 CE court-and-frontier prelude. Liu Xiu travels to Chang'an and returns to Luoyang, receives solar-eclipse and earthquake notices, removes Zhu Fu as Grand Minister of Works, appoints Du Lin, and praises Liu Kun's modest response to miracle-like virtue stories. On the frontier, he receives Punu's heqin request after Xiongnu famine and succession churn, learns that Wuhuan attack has driven Xiongnu north, and removes some roadside and border defenses while using money and silk to attract Wuhuan. The same source makes his Western Regions answer a case of [[CapacityBoundFrontierCommitment|capacity-bound frontier commitment]]: Shanshan asks for a restored protectorate, but Liu Xiu says Han lacks practical capacity to send envoys and troops.

Hanji 724 supplies a 47-48 CE frontier-decision layer. Liu Xiu faces a failed Wuling campaign after Liu Shang is killed, tests [[MaYuanEasternHan|Ma Yuan]]'s age before accepting his request to go south, and receives Bi's Southern Xiongnu submission request while the court doubts Han capacity and Xiongnu intent. Liu Xiu follows [[GengGuoEasternHan|Geng Guo]]'s argument for acceptance as a buffer-and-restoration strategy, but keeps Zhang Gong's opportunistic attack proposal under review rather than immediately striking the weakened Xiongnu field.

Hanji 726 adds a more mixed 49 CE statecraft layer. Liu Xiu is vulnerable to [[LiangSongLinxiangMarquis|Liang Song]]'s accusation after Ma Yuan's death and seizes Ma's seal, but he also receives [[ZhuBoEasternHan|Zhu Bo]]'s corrective memorial and moves away from pure anger. The same source shows a pragmatic ruler rewarding [[ZongJunEasternHan|Zong Jun]] after Zong falsely invokes imperial authority to settle [[WulingManLateHan|Wuling Man]] surrender, then using titles, food, clothing, frontier relocation, and a restored Wuhuan protector office to turn [[Wuhuan|Wuhuan]] submission into managed border capacity.

Hanji 727 and Hanji 729 continue this late-rule frontier profile. Liu Xiu turns [[SouthernXiongnu|Southern Xiongnu]] submission into a protected settlement through edict ritual, hostages, gifts, grain, livestock, guard forces, winter protection, patrol duties, and border-commandery restoration. He refuses destabilizing Northern Xiongnu contact in 51 CE, then accepts [[BanBiaoEasternHan|Ban Biao]]'s more calibrated 52 CE reply to [[NorthernXiongnuLateHan|Northern Xiongnu]] tribute and heqin requests. He also refuses an opportunistic Northern Xiongnu attack because disaster-era people and near-frontier recovery matter more than distant victory.

Hanji 730 and Hanji 731 supply the personnel-and-ritual tension. Liu Xiu recognizes quiet military merit in [[JiaFuEasternHan|Jia Fu]], tests damaging rumors through direct questioning of [[FifthLunEasternHan|Fifth Lun]], and still tries to limit some ritual costs. Yet chenwei text pushes him toward Taishan fengshan, and his reaction to [[HuanTanEasternHan|Huan Tan]] shows how legitimacy-sensitive prophecy becomes dangerous to contradict.

Hanji 732 supplies the end-of-life layer. [[ZizhiTongjian|Zizhi Tongjian]] records Liu Xiu's death in 57 CE in terse date/place/age form, while [[DongguanHanji|Dongguan Hanji]] notices from 41 CE and 44 CE suggest earlier illness and heavy workload. Later sources add two afterlives: Hanji 915 treats Liu Xiu's 33 CE response to the kidnapping of [[YinLihua|Yin Lihua]]'s mother and brother as a hostage-ransom deterrence precedent, and Hanji 899 places Guangwu's Yuanling inside later Eastern Han tomb sacrifice and filial interpretation.

## Key Characteristics

- Restoration founder whose military-political effectiveness defeats Wang Mang's ritualized regime.
- Coalition builder who recognizes commanders and manages suspicion around powerful founding military families.
- Personnel judge who can use maternal kin, scholars, and blunt law-enforcement officials while limiting kinship convenience.
- Ruler vulnerable to severe illness, succession anxiety, and later accusation in Ma Yuan's case, but still reachable through evidence-based corrective memorials.
- Frontier manager who combines capacity-bound deferral, hostage-retention signals, submitted-buffer acceptance, titles, provisioning, protected settlement, office design, and restraint against overextended war.
- Ritual ruler whose frugality is real but qualified by eventual Taishan fengshan and anti-chenwei speech risk.
- Later memory figure for imperial death, succession, hostage deterrence, and Guangwu tomb ritual.

## Qualifications

These sources address Liu Xiu through different windows rather than a single biography. Hanji 721 adds a 43-44 CE personnel-and-law layer but should not be treated as modern rule of law: Liu Xiu's backing of Dong Xuan is a ruler decision after anger, not an independent judicial guarantee. Hanji 722, Hanji 723, Hanji 724, Hanji 727, and Hanji 729 form a changed frontier-contact sequence: 45 CE hostage retention as a narrow Western Regions deterrent signal, 46 CE refusal to restore the Western Regions Protectorate before Han can send envoys and troops, 48 CE acceptance of Bi's Southern Xiongnu vassalage and non-immediate attack deliberation, 50-51 CE protected settlement plus a hard refusal of Northern Xiongnu contact, and a 52 CE calibrated reply that manages Northern Xiongnu tribute without full recognition. Hanji 722 also adds a direct 44 CE illness scare, but it does not diagnose the disease. Hanji 726 qualifies the positive statecraft profile by showing that Liu Xiu can initially react to slander against a dead minister, but it also shows him accepting corrective evidence and rewarding useful field judgment. Hanji 729 and Hanji 730 likewise form a changed-condition sequence from 54 CE fengshan refusal to 56 CE chenwei-backed ceremony. Hanji 732 does not prove Liu Xiu's cause of death; its strongest contribution is long-term health vulnerability under workload.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 721 as the 43-44 CE prelude: Yin-family and Huan Rong crown-prince guidance, Dong Xuan's princess-household law case, Nandun tax restraint, and Wu Han's deathbed advice.
- Reframed Liu Xiu's late-rule personnel profile as combining cautious maternal kin, learned teachers, severe law-enforcement officials, and fiscal restraint before the 44-45 CE illness layer.
- Preserved Hanji 722 as the 44-45 CE prelude to late-rule frontier statecraft: illness, Yin Xing's trusted but restrained role, Guo-branch compensation, Ma Yuan's northern deployment, and hostage retention.
- Preserved Hanji 723-727 as the frontier sequence from protectorate refusal through Southern Xiongnu settlement, Wuling crisis, and corrective field discretion.
- Preserved Hanji 729-732 as the Ban Biao diplomacy, fengshan/chenwei, death, and succession evidence.
