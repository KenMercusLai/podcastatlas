# 森美菜 / Mina Mori

## Overview

[[MoriMina|森美菜]] was a Kyoto art graduate and [[WatamiGroup|和民集团]] employee whose 2008 death after 72 days of employment became a prominent Japanese overwork and labor-accountability case in the current source.

## Current Profile

Mina hoped to use art or work in rural life and was attracted by Watami's agriculture and social-change narrative. Instead, she entered an understaffed late-night restaurant with little practical preparation, a transport arrangement incompatible with closing hours, and assessment-linked study and volunteering. Her conscientiousness and reluctance to abandon a commitment interacted with those conditions; they do not transfer responsibility from the employer to her personality. Her notes, work records, and reported warnings later helped her parents establish a work-related account of her death.

## Key Characteristics

- Trained in Western painting at Kyoto University of the Arts and sought meaningful art- or countryside-related work.
- Was described as conscientious, enduring, self-demanding, and vulnerable to intense pressure.
- Received little kitchen training before being assigned responsibility for high-volume cold dishes.
- Experienced prolonged hours, severe sleep restriction, physical pain, low mood, and slowed functioning.
- Left records that allowed her family to reconstruct burdens hidden by the label of voluntary activity.

## Qualifications

The profile rests on one podcast's reconstruction rather than direct court, medical, or family records. Personality descriptions help explain vulnerability but must not be used to individualize a death whose organizational causes were later legally recognized. Romanization is normalized here as Mina Mori while the source uses the Chinese form 森美菜.

## What Changed

- Created a focused profile separating Mina's aspirations and agency from the employer-created conditions surrounding her death.
