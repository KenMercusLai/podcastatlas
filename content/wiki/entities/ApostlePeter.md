# Peter the Apostle

## Overview

Peter is a leading disciple of [[Jesus]] whom the source reads through his promise of loyalty, three denials after Jesus's arrest, grief, and later tradition of renewed commitment.

## Current Profile

The episode emphasizes Peter's fallibility rather than presenting him as a flawless saint. At the [[LastSupper]], he resists foot washing before accepting Jesus's explanation and insists he will remain loyal; after the arrest, fear leads him to deny Jesus three times. His subsequent weeping makes repentance, not perfection, the center of the portrait. A later *Quo vadis?* tradition is used to extend that arc toward return and martyrdom, but it is not treated as secure first-century history.

## Key Characteristics

- Leading disciple in the Passion narrative.
- Confident declaration of loyalty followed by fear-driven denial.
- Literary figure through whom failure and repentance become compatible with later commitment.
- Subject of later Roman martyrdom tradition whose historicity is separate from the Gospel scene.

## Evidence

- Promise and denial: [[24-dongxifang-de-fanju-zuihou-de-wancan-yu-lanting-yaji-lraekm-bgowhg1wxysfshcwoz5xp]] recounts Peter's pledge, Jesus's prediction, three denials, and grief after the cock crow.
- Later reception: [[24-dongxifang-de-fanju-zuihou-de-wancan-yu-lanting-yaji-lraekm-bgowhg1wxysfshcwoz5xp]] uses the *Quo vadis?* story as a tradition of renewed responsibility.

## Qualifications

This is a one-source literary and reception profile. It does not establish the historical wording of the Passion narrative, harmonize the Gospels, or verify the later Roman encounter and martyrdom tradition.

## What Changed

- Created the profile around fear, denial, repentance, and later return tradition.

## Relationships

- [[Jesus]] - teacher whom Peter follows, denies, and is remembered as serving afterward.
- [[LastSupper]] - setting of Peter's promise and Jesus's prediction.
- [[JudasIscariot]] - contrasting disciple whose betrayal structures the same narrative.
