# 秦穆公 / Duke Mu of Qin

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-12-gongsunyang-qiuzhi-lu-shang-de-la-guanxi-zou-houmen-lvv4-bz8sm9scezci-o-msapkvil]] adds Qin Mu Gong as the explicit prestige ancestor in [[QinXiaogong|秦孝公]]'s recruitment edict. Qin Xiao Gong invokes his hegemonic past to frame Qin's present humiliation as recoverable, turning ancestral memory into a tool for attracting state-strengthening talent.

秦穆公 / Duke Mu of Qin appears in [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-24-shangyang-yue-wo-za-lie-le-lt4yd6pwlezqia5yljg-gankyhut]] through the [[BailiXi|百里奚]] comparison. The episode says he hears of Baili Xi's reputation, avoids making Chu suspicious with an extravagant ransom, and redeems him at the ordinary slave price of five black ram skins.

The source uses Qin Mu Gong as the earlier Qin greatness that later rulers look back toward. After talking with Baili Xi for three days, he appoints him as a great officer; under that remembered ministerial partnership, Qin grows strong enough for Qin Mu Gong to be counted among the Spring and Autumn hegemons.

That precedent matters because [[QinXiaogong|秦孝公]]'s political ideal is framed as restoring Qin Mu Gong's achievement. [[ShangYang|公孙鞅 / 商鞅]] helps Qin Xiao Gong exceed that goal, but the episode contrasts his fate with Baili Xi's more fortunate outsider-minister story.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-25-zhaoliang-dui-shangyang-shuo-ni-bupei-lnqmwjbrc9qomwrdt5brourf-78p]] adds the reason that precedent wounds [[ShangYang|公孙鞅 / 商鞅]] rather than flattering him. In [[ZhaoLiang|赵良]]'s rebuke, Qin Mu Gong's partnership with [[BailiXi|百里奚]] leads to durable public regard: Baili Xi can serve without conspicuous guards and is mourned across Qin after death. Qin Mu Gong therefore anchors the positive contrast for [[PeopleBasedPoliticalSecurity|民心型政治安全]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-112-3-hanfei-yu-yaojia-lunzhan-qinwang-dang-caipan-ljxxlok4uh-ajjirmolybodlspv9]] adds Qin Mu Gong as an ancestral precedent used against biographical purity tests. [[YaoJia|姚贾]]'s defense leans on Qin Mu Gong's recognition of [[BailiXi|百里奚]]: if [[QinShiHuang|嬴政 / 秦始皇]] rejects Yao Jia for low origin and past disgrace, he also undermines a celebrated Qin model of rulerly talent use.
