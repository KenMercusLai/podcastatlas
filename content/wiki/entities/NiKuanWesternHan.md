## Overview

兒宽 / Ni Kuan is a Western Han official whose current profile joins humane local administration and [[TrustBackedFiscalCompliance|trust-backed fiscal cooperation]] to later court roles in resolving authority over an uncertain [[FengshanRitualLegitimacy|fengshan]] procedure and participating in the Taichu calendar project under [[HanWudi|汉武帝]].

## Current Profile

Hanji 348-2 supplies Ni Kuan's local-governance layer. It presents a poor student who works while studying and later becomes an official committed to agriculture, lighter corvee, humane appointments, context-sensitive adjudication, and sharing credit. As left neishi he reportedly adjusts collection to harvest conditions and tolerates arrears. When wartime demand threatens his post, residents gather and deliver grain, which the source interprets as accumulated trust becoming administrative cooperation rather than leniency producing simple noncompliance.

Hanji 359-2 gives Ni Kuan a speaking role during the stalled fengshan consultation. He affirms the rite's importance, concedes that the classics do not preserve an applicable procedure, and says that only a sagely ruler can determine an institution joining ruler, Heaven, and Earth. The answer explains why officials have failed while returning final authorship to Wudi; the host praises its political tact, but the source does not establish Ni Kuan's private motive.

Hanji 371-2 later names Ni Kuan among Sima Qian, Gongsun Qing, Husui, and classical scholars in the Taichu calendar project. That source makes the reform a combined ordering of astronomical cycles, year beginning, dynastic symbols, offices, pitch standards, and ritual. It establishes association with the project but not Ni Kuan's precise technical contribution.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han official serving in Wudi's ritual and calendrical court environment.
- Agriculture-supporting local administrator associated with lighter burdens and humane staffing.
- Tax collector presented as matching pressure to harvest capacity and tolerating temporary arrears.
- Trusted official whose residents reportedly mobilize grain when his position is threatened.
- Frames missing ritual precedent as a limit on ministerial action and a warrant for imperial decision.
- Uses deferential political language that preserves sovereign authority without pretending the classics contain a complete rite.
- Named participant in the multi-official Taichu calendar project.

## Qualifications

All three inputs are short podcast summaries. The current evidence does not supply Ni Kuan's complete biography, exact office sequence, full memorial wording, ritual scholarship, technical calendar work, or private intention. Hanji 348-2 does not document assessment rules, harvest data, the voluntariness of payment, the comparison behind the reported first-place collection, or the exact relation between his education and appointments. The source spellings “倪宽” and “儿宽” are routed to the stable `NiKuanWesternHan` identity, while historical character variants and preferred display form remain open to primary-text normalization.
