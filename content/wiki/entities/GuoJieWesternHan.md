## Overview

郭解 / Guo Jie is a Western Han youxia and local strongman whose current wiki profile is source-scoped to Hanji 282. The episode treats his importance as institutional rather than merely legendary: he could mediate disputes and attract loyalty outside office, but his followers, coercive reach, and association with retaliatory violence made that same capacity dangerous to law and centralized rule.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-282-jianghu-daxia-guojie-weihe-rang-wudi-jidan-lgvcbfgdmw5qclx5l3lr2afixqgl|Hanji 282]] presents Guo Jie through competing judgments. In the episode's reading, [[Shiji|《史记》]] preserves admiration for his personal restraint, low-profile conduct, and willingness to resolve other people's problems, whereas [[HanShu|《汉书》]] emphasizes gatherings of powerful associates and a commoner's exercise of private life-and-death authority.

The episode places those judgments inside [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s centralization. Relocation to Maoling is read as an attempt to weaken influential local households, but Guo Jie's continuing prestige and violence by people acting around him show why physical removal did not automatically dissolve a private network. The host ultimately denies him the ideal title of侠, arguing that retaliatory conduct and local domination fail a standard of tolerance and moral restraint.

## Key Characteristics

- Influential Western Han commoner whose authority rested on reputation, personal ties, and followers rather than formal office.
- Extra-institutional mediator able to settle disputes and supply order where ordinary institutions were perceived as insufficient.
- Politically exposed local strongman because informal authority could survive relocation and compete with centralized command.
- Historiographically divided figure whose social usefulness and personal restraint are weighed against coercion, violence, and private punishment.
- Boundary case between admired youxia memory and the episode's modern organized-crime analogy.

## Qualifications

The source is a compact podcast summary rather than a complete biography or primary-text collation. It does not fully reconstruct Guo Jie's early life, the acts attributed directly to him rather than to followers, the Maoling relocation process, the prosecution, Gongsun Hong's legal reasoning, or the scope of clan punishment. “Organized crime” is a modern analogy used by the host, and neither the favorable Shiji frame nor the critical Han Shu frame should be treated as a neutral or exhaustive verdict. The episode also distinguishes an ideal of侠义 from Guo Jie's conduct, but that normative standard is the host's judgment rather than a settled historical classification.

## What Changed

- Created the profile around the conflict between socially useful mediation and unaccountable private coercion.
- Preserved the Shiji/Han Shu disagreement instead of resolving Guo Jie into either hero or criminal archetype.
- Kept the prosecution and clan punishment source-scoped because the episode defers their full explanation.
