# Zeus

## Overview

Zeus is the Greek sky-father and divine patriarch whose mythic fame, sexual violence, family authority, and Indo-European name history do not make him identical to Roman Jupiter.

## Current Profile

[[59-the-world-cup-of-gods-part-2-glt6474253297]] presents Zeus through a modern tournament defeat by his daughter [[Athena]]. The result opens a contrast between a culturally less attractive patriarchal “alpha god” and the historical importance of a sky-father figure whose name has Indo-European connections. The source's central guardrail is that Roman Jupiter retained specifically Roman civic, ritual, and Capitoline meaning rather than functioning as Zeus under another name.

## Key Characteristics

- Greek patriarch and sky deity associated with an Indo-European sky-father pattern.
- Father of Athena, who is mythically born from his head.
- Marked by repeated sexual predation within mythic narrative.
- Frequently equated with Jupiter, though the source stresses distinct civic and ritual histories.

## Evidence

- Mythic and linguistic profile: [[59-the-world-cup-of-gods-part-2-glt6474253297]] connects Zeus to Athena's birth, patriarchal rule, sexual pursuit, and Indo-European sky-father naming.
- Greek-Roman distinction: [[59-the-world-cup-of-gods-part-2-glt6474253297]] contrasts Zeus with Jupiter's Roman state cult and Capitoline temple.

The episode offers a rapid comparative sketch rather than a full account of Greek cult, local epithets, literature, or Indo-European linguistics.
