# Thomas Edison

## Overview

Thomas Edison appears in this source as the later, publicly dominant claimant in early American cinema history and as the rival around whom the most dramatic theory of [[LouisLePrince|Louis Le Prince]]'s disappearance developed.

## Current Profile

The episode says Edison demonstrated cinematography in May 1891, after Le Prince had recorded surviving films and disappeared. It also describes an 1898 lawsuit in which Edison claimed sole invention of cinematography; Le Prince's son Adolphe testified for the opposing company that his father had invented it first. Edison initially won, although the verdict was later overturned.

Within [[EarlyCinemaInventionPriority]], Edison represents the advantages of public demonstration, continued institutional presence, litigation, and a durable national invention narrative. The episode presents him as jealous and aggressive toward competitors, but it does not establish that he or anyone linked to him caused Le Prince's disappearance.

## Key Characteristics

- American inventor associated with a public cinematography demonstration in 1891.
- Litigant who claimed sole invention of cinematography in the dispute described by the source.
- Historically dominant name against which Le Prince's priority claim was later argued.
- Subject of an unproven foul-play theory about Le Prince's disappearance.

## Evidence

- Demonstration chronology: [[278-france-the-mystery-of-le-prince-glt1101934476]] places Edison's cinematography demonstration in May 1891.
- Patent litigation: [[278-france-the-mystery-of-le-prince-glt1101934476]] describes Edison's 1898 suit, Adolphe Le Prince's defense testimony, the initial judgment, and later reversal.
- Historical memory: [[278-france-the-mystery-of-le-prince-glt1101934476]] argues that Edison's public prominence and Le Prince's absence shaped later origin stories.

## Qualifications

This is a deliberately bounded profile based on one episode about Le Prince, not a general biography or assessment of Edison's work. The episode's characterization of Edison's personality and competitive behavior is source-scoped. It provides no proof that Edison, his employees, or his business interests were involved in Le Prince's disappearance.

## What Changed

- Established a bounded canonical Edison profile for the early-cinema branch.
- Distinguished documented demonstration and litigation from an unproven disappearance theory.
- Connected public and legal visibility to later historical credit.
