## Overview

王衍 / Wang Yan is presented by the supplied episode as the last ruler of [[FormerShu|前蜀]] and the earlier user of the era name 乾德. His importance in the current wiki is narrow: his chronology becomes the forgotten predecessor that embarrasses [[ZhaoKuangyin|赵匡胤]] when the Northern Song court later selects the same era name.

## Current Profile

The episode does not provide a general biography of Wang Yan. It uses him as evidence that official names carry political memory even after a regime disappears. A reported mirror marked “乾德四年” makes the earlier Former Shu era visible inside the Song palace and turns Wang Yan's otherwise distant reign into a legitimacy problem for Zhao Kuangyin's court.

## Key Characteristics

- Last ruler of Former Shu in the episode's account.
- Earlier user of the 乾德 era name later selected by the Northern Song court.
- Posthumous chronological presence in the reported mirror inscription.
- Example of how forgotten predecessor regimes can disrupt a successor court's symbolic language.

## Qualifications

- This profile is bounded to one compact podcast summary and does not reconstruct Wang Yan's reign, deposition, death, family, government, or the full chronology of Former Shu.
- The exact dates of his 乾德 era and the authenticity and provenance of the mirror inscription require independent verification.
- The episode's claim that Zhao Kuangyin changed to 开宝 because of this discovery is preserved as source-attributed, not settled causation.
