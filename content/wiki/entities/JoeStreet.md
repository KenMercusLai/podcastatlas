## Overview

Joe Street is a historian who interprets films about technology founders through cultural history, industrial competition, and changing public attitudes toward Silicon Valley.

## Current Profile

In the current evidence, Street treats Hollywood's renewed attention to [[MarkZuckerberg]], [[SamAltman]], [[ElizabethHolmes]], and [[ElonMusk]] as a possible cultural moment rather than a settled genre boundary. His analysis joins two levels: films convert diffuse concern about technology into character-centered stories, while technology companies' participation in production and distribution can shape the conditions under which those stories circulate.

Street also uses historical analogy and counter-reading. He compares the emerging cycle with 1970s paranoid thrillers shaped by Watergate and surveillance anxiety, while reading *The Internship* against its intended comic surface as a story about insecure labor and dependence on [[Google]]'s advertising system.

## Key Characteristics

- Connects film narratives to public anxiety about concentrated technology power.
- Treats founder-centered storytelling as a simplification of institutional and corporate complexity.
- Distinguishes Hollywood's cultural criticism from its industrial rivalry with technology companies.
- Uses historical genre cycles as qualified analogies rather than completed classifications.
- Reads unintended social implications alongside a film's apparent tone or purpose.

- Industrial analysis: [[tech-20261123-1123-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261123-1123-mp-tech-pod-128|Hollywood brings Big Tech's drama to the big screen]] records his concern that technology companies now compete in production and influence distribution routes for films that may criticize them.
- Historical and interpretive method: [[tech-20261123-1123-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261123-1123-mp-tech-pod-128|Hollywood brings Big Tech's drama to the big screen]] records his comparison with 1970s paranoid thrillers and his dystopian counter-reading of *The Internship*.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to one short interview. It does not establish that the announced films form a coherent genre cycle, independently verify their distribution problems, or show that technology-company control caused any particular release outcome.
