# Ursúa-Aguirre Expedition

## Overview

The Ursúa-Aguirre expedition was a 1560-1561 Spanish venture from Peru into the Amazon in search of [[ElDorado]] that became a mutiny, an anti-royal rebellion under [[LopeDeAguirre]], and a failed attempt to prepare an invasion of Peru from the Atlantic.

## Current Profile

The expedition began as both conquest venture and political disposal mechanism: the colonial government could seek a golden kingdom while sending unstable soldiers away from Peru. Vessel failure, hunger, rain, weak leadership, and the one-way river journey then narrowed the group's choices and made [[PedroDeUrsua|Pedro de Ursua]] vulnerable to overthrow.

After Ursua's murder, the expedition rapidly crossed legitimacy thresholds. Loyalist slogans gave way to acknowledgment of treason, the elevation of Fernando de Guzman, renunciation of [[PhilipII|Philip II]], Aguirre's seizure of command, and attacks on royal administration at Margarita. It reached the Atlantic but collapsed in Venezuela when amnesty made desertion safer than continued loyalty.

## Key Characteristics

- Crown-authorized conquest mission with a secondary function of removing dangerous soldiers from Peru.
- Logistically fragile river expedition dependent on defective vessels, rafts, uncertain provisioning, and downstream movement.
- Mutinous coalition that moved from claimed loyalism to explicit rejection of royal sovereignty.
- Coercive mobile regime held together through oaths, surveillance, purges, and control of information.
- Atlantic-reaching expedition whose geographic achievement did not make its political strategy viable.
- Historiographically difficult event reconstructed largely through interested survivors and hostile accounts.

- Legitimacy transformation: [[543-death-in-the-amazon-aguirre-the-wrath-of-god-glt3694360248]] traces the sequence from Ursua's murder through Guzman's elevation to Aguirre's renunciation of Philip II.
- Coercive organization: [[543-death-in-the-amazon-aguirre-the-wrath-of-god-glt3694360248]] describes purges, private-conversation bans, armed guards, and abandonment of guides and porters.
- Atlantic and political outcome: [[543-death-in-the-amazon-aguirre-the-wrath-of-god-glt3694360248]] follows the force to Margarita and Barquisimeto, where royal amnesty accelerates collapse.

## Qualifications

The expedition's famous horror narrative should not obscure Indigenous labor, guides, and casualties or turn Aguirre into the only responsible participant. Force sizes, exact losses, dialogue, atrocity detail, and motives remain dependent on the source tradition summarized by the episode.

## What Changed

- Created an event profile linking logistical failure, legitimacy change, coercive organization, and collapse under amnesty.
