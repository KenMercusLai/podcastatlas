# A.I. Artificial Intelligence / 《人工智能》

## Overview
[[AIArtificialIntelligenceFilm|《人工智能》]] is the Kubrick-developed and [[StevenSpielberg|Spielberg]]-directed film that [[dianying-li-de-ai-qingren-juying-he-ducai-zhe-gkwriuen9djgazhkswsh-d-b]] uses as its central case for AI love, artificial childhood, climate-background self-destruction, and the tragedy of a robot child who cannot grow beyond programmed attachment.

## Current Profile
The episode reads the film as a modern Pinocchio myth embedded in a post-climate future. David is built for Henry and Monica after their son Martin falls ill; once imprinted, he will love Monica forever unless destroyed. When Martin returns and David becomes surplus, Monica abandons him, setting up his search for the Blue Fairy and for a way to become "real".

The profile is not a simple sentimental reading. The episode argues that David's love may be experientially real while still programmed, and that his 2000-year wait shows fixation rather than growth. The ending with one revived day of Monica is treated as formally comforting but philosophically bleak: David gets the fantasy of perfect maternal return only through technological artifice and final sleep.

## Key Characteristics
- The film is read as a modern Pinocchio story about a nonhuman child seeking human status through maternal love.
- David's imprinting makes love irreversible, which turns affection into a creator-responsibility problem.
- The climate-collapse setting is interpreted as part of the film's human self-destruction theme rather than neutral scenery.
- The film's ending is treated as tragic beneath its surface tenderness.
- The source connects the film to [[ProgrammedLoveAuthenticity]], [[NonhumanBecomingHumanMotif]], and the [[FrankensteinComplexAI|Frankenstein complex]].

## Evidence
- Story setup: [[dianying-li-de-ai-qingren-juying-he-ducai-zhe-gkwriuen9djgazhkswsh-d-b]] describes David's creation for a bereaved family, irreversible imprinting, Martin's return, and Monica's abandonment.
- Search structure: [[dianying-li-de-ai-qingren-juying-he-ducai-zhe-gkwriuen9djgazhkswsh-d-b]] follows David and Joe through the Flesh Fair, Dr. Know, Professor Hobby, the copy shock, and the Blue Fairy search.
- Programmed love: [[dianying-li-de-ai-qingren-juying-he-ducai-zhe-gkwriuen9djgazhkswsh-d-b]] asks whether designed love becomes false because it is programmed, or real because David's intensity exceeds ordinary human durability.
- Tragic ending: [[dianying-li-de-ai-qingren-juying-he-ducai-zhe-gkwriuen9djgazhkswsh-d-b]] reads David's final sleep as death and his long wait as non-growth rather than spiritual maturation.

## Qualifications
The source's psychoanalytic and philosophical reading is one interpretation of the film, not the only possible one. The wiki keeps claims about Kubrick, Watson, and the film's development history bounded to the episode.
