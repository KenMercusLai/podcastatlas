# Liane Young

## Overview
Liane Young is the psychologist and neuroscientist interviewed by [[HiddenBrain]] about theory of mind, intention-sensitive moral judgment, and systematic errors in reading other people's motives.

## Current Profile
In the bounded episode evidence, Young connects ordinary perspective-taking to moral evaluation. People track what another person believes, wants, and intends in order to distinguish an accident from attempted harm, coordinate conversation, follow stories, and assess danger. Her experimental examples show that innocent intention can mitigate accidental harm and malicious intention can matter even when an attempt fails, while catastrophic outcomes, taboo, political identity, and group loyalty can suppress or distort that distinction.

Young's practical recommendation is a disciplined benefit of the doubt under ambiguity: pause, consider situational pressures, and ask what the other person may or may not be trying to do. The episode does not make that a universal innocence presumption; high-stakes uncertainty and credible danger still require attention to action, context, and safety.

## Key Characteristics
- Studies how people represent beliefs and intentions in social and moral judgment.
- Uses controlled accident-versus-attempt scenarios to separate outcome from intent.
- Connects brain evidence with developmental, cultural, political, and interpersonal influences.
- Treats motive-reading as necessary but fallible rather than as direct access to another mind.
