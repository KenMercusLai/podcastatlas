# First Anglo-Afghan War

## Overview
The First Anglo-Afghan War was the 1839-1842 intervention in which the [[EastIndiaCompany|East India Company]] removed [[DostMuhammad|Dost Muhammad]], restored [[ShahShuja|Shah Shuja]], occupied Kabul, and then suffered a catastrophic retreat before Dost Muhammad returned to power.

## Current Profile
[[88-the-first-anglo-afghan-war-glt5156371854]] presents the war as a preventable interaction among [[GreatGame|Great Game]] anxiety, bureaucratic distortion, regime-change politics, logistical excess, and local resistance. The central irony is that [[AlexanderBurnes|Alexander Burnes]] believed Dost Muhammad could be drawn into a British alliance, yet policy treated Russian contact as a reason for invasion.

The military disaster developed after easy initial victories. Occupation complacency, an exposed cantonment, divided commanders, captured artillery, winter closure, and prepared ambushes turned withdrawal into destruction. The eventual restoration of Dost Muhammad makes the war a particularly stark [[ForeignBackedRulerLegitimacyTrap]].

## Key Characteristics
- Began from perceived Russian pressure filtered through imperial intelligence and rivalry.
- Used regime change to restore Shah Shuja despite doubts about the necessity of invasion.
- Combined a large multinational army with an impractical luxury-heavy baggage train and weak mapping.
- Shifted from rapid territorial entry to exposed occupation and insurgent pressure.
- Culminated in the January 1842 retreat from Kabul and the destruction of most of the column.
- Ended with Dost Muhammad restored and politically strengthened by resistance prestige.

## Evidence
- Policy origin: [[88-the-first-anglo-afghan-war-glt5156371854]] links Russian agents, British intelligence fear, and Burnes's sidelined diplomacy to the decision to invade.
- Occupation failure: [[88-the-first-anglo-afghan-war-glt5156371854]] describes the vulnerable cantonment, captured guns, winter isolation, and divided command.
- Retreat: [[88-the-first-anglo-afghan-war-glt5156371854]] follows the column through Jigdallik and Gandamak while qualifying the sole-survivor legend.
- Political outcome: [[88-the-first-anglo-afghan-war-glt5156371854]] says Dost Muhammad returned after agreeing not to admit Russian influence.

## Qualifications
The source is a narrative podcast centered on Dalrymple's interpretation, not a comprehensive military or Afghan social history. Exact troop and survivor totals, the weight of particular personal scandals, and the direct causal force of Russian activity remain source-scoped.

## What Changed
- Established the war as a linked case of threat inflation, foreign-backed rule, occupation failure, and disaster memory.

## Relationships
- [[Afghanistan]] - country and political society in which the intervention occurred.
- [[EastIndiaCompany]] - invading institution and occupation authority.
- [[DostMuhammad]] - displaced ruler restored after the war.
- [[ShahShuja]] - restored ruler whose dependence created a legitimacy problem.
- [[GreatGame]] - imperial rivalry framing the intervention.
- [[OccupationCommandFailure]] - operational pattern that turned occupation into catastrophe.
