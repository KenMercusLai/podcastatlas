# Cicely Gemmell

## Overview

Cicely “Chips” Gemmell was [[WinstonChurchill]]'s personal secretary at [[Chartwell]] from 1947 to 1951 and later a magazine secretary in New York.

## Current Profile

The source presents Gemmell as both a skilled administrative worker and a witness to the unequal intimacy of elite personal service. She organized Churchill's archive largely alone, took dictation, and worked inside a household remembered for laughter, but also cleaned oil-paint brushes, walked his poodle, removed his socks, and was addressed only as “Miss.” Her experience makes proximity to a celebrated statesman visible from below without reducing four years of work to either glamour or humiliation.

Gemmell left when Churchill returned to the premiership because she did not want the added responsibility. After briefly working for Clementine Churchill, she moved to New York in 1953, enjoyed jazz clubs, married, and continued secretarial work for *Life* and Time Inc. She kept Churchill's melancholy portrait of her for the rest of her life.

## Key Characteristics

- Combined demanding archival and secretarial responsibility with personal household service.
- Remembered Chartwell as humorous and companionable despite status and task inequality.
- Exercised agency by leaving before Churchill's return to high office increased the role's burden.
- Built a later life and career in New York beyond the Churchill household.

## Qualifications

The profile rests on one short podcast segment drawing on recollection. It does not reconstruct Gemmell's complete archive work, compensation, employment terms, marriage, magazine career, or later life. The connection between her roughened hands and the portrait's melancholy expression is an episode-level interpretation, while Churchill's final failure to recognize her should not alone define their entire working relationship.
