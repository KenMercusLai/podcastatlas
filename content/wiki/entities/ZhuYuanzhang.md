## Overview

朱元璋 / Zhu Yuanzhang enters the wiki through Hanji 400-2 as the founding Ming ruler used to show how status hierarchy can be enforced through household registration, hereditary occupation, clothing, residence, utensils, and other visible routines.

## Current Profile

The episode presents Zhu Yuanzhang as a ruler who treats social classification as state security. His Ming order is not described only through office hierarchy or fiscal administration; it reaches into who may move, what work a household inherits, which fabrics different occupations may wear, what building forms commoners may use, which boots are permitted in cold northern regions, and how unmarried women and servants should dress or arrange hair.

The profile is therefore a statecraft case rather than a complete biography. Zhu's aim, as the source reconstructs it, is to make the population legible and hierarchy visible enough to distinguish compliant subjects from disorderly people. The episode criticizes the system's anti-merchant discrimination and class-fixing effects while explaining why Zhu could view such details as tied to imperial stability.

## Key Characteristics

- Founding Ming ruler used by the episode as a strong case of social-status fixation.
- Treats household registration, inherited occupation, and restricted migration as tools of order.
- Regulates dress, colors, motifs, fabrics, boots, hats, housing, vessels, and hairstyles as visible status codes.
- Degrades merchant status through fabric and household restrictions in the episode's account.
- Understands ritual clarity and upper-lower distinction as political security rather than mere etiquette.

## Qualifications

This page currently rests on one short popular-history episode and should not be read as a full assessment of Zhu Yuanzhang's reign. Exact Ming legal provisions, regional exceptions, enforcement consistency, and long-run social effects remain source-scoped.
