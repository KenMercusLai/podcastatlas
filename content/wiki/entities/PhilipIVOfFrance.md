# Philip IV of France

## Overview

Philip IV of France appears as the hard Capetian ruler at the height of French royal power whose sons' failure to leave surviving male heirs later destabilizes the succession and opens the route to Valois rule.

## Current Profile

The episode uses Philip IV as both peak and prelude. He represents [[France]]'s early-14th-century confidence: defeating Flemish resistance after the Golden Spurs, extracting reparations, taking Lille, expelling Jews, destroying the Knights Templar, and pressuring Pope Boniface VIII in the path toward Avignon.

The same dynastic success then breaks. Philip's three sons succeed one another rapidly and none leaves a surviving son. That failure ends the direct [[CapetianDynasty|Capetian]] male line and makes [[EdwardIIIOfEngland|Edward III]]'s claim through [[IsabellaOfFrance|Isabella]] politically possible but unacceptable to the French establishment.

## Key Characteristics

- Capetian king ruling France from 1285 to 1314 in the episode's account.
- Presented as a hard ruler at the peak of French monarchical confidence.
- Expands or consolidates royal power through Flanders, Lille, fiscal pressure, and church conflict.
- Father of Isabella, through whom Edward III claims France.
- Father of three sons whose failure to leave surviving sons creates succession rupture.
- Prewar figure whose reign makes later French instability look like a fall from strength.
