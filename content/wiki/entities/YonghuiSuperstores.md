# 永辉超市 / Yonghui Superstores

## Overview
永辉超市 / Yonghui Superstores is a Chinese supermarket chain presented through [[YeGuofu|叶国富]]'s acquisition rationale and proposed transformation after he became its largest shareholder.

## Current Profile
The source frames Yonghui as a large incumbent whose turnaround requires changing the supermarket's economic center. Instead of maximizing supplier fees and shelf access, the new model is supposed to select products for customers, rebuild stores and service, simplify the supplier base, develop private label, and accept that supply-chain change takes longer than visible renovation.

The desired position is neither a direct copy of [[SamsClub|Sam's Club]] nor [[Costco]]. Ye describes a non-membership, smaller-package, fresher, mainstream Chinese household format. [[PangDongLai|胖东来]] supplies the immediate service-and-product benchmark and practical reform support, while global warehouse clubs supply private-label and quality comparisons. Whether this combination can scale beyond remodeled examples remains unresolved.

## Key Characteristics
- Large supermarket incumbent undergoing store, management, assortment, and supply-chain reform.
- Seeks to move from supplier-fee and shelf-rental economics toward customer-facing product ownership.
- Uses Pang Dong Lai-assisted remodels as operating prototypes rather than treating signage changes as transformation.
- Reduces supplier sprawl and raises the intended role of private label.
- Targets Chinese household fit through smaller packs, freshness, frequent purchase, and no membership fee.
- Faces a multi-year execution test in supply chain, store consistency, and customer trust.

## Evidence
- Acquisition rationale and prototype: [[ltdplf6zr1agmhhbopqptutvr-ng-ltdplf6zr1agmhhbopqptutvr-ng]] says Pang Dong Lai-assisted store results convinced Ye that a quality-supermarket route was possible.
- Store and organizational reform: [[ltdplf6zr1agmhhbopqptutvr-ng-ltdplf6zr1agmhhbopqptutvr-ng]] describes month-long closures, physical rebuilding, leadership changes, store closures, and continuing external support.
- Supplier and product reform: [[ltdplf6zr1agmhhbopqptutvr-ng-ltdplf6zr1agmhhbopqptutvr-ng]] reports supplier reduction, a private-label target, and a shift from back-end fees toward customer-needed products.
- Format differentiation: [[ltdplf6zr1agmhhbopqptutvr-ng-ltdplf6zr1agmhhbopqptutvr-ng]] distinguishes the proposed format from membership clubs through small packs, freshness, and mainstream household access.

## Qualifications
The evidence is Ye Guofu's account of an unfinished turnaround. Reported prototype growth, supplier reductions, store closures, staffing choices, private-label targets, and reform timelines are not independently verified here. Remodeled stores can improve traffic before supply-chain economics, quality consistency, labor practice, and network-wide profitability are proven.

## What Changed
- Created Yonghui's profile as an unfinished product-led supermarket transformation rather than a completed quality-retail success.
