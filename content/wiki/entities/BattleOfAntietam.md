# Battle of Antietam

## Overview

The Battle of Antietam was the September 1862 battle that ended [[RobertELee|Robert E. Lee]]'s Maryland campaign and gave [[AbrahamLincoln|Abraham Lincoln]] the political opening to announce the preliminary [[EmancipationProclamation|Emancipation Proclamation]].

## Current Profile

[[201-american-civil-war-outbreak-part-2-glt4100428000]] presents Antietam as the bloodiest single day in American history. Lost Confederate orders gave [[GeorgeMcClellan|George McClellan]] an intelligence advantage, but the battle remained tactically inconclusive; its strategic and political importance came from Lee's retreat into Virginia and Lincoln's ability to announce emancipation after something that could be represented as a Union success.

Antietam therefore links battlefield result to war aims. It did not destroy Lee's army, but it checked an invasion and helped turn a war publicly centered on Union preservation toward an explicit military attack on slavery in rebellious territory.

## Key Characteristics

- September 1862 battle during Lee's Maryland campaign.
- Extremely costly one-day engagement described by the episode as a tactical draw.
- Battle shaped by Union possession of lost Confederate orders.
- Strategic check that sent Lee back into Virginia.
- Political opening for the preliminary Emancipation Proclamation.

## Evidence

### Intelligence and battle result

- [[201-american-civil-war-outbreak-part-2-glt4100428000]] says lost Confederate plans reached Union hands, giving McClellan an advantage without producing a clean battlefield destruction of Lee's army.

### Political consequence

- [[201-american-civil-war-outbreak-part-2-glt4100428000]] connects Lee's retreat to Lincoln's decision to publish a proclamation already drafted before the battle.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to one retrospective podcast summary rather than a tactical reconstruction. The identity of the person who found or transmitted the lost orders, exact casualty comparisons, command decisions, the meaning of “draw,” and the degree to which Lincoln required a victory remain source-scoped. Political consequence does not convert the battle into an unqualified tactical Union victory.

## What Changed

- Established Antietam as a battlefield check whose political effect exceeded its tactical decisiveness.
