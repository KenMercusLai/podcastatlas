# 鲁肃 / Lu Su

## Overview
鲁肃 / Lu Su is a future Eastern Wu adviser whose current wiki profile runs from leaving [[YuanShu|袁术]]'s failing appointment path with [[ZhouYuLateHan|周瑜]], through presenting [[SunQuan|孙权]] with the [[TaShangCeBipartiteStrategy|榻上策二分天下]], to privately reframing the 208 CE surrender debate around Sun Quan's ruler-specific risk.

## Current Profile
Lu Su first enters the wiki as an alignment marker: capable people are not only judging Yuan Shu negatively, but moving households and futures toward Sun Ce's Jiangdong base. Hanji 1052 turns him into a strategic actor. It depicts him as wealthy, organized, and militarily prepared, then shows Zhou Yu recommending him to Sun Quan.

In Sun Quan's first rulerly conversations, Lu Su refuses a simple Han-restoration premise. He argues that the Han house cannot be revived and Cao Cao cannot be quickly destroyed, so Sun Quan should hold Jiangdong, watch for northern distraction, take Huang Zu and Liu Biao's line, control the Yangtze, and only then pursue imperial enterprise. The source frames this as an earlier two-part天下 plan that later events qualify.

Hanji 1101 shows the same strategic habit under immediate coercion. Lu Su remains silent during the public surrender debate, follows Sun Quan out, and privately separates the ministers' options from the ruler's. He can plausibly survive a change of master and seek office under Cao Cao; Sun Quan, as the independent rival, cannot assume equivalent safety. This is [[RoleAsymmetricSurrenderIncentives|身份不对称的投降激励]] and an instance of [[InterestReframingPersuasion|利益重构式说服]]: Lu Su changes whose downside the decision must optimize.

## Key Characteristics
- Rejects Yuan Shu's appointment path and relocates with Zhou Yu toward Sun Ce's Jiangdong base.
- Has independent resources rather than appearing only as a dependent client.
- Turns wealth into organization by selling assets, recruiting men, and training soldiers.
- Becomes visible to Sun Quan through Zhou Yu's recommendation.
- Offers a strategic program that treats Han restoration as no longer practical and centers Jiangdong plus the Yangtze.
- Is valued by Sun Quan despite Zhang Zhao's criticism that he is young and careless.
- Reframes surrender by distinguishing a portable ministerial career from the ruler's non-portable authority and personal exposure.

## Qualifications
The current Lu Su profile is still source-bounded. Hanji 1052 gives a strong strategic entrance, but it is not a full career synthesis. Later events qualify his two-part天下 plan, so the page preserves it as early strategic advice rather than a final map of the period. Hanji 1101 ends immediately after Lu Su asks what surrender would mean for Sun Quan; it does not contain his complete answer, operational recommendation, or Sun Quan's decision.

## What Changed
- Extended Lu Su from long-range Jiangdong strategist into a crisis persuader during Cao Cao's 208 CE pressure.
- Added the minister-versus-ruler surrender distinction as the core of his private intervention.
- Linked his intervention to role-asymmetric incentives and interest reframing without filling in the transcript's missing conclusion.
