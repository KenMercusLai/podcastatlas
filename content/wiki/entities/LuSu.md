# 鲁肃 / Lu Su

## Overview
鲁肃 / Lu Su is a Jiangdong adviser whose current profile joins resource-backed independence, long-range Yangtze strategy, exploratory diplomacy, post-Changban alliance brokerage, and role-sensitive resistance to surrender.

## Current Profile
Lu Su first appears as someone willing to move household and future away from [[YuanShu|袁术]]'s failing path toward Jiangdong. Hanji 1052 shows that this choice rests on an independent base: he is wealthy, organizes followers, trains men, and reaches [[SunQuan|孙权]] through [[ZhouYuLateHan|周瑜]]'s recommendation. His [[TaShangCeBipartiteStrategy|榻上策二分天下]] rejects immediate Han restoration, centers Jiangdong and the Yangtze, and sequences expansion through Huang Zu and Liu Biao.

Hanji 1099 makes Lu Su an alliance broker after Changban. He does not ask defeated [[LiuBei|刘备]] to admit dependence. He contrasts Sun Quan's capacity with [[WuJuLateHan|吴巨]]'s weakness, presents cooperation as shared enterprise, and tells [[ZhugeLiang|诸葛亮]] that he is [[ZhugeJin|诸葛瑾]]'s friend. The pitch combines material comparison, dignity protection, and relationship signaling.

Hanji 1096 backfills the exploratory phase before that pitch. Lu Su turns Liu Biao's death and the divided Jingzhou succession into a multi-purpose mission: travel under cover of mourning, gather intelligence, test Liu Bei and Liu Biao's heirs as partners, organize resistance to Cao Cao, and preserve Jiangdong's territorial interest. Sun Quan's alleged fallback order if Liu Bei collapses means Lu Su's mission carries both coalition-building and contingency hedging.

Hanji 1101 shows the same attention to audience-specific incentives under Cao Cao's threat. Lu Su privately distinguishes the portable career of an ordinary minister from Sun Quan's non-portable ruler status. Hanji 1102 completes the comparison: Lu Su makes his own plausible post-surrender office the foil for Sun Quan's likely loss of power, surveillance, and mortal danger, then immediately recommends recalling Zhou Yu so that the personal-risk insight can be joined to military analysis. His counsel therefore spans both sides of coalition choice: he can make alliance acceptable to a weakened external actor and make surrender unacceptable to an exposed ruler.

## Key Characteristics
- Rejects a failing patron path and relocates resources toward a more viable political base.
- Converts private wealth into organized people and military preparedness.
- Offers long-range strategy grounded in Jiangdong and Yangtze control, using diplomatic cover to combine intelligence gathering, partner testing, and coalition design.
- Brokers alliance by pairing material comparison with face-preserving language.
- Uses personal networks as diplomatic infrastructure through the Zhuge Jin connection.
- Reframes decisions around the specific exposure of the person who must choose.
- Couples persuasive diagnosis with the next institutional step by bringing the appropriate military specialist into the decision.

## Qualifications
The current profile is bounded to six popular-history episodes rather than a complete career. The two-part天下 plan is early strategic advice, not a final prediction. Hanji 1096's precise mission wording and fallback order, Hanji 1099's detailed rhetorical intent, and Hanji 1102's dialogue remain source-presented rather than verbatim documentation. The coalition's contingency does not erase later costly coordination.

## What Changed
- The role-asymmetry argument is now complete: Lu Su specifies both his own portable career and Sun Quan's non-portable danger.
- His profile now links diagnosis to staffing by making Zhou Yu's recall the next step.
- His crisis role extends from private persuasion into the forward planning structure.
- The pre-Changban layer now shows intelligence gathering and contingency design before direct alliance brokerage.
