## Overview

霍去病 / Huo Qubing is synthesized as a victorious Western Han commander whose campaigns have institutional, personnel, doctrinal, and family afterlives, but whose record also carries command-welfare and accountability failures. A source-scoped origin layer attributes his exceptional opportunity to Wei-family mobility, riding and archery talent, palace proximity, and [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s personal cultivation, then interprets his promotion beside [[WeiQingHanGeneral|卫青]] as [[ImperialMilitaryCommandCounterbalancing|皇权下的军事统帅制衡]]. The [[FirstHexiCampaignWesternHan|第一次河西之战]] now runs from light cavalry, penetration, local separation, campaign replenishment, and conditional surrender through sustained pursuit and a claimed decisive engagement near Gaolan Mountain; exact routes, losses, battle mechanics, trophy meaning, and political effects remain episode-attributed. In the [[SecondHexiCampaignWesternHan|第二次河西之战]], he plans to bypass the corridor's narrow frontal approach, reach the enemy rear, and reunite with [[GongsunAoWesternHan|公孙敖]] near Juyan; when Gongsun misses the rendezvous, Huo continues and the source credits his force with major disruption of the western Xiongnu field. The later Hexi surrender layer follows Huo's handling of [[HunyeKingWesternHan|浑邪王]] after the coalition with [[XiutuKingWesternHan|休屠王]] fractures: visible force, selective reward, delegated suppression, leader separation, and rapid relocation stabilize a still-reversible armed submission. The [[MobeiBattleWesternHan|漠北之战]] sources add his eastern deep-cavalry operation against the Xiongnu Left Wise King, the “封狼居胥” rites, crippling remount loss, and the postwar conversion of victory into rising patronage power. Other branches preserve his upstream role in Wuhuan relocation, Zhao Ponu's rise, doctrine adaptation, and Li Gan's battlefield merit, but also report food-distribution failure, a protected killing of Li Gan, and an early death whose specific cause remains unknown.

## Current Profile

The earliest current origin and patronage layer identifies Wei Shaoer as Huo's mother and Huo Zhongru as the absent father he did not know while young, then says Wei Zifu's rise moved Huo into an imperial-affinal network. It attributes Wudi's attention to Huo's riding, archery, and physical ability and presents long palace proximity as the basis for unusual trust and informal military formation. “Direct disciple,” repeated strategy discussions, and specific transmission of battlefield skill remain the host's explanatory reconstruction rather than a documented curriculum. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-326-2-shenjiang-huoqubing-shi-shui-jiao-chulai-de-lut_0gh7nistf1td8pb_1nmzv6mv|Hanji 326-2]]

The same source interprets Huo's high promotion as a second command center beside Wei Qing. That reading gives Huo's rise an institutional function: Wudi could preserve military capacity while avoiding dependence on one general. Kinship does not make their staffs and beneficiaries identical, but the episode does not prove hostile Wei and Huo factions, inevitable conflict, Pingyang Princess's alleged dislike, or Wudi's private appointment motive. Talent, campaign results, kin position, and favor remain complementary explanations.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-307-1-buxuewushu-de-huoqubing-ping-shenme-hengsao-xiongnu-lus99k4vphqaj8xofkm0rqi1nga9|Hanji 307-1]] supplies the first-Hexi layer. It says Huo leads ten thousand cavalry from Longxi, crosses the Yellow River, attacks the Suopu group, and uses light movement, high tempo, penetration, separation, local replenishment, and conditional surrender to keep western groups from organizing together. The source's strongest addition is that operational speed and political disaggregation reinforce each other: protecting the people and property of submitting groups can reduce immediate resistance and isolate stronger centers. The claimed seven-thousand loss, six-day and thousand-li movement, defeated-polity count, route, supply mechanism, promises, and pre-campaign consultation with Zhang Qian remain episode-attributed.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-307-2-huoqubing-gujun-yizhan-zhanshen-neng-you-duo-biantai-ll-egjuu-gn-s-_mazrbbarfmb02|Hanji 307-2]] extends that operation through north-south pursuit near Yanzhi and a closing fight near Gaolan Mountain. It presents tempo as a way to narrow the opponents' geographic choices until Hunye King and Xiutu King turn to battle, then attributes victory to mounted collision, close combat, and a Han weapons advantage. The episode reports 8,900 dead from 13,000 opponents, rulers and officials killed or captured, seizure of a Xiutu sacrificial golden figure, a 2,200-household reward, and Huo's construction of a Yellow River fort called Jincheng. The inner-state narration, route, figures, names, weapons claim, trophy meaning, reward, and fort identity remain source-scoped; the ten-thousand Han force also sits unreconciled beside Hanji 307-1's claimed seven-thousand losses.

The second-Hexi planning frame in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-308-bei-tangshi-chui-cheng-shen-de-ta-jing-shi-ge-luchi-lsjtzqsil36lxejij8gazn58qeeb|Hanji 308]] treats the narrow corridor as a frontal-attack trap that permits withdrawal and pursuit but not destruction of the enemy main force. Huo instead proposes a long movement along the desert edge to reach the rear, while Gongsun Ao takes a northern route and both forces aim to reunite near Juyan's water. This strengthens the existing high-tempo cavalry profile through operational geometry and water-dependent rendezvous, but the episode defers the battle result and does not independently establish its distances, route, conditions, force totals, or authorship of the plan.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-309-1-huoqubing-duohui-hexi-zoulang-zhongguo-bixu-tongyi-llcwkkdgjpx2zjeoyvhdjln3nqik|Hanji 309-1]] supplies that deferred result. After Gongsun Ao becomes lost and misses the reunion, Huo continues rather than withdrawing, reaches the opposing rear, accepts some rulers' surrender, and defeats resisting groups with officers including [[ZhaoPonuWesternHan|赵破奴]]. This supports a profile of command resilience under a failed supporting movement, but the route, surprise mechanics, two-thousand-li distance, casualty and captive totals, subordinate actions, and claims of fatal or permanent Xiongnu damage remain episode-scoped. Continued Xiongnu raids on Dai and Yanmen prevent the victory from being read as the end of the wider war.

The crisis-command layer begins before Huo reaches the surrendering force. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-312-xianghan-de-xiongnu-wang-weihe-turan-neidou-lkbyh1fglhog0d9voaxitwu59eic|Hanji 312]] says Wudi and most court officials suspect a feint because the prospective submission is much larger than the receiving force; Huo nevertheless accepts the assignment with a reported ten thousand troops, including surrendered Xiongnu personnel. The episode then attributes the coalition's collapse to divergent motives and a preemptive attack by Hunye King, but its private plans, bridge schedule, battle movements, and totals remain dramatized or source-scoped.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-313-1-duchuang-huxue-huoqubing-shouxiang-hunyewang-lij9eecrq-igxpvftkey5shu8395|Hanji 313-1]] continues from that fracture. Huo does not treat a verbal submission as sufficient: he establishes an immediate surrender-or-resistance boundary, promises rank to compliant leaders, requires Hunye King to guide the operation and mobilize affiliated forces against resisters, sends the ruler ahead, and moves the remaining cavalry south across the Yellow River. Together the episodes support [[CrisisSurrenderStabilization|危机受降稳控]] while leaving the dramatic dialogue, youth-based cultural explanation, exact totals, and loss claims source-scoped.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-402-2-shudiwushu-de-kuli-duzhou-weihe-zuizhong-deyi-shanzhong-lqrunv51se0w7_2fi2ifs_f7zub4|Hanji 402-2]] uses Huo's campaign as the hinge between anti-[[Xiongnu|匈奴]] warfare and northeastern frontier administration: after Xiongnu's left territory is broken, Wuhuan groups are moved near five commanderies and tasked with watching Xiongnu under Han supervision. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-376-2-cong-liulanger-nixi-cheng-jiangjun-jiemi-hanjiang-zhaoponu-lszmnylwk8zxpjhelkd1gd6jsfsd|Hanji 376-2]] adds a personnel legacy by placing Zhao Ponu's early victories in Huo's successful military phase.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-328-2-gudai-zuqiu-ye-fengkuang-huoqubing-jing-ye-chimi-lh5o5cgy9kg7kd_vl-wvvetsmvsn|Hanji 328-2]] complicates that success with a command-style judgment. It says campaign kitchen carts retained food while nearby troops went hungry, Huo ordered surplus provisions discarded, and a temporary cuju ground was prepared even when the army was exhausted and short of supplies. Cuju may have provided recreation, conditioning, or morale support, but the episode does not show that it repaired the provisioning failure; its stronger synthesis is that organized military activity and care for soldiers must be judged separately.

A distinct doctrine-adaptation branch appears in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-328-1-zhongguo-zuqiu-shijie-diyi-juran-shi-2000nian-lvm7t6lvc2neidswaddufxgdw8w_|Hanji 328-1]]. It contrasts auxiliary cavalry tasks attributed to [[SunBinBingfa|《孙膑兵法》]] and the infantry/chariot-centered force attributed to [[MengTian|蒙恬]] with Huo's long-range mounted operations against Xiongnu. The episode credits [[WeiQingHanGeneral|卫青]] with organizational preparation and makes Huo a case of [[MilitaryDoctrineEraFit|兵法的时代适配]]: inherited principles remain historical knowledge, but literal tactics must be tested against a changed force system.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-327-2-huoqubing-bu-du-sunzi-bingfa-weihe-neng-yongbing-ru-shen-lqafy7cesohwmuwhfilt2ghicsw7|Hanji 327-2]] supplies the reported personal stance and campaign test. When [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] offers instruction in the Sun-Wu texts, Huo reportedly says that war depends on strategic judgment rather than constraint by ancient doctrine; when offered a residence, he says a home is premature while Xiongnu remains undefeated. The source then uses claimed six-day and long-distance Hexi cavalry movements in 121 BCE to contrast literal application of a [[SunziBingfa|Sunzi]] pursuit warning. The durable addition is conditional adaptation, not proof that Huo rejected all study or that the whole text had become useless.

The Mobei culmination adds Huo's eastern force from Dai and Youbeiping, a reported advance of more than two thousand li, defeat of the Left Wise King's army, northern pursuit, and heaven-and-earth rites at Langjuxu and Guyan. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-326-1-tai-weiwu-huoqubing-fenglangjuxu-lujpi26bamjuojmxo0u2lbjyiih3|Hanji 326-1]] contrasts this speed, direct shock, and pursuit with Wei Qing's more formation-centered method. Yet the same source reports that only three wan of fourteen wan expedition horses returned, making Huo's victory a case where tactical and strategic effect coexist with loss of immediate follow-on capacity.

The post-Mobei evidence adds the social conversion of that victory. Huo becomes increasingly prominent while Wei Qing receives no comparable reward, and former Wei-household associates move toward Huo because access to him can produce appointments or better treatment. This supports Huo as a new patronage center without proving that he solicited defectors, that every move was mercenary, or that uncle and nephew were personally hostile. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-327-1-wanwan-meixiangdao-hanwudi-ye-ai-chi-xiangcai-lv_ed7hfumn80ypmwq8nfdpdfwcv|Hanji 327-1]]

The court-violence layer is sharply different. Li Gan serves under Huo and wins merit against Xiongnu, but after Li assaults Huo's uncle [[WeiQingHanGeneral|卫青]], Huo shoots Li during a Ganquan Palace hunt. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-335-dangzhe-wudi-de-mian-huoqubing-weihe-gan-sha-liguang-zhi-zi-lupxhu64fi0d0xl223zq9pwenmy1|Hanji 335]] interprets this as private retaliation and says [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] protects Huo with a deer-accident account. Military success and family loyalty therefore do not settle the legal judgment: this killing is presented as violence outside adjudication made durable by favorite status.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-336-2-huoqubing-yunluo-wudi-zhen-shi-muhou-heishou-ltfkwalc48bj1hav65bg-8qotrf5|Hanji 336-2]] proposes that the killing also damaged Huo's support and heightened Wudi's suspicion of the wider Wei-Huo power field, but the source supplies no direct evidence for either reaction and explicitly labels the theory that Wudi caused Huo's death as speculation. Its firmer family-history branch says Huo acknowledged and supported Huo Zhongru, brought his half-brother Huo Guang to Chang'an for palace service, and left a son whom Wudi kept close after Huo's death.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-336-1-zhanshen-huoqubing-weihe-24sui-turan-baobi-lhsmxfi72dzs8ucjd4n9vhfuhvmq|Hanji 336-1]] supplies the more conservative death-evidence baseline. It dates Huo's death to 117 BCE, reports imperial grief and a Qilian-shaped tomb, and attributes a broad illness account to Chu Shaosun's supplementary table through Huo Guang without naming the disease. Its objections to a contaminated-water epidemic story are the approximately two-year interval and lack of a recorded mass outbreak; accident and murder likewise lack direct support. The title's age twenty-four and body text's age twenty-three remain unresolved rather than silently harmonized.

## Key Characteristics

- Wei-family-connected youth described as a talented palace-raised imperial favorite and alternative senior command center whose post-Mobei prominence attracts office-seeking associates from Wei Qing's household.
- Western Han general associated with the Mobei eastern-route victory, defeat of the Xiongnu Left Wise King, and “封狼居胥” ritual memory.
- Long-range cavalry commander whose Hexi record joins first-campaign speed, political separation, conditional surrender, sustained pursuit, and a claimed Gaolan decision to later desert envelopment, continuation after a failed water-dependent rendezvous, major western disruption, and crisis surrender stabilization; severe claimed losses, routes, supplies, battle mechanics, supporting columns, and horse attrition keep speed and reach materially bounded.
- Commander whose reported cuju activity under supply shortage coexists with a source-scoped failure to distribute surplus food to hungry soldiers.
- Upstream actor in the Wuhuan relocation and protector-office origin story, and military context for Zhao Ponu's rise and Li Gan's battlefield merit.
- Wei Qing's nephew and favored commander whose kin loyalty becomes the proposed motive for a protected, procedureless killing of Li Gan.
- Older half-brother who introduces Huo Guang into Chang'an service and father whose court-connected legacy continues through Huo Zihou, but whose own early death has no securely identified illness or alternative violent cause.

## Qualifications

Hanji 307-1 and 307-2 add the first-Hexi sequence but not an unqualified campaign reconstruction. Their ten-thousand force, seven-thousand Han loss, renewed ten-thousand-force description, routes, distances, Suopu, Yanzhi and Gaolan identifications, defeated-group count, replenishment mechanics, surrender promises, leaders' inner states, battle and weapons claims, 8,900-of-13,000 result, names and ranks, golden-figure meaning, reward, Jincheng fort, and Zhang Qian consultation remain episode-attributed. The force accounting is not reconciled, and the operation must not be merged with the second campaign.

This page remains bounded to seventeen compact podcast sources rather than a full military biography. Hanji 308 supplies the second-Hexi plan, while Hanji 309-1 supplies the missed reunion and outcome; their route, distance, climate, water, force totals, opposing identities, surprise mechanics, captive ranks, rewards, and permanent-clearance language remain episode-scoped. Hanji 312's private plans, bridge timetable, cultural psychology, court dialogue, Changshui composition, location, force totals, and internal battle movements remain episode-attributed.
