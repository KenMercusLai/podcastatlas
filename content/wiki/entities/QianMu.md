## Overview

[[QianMu|钱穆 / Qian Mu]] enters the wiki through Hanji 567 as the source of a story contrasting five-hundred-year and one-year horizons in cultural and governance judgment.

## Current Profile

The episode cites Qian Mu's story about an old temple whose founding abbot planted cypress trees he would never see mature, creating beauty for people five hundred years later. After one old tree is destroyed, a later abbot plants oleander because it can bloom next year. The host uses this contrast to illuminate [[JiaRangWesternHan|Jia Rang]]'s Yellow River policies: the upper strategy may look expensive, slow, and unpopular, but it can preserve order for far longer than a quick visible fix.

In the wiki, Qian Mu's current role is therefore source-scoped and analogical. He is not yet treated through his broader historiography, but through a compact time-horizon story attached to [[LongHorizonWorkEvaluation|long-horizon evaluation]].

## Key Characteristics

- Qian Mu is used as an authority for evaluating action across long civilizational time scales.
- The temple-tree story contrasts patient institution-building with immediately visible but shallow results.
- The episode applies the story to hydraulic policy, making long-term cost-benefit judgment easier to see.
