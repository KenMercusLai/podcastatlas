# OFO / 小黄车

## Overview
OFO / 小黄车 is the shared-bike platform that the wiki now treats as both a manufacturing-risk case and the central collapse case in China's shared-bike capital war.

## Current Profile
[[no-199-zixingche-200-nian-973489465]] first introduced OFO through upstream bicycle manufacturing risk: large promised orders and later financing stress exposed suppliers such as [[PhoenixBicycle]] to [[ReceivablesRisk]]. The No.218 O2O episode adds the fuller operating arc. [[DaiWei|戴威]]'s company moved from Peking University campus bike sharing into a city-scale [[SharedBikeCapitalWar]] against [[Mobike]], backed by investors including [[ZhuXiaohu|朱啸虎]], [[GSRVentures|金沙江创投]], [[Didi]], [[Alibaba]], and others.

The current synthesis is that OFO's low-cost model was powerful but fragile. Cheap bikes and mechanical locks supported rapid deployment and a plausible campus payback story, but city expansion exposed password leakage, private locking, theft, vandalism, subsidies, investor control conflict, SoftBank-style order pressure, failed merger politics, and ultimately the deposit-refund crisis that fixed OFO in public memory.

## Key Characteristics
- OFO began as a campus bike-sharing model before scaling into citywide dockless shared bikes.
- Its low-cost bike and mechanical-lock approach made fast expansion cheaper than Mobike's custom-hardware route.
- The same low-cost approach created control problems around password reuse, private locks, damaged identifiers, and missing bikes.
- Its strategic-investor conflict with Didi, Alibaba-side capital interest, and failed Mobike merger made it a founder-control and platform-war case.
- Its deposit-refund crisis made consumer liabilities and cash discipline central to the shared-bike story.
- Its supplier-order history remains an upstream [[SharedBikeManufacturingShock]] and [[ReceivablesRisk]] case.

## Evidence
- Manufacturing exposure - [[no-199-zixingche-200-nian-973489465]] says OFO's 2017 strategic agreement with [[PhoenixBicycle]] promised very large purchases before financing stress and under-fulfilled orders transmitted pressure upstream.
- Campus origin - [[no-218-o2o-yu-gongxiangjingji-shuyu-danche-chongdianbao-shangmenmeijia-he-anmo-de-mohuan-saqian-niandai-1013073318]] describes OFO's 2015 Peking University launch using contributed yellow bikes, numbers, and mechanical password locks.
- Low-cost expansion risk - [[no-218-o2o-yu-gongxiangjingji-shuyu-danche-chongdianbao-shangmenmeijia-he-anmo-de-mohuan-saqian-niandai-1013073318]] details one-bike-one-code lock weakness, password leakage, private locks, plate removal, repainting, subsidies, and rapid city deployment.
- Capital conflict - [[no-218-o2o-yu-gongxiangjingji-shuyu-danche-chongdianbao-shangmenmeijia-he-anmo-de-mohuan-saqian-niandai-1013073318]] covers Didi executive involvement, SoftBank order targets, Dai Wei's removal of Didi-appointed executives, Alibaba/Tencent-adjacent rivalry, and failed merger plans.
- Deposit crisis - [[no-218-o2o-yu-gongxiangjingji-shuyu-danche-chongdianbao-shangmenmeijia-he-anmo-de-mohuan-saqian-niandai-1013073318]] opens and closes with large online and offline deposit-refund queues and OFO's late self-rescue attempts.

## Qualifications
Precise order counts, user counts, financing amounts, SoftBank target mechanics, and unpaid-deposit totals are treated as source-scoped. The wiki does not independently adjudicate Dai Wei's later activities or legal responsibility.
