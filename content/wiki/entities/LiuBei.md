# 刘备 / Liu Bei

## Overview
刘备 / Liu Bei is a late-Han leader whose current source set follows him from early study and borrowed footholds through repeated base loss, shelter under stronger actors, adviser-enabled state formation, and the post-Changban turn toward alliance with [[SunQuan|孙权]].

## Current Profile
The sources do not present Liu Bei's rise as a straight accumulation of victories. His early trajectory depends on people, offices, troops, local sponsors, and host relationships that can be gained and lost. Study under [[LuZhiLateHan|卢植]] places him in an early elite network; [[TaoQianLateHan|陶谦]]'s Danyang troops and Yuzhou title create a first Xuzhou foothold; and the later Xuzhou succession works because [[MiZhu|糜竺]], [[ChenDeng|陈登]], and [[KongRong|孔融]] judge him legitimate and useful without yet being strong enough to absorb local interests.

That dependence repeatedly becomes danger. [[ZhangFei|张飞]]'s Xiapi failure and [[LyuBu|吕布]]'s grain-backed intervention destroy Liu Bei's base, capture his family, and force survival under a reversed hierarchy. Lü Bu later attacks again when Liu Bei's force appears threatening, pushing Liu Bei to [[CaoCao|曹操]]. Cao Cao protects and rearms him partly because killing a desperate guest would damage talent-attraction credibility. Liu Bei later closes Lü Bu's own surrender path by reminding Cao Cao of Ding Yuan and Dong Zhuo.

Hanji 1099 adds the next major reversal. After Changban, Liu Bei names [[WuJuLateHan|吴巨]] as a destination, but [[LuSu|鲁肃]] redirects him toward Sun Quan. Lu Su makes the stronger dependency politically acceptable by calling it common enterprise and by invoking [[ZhugeJin|诸葛瑾]]'s relationship with [[ZhugeLiang|诸葛亮]]. The episode interprets Liu Bei's acceptance as an alliance-first revision of the [[LongzhongPlan|隆中对]], preserving the organization before it has secured Jingzhou.

## Key Characteristics
- Builds political capacity through portable reputation, Han-clan legitimacy, advisers, sponsors, titles, and shifting local coalitions rather than a continuously held hereditary base.
- Accepts dangerous dependency when organizational survival requires it, including arrangements under Lü Bu, Cao Cao, and later alliance with Sun Quan.
- Attracts local support when elites see him as legitimate enough to protect order but not yet strong enough to erase their interests.
- Converts adviser input into strategy and later state architecture, with Zhuge Liang serving as the clearest source-scoped example.
- Endures repeated losses of territory, family security, troops, and bargaining position without abandoning the wider project.
- Uses reputation and legitimacy both as resources and as constraints, including anti-usurpation pressure against Yuan Shu.
- Can make hard risk judgments about other actors, as shown by the warning that prevents Lü Bu's incorporation.

## Qualifications
These podcast sources mix annalistic narrative with modern management, psychology, and moral interpretation. Exact motives, inner reactions, troop counts, dialogues, and causal labels remain source-scoped. The two versions of Zhang Fei's Xuzhou failure emphasize different mechanisms—drunkenness in Qinji 131-2 and the Cao Bao/Xu Dan sequence in Hanji 999—so the page preserves the layered account rather than forcing a single cause. Liu Bei's acceptance of dependency should not be mistaken for passivity: the relationships are temporary, contested, and repeatedly renegotiated.
