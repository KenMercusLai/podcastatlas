## Overview
Liu Shang is an Eastern Han commander whose wiki profile now spans the Chengdu approach, Liu Xiu's rebuke over the surrendered Chengdu sack, later southwestern campaigning, and the fatal Wuling advance that sets up Ma Yuan's last campaign. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-712-liuxiu-weihe-bei-yuwei-zui-hui-da-zhang-de-huangdi-lt7rx7tblodjfngqfdq33ikrcmyn|Hanji 712]] makes his separated southern camp part of Liu Xiu's operational-risk diagnosis before [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-713-liuxiu-nu-le-mingjiang-wuhan-jing-xuexi-chengdu-lv6555l99it33gw34uydsz4tkbje|Hanji 713]] makes him part of the post-surrender accountability field.

## Current Profile
In Hanji 712, Liu Shang is the separate Han commander south of the river while [[WuHanEasternHan|Wu Han]] camps closer to Chengdu. Liu Xiu warns that their distance prevents reliable mutual support if Gongsun Shu's side attacks sequentially, and the episode says that pattern occurs before Liu Xiu's warning letter arrives. Wu Han later restores the position by secretly moving at night and reuniting with Liu Shang before the renewed fight.

In [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-713-liuxiu-nu-le-mingjiang-wuhan-jing-xuexi-chengdu-lv6555l99it33gw34uydsz4tkbje|Hanji 713]], Liu Shang appears in Liu Xiu's rebuke after Chengdu has surrendered. The emperor groups him with Wu Han in asking how they could burn and plunder families after officials and civilians had already submitted, making Liu Shang part of the command-responsibility field for the Chengdu aftermath.

Later source notes move Liu Shang into the southwestern frontier. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-721-ta-sha-jianu-ma-gongzhu-weihe-fanbei-shang-lryzcejdnrm4-92esnddfvyjkqli|Hanji 721]] frames him inside Liu Xiu's difficult balancing of military command, law, and rank. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-722-rang-liuxiu-xiansi-de-toutongbing-zenme-huishi-logwccdsca7vm2zzssu8ubm8rwra|Hanji 722]] records his successful southwestern campaign against Dong Chan, while [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-724-xiongnu-neiluan-donghan-weishenme-bu-chenji-yao-ta-ming-lpqksa0j1y0tskwv6fpflhovg9b8|Hanji 724]] records his overextended advance into Wuling that ends with his death and heavy army losses.

## Key Characteristics
- Eastern Han commander whose record includes both victory contexts and serious command failure.
- Southern-camp commander whose distance from Wu Han creates mutual-support risk before Chengdu.
- Re-concentration partner when Wu Han escapes encirclement through concealed night movement.
- Participant in the Chengdu aftermath whom Liu Xiu rebukes for violence after surrender.
- Southwestern campaign actor connected to Han efforts against Dong Chan and later Wuling unrest.
- Example of frontier overextension when difficult terrain and local resistance outrun command judgment.

## Qualifications
The page synthesizes only the source notes listed above. Hanji 712 makes Liu Shang crucial to camp geometry and mutual support, but it gives less independent biography or decision-making detail for him than for Wu Han. Hanji 713 reports Liu Xiu's anger and rebuke but does not record a formal punishment for Liu Shang in that episode.
