# Purdue Pharma

## Overview

Purdue Pharma is the OxyContin manufacturer whose internal research and geographically selective launch strategy form the central company case in [[in-triplicate|In Triplicate]].

## Current Profile

The episode reports that Purdue's 1995 physician focus groups identified [[TriplicatePrescriptionMonitoring]] as a major barrier shortly before OxyContin's 1996 launch. Doctors associated the forms with permanent records, possible government inspection, paperwork, and anxiety, leading Purdue to question whether triplicate states were worth intensive marketing.

Purdue then concentrated promotion outside California, Texas, Illinois, Idaho, and New York. The episode treats that choice as an inadvertent natural experiment: the company revealed privately that the regulation affected behavior and then created a geographic contrast in marketing exposure from which researchers later estimated differences in use and harm.

## Key Characteristics

- Used physician research to identify regulatory friction affecting opioid prescribing.
- Treated triplicate-state oversight as a market-access constraint before launching OxyContin.
- Concentrated marketing in less restrictive states, according to the episode's account.
- Generated internal evidence later used in litigation-based research on opioid exposure and outcomes.

## Evidence

### Pre-launch physician research

- [[in-triplicate]] reports that a 1995 Houston focus group repeatedly raised triplicate rules, physician anxiety, and scrutiny risk.

### Geographic marketing strategy

- [[in-triplicate]] says Purdue largely passed over the five remaining triplicate states while applying its full marketing effort elsewhere.

### Later policy evidence

- [[in-triplicate]] describes economists' use of internal Purdue documents to connect the marketing split with lower OxyContin use and slower overdose-death growth in triplicate states.

## Qualifications

The page is bounded to one retrospective podcast summary and is not a complete corporate history, legal record, product-safety assessment, or account of the opioid epidemic. Marketing allocation, sales-force size, prescription revenue, focus-group conclusions, company intent, causal effects, and litigation-document interpretation require the underlying records for independent verification. State differences cannot by themselves assign all later harm to one company or one policy choice.

## What Changed

- Established Purdue's reported recognition of triplicate monitoring as a prescribing deterrent.
- Connected its launch geography to the episode's natural-experiment argument.
- Preserved the distinction between this focused case and a complete allocation of opioid-crisis responsibility.
