## Overview

梁不疑 / Liang Buyi is Liang Ji's brother whose current wiki profile now begins in the 141 CE Liang-family transition, when he becomes Henan Yin as [[LiangJiLateHan|梁冀]] becomes Grand General after [[LiangShangLateHan|梁商]]'s death. Hanji 822 then makes him [[ZhangGangLateHan|张纲]]'s co-target in a 142 CE impeachment under [[EmperorShunOfHan|汉顺帝]], while Hanji 836 adds the scholar-leaning 151 CE layer where Liang Buyi becomes the kinship node through which Liang Ji's suspicion moves from family conflict into surveillance and punishment of visiting士人.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-821-budong-laoban-xinsi-haixiang-jinsheng-meimen-lmjxr8fdxgw-colrlinmujba74fh|Hanji 821]] supplies Liang Buyi's earliest current layer. After Liang Shang dies, Emperor Shun appoints Liang Ji as Grand General and Liang Buyi as Henan Yin, making Liang Buyi part of the first explicit Liang-family office handoff in the current wiki.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-822-chailang-dangdao-ta-jiang-maotou-zhizhi-huangdi-yuefu-lqhpmayvd3p07byr-zoiv-jyhdv|Hanji 822]] supplies the next layer. Zhang Gang refuses a routine inspection circuit, buries his carriage wheel in Luoyang, and impeaches Liang Ji and Liang Buyi on fifteen major charges. Emperor Shun knows the charges are right but cannot immediately punish the brothers because Liang-family harem favor and court networks are too strong.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-836-xiongdi-fanmu-beihou-ruzhe-he-jianchen-de-jiaofeng-lq9wjn5c8mwt-onrtslum4fw53eq|Hanji 836]] presents Liang Buyi as a member of the Liang family whose temperament and associations differ sharply from Liang Ji's. He favors learning, Confucian culture, and contact with scholars, while Liang Ji is portrayed as indulgent, arrogant, and corrupt.

The conflict becomes politically dangerous after Liang Ji uses power to move Liang Buyi into the Guanglu xun post and place his young son Liang Yin as Henan Yin. Liang Buyi resigns, closes his doors with another brother, and stops public contact, but Liang Ji continues to send disguised watchers to monitor his household.

When [[MaRongLateHan|马融]] and [[TianMingLateHan|田明]] pass by and visit Liang Buyi on the way to commandery posts, Liang Ji treats the visit as a threat. The resulting accusations, hair-removal punishment, flogging, and exile make Liang Buyi's household a pressure point where brotherly conflict becomes outer-relative political control.

## Key Characteristics

- Liang-family figure appointed Henan Yin during the 141 CE post-Liang-Shang office transition.
- Liang-family figure directly impeached with Liang Ji before later household surveillance becomes visible.
- Liang-family figure whose scholar associations contrast with Liang Ji's coercive political style.
- Brother whose retreat from office does not end Liang Ji's suspicion or surveillance.
- Household node through which ordinary visits by newly appointed officials become punishable political contact.
- Source case showing that outer-relative power can police kinship boundaries as well as public offices.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 836, Hanji 822, and Hanji 821 and should not be treated as a full Liang Buyi biography. Hanji 821 adds the Henan Yin appointment but does not reconstruct his performance in that office. Hanji 822 establishes the co-impeachment layer but does not preserve the exact fifteen charges here. Hanji 836 does not reconstruct his whole office career, his exact factional program, or his later life after the monitored household episode. Liang Yin remains source-scoped because the note uses him mainly as part of Liang Ji's family-office placement.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 821 as Liang Buyi's first current office layer: Henan Yin during the Liang Shang-to-Liang Ji transition.
- Kept Hanji 822 as Liang Buyi's co-target layer in Zhang Gang's direct impeachment under Emperor Shun.
- Preserved Hanji 836 as the brotherly and scholar-association pressure point in the 151 CE Liang-family conflict.
