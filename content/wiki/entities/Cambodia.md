# Cambodia / 柬埔寨

## Overview

Cambodia appears across the evidence set as an ancient hydraulic and wetland civilization, a country devastated by regional war and Khmer Rouge rule, and a contemporary hub where politically protected scam compounds test state enforcement.

## Current Profile

Cambodia's long historical profile joins [[Angkor|吴哥]] and [[TonleSapLake|Tonle Sap]] to water-managed abundance while showing how hydraulic systems become fragile when knowledge, maintenance, ecology, and social order fail. In the modern rupture, North Vietnamese bases, American bombing and invasion, civil war, failing harvests, and institutional collapse preceded the [[KhmerRouge|Khmer Rouge]] seizure of Phnom Penh and forced removal of its population.

Contemporary casino-linked scam compounds expose a different governance failure: political protection, sanctions, extradition, tourism damage, and stranded workers show how selective raids can leave an organized-fraud system intact.

## Key Characteristics

- Tonle Sap flood pulses and water management supported Angkor's agricultural and imperial scale.
- Hydraulic abundance remained vulnerable to ecological pressure, lost knowledge, and social rupture.
- Regional war, American intervention, civil breakdown, and displacement preceded Khmer Rouge victory.
- Khmer Rouge rule converted revolutionary agrarian ideology into forced urban evacuation and mass violence.
- Contemporary scam compounds link casinos, coercion, fraud, political protection, and weak enforcement.

## Qualifications

The evidence spans very different periods and source types and should not imply one continuous Cambodian pathology. The Angkor account is a travel-and-history synthesis, the 1975 episode compresses a complex war and genocide, and the fraud episode's revenue, compound, sanction, and tourism figures remain source-scoped. American intervention, Cambodian actors, North Vietnamese forces, and Khmer Rouge ideology all require separate causal treatment.
