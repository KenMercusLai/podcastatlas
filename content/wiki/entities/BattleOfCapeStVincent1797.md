# Battle of Cape St Vincent (1797)

## Overview

The Battle of Cape St Vincent was the 14 February 1797 British naval victory in which [[JohnJervis]]'s smaller fleet prevented separated Spanish divisions from combining and made [[HoratioNelson]] a national hero.

## Current Profile

Jervis entered battle with 15 ships of the line against a reported 27 Spanish ships because preventing their junction with France mattered more than numerical caution. He aimed to pass between two separated Spanish groups, turn upwind, and defeat them before they reunited. When smoke and missed signals threatened that design, Nelson broke formation in *Captain* and moved alone against the larger division. Support from *Culloden* and [[CuthbertCollingwood|Collingwood]]'s *Excellent* followed; after *Captain* was badly damaged, Nelson boarded *San Nicolas* and then crossed from it to *San Jose*. The British captured four ships. The episode treats the result as both individual audacity and institutional performance: drilling, gunnery, sea experience, discipline, and shared command intent made the gamble executable, while shattered lower decks and heavy Spanish losses qualify the heroic set piece.

## Key Characteristics

- Strategically important attempt to prevent Spanish-French fleet concentration.
- Battle between a smaller prepared British fleet and a larger but divided Spanish fleet.
- Jervis plan built around penetration, separation, and local numerical advantage.
- Nelson initiative that departed from formation to preserve the wider tactical purpose.
- Successive boarding actions that transformed Nelson's public reputation.
- Victory whose celebrated gallantry coexisted with extreme close-range violence.

## Qualifications

The account comes from one British-centered popular-history episode and strongly foregrounds Nelson's dramatic boarding actions. Exact fleet, gun, casualty, prisoner, timing, and prize figures; the claim that no British officer had captured a ship by boarding for three centuries; reported dialogue; and the invasion consequences remain source-scoped. The victory validates neither every act of disobedience nor a simple claim of inherent British superiority.

## What Changed

- Created the battle profile around divided-fleet strategy, disciplined autonomy, Nelson's local initiative, and the violence beneath heroic memory.
