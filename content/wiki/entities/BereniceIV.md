# Berenice IV

## Overview

Berenice IV was the Ptolemaic daughter who ruled Egypt after [[PtolemyXIIAuletes|Ptolemy XII Auletes]] was expelled and was executed when a Roman-backed expedition restored him.

## Current Profile

[[195-young-cleopatra-part-1-glt1502910094]] places Berenice's reign inside a conflict over taxation, Roman influence, dynastic legitimacy, and Alexandrian political power. She replaced her father after his flight in 58 BCE, but the restored king returned with forces led by Aulus Gabinius and killed her.

Her short reign shows that royal women could become sovereign claimants, while her defeat shows how quickly Ptolemaic succession could be decided by Roman money and military force. The episode uses her fate as part of the dangerous family environment inherited by [[CleopatraVII|Cleopatra VII]].

## Key Characteristics

- Ptolemaic princess who became ruler during her father's exile.
- Beneficiary of Alexandrian opposition to taxation and Roman dependence.
- Dynastic claimant defeated by foreign-backed restoration.
- Royal woman whose execution demonstrates the lethal stakes of Ptolemaic succession.

## Evidence

- Accession: [[195-young-cleopatra-part-1-glt1502910094]] identifies Berenice as the daughter who replaced Auletes after his expulsion.
- Defeat: [[195-young-cleopatra-part-1-glt1502910094]] connects her fall to Gabinius's Roman expedition and Auletes's restoration.
- Dynastic meaning: [[195-young-cleopatra-part-1-glt1502910094]] uses her execution to frame Cleopatra's household as a field of personal as well as political danger.

## Qualifications

The source gives only a compressed account of Berenice's reign. It does not establish her governing program, the composition of her support, the details of her marriage politics, or the exact process by which she was condemned and executed.
