## Overview
The An-era Deng family is the fallen outer-relative network attached to [[EmpressDowagerDengLateHan|Empress Dowager Deng]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-795-dengjia-miewang-hou-weihe-juchao-wei-tamen-mingyuan-lqvmbqrrookphb4becaqbp7h-wi1|Hanji 795]] presents the family's destruction as a politically tempting but procedurally defective post-regency purge: accusations accumulate after Deng power collapses, seven family members die, and public mourning plus Zhu Chong's memorial force [[EmperorAnOfHan|Emperor An]] into limited correction.

## Current Profile
The family is not portrayed as a simple innocent household or a simple guilty faction. The source acknowledges why a new emperor might need to reset a powerful regency family, but it emphasizes that the Deng case lacked the visible proof, interrogation, and measured settlement expected for legitimate state punishment.

The Deng family's fall also creates a comparative template for later An-era politics. Their removal is followed almost immediately by the rise of Geng, Song, Yan, eunuch, wet-nurse, and favorite-household networks, making the purge less a restoration of public order than a change in who controls access.

## Key Characteristics
- Former outer-relative network attached to Empress Dowager Deng's regency.
- Target of repeated accusations after Deng power collapses, including attacks advanced by [[ChenZhongLateHan|Chen Zhong]].
- Defended by [[ZhuChongLateHan|Zhu Chong]] as a meritorious household punished without sufficient evidentiary process.
- Subject to severe consequences, including seven reported deaths and disruption of ancestral and burial arrangements.
- Recipient of limited post-purge relief when Emperor An permits corpses to return for burial and allows cousins to return to Luoyang.
- Structural foil for later favorite networks because anti-Deng cleanup quickly gives way to new kin and palace-intimate power.

## Evidence
- Accusation and purge momentum: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-795-dengjia-miewang-hou-weihe-juchao-wei-tamen-mingyuan-lqvmbqrrookphb4becaqbp7h-wi1|Hanji 795]] describes Chen Zhong's restored anti-Deng role and the push that moves the family into deeper guilt.
- Public defense and procedural critique: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-795-dengjia-miewang-hou-weihe-juchao-wei-tamen-mingyuan-lqvmbqrrookphb4becaqbp7h-wi1|Hanji 795]] reports Zhu Chong carrying a coffin to memorial, arguing that one-sided palace testimony, lack of confrontation, and unclear evidence cannot sustain household destruction.
- Partial relief after backlash: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-795-dengjia-miewang-hou-weihe-juchao-wei-tamen-mingyuan-lqvmbqrrookphb4becaqbp7h-wi1|Hanji 795]] says commoners mourned the Deng family, Emperor An recognized overreach, and surviving relatives gained limited return and burial concessions.
- Replacement by new networks: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-795-dengjia-miewang-hou-weihe-juchao-wei-tamen-mingyuan-lqvmbqrrookphb4becaqbp7h-wi1|Hanji 795]] contrasts Deng removal with immediate appointments and titles for Geng, Song, Yan, eunuch, and wet-nurse-connected actors.

## Qualifications
The source does not give a complete charge sheet or a complete family genealogy. Its strongest claim is procedural: the purge may have had political causes, but the available evidence in this source does not justify the scale, opacity, or one-sidedness of punishment.
