## Overview
The An-era Deng family is the outer-relative network attached to [[EmpressDowagerDengLateHan|Empress Dowager Deng]], visible before its fall through military reward, household discipline, elite education, and internal warning. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-790-lishi-gaosu-ni-ruhe-he-lingdao-da-jiaodao-lulf0r1sdkod3luzwru0-kkrijxk|Hanji 790]] adds [[DengZunLateHan|Deng Zun]]'s Qiang-war reward and [[DengZhiLateHan|Deng Zhi]]'s punishment of his wife and son over Ren Shang's gift; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-792-dengkang-renu-taihou-dagou-weishenme-yao-kan-zhuren-lh3xkjneqfd9sqijqo3rgzsa5vwn|Hanji 792]] supplies a pre-fall internal warning through [[DengKangLateHan|Deng Kang]]; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-794-yidai-nvjun-dengsui-qushi-hou-jing-quanjia-zaoyang-llmlhabdd09opca6-pr80q4mfoxi|Hanji 794]] supplies the first punishment wave after Deng's death; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-795-dengjia-miewang-hou-weihe-juchao-wei-tamen-mingyuan-lqvmbqrrookphb4becaqbp7h-wi1|Hanji 795]] shows the later public defense, partial burial repair, and replacement of Deng power by new favorite networks.

## Current Profile
The family is already politically visible before its purge. Hanji 790 shows Deng Zun receiving the Wuyang marquisate after the killing of Langmo, while [[RenShangLateHan|Ren Shang]] is executed after competing for credit. The same episode shows Deng Zhi punishing his wife and son Deng Feng for accepting Ren Shang's horse and apologizing to the court. This layer does not erase Deng-family privilege; it shows that the family also performs visible risk control when gift-taking and frontier credit threaten its reputation.

Hanji 790 also places Deng-family children beside royal children in a supervised education program under the dowager. That institution-building layer becomes politically double-edged because it can look like cultural governance and like preparation of a wider family-royal power base. Hanji 792 then shows Deng Kang, a Deng cousin with military-administrative proximity, repeatedly urging that court authority be raised and outer-relative power weakened. His punishment after feigned illness and palace-status conflict shows that not every Deng-family actor is aligned with continued family dominance, but it also shows that internal warning cannot reliably restrain the regency core.

The family is not portrayed as a simple innocent household or a simple guilty faction. Hanji 794 explains why Emperor An and his palace intimates could treat the Deng household as a succession threat: Wang Sheng, Li Run, and Jiang Jing had already slandered Deng while fearing that An might be replaced, and after Deng's death the accusation centers on an alleged plan to install [[LiuYiPingyuanWangLateHan|Pingyuan Wang Liu Yi]].

The first purge wave is severe. Titles are abolished, offices are removed, property is confiscated, Deng Fang's household is exiled, some relatives are pressured into suicide, and Deng Zhi and Deng Feng starve. Hanji 795 then gives the family's contested afterlife: [[ZhuChongLateHan|Zhu Chong]] does not prove every Deng actor innocent, but he argues that the court has destroyed a meritorious household without visible proof, direct interrogation, or proportional settlement.

The Deng family's fall also creates a comparative template for later An-era politics. Their removal is followed by the rise of Geng, Song, Yan, eunuch, wet-nurse, and favorite-household networks, making the purge less a restoration of public order than a change in who controls access.

## Key Characteristics
- Outer-relative network attached to Empress Dowager Deng's regency before and after its fall.
- Family whose military reward and household discipline show both privilege and reputation-management pressure.
- Target of succession-replacement accusations centered on Pingyuan Wang Liu Yi.
- Subject to title abolition, office removal, property confiscation, exile, forced death, and starvation in the first purge wave.
- Defended by Zhu Chong as a meritorious household punished without sufficient evidentiary process.
- Contains an internal warning branch through Deng Kang, whose expulsion may become risk separation before the larger purge.
- Structural foil for later favorite networks because anti-Deng cleanup quickly gives way to new kin and palace-intimate power.

- Replacement-threat trigger: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-794-yidai-nvjun-dengsui-qushi-hou-jing-quanjia-zaoyang-llmlhabdd09opca6-pr80q4mfoxi|Hanji 794]] says Wang Sheng fears Emperor An may be deposed and that Wang Sheng, Li Run, and Jiang Jing slander Empress Dowager Deng before the purge.

- First punishment wave: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-794-yidai-nvjun-dengsui-qushi-hou-jing-quanjia-zaoyang-llmlhabdd09opca6-pr80q4mfoxi|Hanji 794]] describes abolished Deng titles, office removals, asset confiscation, exile, suicide pressure, and the deaths of Deng Zhi and Deng Feng.

- Partial relief after backlash: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-795-dengjia-miewang-hou-weihe-juchao-wei-tamen-mingyuan-lqvmbqrrookphb4becaqbp7h-wi1|Hanji 795]] says commoners mourned the Deng family, Emperor An recognized overreach, and surviving relatives gained limited return and burial concessions.
- Replacement by new networks: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-795-dengjia-miewang-hou-weihe-juchao-wei-tamen-mingyuan-lqvmbqrrookphb4becaqbp7h-wi1|Hanji 795]] contrasts Deng removal with immediate appointments and titles for Geng, Song, Yan, eunuch, and wet-nurse-connected actors.

## Qualifications
The source set does not give a complete charge sheet or family genealogy. Its strongest current claim is procedural and structural: the purge may have had political causes, and the sources do not disprove every accusation, but the visible record does not justify the scale, opacity, or one-sidedness of punishment. Deng Zun's reward and Deng Zhi's household punishment show privilege and discipline together; Deng Kang's warning and expulsion show internal differentiation, not full exoneration of the family.

## What Changed
- Added Hanji 790 as the pre-purge layer for Deng Zun's reward, Deng Zhi's household discipline, and Deng-family education politics.
- Preserved Hanji 792 as the internal warning and risk-separation layer through Deng Kang.
- Preserved Hanji 794 as the first purge wave and Pingyuan Wang Liu Yi accusation before Hanji 795's public defense.
- Reframed the family profile around sequence: pre-purge visibility, internal warning, replacement slander, immediate liquidation, then partial correction.
- Preserved Hanji 795's judgment that the purge becomes procedurally defective rather than cleanly exonerated.
