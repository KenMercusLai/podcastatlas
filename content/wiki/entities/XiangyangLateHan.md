# 襄阳 / Xiangyang (late Han)

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-953-luanshi-zhizhong-yaobuyao-jiangjiu-renyi-lrzfimgqatjxyw9zx9ry2kiwxkon|Hanji 953]] marks Xiangyang as the administrative base [[LiuBiao|刘表]] chooses after his initial consolidation of [[Jingzhou|荆州]]. The move follows advice from [[KuaiYue|蒯越]], who treats Xiangyang in the north and [[JianglingLateHan|江陵]] in the south as paired points for controlling the region's commanderies.

In the episode, Xiangyang matters less as a static city entry than as the concrete base that turns Liu Biao's appointment into territorial control. Once local armed clan heads are removed or absorbed, moving the state office there helps Liu Biao stabilize Jingzhou and later make it a refuge for displaced scholars.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1027-jingzhou-zhizhu-liubiao-weihe-nan-cheng-bazhu-ll-ebguqllsf-5ycvpdogihzn3lj|Hanji 1027]] uses Xiangyang as the northern center from which Liu Biao tries to hold Jingzhou. Its contrast is [[SouthernJingzhouLateHan|荆南]]: [[ChangshaCommanderyLateHan|长沙郡]], [[LinglingCommanderyLateHan|零陵郡]], and [[GuiyangCommanderyLateHan|桂阳郡]] are far enough from the center that [[ZhangXianLateHan|张献]] and [[HuanJieLateHan|桓阶]] can turn local commandery pressure toward [[CaoCao|曹操]].
