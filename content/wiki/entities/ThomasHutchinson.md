# Thomas Hutchinson

## Overview

Thomas Hutchinson is the Massachusetts governor whose letters about limiting colonial liberties become, in the episode, a trigger for Benjamin Franklin's break with British political confidence.

## Current Profile

The source presents Hutchinson through the letters Franklin received in December 1772 and passed to radicals in Boston. Franklin's purpose was to show that British repression was being encouraged by bad colonial advice rather than by inherent British hostility, but publication of the letters and the later Cockpit hearing produced the opposite political effect. Hutchinson therefore matters as an indirect catalyst: his correspondence helped expose how quickly imperial trust could collapse.

## Key Characteristics

- Massachusetts governor associated with letters advising limits on colonial liberties.
- Indirect trigger for Franklin's public humiliation before the Privy Council.
- Catalyst in the episode's account of [[ColonialLoyaltyRupture|colonial loyalty rupture]].
- Example of colonial insider advice becoming politically explosive once publicized.

## Evidence

- Political consequence: [[684-franklin-revenge-of-the-american-genius-part-2-glt6013835340]] says the letters were published despite Franklin's request and helped lead to Franklin's Cockpit humiliation.
