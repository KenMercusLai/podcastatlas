## Overview

李通 / Li Tong is an early Eastern Han founding-merit figure whose current wiki role is narrowly bounded to [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-712-liuxiu-weihe-bei-yuwei-zui-hui-da-zhang-de-huangdi-lt7rx7tblodjfngqfdq33ikrcmyn|Hanji 712]]. The source presents him as a once-ordinary participant in Liu Xiu's rise who later seeks distance from central power while retaining honor through special-entry court status.

## Current Profile

Hanji 712 gives Li Tong a compact merit-and-retreat profile. He had joined or initiated early uprising work for [[LiuXiu|光武帝刘秀]] and was connected to Princess Ningping, so Liu Xiu remembered him as unusually intimate and meritorious even after the main Shu campaign narrative returns to Wu Han and Chengdu.

The episode's important point is not a full biography but a political posture. Li Tong asks to retire and returns the Da Sikong seal. Liu Xiu allows the withdrawal from full office while keeping him present as a special-entry participant in court audiences, and later remembers his founding merit by enfeoffing his younger son Li Xiong as Zhao-ling marquis.

## Key Characteristics

- Early Eastern Han founding-merit figure remembered by Liu Xiu for uprising-era service.
- Politically close actor whose status is reinforced by the Princess Ningping connection.
- Officeholder who seeks to move away from the power center after merit has already been established.
- Example of honored withdrawal: he gives up the Da Sikong seal but keeps special-entry court status.
- Source-scoped father of Li Xiong, whose later enfeoffment is framed as Liu Xiu remembering old service.
