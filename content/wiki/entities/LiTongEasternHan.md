## Overview
李通 / Li Tong is an early Eastern Han founding-merit figure whose current wiki role now runs from risky pre-uprising coordination to high office and honored withdrawal. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-661-cong-shusheng-dao-kaiguo-huangdi-liuxiu-shoudeng-lishi-wutai-lmw-rgyoraks9loeouuxk5lyxfs5|Hanji 661]] supplies the first layer: Li Tong uses Liu-restoration prophecy and his cousin Li Yi to contact [[LiuXiu|刘秀]], plans a coordinated Nanyang and Chongling uprising, then suffers family catastrophe when the plan leaks. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-705-1-diwang-moulue-liuxiu-ruhe-yi-sheling-nizhuan-caiyuan-fengyun-lq2m06p7eczj9ebysxpl84bkzzi|Hanji 705-1]] places him in 31 CE as Liu Xiu's Da Sikong appointee after the eclipse-remonstrance and appointment-pressure sequence. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-712-liuxiu-weihe-bei-yuwei-zui-hui-da-zhang-de-huangdi-lt7rx7tblodjfngqfdq33ikrcmyn|Hanji 712]] later shows him returning that office while retaining special-entry honor.

## Current Profile
Hanji 661 gives the pre-uprising layer. Li Tong is linked to a household prophecy through his father Li Shou: Liu power will revive and a Li-surnamed helper will assist it. As New Market and Pinglin forces rise and Nanyang grows unsettled, Li Yi identifies Liu Yan and Liu Xiu as plausible Liu-lineage partners, meets Liu Xiu, and helps turn chenwei talk into a concrete seizure-and-uprising plan. Li Tong intends to act during a military inspection by seizing Zhen Fu and Liangqiu Ci, while Liu Xiu and Liu Yan raise troops at Chongling. The plan leaks before execution, Li Tong flees, and the source says Li Shou and other relatives are killed.

Hanji 705-1 gives the later accession-to-office layer. After Zheng Xing criticizes Liu Xiu's delayed Da Sikong appointment and public uncertainty around Guo Ji, Liu Xiu issues an amnesty and later appoints Li Tong as Da Sikong. The source does not present Li Tong as Zheng Xing's recommended candidate; it presents his appointment as the institutional outcome that closes the vacancy pressure.

Hanji 712 gives Li Tong a compact merit-and-retreat profile. He had joined or initiated early uprising work for [[LiuXiu|光武帝刘秀]] and was connected to Princess Ningping, so Liu Xiu remembered him as unusually intimate and meritorious even after the main Shu campaign narrative returns to Wu Han and Chengdu.

The combined profile is a trajectory from trusted founding merit into high office and then controlled withdrawal. Li Tong asks to retire and returns the Da Sikong seal. Liu Xiu allows the withdrawal from full office while keeping him present as a special-entry participant in court audiences, and later remembers his founding merit by enfeoffing his younger son Li Xiong as Zhao-ling marquis.

## Key Characteristics
- Early Eastern Han founding-merit figure remembered by Liu Xiu for uprising-era service.
- Early anti-Xin coordinator whose first current evidence is a failed but serious Nanyang seizure plan tied to Chongling mobilization.
- Prophecy-mediated connector who turns Liu-restoration expectation into contact with Liu Xiu through Li Yi.
- Family-cost case in which plan leakage kills Li Shou and other relatives before the action succeeds.
- 31 CE Da Sikong appointee in the aftermath of an eclipse-remonstrance and appointment-vacancy pressure sequence.
- Politically close actor whose status is reinforced by the Princess Ningping connection.
- Officeholder who later seeks to move away from the power center after merit has already been established.

## Qualifications
This page intentionally keeps Li Tong's profile source-bounded. Hanji 661 supplies the first current uprising role, but Zhen Fu, Liangqiu Ci, Li Shou, Li Yi's exact kinship/office, inspection mechanics, and casualty number remain source-scoped. Hanji 705-1 establishes his appointment as Da Sikong, but it does not explain why Liu Xiu chose him over Guo Ji or reconstruct the full personnel deliberation. Hanji 712 does not provide Princess Ningping's full biography or Li Xiong's independent career. The name is also disambiguated from the later Cao-era [[LiTongLateHan|李通 / Li Tong (late Han)]].

- Preserved Hanji 705-1 and Hanji 712 as the later high-office and honored-withdrawal layers.
