## Overview
李通 / Li Tong is an early Eastern Han founding-merit figure whose current wiki role now spans high office and honored withdrawal. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-705-1-diwang-moulue-liuxiu-ruhe-yi-sheling-nizhuan-caiyuan-fengyun-lq2m06p7eczj9ebysxpl84bkzzi|Hanji 705-1]] places him in 31 CE as Liu Xiu's Da Sikong appointee after the eclipse-remonstrance and appointment-pressure sequence. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-712-liuxiu-weihe-bei-yuwei-zui-hui-da-zhang-de-huangdi-lt7rx7tblodjfngqfdq33ikrcmyn|Hanji 712]] later shows him returning that office while retaining special-entry honor.

## Current Profile
Hanji 705-1 gives the accession-to-office layer. After Zheng Xing criticizes Liu Xiu's delayed Da Sikong appointment and public uncertainty around Guo Ji, Liu Xiu issues an amnesty and later appoints Li Tong as Da Sikong. The source does not present Li Tong as Zheng Xing's recommended candidate; it presents his appointment as the institutional outcome that closes the vacancy pressure.

Hanji 712 gives Li Tong a compact merit-and-retreat profile. He had joined or initiated early uprising work for [[LiuXiu|光武帝刘秀]] and was connected to Princess Ningping, so Liu Xiu remembered him as unusually intimate and meritorious even after the main Shu campaign narrative returns to Wu Han and Chengdu.

The combined profile is a trajectory from trusted founding merit into high office and then controlled withdrawal. Li Tong asks to retire and returns the Da Sikong seal. Liu Xiu allows the withdrawal from full office while keeping him present as a special-entry participant in court audiences, and later remembers his founding merit by enfeoffing his younger son Li Xiong as Zhao-ling marquis.

## Key Characteristics
- Early Eastern Han founding-merit figure remembered by Liu Xiu for uprising-era service.
- 31 CE Da Sikong appointee in the aftermath of an eclipse-remonstrance and appointment-vacancy pressure sequence.
- Politically close actor whose status is reinforced by the Princess Ningping connection.
- Officeholder who later seeks to move away from the power center after merit has already been established.
- Example of honored withdrawal: he gives up the Da Sikong seal but keeps special-entry court status.
- Source-scoped father of Li Xiong, whose later enfeoffment is framed as Liu Xiu remembering old service.

## Qualifications
This page intentionally keeps Li Tong's profile source-bounded. Hanji 705-1 establishes his appointment as Da Sikong, but it does not explain why Liu Xiu chose him over Guo Ji or reconstruct the full personnel deliberation. Hanji 712 does not provide Princess Ningping's full biography, Li Tong's exact uprising role, or Li Xiong's independent career. The name is also disambiguated from the later Cao-era [[LiTongLateHan|李通 / Li Tong (late Han)]].

## What Changed

- Preserved Li Tong as a Guangwu founding-merit figure distinct from the later Cao-aligned Li Tong.
