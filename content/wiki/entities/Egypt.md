# Egypt

## Overview

Egypt is a Middle Eastern and North African state represented in the wiki through anti-colonial sovereignty and the [[SuezCrisis|Suez Crisis]], the rise and repression of the [[MuslimBrotherhood]], pressure on [[Hamas]] in Gaza diplomacy, and support for Sudan's army.

## Current Profile

The historical branch presents Egypt's formal independence as constrained by British occupation and a 40,000-troop Canal Zone base. Violence around that presence contributed to the 1952 Free Officers coup and [[GamalAbdelNasser|Gamal Abdel Nasser]]'s rise. Britain's agreed withdrawal, the Aswan High Dam financing dispute, and legal nationalisation of the canal company then made sovereignty material: control of territory, infrastructure, revenue, and development finance rather than only a constitutional label.

Later sources show a different state role. Abdel Fattah al-Sisi's coup and repression made Egypt the sharpest reversal of the Muslim Brotherhood's Arab Spring electoral rise. In contemporary regional diplomacy, Egypt pressures Hamas over Gaza implementation and appears both as a backer of Sudan's armed forces and a member of the proposed Sudan mediation “quad.” Across these branches, Egyptian power is consequential but not unilateral: it operates among domestic coercion, neighboring wars, outside sponsors, and implementation gaps.

## Key Characteristics

- Anti-colonial state whose control of the Canal Zone, canal company, and development revenue became tests of effective sovereignty.
- Center of Nasser's nationalist, pan-Arab, developmental, and authoritarian political project.
- Strongest case in the sources of post-Arab Spring repression of the Muslim Brotherhood.
- Regional pressure actor in Gaza diplomacy without sole authority over disarmament or withdrawal.
- Supporter of Sudan's armed forces and participant in a mediation grouping containing rival external sponsors.
- Profile spans distinct periods and should not be read as one continuous policy or regime.

## Qualifications

The sources cover separate historical and contemporary issues rather than a comprehensive national profile. The Suez episode stops before invasion and withdrawal; the later episodes focus narrowly on the Muslim Brotherhood, Gaza, and Sudan. Claims about motives, influence, military support, legal questions, and diplomatic leverage remain episode-attributed, and current relationships may change.
