# Rick Smith

## Overview

Rick Smith is the founder and CEO of [[Axon]], originally Taser International, and an advocate for replacing some armed police encounters with recorded, governed, non-lethal technology.

## Current Profile

Smith's founder account combines persistence with repeated strategic reframing. He moved from a consumer non-lethal weapon to police adoption, from hardware sales to cloud evidence, and from individual devices toward an integrated public-safety platform. In each transition, the claimed social mission and the commercial model reinforce one another: police validation supported demand, body cameras created evidence and recurring storage, and connected drones or robots widen Axon's role in emergency response.

His governance position is conditional rather than anti-surveillance. Smith accepts civil-liberties risk but argues that access rules, audit trails, human authorization, published policies, penalties, and independent review can make sensing and remotely operated force legitimate. The source does not independently test those controls, and the ethics-board dispute shows that consultation and decision authority remain distinct.

## Key Characteristics

- Founder motivated by replacing bullets with a reliable non-lethal way to stop threats.
- Persistent through failed launches, family-backed financing, regulatory scrutiny, litigation, and weak early body-camera demand.
- Uses direct product demonstration to reduce buyer uncertainty.
- Reframed Axon from an electrical-weapon company into a cloud evidence and public-safety platform.
- Supports broad sensing capability when access and use are purpose-limited and auditable.
- Rejects autonomous use-of-force decisions and proposes human and multi-party authorization.

## Evidence

- **Founder persistence and pivoting:** [[allin-intv-ricksmith-ch-allin-intv-ricksmith-ch]] traces the 1993 prototype, seven-year consumer struggle, late-1999 police pivot, family financing, and 2001 IPO.
- **Demonstration-led adoption:** [[allin-intv-ricksmith-ch-allin-intv-ricksmith-ch]] describes live officer demonstrations as the mechanism that translated product claims into purchase orders.
- **Platform transformation:** [[allin-intv-ricksmith-ch-allin-intv-ricksmith-ch]] credits body-camera evidence needs, cloud migration, and software-team development with broadening the company beyond weapons.
- **Governance position:** [[allin-intv-ricksmith-ch-allin-intv-ricksmith-ch]] records support for case identifiers, access controls, dual authorization, audits, penalties, disclosed drone policies, human control of force, and civilian review.

## Qualifications

This profile comes from Smith's own interview. Financial history, product safety, litigation merit, police adoption, evidence effects, community demand, deterrence, and future-system benefits remain founder-reported. His comparison of future robotic systems with current armed policing identifies a relevant baseline but does not prove that the proposed alternative is safe, effective, lawful, or publicly legitimate.
