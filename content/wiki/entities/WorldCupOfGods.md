# World Cup of Gods

## Overview

The World Cup of Gods is a 2021 [[TheRestIsHistory]] listener tournament that uses a sixteen-entry knockout competition to introduce historical deities, disputed divine figures, a deified ruler, and a modern royal cult.

## Current Profile

[[55-the-world-cup-of-gods-preview-glt7465078719]] establishes the comic rules, seeds, predictions, Twitter voting, and roster. Eligibility nominally excludes gods still continuously worshipped in major living traditions while allowing later revivals or marginal worship, making religious discontinuity a selection device rather than a neutral definition of divinity.

The two follow-up episodes turn match results into a comparative-religion structure. [[58-the-world-cup-of-gods-part-1-glt8884399326]] uses the first-round losers to test the boundary among deity, ritual term, saint, ruler cult, literary figure, and modern worship. [[59-the-world-cup-of-gods-part-2-glt6474253297]] uses the surviving field and Athena's final victory over Odin to compare civic ritual, syncretism, literary mediation, conversion, and modern reception. Across the sequence, audience choice is the organizing spectacle, not a scholarly ranking of religious importance.

## Key Characteristics

- Sixteen-entry knockout tournament modeled on sports coverage and decided by listener voting.
- Eligibility rule based on discontinued worship, applied flexibly around revival, continuity, and small modern cults.
- Field spanning Egyptian, Greek, Roman, Norse, Mesopotamian, Anatolian, Levantine, Irish, Aztec, imperial, and modern cases.
- Comic format that uses seeds, upsets, punditry, endorsements, and imagined style clashes as public-history hooks.
- Comparative structure that exposes unstable boundaries around divine identity rather than resolving them.
- Modern reception event whose result records audience familiarity and preference, not ancient prestige.

## Evidence

- Rules and participation: [[55-the-world-cup-of-gods-preview-glt7465078719]] defines the field, distinguishes revival from continuous worship, presents the seeds, and directs listeners to vote on Twitter.
- Category boundaries: [[58-the-world-cup-of-gods-part-1-glt8884399326]] compares Moloch, Brigid, Augustus, Prince Philip, and other first-round figures whose status complicates a single definition of “god.”
- Historical layering: [[59-the-world-cup-of-gods-part-2-glt6474253297]] uses the later rounds to distinguish equivalence, syncretism, literary preservation, political reuse, and modern reinvention.
- Tournament outcome: [[59-the-world-cup-of-gods-part-2-glt6474253297]] records Athena's 52-48 final victory over Odin while treating the vote as a route into historical discussion.

## Qualifications

The tournament is entertainment-led and heavily compressed. Its exclusion of major continuously worshipped gods makes the field structurally selective, while the revival exception and informal worship threshold are not consistent tools for comparative religious classification. Seeds, voter motives, upsets, and the final result should be read as evidence of one show's audience reception; historical claims about individual cults require the fuller evidence and qualifications preserved on their source and entity pages.

## What Changed

- Integrated the preview and both follow-up episodes into one tournament-level profile.
- Distinguished the discontinuity-based eligibility rule from a general definition of divine identity.
- Reclassified the vote as participatory public history and modern reception rather than historical ranking.
