# Don Foster

## Overview

Don Foster is a literary-attribution scholar whose analysis made the claim that [[HenryLivingstonJr]] wrote [[AVisitFromStNicholas|“A Visit from St. Nicholas”]] nationally prominent.

## Current Profile

[[twas-the-night-before-christmas-twas-the-night-before-christmas]] describes Foster combining statistical comparison with close reading of vocabulary, punctuation, meter, and attitudes. His correct identification of Joe Klein as the author of *Primary Colors* helped establish his public authority, and [[MaryVanDusen]] brought the Livingston corpus to him in 1999.

Foster argued that the poem's anapestic rhythm, linguistic details, and cheerful character fit Livingston better than [[ClementClarkeMoore]]. The episode concludes that the case relied too heavily on selective characterization and authority: Moore's relevant writing undermines the exclusion argument, while Foster's later-withdrawn Shakespeare attribution and other disputed work weaken confidence without independently deciding this poem's authorship.

## Key Characteristics

- Combined computer-assisted pattern analysis with literary close reading.
- Correctly identified Joe Klein as the anonymous author of *Primary Colors*.
- Attributed the Christmas poem to Henry Livingston Jr.
- Used meter, vocabulary, punctuation, attitudes, and biography as evidence.
- Later withdrew a celebrated Shakespeare elegy attribution after scholarly criticism.
- Became part of the story because public authority amplified the Livingston theory.

## Evidence

- Method and reputation: [[twas-the-night-before-christmas-twas-the-night-before-christmas]] summarizes Foster's successful and prominent attribution work.
- Livingston analysis: [[twas-the-night-before-christmas-twas-the-night-before-christmas]] reports his use of anapestic meter, adverbial “all,” reindeer-name variants, tone, and candidate biography.
- Reliability qualification: [[twas-the-night-before-christmas-twas-the-night-before-christmas]] notes the withdrawn Shakespeare attribution and other disputed cases while separately presenting direct counterevidence about Moore.

## Qualifications

An expert's unrelated errors do not refute a particular attribution, just as a prior success does not prove it. The relevant question is whether the candidate corpus, feature selection, historical record, and disconfirming evidence support this inference. The supplied episode argues that they do not support Foster's conclusion here.

- Made expert authority an explicit amplifier rather than a substitute for case-specific proof.
