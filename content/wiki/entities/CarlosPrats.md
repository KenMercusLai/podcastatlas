# Carlos Prats

## Overview
Carlos Prats was the Chilean army commander whose commitment to constitutional obedience made him a major obstacle to a coup against [[SalvadorAllende]].

## Current Profile
In [[371-the-1973-chilean-coup-general-pinochet-seizes-power-part-2-glt3740766328]], Prats helps suppress the failed Tanquetazo with [[AugustoPinochet]] as his chief of staff. A road-rage shooting incident, press hostility, and a demonstration by officers' wives then destroy his authority. His August 1973 resignation removes an important restraint and allows Pinochet to take army command.

## Key Characteristics
- His constitutional obedience constrained officers willing to overthrow the elected government.
- He acted directly against the failed June 1973 tank revolt.
- His own loss of judgment in the Alejandrina Cox incident made him politically vulnerable.
- Organized social and media pressure amplified that vulnerability inside the officer corps.
- His resignation changed the command structure shortly before the successful coup.

## Evidence
- Constitutional obstacle: [[371-the-1973-chilean-coup-general-pinochet-seizes-power-part-2-glt3740766328]] says the CIA regarded Prats as a major barrier because he supported constitutional obedience.
- Tanquetazo response: [[371-the-1973-chilean-coup-general-pinochet-seizes-power-part-2-glt3740766328]] credits Prats and Pinochet with suppressing the revolt.
- Removal and succession: [[371-the-1973-chilean-coup-general-pinochet-seizes-power-part-2-glt3740766328]] connects the Cox incident, officers' wives, press hostility, resignation, and Pinochet's August 22 appointment.

## Qualifications
The source gives Prats institutional importance without making him an infallible democratic hero. Its treatment of CIA views, media financing, officer-family pressure, and the exact causal weight of the Cox incident remains source-scoped.

## What Changed
- Created Prats as the constitutionalist command node between the failed and successful coups.
- Added his resignation as an institutional rather than merely biographical turning point.
