# Margaret Atwood / 玛格丽特·阿特伍

## Overview
Margaret Atwood is the Canadian author of [[TheBlindAssassinNovel|《盲刺客》 / The Blind Assassin]], represented here through one [[CultureLimited|文化有限]] discussion of layered narration, gendered violence, memory, and identity.

## Current Profile
The source presents Atwood as a writer who began in poetry and moved across historical fiction, speculative and dystopian fiction, and formally nested novels. Its strongest contribution is work-level rather than biographical: Atwood's decision to let the elderly [[IrisChase|Iris Chase]] narrate her own past shifts *The Blind Assassin* away from an outsider's investigation and toward responsibility, regret, self-definition, and the unstable relation between public record and private experience.

## Key Characteristics
- Canadian poet and novelist working across several literary modes.
- Author of [[TheBlindAssassinNovel|《盲刺客》]], discussed as a Booker-winning novel published in 2000.
- Uses nested genres and documents as functional parts of characterization and thematic argument.
- Places women's constrained speech, desire, violence, and memory at the center of narrative design.
- Treats speculative storytelling as a way to estrange and express real structures of domination.
