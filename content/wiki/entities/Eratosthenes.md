# Eratosthenes

## Overview

Eratosthenes was an Alexandrian scholar whose measurement of the Earth's circumference and work in geography and cartography provide the episode's strongest case that the Library and Museum supported major research beyond literary editing.

## Current Profile

[[361-the-lost-library-of-alexandria-glt1578679297]] uses Eratosthenes to qualify its own corrective. The institution was primarily Greek and literary, but it was not only a repository of poems: his geographical work shows how collected texts, measurement, comparison, and scholarly community could produce durable knowledge.

His achievement does not prove that Alexandria was on the threshold of modern experimental science or industrialization. It instead supplies a bounded example of sophisticated Hellenistic inquiry within the limits of the available source and institutional culture.

## Key Characteristics

- Scholar associated with the Alexandrian Museum and library.
- Major figure in the formation of geography and cartography.
- Known in the source for a remarkably accurate estimate of Earth's circumference.
- Counterexample to reducing Alexandrian scholarship entirely to literary editing.
- Evidence for intellectual achievement without implying a lost modern scientific revolution.

## Evidence

- Earth measurement: [[361-the-lost-library-of-alexandria-glt1578679297]] presents his circumference calculation as a major and unusually accurate achievement.
- Disciplinary breadth: [[361-the-lost-library-of-alexandria-glt1578679297]] connects him to geography and cartography alongside the institution's dominant literary work.
- Interpretive limit: [[361-the-lost-library-of-alexandria-glt1578679297]] contrasts genuine scholarly achievement with exaggerated claims that the library was close to modern science or industry.

## Qualifications

This profile is limited to the episode's use of Eratosthenes as an example. It does not reconstruct his full biography, methods, works, institutional titles, or the exact accuracy and transmission of his measurement.

## What Changed

- Established Eratosthenes as the clearest non-literary achievement in the episode's qualified account of Alexandrian scholarship.
