# Jean-Baptiste Drouet

## Overview

Jean-Baptiste Drouet was the Saint-Menu postmaster whose recognition of [[LouisXVI|Louis XVI]] helped local authorities stop the royal family's attempted escape at Varennes in June 1791.

## Current Profile

The source presents Drouet as a politically consequential local actor rather than a passive witness. After recognizing [[MarieAntoinette|Marie Antoinette]] and matching Louis's face to the image on paper money, he warned officials that the royal party might be heading toward foreign-backed forces. He and Jean Guillaume rode ahead, alerted Varennes, and insisted on the travelers' identity despite apparently valid papers, giving municipal officials and the National Guard time to delay and verify the party.

## Key Characteristics

- Postmaster at Saint-Menu during [[FlightToVarennes|the Flight to Varennes]].
- Recognized the royal couple during a routine horse change.
- Used the king's image on paper money as a practical identification aid.
- Interpreted the flight through fear of counterrevolution and foreign military support.
- Rode ahead to Varennes and pressed local officials to stop the travelers.

- Stopping the party reflected local institutions and collective mobilization as well as individual initiative.
