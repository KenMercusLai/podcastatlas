# Michael Kilgard

## Overview
Michael Kilgard is presented as a University of Texas at Dallas neuroscientist studying adult [[Neuroplasticity]], cortical maps, neuromodulator timing, and [[PairedVagusNerveStimulation]] for targeted rehabilitation.

## Current Profile
In [[how-to-rewire-your-brain-learn-faster-dr-michael-kilgard-scim4914799004]], Kilgard connects cellular learning rules to practical and clinical change. He argues that attention, active experience, feedback, reflection, and sleep determine which circuits are eligible for modification, while precisely timed acetylcholine, norepinephrine, serotonin, or stimulation can bias the active network. His clinical work is represented through vagus stimulation paired with stroke, spinal-cord, PTSD, or tinnitus therapy, with efficacy kept indication-specific and bounded by trials rather than generalized into consumer brain enhancement.

## Key Characteristics
- Explains adult learning through synaptic timing, active circuits, feedback, and neuromodulatory gates rather than passive exposure or one-chemical slogans.
- Favors rich, interactive, real-world learning while treating transfer from highly engineered digital environments as an empirical question.
- Develops [[PairedVagusNerveStimulation]] as a targeted way to reinforce selected rehabilitation events.
- Separates circuit-level disorders and therapies from simple neurotransmitter-deficiency explanations.
- Emphasizes controlled trials, modest effects, off-target harms, and slow translation when interventions can affect memory, personality, and identity.

## Evidence
- Learning model - [[how-to-rewire-your-brain-learn-faster-dr-michael-kilgard-scim4914799004]] presents a four-factor account combining pre-post synaptic timing with short neuromodulatory windows.
- Targeted stimulation - [[how-to-rewire-your-brain-learn-faster-dr-michael-kilgard-scim4914799004]] describes vagus stimulation paired with successful movement or selected tones rather than continuous nonspecific activation.
- Translation boundary - [[how-to-rewire-your-brain-learn-faster-dr-michael-kilgard-scim4914799004]] contrasts indication-specific recovery results with failed fluoxetine translation and variable tinnitus benefit.
