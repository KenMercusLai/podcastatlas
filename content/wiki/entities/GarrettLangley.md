# Garrett Langley

Garrett Langley is the founder and CEO of [[FlockSafety|Flock Safety]] in [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-flock-ceo-garrett-langley-on-controversy-surveillance-state-claims-and-privacy-vs-safety-42470485]]. [[JasonCalacanis|Jason Calacanis]] interviews him about license-plate-reader controversy, "surveillance state" criticism, retention defaults, police misuse, AI caution, city governance, drones, and the boundary between privacy and safety.

Langley's position in the episode is that Flock is a public-safety company, not a generalized surveillance company. He says the license-plate-reader product takes still images of vehicles rather than video, does not use facial recognition, does not look inside cars, does not search for people, and recently moved its default retention from 30 days to seven days.

His governance argument is procedural as much as technical. He says Flock usually works through city councils, treats stored data as a liability, has made [[PoliceDataAccessAudit|audit assistance]] mandatory, wants humans in the loop for AI, and uses transparency portals as part of the oversight story for [[DroneAsFirstResponder|drone-as-first-responder]] deployments.

## Key Claims
- Langley says Flock was started to help people live in safer neighborhoods.
- He claims Flock has helped solve more than one million crimes, find more than 10,000 missing people, and operate in more than 6,000 U.S. cities; the wiki treats these as company claims from the interview.
- He argues that a narrow [[AutomaticLicensePlateReader|automatic license-plate-reader]] architecture, shorter retention, and auditing reduce but do not eliminate [[CivilLibertiesSurveillanceRisk|civil-liberties surveillance risk]].
- He says public resistance often combines concern about Flock with distrust of police departments.
- He rejects predictive-policing automation and says emergency AI features should retain [[HumanJudgmentUnderAI|human judgment]].
