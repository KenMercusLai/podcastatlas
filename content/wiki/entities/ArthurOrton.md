# Arthur Orton

## Overview

Arthur Orton was the working-class butcher identified in the episode as the man who claimed to be the missing aristocrat Roger Tichborne.

## Current Profile

[[400-victorian-britains-maddest-mystery-glt4466006716]] portrays Orton's claim as implausible but socially powerful. His appearance, accent, lack of French, errors, Wapping connections, and missing tattoos contradicted Roger's identity, yet Roger's mother accepted him and [[AndrewBogle]] supported him. Popular subscriptions and [[EdwardKeneally]]'s campaign then made him a class symbol whose appeal no longer depended on consistent aristocratic identity.

## Key Characteristics

- Emerged from Australia claiming to be Roger Tichborne.
- Failed tests of language, education, memory, biography, and bodily identification.
- Received recognition and financial support from Roger's mother.
- Benefited from working-class subscription funding and mass sympathy.
- Was imprisoned after the criminal case, later confessed for money, and retracted the confession.

## Evidence

- Identity contradictions: [[400-victorian-britains-maddest-mystery-glt4466006716]] records wrong names, absent French, Wapping inquiries, physical disparity, and missing tattoos.
- Social support: [[400-victorian-britains-maddest-mystery-glt4466006716]] connects maternal recognition, Bogle, subscriptions, rallies, and courtroom audiences.
- Aftermath: [[400-victorian-britains-maddest-mystery-glt4466006716]] describes imprisonment, confession and retraction, poverty, and burial under Roger's name.

## Qualifications

The profile follows the episode's identification of the claimant as Orton rather than independently reconstructing every dispute. His motives and the mother's reasons for recognition remain interpretive.
