## Overview

姜公 / Jiang Gong is a late-Han Pengcheng recluse in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-849-sushi-ziyou-du-de-fanpangzhuan-jiujing-shuo-de-shui-ltquglqzvezeyp0mqxwgwnlp12lf|Hanji 849]]. The episode places him among five scholars recommended by [[ChenFanLateHan|陈蕃]] to [[EmperorHuanOfHan|汉桓帝]], but his distinctive profile is fraternal and filial conduct joined to refusal of office.

## Current Profile

Jiang Gong's current evidence is source-bounded but vivid. He and his brothers Jiang Zhonghai and Jiang Jijiang are poor, sleep under one quilt, and gain local fame for filial and fraternal conduct. When Jiang Gong and Jiang Jijiang meet robbers, each asks to die in place of the other; the robbers spare them, later repent, return the stolen property, and receive Jiang Gong's hospitality rather than prosecution.

The same source uses Jiang Gong's refusal to make reclusion concrete. Official invitations do not move the brothers, and Huan's wish to see or depict Jiang Gong is thwarted when Jiang Gong hides in a dark room under bedding and prevents a painter from seeing his face.

## Key Characteristics

- Late-Han recluse whose worth is shown through filial and fraternal conduct rather than office.
- Poor household figure whose brothers share hardship without breaking family solidarity.
- Non-retaliatory victim whose conduct makes robbers repent instead of producing a prosecution story.
- Imperial-summons refuser who avoids even portrait capture when Huan tries indirect recognition.
- Source case for reclusion as moral independence rather than social isolation.
