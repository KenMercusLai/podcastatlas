# Queen Emma of Hawaii

## Overview

Queen Emma of Hawaii was the wife of [[KamehamehaIV]], a royal widow, philanthropist, Anglican patron, international traveler, and unsuccessful candidate in Hawaii's 1874 royal election.

## Current Profile

[[12-days-reconquest-of-spain-and-the-old-queen-of-hawaii-glt9407596415]] presents Emma as a Native Hawaiian aristocrat formed within both Hawaiian kinship and English missionary-medical influence. Adopted by an aunt and the English physician Thomas Rooke, she grew up in an English-style household before marrying Kamehameha IV.

The death of their son Albert in 1862 and of Kamehameha IV in 1863 moved Emma from consort to widowed public actor. She supported a hospital, education, and the Anglican Church in Hawaii, then reinforced her international position through travel and a warm relationship with Queen Victoria. Her 1874 candidacy made that British-facing prestige politically relevant, but Kalakaua won the legislative election and Emma remained remembered as the “old queen.”

## Key Characteristics

- Hawaiian aristocrat whose upbringing combined Indigenous rank with English household influence.
- Queen consort and partner in Kamehameha IV's British-facing royal policy.
- Bereaved mother and widow who redirected public life toward health, education, and religion.
- Philanthropic founder associated with hospital care during a period of severe disease pressure.
- Devout Anglican patron linked to the establishment of the Church of Hawaii.
- International royal figure connected to Queen Victoria and European courts.
- Popular but unsuccessful candidate in the 1874 royal succession election.
