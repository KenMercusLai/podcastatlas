# Louis-Charles / Louis XVII

## Overview

Louis-Charles was the young son of [[LouisXVI|Louis XVI]] and [[MarieAntoinette|Marie Antoinette]] whom royalists recognized as Louis XVII after his father's execution. The Republic treated him as politically dangerous because his inherited identity could anchor Bourbon restoration despite his age and lack of practical power.

## Current Profile

While imprisoned in the Temple, Marie Antoinette continued to treat Louis-Charles as king and educate him within Catholic and dynastic practice. Royalists, foreign monarchies, and the [[VendeeWar|Vendée rebellion]] could invoke him as the legitimate sovereign. In July 1793 guards forcibly separated him from his family, and [[JacquesHebert|Jacques Hébert]] placed him under Antoine Simon for a political re-education intended to replace royal identity with sans-culotte identity. A statement signed by the boy alleged incest by his mother; he later endured severe isolation, filth, illness, and neglect. He died in June 1795 at age ten without knowing that his mother had already been executed.

## Key Characteristics

- He possessed little personal agency but great symbolic value as the surviving male Bourbon heir.
- Marie Antoinette's recognition of him as Louis XVII preserved family purpose and dynastic continuity inside the Temple.
- Republican use of “Louis-Charles Capet” rejected the royal identity recognized by restoration movements.
- Forced separation, changed appearance, revolutionary instruction, profanity, service, and use of his signed statement attempted to remake rather than openly execute him.
- Later isolation and neglect destroyed his health and ended the strongest immediate dynastic focus for restoration.

## Qualifications

- The source explicitly warns that royalist accounts shaped parts of Simon's later reputation and the prison narrative.
- Non-royalist testimony is presented as support for severe mistreatment, but exact scenes, dialogue, motives, and day-to-day conditions remain source-scoped.
- Calling him Louis XVII states a royalist dynastic claim rather than recognition by the French Republic.
- The source does not provide a complete medical account of his illness or death.

## What Changed

- A canonical profile now distinguishes the child's lack of practical power from the political force of his inherited identity.

## Relationships

- [[MarieAntoinette]] - mother who treated him as king in captivity and was forcibly separated from him.
- [[LouisXVI]] - executed father whose death made him Louis XVII to royalists.
- [[JacquesHebert]] - Commune official who directed his removal and political re-education.
- [[VendeeWar]] - royalist conflict in which his claimed kingship could unify restoration politics.
