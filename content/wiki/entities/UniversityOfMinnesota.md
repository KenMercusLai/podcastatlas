# University of Minnesota

## Overview
The University of Minnesota appears in the wiki as a public research university attached to both frontier synthetic biology and premium produce commercialization.

## Current Profile
The university's current profile is source-bounded but now has two distinct branches. In one, [[KateAdamalla]]'s lab anchors [[SyntheticSpudCells]] and the [[LifeDefinitionSpectrum]] question about whether lab-built cell-like systems count as living. In the other, [[Honeycrisp]] makes the university a central example of [[PublicAgriculturalResearchCommercialization]]: a university-bred apple released in 1991 became a consumer brand and generated more than $20 million through licensing.

## Key Characteristics
- Public research university used as an institutional anchor for synthetic-cell research.
- Originating institution for [[Honeycrisp]], the episode's benchmark premium apple variety.
- Demonstrates that public research can produce both scientific boundary cases and commercially valuable agricultural products.
- Its agricultural role depends on licensing and branded variety value rather than direct farming.
- Its wiki profile remains institutional and source-scoped, not a general university history.

## Evidence
- Synthetic-biology branch: [[its-not-easy-being-green-zack-polanski-6a82d1a0ff328abd84724cf0|It's not easy being Green]] says [[KateAdamalla]]'s lab made synthetic "spud cells" from chemicals, with no biological ancestors and limited reproduction.
- Life-definition role: [[its-not-easy-being-green-zack-polanski-6a82d1a0ff328abd84724cf0|It's not easy being Green]] uses the university lab to make [[LifeDefinitionSpectrum]] and bottom-up biological engineering legible.
- Honeycrisp origin: [[branded-fruit-how-produce-got-honeycrisp-ified|Branded fruit]] identifies Honeycrisp as a University of Minnesota apple released in 1991.
- Licensing value: [[branded-fruit-how-produce-got-honeycrisp-ified|Branded fruit]] says the university licensed Honeycrisp around a dollar per tree and earned more than $20 million.

## Qualifications
The sources do not justify a broad institutional profile. They show two named research-commercialization contexts: synthetic spud cells and Honeycrisp. The Honeycrisp revenue figure remains source-scoped.
