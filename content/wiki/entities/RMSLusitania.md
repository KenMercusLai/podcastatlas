# RMS Lusitania

## Overview

RMS Lusitania was the Cunard passenger liner sunk by German submarine U-20 off Ireland on 7 May 1915, as presented in [[673-the-first-world-war-the-submarine-strikes-part-3-glt3632974949]].

## Current Profile

The source presents Lusitania as both a symbol of civilian maritime prestige and a legally contested wartime object. It was a fast, luxurious Atlantic liner sailing from New York to Liverpool with passengers and crew, but it also had prewar state subsidy conditions for possible wartime conversion and carried cargo with military relevance. That dual status made the sinking politically explosive: Germany could argue military connection and contraband, while Britain and the United States focused on the killing of passengers without warning.

Its destruction is therefore not only a disaster story. Lusitania becomes the source case where [[UnrestrictedSubmarineWarfare]], [[PassengerLinerAtrocityPropaganda]], and [[NeutralityToInterventionMemory]] meet.

## Key Characteristics

- Prestigious Cunard liner associated with British maritime speed, luxury, and Atlantic passenger service.
- Civilian passenger vessel carrying 1,264 passengers and 702 crew when it left New York on 1 May 1915.
- War-relevant object because of government subsidy conditions and cargo including ammunition, shell components, fuses, metals, and industrial material.
- Target of U-20 off Ireland on 7 May 1915 after Germany had declared waters around Britain and Ireland a war zone.
- Disaster case in which rapid sinking, power failure, severe list, and lifeboat failure produced mass civilian casualties.
- Propaganda object contested by British atrocity framing, German legal-military justification, censorship, and later conspiracy theories.
- Memory marker that helped make Germany's 1917 return to unrestricted submarine warfare resonate in the United States.

## Evidence

- Ship status and voyage: [[673-the-first-world-war-the-submarine-strikes-part-3-glt3632974949]] describes Lusitania as a Cunard liner launched in 1906, sailing from New York to Liverpool, and still carrying civilians despite Germany's war-zone warning.
- Contested wartime status: [[673-the-first-world-war-the-submarine-strikes-part-3-glt3632974949]] notes possible armed-merchant-cruiser conversion conditions and cargo with military relevance, while preserving the episode's refusal to treat this as a sufficient justification for killing passengers without warning.
- Sinking mechanics and casualties: [[673-the-first-world-war-the-submarine-strikes-part-3-glt3632974949]] follows the torpedo strike, second explosion, blackout, lifeboat failure, 18-minute sinking, and common casualty figures of 767 rescued and 1,194 killed.
- Political afterlife: [[673-the-first-world-war-the-submarine-strikes-part-3-glt3632974949]] connects the sinking to German and British propaganda, Wilson's protest notes, and later American intervention memory.

## Qualifications

The page is source-bounded to one podcast episode. It does not settle the full legal history of contraband, the exact physical cause of the second explosion, or every Admiralty and Room 40 decision before the sinking. The episode rejects secret-explosives and deliberate-sacrifice conspiracy theories while still treating cargo secrecy and wartime censorship as real reasons the disaster remained contested.
