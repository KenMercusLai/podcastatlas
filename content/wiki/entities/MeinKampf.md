# Mein Kampf

## Overview

*Mein Kampf* was the prison-era book through which [[AdolfHitler]] consolidated a political self-narrative and expressed antisemitism, eastern expansion, Russian conquest, and “living space” after the failed [[BeerHallPutsch|Beer Hall Putsch]].

## Current Profile

The episode places the book inside the political conversion of failure into preparation. After a sympathetic trial and imprisonment at Landsberg, Hitler dictated the work to Emil Maurice and Rudolf Hess after publisher Max Amann encouraged a book project. The result joined autobiography and movement mythology to ideological claims about Jews, Russia, empire, and territorial expansion.

Its significance in this source is not only textual. Writing the book coincided with Hitler's movement from the self-image of a “drummer” for another leader toward the claim that he himself was the indispensable Führer. The book therefore belongs to the same prison-era consolidation as the strategic turn toward gaining power through democratic mechanisms.

## Key Characteristics

- Prison-era political and autobiographical text dictated after the 1923 putsch.
- Vehicle for Hitler's antisemitic worldview.
- Text linking Russia, eastern empire, and living space.
- Part of the post-putsch construction of Hitler's leader identity.
- Ideological bridge between failed coup politics and later expansionary rule.
