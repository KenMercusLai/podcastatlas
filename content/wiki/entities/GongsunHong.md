## Overview

公孙弘 / Gongsun Hong is a Western Han official whose current profile combines tactful policy influence, political rivalry, and conservative credibility screening. He can preserve room to advise [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] by narrowing rather than directly rejecting imperial priorities, but the Jiaoxi appointment episode presents him as willing to route a scholarly rival into a dangerous office, while Hanji 323-2 presents him as distrusting [[BuShiWesternHan|卜式]]'s unconventional donation proposal.

## Current Profile

Hanji 925 makes Gongsun Hong a positive case for [[StrategicCompromiseAsAgency|策略性妥协保全机会]]. When Han Wudi wants both pressure against the [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] and southwest development, Gongsun Hong avoids flat contradiction and instead narrows the priority problem. His compromise preserves access and future influence under a ruler with strong preferences.

Hanji 367-1 adds a darker use of institutional positioning. The episode says Gongsun Hong resents [[DongZhongshu|董仲舒]] and recommends him as chancellor to [[LiuDuanWesternHan|胶西王刘端]], whose violence toward senior officials makes the post dangerous. The appointment does not kill Dong because Liu Duan respects his scholarship, but Dong still withdraws through illness rather than trust that protection.

Hanji 323-2 supplies a different gatekeeping case. When Bu Shi, still outside office, asks to donate half his property to the state, Gongsun Hong reportedly treats the conduct as abnormal and advises Wudi not to accept it. The host explains the suspicion through Bu Shi's low origin and lack of background. This adds status-filtered disbelief to Gongsun Hong's profile, but the episode does not show that pedigree was his sole reason or that caution toward an unusual offer was inherently irrational.

Together, the sources make Gongsun Hong politically adaptive but morally ambivalent. The same skill in reading incentives that protects his own agency can also be used to expose a rival through formally legitimate appointment or to reject an unconventional candidate before demonstrated service changes the court's judgment.

## Key Characteristics

- Tactful adviser who reframes conflicts instead of opposing imperial desire head-on.
- Resource-priority thinker who argues against dividing attention across simultaneous strategic fronts.
- Survival-aware court actor who protects access and influence through calibrated speech.
- Politically ambivalent rival who can use appointment as an indirect exposure mechanism.
- Institutional gatekeeper whose suspicion of Bu Shi is interpreted as filtering credibility through status and normal career expectations.

## Qualifications

All three sources are popular-history interpretations. Hanji 925 does not reconstruct the full policy debate over Xiongnu and southwest development, while Hanji 367-1 does not independently establish every motive behind Gongsun Hong's recommendation. The rivalry and attempted-removal reading is strongly tied to the episode and transmitted biographical tradition, but the page does not infer a failed murder plan from the appointment alone. Hanji 323-2 preserves the rejection of Bu Shi's proposal more securely than its broad claim that status bias explains the reaction; fraud risk, precedent, political signaling, and other institutional concerns are not independently tested.

## What Changed

- Added Gongsun Hong's rejection of Bu Shi's unconventional wealth-donation proposal.
- Qualified the host's status-bias explanation against other possible reasons for institutional caution.
- Extended his gatekeeping profile from policy advice and rival placement to candidate credibility assessment.
