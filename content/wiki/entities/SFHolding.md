# SF Holding / 顺丰控股

SF Holding / 顺丰控股 appears in [[vol-127-nianbaoji-zhong-de-zhenshi-zhongguo-2025-lqjy2pnfy09zyewj0ljxjuc3gxqv]] as the logistics infrastructure behind Chinese consumer-brand internationalization. The source cites SF's annual-report discussion of supporting coffee, tea-drink, and other consumer brands as they open stores in Singapore and multiple overseas markets.

The episode uses SF to make a structural point: outbound brands do not globalize only through marketing or product taste. They need procurement, warehousing, cold chain, replenishment, fulfillment, and cross-border operating routines, making logistics an invisible layer of [[GlobalResourceAllocationCompany]] and [[ThreeLayerScaleEconomies]].

[[165-nianbaoji-zhong-de-zhenshi-zhongguo-2026-lpredevu-gakn92dwutmulytmslo]] adds SF as both a supply-chain-language and overseas-localization case. The source highlights SF's ordering of safety, resilience, and efficiency, then cites its support for tea-drink brands opening and operating abroad through labels, customs, consolidation, warehousing, and full-chain distribution.

## Source Position
- SF is treated as a platform-like enabler of consumer-chain expansion abroad.
- Its logistics role helps explain why [[MixueBingcheng]]-style and other Chinese beverage brands can move quickly overseas.
- The source links logistics capacity to China's broader corporate globalization, not only parcel delivery.
- Episode 165 treats SF as evidence that [[LocalizedGlobalCompany]] requires logistics, compliance, and local replenishment infrastructure.
- SF extends the episode's claim that supply chains can become exportable infrastructure.
