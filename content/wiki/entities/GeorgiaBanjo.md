# Georgia Banjo

## Overview

Georgia Banjo is an [[EconomistPodcasts]] journalist whose wiki profile spans British technology policy and, through the Shire/Shia Folk trailer, investigation of [[TommyRobinson]]'s political influence.

## Current Profile

Banjo's first wiki branch examines whether AI can become a positive post-Brexit opportunity for the [[UnitedKingdom]]. She reports that British tech startups have raised more than GBP14.5bn in venture capital so far this year and that Britain has 33 AI unicorns, more than France, Germany, and the Netherlands combined.

The Palantir branch complicates that optimism. Banjo presents Palantir's British public-sector footprint as a case where U.S. technology may solve real data-fragmentation problems for the [[NHS]], armed forces, and police, while also raising concerns about surveillance politics, vendor lock-in, procurement process, and digital sovereignty.

The Shire/Shia Folk trailer adds a political-investigation branch. Banjo says she spent six months investigating how [[TommyRobinson]] became a prophet for some powerful people in America, connecting British media exclusion, anti-Islam threat framing, [[ElonMusk]]'s rally amplification, and the [[AmericanRightBritainWarningNarrative|American right's Britain-as-warning narrative]].

## Key Characteristics

- The Intelligence journalist covering British AI and technology policy.
- Analyst of post-Brexit regulatory space, including the possibility that lighter rules could support AI startups.
- Reporter of British AI strengths, including venture funding and unicorn counts.
- Interpreter of the limits of regulatory freedom, since companies selling into the [[EuropeanUnion]] still face EU rules.
- Analyst of public-sector technology dependence through the Palantir-in-Britain case.
- Investigator of Robinson's transatlantic symbolic role in British and American right-wing politics.
