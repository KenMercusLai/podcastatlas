## Overview

庞参 / Pang Can now appears as a late-Han official whose profile spans early Qiang-front surrender management, a later failed campaign with punishment exposure, and a still later taiwei household scandal. The shared pattern is that prior credibility and high rank do not protect a public actor when frontier execution, command coordination, or household control fails.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-787-chuanqi-huanghou-yanji-shi-ruhe-yibubu-shangwei-de-lslngjnedmqgh8h5nfc-qmyahqnm|Hanji 787]] supplies the earliest current Pang Can layer. As Protector of Qiang, Pang uses favor and credibility to induce Qiang groups to surrender, sends their leader to court for reward, lets the leader return, moves the office back to Lingju, and reopens the route between the Hexi corridor and the interior.

The same source then gives a failure layer. Pang leads Qiang and Hu troops in a split advance alongside [[SimaJunLateHan|Sima Jun]], but is defeated after reaching east of Yongshi. Sima Jun's branch continues alone, [[DuJigongLateHan|Du Jigong]] uses feigned flight, Zhong Guang disobeys the grain-cutting task and pursues into danger, and more than three thousand troops are destroyed. Pang claims illness and withdraws, is summoned and imprisoned, but [[MaRongLateHan|Ma Rong]] argues that Pang and Liang Jin should be pardoned for future state service.

Hanji 789 overlaps with the failed-campaign layer but places the wider Qiang crisis in 116-117 CE. It pairs Pang Can with Sima Jun in a split operation against the Qiang-side field around [[LinChangQiang|Lin Chang]] and Du Jigong. The episode says Du defeats Pang Can's branch, while Sima Jun's short-lived success at Dingxi also collapses.

Hanji 815 supplies the later high-office layer. Pang Can is a reputed taiwei praised for loyalty and uprightness, but slander and court isolation make him vulnerable. His household then supplies decisive evidence: his current wife kills a son from his former wife by throwing him into a well. [[ZhuLiangLuoyangLateHan|Zhu Liang]] uses the case to impeach Pang, and [[EmperorShunOfHan|Emperor Shun]] dismisses him under a disaster-and-epidemic frame.

## Key Characteristics

- Holds public standing across frontier and high-court contexts, but neither context is immune to failure.
- Initially succeeds through trust-based Qiang surrender and administrative relocation back to Lingju.
- Suffers a later Qiang-front defeat that contrasts with Sima Jun's brief tactical opening and collapses into punishment exposure.
- Survives imprisonment after Ma Rong argues for pardoning capable failed commanders.
- Later becomes a taiwei whose moral reputation still cannot absorb household murder.
- Serves as a bridge between frontier-command failure and household-misconduct impeachment.

## Qualifications

The source layers are separated by time and office context. Hanji 787 gives both an early surrender success and a failed split operation; Hanji 789 overlaps with the failed-campaign branch while presenting a 116-117 CE chronology. The page does not force a final dating correction from podcast summaries alone. It also does not claim that the Qiang-front defeat caused the later taiwei fall; it treats them as bounded examples of Pang Can's public vulnerability under failed execution and failed household control.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 787's trust-based Qiang surrender, Lingju relocation, failed split advance, imprisonment, and Ma Rong pardon layer.
- Preserved Hanji 789 as an overlapping 116-117 CE Qiang-front defeat layer rather than forcing a settled chronology correction.
- Reframed Pang Can from a one-case household-liability profile into a broader public-failure profile spanning frontier and court.
