## Overview

庞参 / Pang Can now appears as a late-Han official whose profile spans an early proposal to abandon [[LiangzhouLateHan|Liangzhou]], Qiang-front surrender management, a later failed campaign with punishment exposure, and a still later taiwei household scandal. The shared pattern is that public standing does not protect a high official when strategic judgment, frontier execution, command coordination, or household control fails.

## Current Profile

Hanji 784 supplies the earliest current Pang Can layer. He proposes abandoning Liangzhou so the court can concentrate resources against northern threats, and [[DengZhiLateHan|Deng Zhi]] initially accepts the idea. [[YuXuEasternHan|Yu Xu]] blocks the policy by arguing that Liangzhou protects Sanfu, Chang'an, imperial tombs, western military manpower, and Han control over Qiang and Hu pressure. Pang Can therefore enters the current wiki not only as a frontier operator, but also as a negative example inside [[StrategicFrontierAbandonment|strategic frontier abandonment]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-787-chuanqi-huanghou-yanji-shi-ruhe-yibubu-shangwei-de-lslngjnedmqgh8h5nfc-qmyahqnm|Hanji 787]] supplies the next Pang Can layer. As Protector of Qiang, Pang uses favor and credibility to induce Qiang groups to surrender, sends their leader to court for reward, lets the leader return, moves the office back to Lingju, and reopens the route between the Hexi corridor and the interior.

The same source then gives a failure layer. Pang leads Qiang and Hu troops in a split advance alongside [[SimaJunLateHan|Sima Jun]], but is defeated after reaching east of Yongshi. Sima Jun's branch continues alone, [[DuJigongLateHan|Du Jigong]] uses feigned flight, Zhong Guang disobeys the grain-cutting task and pursues into danger, and more than three thousand troops are destroyed. Pang claims illness and withdraws, is summoned and imprisoned, but [[MaRongLateHan|Ma Rong]] argues that Pang and Liang Jin should be pardoned for future state service.

Hanji 789 overlaps with the failed-campaign layer but places the wider Qiang crisis in 116-117 CE. It pairs Pang Can with Sima Jun in a split operation against the Qiang-side field around [[LinChangQiang|Lin Chang]] and Du Jigong. The episode says Du defeats Pang Can's branch, while Sima Jun's short-lived success at Dingxi also collapses.

Hanji 815 supplies the later high-office layer. Pang Can is a reputed taiwei praised for loyalty and uprightness, but slander and court isolation make him vulnerable. His household then supplies decisive evidence: his current wife kills a son from his former wife by throwing him into a well. [[ZhuLiangLuoyangLateHan|Zhu Liang]] uses the case to impeach Pang, and [[EmperorShunOfHan|Emperor Shun]] dismisses him under a disaster-and-epidemic frame.

## Key Characteristics

- Holds public standing across frontier and high-court contexts, but neither context is immune to failure.
- Initially appears as an abandonment-policy voice whose Liangzhou proposal is rejected by Yu Xu.
- Later succeeds through trust-based Qiang surrender and administrative relocation back to Lingju.
- Suffers a Qiang-front defeat that contrasts with Sima Jun's brief tactical opening and collapses into punishment exposure.
- Survives imprisonment after Ma Rong argues for pardoning capable failed commanders.
- Later becomes a taiwei whose moral reputation still cannot absorb household murder.
- Serves as a bridge between strategic misjudgment, frontier-command failure, and household-misconduct impeachment.

## Qualifications

The source layers are separated by time and office context. Hanji 784 gives an abandonment-policy dispute, Hanji 787 gives both an early surrender success and a failed split operation, Hanji 789 overlaps with the failed-campaign branch while presenting a 116-117 CE chronology, and Hanji 815 supplies a later household-liability case. The page does not force a final dating correction from podcast summaries alone, and it does not claim that the Liangzhou proposal, Qiang-front defeat, and later taiwei fall are one direct causal chain.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 784's Liangzhou abandonment proposal as Pang Can's earliest current layer.
- Reframed Pang Can from a frontier-and-household profile into a broader public-failure profile spanning strategic advice, frontier execution, and household control.
- Preserved Hanji 787's trust-based Qiang surrender, failed split advance, imprisonment, and Ma Rong pardon layer.
- Preserved Hanji 789 as an overlapping 116-117 CE Qiang-front defeat layer rather than forcing a settled chronology correction.
