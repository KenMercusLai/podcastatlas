# Sirens

## Overview

The Sirens are the singing death-lure that [[Odysseus]] survives by combining curiosity, restraint, and crew-managed binding.

## Current Profile

In [[687-the-odyssey-hero-of-the-trojan-horse-part-1-glt2393125423]], Circe warns Odysseus that the Sirens lure sailors to death through irresistible song. Odysseus follows the advice by plugging his crew's ears with wax and having himself tied to the mast so he can hear them without steering the ship toward destruction.

## Key Characteristics

- The Sirens turn knowledge and pleasure into a navigational threat.
- Odysseus survives by separating perception from action: he hears while others control the ship.
- The crew's wax and mast-binding make restraint a practical system, not only a personal virtue.
- The episode treats the scene as one more test of homeward desire against seductive interruption.

## Evidence

- Lure to death: [[687-the-odyssey-hero-of-the-trojan-horse-part-1-glt2393125423]] says Circe warns that the Sirens' singing irresistibly draws sailors to destruction.
- Survival design: [[687-the-odyssey-hero-of-the-trojan-horse-part-1-glt2393125423]] describes wax in the crew's ears and Odysseus tied to the mast.
- Controlled curiosity: [[687-the-odyssey-hero-of-the-trojan-horse-part-1-glt2393125423]] frames Odysseus as the only man to hear the Sirens and survive.
