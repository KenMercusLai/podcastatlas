# Henry V / 《亨利五世》

## Overview
Henry V / 《亨利五世》 is the [[WilliamShakespeare|Shakespeare]] history play through which the sources examine ideal-king construction, Agincourt memory, and later patriotic or realist adaptation.

## Current Profile
The play turns English dynastic conflict and war memory into theatrical leadership mythology. It presents Henry as pious, martial, ambitious, reliable, and able to address common soldiers, while still carrying the stain of his father's usurpation and the later fragility of Lancastrian rule. Its breach speech, tennis balls, and foreign-quarrels formulation give later audiences a compact dramatic route into the invasion, but the historical episode repeatedly separates memorable scenes from secure evidence.

The Agincourt source sharpens the boundary between dramatic invention and inherited memory. Shakespeare's St Crispin speech magnified the legend, but a near-contemporary chaplain already reports Henry refusing to wish for more men and framing the English as God's humble few. Film versions show later reuse: Laurence Olivier's 1944 film becomes wartime morale theatre, while Kenneth Branagh's 1989 version restores mud, blood, exhaustion, and grime.

## Key Characteristics
- It is treated as the episode's strongest example of Shakespeare's mature history-play craft.
- It builds an ideal monarch while keeping succession anxiety in the background.
- Its battlefield speeches are remembered as English collective-morale texts.
- Famous scenes such as the tennis balls can preserve the atmosphere of contempt and escalation without being literal records.
- Its most famous outnumbered-army scene intensifies rather than wholly invents an earlier Henry tradition.
- Its modern film versions show how political mood changes adaptation choices.

## Qualifications
The sources show continuity between early Henry memory and Shakespeare but do not establish that every famous speech or scene is historical. The tennis-balls story and the claim that foreign war was primarily a domestic distraction remain interpretive. The play's ideal-king frame should also be held beside [[HenryVOfEngland|the historical Henry's]] coercive invasion and prisoner-killing order.

## What Changed
