# Henry V / 《亨利五世》

## Overview
Henry V / 《亨利五世》 is the [[WilliamShakespeare|Shakespeare]] history play the episode treats as one of his most successful mature history dramas and as the clearest case of ideal-king construction.

## Current Profile
In the source, Henry V matters because it turns English dynastic conflict and war memory into theatrical leadership mythology. The play presents Henry as pious, martial, ambitious, reliable, and able to address common soldiers, while still carrying the stain of his father's usurpation and the later fragility of Lancastrian rule. Film versions then show how later moments reuse the play: the 1944 Laurence Olivier version becomes wartime morale theatre, while the 1989 Kenneth Branagh version brings mud, blood, and historical grime back into the frame.

## Key Characteristics
- It is treated as the episode's strongest example of Shakespeare's mature history-play craft.
- It builds an ideal monarch while keeping succession anxiety in the background.
- Its battlefield speeches are remembered as English collective-morale texts.
- Its modern film versions show how political mood changes adaptation choices.
