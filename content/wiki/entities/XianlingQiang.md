## Overview

先零羌 / Xianling Qiang is a late-Han Qiang group whose current wiki evidence now stretches from a 113 CE Anding campaign notice and a 116 CE Dingxi defeat through later Huan- and Ling-era appearances in 160-161, 168, and 184 CE. The page remains source-bounded and does not treat every appearance as proof of one uninterrupted command organization.

## Current Profile

Hanji 786 supplies the earliest current layer. In 113 CE, Hou Ba and [[MaXianLateHan|Ma Xian]] attack a Xianling Qiang branch in [[AndingCommandery|Anding]]. The notice is brief, but it places the group inside the early An-era Deng-regency war field before the better-developed 116 CE Dingxi layer.

Hanji 789 gives the next current layer. During the Deng-regency Qiang crisis, [[RenShangLateHan|Ren Shang]] defeats Xianling Qiang forces near Dingxi while other Han commanders attack [[LinChangQiang|Lingchang / Lin Chang]] and [[DuJigongLateHan|Du Jigong]]. This places the name inside the long northwest-war background before the later Huan/Ling-era material.

Hanji 855 gives the next major layer: Xianling Qiang, [[ShendiQiang|Shendi Qiang]], and other Qiang groups attack Bingzhou and [[LiangzhouLateHan|Liangzhou]], prompting the court to send [[DuanJiongLateHan|Duan Jiong]] with the Five Camps. After [[HuangfuGuiLateHan|Huangfu Gui]] is appointed zhonglangjiang and defeats Qiang forces, Xianling and other Qiang groups surrender in large numbers because of his prestige and trustworthiness.

Hanji 881 places Xianling Qiang forces at [[FengyishanBattleLateHan|Fengyishan]], where Duan Jiong defeats them during the wider Eastern Qiang campaign context and the source reports more than 8,000 killed. Hanji 929 and Hanji 930 then show a later western disorder role: Xianling Qiang and related groups kill Ling Zheng, elect Beigong Boyu and Li Wenhou, and pull [[BianZhangLateHan|Bian Zhang]] plus [[HanSui|Han Sui]] into military-political management around Ji County.

## Key Characteristics

- Recurrent Qiang group name across several late-Han frontier crises rather than a fully reconstructed continuous polity.
- Earliest current appearance is a brief 113 CE Anding campaign notice.
- Appears in both battlefield defeat contexts and surrender or leadership-selection contexts.
- In 116 CE, functions as one branch of the wider Deng-regency Qiang emergency.
- In 160-168 CE, appears in the Huan-era command-failure, surrender, and Duan Jiong hard-line suppression field.
- In 184 CE, helps form the western uprising environment that draws in Bian Zhang and Han Sui.

## Qualifications

The sources are separated by decades and political contexts. The page treats "Xianling Qiang" as a recurring group label in the wiki evidence, not as a fully continuous leadership biography. Hanji 786 and Hanji 789 also arrive through transcripts with name and place caution, so the 113-116 CE layers should be corroborated by future notes if more detail is added.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 786 as the earliest current Xianling Qiang layer.
- Reframed the page so the 113 Anding notice now precedes the 116 Dingxi defeat.
- Preserved the warning against treating the 113, 116, 160-168, and 184 CE notices as one uninterrupted command structure.
