# Harry S. Truman

## Overview

Harry S. Truman appears in [[153-god-and-the-american-empire-glt8752091834]] as a U.S. president whose early Cold War policy joined strategic conflict, religious pluralism, biblical imagination, and a public opposition between faith and atheistic communism.

## Current Profile

The episode uses two cases. Truman reportedly sought a summit of world religious leaders as the Cold War hardened, exposing tension between constitutional church-state separation and a religiously framed national cause. It also presents his recognition of [[Israel]] alongside an identification with Cyrus as a liberator of the Jews, while noting that professional diplomats warned of consequences in the Arab world.

These examples support a bounded claim that religious understanding could shape Truman's political imagination. They do not establish that religion alone caused recognition of Israel or define his complete foreign policy.

## Key Characteristics

- Framed the emerging Cold War partly as a struggle between religion and irreligion.
- Sought interfaith mobilization while governing under constitutional church-state separation.
- Drew on biblical analogy in understanding the recognition of Israel.
- Balanced moral and religious identification against diplomatic warnings and strategic consequences.
