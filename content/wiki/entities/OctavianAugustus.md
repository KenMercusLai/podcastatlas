# Octavian / Augustus / 屋大维

## Overview

Octavian / Augustus / 屋大维 is [[JuliusCaesar|Caesar]]'s adopted heir, a teenage private-army organizer, triumvir, and civil-war victor who transforms Caesar's inheritance and the failed [[CaesarAssassination|assassination settlement]] into the [[RomanEmpire|Augustan imperial order]].

## Current Profile

The earlier Caesar source identifies Octavian as the unexpected heir who gives Caesar's memory a political vehicle and later succeeds where Caesar failed by presenting one-person rule less provocatively. [[305-the-fall-of-the-roman-republic-glt6995332578]] makes that ascent concrete. At eighteen, against family caution, he takes Caesar's name, claims the fortune, pays promised bequests, builds public support, and recruits approximately 3,000 armed followers.

[[Cicero]] and the Senate legalize Octavian's force for the Mutina campaign, hoping to use him against [[MarkAntony]]. When both consuls die, Octavian becomes the decisive commander near Rome, uses military pressure to obtain the consulship at nineteen, and then forms the [[SecondTriumvirate]] with Antony and Lepidus. He accepts Cicero's death, helps conduct the proscriptions, survives the [[BattleOfPhilippi|Philippi]] campaign despite illness and the loss of his camp, and later defeats Antony and [[CleopatraVII]]. As Augustus, he keeps autocratic power while presenting himself more modestly as a servant of Senate and republic.

## Key Characteristics

- Converts adoption, name, money, and promised public gifts into political legitimacy.
- Uses a privately recruited armed following before obtaining ordinary high office.
- Moves opportunistically between senatorial alliance and agreement with Antony and Lepidus.
- Accepts proscription and the sacrifice of Cicero as part of triumviral consolidation.
- Appears less active than Antony at Philippi but outlasts every major rival.
- Learns from Caesar's fate to clothe durable one-person rule in restrained republican presentation.

## Evidence

- Inheritance and contrast with Caesar: [[124-kaisa-zhisi-lishi-de-yunjiao-shifou-yi-shixiao-781894361]] presents Octavian as adopted heir, colder political operator, and eventual founder of imperial rule.
- Entry into politics: [[305-the-fall-of-the-roman-republic-glt6995332578]] describes his arrival in Italy, adoption of Caesar's name, payment of bequests, recruitment, and approach to Cicero.
- Office through force: [[305-the-fall-of-the-roman-republic-glt6995332578]] follows senatorial legalization, the consuls' deaths, the centurion's demand, and Octavian's election as consul.
- Triumviral and imperial path: [[305-the-fall-of-the-roman-republic-glt6995332578]] connects the proscriptions, Philippi, later victory over Antony and Cleopatra, and the Augustan settlement.

## Qualifications

The current sources emphasize political sequence more than full administration. The precise size and legal character of Octavian's early force, responsibility for individual deaths, his battlefield conduct at Philippi, and the degree to which fear, calculation, or inheritance drove each choice remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Added the mechanisms of Octavian's teenage ascent: name, fortune, bequests, private force, senatorial recognition, and coerced consulship.
- Added his participation in the triumvirate, proscriptions, Philippi, and the later politics of restrained imperial presentation.

## Relationships

- [[JuliusCaesar]] - adoptive father whose name, fortune, and memory enable Octavian's entry.
- [[Cicero]] - temporary senatorial sponsor later sacrificed in the proscriptions.
- [[MarkAntony]] - enemy, triumviral ally, Philippi partner, and eventual defeated rival.
- [[SecondTriumvirate]] - extraordinary regime through which Octavian converts military leverage into shared legal power.
- [[BattleOfPhilippi]] - victory that removes Brutus and Cassius despite Octavian's illness and lost camp.
- [[RepublicanMemoryAgainstDictatorship]] - constraint that helps explain Augustus's less openly monarchical presentation.
- [[RomanEmpire]] - successor order consolidated under Augustus.
