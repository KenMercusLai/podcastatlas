# Mikhail Gorbachev / 米哈伊尔·戈尔巴乔夫

## Overview
Mikhail Gorbachev was the final leader of the [[SovietUnion]] and appears in the wiki through intelligence-mediated Western interpretation and nuclear diplomacy with [[RonaldReagan|Ronald Reagan]].

## Current Profile
[[81-jiandie-yu-pantu-shuwozhiyan-007-de-gushi-ruobaole-xia-693711209]] presents Gorbachev's British and American contacts through the interpretive value of [[OlegGordievsky|Oleg Gordievsky]], whose reporting helped Western leaders distinguish Soviet fear from simple aggression. [[312-reagan-iran-contra-and-the-cold-war-part-3-glt4042202229]] adds direct summit diplomacy: Gorbachev offered major nuclear reductions at Reykjavik, clashed with Reagan over strategic defense, and later signed the INF Treaty. Together, the sources show intelligence interpretation and leader-level bargaining as linked responses to [[ColdWarNuclearMisperception|nuclear misperception]].

## Key Characteristics
- A Soviet reform-era leader whose intentions and political possibilities were interpreted partly through intelligence sources.
- A negotiating counterpart willing to propose major nuclear reductions.
- A leader who treated Reagan's Strategic Defense Initiative as a central obstacle at Reykjavik.
- A participant in a relationship that moved U.S.-Soviet interaction from war-scare conditions toward verifiable arms reduction.

## Evidence
### Western interpretation
- [[81-jiandie-yu-pantu-shuwozhiyan-007-de-gushi-ruobaole-xia-693711209]] describes British trust in Gordievsky's advice around Gorbachev's visit and later American interest in how Gorbachev might read nuclear-disarmament commitments and strategic defense.

### Summit diplomacy
- [[312-reagan-iran-contra-and-the-cold-war-part-3-glt4042202229]] follows four Reagan-Gorbachev summits, the Reykjavik breakdown, the INF Treaty, and the opening of strategic-arms reductions.

## Qualifications
The sources cover Gorbachev mainly from a Western intelligence and Reagan-centered perspective, not as a complete account of Soviet reform, internal politics, or the end of the Cold War. Claims about private trust, individual causal importance, and what either leader could have achieved without domestic constraints remain interpretive.

## What Changed
- Added the four-summit arms-control sequence to the earlier intelligence-mediated profile.
- Clarified strategic defense as both a bargaining obstacle and part of the wider nuclear-risk environment.
- Connected adversary interpretation to later negotiated reductions.

## Relationships
- [[RonaldReagan]] - U.S. counterpart in late-Cold-War summit diplomacy.
- [[OlegGordievsky]] - intelligence source who shaped British and American interpretation of Soviet leadership.
- [[SovietUnion]] - state Gorbachev led during the diplomacy covered here.
- [[ColdWarNuclearMisperception]] - strategic risk that made interpretation and reassurance consequential.
- [[StrategicIntelligenceInterpretation]] - process by which Western leaders tried to understand Soviet intentions and fears.
