# Harfleur

## Overview

Harfleur is the Norman port whose capture opened [[HenryVOfEngland|Henry V's]] 1415 campaign but consumed enough time and manpower to turn victory into a dangerous march toward [[BattleOfAgincourt|Agincourt]].

## Current Profile

Before the invasion, the episode presents Harfleur as a state-backed privateering or pirate base threatening English shipping and the southern coast. The town was strategically attractive as a vulnerable port, a possible second Calais, and a base for recovering [[Normandy]], but its capture was neither quick nor clean. Henry and the Duke of Clarence sealed its western and eastern approaches, artillery breached old walls, and defenders repeatedly used rubble to repair damage. Marsh conditions, poor food, sanitation, and disease weakened the besiegers while bombardment damaged the stronghold Henry hoped to use.

Harfleur surrendered after a short final truce expired without French relief. Henry took the garrison prisoner, expelled old and infirm residents, and allowed women and children to remain or leave. The result supplied a visible victory and port, but not the rapid success needed for a larger 1415 conquest.

## Key Characteristics

- Vulnerable Norman port selected as Henry's first mainland objective.
- Maritime threat and prospective English bridgehead before the 1415 landing.
- Town enclosed from west and east despite tidal, river, mudflat, and marsh obstacles.
- Old defenses made resilient through rubble repairs after English artillery breaches.
- Siege environment that spread disease and imposed substantial English attrition.
- Captured stronghold damaged by the method required to take it.
- Costly victory that narrowed Henry's choices after surrender.

## Qualifications

The episodes' pirate-base framing, garrison estimate, and some surrender details are not independently audited here. Calling Harfleur weak describes its initial material position, not an effortless siege, and Henry's anti-plunder rules did not prevent bombardment, coercive surrender, or civilian displacement.
