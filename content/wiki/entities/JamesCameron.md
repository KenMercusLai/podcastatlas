# James Cameron

## Overview

James Cameron is a filmmaker represented in the wiki through two source-bounded roles: an example used to distinguish excellence from paralyzing perfectionism and the director whose 1997 *Titanic* became the dominant modern retelling of the disaster.

## Current Profile

The current profile first distinguishes high standards from paralysis. Cameron is cited as someone commonly called a perfectionist whose own framing is closer to doing what is correct, completing the work, and moving on, making him a source-scoped example of [[ImperfectActionPractice]] rather than [[PerfectionismAsAvoidance]].

The profile also includes large-scale filmmaking and cultural memory through his 1997 *Titanic*, an expensive, technically detailed production widely expected to fail but eventually becoming the highest-grossing film of its time. Its historical authority remains qualified: Cameron's film helped make Titanic culturally dominant while reinforcing the disputed claim that [[BruceIsmay]] pressured the ship to increase speed.

## Key Characteristics

- Filmmaker used as an example of excellence oriented toward correctness, completion, and moving on.
- Director of the dominant modern cinematic retelling of the [[RMSTitanic|Titanic]] disaster.
- Large-scale production leader associated with detailed physical reconstruction and exceptional cost.
- Cultural interpreter whose film reshaped public memory as well as dramatizing history.
- Example of how technical ambition can coexist with narrative simplification or historical error.

## Evidence

- Excellence and completion: [[129-ruhe-chengwei-zhenzheng-de-buwanmei-zhuyizhe-hunshen-shi-jiner-799220151]] uses Cameron to distinguish doing excellent work from refusing to finish until perfection is guaranteed.
- Production scale and reception: [[432-titanic-the-survivors-part-6-glt4613169046]] describes the Mexican studio lot, huge tank, detailed recreations, expectations of failure, and extraordinary box-office result.
- Historical-memory effect: [[432-titanic-the-survivors-part-6-glt4613169046]] treats the film as the dominant modern Titanic retelling and says its portrayal of [[BruceIsmay]] repeats a false speed-pressure accusation.

## Qualifications

The two sources do not provide a complete biography, filmography, or direct study of Cameron's working methods. The perfectionism discussion is an illustrative secondary mention, and the Titanic episode's judgments about production, historical accuracy, gendered reception, and cultural dominance remain episode-attributed.
