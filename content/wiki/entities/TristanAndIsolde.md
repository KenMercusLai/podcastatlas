# Tristan and Isolde

## Overview

*Tristan and Isolde* is [[RichardWagner]]'s music drama of illicit love, erotic longing, death, and annihilation, adapted from Arthurian legend and represented in the source through its climactic *Liebestod*.

## Current Profile

The episode presents the work as a love triangle among Tristan, Isolde, and King Mark transformed by a love potion and completed through death: Tristan dies in Isolde's arms, and Isolde follows through the *Liebestod*. Its continuous erotic and musical intensity makes love and death inseparable rather than sequential themes.

The work is also embedded in biography and patronage. The source associates its composition with Wagner's affair with [[MathildeWesendonck]], while [[LudwigIIOfBavaria|Ludwig II]] supplied the resources for its first production in 1865 after years of Wagner's inability to stage it.

## Key Characteristics

- Reworks Arthurian material into a music drama centered on illicit love and divided loyalty.
- Joins erotic longing to death, dissolution, and musical climax.
- Culminates in Isolde's *Liebestod* after Tristan's death.
- Is linked in the source to Wagner's relationship with Mathilde Wesendonck.
- Required royal patronage for its first production after a long staging delay.
