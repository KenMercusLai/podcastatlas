# Henry Kissinger

## Overview

Henry Kissinger appears as the U.S. negotiator of the [[ParisPeaceAccords]] and the secretary of state advising [[GeraldFord|Gerald Ford]] during South Vietnam's final collapse.

## Current Profile

The episode presents Kissinger as internally divided between accepting that South Vietnam could not be saved and fearing that abandonment would damage American credibility. He could tell Ford that Vietnam had to be put behind them while still pressing for aid or renewed bombing.

That tension also shapes the unresolved interpretation of the 1973 agreement. Kissinger may have helped construct a politically sufficient interval between U.S. withdrawal and South Vietnamese defeat, or he may have expected American retaliation to deter a decisive North Vietnamese advance.

## Key Characteristics

- Negotiated the Paris settlement with Lê Đức Thọ.
- Treated American credibility as a central consequence of South Vietnamese defeat.
- Alternated between accepting collapse and advocating aid or air power.
- Pressed Ambassador [[GrahamMartin|Graham Martin]] to prepare for evacuation.
- Helped define the final priority shift from broad allied rescue toward American extraction.

- [[324-fall-of-saigon-the-nightmare-begins-part-1-glt6047251362]] presents both the “sufficient interval” interpretation and the claim that Nixon and Kissinger expected retaliatory deterrence to work.

### Advice during collapse

- [[324-fall-of-saigon-the-nightmare-begins-part-1-glt6047251362]] traces Kissinger's movement between resignation, aid requests, bombing proposals, evacuation preparation, and American-priority triage.

## Qualifications

The source does not resolve Kissinger's private intention in 1973 or prove that requested aid and B-52 strikes would have preserved South Vietnam. Its judgments about credibility, betrayal, and personal inconsistency are interpretive and source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Established a profile centered on the tension between accepting defeat and defending American credibility.
- Kept cynical-exit and failed-deterrence readings of the Paris agreement simultaneously open.

## Relationships

- [[ParisPeaceAccords]] - settlement he negotiated as the United States withdrew ground forces.
- [[RichardNixon]] - president whose assurances and authority supported the agreement before Watergate.
- [[GeraldFord]] - president he advised during the final collapse.
- [[NguyenVanThieu]] - allied president who later blamed him for a fatal treaty.
- [[GrahamMartin]] - ambassador he pressed to prepare the Saigon evacuation.
- [[FallOfSaigon]] - defeat that made his credibility concerns and evacuation choices concrete.
