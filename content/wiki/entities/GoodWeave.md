# GoodWeave

## Overview
GoodWeave is the later name of Rugmark, the child-labour-free carpet certification initiative that [[KailashSatyarthi]] describes developing after direct rescue proved unable to stop a replenishing trafficking system.

## Current Profile
Within the bounded source, GoodWeave turns distant consumer concern into a practical market signal. Monitoring, labeling, certification, and supply-chain guarantees let buyers choose an affirmative alternative while creating incentives for exporters and importers to replace child labour with adult work. Its importance is institutional: it connects moral concern to repeatable verification and purchasing behavior.

## Key Characteristics
- Began under the name Rugmark and was later renamed GoodWeave.
- Uses monitoring, labeling, certification, and guarantees to signal carpets represented as free of child labour.
- Targets export-market demand rather than relying only on rescue, protest, exposure, or boycott.
- Is presented as potentially compatible with participating firms' commercial interests and adult employment.
- Faced resistance from actors benefiting from child labour.

## Qualifications
The source does not provide current certification standards, audit methodology, geographic scope, governance, finances, enforcement records, or an independent impact evaluation. The reported fall in child carpet workers cannot by itself identify GoodWeave's causal contribution, establish where all children went, or rule out displacement into unmonitored work.

## What Changed
- Created the profile as the organizational expression of a rescue-to-market strategy.
- Preserved Rugmark as the historical name rather than creating a duplicate entity.
- Separated the certification mechanism from unverified causal impact claims.
