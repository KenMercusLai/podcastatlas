# Siege of Warsaw (1939)

## Overview

The 1939 siege of Warsaw was the defense, encirclement, starvation, artillery bombardment, and aerial attack of the Polish capital during the [[InvasionOfPoland1939|invasion of Poland]], ending in surrender on 27 September.

## Current Profile

German armored forces reached Warsaw's outskirts on 8 September, but defenders and civilians organized by Mayor Stefan Starzyński built barricades and anti-tank defenses that stopped the first assault. Hitler then ordered the city isolated and pounded into submission while Germany refused a request to evacuate civilians.

[[532-hitlers-war-on-poland-the-fall-of-warsaw-part-3-glt6308788391]] uses Starzyński's 23 September broadcast to frame the gap between physical destruction and civic defiance. Repeated artillery and air attack destroyed homes, hospitals, schools, water, electricity, and streets; the major bombardment of 25 September preceded surrender two days later. The siege therefore joins a military objective to coercion of an urban population and prefigures wider occupation violence.

## Key Characteristics

- Civilian and military mobilization after German armored forces reached the capital.
- Failed first assault followed by encirclement, starvation, artillery, and air attack.
- Refusal of civilian evacuation and destruction of medical and urban infrastructure.
- Stefan Starzyński's broadcasts as symbols of civic endurance.
- Surrender under overwhelming coercion rather than the disappearance of Polish national resistance.

- Civilian coercion: [[532-hitlers-war-on-poland-the-fall-of-warsaw-part-3-glt6308788391]] connects encirclement and refused evacuation to bombardment of hospitals, schools, homes, utilities, and streets.
- Civic memory: [[532-hitlers-war-on-poland-the-fall-of-warsaw-part-3-glt6308788391]] centers Starzyński's broadcast and his later arrest and disappearance.
- Outcome: [[532-hitlers-war-on-poland-the-fall-of-warsaw-part-3-glt6308788391]] dates the surrender to 27 September and places it inside the wider partition and occupation of Poland.

## Qualifications

The source is a narrative synthesis rather than a complete operational or social history of the siege. Bomb tonnage, casualty figures, the precise allocation of targets, remembered scenes, Hitler's motives, and the later fate of individual participants remain source-scoped. The 1939 siege should not be conflated with the 1943 Warsaw Ghetto Uprising or the 1944 Warsaw Uprising.

## What Changed

- Established the siege as both an urban military defense and a campaign of civilian coercion.
- Added Starzyński's broadcast as the episode's principal memory frame.
- Explicitly separated the 1939 siege from Warsaw's later wartime uprisings.
