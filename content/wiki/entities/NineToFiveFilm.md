# 9 to 5 (1980 Film)

## Overview
9 to 5 is presented as the 1980 workplace comedy that converted [[NineToFiveOrganization|9to5]]'s clerical-worker movement into popular entertainment and made [[DollyParton]]'s song into a labor anthem.

## Current Profile
The episode treats the film as a bridge between office-worker organizing and mass recognition. Its plot turns the frustrations of women clerical workers into comic revenge against a male boss, then imagines concrete reforms such as flexible hours, child care, job sharing, and equal pay.

The film's production history matters because the episode says real office stories shaped the script. [[JaneFonda]] brought movement material and [[PatriciaResnick]] researched secretaries' work directly. The film then returned to the movement: screenings, rallies, and Parton's song helped expand 9to5's public reach.

## Key Characteristics
- Workplace comedy grounded in real clerical-worker organizing rather than a purely invented office story.
- Revenge fantasy that also stages concrete reforms workers wanted.
- Public representation vehicle for women office workers who had rarely seen themselves centered in popular culture.
- Media amplifier for [[NineToFiveOrganization|9to5]] chapters, rallies, and union expansion.
- Partial and qualified representation because the hosts note its focus on white women and the persistence of larger pay gaps for women of color.
