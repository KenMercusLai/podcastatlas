# Death of a Salesman / 《推销员之死》

## Overview
*Death of a Salesman* is [[ArthurMiller|Arthur Miller]]'s two-act play about [[WillyLoman|Willy Loman]], a declining salesman whose work, family authority, memory, and self-worth are bound to a belief that popularity guarantees success.

## Current Profile
The episode reads the play as a critique of individualized success under postwar prosperity shadowed by Depression memory. Willy has material alternatives, including offered work and family help, but cannot accept them without admitting that the model he taught his sons and used to justify his life has failed. His final attempt to leave insurance money therefore joins care, pride, self-deception, and the wish to remain economically useful.

The tragedy also depends on form. Present action, memory, and fantasy pass through one another without routine blackouts; scenery, music, lighting, and whether actors obey walls make Willy's collapsing temporal boundaries visible. This formal economy is part of the argument, not decoration around it.

## Key Characteristics
- Family tragedy in which an economic success creed becomes an intimate inheritance.
- Critique of popularity, contacts, occupational status, and effort as total measures of human worth.
- Drama whose protagonist has practical options but experiences ideological revision as annihilation of the self.
- Two-act structure that places present, recollection, and fantasy in one permeable stage space.
- Work that can travel beyond the U.S. salesman occupation through family aspiration, modernization, status anxiety, and generational conflict.
- Story whose surviving sons divide between accepting ordinariness and repeating the father's success project.

## Qualifications
