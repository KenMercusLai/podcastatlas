# Tomoe Gozen

## Overview
Tomoe Gozen is the female warrior whose brief appearance in [[TheTaleOfTheHeike]] opens [[661-dawn-of-the-samurai-the-shogun-triumphant-part-4-glt7837412375]] and anchors its discussion of history, legend, and [[OnnaMusha|onna-musha]].

## Current Profile
The episode presents Tomoe as likely historically grounded but heavily amplified by literary memory. She rides with [[KisoYoshinaka]] near the end of his collapse, remains among his last companions, kills a noted enemy captain, and then disappears from the narrative.

Her importance is not only biographical. Tomoe becomes evidence for a broader cultural space in which women from samurai families could learn riding, archery, and weapons, while later traditions try to explain whether she was servant, concubine, wife, commander, warrior survivor, captive, or nun.

## Key Characteristics
- Tomoe is remembered as exceptionally strong, skilled with bow and sword, and capable on horseback.
- Her final ride with Kiso makes her a literary emblem of loyalty and martial prowess.
- Her historical outline is plausible, but the details are filtered through [[TheTaleOfTheHeike]] and later legend.
- Her fame opens a wider discussion of female warriors in samurai culture.

## Evidence
- Warrior depiction: [[661-dawn-of-the-samurai-the-shogun-triumphant-part-4-glt7837412375]] cites [[TheTaleOfTheHeike]]'s description of Tomoe's beauty, strength, battlefield skill, and command ability.
- Final ride: [[661-dawn-of-the-samurai-the-shogun-triumphant-part-4-glt7837412375]] says she remains with Kiso after most companions are gone, kills a strong enemy captain, and leaves only after Kiso orders her away.
- Gender frame: [[661-dawn-of-the-samurai-the-shogun-triumphant-part-4-glt7837412375]] connects Tomoe to girls from samurai families learning riding and archery, the naginata's association with women, and archaeological evidence of female warrior remains.

## Qualifications
The source says later traditions disagree about Tomoe's exact identity and fate, and that there is no contemporary evidence for the claim that she commanded troops at Kurikara Pass.
