## Overview

崔氏 / Cui Shi is the wife of [[ZhuMaichenWesternHan|朱买臣]] in the episode's retelling of a *Book of Han* account and the later “朱买臣休妻” performance tradition. Her personal name is not supplied, and this page uses the episode's label while disambiguating her from other people called Cui Shi.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-286-canbei-wujie-qiannian-zhumaichen-xiuqi-de-fanzhuan-zhenxiang-1-lpvzvzomh6u6zbr1ispaiycenl3f|Hanji 286 part 1]] presents Cui as a spouse who has already shared prolonged poverty and contributed materially to the household. Her conflict with Zhu centers on his recitation while carrying firewood, his limited provision, and his confidence that advancement will arrive at fifty. She leaves because she does not accept his forecast or way of life.

The episode uses this account to challenge the later dramatic stereotype of a shallow, status-seeking wife who returns only after Zhu becomes governor. Its present interpretation is incompatibility under economic pressure, not established greed or infidelity. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-286-canbei-wujie-qiannian-zhumaichen-xiuqi-de-fanzhuan-zhenxiang-2-lrgdpir3ez-hk_eptcq8q3-pzozr|Hanji 286 part 2]] strengthens that qualification: after remarriage, Cui reportedly encounters Zhu hungry and cold and gives him a meal. Separation therefore does not erase residual concern, although the scene and its emotional meaning remain episode-attributed.

## Key Characteristics

- Shares years of poverty with Zhu Maichen before leaving the marriage.
- Is presented as contributing to the household's material survival.
- Values steady, practical work over Zhu's public recitation and promised future advancement.
- Rejects Zhu's prediction that he will become prosperous at fifty.
- Reportedly feeds Zhu after remarriage when she sees him in severe hardship.
- Becomes the object of a durable negative reputation in later “马前泼水” performance tradition.

## Qualifications

The supplied sources do not establish Cui's personal name, independent testimony, legal marital procedure, exact economic contribution, motives, remarriage circumstances, or later death. “崔氏,” jealousy, “休夫,” and “三观不合” are episode labels or interpretations rather than a complete verified biography. Part 2's meal scene complicates a hostility narrative but does not by itself establish remorse, reconciliation, or the full later sequence.
