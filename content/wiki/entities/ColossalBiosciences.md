# Colossal Biosciences

## Overview
Colossal Biosciences is a biotechnology company represented in the wiki through [[BethShapiro]]'s discussion of de-extinction programs and conservation applications.

## Current Profile
In the episode, Colossal organizes mammoth, thylacine, dodo, and dire-wolf work as a cross-taxon technology program. Placental mammals, marsupials, and birds require different cellular and reproductive methods, so the flagship species function as development platforms for genome engineering, cell rejuvenation, reproduction, and genotype-to-phenotype mapping.

The company's stated conservation case is that this shared toolkit can also aid living species. Examples include proposed toxin tolerance in northern quolls, cloning and ancestry restoration in endangered canids, black-footed-ferret cloning and disease resistance, and advisory processes for possible releases. The episode also presents public fascination with charismatic extinct animals as a funding and communication engine, while leaving commercial incentives, independent validation, ecological outcomes, and terminology open to scrutiny.

## Key Characteristics
- Develops biotechnology programs across placental mammals, marsupials, and birds.
- Uses comparative genomics and selected edits to pursue extinct-associated traits rather than exact replicas.
- Reuses de-extinction methods for endangered-species genetic rescue.
- Combines laboratory development with animal care, ecological assessment, and stakeholder advisory processes.
- Uses high-profile species to attract capital, talent, and public attention to conservation technology.

## Evidence
- Platform design - [[bringing-extinct-species-back-to-life-dr-beth-shapiro-scim2211492808]] links mammoth, thylacine, and dodo programs to distinct reproductive and taxonomic problems.
- Trait engineering - [[bringing-extinct-species-back-to-life-dr-beth-shapiro-scim2211492808]] describes 20 selected dire-wolf edits in a gray-wolf background and safer substitute variants for light coat color.
- Living-species applications - [[bringing-extinct-species-back-to-life-dr-beth-shapiro-scim2211492808]] discusses quoll cells, black-footed ferrets, red wolves, and other genetic-rescue cases.
- Release governance - [[bringing-extinct-species-back-to-life-dr-beth-shapiro-scim2211492808]] describes care reports, staged observation, legal controls, and advisory groups including local and scientific stakeholders.
- Research spillovers - [[bringing-extinct-species-back-to-life-dr-beth-shapiro-scim2211492808]] presents ecosystem modeling and artificial-womb research as developmental or prospective branches.

## Qualifications
The current profile rests on one interview with a participant in the work, not an independent audit. It does not verify claimed project milestones, welfare outcomes, conservation benefit, funding allocation, peer-review status, or regulatory compliance. Cell results do not establish organism or ecosystem outcomes; engineered traits do not establish extinct-lineage identity; and artificial wombs, digital twins, and some rewilding benefits remain prospective.
