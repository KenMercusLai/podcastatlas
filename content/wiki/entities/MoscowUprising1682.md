# Moscow Uprising of 1682 / 1682年莫斯科起义

## Overview

The Moscow uprising of 1682 was the armed intervention by the [[Streltsy]] that transformed a disputed Romanov succession into the joint rule of Ivan V and [[PeterTheGreat|Peter the Great]] under [[SophiaAlekseyevna|Sophia Alekseyevna]]'s regency.

## Current Profile

After Fedor III died without an heir, the Naryshkin network backed ten-year-old Peter while the Miloslavsky side backed his older but disabled half-brother Ivan. Rumors that Ivan had been murdered brought aggrieved Streltsy into the Kremlin. Showing Ivan alive temporarily calmed them, but confrontation resumed and produced the killing of Artamon Matveyev, Mikhail Dolgoruky, Naryshkin relatives, and other officials.

The violence forced Peter's mother Natalia to surrender her brother and left the Streltsy as rewarded palace guards and political arbiters. Ivan was then made senior joint tsar, Peter junior tsar, and Sophia regent. The episode treats Peter's exposure to the killings as formative, while the later 1689 reversal showed that the Streltsy's allegiance was not permanently controlled by Sophia.

## Key Characteristics

- It fused a real military grievance with dynastic rumor and factional manipulation.
- Armed entry into the Kremlin made the Streltsy direct participants in succession politics.
- Public proof that Ivan was alive did not stop violence once authority and crowd discipline collapsed.
- The settlement preserved Peter's title while subordinating him within a joint monarchy and regency.
- Memory of the uprising became part of the later justification for Peter's hostility to the Streltsy.

- Coerced settlement: [[562-peter-the-great-the-rise-of-russia-part-1-glt5535116202]] connects threats against the Naryshkins to rewards for the Streltsy and the joint coronation.
- Formative afterlife: [[562-peter-the-great-the-rise-of-russia-part-1-glt5535116202]] links Peter's childhood exposure to his later fear and hatred of the corps.

## Qualifications

The episode distinguishes genuine Streltsy grievances from elite exploitation but cannot precisely allocate responsibility across rumor-makers, commanders, soldiers, and dynastic factions. Its casualty count, dialogue, torture details, and trauma-to-policy causal claim remain source-scoped.

## Relationships

- [[Streltsy]] - armed corps whose intervention drove the uprising and succession settlement.
- [[PeterTheGreat]] - child tsar exposed to the violence and subordinated by its outcome.
- [[SophiaAlekseyevna]] - dynastic actor who emerged as regent from the settlement.
- [[VasilyGolitsyn]] - minister whose later authority depended on the regency created after the uprising.
- [[CoerciveWesternization]] - later reform pattern partly shaped by Peter's hostility to the old military-political order.
