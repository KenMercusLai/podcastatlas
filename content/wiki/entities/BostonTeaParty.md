# Boston Tea Party

## Overview

The Boston Tea Party was the December 16, 1773 destruction of East India Company tea in Boston Harbor that converted a dispute over taxation, monopoly, and parliamentary sovereignty into an imperial crisis.

## Current Profile

The episode presents the action as theatrical and disciplined property destruction. Participants disguised themselves as Mohawks, boarded the ships, destroyed the tea, and tried to avoid damage beyond their target. That restraint did not make the act consensual or legally benign: Benjamin Franklin condemned the property loss, and London answered with the [[CoerciveActs|Coercive Acts]]. The event mattered less as an isolated riot than as the trigger through which punishment of Boston widened colonial resistance.

## Key Characteristics

- Direct action against taxed East India Company tea.
- Protest shaped by [[NoTaxationWithoutRepresentation|representation and sovereignty claims]].
- Carefully bounded destruction rather than generalized looting in the episode's account.
- Later name not commonly used until decades after the event.
- Immediate catalyst for punitive British legislation and wider colonial coordination.

## Evidence

- Commercial setting: [[348-the-boston-tea-party-part-2-glt5189620267]] links the protest to direct East India Company sales, monopoly fears, smuggling competition, and the retained duty.
- Event form: [[348-the-boston-tea-party-part-2-glt5189620267]] describes disguised participants destroying about £10,000 of tea while attempting to avoid unrelated damage.
- Moral dispute: [[348-the-boston-tea-party-part-2-glt5189620267]] records Franklin's condemnation of the destruction as violent injustice.
- Political consequence: [[348-the-boston-tea-party-part-2-glt5189620267]] makes the action the immediate context for the Coercive Acts and escalating colonial solidarity.

## Qualifications

The source is a retrospective podcast synthesis and does not establish every participant's identity or motive. Orderly execution does not erase coercion or property destruction, and later patriotic memory should not be mistaken for the event's contemporary name or a unanimous colonial judgment.

## What Changed

- Created the event as the protest-to-punishment hinge in the revolutionary sequence.
