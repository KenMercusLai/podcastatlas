# Peter Frankopan

## Overview

Peter Frankopan is presented in [[313-climate-apocalypse-glt8815959934]] as an Oxford professor of global history and the author of [[TheEarthTransformed|The Earth Transformed]].

## Current Profile

In this episode, Frankopan argues for an environmental history that uses scientific proxy evidence without collapsing history into climate determinism. He compares distant periods to show that environmental stress becomes historically consequential through institutions, disease, food and energy systems, inequality, and political decisions.

His stance is conditionally optimistic. Present warming is dangerous because it intersects with already fragile systems, but clear diagnosis, targeted technical changes, preparation, and human ingenuity can still reduce harm.

## Key Characteristics

- Works across global history and environmental change rather than treating climate as a self-contained specialty.
- Uses scientific evidence to add texture to written historical records.
- Rejects simple climate-to-collapse explanations in favor of interacting causal systems.
- Treats inequality and institutional resilience as central to environmental outcomes.
- Combines severe present-risk assessment with conditional confidence in adaptation and ingenuity.

## Evidence

- Historical method: [[313-climate-apocalypse-glt8815959934]] has Frankopan connect ice cores, pollen, genomics, tree rings, and satellite imagery to source-critical historical interpretation.
- Causal framework: [[313-climate-apocalypse-glt8815959934]] compares Akkad, the sixth century, the Little Ice Age, Rome, industrialization, and the present without making climate sufficient by itself.
- Governance and inequality: [[313-climate-apocalypse-glt8815959934]] emphasizes political structures, food and energy security, unequal exposure, and outsourced environmental costs.
- Practical outlook: [[313-climate-apocalypse-glt8815959934]] identifies food, energy, and disease resilience while preserving space for targeted technological mitigation.
