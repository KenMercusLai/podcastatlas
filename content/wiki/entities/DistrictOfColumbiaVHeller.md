# District of Columbia v. Heller

## Overview

*District of Columbia v. Heller* was the 2008 Supreme Court case that recognized an individual Second Amendment right to possess a handgun at home and treated the amendment's militia language as prefatory rather than limiting.

## Current Profile

The episode presents *Heller* as the modern doctrinal turning point that required the justices to parse an eighteenth-century sentence using unfamiliar syntax. Its account emphasizes the Court's separation of the militia and arms clauses and contrasts that approach with [[CarrieSullivan]]'s corpus evidence about [[JamesMadison]]'s connected “being” constructions.

The bounded source argues against the decision's grammatical and historical reasoning; it does not provide a full account of the opinions, precedents, subsequent doctrine, or the strongest defense of the Court's interpretation.

## Key Characteristics

- 2008 United States Supreme Court case.
- Concerned a District of Columbia restriction on keeping a handgun at home.
- Recognized an individual constitutional right to possess firearms.
- Treated the militia clause as prefatory and non-limiting.
- Serves as the principal modern interpretation challenged by the episode.
