# Wojtek

## Overview

Wojtek was a Syrian brown bear raised by Polish soldiers during the Second World War, formally enrolled in their army for transport to Italy, associated with ammunition supply at the [[BattleOfMonteCassino|Battle of Monte Cassino]], and later remembered as a symbol of Polish endurance and exile.

## Current Profile

[[533-wojtek-the-bear-who-beat-the-nazis-glt8956421015]] places Wojtek inside the route of [[AndersArmy|Anders' Army]]. Acquired as an orphaned cub near Tehran, he grew among soldiers, with Piotr Prendys as his main carer. His wrestling, swimming, appetite, and persistent mischief made him an affectionate social presence for people who had lost homes and relatives through invasion, Soviet captivity, deportation, and war.

Wojtek was formally enlisted as a private when animal restrictions threatened to separate him from the 22nd Artillery Supply Company on its voyage from Egypt to Italy. At Monte Cassino he reportedly carried shells or ammunition crates, inspiring the company's badge of a bear carrying a shell. After the war he accompanied Polish soldiers to Scotland, then entered Edinburgh Zoo in 1947 and died there in 1963. Comrades' visits and later statues turned his biography into public memory linking [[Poland]] and [[Scotland]].

## Key Characteristics

- Orphaned bear cub adopted and socialized within a displaced Polish military unit.
- Comic, physically interactive companion whose behavior supported morale and group affection.
- Formally enlisted soldier because transport rules did not permit an ordinary animal passenger.
- Reported ammunition helper and visual emblem of the 22nd Artillery Supply Company at Monte Cassino.
- Postwar public symbol of Polish endurance, exile, and Polish-Scottish friendship.

## Qualifications

The exact purchase and early custody sequence varies among accounts. A promotion to corporal is uncertain, and the episode does not independently establish exactly how many shells or crates Wojtek carried, how often he did so, or whether later retelling enlarged a real episode. His official enlistment, companionship, unit symbolism, and public afterlife are distinct claims and should not be treated as one undifferentiated legend.
