## Overview

李贽 appears as the intellectual dissenter who challenges imposed moral orthodoxy and defends “童心” but does not build an organization capable of institutional reform.

## Current Profile

The episode presents Li as rejecting the idea that Confucius and Cheng-Zhu orthodoxy supply one moral standard for every person. He defends ordinary desire and spiritual independence, while banned writings continue to circulate privately.

His dissent remains materially dependent on patrons and friends and does not become a program comparable to an organized reformation. The episode treats that gap as a social and organizational constraint as well as a personal contradiction.

## Key Characteristics

- Critic of uniform Confucian moral orthodoxy.
- Associated with “童心” and the legitimacy of ordinary desire.
- Seeks intellectual and spiritual independence through withdrawal from office.
- Remains economically dependent on supporters.
- Produces influential dissent without an implementable institutional alternative.

## Qualifications

This source-bounded profile does not reconstruct Li's philosophy, texts, networks, prosecution, or death from primary evidence. The comparison with the European Reformation is an interpretive analogy, not proof that the settings offered equivalent possibilities.

## What Changed

- Established Li as the episode's intellectual dissent without organizational conversion case.
