# Karl Deisseroth

## Overview
Karl Deisseroth is the psychiatrist and neuroscientist interviewed in [[essentials-understanding-healing-the-mind-dr-karl-deisseroth-scim2644851631]] about clinical psychiatry, neural circuits, stimulation technologies, and future mental-health treatment.

## Current Profile
The source presents Deisseroth as holding subjective experience and physical mechanism together. He accepts that present-day psychiatry must often reason from words, behavior, and functional impairment, while arguing that mental illness arises through physical cells, circuits, projections, and activity patterns that science can increasingly measure and influence. His optimism is conditional: a cure requires understanding the target, and emerging tools must remain attached to rigorous evidence and patient safety.

## Key Characteristics
- Combines clinical psychiatric experience with circuit-level neuroscience.
- Treats language and behavior as necessary but incomplete psychiatric evidence.
- Emphasizes target discovery before greater stimulation power or precision.
- Supports careful investigation of psychedelics while naming both opportunity and lasting risk.
- Presents scientific rigor and hope as compatible commitments.

## Evidence
- Clinical frame - [[essentials-understanding-healing-the-mind-dr-karl-deisseroth-scim2644851631]] describes diagnostic interviews, treatment adjustment, stigma, and uncertainty from a psychiatrist's perspective.
- Circuit frame - [[essentials-understanding-healing-the-mind-dr-karl-deisseroth-scim2644851631]] says psychiatric disease is physical and involves atypical circuits, connections, and projections.
- Precision boundary - [[essentials-understanding-healing-the-mind-dr-karl-deisseroth-scim2644851631]] contrasts broad electrical stimulation with the conditional selectivity of optogenetics.
- Risk and optimism - [[essentials-understanding-healing-the-mind-dr-karl-deisseroth-scim2644851631]] supports rigorous psychedelic research and closes with qualified optimism about psychiatry's trajectory.
