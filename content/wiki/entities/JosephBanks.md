# Joseph Banks

## Overview

Joseph Banks was the wealthy young naturalist aboard the [[Endeavour]] whose scientific patronage, collecting, journal writing, cultural interpretation, and later colonial influence make him a central but ambivalent figure in the voyage.

## Current Profile

Elite connections and personal wealth let Banks join the expedition with assistants, equipment, and the artist Sydney Parkinson, giving the voyage a natural-history program distinct from but embedded within its naval and astronomical missions. He then studies plants, animals, language, surfing, tattooing, ritual, and exchange across Tahiti, New Zealand, and Australia. He regrets several killings and sometimes challenges [[JamesCook]]'s punitive instincts. Yet his gaze can turn people into specimens: his description of [[Tupia]] as a possible “curiosity” and his sexualized interpretation of a Tahitian display reveal the limits of sympathetic observation.

The episode also separates Banks from Cook in the colonial afterlife. It attributes the later proposal to use Botany Bay as a convict settlement and significant advice on Australian colonization to Banks rather than to Cook.

## Key Characteristics

- Energetic naturalist and recorder of Pacific environments, languages, practices, and material culture.
- Wealthy scientific traveler whose connections, staff, equipment, and collecting ambitions materially expand the expedition.
- Curious observer whose interpretations remain vulnerable to European sexual and social assumptions.
- Witness who expresses regret over killings and sometimes resists punitive escalation.
- Patron-like traveler able to treat another person's journey as collection or spectacle.
- Later advocate and adviser in British Australian colonization according to the episode.

## Evidence

- Scientific party and resources: [[380-captain-cook-historys-greatest-explorer-part-1-glt8106697435]] describes Banks securing passage, bringing assistants and Sydney Parkinson, and loading extensive equipment for natural-history work.
- Natural and cultural observation: [[381-captain-cook-to-the-ends-of-the-earth-part-2-glt6950185057]] describes Banks studying surfing, tattooing, vocabulary, ceremony, flora, and fauna.
- Interpretive limits: [[381-captain-cook-to-the-ends-of-the-earth-part-2-glt6950185057]] contrasts Banks's sexual reading of a Tahitian woman's display with Nicholas Thomas's alternative trade-credential interpretation.
- Moral response: [[381-captain-cook-to-the-ends-of-the-earth-part-2-glt6950185057]] says Banks calls one failed and lethal Māori contact the most disagreeable day of his life and worries about Cook's hard line in Tahiti.
- Colonial afterlife: [[381-captain-cook-to-the-ends-of-the-earth-part-2-glt6950185057]] distinguishes Cook's voyage from Banks's later support for Botany Bay settlement and advice on colonization.
