# Blink (unproduced film)

## Overview

*Blink* is an unproduced screenplay by [[StephenGaghan]], developed with [[MalcolmGladwell]] from ideas in Gladwell's nonfiction book but built as an original father-son drama about social perception, corporate predation, and the desire for approval.

## Current Profile

Teddy is a directionless young man estranged from a ruthless financier father. A professor discovers that Teddy can supposedly distinguish lies from truth and read emotional states with extraordinary accuracy. The ability isolates Teddy in ordinary relationships but makes him valuable when his father needs to select a bankruptcy jurisdiction for an American Steel takeover that could shed pension and healthcare obligations.

The screenplay thus binds an intimate vulnerability to material consequences for workers. Teddy can win paternal recognition by using an ethically ambiguous gift on behalf of the values he rejects. Its fictional premise should not be mistaken for validated lie detection: [[SocialEmotionInferenceLimits]] treats faces and other cues as contextual, probabilistic evidence rather than transparent access to inner state.

The project attracted [[LeonardoDiCaprio]], competitive studio bids, a producer, budget, schedule, and reported green light. Gaghan's rewrite made Teddy younger and recentered the role around [[HeathLedger]]; Ledger's death then severed the relationship through which Gaghan had come to imagine the film. Later enthusiasm suggests revival remained conceivable, not committed.

## Key Characteristics

- Indirect nonfiction adaptation that invents a conventional narrative rather than filming the book's argument.
- Combines extraordinary social perception with the loneliness of seeing through routine evasion.
- Makes paternal approval the emotional engine of a corporate-benefits conflict.
- Uses bankruptcy jurisdiction and judicial selection as a plot device whose legal accuracy is explicitly uncertain.
- Advanced development project whose star attachment and apparent green light did not lead to production.
- Work whose casting evolution and creator grief became inseparable from its development history.

## Qualifications

The profile is based on a retrospective structured summary rather than the screenplay, Gladwell's book, production archive, legal analysis, or participant correspondence. Teddy's capacity is fictional and conflicts with any assumption that real faces transparently reveal emotion or deception. Gladwell doubts the bankruptcy mechanism's accuracy. Studio bids, green-light status, casting intentions, motives, and the causal weight of Ledger's death remain source-scoped, as does any judgment that the screenplay would work on screen.
