# 2014 Sony Pictures Hack

## Overview

The 2014 Sony Pictures hack was a destructive intrusion and disclosure crisis that disabled company systems and exposed corporate email, personal information, scripts, and other confidential material. [[the-sony-hack-the-mistakes-series-the-sony-hack-the-mistakes-series]] examines it as both a cyberattack and the downstream result of decisions surrounding [[TheInterviewFilm|The Interview]].

## Current Profile

The current evidence makes the attack a layered organizational-risk case. [[MichaelLynton]] links the crisis to his exceptional approval of the film without [[SonyPictures]]' cross-functional greenlight review, while later warnings were filtered through a physical-retaliation frame that overlooked [[NorthKorea|North Korea's]] cyber capacity. The case therefore joins governance, executive psychology, geopolitical review, expert uncertainty, and technical threat modeling without proving that any one factor alone caused the intrusion.

The breach also demonstrates why operational recovery and confidentiality recovery differ. Systems can be rebuilt, but released messages, employee records, and unreleased creative material cannot be made private again.

## Key Characteristics

- Combined destructive system disruption with large-scale publication of confidential information.
- Followed controversy over a film depicting the assassination of [[KimJongUn]].
- Exposed the consequences of bypassing an established cross-functional approval process.
- Revealed a gap between physical-retaliation analysis and cyber-capability analysis.
- Produced distribution disruption when major theater chains declined to screen the film.
- Remains partly dependent here on a retrospective executive account rather than a complete forensic and multi-party record.

## Qualifications

The reported cost, North Korean responsibility, causal chain, expert advice, likely greenlight outcome, and allocation of responsibility therefore remain source-scoped.
