# British-French Guarantee to Poland (1939)

## Overview

The British-French guarantee to [[Poland]] was the March 1939 commitment to support Polish independence if it were threatened and Poland resisted, marking a shift from accommodation toward deterrence after Germany occupied Prague.

## Current Profile

The guarantee emerged after the [[OccupationOfCzechoslovakia1939|occupation of Czechoslovakia]] destroyed confidence that Hitler's demands would stop with German-majority territories. Poland had rejected demands over [[DanzigGdansk|Danzig]], a German-controlled corridor, and alignment with the Anti-Comintern Pact because they endangered trade access, foreign-policy autonomy, and sovereign status.

Britain saw the commitment as part of a possible wider coalition, and France was prepared to support Poland. Yet the deterrent contained an operational weakness: Britain could not readily send direct military aid through German-controlled geography. The guarantee could widen a German-Polish war, but it could not by itself prevent Poland's defeat.

Hitler initially reacted with disbelief and anger, then ordered invasion planning. The commitment therefore raised the declared cost of aggression without correcting his expectation that democratic opponents would ultimately retreat.

## Key Characteristics

- Public commitment triggered by a threat to Polish independence and Polish resistance.
- British and French policy shift after the occupation of Prague.
- Attempt to deter Germany through the prospect of a wider war.
- Dependence on Polish refusal to submit rather than an unconditional defense of every territorial arrangement.
- Serious gap between political commitment and capacity for prompt direct relief.
- Signal Hitler discounted while preparing Fall Weiss.

## Evidence

- Policy shift: [[530-hitlers-war-on-poland-countdown-to-armageddon-part-1-glt8685711342]] connects Prague, Daladier's war preparation, Chamberlain's Birmingham speech, and the 31 March parliamentary commitment.
- Polish agency: [[530-hitlers-war-on-poland-countdown-to-armageddon-part-1-glt8685711342]] presents Polish rejection of client-state status and Józef Beck's refusal of peace at the price of independence.
- Credibility gap: [[530-hitlers-war-on-poland-countdown-to-armageddon-part-1-glt8685711342]] notes Britain's geographic inability to send ready direct aid and Hitler's decision to continue invasion planning.

## Qualifications

The source establishes the commitment's political meaning but not that it offered an adequate military plan for saving Poland. It also does not show that inclusion of the Soviet Union was diplomatically available on acceptable terms to Poland or the western powers. Private motives, cabinet calculations, military feasibility, and counterfactual deterrence outcomes remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Established the guarantee as both a real threshold change and an operationally weak deterrent.
- Preserved Polish refusal of client status as an active cause of the diplomatic confrontation.

## Relationships

- [[Poland]] - state whose independence the commitment addressed.
- [[NevilleChamberlain]] - British prime minister who announced the guarantee in Parliament.
- [[France]] - allied guarantor prepared to enter war if Germany attacked.
- [[OccupationOfCzechoslovakia1939]] - event that discredited continued reliance on Hitler's territorial assurances.
- [[OpponentResolveMiscalculation]] - decision error through which Hitler discounted the guarantee's political threshold.
- [[InvasionOfPoland1939]] - attack that activated the commitment but exposed its relief-capacity limits.
