## Overview

Sichuan / 四川 appears in the wiki as a region with two very different evidence clusters: food-cultural learning and political-historical vulnerability. The food sources make Sichuan a place of culinary grammar, market practice, and technical renewal; the history sources make it a place where conquest, taxation, war, famine, epidemic, and social collapse have to be counted as part of regional memory.

## Current Profile

The culinary branch begins with [[66-yuchi-yu-huajiao-liao-chi-de-wo-ke-jiu-bu-kun-le-659957536|Mihuan Chishu 66]], where Sichuan is the setting for [[FuchsiaDunlop|胡霞 / Fuchsia Dunlop]]'s Chengdu learning, market encounters, Sichuan food immersion, and cultural self-recognition through appetite. [[ep245-yinian-yidu-xunwei-zhilv-jinnian-qu-chi-chuangyi-cai-li9wtavemokcckialbbj7unpmqsz|EP245]] adds a contemporary restaurant layer: [[SichuanCuisine|川菜]] innovation can happen inside a strong tradition through soup bases, brining, fish-fragrant reconstruction, heat control, low-temperature technique, and aroma extraction.

The history branch is harsher. [[170-songtaizu-zhisi-zhuying-fusheng-yinmoulun-zhong-de-renxing-mituan-xia-958158115|Song Taizu 170]] uses post-Shu unrest to show that central unification can produce local resentment when conquest is followed by plunder, coercion, taxation, and monopoly pressure. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-388-4-jiemi-gudai-zuican-de-sheng-ta-ceng-si-le-90-baixing-luivvup1twdt6t6dgow9xnfwirbu|Hanji 388-4]] extends that vulnerability into a later war-devastation frame, linking Song-Yuan devastation and the late-Ming and early-Qing catastrophe around [[ZhangXianzhong|张献忠]], other armies, famine, epidemic, and tiger disaster.

## Key Characteristics

- Regional food culture where market contact, cooking practice, mouthfeel, huajiao, street foods, and repeated exposure make taste intelligible.
- Translation site where an outsider's love for [[SichuanCuisine|川菜]] produces both cultural admiration and critical self-recognition.
- Contemporary cuisine-renewal setting where chefs update technique while preserving recognizable Sichuan flavor logic.
- Conquest-afterlife region where unification can create local resistance through violence, taxation, and state monopoly.
- Repeated war-devastation case where population loss is presented as the combined result of armies, famine, epidemic, ecology, and state collapse.

## Evidence

- Culinary learning: [[66-yuchi-yu-huajiao-liao-chi-de-wo-ke-jiu-bu-kun-le-659957536]] places Sichuan and [[Chengdu|成都]] at the center of Dunlop's learning from markets, cooks, offal, rabbit heads, huajiao, and everyday meals.
- Food-cultural translation: [[66-yuchi-yu-huajiao-liao-chi-de-wo-ke-jiu-bu-kun-le-659957536]] uses Sichuan food to discuss [[CulinaryGrammar]], [[FoodTextureAesthetics]], [[FoodCulturalBias]], and [[CulinaryIdentityTransformation]].
- Culinary renewal: [[ep245-yinian-yidu-xunwei-zhilv-jinnian-qu-chi-chuangyi-cai-li9wtavemokcckialbbj7unpmqsz]] says Sichuan chefs can change soup bases, brining, meat treatment, low-temperature cooking, and aroma extraction while keeping regional recognizability.
- Post-conquest resistance: [[170-songtaizu-zhisi-zhuying-fusheng-yinmoulun-zhong-de-renxing-mituan-xia-958158115]] links Sichuan unrest after Song conquest to military abuse, taxation, and monopolies.
- War devastation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-388-4-jiemi-gudai-zuican-de-sheng-ta-ceng-si-le-90-baixing-luivvup1twdt6t6dgow9xnfwirbu]] treats Sichuan as an extreme case of civilian suffering across Song-Yuan war and late-Ming / early-Qing collapse.

## Qualifications

The page does not merge culinary Sichuan with war-devastation Sichuan into one timeless regional essence. The food sources are about learned appetite and restaurant practice; the history sources are about specific conquest and wartime contexts. Hanji 388-4's demographic figures and responsibility allocation should remain source-scoped.
