# Ed Glaeser

## Overview
Ed Glaeser is the economist in [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-dde046734f50fc7c6a18ae9c19ada137-55355169-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-dde046734f50fc7c6a18ae9c19ada137-55355169]] who interprets U.S. housing unaffordability primarily as a failure to build enough homes in high-demand places.

## Current Profile
Glaeser argues that rising prices combined with falling construction point toward supply constraints rather than land exhaustion or inequality alone. His [[ZoningTax|zoning-tax]] frame treats the gap between selling price and production cost as evidence that land-use restrictions and political opposition have manufactured scarcity.

His policy emphasis is institutional: reduce local veto power or make approval financially attractive to local governments, pursue density changes large enough to alter development incentives, and connect federal transport money or state targets to housing production. He still reserves housing support for vulnerable households and rejects the idea that construction alone explains fertility decisions.

## Key Characteristics
- Diagnoses housing scarcity through the joint behavior of prices, production, construction cost, and regulation.
- Treats overlapping rules and local opposition as more important than any single zoning provision.
- Distinguishes national housing supply from the locally concentrated effects of wealthy or institutional buyers.
- Favors strong production incentives and meaningful density increases over nominal permission changes.
- Treats a home primarily as a place to live rather than a speculative investment.
