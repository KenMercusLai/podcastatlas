## Overview

刘濞 / Liu Bi enters the current wiki as [[LiuBang|刘邦]]'s nephew and king of [[WuKingdomEarlyHan|early Han Wu]], then reappears as the leading rebel ruler in the [[RebellionOfTheSevenStatesWesternHan|Rebellion of the Seven States]]. The three sources turn an early same-surname security appointment into a later case of royal power contesting the center, strategic options narrowing under mistrust and hierarchy, and a defeated leader losing the protection that had sustained his alliances.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-189-liubang-siqian-zuihou-yibo-lllydj2xyk4cr7b9d2qx0hvfw-dz|Hanji 189]] supplies Liu Bi's institutional beginning. After [[YingBu|英布]] is defeated, Liu Bang reorganizes the former Jing field and appoints his nephew over three commanderies and fifty-three counties. The grant belongs to [[SameSurnameKingEnfeoffment|same-surname enfeoffment]]: exposed territory is entrusted to royal kin after a dangerous vassal branch falls.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-234-zhouyafu-zhanchang-dalian-hanjingdi-na-lai-de-danzi-li6iqhtuxgq5xwgbn_vb7lljgyxr|Hanji 234]] supplies the later reversal. After the court kills Chao Cuo and sends Yuan Ang and Liu Tong to seek withdrawal, Liu Bi detains the mission, reportedly calls himself an “eastern emperor,” and ignores Mei Cheng's warning that continuing the campaign will expose his political claim, rear, and food route. The episode says he tries to recruit Yuan Ang, then orders him killed after refusal.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-235-liubi-zhisi-jielu-zhichang-shengcun-qian-guize-lp1wxe_buqe_zrilsfwsfzqoa2ef|Hanji 235]] supplies the final narrowing and collapse. Liu Bi rejects [[TianLuboWesternHan|田禄伯]]'s proposed second front after an heir raises the risk of delegated betrayal, then lets senior commanders dispose of an unnamed young general's Luoyang plan through age and status rather than a supplied operational rebuttal. The contrast with [[ZhouQiuWesternHan|周丘]] is sharp: a retainer Liu Bi has disregarded can use one tally and local ties to mobilize quickly, but even that force loses its larger purpose when Liu Bi flees.

The same episode ends Liu Bi's arc. After the Wu-Chu force collapses, he crosses the Yangtze with remnants and seeks refuge from a ruler rendered as “东海王落望.” The court's payment changes that ruler's incentives; an invitation to review troops creates the access for Liu Bi's killing. The joined profile is not that Liu-family enfeoffment mechanically causes rebellion or that either rejected plan would have won. It shows how a large royal base can support resistance, while distrust, status-filtered advice, logistical defeat, and alliance repricing can progressively remove its options and protection.

## Key Characteristics

- Liu Bang's nephew and son of [[LiuXiDaiKing|刘喜]].
- Recipient of a large Wu kingdom after the defeat of Ying Bu and reorganization of the Jing field.
- Same-surname royal appointment that later becomes a leading source of armed resistance to the center.
- Rebel ruler who rejects a withdrawal mission and reportedly claims an eastern imperial title.
- Coalition leader warned that continued advance could expose his political legitimacy, rear, and supply line.
- Decision-maker who rejects one proposal through defection fear and another after senior commanders dismiss its young advocate.
- Defeated leader whose remaining ally reprices the relationship and kills him after accepting a court payment.

Flight and killing:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-235-liubi-zhisi-jielu-zhichang-shengcun-qian-guize-lp1wxe_buqe_zrilsfwsfzqoa2ef|Hanji 235]] says Liu Bi flees south, gathers remnants, trusts the ruler called “东海王落望,” and is killed during a staged troop-review meeting after a court payment.

## Qualifications

These three popular-history notes cover Liu Bi's appointment and late rebellion phases, not his full reign or the complete causes of the Seven States crisis. The size and administration of Wu, long-term preparations, territorial-reduction dispute, Chao Cuo's role, Mei Cheng's letter, “eastern emperor” wording, treatment of the envoys, proposed routes, army totals, rejected plans, Zhou Qiu's mobilization, Liu Bi's flight, payment, killer's identity, and assassination scene remain source-scoped. Hanji 235's “2月30日,” 刘驹／刘居 variation, “桓” abbreviation, and “东海王落望” wording are treated as transcription or identity uncertainties rather than silently normalized facts.

## What Changed

- Added the decision phase in which mistrust and status-filtered advice narrow Liu Bi's strategic alternatives.
- Completed the current arc through defeat, southward flight, alliance repricing, and assassination while preserving the killer's uncertain identity.
- Added Zhou Qiu as counterevidence to Liu Bi's personnel judgment without treating Zhou's coercive methods as legitimate.

## Relationships

- [[LiuBang|刘邦]] - uncle and founding ruler who appoints Liu Bi king of Wu.
- [[LiuXiDaiKing|刘喜]] - father named in the appointment source.
- [[WuKingdomEarlyHan|吴国]] - territorial base first assigned as a dynastic security measure and later mobilized against the center.
- [[RebellionOfTheSevenStatesWesternHan|七国之乱]] - uprising in which Liu Bi becomes the leading royal rebel in the current evidence.
- [[YuanAngWesternHan|袁盎]] - court envoy whom Liu Bi reportedly confines after failed recruitment.
- [[ZhouYafu|周亚夫]] - opposing commander whose supply strategy targets the Wu-Chu coalition.
- [[TianLuboWesternHan|田禄伯]] - subordinate whose separate-theater proposal Liu Bi rejects under betrayal fear.
- [[ZhouQiuWesternHan|周丘]] - disregarded retainer whose tally-backed mobilization still depends on Liu Bi's wider cause.
- [[StatusBasedAdviceDismissal|身份资历式建议否决]] - decision failure exposed when seniority substitutes for testing the young general's plan.
- [[PowerBasedAllianceRepricing|实力重估式阵营转向]] - later mechanism by which Liu Bi's defeat changes the value and risk of protecting him.
- [[SameSurnameKingEnfeoffment|同姓王分封]] - institutional origin of Liu Bi's royal authority.
