# 《兰亭集序》 / Lantingji Xu

## Overview

《兰亭集序》 / *Lantingji Xu* is the preface traditionally attributed to [[WangXizhi|王羲之]] for poems composed at the 353 [[LantingGathering|兰亭雅集]], and a central work in the source's account of Chinese calligraphic and literary memory.

## Current Profile

The work begins from spring landscape and shared pleasure before turning toward aging, change, and death. That movement lets the source read it as art transforming awareness of finitude without denying grief or impermanence. Its public stature, however, is a reception history as well as an originating event: transmitted calligraphic copies and Tang imperial admiration helped make it a cultural emblem. The source also preserves a textual problem because the shorter *Linhe Xu* differs from the received long version.

## Key Characteristics

- Preface traditionally tied to poems from the 353 Lanting gathering.
- Joins sensory pleasure and social presence to awareness of change and death.
- Canonical in both literary and calligraphic reception.
- Known through transmitted copies rather than an uncontested surviving original.
- Textually qualified by the shorter *Linhe Xu* tradition.

## Evidence

- Form and theme: [[24-dongxifang-de-fanju-zuihou-de-wancan-yu-lanting-yaji-lraekm-bgowhg1wxysfshcwoz5xp]] connects gathering, collected poems, preface, landscape, aging, and mortality.
- Reception: [[24-dongxifang-de-fanju-zuihou-de-wancan-yu-lanting-yaji-lraekm-bgowhg1wxysfshcwoz5xp]] attributes its high later status partly to Tang admiration for Wang's calligraphy.
- Textual boundary: [[24-dongxifang-de-fanju-zuihou-de-wancan-yu-lanting-yaji-lraekm-bgowhg1wxysfshcwoz5xp]] notes differences between the received text and *Linhe Xu*.

## Qualifications

This page records the episode's account, not a full textual or calligraphic stemma. Authorship, interpolation, manuscript history, the lost original, and the exact relation among surviving copies remain unresolved here.
