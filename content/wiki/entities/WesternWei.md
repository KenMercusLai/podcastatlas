## Overview
Western Wei was the Guanzhong-based successor state formed after Northern Wei's division, nominally ruled by Yuan emperors but dominated by [[YuwenTai|Yuwen Tai]].

## Current Profile
The episode defines Western Wei by asymmetric adaptation. It began with fewer people, goods, and military resources than Eastern Wei, and neither side could convert campaigns around the Yellow River, Tong Pass, and Luoyang into stable conquest. Western Wei therefore integrated disparate forces through [[GuanzhongOrientedPolicy|regional identity]], the [[EightPillarsFubingSystem|Eight Pillars–fubing system]], and [[ZhouliInstitutionalReconstruction|administrative reconstruction]]. The collapse of Southern Liang authority then allowed it to acquire Hanzhong and Ba-Shu, changing the material base inherited by Northern Zhou.

## Key Characteristics
- Combined nominal Yuan imperial legitimacy with Yuwen Tai's effective command.
- Operated from a weaker demographic and material base than Eastern Wei.
- Used military hierarchy, bestowed identity, and classicizing offices to create a new coalition.
- Reached stalemate in repeated eastern campaigning rather than winning by one decisive battle.
- Converted Southern Liang's civil war into durable territorial and resource gains.

## Qualifications
This account is organized around one episode's explanation of regime growth, not a complete political, fiscal, social, or military history. Its emphasis on institutional integration and southern geography complements rather than disproves accounts centered on campaigns or elite rivalry.

## What Changed
- Established Western Wei as a weak-base regime whose institutional integration and southern expansion changed the balance of power.
