# Al-Khayzuran

## Overview

Al-Khayzuran was the Yemeni-born mother of the Abbasid caliphs al-Hadi and [[HarunAlRashid]], represented in the current evidence as a former enslaved woman who became al-Mahdi's legal wife and a consequential court actor.

## Current Profile

Her rise joins intimate access, legal status, motherhood, and dynastic politics. The episode presents her as powerful enough to matter in the struggle over whether al-Hadi would displace Harun in favor of his own infant son.

Later stories of attempted poisoning and maternal revenge dramatize that conflict but do not securely establish how al-Hadi died. Her strongest current significance is therefore structural: a caliphal mother could exercise real political leverage even when the surviving anecdotes about that leverage are difficult to verify.

## Key Characteristics

- Yemeni-born former enslaved woman who became al-Mahdi's wife.
- Mother of two successive Abbasid caliphs, al-Hadi and Harun al-Rashid.
- Court actor associated with Harun's position in the succession.
- Example of political power exercised through dynastic and household relationships.
- Figure whose remembered agency is entangled with sensational later stories.

## Evidence

- **Dynastic position:** [[378-baghdad-the-golden-age-part-3-glt9689814482]] identifies al-Khayzuran as al-Mahdi's wife and the mother of al-Hadi and Harun.
- **Court influence:** [[378-baghdad-the-golden-age-part-3-glt9689814482]] places her within the conflict over al-Hadi's attempt to replace Harun as heir.
- **Narrative uncertainty:** [[378-baghdad-the-golden-age-part-3-glt9689814482]] reports poisoning and death rumors while explicitly cautioning that their details are unreliable.

## Qualifications

The profile rests on one popular-history episode and on traditions transmitted after the events. The poisoning stories may reveal perceptions of court hostility without proving either an attempted murder or responsibility for al-Hadi's death.

## What Changed

- Established al-Khayzuran as a dynastic court actor while separating structural influence from unverified death narratives.
