# Kandake Queens

## Overview

Kandake queens are the Kushite royal women the episode uses to show ancient African female authority in politics and warfare.

## Current Profile

The source presents Kandake as a royal title or queenly institution in [[Kush]]. [[LukePepera]] uses the Kandake examples to argue that women leaders in African history were not marginal anomalies: they could be associated with campaigns against Egyptians and Romans, and [[Amanirenas]] gives the source a named ruler around the first century BC.

## Key Characteristics

- Kushite queenly institution or title in the episode's account.
- Associated with ruler-level and campaign authority.
- Used as an ancient precedent before the episode turns to [[QueenNjinga]], [[AsanteQueenMothers]], [[RanavalonaI]], and the [[Agojie]].
- Helps separate African female authority from the later European "Amazon" fantasy label.

## Evidence

- Institutional role: [[317-african-amazons-glt5973082485]] says Kandake queens in [[Kush]] led campaigns against Egyptians and Romans.
- Named example: [[317-african-amazons-glt5973082485]] names [[Amanirenas]] as a first-century BC example.
- Comparative placement: [[317-african-amazons-glt5973082485]] uses the Kandake material before the Ndongo, Asante, Madagascar, and Dahomey case studies.

## Qualifications

The source gives only a brief comparative account. It does not reconstruct the full Kandake title, chronology, succession rules, or archaeological record.
