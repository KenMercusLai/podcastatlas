# Dolly Parton

## Overview
Dolly Parton is represented in the wiki as an American country-music artist, songwriter, business actor, philanthropist, cross-coalition cultural figure, and performer whose "9 to 5" became tied to office-worker labor politics.

## Current Profile
The Economist remembrance presents Parton through authorship, autonomy, philanthropy, and cultural trust. Her refusal of an Elvis Presley cover deal for "I Will Always Love You" anchors the wiki's [[ArtistRightsControl]] frame: she protected song rights even when exposure from a powerful intermediary looked valuable. The same source broadens her profile through her Appalachian voice, plain-spoken lyrics, acting, memoir, Dollywood, collaborations, book-giving philanthropy, Christian faith, humor, and refusal to be politically captured.

The Planet Money episode adds a more specific labor-history layer. Parton's "9 to 5" is not only a hit song or film association; the episode presents it as the anthem attached to a real [[OfficeWorkerLaborOrganizing]] movement. Through [[NineToFiveFilm|9 to 5]], Parton's cultural role connects workers' frustrations over pay, promotion, child care, harassment, and boss power to public recognition, screenings, rallies, and [[CultureLedLaborOrganizing]].

## Key Characteristics
- Songwriter-performer whose authorship, voice, and concise storytelling anchor the wiki's account of her appeal.
- Business actor who protected song rights and built durable value around her own work.
- Country-music figure treated as part of wider [[AmericanCulturalExports]].
- Philanthropist and public figure whose book-giving, faith, humor, and restraint helped maintain cross-coalition admiration.
- Actor and songwriter linked to [[NineToFiveFilm|9 to 5]] as a workplace-culture artifact.
- Labor-culture figure whose anthem helped make clerical-worker grievances publicly legible.

## Evidence
- Rights control and business discipline: [[big-shot-does-a-cancer-vaccine-work-6a9001f40c15e359f9cb103c]] says Parton refused an Elvis Presley cover deal because it required half the rights to "I Will Always Love You."
- Artistic range and cultural export: [[big-shot-does-a-cancer-vaccine-work-6a9001f40c15e359f9cb103c]] cites "Coat of Many Colors," "Nine to Five," and "Jolene" as examples of her voice, plain-spoken writing, and story compression.
- Platform breadth and philanthropy: [[big-shot-does-a-cancer-vaccine-work-6a9001f40c15e359f9cb103c]] says she acted, wrote a memoir, developed Dollywood, collaborated widely, and created a charity that gave more than 200 million books to children.
- Cross-coalition appeal: [[big-shot-does-a-cancer-vaccine-work-6a9001f40c15e359f9cb103c]] describes her Christian faith, self-deprecating humor, political carefulness, gay and conservative religious fandom, and COVID-jab encouragement.
- Labor anthem context: [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-121afa1edad69a8165a6651fddddf97c-25337177-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-121afa1edad69a8165a6651fddddf97c-25337177]] presents "9 to 5" as tied to a real labor movement of Boston secretaries and the [[NineToFiveOrganization|9to5]] organization.
- Film and organizing afterlife: [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-121afa1edad69a8165a6651fddddf97c-25337177-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-121afa1edad69a8165a6651fddddf97c-25337177]] says the movie and song helped worker rallies, chapter growth, and recognition of workplace frustrations.
