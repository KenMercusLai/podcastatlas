# Dolly Parton

## Overview
Dolly Parton is represented in the wiki as an American country-music artist, songwriter, business actor, philanthropist, and unusually broad cultural figure.

## Current Profile
The episode presents Parton through an obituary-style cultural remembrance. Her profile begins with songwriting control: she rejected an Elvis Presley cover of "I Will Always Love You" when the deal demanded half the rights, a decision the episode treats as both nerve and business discipline.

The source then broadens her into [[AmericanCulturalExports]]. Parton's voice, Appalachian accent, plain-spoken lyrics, acting, memoir, Dollywood, collaborations, and literacy philanthropy are used to explain why she became a megastar and a [[CrossCoalitionCulturalIcon]]. Her religious faith, self-deprecating humor, refusal to appear politically captured, and explicit affection for both gay and conservative religious worlds make her a culture figure whose appeal crossed ordinary U.S. political and identity boundaries.

## Key Characteristics
- Songwriter-performer whose authorship and voice anchor the source's account of her appeal.
- Business actor who protected song rights and built durable value around her own work.
- Country-music figure treated as part of wider American cultural export power.
- Philanthropist associated in the episode with a book-giving charity that exceeded 200 million books.
- Public figure whose Christian faith, humor, and political carefulness helped maintain cross-coalition admiration.
- Self-made career story grounded by poverty, early songwriting, and leaving Porter Waggoner's orbit.

## Evidence
- Rights control and business discipline: [[big-shot-does-a-cancer-vaccine-work-6a9001f40c15e359f9cb103c]] says Parton refused the Elvis Presley cover deal because it required half the rights to "I Will Always Love You."
- Artistic range: [[big-shot-does-a-cancer-vaccine-work-6a9001f40c15e359f9cb103c]] cites "Coat of Many Colors," "Nine to Five," and "Jolene" as examples of her voice, plain-spoken writing, and story compression.
- Platform breadth and philanthropy: [[big-shot-does-a-cancer-vaccine-work-6a9001f40c15e359f9cb103c]] says she acted, wrote a memoir, developed Dollywood, collaborated widely, and created a charity that gave more than 200 million books to children.
- Independence and origin story: [[big-shot-does-a-cancer-vaccine-work-6a9001f40c15e359f9cb103c]] says she grew up poor, had a songwriting deal before finishing high school, and left Porter Waggoner after he became jealous and abusive.
- Cross-coalition appeal: [[big-shot-does-a-cancer-vaccine-work-6a9001f40c15e359f9cb103c]] describes her Christian faith, self-deprecating humor, two refusals of the Presidential Medal of Freedom, gay and conservative religious fandom, and COVID-jab encouragement.
