# 泗水国 / Sishui Kingdom (Western Han)

## Overview

泗水国 / Sishui Kingdom is the Western Han vassal kingdom whose royal burial landscape is associated in this wiki with the [[SanzhuangHanTombComplex|三庄汉墓群]] and especially [[DaqingdunHanTomb|大青墩汉墓]]. It is distinct from [[SishuiChuHan|汜水]], the Chu-Han river crossing represented by a different Chinese character and historical context.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-345-2-qiannian-wangling-de-guiyi-xianxiang-daodi-zhenxiang-ruhe-ljkkhfbumlsgf7ppz96qvys_ok3d|Hanji 345-2]] supplies the landscape and historical setup: a kingdom reportedly founded under Emperor Wu, continued through five generations and six kings, and represented archaeologically by a planned, multi-mound cemetery. That episode's [[ChendunHanTomb|陈墩汉墓]] shows why cemetery attribution and occupant identity must remain separate; an elite female burial with a non-Liu named seal need not be a king's grave or disprove a royal-cemetery complex.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-346-qiannian-wangling-beihou-yincang-de-jingtian-da-mimi-lophz5ccj4jalgmohvozcol5nelx|Hanji 346]] then makes the kingdom materially vivid at Daqingdun. The most direct link is not size alone: chamber boards labeled “泗水王冢” and “王冢” connect the monument to a royal burial context.

The episode reports six kings in the Western Han line and tries to infer the tomb's occupant by excluding a short-reigning king and the final king deposed under Wang Mang. That inference remains weaker than the cemetery attribution. A silver seal reading [[LiuSuiSishuiSeal|刘绥]] does not match the transmitted royal roster, leaving open whether the seal belonged to an attendant, substitute burial object, secondary person, or someone otherwise absent from the simplified list.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han vassal kingdom represented in the episode through both transmitted king lists and excavated royal-burial evidence.
- Associated with the planned, multi-tomb Sanzhuang landscape rather than one self-interpreting monument.
- Materially linked to Daqingdun by the “泗水王冢” chamber-board label.
- Reported to have six kings, although the episode does not reproduce or independently verify the full documentary record.
- Its cemetery can be identified more confidently than the precise occupant or family role of an individual tomb.

## Evidence

- Cemetery landscape: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-345-2-qiannian-wangling-de-guiyi-xianxiang-daodi-zhenxiang-ruhe-ljkkhfbumlsgf7ppz96qvys_ok3d|Hanji 345-2]] reports multiple mounds organized along a north-south axis and contrasts the elite Chendun burial with the larger Daqingdun candidate.
- Royal-cemetery attribution: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-346-qiannian-wangling-beihou-yincang-de-jingtian-da-mimi-lophz5ccj4jalgmohvozcol5nelx|Hanji 346]] reports “泗水王冢” and “王冢” labels on chamber boards at Daqingdun.
- Political chronology: both [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-345-2-qiannian-wangling-de-guiyi-xianxiang-daodi-zhenxiang-ruhe-ljkkhfbumlsgf7ppz96qvys_ok3d|Hanji 345-2]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-346-qiannian-wangling-beihou-yincang-de-jingtian-da-mimi-lophz5ccj4jalgmohvozcol5nelx|Hanji 346]] report five generations and six kings; Hanji 346 uses reign length and deposition to propose, but not establish, occupant candidates.
- Identity tension: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-346-qiannian-wangling-beihou-yincang-de-jingtian-da-mimi-lophz5ccj4jalgmohvozcol5nelx|Hanji 346]] reports a silver “刘绥” seal whose name does not occur in the supplied royal roster.

## Qualifications

The two episodes are short secondary narratives, not full publications of the excavations or received texts. They do not establish the precise territorial chronology, duration, complete royal genealogy, cemetery phasing, relationship among Chendun, Daqingdun, and unexcavated tombs, or the identity of the principal burials. “Disappeared kingdom” is a narrative description of historical obscurity, not evidence that the polity was absent from all written records. The supplied duration of 125 years also differs from accessible accounts giving 123 years and remains unharmonized here.

## What Changed

- Extended the kingdom from one monumental tomb to the wider Sanzhuang cemetery landscape.
- Added Chendun as evidence that a royal cemetery can include an elite burial whose body and named seal do not identify a Liu king.
- Preserved the five-generation, six-king overlap while qualifying the supplied duration and individual occupant claims.

## Relationships

- [[ChendunHanTomb]] - elite burial demonstrating that cemetery membership does not by itself identify a royal occupant.
- [[DaqingdunHanTomb]] - monumental tomb whose board labels materially connect the site to the kingdom's royal cemetery.
- [[LiuSuiSishuiSeal]] - unresolved named seal that does not match the episode's transmitted list of kings.
