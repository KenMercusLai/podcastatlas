# Joseph Conrad

## Overview

Joseph Conrad was a Polish-born British merchant seaman and English-language novelist whose 1890 service in the [[CongoFreeState|Congo Free State]] became the experiential basis for [[HeartOfDarkness|Heart of Darkness]].

## Current Profile

The episode presents Conrad as a skeptical, conservative writer whose Congo journey destroyed an earlier attraction to the supposed blank spaces of imperial maps. He encountered forced labor, physical abuse, illness, depopulated settlements, and a commercial system organized around ivory; he later condemned Leopold's regime as plunder. Yet his literary attack on that system did not make him a straightforward modern anti-imperialist, and the racial limits of his representation remain central to the novella's reception.

## Key Characteristics

- Born Józef Teodor Konrad Korzeniowski in 1857 to a Polish noble and revolutionary family in what is now Ukraine.
- Built a maritime career in the British Merchant Marine before becoming a full-time writer in 1894.
- Wrote in English as his third language and developed a layered, dreamlike narrative style associated with literary modernism.
- Entered the Congo in 1890 with some belief in the civilising mission but left ill, alienated, and increasingly hostile to Leopoldian exploitation.
- Converted personal experience into fiction while using ambiguity and narrative distance rather than documentary exposition.

## Qualifications

The page reflects one podcast's biographical and literary synthesis. Conrad's inward response to Congo violence, the exact real-life models for Kurtz, and the degree to which his later fiction transcends or reproduces imperial assumptions remain interpretive questions.
