# Mehmed II

## Overview

Mehmed II was the Ottoman sultan whose capture of Constantinople in 1453 supplies the episode's clearest symbolic end to the eastern Roman Empire.

## Current Profile

The episode distinguishes conquest from accepted succession. Mehmed's victory ended Constantinople's Byzantine imperial rule and shocked Latin Christendom, while the survival of Trebizond until 1461 keeps 1453 from being the strict last territorial date.

Mehmed is described as having some awareness of Roman inheritance, but the source argues that Ottoman Constantinople more often displayed the triumph of Islam over the former Christian empire than a durable Roman self-identification. Possession of the capital created a succession claim without settling its content or longevity.

## Key Characteristics

- Ottoman sultan who captured Constantinople in 1453.
- Ruler associated with the dominant symbolic endpoint of Byzantium.
- Conqueror with some consciousness of Roman inheritance.
- Test case for the difference between possession of Rome's capital and continued Roman identity.
