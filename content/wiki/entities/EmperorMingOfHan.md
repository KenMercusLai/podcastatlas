## Overview

[[EmperorMingOfHan|汉明帝]] is now synthesized as a restraint-and-ritual emperor rather than only as a later ritual precedent. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-744-dahan-genggong-qiusheng-caipu-jingrang-beiye-wangchenmoji-lvsumpsnpb1tpk7t0xrx7vatd5mx|Hanji 744]] gives his death, reign-memory, frugal burial instructions, and outer-relative restraint; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-899-hao-qing-dou-tanguan-ljhic1plzmv8k6lu73j5terihanx|Hanji 899]] later shows [[CaiYong|蔡邕]] reading Mingdi's Yuanling sacrifice precedent as an expression of filial ritual intention. The current judgment is that Mingdi's authority is remembered through bounded ceremony: ritual matters, but expenditure, tomb construction, and consort-family advancement are supposed to remain restrained.

## Current Profile

Hanji 744 identifies Mingdi as the second Eastern Han emperor, ruling for eighteen Yongping years and dying in 75 CE at age forty-eight. The episode frames his reign as part of the "Ming-Zhang governance" memory: conservative, administrator-centered, and resistant to uncontrolled palace-family privilege.

The same source emphasizes Mingdi's control of outer relatives. It recounts that he refuses a Guantao princess request to advance her son beyond monetary reward, and that Yan Zhang is not promoted despite competence because his sister's harem position would make the appointment look like consort-family favoritism. Mingdi's restraint is therefore not abstract morality; it is a personnel rule about preventing private access to imperial power from becoming public office.

Hanji 744 also makes Mingdi a model for [[FrugalImperialBurialRestraint|节俭帝陵约束]]. His death instruction avoids a separate sacrificial temple, places his tablet in Yin Taihou's side hall, and orders a simplified Xianjie Ling burial without walls, mound-like display, or treasures. When [[EmperorZhangOfHan|汉章帝]] later considers building a tomb town, [[LiuCangDongpingKing|刘苍]] argues that doing so would violate Mingdi's frugal intent, waste resources, and disturb the people; Zhangdi accepts the remonstrance.

Hanji 899 adds a later ritual-memory layer. When [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]] visits [[LiuXiu|光武帝]]'s Yuanling, Cai Yong argues that Mingdi's tomb-sacrifice arrangement preserves deep filial intention through ritual form rather than expendable ceremony. This makes Mingdi a precedent for emotionally meaningful ceremony as well as for restraint.

Taken together, the sources do not make Mingdi anti-ritual. They present him as a ruler whose legitimacy rests on ordering ritual, family, officials, and death practice so that imperial memory reinforces public restraint.

## Key Characteristics

- Eastern Han second emperor whose source profile now includes reign memory, death, burial instructions, and later ritual interpretation.
- Frugal death-planning figure whose will and Xianjie Ling arrangements restrain temple, treasure, mound, and tomb-town expansion.
- Outer-relative restraint model who blocks palace-family access from becoming office advancement.
- Ritual precedent whose Yuanling sacrifice arrangement is later interpreted by Cai Yong as hidden filial intention.
- Inherited standard for Zhangdi, since Liu Cang uses Mingdi's frugal intent to limit successor construction.
- Conservative governance memory figure associated by the source with administrator-centered "Ming-Zhang governance."

## Qualifications

- Hanji 744 is a compressed reign-and-death profile, not a full biography of Mingdi's policies.
- "Ming-Zhang governance" and population-governance praise are source framing; this page does not independently verify demographic or administrative outcomes.
- The Xianjie Ling details, Guantao-princess example, Yan Zhang example, and exact death/burial dates remain source-grounded to Hanji 744 unless later notes broaden them.
- Hanji 899 shows Mingdi investing in ritual memory, so frugal burial restraint should not be read as rejection of ritual order.
- Mingdi's restraint standard is connected to Zhangdi's opening rule, but later Zhangdi-era Ma-family dynamics require separate evidence from Zhangdi sources.
