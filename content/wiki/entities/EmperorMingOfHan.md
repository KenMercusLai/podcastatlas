## Overview

[[EmperorMingOfHan|汉明帝]] is now synthesized from a Hanji 734-738 early Yongping layer of public teacher honor, Mingtang and Biyong ritual, empress and heir designation, construction-burden remonstrance, punitive temperament, local-official exemplars, royal-kin withdrawal, private-client risk, elite punishment, Western Regions volatility, omen humility, empress ritual memory, Northern Xiongnu trade and envoy dignity, Southern Xiongnu containment, official-capacity advice, eclipse self-criticism, official evaluation, classics education, royal-kin treason judgment, and title-duty correction. Later sources extend that profile through royal affection, Ailao incorporation, Bianqu/Huanghe hydraulic capacity, the Liu Ying case, Confucian ritual, frontier restoration, filial affect, frugal burial, outer-relative restraint, and later ritual precedent. The current profile keeps two traits together: Mingdi can build disciplined order and correct mistakes, but he can also rule through surveillance, anger, and overreach until evidence, ritual constraint, courageous officials, or a public feedback event force reinterpretation.

## Current Profile

Hanji 734 supplies the earliest current layer. In Yongping 2 / 59 CE, Mingdi performs Mingtang sacrifice to [[LiuXiu|Guangwu]], observes the heavens at Lingtai, declares amnesty, visits Biyong for archery, and stages the first Biyong elder-care ceremony. The central figure is his former `Shangshu` teacher [[HuanRongEasternHan|Huan Rong]]. Mingdi makes Huan Rong `wugeng`, gives him ceremonial place and reward, listens to him with text in hand, refuses to answer a classics question while the teacher is present, cares for him in illness, and honors his death. This adds [[ImperialTeacherHonorRitual|imperial teacher-honor ritual]] to Mingdi's profile: public deference to a teacher becomes a way to elevate Confucian learning and model role order for officials.

The same 59 CE layer also shows early royal-kin favor and elite discipline. Mingdi gives unusual favor to Zhongshan king Liu Yan because of Guo and Yin dowager ties, sacrifices to Xiao He and Huo Guang during the Chang'an trip, executes Dou Lin after corruption and deception charges while rebuking [[DouRongEasternHan|Dou Rong]], holds the first Five Suburban seasonal rites, and punishes Yin Feng's killing of Princess Liyi despite Yin-family status. These branches are kept source-scoped because they are less developed than the Huan Rong story, but they show Mingdi's court already joining ritual display, family favor, and legal discipline.

Hanji 735 follows as the next current layer. In Yongping 3 / 60 CE, Mingdi removes and appoints senior ministers, makes [[EmpressMaMingdeEasternHan|Ma]] empress, and establishes Liu Da, the child Ma raises, as crown prince. The same source shows the best and worst sides of his court: [[ZhongliYiEasternHan|Zhongli Yi]] can persuade him to stop North Palace construction by linking an omen to people's farming burden, yet Mingdi is also described as strict, surveillance-prone, and sometimes personally violent toward officials. [[YaoSongEasternHan|Yao Song]]'s ritual quotation defuses one anger scene, while Zhongli Yi's later leniency memorial is protected but not adopted.

Hanji 735 also gives a local and frontier background. [[LiuPingEasternHan|Liu Ping]]'s Quanjiao governance creates unusually strong trust signals, and [[GuoHeEasternHan|Guo He]] is publicly rewarded so commoners can see good administration honored. The Western Regions notice places [[ShacheKingdomLateHan|Shache]] and [[KhotanKingdomLateHan|Khotan]] in violent churn before the Hanji 736 continuation.

Hanji 736 then follows. In 61-63 CE, Mingdi responds to [[LiuCangDongpingKing|Liu Cang]]'s remonstrance by turning back from a planned hunt, then lets Liu Cang return to Dongping after repeated requests to leave the central power field while retaining the piaoji general seal and receiving major gifts. The same source shows Mingdi punishing [[LiangSongLinxiangMarquis|Liang Song]]'s court slander, the implicated-guest danger that [[ZhengZhongFrontierEasternHan|Zheng Zhong]] had avoided earlier, and [[DouMuEasternHan|Dou Mu]]'s false use of [[YinLihua|Yin Taihou]]'s authority for marriage coercion. Mingdi's treasure-cauldron edict adds another restraint layer: auspicious objects may be recognized, but exaggerated saintly praise is to be rejected rather than rewarded.

Hanji 737 supplies the next early Yongping layer. In 64 CE, Mingdi's court buries [[YinLihua|Empress Dowager Yin Lihua]] and gives her the 光烈 posthumous title, tying maternal dynastic memory to [[ImperialConsortPosthumousTitleSystem|empress posthumous-title practice]]. In 65 CE, Mingdi accepts trade contact with [[NorthernXiongnuLateHan|Northern Xiongnu]] despite ongoing raids, receives [[ZongJunEasternHan|Zong Jun]]'s warning that literary or merely clean officials are not enough for actual governance, and reacts to a solar eclipse by soliciting confidential criticism, publishing it, and treating the criticisms as his own crimes.

The earliest Liu Ying and Duliaoying layers show Mingdi balancing tolerance and containment. His退赎诏 returns [[LiuYingChuKingEasternHan|Liu Ying]]'s redemption silk and treats Huang-Lao and Buddhist fasting as harmless devotion, which complicates the later Chu-prison suspicion. After [[ZhengZhongFrontierEasternHan|Zheng Zhong]] detects [[XuguDuhouSouthernXiongnu|Xugu Duhou]] seeking Northern Xiongnu help, Mingdi's court creates [[DuliaoCampEasternHan|Duliaoying]] to prevent Southern/Northern Xiongnu collusion.

Hanji 738 then extends the same 65-67 CE frontier signal problem. Northern Xiongnu offers tribute while continuing frontier raids. Mingdi considers sending a return mission, but Zheng warns that the chanyu will use Han contact to strengthen his prestige, unsettle [[SouthernXiongnu|Southern Xiongnu]], affect [[Wuhuan|Wuhuan]], and make Western Regions states doubt Han resolve. Mingdi rejects the warning, sends Zheng anyway, and imprisons him after repeated memorials. Later Northern Xiongnu testimony confirms that Zheng had once risked death rather than kowtow, so Mingdi recalls and appoints him. This gives Mingdi a correctable but costly judgment pattern before the later Chu-prison corrections.

The same source shows Mingdi trying to make order administrative and educational. He requires annual reporting of the best and worst local officials after sufficient tenure, promotes Confucian education for the crown prince, princes, noble children, and official families, creates palace schooling for outer-relative children, and extends classics study even into guard units. The episode therefore places Mingdi's court between [[AppointmentQualityAsStateCapacity|appointment quality]], [[MeritBasedRewardPunishment|reward-punishment credibility]], and [[LiAsPoliticalOrder|ritual-classics order]].

Hanji 738 also supplies two family-duty tests. [[LiuJingGuanglingKingEasternHan|Guangling King Liu Jing]] first receives fraternal containment after rebellion-suggestive physiognomy talk, but continued shamanic sacrifice and curse activity lead [[FanTiaoEasternHan|Fan Tiao]] to argue that the empire is Gaozu's dynastic realm rather than Mingdi's private property. In the [[DingHongEasternHan|Ding Hong]] branch, Mingdi's court refuses Ding's request to yield his inherited title, and later appoints him shizhong after [[BaoJunEasternHan|Bao Jun]] persuades him to accept public responsibility.

Hanji 739 adds royal affection, southwestern incorporation, hydraulic capacity, and the opening Liu Ying case. Mingdi misses [[LiuCangDongpingKing|Liu Cang]] after a royal visit and praises his "doing good" ethic. He incorporates [[AilaoKingLiuMao|Ailao King Liu Mao]] through county creation and road/crossing works, then sends [[WangJingBianquEasternHan|Wang Jing]] and Wang Wu to repair Bianqu and separate Yellow River/Bian River flows. But the same source begins [[LiuYingChuKingEasternHan|Liu Ying]]'s fall: initial family restraint gives way to suicide, ministerial shame, and wider implication.

Hanji 740 continues the Chu-prison layer. Mingdi's suspicion after Liu Ying's suicide helps create a [[CoerciveImplicationCascade|coercive implication cascade]] of torture, fear, false implication, deaths, exile, and imprisonment. Partial correction arrives through [[LuXuEasternHan|Lu Xu]]'s character evidence, [[JianLangEasternHan|Jian Lang]]'s risky verification, [[EmpressMaMingdeEasternHan|Empress Ma]]'s warning, and [[YuanAnEasternHan|Yuan An]]'s unsupported-case releases. Mingdi is therefore responsive, but often only after high human cost.

Hanji 741 and Hanji 743 supply the agrarian, classics, and frontier restoration profile. Mingdi performs the ploughing rite, visits Confucius's former residence, has royal youths lecture on classics, and then authorizes a four-route campaign after [[GengBingEasternHan|Geng Bing]] and [[DouGuEasternHan|Dou Gu]] push for renewed Western Regions strategy. In 74 CE, his Yuanling dream of [[LiuXiu|Guangwu]] and [[YinLihua|Yin Lihua]] makes filial ritual emotionally concrete, while Dou Gu and Geng Bing's Cheshi campaign restores Han offices in the Western Regions.

Hanji 744 gives the death and restraint layer. Mingdi dies in 75 CE after eighteen Yongping years, and the episode frames his reign as part of "Ming-Zhang governance": conservative, administrator-centered, and resistant to uncontrolled palace-family privilege. His death instructions avoid a separate sacrificial temple, simplify Xianjie Ling, and leave [[EmperorZhangOfHan|Zhangdi]] able to accept Liu Cang's later warning against a costly tomb town.

Hanji 899 supplies later ritual memory. When [[EmperorLingOfHan|Lingdi]] visits Guangwu's Yuanling, [[CaiYong|Cai Yong]] interprets Mingdi's tomb-sacrifice arrangement as preserving filial intention through solemn form rather than dispensable ceremony. Mingdi thus remains a precedent for emotionally meaningful ritual as well as frugal restraint.

## Key Characteristics

- Eastern Han second emperor whose profile joins teacher-honor ritual, succession designation, royal-kin distance management, elite punishment, frontier diplomacy, local exemplars, official evaluation, Confucian education, material state capacity, legal overreach, correction, ritual, filial grief, and frugal restraint.
- Correctable ruler whose judgment can change after evidence, public criticism, or courageous remonstrance, but whose harshness can require subordinates to manage anger and retaliation risk.
- Frontier ruler who treats Northern Xiongnu pressure as a credibility problem, yet initially misses Zheng Zhong's warning about diplomatic and ritual signaling.
- Administrative and educational ruler who uses teacher protocol, annual official review, classics schooling, agrarian ritual, and later conference-style learning to stabilize court order.
- Royal-family ruler whose affection and kinship restraint coexist with treason-law boundaries in the Liu Jing and Liu Ying cases.
- State-capacity ruler whose halted North Palace works, Liu Cang settlement, Ailao incorporation, Bianqu works, Northern Xiongnu campaign, and Western Regions restoration show both ambition and capacity for correction.
- Filial, teacher-honoring, and frugal ritual actor whose Huan Rong ceremonies, Yuanling grief, Shouling order, death instructions, and later ritual memory convert hierarchy and dynastic memory into disciplined form.

## Qualifications

Mingdi should not be flattened into either benevolent filial ruler or punitive autocrat. The current profile holds both together: he builds evaluation, education, frontier capacity, hydraulic works, ritual order, local exemplar display, and frugal memory, while also needing feedback mechanisms to correct construction burdens, misread frontier advice, anger, and later treason overreach. Hanji 734's Li Gong, exact `sanlao` and `wugeng` institutional details, Zhongshan king Liu Yan, Xiao He/Huo Guang sacrifice details, Dou Lin, Yin Feng, Princess Liyi/Liu Shou, Five Suburban seasonal rites, and South Xiongnu succession remain source-scoped. Hanji 735's Zhao Xi, Guo Dan, Yu Yan, Ma Yuan/Yuntai omission, Liu Jian, Liu Xian, exact star-field details, and Shache-Khotan minor succession actors remain source-scoped. Hanji 736's Liu Jian death notice, Three Excellencies personnel changes, Liu Xu marriage target, minor Dou-family names, Shache succession names, and exact treasure-cauldron provenance remain source-scoped. Hanji 737's gendered Yin Lihua reading, Buddhism transmission narrative, and exact Duliaoying details remain source-scoped. Hanji 738's Zheng Zhong and Ding Hong normalizations remain source-sensitive; Liu Gong, Liu Dang, Four Surnames minor marquises, Ding family details, and minor ritual dates remain source-scoped. Hanji 740's Buddhist-secret-organization explanation remains source-scoped rather than a settled cause.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 734 as Mingdi's earliest current layer: Mingtang and Biyong ritual, Huan Rong teacher honor, special royal-kin favor, ancestor-minister sacrifice, Dou and Yin elite discipline, and Five Suburban seasonal rites.
- Reframed Hanji 735 as the next early Yongping layer rather than the earliest current layer.
- Reframed Mingdi's correctability to include halted construction and public self-blame as well as later reversals after misread frontier or legal evidence.
- Preserved Hanji 736-737 as early Yongping royal-kin, anti-flattery, empress-ritual, Northern Xiongnu, Liu Ying tolerance, Duliaoying, and eclipse layers.
- Preserved Hanji 738's official evaluation, classics schooling, Liu Jing's royal-kin case, Ding Hong's title-duty branch, and the later Liu Cang, Ailao, Bianqu, Chu-prison, frontier-restoration, filial, frugal, and outer-relative layers.
