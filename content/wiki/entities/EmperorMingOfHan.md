## Overview

[[EmperorMingOfHan|汉明帝]] is synthesized as a ruler whose profile now begins with royal affection, Ailao incorporation, Bianqu/Huanghe hydraulic capacity, and the opening Liu Ying case before moving into punitive treason-case overreach and partial legal correction. Those layers sit beside agrarian-Confucian ritual order, frontier restoration, filial affect, frugal restraint, outer-relative discipline, and later ritual precedent. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-739-zuoyong-tianxia-meinv-de-huangdi-qishi-ye-hui-gudu-lsoogpawume-fpmitbigklhzdbjl|Hanji 739]] backfills the 68-71 CE opening before [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-740-rang-ni-tihuguanding-de-zhichang-zhihui-zhichang-bibei-lih5b-ighkyoycawm5c-dbrnuiew|Hanji 740]]'s fuller Chu-prison layer and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-741-weishenme-gudai-xiongnu-zongyao-ruqin-zhongyuan-lllpgozgi6yd5pscw8z5ofidk5rp|Hanji 741]]'s agrarian and frontier layer.

## Current Profile

Hanji 739 supplies the earliest current layer. Mingdi personally sends off [[LiuCangDongpingKing|东平王刘苍]] after a royal visit, returns to the palace feeling the absence, and writes to Liu Cang with explicit longing. The same source gives Liu Cang's "为善最乐" answer, making Mingdi's courtly affection visible alongside moralized royal-family praise.

The same source also gives Mingdi a material state-capacity layer before the later Xiongnu campaigns. [[AilaoKingLiuMao|哀牢王柳貌]] submits with a large population, after which the court creates Ailao and Bonan counties and begins difficult road and Lancang/Mekong crossing works. Mingdi then sends [[WangJingBianquEasternHan|王景]] and Wang Wu to repair Bianqu and separate Yellow River/Bian River flows, a successful but very expensive [[BianquHydraulicGovernance|hydraulic governance]] project possible only after postwar exhaustion has eased.

Hanji 739 also opens the Liu Ying case. Mingdi initially refuses to execute [[LiuYingChuKingEasternHan|楚王刘英]], preserves support for Liu Ying's children and mother, and punishes him through deposition and relocation. That familial restraint does not hold the case in place: Yu Yan is rebuked and dies by suicide, Liu Ying also dies by suicide, and the case begins expanding into years of investigation, death, exile, and imprisonment.

Hanji 740 continues that Chu-prison layer and complicates the earlier profile. Mingdi hears accusations around Liu Ying, rebukes [[YuYanEasternHan|Yu Yan]], then continues searching for hidden accomplices after Liu Ying's suicide. The episode presents the resulting Chu-prison case as a destructive [[CoerciveImplicationCascade|coercive implication cascade]]: torture, fear, and loyalty signaling implicate royal kin, local elites, officials, and many households.

The same source also gives a partial-correction layer. Mingdi responds to [[LuXuEasternHan|Lu Xu]]'s family and character evidence, releases [[JianLangEasternHan|Jian Lang]] after first threatening punishment, goes to Luoyang prison, releases more than one thousand people, and later approves [[YuanAnEasternHan|Yuan An]]'s release of unsupported cases after [[EmpressMaMingdeEasternHan|Empress Ma]] warns against excessive punishment. This does not erase the harm; it shows that Mingdi's judgment can move, but often only after high-cost remonstrance and visible evidence.

Hanji 740 also adds Mingdi's religious-suspicion and Shouling layers. The episode treats Liu Ying's Buddhist practice and private organization as one possible reason Mingdi fears hidden networks, while keeping that explanation source-scoped. Mingdi's Shouling order anticipates later burial restraint by limiting tomb construction, offerings, staffing, and unauthorized expansion.

Hanji 741 then shows Mingdi reinforcing imperial order through agriculture, classics, and kinship structure before the frontier campaign: he performs the ploughing rite, visits Confucius's former residence, has royal youths lecture on classics, and grants royal titles within controlled bounds. Northern Xiongnu raids raise the problem that refusing all war can conserve resources but also leave border people doubting the court; Mingdi consults Dou Gu, [[GengBingEasternHan|Geng Bing]], Ma Liao, Liu Zhang, and other officials before authorizing a four-route campaign.

Hanji 743 adds the filial and Western Regions layer. Mingdi dreams of [[LiuXiu|光武帝]] and [[YinLihua|阴丽华]] as alive and young, wakes crying, visits Yuanling, handles parental objects with grief, attributes omens and submissions to Gaozu and Guangwu, and approves Dou Gu's petition to restore the Western Regions Protector and Wuji Colonel offices after the Cheshi campaign.

Hanji 744 gives the death and restraint layer. Mingdi dies in 75 CE at age forty-eight after eighteen Yongping years. The episode frames his reign as part of "Ming-Zhang governance" memory: conservative, administrator-centered, and resistant to uncontrolled palace-family privilege. His death instructions avoid a separate sacrificial temple, simplify Xianjie Ling, and later let [[LiuCangDongpingKing|Liu Cang]] persuade Zhangdi not to build a tomb town.

Hanji 899 adds a later ritual-memory layer. When [[EmperorLingOfHan|Lingdi]] visits Guangwu's Yuanling, [[CaiYong|Cai Yong]] argues that Mingdi's tomb-sacrifice arrangement preserves deep filial intention through ritual form rather than expendable ceremony. This makes Mingdi a precedent for emotionally meaningful ceremony as well as for restraint.

## Key Characteristics

- Eastern Han second emperor whose current profile joins royal affection, hydraulic and frontier state capacity, treason-case overreach, partial correction, ritual order, filial affect, frugality, and outer-relative restraint.
- Royal-family ruler whose longing for Liu Cang and initial restraint toward Liu Ying show kinship feeling before the later legal panic.
- State-capacity ruler whose court can absorb Ailao submission and fund Wang Jing's large Bianqu works once postwar exhaustion has eased.
- Treason-case ruler whose anger and suspicion help turn Liu Ying's case into a coercive implication cascade.
- Correctable but late-moving ruler who responds to Lu Xu, Jian Lang, Empress Ma, and Yuan An only after severe harm has already occurred.
- Agrarian, Confucian, and frontier ruler who uses ploughing, Confucius-site visitation, classical teaching, Northern Xiongnu campaigns, and Western Regions restoration to display recovered imperial order.
- Filial and frugal ritual actor whose Yuanling grief, Shouling order, will, and Xianjie Ling arrangements turn dynastic memory into restraint as well as solemnity.

## Qualifications

Mingdi should not be reduced to either benevolent filial ruler or cruel legal autocrat. The current profile holds both together: royal affection, frontier incorporation, hydraulic success, agrarian ritual, Confucian hierarchy, filial grief, frontier restoration, and frugal restraint coexist with a treason panic that kills, exiles, and imprisons many people before partial correction. Hanji 739's Wang Wu, Fu Gong, Mou Rong, Xing Mu, Yan Guang, Xu Taihou, Fan Tiao, and detailed engineering mechanics remain source-scoped. Hanji 740's Buddhist-secret-organization explanation remains source-scoped rather than a settled cause. Hanji 741's route details, Ma Liao and Liu Zhang's exact contributions, Qin Peng, Geng Zhong, Wu Tang, Lai Miao, Wen Mu, and force counts remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 739 as the earliest current Mingdi layer: Liu Cang affection, Ailao incorporation, Bianqu hydraulic works, and the opening Liu Ying case.
- Reframed Hanji 740 as the continuation and fuller correction layer of a case that opens in Hanji 739.
- Added Wang Jing's Bianqu project and Liu Mao's Ailao submission as material state-capacity evidence inside the Mingdi profile.
- Preserved the existing agrarian-Confucian, frontier, filial, frugal, Shouling, and outer-relative restraint synthesis.
