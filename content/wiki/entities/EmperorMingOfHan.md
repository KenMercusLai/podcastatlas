## Overview

[[EmperorMingOfHan|汉明帝]] is synthesized as a ruler whose profile now includes punitive treason-case overreach and partial legal correction alongside agrarian-Confucian ritual order, frontier restoration, filial affect, frugal restraint, outer-relative discipline, and later ritual precedent. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-740-rang-ni-tihuguanding-de-zhichang-zhihui-zhichang-bibei-lih5b-ighkyoycawm5c-dbrnuiew|Hanji 740]] backfills the 70-71 CE Chu-prison layer before [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-741-weishenme-gudai-xiongnu-zongyao-ruqin-zhongyuan-lllpgozgi6yd5pscw8z5ofidk5rp|Hanji 741]]'s agrarian and frontier layer.

## Current Profile

Hanji 740 supplies the earliest current layer and complicates the earlier profile. Mingdi hears accusations around [[LiuYingChuKingEasternHan|楚王刘英]], rebukes Yu Yan, then continues searching for hidden accomplices after Liu Ying's suicide. The episode presents the resulting Chu-prison case as a destructive [[CoerciveImplicationCascade|coercive implication cascade]]: torture, fear, and loyalty signaling implicate royal kin, local elites, officials, and many households.

The same source also gives a partial-correction layer. Mingdi responds to [[LuXuEasternHan|Lu Xu]]'s family and character evidence, releases [[JianLangEasternHan|Jian Lang]] after first threatening punishment, goes to Luoyang prison, releases more than one thousand people, and later approves [[YuanAnEasternHan|Yuan An]]'s release of unsupported cases after [[EmpressMaMingdeEasternHan|Empress Ma]] warns against excessive punishment. This does not erase the harm; it shows that Mingdi's judgment can move, but often only after high-cost remonstrance and visible evidence.

Hanji 740 also adds Mingdi's religious-suspicion and Shouling layers. The episode treats Liu Ying's Buddhist practice and private organization as one possible reason Mingdi fears hidden networks, while keeping that explanation source-scoped. Mingdi's Shouling order anticipates later burial restraint by limiting tomb construction, offerings, staffing, and unauthorized expansion.

Hanji 741 then shows Mingdi reinforcing imperial order through agriculture, classics, and kinship structure before the frontier campaign: he performs the ploughing rite, visits Confucius's former residence, has royal youths lecture on classics, and grants royal titles within controlled bounds. Northern Xiongnu raids raise the problem that refusing all war can conserve resources but also leave border people doubting the court; Mingdi consults Dou Gu, [[GengBingEasternHan|Geng Bing]], Ma Liao, Liu Zhang, and other officials before authorizing a four-route campaign.

Hanji 743 adds the filial and Western Regions layer. Mingdi dreams of [[LiuXiu|光武帝]] and [[YinLihua|阴丽华]] as alive and young, wakes crying, visits Yuanling, handles parental objects with grief, attributes omens and submissions to Gaozu and Guangwu, and approves Dou Gu's petition to restore the Western Regions Protector and Wuji Colonel offices after the Cheshi campaign.

Hanji 744 gives the death and restraint layer. Mingdi dies in 75 CE at age forty-eight after eighteen Yongping years. The episode frames his reign as part of "Ming-Zhang governance" memory: conservative, administrator-centered, and resistant to uncontrolled palace-family privilege. His death instructions avoid a separate sacrificial temple, simplify Xianjie Ling, and later let [[LiuCangDongpingKing|Liu Cang]] persuade Zhangdi not to build a tomb town.

Hanji 899 adds a later ritual-memory layer. When [[EmperorLingOfHan|Lingdi]] visits Guangwu's Yuanling, [[CaiYong|Cai Yong]] argues that Mingdi's tomb-sacrifice arrangement preserves deep filial intention through ritual form rather than expendable ceremony. This makes Mingdi a precedent for emotionally meaningful ceremony as well as for restraint.

## Key Characteristics

- Eastern Han second emperor whose current profile joins legal overreach, partial correction, ritual order, frontier restoration, filial affect, frugality, and outer-relative restraint.
- Treason-case ruler whose anger and suspicion help turn Liu Ying's case into a coercive implication cascade.
- Correctable but late-moving ruler who responds to Lu Xu, Jian Lang, Empress Ma, and Yuan An only after severe harm has already occurred.
- Agrarian-Confucian ruler who uses ploughing, Confucius-site visitation, and classical teaching to display recovered imperial order.
- Frontier ruler who treats Northern Xiongnu raids as a credibility problem requiring bounded offensive action and Western Regions restoration.
- Filial ritual actor whose Yuanling dream, tears, tomb visit, and attention to parental objects turn dynastic memory into felt hierarchy.
- Frugal death-planning figure whose Shouling order, will, and Xianjie Ling arrangements restrain temple, treasure, mound, and tomb-town expansion.

## Qualifications

Mingdi should not be reduced to either benevolent filial ruler or cruel legal autocrat. The current profile holds both together: agrarian ritual, Confucian hierarchy, filial grief, frontier restoration, and frugal restraint coexist with a treason panic that kills, exiles, and imprisons many people before partial correction. Hanji 740's Buddhist-secret-organization explanation remains source-scoped rather than a settled cause. Hanji 741's route details, Ma Liao and Liu Zhang's exact contributions, Qin Peng, Geng Zhong, Wu Tang, Lai Miao, Wen Mu, and force counts remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 740 as the earliest current Mingdi layer before the frontier, filial, burial, and later ritual-memory episodes.
- Reframed Mingdi's legal profile to include Chu-prison overreach and delayed correction, not only ritual order and restraint.
- Added Mingdi's Shouling order as the predeath layer behind frugal imperial burial restraint.
- Preserved the existing agrarian-Confucian, frontier, filial, frugal, and outer-relative restraint synthesis.
