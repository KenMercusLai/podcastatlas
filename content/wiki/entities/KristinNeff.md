# Kristin Neff

## Overview
Kristin Neff is a psychologist represented here through a [[HiddenBrain]] episode on [[SelfCompassion]], self-criticism, shame, motivation, accountability, and relationships.

## Current Profile
Neff defines self-compassion through self-kindness, mindful recognition of suffering, and common humanity. Her practical account treats the inner critic as a protective but ineffective response rather than an enemy that must be shamed in turn: a person can acknowledge its wish for safety, then replace threat and contempt with encouragement toward a specific change.

Her autobiographical case gives the profile its ethical boundary. After an affair, divorce, rejection, and the other man's death, compassion did not mean denying deception or harm. In her telling, it helped her tolerate guilt without converting it into a globally bad identity, accept responsibility, and reaffirm honesty. Her research claims similarly frame care as a possible support for motivation, apology, healthy behavior, and relationship quality rather than as permission to avoid standards.

## Key Characteristics
- Defines [[SelfCompassion]] through self-kindness, mindfulness, and common humanity.
- Distinguishes behavior-specific guilt from identity-level shame and treats that distinction as relevant to accountability.
- Interprets the inner critic as a threat-based protective strategy whose intention can be acknowledged without retaining its methods.
- Separates striving to do well from perfectionism that makes imperfection proof of personal badness.
- Presents compassion as a sustainable motivator rather than the opposite of conscientiousness or change.
- Offers brief practices involving acknowledgment of pain, common humanity, supportive language, soothing touch, and friend perspective.

## Qualifications
This profile is bounded to one structured episode summary rather than Neff's full publications, measures, clinical views, or research record. Her personal narrative is evidence of her interpretation and experience, not proof that compassion alone produces accountability or repair. The episode's claims about motivation, conscientiousness, apology, depression, anxiety, suicidality, health behavior, procrastination, and relationship satisfaction require the underlying studies before effect sizes, causal direction, population scope, or clinical use can be established. Acute risk or severe and persistent distress requires qualified support rather than a self-guided exercise alone.
