# Michael Wood

## Overview

Michael Wood is a historian and broadcaster who appears in the bounded source as the guest guiding a wide thematic survey of Chinese history.

## Current Profile

Wood organizes the episode around [[ChineseCivilizationalContinuity]], arguing that China repeatedly changes political form while retaining a durable ideal of unity, shared writing and culture, bureaucratic habits, and Confucian moral language. He does not treat continuity as stasis: Qing conquest changed the territorial map, Western coercion accelerated crisis, revolution remade institutions, and Deng-era reform redirected economic life.

His method combines long-range comparison with interview memory and modern political interpretation. That makes the episode an accessible map of recurring themes rather than a detailed chronology or a substitute for specialist debate on each period.

## Key Characteristics

- Historian and broadcaster specializing here in a long-range account of China.
- Uses continuity, fragmentation, reunification, and modernization as organizing themes.
- Connects institutions and political ideas to everyday culture and collective social practice.
- Distinguishes the Chinese heartland from later Qing territorial expansion.
- Treats modern invocations of history as political choices rather than neutral remembrance.

## Qualifications

The bounded evidence is a single broad podcast conversation. Informal interviews, causal comparisons, recollections of acquaintances, and readings of contemporary political motives remain Wood's source-scoped interpretations rather than independently verified findings here.
