## Overview

越王勾践 / King Goujian of Yue is presented across the bounded sources as a patient ruler who survives apparent submission, exploits Wu's overextension, destroys his rival, and then makes victory dangerous for ministers and morally costly for the literary world around him.

## Current Profile

The historical-retelling sources emphasize strategic patience. Within [[Zigong|子贡 / 端木赐]]'s diplomatic cascade, Goujian reassures [[Fuchai|吴王夫差]] while waiting for [[WuState|吴国]] to exhaust itself. A later persuasion source uses that sequence as a warning that a submissive neighbor can become a rear threat to an overextended hegemon.

Victory does not complete a heroic arc. [[FanLi|范蠡]] reads the postwar court as unsafe, leaves, and warns [[WenZhongYue|文种]], whose later death makes Goujian part of a broader exit-risk pattern. The [[YueNvJian|《越女剑》]] discussion adds a literary layer: revenge remains politically effective, but pleasure at [[WuZixu|伍子胥]]'s death and the use of Ah Qing's gift sharpen the cost in human feeling.

## Key Characteristics

- He uses visible submission and time to preserve Yue until Wu becomes vulnerable.
- His story functions as a warning against exposing the rear while campaigning through unreliable partners.
- He converts endurance and humiliation into successful revenge rather than reconciliation.
- Post-victory proximity becomes dangerous for ministers who know the ruler's earlier weakness.
- In Jin Yong's fictional treatment, state success and personal cruelty remain adjacent rather than morally interchangeable.

### Post-victory ministerial danger

- [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-57-gudai-fa-dushi-haiyou-zhe-zuoyong-lj6wi61osaytowgmdp8dtkrwb-si]] grounds Fan Li's departure, warning to Wen Zhong, and the later forced-suicide lesson.

### Literary revenge and human cost

- [[055-zuihou-yijian-fengqing-wuxian-changqi-beihushi-de-jinyong-jiezuo-yuenvjian-lgahdjfqea0-n7m0pl7yynm3spwe]] reads the novella's Goujian as controlled, effective, and cruel, while distinguishing Ah Qing's personal motive from the state's revenge project.

## Qualifications

The sources combine diplomatic anecdote, later historical analogy, and literary criticism. Exact motives, dialogue, inner cruelty, and causal responsibility for later deaths remain source-framed rather than directly established. Jin Yong's portrayal should not be collapsed into the historical ruler, and the Zhouji 84 use of Goujian is an argumentative precedent rather than a full Yue-Wu reconstruction.
