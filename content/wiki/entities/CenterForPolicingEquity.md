## Overview

The Center for Policing Equity is the policing research and reform organization co-founded by [[PhilipAtibaGoff]] and presented in the supplied [[HiddenBrain]] episode as using data, community input, and institutional redesign to reduce racial disparities and police use of force.

## Current Profile

The episode characterizes the center by what it measures: police conduct and outcomes rather than unverifiable private beliefs. Its work applies performance-management logic to public safety, looking for practices and situations that predict harm and then helping agencies or communities change training, deployment, contact, or resource allocation.

The three examples span different intervention levels. Las Vegas changes officer behavior after foot pursuits; Berkeley questions whether low-level traffic violations require an armed stop; Minneapolis uses force data and community input to support investment in homelessness services. Together they make the center's approach preventive and institutional, not merely attitudinal.

## Key Characteristics

- Uses administrative and outcome data to identify racial disparities and high-risk practices.
- Combines measurement with training, policy, contact redesign, or service investment.
- Treats community knowledge as evidence about where policing systems produce harm.
- Distinguishes changing behavior from proving or changing private belief.
- Works across both police practice and non-police public-safety capacity.
- Frames partial, measurable harm reduction as worthwhile while preserving causal uncertainty.

## Qualifications

The bounded source is a favorable public-radio account, not an independent institutional audit. It does not provide the center's methods, contracts, complete intervention portfolio, comparison groups, or causal estimates. Goff explicitly declines to attribute Minneapolis's reported force reduction precisely to the center's work, and the Las Vegas before-and-after figure cannot by itself isolate causation.
