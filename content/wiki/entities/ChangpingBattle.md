# 长平之战 / Battle of Changping

长平之战 / Battle of Changping enters this wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-93-zai-mengli-cangzhe-toutian-gaiming-2-lrlulw0b6v0fb2xpr2i5ifem1h4n]] as the war consequence foreshadowed by [[ZhaoState|赵国]]'s acceptance of [[Shangdang|上党]]. The episode does not yet narrate the campaign; it identifies Shangdang as the prelude and says the next installment will enter the battle.

The source also uses Changping to complicate [[FengTing|冯亭]]'s later image. One tradition has Feng Ting later following [[ZhaoKuo|赵括]] into the Changping fighting and dying there, while another [[Zhanguoce|《战国策》]] branch says he rejected Zhao's reward and returned to Han. The wiki therefore treats Changping here as source-scoped setup rather than a full battle page.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-94-shashen-baiqi-changping-zhi-zhan-zhong-de-caobao-mingjiang-1-loxyybm3njrtnudydafwzu9jp9jz]] moves Changping from foreshadowing into the command-decision phase. In 260 BCE, [[WangHe|王龁]] continues Qin's attack, Zhao suffers repeated defeats, and [[LianPo|廉颇]] shifts to defensive holding. [[ZhaoDan|赵丹 / 赵孝成王]] first loses diplomatic room by sending [[ZhengZhu|郑朱]] to Qin instead of following [[YuQing|虞卿]]'s coalition-first plan, then loses command stability by replacing Lian Po with Zhao Kuo after [[FanJu|范雎]]'s rumor operation.

The same source gives Changping its first Qin-side command contrast. Zhao's replacement is public and rumor-driven; Qin secretly appoints [[BaiQi|白起]] as main commander, keeps Wang He as deputy, and threatens death for disclosure. The battle page should still remain source-scoped because the episode stops before Zhao Kuo's conduct after taking command, but Changping is now clearly a case of diplomatic isolation, [[CounterintelligenceRumorWedge|反间流言楔入]], and [[BattlefieldCommanderReplacementRisk|阵前换将风险]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-94-shashen-baiqi-changping-zhi-zhan-zhong-de-caobao-mingjiang-2-lvqctrazn8ddkcnp-lhspzzmzmns]] moves Changping into the encirclement and starvation stage. After Zhao Kuo replaces Lian Po, he changes the front-line arrangement and attacks across the Dan River; Bai Qi uses feigned retreat and fortified barriers to absorb the attack, then sends side forces and cavalry to cut the Zhao army away from its camp, retreat, and grain route. The battle therefore becomes a case of [[BattlefieldEncirclementLogistics|战场围困与断粮]] rather than only a morality tale about "paper war" command.

The same source widens Changping into a diplomatic starvation crisis. Qin Zhaoxiang Wang moves to Henei and mobilizes males over fifteen to keep Zhao relief and supplies out, while the other states decline effective help. Qi's refusal to lend grain despite Zhouzi's warning makes Changping a concrete case of [[ShieldStateRescueFailure|屏障国救援失灵]] and pushes the trapped Zhao army into forty-six days without food.
