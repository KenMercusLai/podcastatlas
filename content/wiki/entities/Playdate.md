# Playdate

## Overview

Playdate is a small retro-styled handheld game console made and sold by [[PanicSoftware|Panic]].

## Current Profile

In [[should-we-all-be-getting-tariff-refunds-right-now]], Playdate is the physical product that turned a software company into a Malaysian-hardware importer. Its direct checkout and separately recorded tariff line made it possible to link recovered duties to individual customers.

## Key Characteristics

- Handheld console produced for Panic in Malaysia.
- First physical-product manufacturing effort described for the company.
- Early production runs sold out quickly according to the source.
- Direct-sale product whose tariff charge could be recorded by order.

## Evidence

- **Product and production:** [[should-we-all-be-getting-tariff-refunds-right-now]] describes the console, Panic's move into hardware, and Malaysian manufacturing.
- **Demand:** [[should-we-all-be-getting-tariff-refunds-right-now]] reports that the first 20,000 units sold out in 20 minutes and later production also sold out.
- **Refund traceability:** [[should-we-all-be-getting-tariff-refunds-right-now]] uses [[AmyChoi]]'s Playdate order to show how a separate charge can be repaid exactly.

## Qualifications

The sales pace, manufacturing-cost estimate, and tariff burden are source-reported figures. The case does not establish Playdate's broader commercial performance or show that all orders carried the same tariff amount.

## What Changed

- Established Playdate as the product-level bridge between importing, tariff pass-through, and customer refund traceability.
