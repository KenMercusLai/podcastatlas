# Eleonora Duse

## Overview

Eleonora Duse is presented as a leading Italian actor, D'Annunzio's lover and intellectual partner, and a major carrier of his plays to international audiences.

## Current Profile

The episode uses Duse to complicate a story otherwise dominated by [[GabrieleDAnnunzio|D'Annunzio]]'s exploitation of women. She was not merely an appendage to his celebrity: her own fame and performances took his work through Italy and to audiences in Russia, the United States, England, and elsewhere. Their relationship illustrates his attraction to women with major talent or social standing while not softening the evidence of his wider abusive conduct.

## Key Characteristics

- Internationally famous Italian stage actor.
- Performer who expanded the circulation and reputation of D'Annunzio's plays.
- Lover and intellectual partner situated within his celebrity network.
- Example of a talented woman whose agency should not be collapsed into D'Annunzio's self-mythology.

## Evidence

- Interpretive boundary: [[194-the-first-fascist-glt5479566212]] places her prominence beside, not against, its condemnation of D'Annunzio's treatment of women.
