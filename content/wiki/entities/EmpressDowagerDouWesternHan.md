## Overview

窦太后 / Empress Dowager Dou (Western Han) is the senior dowager whose authority structures the early careers of [[DouYingWesternHan|窦婴]] and [[TianFenWesternHan|田蚡]], constrains the newly enthroned [[HanWudi|汉武帝]], and removes policy-aligned officials when a reform challenge reaches her own governing role. Her death and earlier break with Dou Ying help explain his later vulnerability. This page is distinct from the Eastern Han [[EmpressDowagerDouHeLateHan|窦太后]] and [[EmpressDowagerDouMiao|窦妙]].

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-263-weile-shengguan-ren-keyi-duo-beiwei-1-lgewf4qfarqfjfgp50nss2vf_7y|Hanji 263]] presents Dou as the dominant senior authority during Han Jingdi's early reign and as a continuing constraint after Wudi's accession. Dou Ying's military service and connection to her make his household an important access point in Chang'an. Tian Fen's conspicuous service to Dou Ying can therefore function as indirect proximity to Dou's political network.

After Han Jingdi's death, Dou becomes grand empress dowager while [[EmpressDowagerWangWesternHan|王太后]] becomes empress dowager. The episode distinguishes Dou's senior status and governing experience from Wang's closer relationship to her son Wudi. It then places Dou Ying and Tian Fen's appointments inside this layered structure, but does not quantify Dou's exact role in either decision.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-263-weile-shengguan-ren-keyi-duo-beiwei-2-lvwimzpphquwycrfu-2r6z_r3mqu|Hanji 263 part 2]] makes the authority conflict concrete. A proposal attributed to Zhao Wan asks Wudi to stop routinely consulting the grand empress dowager; Dou responds by removing Dou Ying, Tian Fen, and Wang Zang from office. The episode also says Dou already resents Dou Ying for opposing the Liang king's designation as heir, so the new dispute produces a deeper family break than Tian Fen experiences. After Dou's death, the Wang channel becomes dominant enough to restore Tian Fen while Dou Ying remains excluded.

The later conflict shows the negative side of patronage dependence. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-265-dongchaotingbian-ruhe-yanbian-cheng-jieduandazhan-2-lvu23osgv46nn3rbgque1mbhmpgw|Hanji 265 part 2]] says Dou Ying no longer has Dowager Dou's protection when he confronts Tian Fen, while Tian Fen still has Wang's backing. The source does not give a full account of Dou's death or the timing of the protection loss, but it makes her absence politically consequential.

## Key Characteristics

- Senior Western Han dowager with authority extending across the Jingdi-Wudi transition.
- Political patronage center connected to Dou Ying's status and household network.
- Higher in formal and experienced authority than Empress Dowager Wang in the episode's account.
- Constraint on the young Han Wudi's early freedom of decision.
- Uses removal from office against a reform coalition whose proposal would narrow her governing participation.
- Indirect target of Tian Fen's access strategy through Dou Ying.
- Patron whose later absence leaves Dou Ying weaker than the Wang-backed Tian Fen.

## Qualifications

The current profile rests on three compact podcast summaries and is not a complete biography or institutional history. “Effective number one,” the scope of approval power, the proposal's exact wording and procedure, the reasons for the removals and later appointments, the Liang-heir dispute, the precise balance between the two dowagers and Wudi, and the timing or mechanism by which Dou Ying loses her protection remain source-attributed.
