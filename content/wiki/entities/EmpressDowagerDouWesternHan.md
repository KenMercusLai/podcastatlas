## Overview

窦太后 / Empress Dowager Dou (Western Han) is presented from her early movement out of poverty into [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]]'s Dai household and reunion with [[DouGuangguoWesternHan|窦广国]] through her later authority as the senior dowager whose family preferences shape Jingdi-era succession, punishment, and territorial politics and whose patronage constrains the newly enthroned [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]. This page is distinct from the Eastern Han [[EmpressDowagerDouHeLateHan|窦太后]] and [[EmpressDowagerDouMiao|窦妙]].

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-208-yi-tui-wei-jin-chenping-de-houheixue-2-lnz79ec6cz5s1n90tifnnxmfvi2i|Hanji 208 part 2]] now supplies Dou's early-life layer. The episode says she enters the palace from poverty, asks to be sent near home in Zhao, but is mistakenly assigned to Dai, where Liu Heng favors her and she bears [[LiuPiaoWesternHan|刘嫖]] and [[HanJingdi|刘启]]. Liu Qi's designation as crown prince then makes her empress. The sequence is a bounded podcast account, not independent verification of every household and appointment detail.

The same source recounts her reunion with Dou Guangguo through shared childhood memories after he reaches Chang'an. The emotional recognition immediately acquires a public dimension: [[ZhouBo|周勃]] and [[GuanYing|灌婴]], remembering the Lü-family crisis, arrange upright teachers and companions for the newly elevated siblings. Dou's profile therefore begins not only with fortune and kin recovery but with a preventive attempt to shape how a new outer-relative household uses rank.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-240-hanjingdi-kusu-xiongnu-he-taihou-sha-le-wode-ying-lvl4t-2etswvi3ts9gbvor5dh8mi|Hanji 240]] supplies two earlier forms of Dou's family authority. After her grandson [[LiuRongWesternHan|刘荣]] dies in custody, she turns grief into a demand against [[ZhiDuWesternHan|郅都]]: the episode says Jingdi seeks to pardon the official after a later frontier case, but Dou presses for execution. In the separate Liang succession branch, her preference for Liu Wu is checked by [[YuanAngWesternHan|袁盎]]'s Song historical analogy; the episode says she accepts the warning that immediate maternal affection could produce multigenerational disorder and sends Liu Wu back to his fief. Together the cases show senior-dowager power both imposing a punitive outcome and yielding to a dynastic-risk argument.

The two Hanji 242 notes supply the earlier family-power layer. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-242-cong-chengxiang-dao-mianguan-zhouyafu-fanle-zhichang-daji-1-lu6ohybs3wq9p9z-rmzj-j5vjcrs|Part 1]] says Dou presses Jingdi to enfeoff Empress Wang's brother Wang Xin, turning senior-dowager authority toward a different maternal family; Zhou Yafu blocks the request by invoking the rule against rewarding a person without state merit, and the episode presents the encounter as one source of Dou's hostility toward him. It also says Liu Wu repeatedly criticizes Zhou before her, making access to the dowager a channel of cumulative ministerial risk.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-242-cong-chengxiang-dao-mianguan-zhouyafu-fanle-zhichang-daji-2-lvwwjv0w-widjjzafmweqdoepydg|Part 2]] says Dou's support had made Liu Wu a plausible alternative heir, while Jingdi later restricts Liu Wu's access to her because he fears renewed criticism or influence. After Liu Wu dies, Dou blames Jingdi and refuses food; Jingdi responds by granting kingships to all five sons and county estates to five daughters. Her grief therefore has public consequences, but the resulting settlement also fragments Liang rather than restoring its concentrated strength.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-241-liangxiaowang-liuwu-kan-wo-ruhe-huayang-zuosi-lqbllfh9nph-vvu9emzhsxdfj1le|Hanji 241]] shows that influence during the Yuan Ang murder crisis. Dou's distress at the prospect of Liu Wu's execution becomes one side of Tian Shu's argument for burning the Liang records. Tian then tells her that Liu Wu knew nothing and that the guilty advisers are dead; the episode says she resumes eating and recovers. Her grief does not itself adjudicate the case, but its anticipated force reshapes what evidence is allowed to survive and what public account the court adopts.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-263-weile-shengguan-ren-keyi-duo-beiwei-1-lgewf4qfarqfjfgp50nss2vf_7y|Hanji 263]] presents Dou as the dominant senior authority during Han Jingdi's early reign and as a continuing constraint after Wudi's accession. Dou Ying's military service and connection to her make his household an important access point in Chang'an. Tian Fen's conspicuous service to Dou Ying can therefore function as indirect proximity to Dou's political network.

After Han Jingdi's death, Dou becomes grand empress dowager while [[EmpressDowagerWangWesternHan|王太后]] becomes empress dowager. The episode distinguishes Dou's senior status and governing experience from Wang's closer relationship to her son Wudi. It then places Dou Ying and Tian Fen's appointments inside this layered structure, but does not quantify Dou's exact role in either decision.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-263-weile-shengguan-ren-keyi-duo-beiwei-2-lvwimzpphquwycrfu-2r6z_r3mqu|Hanji 263 part 2]] makes the authority conflict concrete. A proposal attributed to Zhao Wan asks Wudi to stop routinely consulting the grand empress dowager; Dou responds by removing Dou Ying, Tian Fen, and Wang Zang from office. The episode also says Dou already resents Dou Ying for opposing the Liang king's designation as heir, so the new dispute produces a deeper family break than Tian Fen experiences. After Dou's death, the Wang channel becomes dominant enough to restore Tian Fen while Dou Ying remains excluded.

The later conflict shows the negative side of patronage dependence. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-265-dongchaotingbian-ruhe-yanbian-cheng-jieduandazhan-2-lvu23osgv46nn3rbgque1mbhmpgw|Hanji 265 part 2]] says Dou Ying no longer has Dowager Dou's protection when he confronts Tian Fen, while Tian Fen still has Wang's backing. The source does not give a full account of Dou's death or the timing of the protection loss, but it makes her absence politically consequential.

## Key Characteristics

- Former palace woman whose unintended transfer to Dai, children, and later sibling reunion precede her senior-dowager authority.
- Newly elevated empress whose household becomes the object of preventive tutorship after the Lü-family crisis.
- Mother, grandmother, and senior dowager whose family grief and preference affect punishment, evidence survival, succession, access, kin enfeoffment, and the division of Liang.
- Political patronage center connected to Dou Ying's status and household network.
- Higher in formal and experienced authority than Empress Dowager Wang in the episode's account.
- Constraint on the young Han Wudi's early freedom of decision.
- Uses removal from office against a reform coalition whose proposal would narrow her governing participation.

## Qualifications

The current profile rests on eight compact podcast summaries and is not a complete biography or institutional history. Hanji 208 part 2's poverty, palace entry, transfer error, favor, children, sibling recognition, and tutorship arrangement remain episode-attributed; it does not prove that Dou or Guangguo would otherwise have repeated Lü-family conduct. Hanji 240's prison responsibility, exact grievance, Zhi Du charge and execution mechanism, Yuan Ang speech, Song precedent, and reasons for Dou's changed position remain episode-attributed. Hanji 241's distress, refusal to eat, Tian Shu's forecast, destroyed records, public report, and recovery remain episode-attributed; the account does not prove Dou ordered evidence destruction or knew the private judgment of Liu Wu's guilt. Hanji 242 part 1's Wang Xin request, founding-rule wording, Zhou's effect on the decision, Dou's reaction, and Liu Wu's lobbying remain source-attributed; it does not establish that kin preference alone determined her view of Zhou. Part 2's succession sequence, court-stay rules, accusation against Jingdi, refusal to eat, consultation, grant details, and claim that Dou missed the anti-concentration effect are likewise bounded. “Effective number one,” the scope of approval power, the proposal's exact wording and procedure, the reasons for removals and later appointments, the precise balance between the two dowagers and Wudi, and the timing or mechanism by which Dou Ying loses her protection also require broader evidence.

## What Changed

- Extended the profile backward from senior dowager authority to palace entry, the Dai household, motherhood, and elevation as empress.
- Added reunion with Dou Guangguo as the moment private kin recovery becomes a court-governance concern.
- Added preventive tutorship while keeping ministerial fear distinct from evidence of Dou-family abuse.

- [[TianFenWesternHan|田蚡]] - rising Wang-family relative who approaches Dou-linked power through Dou Ying.
- [[EmpressDowagerWangWesternHan|王太后]] - junior dowager with a closer mother-son channel to Wudi.
- [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] - young emperor whose early decision space remains constrained by senior dowager authority.
