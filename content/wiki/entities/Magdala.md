# Magdala

## Overview

Magdala is the mountain citadel in [[Abyssinia]] where [[TeodrosII]] holds European hostages, making it the immediate target and symbolic endpoint of the [[AbyssinianExpedition1868|Abyssinian Expedition of 1868]] in [[705-queen-victorias-revenge-the-mad-emperor-of-abyssinia-part-1-glt8608697296]] and the site of the campaign's battle, death scene and plunder in [[706-queen-victorias-revenge-storming-the-citadel-part-2-glt3285474568]].

## Current Profile

In the first episode Magdala is mainly a captivity site: hostages are chained in huts there after earlier periods of travel, negotiation and temporary hope, and the narrative treats the later storming only through objects removed afterwards. The second episode turns the fortress into the campaign's climax. The hostages spend their captivity playing cards, reading the Bible and eating unusually well from old stores while worrying whether Theodros will return before the British and whether he will kill them; Theodros reaches Magdala in a storm on 27 March 1868 with his guns and about 50,000 followers; the British catch their first glimpse of the summit on 6 April after covering more than 300 miles; and on Easter Monday British guns shell the citadel while troops force the outer gate with crowbars and scaling ladders.

The site then becomes the scene of the emperor's death and of an imperial sale. Theodros shoots himself inside the citadel, his body is buried by [[HormuzdRassam]] in the Coptic church on the hilltop, the defenders surrender, and British troops take manuscripts, relics, vestments, crowns, goblets, musical instruments, pottery, umbrellas, wine, carpets, lion skins, toy soldiers and cameras before [[RobertNapier|Napier]] orders a proper distribution and holds a large auction. What remains at Magdala today, in the source's account, is the physical shell of the story: the real Sebastopol gun lies half-buried there, never fired and showing no sign of ever having worked.

## Key Characteristics

- It is a remote mountain citadel that functions as both fortress and prison.
- It converts a diplomatic crisis into a concrete rescue problem with a fixed destination.
- It is the campaign's decisive battlefield, where an army charges downhill into continuous rifle fire.
- It is the place of Teodros's suicide and Coptic burial, and therefore the moral centre of the campaign narrative.
- It becomes an imperial treasure house: devotional, royal, domestic and military objects move from the citadel into soldiers' hands, the auction and later collections.
- Its physical residue anchors later memory, especially the abandoned Sebastopol and the replica in Addis Ababa.

## Evidence

- Captivity site: [[705-queen-victorias-revenge-the-mad-emperor-of-abyssinia-part-1-glt8608697296]] says hostages are returned to Magdala, chained, and held in huts at the mountain citadel.
- Expedition target: [[705-queen-victorias-revenge-the-mad-emperor-of-abyssinia-part-1-glt8608697296]] introduces the British campaign as an operation to rescue nearly sixty European hostages held by [[TeodrosII]] at Magdala.
- Approach and ultimatum: [[706-queen-victorias-revenge-storming-the-citadel-part-2-glt3285474568]] says Napier's force reached the Jidda Gorge about 50 miles away, caught its first glimpse of the summit on 6 April 1868, and sent a final ultimatum demanding the release of the European prisoners.
- Assault: [[706-queen-victorias-revenge-storming-the-citadel-part-2-glt3285474568]] says British guns shelled the citadel on Easter Monday and troops with scaling ladders forced the outer gate with crowbars.
- Death and burial: [[706-queen-victorias-revenge-storming-the-citadel-part-2-glt3285474568]] says Theodros killed himself inside the citadel and Rassam organised the funeral in the Coptic church on the Magdala hilltop the next day.
- Plunder and auction: [[706-queen-victorias-revenge-storming-the-citadel-part-2-glt3285474568]] lists the objects looted from Magdala and says Napier ordered a proper distribution of the booty followed by a gigantic auction.
- Institutional afterlife of the objects: [[705-queen-victorias-revenge-the-mad-emperor-of-abyssinia-part-1-glt8608697296]] notes that objects taken after the later British storming of Magdala entered major institutions.
- Physical residue: [[706-queen-victorias-revenge-storming-the-citadel-part-2-glt3285474568]] says the real Sebastopol lies half-buried at Magdala, never fired and showing no sign of ever having worked.

## Qualifications

The page still does not cover Magdala's geography, earlier history or present condition in depth, and excavation, object provenance and restitution debates remain outside these two episodes. Casualty figures, the contents of the citadel and the auction's scale are source-scoped, and the fortress is described almost entirely from British and hostage testimony.
