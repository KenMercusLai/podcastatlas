# Magdala

## Overview

Magdala is the mountain citadel in [[Abyssinia]] where [[TeodrosII]] holds European hostages in [[705-queen-victorias-revenge-the-mad-emperor-of-abyssinia-part-1-glt8608697296]], making it the immediate target and symbolic endpoint of the [[AbyssinianExpedition1868|Abyssinian Expedition of 1868]].

## Current Profile

The current source uses Magdala mainly as a captivity site before the campaign narrative begins. Hostages are chained in huts at the fortress after earlier periods of travel, negotiation, and temporary hope. The later storming of Magdala is mentioned only in retrospect through the objects removed afterward.

## Key Characteristics

- It functions as a remote mountain fortress and hostage-holding site.
- It turns the diplomatic crisis into a concrete rescue problem.
- It is associated in the source with both captivity and later imperial collection controversy.
- It anchors the transition from correspondence and bargaining to military planning.

## Evidence

- Captivity site: [[705-queen-victorias-revenge-the-mad-emperor-of-abyssinia-part-1-glt8608697296]] says hostages are returned to Magdala, chained, and held in huts at the mountain citadel.
- Expedition target: [[705-queen-victorias-revenge-the-mad-emperor-of-abyssinia-part-1-glt8608697296]] introduces the British campaign as an operation to rescue nearly sixty European hostages held by [[TeodrosII]] at Magdala.
- Afterlife: [[705-queen-victorias-revenge-the-mad-emperor-of-abyssinia-part-1-glt8608697296]] notes that objects taken after the later British storming of Magdala entered major institutions.
