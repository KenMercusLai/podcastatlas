## Overview

上官飞燕 / Shangguan Feiyan is the impersonator at the center of [[GoldenRocDynastyGuLong|《金鹏王朝》]], presenting herself both as an apparently innocent young woman and as 丹凤公主 while helping [[HuoXiuGuLong|霍休]] pursue the fallen kingdom's wealth.

## Current Profile

The bounded source gives Shangguan substantial operational agency. She kills the true princess, changes dress, voice, scent, and manner, recruits or manipulates 霍天青 through attachment, and helps create a restoration claim capable of moving [[LuXiaofeng|陆小凤]] against 阎铁珊 and 独孤一鹤. Her performance makes identity itself part of the mystery, including the failure of [[HuaManlou|花满楼]]'s senses to expose her.

That agency is qualified twice. She is a planner and killer, yet 霍休 treats her as an instrument and ultimately a disposable part of a larger scheme. She also receives less distinct interior and behavioral characterization than the three male leads: the hosts can list the external differences between 飞燕 and 丹凤, but find the two personas easy to confuse. The ending refuses full closure because 上官雪儿 identifies an old woman as her still-living sister, a claim the others dismiss and the novel does not settle.

## Key Characteristics

- Sustains two identities through appearance, voice, scent, manner, and social expectation.
- Converts a restoration grievance into a mechanism for directing heroic violence.
- Uses romantic attachment and hidden information to influence 霍天青.
- Acts as killer and co-conspirator while remaining subordinate to 霍休's wider design.
- Makes disguise central to the mystery but receives limited interior differentiation.
- Retains an unresolved possible survival beyond the apparent exposure of the plot.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to one structured podcast summary rather than the novel or full series. The exact relationship with 霍天青, degree of independence from 霍休, success of the disguise, motives for murder, survival, and identity of the old woman remain source-scoped or unresolved. Criticism of female characterization is a literary judgment, not proof that every reader experiences the two personas as indistinct.
