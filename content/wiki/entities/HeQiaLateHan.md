# 和洽 / He Qia (late Han)

## Overview

和洽 / He Qia (late Han) enters the wiki as an official who warns [[CaoCao|曹操]] that a court preference for frugality has hardened into a visible and easily imitated test of moral worth.

## Current Profile

In [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1114-guanyuan-jiti-zhuangqiong-heqia-quanjian-neng-jiaoxing-caocao-ma-lgqmpyz2zm1avw5e-rdexmo6kswm|Hanji 1114]], He Qia observes officials using worn clothing, walking, and rough daily objects to display integrity. His intervention is not an argument for luxury. He accepts frugality as a useful norm but says it must remain moderate, because an extreme standard exhausts people and creates space for false or eccentric performance.

He Qia's durable role in the current evidence is diagnostic. He identifies a gap between an intended virtue and its observable proxy: when selectors reward the proxy, officials can reproduce the appearance without demonstrating integrity or governing capacity. His advice therefore supplies the corrective voice inside [[SelectionDrivenVirtueSignaling|选官驱动的德行表演]].

## Key Characteristics

- Defends moderate frugality rather than conspicuous austerity or luxury.
- Distinguishes underlying integrity from cheap, visible signals of integrity.
- Treats personnel judgment as role-sensitive because ability and character vary across people.
- Warns that extreme moral standards can create fatigue, conformity, and hypocrisy.

## Qualifications

The current profile rests on one interpretive podcast source. It does not establish the full chronology of He Qia's career, the exact reach of the official fashion he criticized, or whether any particular official's austere conduct was insincere. The episode's modern language of “装穷” is an interpretive shorthand for a signaling problem, not a demonstrated judgment about every participant's motive.

## What Changed

- Created He Qia as the source's corrective voice on extreme frugality and personnel signals.
- Distinguished his moderate-frugality position from both luxury advocacy and blanket suspicion of plain conduct.
