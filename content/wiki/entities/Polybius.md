# Polybius / 波利比乌斯

## Overview

Polybius / 波利比乌斯 is the Greek hostage and historian of Rome's rise whose relationship with [[ScipioAemilianus]] places him close to the final destruction of [[Carthage]].

## Current Profile

After Rome defeated Macedon at Pydna in 168 BC and divided it into weaker units, Polybius was among the Greek elites taken to Italy. His long exposure to Roman politics and leading families developed into a close relationship with Scipio Aemilianus, the son of the victorious Aemilius Paulus and adoptive grandson of Scipio Africanus.

The episode presents Polybius as sympathetic to Carthage's complaints against [[Massinissa]] and therefore as a useful counterweight to [[CatoTheElder]]'s annihilation campaign. It closes with Polybius's report that Scipio wept over the destroyed city and feared Rome would one day share its fate. That scene gives the conquest a tragic interpretation, but transmitted dialogue is not treated as direct access to either man's private words.

## Key Characteristics

- Greek elite hostage produced by Rome's post-Pydna settlement.
- Close observer of Roman politics and aristocratic families.
- Associate of Scipio Aemilianus and member of a more Carthage-sympathetic circle.
- Historian whose account frames Roman victory through imperial impermanence.

- Hostage setting: [[643-romes-greatest-enemy-carthage-destroyed-part-4-glt7699509622]] connects Pydna, the abolition of the Macedonian monarchy, elite detention, and Polybius's arrival in Rome.
- Roman access: [[643-romes-greatest-enemy-carthage-destroyed-part-4-glt7699509622]] describes his familiarity with Roman politics and relationship with Scipio Aemilianus.
- Carthage judgment: [[643-romes-greatest-enemy-carthage-destroyed-part-4-glt7699509622]] attributes to Polybius a view that Carthage's complaints against Massinissa were just.
- Tragic frame: [[643-romes-greatest-enemy-carthage-destroyed-part-4-glt7699509622]] uses his report of Scipio's tears and fear for Rome as the episode's final reflection.

## Qualifications

This is a source-bounded profile, not a full account of Polybius's works, methods, travels, or political career. His position inside the Scipionic circle offered unusual access but could also shape emphasis; reported judgments, dialogue, and emotion remain literary testimony rather than independently recoverable private speech.

## What Changed

- Established Polybius as both a coerced Greek observer of Roman expansion and the mediator of its most famous self-questioning victory scene.

## Relationships

- [[ScipioAemilianus]] - Roman commander with whom Polybius forms a close intellectual and personal relationship.
- [[Carthage]] - defeated city whose complaints and destruction he interprets.
- [[Massinissa]] - ruler against whom he regards Carthaginian complaints as justified.
- [[CatoTheElder]] - opposing voice in the Roman dispute over Carthage's survival.
- [[RomanRepublic]] - expanding polity he studies after being taken to Italy.
- [[ArchivePreservationBias]] - framework for weighing his privileged but situated testimony about conquered societies.
