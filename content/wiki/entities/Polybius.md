# Polybius / 波利比乌斯

## Overview

Polybius / 波利比乌斯 is the Greek hostage and historian of Rome's rise whose admiration for [[HannibalBarca]] and relationship with [[ScipioAemilianus]] shape the surviving interpretation of the Punic Wars.

## Current Profile

The Zama episode uses Polybius as a principal witness who can admire Rome and still praise its greatest enemy. His emphasis on Hannibal's ability to preserve discipline and loyalty in a multiethnic army for years supports a command profile not reducible to Roman hatred or Carthaginian patriotism. At the same time, his Scipionic connections require caution when his narrative distributes prudence, glory, or moral standing among Roman commanders.

After Rome defeated Macedon at Pydna in 168 BC and divided it into weaker units, Polybius was among the Greek elites taken to Italy. His long exposure to Roman politics and leading families developed into a close relationship with Scipio Aemilianus, the son of the victorious Aemilius Paulus and adoptive grandson of Scipio Africanus.

The episode presents Polybius as sympathetic to Carthage's complaints against [[Massinissa]] and therefore as a useful counterweight to [[CatoTheElder]]'s annihilation campaign. It closes with Polybius's report that Scipio wept over the destroyed city and feared Rome would one day share its fate. That scene gives the conquest a tragic interpretation, but transmitted dialogue is not treated as direct access to either man's private words.

## Key Characteristics

- Greek elite hostage produced by Rome's post-Pydna settlement.
- Close observer of Roman politics and aristocratic families.
- Admirer of Hannibal's command capacity despite a broadly pro-Roman interpretation of Rome's rise.
- Associate of Scipio Aemilianus and member of a more Carthage-sympathetic circle.
- Historian whose account frames Roman victory through imperial impermanence.

## Qualifications

This is a source-bounded profile, not a full account of Polybius's works, methods, travels, chronology, or political career. His position inside the Scipionic circle offered unusual access but could also shape emphasis; admiration for Hannibal does not neutralize that location. Casualty figures, tactical sequences, reported judgments, dialogue, and emotion remain literary testimony rather than independently recoverable observation or private speech.

## What Changed

- Extended Polybius from witness to Carthage's destruction to a central interpreter of Hannibal and Zama.
- Made his admiration for Hannibal coexist explicitly with the evidentiary qualification created by Scipionic access.

- [[Massinissa]] - ruler against whom he regards Carthaginian complaints as justified.
- [[CatoTheElder]] - opposing voice in the Roman dispute over Carthage's survival.
- [[RomanRepublic]] - expanding polity he studies after being taken to Italy.
