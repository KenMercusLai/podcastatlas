# Francisco Pizarro

## Overview

Francisco Pizarro was a Spanish conquistador and governor who converted Inca civil-war divisions into control of Cusco, northern defeat, colonial extraction, and Spanish-backed rule before his family faction provoked revolt and his career ended with assassination in 1541.

## Current Profile

[[647-the-fall-of-the-incas-the-king-in-the-north-part-4-glt4740382633]] places Pizarro at the transition from hostage conquest to colonial rule. After killing [[Atahualpa]], he installs Túpac Hualpa and then [[MancoInca]] as Huascar-aligned rulers, presents his advance as liberation from the northern faction, executes Chalcuchima, enters Cusco with Manco, and combines the latter's coronation with the Spanish Requirement and tribute to Charles V. He then orders Cusco's precious metal melted down, directs campaigns toward Quito, refounds Cusco under Spanish law, selects the site of Lima, and distributes land, villages, and labor despite warnings that Indigenous people are free royal subjects.

[[648-the-fall-of-the-incas-battle-for-the-sacred-city-part-5-glt6392035011]] places Pizarro behind the resulting expanding occupation order: he continues distributing land, sends [[DiegoDeAlmagro|Almagro]] toward Chile in an attempt to reduce factional conflict, and governs from Lima while his brothers' abuse helps drive Manco into revolt. When [[QuisoYupanqui]] threatens Lima, local and Indigenous coalition support combines with cavalry to preserve the coastal base.

In [[649-the-fall-of-the-incas-the-last-emperor-part-6-glt4505534064]], that base becomes decisive. Control of Lima keeps Pizarro connected to supplies, reinforcements, weapons, communications, and the Spanish court, allowing promises of arbitration to buy time while Hernando Pizarro raises the army that defeats Almagro.

The episode presents his authority as inseparable from violence and betrayal. He participates in the coercion surrounding [[MancoInca]], orders the killing of Cura Ocllo after using her as a hostage, benefits from Almagro's execution, and reaches extraordinary wealth before excluded Almagristas kill him in his own house. His death continues rather than resolves the [[ConquistadorCivilWarPeru|conquistador civil war]].

## Key Characteristics

- Conquistador who exploited Inca succession conflict through Spanish-backed rulers.
- Governor who converted sacred plunder and military occupation into Cusco's refoundation, Lima's founding, and colonial grants.
- Faction leader able to turn negotiation and delay into military preparation.
- Beneficiary of royal recognition and colonial wealth after the initial conquest.
- Commander associated with hostage-taking, exemplary violence, and lethal factional rivalry.
- Assassinated by followers of Diego de Almagro the Younger in 1541.

## Qualifications

This profile covers the period after Atahualpa's execution rather than Pizarro's full career. Personality judgments, dialogue, force totals, the legal form of early grants, household details, and the assassination reconstruction remain source-scoped. Explaining his advantage through Inca faction, Indigenous allies, Lima, and royal access does not reduce the result to one mechanism or excuse violence by Spanish or Indigenous forces.

## What Changed

- Added puppet succession, Cusco's seizure and plunder, the Quito campaigns, and Lima's foundation to the profile.
- Clarified that conquest and colonial allocation advanced together before Manco's revolt.

- [[MancoInca]] - Inca resistance ruler pursued and coerced by the Pizarro faction.
- [[QuisoYupanqui]] - Inca commander whose attack threatened Pizarro's Lima base.
- [[ConquistadorCivilWarPeru]] - conflict in which Pizarro's family network became one principal faction.
- [[SpanishConquestOfPeru]] - conquest process from which his governorship and wealth emerged.
- [[IndigenousAllianceInSpanishConquest]] - military system on which Pizarro commanders depended.
