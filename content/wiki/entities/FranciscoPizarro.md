# Francisco Pizarro

## Overview

Francisco Pizarro was a Spanish conquistador and governor whose extraction policies and family network helped provoke Manco Inca's revolt before his faction defeated Diego de Almagro and his rule ended with assassination in 1541.

## Current Profile

[[648-the-fall-of-the-incas-battle-for-the-sacred-city-part-5-glt6392035011]] places Pizarro behind an expanding occupation order: he continues distributing land despite royal objections, sends [[DiegoDeAlmagro|Almagro]] toward Chile in an attempt to reduce factional conflict, and governs from Lima while his brothers' abuse helps drive [[MancoInca]] into revolt. When [[QuisoYupanqui]] threatens Lima, local and Indigenous coalition support combines with cavalry to preserve the coastal base.

In [[649-the-fall-of-the-incas-the-last-emperor-part-6-glt4505534064]], that base becomes decisive. Control of Lima keeps Pizarro connected to supplies, reinforcements, weapons, communications, and the Spanish court, allowing promises of arbitration to buy time while Hernando Pizarro raises the army that defeats Almagro.

The episode presents his authority as inseparable from violence and betrayal. He participates in the coercion surrounding [[MancoInca]], orders the killing of Cura Ocllo after using her as a hostage, benefits from Almagro's execution, and reaches extraordinary wealth before excluded Almagristas kill him in his own house. His death continues rather than resolves the [[ConquistadorCivilWarPeru|conquistador civil war]].

## Key Characteristics

- Conquistador governor whose Lima base provided logistical and political advantages.
- Distributor of colonial land grants despite disputed authority and royal restraint.
- Faction leader able to turn negotiation and delay into military preparation.
- Beneficiary of royal recognition and colonial wealth after the initial conquest.
- Commander associated with hostage-taking, exemplary violence, and lethal factional rivalry.
- Politically shrewd but personally opaque in the episode's characterization.
- Assassinated by followers of Diego de Almagro the Younger in 1541.

## Evidence

- Occupation and revolt: [[648-the-fall-of-the-incas-battle-for-the-sacred-city-part-5-glt6392035011]] links land distribution, Pizarro-family conduct, the siege of Cusco, and the threat to Lima.
- Coastal advantage: [[648-the-fall-of-the-incas-battle-for-the-sacred-city-part-5-glt6392035011]] and [[649-the-fall-of-the-incas-the-last-emperor-part-6-glt4505534064]] connect Lima to cavalry success, seaborne reinforcement, and royal communication.
- Factional victory: [[649-the-fall-of-the-incas-the-last-emperor-part-6-glt4505534064]] links Pizarro's negotiations to Hernando's mobilization and the Battle of Las Salinas.
- Colonial coercion: [[649-the-fall-of-the-incas-the-last-emperor-part-6-glt4505534064]] describes Cura Ocllo's hostage use and killing after Manco refuses surrender.
- Assassination: [[649-the-fall-of-the-incas-the-last-emperor-part-6-glt4505534064]] narrates the June 1541 attack by roughly forty Almagrist conspirators.

## Qualifications

This profile covers the uprising and late conquest rather than Pizarro's full career. Personality judgments, wealth ranking, dialogue, force totals, household details, and the assassination reconstruction remain source-scoped. Explaining his advantage through Lima and royal access does not reduce the result to geography or excuse violence by either Spanish faction.

## What Changed

- Added land distribution, family conduct, and the defense of Lima to the causes and survival of his regime.
- Connected the siege-era coastal advantage to his later factional victory.

## Relationships

- [[DiegoDeAlmagro]] - former partner and rival whose execution prolonged their factional feud.
- [[MancoInca]] - Inca resistance ruler pursued and coerced by the Pizarro faction.
- [[QuisoYupanqui]] - Inca commander whose attack threatened Pizarro's Lima base.
- [[ConquistadorCivilWarPeru]] - conflict in which Pizarro's family network became one principal faction.
- [[SpanishConquestOfPeru]] - conquest process from which his governorship and wealth emerged.
- [[IndigenousAllianceInSpanishConquest]] - military system on which Pizarro commanders depended.
- [[Peru]] - colonial territory and society transformed by his conquest and factional rule.
