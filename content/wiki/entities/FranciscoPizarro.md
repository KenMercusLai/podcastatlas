# Francisco Pizarro

## Overview

Francisco Pizarro was a Spanish conquistador and governor whose faction defeated Diego de Almagro's forces but whose rule ended with assassination in Lima in 1541.

## Current Profile

In [[649-the-fall-of-the-incas-the-last-emperor-part-6-glt4505534064]], Pizarro's strength rests as much on geography and royal access as on battlefield command. Control of Lima keeps him connected to supplies, reinforcements, weapons, communications, and the Spanish court, allowing promises of arbitration to buy time while Hernando Pizarro raises the army that defeats [[DiegoDeAlmagro|Almagro]].

The episode presents his authority as inseparable from violence and betrayal. He participates in the coercion surrounding [[MancoInca]], orders the killing of Cura Ocllo after using her as a hostage, benefits from Almagro's execution, and reaches extraordinary wealth before excluded Almagristas kill him in his own house. His death continues rather than resolves the [[ConquistadorCivilWarPeru|conquistador civil war]].

## Key Characteristics

- Conquistador governor whose Lima base provided logistical and political advantages.
- Faction leader able to turn negotiation and delay into military preparation.
- Beneficiary of royal recognition and colonial wealth after the initial conquest.
- Commander associated with hostage-taking, exemplary violence, and lethal factional rivalry.
- Politically shrewd but personally opaque in the episode's characterization.
- Assassinated by followers of Diego de Almagro the Younger in 1541.

## Evidence

- Coastal advantage: [[649-the-fall-of-the-incas-the-last-emperor-part-6-glt4505534064]] contrasts Lima's overseas connections with Almagro's weaker base in Cusco.
- Factional victory: [[649-the-fall-of-the-incas-the-last-emperor-part-6-glt4505534064]] links Pizarro's negotiations to Hernando's mobilization and the Battle of Las Salinas.
- Colonial coercion: [[649-the-fall-of-the-incas-the-last-emperor-part-6-glt4505534064]] describes Cura Ocllo's hostage use and killing after Manco refuses surrender.
- Assassination: [[649-the-fall-of-the-incas-the-last-emperor-part-6-glt4505534064]] narrates the June 1541 attack by roughly forty Almagrist conspirators.

## Qualifications

This profile covers the late conquest and civil-war phase rather than Pizarro's full career. The episode's personality judgments, wealth ranking, dialogue, household details, and assassination reconstruction remain source-scoped. Explaining his advantage through Lima and royal access does not reduce the result to geography or excuse violence by either Spanish faction.

## What Changed

- Established Lima's supply and communication network as a central source of Pizarro's advantage.
- Connected his factional victory directly to the later cycle of revenge that killed him.
