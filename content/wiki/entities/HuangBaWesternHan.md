[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-454-yi-zhi-niao-ganfan-yige-chengxiang-huangba-you-duo-can-lrjgft-s8oi3yt3lrnsh0_m20sxj|Hanji 454]] backfills the later bird-omen embarrassment, while [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-447-siyi-shaqiu-xihan-kuli-yanyannian-ruhe-ziqu-miewang-lhhh0plqayyodt7sluycfh7y1m0l|Hanji 447]] adds the earlier positive layer: Huang's Yingchuan governance is praised for humane instruction, reduced crime, population growth, and contrast with [[YanYannianWesternHan|严延年]]'s fear-based Henan rule.

## Current Profile

The source set now presents Huang Ba as a capable official whose strength does not automatically survive role change. Hanji 447 gives the strongest positive account: under Xuan's praise, Yingchuan is said to show moral instruction, fewer prisoners, care for the weak, and enough livability that people under Yan's harsh Henan rule prefer to migrate there. Hanji 454 says he had been a rare good local official, but after becoming chancellor he feels unable to contribute substantively at the center and turns toward auspicious proof and visible performance. His questions to visiting local officials about moralized indicators such as yielding fields, gender-separated roads, untouched lost property, filial sons, and chaste women show how a superior's taste can become a reporting incentive even without explicit orders.

Zhang Chang's heque memorial makes the warning public. Huang Ba misidentifies an ordinary bird from Zhang's household as a divine bird and prepares to report it as Heaven's approval of local governance, while border officials who recognize the bird stay silent. Zhang argues that the deeper danger is not the bird but the incentive to package false moral achievements and abandon law for reputation. Hanji 455 then shows Huang Ba still politically exposed after that embarrassment: in an insecure position, he recommends Shi Gao, a trusted imperial relative and shizhong, for the taiwei office. Xuan says Huang should attend to instruction, transmission of people's conditions, reduction of unjust imprisonment, and suppression of theft, not propose top-level military appointments. Huang Ba removes his cap, apologizes, waits several days for a decision, escapes further punishment, and thereafter does not make such requests.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han official praised for local governance but portrayed as mismatched after promotion to central chancellorship.
- Humane Yingchuan administrator whose local order contrasts with Yan Yannian's terror-based Henan rule.
- Official whose bird-omen embarrassment shows how reputation pressure can turn ordinary signs and surface indicators into false political evidence.
- Superior whose evaluation preferences risk encouraging local officials to report moralized, visible achievements rather than lawful governance.
- Recommends Shi Gao for taiwei, touching appointment authority, military command, and outer-relative politics at once.
- Receives a direct rebuke from Xuan, then survives by apologizing rather than defending the recommendation.
- Becomes a workplace-analogy case for not volunteering opinions on sensitive personnel matters outside one's authority lane.

## Qualifications

This page is source-scoped to Hanji 447, Hanji 454, and Hanji 455. It does not reconstruct Huang Ba's full official biography, local-governance record, relationship with Zhang Chang, the full original memorial, or the complete Western Han evaluation system beyond the episodes' summaries. The three-source picture is deliberately mixed: Hanji 447 preserves Huang's strong local-governance reputation, while Hanji 454 and Hanji 455 show later judgment failures after promotion.
