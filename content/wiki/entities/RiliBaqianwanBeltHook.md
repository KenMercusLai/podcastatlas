## Overview

“日利八千万”鲤鱼形铜带钩 / Rili Baqianwan Belt Hook is the episode's artifact anchor for interpreting visible wealth aspiration in the [[WesternHanDynasty|Western Han]] commercial world. The host says it was excavated from Liyuan Tomb 19 at Zhongshui, Weining, Guizhou, and that the inscription on the back reads “日利八千万.”

## Current Profile

The belt hook matters in the wiki less as an isolated object than as a compact evidence bundle: a personal accessory, a status-display item, and a written wish for massive profit. The episode explains belt hooks as belt fasteners and weapon-hanging accessories associated with visible rank, taste, and wealth, then treats this example's inscription as a blunt declaration of “get rich” desire.

Its evidentiary value is interpretive. The source uses the object to connect archaeology, five-zhu coin imagination, merchant status, and [[SimaQian|Sima Qian]]'s Huozhi Liezhuan wealth ethic. It does not independently prove the owner's occupation, actual income, or the normal scale of Han merchant profit.

## Key Characteristics

- The object is presented as a fish-shaped bronze belt hook from a Guizhou tomb.
- Its “日利八千万” inscription makes wealth aspiration visible on a personal status object.
- Belt-hook function and ornament place the inscription inside elite or affluent display culture.
- The episode uses the object as a bridge from material culture into [[HanCommercialWealthAspiration|Han commercial wealth aspiration]].
- The artifact's owner, exact social identity, and actual financial capacity remain unresolved in the source.

## Qualifications

The episode treats the inscription as cultural evidence for desire and display, not as a literal business account. It does not settle whether the tomb occupant was a merchant, official, warrior, local elite, or another status holder.
