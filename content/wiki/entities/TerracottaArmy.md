# Terracotta Army / 兵马俑

## Overview

The Terracotta Army is the large funerary force buried near [[QinShiHuangMausoleum|Qin Shi Huang's mausoleum]] and discovered in 1974. It gives the First Emperor's state-building and afterlife ambitions a material form that is more immediate than the hostile literary tradition around him.

## Current Profile

The episode presents thousands of terracotta archers, crossbowmen, infantry, officers, horses, and chariots as both an archaeological wonder and an industrial project. Repeated components and organized production create apparent individuality at monumental scale, linking artistic effect to Qin administrative capacity.

## Key Characteristics

- A funerary military formation attached to [[QinShiHuang|Qin Shi Huang]] and his mausoleum complex.
- A large-scale archaeological discovery made near the former Qin capital region in 1974.
- A material demonstration of specialized labor, modular production, and coordinated state capacity.
- A visual counterweight to a biography otherwise filtered heavily through later hostile texts and legends.
- Part of a still only partly excavated burial landscape whose transmitted mechanisms and mercury rivers require evidentiary caution.

## Evidence

### Funerary army and scale

- [[444-the-first-emperor-of-china-glt5076517033]] describes thousands of military figures, horses, and chariots associated with the imperial tomb.

### Organized production

- [[444-the-first-emperor-of-china-glt5076517033]] cites Frances Wood's interpretation of the army as an industrial project using repeated parts to produce an illusion of individuality.

### Reception and visibility

- [[444-the-first-emperor-of-china-glt5076517033]] treats the 1974 discovery as a principal modern route by which non-specialists encounter the First Emperor.

## Qualifications

- This source does not provide a complete excavation history, workforce reconstruction, or technical typology of the figures.
- The army should not be used to verify every literary claim about the unexcavated central tomb, including mechanical traps or literal rivers of mercury.
- Calling production "industrial" is an interpretive description of scale, organization, and repeatable components, not a claim that Qin manufacture matched modern industry.

## What Changed

- Established the army as its own canonical entity rather than leaving it only inside Qin Shi Huang's biography.
- Connected its apparent individuality to modular production and centralized labor capacity.
- Made its role in modern reception explicit while preserving the boundary between excavated evidence and tomb legend.
