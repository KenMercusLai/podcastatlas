# 《自深深处》 / De Profundis

## Overview

*De Profundis* is [[OscarWilde|Oscar Wilde]]'s long prison letter to [[AlfredDouglas|Lord Alfred Douglas]], joining intimate accusation, continued attachment, suffering, legal disaster, and literary self-reconstruction.

## Current Profile

The literary source presents the letter's mixed emotional logic: Wilde condemns Douglas's effects on his money, health, work, and life while refusing to extinguish love. It therefore connects literary biography to dependence and blame without becoming a simple severance document.

The trial episode adds the material prison conditions of composition. After transfer to Reading Gaol and the arrival of a kinder governor, Wilde gained access to books, a notebook, and a pen. The letter consequently stands at the intersection of punishment and partial custodial mercy: the prison damaged Wilde's body and social world, but a change in regime enabled a major act of reflection and writing.

## Key Characteristics

- Long prison letter written by Wilde to Douglas.
- Text that holds accusation and continuing attachment together.
- Record of the relationship's financial, emotional, creative, and legal costs.
- Work made possible by reading and writing access under a more humane prison governor.
- Bridge between Wilde's aesthetic identity and the bodily, institutional reality of imprisonment.

- Conditions of composition: [[342-the-trials-of-oscar-wilde-downfall-and-prison-part-2-glt5549466795]] locates the writing at Reading Gaol after Wilde received books, notebook access, and more considerate treatment.

## Qualifications

These sources do not provide a textual history, complete close reading, manuscript account, or adjudication of every accusation in the letter. Wilde's retrospective interpretation is evidence of his experience and argument, not neutral proof of Douglas's motives or sole responsibility for the downfall.

## What Changed

- Added the Reading Gaol conditions that enabled the letter's composition.
- Reframed the work as an outcome of both punitive deprivation and limited custodial kindness.
- Clarified that the letter's accusations are intimate testimony rather than independent adjudication.
