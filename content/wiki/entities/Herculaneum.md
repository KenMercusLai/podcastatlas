# Herculaneum

## Overview

Herculaneum is the Roman coastal town between [[MountVesuvius]] and the sea that the sources contrast with [[Pompeii]] in urban character, preservation, modern access, and volcanic hazard sequence during the [[EruptionOfVesuvius79|79 CE eruption]].

## Current Profile

Herculaneum initially received less falling material because the wind carried pumice toward Pompeii, but that relative advantage ended when a fast pyroclastic surge descended roughly twelve hours after the eruption began. People sheltering indoors or waiting near the harbor could not outrun it, and the town was buried before Pompeii's final destruction. The earlier episode characterizes it as more resort-like than working-port Pompeii, notes that modern buildings cover parts of the ancient site, and uses sewer deposits to compare diet across households and shops.

## Key Characteristics

- Coastal Roman city positioned between Vesuvius and the sea.
- Place with an early visual view of the rising eruption column.
- Initially spared Pompeii's heavier wind-driven pumice fall.
- Destroyed earlier than Pompeii by an unsurvivable pyroclastic surge.
- Buried deeply enough to disappear from the surviving landscape in the source's reconstruction.
- Partly overlain by a modern town, constraining excavation and access.
- Site where sewer remains preserve socially distributed evidence of fish, shellfish, meat, vegetables, fruit, grain, and bread.

## Evidence

- Initial conditions: [[355-roman-apocalypse-pompeii-79-ad-glt6664993493]] says Herculaneum could see the column and received lighter early fall because of wind direction.
- Fatal surge: [[355-roman-apocalypse-pompeii-79-ad-glt6664993493]] describes a fast surge reaching the city around twelve hours after eruption onset.
- Harbor and shelter limits: [[355-roman-apocalypse-pompeii-79-ad-glt6664993493]] says neither people near the shore nor those indoors could survive the surge.
- Comparative chronology: [[355-roman-apocalypse-pompeii-79-ad-glt6664993493]] places Herculaneum's burial before Pompeii's last escape window and final flow.
- Urban and dietary comparison: [[16-pompeii-glt3349011345]] contrasts Herculaneum's resort character and modern overbuilding with Pompeii, and reports sewer evidence from multiple social settings.

## Qualifications

The timing, burial depth, speed, resort comparison, food distribution, excavation conditions, and individual victim locations are source-scoped. Initial shelter from pumice did not amount to overall safety. A supposed crucifix is qualified in the earlier episode as more likely the trace of a shelf fitting, so it is not treated as secure Christian evidence.

## What Changed

- Added modern overbuilding, comparative urban character, sewer-based diet evidence, and caution around a supposed Christian trace.
