# Herculaneum

## Overview

Herculaneum is the Roman city between [[MountVesuvius]] and the sea that the source contrasts with [[Pompeii]] to show how wind direction and changing volcanic hazards produced different local timelines during the [[EruptionOfVesuvius79|79 CE eruption]].

## Current Profile

Herculaneum initially received less falling material because the wind carried pumice toward Pompeii, but that relative advantage ended when a fast pyroclastic surge descended roughly twelve hours after the eruption began. People sheltering indoors or waiting near the harbor could not outrun it, and the city was buried before Pompeii's final destruction.

## Key Characteristics

- Coastal Roman city positioned between Vesuvius and the sea.
- Place with an early visual view of the rising eruption column.
- Initially spared Pompeii's heavier wind-driven pumice fall.
- Destroyed earlier than Pompeii by an unsurvivable pyroclastic surge.
- Buried deeply enough to disappear from the surviving landscape in the source's reconstruction.

## Evidence

- Initial conditions: [[355-roman-apocalypse-pompeii-79-ad-glt6664993493]] says Herculaneum could see the column and received lighter early fall because of wind direction.
- Fatal surge: [[355-roman-apocalypse-pompeii-79-ad-glt6664993493]] describes a fast surge reaching the city around twelve hours after eruption onset.
- Harbor and shelter limits: [[355-roman-apocalypse-pompeii-79-ad-glt6664993493]] says neither people near the shore nor those indoors could survive the surge.
- Comparative chronology: [[355-roman-apocalypse-pompeii-79-ad-glt6664993493]] places Herculaneum's burial before Pompeii's last escape window and final flow.

## Qualifications

The timing, burial depth, speed, and individual victim locations are reported through the episode and remain source-scoped. Initial shelter from pumice did not amount to overall safety, illustrating why hazard exposure must be tracked across stages.

## What Changed

- Created a profile distinguishing Herculaneum's lighter early pumice exposure from its earlier destruction by surge.
