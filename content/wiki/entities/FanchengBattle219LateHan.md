## Overview

219年樊城之战 / Battle of Fancheng is the current wiki's event page for Guan Yu's flood-assisted defeat of Yu Jin's field army, siege pressure on Cao Ren, Xu Huang's landward relief, and the simultaneous loss of Guan Yu's Jiangling rear to Eastern Wu.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1166-ruhe-yu-piqi-da-nengli-qiang-de-ren-xiangchu-lhm17dmeln7dmj1czmeq7omxgtmh|Hanji 1166]] says [[GuanYu|关羽]] advances north while [[CaoRen|曹仁]] holds Fancheng and [[YuJin|于禁]] with [[PangDe|庞德]] camps outside. Sustained rain and Han River flooding overwhelm the field position. Guan Yu uses boats to surround survivors; Yu Jin surrenders, while Pang De is captured after continued resistance and executed after refusing submission.

The flood also damages Fancheng and creates pressure to evacuate. [[ManChong|满宠]] argues that the water will fall and that withdrawal could expose the region south of the Yellow River; Cao Ren and the defenders recommit to holding. The source says they remain until [[XuHuangCaoGeneral|徐晃]] arrives, buying [[CaoCao|曹操]] time for a wider response involving [[SunQuan|孙权]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1170-zuoshi-cun-jiaoxing-zhen-de-hui-wanjiebufu-ma-lkju2wdbb-v20gf2vikkr_aibjnx|Hanji 1170]] supplies the relief action and its limits. Cao Cao reinforces Xu Huang while remaining at Mobei. Xu Huang feints toward one siege sector, redirects against another, defeats Guan Yu's returning cavalry, and drives through successive field defenses until the land encirclement opens. Guan Yu's boats still control the Mian River, so land relief does not instantly restore every route.

The same source makes the campaign inseparable from its rear. [[LyuMeng|吕蒙]]'s disguised river movement removes warning posts and reaches [[JianglingLateHan|江陵]], where [[FuShirenLateHan|傅士仁]] and [[MiFangLateHan|糜芳]] surrender. Guan Yu therefore retreats under simultaneous forward reversal and rear-base loss; the campaign is not simply a flood-created victory later canceled by another frontal battle.

## Key Characteristics

- Northern offensive launched from Guan Yu's Jingzhou position in 219 CE.
- Battlefield outcome transformed by extreme rainfall and Han River flooding.
- Produces Yu Jin's surrender and Pang De's refusal and execution.
- Places Fancheng's damaged garrison under severe physical and strategic pressure.
- Preserved by a hold decision, morale recommitment, and later relief rather than by immediate battlefield reversal.
- Relief breaks the land siege through feint, concentration, and pursuit while leaving river control temporarily contested.
- Creates the high point later compressed as Guan Yu “威震华夏,” then turns through coupled frontal relief and rear-area collapse.

## Qualifications

Rainfall, flood mechanics, army and captive totals, positions, wall damage, defections, dialogue, execution sequence, camp counts, place-name readings, feint mechanics, pursuit depth, river control, warning-post capture, surrender timing, and precise Cao-Sun coordination require broader textual comparison. The interpersonal rupture with Guan Yu's rear commanders helps explain vulnerability but cannot replace supply pressure, surprise, military coercion, and alliance interests.

## What Changed

- Added Xu Huang's feint, counterattack, and incomplete landward relief.
- Integrated Jiangling's loss and subordinate surrender into the campaign outcome.
- Reframed the reversal as coupled forward and rear collapse rather than one decisive frontal exchange.
