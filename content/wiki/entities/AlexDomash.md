# Alex Domash

## Overview
Alex Domash is the labor-market source voice in [[working-through-it-the-ai-jobs-boom-dawns-6aa27a3fa6e9aef4cc7741b8]], where he explains why the U.S. data discussed in the episode do not yet support a broad AI jobs-apocalypse narrative.

## Current Profile
The source uses Domash as the analyst who translates AI employment anxiety into occupational evidence. His segment distinguishes AI-attributed layoffs from job creation in data-center infrastructure, skilled trades, and professional technical work.

## Key Characteristics
- Grounds the AI jobs discussion in American labor-market data rather than in abstract automation forecasts.
- Treats AI-attributed layoffs as real but small relative to labor-market churn in the source account.
- Emphasizes infrastructure roles and professional technical roles as AI-boom demand channels.
- Preserves unevenness by noting weaker growth in customer-service and administrative-assistant roles.

## Evidence
- Labor-market framing: [[working-through-it-the-ai-jobs-boom-dawns-6aa27a3fa6e9aef4cc7741b8]] has Domash cite strong job creation, low unemployment, and low youth unemployment as evidence against immediate broad AI labor-market damage.
- Counterweight framing: [[working-through-it-the-ai-jobs-boom-dawns-6aa27a3fa6e9aef4cc7741b8]] has him compare company-attributed AI layoffs with The Economist's estimate of roughly 1 million AI-boom-created jobs.
- Occupational nuance: [[working-through-it-the-ai-jobs-boom-dawns-6aa27a3fa6e9aef4cc7741b8]] has him distinguish paralegals, financial analysts, translators, customer-service representatives, and administrative assistants.
