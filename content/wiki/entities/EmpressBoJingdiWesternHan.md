## Overview

薄皇后 / Empress Bo is the first empress of [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]], whose arranged marriage and later deposition show how palace rank could depend on senior-family protection as well as the emperor's favor.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-238-qiguo-zhiluan-huainanwang-liuan-ruhe-zhuanweiweian-1-lvolhayetcfp4km8mewo9qr-aq0d|Hanji 238]] says Empress Dowager Bo arranged the marriage while Liu Qi was crown prince. After his accession, the Bo woman became empress despite the episode's claim that Jingdi did not favor her. Once the senior dowager died, Jingdi deposed Empress Bo in 151 BCE, reduced her to commoner status, and confined her within the palace. The source uses the sequence as the immediate opening for a later succession and harem realignment rather than as a complete biography.

## Key Characteristics

- Bo-family woman selected for crown prince Liu Qi through an arranged marriage.
- Becomes empress after Liu Qi's accession as Emperor Jing.
- Presented as lacking the emperor's personal favor.
- Politically protected while the senior Empress Dowager Bo remains alive.
- Loses rank and is confined after that protection disappears.

Deposition:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-238-qiguo-zhiluan-huainanwang-liuan-ruhe-zhuanweiweian-1-lvolhayetcfp4km8mewo9qr-aq0d|Hanji 238]] places her deposition, reduction, and palace confinement after the dowager's death in the 151 BCE chronology.

## Qualifications

The present evidence is one short podcast summary. It does not establish Empress Bo's own political activity, speech, household network, legal status after deposition, or later life. The claim that Jingdi disliked her and the direct causal weight assigned to the dowager's death remain episode-attributed. She is distinct from [[BaoJi|薄姬]], the earlier consort and mother of Emperor Wen, although the source places both within the broader Bo-family succession setting.

## What Changed

- Created the first canonical profile for Jingdi's first empress.
- Kept the protection-loss explanation bounded to the source rather than treating it as a complete causal account.
