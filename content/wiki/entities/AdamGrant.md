# Adam Grant

## Overview
Adam Grant is an organizational psychologist, Wharton professor, and author interviewed by [[AndrewHuberman]] about motivation, creativity, feedback, rethinking, character skills, and [[HiddenPotential]].

## Current Profile
Grant's practical psychology repeatedly separates useful mechanisms from moral labels. Delay can be incubation or costly procrastination; rewards can raise output or crowd out autonomy; authenticity can express values or excuse impulsiveness; and confidence can grow through contribution as well as praise. His organizing concern is how people and institutions create conditions in which learning remains possible.

His method combines research summaries, personal experiments, and memorable prompts. He recommends asking for advice rather than generic feedback, scoring how well criticism is received, seeking stories of one's reflected best self, treating opinions as hypotheses, and calibrating standards by task importance.

## Key Characteristics
- Reframes motivation and performance as interactions among person, task, incentives, and environment.
- Treats curiosity, purpose, autonomy, and meaningful contribution as distinct routes into action.
- Favors future-oriented advice and multiple independent evaluators over identity-level judgment.
- Uses [[ScientistModeThinking]] to make revision, disagreement, and failed experiments compatible with confidence.
- Defines potential through growth under challenge rather than initial ability.
- Promotes imperfectionism as selective allocation of excellence, not indifference to quality.
