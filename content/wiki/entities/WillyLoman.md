# Willy Loman / 威利·洛曼

## Overview
Willy Loman is the sixty-three-year-old salesman at the center of [[DeathOfASalesman|《推销员之死》]], represented by the episode as a man who confuses popularity, market usefulness, family provision, and personal worth.

## Current Profile
Willy's tragedy is not simply that his career fails. He has made the belief that being liked produces success into a total explanation of life and passed it to his sons. When employment, memory, and family authority deteriorate together, revising that belief feels less like learning from evidence than erasing the person he thinks he has been.

His suicide is read as a final attempt to convert himself into economic value through life insurance and to preserve the image of provider. The act can contain care for his family without becoming a clear-eyed or successful solution; it remains bound to the same worth-through-utility system that has trapped him.

## Key Characteristics
- Salesman who treats personality, relationships, and popularity as market capital.
- Father who transmits his success creed to Biff and Happy.
- Worker unable to separate loss of employer demand from loss of personhood.
- Character whose present perception is repeatedly invaded by memory and fantasy.
- Man who rejects offered work and help because they threaten his life-defining belief.
- Tragic provider who imagines death and insurance as proof that his family still needs him.

- Success creed: [[vol-355-tuixiaoyuan-zhisi-naxie-shabusi-wode-weishenme-yizhi-zai-dawo-gkwriw4ols9xawr6vqtifmya]] repeatedly identifies Willy's belief that being liked and well connected guarantees success.
- Self as commodity: [[vol-355-tuixiaoyuan-zhisi-naxie-shabusi-wode-weishenme-yizhi-zai-dawo-gkwriw4ols9xawr6vqtifmya]] uses Miller's formulation that the salesman sells himself.
- Refused alternatives: [[vol-355-tuixiaoyuan-zhisi-naxie-shabusi-wode-weishenme-yizhi-zai-dawo-gkwriw4ols9xawr6vqtifmya]] notes the neighbor's job offer and available family help.
- Death as provision: [[vol-355-tuixiaoyuan-zhisi-naxie-shabusi-wode-weishenme-yizhi-zai-dawo-gkwriw4ols9xawr6vqtifmya]] interprets the insurance plan as Willy's last attempt to retain the provider role.

## Qualifications
Willy is a fictional character, and the present profile reflects one interpretive discussion. His suicide should not be generalized as an inevitable response to unemployment or status loss, and the source does not establish that his intended insurance outcome succeeds.

## What Changed
- Created a profile distinguishing material career failure from identity-level commitment to a success myth.
