# American Civil War

## Overview

The American Civil War is the conflict between the Union and Confederacy presented here as a military struggle whose political endurance, slavery crisis, emancipation, and citizenship consequences changed together.

## Current Profile

[[202-american-civil-war-gettysburg-part-3-glt5256515778]] centers the war's 1863-65 endgame. Confederate prospects rested on [[ConfederateCostImpositionStrategy|making continued war politically intolerable]] to the North, while Union advantages in population, logistics, and resources made replacement of losses increasingly unequal. [[BattleOfGettysburg|Gettysburg]] and [[SiegeOfVicksburg|Vicksburg]] sharply changed that balance without making the remaining outcome automatic.

The war also became a struggle over slavery's destruction. Federal policy through the [[EmancipationProclamation]], [[BlackUnionMilitaryServiceCitizenship|Black military service]], and [[EnslavedSelfEmancipation|enslaved people's flight and resistance]] interacted rather than forming a single top-down sequence. The episode closes at Appomattox, where military surrender left Reconstruction and historical memory unresolved.

## Key Characteristics

- Military contest shaped by asymmetric resources, geography, logistics, command, and replacement capacity.
- Political endurance contest in which Northern elections and morale formed part of Confederate strategy.
- Slavery crisis transformed by federal policy, Black military service, and enslaved people's own action.
- Multi-theater war whose turning points cannot be reduced to one famous battlefield.
- Conflict whose surrender settlement did not resolve citizenship, racial power, or memory.

## Evidence

### Strategic and political endurance

- [[202-american-civil-war-gettysburg-part-3-glt5256515778]] links Lee's Pennsylvania invasion and the 1864 election to Confederate hopes that Northern willingness to continue could collapse before Confederate armies did.

### Paired 1863 turning points

- [[202-american-civil-war-gettysburg-part-3-glt5256515778]] pairs the defeat of Lee's invasion with Union control of the Mississippi after Vicksburg.

### Emancipation and agency

- [[202-american-civil-war-gettysburg-part-3-glt5256515778]] joins the proclamation, Black enlistment, unequal treatment, and self-emancipation to the wartime breakdown of slavery.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to one retrospective episode focused on the war's later phase. It does not cover secession, the conflict's opening years, every theater, civilian life in full, diplomacy, wartime governance, or Reconstruction. “Turning point” describes a major change in strategic possibilities rather than a date after which outcomes became inevitable.

## What Changed

- Established the conflict's profile around political endurance, paired military turning points, and emancipation through policy and Black agency.

## Relationships

- [[BattleOfGettysburg]] - battlefield that stopped Lee's 1863 northern invasion.
- [[SiegeOfVicksburg]] - campaign victory that gave the Union control of the Mississippi.
- [[ConfederateCostImpositionStrategy]] - Confederate route to victory through Northern political exhaustion.
- [[EmancipationProclamation]] - federal wartime policy changing the legal and military attack on slavery.
- [[BlackUnionMilitaryServiceCitizenship]] - military participation joining Union capacity to claims for political membership.
- [[EnslavedSelfEmancipation]] - bottom-up action weakening slavery and forcing Union policy choices.
- [[CivilWarMemoryPoliticalAfterlife]] - unresolved public meaning that outlived military surrender.
