# William Pirrie

## Overview

William Pirrie was the shipbuilder and political figure who led [[HarlandAndWolff]] during the planning and construction of [[RMSTitanic|RMS Titanic]].

## Current Profile

[[427-titanic-the-tragedy-begins-part-1-glt3124111041]] presents Pirrie as the commercial organizer connecting [[Belfast]]'s industrial capacity to transatlantic shipping ambition. Born in Quebec City and raised in Ireland after his father's death, he entered Harland & Wolff as a teenage apprentice and became a salesman and leader who helped make it the world's largest shipbuilder. His working relationship with [[WhiteStarLine]] placed him alongside [[BruceIsmay]] in the decision to answer rival liners with Olympic, Titanic, and Britannic.

Pirrie also crossed Belfast's dominant political alignment. Although a Protestant industrial magnate and former lord mayor, his support for Irish Home Rule blocked a Unionist parliamentary path and moved him toward the Liberal Party. The source therefore treats him as both an Edwardian “great man” of industry and a figure whose politics complicated his place within the city's Protestant establishment.

## Key Characteristics

- Teenage Harland & Wolff apprentice who rose to lead the shipbuilding firm.
- Salesman and industrial organizer credited with helping build the world's largest shipyard.
- White Star partner involved in planning Olympic, Titanic, and Britannic.
- Belfast lord mayor in 1897 and prominent owner of several major residences.
- Protestant industrialist whose support for Home Rule separated him from much of the local Unionist establishment.

## Evidence

- Career and industrial leadership: [[427-titanic-the-tragedy-begins-part-1-glt3124111041]] follows Pirrie from apprenticeship at fifteen to leadership of Harland & Wolff and reports more than 11,000 employees by the end of 1910.
- Titanic planning: [[427-titanic-the-tragedy-begins-part-1-glt3124111041]] places Pirrie with Bruce Ismay in White Star's response to Cunard and German competition.
- Public standing: [[427-titanic-the-tragedy-begins-part-1-glt3124111041]] reports W. T. Stead's praise, Pirrie's 1897 lord mayoralty, and his Edwardian elite lifestyle.
- Home Rule politics: [[427-titanic-the-tragedy-begins-part-1-glt3124111041]] says Unionists rejected his South Belfast ambitions because he backed Home Rule and that he later aligned with the Liberals.
