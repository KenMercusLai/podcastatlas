## Overview

袁盎 / Yuan Ang is a Western Han official whose current profile joins a successful succession remonstrance to [[EmpressDowagerDouWesternHan|窦太后]] with his later opposition to a Liang road project and assassination by attackers attributed to the circle of [[LiuWuLiangKingWesternHan|梁孝王刘武]].

## Current Profile

In [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-240-hanjingdi-kusu-xiongnu-he-taihou-sha-le-wode-ying-lvl4t-2etswvi3ts9gbvor5dh8mi|Hanji 240]], Yuan Ang opposes the dowager's wish to make Liu Wu successor to [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]]. He uses the story of Song Xuangong: placing a brother ahead of the ruler's son does not settle succession but transfers the conflict into the next generation, where competing descendant lines help produce a five-generation disorder.

The intervention works by reframing maternal affection as a state-risk problem. Yuan does not merely tell the dowager to suppress feeling; he asks whether preserving a “small love” is worth exposing the dynasty to repeated civil conflict. The episode says she accepts the warning and sends Liu Wu back to Liang, making historical analogy a practical form of court remonstrance.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-241-liangxiaowang-liuwu-kan-wo-ruhe-huayang-zuosi-lqbllfh9nph-vvu9emzhsxdfj1le|Hanji 241]] fills that earlier evidentiary gap. It says Yuan and more than ten other ministers oppose Liu Wu's request for a road from Suiyang toward Changle Palace. After Liu Wu communicates his hatred, favored advisers 羊胜 and 公孙诡 organize repeated assassination attempts. One hired attacker reportedly warns Yuan after hearing his good reputation, but a later attacker kills him outside the Anling gate.

The subsequent investigation traces the attackers to Liang and reaches the two advisers inside Liu Wu's palace. This supplies a plot and protection mechanism, but not a surviving complete royal order or judgment: the episode later says [[TianShuHan|田叔]] burns the testimony and records while privately describing Liu Wu's offense as capital. Yuan's profile can now record a substantial Liang case without turning a politically managed and deliberately damaged evidentiary chain into certainty about every instruction.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han official presented as an opponent of collateral succession in favor of the emperor's established line.
- Remonstrant who uses a historical analogy rather than a direct personal accusation against the dowager or Liang king.
- Advocate of larger dynastic duty over a senior royal's immediate family preference.
- Court actor whose warning reportedly helps send Liu Wu back to his fief.
- Later assassination victim whose Liang-linked grievance, advisers, attackers, investigation, and palace concealment are now narrated, while the precise royal command remains unresolved.

## Qualifications

This profile is based on two short source notes rather than a full biography. The exact succession proposal, road design and effects, Song precedent, speeches, audience procedure, dowager's reasoning, intended victims, attackers, warning episode, death location, investigative evidence, Liu Wu's instructions, and final adjudication require broader textual comparison. Hanji 241 establishes a much fuller alleged Liang mechanism, but its own claim that records were burned prevents treating the reconstructed case as a complete surviving judgment.

## What Changed

- Filled the prior assassination gap with the road grievance, adviser-led plot, repeated attackers, investigation, and palace concealment.
- Kept Liu Wu's precise instruction and legal guilt qualified because the account ends in deliberate evidence destruction rather than a completed judgment.
