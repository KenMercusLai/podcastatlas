# Changing Homelessness

## Overview

Changing Homelessness is the Jacksonville organization presented as maintaining a person-level by-name list for people experiencing homelessness.

## Current Profile

The organization combines annual street outreach with recurring case review. Volunteers search locations that shelter and service records may miss; staff then map named individuals by vulnerability and time without housing and discuss barriers and possible housing resources each Tuesday.

This workflow makes homelessness operationally legible, but the episode also shows its limit: person-level knowledge cannot create apartments at rents clients can afford.

## Key Characteristics

- It searches streets, bridges, cars, and abandoned buildings to find people missed by conventional service records.
- It records people by name rather than as anonymous aggregate counts.
- It uses vulnerability and duration without housing to structure prioritization.
- It holds weekly case review focused on individual barriers and available resources.
- Its housing outcomes remain constrained by the supply and price of deeply affordable units.

## Qualifications

The source does not provide the list's data governance, consent process, coverage rate, prioritization validation, longitudinal housing outcomes, or a full account of Jacksonville's housing market. The workflow should not be treated as proof of program effectiveness by itself.
