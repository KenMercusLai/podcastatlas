# Christmas in Connecticut (1992 film)

## Overview

*Christmas in Connecticut* is a 1992 TNT television remake of the 1945 screwball comedy, produced by [[StanBrooks]] and directed by [[ArnoldSchwarzenegger]]. The remake turns the fraudulent domestic columnist into a television personality and combines romantic comedy with an added action-rescue opening.

## Current Profile

The project began as a small, close update written by Janet Brownell for the early cable market. Schwarzenegger's involvement changed its risk profile and creative direction: action and jeopardy expanded, comedy rewrites followed, and a cast led by Dyan Cannon, Kris Kristofferson, and Tony Curtis worked within an approximately $3 million budget and a 20-day Los Angeles shoot.

The production's ambitions often exceeded its resources and its inexperienced feature director's execution. The episode highlights an unconvincing staged rescue, visible sleigh wheels, and a baby-bath take whose unrinsed shampoo breaks the scene's intended competence and romance. Brownell reportedly experienced the premiere as painful because the released film had departed from her conception while retaining her credit.

TNT heavily promoted the film but premiered it on April 13, 1992, outside the Christmas season. Mixed reviews did not produce a directing career for Schwarzenegger. Its later value in the episode comes instead from production memory: many participants recall commitment, humor, friendship, and the pleasure of having made an improbable work.

## Key Characteristics

- Cable-television remake that modernized a fraudulent domestic persona into a TV celebrity.
- Hybrid of screwball romance and action spectacle created through late creative expansion.
- Star-led production constrained by a television budget, short schedule, and inexperienced feature director.
- Artistically uneven film whose specific staging and continuity failures remain visible onscreen.
- Work remembered more warmly as a shared production experience than as a critical or career success.
