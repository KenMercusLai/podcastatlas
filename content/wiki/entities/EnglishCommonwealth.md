# English Commonwealth

## Overview

The English Commonwealth was the crownless regime created after the 1649 execution of [[CharlesIOfEngland|Charles I]], later reshaped under [[OliverCromwell]]'s Protectorate and ended by the 1660 Restoration.

## Current Profile

The episodes present the Commonwealth as a sequence of improvised settlements rather than one settled constitution. Monarchy and the House of Lords were abolished, but authority remained divided between a purged House of Commons and the army that had made the rupture possible. Barebone's Parliament tested nominated godly government but could not reconcile radical reform with army finance. The [[InstrumentOfGovernment1653|Instrument of Government]] then established a Protector, Council of State, and Parliament without resolving representation, religious government, succession, or civilian control.

The regime mixed experiment with coercion. It permitted some radical and religious openings, built administrative and military capacity, and advanced union and parliamentary claims, yet also taxed heavily, expelled legislators, controlled worship, reconquered Ireland, suppressed the [[Levellers]], and enabled dispossession. Cromwell sought “healing and settling,” but the regime remained a quasi-monarchy backed by the army and undecided between elective and hereditary royal or Protectoral succession. Its institutions collapsed after his death, but parts of its political and state-building inheritance outlived it and monarchy did not return in its prewar form.

## Key Characteristics

- Republic formed after regicide without a fully agreed successor constitution.
- Depended on an unstable alliance between Parliament and the army.
- Passed through Commonwealth and Protectorate forms under changing leadership.
- Produced a written Protectorate constitution without creating settled constitutional legitimacy.
- Combined religious and political experimentation with coercive government.
- Expanded military, administrative, and overseas state capacity.
- Failed to establish durable succession or shared constitutional legitimacy.

## Qualifications

“English Commonwealth” compresses several constitutional phases and a wider three-kingdom problem. The episodes are broad social and political interpretations, not a complete institutional chronology, and “Republic of Britain” should not imply an uncontested modern British state form. Calling the Instrument of Government the first written constitution and rejecting a modern-dictator label depend on contested definitions; neither claim removes army power or coercion.

## What Changed

- Added Barebone's Parliament and the Instrument of Government as distinct attempts to turn revolutionary authority into a settlement.
- Clarified that the Protectorate's quasi-monarchical options remained unresolved at Cromwell's death.
