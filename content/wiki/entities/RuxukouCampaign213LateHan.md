## Overview

濡须口之战（213年） / Ruxukou Campaign (213 CE) is the [[CaoCao|曹操]]-[[SunQuan|孙权]] confrontation that Hanji 1133 locates around the Ruxu waterway after Jiangdong's earlier construction of [[RuxuFortressLateHan|濡须坞]].

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1133-sunquan-rensheng-zui-xiaosa-de-zhanyi-ruxukou-zhi-zhan-lrkz2p2fvh2fpw5owjdj7xg_dc8g|Hanji 1133]] says Cao Cao gains an initial west-bank success but cannot convert it into a decisive crossing or collapse of Sun Quan's defense. Sun Quan uses the river line and naval capacity to sustain the standoff, and his reported light-boat reconnaissance lets Cao Cao observe an organized opponent at close range.

The campaign ends through combined military and environmental judgment rather than a single decisive engagement in the source's telling. Sun Quan warns that spring water will rise while openly stating that Cao Cao remains a threat; Cao Cao assesses the worsening river conditions and withdraws. The episode therefore treats the event as both a defense case and a bounded exit from conflict: credible resistance, changing terrain, direct signaling, and risk reassessment jointly stop further escalation.

## Key Characteristics

- Riverine campaign in which terrain and naval competence limit the stronger northern force.
- Follows earlier Jiangdong preparation at Ruxu for a renewed Cao-side offensive.
- Includes an initial Cao-side success without a decisive strategic breakthrough.
- Uses visible force readiness and direct communication to shape the opponent's assessment.
- Ends with withdrawal as conditions become less favorable to continued attack.
