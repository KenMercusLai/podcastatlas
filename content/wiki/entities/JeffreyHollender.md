# Jeffrey Hollender

## Overview

Jeffrey Hollender is the [[SeventhGeneration]] co-founder and former CEO whose career connects socially responsible business, consumer-brand scaling, founder conflict, board governance, and later advice to mission-driven entrepreneurs.

## Current Profile

Jeffrey joined Alan's environmental catalog with capital-raising connections and a developed interest in responsible business. After their partnership collapsed, he shifted Seventh Generation from a catalog-heavy operation to a wholesale household-products brand, emphasizing health, safety, performance, environmental benefit, and retailer economics. He was later removed by the board amid disputes over leadership role, activism, capital, employee ownership, and executive relationships. His later advice consistently joins purpose to business performance, trial, repeat purchase, controlled growth, and caution about giving partners equity before operating fit is known.

## Key Characteristics

- Mission-driven founder who argues that responsible business still needs a competitive and financial case.
- Brand and financing leader who helped convert Seventh Generation into a nationally distributed consumer-products company.
- Candid critic of green consumption who distinguishes lower-impact disposable products from eliminating harm.
- Founder who accepts responsibility for hurting Alan and for failing to bring the board along before his own removal.
- Advisor who favors role clarity, demonstrated fit, product function, customer education, and sustainable growth.

## Qualifications

The company history is largely founder testimony and does not include direct accounts from directors, employees, investors, or Chuck Maniscalco. Jeffrey's later advice is reflective rather than proof that every mission-driven company should copy Seventh Generation's positioning, ownership, or growth choices.
