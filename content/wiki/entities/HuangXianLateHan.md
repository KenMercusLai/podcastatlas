## Overview

黄宪 / Huang Xian enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-798-donghan-shiqi-de-baixing-you-duo-ku-loolveumdtv4eo-omubfi5z2p2r5|Hanji 798]] as a low-born Runan figure whose moral force is known almost entirely through others' reactions. [[XunShuLateHan|荀淑]], [[DaiLiangLateHan|戴良]], [[ChenFanLateHan|陈蕃]], and [[GuoTaiLateHan|郭泰]] all treat him as unusually deep, while Huang himself leaves little recorded doctrine and does not convert reputation into office.

## Current Profile

Huang Xian's social origin is central to the episode's reading. His father is described as a veterinarian, so the praise from elite scholars is not grounded in family status. Xun Shu meets him at fourteen in Shenyang, bows to him, speaks with him at length, and later tells [[YuanLangLateHan|袁朗]] that Runan has a Yan Hui-like figure.

The rest of the source builds Huang Xian by comparison. Dai Liang is proud and talented, but after seeing Huang he admits inferiority. Chen Fan says months without seeing Huang let base thoughts arise, and later says he would not dare hold high office if Huang were still alive. Guo Tai visits Yuan Lang but stays several days with Huang, then compares Yuan Lang to a clear stream and Huang to a wide, unmeasurable body of water.

The source closes Huang Xian as a person outside office. He is recommended as xiaolian and summoned by senior offices, but he goes briefly to the capital, returns home, lives quietly, does not join corrupt elite currents, dies at forty-eight, and is remembered as a zhengjun. His significance is therefore [[MoralPresenceWithoutOffice|不仕而为道德镜鉴]], not administrative achievement.

## Key Characteristics

- Low-status Runan scholar whose father is identified as a veterinarian.
- Early-recognized moral figure whom Xun Shu treats as teacher-like at age fourteen.
- Benchmark who humbles other talented men rather than proving himself through office.
- Reputation figure known through others' transformed self-judgment more than through preserved writings or policy.
- Office-refusing zhengjun whose public meaning remains outside formal administration.
- Source-bounded moral exemplar whose exact teachings and independent doctrine are not preserved in the episode.

## Qualifications

The source gives Huang Xian through reputation anecdotes rather than direct writings, policy decisions, or a full biography. The host also qualifies the hagiographic tone by joking that Huang's persona may be strong; this page preserves the episode's historical claim as social reputation and moral effect, not as proof of saintly metaphysics.

## What Changed

- Created Huang Xian as the moral center of Hanji 798's Runan branch.
- Framed his importance as office-refusing moral presence rather than administrative action.
