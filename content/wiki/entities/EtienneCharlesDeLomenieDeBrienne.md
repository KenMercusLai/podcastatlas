# Etienne Charles de Lomenie de Brienne

## Overview

Etienne Charles de Lomenie de Brienne was the Archbishop of Toulouse and finance minister whose failed tax and court reforms preceded the [[DayOfTheTiles|Day of the Tiles]], the promise of the [[EstatesGeneral1789|Estates-General]], and the monarchy's admission of effective bankruptcy.

## Current Profile

Brienne entered office after doubting [[CharlesAlexandreDeCalonne|Calonne]]'s warnings, but the source says examination of the accounts revealed an even larger deficit. He pursued land and stamp taxes, exiled the Paris Parlement when it demanded national consent, and later tried to remove the parlements' power to police royal edicts through a new plenary court. Resistance spread across Paris and provincial capitals. In Grenoble the attempt to remove local judges helped trigger the [[DayOfTheTiles|Day of the Tiles]]; by August 1788 Brienne advised [[LouisXVI|Louis XVI]] to call the Estates-General and announced that the treasury could no longer make normal payments.

## Key Characteristics

- Confirmed that the monarchy's deficit was worse than opposition rhetoric had assumed.
- Continued status-spanning tax reform after Calonne's dismissal.
- Used exile and institutional redesign against parlementary obstruction.
- Faced provincial resistance rooted in local identity and economic dependence as well as constitutional principle.
- Concluded that representative consent was unavoidable when credit and administrative coercion failed.
