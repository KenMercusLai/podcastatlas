## Overview

第二次河西之战 / Second Hexi Campaign is presented as a multi-column Western Han operation against [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] in which [[HuoQubing|霍去病]] tries to bypass the narrow [[HexiCorridorWesternHan|河西走廊]], reach the enemy rear through desert movement, and reunite with [[GongsunAoWesternHan|公孙敖]] near Juyan. Gongsun Ao misses the reunion after becoming lost, but Huo continues and the episode credits his unsupported force with severely disrupting the western Xiongnu political and military position. A parallel [[LiGuangHanGeneral|李广]]–[[ZhangQianWesternHan|张骞]] branch survives encirclement but receives a much less favorable court judgment.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-308-bei-tangshi-chui-cheng-shen-de-ta-jing-shi-ge-luchi-lsjtzqsil36lxejij8gazn58qeeb|Hanji 308]] supplies the design and parallel battlefield. Huo rejects a narrow frontal approach that could merely push the opponent away, instead taking a desert-edge route intended to reach the rear. Gongsun Ao takes a separate northern route toward a water-dependent reunion near Juyan. The same episode then follows Li Guang's isolated four-thousand-cavalry force: Li Gan's probe, circular defense, controlled close-range shooting, and visible command presence help it survive until relief, but severe losses yield a court judgment of功过相当, while Zhang Qian is punished for delay.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-309-1-huoqubing-duohui-hexi-zoulang-zhongguo-bixu-tongyi-llcwkkdgjpx2zjeoyvhdjln3nqik|Hanji 309-1]] supplies the deferred western outcome. Gongsun Ao becomes lost and fails to arrive; Huo chooses continuation over withdrawal, crosses the desert, and attacks the opposing rear. The episode says some Lesser Yuezhi-associated rulers surrender, Zhao Ponu and Gao Buzhi defeat resisting groups, and Huo reaches the Qilian area before returning after heavy combat. Court rewards and punishments convert the split outcomes into a performance judgment: Huo and successful subordinates gain status, while Gongsun Ao pays to avoid death and loses rank.

The campaign's strategic importance is narrower than the title's single-act “recovery” frame. The western Xiongnu position is badly weakened, and the episode uses the loss of Qilian and Yanzhi to explain pasture, livestock, resource, and prestige damage. Yet Xiongnu raids on Dai and Yanmen continue, and later surrender and settlement sources are still needed to explain more durable Han control of the corridor.

## Key Characteristics

- Rear-access campaign design intended to convert frontal displacement into deeper disruption of the opposing force.
- Water- and route-dependent multi-column operation whose planned reunion fails when Gongsun Ao becomes lost.
- Resilient but high-risk continuation in which Huo Qubing proceeds without the expected supporting column.
- Split campaign outcome: Huo's western attack produces major rewards, while Li Guang's survival under encirclement yields no reward and delayed commanders are punished.
- Strategic turning point that weakens Xiongnu's western position without by itself ending the wider war or completing all later corridor consolidation.

## Qualifications

The page is bounded to two compact podcast summaries rather than a primary-source campaign reconstruction. Route geometry, authorship of the plan, Juyan identification, climate, water access, distances, force and casualty totals, relief timing, opposing identities, captive ranks, rewards, sentences, and redemption mechanics remain episode-attributed. “Lesser Yuezhi” may compress a more complicated political field, and the transcribed ruler and group names are not secure enough to create canonical identities here. The campaign can be treated as a major western turning point without accepting that one operation completely “recovered,” “unified,” or permanently cleared the entire Hexi Corridor.

## What Changed

- Created a campaign-level synthesis joining Hanji 308's design and parallel battle to Hanji 309-1's deferred western outcome.
- Reclassified the failed Juyan reunion as an operational rupture that Huo's force survives, not as evidence that the planned support became unnecessary.
- Qualified the single-victory recovery narrative with continuing Xiongnu raids and the later surrender-and-settlement sequence.
