## Overview

Pedro de Alvarado was the Spanish commander left in Tenochtitlan by [[HernanCortes|Hernan Cortes]] who ordered the [[ToxcatlMassacre|Toxcatl massacre]] and precipitated open war in the city.

## Current Profile

The episode describes Alvarado as brave, flamboyant, charming, unreliable, and badly matched to an unstable hostage occupation. With about 120 Spaniards, [[Moctezuma]] in custody, supplies and servants disappearing, rumors circulating, and [[Malinche]] absent as principal interpreter, he concluded that a Mexica festival concealed an imminent attack. He struck first without secure evidence of a plot.

The result defines his profile more than the disputed warning does. Spaniards sealed the temple courtyard and killed unarmed dancers and other celebrants, after which armed resistance drove his force back to the palace. Fear may explain his reasoning, but it does not lessen responsibility for turning uncertainty into mass killing and strategic catastrophe.

## Key Characteristics

- Courageous but unreliable subordinate entrusted with command during Cortes's absence.
- Commander operating under isolation, supply anxiety, rumor, and weakened translation.
- Preemptive decision-maker who treated an unverified attack warning as sufficient for lethal action.
- Responsible commander for a massacre of unarmed festival participants.
- Occupation leader whose action destroyed the remaining possibility of stable accommodation.

## Evidence

- Command fit and conditions: [[388-the-fall-of-the-aztecs-the-festival-of-blood-part-5-glt8784071073]] describes Alvarado's character, small garrison, missing supplies, rumors, and lack of Malinche's translation.
- Decision and killing: [[388-the-fall-of-the-aztecs-the-festival-of-blood-part-5-glt8784071073]] connects his preventive logic to closed exits and attacks on unarmed celebrants.
- Strategic result: [[388-the-fall-of-the-aztecs-the-festival-of-blood-part-5-glt8784071073]] follows the massacre into armed uprising, retreat to the palace, and siege.

## Qualifications

The episode cannot determine whether Alvarado sincerely believed the rumors, whether Tlaxcalan informants shaped them, whether greed also mattered, or whether any Mexica attack was planned. Describing fear and communication failure explains conditions; it does not excuse the killings. The casualty total and fine sequence of the courtyard attack remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Established Alvarado as the proximate decision-maker in the collapse from occupation tension to urban war.
- Distinguished uncertain motive from clear command responsibility.

## Relationships

- [[HernanCortes]] - commander who left him in charge of the Tenochtitlan garrison.
- [[Moctezuma]] - captive ruler held during Alvarado's command.
- [[Malinche]] - absent interpreter whose departure narrowed communication.
- [[ToxcatlMassacre]] - atrocity he ordered as a purported preventive strike.
- [[MexicaEmpire]] - polity whose capital rose against his force after the massacre.
