## Overview

Pedro de Alvarado was a Spanish conquistador whose commands in Mexico and Guatemala combined preemptive violence, massacre, enslavement, and coalition armies before his independent Ecuador expedition ended in disaster and withdrawal.

## Current Profile

The episode describes Alvarado as brave, flamboyant, charming, unreliable, and badly matched to an unstable hostage occupation. With about 120 Spaniards, [[Moctezuma]] in custody, supplies and servants disappearing, rumors circulating, and [[Malinche]] absent as principal interpreter, he concluded that a Mexica festival concealed an imminent attack. He struck first without secure evidence of a plot.

The result defines his profile more than the disputed warning does. Spaniards sealed the temple courtyard and killed unarmed dancers and other celebrants, after which armed resistance drove his force back to the palace. Fear may explain his reasoning, but it does not lessen responsibility for turning uncertainty into mass killing and strategic catastrophe.

[[647-the-fall-of-the-incas-the-king-in-the-north-part-4-glt4740382633]] extends that pattern beyond Mexico. Alvarado lands in Ecuador with roughly five hundred Spaniards and four thousand Guatemalan auxiliaries after campaigns in Guatemala marked by enslavement, hanging, burning, and killing by dogs severe enough to prompt later judicial inquiry. His inland expedition loses its way, suffers disease and volcanic ash, and enters the Andes through a lethal pass where Spaniards, Guatemalans, porters, and camp followers die of exposure. He then sells ships and equipment to [[DiegoDeAlmagro|Almagro]] and returns to Guatemala.

## Key Characteristics

- Courageous but unreliable commander willing to treat unverified threat as grounds for preemptive killing.
- Responsible commander for the massacre of unarmed Toxcatl festival participants.
- Conquistador whose Guatemala record included enslavement and exemplary terror.
- Organizer of an independent Ecuador expedition built from Spanish and Guatemalan forces.
- Commander whose environmental and logistical failure killed members of every part of his expedition.
- Rival bought out of Peru rather than defeated in a Spanish battle.

## Evidence

- Command fit and conditions: [[388-the-fall-of-the-aztecs-the-festival-of-blood-part-5-glt8784071073]] describes Alvarado's character, small garrison, missing supplies, rumors, and lack of Malinche's translation.
- Decision and killing: [[388-the-fall-of-the-aztecs-the-festival-of-blood-part-5-glt8784071073]] connects his preventive logic to closed exits and attacks on unarmed celebrants.
- Strategic result: [[388-the-fall-of-the-aztecs-the-festival-of-blood-part-5-glt8784071073]] follows the massacre into armed uprising, retreat to the palace, and siege.
- Guatemala violence: [[647-the-fall-of-the-incas-the-king-in-the-north-part-4-glt4740382633]] summarizes killings and enslavement that later drew judicial scrutiny.
- Ecuador expedition: [[647-the-fall-of-the-incas-the-king-in-the-north-part-4-glt4740382633]] follows the multinational force through environmental disaster and Almagro's buyout.

## Qualifications

The sources cannot determine whether Alvarado sincerely believed the Toxcatl rumors, whether Tlaxcalan informants shaped them, whether greed also mattered, or whether any Mexica attack was planned. The Guatemala atrocities, judicial inquiry, expedition totals, route, mortality, and buyout terms are also episode-attributed. Describing fear, communication failure, ambition, or environmental disaster explains conditions; it does not excuse killings or erase the losses borne by Indigenous auxiliaries and coerced followers.

## What Changed

- Extended Alvarado's profile from the Toxcatl massacre into Guatemala and the failed Ecuador expedition.
- Added the Indigenous coalition manpower, environmental losses, and negotiated withdrawal that bounded his Peruvian intervention.
