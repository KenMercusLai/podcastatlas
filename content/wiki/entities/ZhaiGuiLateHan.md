## Overview

翟瑰 / Zhai Gui enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-796-donghan-mingchen-vs-diwang-shenbianren-shui-neng-ying-lkyzp2v-cjysd65syoyuldlbdb5p|Hanji 796]] as the Guangling Shangshu who reinforces [[YangZhenLateHan|杨震]]'s objection to [[EmperorAnOfHan|汉安帝]]'s palace favorites. His memorial turns the [[WangShengPalaceActorLateHan|王圣]] and [[BoRongLateHan|伯荣]] field into a broader warning about private households, fiscal depletion, and imperial security.

## Current Profile

Hanji 796 gives Zhai Gui a single but important intervention. He memorializes that the earlier Dou and Deng families gained overwhelming honor, offices, wealth, and influence, but their collapse showed the danger of status and title not acquired through the proper public path.

Zhai's warning is institutional rather than merely moral. He argues that offices, ranks, salary, and authority should not slip from the court into private hands, because such transfer endangers the throne itself. The episode also has him contrast Emperor Wen's careful fiscal restraint with An's post-personal-rule grants, empty treasury, and exhausted people.

Emperor An does not accept Zhai's memorial. That nonresponse makes Zhai part of an early An-era feedback failure: the court can still articulate why favorite households threaten the state, but correction does not follow.

## Key Characteristics

- Guangling Shangshu whose current wiki profile is defined by anti-favorite remonstrance.
- Official who uses Dou and Deng outer-relative precedent to warn against private-household overreach.
- Critic of office, title, salary, and power drifting away from public court control.
- Fiscal remonstrant who links favorite rewards to treasury depletion and popular exhaustion.
- Source-bounded actor whose importance is as a diagnostic voice in Emperor An's court.
