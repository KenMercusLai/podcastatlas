# Alternative for Germany

## Overview

Alternative for Germany, or AfD, is a German far-right party that appears in the wiki first as an electoral pressure point on the [[ChristianDemocraticUnionGermany|CDU]], then as a possible state-governance threat in [[SaxonyAnhalt]], and now as a repeated state-election plurality winner.

## Current Profile

The current profile is source-scoped rather than a full party history. In the national-politics branch, AfD matters because its strength in eastern elections raises the political cost of [[JensSpahn]]'s surrogacy double standard for [[FriedrichMerz]] and the [[ChristianDemocraticUnionGermany|CDU]].

The Saxony-Anhalt branch turns AfD from pressure actor into a possible governing actor. The pre-election source presents [[UlrichSiegmund]] as a candidate who could benefit from voter dissatisfaction with incumbents, anti-immigration politics, and the weakening practical effect of the [[BrandmauerCordonSanitaire|Brandmauer]]. Its program is framed around ending asylum, "remigration," a pro-[[Russia]] foreign-policy posture, stopping support for [[Ukraine]], school segregation proposals, and fiscal promises that critics say do not add up.

The post-election source says AfD won 44%, far above expectations, while the CDU fell to 17%. That result confirms the electoral breakthrough but complicates the direct-governing scenario: [[BundnisSahraWagenknecht|BSW]] becomes a possible kingmaker, Siegmund says he does not want a minority government, and [[SvenSchulze]] could remain acting premier if Saxony-Anhalt's parliament cannot elect a successor.

The later state-election source reports another first-place finish, about 38% in Mecklenburg-Vorpommern. Yet coalition exclusion may again keep AfD out of government, reinforcing the distinction between vote leadership and executive control. It also places AfD inside [[GermanCentristFragmentation]] rather than a simple one-directional shift, because a hard-left party simultaneously gains in Berlin.

The [[AliceWeidel]] interview supplies a national governing profile. AfD would seek cheaper energy and restored nuclear power, lower taxes and spending, a looser sovereign-state EU, stronger German defence but no further support for Ukraine, and removals extending beyond people already ordered to leave. The interview also exposes unresolved implementation tensions: fiscal savings are not demonstrated, EU exit remains ambiguous, migration categories expand under questioning, and coalition access is demanded while compromise on central policies is rejected.

## Key Characteristics

- Far-right German party whose eastern-state strength can discipline mainstream conservatives and make AfD the central election story even when it is not governing.
- Party whose possible Saxony-Anhalt majority would test whether the Brandmauer can still block executive control.
- Political vehicle for removal-oriented immigration promises whose stated remigration scope expands from enforceable departures to large refugee populations.
- Russia-friendly and Ukraine-skeptical party that would end German support for Ukraine while strengthening Germany's own defence and restoring Russian energy access.
- Economically nationalist party combining nuclear restoration, deregulation, tax cuts, welfare and subsidy cuts, pension reform, and a looser EU with unresolved fiscal arithmetic.
- Governing-credibility case where manifesto promises, debt constraints, coalition arithmetic, institutional pushback, and refusal to compromise make implementation uncertain.
- Repeated plurality winner whose coalition isolation can still block direct state executive power.

## Evidence

- Electoral pressure on conservatives: [[brave-new-whirl-turkeys-opposition-overhaul-6a69c7766043035f89eb097f]] says AfD leads in important eastern state elections, making [[JensSpahn]]'s scandal more damaging for [[FriedrichMerz]] and the [[ChristianDemocraticUnionGermany|CDU]].
- Saxony-Anhalt executive test: [[right-in-front-afd-could-win-german-state-6a97f0a5a4a22c581728b85f]] frames AfD as capable of winning a majority in [[SaxonyAnhalt]], which would let it try to govern without coalition partners.
- Immigration program: [[right-in-front-afd-could-win-german-state-6a97f0a5a4a22c581728b85f]] describes AfD promises to end asylum and pursue "remigration," with [[UlrichSiegmund]] presenting these as core answers to voters' frustrations.
- Foreign-policy orientation: [[right-in-front-afd-could-win-german-state-6a97f0a5a4a22c581728b85f]] presents the party as pro-[[Russia]], hostile to support for [[Ukraine]], and interested in restoring Russian-language and Russia-exchange programs.
- Governing constraints: [[right-in-front-afd-could-win-german-state-6a97f0a5a4a22c581728b85f]] stresses that Saxony-Anhalt has about EUR25bn in debt and heavy fiscal dependence on transfers, while AfD has little governing experience.
- Election breakthrough: [[all-right-on-the-night-afds-victory-6a9e8573fe3b7e34fd2d258f]] says AfD reached 44% in Saxony-Anhalt and that state intelligence services describe it as right-wing extremist.
- Governing arithmetic: [[all-right-on-the-night-afds-victory-6a9e8573fe3b7e34fd2d258f]] says BSW's five seats, Siegmund's reluctance to lead a minority government, and the lack of a minister-president vote deadline could produce stalemate.
- Mecklenburg-Vorpommern plurality: [[centre-punch-germanys-state-election-shocker-6ab0f8597aa361c6128028b9]] reports about 38% for AfD but says other parties' refusal to cooperate may allow the Social Democrats to remain in power.
- National economic and EU program: [[an-interview-with-alice-weidel-027be4d01e889c07f016e332ffe9a77e]] records Weidel's case for nuclear restoration, deregulation, lower taxes and spending, pension reform, and a looser sovereign-state EU while leaving euro withdrawal ambiguous.
- Foreign and defence policy: [[an-interview-with-alice-weidel-027be4d01e889c07f016e332ffe9a77e]] has Weidel support more German defence capacity but oppose aid to Ukraine and favor renewed Russian energy access and immediate peace talks.
- Removal scope and coalition posture: [[an-interview-with-alice-weidel-027be4d01e889c07f016e332ffe9a77e]] expands remigration to Syrians, Afghans, and postwar Ukrainians and records her demand that the CDU move first because AfD will not compromise on its central policies.

## Qualifications

The first Saxony-Anhalt source is pre-election; the later state sources report breakthroughs but not stable AfD rule. The Weidel interview is a programmatic account tested by an adversarial interviewer, not an enacted budget or verified forecast. Vote shares, savings, debt effects, energy and migration claims, institutional resistance, coalition arithmetic, and possible social backlash remain source-scoped rather than evidence of governing performance.

## What Changed

- Added a national governing program beyond the existing state-election branch.
- Clarified that remigration can extend well beyond people already subject to removal orders.
- Added the implementation tension between demands for coalition access and refusal to compromise on central policies.
