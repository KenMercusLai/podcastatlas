# Black Dinner

## Overview

The Black Dinner is the name given to the 1440 Edinburgh feast at which the young William Douglas and his brother were seized, subjected to a false trial, and killed after accepting royal hospitality.

## Current Profile

[[154-the-most-disastrous-party-in-history-glt6506112996]] presents the dinner as a classic treacherous-feast narrative. The Douglas brothers dine with the young James II before a black bull's head announces death; they are then removed, tried, and beheaded. Its historical power lies in hospitality becoming a trap, while its modern cultural afterlife includes influence on the Red Wedding in *Game of Thrones*.

## Key Characteristics

- Reconciliation or hospitality converted into controlled dynastic violence.
- Young victims placed inside a court they could not safely leave.
- Bull's-head episode functioning as a memorable symbol of impending death.
- Later cultural model for fictional betrayal at a feast.
