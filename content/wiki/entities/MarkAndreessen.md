## Overview
Mark Andreessen is the wiki's established key for Marc Andreessen, the [[Mosaic]] and [[Netscape]] browser figure and [[AndreessenHorowitz]] co-founder who appears across internet history, investor-network conflict, and a long-form account of innovation, institutions, artificial intelligence, and technological risk.

## Current Profile
Across the bounded sources, Andreessen moves from builder to network actor to public theorist. [[RonConway]] associates his Netscape work with making the internet usable and lowering startup costs through the browser and open-source ecosystem. [[ParkerConrad]] places him inside a disputed investor-network intervention during the Zenefits aftermath. In the Huberman conversation, Andreessen interprets exceptional founders through unusual trait combinations and detailed idea mazes, argues that institutions often resist non-consensus builders, and favors human-augmenting, defensive, permissionless approaches to AI. The profile is influential but perspective-bound: its institutional, personality, nuclear, geopolitical, and regulatory conclusions are arguments rather than neutral consensus.

## Key Characteristics
- Internet builder associated with the browser becoming an ordinary-user access layer.
- Venture investor who evaluates founders through depth, obsession, adaptation, and non-consensus judgment.
- Network actor whose institutional relationships can affect conflicts around founders and firms.
- Technological optimist who emphasizes opportunity costs from blocking beneficial innovation.
- AI policy advocate who prefers defensive capability and human augmentation to broad precautionary restriction.
- Institutional critic who connects accreditation, regulation, media power, and status defense to incumbent protection.

## Qualifications
Conway's and Conrad's accounts are participant memories, and Conrad's conflict account lacks complete responses from every named party. Andreessen's Huberman interview is a worldview statement rather than a balanced review: it relies on investor observation, broad historical analogy, and contested political claims. The established wiki key spells his first name “Mark,” while the episode metadata uses the public spelling “Marc”; no duplicate entity was created.
