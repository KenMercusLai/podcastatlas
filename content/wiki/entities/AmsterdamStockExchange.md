# Amsterdam Stock Exchange

## Overview

The Amsterdam Stock Exchange appears as the organized market where [[DutchEastIndiaCompany|VOC]] shares became transferable, price-varying assets and where speculation, short selling, trading rules, and manipulation concerns developed together.

## Current Profile

The episode says the exchange was built several years after the VOC's 1602 foundation and became the city's licensed place for share dealing during limited daily hours. Because VOC share prices were not fixed, investors could resell holdings, speculate on price changes, and attempt short sales.

The Isaac Le Maire case supplies the source's regulation story. After leaving the VOC, he sought to profit from a price decline by selling shares he did not own; rumors and panic were then associated with a market fall, and authorities prohibited manipulation. The example shows market innovation and market governance growing together, although the episode's compressed narrative does not establish every legal or chronological detail.

## Key Characteristics

- Organized secondary market associated with transferable VOC shares.
- Licensed trading location with its own rules and restricted trading hours.
- Venue for price discovery, resale, speculation, and early short selling.
- Site where manipulation concerns prompted regulatory response.
- Central institution in the episode's claim that finance became partly detached from individual voyages and physical trade.

## Evidence

- Market formation: [[351-amsterdam-miracles-money-and-mud-part-1-glt4666881212]] places construction of the exchange several years after the VOC's foundation.
- Trading design: [[351-amsterdam-miracles-money-and-mud-part-1-glt4666881212]] describes variable share prices, resale, licensed dealing, and limited hours.
- Short selling and regulation: [[351-amsterdam-miracles-money-and-mud-part-1-glt4666881212]] uses Isaac Le Maire's campaign to connect short selling, rumors, panic, and prohibition of manipulation.

## Qualifications

The source provides a compact narrative rather than legal or market microhistory. Claims that this was the first stock market, or that one episode directly caused a particular regulation, need broader documentary comparison. The exchange's economic importance also cannot be separated from the VOC's colonial trade system.
