## Overview

卫太子刘据 / Liu Ju enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-396-2-hanwudi-zhizi-liuju-wugu-zhi-an-hou-fuhuo-le-lhj6dzjsikkmbbcjlfgnz1bbok-1|Hanji 396-2]] as the dead former crown prince whose name is used by [[ChengFangsuiWesternHan|程方遂]] in a palace-gate imposture under [[EmperorZhaoOfHan|汉昭帝刘弗陵]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-391-1-wei-le-li-taizi-hanwudi-weihe-xian-sha-chongfei-gouyi-furen-lhsva8zj7n7mfy6u4vyfhrwmbwzg|Hanji 391-1]] adds the prior frame-up branch: the host says Liu Ju was destroyed by false accusation involving [[JiangChongWesternHan|江充]], [[SuWenWesternHan|苏文]], and, source-scopedly, [[LadyGouyiWesternHan|钩弋夫人]] as the hidden mover. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-388-2-yin-chanyan-cuosha-taizi-hanwudi-wannian-zuidade-huihen-lm9e_lrbbcc7a6kf22hbid04_u73|Hanji 388-2]] adds the aftermath: [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] comes to see Liu Ju as forced into fearful rebellion by Jiang Chong and mourns him through punishment and memorial architecture. Hanji 387-1 now supplies the flight interval: after [[EmpressWeiZifuWesternHan|卫子夫]]'s suicide, Liu Ju remains unpardoned despite [[LinghuMaoWesternHan|令胡茂]]'s defense, hides in Hu County, and is exposed when a plea for outside help brings troops. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-387-2-hanwudi-yisheng-nanmo-de-wudian-taizi-liuju-zhisi-fqn_yfiyam5durgrlflosljaho1h|Hanji 387-2]] then supplies the death scene and household-destruction layer: Liu Ju hangs himself when surrounded, two sons die with him, and the infant [[EmperorXuanOfHan|刘病已]] survives only through [[BingJiWesternHan|丙吉]]'s protection.

## Current Profile

The sources do not give Liu Ju a full biography. Hanji 391-1 uses him as the victim of a late-Wudi succession frame-up: the host says Han Wudi later knows Liu Ju was wronged and treats Lady Gouyi as the true behind-the-scenes actor, though that judgment remains source-scoped. Hanji 387-1 adds the fugitive interval before the death scene: the court largely understands his grievance but stays silent, Linghu Mao argues that his military action came from fear after Jiang Chong blocked direct appeal, and Han Wudi is moved without issuing a public pardon. Liu Ju's survival therefore remains materially fragile, dependent on poor hosts and on a failed appeal to a wealthy acquaintance. Hanji 387-2 then narrows the death record: after local officials surround him, Liu Ju refuses capture, closes himself in, and hangs himself; Zhang Fuchang breaks the door, Xin'an magistrate Li Shou lowers the body, the householder is killed, and two imperial grandsons die with him. The same source links the wider household destruction to Liu Bingyi's survival, turning Liu Ju's line from almost erased into a future restoration possibility.

Hanji 388-2 makes the remorse concrete: after extended review of the Wugu cases, Wudi understands Liu Ju as driven by fear, accepts Tian Qianqiu's father-son mitigation, and punishes the visible Wugu actors. Hanji 396-2 gives Liu Ju a posthumous political role: because he was Han Wudi's crown prince and was believed dead after the Wugu affair, the claim that he had returned carries enough charge to unsettle officials and draw a large crowd near [[WeiyangPalace|未央宫]]. The case shows that a dead heir can remain politically active as a memory, rumor, and claimant identity even when the living claimant is false.

## Key Characteristics

- Former Western Han crown prince and son of Han Wudi.
- Figure associated with the Wugu affair whose presumed death before Han Zhao's reign is now grounded in fugitive exposure, surrounded-flight suicide, and collateral household killing.
- Source-scoped victim of a frame-up attributed to Jiang Chong, Su Wen, and the host's hidden Lady Gouyi explanation.
- Fugitive whose defense by Linghu Mao moves Han Wudi without producing public pardon.
- Figure whose grievance Hanji 388-2 says Han Wudi later recognizes through Tian Qianqiu's memorial.
- Dynastic-line figure whose destroyed household still leaves Liu Bingyi alive as a future Emperor Xuan branch.
- Posthumous claimant identity whose name can attract public attention and destabilize a young ruler's court when used by Cheng Fangsui.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 396-2, Hanji 391-1, Hanji 388-2, Hanji 387-2, and Hanji 387-1. It does not reconstruct Liu Ju's full life, his mother and household, the complete Wugu affair, the full evidence for his death, or later historiographical debates about his responsibility. Hanji 387-1 supplies a compact flight-and-remonstrance bridge, not the full text of Linghu Mao's memorial or Hu County arrest mechanics. Hanji 387-2 supplies a compact death-scene and household-casualty account, not full biographies of Liu Jin, Wang Wengxu, Li Shou, or Zhang Fuchang. Hanji 391-1's claim about Lady Gouyi as hidden mover is recorded as the episode's interpretation, while Hanji 388-2's remorse account remains a compact episode-level reading of the aftermath.
