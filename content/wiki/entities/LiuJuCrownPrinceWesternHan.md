## Current Profile

The sources do not give Liu Ju a full biography. Hanji 384-1 uses him as a still-standing but weakening heir whose support network is being cut: Gongsun He is a chancellor-level Wei-family in-law, while Wei Kang inherits Wei Qing's prestige and is treated as a military-capable backing figure. Hanji 385-2 then turns that weakened position into a no-clear-exit palace trap. Jiang Chong's alleged evidence appears inside Liu Ju's own residence while Wudi is ill at Ganquan and communication is blocked. Shi De urges Liu Ju to arrest Jiang Chong's party first and compare the situation to Fusu's death, but Liu Ju initially hesitates because forged commands and killing ministers would themselves look disloyal. Hanji 391-1 later uses Liu Ju as the victim of a late-Wudi succession frame-up: the host says Han Wudi later knows Liu Ju was wronged and treats Lady Gouyi as the true behind-the-scenes actor, though that judgment remains source-scoped.

Hanji 386-2 adds the military prelude to his fugitive status. Liu Ju does not merely "rebel" in a vacuum; the episode presents him as acting under disputed command information, trying to prove or produce imperial authorization through edicts, prisoner releases, tallies, Hu cavalry, the North Army, and armed city residents. His failure is therefore both military and symbolic: Ren An's non-response keeps the North Army out, the rumor that the crown prince is rebelling drains followers, and Wudi's side monopolizes the authoritative command frame.

Hanji 387-1 adds the fugitive interval before the death scene: the court largely understands his grievance but stays silent, Linghu Mao argues that his military action came from fear after Jiang Chong blocked direct appeal, and Han Wudi is moved without issuing a public pardon. Liu Ju's survival therefore remains materially fragile, dependent on poor hosts and on a failed appeal to a wealthy acquaintance. Hanji 387-2 then narrows the death record: after local officials surround him, Liu Ju refuses capture, closes himself in, and hangs himself; Zhang Fuchang breaks the door, Xin'an magistrate Li Shou lowers the body, the householder is killed, and two imperial grandsons die with him. The same source links the wider household destruction to Liu Bingyi's survival, turning Liu Ju's line from almost erased into a future restoration possibility.

Hanji 388-2 makes the remorse concrete: after extended review of the Wugu cases, Wudi understands Liu Ju as driven by fear, accepts Tian Qianqiu's father-son mitigation, and punishes the visible Wugu actors. Hanji 396-2 gives Liu Ju a posthumous political role: because he was Han Wudi's crown prince and was believed dead after the Wugu affair, the claim that he had returned carries enough charge to unsettle officials and draw a large crowd near [[WeiyangPalace|未央宫]]. The case shows that a dead heir can remain politically active as a memory, rumor, and claimant identity even when the living claimant is false.

## Key Characteristics

- Former Western Han crown prince and son of Han Wudi whose Wugu-linked destruction is now grounded in support-network loss, command collapse, fugitive exposure, surrounded-flight suicide, and collateral household killing.
- Source-scoped victim of a frame-up attributed to Jiang Chong, Su Wen, and the host's hidden Lady Gouyi explanation, with Hanji 385-2 adding the palace-search evidence trap and Shi De's Fusu warning.
- Isolated heir whose chancellor-level and Wei-family military support is weakened before he becomes a failed military claimant to imperial command.
- Fugitive whose defense by Linghu Mao moves Han Wudi without producing public pardon.
- Figure whose grievance Hanji 388-2 says Han Wudi later recognizes through Tian Qianqiu's memorial.
- Dynastic-line figure whose destroyed household still leaves Liu Bingyi alive as a future Emperor Xuan branch.
- Posthumous claimant identity whose name can attract public attention and destabilize a young ruler's court when used by Cheng Fangsui.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 396-2, Hanji 391-1, Hanji 388-2, Hanji 387-2, Hanji 387-1, Hanji 386-2, Hanji 384-1, and Hanji 385-2. It does not reconstruct Liu Ju's full life, his mother and household, the complete Wugu affair, the full evidence for his death, or later historiographical debates about his responsibility. Hanji 384-1 supplies a compact support-network account, not the full legal record for Gongsun He, Wei Kang, the princesses, or Du Zhou. Hanji 385-2 supplies a compact palace-search and tutor-advice account, not the full primary-source wording, full biographies of Shi De or Jiang Chong, or a settled legal judgment about every item found in Liu Ju's palace. Hanji 386-2 supplies a compact military-collapse account, not the full command structure, troop lists, legal procedure, or biographies of Ren An, Tian Ren, Bao Shengzhi, Liu Quli, Ma Tong, or Shangqiu Cheng. Hanji 387-1 supplies a compact flight-and-remonstrance bridge, not the full text of Linghu Mao's memorial or Hu County arrest mechanics. Hanji 387-2 supplies a compact death-scene and household-casualty account, not full biographies of Liu Jin, Wang Wengxu, Li Shou, or Zhang Fuchang. Hanji 391-1's claim about Lady Gouyi as hidden mover is recorded as the episode's interpretation, while Hanji 388-2's remorse account remains a compact episode-level reading of the aftermath.
