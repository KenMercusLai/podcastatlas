# Donald Bradman

## Overview

Donald Bradman was an Australian batsman whose exceptional scoring and symbolic national importance made him the strategic target and political centre of the 1932-33 Bodyline series.

## Current Profile

The episode presents Bradman as cricket's greatest statistical outlier and as a problem England believed ordinary tactics could not solve. Douglas Jardine's response—fast leg-theory bowling directed toward the batter's body with close fielders—turned the attempt to contain one player into an imperial controversy.

Bradman's profile is therefore not only statistical. In an Ashes culture where Australia asserted itself by defeating England at an English game, attacking his dominance appeared to test the proclaimed ethics of that shared game. The political force came from the combination of bodily danger, national pride, newsreel publicity, and the symbolic status cricket had acquired within the empire.

## Key Characteristics

- Exceptional Australian batsman described as cricket's largest statistical outlier.
- Strategic problem around whom England built the Bodyline tactic.
- National figure whose treatment intensified Australian anger toward England.
- Symbol of Australian excellence within an inherited British game.
