# Tomorrow Today

## Overview
Tomorrow Today is a podcast hosted in the current sources by [[ShekharNatarajan]]. Its represented episodes pair long-form biography with questions about business identity, resilience, spirituality, survival, public institutions, creative careers, elite sport, inequality, technology, leadership, and human meaning.

## Current Profile
The wiki knows Tomorrow Today through eight interviews. [[AndyStalman]] connects branding to whole-company identity, values, and AI. [[AraceliSegarra]] connects climbing and Everest to fear, team trust, resilience, and AI-era judgment. [[ShyalpaRinpoche]] connects Tibetan Buddhist practice to peace, meaning, and compassionate AI. [[NataliaOlson]] connects planning, logistics, government, and open data to institutional innovation and AI governance. [[AdahSharma]] connects acting, typecasting, controversial visibility, stress, and rejection to AI's expansion of creative access and threat to livelihoods. [[ChetanBhagat]] connects family adversity, education, writing, platform inequality, AI safety, cognition, and the search for meaning after fame. [[AlvaroVizcaino|Álvaro Vizcaíno]] distinguishes accepting uncontrollable conditions from giving up action. [[AndreaFuentes]] adds Olympic artistic swimming, athlete-centered coaching, team transparency, retirement, family loss, and high-intensity breath-hold safety.

## Key Characteristics
- Identified in source metadata as Tomorrow, Today and hosted by [[ShekharNatarajan]].
- Uses biography to move from personal formation into practical, social, and institutional questions.
- Connects leadership and technology to values, trust, judgment, inequality, and purpose.
- Returns to AI as a human, moral, cognitive, labor, and governance problem rather than only a technical tool.
- Moves across business, extreme environments, elite sport, spirituality, logistics, government, and creative labor without abandoning implementation detail.
- Uses guest-specific expertise and experience to test broad claims against concrete mechanisms and tradeoffs.
- Preserves plural interpretations when guests connect practical events to spiritual or existential meaning.

## Qualifications
Only eight Tomorrow Today sources are represented. The page describes an observed editorial pattern rather than the show's complete archive, production history, official mission, or the independent accuracy of every guest claim. The survival and artistic-swimming episodes rely heavily on first-person testimony; they do not independently establish accident mechanics, organizational history, medical causation, or the causal effect of coaching philosophy on results.
