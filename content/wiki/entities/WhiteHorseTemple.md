## Overview

白马寺 / White Horse Temple enters the wiki through Hanji 737 as the Luoyang Buddhist institution attached to the traditional Mingdi dream-and-envoy narrative. The page keeps the claim source-bounded: the episode presents Mingdi's dream of a golden figure, envoys to Tianzhu, returning scriptures and monks, and the building of White Horse Temple as the court-transmission story for early Chinese Buddhism.

## Current Profile

Hanji 737 mentions White Horse Temple after discussing [[LiuYingChuKingEasternHan|楚王刘英]] as an early royal practitioner who revered Huang-Lao and worshiped Buddha. The episode then places a more formal court-transmission story under [[EmperorMingOfHan|汉明帝]]: the emperor dreams of a golden person, asks about it, sends envoys west, receives Buddhist scriptures and monks, and builds White Horse Temple in Luoyang.

The page therefore treats White Horse Temple as a memory node for early Buddhist reception at court, not as proof that Buddhism begins only at that moment. The same episode notes an earlier Ai-era contact through a Great Yuezhi envoy, so the temple is better read as a formalizing institution inside the Han court narrative.

## Key Characteristics

- Luoyang Buddhist institution tied in the source to Mingdi's dream-and-envoy story.
- Appears after Liu Ying's Buddhist/Huang-Lao practice, marking a move from royal private practice to court-sponsored transmission.
- Linked to returned scriptures and monks from Tianzhu in the episode's account.
- Functions as a formal memory node for Buddhism's early Chinese court reception.
- Source-bounded and not used here to settle the broader historical chronology of Buddhist arrival in China.

## Qualifications

The page does not adjudicate modern scholarly debates about the earliest Buddhist transmission to China or the historicity of the Mingdi dream. It records the episode's traditional court narrative and preserves the earlier-contact qualification contained in the same source.

## What Changed

- Framed the temple as a formal court-memory node rather than the only possible Buddhist entry point.
