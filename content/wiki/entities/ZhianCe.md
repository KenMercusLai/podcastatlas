## Overview

《治安策》 / Zhian Ce is a political memorial by [[JiaYi|贾谊]] that enters the wiki through one [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode. The current evidence presents it through two linked governance problems: preserving the dignity and self-command of senior officials while punishing wrongdoing, and reducing the concentrated power of large royal kingdoms through grants to additional sons.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-219-jiayi-de-zhian-ce-cangzhe-guojia-xingshuai-de-mimi-2-lhdbii3duf3kgpwwpmilvuogyjvw|Hanji 219]] reads “可杀不可辱” as an institutional claim. Ministers close to the ruler may be removed or killed, but public mutilation, binding, beating, and abuse by low prison officers can damage the dignity of the offices and sovereign order they represent. Ritual restraint is meant to cultivate shame, self-respect, and officials capable of principled service.

The source also gives the doctrine a coercive boundary. If accusation requires a minister to preserve imperial dignity by committing suicide, the same honor system can suppress defense and punish insistence on clarification. On territorial policy, the episode attributes to Jia Yi a proposal to grant a prince's sons their own territories, weakening the parent kingdom without presenting the measure only as confiscation. This is an intellectual precursor to later fragmentation policy, not the same enactment as [[TuiEnLing|推恩令]].

## Key Characteristics

- Political memorial joining ritual hierarchy to the practical handling of senior officials.
- Argument that the form of punishment can protect or degrade the institutions surrounding the ruler.
- Shame-and-honor model of official self-command rather than reliance on external coercion alone.
- Doctrine with a coercive downside when dignified death replaces defense or due process.
- Early proposal to reduce concentrated royal power through grants to additional sons.

## Qualifications

The current page rests on one compact popular-history episode, not a complete reading, textual history, or edition comparison of the memorial. Ancient quotations, ritual and penal procedure, later effects, and the relationship between Jia Yi's proposal and subsequent policy require primary-text comparison. The source's claim that delayed adoption contributed to the Seven States rebellion is a bounded causal interpretation, not proof that prompt adoption would have prevented it. Praise attributed to [[ZengGuofan|曾国藩]] and [[MaoZedong|毛泽东]] records later reception but does not independently validate every policy claim.

## What Changed

- Created the page from Hanji 219's official-dignity, coercive-boundary, and kingdom-fragmentation evidence.
- Distinguished Jia Yi's early partition proposal from the later formal 推恩令.
