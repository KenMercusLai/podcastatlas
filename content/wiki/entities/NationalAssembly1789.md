# National Assembly of 1789

## Overview

The National Assembly of 1789 was the body declared by Third Estate deputies on 17 June that claimed to represent the French nation and to possess authority over taxation and constitution-making.

## Current Profile

The Assembly emerged from the [[EstatesGeneral1789|Estates-General]] verification deadlock after [[EmmanuelJosephSieyes|Sieyes]] urged unilateral action and parish priests began joining the Third Estate. Its declaration shifted legitimacy from corporate orders toward the nation. The [[TennisCourtOath|Tennis Court Oath]] then bound deputies not to separate before producing a constitution, while resistance after the royal session of 23 June showed that the claim could survive a direct royal command.

## Key Characteristics

- Declared on 17 June 1789 by deputies of the Third Estate and allies.
- Claimed representation of the nation rather than one corporate order.
- Declared taxation unauthorized without national consent while temporarily maintaining existing collection.
- Converted procedural stalemate into constituent authority.
- Sustained collective resistance through oath, publicity, and refusal to disperse.
- Absorbed clergy and nobility after Louis XVI's 27 June order.

## Qualifications

- Claiming to represent the nation did not make the Assembly socially inclusive or settle the later boundaries of citizenship.
- The source does not provide a complete constitutional or procedural history of the Assembly.

## What Changed

- Created the profile to mark 17 June as an institutional transfer of sovereign authority, not merely a renamed meeting.

## Relationships

- [[EstatesGeneral1789]] - corporate assembly from whose deadlock it emerged.
- [[EmmanuelJosephSieyes]] - principal theorist and strategist of its declaration.
- [[HonoreGabrielRiquetiDeMirabeau]] - deputy who dramatized its refusal to disperse.
- [[TennisCourtOath]] - collective pledge that defined its constituent mission.
- [[LouisXVI]] - monarch who first rejected and then conceded its united form.
