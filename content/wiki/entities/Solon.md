# Solon

## Overview

Solon is the Athenian lawgiver represented as a constitutional mediator who limits exploitation, makes law more public, and widens participation without ending elite rule or resolving factional conflict.

## Current Profile

The sources place Solon in an aristocrat-dominated Athens divided between rich and poor and humiliated in conflict with Megara over Salamis. As archon in 594 BC, he strengthens ordinary Athenians' legal position, reduces exploitation, and makes written law more equal while preserving wealth distinctions and elite access to the highest offices.

The combined evidence adds a Council of Four Hundred, broader participation by property-holding commoners, and roles for lower groups in the assembly and juries. This remains a compromise: both aristocrats and commoners are dissatisfied, and Solon's effort to freeze the settlement for ten years does not prevent the later rise of [[Peisistratus]]. His importance lies in creating legal and participatory infrastructure that later reform can build on, not in founding full democracy.

## Key Characteristics

- Responds to debt, exploitation, faction, and military humiliation through constitutional mediation.
- Makes law more public and ordinary Athenians less vulnerable to exclusive elite judgment.
- Widens councils, assemblies, and juries while preserving graded political privilege.
- Produces a mixed settlement that neither side treats as final.
- Serves as an incomplete institutional precursor to tyranny and Cleisthenic democracy.

## Qualifications

The sources give a compressed constitutional account rather than a full reconstruction of Solon's laws, poetry, debt measures, property classes, travel traditions, or later legendary reception. The institutional details and claim that he required ten years without revision remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Added the Council of Four Hundred, assembly participation, and jury service to the settlement.
- Clarified that broader participation remained structured by property and elite officeholding.
- Emphasized the settlement's inability to end faction or prevent tyranny.

## Relationships

- [[Athens]] - city whose social and political crisis Solon mediates.
- [[AthenianDemocracy]] - later order for which his settlement provides incomplete infrastructure.
- [[InstitutionalReformAsPowerReallocation]] - broader frame for changes to law and participation.
- [[Cleisthenes]] - later reformer who moves beyond Solon's elite-popular balance.
- [[Peisistratus]] - tyrant arising after the settlement fails to end factional conflict.
