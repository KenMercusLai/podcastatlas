## Overview

Sean Scanlon is the Connecticut state comptroller who sponsored the [[ConnecticutCitizensAssembly]] as an attempt to break a decades-long property-tax reform stalemate.

## Current Profile

Scanlon framed local property-tax dependence as a roughly 70-year problem that elected officials had repeatedly studied without resolving. After the assembly voted, he initially sought to delay publication until after an election because he feared the proposals would be ignored, denied credit, or used as partisan weapons. He withdrew that objection after an emergency meeting and said he would support the recommendations in the legislature.

## Key Characteristics

- He used an alternative democratic process to address a politically risky fiscal problem.
- He linked high household property-tax burdens to municipal service and school-finance dependence.
- He initially prioritized electoral timing and political reception over the promised publication schedule.
- He reversed his objection after organizers and citizens challenged the delay.
- He supported proposals institutionally while remaining absent from the online release event.
