# Philip II of Macedon

## Overview

Philip II of Macedon was the king who consolidated Macedon, built the military and financial system inherited by [[AlexanderTheGreat|Alexander the Great]], defeated major Greek resistance at Chaeronea, and prepared an invasion of the [[AchaemenidEmpire]] before his assassination.

## Current Profile

[[116-alexander-the-great-part-1-glt3321594885]] treats Philip as the institutional precondition for Alexander's conquest. Gold revenue, political consolidation, the sarissa phalanx, lighter and more flexible infantry, elite shield-bearers, and companion cavalry gave Macedon a combined military system rather than a single wonder weapon. Victory over Athens and Thebes at Chaeronea then made Philip leader of a Greek coalition, with Sparta outside it, and let him present Persian war as both pan-Hellenic revenge and Macedonian expansion.

Philip's multiple marriages, conflict around [[Olympias]], and the uncertain succession setting made the court politically dangerous. His assassination by the bodyguard Pausanias at a wedding benefited Alexander, Olympias, and Persia in different possible reconstructions, but the episode does not establish an instigator. Alexander inherited the invasion project and war machine; he did not simply create them at accession.

Renault's literary-family layer in [[FireFromHeaven|《天堂之火》]] presents Philip as a culturally ambitious northern king who admires southern Greek refinement, arranges Alexander's education, and turns marriage and heir-making into direct threats to his son's position. The episode's language of parental injury and emotional conflict is a reading of the novel, not recovered private testimony.

## Key Characteristics

- Consolidated a previously divided Macedonian kingdom and expanded its fiscal base.
- Built an integrated army around pike infantry, flexible supporting troops, elite guards, and companion cavalry.
- Defeated Athens and Thebes at Chaeronea and converted military supremacy into leadership of most Greek states.
- Framed invasion of Persia through both pan-Hellenic revenge and Macedonian ambition.
- Left a volatile dynastic court and an unresolved assassination whose beneficiaries are not proof of responsibility.
- Combined Greek cultural aspiration and royal education with marriages and succession choices that made household conflict politically consequential.

## Evidence

- State and army formation: [[116-alexander-the-great-part-1-glt3321594885]] connects clan consolidation, gold revenue, the sarissa, lighter equipment, supporting infantry, and cavalry.
- Greek hegemony: [[116-alexander-the-great-part-1-glt3321594885]] links Chaeronea to the defeat of major southern opposition and Philip's leadership of the Persian expedition.
- Dynastic succession: [[116-alexander-the-great-part-1-glt3321594885]] presents multiple marriages, Olympias, Alexander's uncertain position, and Pausanias's attack as the setting for accession.
- Literary family and education: [[15-yalishanda-dadi-de-zaogao-yuansheng-jiating-tiantang-zhi-huo-lr1agsbqonwmohmqp_fngt0t8soq]] reads Philip's cultural aspiration, remarriage, possible replacement heir, and relationship with Alexander through Renault's novel.

## Qualifications

This profile is grounded in one conversational popular-history episode and one literary discussion rather than a full reconstruction of Macedonian institutions. The relative novelty of individual reforms, Philip's motives, the balance of coercion and alliance in Greece, cultural insecurity, family dialogue, parenting psychology, the oracle reported before his death, and responsibility for the assassination remain source-scoped, fictionalized, or unresolved.

## What Changed

- Added Philip's cultural aspiration, royal-education role, and heir-making decisions as a literary formation context for Alexander.
- Preserved the boundary between Renault's family psychology and independently supported biography.

## Relationships

- [[AlexanderTheGreat]] - son and successor who inherited Philip's army and Persian invasion project.
- [[Olympias]] - wife and mother of Alexander within the contested dynastic court.
- [[AchaemenidEmpire]] - intended target of the expedition Philip prepared.
- [[Aristotle]] - tutor Philip appointed for Alexander in the episode's account.
- [[AlexanderImperialSuccessionStrategy]] - later governing project made possible by Philip's initial conquest platform.
