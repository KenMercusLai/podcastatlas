## Overview

黄允 / Huang Yun is a late-Han talent-and-reputation warning case in the 164 CE source layer. Guo Tai first recognizes his high ability while making fame conditional on later self-correction; the following source then shows Huang Yun's reputation collapsing when private conduct cannot sustain public praise.

## Current Profile

The current evidence depends on the two-source sequence. Hanji 861 gives the forecast: [[GuoTaiLateHan|郭泰]] sees Huang Yun as unusually talented and potentially prominent after forty, but the praise is not unconditional. Guo Tai warns that the decisive period will require strict self-correction and self-holding.

Hanji 862 supplies the negative fulfillment of that warning. [[YuanWeiLateHan|袁隗]] praises Huang Yun as a desirable son-in-law, Huang Yun treats the remark as an actionable marriage opportunity, discards his existing wife, and is publicly exposed by her before assembled relatives. The same source groups him with [[JinWenjingLateHan|晋文经]] as a figure whose fame influences official selection opinion despite weak evidence of conduct.

Huang Yun therefore functions less as a great official than as a reputation diagnostic. Talent and elite praise can open doors, but if recognition outruns conduct, public exposure turns social capital into liability. His case also qualifies simple [[LateBloomingTalent|大器晚成式人才显现]] optimism: late prominence requires character able to bear it.

## Key Characteristics

- Talented late-Han man whose potential is recognized but explicitly conditioned by Guo Tai.
- Reputation-risk case where future fame depends on self-correction.
- Marriage-opportunity opportunist who discards his wife after Yuan Wei's praise.
- Publicly exposed figure whose wife turns private hidden misconduct into communal knowledge.
- Paired with Jin Wenjing as an example of fame influencing office selection before conduct is proven.
- Source case for [[EmptyReputationRealityGap|空誉违实]].
