## Overview

黄允 / Huang Yun is a late-Han reputation-collapse case from the 164 CE Huan-era source layer. He is presented as a talented Jiyin man praised and warned by [[GuoTaiLateHan|郭泰]], then ruined when ambition for a Yuan-family marriage exposes the gap between public name and private conduct.

## Current Profile

The source makes Huang Yun's story depend on sequence. Guo Tai first recognizes his talent and says his reputation may become prominent after forty, but the praise is already paired with a warning: if Huang Yun does not correct and hold himself strictly, the same fame will be lost.

That warning is vindicated when [[YuanWeiLateHan|袁隗]] praises Huang Yun as a desirable son-in-law. Huang Yun treats the remark as an actionable marriage opportunity, discards his existing wife, and is publicly exposed by her before assembled relatives. The episode then groups him with [[JinWenjingLateHan|晋文经]] as a figure whose fame begins to shape official selection opinion despite weak evidence of conduct.

Huang Yun therefore functions less as a great official than as a reputation diagnostic: talent and elite praise can open doors, but if reputation outruns conduct, public exposure turns social capital into liability.

## Key Characteristics

- Talented late-Han man whose fame is certified but also qualified by Guo Tai.
- Reputation-risk case where private conduct cannot sustain public image.
- Marriage-opportunity opportunist who discards his wife after Yuan Wei's praise.
- Publicly exposed figure whose wife turns private hidden misconduct into communal knowledge.
- Paired with Jin Wenjing as an example of fame influencing office selection before conduct is proven.
- Source case for [[EmptyReputationRealityGap|空誉违实]].
