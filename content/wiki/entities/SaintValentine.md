# Saint Valentine

## Overview

Saint Valentine is the uncertain martyr figure, or cluster of figures, whose February feast supplied the name later attached to [[ValentinesDayHistoricalFormation|Valentine's Day]].

## Current Profile

[[151-valentines-day-glt1795443565]] distinguishes several traditions: a Valentine associated with Rome and the Via Flaminia, a Bishop of Terni who may have been the same person, and a Bishop of Genoa commemorated on 2 May. Later accounts place a Valentine under Claudius Gothicus and add conversion, healing, imprisonment, and execution stories, but the episode emphasizes that the earliest narrative layer contains no secure romantic or erotic connection.

The saint therefore explains the feast's name more securely than its modern meaning. The romantic association appears later through medieval literature, spring and bird-mating imagery, courtly convention, and evolving social practice rather than through a recoverable act by one historical Valentine.

## Key Characteristics

- Name shared by multiple saint traditions whose identities may have merged.
- Associated in later tradition with martyrdom under Claudius Gothicus.
- Earliest narrative layer lacks a clear romance or courtship theme.
- Feast name later reused by medieval literary and social customs.

## Evidence

- **Uncertain identity:** [[151-valentines-day-glt1795443565]] distinguishes the Roman, Terni, and Genoese Valentine traditions while noting possible conflation.
- **Legendary accretion:** [[151-valentines-day-glt1795443565]] places conversion, healing, imprisonment, and violent death in later narrative development.
- **Romantic disconnect:** [[151-valentines-day-glt1795443565]] finds no secure erotic content in the earliest martyr account and locates the romantic bridge in later medieval culture.

## Qualifications

This profile rests on one popular-history episode rather than specialist hagiographic analysis. The saint's historicity, number, offices, dates, relics, martyrdom circumstances, and relationship among Roman, Terni, and Genoese traditions remain uncertain. Later legends should not be treated as contemporary evidence.

## What Changed

- Established the saint as an uncertain namesake rather than a demonstrated founder of the romantic holiday.
