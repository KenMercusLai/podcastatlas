# Saint Valentine

## Overview

Saint Valentine is the uncertain martyr figure, or cluster of figures, whose February feast supplied the name later attached to [[ValentinesDayHistoricalFormation|Valentine's Day]].

## Current Profile

[[151-valentines-day-glt1795443565]] distinguishes several traditions: a Valentine associated with Rome and the Via Flaminia, a Bishop of Terni who may have been the same person, and a Bishop of Genoa commemorated on 2 May. [[10-bei-yange-de-airen-niulang-zhinv-qingrenjie-teji-lvnjfdt54qvkejizu1wxyju2uc2p|Weird History episode 10]] likewise treats the identity commemorated on 14 February as uncertain and presents the early martyr layer as faith, execution, and healing rather than romance.

The saint therefore explains the feast's name more securely than its modern meaning. Secret military weddings, heart-shaped parchment, an amethyst ring, Cupid imagery, an almond tree, Julia, and a farewell signed “your Valentine” belong to later legendary accretion. The romantic holiday is better explained through those retrospective stories plus medieval literature, spring and bird-mating imagery, courtly convention, and evolving social practice than through one recoverable act by a historical Valentine.

## Key Characteristics

- Name shared by multiple saint traditions whose identities may have merged.
- Associated in later tradition with martyrdom under Claudius Gothicus.
- Earliest narrative layer lacks a clear romance or courtship theme.
- Later legends retrofit weddings, hearts, jewels, letters, and botanical symbols onto the martyr.
- Feast name later reused by medieval literary and social customs.

## Evidence

- **Uncertain identity:** [[151-valentines-day-glt1795443565]] distinguishes the Roman, Terni, and Genoese Valentine traditions while noting possible conflation.
- **Legendary accretion:** [[151-valentines-day-glt1795443565]] places conversion, healing, imprisonment, and violent death in later narrative development.
- **Romantic disconnect:** [[151-valentines-day-glt1795443565]] finds no secure erotic content in the earliest martyr account and locates the romantic bridge in later medieval culture.
- **Later romantic retrofit:** [[10-bei-yange-de-airen-niulang-zhinv-qingrenjie-teji-lvnjfdt54qvkejizu1wxyju2uc2p]] distinguishes the early faith-and-miracle narrative from later secret-wedding, heart, ring, almond-tree, and farewell-letter traditions.

## Qualifications

This profile rests on two popular-history episodes rather than specialist hagiographic analysis. The saint's historicity, number, offices, dates, relics, martyrdom circumstances, and relationship among Roman, Terni, and Genoese traditions remain uncertain. Later legends should not be treated as contemporary evidence, and their increasingly romantic detail is evidence of reception more securely than biography.

## What Changed

- Established the saint as an uncertain namesake rather than a demonstrated founder of the romantic holiday.
- Distinguished specific romantic symbols as later legendary additions rather than early martyr evidence.
