# Kate Porterfield

## Overview

Kate Porterfield is the psychologist in the final episode of [[TheAlabamaMurders]] who assessed [[KennethSmith]] after Alabama's failed attempt to execute him and remained in contact before his second warrant was carried out.

## Current Profile

Porterfield's role joins clinical assessment to the series' moral frame. She attributes Smith's nightmares, depression, nausea, and severe post-traumatic stress to the first execution attempt rather than a prior condition, and argues that isolation, masking, the reused chamber, and anticipation of another execution would intensify that injury. Her closing letter and candle vigil also move the episode from protocol dispute toward relationship, grief, and the person obscured by institutional language.

## Key Characteristics

- Assessed Smith for more than a year after the failed lethal-injection attempt.
- Attributed his severe post-traumatic stress to state-inflicted execution trauma.
- Warned that a second warrant and reused execution setting would aggravate symptoms.
- Linked stress, nausea, and prior vomiting to a practical mask-safety concern.
- Remembered Smith through his relationships and irrepressible spirit after legal options ended.
