# Elissa Epel

## Overview
Elissa Epel is the [[UniversityOfCaliforniaSanFrancisco|UCSF]] stress, aging, metabolism, and emotion researcher featured in the [[control-stress-for-healthy-eating-metabolism-aging-dr-elissa-epel-scim4455888803]] Huberman Lab source note.

## Current Profile
The current wiki evidence uses Epel as a guest expert on how stress becomes biologically and behaviorally consequential. Her profile centers on stress appraisal, rumination, body-based regulation, telomeres, mitochondria, stress eating, pregnancy interventions, caregiving, meditation, radical acceptance, uncertainty tolerance, narrative meaning, and controlled positive stress.

The page stays source-bounded. It does not attempt a complete biography or publication review, and it preserves the episode's open boundaries around meditation biomarkers, Wim Hof physiology, pregnancy outcomes, obesity medications, and causal claims about biological aging.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames stress as a response that depends on perceived demands, available resources, duration, interpretation, and recovery.
- Connects psychological stress to aging biology through telomeres, telomerase, mitochondria, inflammation, and caregiving evidence.
- Treats eating behavior as partly shaped by stress, reward sensitivity, insulin resistance, cravings, and food environments.
- Combines cognitive reappraisal, body-based regulation, environmental cue control, acceptance, and uncertainty tolerance as stress tools.
- Keeps preliminary biomarker and intervention findings qualified rather than treating them as settled clinical protocols.
