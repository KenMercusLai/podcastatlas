## Overview

梁武帝萧衍 / Xiao Yan, Emperor Wu of Liang appears in the current wiki as a comparative ruler used by [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-365-1-jiemi-gudai-diwang-de-yule-jiemu-lpmbd0xutravghvymsw2tsq8ooif|Hanji 365-1]] to connect long life with learning, music, dietary simplicity, Buddhist patronage, and sexual restraint. The profile records that interpretive package without treating correlation as a medically established longevity program.

## Current Profile

The episode contrasts Xiao Yan with [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]. Where Han Wudi is framed through strong outward appetite for expansion, spectacle, and intimacy, Xiao Yan is framed through reduced consumption: reading, music, plain food, no alcohol, vegetarian banquets, occasional one-meal days, and reduced sexual activity later in life. The contrast is a narrative device, not a controlled comparison between rulers.

Religion and political resources are also joined in the profile. The source calls Xiao Yan the “monk emperor” or “bodhisattva emperor,” says he entered monasteries four times, and describes ministers donating money to bring him back to court. It also recounts the traditional exchange in which Bodhidharma denies that temple building and support for monks necessarily produce merit. These details make Buddhist patronage both a devotional practice and a movement of court wealth, while their chronology and interpretation remain source-scoped.

## Key Characteristics

- Liang ruler presented by the episode as living to eighty-six and ranking among the longest-lived Chinese emperors.
- Learned and music-oriented figure whose intellectual habits form part of the source's restraint-centered portrait.
- Associated with simple food, vegetarian court banquets, abstention from alcohol, and occasional one-meal days.
- Major Buddhist patron remembered through repeated monastery entry, donations, temple construction, and support for monastics.
- Later-life sexual restraint used by the host as a proposed longevity explanation.
- Comparative counterpoint to Han Wudi's appetite-centered imperial image.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to one popular-history podcast summary rather than a complete biography or specialist study of Xiao Yan, Southern Dynasties Buddhism, nutrition, sexuality, or aging. The stated age ranking, frequency and mechanics of monastery entry, Bodhidharma dialogue, timing of sexual abstinence, meal frequency, and dietary details remain episode-attributed. Longevity is multifactorial, and the source does not demonstrate that fasting, vegetarianism, abstention from alcohol, or reduced sexual activity caused Xiao Yan's lifespan. Its language about autophagy and conserving essence should not be treated as medical advice.

## What Changed

- Created a bounded profile that separates Xiao Yan's reported habits from the episode's causal longevity claims.
- Preserved Buddhist patronage as both devotional practice and court-resource movement.
- Made the Han Wudi contrast explicit as a narrative frame rather than a controlled historical comparison.
