# Peter Turchin

## Overview

Peter Turchin is a scholar invoked in the sources for [[EliteOverproduction|elite overproduction]] and for the ambition to identify predictive patterns in human societies.

## Current Profile

The current sources present two related but distinct uses of Turchin's work. Elite overproduction interprets a recurring selection problem: education and credential seeking can create more people expecting elite status than the available offices can absorb. Chinese civil-service examinations provide the historical illustration, with candidate numbers growing faster than official posts.

A second reception invokes Turchin as someone applying scientific methods to human societies and credits him with anticipating a troubled 2020, but uses the example mainly to debate whether historical patterns can support prediction. The source does not inspect the forecast or his models, so it broadens the profile without validating predictive success.

## Key Characteristics

- Associated in the source with a structural account of elite competition.
- Treats educated aspiration and scarce positions as a potential source of instability.
- Used comparatively rather than as a complete explanation of Chinese history.
- Invoked in a second source as an example of scientific and predictive ambition in historical social analysis.

## Evidence

- Concept attribution: [[84-exams-glt6507641896]] attributes “elite overproduction” to Turchin.
- Historical application: [[84-exams-glt6507641896]] applies the idea to the gap between Chinese examination candidates and civil-service posts.
- Predictive reception: [[7-the-lessons-of-history-glt2771707131]] attributes a warning about 2020 to Turchin while using it to frame skepticism about forecasting human futures.

## Qualifications

The sources do not survey Turchin's wider scholarship, model specification, datasets, forecast wording, evidence, critics, or other applications. The reported 2020 prediction is not independently audited here, and invocation of scientific method does not establish reliable future prediction.
