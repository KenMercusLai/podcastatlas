# Bartolomeu Dias

## Overview

Bartolomeu Dias was the Portuguese navigator whose 1487-1488 expedition rounded southern Africa and demonstrated that the Atlantic route continued into the Indian Ocean.

## Current Profile

The source presents Dias's achievement as a product of accumulated Portuguese route knowledge, adaptive sailing, and severe uncertainty. Driven west by adverse winds, his expedition passed the Cape without initially seeing it, then made landfall on South Africa's Eastern Cape before returning to Lisbon after sixteen months.

The voyage validated [[JoaoIIOfPortugal|João II's]] African strategy and prepared the route later followed by [[VascoDaGama]]. Yet the source refuses a clean heroic story: Dias's killing of a local person at Mussel Bay becomes an early sign that [[PortugueseMaritimeExpansion|maritime expansion]] would join navigation to violence.

## Key Characteristics

- Portuguese navigator operating under João II's African-route program.
- Commander who used a westward Atlantic swing to overcome adverse coastal winds.
- First European navigator in the source to demonstrate that southern Africa could be rounded by sea.
- Route pioneer whose return made a voyage to India strategically credible.
- Exploration figure whose Mussel Bay encounter exposes early coercive violence.

## Evidence

- Expedition and route: [[228-portugal-the-golden-age-of-discovery-part-2-glt7949934800]] says Dias departed in 1487, sailed west under wind pressure, rounded the Cape, and reached the Eastern Cape.
- Strategic proof: [[228-portugal-the-golden-age-of-discovery-part-2-glt7949934800]] treats his December 1488 return as evidence that the route around Africa existed.
- Violent encounter: [[228-portugal-the-golden-age-of-discovery-part-2-glt7949934800]] says Dias killed a local person with a crossbow during resistance at Mussel Bay.

## Qualifications

The episode does not supply a comprehensive biography or an African account of the encounter. Precise route reconstruction, crew experience, local identity, and the circumstances of the Mussel Bay killing remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Established Dias as both the Cape-route validator and an early case of violence within Portuguese expansion.

## Relationships

- [[JoaoIIOfPortugal]] - monarch whose African-route strategy Dias validated.
- [[VascoDaGama]] - later navigator who used the demonstrated route to reach India.
- [[PortugueseMaritimeExpansion]] - expansion process advanced by Dias's voyage.
- [[OceanicVoyageAttrition]] - risk environment in which the Cape voyage operated.
