# Casey Means

## Overview
Casey Means is presented as a Stanford-trained physician and metabolic-health educator who connects mitochondrial energy production, glucose regulation, chronic inflammation, lifestyle inputs, and self-observation.

## Current Profile
In the current source, Means uses a systems-level metabolic frame to connect conditions usually separated by medical specialty. Her practical emphasis is hierarchical: frequent movement, exercise, real-food nutrient density, sleep, meal timing, emotional health, and environmental inputs come before narrower optimization tools such as temperature exposure or continuous monitoring. The profile is educational rather than clinical, and broad disease mechanisms, personal thresholds, and intervention choices remain source-scoped.

## Key Characteristics
- Defines metabolism around cellular conversion of food energy into usable energy rather than weight or calorie burning alone.
- Uses mitochondrial capacity, inflammation, and oxidative stress as an integrating model for chronic-disease risk.
- Emphasizes everyday movement and skeletal-muscle contraction alongside structured exercise.
- Treats food, sleep, stress, light, temperature, toxins, and nature exposure as interacting inputs rather than isolated hacks.
- Encourages contextual biomarker and continuous-glucose feedback without requiring perfect readings.

## Qualifications
The current profile comes from one long-form interview summarized without full study citations. Claims about disease prevalence, causal mechanisms, biomarker thresholds, food compounds, fasting, temperature exposure, and monitoring should not be treated as individualized medical advice or independently validated consensus statements.
