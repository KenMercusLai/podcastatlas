# Romulus Augustulus

## Overview

Romulus Augustulus was the young western emperor deposed by [[Odoacer]] in 476, making him the central figure in the conventional date for the fall of the western [[RomanEmpire|Roman Empire]].

## Current Profile

The source presents Romulus as the son and instrument of the military figure Orestes, who removed Julius Nepos and elevated his child. His names echoed Rome's legendary founder and its first emperor, giving the final western deposition an unusually memorable symbolism.

Odoacer defeated Orestes, spared and removed Romulus, and declined to appoint another western emperor. Yet Julius Nepos remained alive in Dalmatia until 480, and Odoacer sent the imperial insignia to [[Constantinople]] while seeking legitimacy from its emperor. Romulus therefore anchors a real institutional change without by himself proving the end of Roman government, identity, or eastern imperial continuity.

## Key Characteristics

- Young western emperor elevated through his father Orestes's military power.
- Deposed by Odoacer in 476 rather than killed in the episode's account.
- Namesake of Romulus and Augustus whose name amplified the event's later symbolism.
- Conventional final western emperor, qualified by Julius Nepos's survival until 480.
- Figure whose deposition ended a western imperial office without ending every Roman institution.

## Evidence

- Elevation - [[156-when-did-the-roman-empire-fall-glt4933356600]] says Orestes removed Julius Nepos and made his son emperor.
- Deposition - [[156-when-did-the-roman-empire-fall-glt4933356600]] describes Odoacer toppling Orestes and sending Romulus away.
- Symbolic name - [[156-when-did-the-roman-empire-fall-glt4933356600]] emphasizes the echoes of Rome's founder and Augustus.
- Endpoint qualification - [[156-when-did-the-roman-empire-fall-glt4933356600]] notes Julius Nepos's survival, Constantinopolitan authority, and continued Roman institutions in Italy.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to one popular-history episode and does not reconstruct Romulus's age, title, later life, or independent political agency. Calling him the last western emperor depends on whether Julius Nepos's continuing claim is counted, while calling 476 Rome's fall depends on the institutional test being used.
