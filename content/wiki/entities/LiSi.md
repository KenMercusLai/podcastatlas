# 李斯 / Li Si

李斯 / Li Si enters the wiki through the closing hook of [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-109-2-chunshenjun-bei-huangdi-dajiuge-quanzu-miemen-locma2plu7v-vsssm5dr5frc3szw]]. After completing [[Chunshenjun|春申君]]'s death and the [[WarringStatesFourLords|战国四公子]] evaluation, the episode pivots to Li Si as a low-origin figure who works hard to win [[QinShiHuang|嬴政 / 秦始皇]]'s appreciation.

That source only previews the next branch: Qin orders foreign guest scholars expelled, and Li Si writes [[JianZhuKeShu|《谏逐客书》]] while preparing to leave.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-110-1-lisi-micang-laoshu-he-xiashuidao-laoshu-ni-zenme-xuan-lmv-fw-kgsuokonssq8bkbfvdsb]] develops the promised Li Si branch before the memorial itself. It presents him as a low-origin [[ChuState|楚国]] clerk whose dissatisfaction with minor office crystallizes through [[MousePhilosophy|老鼠哲学]]: toilet mice and granary mice differ because their environments differ, so an ambitious person must move toward the "granary" of better resources and opportunity. The episode then treats Li Si's study under [[Xunzi|荀子]] as deliberate self-investment before he becomes a Qin guest caught by the [[GuestExpulsionOrder|逐客令]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-110-2-qinshihuang-de-cp-weihe-bei-quzhu-chuguo-lj0222vw8ijbvj25iwfzh-qhvzr9]] completes that branch. Li Si is shown leaving Xunzi's school, choosing [[QinState|秦国]] over [[ChuState|楚国]] because Qin offers stronger opportunity, entering through [[LuBuwei|吕不韦]], and then winning [[QinShiHuang|嬴政 / 秦始皇]]'s confidence. When the [[ZhengGuoHydraulicEngineer|郑国]] case turns foreign guests into security suspects, Li Si answers with [[JianZhuKeShu|《谏逐客书》]], arguing that Qin's own historical rise depended on outside talent and that expulsion would strengthen enemy states. Ying Zheng recalls him, restores his office, and later uses him in the conquest strategy.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-112-1-yaojia-shi-ruhe-ping-yizhangzui-po-siguo-hezong-lthw0fvvinmf6ghxpqut60agsueg]] moves Li Si from defending guest talent into using and policing it. He first argues against [[HanFei|韩非]]'s [[CunHan|《存韩》]] by stressing that [[HanState|韩国]] would remain uncertain if Qin attacked [[ZhaoState|赵国]] first. He then spreads damaging information about [[YaoJia|姚贾]] in Zhao, helping turn a Zhao envoy into a Qin recruit whom he recommends to Ying Zheng. After Yao Jia's success, the episode begins Li Si's accusation that Han Fei will still favor Han, but the transcript cuts off before the branch is complete.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-112-2-qinshihuang-yeshi-tade-shumi-llkoy-zvhjjr6af9tflcw1xvogwz]] adds the immediate setup for that rivalry. After [[QinShiHuang|嬴政 / 秦始皇]] reads [[HanFei|韩非]]'s [[Gufen|《孤愤》]] and [[Wudu|《五蠹》]] and wants to keep Han Fei in Qin, Li Si stands against the Han-preservation line and pushes Ying Zheng toward a decision that would make Han Fei give up on [[HanState|韩国]]. The episode therefore makes Li Si not only a rival classmate, but the adviser who turns admiration for Han Fei's text back toward Qin's conquest priority.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-112-3-hanfei-yu-yaojia-lunzhan-qinwang-dang-caipan-ljxxlok4uh-ajjirmolybodlspv9]] completes that accusation. After [[YaoJia|姚贾]] defeats Han Fei's attack on his origin and mission conduct, Li Si argues that Han Fei's royal membership in [[HanState|韩国]] makes him unusable and unreleasable: if Qin employs him, he will favor Han; if Qin releases him, he returns useful knowledge to an enemy. The source then says Li Si sends poison to the imprisoned Han Fei before Ying Zheng's later pardon arrives, making this a case of [[TreasonAccusationTrap|loyalty-suspicion framing]] and [[PrivatePunishmentWithoutProcedure|private punishment without procedure]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-120-2-qinchao-di-yi-tao-vi-sheji-linian-lno-aeljks2vt1k3wjrizsanirtq]] moves Li Si from conquest court politics into post-unification imperial design. The episode names Li Si and [[QinWangWan|王绾]] in the discussion that takes "朕" from older textual usage and turns it into the emperor's exclusive self-reference, making Li Si part of Qin's [[ImperialSymbolicStandardization|帝国符号标准化]] as well as its talent and conquest branches.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-120-3-jiemi-qinshihuang-weihe-faming-junxianzhi-li4bzz54trstteapodnonxzdzkuv]] gives Li Si the decisive administrative argument after unification. Against Wang Wan's proposal to enfeoff imperial sons in distant former Yan, Qi, and Chu territory, Li Si cites [[ZhouRoyalHouse|周]] enfeoffment as a lesson in delayed fragmentation: kinship fades, hereditary lords become rivals, and old wars return. His solution is commanderies and counties for the whole empire, with princes and meritorious officials rewarded by state revenues rather than sovereign territory.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-124-fenshu-kengru-wo-zai-bzhan-wei-qinshihuang-pingfan-ljnhwcouqu35rmvb-cnrzyzj2j6l]] gives the harsh intellectual-policy sequel to that administrative argument. When [[ChunyuYue|淳于越]] uses ancient precedent to attack the commandery-county order, Li Si treats private classical learning as a political risk: scholars who praise the past to criticize the present can weaken unified law, authority, and public obedience. His proposed [[QinBookBurningPolicy|秦代焚书令]] therefore burns private histories and classics while redirecting legitimate legal learning to officials under [[OfficialLearningMonopoly|学在王官式知识垄断]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-127-2-lisi-weisha-bu-bang-yingming-de-fusu-lq-vny5duwwzfubpeijr21oazxbg]] turns Li Si's late-Qin role from policy architect into succession accomplice. After [[QinShiHuang|秦始皇]] dies on tour, Li Si first supports [[ImperialDeathConcealment|concealing the death]] as a stabilizing move until the convoy can reach [[Xianyang|咸阳]]. When [[ZhaoGao|赵高]] proposes using the imperial letter and seals to install [[QinErshi|胡亥]], Li Si initially calls it ruinous, but Zhao Gao reframes [[FuSu|扶苏]]'s succession as Li Si's demotion under [[MengTian|蒙恬]]. The episode therefore makes Li Si's final turn a case where state-order rhetoric gives way to status anxiety and participation in a [[ForgedEdictSuccessionCoup|forged-edict succession coup]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-127-3-haiwai-huigui-yipi-zhujian-jiekai-huhai-lidi-zhi-mi-lja0pankcx4ugtylbxxqkwuavdnw]] gives a conflicting late-Qin role through [[ZhaoZhengShu|《赵正书》]]. In that account, Li Si speaks directly to the dying Qin Shi Huang, stresses that his power comes entirely from the emperor's recognition, denies an intention to seize power after the emperor's death, and then joins [[FengQuji|冯去疾]] in recommending the nearby Hu Hai as heir. The page therefore keeps Li Si's succession role split by source: self-preserving accomplice in the forged-edict account, emergency validator in the Zhao Zheng Shu account.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-129-1-jiemi-wuguang-bei-miekou-de-lishi-zhenxiang-lk1bkhqkwf9upbbj08pjkn8cstyp|Qinji 129-1]] adds Li Si indirectly through his son [[LiYou|李由]]. Li You is named as the [[SanchuanWarringStates|三川]] commandery governor defending [[YingyangWarringStates|荥阳]] against [[WuGuang|吴广]], showing the Li family still embedded in Qin's local military-administrative response while the capital succession order is already under Hu Hai.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-129-2-lishishang-hejiu-neng-wu-duo-da-shi-zhuan-gei-jiugui-laogong-kan-zhe-ji-lvzxpoxclwdrr66bwejd-bga8dp|Qinji 129-2]] moves Li Si from succession accomplice and policy architect into open late-regime self-preservation. [[QinErshi|胡亥 / 秦二世]] blames him for rebellion, while accusers use [[LiYou|李由]]'s failure in Sanchuan as evidence that the Li family cannot control the crisis. The episode says Li Si fears punishment and clings to office, so he answers with 《上书对二世》 rather than resignation or correction.

In the source's reading, that memorial turns Li Si's [[LegalistRulerTechnique|法家君术]] inheritance into [[DuzeExtractionPolitics|督责式压榨政治]]. Li Si argues that the ruler should inspect, punish, and pressure subordinates so the ruler can preserve pleasure and supremacy; Hu Hai accepts the argument and rewards officials for heavier extraction and killing. This makes Li Si a central agent in the court-to-society feedback failure that accelerates Qin's collapse.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-130-4-ruhe-dinglun-qinxiang-lisi-de-xiachang-ljnz0vflgr62hyktrg5cigz7c-7x|Qinji 130-4]] gives the next consequence of that self-preserving route. [[ZhaoGao|赵高]] persuades [[QinErshi|胡亥 / 秦二世]] to stop meeting senior officials and creates [[PalaceAccessMonopoly|宫廷接触垄断]], so Li Si cannot reliably reach the ruler even as chancellor. When Li Si resents the exclusion, Zhao Gao lures him into repeatedly seeking audience at moments when Hu Hai is enjoying himself, turning remonstrance into an irritant.

The source then makes Li Si both target and late critic. Zhao Gao accuses him of dissatisfaction after the [[ShaqiuCrisis|沙丘]] transition and ties [[LiYou|李由]]'s [[SanchuanWarringStates|三川]] post to rebel-collusion rumors. Li Si answers by warning that Zhao Gao has become like [[TianChang|田常 / 田恒]], but Hu Hai gives the memorial to Zhao Gao. Finally, Li Si joins [[FengQuji|冯去疾]] and [[FengJieQin|冯劫 / 冯杰]] in asking Hu Hai to suspend [[AfangPalace|阿房宫]] and reduce burdens; Hu Hai arrests all three. The episode's judgment is that Li Si's end is not pure victimhood: earlier compromise and later flattery help explain why he cannot save himself when the access system turns against him.
