# Phil Sokoloff

## Overview

Phil Sokoloff was an Omaha businessman and heart-health campaigner whose public pressure helped push [[McDonalds|McDonald’s]] and other fast-food chains away from beef-tallow frying.

## Current Profile

After becoming wealthy through drywall corner-bead manufacturing, Sokoloff suffered a heart attack at 43 and redirected substantial money and energy toward cholesterol and saturated-fat advocacy. The source presents him as loving, intensely driven, and convinced that private wealth should serve public health.

His methods included cholesterol testing, advocacy for low-fat school milk, prominent advertisements, and direct confrontation with corporate representatives. Gladwell criticizes the consequences of the campaign’s nutrient-centered framing but does not reduce Sokoloff to a villain; the sharper judgment falls on McDonald’s product decision. Sokoloff’s daughter recalls that he could acknowledge the post-change fries tasted worse.

## Key Characteristics

- Businessman who used manufacturing wealth to finance a personal public-health campaign.
- Heart-attack survivor whose experience made cholesterol and saturated fat an urgent moral concern.
- High-visibility advocate using advertisements, testing, school-milk policy, and televised confrontation.
- Central pressure actor in the 1990 McDonald’s frying-oil change.
- Public-spirited but evidence-limited figure in the episode’s account of nutrition reform.
