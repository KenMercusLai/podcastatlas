# Declaration of the Rights of Woman and of the Female Citizen

## Overview

The *Declaration of the Rights of Woman and of the Female Citizen* was Olympe de Gouges's 1791 intervention demanding that revolutionary rights language include women.

## Current Profile

The episode treats the declaration as a logical test of revolutionary universalism. If rights belonged to human beings rather than only men, excluding women required justification; if women were liable to public punishment, they should also possess public voice and standing. Its dedication to Marie Antoinette sharpened both its independence and its political risk.

## Key Characteristics

- It directly mirrors and challenges male-centered declarations of rights.
- It argues from universal principle rather than special female privilege.
- It links exposure to punishment with entitlement to political speech.
- It was published after the 1791 constitutional settlement excluded women from suffrage.
- Its dedication to Marie Antoinette resisted simple revolutionary factional expectations.
