# Kim Philby / 金·菲尔比

## Overview

Kim Philby / 金·菲尔比 was a Soviet source inside British intelligence whose access to allied secrets made him both a major penetration case and a lasting symbol of Cold War betrayal.

## Current Profile

[[80-jiandie-yu-pantu-shuwozhiyan-007-de-gushi-ruobaole-shang-691866649]] uses Philby as the earlier reverse image of [[OlegGordievsky|Oleg Gordievsky]]: a Soviet source within [[MI6]] contrasts with a later MI6 source within the [[KGB]]. [[125-the-cia-glt6232412389]] adds his Washington liaison role with the new [[CIA]], where access to covert-operation briefings, including attempts to insert agents into Albania, allowed him to pass allied plans to Soviet handlers. His closeness to [[JamesAngleton]] makes the case part of the CIA's later counterintelligence paranoia as well as British institutional memory.

## Key Characteristics

- Penetrated British intelligence on behalf of the Soviet Union.
- Gained access to early CIA covert operations through his Washington liaison role.
- Transmitted allied operational information to Soviet handlers.
- Became an institutional warning story about trust, liaison access, and mole hunting.
- Serves as a reverse-direction contrast to Gordievsky's work for MI6 from inside the KGB.

## Evidence

- Comparative spy history: [[80-jiandie-yu-pantu-shuwozhiyan-007-de-gushi-ruobaole-shang-691866649]] presents Philby as a KGB legend and the earlier inverse of Gordievsky.
- Allied liaison access: [[125-the-cia-glt6232412389]] says Philby received CIA covert-operation briefings in Washington and passed information to Soviet handlers.
- Counterintelligence aftermath: [[125-the-cia-glt6232412389]] connects his relationship with Angleton and 1963 defection to later CIA penetration fears.

## Qualifications

The sources provide a compressed comparative and institutional account, not a full biography or operational inventory. The claim that Philby's betrayal shaped Angleton's later paranoia is plausible in the episode but does not by itself explain every mole hunt or justify Angleton's allegations.
