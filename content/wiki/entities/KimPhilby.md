# Kim Philby / 金·菲尔比

## Overview

Kim Philby was a Soviet source inside British intelligence whose allied access made him a major penetration case and a lasting symbol of Cold War betrayal.

## Current Profile

The sources place Philby's reverse-direction comparison with [[OlegGordievsky|Oleg Gordievsky]] beside his Washington liaison role with the new [[CIA]]. A Soviet source within [[MI6]] contrasts with an MI6 source inside the [[KGB]], while Philby's access to covert-operation briefings, including attempts to insert agents into Albania, allowed him to pass allied plans to Soviet handlers. His closeness to [[JamesAngleton]] made the betrayal part of the CIA's later counterintelligence paranoia as well as British institutional memory. [[80-jiandie-yu-pantu-shuwozhiyan-007-de-gushi-ruobaole-shang-691866649]] supports the comparison, and [[125-the-cia-glt6232412389]] supports the liaison account.

The combined profile includes a moral and motivational judgment from [[37-spies-with-ben-macintyre-glt4382644548]]. Macintyre says Philby began as an ideological communist but later mixed belief with ambition, hubris, adventure, and the addictive pull of secrecy; by exile in Moscow, in his account, communist conviction had faded. The episode argues that perspective affects whether a person is called spy or traitor while still judging Philby's service to Stalinism and the deaths attributed to his disclosures more harshly than Gordievsky's stated effort to avoid bloodshed.

## Key Characteristics

- Penetrated British intelligence on behalf of the Soviet Union.
- Used allied liaison access to transmit covert-operation information to Soviet handlers.
- Became an institutional warning story about trust, privileged access, and mole hunting.
- Began with ideological commitment but is presented as developing mixed motives over time.
- Serves as a reverse-direction and moral contrast to Gordievsky.

## Qualifications

The sources provide compressed comparative and institutional accounts, not a full biography, casualty audit, or operational inventory. Macintyre's claims about Philby's fading belief, internal motives, knowledge of Stalinist violence, and responsibility for deaths remain historical judgments attributed to the episode. Philby's influence on Angleton does not by itself explain or justify every later mole hunt.
