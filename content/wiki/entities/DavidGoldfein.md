# David Goldfein

## Overview

David Goldfein is a former chief of staff of the [[UnitedStatesAirForce|U.S. Air Force]] and a member of the Air Force Academy class of 1982. The bounded source presents him as [[HeatherWilson]]'s uniformed partner in accepting responsibility for the reporting failure exposed by the [[SutherlandSpringsChurchShooting]].

## Current Profile

Goldfein grounds leadership in integrity, service, excellence, and daily renewal of trust. He argues that current leaders must own an institution's failure even when they did not cause it, then demonstrate that responsibility through disclosure, correction, teaching, and personal presence.

His visit to Sutherland Springs makes representation concrete. He asked local officials whether he should attend the funerals and what he should wear, came in uniform at the community's request, declined front-row treatment, and tried not to turn mourning into an event centered on his office. He presents failure as unavoidable but response as teachable: leaders should show future generations how to own, recover from, and learn from grave mistakes.

## Key Characteristics

- Grounds decisions in stated institutional values rather than reputational defense.
- Accepts responsibility for inherited failures without claiming personal causation.
- Seeks permission and guidance from harmed stakeholders before appearing on their behalf.
- Uses personal presence while resisting status and protocol that would displace the community.
- Treats institutional failure as a case for teaching recovery and renewed trust.

## Qualifications

This profile rests on one structured podcast summary and primarily reflects Goldfein's retrospective interpretation. It does not independently evaluate the reporting failure, reforms, community response, or causal effect of the leaders' conduct on press, congressional, legal, or trust outcomes. The funeral scenes should not be generalized into a complete model of victim-centered crisis response.
