# Gustav Stresemann

## Overview

Gustav Stresemann was the Weimar statesman whom the episode credits with stabilizing Germany's 1923 monetary and Ruhr crises through the Rentenmark and a strategy of compliance followed by renegotiation.

## Current Profile

Stresemann entered office during hyperinflation, French occupation of the Ruhr, passive resistance, and intense nationalist anger. The episode presents him as choosing practical stabilization over grievance politics: he introduced the Rentenmark, ended the Ruhr confrontation, and sought to demonstrate good faith before renegotiating Germany's obligations through the process leading to the Dawes Plan.

That approach brought immediate nationalist accusations of surrender, including Nazi hostility. Its significance is therefore double: it shows that the [[WeimarRepublic]] retained real capacity for recovery in 1923, and it clarifies why compromise could be politically vulnerable when opponents treated concession as betrayal.

## Key Characteristics

- Weimar crisis leader associated with the 1923 stabilization.
- Sponsor of the Rentenmark response to hyperinflation.
- Advocate of ending passive resistance in the Ruhr.
- Practitioner of fulfillment followed by negotiated revision.
- Counterexample to claims that Weimar collapse was already inevitable in 1923.

## Evidence

- Monetary stabilization: [[296-the-nazis-the-beer-hall-putsch-part-2-glt4099180386]] credits Stresemann with introducing the Rentenmark during the hyperinflation crisis.
- Ruhr strategy: [[296-the-nazis-the-beer-hall-putsch-part-2-glt4099180386]] connects him to ending the Ruhr confrontation and pursuing good-faith compliance before renegotiation.
- Political cost: [[296-the-nazis-the-beer-hall-putsch-part-2-glt4099180386]] describes Hitler and the nationalist right as treating his pragmatic strategy as surrender.
