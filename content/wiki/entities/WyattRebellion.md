# Wyatt's Rebellion

## Overview

Wyatt's Rebellion is the early-1554 rising against [[MaryI|Mary I]] formed around opposition to her proposed marriage to [[PhilipII|Philip II of Spain]]. It transformed [[LadyJaneGrey|Lady Jane Grey]] into an intolerable reusable claimant and placed [[ElizabethI|Elizabeth I]] under life-threatening suspicion without producing conclusive evidence against her.

## Current Profile

The rebellion demonstrates that Mary's accession victory did not permanently remove the political value of an alternative Tudor regime. Jane or Elizabeth need not direct a revolt openly to become dangerous: opponents can organize around what their survival makes imaginable. A planned multi-region conspiracy collapses into Wyatt's serious Kentish rising of roughly 3,000 men, which Mary defeats after rallying London at the Guildhall.

[[HenryGrey|Henry Grey]]'s participation makes the threat especially immediate by linking Jane's family to renewed resistance. After the rising, the suspended death sentences of Jane and [[GuildfordDudley|Guildford Dudley]] are carried out, and Henry is also executed. Elizabeth is imprisoned in the Tower and at Woodstock; suspicious flight from her household sustains doubt, but Wyatt denies her prior knowledge and no incriminating paper trail appears.

## Key Characteristics

- The proposed Spanish marriage supplies the episode's central grievance context.
- The rising reveals continued opposition after Mary's apparently decisive accession.
- Jane functions as a potential focus even without proven participation.
- Henry Grey's involvement ties family rebellion to claimant risk.
- The rebellion changes the practical meaning of previously suspended death sentences.
- Mary's Guildhall performance and refusal to flee turn female royal presence into a means of defeating revolt.
- Elizabeth's possible complicity remains unresolved because political suspicion exceeds the surviving proof.

## Evidence

- Marriage opposition and revolt: [[294-lady-jane-grey-the-axe-falls-part-2-glt7286066638]] links the rebellion to widespread hostility toward Mary's Spanish marriage.
- Claimant danger: [[294-lady-jane-grey-the-axe-falls-part-2-glt7286066638]] argues that the rising makes Jane too dangerous for Mary to spare.
- Execution aftermath: [[294-lady-jane-grey-the-axe-falls-part-2-glt7286066638]] connects the failed revolt to the deaths of Guildford, Jane, and Henry Grey.
- Plot, campaign, and repression: [[618-elizabeth-i-the-shadow-of-the-tower-part-3-glt7928851339]] supplies the regional plan, Wyatt's Kentish force, Mary's Guildhall mobilization, defeat, and executions.
- Elizabeth question: [[618-elizabeth-i-the-shadow-of-the-tower-part-3-glt7928851339]] preserves household suspicion, lack of documentary proof, Wyatt's exonerating statement, and Elizabeth's imprisonment.

## Qualifications

The sources do not establish a complete social or military history of the rising. Jane's or Elizabeth's usefulness as a claimant does not prove personal participation. Wyatt's statement may be truthful or may protect a future Protestant heir, while the flight of John Ashley supports suspicion without establishing Elizabeth's authorization.

## What Changed

- Expanded the rebellion from Jane's execution context to its regional plan, Kentish campaign, Marian mobilization, and repression.
- Added Elizabeth's imprisonment and the unresolved evidence boundary around her knowledge.
- Identified Mary's public refusal to flee as a successful performance of female sovereignty.
