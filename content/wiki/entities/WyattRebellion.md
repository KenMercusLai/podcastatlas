# Wyatt's Rebellion

## Overview

Wyatt's Rebellion is the early-1554 rising against [[MaryI|Mary I]] associated in the episode with opposition to her proposed marriage to Philip of Spain. Its decisive role in this source is to transform [[LadyJaneGrey|Lady Jane Grey]] from a containable prisoner into a reusable Protestant claimant.

## Current Profile

The rebellion demonstrates that Mary's accession victory did not permanently remove the political value of an alternative Tudor regime. Jane need not direct a revolt herself to become dangerous: opponents can organize around what her survival makes imaginable.

[[HenryGrey|Henry Grey]]'s participation makes the threat especially immediate by linking Jane's family to renewed resistance. After the rising, the suspended death sentences of Jane and [[GuildfordDudley|Guildford Dudley]] are carried out, and Henry is also executed.

## Key Characteristics

- The proposed Spanish marriage supplies the episode's central grievance context.
- The rising reveals continued opposition after Mary's apparently decisive accession.
- Jane functions as a potential focus even without proven participation.
- Henry Grey's involvement ties family rebellion to claimant risk.
- The rebellion changes the practical meaning of previously suspended death sentences.

## Evidence

- Marriage opposition and revolt: [[294-lady-jane-grey-the-axe-falls-part-2-glt7286066638]] links the rebellion to widespread hostility toward Mary's Spanish marriage.
- Claimant danger: [[294-lady-jane-grey-the-axe-falls-part-2-glt7286066638]] argues that the rising makes Jane too dangerous for Mary to spare.
- Execution aftermath: [[294-lady-jane-grey-the-axe-falls-part-2-glt7286066638]] connects the failed revolt to the deaths of Guildford, Jane, and Henry Grey.

## Qualifications

The episode gives only a compressed account of the rebellion and does not establish a complete regional, social, military, or ideological history of the rising. Jane's usefulness as a claimant does not prove that she knew of, approved, or participated in the plot.

## What Changed

- Established the rebellion as the causal bridge between Mary's initial mercy and Jane's execution.
- Distinguished a claimant's political usefulness from personal participation in revolt.
- Connected marriage opposition, family involvement, and regime-security judgment.

## Relationships

- [[MaryI]] - reigning queen challenged by the rising.
- [[LadyJaneGrey]] - captive claimant whose survival becomes intolerable after the revolt.
- [[HenryGrey]] - Jane's father and participant in the rebellion.
