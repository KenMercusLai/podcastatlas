# Kimi K2.5

## Overview
Kimi K2.5 is an open or open-weight AI model release presented by the episode as a notable step in lower-cost, self-controlled, agentic model deployment.

## Current Profile
[[ChamathPalihapitiya|Chamath Palihapitiya]] describes Kimi K2.5 as a trillion-parameter mixture-of-experts model with an “Agent Swarm” mode for parallel multi-step work. The hosts use it less as a verified benchmark winner than as a market-structure signal: capable downloadable or self-hosted models could compress API costs, keep some sensitive data under organizational control, and increase pressure on proprietary labs.

The same provenance that supports the open-model competition thesis creates a security qualification. [[DavidSacks|David Sacks]] warns that a Chinese-origin model used for coding or critical infrastructure could contain hidden behavior or produce injected code. The episode offers no technical audit establishing either compromise or safety.

## Key Characteristics
- Described as a trillion-parameter mixture-of-experts model.
- Presented as open or open-weight rather than accessible only through a closed provider API.
- Associated with an “Agent Swarm” capability for parallel task execution.
- Used as evidence for lower marginal inference cost and greater deployment control.
- Raises model-provenance, validation, and software-supply-chain concerns in sensitive use.

## Qualifications
- Architecture, parameter count, licensing status, benchmark performance, deployment cost, and agent behavior are reported by the episode and not independently verified in the supplied document.
- “Open source” and “open weight” are not interchangeable; the episode summary does not specify the complete license, training-data, or reproducibility boundary.
- A model's national origin does not demonstrate malicious behavior, while local execution does not eliminate risks from weights, dependencies, prompts, tools, or generated code.
