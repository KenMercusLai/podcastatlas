# May 1968 in France

## Overview

May 1968 in France was a student, worker, and political crisis in which mass strikes and street protest appeared to threaten the state before Charles de Gaulle dissolved parliament and won a decisive legislative election.

## Current Profile

[[354-paris-1968-the-return-of-de-gaulle-part-2-glt6246374874]] begins after student protest, police violence, occupations, and strikes have produced the impression that the French state is losing control. Failed compromise at Grenelle, continued worker rejection, opposition maneuvering, communist mobilization, and de Gaulle's temporary disappearance intensified the possibility of regime change.

The episode treats 30 May as the reversal: [[CharlesDeGaulle]] returned, announced elections by radio, threatened emergency powers if disruption continued, and was followed by a mass Gaullist counter-demonstration. The movement then lost momentum and the June election delivered an overwhelming parliamentary majority to the Gaullists. That outcome supports a judgment of immediate political defeat, while the movement's later effects on culture, authority, and theories of institutional power remain disputed.

## Key Characteristics

- Joined student revolt, worker strikes, occupations, union negotiation, and parliamentary opposition without making them a single unified project.
- Created genuine uncertainty about state control even though retrospective accounts know the regime survived.
- Drew meaning from French memories of revolution and the Paris Commune.
- Continued after material concessions because participation, occupation, slogans, and collective possibility had acquired their own momentum.
- Was politically reversed through army reassurance, executive recovery, electoral reframing, and mass pro-order mobilization.
- Produced a clearer immediate electoral defeat than a settled judgment about cultural and intellectual influence.
