# May 1968 in France

## Overview

May 1968 in France was a student, worker, and political crisis that grew from university disputes and police confrontation into mass strikes and apparent regime danger before Charles de Gaulle dissolved parliament and won a decisive legislative election.

## Current Profile

[[353-paris-1968-the-students-revolt-part-1-glt6124707164]] supplies the crisis's first phase. Rapid university expansion, overcrowding, Nanterre's unfinished and isolated campus, paternalistic residence rules, and disciplinary action created a bounded dispute. Closing Nanterre displaced protest to the Sorbonne; arrests and beatings widened sympathy; barricades and memories of the Paris Commune intensified the symbolism; and radio and television carried the confrontation nationally. Worker occupations and strikes then transformed the scale without creating a single student-worker program.

[[354-paris-1968-the-return-of-de-gaulle-part-2-glt6246374874]] follows failed compromise at Grenelle, continued worker rejection, opposition maneuvering, communist mobilization, and de Gaulle's temporary disappearance. It treats 30 May as the reversal: [[CharlesDeGaulle]] returned, announced elections by radio, threatened emergency powers if disruption continued, and was followed by a mass Gaullist counter-demonstration. The June election supports a judgment of immediate political defeat, while the movement's later effects on culture, authority, and institutional power remain disputed.

## Key Characteristics

- Began in concrete university capacity and authority disputes before acquiring a wider revolutionary language.
- Joined student revolt, worker strikes, occupations, union negotiation, and parliamentary opposition without making them a single unified project.
- Created genuine uncertainty about state control even though retrospective accounts know the regime survived.
- Escalated through institutional closure, police violence, barricades, media transmission, and French revolutionary memory.
- Continued after material concessions because participation, occupation, slogans, and collective possibility had acquired their own momentum.
- Was politically reversed through army reassurance, executive recovery, electoral reframing, and mass pro-order mobilization.
- Produced a clearer immediate electoral defeat than a settled judgment about cultural and intellectual influence.

- Origins and escalation: [[353-paris-1968-the-students-revolt-part-1-glt6124707164]] links university expansion, Nanterre grievances, disciplinary action, campus closure, police violence, barricades, and media coverage.
- Coalition limits: [[353-paris-1968-the-students-revolt-part-1-glt6124707164]] distinguishes student anti-authoritarian aims from worker demands and Communist Party strategy; [[354-paris-1968-the-return-of-de-gaulle-part-2-glt6246374874]] adds rejected Grenelle concessions and opposition maneuvering.
- State uncertainty: [[354-paris-1968-the-return-of-de-gaulle-part-2-glt6246374874]] recounts military contingency planning, de Gaulle's departure, and officials preparing for possible flight or takeover.
- Reversal: [[354-paris-1968-the-return-of-de-gaulle-part-2-glt6246374874]] connects the radio address, parliamentary dissolution, Gaullist demonstration, holiday pause, and clearing of occupied institutions.
- Political result and afterlife: [[354-paris-1968-the-return-of-de-gaulle-part-2-glt6246374874]] contrasts the Gaullist election landslide with later debate about Foucault, institutional power, and cultural change.

## Qualifications

The sources form a two-part narrative synthesis with a deliberately skeptical tone toward protester coherence and later claims made for 1968. They do not provide a granular social history of student factions, workplaces, unions, women, immigrants, or regional experience. Participation totals, remembered dialogue, causal weight assigned to policing, media, individual decisions, or holidays, and the relationship between the events and later intellectual movements remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Added the university, policing, barricade, media, and strike sequence that produced the crisis before de Gaulle's reversal.
- Sharpened the distinction between mutually reinforcing student and worker mobilization and a unified coalition.
