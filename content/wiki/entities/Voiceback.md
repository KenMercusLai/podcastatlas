## Overview
Voiceback is an assistive app founded by [[RheaJain]] that clones a person's voice from a short recording and connects it to text-to-speech communication after speech loss.

## Current Profile
The source presents Voiceback as a mission-led product with substantial free downloads but only a recent paid model. Its strongest evidence comes from a personal use case, an adviser with ALS expertise, and a user advisory group. The next growth question is not mass awareness alone: the company must revalidate willingness to pay, retention, pricing, access, safety, and trusted referral paths after introducing payment.

## Key Characteristics
- Was created after Jain's grandmother with ALS rejected robotic voice options.
- Reportedly clones a voice from 10 to 60 seconds of prior audio.
- Had more than 50,000 downloads after one year, largely from free beta users.
- Reportedly generated about $20,000 in the two months after introducing a paywall.
- Uses feedback from a roughly ten-person speech-loss advisory group.
- Faces distribution through clinical and patient communities where trust matters more than broad reach.

## Qualifications
Downloads do not establish sustained assistive use, clinical benefit, safety, or paid product-market fit. The source does not adequately address consent for deceased or third-party recordings, privacy, data security, synthetic-voice misuse, medical-device regulation, or institutional procurement.

- Created the product profile with paid-validation and safety boundaries explicit.
