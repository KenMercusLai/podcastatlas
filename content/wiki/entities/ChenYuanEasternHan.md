## Overview
陈元 / Chen Yuan is an Eastern Han remonstrant whose current wiki role is bounded to [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-705-1-diwang-moulue-liuxiu-ruhe-yi-sheling-nizhuan-caiyuan-fengyun-lq2m06p7eczj9ebysxpl84bkzzi|Hanji 705-1]]. He matters because he opposes a proposal to have the Sili xiaowei supervise the Three Excellencies, turning a personnel-control measure into a warning about ruler-minister trust after Wang Mang.

## Current Profile
In Hanji 705-1, a memorial proposes using the Sili xiaowei to inspect the Three Excellencies. Chen Yuan answers by arguing that a ruler who treats ministers as teachers or honored guests can reach imperial or hegemonic order, while a ruler who humiliates and mistrusts senior ministers repeats the destructive pattern of Wang Mang's Xin regime.

His warning is institutional rather than sentimental. Chen Yuan does not deny that officials need accountability; he argues that the highest ministers cannot be treated as ordinary suspects under a surveillance logic without damaging trust, responsibility, and governance. The episode says Liu Xiu immediately accepts the advice, making Chen Yuan a compact example of remonstrance that converts court structure rather than only stating moral discomfort.

## Key Characteristics
- Guangwu-era court remonstrant known here for defending the dignity and functional authority of the Three Excellencies.
- Uses historical comparison to Wang Mang to make suspicious supervision politically dangerous.
- Frames ruler-minister trust as a governance resource, not a private courtesy.
- Argues through institutional design: accountability should not become a structure that humiliates senior ministers and centralizes suspicion.
- Successful adviser in this source because Liu Xiu accepts the warning quickly.
