# Sejanus

## Overview

Sejanus was [[Tiberius]]'s Praetorian prefect and principal deputy whose power during the emperor's absence from Rome, alleged conspiracy, and fall became central to the history and hostile memory of the later reign.

## Current Profile

[[535-emperors-of-rome-tiberius-slaughter-and-scandal-part-2-glt6029756405]] presents Sejanus as Tiberius's former right-hand man and head of the Praetorian Guard. Tiberius's withdrawal to Capri increased the political importance of delegated authority, helping make Sejanus a symbol of the risks created when an emperor remained sovereign but physically absent from the capital.

After Sejanus was accused of plotting against Tiberius, his fall was followed by reprisals against alleged conspirators. The episode does not independently settle the plot's membership, scale, or Sejanus's intentions. His current profile therefore distinguishes his documented proximity to power and violent removal from the atrocity details through which hostile sources dramatize the episode.

## Key Characteristics

- Praetorian prefect and close deputy of Tiberius.
- Beneficiary of the political space created by the emperor's withdrawal to Capri.
- Alleged conspirator whose fall triggered investigations and reprisals.
- Figure through whom delegated rule became part of Tiberius's tyrannical reputation.

## Evidence

- Delegated authority - [[535-emperors-of-rome-tiberius-slaughter-and-scandal-part-2-glt6029756405]] identifies Sejanus as Tiberius's right-hand man and Praetorian commander during the emperor's absence.
- Conspiracy and fall - [[535-emperors-of-rome-tiberius-slaughter-and-scandal-part-2-glt6029756405]] reports the accusation against Sejanus and the reprisals that followed his removal.
- Reputation effect - [[535-emperors-of-rome-tiberius-slaughter-and-scandal-part-2-glt6029756405]] places Sejanus inside the later image of a fearful emperor manipulated from Rome and then driven to vengeance.

## Qualifications

The current profile is bounded to one podcast's treatment of Sejanus within Tiberius's biography. It does not establish the full chronology of his ascent, the exact conspiracy, the guilt of those punished after his fall, or whether Tiberius was manipulated rather than actively governing through him.

## What Changed

- Created a bounded profile separating delegated authority and violent aftermath from uncertain conspiracy detail.
