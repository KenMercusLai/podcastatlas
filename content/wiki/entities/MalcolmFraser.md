# Malcolm Fraser

## Overview

Malcolm Fraser was an Australian Liberal prime minister whose path to office through the 1975 supply crisis made him hated by much of the left, although the episode portrays his government as pragmatic rather than hard-right.

## Current Profile

[[188-australian-prime-ministers-harold-holt-malcolm-fraser-part-2-glt4635125602]] makes Fraser the opposition strategist who used the Senate to block supply, accepted appointment from Governor-General [[JohnKerr|John Kerr]] after [[GoughWhitlam|Gough Whitlam's]] dismissal, and then won a landslide election. His roughly eight-year premiership is characterized through technocratic pragmatism, acceptance of Vietnamese refugees, and opposition to apartheid before defeat by [[BobHawke|Bob Hawke]] in 1983.

## Key Characteristics

- Liberal opposition leader who forced the 1975 supply confrontation.
- Appointed prime minister after Kerr dismissed Whitlam.
- Won the subsequent election by a large margin.
- Pragmatic, technocratic conservative rather than a consistent hard-right ideologue.
- Accepted Vietnamese refugees and opposed apartheid.
- Led for about eight years before losing to Hawke in 1983.

- Governing profile: [[188-australian-prime-ministers-harold-holt-malcolm-fraser-part-2-glt4635125602]] uses refugee admission and anti-apartheid policy to qualify a simple hard-right label.

## Qualifications

The episode gives far more detail to Fraser's accession and a later scandalous anecdote than to eight years of government. Explanations of the Memphis incident remain disputed and should not determine his political profile; the election result resolved who governed but not every constitutional question raised by the dismissal.

## What Changed

- Established Fraser as both architect and electoral beneficiary of the 1975 supply confrontation.
- Qualified the hostile left-wing memory of his accession with refugee and anti-apartheid policy.
