# Afonso Henriques

## Overview

Afonso Henriques is presented as the ruler who converted the county around Porto into the independent kingdom of [[Portugal]] and drove its frontier south to Lisbon.

## Current Profile

The episode organizes Afonso's state-building around São Mamede in 1128, Ourique in 1139, and the 1147 capture of Lisbon. His career joins dynastic separation from neighboring Christian rulers to the [[IberianReconquista|Reconquista]], showing that Portuguese identity emerged from a shifting Iberian field rather than from fixed modern nations.

At Lisbon, Afonso used the passage of Second Crusade fleets to strengthen his own campaign. This made kingdom formation both local and transnational: northern European crusaders supplied force, while the conduct after surrender remains morally uneven in the source.

## Key Characteristics

- Founder-king in Portuguese historical memory.
- Converted a Burgundian county into an independent kingdom.
- Joined dynastic and territorial state-building to crusading expansion.
- Used a passing Second Crusade fleet to capture Lisbon in 1147.

## Evidence

- Kingdom-formation evidence: [[227-portugal-on-the-edge-of-the-world-part-1-glt1834337589]] links São Mamede and Ourique to Afonso's rise from count's son to king.
- Lisbon evidence: [[227-portugal-on-the-edge-of-the-world-part-1-glt1834337589]] says Afonso persuaded northern European crusaders sailing from Dartmouth to help take Lisbon.
- Political-context evidence: [[227-portugal-on-the-edge-of-the-world-part-1-glt1834337589]] stresses that Portugal and Spain were not yet fixed national identities during this process.

## Qualifications

This is a source-bounded profile rather than a full reign history. The episode treats the three battles as memorable turning points but does not reconstruct their documentary disputes, later mythmaking, or the full diplomatic recognition of the kingdom.
