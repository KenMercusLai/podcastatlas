# Hong Kong / 香港

## Overview
Hong Kong / 香港 is a recurring city node in the wiki, appearing through housing, equity markets, overseas Chinese networks, financial history, aviation hub infrastructure, and postwar professional football.

## Current Profile
Hong Kong's current profile is a city where lived experience, capital markets, cross-border networks, transport infrastructure, and sports institutions overlap. Earlier sources use it as a housing-experience case, a difficult but opportunity-rich equity market, a node in Chaoshan remittance and special-zone comparisons, and a colonial financial-history setting.

The aviation roundtable introduces a new role: Hong Kong as an East-West aviation hub whose value comes from Cathay Pacific, cargo, long-haul routes, free-port status, institutional environment, and Greater Bay Area manufacturing connectivity. This makes Hong Kong more than a market or housing case; it is also an infrastructure gateway whose airport and airline system reshape city space and regional access.

Hong Kong also now has a sports-institution layer in the wiki. It appears as an early Asian professional-football case whose 1968 professionalization emerged from amateur-status conflict rather than planned modernization, then moved through semi-professional jobs, corporate teams, foreign-player escalation, and governance debates over association membership versus owner-controlled football companies.

## Key Characteristics
- Dense lived-experience and housing case where home as consumption must be separated from home as investment.
- Offshore China equity market with liquidity, governance, sell-side, and old-thousand risks.
- Cross-border finance and industrial-transfer node in Chaoshan/Shenzhen special-zone comparison.
- Colonial financial-history setting where conglomerate control, exchange fragmentation, hot money, and retail psychology interacted.
- Aviation and cargo hub linking mainland China, East Asia, Australia, Europe, and North America through airport, airline, and free-port advantages.
- Early Asian professional-football setting where paid labor, commercial patrons, foreign players, and traditional sports associations shaped club governance.

## Qualifications
The aviation source is a branded Cathay anniversary event, so Hong Kong's aviation advantages should be read as source-scoped rather than as a full comparison against Guangzhou, Shenzhen, Singapore, Dubai, or other hubs. The football source has a visible transcript gap from about 41:25 to 57:02, so its commercial-team and industrial-change discussion is incomplete. The finance and housing sources likewise represent podcast analysis, not complete market or planning studies.
