# 柳湘莲 / Liu Xianglian

## Overview

柳湘莲 is the chosen partner, rejecting fiancé, and eventual mourner of [[YouSanjie|尤三姐]] in [[HongLouMeng|《红楼梦》]]. The episode presents him as a fallen-gentry amateur performer whose decision joins understandable self-protection to associative prejudice and irreversible harm.

## Current Profile

Liu Xianglian accepts [[JiaLian|贾琏]]'s proposal and gives the inherited 鸳鸯剑 as a pledge before investigating You Sanjie's circumstances. After [[JiaBaoyu|贾宝玉]] does not directly affirm her character, he reads her connection to 宁国府 as evidence of continuing sexual and relational danger and seeks to withdraw.

The source refuses a simple villain-or-victim verdict. He is not required to ignore compatibility and trust, but he judges without meeting You Sanjie or distinguishing her present commitments from her environment and reputation. Her suicide reveals both the seriousness of her change and the fatal burden placed on that revelation; his grief and renunciation then overturn, rather than vindicate, his earlier certainty.

## Key Characteristics

- Accepts a consequential pledge quickly and investigates only afterward.
- Treats 宁国府's corruption as evidence about a woman associated with it.
- Uses a socially and allegedly legally defensible prior-betrothal reason to exit the match.
- Has a legitimate interest in trust and future relational risk without possessing certainty about You Sanjie's character.
- Revises his judgment only after an irreversible act and then abandons secular life.

## Qualifications

The episode cannot establish Liu Xianglian's private motive, whether he initially sought access to the 贾–薛 circle, or whether sexual history or future family entanglement mattered more. Its legal account of competing betrothals is oral and source-scoped. Explaining his caution does not settle his moral responsibility, and criticizing his prejudice does not imply a duty to marry without trust.

## What Changed

- Established a profile centered on premature commitment, associative suspicion, and belated revision.
- Kept understandable risk assessment distinct from certainty about another person's moral identity.
- Connected his renunciation to the collapse of his own social judgment rather than to romance alone.
