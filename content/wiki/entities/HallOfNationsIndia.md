# Hall of Nations and Nehru Pavilion

## Overview

The Hall of Nations and Nehru Pavilion was a 1972 exhibition complex at Pragati Maidan in New Delhi, designed by Raj Rewal and used by the episode as a major expression of post-independence Indian modernism.

## Current Profile

[[110-history-of-india-in-10-buildings-glt7972990176]] presents the complex as celebrated and award-winning brutalist architecture linked to a secular, liberal, technologically forward-looking Nehruvian national project. Its later demolition, while legal challenges were still underway, makes it the episode's clearest case of [[ArchitecturalErasureAndRegimeMemory]] and extends the [[ModernistHeritageRecognitionGap]] from weak public recognition to active political rupture.

## Key Characteristics

- A post-independence exhibition and pavilion complex completed in 1972.
- Designed by Indian architect Raj Rewal for Pragati Maidan in New Delhi.
- Presented as a high point of Indian brutalist and modernist architecture.
- Associated with Nehruvian secularism, liberalism, technological ambition, and national self-presentation.
- Demolished despite recognition, awards, and unresolved legal challenge.

## Evidence

- Design and date: [[110-history-of-india-in-10-buildings-glt7972990176]] identifies Raj Rewal, Pragati Maidan, and 1972.
- Architectural status: [[110-history-of-india-in-10-buildings-glt7972990176]] describes the complex as a celebrated high point of brutalism that had won awards.
- Political afterlife: [[110-history-of-india-in-10-buildings-glt7972990176]] interprets demolition as an attack on a secular and forward-looking Nehruvian vision of India.

## Qualifications

The political interpretation comes from the episode and does not independently establish every institutional motive behind demolition, the exact legal sequence, or a complete conservation assessment. Architectural value, functional redevelopment, legal process, and regime symbolism should be distinguished even when they interact.

## What Changed

- Established the complex as a post-independence monument whose demolition carries architectural and national-memory significance.
- Added an Indian case in which recognized modernist heritage did not secure preservation.
