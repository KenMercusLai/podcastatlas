## Overview

陈万年 / Chen Wannian enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-473-xihan-yongren-chenwannian-weihe-neng-bei-zairu-shice-ljxaektybs1lvddzuuqca-rpjwxy|Hanji 473]] as a Western Han official whose rise to Yushi Dafu is presented as a negative case of flattery-driven advancement. The episode does not credit him with major achievements; it remembers him through his calibrated service to powerful men and his failed attempt to teach his son [[ChenXianXinOfficial|陈贤]] the same "officialcraft."

## Current Profile

Chen Wannian is synthesized as a source-bounded example of high rank without strong public merit. The source says he succeeds because he reads what different powerful people want. Toward Shi Gao, who is described as wealthy and pleasure-seeking, Chen Wannian spends heavily. Toward Bing Ji, whose reputation is upright and clean, he performs personal devotion during illness, including unusually close bedside service.

The episode treats Bing Ji's deathbed recommendation as the hinge that converts private attentiveness into public office. Chen Wannian's advancement therefore sits near [[BureaucraticAdverseSelection|官场逆淘汰]], but with a Yuan-era nuance: the system does not only reward open slander or patron desire; it can also mistake personalized deference for governable reliability.

His remembered "education" scene makes him more than a generic flatterer. When sick at home, he calls Chen Xian to receive lessons in official survival, but the lesson is essentially sycophantic technique. Chen Xian's sleepy, ironic response and later upright career turn Chen Wannian into a negative paternal model: instruction can be worse than silence when it transmits corrupted standards.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han official who reaches Yushi Dafu despite being presented as mediocre and lacking visible governing achievement.
- Advancement depends on adaptive flattery rather than one fixed tactic.
- Uses money and gifts toward Shi Gao, whose preferences are framed as wealth and indulgence.
- Uses deferential personal care toward Bing Ji, whose moral reputation makes crude bribery less suitable.
- Turns paternal instruction into an attempt to transmit sycophantic survival methods.
- Functions as a negative contrast to his son Chen Xian, whose later profile emphasizes legal skill, integrity, risk-bearing, and refusal under Wang Mang.
