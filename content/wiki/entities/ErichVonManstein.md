# Erich von Manstein

## Overview

Erich von Manstein was a German officer associated with the plan to concentrate armored forces through the Ardennes during the 1940 western campaign.

## Current Profile

[[621-the-nazis-at-war-blitzkrieg-part-2-glt8711886512]] places Manstein, working with [[HeinzGuderian]], behind an alternative to Germany's more conventional original plan through Belgium and the Netherlands. Their proposal treated the northern attack as a diversion, concentrated armored strength through terrain the Allies considered less threatening, crossed the Meuse, and then turned toward the Channel to encircle Allied armies.

The profile is therefore one of operational design under risk. The plan succeeded by exploiting Allied expectations and response speed, but its crowded Ardennes columns were vulnerable and its success was not guaranteed by German material superiority.

## Key Characteristics

- Advocate of an Ardennes-centered alternative to the original western plan.
- Planner of operational surprise through terrain the Allies discounted.
- Associated with concentrating armor and turning toward the Channel after crossing the Meuse.
- Beneficiary of Allied planning assumptions and missed opportunities against vulnerable columns.

## Evidence

- Plan revision: [[621-the-nazis-at-war-blitzkrieg-part-2-glt8711886512]] contrasts Manstein's proposal with the original approach through the Low Countries.
- Encirclement design: [[621-the-nazis-at-war-blitzkrieg-part-2-glt8711886512]] connects the Ardennes crossing and Channel turn to trapping Allied forces in Belgium.
- Risk and contingency: [[621-the-nazis-at-war-blitzkrieg-part-2-glt8711886512]] describes long German traffic jams that Allied air power failed to exploit effectively.
