## Overview

晁错 / Chao Cuo is a Western Han centralizing official whose current evidence begins with [[LiuHeng|汉文帝]]'s examination and promotion, then joins protection through privileged access under [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]] to a strategic case for territorial reduction and the later political exposure created by implementing it.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-225-2-weishenme-shuo-xinyuanjieyi-de-zuxian-shi-zhongguoren-lmk2n0ved6gt9tgoxs1qb2s0kuc3|Hanji 225-2]] supplies the earliest current court layer. Liu Heng solicits direct remonstrance through an examination, prefers Chao's answer, and promotes him from crown-prince household office to中大夫. The episode says Chao then submits more than thirty memorials, including proposals to reduce princely territories and change laws; Liu Heng does not fully adopt them but esteems his ability.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-230-shentujia-yu-sha-chaocuo-weihe-fan-bei-qisi-ltwm5zfpdau5p-lmsnnr8wynfzqj|Hanji 230]] supplies the later access-and-protection layer. As inner capital administrator, Chao repeatedly receives private audiences, changes rules with Jingdi's support, and discusses reducing royal power through demotion and punishment. When Shentu Jia prepares to impeach him for opening a passage through a wall adjoining the Former Emperor's temple precinct, Chao reaches Jingdi overnight, admits the act, and secures protection. Jingdi then tells the court that the wall enclosed unused outer ground and that he ordered the work. The episode therefore shows why Chao can initially survive a severe formal accusation: privileged timing and ruler sponsorship precede his later exposure.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-231-hanjingdi-jiuhou-jing-zaojiu-wulong-chuanwei-shijian-2-lg4ybjhj0w8azd-h_krzhibc0clg|Hanji 231 part 2]] supplies the prewar rationale. Chao reportedly urges reduction under both [[LiuHeng|Emperor Wen]] and Jingdi, citing Liu Bi's bereavement, nonattendance, mining, minting, salt production, fugitive recruitment, and presumed revolt planning. His claim is not that削藩 avoids rebellion, but that delay permits a larger and better prepared one. The court then reduces land from Chu, Zhao, and Jiaoxi through offense-based cases; [[DouYingWesternHan|窦婴]]'s disagreement shows that apparent silence among other officials is not full consensus.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-233-chaocuo-bei-yaozhan-jielouchu-zhichang-zhong-de-daji-lql1pwomxc2t-e6x-cdityt1hwxq|Hanji 233]] follows policy into crisis. Chao defends reduction as necessary for court authority, but princely hatred and the rebels' slogan concentrate on him. His advice that Jingdi campaign personally and his effort to prosecute [[YuanAngWesternHan|袁盎]] weaken his protection. Yuan then converts “诛晁错” into an imperial option by proposing execution, pardon, and restoration of reduced territory.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-234-zhouyafu-zhanchang-dalian-hanjingdi-na-lai-de-danzi-li6iqhtuxgq5xwgbn_vb7lljgyxr|Hanji 234]] supplies the outcome test: Liu Bi does not withdraw after Chao's death. The current judgment therefore separates three questions. Chao offers a coherent timing argument about royal capacity, the chosen implementation helps trigger coalition mobilization, and his execution fails as a peace settlement. None of those findings alone settles whether every reduction was necessary, optimal, or inevitable.

## Key Characteristics

- Examination-selected remonstrant promoted by Emperor Wen before becoming a favored centralizing adviser under Jingdi.
- Official who survives a capital accusation by confessing first and receiving explicit imperial sponsorship.
- Strategist who frames the choice as earlier, smaller rebellion versus later, larger rebellion rather than peace versus war.
- Policy advocate who uses kingdom offenses to support successive reductions against Chu, Zhao, and Jiaoxi.
- Visible owner of a coercive program on whom royal resentment and the coalition slogan concentrate.
- Trusted insider whose crisis advice and attack on Yuan Ang further weaken court protection.
- Political sacrifice whose death removes a named grievance but does not end the rebellion.

## Qualifications

The profile rests on five popular-history notes. Chao's inevitability and scale prediction is a strategic counterfactual, not a demonstrable result. Hanji 225-2's examination procedure, offices, memorial count, policy contents, Liu Heng's response, and wording require comparison with transmitted records. Hanji 230's office, private audiences, rule changes, wall and temple geography, legal exposure, leak, confession, dialogue, and imperial authorization require comparison; the episode supports early patronage protection but does not establish that every later policy change was Chao's alone. The exact memorials, royal conduct, copper and salt system, fugitive recruitment, offenses, reductions, court debate, Dou Ying relationship, father's conduct, military advice, prosecution attempt, execution, and later imperial motives likewise remain bounded. The host's workplace frame may illuminate exposure and coalition failure but cannot replace the institutional conflict between central authority and royal autonomy.

## What Changed

- Backfilled Chao's Emperor Wen-era examination success, promotion, and early memorials.
- Extended his arc from selected remonstrant to protected Jingdi adviser, policy owner, sacrifice, and failed peace test.

## Relationships

- [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]] - ruler who adopts direct reductions and later authorizes Chao's execution.
- [[LiuBiWuKing|吴王刘濞]] - royal threat around whom Chao builds his timing argument.
- [[YuanAngWesternHan|袁盎]] - rival who turns the coalition slogan into a proposal to sacrifice Chao.
- [[DouYingWesternHan|窦婴]] - dissenter over reduction and later intermediary for Yuan.
- [[RebellionOfTheSevenStatesWesternHan|七国之乱]] - crisis triggered by reduction and publicly justified through opposition to Chao.
- [[SameSurnameKingEnfeoffment|同姓王分封]] - institutional order whose concentrated territorial power Chao seeks to reduce.
- [[ShentuJiaWesternHan|申屠嘉]] - chancellor whose planned wall-breach prosecution Chao defeats through prior imperial access.
- [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]] - earlier ruler who promotes Chao after an examination and values his policy memorials without fully adopting them.
