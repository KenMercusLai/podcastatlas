## Overview

晁错 / Chao Cuo is a Western Han policy adviser whose current evidence now begins with a detailed frontier-settlement proposal under [[LiuHeng|汉文帝]], continues through examination-based promotion and privileged access, and ends with his territorial-reduction program, political isolation, execution, and the failure of that sacrifice to stop the [[RebellionOfTheSevenStatesWesternHan|Seven States rebellion]].

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-222-chaocuo-xiang-wendi-tichu-de-sanda-qimou-daodi-you-duo-niu-2-li4ia2aijr2ff4u_zwtx3dvl7jj0|Hanji 222 part 2]] supplies the earliest current policy layer. Chao diagnoses a mismatch between fast, observant Xiongnu raiders and expensive distant levies that arrive slowly and never acquire deep local knowledge. His [[FrontierSettlerDefense|frontier settler defense]] proposal asks the court to prepare strategic fortified towns, houses, tools, tax relief, food, clothing, pardons, ranks, medical and ritual services, family support, nested local organization, trusted leaders, rewards, and riding-and-archery training. The design shows Chao treating defense as a combined problem of incentives, settlement persistence, administration, and combat readiness rather than troop numbers alone. Liu Heng reportedly adopts recruitment for frontier settlement.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-225-2-weishenme-shuo-xinyuanjieyi-de-zuxian-shi-zhongguoren-lmk2n0ved6gt9tgoxs1qb2s0kuc3|Hanji 225-2]] then places Chao inside formal court advancement. Liu Heng solicits direct remonstrance through an examination, prefers Chao's answer, and promotes him from crown-prince household office to中大夫. Chao submits more than thirty memorials, including proposals to reduce princely territories and change laws; the emperor does not fully adopt them but values his ability.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-230-shentujia-yu-sha-chaocuo-weihe-fan-bei-qisi-ltwm5zfpdau5p-lmsnnr8wynfzqj|Hanji 230]] supplies the later access-and-protection layer. Under [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]], Chao receives private audiences and support for rule changes. When [[ShentuJiaWesternHan|申屠嘉]] prepares a capital impeachment over a passage opened through a wall adjoining the Former Emperor's temple precinct, Chao reaches Jingdi first, admits the act, and gains explicit imperial protection. Privileged timing and ruler sponsorship explain his initial resilience but also make his policy ownership highly visible.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-231-hanjingdi-jiuhou-jing-zaojiu-wulong-chuanwei-shijian-2-lg4ybjhj0w8azd-h_krzhibc0clg|Hanji 231 part 2]] gives Chao's prewar rationale for reducing royal territories. He cites [[LiuBiWuKing|刘濞]]'s grievance, nonattendance, mining, minting, salt production, fugitive recruitment, and presumed preparation, arguing not that reduction avoids rebellion but that delay allows a larger and better prepared one. The court reduces land from Chu, Zhao, and Jiaoxi through offense-based cases, while [[DouYingWesternHan|窦婴]]'s disagreement shows that silence elsewhere is not full consensus.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-233-chaocuo-bei-yaozhan-jielouchu-zhichang-zhong-de-daji-lql1pwomxc2t-e6x-cdityt1hwxq|Hanji 233]] follows policy into crisis. Chao defends reduction as necessary, but princely hatred and the rebels' slogan concentrate on him. His advice that Jingdi campaign personally and his effort to prosecute [[YuanAngWesternHan|袁盎]] further weaken his protection. Yuan turns “诛晁错” into an imperial option by proposing execution, pardon, and restoration of reduced land.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-234-zhouyafu-zhanchang-dalian-hanjingdi-na-lai-de-danzi-li6iqhtuxgq5xwgbn_vb7lljgyxr|Hanji 234]] supplies the outcome test: Liu Bi does not withdraw after Chao's death. The current judgment therefore separates policy design from political survival. Chao can construct a detailed frontier institution and a coherent timing argument about royal capacity, yet implementation choices, concentrated ownership, crisis advice, and weak collegial protection still leave him available as a sacrifice whose death fails to settle the war.

## Key Characteristics

- Systems-oriented adviser who joins frontier fortification, migration support, welfare, incentives, local organization, and training into one defensive design.
- Examination-selected remonstrant promoted by Emperor Wen and later favored by Jingdi through privileged access.
- Strategist who frames削藩 as earlier, smaller rebellion versus later, larger rebellion rather than peace versus war.
- Visible owner of coercive centralization on whom royal resentment and the coalition slogan concentrate.
- Trusted insider whose crisis advice and attack on Yuan Ang further weaken court protection.
- Political sacrifice whose execution removes a named grievance but does not end the rebellion.

## Qualifications

The profile rests on six popular-history notes rather than independent primary-source collation. Hanji 222 part 2 supports a detailed proposal and an imperial recruitment order, not verified settlement scale, durable implementation, combat effectiveness, fiscal cost, or voluntariness. Its unit sizes, benefits, offices, attack forecast, and later crown-prince relationship remain episode-attributed.

Chao's削藩 inevitability and scale prediction is a strategic counterfactual, not a demonstrable result. The exact memorials, royal conduct, resource systems, offenses, reductions, court debate, wall and temple geography, legal exposure, military advice, prosecution attempt, execution, and imperial motives remain bounded. The host's workplace frame may illuminate exposure and coalition failure but cannot replace the institutional conflict between central authority and royal autonomy.

## What Changed

- Backfilled Chao's earliest current policy layer: a detailed frontier-settlement system under Emperor Wen.
- Reframed his profile from削藩 specialist to a broader institutional designer concerned with incentives, administration, and durable local capacity.
- Preserved the later judgment that coherent strategy did not protect him from concentrated political exposure or make his execution an effective peace settlement.
