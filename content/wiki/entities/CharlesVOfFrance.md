# Charles V of France

## Overview

Charles V of France is the Dauphin and later king who survives France's post-Poitiers breakdown and recovers most English gains by rebuilding revenue, local loyalty, administration, and a strategy that avoids decisive battle.

## Current Profile

As Dauphin during [[JohnIIOfFrance|John II's]] captivity, Charles faces pressure from [[CharlesTheBad|Charles the Bad]], revolutionary Paris, the [[Jacquerie]], free companies, and [[EdwardIIIOfEngland|Edward III's]] invasions. He cannot prevent raids to Paris, but he refuses the most extreme territorial terms and remains politically intact when Edward fails to take Reims or the capital.

After becoming king in 1364, Charles restores order and revenue and wins back local allegiance. He appoints [[BertrandDuGuesclin|Bertrand du Guesclin]] constable for ability rather than pedigree, accepts Aquitanian appeals against [[EdwardTheBlackPrince|the Black Prince]], and uses [[AttritionalReconquest|attritional reconquest]] to recover most English conquests before his death in 1380.

## Key Characteristics

- Politically resilient Dauphin governing during royal captivity and civil disorder.
- Opponent of extreme English settlement terms.
- Restorer of revenue, administration, order, and local loyalty.
- Patron of ability-based command through du Guesclin.
- Strategist who avoids England's preferred open battles.
- Legal and political exploiter of Aquitanian appeals and treaty ambiguity.

- Command and strategy: [[321-hundred-years-war-a-storm-of-swords-part-4-glt1570526345]] links du Guesclin's appointment and avoidance of open battle to French recovery.

## Qualifications

The source compresses Charles's reign and later campaigns into a strategic reversal. The relative weight of administration, taxation, local allegiance, commanders, English weakness, and legal claims remains source-scoped.
