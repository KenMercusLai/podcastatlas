# OpenEvidence

OpenEvidence is the physician-facing medical AI product discussed in [[e227-meiguo-yiliao-shichang-ai-zhengduozhan-jutou-yazhu-chuangye-gongsi-neng-ying-ma-f14f8686-a6e2-47ea-92c1-ca7e71199f67]]. The episode describes it as a tool for doctors that answers from top medical journals, authoritative clinical guidelines, and licensed sources rather than relying only on a general model's latent knowledge.

Its wiki role is to make [[EvidenceGroundedMedicalRAG]] concrete. [[ZhouYebing]] treats the product as a response to ordinary [[AIHallucination]] and weak citation quality in medical answers, while [[ZhangLu]] frames its moat around content authorization, physician adoption, and workflow placement rather than only base-model capability.

[[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-mark-cuban-on-the-ai-bubble-who-actually-gets-wiped-out-42155640]] adds a patient-preparation and personal-use example through [[MarkCuban|Mark Cuban]]. Cuban says OpenEvidence helped him reason about medication and supplement timing, placing the product in the wiki's [[AIHealthManagement]] branch as a tool that can improve questions and context before clinical care without replacing doctors.

## Key Points
- The episode says OpenEvidence's valuation reached about $12 billion and annual revenue was around $100 million.
- Doctors are described as using it for professional knowledge retrieval, with free physician access and revenue coming mainly from medical advertising, content promotion, and possible enterprise versions.
- The product's strongest safety claim is grounded answering with sources, but its strongest business-model concern is whether pharma promotion can bias what doctors see.
- OpenEvidence links the wiki's earlier [[MedicalLiteratureSearch]] branch to current [[RetrievalAugmentedGeneration]] and [[MedicalAIWorkflowIntegration]].
