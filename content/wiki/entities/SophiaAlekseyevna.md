# Sophia Alekseyevna / 索菲娅·阿列克谢耶芙娜

## Overview

Sophia Alekseyevna was the educated half-sister of [[PeterTheGreat|Peter the Great]] who converted the [[MoscowUprising1682|1682 Moscow uprising]] and a divided succession into a joint monarchy under Ivan V and Peter, with herself as regent.

## Current Profile

The episode presents Sophia as an exceptional political actor within a court culture that normally confined royal women. After Fedor III's death, she opposed the Naryshkin-backed elevation of Peter alone, helped press Ivan's claim, and used the armed pressure of the [[Streltsy]] to establish the dual tsardom. Her government then depended on Streltsy commander Shaklovity and the reforming minister [[VasilyGolitsyn|Vasily Golitsyn]].

Sophia and Golitsyn governed effectively in the source's account, but two failed Crimean campaigns weakened their legitimacy. During the 1689 standoff, foreign officers and eventually the Streltsy moved to Peter; Shaklovity was executed, Golitsyn was exiled, and Sophia was confined to a convent.

## Key Characteristics

- She overcame strong gender restrictions to exercise formal regency power.
- Her authority rested on dynastic legitimacy, armed support, and capable ministerial partnership rather than on a separate hereditary claim.
- She used Peter and Ivan's joint monarchy to prevent exclusive Naryshkin control.
- Her government combined court maneuver with selective Westernizing reform.
- Military failure and shifting elite allegiance exposed the fragility of her coalition.

## Evidence

- Succession intervention: [[562-peter-the-great-the-rise-of-russia-part-1-glt5535116202]] links Sophia to Ivan's claim, Streltsy mobilization, and creation of the joint monarchy.
- Regency and reform: [[562-peter-the-great-the-rise-of-russia-part-1-glt5535116202]] presents her partnership with Golitsyn as an effective and Westernizing government.
- Loss of power: [[562-peter-the-great-the-rise-of-russia-part-1-glt5535116202]] follows the 1689 standoff, defection of foreign officers and Streltsy, and Sophia's confinement.

## Qualifications

The episode's account of Sophia's role in rumor, mobilization, and violence is a narrative interpretation rather than an independent archival finding by this wiki. Its claim that she and Golitsyn governed well, and its explanations for why elites preferred Peter in 1689, remain source-scoped.
