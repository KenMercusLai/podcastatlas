## Overview
杜氏女孩 / Du Family Girls names the three Northeast Catholic girls whose vernacular letters to [[FatherLinNortheastMission|林神父]] anchor [[100-nian-qian-dongbei-nvhai-xie-gei-bali-de-xin-duitan-lishixuezhe-liji-putongren-ruhe-chuanguo-da-lishi-3d36f181-94ad-4883-8db3-5f040b5ed45b]].

## Current Profile
The episode presents them through small names or household rankings such as 杜小二妞, 杜小大子, and 杜小十一, plus Christian names whose exact formal identities are not fully settled in the note. Their letters matter because rural girls with little chance of entering elite history used church literacy and Catholic language to express longing, gender constraint, religious aspiration, and resistance to a changed arrangement after the priest returned to Paris.

## Key Characteristics
- Their written traces are rare because ordinary rural girls were usually less likely than men or elites to leave named documents.
- Their letters mix vernacular Chinese, family-like small names, Catholic vocabulary, and direct emotional language.
- Their literacy came through church education, especially catechism and religious language rather than secular schooling.
- Their gender position is explicit: one line says that if the writer were not female, she would go to the priest for instruction.
- Their story became researchable through archives, fieldwork, genealogy, and descendant contact rather than through a single complete official record.
