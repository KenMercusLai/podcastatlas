# Tyre / 推罗

## Overview

Tyre / 推罗 is the Levantine maritime city presented as the parent of [[Carthage]] and a major center of Phoenician trade, production, religious exchange, myth, and writing.

## Current Profile

The episode explains Tyre's orientation through geography. An island stronghold, fresh water, natural harbors, fertile nearby land, and the barrier of Mount Lebanon made the sea both protection and opportunity. After surviving the Bronze Age collapse, Tyre expanded into space left by weakened regional powers and used ships, skilled processing, and luxury production to serve larger markets.

Biblical and Greek traditions make the city both valued partner and troubling outsider. Hiram supplies timber, craftsmen, and materials to David and Solomon; Jezebel turns Tyrian religion into a hostile biblical memory; and Europa, Cadmus, and Herodotus place Tyre near the imagined beginnings of Europe, Greek writing, and Mediterranean history. These traditions show cultural debt and rivalry but do not function as neutral contemporary records.

Tyre's remembered kinship with Carthage persists in the [[Dido]] story and in Herodotus's report that Tyrian sailors refused a Persian order to attack Carthage. The episode therefore treats city identity as primary without excluding a broader [[PhoenicianIdentityDebate|Phoenician cultural field]].

## Key Characteristics

- Defensible island city with harbors, water, and a strong maritime orientation.
- Producer and processor of luxury goods, especially Tyrian purple.
- Trading center connecting Levantine resources to Mediterranean and wider exchange.
- Biblical partner of Israelite kings and target of hostile religious memory.
- Mythic source of Dido, Europa, Cadmus, and the Carthaginian kinship tradition.
- Major node in Phoenician settlement and alphabet transmission.

## Qualifications

The source reconstructs Tyre through biblical, Greek, archaeological, and modern scholarly frames rather than an intact Tyrian archive. Hiram's trade, Ophir and Tarshish, Herodotus's temple visit, Persian-war conduct, myths, ethnic categories, voyage distances, and causal claims about geography remain source-scoped or contested.

## What Changed

- Established Tyre as the parent-city context needed to interpret Carthage before Roman rivalry.
- Joined geography, production, trade, religion, myth, and writing into one current profile.
- Made city-first identity compatible with, but not identical to, broader Phoenician culture.
