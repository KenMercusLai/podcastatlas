# Trojan Horse

## Overview

The Trojan Horse is the stratagem credited to [[Odysseus]] that brings Troy down before the wandering sequence of [[TheOdyssey]] begins.

## Current Profile

In [[687-the-odyssey-hero-of-the-trojan-horse-part-1-glt2393125423]], the horse functions as Odysseus' first major sign of tactical intelligence. The episode starts after the Achaeans have destroyed Troy and identifies the horse as the decisive trick that made that destruction possible, setting up Odysseus as a hero whose brilliance is bound to deception.

## Key Characteristics

- The horse is a war-ending deception rather than a direct battlefield victory.
- It establishes Odysseus' reputation for invention, cunning, and trickery before his voyage home.
- It links [[TheIliad]]'s Trojan War world to [[TheOdyssey]]'s aftermath and return plot.
- It makes the later danger of Odysseus' pride sharper because his identity has already been built around successful stratagem.

## Evidence

- Decisive stratagem: [[687-the-odyssey-hero-of-the-trojan-horse-part-1-glt2393125423]] credits Odysseus with the Trojan Horse and says it brings Troy down.
- Character signal: [[687-the-odyssey-hero-of-the-trojan-horse-part-1-glt2393125423]] introduces Odysseus as a minor king distinguished by courage, intelligence, inventiveness, and trickery.
- Narrative bridge: [[687-the-odyssey-hero-of-the-trojan-horse-part-1-glt2393125423]] begins the return story from Troy's fall, making the horse the precondition for the voyage.

## Qualifications

The page records the horse only as this episode uses it. It does not attempt to reconstruct every ancient source or later adaptation of the Trojan Horse story.
