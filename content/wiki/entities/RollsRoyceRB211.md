# Rolls-Royce RB211

The Rolls-Royce RB211 is the aircraft-engine program at the center of [[vol-268-liang-ge-lao-si-lai-si-1003563933]]. The episode presents it as a technically ambitious attempt by [[RollsRoyce|Rolls-Royce]] to enter the U.S. wide-body civil-aircraft market through an exclusive engine contract for the [[LockheedL1011TriStar|Lockheed L-1011 TriStar]].

The program matters because its technical promise and commercial terms reinforced each other in the wrong direction. Rolls-Royce accepted higher thrust requirements, fixed prices, delay penalties, three-shaft architecture, and carbon-fiber fan blades at a time when the engineering uncertainty was still high. When the carbon-fiber blades failed bird-strike testing and costs rose, RB211 became a source case for [[FixedPriceEngineeringRisk]].

The episode's long-run reading is not that RB211 was simply a bad engine. After rescue and redesign, it became a strategically valuable civil aero-engine line, especially once fuel efficiency became more important after the 1973 oil shock. The failure was in matching an immature technology program to contract, schedule, and balance-sheet constraints the company could not absorb alone.
