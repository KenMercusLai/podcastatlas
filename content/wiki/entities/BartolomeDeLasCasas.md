# Bartolomé de las Casas

## Overview

Bartolomé de las Casas was a Spanish Catholic writer and former participant in conquest who became a prominent defender of Indigenous people and critic of colonial violence, enslavement, and abuse.

## Current Profile

Across [[309-columbus-villain-or-hero-part-4-glt6751575003]] and [[308-columbus-death-in-the-caribbean-part-3-glt3250512968]], Las Casas matters primarily as evidence that severe criticism of Spanish conquest did not begin in modern politics. His witness helps root [[ColumbusLegacyContestation]] in an early Spanish and Catholic argument over violence, rape, enslavement, forced labor, and Indigenous destruction.

The second-voyage episode adds his movement from opposition to Indigenous enslavement toward a wider attack on slavery as an institution. It also uses his claim that Columbus destroyed two-thirds of Hispaniola's population as an example of testimony with great moral and historiographical importance whose quantitative precision remains disputed.

The episode treats his critical tradition as morally important without making him a complete or unmediated voice for Indigenous experience. His role strengthens [[ContextualExplanationWithoutExoneration]] because it shows that Columbus's historical setting contained arguments against the practices being explained.

## Key Characteristics

- Former participant in conquest who became a critic of its treatment of Indigenous people.
- Spanish Catholic voice within, rather than outside, the colonial world he condemned.
- Witness used to establish a long critical tradition around conquest, enslavement, and forced labor.
- Mediated historical source rather than a substitute for missing written Taíno perspectives.
- Important counterexample to claims that moral criticism of Columbus is purely anachronistic.
- Critic whose demographic claims and changing position on slavery require source-specific qualification.

## Evidence

- Critical conversion: [[309-columbus-villain-or-hero-part-4-glt6751575003]] describes Las Casas as moving from participation in conquest toward defense of Indigenous people.
- Contemporary-rooted moral dispute: [[309-columbus-villain-or-hero-part-4-glt6751575003]] uses Spanish and Catholic criticism to show that violence and enslavement were disputed within the period.
- Memory afterlife: [[309-columbus-villain-or-hero-part-4-glt6751575003]] notes that later Protestant narratives amplified the Spanish critical tradition by connecting it to Catholic oppression.
- Slavery and population testimony: [[308-columbus-death-in-the-caribbean-part-3-glt3250512968]] presents his widening anti-slavery position and qualifies his claim about the destruction of Hispaniola's population.

## Qualifications

The current wiki evidence supplies only a compact account of Las Casas and does not independently evaluate his full biography, writings, reforms, representativeness, demographic estimates, or changing positions. His testimony is mediated and cannot replace Indigenous-authored evidence. The episodes also distinguish condemnation of catastrophic exploitation from a narrower claim about Columbus possessing an intent to exterminate.

## What Changed

- Added his widening opposition from Indigenous enslavement toward slavery as an institution.
- Qualified his Hispaniola population claim without weakening its evidence of contemporary moral indictment.
