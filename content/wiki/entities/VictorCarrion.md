# Victor Carrión

## Overview
Victor Carrión is the Stanford child and adolescent psychiatrist interviewed in [[how-to-heal-from-post-traumatic-stress-disorder-ptsd-dr-victor-carrion-scim6456251481|How to Heal From Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD)]]. The episode presents him as a clinician-researcher connecting developmental trauma, stress physiology, brain development, psychotherapy, school prevention, and organoid research.

## Current Profile
In the bounded source, Carrión's distinctive contribution is a developmental and cue-centered account of PTSD. He argues that children are simultaneously vulnerable because of dependence and plasticity and capable of recovery when supportive environments, individualized tools, and psychotherapy help them replace old danger responses with context-appropriate alternatives.

His clinical framework moves from recognition to agency. A child learns how stress works, identifies personal cues, notices thoughts, emotions, body sensations, and actions, and assembles a toolbox of strategies that can lower arousal before more demanding reflection or trauma narrative work. His research program spans cortisol timing, hippocampal and frontolimbic function, school yoga and mindfulness, treatment trials, epigenetics, and cortisol-exposed neural organoids.

## Key Characteristics
- Specializes in child and adolescent post-traumatic stress while connecting the framework to adult PTSD.
- Distinguishes neutral trauma cues from current threats and treats cue recognition as a route to self-understanding.
- Developed a multimodal cue-centered therapy model combining CBT-related tools, insight, self-efficacy, empowerment, and paced narrative work.
- Treats freeze and dissociation as initially protective developmental responses that can become impairing when rigid or exclusive.
- Uses cortisol, imaging, sleep, and organoid work to investigate biological correlates without presenting them as standalone diagnosis.
- Pursues school-based yoga and mindfulness as prevention alongside clinician-delivered treatment for impairing symptoms.
- Defines recovery through adaptation, belonging, supportive adults, agency, and careful listening rather than presumed childhood resilience.

- Therapy design - [[how-to-heal-from-post-traumatic-stress-disorder-ptsd-dr-victor-carrion-scim6456251481]] attributes to him the cue-centered toolbox, four-corner square, emotional thermometer, positive-thought practice, and narrative-after-preparation sequence.
- Developmental psychiatry - [[how-to-heal-from-post-traumatic-stress-disorder-ptsd-dr-victor-carrion-scim6456251481]] records his warning that pediatric PTSD may resemble ADHD and that children can be both vulnerable and capable of plastic recovery.
