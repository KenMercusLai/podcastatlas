# Simon Sudbury

## Overview

Simon Sudbury was Archbishop of Canterbury and chancellor of England, targeted and executed during the [[PeasantsRevolt1381|Peasants’ Revolt of 1381]] as a symbol of ecclesiastical and governmental oppression.

## Current Profile

The revolt reached Sudbury through both office and property. Rebels sacked Lambeth Palace, his London residence, and burned chancery rolls and other legal material. The attack joined hostility toward an archbishop to rejection of the records and institutions through which government and lordship operated.

After [[RichardIIOfEngland|Richard II]] left the Tower for the Mile End meeting, rebels entered the supposedly secure fortress and seized Sudbury, treasurer Robert Hales, John Legge, and John of Gaunt’s physician. They executed Sudbury and displayed his severed head on London Bridge. His death exemplifies the rising’s capacity to combine named political accountability, religious hostility, and summary violence.

## Key Characteristics

- Archbishop whose church authority overlapped with high royal office.
- Chancellor associated with administrative records attacked by the rebels.
- Named “traitor” figure sought from the Tower rather than an accidental victim of disorder.
- Victim whose execution fused political grievance with religious and institutional violence.

## Evidence

- Institutional targeting: [[414-the-peasants-revolt-londons-burning-part-2-glt6726049231]] links the sack of Lambeth Palace to the burning of chancery and legal records.
- Tower execution: [[414-the-peasants-revolt-londons-burning-part-2-glt6726049231]] records Sudbury’s seizure, beheading, and display on London Bridge.
- Rebel program: [[414-the-peasants-revolt-londons-burning-part-2-glt6726049231]] places his death within demands for the surrender of supposed traitors and later attacks on church hierarchy.

## Qualifications

The source uses Sudbury mainly as a target within the revolt and does not provide a full account of his ecclesiastical career, administrative responsibility, or individual role in the poll tax. Entry into the Tower may have depended on persuasion or inside help, but the mechanism is unresolved.

## What Changed

- Established Sudbury as a combined religious, governmental, and documentary target of the revolt.
