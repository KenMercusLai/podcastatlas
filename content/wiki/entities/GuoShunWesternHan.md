## Overview

郭舜 / Guo Shun enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-550-sha-diguo-taizi-xiatui-jiqian-zhuibing-ta-zenme-zuodao-de-lvzhcjutqnu4or4elds_qg4s1gto|Hanji 550]] as the Western Han protector-general voice arguing that [[Kangju|康居]] should be treated as an unreliable and disrespectful counterpart rather than rewarded for a hostage mission.

## Current Profile

The episode presents Guo Shun as an official reading diplomatic protocol as strategic evidence. Kangju has sent a prince to serve in Chang'an and brings tribute, but Guo argues that this should not be mistaken for real submission. He points to Kangju's refusal to bow to Han envoys, its seating of Han protectorate officials below envoys from other states, and banquet order in which Kangju elites eat before Han officials.

Guo's policy recommendation is rupture with public explanation: send the Kangju prince back, stop dispatching envoys, and declare that Han will not maintain relations with a state that violates ritual respect. The court does not accept the hard line, choosing continued accommodation because this is Kangju's first prince-hostage mission. Guo therefore functions as a diagnostic voice whose warning is preserved even though the final policy favors flexibility.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han frontier official who treats diplomatic ceremony as evidence of real hierarchy.
- Argues that Kangju's hostage and tribute behavior may be extractive rather than submissive.
- Recommends cutting relations and returning Kangju's prince-hostage.
- Loses the immediate policy debate to a court preference for first-contact accommodation.
- Links Wusun, Kangju, and Xiongnu as mutually watchful powers rather than stable dependents.
