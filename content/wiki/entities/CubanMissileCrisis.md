# Cuban Missile Crisis

## Overview

The Cuban Missile Crisis was the 1962 confrontation over Soviet nuclear missiles in Cuba, presented by this source as the strongest example of [[JohnFKennedy|John F. Kennedy]]'s Cold War crisis management.

## Current Profile

The episode emphasizes decision process and restraint. Kennedy convened ExComm, listened across competing advice, resisted demands for immediate air strikes, and selected a naval blockade. The settlement removed Soviet missiles from Cuba and quietly removed American missiles from Turkey. In the hosts' assessment, Kennedy's willingness to avoid a public maximalist victory was central to averting nuclear war.

Within the assassination series, the crisis also tests proposed military motives. The source argues that Kennedy was not weak toward the Soviet Union and suggests that most senior military figures would have recognized the outcome as competent, making a simple story of military rage less persuasive than some conspiracy accounts claim.

## Key Characteristics

- Superpower confrontation over Soviet nuclear missiles deployed in Cuba.
- Deliberative presidential process conducted through ExComm.
- Rejection of immediate air strikes in favor of a naval blockade.
- Settlement involving public Soviet withdrawal from Cuba and quiet U.S. withdrawal from Turkey.
- Case in which restraint and face-saving compromise reduced nuclear escalation risk.

## Qualifications

This page reflects one podcast episode's high-level reconstruction, not the complete diplomatic, military, intelligence, Cuban, Soviet, or Turkish record. It does not allocate all credit for avoiding war, establish every participant's private view, or prove that military officers uniformly approved of Kennedy. The exact counterfactual effects of alternative decisions remain unresolved.
