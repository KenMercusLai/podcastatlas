# Louvre

## Overview
The Louvre appears in [[667-the-mystery-of-the-mona-lisa-glt1735270133]] as the museum setting that turned [[MonaLisa|the Mona Lisa]] into a public object, a theft victim, and finally a mass-tourism destination.

## Current Profile
The source traces the painting's movement from French royal possession into the Louvre after the [[FrenchRevolution|French Revolution]]. It also emphasizes that the painting did not instantly become the supreme museum treasure: before its modern fame, it spent time in less prominent settings, including an obscure corridor at Versailles and briefly Napoleon's bedroom.

## Key Characteristics
- The Louvre gives the Mona Lisa its modern museum-public setting.
- The 1911 theft converted the museum space itself into a spectacle, with visitors coming to see the empty frame.
- The painting's post-theft return intensified its status as a high-art symbol.
- The museum now functions as part of the painting's mass-tourism meaning.
- A dedicated underground gallery planned for 2031 is presented as the next stage of institutional accommodation.

## Evidence
- Museum transition - [[667-the-mystery-of-the-mona-lisa-glt1735270133]] says the painting moved to the Louvre after the French Revolution, with a temporary Napoleonic bedroom episode before public display.
- Theft spectacle - [[667-the-mystery-of-the-mona-lisa-glt1735270133]] says the 1911 theft left an empty frame that drew crowds.
- Tourism pressure - [[667-the-mystery-of-the-mona-lisa-glt1735270133]] says the Mona Lisa is now the most popular object in any museum and that many Louvre visitors go specifically to see it.

## Qualifications
The source focuses on the Mona Lisa, not the Louvre's whole institutional history. The 2031 gallery claim is source-scoped to the episode's account of future planning.

## What Changed
- Created the Louvre as the museum-institution node for the Mona Lisa branch.
