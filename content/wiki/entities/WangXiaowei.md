## Overview
王小伟 / Wang Xiaowei is the Renmin University philosophy professor and artificial-intelligence governance researcher interviewed by [[BuHeShiYi|不合时宜]] about AI, technology philosophy, intimacy, work, emotion, education, and everyday life.

## Current Profile
The current wiki profile is source-scoped to one public podcast conversation. Wang is introduced as a professor in the School of Philosophy at [[RenminUniversityOfChina|中国人民大学]] and a researcher at an artificial-intelligence governance institute. In the episode she uses [[TechnologyPhilosophy|technology philosophy]] to ask how AI changes not only productivity, but the conditions of love, judgment, education, work meaning, and daily life.

Her position is balanced rather than anti-AI. She uses AI herself for reflection, argument, and self-analysis, and she recognizes that AI companionship can matter for lonely or vulnerable adults. Her caution is that smoothness, responsiveness, and optimization may hide losses in reciprocity, bodily co-presence, life rhythm, independent judgment, and ordinary human support.

## Key Characteristics
- Public philosopher applying technology philosophy to ordinary AI-era life rather than only to technical capability.
- Treats AI companionship as ethically complex: useful in some conditions, but different from human intimacy because human recognition carries vulnerability, effort, and mortality.
- Warns that large models can become [[AIJudgmentInfrastructure|judgment infrastructure]] when people use them for intimate, family, educational, and moral advice.
- Connects modern work anxiety to the loss of stable collective narrative and the pressure to use work as self-realization.
- Defends friction, waiting, boredom, body, friendship, and non-optimized time as part of living rather than as inefficiencies to eliminate.
