# BlackBerry Messenger

## Overview

BlackBerry Messenger, or BBM, was [[BlackBerry]]'s mobile messaging service and the asset at the center of [[JimBalsillie]]'s proposed shift from handsets to cross-platform communications infrastructure.

## Current Profile

The episode presents BBM in 2011 as a large existing network rather than an untested startup idea. Balsillie says it had about 80 million users, more than the emerging [[WhatsApp]], and that two major US carriers had committed to bundle a cross-platform version with wireless subscriptions. He had spent roughly eighteen months adding storage, rich media, voice-over-IP, groups, and delivery-status features.

The proposed “SMS 2.0” model would let customers moving to iOS or Android retain their contacts and messaging identity. That made BBM a plausible route from device manufacturer to service layer, but the board cancelled the initiative before launch, so its competitive durability and economics remain unknowable.

## Key Characteristics

- Large incumbent messaging network reportedly serving about 80 million users in 2011.
- Planned cross-platform support intended to survive customer movement to iOS and Android.
- Expanded feature set including storage, rich media, voice, groups, and delivery status.
- Proposed carrier-bundled distribution through mobile subscriptions.
- Strategic tension between service reach and protection of BlackBerry handset differentiation.

## Qualifications

The user count, carrier commitments, launch timing, and feature readiness are episode-reported and not independently verified in the supplied file. Network size does not prove future retention, interoperability, monetization, product quality, or success against WhatsApp and platform-owned messaging services. Because the plan was cancelled, claims of certain success are counterfactual.

## What Changed

- Created a bounded product profile for BBM as the proposed post-handset core of BlackBerry.
- Separated observable scale and preparation from untestable claims of inevitable success.
