# Boston Massacre

## Overview

The Boston Massacre was the 1770 shooting in which British soldiers killed five Bostonians amid an urban confrontation shaped by military occupation, economic friction, crowd action, and competing propaganda.

## Current Profile

The episode presents the event as both violence and a struggle over description. British accounts stressed stones, cudgels, and self-defense; colonial accounts emphasized snowballs and military brutality. [[CrispusAttucks|Crispus Attucks]], described as a Black and Native dockworker, became central to the confrontation but disappeared from Paul Revere's famous image. [[JohnAdams|John Adams]] defended the soldiers to demonstrate due process, using racialized and ethnic language while securing acquittals for most defendants.

## Key Characteristics

- Fatal confrontation between British troops and Boston inhabitants.
- Product of sustained tension between soldiers, workers, merchants, and political crowds.
- Event immediately contested through incompatible self-defense and brutality narratives.
- Founding-memory episode shaped by visual omission and racialized legal argument.
- Escalatory symbol that did not make independence inevitable in 1770.

## Evidence

- Urban conditions: [[347-the-american-revolution-part-1-glt3042850673]] links the shooting to troops' arrival, poor relations, low military pay, smuggling enforcement, economic distress, and Boston's political networks.
- Contested action: [[347-the-american-revolution-part-1-glt3042850673]] preserves different British and colonial accounts of what the crowd threw and why soldiers fired.
- Trial and memory: [[347-the-american-revolution-part-1-glt3042850673]] records Adams's defense, Attucks's role and omission from Revere's image, two manslaughter convictions, and the other acquittals.

## Qualifications

The page follows one retrospective podcast summary rather than the complete trial record. Crowd size, exact sequence, individual responsibility, Attucks's biography, contemporary reporting, and later symbolic use require broader evidence.

## What Changed

- Established the event as a contested legal, racial, military, and propaganda episode rather than a simple inevitable step to independence.
