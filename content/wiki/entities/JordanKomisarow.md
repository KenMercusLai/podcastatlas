# Jordan Komisarow

## Overview

Jordan Komisarow is a Duke trauma neurosurgeon presented as a modern clinical interpreter of [[RobertFKennedy|Robert F. Kennedy]]'s fatal 1968 gunshot injury.

## Current Profile

Komisarow and colleagues reviewed Kennedy's autopsy material and the treating surgeons' testimony, then compared the delayed transfer and 1968 operation with a contemporary Level I trauma pathway. His reconstruction emphasizes direct specialist transport, rapid CT and vascular imaging, coordinated open and endovascular work, controlled removal of damaged tissue, decompression, and improved management of swelling and infection.

He does not guarantee survival. The episode presents him as making a conditional counterfactual: modern treatment would make early death less likely and offers plausible survival paths, but the precise bullet trajectory and brain and vascular damage remain decisive.

## Key Characteristics

- Trauma neurosurgeon applying contemporary practice to a historical injury.
- Reviewer of Kennedy autopsy material and treating-surgeon testimony.
- Emphasizes rapid imaging and simultaneous multidisciplinary intervention.
- Distinguishes plausible survival from complete neurological recovery.
- Preserves uncertainty around trajectory, vascular damage, and outcome.

## Evidence

### Historical reconstruction

- [[guns-part-4-moral-hazard]] describes the review of Kennedy's records, his initial arrival at a hospital without a neurosurgeon, transfer, and operation roughly two and a half hours after the shooting.

### Contemporary pathway

- [[guns-part-4-moral-hazard]] attributes to Komisarow a modern sequence involving Level I activation, CT and angiographic imaging, coordinated vascular and neurosurgical work, decompression, and improved infection and swelling control.

## Qualifications

This is a retrospective counterfactual based on an episode summary, not a prospective prognosis or full case review available in the wiki. Modern capability cannot erase uncertainty about tissue destruction, vascular injury, complications, disability, or long-term recovery. A plausible survival route is not proof that Kennedy would have survived.
