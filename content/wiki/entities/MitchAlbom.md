# Mitch Albom

## Overview

Mitch Albom is a writer and humanitarian presented in the source as a moral witness for [[VoluntaryPayItForwardEducation]], drawing on Morrie Schwartz's teaching about giving and Albom's long involvement with a Haitian orphanage.

## Current Profile

Albom's role is analogical rather than financial. He argues that giving can make the giver feel more alive, then connects that principle to years of recurring work in Haiti after the 2010 earthquake. The orphanage's expectation that older children care for younger ones and supported graduates return for service turns reciprocity into continuing responsibility rather than repayment of a fixed monetary debt.

Four students from the orphanage were attending [[HopeCollege]] in the source's account, creating a direct connection between the service community and [[HopeForward]]. Their example broadens the program's moral claim but does not establish its financial sustainability.

## Key Characteristics

- Connects generosity to the giver's sense of vitality and purpose.
- Returned repeatedly to Haiti and recruited volunteers for practical rebuilding and care work.
- Presents reciprocity as care for others after receiving care oneself.
- Treats service, mentorship, and community responsibility as valid forms of paying opportunity forward.

## Evidence

### Giving ethic

- [[a-good-circle-a-good-circle]] traces Albom's formulation to visits with Morrie Schwartz during Schwartz's terminal illness.

### Sustained service

- [[a-good-circle-a-good-circle]] reports monthly travel to Haiti over thirteen years and volunteer construction of kitchens, showers, and other facilities.

### Reciprocal care

- [[a-good-circle-a-good-circle]] describes older children caring for younger ones and supported graduates returning for two years of orphanage work.

## Qualifications

This profile rests on one structured podcast summary rather than Albom's records, the orphanage's policies, participant testimony, or an independent program evaluation. Travel frequency, service arrangements, graduate expectations, dialogue, motives, and outcomes remain source-scoped.

- Established Albom's initial profile through his giving ethic and sustained Haitian service.
- Distinguished his reciprocity example from evidence that Hope Forward is financially viable.
