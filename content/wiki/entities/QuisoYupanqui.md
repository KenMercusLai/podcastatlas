# Quiso Yupanqui

## Overview

Quiso Yupanqui was an Inca commander who destroyed Spanish relief columns in mountain ambushes but was defeated and killed when he attacked Lima on terrain favorable to cavalry.

## Current Profile

[[648-the-fall-of-the-incas-battle-for-the-sacred-city-part-5-glt6392035011]] presents Quiso Yupanqui as the clearest example of terrain-dependent military advantage during Manco's uprising. In the Andes, his forces use rocks and boulders to destroy several columns sent toward besieged Cusco; near Lima, flat ground allows Spanish cavalry to break his army and kill him with many commanders.

His campaign connects [[SiegeOfCusco1536|the siege]] to the wider attempt to isolate Spanish centers. It also shows why neither technology nor numbers alone explains the result: mobility, terrain, coalition support, and the sustainability of forces changed the balance from one theater to another.

## Key Characteristics

- Inca field commander operating beyond Cusco.
- Effective user of mountain ambushes against Spanish relief forces.
- Leader of the offensive against Lima.
- Commander whose defeat illustrates Spanish cavalry's terrain dependence.
- Participant in a coordinated attempt to isolate multiple Spanish centers.

## Evidence

- Mountain victories: [[648-the-fall-of-the-incas-battle-for-the-sacred-city-part-5-glt6392035011]] credits Quiso with destroying several relief columns using rock and boulder ambushes.
- Lima campaign: [[648-the-fall-of-the-incas-battle-for-the-sacred-city-part-5-glt6392035011]] follows his march to Lima and defeat by cavalry on open ground.
- Strategic connection: [[648-the-fall-of-the-incas-battle-for-the-sacred-city-part-5-glt6392035011]] places the campaign inside the broader effort to prevent relief of Cusco.

## Qualifications

Troop totals, casualties, exact routes, command structure, and the relative contribution of Pizarro's Indigenous allies remain source-scoped. A terrain-centered explanation clarifies the contrast between campaigns but does not exhaust the causes of either outcome.

## What Changed

- Established Quiso's mountain campaign as a major threat to Spanish communications.
- Distinguished the conditions favoring Inca ambushes from those favoring cavalry.
