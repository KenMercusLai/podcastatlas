## Overview

上官太后 / Empress Dowager Shangguan enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-424-huoguang-qushi-hou-huojia-shi-ruhe-fumie-de-ltisydawuqdhqpffdjv_xkwts90p|Hanji 424]] as the empress dowager whose banquet authority is to be used in the Huo family's failed plan to kill major ministers and depose [[EmperorXuanOfHan|汉宣帝]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-396-1-12-sui-huangdi-yu-6-sui-huanghou-shui-ganchu-de-huangtang-shier-lnq-puxt0zdxhfm7pwilxellccdr|Hanji 396-1]] now supplies the origin layer: at six, she is pushed into [[EmperorZhaoOfHan|汉昭帝刘弗陵]]'s palace by [[ShangguanJieWesternHan|上官桀]] and [[ShangguanAnWesternHan|上官安]] through [[DingWairenWesternHan|丁外人]] and [[GaiChanggongzhuWesternHan|鄂邑 / 盖长公主]], after [[HuoGuangWesternHan|霍光]] initially refuses. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-404-hanzhaodi-liufuling-zhendeshi-bei-huoguang-haiside-lu3adnqvomeco0tsoviuqwedjpfs|Hanji 404]] adds her later role as Han Zhao's empress and Huo Guang's granddaughter, making her childlessness part of the succession crisis before Liu He's accession. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-400-1-taidula-hanzhaodi-jing-bipo-gege-yanwang-liudan-zisha-ln0-b64ip2kwpcpen_65e4du3vqc|Hanji 400-1]] adds the family-disaster layer: after Shangguan Jie and Shangguan An are destroyed for rebellion, she is spared because the source presents her as young, uninvolved, and politically protected by her relation to Huo Guang.

## Current Profile

The source set gives Shangguan Taihou a procedural profile rather than a full biography. Hanji 396-1 shows her first as a child acted upon by family strategy: Shangguan Jie and Shangguan An want her in the palace because empress status would lift their branch, while Huo Guang resists the plan before being bypassed through Ding Wairen and the princess. Hanji 404 then shows the later palace structure around her: as Huo Guang's granddaughter and Han Zhao's empress, her potential to bear an heir is favored through Huo's control of the harem, including restrictions meant to prevent other palace women from producing a rival child. The episode does not present her as the decision-maker in that policy, but her position gives Huo a reason to control the emperor's reproductive environment.

Hanji 400-1 adds that Shangguan's early palace position survives a family catastrophe. The source says she is not deposed after the Shangguan plot because she is too young to have participated and remains Huo Guang's granddaughter. Hanji 424 then shows her later institutional role as empress dowager. In the Huo plan, she would host a banquet for Boping Jun Wang Ao, with chancellor Wei Xiang, Xu Guanghan, and other figures summoned as attendants. Huo-linked actors then planned to use commands in her name to kill key ministers and replace Xuan with Huo Yu.

Her importance in these sources is therefore institutional. As empress, she sits at the intersection of harem access, childbearing, and Huo-family power. As empress dowager, her space, invitation, and command language could make a coup look like palace procedure long enough for targeted violence to occur.

## Key Characteristics

- Child palace entrant, Huo Guang's granddaughter, and Han Zhao's empress, whose marriage value is created by Shangguan ambition and whose childlessness helps create the succession vacuum after Han Zhao dies.
- Palace figure around whom Huo Guang's harem-access controls are organized, though the source does not show her directing those controls.
- Young empress spared after her father and grandfather's rebellion because the source treats her as uninvolved.
- Empress-dowager figure whose banquet setting is central to the Huo conspiracy plan.
- Palace-authority channel because commands in her name could legitimize sudden action against ministers.
- Source-scoped procedural actor rather than a fully reconstructed biography.
- Evidence that harem and dowager institutions could shape succession and be imagined as coup infrastructure.

## Qualifications

This page is source-scoped to Hanji 396-1, Hanji 400-1, Hanji 404, and Hanji 424. It does not reconstruct Shangguan Taihou's full life, later empress-installation procedure, agency in Huo Guang's harem policy, agency in the Huo plan, or later status.
