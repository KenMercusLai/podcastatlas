# Charles Zuker

## Overview
Charles Zuker is presented in the full and condensed [[HubermanLab]] interviews, [[the-biology-of-taste-perception-sugar-craving-dr-charles-zuker-scim7979038386]] and [[essentials-the-biology-of-taste-perception-sugar-craving-dr-charles-zuker-scim4352602150]], as a sensory neuroscientist whose laboratory studies how receptor and neural circuits convert taste and internal bodily signals into perception and behavior.

## Current Profile
In the source, Zuker uses taste as a compact experimental system for linking molecular detection to subjective identity, value, and action. The laboratory program is credited with helping identify receptors for sweet, sour, bitter, salty, and umami and with circuit experiments that dissociate recognizing a taste from finding it attractive or aversive.

His account also extends beyond the tongue. Receptor-knockout and post-ingestive preference experiments distinguish immediate sweet liking from delayed nutritive sugar wanting, placing gut glucose sensing and vagal signaling alongside cortical taste perception. He frames obesity and overconsumption partly as nervous-system and body-brain communication problems, while the episode leaves clinical translation unresolved.

## Key Characteristics
- Studies perception by tracing sensory signals from receptors through defined neural circuits to behavior.
- Uses taste because a small set of basic inputs supports experiments on identity, valence, learning, memory, internal state, and decision-making.
- Distinguishes stimulus detection from perception and taste quality from positive or negative value.
- Treats innate sensory programs as modifiable by learning, association, context, and physiological need.
- Separates conscious sweet liking from post-ingestive nutrient reinforcement and sugar wanting.
- Presents gut-brain circuitry as a potential intervention target without claiming an established treatment.

## Evidence
- Research scope - [[the-biology-of-taste-perception-sugar-craving-dr-charles-zuker-scim7979038386]] introduces Zuker's laboratory program across taste, vision, thirst, and perception.
- Taste circuit program - [[the-biology-of-taste-perception-sugar-craving-dr-charles-zuker-scim7979038386]] attributes receptor and circuit experiments to his laboratory, while [[essentials-the-biology-of-taste-perception-sugar-craving-dr-charles-zuker-scim4352602150]] reiterates the fast tongue-to-cortex pathway and taste-quality mapping.
- Plasticity and state - Both [[the-biology-of-taste-perception-sugar-craving-dr-charles-zuker-scim7979038386]] and [[essentials-the-biology-of-taste-perception-sugar-craving-dr-charles-zuker-scim4352602150]] use learned liking, desensitization, and salt deprivation to show that behavioral value is not a fixed relay.
- Gut-brain extension - [[the-biology-of-taste-perception-sugar-craving-dr-charles-zuker-scim7979038386]] and [[essentials-the-biology-of-taste-perception-sugar-craving-dr-charles-zuker-scim4352602150]] both describe sweet-receptor-knockout mice learning to prefer sugar and an intestinal sugar-to-vagus pathway.
