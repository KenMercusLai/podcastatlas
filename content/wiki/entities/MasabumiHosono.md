# Masabumi Hosono

## Overview

Masabumi Hosono was a Japanese civil servant and second-class [[RMSTitanic|Titanic]] passenger whose survival became a case of transnational shame in [[432-titanic-the-survivors-part-6-glt4613169046]].

## Current Profile

The episode says Hosono had been studying the Russian railway system and entered lifeboat 13 after a sailor called for someone to fill a spare place. Although he had resolved not to behave dishonorably, he was ostracized after returning to Japan and lost his job before later regaining it because of his specialized railway knowledge. The source uses Margaret Mel's interpretation to argue that this shame drew heavily on imported Western, especially Anglo-Saxon, expectations of masculine conduct rather than transparently expressing an older Japanese tradition.

## Key Characteristics

- Japanese civil servant with specialist railway knowledge.
- Second-class Titanic passenger returning from study of the Russian railway system.
- Survivor who entered lifeboat 13 when a spare place was called.
- Target of post-disaster ostracism and employment loss.
- Later reinstated because his technical knowledge remained valuable.
- Case showing the international circulation of gendered disaster morality.

## Evidence

- Voyage and survival: [[432-titanic-the-survivors-part-6-glt4613169046]] describes Hosono's railway study, second-class passage, personal resolution, and entry into lifeboat 13.
- Reputation and work: [[432-titanic-the-survivors-part-6-glt4613169046]] records ostracism, job loss, and later reinstatement.
- Interpretation: [[432-titanic-the-survivors-part-6-glt4613169046]] attributes to Margaret Mel the argument that Western masculine expectations shaped the shame imposed on Hosono.

## Qualifications

The interpretation of Hosono's shame is episode-attributed and does not establish one uniform Japanese response or a simple opposition between Western and Japanese traditions. Details of the lifeboat call, institutional decisions, and public reaction remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Created Hosono as a case of survival, masculine honor, and internationally transferred disaster stigma.
