# James Stewart / Earl of Moray

## Overview

James Stewart, Earl of Moray, is [[MaryQueenOfScots|Mary, Queen of Scots]]' half-brother and the Protestant regent whose authority followed her forced abdication, military defeat, and flight to England.

## Current Profile

Moray appears first as Mary's suspected alternative culprit after Darnley's murder and as a noble who left for France rather than support [[JamesHepburnEarlOfBothwell|Bothwell]]'s marriage campaign. Mary's coerced abdication appointed him regent for the infant James. After her escape from Loch Leven, his smaller force defeated her larger army at Langside, making her restoration attempt collapse within weeks.

The English phase gives Moray a dual role: to Elizabeth he looked like a calm, Protestant, effective governor, but he also had to defend a deposition that offended monarchical principle. He supplied Cecil with allegations, including the [[CasketLetters]], that kept Mary from restoration. His assassination in 1570 did not restore Mary; the ensuing civil war ended with English-backed victory for the king's party.

## Key Characteristics

- Half-brother whose rivalry with Mary mixed family, confession, and state power.
- Protestant regent installed through documents Mary signed under duress.
- Military victor at Langside despite Mary's larger rallying force.
- Effective governor in Elizabeth's assessment and useful alternative to restoring Mary.
- Supplier of disputed evidence against the deposed queen.
- Assassinated regent whose death reopened civil war but not Mary's restoration.

## Evidence

- Distance from Bothwell: [[589-mary-queen-of-scots-downfall-part-6-glt8999516788]] says Moray left for France before Bothwell's seizure and marriage.
- Regency and Langside: [[589-mary-queen-of-scots-downfall-part-6-glt8999516788]] grounds Mary's forced appointment of Moray and his rapid defeat of her restoration army.
- English diplomacy: [[589-mary-queen-of-scots-downfall-part-6-glt8999516788]] presents Elizabeth's favorable view of Moray and Cecil's request for a murder case against Mary.
- Evidence and aftermath: [[589-mary-queen-of-scots-downfall-part-6-glt8999516788]] links Moray to the casket allegations, his 1570 assassination, and the later Scottish civil war.

## Qualifications

Moray's absence from the Bothwell marriage campaign does not settle his knowledge of Darnley's murder or every motive behind his return and regency. The episode's favorable administrative comparison is an Elizabethan and host judgment, while the evidence he supplied against Mary remains disputed and politically useful.

## What Changed

- Established Moray as the regent who converted Mary's forced abdication and military defeat into an alternative Protestant government.

## Relationships

- [[MaryQueenOfScots|Mary, Queen of Scots]] - half-sister, deposed sovereign, and defeated restoration rival.
- [[JamesHepburnEarlOfBothwell|James Hepburn / Earl of Bothwell]] - political rival whose marriage to Mary accelerated the coalition against her.
- [[ElizabethI|Elizabeth I]] - neighboring monarch who preferred Moray's effective government to the risks of restoring Mary.
- [[WilliamCecil|William Cecil / Lord Burleigh]] - English minister who requested and used Moray's allegations.
- [[CasketLetters]] - disputed documents Moray supplied in the English inquiry.
- [[MonarchicalDepositionLegitimacy]] - legitimacy problem created by his regency under Mary's coerced abdication.
