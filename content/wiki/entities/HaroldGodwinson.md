# Harold Godwinson

## Overview
Harold Godwinson appears in the wiki through the [[BayeuxTapestry|Bayeux Tapestry]] as the English claimant whose oath, kingship, defeat, and death structure the source's Norman Conquest narrative.

## Current Profile
The episode presents Harold as a politically ambiguous figure. He is sent to Normandy by [[EdwardTheConfessor]], apparently swears that [[WilliamTheConqueror]] should receive the crown, later takes the throne himself, and is killed in the battle sequence that ends with William's victory.

## Key Characteristics
- English claimant central to the tapestry's oath-and-betrayal narrative.
- Visual contrast figure because the source says English and Norman identities are made legible through hair and moustache conventions.
- Defeated ruler whose death helps close the surviving battle sequence.
- Interpretive figure because the source treats the tapestry as political storytelling rather than neutral chronicle.

## Evidence
- Oath and throne sequence - [[ire-and-ice-the-toll-of-americas-deportations-6a993426809ae7c2f4293df9]] says Harold is sent to Normandy, appears to swear support for William, and later takes the throne.
- Battle and death - [[ire-and-ice-the-toll-of-americas-deportations-6a993426809ae7c2f4293df9]] describes the tapestry's battle scenes, Harold's death, English flight, and William's victory.

## Qualifications
This page preserves the source's narrated tapestry account and should not be treated as a full independent historiography of Harold's life or succession claim.
