## Overview

郭伋 / Guo Ji is an Eastern Han official whose current wiki profile now joins two source-bounded roles. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-709-mian-dui-bangjia-an-guren-weihe-buguan-renzhi-sihuo-luknor8yw839qwxogeyzwluo6qe2|Hanji 709]] presents him as a trust-bearing anti-bandit official in [[YingchuanCommandery|颍川]], while [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-711-ta-zhu-liuxiu-da-xia-banbi-jiangshan-weihe-si-fa-biequ-liib38zub0-tgrbtycmlqvohv-w|Hanji 711]] later presents him as a Bingzhou governor warning [[LiuXiu|刘秀]] against overreliance on Nanyang appointments.

## Current Profile

Hanji 709 gives Guo Ji the local pacification layer. Facing bandit leaders and surrendered or captured men, he chooses conversation, release, and return to farming rather than immediate exemplary punishment. He then reports himself for this discretionary leniency. Liu Xiu does not punish him, and the episode says later bandit groups surrender because Guo Ji's word has become credible.

Hanji 711 gives the post-founding personnel layer. As Bingzhou governor passing through Luoyang, Guo Ji tells Liu Xiu that official selection should draw from the whole realm rather than leaning too heavily on Liu Xiu's Nanyang compatriots. The military narrative has just shown Shu nearing collapse, but Guo Ji's advice shifts the frame from conquest to staffing: a founder can trust hometown networks during struggle, yet a national dynasty has to widen appointments to preserve state capacity.

The combined profile is not merely lenient or merely critical. Guo Ji is useful because he sees when credibility is the scarce resource: in Yingchuan, the state needs bandits to believe surrender will be honored; in Luoyang, the dynasty needs officeholding to be credible beyond the founding hometown circle.

## Key Characteristics

- Eastern Han official associated with Yingchuan bandit pacification and later Bingzhou governorship.
- Trust-based local pacifier who releases men to farm so surrender becomes believable.
- Discretionary official who self-impeaches after lenient handling, preserving institutional accountability.
- Personnel adviser who critiques Nanyang-heavy appointment patterns.
- State-capacity witness whose advice links local order and national staffing to credible public authority.
- Advisory actor whose criticisms are made inside Liu Xiu's governing relationship rather than as factional attack.

## Qualifications

This page remains bounded to the two listed source notes. Hanji 709 does not prove that leniency is always better than punishment; it proves that Guo Ji's credibility could turn release into surrender pressure in one anti-bandit setting. Hanji 711 does not prove that every Nanyang appointment was poor; its durable claim is the structural warning that early trust networks can become an administrative constraint if they are not widened.
