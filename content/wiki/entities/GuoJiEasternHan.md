## Overview

Guo Ji / 郭伋 is an Eastern Han official whose current wiki profile comes from [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-711-ta-zhu-liuxiu-da-xia-banbi-jiangshan-weihe-si-fa-biequ-liib38zub0-tgrbtycmlqvohv-w|Hanji 711]]. As Bingzhou governor passing through Luoyang, he tells [[LiuXiu|Liu Xiu]] that official selection should draw from nationwide talent rather than leaning too heavily on Liu Xiu's Nanyang compatriots.

## Current Profile

Hanji 711 uses Guo Ji as a compact post-founding governance warning. The military narrative has just shown Shu nearing collapse, but Guo Ji's advice shifts the frame from conquest to staffing: a founder can trust hometown and early-following networks during struggle, yet a national dynasty has to broaden appointments if it wants durable administrative capacity.

The source does not make Guo Ji an anti-Liu Xiu polemicist. Liu Xiu asks about governing gains and losses, and Guo Ji answers inside that advisory relationship. The durable claim is therefore that Guo Ji names a structural transition problem: once the regime is larger than the founding coalition, appointment quality becomes state capacity.

## Key Characteristics

- Eastern Han governor associated here with Bingzhou and a Luoyang audience with Liu Xiu.
- Personnel adviser who critiques overreliance on Nanyang compatriots in official selection.
- Transition-warning figure who marks the shift from founding trust networks to national bureaucracy.
- State-capacity witness whose advice is framed as governance diagnosis rather than factional attack.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to a single advisory scene in Hanji 711. It does not establish a complete biography of Guo Ji, nor does it prove that every Nanyang appointment was poor. The durable claim is the structural appointment warning: early trust networks can become an administrative constraint if not widened.
