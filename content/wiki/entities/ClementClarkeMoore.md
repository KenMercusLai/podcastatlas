# Clement Clarke Moore

## Overview

Clement Clarke Moore was a New York biblical scholar and writer conventionally credited with [[AVisitFromStNicholas|“A Visit from St. Nicholas”]].

## Current Profile

[[twas-the-night-before-christmas-twas-the-night-before-christmas]] places Moore within the elite Knickerbocker milieu that promoted a safer, domestic, child-centered Christmas. His social position and the poem's prosperous household therefore fit one another, though contextual fit alone cannot prove authorship.

The episode rejects the portrayal of Moore as too severe to have written playful Christmas verse. Researchers identified his use of comparable words, images, rhymes, and phrases, as well as a St. Nicholas letter in anapestic meter and a winter poem addressed to children. This positive comparative evidence supports the conventional attribution more strongly than personality contrast supports Livingston.

## Key Characteristics

- New York biblical scholar associated with the Knickerbocker social world.
- Owner of the Chelsea estate that gave its name to the surrounding Manhattan neighborhood.
- Conventionally credited author of “A Visit from St. Nicholas.”
- Wrote light, child-oriented, winter, and Christmas material relevant to the disputed poem.
- Was caricatured as humorless in arguments for the Livingston attribution.

## Evidence

- Historical fit: [[twas-the-night-before-christmas-twas-the-night-before-christmas]] connects Moore to the social circle promoting domestic Christmas traditions.
- Comparative writing: [[twas-the-night-before-christmas-twas-the-night-before-christmas]] reports parallels in language and imagery and identifies relevant anapestic, St. Nicholas, winter, fairy, and child-oriented verse.
- Rebuttal of character claims: [[twas-the-night-before-christmas-twas-the-night-before-christmas]] says several quotations used to depict Moore as severe changed meaning when restored to context.

## Qualifications

The episode's conclusion is not a substitute for a complete documentary record. Social milieu and stylistic compatibility support but do not independently prove authorship, and the source summary does not reproduce every manuscript, publication, or comparison on which the attribution rests.
