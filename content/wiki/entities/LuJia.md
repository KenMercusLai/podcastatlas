# 陆贾 / Lu Jia

陆贾 / Lu Jia enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-138-wudu-qiannian-de-lishi-zhenxiang-zhaogao-bushi-taijian-2-lvtievjrclb1lxxxxr6k-q7kryto|Qinji 138 part 2]] as one of the persuaders [[ZhangLiang|张良]] tells [[LiuBang|刘邦]] to send against the Qin generals defending [[Yaoguan|峣关]]. He works alongside [[LiShiqi|郦食其]], so his first role here is not an independent biography but a piece of Zhang Liang's pressure-and-persuasion design.

The episode uses Lu Jia to show that negotiation is part of the attack sequence. Zhang Liang has the Liu Bang side raise banners in the hills, then send Li Shiqi and Lu Jia to tempt Qin generals with heavy rewards. When the generals are willing to talk peace, Zhang Liang still warns that soldiers may not follow the generals' private defection preference, turning persuasion into a way to create laxity before an attack rather than into a final settlement.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-166-jiran-xiangyu-bujiang-wude-kan-liubang-ruhe-fuchou-ltwwbn9rzeqzzimmxxfihb9-drj1|Hanji 166]] gives Lu Jia a failed Chu-Han negotiation mission. Liu Bang sends him to Xiang Yu to seek peace and recover Liu Taigong and Lu Zhi, but Xiang Yu refuses. The failure sets up [[HouGongChuHan|侯公]]'s successful mission and the later [[HonggouSettlement|鸿沟议和]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-176-shusuntong-ruhe-wei-dahan-zhiding-liyi-zhidu-loeoqccrfwbmtr634ukyau9xykqy|Hanji 176]] adds Lu Jia's post-conquest teaching role through [[Xinyu|《新语》]]. The episode uses him to tell [[LiuBang|刘邦]] that military conquest cannot by itself sustain the [[WesternHanDynasty|西汉]]; ritual, learning, and civil governance have to follow the battlefield win.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-184-lishi-kongbupian-liubang-de-pengshi-roujiang-2-liebzrdrvo1okonpdjvrjatuwx9i|Hanji 184]] gives Lu Jia a southern frontier mission. Liu Bang sends him to [[ZhaoTuo|赵佗]] with seal and tally after recognizing Zhao Tuo as [[Nanyue|南越]] king. Unlike the Yaoguan case, this mission is not a tactical prelude to immediate attack in the source; it is framed as "先礼后兵," an attempt to make Nanyue submit without spending troops.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-185-yi-zhang-li-zui-shoufu-zhongguo-wanli-jiangtu-lps0moshdfvosnyhtdhrkdvswibh|Hanji 185]] completes that mission. Lu Jia first attacks Zhao Tuo's claimed distance from Han by reminding him of kin, ancestral graves, and Central Plains origin, then uses Liu Bang's victory over [[XiangYu|项羽]] and the threat of family punishment or a southern army to make resistance look irrational. Because he also brings title recognition, his coercion leaves Zhao Tuo a dignified path into submission.

The same episode repeats Lu Jia's [[Xinyu|《新语》]] role at court. When [[LiuBang|刘邦]] says he won the realm on horseback, Lu Jia asks whether he can govern it on horseback too. This makes Lu Jia's page join two branches: frontier submission through speech and post-conquest civil governance through classical admonition.

## Key Claims
- Hanji 185 completes Lu Jia's Nanyue mission: he uses identity, threat, comparative power, and title recognition to make Zhao Tuo submit without battle.
- Hanji 184 adds Lu Jia as the envoy used to convert Liu Bang's recognition of Zhao Tuo into a no-war Nanyue submission attempt.
- Hanji 176 adds Lu Jia as the authorial figure behind 《新语》, the early-Han civil-order lesson given to Liu Bang after conquest.
- Lu Jia appears as a Liu Bang-side envoy in the final Yaoguan-Lantian phase of Qin's collapse.
- His mission belongs to [[PoliticalSurrenderCascade|招降示范级联]], but Zhang Liang treats successful persuasion as tactically incomplete until the army's obedience is known.
- The source pairs Lu Jia with Li Shiqi, linking elite persuasion to Liu Bang's broader westward campaign rather than to battlefield force alone.
- Hanji 166 adds a failed peace-and-hostage mission to Xiang Yu, showing Lu Jia as one negotiator in a sequence rather than the decisive Honggou broker.
