## Overview
李虎 was a Western Wei commander, one of the figures counted among the Eight Pillars, and the grandfather of Tang founder Li Yuan.

## Current Profile
Li Hu's importance in the episode is historiographical as much as military. His missing biography in the *Book of Zhou*, inconsistent rank across records, and expanded campaign credit suggest that Tang ancestral veneration elevated his standing. [[MaejimaYoshitaka|Maejima Yoshitaka]] reconstructs a plausible seventh place among the Eight Pillars rather than a position immediately below Yuwen Tai. Yet a newly excavated Sui epitaph attests some high titles before Tang's foundation, requiring correction of the stronger claim that all such offices were later inventions.

## Key Characteristics
- Belonged to the Western Wei high-command circle later remembered as the Eight Pillars.
- Became a politically important ancestor after his grandson founded the Tang dynasty.
- Has no dedicated biography in the *Book of Zhou* despite later dynastic prominence.
- Appears at inconsistent positions across historical rankings.
- Illustrates both retrospective embellishment and the corrective force of new epigraphic evidence.

## Evidence
- Genealogy and textual absence: [[ltxaost4pgqhjdirwas15vizersj-ltxaost4pgqhjdirwas15vizersj]] identifies Li Yuan as his grandson and notes the missing *Book of Zhou* biography.
- Rank reconstruction: [[ltxaost4pgqhjdirwas15vizersj-ltxaost4pgqhjdirwas15vizersj]] compares offices, ordering, and recorded merit to support a seventh-place estimate.
- Narrative inflation: [[ltxaost4pgqhjdirwas15vizersj-ltxaost4pgqhjdirwas15vizersj]] reports campaigns reframed from participation into independent command.
- Epigraphic correction: [[ltxaost4pgqhjdirwas15vizersj-ltxaost4pgqhjdirwas15vizersj]] says a Sui epitaph confirms some high titles predated Tang.

## Qualifications
The exact ranking and office history remain open. Evidence of Tang-era elevation does not prove that every achievement or title was fabricated, while one epitaph does not make every later ancestral claim contemporary.

## What Changed
- Established Li Hu as a bounded case of dynastic memory inflation corrected, but not erased, by inscriptional evidence.
