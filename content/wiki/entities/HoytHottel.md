# Hoyt Hottel

## Overview

Hoyt Hottel was an MIT combustion specialist who organized a wartime U.S. research competition to develop more effective incendiary weapons.

## Current Profile

[[may-the-best-firebomb-win-may-the-best-firebomb-win]] presents Hottel as the coordinating figure between university scientists, industrial firms, and military testing. Before U.S. entry into the Second World War, he assembled a National Defense Research Committee subcommittee dominated by chemists and focused it on improving fire as a weapon.

His role was institutional as well as technical. He recruited competitors, set comparative trials, connected laboratory work to field deployment, and oversaw testing against reconstructed German and Japanese buildings. The source therefore makes him a case of scientific coordination whose rigor improved knowledge about combustion while serving planned urban destruction.

## Key Characteristics

- MIT professor specializing in combustion and flamethrowers.
- Organizer of an NDRC subcommittee devoted to incendiary research.
- Used competition among academic and industrial teams to identify an effective weapon.
- Connected laboratory formulation to environmental, impact, dispersal, and building-scale tests.
- Treated reconstructed urban housing as an empirical system for comparing destructive performance.
