## Overview

猎骄靡 / Liejiaomi is synthesized as the [[Wusun|乌孙]] ruler whose rise from a Xiongnu-supported exile precedes his later balancing between Han and [[Xiongnu|匈奴]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-370-1-jiemi-hanchao-diyiwei-yu-xiongnu-heqin-de-gongzhu-lgetvmacjnoxhqfzymupxsk7fknk|Hanji 370-1]] supplies his origin and request for [[LiuXijunPrincessWesternHan|刘细君]]; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-370-2-yeye-ba-jiaoqi-jiagei-qinsunzi-nainai-yiye-bian-xifuer-lopw_h3yzk6qtcpksqdm9l2egrvp|Hanji 370-2]] supplies the dual-marriage household and succession arrangement. The latter episode's “列郊弥” is normalized here to the received name 猎骄靡.

## Current Profile

Hanji 370-1 gives Liejiaomi a source-scoped divine-child origin legend: after Wusun's defeat, a guardian sees a wolf nurse him and a crow bring meat, then entrusts him to a Xiongnu chanyu. The chanyu later returns Wusun followers to him; with Xiongnu support he attacks the Yuezhi in the Ili region and builds what the host calls the largest north-Tianshan nomadic polity of the Western Han period. This background makes Wusun both powerful enough to matter and historically dependent enough to fear Xiongnu retaliation when it approaches Han.

The two episodes then present Liejiaomi as a ruler whose household structure is foreign policy. Under Xiongnu pressure he offers one thousand horses and requests a Han bride. He makes Xijun right wife and a Xiongnu princess left wife, preserving relations with both sides while placing the Xiongnu bride in the higher-ranked position given by Hanji 370-2. His great age, separate residence, and limited communication with Xijun make the marriage diplomatically legible but personally thin.

Liejiaomi's succession arrangement carries the alliance into the next generation. Knowing he is old, he asks Xijun to marry his grandson and designated successor [[JunxumiWusun|军须靡]]. The request is coercive for Xijun but, in the episode's political reading, preserves succession continuity and Wusun's room to maneuver between Han and Xiongnu.

## Key Characteristics

- Wusun ruler whose exile, Xiongnu-supported restoration, and western expansion precede his later diplomatic balancing.
- Seeks a Han marriage under Xiongnu pressure and supplies one thousand horses as bridewealth.
- Uses simultaneous marriages to receive ties from both rival powers.
- Gives the Xiongnu princess the left-wife rank and Xijun the right-wife rank in the episode's account.
- Maintains limited day-to-day contact with Xijun despite the marriage's diplomatic visibility.
- Designates grandson Junxumi as successor and asks Xijun to marry him.
- Treats household succession as a mechanism for alliance continuity.

## Qualifications

This profile rests on two compact episode notes. The divine-nurture story is a political origin legend, not independently verified biography; the exact chanyu, chronology, Yuezhi campaign mechanics, Wusun's relative size, and the causal link from Xiongnu threat to the marriage request remain source-scoped. Liejiaomi's exact age, the formal rank relation between left and right wives, his private motives, Wusun's domestic faction map, and the full succession mechanics also remain unsettled. “昆莫” is treated as a ruler title in this context rather than a separate personal identity.
