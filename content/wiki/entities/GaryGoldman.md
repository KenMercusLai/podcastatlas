# Gary Goldman

## Overview
Gary Goldman is a filmmaker and screenwriter presented by the two-part [[RevisionistHistory]] investigation as the creator of an animal-world project called Zootopia who unsuccessfully alleged that Disney later appropriated elements of his pitch.

## Current Profile
The wiki knows Goldman's dispute only through two retrospective podcast summaries. [[zootopia-exposed-part-one-zootopia-exposed-part-one|Part One]] says he devised the name and animal world around 2000, submitted an eight-page *Looney* outline to Disney in 2009, and later identified similarities in Disney's 2016 film before losing a seven-year legal fight. It distinguishes failure to show infringement from a finding about whether Disney used the pitch.

[[zootopia-exposed-part-two-zootopia-exposed-part-two|Part Two]] develops the idea that Gary De'Snake and the stolen-invention plot in [[Zootopia2Film|Zootopia 2]] symbolically recognize the grievance. Goldman reportedly found that reading meaningful, but neither episode supplies the underlying pitch, complete court record, or confirmation of filmmaker intent.

## Key Characteristics
- Originator of the Zootopia project described in the podcast's appropriation account.
- Unsuccessful claimant in a prolonged legal dispute with Disney.
- Creator who perceived continuity across a shared title, animal setting, aspirational theme, and contrasting character types.
- Proposed real-world referent for Gary De'Snake and the sequel's stolen-invention plot.
- Recipient of possible symbolic acknowledgment rather than formal legal or material remedy.

## Evidence
### Authorship dispute
- [[zootopia-exposed-part-one-zootopia-exposed-part-one]] says Goldman devised Zootopia around 2000 and gave Disney a 2009 outline for *Looney*, featuring the animal world, cynical Roscoe, optimistic Mimi, and a challenge to unlimited childhood aspiration.
- [[zootopia-exposed-part-one-zootopia-exposed-part-one]] says Goldman identified related features in Disney's later film and lost three rounds of litigation because the similarities did not establish copyright infringement.
- [[zootopia-exposed-part-two-zootopia-exposed-part-two]] says Goldman pitched Disney an animal kingdom called Zootopia and later lost his claim after Disney released a film with the same name and a related premise.

### Film interpretation and response
- [[zootopia-exposed-part-one-zootopia-exposed-part-one]] joins Gary's name, appearance, manner, Louisiana-associated setting, and stolen family invention into a possible allusion, while preserving coincidence, mockery, apology, and internal maneuver as competing explanations.
- [[zootopia-exposed-part-two-zootopia-exposed-part-two]] links Goldman to Gary De'Snake's stolen family invention and reports that Goldman viewed Jared Bush's film as one family standing up for another.
- [[zootopia-exposed-part-two-zootopia-exposed-part-two]] says Goldman sent [[JaredBush]] a handwritten letter, received no reply, and nevertheless found some peace in the interpretation.

### Family impact
- [[zootopia-exposed-part-one-zootopia-exposed-part-one]] reports that Goldman's wife and sons shared the emotional burden of the litigation, which Gladwell describes as “transactive anger.”

## Qualifications
The current profile is source-scoped and does not adjudicate infringement or appropriation. Similarity, access, protected expression, contractual duties, the exact grounds for each ruling, and the film team's intent require the pitches, court record, films, and production evidence. The first source's statement that the court did not decide use should not be mistaken for a finding that use occurred. Symbolic resemblance cannot substitute for legal or material restitution.

## What Changed
- Added the chronology, pitch elements, litigation outcome, and family impact supplied by Part One.
- Clarified that failure to prove infringement did not settle pitch use, while also providing no affirmative finding that use occurred.
- Strengthened the sequel parallel without converting circumstantial resemblance into confirmed intent.
