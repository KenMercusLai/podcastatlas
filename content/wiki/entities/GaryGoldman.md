# Gary Goldman

## Overview
Gary Goldman is a filmmaker and screenwriter presented by [[zootopia-exposed-part-two-zootopia-exposed-part-two]] as the creator of an animal-kingdom project called Zootopia who unsuccessfully alleged that Disney later appropriated his idea.

## Current Profile
The wiki knows Goldman's dispute only through this retrospective podcast account. It says he pitched Disney, lost a seven-year legal fight, and later interpreted Gary De'Snake in [[Zootopia2Film|Zootopia 2]] as symbolic recognition of his grievance. The episode treats that reading as morally meaningful, but it does not supply the pitches, pleadings, judicial reasoning, or confirmation of the filmmakers' intent.

## Key Characteristics
- Originator of the Zootopia project described in the episode's appropriation account.
- Unsuccessful claimant in a prolonged legal dispute with Disney.
- Proposed real-world referent for Gary De'Snake and the sequel's stolen-invention plot.
- Recipient of possible symbolic acknowledgment rather than formal legal or material remedy.
- Said the Bush family history helped him find a measure of peace.

## Evidence
### Authorship dispute
- [[zootopia-exposed-part-two-zootopia-exposed-part-two]] says Goldman pitched Disney an animal kingdom called Zootopia and later lost his claim after Disney released a film with the same name and a related premise.

### Film interpretation and response
- [[zootopia-exposed-part-two-zootopia-exposed-part-two]] links Goldman to Gary De'Snake's stolen family invention and reports that Goldman viewed Jared Bush's film as one family standing up for another.
- [[zootopia-exposed-part-two-zootopia-exposed-part-two]] says Goldman sent [[JaredBush]] a handwritten letter, received no reply, and nevertheless found some peace in the interpretation.

## Qualifications
The current profile is source-scoped and does not adjudicate infringement or appropriation. Similarity, access, protectable expression, the reasons Goldman lost, and the film team's intent require the underlying court record and production evidence. Symbolic resemblance cannot substitute for a legal finding or material restitution.

## What Changed
- Created a bounded profile distinguishing Goldman's reported grievance from a settled finding of appropriation.
- Recorded his reception of the sequel as possible acknowledgment rather than legal vindication.
