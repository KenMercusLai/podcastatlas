# Fiume

## Overview

Fiume, now Rijeka in Croatia, is presented as the disputed Adriatic port occupied by Gabriele D'Annunzio in 1919 and turned into a fifteen-month experiment in nationalist revolt, aesthetic utopia, mass political theatre, and ethnic coercion.

## Current Profile

The episode places Fiume inside the postwar collision between Italian territorial expectations, Wilsonian self-determination, and the creation of the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes. Invited by local supporters, [[GabrieleDAnnunzio|D'Annunzio]] marched with roughly 200 mutineers; troops sent to stop him often stood aside or joined, and his force grew to more than 2,000 by arrival.

The occupation attracted Italian nationalists, free-love radicals, Bolsheviks, anti-money sectarians, veterans, and adventurers. D'Annunzio proclaimed poetry, beauty, freedom, and love, then ruled through the Commandant persona, balcony dialogue, uniforms, processions, songs, and salutes. Partial blockade and administrative weakness produced hunger, while conflict between an Italian merchant class and a Slavic working class ended in ethnic riots and exclusion from food.

## Key Characteristics

- Strategically important Adriatic port claimed within Italian “mutilated victory” nationalism.
- Unauthorized military occupation enlarged through defection and celebrity authority.
- Coalition space combining nationalism, revolutionary experimentation, aesthetic utopianism, and social radicalism.
- Laboratory for balcony politics, call-and-response, uniforms, salutes, songs, and leader ritual.
- Blockaded and hungry city where spectacle failed to resolve governance and supply.
- Ethnically divided polity whose romantic ideals gave way to coercion against Slavic residents.

## Qualifications

This profile follows one podcast's interpretation and is not a full municipal, diplomatic, demographic, constitutional, or Croatian history of Rijeka. Force sizes, ideological affiliations, Lenin's reported praise, popular motives, and the balance between experiment and disorder remain source-scoped. The city's use as a fascist precursor does not make every participant a fascist.

## What Changed

- Created Fiume as both a postwar territorial crisis and a test of aesthetic politics against administration, scarcity, and ethnic division.
