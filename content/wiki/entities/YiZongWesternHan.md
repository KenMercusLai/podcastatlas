## Overview

义纵 / Yi Zong is synthesized as a Han Wudi-era harsh official whose career cannot be reduced to unqualified cruelty. One source names him as a predecessor in the cruel-official line before [[DuZhouWesternHan|杜周]]; another says he opposes the disruptive execution of告缗, arrests [[YangKeWesternHan|杨可]]'s subordinates, and is executed for resisting the imperial order.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-402-2-shudiwushu-de-kuli-duzhou-weihe-zuizhong-deyi-shanzhong-lqrunv51se0w7_2fi2ifs_f7zub4|Hanji 402-2]] gives Yi Zong only as a chronological and type marker beside [[ZhangTangWesternHan|张汤]] before Du Zhou's rise. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-335-dangzhe-wudi-de-mian-huoqubing-weihe-gan-sha-liguang-zhi-zi-lupxhu64fi0d0xl223zq9pwenmy1|Hanji 335]] supplies a more specific final conflict. Yi judges告缗 enforcement harmful to livelihood and public confidence and detains several of Yang Ke's subordinates. [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] treats this as resistance to the edict and has him killed. The episode preserves a mixed judgment: Yi's harsh service hurt innocents, yet his attempt to restrain fiscal enforcement complicates a flat cruel-official label.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han harsh official situated in the pre-Du-Zhou cruel-official line.
- Enforcer associated with strong action against powerful local actors and harm to innocents.
- Administrative dissenter who opposes the way告缗 is being carried out.
- Official executed after arresting Yang Ke's subordinates without imperial authorization.
- Morally mixed figure whose final resistance does not erase earlier cruelty.

## Evidence

Cruel-official background:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-402-2-shudiwushu-de-kuli-duzhou-weihe-zuizhong-deyi-shanzhong-lqrunv51se0w7_2fi2ifs_f7zub4|Hanji 402-2]] says Zhang Tang and Yi Zong fall before Du Zhou moves to the foreground.

Resistance to fiscal enforcement:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-335-dangzhe-wudi-de-mian-huoqubing-weihe-gan-sha-liguang-zhi-zi-lupxhu64fi0d0xl223zq9pwenmy1|Hanji 335]] says Yi Zong sees告缗 enforcement as disturbing livelihood and arrests several of Yang Ke's subordinates.

Execution and evaluation:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-335-dangzhe-wudi-de-mian-huoqubing-weihe-gan-sha-liguang-zhi-zi-lupxhu64fi0d0xl223zq9pwenmy1|Hanji 335]] says Wudi has Yi executed for resisting the imperial order and cites the mixed judgment “虽残酷，斯称其位.”

## Qualifications

The sources do not reconstruct Yi Zong's complete offices, cases, or legal record. Hanji 335 does not identify the detained subordinates or prove that Yi's action followed regular procedure. Its livelihood-defense reading and attribution of the quoted judgment to Sima Qian remain source-scoped pending textual comparison.

## What Changed

- Expanded Yi Zong from a chronological placeholder into a morally mixed official who resists告缗 implementation.
- Preserved the distinction between criticizing fiscal disruption and endorsing Yi's earlier harsh conduct.
