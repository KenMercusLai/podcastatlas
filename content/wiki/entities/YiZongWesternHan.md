## Overview

义纵 / Yi Zong is synthesized as a Han Wudi-era harsh official whose career joins mass coercion, imperial usefulness, administrative vulnerability, and a final attempt to restrain disruptive告缗 enforcement. His later resistance complicates but does not erase the violence through which he first becomes visible.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-330-ta-weile-sharen-jing-xiwang-dongtian-bianchang-lluuwmkv4rjqgnvodkuzulb9ee3c|Hanji 330]] supplies the early coercive layer. After taking office in Nanyang, Yi investigates [[NingChengWesternHan|宁成]]'s family and destroys the household. In Dingxiang he is then said to combine prisoners and defendants with visiting relatives and friends in a one-day killing of more than four hundred people. The episode treats the resulting fear as part of the performance that makes him promotable under [[HanWudi|汉武帝]], while keeping totals and procedure source-scoped.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-402-2-shudiwushu-de-kuli-duzhou-weihe-zuizhong-deyi-shanzhong-lqrunv51se0w7_2fi2ifs_f7zub4|Hanji 402-2]] places Yi beside [[ZhangTangWesternHan|张汤]] as a predecessor in the cruel-official line before [[DuZhouWesternHan|杜周]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-334-1-shenwei-kuli-de-ta-ruhe-yibubu-lunwei-yehu-lje_l2eoefz2pw2-yj9qve4on94w|Hanji 334-1]] and overlapping [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-334-2-wei-shenme-laoban-geng-xihuan-zuo-biaomian-wenzhang-de-ren-lp6r9orqzbzy8ffkq1fkneds3xzf|Hanji 334-2]] add a later deterioration layer: a damaged road under Yi's responsibility makes Wudi's trip to [[GanquanPalace|甘泉宫]] uncomfortable, and the ruler reads the omission as evidence that Yi did not expect his recovery or did not respect him. Administrative neglect is plausible; inner disloyalty and a direct road-to-execution causal chain are not established.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-335-dangzhe-wudi-de-mian-huoqubing-weihe-gan-sha-liguang-zhi-zi-lupxhu64fi0d0xl223zq9pwenmy1|Hanji 335]] supplies the final conflict. Yi judges告缗 enforcement harmful to livelihood and public confidence and detains several of [[YangKeWesternHan|杨可]]'s subordinates. Wudi treats this as resistance to the edict and has him killed. The complete bounded profile is therefore morally mixed without being exculpatory: a mass-killing official later opposes another destructive enforcement campaign, and the ruler who valued severity discards him when his action no longer aligns with imperial policy.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han harsh official whose early reputation is built through household destruction and mass killing.
- Enforcer whose terror becomes legible upward as administrative performance.
- Administrator whose neglected Ganquan route is converted into evidence of disrespect by the ruler.
- Administrative dissenter who opposes the way告缗 is being carried out.
- Official executed after arresting Yang Ke's subordinates without imperial authorization.
- Morally mixed figure whose final resistance does not erase earlier cruelty.

## Qualifications

The sources do not reconstruct Yi Zong's complete offices, case files, or legal record. Hanji 330's killing totals, Ning Cheng procedure, grouping of prisoners with visitors, and promotion sequence require primary-text comparison. Hanji 334-1 and 334-2 do not establish the road-repair chain of command, Yi's knowledge or intent, an enemy plot, or a direct causal path from road neglect to execution. Hanji 335 does not identify the detained subordinates or prove that Yi's action followed regular procedure; its livelihood-defense reading remains episode-attributed.

## What Changed

- Added Yi's early Nanyang and Dingxiang violence, making his harsh-official label concrete rather than merely chronological.
- Reframed promotion as institutional use of coercive performance without assuming that Wudi personally knew every operational detail.
- Sharpened the mixed judgment: later resistance to告缗 qualifies the career but does not retroactively excuse mass killing.
