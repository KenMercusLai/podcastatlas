# 种暠 / Zhong Hao (late Han)

## Overview

Zhong Hao is a late Han official whose synthesized profile now has three layers: a 144 CE crown-prince credential crisis, a 145 CE Yizhou inspection record, and a later Duliao frontier governorship. Across those settings, he is represented as an official who converts assigned responsibility into visible boundary enforcement instead of treating rank, remoteness, or palace access as excuses for deference.

## Current Profile

The earliest layer places Zhong Hao in charge of Crown Prince Liu Bing's household under [[EmperorShunOfHan|Emperor Shun of Han]]. When [[GaoFanLateHan|Gao Fan]] arrives from the inner palace without edict credentials to fetch the crown prince, Zhong Hao blocks the cart with a sword, demands proof, and allows the transfer only after an edict is produced.

In the 145 CE Yizhou layer, Zhong Hao serves as inspector and reports that the Shu commandery administrator Liu Xuan had been implicated in bribery and cruelty. The episode uses his investigation to show that provincial inspection can translate local abuses into central accountability.

The later Duliao layer shifts him to frontier governance. He investigates an official's death, clears the wrongly accused Hu, punishes responsible officials, imposes discipline on the Han side, and then gains broad Hu submission through fairness rather than inherited coercion alone.

## Key Characteristics

- Crown-prince household guardian who treats missing credentials as a succession-security threat.
- Provincial inspector who escalates commandery abuses into personnel consequences.
- Frontier administrator who uses factual investigation before punishment.
- Cross-ethnic governor who gains Hu submission by making Han officials accountable.
- Name-normalization case where transcript forms such as 崇浩 are linked to received Tongjian 种暠 when the passage and office context support it.
- Example of role-based courage: his decisive acts occur where his assigned office gives him standing to intervene.

- "守拙" as decisive restraint - [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-824-weishenme-women-yao-xue-shouzhuo-lpssy8ltykugdpvmgrzwcpf6-9v9|Hanji 824]] presents Zhong Hao as quiet in ordinary circumstances but willing to risk his life when crown-prince safety is at stake.
- Yizhou anti-corruption role - [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-844-ruhe-liyu-bubaizhidi-lsp49uw8atyiufrf-ryo-9ctos5z|Hanji 844]] records Zhong Hao, as Yizhou inspector, reporting Liu Xuan's bribery and cruelty, prompting execution and succession by the humane administrator Di Jiong.
- Frontier factual investigation - [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-826-luanshi-de-zui-yu-e-beihou-shi-tanlan-de-renxing-ljk0q4zq4zy6l9rvoo-pipdcsijv|Hanji 826]] records Zhong Hao, as Duliao general, discovering that Li Ku had killed himself rather than being murdered by Xianbei.

## Qualifications

The profile is still episodic rather than biographical. It combines three source-bounded scenes and should not yet infer a continuous career ideology beyond the recurring pattern of credential, inspection, and evidence-based accountability. The 144 scene also does not prove Gao Fan's intent; it proves Zhong Hao's refusal to treat undocumented access as sufficient.
