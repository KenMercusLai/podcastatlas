# Northern Rising

## Overview

The Northern Rising is the December 1569 Catholic revolt that confirms Cecil's fear that [[MaryQueenOfScots|Mary, Queen of Scots]] can become a domestic rallying point against [[ElizabethI|Elizabeth I]]. The episode presents it as a northern English rebellion led by Thomas Percy, Earl of Northumberland, and Charles Neville, Earl of Westmorland, with aims of freeing Mary and restoring Catholicism.

## Current Profile

The rising matters because it turns the Mary problem from court intrigue into open rebellion. The rebels capture Durham and celebrate Mass in Durham Cathedral for the first time since Elizabeth's accession, making religious restoration visible in public space. Elizabeth's repression is harsh, with mass hangings, displayed bodies, and melted church bells.

The event supports the episode's larger security synthesis: Mary's claim is dangerous because it can coordinate Catholic sentiment, noble revolt, foreign hopes, and Protestant fear.

## Key Characteristics

- The rising is a domestic Catholic revolt centered on freeing Mary and restoring Catholicism.
- Its northern geography matters because the episode says Catholic feeling is especially strong outside London and the southeast.
- Elizabeth's harsh repression makes the revolt a visible state-security turning point.

## Qualifications

The source presents the rising through the Elizabethan security lens, so its local social and regional causes are not developed independently. Its durable wiki role is as evidence that Mary's claim had domestic mobilizing power, not as a full history of northern Catholicism.
