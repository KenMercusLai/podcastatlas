## Overview

吴公 / Wu Gong is the Henan commandery governor whom the episode presents as [[JiaYi|贾谊]]'s early patron and route into the court of [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]]. The semantic suffix distinguishes him from other people identified only as “吴公” elsewhere in the corpus.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-210-lingnan-shishang-diyi-wen-baowendi-shu-2-lot-wjasnc4esi34rv8cs6p7g1iq|Hanji 210 part 2]] says Wu Gong recognized Jia Yi's literary ability in Henan and brought him into his circle. After Liu Heng summoned Wu Gong to Chang'an as廷尉, Wu Gong reportedly brought Jia Yi with him and recommended the young scholar to the emperor.

The episode uses Wu Gong as a talent-selection bridge: local reputation becomes access to central office through an established official's judgment and sponsorship. Its stronger biographical details, including a claim that Wu Gong's governance ranked first nationally, remain source-scoped.

## Key Characteristics

- Early Western Han Henan commandery governor presented as a capable local administrator.
- Patron who recognizes Jia Yi before the young scholar enters central government.
- Recommender whose own appointment as廷尉 creates Jia Yi's route to Chang'an and imperial notice.

## Qualifications

This profile rests on one popular-history episode rather than an independent biography. Wu Gong's personal name is not supplied, and the episode does not independently establish his performance ranking, patronage mechanics, appointment chronology, or Jia Yi's precise status in his circle. This page does not merge him with later figures also called “吴公.”
