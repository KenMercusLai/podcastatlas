# Marcelo Garcia

## Overview
Marcelo Garcia is the Brazilian jiu-jitsu champion used in [[scim6762906273-scim6762906273|The Art of Learning & Living Life]] as the episode's main example of mastery in scrambles and renewal after victory.

## Current Profile
In the bounded source, [[JoshWaitzkin|Josh Waitzkin]] calls Garcia the "king of the scramble" because his practice concentrates on transitions rather than only stable positions. The profile is therefore less a full athletic biography than a focused case of [[TransitionZoneTraining]]: mastery can emerge where named techniques are still forming and opponents have fewer practiced perceptual frames.

Garcia also anchors [[DynamicQualityMastery]]. After an ADCC victory, the episode says he returned to training, set aside much of the winning repertoire, and began building another one. The story treats repeat excellence as rediscovery under new pressure rather than preservation of the exact formula that already won.

## Key Characteristics
- Trains unstable scrambles and transitions as primary performance territory.
- Uses live resistance and changing positions rather than relying only on static technical categories.
- Represents perceptual advantage through familiarity with more in-between states.
- Returns quickly to practice after success.
- Serves as the episode's clearest case of replacing a winning repertoire instead of defending it indefinitely.

## Evidence
- Scramble mastery - [[scim6762906273-scim6762906273|The Art of Learning & Living Life]] attributes Garcia's advantage to sustained training in transitions between visible positions.
- Post-victory renewal - [[scim6762906273-scim6762906273|The Art of Learning & Living Life]] recounts Garcia returning after an ADCC win and shedding much of the repertoire that produced it.
- Cross-domain use - [[scim6762906273-scim6762906273|The Art of Learning & Living Life]] uses Garcia's practice to explain a learning principle beyond jiu-jitsu without claiming identical technique across domains.

## Qualifications
This profile records Garcia's role in one interview summary, not his complete biography, titles, technical system, or independent coaching record. The wording about being the "king of the scramble," the timing and extent of repertoire replacement, and the causal interpretation of his success are attributed to Waitzkin and remain source-scoped.
