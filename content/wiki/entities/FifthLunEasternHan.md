## Overview

第五伦 / Fifth Lun now has an earlier Guangwu-era career-entry layer before the Zhangdi-era warning, Xiongnu-policy, and integrity profile. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-730-zhongguo-baijiaxing-juran-you-diwu-xing-lsm3xean8fzidhhhxi6-ppphexxh|Hanji 730]] presents him first as a fair Chang'an market administrator whose confidence, candor, and composure win [[LiuXiu|光武帝刘秀]]'s attention and lead to rapid promotion. Later source notes place the same figure in senior Zhangdi-era office: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-744-dahan-genggong-qiusheng-caipu-jingrang-beiye-wangchenmoji-lvsumpsnpb1tpk7t0xrx7vatd5mx|Hanji 744]] makes him a clean Shu official promoted to Sikong, Hanji 745 and Hanji 749 make him an outer-relative warning voice, Hanji 752 makes him an anti-Dou warning voice, and Hanji 755-756 place him in the Xiongnu restitution debate and in the strongest self-scrutiny material.

## Current Profile

Hanji 730 gives Fifth Lun his earliest current office profile. As a Jingzhao subordinate managing the Chang'an market, he is described as fair, clean, orderly, and strict against fraud. The episode also shows political self-confidence: Fifth Lun believes that if he can meet Liu Xiu, he can discuss state affairs with him, and he answers colleagues' mockery by saying that people of different ways cannot plan together.

The same source turns that confidence into court recognition rather than mere boasting. After being recommended as xiaolian and serving the Huaiyang king's medical office, Fifth Lun follows the king to court in 56 CE. Liu Xiu tests him with difficult personal rumors, and Fifth Lun answers directly, explaining the alleged father-in-law and hospitality stories through family circumstance, famine, and war. Liu Xiu then appoints him to Fuyi and quickly redirects him to Kuaiji, where the episode again emphasizes fair and clean administration.

Hanji 744 supplies the later clean-administration and office-entry layer under [[EmperorZhangOfHan|汉章帝]]. The source describes his Shu commandery service as fair, clean, and good at recommending people to fitting offices; Zhangdi appoints him Sikong in late 75. The episode also explains the compound surname Fifth through a Qin-Han relocation genealogy of Tian Qi royal branches, but that identity note remains source-scoped rather than becoming a separate political claim.

Hanji 745 and Hanji 749 make him mixed rather than simply "pro-rescue" or "anti-power." In the Geng Gong debate, he opposes dispatching relief forces after Guan Chong requests aid, while [[BaoYuEasternHan|鲍昱]] successfully argues that abandonment would damage loyal service and frontier credibility. The same source then shows Fifth Lun in his more durable role: warning Zhangdi that Ma-family appointments, guests, and large gifts can turn imperial in-laws into overmighty ministers. Hanji 749 later repeats the institutional form of that concern when Ma Fang receives Qiang command: relatives can receive wealth, but heavy office creates a punishment-versus-kinship dilemma.

Hanji 752 presents Fifth Lun as an early anti-Dou warning voice. The warning is structural rather than personal gossip: [[DouXianEasternHan|窦宪]] and [[DouDuEasternHan|窦笃]] are close to the palace because of Empress Dou, receive favor and reward, and gather guests. Fifth Lun's role is to tell Zhangdi that outer-relative access should be checked before it becomes hard to control. The warning is validated by Dou Xian's estate coercion and later Dou-family expansion.

Hanji 755-756 add the court policy and integrity layers. After Southern Xiongnu seizes Northern Xiongnu people and livestock, Fifth Lun and [[ZhengHongEasternHan|郑洪]] oppose restitution while [[HuanYuEasternHan|桓虞]] and [[YuanAnEasternHan|袁安]] support it. When the dispute escalates, Zhangdi treats heated disagreement as normal in major affairs and orders the ministers back into deliberation. Hanji 756 then frames Fifth Lun as devoted, forthright, plain, honest, and unusually self-scrutinizing about private motive after a refused horse gift.

## Key Characteristics

- Guangwu-era market administrator whose fairness, anti-fraud enforcement, and public trust precede his later senior court profile.
- Direct and confident respondent whose candor before Liu Xiu helps convert local reputation into rapid office promotion.
- Clean commandery administrator promoted to Sikong at the beginning of Zhangdi's reign.
- Cautious rescue skeptic whose non-dispatch position is overridden by Bao Yu in the Geng Gong crisis.
- Early Ma-family warning voice who reads guest networks, large private gifts, and heavy office as political danger.
- Anti-Dou warning voice who sees palace access, favor, and guest networks as structural danger.
- Clean and forthright officeholder whose integrity is framed through plainness, public devotion, and self-scrutiny.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 756, Hanji 755, Hanji 752, Hanji 749, Hanji 745, Hanji 744, and Hanji 730, and normalizes the transcript's 第五轮 wording to 第五伦 / Fifth Lun. Hanji 730 makes Fifth Lun's early promise visible, but it does not by itself explain the origin of the Fifth surname; Hanji 744 supplies that genealogy and keeps it source-scoped. The early Guangwu promotion story should not flatten the later profile into a single virtue tale: Hanji 745 shows his caution can point in different directions, with his non-rescue stance losing to Bao Yu while his Ma-family warning remains durable. Hanji 749 provides a structural warning about Ma Fang's appointment, but Ma Fang's battlefield success means the claim should not be read as a competence accusation. The source's "慎独" framing is treated as the host's ethical synthesis from the horse-gift anecdote, not as a complete doctrine of moral psychology.

## What Changed

- Integrated Hanji 730 as Fifth Lun's earliest current Guangwu-era market, court-test, and Kuaiji-promotion layer.
- Reframed the "Fifth" compound surname as a question raised in Hanji 730 but substantively bounded to Hanji 744's genealogy.
- Preserved Hanji 745 as a mixed rescue-skeptic and Ma-family warning source rather than a simple integrity proof.
- Preserved Hanji 749 and Hanji 752 as the heavy-office, court-example, and anti-Dou warning layers.
- Preserved Hanji 755-756 as the Xiongnu restitution, deliberation, retirement, integrity, and self-scrutiny layers.
