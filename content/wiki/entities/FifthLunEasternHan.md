## Overview

第五伦 / Fifth Lun now has a pre-warning office layer before the anti-Ma, anti-Dou, Xiongnu-policy, and integrity profile. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-744-dahan-genggong-qiusheng-caipu-jingrang-beiye-wangchenmoji-lvsumpsnpb1tpk7t0xrx7vatd5mx|Hanji 744]] presents him as a clean and fair Shu commandery official promoted by [[EmperorZhangOfHan|Emperor Zhang]] to Sikong, with the episode also explaining the compound surname "Fifth" as a source-scoped genealogy. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-745-donghan-zui-xuexing-zhi-zhan-zhengjiu-dabing-genggong-lhg3pppaovl5mxawgmj-dgjgth4d|Hanji 745]] then shows him opposing the immediate [[GengGongEasternHan|Geng Gong]] rescue dispatch while also warning that Ma-family appointments, guests, and gifts could turn outer relatives into "作威作福" ministers. Hanji 749 later has him warn against giving heavy command to imperial in-laws and criticize court luxury as failed example. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-752-shiqu-kaoshan-de-ren-gai-ruhe-chushi-lt0qb7alyh0ipf6mk8hvz92cs297|Hanji 752]] then shows him warning that [[DouXianEasternHan|Dou Xian]] and [[DouDuEasternHan|Dou Du]] are becoming dangerous through empress-side kinship, palace access, favor, and guest networks, while Hanji 755-756 place him in the Xiongnu restitution debate and in the source's strongest integrity material.

## Current Profile

Hanji 744 gives Fifth Lun a clean-administration and office-entry layer. The source describes his Shu commandery service as fair, clean, and good at recommending people to fitting offices; Zhangdi appoints him Sikong in late 75. The episode also explains the compound surname Fifth through a Qin-Han relocation genealogy of Tian Qi royal branches, but that identity note remains source-scoped rather than becoming a separate political claim.

Hanji 745 makes him mixed rather than simply "pro-rescue." In the Geng Gong debate, he opposes dispatching relief forces after Guan Chong requests aid, while [[BaoYuEasternHan|Bao Yu]] successfully argues that abandonment would damage loyal service and frontier credibility. The same source then shows Fifth Lun in his more durable role: he warns Zhangdi that appointing [[MaLiaoEasternHan|Ma Liao]], [[MaFangEasternHan|Ma Fang]], and Ma Guang while their household gathers guests and distributes major gifts risks ministerial overreach and family-state disaster.

Hanji 749 gives the later heavy-office version of the same outer-relative concern. When Zhangdi sends Ma Fang to command the Qiang campaign, Fifth Lun argues that imperial relatives may be granted wealth but should not receive important offices. His reasoning is institutional: if they fail, punishment damages kinship; if they are protected, law loses force. The warning is not refuted by Ma Fang's victory, because the later impeachment of Geng Gong after offending Ma Fang keeps the accountability problem visible.

The same source also makes Fifth Lun a critic of court example. He criticizes harsh local officials and the luxury of princes, princesses, and in-law households because the capital cannot demand better local governance while modeling extraction and display. This places him near [[TopDownExemplarGovernance|top-down exemplar governance]] as well as outer-relative restraint.

Hanji 752 presents Fifth Lun as an early anti-Dou warning voice. The warning is structural rather than personal gossip: Dou Xian and Dou Du are close to the palace because of Empress Dou, receive favor and reward, and gather guests. Fifth Lun's role is to tell Zhangdi that outer-relative access should be checked before it becomes hard to control. The warning is validated by Dou Xian's estate coercion and later Dou-family expansion.

Hanji 755-756 add the court policy and integrity layers. After Southern Xiongnu seizes Northern Xiongnu people and livestock, Fifth Lun and [[ZhengHongEasternHan|Zheng Hong]] oppose restitution while [[HuanYuEasternHan|Huan Yu]] and [[YuanAnEasternHan|Yuan An]] support it. When the dispute escalates, Zhangdi treats heated disagreement as normal in major affairs and orders the ministers back into deliberation. Hanji 756 then frames Fifth Lun as devoted, forthright, plain, honest, and unusually self-scrutinizing about private motive after a refused horse gift.

## Key Characteristics

- Clean commandery administrator promoted to Sikong at the beginning of Zhangdi's reign.
- Early Zhangdi-era minister who warns against giving imperial in-laws heavy office or command.
- Cautious rescue skeptic whose non-dispatch position is overridden by Bao Yu in the Geng Gong crisis.
- Early Ma-family warning voice who reads guest networks and large private gifts as political danger.
- Court-example critic who links harsh administration and elite luxury to failed central modeling.
- Anti-Dou warning voice who sees palace access, favor, and guest networks as structural danger.
- Clean and forthright officeholder whose integrity is framed through plainness, public devotion, and self-scrutiny.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 756, Hanji 755, Hanji 752, Hanji 749, Hanji 745, and Hanji 744 and normalizes the transcript's 第五轮 wording to 第五伦 / Fifth Lun. Hanji 744 adds the clean-Shu and Sikong-appointment layer, but the surname genealogy and exact office-date detail remain source-scoped. Hanji 745 shows his caution can point in different directions: his non-rescue stance loses to Bao Yu, while his Ma-family warning strengthens the later outer-relative profile. Hanji 749 provides a structural warning about Ma Fang's appointment, but Ma Fang's battlefield success means the claim should not be read as a competence accusation. The source's "慎独" framing is treated as the host's ethical synthesis from the horse-gift anecdote, not as a complete doctrine of moral psychology.
