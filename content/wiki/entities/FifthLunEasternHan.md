## Overview

第五伦 / Fifth Lun enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-756-weishenme-quan-ni-de-rao-ren-chu-qie-rao-ren-ltn4tqzy1d-sodhjidncv8dyqxpj|Hanji 756]] as a senior Eastern Han official whose public profile combines forthright court participation, clean officeholding, retirement from the sikong post, and unusually strict self-examination about private motives.

## Current Profile

The episode first places Fifth Lun beside [[ZhengHongEasternHan|Zheng Hong]] and Wei Biao during the dispute over returning [[NorthernXiongnuLateHan|Northern Xiongnu]] captives and livestock. Their anger leads them to submit seals and ribbons, but [[EmperorZhangOfHan|Emperor Zhang]] treats disagreement as normal in major affairs and restores the officials to the discussion.

Fifth Lun's more durable profile is clean and direct public service. The source says he serves with full devotion, states his views sharply, avoids ambiguous fence-sitting, and remains plain and honest in office. When age and illness make continued service difficult, he asks to retire from the sikong post; Zhangdi grants removal and gives him a lifetime two-thousand-shi stipend.

The episode's strongest character evidence is inward. Asked whether he has private motives, Fifth Lun says someone once offered him a fine horse. He refused the gift and did not recommend the donor, but he still noticed himself remembering the donor when later recommending talent. For the source, the point is not corruption but self-scrutiny: Fifth Lun treats even a faint internal pull as evidence that private interest has to be watched.

## Key Characteristics

- Senior minister who participates in open but heated court dispute.
- Forthright official whose opinions are direct rather than evasive.
- Clean officeholder whose integrity is framed through plainness and public devotion.
- Self-scrutinizing figure who notices private motive even when he rejects the gift and does not act on it.
- Retired high official whose departure marks a shift in Zhangdi-era court staffing.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 756 and normalizes the source's 第五轮 wording to 第五伦 / Fifth Lun. It does not reconstruct his full official career. The source's "慎独" framing is treated as the host's ethical synthesis from the horse-gift anecdote, not as a complete doctrine of moral psychology.

## What Changed

- Created Fifth Lun as an Eastern Han clean-office and self-scrutiny figure in the Zhangdi-era court.
