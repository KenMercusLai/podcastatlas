## Overview

第五伦 / Fifth Lun now has an earlier Zhangdi-era warning layer before the anti-Dou, Xiongnu-policy, and integrity profile. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-749-guanliren-bikan-fa-jiangjin-shi-guanli-de-zuijia-shouduan-ma-lnhhoh0oxlstuwakl3rsqvcss-cf|Hanji 749]] shows him warning [[EmperorZhangOfHan|Emperor Zhang]] against giving heavy command to imperial in-laws and criticizing court luxury as failed example. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-752-shiqu-kaoshan-de-ren-gai-ruhe-chushi-lt0qb7alyh0ipf6mk8hvz92cs297|Hanji 752]] then shows him warning that [[DouXianEasternHan|Dou Xian]] and [[DouDuEasternHan|Dou Du]] are becoming dangerous through empress-side kinship, palace access, favor, and guest networks, while Hanji 755-756 place him in the Xiongnu restitution debate and in the source's strongest integrity material.

## Current Profile

Hanji 749 gives Fifth Lun his earliest current role. When Zhangdi sends [[MaFangEasternHan|Ma Fang]] to command the Qiang campaign, Fifth Lun argues that imperial relatives may be granted wealth but should not receive important offices. His reasoning is institutional: if they fail, punishment damages kinship; if they are protected, law loses force. The warning is not refuted by Ma Fang's victory, because the later impeachment of [[GengGongEasternHan|Geng Gong]] after offending Ma Fang keeps the accountability problem visible.

The same source also makes Fifth Lun a critic of court example. He criticizes harsh local officials and the luxury of princes, princesses, and in-law households because the capital cannot demand better local governance while modeling extraction and display. This places him near [[TopDownExemplarGovernance|top-down exemplar governance]] as well as outer-relative restraint.

Hanji 752 presents Fifth Lun as an early anti-Dou warning voice. The warning is structural rather than personal gossip: Dou Xian and Dou Du are close to the palace because of Empress Dou, receive favor and reward, and gather guests. Fifth Lun's role is to tell Zhangdi that outer-relative access should be checked before it becomes hard to control. The warning is validated by Dou Xian's estate coercion and later Dou-family expansion.

Hanji 755-756 add the court policy and integrity layers. After Southern Xiongnu seizes Northern Xiongnu people and livestock, Fifth Lun and [[ZhengHongEasternHan|Zheng Hong]] oppose restitution while [[HuanYuEasternHan|Huan Yu]] and [[YuanAnEasternHan|Yuan An]] support it. When the dispute escalates, Zhangdi treats heated disagreement as normal in major affairs and orders the ministers back into deliberation. Hanji 756 then frames Fifth Lun as devoted, forthright, plain, honest, and unusually self-scrutinizing about private motive after a refused horse gift.

## Key Characteristics

- Early Zhangdi-era minister who warns against giving imperial in-laws heavy office or command.
- Court-example critic who links harsh administration and elite luxury to failed central modeling.
- Anti-Dou warning voice who sees palace access, favor, and guest networks as structural danger.
- Senior minister who initially opposes restitution in the Xiongnu captive-and-livestock dispute.
- Court participant whose resignation gesture is refused because Zhangdi wants continued deliberation.
- Clean and forthright officeholder whose integrity is framed through plainness and public devotion.
- Self-scrutinizing figure who notices private motive even when he rejects the gift and does not act on it.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 749, Hanji 752, Hanji 755, and Hanji 756 and normalizes the transcript's 第五轮 wording to 第五伦 / Fifth Lun. Hanji 749 provides a structural warning about Ma Fang's appointment, but Ma Fang's battlefield success means the claim should not be read as a competence accusation. The source's "慎独" framing is treated as the host's ethical synthesis from the horse-gift anecdote, not as a complete doctrine of moral psychology.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 749 as Fifth Lun's earliest warning layer on in-law command and court example.
- Reframed Hanji 752 as a later anti-Dou version of the same outer-relative access concern.
- Preserved Hanji 755 as his initial no-restitution stance in the Xiongnu policy debate.
- Preserved Hanji 756 as the main integrity, retirement, and self-scrutiny layer.
