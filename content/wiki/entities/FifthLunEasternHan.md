## Overview

第五伦 / Fifth Lun now has an earlier Zhangdi-era warning layer before the Hanji 755-756 policy and integrity profile. Hanji 752 shows him warning [[EmperorZhangOfHan|Emperor Zhang]] that [[DouXianEasternHan|Dou Xian]] and [[DouDuEasternHan|Dou Du]] are becoming dangerous through empress-side kinship, palace access, favor, and guest networks. Hanji 755-756 then place him in the Xiongnu restitution debate and in the source's strongest integrity and self-scrutiny material.

## Current Profile

Hanji 752 presents Fifth Lun as an early anti-Dou warning voice. The warning is structural rather than personal gossip: Dou Xian and Dou Du are close to the palace because of Empress Dou, receive favor and reward, and gather guests. Fifth Lun's role is to tell Zhangdi that outer-relative access should be checked before it becomes hard to control. The warning is validated by Dou Xian's estate coercion and later Dou-family expansion.

Hanji 755-756 add the court policy layer. After Southern Xiongnu seizes Northern Xiongnu people and livestock, [[MengYunEasternHan|Meng Yun]] warns that restitution is needed for Han credibility. Fifth Lun and [[ZhengHongEasternHan|Zheng Hong]] oppose the return, while [[HuanYuEasternHan|Huan Yu]] and [[YuanAnEasternHan|Yuan An]] support it. When the dispute escalates, Fifth Lun joins Zheng Hong and Wei Biao in submitting seals and ribbons, but Zhangdi treats heated disagreement as normal in major affairs and orders the ministers back into deliberation.

Fifth Lun's durable profile is clean and direct public service. Hanji 756 says he serves with full devotion, states his views sharply, avoids ambiguous fence-sitting, and remains plain and honest in office. When age and illness make continued service difficult, he asks to retire from the sikong post; Zhangdi grants removal and gives him a lifetime two-thousand-shi stipend.

The strongest character evidence is inward. Asked whether he has private motives, Fifth Lun says someone once offered him a fine horse. He refused the gift and did not recommend the donor, but he still noticed himself remembering the donor when later recommending talent. For the source, the point is self-scrutiny: Fifth Lun treats even a faint internal pull as evidence that private interest has to be watched.

## Key Characteristics

- Early Zhangdi-era minister who warns against Dou-family access, favor, and guest networks.
- Senior minister who initially opposes restitution in the Xiongnu captive-and-livestock dispute.
- Court participant whose resignation gesture is refused because Zhangdi wants continued deliberation.
- Forthright official whose opinions are direct rather than evasive.
- Clean officeholder whose integrity is framed through plainness and public devotion.
- Self-scrutinizing figure who notices private motive even when he rejects the gift and does not act on it.
- Retired high official whose departure marks a shift in Zhangdi-era court staffing.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 752, Hanji 755, and Hanji 756 and normalizes the transcript's 第五轮 wording to 第五伦 / Fifth Lun. Hanji 752 provides an early outer-relative warning, Hanji 755 provides a policy stance, and Hanji 756 provides the integrity and self-scrutiny material. The source's "慎独" framing is treated as the host's ethical synthesis from the horse-gift anecdote, not as a complete doctrine of moral psychology.
