# Melissa Sheldrup

## Overview

Melissa Sheldrup is the researcher in [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-01b4ea684c25d46bfca84c97e3c8f96f-52625712-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-01b4ea684c25d46bfca84c97e3c8f96f-52625712|Work 2.0: Life, Interrupted]] who studies working memory, interruption recovery, and transcranial direct-current stimulation.

## Current Profile

Sheldrup's contribution is methodological as much as technological. The laboratory task measures performance on a primary rule-based game, answers to interruption questions, and the speed and accuracy of resuming the main task. She treats the host's apparent improvement under stimulation as insufficient evidence because practice, placebo response, response time, and the next action after interruption all have to be considered.

She also supplies the episode's social boundary: cognitive enhancement does not determine what happens to the capacity it creates. Faster recovery can return time to a person or become a reason to assign more work.

## Key Characteristics

- Studies working memory as a factor in recovery from interruption.
- Uses multidimensional performance measures rather than a single correct-answer count.
- Presents transcranial direct-current stimulation as a temporary, diffuse research tool.
- Explicitly rejects causal inference from one participant and two game rounds.
- Separates technical efficiency from decisions about workload and leisure.
