# 1968 United States Presidential Election

## Overview

The 1968 United States presidential election is the contest in which [[RichardNixon]] defeated [[HubertHumphrey]] while [[GeorgeWallace]] carried five states as a third-party candidate.

## Current Profile

The election is synthesized as a close result produced by two different campaign trajectories. Nixon converted a large post-convention lead into a narrow victory through message discipline, controlled television, and appeals to the "forgotten Americans." Humphrey recovered after separating himself from Johnson's Vietnam policy, Democratic organizations reactivated, and the administration announced a bombing halt, but the recovery stopped short of victory.

## Key Characteristics

- National disorder, racial conflict, Vietnam, and Democratic convention chaos shaped the campaign environment.
- Nixon combined reassurance and reconciliation language with law-and-order and anti-busing signals.
- Humphrey's late break with Johnson over bombing North Vietnam restored anti-war support and donors.
- Wallace won five states and roughly ten million votes but lost support to both major candidates in different regions.
- The result remained close enough that California, Ohio, Illinois, and late urban returns mattered on election night.

## Evidence

- Campaign environment and messaging: [[513-america-in-68-nixons-great-comeback-part-6-glt3853956463]] links Nixon's lead to Chicago convention disorder, televised unrest, Vietnam, and a disciplined appeal to quiet middle America.
- Democratic recovery: [[513-america-in-68-nixons-great-comeback-part-6-glt3853956463]] says Humphrey's September 30 bombing position reduced protests, restored liberal support, and narrowed the gap.
- Result and third-party role: [[513-america-in-68-nixons-great-comeback-part-6-glt3853956463]] describes Wallace's five-state showing and Nixon's popular-vote victory by about half a million votes out of more than seventy million.

## Qualifications

This profile reflects one retrospective podcast episode centered on Nixon rather than a comprehensive electoral history. Exact polling movements, turnout mechanisms, regional vote transfers, and the effect of the October bombing halt are not independently audited here.
