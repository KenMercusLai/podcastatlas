# Leopold II

## Overview

Leopold II was the Belgian king who personally controlled the [[CongoFreeState|Congo Free State]], sought [[CharlesGeorgeGordon|General Gordon]]'s anti-slavery prestige for the project, and later defended its coercive extraction through propaganda and lobbying.

## Current Profile

The sources present Leopold through the construction, operation, and defense of the system in his name. Before Gordon returned to Sudan, Leopold offered him a Congo role that could place an admired Christian abolitionist in front of the colonial project; the source suggests Gordon may already have been suspicious. Later claims of progress and civilization accompanied a concessionary economy enforced through coercion, violence, and population flight.

When reform pressure grew, Leopold answered with books, articles, lobby groups, hypocrisy charges, concessions, and attempted influence in Europe and the United States. His own commission corroborated serious abuses, the Kowalski scandal exposed paid American lobbying, and Belgium took over the Congo in 1908, though it compensated Leopold and retained much of the colonial apparatus.

## Key Characteristics

- Held the Congo Free State as a personal possession during Conrad's 1890 journey.
- Sought Gordon's anti-slavery reputation as humanitarian legitimacy for the Congo project.
- Used civilising and developmental claims to justify an extractive colonial project.
- Presided over a system associated in the episode with forced labor, armed violence, and ivory and rubber exports.
- Became a target of Conrad's later denunciation and of reform campaigns connected to [[RogerCasement]].
- Used propaganda, paid advocacy, lobbying, and economic concessions to defend his rule and divide critics.
- Lost control when official corroboration and lobbying scandals made reform pressure harder to contain.

## Evidence

- Political control: [[541-heart-of-darkness-fear-and-loathing-in-the-congo-glt5482142786]] identifies the Congo Free State as Leopold's regime during Conrad's service.
- Gordon recruitment: [[142-general-gordon-and-the-siege-of-khartoum-glt6227664313]] describes Leopold's offer and interprets Gordon's humanitarian reputation as political cover for the Congo project.
- Justification and practice: [[541-heart-of-darkness-fear-and-loathing-in-the-congo-glt5482142786]] contrasts claims of progress with forced labor, beatings, starvation, and extraction.
- Conrad's verdict: [[541-heart-of-darkness-fear-and-loathing-in-the-congo-glt5482142786]] reports Conrad's later description of Leopold and his agents as an obscene predatory system.
- Counter-campaign: [[540-horror-in-the-congo-a-conspiracy-unmasked-part-3-glt5172130471]] describes Leopold's publishing, lobbying, influence efforts, and charges of British hypocrisy.
- Commission and scandal: [[540-horror-in-the-congo-a-conspiracy-unmasked-part-3-glt5172130471]] says his investigating commission substantiated floggings, hostages, killings, and mutilations, while Henry I. Kowalski exposed American influence buying after his contract ended.
- Transfer: [[540-horror-in-the-congo-a-conspiracy-unmasked-part-3-glt5172130471]] records the 1908 Belgian agreement to pay Leopold 50 million francs for the Congo.

## Qualifications

The sources do not provide a full Belgian political biography, independently audit mortality estimates, or adjudicate every reported atrocity. The interpretation of Leopold's purpose in recruiting Gordon and Gordon's suspicion remains source-scoped. Attention to Leopold's image should not substitute for analysis of the institutions and agents that operated the regime.
