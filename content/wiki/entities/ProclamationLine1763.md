# Proclamation Line of 1763

## Overview

The Proclamation Line of 1763 was Britain's attempt to restrict colonial settlement beyond the Appalachians after the [[SevenYearsWar|Seven Years' War]].

## Current Profile

The line appears as a frontier-security measure whose administrative logic conflicted with colonial land hunger. British officials sought to manage Native alliances and limit costly conflict after Pontiac's rebellion, while settlers and speculators such as [[GeorgeWashington|George Washington]] regarded western territory as opportunity opened by victory over France. Enforcing the boundary required troops, binding land policy to the postwar taxation dispute.

## Key Characteristics

- British restriction on settlement west of the Appalachians.
- Temporary security and alliance policy rather than a complete abandonment of expansion.
- Response to the postwar frontier and Pontiac's rebellion.
- Obstacle to colonial settlers and western land speculators.
- Link between territorial governance, troop costs, and imperial revenue.

## Evidence

- Strategic purpose: [[347-the-american-revolution-part-1-glt3042850673]] says Britain sought to restrain settlement and preserve workable relations with Native nations.
- Colonial conflict: [[347-the-american-revolution-part-1-glt3042850673]] contrasts that goal with Washington's and other colonists' interest in trans-Appalachian land.
- Fiscal consequence: [[347-the-american-revolution-part-1-glt3042850673]] links enforcement to regular troops and the question of colonial contribution.

## Qualifications

The source does not provide a full legal text, implementation history, Native diplomatic account, or comparison among colonial regions. The line's temporary character and practical enforcement remain source-scoped.
