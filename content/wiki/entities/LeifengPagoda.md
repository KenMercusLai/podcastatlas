# 雷峰塔 / Leifeng Pagoda

## Overview
雷峰塔 / Leifeng Pagoda appears in the [[BowuzhiPodcast|博物志]] Hangzhou episode through the hosts' visit to related Zhejiang Museum exhibitions on brick inscriptions and underground-palace finds.

## Current Profile
The episode uses Leifeng Pagoda to continue the same interpretation question raised by the ancient-books exhibition. Inscriptions, bricks, reliquary objects, miniature pagodas, gilt trays, small Buddha figures, and a disputed glass bottle can be visually compelling, but the hosts stress that labels must explain object identity and uncertainty clearly.

## Key Characteristics
- Connected in the source to [[QianHongchu|钱弘俶]] and Sun Taizhen, with the early name "皇妃塔" mentioned.
- Remembered through brick inscriptions that record production units, uses, names, symbols, and uncertain marks.
- Associated with popular beliefs that tower bricks could ward off evil, aid sericulture, or help produce sons.
- Represents a case where unclear object labeling can weaken an otherwise strong exhibition.

## Evidence
- Historical association - [[246-hangzhou-bowuguan-yiwei-hangzhi-haojiu-mei-kan-guo-zheme-tuijian-de-zhanlan-le-8217911423-973793]] links the pagoda to Qian Hongchu, Sun Taizhen, and the "皇妃塔" name.
- Collapse explanation - [[246-hangzhou-bowuguan-yiwei-hangzhi-haojiu-mei-kan-guo-zheme-tuijian-de-zhanlan-le-8217911423-973793]] says folk use and theft of bricks contributed to the tower's collapse.
- Exhibition-label issue - [[246-hangzhou-bowuguan-yiwei-hangzhi-haojiu-mei-kan-guo-zheme-tuijian-de-zhanlan-le-8217911423-973793]] notes uncertainty over whether a transparent glass bottle was an excavated object or display support.
