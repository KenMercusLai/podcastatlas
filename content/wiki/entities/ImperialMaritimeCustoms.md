# 中国海关总税务司署 / Chinese Imperial Maritime Customs Service

## Overview

The Chinese Imperial Maritime Customs Service is the late Qing revenue and administrative institution through which [[RobertHart|Robert Hart]] exercised long-term authority.

## Current Profile

In the bounded source, the service shows the double character of imported state capacity. Foreign personnel, rules, training, and port networks could improve revenue collection and administrative consistency, yet control over money, appointments, and force remained politically sensitive. The [[LayOsbornFlotilla|Lay-Osborn Flotilla]] makes that limit visible: customs-backed procurement could not substitute for an agreed sovereign chain of command.

## Key Characteristics

- Customs-revenue institution operating across treaty ports.
- Administrative channel for foreign technical personnel inside Qing government.
- Associated in the source with standardized rules, training, efficiency, and relative probity.
- Revenue base with spillovers into education, diplomacy, and military procurement.

## Evidence

- Institutional expansion: [[40-kuaguo-dangguan-de-keqing-lisi-qinchao-chengxiang-vs-hede-daqing-huangjia-haiguan-zongshuiwusi-luulmw-t1-oe2-klv64-f6nk-fgc3em-m]] credits Hart with extending the service to coastal and inland ports.
- Sovereignty boundary: [[40-kuaguo-dangguan-de-keqing-lisi-qinchao-chengxiang-vs-hede-daqing-huangjia-haiguan-zongshuiwusi-luulmw-t1-oe2-klv64-f6nk-fgc3em-m]] connects customs revenue and personnel to the [[LayOsbornFlotilla|flotilla]] whose command arrangement Qing officials rejected.

## Qualifications

The source does not provide an independent institutional history or audited measures of revenue, corruption, staffing, or performance. Its modernization language is retained as episode attribution and must be balanced against the treaty-port and imperial setting.
