## Overview

[[JiangWan|蒋琬]] is a Shu-Han official whose episode profile moves from failed county administration to senior succession after [[ZhugeLiang|诸葛亮]], making him a case of role fit, second-look evaluation, and bounded tolerance for early failure.

## Current Profile

Hanji 1149 says Jiang Wan followed [[LiuBei|刘备]] into Yi and became magistrate of Guangdu, where drinking and neglected business nearly led to execution. Zhuge Liang intervened with the judgment that Jiang Wan was a state-scale instrument rather than a “hundred-li” county talent and that his governing priority was public stability rather than surface performance.

The episode then follows the second evaluation: Jiang Wan re-enters service, works under Zhuge Liang, receives repeated endorsement, and after Zhuge Liang's death holds senior responsibility including general-in-chief and grand marshal. The durable judgment is not that poor performance should be excused, but that evaluation should distinguish misconduct, assignment scale, and later evidence before closing a career permanently.

## Key Characteristics

- Performs poorly in an early county-magistrate assignment.
- Receives a second chance through Zhuge Liang's scale-of-talent judgment.
- Is presented as stronger in broad state responsibility than routine local administration.
- Builds later trust through service rather than through the original defense alone.
- Becomes part of Shu-Han's post-Zhuge-Liang governing succession.

## Evidence

- Role mismatch and reprieve: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1149-jiemi-sanguo-dingji-moushi-xunyou-de-guanchang-shengcunshu-ljv1fwephe86-qczfy0o1n2yh7um|Hanji 1149]] reports neglected Guangdu business, Liu Bei's anger, and Zhuge Liang's “state instrument” defense.
- Later trust: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1149-jiemi-sanguo-dingji-moushi-xunyou-de-guanchang-shengcunshu-ljv1fwephe86-qczfy0o1n2yh7um|Hanji 1149]] reports renewed service, work under Zhuge Liang, explicit succession endorsement, and later senior Shu-Han office.

## Qualifications

The episode's role-fit reading should not erase Jiang Wan's reported neglect of duty or prove that all poor local performers are hidden strategists. Office sequence, titles, conduct, dialogue, and the causal link between one defense and later advancement require direct historical-text checking.

## What Changed

- Established Jiang Wan's profile around reassessment after a failed early assignment.
- Connected his later succession role to [[TalentRoleFit|人才—岗位适配]] without treating fit as immunity from accountability.

## Relationships

- [[LiuBei|刘备]] - ruler who appoints Jiang Wan at Guangdu and nearly punishes him for neglect.
- [[ZhugeLiang|诸葛亮]] - evaluator, protector, superior, and reported advocate for Jiang Wan's later succession.
- [[TalentRoleFit|人才—岗位适配]] - concept illustrated by the difference between county routine and state-scale responsibility.
- [[SecondInCommandRoleFit|二把手角色适配]] - adjacent framework for the later senior supporting and succession role.
