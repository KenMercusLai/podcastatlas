# Operation Ceasefire (Boston)

## Overview

Operation Ceasefire is the Boston violence-reduction program presented in [[wolfgang-s-law-part-two|Wolfgang's Law - Part Two]] as the first major application of [[FocusedDeterrence]].

## Current Profile

The program targeted a small, identifiable, highly connected group associated with a disproportionate share of serious violence. Its intervention combined direct communication, community moral pressure, practical assistance, clear warnings, and predictable enforcement rather than relying on broad arrests or punishment alone.

## Key Characteristics

- Concentrates attention on a small connected violence network.
- Joins offers of help to credible and clearly communicated consequences.
- Depends on coordination among law enforcement, community actors, and service providers.
- Serves in the episode as both an evidence claim and an implementation model for later cities.

## Evidence

### Intervention design

- [[wolfgang-s-law-part-two]] describes the program's combination of direct communication, community support, assistance, warnings, and enforcement.

### Reported outcome

- [[wolfgang-s-law-part-two]] reports Boston murders falling from 96 in 1995 to 59, 43, 35, and 31 in 1999 after the intervention began.

## Qualifications

The reported time series does not alone identify a causal effect, establish which component mattered most, or show that the design transfers unchanged to other cities. The supplied summary does not provide comparison groups, confidence intervals, displacement analysis, or community-level harms and benefits.

## What Changed

- Created a source-bounded program profile separating the full support-and-enforcement model from punishment-only imitation.
