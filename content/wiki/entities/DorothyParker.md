# Dorothy Parker

## Overview

Dorothy Parker was a writer and screenwriter who, with Alan Campbell, wrote an original [[AStarIsBorn1937|*A Star Is Born*]] sequence in which the intoxicated Norman Maine causes a fatal road collision.

## Current Profile

[[star-struck]] presents Parker's scene as more than plot escalation. By making Norman face the strangers killed by his decision to drive drunk, it converts addiction from private tragedy into public danger and gives responsibility a vivid human form.

The scene was removed before release, so its proposed cultural effect remains counterfactual. Parker's role is nevertheless significant because the abandoned draft shows that a less permissive moral representation was imaginable inside 1937 Hollywood even when the released film chose another center of sympathy.

## Key Characteristics

- Writer and screenwriter credited in the episode with Alan Campbell on the original screenplay.
- Co-author of a fatal-collision sequence centered on an intoxicated Norman Maine.
- Writer whose scene makes addiction's risk to strangers narratively visible.
- Counterfactual cultural figure rather than a proven cause of legal or social reform.

## Evidence

### Screenplay authorship

- [[star-struck]] attributes the deleted Malibu Road collision sequence to Parker and Campbell's screenplay.

### Moral construction

- [[star-struck]] describes the scene ending with the injured Norman staring at the burning wreck he caused.

### Production outcome

- [[star-struck]] reports that Selznick removed the sequence after an internal memo treated its consequences as incompatible with Norman's remaining story.

## Qualifications

The bounded source does not establish the division of labor between Parker and Campbell, reproduce the full screenplay, or compare all drafts. Claims about Parker's intended public message and the scene's hypothetical audience effect remain interpretive.
