# Moon

## Overview
The Moon is Earth's natural satellite and, across the bounded sources, a historical landing site, scientific field environment, cultural symbol, governance problem, industrial proposal, and proving ground for deeper spaceflight.

## Current Profile
The source set now connects the uncertainty of [[ApolloProgram|Apollo]] to renewed lunar activity. [[438-the-moonwalkers-with-tom-hanks-glt1422432857]] restores the Moon as the unfamiliar environment where landing, ascent, rendezvous, geology, memory, and personal artifacts converged. Later sources shift from first arrival to sustained presence: [[Artemis2]], resources, rules, energy, logistics, manufacturing, debris, and the conditional role of the Moon inside [[MoonMarsStrategySplit]].

## Key Characteristics
- Historical destination where Apollo joined extreme mission risk to globally visible human achievement.
- Scientific field site whose rocks and extended surface missions expanded lunar geology.
- Personal and memorial landscape containing flags, photographs, sculpture, texts, initials, and mission hardware.
- Governance environment where non-appropriation, extraction, safety zones, and non-signatory powers create unresolved rules.
- Infrastructure test bed requiring stable power, mobility, communications, logistics, and debris management.
- Potential low-gravity industrial base whose commercial value remains uncertain.
- Near-Earth proving ground for systems that may support, but do not automatically solve, travel to [[Mars]].

## Qualifications
- Apollo anecdotes, risk language, artifact lists, and geology proportions remain episode-attributed rather than a complete lunar-science history.
- International law blocks territorial claims, but the practical boundaries of extraction, safety zones, and enforcement remain contested.
- Resource, helium-3, nuclear-power, orbital-compute, and manufacturing proposals do not establish near-term viability.
- Lunar experience does not transfer automatically to Mars because gravity, atmosphere, radiation, terrain, logistics, and return conditions differ.
- The discarded-rocket case illustrates debris risk but does not establish the scale of future lunar contamination.
