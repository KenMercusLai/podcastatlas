## Overview

韩颓当 / Han Tuidang appears in the current wiki as a Western Han commander sent to receive and dispose of defeated rebels after the [[RebellionOfTheSevenStatesWesternHan|Rebellion of the Seven States]]. His exchange with [[LiuAngJiaoxiKingWesternHan|胶西王刘昂]] gives the episode its clearest statement of the court's legal-political case.

## Current Profile

Han Tuidang first sends Liu Ang a letter stating that he has imperial authority to punish rebels: submission can bring pardon and restoration of rank, while refusal will bring immediate destruction. When Liu Ang appears at his camp and claims that the rising was only against Chao Cuo, Han shifts the question from slogan to authorization and conduct. He asks why the kings did not petition the throne, notes that they lacked an edict and tiger tally, and points to their attack on a neighboring kingdom that refused participation.

The source also identifies Han as a son of the rebel Han Xin, says he returned to Han with his family after his father's death, calls him an important commander in suppressing the Seven States, and names him as an ancestor of [[HanYu|韩愈]]. Those biographical and genealogical claims remain episode-attributed.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han commander acting under an imperial mandate in the rebellion's settlement.
- Negotiator who combines a surrender offer with a threat of immediate destruction.
- Examiner who tests a stated political purpose against lawful authorization and observable wartime conduct.
- Figure whose own family background is presented as crossing from paternal rebellion back into Han service.
- Claimed ancestor of Han Yu in the episode's cultural digression.

## Qualifications

This page is based on one short episode. Han Tuidang's exact title, mandate, letter, dialogue, pardon authority, command role, paternal history, return to Han, and genealogy require comparison with transmitted records. The page does not infer that the offered restoration was available after every form of rebellion or that Han independently determined the final sentence.

## What Changed

- Established Han Tuidang's role in the surrender and adjudication of Liu Ang.
- Identified the shift from declared motive to authorization and conduct as his main analytical contribution.
