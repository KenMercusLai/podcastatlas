## Overview

毋寡 / Wugua is the [[DayuanWesternHan|Dayuan]] king associated in the current evidence with the refusal of [[HanWudi|Han Wudi]]'s horse-exchange mission and the later siege settlement in which Dayuan nobles kill him and offer his head to the Han army.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-372-1-hanwan-zhanzheng-de-beihou-de-lishi-zhenxiang-lqbklk0e0cplyuojpvsrx2u4fnyv|Hanji 372-1]] supplies the decision before war. Wugua and his ministers reason that distance, Xiongnu interference on a northern route, water and pasture scarcity on a southern route, and high mortality among much smaller Han missions make a major punitive expedition implausible. They therefore refuse to surrender the Ershi horses. The judgment separates a sound logistical diagnosis from a failed deterrence forecast: those constraints later damage Han's first expedition, but do not prevent repeated invasion.

The same source does not make Wugua solely responsible for escalation. It says the Han envoy behaves arrogantly, abuses the Dayuan court, and strikes and abandons the golden horse after refusal. Dayuan then expels the mission and has the Yucheng king intercept it, killing the envoys and seizing their property. The episode attributes the court decision to Dayuan actors collectively rather than documenting Wugua's personal order in full.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-374-2-hanwudi-qingjin-quanli-da-dayuan-zhi-bu-zhi-lmfzkllejz3m0vanmnu7gyxtwbul|Hanji 374-2]] gives the endpoint. During Li Guangli's siege, Dayuan nobles blame Wugua for hiding the best horses and killing Han envoys, kill him, and use his head within an offer of horses, grain, and withdrawal. Wugua thus becomes both the prewar decision-maker in the source's narrative and the internal scapegoat through whom Dayuan elites preserve the polity.

## Key Characteristics

- Dayuan king who rejects Han's request for Ershi horses after assessing the invasion route as prohibitively difficult.
- Makes a strong logistical diagnosis but a failed prediction about Han's willingness to launch repeated punitive expeditions.
- Rules within an elite political structure capable of reversing policy and removing the king under siege.
- Becomes the person blamed for horse refusal and envoy killing when nobles seek a negotiated settlement.

## Evidence

- Refusal calculation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-372-1-hanwan-zhanzheng-de-beihou-de-lishi-zhenxiang-lqbklk0e0cplyuojpvsrx2u4fnyv|Hanji 372-1]] attributes to Wugua and his ministers the distance, route, water, pasture, and mission-mortality reasons for judging Han retaliation unlikely.
- Escalation boundary: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-372-1-hanwan-zhanzheng-de-beihou-de-lishi-zhenxiang-lqbklk0e0cplyuojpvsrx2u4fnyv|Hanji 372-1]] says Dayuan expels the insulting mission and then has it intercepted, killed, and robbed.
- Elite removal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-374-2-hanwudi-qingjin-quanli-da-dayuan-zhi-bu-zhi-lmfzkllejz3m0vanmnu7gyxtwbul|Hanji 374-2]] says nobles kill Wugua, present his head, and bargain with horses and provisions to end the siege.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to two popular-history episode summaries. Wugua's name form, the exact structure of Dayuan deliberation, whether he personally ordered the mission's killing, the nobles' stated reasons and private motives, and the details of his death remain source-scoped. Correctly identifying logistical barriers did not make the refusal irrational by itself, and the page does not treat Han's demand for valued horses as an unquestioned entitlement.

## What Changed

- Created a bounded profile connecting Wugua's prewar deterrence calculation to his removal during the siege settlement.
- Distinguished accurate logistical assessment from the mistaken belief that logistical difficulty would prevent Han retaliation.
