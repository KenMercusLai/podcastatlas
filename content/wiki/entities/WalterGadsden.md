# Walter Gadsden

## Overview

Walter Gadsden is the Black teenager photographed beside Birmingham police officer [[RichardMiddleton]] and the dog Leo on May 3, 1963, in an image later transformed into the [[FootSoldierMonument]].

## Current Profile

Gadsden's 1996 oral-history account resists the identity imposed by the image's public afterlife. He said he skipped school to see events surrounding [[MartinLutherKingJr|Martin Luther King Jr.]], but was an observer rather than a movement participant. After passing behind a barricade, he encountered Middleton and Leo; he remembered being bitten as Middleton grabbed him, without presenting the event as a deliberately ordered attack.

His objection is both factual and representational. He rejected the “foot soldier” label, criticized parts of the movement, and objected to [[RonaldMcDowell]] making him smaller, younger, darker-skinned, and more conventionally African-looking. His testimony makes him an agent contesting public history rather than merely the passive subject of an iconic image.

## Key Characteristics

- Teenager captured in a nationally circulated Birmingham civil-rights photograph.
- Described himself as a school-skipping observer rather than an organized marcher.
- Remembered a dog bite but did not allege that Middleton deliberately ordered the attack.
- Rejected the heroic movement identity attached to him by later interpretation.
- Objected to the monument's physical and racialized transformation of his appearance.

## Qualifications

The profile is bounded to one episode summary of an oral history recorded more than three decades after the event. It does not establish every movement of Gadsden, Middleton, or Leo, and Gadsden's rejection of the conventional narrative does not diminish Birmingham's documented system of segregation or the violence faced by civil-rights protesters.

- Preserved both the reported dog bite and the uncertainty about deliberate police intent.
