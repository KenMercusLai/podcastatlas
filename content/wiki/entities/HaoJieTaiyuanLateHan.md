## Overview

郝杰 / Hao Jie is the Taiyuan figure in Hanji 846 who responds to [[LiangJiLateHan|梁冀]]'s pursuit by carrying a coffin to Liang's gate, submitting a petition, and drinking poison to preserve his family. His case pairs with [[HuWuTaiyuanLateHan|Hu Wu]] to show how political terror turns survival into a kin-risk calculation.

## Current Profile

Hanji 846 pairs Hao Jie with Hu Wu as men fond of startling argument and close to [[YuanZhuRemonstrantLateHan|Yuan Zhu]]. Their earlier talent-recommendation letter to senior officials omits Liang Ji, and Liang revives that omission after Yuan Zhu's death.

Hao Jie escapes at first, but concludes he cannot really outrun Liang's power. He then goes to Liang's gate with a coffin on his back, submits a statement, drinks poison, and dies. The source says this sacrifice keeps his family safe, making Hao Jie a contrast to Hu Wu's household destruction.

## Key Characteristics

- Taiyuan associate of Hu Wu and Yuan Zhu.
- Targeted for a recommendation-letter omission after Yuan Zhu's death.
- Fugitive who judges escape from Liang's power impossible.
- Self-sacrifice case where suicide is used to protect kin.
- Source example of political terror converting personal survival into family preservation.
