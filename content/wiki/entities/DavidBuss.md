# David Buss

## Overview
David Buss is the evolutionary psychologist interviewed in [[how-humans-select-keep-romantic-partners-in-short-long-term-dr-david-buss-scim4159413839|How Humans Select & Keep Romantic Partners in Short & Long Term]]. [[AndrewHuberman]] introduces him as a University of Texas at Austin psychology professor whose work examines mate choice, sexual strategies, jealousy, infidelity, deception, stalking, and sexual conflict.

## Current Profile
The bounded source presents Buss as a researcher who interprets human mating through sexual selection, mutual choice, competition, reproductive constraints, status, and social information. His analysis separates long-term pair bonding from short-term mating and distinguishes population-level sex differences from individual prediction.

The profile also includes meaningful revision. Buss says later and larger ovulation studies weakened evidence that had supported a dual-mating strategy, leading him to favor [[MateSwitchingHypothesis]] for many women's affairs. At the same time, his evolutionary-function explanations for jealousy, violence, stalking, and pornography remain hypotheses and organizing interpretations rather than moral approval or complete causal accounts.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses sexual selection, mutual mate choice, and intrasexual competition to organize mating research.
- Separates long-term preferences, short-term strategies, infidelity, jealousy, mate retention, and sexual conflict.
- Reports average sex differences while acknowledging overlap, cultural variation, contextual change, and exceptions.
- Revises the dual-mating account in response to weakened replication and evidence of emotional involvement in affairs.
- Connects high-risk behavior to deception, sexual overperception, dark-triad traits, serial offending, and coercive control.
- Treats evolutionary function as distinct from moral endorsement, though the source does not exhaust alternative explanations.

- Comparative preference claims: [[how-humans-select-keep-romantic-partners-in-short-long-term-dr-david-buss-scim4159413839]] summarizes his 37-culture study and its shared preferences, average sex differences, and culturally variable valuation of virginity.
- Scientific revision: [[how-humans-select-keep-romantic-partners-in-short-long-term-dr-david-buss-scim4159413839]] records his move toward mate switching after larger ovulation studies weakened earlier findings.
- Conflict and safety analysis: [[how-humans-select-keep-romantic-partners-in-short-long-term-dr-david-buss-scim4159413839]] links jealousy, mate retention, dark-triad traits, stalking, coercion, and intimate-partner violence while identifying isolation and verbal denigration as warning signs.
- Disciplinary boundary: [[how-humans-select-keep-romantic-partners-in-short-long-term-dr-david-buss-scim4159413839]] distinguishes ultimate evolutionary explanation from proximate neural machinery and identifies attachment as outside Buss's main expertise.

## Qualifications
This profile is bounded to one episode note, not Buss's full publication record. The note supplies many claims without study designs, sampling details, effect sizes, or competing social and developmental explanations. Population averages do not classify individuals, hypothesized adaptive functions do not justify harmful behavior, and discussion of coercion or violence is not a substitute for professional safety support.

## What Changed
- Created Buss's profile around mutual choice, mating strategies, scientific revision, and conflict-risk analysis.
- Preserved the distinction between population averages and individual prediction.
- Marked evolutionary-function accounts as explanatory hypotheses rather than moral endorsement or causal completeness.
