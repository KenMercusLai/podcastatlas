# David Buss

## Overview
David Buss is the evolutionary psychologist interviewed in [[how-humans-select-keep-romantic-partners-in-short-long-term-dr-david-buss-scim4159413839|How Humans Select & Keep Romantic Partners in Short & Long Term]] and its [[essentials-how-humans-select-keep-romantic-partners-in-the-short-long-term-dr-david-buss-scim3609265030|Essentials edit]]. [[AndrewHuberman]] introduces him as a University of Texas at Austin psychology professor whose work examines mate choice, sexual strategies, jealousy, infidelity, deception, stalking, and sexual conflict.

## Current Profile
The bounded sources present Buss as a researcher who interprets human mating through sexual selection, mutual choice, competition, reproductive constraints, status, and social information. His analysis separates long-term pair bonding from short-term mating and distinguishes population-level sex differences from individual prediction. The Essentials release preserves this framework but, as an edit of the same interview, does not independently strengthen the evidence.

The profile also includes meaningful revision. Buss says later and larger ovulation studies weakened evidence that had supported a dual-mating strategy, leading him to favor [[MateSwitchingHypothesis]] for many women's affairs. At the same time, his evolutionary-function explanations for jealousy, violence, stalking, and pornography remain hypotheses and organizing interpretations rather than moral approval or complete causal accounts.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses sexual selection, mutual mate choice, and intrasexual competition to organize mating research.
- Separates long-term preferences, short-term strategies, infidelity, jealousy, mate retention, and sexual conflict.
- Reports average sex differences while acknowledging overlap, cultural variation, contextual change, and exceptions.
- Revises the dual-mating account in response to weakened replication and evidence of emotional involvement in affairs.
- Connects high-risk behavior to deception, sexual overperception, dark-triad traits, serial offending, and coercive control.
- Treats evolutionary function as distinct from moral endorsement, though the source does not exhaust alternative explanations.

## Qualifications
This profile is bounded to two editorial versions of one interview, not two independent studies or Buss's full publication record. The notes supply many claims without study designs, sampling details, effect sizes, or competing social and developmental explanations. Population averages do not classify individuals, hypothesized adaptive functions do not justify harmful behavior, and discussion of coercion or violence is not a substitute for professional safety support.
