# Hattie McDaniel

## Overview

Hattie McDaniel was a Black American actor in [[GoneWithTheWind|*Gone with the Wind*]] who became the first African American actor to win an Academy Award, while segregation constrained her participation in both the film's Atlanta premiere and the Oscar ceremony surrounding that recognition.

## Current Profile

[[204-gone-with-the-wind-glt3666140136]] uses McDaniel's experience to expose the contradiction between individual cultural recognition and institutional racial exclusion. The Black cast was not invited to the Atlanta premiere, even as white attendees staged nostalgia for the slaveholding South. McDaniel later received a landmark Academy Award, but the episode says the Los Angeles venue's segregation initially barred her and continued to shape her placement.

Her presence in the discussion is therefore not a claim that recognition dissolved the color bar. It shows how a Black performer's achievement could be celebrated within an industry and public culture still organized by exclusion.

## Key Characteristics

- Black American performer in the 1939 film *Gone with the Wind*.
- First African American actor to receive an Academy Award.
- Excluded with the rest of the Black cast from the film's segregated Atlanta premiere.
- Example of individual achievement coexisting with institutional racial constraint.

## Evidence

### Premiere exclusion

- [[204-gone-with-the-wind-glt3666140136]] reports that McDaniel and the other Black cast members were not invited to the Atlanta premiere because segregation remained in force.

### Academy Award milestone

- [[204-gone-with-the-wind-glt3666140136]] identifies McDaniel as the first African American actor to win an Oscar.

### Recognition within segregation

- [[204-gone-with-the-wind-glt3666140136]] says she was initially barred from the segregated Los Angeles ceremony venue, making the award a milestone achieved within continuing constraint.
