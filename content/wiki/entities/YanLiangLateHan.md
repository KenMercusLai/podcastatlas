# 颜良 / Yan Liang (late Han)

## Overview
颜良 / Yan Liang (late Han) enters the wiki as a [[YuanShao|袁绍]] general whose reputation is first downgraded in the pre-[[GuanduCampaign|官渡]] debate and then converted into a concrete loss at [[BaimaReliefLateHan|White Horse]]. The page is intentionally narrow: he functions as a test of whether impressive military names equal usable strategic strength.

## Current Profile
[[KongRong|孔融]] names Yan Liang and [[WenChouLateHan|文丑]] when worrying that Yuan Shao has strong generals as well as territory, troops, and advisers. [[XunYu|荀彧]] answers by separating reputation from battlefield reliability: in the source's framing, Yan Liang and Wen Chou have brute courage but can be captured in a single fight.

White Horse makes that assessment concrete. Yuan Shao sends Yan Liang to attack Dong Commandery administrator [[LiuYanDongCommandery|刘延]], but [[JuShou|沮授]] warns that Yan Liang is impulsive and narrow despite bravery and should not be trusted with independent command. Yuan Shao ignores the warning. After Cao Cao's feigned rear-threat diversion splits Yuan's force, Yan Liang receives news late, fights hastily, and is killed by [[GuanYu|关羽]]. Hanji 1044 then shows the aftermath: Yuan Shao's anger over the loss drives a risky southward pursuit before Yanjin.

## Key Characteristics
- Yuan Shao-side general invoked as evidence of apparent military strength.
- Paired with Wen Chou in Kong Rong's fear and Xun Yu's rebuttal.
- Source-scoped example of reputation or courage not automatically becoming strategic advantage.
- Independent-command risk case because Ju Shou warns against sending him alone to White Horse.
- His death at Guan Yu's hand becomes the emotional trigger for Yuan Shao's risky post-White-Horse pursuit.

- Cao-side downgrade: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1034-yuanshao-de-baiju-zhi-yin-buken-ting-jushou-de-hua-log-wy2zvpdjopbdyexb0ijqmph4|Hanji 1034]] has Xun Yu judge Yan Liang and Wen Chou as brave but capturable rather than as strategic commanders.
- White Horse assignment and warning: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1043-yuanshao-tongma-caocao-jing-zhihaole-tade-piantouteng-ltibjj6xizsrynpgr9oyf-q3ydna|Hanji 1043]] says Yuan Shao sends Yan Liang to attack Liu Yan at White Horse and ignores Ju Shou's warning that Yan Liang is unsuitable for independent command.
- Death at White Horse: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1043-yuanshao-tongma-caocao-jing-zhihaole-tade-piantouteng-ltibjj6xizsrynpgr9oyf-q3ydna|Hanji 1043]] credits Guan Yu with charging toward Yan Liang's banner, killing him, taking the head, and lifting the siege.
- Aftermath trigger: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1044-guanyu-qianli-zou-danqi-xu-ganxie-caocao-busha-enqing-lpptlnc-e4zu4lcjr49bquxdw0pm|Hanji 1044]] places Yuan Shao's anger after Yan Liang's death at the opening of the Yellow River crossing and Yanjin sequence.

## Qualifications
This page should not infer Yan Liang's wider biography from the bounded sources. Hanji 1034 presents him through Cao-side strategic assessment, Hanji 1043 narrates the White Horse attack and death, and Hanji 1044 uses his death mainly as an aftermath trigger and Guan Yu repayment point.

## What Changed
- Clarified that Yan Liang's actual White Horse attack and death belong to Hanji 1043, while Hanji 1044 covers Yuan Shao's angry aftermath.
- Added Ju Shou's commander-fit warning as a direct qualification of Yan Liang's bravery.
