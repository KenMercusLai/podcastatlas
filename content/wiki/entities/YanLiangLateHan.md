# 颜良 / Yan Liang (late Han)

## Overview
颜良 / Yan Liang (late Han) enters the wiki as a [[YuanShao|袁绍]] general whose reputation is first downgraded in the pre-[[GuanduCampaign|官渡]] debate and then converted into a concrete loss before [[YanjinBattleLateHan|延津之战]]. The page is intentionally narrow: he functions as a test of whether impressive military names equal usable strategic strength.

## Current Profile
[[KongRong|孔融]] names Yan Liang and [[WenChouLateHan|文丑]] when worrying that Yuan Shao has strong generals as well as territory, troops, and advisers. [[XunYu|荀彧]] answers by separating reputation from battlefield reliability: in the source's framing, Yan Liang and Wen Chou have brute courage but can be captured in a single fight.

Hanji 1044 turns that assessment into battlefield consequence. After [[GuanYu|关羽]] kills Yan Liang, Yuan Shao reacts with anger and pursues south, setting up the Yellow River crossing debate in which [[JuShou|沮授]] urges caution before Yuan Shao presses toward Yanjin.

## Key Characteristics
- Yuan Shao-side general invoked as evidence of apparent military strength.
- Paired with Wen Chou in Kong Rong's fear and Xun Yu's rebuttal.
- Source-scoped example of reputation or courage not automatically becoming strategic advantage.
- His death at Guan Yu's hand becomes the emotional trigger for Yuan Shao's risky post-White-Horse pursuit.

## Qualifications
This page should not infer Yan Liang's wider biography from the bounded sources. Hanji 1034 presents him through Cao-side strategic assessment, and Hanji 1044 uses his death mainly as a campaign trigger and Guan Yu repayment point.
