# 扬州 / Yangzhou (late Han province)

## Overview
Late-Han Yangzhou is a contested southeastern province where office title, commandery control, river geography, and settlement capacity repeatedly come apart. The current evidence tracks two branches: the Yuan-family struggle over Yangzhou appointment and Jiangdong access, and the later Cao-side effort by [[LiuFuLateHan|Liu Fu]] to stabilize western Yangzhou from [[HefeiLateHan|Hefei]].

## Current Profile
扬州 / Yangzhou (late Han province) enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-969-yuanshi-xiongdi-neidou-shui-neng-jianlou-lsgj6sewo35jpsgfm-koivijtr6r|Hanji 969]] as the province whose office and territory expose the gap between appointment and control. After [[ChenWenLateHan|陈温]] dies, [[YuanShao|袁绍]] sends [[YuanYiLateHan|袁遗]] to lead Yangzhou, but [[YuanShu|袁术]] defeats him. Yuan Shu then uses [[ChenYuWuCommandery|陈瑀]] in the Yangzhou office field, while later fighting his way toward [[Shouchun|寿春]] after [[CaoCao|曹操]] drives him south.

The source also makes Yangzhou a strategic-base problem, not only an office title. The host compares Yangzhou with [[Jingzhou|荆州]] and [[YizhouLateHan|益州]] as the three Yangtze-line provinces and treats Yangzhou as the richest eventual base: the eastern commanderies, including [[DanyangCommandery|丹阳郡]], [[KuaijiCommandery|会稽郡]], and [[YuzhangCommandery|豫章郡]], become the later foundation for [[SunCe|孙策]] and the Jiangdong/Sun-family branch.

Hanji 1051 shifts the lens westward after Lujiang disorder and Yan Xiang's death, showing [[CaoCao|Cao Cao]] recommending Liu Fu to make the Yangzhou inspectorate real from a thin [[JiujiangCommandery|Jiujiang]]/Hefei foothold. The province therefore functions as both a coveted Jiangdong base and a Jiang-Huai frontier that must be built through legitimacy, negotiated absorption, livelihood repair, and defense.

## Key Characteristics
- Formal Yangzhou appointment can be politically meaningful even when commandery-level control remains contested or incomplete.
- The province's lower Yangtze/Jiangdong geography makes it a potential refuge, expansion base, and independent power platform.
- Western Yangzhou becomes a separate Cao-side stabilization problem after Yuan Shu remnants and local armed groups unsettle the Jiang-Huai region.
- Hefei and Jiujiang show how a weak foothold can be turned into real provincial administration through office, reassurance, and settlement policy.
- Yangzhou's late-Han profile splits between Jiangdong/Sun-family base-building and Cao-Sun frontier preparation.

**Strategic base geography**
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-969-yuanshi-xiongdi-neidou-shui-neng-jianlou-lsgj6sewo35jpsgfm-koivijtr6r|Hanji 969]] links Yangzhou and Jiangdong to the conditions that later make the southeast valuable to the Sun family.

**Cao-side western stabilization**
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1051-ta-weihe-bei-chengzuo-shi-zui-you-nengli-de-yangzhou-cishi-ltquwyzzq-sqibj33bpbu3qyvz2h|Hanji 1051]] describes Liu Fu building the Yangzhou office at Hefei when the wider province is fragmented and local armed groups remain outside direct control.

**From title to institutions**
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1051-ta-weihe-bei-chengzuo-shi-zui-you-nengli-de-yangzhou-cishi-ltquwyzzq-sqibj33bpbu3qyvz2h|Hanji 1051]] connects provincial order to reassurance, office grants, livelihood recovery, schools, fortifications, and defensive stores.

## Qualifications
The page currently synthesizes episode-level evidence rather than a full administrative history of Han Yangzhou. It should not be collapsed into later city-focused Yangzhou uses. Hanji 1051 uses source-specific spellings for Liu Fu and Lujiang actors; this page follows the wiki's normalized Liu Fu/Hefei branch while leaving minor identity uncertainty source-scoped.
