# Suetonius

## Overview

Suetonius was a Roman biographer whose life of [[Nero]] preserves essential dynastic, sexual, violent, theatrical, and death narratives while requiring close attention to invective, genre, and retrospective moral patterning.

## Current Profile

[[56-nero-glt7241559003]] uses Suetonius repeatedly but not literally. His account projects corruption backward into Nero's parents and childhood, supplies lurid family and court anecdotes, describes appearance and relationships, and gives the emperor's famous final words. These details make a compelling biography, but their narrative neatness can also turn ancestry, vice, crime, and humiliating death into a single moral design.

The episode therefore treats Suetonius as indispensable evidence rather than a transparent witness. His stories can preserve distorted traces of genuine Neronian acts and performances, yet each claim still needs comparison with coins, archaeology, other texts, genre expectations, and the political uses of Nero after death.

## Key Characteristics

- Roman biographer and major literary source for Nero's life and death.
- Preserver of vivid anecdotes about ancestry, appearance, sexuality, murder, performance, and last words.
- Writer whose moral and biographical design can project later reputation backward into childhood and family.
- Indispensable but interested source requiring claim-level corroboration.

## Evidence

- Ancestral characterization: [[56-nero-glt7241559003]] attributes to Suetonius a portrait of Nero as corrupted through both parents.
- Court and personal narrative: [[56-nero-glt7241559003]] uses his accounts for appearance, relationships, violence, and theatrical self-fashioning while preserving doubt.
- Death scene: [[56-nero-glt7241559003]] identifies Suetonius as the source for *qualis artifex pereo* and notes uncertainty in translation and tone.
- Methodological use: [[56-nero-glt7241559003]] compares literary hostility with material evidence and Nero's own public performance rather than accepting or discarding the biography wholesale.

## Qualifications

This page covers Suetonius only as a source for Nero. It does not synthesize his career, complete works, documentary access, treatment of other emperors, or later reception. A vivid anecdote is not false merely because it is literary, but repetition, moral fit, and hostile context do not independently establish it as fact.
