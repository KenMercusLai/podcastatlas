## Overview

朱浮 / Zhu Fu is a Guangwu-era official whose current profile now has two source-bounded roles. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-685-donghan-dachen-songhong-weihe-buken-zuo-guangwudi-jiefu-ljafg8giyhg5wmumv-g-09lywcu2|Hanji 685]] presents him as Youzhou mu whose recruitment and grain-support policy clashes with [[PengChongEasternHan|彭宠]] before Peng's rebellion. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-701-liuxiu-de-diwang-quanshu-zhezhong-ren-bixu-sha-lmsoxwg1k3k931ccnpeaomxfcu3q|Hanji 701]] later presents him as the zhijinwu who uses a solar eclipse to criticize local-official churn and warn [[LiuXiu|光武帝刘秀]] that short terms create false performance and weak long-term governance.

## Current Profile

Hanji 685 places Zhu Fu inside the northern founding-coalition stress field. As Youzhou mu, he recruits locally reputable people and former Wang Mang-era officials into the state office system and uses commandery grain to support their families. Peng Chong refuses to execute those orders, arguing that the world is not yet settled and military resources should not be drained by too many offices. The episode frames their relationship as both a policy dispute and a personality clash: Zhu Fu is proud and impatient, while Peng Chong is stubborn.

That conflict becomes part of Peng Chong's break with the center. Zhu Fu repeatedly denounces Peng Chong to Liu Xiu for gathering troops and storing grain with unclear intentions. Liu Xiu leaks the message to warn Peng, then summons him to Luoyang while refusing Peng's request that Zhu Fu come too. The source does not make Zhu Fu the sole cause of rebellion, but it makes his conflict with Peng a major pressure channel.

Hanji 701 places Zhu Fu after Liu Xiu's large administrative-reduction order. The state has already cut hundreds of counties and many official posts because war has reduced the population, but Zhu Fu identifies a different personnel problem: even when offices exist, officials cannot govern well if they are replaced before results can become visible.

Zhu Fu's memorial argues from classical precedent and practical incentives. He invokes three-year evaluation, warns that frequent welcoming and sending-off burdens officials, and says short terms make them chase quick visible results or defensive self-presentation. Liu Xiu accepts the advice, after which the source says local governors and commandery administrators are changed much less frequently.

## Key Characteristics

- Guangwu-era official whose current profile centers on a solar-eclipse remonstrance.
- Earlier Youzhou mu whose recruitment and grain-support policy clashes with Peng Chong's wartime resource priorities.
- Denouncer whose reports about Peng Chong's troops and grain become part of Liu Xiu's warning-and-summons pressure.
- Personnel-policy critic who links short office tenure to false achievement and bureaucratic performance theater.
- Long-horizon adviser who argues that public order needs multi-year judgment rather than immediate success metrics.
- Remonstrant whose advice is accepted by Liu Xiu and becomes a reported reduction in local-official turnover.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 701 and Hanji 685. The sources support Zhu Fu's role in two different institutional problems: northern office/resource conflict before Peng Chong's rebellion and later local-official tenure stability. Hanji 685 does not prove Zhu Fu alone caused Peng Chong's rebellion; the same source also stresses reward grievance, Peng's Yuyang resources, Liu Xiu's warning, and pressure from Peng's wife and retainers. Hanji 701 does not reconstruct Zhu Fu's full career, exact memorial wording, later political fate, or whether every later local-office change was caused by his memorial. The route is deliberately Eastern Han-specific and distinct from [[ZhuFuYan|主父偃]].
