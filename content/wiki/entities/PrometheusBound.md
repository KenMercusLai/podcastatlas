# Prometheus Bound / 《被缚的普罗米修斯》

## Overview

*Prometheus Bound* is the ancient Greek tragedy that stages [[Prometheus]]' punishment as an argument about technical knowledge, hope, necessity, political command, and freedom under constraint.

## Current Profile

The drama keeps Prometheus chained to a barren rock while visitors, memories, and prophecies supply most of its movement. That immobility is conceptually productive: the benefactor who gave mortals fire and practical arts can explain and foresee much, but cannot make knowledge identical with physical freedom or mastery of fate.

The episode reads [[Hephaestus]]' reluctant forging of the chain, Prometheus's “blind hopes,” and [[Io]]'s demand to know her suffering as three linked problems. Effective technique can serve a bad command, hope can enable action while concealing risk, and benevolent withholding of truth can conflict with the sufferer's autonomy.

## Key Characteristics

- Tragedy centered on a bound protagonist whose knowledge exceeds his immediate power.
- Catalog of civilization-forming arts broader than modern machine technology.
- Drama of resistance in which constrained action does not eliminate refusal.
- Inquiry into hope, mortality, painful truth, and the limits of foresight.
- Work traditionally attributed to [[Aeschylus]] but subject to modern authorship dispute.

## Qualifications

The current profile comes from one philosophical podcast reading, not a full textual, performance, reception, or authorship study. Its AI, surveillance, medicine, and longevity comparisons are interpretive extensions rather than claims that the ancient drama directly anticipated modern systems.

## What Changed

- Established the drama as a distinct canonical work page.
- Connected its dramatic structure to knowledge limits, hope, technical purpose, and informational autonomy.
