## Overview

卢芳 / Lu Fang is synthesized as an Eastern Han frontier claimant and Xiongnu-linked surrender case whose reintegration remains fragile. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-718-zhengce-ruhe-chengwei-shishang-no-1-de-nvwang-likq4pahq21k6bxbaohwdhcnwg5r|Hanji 718]] records his surrender and reward, while [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-720-donghan-kaiguo-taizi-weihe-fanfu-da-cizhi-baogao-lslizspo8rywnf3cebyhincdhvz0|Hanji 720]] shows renewed defection after fear and Xiongnu backing remain unresolved.

## Current Profile

Hanji 718 places Lu Fang in the diplomatic aftermath of frontier contestation. Lu Fang and [[MinKanEasternHan|闵堪]] surrender to [[LiuXiu|刘秀]], and Liu Xiu responds with high-status incorporation: Lu Fang is enfeoffed as Dai King, Min Kan becomes Dai Chancellor, and the court grants substantial silk and cloth.

The settlement is unstable from the start. The source says the Xiongnu side gave Lu Fang back to Han expecting reward, but Lu Fang claims voluntary surrender; Han also follows that version and avoids paying a bounty. The Chanyu becomes angry, and raids increase, making Lu Fang's surrender a case where face-saving narrative damages frontier trust.

Hanji 720 supplies the next consequence. Lu Fang defects again after a postponed court audience, suggesting that title and gifts do not fully resolve fear, loyalty, or Xiongnu patronage when the underlying security field remains unsettled.

## Key Characteristics

- Eastern Han frontier claimant or rival figure whose surrender is treated as politically valuable.
- Rewarded defector whom Liu Xiu incorporates through kingship, court gifts, and paired appointment with Min Kan.
- Xiongnu-linked actor whose return is disputed between bounty delivery and voluntary surrender narratives.
- Fragile surrender case whose later defection shows the limits of title-based incorporation.

## Qualifications

The current sources do not supply Lu Fang's full rebellion, lineage claim, earlier base, or final outcome. The page should be read as a bounded synthesis of surrender incorporation and frontier-trust failure, not a full biography of the claimant.

## What Changed

- Created Lu Fang as a canonical Eastern Han entity after Hanji 718 and Hanji 720 both made his surrender/defection branch relevant.
- Framed his current profile as rewarded incorporation that fails to remove Xiongnu-linked insecurity.
