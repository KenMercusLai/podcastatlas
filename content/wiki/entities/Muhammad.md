# Muhammad

## Overview

Muhammad is Islam's prophet and the highly probable historical leader whom the sources connect to the [[Quran|Qur'an]], migration, community formation, Arab expansion, [[Mecca]], and the [[Kaaba]], while distinguishing his existence from the contested details of later biography.

## Current Profile

The traditional account presents Muhammad as a Meccan merchant who began receiving revelation around forty, migrated to Yathrib, built a religious-political community, returned to Mecca, destroyed the Kaaba's idols, and left followers who conquered much of the Roman and Persian worlds. That account gives the sanctuary a prophetic restoration narrative rather than merely a political foundation.

The newer source grades this profile instead of accepting or discarding it wholesale. Early non-Muslim references and the Qur'an make Muhammad's existence and leadership of a prophetic movement difficult to deny. Full narrative biographies survive later, however, so exact dates, Meccan geography, participation in the invasion of Palestine, and the fit between the founding movement and later recognisable Islam remain contested.

Holland provisionally retains a merchant background, Abrahamic prophetic self-understanding, the hijra, Yathrib, and community foundation. His proposal that Muhammad led forces into Palestine and died afterward is a source-scoped reconstruction, not the page's settled conclusion.

## Key Characteristics

- Prophet at the center of Muslim revelation and sacred history.
- Historical existence strongly supported relative to the details of his later biography.
- Figure associated with the Qur'an and a post-biblical Abrahamic message.
- Founder or leader of a community linked to migration, political order, and expansion.
- Reconsecrator of the Kaaba in the traditional Muslim account.
- Subject of competing chronological, geographical, and conquest reconstructions.

## Evidence

- **Traditional sacred profile:** [[265-saudi-arabia-the-mystery-of-the-kaaba-glt1528147434]] narrates revelation, opposition, migration, return, and destruction of the Kaaba's idols.
- **Existence and early movement:** [[60-muhammad-glt7621065751]] cites early Syriac and Armenian references and the Qur'an as evidence against denying Muhammad's historicity.
- **Bounded reconstruction:** [[60-muhammad-glt7621065751]] retains merchant, prophetic, hijra, Yathrib, and community elements while questioning other chronology and geography.
- **Textual role:** [[265-saudi-arabia-the-mystery-of-the-kaaba-glt1528147434]] and [[60-muhammad-glt7621065751]] treat Quranic material as the earliest central textual starting point while separating it from later narrative tradition.

## Qualifications

The sources are podcast interpretations rather than a comprehensive biography or scholarly consensus review. Muslim belief about revelation is not adjudicated by secular history. The Doctrina Jacobi's chronology, Palestine campaign, northern-geography hypothesis, Moses typology, exact death date, and later reshaping of memory remain contested or source-scoped. High confidence that Muhammad existed does not transfer automatically to every traditional or revisionist detail.

## What Changed

- Raised confidence in Muhammad's historical existence while widening uncertainty around the detailed biography.
- Added the Qur'an, early external fragments, and late-antique context as distinct evidence classes.
- Added Holland's Palestine reconstruction as a contested model rather than a settled replacement narrative.
