# Henry VIII

## Overview

Henry VIII is the Tudor king whose reign in this source demonstrates how a durable medieval treason framework could be expanded around royal supremacy, religious obedience, speech, marital politics, and feared methods of attack.

## Current Profile

The episode presents Henry as an aggressive user and maker of treason law rather than the originator of its core doctrine. His break with Rome created new religious and political demands, the prosecution of Thomas More made refusal and conscience part of the treason story, Anne Boleyn's indictment turned marital and sexual allegations into a plot against the king, and Thomas Cromwell's fall showed the continuing use of [[ParliamentaryAttainder]].

The [[RichardRoose]] poisoning case makes the personalized logic especially clear: after poisoned porridge killed members and dependants of Bishop John Fisher's household, legislation reclassified poisoning as high treason and imposed boiling as spectacle. Yet most Henrician additions were repealed under Edward VI, qualifying any image of permanent legal transformation.

## Key Characteristics

- Expanded treason beyond the medieval statutory core.
- Joined religious supremacy to political allegiance after the break with Rome.
- Used ordinary prosecution and parliamentary attainder against prominent opponents and former intimates.
- Turned personal fear of poisoning into retroactive offence creation and exemplary punishment.
- Left a contested legal inheritance substantially narrowed after his death.

## Evidence

- Religious and verbal expansion: [[248-medieval-treason-part-1-glt9532907553]] connects the break with Rome, Thomas More, and new forms of religious treason.
- Court and attainder: [[248-medieval-treason-part-1-glt9532907553]] discusses Anne Boleyn's charges and Thomas Cromwell's condemnation without an ordinary trial.
- Poisoning legislation: [[248-medieval-treason-part-1-glt9532907553]] describes Richard Roose's offence being transformed into high treason and punished by boiling.
- Posthumous contraction: [[248-medieval-treason-part-1-glt9532907553]] says Edward VI and his council removed most of Henry's additions.

## Qualifications

This is a treason-law profile, not a general biography or complete account of the English Reformation. The source questions the fairness and political purpose of several prosecutions without resolving every factual allegation. “Pity” in Anne Boleyn's beheading is reported as Henry's framing rather than accepted as a neutral moral judgment.

## What Changed

- Created a source-bounded profile of Henry VIII as an expander of treason law whose additions largely failed to endure.
