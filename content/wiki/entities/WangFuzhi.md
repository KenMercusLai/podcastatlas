# 王夫之 / Wang Fuzhi

## Overview

王夫之 / Wang Fuzhi appears in the bounded sources as a later historical commentator on late-Han leadership and as a Ming-Qing thinker whose wider prominence was substantially constructed through nineteenth-century publication and Hunan regional reception.

## Current Profile

Wang's later historical judgments, rather than a reconstruction of his complete philosophy, anchor two sources. His assessment of [[LyuBu|吕布]] sharpens the distinction between spectacular force and durable loyalty, planning, and trust; his criticism of [[LiuBiao|刘表]] distinguishes genuinely securing [[Jingzhou|荆州]] from occupying a buffer until stronger competitors arrive. These uses appear in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1026-jiemi-sanguo-di-yi-mengjiang-lvbu-de-luomu-lhjf-agujuliglqchumtff3tftxg1]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1027-jingzhou-zhizhu-liubiao-weihe-nan-cheng-bazhu-ll-ebguqllsf-5ycvpdogihzn3lj]].

His later canonical stature is also a history of publication and selection rather than unchanged influence from his lifetime. The bounded account says his works achieved more systematic publication only in the Daoguang period and that [[ZengGuofan|曾国藩]], Zeng Guoquan, and Guo Songtao helped elevate him among late-Qing Hunan scholars. Zeng's Nanjing reprinting and preface emphasized benevolence, ritual, and learning while avoiding Wang's anti-Qing position. [[9-lishi-xiaoshuo-neiwai-de-zengguofan-lo67k333npm5m668-dmjiqtchvza]]

## Key Characteristics

- Serves as a later evaluator of political and military actors rather than a contemporary witness to late-Han events.
- Judges battlefield force insufficient without stable strategy, loyalty, and political trust.
- Distinguishes regional occupation from durable governance and strategic initiative.
- Had limited influence in his own lifetime according to the new source's account.
- Gained renewed prominence through nineteenth-century printing, Hunan scholarly networks, and selective interpretation.
- Could be appropriated for ethical and scholarly authority while his anti-Qing commitments were muted.

## Evidence

- Lü Bu judgment: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1026-jiemi-sanguo-di-yi-mengjiang-lvbu-de-luomu-lhjf-agujuliglqchumtff3tftxg1]] cites Wang to reinforce the gap between martial ability and reliable political attachment.
- Liu Biao judgment: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1027-jingzhou-zhizhu-liubiao-weihe-nan-cheng-bazhu-ll-ebguqllsf-5ycvpdogihzn3lj]] uses Wang to sharpen the strategic ceiling of defensive Jingzhou rule.
- Publication and regional reception: [[9-lishi-xiaoshuo-neiwai-de-zengguofan-lo67k333npm5m668-dmjiqtchvza]] connects Daoguang-era printing and later Hunan scholars to Wang's increased prominence.
- Selective appropriation: [[9-lishi-xiaoshuo-neiwai-de-zengguofan-lo67k333npm5m668-dmjiqtchvza]] says Zeng's reprinting foregrounded learning and ethics while avoiding anti-Qing politics.

## Qualifications

The profile is not a complete account of Wang's metaphysics, ethics, historiography, anti-Qing thought, texts, publication history, or later influence. The two late-Han uses arrive through a contemporary podcast's citation of later commentary, while the publication chronology and motives in the new source require bibliographic verification.

## What Changed

- Added the gap between Wang's limited lifetime influence and his nineteenth-century rise.
- Added printing, Hunan regional identity, and selective interpretation as mechanisms of reception.
- Preserved his source-scoped role as a later evaluator of Lü Bu and Liu Biao.

- [[LyuBu]] - military actor whose force and unreliability Wang is cited to judge.
