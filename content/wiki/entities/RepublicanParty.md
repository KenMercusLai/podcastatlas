# Republican Party

## Overview
The Republican Party is tracked from its Trump-centered institutional and coalition transformation through a reported 2026 midterm defeat that reopens questions about party identity, candidate selection, and the 2028 succession.

## Current Profile
The pre-election sources describe a party reorganized around [[DonaldTrump]] while retaining internal tensions. [[LindseyGraham]] shows older interventionist politics surviving through adaptation to Trump's personal power; Project 2025 and related organizations show a detailed personnel and policy network; the H-1B dispute shows that nativist voters and technology employers do not share one stable program.

The later source reports that this structure failed electorally across congressional, gubernatorial, and red-state contests. Trump-backed candidates with narrow loyalty but weak broader appeal, aggressive immigration enforcement, harsh advertising, and mid-cycle redistricting are all presented as liabilities. The result weakens but does not itself dissolve Trumpism: Republicans must decide whether to preserve its populist identity, return toward conventional conservatism, or build another synthesis.

## Key Characteristics
- Party power and candidate selection centered heavily on Trump loyalty.
- Institutionalized policy network extending beyond one campaign or personality.
- Coalition tension between nativist grassroots politics, technology-capital labor demand, and older conservative or hawkish traditions.
- Candidate-quality risk when primary loyalty does not translate to general-election appeal.
- Post-defeat identity struggle over Trumpism, populism, conventional conservatism, and 2028 succession.

## Qualifications
The later source is an immediate interpretation rather than official returns, voter-level analysis, or evidence of how the party subsequently reorganizes. A single large defeat can expose coalition weakness without proving the end of Trumpism, and the relative effects of candidates, immigration, prices, war, maps, and presidential approval are not isolated.
