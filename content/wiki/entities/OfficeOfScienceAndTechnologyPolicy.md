# White House Office of Science and Technology Policy

## Overview
The White House Office of Science and Technology Policy appears in the All-In science-policy corpus as the coordinating office for U.S. science and technology policy. In the Kratsios interview, [[MichaelKratsios|Michael Kratsios]] says the United States does not have one single science-and-technology agency, so OSTP helps coordinate priorities across agencies, the [[WhiteHouse|White House]], Congress, and implementation partners. In the Weinstein interview, OSTP appears as one of the Washington science-policy nodes that heterodox scientists are urged to engage.

## Current Profile
OSTP's wiki role is the institutional home of the [[NewGoldenAgeScienceAgenda|New Golden Age science agenda]] and a contact point for science reform. The office is presented less as a grant-making agency than as a policy coordinator trying to align federal research priorities, national missions, AI-for-science work, funding experiments, agency implementation reports, and engagement with scientists outside normal consensus channels.

## Key Characteristics
- OSTP coordinates across a distributed federal science-and-technology system rather than replacing the agencies that perform or fund the work.
- The office is tied to presidential priority-setting: AI, quantum computing, nuclear energy, fusion, bio, space, and China competition are named as national science-policy priorities.
- It gives the administration a venue to define what counts as pro-science: Kratsios emphasizes scientists, outcomes, basic research, and depoliticized grant criteria.
- OSTP is connected to implementation discipline because the interview links it to research-priority memos, agency reporting timelines, and future appropriations work.
- Weinstein's interview adds an outside-facing role: OSTP is one of the offices he says scientists should engage if they want to help repair American science.

## Qualifications
The page is source-scoped to All-In interviews. It records how Kratsios describes OSTP's role and how Weinstein frames OSTP as a reform contact; it does not independently audit statutory authority, agency execution, or the full history of OSTP across administrations.
