# White House Office of Science and Technology Policy

## Overview
The White House Office of Science and Technology Policy appears in the All-In Kratsios interview as the coordinating office for U.S. science and technology policy. In the source, [[MichaelKratsios|Michael Kratsios]] says the United States does not have one single science-and-technology agency, so OSTP helps coordinate priorities across agencies, the [[WhiteHouse|White House]], Congress, and implementation partners.

## Current Profile
OSTP's wiki role is the institutional home of the [[NewGoldenAgeScienceAgenda|New Golden Age science agenda]]. The office is presented less as a grant-making agency than as a policy coordinator trying to align federal research priorities, national missions, AI-for-science work, funding experiments, and agency implementation reports.

## Key Characteristics
- OSTP coordinates across a distributed federal science-and-technology system rather than replacing the agencies that perform or fund the work.
- The office is tied to presidential priority-setting: AI, quantum computing, nuclear energy, fusion, bio, space, and China competition are named as national science-policy priorities.
- It gives the administration a venue to define what counts as pro-science: Kratsios emphasizes scientists, outcomes, basic research, and depoliticized grant criteria.
- OSTP is connected to implementation discipline because the interview links it to research-priority memos, agency reporting timelines, and future appropriations work.
