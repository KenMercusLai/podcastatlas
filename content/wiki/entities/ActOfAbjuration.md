# Act of Abjuration

## Overview

The Act of Abjuration is the 1581 Dutch repudiation of [[PhilipII|Philip II of Spain]], marking the point where the [[DutchRevolt]] moves beyond [[LoyalRebellion|loyal-rebellion]] language into formal rejection of Philip and his heirs.

## Current Profile

The Netherlands episode places the act after years of rhetorically restrained revolt. [[HetWilhelmus|Het Wilhelmus]] still voices loyalty to the King of Spain, but by 1581 the rebels formally reject Philip and strip his images and symbols from public instruments. The Elizabethan episode places the same act in a sovereignty search: Dutch rebels seek [[FrancoisDukeOfAnjou|Francois, Duke of Anjou]] as replacement lord before the [[FrenchFury]] collapses that French solution and helps push [[ElizabethI|Elizabeth I]] toward direct intervention.

## Key Characteristics

- Formal Dutch repudiation of Philip II and his heirs in 1581.
- Ends the earlier loyal-rebellion posture that blamed officials while preserving royal language.
- Includes symbolic rupture through removal of Philip's images and signs from public authority.
- Opens or confirms the search for an alternative sovereign, including the Anjou option.
- Becomes part of England's diplomatic background before the Treaty of Nonsuch.

## Evidence

- Formal break and symbol removal: [[680-the-netherlands-the-revolt-that-made-the-modern-world-part-4-glt4209356543]] says the rebels issued the act in 1581 and removed Philip's image and symbols from coins, seals, buildings, and documents.
- Sovereignty search: [[694-elizabeth-i-vs-the-catholics-a-treacherous-conspiracy-part-4-glt1627515087]] places the act alongside Anjou's Dutch proclamation and the failed French option.
- English policy consequence: [[694-elizabeth-i-vs-the-catholics-a-treacherous-conspiracy-part-4-glt1627515087]] treats the act and Anjou's failure as background before the Treaty of Nonsuch.

## Qualifications

The sources use the act mainly as a narrative threshold. They do not provide a full legal-text analysis, provincial voting history, or later constitutional interpretation.
