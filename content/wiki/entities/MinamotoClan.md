# Minamoto Clan

## Overview
The Minamoto Clan is the eastern warrior house that survives Taira suppression, reconstitutes around [[MinamotoNoYoritomo]], and becomes the victorious coalition in the late [[GenpeiWar]].

## Current Profile
The episodes present the Minamoto as an ex-imperial surname lineage that becomes a provincial warrior house, then later as both victims of Taira consolidation and eventual winners of the samurai order. [[658-dawn-of-the-samurai-the-shadow-of-the-sword-part-1-glt7378881498]] explains Minamoto as one of the surnames given to princes removed from the imperial family, creating a bridge between court prestige and provincial military capacity.

[[659-dawn-of-the-samurai-bloodbath-at-the-bridge-part-2-glt3420393571]] shows [[MinamotoNoYoshitomo]] losing the 1159 coup, but it also shows Kiyomori sparing Yoritomo and other Minamoto children. That mercy keeps the clan's future leadership alive in exile.

[[660-dawn-of-the-samurai-japans-greatest-warrior-part-3-glt3581345898]] shows the later Minamoto as militarily ascendant but politically divided: [[KisoYoshinaka]] drives the Taira from Kyoto, then becomes a rival whom Yoritomo must destroy. [[661-dawn-of-the-samurai-the-shogun-triumphant-part-4-glt7837412375]] follows Yoshitsune's campaigns that end Taira power and Yoritomo's political consolidation.

The clan's victory becomes more than dynastic replacement. Under Yoritomo, Minamoto success turns into [[KamakuraShogunateFormation]], where samurai government becomes the durable center of Japanese power.

## Key Characteristics
- The Minamoto begin as an ex-imperial surname lineage that can carry Kyoto prestige into provincial warfare.
- The Minamoto story begins in defeat and exile before becoming a victorious coalition.
- The clan's survival depends on Taira mercy, provincial alliance-building, and Yoritomo's Kanto base.
- The later Minamoto coalition contains both anti-Taira unity and intense internal rivalry.
- Yoshitsune supplies battlefield success while Yoritomo supplies political consolidation.
- The clan's victory depends on adapting from land warfare into amphibious and naval campaigning.
- The end result is a new warrior-government order rather than simple restoration of court politics.

## Evidence
- Origin bridge: [[658-dawn-of-the-samurai-the-shadow-of-the-sword-part-1-glt7378881498]] explains Minamoto as a surname for princes removed from the imperial family and later adopted or amplified by provincial elites because of its prestige.
- Survival after defeat: [[659-dawn-of-the-samurai-bloodbath-at-the-bridge-part-2-glt3420393571]] follows Yoshitomo's failed coup, Yoritomo's exile to Izu, and the later Minamoto appeal around [[PrinceMochihito]] and [[MinamotoNoYorimasa]].
- Internal conflict: [[660-dawn-of-the-samurai-japans-greatest-warrior-part-3-glt3581345898]] follows Kiso's rise, Kyoto occupation, and attack on [[GoShirakawa]]; [[661-dawn-of-the-samurai-the-shogun-triumphant-part-4-glt7837412375]] follows Yoritomo's decision to destroy Kiso after Kiso provokes jealousy and loses legitimacy.
- Anti-Taira campaign: [[661-dawn-of-the-samurai-the-shogun-triumphant-part-4-glt7837412375]] traces Yoshitsune's victories at Ichinotani, Yashima, and [[DanNoUraBattle|Dan-no-ura]].
- Institutional result: [[661-dawn-of-the-samurai-the-shogun-triumphant-part-4-glt7837412375]] says Yoritomo's settlement makes samurai the masters of Japan.

## Qualifications
The sources still do not provide a full genealogy or administrative history of the Minamoto, but part 2 supplies the prewar defeat-and-survival layer that parts 3 and 4 build on.
