# Thutmose III

## Overview

Thutmose III was the young Egyptian king for whom [[Hatshepsut]] first acted as regent and the successful warrior pharaoh who ruled independently after her death.

## Current Profile

The episode presents Thutmose III as a necessary part of Hatshepsut's accession rather than merely her victim. She initially acknowledged him as king, then accumulated and performed full kingship alongside him for roughly two decades. His survival, later military success, and lack of an earlier coup complicate the popular story that he spent her reign consumed by resentment.

After Hatshepsut's death, revolts in Canaan were followed by his victory at Megiddo and a long independent reign. Damage to her monuments is associated with his era, but the episode stresses that it began decades after her death and may have targeted the precedent of female kingship or a later succession problem rather than expressing immediate personal revenge.

## Key Characteristics

- Became king as a child under Hatshepsut's regency.
- Remained junior co-ruler while Hatshepsut adopted full pharaonic office.
- Built an independent reputation through the Megiddo campaign and a long reign.
- Complicates a simple oppressor-victim account of Hatshepsut's rule.
- Is connected to a delayed and politically ambiguous campaign against Hatshepsut's public memory.

## Evidence

- Co-rule and succession: [[299-the-greatest-female-pharaoh-glt5076645168]] describes Hatshepsut first governing for Thutmose III, then ruling as king while he remained in place and later succeeded her.
- Military authority: [[299-the-greatest-female-pharaoh-glt5076645168]] links his independent reign to the suppression of rebellion and victory at Megiddo.
- Memory campaign: [[299-the-greatest-female-pharaoh-glt5076645168]] says the damage to Hatshepsut's monuments was delayed, weakening a direct personal-revenge explanation.

## Qualifications

The source does not recover Thutmose III's private feelings or prove who ordered each act of defacement. Its account of timing and motive is an episode-level synthesis, and the argument that he could have staged an earlier coup is suggestive rather than decisive.

## What Changed

- Established a profile separating Thutmose III's junior co-rule, independent military career, and uncertain role in Hatshepsut's later erasure.
