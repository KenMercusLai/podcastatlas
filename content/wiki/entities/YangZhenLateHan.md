## Overview

杨震 / Yang Zhen is a late-Han moral-reputation figure whose current wiki profile is built from posthumous vindication under [[EmperorShunOfHan|汉顺帝]] and later family-reputation use under [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]]. Hanji 805 shows Shun publicly repairing Yang Zhen's status after the Yan-family collapse; Hanji 914 shows Ling sarcastically invoking Yang Zhen's reputation when [[YangQiLateHan|杨琦]] answers too directly.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-805-tongtou-renxing-de-nvren-jing-neng-yuce-jixiong-ll3bzoyrkbtnvt-aqu9nqelqonew|Hanji 805]] supplies the earlier current layer. After Shun's restoration, Yang Zhen's students Yu Fang and Chen Yi come to the palace gate to seek redress. Shun responds by appointing two of Yang Zhen's sons as lang, awarding money, and reburying Yang Zhen with Three Excellency rites. The source also reports a large-bird omen at Yang Zhen's coffin, after which Shun sacrifices to him with middle-victim rites.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-914-xinren-de-ren-weihe-zong-shang-ni-zui-shen-lvwit-rzrkscn5a3h6wblntxppso|Hanji 914]] supplies the later reputation layer. Yang Qi's direct answer to Emperor Ling's comparison with Emperor Huan irritates Ling, who calls Yang Qi a stubborn descendant of Yang Zhen. In that episode, Yang Zhen functions less as a full biography than as a family-reputation marker: the same moral name that makes Yang Qi's answer legible also gives Ling a way to mock it.

## Key Characteristics

- Posthumously vindicated moral official whose reputation survives into Shun's accession settlement.
- Public-redress figure whose students can reopen his case at the palace gate.
- Ritual-status repair case, with sons appointed, money granted, Three Excellency reburial, and sacrifice.
- Omen-associated reputation figure through the large-bird story reported around his coffin.
- Yang-family moral marker later invoked in court when Yang Qi speaks too directly to Emperor Ling.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 805 and Hanji 914, not a complete Yang Zhen biography. Hanji 805 establishes posthumous ritual repair after Shun's restoration, while Hanji 914 establishes later family-reputation use. The large-bird event is preserved as a source-reported omen story, not independently verified natural evidence. Yu Fang, Chen Yi, and the exact procedural history before redress remain source-scoped.
