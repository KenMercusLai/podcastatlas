## Overview

The [[HiroshimaAtomicBombing|Hiroshima atomic bombing]] appears in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-394-3-shenwa-lishi-heweiji-weihe-dou-fasheng-zai-riben-lspztkfaaq0hcq1no3yum1xdox8a]] as a nuclear-weapons memory case. The episode uses it to stress nuclear violence's sudden bodily destruction and to connect Japan's wartime suffering with later nuclear-risk responsibility.

## Current Profile

Hanji 394-3 presents Hiroshima through the B-29 mission, the "Little Boy" bomb, intense light and heat, immediate casualties, and the host's claim that Japanese authorities initially obscured what had happened. In the wiki, this is not a full military history page; it is a source-scoped memory node inside [[NuclearDisasterMemory]].

The episode's argument depends on the tension between victim memory and responsibility memory. Hiroshima is used to show why nuclear harm should not be minimized, while the Fukushima discharge section argues that later Japanese policy should not pass nuclear risk outward.

## Key Characteristics

- Nuclear-weapons memory case used to illustrate immediate destructive force.
- Japan wartime suffering node that the episode places beside Nagasaki, Fukushima, and Chernobyl.
- Source-scoped example of official narrative control after catastrophic nuclear violence.
- Moral-memory reference used to argue against normalizing or externalizing nuclear harm.
