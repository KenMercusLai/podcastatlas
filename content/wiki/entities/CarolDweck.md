# Carol Dweck

## Overview
Carol Dweck is a psychologist whose research in the bounded sources examines how beliefs and feedback about ability, effort, willpower, and glucose shape response to challenge.

## Current Profile
Dweck's bounded profile joins two belief-sensitive branches of performance. In the willpower branch, beliefs about whether willpower is limited and whether glucose replenishes it affected performance across repeated difficult tasks, supporting [[EgoDepletionDebate]] as an interaction among physiology, expectation, and context rather than a simple fuel gauge. In the ability-and-feedback branch, research named with [[ClaudiaMueller]] contrasts intelligence labels with feedback about effort and persistence; the episode reports differences in later challenge choice, performance, and honest score reporting.

Together the sources present Dweck's contribution as a study of how interpretations alter behavior without making beliefs omnipotent. Body state, strategy, instruction, feedback credibility, and opportunity remain part of the causal picture.

## Key Characteristics
- Tests implicit beliefs about whether willpower is limited or renewable.
- Uses repeated cognitive challenges such as Stroop-style tasks.
- Treats glucose effects as belief-sensitive rather than universally restorative.
- Supplies a mindset qualification without proving physiology irrelevant.
- Connects fixed ability labels to image protection and process feedback to learning-oriented challenge.
- Treats errors and difficulty as interpretation points rather than final identity verdicts.

## Evidence
- Belief manipulation - [[how-to-increase-your-willpower-tenacity-scim7958949675]] summarizes experiments comparing limited and non-limited willpower beliefs.
- Glucose interaction - [[how-to-increase-your-willpower-tenacity-scim7958949675]] reports that glucose effects depended partly on participants' beliefs about depletion and fuel.
- Integrated interpretation - [[how-to-increase-your-willpower-tenacity-scim7958949675]] retains both bodily and belief-related influences across repeated challenges.
- Praise comparison - [[how-to-enhance-performance-learning-by-applying-a-growth-mindset-scim1082295503]] attributes intelligence-versus-effort praise research to Dweck and Mueller.
- Challenge and reporting - [[how-to-enhance-performance-learning-by-applying-a-growth-mindset-scim1082295503]] reports harder task selection, stronger later performance, and more faithful score reporting after effort-focused feedback.

## Qualifications
This profile covers Dweck's role in two podcast summaries rather than her complete scholarship or the full methods and replication history of the studies discussed. It does not establish that belief or feedback can eliminate fatigue, illness, sleep loss, nutritional need, ineffective strategy, missing instruction, depression, structural constraint, or clinical impairment.

## What Changed
- Expanded the profile from willpower beliefs to ability praise, challenge choice, and error interpretation.
- Preserved physiological, strategic, environmental, and evidence-quality boundaries.
