# Carol Dweck

## Overview
Carol Dweck is the psychologist whose work in the episode tests whether beliefs about willpower and glucose alter apparent ego-depletion effects.

## Current Profile
[[how-to-increase-your-willpower-tenacity-scim7958949675]] presents Dweck's studies as a major qualification to a fixed resource model. Participants' beliefs about whether willpower is limited and whether glucose replenishes it affected performance across repeated difficult tasks, supporting [[EgoDepletionDebate]] as an interaction among physiology, expectation, and context rather than a simple fuel gauge.

## Key Characteristics
- Tests implicit beliefs about whether willpower is limited or renewable.
- Uses repeated cognitive challenges such as Stroop-style tasks.
- Treats glucose effects as belief-sensitive rather than universally restorative.
- Supplies a mindset qualification without proving physiology irrelevant.

## Evidence
- Belief manipulation - [[how-to-increase-your-willpower-tenacity-scim7958949675]] summarizes experiments comparing limited and non-limited willpower beliefs.
- Glucose interaction - [[how-to-increase-your-willpower-tenacity-scim7958949675]] reports that glucose effects depended partly on participants' beliefs about depletion and fuel.
- Integrated interpretation - [[how-to-increase-your-willpower-tenacity-scim7958949675]] retains both bodily and belief-related influences across repeated challenges.

## Qualifications
This profile covers only Dweck's role in one podcast summary. It does not establish that belief can eliminate fatigue, illness, sleep loss, nutritional need, depression, or clinical impairment, and it does not summarize her broader mindset scholarship.

## What Changed
- Created a bounded profile for Dweck's belief-sensitive challenge to a fixed willpower-resource account.
