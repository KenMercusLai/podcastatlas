# Weimar Republic

## Overview

The Weimar Republic appears as the post-First World War German state that made *Das Lied der Deutschen* the national anthem in 1922.

## Current Profile

The episode uses the Weimar Republic to show how a defeated and divided state sought a unifying symbol after the First World War. President [[FriedrichEbert]] adopted the song while emphasizing the third verse's language of unity, rights, and freedom, hoping it could bridge liberal and conservative audiences before [[NaziGermany]] later changed the anthem's public burden.

## Key Characteristics

- Postwar German democracy looking for symbols that could unify a humiliated country.
- State that officially adopted *Das Lied der Deutschen* in August 1922.
- Political context where the third verse was emphasized as a democratic unity formula.
- Predecessor regime whose symbol was later appropriated by Nazi political theatre.

- Symbolic need: [[679-germany-the-song-hitler-stole-part-3-glt6217148052]] says the republic sought symbols that could unite a defeated and divided Germany.
- Verse emphasis: [[679-germany-the-song-hitler-stole-part-3-glt6217148052]] says Ebert emphasized the third verse rather than the first.
- Later vulnerability: [[679-germany-the-song-hitler-stole-part-3-glt6217148052]] contrasts Weimar adoption with Nazi emphasis on the first verse after 1933.
