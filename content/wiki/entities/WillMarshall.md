# Will Marshall

## Overview
Will Marshall appears in the All-In liquidity panel as the [[PlanetLabs|Planet Labs]] co-founder explaining how daily Earth imaging, public-company credibility, and cheaper space infrastructure fit together.

## Current Profile
In this source, Marshall is a space-data operator rather than only a space visionary. He explains Planet's public-market journey, its customer mix, and the use of satellite time series for agriculture, disasters, energy, government, and security. He also advances a more speculative view that cheaper launch, smaller satellites, and abundant orbital solar energy could eventually support space-based data centers.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames IPO status as liquidity, cash, and enterprise/government customer credibility.
- Treats satellite miniaturization as at least as important as cheaper launch in expanding space applications.
- Presents daily Earth imaging as a data foundation for [[EarthObservationAI]].
- Argues that space-based solar availability could change data-center economics if launch costs fall enough.
- Keeps Planet Labs positioned broader than defense, while acknowledging large government revenue exposure.

## Evidence
- Public-market role: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-the-ipo-comeback-why-tech-giants-are-finally-going-public-all-in-liquidity-ipo-panel-41557065]] has Marshall describe Planet's listing as liquidity, cash, and credibility for major customers.
- Space-cost thesis: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-the-ipo-comeback-why-tech-giants-are-finally-going-public-all-in-liquidity-ipo-panel-41557065]] says launch costs have fallen several-fold and that satellite miniaturization moved the sector away from billion-dollar, many-ton spacecraft.
- Orbital-compute thesis: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-the-ipo-comeback-why-tech-giants-are-finally-going-public-all-in-liquidity-ipo-panel-41557065]] records Marshall's view that launch costs around $200-$300 per kilogram could make space data centers cheaper than ground facilities.

## Qualifications
Marshall's space data center timeline is contested inside the same source by [[AndrewFeldman|Andrew Feldman]], who emphasizes cluster-building and communications complexity. The orbital-compute forecast should therefore remain a scenario, not a settled prediction.
