# Adelaide of Italy

## Overview

Adelaide of Italy was [[OttoTheGreat]]'s empress, a powerful dowager, rival of [[Theophano]], and later guardian of the Ottonian regime after Theophano's death.

## Current Profile

The episode uses Adelaide to show that elite female authority could produce competition as well as cooperation. After Otto the Great died, she retained influence and attempted to dominate the court of [[OttoII]], but Theophano resisted and became the stronger immediate influence.

Adelaide's dismissive references to Theophano as a “Greek” woman emphasize cultural rivalry and contested status. Yet dynastic continuity ultimately depended on both women: Adelaide returned to political leadership after Theophano died in 991.

## Key Characteristics

- Consecrated empress and experienced dynastic power holder.
- Dowager whose influence survived her husband's death.
- Court rival who marked Theophano as foreign.
- Participant in intergenerational competition over access to the ruler.
- Successor to Theophano's stewardship of the young Otto III.

- Institutional setting - [[460-the-empress-of-the-apocalypse-glt9103397168]] places both women within a wider tenth-century pattern of consecrated queenship and female political authority.

## Qualifications

The source foregrounds rivalry and provides limited evidence for Adelaide's wider government, motives, or later relationship with Otto III. Hostile labels may reflect factional memory as much as stable personal feeling.
