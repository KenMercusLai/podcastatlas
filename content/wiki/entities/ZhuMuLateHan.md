## Overview
朱穆 / Zhu Mu is a Huan-era official whose current wiki profile now begins with his 146 CE staff memorial to [[LiangJiLateHan]] after [[EmperorHuanOfHan]]'s accession, then continues through later Liang Ji warnings, [[JizhouLateHan]] disaster cleanup, and anti-eunuch remonstrance. Across the bounded sources, Zhu tries to convert power into public responsibility through appointment standards, self-limitation, law enforcement, and institutional correction.

## Current Profile
[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-828-da-jiangjun-liangji-shanquan-feili-de-donghan-jianchen-llqnqijagtmdlgitulddxspncu0e|Hanji 828]] supplies the earliest current layer. As a staff officer of Liang Ji, Zhu urges the general-in-chief to prioritize court order over private desire, recruit worthy people, distance evil influences, choose upright teachers for the new emperor, and fill advisory offices such as yilang, yezhe, and daifu with qualified classical and moral officials. He recommends [[ZhongHaoLateHan]] and Luan Ba, but Liang Ji does not adopt the advice.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-834-lishi-gaosu-ni-xiaoren-weihe-chang-qiqi-lj8z2vfu6lpggumcakwejoi6z9hc|Hanji 834]] shows the same pattern later and more sharply. Zhu writes twice to Liang Ji, warning that local suffering, official violence, fiscal pressure, and unrest will attach to the general-in-chief's name if he lets subordinates and appointees abuse public authority. His remedies are concrete: remove unfit officials, reduce luxury spending, refuse local gifts, and strengthen impartial inspection. Liang Ji ignores the substance while still refraining from punishing Zhu directly.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-839-ni-neng-zuodao-wenxin-wukui-ma-lqvlswubf4ak5qfgzjf-owigneua|Hanji 839]] places Zhu in disaster response and law enforcement. Huan appoints him to Jizhou during famine and displacement; Zhu's reputation frightens corrupt officials, and his cleanup includes impeachment, imprisonment, and deaths among local administrators. His verification of [[ZhaoZhongLateHan]]'s father's burial garments exposes ritual overreach, but Huan sends him into the廷尉 process before public petitioning helps win a pardon.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-859-gudai-haoren-weishenme-nan-dang-haoguan-llkyx18ssvserxncvrtkw7xfduad|Hanji 859]] turns the profile into explicit institutional criticism. Zhu argues that zhongchangshi and related posts were not originally eunuch-only offices and should be staffed by virtuous, talented, institutionally literate people. He diagnoses how female-regency mediation normalized eunuch access between inner palace and ministers, angering Huan and inviting later eunuch slander.

## Key Characteristics
- Liang Ji staff officer who tries early to steer outer-relative power toward worthy appointments and emperor education.
- Repeated remonstrant whose concrete advice is heard but not converted into Liang Ji's self-restraint.
- Disaster-response inspector whose Jizhou appointment begins with famine, displacement, and corrupt-official cleanup.
- Law-enforcement official willing to investigate eunuch-family ritual privilege despite political danger.
- Publicly defended by Taixue students as a fair official protecting commoners and legal order.
- Later shangshu and direct anti-eunuch remonstrant.
- Exemplifies public duty that can win narrow pardon yet still fail against the ruler's preferred access structure.

## Qualifications
The current page remains bounded to the listed podcast notes rather than a full biography. Hanji 828 adds an earlier staff-advice layer and a lineage notice but does not prove that Liang Ji could realistically have been converted by different wording. Hanji 839 is earlier than Hanji 859 but was ingested later, so the frontmatter preserves provenance order while the prose uses chronological synthesis. Transcript forms such as "朱牧" are normalized to 朱穆 because the Liang Ji warning, Jizhou inspector case, and later shangshu remonstrance match the established late-Han actor.
