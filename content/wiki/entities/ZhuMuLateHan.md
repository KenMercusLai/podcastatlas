## Overview

朱穆 / Zhu Mu is a Huan-era official whose current wiki profile now begins with his 153 CE [[JizhouLateHan|冀州]] inspector appointment and later continues into his shangshu anti-eunuch remonstrance. In both branches he is a public-law actor who collides with eunuch power: first by investigating [[ZhaoZhongLateHan|赵忠]]'s father's illicit burial privilege, later by arguing that close-attendant offices should return to upright non-eunuch officials.

## Current Profile

The earlier branch in Hanji 839 places Zhu Mu in a disaster-response and law-enforcement setting. Locusts, flooding, famine, and displacement make Jizhou the worst-hit province, and Huan appoints Zhu as inspector. Zhu's reputation frightens local officials before he arrives, and his cleanup includes impeachment, imprisonment, and deaths among corrupt officials. His most dangerous action is ordering verification that Zhao Zhong's father was buried in imperial garments. The tomb opening exposes a rank violation, but Huan becomes angry and sends Zhu into the廷尉 process, where he is sentenced to hard labor.

The public response matters as much as the punishment. [[LiuTaoLateHan|刘陶 / 刘桃]] and thousands of [[Taixue|太学]] students defend Zhu at the palace gate, arguing that he acted fairly against eunuch-family abuse and that punishing him damages the law. Huan eventually pardons Zhu, but the episode does not show a structural repair of eunuch privilege.

The later Hanji 859 branch turns the same profile into explicit institutional criticism. Zhu submits a memorial arguing that zhongchangshi and related posts were not originally eunuch-only offices and should be filled by virtuous, talented, institutionally literate people. He repeats the argument face to face, diagnosing how female-regency mediation had normalized eunuch access between inner palace and ministers. Huan becomes angry, Zhu refuses to rise immediately, and eunuchs later slander him. Zhu therefore becomes a repeated case where correct public enforcement or advice is politically exposed because it threatens palace-proximity power.

## Key Characteristics

- Disaster-response inspector whose Jizhou appointment begins with famine, displacement, and corrupt-official cleanup.
- Law-enforcement official willing to investigate eunuch-family ritual privilege despite political danger.
- Publicly defended by Taixue students as a fair official protecting commoners and legal order.
- Later shangshu and direct anti-eunuch remonstrant.
- Frames eunuch dominance as a departure from older close-attendant staffing practice and palace-access history.
- Exemplifies public duty that can win narrow pardon yet still fail against the ruler's preferred access structure.

## Qualifications

The current page remains bounded to the listed podcast notes rather than a full biography. Hanji 839 is earlier than Hanji 859 but was ingested later, so the frontmatter preserves provenance order while the prose uses chronological synthesis. The transcript form "朱牧" is normalized to 朱穆 because both the Jizhou inspector case and later shangshu remonstrance match the established late-Han actor. The page treats the pardon as a narrow outcome, not proof that Huan repaired eunuch power.

- Added Hanji 839 as Zhu Mu's earliest current profile layer: Jizhou disaster response, corrupt-official cleanup, Zhao Zhong burial enforcement, punishment, and pardon.
