## Overview

朱穆 / Zhu Mu is a Huan-era shangshu whose current wiki profile is bounded by Hanji 859. The episode presents him as a direct anti-eunuch remonstrant who argues that close-attendant offices should return to virtuous, institutionally literate non-eunuch officials, then suffers eunuch retaliation after Emperor Huan rejects the advice.

## Current Profile

Zhu Mu's profile is narrow but structurally important. In Hanji 859, he first submits a memorial attacking eunuch dominance: zhongchangshi and related posts were not always eunuch-only offices, and the court should choose upright people with talent, virtue, and command of old institutions. The argument is public-minded and historically framed rather than merely personal resentment.

The second scene makes the political limit visible. Zhu Mu repeats the point face to face, explaining that He Xi Empress Dowager's female regency made eunuchs useful intermediaries between the inner palace and senior ministers, but that temporary access solution grew into a durable eunuch-power problem. Emperor Huan becomes angry, Zhu Mu refuses to rise immediately, and eunuchs later slander him repeatedly. The page therefore treats Zhu Mu as a source case for righteous advice that identifies a real institutional mechanism but fails because it attacks a palace access structure useful to the ruler.

## Key Characteristics

- Huan-era shangshu and direct anti-eunuch remonstrant.
- Frames eunuch dominance as a departure from older close-attendant staffing practice.
- Argues for selecting virtuous, upright, institutionally knowledgeable people for near-ruler offices.
- Identifies female-regency mediation as one historical reason eunuchs gained routine palace access.
- Suffers retaliation after his remonstrance angers Emperor Huan and threatens eunuch interests.
- Exemplifies the gap between correct public advice and politically acceptable reform.

## Qualifications

The current page is source-bounded to one podcast summary and does not reconstruct Zhu Mu's full biography, offices, writings, or death. The illness or sores after retaliation are preserved as the source's account rather than a medical claim. The transcript form "朱牧" is normalized to 朱穆 because the shangshu, Emperor Huan, and anti-eunuch memorial context match the late-Han actor.
