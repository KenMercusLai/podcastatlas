## Overview

马超 / Ma Chao is a western military successor who retains [[MaTeng|马腾]]'s force after Ma Teng enters court and becomes a principal coalition figure in the 211 CE [[WeinanBattleLateHan|渭南之战]], acting under military pressure while his wider household remains exposed at Ye.

## Current Profile

Hanji 1088 supplies the prehistory to the later campaign. When Cao Cao brings Ma Teng into court as weiwei, Ma Chao receives a subordinate-general title and continues leading the former force. The household later moves to Ye, creating a split between field command and family collateral. The episode then says Ma Chao rises three years later, with Ma Teng's household executed as a result.

Hanji 1105 compresses the 211 campaign itself. Ma Chao represents a militarily substantial but internally divisible coalition: Cao Cao is said to welcome the concentration of opponents because numbers do not guarantee cohesion, and [[JiaXu|贾诩]]'s counsel targets that weakness.

Hanji 1120 adds the opening decision environment. Cao Cao's announced movement against [[ZhangLuFivePecks|张鲁]] requires passage through Guanzhong, and the episode argues that this pressure is designed to make Ma Chao and [[HanSui|韩遂]] move first. Ma Chao's rising is therefore both self-protection against a perceived threat and, in the host's interpretation, the act that transfers public legitimacy to Cao Cao's suppression claim.

## Key Characteristics

- Principal opposing commander in the current Weinan evidence.
- Associated with a broader Guanzhong coalition rather than an isolated force.
- Inherits continuing field command after Ma Teng enters court.
- Acts while the wider Ma household is politically exposed at Ye.
- Responds to an ostensibly Hanzhong-bound movement that the episode reads as coercive pressure on Guanzhong.
- Represents the difference between troop concentration and coalition cohesion.

## Qualifications

The sources do not reconstruct Ma Chao's full career or offer a primary-source comparison of the campaign. Hanji 1088's title, household relocation timing, interval, execution total, and claim that Ma Teng never rebelled remain source-scoped. Hanji 1105's battlefield exchange, Cao Cao's private reaction, and exact mechanism of division are likewise bounded to that episode. Hanji 1120's strongest causal claim—that Cao Cao intended from the outset to force Ma Chao into rebellion and seize the legitimacy advantage—is preserved as the host's interpretation, not settled motive.

## What Changed

- Added the campaign-opening pressure that made the nominal Hanzhong movement look threatening to Guanzhong commanders.
- Reframed Ma Chao's first move as simultaneous self-protection, legitimacy loss, and family-collateral exposure in the source's interpretation.
- Kept Cao Cao's alleged deliberate entrapment as a qualified motive claim.
