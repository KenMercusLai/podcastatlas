# Ludwig Beck

## Overview

Ludwig Beck was the German army chief of staff who warned that an attack on [[Czechoslovakia]] could trigger a wider war and later joined military opposition to [[AdolfHitler]].

## Current Profile

During the 1938 crisis, Beck argued that Germany risked defeat if aggression against Czechoslovakia brought in France and Britain. He sought collective resignation by senior generals, but their refusal left him to resign privately in August rather than turn professional opposition into an immediate institutional break.

War games suggesting Germany might defeat Czechoslovakia quickly and then contain France weakened his case. Beck later became involved with a conspiracy alongside figures including Wilhelm Canaris, Hjalmar Schacht, and Hans Oster, but the episode treats both its political reach and its chances of success cautiously.

## Key Characteristics

- Senior army officer who challenged the strategic timing of Hitler's Czechoslovak gamble.
- Warned that a local invasion could produce an unwinnable wider conflict.
- Tried and failed to organize collective resignation by the generals.
- Resigned privately when senior colleagues would not act together.
- Later participant in a broader anti-Hitler conspiracy whose feasibility remained uncertain.
