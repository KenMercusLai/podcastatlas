# Miloje Vasić

## Overview

Miloje Vasić (1869-1956) was a Serbian archaeologist whose excavations at Vinča-Belo Brdo revealed the material record now associated with the [[VincaCulture|Vinča culture]].

## Current Profile

[[280-serbia-the-birthplace-of-civilisation-glt4941340070]] describes Vasić as Serbia's first major professional archaeologist. After study in Germany, he returned to Serbia in 1901, directed a Belgrade museum, taught archaeology at Belgrade University, and began work at the 12-meter Belo Brdo mound in 1908. The First World War interrupted excavation; British backing supported renewed campaigns from 1929 to 1931.

His fieldwork disclosed sophisticated ceramics, figurines, symbols, and deeply stratified settlement remains, but chronology exposed the limits of comparison before radiocarbon dating. Vasić first connected Vinča to a second-millennium-BCE Aegean world and later proposed a seventh- or sixth-century-BCE Greek colony. Subsequent radiocarbon results placed the occupation thousands of years earlier, preserving the importance of his discovery while overturning his historical explanation.

## Key Characteristics

- Early Serbian professional archaeologist, museum director, and university professor.
- Principal excavator of the deeply layered Belo Brdo site at Vinča.
- Worked across wartime interruption and later international excavation support.
- Correctly recognized an important material complex but assigned it chronologies later rejected by radiocarbon dating.

## Evidence

- Career and fieldwork: [[280-serbia-the-birthplace-of-civilisation-glt4941340070]] gives Vasić's dates, German education, 1901 return, museum and university roles, 1908 start, wartime interruption, and 1929-1931 British-backed campaigns.
- Discovery: [[280-serbia-the-birthplace-of-civilisation-glt4941340070]] credits his lower-layer excavations with revealing ceramics, figurative objects, motifs, and sculptural art.
- Chronological revision: [[280-serbia-the-birthplace-of-civilisation-glt4941340070]] contrasts his Aegean and Greek-colony models with later radiocarbon dating to roughly 5700-4200 BCE.

## Qualifications

This profile derives from one narrative podcast note and does not independently assess Vasić's excavation methods, publication record, institutional career, or changing arguments. His incorrect chronology should be understood in the context of pre-radiocarbon archaeological comparison, without treating technological limits as proof that every inference was reasonable. The exact role and nature of British backing remain source-scoped.
