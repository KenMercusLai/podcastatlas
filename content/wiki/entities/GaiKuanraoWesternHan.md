## Overview

盖宽饶 / Gai Kuanrao enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-441-gaikuanrao-bei-hanxuandi-bisi-beihou-de-zhichang-zhenxiang-tai-fengci-lm7iqjl6m638ulcpvir1jv7gxho0|Hanji 441]] as a Western Han sili xiaowei under [[EmperorXuanOfHan|汉宣帝]]. The episode presents him as clean, upright, and public-minded, but also as harsh, stubborn, and politically exposed because his office and speaking style created enemies.

## Current Profile

Gai's current profile is a loyal-remonstrance tragedy rather than a simple perfect-minister story. He criticizes the court's reliance on law and trusted eunuch-like insiders by contrasting sage government, classical learning, and the replacement of poetry and documents with legal technique. His memorial's reference to "天下为公" and "天下为私" is then read by hostile officials as implying that Xuan should yield the throne to him.

The source says that reading is not Gai's intended meaning, but the wording gives enemies and the ruler a usable path to punishment. [[ZhengChangRemonstratorWesternHan|郑昌]] defends him as an unsupported censorial official whose duty naturally attracts hatred, yet Xuan does not accept the defense. When Gai is handed to the yushi for judgment, he refuses humiliation and kills himself at the north gate of [[WeiyangPalace|未央宫]].

## Key Characteristics

- Clean and upright Western Han supervisory official whose sili xiaowei role made him responsible for capital-area oversight.
- Sharp critic of Xuan-era law-centered governance and trusted palace influence.
- Politically exposed speaker whose classical allusion is recoded as an abdication claim.
- Isolated official with few protectors and many enemies because of office duties and personal harshness.
- Execution-overreach case that gives direct content to Sima Guang's later criticism of Emperor Xuan's severe punishments.

## Qualifications

This page is source-scoped to Hanji 441. It does not reconstruct Gai's full biography, the original Chinese text of the memorial, the complete yushi procedure, or the broader legal history of sili xiaowei authority. The episode's claim that Gai was harsh or alienating is preserved as part of the source's interpretation, not expanded into an independent psychological verdict.
