## Overview

[[BoZhaoWesternHan|薄昭]] is [[BaoJi|薄姬 / 薄太后]]'s brother and [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]]'s maternal uncle, presented by the current source as a trusted outer relative whose military authority, killing of an imperial envoy, and compelled suicide expose the collision between family protection and public law.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-220-minjian-geyao-fengci-wendi-jia-renyi-liuheng-fanying-guguai-2-lplkv_tu9yxh-ys565ebs4unt7nj|Hanji 220 part 2]] distinguishes a terse record from an expanded anecdote. It says the dynastic table links Bo Zhao's suicide to killing an imperial envoy, while later annotation explains the killing through a grudge formed during a drinking incident. Liu Heng reportedly avoids direct execution but escalates from private persuasion to a public funeral performance that compels suicide, then mourns Bo Zhao and permits his son Bo Rong to inherit a reconstituted marquisate.

The source makes Bo Zhao a completed outer-relative risk case rather than a simple villain portrait. [[SimaGuang|司马光]] reportedly describes him as previously known for loyal and generous conduct but argues that military power, inadequate guidance, and confidence in imperial kinship enabled arrogance and lethal abuse. [[CaoPi|曹丕]] therefore treats the appointment itself as the preventable error.

An earlier intermediary role places Bo Zhao between the emperor and another difficult royal relative. Liu Heng has him address [[LiuChangHuainanKing|刘长]] as a senior relative and send a warning letter that lists imperial favors and punished-prince precedents. The episode correctly treats Bo Zhao as Liu Heng's maternal uncle but also calls him “窦太后的弟弟”; that second relationship conflicts with the established identification as [[BaoJi|薄太后]]'s brother and is rejected. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-217-kan-fuhei-hanwendi-ruhe-wansi-yimudi-liuchang-1-lglv6qnnmxlvr9qnles6zeoytmjx|Hanji 217 part 1]]

## Key Characteristics

- Maternal uncle of Emperor Wen and only brother of the living dowager in the episode's account.
- Outer relative entrusted with military authority before the fatal case.
- Senior-family intermediary used to warn Liu Chang about the limits of imperial leniency.
- Alleged killer of an imperial envoy, with the detailed private-grudge motive preserved only as an annotation story.
- Refuses an initial request to commit suicide but yields after officials stage funeral lamentation at his residence.
- Receives imperial mourning and a succession accommodation after punishment.

## Qualifications

The page rests on two popular-history episodes. Hanji 220 part 2 says the main records are sparse and that the detailed drinking dispute comes from annotation, so motive, victim identity, dialogue, procedure, exact offices, and the form of Bo Rong's succession remain source-scoped. Hanji 217 part 1 does not establish the warning letter's exact wording or effect, and its claim that Bo Zhao was Empress Dowager Dou's brother is an identity error rather than usable kinship evidence. Sima Guang's favorable description of Bo Zhao before the crime is a retrospective judgment, not a complete behavioral record.

## What Changed

- Added Bo Zhao's earlier role as the imperial intermediary who warns Liu Chang.
- Rejected the new source's incorrect identification of him as Empress Dowager Dou's brother.

## Relationships

- [[BaoJi|薄姬 / 薄太后]] - sister whose grief supplies the filial and ritual objection to his compelled death.
- [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]] - nephew, appointing ruler, punisher, mourner, and protector of posthumous family continuity.
- [[LiuChangHuainanKing|淮南王刘长]] - royal nephew whom Bo Zhao warns on Liu Heng's behalf.
- [[OuterRelativeCommandAppointmentRisk|外戚任重职风险]] - structural pattern completed by authority, abuse, and difficult accountability.
