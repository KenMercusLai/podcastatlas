# 《桃花源记》 / Peach Blossom Spring

## Overview

[[PeachBlossomSpring|《桃花源记》 / Peach Blossom Spring]] appears in the bounded sources as both a Chinese otherworld-portal comparison and a metaphor for the difference between open-ended discovery and attempted route replication.

## Current Profile

The Alice source uses the fisherman's cave and hidden path to place Wonderland's rabbit hole inside a wider [[OtherworldPortalNarrative|otherworld portal]] tradition. The Weird History source returns to the tale's sequence: the fisherman reaches the place while following curiosity without a calculated destination, but a later marked and official return cannot reproduce the route. That reading turns the work into a decision metaphor without replacing its literary ambiguity.

Together the sources make Peach Blossom Spring a boundary story. A concrete passage moves a character into another spatial and social order, while the failure to return warns that another person's path, memory, or marks are not a guaranteed procedure for reaching the same outcome.

## Key Characteristics

- Uses a narrow passage and hidden place to make a change of world perceptible.
- Supports cross-cultural portal comparison without implying direct influence on *Alice in Wonderland*.
- Contrasts curiosity-led discovery with goal-directed attempted return.
- Makes route knowledge contingent: remembered marks do not turn an experience into a reproducible formula.

## Evidence

- Portal evidence: [[180-ailisi-mengyou-xianjing-shijie-duo-huangdan-wo-yeshi-ziji-de-zhuzai-xia-975539611]] compares the tale's cave and hidden route with Alice's rabbit hole and other thresholds into altered rule-worlds.
- Decision-metaphor evidence: [[27-zhongwenxi-yingwenxi-hai-neng-xing-ma-wenshi-zuojia-ludapeng-zhangxiangrong-liao-daxue-zhuanye-xuanze-lhtme10bosqmslncrssyessqmqjc]] contrasts the fisherman's uncalculated exploration with the later attempt to retrace a marked route in major choice and life planning.

## Qualifications

The Alice comparison is structural, not an origin claim. The “fisherman's path” and “ford-seeker's path” distinction is an interpretive framework attributed by the episode to Zhang Wenjiang, not the only valid reading of Tao Yuanming's text. The metaphor should not be used to deny the value of planning, guidance, or material constraints.
