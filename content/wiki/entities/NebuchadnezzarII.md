# Nebuchadnezzar II

## Overview

Nebuchadnezzar II is the Neo-Babylonian king presented as the builder of imperial [[Babylon]], conqueror of Judah, destroyer of Jerusalem and Solomon's Temple, and later biblical image of a ruler who is both tyrant and instrument of divine punishment.

## Current Profile

The episode places Nebuchadnezzar's accession around 605 BC after Babylonian victory over Egypt in Syria. His imperial reach supplied the wealth and labor context for rebuilding Babylon's walls, Ishtar Gate, processional architecture, and Etemenanki, making the capital a model of monumental, commercial, cosmopolitan power. The Hanging Gardens remain uncertain: later tradition attributes them to a Median wife, but the episode emphasizes missing Babylonian archaeological evidence and a possible Nineveh location.

His western campaigns produced a different memory. After conquering Jerusalem in 597 BC, removing its king and elite, and installing Zedekiah, Nebuchadnezzar answered rebellion by destroying the city and Temple, killing Zedekiah's sons, blinding him, and deporting another elite group. In biblical interpretation this violence feeds both the historical crisis of [[BabylonianExileIdentityFormation]] and the larger paradox that a foreign tyrant can still act within God's judgment.

## Key Characteristics

- Neo-Babylonian ruler whose victory over Egypt opened imperial expansion into Syria and Judah.
- Monumental builder associated with Babylon's walls, gates, processional spaces, and great ziggurat.
- Imperial conqueror who used selective elite deportation as a strategy of rule.
- Destroyer of Jerusalem and Solomon's Temple after Zedekiah's revolt.
- Later biblical figure combining tyranny, arrogance, punishment, and instrumentality within divine history.

## Evidence

- Imperial and monumental rule: [[145-babylon-glt7141377161]] connects Nebuchadnezzar's accession and western victory to Babylon's walls, Ishtar Gate, Etemenanki, wealth, and cosmopolitan scale.
- Judah and exile: [[145-babylon-glt7141377161]] follows the 597 BC conquest, Zedekiah settlement and revolt, destruction of Jerusalem and its Temple, punishment of the king, and deportation of nobles, priests, and scribes.
- Biblical afterlife: [[145-babylon-glt7141377161]] describes Nebuchadnezzar as both a tyrant and an instrument of punishment, while noting that Daniel transfers or distorts elements of later Babylonian history.

## Qualifications

This profile follows one public-history episode rather than a complete reign study. City population and monument measurements, the Hanging Gardens attribution, reported punishment details, exact deportation composition, motives, and Daniel's portrait remain source-scoped. Nebuchadnezzar II must also be distinguished from the earlier Nebuchadnezzar I discussed in [[181-the-birth-of-babylon-glt1570061216]].
