## Overview

《鹿鼎记》 / *The Deer and the Cauldron* is a [[JinYong|金庸]] novel represented here through [[WeiXiaobao|韦小宝]]'s teachers, informal education, sworn relationships, and survival inside overlapping court, Jianghu, military, and frontier networks.

## Current Profile

The episode reads the novel as partly anti-wuxia because its protagonist does not progress through disciplined mastery toward martial supremacy. Formal apprenticeship, concrete tricks, street knowledge, court observation, emotional attachment, and political alliance all remain distinct. The skills that repeatedly work are often escape, performance, judgment of people, tactical imitation, and the ability to recruit capacities Wei does not possess himself.

The same reversal structures brotherhood. Ritual and inherited stories of义气 still matter, but sworn ties do not produce one uniform duty. Genuine friendship, mutual advantage, political coordination, and emergency compromise coexist, and the novel tests loyalty through conflicts among relationships rather than through a single absolute code.

## Key Characteristics

- Reworks martial apprenticeship by separating formal lineage from effective learning.
- Makes observation, imitation, escape, and social judgment central forms of competence.
- Places court, marketplace, military, religious, and Jianghu actors inside one relationship network.
- Treats sworn brotherhood as both moral language and practical coordination.
- Tests loyalty through ranked attachments and conflicting obligations.
- Uses secondary characters to expose different forms of teaching, love, fidelity, betrayal, and survival.

- [[lowwo0ixlp--ouxy87p42cult3k4]] distinguishes sincere, mixed-interest, and expedient sworn ties, then shows military and political allies completing tasks Wei cannot accomplish through martial skill alone.

- [[lowwo0ixlp--ouxy87p42cult3k4]] uses 九难, Hu Yizhi, 杨溢之, 多隆, 施琅, and other secondary figures to compare reciprocal care, idealized love, friendship across factions, ranked loyalty, and betrayal.

## Qualifications

This profile derives from one podcast episode focused on teachers and sworn brothers rather than the novel's complete plot, publication history, editions, or reception. Its anti-wuxia framing, character motives, historical analogies, and claims about Jin Yong's values are interpretations rather than settled authorial intent.
