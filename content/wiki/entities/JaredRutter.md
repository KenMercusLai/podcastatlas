# Jared Rutter

## Overview
Jared Rutter is the biochemistry and mitochondrial metabolism researcher featured in [[how-mitochondria-control-your-metabolism-dr-jared-rutter-scim5888935664|the Huberman Lab mitochondria episode]].

## Current Profile
The source presents Rutter as a University of Utah biochemistry professor and Howard Hughes Medical Institute investigator whose work centers on mitochondria, metabolism, and cellular energy allocation. His public-explanation role in the episode is to replace a simple "mitochondria as powerhouses" frame with a decision-making frame: cells route nutrients toward ATP, biomass, lactate export, fat use, or disease-linked growth depending on cell identity and state.

Rutter's research credibility in the episode is tied especially to the [[MitochondrialPyruvateCarrier]] discovery. He describes collaborative work using yeast, fruit flies, and human cells to identify MPC1 and MPC2 as the carrier that allows pyruvate to enter mitochondria, then uses that mechanism to explain heart metabolism, developmental lethality in full MPC knockout mice, and pathological heart enlargement when MPC is removed in heart tissue.

## Key Characteristics
- Explains whole-body metabolism through [[CellularMetabolismMosaic]] rather than a single calorie-rate model.
- Uses mitochondria as resource-allocation organelles, not only ATP factories.
- Connects basic cell biology to disease cases including aging hypotheses, heart failure, cancer metabolism, inflammatory disease, and future diagnostics.
- Treats model organisms and genetics as central tools for finding mechanisms relevant to human biology.
- Preserves uncertainty around aging mechanisms, cellular-resolution metabolic imaging, scent-based diagnostics, and cancer-treatment prediction.

## Evidence
- Research role: [[how-mitochondria-control-your-metabolism-dr-jared-rutter-scim5888935664]] introduces Rutter as a University of Utah professor and HHMI investigator studying mitochondria and metabolism.
- MPC mechanism: [[how-mitochondria-control-your-metabolism-dr-jared-rutter-scim5888935664]] says Rutter's lab collaborated with Carl Thummel's lab and used yeast, flies, and human cells to identify MPC1 and MPC2.
- Public synthesis style: [[how-mitochondria-control-your-metabolism-dr-jared-rutter-scim5888935664]] has Rutter explain pyruvate, lactate, cancer metabolism, fuel toxicity, and ROS through dynamic cellular feedback rather than fixed metabolic percentages.
- Evidence boundaries: [[how-mitochondria-control-your-metabolism-dr-jared-rutter-scim5888935664]] explicitly marks limits around why mitochondria decline with age, how to image metabolism in living humans, and how future cancer therapy might choose combinations.
