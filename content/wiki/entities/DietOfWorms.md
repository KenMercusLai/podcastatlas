# Diet of Worms

## Overview

The Diet of Worms was the 1521 imperial assembly at which [[MartinLuther]] was ordered to acknowledge his books and recant before [[CharlesV]] and the assembled elite of the [[HolyRomanEmpire]].

## Current Profile

The hearing concentrated a wider conflict into a public confrontation. Luther's celebrity and [[FrederickTheWise|Frederick the Wise's]] leverage secured his appearance under safe conduct; the interrogators tried to restrict him to direct answers, while Luther reframed the demand as a test of salvation, Scripture, and conscience.

Luther refused recantation unless answered from Scripture. Charles defended church tradition and later imposed the Edict of Worms, but Luther left under safe conduct before being hidden at the [[Wartburg]]. The episode therefore treats Worms as both legal process and historical theatre.

## Key Characteristics

- Imperial assembly rather than a purely ecclesiastical trial.
- Public confrontation structured around authorship and recantation.
- Clash between scriptural-conscientious justification and institutional continuity.
- Protected by temporary safe conduct despite the danger of heresy prosecution.
- Converted through print into a heroic narrative larger than the official record.

## Qualifications

The exact “Here I stand” wording is absent from the official interrogation record and appears in a version circulated by Luther's supporters. The episode's Gospel parallels and historical-theatre framing are interpretations of the event's public meaning.

## What Changed

- Established Worms as the legal, political, theological, and media hinge between Luther's challenge and outlaw status.
- Recorded the source distinction behind the famous phrase.

## Relationships

- [[MartinLuther]] - condemned reformer who refused an unconditional recantation.
- [[CharlesV]] - emperor who heard Luther and defended Catholic continuity.
- [[FrederickTheWise]] - elector whose leverage helped secure the hearing and safe conduct.
- [[SolaScripturaInterpretivePluralism]] - authority problem exposed by Luther's claim that Scripture was plain under spiritual illumination.
