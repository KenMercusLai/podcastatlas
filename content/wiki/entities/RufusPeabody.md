# Rufus Peabody

## Overview

Rufus Peabody is the quantitative sports bettor whose education, modeling, and treatment by mobile sportsbooks anchor [[gambling-with-michael-lewis-gambling-with-michael-lewis|Gambling with Michael Lewis]].

## Current Profile

The episode presents Peabody as motivated by explaining athletic outcomes rather than by a childhood identity as a gambler. At Yale he studied statistics and betting-market biases, then learned bookmaking logic during an internship at [[RoxyRoxborough|Roxy Roxborough's]] Las Vegas Sports Consultants.

His betting method searches for repeatable drivers beneath surface outcomes: pitch movement, bat speed, exit velocity, aging, and other deeper performance measures. The source's decisive mobile-market case is that [[DraftKings]] reduced his golf limits after a week in which he lost money because later line movement still indicated that his wagers contained information likely to win over time.

## Key Characteristics

- Uses statistical models to explain why outcomes occur rather than extrapolating surface results.
- Studies psychological bias as a potential gap between public prices and estimated true value.
- Treats market movement after a wager as evidence that other participants value the information.
- Coordinates capital and distributed wager placement when opportunities exceed one person's limits.
- Illustrates how a bettor can lose in the short term while still being classified as a durable operator risk.
