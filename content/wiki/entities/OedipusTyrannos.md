# Oedipus Tyrannos / Oedipus Rex

## Overview

*Oedipus Tyrannos*, also known as *Oedipus Rex*, is [[Sophocles]]' tragedy of a Theban ruler whose investigation of an old murder reveals his own parricide, incest, and civic pollution.

## Current Profile

The play begins not with the Sphinx but with plague. Apollo's demand that Laius's murderer be expelled makes knowledge a public necessity, and [[Oedipus]] commits himself to an inquiry whose witnesses, memories, prophecy, and messenger reports steadily collapse the distinction between detective and criminal. The form joins tragic inevitability to procedural discovery: each attempt to disprove the oracle supplies another part of its confirmation.

Recognition does not simplify guilt. Oedipus killed Laius and married Jocasta without knowing their identities, yet Thebes still suffers and the truth still destroys the royal house. Jocasta hangs herself; Oedipus uses her brooches to blind himself; and rule passes away from him. The episode treats the ending as pity for someone both responsible and trapped rather than as proof of a consciously desired crime.

## Key Characteristics

- Opens with plague and pollution, turning hidden kinship into a civic emergency.
- Uses investigation, witness convergence, dramatic irony, recognition, and reversal as a tightly compressed tragic structure.
- Makes the investigator the offender he seeks without making his earlier acts knowingly incestuous or parricidal.
- Contrasts [[Tiresias]]' blind knowledge with Oedipus's sighted ignorance and later self-blinding.
- Places tyrannos, divine necessity, public confidence, and unstable human knowledge in the same dramatic field.

## Evidence

- Inquiry structure: [[603-greek-myths-the-riddle-of-the-sphinx-part-2-glt9108845593]] follows the oracle, Tiresias, Jocasta's crossroads detail, the Corinthian messenger, and the surviving slave toward recognition.
- Unwitting guilt: [[603-greek-myths-the-riddle-of-the-sphinx-part-2-glt9108845593]] emphasizes that Oedipus does not know Laius is his father or Jocasta his mother when the acts occur.
- Civic and political horizon: [[603-greek-myths-the-riddle-of-the-sphinx-part-2-glt9108845593]] connects plague, pollution, tyrannos, and Athenian war experience without reducing the play to a topical allegory.

## Qualifications

The detective-story label is a modern analogy, and the later “three unities” formulation is not identical to Sophocles' own stated program. The source does not establish one performance date, one audience response, or one political key. Its plot follows Sophocles' selection from variant traditions rather than a canonical biography of Oedipus.

## What Changed

- Created the play profile around inquiry, unwitting guilt, civic pollution, and historically situated reception.
