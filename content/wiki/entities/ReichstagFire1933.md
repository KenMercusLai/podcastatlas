# Reichstag Fire (1933)

## Overview

The Reichstag fire was the 27 February 1933 burning of the German parliament building that [[AdolfHitler]] and other Nazi leaders converted into a communist-conspiracy pretext for mass arrest and emergency rule.

## Current Profile

The episode attributes the fire to [[MarinusVanDerLubbe|Marinus van der Lubbe]] acting alone after earlier failed arson attempts. It rejects both the claim that Nazis secretly organized the blaze and the claim that it formed part of a communist insurrection.

The fire's decisive historical role lay in appropriation. Hitler demanded ruthless action, police used existing lists to arrest thousands of communists, and the cabinet secured the [[ReichstagFireDecree|Reichstag Fire Decree]] within a day. A contingent crime was thereby converted into a state narrative of existential emergency.

## Key Characteristics

- It occurred after Hitler became chancellor but before the March 1933 election.
- The episode attributes the fire to van der Lubbe acting alone.
- Nazi leaders immediately framed it as proof of coordinated communist revolt.
- Police preparedness allowed rapid mass arrests from existing lists.
- It supplied the public emergency pretext for suspending liberties and expanding central power.

## Qualifications

The fire's authorship has produced enduring controversy. This page records the episode's explicit lone-actor judgment rather than claiming an independent forensic resolution. Precise fire behavior, chronology, arrest totals, and leaders' private knowledge remain source-scoped.
