# Galahad

## Overview

Galahad is the later Grail knight who succeeds where Perceval and [[Lancelot]] fail because romance joins perfect lineage and martial excellence to chastity, holiness, and visionary fulfillment.

## Current Profile

[[346-the-mystery-of-the-holy-grail-part-2-glt9518077570]] places Galahad in a developed Round Table quest rather than Chrétien's original unfinished narrative. His arrival is marked by the Siege Perilous and sword-in-the-stone signs; his parentage joins Lancelot to the Grail-guardian line; and his purity contrasts with Lancelot's affair with [[Guinevere]].

Galahad makes [[ChivalricSacralization]] more programmatic. The successful knight does not merely locate an object: he receives a beatific vision expressed through the vocabulary of daring, prowess, and marvel. [[714-king-arthur-lancelot-and-the-round-table-part-2-glt6459272632]] sharpens the father-son correction by making Lancelot's adulterous love a disqualification measured through 24 years and a 24-day exclusion from the Grail. Yet the same source judges Galahad's flawless severity less emotionally compelling, showing that moral perfection and narrative attraction do not coincide.

## Key Characteristics

- Later replacement or culmination of the earlier Perceval-centered quest.
- Son of Lancelot and a woman from the Grail-guardian lineage.
- Miraculously authenticated by the Siege Perilous and sword signs.
- Chaste knight whose holiness distinguishes him from Lancelot.
- Recipient of the Grail quest's culminating beatific vision.
- Sacred correction whose perfection limits the inner conflict that makes Lancelot dramatically compelling.

## Evidence

### Sacred qualifications

- [[346-the-mystery-of-the-holy-grail-part-2-glt9518077570]] joins Galahad's lineage to Lancelot, the Grail family, Siege Perilous, sword signs, successful quest, and beatific vision.

### Correction of Lancelot

- [[714-king-arthur-lancelot-and-the-round-table-part-2-glt6459272632]] contrasts Galahad's chastity and direct vision of God with Lancelot's exclusion and prolonged unconsciousness.

### Emotional limitation

- [[714-king-arthur-lancelot-and-the-round-table-part-2-glt6459272632]] treats Galahad as remote and severe beside his morally divided father.

## Qualifications

Galahad belongs to a later textual stage and should not be projected backward into Chrétien's *Perceval*. The sources' interpretation of his name, Cistercian role, 24-year symbolism, and ideological purpose is suggestive rather than a complete study of the prose cycles. Calling him less appealing records the hosts' literary judgment, not a historical audience consensus.

## What Changed

- Sharpened Galahad as a sacred correction to Lancelot whose moral perfection also reduces dramatic conflict.
