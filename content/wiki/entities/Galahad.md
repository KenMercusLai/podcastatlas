# Galahad

## Overview

Galahad is the later Grail knight who succeeds where Perceval and Lancelot fail because romance joins perfect lineage and martial excellence to chastity, holiness, and visionary fulfillment.

## Current Profile

[[346-the-mystery-of-the-holy-grail-part-2-glt9518077570]] places Galahad in a developed Round Table quest rather than Chrétien's original unfinished narrative. His arrival is marked by the Siege Perilous and sword-in-the-stone signs; his parentage joins Lancelot to the Grail-guardian line; and his purity contrasts with Lancelot's affair with Guinevere.

Galahad makes [[ChivalricSacralization]] more programmatic. The successful knight does not merely locate an object: he receives a beatific vision expressed through the vocabulary of daring, prowess, and marvel. The tradition thereby absorbs martial excellence into a Christian hierarchy of holiness.

## Key Characteristics

- Later replacement or culmination of the earlier Perceval-centered quest.
- Son of Lancelot and a woman from the Grail-guardian lineage.
- Miraculously authenticated by the Siege Perilous and sword signs.
- Chaste knight whose holiness distinguishes him from Lancelot.
- Recipient of the Grail quest's culminating beatific vision.

## Evidence

- Genealogy and contrast: [[346-the-mystery-of-the-holy-grail-part-2-glt9518077570]] joins Galahad's lineage to Lancelot's failure and the Grail family.
- Miraculous authentication: [[346-the-mystery-of-the-holy-grail-part-2-glt9518077570]] describes the Siege Perilous and floating sword-in-stone signs.
- Successful quest: [[346-the-mystery-of-the-holy-grail-part-2-glt9518077570]] identifies Galahad as the Round Table knight who wins the Grail.
- Visionary culmination: [[346-the-mystery-of-the-holy-grail-part-2-glt9518077570]] connects his beatific vision to chivalric language and militant Christian ideals.

## Qualifications

Galahad belongs to a later textual stage and should not be projected backward into Chrétien's *Perceval*. The episode's interpretation of his name, Cistercian resonances, and ideological role is suggestive but source-scoped rather than a complete study of the relevant romance cycle.

## What Changed

- Created a profile distinguishing Galahad's perfected sacred knighthood from Perceval's unfinished moral education.
