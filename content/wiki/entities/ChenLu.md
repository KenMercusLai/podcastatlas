# Chen Lu / 陈箓

## Overview

陈箓 was a French-trained jurist and veteran Chinese diplomat who later became foreign minister of the Japanese-backed Reformed Government and was assassinated in Shanghai in 1939.

## Current Profile

The bounded source presents Chen's career as a sharp political reversal. He studied law in France, contributed to late-Qing legal translation and codification, and then held senior diplomatic posts under the Republic, including work connected to Mongolia, Mexico, France, and the foreign ministry. After the Nanjing government displaced the Beiyang political order and ended his official career, he later accepted office in a Japanese-backed regime created during the occupation of eastern China.

On 19 February 1939, gunmen entered his Shanghai residence and killed him in front of family members and guests. The assassins left an anti-collaborationist proclamation, making his death both a covert operation and a public message about the cost of serving an occupation regime.

## Key Characteristics

- Combined French legal education with late-Qing legal translation and codification work.
- Built a long Republican diplomatic career across ministry and overseas posts.
- Lost office when the Nanjing government displaced the Beiyang establishment.
- Joined the Japanese-backed Reformed Government as foreign minister.
- Became a symbolic target of Nationalist “traitor-elimination” violence in occupied Shanghai.
