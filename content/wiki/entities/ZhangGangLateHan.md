## Overview

张纲 / Zhang Gang enters the wiki before his better-known buried-wheel scene as an early critic of eunuch-linked privilege under [[EmperorShunOfHan|汉顺帝]]. Hanji 817 shows his memorial against unmerited offices, salaries, and titles being ignored; Hanji 822 then shows him turning an imperial inspection mission away from routine local review and toward the protected center of corruption. Hanji 823 completes the immediate aftereffect: Liang Ji sends him to Guangling as a dangerous assignment, but Zhang Gang uses direct contact, grievance recognition, credible amnesty, and post-surrender settlement to bring Zhang Ying's long-running rebellion into submission. Hanji 825 then shows the limit of that success after Zhang Gang dies and Zhang Ying rises again.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-817-kan-si-ruanruo-de-ren-shize-huode-geng-tongtou-llmgqys237de3dzb6ij-4szyetye|Hanji 817]] supplies Zhang Gang's earliest current layer. In 135 CE, after the court allows eunuchs' adopted sons to inherit titles, Zhang Gang memorializes that petty and undeserving people are receiving offices, salaries, and nobility. Shun does not adopt the protest, so Zhang Gang appears first as a truth-telling censor whose warning enters court record without producing policy repair.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-822-chailang-dangdao-ta-jiang-maotou-zhizhi-huangdi-yuefu-lqhpmayvd3p07byr-zoiv-jyhdv|Hanji 822]] then places Zhang Gang among the eight inspectors sent after a 142 CE amnesty to praise good officials and investigate corrupt ones. Instead of departing for a provincial circuit like the others, he buries his carriage wheel at Luoyang's duting and says that wolves are blocking the road, so it is pointless to ask only about foxes.

The action reframes the inspection problem. Zhang Gang submits a memorial against [[LiangJiLateHan|梁冀]] and [[LiangBuyiLateHan|梁不疑]] with fifteen major accusations, producing shock in Luoyang. The source says [[EmperorShunOfHan|汉顺帝]] knows the memorial is correct but cannot immediately punish the Liang brothers because Liang-family harem favor and court networks are too strong.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-823-bingbuxueren-shou-zeikou-ta-ruhe-zuodao-de-luai1f0nhb-ddajli99dw4bxj9m2|Hanji 823]] supplies the local-governance branch that was previously only visible in outline. Liang Ji, hating Zhang Gang, appoints him as [[GuanglingCommandery|广陵郡]] administrator in a district where [[ZhangYingGuanglingLateHan|张婴]] has disturbed Yang and Xu for more than ten years. Zhang Gang does not ask for troops; he arrives by single carriage, dismisses most attendants at Zhang Ying's camp, and requests a meeting by letter.

His persuasion works because it is not simple leniency. Zhang Gang tells Zhang Ying that earlier two-thousand-shi officials were indeed greedy and violent, which explains the rebels' anger, but he also says rebellion is not righteous and that refusal could bring multi-province imperial force. He then makes surrender concrete: Zhang Ying's followers are dispersed, residence and farmland are arranged, and descendants who want office are drawn into local service. Zhang Gang's later death draws mourning and burial labor from Zhang Ying's group, while Liang Ji blocks the fuller court reward.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-825-lishi-gaosu-ni-dang-ni-meiyong-shi-renxing-zui-zisi-luv-8z9axuejnfhlkgsuxee3uked|Hanji 825]] supplies the later aftereffect. After Zhang Gang dies, Zhang Ying again gathers several thousand people and occupies Guangling. This does not erase the success of Hanji 823; it shows that Zhang Gang's settlement depended heavily on his personal credibility and did not become a durable institutional channel after he was gone.

## Key Characteristics

- Inspector who refuses to treat local officials as the only proper target when the central powerholder is the root problem.
- Early anti-privilege censor whose eunuch-title protest reaches Shun without changing policy.
- Remonstrant whose "wolves before foxes" framing turns hierarchy into an accountability argument.
- Politically exposed anti-Liang voice whose truthful accusation creates retaliation risk.
- Pacification official who turns grievance acknowledgement into surrender without beginning from troop escalation.
- Administrator who makes surrender durable through housing, farmland, and office pathways for the rebel group.
- Local authority figure whose death is mourned by former rebels yet followed by renewed Guangling disorder.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 822, Hanji 825, Hanji 823, and Hanji 817. It does not reconstruct Zhang Gang's full career, family background, the full wording of the 135 CE memorial, the complete text of all fifteen charges, the court debate over his reward, or the later suppression details after Zhang Ying rises again. Hanji 823's transcriptal 张刚 and 张英 are normalized to 张纲 and 张婴 because the received Tongjian passage and Hanji 825 identity context support those forms.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 817 as Zhang Gang's earliest current layer: an ignored anti-eunuch-privilege memorial under Shun.
- Preserved Hanji 823 as the full Guangling pacification layer between Zhang Gang's buried-wheel impeachment and Zhang Ying's later renewed revolt.
- Reframed Zhang Gang from only a court remonstrant into a paired central-accountability and local-settlement official.
- Reframed Hanji 825's renewed revolt as post-death fragility after a documented earlier surrender rather than as the first visible Zhang Ying event.
