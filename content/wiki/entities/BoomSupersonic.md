# Boom Supersonic

## Overview
Boom Supersonic is [[BlakeScholl]]'s commercial aerospace company pursuing faster passenger travel through [[OvertureSupersonicAirliner|Overture]], the [[XB1SupersonicDemonstrator|XB-1]] demonstrator, internally developed engines, and [[BoomlessCruise]].

## Current Profile
The bounded sources present Boom as a staged hard-tech de-risking program rather than a single aircraft bet. Its sequence runs from the [[AllBusinessClassSupersonicModel|premium-market thesis]] and customer intent through XB-1 flight proof, the [[RollsRoyce|Rolls-Royce]] break, engine vertical integration, boomless overland operation, regulatory advocacy, and a proposed ground-power business. Part 2 adds the connective tissue: shared core technology, [[Makeboom|integrated simulation]], factory acceleration, recruiting, and power revenue are meant to reinforce the aircraft mission, although they also widen execution risk before the passenger aircraft is certified.

## Key Characteristics
- Boom narrows the first passenger market around premium travelers and time savings rather than claiming immediate mass affordability.
- XB-1 serves as an organizational learning platform and visible proof that the company can design and fly a supersonic jet.
- The company responded to engine-supplier failure by taking responsibility for engine design, turbine components, assembly, and testing.
- [[BoomlessCruise]] links aircraft operations, engine capability, weather, and regulation into a proposed overland-speed advantage.
- The [[GroundPowerEngineValidationFlywheel|ground-power program]] reuses the engine core for behind-the-meter generation, seeking revenue and reliability evidence before aviation service.
- Boom's strategy remains exposed to certification, manufacturing, schedule, fuel, emissions, demand, financing, and multi-market execution risk.
- The company treats digital engineering and reconfigurable turbine production as potential commercial and [[CommercialManufacturingSurgeCapacity|national-resilience]] infrastructure.

## Qualifications
The evidence inventory is dominated by interviews with Boom's founder. Customer demand, output, reliability, water use, fare, regulatory, defense-readiness, and service-date claims are not independently validated here. Ground operation can generate useful data without automatically satisfying aircraft certification, duty-cycle, integration, or passenger-safety requirements. Expanding into power may finance the aircraft program or distract scarce manufacturing and management capacity.
