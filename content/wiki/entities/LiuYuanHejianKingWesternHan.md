## Overview

河间王刘元 / Liu Yuan is presented as a later successor in [[LiuDeHejianKingWesternHan|河间献王刘德]]'s line whose coercion of royal consorts, retaliation against an official's family, and violence after deposition turn inherited kingship into a case of escalating royal accountability.

## Current Profile

Hanji 237 part 2 places Liu Yuan in the sixth generation of the Hejian royal line. It says he brought former consorts from other royal houses into his household and, after a provincial accusation triggered investigation, coerced seven women to die. The first response is limited territorial punishment: two counties are removed rather than the king being deposed.

The profile changes after Liu Yuan retaliates against 少史留贵. When the official escapes and seeks to report him, Liu Yuan has the official's mother killed. The central court then treats him as unfit to rule, removes his kingship, and sends him to Fangling. The supplied summary incorrectly assigns both stages to [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]; the canonical profile leaves the emperor unnamed because the received chronology places them under later rulers.

Deposition does not end the violence. The episode says Liu Yuan later beats his wife in a carriage and forces her to shave her head. A regional official requests prosecution, but Liu Yuan dies before the case is completed. His trajectory therefore shows partial punishment failing to reform conduct, followed by removal of office without immediate behavioral change.

## Key Characteristics

- Later Western Han Hejian king in the dynastic line descending from Liu De.
- Uses royal household power to obtain former consorts from other princely branches.
- Coerces seven women to die after a provincial accusation brings investigation.
- Initially loses two counties rather than the kingship itself.
- Retaliates against 留贵 by arranging the killing of the official's mother.
- Is deposed and sent to Fangling after the court judges him unfit to rule.
- Continues violent conduct after deposition and dies before a later prosecution concludes.

## Qualifications

The current profile is bounded to one compact popular-history summary. Its emperor attribution is chronologically incorrect, so the canonical page does not reproduce it. The exact identities and kinship positions of the former consorts, Liu Yuan's motives, the provincial and judicial procedure, the meaning of the two-county reduction, and the later assault remain source-scoped unless compared with a full critical edition. The received record supports the broad sequence but does not make every modern moral or psychological inference established fact.

## What Changed

- Created Liu Yuan as a distinct later Hejian king rather than conflating him with Liu De or with Han Wudi-era royal cases.
- Separated the initially limited territorial penalty from the later deposition after retaliatory killing.
- Corrected the episode's Han Wudi attribution by preserving the later Western Han chronology boundary.
