## Overview

路博德 / Lu Bode is a Western Han commander whose current profile spans Juyan frontier construction, support for [[LiGuangliWesternHan|李广利]] in a large anti-[[Xiongnu|匈奴]] campaign, and intended support for [[LiLingWesternHan|李陵]]. His refusal to accept the latter subordinate role and his seasonally cautious memorial become consequential when [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] mistakes the memorial for evidence that Li Ling has lost courage.

## Current Profile

Hanji 374-1 gives Lu Bode an earlier fixed-defense role in 102 BCE. As Han prepares for risk after a Xiongnu succession, Wudi orders the Qiangnu Duwei to build at Juyan and hold the position within a wider wall, fort, beacon, and garrison program.

Hanji 380-1 gives Lu Bode a narrow operational role in a 97 BCE campaign: he leads more than ten thousand men to rendezvous with Li Guangli while other Han forces depart from Wuyuan and Yanmen.

Hanji 377-1 supplies an earlier and more independent decision. After Li Ling volunteers to lead five thousand infantry in 99 BCE, Wudi orders Lu to support him en route. The episode says Lu does not want to serve as Li Ling's rear support and memorializes that autumn is a poor time to fight because Xiongnu horses are fat, recommending a joint spring campaign instead. Wudi reads this as a message engineered by a frightened Li Ling, becomes angry at Li Ling rather than Lu, sends Lu toward Xihe, and orders Li Ling onto a separate September route. Lu's advice can be militarily coherent even though his role resistance and the court's interpretation damage coordination.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han commander titled Qiangnu Duwei in the episode.
- Assigned to construct and hold the Juyan position during a broader frontier-defense reinforcement.
- Assigned to join Li Guangli during the 97 BCE anti-Xiongnu campaign.
- Intended support commander for Li Ling's five thousand-man infantry operation in 99 BCE.
- Seasonally cautious memorialist whose delay recommendation is entangled with refusal to accept a subordinate role.
- Actor in a command misattribution that separates the support and combat missions before Li Ling's isolated defeat.

## Qualifications

These sources do not reconstruct Lu Bode's full office history, earlier campaigns, later career, or exact command structure. Hanji 374-1's wording is normalized from “陆博德” to the established 路博德 identity, but the exact installation type and force size remain source-scoped. Hanji 377-1 attributes both role aversion and seasonal reasoning to his memorial, but does not establish the full private motive, whether spring delay was operationally preferable, or the complete court exchange. Its 99 BCE deployment should not be merged with Hanji 380-1's 97 BCE campaign notice without chronology checking.

## What Changed

- Added the 102 BCE Juyan construction-and-garrison role before Lu Bode's later mobile campaign assignments.
- Preserved the distinction between plausible seasonal caution and the episode's source-scoped claim that he resisted subordinate status.
