# Galerius

## Overview

Galerius was [[Diocletian]]'s eastern Caesar in the [[Tetrarchy]], a commander in the Persian victory that produced the Treaty of Nisibis and an advocate of harsh action against Christians.

## Current Profile

The episode places Galerius over Greece and the Balkans while Diocletian retained the richer eastern provinces. Their war against Persia culminated in capture of the Persian royal family and a favorable 299 settlement that strengthened Rome's eastern frontier.

Galerius also appears in the episode's account of the Great Persecution as the figure pressing Diocletian toward greater severity. After the 305 abdications he became Augustus, but the new succession excluded powerful imperial sons and did not stabilize the regime.

## Key Characteristics

- Eastern Caesar responsible for Greece and the Balkans in the tetrarchic division.
- Military partner in the victory over Persia and Treaty of Nisibis.
- Advocate of harsher anti-Christian policy in the episode's account.
- Augustus after Diocletian's abdication.
- Participant in a succession settlement undermined by excluded dynastic claimants.

## Evidence

- Tetrarchic role: [[260-croatia-the-man-who-saved-the-roman-empire-glt9631298578]] assigns Galerius the Balkan and Greek sphere as Diocletian's Caesar.
- Persian settlement: [[260-croatia-the-man-who-saved-the-roman-empire-glt9631298578]] links him and Diocletian to the victory and favorable Treaty of Nisibis.
- Persecution and succession: [[260-croatia-the-man-who-saved-the-roman-empire-glt9631298578]] presents him as a pressure source for persecution and as an Augustus in the unstable 305 settlement.

## Qualifications

The allocation of personal responsibility for the Great Persecution is filtered through later evidence and the episode's interpretation; Galerius's pressure does not remove Diocletian's responsibility as senior ruler. This page does not cover Galerius's entire reign or later religious policy.

## What Changed

- Created Galerius as eastern Caesar, Persian-war commander, persecution advocate, and successor Augustus.

## Relationships

- [[Diocletian]] - senior Augustus and eastern partner.
- [[Tetrarchy]] - structure in which Galerius advanced from Caesar to Augustus.
- [[Maximian]] - counterpart senior ruler in the West before the 305 abdication.
- [[ConstantineTheGreat|Constantine the Great]] - excluded dynastic claimant whose later rise destabilized the settlement.
