# Tupamaros

## Overview

The Tupamaros, formally the Movimiento de Liberacion Nacional, were the Uruguayan urban guerrilla movement founded by [[RaulSendic|Raul Sendic]] and central to [[261-uruguay-the-tupamaros-glt1539968794]].

## Current Profile

The episode places the movement inside [[Uruguay]]'s postwar economic decline and Cold War radicalization. It began with cellular organization, robberies, and a Robin Hood reputation, then escalated into kidnapping, selective assassination, and [[UrbanGuerrillaArmedPropaganda]]. These tactics created publicity and exposed state weakness, but also helped open a [[DemocraticSecurityEscalationTrap]] in which counterinsurgency expanded into torture, mass imprisonment, military power, and dictatorship.

## Key Characteristics

- Combined socialist labor politics with Marxism, anti-imperialism, and Tupac Amaru II symbolism.
- Used cellular urban organization rather than a rural guerrilla model in the episode's account.
- Treated robbery as financing, publicity, and reputation-making.
- Made kidnapping a signature tactic with political and sometimes ransom demands.
- Escalated after emergency rule into selective killing and armed propaganda.
- Was militarily crushed while the state's response also destroyed democratic government.

## Evidence

- Formation and symbolism: [[261-uruguay-the-tupamaros-glt1539968794]] links Sendic's labor activism, the Cuban Revolution's aftermath, Marxism, and the name derived from Tupac Amaru II.
- Tactical development: [[261-uruguay-the-tupamaros-glt1539968794]] follows robberies, the Punta del Este casino episode, kidnappings, captive-audience speeches, and selective assassination.
- Escalation and defeat: [[261-uruguay-the-tupamaros-glt1539968794]] connects the Mitrione and Jackson cases to mass arrests, torture, military expansion, and the movement's destruction.

## Qualifications

This profile is based on one narrative podcast episode, not a complete organizational history. The source does not settle membership totals, internal debates, command responsibility for every operation, popular support, or the relative causal weight of guerrilla violence and pre-existing state weakness. Explaining escalation does not excuse kidnapping or killing, and condemning those tactics does not legitimize torture or dictatorship.

## What Changed

- Created the movement page and separated tactical effectiveness from democratic consequences.
- Preserved the difference between early redistributive reputation and later coercive escalation.
