# Maya Shankar

## Overview
Maya Shankar is a cognitive scientist, former White House behavioral-science adviser, and podcast host whose work in the supplied sources connects identity change with goal pursuit and the “middle problem” of motivation.

## Current Profile
In [[how-to-shape-your-identity-goals-dr-maya-shankar-scim1420437673]], Shankar uses her path from Juilliard-bound violinist through injury, cognitive science, public policy, and podcasting to argue for [[PurposeAnchoredIdentity|anchoring identity in durable motives]] rather than one role. She links that flexibility to curiosity, uncertainty, challenge-oriented feedback, differentiated empathy, and adaptable goal design.

[[goals-toolkit-how-to-set-achieve-your-goals-scim1532399364]] separately credits her with the “middle problem”: motivation can sag between a salient beginning and endpoint across sessions, weeks, or longer cycles. Smaller chunks can make that middle more tractable without proving that every motivation dip has one cause.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses autobiographical identity disruption to connect psychological research with practical adaptation.
- Distinguishes a durable motive or “why” from a fragile role-only definition of self.
- Treats curiosity and small tests as a bridge into new directions after loss.
- Connects goal framing, agency, physiological state, slack, fresh starts, time horizons, and reward pairing to pursuit.
- Differentiates comfort from feedback that actively tests an interpretation.
- Supplies a temporal account of middle-stage motivation and supports smaller chunks as one response.

## Evidence
- Identity and transition - [[how-to-shape-your-identity-goals-dr-maya-shankar-scim1420437673]] connects Shankar's violin injury and later roles through curiosity, connection, and improvement.
- Goal and feedback tools - [[how-to-shape-your-identity-goals-dr-maya-shankar-scim1420437673]] presents approach framing, agency, slack, fresh starts, temptation bundling, perspective distance, and cognitive advisers.
- Middle-problem attribution - [[goals-toolkit-how-to-set-achieve-your-goals-scim1532399364]] credits Shankar for the pattern and its application across several goal timescales.

## Qualifications
The sources are podcast summaries rather than complete research reviews. They do not provide full citations, samples, effect sizes, or boundary conditions for many studies discussed. Shankar's career transition is an illustrative case rather than proof that every loss can be resolved through purpose reframing, and the practical tools do not replace clinical care or structural support.
