## Overview
中国足球超级联赛 / Chinese Super League is the Chinese top football league used by the wiki as a sports-rights bubble example, the competitive arena shaped by [[Evergrande]]'s football project, and a live policy-design case for expansion, financial discipline, calendar reform, foreign-player rules, and Asian competitiveness.

## Current Profile
The league's current profile has four connected branches. In media rights, it shows that a local market can be overvalued when platforms pay beyond recoverable audience, advertising, membership, and sublicensing economics. In football legacy, it is where [[GuangzhouFC]] rose, the [[ChineseFootballGoldenYuanEra]] became a multi-club arms race, and successor identity now matters. In domestic policy, expansion is viable only if [[ChineseLeagueOne]], [[ChineseFootballFinancialDiscipline]], [[ChineseFootballCalendarPolicy]], and [[ChineseFootballForeignPlayerPolicy]] move together.

The Asian-competition branch makes the league the base layer for continental performance. A 30-round season with irregular congestion and stoppages is presented as insufficiently stable; more meaningful matches, protected club continuity, deeper rosters, and better AFC coordination are argued to matter more immediately than a rushed cross-year conversion. National-team goals remain important, but should not repeatedly suspend the institution expected to develop players and clubs.

## Key Characteristics
- Local sports-rights case showing that bubble conditions depend on monetization capacity, not only absolute rights prices.
- Competitive stage for Evergrande-era [[GuangzhouFC]]'s rapid rise, eight domestic titles, and two Asian titles.
- Spending-arms-race arena where the source dates the true golden-yuan era to the broader 2016 multi-club escalation.
- Current identity target because [[GuangzhouLeopard]]'s promotion could mean Guangzhou football returns to the top league without resolving [[GuangzhouFC]] supporter continuity.
- Expansion candidate whose move from 16 to 18 clubs is argued to require advance rules, second-tier linkage, and playoff-style transition design.
- Policy testbed where calendar quality, foreign-player roster depth, and club financial discipline interact with market recovery.
- Domestic base whose match supply, continuity, and squad quality shape [[AFCChampionsLeague]] readiness, while repeated national-team stoppages can undermine the institution expected to develop that capacity.

## Qualifications
The rights-market source is about media economics, not on-field quality. The football episodes are interpretive discussions, not a complete league history, adopted regulation, audited financial dataset, or causal study of match volume and player development. Golden-yuan timing, supporter adoption, expansion, roster changes, AFC incentives, and national-team effects remain source-scoped.
