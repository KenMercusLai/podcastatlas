## Overview
中国足球超级联赛 / Chinese Super League is the Chinese top football league used by the wiki as both a sports-rights bubble example and the competitive arena shaped by [[Evergrande]]'s football project. One source uses it to define local monetization limits in sports media rights; the Evergrande legacy source uses it to discuss club spending, domestic salary escalation, supporter continuity, and Guangzhou's possible return through [[GuangzhouLeopard]].

## Current Profile
The league's current profile has two distinct branches. In the media-rights branch, the league shows that a local rights market can be overvalued when platforms pay more than audience, advertising, membership, and sublicensing economics can support. In the football-legacy branch, the league is where [[GuangzhouFC]] rose rapidly after 2010, where the [[ChineseFootballGoldenYuanEra]] later became a multi-club arms race, and where successor identity now matters because Guangzhou football may return before Guangzhou FC's original institutional lineage does.

## Key Characteristics
- Local sports-rights case showing that bubble conditions depend on monetization capacity, not only absolute rights prices.
- Competitive stage for Evergrande-era [[GuangzhouFC]]'s rapid rise, eight domestic titles, and two Asian titles.
- Spending-arms-race arena where the source dates the true golden-yuan era to the broader 2016 multi-club escalation.
- Salary-expectation reset environment for domestic players after other clubs began competing for Guangzhou's player base.
- Current identity target because [[GuangzhouLeopard]]'s promotion could mean Guangzhou football returns to the top league without resolving [[GuangzhouFC]] supporter continuity.

## Qualifications
The rights-market source is about media economics, not on-field quality. The Evergrande source is an interpretive football conversation, not a complete league history or audited salary dataset. League-wide golden-yuan timing and supporter adoption forecasts remain source-scoped.
