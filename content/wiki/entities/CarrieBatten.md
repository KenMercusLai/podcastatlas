# Carrie Batten

## Overview

Carrie Batten is the [[NewYorkMagazine|New York Magazine]] contributing writer interviewed by [[MarketplaceTech]] about [[Meta]]'s attempt to make [[RayBanSmartGlasses|Ray-Ban smart glasses]] more culturally acceptable. In the wiki, her role is as a reporting source on smart glasses as fashion, creator infrastructure, and privacy-laden social object rather than only as hardware.

## Current Profile

Batten's comments connect product adoption to social context. She treats the glasses as a device whose technical use cases can be real, especially when the wearer needs hands-free first-person capture, while also arguing that eye-level recording changes how bystanders feel in ordinary rooms.

Her strongest contribution is the distribution diagnosis. She says Meta may make the product mainstream through creator partnerships, gifting, and celebrity-fashion visibility even when she had trouble finding people who bought the glasses without receiving them for free.

## Key Characteristics

- Batten frames smart glasses as a cultural and fashion problem, not only a gadget review.
- She keeps utility and social discomfort in the same analysis: travel, cooking, and gardening can work, but bystander capture remains hard to avoid.
- She treats women's adoption as part of the product strategy because Meta is trying to move beyond a male-coded gadget audience.
- She is skeptical that mainstreaming would prove organic demand if it is driven mainly by Meta's creator and promotional machinery.
