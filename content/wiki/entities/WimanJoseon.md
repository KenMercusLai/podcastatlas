## Overview

卫氏朝鲜 / Wiman Joseon is the polity founded by [[WimanJoseonFounder|卫满]] after migration from Yan. Its Han-recognized frontier role, regional expansion, later control of neighboring rulers' court access, envoy violence, retaliation, battlefield resistance, and final conquest form one long arc from delegated intermediation to imperial war.

## Current Profile

Hanji 364-2 supplies the polity's origin and the institutional dispute before the war. Wiman crosses the Pei River with refugees and followers, establishes himself at [[WangxianCity|王险城]], and receives recognition as a Han outer vassal expected to defend the frontier without blocking nearby rulers from the imperial court. His later expansion gives the dynasty inherited by [[UgeoWimanJoseon|卫右渠]] greater regional weight. Under Ugeo, the episode says that direct court access is obstructed, and Han pressure to reopen it fails.

Hanji 366-1 then supplies the war's opening sequence. After [[SheHeWesternHan|涉何]] kills a Joseon escort officer and receives a Liaodong appointment, Joseon retaliates and kills him. Joseon defeats Yang Pu's first force, holds Xun Zhi at the Pei River, and then approaches submission under renewed pressure. That off-ramp fails when the crown prince refuses a Han demand that his armed following disarm.

At Wangxian City, Wiman Joseon exploits or is protected by the split between Xun Zhi and Yang Pu, preferring Yang Pu's surrender channel while resisting Xun Zhi. Hanji 366-2 shows that after Yang Pu's arrest creates unified Han command, senior officials defect, Ugeo is killed, and remaining resistance under Seong Gi is overcome through persuasion from Ugeo's son and the son of a defector. Han then converts the conquered space into the [[HanFourCommanderies|汉四郡]].

## Key Characteristics

- Regional polity represented across immediate escalation, resistance, failed submission, siege, and conquest.
- Refugee-founded polity whose authority grows through Han recognition, coalition building, and regional expansion.
- Frontier intermediary expected to preserve neighboring rulers' direct access to the Han court.
- Actor in a retaliation cycle after a Han envoy kills one of its escort officers.
- Defender whose ruler offers submission but whose crown prince withdraws under reciprocal fear.
- Defender able to navigate competing Han military and diplomatic channels.
- Court whose senior officials divide when unified Han pressure increases.

## Qualifications

This page does not adopt Hanji 366-2's broad claim that the region was already Yan territory in the Spring and Autumn period. Hanji 364-2 adds an origin narrative but remains insufficient to settle the polity's boundaries, population, political offices, the legal meaning of outer-vassal status, or the exact geography of the Pei River and Wangxian City. Hanji 366-1's 106 BCE date and detailed submission terms remain source-scoped, while Hanji 366-2's closing Jizi passage is moral historiography rather than a direct description of this final regime.

## What Changed

- Extended the profile backward to Wiman's refugee coalition, Han recognition, regional expansion, and the inherited obligation to preserve neighboring rulers' court access.
- Reframed the She He incident as the violent end of an older access-and-hierarchy dispute rather than an isolated border clash.
