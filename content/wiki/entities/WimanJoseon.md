## Overview

卫氏朝鲜 / Wiman Joseon is the polity whose war with Western Han escalates through envoy violence and retaliation, passes through battlefield resistance and an aborted submission, and ends after the prolonged siege of [[WangxianCity|王险城]], divided Han command, elite defection, the killing of [[UgeoWimanJoseon|卫右渠]], and final internal capitulation.

## Current Profile

Hanji 366-1 supplies the war's opening sequence. After [[SheHeWesternHan|涉何]] kills a Joseon escort officer and receives a Liaodong appointment, Joseon retaliates and kills him. Joseon defeats Yang Pu's first force, holds Xun Zhi at the Pei River, and then approaches submission under renewed pressure. That off-ramp fails when the crown prince refuses a Han demand that his armed following disarm.

At Wangxian City, Wiman Joseon exploits or is protected by the split between Xun Zhi and Yang Pu, preferring Yang Pu's surrender channel while resisting Xun Zhi. Hanji 366-2 shows that after Yang Pu's arrest creates unified Han command, senior officials defect, Ugeo is killed, and remaining resistance under Seong Gi is overcome through persuasion from Ugeo's son and the son of a defector. Han then converts the conquered space into the [[HanFourCommanderies|汉四郡]].

## Key Characteristics

- Regional polity represented across immediate escalation, resistance, failed submission, siege, and conquest.
- Actor in a retaliation cycle after a Han envoy kills one of its escort officers.
- Defender whose ruler offers submission but whose crown prince withdraws under reciprocal fear.
- Defender able to navigate competing Han military and diplomatic channels.
- Court whose senior officials divide when unified Han pressure increases.

## Qualifications

This page does not adopt Hanji 366-2's broad claim that the region was already Yan territory in the Spring and Autumn period. The sources are also insufficient for a full account of Wiman Joseon's origins, boundaries, population, political offices, or the later history and exact geography of the commanderies. Hanji 366-1's 106 BCE date and detailed submission terms remain source-scoped, while Hanji 366-2's closing Jizi passage is moral historiography rather than a direct description of this final regime.

## What Changed

- Extended the profile backward from the final siege to envoy violence, retaliation, early military resistance, and an aborted submission.
