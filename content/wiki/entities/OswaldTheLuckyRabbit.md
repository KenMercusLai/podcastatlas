## Overview

Oswald the Lucky Rabbit was the successful early animated character whose loss made ownership, distributor leverage, collaborator retention, and brand control central to Walt Disney's later strategy.

## Current Profile

Both [[the-walt-disney-company-1]] and [[614-walt-disney-the-great-american-storyteller-glt5465018421]] use Oswald as the negative lesson preceding [[MickeyMouse]]. Disney had helped create a popular character, but Charles Mintz and the distributor controlled the rights and recruited away animators, leaving creative success disconnected from durable control.

The consequence was strategic as well as emotional. Walt resolved not to lose control of later characters, making [[IPOwnership]] and dependence on outside distribution recurring concerns in the company's development.

## Key Characteristics

- Early successful Disney-associated animated character.
- Character whose rights remained outside Walt Disney's control.
- Case of distributor leverage over a dependent creative studio.
- Trigger for Disney's later emphasis on ownership and brand control.

## Qualifications

The sources are Disney-centered business and cultural histories. They do not provide a complete contract history, allocate all creative credit, or independently establish the precise private motives of Mintz, the distributor, Disney, or departing animators.
