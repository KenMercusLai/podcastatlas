## Overview

宋贵人 / Song Guiren now has both the original Zhangdi-era palace case and the later He-era ritual repair. Hanji 751 shows her as [[LiuQingQingheKing|Liu Qing]]'s mother, targeted by [[EmpressDowagerDouHeLateHan|Empress Dou]] during the replacement of Liu Qing with [[EmperorHeOfHan|Liu Zhao]]. Hanji 770 then shows Liu Qing using the Liang redress opening to request seasonal sacrifices for her after [[EmpressDowagerDouHeLateHan|Dou Taihou]]'s death.

## Current Profile

Hanji 751 gives the original danger. Song Guiren is one of the Song sisters selected during [[EmpressMaMingdeEasternHan|Empress Ma]]'s period of influence, and her son Liu Qing becomes crown prince. After Empress Ma dies and Empress Dou gains Zhangdi's favor, Song's position becomes vulnerable because Liu Qing blocks the succession path of Liu Zhao, the child Empress Dou raises as her own.

The source says Empress Dou and her family monitor the Song sisters, collect small faults, and turn Song Guiren's illness-related request for fresh rabbit meat into a wugu-style accusation. [[EmperorZhangOfHan|Emperor Zhang]] accepts the charge, Liu Qing is moved out of the crown-prince residence and demoted to Qinghe king, and the Song sisters are expelled from the inner palace. [[CaiLunLateHan|Cai Lun]], then a xiaohuangmen, examines them; both drink poison, while Song Yang is removed from office and sent home.

Hanji 770 gives the delayed ritual repair. After [[LiangGuirenHeLateHan|Liang Guiren]] is posthumously recognized, Liu Qing requests grave offerings for Song Guiren. [[EmperorHeOfHan|Emperor He]] grants the request and orders the imperial kitchen to provide offerings in all four seasons. Liu Qing treats the grant as belated filial care: he could not support his mother while she was alive, but he can now sacrifice to her. He still does not ask for a shrine because that might appear to equate Song's status with Liang's higher birth-mother recognition.

Song Guiren's current significance is therefore comparative and sequential. Hanji 751 explains how her death emerges from succession politics; Hanji 770 shows a lower-status ritual repair that acknowledges wrong without granting the same title, tomb, or shrine implications as [[ImperialBirthMotherRecognition|imperial birth-mother recognition]].

## Key Characteristics

- Mother of Liu Qing, whose crown-prince status makes Song politically dangerous to Empress Dou's foster-maternal project.
- Victim of monitored palace accusation, where illness and rabbit meat are turned into a curse charge.
- Consort whose death follows Liu Qing's demotion and Cai Lun's interrogation of the Song sisters.
- Parallel victim of Dou Taihou's coercive palace politics alongside Liang Guiren.
- Figure whose posthumous recognition proceeds through Liu Qing's petition rather than imperial birth-mother elevation.
- Lower-status ritual-repair case centered on seasonal offerings rather than title, tomb, or shrine equality.

## Qualifications

This page does not establish Song Guiren's full palace biography or complete kinship network. Hanji 751 supplies the accusation and death sequence but keeps the Song sisters' exact individual identities, Song Yang's full profile, and case record details source-scoped. Hanji 770 supplies the later sacrifice grant, not a full equality of Song's status with Liang Guiren's imperial birth-mother recognition.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 751 as the original Song Guiren palace case: crown-prince motherhood, accusation, expulsion, interrogation, and death.
- Reframed Hanji 770's seasonal sacrifices as delayed ritual repair after a concrete Zhangdi-era succession purge.
- Preserved Song's distinction from Liang Guiren: both are wronged consorts, but only Liang receives imperial birth-mother elevation in the current evidence.
