## Overview

宋贵人 / Song Guiren enters the wiki as the mother of [[LiuQingQingheKing|Qinghe King Liu Qing]] and a parallel victim in the [[EmpressDowagerDouHeLateHan|Dou Taihou]] grievance field. Hanji 770 gives her a narrow but meaningful posthumous profile through Liu Qing's request for seasonal sacrifices.

## Current Profile

Hanji 770 says Song Guiren, like [[LiangGuirenHeLateHan|Liang Guiren]], had been forced to death by Dou Taihou. Unlike Liang, Song is not the emperor's hidden birth mother in this source; her posthumous recognition proceeds through her son Liu Qing's petition after the Liang case makes it safer to speak.

[[EmperorHeOfHan|Emperor He]] grants Liu Qing's request and orders the imperial kitchen to provide offerings in all four seasons. Liu Qing treats the grant as a belated form of filial care: he could not support his mother while she was alive, but he can now sacrifice to her. At the same time, he does not request a shrine because he fears it would look like equating Song with Liang's newly elevated maternal status.

Song Guiren's current significance is therefore comparative. Her page shows the lower-status counterpart to [[ImperialBirthMotherRecognition|imperial birth-mother recognition]]: the court can restore ritual care for a wronged mother without giving every case the same title, tomb, or shrine implications.

## Key Characteristics

- Mother of Qinghe King Liu Qing in Hanji 770.
- Parallel victim of Dou Taihou's coercive palace politics alongside Liang Guiren.
- Receives posthumous ritual support through her son's petition rather than direct imperial birth-mother elevation.
- Seasonal sacrifices become the key form of recognition in the current evidence.
- Source-bounded figure whose full biography and earlier palace circumstances remain outside the present wiki evidence.
