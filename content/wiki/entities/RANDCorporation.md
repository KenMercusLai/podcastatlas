# RAND Corporation

## Overview

RAND Corporation appears here as the American research institution commissioned to study Viet Cong motivation and morale when the United States lacked sufficient Vietnamese language and country expertise inside government.

## Current Profile

Through the [[VietCongMotivationAndMoraleProject]], RAND organized extended interviews with prisoners and defectors, Vietnamese-language field teams, and a Saigon process of transcription, translation, and editing. The resulting archive reached approximately 62,000 pages according to the episode and supplied an unusually detailed view of adversary experience.

The institution also became the site of a methodological and policy conflict. [[LeonGoure]] used selected morale findings to argue that bombing was working, while [[ConradKellen]] read durable commitment in the same archive. RAND's later concern that Goure had bypassed review shows an attempted institutional correction, but the episode argues that abundant evidence and a scientific identity did not prevent politically consequential disagreement.

## Key Characteristics

- Pentagon-funded research organization used to compensate for limited official Vietnam expertise.
- Builder of a large translated interview archive from prisoners and defectors.
- Institution whose common evidence base supported sharply opposed strategic conclusions.
- Policy intermediary whose analysts' findings reached military, intelligence, press, Pentagon, and White House audiences.
- Review system that intervened only after one interpretation had already gained substantial influence.

## Qualifications

This source covers one Vietnam-era project rather than RAND's full institutional history. It does not expose the complete interview corpus, contracts, review rules, internal debate, or later scholarship, and it should not imply that RAND held one unified conclusion about the war.
