# Mary-Frances O'Connor

## Overview
Mary-Frances O'Connor is a clinical psychologist, psychiatry professor, and grief researcher presented in the source as director of a grief, loss, and social stress laboratory at the University of Arizona.

## Current Profile
In [[healing-from-grief-loss-dr-mary-frances-oconnor-scim5061041393]], O'Connor translates bereavement research into an attachment-and-learning account. She distinguishes grief, the response occurring now, from grieving, the longer process by which the brain and body learn to live with physical absence. Her central position is that adaptation means integration rather than severance: a relationship with the deceased may continue internally even as expectations of contact change.

Her account joins neuroscience, clinical psychology, public health, ritual, and lived experience. She treats yearning, protest, despair, guilt, joy, bodily risk, and restored activity as intelligible parts of grieving, while resisting fixed stages, moral judgments about the correct emotional trajectory, and one-size-fits-all optimization.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames grief through attachment, learning, and continuing internal bonds.
- Distinguishes momentary grief from the changing process of grieving.
- Connects yearning to motivational circuitry without describing attachment as addiction.
- Treats bereavement as a public-health and bodily-risk issue as well as a psychological experience.
- Supports non-linear oscillation between loss and restoration rather than mandatory stages.
- Keeps clinical, suicide, medication, and proof-of-concept research boundaries explicit.

## Evidence
- Attachment and learning: [[healing-from-grief-loss-dr-mary-frances-oconnor-scim5061041393]] presents the "gone but everlasting" conflict and grieving as learning a changed world.
- Continuing bonds: [[healing-from-grief-loss-dr-mary-frances-oconnor-scim5061041393]] replaces "letting go" with integration and an evolving internal relationship.
- Brain and body: [[healing-from-grief-loss-dr-mary-frances-oconnor-scim5061041393]] connects yearning with nucleus-accumbens activity and bereavement with cardiovascular and blood-pressure risk.
- Practical care: [[healing-from-grief-loss-dr-mary-frances-oconnor-scim5061041393]] discusses social support, progressive muscle relaxation, movement, gradual re-entry, attention shifting, and professional help.
- Model boundaries: [[healing-from-grief-loss-dr-mary-frances-oconnor-scim5061041393]] rejects a prescribed five-stage order and labels the aspirin work as preliminary rather than medical guidance.

## Qualifications
This profile is bounded to one long-form interview and does not establish a complete bibliography or independent evaluation of O'Connor's research. Cardiovascular multipliers, grief prevalence, neuroimaging, prairie-vole, belief-system, and intervention findings remain source-scoped. The guest explicitly says suicide itself is outside her expertise, and the conversation is public education rather than individualized medical or psychological care.
