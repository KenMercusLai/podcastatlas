# Andrew Newton

## Overview

Andrew “Gino” Newton is the airline pilot recruited through intermediaries who killed Norman Scott's dog Rinka and failed to shoot [[NormanScott|Scott]] on Exmoor in 1975.

## Current Profile

[[65-a-very-british-scandal-glt1741780940]] presents Newton as an inexperienced and improbable assassin at the end of a chain running from Thorpe associate David Holmes through John Le Mesurier and George Deacon. Newton allegedly received an offer of £10,000, approached Scott under the pretense that he would protect him, drove him across Exmoor, killed Rinka, and then failed to kill Scott when the gun jammed.

Newton's role matters less as evidence of professional conspiracy than as proof that the danger to Scott became physical. The intermediaries increased deniability, while Newton's inexperience made the operation chaotic and left Scott alive to connect the attack to his earlier allegations.

## Key Characteristics

- Airline pilot rather than an experienced contract killer.
- Recruited through several intermediaries rather than directly in the episode's account.
- Allegedly offered £10,000, which he later said was inconsistent with mere intimidation or blackmail.
- Gained Scott's cooperation by claiming that he would protect him from a threat.
- Killed Rinka and failed to shoot Scott when the weapon jammed.

## Evidence

- Recruitment chain: [[65-a-very-british-scandal-glt1741780940]] traces contact from David Holmes through Le Mesurier and Deacon to Newton.
- Payment and purpose: [[65-a-very-british-scandal-glt1741780940]] reports the alleged £10,000 offer and Newton's later argument about what that sum implied.
- Exmoor attack: [[65-a-very-british-scandal-glt1741780940]] describes the protective pretext, drive, killing of Rinka, gun failure, and abandonment of Scott.

## Qualifications

This is a source-bounded account of Newton's place in the Thorpe scandal, not a full biography or independent reconstruction of his instructions, payment, or intent. The episode's account of the recruitment chain and financial purpose includes allegation and retrospective interpretation; Newton's actions against Rinka and Scott do not by themselves establish who authorized them.
