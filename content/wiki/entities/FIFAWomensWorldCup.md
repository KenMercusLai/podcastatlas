## Overview
The FIFA Women's World Cup appears in the Zhao Lina interview as the international tournament context where women's football, national-team identity, stadium atmosphere, and athlete self-recognition become visible.

## Current Profile
The current wiki evidence uses the 2015 Canada Women's World Cup through Zhao Lina's memory. The tournament is not analyzed as a business or governance structure here; it matters because a reserve goalkeeper could still feel seen when warming up before a large crowd. That scene turns the World Cup into an experiential threshold: elite competition, spectatorship, and national-team presence can change how an athlete understands her own relationship to the sport.

## Key Characteristics
- The tournament can make women's football visibly consequential to players even before they become starters.
- Its stadium atmosphere gives the source a concrete example of football meaning beyond the final score.
- It connects elite national-team selection to broader questions of fandom, girls' football, and public recognition.
