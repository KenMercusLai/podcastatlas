# Wangwang / 旺旺

## Overview
[[WangwangDog|Wangwang / 旺旺]] was a stray dog in [[Guangdong]] whose killing, along with three newborn puppies, became the focal case for the source's discussion of animal cruelty, public outrage, censorship-bounded memory, and cross-border animal-protection action.

## Current Profile
The source presents 旺旺 as a locally fed stray dog who was beaten, burned, filmed, and widely mourned after the footage circulated online. The public reaction turned 旺旺 from an individual animal into a symbol for [[AnimalPainLegalRecognition]], [[ChinaAnimalProtectionLawGap]], [[AnimalProtectionCodedExpression]], and [[HongKongAnimalSolidarity]]. The page records this public-memory role without independently reconstructing the case beyond the episode.

## Key Characteristics
- 旺旺 is described as a street dog cared for by local villagers before the killing.
- The case became unusually visible because the victims included newborn puppies and the alleged perpetrators were boys under 14.
- Public mourning framed 旺旺 as a mother, family-like being, and wronged life rather than as property.
- The name itself became sensitive enough that supporters used coded references and substitute images.
- Hong Kong memorials, tram and bus ads, signs, and online candle-lighting extended 旺旺's memory beyond the mainland event site.
