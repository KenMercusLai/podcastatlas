# 《守望者》 / Watchmen

## Overview

*Watchmen* is Alan Moore's revisionist superhero work, represented here as an alternative-history test of what masked heroes, absolutist moral judgment, and godlike power would mean if inserted into modern politics rather than protected by uncomplicated genre convention.

## Current Profile

The work asks what superheroes would become if they had continued into recognizable twentieth-century history. Their presence alters geopolitics and the Vietnam War, while a genuinely godlike figure turns the fantasy of intervention into a problem of scale, accountability, and human significance.

Rorschach supplies the sharpest moral boundary. One source compares his disgust with social corruption to Holden Caulfield's sensitivity, then distinguishes Holden's frightened, tender, mostly nonviolent anger from Rorschach's rigid vigilante purity. The comparison shows how refusal of compromise changes when private judgment is armed and made absolute.

The later television continuation brings the Tulsa race massacre and the Ku Klux Klan's masked vigilantism into the frame. This does not make all masked heroes equivalent to racist terror; it exposes how masks, moral certainty, violence, and claims to order can serve radically different politics.

## Key Characteristics

- Revisionist superhero narrative inserting extraordinary actors into modern historical events.
- Alternative-history test of how superhuman power changes war and politics.
- Critique of masked vigilantism and privately authorized moral judgment.
- Rorschach case linking purity, disgust, violence, and antihero reception.
- Later television afterlife connecting masks to race history and political memory.

## Qualifications

These sources use *Watchmen* selectively rather than providing a complete textual, production, adaptation, or reception history. The Holden comparison is interpretive, and the episode's brief account does not establish Alan Moore's full politics or adjudicate differences between the comic, film, and television continuation. Masked form alone does not establish moral equivalence between heroes, state actors, and racist organizations.
