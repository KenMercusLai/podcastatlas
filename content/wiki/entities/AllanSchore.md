# Allan Schore

## Overview
Allan Schore is the clinician and psychoanalytic theorist interviewed in [[how-relationships-shape-your-brain-dr-allan-schore-scim1947263719]] about early attachment, affect regulation, implicit communication, and psychotherapy.

## Current Profile
Across the bounded sources, Schore's work presents attachment as a psychobiological regulation process rather than only a conscious belief or behavioral style. He links infant-caregiver face, voice, gesture, arousal, synchrony, and repair to the later balance between self-regulation and regulation with other people. The solo bonding episode emphasizes mutual autonomic coordination and shared predictable experience, while Schore's later direct interview supplies the fuller rupture-repair and psychotherapy account.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames attachment as affect communication and affect regulation.
- Emphasizes early nonverbal, implicit, and bodily interaction.
- Connects autonomic coordination and predictable shared experience to later adult bonding.
- Distinguishes self-regulation from interactive regulation and treats security as capacity for both.
- Treats repair after misattunement as central to good-enough caregiving and therapy.
- Gives the right hemisphere a broad role in emotion, context, unconscious processing, and relational synchrony.
- Extends his framework into adult intimacy, music, pets, creativity, and attention.

## Evidence
- Attachment framework: [[how-relationships-shape-your-brain-dr-allan-schore-scim1947263719]] has Schore define attachment through affect communication and regulation and compare secure, avoidant, anxious, and disorganized patterns.
- Repair and clinical mechanism: [[how-relationships-shape-your-brain-dr-allan-schore-scim1947263719]] emphasizes re-attunement after mismatch and describes therapy as emotionally meaningful right-brain-to-right-brain regulation.
- Wider application: [[how-relationships-shape-your-brain-dr-allan-schore-scim1947263719]] applies the framework to adult relationships, music, companion animals, creativity, texting, and early-caregiving policy.
- Earlier synthesis: [[science-of-social-bonding-in-family-friendship-romantic-love-scim2455538543]] summarizes Schore's framework through infant-caregiver autonomic coordination, shared narrative, and reuse of early systems in adult attachment.

## Qualifications
This profile is bounded to two public podcast summaries rather than Schore's full scholarly corpus or clinical record. Its strong hemispheric, developmental, fetal-stress, autonomic-synchrony, neuroanatomical, and psychotherapy-mechanism claims remain source-scoped and should not be treated as diagnosis, causal proof, or individualized treatment guidance.
