# Io / 伊娥

## Overview

Io is the suffering mortal wanderer in [[PrometheusBound|《被缚的普罗米修斯》]] whose demand to know her future turns prophecy into a question of truth, care, and autonomy.

## Current Profile

The episode presents Io as harmed by [[Zeus]]'s desire, transformed into a cow, driven by a gadfly, and forced into wandering. She mirrors the fixed Prometheus: he suffers while unable to move, while she suffers because she cannot stop moving.

Prometheus initially judges ignorance kinder because her future contains prolonged pain. Io rejects care she did not request and insists on disclosure. The prophecy then has a double effect: it adds knowledge of suffering but also reveals an endpoint in Egypt and a descendant, Heracles, who will free Prometheus.

## Key Characteristics

- Mortal sufferer subjected to divine coercion and involuntary wandering.
- Dramatic counterpart to the immobilized Prometheus.
- Claimant of the authority to know painful information about her own life.
- Example of knowledge worsening present pain while also restoring bounded hope.

- Informational autonomy: [[370-jishu-geile-renlei-mangmu-de-xiwang-ma-beifude-puluomixiusi-lveku27utltloytqi8ycq-nvurhd]] has Io reject Prometheus's unsolicited protective silence.
- Future endpoint: [[370-jishu-geile-renlei-mangmu-de-xiwang-ma-beifude-puluomixiusi-lveku27utltloytqi8ycq-nvurhd]] says the prophecy gives her suffering a terminus and connects her line to Prometheus's release.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to the episode's reading of *Prometheus Bound* and does not synthesize every ancient variant of Io's myth. The medical-ethics analogy concerns a shared autonomy problem, not an equivalence between prophecy and clinical prognosis.

## What Changed

- Established Io as a truth-and-autonomy figure rather than only a mythic wanderer.
- Connected painful disclosure to the possibility of renewed hope.
