# Terry Sejnowski

## Overview
Terry Sejnowski is presented as a computational neuroscientist at the [[SalkInstitute]] who connects biological learning systems, computational models, and artificial intelligence.

## Current Profile
In [[how-to-improve-at-learning-using-neuroscience-ai-dr-terry-sejnowski-scim2892046069]], Sejnowski uses an [[AlgorithmicLevelOfBrainExplanation]] to bridge neurons and behavior. His account joins basal-ganglia learning, prediction errors, cognitive and procedural practice, sleep-dependent consolidation, and AI as a hypothesis-generating partner rather than an autonomous authority.

The profile is both scientific and educational. He argues that expertise requires repeated active practice, that sleep and low-input periods participate in learning, and that tools do not eliminate the need for foundations. His AI stance is exploratory but bounded: models can search, compare, predict, and generate ideas, while experts remain responsible for evaluation and action.

## Key Characteristics
- Computational neuroscientist linking biological mechanisms to algorithms and behavior.
- Treats cognitive instruction and procedural practice as complementary learning systems.
- Connects dopamine-linked prediction errors to value updating and action learning.
- Presents sleep, exercise, and problem incubation as parts of durable cognitive performance.
- Frames AI as a partner for research and medicine rather than a replacement for expertise.
