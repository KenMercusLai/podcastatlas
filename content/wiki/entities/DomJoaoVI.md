# Dom Joao VI

## Overview

Dom Joao VI appears in the episode as the Portuguese ruler whose flight to Rio during the Napoleonic crisis made [[Brazil]] a center of imperial government before independence.

## Current Profile

The episode treats Dom Joao VI as a precondition for Brazil's unusual independence path. Napoleon's pressure on [[Portugal]] pushed the royal family and court across the Atlantic in 1807, creating an empire governed from a colony. Joao's later decision to leave [[DomPedroI]] as regent helped make Brazilian separation dynastically continuous rather than purely anti-monarchical.

## Key Characteristics

- Portuguese monarch whose court moved to Rio in 1807 under Napoleonic pressure.
- Elevated Brazil's status inside the United Kingdom of Portugal, Brazil and the Algarves.
- Left Pedro as regent when he returned to Portugal.
- Advised that Pedro should lead Brazil if separation became unavoidable.
- Makes Brazilian independence look like a dynastic branch as well as an anti-colonial break.

## Evidence

- Court relocation: [[681-brazil-the-emperors-anthem-part-5-glt5596929385]] says around 15,000 members of the Portuguese royal family, court, and elite sailed to Brazil in 1807.
- Status elevation: [[681-brazil-the-emperors-anthem-part-5-glt5596929385]] says Joao remained in Rio after Napoleon's defeat and elevated Brazil to equal status with Portugal.
- Regency setup: [[681-brazil-the-emperors-anthem-part-5-glt5596929385]] says Joao left Pedro in Brazil and framed Pedro-led separation as preferable if a break came.
