# Immanuel Kant / 康德

## Overview

Immanuel Kant appears across the wiki as a reference for responsible moral agency in punishment, truth-priority judgment, active Enlightenment reason, and an early attempt to explain the [[LisbonEarthquake1755|Lisbon earthquake]] through natural causes.

## Current Profile

The legal source presents Kantian retribution as a dignity claim: punishment addresses a responsible agent and should not reduce a person to an instrument of deterrence. The Arendt source uses Kant as a truth-priority reference when consequences tempt silence, while the algorithm source invokes his call to use one's own reason against passive dependence on rankings and recommendations.

The natural-inquiry branch concerns Lisbon. Kant collects reports, publishes three articles, and proposes a physical explanation that the episode says is scientifically outdated. His significance in this bounded account lies less in the correctness of the theory than in trying to replace providential certainty with evidence-guided causal explanation.

## Key Characteristics

- Retributivist reference joining punishment to responsibility, dignity, and proportionality.
- Truth-priority reference for judgment that is not wholly controlled by consequences.
- Enlightenment advocate of active reason rather than outsourced judgment.
- Early natural investigator of the Lisbon earthquake whose theory did not survive later science.

## Qualifications

All four sources use Kant selectively rather than reconstructing his complete philosophy. The punishment and truth links are compressed interpretive references; the algorithm use is analogical; and the earthquake episode does not specify the complete texts, evidence base, or chronology of Kant's theory. “Early scientific” should not be read as “correct by modern seismology.”
