# 汪一鸣 / Wang Yiming (reproductive-medicine doctor)

## Overview
汪一鸣 is presented in VOL.77, VOL.78, and VOL.84 of [[ZheBingShuoLaiHuaChang|这病说来话长]] as a Tongji Hospital reproductive-medicine andrology attending physician discussing male fertility, sexual function, genital health, sex education, consent, and clinical help-seeking.

## Current Profile
The episodes position Wang as a clinical voice arguing that many adult anxieties and apparent sexual-health problems are intensified by missing or inaccurate education. VOL.77 separates sexual function, fertility, reproductive hormones, prostate health, and psychological state while showing how shame, online advertising, and self-labeling can worsen distress. He connects prevention with care navigation: people should understand bodies, boundaries, and contraception before crisis, then seek qualified assessment for persistent symptoms rather than infer diagnosis from one experience or finding.

He also broadens clinical discussion across the life course. VOL.77 links adult masturbation guilt to questions that should have been answered during adolescence and recounts beginning school sex-education work in 2014. VOL.84 protects children's refusal rights and makes older and disabled people's sexual needs visible without voyeurism, shame, or automatic denial of agency. VOL.78 adds an anatomy- and symptom-sensitive approach to foreskin care: he distinguishes retractable long foreskin, phimosis, and urgent paraphimosis; resists universal circumcision; and emphasizes pediatric development, diabetes, anesthesia, and the harm of excessive tissue removal.

## Key Characteristics
- Treats sex education as preventive health literacy rather than only biological instruction.
- Connects consent and harassment prevention with responsibility placed on potential perpetrators and institutions as well as individuals.
- Explains sexual pain and infection testing through cause-specific assessment rather than embarrassment or self-diagnosis.
- Directs unintended-pregnancy care toward qualified institutions and warns about misleading low-price advertising.
- Includes children, men, women, older adults, and disabled people in sexual-health discussion.
- Distinguishes routine foreskin variation, chronic treatment indications, and an acute circulation-threatening emergency.
- Separates sexual performance from fertility and interprets masturbation through function, distress, symptoms, and shame rather than a universal frequency rule.
