# Wine / 葡萄酒

## Overview

Wine / 葡萄酒 is an agricultural drink, sensory classification system, cultural archive, commercial category, traded and ritual object, technology-dependent product, and prestige market.

## Current Profile

Across the bounded sources, wine begins with grapes, site, weather, farming, fermentation, storage, and human choice, but never remains only agricultural. Repeated comparison makes some sensory patterns learnable; stories, memories, and preference shape use; distribution and education teach unfamiliar regional value; and institutions turn origin, estate, method, and price into market authority.

The historical branch follows wine through early archaeology, Mediterranean trade, Greek and Roman drinking cultures, Christian ritual, Islamic legal-poetic ambivalence, English bottle technology, French classification, and the [[JudgmentOfParisWine|Judgment of Paris]]. The competitive branch clarifies that blind tasting can suppress labels and prestige while still depending on sample choice, scoring, typicity, learned priors, and team inference. The [[Champagne|Champagne]] case joins bottle fermentation, aging, blending, appellation rules, coordinated scarcity, climate adaptation, and cultural localization. Its Chinese branch adds a development problem: favorable regions require technical continuity, credible origin governance, consumer demand, accumulated expertise, and locally intelligible communication before potential becomes durable strength.

## Key Characteristics

- Wine is a farmed, fermented, place-bound product before it becomes a luxury sign or scored category.
- A bottle can archive agriculture, technology, trade, ritual, family, market change, and sensory memory.
- Comparative tasting is trainable, but competition, criticism, and personal enjoyment use different criteria.
- Wine value is taught through vocabulary, trusted channels, producer stories, classification, and market proof.
- Regions change because climate, technique, demand, regulation, and human succession are not fixed.
- Industry maturity depends on continuity of rules, skills, demand, and institutions as well as vineyard potential.
- Protected-origin wine can use collective governance to stabilize quality and scarcity while still creating entry, price, and status barriers.

## Qualifications

The sources are sensory-cultural and practitioner conversations, a founder-advice case, and a broad narrative history. Together they support a layered map, not a complete viticulture, law, market, climate, or quality history. Blind tasting reduces some reputation cues but does not establish perfect objectivity. Competition records, talent estimates, legal and institutional detail, regional quality judgments, market shares, and vintage assessments remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Added competitive blind tasting as controlled classification, event strategy, and team decision-making.
- Distinguished competition typicity from critic evaluation and personal enjoyment.
- Added Chinese industry continuity, regulation, consumer-demand, and regional-development constraints.
- Added Champagne as a protected-origin, bottle-aged, climate-sensitive, and culturally localized wine system.
