# National Science Foundation

## Overview
The National Science Foundation appears in the All-In Kratsios interview as the federal science-funding agency most directly associated with the episode's grant-reform discussion. [[MichaelKratsios|Michael Kratsios]] uses NSF examples to discuss DEI-related grant controversy, peer review, meta-science, reviewer "golden tickets," flexible grant length, and portable graduate fellowships.

## Current Profile
NSF's wiki role in this source is two-sided. It is treated as part of U.S. research capacity, but also as an agency whose incentives can become too consensus-oriented, too political, or too administratively fixed unless funding experiments test alternative selection methods.

## Key Characteristics
- NSF is central to the episode's dispute over whether federal science funding is being cut, redirected, or depoliticized.
- Kratsios cites a source-scoped Senate analysis claiming a large share of recent NSF grants were DEI-related and argues that required DEI factors politicize science.
- The agency is presented as a test bed for meta-science units and reviewer golden-ticket grant selection.
- NSF graduate fellowships are used as the episode's clearest example of funding scientists rather than institutions because recipients can choose where to study.
- NSF is part of the broader U.S. research-capacity system that the episode compares against China's more top-down model.

## Qualifications
The DEI grant-share claim is preserved as a source-scoped claim from the interview, not as an independently verified NSF audit. The page also does not evaluate whether golden tickets or other funding experiments will improve scientific output.
