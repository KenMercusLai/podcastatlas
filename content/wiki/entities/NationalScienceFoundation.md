# National Science Foundation

## Overview
The National Science Foundation appears in the All-In science-policy corpus as a federal science-funding agency tied to grant reform, peer review, and research-risk tolerance. [[MichaelKratsios|Michael Kratsios]] uses NSF examples to discuss DEI-related grant controversy, meta-science, reviewer "golden tickets," flexible grant length, and portable graduate fellowships. [[EricWeinstein|Eric Weinstein]] later uses NSF-adjacent grant culture as part of his critique that American science funds too much low-variance work.

## Current Profile
NSF's wiki role is two-sided. It is treated as part of U.S. research capacity, but also as an agency whose incentives can become too consensus-oriented, too political, or too administratively fixed unless funding experiments test alternative selection methods and protect serious high-upside work.

## Key Characteristics
- NSF is central to the episode's dispute over whether federal science funding is being cut, redirected, or depoliticized.
- Kratsios cites a source-scoped Senate analysis claiming a large share of recent NSF grants were DEI-related and argues that required DEI factors politicize science.
- The agency is presented as a test bed for meta-science units and reviewer golden-ticket grant selection.
- NSF graduate fellowships are used as the episode's clearest example of funding scientists rather than institutions because recipients can choose where to study.
- NSF is part of the broader U.S. research-capacity system that the episode compares against China's more top-down model.
- Weinstein's interview adds a stronger critique of grant culture: proposals tied to predictable outcomes can select modest work and discourage heterodox science.

## Qualifications
The DEI grant-share claim is preserved as a source-scoped claim from the Kratsios interview, not as an independently verified NSF audit. The page also does not evaluate whether golden tickets, high-beta funding, or other funding experiments will improve scientific output.

## What Changed

- Broadened NSF from Kratsios's grant-reform test bed into part of the wider science-risk-tolerance debate.
