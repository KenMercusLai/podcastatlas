# Newgate Prison

## Overview

Newgate Prison was a long-lived London prison beside the Old Bailey whose location joined confinement, trial, punishment, public execution, religion, and urban memory.

## Current Profile

The episode places the prison at the Roman and medieval western edge of the city, where a gate, court, church, tavern, execution site, and later drinking fountain condensed successive forms of authority and reform. A prison stood there from the late twelfth century until 1902; after executions moved from Tyburn in 1783, the district also became a major site of public hanging.

St Sepulchre's execution bell and repentance verses framed punishment religiously, while reported surviving cells and literary visitors sustain the site's memory after demolition. The adjacent Victorian drinking fountain adds a different moral program: public provision and temperance beside a landscape formerly organized around exemplary death.

## Key Characteristics

- Prison associated with the site of a Roman gate and the later Old Bailey.
- Spatial link between arrest, confinement, trial, execution, and religious preparation for death.
- Major public-execution setting after the move from Tyburn.
- Demolished institution whose memory survives through neighboring buildings, reported cells, names, and stories.
- Urban site where punitive spectacle and Victorian public-service reform became juxtaposed.

- Reform juxtaposition: [[212-haunted-london-part-4-glt8938796735]] places an early Metropolitan public drinking fountain and temperance symbolism beside the former gallows landscape.

## Qualifications

This is a place-memory profile based on one walking-tour episode, not a complete institutional history of the prison, English criminal law, incarceration conditions, or execution practice. Claims about preserved cells, exact site boundaries, primacy of the fountain, literary attendance, and local anecdotes remain source-scoped.
