# 凌统 / Ling Tong

## Overview
凌统 / Ling Tong is an Eastern Wu officer whose profile joins frontline courage at Jiangxia, a private blood feud against [[GanNing|甘宁]], and later rear-guard trust inside the command structure that relieves Gan Ning at Yiling.

## Current Profile
Hanji 1085 places Ling Tong beside [[DongXiLateHan|董袭]] in the small armored assault against [[HuangZu|黄祖]]'s anchored warships at [[MianKouLateHan|沔口]]. The operation breaks the river barrier and permits the wider attack on Xiakou.

Hanji 1086 then reveals the unresolved personal cost inside Wu. Gan Ning had killed Ling Tong's father [[LingCaoLateHan|凌操]] while defending Huang Zu, so [[SunQuan|孙权]] forbids revenge and separates the officers rather than claiming reconciliation. Hanji 1109 later gives Ling Tong a rear-guard role: [[LyuMeng|吕蒙]] estimates that he can hold the existing position for ten days, enabling a temporary force concentration to relieve Gan Ning.

## Key Characteristics
- Leads a small armored assault party in the Mian Kou barrier breach.
- Carries a filial revenge claim against the officer who killed his father.
- Accepts ruler-imposed limits and separation instead of prosecuting the feud.
- Later holds a defensive assignment while senior commanders shift force to relieve Gan Ning.
- Remains trusted operational capacity despite unresolved private history.

## Qualifications
The sources give no full biography or independent account of Ling Tong's exact conduct during either the Mian Kou assault or the ten-day rear-guard assignment. Assault-party size and armor, repeated revenge attempts, and the precise separation arrangement remain source-scoped. Later operational coordination does not prove personal reconciliation with Gan Ning.

## What Changed
- Added Ling Tong's frontline role in breaking the Mian Kou barrier.
- Integrated battlefield courage, private feud, and later rear-guard trust into one current profile.
- Preserved the distinction between command cooperation and personal reconciliation.
