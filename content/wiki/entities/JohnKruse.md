# John Kruse

## Overview
John Kruse is the psychiatrist and ADHD specialist interviewed in [[improve-focus-with-behavioral-tools-medication-for-adhd-dr-john-kruse-scim4112569463]] about diagnosis, attention control, behavioral structure, circadian timing, and medication selection across children and adults.

## Current Profile
Kruse presents ADHD as a biologically influenced neurodevelopmental condition whose expression also changes with environment and structure. His clinical frame combines developmental and cross-setting diagnosis with practical support for sleep, meals, movement, scheduling, distraction control, and individualized medication decisions.

He resists both trivialization and one-protocol treatment. Ordinary distractibility does not establish ADHD, but diagnosed ADHD can create serious educational, relationship, substance-use, accident, and mortality risk. Likewise, stimulant efficacy does not remove the need to assess psychiatric history, cardiovascular context, patient preference, side effects, and alternatives.

## Key Characteristics
- Practices psychiatry with a stated specialization in ADHD across children and adults.
- Defines ADHD primarily through impaired control over attention and executive function rather than absence of attention.
- Integrates biological contribution with environmental structure, sleep timing, meals, movement, and family or work context.
- Uses shared risk-benefit discussion rather than one rigid medication sequence.
- Keeps emerging or mixed evidence around supplements, neurofeedback, games, microbiome interventions, and substance use qualified.
