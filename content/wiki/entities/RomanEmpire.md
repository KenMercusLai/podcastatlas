## Overview

The Roman Empire / 罗马帝国 now appears in the wiki from two directions: internally, as the imperial order emerging after the [[RomanRepublic|Roman Republic]] crisis and [[CaesarAssassination|Caesar's assassination]]; externally, as [[DaQin|Da Qin]], the far-western target of [[GanYingEasternHan|Gan Ying]]'s Eastern Han mission.

## Current Profile

The Caesar source presents the Roman Empire as the institutional outcome that the assassination of [[JuliusCaesar|Julius Caesar]] failed to prevent. The sequence runs from Caesar's death through popular backlash, the rise of [[OctavianAugustus|Octavian / Augustus]], conflict with [[MarkAntony|Mark Antony]] and [[CleopatraVII|Cleopatra VII]], and the emergence of imperial rule. The key irony is that killing the figure feared as a would-be king did not restore republican government; monarchy-like authority survived by taking a less openly royal form.

Hanji 771 adds the outside-distance layer. The episode identifies Da Qin with the Roman Empire and makes it the destination of Gan Ying's mission under [[BanChaoEasternHan|Ban Chao]]. Gan Ying does not arrive. He reaches [[ParthianEmpire|Parthia / Anxi]]'s western boundary, hears that the sea route is long and dangerous, and returns to report mediated information to [[EmperorHeOfHan|Emperor He of Han]]. The same source notes that a later Roman/Da Qin envoy reaches Luoyang under the emperor the host identifies as Marcus Aurelius, but that later contact remains source-scoped here.

## Key Characteristics

- Imperial order that emerges after the late Roman Republic's institutional crisis.
- Political aftermath showing that tyrannicide can remove a ruler without restoring the institutions that constrained one-person rule.
- Far-western polity identified by Hanji 771 with Da Qin in Eastern Han geographical knowledge.
- Target of Gan Ying's mission but not directly reached in the current Han source.
- Known to Emperor He's court through Parthian-mediated route and description before the later source-scoped envoy notice.
- Bridges the wiki's Roman political-history branch with its Eastern Han Western Regions and route-knowledge branch.

## Qualifications

This page is not a complete Roman Empire history. The Caesar source covers the transition from republic to empire, while Hanji 771 covers Han-side naming and mediated contact. The page treats Da Qin as the source's Roman Empire identification without using it to settle every historical-geography debate about the term or Gan Ying's route.
