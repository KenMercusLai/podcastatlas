# Jacques Necker

## Overview

Jacques Necker was Louis XVI's popular Swiss Protestant finance minister whose dismissal on 11 July 1789 helped trigger the Paris mobilization that culminated in the [[StormingOfTheBastille|storming of the Bastille]].

## Current Profile

The source presents Necker less through a complete account of his financial policy than through his political meaning in crisis. Amid debt, hunger, high bread prices, and troop concentration, [[LouisXVI|Louis XVI]]'s dismissal of a minister associated with reform was read as evidence that the court was preparing to move against the National Assembly and the people. Necker's departure for Brussels and pledge to honor personal security for grain shipments reinforced his image as a public-minded counterweight to court reaction. His wax likeness then became part of the first street procession mobilized by [[CamilleDesmoulins|Camille Desmoulins]].

## Key Characteristics

- Swiss Protestant finance minister serving the Bourbon monarchy.
- Popular public figure associated with reform and financial credibility.
- Dismissal interpreted as a political attack rather than a routine ministerial change.
- Personal financial support for grain shipments used as evidence of public commitment.
- Image carried through Paris during the mobilization preceding the Bastille.

## Qualifications

- The source does not provide a complete assessment of Necker's fiscal program, responsibility for the debt crisis, or later career.
- Popularity in Paris does not by itself establish the effectiveness of his policies.
- The court's private purpose in dismissing him and the crowd's many motives remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Establishes Necker's dismissal as the immediate political signal that converted structural crisis into mass mobilization.

## Relationships

- [[LouisXVI]] - king who dismissed him during the July crisis.
- [[CamilleDesmoulins]] - speaker who used news of his removal to call the crowd to arms.
- [[StormingOfTheBastille]] - event produced partly by the panic following his dismissal.
