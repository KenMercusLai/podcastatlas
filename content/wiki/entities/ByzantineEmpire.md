# Byzantine Empire / 拜占庭帝国

## Overview

The Byzantine Empire appears in the wiki as the continuing eastern Roman state: a reconquering late-antique power, a tenth-century diplomatic and cultural standard, and an eleventh-century military-court system able to project resources far beyond its borders.

## Current Profile

In North Africa, Justinian's reconquest replaced the [[VandalKingdom]] and made [[Ceuta]] a remote western outpost. In the tenth century, recovery under rulers including [[NikephorosIIPhokas]] restored military confidence, while the court's insistence on being Roman made western titulature and marriage negotiations matters of sovereignty. [[Theophano]]'s 972 marriage then carried Byzantine dynastic prestige, ceremony, and political culture into the Saxon court without ending competition over Roman legitimacy.

The eleventh-century Harald branch shows another mode of influence. Imperial fleets and armies campaigned across multiple theaters; the [[VarangianGuard]] incorporated foreign warriors; and court rank, pay, and treasure could become political capital elsewhere. Dynastic legitimacy, urban revolt, monasteries, military allegiance, and ritual status all shaped succession at Constantinople.

## Key Characteristics

- Eastern Roman state whose identity, titles, and diplomatic standing survived ancient territorial fragmentation.
- Reconquering and frontier-holding power with reach from North Africa to the eastern Mediterranean.
- Military system able to operate across theaters and incorporate foreign specialists.
- Court hierarchy that converted service, marriage, ceremony, rank, and wealth into portable prestige.
- Dynastic and urban political order in which palace intrigue, popular legitimacy, military allegiance, monasteries, and ritual could decide succession.
- Cultural center whose practices and imperial ideas influenced courts outside its territory.

## Qualifications

The current profile is episodic rather than comprehensive: the sources cover Ceuta from late antiquity into Islamic conquest, tenth-century Ottonian diplomacy, and Harald's eleventh-century service. Theophano's personal influence, Nikephoros's characterization, and Otto III's intellectual debt remain source-scoped; Harald's itinerary, promotions, treasure, court involvement, imprisonment, and escape depend on evidence of unequal reliability.

## What Changed

- Added tenth-century military recovery and the empire's continuing Roman self-identification.
- Added Theophano's marriage as evidence for diplomatic and cultural projection into the western court.
- Connected contested titulature, dynastic succession, court culture, and military service as different expressions of imperial power.
