# Tobias Furneaux

## Overview

Tobias Furneaux was the captain of Adventure on [[JamesCook]]'s second Pacific voyage and is presented as an example of restraint after extreme expeditionary violence.

## Current Profile

The episode's bounded profile centers on Furneaux's response after ten Adventure men were killed and consumed by Māori near Queen Charlotte Sound in December 1773. He rejected demands for retaliation on the ground that revenge would not restore the dead and returned without attacking Māori communities.

The decision matters because it prevents the episode's account of Enlightenment restraint from depending on Cook alone. It shows that at least one other British naval commander treated punitive revenge as a choice rather than an automatic obligation, while the source provides little evidence about Furneaux's broader career or conduct.

## Key Characteristics

- Royal Navy captain commanding Adventure during Cook's second voyage.
- Expedition leader operating separately from Cook after the ships divided.
- Commander confronted with the killing of ten members of his ship's company.
- Decision-maker who rejected collective retaliation as unable to restore the dead.
- Narrowly evidenced example of restraint rather than a comprehensive moral profile.

## Evidence

- Command role: [[725-the-death-of-captain-cook-at-worlds-end-part-1-glt6692579488]] identifies Furneaux as Adventure's captain on the second voyage.
- Violent loss: [[725-the-death-of-captain-cook-at-worlds-end-part-1-glt6692579488]] reports that ten Adventure men were killed and consumed near Queen Charlotte Sound.
- Retaliation decision: [[725-the-death-of-captain-cook-at-worlds-end-part-1-glt6692579488]] says Furneaux rejected revenge because it would not restore the dead and returned without attacking Māori communities.

## Qualifications

This one-source profile isolates a single decision from a popular-history episode. It does not establish every circumstance of the killings, Māori perspectives, Furneaux's motives, the full crew debate, or whether restraint characterized his wider command.

## What Changed
