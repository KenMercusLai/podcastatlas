# John Knox

## Overview

John Knox is the Scottish Protestant reformer whose preaching helped turn noble opposition to [[MaryOfGuise|Mary of Guise]] into revolution and whose later confrontation with [[MaryQueenOfScots|Mary, Queen of Scots]] made religious resistance a direct challenge to royal authority.

## Current Profile

Knox's revolutionary role grew from conversion, persecution, and exile. The episode traces him from Catholic priesthood through the St Andrews Protestant stronghold, French galley captivity, Edwardian England, and Calvin's Geneva. His 1558 attack on female rule targeted Catholic queens, but it also made him unwelcome to Elizabeth's government.

Returning to Scotland in May 1559, Knox preached within a movement already protected by Protestant nobles. His sermons at Perth and St Andrews helped turn elite organization into iconoclastic action against Catholic institutions, while anti-French resistance and English support widened the revolt. The episode treats him as a catalytic voice rather than the sole cause of the Scottish Reformation.

Knox later became an advocate of conditional obedience. In his September 1561 meeting with Mary, he argued that princes who exceeded their proper bounds could be resisted, restrained, and even imprisoned because obedience to God outranked obedience to a monarch. Mary immediately recognized the constitutional implication: subjects following Knox's religious judgment could make their sovereign answerable to them.

Knox also attacked the French entertainment, dancing, and court culture surrounding Mary. These objections joined confessional hostility to suspicion of a foreign-influenced private world at Holyrood. The episode nevertheless treats his challenge as larger than personal austerity: Scottish Protestants understood their country as a community with a providential religious purpose, giving the reformed order confidence against a Catholic queen.

## Key Characteristics

- Protestant reformer who subordinated political obedience to obedience to God.
- Exile and former galley prisoner shaped by English and Genevan Protestant networks.
- Preacher whose return helped convert noble organization into revolutionary public action.
- Public critic of Mary's Catholic worship and French court culture.
- Advocate of resistance when a ruler violated religious bounds.
- Representative of a confident Scottish Protestant order rather than an isolated preacher.
- Source whose own Reformation history is vivid but interested testimony.

## Qualifications

This profile is grounded in two narrative podcast episodes, which rely partly on Knox's own partisan history and modern historians' interpretations. The precise causal relationship between sermons and iconoclasm, his conversion path, exact dialogue, private motives, the scope of his political theory, and the claim that the Scottish Reformation was Europe's first modern revolution remain source-scoped. His criticism of court culture should not be reduced to personal dislike when the episodes frame it within a wider confessional struggle.

## What Changed

- Extended Knox's profile backward from confrontation with Mary to exile, international Reformation formation, and the 1559 revolt.
- Distinguished his catalytic preaching from the noble, anti-French, and English forces also driving revolution.
