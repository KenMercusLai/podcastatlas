# Wahhabism / 瓦哈比主义

## Overview

Wahhabism is the conservative Sunni reform movement presented in the current source as a historic partner of the Saudi monarchy and a major institutional target of [[SaudiVision2030]].

## Current Profile

The episode describes a long exchange in which Saudi rulers supplied political protection and Wahhabi institutions supplied religious legitimacy. [[MohammedBinSalman|MBS]] is portrayed as changing that settlement by limiting religious-police power and reducing clerical control of public life, moving religion from a co-governing force toward a subordinated state function.

The source explicitly warns against equating Wahhabism automatically with terrorism or every form of violent extremism. It later claims some accommodation with previously displaced clerics as Saudi economic pressure and regional conflict grew, but that reported reversal remains source-scoped.

## Key Characteristics

- Eighteenth-century Sunni reform movement emphasizing strict monotheism and early Islamic practice in the source account.
- Historic provider of religious legitimacy to the Saudi monarchy.
- Formerly associated with strong institutional influence over public morality and social order.
- Constrained by MBS through reduced religious-police and clerical authority.
- Not treated by the source as synonymous with terrorism or all Islamist militancy.

- Institutional change: [[304-shate-alabo-guoqu-shinian-zai-jinxing-yichang-zenyang-de-zhengzhi-gaige-lsm0nzvie2-h_sgbmhavg7xbv74t]] says MBS reduced religious-police power and clerical intervention in public life.
- Boundary: [[304-shate-alabo-guoqu-shinian-zai-jinxing-yichang-zenyang-de-zhengzhi-gaige-lsm0nzvie2-h_sgbmhavg7xbv74t]] explicitly distinguishes Wahhabi influence on some radical groups from direct identity with terrorist organizations.

## Qualifications

The source supplies a compressed political history, not a full theological or institutional account. The movement’s internal diversity, global transmission, and the reported return of particular clerics are not independently documented here.

## What Changed

- Added Wahhabism as a historical pillar of Saudi rule and a target of current centralization.
- Preserved the source’s distinction between religious conservatism and automatic terrorist identity.

## Relationships

- [[SaudiArabia]] - state whose monarchy historically shares legitimacy with the movement in the source account.
- [[MohammedBinSalman]] - ruler described as subordinating its institutional power.
- [[SaudiVision2030]] - reform program that changes the religion-state settlement.
- [[AuthoritarianModernization]] - context in which social liberalization can coexist with political closure.
