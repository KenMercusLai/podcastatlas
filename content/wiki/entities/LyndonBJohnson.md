# Lyndon B. Johnson

## Overview

Lyndon B. Johnson appears across the wiki as a president whose use of power touches private wealth, pressure on independent institutions, Vietnam policy, peace diplomacy, and the succession crisis inside the Democratic Party in 1968.

## Current Profile

Johnson's 1968 role is internally divided. [[EugeneMcCarthy|Eugene McCarthy]]'s New Hampshire challenge and [[RobertFKennedy|Robert F. Kennedy]]'s entry preceded his withdrawal from the nomination race, while mutual hostility between Johnson and Kennedy made opposition to Vietnam inseparable from personal succession conflict. His administration's war policy burdens [[HubertHumphrey]], yet Johnson is later ambivalent between Humphrey and [[RichardNixon]], whom he may respect more and who promises not to attack his policy. The October 31 bombing halt creates a possible late advantage for Humphrey, while Johnson's suspicion of the Nixon-Chennault backchannel turns peace diplomacy into an electoral controversy.

The broader profile also contains two institutional caution cases. The Johnson-Martin confrontation shows a Federal Reserve chair resisting intense presidential pressure. The KTBC business story is used as a historical comparison for political access and office-adjacent private wealth.

## Key Characteristics

- Incumbent president whose Vietnam policy constrained the 1968 Democratic nominee.
- Withdrew from the nomination contest after McCarthy's challenge and Kennedy's entry transformed the succession fight.
- Maintained an intensely hostile relationship with Kennedy that complicated interpretations of policy disagreement.
- Pursued a late bombing halt and Paris talks while doubting South Vietnamese cooperation.
- Suspected Nixon's campaign of encouraging South Vietnam not to join the talks.
- Pressured Federal Reserve chair William McChesney Martin but did not reverse the rate decision.
- Serves as a historical case for political influence intersecting with family media wealth.

## Qualifications

The 1968 sources do not establish that Kennedy's opposition to Johnson was merely personal, that Johnson withdrew for one cause, that the bombing halt was purely electoral, or that the Chennault channel caused South Vietnam's refusal. The KTBC and Federal Reserve episodes use Johnson as a comparative case and do not supply a complete presidency, regulatory record, or family financial audit.
