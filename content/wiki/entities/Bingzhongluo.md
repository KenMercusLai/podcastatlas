# Bingzhongluo / 丙中洛

## Overview
Bingzhongluo / 丙中洛 is the Nujiang Gorge destination described in [[245-nujiang-chunri-mijing-chutan-8221838951-646453]] as a "place where humans and gods live together."

## Current Profile
The episode presents Bingzhongluo as the trip's concentrated village-and-sacred-landscape node. Its appeal comes from terraced fields, fast-changing clouds, nearby mountains, the Nujiang First Bend viewpoint, Puhua Temple's Tibetan Buddhist ritual practice, Chongding Catholic Church, and a local memory of multiple religions and ethnic groups sharing the same valley.

## Key Characteristics
- Combines scenic village landscape with sacred-mountain and river viewpoints.
- Shows fast weather shifts that change what can be seen in minutes.
- Hosts Tibetan Buddhist, Catholic, and Protestant religious traces within a small area.
- Carries both local reverence and visitor-facing tourism language.
- Works as a key emotional transition from difficult road travel into a seemingly sheltered place.

## Evidence
- Village landscape: [[245-nujiang-chunri-mijing-chutan-8221838951-646453]] describes lodging views of terraced fields, swallows, high mountains, and quickly clearing cloud.
- Sacred geography: [[245-nujiang-chunri-mijing-chutan-8221838951-646453]] visits Gongdang sacred mountain, explains the "white lion" meaning, and clarifies that Nujiang First Bend is a named local entry-point bend rather than literally the river's largest or first bend.
- Tibetan Buddhist ritual: [[245-nujiang-chunri-mijing-chutan-8221838951-646453]] records a Puhua Temple incense-offering experience using aromatic plants, grains, mantra recitation, and guide-led ritual vocalization.
- Christian history: [[245-nujiang-chunri-mijing-chutan-8221838951-646453]] discusses Chongding Catholic Church, a foreign missionary remembered locally after the 1905 Baihanluo church incident, grapevine introduction, and quieter Protestant church presence.

## Qualifications
The religious and ethnic distribution described here is source-scoped to the hosts' trip explanations and should not be treated as a complete demography.
