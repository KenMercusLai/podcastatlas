# Macbeth (historical king)

## Overview

Macbeth was an 11th-century ruler associated with Moray who defeated Duncan in battle in 1040 and ruled Scotland for about seventeen years before the restoration of Duncan's line.

## Current Profile

[[104-macbeth-glt3313769671]] places Macbeth inside [[MedievalScottishSuccession|a succession system with several plausible adult royal claimants]], not outside a settled hereditary order. After family conflict in Moray and a marriage to [[Gruoch]] that strengthened his dynastic position, he faced Duncan's attack, killed him in battle, and became king.

The episode interprets Macbeth's reign as comparatively successful and stable. His pilgrimage to Rome with Gruoch, survival after the 1054 Northumbrian invasion, continued fighting until 1057, and burial on Iona do not fit [[MacbethPlay|the play's]] brief reign of terror. His later usurper image is treated as a product of dynastic and literary memory after Duncan's line returned.

## Key Characteristics

- Moray-linked dynastic claimant operating within a competitive royal kin group.
- Victor over Duncan in battle rather than murderer of an elderly guest-king.
- Ruler of roughly seventeen years whose reign appears stable enough for a Roman pilgrimage.
- Husband of [[Gruoch]], whose ancestry strengthened their dynastic claim.
- King who survived the 1054 invasion but was defeated in 1057.
- Historical figure whose reputation was transformed by later genealogy, chronicle, and drama.

## Evidence

- Claim and accession: [[104-macbeth-glt3313769671]] connects Macbeth to Moray, royal descent, Gruoch's lineage, Duncan's attack, and the 1040 battlefield victory.
- Reign and status: [[104-macbeth-glt3313769671]] cites the long reign, Roman pilgrimage, Gruoch's queenly title, and honorable Iona burial as evidence against the theatrical tyrant profile.
- Fall and memory: [[104-macbeth-glt3313769671]] follows the 1054 invasion, final defeat in 1057, dynastic extinction, and later recasting as a usurper.

## Qualifications

The surviving early-medieval evidence is sparse and later politically shaped. Macbeth's responsibility for Gilla Comgain's death, the exact military sequence after 1054, the breadth of his popularity, and the source's description of his final return from retirement remain uncertain or source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Created a historical profile separate from Shakespeare's dramatic character.
- Recorded competitive succession, long rule, pilgrimage, and honorable burial as the main corrections to the usurper myth.
