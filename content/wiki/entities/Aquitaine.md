# Aquitaine

## Overview

Aquitaine appears as the broader French duchy whose reduced English-held remnant, [[Gascony]], remains the legal and territorial pressure point that helps open the [[HundredYearsWar|Hundred Years' War]].

## Current Profile

The episode treats Aquitaine as both inheritance residue and war threshold. Earlier English kings had once held much larger continental lands under the Angevin system, but by the early 14th century their surviving French position is narrowed to Gascony and Bordeaux.

French pressure on Aquitaine intensifies under [[EdwardIIOfEngland|Edward II]], when French forces invade in 1324 and overrun most of the duchy except Bordeaux. In May 1337, [[PhilipVIOfFrance|Philip VI]] declares the confiscation of the duchy, a move the hosts treat as effectively a declaration of war against [[EdwardIIIOfEngland|Edward III]].

## Key Characteristics

- Larger duchy behind the English-held Gascony remnant.
- Symbol of England's diminished but persistent continental position.
- Exposed to French investigation, invasion, homage demands, and forfeiture threats.
- Invaded by France in 1324 during Edward II's weakness.
- Confiscated by Philip VI in 1337.
- War-threshold object in the episode's origin narrative.
