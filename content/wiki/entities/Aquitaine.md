# Aquitaine

## Overview

Aquitaine is a western European duchy whose attempts to preserve autonomy recur across the wiki: between Frankish and Umayyad power in the eighth century, then between English possession and French overlordship during the [[HundredYearsWar|Hundred Years' War]].

## Current Profile

The Tours episode presents early-eighth-century Aquitaine as an independent regional power under [[OdoOfAquitaine|Odo]]. Odo first defended it without Frankish help at Toulouse, then sought security through a marriage alliance with the Berber leader Uthman. [[AbdAlRahmanAlGhafiqi|Abd al-Rahman's]] campaign broke that arrangement, sacked Bordeaux, and defeated Odo near the Garonne. Odo's submission to [[CharlesMartel]] in exchange for aid made the [[BattleOfTours]] a turning point in Aquitanian autonomy as well as Frankish-Umayyad conflict.

The Hundred Years' War source treats later Aquitaine as both inheritance residue and war threshold. Earlier English kings had once held much larger continental lands under the Angevin system, but by the early 14th century their surviving French position is narrowed to Gascony and Bordeaux.

French pressure on Aquitaine intensifies under [[EdwardIIOfEngland|Edward II]], when French forces invade in 1324 and overrun most of the duchy except Bordeaux. In May 1337, [[PhilipVIOfFrance|Philip VI]] declares the confiscation of the duchy, a move the hosts treat as effectively a declaration of war against [[EdwardIIIOfEngland|Edward III]].

After Poitiers and Edward's failed 1359-1360 conquest campaign, the [[TreatyOfBretigny|Treaty of Brétigny]] greatly enlarges Aquitaine and is intended to free it from French overlordship. [[EdwardTheBlackPrince|The Black Prince]] governs it through a magnificent but costly court in territory damaged by war and free companies.

Heavy taxation and the prince's Castilian debts weaken loyalty. Aquitanian subjects appeal to [[CharlesVOfFrance|Charles V]], and the prince rejects a French summons because he claims sovereign status. This unresolved appellate conflict gives France a route back into the duchy and helps turn Aquitaine from England's treaty prize into the center of its territorial collapse.

## Key Characteristics

- Early medieval duchy balancing Frankish pressure against Umayyad campaigning from Iberia.
- Polity whose ruler used battle and cross-confessional alliance before accepting Frankish protection and subordination.
- Larger duchy behind the later English-held Gascony remnant.
- Symbol of England's diminished but persistent continental position before 1337.
- Exposed to invasion, homage demands, forfeiture threats, and conflicting sovereignty claims.
- Greatly enlarged as a sovereign English possession under Brétigny.
- Destabilized across periods when external protection, taxation, local allegiance, and autonomy ceased to align.

## Qualifications

The page joins two widely separated episodes and does not imply institutional continuity between Odo's early-eighth-century polity and the later Angevin, English-held, or French duchy in every respect. It does not cover the intervening territorial transformations, the earlier Angevin inheritance in detail, or later reconquest campaign by campaign. Odo's status, alliance terms, force sizes, and submission remain episode-scoped, as do Brétigny's exact renunciations, local variation, tax burden, and appellate law.

## What Changed

- Extended Aquitaine from the 1337 war threshold to the enlarged but unstable Brétigny settlement.
- Added taxation, local appeals, and contested sovereignty as mechanisms of English reversal.
- Added Odo's earlier independence, cross-confessional frontier strategy, defeat, and submission before Tours.
