# Henry Percy 'Hotspur'

## Overview

Henry Percy, known as Hotspur, was the rebel noble defeated and killed at the [[BattleOfShrewsbury]], later transformed by Shakespeare into Prince Hal's dramatic rival.

## Current Profile

The episode separates the historical Hotspur from the symmetrical rival created for Shakespeare's stage. Hotspur was older than [[HenryVOfEngland|Prince Henry]] and entered the 1403 crisis with an established military reputation. He moved toward Chester and Shrewsbury, used Ricardian rhetoric, and may have intended to advance an alternative Mortimer claim.

His plan depended on speed, recruitment, and a broader coalition, but [[HenryIVOfEngland|Henry IV's]] forced march brought battle before those advantages matured. When rebel archery and difficult ground failed to stop the royal army, Hotspur charged toward the king and died. The source leaves his killer unidentified and rejects confidence in the Shakespearean duel with Prince Hal.

## Key Characteristics

- Experienced noble soldier rather than Prince Henry's exact contemporary.
- Rebel who used Richard II's memory to mobilize support.
- Commander seeking recruitment and coalition advantage around Chester and Shrewsbury.
- Leader whose final attack aimed at Henry IV personally.
- Historical figure recast as Prince Hal's theatrical double and victim.
