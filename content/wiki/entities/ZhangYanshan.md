# 张衍山 / Zhang Yanshan

## Overview
张衍山 / Zhang Yanshan is identified in [[vol-189-lunuzheng-zhenxiang-ni-renzhule-shenghuo-de-qingxu-que-zai-kaiche-shi-shikong-lrhaufg_zvbv4wn86-vjh3q3n4ws]] as a psychological therapist affiliated with Shenzhen Mental Health Center and Shenzhen Kangning Hospital.

## Current Profile
The episode presents Zhang Yanshan as a practical interpreter of [[RoadRageAndDrivingEmotion|road rage]] across road conditions, temperament, life stress, and driving behavior. He treats congestion and uncertain arrival time as real stressors, but emphasizes that aggressive lane changing, overtaking, horn use, or using speed as emotional discharge adds danger without reliably saving time.

Zhang's immediate strategy is to widen the gap between arousal and action: slow the breath, reduce stimulation, accept that urgency cannot remove all traffic constraints, and make route and timing choices before frustration peaks. He also applies [[Deindividuation|deindividuation]] cautiously to the enclosed car, where horns and vehicle movements replace richer face-to-face feedback.

## Key Characteristics
- Connects road rage to congestion, uncertainty, perceived unfairness, temperament, and accumulated work or family stress.
- Treats unsafe action, not the mere presence of anger, as the central behavioral boundary.
- Warns that excitement, conversation, and phone calls can impair attention as well as anger.
- Recommends slow breathing, lower stimulation, realistic timing, and route choice as in-car or pre-drive regulation supports.
- Uses the enclosed-car setting to explain how mediated, partly anonymous interaction can loosen ordinary restraint.
