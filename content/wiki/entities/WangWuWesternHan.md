## Overview

王乌 / Wang Wu is a Western Han envoy presented across Hanji 368-1 and 368-2 as a northern-border native chosen by [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] for cultural fluency in negotiations with [[Xiongnu|匈奴]]. His ritual flexibility repeatedly gains access and favorable words, but the completed sequence shows that neither the promised princely hostage nor the chanyu's proposed Chang'an visit becomes a verified settlement.

## Current Profile

Wang Wu's distinctive capability is cultural and procedural fluency. He understands that access to the chanyu requires compliance with Xiongnu audience rules, so he leaves the Han tally outside, changes clothing, marks his face, and enters alone. The sources interpret those choices as tactical concessions rather than abandonment of office.

Once admitted, Wang Wu separates form from substance. In the first mission he still presents Han's demand for submission and pairs it with material inducement; the chanyu reportedly offers to send his son to Chang'an. [[YangXinWesternHan|杨信]]'s follow-up then reveals that the hostage term is not mutually agreed, showing that Wang Wu obtained a message rather than an implemented commitment.

Wang Wu is sent again and again follows Xiongnu ritual. This time the chanyu says he will visit Chang'an and form a fraternal agreement, prompting Han to prepare a residence. The transmitted episode ultimately characterizes both rounds of favorable language as deception intended to draw more Han goods. Wang Wu's profile therefore joins genuine access skill to a verification failure: procedural success lets negotiation occur but does not establish counterpart intent.

## Key Characteristics

- Frontier-raised envoy selected for familiarity with Xiongnu culture and audience practice.
- Willing to relax symbolic protocol when doing so preserves access to negotiation.
- Keeps Han's substantive demand distinct from the ritual concessions used to deliver it.
- Produces two reported openings—a princely hostage and a chanyu visit—without securing implementation.
- Illustrates both the value of cultural fluency and its vulnerability to strategic deception.

## Qualifications

Both supplied notes call the envoy “王巫”; this page normalizes the name to 王乌 and does not treat the forms as separate people. The sources are short structured summaries rather than primary-text studies. They do not settle Wang Wu's wider biography, the exact ritual meaning of leaving the tally, whether face marking was permanent, the full bargaining terms, or his private assessment of the chanyu. The failed promises do not prove that Wang Wu was personally negligent: mandate ambiguity, counterpart deception, court optimism, and the absence of staged verification may all contribute.
