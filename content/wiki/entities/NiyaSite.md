# Niya Site / 尼雅遗址

## Overview
The Niya Site is the archaeological setting through which the current Hanji 689 sources reconstruct [[JingjueKingdom]]. Hanji 689-2 foregrounds ruins and documents, while Hanji 689-3 foregrounds the tomb context of the [[FiveStarsOutOfTheEastBrocade]].

## Current Profile
The current profile treats Niya as both a ruin field and an evidence chain. Hanji 689-2 places it near the Niya River in Minfeng County, describes preserved domestic remains under desert conditions, and uses [[KharosthiNiyaDocuments]] plus a Han wooden slip to identify it with Jingjue. Hanji 689-3 adds the 1995 Sino-Japanese excavation and elite tomb evidence that makes the brocade central to Jingjue's later public memory.

## Key Characteristics
- Niya is presented as the material anchor for identifying the ancient Jingjue polity.
- Its ruins preserve domestic architecture and everyday remains in a desert-covered oasis setting.
- Kharosthi and Han wooden documents make the site a textual as well as archaeological archive.
- Stein's discovery and removal activity make Niya a cultural-heritage-loss case as well as a discovery case.
- The 1995 tomb excavation ties Niya to the five-star brocade and a possible elite Jingjue context.
- Niya's ecology helps explain abandonment, but the site is not reduced to an ecology-only collapse story.

## Evidence
- Ruin setting: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-689-2-gujin-duihua-jiesuo-gui-chuideng-zhong-jingjue-guguo-de-shixue-zhi-mi-loznfitfv-dwsbgelk2wmdnk-kvu|Hanji 689-2]] places Niya around the Niya River and describes ruins with houses, posts, doors, windows, hearths, and ovens still legible in the desert.
- Discovery and loss: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-689-2-gujin-duihua-jiesuo-gui-chuideng-zhong-jingjue-guguo-de-shixue-zhi-mi-loznfitfv-dwsbgelk2wmdnk-kvu|Hanji 689-2]] says Aurel Stein acquired Kharosthi documents, investigated Niya, removed document batches, and helped trigger later foreign collecting and looting.
- Jingjue identification: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-689-2-gujin-duihua-jiesuo-gui-chuideng-zhong-jingjue-guguo-de-shixue-zhi-mi-loznfitfv-dwsbgelk2wmdnk-kvu|Hanji 689-2]] says a Han wooden slip with Jingjue royal wording verified the identification of Niya with ancient Jingjue.
- Tomb and brocade context: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-689-3-wuxing-chu-dongfang-li-zhongguo-yige-gulao-yuyan-de-xiandai-jiedu-lqccxzbvg-jziurfxm5tr5k4jlvq|Hanji 689-3]] places the 1995 brocade discovery at Niya in a two-person tomb with possible elite or royal context.
- Archaeology as reconstruction: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-689-3-wuxing-chu-dongfang-li-zhongguo-yige-gulao-yuyan-de-xiandai-jiedu-lqccxzbvg-jziurfxm5tr5k4jlvq|Hanji 689-3]] uses the artifact to connect Jingjue with Han diplomacy, Western Regions warfare, and omen language.
- Abandonment pressures: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-689-3-wuxing-chu-dongfang-li-zhongguo-yige-gulao-yuyan-de-xiandai-jiedu-lqccxzbvg-jziurfxm5tr5k4jlvq|Hanji 689-3]] says the Niya River dried under the arid Taklamakan environment, while [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-689-2-gujin-duihua-jiesuo-gui-chuideng-zhong-jingjue-guguo-de-shixue-zhi-mi-loznfitfv-dwsbgelk2wmdnk-kvu|Hanji 689-2]] adds Supi attack pressure from document evidence.

## Qualifications
Niya is a strong archaeological anchor for Jingjue in the current sources, but the exact identity of the tomb occupants, the full corpus history of the documents, and the precise chain from oasis stress to political disappearance remain reconstructed from limited evidence. The site supports a multi-cause interpretation rather than proving any one collapse mechanism by itself.

## What Changed
- Added Hanji 689-2's Stein, Kharosthi-document, Han wooden-slip, and cultural-loss layers.
- Preserved Hanji 689-3's 1995 tomb, brocade, and ecological-decline evidence.
- Reframed Niya as an archive of ruins, documents, artifacts, and loss rather than only the brocade's excavation site.

## Relationships
- [[JingjueKingdom]] - polity reconstructed through Niya ruins, documents, and tomb evidence.
- [[KharosthiNiyaDocuments]] - textual corpus that identifies the site and preserves Supi-pressure clues.
- [[AurelStein]] - collector-explorer whose activity frames Niya's modern discovery and evidence dispersal.
- [[FiveStarsOutOfTheEastBrocade]] - artifact found in the Niya tomb context described by Hanji 689-3.
- [[WesternRegionsProtectorate]] - Han frontier institution that gives the site historical context.
- [[ChineseAstrologicalPolitics]] - interpretive frame connected to the brocade phrase discovered at the site.
- [[CapacityBoundFrontierCommitment]] - later Han protection limits help explain why the oasis polity becomes vulnerable.
