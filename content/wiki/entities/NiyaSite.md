# Niya Site / 尼雅遗址

## Overview
The Niya Site is the archaeological setting through which Hanji 689-3 reconstructs [[JingjueKingdom]] and the [[FiveStarsOutOfTheEastBrocade]]. In the episode, the site sits in Minfeng County, Hotan, Xinjiang, and preserves the material remains that turn a Western Regions polity from textual memory into excavated evidence.

## Current Profile
The source treats Niya as both discovery site and historical argument. The 1995 Sino-Japanese excavation provides a tomb context for the brocade, while the broader ruins support a story of Jingjue's Han connection, later decline, ecological stress, and eventual disappearance from the inhabited oasis landscape.

## Key Characteristics
- Niya is presented as the excavation site for the brocade artifact central to Hanji 689-3.
- The tomb evidence is used to argue for a high-status or royal Jingjue context.
- The site anchors a shift from textual Western Regions history to archaeological reconstruction.
- The Niya River and Taklamakan-edge ecology are part of the source's explanation for decline.
- Later abandonment is tied to environmental drying and regional conflict rather than a single cause.

## Evidence
- Excavation setting: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-689-3-wuxing-chu-dongfang-li-zhongguo-yige-gulao-yuyan-de-xiandai-jiedu-lqccxzbvg-jziurfxm5tr5k4jlvq|Hanji 689-3]] places the 1995 discovery at Niya in Minfeng County, Hotan, Xinjiang.
- Elite tomb context: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-689-3-wuxing-chu-dongfang-li-zhongguo-yige-gulao-yuyan-de-xiandai-jiedu-lqccxzbvg-jziurfxm5tr5k4jlvq|Hanji 689-3]] describes a two-person tomb, pottery with "王", and a male occupant bearing the brocade.
- Archaeology as reconstruction: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-689-3-wuxing-chu-dongfang-li-zhongguo-yige-gulao-yuyan-de-xiandai-jiedu-lqccxzbvg-jziurfxm5tr5k4jlvq|Hanji 689-3]] uses the artifact to connect Jingjue with Han diplomacy, Western Regions warfare, and omen language.
- Environmental decline: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-689-3-wuxing-chu-dongfang-li-zhongguo-yige-gulao-yuyan-de-xiandai-jiedu-lqccxzbvg-jziurfxm5tr5k4jlvq|Hanji 689-3]] says the Niya River dried under the arid Taklamakan environment, shrinking the oasis base and pushing inhabitants upstream.
- Multi-cause disappearance: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-689-3-wuxing-chu-dongfang-li-zhongguo-yige-gulao-yuyan-de-xiandai-jiedu-lqccxzbvg-jziurfxm5tr5k4jlvq|Hanji 689-3]] joins ecology, warfare, and loss of Han protection in Jingjue's post-fourth-century disappearance.

## Qualifications
The source uses Niya as a strong archaeological anchor, but the exact identity of the tomb occupants and the precise path from site decline to population movement remain reconstructed from limited evidence.
