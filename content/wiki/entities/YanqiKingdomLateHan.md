## Overview

焉耆国 / Yanqi Kingdom appears in this wiki as a Western Regions polity repeatedly used to test Han control. Hanji 769 now supplies the earlier [[BanChaoEasternHan|Ban Chao]] conquest and hostage-submission layer, while Hanji 807 preserves the later [[BanYongLateHan|Ban Yong]] and [[ZhangLangLateHan|Zhang Lang]] command-credit failure around Yanqi's renewed holdout status.

## Current Profile

Hanji 769 presents Yanqi as the target of Ban Chao's 94 CE campaign. Ban Chao raises troops from [[QiuciKingdom|Qiuci]], Shanshan, and other Western Regions states, kills Yanqi king Guang and the Weili king at Chen Mu's old city, sends the heads to [[LuoyangLateHan|Luoyang]], permits harsh plunder and killing, and installs the pro-Han Yuanmeng as king. The source says more than fifty Western Regions states then send sons as hostages and submit to Han, making Yanqi a coercive consolidation case rather than only a local campaign target.

In [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-807-banchao-zhizi-weihe-hui-hanyuan-ruyu-liiwgi77g2on1z2vnwaf1-wvph2d|Hanji 807]], Yanqi returns as a later holdout after most Western Regions city-states have again returned to Han alignment. Its king, rendered by the source as Yuanmeng, has not yet submitted, so Ban Yong asks to attack.

The campaign against Yanqi is designed as a coordinated two-route operation. Ban Yong mobilizes more than 40,000 troops from Western Regions polities, while [[ZhangLangLateHan|Zhang Lang]] brings more than 3,000 from the Hexi four commanderies. Zhang Lang attacks early, kills many people, and Yanqi's king submits to him out of fear. That surrender clears Zhang Lang's penalty risk but produces the report environment in which Ban Yong is recalled, jailed, and removed.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Regions polity defined first by Ban Chao's forceful conquest and later by renewed holdout status.
- Campaign target rather than an internally synthesized polity in the current wiki evidence.
- Hanji 769's surrender layer depends on killing the king, installing a pro-Han replacement, and hostage submission from surrounding states.
- Its surrender is induced by fear after Zhang Lang's early attack rather than by Ban Yong's planned synchronized operation.
- Functions as the stage where frontier military outcome and court blame attribution diverge.

## Qualifications

The source transcripts write Yanqi as 燕齐 and the king as 元梦; this page uses the received Western Regions route 焉耆国 / Yanqi because the Ban Chao and Ban Yong contexts support that normalization. Hanji 769 names the earlier king as Guang and the replacement as Yuanmeng, while Hanji 807 uses Yuanmeng for the later holdout king in the podcast summary; the page records both source usages without trying to reconstruct the full royal sequence. Exact location names, casualties, and long-term settlement after submission remain source-scoped.
