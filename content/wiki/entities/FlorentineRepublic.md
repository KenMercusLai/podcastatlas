## Overview

The Florentine Republic / 佛罗伦萨共和国 was a guild-based polity whose rotating offices coexisted with elite faction, manipulated selection, informal Medici succession, Savonarolan authority, wider post-1494 participation, military reform, and repeated foreign intervention before hereditary Medici government displaced republican rule.

## Current Profile

Guild membership conditioned officeholding; names drawn every two months formed a Signoria and *gonfaloniere*; a great bell could summon a *parlamento*, which could establish an emergency *balia*. These devices dispersed authority in principle but could be captured when factions controlled candidate bags, assemblies, clients, taxes, exile, confiscation, or force.

The Albizzi used emergency machinery to exile [[CosimoDeMedici|Cosimo de' Medici]] in 1433. A later Medici majority recalled him, after which he preserved republican forms while making officeholders dependent on his network. [[PieroTheGouty|Piero the Gouty]] inherited this predominance, and [[LorenzoDeMedici|Lorenzo]] later concentrated legislation, appointments, and foreign policy through the Council of 70. Prosperity and cultural patronage coexisted with diminished liberty, bank weakness, violence, and public-resource use.

[[PieroDeMedici|Piero]] accepted [[CharlesVIIIOfFrance|Charles VIII]]'s demands without authorization, and the resulting expulsion and confiscation staged recovery of public authority. The post-1494 settlement created a Venetian-style grand council with wider male participation. [[GirolamoSavonarola|Savonarola]] supported it but exercised [[InformalReligiousPoliticalAuthority|informal authority]] through sermons and followers rather than office; the state constrained his youth companies, prohibited preaching, and survived his fall.

The republic later employed [[NiccoloMachiavelli|Machiavelli]], sought a standing army, and placed Michelangelo's *David* near the seat of government as a symbol of civic liberty against stronger enemies. Papal and Spanish force restored Medici control in 1512 after violence at Prato made resistance costly.

The new source complicates that endpoint: another anti-Medici republican interval emerged amid the Sack of Rome crisis before imperial force reconquered Florence and enabled hereditary ducal rule. Republican disappearance was therefore a repeated military-political process, not one clean constitutional event.

## Key Characteristics

- Guild-based polity using short Signoria terms, lot selection, a *gonfaloniere*, and emergency assemblies.
- Republic whose anti-domination procedures were vulnerable to candidate control, clients, guarded assemblies, and force.
- Economically and culturally productive city whose prosperity did not guarantee political liberty.
- Formal state repeatedly dominated by private Medici succession before open hereditary rule.
- Post-1494 republic with wider participation, moral conflict, military reform, and powerful civic symbolism.
- Civic order capable of resisting coups but also of extrajudicial revenge and collective punishment.
- Polity displaced through repeated papal, Spanish, and imperial-backed intervention.

## Qualifications

These sources provide selected narrative episodes rather than a complete constitutional, social, or economic history. Eligibility, participation, bag scrutiny, assembly intimidation, Signoria independence, public sentiment, coercive responsibility, victim counts, Prato, papal or imperial authorization, military reform, and the later republican chronology require specialist evidence. “Republic” did not mean universal participation, and restoration did not eliminate informal or foreign power.

## What Changed

- Added standing-army reform and *David* as a material symbol of republican liberty.
- Replaced a single 1512 endpoint with repeated restoration, republican revival, and imperial-backed reconquest.
- Added Prato's violence to the coercive environment of the first Medici return.
- Extended the trajectory into hereditary ducal displacement.
