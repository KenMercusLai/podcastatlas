# Abdul Dulloo

## Overview

Abdul Dulloo is the physiologist who explains the continuing scientific value of the [[MinnesotaStarvationExperiment]] and the uneven restoration of fat and lean tissue after severe weight loss.

## Current Profile

In the episode, Dulloo treats the Minnesota records as a gold standard for studying starvation in previously normal-weight adults because they cover controlled restriction and recovery in detail. He explains that recovery weight is not compositionally neutral: fat can return faster than muscle, abdominal fat can overshoot, and lean mass and physical function can lag.

His counterfactual modern study would add measurement of activity, muscle, immune function, and other physiological changes. Yet the wish to improve measurement does not remove the ethical and practical barriers that make the historic volunteers' disciplined, uncoerced participation so scientifically unusual.

## Key Characteristics

- Interprets the Minnesota study as unusually strong controlled evidence on starvation and recovery.
- Distinguishes restoration of body weight from restoration of muscle, function, and healthy fat distribution.
- Connects historical evidence to malnutrition, cancer treatment, eating disorders, obesity, and weight-loss recovery.
- Would use modern monitoring to study activity, muscle, immune function, and other changes more precisely.
- Credits the volunteers' sustained participation as a major contribution while recognizing that comparable work is difficult to repeat.
