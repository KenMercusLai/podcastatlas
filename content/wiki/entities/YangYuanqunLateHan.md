## Overview
Yang Yuanqun was a late-Han official whose Beihai record becomes a compact case of local corruption protected by eunuch access. His importance in the wiki is not administrative achievement but the way his prosecution against public-property abuse rebounds onto [[LiYingLateHan|Li Ying]] once bribery reaches the palace.

## Current Profile
The current evidence presents Yang Yuanqun as a Beihai administrator with a strong local family background and a pattern of illegal appropriation during and after office. When the court orders Li Ying to investigate, Yang does not answer the charge through legal vindication; he uses eunuch bribery to reverse the pressure.

This makes Yang a useful early marker for the later Party Prohibition sequence. Before Li Ying is publicly restored and then celebrated as a model official, his attempt to enforce against Yang already shows a regime in which eunuch-mediated access can make ordinary investigation dangerous to the investigator.

## Key Characteristics
- Local elite official whose Beihai tenure is associated with corruption and public-property abuses.
- Defendant whose strongest protection comes from bribing eunuch intermediaries rather than clearing the underlying accusation.
- Trigger case in which Li Ying's enforcement role is punished instead of rewarded.
- Example of how local corruption and palace access reinforce one another under Emperor Huan.
