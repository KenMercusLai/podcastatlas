# Las Vegas Grand Prix

## Overview

The Las Vegas Grand Prix is a [[FormulaOne]] street race used in the supplied episode as a case study in dense-crowd connectivity, event operations, and data-rich spectator access.

## Current Profile

The episode describes a roughly 3.8-mile circuit through a busy entertainment district, with cars reaching about 220 miles per hour and approximately 150,000 people attempting to connect. [[TMobile]] reportedly installed about 50 sites to support applications, ticketing, navigation, communications, payments, entertainment, video, and telemetry. The event therefore illustrates how a spatially fragmented live product can be coordinated through shared digital infrastructure, while the sponsored source leaves performance and alternatives untested.

## Key Characteristics

- Uses a long urban street circuit that complicates spectator movement and visibility.
- Integrates e-ticketing, changing walking routes, communications, and point-of-sale services.
- Combines in-person viewing with F1 TV, timing, telemetry, and race-position data.
- Serves as a high-density network and event-operations test case.
- Adds concerts and fan zones intended to engage audiences beyond committed racing fans.

## Qualifications

Crowd size, site count, speed, operational benefit, and causation are episode-reported figures from a paid T-Mobile partnership. The source does not provide an outage record, independent coverage map, accessibility audit, cost comparison, privacy assessment, or evidence that every service depended specifically on 5G.
