# Saint-Domingue

## Overview

Saint-Domingue was the exceptionally wealthy French plantation colony that became the principal theater of the [[HaitianRevolution]] and the territorial predecessor of independent [[Haiti]].

## Current Profile

The episode presents Saint-Domingue as a concentrated version of the Atlantic system: sugar wealth depended on intensive enslaved labor, violent discipline, maritime commerce, and a legally stratified population of enslaved people, white colonists, and free people of color. Property ownership did not erase ancestry-based disability; some free people of African descent owned property or enslaved people yet remained below poor whites in law and status.

French revolutionary equality entered this unstable order rather than a social blank slate. Maroon communities had already demonstrated continued resistance, free people of color pressed for civic equality, white factions defended colonial privilege, and the enslaved majority faced an exceptionally coercive regime. Revolution in France opened a struggle in which these groups, French forces, and foreign powers repeatedly changed alliances.

## Key Characteristics

- French Caribbean colony built on high-value plantation production.
- Extremely wealthy export economy dependent on enslaved labor.
- Legally differentiated enslaved, white, and free-colored populations.
- Racial ancestry operating as a hereditary status marker despite freedom or property.
- Maroon resistance predating the general uprising.
- Colonial setting transformed into the central battlefield of the Haitian Revolution.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to one structured podcast summary, not a complete economic, demographic, legal, or political history of the colony. Wealth rankings, population structure, legal changes, punishment accounts, factional alignments, and the sequence of revolutionary decisions remain episode-attributed.

## What Changed

- Established Saint-Domingue as the colonial setting in which plantation wealth, racial status, and revolutionary universalism collided.
- Distinguished legal freedom and property from racial equality.
