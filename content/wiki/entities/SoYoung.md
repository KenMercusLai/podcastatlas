# So-Young / 新氧

## Overview

So-Young / 新氧 is the Chinese medical-aesthetic company discussed in [[ep153-ep153]] as moving from an online lead-generation platform into a directly operated light-medical-aesthetic clinic chain.

## Current Profile

The episode presents the transition as a change in responsibility, not only channel. After the platform's reported revenue plateau, New Oxygen concluded that listings, reviews, and complaint penalties could not reliably change aggressive sales, prepaid lock-in, or inconsistent offline delivery. Its clinic strategy therefore brings service selection, pricing, procurement, training, workflow, digital systems, and store economics under company control.

The operating thesis combines a narrow repeatable treatment menu, centralized buying, mall-based small stores, physician-led consultation tests, no stored-value cards, visible preparation, and repeat-purchase measurement. Reported unit economics show early positive signals, but the group was still loss-making and the oldest stores provide only a short operating history for judging national replication.

## Key Characteristics

- Public-company transition from medical-aesthetic traffic intermediary to direct service operator.
- Headquarters-controlled clinic model with centralized services, prices, procurement, training, systems, and store design.
- Focus on recurring anti-aging and skin-management treatments rather than broad plastic-surgery coverage.
- Trust strategy built around transaction-by-transaction payment, reduced sales pressure, visible workflow, and customer ratings.
- Experienced platform users and referrals used as the initial demand base for new clinics.
- Long-horizon expansion supported by accumulated capital and a stated cash-reserve floor.
- AI-native clinic exploration that remains subordinate to clinical responsibility and regulatory limits.

## Evidence

- Strategic transition: [[ep153-ep153]] reports a plateau in platform revenue and the conclusion that information transparency could not control offline delivery incentives.
- Operating system: [[ep153-ep153]] describes headquarters control, limited services, centralized procurement, clinician training, treatment guidance, open store design, and digital process displays.
- Retention model: [[ep153-ep153]] reports no stored-value cards, tests of direct physician consultation, and evaluation through repeat purchase and customer lifetime value.
- Rollout evidence: [[ep153-ep153]] reports about seventy stores in seventeen or eighteen cities, rapid recent growth, and unit profitability for most stores in months seven through ten.
- Capital and AI: [[ep153-ep153]] connects the model to prior financing, a stated RMB 800 million cash floor, and a planned store-level AI-native experiment.

## Qualifications

The current profile relies on one founder interview summarized by the source file. Revenue, traffic, treatment volume, growth, cash, store count, unit profitability, and payback are not independently verified here. Unit profitability does not establish group profitability, clinical outcomes, durable same-store demand, capital efficiency, or thousand-store transferability. Visible preparation and standardized process do not replace current provider, product, indication, consent, or complication-support checks.

## What Changed

- Created the company profile around its platform-to-owned-supply transition.
- Kept reported unit profitability separate from unresolved group economics and national replication.
