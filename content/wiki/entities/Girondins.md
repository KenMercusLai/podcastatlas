# Girondins / 吉伦特派

## Overview

The Girondins were a republican faction in the National Convention associated with prosperous provincial cities, relatively liberal economic policy, distrust of Parisian crowd power, and resistance to escalating revolutionary violence.

## Current Profile

The sources present the Girondins as neither monarchists nor a neutral constitutional center. During [[LouisXVI|Louis XVI]]'s trial they accepted republican guilt while seeking popular ratification and later reprieve, positions opponents recoded as disguised royalism. Their later conflict with the Montagnards became mutually destructive: failed prosecution of [[JeanPaulMarat|Marat]], association with Dumouriez, and forced purge under armed pressure marked the collapse of representative restraint and helped produce provincial federalist revolt.

## Key Characteristics

- Their base and political imagination were more provincial than the Paris-centered Montagnards.
- They favored relatively liberal economic policy and distrusted coercive popular politics in the capital.
- They proposed a popular referendum on Louis's verdict and later sought a reprieve without abandoning republicanism.
- They opposed Marat's bloodshed but failed to defeat him through prosecution.
- Dumouriez's defection badly damaged them because he had been a Girondin foreign minister.
- Armed crowds and National Guards forced the Convention to suspend and arrest twenty-nine Girondin deputies in June 1793.
- Corday identified with their cause after fugitive deputies gathered in Caen.

### Republican faction and policy conflict

- [[125-mala-zhisi-beibi-de-ansha-huo-zhengyi-de-chujue-783972516]] distinguishes Girondin republican restraint from Jacobin terror and connects factional conflict to prices, popular pressure, and treason language.
- [[707-the-terror-an-assassination-in-paris-part-1-glt6580043397]] adds provincial support, economic liberalism, and their distrust of Parisian crowds.
- [[547-the-french-revolution-the-execution-of-the-king-part-4-glt2184509954]] shows the earlier referendum and reprieve disputes turning disagreement over punishment and procedure into suspicion of royalism.

### Defeat and purge

- [[707-the-terror-an-assassination-in-paris-part-1-glt6580043397]] follows the backfiring Marat trial, Dumouriez's defection, armed encirclement of the Convention, and arrest of twenty-nine deputies.

### Provincial afterlife

- [[707-the-terror-an-assassination-in-paris-part-1-glt6580043397]] connects the purge to federalist resistance in Lyon, Marseille, Bordeaux, and Caen and to Corday's decision.

## Qualifications

- The sources emphasize the Marat-Corday crisis and do not provide a complete history of Girondin membership or policy.
- Girondin recognition of revolutionary self-destruction did not prevent their own partisan rhetoric from intensifying the conflict.
- "Federalist" was a revolutionary political label and should not be assumed to describe one uniform provincial program.
- The source does not establish that every Girondin shared one motive or position on Louis's punishment.

## What Changed

- The faction's pre-purge profile now includes its referendum and reprieve positions during Louis's trial.
- Opposition to execution is separated explicitly from monarchism.
- The trajectory from procedural suspicion to prosecution failure, June purge, and provincial revolt is now visible.

## Relationships

- [[JeanPaulMarat]] - radical opponent whose prosecution became their political defeat.
- [[LouisXVI]] - condemned king whose punishment exposed the faction to royalist suspicion.
- [[NationalConvention]] - assembly in which their referendum and reprieve proposals were defeated.
- [[CharlotteCorday]] - sympathizer who tried to avenge or rescue their cause through assassination.
- [[FrenchRevolution]] - republican conflict in which their purge marked a decisive rupture.
- [[RevolutionaryJusticeMercyConflict]] - conflict through which their procedural position acquired factional danger.
- [[CrisisValidatedConspiracyPolitics]] - Dumouriez's betrayal magnified suspicion against the faction.
