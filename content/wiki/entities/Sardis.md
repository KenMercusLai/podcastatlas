# Sardis

## Overview

Sardis appears in the wiki as the former Lydian capital and Persian satrapal center whose burning in 498 BC makes Athenian punishment unavoidable in the episode's account.

## Current Profile

The episode gives Sardis two roles. In the imperial backstory, [[CyrusTheGreat]] conquers Lydia and Sardis, bringing the Ionian Greeks under Persian rule. In the [[IonianRevolt]], Athenians, Eretrians, and Ionians march inland, capture and burn the lower city, and destroy a temple of Cybele while the governor remains in the Acropolis.

Sardis therefore becomes more than a place-name. Its burning turns a local Ionian revolt into a symbolic insult against imperial order, religious space, and Darius's authority.

## Key Characteristics

- Sardis is presented as the Lydian capital whose conquest helps fold Ionian Greeks into Persian rule.
- Under Persian rule it functions as a governor's capital and inland target for the rebels.
- The 498 BC burning of the lower city is the episode's decisive provocation.
- The destruction of Cybele's temple makes the raid religiously and symbolically charged.
- Darius's memory of Sardis frames later retaliation against Athens.
