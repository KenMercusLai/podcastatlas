# Laurie Rogers Stokes

## Overview

Laurie Rogers Stokes is the historian interviewed about the social, religious, legal, and political life of [[Puritans]] in seventeenth-century [[MassachusettsBayColony]].

## Current Profile

Stokes uses town records, laws, religious practice, clothing, marriage, sexuality, punishment, and political institutions to replace a single stereotype with a bounded institutional history. Her interpretation emphasizes mediation, literacy, representative government, and congregational autonomy without extending those achievements into a general defense of the colony: patriarchy, religious coercion, Indigenous dispossession, enslavement, and cultural destruction remain central to the account.

## Key Characteristics

- Interprets Puritanism through everyday institutions and records rather than inherited cultural imagery.
- Distinguishes reforming Puritans from the separatist Mayflower Pilgrims.
- Treats civic participation and religious discipline as connected parts of a covenantal community.
- Uses marriage, sexuality, clothing, music, and punishment to test familiar stereotypes.
- Separates rights within the settler community from the colony's treatment of Indigenous peoples.
- Presents the [[SalemWitchTrials|Salem witch trials]] as exceptional institutional breakdown rather than a complete model of Puritan justice.
