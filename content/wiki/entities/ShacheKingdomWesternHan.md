## Overview

莎车国 / Shache Kingdom (Western Han) enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-427-yiren-zhengfu-shacheguo-xihan-fengfengshi-zenme-zuodao-de-loxxi2qynbrbmfqgokos30sypsxp|Hanji 427]] as a small but strategically exposed Western Regions polity whose succession crisis lets [[FengFengshiWesternHan|冯奉世]] demonstrate both emergency frontier capacity and unauthorized-command risk. This page is disambiguated from the later Eastern Han [[ShacheKingdomLateHan|莎车国]] page.

## Current Profile

Hanji 427 places Western Han Shache near the Kunlun-Congling-Pamir field, with a modest population and pressure from Qiang groups, Qiuci, and neighboring oasis politics. Its old king dies without an heir and asks that Wannian, a son of [[XieyouPrincessWesternHan|解忧公主]], be installed. The episode reads the request as a protection bargain: Shache seeks shelter under both Han prestige and [[Wusun|乌孙]] connections.

That arrangement fails because local legitimacy and rule quality do not follow from outside backing alone. Wannian lacks the local Saka/Sai lineage connection described by the source and rules harshly. The old king's brother Hutuzheng then kills Wannian and the Han envoy, takes the throne, and blocks the southern Silk Road route. Feng Fengshi responds before a Chang'an order can arrive, issues a false command to gather Western Regions cavalry, and brings overwhelming pressure to the city.

The surrender is internal rather than a long siege. Shache nobles remember the precedent of city elites sacrificing a ruler to preserve the population, kill Hutuzheng, and bring his head out to surrender. Feng enters Shache, installs another son of the old king, disperses allied troops, and sends the rebel head to Chang'an. Shache therefore becomes a case where a weak local regime collapses when outside deterrence makes elite self-preservation more attractive than resistance.

## Key Characteristics

- Small Western Regions polity presented as geographically important despite limited population.
- Seeks Han-Wusun protection by requesting installation of Xieyou Princess's son Wannian.
- Succession legitimacy remains fragile because outside-linked kingship does not guarantee local acceptance.
- Hutuzheng's killing of Wannian and a Han envoy turns local succession conflict into a Han authority crisis.
- Feng Fengshi's allied cavalry pressure defeats Shache by inducing internal elite surrender rather than by direct annihilation.
- Regime replacement restores a Shache royal-line successor under Han-aligned pressure.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 427. It does not reconstruct Shache's full Western Han history, precise population record, old royal genealogy, relationship to Shule, Saka/Sai identity, later Xuanzang terminology, or the full bureaucratic handling of the southern route. The episode's Shache layer should stay separate from the later Eastern Han Shache profile unless a future source explicitly bridges the two.
