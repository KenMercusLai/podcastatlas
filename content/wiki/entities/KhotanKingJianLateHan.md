## Overview

于阗王建 / King Jian of Khotan is the ruler killed in Wang Jing's banquet plot in Hanji 838. The page treats him primarily as the target of a rumor-driven frontier intervention: he is accused by a rival, attends the banquet because he believes he has no guilt, and is killed before any stable adjudication occurs.

## Current Profile

King Jian is already in rivalry with [[JumiKingChengguoLateHan|居弥王成国]] when Zhao Ping's death becomes politically useful. Chengguo claims that Jian had a Xiongnu doctor poison or harm Zhao Ping, but the source presents this through Chengguo's hostile report rather than as settled fact.

When [[WangJingXiyuLateHan|王敬]] invites him to a banquet, Jian receives warning beforehand but still attends with a small retinue because he believes he has done nothing wrong. Wang orders him surrounded during a toast, and Chengguo's registrar Qin Mu beheads him. Jian's death triggers Khotanese retaliation and a brief succession struggle before his son Anguo is installed.

## Key Characteristics

- Khotan king accused by a hostile Jumi ruler after Zhao Ping's death.
- Victim of an unverified murder accusation turned into coercive envoy action.
- Attends Wang Jing's banquet despite warning because he expects innocence to matter.
- Killed by Chengguo's registrar Qin Mu during Wang Jing's seizure attempt.
- Father of Anguo, whose later installation restores Khotanese succession after Shubo's failure.

## Qualifications

This page is source-bounded to the Hanji 838 incident. It does not verify Chengguo's accusation against Jian or reconstruct Jian's reign outside the banquet-killing sequence.
