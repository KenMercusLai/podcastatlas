# Alexandria

## Overview

Alexandria was the planned Mediterranean capital founded by Alexander in Egypt and developed by the Ptolemies into a port, palace city, dynastic showcase, and Greek-speaking intellectual center.

## Current Profile

[[361-the-lost-library-of-alexandria-glt1578679297]] presents the city as a deliberately spectacular foundation between the Mediterranean and Lake Mariotis. Deep-water harbors, the Pharos, commercial networks, palace institutions, and [[PtolemyI]]'s possession of Alexander's body helped the new dynasty turn urban design and cultural spending into legitimacy.

Alexandria was multilingual and cosmopolitan, but its [[MuseumOfAlexandria|Museum]] and [[LibraryOfAlexandria|library]] remained primarily Greek in scholarly orientation. [[196-julius-caesar-cleopatra-part-2-glt3216275988]] shows the palace capital as a political and military system: Caesar's small force occupied the royal quarter, the Ptolemaic succession fractured into rival camps, and the [[AlexandrianWar]] exposed harbor, fleet, palace, and stored books to urban conflict. Later civic violence, imperial wars, and damage to the palace quarter make that instability part of the library's gradual-disappearance story.

## Key Characteristics

- Planned Hellenistic city founded after Alexander's conquest of Egypt.
- Maritime and commercial center connected by harbors to the Mediterranean.
- Ptolemaic capital that used spectacle and Alexander's memory for dynastic legitimacy.
- Multilingual metropolis whose court scholarship was nevertheless strongly Greek-centered.
- Home of the Museum and Library of Alexandria within or near the royal quarter.
- Dynastic and military arena where Roman civil war became an Alexandrian succession war.

- Dynastic capital: [[361-the-lost-library-of-alexandria-glt1578679297]] links Ptolemy's seizure of Alexander's body and cultural patronage to successor legitimacy.

## Qualifications

The page is grounded in two episodes focused on the library and Caesar-Cleopatra crisis, not a complete urban history. Comparisons with Dubai, Las Vegas, or Brasilia are explanatory analogies, while details about ancient automation, addresses, banks, riot chronology, palace decisions, popular alignments, and fire damage remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Added the palace quarter, harbor, and succession conflict as an integrated wartime urban system.
- Connected the Alexandrian War to the city's longer vulnerability without treating one fire as the library's total end.
