## Overview

孺子婴 / Ruzi Ying, rendered in the sources as 刘婴, is synthesized as a late [[WesternHanDynasty|Western Han]] descendant turned into a vulnerable restoration symbol. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-628-ruguo-chuanyue-jiao-ni-ruhe-dang-huangdi-lljocpgp247g7frbrkta39zrtlc|Hanji 628]] adds the pre-selection field: after [[HanPingdi|汉平帝]] dies, [[WangMang|王莽]] avoids adult Liu-line royals and pushes the court toward young descendants of Emperor Xuan because a child successor is easier to control. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-629-liujiaren-shui-kangqi-fankang-wangmang-de-daqi-lmhws7-jptdh9tf45bqtlfvzdw55|Hanji 629]] then supplies the formal 6 CE installation layer: Wang Mang selects the two-year-old Liu Ying by claiming the most auspicious divination result and makes him crown prince under the "Ruzi" label. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-634-huozai-nanren-yingzi-beihou-de-ta-can-can-can-lm3wub0l70yxwwyvwi15j93i9lwk|Hanji 634]] then adds the 9 CE settlement layer: after Wang Mang removes the Han name, Liu Ying is made Ding'an Duke with promised land, households, temples, Han laws, and Han colors, while the episode says these promises are largely empty. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-675-luanshi-fenzheng-zhizhong-liuxiu-ruhe-jueqi-lrvxu1q4-ysg4kfxkf25hswygfyo|Hanji 675]] later says he descends from Emperor Xuan, is isolated so completely that normal speech and recognition fail to develop, and in Jianwu 1 / 25 CE is reused by [[FangWangGengshi|方望]] and Gong Lin as a claimant at Linjing, where Liu Xuan's forces kill the group.

## Current Profile

The current profile is not a full biography; it is a source-bounded account of royal identity as exposure and containment. Hanji 628 shows why youth matters before Liu Ying's later title: Wang Mang's succession design needs a Liu-line heir whose genealogy can carry Han continuity but whose age lets Wang Mang keep practical power. Hanji 629 shows that this logic becomes a formal crown-prince arrangement in 6 CE, with divination language used to make the selection appear ritually sanctioned. Hanji 634 shows Wang Mang keeping Liu Ying alive as an old-dynasty token while stripping real authority from Han. The Ding'an Duke arrangement promises ritual continuity but also confines Han memory to a managed fief whose practical protections are doubtful in the episode's telling.

Hanji 675 presents Ruzi Ying first as a child whose symbolic value to Wang Mang damages his ordinary life, then as an adult whose Liu lineage makes him useful to later actors seeking a Han restoration banner.

The Linjing episode makes the figurehead problem lethal. Fang Wang and Gong Lin can gather several thousand people around Ruzi Ying's name, but the source gives him no independent command, policy, or agency. Liu Xuan's chancellor Li Song suppresses the group, so the old royal name produces risk without producing enough military capacity to survive.

## Key Characteristics

- Han royal descendant whose lineage remains politically usable before and after Wang Mang's usurpation.
- Child-successor candidate type selected because youth makes control easier for Wang Mang.
- Two-year-old crown prince installed under Wang Mang's divination-backed 6 CE regency settlement.
- Ding'an Duke settlement figure used to contain Han legitimacy after Wang Mang removes the Han name.
- Old-dynasty ritual token promised temples, laws, colors, land, and households but denied actual rule.
- Child imperial figure whose isolation under Wang Mang is presented as personally destructive.
- Figurehead with no demonstrated independent command in the source.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 628, Hanji 629, Hanji 634, and Hanji 675. The source set emphasizes Ruzi Ying's vulnerability, ceremonial containment, and symbolic reuse, but it does not reconstruct his full childhood chronology, internal household conditions, or the detailed military makeup of Fang Wang's Linjing group. Hanji 628 supports the young-heir selection logic without proving the full deliberation behind the final choice. Hanji 629 supports the 6 CE crown-prince installation and divination claim, but not the private mechanics behind the claimed auspicious result. Hanji 634 preserves Wang Mang's promised Ding'an arrangements as source claims while also recording the host's judgment that they were not practically fulfilled. This page is distinct from [[LiuYing|刘盈 / Liu Ying]], the Western Han crown prince and Emperor Hui, and from [[LiuYingChuKingEasternHan|刘英 / Liu Ying]], the Eastern Han Chu king.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 629's formal 6 CE crown-prince installation layer on top of Hanji 628's young-heir selection logic.
- Preserved Hanji 634's Ding'an Duke settlement, empty Han-continuity promises, and Wang Mang's tearful handoff performance.
- Preserved Hanji 675's figurehead-vulnerability reading: royal descent can create danger without creating agency or protection.
