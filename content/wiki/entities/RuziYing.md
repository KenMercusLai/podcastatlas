## Overview

孺子婴 / Ruzi Ying, rendered in the source as 刘婴, enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-675-luanshi-fenzheng-zhizhong-liuxiu-ruhe-jueqi-lrvxu1q4-ysg4kfxkf25hswygfyo|Hanji 675]] as a late Western Han descendant turned into a vulnerable restoration symbol. The episode says he descends from [[WesternHanDynasty|Western Han]] Emperor Xuan, is selected by [[WangMang|王莽]] as a child imperial figure, then is isolated so completely that normal speech and recognition fail to develop. In Jianwu 1 / 25 CE, [[FangWangGengshi|方望]] and Gong Lin again use him as a claimant at Linjing, where Liu Xuan's forces kill the group.

## Current Profile

The current profile is not a full biography; it is a source-bounded account of royal identity as exposure. Hanji 675 presents Ruzi Ying first as a child whose symbolic value to Wang Mang damages his ordinary life, then as an adult whose Liu lineage makes him useful to later actors seeking a Han restoration banner.

The Linjing episode makes the figurehead problem lethal. Fang Wang and Gong Lin can gather several thousand people around Ruzi Ying's name, but the source gives him no independent command, policy, or agency. Liu Xuan's chancellor Li Song suppresses the group, so the old royal name produces risk without producing enough military capacity to survive.

## Key Characteristics

- Han royal descendant whose lineage remains politically usable after Wang Mang's usurpation.
- Child imperial figure whose isolation under Wang Mang is presented as personally destructive.
- Adult claimant reused by Fang Wang and Gong Lin in Jianwu 1.
- Figurehead with no demonstrated independent command in the source.
- Victim whose royal identity increases exposure rather than protection.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 675. The source emphasizes Ruzi Ying's vulnerability and symbolic use, but it does not reconstruct his full childhood chronology, formal titles, internal household conditions, or the detailed military makeup of Fang Wang's Linjing group. This page is distinct from [[LiuYing|刘盈 / Liu Ying]], the Western Han crown prince and Emperor Hui, and from [[LiuYingChuKingEasternHan|刘英 / Liu Ying]], the Eastern Han Chu king.
