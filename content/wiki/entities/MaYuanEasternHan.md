## Overview

马援 / Ma Yuan is now synthesized as both a frontier general whose death exposes the danger of posthumous slander and a later-remembered warning voice about court patronage risk. Hanji 726 supplies the final campaign, [[LiangSongLinxiangMarquis|梁松]] accusation, family humiliation, [[ZhuBoEasternHan|朱勃]] defense, and [[EmpressMaMingdeEasternHan|明德马皇后]] family aftermath. Hanji 728 then uses Ma Yuan as the figure who sees elite guest networks and princely recruitment as dangers before they become legally visible.

## Current Profile

Hanji 726 places Ma Yuan's final profile in 49 CE. He dies in camp while campaigning against [[WulingManLateHan|Wuling Man]], after previously voicing the ideal that a man should die on the frontier and return wrapped in horsehide. His death does not settle his honor. Liang Song, sent to rebuke and monitor him, uses the moment to accuse him; coix seeds brought from Jiaozhi are reframed as supposed treasure, and [[LiuXiu|Liu Xiu]] angrily seizes Ma Yuan's Xinxihou seal.

The same source makes Ma Yuan's reputation depend on others after death. His family buries him hastily, binds themselves, pleads at the palace gate, and learns the accusation only when Liu Xiu shows Liang Song's memorial. Zhu Bo then reframes the case around twenty-two years of service, western and southern campaigns, wounds, epidemic death, and proportional judgment. Ma Yuan's family line also moves into palace history when a young daughter enters the crown-prince palace and later becomes Mingde Empress Ma.

Hanji 728 adds a separate remembered function. Ma Yuan warns that [[WangPanEasternHan|Wang Pan]]'s high-profile capital associations are unsafe for a family that should be keeping a low profile, and he separately warns that princes gathering guests after order has stabilized can create disaster. Later punishment of Wang Pan, continued princely access by [[WangSuEasternHan|Wang Su]], and the prince-guest purge make Ma Yuan a diagnostic figure for political risk before punishment arrives.

## Key Characteristics

- Frontier general whose final campaign against Wuling Man ends in camp death and posthumous accusation.
- Victim of reputational damage after death, when alleged treasure baggage overwhelms his ability to answer.
- Family patriarch whose fall produces immediate fear, ritual disorder, and a later palace branch through Mingde Empress Ma.
- Figure whose service record is defended through merit, wounds, campaign hardship, and epidemic context.
- Political-risk diagnostician who reads elite association and prince-guest networks as structurally dangerous.
- Bridge between frontier service, court slander, family survival, and later Eastern Han palace politics.

## Qualifications

This page still does not reconstruct Ma Yuan's complete military biography. Hanji 726 explains the posthumous accusation and defense but does not prove every motive behind Liang Song, the Dou-family marriage break, or Liu Xiu's final formal settlement. Hanji 728 supplies remembered warnings after Ma Yuan's death, so its political-risk function should be read as a later narrative use of Ma Yuan, not as the same event sequence as the 49 CE slander case.
