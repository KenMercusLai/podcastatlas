## Overview

马援 / Ma Yuan is synthesized as a frontier general, commandery governor, frontier-resource strategist, family disciplinarian, court-risk diagnostician, policy adviser, and former Wei-side insider whose danger begins before his posthumous slander. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-703-donghan-kaiguo-hou-zui-xianhe-de-jiazu-pingsha-shi-ta-lgg-fra8ostp2xsuugph6o-ilzac|Hanji 703]] now adds the earliest current strategic-warning layer: Ma Yuan explains his old tie to [[WeiXiaoEasternHan|Wei Xiao]], presents his earlier eastward report on [[LiuXiu|Liu Xiu]] as honest guidance, asks to brief Liu Xiu on anti-Wei strategy, and then helps divide Wei-side commanders through cavalry-backed persuasion. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-704-weixiao-wo-da-ni-shi-bu-dui-dan-ni-ping-sha-hai-shou-lgi-cjyledgzldrie6kwpvejwsls|Hanji 704]] then sharpens that role through his letter to [[YangGuangWeiXiaoRetainer|Yang Guang]], arguing that Wei Xiao's two-commandery position cannot stand against the wider realm and that silence could produce clan disaster. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-711-ta-zhu-liuxiu-da-xia-banbi-jiangshan-weihe-si-fa-biequ-liib38zub0-tgrbtycmlqvohv-w|Hanji 711]] adds the frontier-resource policy layer around Jincheng, Poqiang, irrigable land, and Huangzhong risk. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-714-shi-shui-di-yi-ge-han-chu-nan-er-dang-ma-ge-guo-shi-lpkqxrl3dsok4ui0lij4c3x8zfpi|Hanji 714]] adds the next Wudu/Qiang layer: Ma Yuan defeats Qiang forces, governs through delegated routine and selective intervention, refuses panic mobilization after a false Qiang alarm, and becomes the idiom anchor for [[HorsehideBurialResolve|马革裹尸式边将生死观]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-718-zhengce-ruhe-chengwei-shishang-no-1-de-nvwang-likq4pahq21k6bxbaohwdhcnwg5r|Hanji 718]] adds the later 40 CE adviser layer, while [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-719-guangwudi-liuxiu-suanbusuan-zhanan-lk8eme3wwyk5qkjjfxkp0zsc77ix|Hanji 719]] gives the Jiaozhi command prelude.

## Current Profile

Hanji 703 supplies the earliest current Ma Yuan layer. Ma Yuan explains to Liu Xiu that Wei Xiao had earlier sent him east to observe Liu Xiu and Luoyang, and that Ma Yuan's favorable report was meant to lead Wei Xiao toward the better side rather than deceive him. After Wei Xiao rebels, Ma Yuan asks to present anti-Wei strategy directly to Liu Xiu.

Liu Xiu then uses Ma Yuan's insider credibility operationally. Ma Yuan moves with more than five thousand cavalry as a shock and persuasion force among Wei-side commanders and Qiang leaders, including [[GaoJunEasternHan|Gao Jun]], Ren Yu, and unnamed Qiang chiefs. The source emphasizes his analysis of interests, outlook, and battle conditions rather than only combat. Ma Yuan also writes Yang Guang, asking him to persuade Wei Xiao and warning that Wei Xiao's son Wei Xun is exposed in Luoyang as a hostage.

Hanji 704 then supplies the next warning-letter layer. Ma Yuan writes Yang Guang in Wei Xiao's camp, urging him to join Niu Han and others in remonstrating with Wei Xiao or leave if persuasion fails. The advice uses territorial arithmetic and role ethics: Wei Xiao holds only two commanderies against the remaining 104, while Yang Guang's public ruler-minister tie and private friendship tie both require frank counsel. Yang Guang does not answer, but the source also says many generals respect Ma Yuan enough to consult him when doubts arise.

Hanji 711 supplies an earlier frontier-resource layer around Jincheng, Poqiang, and Huangzhong. After Ma Cheng captures Hechi and Qiang groups are defeated or relocated, some officials want to abandon territory west of Poqiang. Ma Yuan argues against abandonment because the area has strong towns, fertile irrigable land, and strategic value; if Qiang groups occupy Huangzhong, future harm will be greater.

Liu Xiu adopts Ma Yuan's advice, and the source makes implementation practical rather than rhetorical. Ma Yuan appoints officials, repairs city walls, digs canals, encourages farming and pastoral work, draws back more than 3,000 people, and restores or confirms titles for Qiang groups beyond the frontier. This adds a [[FrontierResourceIntegration|frontier-resource integration]] layer before the later Wudu victory and horsehide-burial profile.

Hanji 714 supplies Ma Yuan's next direct military-governance layer in Jianwu 12 / 36 CE. As Longxi governor, he defeats Canlang Qiang and allied Qiang forces after they attack Wudu, and the episode says more than ten thousand people surrender before Longyou returns to peace. The same source frames his local rule as "恩威并施": routine affairs are trusted to clerks, while the governor intervenes when powerful families bully people or officials break law.

The same episode makes judgment central to Ma Yuan's frontier profile. When a private dispute is misreported as another Qiang revolt and the Didao county magistrate asks to close gates and mobilize troops, Ma Yuan judges that the Qiang will not return and tells the magistrate to stand down. Hanji 714 also foregrounds the "马革裹尸" idiom as Ma Yuan's border-general ethos, while Hanji 722 later supplies the fuller direct speech layer.

Hanji 718 gives Ma Yuan's earliest current late-Guangwu adviser layer. After [[LiuXiu|刘秀]] regrets executing Zhang Ji and more than ten administrators for land-measurement and grain fraud, Ma Yuan says they deserved death because they damaged the people. The same source has Ma Yuan recommend restoring five-zhu coins because they are convenient for ordinary use, and it previews his later 43 CE Jiaozhi campaign against Zheng Ce and Zheng Er.

Hanji 719 gives the command prelude. He and Duan Zhi suppress Li Guang's rebellion around Wancheng, then Liu Xiu appoints Ma Yuan as Fubo General for the campaign against Zheng Ce in [[JiaozhiCommandery|Jiaozhi]]. The source therefore places Ma Yuan inside two simultaneous frontier needs: local rebellion suppression and preparation for the southern expedition.

Hanji 722 supplies the next layer. After returning from Jiaozhi in 44 CE, Ma Yuan hears that [[Xiongnu|Xiongnu]] and [[Wuhuan|Wuhuan]] continue to trouble the northern frontier and wants to request another campaign. He frames proper military death as dying on the border and returning wrapped in horsehide rather than dying in bed. [[LiuXiu|Liu Xiu]] later permits him to campaign and gives him a public ritual sendoff.

The same source also gives Ma Yuan's earliest current warning to Liang Song and Dou Gu. Before departure, he tells the two high-status young men that wealth and rank can return to poverty and low status. In the host's reading, Ma Yuan is acting as an elder who understands class position: staying high requires adopting the conduct, speech restraint, and self-discipline that the position demands.

Hanji 724 supplies the later Wuling campaign-entry layer. After [[WulingManLateHan|Wuling Man]] forces kill Liu Shang and later resist Li Song and Ma Cheng, Ma Yuan asks to go despite being sixty-two. Liu Xiu hesitates over his age, but Ma Yuan puts on armor, mounts a horse, and demonstrates that he can still campaign. Before departure he says he is willing to die for the state and identifies one main danger: powerful young nobles inside the army may be hard to command.

Hanji 725 adds the prehistory of Ma Yuan's court exposure. When Liang Song visits him during illness and bows below the bed, Ma Yuan does not return the salute because he understands Liang as a junior generation despite Liang's status as Liu Xiu's son-in-law. The same source then narrates Ma Yuan's letter to Ma Yan and Ma Dun, warning them against loose talk, criticism of others' faults, and public discussion of current law. [[LongBogaoEasternHan|Long Bogao]] is the safe model because imperfect imitation still leads toward caution; [[DuJiliangEasternHan|Du Jiliang]] is the risky model because failed imitation can become frivolity.

That private letter becomes a public problem. Du Jiliang's enemy uses it to complain to Liu Xiu, and Liang Song and Dou Gu are rebuked because they associate with Du. Hanji 725 therefore turns Ma Yuan's family discipline into a [[PrivateLetterPoliticalWeaponization|private-letter weaponization]] case and explains why Liang and Dou have resentment before Ma Yuan's final crisis.

Hanji 725 and Hanji 726 together place Ma Yuan's final profile in the Wuling campaign. Hanji 725 says he defeats Wuling Man forces at Linxiang and then chooses the shorter Hutou route over [[GengShuEasternHan|Geng Shu]]'s flatter Chongxian route. After the army is blocked by water, high-ground defense, heat, and disease, Ma Yuan himself falls ill but still rises to inspect enemy movements. Geng Shu's complaint reaches court through [[GengYanEasternHan|Geng Yan]], and Liu Xiu sends Liang Song to rebuke and monitor Ma Yuan.

Hanji 726 continues from Ma Yuan's death in camp. Liang Song uses the moment to accuse him; coix seeds brought from Jiaozhi are reframed as supposed treasure, and Liu Xiu angrily seizes Ma Yuan's Xinxihou seal. Ma Yuan's family buries him hastily, binds themselves, pleads at the palace gate, and learns the accusation only when Liu Xiu shows Liang Song's memorial. [[ZhuBoEasternHan|Zhu Bo]] then reframes the case around twenty-two years of service, western and southern campaigns, wounds, epidemic death, and proportional judgment.

Hanji 728 adds a separate remembered function. Ma Yuan warns that [[WangPanEasternHan|Wang Pan]]'s high-profile capital associations are unsafe for a family that should be keeping a low profile, and he separately warns that princes gathering guests after order has stabilized can create disaster. Later punishment of Wang Pan, continued princely access by [[WangSuEasternHan|Wang Su]], and the prince-guest purge make Ma Yuan a diagnostic figure for political risk before punishment arrives.

## Key Characteristics

- Strategic-warning figure whose current sequence begins with old Wei-side insider reporting, anti-Wei planning, cavalry-backed persuasion, and letters into Wei Xiao's camp.
- Border-service ethos figure associated with horsehide-burial language in Hanji 714's idiom framing and Hanji 722's later direct speech.
- Family and elite disciplinarian whose advice teaches speech restraint, careful model selection, and the reversibility of wealth and rank.
- Writer exposed by private-letter circulation when family advice becomes court accusation.
- Victim of reputational damage after death, when alleged treasure baggage overwhelms his ability to answer.
- Family patriarch whose fall produces immediate fear, ritual disorder, and a later palace branch through [[EmpressMaMingdeEasternHan|Mingde Empress Ma]].
- Political-risk diagnostician who reads elite association and prince-guest networks as structurally dangerous.

## Qualifications

This page still does not reconstruct Ma Yuan's complete military biography. Hanji 703 adds early insider-report, anti-Wei strategy, cavalry-persuasion, and Yang Guang letter evidence but leaves Ren Yu, Wang Yuan's advice wording, Wei Xun's exact hostage conditions, unnamed Qiang leaders, and the full route from persuasion to surrender source-scoped. Hanji 704 adds the sharper strategic-warning evidence but leaves Yang Guang's later choice, Niu Han's action, exact letter text, and Wei Xiao-camp internal politics source-scoped. Hanji 711 adds Jincheng/Poqiang/Huangzhong frontier-resource evidence but leaves exact Qiang group identities, settlement geography, and implementation scope source-bounded. Hanji 714 adds Wudu, Qiang, commandery-governance, and idiom-framing evidence but does not make the full "马革裹尸" speech a 36 CE utterance; the direct speech layer remains Hanji 722. Hanji 718 adds adviser, currency, and Jiaozhi outcome notices but compresses the 43 CE military result; the operational campaign remains source-bounded elsewhere. Hanji 719 adds a 41 CE prelude but leaves Li Guang, Duan Zhi, Liu Long, and exact operational geography source-scoped. Hanji 722 adds a northern-frontier and status-warning layer, but the exact garrison location and later tactical outcome remain source-scoped. Hanji 724 adds the Wuling entry point but not the full campaign outcome. Hanji 725 clarifies the resentment and Wuling route prehistory but leaves the exact path by which the letter circulated partly inferred. Hanji 726 explains the posthumous accusation and defense but does not prove every motive behind Liang Song, the Dou-family marriage break, or Liu Xiu's final formal settlement. Hanji 728 supplies remembered warnings after Ma Yuan's death, so its political-risk function should be read as a later narrative use of Ma Yuan, not as the same event sequence as the 49 CE slander case.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 703 as the earliest current strategic-warning layer: old Wei-side reporting, anti-Wei strategy, cavalry-backed persuasion, and hostage-family pressure through Yang Guang.
- Reframed Hanji 704 as the sharper follow-on warning-letter layer: Yang Guang advice, two-commandery arithmetic, and dual public/private duty frame.
- Preserved Hanji 711-714 as the frontier-resource and Wudu/Qiang sequence: Jincheng/Poqiang retention, repair, delegated governance, false-alarm judgment, and horsehide-burial idiom framing.
- Preserved Hanji 718-726 as Ma Yuan's fiscal-punishment, five-zhu coin, Jiaozhi, northern-frontier resolve, Wuling campaign, posthumous accusation, family crisis, and Zhu Bo vindication sequence.
- Preserved Hanji 728 as the later remembered political-risk warning about Wang Pan and princely guest networks.
