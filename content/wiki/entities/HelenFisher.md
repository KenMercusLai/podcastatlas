# Helen Fisher

## Overview

Helen Fisher is the relationship researcher whose work the episode uses to distinguish desire, romantic love, and attachment and to introduce four broad mate-selection types.

## Current Profile

The episode attributes to Fisher a staged distinction among desire, love, and attachment, which often but not invariably appear in that order. It also summarizes dating-site research grouping people into dopamine-, serotonin-, testosterone-, and estrogen-associated types and reporting similarity for the first two pairings and complementarity for the latter two. These categories function here as source-scoped tendencies rather than biological identities or partner-selection rules.

## Key Characteristics

- Associated with separating desire, romantic love, and attachment as related but distinct systems.
- Associated in the episode with “positive delusion” as a bond-supporting valuation of a partner.
- Uses broad neurochemical or hormonal type labels to discuss mate selection.
- Supplies a framework that allows both similarity and complementarity rather than one universal compatibility rule.

## Evidence

- Relationship phases: [[the-science-of-love-desire-and-attachment-scim1112390541]] attributes the desire-love-attachment distinction to Fisher's work.
- Partner valuation: [[the-science-of-love-desire-and-attachment-scim1112390541]] connects her discussion to construing a partner as uniquely meaningful.
- Mate-selection types: [[the-science-of-love-desire-and-attachment-scim1112390541]] summarizes dopamine, serotonin, testosterone, and estrogen types and reported pairing patterns.

## Qualifications

The supplied source does not provide the underlying instruments, samples, effect sizes, replication record, or limits of the four-type framework. Neurochemical labels should not be treated as direct measurements, diagnoses, fixed personalities, gender essences, or prescriptions for choosing a partner.

- Created a source-bounded profile separating Fisher's useful phase distinction from stronger typological claims.
