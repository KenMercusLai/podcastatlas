# Pronoun Trouble

## Overview

*Pronoun Trouble* is [[JohnMcWhorter]]'s book about the histories and controversies attached to English pronouns. The episode describes chapters on “I,” “you,” “he,” “she,” “it,” “we,” and “they,” with “you” as McWhorter's favorite chapter to write.

## Current Profile

The conversation presents the book as a broad language history rather than a work conceived only around current gender debates. Its “you” chapter becomes a compact case of grammatical loss, functional overload, and social repair: the earlier singular/plural distinction disappears, “you” carries singular, plural, and indefinite work, and speakers create regional or informal alternatives.

The book's role in the episode is analytical rather than comprehensive. It supplies the organizing question—how English arrived at its current “you”—but the supplied summary does not cover the other pronoun chapters in enough detail to reconstruct their arguments.

## Key Characteristics

- Organizes English-language history around individual pronouns.
- Connects each pronoun's history with a related controversy.
- Treats “you” as a case of collapsed number distinctions and continuing repair.
- Was conceived as a wider language book rather than solely as a response to transgender-pronoun debate.
- Is represented through one interview focused heavily on a single chapter.
