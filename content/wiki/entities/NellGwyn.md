## Overview

Nell Gwyn was a Restoration actress and mistress of [[CharlesIIOfEngland|Charles II]] remembered for popular wit, Protestant Englishness, and the aristocratic recognition of her sons.

## Current Profile

[[97-top-ten-mistresses-glt8855582591|Top Ten Mistresses]] presents Gwyn as rising from poverty around Drury Lane and Covent Garden through orange selling and the stage into royal intimacy. Her public persona mattered politically: she was framed as an earthy Protestant Londoner in contrast with the king's French Catholic mistress Louise de Kerouaille. Royal favour also produced a durable dynastic outcome because her sons entered aristocratic life, including the line of the dukes of St Albans.

## Key Characteristics

- Working-class London background associated with the Restoration theatre district.
- Orange seller and actress before becoming a royal mistress.
- Popular, witty, and publicly identified with Protestant Englishness.
- Mother whose children by Charles II received aristocratic recognition.
