## Overview
沈阳 / Shenyang is the capital of Liaoning and a Northeast Chinese city appearing through contested Xita memories, layered historical space, deindustrialization, and a dense ecology of street morning markets.

## Current Profile
The sources do not provide a comprehensive city profile. Together they show a city where Qing, colonial, railway, industrial, post-industrial, tourism, and consumer layers remain legible in ordinary routes. Personal memory and historical estrangement make schools, hospitals, bridges, markets, and vanished landmarks newly observable, while morning markets demonstrate that post-layoff work and public services continue through active management rather than simple institutional absence.

## Key Characteristics
- The city is presented through neighborhood memory and ordinary routines rather than only monuments or formal planning.
- Xita and nearby routes reveal overlapping religious, railway, colonial, industrial, and commercial histories.
- Wide, straight roads and reusable spaces make short-duration street markets comparatively workable.
- Morning markets join low-cost food access, self-employment, social recognition, and regional produce.
- Market governance combines temporary closure, stall placement, noise mitigation, cleanup, and strict reopening of shared space.
- Deindustrialization and tourism reshape work and representation without making the city socially or institutionally empty.

## Qualifications
These sources are selective cultural and historical discussions, not a general demographic, economic, or administrative survey. The count of morning markets, comparisons with US industrial cities, and claims about tourism or livestreaming remain source-scoped. Xita, deindustrialization, and morning-market urbanism illuminate interacting but non-identical parts of the city.
