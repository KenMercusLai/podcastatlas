# James Hamilton / Earl of Arran

## Overview

James Hamilton, Earl of Arran, is the heir apparent and regent whose unstable English, Protestant, Catholic, and French alignments expose the constraints of governing for the infant [[MaryQueenOfScots|Mary, Queen of Scots]].

## Current Profile

Arran had the strongest claim if Mary died and secured parliamentary recognition as regent and official heir. He initially negotiated with [[HenryVIII|Henry VIII]], entertained Protestant reform, and signed the [[TreatyOfGreenwich1543|Treaty of Greenwich]], but could not deliver either custody of Mary or Scottish consent to the English marriage.

After [[MaryOfGuise|Mary of Guise]] secured the queen at Stirling, Arran abandoned the English alignment, returned publicly toward Catholicism, and took part in Mary's coronation. The [[RoughWooing|Rough Wooing]] and defeat at [[BattleOfPinkie|Pinkie]] weakened his position further; French intervention preserved Scotland from forced submission and brought him the French title Duke of Châtellerault as part of Mary's betrothal settlement.

## Key Characteristics

- Heir apparent and parliamentary regent during Mary's infancy.
- Flexible factional actor moving among Protestant, English, Catholic, and French positions.
- Signatory unable to make the Treaty of Greenwich politically effective.
- Regent blamed for failure during the Rough Wooing.
- Participant in the French-backed settlement that sent Mary abroad.

The source's description of Arran as slippery and inconstant is a narrative judgment. His private religious convictions, bargaining motives, responsibility for military failure, and freedom under noble and foreign pressure remain source-scoped.

- Established Arran as the regency hinge between the failed English marriage and the successful French settlement.
