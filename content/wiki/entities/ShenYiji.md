## Overview

Shen Yiji / 审食其 appears first as a companion of [[LiuTaigong|刘太公]] and [[LuZhi|吕雉]] captured after the [[PengchengBattle|彭城之战]], later as Biyang Hou, investigator, and palace-centered chancellor, and finally as a politically exposed former chancellor killed by [[LiuChangHuainanKing|淮南王刘长]].

## Current Profile

The source set moves Shen Yiji through four distinct positions. In the Pengcheng aftermath, he belongs to Liu Bang's vulnerable family group and becomes hostage leverage for [[XiangYu|项羽]]. Late in Liu Bang's reign, he holds enough official standing to be sent with [[ZhaoYao|赵尧]] to investigate Lu Wan after the Yan king refuses summons.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-199-zhongguo-lishishang-di-yi-wei-nvhuangdi-bu-shi-wuhou-2-luhqch956l2lwf_n-kfyaseyodpr|Hanji 199 part 2]] connects those stages. It explains Lu Zhi's trust through Shen Yiji's care for her during captivity, then makes him left chancellor after Wang Ling's removal. The episode says he does not conduct ordinary chancellorial business and instead performs palace-facing security, communication, and monitoring work resembling the chamberlain's function. His title, practical remit, and access therefore form a [[NominalOfficePracticalPowerMismatch|名位—实权错位]], while Chen Ping retains the external administrative title but limited freedom.

The same episode places Shen Yiji inside a reported succession-security crisis at Liu Bang's death. Lu Zhi allegedly discusses withholding the death announcement and killing merit ministers with him; Li Shang then reaches Lu Zhi through Shen Yiji and stops the plan. This supports a profile of trusted intermediary access, but not independent proof of the plan, wording, command chain, or Shen Yiji's agreement with every proposed action.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-372-3-xihan-huangdi-dou-you-naxie-nanpengyou-lkfxzzsl2dc-vnaxupjlfn14j6kt|Hanji 372-3]] then adds a dangerous court relationship: it says Liu Ying wants to kill Shen Yiji because of his intimacy with Lu Zhi, but [[PingyuanjunZhuJian|平原君朱建]] works through [[HongRu|闳孺]] to avert the killing. The episode thereby makes Shen Yiji a point where dowager intimacy, imperial anger, and favorite mediation intersect.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-213-gei-liubang-dai-lvmao-de-shenyiqi-xiachang-ruhe-1-loi3nr4qfrtssqayn-8qb7yjx1xx|Hanji 213 part 1]] supplies his death. Liu Chang blames Shen Yiji for failing to secure Lu Zhi's intervention for his imprisoned mother, visits with a concealed hammer, strikes him, and has an attendant behead him. The episode itself argues that [[LiuBang|刘邦]] and Lu Zhi bear more direct responsibility, making Shen Yiji a displaced revenge target. It further speculates that [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]] benefited because Shen Yiji knew politically dangerous details about the accession settlement, but no direct permission or conspiracy evidence is supplied.

## Key Characteristics

- His earliest role is relational and vulnerable rather than independently political: he is captured with Liu Bang's family group.
- His later Biyang Hou status places him inside formal investigation and court service.
- His association with Lu Zhi becomes both a source of proximity and a cause of lethal exposure.
- His left-chancellor title masks a palace-centered practical role, and his intermediary access can transmit both coercive plans and arguments that restrain them.
- He survives the episode's threatened killing through indirect mediation rather than his own demonstrated access to Liu Ying.
- The latest identification is contextually strong but textually qualified because the transcript corrupts his name.
- His death combines private-revenge explanation, selective royal pardon, and an unproven accession-security theory.

## Qualifications

Hanji 199 part 2 writes the name as “沈一基,” and Hanji 372-3 writes “沈逸季”; the wiki normalizes both to Shen Yiji from the Biyang Hou title, hostage biography, cited textual context, and Lu Zhi relationship. Hanji 199's characterization of captivity care, death-concealment plan, purge proposal, Li Shang's intervention, left-chancellor remit, palace monitoring, and ministerial dependence requires comparison with transmitted texts. Hanji 372-3's intimacy claim and Liu Ying motive are not independently verified here. Hanji 213 part 1 supplies a death narrative but not direct evidence that Liu Heng authorized it, that Shen Yiji could reverse the accession settlement, or that ordinary homicide procedure was deliberately bypassed for that purpose. Exact responsibility for Liu Chang's mother's death remains text-dependent.

## What Changed

- Added his left-chancellor title while distinguishing it from his reported palace-facing practical remit.
- Linked his later proximity to Lu Zhi with the captivity-era care offered as its explanation.
- Added his intermediary role in the reported death-concealment and merit-minister purge episode.
