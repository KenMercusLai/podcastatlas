# Harold McGee

## Overview
Harold McGee is a food-science writer and interdisciplinary interpreter presented in the source as connecting chemistry, culinary history, sensory experience, and practical cooking.

## Current Profile
McGee's method begins with curiosity about ordinary kitchen claims. Rather than treating tradition as automatically correct or obsolete, he tests practices such as whipping egg whites in copper and asks what physical or chemical effect could explain the result. He applies the same method to heat, browning, brewing, fermentation, aging, chewing, and the interaction between food and the eater.

His account is deliberately plural. A food's experience depends on ingredients and preparation, but also on sensory biology, prior exposure, expectation, culture, and attention. That makes his food science both mechanistic and humanistic: [[CulinaryFlavorTransformation]] explains how food changes, while [[SensoryPreferencePlasticity]] and [[ContextualFlavorPerception]] explain why eaters do not encounter those changes identically.

## Key Characteristics
- Tests inherited culinary practice against observable effects instead of assuming that tradition or laboratory authority is sufficient.
- Explains cooking through accessible chemistry involving proteins, fats, carbohydrates, volatile compounds, microbes, and extraction.
- Treats eating as a process that continues in the mouth through chewing, saliva, temperature, sequence, and attention.
- Connects sensory mechanisms to cultural learning, history, agriculture, and individual preference.
- Maintains uncertainty around claims that the episode presents as speculative, variable, or unsupported by a clear literature review.
