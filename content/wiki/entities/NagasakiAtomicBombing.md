## Overview

The [[NagasakiAtomicBombing|Nagasaki atomic bombing]] appears as the second atomic-bombing case in a nuclear-harm sequence and as a distinct part of the unresolved dispute over Japan's surrender, wartime necessity, and postwar scientific responsibility.

## Current Profile

Hanji 394-3 says the United States dropped a second atomic bomb on Nagasaki three days after Hiroshima, then gives source-scoped casualty claims before noting the August 15 surrender announcement. [[344-oppenheimer-the-witch-hunt-part-2-glt1594914557]] adds that the larger decision remains disputed and deliberately unresolved, including claims about invasion casualties, rapid war termination, Soviet signaling, and Japan's movement toward surrender.

The current synthesis keeps Nagasaki distinct rather than treating it as a repetition of Hiroshima. Its timing sharpens questions about whether the second use had the same military or political justification, while both bombings shape Oppenheimer's burden and the later memory of nuclear destruction.

- Second atomic-bombing case in the episode's account of Japan's nuclear wartime suffering.

## Qualifications

These sources do not settle the military, diplomatic, ethical, medical, casualty, surrender-timing, or Soviet-signaling debates around Nagasaki. The temporal link to surrender is not by itself proof of a single cause, and the second use requires analysis distinct from Hiroshima.

- Connected Nagasaki to Oppenheimer's postwar responsibility without assigning him final use authority.
