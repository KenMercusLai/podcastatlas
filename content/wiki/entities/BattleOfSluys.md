# Battle of Sluys

## Overview

The Battle of Sluys was Edward III's June 1340 naval victory over an anchored French fleet near [[Flanders]], securing a major early advantage in the [[HundredYearsWar|Hundred Years' War]].

## Current Profile

The episode presents the battle as an improvised combined operation rather than a later-style naval artillery contest. [[EdwardIIIOfEngland|Edward III]] assembled English shipping, embarked longbowmen and men-at-arms, and attacked French ships chained into defensive lines. English missile fire supported boarding actions, while Flemish allies struck from the rear.

The victory destroyed or captured much of the French fleet, curtailed the immediate landing threat to [[England]], and restored Edward's prestige. It did not resolve his larger strategic problem: debt and continental deadlock continued despite the Channel success.

## Key Characteristics

- June 1340 fleet battle near the Flemish port of Sluys.
- English use of merchant shipping as platforms for archers and boarding infantry.
- French chained defensive lines that became vulnerable to coordinated attack.
- Flemish intervention against the French rear.
- Major prestige and maritime-security gain without a corresponding fiscal solution.

## Evidence

- Force design: [[319-hundred-years-war-triumph-of-the-longbow-part-2-glt3100937638]] describes Edward filling gathered ships with longbowmen and men-at-arms.
- Battle mechanism: [[319-hundred-years-war-triumph-of-the-longbow-part-2-glt3100937638]] links English missile advantage, boarding, and a Flemish rear attack to French collapse.
- Strategic effect: [[319-hundred-years-war-triumph-of-the-longbow-part-2-glt3100937638]] treats Sluys as securing the Channel and repairing Edward's prestige while leaving his finances strained.

## Qualifications

The episode's description of total victory and control of the Channel is a strategic summary, not proof that later French maritime activity became impossible. Exact fleet numbers, losses, and tactical sequencing are not independently audited here.

## What Changed

- Created Sluys as the maritime-security victory connecting Flanders, English archery, and Edward's continental strategy.
