# Leonard Cohen / 莱昂纳德·科恩

## Overview

Leonard Cohen is a Canadian poet-musician represented both as a cultural entry point into [[Canada]] and as the songwriter whose prolonged composition and continued revision supplied the evolving material for [[HallelujahSong|“Hallelujah.”]]

## Current Profile

Cohen's profile joins cultural perception with creative process. [[YanFeiSociologist|颜飞]] invokes his lyric about light entering through cracks to make Canada legible through poetry, music, and personal attachment rather than scenery alone. This remains a source-scoped cultural use rather than a full account of Cohen's Canadian identity or career.

The creative-process evidence centers on “Hallelujah.” Cohen reportedly worked on it for years, generated many verse variants, recorded a poorly received version, and continued changing the song in live performance. He then supplied extensive lyric material to [[JohnCale]], whose selection and arrangement became the basis for [[JeffBuckley]]'s later interpretation.

Together, the sources present Cohen through both perception and process: a voice through which others approach place and a creator willing to leave a work open to further discovery. Neither source supports a comprehensive biography, and the song's later form cannot be attributed to Cohen alone without erasing its interpretation chain.

## Key Characteristics

- Combines poetic and musical identity in the sources' cultural framing.
- Serves as a personal, affective entry into Canada rather than a generic national symbol.
- Is represented as composing through prolonged dissatisfaction and high-volume revision.
- Continues revising after recording rather than treating release as the end of the work.
- Makes later reinterpretation possible by sharing a larger body of material with another musician.

## Qualifications

Verse counts, years of labor, label decisions, motives, and the relative contribution of each later interpreter remain source-scoped.
