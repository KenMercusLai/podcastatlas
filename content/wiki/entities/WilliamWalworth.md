# William Walworth

## Overview

William Walworth was the mayor of London whose advocacy of armed action and fatal confrontation with [[WatTyler|Wat Tyler]] made him a decisive loyalist actor in the suppression of the [[PeasantsRevolt1381|Peasants’ Revolt of 1381]].

## Current Profile

Walworth first appears urging force while other royal advisers warned that the government lacked the men to avoid a bloodbath. At Smithfield he tried to arrest Tyler after an exchange of insults; when Tyler drew a dagger, Walworth struck him. Tyler was later removed from St Bartholomew’s Hospital and beheaded.

The blow did not itself settle the crisis. Rebel archers prepared to shoot, and [[RichardIIOfEngland|Richard II]] rode forward to claim their allegiance. Loyal city forces were then mustered and surrounded the crowd at Clerkenwell. Walworth therefore represents the coercive side of the settlement, paired with rather than substituted for the king’s performance of authority.

## Key Characteristics

- London mayor committed earlier than cautious advisers to armed suppression.
- Civic official who attempted Tyler’s arrest and struck the fatal initiating blow.
- Loyalist actor whose violence nearly triggered immediate confrontation with rebel archers.
- Participant in a settlement requiring both royal charisma and mobilized city force.

## Evidence

- Strategic stance: [[414-the-peasants-revolt-londons-burning-part-2-glt6726049231]] contrasts Walworth’s demand for armed action with Salisbury’s warning about inadequate force.
- Smithfield confrontation: [[414-the-peasants-revolt-londons-burning-part-2-glt6726049231]] narrates the arrest attempt, dagger, and blow against Tyler.
- Suppression: [[414-the-peasants-revolt-londons-burning-part-2-glt6726049231]] links the royal party and city muster to the rebels’ encirclement and surrender.

## Qualifications

The source does not establish whether Tyler’s death was spontaneous, anticipated, or organized. Walworth’s action is therefore presented as a decisive event with disputed planning, not as proof of a complete prior suppression plan.

## What Changed

- Established Walworth’s coercive intervention as the trigger for Richard’s complementary act of royal crisis management.

## Relationships

- [[WatTyler]] - rebel leader he attempted to arrest and struck at Smithfield.
- [[RichardIIOfEngland]] - king whose personal intervention contained the danger created by Tyler’s fall.
- [[PeasantsRevolt1381]] - uprising he urged the government to suppress by force.
- [[PopularRevoltCoalition]] - heterogeneous movement whose scale made his preferred armed response dangerous.
