# J. L. Moreno

## Overview

Jacob Levy Moreno was a psychiatrist and psychodrama pioneer whose collaboration with [[HelenHallJennings|Helen Hall Jennings]] at the [[NewYorkStateTrainingSchoolForGirls|New York State Training School for Girls]] helped establish sociometry and social network analysis.

## Current Profile

[[acting-out]] presents Moreno as an ambitious theorist seeking a scientific level between individual psychoanalysis and population averages. Psychodrama supplied one bridge: spontaneous enactment made roles and group interaction observable, while the Hudson study translated interpersonal preference into questionnaires, observation, and visual maps.

The study was both innovative and ethically incomplete. Moreno and Jennings reportedly mapped an entire residential community, interpreted a cluster of escapes as network transmission, and used relationship data to reorganize cottages. Yet Moreno's published account acknowledged segregation without substantially analyzing corporal punishment, racialized labor, abuse, or the institution's power over the residents. The episode therefore treats his achievement and blind spot as inseparable rather than canceling either one.

## Key Characteristics

- Psychiatrist who developed psychodrama as an enacted alternative to exclusively verbal analysis.
- Sought to measure the interpersonal field between individual psychology and aggregate social statistics.
- Supplied the expansive theoretical vision behind the Hudson sociometric study.
- Used sociograms to make large quantities of relational data visually intelligible.
- Helped institutionalize sociometry while leaving the school's coercive racial order underexamined.

## Evidence

### Methodological innovation

- [[acting-out]] traces Moreno's path from psychodrama to preference surveys, observation, sociograms, public presentation, and the 1934 publication *Who Shall Survive?*

### Network intervention

- [[acting-out]] attributes to Moreno and Jennings the network-contagion interpretation of the 1932 escapes and the reported post-reorganization decline in runaways.

### Institutional blind spot

- [[acting-out]] contrasts the relational detail of the maps with Moreno's limited treatment of segregation, forced labor, punishment, and abuse.

## Qualifications

The source is a critical narrative summary, not Moreno's complete work, Jennings's archive, the underlying data, or a history of social network analysis. Its judgments about Moreno's ambition, desire for institutional standing, and reasons for omission are interpretations. The claimed intervention effect is self-reported and does not isolate alternative causes, while the study's blind spots do not make every relational observation false.

## What Changed

- Created a profile joining Moreno's methodological importance to the institutional realities his published model did not adequately represent.
- Distinguished observed network structure from a sufficient causal account of escape.
