## Overview

胶西王刘昂 / Liu Ang was a Western Han vassal king presented in the current source as a defeated participant in the [[RebellionOfTheSevenStatesWesternHan|Rebellion of the Seven States]] whose military surrender, legal defense, and suicide make him a central case of postwar royal accountability.

## Current Profile

After returning to Jiaoxi with defeated troops, Liu Ang reportedly strips to the waist, sleeps on a mat, drinks water, and apologizes to his mother in a ritualized display of guilt. His heir Liu De proposes attacking the tired departing Han army and, if necessary, escaping to an island, but Liu Ang judges that his remnant force cannot recover the war.

When [[HanTuidangWesternHan|韩颓当]] offers surrender under imperial authority, Liu Ang argues that the kings rose only to kill Chao Cuo for altering Gaozu's laws and reducing royal lands, and that they withdrew after Chao's death. Han answers that this does not explain their failure to petition the emperor, their mobilization without an edict or tiger tally, or their attack on a neighboring kingdom that refused rebellion. Liu Ang then hears the imperial order, accepts that he deserves death, and dies by suicide; the source also reports the deaths of his mother and heir.

## Key Characteristics

- Jiaoxi king and defeated participant in the Seven States coalition.
- Ruler who rejects a final high-risk attack because he recognizes the collapse of his military position.
- Defendant who presents “诛晁错” and withdrawal after Chao's death as a limited-purpose justification.
- Royal actor whose defense fails when tested against authorization, petition procedure, and attacks on nonparticipants.
- Prince whose surrender ends in suicide and reported destruction of his immediate family branch.

## Qualifications

This profile rests on one short popular-history episode. The penitential ritual, Liu De's proposal, surrender terms, dialogue, imperial order, legal categories, sequence and manner of death, and fate of the royal family require comparison with transmitted records. The supplied text alternates “焦西” and 胶西; the canonical name is normalized to 胶西 from the surrounding historical context.

## What Changed

- Established Liu Ang as the episode's principal defeated royal defendant.
- Separated his declared anti-Chao purpose from the conduct and authorization questions used to reject it.
