## Overview

李敢 / Li Gan enters the wiki as [[LiGuangHanGeneral|李广]]'s surviving son, a decorated Western Han officer whose battlefield reputation is cut short by an unresolved elite grievance. The source moves him from courage against [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] to an assault on [[WeiQingHanGeneral|卫青]] after Li Guang's death and then to his own killing by [[HuoQubing|霍去病]].

## Current Profile

The battlefield setting behind the small mounted probe places Li Guang's force under encirclement and visible fear. Li Gan leads several dozen riders through the opposing formation and returns without reported loss, then tells the troops that the enemy is manageable. The action functions as reconnaissance and a costly morale signal rather than proof that the numerical danger was small.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-335-dangzhe-wudi-de-mian-huoqubing-weihe-gan-sha-liguang-zhi-zi-lupxhu64fi0d0xl223zq9pwenmy1|Hanji 335]] presents Li Gan as both a promising commander and an actor who bypasses procedure. He reportedly penetrates a Xiongnu formation with a small mounted party, later campaigns under Huo Qubing, captures a Left Wise King's drum and banner, and gains a marquisate. After blaming Wei Qing for the inquiry that precedes Li Guang's suicide, he attacks Wei; Wei conceals the incident. Huo later shoots Li Gan during an imperial hunt, and [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] protects Huo through a deer-accident account.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-336-2-huoqubing-yunluo-wudi-zhen-shi-muhou-heishou-ltfkwalc48bj1hav65bg-8qotrf5|Hanji 336-2]] repeats that sequence but shifts attention to its possible political aftermath. The host argues that killing a prominent military-family member may have cost Huo Qubing support and increased imperial suspicion. Those effects are not independently documented and do not revise the narrower established profile of Li Gan as both assailant and victim of procedureless retaliation.

## Key Characteristics

- Surviving son of Li Guang and bearer of the family's military reputation.
- Daring cavalry officer presented as capable of restoring troop confidence under pressure.
- Decorated participant in Huo Qubing's anti-Xiongnu campaign and a newly enfeoffed commander.
- Grieving son who converts blame for Li Guang's death into a physical assault on Wei Qing.
- Victim of procedureless retaliation and favorite-protected non-punishment at the imperial court.

- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-335-dangzhe-wudi-de-mian-huoqubing-weihe-gan-sha-liguang-zhi-zi-lupxhu64fi0d0xl223zq9pwenmy1|Hanji 335]] says Li Gan rides into a much larger Xiongnu force with a small party, returns to reassure Li Guang's troops, and later wins battlefield merit and a marquisate under Huo Qubing.

Grievance and assault:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-335-dangzhe-wudi-de-mian-huoqubing-weihe-gan-sha-liguang-zhi-zi-lupxhu64fi0d0xl223zq9pwenmy1|Hanji 335]] says Li Gan blames Wei Qing after Li Guang's suicide and assaults him, after which Wei conceals the event.

Killing and protection:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-335-dangzhe-wudi-de-mian-huoqubing-weihe-gan-sha-liguang-zhi-zi-lupxhu64fi0d0xl223zq9pwenmy1|Hanji 335]] says Huo Qubing shoots Li Gan during a Ganquan Palace hunt and Wudi attributes the death to a deer collision.

## Qualifications

This profile rests on three compact, overlapping podcast retellings rather than a full military or legal biography. Hanji 308's rider count, route through the opposing formation, absence of losses, dialogue, and morale effect remain episode-attributed, and the probe occurs in an earlier campaign than Li Guang's final Mobei failure. The exact battlefield totals, sequence of Li Guang's final campaign, nature of Li Gan's assault, Huo Qubing's motive, shooting mechanics, degree of imperial foreknowledge, and effect on Huo's reputation remain source-scoped. Calling the death retaliation or a turning point is interpretation, not a separately demonstrated finding.

## What Changed

- Added the source-scoped claim that Li Gan's killing may have damaged Huo Qubing politically, without treating that consequence as established.
- Added the encirclement context that makes Li Gan's mounted probe both reconnaissance and morale signaling.
