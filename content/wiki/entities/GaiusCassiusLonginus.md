# Gaius Cassius Longinus / 卡西乌斯

## Overview

Gaius Cassius Longinus / 卡西乌斯 is a principal organizer of the [[CaesarAssassination|Caesar conspiracy]] and later eastern commander whose defeat and premature suicide at [[BattleOfPhilippi|Philippi]] help destroy the republican military coalition.

## Current Profile

The Caesar source presents Cassius as an active opponent of Caesar's dictatorship who understands that the plot requires [[MarcusBrutus]]'s symbolic authority. He embodies its practical organization—recruitment, timing, secrecy, and public framing—while remaining morally mixed through ambition, rivalry, and fear.

[[305-the-fall-of-the-roman-republic-glt6995332578]] extends that organizing capacity into civil war. Cassius and Brutus raise money and approximately twenty legions in the East, add cavalry and mounted archers, and establish a strong position near Philippi. Antony defeats Cassius's wing while Brutus succeeds against Octavian's. Before learning of that success, Cassius kills himself; the episode treats the information failure as a potentially decisive turn in the campaign.

## Key Characteristics

- Practical organizer who gives operational structure to the anti-Caesar plot.
- Depends on Brutus's republican name to legitimate the conspiracy publicly.
- Converts exile and provincial access into a large eastern army and supply position.
- Shares responsibility for coercion and atrocity within the liberator coalition.
- Dies after local defeat without knowing that Brutus has won elsewhere.

## Evidence

- Conspiracy role: [[124-kaisa-zhisi-lishi-de-yunjiao-shifou-yi-shixiao-781894361]] presents Cassius as early organizer and recruiter who needs Brutus's authority.
- Eastern mobilization: [[305-the-fall-of-the-roman-republic-glt6995332578]] describes fundraising, troop formation, cavalry, archers, and the fortified Philippi position.
- Defeat and death: [[305-the-fall-of-the-roman-republic-glt6995332578]] says Antony defeats Cassius's forces and Cassius dies before receiving accurate news of Brutus's wing.

## Qualifications

The current sources do not provide a full biography or independently settle Cassius's motives. Troop totals, the scale of liberator atrocities, battlefield communications, and the counterfactual claim that his suicide decided the war remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Added the information failure surrounding his suicide as a qualified possible turning point.
