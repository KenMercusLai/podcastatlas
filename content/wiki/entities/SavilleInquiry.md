# Saville Inquiry

## Overview
The Saville Inquiry appears in the episode as the later official investigation that reframed [[BloodySunday]] and enabled [[DavidCameron]]'s 15 June 2010 apology.

## Current Profile
Within the wiki's current evidence, Saville is the inquiry that rejects the central exculpatory claims around Bloody Sunday. The episode contrasts it with the earlier [[WidgeryInquiry]] and says Saville found no supporting evidence that soldiers had faced sniper fire or that the victims were armed. Its importance is not only legal; it changes how state responsibility is publicly acknowledged decades after the killings.

## Key Characteristics
- Later official inquiry into Bloody Sunday.
- Corrective counterpoint to the earlier Widgery account.
- Evidentiary basis for Cameron's public apology in 2010.
- Institutional mechanism for reassessing state violence and official narrative.
- Key source frame for the episode's moral judgment on Bloody Sunday.

## Evidence
- Cameron frame: [[700-the-troubles-bloody-sunday-part-4-glt3909829334]] opens with Cameron's 15 June 2010 statement after the Saville report.
- Rejection of sniper claim: [[700-the-troubles-bloody-sunday-part-4-glt3909829334]] says Saville found no witnesses, guns, or bullets supporting the paratroopers' sniper-fire account.
- Victim status: [[700-the-troubles-bloody-sunday-part-4-glt3909829334]] treats the victims as unarmed civilians and uses Saville to support that reassessment.
- Widgery contrast: [[700-the-troubles-bloody-sunday-part-4-glt3909829334]] contrasts Saville with an earlier inquiry that had largely exonerated the paratroopers.

## Qualifications
