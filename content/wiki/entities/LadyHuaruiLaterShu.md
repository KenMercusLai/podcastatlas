## Overview

花蕊夫人 / Lady Huarui (Later Shu) is the disambiguated Later Shu woman whom the supplied episode identifies as a consort of [[MengChangLaterShu|孟昶]] and later a favored woman in [[ZhaoKuangyin|赵匡胤]]'s palace. The page keeps that identification separate because the episode itself notes that multiple women across the Five Dynasties and Ten Kingdoms period bore the title 花蕊夫人.

## Current Profile

Her current wiki profile joins three kinds of memory without treating them as equally verified: a conquest-era palace transfer, the alleged “乾德四年” mirror that exposes an era-name collision, and literary or aesthetic traditions about poetry and flowers. She functions as the narrative carrier of an object between courts, but the episode does not establish the mirror's provenance or resolve the competing identities attached to the 花蕊夫人 title.

## Key Characteristics

- Later Shu consort identified by the episode with the title 花蕊夫人.
- Reported owner of the mirror that revealed an earlier 乾德 era date.
- Conquest-era figure said to have moved from Meng Chang's household into Zhao Kuangyin's palace.
- Poet associated with numerous palace poems, peonies, and red gardenias.
- Identity requiring explicit separation from other women also called 花蕊夫人.

### Literary and aesthetic memory

### Identity ambiguity

- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-301-tai-liqi-jiemi-nianhao-zui-da-wulong-shijian-lrsbd_9zfkcofnosolvrjvfcdfyg|Hanji 301]] distinguishes at least three women called 花蕊夫人, making the title insufficient by itself for identity resolution.

## Qualifications

- The current profile is bounded to one podcast summary and is not a verified biography or literary catalog.
- Her personal name, precise status under Meng Chang and Zhao Kuangyin, mirror ownership, poem corpus, preferences, and later fate remain source-scoped.
- The episode's claims that she resembled Li Yu's empress, that Zhao Kuangyin had a romantic connection with that woman, and that resemblance explained his favor are court anecdotes and are not adopted as established fact.
- Her alleged neglect after [[SongEmpress|宋皇后]] entered the palace does not by itself establish the chronology or cause of her later fate.

## What Changed

- Added a disambiguated Later Shu profile rather than merging all women titled 花蕊夫人.
- Separated the mirror narrative, palace-transfer claim, and literary memory by evidence type.
