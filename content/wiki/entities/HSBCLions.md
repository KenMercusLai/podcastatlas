## Overview

The HSBC lions / 汇丰铜狮 are paired reclining male-lion sculptures first commissioned for [[HSBC]]'s Shanghai headquarters and later copied for Hong Kong and other HSBC buildings.

## Current Profile

[[gkwriaiorn5rawbazwtstr0k-gkwriaiorn5rawbazwtstr0k|Vol.278]] presents the lions as a corporate image that escaped its original setting. The Shanghai pair arrived in 1923 and were named Stephen and Stitt after HSBC managers; a segmented cast produced a related Hong Kong pair in 1935. Wartime removal, postwar recovery, preservation of the Shanghai originals, later replicas, banknote circulation, and feng-shui-inflected installation ceremonies turned one architectural commission into a recognizable global emblem.

The afterlife is not simple continuity. Commercial stone copies often change material, orientation, sex attribution, and meaning. A pair of reclining male lions can become a supposed male-female “招财/守财” set sold as a generic bank ornament, showing how brand recognition can persist while historical attribution disappears.

## Key Characteristics

- Originated as two reclining male bronze lions for HSBC's 1923 Shanghai headquarters.
- The open- and closed-mouth figures were named Stephen and Stitt and positioned facing one another rather than frontally guarding a doorway.
- The 1935 Hong Kong pair and later global versions made replication part of HSBC's corporate visual system.
- Banknote imagery expanded recognition beyond visitors to bank buildings.
- Commercial copies frequently alter pose, placement, gender story, material, and auspicious explanation.

- [[gkwriaiorn5rawbazwtstr0k-gkwriaiorn5rawbazwtstr0k|Vol.278]] dates the Shanghai commission to the early 1920s, distinguishes the later Hong Kong cast, and describes their different wartime preservation histories.

### Corporate circulation

- [[gkwriaiorn5rawbazwtstr0k-gkwriaiorn5rawbazwtstr0k|Vol.278]] connects the lions to HSBC buildings, Hong Kong banknotes, installation rituals, and a corporate reading of the closed-mouth Stitt as stable.

### Commercial reinterpretation

- [[gkwriaiorn5rawbazwtstr0k-gkwriaiorn5rawbazwtstr0k|Vol.278]] reports “港币狮” and “汇丰狮” as trade names and documents copies that abandon the original orientation or invent male-female and wealth-attraction meanings.

## Qualifications

The source is a structured podcast summary, not a catalogue raisonné or archival study. Exact design influence, casting details, naming history, banknote chronology, ritual practice, copy lineage, and workshop geography remain source-scoped. Similar reclining lions at another bank are evidence of resemblance, not proof of direct authorization by HSBC.

## What Changed

- Established the original Shanghai commission and later Hong Kong/global replication as one connected corporate-symbol history.
- Distinguished the HSBC pair from conventional Chinese guardian-lion pose and gender conventions.
- Added banknotes, commercial copying, and auspicious reinterpretation as mechanisms of diffusion.
