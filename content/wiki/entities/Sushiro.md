# Sushiro / 寿司郎

## Overview
Sushiro is a Japanese conveyor-belt sushi chain discussed in relation to China expansion, seafood supply chains, standardized localization, and a first U.S. store planned for New York.

## Current Profile
Earlier sources use Sushiro to show how Japanese restaurant chains carry standardized operations into China while adapting menus, mall sites, and seafood supply chains. [[daisen-jinru-diandong-yashua-lingyu-chuantong-jindian-jiameng-chixu-shousuo-1010976825]] adds a U.S. expansion test: the chain plans an October New York opening with three floors, 170 seats, local items such as cheese shrimp rolls, and no tipping. The current profile is therefore a global-chain localization case, not only a China-market case.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses conveyor-belt sushi operations with standardized workflows and consumer-facing variety.
- Adapts overseas menus and formats to local tastes while retaining a Japanese chain identity.
- Relies on seafood and fishmeal-linked supply chains that can shape cost and sourcing.
- Uses dense urban sites and mall-like dining environments for overseas expansion.
- Tests U.S. entry through a large New York flagship and a no-tipping model.
- Reflects Japan-market maturity pressure by seeking growth abroad.
