## Overview

Snorri Sturluson was a medieval Icelandic author and political actor associated with the [[ProseEdda|Prose Edda]] and kings' saga writing.

## Current Profile

[[103-the-norse-sagas-glt8280648151]] presents Snorri as the likely author of the Prose Edda, a handbook preserving mythological knowledge needed by poets, and as a participant in the volatile 13th-century political world in which Norwegian royal power increasingly constrained Icelandic independence. His authorship and eventual killing make literary preservation inseparable from Christian-era politics and institutional conflict.

## Key Characteristics

- Likely author of the Prose Edda.
- Associated with kings' saga composition as well as poetic instruction.
- Preserved older mythological reference through a Christian medieval framework.
- Participated in the 13th-century conflict surrounding Iceland and Norwegian power.

## Evidence

- Literary role: [[103-the-norse-sagas-glt8280648151]] identifies Snorri as the likely Prose Edda author and associates him with kings' sagas.
- Preservation function: [[103-the-norse-sagas-glt8280648151]] describes the Prose Edda as a guide for poets who needed old mythological references.
- Political context: [[103-the-norse-sagas-glt8280648151]] places Snorri within civil conflict and notes that he was killed during the period.
