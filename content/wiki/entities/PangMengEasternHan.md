## Overview
Pang Meng is presented as a previously trusted Guangwu-era subordinate whose rebellion grows from unverified suspicion rather than from a long public opposition. His case turns ambiguous command communication into open defection, making him a compact example of how personal trust can fail when status signals are interpreted as threats.

## Current Profile
In [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-693-panchu-liuxiu-de-pengchong-ruhe-yin-beipan-er-wang-lqler4fu9wqztsrgzsxhs1519nm]], Pang Meng had been regarded by [[LiuXiu|Liu Xiu]] as modest, reliable, and exceptional among trusted officials. During operations against Dong Xian, however, an imperial command is summarized as naming Gai Yan rather than Pang Meng. Pang Meng interprets the omission as evidence that Liu Xiu and Gai Yan have turned against him, does not clarify the matter, attacks Gai Yan, joins Dong Xian, and calls himself king of Dongping.

The rebellion quickly becomes a test of loyalty around Pengcheng. Pang Meng captures Pengcheng and seeks to kill Chu commandery's Sun Meng, but [[LiuPingEasternHan|Liu Ping]] physically shields Sun Meng, asks to die in his place, and later revives Sun Meng by giving him blood from his wounds. Liu Xiu reacts to Pang Meng's revolt with explicit anger and admits that he had badly misjudged him.

## Key Characteristics
- Formerly trusted subordinate: Pang Meng's rebellion is striking because Liu Xiu had publicly treated him as unusually reliable.
- Suspicion-driven rebel: his revolt begins from interpreting an ambiguous omission in an edict as a sign of imminent danger.
- Non-communicating defector: the source emphasizes that he does not first verify Liu Xiu's intent.
- Opportunistic title claimant: after joining Dong Xian, he claims kingship as king of Dongping.
- Catalyst for loyalty display: his violence against Sun Meng creates the setting for Liu Ping's self-substituting protection.

- Suspicion mechanism: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-693-panchu-liuxiu-de-pengchong-ruhe-yin-beipan-er-wang-lqler4fu9wqztsrgzsxhs1519nm]] connects Pang Meng's rebellion to his reading of an omitted command signal as distrust.

## Qualifications
- The page follows the episode's normalized identity as 庞萌 / Pang Meng; the transcript form "庞蒙" is treated as an episode-level variant.
- The exact form of Gai Yan's name in the transcript remains source-scoped because the strategic role is clear but the transcription is unstable.
- Current evidence explains the rebellion through suspicion, but it does not reconstruct Pang Meng's whole career or every military step.
