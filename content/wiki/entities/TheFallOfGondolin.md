# The Fall of Gondolin

## Overview

*The Fall of Gondolin* is an early [[JRRTolkien|Tolkien]] legendarium story, written in 1917, in which a hidden city is destroyed by an assault involving mechanical dragons.

## Current Profile

[[225-j-r-r-tolkien-glt9725776786]] places the work inside Tolkien's wartime formation. He began writing mythic material during illness and military postings, and the episode treats Gondolin's doomed city, machinery, terror, and despair as imaginative transformations made under the pressure of the First World War.

The story is also evidence that Tolkien's larger invented past preceded [[TheHobbit]]. Its mythic scale grew through language, names, fragments, and northern literary inheritance rather than being retrofitted only after the later book became successful.

## Key Characteristics

- Major early story in Tolkien's developing legendarium.
- Doomed-city narrative written during the First World War.
- Combines mythic warfare with destructive machinery and mechanical dragons.
- Evidence that Tolkien's invented history predates *The Hobbit*.

## Evidence

- Wartime composition: [[225-j-r-r-tolkien-glt9725776786]] dates the story to 1917 and places it among writings begun during illness and postings.
- Form and imagery: [[225-j-r-r-tolkien-glt9725776786]] emphasizes the doomed city and mechanical dragons as part of the episode's war interpretation.
- Legendarium sequence: [[225-j-r-r-tolkien-glt9725776786]] connects the story to the earlier Earendel fragment and later mythology-building project.

## Qualifications

The source offers a biographical-historical reading rather than a textual history of the story's many versions. Its connection between mechanized war and mechanical dragons is plausible but interpretive, not proof that battlefield experience supplies a complete causal explanation.

## What Changed

- Established the story as an early wartime stage of Tolkien's legendarium.
- Qualified the link between wartime machinery and fictional mechanical dragons.
