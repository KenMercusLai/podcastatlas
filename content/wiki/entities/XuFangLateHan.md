## Overview

徐防 / Xu Fang is an Eastern Han senior official whose current wiki role centers on [[Taixue|Taixue]] examination reform and high-office movement during the transition from [[EmperorHeOfHan|Emperor He]] to [[EmpressDowagerDengLateHan|Deng]]'s regency. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-776-hanshi-tanmi-cailun-shi-zenyang-faming-zaozhishu-de-ljqhhcywxr-yggzmcgzbbtg-q-hb|Hanji 776]] makes him a classical-testing reformer before later sources place him inside Deng-era staffing and disaster blame.

## Current Profile

Hanji 776 says Xu Fang is appointed sikong in 102 CE and quickly memorializes on Taixue testing. He criticizes doctoral disciples for departing from recognized scholarly lineages, relying on personal opinion, tolerating one another's errors, and fighting over interpretations during examinations. His proposed repair is to test against explicit classical passages and fixed interpretive points, rewarding source-grounded explanation and marking unsupported personal conflict as wrong.

The later sources make his high office part of the regency transition. Hanji 778 names Xu Fang among Deng's opening appointments, while Hanji 780 says he is removed as taiwei under combined disaster, omen, rebellion, and flood pressure. Xu Fang therefore appears as both an institutional repairer and a blame-bearing senior minister.

## Key Characteristics

- Senior Eastern Han official active across the Emperor He and Deng-regency transition.
- Taixue examination reformer who favors explicit textual grounding over free-form personal interpretation.
- High-office holder whose roles move through sikong, situ, and later taiwei notices in the current sources.
- Example of how education repair and court accountability can be attached to the same ministerial career.

- He-era office movement: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-776-hanshi-tanmi-cailun-shi-zenyang-faming-zaozhishu-de-ljqhhcywxr-yggzmcgzbbtg-q-hb|Hanji 776]] records his 102 sikong appointment and later movement to situ in 104 CE.

## Qualifications

The current evidence does not reconstruct Xu Fang's whole career or settle every office title in a full chronology. It supports a narrower profile: a senior official whose Taixue reform shows classical examination standardization, and whose later Deng-regency office notices show how senior ministers could be used for staffing and blame under crisis.

## What Changed

- Created Xu Fang as the canonical page for the 102 CE Taixue examination reform.
- Connected that reform to his later Deng-regency staffing and removal notices without treating those notices as a full biography.
