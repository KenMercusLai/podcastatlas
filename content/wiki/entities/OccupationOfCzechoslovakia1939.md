# Occupation of Czechoslovakia (1939)

## Overview

The March 1939 destruction of the remaining Czechoslovak state was [[NaziGermany|Nazi Germany]]'s openly imperial move beyond the post-Munich annexation of German-majority territory, producing a protectorate in Bohemia and Moravia and a dependent Slovak state.

## Current Profile

After Munich, Hitler ordered planning to liquidate what remained of Czechoslovakia and sought its industry, arms, gold, foreign exchange, and strategic position. Germany exploited conflict between Prague and Slovak leaders: [[JozefTiso|Jozef Tiso]] was pressed to declare independence under German protection, while Hungary took Subcarpathian Ruthenia.

President [[EmilHacha|Emil Hácha]] was summoned to Berlin and threatened with invasion and the bombing of Prague. He accepted German protection under coercion shortly before German forces crossed the border. The resulting Protectorate of Bohemia and Moravia ended meaningful Czech sovereignty without the ethnic self-determination pretext used for Austria or the Sudetenland.

That break mattered beyond the territory seized. Prague exposed the limits of Hitler's previous assurances, unsettled Germans who did not understand the absorption of a non-German population, and pushed Britain and France toward the [[BritishFrenchGuaranteeToPoland|guarantee of Poland]].

## Key Characteristics

- Planned liquidation of the post-Munich Czechoslovak state.
- Use of Slovak separatism, Hungarian pressure, ultimatum, and military threat.
- Coerced submission of the Czech president before a pre-scheduled occupation.
- Seizure of industrial, financial, military, and strategic assets.
- Creation of the Protectorate of Bohemia and Moravia and a dependent Slovak state.
- Diplomatic turning point that discredited the remaining self-determination case for Nazi expansion.

## Evidence

- Preparation and motive: [[530-hitlers-war-on-poland-countdown-to-armageddon-part-1-glt8685711342]] connects Hitler's post-Munich order to industrial capacity, arms, currency, gold, pride, and racial-imperial ambition.
- State dismemberment: [[530-hitlers-war-on-poland-countdown-to-armageddon-part-1-glt8685711342]] describes pressure on Tiso, threats against Hácha, the unopposed German entry, and the territorial settlement imposed afterward.
- Diplomatic effect: [[530-hitlers-war-on-poland-countdown-to-armageddon-part-1-glt8685711342]] presents Prague as a shift in German, British, and French perceptions because conquest could no longer be defended as gathering Germans into the Reich.

## Qualifications

This profile rests on one narrative podcast note rather than a complete Czech, Slovak, Hungarian, German, British, or French history. The coercive setting is clear in the source, but exact threats, dialogue, asset valuations, popular attitudes, and the relative weight of economic, racial, strategic, and personal motives remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Established Prague's occupation as the bridge from Munich-era revisionism to open imperial conquest.
- Connected Czechoslovakia's destruction to the later attempt to deter an attack on Poland.
