# Ed Koch

## Overview

Ed Koch was the New York politician whose 1977 mayoral rise represented a turn toward tougher crime, union, budget, and development politics.

## Current Profile

[[360-fear-city-new-york-in-the-1970s-glt7107047696]] presents Koch as a response to exhaustion with disorder and fiscal paralysis. He described himself as liberal but sane while supporting harder crime policy, the death penalty, confrontation with unions, budget restraint, higher subway fares, and greater room for private development. In the episode's chronology, Koch is less a complete break than the political consolidation of changes already forced by the Beame-era crisis.

## Key Characteristics

- He displaced [[AbrahamBeame]] as the Democratic mayoral candidate after the blackout.
- He combined a liberal identity with a conspicuously tougher law-and-order posture.
- His program favored budget restraint and confrontation with public-sector unions.
- He accepted higher user costs, including subway fare increases, as part of fiscal repair.
- His administration gave private developers a larger role in the city's recovery model.
