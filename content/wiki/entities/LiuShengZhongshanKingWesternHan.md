## Overview

中山王刘胜 / Liu Sheng is presented in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-343-1-ta-yisheng-huangyin-jiaoshe-houren-que-dingli-mobai-lnspg6ektohor19yhtcqulhl4cvt|Hanji 343-1]] as a Western Han vassal king remembered for immersion in wine and sex, a forty-one-year reign, and an exceptionally large number of children.

## Current Profile

The episode's most useful contribution is not a settled claim about Liu Sheng's inner motives but a contrast between reputation and political effect. The host argues that a pleasure-centered, noncompetitive public image may have made Liu Sheng appear harmless to [[HanWudi|汉武帝]], allowing him to avoid the danger that ambition could bring to a vassal king. That reading fits [[SelfDeprecatingPoliticalSurvival|自污式政治自保]], but remains an attributed hypothesis because the source provides no direct evidence that Liu Sheng deliberately constructed the image.

The biographical frame is similarly bounded by the episode: Liu Sheng is said to have been enfeoffed at twelve, ruled for forty-one years, died at fifty-three, and had more than 120 children according to the *Hanshu*. These details establish the profile supplied by the episode, not an independently verified full biography.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han vassal king associated in the episode with a long and politically stable tenure.
- Historically characterized as immersed in wine and sex rather than active in central power competition.
- Interpreted by the host as possibly using visible indulgence and nonambition to reduce imperial suspicion.
- Reported to have been enfeoffed at twelve, reigned for forty-one years, and died at fifty-three.
- Credited by the episode's reading of the *Hanshu* with more than 120 children.

## Qualifications

The current profile is grounded in one short, structured podcast summary. It does not reproduce the relevant *Hanshu* passage, establish that the negative characterization originated there in precisely this form, or demonstrate deliberate strategic intent. Long survival and visible indulgence are compatible with the host's explanation, but they do not by themselves prove it; pleasure, privilege, later moral framing, and political signaling could overlap.
