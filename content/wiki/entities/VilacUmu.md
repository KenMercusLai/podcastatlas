# Vilac Umu

## Overview

Vilac Umu was an Inca high priest and resistance organizer who left Almagro's Chile expedition, urged Manco Inca to reject Spanish domination, and helped prepare the 1536 uprising.

## Current Profile

[[648-the-fall-of-the-incas-battle-for-the-sacred-city-part-5-glt6392035011]] places Vilac Umu at the junction of religious authority, imperial communication, and military resistance. After accompanying [[DiegoDeAlmagro]] south with forces supplied by [[MancoInca]], he deserts the brutal expedition, returns to Cusco, and presses Manco to resist.

He then helps prepare weapons, food, and messages carried through the khipu-based network. His role shows that the uprising drew on surviving Inca institutions rather than only on Manco's personal grievance.

## Key Characteristics

- High-ranking Inca religious authority.
- Participant in and deserter from Almagro's Chile expedition.
- Advocate of resistance after witnessing Spanish treatment of Indigenous porters.
- Organizer of weapons, supplies, and communications for Manco's revolt.
- Link between ritual legitimacy and practical mobilization.

## Evidence

- Chile expedition: [[648-the-fall-of-the-incas-battle-for-the-sacred-city-part-5-glt6392035011]] follows Vilac Umu south and back to Cusco after forced porterage and hardship.
- Resistance advocacy: [[648-the-fall-of-the-incas-battle-for-the-sacred-city-part-5-glt6392035011]] identifies him as an important voice urging Manco to act.
- Mobilization: [[648-the-fall-of-the-incas-battle-for-the-sacred-city-part-5-glt6392035011]] links him to stockpiling and khipu-carried messages before the uprising.

## Qualifications

The episode supplies only a compressed profile, and its reconstruction depends heavily on later narrative sources. His exact authority, route, reasons for desertion, and division of responsibility with Manco remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Established Vilac Umu as a key institutional organizer rather than only a religious symbol.
- Connected the Chile expedition's coercion to the preparation of revolt.
