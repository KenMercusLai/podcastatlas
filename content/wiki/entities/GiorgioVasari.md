# Giorgio Vasari

## Overview
Giorgio Vasari appears in [[667-the-mystery-of-the-mona-lisa-glt1735270133]] as the early biographical witness who identified [[MonaLisa|the Mona Lisa]]'s sitter as [[LisaGherardini|Lisa]], wife of [[FrancescoDelGiocondo]].

## Current Profile
The episode treats Vasari's account as important but historically tested rather than simply accepted. His claim is strengthened by the source's argument that he lived near the Giocondo family while Lisa was still alive and may have drawn on local or family memory. The 2005 Heidelberg discovery then makes his identification look much more reliable.

## Key Characteristics
- Vasari is the early source for the Lisa del Giocondo identification.
- His account connects La Gioconda to cheerfulness or joy.
- He supplied the colorful detail that musicians and jesters kept Lisa merry while Leonardo painted her.
- His proximity to the Giocondo family makes local-memory transmission plausible.
- The Heidelberg evidence shifts his testimony from contested tradition toward corroborated identification.

## Evidence
- Identification - [[667-the-mystery-of-the-mona-lisa-glt1735270133]] says Vasari named Lisa, wife of Francesco del Giocondo, as the sitter.
- Narrative detail - [[667-the-mystery-of-the-mona-lisa-glt1735270133]] reports Vasari's claim about singers, musicians, and jesters.
- Corroboration - [[667-the-mystery-of-the-mona-lisa-glt1735270133]] says Agostino Vespucci's 1503 note mentioning Leonardo and Lisa del Giocondo proves Vasari was right on the central identification.

## Qualifications
The source does not treat every Vasari anecdote as equally certain. The episode uses later archival evidence to distinguish the sitter claim from more decorative narrative details.

## What Changed
- Created Vasari as the source-history node for the Mona Lisa identification.
