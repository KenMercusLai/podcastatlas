# Champ de Mars Massacre

## Overview

The Champ de Mars massacre was the 17 July 1791 armed dispersal of a republican petition gathering by Paris authorities and the National Guard, marking a decisive rupture between revolutionary government and revolutionary crowds.

## Current Profile

The event followed the Assembly's decision to retain [[LouisXVI|Louis XVI]] after [[FlightToVarennes|Varennes]] and the [[CordeliersClub|Cordeliers]]' call for a republican petition. A prior lynching, terrorist rumors about two men hidden beneath the altar, anticipated confrontation, thrown stones, and an unidentified shot preceded National Guard fire and a cavalry charge. [[JeanSylvainBailly|Bailly]], [[MarquisDeLafayette|Lafayette]], and the [[FeuillantClub|Feuillants]] treated the outcome as suppression of disorder; radicals treated revolutionary violence against revolutionaries as evidence of elite betrayal.

## Key Characteristics

- Republican petition mobilization produced by the post-Varennes legitimacy crisis.
- Mixed gathering that included political militants and families on a Sunday outing.
- Escalation shaped by rumor, prior lynching, armed expectation, thrown objects, and gunfire.
- Use of martial law, National Guard volleys, and cavalry against a revolutionary crowd.
- Wide and unresolved disparity in reported death totals.
- Immediate repression through a retroactive anti-riot decree and mass arrests.
- Political break that strengthened constitutional moderates temporarily while deepening radical suspicion.

## Qualifications

- The crowd estimate, death toll, responsibility for the first decisive shot, and precise command chain remain source-scoped or contested.
- Calling the gathering either wholly peaceful or simply insurrectionary flattens its mixed composition and the escalation before the shooting.
- The episode's likely voyeurism explanation for the two hidden men is a historical judgment, not established intent.

## What Changed

- Adds the missing 1791 rupture linking Varennes to later revolutionary repression and factional polarization.
- Distinguishes the massacre's immediate disorder-control rationale from its longer legitimacy cost.
