# William Shakespeare / 威廉·莎士比亚

## Overview

William Shakespeare / 威廉·莎士比亚 appears in the wiki as a dramatist whose work reshapes literary interpretation, public memory, political analogy, English-language culture, and live theatre practice.

## Current Profile

The complete evidence set shows Shakespeare working through transformative recombination rather than isolated invention. [[JuliusCaesarPlay|Julius Caesar]], the sonnets, English history plays, and [[MacbethPlay|Macbeth]] take inherited reports, chronicles, genres, and legends and give them memorable language, dramatic structure, ambiguity, and performance life.

The Globe-centered source grounds this achievement in a commercial mixed-audience theatre. The Macbeth source adds the Jacobean political layer: royal patronage, Banquo's favorable recasting, post-[[GunpowderPlot]] equivocation, and James's witchcraft concerns show how immediate power can shape adaptation without exhausting a play's later meaning.

## Key Characteristics

- Shakespeare's scenes and phrases can become stronger than uncertain historical record in public memory.
- His writing sustains rereading through ambiguity, wordplay, rhythm, allusion, and performance.
- His theatre belongs to commercial mixed-audience culture as much as to high literary culture.
- His originality lies partly in transforming inherited stories rather than inventing plots from nothing.
- His political drama absorbs legitimacy, regicide, patronage, conspiracy, and national feeling.
- His influence travels through print, school, film, stage practice, idiom, and cross-cultural adaptation.

## Qualifications

The sources do not provide a comprehensive biography. Author identity, coauthorship, precise influence from court events, individual idiom claims, historical-play accuracy, and the extent of political intention remain qualified. A court-facing adaptation can also exceed patronage and propaganda through later performance and interpretation.
