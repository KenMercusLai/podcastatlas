# Mayerling Incident

## Overview

The Mayerling Incident is the January 1889 death of [[CrownPrinceRudolf]] and [[MaryVetsera]] at Rudolf's hunting lodge.

## Current Profile

The Mayerling sequence treats the incident as a private catastrophe that is intelligible only after a long prehistory. Part 1 opens with the locked room and then backfills Rudolf's family, imperial, Viennese, marital, political, and illness context before Mary's first direct access to him. Part 2 follows the affair into death-pact language, the January 1889 deaths, and the struggle over burial, public reporting, Mary's erasure, and Habsburg succession.

## Key Characteristics

- Mayerling is framed as both gothic melodrama and court-political crisis.
- The prehistory connects the event to [[DynasticHeirRoleEntrapment]], [[AustroHungarianDualMonarchy]], and [[FinDeSiecleViennaCrisisReading]].
- The immediate event is reconstructed through hunting routines, hidden presence, valet discovery, and reported gunshot wounds.
- The aftermath centers on Catholic burial for Rudolf and suppression of Mary.
- The episode treats conspiracy theories as evidence of unsettled memory rather than as established explanation.
- The succession consequences push the imperial line toward [[KarlLudwigOfAustria]] and [[FranzFerdinand]].

## Qualifications

The sources reconstruct a famous scandal from letters, testimony, newspapers, funeral ritual, and retrospective accounts. They explicitly keep coercion, consent, psychological causation, embellishment, and conspiracy claims uncertain.
