# Michael Thaddeus

## Overview

Michael Thaddeus is the [[ColumbiaUniversity]] mathematics professor whose independent analysis of the university's submissions to [[USNewsAndWorldReport|U.S. News and World Report]] anchors the episode's ranking-data case.

## Current Profile

Thaddeus begins from a plausibility problem: Columbia's rise to a tie for second seemed difficult to reconcile with differences in endowment and physical space and with his own experience of class sizes. He then turns that suspicion into an audit, extracting enrollment information for roughly 30,000 classes and comparing public or official records across several ranking inputs.

The source presents his strongest role as methodological rather than merely accusatory. He identifies discrepancies in multiple independently checkable measures, publishes charts and computer-based findings, and later challenges the specificity of Columbia's account of its corrections. The supplied summary does not reproduce his dataset or establish who created the disputed figures.

## Key Characteristics

- Uses public and institutional records to test high-stakes self-reported metrics.
- Moves from a plausibility concern to a reproducible quantitative comparison.
- Distinguishes observable discrepancies from unresolved intent and responsibility.
- Continues scrutinizing whether later institutional corrections identify what changed and over what period.
