## Overview

孔臧 / Kong Zang is a Western Han scholar-official whom the current source presents as declining appointment as Yushi Dafu in favor of the Taichang office, where classical learning, ritual, education, and work on the Kong family tradition better matched his stated purpose.

## Current Profile

Hanji 285 introduces Kong Zang after the execution of [[ZhuFuYan|主父偃]], using him as a contrast in office choice rather than as a participant in that case. When [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] plans to appoint him Yushi Dafu, Kong says that his specialization lies in the Confucian classics and asks instead to serve as Taichang. The emperor accepts and grants him honors presented as comparable in weight to those of the highest ministers.

The episode explains the fit institutionally. Taichang manages ancestral-temple ritual and also oversees cultural and educational functions, including doctors and the imperial academy. Kong Zang's choice is therefore presented as alignment between knowledge, family tradition, and office rather than simple refusal of status.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han scholar-official associated with the Kong lineage and classical learning.
- Declines the Yushi Dafu appointment in the episode's account.
- Requests the Taichang office as a closer fit for ritual and scholarly work.
- Plans to work with his cousin Kong Anguo on received ancient teaching and the Kong family tradition.
- Represents expertise-aligned office choice rather than maximal pursuit of nominal rank.

## Evidence

Office choice and scholarly aim:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-285-ta-bangzhu-hanwudi-da-yitong-weihe-fan-bei-miezu-lrmpubvjtwsqiujvii6wjj32e8ea|Hanji 285]] says Kong Zang declines Yushi Dafu, requests Taichang, and explains the choice through his study of the Five Classics and desire to organize earlier teaching.

Institutional fit and reward:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-285-ta-bangzhu-hanwudi-da-yitong-weihe-fan-bei-miezu-lrmpubvjtwsqiujvii6wjj32e8ea|Hanji 285]] describes Taichang as responsible for ancestral ritual and for educational institutions, and says Wudi accepts the request while granting unusually weighty honors.

## Qualifications

The profile is bounded to one compact popular-history source. It does not independently establish Kong Zang's genealogy, the exact relationship to Kong Anguo, his full writings, the wording of the exchange, the office chronology, or every jurisdiction of Taichang. The source's claim that Confucian elevation made the post uniquely advantageous for the Kong family is an interpretive institutional fit, not proof of private motive.
