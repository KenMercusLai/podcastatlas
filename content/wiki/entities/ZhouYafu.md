## Overview

周亚夫 / Zhou Yafu appears in the current wiki through two distinct source-scoped roles: a minister-general whose rise and death fulfill [[XuFuPhysiognomist|许负]]'s prediction narrative, and a superior who reportedly recognizes that [[ZhaoYuWesternHan|赵禹]]'s cleanliness and competence do not by themselves make his severity safe for major office.

## Current Profile

The current profile joins a narrated life arc to a bounded personnel judgment. Xu Fu is said to predict that Zhou will inherit a marquis title, become a major military and political figure, and finally die of hunger; the story fulfills those stages through his brother's loss of the title, Zhou's rise around the Seven States crisis, and imprisonment followed by refusal of food after his son's burial-armor purchase triggers a rebellion accusation.

As a superior, Zhou reportedly refuses to treat colleagues' praise of Zhao Yu's cleanliness and fairness as sufficient evidence of office fitness. He sees strict textual legality and harsh treatment of self and others as reasons not to entrust Zhao with important responsibility. This presents Zhou as separating probity from discretionary fitness, though the evidence does not reconstruct his broader administrative practice.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han marquis and major military-political figure in the Xu Fu prediction narrative.
- Initially skeptical that either inheritance or death by hunger could fit his circumstances.
- Narratively linked to the Seven States crisis and later imprisonment and self-starvation.
- Presented in Hanji 347-1 as able to distinguish clean reputation from fitness for complex office.
- Cautious about harsh, strictly textual legal judgment when it lacks flexibility.

## Qualifications

The prophecy material is preserved as the episode's narrative, not evidence that physiognomy reliably predicts lives. Hanji 347-1 transcribes Zhou's name as “周雅夫”; this page normalizes it to 周亚夫 from context. The source supports a narrow personnel judgment about Zhao Yu, not a general claim that Zhou Yafu always governed flexibly or opposed strict law.
