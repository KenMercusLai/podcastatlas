## Overview

周亚夫 / Zhou Yafu appears in the current wiki through three source-scoped roles: a minister-general whose rise and death fulfill [[XuFuPhysiognomist|许负]]'s prediction narrative, a superior who reportedly recognizes that [[ZhaoYuWesternHan|赵禹]]'s cleanliness and competence do not by themselves make his severity safe for major office, and a dismissed chancellor whose unresolved meal-without-chopsticks setup marks worsening relations with [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]].

## Current Profile

The current profile joins a narrated life arc to a bounded personnel judgment. Xu Fu is said to predict that Zhou will inherit a marquis title, become a major military and political figure, and finally die of hunger; the story fulfills those stages through his brother's loss of the title, Zhou's rise around the Seven States crisis, and imprisonment followed by refusal of food after his son's burial-armor purchase triggers a rebellion accusation.

As a superior, Zhou reportedly refuses to treat colleagues' praise of Zhao Yu's cleanliness and fairness as sufficient evidence of office fitness. He sees strict textual legality and harsh treatment of self and others as reasons not to entrust Zhao with important responsibility. This presents Zhou as separating probity from discretionary fitness, though the evidence does not reconstruct his broader administrative practice.

Hanji 244 adds only the beginning of Zhou's final conflict. The episode says he had entered Jingdi's “psychological blacklist,” claimed illness, and was removed as chancellor; it previews a banquet where meat is served without chopsticks as a further political test. Because the supplied episode stops before the scene is completed, the page does not infer the emperor's motive, Zhou's full response, or a direct causal path from the meal to his later death.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han marquis and major military-political figure in the Xu Fu prediction narrative.
- Initially skeptical that either inheritance or death by hunger could fit his circumstances.
- Narratively linked to the Seven States crisis and later imprisonment and self-starvation.
- Presented in Hanji 347-1 as able to distinguish clean reputation from fitness for complex office.
- Cautious about harsh, strictly textual legal judgment when it lacks flexibility.
- Removed from the chancellorship after claiming illness in Hanji 244's incomplete setup to the chopstick-banquet episode.

## Qualifications

The prophecy material is preserved as the episode's narrative, not evidence that physiognomy reliably predicts lives. Hanji 347-1 transcribes Zhou's name as “周雅夫”; this page normalizes it to 周亚夫 from context. That source supports a narrow personnel judgment about Zhao Yu, not a general claim that Zhou always governed flexibly or opposed strict law. Hanji 244's “psychological blacklist” and chopstick-to-killing language are the host's interpretation, and the transcript cutoff prevents the banquet claim from being evaluated within this source.
