## Overview

刘杨 / Liu Yang, Zhending King is the Hebei ally and Guo-family maternal-uncle figure whose support helps explain [[GuoShengtong|郭圣通]]'s marriage to [[LiuXiu|刘秀]] in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-719-guangwudi-liuxiu-suanbusuan-zhanan-lk8eme3wwyk5qkjjfxkp0zsc77ix|Hanji 719]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-686-liuxiu-fashi-zhi-ai-yinlihua-weihe-li-guoshi-wei-hou-lnqlqgq54srb1zzzgemfwuwujhvc|Hanji 686]] adds an earlier recall layer: when Guo is made empress in 26 CE, the source identifies her as Liu Yang's niece and says Liu Yang had already died in a prior Liu Xiu-linked consolidation field.

## Current Profile

Hanji 719 presents Liu Yang as a Western Han royal descendant and Guo Shengtong's maternal uncle. When Liu Xiu is isolated and under pressure from the [[WangLangHebeiClaimant|王郎]] crisis in Hebei, Liu Yang's alliance offers military value. The marriage to Guo is therefore framed as a political alliance rather than only a romantic or household event.

The episode's later point is depreciation. Once Liu Xiu's position strengthens and the founding coalition is no longer dependent on Liu Yang's backing, Guo's alliance value declines. Liu Yang remains important in the current wiki as the person who makes Guo's original palace status politically legible.

Hanji 686 adds that Liu Yang is still relevant to the 26 CE empress decision as Guo's kinship background. The source recalls him as the already-dead Liu Yang indirectly linked to Liu Xiu's prior consolidation violence, while Guo is nevertheless made empress and her son Liu Qiang crown prince. The page keeps that death context source-scoped because Hanji 686 does not explain the full incident.

## Key Characteristics

- Hebei royal-descendant ally in Liu Xiu's weak founding moment.
- Maternal uncle of Guo Shengtong in the source's alliance explanation.
- Supplies the political-military context for Guo's marriage to Liu Xiu.
- Already-dead kinship background figure recalled when Guo becomes empress in 26 CE.
- Becomes an example of a founding alliance whose value declines after consolidation.
- Disambiguated from Liu Yang / Liu Zhuang / Mingdi and from the Zhending place route.

## Qualifications

This page is deliberately narrow. It does not reconstruct Liu Yang's full rebellion history, offices, death, or lineage beyond what the current source notes require. Hanji 686 recalls his death relationship to Liu Xiu without narrating the incident, so the page preserves it only as source-scoped context. The route includes Zhending and Eastern Han suffixes to avoid collision with Liu Yang / Liu Zhuang and with other Liu Yang figures.
