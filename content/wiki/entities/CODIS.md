# CODIS

## Overview
CODIS is the FBI-linked national criminal DNA database discussed in [[tech-20260831-0831-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260831-0831-mp-tech-pod-128]]. The episode uses it to show how DNA collected from immigration or federal detainees can become part of future policing searches.

## Current Profile
In this source, CODIS is not only a forensic database for criminal investigations. It becomes the durable infrastructure through which [[USDepartmentOfHomelandSecurity|DHS]] and [[USImmigrationAndCustomsEnforcement|ICE]] detainee samples can persist after detention, be searched by law enforcement, and affect biological relatives through [[GeneticLineupPrivacyRisk]].

## Key Characteristics
- National criminal-policing DNA database linked in the source to the [[FederalBureauOfInvestigation|FBI]].
- Long-running database with roughly 28 million profiles accumulated over about 25 years, according to the episode.
- Rapidly expanded by about 3.3 million DHS-submitted profiles in recent years.
- Retention appears indefinite to Georgetown's center, with no regular review-and-removal process identified in the episode.
- Family-exposure risk arises because DNA profiles can implicate biological relatives who were never detained.

## Evidence
- Scale and growth - [[tech-20260831-0831-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260831-0831-mp-tech-pod-128]] says CODIS has roughly 28 million total profiles and that DHS has added about 3.3 million in recent years.
- Retention concern - [[tech-20260831-0831-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260831-0831-mp-tech-pod-128]] attributes the indefinite-retention concern to [[StevieGleiberson]] and Georgetown's center.
- Policing use - [[tech-20260831-0831-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260831-0831-mp-tech-pod-128]] says CODIS is searched in criminal investigations by policing agencies from federal to local levels.
- Family exposure - [[tech-20260831-0831-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260831-0831-mp-tech-pod-128]] links CODIS inclusion to Gleiberson's "perpetual genetic lineup" warning.

## Qualifications
The episode does not include CODIS administrators' explanation of profile categories, expungement rules, or safeguards. Database scale, projected share, and removal-process claims should remain tied to the Georgetown-centered source until later sources refine them.

## What Changed
- Initial profile establishes CODIS as the database infrastructure that turns DHS/ICE DNA collection into a long-term policing issue.
