## Overview

夜郎 / Ye Lang is a southwestern polity whose profile now spans [[TangMengWesternHan|唐蒙]]'s early attempt to open it under [[YelangKingDuotongWesternHan|多同]], its reported post-Nanyue submission and royal recognition under Han Wudi, and a later 27 BCE crisis in which [[YelangKingXingWesternHan|兴]] challenges Han authority before [[ChenLiWesternHan|陈立]] kills him and suppresses the remaining resistance.

## Current Profile

Hanji 269-1 supplies the earliest current layer. Shu sauce reaching Nanyue through merchants reportedly reveals Ye Lang's place in an indirect trade and river network. Tang Meng proposes using that corridor, Han prestige, Ba-Shu resources, and local manpower to approach Nanyue and establish officials. Duo Tong and the chiefs accept gifts and a provisional arrangement while expecting distance and terrain to prevent complete Han possession.

Hanji 355 supplies a later incorporation layer. After Nanyue falls, the episode says the Ye Lang ruler travels to Chang'an, receives recognition as king, and enters a broader sequence of southwestern submissions and commandery creation. Neither source establishes that the earlier bargain or later recognition erased local autonomy or produced continuous, uniform control.

The later sources present Ye Lang as the largest local power around [[ZhangkeCommanderyWesternHan|牂柯郡]] and as part of a mixed frontier world where Han commandery authority, local polities, and neighboring rulers coexist uneasily.

Ye Lang's political importance in this source comes from escalation. Its ruler does not merely fight neighbors; he rejects Han mediation, stages wooden images of Han officials as archery targets, and gives [[DuQingWesternHan|杜清]] reason to warn that the polity could consolidate nearby forces before attacking Zhangke.

Hanji 513 turns that risk forecast into an outcome. Chen Li kills Xing in a carefully staged meeting, which causes many surrounding rulers and troops to surrender, yet Ye Lang does not vanish instantly: Xing's father-in-law Wengzhi and son Yewu gather remnants and coerce twenty-two villages before Chen Li breaks the remaining force through grain, water, and counterintelligence pressure.

## Key Characteristics

- Southwestern polity tied in the episodes to present-day Guizhou.
- Trade and river-network node that becomes a possible northwestern approach to Nanyue.
- Site of Tang Meng's gift-backed, chief-mediated, and explicitly conditional agreement under Duo Tong.
- Dependent ruler reportedly recognized by Han Wudi after Nanyue's fall.
- Largest local power around the Zhangke frontier setting described by the source.
- Fights Gouding and Louwo before the conflict threatens Han commandery territory.
- Later becomes dangerous because local war can become coordinated resistance once Han authority intervenes.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 269-1, Hanji 355, Hanji 512, and Hanji 513 and does not reconstruct Ye Lang's full political history, geography, archaeology, or degree of autonomy. The sources give widely separated moments, not a continuous chronology, full map, or institutional account. The earliest source's sauce route, river name, manpower figures, gift effects, chiefs' motives, and claim of peaceful expansion remain episode-attributed; commodity circulation and provisional agreement do not prove direct trade, uniform submission, or durable administration.

## What Changed

- Added the pre-conquest Tang Meng layer: commodity-based route inference, river strategy, gift-backed negotiation, and conditional acceptance under Duo Tong.
- Distinguished that initial bargain from post-Nanyue royal recognition and the much later Xing crisis.
- Strengthened the judgment that recognition and commandery proximity did not erase local political capacity.
