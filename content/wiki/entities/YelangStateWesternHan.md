## Overview

夜郎 / Ye Lang is a southwestern polity whose profile spans [[TangMengWesternHan|唐蒙]]'s negotiated opening under [[YelangKingDuotongWesternHan|多同]], revolt against coercive Han road construction, reported de-escalation by [[SimaXiangru|司马相如]], post-Nanyue royal recognition, and a later 27 BCE crisis in which [[YelangKingXingWesternHan|兴]] challenges Han authority before [[ChenLiWesternHan|陈立]] kills him and suppresses the remaining resistance.

## Current Profile

Hanji 269-1 supplies the earliest current layer. Shu sauce reaching Nanyue through merchants reportedly reveals Ye Lang's place in an indirect trade and river network. Tang Meng proposes using that corridor, Han prestige, Ba-Shu resources, and local manpower to approach Nanyue and establish officials. Duo Tong and the chiefs accept gifts and a provisional arrangement while expecting distance and terrain to prevent complete Han possession.

Hanji 269-2 shows how quickly the initial arrangement can reverse. Compulsory road labor, deaths, flight, and executions reportedly turn local fear into armed resistance. Wudi then sends Sima Xiangru rather than a mass punitive force; the episode credits proclamation, meetings, and persuasion with calming the crisis before further western groups request Han officials and the frontier infrastructure expands.

Hanji 355 supplies a later incorporation layer. After Nanyue falls, the episode says the Ye Lang ruler travels to Chang'an, receives recognition as king, and enters a broader sequence of southwestern submissions and commandery creation. None of these episodes establishes that negotiation, pacification, or later recognition erased local autonomy or produced continuous, uniform control.

The later sources present Ye Lang as the largest local power around [[ZhangkeCommanderyWesternHan|牂柯郡]] and as part of a mixed frontier world where Han commandery authority, local polities, and neighboring rulers coexist uneasily.

Ye Lang's political importance in this source comes from escalation. Its ruler does not merely fight neighbors; he rejects Han mediation, stages wooden images of Han officials as archery targets, and gives [[DuQingWesternHan|杜清]] reason to warn that the polity could consolidate nearby forces before attacking Zhangke.

Hanji 513 turns that risk forecast into an outcome. Chen Li kills Xing in a carefully staged meeting, which causes many surrounding rulers and troops to surrender, yet Ye Lang does not vanish instantly: Xing's father-in-law Wengzhi and son Yewu gather remnants and coerce twenty-two villages before Chen Li breaks the remaining force through grain, water, and counterintelligence pressure.

## Key Characteristics

- Southwestern polity tied in the episodes to present-day Guizhou.
- Trade and river-network node that becomes a possible northwestern approach to Nanyue.
- Site of Tang Meng's gift-backed, chief-mediated, and explicitly conditional agreement under Duo Tong.
- Community whose reported resistance links compulsory construction, mortality, flight punishment, and failed frontier legitimacy.
- Site where the episode credits Sima Xiangru's proclamation and elite communication with de-escalation.
- Dependent ruler reportedly recognized by Han Wudi after Nanyue's fall.
- Later becomes dangerous because local war can become coordinated resistance once Han authority intervenes.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 269-1, Hanji 269-2, Hanji 355, Hanji 512, and Hanji 513 and does not reconstruct Ye Lang's full political history, geography, archaeology, or degree of autonomy. The sources give selected and widely separated moments, not a continuous chronology, full map, or institutional account. The sauce route, river and road names, manpower and mortality figures, gift effects, chiefs' motives, uprising sequence, and claimed effect of Sima's proclamation remain episode-attributed. Commodity circulation, provisional agreement, short-term calm, and royal recognition do not prove direct trade, uniform submission, or durable administration.

## What Changed

- Added the immediate reversal from conditional agreement to revolt under coercive road labor and military-law enforcement.
- Added Sima Xiangru's reported proclamation-and-persuasion de-escalation without treating it as proof of durable consent.
- Strengthened the judgment that access, pacification, royal recognition, and commandery proximity are distinct layers of control.
