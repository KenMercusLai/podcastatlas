# Leonardo da Vinci

## Overview
Leonardo da Vinci appears in [[667-the-mystery-of-the-mona-lisa-glt1735270133]] as the painter of [[MonaLisa|the Mona Lisa]] and as the Renaissance genius whose reputation helped turn one portrait into a global cultural object. The episode also emphasizes that "da Vinci" means "from Vinci" rather than functioning as a modern surname.

## Current Profile
The source presents Leonardo as the illegitimate son of a Florentine notary, an early apprentice and major Florentine talent, and an artist-engineer who spent roughly eighteen years with Ludovico Sforza in Milan. By 1500 he had returned to Florence after Sforza's fall, wealthy but needing patrons and carrying a reputation for beginning projects without finishing them.

## Key Characteristics
- Leonardo is framed as a major Renaissance artist-engineer with Florentine and Milanese career phases.
- His reputation for genius, mystery, incompletion, and experiment is central to the Mona Lisa's later status.
- His handling of sfumato and lifelike portraiture gives the Mona Lisa its core visual ambiguity.
- His retention of the Mona Lisa is presented as historically unresolved.
- His move to France under Francis I explains why the painting entered French royal and museum collections.

## Evidence
- Career setting - [[667-the-mystery-of-the-mona-lisa-glt1735270133]] describes Leonardo's Florentine origin, apprenticeship, Milanese service under Ludovico Sforza, and return to Florence after political upheaval.
- Reputation and style - [[667-the-mystery-of-the-mona-lisa-glt1735270133]] links Leonardo to genius, unfinished projects, sfumato, lifelike portraiture, and an invented landscape.
- Mona Lisa custody - [[667-the-mystery-of-the-mona-lisa-glt1735270133]] says it remains unclear why Leonardo kept the painting or why no payment record survives.
- France connection - [[667-the-mystery-of-the-mona-lisa-glt1735270133]] says Leonardo died in France in 1519 and that Francis I bought the Mona Lisa.

## Qualifications
The source is focused on the Mona Lisa, not a full Leonardo biography. Claims about his career, patrons, and working habits are included only as they explain the painting's creation and later fame.
