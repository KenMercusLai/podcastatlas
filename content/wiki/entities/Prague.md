# Prague / 布拉格

## Overview

Prague is the Czech capital whose wiki profile combines concrete geographical identity with a layered urban record of medieval state building, religious conflict, imperial rule, twentieth-century repression, democratic transition, literature, and everyday travel.

## Current Profile

In the Kundera source, Prague matters because confusing it with Budapest, Poland, or a generic Eastern Europe exposes the gap between performed solidarity and concrete knowledge. The city represents a specific Central European history tied to university life, [[JanHus|Jan Hus]], 1968 repression, exile, and Czech refusal to be dissolved into careless geopolitical shorthand.

The travel source makes that specificity spatial. [[CharlesIV|Charles IV's]] university, New Town, bridge, and cathedral projects establish a fourteenth-century layer; Old Town Square connects Hus, astronomical time, Hussite memory, and the executions following the Bohemian revolt; Wenceslas Square carries both the scars of the [[PragueSpring|1968 invasion]] and the mass politics of the [[VelvetRevolution|1989 transition]]. St. Vitus Cathedral's centuries of interruption and completion, later bridge sculpture, Golden Lane, Kafka memory, cafés, pubs, and mixed architectural styles show a city built by accumulation rather than a single preserved age.

## Key Characteristics

- Concrete Central European city whose identity is obscured by careless East-European substitution.
- Imperial and educational center strongly shaped by Charles IV's fourteenth-century program.
- Urban landscape where Hus, Hussite conflict, Bohemian revolt, and later religious-political change remain visible.
- Site where 1968 repression and 1989 peaceful transition are remembered in public space.
- Multi-period built environment combining Romanesque, Gothic, Baroque, Art Nouveau, Cubist, and later work.
- Literary and social city associated in the source with Kafka, cafés, beer, bridges, markets, and public gathering.

## Qualifications

Both sources are interpretive: one reads Prague through a novel and the other through a personal itinerary. Monument dates, superlatives, architectural attribution, reported damage, and historical episodes remain episode-scoped unless independently sourced. Tourism visibility also privileges the old center and famous sites over housing, labor, migration, infrastructure, suburbs, and contemporary inequality.
