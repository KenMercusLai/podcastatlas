# Great Western Schism

## Overview

The Great Western Schism was the late-14th-century division in which rival popes and obediences claimed authority from Rome and Avignon.

## Current Profile

[[356-the-blood-drinking-bride-of-christ-glt8496536394]] introduces the schism through the aftermath of [[GregoryXI|Gregory XI]]'s return to Rome and death. The cardinals' contested succession produced rival popes, and [[CatherineOfSiena|Catherine]] backed the Roman claimant [[UrbanVI|Urban VI]], showing how charismatic religious authority could be recruited into an institutional legitimacy struggle.

## Key Characteristics

- Emerged after the papacy's return from Avignon and Gregory XI's death.
- Divided western Christian allegiance between rival papal claimants.
- Converted questions of election and location into a wider legitimacy crisis.
- Drew Catherine's mystical reputation into active papal politics.

## Evidence

- Succession sequence: [[356-the-blood-drinking-bride-of-christ-glt8496536394]] connects Gregory's death, cardinal conflict, and rival popes.
- Catherine's alignment: [[356-the-blood-drinking-bride-of-christ-glt8496536394]] records her support for Urban and address to cardinals.
