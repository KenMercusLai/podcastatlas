# Dacher Keltner

## Overview
Dacher Keltner is the UC Berkeley psychologist and Greater Good Science Center co-director interviewed by [[AndrewHuberman]] in the Huberman Lab source on awe, emotion, social bonding, embarrassment, teasing, community life, psychedelics, and shared embodied experience.

## Current Profile
The source presents Keltner as an emotion scientist whose work broadens classical facial-expression research beyond anger, fear, sadness, disgust, surprise, and smiling into a richer set of social emotions. His strongest contribution in this source is [[AweEmotion]]: awe is treated as measurable through expression, voice, goosebumps, vagal tone, attention, inflammation-related claims, and real-world settings such as nature, museums, concerts, sport, and collective rituals.

Keltner's social-emotion profile is not limited to awe. He also links embarrassment, teasing, music, campfires, saunas, psychedelics, moral beauty, and urban design to the same larger question: how human beings move out of isolated self-focus and into trust, belonging, and shared meaning.

## Key Characteristics
- Treats awe as a scientifically measurable emotion with bodily, attentional, social, and health-related dimensions.
- Prefers real-world study settings alongside laboratory measures, including museums, forests, Yosemite visitors, concerts, and awe walks.
- Connects emotion science to social bonding through music, sport, campfires, teasing, embarrassment, and embodied rituals.
- Frames moral beauty, including courage, kindness, justice, and integrity, as a source of awe.
- Warns that excessive self-focus, meanness, status striving, and poorly designed digital life can block awe and shared connection.
- Keeps psychedelic awe discussion cautious by emphasizing context, guidance, safety, and limits around casual microdosing.
