## Overview

梅福 / Mei Fu enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-544-yuangong-yue-zhongcheng-laoban-yue-huangkong-lk52zm46585z2noqxmtfn9kpwek2|Hanji 544]] as the Nanchang county commandant from Jiujiang who memorializes [[HanChengdi|汉成帝]] after [[WangZhangWesternHan|王章]] is killed for criticizing [[WangFengWesternHan|王凤]]. His role is to convert a punished-speaker case into a broader warning about blocked remonstrance, outer-relative power, and the danger of personal favorites.

## Current Profile

Mei Fu's source profile is narrow but pointed. The episode first places him after the Shanyang rebellion and Wang Zhang's death, when the court atmosphere has become fearful. Mei Fu then writes to Chengdi, saying that a world where people treat speech itself as dangerous is already in serious trouble.

His memorial links practical governance to speech protection. If a ruler can receive advice quickly and turn toward correction, talented people gather around him; if direct critics are killed, officials learn silence and the ruler loses access to danger signals. He therefore asks Chengdi to issue a no-speech-ban command and to prevent Wang-family outer-relative authority from repeating older Lu, Huo, and Shangguan patterns.

The episode's management analogy makes Mei Fu a voice for institutional rather than personal loyalty. The host reads his "天下为公，天子无私" frame as an argument that a durable organization cannot be built around favorites, family feeling, or personal worship; it needs rules, feedback, and supervised power.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han memorialist who speaks after Wang Zhang's death makes court criticism dangerous.
- Treats protected remonstrance as a way to attract talent and keep governance correctable.
- Warns Chengdi that killing critics will make officials unable or unwilling to name faults.
- Uses outer-relative precedents to argue for early restraint of favored kin.
- Supplies the episode's bridge from court politics to modern organizational loyalty.
