# Henry Stafford, Duke of Buckingham

## Overview

Henry Stafford, Duke of Buckingham, is Richard III's principal ally in the seizure and delegitimation of Edward V who later rebelled for Henry Tudor and became the leading alternative suspect in the princes' deaths.

## Current Profile

Buckingham helped Richard convert access to the young king into control, promoted the case for Richard's crown, and belonged to the inner coalition capable of benefiting from the princes' exclusion. His later rebellion and execution create a plausible motive for later blame, but the episode emphasizes that access to the Tower would probably still have required Richard's knowledge.

## Key Characteristics

- Ally who coordinated with Richard around the interception of Edward V's party.
- Promoter of public support for Richard's accession.
- Later rebel who shifted toward Henry Tudor and was executed.
- Main alternative suspect in Ricardian and literary accounts of the princes' deaths.
- Weak substitute for Richard as sole culprit because Tower access remained under royal control.

## Evidence

- Seizure and accession: [[139-the-princes-in-the-tower-part-2-glt4685691538]] places Buckingham beside Richard in the arrests and later campaign for kingship.
- Rebellion: [[139-the-princes-in-the-tower-part-2-glt4685691538]] follows his defection, failed rising, capture, and execution.
- Alternative-suspect analysis: [[139-the-princes-in-the-tower-part-2-glt4685691538]] tests his motive and opportunity but argues he could not plausibly operate in the Tower without Richard's knowledge.

## Qualifications

The episode does not prove Buckingham's role in the deaths or fully explain his rebellion. Richard's failure to accuse him is suggestive but not decisive evidence.

## What Changed

- Created a profile separating Buckingham's clear role in usurpation from his unproved role in murder.
