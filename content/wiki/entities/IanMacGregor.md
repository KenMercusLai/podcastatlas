# Ian MacGregor

## Overview

Ian MacGregor is a historian and author whose archival research on [[PavlovsHouse|Pavlov's House]] and the end of the [[BattleOfStalingrad]] anchors this episode of [[TheRestIsHistory]].

## Current Profile

MacGregor approaches Stalingrad through a tightly bounded place and the records around it. He treats Pavlov's House as a tactically useful Soviet position while separating that role from the simplified newspaper and postwar legend. His comparison of Soviet reporting with German combat diaries and personal papers finds that the building's later fame was not matched by a distinctive German operational fixation.

His research also uses Friedrich Roske's unpublished papers to revise the familiar surrender narrative at the Univermag and follows both Roske and [[FriedrichPaulus]] into Soviet captivity and postwar life. The method presented in [[215-stalingrad-and-the-red-army-part-2-glt4269997308]] therefore joins battlefield reconstruction to source criticism and the study of public memory.

## Key Characteristics

- Historian and author specializing in the Battle of Stalingrad.
- Uses German combat diaries, personal papers, memoirs, and Soviet reporting comparatively.
- Distinguishes genuine tactical importance from later symbolic inflation.
- Reconstructs the surrender through Roske's unpublished account as well as the official narrative.
- Connects battlefield history to the continuing politics of commemoration in Volgograd.

## Evidence

- Pavlov's House research: [[215-stalingrad-and-the-red-army-part-2-glt4269997308]] attributes the comparison of Soviet legend, German division diaries, and a German commander's papers to MacGregor.
- Surrender research: [[215-stalingrad-and-the-red-army-part-2-glt4269997308]] presents his use of Roske's memoir to challenge who initiated contact at the Univermag.
- Memory research: [[215-stalingrad-and-the-red-army-part-2-glt4269997308]] reports his observations of tours, education, and naming debate in modern Volgograd.

## Qualifications

The current profile derives from one interview promoting MacGregor's *The Lighthouse of Stalingrad*. His archival conclusions are reported through the episode rather than independently checked against the underlying German and Soviet records. Absence from the German documents he examined does not prove that no German unit ever noticed or attacked Pavlov's House.

## What Changed

- Established MacGregor as a source-critical historian of Stalingrad's battlefield reality and symbolic afterlife.
