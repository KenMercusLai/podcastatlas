# George Whitman

## Overview
George Whitman is presented in the episode as the founder of the current [[ShakespeareAndCompany]] bookshop in Paris.

## Current Profile
The source's bounded profile centers on institutional inheritance and mythmaking. Whitman founded the shop under the name Le Mistral, later renamed it Shakespeare and Company after [[SylviaBeach]]'s earlier shop, named his daughter Sylvia, and encouraged some playful myths around his own lineage and the shop's history.

## Key Characteristics
- He founded the current shop 75 years before the episode, originally under the name Le Mistral.
- He renamed the shop after Sylvia Beach's earlier Shakespeare and Company, linking the current business to modernist literary memory.
- He helped cultivate the mythology that now surrounds the shop.
- His family succession connects him to [[SylviaWhitman]], the current owner.

## Evidence
- Founding evidence: [[bots-on-the-ground-china-leads-humanoid-race-6a91564cee851f3f31239bbf]] says George Whitman founded the shop 75 years earlier under the name Le Mistral.
- Renaming evidence: [[bots-on-the-ground-china-leads-humanoid-race-6a91564cee851f3f31239bbf]] says he renamed it Shakespeare and Company after Sylvia Beach's earlier bookshop.
- Mythmaking evidence: [[bots-on-the-ground-china-leads-humanoid-race-6a91564cee851f3f31239bbf]] records Sylvia Whitman's comment that her father encouraged some myths, including a playful claim about Walt Whitman being his grandfather.
- Succession evidence: [[bots-on-the-ground-china-leads-humanoid-race-6a91564cee851f3f31239bbf]] says he named his daughter Sylvia, linking the current family story to the earlier shop's founder.
