# The Social Reckoning

## Overview
*The Social Reckoning* is an Aaron Sorkin film presented by the source as a sequel to *The Social Network* and as a dramatization of [[FrancesHaugen]]'s Facebook disclosures.

## Current Profile
The film follows journalist Jeff Horwitz as he receives information from Haugen about alleged harms to teenagers on [[Facebook]] and [[Instagram]]. The episode's reviewer finds the subject important but the treatment overly earnest and less surprising than the 2010 predecessor because Facebook's reputation has already deteriorated and its controversies are widely known.

The review also treats the film as a case in [[PlatformTruthArbitrationDilemma]]. Its demand that Facebook suppress harmful or false content confronts the question of who decides truth at global scale; the reviewer argues that the film does not fully resolve that tension.

## Key Characteristics
- Dramatizes real people and events around the Facebook Files disclosures.
- Centers youth harm, engagement incentives, and platform responsibility.
- Arrives after Facebook criticism has become culturally familiar rather than novel.
- Is assessed as more sermon-like and less dramatically precise than its predecessor.
- Exposes but does not resolve the conflict between content intervention and private truth arbitration.

## Evidence
- Story and source material: [[valley-of-the-shadow-of-debt-bond-market-jitters-69104aa52182b702669d450d025a7dac]] identifies Horwitz and Haugen as the central journalist and whistleblower and describes the film as based on real events.
- Cultural-timing judgment: [[valley-of-the-shadow-of-debt-bond-market-jitters-69104aa52182b702669d450d025a7dac]] contrasts Facebook's admired 2010 image with its later familiarity as a target of politicians, litigators, and parents.
- Moderation dilemma: [[valley-of-the-shadow-of-debt-bond-market-jitters-69104aa52182b702669d450d025a7dac]] says the film asks Facebook to suppress harms but treats the resulting speech-governance problem too simply.

## Qualifications
This profile records one critic's account and evaluation. It does not independently verify the film's depiction of Horwitz, Haugen, Sorkin, Zuckerberg, Facebook's systems, or the reported settlement, and it does not treat dramatic simplification as evidence that the underlying youth-harm allegations are false.
