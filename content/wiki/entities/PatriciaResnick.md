# Patricia Resnick

## Overview
Patricia Resnick is presented as the writer who helped turn real clerical workers' stories into the script of [[NineToFiveFilm|9 to 5]].

## Current Profile
The episode positions Resnick as a researcher-writer rather than a detached gag writer. Because she had not worked in a clerical job, she spent time with secretaries at a downtown Los Angeles insurance company and observed the office hierarchy that the film would later exaggerate into comedy.

Her profile also mirrors the film's own critique. Resnick inserted practical reforms such as job sharing and workplace child care, but she says director Colin Higgins kept her off the set. The episode uses that experience as a behind-the-scenes example of men taking credit or control over women's work.

## Key Characteristics
- Screenwriter who translated real clerical-worker experiences into workplace comedy.
- Researcher who observed office hierarchy directly before writing from the material.
- Interpreter of [[GenderedOfficeHierarchy]] through sex-segregated roles, promotion exclusion, and boss power.
- Writer who embedded practical reforms, including job sharing and child care, into the film's fantasy.
- Behind-the-scenes example of the same credit-taking and exclusion problem the movie criticized.
