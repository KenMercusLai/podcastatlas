# 祭肜 / Ji Tong (Eastern Han)

## Overview

Ji Tong / 祭肜 is a Liaodong taishou whose current evidence makes him a reward-backed frontier-proxy manager. Hanji 725 gives the detailed 49 CE [[LiuXiu|Guangwu]] layer, where he turns Xianbei leader [[PianHeXianbei|Pian He]] against the Xiongnu. Hanji 733-1 later preserves a northeastern security-result notice in which Pian He is used against Chishan Wuhuan and border pressure eases under [[EmperorMingOfHan|Mingdi]].

## Current Profile

Hanji 725 gives Ji Tong's most detailed current profile. When Liaodong is disturbed by outside-frontier incursions in 49 CE, he does not answer only with direct Han force. He recruits outsiders toward submission, gives Pian He material support, and tells him that real loyalty should be proven by attacking the Xiongnu and bringing enemy heads to Han officials.

The result is a frontier commitment mechanism. Pian He attacks the Xiongnu, presents heads, and then repeats that action annually for rewards. Ji Tong's significance is therefore not a pitched Han expedition but mediated leverage: a local commander converts gifts and trust into a proxy's continuing hostility toward another frontier power.

Hanji 733-1 gives a compressed or later result layer. It says Ji Tong sends Pian He against Chishan Wuhuan, outside-frontier groups submit, and the Han state withdraws the Wuyuan border garrison because the northeast is calm. The page preserves both target notices rather than forcing the Xiongnu and Chishan Wuhuan references into one identical event.

## Key Characteristics

- Liaodong official with operational influence over northeastern frontier politics.
- Uses Xianbei force and reward incentives rather than relying only on direct Han deployment.
- Turns Pian He's submission into repeated enemy-directed action against Xiongnu targets.
- Associated in Hanji 733-1 with Chishan Wuhuan defeat and broader submission outside the frontier.
- His action contributes to a wider Han security result, including later garrison withdrawal.

## Qualifications

- Hanji 725 and Hanji 733-1 differ in target emphasis: the former foregrounds attacks on the Xiongnu, while the latter names Chishan Wuhuan. The wiki keeps both as frontier-proxy evidence and does not collapse them into one fully reconciled event.
- The sources do not provide Pian He's internal motives, Ji Tong's full biography, or a detailed Wuhuan/Xianbei political history.
- This page should not be confused with [[JitongSpiritMedium]], which is a concept page using a different term.
