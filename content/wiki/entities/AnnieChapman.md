# Annie Chapman

## Overview

Annie Chapman was a 47-year-old woman killed in the backyard of 29 Hanbury Street on 8 September 1888. Her recoverable life and final hours connect the [[JackTheRipper|Jack the Ripper]] case to illness, separation, alcohol use, unstable lodging, and the daily need to secure money for a bed.

## Current Profile

[[625-jack-the-ripper-horror-in-whitechapel-part-2-glt2555108111]] describes Chapman as born in West London, separated from her husband, affected by alcoholism, ill with tuberculosis, and moving among lodging houses. On the night before her death she lacked lodging money, appears to have spent money on drink, and was turned out shortly before 2 a.m.

Her murder is partly reconstructed through John Richardson, Elizabeth Long, and Albert Cadosch. Their accounts make a short window around 5:30–5:45 a.m. plausible and suggest a rapid attack in a populated setting, but they do not settle Chapman's exact movements, whether she was soliciting, or every inference drawn from the injuries.

## Key Characteristics

- Whitechapel murder victim whose identity and parts of her life are historically recoverable.
- Woman whose separation, illness, alcohol use, and lodging insecurity compounded one another.
- Needed immediate bed money shortly before her death, placing economic survival beside nighttime exposure.
- Final timeline depends on several imperfect witness accounts rather than a single conclusive observation.
- Injuries shaped debate over the killer's speed and anatomical familiarity without proving profession or identity.
