# Steve Hilton

## Overview
Steve Hilton is a California Republican gubernatorial candidate whose campaign centers on affordability, deregulation, government downsizing, energy costs, and a challenge to prolonged Democratic control.

## Current Profile
Hilton's current profile is a change-candidate campaign against California's governing status quo. Across his two substantive interviews, he links tax relief for working households to spending restraint, audits, regulatory reduction, housing and new-city development, in-state energy, cheaper gasoline, public-order enforcement, and treatment capacity. The latest source expands the first-$100,000 income-tax exemption described in April to $150,000 and adds a 10% state-workforce cut, a regulatory-count target, high-speed-rail cancellation, and a $73 vehicle-registration cap.

His electoral theory is cross-party and cost-led: dissatisfied Democrats, independents, Republicans, and non-college-educated workers may support a Republican candidate if the race is framed around living costs and basic competence rather than national partisan conflict.

## Key Characteristics
- California Republican gubernatorial candidate using affordability as the central campaign issue.
- British-born Thatcher-influenced political actor who frames California through 1970s Britain analogies.
- Anti-capture reformer in his own telling, targeting unions, trial lawyers, environmental litigation, spending growth, and one-party governance.
- Platform candidate linking tax relief, spending audits, workforce and regulatory cuts, housing expansion, cheaper energy and vehicle costs, public safety, and homelessness enforcement.
- Electoral strategist relying on California's top-two system, Republican consolidation, and economically dissatisfied cross-party voters.

## Qualifications
Hilton's policy figures, polling, causal claims, and projected savings remain source-scoped campaign arguments. The shift from a $100,000 to a $150,000 tax exemption may represent policy development, but the later episode does not provide a revised fiscal model. A Republican governor would still face a Democratic legislature and other legal or administrative constraints for major tax, spending, staffing, rail, and regulatory changes.

## What Changed
- Expanded the tax proposal from a $100,000 to a $150,000 income exemption while preserving the unresolved implementation constraint.
- Added state-workforce, regulatory-count, gasoline, high-speed-rail, registration-fee, and new-city proposals.
- Sharpened Hilton's coalition theory around working and non-college-educated cross-party voters.
