## Overview

赵婴齐 / Zhao Yingqi is presented as the third king of [[Nanyue|南越]], first sent by [[ZhaoHuNanyue|赵胡]] into long court service at Chang'an and later a ruler who reduces open symbolic confrontation with Han while preserving practical autonomy. He hides the imperial seals of [[ZhaoTuo|赵佗]] and Zhao Hu, seeks Han recognition for [[QueenDowagerJiuNanyue|樛氏]] and [[ZhaoXingNanyue|赵兴]], but refuses repeated summons to Chang'an.

## Current Profile

Hanji 348-2 supplies the court-service opening. After Han intervenes against Minyue, Zhao Hu reportedly sends his heir to Chang'an in gratitude. The host interprets [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s reception and cultivation of Zhao Yingqi as an attempt to shape a compliant future Nanyue king.

Hanji 349-1 develops that opening. It calls Zhao Yingqi a hostage, describes him as serving near Wudi in a palace-guard role for twelve years, and says Wudi arranges his marriage to an unnamed woman from the old Zhao region. She bears Zhao Xing and Zhao Cigong and appears to be the later Lady Jiu, although the source does not name her. The episode treats office, intimacy, shared regional association, and family formation as one long influence strategy; the formal hostage status, office title, planted-agent claim, and psychological mechanism remain source-scoped.

Hanji 349-2 places Zhao Yingqi between two political orders. The episode says twelve years of attention from Han Wudi make him recognize that Han power is rising and open imperial pretension is dangerous. He therefore does not commission an imperial seal for himself and conceals the two inherited seals.

This concession remains bounded. Zhao Yingqi retains local ruler-like enjoyment of power and repeatedly claims illness rather than attending court, because the source says he fears being subjected to the laws used against interior vassal kings. He accepts court recognition of his queen and heir without accepting the personal attendance that could narrow his autonomy.

His succession arrangements also create the next political stage. The Han-born Lady Jiu becomes queen, Zhao Xing becomes heir, and after Zhao Yingqi's death they occupy Nanyue's royal center. The episode treats this as a potential gain for Wudi's long incorporation plan, but later events show that their position does not eliminate locally rooted opposition.

## Key Characteristics

- Third Nanyue king and successor to Zhao Hu after prolonged court service at Chang'an.
- Target of a reported marriage-and-proximity cultivation strategy during twelve years near Wudi.
- Concealer of the two inherited imperial seals rather than commissioner of a new one.
- Formally compliant ruler who seeks Han recognition for queen and heir.
- Repeated refuser of court attendance at Chang'an.
- Protector of practical local autonomy against interior-vassal constraints.
- Father of Zhao Xing and Zhao Cigong and husband of the probable future Queen Dowager Jiu.

## Qualifications

Hanji 349-2 writes his name as “赵英齐”; the wiki normalizes it to “赵婴齐,” matching the adjacent episode title and established historical form. Hanji 348-2 and Hanji 349-1 do not establish the formal court-service terms, office title, degree of coercion, or Wudi's private intention. Hanji 349-1 does not name Zhao Yingqi's new wife, so identifying her as Lady Jiu rests on the Zhao Xing maternity and adjacent succession sequence. The twelve-year cultivation claim, shared-region matchmaking logic, planted-agent interpretation, exact motives for hiding the seals or refusing court, illness pretexts, and degree of local independence are episode-attributed. The sources do not provide a complete biography or reign chronology.

## What Changed

- Added the episode's twelve-year palace-service and arranged-marriage mechanisms to the Chang'an cultivation account.
- Sharpened the outcome boundary: prolonged proximity and family influence precede selective accommodation, not complete obedience.
