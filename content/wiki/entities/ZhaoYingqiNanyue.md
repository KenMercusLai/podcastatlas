## Overview

赵婴齐 / Zhao Yingqi is presented as the third king of [[Nanyue|南越]], a ruler who reduces open symbolic confrontation with Han while preserving practical autonomy. He hides the imperial seals of [[ZhaoTuo|赵佗]] and [[ZhaoHuNanyue|赵胡]], seeks Han recognition for [[QueenDowagerJiuNanyue|樛氏]] and [[ZhaoXingNanyue|赵兴]], but refuses repeated summons to Chang'an.

## Current Profile

Hanji 349-2 places Zhao Yingqi between two political orders. The episode says twelve years of attention from [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] make him recognize that Han power is rising and open imperial pretension is dangerous. He therefore does not commission an imperial seal for himself and conceals the two inherited seals.

This concession remains bounded. Zhao Yingqi retains local ruler-like enjoyment of power and repeatedly claims illness rather than attending court, because the source says he fears being subjected to the laws used against interior vassal kings. He accepts court recognition of his queen and heir without accepting the personal attendance that could narrow his autonomy.

His succession arrangements also create the next political stage. The Han-born Lady Jiu becomes queen, Zhao Xing becomes heir, and after Zhao Yingqi's death they occupy Nanyue's royal center. The episode treats this as a potential gain for Wudi's long incorporation plan, but later events show that their position does not eliminate locally rooted opposition.

## Key Characteristics

- Third Nanyue king and successor to Zhao Hu.
- Concealer of the two inherited imperial seals rather than commissioner of a new one.
- Formally compliant ruler who seeks Han recognition for queen and heir.
- Repeated refuser of court attendance at Chang'an.
- Protector of practical local autonomy against interior-vassal constraints.
- Father of Zhao Xing and husband of the future Queen Dowager Jiu.

## Qualifications

The supplied summary writes his name as “赵英齐”; the wiki normalizes it to “赵婴齐,” matching the adjacent episode title and established historical form. The twelve-year cultivation claim, his exact motives for hiding the seals or refusing court, his illness pretexts, and the degree of local independence are episode-attributed. This source does not provide a complete biography, reign chronology, or direct evidence that every succession choice was part of a coordinated Han plan.

## What Changed

- Created a profile distinguishing Zhao Yingqi's symbolic concession from the continued defense of local authority.
- Connected his queen-and-heir petition to the later Zhao Xing and Queen Dowager Jiu crisis without treating their policy as predetermined.

## Relationships

- [[ZhaoXingNanyue|赵兴]] - son and designated heir who succeeds him.
