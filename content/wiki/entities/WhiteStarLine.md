# White Star Line

## Overview

White Star Line was the passenger-shipping company operating [[RMSTitanic|RMS Titanic]] and marketing transatlantic passage to migrants in [[430-titanic-the-iceberg-strikes-part-4-glt2129072034]].

## Current Profile

The episode presents White Star as both a transport company and a migration intermediary. It competed through unusually comfortable third-class accommodation, promoted American opportunity abroad, and carried passengers whose journeys depended on family, work, religious, and village networks. That commercial openness was selective: the source says the line discouraged Eastern Europeans from traveling through Southampton even while providing kosher food aboard.

## Key Characteristics

- Operator of Titanic and commercial organizer of passenger movement across the Atlantic.
- Competed through improved third-class cabins, sanitation, dining, and shared facilities.
- Used overseas agents and publicity to sell the United States as a destination.
- Served heterogeneous labor, family, and refuge migrations rather than one uniform passenger market.
- Combined passenger recruitment with selective routing and exclusion.

## Evidence

- Accommodation strategy: [[430-titanic-the-iceberg-strikes-part-4-glt2129072034]] describes two- and four-berth cabins, washbasins, ventilation, baths, showers, common rooms, bars, and a large dining saloon.
- Migration marketing: [[430-titanic-the-iceberg-strikes-part-4-glt2129072034]] says company agents advertised American opportunity abroad.
- Selective access: [[430-titanic-the-iceberg-strikes-part-4-glt2129072034]] says White Star discouraged Eastern Europeans from the Southampton route while offering kosher food.
- Disaster context: [[430-titanic-the-iceberg-strikes-part-4-glt2129072034]] places company chairman [[BruceIsmay]] aboard Titanic as ice warnings accumulated.

## Qualifications

This profile rests on one narrative podcast installment rather than company records or a full business history. Comparative comfort did not erase class hierarchy, migration screening, or later inequality in evacuation. The reasons, implementation, and scope of the reported Eastern European discouragement require additional evidence.
