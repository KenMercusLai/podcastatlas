# Georg Elser

## Overview

Georg Elser was a German carpenter who independently attempted to kill [[AdolfHitler]] and other Nazi leaders with a timed bomb in Munich on 8 November 1939.

## Current Profile

Elser appears in the source as an ordinary skilled worker rather than a member of an elite conspiracy. He disliked Nazi treatment of workers, feared another war, and concluded on his own that removing Hitler and the senior leadership was necessary. After observing poor security at the annual [[BeerHallPutsch]] commemoration, he selected a pillar behind Hitler's speaking position and developed a technically demanding plan around the event's predictable routine.

He stole explosives and detonators, tested components, measured the hall, rented a Munich room, and spent about thirty nights hiding inside the building and carving a bomb chamber into the pillar. He set the device for 9:20 p.m. on 8 November 1939 and left for the Swiss border. Hitler unexpectedly began and ended his speech early, departing thirteen minutes before the explosion.

The blast killed eight people and injured more than sixty but no senior Nazi leaders. Border officials stopped Elser near Konstanz and found incriminating objects; he was tortured and confessed. The regime kept him alive in custody, later transferred him to Dachau, and had him shot in April 1945. The failure paradoxically aided Hitler by producing sympathy, weakening immediate coup hopes, and feeding a providential story of survival.

## Key Characteristics

- Independent working-class opponent rather than participant in the senior military conspiracy.
- Motivated in the episode by opposition to Nazi labor repression and fear of war.
- Combined patient reconnaissance, craft skill, explosives testing, concealment, and timing.
- Came within thirteen minutes of killing Hitler through a change in the event schedule he could not control.
- Was tortured, imprisoned, and executed by the Nazi regime.
- Became a case where courageous resistance and tragic civilian casualties coexist.

## Evidence

- Motive and independence: [[620-the-nazis-at-war-hitler-strikes-west-part-1-glt4381972581]] presents Elser as acting without a wider organization after deciding that Hitler and the Nazi leadership had to be removed.
- Preparation and skill: [[620-the-nazis-at-war-hitler-strikes-west-part-1-glt4381972581]] traces his scouting, theft and testing of explosives, measurements, repeated night work, hidden chamber, and timer.
- Near success and casualties: [[620-the-nazis-at-war-hitler-strikes-west-part-1-glt4381972581]] places Hitler's departure at 9:07 and the explosion at 9:20, with eight deaths and more than sixty injuries.
- Arrest and death: [[620-the-nazis-at-war-hitler-strikes-west-part-1-glt4381972581]] connects the border search to confession under torture, privileged detention, transfer to Dachau, and execution in April 1945.
- Political aftermath: [[620-the-nazis-at-war-hitler-strikes-west-part-1-glt4381972581]] argues that public horror and sympathy for Hitler discouraged the immediate army plot and strengthened Hitler's providential self-understanding.

## Qualifications

The profile is bounded to one narrative podcast summary, not a complete biography or forensic reconstruction. Elser's exact political commitments, every element of the bomb design, casualty figures, remembered dialogue, treatment in custody, public opinion, and the counterfactual consequences of success remain source-scoped. Recognizing the moral seriousness and courage of resistance does not erase the deaths of people in the hall.
