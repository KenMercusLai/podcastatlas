# Rumiñawi

## Overview

Rumiñawi was an Inca commander who seized independent power around Quito after Atahualpa's death, resisted Spanish-led forces, destroyed the capital before their arrival, and was captured and executed in 1535.

## Current Profile

[[647-the-fall-of-the-incas-the-king-in-the-north-part-4-glt4740382633]] presents Rumiñawi as the most feared leader to emerge from the northern faction's fragmentation. He reportedly kills Atahualpa's brother Quiliscacha and his entourage at a drugged wake, converting Quiliscacha's body into a drum as a warning before ruling Quito independently.

Against Sebastián de Benalcázar and his Cañari-backed force, Rumiñawi fields a large army at Teocajas and fights hard enough to kill horses and halt cavalry charges before the Spaniards withdraw by night. He then removes treasure, Atahualpa's family, and thousands of women from Quito, burns palaces and storehouses, and continues resistance until his capture, torture, and execution in June 1535.

## Key Characteristics

- Northern Inca commander turned independent ruler around Quito.
- Violent eliminator of a rival Atahualpa-family claim.
- Field commander able to resist Spanish cavalry at Teocajas.
- Practitioner of scorched-earth withdrawal who denied Quito's wealth to the invaders.
- Captured, tortured, and executed after continued resistance.

## Qualifications

The profile depends on a popular-history narrative rooted heavily in Spanish chroniclers. The wake, human drum, troop totals, removal of four thousand women, killing of Virgins of the Sun, battle sequence, torture, and motives remain source-scoped rather than independently verified here. His brutality does not make Spanish atrocities or coercive alliance-building incidental.
