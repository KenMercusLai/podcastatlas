## Overview

檀石槐 / Tanshihuai is the Xianbei leader whose current wiki evidence now begins with a 156 CE leadership-formation layer before moving into refusal of Han recognition, organized territorial command, battlefield coordination, hostage pressure, and post-death succession weakening. Hanji 842 presents him as a strong, intelligent, law-making, dispute-settling leader trusted by Xianbei people and already expanding in multiple directions. Hanji 875 then shows him refusing Han title and marriage diplomacy and dividing the occupied field into eastern, central, and western parts. Hanji 909-910 show him defeating a major Han expedition and using captured family members as pressure, while Hanji 920 uses his death to explain weaker succession under [[HeLianXianbei|和连]] and [[KuitouXianbei|魁头]].

## Current Profile

Tanshihuai's current profile is not just a later succession marker or a raider label. Hanji 842 supplies the earliest bounded layer: he is described as physically powerful, intelligent, and brave; he builds authority through strict legal rules and fair dispute settlement; he is trusted by Xianbei groups; and he establishes a political center before expanding south against Han frontier commanderies, north against Dingling, east against Fuyu, and west against Wusun. That evidence makes the Xianbei threat organizational from the beginning.

Hanji 875 then presents Tanshihuai as a frontier leader who refuses incorporation into Han recognition systems. The Han court sends seals, title recognition, and marriage alliance, but he refuses and increases raiding. The same source says he divides his field into eastern, central, and western regions, each led by a senior figure, making the threat durable rather than episodic.

Hanji 909 shows the military consequence of that organization. When [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]] sends three Han cavalry columns deep into [[Xianbei|鲜卑]] territory in 177 CE, Tanshihuai mobilizes broad Xianbei elites and crushes [[XiaYuLateHan|夏育]], [[TianYanLateHan|田晏]], and [[ZangMinLateHan|臧旻]]. Hanji 910 then shows the coercive side of his command through the Zhao Bao hostage case: Tanshihuai places Zhao Bao's captured mother before the army to fracture loyalty and family duty.

Hanji 920 adds the succession contrast. After Tanshihuai's death, Xianbei still invades [[YouzhouLateHan|幽州]] and [[BingzhouLateHan|并州]], but He Lian is presented as less capable and morally weaker. The page therefore treats Tanshihuai as a high-capacity organizer whose death does not end frontier pressure but changes its leadership quality.

## Key Characteristics

- Builds Xianbei authority through personal strength, intelligence, strict rules, fair dispute settlement, and trust.
- Establishes a political center and expands against Han, Dingling, Fuyu, and Wusun directions in the 156 CE layer.
- Refuses Han seal/title recognition and marriage diplomacy instead of accepting nominal incorporation.
- Organizes Xianbei territory into eastern, central, and western parts under senior leaders.
- Coordinates broad Xianbei response capable of defeating a three-route Han expedition.
- Uses hostage pressure against Zhao Bao, showing psychological and kinship leverage in frontier warfare.
- Leaves a succession field where Xianbei pressure continues under weaker successors.

## Qualifications

The frontmatter source order preserves the page's historical edit order rather than chronological order. Hanji 842 supplies the earliest chronological layer but was appended after the existing source inventory. The transcript forms 谭石槐, 谭世怀, and 谭世桓 are normalized to 檀石槐 because the Xianbei leadership, frontier expansion, seal/title refusal, tripartite command, He Lian, and Kuitou sequence match the established late-Han Xianbei route.

## What Changed

- Hanji 842 adds the earliest current layer for Tanshihuai's personal authority, legal organization, and multi-directional expansion.
- The profile now treats later refusal of Han recognition and expedition victory as consequences of an already organized Xianbei leadership base.
- Added Li Ying's 156 CE Duliao appointment as the Han response attached to Tanshihuai's Yunzhong pressure.

- [[ZhaoBaoLateHan|赵苞 / 赵包]] - Han frontier official pressured through family hostage tactics.
