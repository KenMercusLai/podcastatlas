# Jeannie Fromer

## Overview

Jeannie Fromer is an intellectual-property law professor who provides the trade-secret and worker-power framework in [[nooks-crannies-nooks-crannies|Nooks & Crannies]].

## Current Profile

Fromer explains trade secrecy through both corporate practice and the Willy Wonka story: firms seek to prevent valuable knowledge from leaving, but perfect secrecy can demand extreme control over the people who possess it. She treats that tension as especially important when an employer can characterize broad professional knowledge as secret and thereby impede future work.

Her discussion does not deny legitimate trade-secret protection. It instead separates protection of specific, commercially valuable information from overbroad claims that may substitute for [[NonCompeteAgreements]] and strengthen [[TradeSecretAsLaborControl]].

## Key Characteristics

- Intellectual-property scholar supplying the episode's legal framework.
- Uses the Willy Wonka analogy to make industrial secrecy accessible.
- Emphasizes that sharing a secret changes power between owner and recipient.
- Warns that expansive secrecy claims can constrain experienced workers.

## Evidence

### Secrecy and organizational control

- [[nooks-crannies-nooks-crannies]] attributes to Fromer the comparison between Wonka's closed factory and real firms' precautions against industrial espionage.

### Worker mobility

- [[nooks-crannies-nooks-crannies]] reports her warning that firms can overstate what is secret and may lean more heavily on trade-secret claims as non-competes are restricted.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to one structured episode summary, not Fromer's complete scholarship, publications, institutional biography, or a transcript. The Willy Wonka comparison is explanatory rather than evidence that fictional and real employment arrangements are equivalent.
