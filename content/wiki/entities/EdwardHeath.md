# Edward Heath

## Overview
Edward Heath is the British prime minister in the current Troubles source sequence, responsible for decisions from internment through Stormont's suspension and direct rule.

## Current Profile
The current profile is Heath as a reluctant crisis centralizer. Part 3 presents him as inheriting a deteriorating [[NorthernIreland]] conflict with little prior knowledge or enthusiasm, then authorizing [[OperationDemetrius]] to support [[BrianFaulkner]] and avoid [[NorthernIrelandDirectRule|direct rule]]. Part 4 shows that avoidance failing: after [[BloodySunday]] and escalating bombings, Heath offers Faulkner reduced authority under British control of law and order or direct rule, then suspends [[Stormont]] on 24 March 1972 and sends [[WillieWhitelaw]] to Belfast.

## Key Characteristics
- British prime minister facing a Northern Ireland crisis he initially tries to manage through Stormont.
- Authorizes internment despite warnings that it may fail and aid IRA recruitment.
- Weighs withdrawal, continued support for Faulkner, direct rule, and constitutional alternatives.
- Suspends Stormont after Faulkner refuses reduced authority.
- Representative of British policy drift turning into unavoidable central responsibility.

## Evidence
- Inherited crisis: [[699-the-troubles-return-of-the-ira-part-3-glt9519811642]] says Heath entered office amid warnings that the best case was costly stalemate and the worst case could be civil war.
- Internment approval: [[699-the-troubles-return-of-the-ira-part-3-glt9519811642]] says he authorized Operation Demetrius despite warnings from the army, RUC, and Jack Lynch.
- Strategic alternatives: [[700-the-troubles-bloody-sunday-part-4-glt3909829334]] says the British government considered withdrawal, direct rule, continued support for Faulkner, border redrawing, a partition referendum, and association with Ireland.
- Direct rule decision: [[700-the-troubles-bloody-sunday-part-4-glt3909829334]] says Heath offered Faulkner reduced authority or direct rule and announced Stormont's suspension on 24 March 1972.

## Qualifications
The sources present Heath as poorly prepared and reactive, but they also acknowledge constrained options: withdrawal risked civil war and wider Irish intervention, while continuing Stormont rule after Bloody Sunday and bombings had become untenable.

## What Changed
- Added Heath's move from avoiding direct rule to imposing it.
- Added the episode's qualification that immediate British withdrawal might have produced greater sectarian bloodshed.
