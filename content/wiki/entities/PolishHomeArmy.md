# Polish Home Army / 波兰家乡军

## Overview

The Polish Home Army was an underground resistance force in occupied Poland that received [[WitoldPilecki]]'s Auschwitz intelligence and led the 1944 Warsaw Uprising discussed in the bounded source.

## Current Profile

[[lsehilqpxbu9hutqm87jyfejx6wy]] presents the Home Army as the outside organization Pilecki hoped could transform camp intelligence into rescue or revolt. It received his reports but judged an assault on [[AuschwitzBirkenau|Auschwitz]] too risky and beyond its ability to sustain, evacuate, and protect the prisoner population.

In Warsaw in August 1944, the organization instead attempted to drive out German forces and establish political control before Soviet arrival. The source emphasizes limited arms, insufficient coordination with the Red Army, broad civilian participation, German reinforcement and atrocity, and surrender on 2 October after catastrophic human and material loss.

## Key Characteristics

- Operated as a clandestine military organization under German occupation.
- Received prisoner intelligence and confronted a gap between knowledge and feasible intervention.
- Evaluated camp liberation through combat, evacuation, medical, and protection constraints.
- Mobilized armed fighters and extensive civilian support during the Warsaw Uprising.
- Pursued both immediate military resistance and postwar political position.

## Evidence

- Auschwitz decision: [[lsehilqpxbu9hutqm87jyfejx6wy]] describes Pilecki's reports and the Home Army's rejection of a direct assault as militarily and logistically infeasible.
- Warsaw Uprising: [[lsehilqpxbu9hutqm87jyfejx6wy]] describes the uprising's strategic purpose, limited weapons, civilian participation, German response, surrender, casualties, and destruction.
- Pilecki connection: [[lsehilqpxbu9hutqm87jyfejx6wy]] follows Pilecki from camp reporting into combat and captivity after the uprising.

## Qualifications

This is not a complete institutional history of the Home Army, its command, ideology, regional operations, relations with Jewish resistance, rival Polish formations, the government-in-exile, or the Soviet Union. The source's explanation of why Auschwitz was not attacked and why the Warsaw Uprising began is a compact narrative; feasibility, coordination, casualty totals, political motives, and counterfactual alternatives remain source-scoped or contested.

## What Changed

- Established the Home Army as the organizational bridge between Pilecki's camp intelligence and wider resistance.
- Framed non-intervention at Auschwitz as a combined military, evacuation, and protection problem.
- Added the Warsaw Uprising as an example of armed action whose political aim and human cost cannot be separated.
