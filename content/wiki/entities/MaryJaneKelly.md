# Mary Jane Kelly

## Overview

Mary Jane Kelly was murdered at 13 Miller's Court on 9 November 1888 and is generally treated as the last of the canonical victims associated with [[JackTheRipper|Jack the Ripper]]. The wiki's evidence emphasizes both her severe economic vulnerability and the unusual uncertainty surrounding her earlier life.

## Current Profile

Kelly's recoverable final circumstances are clearer than her biography. She rented a small room in Miller's Court, owed six weeks' rent, and had lived with fish-market porter Joseph Barnett until an argument roughly ten days before her death. After Barnett lost his job, the couple's finances worsened and Kelly reportedly returned to soliciting. Barnett visited on 8 November but had neither work nor money to offer.

Accounts of Kelly's origins, family, marriage, education, work in a Knightsbridge brothel, time in Paris, names, appearance, and hair color remain contradictory or unverified. The episode therefore treats her self-reported past neither as established biography nor as proof of deception. Stories may have supplied protection, glamour, or escape, but motive cannot be recovered confidently.

Her indoor murder gave the offender privacy and time, producing injuries more extensive than those in earlier outdoor attacks. Witness claims about men seen with her, cries around 4 a.m., and medical estimates constrain possible timelines without identifying the killer. Public mourning at the removal of her body and the inability to locate family for her funeral place personal isolation beside mass spectacle.

## Key Characteristics

- Last likely canonical Whitechapel victim, murdered indoors at Miller's Court.
- Subject of an unusually uncertain and partly self-narrated pre-London biography.
- Exposed to danger through rent debt, a partner's unemployment, housing insecurity, and survival work.
- Remembered through conflicting witness accounts that require explicit confidence limits.
- Publicly mourned while institutionally and familially isolated at burial.
