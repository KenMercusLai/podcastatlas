# Zhang Bo / 张博

## Overview
[[ZhangBoAnimalLaw|Zhang Bo / 张博]] is the University of Toronto animal-law scholar interviewed in the 旺旺 episode about childhood life education, animal-protection law, and whether animal suffering can count as a legal reason in itself.

## Current Profile
The source uses Zhang Bo to move the episode from outrage over punishment into legal and educational structure. He argues that many families teach children to avoid animals as dirty or dangerous without teaching observation and respect, and that mainland animal-related rules often protect food safety, public health, property, or social order rather than animal pain. His comments ground [[ChildAnimalCrueltyLifeEducation]], [[ChinaAnimalProtectionLawGap]], and [[AnimalPainLegalRecognition]].

## Key Characteristics
- He is identified as an animal-law scholar at the University of Toronto.
- His educational argument connects fear-based avoidance of animals with weak everyday coexistence learning.
- His legal argument distinguishes human-interest proxies from direct recognition of animal suffering.
- He frames animal-protection legislation as a baseline-duty system rather than a demand to give animals identical rights to humans.

## Evidence
- Education and legal analysis: [[liulangquan-wangwang-zhisi-he-yichang-kuajing-dongbao-shengyuan]] reports Zhang Bo's comments on family teaching, fear of bites or rabies, lack of a dedicated animal-protection law, and the human-interest translation of animal harm.
- Legislative boundary: [[liulangquan-wangwang-zhisi-he-yichang-kuajing-dongbao-shengyuan]] reports his view that legislation would establish social baselines such as anti-abandonment, rescue responsibility, and enforcement duties.
