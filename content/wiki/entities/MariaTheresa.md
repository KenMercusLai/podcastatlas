# Maria Theresa

## Overview

Maria Theresa was the Habsburg ruler who survived a disputed female succession, restored dynastic strength, and used her large family for diplomacy, including [[MarieAntoinette|Marie Antoinette]]'s marriage into the French monarchy.

## Current Profile

The source presents Maria Theresa chiefly through the 1770 marriage alliance joining Habsburg Austria to Bourbon France after a major European diplomatic realignment. Marie Antoinette's identity as her mother's Austrian daughter remained politically stronger in France than any paternal French connection and made the young queen vulnerable to claims of foreign influence.

The Habsburg survey supplies the earlier succession frame. Charles VI's lack of a son led to the Pragmatic Sanction; after his death Maria Theresa defended her inheritance, appealed to Hungarian support, and used sixteen children to rebuild dynastic continuity. The episode praises her determination, Catholicism, and social energy, but does not independently reconstruct the wars, administration, or full costs of her reign.

## Key Characteristics

- Habsburg ruler and mother of Marie Antoinette.
- Female successor whose inheritance depended on the Pragmatic Sanction and political-military defense after Charles VI's death.
- Used dynastic marriage within the Franco-Austrian alignment against Britain and Prussia.
- Remained the dominant parental reference in French perceptions of her daughter.
- Presented by the Habsburg family narrative as the ruler who converted succession vulnerability into renewed dynastic strength.
