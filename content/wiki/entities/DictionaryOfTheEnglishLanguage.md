# A Dictionary of the English Language

## Overview

*A Dictionary of the English Language* was [[SamuelJohnson]]’s major lexicographical project, presented by the episode as nine years of collaborative labor that converted a precarious writer into a commanding public literary figure.

## Current Profile

Johnson received the commission in 1746 after poetry, biography, translation, criticism, moral essays, and periodical work had demonstrated his learning and command of English. He completed the Dictionary with six assistants, five of them Scottish, a detail that complicates the simple image of Johnson as uniformly hostile to Scots.

The project’s public meaning exceeded word definition. Johnson’s definition of the lexicographer as a “harmless drudge” foregrounded labor, while the Earl of Chesterfield episode exposed patronage’s limits: after feeling neglected, Johnson rejected Chesterfield’s late praise and implied sponsorship. Completion brought fame and relief from some literary drudgery, though it did not immediately end debt.

## Key Characteristics

- Large lexicographical project grounded in Johnson’s learning and practical experience of commercial writing.
- Collaborative work completed with six assistants rather than a solitary-genius artifact.
- Source of durable literary authority and public fame.
- Test case for conflict between labor already performed and patronage claimed after success.
- Achievement that improved Johnson’s position without immediately guaranteeing financial security.
