# Kelly Starrett

## Overview
Kelly Starrett is a physical therapist and movement educator who presents mobility, position, tissue care, and training as practical tools for preserving what people can do in sport and daily life.

## Current Profile
In [[how-to-improve-your-mobility-posture-flexibility-dr-kelly-starrett-scim3685343544]], Starrett argues that modern routines narrow the range of positions and movement patterns people regularly use. His response is environmental and behavioral as much as programmatic: sit on the floor, change work positions, practice getting down and up, warm up through play, load useful ranges, and keep training repeatable.

His framework is capacity-oriented rather than appearance-oriented. Posture becomes the ability to organize positions for breathing and force transfer, tissue work becomes a temporary opportunity to move better, and fitness becomes a reserve to spend on family, sport, work, and adventure. He also distinguishes ordinary, modifiable pain episodes from trauma and red flags, though the source does not make that distinction a substitute for clinical assessment.

## Key Characteristics
- Treats movement variety and regular positional exposure as foundations of mobility.
- Reframes posture as task-dependent position and capacity rather than one permanent ideal alignment.
- Uses the gym as both a training environment and a place to notice changing range, asymmetry, control, and tolerance.
- Favors brief, tolerable self-mobilization that creates a window for movement over indiscriminate or extreme tissue work.
- Connects pelvic-floor function with breathing, abdominal pressure, hip mechanics, and whole-body position.
- Prioritizes consistent, enjoyable training that supports life over maximal optimization or rigid fitness identity.

## Evidence
- Daily movement practice - [[how-to-improve-your-mobility-posture-flexibility-dr-kelly-starrett-scim3685343544]] recommends floor sitting, squatting, varied positions, and getting down and up as recurring exposure rather than a separate mobility chore.
- Position and range - [[how-to-improve-your-mobility-posture-flexibility-dr-kelly-starrett-scim3685343544]] links useful positions with breathing, force transfer, hip extension, glute control, and task performance.
- Tissue and symptom management - [[how-to-improve-your-mobility-posture-flexibility-dr-kelly-starrett-scim3685343544]] presents foam rolling, compression, breathing, and D2R2 as ways to change symptoms or range while identifying trauma and systemic red flags as escalation points.
- Sustainable fitness - [[how-to-improve-your-mobility-posture-flexibility-dr-kelly-starrett-scim3685343544]] favors repeatability, play, movement variety, community, sleep, and household food culture over isolated performance metrics.

## Qualifications
This profile is based on one broad podcast summary rather than a systematic review of Starrett's books, clinical practice, research basis, or changes in his views. Cultural, injury-healing, concussion, nutrition, supplementation, heat, cold, and tissue-mechanism claims remain attributed and source-scoped. His self-care suggestions are public education and do not replace physical therapy, medical assessment, pelvic-floor care, or individualized training.
