# Jack Feldman

## Overview
Jack Feldman is presented in the bounded source as a UCLA neurobiology professor whose laboratory helped establish the brainstem circuitry that generates breathing rhythm.

## Current Profile
In [[breathing-for-mental-physical-health-performance-dr-jack-feldman-scim4863659802]], Feldman explains breathing as both an automatic life-support process and an unusually accessible route for voluntary influence over brain and body state. His work centers on the pre-Botzinger complex, a second oscillator involved in active expiration, sigh-generating circuits, ascending respiratory signals, and experimental tests of how altered breathing changes arousal or fear-related behavior.

His public stance is notably cautious. He treats several popular breathwork ideas as plausible, describes his own box-breathing practice without presenting it as optimized, and repeatedly calls for controlled human studies that compare patterns, durations, mechanisms, and outcomes.

## Key Characteristics
- Studies the neural circuits that generate inspiration, active expiration, sighing, and breathing-related state changes.
- Connects brainstem rhythm generation to respiratory muscles, gas exchange, lung mechanics, and ascending brain pathways.
- Uses animal circuit manipulation to test causal mechanisms while distinguishing those findings from human treatment evidence.
- Treats breathwork effects as potentially multi-pathway rather than attributing them to one universal mechanism.
- Communicates practical breathing ideas with explicit uncertainty about optimal protocols and clinical translation.

## Evidence
- Rhythm generation - [[breathing-for-mental-physical-health-performance-dr-jack-feldman-scim4863659802]] presents Feldman's foundational work on the pre-Botzinger complex and later work on active-expiration circuitry.
- Sigh circuitry - [[breathing-for-mental-physical-health-performance-dr-jack-feldman-scim4863659802]] describes bombesin-related signaling, targeted cell ablation, and the unresolved interpretation of deteriorating animal health after sigh loss.
- Brain-state pathways - [[breathing-for-mental-physical-health-performance-dr-jack-feldman-scim4863659802]] links respiration to locus-coeruleus projections, vagal input, olfactory airflow, carbon dioxide, pH, and voluntary control.
- Translational caution - [[breathing-for-mental-physical-health-performance-dr-jack-feldman-scim4863659802]] separates mouse fear-conditioning results and plausible mechanisms from controlled human breathwork evidence.
- Practical stance - [[breathing-for-mental-physical-health-performance-dr-jack-feldman-scim4863659802]] records a simple personal box-breathing practice while emphasizing protocol-comparison gaps.
