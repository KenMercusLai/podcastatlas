## Overview

申屠嘉 / Shentu Jia is a Western Han chancellor whose current wiki profile is bounded to an unsuccessful attempt to prosecute [[ChaoCuoWesternHan|晁错]] early in [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]]'s reign. The case shows a formal senior minister losing leverage when a favored adviser secures private imperial protection before the accusation reaches open court.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-230-shentujia-yu-sha-chaocuo-weihe-fan-bei-qisi-ltwm5zfpdau5p-lmsnnr8wynfzqj|Hanji 230]] places Shentu Jia among the officials who establish Gaozu's and Emperor Wen's ancestral titles at the start of Jingdi's reign. His later conflict with Chao grows from unequal access as well as policy disagreement: Chao repeatedly receives private audiences, changes rules with the emperor's support, and discusses reducing the royal kingdoms, while the chancellor's advice is rejected.

Shentu Jia then tries to use Chao's opening of a southern passage through a wall associated with the Former Emperor's temple precinct as a capital case. Chao learns of the plan and reaches Jingdi overnight. At court the next day, Jingdi says the wall enclosed unused outer ground and that he authorized the work, leaving Shentu Jia unable to carry the impeachment forward.

The episode reports that Shentu Jia regrets not killing Chao before presenting the memorial, returns home, and dies after vomiting blood in anger. The durable judgment is political rather than medical: his office and legal charge cannot overcome the combined advantages of prior access, admission, and ruler sponsorship. The exact wall, applicable law, procedural authority to execute first, and cause of death remain source-scoped.

## Key Characteristics

- Chancellor associated with the opening ritual settlement of Jingdi's reign.
- Senior official whose advice loses ground as Chao Cuo gains repeated private access.
- Rival who converts a temple-precinct wall breach into a proposed capital prosecution.
- Prosecutor who loses the timing contest when Chao reaches the emperor before open court.
- Minister whose formal authority is defeated by the ruler's factual and political sponsorship of the accused.

## Qualifications

This is a one-source profile, not a complete biography of Shentu Jia's career or chancellorship. The ancestral-title process, his broader advice, hostility toward Chao, exact wall and temple geography, applicable capital law, authority to arrest or execute before memorializing, leaked information, court speeches, emotional state, and medical cause of death remain episode-attributed. The page supports an access-and-patronage explanation for the failed impeachment, not a general rule that formal office is always powerless against favorites.
