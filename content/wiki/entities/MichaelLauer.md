# Michael Lauer

## Overview

Michael Lauer is presented as the NIH deputy director for extramural research who examined whether peer-review scores predicted the later citation impact of funded research.

## Current Profile

Lauer expected expert scores to show a meaningful relationship with later impact but reportedly found no strong correlation within the funded grant range. In the episode, that result makes him evidence for humility about fine-grained rankings rather than for abandoning expert review: reviewers can screen out clearly weak proposals, while a lottery can allocate awards among the qualified remainder. Citation impact is only one outcome, and the supplied summary does not expose the analysis in enough detail to adjudicate its scope.

## Key Characteristics

- He studies the predictive performance of NIH grant review rather than assuming score precision.
- He distinguishes expert screening from reliable ranking among fundable proposals.
- He treats scientific discovery as inherently difficult to forecast.
- His analysis motivates a portfolio or lottery response to residual uncertainty.
