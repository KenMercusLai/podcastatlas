# 白展堂 / Bai Zhantang

## Overview

白展堂 is a character in [[WulinWaizhuan|《武林外传》]]. The bounded source follows his movement from apparently trivial wagers to compulsive loss chasing and a final bet of his life.

## Current Profile

[[110-wulin-waizhuan-xiaodu-yiqing-zuihaode-jiedu-xuanchuanpian-lor4w6cimesyf4dlwgr5ywfgwgxb]] presents Bai's central contradiction through his own language. He claims to play only for fun and invokes “小赌怡情,” yet refuses to play without winning, induces reluctant friends to stake goods and labor, and continues whenever he is offered a clean stopping point. After punishment fails to change his view, losses to [[DuanzhiXuanyuan|断指轩辕]] make the contradiction increasingly visible.

His escalation is sustained by explanation. He attributes defeat to luck, seat position, the game itself, and other temporary conditions, so each loss becomes a reason to change circumstances rather than stop. By the time he has rolled three sixes, certainty of victory makes him accept a life stake without investigating the trap. Fear finally breaks this cycle: the staged threat of losing his hand lets him understand that refusing the first wager would have prevented every later debt.

## Key Characteristics

- Recasts gambling as harmless entertainment even while insisting that winning is its purpose.
- Uses unequal rules, provocation, and noncash stakes to draw other inn residents into play.
- Treats both winning and losing as reasons to continue.
- Externalizes defeat to temporary conditions and changes games or positions rather than stopping.
- Escalates from chores and food to wages, years of labor, and his life.
- Changes his stated belief only after the consequences become personally immediate.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to one story as reconstructed by a podcast. It does not represent Bai Zhantang's full series arc or establish a clinical diagnosis. The final dice result, threatened mutilation, and debt cancellation are comic-dramatic devices, while claims about addiction mechanisms remain the hosts' interpretation rather than creator testimony.

## What Changed

- Distinguished accepting a wager's outcome from recognizing participation itself as the error.
- Identified external attribution as the bridge from repeated loss to continued play.
