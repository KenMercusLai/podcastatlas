# 白展堂 / Bai Zhantang

## Overview

白展堂 is a character in [[WulinWaizhuan|《武林外传》]]. The bounded sources present him both as a former fugitive whose restraint corrects [[GuoFurong|郭芙蓉]]'s romantic image of Jianghu and as a gambler who later loses that restraint through rationalization and escalating stakes.

## Current Profile

[[110-wulin-waizhuan-xiaodu-yiqing-zuihaode-jiedu-xuanchuanpian-lor4w6cimesyf4dlwgr5ywfgwgxb]] presents Bai's central contradiction through his own language. He claims to play only for fun and invokes “小赌怡情,” yet refuses to play without winning, induces reluctant friends to stake goods and labor, and continues whenever he is offered a clean stopping point. After punishment fails to change his view, losses to [[DuanzhiXuanyuan|断指轩辕]] make the contradiction increasingly visible.

His escalation is sustained by explanation. He attributes defeat to luck, seat position, the game itself, and other temporary conditions, so each loss becomes a reason to change circumstances rather than stop. By the time he has rolled three sixes, certainty of victory makes him accept a life stake without investigating the trap. Fear finally breaks this cycle: the staged threat of losing his hand lets him understand that refusing the first wager would have prevented every later debt.

Bai also carries the inverse side of his character. He hides substantial ability behind an apparently timid runner because fighting risks exposure to official pursuit. He says that martial training was imposed by his family and displaced his wish to become a painter; life as a fugitive brought violence, dependence, and fear rather than the freedom Guo imagines. His intervention therefore joins competence to avoidance and restraint, although Guo's staged accusation nearly forces him to flee again.

Together, the sources make Bai's restraint domain-specific rather than absolute. He can understand the cost of martial conflict and argue that force should answer genuine injustice, yet still fail to recognize a different escalation loop when gambling activates competitiveness and certainty.

## Key Characteristics

- Recasts gambling as harmless entertainment even while insisting that winning is its purpose.
- Uses unequal rules, provocation, and noncash stakes to draw other inn residents into play.
- Treats both winning and losing as reasons to continue.
- Externalizes defeat to temporary conditions and changes games or positions rather than stopping.
- Escalates from chores and food to wages, years of labor, and his life.
- Changes his stated belief only after consequences become immediate, yet elsewhere conceals martial competence because visibility threatens reform and safety.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to two stories as reconstructed by a podcast. It does not represent Bai Zhantang's full series arc or establish a clinical diagnosis, production history, or creator intention. The final dice result, threatened mutilation, debt cancellation, point-striking, and fugitive comedy are dramatic devices, while claims about addiction, psychology, and his role as Guo's mirror remain interpretive.

## What Changed

- Added Bai's opening-story identity as a hidden martial expert and fugitive seeking reform.
- Distinguished capable restraint in violence from his later failure of restraint in gambling.
- Added his unrealized wish to paint as a qualification to romantic accounts of martial vocation.
