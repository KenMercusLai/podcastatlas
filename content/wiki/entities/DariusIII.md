# Darius III

## Overview

Darius III was the final Achaemenid king, whose defeat by [[AlexanderTheGreat|Alexander the Great]] at the [[BattleOfGaugamela]], flight east, and death during Bessus's revolt enabled Alexander to present conquest as legitimate imperial succession.

## Current Profile

[[117-alexander-the-great-part-2-glt9707946822]] presents Darius as a ruler still capable of raising a large, diverse army and choosing favorable ground after earlier defeat. He offers Alexander the western part of the empire, then assembles infantry, cavalry, scythed chariots, and elephants around Gaugamela. The plan is treated as plausible rather than foolish, but Alexander's cavalry exploits a gap and the Persian line collapses.

Afterward Darius retreats east with Bessus. Bessus claims kingship and Darius is abandoned mortally wounded, allowing Alexander to honor the dead king, punish the usurper, and recast himself from foreign conqueror as Darius's avenger and heir to the [[AchaemenidEmpire]].

## Key Characteristics

- Final king in the Achaemenid succession presented by the source.
- Commands a large, regionally diverse imperial army at Gaugamela.
- Offers territorial compromise before the decisive battle.
- Loses control after military defeat and eastern elite defection.
- Becomes politically useful to Alexander in death as the predecessor to be avenged and inherited.

## Evidence

- Settlement and mobilization: [[117-alexander-the-great-part-2-glt9707946822]] describes Darius's western-territory offer and renewed army at Gaugamela.
- Defeat: [[117-alexander-the-great-part-2-glt9707946822]] attributes the battlefield collapse to a gap exploited by Alexander's cavalry.
- Death and succession: [[117-alexander-the-great-part-2-glt9707946822]] connects Bessus's revolt and Darius's abandonment to Alexander's new claim as avenger and heir.

## Qualifications

This profile depends on one conversational retelling shaped by later Greek and Roman sources. Army size, tactical sequence, reported offers and speeches, Darius's conduct in defeat, his final exchange, and Bessus's exact responsibility should remain source-scoped rather than treated as transparent contemporary evidence.

## What Changed

- Established Darius as a defeated ruler whose death becomes central to Alexander's succession claim.
