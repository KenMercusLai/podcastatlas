# Pope Leo III

## Overview

Pope Leo III is represented as the politically vulnerable bishop of Rome whose alliance with [[Charlemagne]] restored him after a violent challenge and made the papal coronation of a western emperor possible in 800.

## Current Profile

Leo succeeded Hadrian I without the same aristocratic base and faced hostility from his predecessor's relatives. After opponents attacked him in 799, he escaped north to Charlemagne at Paderborn, turning Frankish protection into the practical foundation of his restoration.

Charlemagne entered Rome with an army and retinue, convened a synod, and cleared Leo before the pope crowned him emperor on Christmas Day. The episode presents this not as unilateral papal invention but as a reciprocal settlement: Leo gained protection and office, while Charlemagne gained sacred recognition for a western Roman claim.

## Key Characteristics

- Pope whose weaker aristocratic position exposed him to a violent Roman challenge.
- Supplicant who sought Frankish protection after escaping his opponents.
- Beneficiary of Charlemagne's armed arrival and synodal settlement.
- Coronator who made papal ritual central to the western imperial revival.

## Qualifications

The source is a short conversational account rather than a full study of Leo's pontificate, Roman factions, the accusations against him, or the coronation's planning. The reported blinding, tongue removal, miraculous recovery, and exact prior agreement with Charlemagne remain narrative or interpretive claims.

## What Changed

- Established Leo as the vulnerable papal partner whose restoration and coronation joined protection to imperial recognition.

## Relationships

- [[Charlemagne]] - Frankish protector whom Leo crowned emperor.
- [[RomanImperialLegitimacy]] - papal recognition and Roman ceremony strengthened the western claim.
- [[HolyRomanEmpire]] - later imperial order that treated the coronation as a foundational moment.
- [[ByzantineEmpire]] - weakening protector in Italy whose reduced reach encouraged the papal turn toward the Franks.
