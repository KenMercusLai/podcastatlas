# Pope Leo III

## Overview

Pope Leo III is represented as the politically vulnerable bishop of Rome whose alliance with [[Charlemagne]] restored him after a violent challenge and made the papal coronation of a western emperor possible in 800.

## Current Profile

Leo succeeded Hadrian I without the same aristocratic base and faced hostility from his predecessor's relatives. He quickly sought [[Charlemagne]]'s support and departed from the customary papal notification of Constantinople. After opponents attacked him in 799, he escaped north to Charlemagne at Paderborn, turning Frankish protection into the practical foundation of his restoration.

Charlemagne entered Rome with an army and retinue, and [[Alcuin]]'s argument that a pope could not be tried helped set aside the accusations against Leo. The pope then crowned Charlemagne emperor on Christmas Day. The fuller evidence makes surprise unlikely but does not reduce Leo to a ceremonial instrument: the crown joined his papal authority to Charlemagne's protection in a reciprocal settlement.

## Key Characteristics

- Pope whose weaker aristocratic position exposed him to a violent Roman challenge.
- Early seeker of Frankish support who loosened the customary political orientation toward Constantinople.
- Supplicant who sought Frankish protection after escaping his opponents.
- Beneficiary of Charlemagne's arrival and a legal-jurisdictional settlement.
- Coronator who made papal ritual central to the western imperial revival.

## Qualifications

The sources are conversational accounts rather than full studies of Leo's pontificate, Roman factions, the accusations against him, or canon law. One supplied summary dates Leo's attack to 797 while the earlier source and current synthesis use 799, so the chronology remains explicitly unresolved here. The reported blinding, tongue removal, miraculous recovery, and exact prior agreement with Charlemagne remain narrative or interpretive claims. Evidence of preparation does not establish that Leo and Charlemagne shared identical aims.

## What Changed

- Clarified Leo's early turn toward Frankish support and the jurisdictional reasoning behind his restoration.
- Strengthened the reciprocal-settlement reading while preserving uncertainty about coordination and motive.

## Relationships

- [[Charlemagne]] - Frankish protector whom Leo crowned emperor.
- [[RomanImperialLegitimacy]] - papal recognition and Roman ceremony strengthened the western claim.
- [[HolyRomanEmpire]] - later imperial order that treated the coronation as a foundational moment.
- [[ByzantineEmpire]] - weakening protector in Italy whose reduced reach encouraged the papal turn toward the Franks.
- [[Alcuin]] - adviser whose legal argument helped remove the accusations from ordinary trial.
