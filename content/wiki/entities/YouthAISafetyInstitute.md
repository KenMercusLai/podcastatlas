# Youth AI Safety Institute

## Overview
The Youth AI Safety Institute is presented in [[tech-20261105-1105-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261105-1105-mp-tech-pod-128]] as the [[CommonSenseMedia]] initiative associated with an independent assessment of teen-focused [[ChatGPT]] safeguards.

## Current Profile
Within the bounded source, the institute evaluates whether product promises translate into dependable child-safety behavior. Its reported test used roughly 4,000 prompts through simulated teen accounts and examined crisis-resource activation, guided study, and personalized friend-like interaction. The source frames this work as independent risk assessment, while preserving [[OpenAI]]'s objection that the methodology does not accurately represent safeguards in practice.

## Key Characteristics
- Focuses on AI risks affecting children and teenagers.
- Uses simulated teen accounts and prompt-based product testing.
- Evaluates crisis handling, learning boundaries, and relational behavior together.
- Treats human supervision as necessary even when product safeguards exist.
- Seeks better company-data access for independent safety assessment.

## Evidence
- Evaluation scope - [[tech-20261105-1105-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261105-1105-mp-tech-pod-128]] describes testing of sensitive-topic protection, parental-alert-related safety, study guidance, and personalized interaction.
- Reported findings - [[tech-20261105-1105-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261105-1105-mp-tech-pod-128]] attributes missed crisis resources, bypassable study safeguards, and persistent friend-like behavior to the institute's assessment.
- Independence boundary - [[tech-20261105-1105-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261105-1105-mp-tech-pod-128]] records pre-publication discussion with OpenAI and an unresolved methodological disagreement.

## Qualifications
The episode does not supply the underlying assessment, prompt corpus, account configuration, institute charter, staffing, or OpenAI's detailed methodological critique. The reported test size, failure rate, and unacceptable-risk judgment therefore remain attributed findings rather than independently verified wiki conclusions.
