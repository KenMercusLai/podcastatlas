# Regnans in Excelsis

## Overview

Regnans in Excelsis is the papal bull of 1570 that excommunicates [[ElizabethI|Elizabeth I]] and, in the episode's framing, changes Catholic loyalty in England from private religious nonconformity into a direct political-security problem. The bull is issued by Pope Pius V after the Northern Rising crisis and calls Elizabeth the "pretended Queen of England."

## Current Profile

The bull matters less as abstract doctrine than as a loyalty shock. By telling Catholics they must renounce loyalty to Elizabeth, it makes Cecil's fear more concrete: Catholic allegiance can be read as incompatible with obedience to the queen. The hosts say the bull effectively makes Elizabeth a legitimate target for plotting and assassination.

That does not prove that every Catholic subject becomes a plotter. It supplies the political and theological condition under which Elizabethan ministers can securitize recusancy, priest missions, and Mary-linked claims.

## Key Characteristics

- The bull excommunicates Elizabeth and denies her legitimate queenship.
- It recasts Catholic obedience as a conflict between papal authority and loyalty to Elizabeth.
- It intensifies the security reading of Mary Stuart, recusancy, rebellion, and assassination risk.

## Qualifications

The episode presents the bull through English Protestant security consequences. Its Catholic theological claims and reception among individual English Catholics are not fully analyzed here, so the page should not imply uniform Catholic action after 1570.

## What Changed

- Adds the papal bull as the explicit loyalty rupture that hardens Elizabethan anti-Catholic security politics.
