# John Kerr

## Overview

John Kerr was the Australian governor-general who dismissed Prime Minister Gough Whitlam during the 1975 supply crisis and appointed opposition leader Malcolm Fraser.

## Current Profile

[[188-australian-prime-ministers-harold-holt-malcolm-fraser-part-2-glt4635125602]] presents Kerr as a former Labor-associated figure appointed by [[GoughWhitlam|Whitlam]] but increasingly alienated from him. On 11 November 1975, with supply blocked and government funds running down, Kerr used the governor-general's reserve powers to dismiss Whitlam and commission [[MalcolmFraser|Fraser]]. That decision makes him the institutional hinge of the [[AustralianConstitutionalCrisis1975|1975 Australian constitutional crisis]].

## Key Characteristics

- Governor-general appointed during Whitlam's government.
- Had earlier associations with Labor before moving politically away.
- Feared that Whitlam might remove him during the crisis.
- Dismissed Whitlam on 11 November 1975.
- Commissioned Fraser as prime minister pending an election.
- Remains central to arguments over legality, convention, and democratic legitimacy.

- Relationship and decision: [[188-australian-prime-ministers-harold-holt-malcolm-fraser-part-2-glt4635125602]] links Kerr's political background and deteriorating relationship with Whitlam to the surprise dismissal.

## Qualifications

This one-source account is compressed and sometimes psychologizes Kerr's motives. It does not fully reconstruct legal advice, correspondence, reserve-power doctrine, or the later archival dispute, so claims about fear, resentment, and foreign involvement remain attributed or source-scoped.
