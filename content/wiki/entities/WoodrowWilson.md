# Woodrow Wilson

## Overview

Woodrow Wilson appears in [[673-the-first-world-war-the-submarine-strikes-part-3-glt3632974949]] as the U.S. president managing American neutrality after the sinking of [[RMSLusitania|RMS Lusitania]].

## Current Profile

The source presents Wilson as a restraint-minded neutral leader under rising public and diplomatic pressure. After the deaths of 128 Americans on Lusitania, he initially emphasized peace and avoided immediate war, but he also sent Germany protest notes warning that further attacks could have grave consequences. His position split American politics and his own Cabinet, contributing to Secretary of State William Jennings Bryan's resignation.

Wilson's role in this page is therefore source-bounded: he is not yet the later wartime president in a full U.S. entry into war narrative, but he is the person through whom [[NeutralityToInterventionMemory]] begins to harden.

## Key Characteristics

- U.S. president trying to preserve neutrality after German submarine attacks affected American citizens.
- Publicly restrained after Lusitania, including the episode's cited line about being "too proud to fight."
- Pressured from the interventionist side by figures such as [[TheodoreRoosevelt]].
- Used protest notes to warn Germany that future attacks on American lives and ships could amount to a war issue.
- Faced internal dissent when William Jennings Bryan resigned over the perceived provocativeness of the protest.
- Kept the United States out of the war in 1915 and campaigned in 1916 on that record.
- Became part of the later path by which Lusitania memory helped frame Germany's 1917 submarine escalation.

## Evidence

- Neutral restraint: [[673-the-first-world-war-the-submarine-strikes-part-3-glt3632974949]] says Wilson emphasized peace after Lusitania and the United States did not enter the war in 1915.
- Diplomatic escalation: [[673-the-first-world-war-the-submarine-strikes-part-3-glt3632974949]] describes protest notes warning Germany against further attacks affecting Americans.
- Cabinet split: [[673-the-first-world-war-the-submarine-strikes-part-3-glt3632974949]] links Bryan's resignation to disagreement over Wilson's protest strategy.
- Delayed intervention: [[673-the-first-world-war-the-submarine-strikes-part-3-glt3632974949]] stresses that the United States did not enter immediately, but that Lusitania shaped the later atmosphere.
