# 铜雀台 / Tongque Platform

## Overview

铜雀台 / Tongque Platform is the monumental platform that Hanji 1115 places at [[YeLateHan|邺城]] under [[CaoCao|曹操]]. Its durable wiki significance is twofold: it projects northern conquest into built political memory, and its later literary afterlife shows how a famous fictional scene can detach a monument from its chronology.

## Current Profile

The source dates construction to the winter of Jian'an 15, after Cao Cao's consolidation of the north, and reads the platform as a display of achievement and authority. A bronze-bird discovery supplies the episode's naming legend, but that omen story remains source-scoped.

The platform's best-known popular association comes from [[RomanceOfTheThreeKingdoms|《三国演义》]] and the image of “铜雀春深锁二乔.” Hanji 1115 rejects a literal historical reading: the platform and the relevant fu did not yet exist when the novel places [[ZhugeLiang|诸葛亮]]'s provocation of [[ZhouYuLateHan|周瑜]] before Red Cliffs. The platform is therefore both a political monument and a chronology test for separating literary drama from event history.

## Key Characteristics

- Monumental platform at late-Han Ye associated with Cao Cao's regime.
- Built-environment expression of northern conquest, achievement, and authority in the episode's reading.
- Subject of a bronze-bird naming legend that is not independently verified here.
- Later literary symbol connected to the Two Qiaos and Red Cliffs despite a chronological mismatch.

## Evidence

- Monument and political display: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1115-caocao-jian-tongquetai-zhen-jiu-weile-suo-erqiao-lhqombf5uxbwvg_pz8atvk9l-izs|Hanji 1115]] dates the construction to Jian'an 15 and interprets it as a public statement of Cao Cao's accomplishments.
- Literary afterlife and chronology: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1115-caocao-jian-tongquetai-zhen-jiu-weile-suo-erqiao-lhqombf5uxbwvg_pz8atvk9l-izs|Hanji 1115]] notes that Red Cliffs predates both the platform and the relevant fu, making the novel's persuasion scene fictional construction.

## Qualifications

This profile is grounded in one podcast source note. The exact construction chronology, architectural form, naming tradition, and textual history should be checked against underlying historical and literary sources before being treated as exhaustive. Rejecting the Two-Qiaos scene as history does not reduce its importance as literature or cultural memory.

## What Changed

- Created a canonical page separating the historical monument from its later literary association.
- Recorded the platform's political-display function and the chronological objection to the “locking the Two Qiaos” story.
