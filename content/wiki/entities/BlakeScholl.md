# Blake Scholl

## Overview
Blake Scholl is the founder and CEO of [[BoomSupersonic]], a commercial supersonic-aircraft company developing [[OvertureSupersonicAirliner|Overture]], the [[XB1SupersonicDemonstrator|XB-1]] demonstrator, and an internally controlled engine path.

## Current Profile
Across the three bounded sources, Scholl is an outsider hard-tech founder who moved from software and [[BarcodeHero|mobile commerce]] into aviation through personal motivation, self-education, market modeling, [[RecursiveExpertRecruiting]], and staged technical proof. The Part 2 interview sharpens his profile as an integrated product and industrial operator: he treats the supplier-engine failure, [[AircraftPropulsionCoDesign|engine–airframe co-design]], a data-center turbine, flexible manufacturing, and the supersonic mission as one control-and-financing strategy.

## Key Characteristics
- He chose faster passenger travel as a mission he considered worth the durable pain of founding a company, despite lacking a conventional aerospace résumé.
- He used textbooks, market models, expert chains, [[YCombinator]], customer intent, and XB-1 to replace assumed credentials with successive evidence.
- He interprets the [[RollsRoyce|Rolls-Royce]] break as a strategic error corrected through [[CrisisForcedVerticalIntegration|engine ownership]] and in-house manufacturing.
- He combines product advocacy with regulatory entrepreneurship, using visible flight proof and [[BoomlessCruise]] to argue for performance-based overland rules.
- He now frames [[GroundPowerEngineValidationFlywheel|ground power]] as an adjacent market that could finance aviation and generate engine-reliability evidence.
- He argues that commercial manufacturing and reconfigurable production can create [[CommercialManufacturingSurgeCapacity|defense surge capacity]], while acknowledging no demonstrated conversion case.
