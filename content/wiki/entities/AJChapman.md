# A. J. Chapman

## Overview

A. J. Chapman was the Oklahoma medical examiner who proposed the drug sequence that became the widely adopted three-drug lethal-injection protocol.

## Current Profile

In [[TheAlabamaMurders]], Chapman proposed a sedative and paralytic in 1977 and then added potassium chloride to stop the heart. Oklahoma adopted the sequence rapidly and other states copied it, although the episode says it had not been experimentally tested. Chapman later defended the absence of testing by arguing that the drugs' properties were already familiar from anesthesia, an analogy the episode contests because clinical anesthesia aims to preserve life while execution uses extreme doses to cause death.

## Key Characteristics

- Oklahoma medical examiner associated with the original three-drug lethal-injection sequence.
- Designer working in response to constitutional and public concern about visible execution methods.
- Proponent of a sedative, paralytic, and potassium-chloride sequence.
- Figure whose medical analogy helped an untested protocol acquire an appearance of humane expertise.
- Later defender of the view that separate experimental testing was unnecessary.

## Evidence

### Protocol design and adoption

- [[the-alabama-murders-part-4-the-protocol]] attributes the 1977 sequence to Chapman and reports its rapid legislative approval in Oklahoma and adoption by 30 other states.

### Testing rationale

- [[the-alabama-murders-part-4-the-protocol]] reports that the protocol was not experimentally tested and that Chapman regarded existing anesthetic knowledge as sufficient.

## Qualifications

This profile relies on one structured episode summary rather than Chapman's original proposal, legislative record, correspondence, later interviews, pharmacology evidence, or competing histories of lethal injection. It does not establish his full intentions, every revision to the protocol, or uniform adoption across states.
