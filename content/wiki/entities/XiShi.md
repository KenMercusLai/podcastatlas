## Overview

西施 is the historically remembered beauty and [[FanLi|范蠡]]'s beloved in [[YueNvJian|《越女剑》]], where her climactic appearance stops [[AQingYueNvJian|阿青]]'s attempted attack and gives “西子捧心” a martial-romantic origin.

## Current Profile

Within the source's literary reading, Xi Shi is powerful through absence and reaction. Fan Li describes her through landscape-like images while failing to notice Ah Qing's questions; at the climax Xi Shi scarcely needs speech because Ah Qing's amazement establishes her beauty. Ah Qing stops the bamboo staff, but its residual force injures Xi Shi's chest, joining the public image of beauty in pain to the private wound of a rival who leaves unseen.

This page does not resolve the historical Xi Shi. It records the source's analysis of Jin Yong's fictionalized figure and the way later memory privileges her visible beauty over Ah Qing's unrecorded loss.

## Key Characteristics

- She is initially constructed through Fan Li's memory rather than direct presence.
- Her beauty is conveyed through another woman's halted violence and astonishment rather than self-description.
- She connects private love to Yue-Wu political history without being reduced to a developed political agent in this source.
- “西子捧心” becomes both an image of beauty and evidence of the conflict that produced it.
- Her prominence in later memory contrasts with Ah Qing's narrative disappearance.

## Qualifications

The source is literary criticism of a fictional work, not an independent historical reconstruction. Xi Shi's relationship with Fan Li, political role, inner state, beauty, injury, and the origin of “西子捧心” are discussed as elements of the novella or the hosts' interpretation and should not be promoted into historical fact.
