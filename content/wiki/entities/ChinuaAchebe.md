# Chinua Achebe

## Overview

Chinua Achebe was a Nigerian novelist and critic whose 1975 lecture argued that [[JosephConrad]]'s [[HeartOfDarkness|Heart of Darkness]] dehumanizes Africans and uses Africa as a backdrop for European moral discovery.

## Current Profile

The episode treats Achebe's intervention as foundational to postcolonial criticism and accepts important parts of it. African characters in the novella seldom receive ordinary speech or full interiority, while primeval imagery can turn the continent into Europe's symbolic opposite. The hosts nevertheless resist reducing the work entirely to that indictment, arguing that its attack on colonial violence retains moral force.

## Key Characteristics

- Reframed discussion of *Heart of Darkness* by making its racial representation central rather than secondary.
- Objected to Africa's use as a setting in which Europeans discover their own inner darkness.
- Highlighted the lack of serious African characters and the dehumanizing treatment of African speech and bodies.
- Serves in the episode as a necessary challenge to a purely celebratory anti-imperial reading.

## Qualifications

This page covers Achebe only as a critic of Conrad. It does not summarize his fiction, his full lecture, or the broader scholarly debate that followed it.
