# 黄权 / Huang Quan (Late Han)

## Overview

黄权 / Huang Quan enters the current evidence as a Yizhou official whose correct warning about [[LiuBei|刘备]] as a rival power center is followed by service under Liu Bei and action on the Hanzhong-Ba frontier.

## Current Profile

Hanji 1124 places Huang Quan at the decision stage before Liu Bei receives troops, supplies, or delegated command. He argues that treating Liu Bei as an ordinary subordinate will not satisfy a leader of Liu Bei's standing, while receiving him with full honors will give the guest enough security and status to endanger the host. Liu Zhang rejects the warning and sends Huang Quan to Guanghan.

The warning identifies a structural dilemma rather than merely distrusting Liu Bei's character: a strong invited ally may be too autonomous to subordinate but too dangerous to empower.

Hanji 1155 supplies the first later-career bridge. Huang Quan has warned Liu Bei that control of Hanzhong bears directly on the security of Ba and Yizhou. Liu Bei then sends him toward Zhang Lu or associated Ba-region targets; after learning that Zhang Lu has already submitted to [[CaoCao|曹操]], Huang Quan turns to other operations. The source says the specific action it narrates occurred later than its placement beside the 215 aftermath, so the page retains the strategic continuity while keeping the operational chronology qualified.

## Key Characteristics

- Identifies the dual-power risk before Liu Bei's entry and material empowerment.
- Frames the problem through incompatible host choices: insufficient subordination or dangerous honor and autonomy.
- Gives costly advice that Liu Zhang rejects before removing him from the immediate decision center.
- Later serves Liu Bei and identifies Hanzhong as a gateway whose loss threatens Ba and Yizhou.
- Adapts his objective after Zhang Lu's submission removes the original diplomatic or military target.

## Qualifications

The supplied podcast summaries do not establish the exact wording, office chronology, administrative significance of the Guanghan assignment, or Huang Quan's full later career. The “two tigers” framing is an interpretation of the power structure, not proof that every form of external assistance was impossible. Hanji 1155's proposed 217 date, target identities, route, and sequence require direct textual verification.
