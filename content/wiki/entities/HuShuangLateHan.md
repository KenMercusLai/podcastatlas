## Overview

胡爽 / Hu Shuang is a late-Han Nan Commandery chief clerk whose current wiki profile is source-bounded to the 162 CE Jingzhou crisis. He becomes the duty-facing counterpoint to [[LiSuNanCommanderyLateHan|南郡太守李肃]]: when Li Su tries to flee after regional defeat and fear, Hu Shuang argues that an official responsible for connected cities must mobilize aid rather than abandon office.

## Current Profile

Hu Shuang appears in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-856-lishi-gaosu-ni-ren-ruo-xinluan-zhenjiao-zi-luan-lo7m-9mz-pvstxtfgavrne4bszks|Hanji 856]] after the southern rebellion branch has already exposed Ma Mu, Liu Du, and Li Su's fear. Li Su plans to desert Nan Commandery while Wuling Man forces are pressing the Jiangling region. Hu Shuang physically holds Li Su's horse and remonstrates that a commandery with cities nearby should raise flags and drums, send signals, and call other counties to help rather than throw away its seal and become a fugitive.

The source presents Hu Shuang less as a successful crisis commander than as an ethical boundary in a collapsing command chain. His advice does not save the immediate situation, because Li Su kills him and flees anyway. But Emperor Huan's later response, executing Li Su while reducing Ma Mu and Liu Du's penalties, makes Hu Shuang's death the benchmark against which dereliction and panic are judged.

## Key Characteristics

- Nan Commandery chief clerk under Li Su during the 162 CE Jingzhou crisis.
- Treats military emergency as a duty to mobilize nearby jurisdictions rather than preserve personal safety.
- Remonstrates directly and physically by holding Li Su's horse when the administrator tries to flee.
- Killed by Li Su, making him the crisis's strongest contrast to panic-driven dereliction.
- Posthumously honored through family exemption and appointment, according to the normalized source context.
- Bridges official duty, local crisis coordination, and emotion discipline under fear.
