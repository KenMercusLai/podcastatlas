# Stephen Copley

## Overview

Stephen Copley is an Australian researcher represented as a co-developer of [[MaturityBasedCorrectiveAdjustment|Maturity-Based Corrective Adjustment Procedures]] for adolescent swimming.

## Current Profile

In [[outliers-revisited-outliers-revisited|Outliers, Revisited]], Copley and [[MichaelRomann]] use height, weight, chronological age, and sitting height to estimate biological maturity and provisionally adjust swimming times. The purpose is not to erase competition results but to reveal late-developing swimmers whose current performance may understate underlying potential.

The episode asks Copley whether the same principle could extend to education. He accepts that age and development may matter while raising the unresolved problem of how many other influential factors an academic system would also need to adjust.

## Key Characteristics

- Co-developer of a maturity-based correction procedure for youth swimming.
- Focuses on distinguishing present performance from developmentally obscured potential.
- Treats transfer from sport to education as an open design problem rather than an established result.

## Evidence

### Swimming adjustment

- [[outliers-revisited-outliers-revisited]] reports that the procedure changed every examined race order and could move some late maturers from outside the top twenty into the top three.

### Cross-domain caution

- [[outliers-revisited-outliers-revisited]] attributes to Copley the question of which additional influences would require adjustment in education.

## Qualifications

The episode summary does not supply the underlying paper, validation design, error bounds, sex and age coverage, or prospective retention outcomes. Adjusted ranking is a talent-identification aid, not proof of later elite performance or a validated academic analogue.

- Preserved Copley's caution about transferring the method into education.
