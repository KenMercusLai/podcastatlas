# Sonja Lyubomirsky

## Overview
Sonja Lyubomirsky is the psychologist represented in a [[HiddenBrain]] episode about happiness forecasting, [[HedonicAdaptationAndContrast]], relationships, savoring, and the changing emotional meaning of fortune.

## Current Profile
Lyubomirsky's account begins with a recurring prediction error: people imagine the dramatic moment of gaining sight, a job, money, status, or a relationship, then underrepresent the ordinary routines that will surround it. Her own LASIK surgery and academic career illustrate how quickly a formerly extraordinary condition can become background. She connects that shift to rising aspirations and new comparison groups rather than treating it as simple ingratitude.

Her practical position is neither permanent positivity nor passive acceptance. Variety, intentional gaps, imagined loss, gratitude, social contact, and intrinsically rewarding activity may renew attention to familiar goods. At the same time, adaptation can aid recovery after setbacks, severe circumstances remain consequential, and immediate labels of good or bad fortune may change as consequences unfold.

## Key Characteristics
- Explains happiness prediction errors through focal attention to change and neglect of ordinary future context.
- Treats hedonic adaptation as a mechanism affecting gains and losses rather than a moral failure to appreciate life.
- Connects escalating aspirations and shifting social comparison with the hedonic treadmill.
- Presents scarcity, variety, imagined absence, impermanence, gratitude, and savoring as bounded attention practices.
- Distinguishes passionate love from companionate love without treating reduced intensity as automatic relationship failure.
- Emphasizes social connection and intrinsic activity rewards as renewable contributors to daily wellbeing.

## Qualifications
This profile is bounded to one structured podcast summary rather than Lyubomirsky's complete publications, institutional biography, measures, or research record. Personal examples illustrate her interpretation but do not establish population effects. The episode's claims about genetics, culture, adaptation speed, writing, savoring, relationships, social interaction, motivation, and intervention effects require underlying studies before effect size, causal direction, population scope, or clinical use can be established. Severe poverty, abuse, war, disability, illness, trauma, and depression cannot be reduced to forecasting error or gratitude practice.
