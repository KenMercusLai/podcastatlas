# James Connolly

## Overview

James Connolly was an Edinburgh-born Irish socialist, labour organizer, former British soldier, and commander of the [[IrishCitizenArmy|Irish Citizen Army]] who joined the [[EasterRising|Easter Rising]].

## Current Profile

The episode places Connolly at the junction of class and national revolution. Dublin's one-room tenements, missing sanitation, and the 1913 lockout supplied a social crisis that nationalist memory can obscure when it treats 1916 only through poetry and flags. The Citizen Army emerged from this labour conflict and later joined the IRB-led rising.

Connolly fought from the GPO command structure, was wounded, and had to be carried during the retreat toward Moore Street. His presence prevents the Rising from being read as only Catholic, cultural, or constitutional nationalism, although the source does not claim that all participants shared his socialism.

## Key Characteristics

- Irish socialist born in Edinburgh to Irish immigrant parents.
- Former British Army soldier whose later politics opposed British rule in Ireland.
- Labour organizer formed by Dublin poverty and the 1913 lockout.
- Leader of the Irish Citizen Army.
- Revolutionary commander wounded during the GPO fighting and retreat.
- Figure linking social grievance to separatist republican action.

## Evidence

- Labour formation: [[339-ireland-the-easter-rising-1916-part-4-glt7095481415]] connects Connolly and the Citizen Army to the 1913 lockout and severe Dublin poverty.
- Revolutionary coalition: [[339-ireland-the-easter-rising-1916-part-4-glt7095481415]] places his militia alongside the IRB and Volunteers without erasing their ideological differences.
- Rising experience: [[339-ireland-the-easter-rising-1916-part-4-glt7095481415]] records his wound and stretcher evacuation from the burning GPO.

## Qualifications

One episode cannot provide a full account of Connolly's socialist thought, trade-union work, military service, or negotiations with the IRB. The Rising's later nationalist memory may understate class politics, but the source does not establish that labour grievance was the dominant motive for all rebels.

## What Changed

- Established Connolly as the principal bridge between Dublin labour conflict and the Rising's republican coalition.
- Kept socialist and nationalist motives connected without treating them as identical.
