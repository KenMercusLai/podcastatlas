# Malcolm X

## Overview

Malcolm X was a Black political leader whose 1963 house-versus-field metaphor is used by the episode to indict successful African Americans who gain access by identifying with white power and turning away from their community.

## Current Profile

The episode begins with Malcolm X's distinction between enslaved people attached to the master's household and those seeking separation and freedom. It extends the metaphor to successful Black Americans whose advancement appears to require loyalty to dominant institutions, initially placing [[SammyDavisJr|Sammy Davis Jr.]] within that harsh moral category.

The rest of the episode does not directly refute the critique but tests its limits. Davis's civil-rights work, racist abuse, economic responsibility, and [[Tokenism|token isolation]] show that visible accommodation can be both politically consequential and structurally constrained. Malcolm X therefore functions here as the clearest statement of community accountability, not as the final or complete explanation of Davis.

## Key Characteristics

- Uses a slavery-derived metaphor to distinguish identification with domination from the pursuit of freedom.
- Directs moral scrutiny toward successful Black figures whose access appears to depend on community betrayal.
- Supplies the episode's strongest initial interpretation of Davis as a sellout.
- Functions as a counterweight to explanations that could turn structural constraint into complete exoneration.

## Qualifications

This is a narrow profile derived from one episode summary and one reported 1963 speech, not a full account of Malcolm X's life, evolving politics, religious thought, organizing, writings, or later views. The episode uses a provocative historical metaphor as an interpretive frame; that metaphor should not flatten differences among slavery, twentieth-century employment, celebrity, electoral politics, and voluntary association.
