# 脉脉 / Maimai

## Overview
Maimai / 脉脉 is a Chinese professional-networking, workplace-community, and recruiting platform founded by [[LinFanMaimai|林凡]].

## Current Profile
The earlier mobile-community history source presents Maimai as a Chinese [[LinkedIn]]-like product whose professional identity and second-degree connections were joined by a high-engagement workplace discussion layer. The newer founder interview makes the operating model more precise: recruitment is described as the main revenue business, community supplies workplace and industry information, and real-identity networking supports job seeking, sales, business development, marketing, and recruiting.

The current synthesis replaces the loose label “anonymous community” with [[VerifiedPseudonymousWorkplaceCommunity|verified pseudonymity]]. Users can publish under nicknames, but Lin says the backend verifies real identity, company, and occupation. That design can lower retaliation risk around layoffs, pay, management, and business discussion, while still leaving the platform responsible for rumor, defamation, privacy, emotional escalation, and moderation.

The platform's career value may rise as easy job mobility weakens. In Lin's account, users who once needed little more than a résumé now have more reason to maintain professional ties, visible competence, and occupational reputation. This is a founder interpretation, not independent proof that Chinese professional networking will converge on LinkedIn's market pattern.

## Key Characteristics
- Combines recruitment, workplace community, and identity-based professional networking in one product.
- Uses nickname-facing expression over backend identity and employment verification.
- Serves as a workplace information surface for layoffs, compensation, employers, managers, and industry change.
- Monetizes principally through recruiting in the founder's account while community activity supports attention and information value.
- Faces persistent [[PlatformCommunityGovernance]] tension between protected expression, credibility, privacy, rumor control, and fair moderation.
- Becomes more strategically relevant when job search depends on relationships and visible professional reputation rather than abundant offers alone.

## Qualifications
Maimai's user count, activity mix, deletion-vote outcomes, job-demand data, and market-leading status are founder-reported in the new interview. Backend verification can deter some abuse and support credibility, but it does not prove every post, eliminate retaliation risk, or resolve the tradeoff between protected speech and reputational harm. The earlier source's “anonymous” wording is retained as historical shorthand but narrowed by the newer operational description.
