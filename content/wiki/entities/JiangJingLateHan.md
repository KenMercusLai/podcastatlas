## Overview
Jiang Jing is introduced in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-795-dengjia-miewang-hou-weihe-juchao-wei-tamen-mingyuan-lqvmbqrrookphb4becaqbp7h-wi1|Hanji 795]] as a palace actor rewarded for helping bring [[EmperorAnOfHan|Emperor An]] from Qinghe to the throne. His titles and offices make him a key early node in the post-Deng favorite network alongside [[LiRunLateHan|Li Run]], [[WangShengPalaceActorLateHan|Wang Sheng]], [[BoRongLateHan|Bo Rong]], and [[FanFengEunuchLateHan|Fan Feng]].

## Current Profile
The source frames Jiang Jing as a service-to-access beneficiary. His accession service is converted into enfeoffment as Duxiang Hou, promotion to Zhongchangshi, and appointment as Da Changqiu, placing him close to imperial household channels just as the Deng regency network collapses.

Jiang Jing's importance is structural. Hanji 795 does not yet show a specific policy forged by him, but it places him inside the same access environment that Yang Zhen criticizes: rewards for intimate service, palace proximity, and household transmission replace the public appointment order that anti-Deng politics claimed to restore.

## Key Characteristics
- Eunuch or palace-intimate actor rewarded for escorting Emperor An from Qinghe during the accession transition.
- Enfeoffed as Duxiang Hou and promoted to Zhongchangshi after the Deng family's fall.
- Also identified as Da Changqiu, giving him a powerful household-office position near imperial women and palace administration.
- Paired with Li Run as an accession-service favorite and with Fan Feng, Wang Sheng, and Bo Rong in a wider post-Deng network.
- Evidence currently shows position and access more than a detailed independent policy agenda.
