## Overview

湖阳公主 / Princess Huyang is [[LiuXiu|光武帝刘秀]]'s sister in two current source-bounded scenes. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-685-donghan-dachen-songhong-weihe-buken-zuo-guangwudi-jiefu-ljafg8giyhg5wmumv-g-09lywcu2|Hanji 685]] presents her as a widow who praises [[SongHongEasternHan|宋弘]] and prompts Liu Xiu's indirect marriage test. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-721-ta-sha-jianu-ma-gongzhu-weihe-fanbei-shang-lryzcejdnrm4-92esnddfvyjkqli|Hanji 721]] later presents her protected slave case, where [[DongXuanEasternHan|董宣]]'s enforcement becomes politically meaningful because the offender is shielded by a royal household.

## Current Profile

Hanji 685 gives the earlier marriage scene. Huyang is widowed and, while discussing court officials with Liu Xiu, praises Song Hong's appearance, virtue, and moral standing. Liu Xiu then hides Huyang behind a screen and tests Song by saying that high rank and wealth make it natural to change friends or wives. Song answers with the "糟糠之妻不下堂" maxim, so Liu Xiu tells Huyang the matter cannot be arranged.

Hanji 721 presents Princess Huyang through a later legal conflict. A household slave commits murder and hides in the princess residence, leaving ordinary officials unable or unwilling to enter. When the princess later travels with the slave in her carriage, Dong Xuan intercepts the procession, rebukes her for sheltering the offender, and executes the slave.

Afterward, Princess Huyang complains to Liu Xiu and tries to turn the case into an imperial-authority comparison. She says that when Liu Xiu was still a commoner he could hide fugitives without officials daring to enter, but now as emperor he seems less powerful than a county magistrate. Liu Xiu answers that emperor and commoner are different, then releases Dong Xuan.

The sources therefore use Princess Huyang structurally in two different ways. In Hanji 685, her court proximity creates a possible princess marriage that remains bounded because Liu Xiu accepts Song's refusal. In Hanji 721, her household proximity makes an offender protected, but that same proximity lets Liu Xiu make a public signal by refusing to reverse Dong Xuan's enforcement.

## Key Characteristics

- Eastern Han princess and Liu Xiu's sister in the episode's legal-conflict scene.
- Widowed royal woman whose praise of Song Hong prompts Liu Xiu's indirect marriage test.
- Court-proximity figure whose marriage interest remains non-coercive because Liu Xiu accepts Song Hong's boundary.
- Royal-household protector whose residence initially shields a murderous slave from ordinary arrest.
- Uses palace access to complain directly to Liu Xiu after Dong Xuan's enforcement.
- Becomes the protected-household side of an early Eastern Han law-over-kinship case.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 721 and Hanji 685 and does not infer Princess Huyang's full life, property, marriage history beyond widowhood, or later political role. Hanji 685 shows royal marriage interest but not coercion; the refusal is accepted. Hanji 721's point is not that every princess household had the same legal position; it is that this one protected household becomes an unusually clear test of whether royal kinship can shelter lethal wrongdoing. The transcript's 胡阳公主 is normalized to 湖阳公主 and kept distinct from [[HuyangLateHan|胡阳]], the unrelated late-Han campaign place.
