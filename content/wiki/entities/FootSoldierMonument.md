# Foot Soldier Monument

## Overview

*Foot Soldier* is [[RonaldMcDowell]]'s 1995 sculpture in Birmingham's Kelly Ingram Park, based on Bill Hudson's 1963 photograph of [[WalterGadsden]], [[RichardMiddleton]], and the police dog Leo.

## Current Profile

The monument was commissioned under Birmingham's first Black mayor, Richard Arrington, and dedicated to ordinary participants in the city's civil-rights struggle. It gives physical permanence to an image associated with the campaign that exposed segregation and helped build national pressure for civil-rights legislation.

It is not a literal three-dimensional copy. McDowell shrank and youthfully recast Gadsden, enlarged and visually blinded Middleton, made Leo wolf-like, and changed Gadsden's posture into open-handed nonviolence. Those choices clarify a symbolic confrontation between racist state power and an innocent Black child, but they also overwrite Gadsden's bystander identity and the episode's evidence that Middleton may have been restraining the dog.

## Key Characteristics

- Public sculpture unveiled in Kelly Ingram Park in May 1995.
- Commissioned to honor ordinary participants in Birmingham's civil-rights movement.
- Derived from an iconic 1963 news photograph but deliberately departs from it.
- Uses scale, posture, vision, and animal form to create a clear moral hierarchy.
- Embodies Black civic authority over a landscape and history previously controlled by white power.
- Holds symbolic historical truth in tension with disputed facts about named individuals.

## Qualifications

The monument's broader account of Birmingham's racist order does not verify its apparent story about these three figures. Conversely, correcting Gadsden's role and qualifying Middleton's intention do not make the city's segregation or police violence unreal.
