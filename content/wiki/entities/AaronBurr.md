# Aaron Burr / 阿伦·伯尔

## Overview

Aaron Burr was the U.S. vice president who killed [[AlexanderHamilton|Alexander Hamilton]] in the 1804 [[HamiltonBurrDuel|Hamilton-Burr duel]] and whose political reputation was permanently damaged by the event.

## Current Profile

The wiki now treats Burr as more than a compact dueling example. The Hamilton episode presents him as Hamilton's mirror: orphaned young, educated at Princeton, brave in the Continental Army, successful as a New York lawyer and politician, and capable of progressive positions on slavery, nativism, and women's votes. At the same time, the episode's central judgment is that Burr appeared opportunistic and power-seeking, especially in the 1800 election deadlock, his vice presidency, and his 1804 attempt to win New York with Federalist support. The duel makes that ambiguity politically fatal: he may have acted within the ritual frame, but the source keeps open the darker possibility that ambition and resentment shaped the fatal shot.

## Key Characteristics

- Talented founding-era lawyer, soldier, and New York politician.
- Aligned with the [[DemocraticRepublicanParty|Democratic-Republicans]] while remaining more personally opportunistic than programmatic in the episode's portrait.
- Became vice president after the 1800 Jefferson-Burr tie and House deadlock.
- Treated Hamilton's reported insult and opposition in New York as an honor challenge.
- Fired the shot that killed Hamilton at Weehawken on July 11, 1804.
- Politically ruined after the duel, then later tried for treason over a disputed western plot without proven conviction in the episode's account.

## Qualifications

The Hamilton episode explicitly warns that Burr's character is difficult to recover because hostile accounts and later villainy shape the archive. The duel page therefore records Burr as dangerously ambitious in the source's interpretation, not as a fully settled psychological verdict.

- Expanded Burr from a brief U.S. dueling example into a political actor shaped by party conflict, ambition, honor culture, and post-duel ruin.
- Added the source's qualification that Burr's villain image depends partly on hostile and retrospective evidence.
