## Overview

刘兴 / Liu Xing, Zhongshan King, enters the wiki as [[HanChengdi|汉成帝]]'s younger brother and the main alternative to the Dingtao king [[HanAidi|刘欣]] in the late-Chengdi succession problem. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-551-jiemi-hanchengdi-de-huangwei-chuancheng-zhimi-lkefo-pkg_n8ll57kdsofcrpad_v|Hanji 551]] gives the performance layer: Liu Xing appears ill-prepared in court questioning, classics recitation, dining etiquette, and dress awareness, while Liu Xin performs legal and ritual competence. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-552-tongtou-renxing-de-ren-zhichang-pingbuqingyun-lnnhr8e-67qdqwhzi1h0qav-valw|Hanji 552]] gives the formal debate layer: [[KongGuangWesternHan|孔光]] argues from kinship proximity and [[Shangshu|《尚书》]] precedent that the Zhongshan king should be heir, but Chengdi rejects that route and compensates the Zhongshan side.

## Current Profile

Hanji 551 shows Liu Xing's candidacy before the formal palace debate. He is one of the closest eligible imperial kinsmen because Chengdi has no surviving son. Yet when he comes to Chang'an, he brings only one tutor, cannot explain the limited retinue when Chengdi asks, recites Shangshu badly, continues eating after Chengdi has stopped, and rises without noticing that his sock-tie has come loose. Chengdi reads these signs as lack of capacity, especially when they are contrasted with Liu Xin's legal fluency, Shijing interpretation, and more impressive escort.

The episode does not flatten Liu Xing into simple incompetence. The host suggests that he may have seen the succession field clearly: Chengdi favored Liu Xin, Fu Taihou was already building palace support, and losing a struggle for heir status could be deadly. In that reading, Liu Xing's poor display can be interpreted as a possible protective retreat, though this remains source inference rather than a stated motive.

Hanji 552 then turns Liu Xing into the object of Kong Guang's classical dissent. Kong Guang argues that, as Chengdi's brother, the Zhongshan king is closer in kinship than the Dingtao king and fits a brother-succession precedent. Chengdi rejects the position, partly on fitness and partly on ritual discomfort with turning a brother into a son for temple succession, then gives the Zhongshan side honors and added households as compensation.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han royal kinsman and Chengdi's younger brother whose claim competes with the Dingtao nephew line.
- Presented in Hanji 551 as failing visible court-performance tests around officials, classics, dining, and dress.
- Possible case of self-protective underperformance, but only as host inference.
- Becomes the candidate defended by Kong Guang's Shangshu-backed succession argument in Hanji 552.
- Ultimately loses the succession contest to Liu Xin while receiving compensatory treatment through the Zhongshan side.

## Qualifications

The page does not settle Liu Xing's inner motive. Hanji 551 supports the visible performance failures and the host's possible self-protection interpretation, but it does not preserve Liu Xing's own explanation. Hanji 552 supports Kong Guang's argument for the Zhongshan candidate and Chengdi's rejection, but not the full legal record of Liu Xing's household, later life, or every compensatory measure. The source transcript's Dingtao-name confusion is normalized here by distinguishing [[HanAidi|刘欣]] from Liu Xing.

## What Changed

- Created a separate Zhongshan king page so the succession alternative in Hanji 551 and Hanji 552 no longer sits as an unnamed candidate.
- Linked the visible incompetence account to Kong Guang's later classical argument while preserving the self-protection reading as source-scoped.
