# Second Mexican Empire

## Overview

The Second Mexican Empire was the French-backed monarchy ruled by [[FerdinandMaximilian]] from 1864 until republican victory and his execution in 1867.

## Current Profile

The empire emerged from Mexico's liberal-conservative civil conflict, suspended foreign debt payments, [[NapoleonIII]]'s hemispheric ambitions, and the temporary constraint placed on the [[UnitedStates]] by its civil war. Mexican conservatives supplied the invitation and France supplied decisive coercive capacity, while [[BenitoJuarez|Benito Juarez]] maintained the constitutional republican alternative.

The regime's internal coalition was unstable. Maximilian retained liberal Church-property reforms and pursued other progressive measures, disappointing conservative Catholics without erasing the monarchy's occupation-backed origin. Bankruptcy, renewed U.S. pressure, French withdrawal, and inadequate local military support then exposed the absence of an autonomous base.

## Key Characteristics

- Created through the convergence of domestic conservative sponsorship and French intervention.
- Depended on foreign military power more than demonstrated national consent.
- Combined a conservative monarchical invitation with a liberal monarch's policies.
- Operated inside a narrow geopolitical window created by the U.S. Civil War.
- Collapsed when external support ended and republican resistance persisted.
- Left competing republican-national and romantic-imperial memories.

## Qualifications

The episode is a compact narrative centered on Maximilian and does not provide a complete regional, social, military, fiscal, or Indigenous history of the empire. Claims about popular support and the inevitability of republican victory remain sensitive to time, place, and source selection.
