## Overview

孔仅 / Kong Jin enters the wiki as a merchant-background Western Han fiscal official whom Hanji 340-2 associates with state use of commercial and metallurgical expertise and promotion to da nong ling under [[HanWudi|汉武帝]].

## Current Profile

The bounded evidence is brief but now covers both recruitment and later promotion. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-321-huangdi-gen-shangren-qiang-fanwan-ni-jianguo-ma-luab_ikpgy_tmykmw-6e6voi-ghv|Hanji 321]] says Kong Jin came from iron commerce and was recruited with salt merchant [[DongguoXianyangWesternHan|东郭咸阳]] because the agricultural-finance administration lacked salt-and-iron operating expertise. It associates the new team with local iron offices that directly control smelting, tool production, and sale, while using a hybrid official-purchase system for salt.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-340-2-gudai-de-qian-shi-ruhe-zhuzao-de-ljfkh_x6jed2lwvddgyuln6hlb1h|Hanji 340-2]] later promotes Kong Jin to da nong ling while [[SangHongyangWesternHan|桑弘羊]] becomes da nong cheng. His value in both accounts is functional: the court uses knowledge drawn from commerce and production to expand revenue and operate salt-and-iron policy.

This does not yet establish Kong Jin's complete career, his individual share of policy design, or a direct operational role in the Shanglin Three Offices. The source places his promotion beside monetary reform but specifically attributes the proposal to end local minting to Sang Hongyang.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han official presented as coming from iron commerce.
- Recruited for commercial and technical experience during fiscal pressure.
- Associated with salt-and-iron state administration.
- Linked to local iron offices controlling smelting, tool production, and sale, without proof of his sole authorship or jurisdiction.
- Promoted to da nong ling while Sang Hongyang is promoted to da nong cheng.
- Illustrates the court's conversion of private-sector expertise into central fiscal capacity.

## Qualifications

This profile rests on two compact popular-history sources. It does not reconstruct Kong Jin's dates, full office sequence, relationship with Dongguo Xianyang, responsibility for individual salt-and-iron measures, jurisdiction over particular iron offices, or role in coin production. Hanji 321's “孔杰” and Hanji 340-2's “孔锦” are normalized to the received 孔仅 from the shared iron-merchant and salt-and-iron context; Hanji 340-2's “大农城” is normalized to 大农丞 where the referent is clear.
