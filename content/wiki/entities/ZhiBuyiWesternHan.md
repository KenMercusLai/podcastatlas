## Overview

直不疑 / Zhi Buyi is a Western Han official whom Hanji 244 presents as rising from palace guard service to 御史大夫 while maintaining a deliberately low-profile, non-contentious style.

## Current Profile

The current profile rests on two conduct anecdotes and one administrative characterization. When wrongly accused of taking a colleague's money, Zhi reportedly reimburses the loss instead of contesting the charge; when later accused of adultery with a nonexistent sister-in-law, he does not seek imperial vindication. The source connects this [[AccusationResponseRestraint|accusation-response restraint]] to Huang-Lao learning, routine continuity, reluctance to advertise merit, and the honorific image of a “长者.”

This pattern is narrower than passive submission. The episode depicts Zhi as continuing to hold office and being promoted under [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]], but it gives little information about his substantive decisions as 御史大夫 or the institutional consequences of his silence.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han official promoted from palace guard service through 中大夫 to 御史大夫 in the episode's account.
- Absorbs a mistaken theft accusation by compensating the loss before the truth emerges.
- Declines to answer an impossible accusation that he committed adultery with a sister-in-law despite reportedly having no elder brother.
- Associated by the host with Huang-Lao learning and [[DaoistNaturalWuwei|low-profile non-forcing conduct]].
- Presented as preserving established administrative practice and avoiding publicity for his own achievements.
- Remembered in the source as a “长者,” meaning a person of restrained and modest character.

## Qualifications

The page follows a single popular-history episode rather than a full biography. The source does not establish whether silence caused Zhi's reputation, protected his career, harmed anyone else, or would be prudent under accusations involving public safety, legal liability, or unequal power. The Huang-Lao explanation, exact office sequence, inner thoughts, dialogue, and “长者” interpretation remain source-scoped.
