## Overview

The Iran Prosperity Project is presented by [[RezaPahlavi|Reza Pahlavi]] and [[ShervinPishevar|Shervin Pishevar]] as a transition and reconstruction plan for a post-Islamic-Republic [[Iran]]. The source says it addresses immediate stabilization, a democratic constitutional sequence, and longer-term economic reopening.

## Current Profile

Pahlavi emphasizes the first hundred days: preserve security and public order, avoid state collapse, and include personnel from the existing system who are willing to join the future and are not responsible for atrocities. Pishevar describes a wider 175-page sequence moving from transition to referendum, constitutional assembly, a vote on a constitution, and economic rebuilding.

The project therefore joins political legitimacy to administrative continuity and investment planning. It assumes that a clean break from regime rule need not mean indiscriminate exclusion of existing personnel, and that constitutional choice must precede the final form of government. It also frames reconstruction as a global business opportunity, especially for the Iranian diaspora and the United States.

The supplied episode does not provide the plan itself. Its contents, governance, financing, vetting standards, operational readiness, and public mandate are known only through the advocates' summary.

## Key Characteristics

- Transition plan spanning immediate stabilization, constitutional choice, and economic reconstruction.
- First-hundred-days focus on security, continuity, and selective inclusion of existing personnel.
- Democratic sequence involving a referendum, constitutional assembly, and constitution vote.
- Clean-break principle paired with a stated distinction between regime service and personal responsibility for atrocities.
- Economic-reopening thesis centered on diaspora capacity and international investment.

## Qualifications

This profile is based on one interview and not on the reported 175-page plan. The source does not specify who controls the project, how representatives were selected, how the first hundred days would be financed, who conducts atrocity vetting, what legal authority a transitional administration would possess, how minority and regional interests would be represented, or how security institutions would be subordinated to civilian rule. Economic totals and implementation confidence remain promotional claims.
