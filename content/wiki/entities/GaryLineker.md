# Gary Lineker

## Overview

Gary Lineker is an English former footballer and broadcaster whose first-person World Cup memories connect elite play, national identity, media work, and criticism of football governance.

## Current Profile

[[254-the-world-cup-the-falklands-despots-and-corruption-part-3-glt5891154284]] follows Lineker from childhood memories of Mexico 1970 through his 1986 Golden Boot, England's 1990 semi-final run, and later broadcasting encounters with World Cup host politics. His account makes the tournament both a career-changing sporting stage and a political institution shaped by war memory, corruption allegations, human-rights concerns, and continuing public scrutiny.

## Key Characteristics

- England striker who won the 1986 World Cup Golden Boot.
- First-person witness to England-Argentina in 1986 and England's cultural football revival in 1990.
- Player who treats international selection as emotionally serious rather than cynical professional obligation.
- Broadcaster who preserves the World Cup's sporting value while criticizing FIFA-era host selection and governance.
- Advocate for discussing political and human-rights concerns during tournament coverage rather than letting spectacle displace them.

- Historical witness: [[254-the-world-cup-the-falklands-despots-and-corruption-part-3-glt5891154284]] supplies his memories of Maradona, Falklands framing, Italia 1990, Gascoigne's tears, and anti-German war references.
