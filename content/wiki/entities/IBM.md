# IBM

## Overview
IBM is a computing company whose bounded sources span punched-card administration under Nazi rule, missed relational-database commercialization, the strategic durability of an integrated mainframe stack, and a sponsored commitment to coordinated open-source security. Together they show that technical integration creates competitive capacity, governance responsibility, and obligations toward shared infrastructure on which customers depend.

## Current Profile
The current synthesis holds four periods together without flattening them. IBM's customized punched-card operations are presented as part of data-enabled persecution and corporate complicity; its later database research illustrates how incumbent hardware economics can suppress software commercialization; and its mainframe history illustrates the resilience benefits of controlling differentiated hardware, engineering, and software layers. The newest sponsored source presents IBM and [[RedHat|Red Hat]] pooling capital and engineering through [[ProjectLightwell]] to repair shared open-source dependencies. Vertical integration and ecosystem coordination are therefore neither inherently virtuous nor automatically effective: their value and risk depend on governance, incentives, evidence of outcomes, and how power is shared with users and communities.

## Key Characteristics
- Built tightly integrated information systems whose operational value depended on customization, maintenance, expertise, and organizational control.
- Carries a source-scoped historical responsibility branch around punched-card support for Nazi administration and persecution.
- Illustrates incumbent inhibition when a new software market threatens an established hardware profit pool.
- Uses mainframe integration, chip design, engineering, resilience, and software as a long-duration competitive system.
- Supports selective vertical integration of differentiated layers while purchasing commodity components.
- Presents large-scale pooled remediation as a response to under-resourced shared software, while leaving program governance and outcomes unproven.

## Evidence
### Integration and moral responsibility
- [[133-ibm-yu-nacui-weishenme-putongren-bu-ying-zanmei-jizeizhuyi-815684110]] argues that cards, machines, programs, service, subsidiary control, and expertise linked IBM and [[Dehomag|Dehomag]] to Nazi administrative capacity.

### Incumbent business-model constraint
- [[vol-265-kuayue-50-nian-de-meiguo-banben-zhizi-1001004591]] says IBM research helped define the relational-database opportunity but that [[Oracle]] commercialized it while IBM remained tied to mainframe economics.

### Durable integrated platform
- [[88-audio-nikesharvink-88-audio-nikesharvink]] presents the 65-year mainframe business as evidence that control of differentiated stack layers can support resilience and long-term value even after fabrication is outsourced.

### Shared open-source defense
- [[the-5-billion-effort-to-help-secure-the-world-s-shared-software-the-5-billion-effort-to-help-secure-the-world-s-shared]] presents IBM and Red Hat's $5 billion Project Lightwell commitment, clearinghouse model, and potential 20,000-person scale as a response to AI-accelerated vulnerability discovery and fragmented maintenance.

## Qualifications
- The Nazi-era claims derive from one podcast's historical account and remain source-scoped; the page does not substitute for archival adjudication of legal or individual responsibility.
- The database-commercialization account simplifies a multi-actor history and does not prove that hardware incentives alone determined IBM's choices.
- Mainframe longevity does not show that complete vertical ownership is always efficient; the newer source itself favors buying commodity layers and integrating selectively.
- The Lightwell account is paid IBM content and supplies no independent budget schedule, staffing baseline, governance record, maintainer acceptance evidence, audit, or measured security outcome.

## What Changed
- Added IBM's sponsored move from controlling differentiated proprietary layers toward coordinating repair of shared open-source infrastructure.
- Qualified announced budget and staffing scale with missing governance, community, and outcome evidence.
- Extended the governance judgment from control of IBM's own stack to power shared across an ecosystem.
