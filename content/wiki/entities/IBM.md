# IBM

## Overview
IBM is a computing company whose bounded sources span punched-card administration under Nazi rule, missed relational-database commercialization, and the strategic durability of an integrated mainframe stack. Together they show that technical integration creates both competitive capacity and responsibility for how systems are deployed.

## Current Profile
The current synthesis holds three periods together without flattening them. IBM's customized punched-card operations are presented as part of data-enabled persecution and corporate complicity; its later database research illustrates how incumbent hardware economics can suppress software commercialization; and its mainframe history illustrates the resilience benefits of controlling differentiated hardware, engineering, and software layers. Vertical integration is therefore neither inherently virtuous nor automatically innovative: its value and risk depend on governance, incentives, and which layers the company chooses to control.

## Key Characteristics
- Built tightly integrated information systems whose operational value depended on customization, maintenance, expertise, and organizational control.
- Carries a source-scoped historical responsibility branch around punched-card support for Nazi administration and persecution.
- Illustrates incumbent inhibition when a new software market threatens an established hardware profit pool.
- Uses mainframe integration, chip design, engineering, resilience, and software as a long-duration competitive system.
- Supports selective vertical integration of differentiated layers while purchasing commodity components.

## Evidence
### Integration and moral responsibility
- [[133-ibm-yu-nacui-weishenme-putongren-bu-ying-zanmei-jizeizhuyi-815684110]] argues that cards, machines, programs, service, subsidiary control, and expertise linked IBM and [[Dehomag|Dehomag]] to Nazi administrative capacity.

### Incumbent business-model constraint
- [[vol-265-kuayue-50-nian-de-meiguo-banben-zhizi-1001004591]] says IBM research helped define the relational-database opportunity but that [[Oracle]] commercialized it while IBM remained tied to mainframe economics.

### Durable integrated platform
- [[88-audio-nikesharvink-88-audio-nikesharvink]] presents the 65-year mainframe business as evidence that control of differentiated stack layers can support resilience and long-term value even after fabrication is outsourced.

## Qualifications
- The Nazi-era claims derive from one podcast's historical account and remain source-scoped; the page does not substitute for archival adjudication of legal or individual responsibility.
- The database-commercialization account simplifies a multi-actor history and does not prove that hardware incentives alone determined IBM's choices.
- Mainframe longevity does not show that complete vertical ownership is always efficient; the newer source itself favors buying commodity layers and integrating selectively.
