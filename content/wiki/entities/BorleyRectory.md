# Borley Rectory

## Overview

Borley Rectory is presented as one of England's most famous haunted-house cases and as a study in investigation, publicity, fraud, fire, and paranormal tourism.

## Current Profile

[[66-ghosts-glt2309926019]] links a walking nun, ghostly writing, and a troubled clerical household to [[HarryPrice]]'s six-month tenancy and technology-assisted investigation. The episode argues that Price moved from professional skepticism toward marketable belief, making the rectory a case where investigation and publicity cannot be cleanly separated.

The building's fire did not end the story. Ruins, photographs, alleged fraud, and continuing visitors gave the site an afterlife, illustrating how a haunting can persist after its physical setting has largely disappeared.

## Key Characteristics

- Famous English rectory haunting with recurring visual and written motifs.
- Domestic and clerical setting shaped by household conflict.
- Investigated with high-end contemporary equipment.
- Publicity and commercial incentive entangled with evidentiary claims.
- Fire, ruins, photography, and tourism extended the haunting's afterlife.

## Evidence

- Haunting narrative - [[66-ghosts-glt2309926019]] describes the nun, writing, and clerical-household disturbances.
- Professional investigation - [[66-ghosts-glt2309926019]] describes [[HarryPrice]] renting the rectory and applying contemporary technology.
- Fraud and afterlife - [[66-ghosts-glt2309926019]] discusses suspected arson for insurance, the cropped floating-brick photograph, and continuing paranormal tourism.

## Qualifications

The episode's fraud, arson, motive, and image-manipulation claims are not independently adjudicated here. Fame and technological investigation do not establish a supernatural cause, while evidence of some fraud does not automatically explain every reported incident.
