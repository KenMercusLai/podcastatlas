# 夷陵 / Yiling (Late Han)

## Overview
夷陵 / Yiling (Late Han) is the mountain-and-river position whose seizure by [[GanNing|甘宁]] opens a forward Wu pressure point during the post-Red-Cliffs contest for [[JianglingLateHan|江陵]].

## Current Profile
The source presents Yiling as difficult for a large army to assault directly but vulnerable to a small force that understands its waterways, roads, and defensive assumptions. Gan Ning's regional familiarity and selected troops let him move quickly into the position, after which Cao-side forces must respond away from the main Jiangling confrontation.

Its strategic meaning is relational: control supports pressure on Jiangling and the middle-Yangtze corridor, helping Wu convert the Red Cliffs victory into territorial initiative.

## Key Characteristics
- Mountain-and-river strongpoint linked to Jiangling's defense.
- Difficult frontal target but exposed to locally informed surprise.
- Forward position that pulls Cao-side attention from the main front.
- Tactical gain whose value comes from the larger Yangtze corridor.
