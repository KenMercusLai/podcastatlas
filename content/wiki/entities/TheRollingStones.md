# The Rolling Stones

## Overview

The Rolling Stones are a British band whose late-1960s history joined music, celebrity, class aspiration, drugs, tabloid attention, and countercultural symbolism. The current evidence follows them from [[RedlandsDrugCase|Redlands]] and psychedelic uncertainty through [[BrianJones|Brian Jones]]'s death to Hyde Park, [[AltamontFreeConcert|Altamont]], and album-rock durability.

## Current Profile

The band occupied a rebellious public role while its members also sought country houses and traditional status markers. This gap made them useful as public symbols: they could be condemned as threats to respectable Britain even while parts of the establishment defended them against disproportionate punishment.

Musically, 1967-1969 was a reboot from *Their Satanic Majesties Request* toward blues-rooted *Beggars Banquet* and *Let It Bleed*. [[PopToAlbumRockTransition|Album-oriented rock]] changed listening expectations and commercial value while Jones's unreliability, removal, and replacement by Mick Taylor changed the lineup. Altamont then attached the band to the decade's darker close, though their later albums established the enduring rock template of rebellion, danger, and longevity.

## Key Characteristics

- Their public image carried more meaning than private conduct alone could support.
- Country-house aspirations complicated a simple anti-establishment identity.
- They adapted from psychedelic fashion toward blues-rooted album rock.
- Internal power shifted from founder Jones toward [[MickJagger]] and [[KeithRichards]].
- Scandal and tragedy became inseparable from their mythology.
- Post-Altamont survival turned temporary rebellion into a durable institution.
