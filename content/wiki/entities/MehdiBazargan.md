# Mehdi Bazargan

## Overview

Mehdi Bazargan was the moderate prime minister of [[Iran]]'s interim government after the 1979 revolution. The bounded sources follow his appointment by [[RuhollahKhomeini|Ruhollah Khomeini]], promises of constitutional and electoral government, institutional rivalry with clerical power, and resignation after the embassy seizure as a sequence in the narrowing of Iran's post-revolutionary political field.

## Current Profile

[[637-revolution-in-iran-rise-of-the-ayatollah-part-2-glt7243579163]] places Bazargan at the initial point of dual authority. Khomeini appointed him as a rival prime minister against Shapour Bakhtiar, while Bazargan promised a constituent assembly, constitution, democratic elections, and republican administration. Those promises gave the revolution a recognizable transitional program, but Khomeini's insistence on Sharia and clerical guardianship limited how autonomous that program could become.

[[638-revolution-in-iran-the-hostage-crisis-part-3-glt6844236579]] then places Bazargan between revolutionary legitimacy and a continuing diplomatic relationship with the [[UnitedStates]]. His government represented a more moderate administrative channel, but street power and the Revolutionary Council increasingly favored Khomeini's clerical allies.

The embassy occupation made that tension decisive. Bazargan could neither reverse the seizure nor sustain his government's authority against a popular action Khomeini endorsed. His resignation within days therefore appears less as an isolated personnel change than as evidence that the hostage crisis was reorganizing domestic power before it became a prolonged bilateral negotiation.

## Key Characteristics

- Moderate head of Iran's post-revolutionary interim government.
- Transitional leader associated with a constituent assembly, new constitution, elections, and republican administration.
- Advocate of a governing channel more open to conventional state administration and foreign diplomacy than the emerging hardline coalition.
- Political rival to the clerical Revolutionary Council around Khomeini.
- Leader whose resignation after the embassy seizure signaled the collapse of a moderate institutional counterweight.

- [[638-revolution-in-iran-the-hostage-crisis-part-3-glt6844236579]] contrasts Bazargan's interim government with the clerical Revolutionary Council and the street power of Khomeini's supporters.

- [[638-revolution-in-iran-the-hostage-crisis-part-3-glt6844236579]] connects the embassy occupation, Khomeini's endorsement, and Bazargan's resignation to the strengthening of revolutionary hardliners.

## Qualifications

The current profile rests on two narrative podcast episodes, not a complete biography of Bazargan, his political thought, administrative record, revolutionary role, or later opposition. The sources support the direction of the power shift but do not establish how much autonomy Khomeini originally intended for him or how much the embassy seizure, prior institutional weakness, clerical organization, or other revolutionary conflicts caused his resignation.
