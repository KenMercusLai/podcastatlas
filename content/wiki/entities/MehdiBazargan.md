# Mehdi Bazargan

## Overview

Mehdi Bazargan was the moderate prime minister of [[Iran]]'s interim government after the 1979 revolution. The bounded source uses his conflict with clerics around [[RuhollahKhomeini|Ruhollah Khomeini]] and resignation after the embassy seizure to mark the narrowing of Iran's post-revolutionary political field.

## Current Profile

[[638-revolution-in-iran-the-hostage-crisis-part-3-glt6844236579]] places Bazargan between revolutionary legitimacy and a continuing diplomatic relationship with the [[UnitedStates]]. His government represented a more moderate administrative channel, but street power and the Revolutionary Council increasingly favored Khomeini's clerical allies.

The embassy occupation made that tension decisive. Bazargan could neither reverse the seizure nor sustain his government's authority against a popular action Khomeini endorsed. His resignation within days therefore appears less as an isolated personnel change than as evidence that the hostage crisis was reorganizing domestic power before it became a prolonged bilateral negotiation.

## Key Characteristics

- Moderate head of Iran's post-revolutionary interim government.
- Advocate of a governing channel more open to conventional state administration and foreign diplomacy than the emerging hardline coalition.
- Political rival to the clerical Revolutionary Council around Khomeini.
- Leader whose resignation after the embassy seizure signaled the collapse of a moderate institutional counterweight.

The source supports the direction of the power shift but does not by itself measure how much the seizure, prior institutional weakness, clerical organization, or other revolutionary conflicts caused his resignation.
