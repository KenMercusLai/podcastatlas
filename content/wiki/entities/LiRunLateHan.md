## Overview

李润 / Li Run appears in the Shun restoration block as a palace eunuch whose alignment is coerced at the coup's decisive gate and then remains politically ambiguous after victory. Hanji 803 makes him the spared guard forced into the restoration coalition; Hanji 804 and Hanji 805 show why partial or suspect alignment matters after the coup succeeds.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-803-han-shundi-ruhe-zai-xuefengxueyu-zhong-dengshang-huangwei-lqvno7t-gcgxtbs3phuflr4ybe2s|Hanji 803]] places Li Run at Zhangtai Gate with Jiang Jin, Liu An, and Chen Da when [[SunChengLateHan|孙程]] and [[WangKangLateHan|王康]] launch the restoration coup. The coup group kills the other three as [[EmpressDowagerYanLateHan|Yan Taihou]]'s loyalists, puts a blade to Li Run's neck, and demands that he join the effort to enthrone [[EmperorShunOfHan|Liu Bao / 汉顺帝]]. Li Run accepts and then helps summon shangshu officials and bring the imperial carriage into the South Palace.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-804-gei-ni-tang-de-ren-bu-yiding-shi-guiren-lqongdqg-hgetoegpylv-os86buh|Hanji 804]] explains the reward consequence. Li Run is excluded from the full restoration reward group because he joins after the point-of-no-return moment rather than committing at the first gate action. His case clarifies why the later reward list is also a commitment record.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-805-tongtou-renxing-de-nvren-jing-neng-yuce-jixiong-ll3bzoyrkbtnvt-aqu9nqelqonew|Hanji 805]] makes Li Run part of the cleanup field. Yu Xu later impeaches Li Run with other Yan-connected ministers and older eunuchs, showing that being useful during the forced transition does not erase suspicion about prior alignment.

## Key Characteristics

- Gate-side palace actor whose survival depends on accepting coerced alignment with the restoration coup.
- Operational contributor after coercion because he helps summon officials and move the imperial carriage.
- Excluded or limited in reward status because he did not commit at the initial coup threshold.
- Later cleanup target, suggesting his prior Yan-side or non-restoration alignment remains politically visible.
- Example of how palace coups sort actors by timing as well as formal participation.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 803, Hanji 804, and Hanji 805. It does not prove Li Run's private loyalties, later punishment, or full office history. The current profile treats him as a coerced and politically ambiguous actor because the sources show forced participation, limited reward, and later suspicion rather than a settled faction biography.
