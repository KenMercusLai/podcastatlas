## Overview

田叔 / Tian Shu is an early Western Han retainer, official, and investigator whose current profile joins conspicuous loyalty to [[ZhangAo|张敖]], recruitment by [[LiuBang|刘邦]], and a later decision under [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]] to destroy evidence in the capital case against [[LiuWuLiangKingWesternHan|梁孝王刘武]].

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-180-xihan-diyi-yinghan-guangao-de-gushi-1-li5u-dmsmb1lp4ny3rk02kaiyu4k|Hanji 180]] presents Tian Shu as a Zhao retainer not named in the arrest order after [[GuanGao|贯高]]'s assassination plot is exposed. Tian, [[MengShuHan|孟叔]], and other followers shave their heads, wear chains and prison clothing, and follow the arrested Zhang Ao toward Chang'an as if they were household slaves. Their conduct makes loyalty visible before Guan Gao's testimony clears the king of participation.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-180-xihan-diyi-yinghan-guangao-de-gushi-2-lutkapdijrgpwfrz3saaowxtk-i2|Hanji 180 part 2]] says Liu Bang later praises and appoints Tian Shu and Meng Shu. The host reads the appointments as both reward and regional stabilization: after Zhang Ao loses Zhao and leading ministers die, the court can absorb respected local loyalists rather than leave the conquered political network wholly alienated.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-241-liangxiaowang-liuwu-kan-wo-ruhe-huayang-zuosi-lqbllfh9nph-vvu9emzhsxdfj1le|Hanji 241]] shows Tian in a harder royal case. After investigation of [[YuanAngWesternHan|袁盎]]'s assassination reaches Liang, Tian returns with testimony and records but burns them at Bachang before audience. He tells Jingdi that Liu Wu has incurred a capital offense, then argues that execution would devastate [[EmpressDowagerDouWesternHan|窦太后]] while non-execution after formal proof would empty Han law of authority.

Tian's solution is [[EvidenceSuppressionDynasticCompromise|证据切断式宗室妥协]], not exoneration. He reports to the dowager that the king knew nothing and that guilty advisers have died, giving the court a usable public account after privately affirming capital guilt. The episode rewards the judgment by making him chancellor of Lu, but the archival, legal, and equality costs remain explicit.

## Key Characteristics

- Zhao retainer who makes loyalty visible by voluntarily sharing his arrested lord's degradation.
- Respected local figure absorbed into Han office after Zhang Ao's exoneration and demotion.
- Investigator who distinguishes his private judgment of guilt from the case the court can politically sustain.
- Mediator who resolves a conflict between law and royal kinship by destroying the evidentiary route to final enforcement.
- Official whose solution protects short-term dynastic stability at the cost of archival truth and equal legal treatment.

## Qualifications

These three podcast notes do not form a complete biography. Hanji 180's follower count, dress, speeches, recruitment, appointments, praise, and pacification interpretation require comparison. Hanji 241's investigative mandate, exact evidence, Bachang location, authority to burn records, speech, legal standard, public report, causal effect on the dowager, and Lu appointment remain episode-attributed. The profile describes a politically effective solution without treating evidence destruction or unequal royal treatment as legally neutral.
