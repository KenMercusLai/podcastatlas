# 1978 FIFA World Cup

## Overview

The 1978 FIFA World Cup was the Argentina-hosted tournament used by the ruling military junta as a national and international spectacle while disappearances, torture, and killing continued.

## Current Profile

The episode presents Argentina 1978 as an unusually stark case of World Cup joy coexisting with authoritarian violence. The junta invested heavily in stadiums and infrastructure, concealed visible poverty, resisted boycott pressure, and gained the image of national unity when Argentina won. Yet the tournament's memory remains inseparable from the torture center near the final stadium and from allegations surrounding Argentina's required four-goal win over Peru, which the source treats as powerful but unproven rather than established match-fixing.

## Key Characteristics

- World Cup awarded before the 1976 coup but staged under Jorge Rafael Videla's junta.
- High-cost prestige project combining infrastructure, image management, diplomacy, and commercial interest.
- Sporting triumph appropriated as evidence of national unity amid the Argentine Dirty War.
- Tournament marked by boycott pressure that did not prevent most teams and companies from participating.
- Event whose 6–0 Argentina-Peru match remains politically controversial but evidentially unsettled.
- Celebration physically close to torture and imprisonment in Buenos Aires.

## Qualifications

The page records the episode's historical account rather than independently adjudicating casualty totals, spending shares, diplomatic pressure, Operation Condor links, drug use, match-fixing, or individual motives. The tournament's propaganda value does not establish that all celebrating Argentines supported the junta or that victory produced lasting regime stability.
