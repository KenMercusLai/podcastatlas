# Pistachio / 开心果

## Overview
Pistachio is a tree nut discussed as both a familiar health-positioned snack and a visually distinctive ingredient whose slow biological supply response collided with rapidly expanding food-and-drink demand.

## Current Profile
The source presents pistachio as a concentrated, climate-exposed agricultural commodity. Chinese consumption is highly import-dependent, with the United States and Iran supplying almost all reported imports. Heat, drought, irrigation interruption, poor pollination, empty shells, groundwater restrictions, and shipping disruption can therefore combine rather than remain isolated local problems.

Demand has also broadened beyond snacking. Pistachio's green color, flavor, plant-protein positioning, and compatibility with chocolate, drinks, ice cream, spreads, and desserts make it especially legible in social media and product launches. Because new trees take years to bear and longer to reach full output, a viral demand acceleration can move retail price and availability long before acreage expansion helps.

## Key Characteristics
- Perennial crop with a long delay between planting, first harvest, and maximum output.
- Chinese market is described as overwhelmingly import-dependent.
- Supply is concentrated in U.S. and Iranian production within the source's China-import frame.
- Sensitive to heat, drought, irrigation reliability, pollination, groundwater policy, and transport routes.
- Sold as both a snack and a defining ingredient across confectionery, beverages, ice cream, and desserts.
- Green color and premium-health associations increase product and social-media visibility.
- Defining flavor limits substitution in products marketed specifically around pistachio.

## Evidence
- Supply concentration and import dependence: [[kafeidou-shanmu-xiangou-huangniu-jiajia-kaixinguo-weishenme-biande-jinqiao-gkwriw4or9-vag3oegttwe_s]] reports roughly 300 tonnes of Chinese production versus more than 130,000 tonnes of imports in 2024, with the United States and Iran accounting for almost all imports.
- Climate and infrastructure exposure: [[kafeidou-shanmu-xiangou-huangniu-jiajia-kaixinguo-weishenme-biande-jinqiao-gkwriw4or9-vag3oegttwe_s]] links Iranian losses to heat, drought, power and irrigation disruption and California losses to heat-related pollination problems and empty shells.
- Slow supply response: [[kafeidou-shanmu-xiangou-huangniu-jiajia-kaixinguo-weishenme-biande-jinqiao-gkwriw4or9-vag3oegttwe_s]] says new trees need about eight years for a first crop and about twelve years for maximum production.
- Cross-category demand: [[kafeidou-shanmu-xiangou-huangniu-jiajia-kaixinguo-weishenme-biande-jinqiao-gkwriw4or9-vag3oegttwe_s]] connects existing snack demand with pistachio drinks, ice cream, desserts, paste, and Dubai chocolate.

## Qualifications
Production, trade, price, harvest-loss, product-launch, maturation, and order figures come from one compact business-news episode and are not independently audited here. The source focuses on China's import exposure rather than the complete global production-and-trade system. A viral product can intensify demand without proving that all price movement is caused by social media, and announced acreage does not guarantee eventual yield.

## What Changed
- Established pistachio as a canonical commodity entity linking perennial-crop supply lags with cross-category ingredient demand.
- Preserved the distinction between long-standing snack demand and the newer Dubai-chocolate acceleration.
