# Andrew Preston

## Overview

Andrew Preston is the historian and guest in [[153-god-and-the-american-empire-glt8752091834]], where he interprets religion as a constitutive part of modern American political culture and foreign policy rather than as decorative presidential rhetoric.

## Current Profile

Preston's framework connects institutional structure to international conduct. Church-state separation prevented religious institutions from being automatically discredited with the state, while competition encouraged a changing “marketplace of faith.” Abroad, he distinguishes a militant “sword of the spirit” from a reformist “shield of faith,” allowing religious arguments to support intervention, restraint, pluralism, and human-rights pressure at different moments.

His examples run from the late-1930s construction of a public Judeo-Christian tradition through Truman, Reagan, Bush, and Trump. The episode's broad historical claims remain attributable to Preston and require wider evidence before being treated as exhaustive explanations of American religion or diplomacy.

## Key Characteristics

- Interprets American religious persistence through disestablishment, competition, innovation, and political culture.
- Treats religion as part of foreign-policy belief and coalition formation, not merely rhetorical packaging.
- Distinguishes militant and reformist religious impulses rather than equating faith with interventionism alone.
- Uses presidential episodes to connect domestic religious pluralism with changing international moral frames.

## Evidence

### Institutional persistence

- [[153-god-and-the-american-empire-glt8752091834]] attributes American religious durability to church-state separation and a competitive “marketplace of faith.”

### Foreign-policy interpretation

- [[153-god-and-the-american-empire-glt8752091834]] uses the “sword” and “shield” distinction and examples from Truman through Trump to show multiple religious routes into foreign policy.

## Qualifications

The current profile rests on one short podcast conversation. It records Preston's interpretive framework, not a complete bibliography, systematic comparison with Europe, or independent verification of every presidential motive and diplomatic anecdote.
