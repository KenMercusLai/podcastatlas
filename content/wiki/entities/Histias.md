# Histias

## Overview

Histias appears in the wiki as the Miletian ruler whose loyalty to [[DariusI]], court removal, and suspected intrigue form one of the unstable elite channels behind the [[IonianRevolt]].

## Current Profile

The episode presents Histias first as a Persian-backed Ionian tyrant whose position depends on Darius. During Darius's Scythian campaign, Histias opposes [[Miltiades]]' proposal to cut the Danube bridge because Ionian tyrants rely on Persian power. Darius rewards him with land and favor but later draws him to court as a table companion, a promotion the hosts read as a gilded cage.

Histias returns during the revolt under suspicion. Herodotus's tattooed-scalp message story is treated as vivid but source-bound evidence of his reputation for clever plotting. His end is brutal: capture, impalement, beheading, and a dignified burial of his head by Darius.

## Key Characteristics

- Histias is a Persian-backed Ionian tyrant whose loyalty is bound to self-preservation.
- His opposition to cutting Darius's Danube bridge shows the limits of Greek solidarity under Persian patronage.
- His court promotion is interpreted as honorable containment.
- Later suspicion makes him a symbol of unstable imperial-client politics.
- Herodotean anecdote gives him a clever but untrustworthy profile.
