# Nirao Shah

## Overview
Nirao Shah is presented as a Stanford neuroscientist studying how genes, hormones, and neural circuits shape sex-linked differences in innate social behaviors.

## Current Profile
In the episode, Shah explains sexual differentiation as a timed developmental process rather than a direct readout of adult hormone concentration. He uses SRY, gonadal hormones, DHT conversion, aromatization, receptor function, cell survival, and circuit connectivity to connect early organization with later activation. His experimental examples focus on hypothalamic circuits for mating, aggression, parenting, sex recognition, reward, and reproductive-state plasticity.

Shah repeatedly limits translation. Mouse and prairie-vole experiments can reveal circuit mechanisms, but they do not directly settle human gender identity, sexual orientation, social policy, hormone intervention, or environmental-exposure risk. This distinction between mechanistic confidence and human inference is central to his profile in the wiki.

## Key Characteristics
- Studies gene, hormone, and circuit mechanisms of innate social behavior.
- Uses mouse and prairie-vole models to isolate causal neural and receptor contributions.
- Distinguishes early organizational hormone effects from later activating effects.
- Treats hypothalamic circuits as context-sensitive networks rather than single behavior switches.
- Keeps animal sex-biology findings separate from broad conclusions about human gender and culture.
