[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-407-zaiwei-jin-27-tian-han-feidi-liuhe-ruhe-bei-huoguang-feichu-lpob_9jkyomzrbeqnbjekqbpjeog|Hanji 407]] adds an earlier version of his sign-reading directness: he warns [[LiuHeHaihunHou|刘贺]] that prolonged cloudy weather means a plot against the ruler, and is bound for what Liu He treats as dangerous speech. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-416-xihan-xiahousheng-ruhe-pai-hanxuandi-de-mapi-lvqbs--20kvjimwdwgfciq-n6wee|Hanji 416]] backfills his release, direct-speech, praise, and teacher-scholar profile under [[EmperorXuanOfHan|汉宣帝]], while [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-412-2-dangzhe-qunchen-he-hanxuandi-de-mian-pengji-hanwudi-xiahousheng-you-duogan-lpcrynjdx85iv75pypqvooqry3vi|Hanji 412-2]] supplies the immediate cause of his later imprisonment.

## Current Profile

Across the sources, Xiahou Sheng functions as a trusted scholar whose speech is forceful because it is backed by moral credibility and classical sign-reading. Hanji 407 shows the earlier danger of that role under Liu He: Xiahou translates cloudy weather through the Hongfan tradition into a warning that ministers may be plotting against the ruler, and the warning is accurate enough to frighten Huo Guang but unwelcome enough that Liu He has Xiahou bound. Hanji 412-2 gives the later rupture behind his Xuan-era reputation: during Xuan's push to honor Han Wudi with sacrificial music, Xiahou publicly accepts Wudi's conquest record but argues that dead soldiers, fiscal exhaustion, displacement, famine, and cannibalism make ritual glorification undeserved. The speech threatens Xuan's Wudi-based legitimacy project, brings official impeachment, and sends Xiahou to death-row custody with [[HuangBaWesternHan|黄霸]].

Hanji 412-2 and Hanji 416 together make the prison sequence more than punishment. Huang's refusal to join the impeachment and his request to study the [[Shangshu|《尚书》]] turn confinement into a teacher-student setting; Hanji 416 then shows Xuan releasing and promoting the two after a disaster amnesty because he reads their continued study as evidence of character. Xiahou's informality before the emperor and his disclosure of imperial words would be dangerous in another mouth, but the source says Xuan trusts him because he is direct, unselfish, and learned.

Hanji 416 also supplies the page's praise-and-memory layer. Xiahou answers Xuan's anger over leaked words by saying that good royal speech should be spread like the sayings of sage rulers. The episode reads this as [[PraiseAsPoliticalMemory|praise as political memory]]: Xiahou flatters by placing Xuan inside a transmissible sage-ruler frame, not by abandoning his role as a truth-telling adviser. Hanji 412-2 is therefore the necessary contrast: the same scholar who later praises Xuan first refuses to let Wudi's ritual memory ignore political and human cost. Hanji 430 then shows the same moral-interpreter role in another register. When Xuan worries that Bing Ji may die before receiving his marquis seal, Xiahou argues that a person with hidden virtue will enjoy blessing before death and pass blessing to descendants. Bing Ji's recovery becomes the episode's proof scene for [[YindeRewardCausality|阴德福报因果]].

## Key Characteristics

- Scholar-official and crown-prince tutor whose authority rests on learning, uprightness, and a reputation for having no private agenda.
- Omen interpreter whose warning to Liu He is punished even though it overlaps with Huo Guang's real conspiracy.
- Public remonstrant who weighs Han Wudi's conquests against military, fiscal, and civilian devastation when ritual praise is being discussed.
- Prison learner released and promoted after Xuan treats continued study with Huang Ba as evidence of character.
- Direct speaker whose informality before the emperor is tolerated because it reads as plain truth rather than contempt.
- Uses praise to frame Xuan's words as worthy of sage-ruler transmission and later political memory.

## Qualifications

This page is source-scoped to Hanji 407, Hanji 412-2, Hanji 416, and Hanji 430. It preserves Xiahou Sheng's Hanji 407 cloudy-weather warning as the episode's Hongfan-based omen interpretation, not as an independent proof of supernatural causality. It preserves Xiahou Sheng's anti-Wudi speech as the episode's account of a ritual and legitimacy dispute, not as a complete assessment of Han Wudi's reign. It preserves Xiahou Sheng's hidden-virtue claim as the episode's moral interpretation, not as a medical explanation. Hanji 416's "flattery" reading is likewise treated as the host's interpretation of a court-rhetorical move that depends on Xiahou's prior credibility; it is not generalized into a claim that praise is always safe.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 407's earlier omen-warning scene under Liu He, where Xiahou's Hongfan-based warning is punished and also alarms Huo Guang.
- Preserved Hanji 412-2 as the cause of Xiahou's imprisonment: public cost-based criticism of Han Wudi's proposed ritual honor, followed by impeachment and prison Shangshu study with Huang Ba.
- Reframed Hanji 416's release, trusted directness, praise as political memory, and Shangshu-teacher honor as the later reuse of a previously punished remonstrant.
- Preserved Hanji 430's Bing Ji hidden-virtue interpretation as a later moral-causal branch.
