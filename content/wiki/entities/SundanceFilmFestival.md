## Overview

The Sundance Film Festival is presented by [[PlanetMoney|Planet Money]] as both a celebration of filmmaking outside Hollywood and a concentrated market where independent films seek theatrical or streaming distribution.

## Current Profile

The episode traces Sundance from Robert Redford's shelter for independent filmmaking to a major acquisition venue after the commercial success of *Sex, Lies, and Videotape*. At the 2026 festival, screenings function as cultural events, demand tests, buyer meetings, publicity opportunities, and negotiation triggers. Selection confers attention but does not guarantee distribution, and the small buyer pool makes timing, attendance, reviews, and corporate approval consequential.

## Key Characteristics

- Combines artistic selection with a commercial acquisition market.
- Concentrates filmmakers, agents, buyers, critics, influencers, and audiences.
- Makes public reaction and visible buyer interest economically consequential.
- Supports screenings alongside private meetings, parties, and promotional events.
- Offers discovery and validation without guaranteeing distribution or success.

## Qualifications

The source views Sundance primarily through one sales company's acquisition work. It does not provide a full festival history or systematically represent unsold films, noncommercial programming, filmmakers without agents, or rival buyers and sellers.
