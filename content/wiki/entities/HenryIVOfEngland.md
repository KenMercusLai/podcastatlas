# Henry IV of England

## Overview

Henry IV is the Lancastrian king whose disputed accession and opportunistic interventions in France shaped the inheritance of [[HenryVOfEngland|Henry V]].

## Current Profile

The source presents Henry's reign as formally covered by an Anglo-French truce but strategically closer to a cold war. French authorities denied his full royal legitimacy, supported English enemies in Scotland and Wales, and treated him as Henry of Lancaster, while English interests faced maritime pressure from [[Harfleur]].

French factional conflict also gave Henry leverage. He backed the Burgundian side in 1411 and the Armagnac side in 1412, extracting wealth and concessions without committing to a stable French partner. His usurpation nevertheless left [[LancastrianLegitimacy|a dynastic weakness]] that his son inherited.

## Key Characteristics

- Founder of the Lancastrian royal regime represented as disputed by France.
- Ruler operating under truce amid continuing Anglo-French strategic hostility.
- Opportunistic intervener who supported different French factions in succession.
- Father whose contested accession shaped Henry V's domestic risk calculus.

## Evidence

- Disputed status: [[487-hundred-years-war-henry-v-s-invasion-of-france-part-1-glt7554933021]] says French authorities referred to him as Henry of Lancaster rather than recognizing him as rightful king.
- Cold-war setting: [[487-hundred-years-war-henry-v-s-invasion-of-france-part-1-glt7554933021]] connects French support for Scotland and Wales and Harfleur's maritime threat to continuing competition under truce.
- Factional intervention: [[487-hundred-years-war-henry-v-s-invasion-of-france-part-1-glt7554933021]] describes English expeditions supporting opposite French factions in 1411 and 1412.

## Qualifications

The source uses Henry IV chiefly to explain the strategic and legitimacy inheritance of Henry V. It does not offer a full account of his seizure of the throne, domestic government, rebellions, or later illness, and it does not treat his shifting French alignments as ideological commitments.

## What Changed

- Established Henry IV as the link between Lancastrian insecurity, Anglo-French cold-war pressure, and opportunistic intervention in France.
