## Overview

Emperor Ping of Han / 汉平帝 is synthesized as the child [[WesternHanDynasty|Western Han]] emperor whose reign, imperial marriage, adoptive-heir status, and death let [[WangMang|王莽]] move from regent toward acting emperor and eventual founder of the [[XinDynasty|新朝]]. [[126-wangmang-zhisi-shensheng-diguo-de-huixuanbiao-he-huixuanbiao-de-huixuanbiao-786255780]] treats Ping as the young ruler Wang Mang elevated and married to his favored daughter, while cautioning that Wang Mang may still have imagined himself at first as a Huo Guang-style regent. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-615-chengmu-jieshe-gudai-caili-zheme-gao-lkllbe015xkb7wlhkobtastkifdo|Hanji 615]] adds the 3 CE marriage-procedure layer: Wang Zhengjun sends officials to inspect Wang Mang's daughter, high officials divine the match, the court notifies Han ancestors, and the bride-price precedent makes Ping's marriage a public dynastic act rather than a private household tie. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-618-wangmang-weihe-shi-duoquan-de-touhao-wanjia-lqcaoli6r7lbdzqy7emw8sbzs0wq|Hanji 618]] adds the adjacent adoptive-lineage layer: Wang Mang has officials lectured on "嗣子大义" so Ping's status as [[HanAidi|汉哀帝]]'s heir can be used to suppress claims of private attachment to the Wei birth family. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-627-1-nvxing-biting-dute-shijiao-jingqi-yinyu-beihou-de-lishi-zhihui-lqomjfejmglm9hylkomktx6rtbtt|Hanji 627-1]] adds the illness-and-death narrative before succession: Ping resents Wang Mang after separation from his mother and Wei relatives, then the episode follows [[ZizhiTongjian|《资治通鉴》]]'s pepper-wine poisoning account while contrasting [[HanShu|《汉书》]]'s illness notices. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-628-ruguo-chuanyue-jiao-ni-ruhe-dang-huangdi-lljocpgp247g7frbrkta39zrtlc|Hanji 628]] adds the succession aftermath: Ping's death in 5 CE opens the heir-selection field that Wang Mang narrows toward controllable children and then uses to justify "摄皇帝" authority.

## Current Profile

Ping's minority made court management decisive. In the Xiangrui source, Wang Mang uses kinship marriage, regent reputation, and Zhougong-like moral performance around Ping's illness to strengthen his position without the wiki treating the poisoning accusation as settled fact. Hanji 615 makes the marriage mechanism concrete: the empress candidate is evaluated through official report, divination, sacrificial notification, and a huge bride-price, so Ping's marriage becomes a ritual bridge between Han imperial continuity and Wang-family ascent. Hanji 618 then adds that Ping's heir identity could also be interpreted against his birth family: Wang Mang's public lecture on adoptive duty turns "sonship" into a political rule that helps justify the Wei-clan purge.

Hanji 627-1 makes the source tension more explicit. It narrates Ping's resentment after he cannot see his mother and after Wei-family relatives are killed, then follows the poisoning story in which Wang Mang offers pepper wine and writes a prayer text asking to die in Ping's place. The same source keeps the textual difference visible by saying 《汉书》 records illness and prayer performance more cautiously than 《资治通鉴》.

Hanji 628 makes Ping's death a succession mechanism. Once Ping is gone, Wang Mang can argue against adult Liu-line successors, summon younger Emperor Xuan descendants, and let the court describe an interim imperial-power package as necessary guardianship for a future child heir. Ping's role is therefore not only personal vulnerability but the institutional vacancy that lets regency become near-sovereignty.

## Key Characteristics

- Child emperor whose minority made regency and court management decisive.
- Marriage-alliance figure whose household link to Wang Mang tightened Wang-family access to imperial status.
- Imperial groom whose empress selection and bride-price procedure turned marriage into a dynastic ritual event.
- Adoptive-heir figure whose ritual status as Aidi's successor could be used to subordinate birth-family attachment.
- Illness-and-death boundary case through which Wang Mang performed Zhougong-like loyalty and faces a source-scoped poisoning accusation.
- Succession trigger whose death opened a broad Liu-line heir field.
- Political absence whose replacement problem let Wang Mang prefer a controllable young successor and claim acting-emperor authority.

## Evidence

- Child-regency role: [[126-wangmang-zhisi-shensheng-diguo-de-huixuanbiao-he-huixuanbiao-de-huixuanbiao-786255780]] says Wang Mang elevated Ping and operated through regent-like authority.
- Marriage tie: [[126-wangmang-zhisi-shensheng-diguo-de-huixuanbiao-he-huixuanbiao-de-huixuanbiao-786255780]] says Wang Mang married his favored daughter to Ping.
- Marriage ritual procedure: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-615-chengmu-jieshe-gudai-caili-zheme-gao-lkllbe015xkb7wlhkobtastkifdo|Hanji 615]] says Wang Zhengjun sends officials to evaluate Wang Mang's daughter, officials divine the match, and the court reports the marriage to Han ancestors.
- Bride-price scale: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-615-chengmu-jieshe-gudai-caili-zheme-gao-lkllbe015xkb7wlhkobtastkifdo|Hanji 615]] says imperial precedent set the empress bride-price at golden value of 20,000 jin, rendered by the episode as 200 million cash.
- Adoptive-heir doctrine: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-618-wangmang-weihe-shi-duoquan-de-touhao-wanjia-lqcaoli6r7lbdzqy7emw8sbzs0wq|Hanji 618]] says Wang Mang has Zongbo Feng lecture officials that Ping, as Aidi's heir, should follow adoptive-son duty rather than birth-family feeling toward the Wei clan.
- Political analogy: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-618-wangmang-weihe-shi-duoquan-de-touhao-wanjia-lqcaoli6r7lbdzqy7emw8sbzs0wq|Hanji 618]] says the Jin-family collateral title dispute is used as a model for Pingdi's succession position.
- Illness performance: [[126-wangmang-zhisi-shensheng-diguo-de-huixuanbiao-he-huixuanbiao-de-huixuanbiao-786255780]] says Wang Mang staged a Zhougong-like prayer to die in Ping's place and hid the prayer text; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-627-1-nvxing-biting-dute-shijiao-jingqi-yinyu-beihou-de-lishi-zhihui-lqomjfejmglm9hylkomktx6rtbtt|Hanji 627-1]] gives the prayer text its Zhougong金縢 model.
- Death-cause source tension: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-627-1-nvxing-biting-dute-shijiao-jingqi-yinyu-beihou-de-lishi-zhihui-lqomjfejmglm9hylkomktx6rtbtt|Hanji 627-1]] says 《资治通鉴》 adopts a pepper-wine poisoning narrative while 《汉书》 gives illness-centered records and annotation detail.
- Succession crisis: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-628-ruguo-chuanyue-jiao-ni-ruhe-dang-huangdi-lljocpgp247g7frbrkta39zrtlc|Hanji 628]] says Ping's 5 CE death forces Wang Zhengjun and the court to choose a successor from Liu-line descendants.
- Acting-emperor aftermath: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-628-ruguo-chuanyue-jiao-ni-ruhe-dang-huangdi-lljocpgp247g7frbrkta39zrtlc|Hanji 628]] says the ministers use the youth of the candidate pool and the white-stone fuming to justify Wang Mang's "摄皇帝" status.

## Qualifications

The source set does not prove that Wang Mang poisoned Ping. The Xiangrui source explicitly cautions that the poisoning claim lacks firm evidence and that Ping's survival would not obviously have ended Wang Mang's regency. Hanji 615 supports the marriage-procedure and bride-price layer, but the exact ritual wording, money conversion, and court actors' inner motives remain source-scoped. Hanji 618 supports the adoptive-lineage argument and Wei-clan political frame, but Zongbo Feng, Zhen Han, the Jin-family genealogy, and Wang Mang's inner motives remain source-scoped. Hanji 627-1 strengthens the caution by recording the 《资治通鉴》 and 《汉书》 difference, while still presenting the poisoning version as the episode's main narrative. Hanji 628 supports the post-death succession and acting-emperor sequence, but the exact adult Liu candidates, Wang Mang's inner motive, and the legal validity of the succession argument remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 615's 3 CE imperial-marriage layer: candidate inspection, divination, ancestral notification, and bride-price precedent.
- Added Hanji 618's 3 CE adoptive-lineage layer: "嗣子大义," Wei-clan purge justification, and the Jin-family collateral succession analogy.
- Preserved Hanji 627-1's Pingdi resentment, pepper-wine poisoning narrative, Zhougong金縢 prayer performance, and 《汉书》/《资治通鉴》 death-cause contrast.
- Preserved Hanji 628 as the post-death succession crisis and "摄皇帝" transition layer.
