# Jonathan Swift / 乔纳森·斯威夫特

## Overview

Jonathan Swift was the Irish-born writer and political satirist whose [[GulliversTravels|《格列佛游记》]] uses scale, bodily comedy, travel-document seriousness, absurd invention, and estrangement to attack vanity, violence, abstract rationality, and technical confidence detached from life.

## Current Profile

The literature episode presents Swift as politically engaged and morally severe without reducing him to a misanthrope. His disgust at cruelty, party conflict, technical arrogance, and human self-importance remains compatible with the hope that people can become better. That reading requires [[AuthorCharacterSeparation]]: [[LemuelGulliver]]'s final inability to tolerate ordinary humans is a damaged character outcome, not a transparent statement of Swift's ideal.

The cultural-decline episode concentrates on Laputa and the Academy. Projects such as extracting sunlight from cucumbers, restoring food from excrement, building from the roof downward, and eliminating words satirize abstract design that ignores ordinary judgment, material failure, language history, and lived need. The target is not invention in itself but reform insulated from feedback and able to explain failure as insufficient radicalism.

## Key Characteristics

- Political satirist who embeds institutional criticism inside entertaining travel fiction.
- Uses bodily comedy, scale reversal, and pseudo-documentary detail to puncture human self-flattery.
- Critic of technical and abstract reason when it loses contact with ordinary life and corrective feedback.
- Morally severe writer whose disgust should not be flattened into simple hatred of humanity.
- Author whose characters and fictional societies require interpretive separation from his own final judgment.

## Qualifications

The current sources interpret Swift chiefly through *Gulliver's Travels* and do not cover his complete life, religious office, Irish political writing, poetry, or wider prose. The modern comparison to technology industries belongs to the new episode's interpretive framing. Absurd fictional experiments do not establish that Swift rejected science or innovation as such, and Gulliver's views cannot be transferred directly to the author.
