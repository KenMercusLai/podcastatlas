# Mark Granovetter

## Overview

Mark Granovetter is the sociologist whose collective-behavior threshold model supplies the explanatory framework in [[the-big-man-can-t-shoot-the-big-man-can-t-shoot|The Big Man Can’t Shoot]]. The episode applies his distinction between private belief and action under peer pressure to riots, basketball technique, and institutional decision-making.

## Current Profile

Granovetter’s model asks how many other people must act before a particular person joins them. A low-threshold actor needs little visible precedent; a high-threshold actor may privately approve of an action yet wait for widespread participation. The sequence of heterogeneous thresholds can make collective behavior spread even when individual beliefs alone would not predict it.

Gladwell uses the model interpretively rather than formally. [[RickBarry]] is cast as willing to shoot underhanded with almost no social support, whereas [[WiltChamberlain]] abandons a method he knows can work because its stigma remains high. The episode also extends the logic to NFL leaders who resist economically or strategically unconventional choices.

## Key Characteristics

- Sociologist associated in the episode with a threshold model of collective behavior.
- Separates personal belief from willingness to act in front of peers.
- Models individuals as differing in the amount of visible participation they require.
- Explains how local social sequences can produce collective outcomes.
- Provides the episode’s bridge from riot participation to sport and organizational convention.
