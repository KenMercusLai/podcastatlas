# 田广 / Tian Guang (Qi King)

田广 / Tian Guang enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-151-huanyuan-pengcheng-zhizhan-xiangyu-zai-fengshen-1-lspx-bqe5kczq6bmn6thlerfbrmu|Hanji 151 part 1]] as [[TianRong|田荣]]'s son, installed by [[TianHeng|田横]] as Qi king after Tian Rong's death. This page is disambiguated from [[TianGuang|田光]], the Yan elder connected to [[JingKe|荆轲]].

In this source, Tian Guang's importance is dynastic and tactical rather than personal. Tian Heng gathers scattered Qi soldiers, uses Tian Guang to preserve the restored Tian-line kingship, and continues resistance against [[XiangYu|项羽]] around [[JuWarringStates|莒城 / 城阳]]. The move keeps [[QiState|齐国]] from becoming pacified after Tian Rong is killed.

The source uses Tian Guang's installation to explain the timing of [[PengchengBattle|彭城之战]]. Xiang Yu remains stuck trying to finish the Qi war while [[LiuBang|刘邦]] builds a coalition and moves on [[Xuzhou|彭城]]. Tian Guang therefore marks the point where Tian Rong's personal defeat becomes a new Qi resistance center.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-162-3-zuipao-wang-lishiqi-budong-yibing-naxia-70-yu-cheng-lq88alghxqxxcddk4zzqodkezipx|Hanji 162-3]] brings Tian Guang back after the Chu-Han balance has shifted again. [[LiShiqi|郦食其]] arrives at [[Linzi|临淄]] and argues that the world is turning toward [[LiuBang|刘邦]] because Han rewards merit and Chu under [[XiangYu|项羽]] has lost legitimacy through broken promises, Yi Emperor's death, and weak reward practice.

Tian Guang accepts the argument enough to send envoys to Liu Bang and seek reconciliation, and Qi relaxes its defenses while waiting for a Han settlement. This does not become a clean surrender branch because [[HanXin|韩信]], urged by [[KuaiChe|蒯彻 / 蒯通]], attacks despite Li Shiqi's success. Tian Guang's page therefore now links Qi's anti-Chu resistance, its attempted diplomatic pivot to Han, and the danger created when Han military and diplomatic timing diverge.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-163-liubang-shouxia-moushi-ruhe-bei-hanxin-neijuan-cansi-lubinu6hoihu59knmlixmaryhnq7|Hanji 163]] turns that timing divergence into open disaster. When Han Xin attacks after Qi has relaxed its defenses, Tian Guang and [[TianHeng|田横]] decide that Li Shiqi has deceived them and have him cooked when he cannot stop the army. Tian Guang then flees toward Gaomi and asks [[XiangYu|项羽]] for help, making Qi's surrender failure part of both [[CommandSynchronizationFailure|军令同步失灵]] and the wider Chu-Han eastern-front struggle.
