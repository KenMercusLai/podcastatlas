# 广川王刘海阳 / Liu Haiyang, Guangchuan King (Western Han)

## Overview
Liu Haiyang is presented by [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-464-gudai-de-chungongtu-daodi-you-duo-ye-lp-qvincrx1zfp5z2ggjygfnpbca|Hanji 464]] as a Western Han Guangchuan king removed in Ganlu 4 / 50 BCE after accusations of killing, sexual coercion, and conduct the court classified as bestial.

## Current Profile
The sole source frames Liu Haiyang as a royal-kin impunity case rather than as an art patron. After fifteen years as king, he allegedly joined his brother Liu Diao in killing three members of a household, compelled a married sister into sexual conduct with a favored attendant, and staged a relatives' banquet in a hall decorated with explicit images. The court abolished his kingship, sent him to Fangling, and converted the kingdom into Guangchuan Commandery.

The imagery matters because it is embedded in coerced performance and political condemnation. That setting differs from the instructional, marital, protective, funerary, or collectible functions grouped under [[AncientChineseEroticImagery|中国古代春宫图的社会功能]].

## Key Characteristics
- Western Han royal prince whose status is portrayed as enabling escalating household violence and sexual coercion.
- Removed from kingship in the episode's Ganlu 4 / 50 BCE chronology and relocated to Fangling.
- His banquet turns sexually explicit imagery into part of compulsory spectacle rather than private instruction or consensual viewing.
- The court's “禽兽行” judgment places the case inside a royal-discipline and legitimacy boundary.

## Evidence
- Removal and punishment - [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-464-gudai-de-chungongtu-daodi-you-duo-ye-lp-qvincrx1zfp5z2ggjygfnpbca|Hanji 464]] says the court removed Liu Haiyang, sent him to Fangling, and changed the kingdom into a commandery.
- Violence and coercion - [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-464-gudai-de-chungongtu-daodi-you-duo-ye-lp-qvincrx1zfp5z2ggjygfnpbca|Hanji 464]] attributes a three-person killing, coerced intrafamilial sexual conduct, and the image-filled banquet to him.
- Image setting - [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-464-gudai-de-chungongtu-daodi-you-duo-ye-lp-qvincrx1zfp5z2ggjygfnpbca|Hanji 464]] uses the banquet as its `Zizhi Tongjian` bridge into a wider account of erotic imagery.
