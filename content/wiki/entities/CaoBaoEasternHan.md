## Overview
曹褒 / Cao Bao was an Eastern Han ritual specialist whose Han-rites project carried earlier imperial sponsorship but failed to survive organized court resistance under [[EmperorHeOfHan|Emperor He]]. The wiki keeps him distinct from [[CaoBaoLateHan|曹豹 / Cao Bao]], a different late-Han person.

## Current Profile
Cao Bao's current wiki profile is source-bounded to [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-768-lishi-gaosu-ni-jidu-shi-shaxin-lgk8qoxvs5ax8oikgecrxqvispna|Hanji 768]]. Emperor Zhang had assigned him to create Han institutional rites, and Emperor He used those rites during his capping ceremony. When [[ZhangPuEasternHan|Zhang Pu]] and Zhang Min repeatedly attacked the work from a Taixue-orthodoxy position, Emperor He recognized some political self-interest in the opposition but still stopped using the rites.

## Key Characteristics
- Ritual specialist attached to an imperial project of defining Han rites.
- Depended on imperial sponsorship rather than an independent political base.
- Became the target of orthodox institutional resistance from Taixue-linked officials.
- Exposed Emperor He's weakness: the ruler could know the opposition was self-interested yet still abandon the work.
- Should not be conflated with the later figure 曹豹 represented by [[CaoBaoLateHan]].

## Evidence
- Imperial sponsorship and abandonment: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-768-lishi-gaosu-ni-jidu-shi-shaxin-lgk8qoxvs5ax8oikgecrxqvispna|Hanji 768]] says Emperor Zhang had ordered Cao Bao to create Han rites, Emperor He used them in his capping ceremony, and Emperor He later stopped using them after Zhang Pu and Zhang Min submitted repeated objections.
- Opposition pattern: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-768-lishi-gaosu-ni-jidu-shi-shaxin-lgk8qoxvs5ax8oikgecrxqvispna|Hanji 768]] frames Zhang Pu and Zhang Min as figures whose objections carried both ritual-orthodoxy claims and professional self-interest.
- Leadership signal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-768-lishi-gaosu-ni-jidu-shi-shaxin-lgk8qoxvs5ax8oikgecrxqvispna|Hanji 768]] uses Cao Bao's case to illustrate a weak superior's failure to reward correct work and punish obstructive behavior.

## Qualifications
Cao Bao's page currently rests on one source note, so the exact technical content of his rites, the scope of Taixue opposition, and the administrative mechanics of stopping their use remain thin. The source presents the case through a leadership lens, not as a full ritual-history dossier.

- Added Cao Bao as a concrete instance of ritual reform depending on active imperial enforcement.
