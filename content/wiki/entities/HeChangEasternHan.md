## Overview

何敞 / He Chang enters the wiki as the Eastern Han official who connects anti-war remonstrance, anti-luxury criticism, and rescue speech under [[DouXianEasternHan|Dou Xian]]'s power. In Hanji 761, he first argues against the Northern Xiongnu expedition and Dou-family mansion construction, then intervenes when [[ZhiShouEasternHan|Zhi Shou]] is sentenced to death.

## Current Profile

He Chang's first function is policy criticism. He argues that the [[NorthernXiongnuLateHan|Northern Xiongnu]] has not committed a fresh rebellion or humiliation requiring revenge, while large spring mobilization will interfere with farming and intensify public resentment. He also criticizes mansions for [[DouDuEasternHan|Dou Du]] and [[DouJingEasternHan|Dou Jing]] because public hardship, fiscal emptiness, and campaign mobilization make luxury construction politically exemplary in the wrong direction.

His second function is legal and moral rescue. When Dou Xian's counteraccusation turns Zhi Shou's criticism into a death case, He Chang argues that a confidential court official is supposed to correct great ministers, that silence before wrongdoing would be the true crime, and that killing a loyal critic for "slander" will damage future speech. The sentence is reduced from death to exile, but Zhi kills himself before departure.

He Chang is therefore not simply a successful rescuer. His memorial shows that argument can still mitigate punishment, but the outcome also shows the limits of mitigation inside a dowager-favored outer-relative court.

## Key Characteristics

- Remonstrant who links military timing, moral justification, farming season, and fiscal burden.
- Critic of Dou-family mansion construction as public harm during mobilization.
- Rescue advocate who reframes Zhi Shou's direct criticism as official duty.
- Partial success case because death becomes exile while political safety still collapses.
- Court-feedback figure who exposes the cost of killing loyal remonstrants.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 761. It does not reconstruct He Chang's complete biography or later office path, and it treats the rescue as partial because the source explicitly preserves Zhi Shou's suicide after mitigation.
