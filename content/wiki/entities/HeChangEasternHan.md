## Overview

何敞 / He Chang now appears as the Eastern Han official who connects omen skepticism, late-[[EmperorZhangOfHan|Zhangdi]] fiscal restraint, murder-case procedure, anti-war remonstrance, anti-luxury criticism, and rescue speech under [[DouXianEasternHan|Dou Xian]]'s power. Hanji 757 shows him warning that strange palace signs should not be read automatically as auspicious. Hanji 758 shows him warning that lavish rewards and royal-kin residence are wrong during disaster and frontier strain. Hanji 760 shows him helping redirect the investigation of [[LiuChangDuxiangHouEasternHan|Liu Chang]]'s murder away from [[LiuGangEasternHan|Liu Gang]] and toward the capital culprit. Hanji 761 then shows him arguing against the Northern Xiongnu expedition and Dou-family mansion construction before intervening when [[ZhiShouEasternHan|Zhi Shou]] is sentenced to death.

## Current Profile

Hanji 757 supplies He Chang's omen-skeptical layer. Zhangdi changes the era name after many auspicious signs are reported around the capital and the realm. He Chang argues that unusual signs should be read cautiously: auspicious signs follow virtue, disasters arise from bad government, and strange birds or grasses appearing in palace spaces should not be treated as simple celebration. [[SongYouEasternHan|Song You]] and [[YuanAnEasternHan|Yuan An]] are afraid to respond, showing the political risk of challenging a favorable-omen mood.

Hanji 758 supplies He Chang's fiscal-boundary role. Zhangdi has rewarded officials and royal kin generously while floods, droughts, Qiang attacks, and exhausted public-private finances make state reserves precious. He Chang addresses [[SongYouEasternHan|Song You / 宋由]] as Taiwei, urges him to return the reward as an example, asks him to explain the burden to the emperor, and recommends sending royal princes and marquises back to their fiefs.

Hanji 760 supplies He Chang's case-review role. He tells Song You that Liu Chang is a royal kinsman and vassal who came to the capital to mourn the former emperor, yet was murdered among armed guards. He argues that investigators are blindly seeking a culprit without knowing the killer's identity. Song You accepts his participation, and the case is quickly solved through review with the Three Excellencies' offices.

Hanji 761 supplies He Chang's policy criticism. He argues that the [[NorthernXiongnuLateHan|Northern Xiongnu]] has not committed a fresh rebellion or humiliation requiring revenge, while large spring mobilization will interfere with farming and intensify public resentment. He also criticizes mansions for [[DouDuEasternHan|Dou Du]] and [[DouJingEasternHan|Dou Jing]] because public hardship, fiscal emptiness, and campaign mobilization make luxury construction politically exemplary in the wrong direction.

His second Hanji 761 function is legal and moral rescue. When Dou Xian's counteraccusation turns Zhi Shou's criticism into a death case, He Chang argues that a confidential court official is supposed to correct great ministers, that silence before wrongdoing would be the true crime, and that killing a loyal critic for "slander" will damage future speech. The sentence is reduced from death to exile, but Zhi kills himself before departure.

He Chang is therefore not simply a successful rescuer. His memorials show that argument can sometimes mitigate or redirect power, but the outcomes also show the limits of advice when royal affection, outer-relative power, and court risk outrun institutional restraint.

## Key Characteristics

- Omen-skeptical remonstrant who treats palace anomalies as a warning rather than automatic legitimacy.
- Fiscal remonstrant who treats lavish rewards and royal-kin residence as wrong during disaster, frontier pressure, and exhausted public finance.
- Case reviewer who challenges a politically convenient investigation after Liu Chang's murder.
- Remonstrant who links military timing, moral justification, farming season, and fiscal burden.
- Critic of Dou-family mansion construction as public harm during mobilization.
- Rescue advocate who reframes Zhi Shou's direct criticism as official duty.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 757, Hanji 758, Hanji 760, and Hanji 761. It does not reconstruct He Chang's complete biography or later office path. Hanji 757 gives an omen-warning speech under Zhangdi; Hanji 758 gives fiscal and royal-kin restraint before Zhangdi's death; Hanji 760 gives procedural correction in the Liu Chang case; Hanji 761 gives anti-campaign, anti-luxury, and Zhi Shou rescue remonstrance. The source spelling 何畅 in Hanji 757 is normalized cautiously to the existing 何敞 route because the court setting and next-source continuity align. The Zhi Shou rescue remains partial because the source explicitly preserves Zhi's suicide after mitigation. Hanji 760 does not by itself give He control over the final punishment of Dou Xian.
