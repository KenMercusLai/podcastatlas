## Overview

何敞 / He Chang now appears as the Eastern Han official who connects murder-case procedure, anti-war remonstrance, anti-luxury criticism, and rescue speech under [[DouXianEasternHan|Dou Xian]]'s power. Hanji 760 shows him helping redirect the investigation of [[LiuChangDuxiangHouEasternHan|Liu Chang]]'s murder away from [[LiuGangEasternHan|Liu Gang]] and toward the capital culprit. Hanji 761 then shows him arguing against the Northern Xiongnu expedition and Dou-family mansion construction before intervening when [[ZhiShouEasternHan|Zhi Shou]] is sentenced to death.

## Current Profile

Hanji 760 supplies He Chang's first current role as case reviewer. He tells [[SongYouEasternHan|Song You]] that Liu Chang is a royal kinsman and vassal who came to the capital to mourn the former emperor, yet was murdered among armed guards. He argues that investigators are blindly seeking a culprit without knowing the killer's identity. Song You accepts his participation, and the case is quickly solved through review with the Three Excellencies' offices.

Hanji 761 supplies He Chang's policy criticism. He argues that the [[NorthernXiongnuLateHan|Northern Xiongnu]] has not committed a fresh rebellion or humiliation requiring revenge, while large spring mobilization will interfere with farming and intensify public resentment. He also criticizes mansions for [[DouDuEasternHan|Dou Du]] and [[DouJingEasternHan|Dou Jing]] because public hardship, fiscal emptiness, and campaign mobilization make luxury construction politically exemplary in the wrong direction.

His second function is legal and moral rescue. When Dou Xian's counteraccusation turns Zhi Shou's criticism into a death case, He Chang argues that a confidential court official is supposed to correct great ministers, that silence before wrongdoing would be the true crime, and that killing a loyal critic for "slander" will damage future speech. The sentence is reduced from death to exile, but Zhi kills himself before departure.

He Chang is therefore not simply a successful rescuer. His memorial shows that argument can still mitigate punishment, but the outcome also shows the limits of mitigation inside a dowager-favored outer-relative court.

## Key Characteristics

- Case reviewer who challenges a politically convenient investigation after Liu Chang's murder.
- Remonstrant who links military timing, moral justification, farming season, and fiscal burden.
- Critic of Dou-family mansion construction as public harm during mobilization.
- Rescue advocate who reframes Zhi Shou's direct criticism as official duty.
- Partial success case because death becomes exile while political safety still collapses.
- Court-feedback figure who exposes the cost of killing loyal remonstrants.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 760 and Hanji 761. It does not reconstruct He Chang's complete biography or later office path, and it treats the Zhi Shou rescue as partial because the source explicitly preserves Zhi's suicide after mitigation. Hanji 760 adds procedural correction before the policy-remonstrance layer, but it does not by itself give He control over the final punishment of Dou Xian.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 760 as He Chang's first current layer: legal-procedural correction in the Liu Chang murder case.
- Reframed He Chang as both an investigation actor and a later anti-campaign, anti-luxury, and rescue-remonstrance actor.
