# Invasion and Partition of Poland (1939)

## Overview

The 1939 invasion and partition of [[Poland]] grew from the occupation of Prague, failed German attempts to reduce Poland to client status, the [[BritishFrenchGuaranteeToPoland|British-French guarantee]], the [[MolotovRibbentropPact|Nazi-Soviet pact]], delayed Polish mobilization, and the [[GleiwitzIncident|Gleiwitz false flag]] into German and Soviet attacks that ended Polish sovereignty inside its territory under two occupation systems.

## Current Profile

The campaign's diplomatic preparation began before the Nazi-Soviet pact. After the [[OccupationOfCzechoslovakia1939|occupation of Prague]], German demands for Danzig, extraterritorial transit across the Polish Corridor, and Polish alignment with the Anti-Comintern Pact appeared to threaten Poland's trade access and independent foreign policy. Poland refused subordinate status, Britain and France guaranteed its independence, and Hitler ordered Fall Weiss despite the new commitment.

The final crisis joined isolation, coercion, and staged justification. Hitler used the Nazi-Soviet bargain, repeated territorial demands, attempts to detach Britain from its guarantee, and deadlines presented as negotiation while invasion plans continued. Britain and France discouraged early Polish mobilization to avoid appearing provocative; when mobilization finally began, Germany was already moving from coercive diplomacy to fabricated frontier incidents and attack.

The invasion then became three connected events: rapid military defeat, strategic abandonment, and ideological violence. German forces attacked from the west, East Prussia, and Slovakia with concentrated mobile formations and overwhelming early air power. Poland's plan depended on withdrawing to defensive lines until Britain and France attacked in the west, but their declarations of war produced only a limited Saar advance and no relief capable of changing the campaign.

The Soviet invasion removed Poland's eastern refuge and completed the strategic encirclement. Germany annexed western territory and established the General Government in the center and south, while the Soviet Union occupied the east. Military conquest immediately became social destruction: German bombing and strafing hit civilians, SS units targeted elites and Jews, and occupation authorities attacked education, culture, leadership, and the conditions of Polish national continuity.

## Key Characteristics

- Multi-directional German attack followed by Soviet invasion from the east.
- Polish refusal of German client-state demands, a western guarantee without rapid aid, and later diplomatic isolation through the Molotov-Ribbentrop Pact.
- Delayed mobilization under allied pressure and a false-flag pretext immediately before attack.
- Early demonstration of concentrated mobile warfare and Luftwaffe dominance.
- Polish strategy dependent on western military relief that did not arrive.
- Civilian bombing, village destruction, execution, and elite targeting embedded in conquest.
- Territorial partition replacing battlefield defeat with rival occupation systems.

## Qualifications

This profile is grounded in three consecutive narrative episodes covering the prewar break over Prague and Danzig, final diplomacy, German campaign, and [[SiegeOfWarsaw1939|Warsaw]]. Soviet occupation, Polish operations outside the highlighted episodes, regional civilian experience, and later resistance require broader evidence. The sources support client-state pressure and a mobilization-delay mechanism without proving which concessions, guarantees, alliances, or earlier mobilization decisions would have prevented or changed the war. Protocol details, force and casualty totals, execution and deportation estimates, motives, reported dialogue, and counterfactual outcomes remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Extended the campaign's prehistory back to Prague, the Danzig-corridor demands, and Poland's refusal of client status.
- Added the western guarantee as a real political threshold whose weak relief capacity failed to prevent Poland's defeat.

- [[SovietUnion]] - eastern invader and partitioning power.

- [[GleiwitzIncident]] - fabricated German pretext staged immediately before invasion.
- [[OpponentResolveMiscalculation]] - failed German forecast that Britain and France would not honor their commitment.

- [[BritishFrenchGuaranteeToPoland]] - deterrent commitment activated by invasion but unable to provide timely relief.
- [[SiegeOfWarsaw1939]] - decisive urban defense and bombardment within the campaign.
- [[Blitzkrieg]] - operational pattern demonstrated through concentrated speed and air power.
- [[PhoneyWar]] - western strategic lull that followed declarations of war without saving Poland.
