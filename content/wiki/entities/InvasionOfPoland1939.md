# Invasion and Partition of Poland (1939)

## Overview

The 1939 invasion and partition of [[Poland]] grew from coercive diplomacy, the [[MolotovRibbentropPact|Nazi-Soviet pact]], delayed Polish mobilization, and the [[GleiwitzIncident|Gleiwitz false flag]] into [[NaziGermany|Nazi Germany]]'s attack on 1 September and the [[SovietUnion|Soviet Union]]'s eastern invasion on 17 September, ending Polish sovereignty inside its territory under two occupation systems.

## Current Profile

The campaign's diplomatic preparation joined isolation, coercion, and staged justification. Hitler used the Nazi-Soviet bargain, repeated territorial demands, attempts to detach Britain from its guarantee, and deadlines presented as negotiation while invasion plans continued. Britain and France discouraged early Polish mobilization to avoid appearing provocative; when mobilization finally began, Germany was already moving from coercive diplomacy to fabricated frontier incidents and attack.

The invasion then became three connected events: rapid military defeat, strategic abandonment, and ideological violence. German forces attacked from the west, East Prussia, and Slovakia with concentrated mobile formations and overwhelming early air power. Poland's plan depended on withdrawing to defensive lines until Britain and France attacked in the west, but their declarations of war produced only a limited Saar advance and no relief capable of changing the campaign.

The Soviet invasion removed Poland's eastern refuge and completed the strategic encirclement. Germany annexed western territory and established the General Government in the center and south, while the Soviet Union occupied the east. Military conquest immediately became social destruction: German bombing and strafing hit civilians, SS units targeted elites and Jews, and occupation authorities attacked education, culture, leadership, and the conditions of Polish national continuity.

## Key Characteristics

- Multi-directional German attack followed by Soviet invasion from the east.
- Diplomatic isolation and secret partition planning through the Molotov-Ribbentrop Pact.
- Delayed mobilization under allied pressure and a false-flag pretext immediately before attack.
- Early demonstration of concentrated mobile warfare and Luftwaffe dominance.
- Polish strategy dependent on western military relief that did not arrive.
- Civilian bombing, village destruction, execution, and elite targeting embedded in conquest.
- Territorial partition replacing battlefield defeat with rival occupation systems.

## Evidence

- Diplomatic and propagandistic preparation: [[531-hitlers-war-on-poland-the-pact-with-stalin-part-2-glt5437884494]] connects the Nazi-Soviet bargain, failed pressure on Britain, delayed Polish mobilization, fixed invasion orders, and the Gleiwitz false flag.
- Military collapse: [[531-hitlers-war-on-poland-the-pact-with-stalin-part-2-glt5437884494]] and [[532-hitlers-war-on-poland-the-fall-of-warsaw-part-3-glt6308788391]] connect delayed mobilization and diplomatic isolation to geographic exposure, German concentration, air superiority, and fast penetration.
- Strategic isolation: [[532-hitlers-war-on-poland-the-fall-of-warsaw-part-3-glt6308788391]] describes limited French action, British restraint, and the Soviet invasion that ended the remaining eastern option.
- Ideological violence: [[532-hitlers-war-on-poland-the-fall-of-warsaw-part-3-glt6308788391]] connects civilian attack, village burning, Einsatzgruppen lists, anti-Jewish violence, and the destruction of Polish institutions.
- Partition and occupation: [[532-hitlers-war-on-poland-the-fall-of-warsaw-part-3-glt6308788391]] distinguishes German annexation, the General Government, and Soviet-held eastern territory.

## Qualifications

This profile is grounded in two consecutive narrative episodes focused on the final diplomatic crisis, German campaign, and [[SiegeOfWarsaw1939|Warsaw]]. Soviet occupation, Polish operations outside the highlighted episodes, regional civilian experience, and later resistance require broader evidence. The new source supports a mobilization-delay mechanism without proving how much an earlier order would have changed the outcome. Protocol details, force and casualty totals, execution and deportation estimates, motives, reported dialogue, and counterfactual diplomatic or military outcomes remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Added the Nazi-Soviet pact and secret partition logic to the campaign's prehistory.
- Added coercive diplomacy, delayed Polish mobilization, and the Gleiwitz false flag to the invasion sequence.
- Distinguished Britain's declaration of war from the absence of timely military relief to Poland.
