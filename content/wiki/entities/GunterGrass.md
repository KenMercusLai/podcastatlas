# Günter Grass / 君特·格拉斯

## Overview

Günter Grass is presented as a German writer and visual artist whose fiction joins [[DanzigGdansk|Danzig/Gdańsk]], German historical responsibility, political argument, grotesque invention, and difficult moral ambiguity.

## Current Profile

The source places [[TheFlounder|《比目鱼》]] beside *The Tin Drum* and the wider Danzig trilogy. Grass's writing repeatedly brings Nazism, war, nation, gender, and contemporary politics into formally unruly fiction. His public moral authority is complicated by the late disclosure that he had served in the Waffen-SS when young; the episode treats this as a serious biographical controversy rather than a reason either to erase the work or accept its politics uncritically.

## Key Characteristics

- Builds fiction from the layered German, Polish, and Kashubian history of Danzig/Gdańsk.
- Combines political engagement with grotesque, mythic, satirical, and historically expansive forms.
- Uses contradictory voices instead of converting literature into a single political instruction.
- Worked across writing, poetry, drawing, and sculpture.
- Carries a contested public profile because moral intervention and delayed disclosure of Waffen-SS service coexist.

Labels such as magical realism, claims about his party activity, the details of military service and disclosure, and judgments about translations or feminist reception remain source-scoped.
