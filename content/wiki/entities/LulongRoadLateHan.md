## Overview

卢龙道 / Lulong Road is the difficult, long-disused northern route that [[TianChou|田畴]] proposes for [[CaoCao|曹操]]'s surprise movement against [[Wuhuan|乌桓]]. It converts an apparently blocked campaign into a rear approach and later becomes the principal merit behind Cao Cao's attempt to reward Tian Chou.

## Current Profile

Hanji 1078 supplies the road's operational role. Summer rain has made the ordinary coastal approach impassable while Wuhuan guards the obvious route. Tian Chou says the old road from Lulong toward Liucheng has been broken for more than two centuries but remains faintly traversable; he proposes that Cao Cao appear to withdraw, cross by Lulong and Baitan, and strike the lightly defended rear. The episode then describes a road-opening march of more than five hundred li through Xu Wu Mountain before Wuhuan receives late warning.

Hanji 1110 supplies the commemorative consequence. It recalls the Lulong Road advice as a major cause of campaign success and makes that contribution the basis for Cao Cao's repeated attempt to enfeoff Tian Chou.

## Key Characteristics

- Long-disused northern approach treated as passable through local knowledge and new road work.
- Alternative to a rain-blocked and enemy-guarded coastal axis.
- Hidden rear approach paired with an apparent withdrawal and a misleading roadside notice.
- Surprise axis presented as an important cause of Cao Cao's victory.
- Campaign contribution later converted into a disputed merit-reward claim.

## Qualifications

Hanji 1078 is a short popular-history treatment rather than a route reconstruction. The statement that the road had been unusable for more than two centuries, the Baitan and Liucheng sequence, exact distances, engineering scale, Xianbei crossing, and opponent awareness remain source-scoped. Hanji 1110 recalls the campaign mainly to explain the later reward dispute and does not independently establish those details.

## What Changed

- Added the blocked coastal approach that makes Lulong Road strategically valuable.
- Added the feigned withdrawal, misleading notice, rear-attack purpose, and road-opening march.
- Retained the later reward dispute as the route's commemorative consequence.
