# The New Dark Ages

## Overview
The New Dark Ages is [[JamesMarriott]]'s polemic on declining reading and the attention effects of smartphones, as represented in The Intelligence.

## Current Profile
The episode uses the book to connect falling long-form reading with a wider return of oral-style media. Its central contribution is not a claim that speech is inherently irrational, but that media favor different structures: edited writing can sustain abstraction and ordered argument, while live speech often advances through repetition, accumulation, vividness, and audience response.

## Key Characteristics
- Synthesizes evidence and anecdotes about reduced reading.
- Treats smartphones as competitors for sustained attention.
- Draws on Walter Ong's distinction between oral and literate cultures.
- Helps explain podcast and political styles that work better aloud than on the page.

## Evidence
- **Reading decline:** [[the-end-of-the-world-is-ai-an-existential-threat-6aaa68a64cef87591d2b659b]] introduces the book through concerns from parents, professors, and adult readers.
- **Media form:** [[the-end-of-the-world-is-ai-an-existential-threat-6aaa68a64cef87591d2b659b]] relays its contrast between oral back-looping and deliberately structured writing.
- **Political consequence:** [[the-end-of-the-world-is-ai-an-existential-threat-6aaa68a64cef87591d2b659b]] applies the frame to podcasts, epithets, and concrete political symbols.

## Qualifications
The episode partly resists decline nostalgia: printed polemic once helped drive witch hunts, and text-based platforms can also reward anger and simplification. The book's empirical claims are not independently assessed here.
