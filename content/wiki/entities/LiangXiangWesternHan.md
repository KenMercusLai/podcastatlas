## Overview

梁相 / Liang Xiang is synthesized as a Western Han Tingwei whose procedural caution becomes politically dangerous under [[HanAidi|汉哀帝]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-598-gudai-chaotang-de-douzheng-you-duo-canku-luof-1bxomtcsrkcvmuplpugaw4d|Hanji 598]] introduces him through the Dongping king Liu Yun rebellion case: he finds contradictory testimony and requests higher-level review, but Aidi interprets that request as an effort to delay punishment.

## Current Profile

Liang Xiang's wiki role is not yet a full biography. He functions as the legal-procedure trigger for the Wang Jia conflict. The episode says winter execution timing mattered: if the case dragged past winter, prisoners might avoid death through timing or amnesty. Liang Xiang's request to bring prisoners to Chang'an for senior review could therefore be read in two ways: as due process over weak confessions or as political obstruction.

Hanji 598 emphasizes that Aidi chooses the second reading. Shangshu figures initially find Liang's proposal feasible, but Aidi's illness and suspicion lead him to dismiss Liang Xiang and others. Later, [[WangJiaWesternHan|王嘉]]'s attempt to recommend the same group turns Liang Xiang's case into evidence against Wang Jia.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han Tingwei handling the Dongping king Liu Yun rebellion case.
- Notices flaws in testimony and seeks review by senior court officials.
- Operates under execution-season pressure where delay can affect life and death.
- Becomes a target of Aidi's suspicion because procedure is read as disloyal protection.
- His later rehabilitation by Wang Jia becomes accusation material against Wang Jia.

## Qualifications

The source does not provide Liang Xiang's full career, the full Liu Yun case file, or an independent evaluation of whether the confessions were actually false. The episode's main use of Liang Xiang is procedural and political: his request shows how legal caution can become suspect when the ruler is fearful and punitive.
