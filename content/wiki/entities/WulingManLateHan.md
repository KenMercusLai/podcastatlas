## Overview

武陵蛮 / Wuling Man is the southern [[Jingzhou|Jingzhou]] frontier population whose current wiki evidence now spans an early Eastern Han 49 CE campaign and later late-Han rebellion branches. Hanji 726 supplies the [[MaYuanEasternHan|Ma Yuan]] death and [[ZongJunEasternHan|Zong Jun]] surrender-settlement layer; Hanji 818 supplies the 137 CE tax-triggered revolt under [[EmperorShunOfHan|Emperor Shun]]; and Hanji 839 supplies a later 153 CE rebellion-and-surrender notice under [[YingFengLateHan|Ying Feng]].

## Current Profile

Hanji 726 gives the earliest current layer. Ma Yuan dies in the Wuling campaign while epidemic is ravaging the Han army and the opposing side is also hungry. After Ma's death, Zong Jun uses an unauthorized imperial surrender order, sends an acting official into the enemy camp, follows with troops, and secures surrender. The rebels kill their leaders, return to their commanderies, and receive newly appointed local officials.

Hanji 818 makes Wuling Man a fiscal-governance warning. The Wuling administrator proposes levying submitted Man groups under the same rent and tax expectations as Han subjects. [[YuXuEasternHan|Yu Xu]] argues that inherited light-burden arrangements are a stabilizing bargain, but Shun rejects the warning. Collection pressure and tribute-cloth mismatch contribute to revolt; [[LiJinWulingLateHan|Li Jin]] then suppresses and settles the population through military defeat, better local officials, and soothing measures.

Hanji 839 records a later recurrence. Zhanshan and others rebel, and Ying Feng, then Wuling administrator, recruits or persuades them into surrender. The page retains its inherited "late Han" route name, but the current evidence now begins earlier in Eastern Han.

## Key Characteristics

- Southern Jingzhou frontier population whose current evidence includes 49 CE, 137 CE, and 153 CE layers.
- Campaign opponent in Ma Yuan's final expedition and Zong Jun's discretionary surrender settlement.
- Submitted group whose inherited lighter fiscal status is treated by Yu Xu as a political settlement.
- Recurrent rebel force that requires both military suppression and post-conflict administrative repair.
- Population whose stabilization depends on credible surrender terms, local officials, and burden management rather than punishment alone.

## Qualifications

The page does not provide a full ethnic, regional, or military history of Wuling Man communities. Hanji 726 gives a surrender-settlement branch but not the full causes of the 49 CE campaign. Hanji 818 gives a stronger cause chain for the 137 CE revolt but leaves detailed community organization, tribute quantities, and appointee names source-scoped. Hanji 839 records only a compact later rebellion notice.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 726 as the earliest current Wuling Man layer: Ma Yuan's final campaign, Zong Jun's discretionary edict, surrender, dispersal, and local appointments.
- Reframed the page from only late-Han tax and Ying Feng evidence into a longer Eastern Han frontier-settlement sequence.
- Kept the inherited page route while qualifying that the evidence now begins before the late-Han branches.
