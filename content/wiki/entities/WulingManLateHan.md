## Overview

武陵蛮 / Wuling Man is the southern [[Jingzhou|Jingzhou]] frontier population whose current wiki evidence now begins with a 47-48 CE revolt that kills Liu Shang and draws [[MaYuanEasternHan|Ma Yuan]] into his final campaign. Hanji 724 supplies the failed Liu Shang expedition and Ma Yuan deployment prelude; Hanji 726 supplies Ma Yuan's death and [[ZongJunEasternHan|Zong Jun]]'s surrender-settlement layer; Hanji 818 supplies the 137 CE tax-triggered revolt under [[EmperorShunOfHan|Emperor Shun]]; and Hanji 839 supplies a later 153 CE rebellion-and-surrender notice under [[YingFengLateHan|Ying Feng]].

## Current Profile

Hanji 724 now gives the earliest current layer. Wuling Man leader Xiang Dancheng and others rebel after a separate Nan Commandery revolt is suppressed. Liu Shang leads more than ten thousand elite troops up the Yuan River toward Wuxi, advances too deeply into difficult terrain, and is killed with his army. Later, after Li Song and Ma Cheng fail to defeat the Wuling forces at Linyuan, Ma Yuan requests command despite his age and is sent toward Wuxi with Ma Wu and Geng Shu.

Hanji 726 gives the next settlement layer. Ma Yuan dies in the Wuling campaign while epidemic is ravaging the Han army and the opposing side is also hungry. After Ma's death, Zong Jun uses an unauthorized imperial surrender order, sends an acting official into the enemy camp, follows with troops, and secures surrender. The rebels kill their leaders, return to their commanderies, and receive newly appointed local officials.

Hanji 818 makes Wuling Man a fiscal-governance warning. The Wuling administrator proposes levying submitted Man groups under the same rent and tax expectations as Han subjects. [[YuXuEasternHan|Yu Xu]] argues that inherited light-burden arrangements are a stabilizing bargain, but Shun rejects the warning. Collection pressure and tribute-cloth mismatch contribute to revolt; [[LiJinWulingLateHan|Li Jin]] then suppresses and settles the population through military defeat, better local officials, and soothing measures.

Hanji 839 records a later recurrence. Zhanshan and others rebel, and Ying Feng, then Wuling administrator, recruits or persuades them into surrender. The page retains its inherited "late Han" route name, but the current evidence now begins earlier in Eastern Han.

## Key Characteristics

- Southern Jingzhou frontier population whose current evidence includes 47-49 CE, 137 CE, and 153 CE layers.
- Campaign opponent whose terrain advantage first destroys Liu Shang's army, then becomes Ma Yuan's final expedition and Zong Jun's discretionary surrender settlement.
- Submitted group whose inherited lighter fiscal status is treated by Yu Xu as a political settlement.
- Recurrent rebel force that requires both military suppression and post-conflict administrative repair.
- Population whose stabilization depends on credible surrender terms, local officials, and burden management rather than punishment alone.
- Evidence that southern-frontier campaigns can fail through terrain, overconfidence, and command conditions before settlement becomes possible.

## Qualifications

The page does not provide a full ethnic, regional, or military history of Wuling Man communities. Hanji 724 gives the failed campaign and Ma Yuan deployment prelude, while Hanji 726 gives the later surrender-settlement branch under epidemic and hunger conditions. Hanji 818 gives a stronger cause chain for the 137 CE revolt but leaves detailed community organization, tribute quantities, and appointee names source-scoped. Hanji 839 records only a compact later rebellion notice.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 724 as the earliest current Wuling Man layer: Liu Shang's terrain-driven defeat and Ma Yuan's late-life deployment.
- Reframed Hanji 726 as the follow-on settlement layer after the failed Liu Shang and Ma Yuan campaign prelude.
- Preserved the page's longer Eastern Han frontier-settlement sequence before the late-Han tax and Ying Feng evidence.
- Kept the inherited page route while qualifying that the evidence now begins before the late-Han branches.
