## Overview

武陵蛮 / Wuling Man is the southern Jingzhou frontier population whose current wiki profile now spans a 137 CE tax-triggered revolt under [[EmperorShunOfHan|汉顺帝]] and a later 153 CE rebellion-and-surrender notice. Hanji 818 supplies the earlier fiscal-stability case: [[YuXuEasternHan|虞诩]] warns that taxing submitted groups like Han households will produce more disorder than revenue, Shun ignores him, and the resulting revolt requires [[LiJinWulingLateHan|李进]] to suppress and settle it. Hanji 839 then names Zhanshan and others as rebel leaders and credits [[YingFengLateHan|应奉]], then Wuling administrator, with bringing them back into submission.

## Current Profile

Hanji 818 makes Wuling a fiscal-governance warning before the later Ying Feng branch. The Wuling administrator proposes levying submitted Man groups under the same rent and tax expectations as Han subjects; most officials agree, but Yu Xu argues that inherited light-burden arrangements are a stabilizing bargain. Shun rejects the warning, and tribute-cloth mismatch plus collection pressure contributes to killings and revolt.

The same source gives the immediate repair layer. Wuling Man forces besiege Chongcheng and attack another strategic point; Shun sends Li Jin as Wuling administrator, and Li Jin defeats the rebels, appoints good local officials, and soothes the population afterward. Hanji 839 then gives a later compact notice: Zhanshan and others rebel again, and Ying Feng uses surrender recruitment to bring them back.

## Key Characteristics

- Southern Jingzhou frontier population whose current evidence begins with a 137 CE tax-stability failure.
- Submitted group whose inherited light-burden status is treated by Yu Xu as a political settlement, not merely a privilege.
- Rebel force that requires Li Jin's military suppression and administrative repair after Shun rejects the warning.
- Later recurrent rebellion branch led by Zhanshan and others, whose details remain source-scoped.
- Re-enters submission in Hanji 839 through Ying Feng's招降 rather than through a narrated punitive campaign.

## Qualifications

The page should not be read as a full ethnic, regional, or military history of Wuling Man communities. Hanji 818 gives a stronger cause chain for the 137 CE revolt but still leaves detailed community organization, exact tribute quantities, and postwar local appointees source-scoped. Hanji 839 records only a later rebellion notice and leaves Zhanshan, the prior rebellion, and settlement terms source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 818 as the earlier Wuling layer: Yu Xu's tax warning, Shun's rejection, revolt, Li Jin's suppression, and administrative repair.
- Reframed Hanji 839 as a later recurrence and surrender notice rather than the only current evidence for Wuling Man.
