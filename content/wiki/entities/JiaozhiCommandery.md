## Overview
交趾郡 / Jiaozhi Commandery is a southern Han frontier setting whose wiki profile now spans six source layers. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-699-gan-yu-liuxiu-zhengxiong-de-ta-weihe-candan-shouchang-lth-8mndudj3khf9cnsouj86c1-z|Hanji 699]] supplies an early Guangwu submission and instruction layer through Deng Rang, [[CenPengEasternHan|岑彭]], [[XiGuangEasternHan|锡光]], and [[RenYanEasternHan|任延]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-718-zhengce-ruhe-chengwei-shishang-no-1-de-nvwang-likq4pahq21k6bxbaohwdhcnwg5r|Hanji 718]] adds the early 40 CE [[ZhengCeJiaozhi|征侧]] and [[ZhengErJiaozhi|征贰]] revolt; Hanji 819 backfills the 138 CE crisis that [[LiGuLateHan|李固]] resolves by rejecting a large distant expedition; Hanji 920 adds the 181 CE Wuhu Man and [[LiangLongLateHan|梁龙]] rebellion suppressed by [[ZhuJunLateHan|朱儁]]; Hanji 927 adds [[JiaCongLateHan|贾从]]'s later root-cause pacification; and Hanji 1119 moves the commandery into [[ShiXie|士燮]]'s long local rule and Sun-Wu consolidation after his death.

## Current Profile
[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-699-gan-yu-liuxiu-zhengxiong-de-ta-weihe-candan-shouchang-lth-8mndudj3khf9cnsouj86c1-z|Hanji 699]] supplies Jiaozhi's earliest current restoration layer. Jiaozhi Mu Deng Rang responds to Cen Peng's friendship letter and Liu Xiu's proclamations, sends tribute with other southern or Jiangnan actors, and receives enfeoffment. The same episode says Xi Guang in Jiaozhi and Ren Yan in Jiuzhen teach ritual, marriage customs, and agriculture, making local incorporation appear through elite submission plus durable instruction before the later Zheng sisters revolt.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-718-zhengce-ruhe-chengwei-shishang-no-1-de-nvwang-likq4pahq21k6bxbaohwdhcnwg5r|Hanji 718]] supplies Jiaozhi's earliest current Eastern Han layer. [[SuDingJiaozhi|苏定]]'s attempted punishment of Zheng Ce triggers a sister-led revolt in spring 40 CE; Jiuzhen, Rinan, Hepu, and other groups respond; Zheng Ce takes sixty-five cities, declares herself king, and makes Miling the capital. The source says the Jiaozhi inspector and commandery administrators can only defend themselves until [[MaYuanEasternHan|马援]] suppresses the revolt in 43 CE.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-819-2-ren-gong-bi-4-wanren-tade-quanmou-lingren-paianjuejue-lsslj3y-krnd8h-c8x5pgsqeinff|Hanji 819]] makes Jiaozhi a logistical and appointment-quality test under [[EmperorShunOfHan|汉顺帝]]. The court majority wants to send a major general and 40,000 troops south; Li Gu warns that long-distance recruitment would damage the recruiting regions, expose soldiers to disease and exhaustion, and impose enormous grain cost. He proposes [[TemporaryFrontierRetrenchment|临时边防收缩]] for Rinan, rewards and division tactics, and the appointments of [[ZhangQiaoLateHan|张乔]] and [[ZhuLiangLateHan|祝良]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-920-mingwu-zhege-daoli-caineng-zhuan-daqian-lqmabg3bpyz6ct2v4arx-iun57ws|Hanji 920]] gives Jiaozhi an 181 CE rebellion layer. The source says [[WuhuManJiaozhi|乌浒蛮]] rebellion had persisted for a long time, local officials could not settle it, and Liang Long and others then rose and broke commanderies and counties. [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]] sends Zhu Jun as inspector; Zhu Jun kills Liang Long and other rebel leaders and pacifies the rebellion in less than a month.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-927-zuo-shengyi-de-zuigao-jingjie-shi-shenme-lngfbjn6xui9k-9lp-ihdqkhpbpr|Hanji 927]] supplies the extraction-diagnosis layer. It introduces Jiaozhi as a southern commandery in the region of today's northern Vietnam and the Red River valley, with pearls and distance from the capital making earlier inspectors treat the post as a temporary enrichment opportunity. Jia Cong's repair then turns the page toward cause investigation, burden relief, reassurance, removal of violent leaders, and clean county appointments.

The commandery's late-Han local order centers on Shi Xie, who becomes Jiaozhi administrator in 187 CE, joins family offices across [[Jiaozhou|交州]] to classical reputation and reception of northern scholars, and remains in place by cooperating with successive inspectors and later [[SunQuan|孙权]]. After Shi Xie's death in 226 CE, Sun Quan appoints a replacement, [[ShiHui|士徽]] claims the office, and [[LyuDai|吕岱]] obtains the surrender and execution of six Shi brothers. Jiaozhi therefore becomes a case not only of rebellion and pacification, but of [[PersonalizedLocalAutonomySuccession|个人化地方自治继承断裂]] in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1119-ta-shengqian-guishun-sunquan-sihou-weihe-jiazu-bei-ganjin-shajue-ll4mblhk7ydn3pdxg-5zucstokzz|Hanji 1119]].

## Key Characteristics
- Early Guangwu submission and instruction theater where tribute, enfeoffment, ritual teaching, and agricultural teaching precede later revolt.
- Eastern Han revolt theater where local resentment escalates into female-led kingship.
- Distant southern commandery where logistics, disease, and grain cost make large expedition plans risky.
- Repeated rebellion and succession-conflict theater, appearing in 138, 181, 184, and post-226 CE source layers.
- Place where appointment quality can matter more than troop scale when officials have local credibility and suitable methods.
- Legitimacy-failure setting where extraction and blocked complaints help explain later rebellion.
- Comparative control site: Li Gu's appointments, Zhu Jun's suppression, Jia Cong's repair, and Sun Wu's replacement of the Shi network are distinct response models.

## Qualifications
This page is a bounded synthesis from six podcast source notes, not a complete administrative history of Jiaozhi. Hanji 699 gives a court-facing integration narrative but not local reception, coercion level, or full office chronology for Deng Rang, Xi Guang, and Ren Yan. Hanji 718 adds a 40 CE revolt and a compressed 43 CE suppression notice but does not provide full geography, city list, ethnic classification, or campaign route. The 138 CE branch in Hanji 819 includes transcript variants for Jiaozhi, Jiuzhen, and Rinan; the page normalizes those names cautiously. Hanji 920 emphasizes rapid suppression, Hanji 927 emphasizes extraction and repair, and Hanji 1119 emphasizes brokered local autonomy followed by violent centralization; these are distinct chronological models rather than direct contradictions. Hanji 1119's cultural-flourishing claims, administrative redivision, surrender assurances, and personal motives require broader textual comparison.

## What Changed
- Extended Jiaozhi from early-Eastern-Han frontier governance into Shi Xie's late-Han local order.
- Added the post-226 conflict between Sun Quan's appointment authority and Shi Hui's inherited capacity.
- Distinguished personal accommodation under Shi Xie from hereditary entitlement after his death.
- Expanded the response comparison to include Sun-Wu administrative division, induced surrender, and elite decapitation.
