## Overview

周丘 / Zhou Qiu is a Xiapi native and disregarded retainer of [[LiuBiWuKing|吴王刘濞]] who turns a borrowed tally and local relationships into a rapid but patron-dependent mobilization during the [[RebellionOfTheSevenStatesWesternHan|Rebellion of the Seven States]].

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-235-liubi-zhisi-jielu-zhichang-shengcun-qian-guize-lp1wxe_buqe_zrilsfwsfzqoa2ef|Hanji 235]] says Zhou had fled Xiapi after breaking the law, drank heavily, and appeared unreliable enough that Liu Bi excluded him while appointing other guests to military and administrative roles. Zhou does not demand a command. He asks only for a tally representing Liu Bi's authority and promises a return.

The source then presents Zhou as an unexpectedly effective local operator. He enters Xiapi overnight, falsely presents himself through eastern-imperial authority, has the county chief killed under a fabricated accusation, and uses relationships with local notables and officials plus the threat of an approaching Wu army to obtain surrender. His reported force grows from more than thirty thousand to more than one hundred thousand as he moves north.

That capacity is real in the episode but not independent. When Zhou learns that Liu Bi has fled, he concludes that the larger project and partner needed to sustain his position are gone. He returns to Xiapi and later dies after severe fear and illness. The current synthesis therefore joins underestimated execution ability to dependence on borrowed legitimacy and a wider military cause.

## Key Characteristics

- Xiapi native and fugitive whose drinking and conduct lead Liu Bi to disregard him.
- Retainer who asks for a symbolic authorization instrument rather than a formal command.
- Local operator who combines impersonated authority, coercion, personal ties, and fear to secure Xiapi.
- Rapid mobilizer whose reported force expands far beyond his initial position.
- Actor whose apparent autonomy collapses when Liu Bi's wider rebellion loses military and political viability.

## Qualifications

The page rests on one popular-history episode. Zhou's prior offense, drinking, exact relationship to Liu Bi, tally, impersonation, fabricated accusation, killing, surrender mechanism, force totals, campaign geography, interpretation of patron dependence, illness, and cause of death remain source-scoped. His success demonstrates coercive execution and network use in this telling; it does not make those methods legitimate or show that the force could survive sustained opposition.

## What Changed

- Created Zhou Qiu as a case of underestimated local execution capacity built on borrowed authority.
- Qualified his rapid mobilization by making its dependence on Liu Bi's wider project explicit.
