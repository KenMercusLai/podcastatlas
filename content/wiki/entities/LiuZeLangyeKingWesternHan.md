## Overview

琅邪王刘泽 / Liu Ze is the Western Han prince whom [[LiuXiangQiKingWesternHan|齐王刘襄]] reportedly lures to Linzi and detains before absorbing Langye's troops, after which Liu Ze secures release by presenting himself as a useful senior Liu-family voice in the capital.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-206-zhu-lv-an-liu-shishang-diyi-miemen-an-1-liolyhn4i9_nuhxbm_srpomoxaxb|Hanji 206 part 1]] presents Liu Ze as both a target of Qi's coercive mobilization and an adaptive political actor. [[ZhuWuWesternHan|祝午]] tells him that the young Liu Xiang needs an experienced commander and invites him to Linzi; once he arrives, Liu Xiang detains him and uses Zhu Wu to requisition Langye's forces.

Liu Ze escapes the trap through succession rhetoric. He tells Liu Xiang that the throne should belong to him as Liu Bang's eldest legitimate grandson, but argues that Liu Ze's own senior standing within the Liu house makes him useful in Chang'an, where ministers may seek his judgment. Liu Xiang accepts the argument and sends him onward with an escort. The episode supports the sequence but does not establish whether Liu Ze sincerely backed Liu Xiang, merely improvised for release, or later acted on the promised advocacy.

## Key Characteristics

- Liu-family prince ruling Langye in the episode's succession-crisis setting.
- Victim of a false consultation offer that removes him from his own command base.
- Regional ruler whose troops are absorbed into Liu Xiang's army while he is detained.
- Adaptive negotiator who uses Liu Xiang's imperial ambition as leverage for release.
- Claimed senior royal voice whose expected influence in Chang'an remains unverified here.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to one structured popular-history summary. Liu Ze's exact genealogy, rank seniority, military authority, dialogue, motives, detention conditions, escort, capital role, and later conduct require comparison with transmitted histories. The semantic key distinguishes this Langye king from [[LiuZeQiRoyalWesternHan|刘泽]], the later Qi royal descendant involved in [[LiuDanYanKingWesternHan|燕王刘旦]]'s rebellion.

## What Changed

- Created a disambiguated profile for the Langye king detained during Liu Xiang's mobilization.
- Preserved uncertainty between sincere succession advocacy and tactical self-release.
