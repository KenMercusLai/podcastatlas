## Overview

琅邪王刘泽 / Liu Ze is synthesized as a distant Liu relative who advances through military merit, Lü-family marriage, high office, and [[TianZichunWesternHan|田子春]]'s paid kingship campaign before later appearing as the Langye prince whom [[LiuXiangQiKingWesternHan|齐王刘襄]] detains and dispossesses of his troops.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-202-lishishang-yin-lengbaoli-bei-esi-de-ren-shi-ta-2-lrs7h5hhj0m0oixd6gw8flqpi0nd|Hanji 202 part 2]] backfills Liu Ze's pre-kingship career. It presents him as a distant Liu relative who joins Liu Bang's group, serves under [[FanKuai|樊哙]] during the Chen Xi rebellion, captures [[WangHuang|王黄]], and receives the Yingling marquisate. His marriage to Fan Kuai and [[LuXuWesternHan|吕媭]]'s daughter connects Liu, Lü, and founding-merit networks; the episode also credits him with palace-guard and general commands.

Rank becomes the unresolved ambition in Hanji 202. Although the episode describes a large estate and important offices, Liu Ze wants promotion from marquis to king. He pays Tian to arrange it; after a two-year delay, Tian goes to Chang'an and begins building access through a trusted palace insider's household.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-203-taihuangtaihou-lvzhi-sansha-zhaowang-lqblvnengllzwnfc1yjyy8b9v71g|Hanji 203]] completes that source sequence. Tian first helps channel a petition for [[LuLuWesternHan|吕禄]]'s kingship, then argues that making an older Liu relative king can calm resentment over the Lü grant. Liu Ze's merit, senior kin status, command record, and marriage to Fan Kuai and Lü Xu's daughter make him the proposed counterweight. Lu Zhi reportedly grants him Langye from Qi territory; Tian sends him out of Chang'an before an attempted recall can retrieve the decree.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-206-zhu-lv-an-liu-shishang-diyi-miemen-an-1-liolyhn4i9_nuhxbm_srpomoxaxb|Hanji 206 part 1]] then presents Liu Ze as both a target of Qi's coercive mobilization and an adaptive political actor. [[ZhuWuWesternHan|祝午]] tells him that the young Liu Xiang needs an experienced commander and invites him to Linzi; once he arrives, Liu Xiang detains him and uses Zhu Wu to requisition Langye's forces.

Liu Ze escapes the trap through succession rhetoric. He tells Liu Xiang that the throne should belong to him as Liu Bang's eldest legitimate grandson, but argues that Liu Ze's own senior standing within the Liu house makes him useful in Chang'an, where ministers may seek his judgment. Liu Xiang accepts the argument and sends him onward with an escort. The episode supports the sequence but does not establish whether Liu Ze sincerely backed Liu Xiang, merely improvised for release, or later acted on the promised advocacy.

## Key Characteristics

- Distant Liu relative whose pre-kingship status combines military merit, marquisate, marriage, and office.
- Ambitious marquis whose indirect palace campaign is presented as the cause of his promotion to king of Langye.
- Compensating Liu-family beneficiary used to answer resistance to Lü-family enfeoffment.
- Liu-family prince ruling Langye in the later succession-crisis setting.
- Victim of a false consultation offer that removes him from his command base while Liu Xiang absorbs his troops.
- Adaptive negotiator who uses Liu Xiang's imperial ambition as leverage for release.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to three structured popular-history summaries. Liu Ze's exact genealogy, merit ranking, estate size, offices, marriage, payment, royal-title process, rank seniority, military authority, territorial grant, departure, dialogue, motives, detention conditions, escort, capital role, and later conduct require comparison with transmitted histories. Hanji 203 completes the podcast's own causal story but does not independently verify it. The semantic key distinguishes this Langye king from [[LiuZeQiRoyalWesternHan|刘泽]], the later Qi royal descendant involved in [[LiuDanYanKingWesternHan|燕王刘旦]]'s rebellion.

## What Changed

- Completed the previously unresolved kingship campaign with the episode's compensating-grant logic, Langye decree, departure, and attempted recall.
- Reframed Liu Ze's royal title as a narrated outcome of Tian's brokerage while retaining the source boundary around that causation.
- Preserved uncertainty between sincere succession advocacy and tactical self-release in the later crisis.
