## Overview

琅邪王刘泽 / Liu Ze is synthesized as a distant Liu relative who advances through military merit, Lü-family marriage, high office, and a paid kingship campaign before later appearing as the Langye prince whom [[LiuXiangQiKingWesternHan|齐王刘襄]] detains and dispossesses of his troops.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-202-lishishang-yin-lengbaoli-bei-esi-de-ren-shi-ta-2-lrs7h5hhj0m0oixd6gw8flqpi0nd|Hanji 202 part 2]] backfills Liu Ze's pre-kingship career. It presents him as a distant Liu relative who joins Liu Bang's group, serves under [[FanKuai|樊哙]] during the Chen Xi rebellion, captures [[WangHuang|王黄]], and receives the Yingling marquisate. His marriage to Fan Kuai and [[LuXuWesternHan|吕媭]]'s daughter connects Liu, Lü, and founding-merit networks; the episode also credits him with palace-guard and general commands.

Rank remains the unresolved ambition. Although the episode describes a large estate and important offices, Liu Ze wants promotion from marquis to king. He pays [[TianZichunWesternHan|田子春]] to arrange it; after a two-year delay, Tian goes to Chang'an and begins building access through a trusted chief eunuch's household. The episode stops before the maneuver or grant, so this page does not treat Tian's route as the established cause of Liu Ze's later royal title.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-206-zhu-lv-an-liu-shishang-diyi-miemen-an-1-liolyhn4i9_nuhxbm_srpomoxaxb|Hanji 206 part 1]] then presents Liu Ze as both a target of Qi's coercive mobilization and an adaptive political actor. [[ZhuWuWesternHan|祝午]] tells him that the young Liu Xiang needs an experienced commander and invites him to Linzi; once he arrives, Liu Xiang detains him and uses Zhu Wu to requisition Langye's forces.

Liu Ze escapes the trap through succession rhetoric. He tells Liu Xiang that the throne should belong to him as Liu Bang's eldest legitimate grandson, but argues that Liu Ze's own senior standing within the Liu house makes him useful in Chang'an, where ministers may seek his judgment. Liu Xiang accepts the argument and sends him onward with an escort. The episode supports the sequence but does not establish whether Liu Ze sincerely backed Liu Xiang, merely improvised for release, or later acted on the promised advocacy.

## Key Characteristics

- Distant Liu relative whose pre-kingship status combines military merit, marquisate, marriage, and office.
- Ambitious marquis who pays Tian Zichun to seek promotion to king through an indirect palace route.
- Liu-family prince ruling Langye in the later succession-crisis setting.
- Victim of a false consultation offer that removes him from his own command base.
- Regional ruler whose troops are absorbed into Liu Xiang's army while he is detained.
- Adaptive negotiator who uses Liu Xiang's imperial ambition as leverage for release.
- Claimed senior royal voice whose expected influence in Chang'an remains unverified.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to two structured popular-history summaries. Liu Ze's exact genealogy, merit ranking, estate size, offices, marriage, payment, royal-title process, rank seniority, military authority, dialogue, motives, detention conditions, escort, capital role, and later conduct require comparison with transmitted histories. Hanji 202 stops before Tian's decisive action, so later kingship cannot validate the incomplete causal story. The semantic key distinguishes this Langye king from [[LiuZeQiRoyalWesternHan|刘泽]], the later Qi royal descendant involved in [[LiuDanYanKingWesternHan|燕王刘旦]]'s rebellion.

## What Changed

- Backfilled Liu Ze's reported military merit, marriage network, offices, and unfinished paid kingship campaign.
- Preserved the causal gap between Tian Zichun's access setup and Liu Ze's later royal title.
- Retained uncertainty between sincere succession advocacy and tactical self-release in the later crisis.
