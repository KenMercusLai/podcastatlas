# Flora MacDonald

## Overview

Flora MacDonald appears as the woman who helped [[CharlesEdwardStuart|Charles Edward Stuart]] escape and later became a living bridge between the 1745 rising and [[SamuelJohnson]] and [[JamesBoswell]]’s 1773 Hebridean tour.

## Current Profile

The episode says MacDonald helped the defeated Stuart claimant travel while disguised as her Irish maid. When Johnson and Boswell met her on Skye, Boswell treated Johnson’s greeting with her and Johnson’s sleeping in a bed once used by the prince as a summit of Jacobite romance.

Her role in this source is therefore both historical and memorial. She participated in an escape, but the later travelers also framed her as a surviving emblem inside their literary reconstruction of Jacobite Britain.

## Key Characteristics

- Participant in Charles Edward Stuart’s post-Culloden escape.
- Living carrier of Jacobite memory when Johnson and Boswell visited in 1773.
- Figure through whom Boswell converted political history into a staged literary encounter.
- Source-bounded emblem of the overlap between biography, landscape, and romantic memory.

## Evidence

- Escape role: [[652-londons-golden-age-the-ghosts-of-culloden-part-3-glt2422514848]] describes her helping Charles travel in disguise.
- Later encounter: [[652-londons-golden-age-the-ghosts-of-culloden-part-3-glt2422514848]] records Johnson and Boswell meeting her on Skye.
- Memorial function: [[652-londons-golden-age-the-ghosts-of-culloden-part-3-glt2422514848]] interprets Boswell’s delight as part of the tour’s Jacobite romance.

## Qualifications

The episode provides only a brief account of MacDonald and views the meeting mainly through Boswell. It does not reconstruct her full life, politics, motives, or her own interpretation of the 1773 visitors.

## What Changed

- Created Flora MacDonald as both an escape participant and a living site of Jacobite memory.
