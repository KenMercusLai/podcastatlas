# Marie of France, Countess of Champagne

## Overview

Marie of France, Countess of Champagne, was the aristocratic patron for whom [[ChretienDeTroyes|Chrétien de Troyes]] composed *The Knight of the Cart*, connecting troubadour love culture to Arthurian romance.

## Current Profile

[[714-king-arthur-lancelot-and-the-round-table-part-2-glt6459272632]] places Marie at a dynastic and cultural intersection. As daughter of Louis VII and Eleanor of Aquitaine and granddaughter of troubadour William IX of Aquitaine, she inherited associations with southern French lyric culture while presiding over a northern aristocratic court receptive to Arthurian material.

Her importance in the episode is patronal rather than authorial. Chrétien's commission joins forbidden love, service, secrecy, and inner suffering to the adventures of [[Lancelot]] and [[Guinevere]], making Marie's court a site of cross-Channel literary recombination despite Plantagenet-Capetian rivalry.

## Key Characteristics

- Daughter of Louis VII and Eleanor of Aquitaine.
- Granddaughter of William IX of Aquitaine, the earliest surviving troubadour named by the episode.
- Countess whose court supported influential vernacular romance.
- Patron associated with Chrétien's *Knight of the Cart*.
- Cultural intermediary between troubadour love ideals and Arthurian storytelling.

## Evidence

### Dynastic and cultural position

- [[714-king-arthur-lancelot-and-the-round-table-part-2-glt6459272632]] connects Marie's French royal parentage and Aquitainian troubadour inheritance to the literary culture of her court.

### Patronage

- [[714-king-arthur-lancelot-and-the-round-table-part-2-glt6459272632]] says Chrétien wrote *The Knight of the Cart* for Marie around 1180 and frames the romance as a fusion of Arthurian enthusiasm with fashionable forbidden-love narratives.

### Cross-Channel mediation

- [[714-king-arthur-lancelot-and-the-round-table-part-2-glt6459272632]] places her court within cultural exchange that continued across political rivalry between the French monarchy and the Plantagenet realm.

## Qualifications

The profile is bounded to one popular-history episode and should not turn patronage into sole authorship or proven control over every theme in the romance. The precise commission, Marie's intentions, the organization of her court, and the transmission of troubadour conventions require direct textual and documentary study.

## What Changed

- Established Marie as a patronal intermediary in the episode's account of Arthurian romance and courtly love.
