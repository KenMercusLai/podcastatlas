## Overview

杨阜 / Yang Fu is a Liangzhou-Guanzhong official whose profile now connects strategic assessment before [[GuanduCampaign|官渡之战]] to armed resistance against [[MaChao|马超]] around Ji. He first makes competing regimes legible to western commanders and later becomes a participant whose kin, wounds, and refusal of reward are used to define costly loyalty.

## Current Profile

Hanji 1035 presents Yang Fu as a strategic assessor. After a mission to [[Xudu|许都]], he compares [[YuanShao|袁绍]] and [[CaoCao|曹操]] through decisiveness, legal discipline, army strength, talent policy, and subordinate commitment rather than territory or prestige alone. His report gives Guanzhong commanders a condition-sensitive forecast before Guandu and exemplifies [[XingshiqingStrategicAssessment|行势情战略评估]].

Hanji 1138 adds the later resistance branch. Yang Fu and [[JiangXuLateHan|姜叙]] raise forces against Ma Chao, while [[ZhaoAngLateHan|赵昂]], Zhao Qu, and Liang Kuan participate in the wider Ji-Qishan operation. The episode says seven of Yang Fu's kinsmen die and that he continues after multiple wounds. After [[XiahouYuan|夏侯渊]] helps force Ma Chao toward Hanzhong, Yang Fu reportedly resists a marquisate because the local superior was not saved and Ma Chao was not captured. The durable profile is a shift from judging institutional capacity to accepting personal cost in defense of a political order.

## Key Characteristics

- Cross-regional observer moving between Guanzhong/Liangzhou politics and Cao Cao's Xudu court.
- Evaluates Yuan Shao as lenient but indecisive, with a core team that lacks determination and misses opportunities.
- Evaluates Cao Cao as decisive, law-disciplined, militarily strong, open in talent use, and able to secure full subordinate effort.
- Makes Cao-Yuan rivalry legible to Guanzhong commanders who are waiting to see which side will prevail.
- Helps initiate armed resistance to Ma Chao around Ji and Qishan.
- Is portrayed as continuing after severe kin losses and personal wounds, then treating incomplete success as a reason to refuse reward.

## Qualifications

The current wiki evidence consists of two popular-history summaries rather than a full biography or primary-source collation. Wei Duan is named as the sender in Hanji 1035, but the wiki does not yet require a separate page from that evidence alone. Hanji 1138's exact uprising sequence, wound and kin counts, dialogue, enfeoffment, reasons for refusal, and long career require broader verification. The source's praise of loyalty does not by itself settle the wider moral cost of the revolt or family retaliation.

## What Changed

- Extended Yang Fu from pre-Guandu strategic assessor to participant in the Ji resistance against Ma Chao.
- Added reported kin loss, wounds, and reward refusal as source-scoped evidence of his costly loyalty standard.
