## Overview

杨阜 / Yang Fu is a Liangzhou-Guanzhong official whose profile connects strategic assessment before [[GuanduCampaign|官渡之战]] to covert organization and armed resistance against [[MaChao|马超]] around Ji. He first makes competing regimes legible to western commanders and later turns funeral leave, kinship access, outside forces, and city insiders into a revolt whose personal costs define the source's ideal of loyalty.

## Current Profile

Hanji 1035 presents Yang Fu as a strategic assessor. After a mission to [[Xudu|许都]], he compares [[YuanShao|袁绍]] and [[CaoCao|曹操]] through decisiveness, legal discipline, army strength, talent policy, and subordinate commitment rather than territory or prestige alone. His report gives Guanzhong commanders a condition-sensitive forecast before Guandu and exemplifies [[XingshiqingStrategicAssessment|行势情战略评估]].

Hanji 1135 places Yang Fu before the siege as a frontier-risk forecaster. When Cao Cao abandons the pursuit of Ma Chao to suppress the Hejian rebellion, Yang Fu warns that Ma Chao's combat ability and standing among Qiang and Hu groups will expose the territories west of Longshan unless Liangzhou defenses are strengthened. Ma Chao's recovery and siege of Ji give the warning retrospective weight, while Cao Cao's center-first choice shows that accurate advice can still be rejected when another risk ranks higher.

Hanji 1136 then places Yang Fu inside the defense of Ji. The episode says [[WeiKangLateHan|韦康]] retained him after succeeding his father as Liangzhou inspector, despite Cao Cao's earlier interest in recruiting him. After [[YanWenLateHan|阎温]]'s relief mission fails and the city loses hope, Yang Fu argues against surrender and frames continued defense as a shared obligation to Liangzhou's people. Wei Kang nevertheless opens the city, so Yang Fu's later conspiracy begins as the continuation of two rejected judgments: strengthen Liangzhou before withdrawal, then continue defending Ji.

Hanji 1137 supplies the resistance's formation. After Ma Chao reportedly kills the surrendered inspector [[WeiKangLateHan|韦康]], Yang Fu outwardly serves the new regime while waiting for an opportunity. His wife's death gives him leave to return for burial, where he recruits his maternal cousin [[JiangXuLateHan|姜叙]]; Jiang Xu's mother supports action, and the plan connects Zhao Ang, Yin Feng, and Li Jun outside Ji with Zhao Qu and Liang Kuan inside. The source therefore presents Yang Fu as a coalition organizer rather than only a battlefield loyalist.

Hanji 1138 supplies the execution and cost. Yang Fu and Jiang Xu raise forces against Ma Chao, while [[ZhaoAngLateHan|赵昂]], Zhao Qu, and Liang Kuan participate in the Ji-Qishan operation. The episode says seven of Yang Fu's kinsmen die and that he continues after multiple wounds. After [[XiahouYuan|夏侯渊]] helps force Ma Chao toward Hanzhong, Yang Fu reportedly resists a marquisate because the local superior was not saved and Ma Chao was not captured. The durable profile is a shift from judging institutional capacity to building and personally bearing the cost of resistance.

## Key Characteristics

- Cross-regional observer moving between Guanzhong/Liangzhou politics and Cao Cao's Xudu court.
- Contrasts Yuan Shao's hesitation and missed opportunities with Cao Cao's decisiveness, legal discipline, military strength, talent use, and committed subordinates.
- Makes Cao-Yuan rivalry legible to Guanzhong commanders who are waiting to see which side will prevail.
- Warns that Cao Cao's withdrawal without stronger Liangzhou defense will let Ma Chao rebuild through local military standing and support.
- Opposes surrender at Ji, then uses nominal submission, funeral leave, kinship access, and inside-outside coordination to prepare resistance after Wei Kang's death.
- Helps initiate armed resistance to Ma Chao around Ji and Qishan.
- Is portrayed as continuing after severe kin losses and personal wounds, then treating incomplete success as a reason to refuse reward.

## Qualifications

The current wiki evidence consists of five popular-history summaries rather than a full biography or primary-source collation. Wei Duan is named as the sender in Hanji 1035, but the wiki does not yet require a separate page from that evidence alone. Hanji 1135's office, warning language, Qiang-Hu generalization, defense counterfactual, and causal link to Ma Chao's recovery, Hanji 1136's succession account, Cao-side recruitment interest, dialogue, and defense rationale, Hanji 1137's kinship, funeral timing, conspiracy membership, and motive, and Hanji 1138's exact uprising sequence, wound and kin counts, enfeoffment, and reward refusal require broader verification. Praise of loyalty does not settle the wider moral cost of continued siege, revolt, or family retaliation.

## What Changed

- Backfilled Yang Fu's warning before Cao Cao withdrew from the western pursuit.
- Extended his profile from comparative assessment into frontier-risk forecasting.
- Reframed the later resistance as continuity with two rejected judgments rather than only revenge after Wei Kang's death.
- Preserved the distinction between an accurate warning and Cao Cao's competing center-security priority.
