# David Rizzio

## Overview

David Rizzio was [[MaryQueenOfScots|Mary, Queen of Scots]]' Italian secretary and musician whose foreignness, Catholicism, court access, and unpopularity made him the chosen target of a noble coup.

## Current Profile

Rizzio rose from valet and musician to Mary's private secretary. The episode describes him as witty and companionable but also ostentatious, venal, administratively weak, and resented by nobles who regarded a foreign servant's influence as an affront to rank.

Those traits made him useful as a scapegoat when [[HenryStuartLordDarnley|Darnley]] abandoned Catholic-restoration rhetoric and allied with [[JamesStewartEarlOfMoray|Moray]]'s exiles. Claims that Rizzio controlled Mary or fathered her child converted a struggle over Darnley's authority, noble restoration, and religion into an attack on a vulnerable court favorite. The episode finds no real evidence of a sexual relationship.

On 9 March 1566, armed conspirators entered Mary's Holyrood rooms, dragged Rizzio from her supper, and stabbed him repeatedly. His killing was therefore not an isolated court murder but the violent mechanism of an attempted transfer of power.

## Key Characteristics

- Italian Catholic courtier whose music, wit, and manners won Mary's company.
- Social outsider whose rapid promotion offended aristocratic hierarchy.
- Secretary criticized by the source for incompetence, venality, and display.
- Scapegoat for Darnley's marital humiliation and the lords' fear of Catholic influence.
- Murder victim whose alleged affair with Mary is unsupported in the episode.

## Qualifications

The source's judgments about Rizzio's competence, corruption, appearance, and manners are narrative assessments rather than a complete independent profile. The sexual allegation is treated as politically useful rumor, not established fact, and the exact number of wounds remains source-reported.

## What Changed

- Established Rizzio as a politically selected scapegoat whose murder served a wider coup against Mary.
