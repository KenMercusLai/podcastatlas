# Austria

## Overview

Austria appears in the wiki as the former Habsburg cultural center remembered by [[StefanZweig|Stefan Zweig]] and as the small interwar state that [[NaziGermany|Nazi Germany]] coerced, invaded, absorbed, and plundered in the 1938 [[Anschluss]].

## Current Profile

The Zweig source treats Austria, especially [[Vienna]], as a memory structure for educated Jewish cosmopolitan life before war, nationalism, border hardening, and exile. That world was real but class-bounded: wealth, language, education, and cultural networks gave some residents a mobility unavailable to most Europeans.

The Anschluss source begins after the Habsburg collapse, when the Allies prevented German-speaking Austria from joining Germany. Interwar Austria was divided among socialist Vienna, Catholic authoritarianism, pan-German sentiment, and an Austrian Nazi movement. [[EngelbertDollfuss]] and [[KurtVonSchuschnigg]] opposed Nazi takeover without governing as liberal democrats.

In March 1938, Hitler used political pressure, ultimatums, Austrian Nazi action, and threatened invasion to eliminate Austrian independence. German troops encountered substantial enthusiasm, yet the state was not joining Germany as an equal partner: its institutions, army, banks, reserves, infrastructure, art, and resources were subordinated or seized. Jews and opponents faced immediate detention, dispossession, humiliation, and flight pressure. This dual record grounds the qualified [[AustrianFirstVictimNarrative|first-victim narrative]].

## Key Characteristics

- Former imperial and Viennese cultural setting for a socially bounded cosmopolitan Europe.
- Small post-1918 state whose desired union with Germany was initially blocked by the peace settlement.
- Politically divided society containing socialist, Catholic-authoritarian, pan-German, Nazi, and independence currents.
- State coerced and erased through the 1938 Anschluss rather than admitted as an equal partner.
- Society in which enthusiastic reception and local Nazi participation coexisted with coercion, victimization, and resistance.
- Site of immediate anti-Jewish violence, dispossession, detention, and centralized forced-emigration administration.

## Qualifications

The sources do not provide a comprehensive national history. The Anschluss evidence rejects both a uniformly resistant Austria and a freely unanimous union: official plebiscite results were produced under dictatorship, while crowd enthusiasm cannot establish every person's motive. The Belle Epoque memory is also socially selective and should not be generalized to all Austrians.
