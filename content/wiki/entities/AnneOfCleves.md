# Anne of Cleves

## Overview

Anne of Cleves was Henry VIII's fourth wife, chosen for a Protestant-leaning German alliance and able to convert a rejected political marriage into a comparatively secure settlement in England.

## Current Profile

The episode places [[ThomasCromwell|Thomas Cromwell]] behind the diplomatic search that led to Anne and treats Hans Holbein's portrait as part of the court's pre-marital decision process. Henry's disappointment after the Rochester encounter and his claim that the marriage could not be consummated rapidly undermined the match.

Anne's acceptance of annulment distinguishes her outcome from the resistance and lethal conflict surrounding other marriages. She received property, remained in England, and outlived the other five wives, making accommodation a form of survival within severe power asymmetry rather than evidence that the original match was inconsequential.

## Key Characteristics

- German dynastic bride selected for an international religious alliance.
- Known to Henry partly through mediated portraiture before personal meeting.
- Rejected after a failed encounter and disputed consummation.
- Accepted annulment and negotiated a materially favorable English settlement.
- Outlived Henry's other wives and avoided the lethal consequences of resistance.

## Qualifications

The episode's comic treatment of appearance and the Rochester disguise should not be treated as neutral proof of Anne's attractiveness, Henry's motives, or the marriage's legal facts. Her acceptance of settlement occurred under royal power and should not be romanticized as unconstrained bargaining.

## What Changed

- Established Anne's marriage as a failed alliance project followed by successful risk management.
- Reframed the “divorced” mnemonic outcome as negotiated survival under unequal power.
