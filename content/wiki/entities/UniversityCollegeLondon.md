# University College London

## Overview

University College London appears as a research institution connecting comparative urban-crime methodology with materials work on highly reflective passive-cooling coatings.

## Current Profile

The crime source uses [[MattAshby]] and colleagues' cross-city dataset to move safety claims away from viral anecdotes and toward harmonized boundaries and definitions, while retaining the limits of any international comparison.

A separate materials branch attributes to UCL a coating made from silica-aerogel beads and polymer that reportedly reflects about 94% of sunlight while scattering light diffusely rather than behaving like a mirror. The proposed application to [[TransportForLondon]] buses places the institution between laboratory performance and real urban deployment.

## Key Characteristics

- Research institution represented across distinct urban-data and materials-science domains.
- Institutional home for work on more comparable large-city crime data.
- Source-attributed developer of a highly reflective silica-aerogel and polymer coating.
- Bridge between laboratory research and public-system testing through London buses.
- Evidence anchor whose findings retain methodological and deployment qualifications.

## Qualifications

The sources cover two research examples rather than the institution as a whole. Crime comparisons remain imperfect because reporting, definitions, and boundaries vary. The coating's reflectivity, below-ambient performance, cost, durability, safety, and fleet-scale status remain episode-reported and context-dependent.
