## Overview

The 第二次鸦片战争 / Second Opium War (1856–1860) was a conflict in which Britain and France used military force to expand treaty access and diplomatic claims against the Qing; this source approaches its Guangzhou phase through [[YeMingchen|Ye Mingchen]] and the contested Arrow incident.

## Current Profile

The episode presents the war as both coercive imperial escalation and Qing strategic failure. British consul Harry Parkes used the detention of Chinese sailors from the Arrow—whose British registration had reportedly expired—as grounds for demands, while entry into Guangzhou and treaty revision carried a longer history of unresolved pressure. Ye released or transferred personnel but would not concede entry.

Initial British withdrawal reinforced Ye's belief that firmness worked. When British and French forces returned, Qing troops were dispersed by internal rebellions, local officials were divided, and Guangzhou's population and order collapsed under bombardment. The city's fall and Ye's capture therefore cannot be explained either by his character alone or by treating the disputed incident as a neutral legal trigger.

## Key Characteristics

- Treaty-era imperial war driven by demands extending beyond the immediate Arrow dispute.
- Conflict connected to the simultaneous Indian rebellion through British troop allocation and coalition building.
- Guangzhou campaign shaped by Qing internal wars, dispersed troops, local division, and military imbalance.
- Case in which a limited earlier retreat produced misleading confidence about later escalation.
- Political crisis followed by court blame shifting toward the captured local official.

## Qualifications

This is a Guangzhou-centered account, not a complete history of the 1856–1860 war. It does not fully cover northern campaigns, treaty negotiations, France's independent motives, other Qing officials, allied military operations, or civilian harm. Legal and factual claims about the Arrow remain episode-attributed.
