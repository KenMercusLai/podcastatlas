## Overview

窦太后 / Empress Dowager Dou now has a fuller pre-dowager Zhangdi layer before the existing He-era regency layers. Hanji 751 shows her as the childless empress who raises [[EmperorHeOfHan|Liu Zhao]], attacks [[SongGuirenZhangHeLateHan|Song Guiren]] and [[LiuQingQingheKing|Liu Qing]], and makes Liu Zhao's crown-prince path possible. Hanji 752 then shows her treating [[LiangGuirenHeLateHan|Liang Guiren]]'s birth-mother line as a threat to Dou-family maternal status. Hanji 759-761 show her as the living dowager whose authority enables [[DouXianEasternHan|Dou Xian]] and the Dou brothers. Hanji 770 shows her posthumous status challenged after the hidden Liang birth-mother case is revealed.

## Current Profile

Hanji 751 gives the first origin of the later legitimacy problem. Empress Dou has no son, raises Liu Zhao as her own, and therefore has a direct interest in replacing Liu Qing as crown prince. The source says she works through her mother and family to collect Song-family faults, monitor the Song sisters, and transform Song Guiren's request for rabbit meat during illness into a curse accusation. Zhangdi accepts the charge, Liu Qing is demoted to Qinghe king, Liu Zhao becomes crown prince, and the Song sisters die after examination by [[CaiLunLateHan|Cai Lun]]. This makes Dou's adoptive-mother status start through successful palace accusation, not only later regency power.

Hanji 752 gives the second Zhangdi-era layer. Liu Zhao's elevation makes Liang Guiren's family politically meaningful because Liang is the birth mother of the child Dou has raised. The source links Dou jealousy and the accusation route against Liang Song to imprisonment, exile of Liang relatives to Jiuzhen, and the grief deaths of Liang Guiren and her sister. This makes Dou's later adoptive-mother status rest beside a suppressed birth-mother line rather than on uncontested household order.

Hanji 759 presents the first living-dowager layer. After Emperor Zhang dies, Emperor He is a child ruler and Dou governs from the empress-dowager position. Her authority lets Dou Xian and the Dou brothers stand close to the emperor and dowager, and the court receives the Southern Xiongnu request to attack [[NorthernXiongnuLateHan|Northern Xiongnu]]. She consults [[GengBingEasternHan|Geng Bing]], who supports the campaign idea.

Hanji 760-761 present the living dowager as both ruler-adjacent authority and family protector. Her relationship with [[LiuChangDuxiangHouEasternHan|Duxiang marquis Liu Chang]] creates the prelude to Dou Xian's murder of Liu Chang; when the case points to Dou, she confines him but accepts his request to attack Northern Xiongnu as a substitute for ordinary punishment. She then ignores anti-campaign remonstrance, orders mansions for [[DouDuEasternHan|Dou Du]] and [[DouJingEasternHan|Dou Jing]], and favors Dou in the [[ZhiShouEasternHan|Zhi Shou]] case before [[HeChangEasternHan|He Chang]] secures mitigation.

Hanji 770 begins after Dou Taihou's death in 97 CE. Liang relatives reveal that Liang Guiren, Emperor He's birth mother, had not received proper recognition. Senior officials propose stripping Dou's dowager title and burial pairing with Emperor Zhang, but Emperor He refuses because he had served her as mother for ten years. Her current profile is therefore ambivalent: she is an active suppressor and Dou-family enabler, but she remains ritually protected as the emperor's adoptive mother.

## Key Characteristics

- Childless Zhangdi-era empress who builds a foster-maternal claim by raising Liu Zhao.
- Palace accuser whose campaign removes Song Guiren and Liu Qing from the crown-prince line.
- Zhangdi-era empress whose maternal-status anxiety helps suppress Liang Guiren's birth-mother line.
- Emperor He's adoptive mother and formal empress dowager whose authority opens Dou-family access around a child emperor.
- Living authority whose relationship with Liu Chang creates the murder-case prelude to Dou Xian's campaign.
- Family protector who lets Dou Xian seek military redemption instead of ordinary punishment.
- Retains dowager dignity because Emperor He preserves the adoptive mother-son relationship and burial order.

## Qualifications

This page remains bounded to Hanji 751, Hanji 752, Hanji 759, Hanji 760, Hanji 761, and Hanji 770 and does not reconstruct Dou Taihou's full life, independent defense, or every regency decision. Hanji 751 and Hanji 752 are pre-dowager empress layers; the page keeps them here because they explain the later adoptive-mother and birth-mother conflict. Hanji 770 gives a posthumous status and victim-redress layer. The page treats those layers as sequentially compatible rather than contradictory.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 751 as the earliest pre-dowager layer: childlessness, Liu Zhao adoption, Song Guiren accusation, Liu Qing deposition, and Song-sister deaths.
- Reframed Hanji 752's Liang suppression as the second stage of Dou's maternal-status problem after Liu Zhao has already displaced Liu Qing.
- Reframed Hanji 770's posthumous revelation as the delayed exposure of Zhangdi-era conflicts, not only a late He-era surprise.
- Preserved her living-dowager profile around Dou-family access, Liu Chang, campaign authorization, mansion construction, and Zhi Shou.
