# Neanderthals

## Overview

Neanderthals were an extinct human lineage that occupied Europe and parts of western Asia until roughly 40,000 years ago. [[127-neanderthals-glt5202048433]] treats them as a distinct form of humanity whose material, social, and genetic history cannot be reduced to a primitive stage on the way to Homo sapiens.

## Current Profile

The source presents a cold-adapted, heavily built population living in generally small and genetically vulnerable groups. Neanderthals made prepared-core tools, worked hides, used pigments and ornaments, entered deep caves with fire or lighting, cared for severely impaired individuals, and intentionally buried at least some dead. Those practices support [[NeanderthalBehavioralComplexity]], though they do not disclose a complete language, religion, political organization, or symbolic system.

Their biological disappearance was not total erasure. Repeated contact and [[ArchaicHumanInterbreeding]] left Neanderthal ancestry in most present-day populations outside Africa. Their extinction is therefore best understood through [[NeanderthalExtinctionCausalPluralism]]: climate instability, low numbers and diversity, competition, pathogen exchange, demographic absorption, and chance may have combined rather than one superior population simply exterminating another.

## Key Characteristics

- Distinct human lineage closely related to Homo sapiens and capable of interbreeding with it.
- Cold-adapted body form described as short, broad, muscular, and powerful relative to average recent Europeans.
- Technologically capable makers of prepared-core tools who also worked skins and likely used clothing and shelters.
- Social beings who probably used language, transmitted culture, cared for vulnerable members, and buried some dead.
- Users of pigments, animal teeth, shells, and raptor talons whose precise symbolic meanings remain uncertain.
- Small, dispersed, and genetically low-diversity populations vulnerable to environmental and demographic shocks.

## Evidence

### Bodies, technology, and adaptation

- [[127-neanderthals-glt5202048433]] describes cold-adapted anatomy, Levallois tool production, hide-working traces, likely clothing and shelter, and the illuminated deep-cave structures at Bruniquel.

### Social and symbolic behavior

- [[127-neanderthals-glt5202048433]] connects intentional burials, long-term care of the disabled Shanidar 1 individual, pigments, ornaments, and probable speech to a substantially narrowed behavioral gap with Homo sapiens.

### Population history

- [[127-neanderthals-glt5202048433]] reports low genetic variation, repeated climate shifts, long contact with modern humans, and surviving Neanderthal ancestry as evidence for demographic vulnerability plus partial absorption.

## Qualifications

The profile is bounded to one public-history interview and does not independently adjudicate the full paleoanthropological literature. Species rank remains debated; probable language is not a recovered language; burial does not prove an afterlife belief; pigments and structures do not prove ritual; disputed dates and cave markings require continued scrutiny. Cannibalism, violence, friendly exchange, capture, adoption, sex-biased gene flow, disease effects, and every proposed extinction mechanism remain unevenly evidenced.

## What Changed

- Established a synthesis-first profile replacing the primitive-predecessor image with a distinct, behaviorally complex human lineage.
- Joined physical extinction to genetic survival through interbreeding.
- Framed disappearance as a multicausal demographic process rather than a settled conquest story.
