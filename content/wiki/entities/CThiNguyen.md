# C. Thi Nguyen

## Overview
C. Thi Nguyen is the philosopher interviewed in [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-6abee3c124a8134e46fbc03d3d8edac7-63678825-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-6abee3c124a8134e46fbc03d3d8edac7-63678825]] about games, play, metrics, and the danger of letting portable scores replace context-sensitive values.

## Current Profile
Nguyen presents play as an intrinsic component of flourishing rather than a productivity tool. Drawing on Bernard Suits, he treats games as voluntary acceptance of unnecessary obstacles and adds a focus on the chosen quality of action: people can select the kind of concentration, puzzle, movement, or social difficulty they want to inhabit. His complementary critique of metrics is not anti-data; it asks institutions and individuals to remember what a score deletes and to preserve judgment about whether the scored activity remains worth pursuing.

## Key Characteristics
- Connects the philosophy of games to agency, attention, aesthetics, and human flourishing.
- Distinguishes voluntary and revisable game scoring from centralized institutional measurement.
- Names [[MetricValueCapture|value capture]] as the internal replacement of rich values by simple proxies.
- Defends qualitative judgment and plural value without denying the practical or lifesaving uses of data.
- Uses games and playfulness as models for moving among perspectives and reassessing rules.
