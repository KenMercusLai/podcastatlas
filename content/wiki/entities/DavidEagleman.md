# David Eagleman

## Overview
David Eagleman is a neuroscientist, author, entrepreneur, and science communicator whose work in the source spans brain plasticity, sensory substitution, time perception, memory, dreaming, and social identity.

## Current Profile
In the bounded evidence, Eagleman works by linking neuroscience experiments to everyday design problems: how people learn, precommit against temptation, interpret unusual sensory channels, remember high-stress events, and respond to group labels. He presents the brain as an adaptive model-building system while repeatedly distinguishing flexibility from guaranteed improvement and low-level neural response from inevitable behavior.

## Key Characteristics
- Explains plasticity through cortical competition, practice, novelty, attention, and goal-directed adaptation.
- Translates future-self conflict into practical [[UlyssesContract|commitment devices]].
- Connects [[SensorySubstitution]] research to [[Neosensory]] and the possibility of adding new information channels.
- Uses experiments on freefall and eyewitness memory to separate lived certainty from accurate mechanism or recall.
- Treats dreams, imagery, and group identity as testable but still qualified neuroscience problems.
