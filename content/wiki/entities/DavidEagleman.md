# David Eagleman

## Overview
David Eagleman is a neuroscientist, author, entrepreneur, and science communicator whose bounded profile spans brain plasticity, sensory substitution, time, memory, dreams, group identity, animal sensory worlds, and cross-species translation.

## Current Profile
Across the complete source set, Eagleman presents brains as adaptive model-building systems whose perception is constrained by available sensors but can learn structured information through new channels. His Huberman Lab interview connects that plasticity to learning, imagery, memory, dreams, and social identity. Entangled Intelligences extends the same logic beyond humans: every species inhabits an Umwelt sampled from a wider environment, so AI-mediated translation may decode signals while still failing to reproduce another being's sensory and conceptual world.

## Key Characteristics
- Explains plasticity through cortical competition, practice, novelty, attention, and goal-directed adaptation.
- Connects [[SensorySubstitution]] and [[Neosensory]] to learned information channels beyond familiar senses.
- Uses timing and eyewitness examples to separate lived certainty from accurate mechanism or recall.
- Treats dreams, imagery, and group identity as testable but qualified neuroscience problems.
- Uses Umwelt differences to bound claims about animal translation and shared experience.

## Qualifications
The sources are broad public-science summaries rather than systematic reviews. Dream-defense theory, remapping timescales, sensory phenomenology, empathy imaging, animal translation, and genetic uplift remain hypothesis-, experiment-, forecast-, or source-scoped. Partial signal decoding should not be treated as complete access to another species' experience.

- Added Umwelt as a boundary on claims of shared meaning and experience.
