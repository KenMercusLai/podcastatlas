# Miletus

## Overview

Miletus appears in the wiki as the wealthy Ionian city whose elite instability, failed expansion politics, and destruction anchor the [[IonianRevolt]].

## Current Profile

The episode presents Miletus as rich, sophisticated, and intellectually important, but politically fragile under Persian-backed tyranny. [[Histias]] is removed to Darius's court, [[Aristagoras]] takes over, and local social pressure plus the failed Naxos expedition make revolt a way out of immediate danger.

Miletus also becomes the revolt's trauma point. After the Ionian fleet is destroyed near Lade in 494 BC, the city is stormed and its people are killed, violated, enslaved, castrated, or deported in the episode's account. A later Athenian tragedy about the city's fall is said to upset the audience so deeply that the playwright is fined.

## Key Characteristics

- Miletus is treated as the leading Ionian city in wealth, sophistication, and intellectual importance.
- Persian-backed tyranny makes its local politics dependent on imperial favor.
- Aristagoras's failed Naxos scheme turns Miletus into the revolt's launch point.
- The city's destruction becomes a warning to Athens about Persian punishment.
- Athenian emotional reaction to Miletus's fall links Ionian suffering to mainland Greek fear.
