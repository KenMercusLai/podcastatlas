# Qunar

## Overview
Qunar is a Chinese travel-search and booking platform represented in the wiki through its historical competition with [[Ctrip]] and a newer holiday-travel report on [[MultiCityItineraryTravel|multi-city itinerary travel]].

## Current Profile
The two OTA-history sources describe Qunar as a search- and price-comparison challenger whose aggregation, advertising, transaction, mobile, and public comparison positioning pressured Ctrip before the 2015 capital integration. This makes Qunar evidence that online-travel competition developed through several models rather than one booking template.

The newest source uses Qunar as a market-observation surface rather than retelling its ownership history. Its reported hotel-order data show more bookings spanning two or more cities during the National Day period, illustrating how OTA transaction data can reveal itinerary changes as transport connectivity and traveler preferences shift.

## Key Characteristics
- Originated as a travel-search and price-comparison competitor.
- Pressured Ctrip through public comparison, mobile traffic, and alternative discovery.
- Later entered the capital-integration history behind [[OTAPlatformConcentration]].
- Produces booking data that can reveal emerging travel patterns.

## Evidence
- **Competition model:** [[kunzai-xitong-li-de-jiudian-ni-buzhidao-de-xiecheng-longduan-lianchengshi-keji-luandun]] distinguishes Qunar's comparison-search route from other OTA models.
- **Mobile pressure and consolidation:** [[ep91-dingfang-dingpiao-dingjiangshan-xiecheng-51-yi-wei-aoman-maidan-lovhfkz4rklv1ik-uqyeswrdf3uw]] uses Qunar and [[ZhuangChenchao]] to show how mobile downloads and public price comparison challenged Ctrip before capital integration.
- **Travel-pattern observation:** [[guoqing-jiaqi-duocheng-chuanyou-redu-pansheng-xingbake-yin-wutang-yinliao-zhong-hantang-bei-qisu-1021649123]] attributes a greater-than-30% year-over-year increase in two-or-more-city hotel orders to a Qunar report.

## Qualifications
The newest source does not describe the booking-data methodology, absolute order volume, cancellation treatment, geographic mix, or whether observed customers are representative of all holiday travelers. Historical competition and current reporting roles should not be treated as one unchanged business model.
