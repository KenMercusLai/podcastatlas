# Cable Sasser

## Overview

Cable Sasser is the CEO of [[PanicSoftware|Panic]] featured in [[should-we-all-be-getting-tariff-refunds-right-now]].

## Current Profile

Sasser describes how the small software company absorbed initial [[Playdate]] tariffs, later itemized the charge for customers, and then chose to return traceable customer payments after recovering the invalidated duties. He frames the decision as a collective expression of company identity despite the meaningful financial value of retaining the money.

## Key Characteristics

- Leader of a small company that moved from software into imported hardware.
- Advocate of transparent tariff itemization when the company could no longer absorb the charge.
- Participant in a staff-level decision to return identifiable customer tariff payments.
- Treats customer trust and organizational identity as part of the refund decision.

## Evidence

- **Importer constraint:** [[should-we-all-be-getting-tariff-refunds-right-now]] attributes the initial absorption and later pass-through account to Sasser and Panic's small-company economics.
- **Refund choice:** [[should-we-all-be-getting-tariff-refunds-right-now]] reports that Sasser and staff concluded the customer-related refund money should be returned.
- **Values claim:** [[should-we-all-be-getting-tariff-refunds-right-now]] records his view that keeping the money would have mattered financially but would not have matched what the company believed was right.

## Qualifications

The source reports Sasser's account and the successful customer refund case, but does not independently audit Panic's tariff totals, internal deliberation, or the long-term commercial effect of the decision.

## What Changed

- Established Sasser's role in Panic's tariff itemization and consumer-refund policy.
