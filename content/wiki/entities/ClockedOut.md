# Clocked Out

## Overview
Clocked Out is a Marketplace reporting series about Americans who have left the workforce because they wanted to or because circumstances required it.

## Current Profile
The two represented [[MarketplaceTech]] episodes make broad workforce-exit questions concrete through personal cases. [[ChrisBell]] leaves technology management after recurring layoff responsibility and defines retirement as control over future work; [[DrewHaven]] and [[LindsayHaven]] arrive outside paid employment through cultural disillusionment, health, education, caregiving, family change, and a household decision to define enough. Together, the cases treat exit as a revisable life arrangement rather than a single moment or a guarantee of permanent nonwork.

## Key Characteristics
- Covers voluntary and involuntary exits from the American workforce.
- Uses individual stories to connect labor statistics with lived career and household decisions.
- Includes technology work as one branch rather than defining workforce exit as a technology-only phenomenon.
- Keeps post-exit identity, community, caregiving, health, and financial tradeoffs visible alongside the decision to leave.
- Extends across Marketplace's morning and afternoon programming according to the launch episode.

- Technology exit cases - [[tech-20260907-0907-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260907-0907-mp-tech-pod-128]] follows Chris Bell's early retirement after a technology design and management career; [[tech-20261109-1109-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261109-1109-mp-tech-pod-128]] follows Drew and Lindsay Haven after different departures from technology work converge into a shared job-free household.
- Life after employment - [[tech-20261109-1109-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261109-1109-mp-tech-pod-128]] describes caregiving, identity pressure, community rebuilding, lower consumption, and transport constraints after paid work stops organizing daily life.

## Qualifications
Only two short personal cases are represented here. The launch episode's broad claim that labor-force participation had fallen sharply is not accompanied by the underlying measure, comparison window, or demographic decomposition. Neither case establishes how common, sustainable, or accessible voluntary workforce exit is across households.

## What Changed
- Expanded the series from one early-retirement case to a couple whose exits involve caregiving, health, identity, community, and lower consumption.
- Clarified that the represented cases are revisable household arrangements rather than evidence of permanent withdrawal or broad prevalence.
