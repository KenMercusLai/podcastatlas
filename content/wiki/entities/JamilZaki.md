# Jamil Zaki

## Overview
Jamil Zaki is the Stanford psychologist and Social Neuroscience Laboratory director interviewed by [[AndrewHuberman]] about cynicism, trust, social inference, connection, workplace collaboration, media negativity, and political division.

## Current Profile
The source presents Zaki as a social psychologist who distinguishes emotional pessimism from an evidence-updating method. His central framework, [[CynicismAndHopefulSkepticism]], treats cynicism as a fixed theory that people are fundamentally selfish, greedy, and dishonest, while hopeful skepticism asks what the available evidence actually supports.

Zaki's practical contribution is experimental rather than exhortative. He recommends testing guarded forecasts through bounded social risks, noticing ordinary cooperation, documenting encounters that violate negative expectations, and sharing core beliefs that people may wrongly assume are rare. He extends this approach from personal relationships to workplaces, media perception, and political dialogue without claiming that danger, exploitation, extremism, or disagreement are unreal.

## Key Characteristics
- Defines cynicism as a rigid theory of human motives rather than merely a bad mood.
- Uses social and behavioral evidence to show how pessimistic forecasts can block corrective experience.
- Treats trust as relational and reciprocal rather than as an all-or-nothing personality virtue.
- Connects media negativity and outrage amplification to distorted beliefs about representative human behavior.
- Applies belief correction to political rivals while preserving real disagreement and threat boundaries.
- Offers encounter counting, social savoring, and calculated social experiments as practical updating tools.
