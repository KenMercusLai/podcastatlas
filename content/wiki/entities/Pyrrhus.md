# Pyrrhus of Epirus / 伊庇鲁斯的皮洛士

## Overview

Pyrrhus of Epirus / 伊庇鲁斯的皮洛士 is the Hellenistic king invited by Tarentum to resist the [[RomanRepublic]], whose expensive battlefield successes give the "Pyrrhic victory" its name but fail to stop Roman control of Italy.

## Current Profile

Pyrrhus enters southern Italy in 280 BC with cavalry, a Macedonian-style phalanx, and war elephants, representing the most sophisticated Hellenistic army Rome had yet faced. He defeats Roman forces at Heraclea and again in 279 BC, but the losses are difficult for his expeditionary force to replace while Rome can mobilize another army.

Unable to compel a settlement, Pyrrhus moves to Sicily to fight [[Carthage]], returns to Italy, and ultimately withdraws. His campaign therefore tests rather than overturns Rome's [[RomanCivicMilitaryIncorporation|civic-military incorporation]] model. The episode uses his reported description of Sicily as a future battleground to connect the end of Greek resistance in Italy to the [[FirstPunicWar]].

## Key Characteristics

- Hellenistic king and expeditionary commander invited into Italy by Tarentum.
- Brings phalanx warfare, cavalry, and elephants against Rome.
- Wins battles while suffering losses his force cannot readily replace.
- Fights both Rome in Italy and Carthage in Sicily.
- Leaves without preventing Roman domination of southern Italy.

## Evidence

- Italian intervention: [[423-carthage-vs-rome-the-wolf-at-the-gates-part-3-glt2669872613]] describes Tarentum's appeal and Pyrrhus's 280 BC crossing with a Hellenistic combined-arms force.
- Costly victories: [[423-carthage-vs-rome-the-wolf-at-the-gates-part-3-glt2669872613]] connects Heraclea and the 279 BC victory to heavy losses and Rome's continued mobilization.
- Sicily and withdrawal: [[423-carthage-vs-rome-the-wolf-at-the-gates-part-3-glt2669872613]] follows Pyrrhus from Sicily back to Italy and then out of the war after he fails to break Rome.
- Strategic transition: [[423-carthage-vs-rome-the-wolf-at-the-gates-part-3-glt2669872613]] links his departure to Rome's occupation of Tarentum and the coming Roman-Carthaginian struggle for Sicily.

## Qualifications

This profile is based on one narrative podcast episode and is limited to Pyrrhus's role in Roman-Carthaginian prehistory. Battle losses, negotiations, motives, elephant effects, reported dialogue, and the Sicily "killing field" prophecy remain source-scoped ancient traditions or interpretations.

## What Changed

- Established Pyrrhus as the Hellenistic challenger whose tactical victories expose the strategic strength of Roman replacement capacity.
- Connected his Italian and Sicilian campaigns to the transition from Roman-Carthaginian cooperation to rivalry.

## Relationships

- [[RomanRepublic]] - Italian opponent that survives Pyrrhus's battlefield victories and takes Tarentum after his withdrawal.
- [[Carthage]] - Sicilian opponent and Roman treaty partner during Pyrrhus's intervention.
- [[RomanCivicMilitaryIncorporation]] - Roman mobilization system that helps absorb the losses Pyrrhus inflicts.
- [[FirstPunicWar]] - later Sicilian conflict anticipated by the strategic position left after Pyrrhus's departure.
