# European Union Digital Services Act

The European Union Digital Services Act appears in [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-under-secretary-of-state-sarah-b-rogers-on-dismantling-the-censorship-industrial-complex-39828360]] as the EU platform-regulation regime most directly criticized by [[SarahBRogers|Sarah B. Rogers]]. In the episode's account, the DSA includes content-based obligations, hate-speech rules, risk controls, and a [[TrustedFlaggerSystem|trusted flagger]] mechanism that can channel reports from selected organizations to platforms.

The source's strongest claim is extraterritorial. Rogers argues that the [[EuropeanUnion]] can regulate platforms inside Europe, but that fines or threats against American companies for hosting speech lawful under U.S. norms become a [[CrossBorderPlatformSpeechRegulation]] conflict.

## Key Claims
- Vague or broad content categories can lead risk-averse platforms to remove more speech than regulators explicitly demand.
- Trusted flagger systems may turn selected NGOs into privileged moderation inputs rather than ordinary public critics.
- Large platform fines can operate like a de facto tariff when foreign regulators repeatedly target American technology companies.
- The source connects the DSA to [[Twitter|X]] because Rogers and the hosts discuss EU digital fines and political pressure around the platform.
