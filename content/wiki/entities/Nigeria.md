# Nigeria

## Overview
Nigeria appears across the wiki as a large African economy where food affordability, connectivity, small-business finance, insecurity, public-service weakness, and electoral participation reveal the gap between national policy and daily capability.

## Current Profile
The food evidence begins with the [[JollofIndex]]: diesel, roads, weather, seasonality, currency pressure, and farming constraints make a culturally central meal consume a large share of minimum-wage income. The latest episode deepens that picture through [[BornoState]], where subsidy removal, devaluation, weak harvests, aid cuts, energy costs, violence, and malnutrition coexist with improved macroeconomic indicators.

Connectivity is another mixed-infrastructure problem. Mobile-first access brought many people online, but streaming, video calls, and AI applications expose missing fibre and reliability. [[Starlink]] adds competitive pressure and remote coverage without replacing terrestrial networks; [[Eutelsat]] represents a different provider and backhaul path.

The entrepreneurship evidence shows policy capacity rather than only failure. The [[YouWinProgram]] used screened and partly randomized grants to help firms such as [[LarkluxPaint]] cross the [[SmallBusinessFinancingGap]], though grant success does not resolve the wider [[MissingMiddle]]. The latest electoral evidence adds a political qualification: hardship does not automatically remove an incumbent when organization, identity, insecurity, and low participation shape outcomes.

## Key Characteristics
- Large, regionally varied country where macroeconomic aggregates can diverge sharply from household food security and service access.
- Food-affordability case linking transport, currency, energy, harvests, and local production to diet.
- Mobile-first connectivity market moving toward a mixed fibre, mobile, and satellite system.
- Development-policy case for direct entrepreneur grants and the difficulty of scaling very small firms.
- Security and aid-pressure case concentrated in Borno's schools, farms, clinics, and local economy.
- Electoral case where incumbency, identity, party machinery, danger, and low turnout can outweigh broad dissatisfaction.

## Evidence
- Food affordability: [[latin-lessons-the-donroe-doctrine-boost-6a4233e26b6690f46aae4e3d]] uses the Jollof Index to connect staple costs with diesel, roads, weather, seasonality, and farming constraints.
- Connectivity stack: [[in-it-to-bin-it-nigel-farage-v-count-binface-6a58abcdc152a357db17c20e]] presents satellite service as a useful complement and competitive pressure rather than a complete substitute for fibre and mobile networks.
- Firm growth and grants: [[piles-of-cash-and-a-town-of-solutions-in-kenya-nigeria-summer-school]] follows YouWin's screened and randomized grants, reported job creation, and missing-middle rationale.
- Reform, insecurity, and elections: [[songs-of-disenchantment-the-sorrow-of-nigeria-6275426999307c5f9d5c7073a5b16e0d]] connects improved macro stability with persistent hardship and explains how low participation and incumbent organization may shape the election.

## Qualifications
The four sources cover selected markets, programs, regions, and political mechanisms rather than a representative national survey. Price, employment, turnout, growth, inflation, malnutrition, and connectivity figures are source-dated. Borno's conflict conditions should not be generalized to every state, while Abuja connectivity and one grant program do not define all Nigerian infrastructure or enterprise.
