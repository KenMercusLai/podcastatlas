# Wim Hof

## Overview
Wim Hof is the breathwork and cold-exposure figure named in the [[control-stress-for-healthy-eating-metabolism-aging-dr-elissa-epel-scim4455888803]] Huberman Lab source note as a collaborator or design influence for a UCSF positive-stress study.

## Current Profile
The current wiki evidence uses Hof narrowly, not as a full biography. In this source, [[ElissaEpel]] says her experience with Wim Hof breathing helped her think about high-arousal positive stress, and Hof helped design a study comparing low-arousal practices such as mindfulness or slow breathing with high-arousal practices such as exercise and the Wim Hof method.

The profile remains source-scoped because the episode says biomarker work is still being analyzed. The page does not independently validate Hof's method, safety, claims, or broader public profile.

## Key Characteristics
- Appears as the named figure behind the Wim Hof method discussed in Epel's positive-stress branch.
- Functions in the source as a high-arousal body-based practice example rather than as the central guest.
- Is tied to preliminary research questions about positive emotion, stress, telomerase, mitochondria, gene expression, and autonomic pathways.

## Evidence
- Positive-stress role - [[control-stress-for-healthy-eating-metabolism-aging-dr-elissa-epel-scim4455888803]] says Epel became interested in body-based positive stress after experiencing Wim Hof breathing.
- Study-design role - [[control-stress-for-healthy-eating-metabolism-aging-dr-elissa-epel-scim4455888803]] says Hof helped design a UCSF study comparing low-arousal and high-arousal stress-regulation methods.
- Evidence boundary - [[control-stress-for-healthy-eating-metabolism-aging-dr-elissa-epel-scim4455888803]] says stress, anxiety, and depression fell across groups, but telomerase, mitochondrial, gene-expression, sympathetic, and parasympathetic analyses were still planned or pending.
