# Trevor Phillips

## Overview

Trevor Phillips is the guest historian and personal witness in [[401-windrush-the-story-of-black-britain-glt4826407019]], where he discusses the research he conducted with his brother Mike Phillips on [[EmpireWindrush]] and the history of Caribbean settlement in Britain.

## Current Profile

Phillips combines documentary history with family experience. His parents came to Britain in 1950, retained an expectation of return, and raised a mobile family spanning Britain and British Guiana/Guyana. He uses that perspective to argue that Caribbean Britons should be understood through their own ambitions, institutions, wartime service, family strategies, religion, and sense of belonging rather than mainly through white reaction or victimhood.

## Key Characteristics

- Treats [[EmpireWindrush]] as a transformative event without making it the sole origin of Black Britain.
- Centers [[MigrantHistoricalAgency]] and resists turning Caribbean Britons into symbols in other people's arguments.
- Explains migration as mobile, family-based, and initially compatible with plans to return.
- Distinguishes Caribbean migration from South Asian, East African Asian, Eastern European, and later African migration histories.
- Uses clothing, cricket, churches, family formation, and mixed heritage to connect lived experience with decolonization and national change.

## Evidence

- Historical method: [[401-windrush-the-story-of-black-britain-glt4826407019]] describes Phillips and Mike Phillips choosing Windrush because film, records, and surviving passengers allowed a Caribbean British story to be reconstructed from within.
- Family and migration: [[401-windrush-the-story-of-black-britain-glt4826407019]] uses his parents' 1950 arrival, continuing return plans, and his childhood movement between Britain and Guyana to ground [[PostwarCaribbeanBritishSettlement]].
- Public-memory boundary: [[401-windrush-the-story-of-black-britain-glt4826407019]] records his insistence that [[WindrushNationalMemory]] should not absorb every non-white or immigrant history into one symbolic narrative.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to one podcast episode and its account of Phillips's family and historical argument. Demographic totals, church-attendance comparisons, mixed-race international comparisons, and interpretations of other migrant groups remain source-scoped rather than a complete account of his scholarship or public career.
