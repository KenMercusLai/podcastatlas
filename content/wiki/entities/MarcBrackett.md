# Marc Brackett

## Overview
Marc Brackett is the Huberman Lab guest in [[how-to-better-regulate-your-emotions-dr-marc-brackett-scim6338474733]] and [[how-to-increase-your-emotional-intelligence-dr-marc-brackett-scim6059113217]], identified as a Yale psychology professor and director of the [[YaleCenterForEmotionalIntelligence]].

## Current Profile
Across the two sources, Brackett presents emotional intelligence as a concrete skill set rather than a vague appeal to feel more. His practical frame defines regulation as using emotions wisely for goals, relationships, decisions, performance, and values. He distinguishes accepting emotions from expressing every emotion, and he treats emotion vocabulary, co-regulation, vulnerability, school culture, leadership, and supportive adult relationships as trainable domains.

Brackett's strongest wiki contribution is to make [[EmotionRegulationToolkit]] more operational. Through [[RULERFramework]], PRIME, [[MetaMoment]], [[MoodMeterEmotionalCheckIn]], and precise emotion labeling, he turns regulation into a sequence of recognition, understanding, naming, expression judgment, strategy choice, and deliberate response. His bullying and Uncle Marvin narratives add a relational and institutional layer: adults must look beneath behavior, give people permission to feel, intervene in repeated power-asymmetric harm, and model [[EmotionMentorship]].

## Key Characteristics
- Treats emotions as useful information rather than good or bad states.
- Defines regulation around goals, context, person, emotion, and strategy.
- Frames emotional intelligence as definable, measurable, teachable, and socially consequential.
- Emphasizes precise vocabulary as a condition for effective regulation and communication.
- Connects vulnerability with responsibility, boundaries, and trust rather than uncontrolled disclosure.
- Applies emotion skills across families, schools, workplaces, leadership, masculinity, and technology.
- Uses autobiographical cases to connect emotional skill, bullying prevention, and supportive adult responsibility.

## Evidence
- Practical definition - [[how-to-better-regulate-your-emotions-dr-marc-brackett-scim6338474733]] records Brackett defining emotion regulation as using emotions wisely to achieve goals.
- Skill model - [[how-to-better-regulate-your-emotions-dr-marc-brackett-scim6338474733]] has Brackett summarize emotional intelligence through recognizing, understanding, labeling, expressing, and regulating emotions.
- Vocabulary work - [[how-to-better-regulate-your-emotions-dr-marc-brackett-scim6338474733]] distinguishes anxiety, stress, pressure, and fear as different states requiring different responses.
- Vulnerability boundary - [[how-to-better-regulate-your-emotions-dr-marc-brackett-scim6338474733]] says useful vulnerability names the feeling and what one is doing about it, rather than spilling fear onto others.
- Systemic education frame - [[how-to-better-regulate-your-emotions-dr-marc-brackett-scim6338474733]] argues that school emotional-intelligence work requires shared language across leaders, teachers, students, and parents.
- Check-in and nonjudgment - [[how-to-increase-your-emotional-intelligence-dr-marc-brackett-scim6059113217]] explains the Mood Meter and stresses that unpleasant emotions are neither bad nor always in need of fixing.
- Mentorship and bullying - [[how-to-increase-your-emotional-intelligence-dr-marc-brackett-scim6059113217]] connects Uncle Marvin's support and Brackett's bullying history to permission to feel, adult intervention, perspective-taking, and regulation practice.

## Qualifications
This profile is bounded to two Huberman Lab source notes. Claims about school outcomes, employer preferences, adolescent AI use, RULER effects, phone-free camp results, compassionate online requests, suppression, and student emotion percentages are episode-grounded rather than an independent literature review. Both local source bodies use "Mark Brackett" in places, but their titles and the canonical page use Marc Brackett.
