## Overview

[[ChenGuiDuliaoGeneralLateHan|陈龟]] is a late-Han frontier official whose current wiki profile now spans both damaging overreach and later administrative repair. Hanji 820 shows him coercing an uninvolved Southern Xiongnu chanyu into suicide and being jailed and removed; Hanji 843 then shows him as a 158 CE Duliao general diagnosing frontier disorder through corrupt officials, bought office, exhausted provinces, and weak commanders; Hanji 844 shows his recall after [[LiangJiLateHan|Liang Ji]]'s accusation and his death by hunger strike after an ignored anti-Liang memorial.

## Current Profile

Chronologically, Hanji 820 supplies the first layer. After [[LiangBingLateHan|Liang Bing]] is recalled for illness during the [[SouthernXiongnu|Southern Xiongnu]] revolt, Chen Gui acts in the Xiongnu command role from his Wuyuan administrator position. He forces [[XiuliChanyu|Xiuli]] and the Left Wise King toward suicide even though the episode says the chanyu was not part of [[WusiSouthernXiongnu|Wusi]] and [[CheNiuChanyu|Che Niu]]'s plot. He also proposes moving close chanyu kin inward. The court later jails and removes him. The episode can read him as a person made to bear blame, but the page treats the case primarily as unauthorized and damaging intervention.

Hanji 843 gives a very different later profile. In 158 CE, Southern Xiongnu, Wuhuan, and Xianbei pressure across the frontier, and Chen Gui answers before leaving for the Duliao post by diagnosing corrupt governors, bought office, exhausted provinces, floods, insects, and weak commanders as causes of military failure. [[EmperorHuanOfHan|Emperor Huan]] adopts much of the program: replacing inspectors and frontier officials, empowering capable commanders, and remitting [[BingzhouLateHan|Bingzhou]] and [[LiangzhouLateHan|Liangzhou]] burdens for one year.

Hanji 844 then shows the fragility of that service record once Liang Ji turns an old grievance into recall, ignored memorial, and death by hunger strike. Chen Gui's later anti-Liang witness role is morally forceful, but Hanji 820 now prevents the wiki from presenting him as only a clean repair official or martyr.

## Key Characteristics

- Frontier official whose record contains both unauthorized coercive overreach and later root-cause repair.
- Early Southern Xiongnu case shows command discretion turning into chanyu-legitimacy damage.
- Later 158 CE diagnosis centers internal governance: corrupt officials, bought offices, bad commanders, and impossible burdens matter as much as enemy movement.
- Administrative remedy combines personnel cleanup, frontier-command fit, legal authority, and fiscal/corvee relief for disaster-hit provinces.
- Huan-era service shows limited policy adoption before direct confrontation with Liang Ji becomes impossible.
- Final anti-Liang memorial makes him an early witness before the later 159 CE anti-Liang sequence becomes executable.
- Must be kept distinct from [[ChenGui|陈珪]], the later Xuzhou adviser with a separate Three Kingdoms-era profile.

- Anti-Liang witness role and death: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-844-ruhe-liyu-bubaizhidi-lsp49uw8atyiufrf-ryo-9ctos5z|Hanji 844]] presents Chen Gui's memorial as a direct list of Liang's misconduct, says Huan receives it without visible action, and says Chen Gui dies after seven days of hunger strike.

## Qualifications

The page is bounded to three podcast source notes and should not infer a complete biography from them alone. Hanji 820 and Hanji 843 are not simple contradiction: the current synthesis treats them as different moments in one frontier career, where early overreach and later reform can coexist. The "背锅" reading in Hanji 820 remains qualified because Chen Gui is punished after actions the episode itself presents as unauthorized and harmful.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 820 as Chen Gui's earliest current layer, introducing unauthorized chanyu coercion and removal before the Huan-era repair material.
- Reframed Chen Gui from only a frontier repair official and anti-Liang martyr into a mixed case of damaging discretion, later administrative competence, and political vulnerability.
- Clarified that the blame-bearing theme in Hanji 820 is not the same as morally clean responsibility-taking.
