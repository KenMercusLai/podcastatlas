# Luobo Paidui / 萝博派对

## Overview
Luobo Paidui / 萝博派对 is the open-source humanoid robotics startup founded by [[HuangYiRobotics|Huang Yi / 黄一]] in [[22-sui-de-jushen-ceo-5-lun-rongzi-guo-yi-meiyuan-buzhi-tian-you-duo-gao-yinian-chile-shi-nian-de-ku-duitan-huangyi-luobo-paidui-chuangshiren-ceo-ljwdmocryiblf0k3rkz8eonkqox]]. The episode says the company completed five financing rounds totaling more than 100 million dollars, grew to about 140 people, and uses open source to reduce duplicated effort in embodied AI.

## Current Profile
The company's source-scoped profile is a body-platform-first robotics startup. [[RPORobot|RPO]] supplies current small-scale orders and education/research feedback, while [[RP1Robot|RP1]] is the planned productized platform for a larger research-education customer base. Luobo Paidui also explores low-level control, reinforcement-learning demos, and foundation-model-adjacent work, but Huang says the near-term base remains humanoid body hardware and product iteration rather than heavy pretraining or broad consumer entertainment.

## Key Characteristics
- Open-source body platform: the company treats open source as a way to lower collaboration friction and attract users onto its robot body.
- Research-education wedge: schools, labs, and startups are the most important current customer group because they can produce feedback, papers, and ecosystem reuse.
- Capital-accelerated iteration: venture financing is framed as a way to buy time and position in a fast-moving, capital-intensive market.
- Body-to-model optionality: the company is positioned between model companies and robot bodies, with later model work left open.
- Productization pressure: the RPO-to-RP1 transition exposes manufacturing, reliability, packaging, support, and scale-up constraints.
- Organization scaling tension: high talent density remains important, but manufacturing, delivery, sales, and supply chain need more process.

## Qualifications
The company's financing, order, headcount, and roadmap details come from a founder interview rather than independent financial or operating disclosures. The source also treats the embodied-AI market as bubbly, so current funding and attention should not be read as proof of durable commercialization.
