# Mohammad Mossadegh

## Overview

Mohammad Mossadegh was Iran's prime minister during the 1951-1953 oil-nationalization crisis and the leader overthrown in the 1953 coup discussed in the source.

## Current Profile

[[259-iran-england-the-little-satan-glt8347276204]] situates Mossadegh within a longer sovereignty dispute rather than treating nationalization as an isolated act. Soviet pressure, Iran's rejection of a northern Soviet oil concession, unequal British oil terms, and Britain's own postwar nationalizations all strengthened the argument that Iran could reclaim control of its southern oil industry.

## Key Characteristics

- Prime minister associated with nationalization of Iran's British-linked oil industry.
- Represents a postwar sovereignty challenge to opaque and unequal concession terms.
- Governed during a crisis involving British diplomatic rupture and Anglo-American coordination.
- Became a durable reference point for later Iranian fears of embassy-based foreign interference.
