# Mohammad Mossadegh

## Overview

Mohammad Mossadegh was Iran's prime minister during the 1951-1953 oil-nationalization crisis and the leader overthrown in the [[IranianCoup1953|1953 coup]]. The bounded sources present his removal as both the end of a sovereignty struggle and a lasting legitimacy burden for the restored Shah.

## Current Profile

[[259-iran-england-the-little-satan-glt8347276204]] situates Mossadegh within a longer sovereignty dispute rather than treating nationalization as an isolated act. Soviet pressure, Iran's rejection of a northern Soviet oil concession, unequal British oil terms, and Britain's own postwar nationalizations all strengthened the argument that Iran could reclaim control of its southern oil industry.

[[636-revolution-in-iran-fall-of-the-shah-part-1-glt5083873634]] compresses that history into the revolution's prehistory. It identifies nationalization as the trigger for a CIA-backed overthrow and argues that the intervention helped later opponents portray [[MohammadRezaPahlavi|Mohammad Reza Pahlavi]] as a Western client.

## Key Characteristics

- Prime minister associated with nationalization of Iran's British-linked oil industry.
- Represents a postwar sovereignty challenge to opaque and unequal concession terms.
- Governed during a crisis involving British diplomatic rupture and Anglo-American coordination.
- Became a durable reference point for later Iranian fears of embassy-based foreign interference.
- His overthrow became part of the revolutionary legitimacy case against the Shah's foreign backing.

## Qualifications

These sources provide concise relational histories, not a full political biography. Mossadegh's domestic coalition, constitutional authority, economic policy, the coup's internal Iranian participants, and the exact allocation of British and American responsibility need broader evidence before stronger judgments are adopted.
