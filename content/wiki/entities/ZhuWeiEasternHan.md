## Overview

朱鲔 / Zhu Wei is a former Gengshi-aligned Luoyang defender whose surrender becomes an early test of [[LiuXiu|Liu Xiu]]'s founding clemency. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-679-zhuwei-shahai-liuxiu-gege-weihe-reng-neng-shanzhong-lqns6ktym8rgv3y5tlczcyfyzxdb|Hanji 679]] presents him as personally exposed because he had joined Liu Yan's killing and opposed Liu Xiu's northern mission, yet Liu Xiu chooses unification and credibility over revenge. Zhu Wei's submission helps deliver [[LuoyangLateHan|Luoyang]] and makes a former enemy's survival part of the new regime's public trust.

## Current Profile

Zhu Wei holds Luoyang for Liu Xuan's side after Liu Xiu has founded a new order. His refusal to surrender is rooted in fear rather than simple military confidence: he expects Liu Xiu to avenge Liu Yan and remembers that he had argued against sending Xiao Wang / Liu Xiu north.

The surrender depends on two trust devices. Liu Xiu gives a public promise to preserve Zhu Wei's office and title, swearing by the Yellow River that he will not break faith. Cen Peng, once Zhu Wei's subordinate, carries the message; Zhu Wei tests him by lowering a rope from the city wall and asking whether Cen Peng dares to climb it.

After the test, Zhu Wei binds himself and goes to Liu Xiu. Liu Xiu personally unbinds him, sends him back to Luoyang overnight, and receives Zhu Wei, Su Mao, and the army's surrender the next day. The source says Zhu Wei is appointed Pingdi General and Fugou Marquis, later serves as Shaofu, and leaves a hereditary title, so the episode's answer to why he "ends well" is that Liu Xiu treats credible surrender as more valuable than private vengeance.

## Key Characteristics

- Luoyang holdout whose surrender transfers a major capital base to Liu Xiu.
- High-risk former enemy because his past conduct touches Liu Yan's death and Liu Xiu's earlier political survival.
- Trust-test actor who requires both Liu Xiu's oath and Cen Peng's personal risk before submitting.
- Surrender-cascade figure whose return to Luoyang makes the next day's army submission possible.
- Incorporated former opponent whose survival, office, and title turn clemency into a founding signal.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 679. It does not reconstruct Zhu Wei's full earlier Gengshi career or later Eastern Han office sequence beyond the episode's summary. The title and office forms, Su Mao's exact subordinate role, and Xiao Wang/Liu Xiu naming remain source-scoped. The evidence supports a credible-surrender reading, not a claim that Liu Xiu forgave every former enemy without constraint.
