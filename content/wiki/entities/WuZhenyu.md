# 吴镇宇 / Wu Zhenyu

## Overview
吴镇宇 / Wu Zhenyu is the actor interviewed in [[ep281-duihua-wuzhenyu-kun-zai-chuangshang-li-de-women-jueding-zhongxin-zhaohui-ziji-lvvreapoykpj5xtlryaaupu1xo7r]], where he discusses playing Yu Songfan in [[ChanDrama|《蝉》]] and uses the role to reflect on acting, aging, anger, death, love, and AI-era creative work.

## Current Profile
In this source, Wu appears as an intuition-led actor who treats performance as entering a life rather than executing an external plan. He says the role becomes set when the first scene and first line are spoken, while language, body movement, location, co-actors, and later physical changes help him inhabit and leave the character.

The episode also frames him as a reflective but unsystematic speaker. His comments jump from character work to childhood communication habits, fairy tales, dogs, anger, Alzheimer's, and love, but the through line is consistent: real creative and emotional life cannot be fully rehearsed, standardized, or smoothed into a perfect answer.

## Key Characteristics
- Intuition-led acting - he relies on first-line character entry, movement, environment, and immediate scene logic rather than only prewritten analysis.
- Embodied role boundary - he describes Yu Songfan's conservative look and his post-shoot hair coloring as part of leaving the character's body-image behind.
- Aging realism - he speaks of old age through pain, illness, curiosity, friendship, loneliness, and the possibility of restarting practice.
- Imperfection defense - he argues that AI does not replace actors because performance includes psychology, lens relation, director intention, personal limits, and irreplaceable irregularities.
- Anger as signal - he treats anger as a possible driver of art and social change, while acknowledging that it can burden people around him.
- Love as containing frame - he presents love as something that surrounds fear, grief, joy, anger, evil, and desire rather than one emotion among them.
