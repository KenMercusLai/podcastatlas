# Goree Island

## Overview

Gorée is an island off Dakar in [[Senegal]] whose preserved colonial architecture and [[HouseOfSlavesGoree|House of Slaves]] made it a major site of Atlantic-slavery memory and African American pilgrimage.

## Current Profile

The episode separates Gorée's real commercial and colonial history from its later representation as a mass slave-export hub. Portuguese, Dutch, English, and French rule, offshore geography, European disease risk, and Franco-African trading dynasties made the island a useful interface. Later harbor limitations and commercial decline helped preserve its built environment.

Its contemporary significance is principally commemorative in this source. UNESCO recognition, museum storytelling, famous visitors, tourism, and diaspora pilgrimage made the island a sacred memory site even though the episode says it was statistically minor in Atlantic slave exports.

## Key Characteristics

- Offshore colonial trading post successively controlled by several European powers.
- Commercial interface shaped by coastal geography, disease, and African-European networks.
- Home of signare-linked Creole trading dynasties and preserved eighteenth-century architecture.
- Independence-era heritage and tourism site centered on the House of Slaves.
- Globally powerful slavery memorial whose promoted embarkation narrative is historically disputed.

## Evidence

- Colonial-commercial setting: [[272-senegal-the-door-of-no-return-glt8611353465]] links the island's position and disease environment to Portuguese, Dutch, English, and French trade and to signare-centered dynasties.
- Preservation and commemoration: [[272-senegal-the-door-of-no-return-glt8611353465]] connects commercial decline, preserved buildings, museum development, UNESCO status, and diaspora tourism.
- Disputed slave-trade scale: [[272-senegal-the-door-of-no-return-glt8611353465]] says most regional captives departed from Senegal and Gambia river-mouth depots and presents Gorée as statistically minor.

## Qualifications

This profile rests on one podcast episode rather than specialist archival or archaeological review. It does not deny that enslaved people passed through Gorée or that slavery formed part of the island's commerce; it rejects the much larger numerical and functional claims attached to the House of Slaves. Exact export totals, dates, mortality comparisons, and claims of scholarly consensus remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Established Gorée as a distinct colonial-history and public-memory entity.
- Separated the island's documented trading role from the mass-embarkation story.
- Added diaspora pilgrimage and tourism as sources of the site's modern symbolic power.
