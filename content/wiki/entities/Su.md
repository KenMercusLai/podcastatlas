# 素 / Su

素 is the North America-based humanities PhD guest in [[168-yin-yu-xin-yibu-riben-laogong-yimin-de-kuayang-huikuan-shi-lmgfmmgkawoa-6xyucnm1ljebzwu]]. The episode presents her as a researcher trained across modern Chinese history, Japanese-language sources, U.S. history, global history, and financial history, using multilingual archives to study [[JapaneseLaborMigrationRemittances]].

Her research path in the episode matters because the topic grows from method rather than only theme. She moves from Qing diplomatic-travel research and U.S. newspaper materials into Japanese-language Hawaiian and Californian newspapers, then into Japanese foreign-ministry and [[YokohamaSpecieBank|Yokohama Specie Bank]] records. That path makes her a source node for [[ArchivePreservationBias]] and for the episode's comparison between Japanese state-bank records and Chinese [[QiaopiRemittanceNetworks]].

## Key Claims
- Good historical research often needs new sources, a new angle, or a new method.
- Migration history should not reduce Asian migrants to identity or exclusion; wage work, family obligation, and remittance infrastructure also shaped their lives.
- State archives are valuable but partial, because government preservation choices make some people and transactions easier to see than others.
