# Roger Morrice

## Overview

Roger Morrice was a late-seventeenth-century English newsletter writer whose detailed reports provide the episode's principal contemporary evidence for reassessing [[SirJohnKnight]]'s prosecution and acquittal.

## Current Profile

[[guns-part-1-the-sudden-celebrity-of-sir-john-knight]] describes Morrice as producing private political newsletters during the 1680s. Their difficult shorthand kept them inaccessible until a cryptographer and several generations of historians decoded and published the material in a multi-volume edition.

The episode treats the newsletters as informed contemporary reporting that can often be checked against other records and that preserves details absent from later legal digests. For Knight, those details include his role in anti-Catholic agitation, his companions leaving arms at Bristol's boundary, his claim to have left his own weapons outside St. Michael's, and the prosecution's political character.

Morrice's value is methodological: his reports do not automatically settle the legal meaning of the acquittal, but they expose how much context a short later case summary can remove.

## Key Characteristics

- Writer of detailed private political newsletters in the 1680s.
- User of a difficult shorthand that delayed broad scholarly access to his work.
- Contemporary reporter whose material contains details missing from later legal case summaries.
- Principal source for the episode's contextual reconstruction of Knight's arrest and trial.
- Example of how newly accessible archives can alter the interpretation of an established precedent.

## Qualifications

The supplied file does not reproduce Morrice's shorthand, edited text, editorial apparatus, provenance, or corroborating records. Contemporary reporting can still contain bias, error, and incomplete observation. The episode's claim that the newsletters are generally reliable supports their evidentiary importance but not the certainty of every reported detail.
