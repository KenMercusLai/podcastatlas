# Pierre Cauchon

## Overview

Pierre Cauchon was the bishop who led the ecclesiastical prosecution of [[JoanOfArc|Joan of Arc]] in Rouen under Anglo-Burgundian political power.

## Current Profile

The campaign source introduces Cauchon as the bishop who proposed converting Burgundian capture into an English-sponsored church prosecution. Because Joan had been seized within his bishopric of Beauvais, he claimed jurisdiction; the English could buy her and deliver her to the church while retaining the political benefit of charges including heresy, sorcery, and demonic influence.

The trial episode presents Cauchon as a politically aligned inquisitorial judge rather than a careless theatrical prosecutor. He opened the case in January 1431, investigated Joan's reputation and origins, confirmed her virginity through examination, assembled clerical and legal expertise, and spent about six weeks preparing before formal interrogation began.

His attention to procedure did not make the tribunal neutral. The participating French clergy overwhelmingly belonged to the Burgundian or Lancastrian side, Joan lacked counsel, English guards controlled her imprisonment, and acceptance of her revelations would have damaged [[HenryVIOfEngland|Henry VI]] while validating [[CharlesVIIOfFrance|Charles VII]]. Cauchon's role therefore exemplifies a proceeding in which institutional form and political purpose reinforce one another.

## Key Characteristics

- Bishop and chief organizer of Joan's heresy prosecution.
- Jurisdictional entrepreneur who linked the place of capture to an English-funded church proceeding.
- Careful user of inquisitorial investigation and consultation.
- Judge operating inside an Anglo-Burgundian political environment.
- Interrogator focused on revelation, obedience, and male clothing.
- Sentencer whose treatment of abjuration and relapse led from imprisonment to secular execution.

## Evidence

- Preparation: [[635-joan-of-arc-for-fear-of-the-flames-part-4-glt8113477235]] attributes six weeks of investigation and a large body of clerical and legal participants to Cauchon's process.
- Jurisdiction and purchase: [[634-joan-of-arc-heroine-in-chains-part-3-glt6235222836]] attributes to Cauchon the proposal that English authorities buy Joan and deliver her for church trial under Beauvais jurisdiction.
- Political alignment: [[635-joan-of-arc-for-fear-of-the-flames-part-4-glt8113477235]] places the tribunal under English custody and among French participants aligned with Burgundian or Lancastrian power.
- Judgment sequence: [[635-joan-of-arc-for-fear-of-the-flames-part-4-glt8113477235]] follows Cauchon from the public heresy sentence through perpetual imprisonment after abjuration and renewed condemnation after relapse.

## Qualifications

The sources do not establish that Cauchon's every theological judgment was insincere or dictated directly by English officials. Procedural diligence cannot establish impartiality, and political usefulness cannot by itself establish that every participant disbelieved the charges. The exact negotiation over jurisdiction, purchase, and charge selection remains compressed, while his wider career and the complete trial record remain outside this evidence.

## What Changed

- Extended Cauchon's profile backward from trial administration to his jurisdictional and purchase proposal after Joan's capture.
- Connected the legal forum directly to Bedford's attempt to reverse the political meaning of Reims.
