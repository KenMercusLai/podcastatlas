# Putian Medical Network / 莆田医疗经营网络

## Overview
The Putian medical network is [[Banlatte]]'s nameable subject behind the loose public label “莆田系”: overlapping families, operators, companies, brands, and hometown ties that replicated medical businesses across China without forming one centrally controlled organization.

## Current Profile
Its historical route runs from itinerant skin-treatment sales through sexually transmitted infection treatment, public-hospital department contracting and equipment cooperation into specialist hospitals, comprehensive care, obstetrics, medical aesthetics, dentistry, ophthalmology, and checkups. The network form explains rapid copying and shared methods, but does not prove that institutions with similar names, former shareholders, or Putian origins share current ownership or legal responsibility.

## Key Characteristics
- Kinship, apprenticeship, and hometown ties transferred operating methods and enabled national replication.
- Advertising measurement, referral payments, public-hospital branding, and later online leads made patient acquisition a central business capability.
- High acquisition and fixed costs combined with patient information asymmetry to create recurring risks of overdiagnosis, overtreatment, and price escalation.
- Department contracting and renamed “technical” or “project” cooperation blurred public authority and private commercial control.
- The label covers heterogeneous actors, changing shareholders, transferred brands, and divergent conduct rather than a unified command structure.
- Self-regulation remained weak where an association lacked enforcement power and operators did not complete promised internal review.
- Formal hospital and consumer-health expansion changed the asset base but did not automatically remove governance, continuity, or compliance risk.

- **Replication and acquisition:** The episode traces teams from itinerant rooms and street notices to television advertising, tracked lead sources, hospital departments, equipment revenue-sharing, and online lead funnels. [[gkwrimaorinia5ejiwtsfhox-gkwrimaorinia5ejiwtsfhox]]
- **Patient harm and enforcement:** Infertility, gynecology, and male-hospital cases include documented improper diagnosis or treatment, unregistered staff, operating-table escalation, criminal convictions, and corrupt protection. [[gkwrimaorinia5ejiwtsfhox-gkwrimaorinia5ejiwtsfhox]]
- **Network boundary:** The source repeatedly warns that shared brands, family origin, historical ownership, and the “莆田系” label do not establish one controller; its Wuxi example separates a founding family's 2020 sale from conduct investigated in 2024. - **Transition limits:** Comprehensive hospitals, listed obstetrics groups, medical aesthetics, and other consumer-health services show diversification alongside losses, delisting, closure, and unverifiable scale claims. [[gkwrimaorinia5ejiwtsfhox-gkwrimaorinia5ejiwtsfhox]]

## Qualifications
- “莆田系” is a social and industry label, not a demonstrated legal group. Findings about one hospital or defendant should not be generalized to every Putian-origin operator.
- Several market-size, revenue, failure-rate, and family-network statements come from media reports, participants, or industry claims summarized by the episode rather than independently reproduced datasets.
- The source emphasizes serious verified cases without claiming that all private hospitals, medical-aesthetic firms, or Putian businesses use the same methods.

## What Changed
- The wiki now separates a loose medical-business network from [[Putian|the city]] and from any single company.
- It ties replication strength and self-regulation weakness to the same decentralized family-network structure.
- It makes changing control and brand ownership part of responsibility attribution.
