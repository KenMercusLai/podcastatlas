# Tom Perry Jr.

## Overview

Tom Perry Jr. is a prison minister who maintained a long friendship with [[JohnForrestParker]] and accompanied him through his final day and 2010 execution.

## Current Profile

Perry describes death-row ministry as a commitment to remain with condemned people “all the way” if asked, including through execution and its aftermath. With Parker, that meant years of monthly and extended visits, friendship beyond formal preaching, presence with family, and preservation of Parker's memory. His account also exposes a limit of witness observation: Perry sincerely believed Parker appeared peaceful, while the lethal-injection protocol prevented him from knowing what Parker experienced internally.

## Key Characteristics

- Prison minister who estimates that he accompanied 12 or 13 condemned men through executions.
- Practitioner of reliable presence as the core of ministry.
- Long-term friend of Parker after an initially difficult religious encounter.
- Supporter of condemned prisoners' families before, during, and after executions.
- Witness who distinguished the dignity offered by individual officers from the larger protocol's uncertainty.
- Keeper of Parker's memory who asked that it not be degraded or reduced to the execution alone.

## Evidence

### Sustained ministry

- [[the-alabama-murders-part-4-the-protocol]] describes Perry's monthly visits, twice-yearly extended visits, mentor Ben Sherrod's “all the way” commitment, and the support of Perry's wife.

### Friendship and final accompaniment

- [[the-alabama-murders-part-4-the-protocol]] recounts Parker's friendship with Perry, their final family gathering, Parker's request that Perry care for his parents, and their shared “I love you” hand signal.

### Witness limits and memory

- [[the-alabama-murders-part-4-the-protocol]] contrasts Perry's good-faith impression of a peaceful death with the episode's account of hidden pulmonary injury, sedation, and paralysis, and reports that he keeps the final-day photograph beside his professional license.

## Qualifications

This profile is based on one structured episode summary and principally on Perry's own recollections. It does not independently assess the theology or wider record of his ministry, the views of all prisoners and families he served, or Parker's culpability. Perry's observation of outward calm is evidence of what witnesses could see, not a physiological determination of awareness or pain.

## What Changed

- Established Perry's sustained accompaniment as the episode's human counterexample to institutional indifference.
- Distinguished his sincere witness account from what the execution protocol allowed him to know.
