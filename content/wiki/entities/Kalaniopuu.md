# Kalaniʻōpuʻu

## Overview

Kalaniʻōpuʻu was the ruling king encountered by [[JamesCook]] at Kealakekua Bay in 1779, called “Terreeoboo” in British records and placed at the center of the failed hostage-taking that preceded Cook's death.

## Current Profile

The episode presents Kalaniʻōpuʻu as an active sovereign rather than a background figure in Cook's final voyage. He visited Cook in state on 26 January, brought food, clothing, and valuable featherwork, and participated in a political and ceremonial relationship whose precise Hawaiian meaning is not fully recoverable from the British record.

His later questions about the expedition's departure and his alarm at its unexpected return suggest that hospitality had limits shaped by resources, ritual timing, political authority, or some combination of them. When Cook tried to take him aboard as leverage for a stolen cutter, Kalaniʻōpuʻu initially walked with him but stopped after a wife and chiefs intervened. The crowd's resistance turned the attempted transfer of a king into the immediate political crisis surrounding Cook's death.

## Key Characteristics

- Hawaiian ruler whose authority structured the expedition's access, gifts, departure, and return.
- Participant in a lavish but culturally ambiguous ceremonial exchange with Cook.
- Political actor who questioned the renewed British presence after the expedition's unexpected return.
- Intended hostage whose removal was opposed by his household, chiefs, and an armed crowd.
- Figure known mainly through British journals and later reconstruction rather than a balanced Hawaiian archive.

## Qualifications

This profile rests on one popular-history episode based heavily on British accounts. It does not establish Kalaniʻōpuʻu's private beliefs about Cook, the exact political structure around him, whether the January gifts ended a ritual obligation, or whether resource pressure, festival timing, occupation fear, insult, and tactical concern had separable weights in his response. British renderings of names and speech require caution.
