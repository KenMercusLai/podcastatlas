# Erich von Falkenhayn

## Overview

Erich von Falkenhayn was the German chief of the General Staff who replaced [[HelmuthVonMoltkeTheYounger|Helmuth von Moltke the Younger]] after the Marne, pressed the late-1914 offensive around Ypres, and then judged that Germany could not obtain outright battlefield victory.

## Current Profile

[[597-the-first-world-war-the-massacre-of-the-innocents-part-4-glt9434623550]] introduces Falkenhayn during the transition from the failed opening offensive to the race north. He saw Ypres as a base for movement toward Dunkirk, Calais, and Boulogne, making the battle a struggle over the Channel ports and Britain's link to France.

The episode attributes repeated October-November pressure to Falkenhayn, including reinforcement with fresh infantry, heavy guns, and howitzers. Those assaults repeatedly endangered the [[FirstBattleOfYpres|Ypres]] defense but did not produce a breakthrough. Around 25 November he ordered western forces to hold ground and entrench.

The same source gives him a political-strategic afterlife beyond the battle. On 18 November he reportedly told Chancellor Bethmann Hollweg that Germany could not win outright by battlefield means and should pursue a separate peace with Russia or France, preferably Russia. The proposal was rejected, while eastern victories elevated Hindenburg and Ludendorff as rival German heroes.

## Key Characteristics

- Successor to Moltke after Germany's Marne setback.
- Advocate of taking Ypres as a route toward the Channel ports.
- Commander associated with repeated costly attacks during the First Battle of Ypres.
- User of manpower and heavy artillery concentration without securing a breakthrough.
- Ordered the western armies to entrench after the offensive failed.
- Recognized limits to outright military victory and proposed a separate political settlement.

Force ratios, responsibility for particular attacks, the precise wording and feasibility of his peace advice, and Bethmann Hollweg's reasons for rejection remain source-scoped.
