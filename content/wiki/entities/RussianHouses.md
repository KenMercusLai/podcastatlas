# Russian Houses

## Overview
Russian Houses are cultural centers that officially promote Russian language and culture abroad. The source places their African activities within a wider Russian influence effort while distinguishing ordinary cultural and educational functions from reported war messaging, religious activity, and allegations of military recruitment.

## Current Profile
In the episode's account, a Russian House in São Tomé and Príncipe offers cultural displays and language learning that can support study in Russia. Across the wider network, the centers may also screen films presenting Russia's account of the Ukraine war or its Sahel activity and host Orthodox services. Ukrainian intelligence alleges that some centers recruit Africans to fight for Russia, but the supplied summary provides no independent corroboration or center-level findings.

## Key Characteristics
- Provide cultural displays and Russian-language instruction.
- Can connect students to university study in Russia.
- Are described as venues for state-aligned films about war and security.
- May host Orthodox Christian services.
- Sit inside a broader media and political influence ecosystem rather than operating as a stand-alone channel.

## Qualifications
The network's centers, governance, programmes, audiences, and local partnerships may differ substantially. Cultural participation does not prove political agreement, and the source says most Africans neither consume Russian state media regularly nor attend Russian House events. Recruitment, propaganda effects, audience reach, and damage to social cohesion remain alleged or difficult to measure.
