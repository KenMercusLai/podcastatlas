# Gavrilo Princip

## Overview

Gavrilo Princip was the nineteen-year-old Bosnian Serb conspirator who fatally shot [[FranzFerdinand]] and [[SophieChotek|Sophie Chotek]] in Sarajevo on 28 June 1914.

## Current Profile

The earliest episode presents Princip as a poor Orthodox Bosnian Serb born in rural Obljaj in 1894. Schooling took him to Sarajevo at thirteen, where initial academic success gave way to unstable lodging, poverty, declining performance, and immersion in literature. Austro-Hungarian education expanded his opportunities while urban inequality, imperial rule, and a small radical student milieu gave his dislocation political language. [[DaniloIllich|Danilo Illich]] exposed him to revolutionary writing, and [[YoungBosnia|Young Bosnia]] offered a loose South Slav nationalist network rather than a single disciplined party.

Princip's politics were Yugoslav or South Slav as well as Serbian. He hated Austro-Hungarian rule, imagined Serbia as a base for South Slav unification, and drew on Kosovo sacrifice narratives. [[BogdanZerajic|Bogdan Zerajic]]'s failed attack on Bosnia's governor and suicide became a [[PoliticalMartyrEmulation|martyr model]]: Princip reportedly visited his grave and understood political violence as both liberation and self-transformation. Failed examinations and rejection as too weak for the First Balkan War intensified his need to prove himself, but the sources do not reduce his later choice to personal frustration.

Princip may have helped initiate the plot with Nedeljko Cabrinovic and Trifko Grabez, but the students depended on older Serbian nationalist networks. Milan Ciganovic trained them and connected them to pistols, bombs, cyanide, documents, couriers, and border assistance associated with [[BlackHand|the Black Hand]] milieu. Princip appears more disciplined than some companions, yet the journey also exposed amateur behavior and improvised planning.

On 28 June, the operation initially failed. After other conspirators did not act and Cabrinovic's bomb missed, Princip waited near Moritz Schiller's delicatessen. A route-communication failure brought the returning motorcade to his position, where the car stopped while correcting a wrong turn. He fired twice at close range, was prevented from killing himself, and later said Franz Ferdinand's reforms threatened South Slav union. His age spared him execution, but he died during a twenty-year prison sentence.

## Key Characteristics

- Rural Bosnian Serb student whose move to Sarajevo joined education and literary ambition to poverty, unstable lodging, and alienation.
- South Slav or Yugoslav nationalist whose anti-Habsburg politics exceeded a purely ethnic-Serb program.
- Participant in Young Bosnia's loose radical milieu and recipient of Danilo Illich's literary and revolutionary influence.
- Admirer of failed assassin Bogdan Zerajic who treated political murder as collective liberation, sacrifice, and personal self-transformation.
- Possible plot initiator who nevertheless depended on older Black Hand-linked intermediaries for operational capability.
- Beneficiary of an unplanned second opportunity after the plot's first phase failed.

- Network dependence: [[466-the-murder-of-franz-ferdinand-the-conspiracy-part-2-glt2060941792]] follows Ciganovic, Tankosic, weapons, training, cyanide, documents, couriers, and the border route.

## Qualifications

The sources are narrative reconstructions rather than a complete biography or forensic case file. Princip left limited reliable evidence, records were destroyed, and later anecdotes were embellished; his exact early motives, guest-house story, private psychology, and famous arrest photograph therefore require caution. The anonymous clipping, plot initiation, cafe locations, Black Hand ritual, exact command chain, intended victim of the second shot, immediate knowledge of the result, and a famous cell-wall verse also remain uncertain. Poverty, alienation, failed examinations, physical insecurity, and martyr aspiration qualify but do not replace his stated political motive, and neither motive makes every later Yugoslav commemoration a direct expression of his own views.

## What Changed

- Extended the profile to rural childhood, Sarajevo schooling, literary ambition, poverty, and unstable student life.
- Distinguished South Slav Yugoslav nationalism from a purely ethnic-Serb account of his politics.
- Identified Young Bosnia and Danilo Illich as the loose social and intellectual setting of his early radicalization.
- Strengthened Zerajic's role from general admiration to a concrete martyr script while preserving psychological uncertainty.
