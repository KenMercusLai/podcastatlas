# Gavrilo Princip

## Overview

Gavrilo Princip was the nineteen-year-old Bosnian Serb conspirator who fatally shot [[FranzFerdinand]] and [[SophieChotek|Sophie Chotek]] in Sarajevo on 28 June 1914.

## Current Profile

The conspiracy episode presents Princip as poor, bookish, resentful of Austro-Hungarian rule, and committed to a South Slav Yugoslavia. Rejected as physically weak when he tried to join the Balkan Wars, he sought significance through political violence and admired Bogdan Zerajic, a failed assassin whose grave he visited on the eve of the attack. His politics and personal self-mythology reinforced one another: he expected both to strike an imperial target and to die as a martyr.

Princip may have helped initiate the plot with Nedeljko Cabrinovic and Trifko Grabez, but the students depended on older Serbian nationalist networks. Milan Ciganovic trained them and connected them to pistols, bombs, cyanide, documents, couriers, and border assistance associated with [[BlackHand|the Black Hand]] milieu. Princip appears more disciplined than some companions, yet the journey also exposed amateur behavior and improvised planning.

On 28 June, the operation initially failed. After other conspirators did not act and Cabrinovic's bomb missed, Princip waited near Moritz Schiller's delicatessen. A route-communication failure brought the returning motorcade to his position, where the car stopped while correcting a wrong turn. He fired twice at close range, was prevented from killing himself, and later said Franz Ferdinand's reforms threatened South Slav union. His age spared him execution, but he died during a twenty-year prison sentence.

## Key Characteristics

- Young Bosnian Serb nationalist whose Yugoslav politics combined with alienation, physical insecurity, and a desire for martyrdom.
- Admirer of failed assassin Bogdan Zerajic who treated political murder as both collective action and personal self-transformation.
- Possible plot initiator who nevertheless depended on older Black Hand-linked intermediaries for operational capability.
- Trained marksman who crossed into Bosnia through a network of military, frontier, peasant, and nationalist assistance.
- Beneficiary of an unplanned second opportunity after the plot's first phase failed.
- Captured immediately after killing Franz Ferdinand and Sophie, then spared execution because he was nineteen.

## Qualifications

The sources are narrative reconstructions rather than a complete biography or forensic case file. The anonymous clipping, plot initiation, cafe locations, Black Hand ritual, exact command chain, intended victim of the second shot, Princip's immediate knowledge of the result, and a famous cell-wall verse remain uncertain. Personal insecurity and martyr aspiration qualify but do not replace his stated political motive, and neither motive makes every later Yugoslav commemoration a direct expression of his own views.

## What Changed

- Clarified that Franz Ferdinand's reform potential, not a reliable belief that he led a war party, made him a strategic target.
