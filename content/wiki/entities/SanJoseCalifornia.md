# San Jose, California

## Overview
San Jose is a large California city used by Mayor [[MattMahan|Matt Mahan]] as the operating case for his outcome-based approach to government.

## Current Profile
In the supplied interview, San Jose is less a general city profile than a claimed policy demonstration. Mahan says the city used public dashboards, explicit targets, lower-cost interim shelter, permitting changes, fee reductions, and program reallocation to improve safety, reduce unsheltered homelessness, and enable housing despite slower revenue growth.

The same account also exposes local constraints. San Jose carries substantial unfunded pension costs, must coordinate police, firefighters, social workers, housing approvals, and treatment capacity, and still has people who refuse low-barrier shelter. The source therefore presents the city as evidence of better management, not proof that urban housing, homelessness, and fiscal pressures are solved.

## Key Characteristics
- Municipal proof point for [[OutcomeBasedGovernmentAccountability]] through public dashboards, baselines, comparative metrics, and targets.
- Lower-cost shelter strategy using sleeping cabins rather than relying only on very expensive permanent units.
- Housing-supply approach emphasizing faster approvals, lower fees, and thousands of unblocked homes.
- Public-safety and homelessness strategy combining service offers with enforcement and possible compulsory treatment.
- Pension-constrained city whose general fund still bears legacy unfunded-liability costs.

## Evidence
- Performance claims: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-how-matt-mahan-thinks-he-can-save-california-40578730]] records Mahan's claims about safety, unsheltered homelessness, shelter beds, housing approvals, and permit speed.
- Cost and shelter model: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-how-matt-mahan-thinks-he-can-save-california-40578730]] contrasts roughly $85,000 sleeping cabins with permanent units he says can approach $1 million each.
- Fiscal constraint: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-how-matt-mahan-thinks-he-can-save-california-40578730]] says unfunded pension liabilities consume a significant share of the general fund and describes reforms for newer employees.

## Qualifications

The source does not provide consistent comparison cities, causal evaluations, resident perspectives, total-program costs, long-run housing outcomes, treatment outcomes, or a complete municipal budget. Statewide transferability is untested.
