## Overview

夏姬 / Xia Ji is the Spring-and-Autumn Zheng princess used in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-536-4-jiemi-zhaofeiyan-jiemei-wuming-yiyun-lo557jbq9ni-xissplybtcuraelm|Hanji 536-4]] as a comparative case for [[GenderedConsortScapegoating|红颜祸水式替罪叙事]]. She is distinct from the Qin figure [[XiaTaihou|夏姬 / 夏太后]] and therefore uses a semantic suffix in the wiki route.

## Current Profile

The source presents Xia Ji less as a fully recoverable biography than as a memory problem. She marries Chen official Xia Yushu, bears Xia Zhengshu, and after Xia Yushu's death becomes linked to Chen Ling Gong and two ministers. When the men drink in her house and mock Xia Zhengshu's parentage, Xia Zhengshu kills Chen Ling Gong, yet later narration remembers Xia Ji as the scandal center more than it remembers the male behavior that provoked the crisis.

The second half of the source's Xia Ji account follows Chu intervention and Wu Chen's moral performance. Chu Zhuang Wang wants Xia Ji, Wu Chen persuades him that taking her would make the war look lust-driven, and Wu Chen later blocks another Chu minister before arranging for Xia Ji to return to Zheng and then marrying her himself. The episode uses this sequence to argue that public moral language can conceal private desire.

## Key Characteristics

- Spring-and-Autumn Zheng princess remembered through a heavily sexualized and blame-laden historical afterlife.
- Figure around whom male ruler desire, ministerial rivalry, humiliation, and violence are narrated.
- Used by the source as a parallel to the Zhao sisters because later memory can overconcentrate male choices onto a woman's beauty.
- Connected to Wu Chen's public moral restraint and later private pursuit, making her case a moral-performance example.
- Source-scoped rather than fully adjudicated: the episode uses her story for historiographical analysis, not a complete biographical reconstruction.

## Evidence

- Marriage and family setting: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-536-4-jiemi-zhaofeiyan-jiemei-wuming-yiyun-lo557jbq9ni-xissplybtcuraelm|Hanji 536-4]] says Xia Ji is a Zheng princess married to Xia Yushu and mother of Xia Zhengshu.
- Chen court scandal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-536-4-jiemi-zhaofeiyan-jiemei-wuming-yiyun-lo557jbq9ni-xissplybtcuraelm|Hanji 536-4]] says Chen Ling Gong and two ministers became her lovers and mocked Xia Zhengshu's parentage while drinking at her house.
- Violence and blame displacement: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-536-4-jiemi-zhaofeiyan-jiemei-wuming-yiyun-lo557jbq9ni-xissplybtcuraelm|Hanji 536-4]] uses Xia Zhengshu's killing of Chen Ling Gong to show how later blame can settle on Xia Ji rather than on the men who created the insult.
- Wu Chen sequence: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-536-4-jiemi-zhaofeiyan-jiemei-wuming-yiyun-lo557jbq9ni-xissplybtcuraelm|Hanji 536-4]] says Wu Chen first blocks Chu Zhuang Wang and another Chu minister from taking Xia Ji, then arranges for Xia Ji to return to Zheng and later marries her himself.
- Historiographical use: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-536-4-jiemi-zhaofeiyan-jiemei-wuming-yiyun-lo557jbq9ni-xissplybtcuraelm|Hanji 536-4]] explicitly uses the Xia Ji case to explain how "荡妇" labels can absorb responsibility for male desire, political disorder, and rhetorical hypocrisy.

## Qualifications

This page is grounded in one popular-history episode. It does not reconstruct Xia Ji's full `Zuo Zhuan` record, the chronology of Chen and Chu politics, or every textual variant. The source's durable wiki value is its use of Xia Ji as a gendered-memory case, not a final court-history adjudication.
