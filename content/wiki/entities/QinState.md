# 秦国 / Qin State

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-99-3-xunzi-bingfa-li-cangzhe-renxing-de-mimi-lgfcp4hkruyhhrjguqhjbhllvwp43]] adds Qin as the strongest example inside [[Xunzi|荀子]]'s military hierarchy short of hegemonic and kingly armies. The episode treats Qin's strength as real and cumulative from [[QinXiaogong|秦孝公]] to [[QinZhaoxiangwang|秦昭襄王]]: harsh control, surveillance, reward, punishment, and military-merit incentives can produce formidable [[ProfitDrivenArmyLimits|profit-driven battlefield power]]. Xunzi's point is evaluative rather than dismissive: Qin's锐士 can outrank Qi技击 and Wei武卒 while still falling below [[XunzianMilitaryLegitimacy|renyi-based military legitimacy]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-99-4-shui-shi-kongzi-hou-zui-weidade-sixiangjia-lr4phoq9chc4tz0yb8y95gy21qdd]] adds Qin's post-Zhou consolidation and succession puzzle. After the [[ZhouRoyalHouse|周王室]] collapse, Zhou people flee east rather than accept Qin rule, while Qin carries Zhou treasures back to [[Xianyang|咸阳]]. The same source then records [[QinZhaoxiangwang|秦昭襄王]]'s death in 251 BCE and highlights that the six states send condolences instead of using the succession moment to attack, showing Qin's prestige or deterrence as the next explanatory problem.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-100-1-huangdi-zhong-de-chaochang-daiji-wang-guoshi-le-lt75scavlm8qjn6ulpd-b2kwzwjf]] answers that puzzle by adding Qin's late Zhaoxiang-era ritual hierarchy. The episode treats "天下来宾," punitive action against late [[WeiState|魏国]], a tianzi-like sacrifice at old Qin Yong city, and [[HanHuanhuiwang|韩桓惠王]]'s personal mourning as evidence of [[QuasiRoyalQinHegemony|准天子式秦霸权]]. Qin's dominance is therefore not only territorial; it is also visible in which states attend, apologize, mourn, and fear retaliation for failed protocol.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-101-1-zhe-wei-daomei-junwang-dengji-santian-ji-cusi-lll-bcc7basaf6-nzwqtmcb5qw6w]] moves Qin from late-Zhaoxiang hierarchy into the compressed Xiaowen-Zhuangxiang succession. It distinguishes [[QinXiaowenwang|安国君柱 / 秦孝文王]]'s earlier succession transition from formal 250 BCE accession, records his death after three days, and keeps [[LuBuwei|吕不韦]] murder suspicion unresolved. Qin's court then immediately elevates [[QinZhuangxiangwang|子楚 / 秦庄襄王]] and the two maternal lines of [[HuayangFuren|华阳夫人]] and [[XiaTaihou|夏姬 / 夏太后]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-104-yinmou-shisuan-rang-qinguo-bantu-you-you-you-pengzhang-le-lgnyhbduqutegtksrjwcvhmn8-t6]] adds Qin's early Ying Zheng capacity branch. Even while the young ruler depends on [[LuBuwei|吕不韦]] and internal unrest persists around Jinyang, Qin absorbs [[HanState|韩国]]'s hostile [[ZhengGuoCanal|郑国渠]] proposal because it also serves Guanzhong irrigation and war logistics. The episode makes Qin's rise depend on turning enemies' delaying tactics into durable state capacity, then shows renewed attack on [[WeiState|魏国]] as the external payoff.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-105-zhanzheng-huayang-zhaoguo-limu-zui-hui-shua-ljtqdg46a8o13zzsjv-p-fpol5oh]] keeps Qin's early Ying Zheng pressure active in 244 BCE. The source says Qin is suffering famine but still sends [[MengAo|蒙敖]] against [[HanState|韩国]], taking twelve cities. Qin's role here is a narrow but important continuity point: internal strain and resource scarcity do not stop targeted eastward campaigns when the opponent is weak enough.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-106-jingren-faxian-xinlingjun-de-feizhengchang-siwang-lsj3kd2tb4tqtuvhwvsdwkh-h5el]] extends that pressure through a harsher disaster setting. In 243 BCE, Qin faces "蝗、疫"; the episode expands this into locust-driven crop loss, hunger, and epidemic risk, then says Qin mobilizes private grain through [[RankSaleDisasterFinance|鬻爵赈灾融资]]. The following year Qin sends [[MengAo|蒙敖]] against [[WeiState|魏国]], takes twenty cities, and creates [[DongCommandery|东郡]], showing disaster response and conquest administration in the same sequence.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-107-zuihou-yici-hezong-faqin-fa-le-ge-jimo-lrzq1qks7z-oromd-5f-dgqjwjhw]] shows Qin surviving the final anti-Qin [[HezongAlliance|合纵]] campaign. The source preserves conflicting accounts over whether [[Chunshenjun|黄歇 / 春申君]] or [[PangNuan|庞煖]] led the coalition, but the Qin-facing result is stable: the eastern states gain nothing durable, and Qin continues pressure afterward by taking additional territory into [[DongCommandery|东郡]] and forcing [[ChuState|楚国]] to relocate to [[ShouchunWarringStates|寿春]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-108-1-lishi-gan-dangmian-dingzhuang-qinshihuang-diyi-ren-lg0dssyicp387foiraohkv1x5aij]] adds the next Qin sequence in 238 BCE. Externally, [[YangDuanhe|杨端和]] continues the Wei campaign until the episode says Qin's border reaches [[QiState|齐国]], narrowing any remaining practical basis for [[HezongAlliance|合纵]]. Internally, the state absorbs the [[LaoAi|嫪毐]] rebellion at [[YongchengQin|雍城]], the punishment of [[ZhaoJi|赵姬]], and [[MaoJiao|茅焦]]'s [[DeathDefyingRemonstrance|赴死式直谏]], making Qin's consolidation depend on court feedback as well as military pressure.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-110-1-lisi-micang-laoshu-he-xiashuidao-laoshu-ni-zenme-xuan-lmv-fw-kgsuokonssq8bkbfvdsb]] moves Qin into Ying Zheng's tenth year, 237 BCE, and shows a talent-policy backlash after internal consolidation. With [[LuBuwei|吕不韦]] removed from central office and foreign-origin figures such as [[LaoAi|嫪毐]] and [[ZhengGuoHydraulicEngineer|郑国]] available as cautionary examples, Qin royal-clan ministers push the [[GuestExpulsionOrder|逐客令]]. The episode makes Qin's old talent-absorption strength unstable: imported expertise and guest service can become suspect when court factions translate security anxiety into a purge of outsiders like [[LiSi|李斯]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-110-2-qinshihuang-de-cp-weihe-bei-quzhu-chuguo-lj0222vw8ijbvj25iwfzh-qhvzr9]] supplies the reversal and strategic payoff. [[LiSi|李斯]] uses [[JianZhuKeShu|《谏逐客书》]] to argue that Qin's own past greatness came from accepting guest talent under rulers such as [[QinMugong|秦穆公]], [[QinXiaogong|秦孝公]], [[YingSi|秦惠王 / 嬴驷]], and [[QinZhaoxiangwang|秦昭襄王]]. Qin therefore cancels the expulsion order and keeps a talent policy aligned with conquest, while the source's closing says Qin then combines bribery, ruler-minister alienation, targeted killing, and military attack against the remaining states.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-111-1-ruoda-de-zhaoguo-jing-wang-yu-yige-jinv-zhi-shou-lhrqb8-swlkuyoydgjyn8ldnlkzc]] gives the next field application of that conquest posture in 236 BCE. After [[ZhaoState|赵国]] attacks [[YanState|燕国]], a Yan envoy warns [[QinShiHuang|嬴政 / 秦始皇]] that a Zhao annexation of Yan would create a larger Qin problem. Qin then sends [[WangJianQin|王翦]], [[HuanYiQin|桓齮]], and [[YangDuanhe|杨端和]] against Zhao, turning [[WeakStateMutualPredation|weak-state mutual predation]] into another Qin opening.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-111-2-wo-yu-lvbuwei-de-gekong-duihua-ltpbkg1echouxwjyhsfax2swfzch]] gives the internal and diplomatic aftermath in 235 BCE. Internally, Qin finishes the Lu Buwei branch by forcing the former chancellor toward Shu and then punishing mourners after his suicide, turning old retainer networks into a security problem. Externally, the episode says drought does not stop war: Wei attacks Chu and Qin sends four armies to aid Wei, making late Qin power visible both in court cleanup and in choosing when to support one rival state against another.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-112-1-yaojia-shi-ruhe-ping-yizhangzui-po-siguo-hezong-lthw0fvvinmf6ghxpqut60agsueg]] turns that post-cleanup posture toward anti-coalition diplomacy. [[LiSi|李斯]] first pushes the "attack Han first" line, then helps convert [[YaoJia|姚贾]] from a [[ZhaoState|赵国]] envoy into a Qin asset. [[QinShiHuang|嬴政 / 秦始皇]] backs Yao Jia visibly with vehicles, money, clothing, and a sword, and the mission breaks a Zhao-Han-Wei-Chu [[HezongAlliance|合纵]] plan over three years through persuasion and bribery. Qin's strength here is institutional and diplomatic as much as military: it can absorb displaced talent, fund long envoy work, and turn rival-court incentives against coordination.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-112-2-qinshihuang-yeshi-tade-shumi-llkoy-zvhjjr6af9tflcw1xvogwz]] adds the adjacent military and intellectual setup. Militarily, [[HuanYiQin|桓齮]] devastates Zhao in 234 BCE, is defeated by [[LiMu|李牧]], and then renews attacks in 233 BCE. Strategically, Qin prepares to attack [[HanState|韩国]] first, while Ying Zheng's admiration for [[HanFei|韩非]]'s [[Gufen|《孤愤》]] and [[Wudu|《五蠹》]] creates a talent-attraction problem inside a conquest plan: Qin can want Han Fei's mind without ceasing to make Han its target.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-112-3-hanfei-yu-yaojia-lunzhan-qinwang-dang-caipan-ljxxlok4uh-ajjirmolybodlspv9]] makes Qin's late-conquest talent policy double-edged. The same court can reward [[YaoJia|姚贾]] after a successful bribery-and-persuasion mission and still destroy [[HanFei|韩非]] once [[LiSi|李斯]] reframes a foreign royal as a permanent security problem. Qin's advantage therefore includes talent absorption, but also a court environment where loyalty suspicion and ministerial rivalry can eliminate a useful mind without slowing conquest.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-113-limu-ruhe-yong-yiji-zhili-zu-qinguo-tongyi-luymqpcolsvyz5miodit3wdlx0ob]] shifts Qin from court removal and coalition sabotage into explicit state destruction. Qin receives land from [[HanState|韩国]] and [[WeiState|魏国]], captures [[HanAn|韩王安 / Han An]] in 230 BCE, and then attacks [[ZhaoState|赵国]] in 229 BCE after earthquake, famine, and drought have weakened it. Yet the source still makes [[LiMu|李牧]] the obstacle Qin avoids through indirect means: [[DunRuo|顿弱]]'s rumor work and bribery of [[GuoKai|郭开]] turn Zhao's own court against Li Mu and [[SimaShang|司马尚]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-114-1-jintian-liaoliao-yingzheng-dou-you-naxie-canren-shouduan-lmqky6g3qi9nklpdnye4lk5rrbz6]] gives Qin the next conquest payoff. With Li Mu and Sima Shang removed, [[WangJianQin|王翦]] defeats Zhao's replacement command, captures [[Handan|邯郸]], and takes [[ZhaoYoumiuWang|赵迁 / 赵幽缪王]] alive. [[QinShiHuang|嬴政 / 秦始皇]] then uses Handan as a personal-revenge site, while Zhao Qian's continued survival in exile weakens [[ZhaoJia|赵嘉]]'s ability to claim the Zhao title from [[DaiState|代地 / 代国]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-114-2-yanguo-cisha-qinshihuang-weihe-hui-xuan-jingke-lsewjrmgup1yanf8ipn-i0ykqwjx]] shows Qin's pressure working even when Qin is mostly offstage. Wang Jian's Yan-facing position, Qin's threat to punish sheltering [[FanWuji|樊无忌]], and the collapse of practical hezong timing push [[YanTaiziDan|燕太子丹]] toward [[WeakStateAssassinationStrategy|weak-state assassination]] as a substitute for direct resistance.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-115-1-jingke-ciqin-sihu-you-dianer-caoshuai-le-lgz1adsn1t8jjiaobmtfedpr-ixn]] makes Qin's late-conquest advantage the reason that shortcut is strategically limited. The source argues that even if [[JingKe|荆轲]] killed [[QinShiHuang|嬴政 / 秦始皇]], Qin might suffer court disorder like the crisis after [[QinWuwang|秦武王]]'s death but would still retain the institutional momentum built from [[ShangYangReforms|商鞅变法]], [[FarmingWarStatecraft|耕战逻辑]], and [[QinAdministrativeStandardization|administrative standardization]].

The same episode also shows Qin's court as both powerful and permeable. [[MengJia|蒙嘉]] can translate [[YanState|燕国]]'s surrender script, [[FanWuji|樊无忌]]'s head, and the [[DuKangMap|督亢地图]] into a high-ceremony audience, but that very ceremony overwhelms [[QinWuyang|秦武阳]]. Qin is therefore not merely a battlefield machine; its ritual center becomes the stage on which the weak-state assassination plan has to survive.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-115-2-qinwang-danan-busi-zhihou-ganle-jian-ni-buzhidao-de-shi-lidfjb6-nggubw8vpz5fvnfacrqu]] completes that hostile-access branch. The Qin court almost loses its ruler because envoy protocol, unarmed ministers, outside guards, and Ying Zheng's long sword create a short window for [[JingKe|荆轲]]. Once Ying Zheng survives and counters, Qin converts palace shock into eastward war: more troops are sent into the former Zhao area, [[WangJianQin|王翦]] attacks [[YanState|燕国]], and the Yan-Dai force under [[ZhaoJia|赵嘉]] is defeated west of the Yi River.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-116-shishang-zuican-taizi-bei-fuwang-qinshou-zhansha-lthruqxtd4iqofdxzvim58ph8rlk]] extends that conversion from retaliation to terminal conquest logic. Qin takes Ji, pushes [[YanWangXi|燕王喜]] and [[YanTaiziDan|燕太子丹]] toward Liaodong, and does not stop when Dan is killed as an appeasement payment. The source then turns Qin's attention south: [[WangBenQin|王贲]] attacks [[ChuState|楚国]], while Ying Zheng chooses [[LiXinQin|李信]] and [[MengTian|蒙恬]] after Li Xin's lower troop estimate displaces [[WangJianQin|王翦]]'s more cautious 600,000-person requirement.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-117-1-papa-dalian-lishishang-shuangwen-de-kaishan-bizu-lr0h-kfeguvatattnj-mz8fgvnc]] shows Qin's conquest machine operating on a parallel Wei front. [[WangBenQin|王贲]] attacks [[WeiState|魏国]], uses water against [[Daliang|大梁]], and destroys Wei after [[WeiWangJia|魏王假]] surrenders. The same episode then contrasts Qin's talent-use tradition with Wei's talent loss and shifts into the [[TangJuEnvoy|唐雎]] story, where Qin power is rhetorically checked even though the historical details remain doubtful.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-117-2-xuezhedian-qinchao-wujiang-jingran-ruci-gaoqingshang-lsyw3eitcnzfexl1l6vfh2gbtaoy]] shows that Qin's conquest machine can still suffer a severe operational correction. [[LiXinQin|李信]] and [[MengTian|蒙恬]] initially advance against [[ChuState|楚国]], but [[XiangYan|项燕]] defeats them near [[NanCommandery|南郡]]. Qin then recovers by having [[QinShiHuang|嬴政 / 秦始皇]] return to [[WangJianQin|王翦]]'s 600,000-person requirement, while Wang Jian manages the danger of commanding so much state force through [[GreedPersonaSelfProtection|贪财人设式自保]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-118-1-wangjian-lv-liushiwan-dajun-duizhan-xiangyan-haoxi-kaichang-le-lsf2puinwg0m3bgyi25znof7aznu]] turns that recovery into a logistical campaign. Qin's 600,000-person army does not simply crush Chu on contact; it can sit behind fortifications, remain fed, and wait for [[ChuState|楚国]]'s mobilized army to exhaust provisions and morale. Qin's advantage is therefore scale plus endurance, and [[FortifiedAttritionPatience|坚壁耗敌式耐心]] becomes another expression of state capacity.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-118-2-chi-gua-le-wangshi-de-gua-bao-tian-lns7jt-kbv3qunm66hzltdgugbco]] turns the same Chu endpoint into a loyalty and administration problem. [[ChangpingJun|昌平君]] is read as a Qin chancellor whose [[PreQinSurnameClanIdentity|Chu royal identity]] helps explain distrust, exile, and rebellion, while Qin's post-victory setup of a temporary Chu commandery becomes a [[ConquestCommanderyTransition|灭国后的郡县化过渡]] case. Qin's conquest capacity is therefore military, personnel-screening, and administrative at once.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-119-1-chaijie-jingke-ciqin-bi-bai-de-mimi-lnonhw9tlsu5dudu8750zvgeaz1p]] adds the late Yan endpoint in 222 BCE. Qin sends [[WangBenQin|王贲]] against Liaodong, captures [[YanWangXi|燕王喜]], and extinguishes [[YanState|燕国]] after the earlier [[JingKe|荆轲]] attempt and [[YanTaiziDan|燕太子丹]] killing have failed to divert Qin's objective. The source therefore strengthens the page's conquest-machine theme: Qin is not satisfied by revenge or appeasement when the strategic goal is state destruction and incorporation.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-119-3-lishishang-di-yi-wei-zhenzheng-yiyishang-chuilian-tingzheng-taihou-liq9d3itmbar00ij43zzed-mulul]] pushes Qin to the last stage before Qi. In the north, [[WangBenQin|王贲]] uses the now-controlled Yan region to attack [[DaiState|代国]] and capture [[ZhaoJia|赵嘉]]. In the south, [[WangJianQin|王翦]] pacifies Jiangnan, subdues [[Baiyue|百越]], and establishes [[KuaijiCommandery|会稽郡]]. The episode's Qi analysis shows the other side of Qin power: long reassurance of distant [[QiState|齐国]] works under [[YuanjiaoJingongStrategy|远交近攻]] until the remaining targets have disappeared.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-120-1-shui-shi-huangdi-zhong-de-mabaonan-lhw8vsj6ta6rjdkugcvfohaqqxc0]] completes the state-conquest arc. In 221 BCE, Qin uses Wang Ben's northern route to force [[QiState|齐国]] into surrender, creating the transition from the [[WarringStatesPeriod|战国时期]] to the Qin imperial order. The source then shifts to Qin's need to govern memory: the new regime portrays each conquered state as the oath-breaker, rebel, aggressor, assassin sponsor, or conspirator, making Qin's expansion legible as [[ConquestSelfDefenseNarrative|兼并自卫叙事]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-120-2-qinchao-di-yi-tao-vi-sheji-linian-lno-aeljks2vt1k3wjrizsanirtq]] adds the first symbolic design layer after that conquest endpoint. Qin does not only absorb territory; it standardizes sovereignty language around "皇帝," "制," "诏," and the exclusive imperial "朕," abolishes the old [[PosthumousNamePolitics|谥号]] mechanism in favor of numbered emperors, and builds a water-virtue package through [[YinYangFivePhasesPoliticalTheory|阴阳五行政治理论]]. Black color, October year-start, number six, 德水 naming, and 黔首 terminology become state-level signs of the new order.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-120-3-jiemi-qinshihuang-weihe-faming-junxianzhi-li4bzz54trstteapodnonxzdzkuv]] adds the territorial settlement behind that symbolic order. [[QinWangWan|王绾]] proposes using imperial sons to govern distant former Yan, Qi, and Chu lands, but [[LiSi|李斯]] argues that Qin should avoid recreating the Zhou pattern of hereditary lords drifting into war. Qin therefore makes the commandery-county system empire-wide, rewarding relatives and officials through state revenues rather than sovereign fiefs; in the source's long view, this is the institutional branch behind "百代皆行秦政法."

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-120-4-bing-liuguo-hou-qinshihuang-jiao-ni-ruhe-shou-jiangshan-lgwsd0c3wkl-cphma8sm3ftkowwr]] adds what Qin then does to make that empire harder to reverse. It collects weapons and casts them into palace figures at [[Xianyang|咸阳]], standardizes measurement instruments and inspections, relocates 120,000 powerful households to the capital, and uses direct local government to push Qin-style order into conquered society. The source therefore turns Qin's post-unification profile from symbolic and territorial design into a full [[ImperialHomogenization|imperial homogenization]] program backed by [[PostConquestDisarmament|disarmament]] and [[EliteRelocationControl|elite relocation]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-121-yingzheng-zao-shijie-zuizao-gaosu-gonglu-2200nian-huangcao-busheng-ls1u8dmuvs5bbrzadf2dtd00js-p]] adds the old-land and infrastructure sequel. Qin Shi Huang's westward route through [[LongxiCommandery|陇西郡]] and [[BeidiCommandery|北地郡]] is read as a return into Qin's old ritual geography for [[HighTempleRite|高庙礼]] at [[YongchengQin|雍城]], while [[QinJimiao|极庙]], [[Taiyi|太一]], [[QinCosmicCapitalPlanning|秦代宇宙化都城规划]], and [[QinChidao|驰道]] show Qin converting victory into roads, ritual architecture, and capital-centered cosmic order.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-122-1-jiemi-qinshihuang-fengchan-weishenme-xuan-taishan-lkcyayuqrtsgy-k4bhdvy-l-lapo]] adds the eastern sacred-geography counterpart. Qin no longer only displays order from [[Xianyang|咸阳]] or old Qin lands; it carries imperial presence into former six-state territory and uses [[Taishan|泰山]] to claim [[MandateOfHeavenLegitimacy|天命合法性]]. The source also preserves a Qin-versus-Qi-Lu ritual tension: Qin summons scholars from the eastern learned world, but their disagreement leaves Qin's own heaven-sacrifice procedure as the practical ritual template for [[FengshanRitualLegitimacy|封禅礼制合法性]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-122-2-ribenren-daodi-shi-bushi-xufu-de-houdai-lhgbbhg3zw8rlx1jjb7lpb5ihihh]] gives that eastern claim a coastal and textual sequel. Qin absorbs former Qi sacred geography by sacrificing to the [[QiEightSpirits|齐地八神]], turns [[LangyaMountain|琅琊山]] into a settlement-and-inscription site, and has the [[LangyaInscriptionPoliticalProgram|琅琊刻石政治纲领]] restate agriculture, unified standards, law, peace, and imperial reach. The same source shows a risk inside Qin's sacred-geographic expansion: Yan-Qi fangshi can translate the emperor's sea-facing hopes into [[XuFu|徐福]]'s costly [[Penglai|蓬莱]] mission.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-122-3-qinshihuang-jing-he-shenxian-si-qilai-le-ljnw5estg6dvgfllm3kfcq6ulhxu]] adds the return-route side of Qin's post-unification tour. At [[Xuzhou|彭城 / 徐州]], Qin cannot recover [[Jiuding|九鼎]] despite ritual preparation and mass search labor; at [[XiangshanShrine|湘山祠]], Qin Shi Huang converts a river-wind obstruction into punishment of a Chu sacred landscape. This makes Qin's imperial order look uneven across conquered cultures: former-Qi gods can be worshiped and absorbed, while a Chu deity's resistance is answered through visible domination.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-123-1-shen-zhi-yuyan-wangqin-zhe-hu-jing-shi-zhi-lg7m3vfdbcweb4joxe4jvhxjx6y8]] shows Qin's post-unification order facing two pressures at once. Internally, [[ZhangLiang|张良]]'s [[Bolangsha|博浪沙]] attack reveals that destroyed-state memory can still produce targeted resistance even after [[PostConquestDisarmament|weapon seizure]]. Administratively, Qin Shi Huang's order to remove large walls and levees extends [[ImperialHomogenization|imperial homogenization]] into the built environment. Externally, [[LuSheng|卢生]]'s "亡秦者胡" report gives a prophecy frame for sending [[MengTian|蒙恬]] north against [[Xiongnu|匈奴]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-123-2-gudai-shiqi-de-daochamen-daodi-you-duo-weixian-lgcknwubwm3uzqmzgbgdlxlvcwxy]] explains why Qin continues war after the old interstate field has been absorbed. The episode argues that [[ShangYangReforms|商鞅变法]], [[FarmingWarStatecraft|耕战逻辑]], and the [[MilitaryMeritRankSystem|军功爵制]] left Qin with a reward-seeking military society that could not simply be told to rest. Southern war against [[Baiyue|百越]] drafts捕亡人,赘婿, and merchants under [[MarginalStatusConscription|边缘身份征发]], then becomes [[GuilinCommandery|桂林郡]], [[NanhaiCommandery|南海郡]], and [[XiangCommandery|象郡]]; northern war against [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] lets Meng Tian recover territory, create counties, link walls, and hold [[Shangjun|上郡]]. Qin's post-unification state therefore keeps converting social pressure into frontier administration.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-124-fenshu-kengru-wo-zai-bzhan-wei-qinshihuang-pingfan-ljnhwcouqu35rmvb-cnrzyzj2j6l]] adds Qin's textual-control layer in 213 BCE. After [[ChunyuYue|淳于越]] uses antiquity to argue against direct rule, [[LiSi|李斯]] frames private scholars, private histories, and classical criticism as threats to central law and unity. Qin's [[QinBookBurningPolicy|book-burning policy]] therefore becomes another post-unification control instrument alongside commanderies, measurements, elite relocation, disarmament, roads, and frontier campaigns: the state now tries to govern what counts as legitimate learning and political judgment through [[OfficialLearningMonopoly|学在王官式知识垄断]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-125-2-lishishang-qinshihuang-weihe-yao-fenshu-kengru-lulaivzp1xqs71nbaltr-ygpjihw]] completes the separate坑儒 branch in 212 BCE. The source ties Qin's late imperial violence to the failed immortal-search economy, [[HouShengFangshi|侯生]] and [[LuSheng|卢生]]'s flight, [[CourtFeedbackCollapse|君臣反馈失灵]], interrogation in [[Xianyang|咸阳]], and a mass punishment publicized as deterrence. It therefore adds [[QinKengruIncident|秦代坑儒事件]] as a political-violence endpoint of late Qin control rather than as a simple continuation of [[QinBookBurningPolicy|焚书]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-125-1-qinshihuang-xiujian-shishang-zuida-lanweilou-afanggong-lhqc6jw4jzoyrg7jzc2mbxdebj4]] adds Qin's late imperial-construction and palace-secrecy layer in 212 BCE. [[QinZhidao|秦直道]], [[AfangPalace|阿房宫]], [[QinShiHuangMausoleum|秦始皇陵]], material transport, palace networks, and forced relocation all show a state still able to mobilize enormous resources. The same source makes that capacity politically brittle through [[ImperialConstructionOverload|帝国工程过载]] and [[ImperialItinerarySecrecy|帝王行踪保密]]: overlapping projects strain labor and succession timing, while immortal-seeking advice turns palace geography into lethal information control.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-126-qinshihuang-siyin-zhimi-2-ln15p5qb7lc20goni1ibcpmn3wca]] adds the state-action side of the 211 BCE omen crisis. After "祖龙死" and "游徙吉," Qin can still mobilize population and rank incentives quickly: thirty thousand households are sent toward the northern frontier, where the episode reads the target as 北河 and 榆中 rather than a migration out of "河北." The same source therefore makes late Qin state capacity serve both [[QinFrontierBufferManagement|frontier management]] and [[OmenCountermeasureMobility|omen-countermeasure mobility]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-127-3-haiwai-huigui-yipi-zhujian-jiekai-huhai-lidi-zhi-mi-lja0pankcx4ugtylbxxqkwuavdnw]] adds Qin's succession-source conflict after that final tour. In the received account, state capacity is turned toward concealment, forged written command, and fast return; in the [[ZhaoZhengShu|《赵正书》]] account, the same distance from [[Xianyang|咸阳]] becomes an argument for [[EmergencySuccessionLegitimation|临终就近立储]]. The episode therefore makes Qin's late state order look both highly capable and institutionally fragile: a ruler-centered empire can move people, roads, documents, and mourning rituals, but still lacks a succession procedure immune to source conflict and factional capture.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-128-1-buxiang-cuanwei-de-taijian-bushi-ge-hao-jianchen-lh23xj9qxvubj5rlfa9fxmddgbiu]] adds the first-year consequence of that fragility. [[QinErshi|胡亥 / 秦二世]] can imitate Qin Shi Huang's inspection-tour form and extend old inscriptions, but the episode says the act lacks the founding ruler's earned authority and adds burden. Back in Xianyang, [[ZhaoGao|赵高]] turns Hu Hai's fear of royal brothers and old ministers into harsh-law elimination, so Qin's late legal and administrative machinery begins consuming its own imperial family and former pillars through [[SuccessionLegitimacyPurge|继位合法性清洗]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-128-2-qilai-bei-yapo-de-renmen-lpx9-iicwog4nj-nudaj-i4ajb25]] turns that internal damage into the first open mass revolt. The source says Hu Hai's rule intensifies harsh law, revived [[AfangPalace|阿房宫]] construction, capital-guard recruitment, and commandery transport burdens until social pressure becomes "官逼民反." When rain makes a nine-hundred-person conscript unit miss its deadline at [[DazexiangUprising|大泽乡]], Qin's context-blind punishment regime creates a [[FatalDeadlineRebellionTrigger|误期死局式起义触发]], and [[ChenSheng|陈胜]] and [[WuGuang|吴广]] turn desperation into anti-Qin rebellion through [[RebelMandateManufacture|起义天命制造]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-129-2-lishishang-hejiu-neng-wu-duo-da-shi-zhuan-gei-jiugui-laogong-kan-zhe-ji-lvzxpoxclwdrr66bwejd-bga8dp]] shows Qin fighting collapse through both coercive recovery and worsening political incentives. In the field, [[ZhangHanQin|章邯]] defeats [[TianZang|田臧]] and other Zhang Chu commanders after [[WuGuang|吴广]]'s death, proving Qin can still exploit rebel fragmentation. At court, [[LiSi|李斯]] answers [[QinErshi|胡亥 / 秦二世]]'s blame with [[DuzeExtractionPolitics|督责式压榨政治]], encouraging tax extraction and killing as official performance and further eroding the regime's social base.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-130-4-ruhe-dinglun-qinxiang-lisi-de-xiachang-ljnz0vflgr62hyktrg5cigz7c-7x]] adds the next court-side failure. [[ZhaoGao|赵高]] turns Hu Hai's palace seclusion into [[PalaceAccessMonopoly|宫廷接触垄断]], then uses [[LiYou|李由]]'s [[SanchuanWarringStates|三川]] office and rebel rumors to isolate [[LiSi|李斯]]. When Li Si, [[FengQuji|冯去疾]], and [[FengJieQin|冯劫 / 冯杰]] advise stopping [[AfangPalace|阿房宫]] and easing burdens, Hu Hai treats the advice as ministerial failure and imprisons them. Qin therefore retains offices and memorial forms while losing the ability to convert elite warning into state repair.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-102-1-lvbuwei-shi-jiangziya-houren-lveg-wsmggroaxjsnxopaevexqod]] gives Qin Zhuangxiang Wang's first-year state actions. Qin appoints [[LuBuwei|吕不韦]] as chancellor, uses him to destroy [[EasternZhouPolity|东周国]] after its anti-Qin plotting, and rewards him as Wenxin Hou. Qin also sends [[MengAo|蒙敖]] against [[HanState|韩国]], takes Chenggao, and establishes [[SanchuanWarringStates|三川]] commandery, so the succession payoff immediately appears as both court staffing and eastward administrative consolidation.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-12-gongsunyang-qiuzhi-lu-shang-de-la-guanxi-zou-houmen-lvv4-bz8sm9scezci-o-msapkvil]] adds Qin's recruitment-turn between accession pressure and institutional reform. [[QinXiaogong|秦孝公]] invokes [[QinMugong|秦穆公]]'s old greatness, names Qin's disorder and lost western lands as shame, and promises office plus land to anyone who can strengthen the state. [[ShangYang|公孙鞅 / 商鞅]] then enters through [[JingJian|景监]], showing that Qin's openness to talent still runs through [[PrivateAccessTalentChannel|private access]] before it becomes [[ShangYangReforms|商鞅变法]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-13-gongsunyang-keke-bianfa-re-shashen-1-lgmjapc2eecefbdshnpenzfcv6ga]] adds Qin's institutional reform branch under [[QinXiaogong|秦孝公]]. [[ShangYang|公孙鞅 / 商鞅]] receives the ruler's backing, defeats [[GanLong|甘龙]]'s custom-based objection, and begins [[ShangYangReforms|商鞅变法]] through household registration, mutual surveillance, agricultural incentives, severe punishment, [[MilitaryMeritRankSystem|军功爵制]], and [[XimuLixin|徙木立信]]. The episode turns Qin from a rising military pressure into a state remaking its social order for war and control.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-13-gongsunyang-keke-bianfa-re-shashen-2-lk-d9uibhneerfol6z2rrle-6ypm]] shows the first major public enforcement test of that remade order. [[YingSi|太子嬴驷]]'s violation is handled through punishment of the prince's tutors, linking [[GongziQian|公子虔]] and [[GongsunJia|公孙贾]] to a [[HighStatusLawEnforcementSignal|high-status law-enforcement signal]]. The source says this produces visible obedience by the next day and, over ten years, a Qin society that is orderly, publicly war-ready, and privately restrained.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-17-hanzhaohou-he-shenbuhai-lghjmjhq9qs4se96c5ofgz6caqof]] gives Qin the next paired military and administrative turn. Qin under [[ShangYang|公孙鞅 / 商鞅]] presses Wei at [[Anyi|安邑]] and [[Guyang|固阳]], pushing Wei toward the [[ZhangshuiAlliance|漳水之盟]] with Zhao and accommodation with Qi. In 350 BCE the same reform branch moves Qin's capital to [[Xianyang|咸阳]], enforces [[HouseholdSeparationLaw|分居令]], and expands [[QinAdministrativeStandardization|county, land, and measurement standardization]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-18-zhongguo-lishishang-di-yi-li-rentoushui-lgqnequk0rxejqgihwdqxqyqnsvq]] adds Qin's fiscal and status branch. [[ShangYang|公孙鞅 / 商鞅]]'s 348 BCE tax reform is read as likely [[PollTax|人头税]], while [[ZhouRoyalHouse|周王室]]'s 343 BCE [[ZhouHegemonRecognition|recognition of Qin hegemon status]] elevates [[QinXiaogong|秦孝公]] among the lords. Qin Xiao Gong does not attend personally; [[GongziShaoguan|公子少官]] appears with troops at [[Fengze|逢泽]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-22-gongsunyang-zha-wei-gongzi-ang-lss1l7lojfwgwdtcje7m5ijodpxi]] gives Qin a direct 340 BCE offensive branch. Shang Yang advises Qin Xiao Gong that [[WeiState|魏国]]'s post-Maling weakness is an opening for Qin to seize western advantage and push east. The campaign also shows Qin's [[FarmingWarStatecraft|耕战逻辑]] in action: war is not only foreign policy, but the reward channel that makes an empty-handed withdrawal improbable under the [[MilitaryMeritRankSystem|军功爵制]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-23-weihuiwang-hui-buyong-gongshu-zhi-yan-lveaqklo7oncmqudgmdk7l03qhsf]] adds the payoff and succession cost. Qin turns [[GongziAng|公子卬 / 公子昂]]'s capture into battlefield victory, receives Hexi territory, rewards [[ShangYang|公孙鞅 / 商鞅]] with [[Shangyu|商於]], and then loses [[QinXiaogong|秦孝公]]. After [[YingSi|嬴驷]] succeeds, the same Qin legal order that strengthened the state becomes the setting for [[GongziQian|公子虔]]'s accusation and Shang Yang's [[LegalistSelfBindingBackfire|作法自毙]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-24-shangyang-yue-wo-za-lie-le-lt4yd6pwlezqia5yljg-gankyhut]] reframes Qin as the place where outsider talent can rise quickly and become trapped. [[QinMugong|秦穆公]]'s recognition of [[BailiXi|百里奚]] supplies an earlier successful model, while [[QinXiaogong|秦孝公]]'s backing lets Shang Yang exceed the old restoration ideal. The same episode stresses that Qin's harsh reform order and accumulated resentment make Shang Yang's exit problem inseparable from Qin's state-building success.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-25-zhaoliang-dui-shangyang-shuo-ni-bupei-lnqmwjbrc9qomwrdt5brourf-78p]] adds Qin as the society whose response decides a minister's safety. [[ZhaoLiang|赵良]] contrasts Qin's mourning for [[BailiXi|百里奚]] with Qin's resentment toward [[ShangYang|公孙鞅 / 商鞅]], then treats Shang Yang's guard dependence as evidence that Qin's reform success did not produce [[PeopleBasedPoliticalSecurity|stable social support]] for its architect.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-27-weiguan-weihuiwang-he-qiweiwang-shi-za-shangye-hupeng-lminlxhrm5bethub9kyy-8blimrb]] extends Qin's pressure into 335 BCE. Qin attacks [[HanState|韩国]] and takes [[YiyangWarringStates|宜阳]], which the episode describes as a long-coveted strategic target comparable to Wei's Hexi lands. The notice keeps [[QinEastwardPressure|Qin eastward pressure]] active even as the episode's main focus shifts to Qi-Wei diplomacy.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-30-suqin-de-kaigua-rensheng-lkmn-fruukdxgkddx7zb4lc3o9bw]] adds Qin as a failed stop in [[SuQin|苏秦]]'s renewed lobbying itinerary. Su Qin approaches [[YingSi|秦惠文王]], but the episode says Qin has just executed [[ShangYang|公孙鞅 / 商鞅]] and is not receptive to wandering persuaders. Qin also remains the distant threat in Su Qin's later argument that [[YanState|燕国]] needs [[ZhaoState|赵国]] as a protective shield.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-31-hezong-lianheng-weiou-qin-lncpgmxrojc0y1meg9a3ul-o9nih]] makes Qin the active enemy around which [[HezongAlliance|合纵]] forms. Qin's attack on [[WeiState|魏国]], capture of [[LongJia|龙甲]], and seizure of Diaoyin force Su Qin to stabilize the western threat by sending [[ZhangYiStrategist|张仪]] into Qin. The same episode introduces [[LianhengStrategy|连横]] as the Qin-facing alternative that Su Qin attacks rhetorically even while using Zhang Yi's rise at Qin for tactical cover.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-33-lishi-diyi-shuangwen-nanzhujiao-suqin-lvebjyhvzctjgaevwwoddpvg9nr0]] adds Qin as the state allegedly checked by Su Qin's completed alliance. [[Shiji|《史记》]] says Qin troops did not dare leave [[HanguPass|函谷关]] for fifteen years, but the episode marks this as a claim to test against the coming [[ZizhiTongjian|《资治通鉴》]] account of renewed Qin action and alliance collapse.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-34-qinguo-zongli-ruhe-wanzhuan-liuguo-lsenzwp0kr5wscb0btvuzmkibriq]] supplies that sequel. Qin uses [[GongsunYan|犀首公孙衍]] to disturb the Qi-Wei-Zhao side of [[HezongAlliance|合纵]], keeps taking or extracting Wei territory from [[Yinjin|殷晋]] through [[Shaoliang|少梁]], [[HexiWarringStates|河西]], [[Jiaoyi|焦邑]], and [[QuwoWarringStates|曲沃]], then lets [[ZhangYiStrategist|张仪]] turn the return of [[PuyangWarringStates|蒲阳]] into [[Shangjun|上郡]]'s fifteen counties. Qin's strength here is diplomatic as well as military: it can alternate force, concessions, hostage signaling, and psychological pressure.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-35-lishishang-diyi-qibing-jiaofu-shi-ta-lnxgnu1czkl-ftfb3gq9kzxwnxdk]] widens Qin's problem from the eastern Wei front to its western/rear frontier. Qin defeats [[YiquState|义渠]], occupies part of its territory, and incorporates captured land into Qin's county system, but the episode stresses that Yiqu is not yet destroyed. The same source resolves Zhouji 34's closing question by reading Qin's return of [[Jiaoyi|焦邑]] and [[QuwoWarringStates|曲沃]] to [[WeiState|魏国]] as [[ZhangYiStrategist|张仪]]'s half-pull, half-hit diplomacy before [[YingSi|秦惠文王]] claims kingly status.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-36-weisijun-de-diwang-zhishu-lt5cpdm9yqdj4erwykd0kubfpucz]] gives Qin the kingly-title payoff. [[YingSi|秦惠文王]] claims royal title in 324 BCE, and the episode says the move causes less shock than [[XuzhouMutualKingship|徐州相王]] because the precedent exists and Qin's strength makes resistance less attractive. Qin also functions as the great-power priority in [[WuxiaWarringStates|武夏]]'s later analogy to [[LiangHuiWang|魏惠王 / 梁惠王]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-37-suqin-liuguo-jituan-ceo-de-bukan-qingshi-fj2kdqlaxzyy2nqtcutktuwrq5fo]] keeps Qin active in the same 324-323 BCE frame. [[ZhangYiStrategist|张仪]] attacks [[WeiState|魏国]], takes [[ShanchengWarringStates|陕城]], and returns the people while keeping the place, making Qin's pressure selective and territorial. The following year Zhang Yi meets Qi and Chu ministers at [[Niesang|啮桑]], possibly to improve Qin's diplomatic room or to counter [[GongsunYan|犀首公孙衍]], who has left Qin for Wei and is now organizing against Qin.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-38-gao-jianzhi-de-zhangyi-bu-yiban-lobhcpz679psy54k0iduccqkfcrn]] adds Qin as the beneficiary of Zhang Yi's ambiguous move to Wei. Through a [[Zhanguoce|《战国策》]] supplement, [[QinWuwang|秦武王]] accepts Zhang Yi's argument that sending him to [[WeiState|魏国]] can draw [[QiState|齐国]] into conflict and give Qin room to move. In the annalistic notice, when Wei refuses Zhang Yi's proposed Qin-facing posture, Qin attacks and takes [[QuwoWarringStates|曲沃]] and [[PingzhouWarringStates|平州]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-39-lishishang-zhangyi-shi-zenyang-nudui-qiangdi-de-lv2gc2wtcxuhugfgixk7rbbhpa6x]] shows Qin's influence working through personnel psychology as well as attack. [[YingSi|秦惠文王]] secretly rewards [[ZhangYiStrategist|张仪]] despite the failed Wei mission, creating [[GraceDebtLoyaltyPressure|恩赏负债式忠诚压力]] that pulls Zhang Yi's allegiance back toward Qin. The episode also says Zhang Yi secretly coordinates with Qin against [[WeiState|魏国]], making the Wei chief-minister role another channel for Qin-facing pressure.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-45-yi-shi-wei-jian-junwang-daodi-gai-bu-gai-sharen-chengxing-lo3cok7cfwzrxesp-i8miz1fc1db]] adds two early 周纪三 Qin pressure points. In 319 BCE Qin attacks [[HanState|韩国]] and takes Yanling. In 318 BCE, five eastern states reach [[HanguPass|函谷关]] against Qin, but Qin cuts the Chu army's grain route and triggers the coalition's withdrawal. The episode therefore keeps Qin as the state strong enough to generate [[HezongAlliance|合纵]] while also exposing [[CoalitionSelfPreservationFailure|联盟自保失灵]] among its opponents.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-46-ma-ni-shi-ge-erbaiwu-de-lishi-laiyuan-lkz5hiamaojtvanllbunecy3g3ff]] gives Qin the counterattack sequel in 317 BCE. Under [[Chulizi|樗里子]], Qin pursues [[HanState|韩国]] and [[WeiState|魏国]] after the failed Hangu Pass campaign, wins heavily at [[XiuyuBattle|修鱼之战]], and continues fighting at [[ZhuozeBattle|浊泽]]. The source makes Qin's strength feel less like passive defense behind a pass and more like retaliatory reach into the central plains.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-48-junwang-weisha-buneng-chuanwei-zaixiang-lnwhljnn888mzlj7let2ptn1pcdm]] adds the 314 BCE multi-front notices in Zhou Nan Wang's first year. Qin attacks [[YiquState|义渠]] and takes twenty-five cities, suppresses a source-scoped Wey/Quwo unrest notice by taking [[QuwoWarringStates|曲沃]], and defeats [[HanState|韩国]] at [[AnmenWarringStates|岸门]], after which Han sends crown prince [[HanXiangwang|韩仓 / 韩襄王]] to Qin as hostage. The cluster keeps Qin's eastward pressure linked to rear-frontier instability and nearby hostage diplomacy.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-50-zhangyi-shi-zenyang-ba-chuhuaiwang-huyou-que-de-loxg3ylhmskvdm-axtco5y-acege]] gives Qin a 313 BCE two-track move. [[Chulizi|樗里子]] attacks [[ZhaoState|赵国]] and captures Lingcheng's defender, while [[YingSi|秦惠文王]] sends [[ZhangYiStrategist|张仪]] to break [[ChuState|楚国]] away from [[QiState|齐国]] before Qin attacks Qi. Qin's pressure here is diplomatic as well as military: the promised [[Shangyu|商於]] land isolates Chu first, then the six-li reversal turns Chu's anger into a separate Qin-Chu conflict.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-51-chuhuaiwang-qin-xiao-er-kan-jiujiu-ruhe-mie-le-ni-ltfkdlivomnvux5xodh2rlfqf6ac]] records the military payoff of that conflict in 312 BCE. Qin defeats Chu at [[DanyangBattle|丹阳之战]], captures [[QuGai|屈匄]], takes [[Hanzhong|汉中]], then draws Chu deeper through [[Wuguan|武关]] and [[Shangyu|商於]] before winning again near [[LantianBattle|蓝田之战]]. Qin therefore converts Zhang Yi's alliance-breaking diplomacy into territory, battlefield attrition, and a settlement in which Chu cedes cities.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-52-chenzhuang-panqin-duanqi-wan-chifan-fangxia-wan-ma-niang-ls13ixmbhflepjgpelxgu6idjqa2]] adds Qin's southwestern governance problem after its conquest of [[BaStateWarringStates|巴国]] and [[ShuStateWarringStates|蜀国]]. The source traces the setup to [[SimaCuo|司马错]] persuading [[YingSi|秦惠文王]] to attack Shu, then shows Qin leaving [[ShuHouNingTong|蜀侯宁通]] as marquis and appointing [[ChenZhuangWarringStates|陈庄]] as chancellor. Chen Zhuang's later killing of Shu Hou Ning Tong and rebellion makes Qin's strength depend not only on expansion but on controlling local appointees in [[BaShu|巴蜀]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-53-qinhuiwang-yong-zhangyi-huan-dipi-lvmo2cwbafcg2gnxliuo18fewimk]] returns Qin from southwestern rebellion to Chu-facing diplomacy. Qin proposes exchanging land around [[Wuguan|武关]] and [[Shangyu|商於]] for [[Qianzhong|黔中]], while Chu counters by demanding [[ZhangYiStrategist|张仪]]. The episode reads Qin's advantage broadly: Qin's strength deters Chu from killing Zhang Yi, Qin's Ba-Shu base threatens Chu from the west, and [[YingSi|秦惠文王]] may gain whether Zhang Yi is used, ransomed, or killed. Qin therefore appears as a state capable of converting both territory and personnel risk into leverage.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-54-wo-zun-ni-shi-wang-ni-dang-wo-shi-pi-lvhcz0ban9frlmwshd9vch-q7-ie]] turns Qin's leverage into a multi-state persuasion campaign. Zhang Yi uses Qin's military strength, geography, and alleged control over other states to pressure [[HanState|韩国]], [[QiState|齐国]], and [[ZhaoState|赵国]] toward [[LianhengStrategy|连横]], then visits [[YanState|燕国]]. Qin also becomes a succession-risk setting: [[YingSi|秦惠文王]] dies before Zhang Yi returns to [[Xianyang|咸阳]], and [[QinWuwang|秦武王]]'s accession exposes Zhang Yi to [[PatronSuccessionExposure|靠山更替暴露风险]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-55-zhangyi-tongshi-guiren-qinhuiwang-lsclfbwn06fgzooipf5up9sdkedy]] shows the aftermath. Yan gives five cities and seeks peace, but Qin's court transition shakes the lianheng settlement enough that the lords renew [[HezongAlliance|合纵]]. Qin then becomes the audience for Zhang Yi's exit pitch: send him to [[WeiState|魏国]], let [[QiState|齐国]] attack, and use the distraction to threaten Han and [[SanchuanWarringStates|三川]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-58-zhaowulingwang-ruhe-dazao-qiangguo-lq9mgpib0whh0w-jkv0kxcafbnnz]] completes the Qin Wuwang branch from another angle. Qin reinforces [[GanMao|甘茂]] after the Xirang oath is invoked, takes [[YiyangWarringStates|宜阳]], and forces [[HanState|韩国]] to seek peace. The state then suffers a sudden succession shock when Qin Wuwang dies at [[Luoyi|洛邑]] after the ding-lifting episode, bringing [[QinZhaoxiangwang|秦昭襄王]] and [[XuanTaihou|芈八子 / 宣太后]] into the next Qin configuration.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-59-yichao-junwang-yichaochen-ljq-qkueix0rnzfekzuadicce1bh]] shows that configuration beginning under pressure. Qin administers newly taken Yiyang through [[XiangShou|向寿]], accepts Gan Mao's advice to return [[WusuiWarringStates|武遂]] to Han, and then watches Gan Mao's position collapse as Qin Wuwang's death changes the court balance. The same source says Qin Wuwang's brothers fight for the throne and [[WeiRan|魏冉]] secures Ying Ji as Qin Zhaoxiang Wang, while Chu, Qi, and Han use Qin's transition to rebuild [[HezongAlliance|合纵]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-60-zhongguo-di-yi-wei-bachi-chaozheng-de-taihou-jingshi-ta-ltazis9qo0dwvwmzxikk8mmpvosc]] turns that transition into Qin's early Zhaoxiang Wang court order. [[WeiRan|魏冉]] guards [[Xianyang|咸阳]], suppresses [[YingZhuang|嬴壮]]'s revolt, and helps consolidate [[XuanTaihou|芈八子 / 宣太后]]'s [[QueenMotherRegency|太后摄政]] and [[ForeignRelativeCourtPolitics|楚系外戚政治]]. Qin then warms relations with [[ChuState|楚国]] through marriage, the [[Huangji|黄棘]] meeting, and the return of [[Shangyong|上庸]], while attacking [[WeiState|魏国]] at [[PubanWarringStates|蒲阪]], [[YangjinWarringStates|阳晋]], and [[FenglingWarringStates|封陵]] and retaking [[WusuiWarringStates|武遂]] from [[HanState|韩国]]. After [[MiHeng|芈横]] kills a Qin official and escapes, Qin repairs relations with Han and Wei at [[LinjinWarringStates|临晋]] and returns Puban to Wei, making Qin's diplomacy a clear case of [[WarringStatesAllianceSwitching|战国同盟转向]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-68-jingtan-songkangwang-huangyinwudu-de-gouxue-lishi-1-lss3e9g45jyndnvmsexwibwce5oo]] shows Qin pressing beyond those early Zhaoxiang settlements. After Wei and Han have already ceded land, Qin sends [[BaiQi|白起]] and [[SimaCuo|司马错]] against [[WeiState|魏国]], later takes [[Anyi|安邑]], and tests a higher title by proposing Qin Zhaoxiang Wang as western emperor and [[QiMinWang|齐湣王]] as eastern emperor. The episode makes Qin's power both territorial and symbolic, but [[SuDai|苏代]]'s response and renewed [[HezongAlliance|合纵]] pressure force Qin to drop the emperor title.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-62-ji-er-kuang-ni-jiujiu-de-tui-a-lumch-ibj8m94abjh5nvr3qhiqff]] continues that reversal into coercion against Chu's ruler. Qin invites [[ChuHuaiwang|楚怀王]] to [[Wuguan|武关]], seizes him, takes him to [[Xianyang|咸阳]], and demands [[WuJunWarringStates|巫郡]] plus [[Qianzhong|黔中]]. The episode explicitly condemns Qin's bad faith but explains why Qin dares to act: Chu is diplomatically isolated after years of alliance failures and weakened court feedback.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-63-degao-wangzhong-mengchangjun-shi-weijunzi-lmd0owe3rq-faponbwzhkaigprin]] gives Qin two follow-on moves in Zhou Nan Wang's seventeenth year. First, Qin treats [[Mengchangjun|孟尝君 / 田文]] as a loyalty risk because he is a [[QiState|齐国]] aristocrat, replaces him with [[LouHuan|楼缓]], and almost kills him before his retainers help him escape. Second, after [[ChuState|楚国]] installs [[MiHeng|芈横 / 楚顷襄王]] and reduces the leverage of Chu Huaiwang's detention, Qin attacks Chu through [[Wuguan|武关]], killing fifty thousand and taking sixteen cities.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-73-lishishang-ziyou-lianai-de-kaichuangzhe-shi-shui-lkumfsfialqgdl6qpb3c-hnz7h9u]] adds Qin's post-anti-Qi coalition reaction. After [[LeYi|乐毅]] compensates distant allies with wealth, Qin is presented as unwilling to settle for treasure; it attacks [[WeiState|魏国]], captures [[AnchengWarringStates|安城]], and advances toward [[Daliang|大梁]]. The episode therefore makes Qin a driver of [[PostCoalitionSpoilsImbalance|联军战后利益失衡]] as well as a continuing [[QinEastwardPressure|eastward pressure]] force.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-74-huanyuan-wanbi-guizhao-zhenxiang-lg1irvbbc1wp8oph2kmpatfoxebk]] adds a Qin-Zhao exchange branch rather than a direct conquest branch. [[QinZhaoxiangwang|秦昭襄王]] offers fifteen cities for [[HeShiBi|和氏璧]], but Qin's failure to discuss actual city transfer lets [[LinXiangru|蔺相如]] turn the exchange into [[WanbiGuizhaoDiplomacy|完璧归赵式外交]]. Qin still has the stronger coercive position, but the episode stresses that visible non-performance and envoy handling limit what Qin gains once the jade has returned to Zhao.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-76-shui-geile-chuqingxiangwang-de-zixin-lsut4cfic7xbbrmbmimrruf7n6y0]] returns Qin to direct Zhao-facing pressure. After [[ZhaoHe|赵惠文王赵何]] refuses Qin's proposal to attack [[QiState|齐国]], Qin attacks [[ZhaoState|赵国]], takes cities, attacks again the next year, and captures [[ShichengWarringStates|石城]]. The same episode records [[WeiRan|魏冉]] returning as chancellor and previews the [[Mianchi|渑池]] meeting invitation, so Qin's pressure combines retaliation, territory, court staffing, and risky diplomacy.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-77-lianpo-yu-linxiangru-shishang-zui-zhuming-de-yidui-hao-jiyou-lmelnhhtfuzezvmmsuw4yf10plsb]] adds the next step in 280-279 BCE. Qin uses [[BaiQi|白起]] against Zhao and then Chu, while [[SimaCuo|司马错]] leads a difficult southwestern move through [[BaShu|巴蜀]] toward Chu's Qianzhong region. The episode also shows a limit to Qin coercion: at [[Mianchi|渑池]], [[LinXiangru|蔺相如]] prevents Qin Zhaoxiang Wang's banquet pressure from becoming a one-sided humiliation of Zhao.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-82-daqin-de-hewuqi-baiqi-laile-lo0w8mifkijhzmx7fdwcimmv7ade]] shows Qin converting the Chu front into both terror and administration. [[BaiQi|白起]] breaks the Chu capital region, Qin creates [[NanCommandery|南郡]], then takes [[WuJunWarringStates|巫郡]] and [[Qianzhong|黔中]] and reorganizes Qianzhong as a Qin commandery. The episode also explains Qin's target switch: because [[ChuState|楚国]] gains [[StrategicDepthDefense|strategic depth]] after moving toward Chen, Qin first pounds [[WeiState|魏国]] through Bai Qi and [[WeiRan|魏冉]] to control the central-plains passage problem.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-83-zhuhou-bailan-jing-chengle-jingdian-ji-zhong-ji-ljp7nbqnrctr5mw6i9u982abmx-1]] shows Qin turning another state's plea into its own strategic gain. When [[HanState|韩国]] is attacked by [[WeiState|魏国]] and [[ZhaoState|赵国]] at [[HuayangBattle|华阳之战]], the Han envoy's [[ThreatenedDefectionLeverage|投敌威胁式求援]] makes non-intervention costly for Qin. [[WeiRan|魏冉]] and [[BaiQi|白起]] then rescue Han and crush Wei-Zhao, after which Wei's land-for-peace debate reinforces Qin's role in [[LandForPeaceExhaustionTrap|抱薪救火式割地求和]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-84-fangzhi-qinguo-miechu-jing-yong-zhe-zhao-llgh12eistejijsl6yildzkixocm]] shows Qin choosing restraint after that leverage has pulled Han and Wei into its orbit. [[Chunshenjun|黄歇 / 春申君]] persuades [[QinZhaoxiangwang|秦昭襄王]] that another anti-Chu campaign through Han and Wei would create rear-route danger and let [[QiState|齐国]], [[ZhaoState|赵国]], or Wei profit while Qin bears the risk. Qin therefore stops [[BaiQi|白起]], dismisses Han-Wei troops, and turns temporary restraint into Qin-Chu alliance logic.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-85-xuantaihou-weihe-sitong-30-nian-hou-ansha-le-laoqingren-lg7zy6vqtdjcufou4-mox5qkg2ct5]] moves Qin from the Chu route problem to the northern rear-frontier problem. The episode says [[XuanTaihou|芈八子 / 宣太后]]'s long relationship with the [[YiquState|义渠]] king helped keep Qin's rear stable during Qin's external campaigns. Once the balance changes, she kills him at [[GanquanPalace|甘泉宫]], Qin destroys Yiqu, and the new [[BeidiCommandery|北地郡]] joins [[LongxiCommandery|陇西郡]] and [[Shangjun|上郡]] as a defensive line. Qin's rise therefore looks like simultaneous eastward pressure, southern corridor calculation, and rear-frontier absorption.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-87-xialu-xiangfeng-yongzhe-sheng-zhongyu-zhaodao-chuchu-le-loppr83sdq5hhesmqihckwmhpl2h]] adds a failed Zhao-front pressure case. Qin attacks [[EyuBattle|阏与 / 燕宇之战]] after a broken Qin-Zhao settlement, but [[ZhaoShe|赵奢]] lets a Qin spy observe apparent inaction, then force-marches, camps near the battlefield, and beats Qin to the northern high ground. The episode therefore adds an example where Qin pressure is checked by deception and terrain timing rather than by coalition diplomacy.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-88-ni-zenme-kandai-fenghuangnan-1-lpuxkdq-rvnifuoneq212g53w5z5]] turns Qin's strength inward toward court control. [[WeiRan|魏冉]] recommends [[KeqingZao|客卿灶 / 客卿造]] to attack [[QiState|齐国]] and seize territory near [[Dingtao|陶 / 定陶]], but the episode reads this as [[PrivateFiefExpansionThroughStateWar|以国战扩私邑]] rather than a clean Qin state-interest campaign. The same source then brings [[FanJu|范雎]] toward Qin through [[WangJiQin|王稽 / 王继]], making Qin both the state whose military machine can be bent by an overmighty minister and the state that can absorb talent damaged by [[WeiState|魏国]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-88-ni-zenme-kandai-fenghuangnan-2-lpzyziww177yvvjksmuioa-p-1qi]] gives Qin the strategic answer to that court-control problem. Fan Ju criticizes distant attack on Qi, cites [[QiMinWang|齐湣王]]'s unretainable attack on [[ChuState|楚国]], and proposes [[YuanjiaoJingongStrategy|远交近攻]]: Qin should manage far states diplomatically and attack neighboring states whose land can be directly kept. The source therefore makes Qin's rise depend not only on force, but on making strategy subordinate to ruler authority rather than to Wei Ran's private geography.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-89-qinwang-jing-bei-ta-dui-mabaonan-ljugedx8qxhtuzd1ja-xiwxgse19]] continues Qin's near-front pressure while exposing the court condition behind it. Qin fails against Zhao's Yan Yu / Eyu under [[HuShangHuyang|胡伤 / 胡阳]], attacks Wei through [[QinWangWan|武大夫绾]], and takes Xingqiu after [[QinDaotaizi|秦悼太子]] dies in Wei. Fan Ju then turns to [[QinFourNoblesCourtPower|秦国四贵权力结构]], arguing that Qin's state force will remain distorted unless [[QinZhaoxiangwang|秦昭襄王]] can free policy from [[XuanTaihou|宣太后]], [[WeiRan|魏冉]], and the royal-kin bloc.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-90-jiemi-xianqin-zui-wanmei-de-fuchou-liu7gbl-bffknkw6dx5udqcq4tcm]] shows that recovered state force becoming Fan Ju's instrument. Qin reduces Wei Ran and the four-nobles bloc, then keeps pressure on nearby [[WeiState|魏国]] until Wei sends [[XuJia|须贾]] for peace. Qin's threat against [[Daliang|大梁]] gives Fan Ju's private demand for [[WeiQiWarringStates|魏齐]]'s head the form of state coercion, making Qin the setting for [[PersonalRevengeThroughStatePower|以国力行私仇]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-91-chulong-ruhe-qiaokai-shougua-zhaotaihou-de-men-1-lmgd7kdl1nrfwcrtkqttumtlpqok]] keeps Qin's 265 BCE pressure active while explaining why Zhao is now vulnerable. Qin attacks [[ZhaoState|赵国]] and is also attacking [[HanState|韩国]], so the source treats the Zhao campaign as compatible with [[YuanjiaoJingongStrategy|远交近攻]] rather than as a simple strategic reversal. Qin's pressure pushes Zhao toward [[QiState|齐国]] and indirectly creates the [[ChangAnJunZhao|长安君]] hostage crisis.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-91-chulong-ruhe-qiaokai-shougua-zhaotaihou-de-men-2-liy5yulg2ysop-jwfigvdkub1pk7]] gives the limit of that pressure: once Chang'an Jun is sent to Qi and Qi intervenes, Qin withdraws from Zhao. In this source, Qin's attack remains effective enough to force Zhao's domestic hostage decision, but not enough to hold the campaign against a repaired Qi-Zhao response.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-92-lishishang-di-yi-wei-zhuming-tiangou-shi-shui-1-lmwf0q0-3hxb6d4hro8fdmc3zdxp]] moves Qin's pressure back to [[HanState|韩国]]. Under [[FanJu|范雎]]'s [[YuanjiaoJingongStrategy|远交近攻]], [[BaiQi|白起]] takes cities, captures [[HanNanyangWarringStates|韩国南阳]], attacks [[TaihangRoad|太行道]], and isolates [[Shangdang|上党]]. The episode also shows Qin using [[InterestBasedCoalitionSabotage|money-based sabotage]] against renewed [[HezongAlliance|合纵]] organizers in [[ZhaoState|赵国]], while Qin's handling of [[MiWan|太子完]] keeps hostage suspicion active on the Chu front.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-92-lishishang-di-yi-wei-zhuming-tiangou-shi-shui-2-lt1nwraiuuvpy2tzo-zwaoqsajzx]] shows Qin after that hostage suspicion fails. Mi Wan reaches Chu, Huang Xie discloses the deception, and Qin has to choose between punishing the remaining Chu actor and salvaging influence with the likely new king. [[FanJu|范雎]]'s advice to send Huang Xie back makes Qin's hostage policy look pragmatic even after tactical loss.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-93-zai-mengli-cangzhe-toutian-gaiming-1-ljshdryvf9qgabtn-siontthzsok]] shows Qin pressure continuing on two fronts after that transition. [[MiWan|楚考烈王]] cedes land to Qin in his first year, and [[BaiQi|白起]]'s earlier Han-front route severance now pushes [[FengTing|冯亭]] to offer [[Shangdang|上党]] to [[ZhaoState|赵国]]. Qin's campaign therefore keeps working even when Qin is not the deliberating court: its pressure shapes Chu concession, Han local desperation, and Zhao's tempting but dangerous choice.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-93-zai-mengli-cangzhe-toutian-gaiming-2-lrlulw0b6v0fb2xpr2i5ifem1h4n]] adds Qin as the hidden capacity standard inside Zhao's debate. [[ZhaoBao|赵豹 / 平阳君]] warns that Qin's cattle farming, water transport, and reward incentives give it durable military-administrative advantage. The episode therefore makes Qin frightening even offstage: Zhao's acceptance of [[Shangdang|上党]] is not only a diplomatic insult to Qin, but a decision to face a state whose [[FarmingWarStatecraft|耕战逻辑]] and [[MilitaryMeritRankSystem|军功爵制]] make retaliation credible.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-94-shashen-baiqi-changping-zhi-zhan-zhong-de-caobao-mingjiang-1-loxyybm3njrtnudydafwzu9jp9jz]] shows Qin converting that credible retaliation into diplomatic and command advantage. Qin receives [[ZhengZhu|郑朱]] visibly so other states see Zhao seeking peace, while not actually settling. [[FanJu|范雎]] then uses money and agents to make [[ZhaoDan|赵丹 / 赵孝成王]] distrust [[LianPo|廉颇]] and appoint [[ZhaoKuo|赵括]]. Finally, Qin secretly places [[BaiQi|白起]] above [[WangHe|王龁]] and threatens death for anyone who reveals the appointment, giving Qin an information advantage as [[ChangpingBattle|长平之战]] turns toward battle.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-94-shashen-baiqi-changping-zhi-zhan-zhong-de-caobao-mingjiang-2-lvqctrazn8ddkcnp-lhspzzmzmns]] shows Qin converting that information advantage into a logistics victory. Bai Qi lures Zhao Kuo's attacking army against prepared barriers, cuts its rear and food route with side forces and cavalry, and uses fieldworks to keep the separation closed. Once the supply route is severed, Qin Zhaoxiang Wang's Henei mobilization makes the encirclement a whole-state effort rather than only a battlefield maneuver.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-94-shashen-baiqi-changping-zhi-zhan-zhong-de-caobao-mingjiang-3-ljwz8ebk0iyfleivkefvvoonrqb9]] gives Qin the victory's brutal endpoint and its limit. After Zhao Kuo's breakout fails and the Zhao army surrenders, Bai Qi kills the surrendered troops, a decision the source frames through captive security and moral horror. Qin's later attempt to keep pressure on Zhao through [[WangLing|王陵]] at [[Handan|邯郸]] then shows that even a massive victory leaves Qin exposed to [[WarCostTempoDiscipline|war-cost limits]] when people and food are depleted.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-95-changping-zhi-zhan-hou-qinguo-weishenme-meiyou-mie-le-zhaoguo-1-lq4wvkd8qx3q7zji6fnnkxi3qcl1]] explains the first pause after that endpoint. Qin continues pressure through [[WangHe|王龁]] against [[WuanWarringStates|武安]] and [[Pilao|皮牢]] and through [[SimaGeng|司马梗]] against [[TaiyuanWarringStates|太原]] and [[Shangdang|上党郡]], but [[SuDai|苏代]] makes [[FanJu|范雎]] fear that [[BaiQi|白起]]'s destruction of [[ZhaoState|赵国]] would turn the general into a rank threat. Qin therefore takes [[Yuanyong|垣雍]] from [[HanState|韩国]] and six cities from Zhao, buying rest while still extracting territory.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-95-changping-zhi-zhan-hou-qinguo-weishenme-meiyou-mie-le-zhaoguo-2-lutsvwvnwzsezoorhe-vu7yjthyx]] shows the pause failing to become stable peace. Zhao's reluctance to deliver the six cities turns Qin's accepted rest-and-cession bargain into anger and renewed pressure, while Yu Qing's proposed Qi transfer tries to turn the same land cost into anti-Qin leverage. The episode's Wei branch adds a structural explanation for why Qin remains frightening after one victory: neighboring ministers may hope Qin stops with Zhao, but [[KongBin|孔斌]] reads Qin's post-[[ShangYangReforms|商鞅变法]] order as a war machine that needs continued campaigns.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-95-changping-zhi-zhan-hou-qinguo-weishenme-meiyou-mie-le-zhaoguo-3-lsrmq1-rjqnsja8g07bhaqvhxhpi]] shows that Qin's pressure also travels through hostage and revenge channels. [[QinZhaoxiangwang|秦昭襄王]] detains [[Pingyuanjun|平原君]] to force [[WeiQiWarringStates|魏齐]]'s head for [[FanJu|范雎]], converting private vengeance and aristocratic hosting into a Qin-Zhao coercion problem. Qin releases Pingyuanjun after receiving the head, but continues military pressure through [[WangLing|王陵]] against [[Handan|邯郸]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-96-maosui-zijian-1-lvqbmaabsvbdz2zibgmseidx5phe]] makes that continued pressure hit a command limit. Wang Ling fails at Handan, reinforcements add losses without producing a breakthrough, and Qin Zhaoxiang Wang cannot compel [[BaiQi|白起]] to take the assignment even after [[FanJu|范雎]] urges him. Qin keeps the campaign alive by appointing [[WangHe|王龁]], but the episode makes Qin's post-Changping power look constrained by distance, depletion, enemy morale, and allied-rescue risk.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-96-maosui-zijian-2-ltlobdujy-hdipimfdupvwod2ech]] shows Qin using intimidation to keep that allied-rescue risk from materializing. [[MaoSui|毛遂]] secures [[ChuState|楚国]]'s commitment to aid Zhao, but Qin's warning makes [[WeiAnxiWang|魏安釐王]] halt [[JinBi|晋鄙]] at [[YeWarringStates|邺城]], which also blocks the likely route for Chu relief. Qin's pressure then appears as hierarchy as well as siege: [[XinyuanYan|新垣衍]] proposes that Zhao and Wei honor [[QinZhaoxiangwang|秦昭襄王]] as western emperor to buy withdrawal.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-97-1-baiqi-zhisi-lrqoq1sbxljbvuayq3o1hxebxhu6]] records the failure of that intimidation once [[Xinlingjun|信陵君]] forces Wei's army into motion and Chu aid joins the field. Qin's continued Handan pressure now produces battlefield losses, repeated aid requests from [[WangHe|王龁]], and a court crisis around [[BaiQi|白起]], whose warning against the campaign looks correct after the fact. The episode makes Qin's post-Changping position less like unstoppable conquest than a stressed war machine whose ruler cannot tolerate a famous commander saying the failed campaign was predictable.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-98-lishishang-shouge-laolai-dengchang-le-luxsxepdedsxd-zprezfg3u3bsnu]] shows Qin recovering the initiative in the chronicle frame after the Handan setback. Qin pressure continues against [[HanState|韩国]] and [[ZhaoState|赵国]], and when [[ZhouNanwang|周赧王]] joins the anti-Qin line under the [[ZhouRoyalHouse|周王室]] name, Qin punishes the royal house directly by absorbing [[WesternZhouPolity|西周国]]'s cities and people. The source therefore makes Qin's rise the reason 周纪 ends and the narrative prepares to enter Qin-centered chronology.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-99-1-lishishang-diyi-chounan-nixi-zuo-qinxiang-lvmdrgbhvpbiyngm-nviunour0sk]] turns that preparation into explicit [[ChroniclePeriodization|编年分期]]. The episode distinguishes Qin as a state with centuries of history from the formal Qin dynasty that begins in 221 BCE, then uses [[QinZhaoxiangwang|秦昭襄王]]'s fifty-second year as the opening Qin纪 frame. Qin is therefore both a still-Warring-States polity and the chronicle's new dating center after the Zhou royal line collapses.

The same source also shows Qin's internal cost after the [[Handan|邯郸]] campaign. [[BaiQi|白起]] is gone, [[ZhengAnping|郑安平]] has defected, and [[WangJiQin|王稽 / 王继]] is executed, leaving [[FanJu|范雎]] exposed and giving [[CaiZe|蔡泽]] a chance to argue that completed merit should be followed by retreat.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-07-liangqi-cisha-1-lvkp-ju7pf5z5cz3thojv3s2fkyx]] adds an early Warring States Qin chronology branch: Qin attacks [[WeiState|魏国]] in 401 BCE, then [[QinJianGong|秦简公]] dies and his son [[QinHuiGong|秦惠公]] succeeds him in 400 BCE.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-08-yidai-mingjiang-wuqi-cansi-2-lvaqlgyccrugfo0l-wuwf1mt1kcm]] continues the succession line: Qin Hui Gong dies in 387 BCE and his son [[QinChugong|秦出公]] succeeds. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-10-yi-jiazu-danwei-you-mayi-chi-daxiang-de-jingshen-1-lq3t7lwpmq1oyb8zmmmn2tywnu8d]] adds a Qin-origin and diplomacy branch. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-11-shijie-fengqi-yunyong-1-lt60b2gddiosf5kade35uopwohi5]] adds Qin's early [[ZhouXianwang|周显王]] military-pressure branch. Qin defeats a Wei-Han joint force at Luoyang Jinjiao in 366 BCE, defeats the Three Jin coalition at [[ShimenBattle|石门之战]] in 364 BCE, and continues attacking Wei at Shaoliang in 363 BCE. The source treats this as [[QinEastwardPressure|Qin eastward pressure]] becoming visible before later Qin dominance.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-11-shijie-fengqi-yunyong-2-llzzalowv5vmbvgd6l-jujltkbyf]] extends that early branch into 362 BCE. Qin defeats [[WeiState|魏国]] at [[Shaoliang|少梁]], captures a Wei figure rendered as [[GongshuWeiChancellor|公孙措 / 公孙痤 / 公叔痤]], and then moves from [[QinXianGong|秦献公]] to [[QinXiaogong|秦孝公]]. The episode's main addition is [[QinDiplomaticExclusion|秦 diplomatic exclusion]]: Qin is militarily dangerous but still treated by eastern states as a peripheral, not-quite-normal polity outside their meeting order.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-14-qiweiwang-buming-zeyi-yiming-jingren-lrs6rbifyiqrojxg0mccpg7sf9rv]] keeps Qin as the western pressure source in 358 BCE. Qin defeats [[HanState|韩国]] at West Mountain, prompting [[WeiState|魏国]] to send [[LongJia|龙甲]] to build defensive works, while the same year Qin receives [[YouyinHei|右尹黑]] from [[ChuState|楚国]] for a marriage-diplomacy mission.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-15-jiu-bu-chun-yinfa-de-shijie-dazhan-li-iojxthq8komhlhyumho3yvtem]] continues the same pressure into 355-354 BCE. [[QinXiaogong|秦孝公]] meets [[LiangHuiWang|魏惠王]] at Duping, but the episode infers that the meeting fails because Qin soon defeats [[WeiState|魏国]] at Yuanli and occupies [[Shaoliang|少梁]]. The source also uses Qin's enemy-head reward formula to connect this war notice back to the [[MilitaryMeritRankSystem|军功爵制]].
