# 秦国 / Qin State

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-12-gongsunyang-qiuzhi-lu-shang-de-la-guanxi-zou-houmen-lvv4-bz8sm9scezci-o-msapkvil]] adds Qin's recruitment-turn between accession pressure and institutional reform. [[QinXiaogong|秦孝公]] invokes [[QinMugong|秦穆公]]'s old greatness, names Qin's disorder and lost western lands as shame, and promises office plus land to anyone who can strengthen the state. [[ShangYang|公孙鞅 / 商鞅]] then enters through [[JingJian|景监]], showing that Qin's openness to talent still runs through [[PrivateAccessTalentChannel|private access]] before it becomes [[ShangYangReforms|商鞅变法]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-13-gongsunyang-keke-bianfa-re-shashen-1-lgmjapc2eecefbdshnpenzfcv6ga]] adds Qin's institutional reform branch under [[QinXiaogong|秦孝公]]. [[ShangYang|公孙鞅 / 商鞅]] receives the ruler's backing, defeats [[GanLong|甘龙]]'s custom-based objection, and begins [[ShangYangReforms|商鞅变法]] through household registration, mutual surveillance, agricultural incentives, severe punishment, [[MilitaryMeritRankSystem|军功爵制]], and [[XimuLixin|徙木立信]]. The episode turns Qin from a rising military pressure into a state remaking its social order for war and control.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-13-gongsunyang-keke-bianfa-re-shashen-2-lk-d9uibhneerfol6z2rrle-6ypm]] shows the first major public enforcement test of that remade order. [[YingSi|太子嬴驷]]'s violation is handled through punishment of the prince's tutors, linking [[GongziQian|公子虔]] and [[GongsunJia|公孙贾]] to a [[HighStatusLawEnforcementSignal|high-status law-enforcement signal]]. The source says this produces visible obedience by the next day and, over ten years, a Qin society that is orderly, publicly war-ready, and privately restrained.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-17-hanzhaohou-he-shenbuhai-lghjmjhq9qs4se96c5ofgz6caqof]] gives Qin the next paired military and administrative turn. Qin under [[ShangYang|公孙鞅 / 商鞅]] presses Wei at [[Anyi|安邑]] and [[Guyang|固阳]], pushing Wei toward the [[ZhangshuiAlliance|漳水之盟]] with Zhao and accommodation with Qi. In 350 BCE the same reform branch moves Qin's capital to [[Xianyang|咸阳]], enforces [[HouseholdSeparationLaw|分居令]], and expands [[QinAdministrativeStandardization|county, land, and measurement standardization]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-18-zhongguo-lishishang-di-yi-li-rentoushui-lgqnequk0rxejqgihwdqxqyqnsvq]] adds Qin's fiscal and status branch. [[ShangYang|公孙鞅 / 商鞅]]'s 348 BCE tax reform is read as likely [[PollTax|人头税]], while [[ZhouRoyalHouse|周王室]]'s 343 BCE [[ZhouHegemonRecognition|recognition of Qin hegemon status]] elevates [[QinXiaogong|秦孝公]] among the lords. Qin Xiao Gong does not attend personally; [[GongziShaoguan|公子少官]] appears with troops at [[Fengze|逢泽]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-22-gongsunyang-zha-wei-gongzi-ang-lss1l7lojfwgwdtcje7m5ijodpxi]] gives Qin a direct 340 BCE offensive branch. Shang Yang advises Qin Xiao Gong that [[WeiState|魏国]]'s post-Maling weakness is an opening for Qin to seize western advantage and push east. The campaign also shows Qin's [[FarmingWarStatecraft|耕战逻辑]] in action: war is not only foreign policy, but the reward channel that makes an empty-handed withdrawal improbable under the [[MilitaryMeritRankSystem|军功爵制]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-23-weihuiwang-hui-buyong-gongshu-zhi-yan-lveaqklo7oncmqudgmdk7l03qhsf]] adds the payoff and succession cost. Qin turns [[GongziAng|公子卬 / 公子昂]]'s capture into battlefield victory, receives Hexi territory, rewards [[ShangYang|公孙鞅 / 商鞅]] with [[Shangyu|商於]], and then loses [[QinXiaogong|秦孝公]]. After [[YingSi|嬴驷]] succeeds, the same Qin legal order that strengthened the state becomes the setting for [[GongziQian|公子虔]]'s accusation and Shang Yang's [[LegalistSelfBindingBackfire|作法自毙]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-24-shangyang-yue-wo-za-lie-le-lt4yd6pwlezqia5yljg-gankyhut]] reframes Qin as the place where outsider talent can rise quickly and become trapped. [[QinMugong|秦穆公]]'s recognition of [[BailiXi|百里奚]] supplies an earlier successful model, while [[QinXiaogong|秦孝公]]'s backing lets Shang Yang exceed the old restoration ideal. The same episode stresses that Qin's harsh reform order and accumulated resentment make Shang Yang's exit problem inseparable from Qin's state-building success.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-25-zhaoliang-dui-shangyang-shuo-ni-bupei-lnqmwjbrc9qomwrdt5brourf-78p]] adds Qin as the society whose response decides a minister's safety. [[ZhaoLiang|赵良]] contrasts Qin's mourning for [[BailiXi|百里奚]] with Qin's resentment toward [[ShangYang|公孙鞅 / 商鞅]], then treats Shang Yang's guard dependence as evidence that Qin's reform success did not produce [[PeopleBasedPoliticalSecurity|stable social support]] for its architect.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-27-weiguan-weihuiwang-he-qiweiwang-shi-za-shangye-hupeng-lminlxhrm5bethub9kyy-8blimrb]] extends Qin's pressure into 335 BCE. Qin attacks [[HanState|韩国]] and takes [[YiyangWarringStates|宜阳]], which the episode describes as a long-coveted strategic target comparable to Wei's Hexi lands. The notice keeps [[QinEastwardPressure|Qin eastward pressure]] active even as the episode's main focus shifts to Qi-Wei diplomacy.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-30-suqin-de-kaigua-rensheng-lkmn-fruukdxgkddx7zb4lc3o9bw]] adds Qin as a failed stop in [[SuQin|苏秦]]'s renewed lobbying itinerary. Su Qin approaches [[YingSi|秦惠文王]], but the episode says Qin has just executed [[ShangYang|公孙鞅 / 商鞅]] and is not receptive to wandering persuaders. Qin also remains the distant threat in Su Qin's later argument that [[YanState|燕国]] needs [[ZhaoState|赵国]] as a protective shield.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-31-hezong-lianheng-weiou-qin-lncpgmxrojc0y1meg9a3ul-o9nih]] makes Qin the active enemy around which [[HezongAlliance|合纵]] forms. Qin's attack on [[WeiState|魏国]], capture of [[LongJia|龙甲]], and seizure of Diaoyin force Su Qin to stabilize the western threat by sending [[ZhangYiStrategist|张仪]] into Qin. The same episode introduces [[LianhengStrategy|连横]] as the Qin-facing alternative that Su Qin attacks rhetorically even while using Zhang Yi's rise at Qin for tactical cover.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-33-lishi-diyi-shuangwen-nanzhujiao-suqin-lvebjyhvzctjgaevwwoddpvg9nr0]] adds Qin as the state allegedly checked by Su Qin's completed alliance. [[Shiji|《史记》]] says Qin troops did not dare leave [[HanguPass|函谷关]] for fifteen years, but the episode marks this as a claim to test against the coming [[ZizhiTongjian|《资治通鉴》]] account of renewed Qin action and alliance collapse.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-34-qinguo-zongli-ruhe-wanzhuan-liuguo-lsenzwp0kr5wscb0btvuzmkibriq]] supplies that sequel. Qin uses [[GongsunYan|犀首公孙衍]] to disturb the Qi-Wei-Zhao side of [[HezongAlliance|合纵]], keeps taking or extracting Wei territory from [[Yinjin|殷晋]] through [[Shaoliang|少梁]], [[HexiWarringStates|河西]], [[Jiaoyi|焦邑]], and [[QuwoWarringStates|曲沃]], then lets [[ZhangYiStrategist|张仪]] turn the return of [[PuyangWarringStates|蒲阳]] into [[Shangjun|上郡]]'s fifteen counties. Qin's strength here is diplomatic as well as military: it can alternate force, concessions, hostage signaling, and psychological pressure.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-35-lishishang-diyi-qibing-jiaofu-shi-ta-lnxgnu1czkl-ftfb3gq9kzxwnxdk]] widens Qin's problem from the eastern Wei front to its western/rear frontier. Qin defeats [[YiquState|义渠]], occupies part of its territory, and incorporates captured land into Qin's county system, but the episode stresses that Yiqu is not yet destroyed. The same source resolves Zhouji 34's closing question by reading Qin's return of [[Jiaoyi|焦邑]] and [[QuwoWarringStates|曲沃]] to [[WeiState|魏国]] as [[ZhangYiStrategist|张仪]]'s half-pull, half-hit diplomacy before [[YingSi|秦惠文王]] claims kingly status.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-36-weisijun-de-diwang-zhishu-lt5cpdm9yqdj4erwykd0kubfpucz]] gives Qin the kingly-title payoff. [[YingSi|秦惠文王]] claims royal title in 324 BCE, and the episode says the move causes less shock than [[XuzhouMutualKingship|徐州相王]] because the precedent exists and Qin's strength makes resistance less attractive. Qin also functions as the great-power priority in [[WuxiaWarringStates|武夏]]'s later analogy to [[LiangHuiWang|魏惠王 / 梁惠王]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-37-suqin-liuguo-jituan-ceo-de-bukan-qingshi-fj2kdqlaxzyy2nqtcutktuwrq5fo]] keeps Qin active in the same 324-323 BCE frame. [[ZhangYiStrategist|张仪]] attacks [[WeiState|魏国]], takes [[ShanchengWarringStates|陕城]], and returns the people while keeping the place, making Qin's pressure selective and territorial. The following year Zhang Yi meets Qi and Chu ministers at [[Niesang|啮桑]], possibly to improve Qin's diplomatic room or to counter [[GongsunYan|犀首公孙衍]], who has left Qin for Wei and is now organizing against Qin.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-38-gao-jianzhi-de-zhangyi-bu-yiban-lobhcpz679psy54k0iduccqkfcrn]] adds Qin as the beneficiary of Zhang Yi's ambiguous move to Wei. Through a [[Zhanguoce|《战国策》]] supplement, [[QinWuwang|秦武王]] accepts Zhang Yi's argument that sending him to [[WeiState|魏国]] can draw [[QiState|齐国]] into conflict and give Qin room to move. In the annalistic notice, when Wei refuses Zhang Yi's proposed Qin-facing posture, Qin attacks and takes [[QuwoWarringStates|曲沃]] and [[PingzhouWarringStates|平州]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-39-lishishang-zhangyi-shi-zenyang-nudui-qiangdi-de-lv2gc2wtcxuhugfgixk7rbbhpa6x]] shows Qin's influence working through personnel psychology as well as attack. [[YingSi|秦惠文王]] secretly rewards [[ZhangYiStrategist|张仪]] despite the failed Wei mission, creating [[GraceDebtLoyaltyPressure|恩赏负债式忠诚压力]] that pulls Zhang Yi's allegiance back toward Qin. The episode also says Zhang Yi secretly coordinates with Qin against [[WeiState|魏国]], making the Wei chief-minister role another channel for Qin-facing pressure.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-45-yi-shi-wei-jian-junwang-daodi-gai-bu-gai-sharen-chengxing-lo3cok7cfwzrxesp-i8miz1fc1db]] adds two early 周纪三 Qin pressure points. In 319 BCE Qin attacks [[HanState|韩国]] and takes Yanling. In 318 BCE, five eastern states reach [[HanguPass|函谷关]] against Qin, but Qin cuts the Chu army's grain route and triggers the coalition's withdrawal. The episode therefore keeps Qin as the state strong enough to generate [[HezongAlliance|合纵]] while also exposing [[CoalitionSelfPreservationFailure|联盟自保失灵]] among its opponents.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-46-ma-ni-shi-ge-erbaiwu-de-lishi-laiyuan-lkz5hiamaojtvanllbunecy3g3ff]] gives Qin the counterattack sequel in 317 BCE. Under [[Chulizi|樗里子]], Qin pursues [[HanState|韩国]] and [[WeiState|魏国]] after the failed Hangu Pass campaign, wins heavily at [[XiuyuBattle|修鱼之战]], and continues fighting at [[ZhuozeBattle|浊泽]]. The source makes Qin's strength feel less like passive defense behind a pass and more like retaliatory reach into the central plains.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-50-zhangyi-shi-zenyang-ba-chuhuaiwang-huyou-que-de-loxg3ylhmskvdm-axtco5y-acege]] gives Qin a 313 BCE two-track move. [[Chulizi|樗里子]] attacks [[ZhaoState|赵国]] and captures Lingcheng's defender, while [[YingSi|秦惠文王]] sends [[ZhangYiStrategist|张仪]] to break [[ChuState|楚国]] away from [[QiState|齐国]] before Qin attacks Qi. Qin's pressure here is diplomatic as well as military: the promised [[Shangyu|商於]] land isolates Chu first, then the six-li reversal turns Chu's anger into a separate Qin-Chu conflict.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-51-chuhuaiwang-qin-xiao-er-kan-jiujiu-ruhe-mie-le-ni-ltfkdlivomnvux5xodh2rlfqf6ac]] records the military payoff of that conflict in 312 BCE. Qin defeats Chu at [[DanyangBattle|丹阳之战]], captures [[QuGai|屈匄]], takes [[Hanzhong|汉中]], then draws Chu deeper through [[Wuguan|武关]] and [[Shangyu|商於]] before winning again near [[LantianBattle|蓝田之战]]. Qin therefore converts Zhang Yi's alliance-breaking diplomacy into territory, battlefield attrition, and a settlement in which Chu cedes cities.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-52-chenzhuang-panqin-duanqi-wan-chifan-fangxia-wan-ma-niang-ls13ixmbhflepjgpelxgu6idjqa2]] adds Qin's southwestern governance problem after its conquest of [[BaStateWarringStates|巴国]] and [[ShuStateWarringStates|蜀国]]. The source traces the setup to [[SimaCuo|司马错]] persuading [[YingSi|秦惠文王]] to attack Shu, then shows Qin leaving [[ShuHouNingTong|蜀侯宁通]] as marquis and appointing [[ChenZhuangWarringStates|陈庄]] as chancellor. Chen Zhuang's later killing of Shu Hou Ning Tong and rebellion makes Qin's strength depend not only on expansion but on controlling local appointees in [[BaShu|巴蜀]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-53-qinhuiwang-yong-zhangyi-huan-dipi-lvmo2cwbafcg2gnxliuo18fewimk]] returns Qin from southwestern rebellion to Chu-facing diplomacy. Qin proposes exchanging land around [[Wuguan|武关]] and [[Shangyu|商於]] for [[Qianzhong|黔中]], while Chu counters by demanding [[ZhangYiStrategist|张仪]]. The episode reads Qin's advantage broadly: Qin's strength deters Chu from killing Zhang Yi, Qin's Ba-Shu base threatens Chu from the west, and [[YingSi|秦惠文王]] may gain whether Zhang Yi is used, ransomed, or killed. Qin therefore appears as a state capable of converting both territory and personnel risk into leverage.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-54-wo-zun-ni-shi-wang-ni-dang-wo-shi-pi-lvhcz0ban9frlmwshd9vch-q7-ie]] turns Qin's leverage into a multi-state persuasion campaign. Zhang Yi uses Qin's military strength, geography, and alleged control over other states to pressure [[HanState|韩国]], [[QiState|齐国]], and [[ZhaoState|赵国]] toward [[LianhengStrategy|连横]], then visits [[YanState|燕国]]. Qin also becomes a succession-risk setting: [[YingSi|秦惠文王]] dies before Zhang Yi returns to [[Xianyang|咸阳]], and [[QinWuwang|秦武王]]'s accession exposes Zhang Yi to [[PatronSuccessionExposure|靠山更替暴露风险]].

秦国 / Qin State appears in [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-06-weiwenhou-xuan-shui-dang-zaixiang-ne-2-lmfzo4sl9nd5kz3mrxdl1dl9efei]] through [[QinYing|秦嬴]], whom the episode explains as a Qin princess by name pattern rather than as someone surnamed Qin. The source also uses Qin-Jin intermarriage to explain the idiom "秦晋之好."

This page is source-scoped and mainly supports the episode's kinship and assassination-attribution example. It connects backward to [[JinState|晋国]] and forward to the wiki's existing Qin-related material through [[QinShiHuang|秦始皇]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-07-liangqi-cisha-1-lvkp-ju7pf5z5cz3thojv3s2fkyx]] adds an early Warring States Qin chronology branch: Qin attacks [[WeiState|魏国]] in 401 BCE, then [[QinJianGong|秦简公]] dies and his son [[QinHuiGong|秦惠公]] succeeds him in 400 BCE.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-08-yidai-mingjiang-wuqi-cansi-2-lvaqlgyccrugfo0l-wuwf1mt1kcm]] continues the succession line: Qin Hui Gong dies in 387 BCE and his son [[QinChugong|秦出公]] succeeds. The same source also makes Qin a strategic pressure point for [[WuQi|吴起]]'s [[ChuState|楚国]] reforms because Wu Qi opposes making peace with Qin.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-08-yidai-mingjiang-wuqi-cansi-3-ltca-ikuzo9ej7a0lfrgwv2-wulb]] completes Qin Chugong's short branch with a coup in 385 BCE. Shuzhang Gai brings [[QinXianGong|秦献公]] back from exile in [[WeyState|卫国]], installs him, and kills Qin Chugong and his mother. The episode treats the coup as evidence that Qin's pre-Shang-Yang politics were still vulnerable to regency legitimacy problems, palace influence, and returning-royal claims.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-10-yi-jiazu-danwei-you-mayi-chi-daxiang-de-jingshen-1-lq3t7lwpmq1oyb8zmmmn2tywnu8d]] adds a Qin-origin and diplomacy branch. Through [[Shiji|《史记》]]'s [[TaishiDan|太史旦]] story, the episode traces Qin from [[QinFeizi|非子]] under [[ZhouXiaowang|周孝王]] to [[QinXiangGong|秦襄公]]'s recognition by [[ZhouPingwang|周平王]] and [[QinWengong|秦文公]]'s recovery of western territory, then reads the speech to [[QinXianGong|秦献公]] as [[KinshipLegitimacyDiplomacy|kinship legitimacy diplomacy]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-11-shijie-fengqi-yunyong-1-lt60b2gddiosf5kade35uopwohi5]] adds Qin's early [[ZhouXianwang|周显王]] military-pressure branch. Qin defeats a Wei-Han joint force at Luoyang Jinjiao in 366 BCE, defeats the Three Jin coalition at [[ShimenBattle|石门之战]] in 364 BCE, and continues attacking Wei at Shaoliang in 363 BCE. The source treats this as [[QinEastwardPressure|Qin eastward pressure]] becoming visible before later Qin dominance.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-11-shijie-fengqi-yunyong-2-llzzalowv5vmbvgd6l-jujltkbyf]] extends that early branch into 362 BCE. Qin defeats [[WeiState|魏国]] at [[Shaoliang|少梁]], captures a Wei figure rendered as [[GongshuWeiChancellor|公孙措 / 公孙痤 / 公叔痤]], and then moves from [[QinXianGong|秦献公]] to [[QinXiaogong|秦孝公]]. The episode's main addition is [[QinDiplomaticExclusion|秦 diplomatic exclusion]]: Qin is militarily dangerous but still treated by eastern states as a peripheral, not-quite-normal polity outside their meeting order.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-14-qiweiwang-buming-zeyi-yiming-jingren-lrs6rbifyiqrojxg0mccpg7sf9rv]] keeps Qin as the western pressure source in 358 BCE. Qin defeats [[HanState|韩国]] at West Mountain, prompting [[WeiState|魏国]] to send [[LongJia|龙甲]] to build defensive works, while the same year Qin receives [[YouyinHei|右尹黑]] from [[ChuState|楚国]] for a marriage-diplomacy mission.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-15-jiu-bu-chun-yinfa-de-shijie-dazhan-li-iojxthq8komhlhyumho3yvtem]] continues the same pressure into 355-354 BCE. [[QinXiaogong|秦孝公]] meets [[LiangHuiWang|魏惠王]] at Duping, but the episode infers that the meeting fails because Qin soon defeats [[WeiState|魏国]] at Yuanli and occupies [[Shaoliang|少梁]]. The source also uses Qin's enemy-head reward formula to connect this war notice back to the [[MilitaryMeritRankSystem|军功爵制]].
