# Los Angeles Country Club

## Overview

Los Angeles Country Club is a private, member-owned golf club used by [[MalcolmGladwell]] as the central example in a dispute over valuable urban land, property-tax preferences, exclusivity, and possible reciprocal public access.

## Current Profile

The bounded source describes the club as occupying roughly 325 acres across two large parcels. Gladwell estimates the land at $9 billion and contrasts an ordinary-tax counterfactual of about $90 million annually with a reported payment of about $200,000, while the later parcel-tax proposal would reportedly produce a bill near $55 million.

When [[AdrinNazarian]]'s proposal gains attention, the club warns members that it has retained experienced advisers and seeks useful expertise and relationships. Gladwell proposes a negotiated alternative in which one parcel becomes a public park and the other remains a private course.

## Key Characteristics

- Large private golf property in land-scarce West Los Angeles.
- Member-owned institution benefiting from golf-course valuation rules and Proposition 13-era assessment continuity.
- Central example in the episode's claim that public subsidy supports private exclusion.
- Potential negotiating counterparty for a park, access, or tax settlement.
