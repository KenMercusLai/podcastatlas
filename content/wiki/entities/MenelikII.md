# Menelik II

## Overview

Menelik II enters the wiki through [[706-queen-victorias-revenge-storming-the-citadel-part-2-glt3285474568]] as the Ethiopian ruler who ends the long political collapse that followed the death of [[TeodrosII]], by defeating an Italian army at the Battle of Adwa in 1896.

## Current Profile

The source names Menelik only at the end of the campaign story, as the answer to what happened after [[Magdala]]. With the emperor dead and no successor installed by the departing British force, [[Abyssinia]] was left leaderless and suffered roughly twenty years of chaos and civil war, running into the war in Sudan against the Mahdi and General Gordon. The episode's timeline closes only at the end of the century, when Menelik II beat the Italians at Adwa, which it calls a landmark humiliation of a European army by an African one.

Menelik therefore functions in this branch as the counterpoint to the expedition's narrow aims. The British achieved the rescue and left; the reconstitution of Ethiopian power, and the most famous African victory over a European army in the period, came from within Abyssinia roughly three decades later.

## Key Characteristics

- He is the source's endpoint for the post-1868 vacuum rather than a developed character.
- His significance is tied to Adwa, framed as a decisive African victory over a European colonial army.
- He stands opposite [[TeodrosII]] in the branch's memory structure: the emperor who dies resisting invasion and the emperor who later defeats one.
- The source does not describe his reign, reforms, or the Italian campaign beyond the result.

## Evidence

- Post-Magdala vacuum and its end: [[706-queen-victorias-revenge-storming-the-citadel-part-2-glt3285474568]] says Abyssinia was left leaderless, suffered about twenty years of chaos and civil war, and that the period ended only under Menelik II.
- Adwa: [[706-queen-victorias-revenge-storming-the-citadel-part-2-glt3285474568]] says Menelik beat the Italians at the Battle of Adwa in 1896, a landmark humiliation of a European army by an African one.

## Qualifications

The page is deliberately minimal because the source gives Menelik one closing sentence. His reign, the Italian invasion, the treaty disputes behind Adwa, and the wider Scramble for Africa context all need other sources before the wiki can treat this as an Ethiopian history profile rather than an endnote to a British campaign.
