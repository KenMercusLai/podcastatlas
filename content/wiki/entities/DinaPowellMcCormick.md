# Dina Powell McCormick

## Overview

Dina Powell McCormick is presented in the bounded source as [[Meta]]'s president and vice chairwoman and a former adviser to [[DonaldTrump]]. Her role in the episode is to connect Meta's AI strategy to data-center siting, workforce development, product legitimacy, child safety, smart glasses, and open-model distribution.

## Current Profile

Powell McCormick's public case is a social-license strategy for AI expansion. She argues that communities should choose whether to host data centers, but that companies must answer concrete questions about power, water, noise, pollution, taxes, schools, first responders, and jobs. She uses [[RichlandParish|Richland Parish, Louisiana]] as the model: negotiate costs and benefits up front, communicate continuously, and show visible local gains.

Her workforce argument treats labor scarcity as a direct buildout constraint. Through [[AmericasWorkforceAcademy|America's Workforce Academy]], she describes paid training, portable credentials, and guaranteed employment as mechanisms for bringing paycheck-to-paycheck workers into fiber and construction roles. On consumer products and safety, she defends Meta's under-18 defaults and argues that [[RayBanSmartGlasses|Ray-Ban smart glasses]] have practical, accessible uses, while the adversarial exchange leaves Meta's broader youth-harm record contested.

## Key Characteristics

- Frames AI infrastructure as a negotiated community compact rather than only a national strategic necessity.
- Treats paid training and job certainty as practical answers to the skilled-trades shortage.
- Uses local officials, workforce outcomes, and accessibility cases to make Meta's buildout tangible.
- Defends company-level child-safety defaults while rejecting the premises of broader addiction and liability criticism.
- Presents broad AI distribution and open models as stabilizing, reflecting [[MarkZuckerberg|Mark Zuckerberg]]'s stated philosophy.

## Qualifications

The bounded source is a friendly executive interview built around a produced company case study. It offers no independent audit of economic impact, water use, utility allocation, workforce retention, child-safety effectiveness, or model openness. Powell McCormick had been at Meta for about eight months at the time of the interview, and she explicitly disputes rather than resolves the premises of the child-safety criticism.
