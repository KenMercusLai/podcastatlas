# Plato / 柏拉图

## Overview

Plato / 柏拉图 is represented in the wiki through three different uses: a comic workplace persona, a political-philosophy source for the cave allegory, and the classical starting point for the [[Atlantis]] reception branch.

## Current Profile

The current Plato profile is source-bounded rather than doctrinally complete. One source uses him as the intern-viewpoint figure inside [[PlatoGoesToWork|《柏拉图上班记：哲学家的职场百态》]], where philosophy becomes office satire. Another uses the cave allegory to frame algorithmic media as a system that preselects what people see before they judge. The Atlantis branch positions Plato as the authorial baseline later interpreters keep transforming.

Across these sources, Plato's wiki role is less a survey of all Platonic philosophy than a set of reusable interpretive frames. The cave becomes a way to discuss algorithmic filtering; the workplace satire turns philosopher biography into organizational comedy; Atlantis becomes a warning story whose later reception shows how myth can be reversed, relocated, and overread.

## Key Characteristics

- Plato functions as an interpretive source rather than a fully biographical subject in the current wiki.
- The cave allegory is used to explain how recommendation feeds, search rankings, and platform signals shape perceived reality.
- The office-satire branch makes Plato a learner figure whose onboarding exposes philosophical ideas through work routines.
- The Atlantis branch presents Plato's story as a political and cultural warning about wealth, empire, hubris, and catastrophe.
- Later Atlantis theories matter because they show how far reception can move from Plato's location and moral structure.

- Atlantis baseline: [[315-atlantis-legacy-of-the-lost-empire-part-2-glt2807981945]] contrasts Plato's Atlantis as an Atlantic, imperial, hubristic, destroyed power with later utopian, nationalist, racial, esoteric, and lost-civilization versions.

## Qualifications

The page is not a general Plato encyclopedia entry. The current profile is limited to the three ingested uses, and the Atlantis interpretation follows the Rest Is History episode's reading of Plato rather than settling all specialist debates about the *Timaeus* and *Critias*.
