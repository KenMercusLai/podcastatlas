# Agamemnon

## Overview

Agamemnon is the mythic Achaean commander and king of [[Mycenae]] whose conflicts with Achilles and [[Clytemnestra]] expose the violence of heroic status, wartime command, sacrifice, and dynastic revenge.

## Current Profile

In Iliad-centered sources, Agamemnon's seizure of Briseis turns war spoils into a public contest over honor and authority. His later compensation cannot answer Achilles' deeper objection to spending a mortal life for another ruler's status. A cross-epic source makes the social mechanism explicit: heroic distribution, rank, and recognition operate together inside an ancient Greek honor community.

The new source extends his profile beyond the Iliad. He deceives Clytemnestra and sacrifices Iphigenia for the fleet, returns from Troy with Cassandra, and is killed in revenge. His Homeric title “lord of men” may preserve the Bronze Age rulership term *wanax/anax*, but Schliemann's gold mask is centuries too early and supplies no evidence for Agamemnon as an identifiable historical king.

## Key Characteristics

- He embodies command as a hierarchy of public honor, booty, and recognition.
- His seizure of Briseis fractures the Greek coalition from within.
- His sacrifice of Iphigenia makes political and military success inseparable from household betrayal.
- His return with Cassandra intensifies the moral grounds for Clytemnestra's revenge without making murder uncomplicated.
- His title may preserve Bronze Age institutional language even though his biography remains unverified.

## Evidence

- Honor and distribution: [[96-hema-shishi-zai-jiazhuang-yongsheng-de-shidai-women-zhongdu-siwang-yiliyate-pian-725317514]] and [[ep269-cong-manwei-yuzhou-dao-aodesai-weihe-zongyao-fanpai-yingxiong-shishi-ltklotheqpup6oamyflfzpxtipsw]] connect Briseis, compensation, rank, and public recognition.
- Household violence: [[458-helen-of-troy-a-family-of-blood-part-2-glt7255356131]] traces Iphigenia's sacrifice, Cassandra's arrival, Agamemnon's murder, and the wider cursed lineage.
- Historical-memory boundary: [[458-helen-of-troy-a-family-of-blood-part-2-glt7255356131]] links *wanax* and *anax* while rejecting the so-called Mask of Agamemnon as a personal identification.

## Qualifications

- Agamemnon is not established as a historical individual.
- A Bronze Age title or plausible palace society can survive in poetry without preserving an exact king, war, or genealogy.
- His motives and moral profile differ across epic, tragedy, and later retelling; this page synthesizes the bounded sources rather than one canonical biography.

## What Changed

- Extended Agamemnon from Iliadic honor conflict into sacrifice, homecoming, revenge, and tragic justice.
- Added the distinction between possible institutional-language memory and failed artifact-level identification.

## Relationships

- [[Clytemnestra]] - wife who kills him after Iphigenia's sacrifice and his return with Cassandra.
- [[HelenOfTroy]] - sister-in-law whose story supplies the war's mythic cause.
- [[TheOresteia]] - tragic trilogy organized around his return, murder, and its retaliatory consequences.
- [[Mycenae]] - Bronze Age citadel and mythic kingdom attached to his rule.
- [[HomericBronzeAgeMemory]] - framework separating plausible institutional residue from historical biography.
