## Overview

浑邪王 / Hunye King is presented as a western [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] ruler whose earlier defeat and escape from [[HuoQubing|霍去病]] precede his submission to [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]. The surrender then unfolds from fear of internal punishment and a fractured alliance with [[XiutuKingWesternHan|休屠王]] through four governance stages: an unstable armed transfer, ceremonial status conversion at Chang'an, domestic costs disputed by [[JiAnWesternHan|汲黯]], and dispersal of his former followers into supervised frontier communities. The current evidence does not reconstruct his full biography or independently settle the campaigns or surrender chronology.

## Current Profile

The military-pressure stage appears in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-307-2-huoqubing-gujun-yizhan-zhanshen-neng-you-duo-biantai-ll-egjuu-gn-s-_mazrbbarfmb02|Hanji 307-2]]. It says Huo pursues Hunye King and Xiutu King north and south around Yanzhi Mountain until their forces are compressed toward Gaolan Mountain, the Yellow River, and the Han frontier. They turn to fight, suffer a claimed 8,900 dead from 13,000, and escape with only a small remainder. The route, force and casualty figures, inner-state narration, battle mechanics, leader names, and size of the remnant remain episode-attributed.

The pre-transfer stage then adds pressure from inside Xiongnu. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-312-xianghan-de-xiongnu-wang-weihe-turan-neidou-lkbyh1fglhog0d9voaxitwu59eic|Hanji 312]] says Hunye King and Xiutu King fear that the chanyu will execute them after western losses and therefore discuss surrender together. The episode presents Hunye King as committed to departure, while attributing to Xiutu King a plan to exploit the crossing and occupy the pasture left open in Hexi. Hunye King's preemptive attack turns shared danger into internal combat, but the private plan, discovery, battlefield sequence, and death mechanics remain source-scoped.

The armed-transfer stage therefore begins before submission is secure. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-313-1-duchuang-huxue-huoqubing-shouxiang-hunyewang-lij9eecrq-igxpvftkey5shu8395|Hanji 313-1]] says the joint surrender has fractured, Xiutu resistance has produced internal fighting, and armed groups remain free to flee. Huo Qubing treats Hunye King's declaration as the start of stabilization rather than its completion: he imposes a public surrender-or-resistance boundary, requires Hunye King to guide the operation, and uses his authority and forces against departing Xiutu groups.

The same episode says Huo sends Hunye King toward Chang'an ahead of the larger body and moves the remaining cavalry south across the Yellow River. This makes Hunye King both a surrendering ruler and an intermediary whose local authority helps Han separate compliant from resisting groups. It does not prove his private motive, the voluntariness of every follower, or the episode's exact troop and casualty totals.

The ceremonial stage turns controlled movement into publicly recognized status. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-313-2-ta-touxiang-xihan-shoufeng-wanhuhou-lu7zpyyfemjcntnubgsfr7bu9_tg|Hanji 313-2]] frames the Northern Army formation, Chang'an, and Weiyang Palace as a combined welcome and demonstration of power, then says Hunye King receives a ten-thousand-household marquisate. Smaller fiefs for associated leaders and horse-keeping service for the Xiutu King's sons show incorporation as differentiated rather than equal. The episode's prostration scene is imaginative narration, and its exact title, names, figures, and ranks require primary-source comparison.

The domestic-cost stage reveals the other side of that display. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-314-1-xiongnu-wang-guijiang-wudi-weihe-nusha-500-hanren-ljb-ocshiauhvyra88aykkqlxkak|Hanji 314-1]] says Wudi treats vehicles, borrowed horses, and rewards as signals that surrender is credible, while Ji An argues that the court should not exhaust the treasury, compel civilians, or elevate a surrendered subordinate over the people who paid for the war. When Han merchants trade with the surrendered Xiongnu in Chang'an, severe sentences turn incorporation into a dispute over changed legal context and disproportionate internal harm.

The longer settlement stage changes the structure of the surrendered force. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-315-1-hanwudi-gao-qiangchai-yiju-shoufu-xiongnu-wang-llyk4i76izmxe_el_ewpbbayqq5i|Hanji 315-1]] says Hunye King's former followers were divided among five northwestern commanderies, retained Xiongnu customs inside subordinate-state communities, and were supervised by Han personnel. Their front-line placement is interpreted as both a commitment test and a way to use incorporated capacity against remaining Xiongnu, while the resulting security change is linked to the [[HexiCorridorWesternHan|西汉河西走廊]]'s emergence as a western base.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Xiongnu ruler whose claimed Gaolan defeat and escape precede threatened internal punishment and a joint submission that fractures before Han control is secure.
- Intermediary required to guide Huo Qubing and direct affiliated forces against resisting Xiutu groups.
- Leader separated from the larger armed population and sent toward Chang'an as part of rapid stabilization.
- Surrendered ruler whose ceremonial entry and reported ten-thousand-household marquisate convert submission into visible Han-recognized status.
- Focal point of a Han reception whose vehicles, horses, labor, rewards, and legal enforcement carry substantial domestic costs.
- Ruler whose former followers are later dispersed among five supervised frontier communities while retaining Xiongnu customs.

## Evidence

Military defeat before surrender:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-307-2-huoqubing-gujun-yizhan-zhanshen-neng-you-duo-biantai-ll-egjuu-gn-s-_mazrbbarfmb02|Hanji 307-2]] says sustained pursuit forces Hunye King and Xiutu King into battle near Gaolan, where most of the reported force is lost before the two rulers escape.

Fractured surrender and enforcement role:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-312-xianghan-de-xiongnu-wang-weihe-turan-neidou-lkbyh1fglhog0d9voaxitwu59eic|Hanji 312]] says fear of chanyu punishment initiates the joint plan, while divergent commitment and alleged competition for Hexi produce internal fighting before the Han crossing.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-313-1-duchuang-huxue-huoqubing-shouxiang-hunyewang-lij9eecrq-igxpvftkey5shu8395|Hanji 313-1]] says the planned joint surrender breaks down and Huo requires Hunye King to guide the operation and mobilize his own side against resisters.

Separation and armed transfer:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-313-1-duchuang-huxue-huoqubing-shouxiang-hunyewang-lij9eecrq-igxpvftkey5shu8395|Hanji 313-1]] says Hunye King is sent ahead while more than forty thousand cavalry are moved south; the exact sequence and total remain episode-attributed.

Ceremonial reception and differentiated status:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-313-2-ta-touxiang-xihan-shoufeng-wanhuhou-lu7zpyyfemjcntnubgsfr7bu9_tg|Hanji 313-2]] says military display and monumental court space frame Hunye King's arrival before he receives a ten-thousand-household marquisate and associated leaders receive smaller rewards.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-313-2-ta-touxiang-xihan-shoufeng-wanhuhou-lu7zpyyfemjcntnubgsfr7bu9_tg|Hanji 313-2]] contrasts those rewards with the Xiutu King's sons' assignment to palace horse-keeping service; names, titles, ranks, and figures remain episode-attributed.

Reception and domestic burden:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-314-1-xiongnu-wang-guijiang-wudi-weihe-nusha-500-hanren-ljb-ocshiauhvyra88aykkqlxkak|Hanji 314-1]] presents Wudi's reception logistics and Ji An's objection to the resulting state and civilian burden.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-314-1-xiongnu-wang-guijiang-wudi-weihe-nusha-500-hanren-ljb-ocshiauhvyra88aykkqlxkak|Hanji 314-1]] says trade between Chang'an merchants and the surrendered Xiongnu leads to more than five hundred death sentences under a disputed frontier-law interpretation.

Dispersal and frontier incorporation:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-315-1-hanwudi-gao-qiangchai-yiju-shoufu-xiongnu-wang-llyk4i76izmxe_el_ewpbbayqq5i|Hanji 315-1]] says Hunye King's former followers are distributed among five commanderies, retain their customs, and live under Han supervision on the Xiongnu-facing frontier.

## Qualifications

The profile rests on six compact podcast summaries and does not independently establish Hunye King's full title history, private motives, or family. Hanji 307-2's route, Yanzhi and Gaolan identifications, ten-thousand Han force, two-thousand-li pursuit, 8,900-of-13,000 result, leader names and ranks, battle mechanics, and escape details remain episode-attributed. Hanji 312's chanyu-execution plan, Xiutu King's territorial scheme, bridge timetable, dialogue, cultural psychology, troop totals, and battle movements remain episode-attributed; its identification of Jin Midi as an Xiutu prince clarifies the branch without making Hunye King his father. Hanji 313-1 conflicts internally by saying in one place that Hunye King killed Xiutu King and elsewhere that Xiutu King killed himself. Its dialogue, cultural psychology, battle movements, casualty count, population totals, and minimal-Han-loss claim remain source-scoped. Hanji 313-2's prostration scene is imaginative narration; its “洛阴侯” rendering, leaders' names and titles, military and architectural scale, fief figures, and treatment of the Xiutu King's sons require primary-source comparison. Hanji 314-1's vehicle, reward, merchant, and sentence figures likewise remain episode-attributed, and it does not prove that every death sentence was executed. Hanji 315-1 does not establish the legal form, degree of autonomy, voluntariness, population, command chain, or security effect of the five subordinate-state communities; “以夷制夷,” the territorial figures, and the chronology of the four Hexi commanderies remain interpretive or contested.

## What Changed

- Added the claimed Gaolan defeat and escape as military pressure preceding the later punishment fear and surrender sequence.
- Kept the battle route, figures, motives, leader identities, and escape details source-scoped rather than using later surrender to validate them.

## Relationships

- [[Xiongnu]] - polity and political world from which Hunye King surrenders and against which his former followers are later positioned.
- [[HuoQubing]] - Han commander who forces a public choice, uses Hunye King's local authority, and rapidly relocates the armed population.
- [[HanWudi]] - Han ruler who receives and rewards the surrendered population and is credited with its later dispersal.
- [[JiAnWesternHan]] - remonstrant who contests the fiscal, labor, and legal treatment surrounding the reception.
- [[CrisisSurrenderStabilization]] - operational pattern in which Hunye King becomes both surrendering leader and enforcement intermediary.
- [[SurrenderReceptionDomesticBurden]] - governance conflict made visible by the later reception and merchant cases.
- [[WeiyangPalace]] - ceremonial setting used by the episode to make Han hierarchy and imperial scale visible to the surrendering group.
- [[SurrenderedForceDispersal]] - post-reception pattern of splitting, supervising, and front-line positioning the former armed population.
- [[HexiCorridorWesternHan]] - corridor whose strategic consolidation the episode links to the surrender and settlement.
- [[JinMidiWesternHan]] - Xiutu prince whose survival and later Han career clarify that he belongs to the defeated rival branch rather than Hunye King's family.
- [[XiutuKingWesternHan]] - initial surrender partner and later opponent whose divergent commitment turns the transfer into an internal armed crisis.
