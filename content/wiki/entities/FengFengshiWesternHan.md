# 冯奉世 / Feng Fengshi (Western Han)

## Overview

冯奉世 / Feng Fengshi enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-481-wei-feng-xihan-fengfengshi-yiju-mie-diguo-libgqzvquegllma9soju57k_jxue|Hanji 481]] as the [[HanYuandi|汉元帝]]-era right general who volunteers to suppress a Qiang rebellion in [[LongxiCommandery|陇西郡]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-482-renqing-bulidan-xiaoying-zhihao-nide-xuanze-kunnan-zheng-ljuimsm-o4mglmsntyp8w5ru-dt8|Hanji 482]] continues the same campaign into assembled-force victory and uses Feng as the management contrast to [[BuridanIndecisionEffect|布利丹效应]]. The source set makes him a frontier decision case rather than a full biography: his importance is the way he links enemy assessment, troop scale, provisioning, deterrence, campaign duration, and timely action.

## Current Profile

Hanji 481 presents Feng Fengshi against a silent court. When poor harvests and the [[XianjieQiangWesternHan|Xianjie Qiang]] rebellion make senior ministers hesitate, Feng argues that a frontier revolt must be answered quickly or other groups may cease to fear Han power. He asks to command a large expedition and says that good commanders do not repeatedly levy for one war or repeatedly transport grain for the same campaign.

His calculation is specific rather than merely brave. Feng estimates the rebels at more than thirty thousand, notes that a strict ratio would imply sixty thousand Han troops, then lowers the request to forty thousand because he judges Qiang weapons inferior and expects a one-month resolution. When the court instead sends twelve thousand and a forward detachment is defeated, Feng's warning about insufficient initial force becomes Hanji 481's core proof point. Hanji 482 then completes the pattern: after the Han army gathers and advances together, it defeats the rebels, pushes survivors beyond the frontier, and lets the court shift from emergency mobilization to rest, defense, and tuntian.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han right general active under Han Yuandi in a 42 BCE Qiang crisis.
- Volunteers when the chancellor and other senior ministers have no clear proposal.
- Treats deterrence against other frontier groups as part of the military objective.
- Calculates force size from enemy numbers, weapon quality, and desired campaign duration.
- Warns that too-small deployment can fail at both attack and defense.
- Represents decisive initial commitment as potentially cheaper than repeated escalation.
- Becomes a management exemplar for timely decision-making against Buridan-style hesitation.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 481 and Hanji 482 and does not reconstruct Feng Fengshi's full career, family lineage, later rewards, or complete postwar settlement. Hanji 482 supplies the campaign-result continuation, but the managerial lessons remain the host's analogy rather than an independent administrative record.
