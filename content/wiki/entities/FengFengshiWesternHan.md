# 冯奉世 / Feng Fengshi (Western Han)

## Overview

冯奉世 / Feng Fengshi enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-481-wei-feng-xihan-fengfengshi-yiju-mie-diguo-libgqzvquegllma9soju57k_jxue|Hanji 481]] as the [[HanYuandi|汉元帝]]-era right general who volunteers to suppress a Qiang rebellion in [[LongxiCommandery|陇西郡]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-482-renqing-bulidan-xiaoying-zhihao-nide-xuanze-kunnan-zheng-ljuimsm-o4mglmsntyp8w5ru-dt8|Hanji 482]] continues the same campaign into assembled-force victory and uses Feng as the management contrast to [[BuridanIndecisionEffect|布利丹效应]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-427-yiren-zhengfu-shacheguo-xihan-fengfengshi-zenme-zuodao-de-loxxi2qynbrbmfqgokos30sypsxp|Hanji 427]] now backfills an earlier [[EmperorXuanOfHan|汉宣帝]]-era mission in which Feng uses unauthorized allied cavalry mobilization to settle the [[ShacheKingdomWesternHan|莎车国]] crisis. The source set makes him a frontier decision case rather than a full biography: his importance is the way he links enemy assessment, troop scale, provisioning, deterrence, campaign duration, field timing, and authorization risk.

## Current Profile

Hanji 427 presents the earlier field-envoy version of Feng Fengshi. At Yixun, he learns that Shache has killed the Wusun-linked king Wannian and a Han envoy, that the southern route is blocked, and that [[ZhengJiWesternHan|郑吉]] is away on the northern route. Instead of waiting for Chang'an, Feng falsely issues imperial command to gather Western Regions cavalry. The pressure of about fifteen thousand allied riders makes Shache nobles kill Hutuzheng and surrender, but [[XiaoWangzhiWesternHan|萧望之]] later warns Xuan not to normalize envoy-led unauthorized war. Feng is promoted but not made a marquis.

Hanji 481 presents Feng Fengshi against a silent court later under Yuan. When poor harvests and the [[XianjieQiangWesternHan|Xianjie Qiang]] rebellion make senior ministers hesitate, Feng argues that a frontier revolt must be answered quickly or other groups may cease to fear Han power. He asks to command a large expedition and says that good commanders do not repeatedly levy for one war or repeatedly transport grain for the same campaign.

His calculation is specific rather than merely brave. Feng estimates the rebels at more than thirty thousand, notes that a strict ratio would imply sixty thousand Han troops, then lowers the request to forty thousand because he judges Qiang weapons inferior and expects a one-month resolution. When the court instead sends twelve thousand and a forward detachment is defeated, Feng's warning about insufficient initial force becomes Hanji 481's core proof point. Hanji 482 then completes the pattern: after the Han army gathers and advances together, it defeats the rebels, pushes survivors beyond the frontier, and lets the court shift from emergency mobilization to rest, defense, and tuntian.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han frontier actor whose source layers run from Xuan-era Western Regions envoy to Yuan-era right general.
- Uses field timing and Han prestige to mobilize allied Western Regions cavalry during the Shache crisis.
- Volunteers when the chancellor and other senior ministers have no clear proposal.
- Treats deterrence against other frontier groups as part of the military objective.
- Calculates force size from enemy numbers, weapon quality, and desired campaign duration.
- Warns that too-small deployment can fail at both attack and defense.
- Represents decisive action as potentially cheaper than delayed or underpowered response, while remaining exposed to authorization limits.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 481, Hanji 482, and Hanji 427 and does not reconstruct Feng Fengshi's full career, family lineage, later rewards, or complete postwar settlement. Hanji 427 supplies an earlier Western Regions mission and reward-boundary layer, but not the complete Shache record, legal mechanics of false command, or full later court evaluation. Hanji 482 supplies the campaign-result continuation, but the managerial lessons remain the host's analogy rather than an independent administrative record.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 427's earlier Shache crisis layer, making Feng a field-envoy case in emergency discretion and unauthorized mobilization before his later Qiang-campaign force-sizing role.
- Preserved Hanji 482's victory continuation and the source's use of Feng as a management exemplar against Buridan-style hesitation.
