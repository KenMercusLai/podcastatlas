# Siege of Vicksburg

## Overview

The Siege of Vicksburg was Ulysses S. Grant's Mississippi campaign victory, ending in surrender on July 4, 1863 and giving the Union control of the Mississippi River.

## Current Profile

[[202-american-civil-war-gettysburg-part-3-glt5256515778]] presents Vicksburg as the strategic counterpart to [[BattleOfGettysburg|Gettysburg]]. [[UlyssesSGrant|Ulysses S. Grant]]'s victory split the Confederacy, made Texas more remote from its eastern core, and strengthened the longer encirclement of Virginia.

Its importance is geographic and logistical rather than primarily symbolic. Paired with Lee's retreat from Pennsylvania, the surrender showed that Confederate armies were losing initiative in two decisive theaters at once.

## Key Characteristics

- Union siege and campaign victory under Ulysses S. Grant.
- Surrender on July 4, 1863, immediately after Gettysburg.
- Secured Union control of the Mississippi River.
- Divided Confederate territory and weakened east-west connection.
- Strategic turning point whose significance complements Gettysburg's battlefield and memory prominence.

## Qualifications

This page reflects a brief episode segment rather than a full campaign history. It does not reconstruct Grant's maneuvers, siege operations, civilian conditions, Confederate command, or casualties. The campaign's role in eventual victory is strong, but the episode does not claim that the war ended or became politically predetermined in July 1863.
