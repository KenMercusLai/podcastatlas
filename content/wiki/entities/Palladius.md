# Palladius

## Overview

Palladius was a fifth-century bishop whom Prosper of Aquitaine records as sent by Pope Celestine in 431 to Christians in [[Ireland]].

## Current Profile

Palladius matters chiefly because his mission qualifies the popular claim that [[SaintPatrick|Saint Patrick]] first brought Christianity to Ireland. The episode distinguishes Palladius's appointment to believers already present from Patrick's self-presentation as a missionary to people beyond the Roman Christian world.

Later tradition may have blurred the two careers. The episode mentions T. F. O'Rahilly's theory that Palladius and Patrick became entangled in memory, but does not adopt either full identification or a reconstructed two-Patrick solution.

## Key Characteristics

- Bishop recorded by Prosper of Aquitaine as appointed in 431.
- Sent to existing Irish Christians rather than necessarily to an entirely non-Christian island.
- Evidence that Christianity in Ireland did not begin with Patrick alone.
- Figure whose historical memory may have become entangled with Patrick's later tradition.

## Qualifications

The episode gives only a compact treatment of Palladius. His activity, duration in Ireland, later reputation, and precise relationship to Patrick are not reconstructed. The conflation theory remains a historiographical possibility, not a settled identification.

## What Changed

- Established Palladius as the main qualification to a single-founder account of Irish Christianity.
- Preserved uncertainty over whether later Patrick tradition absorbed parts of Palladius's career.
