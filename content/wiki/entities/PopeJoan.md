# Pope Joan

## Overview

Pope Joan is the legendary woman said to have disguised herself as a man, entered the papal court, become pope, and been exposed by giving birth during a public procession.

## Current Profile

[[403-the-mystery-of-the-pregnant-pope-glt1981561492]] rejects Joan as a historical pope because the first surviving versions are late and the tale cannot fit the known papal chronology. Her importance is instead cultural: accepted traditions, ritual explanations, visual commemoration, and Reformation polemic made the legend a durable vehicle for arguments about papal legitimacy and [[FemaleSacredAuthorityConstraint]].

## Key Characteristics

- A fictional female pope built from medieval chroniclers' increasingly specific details.
- A legend believed and institutionally repeated for centuries despite the lack of contemporary evidence.
- A bodily scandal that tested whether papal office had to be visibly male.
- A cautionary figure through whom female sacred authority is imagined and then punished.

## Evidence

- Late construction: [[403-the-mystery-of-the-pregnant-pope-glt1981561492]] places the earliest surviving account centuries after the alleged events and notes the chronology problem.
- Narrative growth: [[403-the-mystery-of-the-pregnant-pope-glt1981561492]] contrasts [[JeanDeMailly]]'s punitive account with [[MartinOfPoland]]'s more elaborate biographical and ceremonial version.
- Institutional afterlife: [[403-the-mystery-of-the-pregnant-pope-glt1981561492]] reports Joan in papal lists, Roman tradition, Siena Cathedral imagery, and Protestant polemic.
- Gendered function: [[403-the-mystery-of-the-pregnant-pope-glt1981561492]] reads public childbirth and punishment as a narrative restoration of male ecclesiastical order.

## Qualifications

The legend's historical influence does not make Joan historical. The source offers several mutually compatible formation contexts but does not prove one origin, and reports of papal sex verification through perforated chairs are themselves treated as legendary.

## What Changed

- Created a profile separating Joan's historical nonexistence from the legend's institutional consequences.
