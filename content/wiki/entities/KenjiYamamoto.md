# Kenji Yamamoto

## Overview
Kenji Yamamoto is the filmmaker interviewed by [[MarketplaceTech]] about [[TheDreamersAndI]], his PBS documentary on Silicon Valley hacker houses.

## Current Profile
Yamamoto's role in the wiki is as a documentary witness to [[HackerHouseStartupInfrastructure]] before it became a more formal accelerator-adjacent category. His account centers on lived access: he encountered hacker houses while seeking affordable Bay Area housing, then used [[StartupEmbassy]] and its residents to show how startup ambition, immigration, sacrifice, crowding, and mission identity can occupy the same house.

## Key Characteristics
- Documentary observer of hacker houses rather than an operator, investor, or resident founder in the episode.
- Uses [[StartupEmbassy]] to make startup community visible through domestic space, shared meals, bunk beds, and founder stories.
- Frames [[CarlosStartupEmbassy|Carlos]], [[HabibayStartupEmbassyResident|Habibay]], and [[LucasStartupEmbassyResident|Lucas]] as examples of entrepreneurial idealism under practical strain.
- Connects Silicon Valley's appeal to immigration, freedom of thought, and willingness to struggle toward opportunity.

## Evidence
- Documentary role - [[tech-20260908-0908-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260908-0908-mp-tech-pod-128]] identifies Yamamoto as the filmmaker behind [[TheDreamersAndI]] and the interview's main source of observation.
- Hacker-house access - [[tech-20260908-0908-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260908-0908-mp-tech-pod-128]] says he first found hacker houses while looking for affordable temporary Bay Area housing.
- Entrepreneurial framing - [[tech-20260908-0908-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260908-0908-mp-tech-pod-128]] ties his interpretation of Carlos, Habibay, and Lucas to persistence, purity, perfectionism, and immigrant opportunity.
