## Overview

Fan Pang is a late-Han party-prohibition figure whose current profile now begins inside the first Party Prohibition prison case in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-874-zhenjing-muzi-xiangcan-tianli-nanrong-lpr3eglbnwbve1clrttapw6k5zdq|Hanji 874]]. There he refuses prison worship of Gao Yao / 皋陶 because ritual cannot substitute for justice under a false accusation. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-876-zhichang-zong-pengbi-shuoming-ni-meiyou-zhaodui-guanxi-lrjob-a07t29hdr0iogrmcsjr7qp|Hanji 876]] then shows him shackled and interrogated by [[WangFuLateHan|王甫]], defending mutual moral esteem as the pursuit of good rather than criminal faction. Hanji 877, Hanji 892, and Hanji 893 carry that reputation through release without rehabilitation, honor-list visibility, voluntary imprisonment, non-implication of helpers, and family farewell.

## Current Profile

Fan Pang's current evidence forms a five-stage profile: prison ritual refuser, imprisoned moral defendant, released but politically disabled figure, visible reputation holder, and death-facing party victim. Hanji 874 supplies the first stage. After [[LiYingLateHan|李膺]]'s case expands to roughly 200 people, Fan Pang is among the prisoners. When others worship Gao Yao, Fan refuses, arguing that if Gao Yao knows he is innocent then Gao Yao will appeal to Heaven, and if he is guilty then worship is useless. Other prisoners stop worshiping, making his refusal a collective challenge to ritual under unjust prosecution.

Hanji 876 supplies the next prison layer. Wang Fu asks why the prisoners mutually praise one another and behave like a "party"; Fan Pang answers through a good-and-evil distinction, presenting their association as ethical orientation rather than conspiratorial self-interest. His defense moves Wang Fu enough to remove heavy restraints from the prisoners, but it does not by itself end the political case.

Hanji 877 supplies the immediate aftermath. The amnesty frees Fan Pang from prison but leaves him inside [[RegistryBasedPoliticalExclusion|登记造册式政治禁锢]]: the party figures are sent home, registered, and barred from future office. His visit to [[HuoXuLateHan|霍谞]] is read through [[TacitGratitudeRestraint|心照不宣式感恩克制]] rather than ingratitude, while his discomfort with a large Runan reception shows how public fame remains dangerous after formal release.

Hanji 892 and Hanji 893 explain why that fame later matters. Fan Pang's name belongs to the士人 reputation field that turns into [[ReputationListPersecutionTarget|名望榜单迫害目标化]]. When the second party-prohibition disaster arrives, he recognizes the arrest order, goes to prison voluntarily, refuses [[GuoYiLateHan|郭揖]]'s escape plan to avoid implicating him, and accepts that a life of public名节 may not coexist with survival.

## Key Characteristics

- First Party Prohibition prisoner who refuses ritual performance when it cannot correct an unjust case.
- Moral defendant who explains party-like association as following good and avoiding evil.
- Released party figure whose amnesty does not restore office eligibility or ordinary safety.
- Tacitly grateful actor who visits Huo Xu without turning rescue into public repayment.
- Reputation figure whose name and reception visibility become dangerous before the second crackdown.
- Voluntary prisoner who recognizes the arrest order and goes to prison instead of hiding.
- Non-implicating son and father who refuses to transfer danger to helpers or give his child a simple survival rule.

- Collective effect: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-874-zhenjing-muzi-xiangcan-tianli-nanrong-lpr3eglbnwbve1clrttapw6k5zdq|Hanji 874]] says other prisoners stop worshiping after Fan Pang's refusal.

- Interrogator sympathy: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-876-zhichang-zong-pengbi-shuoming-ni-meiyou-zhaodui-guanxi-lrjob-a07t29hdr0iogrmcsjr7qp|Hanji 876]] says Wang Fu is moved and orders officials to remove heavy restraints from Li Ying and the others.

- Tacit gratitude and visibility caution: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-877-ta-weihe-lengluo-jiuming-enren-loofs4rugl-q8kunfb-jpj5mgvhi|Hanji 877]] says Fan Pang visits Huo Xu without explicit thanks and then withdraws from an overly grand Runan reception because conspicuous attention can bring trouble.
- Honor-list visibility: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-892-xiu-zhiyu-guren-youshenme-jiejing-lnobbkp6mj4xyb9jeuqsiuqogct|Hanji 892]] includes Fan Pang in the士人 reputation-list field before the crackdown.

- Refusal to implicate and family farewell: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-893-rongyu-bang-ruhe-biancheng-siwang-mingdan-lmxzqoauf79-swwnk-0cevav0gq|Hanji 893]] says Guo Yi tries to free him and flee with him, but Fan Pang refuses to involve Guo Yi; [[FanPangMotherLateHan|范滂母]] then treats his shared fame with Li Ying and Du Mi as incompatible with expecting long life.
- Moral indictment: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-893-rongyu-bang-ruhe-biancheng-siwang-mingdan-lmxzqoauf79-swwnk-0cevav0gq|Hanji 893]] says Fan Pang tells his young son that doing evil is impossible, yet doing no evil has still led him to death.

## Qualifications

This page is grounded in Hanji 874, Hanji 876, Hanji 877, Hanji 892, and Hanji 893 and still does not reconstruct Fan Pang's full career outside those source windows. Hanji 874 adds a ritual-refusal scene before Hanji 876's interrogation scene; the two prison episodes are adjacent but distinct. Hanji 876's claim that Wang Fu is moved by Fan Pang is preserved as an early prison-scene layer and does not erase Wang Fu's later role in eunuch-side repression. Transcript variants such as 范胖 are normalized to 范滂.

## What Changed

- Hanji 874 adds Fan Pang's refusal to worship Gao Yao before the existing Hanji 876 prison defense.
- The page now treats ritual legitimacy and moral association as two separate prison-facing defenses.
- Fan Pang's later voluntary imprisonment and non-implication of helpers now sit on top of an earlier collective refusal scene.
