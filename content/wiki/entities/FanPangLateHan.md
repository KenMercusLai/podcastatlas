## Overview

Fan Pang is a late-Han official and party-prohibition figure whose current profile now begins in 159 CE, before his later Runan and prison evidence. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-849-sushi-ziyou-du-de-fanpangzhuan-jiujing-shuo-de-shui-ltquglqzvezeyp0mqxwgwnlp12lf|Hanji 849]] presents him as a young Runan talent recognized by [[HuangQiongLateHan|黄琼]] and sent as a Qingzhao envoy to inspect local administration. The later pages carry that same integrity through Runan delegated清裁, unjust imprisonment, release without rehabilitation, honor-list visibility, voluntary return to prison, non-implication of helpers, and family farewell.

## Current Profile

Fan Pang's current evidence forms a seven-stage profile: court-recognized inspector, local moral-enforcement aide, prison ritual refuser, imprisoned moral defendant, released but politically disabled figure, visible reputation holder, and death-facing party victim. Hanji 849 supplies the earliest layer. Huang Qiong recruits Fan Pang as a morally serious Runan talent, and Fan Pang's Qingzhao mission to [[JizhouLateHan|冀州]] is remembered as so forceful that corrupt governors and county officials abandon office before he arrives. When challenged for impeaching too many people, he answers that he has named only urgent, harmful actors and will accept punishment if his charges prove false.

The later local stage matters because it shows that the Qingzhao profile is not a one-off anecdote. In Runan, [[ZongZiLateHan|宗资]] entrusts administration to him, and Fan Pang's清裁 is strong enough that he refuses to appoint his own nephew Li Song despite [[TangHengLateHan|唐衡]]'s recommendation. A beaten subordinate insists Fan Pang's standard cannot be violated, showing that Fan's moral authority disciplines the office around him.

The first prison stage comes after [[LiYingLateHan|李膺]]'s case expands to roughly 200 people. Fan Pang refuses to worship Gao Yao / 皋陶 because ritual cannot substitute for justice: if Gao Yao knows he is innocent, justice should appeal for him; if he is guilty, worship is useless. Other prisoners stop worshiping, making his refusal a collective challenge to ritual under unjust prosecution.

The next prison layer comes through [[WangFuLateHan|王甫]]'s interrogation. Wang Fu asks why the prisoners mutually praise one another and behave like a "party"; Fan Pang answers through a good-and-evil distinction, presenting their association as ethical orientation rather than conspiratorial self-interest. His defense moves Wang Fu enough to remove heavy restraints from the prisoners, but it does not by itself end the political case.

Hanji 877 supplies the immediate aftermath. The amnesty frees Fan Pang from prison but leaves him inside [[RegistryBasedPoliticalExclusion|登记造册式政治禁锢]]: the party figures are sent home, registered, and barred from future office. His visit to [[HuoXuLateHan|霍谞]] is read through [[TacitGratitudeRestraint|心照不宣式感恩克制]] rather than ingratitude, while his discomfort with a large Runan reception shows how public fame remains dangerous after formal release.

Hanji 892 and Hanji 893 explain why that fame later matters. Fan Pang's name belongs to the士人 reputation field that turns into [[ReputationListPersecutionTarget|名望榜单迫害目标化]]. When the second party-prohibition disaster arrives, he recognizes the arrest order, goes to prison voluntarily, refuses [[GuoYiLateHan|郭揖]]'s escape plan to avoid implicating him, and accepts that a life of public名节 may not coexist with survival.

## Key Characteristics

- Court-recognized Qingzhao envoy whose anti-corruption inspection treats cleanup as loyal service.
- Runan功曹 whose delegated清裁 resists patronage and turns moral judgment into local administrative power.
- First Party Prohibition prisoner who refuses ritual performance when it cannot correct an unjust case.
- Moral defendant who explains party-like association as following good and avoiding evil.
- Released party figure whose amnesty does not restore office eligibility or ordinary safety.
- Voluntary prisoner who recognizes the arrest order and goes to prison instead of hiding.
- Non-implicating son and father who refuses to transfer danger to helpers or give his child a simple survival rule.

## Qualifications

This page is grounded in Hanji 849, Hanji 871, Hanji 874, Hanji 876, Hanji 877, Hanji 892, and Hanji 893 and still does not reconstruct Fan Pang's full career outside those source windows. The Qingzhao envoy title, Li Song, and Zhu Ling details remain source-scoped because the current evidence does not require independent canonical pages for every office detail or subordinate. Hanji 876's claim that Wang Fu is moved by Fan Pang is preserved as an early prison-scene layer and does not erase Wang Fu's later role in eunuch-side repression. Transcript variants such as 范胖 are normalized to 范滂.

## What Changed

- Hanji 849 adds a 159 CE Qingzhao-envoy and Huang Qiong recruitment layer before the Runan功曹 evidence.
- The current profile now separates imperial inspection, local delegated清裁, prison ritual refusal, and later party-defense speech as distinct layers.
- Fan Pang's later dangerous reputation now rests on earlier inspection, enforcement, and patronage-resistance records.
