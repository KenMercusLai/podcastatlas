# Edward IV

## Overview

Edward IV is the Yorkist king whose sudden death in April 1483 left a child heir, a divided court, and an unresolved struggle over who would control the succession.

## Current Profile

The episode treats Edward's death rather than his reign as the decisive hinge. His twelve-year-old heir was away from London with the Woodville affinity, Richard of Gloucester held northern power, and the London council tried to preserve a compromise that fear and armed mobilization quickly destroyed.

## Key Characteristics

- Yorkist king whose death opened the immediate protectorate and custody crisis.
- Father of [[EdwardV]] and [[RichardOfShrewsbury]], whose legitimacy was later attacked to authorize Richard III's crown.
- Husband of [[ElizabethWoodville]], whose family network Richard represented as a threat.
- Reportedly attempted a deathbed reconciliation among rival court factions, but left no settlement strong enough to survive him.

## Evidence

- Succession setting: [[139-the-princes-in-the-tower-part-2-glt4685691538]] dates Edward's death to 9 April 1483 and places his heir, queen, council, and brother in separate centers of power.
- Family legitimacy: [[139-the-princes-in-the-tower-part-2-glt4685691538]] follows the later claim that Edward's marriage was invalid and his children therefore illegitimate.
- Failed reconciliation: [[139-the-princes-in-the-tower-part-2-glt4685691538]] reports chronicle evidence that Edward tried to reconcile Hastings and the Woodvilles before dying.

## Qualifications

This profile covers Edward IV only as the predecessor whose death opens the episode's crisis. It does not evaluate his full reign, wars, marriages, governance, or the competing legal claims about his marriage.
