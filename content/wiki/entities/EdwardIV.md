# Edward IV

## Overview

Edward IV is the Yorkist warrior-king whose military restoration, Woodville marriage, factional management, and sudden death in April 1483 created the political inheritance behind the princes-in-the-Tower crisis.

## Current Profile

Edward turned his father's failed claim into kingship through precocious military success, survived Warwick and Clarence's revolt, and returned from exile with Burgundian support. His secret marriage to Elizabeth Woodville asserted independence but redirected patronage toward her family and alienated older magnates. After restoration he appeared to secure the dynasty through two sons and loyal service from Richard of Gloucester, yet his sudden death left the twelve-year-old heir away from London with the Woodville affinity while rival power centers had no time to agree on a settlement.

## Key Characteristics

- Martial Yorkist usurper who won the throne young, lost it temporarily, and recovered it through alliance and battle.
- Husband of [[ElizabethWoodville]], whose unexpected marriage promoted a new affinity and ruptured Warwick's expected influence.
- Father of [[EdwardV]] and [[RichardOfShrewsbury]], whose legitimacy was later attacked to authorize Richard III's crown.
- Ruthless dynastic survivor whom the hosts hold responsible for ordering Henry VI's death.
- King who entrusted Edward V's disciplined Ludlow household to [[AnthonyWoodville]].
- Reportedly attempted a deathbed reconciliation among rival court factions, but left no settlement strong enough to survive him.

## Evidence

- Rise, marriage, and restoration: [[138-the-princes-in-the-tower-part-1-glt6274021473]] follows Edward from youthful Yorkist victory through the Woodville marriage, exile, Burgundian-backed return, Barnet, and Tewkesbury.
- Family government: [[138-the-princes-in-the-tower-part-1-glt6274021473]] describes the Woodvilles' advancement, Edward V's Ludlow upbringing, and Richard of Gloucester's loyal northern service.
- Succession setting: both [[138-the-princes-in-the-tower-part-1-glt6274021473]] and [[139-the-princes-in-the-tower-part-2-glt4685691538]] date Edward's death to 9 April 1483 and place his heir, queen, council, and brother in separate centers of power.
- Family legitimacy: [[139-the-princes-in-the-tower-part-2-glt4685691538]] follows the later claim that Edward's marriage was invalid and his children therefore illegitimate.
- Failed reconciliation: [[139-the-princes-in-the-tower-part-2-glt4685691538]] reports chronicle evidence that Edward tried to reconcile Hastings and the Woodvilles before dying.

## Qualifications

The sources concentrate on the route to the 1483 crisis rather than offering a full reign. The causal weight of the Woodville marriage should not be overstated: magnate ambition, European rivalry, dynastic claims, regional power, and Edward's sudden death also shaped the outcome. Henry VI's killing is attributed to Edward, while Richard's possible complicity remains uncertain.

## What Changed

- Expanded the profile from Edward's death to his rise, marriage, exile, restoration, dynastic violence, and factional legacy.
- Identified the Woodville marriage as a major patronage rupture without treating it as a sufficient cause of the later usurpation.
