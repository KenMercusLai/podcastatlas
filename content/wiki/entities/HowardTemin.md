# Howard Temin

## Overview
Howard Temin is the virologist presented as inferring an RNA-to-DNA step from Rous sarcoma virus and independently discovering reverse transcriptase with [[DavidBaltimore]].

## Current Profile
At the University of Wisconsin, Temin studied how an RNA tumor virus could transform a cell in a stable, heritable-looking way. He treated that persistence as a mechanistic anomaly rather than experimental noise and proposed that viral RNA must somehow be copied into DNA. After roughly a decade of professional isolation, the 1970 enzyme experiment supplied the missing mechanism. The episode also emphasizes his moral directness, using his Nobel-banquet criticism of smoking to connect scientific dissent with public-health conviction.

## Key Characteristics
- Followed a persistent anomaly from cellular transformation to an RNA-to-DNA hypothesis.
- Maintained the hypothesis through prolonged skepticism and professional isolation.
- Independently detected reverse transcriptase in Rous sarcoma virus in 1970.
- Shared the 1975 Nobel Prize with Baltimore and Renato Dulbecco.
- Is portrayed as joining intellectual confidence, curiosity, and a strong moral compass.
