# David Baltimore

## Overview
David Baltimore is the virologist who independently discovered reverse transcriptase in 1970 and helps the episode explain why a short decisive experiment can depend on years of conceptual preparation.

## Current Profile
The episode places Baltimore beside [[HowardTemin]] but in a distinct experimental branch: Temin detected the enzyme in Rous sarcoma virus, while Baltimore detected it in a mouse leukemia virus connected to [[LudwigGross]]'s earlier research. Baltimore's recollection that the decisive work took only two days becomes evidence for a broader methodological claim—the elapsed experiment can be brief because the difficult work lies in knowing what mechanism to seek and how to make it detectable.

## Key Characteristics
- Independently detected an RNA-to-DNA enzyme in a mouse leukemia virus.
- Helped establish reverse transcriptase and retroviruses as a coherent mechanism and virus class.
- Shared the 1975 Nobel Prize with Temin and Renato Dulbecco.
- Emphasizes question selection and experiment design as prerequisites for rapid discovery.
