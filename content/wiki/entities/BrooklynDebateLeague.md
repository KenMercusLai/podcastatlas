## Overview

The Brooklyn Debate League is the debate-education organization that [[MalcolmGladwell]] consults after his Munk Debate loss. In the episode, founder K.M. “Deco” DiColandrea, Sasan Khosravi, Jonathan Conyers, and high-school debaters analyze the performance and teach listening through [[ArgumentFlowing]].

## Current Profile

The organization presents debate as persuasion evaluated by an audience, not as parallel self-expression between opponents. Its coaching joins audience empathy, framing analysis, emotional distance, precise argument tracking, and post-round recognition of an opponent's strengths.

The episode's most distinctive contribution is pedagogical: listening becomes observable work that can be practiced and corrected. Students first identify claims and evidence, then preserve their sequence across responses. Compliments at the end of a debate provide a separate norm for keeping adversarial disagreement from becoming personal contempt.

## Key Characteristics

- Teaches debate as audience-centered persuasion rather than personal testimony alone.
- Treats intellectual empathy as understanding what a judge values and connecting an argument to those values.
- Uses [[ArgumentFlowing]] to make listening and response tracking explicit.
- Coaches debaters to contest unfavorable definitions and framing before arguing within them.
- Separates intense disagreement from personal hostility through post-debate recognition.

## Qualifications

The supplied source is Gladwell's edited account of a coaching intervention, not an independent organizational profile, curriculum evaluation, or comparative study of debate education. It does not establish how consistently every league program uses these methods, how participants are assessed, or whether the pedagogy improves long-term listening outside competitive debate.
