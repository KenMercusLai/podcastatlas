# Mary Fisher

## Overview

Mary Fisher was a seventeenth-century Yorkshire servant who became a Quaker missionary, endured punishment and imprisonment, traveled across the Atlantic and into the Ottoman world, and later formed a lasting marriage.

## Current Profile

[[357-historical-love-island-the-sequel-glt7719682575]] follows Fisher from domestic service into Quaker conviction after hearing George Fox preach. Whipping in Cambridge, imprisonment in Boston, and missions in Barbados and the Ottoman Empire make religious purpose—not romance—the organizing force of her early life. Her later marriage to John Cross and burial beside him in South Carolina allow the episode to cast her as an earnest outsider who unexpectedly finds durable love.

## Key Characteristics

- Yorkshire housemaid transformed by early Quaker preaching.
- Missionary whose religious commitments crossed class and geographic boundaries.
- Survivor of whipping, imprisonment, and exclusion.
- Traveler associated with Barbados, New England, and the Ottoman Empire.
- Later wife of John Cross and emigrant to South Carolina.
