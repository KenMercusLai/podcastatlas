# Raymond of Capua

## Overview

Raymond of Capua was Catherine of Siena's confessor and hagiographic biographer, and his account is a principal transmission route for stories about her visions, temptations, mystical marriage, miracles, and sanctity.

## Current Profile

[[356-the-blood-drinking-bride-of-christ-glt8496536394]] repeatedly relies on Raymond's account for episodes that make [[CatherineOfSiena|Catherine]] legible as a saint: demonic sexual temptations, Christ's presence in her room, miraculous literacy, mystical marriage, and the ring identified with Christ's flesh. His work is therefore both indispensable evidence for Catherine's remembered life and evidence shaped by the purpose of establishing holiness.

## Key Characteristics

- Confessor and biographer closely associated with Catherine's saintly memory.
- Major source for intimate visions and miracle narratives.
- Transmits theological meaning as well as claims about events.
- Requires hagiographic source criticism rather than literal or wholesale dismissal.

## Evidence

- Vision tradition: [[356-the-blood-drinking-bride-of-christ-glt8496536394]] attributes temptation, conversation with Christ, mystical marriage, and miraculous literacy stories to Raymond's account.
- Evidentiary role: [[356-the-blood-drinking-bride-of-christ-glt8496536394]] identifies dependence on hagiography as a central limitation in reconstructing Catherine's life.

## Qualifications

This profile is limited to Raymond's role in one podcast episode and does not reconstruct his full ecclesiastical career, text, theology, chronology, or manuscript tradition. Proximity does not make every narrated event independently verifiable, while hagiographic purpose does not make the account historically useless.
