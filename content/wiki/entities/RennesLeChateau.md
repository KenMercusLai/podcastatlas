# Rennes-le-Château

## Overview

Rennes-le-Château is a village in southwestern France whose priest, treasure legend, regional Cathar associations, and tourism economy became central to the modern [[PrioryOfSion|Priory of Sion]] conspiracy narrative.

## Current Profile

[[301-the-real-da-vinci-code-glt5654192229]] separates several layers that later retellings fused. Bérenger Saunière, priest from 1885 to 1909, renovated his church with money the episode attributes to payments for more masses than he could perform. Noël Corbu, who later operated a hotel and restaurant on property connected to Saunière, promoted treasure and hidden-manuscript stories that could attract visitors. [[PierrePlantard]] then connected the location to forged Merovingian and Priory documentation, and later writers added the sacred bloodline.

The village's location in so-called Cathar country made the story more resonant. Its later association with Cathars, Templars, crystals, treasure, and esoteric tourism shows how a place can retain economic and symbolic value from a narrative even when the narrative's documentary basis is rejected.

## Key Characteristics

- Small village whose modern fame rests heavily on accumulated treasure and conspiracy stories.
- Associated with Bérenger Saunière's unexplained spending, for which the episode gives an illicit mass-selling explanation.
- Commercially promoted through Noël Corbu's treasure and hidden-manuscript narratives.
- Incorporated by Plantard and later writers into the Priory and sacred-bloodline mythology.
- Made narratively plausible by proximity to the contested history and modern reception of the [[Cathars]].

## Evidence

- Priest and money: [[301-the-real-da-vinci-code-glt5654192229]] distinguishes Saunière's church spending and catalog-bought sculpture from treasure or papal-blackmail explanations.
- Commercial legend: [[301-the-real-da-vinci-code-glt5654192229]] attributes treasure and manuscript promotion to Corbu's effort to attract visitors to his remote business.
- Conspiracy integration: [[301-the-real-da-vinci-code-glt5654192229]] traces de Sède, Lincoln, Plantard, and later publishing through the village story.
- Regional afterlife: [[301-the-real-da-vinci-code-glt5654192229]] describes later Cathar, Templar, crystal, and esoteric tourism.

## Qualifications

The claim about Corbu's commercial motive is an interpretation, and the episode is not a complete local economic or architectural history. Rejecting treasure and sacred-bloodline explanations does not by itself settle every detail of Saunière's finances or the beliefs of later visitors.

## What Changed

- Established a layered profile separating Saunière's finances, Corbu's tourist legend, Plantard's fabrication, and later esoteric branding.
