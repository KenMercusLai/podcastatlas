# Wat Tyler

## Overview

Wat Tyler was the principal Kentish leader of the [[PeasantsRevolt1381|Peasants’ Revolt of 1381]], organizing its march toward London before his expanded demands and death at Smithfield precipitated the rising’s decisive crisis.

## Current Profile

Almost nothing about Tyler before 1381 is secure; even suggestions of military experience remain speculative. By 10 June he had emerged as captain of the Kentish rebels and led their organized movement from Canterbury toward London. The early demands associated with the march included abolition of serfdom, free choice of employer and negotiated wages, and seizure of church wealth.

At the Smithfield meeting of 15 June, after the Mile End concessions, Tyler demanded abolition of poaching restrictions and manorial courts together with a severe reduction of church hierarchy and property. These claims place him at the junction of social, legal, economic, and religious radicalism rather than within a single modern ideology.

The confrontation became fatal after Tyler was insulted as a rebel and robber. [[WilliamWalworth|William Walworth]] attempted to arrest him, Tyler drew a dagger, and Walworth struck him; Tyler was carried to St Bartholomew’s Hospital, then seized and beheaded. Whether the encounter was planned remains unclear. His fall left armed archers facing the royal party until [[RichardIIOfEngland|Richard II]] claimed leadership of the rebels and redirected them toward Clerkenwell.

## Key Characteristics

- Rebel leader associated with the rising’s most expansive social and religious demands.
- Kentish captain who led the organized march from Canterbury toward London.
- Negotiator who continued pressing claims after the Mile End settlement.
- Figure whose conduct at Smithfield is reconstructed through hostile and uncertain narrative evidence.
- Death catalyst whose removal created both a massacre risk and an opening for royal intervention.

## Evidence

- Emergence and march: [[413-the-peasants-revolt-england-erupts-part-1-glt8198769595]] identifies Tyler as Kentish captain by 10 June and links his leadership to the London advance and early program.
- Political program: [[414-the-peasants-revolt-londons-burning-part-2-glt6726049231]] attributes demands on hunting, manorial courts, and church hierarchy to Tyler at Smithfield.
- Fatal confrontation: [[414-the-peasants-revolt-londons-burning-part-2-glt6726049231]] narrates the attempted arrest, dagger, blows, hospitalization, seizure, and beheading.
- Immediate consequence: [[414-the-peasants-revolt-londons-burning-part-2-glt6726049231]] links Tyler’s fall to the archers’ threat and Richard’s personal intervention.

## Qualifications

The sources do not provide a full biography of Tyler or independent verification of the confrontation’s dialogue and sequence. His origins and possible military experience remain speculative, the degree of planning is unresolved, and descriptions such as “rebel and robber” belong to the conflict’s hostile framing rather than a neutral profile.

## What Changed

- Added Tyler’s emergence as Kentish captain and organizer of the Canterbury-to-London march.
- Joined the early anti-serfdom and free-labor program to his later Smithfield demands.
