# UBS / 瑞银

## Overview
[[UBS|UBS / 瑞银]] is a Swiss global bank whose acquisition of [[CreditSuisse|Credit Suisse]] made it still larger relative to [[Switzerland|Switzerland]] and intensified the country's too-big-to-fail problem.

## Current Profile
The bounded source presents UBS as a profitable post-merger institution caught between the benefits of its Swiss base and a proposed capital rule designed to protect that same base from foreign-subsidiary losses. Reported headquarters-relocation discussions create an option for resisting the rule, but they are not evidence of a decided move or a withdrawal of Swiss retail operations.

## Key Characteristics
- Operates as a global bank with assets unusually large relative to the economy of its home state.
- Became the emergency acquirer of [[CreditSuisse|Credit Suisse]] in 2023 under state-supported crisis arrangements.
- Faces a proposed [[SystemicallyImportantBankCapitalCoverage|parent-capital coverage]] rule that would retain more loss-absorbing equity inside the group.
- Has shareholders whose dividends and buybacks would compete with the proposed capital requirement.
- Depends on Swiss talent, institutional stability, legal credibility, and banking identity even if its formal headquarters could move.

## Evidence
- Scale and rescue role: [[shangye-xiaoyang-51-shenme-ruiyin-yaoba-zongbu-banli-ruishi-gkwrijiotaj1agndggtvuvds]] reports roughly $1.7tn in assets and describes the 2023 Credit Suisse acquisition as a state-enabled containment of systemic risk.
- Capital conflict: [[shangye-xiaoyang-51-shenme-ruiyin-yaoba-zongbu-banli-ruishi-gkwrijiotaj1agndggtvuvds]] says the revised proposal could require about $16bn of additional capital, affecting distributions or encouraging asset sales and balance-sheet reduction.
- Relocation option: [[shangye-xiaoyang-51-shenme-ruiyin-yaoba-zongbu-banli-ruishi-gkwrijiotaj1agndggtvuvds]] distinguishes moving the group headquarters from closing the Swiss bank and treats the signal as potentially strategic.

## Qualifications
The episode is a compact secondary-source explainer based partly on anonymous reporting. It does not independently verify UBS's internal deliberations, prove U.S. acceptance, establish a merger plan, or show what rule Switzerland will finally enact. Asset size, capital needs, savings, profits, inflows, and shareholder effects remain source-scoped and time-sensitive.

## What Changed
- Established UBS as the surviving institution through which the Credit Suisse rescue now changes Swiss systemic risk.
- Added the tension between shareholder distributions and parent-level loss-absorbing capital.
- Added headquarters relocation as a possible but unconfirmed regulatory bargaining option.
