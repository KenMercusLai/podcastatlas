## Overview

张奂 / Zhang Huan is a late-Han frontier and court official whose current wiki profile now begins with rehabilitation through [[HuangfuGuiLateHan|皇甫规]]'s recommendation in the 163 CE frontier crisis. Later layers make him a commander who combines pacification, selective force, court remonstrance, and survival inside unstable imperial politics.

## Current Profile

Zhang Huan's earliest current layer is a return from political disability into frontier usefulness. Hanji 859 says he had been barred from office after the Liang Ji case, that Huangfu Gui defended him repeatedly, and that he was first reused as Wuwei taishou. When frontier war intensified, Huangfu Gui then recommended Zhang as the better large-army commander and accepted a lower role, so the court made Zhang Duliao general.

The subsequent frontier record emphasizes calibrated command rather than one-note militarism. Across Hanji 870, 875, 878, 883, 887, 889, and 890, Zhang appears in famine redeployment, recall, Eastern Qiang pacification, selective pursuit, and court crisis. He can accept command, pursue enemies when needed, and also prefer settlement when the political objective is stabilization rather than destruction.

His court-politics profile is mixed but resilient. In Hanji 887-890, he becomes entangled in anti-eunuch maneuvers and countercoup dynamics, survives by reading danger quickly, and later remonstrates or refuses cooperation when a policy threatens state interest. Hanji 902 preserves him as an emotional-control and endurance example, so the full profile is not merely battlefield skill: it is the capacity to move between frontier coercion, negotiated order, and dangerous court judgment.

## Key Characteristics

- Rehabilitated frontier talent whose return to office depends on Huangfu Gui's repeated defense.
- Large-army commander preferred by Huangfu Gui during the 163 CE frontier emergency.
- Uses a mix of coercion and settlement in Qiang and border policy instead of default extermination.
- Operates in both field command and court crisis, where tactical success can become political vulnerability.
- Reads danger and survives factional turbulence better than many late-Han peers.
- Practices remonstrance and selective noncooperation when policy crosses his state-interest threshold.
- Serves as a bridge between frontier command, court personnel dependence, and late-Han survival politics.
