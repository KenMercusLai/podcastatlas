## Overview

张奂 / Zhang Huan is a late-Han official, scholar, and frontier figure whose current wiki profile joins three early [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]]-era danger cases. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-889-weishenme-haoren-bu-changming-huohai-yi-qiannian-lt-g7qgkcf-fhy3xccecjagwqhku|Hanji 889]] shows his post-coup moral injury: he hates eunuch domination, is used by the eunuch side, refuses a compromised marquisate, and then tries to repair the [[DouWuLateHan|窦武]] and [[ChenFanLateHan|陈蕃]] grievance. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-890-xiang-dangguan-xian-xue-duifu-xiaoren-ltnvh6qu-mr8flsdlxf-e6rknpis|Hanji 890]] continues the direct-remonstrance consequences, while [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-902-kongzhi-qingxu-shi-rensheng-bixiuke-ljynylvdlsdtjf4es9jmilav863b|Hanji 902]] later makes him a survival-first contrast with [[DuanJiongLateHan|段熲]].

## Current Profile

Zhang Huan's page now begins with compromised reward rather than only direct protest. In Hanji 889, he is promoted to dasinong and granted a marquisate after the failed anti-eunuch coup, but refuses the title because the victory that elevated him also used him. He then answers an omen consultation by asking that Dou Wu and Chen Fan receive proper burial, their families and followers be released from punishment, and [[EmpressDowagerDouMiao|窦皇太后]] receive due respect. [[EmperorLingOfHan|Emperor Ling]] is initially inclined to accept the memorial, but eunuch opposition blocks decisive repair.

Hanji 890 shows the next stage of the same exposure. Zhang Huan continues direct pro-party and anti-eunuch memory pressure, then joins [[LiuMengLateHan|刘猛]] in recommending [[WangChangLateHan|王畅]] and [[LiYingLateHan|李膺]] for high office. That posture angers [[CaoJieLateHan|曹节]]'s side and brings detention, rebuke, and a salary fine.

Hanji 902 shows a later and harsher risk environment. After [[WangYuLateHan|王寓]]'s refused recommendation becomes an accusation, Duan Jiong's pressure threatens Zhang's life. Zhang survives by lowering his posture in a letter, withdrawing from office, and turning his remaining agency toward teaching and writing. The synthesis is not that Zhang is timid; it is that his career shows why direct moral exposure and later self-preserving humility both belong to late-Han political survival.

## Key Characteristics

- Zhang Huan is presented as hostile to eunuch-side injustice but still vulnerable to being used by the side he opposes.
- He refuses a morally compromised reward, making guilt and reputational self-preservation part of his profile before formal punishment arrives.
- He uses omen and loyalty language to reopen dangerous anti-eunuch grievances around Dou Wu, Chen Fan, and Empress Dowager Dou.
- His public recommendations connect him to the party-figure reputation field around Wang Chang and Li Ying.
- He is politically exposed because personal grievance, party-prohibition labeling, and palace-side interests can convert upright conduct into liability.
- His later humbled letter and post-office writing turn survival into preserved agency rather than mere escape.

## Qualifications

- Hanji 889 and Hanji 890 render the name as "张宦"; this page normalizes to 张奂 because the role, office context, and existing late-Han pattern match Zhang Huan.
- Hanji 889's short transcript is useful for sequence and interpretation but should not be treated as independent proof of every proper name without cross-checking.
- The current page is still episode-bounded and should not be treated as a full biography of Zhang's frontier service.
- The survival reading should not erase Zhang's earlier directness; the page intentionally keeps both the exposed remonstrant and the humbled survivor in one profile.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 889 as Zhang Huan's immediate post-coup guilt, reward-refusal, and omen-memorial branch.
- Reframed the page so his direct remonstrance begins with compromised elevation before continuing into Hanji 890's punishment.
- Preserved Hanji 902's later survival-first profile as a later adaptation rather than a contradiction.
