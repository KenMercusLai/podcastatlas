# Aeneas

## Overview

Aeneas is the Trojan exile and son of Venus whose divinely directed journey in the [[Aeneid]] makes him an ancestor of Roman destiny and the agent of [[Dido]]'s tragedy.

## Current Profile

The episode introduces Aeneas through the tension between obligation and harm. Shipwreck brings him to unfinished [[Carthage]], where Dido welcomes him and hears his account of Troy. Their love cannot override the command that he continue toward Italy and the future Rome. He leaves, Dido dies, and her curse gives their private rupture a retrospective connection to Hannibal and the Punic conflict.

Aeneas is therefore not presented simply as a romantic betrayer or faultless national hero. He obeys a destiny larger than himself, but obedience does not cancel its victims. His role in the episode is to embody duty under an imperial narrative whose legitimacy and cost remain visible at the same time.

## Key Characteristics

- Trojan exile whose journey is oriented toward the future foundation of Rome.
- Son of Venus and participant in a divinely ordered history.
- Guest and lover of Dido after reaching Carthage.
- Hero who subordinates attachment to commanded political destiny.
- Figure whose obedience remains morally costly even when the poem treats it as necessary.

## Qualifications

The page describes Aeneas as an epic and legendary figure, not a securely recoverable historical traveler. The relationship with Dido is chronologically incompatible with straightforward foundation history, and divine commands, dialogue, motives, and the curse's relation to later war belong to literary interpretation.

## What Changed

- Established Aeneas as a duty-bound hero whose Roman destination does not erase the damage caused along the way.
- Separated his epic role from historical claims about Carthage's foundation.

## Relationships

- [[Virgil]] - poet who makes Aeneas's duty central to Roman foundation memory.
- [[Aeneid]] - epic in which his Trojan exile becomes Roman destiny.
- [[Dido]] - host and lover abandoned when he resumes the commanded journey.
- [[Carthage]] - city where private tragedy is linked retrospectively to Roman-Carthaginian conflict.
- [[ImperialDestinyHumanCost]] - framework for reading the harm produced by his required departure.
