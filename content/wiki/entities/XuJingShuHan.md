# 许靖 / Xu Jing (Shu-Han)

## Overview
许靖 / Xu Jing is presented in Hanji 1146 as a widely famous evaluator of people whose public reputation exceeds [[LiuBei|刘备]]'s private judgment of his ability, conduct, and loyalty.

## Current Profile
The episode associates Xu Jing and his younger cousin [[XuShao|许劭]] with reputation-making judgments that could affect a scholar's standing. It then traces Xu Jing through repeated displacement and service before Liu Bei's Yizhou campaign. His reported attempt to surrender during the siege of Chengdu leads Liu Bei to despise him and resist substantive employment.

[[FaZheng|法正]] argues that the new ruler cannot treat this as a purely private personnel decision. Because Xu Jing is famous, public neglect could be read as contempt for worthy people and damage future recruitment. Liu Bei therefore grants Xu Jing high office and honor while withholding substantive military and administrative authority. The result distinguishes reputational inclusion from operational trust.

## Key Characteristics
- Possesses broad public fame rooted partly in elite reputation assessment.
- Is judged by Liu Bei as weaker in ability, conduct, and loyalty than his standing suggests.
- Becomes politically costly to ignore because treatment of a famous man signals how a ruler treats talent generally.
- Receives high rank and ceremonial respect without corresponding operational authority.
- Illustrates the gap between public reputation, private assessment, and role-specific trust.

## Qualifications
The current profile comes from one structured podcast summary. Xu Jing's itinerary, kinship with Xu Shao, attempted surrender, degree of fame, actual competence, character, offices, duties, and Liu Bei's motives require broader evidence. Fa Zheng's recommendation may explain the appointment without proving that reputation was its only cause. This 许靖 is distinct from [[XuJingLateHan|徐敬]].
