# Zachary Knight

## Overview
Zachary Knight is presented in the source as a UCSF physiology professor and Howard Hughes Medical Institute investigator studying the neural control of hunger, thirst, satiety, salt appetite, and body weight.

## Current Profile
In the episode, Knight explains homeostatic behavior as coordinated control across timescales. Brainstem circuits help terminate a meal from immediate gut signals, while hypothalamic and forebrain circuits track longer-term energy or fluid need. His laboratory's AGRP and thirst-neuron recordings supply the episode's central predictive-control result: sensory cues can suppress a drive before food or water has corrected the underlying physiology, with later internal feedback confirming or revising the forecast.

Knight also connects basic circuit research to obesity and drug development. He treats body-weight variation as biologically defended yet environmentally shifted, separates endogenous GLP-1 physiology from high-exposure pharmacology, and remains optimistic that multi-target anti-obesity medicines will offer different efficacy and side-effect profiles. His practical guidance stays comparatively restrained: emphasize whole foods and adequate protein, but do not turn incomplete circuit maps into individualized prescriptions.

## Key Characteristics
- Explains hunger, thirst, and satiety through interacting brain, gut, endocrine, sensory, and learning systems.
- Uses rapid AGRP and thirst-neuron responses to argue that homeostatic control is predictive rather than purely reactive.
- Separates short-term consummatory control from longer-term appetitive and reserve-regulation systems.
- Frames obesity through both genetic susceptibility and environmental change rather than a willpower-only account.
- Distinguishes endogenous hormone signaling from pharmacologic GLP-1 exposure and next-generation multi-agonist treatment.
- Keeps animal-circuit mechanisms and practical human advice inside explicit translation and evidence limits.

- Multi-timescale homeostasis - [[the-science-of-hunger-medications-to-combat-obesity-dr-zachary-knight-scim7873255890]] contrasts brainstem meal-termination signals with hypothalamic and forebrain control of food seeking, energy reserves, osmolality, and thirst.
