# Curtis LeMay

## Overview

Curtis LeMay was a U.S. air commander associated with the incendiary bombing of Japanese cities in World War II, later head of Strategic Air Command, and George Wallace's vice-presidential running mate in 1968.

## Current Profile

In [[TheBomberMafiaBook|The Bomber Mafia]] discussion, LeMay is contrasted with [[HaywoodHansell]]. After the precision-bombing approach failed, LeMay adopted incendiary urban bombing with napalm as a means of forcing Japan's defeat. Gladwell emphasizes the catastrophic alternatives and command constraints, while Grant resists allowing contextual sympathy to eclipse civilian suffering.

[[goodbye-farewell-and-amen-goodbye-farewell-and-amen]] extends that contrast across Japan, Korea, and Vietnam. It presents LeMay as believing that once war began, overwhelming force was the moral way to shorten it. His Korea argument treats initial restraint as the cause of a longer and ultimately more destructive campaign, but Vietnam exposes the danger of carrying that answer into a strategically different conflict. The profile that emerges is not only aggressive; it is technologically and doctrinally locked onto the instrument that had made him successful.

LeMay later brought military stature and an aggressive bombing reputation to the [[AmericanIndependentParty]] ticket, but his unveiling became a campaign failure. Despite aides' attempts to keep him away from the topic, he discussed possible nuclear-weapons utility and called public attitudes a phobia, making the ticket appear dangerous and poorly controlled.

## Key Characteristics

- Former Strategic Air Command leader with an aggressive air-power reputation.
- Commander associated in the bounded source with incendiary urban bombing against Japan.
- Strategic contrast to Hansell's precision-bombing ideal.
- Advocate of overwhelming early force as a way to reduce total war deaths.
- Reused an air-power solution across Japan, Korea, and Vietnam despite changing strategic conditions.
- Selected after far-right backers rejected a more moderate running-mate option.
- Publicly entertained nuclear-weapons use at his campaign unveiling.
