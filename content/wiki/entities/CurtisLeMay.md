# Curtis LeMay

## Overview

Curtis LeMay was a U.S. air commander associated with the incendiary bombing of Japanese cities in World War II, later head of Strategic Air Command, and George Wallace's vice-presidential running mate in 1968.

## Current Profile

In the Bomber Mafia sources, LeMay is contrasted with [[HaywoodHansell]]. [[may-the-best-firebomb-win-may-the-best-firebomb-win]] first places him inside precision doctrine, requiring crews to study maps and targets because accurate attack depended on preparation and recognition under poor visibility. [[bombs-away-lemay-bombs-away-lemay]] then presents him as an unusually capable navigator, pilot, mechanic, organizer, and practical commander who shared aircrew risk but elevated action over hesitation. That combination made him effective at solving operational problems and dangerous when task completion displaced reconsideration of civilian consequences.

After Hansell's precision campaign failed, LeMay first persisted with high-altitude attacks and then treated the jet stream as an operational chain: flying below it required lower altitude, daylight at low altitude increased vulnerability, and night operations removed precision. He chose low-altitude incendiary area bombing, stripped most defensive guns to increase bomb load, and launched Operation Meetinghouse against a densely populated Tokyo district. The episode reports more than 100,000 deaths that night and raids on another 66 cities, while refusing to let claimed military success settle moral legitimacy.

[[goodbye-farewell-and-amen-goodbye-farewell-and-amen]] extends that contrast across Japan, Korea, and Vietnam. It presents LeMay as believing that once war began, overwhelming force was the moral way to shorten it. His Korea argument treats initial restraint as the cause of a longer and ultimately more destructive campaign, but Vietnam exposes the danger of carrying that answer into a strategically different conflict. The profile that emerges is not only aggressive; it is technologically and doctrinally locked onto the instrument that had made him successful.

LeMay later brought military stature and an aggressive bombing reputation to the [[AmericanIndependentParty]] ticket, but his unveiling became a campaign failure. Despite aides' attempts to keep him away from the topic, he discussed possible nuclear-weapons utility and called public attitudes a phobia, making the ticket appear dangerous and poorly controlled.

## Key Characteristics

- Highly capable practical air commander who shared operational risk and prioritized decisive action.
- Initially presented as enforcing the preparation needed for precision bombing.
- Commander associated in the bounded sources with incendiary urban bombing against Japan.
- Adapted to the jet stream and targeting failure by shifting from high-altitude precision attacks to low-altitude night area bombing.
- Advocate of overwhelming early force as a way to reduce total war deaths.
- Reused an air-power solution across Japan, Korea, and Vietnam despite changing strategic conditions.
- Later Strategic Air Command leader and Wallace running mate whose nuclear remarks created a campaign crisis.

## Qualifications

The page covers LeMay through four interpretive podcast discussions of bombing and one account of Wallace's campaign. It does not independently assess his complete military career, command authority, target decisions, casualty record, strategic doctrine, or the full context of remarks attributed to him about Korea, Vietnam, and nuclear weapons. The precision-preparation account, USS Utah story, Operation Meetinghouse figures, and claims that air power avoided invasion remain source-scoped; counterfactual claims that greater initial violence would have shortened war remain unresolved.
