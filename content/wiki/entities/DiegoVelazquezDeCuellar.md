## Overview

Diego Velazquez de Cuellar was the governor of Cuba who commissioned [[HernanCortes|Hernan Cortes]] to explore westward but did not authorize a conquest of Mexico.

## Current Profile

The episodes first place Velazquez in the [[SpanishCaribbeanExpansion|conquest and settlement of Cuba]], where he pursued [[Hatuey]], distributed encomiendas, sent gold to the crown, and built a colonial government in which [[HernanCortes|Hernan Cortes]] served as secretary and magistrate. Their relationship mixed patronage with rivalry: Cortes's humiliation and forced marriage after a dispute involving Catalina Suarez supplied one source-scoped reason for later resentment.

After the Cordoba and Grijalva expeditions revealed organized mainland societies and gold, Velazquez needed to move before rival colonial authorities did. Relatives declined the risk, so he selected Cortes as capable but supposedly controllable. The later episode presents Velazquez as sending Cortes to find Grijalva, explore Yucatan, investigate routes and reported peoples, spread Christianity, and gather information about gold. When he became alarmed by Cortes and tried to cancel the expedition, Cortes sailed anyway.

Velazquez's authority was then displaced through the [[VeraCruzLegalCoup|Vera Cruz legal coup]]. Cortes founded a municipality whose council appointed him chief justice and captain general, then sent treasure to Spain rather than Cuba in an appeal for direct royal recognition.

## Key Characteristics

- Governor constrained from traveling by crisis in Cuba.
- Conqueror and colonizer whose Cuban authority rested on violent expansion, encomiendas, gold, and royal patronage.
- Sponsor reacting to earlier westward voyages and competition from other colonial authorities.
- Sponsor of an exploratory and evangelical mission that stopped short of authorizing conquest.
- Superior who tried unsuccessfully to revoke Cortes's command.
- Rival authority bypassed through municipal legal form and direct appeal to the crown.

## Qualifications

These sources give Velazquez mainly through the Cuban conquest and the authority Cortes evaded, not as a complete governor's biography. The Hatuey stories, Catalina Suarez dispute, exact reasons for selecting Cortes, and participants' motives remain source-scoped. The commission's ambiguities gave Cortes room to reinterpret opportunity, but they do not amount to conquest authorization.

## What Changed

- Extended the profile backward to the conquest and colonial government of Cuba.
- Made earlier reconnaissance, gold, and inter-colonial competition causal to Cortes's selection.
- Established the limited commission against which Cortes's later legal claims must be judged.
- Connected Velazquez's displacement to the founding of Vera Cruz and direct royal appeal.
