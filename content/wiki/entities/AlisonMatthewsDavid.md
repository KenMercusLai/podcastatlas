# Alison Matthews David

## Overview

Alison Matthews David is a fashion, dress, and textiles historian whose work connects clothing to technology, labor, medicine, gender, class, crime, and regulation. In the covered episode, she discusses her book [[FashionVictimsBook|Fashion Victims]] and its history of dangerous dress.

## Current Profile

Matthews David approaches fashion as material and occupational history rather than as style alone. She follows how textile machinery, flammable fabrics, restrictive silhouettes, mercury felt, arsenical dyes, lead cosmetics, and modern garment processes distribute risk between wearers and makers.

Her interpretation avoids a simple ignorance story. People could know that a product was dangerous yet continue using or producing it because it delivered status, beauty, comfort, profit, or employment and because regulation and bargaining power were weak. She also cautions against judging historical wearers too easily: crinolines could create bodily space, corsets could feel supportive, and fashionable clothing could provide pleasure even when it imposed costs.

## Key Characteristics

- Reads dress and textiles as evidence about technology, labor, public health, and social power.
- Centers makers and performers alongside consumers when identifying fashion's victims.
- Explains persistent danger through incentives, status, regulation, and worker power as well as knowledge gaps.
- Preserves wearer agency and historical experience without minimizing material harm.
- Connects historical poisons and fire hazards to contemporary global garment-work exposure.
