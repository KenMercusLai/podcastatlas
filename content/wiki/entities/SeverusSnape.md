# Severus Snape / 西弗勒斯·斯内普

## Overview

Severus Snape is a Hogwarts teacher represented here through two sharply bounded uses: a character in [[HarryPotterAndThePhilosophersStone|《哈利·波特与魔法石》]] whose hostility supports a false accusation, and an analogy used to describe [[IsaacNewton|Isaac Newton / 牛顿]] without establishing literary influence.

## Current Profile

In the first Harry Potter story, Snape's public severity, hostility toward Harry, discretionary punishment, and visible spell-casting behavior make him a credible suspect. The reveal that he was protecting Harry rather than cursing him does not erase the conduct that made suspicion plausible; it changes the causal interpretation of selected scenes. Later-series knowledge of his love for Lily and double-agent role further complicates rereading, but the bounded source treats those facts mainly as retrospective context.

The Newton comparison remains separate. Similarities in childhood injury, isolation, brilliance, bitterness, severity, and atmosphere make Snape a useful analogy, not evidence that Newton was a prototype or direct source.

## Key Characteristics

- Functions as a plausible false suspect because real hostility supports an incorrect causal conclusion.
- Combines protective action with behavior that remains punitive, biased, and difficult to excuse.
- Gains additional ambiguity when later revelations change how earlier scenes are read.
- Serves as a cultural analogy for wounded brilliance and severity without proving authorial influence.

## Qualifications

The sources do not provide a complete character study. The Harry Potter discussion uses later-series knowledge while focusing on the first installment, and the Newton episode supplies no evidence of direct literary influence. Protective action in one scene should not be generalized into acquittal for Snape's broader teaching conduct.
