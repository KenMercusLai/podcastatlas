# Charles François Dumouriez

## Overview

Charles François Dumouriez was a French general whose 1792 campaigns helped stop the Prussians at [[BattleOfValmy|Valmy]] and then carried the revolutionary war into Belgium.

## Current Profile

The sources present Dumouriez first as the Girondin foreign minister and veteran who sold Belgium as an easy liberation campaign, assuming its population would rise when French forces crossed the border. As an operational commander later redirected to France's defence, his position helped deter Brunswick by threatening Prussian supply lines; after Valmy, victory at Jemappes and the advance into Belgium made him central to the shift from defensive survival toward [[RevolutionaryIdeologicalWar|ideological expansion]].

## Key Characteristics

- He initially preferred a campaign in Belgium but was ordered to block the Prussian invasion.
- He promoted the opening Belgian campaign as easy and treated local support as an assumption rather than an established fact.
- His maneuvering created a threat to Brunswick's supply and retreat routes.
- Kellermann's reinforcement enabled the French stand at Valmy.
- He resumed the offensive after the Prussian withdrawal.
- His victory at Jemappes opened Brussels and further Belgian advances to French forces.

## Qualifications

- This page is bounded to Dumouriez's role from Valmy through the 1792 Belgian offensive.
- His later attempted move against Paris and defection, represented elsewhere in the wiki, qualify any purely heroic profile but are not narrated in this episode.
- The claim that Belgians would rise for France was a policy premise, not demonstrated consent.
- The source does not isolate his decisions from Prussian logistical failure or the contributions of Kellermann and French regulars.

## What Changed

- The profile now begins with the overconfident Belgian liberation premise that preceded his later military success.
