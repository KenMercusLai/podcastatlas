## Overview

刘宽 / Liu Kuan is a late-Han official whose profile now combines humane local governance with later senior-office accountability. Hanji 870 gives the fullest character portrait: Liu Kuan is summoned under [[EmperorHuanOfHan|汉桓帝]] as shangshu ling after earlier commandery service marked by leniency, teaching, and punishment designed to shame rather than injure.

## Current Profile

Liu Kuan is no longer only a name in later [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]] office churn. Hanji 870 backfills why his reputation matters: he governs through calmness and moral instruction, using a reed whip for wrongdoing so that punishment signals shame without adding bodily pain. He also teaches different audiences differently, urging elders toward farming and younger people toward filial piety, sibling affection, and study.

Hanji 907 and Hanji 909 then place the same official type inside late-Han high-office instability. He moves through senior posts before the failed Xianbei expedition and is later removed as taiwei after a solar eclipse following military failure. The current synthesis therefore treats him as a humane-governance figure whose later role becomes annalistic office responsibility rather than a developed policy voice.

## Key Characteristics

- Humane local administrator associated with calm conduct, lenient punishment, and moral education.
- Uses punishment as shame and correction rather than bodily suffering.
- Promoted under Emperor Huan to shangshu ling after earlier commandery service.
- Later appears in Emperor Ling-era senior-office churn before and after frontier failure.
- Functions in Hanji 909 as an omen-linked responsibility marker rather than as the author of the failed Xianbei policy.

## Evidence

- Local leniency and teaching: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-870-gudai-tizhinei-buganle-meiyou-tuilu-lpwbniu9ifpt6cp4ip9czqwsh5xd|Hanji 870]] says Liu Kuan served three commanderies, avoided shouting even under urgency, used a reed whip for correction, encouraged farming among elders, and urged younger people toward filial piety, sibling affection, and learning.
- Huan-era appointment: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-870-gudai-tizhinei-buganle-meiyou-tuilu-lpwbniu9ifpt6cp4ip9czqwsh5xd|Hanji 870]] says the court summons Liu Kuan as shangshu ling, identifying him as the son of a former senior minister in the episode's account.
- Senior-office churn: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-907-suoyou-de-bing-doushi-ni-de-xinbing-ltqzyohcauduwuanlvqph9l81d79|Hanji 907]] places Liu Kuan in the 176 CE senior-office movement before the later Xianbei-expedition failure.
- Eclipse accountability: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-909-2025-ruhe-yangcheng-yi-shen-guiqi-lomndmphrybunjbbbbn9ebafcsj|Hanji 909]] says Liu Kuan is removed as taiwei after the tenth-month first-day solar eclipse that follows the failed 177 CE Xianbei expedition, with [[MengYuLateHan|孟郁]] appointed after him.

## Qualifications

The current evidence does not reconstruct Liu Kuan's full biography or all offices. Hanji 870 gives a moralized official portrait, while Hanji 907 and Hanji 909 use him mainly as part of senior-office chronology. The source's reference to his father is kept source-scoped because the current wiki does not need a separate canonical page for that family detail.
