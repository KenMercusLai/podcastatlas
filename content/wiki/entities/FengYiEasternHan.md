## Overview

冯异 / Feng Yi is synthesized as a Guangwu-era Eastern Han commander whose current wiki profile joins remembered founding hardship with the western campaign against [[WeiXiaoEasternHan|隗嚣]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-701-liuxiu-de-diwang-quanshu-zhezhong-ren-bixu-sha-lmsoxwg1k3k931ccnpeaomxfcu3q|Hanji 701]] shows [[LiuXiu|光武帝刘秀]] publicly recalling Feng Yi's early service, flight-era food support, and Guanzhong pacification before Feng Yi answers by reminding the ruler not to forget the Hebei struggle. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-702-fengyi-de-yichang-ju-ruhe-zhansha-weixiao-liangwanren-lqckx-aclchkzqqe3pttnklu9vot|Hanji 702]] gives the defining battlefield layer: after a Han-side retreat, Feng Yi rejects fear-driven delay, reaches the disputed city before Wei Xiao's advancing force, conceals his position, and routs the enemy through a sudden sortie. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-705-1-diwang-moulue-liuxiu-ruhe-yi-sheling-nizhuan-caiyuan-fengyun-lq2m06p7eczj9ebysxpl84bkzzi|Hanji 705-1]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-709-mian-dui-bangjia-an-guren-weihe-buguan-renzhi-sihuo-luknor8yw839qwxogeyzwluo6qe2|Hanji 709]] keep him visible as the western conflict continues against Wei Xiao and later holdout forces.

## Current Profile

Hanji 701 supplies Feng Yi's court-recognition layer. When Feng Yi comes from Chang'an to Luoyang, Liu Xiu tells the assembled officials that Feng Yi had served as his early uprising clerk, cut through hardship, and later pacified Guanzhong. Liu Xiu also recalls Feng Yi feeding him bean gruel at Wulou Pavilion and wheat rice by the Hutuo River during flight, making old service and personal survival memory part of public court recognition.

Feng Yi's answer is restrained rather than self-advertising. He thanks Liu Xiu and invokes the Guan Zhong-Qi Huan Gong precedent to ask the ruler not to forget the difficulty in Hebei, while also saying that he will not forget Liu Xiu's earlier grace. In the current evidence this makes Feng Yi not only a battlefield actor but a minister who can turn remembered favor into a reciprocal warning about political memory.

Hanji 702 places Feng Yi after the Han army has retreated from Longshan. Liu Xiu assigns him to hold a target city, but before he arrives, Wei Xiao sends Wang Yuan and Xing Xun with more than 20,000 troops down Longshan to seize it. Feng Yi's officers, still shaken by the previous defeat, want to stop, camp in a favorable place, and avoid direct contention.

Feng Yi reads the situation differently. The key issue is not whether the Han army should seek a field battle, but whether the enemy will first occupy a city whose loss could shake Sanfu. He argues that the Han side may lack enough offensive momentum but still has defensive capacity, so speed rather than caution becomes decisive.

He then advances quickly, enters the city secretly, closes the gates, and hides his force. When Xing Xun's army arrives without realizing Feng Yi is already inside, Feng Yi makes drums and flags visible and suddenly sorties. The enemy falls into disorder, the Han side pursues, and the source treats the result as the episode's example of identifying and seizing a battlefield decision point.

The same source carries the result beyond one tactical action. Ji Zun also defeats Wang Yuan on another line; Beidi local strongmen begin turning away from Wei Xiao; Liu Xiu sends Feng Yi into Yiqu; and Feng Yi defeats Jia Lan plus Xiongnu-linked forces before Beidi, [[Shangjun|上郡]], and [[AndingCommandery|安定郡]] submit.

Hanji 705-1 shows Feng Yi still active in the same western pressure field. Wei Xiao invades Anding with more than 30,000 troops, but Feng Yi and Ji Zun prevent success before the later Lüeyang attrition sequence becomes central.

Hanji 709 preserves the downstream cost of the same front. Its source note says Feng Yi's final Wei Chun campaign shows that holdout pacification remains costly even after broader Eastern Han consolidation, but it does not provide enough detail to turn that later campaign into the core of this page.

## Key Characteristics

- Guangwu western-campaign commander whose current profile begins with first occupying a disputed city before Wei Xiao's side can take it.
- Remembered founding follower whose early hardship support is made publicly visible by Liu Xiu.
- Court respondent who turns praise and reward into a reminder that the ruler should preserve difficult founding memory.
- Battlefield reader who distinguishes a decisive terrain problem from a fear-driven question of whether to fight at all.
- Commander who uses secrecy, closed gates, drums, flags, and sudden sortie to convert defense into tactical surprise.
- Regional counterblow actor whose victory helps shift Beidi, Shangjun, and Anding away from Wei Xiao's field.
- Recurring anti-Wei commander who remains visible in later Anding and Wei Chun campaign notices.

## Qualifications

The current page does not reconstruct Feng Yi's full biography or broader founding-war career. Hanji 701 supplies a court-memory and reward scene, but it does not give the full chronology of Feng Yi's early service or Guanzhong pacification. Hanji 702 is strong evidence for his decision style in one western campaign episode, but the source summary leaves the target city's name unclear and keeps Wang Yuan, Xing Xun, Jia Lan, local Beidi strongmen, and Xiongnu-side forces source-scoped. Hanji 705-1 and Hanji 709 confirm that Feng Yi remains active across the anti-Wei front, but they provide only compact notices rather than detailed campaign narratives.
