# 赵云 / Zhao Yun

## Overview
赵云 / Zhao Yun is a [[LiuBei|刘备]] officer whose current evidence joins family rescue during the [[ChangbanBattle|长坂坡之战]], administration and political restraint in Guiyang, post-conquest remonstrance in Chengdu, and tactical nerve at the Han River.

## Current Profile
Hanji 1094 places Zhao Yun inside the Changban collapse. A report says he has fled north, where defection toward Cao Cao is plausible, but Liu Bei rejects the accusation. Zhao Yun returns after rescuing Liu Bei's family members and carrying [[LiuChan|刘禅]], turning apparent desertion into a loyalty-under-disorder case. Hanji 1109 then gives him an administrative-military role in Liu Bei's post-Red-Cliffs consolidation: the Guiyang appointment helps turn surrender into governed territory while [[ZhugeLiang|诸葛亮]] supervises revenue across the southern commanderies.

Hanji 1147 broadens that profile. It presents Zhao Yun's refusal of [[ZhaoFanLateHan|赵范]]'s marriage proposal as caution toward a coerced surrender; his opposition to distributing Chengdu land and houses as [[PostConquestPropertyRestoration|战后民产归还]] grounded in both civilian welfare and state capacity; and his open-gate maneuver at the Han River as courage based on adversary judgment rather than reckless attack. The resulting profile is loyalty with an object larger than personal attachment: protection of dependants, civilians, governability, and the ruler's longer project.

## Key Characteristics
- Preserves family continuity during battlefield collapse despite an accusation of defection.
- Converts battlefield service into territorial responsibility through the Guiyang appointment.
- Refuses a politically risky kinship tie when the other party's submission remains uncertain.
- Opposes rewarding conquerors with civilian property and links restoration to settlement, support, taxation, and recruitment.
- Uses deception and timing at the Han River, making courage inseparable from judgment of enemy psychology.
- Grounds loyalty in the episode's claimed preference for benevolent rule rather than private attachment alone.

## Qualifications
This page remains source-bounded and does not project Zhao Yun's wider literary reputation backward. Hanji 1094 supplies a rescue sequence but not a complete operational reconstruction; the exact family members rescued, route, dialogue, promotion, and later “seven entries and exits” imagery require broader evidence. Hanji 1147 is a structured summary rather than a transcript and attributes part of its material to the lost *Yun Biezhuan* preserved through Pei Songzhi's annotations. Zhao Yun's early motives, the Chengdu quotation and policy implementation, Zhao Fan's intent and flight, the Han River sequence, Liu Bei's wording, the 229 death date, and the later Shunping posthumous title require direct textual comparison. Zhao Fan's flight can support Zhao Yun's caution without proving that every private motive inferred by the host was correct.

## What Changed
- Added Chengdu remonstrance as evidence that Zhao Yun's loyalty includes civilian welfare and durable governability.
- Added the Zhao Fan marriage refusal as political-risk restraint within the Guiyang appointment.
- Added the Han River maneuver as calculated tactical courage rather than simple boldness.
- Qualified the new material by its structured-summary form and reported *Yun Biezhuan* provenance.
