# Kate Moran

## Overview

Kate Moran is the user-experience researcher interviewed by [[MarketplaceTech]] about consumer AI product fit, agentic convenience and privacy, deployment barriers, and the difference between exposure and voluntary adoption.

## Current Profile

Moran evaluates AI from the user's problem outward. She argues that model capability does not establish product value when people must invent their own use cases, learn specialist terminology, or build software they never wanted to maintain. Her preferred examples package AI around a legible outcome and reduce configuration work.

Her position is not that convenience lacks value. It is that useful agents obtain that convenience by gaining more context and authority, so privacy, security, unintended action, and recovery become part of the product experience. She applies the same behavioral standard to adoption statistics: return by choice is stronger evidence than exposure or mandated activity.

## Key Characteristics

- User-experience researcher focused on how nontechnical consumers encounter AI products.
- Advocate of concrete, need-led product framing over capability-first interfaces.
- Skeptic of predictions that most consumers will replace packaged software by building their own.
- Analyst of the privacy-and-control costs attached to agentic convenience.
- Proponent of voluntary repeated use as a stronger adoption signal than exposure or compliance activity.
- Supporter of grassroots workplace experimentation informed by teams' knowledge of their own work.
