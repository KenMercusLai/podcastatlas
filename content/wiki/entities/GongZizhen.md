# 龚自珍 / Gong Zizhen

## Overview
龚自珍 appears in the wiki as both a later poet in the literary memory of [[JingKe|荆轲]] and a mid-Qing thinker who diagnosed a dynasty that retained the appearance of order while entering decline.

## Current Profile
The two bounded sources show distinct but compatible roles. A Zizhi Tongjian podcast cites 《舟中读陶诗三首》 to place Gong in a reception chain after [[TaoYuanming|陶渊明]], preserving melancholy recognition of Jing Ke and a narrowing youxia world. The [[HuzuoHuyou|忽左忽右]] source places Gong inside the Qing bureaucracy and a New Text learning milieu, where his distinction among flourishing order, decline, and disorder supports reform before breakdown rather than fatalistic expectation of immediate chaos.

## Key Characteristics
- Preserves the literary afterlife of Jing Ke through a later response to Tao Yuanming.
- Is presented as a well-connected official rather than a detached modern intellectual.
- Uses dynastic stages to distinguish hidden decline from visible disorder.
- Treats self-reform as possible before accumulated problems become regime replacement.
- Appears more pessimistic about his period than [[HongLiangji]], without asserting that collapse must occur immediately.

## Evidence
- Literary-memory evidence: [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-115-2-qinwang-danan-busi-zhihou-ganle-jian-ni-buzhidao-de-shi-lidfjb6-nggubw8vpz5fvnfacrqu]] cites Gong's response to Tao Yuanming while reflecting on Jing Ke's durable afterlife.
- Institutional-position evidence: [[501-cong-hongliangji-gongzizhen-dao-weiyuan-qingzhongye-de-ren-ruhe-kandai-ziji-guojia-weilai-lgu0h9kaxmfwzoe1byxeyq7png9o]] presents Gong as a central-government official with elite scholarly family connections.
- Cycle-diagnosis evidence: [[501-cong-hongliangji-gongzizhen-dao-weiyuan-qingzhongye-de-ren-ruhe-kandai-ziji-guojia-weilai-lgu0h9kaxmfwzoe1byxeyq7png9o]] attributes to Gong a declining-world analysis distinct from the progressive Gongyang “three ages” and connects it to reform while time remains.

## Qualifications
The literary source uses Gong briefly and does not interpret his wider political thought. The Qing source reports Sun Ming's oral interpretation of Gong's categories; exact wording, intellectual genealogy, and historical context should be checked against Gong's texts and the book.

## What Changed
- Expanded Gong from a literary-reception figure into a mid-Qing official and cycle-diagnosis thinker.
- Added his reform-before-collapse position while preserving the earlier Jing Ke poetry branch.
