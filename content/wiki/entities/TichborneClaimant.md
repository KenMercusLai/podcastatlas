# Tichborne Claimant

## Overview

The Tichborne Claimant was the Victorian identity and inheritance case in which [[ArthurOrton]] claimed to be the missing aristocrat Roger Tichborne and generated years of litigation, publicity, funding, and political agitation.

## Current Profile

[[400-victorian-britains-maddest-mystery-glt4466006716]] presents the case as more than a weak imposture. Roger's mother accepted Orton despite major discrepancies; [[AndrewBogle]] supported him; and working-class contributors turned the lawsuit into a cause against elite institutions. Civil failure and criminal imprisonment established the legal outcome, but [[EdwardKeneally]]'s campaign preserved the claimant as a populist symbol.

## Key Characteristics

- Began with Roger Tichborne's disappearance and presumed death after a shipwreck.
- Joined inheritance to disputed memory, bodily identification, and maternal recognition.
- Depended on Bogle's support despite numerous contradictions in Orton's performance.
- Attracted subscriptions, courtroom audiences, print coverage, rallies, and merchandise.
- Ended legally in conviction while remaining culturally ambiguous to supporters.

## Evidence

- Identity failure: [[400-victorian-britains-maddest-mystery-glt4466006716]] contrasts Roger's education, French, appearance, and identifying marks with Orton.
- Witness and empire: [[400-victorian-britains-maddest-mystery-glt4466006716]] follows Bogle from Jamaican enslavement to Australia and the trials.
- Popular afterlife: [[400-victorian-britains-maddest-mystery-glt4466006716]] describes subscriptions, entertainment, organizing, merchandise, and burial.
