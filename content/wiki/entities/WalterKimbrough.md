# Walter Kimbrough

## Overview

Walter Kimbrough is presented in [[project-dillard-project-dillard|Project Dillard]] as the president of [[DillardUniversity]] and a critic of college rankings that reward wealth, selectivity, and student advantage over access and development.

## Current Profile

Kimbrough calls the ranking system a "perpetuation of privilege" and explains how conditions outside the classroom affect completion. His example of a student without secure housing during holiday closures connects an aggregate graduation rate to family income, employment, illness, and housing stability.

His institutional priorities diverge from the incentives in the episode's thought experiment. Given major new funding, he would expand Dillard and educate more students; the ranking model instead rewards admitting fewer people and recruiting students already positioned to graduate and donate. He treats a higher rank purchased by changing the student body as loss of institutional purpose rather than improvement.

The episode also portrays Kimbrough as accessible to students and willing to challenge the ranking publisher directly about why admitting poor, Black, part-time, and non-traditional students should lower an institution's standing.

## Key Characteristics

- Defends access-oriented higher education against prestige incentives.
- Interprets completion metrics in light of students' material circumstances.
- Prioritizes expanding educational opportunity over increasing selectivity.
- Uses Dillard's Black-physics outcomes to argue for developmental measures of quality.
- Treats institutional mission as a boundary on ranking optimization.
