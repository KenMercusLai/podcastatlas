# Andrew Bogle

## Overview

Andrew Bogle was a formerly enslaved Jamaican man, Tichborne servant, Australian migrant, and central witness who supported [[ArthurOrton]]'s claim to be Roger Tichborne.

## Current Profile

[[400-victorian-britains-maddest-mystery-glt4466006716]] makes Bogle the main link between Jamaican slavery, aristocratic household service, imperial migration, Australia, and the Victorian courtroom. [[ZadieSmith]] finds his recognition implausible but leaves motive unresolved: belief, strategy, recompense, or some mixture. Trial transcripts, as described, present him as composed, witty, candid, and widely respected.

## Key Characteristics

- Was born into slavery on a Jamaican plantation according to archives discussed by Smith.
- Served the Tichborne family, married in England, and moved to Australia.
- Recognized or professed to recognize Orton as Roger Tichborne.
- Lost an annual family payment after supporting the claim.
- Became a dignified but morally ambiguous witness.

## Evidence

- Life trajectory: [[400-victorian-britains-maddest-mystery-glt4466006716]] traces Bogle through plantation records, service, England, Australia, and London.
- Witness role: [[400-victorian-britains-maddest-mystery-glt4466006716]] treats his support as decisive and personally costly.
- Courtroom profile: [[400-victorian-britains-maddest-mystery-glt4466006716]] describes calm, wit, candor, and dignity.

## Qualifications

Bogle's inner beliefs cannot be recovered from the episode. Smith's view is informed by archives and fiction-making but remains an interpretation; admiration around the trial does not resolve sincerity or strategy.
