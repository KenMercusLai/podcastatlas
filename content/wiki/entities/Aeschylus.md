# Aeschylus

## Overview

Aeschylus was an Athenian tragedian and Persian Wars veteran whose [[TheOresteia|Oresteia]] turns the cursed house of [[Agamemnon]] into a public argument about revenge, judgment, and civic order.

## Current Profile

In the episode, Aeschylus adapts inherited Homeric and mythic material rather than simply repeating it. *Agamemnon* gives [[Clytemnestra]] commanding dramatic force; the later plays follow Orestes from commanded matricide and pursuit by the Furies to trial before a divided jury, with Athena's vote producing acquittal.

This resolution is read politically as well as religiously: Athens presents its court and civic institutions as capable of absorbing a blood feud that kinship retaliation cannot end.

## Key Characteristics

- He reworks older epic and mythic material for the Athenian tragic stage.
- His Oresteia links household murder to a crisis of justice and political order.
- He gives Clytemnestra unusual dramatic authority and moral intelligibility.
- He uses divine and civic institutions together rather than depicting a purely secular legal transition.

## Evidence

- Dramatic adaptation: [[458-helen-of-troy-a-family-of-blood-part-2-glt7255356131]] identifies the 458 BCE trilogy as an Aeschylean reuse of the wider Homeric feast.
- Civic adjudication: [[458-helen-of-troy-a-family-of-blood-part-2-glt7255356131]] emphasizes Athena, the split jury, and the courtroom ending of Orestes' pursuit.
- Female power: [[458-helen-of-troy-a-family-of-blood-part-2-glt7255356131]] describes Clytemnestra as the trilogy's most powerful figure.

## Qualifications

- The episode offers an interpretive summary, not a complete account of Aeschylus' works or the Oresteia's textual and performance history.
- The trial does not simply erase violence or divine authority; it transforms and contains them through a mythic civic settlement.
- Claims about democratic propaganda and Athenian gender anxiety are interpretations of the drama's political work.

## What Changed

- Established Aeschylus as the key adapter connecting heroic family violence to Athenian civic judgment.
