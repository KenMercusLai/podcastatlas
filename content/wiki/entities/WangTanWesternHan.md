## Overview

## Current Profile

Wang Tan's current profile is mostly structural. Hanji 523 places him at the edge of supreme Wang-family succession: Chengdi wants him to replace the dying Wang Feng, but Wang Feng refuses, grouping Wang Tan and other close relatives as too extravagant and beyond proper ministerial restraint. The episode also implies a personal layer, since Wang Tan and others had not treated Wang Feng with the deference that [[WangYinWesternHan|王音]] showed.

Hanji 533 shows Wang Tan from the other side of that missed choice. By 17 BCE he is dead, and Chengdi regrets not having used him as a stronger counterweight. His absence then helps explain why Chengdi turns to Wang Shang. The source compares Wang Tan and Wang Shang as figures who can become less careful after receiving favor, sharpening the contrast with Wang Yin's caution.

## Key Characteristics

- Ping'a Hou and Wang-family figure in Chengdi-era court politics.
- Proposed by Chengdi as a possible Wang Feng successor in Hanji 523.
- Rejected by Wang Feng as extravagant and unsuitable to command people.
- Dead by Hanji 533's opening moment in 17 BCE.
- Remembered by Chengdi as a missed counterweight option.
- Functions as a structural absence that leads to Wang Shang's renewed empowerment.

## Qualifications

This page is source-scoped to Hanji 533 and Hanji 523 and does not reconstruct Wang Tan's full family line, office history, earlier actions, or exact relationship to other Wang-family figures. The episode uses him mainly as a rejected or absent balancing instrument in Chengdi's handling of Wang-family power.
