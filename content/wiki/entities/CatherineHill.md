# Catherine Hill

## Overview

Catherine Hill is the economist and Vassar president presented in [[food-fight-food-fight|Food Fight]] as redirecting institutional resources toward socioeconomic and racial diversity.

## Current Profile

Hill's earlier work at Williams College reportedly showed that families in the bottom 40 percent of the income distribution faced a very high relative college cost. At [[VassarCollege]], she responded by increasing financial aid and Pell Grant enrollment without treating lower-income students as academically weaker.

Her case also exposes the operational cost of that commitment. More aid means less collected tuition, while the college still needs affluent students who may expect private rooms, bathrooms, improved housing, and better food. Hill therefore embodies a values-driven strategy constrained by [[FullPayStudentCrossSubsidy]] and [[CollegeAccessAmenityCompetition]].

## Key Characteristics

- Economist focused on the affordability of elite higher education.
- Vassar president associated with increased socioeconomic and racial diversity.
- Argues that colleges can admit more talented low-income students without reducing academic standards.
- Treats dining, housing, and facility upgrades as real opportunity costs of aid expansion.
- Defends education across backgrounds as part of academic quality.

## Evidence

### Affordability diagnosis

- [[food-fight-food-fight]] attributes to Hill the finding that bottom-40-percent families could be asked to spend about half their pretax income to attend Williams.

### Institutional action

- [[food-fight-food-fight]] associates her Vassar presidency with an aid-budget increase from about $25 million to roughly $60 million and a Pell share near 23 percent.

### Competitive constraint

- [[food-fight-food-fight]] records her view that rising inequality brings affluent applicants with higher amenity expectations into a market Vassar cannot ignore.

## Qualifications

This profile is drawn from one episode summary, not a complete biography, policy record, or independent evaluation. The reported figures, causal attribution, applicant expectations, and institutional alternatives remain source-scoped, and the episode gives more attention to the moral force of Hill's strategy than to all possible financial counterfactuals.

## What Changed

- Established Hill as the decision-maker connecting affordability evidence to institutional aid expansion.
- Added the tension between an access mission and dependence on amenity-sensitive full-paying students.
