# Battle of Gaugamela

## Overview

The Battle of Gaugamela in 331 BC was the decisive victory through which [[AlexanderTheGreat|Alexander the Great]] broke [[DariusIII|Darius III]]'s main army and opened the Achaemenid imperial capitals to Macedonian conquest.

## Current Profile

[[117-alexander-the-great-part-2-glt9707946822]] reconstructs Gaugamela as a Persian attempt to use prepared open ground, cavalry, chariots, elephants, and superior numbers against Alexander's smaller force. It rejects the Greek tradition's million-man figure and offers a rough order of magnitude of about 100,000 Persians against roughly half as many Macedonians, while keeping the figures uncertain.

The tactical hinge is a gap in the Persian line. Alexander's cavalry drives through it, Darius and the Persian force flee, and the road opens to Babylon, Susa, and Persepolis. The battle is decisive without by itself completing the conquest: Darius survives, eastern resistance continues, and Alexander must decide whether victory means settlement or further pursuit.

## Key Characteristics

- Decisive field battle between Alexander and Darius in 331 BC.
- Persian plan built around open ground, cavalry, chariots, elephants, and numerical depth.
- Macedonian victory attributed to maneuver and exploitation of a gap in the Persian line.
- Opens the imperial heartland without immediately ending political resistance.
- Survives through later narrative sources whose numbers and dramatic anecdotes require caution.

## Evidence

- Force design: [[117-alexander-the-great-part-2-glt9707946822]] describes Darius's selected battlefield and diverse imperial army.
- Tactical result: [[117-alexander-the-great-part-2-glt9707946822]] identifies Alexander's cavalry attack through a line gap as the battle's decisive movement.
- Strategic consequence: [[117-alexander-the-great-part-2-glt9707946822]] follows victory with the capture of Babylon, Susa, and Persepolis.

## Qualifications

The episode's suggested troop totals are estimates offered against implausible ancient numbers, not verified counts. The oversleeping story, exact deployment, timing, gap formation, casualty scale, and reasons for Persian flight remain filtered through later accounts and a compressed podcast reconstruction.

## What Changed

- Established Gaugamela as the battlefield hinge between defeating a Persian army and attempting to inherit its empire.

## Relationships
