# Camille Paglia / 卡米尔·帕利亚

## Overview
Camille Paglia / 卡米尔·帕利亚 appears in [[367-shehui-gaige-neng-dailai-xingbie-hexie-ma-lmxou4dgsnvkdwplhgubtkmv7pl]] as the author of [[SexualPersonae|《性面具》 / Sexual Personae]] and a critic of explanations that derive gender conflict wholly from social inequality or patriarchal construction.

## Current Profile
The current source presents Paglia as an internal dissenter from mainstream feminist theory rather than as an opponent of equal rights. Her account treats nature as blind and non-moral, civilization as an artificial order that both protects and oppresses, sexuality as a recurring encounter with bodily forces, and maternity as a form of power that exceeds political status. The episode uses these propositions to ask why desire, dependence, aggression, and control might persist after legal reform.

## Key Characteristics
- Separates support for equal rights from a total social-construction theory of gender conflict.
- Treats nature as powerful, indifferent, and resistant to moral or political wishes.
- Interprets civilization as both constraint and protection against violence, desire, reproduction, and mortality.
- Reads patriarchal control partly through male dependence on and anxiety about maternal reproductive power.
- Describes maternity through simultaneous agency, passivity, creation, danger, and loss of control.
- Uses symbolic and psychoanalytic interpretation more than empirical social-science demonstration in the current source.

## Evidence
- Construction boundary - [[367-shehui-gaige-neng-dailai-xingbie-hexie-ma-lmxou4dgsnvkdwplhgubtkmv7pl]] presents Paglia as challenging the sufficiency, not the existence, of social and institutional explanations.
- Nature and order - [[367-shehui-gaige-neng-dailai-xingbie-hexie-ma-lmxou4dgsnvkdwplhgubtkmv7pl]] attributes to her a view of society as an artificial defense against non-moral natural forces.
- Sex and maternity - [[367-shehui-gaige-neng-dailai-xingbie-hexie-ma-lmxou4dgsnvkdwplhgubtkmv7pl]] uses her account of desire, reproduction, maternal dependence, and bodily risk to explain recurring psychological tension.
- Feminist inheritance - [[367-shehui-gaige-neng-dailai-xingbie-hexie-ma-lmxou4dgsnvkdwplhgubtkmv7pl]] presents her claim that modern feminism may inherit a masculine ideal of freedom as independence from body and nature.

## Qualifications
This profile is based on one podcast's interpretation of one major book, not a comprehensive reading of Paglia's publications or reception. The episode openly notes that its account is psychoanalytic, symbolic, binary in its treatment of sex, and thin on empirical validation. Claims about men, women, maternity, and civilization therefore remain attributed rather than generalized.
