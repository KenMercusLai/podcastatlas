# David Galenson

## Overview

David Galenson is the economist whose distinction between conceptual and experimental innovators supplies the episode's organizing framework for different paths to creative achievement.

## Current Profile

As presented by [[MalcolmGladwell]], Galenson contrasts creators who begin with a clear, articulable conception and execute it rapidly with creators who find their aims through trial, revision, and dissatisfaction. Picasso and Cézanne serve as visual-art examples before the episode applies the distinction to [[ElvisCostello]], [[LeonardCohen]], and [[HallelujahSong|“Hallelujah.”]]

The framework is most useful as a process heuristic. The episode does not establish that every artist belongs permanently to one group, that early and late achievement always map onto these methods, or that experimental persistence necessarily produces important work.

## Key Characteristics

- Distinguishes plan-led execution from discovery through repeated attempts.
- Connects creative method with different career and completion patterns.
- Provides a cross-domain vocabulary used for painting and music.
- Is represented through Gladwell's interpretation rather than a full account of Galenson's scholarship.

The source is a structured summary of a podcast episode, not Galenson's original scholarship or a critical literature review. Definitions, examples, career-age claims, and the extension from visual art to music remain bounded to the episode's presentation.
