# 赤壁之战 / Battle of Red Cliffs

## Overview
赤壁之战 / Battle of Red Cliffs is the late-Han river battle in which the Sun-Liu side defeats [[CaoCao|曹操]]'s southern expedition and forces a retreat from the middle Yangtze.

## Current Profile
The combined source set makes Red Cliffs a multi-causal campaign whose political and diplomatic preparation precedes the fire attack. Hanji 1096 begins with exploratory diplomacy: [[LuSu|鲁肃]] uses mourning as cover to assess divided Jingzhou, test [[LiuBei|刘备]] as a partner, and preserve a fallback if that partner collapses. Hanji 1100 adds [[ZhugeLiang|诸葛亮]]'s direct coalition pitch: resistance is made a ruler-dignity choice, then supported through claims about Liu-side forces, Cao-side fatigue, poor water-war adaptation, weak Jingzhou loyalty, and the potential tripartite payoff. Lu Su later shows Sun Quan that surrender exposes ruler and adviser differently. [[ZhouYuLateHan|周瑜]] then contests Cao Cao's claim to represent lawful Han authority, challenges the threatening force total, and turns western pressure, winter fodder, climate, disease, river-war adaptation, fatigue, and newly absorbed Jingzhou forces into a resistance case. Sun Quan closes debate and assigns Zhou Yu and [[ChengPuLateHan|程普]] an initial field force with promised reinforcement.

Cao Cao enters after successive victories, rejects [[JiaXu|贾诩]]'s consolidation-first advice, and tries to defeat Jiangdong before Sun Quan and [[LiuBei|刘备]] can strengthen their alliance. Yet nominal superiority is weakened by epidemic disease, unfamiliar climate and river terrain, northern soldiers' poor water adaptation, and reliance on newly absorbed Jingzhou naval forces.

Linking ships reduces seasickness and improves movement but creates a shared fire vulnerability. [[HuangGai|黄盖]] recognizes it, uses feigned surrender to bring prepared vessels close, and [[ZhouYuLateHan|周瑜]] follows the resulting fire with direct pressure. Cao Cao retreats through [[HuarongRoadLateHan|华容道]], destroys remaining ships and supplies, and establishes a Jiangling-Fancheng defense.

The battle's political meaning remains contested. Cao Cao foregrounds disease, deliberate ship burning, and withdrawal while minimizing Zhou Yu's credit. Meanwhile, victory gives the Sun-Liu alliance a new distribution problem over [[Jingzhou|荆州]], so removal of the common threat exposes divergent territorial interests.

## Key Characteristics
- Campaign whose outcome emerges from command judgment, disease, terrain, force adaptation, deception, fire, and follow-on attack.
- Coalition formation that moves from hedged partner exploration through dignity and capacity argument to a resistance decision built from ruler-risk analysis, legitimacy reframing, force verification, and operational delegation.
- Linked-ship solution that reduces immediate movement problems while concentrating fire risk.
- Major check on Cao Cao's attempt to extend control south of the Yangtze.
- Retreat followed by materiel denial and corridor defense around Jiangling and Fancheng.
- Political meaning contested through rival explanations of disease, fire, agency, and military credit.
- Alliance victory that creates a new dispute over Jingzhou's territory and revenue.

## Qualifications
These are five popular-history episodes, not a complete military history. Exact troop and casualty figures, wind and route detail, private dialogue, the degree of epidemic impact, and the claim that Cao Cao's psychology was decisive all require broader evidence. Hanji 1096's fallback instruction, Hanji 1100's tidy persuasion sequence and psychological effects, and Hanji 1102's force estimates are decision evidence rather than independently verified records. Disease, enemy fire, withdrawal, and self-destruction of abandoned materiel can coexist; later alliance conflict and earlier hedging do not erase real coordination against a common threat.

## What Changed
- Added Zhuge Liang's direct coalition-persuasion stage between exploratory diplomacy and Jiangdong's final commitment.
- Connected dignity rhetoric to the operational claims later expanded by Zhou Yu without treating the two arguments as identical.
- Preserved the distinction between narrated persuasion effects and verified military readiness.

- [[ZhouYuLateHan|周瑜]] - Sun-side commander who accepts the plan and follows the fire with offensive pressure.
- [[CaoCao|曹操]] - defeated northern commander whose force, readiness, retreat, and later narrative are central to the campaign.
- [[SunQuan|孙权]] - ruler whose coalition wins and who receives Cao Cao's reputational counter-message.
- [[LuSu|鲁肃]] - adviser whose unequal-surrender-risk argument helps make resistance politically intelligible.
- [[ChengPuLateHan|程普]] - commander paired with Zhou Yu in the initial deployment and later Jiangling front.
- [[LiuBei|刘备]] - allied actor whose cooperation and post-battle territorial interests remain distinct from Sun Quan's.
- [[HuarongRoadLateHan|华容道]] - terrain bottleneck in Cao Cao's post-battle retreat.
- [[Jingzhou|荆州]] - territorial prize that turns common victory into a distribution problem.
- [[PostSuccessArrogance|得势后的骄傲]] - interpretive frame for Cao Cao's compression of campaign uncertainty after successive victories.
