# 赤壁之战 / Battle of Red Cliffs

## Overview
赤壁之战 / Battle of Red Cliffs is the late-Han river battle in which the Sun-Liu side defeats [[CaoCao|曹操]]'s southern expedition and forces a retreat from the middle Yangtze.

## Current Profile
The combined source set makes Red Cliffs a multi-causal campaign rather than a fire trick in isolation. Cao Cao enters after successive victories, rejects [[JiaXu|贾诩]]'s consolidation-first advice, and tries to defeat Jiangdong before [[SunQuan|孙权]] and [[LiuBei|刘备]] can strengthen their alliance. Yet nominal superiority is weakened by epidemic disease, unfamiliar climate and river terrain, northern soldiers' poor water adaptation, and reliance on newly absorbed Jingzhou naval forces.

Linking ships reduces seasickness and improves movement but creates a shared fire vulnerability. [[HuangGai|黄盖]] recognizes it, uses feigned surrender to bring prepared vessels close, and [[ZhouYuLateHan|周瑜]] follows the resulting fire with direct pressure. Cao Cao retreats through [[HuarongRoadLateHan|华容道]], destroys remaining ships and supplies, and establishes a Jiangling-Fancheng defense.

The battle's political meaning remains contested. Cao Cao foregrounds disease, deliberate ship burning, and withdrawal while minimizing Zhou Yu's credit. Meanwhile, victory gives the Sun-Liu alliance a new distribution problem over [[Jingzhou|荆州]], so removal of the common threat exposes divergent territorial interests.

## Key Characteristics
- Campaign whose outcome emerges from command judgment, disease, terrain, force adaptation, deception, fire, and follow-on attack.
- Linked-ship solution that reduces immediate movement problems while concentrating fire risk.
- Major check on Cao Cao's attempt to extend control south of the Yangtze.
- Retreat followed by materiel denial and corridor defense around Jiangling and Fancheng.
- Political meaning contested through rival explanations of disease, fire, agency, and military credit.
- Alliance victory that creates a new dispute over Jingzhou's territory and revenue.

## Qualifications
These are two adjacent popular-history episodes, not a complete military history. Exact troop and casualty figures, wind detail, route detail, private dialogue, the degree of epidemic impact, and the claim that Cao Cao's psychology was decisive all require broader evidence. Disease, enemy fire, withdrawal, and self-destruction of abandoned materiel can coexist; the sharper dispute concerns relative causal weight and ownership of victory. The alliance's later conflicts do not erase real coordination against a common threat.

## What Changed
- Added the pre-battle decision, readiness, disease, terrain, and water-adaptation layer.
- Reframed linked ships as a local adaptation that creates a concentrated downstream vulnerability.
- Connected the fire attack to the Huarong retreat and immediate defensive regrouping.
- Strengthened the non-monocausal boundary around pride, disease, and enemy action.

## Relationships
- [[HuangGai|黄盖]] - officer whose vulnerability diagnosis and feigned surrender enable the close-range fire attack.
- [[ZhouYuLateHan|周瑜]] - Sun-side commander who accepts the plan and follows the fire with offensive pressure.
- [[CaoCao|曹操]] - defeated northern commander whose force, readiness, retreat, and later narrative are central to the campaign.
- [[SunQuan|孙权]] - ruler whose coalition wins and who receives Cao Cao's reputational counter-message.
- [[LiuBei|刘备]] - allied actor whose cooperation and post-battle territorial interests remain distinct from Sun Quan's.
- [[HuarongRoadLateHan|华容道]] - terrain bottleneck in Cao Cao's post-battle retreat.
- [[Jingzhou|荆州]] - territorial prize that turns common victory into a distribution problem.
- [[PostSuccessArrogance|得势后的骄傲]] - interpretive frame for Cao Cao's compression of campaign uncertainty after successive victories.
- [[PostDefeatNarrativeCounterattack|败后叙事反击]] - mechanism by which Cao Cao tries to limit the political consequences of defeat.
- [[InterestStructuredAllianceFragility|利益结构式联盟脆弱性]] - relationship pattern activated when allies must divide the postwar prize.
