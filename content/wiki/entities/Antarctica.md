## Overview
Antarctica is the protected continent used by [[the-continent-nobody-owns-everyone-benefits-from-summer-school]] to show that economic value can exist without ordinary private ownership, dense markets, or everyday money use.

## Current Profile
The current wiki profile treats Antarctica as a global commons whose main economic importance comes from remaining largely undeveloped. Tourism and fisheries are visible market activities, but the source's central judgment is that Antarctica's largest value comes through [[AntarcticClimateServices]]: reflective ice, carbon absorption in the [[SouthernOcean]], and stored water that limits sea-level-rise damage.

## Key Characteristics
- Antarctica functions as a scarce global environmental asset rather than a normal privately owned territory.
- Its governance is associated in this source with the [[AntarcticTreaty]], peaceful use, science, trust, collaboration, and sanctions.
- Its market activity is limited but not absent: tourism, fisheries, and science logistics all create recognizable economic behavior.
- Its climate value is presented as much larger than its direct commercial value.
- [[McMurdoStation]] shows that even protected scientific infrastructure creates labor, retail, logistics, and settlement questions.

## Evidence
- Commons and governance profile: [[the-continent-nobody-owns-everyone-benefits-from-summer-school]] describes Antarctica as treaty-protected for peace and science rather than ordinary military or mining use.
- Value stack: [[the-continent-nobody-owns-everyone-benefits-from-summer-school]] compares smaller tourism and fisheries values with a much larger annual estimate once climate services are counted.
- Human settlement layer: [[the-continent-nobody-owns-everyone-benefits-from-summer-school]] uses McMurdo's store, dormitories, waste rules, and worker life to show ordinary economic problems inside an exceptional place.

## Qualifications
The $180 billion annual estimate, the 46-country treaty-signatory statement, and the no-mining/no-military framing are source-scoped to the episode note. This page should not be read as a full legal or climate-science reference for Antarctica.
