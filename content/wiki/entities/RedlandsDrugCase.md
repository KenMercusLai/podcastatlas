# Redlands Drug Case

## Overview

The Redlands Drug Case began with a raid on [[KeithRichards]]'s Sussex country house on 12 February 1967 and became a national conflict over drugs, celebrity, punishment, class, and youth culture. [[MickJagger]], Richards, and Robert Fraser received prison sentences, but appeals and establishment criticism complicated a simple repression narrative.

## Current Profile

The raid followed a *News of the World* tip after the newspaper had falsely identified Jagger as openly taking drugs. Police found amphetamines in Jagger's jacket and multiple drugs on Fraser; Richards was charged with permitting cannabis use. Stories about Marianne Faithfull's fur rug widened the gap between evidence and public imagination.

Jagger received three months, Fraser six, and Richards twelve. *The Times* argued that prejudice against Jagger's image should not determine punishment. Richards's conviction was overturned; Jagger's remained but imprisonment was removed; Fraser served four months. Surveyed youth support for punishment prevents a clean generational binary.

## Key Characteristics

- A tabloid tip joined press rivalry to police enforcement.
- The country-house setting converted drugs into symbolic class transgression.
- Sentences exceeded the apparent scale of Jagger's and Richards's conduct.
- Establishment institutions both punished and defended the defendants.
- Fraser bore the harshest lasting consequence.
- Public opinion divided within generations as well as between them.

## Qualifications

The episode rejects a coordinated Wilson-government plot but infers retaliatory tabloid motives and symbolic judicial hostility. Those motives, legal reasoning, survey interpretation, and private details remain source-scoped. Fraser's heroin possession materially distinguished his case.
