# Lord Alfred Douglas / 波西

## Overview

Lord Alfred “Bosie” Douglas was [[OscarWilde|Oscar Wilde]]'s lover, the addressee of [[DeProfundis]], and a central figure in the family conflict, litigation, prison writing, reunion, and ideological reversals surrounding Wilde's downfall.

## Current Profile

Douglas first enters the wiki through a literary discussion that frames him through money, illness, dependence, love, and the disastrous legal conflict with his father. The trial episode broadens that narrow role. He visited Wilde before the criminal trial but left for France, later described Wilde's destruction as the work of a public determined to crush him, and defended same-sex love against legal interference where there was no harm, coercion, or abuse of minors.

His profile is defined by contradiction rather than one settled moral role. He was central to Wilde's attachment and downfall, then reunited with him after prison despite Wilde's claims of reform; that reunion helped cost Wilde financial support from his wife. The episode also emphasizes Douglas's later Catholic turn, repudiation of homosexuality, antisemitism, and imprisonment for libelling Winston Churchill.

## Key Characteristics

- Wilde's lover and the addressee of his prison letter.
- Participant in the family conflict that drove Wilde's failed libel action and legal exposure.
- Absent from England during the criminal trial after visiting Wilde in custody.
- Defender, at one stage, of consensual same-sex love against legal interference.
- Post-release partner whose reunion with Wilde carried material family consequences.
- Later reactionary whose Catholicism, anti-homosexual position, antisemitism, and libel conviction sharply reversed his earlier defense.

## Qualifications

The sources develop Douglas primarily through his relationship with Wilde rather than as a complete independent biography. Responsibility for Wilde's decisions, the precise dynamics of their relationship, private motives, the wording and context of letters, and the causes of Douglas's later ideological reversal remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Extended Douglas from lover-and-letter addressee into the trial, exile, reunion, and later-life sequence.
- Added the contradiction between his defense of same-sex love and his later anti-homosexual politics.
- Added the material consequence of the post-prison reunion for Wilde's family support.
