# Lord Alfred Douglas / 波西

## Overview

Lord Alfred “Bosie” Douglas was [[OscarWilde|Oscar Wilde]]'s lover, the addressee of [[DeProfundis]], and a central figure in the socially unequal relationships, family conflict, litigation, prison writing, reunion, and ideological reversals surrounding Wilde's downfall.

## Current Profile

Douglas first enters the wiki through a literary discussion that frames him through money, illness, dependence, love, and the disastrous legal conflict with his father. Part 1 dates the affair from 1892, presents Douglas as more experienced in same-sex relationships, and says he drew Wilde further into a London world of rent boys and socially subordinate young men. It also makes him a driver of confrontation: he dismissed his father's threats and encouraged Wilde's libel action alongside [[RobbieRoss|Robbie Ross]]. These influences do not remove Wilde's agency.

Part 2 follows Douglas into absence, defense, reunion, and reversal. He visited Wilde before the criminal trial but left for France, later described Wilde's destruction as the work of a public determined to crush him, and defended same-sex love against legal interference where there was no harm, coercion, or abuse of minors.

His profile is defined by contradiction rather than one settled moral role. He was central to Wilde's attachment and downfall, then reunited with him after prison despite Wilde's claims of reform; that reunion helped cost Wilde financial support from his wife. The episode also emphasizes Douglas's later Catholic turn, repudiation of homosexuality, antisemitism, and imprisonment for libelling Winston Churchill.

## Key Characteristics

- Wilde's lover and the addressee of his prison letter.
- More experienced partner who is said to have introduced Wilde to a riskier and more class-stratified sexual world.
- Participant in the family conflict that drove Wilde's failed libel action and legal exposure.
- Absent from England during the criminal trial after visiting Wilde in custody.
- Defender, at one stage, of consensual same-sex love against legal interference.
- Post-release partner whose reunion with Wilde carried material family consequences.

## Qualifications

The sources develop Douglas primarily through his relationship with Wilde rather than as a complete independent biography. Responsibility for Wilde's decisions, the precise dynamics and chronology of their relationship, claims about younger men, private motives, the wording and context of letters, and the causes of Douglas's later ideological reversal remain source-scoped.
