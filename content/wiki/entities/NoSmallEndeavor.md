# No Small Endeavor

## Overview

No Small Endeavor is [[LeeCCamp]]'s interview program about moral life, meaning, faith, and human formation. The bounded source presents it through a conversation with [[MalcolmGladwell]].

## Current Profile

The program's method in this episode is biographical and philosophical. It begins with formative family stories and childhood freedom, then uses them to examine curiosity, explanation, identity, higher education, storytelling, happiness, failure, envy, and committed effort.

Rather than requiring agreement on one doctrine of the good life, the conversation lets concrete stories test abstract positions. The Nashville Good Samaritan encounter becomes a case about moral action, therapy becomes a case about costly failure and retrospective education, and celebrity becomes a case about comparing whole lives rather than isolated advantages.

## Key Characteristics

- Uses long-form conversation to connect biography with ethical and philosophical questions.
- Treats curiosity as an interviewing practice and a moral stance toward another person.
- Moves between theology, practical judgment, storytelling craft, education, and vocation.
- Relies on concrete life episodes to test abstractions about happiness and responsibility.

## Evidence

- Biographical method: [[malcolm-on-no-small-endeavor-malcolm-on-no-small-endeavor]] moves from Gladwell's parents and childhood into his professional habits.

- Good-life scope: [[malcolm-on-no-small-endeavor-malcolm-on-no-small-endeavor]] joins educated desire, discrete happiness, failure, whole-life acceptance, and meaningful commitment.
