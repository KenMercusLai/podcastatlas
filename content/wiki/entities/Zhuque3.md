# Zhuque-3 / 朱雀三号

[[zhongting-zhuque-sanhao-fuzongshi-huojian-huishou-zhiyou-cheng-yu-bai-meiyou-zhongjiantai-s9e41-d9e11e3b-7c7e-4beb-b19f-6f90fff38714]] adds the vehicle-design and first-flight failure details behind the later Yao-2 milestone. [[DongKai|董凯]] describes Zhuque-3 as a liquid-oxygen/methane, stainless-steel reusable rocket whose complete configuration is about 76 meters long, with nine first-stage engines and one second-stage engine.

The replay source says the 2025 first-flight vehicle was about 10 meters shorter than the complete version and used lower-thrust 12A engines rather than the planned 12B engines. It reached orbit, but recovery failed near the landing point when terminal landing ignition did not establish thrust across the planned five-engine sequence. The episode therefore adds [[RocketRecoveryBinaryOutcome]]: orbit insertion can pass while recovery remains a failure.

The same source links Zhuque-3 to [[ReuseFirstRocketDesign]] and [[RecoveryRangePayloadTradeoff]]. Dong says the first flight used a conservative roughly 390 km recovery range for safety, while a fuller-performance reusable mission would put the recovery field around 550-600 km away and trade less propellant reserve for more payload.

[[lanjian-hangtian-wancheng-zhongguo-shouci-ludi-huojian-huishou-yushu-keji-shizhi-chaoguo-3000-yi-1007302506]] updates the Zhuque-3 branch from anticipated recovery capability to a reported land-recovery success. The episode says Zhuque-3 Yao-2 launched on August 19, 2026 and its first stage landed in [[Gansu]], making it China's first successful land recovery of a carrier rocket in the source's account.

Zhuque-3 is the [[LandSpace]] reusable-rocket project used as a comparison case in [[ruhe-douzhu-yike-huojian-s10e21-b66fdf0f-d428-4f0c-8412-b0c7581132d0]]. The episode says an earlier Zhuque-3 attempt performed well through much of flight and guidance, but final landing ignition became unstable and recovery failed.

The source frames Zhuque-3 as part of China's commercial reusable-rocket track. [[JerryTaikongSeng]] argues that failures can still produce valuable real-flight data, and the episode treats a future Zhuque-3 success as a possible confidence signal for commercial space if it combines orbital delivery with successful recovery.
