## Overview

少帝刘弘 / Liu Hong is the young ruler displaced and killed during the anti-Lü settlement that installs [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]]. This page distinguishes him from [[LiuHongQiKingWesternHan|齐王刘弘]], the later son of Emperor Wu.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-207-liubang-xiaoyizi-lvxu-bei-gunsha-1-lsrs42rb8-qref1eevqdnhe3mufv|Hanji 207 part 1]] presents Liu Hong as an incumbent isolated by [[ChenPing|陈平]], [[ZhouBo|周勃]], and the victorious anti-Lü ministers. They deny that he and the other Hui-era princes are biological sons of [[LiuYing|汉惠帝刘盈]], treat their continued existence as a future retaliation risk, and decide to remove the line before selecting Liu Heng. The source reports the claim but does not independently establish the princes' parentage or culpability.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-207-liubang-xiaoyizi-lvxu-bei-gunsha-2-luij-woqemjhmopkgrgnl9bclfwd|part 2]] supplies the physical transfer. [[LiuXingjuWesternHan|刘兴居]] and [[XiahouYing|夏侯婴]] enter the palace, tell Liu Hong that he is not of Liu blood, dismiss his guards, and move him to a government residence. Remaining palace guards still initially identify the incumbent inside as emperor and block Liu Heng's entry until Zhou Bo intervenes. The episode then says Liu Heng orders Liu Hong and three other young princes killed after enthronement, joining palace clearance to the broader [[SuccessionLegitimacyPurge|继位合法性清洗]].

## Key Characteristics

- Young incumbent whose court position collapses after the anti-Lü coalition controls the capital.
- Target of a disputed parentage claim used to deny succession legitimacy.
- Ruler physically removed from the palace before Liu Heng enters it.
- Figure whose remaining guards briefly preserve the practical force of incumbent recognition.
- Victim of the post-victory elimination of rival Hui-era prince lines.

## Evidence

Isolation and legitimacy denial:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-207-liubang-xiaoyizi-lvxu-bei-gunsha-1-lsrs42rb8-qref1eevqdnhe3mufv|Hanji 207 part 1]] says the winning ministers isolate Liu Hong, deny the Hui-era princes' biological legitimacy, and decide to kill them before installing a replacement.

Palace removal and residual recognition:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-207-liubang-xiaoyizi-lvxu-bei-gunsha-2-luij-woqemjhmopkgrgnl9bclfwd|Hanji 207 part 2]] says Liu Xingju and Xiahou Ying clear Liu Hong from the palace, while armed guards initially continue to defend the incumbent title against Liu Heng's entry.

Death after accession:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-207-liubang-xiaoyizi-lvxu-bei-gunsha-2-luij-woqemjhmopkgrgnl9bclfwd|Hanji 207 part 2]] attributes to Liu Heng an order killing Liu Hong and three other young princes after the new ruler takes power.

## Qualifications

Both sources are structured popular-history summaries. They do not independently prove Liu Hong's parentage, personal knowledge, legal status, responsibility for Lü-family government, precise age, deposition procedure, confinement, or death order. The ministers' security interest may explain their actions without validating the biological claim or the killings.

## Relationships

- [[LiuYing|汉惠帝刘盈]] - claimed father whose biological relationship to Liu Hong is denied by the victorious ministers.
- [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]] - replacement ruler whose entry follows Liu Hong's removal and whose first orders reportedly include his death.
- [[ChenPing|陈平]] and [[ZhouBo|周勃]] - accession-makers who help deny the incumbent line and install Liu Heng.
- [[LiuXingjuWesternHan|刘兴居]] and [[XiahouYing|夏侯婴]] - actors who physically remove him from the palace.
- [[SuccessionLegitimacyPurge|继位合法性清洗]] - mechanism that converts disputed legitimacy into elimination of the incumbent and related princes.
- [[LiuHongQiKingWesternHan|齐王刘弘]] - later namesake from whom this page is explicitly disambiguated.
