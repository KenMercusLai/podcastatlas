## Overview

傅士仁 / Fu Shiren is a rear commander in the current 219 CE evidence whose accumulated grievance and fear of punishment are turned into surrender through [[YuFan|虞翻]]'s letter, after which [[LyuMeng|吕蒙]] uses his presence to increase pressure on [[MiFangLateHan|糜芳]] at [[JianglingLateHan|江陵]].

## Current Profile

The bounded source pairs Fu Shiren with Mi Fang behind [[GuanYu|关羽]]'s [[FanchengBattle219LateHan|Fancheng]] front. Both are said to have suffered Guan Yu's contempt, both fall short of provisioning demands after the capture of Yu Jin's large force, and both face a threatened reckoning when Guan Yu returns.

Lyu Meng learns of the rupture and has Yu Fan send Fu Shiren a letter analyzing his position. Fu Shiren surrenders first and is then brought before Jiangling, where his visible defection helps make Mi Fang's continued resistance look less viable. His role is therefore both individual surrender and an instrument in a second surrender.

## Key Characteristics

- Rear commander exposed by the demands of Guan Yu's northern campaign.
- Reportedly carries accumulated grievance from repeated contempt.
- Faces immediate fear after a threatened punishment for inadequate supply.
- Surrenders after written pressure from Yu Fan.
- Becomes visible leverage in Lyu Meng's approach to Jiangling and Mi Fang.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to one structured popular-history summary. Fu Shiren's exact office and location, the text and delivery of Yu Fan's letter, provisioning responsibility, prior relationship with Guan Yu, surrender conditions, and degree of coercion require comparison with underlying chronicles. The source's moral focus on disrespect should not obscure supply strain, isolation, surprise, or survival incentives.
