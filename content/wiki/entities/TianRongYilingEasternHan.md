## Overview

Tian Rong / 田戎 is an Eastern Han-era regional actor around Yiling and Liqiu whose current wiki role now begins with an independent Yiling armed field before becoming a failed-surrender case. He is distinct from [[TianRong|田荣]], the Chu-Han Qi figure. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-674-ruoguo-wuwaijiao-gengshi-huangdi-liuxuan-you-duo-biequ-lu59astedsljoefnl6yr9d4ilirh|Hanji 674]] shows him taking Yiling, calling himself Saodi Dajiangjun, and raiding commanderies with tens of thousands. In [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-690-jiaoyou-bushen-you-duo-kepa-kankan-tianrong-de-jingli-lpbms8pebzdo2ldiwho1cba81njk|Hanji 690]], Tian Rong moves toward submission after [[QinFengEasternHan|秦丰]]'s defeat, but [[XinChenEasternHan|辛臣]]'s theft and defection make the surrender channel look compromised. Tian Rong then lets divination override his prior judgment, rebels with Qin Feng, loses to [[CenPengEasternHan|岑彭]], and retreats to Yiling.

## Current Profile

Hanji 674 supplies the earlier regional-power layer. Tian Rong is a Runan man who takes Yiling, calls himself Saodi Dajiangjun, and raids nearby commanderies with tens of thousands. This makes the later Hanji 690 surrender problem an episode in an already-armed regional field, not the start of his profile.

Hanji 690 presents Tian Rong as neither simply defiant nor already committed to rebellion. His first calculation is pragmatic: Qin Feng's strength has failed under Han pressure, so submission to [[LiuXiu|光武帝刘秀]]'s side looks safer than continued isolation. Xin Chen initially tries to slow that decision by mapping other surviving powers such as [[PengChongEasternHan|彭宠]], [[ZhangBuEasternHan|张步]], [[DongXianEasternHan|董宪]], and [[GongsunShuEasternHan|公孙述]], but Tian Rong still chooses to move.

The failure comes from trust collapse. Tian Rong leaves Xin Chen to guard Yiling while he moves toward Liqiu, but Xin Chen steals hidden valuables, defects to Cen Peng, and sends a letter urging immediate submission. Tian Rong reads that letter as possible betrayal rather than reassurance. The source's key judgment is that the surrender option did not disappear militarily first; it became psychologically and politically contaminated.

Tian Rong then turns to burned-shell divination, receives an ominous crack, and reverses course. The episode uses this as a decision-failure case: under uncertainty, he trades a concrete assessment of Qin Feng's defeat for a sign-reading procedure, joins Qin Feng, and is promptly defeated by Cen Peng.

## Key Characteristics

- Eastern Han Yiling/Liqiu regional actor whose profile begins with a self-titled armed field, distinct from the Chu-Han Qi figure [[TianRong|田荣]].
- Initially pragmatic surrender candidate after Qin Feng's defeat.
- Leader whose decision environment is disrupted by an insider's theft, defection, and suspicious letter.
- Actor whose final choice is redirected by divination after ordinary trust has collapsed.
- Rebel ally of Qin Feng who is defeated by Cen Peng and driven back to Yiling.

## Qualifications

This is a two-source bounded profile. Hanji 674 supplies Tian Rong's regional rise but keeps exact local geography, force composition, title mechanics, and full military strength source-scoped. Hanji 690 supplies the surrender failure and defeat layer but does not reconstruct the full period between the Yiling rise and Qin Feng's defeat. The page treats Xin Chen's action as a trigger for trust collapse, not as proof that Tian Rong's later defeat had only one cause.

## What Changed
