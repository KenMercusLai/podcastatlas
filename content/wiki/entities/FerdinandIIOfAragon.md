# Ferdinand II of Aragon

## Overview

Ferdinand II of Aragon was a Spanish monarch who, with [[IsabellaIOfCastile]], completed the conquest of Granada, sponsored [[ChristopherColumbus]], defended Spanish Atlantic claims, authorized settlement, and intervened as the Caribbean project became violent and administratively unstable.

## Current Profile

[[306-columbus-the-adventure-begins-part-1-glt6089901915]] places Ferdinand in the pre-voyage decision field: Granada's fall intensified crusading confidence but left financial needs, while Portuguese competition and his official [[LuisDeSantangel|Luis de Santángel]] helped recast Columbus's rejected geography as an affordable strategic wager.

In [[308-columbus-death-in-the-caribbean-part-3-glt3250512968]], Ferdinand's role joins geopolitical urgency to royal oversight. Anxiety that Portugal might exploit knowledge of the westward voyage helps explain the speed of papal diplomacy and the enlarged second expedition. Columbus remains useful as a navigator and claimant, but his overseas monopoly and autonomous authority become less acceptable as reports of disorder arrive.

The monarchy's response—opening private voyages, sending Juan Aguado to investigate, and continuing to stress conversion—marks a transition from dependence on Columbus toward wider imperial management.

## Key Characteristics

- Co-sponsor of Columbus and the second-voyage settlement project.
- Co-grantor of the 1492 hereditary titles and profit share Columbus demanded.
- Monarch operating within intense Atlantic rivalry with Portugal.
- Beneficiary of papally backed Spanish territorial claims.
- Overseer wary of Columbus creating an uncontrolled overseas domain.
- Participant in investigation and diversification of Atlantic enterprise after colonial failure.

## Evidence

- Sponsorship threshold: [[306-columbus-the-adventure-begins-part-1-glt6089901915]] links Ferdinand's court, Granada, Santángel, Portuguese competition, and the April 1492 terms.
- Diplomatic urgency: [[308-columbus-death-in-the-caribbean-part-3-glt3250512968]] links Portuguese rivalry to papal backing and rapid preparation of a larger expedition.
- Settlement sponsorship: [[308-columbus-death-in-the-caribbean-part-3-glt3250512968]] connects Ferdinand and Isabella to confirmed titles, colonists, conversion, gold seeking, and mining plans.
- Imperial intervention: [[308-columbus-death-in-the-caribbean-part-3-glt3250512968]] describes the breaking of Columbus's monopoly and Juan Aguado's investigative mission.

## Qualifications

This source gives Ferdinand mainly through joint monarchical decisions and does not separate his personal position from Isabella's in detail. Moral concern, jurisdiction, revenue, Portugal, and fear of an autonomous governor all appear relevant, but their relative weight remains source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Added the Granada, finance, rivalry, and status-contract prehistory of Ferdinand's sponsorship.
- Clarified that royal approval followed technically grounded rejection rather than proof of Columbus's geography.
