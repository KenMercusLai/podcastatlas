# Harun al-Rashid

## Overview

Harun al-Rashid is an Abbasid caliph represented here principally through the gap between historical rulership and his mythologized role in [[ArabianNights|The Arabian Nights]].

## Current Profile

The episode treats Harun as part of the collection's Baghdad setting, often appearing with Jafar and Zubayda. These are recognizable historical names transformed by storytelling into mobile characters who can enter streets, adventures, moral tests, and nested narratives.

His profile therefore concerns literary afterlife more than a complete reign. The tales show how a ruler can become shorthand for a city's magnificence and danger while drifting away from documentary biography.

## Key Characteristics

- Abbasid caliph associated with Baghdad's remembered court.
- Recurring ruler figure in the Arabian Nights tradition.
- Historical person transformed through oral and literary retelling.
- Symbolic bridge between Abbasid political history and global popular imagination.
- Example of why literary afterlife must be separated from documentary biography.

## Evidence

- **Literary court:** [[379-baghdad-the-arabian-nights-part-4-glt8529456010]] identifies Harun, Jafar, and Zubayda as historical figures appearing in mythologized form.
- **Nested fiction:** [[379-baghdad-the-arabian-nights-part-4-glt8529456010]] uses the Tale of Attaf to place Jafar inside a story that reflexively contains his adventures, illustrating the court's literary transformation.
- **City symbolism:** [[379-baghdad-the-arabian-nights-part-4-glt8529456010]] connects Harun's fictional presence to Baghdad's role as the collection's most vivid urban setting.

## Qualifications

This page is not a political biography of Harun's reign. It records one episode's account of his literary afterlife, and no claim made by a tale about his conduct should be treated automatically as historical evidence.
