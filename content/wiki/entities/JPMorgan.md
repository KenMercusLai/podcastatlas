# J.P. Morgan

## Overview

J. P. Morgan was the financier who appears in the wiki as both a private organizer of market stabilization during the Panic of 1907 and the ultimate financial backer associated with [[RMSTitanic|Titanic]].

## Current Profile

The two sources place Morgan at different scales of early twentieth-century capitalism. [[ep76-chuanyue-1940-wo-yu-gupiao-dazuoshou-lifumoer-de-zuihou-duihua-lr0fb5yfiwoxaamdwjjgh4rjeprg]] presents him asking [[JesseLivermore]] to stop pressing short positions during the 1907 panic because further selling could deepen systemic stress. [[428-titanic-kings-of-the-world-part-2-glt7773063166]] presents him as the ultimate financial backer behind Titanic, with elaborate first-class suites designed partly around his expected use and his presence at the ship's launch.

Together the sources show financial power operating as crisis coordination and industrial patronage, but neither supplies a full banking, corporate-control, or personal biography.

## Key Characteristics

- Financier portrayed as intervening informally during the 1907 market panic.
- Actor who connected a private trader's profitable position to system-level market stress.
- Ultimate financial backer associated with Titanic's construction.
- Intended elite passenger around whom some of Titanic's most elaborate accommodation was reportedly planned.
- Figure connecting finance to both stabilization and prestige industrial investment.

## Evidence

- Market stabilization: [[ep76-chuanyue-1940-wo-yu-gupiao-dazuoshou-lifumoer-de-zuihou-duihua-lr0fb5yfiwoxaamdwjjgh4rjeprg]] says Morgan asked Livermore to stop shorting during the 1907 panic.
- Titanic backing: [[428-titanic-kings-of-the-world-part-2-glt7773063166]] identifies Morgan as the project's ultimate financial backer and links him to the most elaborate suites and the 1911 launch.

## Qualifications

The Livermore episode uses a fictionalized teaching conversation, and the Titanic episode is a retrospective narrative. They do not establish the exact legal structure of Morgan's control, the wording or mechanism of his 1907 intervention, his role in individual Titanic design decisions, or the reasons he did not sail.
