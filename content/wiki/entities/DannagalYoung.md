# Dannagal Young

## Overview

Dannagal Young is represented as a communication scholar explaining how identity, information needs, political sorting, and framing shape news interpretation.

## Current Profile

Young's central contribution is [[NewsConsumerNeeds]]: people seek comprehension, control, and community as well as factual accuracy. Because political affiliation can supply a social team, information is often interpreted in ways that preserve group standing and belonging.

She also qualifies the sealed echo-chamber story. Partisan outlets frequently display opponents, but hostile selection and interpretation can turn that exposure into [[PartisanFramingAcrossExposure]] and [[AffectivePolarization]]. Her account therefore separates absence of exposure from the emotional and narrative form in which exposure occurs.

## Key Characteristics

- Explains news use through comprehension, control, and community.
- Connects racial realignment and demographic sorting to stronger political identity.
- Distinguishes cross-viewpoint exposure from constructive understanding.
- Treats media choice and media influence as mutually interacting rather than one-way.
