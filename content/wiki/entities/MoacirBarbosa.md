# Moacir Barbosa

## Overview

Moacir Barbosa was Brazil's Black goalkeeper in the decisive 1950 World Cup match and became a long-term scapegoat for the [[Maracanazo]].

## Current Profile

The episode uses Barbosa's afterlife to show how collective national disappointment can be racialized and personalized. Although Uruguay's victory was a team and match outcome, Brazilian commentary disproportionately blamed Black players and attached the country's grief to its goalkeeper. Barbosa's account of being identified decades later as the man who made Brazil cry, and his exclusion from a 1994 training camp, show the punishment persisting far beyond ordinary sporting criticism.

## Key Characteristics

- Goalkeeper for Brazil in the decisive 1950 match against Uruguay.
- Black player subjected to racialized blame after national defeat.
- Individual made to carry responsibility for a collective sporting failure.
- Public figure whose association with the loss persisted for decades.
- Case connecting football memory to prejudice and scapegoating.
