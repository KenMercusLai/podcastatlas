# Freakonomics Radio

## Overview
Freakonomics Radio is the economics and public-policy podcast whose episodes 695 and 696 examine the U.S. fertility gap and housing as one possible constraint on family formation.

## Current Profile
In [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-58f17fc3725ef3102277ae962324f7dd-59875813-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-58f17fc3725ef3102277ae962324f7dd-59875813]], the show uses disagreement among [[MelissaKearney]], [[LymanStone]], and [[NickBloom]] to map low fertility without presenting a single causal explanation or policy cure. [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-dde046734f50fc7c6a18ae9c19ada137-55355169-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-dde046734f50fc7c6a18ae9c19ada137-55355169]] then narrows the inquiry to housing, combining causal qualifications with Austin and New York policy cases. Across both, the organizing move is to separate population targets from whether people can realize their own family intentions.

## Key Characteristics
- Connects personal choices with labor, housing, and public policy.
- Preserves disagreement about financial incentives and remote-work effects.
- Distinguishes evidence, interpretation, and speculation in the supplied summary.
- Uses a sequel structure to move from a multi-causal fertility map to a bounded policy lever.
