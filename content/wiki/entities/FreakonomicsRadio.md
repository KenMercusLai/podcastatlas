# Freakonomics Radio

## Overview
Freakonomics Radio is the economics and public-policy podcast whose supplied episodes examine the U.S. fertility gap, housing constraints, and the production system that makes American construction unusually slow and expensive.

## Current Profile
In [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-58f17fc3725ef3102277ae962324f7dd-59875813-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-58f17fc3725ef3102277ae962324f7dd-59875813]], the show uses disagreement among [[MelissaKearney]], [[LymanStone]], and [[NickBloom]] to map low fertility without presenting a single causal explanation or policy cure. [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-dde046734f50fc7c6a18ae9c19ada137-55355169-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-dde046734f50fc7c6a18ae9c19ada137-55355169]] narrows the inquiry to housing, combining causal qualifications with Austin and New York policy cases. [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-76efcd195d1b26a4faab0a64b31c1972-52966951-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-76efcd195d1b26a4faab0a64b31c1972-52966951]] then examines why the construction system itself struggles to produce buildings efficiently and why modular technology requires institutional support. Across all three, the organizing move is a qualified multi-causal diagnosis followed by bounded policy mechanisms rather than a single cure.

## Key Characteristics
- Connects personal choices with labor, housing, and public policy.
- Preserves disagreement about financial incentives and remote-work effects.
- Distinguishes evidence, interpretation, and speculation in the supplied summary.
- Uses a sequel structure to move from a multi-causal fertility map to housing policy and then construction capacity.
- Tests technological solutions against regulation, incentives, demand, coordination, and historical failure.
