# Mark D'Esposito

## Overview
Mark D'Esposito is the neurologist and cognitive neuroscientist interviewed by [[AndrewHuberman]] about frontal control, working memory, neuromodulators, brain injury, aging, and rehabilitation.

## Current Profile
In [[how-to-optimize-cognitive-function-brain-health-dr-mark-desposito-scim4205449835]], D'Esposito presents the prefrontal cortex as a broadly connected control system rather than a solitary command center. He links [[ExecutiveFunction]] to maintaining rules and goals, [[WorkingMemoryActiveControl]] to holding and manipulating unavailable information, and [[GoalManagementTraining]] to the practical problem of transferring cognitive training into daily life. His broader clinical and research frame emphasizes optimization rather than maximization: dopamine effects depend on baseline state, concussion recovery should progress as tolerated, and [[BrainNetworkModularity]] may help explain why people respond differently to the same intervention.

## Key Characteristics
- Explains frontal function through rules, goals, context, and distributed network influence.
- Treats working memory as an active control process rather than passive storage.
- Uses baseline-dependent response to reject simple more-neuromodulator-is-better claims.
- Favors rehabilitation that teaches planning, monitoring, refocusing, and real-world transfer.
- Connects individual treatment response to measurable brain-network organization while preserving uncertainty.

## Evidence
- Frontal-control account - [[how-to-optimize-cognitive-function-brain-health-dr-mark-desposito-scim4205449835]] attributes planning, prioritization, flexible behavior, and rule use to interacting frontal and distributed systems.
- Working-memory and dopamine account - [[how-to-optimize-cognitive-function-brain-health-dr-mark-desposito-scim4205449835]] defines active maintenance and manipulation, then uses baseline-dependent bromocriptine effects to illustrate an inverted-U.
- Rehabilitation account - [[how-to-optimize-cognitive-function-brain-health-dr-mark-desposito-scim4205449835]] describes goal-management training, staged concussion activity, mindfulness, exercise, and possible multimodal future treatment.
- Network account - [[how-to-optimize-cognitive-function-brain-health-dr-mark-desposito-scim4205449835]] reports modularity as a candidate predictor across traumatic brain injury and other settings while acknowledging small studies and the need for simpler proxies.
