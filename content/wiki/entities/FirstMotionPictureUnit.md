# First Motion Picture Unit

## Overview

The First Motion Picture Unit was a United States Army Air Forces filmmaking organization that produced training films and newsreels during the Second World War. The bounded source locates it at the former Hal Roach Studios in Culver City, informally called “Fort Roach.”

## Current Profile

[[teaser-the-compromises-of-al-levitt-teaser-the-compromises-of-al-levitt]] presents the unit as an institutional crossing point for politically divergent Hollywood personnel. [[AlLevitt|Al Levitt]], a former Young Communist League member and aspiring screenwriter, served there for about eighteen months, while [[RonaldReagan|Ronald Reagan]] narrated many of its films.

The teaser briefly illustrates the unit's output with a film describing the B-29 bomber's size, engines, and fuel capacity. Its evidence is sufficient to establish the unit's training-and-newsreel role and the two men's overlap, but not its complete organization, output, or strategic effect.

## Key Characteristics

- A wartime Army Air Forces unit producing audiovisual training and information material.
- Based at the government-occupied former Hal Roach Studios in Culver City.
- Informally known as “Fort Roach” by people associated with the post.
- A workplace bringing Hollywood skills and military communication needs together.
- The shared institutional setting for Levitt's service and Reagan's film narration.
