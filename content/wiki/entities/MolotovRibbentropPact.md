# Molotov-Ribbentrop Pact

## Overview

The Molotov-Ribbentrop Pact was the August 1939 German-Soviet non-aggression agreement whose secret protocol divided eastern European spheres of influence and cleared an immediate diplomatic obstacle to the [[InvasionOfPoland1939|invasion and partition of Poland]].

## Current Profile

The episode treats the pact as a convergence of incompatible short-term interests rather than ideological reconciliation. [[AdolfHitler]] wanted to attack Poland without facing a simultaneous Soviet alignment with Britain and France. [[JosephStalin]], after purges had weakened military leadership and expertise, wanted time to rebuild and judged western approaches to Moscow unserious.

A trade agreement preceded [[JoachimVonRibbentrop]]'s 23 August mission to Moscow. The public non-aggression pact shocked fascists, communists, and western observers; the secret protocol assigned Finland, Estonia, much of Latvia, eastern Poland, and Bessarabia to a Soviet sphere. It did not guarantee German strategic success: Britain reaffirmed its Polish commitment, and the pact's partition logic preceded Germany's later attack on the Soviet Union.

## Key Characteristics

- Tactical accommodation between ideological enemies with incompatible longer-term aims.
- Non-aggression agreement preceded by economic exchange and accelerated by Hitler's invasion timetable.
- Secret territorial protocol dividing eastern European states and regions without their consent.
- Immediate enabler of Germany's attack on Poland and later Soviet entry from the east.
- Diplomatic shock that failed to dissolve Britain's guarantee to Poland.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to one narrative episode and does not independently verify the protocol map, negotiating record, or comparative weight of Soviet, German, British, French, and Polish choices. “Buying time” explains a Soviet incentive but does not establish one motive, excuse the partition, or mean Stalin accurately forecast Germany's later timing. The pact enabled invasion without alone causing the wider war.

## What Changed

- Established the pact as a short-term strategic bargain and secret territorial division rather than ideological convergence.
- Distinguished removal of the immediate Soviet obstacle from Hitler's failed assumption that Britain and France would also stand aside.
