## Overview
Maria Curi is an [[Axios]] technology-policy analyst who appears across [[MarketplaceTech]] Bytes and policy episodes. The source pages sometimes spell her surname as Curie or Currie, but they consistently use the same analyst role and topic lane.

## Current Profile
Curi's recurring wiki role is to connect individual technology stories to governance capacity. Her strongest through line is that AI and platform products move faster than liability doctrine, state-federal authority, and public trust: she discusses social media product liability, AI intimate-image abuse, AI worker literacy, prediction-market safeguards, state AI regulation, frontier release review, model access restrictions, political deepfakes, and text watermarking. The September 11 episode extends her profile from discrete policy fights into an integrated AI-risk and infrastructure briefing: public AI-doom attention, independent incident review, congressional will, regulatory capture, international safety competition, data-center geography, and AI dubbing all become parts of one policy landscape.

## Key Characteristics
- Curi translates fast-moving technology stories into policy categories such as liability, disclosure, jurisdiction, safety review, and public trust.
- Her AI-governance analysis often focuses on the gap between voluntary company action and enforceable public rules.
- She treats state-level action as important when federal legislation stalls, but also tracks federal preemption, constitutional limits, and agency authority.
- Her Marketplace Tech appearances often connect multiple domains in one episode: AI safety, platforms, prediction markets, satellites, books, wearables, and media tools.
- The newest source adds infrastructure geography and international coordination to her recurring AI-policy frame.
