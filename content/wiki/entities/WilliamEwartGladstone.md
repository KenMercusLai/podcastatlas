# William Ewart Gladstone

## Overview

William Ewart Gladstone was the British Liberal prime minister whose government's anti-slavery commitments, reluctance to deepen military intervention, unclear instructions to [[CharlesGeorgeGordon|General Gordon]], and delayed relief expedition shaped the Khartoum crisis.

## Current Profile

The episode places Gladstone inside a genuine policy contradiction. Liberal anti-slavery sentiment made abandonment of Sudan's humanitarian cause politically difficult, while concern about cost and imperial entanglement argued against intervention. Sending Gordon appeared to reconcile those pressures because one famous moral agent might evacuate Khartoum without a large war; instead, an unstable mandate and Gordon's refusal to leave enlarged the commitment.

After Khartoum fell, public grief compressed the policy failure into personal blame. Critics turned Gladstone's “Grand Old Man” nickname into “Murderer of Gordon,” illustrating how [[MassPressImperialPolicyPressure|mass press pressure]] and [[ImperialMartyrdomMemory|martyr memory]] could erase the distributed responsibility of ministers, Cairo officials, military advocates, and Gordon himself.

## Key Characteristics

- Liberal prime minister balancing abolitionist pressure against military cost and imperial restraint.
- Head of a government that sent Gordon without a stable shared understanding of his mission.
- Reluctant authorizer of the relief expedition after months of delay.
- Main public target after Gordon's death and the relief force's late arrival.

## Qualifications

The episode does not reconstruct cabinet papers, formal orders, parliamentary debate, military feasibility, or Gladstone's full foreign-policy record. Its dramatic framing identifies delay and ambiguity but cannot assign all causal responsibility to one prime minister; Gordon's autonomy, Cairo's appointment, logistics, press agitation, and military lobbying also mattered.
