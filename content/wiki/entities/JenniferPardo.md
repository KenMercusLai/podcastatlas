# Jennifer Pardo

## Overview

Jennifer Pardo is the speech psychologist who provides [[this-is-your-captain-speaking]] with its account of phonetic convergence and [[CommunicationAccommodation]].

## Current Profile

Pardo explains that speakers may alter speech to affiliate with an in-group or distinguish themselves from an out-group. The process can operate automatically, may be harder to suppress during multitasking, and is illustrated by research in which college roommates' speech became more similar over a semester.

She also supplies the episode's principal empirical qualification: listeners may notice a few shared features and conclude that pilots sound alike even though systematic comparison would probably reveal more differences than the stereotype admits.

## Key Characteristics

- Speech psychologist explaining phonetic convergence and social affiliation.
- Treats accommodation as potentially automatic rather than necessarily conscious imitation.
- Connects multitasking with reduced suppression of convergent speech behavior.
- Qualifies categorical claims by distinguishing listener perception from measured similarity.

## Evidence

### Accommodation mechanism

- [[this-is-your-captain-speaking]] attributes to Pardo the in-group and out-group account of speech adjustment and the roommate-convergence example.

### Empirical qualification

- [[this-is-your-captain-speaking]] attributes to Pardo the warning that scientific comparison would likely reveal substantial variation among pilots.

## Qualifications

The page is based on one structured episode summary rather than Pardo's publications, study methods, acoustic data, effect sizes, or a pilot-specific experiment. The roommate finding supports the plausibility of convergence but does not prove the origin or magnitude of pilot speech patterns.
