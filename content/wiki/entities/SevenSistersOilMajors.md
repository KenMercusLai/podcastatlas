# Seven Sisters Oil Majors

## Overview

The Seven Sisters were the dominant American and European oil-company group whose control over concessions, production, posted prices, distribution, and profits preceded the rise of meaningful producer-state power through [[OPEC]].

## Current Profile

[[the-secret-meeting-that-launched-opec]] presents the companies as a coordinated commercial order capable of setting drilling terms and cutting posted prices without consulting producing states. [[120-the-oil-weapon-glt6620754143]] extends that profile into the 1973 crisis: decolonization, rising producer sovereignty, and Western import dependence reduced the ability of companies and their home governments to keep oil politics insulated from Middle Eastern state interests.

Their role is therefore not only as large corporations but as the institutional predecessor against which [[OilProducerSupplyCoordination]] developed. The 1973 shock marked a visible redistribution of leverage, although it did not erase company expertise, infrastructure, or market importance.

## Key Characteristics

- Group of major Western oil companies dominating the pre-OPEC international oil system.
- Controlled important concession, production, pricing, distribution, and profit channels.
- Coordinated terms in ways that limited producing states' bargaining power.
- Provoked producer cooperation after unilateral price decisions.
- Lost relative political leverage as decolonization and producer sovereignty advanced.

## Evidence

- Company coordination and producer grievance: [[the-secret-meeting-that-launched-opec]] connects drilling rights, posted-price cuts, and the 1959 producer meeting that preceded OPEC.
- Profit and political control: [[120-the-oil-weapon-glt6620754143]] describes the companies as capturing much of the value and power in the older oil order.
- Relative decline: [[120-the-oil-weapon-glt6620754143]] links decolonization and the 1973 crisis to producing states' greater autonomy.

## Qualifications

The two sources use the Seven Sisters as a system-level category and do not provide equal corporate histories of every member. Coordination and market dominance do not mean the companies acted identically, controlled every producer state, or ceased to matter after 1973. The exact division of power among firms, governments, and producing states changed over time.

## What Changed

- Extended the group from OPEC's late-1950s origin story into the decolonization and 1973 transfer of leverage.
- Made control over profits and political insulation explicit alongside posted-price power.

## Relationships

- [[OPEC]] - producer-state organization formed against the older company-centered order.
- [[OilProducerSupplyCoordination]] - state-side mechanism that challenged company control.
- [[OilCrisis1973]] - visible turning point in the redistribution of oil-market leverage.
- [[WandaJablonski]] - journalist whose reporting helped producer officials understand company power.
- [[EnergyTradingScaleAdvantage]] - later form of oil-major power based on logistics and market intelligence.
