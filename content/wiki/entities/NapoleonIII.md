# Napoleon III

## Overview

Napoleon III was the French president-turned-emperor whose intervention in [[Mexico]] installed [[FerdinandMaximilian]] as ruler of the [[SecondMexicanEmpire]] before military cost and renewed United States pressure led him to withdraw.

## Current Profile

The episode places Napoleon III's Mexican project between ideology and opportunism. Mexican debt claims supplied an opening, conservative intermediaries promised easy support, the [[AmericanCivilWar|U.S. Civil War]] temporarily constrained Washington, and a Catholic "Latin" bloc offered a vision of French influence against an expanding Anglo-Saxon United States.

He selected a Habsburg rather than a Bonaparte to make the new monarchy appear independent, but misled Maximilian about British backing and Mexican enthusiasm. When costs mounted and the United States again threatened pressure under the Monroe Doctrine, Napoleon III abandoned the regime despite Carlota's appeal and his earlier assurances.

## Key Characteristics

- Combined electoral ascent with an 1851 coup and imperial self-coronation.
- Used debt enforcement as one layer of a wider geopolitical intervention.
- Sought a Catholic Latin sphere in the Americas to constrain United States influence.
- Preferred indirect dynastic legitimacy to overt Bonapartist annexation.
- Withdrew when military, financial, and diplomatic costs exceeded the project's value.

## Qualifications

This is a Mexico-centered profile rather than a full account of Napoleon III's domestic rule, diplomacy, colonial policy, or the Franco-Prussian War. The episode distinguishes British tolerance of French pressure on the United States from active British support for the Mexican empire.
