# Joseph Joffre

## Overview

Joseph Joffre was the French commander in chief who recovered from the opening defeats of 1914, reorganized command, identified Germany's exposed position, and coordinated the Allied counterattack at the [[FirstBattleOfMarne|First Battle of the Marne]].

## Current Profile

[[596-the-first-world-war-the-miracle-on-the-marne-part-3-glt2229565266]] treats Joffre's composure as his central command quality. After the Battle of the Frontiers and the retreat toward Paris, he did not deny the crisis: he removed ineffective commanders, promoted more energetic officers, and waited for the advancing German armies to become stretched before committing to a counterattack.

His role was coalition as well as operational. General Order No. 6 concentrated the attack against the German First Army, but the British Expeditionary Force had to remain inside the Allied plan. Joffre personally appealed to Sir John French and secured British participation in the advance through the gap between the German First and Second Armies.

The profile is therefore one of recovery rather than uninterrupted success. The same commander associated with France's failed opening battles became the organizer of the moment that stopped the German advance, without making the subsequent battle easy or ending the war.

## Key Characteristics

- Maintained composure after severe opening defeats and retreat.
- Replaced commanders and rebuilt operational initiative.
- Recognized German logistical exhaustion and exposed deployment.
- Issued the order concentrating the Allied counterattack.
- Used personal diplomacy to preserve British participation.
- Treated the battle as an urgent coalition decision rather than a purely French operation.

## Evidence

- Recovery and reorganization: [[596-the-first-world-war-the-miracle-on-the-marne-part-3-glt2229565266]] connects Joffre's calm to commander removal, reinforcement, and renewed planning after the Battle of the Frontiers.
- Timing: [[596-the-first-world-war-the-miracle-on-the-marne-part-3-glt2229565266]] says he acted once intelligence and German overextension exposed a counterattack opportunity.
- Coalition leadership: [[596-the-first-world-war-the-miracle-on-the-marne-part-3-glt2229565266]] follows his meeting with Sir John French and the British agreement to advance.
- Strategic result: [[596-the-first-world-war-the-miracle-on-the-marne-part-3-glt2229565266]] credits his timing, together with soldiers, Gallieni, British participation, and German errors, for the Marne reversal.

## Qualifications

This is a Marne-centered profile, not a complete account of Joffre's command or later career. The source's favorable emphasis on calm and timing does not erase the opening French defeats, enormous losses, coalition friction, or contributions from subordinate commanders and ordinary soldiers. Reported dialogue and judgments about individual credit remain episode-attributed.
