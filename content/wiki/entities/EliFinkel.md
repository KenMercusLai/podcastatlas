# Eli Finkel

## Overview
Eli Finkel is a social psychologist presented by [[HiddenBrain]] as explaining why modern marriage combines unusually ambitious expectations with unusually demanding resource requirements.

## Current Profile
Finkel maps changes in American marriage onto a climb from survival and security toward love, esteem, authenticity, and self-actualization. His central judgment is conditional: elevated expectations can make marriage fragile when time and psychological energy are scarce, but can also support forms of intimacy and growth that narrower historical models did not attempt.

His practical framework offers three broad responses when demands exceed resources: invest more in the relationship, use interpretive practices that reduce hostile dispositional judgments, or selectively ask less of the marriage by meeting some needs elsewhere. He also presents compatibility as partly developable and uses [[MichelangeloEffect]] to describe partners helping one another approach valued possible selves.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames modern marriage through the interaction between relational expectations and available investment.
- Treats high expectations as conditionally valuable rather than uniformly destructive.
- Uses historical comparison to separate current marital ideals from timeless human constants.
- Offers investment, interpretation, and need diversification as distinct response paths.
- Treats compatibility and partner-supported growth as relational processes rather than fixed traits.
- Keeps consensual non-monogamy framed as negotiated, high-risk, and non-universal.
