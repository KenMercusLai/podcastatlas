# 汉顺帝 / Emperor Shun of Han

## Overview

汉顺帝 / Emperor Shun of Han appears in this wiki as a late Eastern Han ruler whose reign combines real moments of correction with brittle court, palace, frontier, and succession systems. The current synthesis now spans 129-144 CE: early commandery, garrison, Taixue, and royal-fief repairs; selective acceptance of personnel and disaster remonstrance; repeated palace and eunuch dependence; mixed frontier judgment; and a final succession that leaves effective power with the Liang family.

## Current Profile

The 129-131 CE layer shows early repair capacity before the later court-failure sequence dominates. Shun accepts [[YuXuEasternHan|虞诩]]'s proposal to restore [[AndingCommandery|安定郡]], [[BeidiCommandery|北地郡]], and [[Shangjun|上郡]], treating frontier geography as protection for Chang'an, imperial tombs, pasture, irrigation, and transport. He also revives [[YiwuGarrisonLateHan|伊吾屯田]], creates a Yiwu sima, repairs [[Taixue|太学]] after lecture stoppage and building decay, and sends [[ShenJingLateHan|沈景]] to discipline [[LiuZhengHejianKing|河间王刘政]] in Hejian.

That early layer is not uniformly mild or successful. In the Western Regions, Shun offers to pardon Khotan if [[KhotanKingdomLateHan|于阗国]] returns [[JumiKingdomLateHan|拘弥国]], but Fang Qian refuses. In the Ban Shi case, Shun's anger after [[BanShiLateHan|班始]] kills [[YinChengPrincess|阴城公主]] produces waist-chopping for Ban Shi and collateral execution and display of his full siblings. On the Qiang frontier, Han Hao's over-close Huangzhong tuntian relocation alarms neighboring groups until [[MaXuLateHan|马续]] asks to move the fields back.

The 132-134 CE layers show Shun listening to correction when advice is concrete and hard to dismiss. He abandons lottery-based empress selection after [[HuGuangLateHan|胡广]] and Guo Qian invoke ordered criteria, elevates [[EmpressDowagerLiangNaLateHan|梁妠]], accepts [[ZuoXiongLateHan|左雄]]'s local-official tenure and Xiaolian reforms, and lets [[LangYiLateHan|郎顗]], [[LiGuLateHan|李固]], [[MaRongLateHan|马融]], [[ZhouJuLateHan|周举]], and [[ZhangHengLateHan|张衡]] use fires, drought, earthquakes, and ground splits to criticize appointment privilege, local churn, court-core disorder, palace luxury, harem policy, tuchen evidence, and undelivered good government.

The same middle layer exposes the limit of that openness. Shun drops the Liang-family title attempt after Zuo's objection and Liang Shang's refusals but keeps [[SongELateHan|宋娥]]'s title; he sends Song E out of palace access after Li Gu's critique, then lets palace retaliation reach Li Gu through a direct-edict investigation. He can repair public-office dignity after [[LiuJuLateHan|刘矩]] is beaten and dismiss [[PangCanLateHan|庞参]] after household scandal, yet the pattern is reactive and scandal-driven rather than structurally secure.

The 135-144 CE layers deepen the contradiction. Shun allows eunuch adopted sons to inherit titles, ignores [[ZhangGangLateHan|张纲]]'s protest, presses [[LiangShangLateHan|梁商]] into Grand General office, cancels [[WangGongTaiweiLateHan|王龚]]'s eunuch-backed inquiry after Li Gu and Liang Shang intervene, rejects Yu Xu's later [[WulingManLateHan|武陵蛮]] tax warning, and must send [[LiJinWulingLateHan|李进]] after revolt follows. He accepts Li Gu's low-cost Jiaozhi plan and some legal and Southern Xiongnu restraint, but he also chooses [[MaXianLateHan|马贤]] against repeated warnings and appoints [[LiangJiLateHan|梁冀]] after Liang Shang dies despite the tradition's criticism of Liang Ji's known violence.

By the final year, Shun's court still performs repairs but cannot escape structural fragility. [[HuangQiongLateHan|黄琼]] broadens Xiaolian criteria, Shun designates Liu Bing as crown prince, and [[ZhongHaoLateHan|种暠]] later blocks an undocumented crown-prince transfer. Repeated disasters, frontier disorder, tomb insecurity, and child succession show that Shun's reign contains capable interventions without building a durable command system.

## Key Characteristics

- Responsive to concrete corrective advice when advisers translate ritual, omen, personnel, or frontier warnings into specific action.
- Uses appointments and institutional repair as tools of state capacity, from Shen Jing in Hejian and Taixue rebuilding to Zuo Xiong's Xiaolian rules and Li Gu's Jiaozhi plan.
- Uneven at private-favor and palace boundaries, especially around Song E, eunuch privileges, Liang-family ascent, and direct-edict retaliation.
- Mixed in frontier judgment: he accepts Yu Xu's commandery restoration and Ma Xu's Qiang-field correction, yet rejects Yu Xu's Wuling tax warning and ignores Ma Xian warnings.
- Capable of harsh, status-protective punishment once royal or household scandal becomes visible.
- Dependent on eunuch, Liang-family, and palace networks whose office power grows across the reign.
- Associated with disasters, unrest, and succession insecurity that the source tradition treats as political warning signs.

- Palace dependence and reactive accountability: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-817-kan-si-ruanruo-de-ren-shize-huode-geng-tongtou-llmgqys237de3dzb6ij-4szyetye|Hanji 817]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-818-guanchang-weishenme-yao-duo-jiaoyou-neng-jiuming-liyziudpezaoa4l6pbsq0wk2qdjdr|Hanji 818]] show eunuch adopted-son title inheritance, Zhang Gang's ignored protest, Liang Shang's appointment pressure, Wang Gong's canceled inquiry, Yu Xu's rejected Wuling warning, Li Jin's later repair, Song E corruption punishment, and failed summons to Fa Zhen.
- Low-cost repair alongside command failure: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-819-2-ren-gong-bi-4-wanren-tade-quanmou-lingren-paianjuejue-lsslj3y-krnd8h-c8x5pgsqeinff|Hanji 819]], [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-820-zhichang-jingshi-xiaoxin-lingdao-rang-ni-bei-heiguo-lqkejieiihmupfifuk8sxp3oadap|Hanji 820]], and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-821-budong-laoban-xinsi-haixiang-jinsheng-meimen-lmjxr8fdxgw-colrlinmujba74fh|Hanji 821]] show Shun accepting Li Gu's Jiaozhi alternative, legal narrowing, Liang-family appointment restraint, and Southern Xiongnu surrender policy while mishandling Ma Xian and handing power to Liang Ji.
- Final accountability and succession fragility: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-822-chailang-dangdao-ta-jiang-maotou-zhizhi-huangdi-yuefu-lqhpmayvd3p07byr-zoiv-jyhdv|Hanji 822]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-824-weishenme-women-yao-xue-shouzhuo-lpssy8ltykugdpvmgrzwcpf6-9v9|Hanji 824]] show selective punishment of Zhang Kui, continued relative protection, Huang Qiong's selection repair, Liu Bing's crown-prince designation, Shun's death, child accession, and disasters and unrest around the end of the reign.

## Qualifications

This page synthesizes fourteen source notes, not a complete reign history. Hanji 810 now supplies the earliest current layer and backfills events before Hanji 811; because it is a narrated podcast source, minor figures such as Fang Qian, King Xing of Jumi, Xu You, Xu Jing, Liu Qi, Geng Ye, and Han Hao remain source-scoped until recurring evidence appears. The current profile should avoid flattening Shun into either a reformer or a failed ruler alone: the same reign contains accepted repair advice, private-favor leakage, harsh kinship punishment, and frontier misjudgment. Names and route normalizations remain subject to later correction if additional sources sharpen the Western Regions, Qiang, Wuling, Song E, Liang-family, Zhang Kui, or succession sequences.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 810 as the earliest Shun layer, covering Yu Xu's commandery restoration, Khotan-Jumi response, Ban Shi and Yincheng, Shen Jing in Hejian, Yiwu tuntian, Taixue repair, and Ma Xu's Qiang-field reversal.
- Reframed Hanji 811 as the follow-on empress-selection and local-official setup layer rather than the earliest Shun evidence.
- Compressed the prior Hanji 812-816 material into a single corrective-remonstrance and private-favor boundary synthesis.
- Preserved the Hanji 817-821 contrast between palace dependence, rejected warning, low-cost repair, and failed frontier command.
- Preserved the Hanji 822 and Hanji 824 final-year judgment that selective accountability and selection repair coexist with succession fragility.
