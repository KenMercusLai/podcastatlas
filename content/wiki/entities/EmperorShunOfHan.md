# 汉顺帝 / Emperor Shun of Han

## Overview

Emperor Shun of Han appears in this wiki as a late Eastern Han ruler whose reign mixes bounded responsiveness with brittle command, palace, and succession structures. The current synthesis now spans 139-144 CE: legal narrowing and frontier-response choices in Hanji 820, Ma Xian's appointment failure and the Liang-family handoff in Hanji 821, 141-142 personnel accountability in Hanji 822, and the 143-144 final-year layer ending in the accession of the child emperor Liu Bing.

## Current Profile

Hanji 820 adds Shun's earliest current layer. He accepts [[LiangShangLateHan|Liang Shang]]'s advice to narrow the Zhang Kui accusation case to direct plotters, adjusts [[LiangBuyiLateHan|Liang Buyi]]'s appointment after Liang Shang says the young son is not fit for Step-Barracks Colonel, and later accepts Liang Shang's advice that [[MaXuLateHan|Ma Xu]] should use fortified defense, rewards, and surrender signaling against the [[SouthernXiongnu|Southern Xiongnu]] revolt. The same layer also exposes weakness: the court first rebukes a chanyu said not to have plotted, then has to punish [[ChenGuiDuliaoGeneralLateHan|Chen Gui]] after Chen Gui's unauthorized coercion worsens the frontier legitimacy problem.

In the 141 CE layer from [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-821-budong-laoban-xinsi-haixiang-jinsheng-meimen-lmjxr8fdxgw-colrlinmujba74fh|Hanji 821]], Shun insists on using [[MaXianLateHan|Ma Xian]] against the Western Qiang despite Liang Shang's opposition. He ignores warning from [[MaRongLateHan|Ma Rong]] and [[HuangfuGuiLateHan|Huangfu Gui]], rejects Huangfu Gui's later request to attack with selected troops, and then appoints [[LiangJiLateHan|Liang Ji]] as Grand General after Liang Shang dies, even though Sima Guang's judgment says Liang's violent character was already known.

In the 141-142 CE layer from [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-822-chailang-dangdao-ta-jiang-maotou-zhizhi-huangdi-yuefu-lqhpmayvd3p07byr-zoiv-jyhdv|Hanji 822]], Shun's court contains competing signs of responsiveness and dysfunction. He rewards Zuo Xiong's appointment-quality reforms, accepts some correction, removes the powerful eunuch Zhang Kui when evidence accumulates, and yet also shields relatives, evades structural criticism, and presides over a court where reform pressure is redirected toward scapegoats.

In the 143-144 CE final-year layer from [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-824-weishenme-women-yao-xue-shouzhuo-lpssy8ltykugdpvmgrzwcpf6-9v9|Hanji 824]], the court continues to make selective repairs: [[HuangQiongLateHan|Huang Qiong]] broadens xiaolian criteria, frontier officers fight the Qiang, and Shun appoints Liu Bing as crown prince. At the same time, repeated earthquakes, spreading banditry, tomb insecurity, and [[ZhongHaoLateHan|Zhong Hao]]'s need to block an undocumented crown-prince transfer show that state capacity remains brittle at the moment of succession.

## Key Characteristics

- Responsive to concrete corrective advice when it can be framed as narrowing punishment, improving appointments, or managing surrender.
- Weak at sustaining frontier command quality when his preferred commander or court impulse conflicts with expert warning.
- Dependent on Liang-family and palace networks whose office power grows across his final years.
- Able to punish visible eunuch or frontier-official misconduct after damage becomes hard to ignore.
- Reign repeatedly paired with disasters, unrest, and tomb-security problems that the source tradition treats as political warning signs.
- Final-year ruler who secures a crown-prince designation but leaves a two-year-old successor.
- Transitional ruler whose late choices help set up the Chong, Zhi, and Huan-era succession and Liang-family power sequence.

## Evidence

- Legal narrowing and appointment restraint: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-820-zhichang-jingshi-xiaoxin-lingdao-rang-ni-bei-heiguo-lqkejieiihmupfifuk8sxp3oadap|Hanji 820]] shows Shun accepting Liang Shang's narrower handling of the Zhang Kui accusation case and changing Liang Buyi's too-early military appointment.
- Southern Xiongnu response: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-820-zhichang-jingshi-xiaoxin-lingdao-rang-ni-bei-heiguo-lqkejieiihmupfifuk8sxp3oadap|Hanji 820]] shows Shun pivoting from a rough chanyu rebuke and Chen Gui's punished overreach to Liang Shang and Ma Xu's defensive-surrender policy.
- Qiang appointment failure: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-821-budong-laoban-xinsi-haixiang-jinsheng-meimen-lmjxr8fdxgw-colrlinmujba74fh|Hanji 821]] says Shun chooses Ma Xian despite Liang Shang's objection and ignores Ma Rong and Huangfu Gui before Ma Xian's fatal defeat.
- Rejected frontier repair proposal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-821-budong-laoban-xinsi-haixiang-jinsheng-meimen-lmjxr8fdxgw-colrlinmujba74fh|Hanji 821]] says Shun rejects Huangfu Gui's request for 5,000 selected troops to attack in coordination with Zhao Chong.
- Liang-family office handoff: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-821-budong-laoban-xinsi-haixiang-jinsheng-meimen-lmjxr8fdxgw-colrlinmujba74fh|Hanji 821]] says Shun appoints Liang Ji as Grand General after Liang Shang's death and Liang Buyi as Henan Yin.
- Reform responsiveness and limits: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-822-chailang-dangdao-ta-jiang-maotou-zhizhi-huangdi-yuefu-lqhpmayvd3p07byr-zoiv-jyhdv|Hanji 822]] records Shun rewarding Zuo Xiong's reforms while also using selective correction and relative protection.
- Court accountability under pressure: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-822-chailang-dangdao-ta-jiang-maotou-zhizhi-huangdi-yuefu-lqhpmayvd3p07byr-zoiv-jyhdv|Hanji 822]] follows the Zhang Kui case from exposure through execution, showing eunuch punishment only after accusation, investigation, and wider pressure align.
- Final-year selection repair and succession: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-824-weishenme-women-yao-xue-shouzhuo-lpssy8ltykugdpvmgrzwcpf6-9v9|Hanji 824]] records Huang Qiong's xiaolian reform, Liu Bing's crown-prince designation, Zhong Hao's crown-prince household appointment, Shun's death, and Liu Bing's accession at age two.
- Disaster and unrest backdrop: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-824-weishenme-women-yao-xue-shouzhuo-lpssy8ltykugdpvmgrzwcpf6-9v9|Hanji 824]] pairs Liangzhou earthquakes, capital and northern earthquakes, Yang-Xu banditry, Rinan revolt, and Xianling robbery with the end of Shun's reign.

## Qualifications

This page is a synthesis of four source notes, not a full reign history. Hanji 820 adds real responsiveness before the later failure pattern, so the current profile should avoid flattening Shun into either reformer or failed ruler alone. The normalized Hanji 820 names are bounded to this ingest's source note and should be checked again if a later source gives sharper evidence for the Southern Xiongnu chanyu sequence.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 820 as the earliest current Shun layer, covering the Zhang Kui case, Liang Buyi appointment restraint, Southern Xiongnu response, and Chen Gui overreach.
- Reframed Shun's responsiveness as real but bounded by palace networks and uneven frontier judgment.
- Connected the Southern Xiongnu crisis to the later Ma Xian appointment failure rather than treating Hanji 821 as an isolated start.
- Preserved the 143-144 final-year succession and disaster layer from Hanji 824.

## Relationships

- [[EmperorChongOfHan|Emperor Chong of Han]] - Shun designates Liu Bing crown prince and is succeeded by him in 144.
- [[ZhongHaoLateHan|Zhong Hao]] - Shun appoints Zhong Hao to the crown-prince household, creating the office basis for Zhong Hao's later credential refusal.
- [[HuangQiongLateHan|Huang Qiong]] - Huang Qiong's selection reform is accepted under Shun.
- [[MaXuLateHan|Ma Xu]] - commander whose Southern Xiongnu policy Shun authorizes after Liang Shang's advice.
- [[MaXianLateHan|Ma Xian]] - commander whose failed appointment makes Shun's frontier judgment a central issue.
- [[LiangShangLateHan|Liang Shang]] - grandee whose legal, appointment, and frontier advice Shun sometimes accepts and sometimes overrules.
- [[LiangJiLateHan|Liang Ji]] - outer-relative successor whom Shun appoints Grand General despite later criticism of known violent character.
- [[LiangBuyiLateHan|Liang Buyi]] - Liang-family son whose early appointment is softened and later office rises in Shun's court.
- [[ChenGuiDuliaoGeneralLateHan|Chen Gui]] - frontier official punished after unauthorized intervention under Shun.
- [[SouthernXiongnu|Southern Xiongnu]] - frontier polity whose 140 CE revolt tests Shun's command and surrender policy.
- [[CourtFeedbackCollapse]] - Shun's reign supplies repeated evidence of correction attempts constrained by palace and family power.
- [[AppointmentQualityAsStateCapacity|Appointment Quality as State Capacity]] - Shun's court is a recurring setting for debates over whether personnel selection can repair governance.
