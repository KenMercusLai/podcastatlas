# 邺城 / Ye (Late Han)

## Overview
邺城 / Ye (Late Han) is [[YuanShao|袁绍]]'s Jizhou headquarters city in the wiki, first appearing as the seat transferred from [[HanFuLateHan|韩馥]], later threatened by Wei Commandery and Black Mountain violence, and in Hanji 1061 becoming [[CaoCao|曹操]]'s siege target during the Yuan-family succession collapse.

## Current Profile
Ye's current profile has three linked functions. It is first Yuan Shao's Jizhou seat, acquired after Han Fu sends the seal ribbon and Yuan Shao's troops reach the city. That handover is not frictionless: clearer Han Fu subordinates leave, and [[GengWuLateHan|耿武]] with [[MinChunLateHan|闵纯]] die trying to resist it.

Ye is also a headquarters whose vulnerability includes households and officials' families. During the [[WeiCommanderyLateHan|魏郡]] revolt, Wei commandery soldiers and [[YuDuLateHan|余毒]]'s [[HeishanArmy|黑山军]] attack the city, and Yuan Shao's followers panic because many families are inside. [[TaoShengLateHan|陶生]] prevents a household disaster by entering from the western side, shutting the gate, protecting Yuan Shao's family and state officials' families, and escorting them to Chiqiu.

The latest layer makes Ye the focal point of Cao Cao's intervention in the Yuan succession collapse. [[YuanShang|袁尚]] retreats toward Ye when Cao advances, later leaves [[ShenPeiLateHan|审配]] and [[SuYouLateHan|苏由]] to defend it while he attacks [[YuanTan|袁谭]] again, and Cao surrounds the city with earth hills and tunnels. The surrounding campaign also clears grain-route threats and repairs water transport, making Ye a siege target tied to logistics, internal response, and perimeter control.

## Key Characteristics
- Yuan Shao's late-Han Jizhou seat, acquired through Han Fu's handover.
- Headquarters whose political meaning includes family and official-household security, not only walls.
- Repeatedly appears when Yuan power is vulnerable to internal or nearby revolt.
- Becomes the Yuan-family capital target that Cao Cao can attack once Yuan Tan and Yuan Shang split.
- In Hanji 1061, its siege depends on internal response attempts, siege works, river logistics, and surrounding county pressure.
