# Ctrip / Trip.com Group

Ctrip is the central company in [[kunzai-xitong-li-de-jiudian-ni-buzhidao-de-xiecheng-longduan-lianchengshi-keji-luandun]]. The [[KejiLuandun]] episode presents it as China's dominant [[OnlineTravelAgency]], built from early hotel booking, call centers, membership-card distribution, ticketing operations, the 2003 [[Nasdaq]] listing, and later competitive consolidation.

The source's main claim is that Ctrip's power comes from both capital history and operating infrastructure. [[LiangJianzhang]], [[JiQi]], [[ShenNanpeng]], and [[FanMin]] supplied the founding mix; [[WuHai]], [[Shangzhixing]], [[ModernExpressTravel]], [[WangShengli]], and [[CoastAirService]] supplied early booking and fulfillment capacity; and later ties to [[Qunar]], [[Elong]], and [[TongchengTravel]] helped turn competition into a broader platform system.

[[140-jiudian-jituan-weishenme-dou-rezhongyu-dazao-huiyuan-he-jifen-tixi-chuantai-yuanxingzhe-yu-suibingjiang-lj-xa3wxzjog6-8zm9nfakh6ulew]] adds Ctrip as the demand channel hotel groups try to partially route around through [[HotelLoyaltyPrograms]] and [[HotelDirectBookingChannels]]. The source also notes that post-pandemic Chinese OTAs, including Ctrip and [[Fliggy]], have made their own membership benefits more valuable, turning hotel loyalty into a channel contest rather than a one-sided hotel win.

[[ep91-dingfang-dingpiao-dingjiangshan-xiecheng-51-yi-wei-aoman-maidan-lovhfkz4rklv1ik-uqyeswrdf3uw]] adds the penalty-centered update. The [[YiLaoYongYi|一劳永逸]] episode says the [[StateAdministrationForMarketRegulation]] penalty against Ctrip totaled 51.79亿元 and centered on merchant-side [[TravelPlatformMerchantExclusivity]] and [[TravelPriceParityEnforcement]], while consumer-side cancellation and price-disclosure complaints remained part of the broader trust problem. It also frames [[CtripWendao]], self-operated customer service, global expansion, and accumulated travel data as possible assets for rebuilding trust through [[AITravelPlanning]] and better service.

## Key Points
- The episode frames Ctrip as a platform whose hotel inventory, traffic, app habits, invoice handling, and business-travel convenience reinforce [[OTAPlatformConcentration]].
- Its e-booking and PMS-adjacent supplier systems are treated as efficiency improvements that later increased [[HotelPMSInventoryControl]] and [[HotelPlatformPricingPower]] concerns.
- The source distinguishes monopoly position from abuse: the real policy problem is whether Ctrip uses its position to force promotions, distort prices, or hide fees.
- Ctrip's global expansion is represented through [[MakeMyTrip]], [[Skyscanner]], the Trip.com brand, and comparison with [[BookingHoldings]] and [[ExpediaGroup]].
- Hotel groups can resist some Ctrip dependence through direct channels, but only when their brand portfolio and member base are strong enough.
- The penalty-centered episode makes the abuse mechanics more concrete: exclusive traffic status, full-network-lowest-price promises, cross-platform price monitoring, automatic repricing, and order-reserve controls can all strengthen [[HotelPlatformPricingPower]].
- Ctrip's future path in the episode is not only compliance; it depends on whether support, data, internationalization, and [[CtripWendao]] can make the platform feel like a value-creating travel assistant rather than only a traffic tollgate.
