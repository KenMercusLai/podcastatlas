# 驰道 / Qin Chidao Road System

## Overview

[[QinChidao|驰道]] is the Qin imperial road system described as broad, mainly tamped-earth routes linking [[Xianyang|咸阳]] with the territories of the unified empire and supporting imperial travel, military dispatch, and administrative communication.

## Current Profile

The first source places chidao inside Qin's post-conquest order-making: road width, central and side lanes, crossings, and roadside planting turn movement into a regulated extension of [[ImperialHomogenization|帝国整齐划一]] and [[QinAdministrativeStandardization|秦国行政标准化]]. The new comparison adds a wider Qin-Han operating profile. Roads worked with bridges, relay facilities, local maintenance, official travel, and military movement, while their imperial center lanes expressed unequal access rather than modern public-highway rights. Tamped-earth construction could be impressive yet remain vulnerable to rain, snow, distance, inconsistent upkeep, and changing use.

## Key Characteristics

- Radiated from the Qin capital while also belonging to a wider network of named regional routes.
- Used road form and lane rules to encode imperial rank and permitted movement.
- Relied mainly on tamped earth, with drainage profiles, roadside planting, and some use of sand or cobbles described in the broader Qin-Han evidence.
- Supported conquest, frontier deployment, ruler tours, political display, orders, and information transmission.
- Depended on corvée labor, convict labor, local officials, and recurring maintenance rather than construction alone.
- Included reported dimensions and usage rules whose application likely varied by capital proximity, time, terrain, and enforcement.

## Evidence

- Post-unification standardization: [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-121-yingzheng-zao-shijie-zuizao-gaosu-gonglu-2200nian-huangcao-busheng-ls1u8dmuvs5bbrzadf2dtd00js-p|Qinji 121]] presents broad tamped routes, lane hierarchy, controlled crossings, and roadside trees as material ordering of imperial movement.
- Ritual and capital geography: [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-121-yingzheng-zao-shijie-zuizao-gaosu-gonglu-2200nian-huangcao-busheng-ls1u8dmuvs5bbrzadf2dtd00js-p|Qinji 121]] connects roads around Xianyang, [[Lishan|骊山]], and palace-ritual sites to [[QinCosmicCapitalPlanning|秦代宇宙化都城规划]].
- Military, political, and communication functions: [[8-gudai-de-gaosu-gonglu-qinhan-chidao-yu-luoma-dalu-lhldvw-jzrdg2kqafexwnzdidgli|Weird History 8]] joins Qin-Han roads to troop dispatch, tours, revolt prevention, official messages, relay facilities, and beacons.
- Construction and limits: [[8-gudai-de-gaosu-gonglu-qinhan-chidao-yu-luoma-dalu-lhldvw-jzrdg2kqafexwnzdidgli|Weird History 8]] emphasizes tamped earth, possible camber and drainage, rain disruption, variable widths, and preservation bias toward high-standard routes near political centers.
- Labor and upkeep: [[8-gudai-de-gaosu-gonglu-qinhan-chidao-yu-luoma-dalu-lhldvw-jzrdg2kqafexwnzdidgli|Weird History 8]] attributes construction to conscripted commoners and convicts under senior officials and maintenance to local administrators and基层 personnel.

## Qualifications

The reported fifty-step width, precise lane allocation, crossing terminology, planting interval, surface details, and interpretation of “金椎” are source-scoped or disputed. Capital-area remains do not establish uniform empire-wide standards. The newer source infers that peripheral access and maintenance were looser, but this is not direct evidence for every route. Calling chidao an “ancient highway” is a functional analogy, not a claim of modern surfaces, speeds, access rights, or public use.

## What Changed

- Expanded chidao from post-unification symbolic ordering into a combined military, administrative, relay, labor, and maintenance system.
- Qualified uniform-width and uniform-enforcement readings by distance, terrain, preservation, and time.
- Added weather and tamped-earth limits to the earlier high-capacity profile.
- Distinguished imperial infrastructure from a modern public highway.
