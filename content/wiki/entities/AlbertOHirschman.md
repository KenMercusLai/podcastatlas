# Albert O. Hirschman

## Overview

Albert O. Hirschman is an economist invoked in [[hamlet-was-wrong-hamlet-was-wrong|Hamlet Was Wrong]] as the intellectual source for a view of doubt that enables action instead of paralysis.

## Current Profile

The episode presents Hirschman's “Hamlet was wrong” idea through [[MalcolmGladwell]]'s eulogy for his father. On this account, uncertainty should weaken the fantasy of perfect prediction, not prevent experimentation, commitment, or revision.

Within the episode's hiring argument, Hirschman's role is philosophical rather than empirical. Accepting that plans and people remain partly unpredictable can support decisive action and compassion after failure, but it does not remove the need for safeguards proportionate to a decision's stakes.

## Key Characteristics

- Associated in the source with action under irreducible uncertainty.
- Treats doubt as potentially liberating rather than necessarily paralyzing.
- Provides the philosophical endpoint for Gladwell's hiring argument.
- Is connected to improvisation, experimentation, and revisable commitment.
