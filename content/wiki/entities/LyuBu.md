# 吕布 / Lü Bu

## Overview
吕布 / Lü Bu is a late-Han cavalry commander and warlord whose extraordinary martial force repeatedly creates real political openings. The wiki's current synthesis treats him as dangerous but structurally fragile: he can kill patrons, threaten rivals, seize cities, and escape traps, yet he does not convert force into durable trust, stable command, or sustained initiative.

## Current Profile
Lü Bu is not simply brave without intelligence. Across the sources he detects assassination danger, nearly destroys Cao Cao at Puyang, mediates Liu Bei and Yuan Shu through a credible force display, and repeatedly attracts talented officers and advisers. His recurring failure is command integration. He changes patrons, overclaims merit, alienates hosts, underuses disciplined subordinates, and reverses decisions under fear, anger, household pressure, or immediate threat. Hanji 1024 sharpens the Xiapi branch: before the final water siege and mutiny, Lü Bu already has Gao Shun, Zhang Liao, Chen Gong, Yuan Shu, and Taishan-linked support available, but rejects early initiative and abandons a supply-exhaustion plan before those assets can become strategy.

## Key Characteristics
- Extraordinary personal force and close-access violence make Lü Bu tactically dangerous in both court and battlefield settings.
- Affiliation instability repeatedly damages trust with patrons, hosts, allies, and subordinates.
- He shows tactical perception and survival skill, but weak strategic continuity once a campaign requires timing, logistics, or patient coordination.
- His command system depends on capable people he cannot consistently trust or use, especially Chen Gong, Gao Shun, Zhang Liao, and later the Xiapi defectors.
- Under pressure he oscillates among arrogance, fear, suspicion, bargaining, and short-term family or factional advice.
- His final non-incorporation by Cao Cao follows cumulative political unreliability, not a single battlefield loss.

## Qualifications
Lü Bu is not a simple fool or pure brute. Several sources show quick danger-reading, battlefield courage, tactical improvisation, and successful coercive mediation. The sources also rely on a podcast's narrative and moral interpretation of classical history, so claims about motive, arrogance, desire, and inner order should be treated as source-scoped readings. His failure also depends on external constraints: Cao Cao's adviser network, siege logistics, Yuan Shu's unreliability, and Xiapi factional strain are structural pressures, not just character defects.

## What Changed
- Hanji 1024 moves the Xiapi failure one step earlier: before the alcohol-ban and water-siege triggers, Lü Bu already loses initiative by rejecting Chen Gong's timing advice and abandoning the inner-outer attrition plan.
- It qualifies any low-ability reading by showing that Lü Bu still has real assets: Gao Shun, Zhang Liao, Chen Gong, Yuan Shu's rescue channel, and Taishan-linked forces.
- It strengthens the profile from unstable affiliation alone toward unstable self-command under pressure.
