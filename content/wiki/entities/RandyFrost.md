# Randy Frost

## Overview

Randy Frost is a psychologist whose hoarding research supplies [[dragon-psychology-101-dragon-psychology-101|Dragon Psychology 101]] with its account of why possessions can feel useful, beautiful, emotionally charged, and inseparable from memory or identity.

## Current Profile

Frost initially associated hoarding with obsessive-compulsive disorder but, in the episode's account, came to emphasize pleasure and attachment rather than only intrusive danger or contamination fears. He identifies three recurring motives for retaining objects: possible future usefulness, emotional connection, and appreciation of physical beauty.

His “Proust effect” describes possessions as cues that unlock vivid autobiographical memory. When a person relies heavily on such cues, disposal may feel like erasing an event or part of the self. Frost therefore describes hoarding as a possible gift that becomes harmful when intense sensitivity to objects cannot be managed.

## Key Characteristics

- Psychologist studying hoarding and attachment to possessions.
- Distinguishes pleasure- and meaning-centered retention from a purely fear-centered OCD model.
- Identifies instrumental, emotional, and aesthetic motives for keeping objects.
- Uses the “Proust effect” to explain possessions as autobiographical memory cues.
- Frames heightened object sensitivity as a capacity that can become impairing when unmanaged.

### Memory and identity

### Gift and impairment

- [[dragon-psychology-101-dragon-psychology-101]] reports Frost's view that intensified appreciation can resemble giftedness while becoming harmful when it cannot be controlled.

## Qualifications

The supplied source is a podcast summary, not Frost's clinical publications, diagnostic criteria, study data, or a systematic account of hoarding disorder. The three motives and “Proust effect” are presented through selected cases and should not be treated as a complete diagnostic model. Applying this psychology to museums is an analogy rather than a clinical finding about institutions.
