# Jiang Qing

## Overview

Jiang Qing appears as Mao Zedong's wife, a leading Cultural Revolution radical, an opponent of opening to the United States, and the most prominent member of the group later blamed as the Gang of Four.

## Current Profile

The source places Jiang inside the elite conflict that continued after the [[RedGuards]]' classic mass-mobilization phase. As Zhou Enlai sought American contact and foreign investment to repair damage associated with the [[CulturalRevolution]], Jiang and the other future Gang of Four members opposed the opening.

After [[MaoZedong]] died in 1976, Hua Guofeng arrested the Gang of Four. Jiang then argued that she had acted as Mao's instrument. The episode treats the prosecution as part of a wider [[ChineseCommunistParty]] settlement that concentrated blame on the group while condemning the Cultural Revolution without making Mao personally unusable.

## Key Characteristics

- Mao's wife and a leading radical associated with the Cultural Revolution.
- Member of the group retrospectively named the Gang of Four.
- Opponent of Zhou Enlai's effort to open relations with the United States.
- Arrested with the other Gang of Four members shortly after Mao's death.
- Used subordination to Mao as a defense against personal responsibility.

## Evidence

- Foreign-policy conflict: [[173-chairman-mao-the-cultural-revolution-glt2886812821]] places Jiang and the future Gang of Four against Zhou's American opening.
- Post-Mao arrest: [[173-chairman-mao-the-cultural-revolution-glt2886812821]] describes Hua Guofeng's rapid detention of the group after Mao's death.
- Responsibility narrative: [[173-chairman-mao-the-cultural-revolution-glt2886812821]] reports Jiang's attempt to present herself as Mao's instrument within the party's selective attribution of blame.

## Qualifications

The episode does not provide a full biography, trial history, institutional account of the Central Cultural Revolution Group, or independent allocation of responsibility among Jiang, Mao, other leaders, local factions, and state organizations. Her reported defense does not settle either agency or culpability.

## What Changed

- Established Jiang's profile through radical factional politics, opposition to American opening, arrest, and the post-Mao struggle over responsibility.

- [[DengXiaoping]] - victim of the period whose later reform era depended on defeating its radical inheritance.
