## Overview
Geng Yan / 耿弇 is synthesized as an Eastern Han founding general whose achievement combines military-family capital, correct alignment with [[LiuXiu|刘秀 / 光武帝]], battlefield deception, surrendered-force dispersal, suspicion management, and postwar command withdrawal. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-733-2-gengyan-youzhizhe-shijingcheng-jiangmen-huzi-yongguan-sanjun-lnlfumtdzrigby9ox4v6pzbeogeh|Hanji 733-2]] treats him as a Yuntai Twenty-Eight General, ranked fourth, and as the recipient of Liu Xiu's "有志者事竟成" praise; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-697-yi-kou-qi-kan-dong-donghan-mingjiang-gengyan-de-chuanqi-rensheng-lpbhbmedezq7pjuz5l3q-s1unh7c|Hanji 697]] backfills the Qi endgame and compact biography; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-715-shishang-zui-zhihui-de-yici-duoquan-loqkawl247maw9fn-nomaw5gzkgu|Hanji 715]] places his returned seals inside a broader post-unification settlement.

## Current Profile
Geng Yan comes from a powerful northern military household: his father [[GengKuangEasternHan|耿况]] holds command resources in Shanggu/Youzhou, and the family is remembered in the episode as a line of capable generals. The episode does not reduce his career to inheritance; it emphasizes that he turns access to horses, military culture, and local networks into usable service by identifying Liu Xiu as the more credible center of authority.

His early choice is political as much as military. While traveling during the Wang Mang collapse and Gengshi transition, the [[WangLangHebeiClaimant|王郎]] crisis interrupts his route and draws him toward Liu Xiu. He then helps Liu Xiu secure Youzhou cavalry, rejects unreliable Gengshi appointees, and becomes one of the commanders through whom Liu Xiu converts northern local force into imperial founding capacity.

The Peng Chong rebellion shows the risk around his family position. [[PengChongEasternHan|彭宠]] has prior ties with the Geng family and tries to pull them in, so Geng Yan and Geng Kuang must make loyalty credible to Liu Xiu. Geng Yan's request to return to Luoyang and Geng Kuang's dispatch of [[GengGuoEasternHan|耿国]] to serve at court act as signals that reduce suspicion while preserving military usefulness.

On campaign, Geng Yan is portrayed as a commander of tempo, deception, and follow-through. In Qinghe and Qi-region fighting, he uses encirclement with an exit, misdirection about targets, refusal to wait passively for imperial help, feigned retreat, ambush of retreat paths, psychological pressure, strongpoint attacks, encirclement-relief, and avoid-strong/strike-weak tactics. Hanji 697 makes the Qi finish more concrete: after Liu Xiu's inducement splits [[ZhangBuEasternHan|张步]] from Su Mao, Zhang Bu kills Su Mao and surrenders; Geng Yan sends him to Liu Xiu, returns to Pingshou, disperses more than 100,000 troops and more than 7,000 wagons, accepts Wuxiao remnants at Chengyang, and completes Qi pacification.

His later profile combines biography and settlement. Hanji 697 says he resigns the great-general office in Jianwu 13 / 37 CE and dies in Yongping 1 / 58 CE at age 56, while Hanji 715 supplies the collective context: after unification, Geng Yan appears among the generals who surrender military seals and ribbons and leave direct command as honored marquises. His retreat is therefore not only an individual virtue story; in the current evidence it is also one piece of Liu Xiu's [[HonorsWithoutOfficeSettlement|honors-without-office settlement]] for the founding coalition.

## Key Characteristics
- Founding general who converts family military capital into state-building service for Liu Xiu.
- Early political judge who distinguishes Liu Xiu from unstable transitional claimants and Gengshi appointees.
- Northern cavalry broker whose family network helps Liu Xiu mobilize Youzhou force.
- Battlefield tactician who favors deception, tempo, and controlled pursuit over simple frontal pressure.
- Consolidation commander who converts the Zhang Bu victory into surrendered-force dispersal and territorial pacification.
- High-merit commander who actively manages ruler suspicion when personal and family power become risky.
- Late-career figure whose 37 CE withdrawal is both individual post-merit restraint and part of Liu Xiu's collective command settlement.

## Qualifications
The current page rests on three podcast source notes rather than a complete biography. Hanji 697 is a compact recap/profile that overlaps Hanji 733-2 but adds a clearer Zhang Bu surrender aftermath and late-life chronology. Traditional-looking details such as Yuntai ranking, pacified commandery counts, captured city counts, troop and wagon totals, "never defeated" framing, and death-age notices should remain source-scoped until checked against primary histories such as [[HouHanshu|《后汉书》]]. Hanji 715 confirms the postwar withdrawal setting but does not independently reconstruct Geng Yan's full office chronology. Several minor names in the transcripts appear vulnerable to ASR or normalization error, so the profile links only the actors whose identification is stable within the bounded evidence.
