## Overview
Geng Yan / 耿弇 is synthesized as an Eastern Han founding general whose achievement combines military-family capital, correct alignment with [[LiuXiu|刘秀 / 光武帝]], early rejection of [[WangLangHebeiClaimant|王郎]], northern-route counsel, Shanggu coalition movement, post-Wang-Lang strategic counsel, Youzhou force mobilization, battlefield deception, morale control, surrendered-force dispersal, suspicion management, and postwar command withdrawal. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-669-suanming-xiansheng-kao-huyou-yeneng-dangshang-huangdi-liygphsrxj01-wn3vvwoyu77-bkp|Hanji 669]] now supplies the earliest current layer: Geng Yan is sent by [[GengKuangEasternHan|Geng Kuang]] toward Chang'an, rejects companions' advice to join Wang Lang's Liu Ziyu claim, seeks Liu Xiu at Lunu, becomes Liu Xiu's changshi, and advises a northern route toward Shanggu/Yuyang resources. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-671-nide-lingdao-shifou-zhide-zhuisui-jinlai-duibi-xia-lrjwus-s14y1x2y4r3f-dvri1nv|Hanji 671]] then shows him separated from Liu Xiu, returned to Geng Kuang, and moving south with [[KouXunEasternHan|Kou Xun]] and [[JingDanEasternHan|Jing Dan]] after Shanggu chooses Liu Xiu over Wang Lang. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-673-diwang-quanmou-shienshu-rang-xiashu-dui-ni-sixintadi-lrmv-gchamvgmui8keducozwshghd|Hanji 673]] supplies the post-Wang-Lang layer, where Geng Yan urges Liu Xiu not to demobilize or obey [[LiuXuanGengshiEmperor|刘玄]]'s recall, then helps force Youzhou cavalry into Liu Xiu's camp. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-695-ta-xinku-da-xia-qianli-jiangshan-weihe-gongshou-songren-lttlnoazrrg5dtdvfqmngcbfeody|Hanji 695]] supplies the Qi-outer-position opening before the detailed [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-696-bi-hanxin-geng-qiang-zhe-wei-donghan-mingjiang-zhen-de-meng-lkomaa6qhwwqwmb48vxyabay0skt|Hanji 696]] Linzi battle; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-697-yi-kou-qi-kan-dong-donghan-mingjiang-gengyan-de-chuanqi-rensheng-lpbhbmedezq7pjuz5l3q-s1unh7c|Hanji 697]] gives the Qi endgame and compact biography; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-733-2-gengyan-youzhizhe-shijingcheng-jiangmen-huzi-yongguan-sanjun-lnlfumtdzrigby9ox4v6pzbeogeh|Hanji 733-2]] treats him as a Yuntai Twenty-Eight General and recipient of Liu Xiu's "有志者事竟成" praise; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-715-shishang-zui-zhihui-de-yici-duoquan-loqkawl247maw9fn-nomaw5gzkgu|Hanji 715]] places his returned seals inside a broader post-unification settlement.

## Current Profile
Geng Yan comes from a powerful northern military household: his father [[GengKuangEasternHan|耿况]] holds command resources in Shanggu/Youzhou, and the family is remembered in the episode as a line of capable generals. The episode does not reduce his career to inheritance; it emphasizes that he turns access to horses, military culture, and local networks into usable service by identifying Liu Xiu as the more credible center of authority.

Hanji 669 supplies his earliest current alignment scene. Sent by Geng Kuang toward Chang'an, Geng Yan reaches Songzi during Wang Lang's uprising. His followers Sun Chang and Wei Bao argue that Wang Lang's Emperor Cheng-son claim deserves support, but Geng Yan calls Wang Lang fraudulent, says he will bring troops to destroy the claimant, and then seeks Liu Xiu at Lunu. Liu Xiu keeps him as changshi. When Liu Xiu's staff want to flee south under Wang Lang pressure, Geng Yan advises moving north toward Shanggu and Yuyang cavalry resources, and Liu Xiu calls him his "北道主人."

His early choice is political as much as military. Hanji 671 gives the next pre-victory alignment scene: during the Wang Lang crisis, Geng Yan separates from Liu Xiu, returns to Geng Kuang at Changping, and becomes part of the Shanggu decision over whether to obey Wang Lang's Handan camp or support Liu Xiu. After Kou Xun and Min Ye argue for Liu Xiu, Geng Yan moves south with Kou Xun and Jing Dan, attacks Wang Lang-appointed officials along the route, reaches Guang'a, and enters the city to meet Liu Xiu.

Hanji 673 makes the Wang Lang aftermath decisive. Geng Yan tells Liu Xiu that [[TongmaRebelArmyEasternHan|Tongma]], Chimei, and other forces remain strong, that Liu Xuan cannot handle them, and that Gengshi generals and royal relatives have alienated the people. The counsel pushes Liu Xiu from nominal Gengshi subordination toward independent Hebei power while preserving the formal excuse that Hebei is not yet pacified.

While traveling during the Wang Mang collapse and Gengshi transition, the [[WangLangHebeiClaimant|王郎]] crisis interrupts his route and draws him toward Liu Xiu. He then helps Liu Xiu secure Youzhou cavalry, rejects unreliable Gengshi appointees, executes Wei Shun and Cai Chong in Shanggu after Wu Han kills Miao Zeng, and becomes one of the commanders through whom Liu Xiu converts northern local force into imperial founding capacity.

The Peng Chong rebellion shows the risk around his family position. [[PengChongEasternHan|彭宠]] has prior ties with the Geng family and tries to pull them in, so Geng Yan and Geng Kuang must make loyalty credible to Liu Xiu. Geng Yan's request to return to Luoyang and Geng Kuang's dispatch of [[GengGuoEasternHan|耿国]] to serve at court act as signals that reduce suspicion while preserving military usefulness.

On campaign, Geng Yan is portrayed as a commander of tempo, deception, and follow-through. Hanji 695 gives the pre-Linzi Jinan layer: Geng Yan attacks Zhu'a, leaves an escape gap so routed soldiers carry fear into Zhongcheng, then uses released captives and a false three-day attack notice at Juli to draw Fei Yi out of position. By holding high ground, killing Fei Yi, displaying the head, and sweeping more than forty camps, he turns one tactical deception into Jinan pacification. Hanji 696 then makes the [[Linzi|临淄]] battle sequence concrete: he publicly announces an attack on Xi'an while striking weaker Linzi, captures the city quickly, provokes Zhang Bu into marching, shows weakness at the right time, fights from prepared ground, hides a thigh wound to preserve morale, refuses to leave the battle for Liu Xiu to finish, and places ambushes along the retreat route. Hanji 733-2 gives the broader Qinghe and Qi-region repertoire: encirclement with an exit, target misdirection, refusal to wait passively for imperial help, feigned retreat, and ambush. Hanji 697 then makes the Qi finish more concrete: after Liu Xiu's inducement splits [[ZhangBuEasternHan|张步]] from Su Mao, Zhang Bu kills Su Mao and surrenders; Geng Yan sends him to Liu Xiu, returns to Pingshou, disperses more than 100,000 troops and more than 7,000 wagons, accepts Wuxiao remnants at Chengyang, and completes Qi pacification.

His later profile combines biography and settlement. Hanji 697 says he resigns the great-general office in Jianwu 13 / 37 CE and dies in Yongping 1 / 58 CE at age 56, while Hanji 715 supplies the collective context: after unification, Geng Yan appears among the generals who surrender military seals and ribbons and leave direct command as honored marquises. His retreat is therefore not only an individual virtue story; in the current evidence it is also one piece of Liu Xiu's [[HonorsWithoutOfficeSettlement|honors-without-office settlement]] for the founding coalition.

## Key Characteristics
- Founding general who converts family military capital and Shanggu alignment into state-building service for Liu Xiu.
- Early political judge who rejects Wang Lang's dynastic claim, seeks Liu Xiu before Wang Lang is defeated, and then urges Liu Xiu not to demobilize afterward.
- Northern cavalry broker whose family network helps Liu Xiu mobilize Youzhou force.
- Battlefield tactician who favors fear transmission, false timing, false targets, provocation, morale control, tempo, and controlled pursuit over simple frontal pressure.
- Consolidation commander who converts the Zhang Bu victory into surrendered-force dispersal and territorial pacification.
- High-merit commander who actively manages ruler suspicion when personal and family power become risky.
- Late-career figure whose 37 CE withdrawal is both individual post-merit restraint and part of Liu Xiu's collective command settlement.

## Qualifications
The current page rests on eight podcast source notes rather than a complete biography. Hanji 669 adds the earliest Wang Lang rejection and northern-route advice layer but keeps Sun Chang, Wei Bao, Songzi, Lunu, exact office rendering, route details, and the precise Shanggu/Yuyang force plan source-scoped; the transcript form 耿眼 is normalized to [[GengYanEasternHan|耿弇]] because the bounded sequence identifies him. Hanji 671 adds the Shanggu alignment and Guang'a reunion layer but keeps Min Ye, Ge Yan, exact county captures, and force totals source-scoped. Hanji 673 adds the counsel-and-mobilization layer but keeps Miao Zeng, Wei Shun, Cai Chong, the exact Youzhou commandery response, Tongma-linked force names, and force totals source-scoped. Hanji 695 adds the Zhu'a-Zhongcheng-Juli prelude but keeps Fei Yi, Fei Gan, Zhu'a, Zhongcheng, Juli, Lixia, exact force counts, and Jinan camp counts source-scoped; its Fei Yi is not the existing Warring States [[FeiYi|肥义]] page. Hanji 696 is a detailed battle note, Hanji 697 is a compact recap/profile that overlaps Hanji 733-2 but adds a clearer Zhang Bu surrender aftermath and late-life chronology, and Hanji 715 confirms the postwar withdrawal setting without independently reconstructing Geng Yan's full office chronology. Traditional-looking details such as Yuntai ranking, pacified commandery counts, captured city counts, troop and wagon totals, "never defeated" framing, force estimates, injury detail, and death-age notices should remain source-scoped until checked against primary histories such as [[HouHanshu|《后汉书》]]. Several minor names in the transcripts appear vulnerable to ASR or normalization error, so the profile links only the actors whose identification is stable within the bounded evidence.

- Preserved the distinction between the source's Hebei Wang Lang claimant and the later-Han/Three Kingdoms Wang Lang page.
