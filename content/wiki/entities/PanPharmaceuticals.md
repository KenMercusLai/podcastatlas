# Pan Pharmaceuticals

## Overview
Pan Pharmaceuticals is the Australian contract manufacturer the source uses to date the industrialisation of supplement production and to explain why small brands could hand off everything except marketing.

## Current Profile
The episode says Pan was founded in 1974 as a company focused solely on supplement contract manufacturing, and that later reporting estimated it produced around 70% of Australia's vitamin, mineral, and herbal products by about 2003. Before it existed, mid-century Australian supplement brands were small workshops that occasionally rented capacity from drug factories for larger volumes.

Pan's significance in the source is structural rather than biographical. It represents the pure contract-manufacturing model, compared with the pharmaceutical industry's CMO or OEM arrangement, and it anchors the following generation of factories that extended operations into formulation and raw materials, labelling and packaging testing, product registration, and export - the move from CMO to CDMO. Because each dose form has its own process and stability problems, from tablets and compound ingredients to soft gels, powders, and liquids, that capability gave brand owners the option of supplying nothing but a concept.

## Key Characteristics
- Founded in 1974 as a dedicated supplement contract manufacturer rather than a brand.
- Centralised Australian production capacity to a degree the source describes as roughly 70% of national output by about 2003.
- Represents the CMO stage before contract manufacturers moved into development and registration work.
- Encodes dose-form process complexity as the reason manufacturing capability is not trivial even when a brand is.
- Enables brands to concentrate on marketing and core research while factories handle specification, testing, and registration.

## Qualifications
