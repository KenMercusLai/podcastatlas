# University of Bologna / 博洛尼亚大学

## Overview

The University of Bologna is the episode's principal case for the emergence of a medieval European university through legal study, teacher-student corporation, and demand for trained jurists.

## Current Profile

The source associates Bologna's rise with the renewed study of Roman law and with legal expertise demanded by church-imperial conflict and government. Its importance lies less in a simple “first university” label than in showing how a field of study, mobile students, teachers, credentials, and negotiated corporate privileges could become a durable institution.

## Key Characteristics

- Medieval center of legal education in the source's account.
- Example of university formation through a scholarly corporation rather than a modern national plan.
- Links professional demand to curriculum, credentialing, and institutional growth.
- Illustrates negotiated autonomy between academic members and external authorities.

## Evidence

- Legal and corporate formation: [[7-xungen-suyuan-gudai-dongxifang-de-daxue-boshi-shuoshi-he-xueshi-ltuhvvskxxv3rzzfal-8insvutvx]] connects Bologna to Roman-law study, jurist demand, and the student-teacher guild model.

## Qualifications

The “oldest university” label, founding chronology, rediscovery narrative, exact corporate structure, and causal weight of papal-imperial conflict remain source-attributed. The page does not treat Bologna as the sole origin of all later university forms.
