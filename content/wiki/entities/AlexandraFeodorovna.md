# Alexandra Feodorovna

## Overview

Alexandra Feodorovna was the German-born Russian empress, wife of [[NicholasII|Nicholas II]], and mother of [[AlexeiNikolaevich|Alexei Nikolaevich]]. Her son's hemophilia, her isolation at court, and her trust in [[GrigoryRasputin|Grigory Rasputin]] made her vulnerable to sexual and wartime conspiracy stories that the source distinguishes from established conduct.

## Current Profile

[[128-rasputin-glt1290733613]] presents Alexandra as Queen Victoria's granddaughter, shy, anxious, intensely pious, and never fully accepted in Russia. Alexei's life-threatening hemophilia made the imperial couple desperate for reassurance. His apparent recovery after Rasputin's 1912 message deepened her conviction that the Siberian religious figure could protect him.

The episode rejects the claim that Alexandra and Rasputin were lovers, comparing the sexualized attack to defamatory traditions around [[MarieAntoinette|Marie Antoinette]]. During the First World War, Nicholas's move to army headquarters left Alexandra more visible in the capital; her German birth and Rasputin's access then fed stories that they governed Russia, served Germany, or participated in broader conspiracies. The source treats those rumors as evidence of political toxicity, not proof of the alleged relationships or treason.

## Key Characteristics

- German-born and poorly integrated into a court and public that already viewed her with suspicion.
- Intensely pious, private, and emotionally shaped by her son's dangerous hereditary illness.
- Trusted Rasputin after his reassurance coincided with Alexei's recovery from a severe bleeding crisis.
- Became a target of sexual, foreign-agent, and conspiracy allegations during imperial decline and war.
- Remained emotionally dependent on Rasputin without the source establishing a sexual affair.
