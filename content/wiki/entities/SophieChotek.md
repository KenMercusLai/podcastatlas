# Sophie Chotek

## Overview

Sophie Chotek, Duchess of Hohenberg, was [[FranzFerdinand]]'s wife and a victim of the 28 June 1914 Sarajevo assassination.

## Current Profile

The biographical episode presents Sophie as the emotionally stabilizing center of Franz Ferdinand's private life. Although Catholic and aristocratic, she did not meet Habsburg house-law requirements for an equal bride. Their [[MorganaticMarriage|morganatic marriage]] therefore barred her from becoming empress or queen of Hungary, excluded their children from succession, and subjected her to separate entrances, lower seating, and repeated court humiliation. At Konopiste, however, she and their three children anchored a domestic life of gardens and family evenings.

The Sarajevo episode presents her as more than a passive companion or secondary casualty. The visit temporarily gave the couple unusual public freedom: Franz Ferdinand carried her parasol, walked beside her, and displayed their affection. She calmed him after the failed bombing, spoke about their children at City Hall, and refused his proposal that she return separately to safety. After [[GavriloPrincip]] shot them, she died shortly before her husband; funeral protocol restored the hierarchy their public tenderness had briefly challenged.

## Key Characteristics

- Wife and emotional partner of [[FranzFerdinand]], presented as a calming influence and center of family life.
- Catholic aristocrat deemed ineligible for equal Habsburg dynastic marriage despite her lineage.
- Subject to lower court rank and exclusion of her children from succession under the morganatic settlement.
- Publicly affirmed by Franz Ferdinand through gestures that departed from Habsburg protocol.
- Chose to remain beside her husband after the first attack rather than return separately.
- Treated as dynastically subordinate even in death through coffin placement and the exclusion of her children.

## Qualifications

This profile is grounded in two narrative-history episodes focused on marriage, family life, the final days, and the funeral. It does not provide a complete biography, independent assessment of Sophie's politics, or comparative institutional history of morganatic marriage. Claims about her emotional influence remain interpretive, and her decision to stay with Franz Ferdinand should not be turned into blame for the assassination.

## What Changed

- Extended Sophie backward from Sarajevo into the courtship, morganatic settlement, repeated humiliation, and family household.
- Reframed the Sarajevo partnership as the public expression of a longer private stabilizing role.
