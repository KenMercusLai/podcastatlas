# Sophie Chotek

## Overview

Sophie Chotek, Duchess of Hohenberg, was [[FranzFerdinand]]'s wife and a victim of the 28 June 1914 Sarajevo assassination.

## Current Profile

The episode presents Sophie as more than a passive companion or secondary casualty. The couple's morganatic marriage subjected her to court humiliations, yet the Sarajevo visit temporarily gave them unusual public freedom: Franz Ferdinand carried her parasol, walked beside her, and displayed their affection. She calmed him after the failed bombing, spoke about their children at City Hall, and refused his proposal that she return separately to safety. After [[GavriloPrincip]] shot them, she died shortly before her husband; funeral protocol then restored the hierarchy their public tenderness had briefly challenged.

## Key Characteristics

- Wife and emotional partner of [[FranzFerdinand]], with a calming influence during the Sarajevo visit.
- Subject to lower court rank because the marriage was morganatic, despite her husband's status as imperial heir.
- Publicly affirmed by Franz Ferdinand through gestures that departed from Habsburg protocol.
- Chose to remain beside her husband after the first attack rather than return separately.
- Treated as dynastically subordinate even in death through coffin placement and the exclusion of her children.

## Evidence

- Public partnership: [[468-the-murder-of-franz-ferdinand-the-crime-part-4-glt1158335154]] describes Franz Ferdinand carrying Sophie's parasol and walking beside her at the barracks.
- Crisis role: [[468-the-murder-of-franz-ferdinand-the-crime-part-4-glt1158335154]] says she calmed his angry response at City Hall and refused to leave him before the revised journey.
- Death: [[468-the-murder-of-franz-ferdinand-the-crime-part-4-glt1158335154]] follows her abdominal wound, concern for her husband, and death at the governor's mansion.
- Dynastic hierarchy: [[468-the-murder-of-franz-ferdinand-the-crime-part-4-glt1158335154]] describes her lower coffin, restricted insignia, and the exclusion of the Hohenberg children from the funeral.

## Qualifications

This profile is grounded in one narrative-history episode focused on the final day and funeral. It does not provide a complete biography, independent assessment of Sophie's politics, or full institutional history of morganatic marriage. Her decision to stay with Franz Ferdinand is historically consequential in the narrative but should not be turned into blame for the assassination.

- Established the contrast between public marital partnership and continuing dynastic subordination.
