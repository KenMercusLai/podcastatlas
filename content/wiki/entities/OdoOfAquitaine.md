# Odo of Aquitaine

## Overview

Odo of Aquitaine was the duke who tried to preserve [[Aquitaine]] between expanding Frankish power under [[CharlesMartel]] and Umayyad forces moving north from [[AlAndalus]].

## Current Profile

Odo appears first as a successful independent ruler. When Charles declined to help in 721, Odo defeated an Umayyad siege force at Toulouse, buying time for both Aquitaine and the Franks. He later tried to secure his southern frontier through an alliance with the Berber leader Uthman, sealed by the marriage of Odo's daughter.

The arrangement failed when [[AbdAlRahmanAlGhafiqi|Abd al-Rahman al-Ghafiqi]] killed Uthman and campaigned into Aquitaine. After Bordeaux was sacked and Odo was defeated near the Garonne, he sought Charles's help and accepted subordination. His troops then strengthened the Frankish side at the [[BattleOfTours]], making his loss of autonomy part of the victory's political result.

## Key Characteristics

- Regional ruler defending Aquitanian independence from both northern and southern pressure.
- Victor over an Umayyad siege force at Toulouse in 721.
- Pragmatic diplomat willing to make a cross-confessional marriage alliance with Uthman.
- Defeated duke who traded submission to Charles Martel for military aid.
- Contributor to the coalition that stopped Abd al-Rahman near Tours.

## Qualifications

The profile rests on one popular-history episode rather than a complete study of Aquitanian government or early-eighth-century sources. Toulouse casualty figures are treated as exaggerated, while the terms of the Uthman alliance, the fate of Odo's daughter, force sizes, and Odo's precise role at Tours remain episode-attributed. Cross-confessional alliance disproves a simple two-bloc model but does not make religion irrelevant.
