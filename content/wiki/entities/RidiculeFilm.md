# Ridicule (film)

## Overview

Ridicule is Patrice Leconte's historical drama about a provincial nobleman seeking royal support for a marsh-drainage project at the court of Louis XVI.

## Current Profile

[[242-french-history-on-film-glt2672520929]] presents the film as its strongest example of history dramatized through a past worldview. At Versailles, practical merit does not grant access: wit, timing, status, humiliation, and social performance determine whether the protagonist can reach power. The coming Revolution remains in the background as the court's elegant code exposes exclusion and public-purpose failure.

## Key Characteristics

- Makes courtly wit a mechanism of political access rather than decorative dialogue.
- Connects a practical public-health project to aristocratic gatekeeping.
- Reconstructs unfamiliar social rules without making them morally neutral.
- Uses exclusion, ritual, and exile to foreshadow revolutionary breakdown.

## Evidence

- Social mechanism: [[242-french-history-on-film-glt2672520929]] emphasizes the protagonist's need to master verbal cruelty and performance before power will hear his proposal.
- Historical interpretation: [[242-french-history-on-film-glt2672520929]] calls the film exemplary because its past social code governs the drama rather than serving as costume alone.
