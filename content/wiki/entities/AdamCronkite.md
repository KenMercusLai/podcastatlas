# Adam Cronkite

## Overview

Adam Cronkite is presented as an organizer who tested lottery-selected student councils in Cochabamba, Bolivia, after concluding that conventional political participation and protest did not supply a practical reform mechanism.

## Current Profile

Cronkite's experiment replaced campaigns, speeches, posters, voting, and recounts with voluntary entry and the drawing of colored fava beans. His interpretation is that elections discourage some capable students from entering, narrow representation toward familiar social groups, and reward signals that predict campaigning better than governing. The councils' reported projects and several initially underestimated students support that possibility, but the source does not provide a controlled comparison or long-term outcome record.

## Key Characteristics

- He treats willingness to campaign as distinct from capacity to serve.
- He uses voluntary [[Sortition]] as a low-cost entry mechanism for student government.
- He emphasizes social breadth and collaboration rather than charisma alone.
- He treats leadership as partly developed through responsibility rather than fully visible before selection.
- He favors institutional experiments that expose prediction error in familiar selection systems.

## Evidence

### Participation and representation
- [[the-powerball-revolution-the-powerball-revolution]] reports that roughly three-quarters of participating council members said they would not have run in a conventional election and that selected teams crossed social circles.

### Leadership development
- [[the-powerball-revolution-the-powerball-revolution]] recounts students initially read as bored, shy, or peripheral becoming dependable and effective contributors after receiving responsibility.

### Practical output
- [[the-powerball-revolution-the-powerball-revolution]] attributes first-aid, library, identification-card, field-trip, and competition projects to lottery-selected councils.

## Qualifications

The page relies on one edited episode summary rather than Cronkite's own publications, full program data, or independent school evaluations. Reported projects show activity, not comparative effectiveness; voluntary entry still creates self-selection; and the source provides limited evidence about accountability, failure, durability, or the schools that declined to participate.

## What Changed

- Established Cronkite's student-council experiments and three-part critique of electoral selection.
- Kept reported participation, representation, and leadership effects distinct from causal proof.
