# Thomas Paine / 托马斯·潘恩

## Overview

Thomas Paine was an English radical whose accessible republican writing helped make American independence a mass political argument and who later opposed executing Louis XVI during the [[FrenchRevolution|French Revolution]].

## Current Profile

The sources now connect Paine's revolutionary persuasion to his restraint about punishment. [[CommonSensePamphlet|Common Sense]] made independence, republican government, and America's universal significance legible to a broad colonial audience. *The American Crisis* then supplied morale language during the winter of 1776. In France, however, Paine's republicanism did not become support for unlimited revolutionary killing: he opposed the king's execution and later criticized Napoleon.

## Key Characteristics

- English radical from Norfolk rather than an American-born revolutionary.
- Author of the widely circulated [[CommonSensePamphlet|Common Sense]].
- Advocate of American independence and republican government.
- Popularizer of [[AmericanRevolutionaryUniversalism|America as a universal political cause]].
- Author of morale-building crisis prose during the low point of 1776.
- Republican opponent of Louis XVI's execution and unlimited revolutionary punishment.

## Evidence

### American revolutionary persuasion

- [[349-the-birth-of-the-united-states-part-3-glt9804967639]] presents *Common Sense* as an accessible, subsidized bestseller making explicit arguments for independence and republicanism.
- [[349-the-birth-of-the-united-states-part-3-glt9804967639]] emphasizes Paine's claim that the American cause belonged to all humanity and uses *The American Crisis* to frame Washington's winter campaign.

### Republican restraint in France

- [[125-mala-zhisi-beibi-de-ansha-huo-zhengyi-de-chujue-783972516]] presents Paine as supporting a French republic while opposing the execution of Louis XVI.
- [[125-mala-zhisi-beibi-de-ansha-huo-zhengyi-de-chujue-783972516]] uses his position as a counterweight to [[JeanPaulMarat|Marat]] and [[RevolutionaryTerrorPolitics|revolutionary terror politics]].

## Qualifications

The sources use Paine selectively: one centers his American pamphleteering and the other his French anti-execution position. They do not supply a full biography, textual history, reception study, or account of his wider religious and political controversies.

## What Changed

- Reframed Paine from a French revolutionary restraint figure into a transatlantic republican whose American mass persuasion and later anti-execution stance belong to one profile.
