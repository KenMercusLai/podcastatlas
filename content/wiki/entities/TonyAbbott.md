# Tony Abbott

## Overview

Tony Abbott was an Australian conservative prime minister whose British identity, Catholic formation, monarchism, climate skepticism, hard-line border policy, and gender-charged conflict with [[JuliaGillard|Julia Gillard]] define the bounded profile.

## Current Profile

The sources present Abbott as the overtly pro-British pole of recent Australian politics: London-born, English-paternal, Oxford-educated, formerly in training for the priesthood, and nicknamed the “mad monk.” He became opposition leader during Gillard's premiership, defeated [[KevinRudd|Kevin Rudd]] at the 2013 election, reintroduced knighthoods and damehoods, dismissed climate concern, and implemented Operation Sovereign Borders with offshore processing. [[MalcolmTurnbull|Malcolm Turnbull]] later displaced him through an internal party challenge.

The gendered rivalry is made concrete by Gillard's misogyny speech, in which she cited Abbott's remarks about abortion, housewives, and women and his proximity to signs attacking her in gendered terms. His later accession forms part of the episode's pessimistic judgment about whether Australia's first woman prime minister changed the underlying dynamics of political leadership.

## Key Characteristics

- British-born, Oxford-educated, Catholic conservative.
- Former trainee for the priesthood and strongly monarchist politician.
- Combative opposition leader in a gender-charged rivalry with Gillard.
- Direct addressee of Gillard's parliamentary misogyny speech.
- Skeptical of climate-change arguments in a mining-dependent economy.
- Associated with offshore processing and Operation Sovereign Borders.
- Removed from office by Malcolm Turnbull after about two years.
