# Jenny the Orangutan

## Overview

Jenny was an orangutan at London Zoo whose encounters with [[CharlesDarwin]] in 1838 became an important episode in his thinking about continuity between humans and other animals.

## Current Profile

The source says Jenny arrived from Borneo late in 1837, lived in the heated giraffe house, wore human clothing, and drank tea. Darwin observed her emotions, jealousy, problem solving, tool use, and reaction to a mirror, describing her in childlike terms soon after returning from the Beagle voyage.

Jenny therefore belongs to two histories at once: Victorian animal display and the observational path toward evolutionary interpretation. Her humanized presentation made comparison inviting, but it also reflected a captive institution that staged an ape for visitors.

## Key Characteristics

- Orangutan displayed at London Zoo in the late 1830s.
- Observed repeatedly by Darwin on and after 28 March 1838.
- Presented through human clothing, tea drinking, and childlike comparison.
- Displayed behavior Darwin read as emotional, intentional, and cognitively continuous with human behavior.
- Scientific importance is inseparable from a Victorian captive-display setting.

## Qualifications

This profile is based on one narrative-history episode rather than Darwin's complete notebooks, zoo archives, or a modern orangutan-behavior study. The source also says a later orangutan was called Jenny, so reports must not silently merge the two animals.

## What Changed

- Created a distinct profile for the first London Zoo orangutan called Jenny.
- Connected Darwin's observations to both evolutionary continuity and captive display.
