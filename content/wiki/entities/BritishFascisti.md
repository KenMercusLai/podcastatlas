# British Fascisti

## Overview

The British Fascisti was an early British fascist organization founded by [[RhodaLintornOrman|Rhoda Lintorn-Orman]] in May 1923, combining anti-Bolshevism, patriotic service, uniforms, military and aristocratic networks, Conservative overlap, and street activism before [[OswaldMosley|Oswald Mosley]] created a more plausible mass-leadership project.

## Current Profile

The organization emerged from Lintorn-Orman's alarm about Bolsheviks, socialists, and foreigners. Brigadier General Robert Blakeney helped run it and described it as a grown-up Boy Scout movement, capturing its mixture of discipline, service, uniform culture, and political mobilization.

Its social base included military figures, landed gentry, aristocrats, and women. The episode connects elite participation to resentment over taxation, death duties, Irish upheaval, lost servants, and declining political authority. At the same time, the organization overlapped with the Conservative Party: members were encouraged to vote Conservative, and Tory MP Patrick Hannon assisted with dinners, rooms, and leadership activity.

The British Fascisti held public meetings and confronted communists, but it remained loyal to king, country, and Conservative electoral politics rather than becoming a successful independent mass party. Its internal tensions helped produce the more militant [[NationalFascisti]], while former participant [[WilliamJoyce|William Joyce]] later moved into Mosley's movement and Nazi propaganda.

## Key Characteristics

- Early British fascist organization founded in May 1923.
- Anti-Bolshevik and nationalist movement shaped by service, discipline, and uniform culture.
- Network containing military officers, landed elites, aristocrats, and prominent women.
- Organization overlapping with Conservative politics rather than cleanly replacing it.
- Street movement whose members disrupted opponents and fought communists.
- Politically limited precursor to Mosley's later Blackshirt organization.

## Qualifications

The source gives a narrative introduction rather than a complete organizational history. Meeting attendance, membership, the degree of Conservative institutional support, individual motives, and the balance between patriotic service, vigilantism, and explicit fascist doctrine remain source-scoped. Conservative overlap does not make the Conservative Party as a whole fascist.

## What Changed

- Created a profile of the organization as an early, socially connected, but politically limited precursor to Mosley's movement.

## Relationships

- [[RhodaLintornOrman]] - founder whose service background and anti-Bolshevism shaped the organization.
- [[NationalFascisti]] - more militant splinter from the early movement.
- [[WilliamJoyce]] - early member who enjoyed violent confrontation with communists.
- [[BritishFascism]] - wider current for which the organization provided an early institutional form.
- [[OswaldMosley]] - later leader whose credibility and charisma exposed the early organization's leadership limits.
