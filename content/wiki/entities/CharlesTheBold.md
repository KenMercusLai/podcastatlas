# Charles the Bold

## Overview

Charles the Bold is the Burgundian duke whose attempt to connect the dynasty's northern and southern lands ended with defeat and death at Nancy.

## Current Profile

Charles inherited a wealthy but discontinuous [[Burgundy]]. He tried to turn its collection of jurisdictions into a connected middle kingdom by acquiring and conquering territory between the Low Countries and the old duchy.

The episode treats this ambition as strategically intelligible but recklessly executed. Expansion made Charles vulnerable to a coalition encouraged by Louis XI, while his defeat by the Swiss and death at Nancy converted a military crisis into a succession crisis because he left only [[MaryOfBurgundy|Mary of Burgundy]].

## Key Characteristics

- Territorial consolidator seeking to connect Burgundy's northern and southern possessions.
- Aspiring royal ruler who inherited Philip the Good's quasi-sovereign court.
- Aggressive military actor associated with hubris and overextension.
- Opponent of French efforts to contain Burgundian independence.
- Duke defeated and killed at Nancy.
- Father of one legitimate daughter and no male heir.

## Qualifications

Calling Charles "the Reckless" is Van Loo's interpretive reframing rather than a neutral title. The episode's focus on personality and male succession should not obscure the larger coalition, territorial, legal, and military forces behind Burgundy's collapse.

## What Changed

- Created Charles as the ruler whose consolidation strategy exposed Burgundy's military and dynastic fragility.

## Relationships

- [[PhilipTheGood]] - father whose powerful but discontinuous inheritance Charles received.
- [[Burgundy]] - composite state he attempted to territorialize and elevate.
- [[MaryOfBurgundy]] - daughter whose succession preserved the northern inheritance.
- [[France]] - monarchy that sponsored opposition and recovered the old duchy after his death.
- [[BurgundianStateFormation]] - state-building project his expansion tried and failed to complete.
