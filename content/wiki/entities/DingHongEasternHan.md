## Overview

丁鸿 / Ding Hong now has an early Mingdi-era title-succession layer before his later He-era anti-Dou remonstrance. Hanji 738 presents him as an heir who tries to yield his father's title until [[BaoJunEasternHan|Bao Jun]] reframes inheritance as public responsibility; Hanji 766 later presents him as the minister who uses a solar eclipse and precedent to warn [[EmperorHeOfHan|Emperor He]] against [[DouXianEasternHan|Dou Xian]]'s outer-relative monopoly.

## Current Profile

Hanji 738 supplies Ding Hong's first current layer. After his father dies, Ding is expected to inherit the marquisate, but he reports illness and asks to yield the title to his younger brother. The court refuses. After the burial, Ding leaves quietly, making the source's problem not open ambition but attempted escape from assigned status.

Bao Jun corrects him by distinguishing context-bound yielding from public duty. Examples such as Bo Yi and Ji Zha may make sense in disorder, but Bao argues that in an ordered state one cannot abandon a title given by public authority because of personal affairs. Ding returns, inherits, and is later recommended by Bao for classics learning and conduct. Mingdi appoints him shizhong.

Hanji 766 shows the later court-remonstrance layer. After [[YuanAnEasternHan|Yuan An]] dies and Dou Xian's household becomes the effective approval center for officials, Ding Hong uses the 92 CE solar eclipse to warn Emperor He that maternal relatives without virtue can become dynastic threats. He cites older Lu-clan and Ai-Ping precedents and turns officials' visits to the Dou household before taking office into evidence of court capture.

The current profile therefore joins two kinds of public duty. In youth, Ding is corrected into accepting inherited responsibility; later, he uses ministerial office and omen language to defend the dynasty against outer-relative monopoly.

## Key Characteristics

- Eastern Han minister whose profile spans title succession, classics-backed recommendation, and anti-Dou remonstrance.
- Attempts to yield inherited status before Bao Jun reframes that refusal as abandonment of state-assigned duty.
- Enters court service after being recommended for classics learning and moral conduct.
- Becomes situ after Yuan An's death in the 92 CE anti-Dou crisis.
- Uses a solar eclipse and historical precedent to criticize outer-relative court capture.
- Functions as a bridge between personal acceptance of duty and later public warning against kinship power.

## Qualifications

The wiki continues to normalize transcript 丁弘 to 丁鸿 because the He-era office, solar-eclipse memorial, and anti-Dou setting match the received Eastern Han actor. Hanji 738's father, brother, and marquisate details remain source-scoped where the transcript's 丁臣/Ding Chen-like form may reflect unstable transcription. Ding's later memorial helps prepare the anti-Dou environment, but Hanji 766 assigns the operational purge channel to Emperor He, [[ZhengZhongEasternHan|Zheng Zhong]], [[LiuQingQingheKing|Liu Qing]], palace guards, and northern army command.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 738 as Ding Hong's title-succession and early court-entry layer.
- Connected Bao Jun's public-duty correction to Ding's later ministerial warning against outer-relative capture.
- Preserved Hanji 766's normalization and anti-Dou remonstrance profile.
