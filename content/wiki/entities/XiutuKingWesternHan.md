## Overview

休屠王 / Xiutu King is presented as a western [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] ruler who initially discusses surrender with [[HunyeKingWesternHan|浑邪王]] after both fear punishment from the chanyu, but whose commitment breaks before the Han reception is complete. His defeat makes the planned joint surrender a divided armed transfer and places his surviving sons, including [[JinMidiWesternHan|金日磾]], on a later path into Han service.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-312-xianghan-de-xiongnu-wang-weihe-turan-neidou-lkbyh1fglhog0d9voaxitwu59eic|Hanji 312]] gives Xiutu King and Hunye King the same immediate pressure: western losses make them fear a summons and execution by the chanyu. Their shared danger produces a joint surrender plan, but the episode does not treat shared intent as durable commitment.

The source attributes a separate territorial calculation to Xiutu King. It says he intends to let Hunye King's force cross the Yellow River, destroy the northern bridge, and return to occupy the pasture and authority left open in the [[HexiCorridorWesternHan|河西走廊]]. Hunye King allegedly discovers the plan, attacks first, and defeats him. These motives, timings, and battlefield details are dramatized reconstruction, while the narrower durable judgment is that division inside the prospective surrender coalition precedes Han control.

The episode says Xiutu King's two sons survive after dismounting and leading Hunye King's horse. It identifies one as Jin Midi, whose later palace career is covered by separate sources. That relationship adds origin context without proving the exact surrender scene or resolving how Xiutu King died.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Xiongnu ruler placed under threatened internal punishment after military losses.
- Initial partner in a proposed joint surrender whose commitment is presented as conditional and reversible.
- Territorial actor whom the episode says sought to gain the Hexi pasture and power vacuum created by Hunye King's departure.
- Defeated ruler whose death fractures the surrendering coalition before Huo Qubing's reception is secure.
- Father of two surviving princes, including Jin Midi, who later enter the Han incorporation story.

## Qualifications

This profile rests on one compact and highly dramatized podcast summary rather than a full biography or primary-source comparison. The chanyu's intended execution, Xiutu King's private strategy, pasture motive, bridge timetable, dialogue, troop totals, camp dispositions, battle movements, and his sons' exact submission scene remain source-scoped. Hanji 312 says Hunye King kills Xiutu King, while Hanji 313-1 contains competing death accounts; no death mechanism is settled here. The source also does not establish Xiutu King's full title history, territory, family, or the complete route by which Jin Midi entered Han custody.

## What Changed

- Created a focused profile separating Xiutu King's shared fear of punishment from the episode's more speculative territorial and tactical motives.
- Connected the failed joint surrender to the later stabilization crisis without resolving the conflicting death accounts.
- Added Jin Midi's father-son origin link while keeping the exact survival and capture sequence source-scoped.
