## Overview

休屠王 / Xiutu King is presented as a western [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] ruler whose claimed battlefield defeat by [[HuoQubing|霍去病]] includes the loss of a sacrificial golden figure before he later discusses surrender with [[HunyeKingWesternHan|浑邪王]]. Fear of punishment from the chanyu produces their joint plan, but his commitment breaks before the Han reception is complete. His later defeat makes the surrender a divided armed transfer and places his surviving sons, including [[JinMidiWesternHan|金日磾]], on a path into Han service.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-307-2-huoqubing-gujun-yizhan-zhanshen-neng-you-duo-biantai-ll-egjuu-gn-s-_mazrbbarfmb02|Hanji 307-2]] supplies an earlier military-pressure layer. It says Huo pursues Xiutu King and Hunye King until their forces turn to fight near Gaolan Mountain, reports 8,900 dead from 13,000, and says both rulers escape with a small remainder. A sacrificial golden figure associated with the Xiutu group becomes Han booty. The route, figures, battle sequence, survival details, ownership, and ritual meaning remain source-scoped.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-312-xianghan-de-xiongnu-wang-weihe-turan-neidou-lkbyh1fglhog0d9voaxitwu59eic|Hanji 312]] gives Xiutu King and Hunye King the same later pressure: western losses make them fear a summons and execution by the chanyu. Their shared danger produces a joint surrender plan, but the episode does not treat shared intent as durable commitment.

The source attributes a separate territorial calculation to Xiutu King. It says he intends to let Hunye King's force cross the Yellow River, destroy the northern bridge, and return to occupy the pasture and authority left open in the [[HexiCorridorWesternHan|河西走廊]]. Hunye King allegedly discovers the plan, attacks first, and defeats him. These motives, timings, and battlefield details are dramatized reconstruction, while the narrower durable judgment is that division inside the prospective surrender coalition precedes Han control.

The episode says Xiutu King's two sons survive after dismounting and leading Hunye King's horse. It identifies one as Jin Midi, whose later palace career is covered by separate sources. That relationship adds origin context without proving the exact surrender scene or resolving how Xiutu King died.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Xiongnu ruler whose claimed Gaolan defeat, escape, and loss of a sacrificial golden figure precede threatened internal punishment.
- Initial partner in a proposed joint surrender whose commitment is presented as conditional and reversible.
- Territorial actor whom the episode says sought to gain the Hexi pasture and power vacuum created by Hunye King's departure.
- Defeated ruler whose death fractures the surrendering coalition before Huo Qubing's reception is secure.
- Father of two surviving princes, including Jin Midi, who later enter the Han incorporation story.

- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-312-xianghan-de-xiongnu-wang-weihe-turan-neidou-lkbyh1fglhog0d9voaxitwu59eic|Hanji 312]] says the chanyu's intended punishment pushes Xiutu King and Hunye King to discuss surrender.

- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-312-xianghan-de-xiongnu-wang-weihe-turan-neidou-lkbyh1fglhog0d9voaxitwu59eic|Hanji 312]] attributes to Xiutu King a plan to use the crossing to isolate Hunye King's force and occupy the vacated corridor; the private motive and plan remain episode-attributed.

Defeat and surviving sons:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-312-xianghan-de-xiongnu-wang-weihe-turan-neidou-lkbyh1fglhog0d9voaxitwu59eic|Hanji 312]] says Hunye King attacks Xiutu King's camp and preserves his two sons after submission, identifying one as Jin Midi.

## Qualifications

This profile rests on two compact and highly dramatized podcast summaries rather than a full biography or primary-source comparison. Hanji 307-2's route, Gaolan identification, force and casualty totals, battle mechanics, leader names, escape, and sacrificial golden figure's ownership and meaning remain source-scoped. Hanji 312's intended execution, Xiutu King's private strategy, pasture motive, bridge timetable, dialogue, troop totals, camp dispositions, battle movements, and his sons' exact submission scene likewise remain episode-attributed. Hanji 312 says Hunye King kills Xiutu King, while Hanji 313-1 contains competing death accounts; no death mechanism is settled here. The sources also do not establish Xiutu King's full title history, territory, family, or the complete route by which Jin Midi entered Han custody.

## What Changed

- Added the claimed Gaolan defeat, escape, and sacrificial golden-figure loss as pressure preceding the surrender crisis.
- Preserved the battle figures, symbolic interpretation, and Xiutu King's later death mechanism as unresolved source-scoped claims.
