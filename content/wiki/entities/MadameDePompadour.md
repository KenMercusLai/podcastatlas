## Overview

Madame de Pompadour, born Jeanne-Antoinette Poisson, was a favourite of [[LouisXV|Louis XV]] whose political standing continued after their sexual relationship ended.

## Current Profile

[[97-top-ten-mistresses-glt8855582591|Top Ten Mistresses]] presents Pompadour as prepared from childhood for entry into the king's orbit and as unusually successful at converting personal favour into lasting trust. Her continuing position is linked to diplomacy around the French-Austrian alliance and the Seven Years' War, although the episode's broad account does not prove individual responsibility for those policies. Her case most clearly separates sexual status from the longer work of court confidence and political access.

## Key Characteristics

- Bourgeois-born woman prepared for elite sociability and royal attention.
- Royal mistress whose relationship with Louis XV developed into durable political confidence.
- Court intermediary associated with high diplomacy and wartime policy.
- Figure remembered through both cultural prestige and blame for royal government.

## Evidence

- Formation and access: [[97-top-ten-mistresses-glt8855582591]] identifies her birth name and describes her upbringing as directed toward the king's orbit.
- Continuing trust: [[97-top-ten-mistresses-glt8855582591]] says Louis XV continued to rely on her after the sexual relationship faded.
- Policy association: [[97-top-ten-mistresses-glt8855582591]] connects her influence with the French-Austrian alliance and Seven Years' War.

## Qualifications

The episode does not reconstruct ministerial decision-making or distinguish advice, brokerage, patronage, and responsibility for policy outcomes. The attribution of “après nous, le déluge” and the degree of Pompadour's responsibility for alliance or war require stronger evidence. Hostile court and gendered narratives may both exaggerate and condemn influence that remained dependent on the king.
