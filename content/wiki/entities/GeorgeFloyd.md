## Overview

George Floyd is the man whose fatal restraint by Minneapolis officers anchors the paired *Memorial Day, 2020* episodes' analysis of medical distress, threat interpretation, failed intervention, and institutional accountability.

## Current Profile

The episodes follow Floyd's worsening medical condition during an arrest over an alleged counterfeit $20 bill. Part 1 emphasizes that he was unarmed, not reported as hostile, and repeatedly deferential; his claustrophobia, breathing difficulty, attempts to lift his chest, weakening voice, unconsciousness, seizure, and absent pulse were nevertheless processed through a resistance-and-threat frame. Part 2 asks why officers who recognized danger did not end the restraint and connects hierarchy during the encounter with the department's earlier failure to remove [[DerekChauvin]].

## Key Characteristics

- He was approached after a Cup Foods report concerning an allegedly counterfeit $20 bill.
- He was unarmed, was not initially described as hostile, and repeatedly used deferential language during the encounter.
- He resisted placement in the squad car and suffered a mouth injury before officers escalated the ambulance request.
- His respiratory struggle, weakening, loss of consciousness, seizure, and absent pulse supplied cumulative evidence of medical crisis while restraint continued.
- His death is presented as the culmination of individual action, failed intervention, peer influence, and institutional non-removal.

### Interpretation of behavior

- [[memorial-day-2020-part-1-memorial-day-2020-part-1]] argues that conduct consistent with fear and respiratory distress was repeatedly interpreted as resistance through [[HostileIntentAttribution]] and preserved through [[DecisionFixation]].

### Institutional interpretation

- [[memorial-day-2020-part-2-memorial-day-2020-part-2]] connects the fatal restraint to Chauvin's earlier conduct and to discipline systems that had not removed him.

## Qualifications

The sources are structured podcast summaries rather than the body-camera record, trial record, medical evidence, or a complete history of Floyd's life. Part 2's “excited delirium” discussion is an officer's training-based interpretation, and the episode itself notes that the label is not an officially recognized clinical diagnosis. The psychological frameworks interpret officer behavior; they do not diagnose Chauvin or reduce Floyd's death to cognition alone. The page does not adopt Gladwell's unprovable counterfactual about what would have happened without Chauvin.

## What Changed

- Added the cumulative medical evidence and the distinction between observed distress and inferred resistance.
- Connected Floyd's treatment to fixation and hostile-intent attribution without reducing the case to those mechanisms.

## Relationships

- [[ThomasLane]] - officer who recognized distress and proposed changing Floyd's position but did not end his participation.
- [[DerekChauvin]] - senior officer whose restraint and prior conduct anchor the episode's preventability claim.
- [[MitigatedSpeech]] - communication mechanism used to explain why Lane's warnings did not become direct intervention.
- [[PoliceDisciplineRemovalBarriers]] - institutional mechanism the episode connects to Chauvin's continued employment.
- [[DecisionFixation]] - failure to reconstruct the encounter after Floyd's condition contradicted the resistance frame.
- [[HostileIntentAttribution]] - mechanism used to explain why fear and distress could be read as threat.
