# Yu Tianfang / 于天放

## Overview

Yu Tianfang was a Chinese Communist and [[NortheastAntiJapaneseUnitedArmy|Northeast Anti-Japanese United Army]] fighter who escaped Japanese puppet-state imprisonment in July 1945 after months of observation, planning, torture, and coordination with fellow prisoner Zhao Zhongliang.

## Current Profile

[[lsehilqpxbu9hutqm87jyfejx6wy]] traces Yu from student activism and underground teaching into prolonged anti-Japanese armed struggle in Heilongjiang. After betrayal and arrest in December 1944, he refused inducements and endured torture while preserving his strength for escape. Sounds, a briefly opened window, other prisoners, guard routines, removable hardware, keys, weather, and a retained regional map became parts of a cumulative intelligence picture.

Yu and Zhao Zhongliang acted on 12 July 1945, killing a guard after a long struggle, taking keys and a compass, and leaving through a window and wall when the last door could not be opened. Zhao was later killed; Yu survived through night movement, field concealment, and assistance from villagers and even some people ordered to search for him. He learned of Japan's surrender on 16 August. The episode then follows his public career and later political persecution, ending with posthumous rehabilitation in 1982.

## Key Characteristics

- Combined student activism, clandestine work, and armed resistance in Northeast China.
- Preserved physical capacity under imprisonment because escape was an objective from the beginning.
- Built an escape plan from small observations accumulated across place, routine, people, tools, season, and terrain.
- Coordinated with Zhao Zhongliang while adapting when keys and doors did not work as expected.
- Depended on civilian concealment and discretionary non-enforcement during the flight.
- Reached senior provincial, military, university, and consultative roles before later political persecution and rehabilitation.

## Qualifications

The prison narrative is presented chiefly through Yu's later memoir as summarized by the episode, so dialogue, timing, cognition, guard behavior, the twenty-minute struggle, route reconstruction, and the intentions of helpers remain source-scoped. The episode does not provide an independent prison record or a full institutional history of the Northeast resistance. His later persecution and 1982 rehabilitation are noted without enough detail here to adjudicate responsibility or process.

## What Changed

- Added Yu as a resistance organizer whose escape depended on long-duration observation and adaptive execution.
- Distinguished Zhao Zhongliang's fatal outcome from Yu's survival and emphasized civilian help as a condition of escape.
- Connected wartime resistance to Yu's postwar public service, persecution, and rehabilitation.
