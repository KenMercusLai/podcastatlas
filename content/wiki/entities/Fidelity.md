# Fidelity

Fidelity appears in [[vanguard-1]] as both an early competitive foil and a later platform competitor to [[Vanguard]]. In the go-go years, Fidelity's aggressive growth-fund success helped make [[WellingtonManagement]] look too conservative, contributing to the pressure that led [[JohnBogle]] into the Ivest merger.

Later in the episode, Fidelity represents a different threat: a broad brokerage, retirement-plan, and fund platform that can host Vanguard ETFs while also subsidizing low-fee products through adjacent business lines. That makes Fidelity part of the [[FinancialPlatformIncentives]] comparison around whether distribution, advice, and fund economics stay aligned with ordinary investors.

[[145-jijin-tougu-zhide-xinren-ma-lrckug0zjqolcczni8ajikb0k5mi]] adds Fidelity as part of the advisor-value research comparison. The source groups Fidelity with [[Vanguard]] as institutions whose research supports the claim that advisory value often comes from investor behavior management, not only from choosing better funds.

## Key Claims
- Fidelity helped set the competitive context that pulled Wellington away from its conservative balanced-fund identity.
- The company later competed with Vanguard from a stronger retail brokerage and 401(k) platform position.
- Its ability to subsidize low-fee products from other profit pools creates a different structure from [[FundholderMutualOwnership]].
- Episode 145 adds Fidelity to the [[InvestmentBehaviorCoaching]] branch as a research reference around advisor value.
