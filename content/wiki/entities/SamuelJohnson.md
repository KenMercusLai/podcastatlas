# Samuel Johnson

## Overview

Samuel Johnson appears in the Franklin episode as a British critic whose attack on American liberty rhetoric exposes the slavery contradiction inside colonial claims to freedom.

## Current Profile

The episode uses Johnson as Franklin's sharp ideological opposite in London. Johnson's critique is not presented as a full theory of empire; its function is to puncture American revolutionary rhetoric by asking why colonists who owned enslaved people claimed liberty for themselves. That makes him an external moral critic of the same contradiction Franklin later addressed only partially and late.

## Key Characteristics

- British literary and political voice positioned against Franklin's American claims.
- Critic of colonial liberty rhetoric because many American advocates of freedom owned enslaved people.
- Foil for Franklin's Enlightenment persona and the wider [[FoundingLibertySlaverySelfCorrection|founding liberty-slavery problem]].

## Qualifications

The source uses Johnson mainly as a critical foil. It does not ask whether Johnson's own politics were consistent, nor does it treat his anti-American critique as settling the legitimacy of British imperial policy.
