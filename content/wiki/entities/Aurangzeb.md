# Aurangzeb

## Overview

Aurangzeb was a Mughal prince and emperor who prevailed in the succession war triggered by [[ShahJahan]]'s illness in 1657. The current bounded profile focuses on his defeat of his brothers, execution of Dara Shukoh, seizure of the throne, and confinement of his father.

## Current Profile

[[362-the-taj-mahal-love-and-death-glt2445608477]] contrasts Aurangzeb with Dara Shukoh, Shah Jahan's preferred heir, whom the episode characterizes as liberal, multicultural, and Sufi. Aurangzeb's military and political victory included Dara's humiliation and execution and the removal of rival brothers.

After taking power, Aurangzeb imprisoned Shah Jahan at Agra Fort for eight years. When his father died, Shah Jahan was buried beside [[MumtazMahal]] in the [[TajMahal]] without the grand procession Jahanara reportedly wanted. Aurangzeb therefore enters this knowledge branch primarily as the actor who transformed Shah Jahan's final years and the monument's burial history.

## Key Characteristics

- Mughal prince who converted an imperial illness into a successful succession bid.
- Military and political victor over his brothers, including Dara Shukoh.
- Ruler who had Dara executed and confined Shah Jahan at Agra Fort.
- Successor whose decisions shaped Shah Jahan's death and burial at the Taj Mahal.
- Figure presented here only through the succession and Taj Mahal narrative, not a full reign profile.

## Evidence

- Succession victory: [[362-the-taj-mahal-love-and-death-glt2445608477]] describes the struggle after Shah Jahan's 1657 illness and Aurangzeb's defeat of his brothers.
- Coercive consolidation: [[362-the-taj-mahal-love-and-death-glt2445608477]] reports Dara's humiliation and execution and Shah Jahan's eight-year confinement.
- Burial consequence: [[362-the-taj-mahal-love-and-death-glt2445608477]] says Shah Jahan was buried beside Mumtaz without the requested grand procession.

## Qualifications

This source supplies only a narrow succession-era profile and does not assess Aurangzeb's long reign, administration, wars, regional politics, patronage, or religious policies. The contrast with Dara is the episode's characterization and should not be expanded into a complete binary judgment.
