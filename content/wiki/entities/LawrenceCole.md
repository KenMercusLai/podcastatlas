# Lawrence Cole

## Overview

Lawrence Cole is the early-twentieth-century psychologist whose raccoon puzzle-box research is recovered in [[rat-vs-raccoon-rat-vs-raccoon|Rat vs. Raccoon]].

## Current Profile

Cole studied psychology at Harvard when animal experiments were becoming a way to investigate human behavior. Over an academic year, he kept a small raccoon colony and presented the animals with puzzle boxes secured by increasingly complicated locks. He interpreted their success, curiosity, and apparent retention as evidence that raccoons might hold mental images and could be useful comparative models of people.

The episode places his work at a methodological fork. Behaviorism's preference for controllable, repeatable behavior made difficult-to-house raccoons unattractive, so Cole's proposed research program lost ground to laboratory rats. His historical importance in this source is therefore less a settled verdict on raccoon intelligence than an example of a plausible model organism being excluded by the practical and theoretical demands of a field.

## Key Characteristics

- Conducted early puzzle-box experiments with a small raccoon colony.
- Interpreted curiosity and retained solutions as possible evidence of mental representation.
- Proposed raccoons as comparative models for understanding human psychology.
- Worked before behaviorist standards made controllability and repeatability dominant selection criteria.
- Represents a neglected branch in the history of animal cognition research.
