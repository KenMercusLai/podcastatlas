# 唐寅 / Tang Yin (Ming Painter)

## Overview
Tang Yin, also called Tang Bohu in the episode, enters the wiki as a Ming painter whom [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-464-gudai-de-chungongtu-daodi-you-duo-ye-lp-qvincrx1zfp5z2ggjygfnpbca|Hanji 464]] presents as a high point in the history of Chinese erotic imagery.

## Current Profile
The episode links Tang Yin's painting market to the aftermath of an examination scandal. It says that after returning home, selling property, and later relying on painting for income, weak sales for copies of Tang and Song masters led him toward commercially popular pleasure images. His skill in depicting women is then used to support the claim that his erotic pictures were especially valued.

The source also associates `蜕食闲宴`, `静春图卷`, and `花阵六奇` with a twenty-four-image group later called `风流绝唱图`, with popular erotic verses attached to each image and later inclusion in a Qing prohibited-books list. All of those work-history details remain source-scoped.

## Key Characteristics
- Ming painter whose career is framed through examination failure, livelihood pressure, and commercial adaptation.
- Presented as particularly skilled in depicting women, with `秋风纨扇图` offered as the episode's comparison.
- Attributed a major role in the episode's claimed peak of erotic painting.
- Connects artistic production, popular demand, print-text combination, and later prohibition.

## Qualifications
This page does not independently authenticate the erotic works or reconstruct Tang Yin's examination case, finances, catalogue, or Qing censorship history. The causal claim that poor sales prompted a genre shift and all titles, groupings, attributions, and prohibition details are limited to the episode's account.
