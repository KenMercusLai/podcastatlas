## Overview

西汉河南地之战 / Western Han Henan Campaign is the episode's account of [[WeiQingHanGeneral|卫青]] recovering the [[HetaoRegionWesternHan|河套／河南地]] from Xiongnu-affiliated Loufan and White Sheep forces, then receiving the title Marquis of Changping.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-280-heshuo-dajie-weiqing-ruhe-yizhan-fenghou-1-lu5pruhgiv4c2jetewxp65lmkwqc|Hanji 280 part 1]] presents the campaign as a main-effort and containment operation. Wei Qing advances from Yunzhong along the Yinshan southern route, moves through Jiuyuan to Gaoque, and uses the northern position to cut retreat before attacking the Hetao defenders from behind. [[LiXiWesternHan|李息]] moves from Dai to watch possible reinforcement.

The reported result is not only enemy loss. The capture of people, livestock, and territory leads to Wei Qing's Changping enfeoffment and creates the field for [[ShuofangCommanderyWesternHan|朔方郡]]'s fortification, migration, production, and garrison program. The event therefore links tactical surprise to territorial consolidation without treating the second as automatic or costless.

## Key Characteristics

- Rearward maneuver from Yunzhong through Jiuyuan and Gaoque rather than a direct southern assault.
- Main effort under Wei Qing paired with Li Xi's reported containment of reinforcement.
- Retreat denial and surprise against Loufan and White Sheep forces oriented toward the expected southern threat.
- Major reported capture of people and livestock, followed by Wei Qing's Changping enfeoffment.
- Opening for commandery, fortification, settlement, farming, and resource development rather than a self-sufficient final victory.

## Qualifications

The page rests on one compact popular-history summary. The event name is descriptive because the title says “河朔大捷” while the body says 河南地 and 河套. The date, force size, route, feint, exact position of Gaoque, retreat geometry, Li Xi's mission, defender awareness, casualties, captives, livestock, and reward details remain source-scoped. A major capture does not by itself establish permanent control; the source's own expensive postwar program shows that territory required continued administration and settlement.
