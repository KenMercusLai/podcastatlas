## Overview

匡衡 / Kuang Heng enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-570-xinyang-buke-queshi-yingxiong-burong-dihui-lrrcbw5rrurytgjxasbp12buf82l|Hanji 570]] as the earlier chancellor whom [[GengYuWesternHan|耿育]] blames for blocking fuller recognition of [[ChenTangWesternHan|陈汤]] and [[GanYanshouWesternHan|甘延寿]] after the [[ZhizhiChanyu|郅支单于]] campaign. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-484-gudai-huangdi-de-hunyin-shenghuo-you-duo-mafan-lv7yfmymwhqdejvr0hxntjszlqwe|Hanji 484]] adds a Yuan-era household remonstrance role: Kuang Heng warns Yuan that favor toward [[FuZhaoyiWesternHan|傅昭仪]] and [[LiuKangDingtaoKingWesternHan|刘康]] must not disorder empress-consort and嫡庶 hierarchy. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-497-huangniba-diao-kudang-xihan-mingjiang-chentang-dangguan-you-duo-nan-liz2ssrmijhowebl5hhx8-61ybmp|Hanji 497]] backfills the original reward decision: Kuang Heng is one of the court opponents whose objections help reduce the award, while the host reads his stance partly as face-saving after earlier advice was not accepted. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-499-liubang-fuwoqilai-dasi-liushi-zhegezeng-zengsunzi-lndfgjehpe7qc36hejxba3znlz0p|Hanji 499]] backfills his first accession-advice role: after Yuan's death, Kuang Heng advises the new [[HanChengdi|汉成帝]] on mourning, marriage ritual, classical study, serious associates, and ruler bearing. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-500-taijian-shixian-zhisi-zhan-de-yuegao-si-de-yuecan-lvu7w8xa8mjn8_mbxew7e4afupra|Hanji 500]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-502-rang-laoban-bei-heiguo-shi-shui-zai-zhaosi-ls833sgrdjz9e1tupiiqillagohb|Hanji 502]] backfill his early Chengdi cleanup position: after [[ShiXianWesternHan|石显]] loses Yuan's backing and Chengdi moves him out of the inner channel, Kuang Heng joins [[ZhangTanWesternHan|张谭]] in impeaching Shi Xian, but is later attacked by [[WangZunWesternHan|王尊]] for past accommodation and for language that appears to shift blame toward Yuan. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-507-ta-zhansha-xiongnu-chanyu-weihe-fanbei-xiayu-lhfkej7pqtikx5st73sls-kwo_ze|Hanji 507]] adds a more direct Chen Tang conflict: Kuang Heng impeaches Chen Tang for taking confiscated [[Kangju|康居]] property and abusing distance from the court. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-505-xihan-de-huicheng-yaoyan-da-le-shui-de-lian-ljuwccxx_aexasafkpbl-y2rvos5|Hanji 505]] backfills his own downfall under Chengdi: the host contrasts Kuang Heng's poor-origin "cutting the wall for light" reputation with accusations of excess land and misappropriated property that reduce him to commoner status.

## Current Profile

The sources give Kuang Heng several different roles. Hanji 484 shows him as a classical remonstrant before the later Chengdi transition: he ties ruler self-correction, ancestral continuity, household order, empress-consort distinction, and嫡庶 hierarchy together in order to contain Yuan's favor toward Fu Zhaoyi and Liu Kang. Hanji 497 uses him as an original reward opponent after Chen Tang and Gan Yanshou's victory; the source reads his resistance less as a full legal biography than as ministerial face-saving inside a decision Yuan still wants to settle. Hanji 507 later uses him as the chancellor who turns Chen Tang's Kangju-property conduct into formal impeachment. Hanji 570 then uses him as the court actor whose obstruction lets Geng Yu show how a military achievement can be diminished inside bureaucratic reward politics.

Kuang Heng's significance here lies in the downstream effect of under-recognition. If the reward for extraordinary service becomes small and the later contributor is vulnerable to attack, then the state teaches future actors that battlefield risk can be converted into court disappointment.

Hanji 480 and Hanji 505 together add a moral-career arc rather than a reward-politics arc. Hanji 480 restores the full contrast behind the schoolbook story: Kuang Heng's poverty, study, and learning are real, but the episode stresses that study and status do not guarantee durable integrity once power, land, and family scandal enter. Hanji 505 then gives the later Chengdi-era closing notice, using excess fief land and misappropriated property to turn Kuang Heng into a caution about restraint after success.

Hanji 500 and Hanji 502 add the survival logic behind Kuang Heng's early Chengdi position. Hanji 500 presents the timing advantage: Kuang Heng attacks after Shi Xian loses Yuan, is moved out of Zhongshu access, and no longer looks equally protected. Hanji 502 then shows the vulnerability that follows success: his anti-Shi-Xian posture comes after a period of accommodation, and blaming officials' fear of Shi Xian can be heard as making Emperor Yuan bear the black pot. His response is not counterattack but visible fear, shame, and seal-returning submission. Chengdi's refusal to remove him leaves Kuang Heng as a retained senior minister who can stabilize offices and absorb future disaster responsibility.

Hanji 499 adds a more positive opening role before those vulnerabilities appear. Kuang Heng's accession advice treats ritual and conduct as institutional authority: Chengdi should grieve without indulgence, regulate marriage and desire, reject frivolous music and clever speech, study the classics, and make his bearing a public model. The host's modern leadership reading turns this into an authority lesson rather than a mere etiquette note.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han chancellor figure known here through disaster-and-amnesty remonstrance, Yuan-era household remonstrance, Chengdi accession advice, Shi Xian cleanup politics, Chen Tang reward opposition, later Chen Tang impeachment, and his own demotion.
- Appears in Hanji 497 as an original reward opponent whose stance helps shrink Yuan's grant, and is later blamed by Geng Yu for obstructing proportionate recognition.
- Directly impeaches Chen Tang for Kangju-property misconduct in Hanji 507.
- Gives Chengdi accession advice about mourning, marriage ritual, classical study, serious associates, and ruler bearing in Hanji 499.
- Times the Shi Xian impeachment after Chengdi moves Shi Xian away from the inner access channel.
- Survives Wang Zun's early-Chengdi impeachment by using submissive resignation gestures rather than escalating the dispute.
- Functions as a negative example of both bureaucratic reward narrowing and loss of restraint after rising from poverty, ending in commoner reduction after excess-land and property accusations.

## Evidence

- Original reward objection: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-497-huangniba-diao-kudang-xihan-mingjiang-chentang-dangguan-you-duo-nan-liz2ssrmijhowebl5hhx8-61ybmp|Hanji 497]] says Kuang Heng opposes fuller reward for Chen Tang and Gan Yanshou, and the host reads the final reduction to three hundred households as partly preserving senior ministers' face.
- Chen Tang impeachment: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-507-ta-zhansha-xiongnu-chanyu-weihe-fanbei-xiayu-lhfkej7pqtikx5st73sls-kwo_ze|Hanji 507]] says Kuang Heng accuses Chen Tang of taking confiscated Kangju property and treating distant Western Regions conduct as beyond court scrutiny.
- Obstruction claim: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-570-xinyang-buke-queshi-yingxiong-burong-dihui-lrrcbw5rrurytgjxasbp12buf82l|Hanji 570]] says Geng Yu accuses Kuang Heng of blocking the grading of Chen Tang and Gan Yanshou's merit.
- Reduced reward: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-570-xinyang-buke-queshi-yingxiong-burong-dihui-lrrcbw5rrurytgjxasbp12buf82l|Hanji 570]] says the result was only several hundred households, which the memorial treats as chillingly insufficient.
- Institutional effect: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-570-xinyang-buke-queshi-yingxiong-burong-dihui-lrrcbw5rrurytgjxasbp12buf82l|Hanji 570]] links this treatment to later fear among people who might otherwise risk themselves for Han.
- Shi Xian timing: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-500-taijian-shixian-zhisi-zhan-de-yuegao-si-de-yuecan-lvu7w8xa8mjn8_mbxew7e4afupra|Hanji 500]] says Kuang Heng and Zhang Tan impeach Shi Xian after Chengdi's accession, Shi Xian's transfer, and the loss of Yuan's backing.
- Accession advice: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-499-liubang-fuwoqilai-dasi-liushi-zhegezeng-zengsunzi-lndfgjehpe7qc36hejxba3znlz0p|Hanji 499]] says Kuang Heng advises Chengdi to discipline mourning, marriage, desire, associations, classical study, and imperial bearing.
- Household remonstrance: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-484-gudai-huangdi-de-hunyin-shenghuo-you-duo-mafan-lv7yfmymwhqdejvr0hxntjszlqwe|Hanji 484]] says Kuang Heng warns Yuan that favor toward Fu Zhaoyi and Liu Kang endangers the proper distinction among empress, consorts, legitimate son, and secondary sons.
- Self-correction frame: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-484-gudai-huangdi-de-hunyin-shenghuo-you-duo-mafan-lv7yfmymwhqdejvr0hxntjszlqwe|Hanji 484]] says Kuang Heng urges Yuan to recognize personal weaknesses and carry forward ancestral institutions.
- Authority lesson: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-499-liubang-fuwoqilai-dasi-liushi-zhegezeng-zengsunzi-lndfgjehpe7qc36hejxba3znlz0p|Hanji 499]] uses Kuang Heng's advice to argue that leaders who are only easygoing and lack measured authority struggle to win respect.
- Wang Zun impeachment: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-502-rang-laoban-bei-heiguo-shi-shui-zai-zhaosi-ls833sgrdjz9e1tupiiqillagohb|Hanji 502]] says Wang Zun accuses Kuang Heng and Zhang Tan of failing to report and punish Shi Xian while he was powerful.
- Dangerous wording: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-502-rang-laoban-bei-heiguo-shi-shui-zai-zhaosi-ls833sgrdjz9e1tupiiqillagohb|Hanji 502]] says Wang Zun objects that Kuang Heng's later accusation makes officials' fear of Shi Xian greater than fear of the emperor, thereby dishonoring the ruler.
- Submission and retention: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-502-rang-laoban-bei-heiguo-shi-shui-zai-zhaosi-ls833sgrdjz9e1tupiiqillagohb|Hanji 502]] says Kuang Heng is terrified, ashamed, returns his chancellor and marquis seals, and is still reassured and retained by Chengdi.
- Poor-origin contrast: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-505-xihan-de-huicheng-yaoyan-da-le-shui-de-lian-ljuwccxx_aexasafkpbl-y2rvos5|Hanji 505]] recalls Kuang Heng's rise from poverty and "cutting the wall for light" reputation before describing his fall.
- Disaster remonstrance: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-480-keben-bugan-ti-jiekai-zaobitouguang-de-heian-houxu-lhn89rqrj1dtntixgt5a7vd8p6ao|Hanji 480]] says Kuang Heng answers Yuan's disaster inquiry by arguing that amnesty cannot reduce crime unless instruction, court conduct, and capital custom are repaired.
- Poor-scholar complexity: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-480-keben-bugan-ti-jiekai-zaobitouguang-de-heian-houxu-lhn89rqrj1dtntixgt5a7vd8p6ao|Hanji 480]] presents the "cutting the wall for light" story as true but incomplete, because Kuang Heng's later excess-land case complicates the simple study-success moral.
- Demotion: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-505-xihan-de-huicheng-yaoyan-da-le-shui-de-lian-ljuwccxx_aexasafkpbl-y2rvos5|Hanji 505]] says Kuang Heng is accused of excess land and misappropriated property, loses office, and is reduced to commoner.

## Qualifications

This page is source-bounded to Hanji 500, Hanji 570, Hanji 505, Hanji 507, Hanji 502, Hanji 499, Hanji 497, Hanji 484, and Hanji 480. It records Kuang Heng's disaster-amnesty remonstrance, Yuan-era household remonstrance, accession advice, Shi Xian impeachment timing, Chen Tang reward opposition and later impeachment, Geng Yu's accusation about the reward dispute, Hanji 505's account of Kuang Heng's demotion, and Hanji 502's early-Chengdi impeachment-survival case; it does not reconstruct Kuang Heng's full career, motives, office record, exact memorial texts, exact land measurement, property case file, original Shi Xian-era conduct, or the legal basis for the original Chen Tang and Gan Yanshou reward.
