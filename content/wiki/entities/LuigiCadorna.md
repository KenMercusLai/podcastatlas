# Luigi Cadorna

## Overview

Luigi Cadorna is presented as Italy's First World War commander whose faith in relentless forward attack helped turn the [[IsonzoFront|Isonzo front]] into a costly attritional disaster.

## Current Profile

The episode portrays Cadorna as rigid, touchy, unforgiving, and committed to the idea that offensive will could overcome geography and modern defenses. His plan imagined a drive through the Alps toward Trieste, Ljubljana, Zagreb, and the Habsburg heartland, but Italy's army mobilized slowly and lacked the equipment, coordination, literacy, and artillery base needed for such a campaign. When early attacks failed, he sacked officers at scale instead of fundamentally changing the method.

## Key Characteristics

- Italian general commanding the early campaign against [[AustriaHungary|Austria-Hungary]].
- Advocate of relentless forward movement and frontal attack.
- Commander whose strategic ambition exceeded Italy's mobilization and equipment capacity.
- Leader who treated officer replacement as the main answer to failure.
- Central figure in turning the Isonzo battles into repeated high-casualty attacks with little gain.

## Evidence

- Command role: [[672-the-first-world-war-italys-doomed-campaign-part-2-glt3383347829]] places Cadorna at Udine after Italy's 23 May 1915 war declaration.
- Offensive plan: [[672-the-first-world-war-italys-doomed-campaign-part-2-glt3383347829]] says he aimed through the Alps toward Trieste, Ljubljana, Zagreb, and the Habsburg center.
- Army weakness: [[672-the-first-world-war-italys-doomed-campaign-part-2-glt3383347829]] links his plan to shortages of guns and artillery, literacy and dialect barriers, and slow mobilization.
- Officer purges: [[672-the-first-world-war-italys-doomed-campaign-part-2-glt3383347829]] reports extensive dismissals of generals, colonels, and battalion commanders over two years.
- Isonzo losses: [[672-the-first-world-war-italys-doomed-campaign-part-2-glt3383347829]] follows the first four Isonzo battles as repeated attacks against fortified high ground.
