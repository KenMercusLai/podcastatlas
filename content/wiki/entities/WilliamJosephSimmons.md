# William Joseph Simmons

## Overview

William Joseph Simmons was the founder of the second [[KuKluxKlan]], relaunching it in Georgia in 1915 by combining first-Klan memory, fraternal organization, and imagery circulated by [[ThomasDixonJr]] and [[BirthOfANation|*The Birth of a Nation*]].

## Current Profile

The source presents Simmons as a failed Methodist preacher and prolific joiner of American fraternal orders who understood ceremony, rank, fantasy, and belonging as organizational resources. His Stone Mountain launch used a burning cross that came through fiction and film rather than continuous first-Klan practice.

Simmons's early organization remained small. Its transformation into a mass business followed his agreement with [[EdwardYoungClarke]] and [[ElizabethTyler]], whose recruiters retained most membership revenue and localized its enemies. Simmons supplied the charter, mythology, and title of Imperial Wizard; the marketers supplied the scalable sales system that outgrew his initial capacity.

## Key Characteristics

- Revived the Klan name in 1915 as a new organization rather than a simple continuation.
- Adapted fraternal rank, ritual, titles, secrecy, and sociability to white-supremacist politics.
- Adopted cross-burning and costume imagery circulated through fiction and film.
- Mixed ideological nostalgia with personal status and commercial ambition.
- Depended on Clarke and Tyler to turn a weak early organization into a national recruitment business.

## Qualifications

The episode offers an interpretive character sketch rather than a full biography. Its claims about failure, fantasy, motives, finances, exact early membership, and the balance between belief and money-making remain source-scoped. Founding authority should not be confused with sole authorship of later growth or political strategy.

## What Changed

- Created Simmons as the fraternal founder and symbolic inventor of the second Klan.
- Distinguished his initial organization from the marketers' later scaling system.
