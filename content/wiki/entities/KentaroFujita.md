# Kentaro Fujita

## Overview
Kentaro Fujita is the psychology researcher interviewed in [[master-self-control-overcome-procrastination-dr-kentaro-fujita-scim8156556751]] about self-control, motivation, delayed gratification, goals, and procrastination.

## Current Profile
In the episode, Fujita shifts self-control away from a narrow willpower story. He treats willpower as effortful inhibition, but self-control as a broader, learnable set of strategies involving attention, distance, meaning, social modeling, intrinsic interest, and goal-pattern choice. His role is to keep classic findings useful while making their boundaries visible: the marshmallow test depends on trust and context, ego depletion remains contested, and no single tactic works for every person or situation.

## Key Characteristics
- Distinguishes willpower from broader self-control strategy.
- Uses [[MarshmallowTestContext]] to qualify delayed-gratification evidence.
- Frames [[PsychologicalDistanceSelfControl]] and meaningful "whys" as core tools.
- Treats [[SelfControlStrategyToolkit]] as context-sensitive rather than one-size-fits-all.
- Keeps [[AbstinenceModerationSelfControl]] and [[GoalDisengagementAndReengagement]] as fit and tradeoff questions.

## Evidence
- Willpower distinction - [[master-self-control-overcome-procrastination-dr-kentaro-fujita-scim8156556751]] has Fujita define willpower as effortful inhibition while describing self-control as including other behavioral and psychological strategies.
- Contextual evidence reading - [[master-self-control-overcome-procrastination-dr-kentaro-fujita-scim8156556751]] uses trust, socioeconomic stability, and strategy training to qualify marshmallow-test interpretation.
- Strategy fit - [[master-self-control-overcome-procrastination-dr-kentaro-fujita-scim8156556751]] says different people and contexts need different tools, and failed attempts can show poor tool fit rather than a defective person.
- Goal tradeoffs - [[master-self-control-overcome-procrastination-dr-kentaro-fujita-scim8156556751]] discusses abstinence, moderation, single-goal intensity, multiple goals, intrinsic motivation, and disengagement from impossible goals.
