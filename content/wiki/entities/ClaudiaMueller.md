# Claudia Mueller

## Overview
Claudia Mueller is the psychologist named with [[CarolDweck]] for research comparing intelligence-focused and effort-focused praise in children.

## Current Profile
In [[how-to-enhance-performance-learning-by-applying-a-growth-mindset-scim1082295503]], Mueller's coauthored praise research anchors [[ProcessFocusedPerformanceFeedback]]. The episode reports that labeling children smart shifted later choices toward protecting that identity, whereas feedback about effort and persistence supported harder task selection and later performance.

Her role in the bounded source is specific: the work illustrates how a small change in feedback language can alter the meaning of difficulty. It does not show that generic effort praise, unsupported persistence, or one script can overcome poor strategy or missing opportunity.

## Key Characteristics
- Coauthored the intelligence-versus-effort praise research central to the episode.
- Examines how feedback affects challenge choice, motivation, performance, and self-presentation.
- Supports process language that describes controllable actions rather than fixed identity.
- Provides experimental grounding for one practical application of [[GrowthMindset]].

## Evidence
- Praise comparison - [[how-to-enhance-performance-learning-by-applying-a-growth-mindset-scim1082295503]] names Mueller with Dweck and contrasts intelligence praise, effort praise, and non-specific feedback.
- Challenge selection - [[how-to-enhance-performance-learning-by-applying-a-growth-mindset-scim1082295503]] reports more image-protecting easy-task choices after intelligence praise and more learning-oriented hard-task choices after effort praise.
- Performance and reporting - [[how-to-enhance-performance-learning-by-applying-a-growth-mindset-scim1082295503]] reports stronger later performance and more faithful score reporting in the effort-praise condition.

## Qualifications
The supplied episode summary does not provide complete study methods, samples, effect sizes, or replication context. The evidence supports a feedback-design boundary, not praise of effort regardless of strategy, accuracy, outcome, safety, or environmental constraint.
