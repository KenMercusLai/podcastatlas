# Barry Goldwater

## Overview

Barry Goldwater was the 1964 Republican presidential nominee whose campaign supplied the national platform for [[RonaldReagan|Ronald Reagan]]'s breakthrough televised speech.

## Current Profile

[[311-reagan-the-road-to-the-white-house-part-2-glt9813084891]] presents Goldwater as a conservative insurgent who attracted activists and wealthy California supporters but frightened many moderate Republicans and suffered a landslide defeat. His importance in this source lies less in victory than in succession: Reagan's fundraising address for him distilled a decade of speaking practice and convinced some backers that Reagan could carry the same broad cause with greater warmth and electoral reach.

## Key Characteristics

- A libertarian-leaning conservative presented as unusually right-wing by the Republican standards of 1964.
- The nominee around whom an emerging conservative donor and activist network organized.
- A losing candidate whose campaign nevertheless created a national opening for Reagan.
- An ideological standard-bearer whose direct style was less electorally adaptable than Reagan's anecdotal performance.
