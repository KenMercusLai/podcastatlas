# James Angleton

## Overview

James Angleton appears in [[125-the-cia-glt6232412389]] as the [[CIA]]'s counterintelligence chief from 1954 to 1974 and as a figure whose relationship with [[KimPhilby]] joined genuine penetration risk to widening institutional paranoia.

## Current Profile

The episode presents Angleton's suspicion as double-edged. Philby's betrayal gave serious grounds for vigilance, yet Angleton's later belief that numerous Western leaders and institutions were penetrated turned counterintelligence into damaging mole hunting. The source also contrasts his tenure with the later betrayal by [[AldrichAmes]], while not establishing that Angleton's methods alone prevented earlier penetration.

## Key Characteristics

- Led CIA counterintelligence for two decades during the Cold War.
- Remained close to Philby before the latter's 1963 defection.
- Treated deception and penetration as pervasive institutional risks.
- Illustrates how justified vigilance can expand into self-damaging suspicion.

## Evidence

- Philby relationship: [[125-the-cia-glt6232412389]] says Angleton remained close to Philby even after the British liaison had received sensitive covert-operation briefings.
- Counterintelligence leadership: [[125-the-cia-glt6232412389]] identifies his 1954-1974 tenure and describes allegations of communist penetration involving Western public figures.
- Later comparison: [[125-the-cia-glt6232412389]] contrasts the episode's claim of no Soviet mole during his tenure with [[AldrichAmes]]'s later betrayal.

## Qualifications

The episode's claim that Angleton prevented Soviet penetration while in office is not independently established here and should not be read as proof that his suspicions or methods were proportionate. The page does not adjudicate each alleged mole, the operational damage from his searches, or the full chronology of CIA penetration cases.

- Created a counterintelligence profile that keeps both real penetration danger and the cost of institutional paranoia visible.
