# Álvaro Vizcaíno

## Overview
Álvaro Vizcaíno is presented in the current source as a surfer, entrepreneur, survival-story author, psychology and coaching student, speaker, and the subject of the film *Solo*. His [[TomorrowToday]] interview centers on surviving a 2014 cliff fall and multiple days near Cofete in Fuerteventura.

## Current Profile
Vizcaíno describes leaving a comfortable but unfulfilling commercial career in Madrid to build a tourism-technology business in Fuerteventura and live near the ocean. After an impulsive route choice ended in a roughly 50-metre fall, severe pelvic and hand injuries, isolation, exposure, and an eventual swim toward possible rescue, he reorganized the experience around acceptance, gratitude, fear as information, and the smallest available next action.

His later public work turns the ordeal into a practical philosophy: do not wait for fear to disappear, distinguish accepting reality from abandoning action, and ground self-trust in present conduct rather than a guaranteed outcome. The profile remains autobiographical. The source does not independently verify the accident's mechanics, establish the causal role of spiritual experiences or visualization, or make his entrepreneurial advice universally safe.

## Key Characteristics
- Moved from Madrid to Fuerteventura to build a tourism-technology business and surf regularly.
- Reports surviving a cliff fall, severe injury, isolation, exposure, dehydration, and rescue at sea in 2014.
- Treats fear as information that can advise action without controlling the final decision.
- Uses acceptance and gratitude to describe release from futile resistance rather than passive defeat.
- Reduces overwhelming action to the next metre, wave, or manageable decision.
- Later studied psychology, transpersonal psychology, and coaching and began speaking publicly.
- Values plural interpretations of his survival account, including physiology, chance, attitude, spirituality, and religion.
