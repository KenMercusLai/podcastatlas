# Nujiang Gorge / 怒江峡谷

## Overview
Nujiang Gorge / 怒江峡谷 is the main landscape and travel corridor in [[245-nujiang-chunri-mijing-chutan-8221838951-646453]], where the hosts travel north from Baoshan toward Shiyueliang and [[Bingzhongluo|Bingzhongluo]].

## Current Profile
The episode frames Nujiang Gorge as an overwhelming concentration of geography, infrastructure, ecology, ethnicity, religion, and food. It is scenic, but the source resists treating it as scenery alone: the road is demanding, the river is loud and forceful, villages and religious sites sit close to the highway, and older transport practices remain visible through [[TeaHorseRoad|Tea Horse Road]] memory, documentary footage, and zip-line stories.

## Key Characteristics
- Combines dramatic canyon landscape with difficult road travel.
- Functions as a live case of [[ThreeParallelRiversLandscape|Three Parallel Rivers]] scale and biodiversity.
- Contains multi-ethnic and multi-religious village life rather than a single cultural identity.
- Makes older transport systems visible beside new roads, bridges, and relocation housing.
- Requires ethical attention because living communities can be too easily turned into spectacles.

## Evidence
- Road and river corridor: [[245-nujiang-chunri-mijing-chutan-8221838951-646453]] describes National Highway 219 running along the gorge, with long car time, tight curves, tunnels, villages, and fast Nujiang water.
- Natural force: [[245-nujiang-chunri-mijing-chutan-8221838951-646453]] notes changing water color, loud current, wood-catching riverbank structures, and the claim that the Nujiang mainstream remains without large hydropower dams.
- Multi-layered culture: [[245-nujiang-chunri-mijing-chutan-8221838951-646453]] discusses Lisu, Nu, Dulong, Tibetan, and Han communities, Arong-guniang legends, Shiyueliang mythology, Puhua Temple, Chongding Church, and quieter Protestant churches.
- Mobility transition: [[245-nujiang-chunri-mijing-chutan-8221838951-646453]] contrasts current roads and bridges with the older Tea Horse Road, mule transport in [[DelamuDocumentary|Delamu]], and zip lines that are shifting from necessity toward memory or tourism.

## Qualifications
The source is a travel account rather than a full regional history. Several ethnic and religious explanations are explicitly marked by the hosts as incomplete or uncertain.
