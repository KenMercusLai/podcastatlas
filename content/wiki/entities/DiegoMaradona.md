# Diego Maradona

## Overview

Diego Maradona was an Argentine footballer represented in the source as a player of exceptional skill whose 1986 World Cup performance became inseparable from trickster mythology, national identity, and the Falklands War's recent memory.

## Current Profile

In [[254-the-world-cup-the-falklands-despots-and-corruption-part-3-glt5891154284]], Maradona's two goals against England compress contrary meanings into one match: the handball becomes injustice or admired cunning depending on viewpoint, while the solo goal displays extraordinary ability. [[GaryLineker]] also recalls his charisma, addiction, and near-religious reception among Boca Juniors supporters, making Maradona a sporting icon whose public meaning exceeded match performance.

## Key Characteristics

- Exceptional footballer at the center of Argentina's 1986 World Cup campaign.
- Author of both the disputed handball goal and celebrated solo goal against England.
- National symbol whose cunning against England acquired political meaning in Argentina.
- Charismatic celebrity whose talent coexisted with addiction and personal trouble.
- Folk-hero figure treated with intense devotion beyond ordinary sporting admiration.
