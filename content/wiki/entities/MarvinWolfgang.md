# Marvin Wolfgang

## Overview

Marvin Wolfgang was a criminologist whose Philadelphia birth-cohort study supplies the empirical origin for the concentration pattern that [[wolfgang-s-law-part-one|Wolfgang's Law - Part One]] calls “Wolfgang's Law.”

## Current Profile

The episode reports that Wolfgang studied 9,945 boys born in Philadelphia in 1945 who remained in the city through age 18. Within that cohort, 627 boys—about 6 percent—accounted for more than half of recorded offenses, all but one homicide, and roughly three-quarters of rapes and robberies. His role in the episode is to shift the unit of analysis from a supposedly dangerous population to a much smaller recurrent-offending group.

## Key Characteristics

- Criminologist associated with a large Philadelphia male birth-cohort study.
- Documented a highly unequal distribution of recorded offending within the cohort.
- Provides the historical foundation for the episode's broader [[ViolenceConcentration]] argument.
- Is represented through one episode summary rather than a complete account of his scholarship.

## Evidence

### Cohort and concentration finding

- [[wolfgang-s-law-part-one]] reports the cohort size, the 627-member high-offending group, and its disproportionate share of serious recorded offenses.
