# 冯燕飞 / Feng Yanfei (Vascular Surgery)

## Overview
冯燕飞 / Feng Yanfei is a vascular-surgery doctor in [[ZheBingShuoLaiHuaChang|这病说来话长]] episodes on symptom routing, vascular imaging and treatment, prolonged immobility, thrombosis prevention, varicose veins, compression stockings, health-product claims, and patient trust.

## Current Profile
In this source, Feng's role is to make vascular-risk categories concrete for ordinary travelers. She names higher-risk contexts such as pregnancy, tumors, thrombophilia, surgery, older age, hormonal medication, and prior risk factors, then returns the advice to daily prevention: drink a reasonable amount of water, interrupt sitting, move briefly when possible, and use ankle-pump movement when standing up is not practical.

Her triage contribution is comparative and practical. She helps distinguish sudden unilateral swelling and pain from bilateral dependent swelling that may improve after rest, while still keeping the final judgment inside clinical assessment when thrombosis is plausible.

VOL.136 extends this profile from travel into routine sitting and standing. Feng explains pregnancy-related venous pressure, the limited reversibility of established valve or vessel-wall damage, compression classes and wear context, and ankle-pump movement. Her boundary is consistent across both sources: compression can manage pressure or progression in selected people, but it does not restore a damaged vein, and suspected acute thrombosis is not an ordinary exercise or self-compression situation.

VOL.128 adds her younger-doctor and continuity perspective. She describes older vascular patients initially bypassing her for a male doctor of similar age, then becoming more accepting through senior introduction, independent clinic work, patient explanation, and visible responsibility. A repeatedly admitted older woman with an already amputated leg trusted Feng to manage the other leg despite a difficult nonhealing wound and a strong preference for limb preservation.

VOL.111 broadens her profile from venous and patient-trust topics to the specialty's full routing logic. Feng maps carotid, subclavian, aortic, visceral, lower-limb arterial, venous, renal-vein, and lymphatic presentations across other departments; distinguishes ultrasound, contrast CT, angiography, conservative care, open surgery, and endovascular treatment; and rejects fish oil, topical creams, wine, vinegar, onions, or wood ear as substitutes for vascular-risk control and indicated care.

## Key Characteristics
- Names venous-thrombosis risks and explains ordinary [[TravelThrombosisPrevention|travel prevention]] through hydration, movement breaks, and ankle-pump exercise.
- Distinguishes unilateral concerning swelling from more symmetric dependent swelling while keeping suspected acute thrombosis outside casual exercise or compression advice.
- Explains varicose-vein progression, compression stockings, ankle pumps, and position changes as management tools rather than cures for sedentary or standing work.
- Shows how explanation, responsibility, repeated care, and respect for a patient's limb-preservation preference can build trust without erasing disease limits.
- Explains vascular surgery as a head-to-foot, cross-specialty routing problem rather than a single-organ service.
- Separates imaging and treatment options by mechanism, anatomy, severity, and indication.
- Draws a firm boundary between vascular medicine and products marketed to “soften” or “clean” vessels.
