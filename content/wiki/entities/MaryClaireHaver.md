# Mary Claire Haver

## Overview
Mary Claire Haver is the OBGYN and menopause-health guest in a [[HubermanLab]] episode on perimenopause, menopause, hormone therapy, nutrition, exercise, sexual health, and women's-health research gaps.

## Current Profile
In the current wiki evidence, Haver presents menopause as the end of ovarian function and a multi-system endocrine transition rather than only the calendar point twelve months after a final menstrual period. Her clinical stance combines symptom recognition and differential diagnosis with patient agency: mental-health, cognitive, vasomotor, bleeding, musculoskeletal, metabolic, sexual, and urinary changes deserve evaluation, but they should not automatically be attributed to menopause.

Haver is strongly favorable toward individualized hormone therapy when appropriate, especially nearer the menopause transition, while distinguishing systemic therapy from local vaginal estrogen and naming cancer, unexplained bleeding, active clot treatment, liver disease, age, and time-since-menopause considerations. She pairs medical treatment with resistance training, protein, fiber-rich eating, sleep, and body-composition awareness rather than presenting hormones as a complete health strategy.

## Key Characteristics
- Reframes menopause as a whole-body loss of ovarian signaling rather than a single calendar diagnosis.
- Treats symptom history and differential diagnosis as more useful than one perimenopause blood test.
- Supports individualized, timing-sensitive hormone-therapy discussions with explicit contraindications and route choices.
- Distinguishes systemic hormone therapy from local vaginal estrogen for genitourinary symptoms.
- Prioritizes resistance training, protein, fiber-rich nutrition, sleep, and metabolic health through midlife.
- Criticizes gaps in menopause research, clinician education, and women's access to informed care.

## Evidence
- Transition model - [[how-to-navigate-menopause-perimenopause-for-maximum-health-vitality-dr-mary-claire-haver-scim1557774220]] has Haver describe declining estradiol, progesterone, and testosterone signaling across brain, bone, cardiovascular, metabolic, skin, sleep, sexual, and urinary systems.
- Clinical interpretation - [[how-to-navigate-menopause-perimenopause-for-maximum-health-vitality-dr-mary-claire-haver-scim1557774220]] emphasizes symptoms and differential diagnosis because perimenopause hormone levels can be volatile and overlapping conditions can mimic the transition.
- Treatment stance - [[how-to-navigate-menopause-perimenopause-for-maximum-health-vitality-dr-mary-claire-haver-scim1557774220]] distinguishes systemic and local therapy, discusses timing and route, and names conditions that can delay or preclude systemic treatment.
- Lifestyle frame - [[how-to-navigate-menopause-perimenopause-for-maximum-health-vitality-dr-mary-claire-haver-scim1557774220]] prioritizes resistance training, protein, fiber, plant diversity, sleep, and waist-to-hip or body-composition awareness.

## Qualifications
This profile is bounded to one podcast source and does not independently verify every epidemiologic number, mechanistic explanation, or treatment claim in the conversation. Haver's hormone, testosterone, supplement, GLP-1, weighted-vest, and acupuncture recommendations remain public education that requires individualized clinical interpretation.
