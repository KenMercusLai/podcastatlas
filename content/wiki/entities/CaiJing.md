# 蔡京 / Cai Jing

## Overview

蔡京 / Cai Jing enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-396-4-wanganshi-bianfa-zenme-daozhi-beisong-miewang-de-lslupw7c76poanj-zzmh59z1iofr|Hanji 396-4]] as a late [[NorthernSong|北宋]] official used to connect [[WangAnshiReforms|王安石变法]] to later fiscal extraction and dynastic collapse narratives.

## Current Profile

The source presents Cai Jing less as an isolated corrupt minister than as a successor to a new-law fiscal logic. In the episode's argument, he imitates the extraction side of Wang Anshi's reforms by using state power, palace supply institutions, and projects such as flower-and-rock transport to draw wealth from society in order to sustain court finance and imperial desire.

The page should remain source-scoped. This episode does not give a full Cai Jing biography, nor does it weigh alternative scholarly evaluations of his career; it uses him as a late-stage example of reform-era state commercial extraction turning into more visible burden and resentment.

## Key Characteristics

- Cai Jing is characterized as a famous "奸相" and new-party figure in the episode's moral-political frame.
- His role is defined by continuity with the extractive side of the Wang Anshi reform legacy rather than by independent policy detail.
- He is linked to fiscal support for court spending and imperial desire through institutions such as the Yingfeng and Zaozuo bureaus.
- Flower-and-rock transport becomes the episode's emblem for late Northern Song burden and extraction.
- The source uses Cai Jing to bridge policy history, popular literary memory around the Water Margin setting, and Northern Song collapse.

## Qualifications
