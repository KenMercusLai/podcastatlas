# Kick (streaming platform)

## Overview
Kick is a less-moderated streaming platform presented by the source as attracting controversial creators through favorable revenue terms and a close corporate relationship with the gambling network [[StakeCasino|Stake]].

## Current Profile
In the episode's manosphere-economy account, Kick illustrates how a smaller platform can compete for creators without matching the largest services' audience. The reported offer of 95% of eligible revenue gives streamers a reason to exchange broad but less committed reach for a more loyal audience and better direct economics. Prominent streams featuring Stake games connect that creator proposition to gambling-derived commercial activity.

## Key Characteristics
- Competes partly through creator revenue shares rather than audience scale alone.
- Applies less moderation than major mainstream video platforms in the source's account.
- Hosts controversial personalities whose notoriety can increase after mainstream removal.
- Shares a parent company with [[StakeCasino|Stake]], according to the episode.
- Benefits from clipping networks that redistribute stream excerpts onto larger services.

## Evidence
- Creator economics: [[social-insecurity-numbers-the-money-behind-the-manosphere-7eb4966be1685ee4f19adde69e240aaa]] reports that many Kick streamers receive 95% of eligible revenue streams.
- Gambling relationship: [[social-insecurity-numbers-the-money-behind-the-manosphere-7eb4966be1685ee4f19adde69e240aaa]] says Kick and Stake share a parent company and that prominent Kick streamers play Stake games during broadcasts.
- Deplatforming role: [[social-insecurity-numbers-the-money-behind-the-manosphere-7eb4966be1685ee4f19adde69e240aaa]] uses Kick to explain why removal from a major platform need not end a controversial creator's business.

## Qualifications
The episode does not provide Kick's financial statements, creator contracts, moderation data, audience composition, or a comparison covering all revenue categories. The 95% figure applies to eligible revenue streams rather than necessarily to every source of creator income. Hosting controversial creators does not establish that every Kick streamer belongs to the manosphere or promotes gambling.
