# 子路 / Zilu / Zhong You

## Overview
子路 / Zilu / Zhong You enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-391-4-zhongguo-meiyou-guizu-jingshen-bie-kanbuqi-womende-wenhua-lvi4atpkus7-diuazkgask92tsds]] as [[Confucius|孔子]]'s disciple whose death scene is used to explain aristocratic or gentlemanly dignity.

## Current Profile
The source does not build a full biography of Zilu. It uses the story of Zilu fixing his cap after his cap string is cut in battle to show that ritual form can express dignity, self-command, and identity even when survival is no longer possible.

The host contrasts this reading with a modern anti-ritual critique that treats the act as evidence of harmful Confucian formalism. The page therefore records a source-scoped interpretive role: Zilu is a death-facing dignity example, not a universal proof that ritual should outrank life in every situation.

## Key Characteristics
- Confucian disciple: the source identifies Zilu through his relationship to [[Confucius|孔子]].
- Loyalty under danger: the source says he joined the conflict out of loyalty even though he could have avoided it.
- Ritual self-command: his final act is fixing his cap before death.
- Interpretive tension: the same act can be read as noble composure or as destructive attachment to ritual.

## Qualifications
This page is source-scoped and does not attempt a complete Zilu biography. The episode's admiration for his composure should also be kept distinct from [[RecklessCourageDecisionRisk|有勇无谋决策风险]], where another source uses Zilu to warn against courage that spends the actor too early.
