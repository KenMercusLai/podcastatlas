# Pliny the Younger

## Overview

Pliny the Younger is the Roman eyewitness whose two letters supply the central literary testimony for the [[EruptionOfVesuvius79|79 CE eruption]], his own escape from Misenum, and [[PlinyTheElder|Pliny the Elder]]'s fatal rescue attempt.

## Current Profile

The source follows Pliny as a young observer who remained at Misenum when his uncle sailed. After earthquakes intensified, he evacuated with his mother, saw the sea withdraw and the distant cloud crowned with fire, and survived darkness, falling ash, fear of suffocation, and widespread belief that the world was ending. His comparison of the eruption cloud to a pine tree remains a key descriptive image, while his portrait of his uncle is treated as both testimony and crafted commemoration.

## Key Characteristics

- Young eyewitness positioned across the bay from Vesuvius at Misenum.
- Letter writer whose account supplies eruption shape, darkness, panic, retreating sea, and evacuation details.
- Son evacuating alongside his mother amid earthquakes and ash.
- Nephew who memorialized Pliny the Elder as calm, brave, and public-minded.
- Essential but interested literary source whose transmitted eruption date is disputed.

## Evidence

- Cloud description: [[355-roman-apocalypse-pompeii-79-ad-glt6664993493]] attributes the pine-tree comparison to Pliny's later account.
- Misenum experience: [[355-roman-apocalypse-pompeii-79-ad-glt6664993493]] describes tremors, evacuation, sea withdrawal, fire, darkness, ash, and fear.
- Family testimony: [[355-roman-apocalypse-pompeii-79-ad-glt6664993493]] uses his account for his uncle's route, conduct, and death.
- Dating problem: [[355-roman-apocalypse-pompeii-79-ad-glt6664993493]] contrasts the transmitted 24 August date with autumnal archaeological evidence.

## Qualifications

Pliny wrote retrospectively and shaped his uncle's image for readers. Textual transmission complicates the eruption date, and his view from Misenum cannot directly establish every event inside Pompeii, Herculaneum, or Stabiae.
