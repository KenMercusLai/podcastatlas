## Overview

The 1857年印度大起义 / Indian Rebellion of 1857 was a heterogeneous uprising that began among Indian soldiers of the [[EastIndiaCompany|East India Company]], expanded through wider grievances, and made [[BahadurShahII|Bahadur Shah II]] its nominal sovereign after rebels entered Delhi.

## Current Profile

The episode separates trigger from deeper causes. Rumors that new rifle cartridges used cow and pig fat violated Hindu and Muslim prohibitions, but Company extraction and the shift from indirect accommodation of local elites toward more direct rule had already disrupted political and social arrangements. Rebels in Delhi used the last Mughal emperor's prestige to claim wider legitimacy.

Symbolic unity did not produce operational unity. Factions resisted central command, looting and killings occurred, and the emperor and his sons could not control forces even within Delhi. Britain recovered, mobilized support from other Indian powers, retook the city, killed or prosecuted Mughal princes, tried the emperor, and exiled him. The uprising then became the immediate transition point for the end of Company rule in 1858.

## Key Characteristics

- Military uprising whose immediate cartridge grievance interacted with deeper political, economic, and social disruption.
- Rebellion against a company-state fought by soldiers trained and employed within that state.
- Heterogeneous coalition lacking reliable centralized command.
- Attempt to convert residual Mughal prestige into all-India legitimacy.
- Conflict suppressed through British reinforcement and support from some Indian polities.
- Constitutional hinge between [[EastIndiaCompany|Company]] rule and direct Crown rule.

## Qualifications

Names such as rebellion, mutiny, and war of independence emphasize different participants and interpretations; this page uses the source's broad “uprising” frame without asserting a single motive or unified national program. Regional campaigns, civilian participation, atrocities, Indian allies of Britain, casualty totals, and the emperor's responsibility require fuller evidence.

## What Changed

- Created a bounded event profile separating immediate trigger, structural grievances, symbolic leadership, fragmented command, and constitutional aftermath.

## Relationships

- [[BahadurShahII]] - nominal sovereign whose title supplied legitimacy without effective command.
- [[EastIndiaCompany]] - employer of the initiating soldiers and governing order displaced after suppression.
- [[MughalEmpire]] - residual dynasty whose formal end followed the uprising.
- [[Delhi]] - principal symbolic center in this source's account.
- [[BritishEmpire]] - imperial network that shifted troops, recruited allies, and absorbed Company rule.
- [[SecondOpiumWar]] - simultaneous Asian conflict connected through British military allocation.
