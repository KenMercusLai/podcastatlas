## Overview

河套地区（西汉） / Hetao Region (Western Han) is the Yellow River bend and adjoining 河南地 treated by Hanji 280 part 1 as pasture, horse country, river-protected frontier, salt-producing field, and the territorial objective of [[HetaoCampaignWesternHan|西汉河南地之战]]. The semantic suffix distinguishes this place from [[HeTao|He Tao]], the modern engineer.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-280-heshuo-dajie-weiqing-ruhe-yizhan-fenghou-1-lu5pruhgiv4c2jetewxp65lmkwqc|Hanji 280 part 1]] explains the region's military value through ecology and position. Water and grass support livestock and horses, the Yellow River can serve as barrier and transport constraint, and salt adds a productive resource. Qin reportedly took and fortified the field under Meng Tian, lost it during dynastic collapse, and Han later recovered it under Wei Qing.

The source's durable point is that strategic geography needs an operating system. Recovery is followed by [[ShuofangCommanderyWesternHan|朔方郡]], walls, garrisons, settlement, farming, salt development, and placement of captives and submitters. Those measures increase control while transferring large costs to the treasury, transport system, soldiers, laborers, and moved households.

## Key Characteristics

- Yellow River bend frontier also described in the source as 河南地.
- Water-and-pasture base suited to livestock, horses, and mobile military capacity.
- Barrier and staging area between agrarian state power and Xiongnu fields.
- Salt and cultivable land make control economically useful as well as militarily valuable.
- Territory whose durable incorporation depends on fortification, administration, population movement, production, and garrisoning.

## Qualifications

This page represents the episode's Western Han strategic-geography frame, not a timeless or precisely bounded regional definition. The equivalence among 河套, 河南地, and every modern geographic subdivision is not assumed. Claims about climate, horse quality, Qin forts, salt output, migration, mixed population, and irreversible incorporation remain source-scoped. The source emphasizes state strategy; it does not independently recover the perspectives or costs borne by local and displaced populations.
