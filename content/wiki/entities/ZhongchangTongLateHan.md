## Overview

仲长统 / Zhongchang Tong is a late Eastern Han political critic whose current profile joins blunt personal diagnosis and timely exit with `Changyan` critiques of hollow office, captured authority, hereditary succession, and elite extraction.

## Current Profile

Zhongchang Tong's profile joins personal risk judgment to institutional criticism. He is identified as a Shanyang Gaoping writer, style name Gongli, born in 180 CE, and associated with the substantial late-Han `Changyan` corpus. His durable institutional diagnosis is that the Three Excellencies retain rank and blame after practical authority moves into Shangshu and protected inner-court channels, allowing outer relatives, favorites, and eunuchs to convert access into patronage, office sale, and abusive appointments. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-780-zui-duo-zai-duo-nan-de-shi-dai-donghan-lvgcumrfjblrofer532ees3tdoa|Hanji 780]] supplies this identity and power-responsibility frame.

The same critical independence appears in his encounter with [[GaoGanLateHan|高干]]: Zhongchang Tong says that ambition exceeds judgment and that appreciating worthy people is not the same as recognizing them, then leaves Bingzhou when the criticism is rejected. [[XunYu|荀彧]] later recommends him to [[CaoCao|曹操]]. His succession critique extends the institutional diagnosis through [[HereditarySuccessionCapabilityDecay|世袭继承能力衰减]]: birth transfers position without ensuring fitness, and insulated successors may turn desire into forced labor and taxation. These biographical and succession layers come from [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1074-quan-qing-chaoye-de-simayi-weihe-meici-jian-ta-dou-guibai-lpiw4ebdo2s5cucfcpmpkrhfnubi|Hanji 1074]].

## Key Characteristics

- Late Eastern Han political critic associated with the substantial `Changyan` corpus.
- Gives blunt personal advice but leaves when the recipient proves unable to accept correction.
- Diagnoses the Three Excellencies as high-status offices with limited real authority.
- Treats Shangshu channels and protected inner-court access as practical power centers.
- Connects patronage and corrupt appointments to local and frontier disorder.
- Connects hereditary succession and elite insulation to extraction and political decline.

- Power-responsibility diagnosis: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-780-zui-duo-zai-duo-nan-de-shi-dai-donghan-lvgcumrfjblrofer532ees3tdoa|Hanji 780]] says formal senior offices became hollow while Shangshu and inner-court channels accumulated practical power.
- Appointment consequences: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-780-zui-duo-zai-duo-nan-de-shi-dai-donghan-lvgcumrfjblrofer532ees3tdoa|Hanji 780]] links patronage and office sale to exploitative local and frontier administration.
- Gao Gan encounter and exit: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1074-quan-qing-chaoye-de-simayi-weihe-meici-jian-ta-dou-guibai-lpiw4ebdo2s5cucfcpmpkrhfnubi|Hanji 1074]] says Zhongchang Tong criticizes Gao Gan's judgment and talent recognition, then leaves when the advice is unwelcome.
- Cao-side recommendation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1074-quan-qing-chaoye-de-simayi-weihe-meici-jian-ta-dou-guibai-lpiw4ebdo2s5cucfcpmpkrhfnubi|Hanji 1074]] says Xun Yu later recommends Zhongchang Tong to Cao Cao for a Shangshu Lang post.
- Succession critique: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1074-quan-qing-chaoye-de-simayi-weihe-meici-jian-ta-dou-guibai-lpiw4ebdo2s5cucfcpmpkrhfnubi|Hanji 1074]] presents `Changyan` as linking hereditary capability mismatch and elite insulation to forced labor, taxation, and regime decline.

## Qualifications

Both sources are podcast treatments rather than a direct critical edition of `Changyan`. Hanji 780 compresses institutional history, while Hanji 1074 compresses Zhongchang Tong's encounter with Gao Gan, recommendation, office, and political-cycle theory. Exact dialogue, causal links to Gao Gan's death, the surviving extent of `Changyan`, and the “富不过三代” bridge remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Added Zhongchang Tong's criticism of Gao Gan and timely departure from Bingzhou.
- Added Xun Yu's recommendation and the source's Shangshu Lang appointment claim.
- Extended the `Changyan` profile from power-responsibility mismatch to hereditary succession, elite insulation, and extraction.
