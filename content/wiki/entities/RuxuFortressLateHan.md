## Overview

濡须坞 / Ruxu Fortress is the defensive position that [[LyuMeng|吕蒙]] urges [[SunQuan|孙权]] to build at the Ruxu waterway as protection against a renewed [[CaoCao|曹操]] offensive. Hanji 1133 then places a Cao-Sun confrontation in that prepared Ruxu setting.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1128-meiyou-heikeji-guren-ruhe-diaocha-nimingxin-lqdm57jzw9wqo_umlasz2j3vlzqh|Hanji 1128]] says other commanders consider a fort unnecessary because troops can land for battle and return to ships afterward. Lyu Meng rejects the assumption that reboarding will always remain possible: battles can be lost, pursuit can be fast, and soldiers may need immediate cover before reaching the fleet.

Sun Quan accepts the argument and funds construction. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1133-sunquan-rensheng-zui-xiaosa-de-zhanyi-ruxukou-zhi-zhan-lrkz2p2fvh2fpw5owjdj7xg_dc8g|Hanji 1133]] supplies the anticipated confrontation: Cao Cao attacks in the Ruxu area, gains an initial west-bank success, but faces a sustained river defense until rising-water conditions and Jiangdong's naval advantage make withdrawal more attractive. The new source validates the strategic relevance of preparing the Ruxu corridor without proving that the fortification alone caused the outcome.

Across the two sources, Ruxu is not merely a fixed wall. It is operational fallback infrastructure connecting land combat, river mobility, retreat timing, force preservation, and the ability to keep resisting long enough for campaign conditions to change.

Hanji 1157 adds a second, distinct use of the same defense system in the [[RuxukouCampaign217LateHan|217 CE Ruxu campaign]]. The confrontation again ends without Cao-side conquest; afterward Sun Quan assigns [[ZhouTaiEasternWu|周泰]] to hold Ruxu and publicly supports his command over officers who initially resist the appointment. The fortification is therefore both an operational fallback and a continuing command responsibility across campaigns.

## Key Characteristics

- Riverine defensive work tied to both land and naval operations.
- Built in anticipation of renewed Cao-side pressure.
- Designed around the possibility of defeat rather than assumed victory.
- Provides a temporary refuge when pursuit prevents orderly re-embarkation.
- Material expression of Lyu Meng's risk-sensitive advice and Sun Quan's acceptance.
- Located within a campaign environment where river conditions and naval competence can change the attacker's expected return.
- Remains a continuing command post across distinct 213 and 217 CE confrontations.

## Qualifications

The sources do not establish the fortification's exact date, engineering form, dimensions, labor system, cost, location boundaries, or specific battlefield contribution. Hanji 1133 and Hanji 1157 describe defense around Ruxu but do not isolate the fort as the cause of either withdrawal. “Ruxu Fortress” is a functional English label for the source's 濡须坞, not a claim that it matched a standardized fortress type.

## What Changed

- Connected the construction argument to Hanji 1133's later Ruxu campaign while preserving uncertainty about the fort's direct operational effect.
- Added river conditions and naval competence as campaign-level context around the prepared fallback.
- Added the distinct 217 CE campaign and Zhou Tai's postwar command responsibility.
