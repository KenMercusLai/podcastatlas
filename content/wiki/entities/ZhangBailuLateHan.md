## Overview

张白禄 / Zhang Bailu is presented as an early Emperor An-era coastal rebel leader whose pirate coalition moves from an initial surrender-and-release cycle into renewed rebellion, testing whether Eastern Han punishment, amnesty, and military pursuit can produce credible pacification.

## Current Profile

Hanji 783 now supplies the earliest current layer under the spelling Zhang Bolu / 张伯禄. In 109 CE, Zhang attacks nine coastal commanderies and kills local officials. The court mobilizes troops under Pang Xiong; Zhang surrenders and is released, but then escapes and gathers people again. The episode treats the decision not to execute or securely settle him as a failed pacification choice because his later renewed attack kills a commandery governor and a county magistrate.

Hanji 785 then develops Zhang Bailu as a coastal rebel associated with pirate activity in the Xuzhou-Qingzhou maritime zone. His followers grow to several thousand and kill high-ranking local officials, including an official identified as a two-thousand-dan officer and later county-level leadership. This makes the rebellion more than ordinary banditry: it creates a local governance crisis during [[EmpressDowagerDengLateHan|Empress Dowager Deng]]'s regency.

The same source makes Zhang Bailu a test case for amnesty credibility. [[FaXiongLateHan|Fa Xiong]] predicts that pursuit after an imperial amnesty will frighten the rebels away from surrender and into distant islands. When the rebels return captives, the episode suggests they were considering re-entry into settled space. Donglai troops' failure to lower military pressure sends them fleeing to Liaodong islands.

Hanji 786 continues the sequence under the spelling Zhang Bolu/张伯路, recording his later defeat by Li Jiu and his death. The current page treats the two spellings as one rebellion sequence unless later source evidence requires separation.

## Key Characteristics

- Coastal rebel leader associated with pirate forces around coastal commanderies, Xuzhou, Qingzhou, Donglai, and Liaodong island routes.
- His movement reaches several thousand people and kills senior local officials, making it politically significant.
- His first current cycle includes surrender, release, escape, and renewed violence before the later amnesty problem.
- Functions as a surrender-credibility case: amnesty opens a possible exit, but visible military pursuit closes it.
- The rebellion is mobile and terrain-sensitive, shifting from settled coastal areas toward island refuges.
- The page preserves the Zhang Bailu/Zhang Bolu naming variation across adjacent source notes.

## Qualifications

The current evidence is limited to three adjacent Hanji source notes. It does not yet establish Zhang Bailu's background, social base, exact island geography, or whether the spelling variation reflects transcription, oral presentation, or a source distinction. Hanji 783's critique of releasing Zhang remains the host's interpretation of a pacification failure, not a complete legal record of why the first surrender was handled that way.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 783 as the earliest current layer, showing the initial attack, surrender, release, escape, and renewed attack before Hanji 785's amnesty-centered account.
- Preserved the naming qualification connecting Zhang Bailu in Hanji 785 with Zhang Bolu / Zhang Bolu-like forms in Hanji 783 and Hanji 786.
