## Overview

孙夫人 / Lady Sun is [[SunQuan|孙权]]'s sister and the wife of [[LiuBei|刘备]] in the source's post-Red-Cliffs political-marriage account; her personal name is not preserved there.

## Current Profile

Hanji 1113 presents Lady Sun inside a strategic bargain rather than a private romance. Sun Quan needs Liu Bei as a partner against [[CaoCao|曹操]] while fearing his growth in [[Jingzhou|荆州]], and the marriage creates a kinship tie across the coalition. The episode describes her as intelligent, forceful, and attended by more than one hundred armed women, with Liu Bei reportedly fearful when entering her quarters.

The current evidence supports her political position more securely than later naming traditions. The episode says the historical record calls her “孙夫人,” while “孙尚香” belongs to later literary reception and “枭姬” is an epithet. Its account of her being exchanged as an alliance resource foregrounds constrained agency but does not recover her own intent.

## Key Characteristics

- Sun-family woman placed at the center of a post-Red-Cliffs marriage alliance.
- Wife of Liu Bei in a relationship shaped by unequal strategic dependence and mutual suspicion.
- Portrayed by the source as intelligent, forceful, and surrounded by armed female attendants.
- Historically unnamed within the episode's cited tradition, despite later literary naming as “孙尚香.”
- Personal agency remains difficult to reconstruct because the source primarily records her political function and received image.

## Qualifications

This is a single popular-history source. Her age, attendant count, temperament, household conduct, Fa Zheng's advice, later fate, the exact origin of “孙尚香,” and use of “枭姬” require textual verification. Reading her as wholly passive would also exceed the evidence: the episode emphasizes political instrumentalization, but it does not provide her own testimony or enough material to settle her agency.

## What Changed

- Created a canonical page that separates the historically unnamed Lady Sun from later literary naming.
- Located the marriage inside the Sun-Liu alliance's simultaneous need and mistrust.
- Preserved the gendered political-instrument interpretation as source-scoped rather than recovered biography.
