# Ober Ost

## Overview

Ober Ost was the German military command and occupation system established under [[PaulVonHindenburg]] and [[ErichLudendorff]] across parts of the Baltic, Belarus, and eastern Poland during the First World War.

## Current Profile

The episode places Ober Ost downstream of [[BattleOfTannenberg1914|Tannenberg]]. Victory raised Hindenburg and Ludendorff's prestige, and by the end of 1914 they gained wide authority in the east. The resulting regime is described as harsh and repressive military rule, but the hosts reject a direct equation with Nazi occupation: institutional and geographic continuities may matter without collapsing different ideologies, aims, and levels of violence into one category.

## Key Characteristics

- Military administration created under the Hindenburg-Ludendorff command partnership.
- Exercised German authority across occupied eastern territories.
- Emerged from the prestige and institutional power generated by Tannenberg.
- Combined command, occupation, extraction, and population rule.
- Invites continuity questions with later German eastern rule while requiring strong historical qualification.

## Qualifications

This page rests on a short segment of one narrative episode and is not a full administrative, economic, social, or comparative occupation history. Territory, chronology, institutions, local experience, labor and resource policy, and continuity with later regimes require specialist sources. Rejecting equivalence with Nazism does not make the occupation benign.

## What Changed

- Established Ober Ost as the occupation-regime bridge between Tannenberg's command partnership and wider German power in the east.
