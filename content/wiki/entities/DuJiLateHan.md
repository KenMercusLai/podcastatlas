# 杜畿 / Du Ji (late Han)

## Overview

杜畿 / Du Ji is the [[XunYu|荀彧]]-recommended administrator who enters a contested [[HedongCommandery|河东郡]] alone, disperses hostile local capacity through calibrated concession, survives the resulting coalition through distributed defense and outside reinforcement, and then turns the commandery into a prosperous and defensible Cao-side base.

## Current Profile

Du Ji's Hedong role begins as an appointment and entry problem. [[CaoCao|曹操]] needs a reliable governor for a traffic hub exposed to [[GaoGanLateHan|高干]], [[ZhangShengLateHan|张盛]], and suspicious local action by [[WeiGuLateHan|卫固]] and [[FanXianLateHan|范先]]. Xun Yu recommends Du Ji, but outgoing administrator [[WangYiHedongLateHan|王邑]] resists the handoff. Du Ji then argues that a large punitive force could frighten undecided households into rebellion, enters alone, remains outwardly composed under intimidation, and grants visible authority to hostile elites while recruitment and leave rules disperse their concentrated manpower. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1070-jiaochu-shaxue-weimeng-quan-buxia-panjiang-caocao-lqvyfglj3qefhyxyebfvvda8om5o|Hanji 1070]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1071-danqiang-pima-chuang-hedong-zhe-wei-mingchen-you-duo-niu-lnfo6i2lx7nmc1mrojfkqic7zlfk|Hanji 1071]] ground this sequence.

When the coalition attacks, Du Ji gives up the commandery seat without surrendering the wider region. County defenses, a previously cultivated support network, and denial of grain prevent the attackers from converting pressure into control; outside forces associated with [[MaTeng|马腾]] then finish the military crisis. The result depends on both distributed local holding power and external coordination rather than either alone. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1072-duji-danqi-shou-hedong-jing-shi-baixing-yuan-wei-ta-fusi-ltiuwt7v-rjcjpjpovqf9ffl88hu|Hanji 1072]] supplies the outcome.

Du Ji's longer administration joins lenient dispute handling, farming and sericulture, schooling, fortification, weapons production, and troop training into one settlement system. The later Weinan campaign provides the payoff: Hedong reportedly remains stable during Ma Chao's uprising, supplies Cao Cao's army, and retains a large grain surplus after victory. Civil order, productive depth, storage, and transport thus become military capacity without requiring the administrator to fight at the front. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1072-duji-danqi-shou-hedong-jing-shi-baixing-yuan-wei-ta-fusi-ltiuwt7v-rjcjpjpovqf9ffl88hu|Hanji 1072]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1123-ta-bu-shang-zhanchang-ruhe-li-xia-xianhe-zhangong-losloy1puogq4o9azo2x_ombrbyz|Hanji 1123]] support this long-run profile, while the latter's childhood and later-office sketch remains less securely grounded than the Hedong sequence.

## Key Characteristics

- Selected by Xun Yu for a route-critical commandery with local-elite defection risk.
- Enters alone after judging that a punitive army could manufacture broader rebellion.
- Uses composure, apparent concession, office grants, recruitment design, and leave to buy time and disperse hostile capacity.
- Relinquishes the administrative center tactically while preserving county-level defense and support.
- Uses accumulated local trust and food denial to prevent rebels from consolidating.
- Depends on Cao-side coordination and western reinforcement to finish the military crisis.
- Combines lenient civil rule, agricultural growth, education, defense preparation, and later military supply into a durable regional base.

## Qualifications

The four source notes now cover appointment, entry, defense, settlement, and later wartime supply but remain short podcast syntheses rather than a complete institutional biography. Hanji 1071's motive-reading, office mechanics, alternate route, official and troop totals, and claim that personnel sorting worked exactly as intended require textual corroboration. Hanji 1072's “distributed warfare,” voluntary-choice, psychological-reading, and performance language is interpretive; the allocation of credit between Du Ji's internal holding action and Ma Teng-associated reinforcement also remains source-scoped. Hanji 1123's exact surplus, childhood and filial-reputation narrative, office chronology, shipwreck location, age, and Cao Pi's reaction require direct textual verification.

## What Changed

- Added the 211 CE payoff: Hedong's stability and grain supply turn long-run civil administration into campaign capacity.
- Distinguished rear-area military merit from personal battlefield service without treating logistics as the sole cause of victory.
- Added a qualified early-and-late biographical frame while keeping its precise details source-scoped.
