# Anne Heggli

## Overview
Anne Heggli is the observational hydrometeorologist in [[02026-heggli-keats-csank-podcast-v2]], where she explains how [[NevCAN]] measures water, weather, snow, soil, and energy across an elevation transect in Nevada's Great Basin.

## Current Profile
Heggli's role in the wiki is to make the labor and institutional continuity behind environmental evidence visible. Her account links field maintenance, sensor comparison, quality control, direct observation, and long-term stewardship: a large dataset becomes scientifically useful only when people keep instruments working, diagnose ambiguous failures, document corrections, and preserve continuity beyond normal grant cycles.

## Key Characteristics
- She describes herself as an observational scientist studying how rain and snow arrive, persist, and leave a landscape.
- She has worked since 2022 to quality-control roughly fifteen years of ten-minute NEVCAN observations.
- She treats cross-sensor reasoning and field knowledge as essential to distinguishing real events from spikes, drift, flat lines, obstruction, or equipment failure.
- She argues that model-ready data are also AI-ready data and that poor inputs allow errors to scale confidently.
- She frames diverse collaborators, durable processes, and stewardship succession as prerequisites for a 10,000-year record.

## Evidence
### Observational Practice
- [[02026-heggli-keats-csank-podcast-v2]] describes Heggli's work tracing precipitation, snow, soil moisture, and atmospheric conditions across the NEVCAN transect.

### Data Quality and Maintenance
- [[02026-heggli-keats-csank-podcast-v2]] says she reviews observations station by station, year by year, and sensor by sensor, using related measurements to diagnose anomalies and prevent bad data at the instrument where possible.

### Long-Term Stewardship
- [[02026-heggli-keats-csank-podcast-v2]] connects her account to the largely unpaid continuity work of Scotty Strachan and Greg McCurdy after formal funding ended and to her call for shared, inheritable monitoring practices.
