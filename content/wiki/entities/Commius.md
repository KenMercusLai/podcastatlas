# Commius

## Overview

Commius was an Atrebatic king whose career connected [[JuliusCaesar]]'s campaigns in Gaul and Britain, first as Caesar's envoy and later as his opponent.

## Current Profile

Caesar sent Commius ahead of the 55 BC expedition to negotiate with British rulers, but he was detained and released only after the Roman landing. In 54 BC he again acted as an intermediary, carrying the settlement through which [[Cassivellaunus]] submitted, [[Mandubracius]] was restored, and hostages and tribute were promised.

The alliance did not endure. Commius later joined the Gallic revolt and the attempt to relieve Vercingetorix at Alesia. After conflict and negotiation with Rome, he moved to Britain and ruled in the Hampshire region, making his life a case of client brokerage turning into resistance and cross-Channel relocation.

## Key Characteristics

- Atrebatic king operating across Gaul and Britain.
- Diplomatic intermediary used by Caesar before and during the British expeditions.
- Client whose loyalty depended on changing political conditions rather than fixed attachment to Rome.
- Later participant in Gallic resistance to Caesar.
- British ruler associated with early inscribed coinage.

- Cross-Channel rule: [[499-the-roman-conquest-of-britain-julius-caesars-invasion-part-1-glt9770560726]] places his later kingdom in southern Britain and connects his name to coinage.

## Qualifications

The episode compresses a complicated career preserved mainly through Roman evidence. His exact status, the terms of his break and accommodation with Rome, the chronology of his move, and whether coins bearing his name constitute the first writing in Britain remain source-scoped.
