# Ronald McDowell

## Overview

Ronald McDowell is the self-taught artist who designed the [[FootSoldierMonument]] for Birmingham's Kelly Ingram Park from Bill Hudson's 1963 photograph of [[WalterGadsden]], [[RichardMiddleton]], and the police dog Leo.

## Current Profile

McDowell approached the commission as symbolic public history rather than neutral reproduction. He made Gadsden younger and smaller, enlarged Middleton and emphasized his dark glasses, changed Leo into a wolf-like animal, and shifted Gadsden's posture toward open-handed nonviolence. He described the blinded officer and attacking dog as embodiments of racism unable to recognize its victim's humanity.

His candor makes artistic transformation central rather than incidental to the work. The sculpture misdescribes important facts about the particular encounter, including Gadsden's movement role and possibly Middleton's intention, while deliberately expressing a broader judgment about Birmingham's racial order and the authority of Black citizens to commemorate it.

## Key Characteristics

- Self-taught artist commissioned for a first major sculpture by Birmingham's Black political leadership.
- Used a famous news photograph as source material rather than as a binding literal template.
- Altered scale, posture, complexion, vision, and animal form to clarify unequal power.
- Framed the officer and dog as symbols of racism rather than portraits limited to individual intention.
- Treated public art as a way for formerly marginalized people to claim authority over historical narration.
