# Rex Hunt

## Overview

Rex Hunt was governor of the [[FalklandIslands]] when Argentine forces invaded on 2 April 1982.

## Current Profile

The source presents Hunt's conduct as resistance bounded by civilian and military responsibility. With Government House surrounded and the small British defense force heavily outnumbered, he ordered the Royal Marines to lay down their weapons to prevent further bloodshed while avoiding a formal surrender. He told Islanders that Britain would return, refused General Osvaldo García's handshake, and rejected the occupation as unlawful.

This profile makes symbolic conduct part of crisis leadership without confusing it with battlefield capacity. Hunt could not stop the invasion, but his language and refusal to legitimize it preserved continuity between the local administration, Islander allegiance, and the later British counteroffensive.

## Key Characteristics

- Governor responsible for the Islands during the Argentine invasion.
- Chose to stop hopeless resistance rather than incur additional casualties.
- Distinguished laying down arms from formally accepting Argentine sovereignty.
- Used radio communication to reassure Islanders that Britain would return.
- Preserved symbolic and legal opposition to the occupiers after military defeat.
