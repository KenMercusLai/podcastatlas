# Rex Hunt

## Overview

Rex Hunt was governor of the [[FalklandIslands]] when Argentine forces invaded on 2 April 1982.

## Current Profile

The sources present Hunt's conduct as a rapid transition from civilian administration to resistance bounded by civilian and military responsibility. After receiving warning on 1 April that an Argentine task force was expected, he ordered classified papers burned, closed schools, sent his family away, and concentrated 69 Royal Marines around Government House. With the residence surrounded after the landing, he ordered them to lay down their weapons to prevent further bloodshed while avoiding a formal surrender. He told Islanders that Britain would return, refused General Osvaldo García's handshake, and rejected the occupation as unlawful.

This profile makes symbolic conduct part of crisis leadership without confusing it with battlefield capacity. Hunt could not stop the invasion, but his language and refusal to legitimize it preserved continuity between the local administration, Islander allegiance, and the later British counteroffensive.

## Key Characteristics

- Governor responsible for the Islands during the Argentine invasion.
- Converted late warning into document destruction, civilian precautions, and a concentrated local defense.
- Chose to stop hopeless resistance rather than incur additional casualties.
- Distinguished laying down arms from formally accepting Argentine sovereignty.
- Used radio communication to reassure Islanders that Britain would return.
- Preserved symbolic and legal opposition to the occupiers after military defeat.

## Qualifications

The page rests on two retrospective British podcast summaries rather than Hunt's papers, direct Islander testimony, or Argentine operational records. Exact dialogue, warning chronology, command procedure, force numbers, and the distinction between surrender and laying down arms remain source-scoped.

- Extended the profile to the warning and defensive preparations that preceded the surrender decision.
