## Overview

Wang Mang / 王莽 is synthesized as the Xin founder whose rise depended on late [[WesternHanDynasty|Western Han]] weakness, [[WangZhengjun|王政君]]'s consort-kin position, Confucian moral prestige, omen politics, and archaising reform, and whose fall becomes an object lesson in legitimacy collapse. [[126-wangmang-zhisi-shensheng-diguo-de-huixuanbiao-he-huixuanbiao-de-huixuanbiao-786255780]] resists both simple villainy and clean rehabilitation by showing how sacred Han legitimacy, moral reputation, [[AuspiciousOmenPolitics|祥瑞政治]], and [[WangMangReforms|王莽改制]] could make usurpation imaginable before destroying practical rule. [[87-guonian-zui-shihe-liao-xuanxue-tantao-shenxian-fangshu-fazhanshi-jie-shangji-704051550]] adds the ritual-astrological side through Wang Mang's North Dipper practices. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-649-guanchang-li-de-renqing-shigu-you-duo-xianshi-ljcwds5n-xdxjo-ewt2arrgf9ib|Hanji 649]] now supplies the 15 CE legitimacy-anxiety and bureaucratic-paralysis layer: Wang Mang treats a dead-yellow-dragon rumor as a legitimacy threat, sacrifices Chen Qing to manage Xiongnu anger, multiplies ritual ordering and inspection, and produces officials who avoid initiative because the ruler's own usurpation makes him fear imitation. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-655-wanzhong-guixin-dao-minyuan-feiteng-wangmang-zuocuo-le-na-yibu-lseopbzgmas0hyjrliikzgp5oxko|Hanji 655]] supplies the 18-19 CE priority-failure layer: [[FanChongChimei|樊崇]] and other Qing-Xu rebels grow, Wang Mang provokes Xiongnu succession politics without usable follow-through, projects order through calendar, era-name, immortality, and music spectacle, increases extraction, and dismisses [[YanYouXinGeneral|严尤]] for saying Shandong rebels matter more urgently than [[Xiongnu|匈奴]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-657-wangmang-fei-taizi-you-duo-erxi-lklx1rd39kgz-by4tbcpaagx4z-l|Hanji 657]] supplies the 20 CE omen-succession and coercive-control layer: Wang Mang reads violent wind as proof that [[WangLingXinPrince|王陵]]'s apparent heir status is improper, restores [[WangAnXinPrince|王安]], launches costly Nine Temples construction, faces [[MaShiqiuXinRebel|马士求]]'s plot, tightens private-coinage punishment, praises [[TangZunXinOfficial|唐尊]]'s austerity and ritual policing, and imprisons [[ZhiYunXinAstrologer|智韵]] for Han-restoration remonstrance. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-658-erzi-queshi-buyinggai-wangmang-nusha-taizi-ln4tu6ly8eie6jspvrurzn2rjjp|Hanji 658]] supplies the 21 CE household-collapse and policy-stress layer: [[EmpressXiaomuXin|孝睦皇后]] dies, [[WangLinXinCrownPrince|王林]] is forced to suicide after the [[YuanBiXinPalaceAttendant|元璧]] scandal, [[LiuYingWangLinWife|刘英]] kills herself, Wang An dies, private sons [[WangXingXinPrince|王兴]] and [[WangKuangXinPrince|王匡]] are recalled, and private coinage enforcement turns into social disaster. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-660-2-yi-ji-kan-dong-chimei-jun-de-dansheng-lgqk1gkvh-0cwder6g5-wryi6mce|Hanji 660-2]] adds the following 22 CE pre-Liu-Xiu layer: Wang Mang completes the Nine Temples and performs sacred spectacle while the east faces famine, [[ChimeiRebellion|赤眉]] grows, official armies abuse civilians, relief grain is stolen, and [[WangYeXinOfficial|王业]] misleads him about urban hunger. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-663-gengshidi-liuxuan-shi-tan-fu-bushangqiang-de-lanni-lott3pdjjunwlv3wgksn4r5tiqyz|Hanji 663]] supplies the immediate pre-Kunyang mobilization layer: Wang Mang tries to project vigor by dyeing his hair, installing a new empress and consorts, issuing amnesty, dispatching commanders, and threatening a million-man expedition under [[WangYiXinGeneral|王邑]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-664-kunyang-zhizhan-jueding-guoyun-de-lishi-zhuanzhedian-lihrwawp4x1w1pn4aibdohq4-l-4|Hanji 664]] supplies the Kunyang defeat as the military turning point and shows Wang Mang answering accusations about poisoning [[HanPingdi|汉平帝]] through public document display. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-665-ta-busi-liuxiu-ye-nandang-huangdi-liauc3k3omnzqu09dqicxifjxy1t|Hanji 665]] fills the gap between Kunyang and Chang'an by showing internal abduction-surrender plotting, panic purges, continued regional uprisings, and the southern-suburb crying ritual. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-666-xihan-zuihou-yiwei-huanghou-weihe-touhuo-zifen-lrjurgjzvwezkppvgeupu6ormmzw|Hanji 666]] then supplies the immediate pre-death collapse through failed command incentives, hostage pressure, Chang'an breach, Weiyang Palace fire, [[EmpressWangPingdi|汉平帝王皇后]]'s self-immolation, and Wang Mang's final mandate self-deception. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-667-ta-weihe-duo-le-wangmang-de-shetou-hai-chidiao-ludwjghch4wdv3gour9gmg2pvx3t|Hanji 667]] supplies the terminal death scene: flight to Jian Terrace, killing by [[DuWuWangMangKiller|杜吾]], decapitation, dismemberment, head display, and popular tongue-directed revenge.

## Current Profile

Wang Mang's core wiki role remains the late-Western-Han legitimacy reversal. The Xiangrui source argues that Han founding myths and Heaven-centered rule made the Liu house sacred, but also made it thinkable that Heaven could transfer approval elsewhere. Wang Mang turns filial reputation, self-denial, family-punishment performance, and omen production into political capital while moving from regent to emperor.

His rule is treated as overcontrolled rather than merely ideological. Wang fields, anti-commerce rhetoric, monopolies, taxes, credit policy, currency churn, frontier symbolic hierarchy, and coercive moral governance make [[IdeologicalOvercontrol|ideological overcontrol]] a governing failure: ideas matter, but they outrun institutions, feedback, and material capacity.

Episode 87 adds that Wang Mang's crisis practice is also embodied and spatial. His "威斗" and North Dipper orientation make celestial legitimacy a court ritual behavior, linking him to [[ChineseAstrologicalPolitics]] and later [[DaoistRitualTechnology|Daoist pacing-the-Dipper]] traditions.

Hanji 658 adds the earlier household-collapse layer. Wang Mang's wife Empress Xiaomu dies after long grief over sons he had already killed; Wang Lin's involvement with Yuan Bi creates fear and a failed patricide plot; Wang Mang suppresses the scandal through torture, forced suicide, and disappearing officials; Liu Ying's astrology branch and suicide widen the death chain; and Wang An's illness leaves Wang Mang without publicly recognized sons. The later recall of Wang Xing and Wang Kuang makes Wang Mang's moral persona and succession need visibly collide.

Hanji 659 adds that the same year is also an information and talent-retention failure. Wang Mang welcomes palace-selection and immortality rhetoric, symbolically attacks [[LiuBang|刘邦]]'s temple, refuses [[GongsunLuXinOfficial|公孙禄]]'s argument that the crisis is internal, rejects reports that rebels are driven by poverty and cold, orders such explanations punished, and later removes [[TianKuangXinOfficial|田邝]] after his unauthorized local mobilization and Qing-Xu victories prove too successful for Wang Mang's comfort.

Hanji 649 now adds the 15 CE legitimacy-anxiety prelude to the administrative failures in Hanji 650. Wang Mang treats a yellow-dragon death rumor as a symbolic attack on imperial authority, cannot find the rumor source, and then handles the Xiongnu corpse-return dispute by killing Chen Qing as a blamed subordinate before sending envoys and gifts. The same source shows him investing in ritual, music, territorial design, and inspection while local vacancies, acting officials, intrusive envoys, tax pressure, bribery, and fear-driven official passivity create [[LegitimacyAnxietyAdministrativeParalysis|legitimacy-anxiety administrative paralysis]] and [[DiplomaticScapegoatBlameTransfer|diplomatic scapegoat blame transfer]].

Hanji 650 adds the following 15-16 CE administrative and fiscal prelude. Wang Mang protects power by putting treasuries, grain, and confidential memorial handling under eunuchs and intimate attendants, but this produces [[SecrecyDrivenAdministrativeBottleneck|secrecy-driven administrative bottleneck]]: information is hidden from normal offices, petitions and prison cases stall, guard rotation stops, and Wang Mang's own approval time becomes a system limit. The same source shows him answering disaster, frontier grain pressure, and unpaid salaries with a fifteen-rank variable salary system that links pay to harvest, disaster, and palace supply conditions, but proves too complex to pay officials reliably. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-651-hetu-luoshu-zhi-mi-ni-neng-pojie-ma-lvkgovzsk0yqjb-vbvfxl0ikx2u9|Hanji 651]] adds the immediate 16 CE omen-and-war layer: officials read a riverbank collapse and watercourse change through [[HetuLuoshu|河图洛书]] as "earth suppressing water," congratulate Wang Mang on a sign of Xiongnu defeat, and he responds with frontier deployment while the southwest Quting war consumes soldiers, grain, and civilian property.

Hanji 655 adds the following 18-19 CE layer that explains why the later feedback failures have a field to grow in. Wang Mang faces Fan Chong's expanding eastern rebel group, parallel Qing-Xu risings, and failed official suppression, yet escalates Xiongnu succession intervention through Xu Bu Dang even though the army cannot be assembled. He then answers the crisis with [[RitualSpectacleCrisisDisplacement|ritual spectacle]], speculative anti-Xiongnu marvel recruitment, a one-thirtieth levy, textile and horse burdens, and dismissal of Yan You's advice to handle Shandong first. This makes [[CrisisPriorityMisalignment|crisis priority misalignment]] an early driver of the collapse pattern already visible in Hanji 656 and Hanji 659.

Hanji 657 adds the intervening 20 CE layer. Wang Mang converts wind, thunder, poor harvests, frontier danger, and rebellion into a succession-naming problem, moving Wang Ling away from apparent crown-prince status and restoring Wang An. In the same crisis field, he begins the Nine Temples project, expands punishment after Ma Shiqiu's plot, pushes private-coinage enforcement toward household and neighbor liability, elevates Tang Zun's austerity behavior, and jails Zhi Yun for saying Han will revive. This tightens the profile of Wang Mang as a ruler who treats omen interpretation, ritual construction, punishment, currency credit, and heir naming as connected tools for preserving authority.

Hanji 660-2 adds the next-stage spectacle-versus-crisis layer. Wang Mang completes the Nine Temples and performs an elaborate procession that officials privately interpret as funerary rather than immortal, while Guandong famine, cannibalism, Chimei growth, official-army abuse, and refugee deaths make the regime's local order visibly fail. The same source shows Wang Mang misreading famine through bad advice and false reports: grass-paste relief wastes effort, relief grain is stolen, and Wang Ye stages polished rice and meat soup as evidence that people are eating well.

Hanji 666 adds the immediate pre-death collapse layer. Wang Mang's Nine Tiger general deployment fails as command design because family hostages and tiny rewards undermine loyalty when the palace still holds large wealth. The defeat at Huayin, suicides or flight among generals, armed prisoners scattering after oath-taking, local offices surrendering, tomb and ritual-building destruction, and the burning of Weiyang Palace show that military, administrative, familial, and symbolic order are already failing before Wang Mang is killed.

Hanji 663 now supplies the immediate pre-Kunyang crisis-management layer. Wang Mang answers the spread of Han-restoration forces by trying to make his own body and court look vigorous: he dyes his hair and beard, establishes a new empress and expanded palace women, issues amnesty, and mixes surrender offers with threats. Militarily, he sends Wang Kuang, Ai Zhang, Yan You, Chen Mao, Wang Yi, and Wang Xun into anti-rebel campaigns, while the largest army moves toward Kunyang with specialists, symbolic force, and animals meant to heighten awe.

Hanji 664 supplies the following Kunyang-turning-point layer. Wang Mang's side still has a huge army under Wang Yi, but the battle exposes that scale cannot compensate for rejected advice, rigid siege design, and battlefield noncoordination. The same source also shows Wang Mang defending himself against Han-side claims that he poisoned Han Pingdi by displaying stored prayer documents and weeping before court officials. The wiki reads this as an earlier public self-vindication performance that foreshadows later mandate self-deception without treating the poisoning charge as settled by this note.

Hanji 665 supplies the post-Kunyang panic and internal betrayal layer. Wang She, State Teacher Duke Liu Xiu, and Dong Zhong plot to abduct Wang Mang and surrender to the Gengshi side; Sun Ji informs on them; Wang She and the state-teacher duke kill themselves; Dong Zhong is dismembered; and Wang Mang buries associated families and officials alive after forcing soldiers to dig the pits. The same source shows Wang Mang recalling Wang Yi after Kunyang, appointing Zhang Han, Cui Fa, and Miao Xin, facing northwest risings, and trying to answer collapse with a southern-suburb ritual in which officials, commoners, and selected grief-performers cry while he eats porridge and distributes titles.

The same source makes Wang Mang's final psychology and family cost visible. His daughter, the former Han Pingdi empress, enters the fire saying she cannot face the Han house, turning Xin collapse into a dynastic-identity rupture inside the palace. Wang Mang then retreats into ritual posture, sky-blue clothing, a jade dagger, divination-directed seating, and a claim that Heaven gave virtue to him. The wiki reads this as [[TerminalMandateSelfDeception|terminal mandate self-deception]]: mandate language no longer organizes rule but helps the ruler refuse the evidence of collapse.

Hanji 667 supplies the focused death and posthumous-memory layer. Wang Mang's final refuge at Jian Terrace is framed as a reversal from former imperial banquet space into a dead end. A market butcher-soldier, Du Wu, kills him; soldiers cut off the head and dismember the body; the head is sent to the Gengshi center and displayed; and the crowd's cutting and eating of the tongue is presented as an extreme, source-scoped symbolic revenge against a ruler remembered for persuasive deception.

## Key Characteristics

- Usurper-founder whose path to the [[XinDynasty|Xin dynasty]] depended on Western Han dynastic weakness, Wang-family access, moral reputation, and omen politics.
- Ideological ruler whose Confucian, archaizing, economic, ritual, and frontier programs outran administrative and social capacity.
- Ritual-political actor who turned Heaven, prophecy, auspicious signs, and North Dipper practice into tools of legitimacy.
- Crisis-image and feedback-destroying ruler who tries to answer household loss, rebellion, disaster, and policy failure through rumor suppression, diplomatic scapegoating, ritual overdesign, intrusive inspection, fear-driven bureaucratic inaction, confidential-channel secrecy, personal approval loops, salary redesign, Hetu-Luoshu omen readings, calendar/music/immortality spectacle, speculative talent theater, unsupported Xiongnu intervention, Shandong-warning dismissal, heir-name correction, palace appointments, intimidation, punishment of internal-cause criticism, forbidden hunger explanations, and removal of a capable field operator.
- Collapsing commander whose Kunyang and final emergency expose the gap between spectacular force and usable command through rigid coordination, internal abduction plots, hostage pressure, meager rewards, failed oath mobilization, and officer flight or suicide.
- Negative case for [[DynasticLegitimacyBackfire|dynastic legitimacy backfire]]: the sacred logic that supported Han could be redirected against Han and then fail Wang Mang as well.
- Defeated ruler whose death and body become a public object of revenge, humiliation, and moral reversal.

## Qualifications

The wiki does not reduce Wang Mang to a single moral label. The Xiangrui source deliberately complicates inherited condemnation and modern rehabilitation, while still treating his rule as a failed fusion of legitimacy, reform, and coercive control. Hanji 649 supports the dead-dragon rumor, Chen Qing scapegoat, Xiongnu mission, ritual workload, inspection, bribery, and official-passivity layer, but Chen Qing, Wang Xian the envoy, Wang Xi, Xu Pu Dang, Xu Pu She, Youchu Weigu Xiwang, Huangshan Palace, "公奴", "善鱼", exact offices, Xiongnu-side motives, and Wang Mang's inner psychology remain source-scoped. Hanji 650 supports the secrecy, administrative backlog, disaster-pressure, and salary-system layer, but eunuch office names, exact confidential-memorial mechanics, Kong Ren, full disaster geography, salary calculations, and Wang Mang's inner motives remain source-scoped. Hanji 651 supports the Hetu-Luoshu omen, anti-Xiongnu deployment, and southwest-war burden layer, but Feng Mao/Feng Ying transcript mixing, Ren Meng, Shi Xiong, Quting geography, exact casualty ratios, and Wang Mang's inner belief in the omen remain source-scoped. Hanji 655 supports the early rebel-growth, Xiongnu-intervention, ritual-spectacle, extraction, and Yan You dismissal layer, but Pang An, Xu Xuan, Xie Lu, Yang Yin, Diao Zidu, Xu Bu Dang, Xu Bu Can, named Xiongnu rulers, anti-Xiongnu inventors, and exact force counts remain source-scoped. Hanji 657 supports the omen-succession, Nine Temples construction, Ma Shiqiu plot, private-coinage enforcement, Tang Zun austerity, and Zhi Yun remonstrance layer, but Wanglu Hall, Wang Dan, precise title logic, temple dimensions, punishment procedure, and Wang Mang's inner belief remain source-scoped. Hanji 658 supports the household-collapse, succession, frontier-requisition, famine, and private-coinage punishment layer, but Yuan Bi, Liu Ying's astrology, Wang Lin's inner motive, Wang Xing and Wang Kuang's prior lives, exact campaign details, and casualty ratios remain source-scoped. Hanji 659 supports the root-cause reporting, Tian Kuang removal, and Han-symbol destruction layer, but Yang Cheng, Gongsun Lu's complete office history, Lu Kuang, Tian Kuang's exact later career, Qin Feng's full early trajectory, Chi Zhaoping's later fate, and Wang Mang's inner motives remain source-scoped. Hanji 660-2 supports the Nine Temples, Chimei-origin, official-army abuse, famine-relief, and misinformation layer, but Jing Shang, exact temple procession mechanics, grass-paste details, Wang Ye's full office, and minor commanders remain source-scoped. Hanji 663 supports the immediate pre-Kunyang image-management and mobilization layer, but Wang Kuang, Ai Zhang, Yan You, Chen Mao, Wang Xun, Ju Wuba, the exact troop totals, animal deployment details, and Wang Mang's inner state remain source-scoped. Hanji 664 supports Kunyang as the military turning point and Wang Mang's public document display as a self-vindication performance, but it does not independently settle the Han Pingdi poisoning charge, exact troop totals, or Wang Mang's inner state. Hanji 665 supports the internal conspiracy, purge, regional-rising, and crying-ritual layer, but Wang She, State Teacher Duke Liu Xiu, Dong Zhong, Sun Ji, Zhang Han, Cui Fa, Miao Xin, Wei Ao, Wei Cui, Wei Yi, Yang Guang, Zhou Zong, and precise ritual mechanics remain source-scoped. Hanji 666 strongly supports the immediate collapse pattern but keeps Nine Tiger names, exact micro-chronology, palace-room geography, and Wang Mang's inner psychology source-scoped. Hanji 667 is strong evidence for how the episode narrates Wang Mang's death and postmortem humiliation, but its final dialogue, exact battlefield micro-sequence, Gongbinjiu name form, and the psychology of the tongue-eating act remain source-scoped.
