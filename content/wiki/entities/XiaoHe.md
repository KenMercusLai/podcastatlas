## Overview

萧何 / Xiao He is the administrative founder who turns [[LiuBang|刘邦]]'s local Pei network into a governing and logistical system: he helps mobilize [[PeiCounty|沛县]], captures Qin records, protects the Hanzhong-Guanzhong base, recognizes [[HanXin|韩信]], and sustains the armies that found the [[WesternHanDynasty|Western Han]]. The same indispensable service later becomes exposure when popular credit and accumulated merit are interpreted as possible independent power.

## Current Profile

Across the bounded evidence, Xiao He's distinctive capacity is institutional conversion. He sees administrative archives where others see treasure, treats retreat to Hanzhong as base preservation, turns Han Xin's neglected ability into publicly authorized command, and makes [[GuanzhongRegion|关中]] supply, law, records, recruitment, and reserve manpower support Liu Bang's distant campaigns. Liu Bang's own victory explanation and the later “功人” ranking recognize that durable rear organization can outweigh visible battlefield wounds.

His political position is nevertheless morally and strategically mixed. Trust in Xiao He helps raise Han Xin and later makes Xiao He the credible lure in Han Xin's fatal palace summons; the precise conspiracy and execution traditions remain contested across sources. Xiao He also counsels investigation rather than immediate punishment in the [[YingBu|英布 / 黥布]] accusation, but even measured inquiry can accelerate rebellion in a court already shaped by fear.

Hanji 190 makes the late danger explicit. Xiao He's proposal to let households cultivate unused imperial-park land is framed as economical and beneficial, yet Liu Bang reads it as bribery and competition for popular support. [[WangWeiweiWesternHan|王卫尉]] secures his release by pointing to Xiao He's long wartime control of Guanzhong and the proper duty of a chancellor. Xiao He's barefoot apology and increased caution show that exoneration does not remove the ruler-minister asymmetry.

## Key Characteristics

- Converts records, grain, manpower, law, and territory into durable governing capacity.
- Recognizes high-value talent early and insists that authority be granted through credible public form.
- Anchors Liu Bang's rear while field commanders operate elsewhere.
- Receives formal recognition as the leading “功人” among founding merit-holders.
- Combines cautious verification with willingness to use trusted access in lethal court politics.
- Remains vulnerable because merit, office, and popular benefit can be reinterpreted as competing power.

## Qualifications

The source set consists largely of popular-history podcast summaries and frequently distinguishes chronicle report, host inference, dramatic lore, and settled judgment. The timing of Xiao He's promotion to 相国 differs between the reported [[ZizhiTongjian|《资治通鉴》]] and [[Shiji|《史记》]] frames. Han Xin's alleged plot, Liu Bang's role, the “three not die” promise, and the execution method remain disputed or source-scoped. The palace-grandness motive and the exact identity of Hanji 190's imperial park also remain interpretive rather than independently resolved.

## What Changed

- Recast Xiao He's accumulated record as institutional conversion rather than a chronological source appendix.
- Added the imperial-park proposal, imprisonment, Wang's defense, release, and post-release caution.
- Made popular credit an explicit danger alongside high merit and office.
- Preserved the contested Han Xin and office-chronology evidence as qualifications.

- [[HanXin|韩信]] - commander whom Xiao He recognizes and elevates, then later helps lure into court under disputed circumstances.
- [[GuanzhongRegion|关中]] - rear base through which Xiao He converts territory into supply, recruitment, records, and state continuity.
- [[WangWeiweiWesternHan|王卫尉]] - official whose remonstrance secures Xiao He's release.
- [[PoliticalCreditAppropriation|善行君恩化]] - framework for why beneficial ministerial action can look like competing legitimacy.
