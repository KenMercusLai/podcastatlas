# Ranavalona I

## Overview

Ranavalona I is the Malagasy ruler the episode presents as a case where anti-European resistance, hostile European reputation, and harsh domestic rule must be held together.

## Current Profile

The source introduces Ranavalona through a hostile literary description from *Flashman's Lady*, then has [[LukePepera]] qualify her reputation as shaped heavily by 19th-century London missionaries and European sources. That source criticism does not exonerate her. The episode still describes her as strongly traditionalist, hostile to the European and Christian opening associated with Radama, and connected to tangena ordeal, Christian persecution, public denunciations, executions, spy networks, overwork, and harsh royal projects. Her successful repulse of British and French coastal pressure makes her central to [[HeroicResistanceViolenceAmbiguity]].

## Key Characteristics

- Malagasy ruler whose reputation is filtered through hostile European and missionary sources.
- Traditionalist monarch who reversed or resisted Radama-era European and Christian influence.
- Harsh ruler associated in the source with ordeal, persecution, executions, overwork, and forced projects.
- Successful resister of British and French coastal attack, embargo pressure, and insult politics.
- Central example of why anti-colonial resistance does not erase domestic coercion.

## Evidence

- Reputation caution: [[317-african-amazons-glt5973082485]] says Ranavalona's reputation as mad or purely evil was largely shaped by 19th-century London missionaries and European sources.
- Traditionalist reversal: [[317-african-amazons-glt5973082485]] says she reversed her husband Radama's openness to European and Christian influence.
- Ordeal and persecution: [[317-african-amazons-glt5973082485]] says she restored the tangena trial by ordeal and persecuted Christians through denunciations, executions, and a spy network.
- Death-count qualification: [[317-african-amazons-glt5973082485]] says Pepera rejects the claim that Madagascar's population halved under her rule, while still acknowledging deaths from persecution, overwork, and harsh royal projects.
- Foreign pressure: [[317-african-amazons-glt5973082485]] says British and French forces attacked the coast and were pushed back.

## Qualifications

The source deliberately keeps European and missionary source bias visible. The current profile does not settle Ranavalona's full reign or population effects; it preserves the narrower source judgment that hostile reputation and real brutality can both be true interpretive pressures.
