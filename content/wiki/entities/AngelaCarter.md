# Angela Carter / 安吉拉·卡特

Angela Carter / 安吉拉·卡特 is the writer and editor at the center of [[38-anjila-kate-jingguai-gushiji-o-mirende-hanfu-609607139]], where [[MihuanChishu|蜜獾吃书]] reads her through [[JingguaiGushiji|《精怪故事集》]], [[TheBloodyChamber|《染血之室》]], [[TheSadeianWoman|《萨德的女人》]], and [[WiseChildrenCarter|《明智的孩子》]]. The episode frames her as a "literary witch": a postwar writer who keeps fantasy, gothic, psychoanalysis, feminism, and old folk-story roughness alive against both narrow realism and patriarchal story cleaning.

The earlier [[kaitianchuang-moyu-ganxie-chui-dali-553650149]] page only preserved Carter as a future-topic preview. Episode 38 fulfills that lead and replaces the thin preview with source-grounded detail: Carter matters here because she makes old wives, grandmothers, mothers, desiring girls, trickster wives, violent women, and comic household revolt central to fairy-tale reading.

## Key Claims
- Carter's source-scoped literary image is not clean realism but witchy, gothic, erotic, comic, and folkloric imagination.
- [[JingguaiGushiji|《精怪故事集》]] is presented as an anthology that keeps female-forward folk stories strange rather than turning them into polite morals.
- The episode links Carter to [[FemaleOralStorytelling]] because her story authority is connected to grandmothers, old wives, mothers, and domestic oral transmission.
- Carter's feminism in this source works through [[UnrulyWomenInFolktales]] and [[FeministFairyTaleRewriting]], not through idealized heroines.
- [[TheSadeianWoman|《萨德的女人》]] gives the episode a way to discuss sex, repression, liberation, and the rejection of fertility-only female value.
- [[WiseChildrenCarter|《明智的孩子》]] extends the maternal-storytelling frame into lineage, family, and the source's father/mother contrast.
