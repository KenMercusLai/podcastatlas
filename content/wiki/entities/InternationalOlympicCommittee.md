# International Olympic Committee

## Overview

The International Olympic Committee is the governing institution of the [[ModernOlympicGames]], represented here through its management of amateurism, political crisis, host-city commitments, protest, and event continuity.

## Current Profile

[[81-modern-olympics-part-2-glt4846758176]] presents the IOC less as a neutral custodian than as an institution whose neutrality claims repeatedly collide with power. State-supported Eastern Bloc athletes strained nominal amateur rules; Mexico 1968 placed massacre and podium protest beside the Games; Munich 1972 placed murdered athletes beside the decision to continue; and the host-city model encouraged expensive commitments with disputed public returns.

The episode's criticism centers especially on [[AveryBrundage]], but its wider argument is institutional: continuity, ceremony, and claimed political separation can protect the event while leaving repression, athlete rights, security, and public cost insufficiently addressed.

## Key Characteristics

- Governs the Olympic movement and protects continuity of the Games.
- Has relied on political-neutrality language despite repeated geopolitical conflict.
- Historically faced an unstable boundary between amateur rules and state-supported full-time training.
- Influences host-city obligations whose costs and benefits are distributed beyond the sporting institution.
- Must balance event continuity against athlete welfare, protest rights, security, and public legitimacy.

## Evidence

- Amateurism problem: [[81-modern-olympics-part-2-glt4846758176]] describes how Communist state support fit poorly with older amateur categories.
- Protest and repression: [[81-modern-olympics-part-2-glt4846758176]] connects Mexico 1968 to the massacre and the punishment of Tommie Smith and John Carlos.
- Crisis continuity: [[81-modern-olympics-part-2-glt4846758176]] describes the decision to resume competition after the Munich killings.
- Host-city model: [[81-modern-olympics-part-2-glt4846758176]] criticizes costly commitments and uncertain economic or participation returns.

## Qualifications

This page records the episode's critical interpretation rather than a complete institutional history or legal account of the IOC. It does not independently establish current IOC rules, finances, host contracts, or reforms after the period discussed.
