# Stan Brooks

## Overview

Stan Brooks is a film and television producer represented here through his development of [[ChristmasInConnecticut1992|Christmas in Connecticut]], his collaboration and friendship with [[ArnoldSchwarzenegger]], and his later service on the California Film Commission.

## Current Profile

Brooks initially conceived a modest TNT remake of the 1945 screwball comedy for the expanding cable market. He updated its fraudulent domestic columnist into a television personality, hired Janet Brownell for a script close to the original, and cast Dyan Cannon before Schwarzenegger's unexpected offer transformed the project's scale and tone.

His producing judgment favored preserving the rare directing commitment. When Schwarzenegger asked for more action, danger, and comedy, Brooks accepted major changes and further writers rather than risk losing him. The reported $3 million production remained constrained, however, and its 20-day shoot exposed the gap between ambition and execution.

Brooks's retrospective philosophy is practical rather than prestige-led: he preferred regularly making television movies to waiting years for a more exalted feature because making movies was itself the goal. His friendship with Schwarzenegger endured after the film, and his later California Film Commission role included advocacy for production tax credits, though the episode does not isolate his effect on the credits adopted in 2009.

## Key Characteristics

- Developed compact television projects for the early cable market.
- Protected an unusual star-director commitment even when it substantially changed the script.
- Accepted ambitious creative expansion inside a fixed television budget and schedule.
- Treated repeated opportunities to make films as valuable independently of prestige.
- Sustained a professional friendship that later extended into public film-policy service.
