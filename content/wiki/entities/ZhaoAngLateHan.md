## Overview

赵昂 / Zhao Ang is a late-Han Liangzhou official presented through the resistance to [[MaChao|马超]] around Ji. His role joins local military participation to an extreme family-collateral decision: he and [[WangYiZhaoAngWife|王异]] send their son Zhao Yue as Ma Chao's hostage and continue supporting the uprising despite the risk.

## Current Profile

The current evidence places Zhao Ang among the officials who join [[YangFuLateHan|杨阜]] and [[JiangXuLateHan|姜叙]] after Zhao Qu and Liang Kuan induce Ma Chao to leave Ji. Zhao Ang participates in the Qishan position while the city is closed against Ma Chao. The source presents this as loyalty and resistance, but Zhao Yue's later killing makes the transferred family cost part of the profile rather than incidental background.

## Key Characteristics

- Local late-Han official aligned with resistance to Ma Chao around Ji.
- Husband of Wang Yi and father of Zhao Yue, whom Ma Chao holds as a hostage.
- Participates in the Qishan branch while internal collaborators close Ji against Ma Chao.
- Embodies a loyalty judgment whose political objective and family cost cannot be separated.

## Qualifications

The current profile rests on one structured popular-history summary rather than a primary-source collation. Zhao Ang's office, the exact operational sequence, dialogue, family deliberation, and responsibility for accepting Zhao Yue's exposure require broader verification. “Loyalty” records the source's moral framing and does not erase the ethical problem of transferring mortal risk to a child.
