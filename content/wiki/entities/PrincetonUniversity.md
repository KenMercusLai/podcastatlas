# Princeton University

## Overview

Princeton University appears both as a research institution cited on housing-permitting costs and as an institution confronting whether honoring former president [[WoodrowWilson]] was compatible with belonging for students harmed by his racial legacy.

## Current Profile

In the housing branch, Princeton is a research context rather than the subject of a broader campus profile. A working paper cited with [[MIT]] connects Los Angeles permitting delay to [[PermittingDelayCost]] and [[HousingAffordabilitySupplyMechanics]].

The institutional-memory branch makes Princeton itself the case. Student activists sought removal of Wilson's name from its public-policy school because of his racism and role in segregating the federal civil service. Gladwell agreed with removal and rejected a plaque as inadequate, but argued that rhetoric disclaiming any obligation to Princeton failed to communicate a desire to heal an institution the protesters also valued. Princeton's review committee retained the name at the time described, making the dispute evidence of contested memory and persuasion failure rather than proof of Gladwell's proposed alternative.

## Key Characteristics

- Research university associated in one source with permitting-delay economics.
- Institution whose buildings and public identity are presented as heavily shaped by wealthy white male benefactors.
- Former home and commemorator of [[WoodrowWilson]], whose racial record became a campus dispute.
- Site where activists, alumni, and a review committee advanced different standards for honor, context, harm, and institutional belonging.
- Example used to distinguish the justice of a demand from the persuasive strategy used to advance it.

## Qualifications

The housing source only cites a working paper and does not establish Princeton's institutional position on zoning. The Wilson source is a structured episode summary, not a full record of the protests, committee process, university governance, or every participant's argument. Gladwell's proposed boycott was not tested, and the episode's critique of protest rhetoric may understate the value of anger, direct pressure, and the costs students had already borne. This page records the decision as described in a 2016 episode and does not treat it as Princeton's current naming policy.
