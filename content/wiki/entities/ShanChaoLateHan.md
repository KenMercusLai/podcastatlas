## Overview

单超 / Shan Chao is a Huan-era eunuch marquis whose profile now runs from the pre-coup anti-[[LiangJiLateHan|Liang Ji]] oath into reward politics, family protection, and 160 CE burial honor. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-847-nvren-biande-haoming-cong-dongde-zhe-liang-zi-kaishi-lrlov43jtz0vq-o-5m8r2cqo0etx|Hanji 847]] presents him as one of the palace insiders [[TangHengLateHan|Tang Heng]] identifies for [[EmperorHuanOfHan|Emperor Huan]] and as a risk-aware participant in the secret oath. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-848-shunjian-kuatai-wo-weihe-shuo-ta-si-you-yu-gu-lgqvgmxlwga2fknmtxpj3ya9chk0|Hanji 848]] names him among the five eunuchs who help remove Liang and then receive county marquisates. Hanji 850 makes him one of the large post-Liang recipients criticized by [[LiYunLateHan|Li Yun]], Hanji 851 shows him protecting a kinship corruption field, and Hanji 853 records his death and lavish burial treatment.

## Current Profile

Hanji 847 supplies the pre-coup role. Tang Heng identifies Shan Chao as one of the close attendants opposed to the Liang family. In the secret meeting, Shan Chao and the others recognize Liang as a state danger but worry that their own position is weak and that Huan may lose nerve. The oath scene then uses Shan Chao's body as the blood source when Huan fears cutting himself, and Shan Chao tells Huan not to keep discussing the plot, preserving secrecy.

Hanji 848 supplies the founding reward role. Huan plots with Shan Chao and four other eunuchs before the palace operation against Liang Ji. After the coup succeeds, Shan Chao is enfeoffed as a county marquis with 20,000 households, the largest named grant in the five-eunuch reward group. Hanji 850 keeps the same settlement in view: Li Yun folds the reward scale of the [[FiveMarquisesHuanEra|Five Marquises]] into a broader warning that court favor has outrun military and institutional balance.

Hanji 851 adds the active-power layer before his death. Although sick, Shan Chao is appointed Chariot-and-Cavalry General. His nephew [[ShanKuangLateHan|单匡]] becomes a corrupt Jiyin administrator; [[FifthZhongLateHan|第五种]] and [[WeiYuLateHan|魏宇]] expose the case; Shan Kuang hires [[RenFangLateHan|任芳]] to kill Wei Yu; and [[YangBingLateHan|杨秉]] is punished after asking to bring Shan Kuang to Luoyang. Shan Chao later uses [[ShusunWujiLateHan|叔孙无忌]]'s disorder to frame Fifth Zhong, while his maternal grandson [[DongYuanLateHan|董元]] makes Fifth Zhong's exile dangerous.

Hanji 853 then records Shan Chao's death and burial. [[EmperorHuanOfHan|汉桓帝]] grants imperial coffin and clothing gifts, calls out Five Camps cavalry, and uses the construction office for the tomb. The same source says the remaining marquises become still more indulgent after his death, making Shan Chao a hinge between reward, protected-family power, and local predation.

## Key Characteristics

- Huan-era eunuch conspirator whose role begins in the secret anti-Liang oath before the visible coup.
- Risk-control voice who worries about Huan's resolve and then tells him to stop discussing the plan.
- Largest named recipient in the Five Marquises founding reward.
- Sick but elevated court favorite whose rank continues to rise in Hanji 851.
- Patronage source for Shan Kuang's corruption, witness-suppression attempt, and failed confrontation.
- Retaliation actor who uses Shusun Wuji's disorder to frame Fifth Zhong.
- Burial-status signal of continuing court favor after the Liang Ji crisis.

## Qualifications

This page remains bounded to Hanji 847, Hanji 848, Hanji 850, Hanji 851, and Hanji 853. Hanji 847 supplies a vivid oath scene, but the bite detail remains source-scoped until corroborated by another note. Hanji 848 explains why Shan Chao belongs at the founding of the Five Marquises, but it does not provide a full individual biography. Hanji 851's sick but active Shan Chao is not a contradiction of Hanji 853's later death and burial. Shan Chao's page should not absorb the entire Five Marquises chronology, but it includes the Shan Kuang branch because the protection and retaliation flow directly through his family network.

## What Changed

- Hanji 847 adds Shan Chao's secret-oath role, concern about Huan's resolve, blood-oath bite detail, and secrecy warning.
- Hanji 848 remains Shan Chao's founding coup role and 20,000-household grant.
- Hanji 851 adds Shan Chao's sickbed elevation, Shan Kuang protection, Fifth Zhong retaliation, and Dong Yuan exile-threat branch between Hanji 850 and Hanji 853.
- The page now treats Shan Chao as an active patronage shield before his burial becomes a status signal.
