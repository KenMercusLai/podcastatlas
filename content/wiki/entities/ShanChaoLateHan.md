## Overview

单超 / Shan Chao is a Huan-era eunuch marquis whose current profile starts with a large 159 CE enfeoffment in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-850-huandi-weihe-zhiyi-sha-zhongchen-lokxtceq7vv-giec6iufwwk9fwk7|Hanji 850]] and continues with lavish burial treatment in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-853-jiemi-hanchao-ban-de-zhaoshi-gu-er-lvsa2pby8iyfevfoobobpik52mij|Hanji 853]]. His biography matters chiefly as evidence of [[EmperorHuanOfHan|汉桓帝]]'s post-Liang reward politics and as the trigger for the episode's account of the remaining [[FiveMarquisesHuanEra|Five Marquises]] becoming more indulgent.

## Current Profile

Hanji 850 says Shan Chao is one of the eunuchs rewarded after the fall of [[LiangJiLateHan|梁冀]]. His grant is presented as especially large, and [[LiYunLateHan|李云]] folds the size of the Five Marquises' rewards into a broader warning that court favor has outrun military and institutional balance.

Hanji 853 says Shan Chao dies in the same 160 CE layer as Huan's amnesty and the Li-family survival story. Huan grants him imperial coffin and clothing gifts, calls out Five Camps cavalry, and has the construction office lead his tomb work. The episode treats this as a public display of court favor.

After Shan Chao's death, the source says the remaining four marquises become still more arrogant and indulgent. Shan Chao therefore functions as a hinge: his funeral shows how high the eunuch-favorite reward could rise, while his death frames the group's intensified local predation.

## Key Characteristics

- Huan-era eunuch marquis whose large post-Liang reward becomes part of Li Yun's critique.
- Burial-status signal of continuing court favor after the Liang Ji crisis.
- Narrative hinge for the remaining marquises' indulgence and arrogance.
- Source-bounded node in the wider eunuch patronage and local violence field.

## Qualifications

This page remains limited to Hanji 850's reward notice and Hanji 853's death and burial notice. It should not be read as a full biography of Shan Chao or a complete account of the Five Marquises' enfeoffment.
