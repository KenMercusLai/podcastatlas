# Kalshi

## Overview
Kalshi is a federally regulated U.S. prediction-market platform tracked in the wiki as an information-aggregation product, gambling-law test, sports-volume platform, and integrity-governance case.

## Current Profile
The bounded evidence resists a single label. [[before-kalshi-and-polymarket-there-was-the-iowa-electronic-markets]] places Kalshi in the lineage of small academic election markets, while [[do-prediction-market-bettors-make-anything-better]] shows how commercial scale, sports volume, app design, and derivatives-law positioning make the product look increasingly gambling-like. Marketplace Tech sources add harmful-event contracts, state-federal conflict, insider controls, and platform guardrails.

At Super Bowl scale, the market is framed as an ecology of informed sharps and liquidity-providing squares. That may improve prices, but it also sharpens the legitimacy problem when non-public information, manipulable events, or systematically weaker participants drive returns.

## Key Characteristics
- Runs yes-or-no event contracts under federal commodities-market regulation.
- Treats prices as forecasts while offering an app experience that can resemble gambling.
- Has substantial sports exposure in the supplied sources.
- Faces state challenges arguing that federally approved contracts still function as gambling.
- Uses platform guardrails against candidate, athlete, coach, referee, and other insider participation.
- Depends on market integrity because users may leave when another trader can know or control the result.

## Qualifications
Platform volume, market share, reimbursement, trader-profit, and event-level figures are source-reported rather than independently audited here. A market can aggregate useful information and still create gambling, manipulation, addiction, or fairness concerns. Guardrails improve legitimacy only if identity, affiliated trading, enforcement, and information-source rules are workable at scale.
