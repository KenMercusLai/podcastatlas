# IKEA

## Overview
IKEA enters the wiki through [[daisen-jinru-diandong-yashua-lingyu-chuantong-jindian-jiameng-chixu-shousuo-1010976825]] as the home-furnishing retailer using European price cuts to respond to cost-of-living pressure and protect affordability.

## Current Profile
The source frames IKEA's 2026 European price-cut plan as a deliberate affordability reset rather than a narrow promotion. It reports a EUR 1.2 billion investment, hundreds of products with average reductions of 15%-25%, and supply-chain changes such as packaging and automation that help fund lower prices. The same source says China has not yet followed the European price-cut pattern, which keeps the strategy geographically bounded.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses price reductions as a strategic response to consumer cost pressure in Europe.
- Funds lower prices partly through supply-chain and operating-cost improvements.
- Focuses on broad basket affordability rather than a single product launch.
- Keeps geography important because the source says China has not yet copied the European cuts.
- Fits a wider retail pattern in which value perception is rebuilt through lower visible prices.

## Evidence
- Price-cut evidence: [[daisen-jinru-diandong-yashua-lingyu-chuantong-jindian-jiameng-chixu-shousuo-1010976825]] says IKEA announced a EUR 1.2 billion European price-cut program on 2026-09-01.
- Scope evidence: [[daisen-jinru-diandong-yashua-lingyu-chuantong-jindian-jiameng-chixu-shousuo-1010976825]] says hundreds of products would see average reductions of 15%-25%.
- Cost evidence: [[daisen-jinru-diandong-yashua-lingyu-chuantong-jindian-jiameng-chixu-shousuo-1010976825]] links the cuts to supply-chain savings, packaging changes, and automation.
- Geography evidence: [[daisen-jinru-diandong-yashua-lingyu-chuantong-jindian-jiameng-chixu-shousuo-1010976825]] says IKEA China has not yet followed the same cuts.

## Qualifications
The page does not establish whether the European cuts improve traffic, margin, or market share. It also does not infer a global IKEA strategy beyond what the source says about Europe and China.
