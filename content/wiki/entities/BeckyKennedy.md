# Becky Kennedy

## Overview
Becky Kennedy is the clinical psychologist and [[GoodInside]] founder featured in [[protocols-for-excellent-parenting-improving-relationships-of-all-kinds-dr-becky-kennedy-scim7905596498|this Huberman Lab parenting conversation]].

## Current Profile
The source presents Kennedy as a translator of relational and psychological ideas into language parents can use during conflict. Her framework begins from the premise that children are “good inside”: difficult behavior should be addressed firmly, but it more often signals feelings without sufficient skills than a bad identity. She organizes the adult role around [[ParentingSturdiness]], pairing enforceable boundaries with empathy while preserving responsibility for safety and family decisions.

Kennedy applies the model beyond young children. She uses repair after yelling, low-defensiveness observation, family problem-solving, continued bids toward separating adolescents, and decisive intervention when a teen's safety or overall functioning deteriorates. She also extends the same distinction between feelings and decisions to adult relationships and the relationship with oneself.

## Key Characteristics
- Defines sturdy leadership as staying connected to oneself and another person simultaneously.
- Treats boundaries as adult actions rather than demands whose success depends on compliance.
- Uses validation to acknowledge inner experience without making emotion the decision-maker.
- Reframes difficult behavior through missing skills, regulation, shame, and frustration tolerance rather than fixed badness.
- Makes rupture and repair ordinary parts of parenting rather than evidence that only perfect parents are safe.
- Offers concrete scripts designed for emotionally charged moments.

## Evidence
- Core model: [[protocols-for-excellent-parenting-improving-relationships-of-all-kinds-dr-becky-kennedy-scim7905596498]] defines sturdiness through simultaneous self-connection and child-connection.
- Boundary practice: [[protocols-for-excellent-parenting-improving-relationships-of-all-kinds-dr-becky-kennedy-scim7905596498]] distinguishes a request to turn off a television from an adult taking responsibility for enforcing the limit.
- Repair practice: [[protocols-for-excellent-parenting-improving-relationships-of-all-kinds-dr-becky-kennedy-scim7905596498]] recommends direct apology after yelling, explicit removal of child blame, and a credible next-step intention.
- Adolescent practice: [[protocols-for-excellent-parenting-improving-relationships-of-all-kinds-dr-becky-kennedy-scim7905596498]] combines continued connection with intervention when self-harm, violence, substance use, withdrawal, or narrowing functioning raises safety concern.

## Qualifications
This profile is bounded to one podcast source note and is not a complete biography, publication record, or evaluation of Kennedy's clinical work. “Deeply feeling kids” is presented as her practical description, not a diagnosis, and the episode's parenting scripts are public psychoeducation rather than individualized mental-health, family-safety, or emergency guidance.
