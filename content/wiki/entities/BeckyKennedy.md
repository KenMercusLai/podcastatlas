# Becky Kennedy

## Overview
Becky Kennedy is a clinical psychologist and [[GoodInside]] founder whose two [[HubermanLab]] conversations translate parenting and relationship psychology into practical language for boundaries, emotional responsibility, repair, shame, and learning.

## Current Profile
The sources present Kennedy as a translator of relational and psychological ideas into language parents can use during conflict. Her framework begins from the premise that children are “good inside”: difficult behavior should be addressed firmly, but it more often signals feelings without sufficient skills than a bad identity. She organizes the adult role around [[ParentingSturdiness]], pairing enforceable boundaries with empathy while preserving responsibility for safety and family decisions.

The second conversation sharpens the emotional-responsibility branch. [[EmotionalResponsibilityBoundary]] distinguishes guilt after violating one's values from absorbing another person's disappointment, while still requiring truthful explanation and repair after actual harm. [[ShameSafeLearning]] extends the good-inside premise into learning: shame can isolate and freeze reflection, whereas stories, questions, and generous interpretations can preserve accountability while making disclosure safer.

Kennedy applies the model beyond young children. She uses repair after yelling, low-defensiveness observation, family problem-solving, continued bids toward separating adolescents, grounded responses to projection, and decisive intervention when safety deteriorates. She also connects capability to [[FrustrationToleranceLearningSpace]]: adults model struggle, avoid reflexive rescue, reduce hard tasks to a workable first step, and treat quitting as a contextual judgment rather than a character verdict.

## Key Characteristics
- Defines sturdy leadership as staying connected to oneself and another person simultaneously.
- Treats boundaries as adult actions rather than demands whose success depends on compliance.
- Uses validation to acknowledge inner experience without making emotion the decision-maker.
- Distinguishes values-based guilt from taking ownership of another person's distress.
- Reframes difficult behavior through missing skills, urges, regulation, shame, and frustration tolerance rather than fixed badness.
- Makes rupture and repair ordinary parts of parenting rather than evidence that only perfect parents are safe.
- Uses stories, questions, grounding phrases, and very small steps as tools for emotionally charged moments.

## Qualifications
This profile is bounded to two podcast source notes with the same guest and is not a complete biography, publication record, or evaluation of Kennedy's clinical work. Repetition across the interviews is elaborating provenance rather than independent confirmation. “Deeply feeling kids” is a practical description, not a diagnosis, and the scripts are public psychoeducation rather than individualized mental-health, family-safety, or emergency guidance.

## What Changed
- Added the distinction between values-based guilt and absorbed distress.
- Added shame-safe learning through stories, questions, urge–behavior separation, and generous interpretation.
- Expanded frustration tolerance from household limits into puzzles, school, sports, confidence, quitting, and small first steps.
