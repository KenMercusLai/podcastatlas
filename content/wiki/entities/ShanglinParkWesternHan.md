## Overview

上林苑 / Shanglin Park is a Western Han imperial landscape whose boundary repeatedly turns reserved royal space into a dispute over ordinary cultivation, shared resources, revenue, displacement, and the political ownership of public benefit.

## Current Profile

Hanji 190 places an early version of the conflict under [[LiuBang|刘邦]]. [[XiaoHe|萧何]] proposes letting people farm unused land in the Chang'an imperial park, joining household livelihood to lower maintenance burden and grain return. Liu Bang instead reads the proposal as merchant bribery and an attempt to win popular support, so a land-use proposal becomes a court-security crisis.

Hanji 253 supplies a later expansion conflict under [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]. Survey and compensation planning give the enlargement an administrative form, but [[DongfangShuo|东方朔]] argues that replacement wasteland cannot preserve fertile soil, forest and water access, tax value, homes, graves, or ruler safety. Wudi reportedly rewards the critic yet proceeds. Across the two episodes, imperial parkland is therefore neither simply empty nor simply private: its classification determines who may use resources and who receives political credit for changing that use.

## Key Characteristics

- Reserved Western Han imperial landscape associated with cultivation limits and later hunting expansion.
- Contains or is represented as containing land whose “unused” status is politically contested.
- Park boundaries redistribute livelihood, resource access, revenue, maintenance work, and displacement cost.
- Public-benefit arguments can be rejected through suspicion even when their administrative logic is acknowledged.
- Under Wudi, survey and compensation planning coexist with criticism that replacement land is not equivalent.

## Evidence

Unused land and cultivation proposal:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-190-xiaohe-ruyu-lggvu7f-6lodqnb1eqnmdmq5c3gm|Hanji 190]] says Xiao He asks that commoners cultivate unused imperial-park land and is imprisoned when Liu Bang interprets the request as bribery and independent popular-credit seeking.

Survey, compensation, and public cost:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-253-hanwudi-nimen-shuo-de-dou-dui-dan-wo-jiushi-bu-ting-lhvsfcppigvfd0j2xdhqorxqvwb_|Hanji 253]] says Wuqiu Shouwang surveys and prices a proposed enlargement and that agencies identify wasteland for compensation.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-253-hanwudi-nimen-shuo-de-dou-dui-dan-wo-jiushi-bu-ting-lhvsfcppigvfd0j2xdhqorxqvwb_|Hanji 253]] attributes to Dongfang Shuo objections about fertile land, forest and water access, tax revenue, homes, graves, and hunting hazards; the enlargement reportedly proceeds.

## Qualifications

Both inputs are popular-history summaries rather than a complete institutional and geographical history. Hanji 190 calls the site a Chang'an imperial garden, so identification with the canonical Shanglin page is contextually strong but its exact boundary and administrative phase remain source-scoped. The acreage, land values, compensation quality, household counts, maintenance obligations, and long-run effects require primary-source comparison.

## What Changed

- Added the Liu Bang-era conflict over allowing households to cultivate unused imperial-park land.
- Reframed the park across two reigns as a recurring boundary between reserved space and public use.
- Added political-credit suspicion as distinct from Wudi's later rewarded-but-unadopted remonstrance.

## Relationships

- [[XiaoHe|萧何]] - chancellor whose cultivation proposal turns unused parkland into a public-benefit and political-credit dispute.
- [[LiuBang|刘邦]] - ruler who rejects the proposal and imprisons Xiao He.
- [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] - ruler who reportedly approves later enlargement despite criticism.
- [[DongfangShuo|东方朔]] - remonstrant who identifies the enlargement's livelihood, fiscal, displacement, and safety costs.
- [[PoliticalCreditAppropriation|善行君恩化]] - explains why beneficial use can become threatening when credit appears to attach to a minister.
- [[RewardedUnadoptedRemonstrance|赏谏不纳]] - later governance pattern in which criticism is recognized without changing the policy.
