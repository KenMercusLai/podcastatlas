# Constantius Chlorus

## Overview

Constantius Chlorus was the western Caesar who recovered Britain for the [[RomanEmpire|Roman Empire]], became Augustus in 305, and left a dynastic opening when he died at York and his son [[ConstantineTheGreat|Constantine]] claimed power.

## Current Profile

Within [[Diocletian]]'s [[Tetrarchy]], Constantius held responsibility for Gaul and Britain under the western Augustus [[Maximian]]. His recovery of Britain helped demonstrate the system's capacity to assign strong regional commands against simultaneous threats.

His promotion after Diocletian and Maximian abdicated also exposed the system's succession weakness. Constantine was not appointed Caesar, but after joining his father in Britain he asserted a claim when Constantius died, returning heredity and army acclamation to a design intended to manage succession through appointment.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Caesar responsible for Gaul and Britain.
- Commander credited with recovering Britain from breakaway rule.
- Augustus after the 305 abdications.
- Father of Constantine and bridge between appointed and dynastic legitimacy.
- Death at York became a trigger for Constantine's direct claim.

## Qualifications

This page covers Constantius mainly as a tetrarch and as Constantine's father. It does not provide a complete account of the British reconquest, his religious policy, his wider campaigns, or the competing legal and military bases of Constantine's accession.

- Created Constantius Chlorus as western tetrarch, restorer of Britain, and dynastic hinge in the system's collapse.
