## Overview

黄帝 / Huangdi appears in the current source set as a mythic route model, a humanized sage ruler, and an immortalized ancestor whose tomb requires explanation. These uses share political force but remain source-scoped interpretations rather than proof of a recoverable itinerary, biography, or divine ascent.

## Current Profile

The current profile joins movement, narrative, and ritual. Huangdi can serve as a possible precedent for [[QinShiHuang|秦始皇]]'s repeated post-unification travel: following a culture-ordering ancestor's broad movement lets a new conqueror appear as an organizer of the realm, not merely a monarch inspecting acquired territory. This route reading remains explicitly source-scoped in [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-122-3-qinshihuang-jing-he-shenxian-si-qilai-le-ljnw5estg6dvgfllm3kfcq6ulhxu|Qinji 122-3]].

The historical-narrative case contrasts Huangdi's place in marvelous, five-phase, omen, and chenwei traditions with [[SimaQian|司马迁]]'s allegedly more human and Confucian account. By making Huangdi resemble Yao and Shun as an exemplary ruler, historical narrative gives the Han court an ancestral language for imperial legitimacy; [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s sacrifice at the [[Qiaoshan|Qiaoshan]] tomb makes that ancestor ritually present. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-360-2-hanwudi-de-fengkuang-qiuxian-shoupian-lu-fu4ht-tl4fyr8dsx9sakdcmm-orc|Hanji 360-2]] sharpens the tension: Wudi asks how an immortal can have a tomb, and [[GongsunQingFangshi|公孙卿]] preserves both claims by describing the grave as a burial of clothing left by an ascended Huangdi.

## Key Characteristics

- Mythic culture-ordering ancestor whose political afterlife matters more here than a settled biography.
- Possible route model for Qin Shi Huang's repeated post-conquest touring.
- Figure embedded in divine, five-phase, omen, and chenwei traditions before or alongside historical narration.
- Humanized sage-ruler whom Hanji 360-1 attributes to Sima Qian's selective historical synthesis.
- Immortalized ancestor whose visible tomb is reinterpreted as a clothing burial rather than bodily remains.
- Legitimacy ancestor connecting imperial movement, tomb sacrifice, historical continuity, and exemplary kingship.

## Evidence

- Imperial route precedent - [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-122-3-qinshihuang-jing-he-shenxian-si-qilai-le-ljnw5estg6dvgfllm3kfcq6ulhxu|Qinji 122-3]] suggests that Qin Shi Huang's movement after unification may imitate Huangdi's legendary travels.
- Mythic-to-historical transformation - [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-360-1-simaqian-taishigong-chenghao-jing-you-ci-er-lai-lq6ulwhl_jscf6ipkjxev6zvwbrf|Hanji 360-1]] says Sima Qian filters marvelous traditions and reshapes Huangdi as a human Confucian-style ruler.
- Han legitimacy work - [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-360-1-simaqian-taishigong-chenghao-jing-you-ci-er-lai-lq6ulwhl_jscf6ipkjxev6zvwbrf|Hanji 360-1]] links Han Wudi's Qiaoshan sacrifice and Sima Qian's Huangdi narrative to the question of imperial authority's legitimate origin.
- Tomb-and-ascent reconciliation - [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-360-2-hanwudi-de-fengkuang-qiuxian-shoupian-lu-fu4ht-tl4fyr8dsx9sakdcmm-orc|Hanji 360-2]] reports Gongsun Qing's claim that ministers buried Huangdi's clothing after his ascent, allowing the tomb and immortality story to coexist.

## Qualifications

All current uses are interpretive and episode-bounded. Qinji 122-3 does not prove that Qin Shi Huang consciously copied a recoverable Huangdi route. Hanji 360-1 does not establish Huangdi's historicity or provide a full textual comparison demonstrating exactly what Sima Qian rejected, retained, or intended. Hanji 360-2 does not verify divine ascent, the tomb's contents, or Gongsun Qing's intent. The contrast between “divine” and “human” Huangdi is analytically useful but should not imply that myth, ritual, and history became cleanly separable.

## What Changed

- Added the clothing-tomb explanation that reconciles Huangdi's reported ascent with a visible grave.
- Clarified that humanized sage kingship and immortalized ancestry remain competing but politically usable Huangdi images.
