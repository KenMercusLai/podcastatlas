# Biltong

## Overview

Biltong is a slowly air-dried, seasoned meat associated with [[SouthAfrica]], formed through Indigenous preservation practices, Dutch settlement, and spices and vinegar connected to wider trade routes.

## Current Profile

The episode resists a single-community origin story. Khoisan people already dried salted meat before Dutch settlers arrived at the Cape; settlers combined drying with vinegar and spices including cloves, black pepper, and coriander. The food later became associated with Boer migration and, during apartheid, could be perceived as white, while dried-meat traditions and variations also remained deep in black South African cuisines.

Biltong operates as everyday food and national symbol. Beef, springbok, wildebeest, kudu, eland, and ostrich versions appear at petrol stations, rugby matches, township butcheries, lunch boxes, homes, and wineries. Migration and global protein demand have carried it abroad, where peri-peri and mushroom variants show adaptation without erasing the slow-drying method and cultural distinction from jerky.

## Key Characteristics

- Slow air-dried meat preservation rather than a fast, sweet jerky process.
- Multicultural formation involving Khoisan practice, Dutch language and settlement, and Asian-linked spices.
- Everyday South African food spanning class, age, venue, and region.
- Product entangled with Boer history, apartheid-era racial perception, and black culinary variation.
- Globalizing category adapted through new spices, markets, and plant-based versions.

## Qualifications

The source is a short structured podcast summary rather than an archaeological, linguistic, culinary, or oral-history study. It does not settle priority among preservation traditions, the chronology of vinegar and spice use, regional variation, commercial market size, or how representative the reported racial associations are. “Biltong is not jerky” records a cultural and process distinction, not an absolute taxonomy for every product sold under either name.

## What Changed
