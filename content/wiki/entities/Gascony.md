# Gascony

## Overview

Gascony appears as the reduced remnant of English-held [[Aquitaine]] centered on Bordeaux, and as the territorial hinge where English royal sovereignty and French feudal overlordship collide before the [[HundredYearsWar|Hundred Years' War]].

## Current Profile

The source treats Gascony as a structural cause of Anglo-French conflict. The 1259 Treaty of Paris recognizes English rule there but requires the English king to perform homage to the French king. That makes the English ruler sovereign at home and subordinate in France.

Gascony also matters economically. Bordeaux exports large quantities of wine to [[England]], making the region part of a wider commercial relationship as well as a legal dispute. The ability of Gascon subjects to appeal over the English king's head to the French king makes local disorder a tool of French pressure.

## Key Characteristics

- English-held remnant of the larger Aquitaine inheritance.
- Centered on Bordeaux in the episode's account.
- Governed through a homage arrangement created by the 1259 Treaty of Paris.
- Allows appeals from Gascon subjects to the French king.
- Creates humiliation and sovereignty ambiguity for English kings.
- Economically tied to England through Bordeaux wine exports.
