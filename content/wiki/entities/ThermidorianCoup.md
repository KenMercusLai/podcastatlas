# Thermidorian Coup

## Overview

The Thermidorian Coup was the 26–27 July 1794 confrontation in which the [[NationalConvention|National Convention]] arrested and outlawed [[MaximilienRobespierre|Robespierre]] and his allies, defeated the Paris Commune's attempted resistance, and opened the political reaction against the Terror.

## Current Profile

The source presents 9 Thermidor as a contingent collision of institutional rivalry, personal survival, economic alienation, rhetoric, and failed command. The [[LawOf22Prairial|Law of 22 Prairial]] and Robespierre's unnamed accusations gave otherwise divided deputies a common defensive motive. The Commune then recovered the prisoners and assembled armed strength, but [[FrancoisHanriot|François Hanriot]] withdrew from an exposed Convention and Robespierre failed to direct the Hôtel de Ville. The Convention used that pause to outlaw its opponents and organize forces under [[PaulBarras|Paul Barras]]. Victory enabled former practitioners of terror to blame Robespierre disproportionately, yet the reaction soon reduced committee power and dismantled central parts of revolutionary government.

## Key Characteristics

- It began as a defensive coalition among threatened insiders rather than a unified anti-terror program.
- Robespierre's vague final denunciations enlarged the alliance against him by leaving targets uncertain.
- The Convention converted interruption, voting, outlawry, and military organization into renewed institutional authority.
- The Commune briefly created a credible armed alternative but lost momentum through hesitation and weak popular support.
- Wage controls, scarcity, and suppression of section politics reduced sans-culotte willingness to mobilize.
- The victors concentrated blame on Robespierre despite their own participation in revolutionary violence.
- The aftermath expanded from removal of one faction into dismantling parts of the Terror's governing system.

## Evidence

### Coalition and Convention action

- [[712-the-terror-the-fall-of-robespierre-part-6-glt1154404105]] connects Prairial, committee rivalry, vague denunciation, Tallien's intervention, and the arrest votes to a coalition of anticipatory self-preservation.

### Failed insurrection

- [[712-the-terror-the-fall-of-robespierre-part-6-glt1154404105]] follows the Commune's prisoner recovery, Hanriot's armed opportunity and retreat, Robespierre's indecision, dispersal at the Hôtel de Ville, and the Convention's counter-mobilization.

### Political aftermath

- [[712-the-terror-the-fall-of-robespierre-part-6-glt1154404105]] links execution of the Robespierrists to retrospective blame, repeal of Prairial, reduced committee power, Jacobin closure, economic deregulation, and prosecution of former terrorists.

## Qualifications

- The coup did not begin as a principled or coherent repudiation of political violence; several leaders had administered terror themselves.
- Military hesitation mattered, but the counterfactual claim that an immediate assault would certainly have succeeded is not established.
- Popular non-mobilization had multiple causes, including wages, food, local repression, factional ambiguity, and uncertainty about authority.
- Precise private plans, motives, crowd counts, timings, injuries, and causal weights remain source-scoped.
