## Overview

郭沫若 is represented in the current wiki through three source-scoped roles: modern skeptic of a Qin paternity rumor, controversial participant in Republican literary politics, and public advocate associated with the risky excavation of [[MingDingling|明定陵]].

## Current Profile

As a historical source critic, Guo is cited as a skeptic of the claim that [[QinShiHuang|嬴政 / 秦始皇]] was [[LuBuwei|吕不韦]]'s biological son. The reasoning attributed to him is historiographical: the report is isolated, internally strained, and too similar to the [[Chunshenjun|春申君]] succession scandal to accept without caution. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-97-4-shishang-wei-shiye-song-laopo-diyi-ren-lo8omg01gkwlojkpiflaep99x-vz|Zhouji 97-4]]

His literary-political role is more contested. One episode suggests that some of [[LuXun|鲁迅]]'s myth rewriting responds to Guo's earlier mythic writing and Creation Society attacks, and uses Guo's alleged movement from attacking to later praising Lu Xun as an example of opportunistic reversal. That remains an interpretation, not a complete biography. [[75-gushi-xinbian-kulou-bei-fuhuo-hou-hui-ganji-zhuangzi-ma-681010087|Mihuan Chishu 75]]

The archaeology-policy controversy adds a third role. Guo is said to have strongly promoted Ming Dingling's excavation and later wanted to find Liu Ji's tomb, with Dingling's reported conservation losses presented as the outcome. The current synthesis preserves the warning while withholding sole-person blame because the authorization, field leadership, conservation, storage, and custody chain is not documented here. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-311-wenxuejia-guomoruo-ruhe-niangjiu-kaogu-dabeiju-lpq9bozhrdxqn7tiotyx3j-epm9l|Hanji 311]]

## Key Characteristics

- Modern intellectual cited as a source-critical skeptic of the Lu Buwei paternity story.
- Writer placed by one literary podcast in Republican-era myth-rewriting and polemical networks around Lu Xun.
- Used source-scopingly as an example of changing public positions after Lu Xun's death.
- Associated by one popular-history episode with advocacy for the Ming Dingling excavation and interest in Liu Ji's tomb.
- Archaeological responsibility, ancestry lore, family tragedy, and personal motive remain unverified in the current evidence.

## Evidence

Historical source criticism:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-97-4-shishang-wei-shiye-song-laopo-diyi-ren-lo8omg01gkwlojkpiflaep99x-vz|Zhouji 97-4]] cites Guo's doubts about the Qin Shi Huang paternity story through isolation, internal tension, and narrative resemblance.

Literary politics:
- [[75-gushi-xinbian-kulou-bei-fuhuo-hou-hui-ganji-zhuangzi-ma-681010087|Mihuan Chishu 75]] connects Guo to myth-writing dialogue, attacks on Lu Xun, and later praise in its broader critique of instrumental positions.

Archaeology controversy:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-311-wenxuejia-guomoruo-ruhe-niangjiu-kaogu-dabeiju-lpq9bozhrdxqn7tiotyx3j-epm9l|Hanji 311]] centers Guo in the Dingling excavation story and links the project to reported artifact damage and loss.

## Qualifications

These sources use Guo as a supporting figure rather than offering a full biography or archival study. The literary source's opportunism judgment, the archaeology episode's “forced excavation” language, Guo's exact authority and responsibility, his alleged descent from Guo Zixing, the supposed motive for Ming-tomb interest, his reported Kang-tomb ambition, and the juxtaposition with family suicides all remain source-scoped. The family anecdotes do not explain archaeological policy, and the Dingling case requires an institutional rather than single-person record.
