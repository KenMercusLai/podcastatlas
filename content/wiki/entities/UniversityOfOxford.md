# University of Oxford / 牛津大学

## Overview

The University of Oxford appears in the episode as an English university formed through the accumulation and movement of scholars rather than a single modern founding act.

## Current Profile

The source links Oxford's growth to scholar movement from continental Europe and treats the 1209 conflict and departure of some scholars as part of Cambridge's formation. Oxford therefore illustrates how medieval universities could emerge, divide, migrate, and acquire political weight through organized academic communities.

## Key Characteristics

- Early English case of gradual university formation.
- Institution strengthened through cross-border scholar movement in the source's account.
- Example of conflict between academic members and town authority.
- Parent setting for the migration associated with Cambridge's formation.

## Evidence

- Formation and migration: [[7-xungen-suyuan-gudai-dongxifang-de-daxue-boshi-shuoshi-he-xueshi-ltuhvvskxxv3rzzfal-8insvutvx]] connects Oxford's growth to scholar aggregation and the later departure of members toward Cambridge.

## Qualifications

The exact dates, motives, institutional continuity, and causal story connecting Paris, Oxford, the 1209 conflict, and Cambridge remain episode-attributed. Gradual institutional formation should not be converted into one unqualified founding date.
