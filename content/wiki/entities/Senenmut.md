# Senenmut

## Overview

Senenmut was a prominent non-royal official under [[Hatshepsut]], associated with her daughter, estates, audience chamber, royal works, obelisks, and mortuary temple.

## Current Profile

The episode treats Senenmut's exceptional accumulation of offices, 26 surviving statues, two planned tombs, and discreet appearance within Djeser-Djeseru as evidence of unusual royal favor and a close working partnership. His authority connected court administration, the Amun establishment, royal education, and monumental construction.

That prominence has encouraged a romantic reading of his relationship with Hatshepsut, reinforced in popular discussion by his apparent lack of a wife and an ambiguous sexual ostracon. The episode does not treat those clues as proof. It also leaves unresolved why he was buried in neither tomb and whether his disappearance reflects disgrace, assassination, ordinary death, or another unknown event.

## Key Characteristics

- Non-royal official who accumulated unusually broad court and estate responsibilities.
- Tutor to Hatshepsut's daughter and steward of royal and Amun property.
- Overseer associated with Hatshepsut's obelisks and Djeser-Djeseru temple.
- Received exceptional visual commemoration and access within a royal sacred complex.
- Subject of an influential but unproven romantic interpretation.

## Evidence

- Administrative range: [[299-the-greatest-female-pharaoh-glt5076645168]] lists tutoring, audience-chamber, estate, and works offices.
- Monumental partnership: [[299-the-greatest-female-pharaoh-glt5076645168]] connects Senenmut to the obelisks and mortuary temple and notes his image in the upper sanctuary.
- Exceptional status and uncertainty: [[299-the-greatest-female-pharaoh-glt5076645168]] reports 26 statues, two tombs, no identified burial, and ambiguous evidence used to suggest intimacy.

## Qualifications

The source cannot establish that Senenmut designed every feature attributed to him, that he and Hatshepsut were lovers, or why he disappeared from the record. The sexual ostracon's identities are uncertain, and absence of a known wife or burial is not positive evidence for one explanation.

## What Changed

- Established Senenmut as an administrative and monumental partner whose political importance is firmer than the romance narrative.

## Relationships
