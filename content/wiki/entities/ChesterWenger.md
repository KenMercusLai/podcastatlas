# Chester Wenger

## Overview

Chester Wenger is the Mennonite pastor whose conflict between church doctrine and care for his gay son [[PhilWenger|Phil Wenger]] anchors [[generous-orthodoxy-generous-orthodoxy|Generous Orthodoxy]].

## Current Profile

The episode presents Wenger as a deeply established insider rather than a detached critic. He came from a prominent Mennonite family, served as a missionary and pastor, invited women to preach before his Lancaster community ordained them, and held ministerial credentials from 1948 until officiating at Phil and Steve's 2014 wedding.

His dissent joined action, cost, and continued belonging. After signing the marriage certificate and losing his credentials, he wrote an open letter asking what Jesus required toward LGBTQ children facing rejection and harm. He accepted that church leaders believed discipline necessary, used shared scripture rather than contempt, and refused to make either his son or his religious community disposable.

## Key Characteristics

- Lifelong Mennonite pastor whose authority came from sustained service inside the tradition.
- Father who moved from uncertainty about Phil's sexuality toward acceptance and public solidarity.
- Quiet boundary-tester who invited women to preach before local ordination practice changed.
- Officiant who knowingly violated church rules by conducting and certifying his son's same-sex wedding.
- Cost-bearing dissenter who lost credentials held since 1948 without renouncing his church identity.
- Scriptural reform advocate who framed LGBTQ inclusion through Christian duty, family care, and reconciliation.

## Qualifications

The page rests on one structured episode summary rather than Wenger's complete letter, church records, family testimony beyond the episode, or an independent history of Mennonite LGBTQ policy. Respectful acceptance of institutional discipline does not make that discipline just, and Wenger's insider credibility should not become a condition imposed on people with less standing or safety. The source supports Gladwell's interpretation of Wenger as [[GenerousOrthodoxy|generously orthodox]], but cannot prove that rhetorical respect caused later institutional change.

## What Changed

- Created Wenger's profile around costly family solidarity within continuing religious belonging.
- Distinguished respect for a church from obedience to every one of its rules.
