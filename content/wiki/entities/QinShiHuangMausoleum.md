# 秦始皇陵 / Qin Shi Huang Mausoleum

秦始皇陵 / Qin Shi Huang Mausoleum enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-125-1-qinshihuang-xiujian-shishang-zuida-lanweilou-afanggong-lhqc6jw4jzoyrg7jzc2mbxdebj4|Qinji 125-1]] as the [[Lishan|骊山]] burial project [[QinShiHuang|秦始皇]] begins when he first inherits the Qin throne. The episode uses it to show that late Qin construction pressure did not begin only with [[AfangPalace|阿房宫]]: the emperor's tomb had been absorbing labor and materials for decades.

The source places the mausoleum in today's Lintong area and describes a large square burial zone. Its narrative function is priority: when Qin Shi Huang dies before Afang Palace is complete, the regime pauses Afang and redirects labor toward finishing the mausoleum so the dead emperor can be buried.

The mausoleum therefore helps explain both Afang Palace's incompletion and [[ImperialConstructionOverload|帝国工程过载]]. The late Qin state can mobilize huge labor pools, but overlapping imperial projects make that capacity politically and socially costly.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-127-4-qinshihuangling-de-jingtian-mimi-lhgwr2of7zcmg2ggbcdzragwstyi|Qinji 127-4]] fills in the burial scene itself. The source describes Qin Shi Huang's body being interred at Lishan after roughly two months, with a deep tomb, bronze sealing, treasures, automatic crossbow mechanisms, mercury rivers and seas, a star-patterned ceiling, and long-burning lamps fed by "人鱼膏." The episode treats these details as the tomb's imperial imagination, while noting only limited separation between transmitted account and archaeological confirmation.

The same source makes the mausoleum a case of [[MausoleumSecrecyKilling|陵墓保密杀戮]]. Childless concubines are forced into burial, and artisans who know the tomb's mechanisms are sealed in so they cannot disclose its secrets. The mausoleum is therefore both a funerary monument and a lethal secrecy regime.

## Key Claims
- The source says Qin Shi Huang begins the mausoleum project in the year he becomes Qin king.
- Afang Palace and the Lishan mausoleum together mobilize 700,000 punished laborers in the source's account.
- After Qin Shi Huang dies, burial priority shifts workers away from Afang Palace and toward the mausoleum.
- The mausoleum is treated as a political priority, not merely a funerary site.
- Qinji 127-4 adds the tomb interior tradition: mechanisms, mercury waterways, celestial ceiling, treasures, and long-burning lamps.
- The same source records burial violence against childless concubines and artisans who knew the tomb's mechanisms.
