# 胡长 / Hu Chang (Western Han)

## Overview

胡长 / Hu Chang is a Western Han classics figure who appears in this wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-560-zhichang-bu-bei-keng-jiao-ni-zhe-zhao-bu-zhan-er-sheng-fqgut61bpihhdit1d2i7tdo2ridp|Hanji 560]] as [[ZhaiFangjinWesternHan|翟方进]]'s jealous counterpart in a shared [[Chunqiu|《春秋》]] teaching setting under [[HanChengdi|汉成帝]].

## Current Profile

The source does not build a full biography for Hu Chang. It uses him as the antagonist in a compact interpersonal case: he and Zhai Fangjin both lecture on the Spring and Autumn Annals by Chengdi's order, but Zhai's greater learning and reputation make Hu jealous. Hu responds by belittling Zhai, including in public settings.

Hu's profile changes when Zhai refuses retaliation and instead sends disciples to attend Hu's lectures, ask questions, and record Hu's interpretations. The source says Hu eventually recognizes the respect embedded in this conduct, feels ashamed, stops disparaging Zhai, praises his character, and becomes his friend. Hu therefore functions less as a villain than as a case of status insecurity that can be redirected by visible recognition.

## Key Characteristics

- Hu Chang is presented as a Western Han classics lecturer connected to the court's Spring and Autumn teaching.
- His conflict with Zhai Fangjin begins from envy over scholarship and reputation.
- His public belittling of Zhai shows status threat turning into reputational attack.
- He is responsive to repeated respectful conduct rather than fixed in hostility.
- His reversal makes him a witness to Zhai's conflict style: shame, praise, and friendship replace rivalry.
