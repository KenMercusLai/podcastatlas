# Jingjue Kingdom / 精绝古国

## Overview
Jingjue Kingdom is the Western Regions polity that Hanji 689-3 reconstructs around the [[NiyaSite]] and the [[FiveStarsOutOfTheEastBrocade]]. The source treats Jingjue as a small but strategically exposed oasis kingdom whose fortunes depended on Han protection, regional power balances, and environmental viability.

## Current Profile
In this source, Jingjue appears first as the probable political context for an elite tomb and brocade artifact, then as a Han-connected Western Regions state that remained reliable under the Western Han before weakening after [[WangMang]] and the loss of effective central protection. Its later decline is explained through annexation pressure from nearby polities, a refused Eastern Han protection request, ecological drying, and long-term invasion pressure before disappearance after the fourth century.

## Key Characteristics
- Jingjue is tied to Niya's archaeological remains and the brocade's elite tomb context.
- The episode reads Jingjue as a small Western Regions state within Han diplomatic and military reach.
- Its Western Han reliability is contrasted with later vulnerability after central protection weakens.
- Regional annexation wars by stronger oasis powers make Jingjue's survival precarious.
- Liu Xiu's refusal to resume management illustrates capacity-limited Han frontier commitment.
- Ecological drying and long-term invasion pressure help explain Jingjue's disappearance.

- Han tie: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-689-3-wuxing-chu-dongfang-li-zhongguo-yige-gulao-yuyan-de-xiandai-jiedu-lqccxzbvg-jziurfxm5tr5k4jlvq|Hanji 689-3]] says Jingjue remained a reliable Western Han partner before Wang Mang's usurpation disturbed the order.
- Regional pressure: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-689-3-wuxing-chu-dongfang-li-zhongguo-yige-gulao-yuyan-de-xiandai-jiedu-lqccxzbvg-jziurfxm5tr5k4jlvq|Hanji 689-3]] names Shache, Khotan, Shanshan, and other states as part of the annexation-war environment after Xiongnu weakness and Western Han collapse.
- Refused protection: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-689-3-wuxing-chu-dongfang-li-zhongguo-yige-gulao-yuyan-de-xiandai-jiedu-lqccxzbvg-jziurfxm5tr5k4jlvq|Hanji 689-3]] says Jingjue requested Liu Xiu's management after Eastern Han foundation but was refused because the dynasty lacked spare capacity.
- Ecological and military decline: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-689-3-wuxing-chu-dongfang-li-zhongguo-yige-gulao-yuyan-de-xiandai-jiedu-lqccxzbvg-jziurfxm5tr5k4jlvq|Hanji 689-3]] combines Niya River drying, oasis migration, Supi incursions, and post-fourth-century disappearance.
- Later memory: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-689-3-wuxing-chu-dongfang-li-zhongguo-yige-gulao-yuyan-de-xiandai-jiedu-lqccxzbvg-jziurfxm5tr5k4jlvq|Hanji 689-3]] says Xuanzang later encountered the area as a Shanshan-associated prefecture with harsh conditions and basic production.

## Qualifications
The source's Jingjue account is a synthesis of archaeology, later travel memory, textual history, and environmental explanation. It does not establish that the brocade alone proves Jingjue's political status or that ecology alone caused the kingdom's disappearance.
