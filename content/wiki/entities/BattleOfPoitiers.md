# Battle of Poitiers

## Overview

The Battle of Poitiers was the 1356 English-Gascon victory in which the [[EdwardTheBlackPrince|Black Prince's]] exhausted and outnumbered army defeated the forces of [[JohnIIOfFrance|John II of France]] and captured the king.

## Current Profile

The battle emerges from a failed campaign junction rather than a planned English search for decisive combat. A flooded Loire prevents the Black Prince from joining Lancaster, and the French army cuts his retreat toward Gascony. Short of food and water, the English take a defensive hill south of Poitiers while papal mediation fails because John expects an easy victory.

French tactical adaptation is real but incomplete. Much of the army dismounts to avoid another Crécy-style cavalry defeat, yet a mounted opening attack is broken by archery, the successive French lines lose coordination, and the Duke of Orléans withdraws after misreading the Dauphin's removal. John advances with the final line, partly under chivalric pressure not to retreat, before English-Gascon flanking attacks collapse it.

The outcome kills [[GeoffreyDeCharny|Geoffrey de Charny]], captures John and many nobles, and transfers the crown jewels to English hands. The victory supplies enormous diplomatic leverage but also begins the [[BattlefieldVictoryPoliticalControl|political-control problem]] developed in the next episode.

## Key Characteristics

- Battle forced by failed English concentration and a blocked retreat.
- Weak English logistical position offset by defensive ground and French impatience.
- French attempt to learn from Crécy through dismounted combat.
- Coordination failure across successive French lines.
- English archery followed by mounted and rear flanking attacks.
- Capture of the French king and large numbers of nobles.

## Evidence

- Campaign trap: [[320-hundred-years-war-the-black-prince-part-3-glt6004693022]] links the flooded Loire, failed junction, French pursuit, and blocked Gascon road to the decision to fight.
- Position and mediation: [[320-hundred-years-war-the-black-prince-part-3-glt6004693022]] describes English exhaustion and supply shortage before John rejects negotiation.
- Tactical interaction: [[320-hundred-years-war-the-black-prince-part-3-glt6004693022]] connects archery, dismounted French lines, withdrawals, chivalric constraints, and flanking attacks.
- Strategic result: [[320-hundred-years-war-the-black-prince-part-3-glt6004693022]] records Charny's death and the capture of John II, his son Philip, nobles, and crown jewels.

## Qualifications

This profile follows one narrative-history episode. Exact force sizes, casualty and prisoner totals, battlefield locations, the identity of the first flanking captain, and individual motives remain source-scoped. French adaptation should not be reduced to simple incompetence: the source credits dismounting as a response to Crécy even though command and timing then fail.
