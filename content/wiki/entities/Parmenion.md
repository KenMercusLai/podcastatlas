# Parmenion

## Overview

Parmenion appears as [[PhilipIIMacedon|Philip II's]] senior general and a source of cautious strategic counsel inside [[AlexanderTheGreat|Alexander the Great's]] increasingly personal command system.

## Current Profile

The episode uses Parmenion as both officer and narrative foil. Alexander's Granicus prestige strengthens the young king relative to the inherited senior commander. At Miletus, Parmenion recommends accepting naval battle, while Alexander refuses to risk the campaign in Persia's strongest medium. During Alexander's illness, Parmenion reportedly warns that the physician may have been bribed to poison him, enabling a dramatic trust scene whose precise historicity is uncertain.

## Key Characteristics

- Represents continuity with Philip's army and senior command generation.
- Offers more cautious or conventionally risk-sensitive advice than Alexander in surviving narratives.
- Helps reveal Alexander's growing independence and authority over the inherited officer corps.
- Appears in anecdotes shaped to dramatize the king's judgment and nerve.

## Evidence

- Command standing: [[dchha74-mania-for-subjugation-iv-dchha74-mania-for-subjugation-iv]] connects Granicus to Alexander's rising credibility relative to Parmenion.
- Naval counsel: [[dchha74-mania-for-subjugation-iv-dchha74-mania-for-subjugation-iv]] attributes the proposed sea battle at Miletus to Parmenion.
- Poison warning: [[dchha74-mania-for-subjugation-iv-dchha74-mania-for-subjugation-iv]] preserves the physician-warning episode as a dramatic, source-dependent anecdote.

## Qualifications

The profile comes from one episode's comparison of late traditions. Ancient narratives may exaggerate disagreement to make Alexander's successful choices appear more exceptional, and the reported messages and dialogue cannot be treated as verbatim evidence.

## What Changed

- Created Parmenion as the senior-command node linking Philip's inheritance to Alexander's growing personal authority.
