# João II of Portugal

## Overview

João II of Portugal was the monarch who rejected [[ChristopherColumbus|Christopher Columbus's]] westward proposal while strengthening Portugal's African route toward Asia.

## Current Profile

The two sources present João's decision as informed rather than obscurantist. His experts correctly judged Asia much farther west than Columbus claimed, while voyages down West Africa and [[BartolomeuDias|Bartolomeu Dias's]] rounding of the Cape made the speculative alternative strategically unnecessary.

The Portugal episode broadens João from a sponsor-rejection figure into a ruler pursuing wealth, a route to India, and the hoped-for Christian ally Prester John. Expeditions associated with his reign reached the Congo, Angola, and Namibia and probed West African rivers and gold routes.

## Key Characteristics

- Patron of an established Portuguese maritime program.
- Recipient of Columbus's first state-sponsorship proposal.
- Ruler whose experts identified the proposal's distance error.
- Strategic decision-maker favoring the advancing African route toward India.
- Monarch whose exploration policy joined commerce, Christian alliance-seeking, and geographic intelligence.

## Evidence

- Technical review: [[306-columbus-the-adventure-begins-part-1-glt6089901915]] says João's experts rejected Columbus's underestimated ocean distance.
- Strategic context: [[306-columbus-the-adventure-begins-part-1-glt6089901915]] links the rejection to Portuguese progress around Africa, strengthened by Dias rounding the Cape.
- African-route program: [[228-portugal-the-golden-age-of-discovery-part-2-glt7949934800]] connects João to Diogo Cão's Congo and southern African voyages, river exploration, gold-seeking, and the search for Prester John.
- Route validation: [[228-portugal-the-golden-age-of-discovery-part-2-glt7949934800]] treats Dias's 1488 return as proof that Africa could be rounded.

## Qualifications

The sources do not reconstruct João's court or policy comprehensively. The review procedure, chronology, expedition sponsorship, Prester John expectations, and balance among technical, commercial, religious, and strategic motives remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Extended João from Columbus's informed rejecter to the ruler directing the African-route strategy that made rejection rational.
- Added the commercial, Christian-alliance, and geographic-intelligence aims of his exploration policy.
