## Overview

《僵尸先生》 is a 1985 Hong Kong horror-comedy represented here through [[ZhiZuiJinMiFM|纸醉金迷FM]]'s plot reconstruction and discussion of its craft, folk materials, production risk, and genre legacy.

## Current Profile

The film follows 九叔 and his apprentices 秋生 and 文才 after the exhumed 任老太爷 becomes a jiangshi. Its durable design comes from combining a highly competent master with fallible comic assistants and giving ritual objects, martial action, bodily constraints, and supernatural threats clear scene-level functions. The grave and corpse plot runs beside 秋生's relationship with the ghost 董小玉, widening the tonal range while also making the whole feel assembled from partly independent attractions.

The bounded source treats the film as formative for [[HongKongJiangshiCinema|Hong Kong jiangshi cinema]], not as a documentary record of folk religion. Its ink lines, glutinous rice, breath-holding, talismans, geomancy, and corpse rules are a [[CinematicFolkloreRecombination|cinematic recombination]] whose effectiveness lies in legibility, rhythm, and reuse. The reported path from an uncertain, over-budget production to Taiwan and Hong Kong success supports the genre-history claim, but the figures and influence chain have not been independently checked here.

## Key Characteristics

- Combines horror, comedy, martial action, ghost romance, and ritual fantasy within one commercial film.
- Builds the central trio around expert authority and two contrasting comic apprentices.
- Turns props and physical constraints into visible, repeatable supernatural rules.
- Uses breath-holding to make suspense bodily and participatory.
- Mixes prepared payoff with conspicuous negligence used to keep the plot moving.
- Treats cremation as both rejected advice and the final solution.
