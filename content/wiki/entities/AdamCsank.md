# Adam Csank

## Overview
Adam Csank is the paleoclimatologist in [[02026-heggli-keats-csank-podcast-v2]], where he explains how tree rings can reconstruct climates from recent centuries to ancient forests millions of years old.

## Current Profile
Csank treats tree rings as biological proxies that become interpretable through calibration rather than as direct climate readings. His account links [[NevCAN]]'s local measurements to bristlecone physiology, growth limits, frost injury, volcanic events, drought, and radiocarbon evidence, while emphasizing that climate-growth relationships can shift during a tree's life.

## Key Characteristics
- He uses tree rings to reconstruct past climate across timescales beyond direct instrumental records.
- He applies the law of the minimum: growth reflects whichever necessary resource is most limiting at a site.
- He distinguishes water-limited lower-elevation growth from temperature-limited high-elevation growth while treating the boundary as mobile.
- He warns that stationarity assumptions may fail when climate-growth relationships change across long-lived trees' lifespans.
- He values monitoring whose most important future uses cannot yet be predicted.

## Evidence
### Proxy Interpretation
- [[02026-heggli-keats-csank-podcast-v2]] says tree rings record biological response, so their relation to temperature and moisture must be calibrated against direct observations.

### Changing Growth Limits
- [[02026-heggli-keats-csank-podcast-v2]] reports accelerated high-elevation growth since about the 1980s and asks when warming benefits will give way to water limitation.

### Unforeseen Long Records
- [[02026-heggli-keats-csank-podcast-v2]] connects continuous monitoring to later study of Miyake events, frost scars, volcanic eruptions, ecological recovery, and questions not known when the measurements began.

## Qualifications
The source attributes several findings and interpretations to Csank without providing study citations, uncertainty intervals, or a full methods discussion. Growth acceleration, resilience comparisons, elevation shifts, ancient-climate reconstructions, and claims about exceptional warming rates therefore remain episode-scoped here.
