# Éamon de Valera

## Overview

Éamon de Valera was an Irish republican political leader represented here through his treaty alternative, anti-treaty alignment, wartime marginalization, and later constitutional transformation of the Irish Free State.

## Current Profile

The treaty episode presents de Valera as the republican leader who opened talks with [[DavidLloydGeorge|David Lloyd George]] but controversially stayed out of the decisive London delegation. He proposed external association: an Irish republic linked to, but not inside, the British imperial system. He called the delegates plenipotentiaries while requiring major questions to return to Dublin and later told them not to sign without further consultation, creating a disputed gap between formal authority and political instruction. The episode offers several possible explanations for his absence but does not establish one.

The civil-war episode presents him as politically central to rejecting the Anglo-Irish Treaty but less influential over the anti-treaty military campaign once armed leaders took control. His later “Document No. 2” retained external association without recognizing the British monarch as head of state or swearing allegiance. After military defeat, he entered parliament under the disputed oath in 1927, won power in 1932, removed the oath, and secured the Treaty Ports in 1938, shifting part of the republican program from insurrection to constitutional statecraft.

## Key Characteristics

- He proposed external association as an alternative to the treaty's crown relationship and imperial membership.
- He delegated the London negotiations under instructions whose tension between plenipotentiary status and required consultation remained consequential.
- He aligned with the anti-treaty cause but was increasingly sidelined by military leaders during the civil war.
- His later willingness to swear the oath as a procedural requirement marked a transition into parliamentary opposition.
- In government he removed the oath and recovered the Treaty Ports while operating through the institutions created by the treaty settlement.
- His eventual dominance makes comparisons with Collins tempting, but the episode cannot establish what either man's survival or choices would have changed.

## Qualifications

This is a two-source, bounded profile of one political sequence, not a full biography or assessment of de Valera's long rule. The episodes' accounts of his influence, later attitude to the oath, relationship with [[MichaelCollinsIrishRevolutionary|Collins]], and marginalization by militarists remain attributed to their speakers. His absence from London is variously explained as pride, tactical positioning, fear of compromise, or reluctance to face Lloyd George; none is established here. The sources do not settle the feasibility of external association, the delegates' legal authority, responsibility for the war, or counterfactual claims about partition, church influence, economics, and party competition.
