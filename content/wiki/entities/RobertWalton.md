# Robert Walton

## Overview

Robert Walton is the Vassar finance vice president in [[food-fight-food-fight|Food Fight]] who explains the college's dependence on tuition and endowment income and argues for a public-benefit standard for tax-exempt educational wealth.

## Current Profile

Walton makes [[VassarCollege|Vassar College's]] access strategy legible as a constrained financial system. The episode attributes roughly two-thirds of its annual budget to collected fees and one-third to the endowment, with about 1,000 full- or near-full-paying students generating a critical share of revenue.

He also supplies the episode's governance claim. Colleges with large tax-advantaged endowments should deliver a public benefit rather than merely protect wealth or educate affluent students. His account connects [[FullPayStudentCrossSubsidy]] to [[UniversityEndowmentPublicBenefit]] while acknowledging that parents still judge visible food and housing.

## Key Characteristics

- Vassar finance officer translating access policy into revenue and endowment constraints.
- Emphasizes the financial importance of a limited pool of full-paying students.
- Treats complaints about food and housing as real but not the college's principal mission.
- Argues that tax exemption creates a public-benefit obligation for wealthy colleges.
- Presents financial fragility as the cost of mission choice rather than simple mismanagement.

## Evidence

### Revenue structure

- [[food-fight-food-fight]] attributes to Walton and Vassar's finance team an annual budget near $175 million, funded roughly two-thirds by fees and one-third by the endowment.

### Full-pay dependence

- [[food-fight-food-fight]] reports that losing even 50 of roughly 1,000 full-paying students would be financially damaging.

### Public obligation

- [[food-fight-food-fight]] attributes to Walton the view that tax-exempt colleges with large endowments owe a public benefit beyond educating wealthy students.

## Qualifications

The supplied source is a structured summary rather than audited financial statements, a complete interview transcript, or a study of endowment law and restrictions. Budget shares, thresholds, motives, and governance conclusions remain episode-scoped, and tax exemption alone does not determine a unique spending policy.

## What Changed

- Established Walton as the episode's principal interpreter of Vassar's financial fragility.
- Added an explicit public-benefit argument for endowment stewardship.
