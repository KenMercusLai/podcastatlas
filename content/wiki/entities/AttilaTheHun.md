## Overview

阿提拉 / Attila the Hun appears in the wiki through Hanji 764's qualified long-range comparison between [[NorthernXiongnuLateHan|Northern Xiongnu]] displacement, European Huns, Gothic movement, and pressure on the [[RomanEmpire|Roman Empire]]. The source presents Attila as the third and most culturally remembered Hunnic invasion figure, while explicitly leaving the Northern-Xiongnu-to-Hun identity chain as a source-scoped association.

## Current Profile

Attila's current profile is not biographical completeness but memory function. The episode describes him as the leader under whom the Huns launch a major pressure cycle against the Western Roman world, earning the later nickname "Scourge of God" in the source's framing. The source says plague prevents complete occupation in one campaign, then records Attila's death in 453 and the dissolution of Hunnic power amid internal struggle and Eastern Roman counterattack.

The episode also uses Attila to show how historical memory migrates into literature. Attila and the Huns become an enduring European narrative of invasion, revenge, and terror. The page therefore connects Attila to the wiki's migration-cascade concept without treating Dou Xian's victory as direct proof of Attila's origin.

## Key Characteristics

- Hunnic leader used as the remembered European endpoint of the episode's migration-cascade narrative.
- Roman-pressure figure whose campaigns are framed as part of a broader Gothic and Hunnic destabilization sequence.
- Cultural-memory figure associated with terror, revenge, and the "Scourge of God" label in the source account.
- Chronology marker whose death in 453 precedes the collapse of Hunnic political unity in the episode.
- Evidentiary caveat node because his link back to Northern Xiongnu displacement is qualified rather than settled.

## Qualifications

This page is source-bounded to Hanji 764 and does not reconstruct Attila's full historical career. The connection between Attila's Huns and earlier Northern Xiongnu displacement is retained as a qualified source association, not a settled ethnogenesis or direct-causation claim.
