# Indulgents

## Overview

The Indulgents were the late-1793 revolutionary faction associated with [[GeorgesDanton|Georges Danton]], [[CamilleDesmoulins|Camille Desmoulins]], moderation, peace, and clemency.

## Current Profile

The faction emerged as military recovery and the excesses of repression made continuation of terror harder to justify. Danton favored winding down emergency coercion, while Desmoulins used *[[LeVieuxCordelier|Le Vieux Cordelier]]* to demand a Committee of Clemency. Their position did not make them detached liberals: Danton had participated in earlier emergency violence, and the newspaper initially attacked the Hébertists. Once Hébert's faction was eliminated, however, the governing committees treated Indulgent criticism as a conspiratorial threat and executed its central figures.

## Key Characteristics

- It formed around opposition to continuing terror rather than opposition to the Revolution.
- Danton supplied political stature while Desmoulins supplied public journalism.
- It advocated clemency, peace, and stable government.
- Its members remained vulnerable through earlier violence, corruption, and factional association.
- Its destruction removed a prominent moderate alternative inside the revolutionary camp.

## Evidence

### Clemency program

- [[710-the-terror-death-at-the-guillotine-part-4-glt5038102951]] links Danton's return, Desmoulins's newspaper, and the Committee of Clemency proposal to the Indulgent label.

### Elimination

- [[710-the-terror-death-at-the-guillotine-part-4-glt5038102951]] follows failed reconciliation, arrest, constructed conspiracy, truncated trial, and execution of the faction's leaders.

## Qualifications

- “Moderate” is relational: Danton's earlier conduct and the faction's initial attack on Hébert prevent a simple civil-libertarian profile.
- The source does not establish a disciplined party, full membership list, or unified program.
- Motives, private negotiations, and the balance among peace, self-protection, and principle remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- A canonical profile now distinguishes revolutionary clemency politics from both Hébertist escalation and counterrevolution.

## Relationships

- [[GeorgesDanton]] - principal political figure associated with the faction.
- [[CamilleDesmoulins]] - journalist whose clemency campaign articulated its public case.
- [[LeVieuxCordelier]] - publication through which its criticism reached a wide audience.
- [[Hebertists]] - radical faction eliminated immediately before the government turned against the Indulgents.
- [[RevolutionaryPurgeSecurityDilemma]] - dynamic intensified when both flanks were destroyed.
