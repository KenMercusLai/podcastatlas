# flomo

## Overview
flomo is presented as a note-taking product co-founded by [[ShaonanFlomo|少楠]], with an emerging emphasis on voluntary personal context, reflection, and care rather than only generic knowledge management.

## Current Profile
The episode describes flomo's strategic premise as a response to falling retrieval costs. When generic facts and quotations can be searched or regenerated, the more differentiated record is why something mattered to a person, what changed, how they felt, and what followed. flomo's reported direction therefore moves the “second brain” metaphor away from storing the external world and toward preserving enough self-authored context for a person—and potentially an AI assistant—to notice patterns across lived experience.

## Key Characteristics
- Centers low-friction, voluntary recording of thoughts, events, states, and emotions.
- Distinguishes personal context from generic knowledge that can be retrieved on demand.
- Treats active recording as an attention choice, not merely automated behavioral capture.
- Explores AI pattern recognition and emotional support grounded in accumulated records.
- Frames care and self-understanding as product goals alongside or instead of efficiency.

## Evidence
### Personal-context direction
- [[lgvsxbt7tstj2vhxupcrxnrf3g4v-lgvsxbt7tstj2vhxupcrxnrf3g4v]] reports that flomo's user research found personal thought, mood, and state records becoming prominent while knowledge-management records declined.

### Voluntary record quality
- [[lgvsxbt7tstj2vhxupcrxnrf3g4v-lgvsxbt7tstj2vhxupcrxnrf3g4v]] distinguishes active recording, which spends attention and signals salience, from automatically collected behavioral data.

### AI and care
- [[lgvsxbt7tstj2vhxupcrxnrf3g4v-lgvsxbt7tstj2vhxupcrxnrf3g4v]] describes using sufficient personal context to identify patterns and return situated feedback, while asking whether an agent should care about fatigue rather than efficiency alone.

The supplied summary does not establish the size or representativeness of the reported survey, the accuracy of AI-inferred patterns, or the product's current and future implementation.
