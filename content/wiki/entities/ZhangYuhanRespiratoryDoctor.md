## Current Profile
The supplied source presents Zhang as a clinical explainer who separates everyday respiratory labels from diagnoses. She uses anatomy, symptom patterns, imaging, laboratory testing, overall condition, and change over time to distinguish upper and lower respiratory disease, colds and influenza, pneumonia and lung infection, chronic sputum, asthma variants, and extensive lung opacity.

Her pediatric contribution focuses on mycoplasma pneumonia. She identifies persistent high fever, severe cough, breathing effort, reduced exercise tolerance, and possible small-airway obstruction as reasons for assessment or follow-up, while rejecting prophylactic azithromycin and blind pathogen guessing. She also treats ordinary care settings, qualified pediatric capacity, and household prevention as part of respiratory care.

## Key Characteristics

- Connects pediatric mycoplasma pneumonia to severity recognition, follow-up, and small-airway complications.

- Frames masks, ventilation, hand hygiene, cough etiquette, and household cleaning as complementary precautions.
