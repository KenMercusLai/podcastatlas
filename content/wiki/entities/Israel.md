# Israel

## Overview
Israel is a state whose wiki profile spans Jewish national self-determination, Palestinian displacement and occupation, domestic coalition politics, regional war, alliance management, and modern defense pressure.

## Current Profile
Across the bounded sources, Israel appears simultaneously as a security-seeking state exposed to Hamas, Hezbollah, Iranian missiles and drones, and regional deal fragility; as an electoral democracy whose coalitions, Haredi conscription dispute, and post-October-7 trauma shape policy; and as the dominant power governing unequal Palestinian territorial and legal regimes. The new historical source adds the Nakba, return-right asymmetry, Zionist demographic logic, and one-state equal-rights critique, but its explicitly pro-Palestinian perspective does not erase the later sources' security, diplomatic, and domestic-political frames.

## Key Characteristics
- Security policy is shaped by armed threats from Hamas, Hezbollah, Iran, missiles, drones, and fear of strategic surprise.
- Domestic decisions reflect coalition dependence, election timing, far-right settlement politics, conscription, and post-October-7 trauma.
- Relations with Palestinians combine Gaza war and blockade, West Bank settlement expansion, fragmented territory, and differentiated legal status.
- Regional diplomacy is vulnerable when Israel is outside negotiations or distrusts enforcement against adjacent armed groups.
- U.S. support remains powerful but politically contested across parties, candidates, and changing alliance expectations.
- The state's Jewish-national character creates unresolved conflict over refugees, demographic majority, equality, and the boundary between Zionism and Jewish identity.

## Qualifications
The sources differ sharply in date, scope, and viewpoint. Several preserve analyst or guest claims rather than verified event history. The 2021 episode foregrounds Palestinian rights and underdevelops Israeli security reasoning and Hamas violence, while later Economist and interview sources foreground implementation, elections, alliances, or military threats. No single source supplies a comprehensive legal or historical account.

## What Changed
- Added 1948 displacement and Palestinian return rights to a profile previously centered on recent war, elections, settlements, diplomacy, and defense.
- Added the distinction between Jewish identity and Zionist state policy.
- Added differentiated Palestinian legal status and territorial fragmentation as structural features requiring explicit qualification of Israel's democratic profile.
- Preserved competing one-state equal-rights and two-state prescriptions without selecting a settled solution.

## Relationships
- [[Palestinians]] - people whose displacement, citizenship, residence, land, and political rights are central to the state conflict.
- [[Hamas]] - armed and governing rival in Gaza whose disarmament is tied to Israeli withdrawal.
- [[Gaza]] - blockaded and war-damaged territory central to security, humanitarian, and governance disputes.
- [[WestBank]] - territory where settlements, outposts, mobility, and land control create escalating conflict.
- [[Iran]] - regional military adversary connected to nuclear, missile, drone, and proxy threats.
- [[Hezbollah]] - armed movement whose capacity drives Israeli operations and ceasefire skepticism in Lebanon.
- [[Zionism]] - national project connecting Jewish statehood to demographic and territorial controversy.
- [[AntisemitismIsraelCriticismBoundary]] - distinction between criticism of Israeli policy and collective hostility toward Jews.
