# Alan Castel

## Overview
Alan Castel is the psychology and cognitive-aging guest in the [[HubermanLab]] source note on memory, learning, and successful aging.

## Current Profile
In [[how-to-improve-your-memory-cognitive-function-at-any-age-dr-alan-castel-scim5781519002]], Castel presents memory as selective and reconstructive rather than camera-like. His practical emphasis is active: people remember better when they notice deliberately, try retrieval before checking, make informative errors, use names or routes in context, and connect learning to curiosity or personal relevance.

His aging profile is equally selective. The episode has him distinguish normal repetition or source-memory decline from pathological repetition, connect walking and balance to later-life function, and frame aging through attitude, adapting, balance, connection, purpose, social contact, resilience, and wisdom rather than simple youth preservation.

## Key Characteristics
- Explains memory through reconstruction, metacognition, source memory, eyewitness error, and selective attention.
- Uses ordinary examples such as names, the Apple logo, hotel exit maps, photo taking, emergency routines, and repeated stories to make memory limits concrete.
- Treats mistakes, active search, retrieval practice, curiosity, and corrective feedback as central learning conditions.
- Frames cognitive aging as uneven, with losses in some memory systems but potential gains in selectivity, emotional regulation, wisdom, and resilience.
- Keeps later-life advice broad and behavioral: movement, balance, purpose, connection, and built-in challenge matter more than a single protocol.

## Evidence
