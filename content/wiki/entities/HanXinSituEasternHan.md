## Overview

韩歆 / Han Xin (Eastern Han Situ) is currently synthesized as a Guangwu-era high official whose appointment follows the post-unification personnel settlement and whose blunt disaster warning later becomes a lethal remonstrance case. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-715-shishang-zui-zhihui-de-yici-duoquan-loqkawl247maw9fn-nomaw5gzkgu|Hanji 715]] places his Da Situ appointment in the 37 CE order; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-716-luanshi-zhizhong-lvfa-you-duo-zhongyao-lqxuinbrl-rop8d9yxgwx9z4fr4i|Hanji 716]] then develops the remonstrance-death case. The route is disambiguated from [[HanXin|韩信]], the Chu-Han general.

## Current Profile

Hanji 715 supplies the office-entry layer: [[LiuXiu|Liu Xiu]] appoints Han Xin as Da Situ in the same 37 CE settlement that also rewards founding merit and regularizes postwar authority. Hanji 716 then shows the danger of that high office. In Jianwu 15 / 39 CE, Han Xin serves as Da Situ and is known in the episode for direct, unguarded speech. He warns Liu Xiu in severe terms that the realm will face major famine. Liu Xiu removes him from office and sends him home, then dispatches an envoy with a rebuking edict.

The lethal point is not a formal trial as currently preserved, but the pressure created by imperial anger. Han Xin and his son Han Ying become frightened and desperate enough to kill themselves. The public reaction is hostile because Han Xin has high reputation and no concrete crime is shown in the source. Liu Xiu then grants money and grain to the Han household and gives Han Xin full burial honors, making the case an early Guangwu qualification inside [[DirectRemonstranceCriminalization|direct remonstrance criminalization]].

## Key Characteristics

- Eastern Han Da Situ appointed in the 37 CE post-unification personnel settlement.
- High official whose office removal follows blunt famine prediction before Liu Xiu.
- Direct-speaking official whose warning is treated as intolerable enough to trigger imperial reprimand.
- Lethal remonstrance case where dismissal and rebuke, not a developed formal sentence, produce suicide.
- High-reputation actor whose death creates public dissatisfaction and posthumous appeasement.
- Source case qualifying Guangwu's otherwise positive image as a ruler able to accept criticism.

## Qualifications

This page is built from two source notes and preserves a spelling caution: the Hanji 716 transcript writes 韩鑫, while the canonical route uses 韩歆 for the Eastern Han Situ context. The sources do not show a formal conviction or execution order, so the durable claim is narrower than a legal prosecution: imperial dismissal and reprimand created fatal pressure after blunt advice. Hanji 715 supplies appointment context but not a full biography. Han Ying remains source-scoped until more evidence warrants a page.
