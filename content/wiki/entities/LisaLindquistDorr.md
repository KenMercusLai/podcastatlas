# Lisa Lindquist Dorr

## Overview

Lisa Lindquist Dorr is a historian whose study of Virginia cases involving accusations by white women against Black men supplies the episode's evidence for Jim Crow's variable enforcement.

## Current Profile

[[hitler-s-olympics-part-8-vater-it-is-to-be-fayetteville-hitler-s-olympics-part-8-vater-it-is-to-be-fayetteville|Hitler’s Olympics, Part 8]] attributes to Dorr a study of 288 Virginia cases from 1900 to 1960. The reported outcomes ranged from extralegal killing, execution, and maximum prison sentences to short sentences, acquittal, pardon, or parole; the last group was the largest.

The episode uses her interpretation to reject a picture of Jim Crow as one mechanically applied rule. White authorities could invoke racial hierarchy, gender authority, labor needs, local testimony, and selective leniency while preserving white control. The William Harper case, in which white witnesses contradicted an accusation and a death sentence gave way to acquittal, makes that flexibility concrete.

## Key Characteristics

- Historian of race, gender, sexuality, and law in the American South.
- Uses case outcomes to examine how racial hierarchy operated in practice.
- Emphasizes variation without denying the governing structure of white supremacy.
- Connects discretionary enforcement to Jim Crow's social and economic durability.

## Qualifications

This profile is limited to the episode's compressed account of one body of research. The numerical categories and Harper narrative have not been checked here against Dorr's publication, case records, or a complete Virginia legal history. Variable outcomes do not establish equal justice or weaken the evidence of systemic racial domination.
