## Overview

樊英 / Fan Ying appears in this wiki as a late Eastern Han Nanyang recluse whose high reputation, repeated office refusal, and disappointing visible contribution become a test case for judging reclusion. He is not treated as a simple fraud or sage: the current evidence makes him a boundary case where fame, virtue, office capacity, and public duty have to be separated.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-808-ta-jiujing-shi-mingjia-haishi-pianzi-lhmsw7zusdkqddk-zu27oj6hfs2m|Hanji 808]] presents Fan Ying as a renowned figure from [[NanyangCommandery|南阳郡]] who lives in seclusion, is repeatedly recommended, and refuses summons on illness grounds. Local authorities eventually force him toward [[LuoyangLateHan|洛阳]], while the court continues to treat him as a prized hidden worthy through medical attention, gifts, a lecture platform, teacher ritual, policy consultation, and offices such as Wuguan Zhonglangjiang and Guanglu Dafu.

That treatment does not settle his quality. The episode says listeners were disappointed by his answers, and Zhang Kai's criticism makes the case sharper: someone with such a name should either enter office to assist the ruler and save the people, or withdraw cleanly rather than hover between reputation and service. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-809-weishenme-quan-ni-buyao-luan-pai-mapi-lnv2l-ehkzmf2ifz3semscaqftku|Hanji 809]] then uses Fan Ying as the cautionary example in [[LiGuLateHan|李固]]'s warning to [[HuangQiongLateHan|黄琼]] that high reputation becomes dangerous when it cannot be matched by public contribution.

## Key Characteristics

- Nanyang recluse with a strong moral and scholarly reputation before entering the court's attention.
- Repeatedly refuses summons, making his public identity depend on withdrawal rather than office achievement.
- Becomes a target of coercive recruitment despite the ruler's simultaneous use of courtesy and honors.
- Receives high-status treatment and offices without becoming a clear source of durable governance repair.
- Functions as a negative or at least cautionary comparison for Huang Qiong's later conversion of reputation into usable service.

## Evidence

- Reputation and summons: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-808-ta-jiujing-shi-mingjia-haishi-pianzi-lhmsw7zusdkqddk-zu27oj6hfs2m|Hanji 808]] says Fan Ying was famous from youth, lived hidden in Nanyang, and was repeatedly recommended by local and court channels.
- Coercive recruitment: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-808-ta-jiujing-shi-mingjia-haishi-pianzi-lhmsw7zusdkqddk-zu27oj6hfs2m|Hanji 808]] records illness-based refusals, local pressure, forced movement to Luoyang, and continued imperial attempts to convert him into service.
- Honor without clear performance: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-808-ta-jiujing-shi-mingjia-haishi-pianzi-lhmsw7zusdkqddk-zu27oj6hfs2m|Hanji 808]] says his answers disappointed observers even after elaborate courtesy and appointment.
- Later cautionary use: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-809-weishenme-quan-ni-buyao-luan-pai-mapi-lnv2l-ehkzmf2ifz3semscaqftku|Hanji 809]] makes Fan Ying the example of reputation that invites suspicion when it does not produce public contribution.

## Qualifications

The wiki should not overstate the verdict. [[SimaGuang|司马光]]'s standard in Hanji 808 allows principled withdrawal when politics is violent, the Way cannot be practiced, or service would only place an upright person among destructive courtiers. The source also leaves room for the host's partial sympathy: a sick court may require time even from a good doctor. Fan Ying is therefore a boundary case for [[ReclusionAuthenticityAssessment|真隐士与伪隐士辨别]], not a settled proof that he was a deliberate impostor.

There is also a chronology-label caution. The episode frames the passage around Shun's Yongjian opening but some wording names Emperor An; this page treats the case as part of the early [[EmperorShunOfHan|汉顺帝]] evidence because the source's chronology and neighboring Hanji 809 point there.

## What Changed

- Created Fan Ying as a standalone page because Hanji 808 supplies a complete summons-and-reclusion profile and Hanji 809 already used him as an important comparison.
- Distinguished Fan Ying's reputation, possible virtue, and visible governing contribution instead of reducing him to either sage or fraud.
- Added the Shun-era chronology-label caution from the source note.
