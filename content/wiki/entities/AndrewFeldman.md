# Andrew Feldman

## Overview
Andrew Feldman is the CEO of [[Cerebras]] and an All-In interview guest on AI inference, wafer-scale hardware, open-model sovereignty, data-center infrastructure, and chip geopolitics.

## Current Profile
Feldman's central argument is that AI product quality depends on elapsed inference time as well as model capability. Deep research, coding, guardrails, and recursive reasoning trigger many serial queries, so faster serving can change what users attempt rather than merely make an existing task finish sooner.

His broader infrastructure view is system-level: calculation, memory, and transport interact with power, cooling, construction, grid policy, and community consent. He is optimistic that AI demand remains early, but treats memory shortages, power delivery, and geopolitical policy as near-term constraints.

## Key Characteristics
- Advocates wafer-scale, SRAM-rich hardware for low-latency inference.
- Treats deep research and agent workflows as cascades of smaller inference calls.
- Frames power and memory as constraints alongside raw calculation speed.
- Connects open models and hardware diversity to enterprise and national sovereignty.
- Supports data-center community investment and ratepayer protection as part of deployment.
- Takes a strongly optimistic view of long-run AI demand while acknowledging job displacement and supply constraints.

## Qualifications
Backlog, speed, purchase-order, megawatt, architecture, memory-market, and demand claims are company-executive statements rather than independently benchmarked results. Feldman's geopolitical and administration comparisons are opinions. Faster inference can improve usability without proving lower total cost, better answers, or general superiority across workloads.
