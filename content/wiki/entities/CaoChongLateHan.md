## Overview

[[CaoChongLateHan]] is a favored young son of [[CaoCao|曹操]] whose death at thirteen becomes both a family tragedy and a revealing succession event in Hanji 1111.

## Current Profile

The source identifies Cao Chong through the well-known elephant-weighing story but centers on his terminal illness and death. Cao Cao reportedly prays for his recovery and later regrets the killing of Hua Tuo, imagining that the physician might have saved him. That regret establishes the intensity of Cao Cao's grief, not the illness or the viability of a cure.

The political weight appears in Cao Cao's statement to [[CaoPi|曹丕]] that the death is his own misfortune but benefits the other sons. Cao Pi is also reported to say later that the realm might not have come to him had Cao Chong lived. Together these remarks support Cao Chong as a serious potential contender created by paternal preference, while stopping short of proving formal designation.

## Key Characteristics

- Favored child whose early intelligence and paternal attachment give him unusual family standing.
- Potential succession contender inferred from Cao Cao's and Cao Pi's reported remarks rather than a documented appointment.
- Early death whose political effect benefits surviving brothers even as it devastates Cao Cao.
- Posthumous subject of a proposed marriage that [[BingYuanLateHan|邴原]] blocks on ritual grounds.

## Qualifications

The current evidence is a short popular-history source note. It does not identify Cao Chong's illness, establish that Hua Tuo could have saved him, or document Cao Chong as a formally designated heir. The elephant-weighing story is identifying context rather than independently examined evidence here.
