# 刘晔 / Liu Ye (late Han)

## Overview
刘晔 / Liu Ye is a late-Han strategist whose bounded profile combines decisive crisis violence, resource-aware troop refusal, accurate diagnosis of Sun Ce's Lujiang lure, and a later timing warning after Cao Cao's conquest of Hanzhong.

## Current Profile
The current synthesis presents Liu Ye as a strategist with unusually clear cost judgment. In Hanji 1037, he preempts [[ZhengBaoLateHan|郑宝]] by killing him at a banquet, then refuses to inherit Zheng Bao's thousands because undisciplined armed followers without food and money would become a liability. In Hanji 1038, he correctly reads [[SunCe|孙策]]'s gifts and humility as bait and warns [[LiuXunLateHan|刘勋]] that leaving [[LujiangCommandery|庐江郡]] for Shangliao will expose the rear.

Hanji 1153 extends the pattern from opponent diagnosis to time-sensitive advice under Cao Cao. After [[HanzhongCampaign215|曹操西征汉中]] succeeds, Liu Ye argues that Liu Bei's newly conquered Yizhou is politically unsettled and will become more defensible once Zhuge Liang organizes government and Guan Yu and Zhang Fei secure the approaches. When Cao Cao asks again seven days later, Liu Ye reportedly says the window has closed. The episode uses his continued argument to contrast Sima Yi's greater caution around the ruler, but the exact exchange, feasibility of invasion, and career consequences remain source-scoped.

## Key Characteristics
- Acts decisively when Zheng Bao threatens to seize and use his reputation.
- Uses Cao Cao's envoy as an authority signal to control Zheng Bao's followers after the killing.
- Separates troop headcount from usable capacity, refusing followers he cannot feed or discipline.
- Recognizes Sun Ce's apparent deference as strategic bait rather than sincere help.
- Connects campaign tempo to rear-base exposure and food constraints.
- Fails institutionally because Liu Xun receives his followers but later ignores his warning.
- Treats political consolidation as a changing military variable and persists after initial rejection, linking delay to both operational and advisory risk.

- Integration-cost judgment: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1037-zhongyu-caocao-or-liubiao-jiemi-hansong-de-zhongyi-jueze-lomrbxffcu6euantozh2fjvrykva|Hanji 1037]] has Liu Ye reject command of Zheng Bao's thousands because they are undisciplined and he lacks the economic base to supply them.
- Strategic diagnosis: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1038-sanshiliu-ji-zhi-diaohu-lishan-sunce-zhiqu-wancheng-lhomk8hfdidl61degwbqtj2xkj9z|Hanji 1038]] has Liu Ye warn that Shangliao is hard to capture and that Sun Ce may attack Liu Xun's rear once the main army leaves.
- Deception recognition: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1038-sanshiliu-ji-zhi-diaohu-lishan-sunce-zhiqu-wancheng-lhomk8hfdidl61degwbqtj2xkj9z|Hanji 1038]] makes Liu Ye the figure who sees through the gift-and-humility performance at the center of the [[LureEnemyOutOfPosition|调虎离山]] operation.
- Hanzhong aftermath: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1153-liubei-jie-jingzhou-qishi-shi-chang-zhanlue-pianju-lrbfbssflrnxr5xjeuvgxhxbvxxt|Hanji 1153]] attributes to Liu Ye both the immediate-invasion argument and the later judgment that Yizhou's panic had passed.

## Qualifications
The transcript renders the name as "刘业" in Hanji 1038; the wiki normalizes this to 刘晔 / Liu Ye while preserving the source variant in source-note prose. Hanji 1153's seven-day sequence, dialogue, political-risk comparison, and prediction of rapid Yizhou stabilization require textual and operational comparison; correct timing diagnosis does not prove that Cao Cao could have conquered the region. The current page remains bounded to the Zheng Bao, Liu Xun, Lujiang-warning, and Hanzhong-aftermath branches, not Liu Ye's full career.

## What Changed
- Added the Hanzhong aftermath as a second warning case centered on consolidation speed and opportunity decay.
- Distinguished Liu Ye's timing judgment from proof that an immediate Yizhou invasion was feasible.
