## Overview

袁敞 / Yuan Chang is the An-era Yuan-family official whose profile now begins with his promotion from guangluxun to sikong before his rigid independence from the Deng-family power center becomes vulnerable after household correspondence leaks court secrets.

## Current Profile

Hanji 788 adds Yuan Chang's office-entry layer. In the same sequence where [[LiuKaiEasternHan|Liu Kai]] moves from sikong to situ, Yuan Chang is appointed sikong from guangluxun. The source gives little interpretation at this point, but it places him among the senior court officeholders operating inside the Deng-regency crisis field.

Hanji 789 then presents Yuan Chang as an upright but politically stiff official. He is placed near his father Yuan An's rank and does not flatter the [[DengFamilyAnEraLateHan|Deng family]], which makes [[EmpressDowagerDengLateHan|Empress Dowager Deng]] reluctant to rely on him even though his lineage and office are weighty.

The fatal case comes from his household. Yuan Chang's son exchanges private letters with [[ZhangJunAnEraLateHan|Zhang Jun]], a shangshulang, and the letters leak high-level court secrets. Yuan Chang is removed from office and, fearing humiliation, kills himself. Zhang Jun is nearly executed but gains sympathy after a plea and is spared. The court later reduces Yuan Chang's offense, hides the suicide in public treatment, restores honor, and gives him a funeral appropriate to a high official.

The episode connects this posthumous handling to later politics. It reads the Yuan family's "four generations, three excellencies" reputation as a background asset for later figures such as [[YuanShao|Yuan Shao]] and [[YuanShu|Yuan Shu]], while still treating Yuan Chang's immediate case as a warning about moral posture without household-information discipline.

## Key Characteristics

- High-ranking Runan Yuan official whose status comes from lineage, office, and moral reputation.
- Moves from guangluxun to sikong before the later court-secret case defines his wiki profile.
- Refuses to ingratiate himself with Deng-family power, preserving dignity but reducing protection.
- Falls through a household correspondence leak rather than direct policy failure.
- Receives posthumous mitigation, showing that court reputation can recover part of a disgraced official's public standing.
- Serves as a prehistory layer for Yuan Shao and Yuan Shu's later family-prestige capital.

## Qualifications

The page is based on two source notes and does not attempt a full Runan Yuan lineage history. Hanji 788 gives an office appointment, while Hanji 789 gives the substantive political case. The practical synthesis remains bounded: Yuan Chang's case links high office, moral independence, household communication risk, and reputation repair under the Deng regency.
