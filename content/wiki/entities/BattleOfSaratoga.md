# Battle of Saratoga

## Overview

The Battle of Saratoga is the 1777 American victory ending in Burgoyne's surrender and helping persuade France that the rebellion was viable enough for open alliance.

## Current Profile

The episode places Saratoga inside a failed British Hudson strategy. Howe moved toward Philadelphia rather than coordinating a northward advance from New York, while Burgoyne moved south from Quebec with a slow, baggage-heavy force. Failed attacks near Bemis Heights left him isolated and undersupplied, and his surrender on 17 October turned inland operational failure into an international political signal.

## Key Characteristics

- Culmination of Britain's failed effort to control the Hudson Valley and isolate New England.
- Campaign shaped by distance, terrain, baggage, supply, and divided command priorities.
- American force associated in the episode with [[HoratioGates|Horatio Gates]] and New England militia.
- Followed failed British attacks on 19 September and 7 October 1777.
- Ended with Burgoyne's surrender on 17 October.
- Strengthened the case for open French intervention.

## Evidence

- Strategic design: [[349-the-birth-of-the-united-states-part-3-glt9804967639]] describes the intended convergence of Howe from New York and Burgoyne from Quebec along the Hudson corridor.
- Coordination failure: [[349-the-birth-of-the-united-states-part-3-glt9804967639]] says Howe concentrated on Philadelphia while Burgoyne advanced slowly with a large baggage train.
- Battlefield outcome: [[349-the-birth-of-the-united-states-part-3-glt9804967639]] reports failed British attacks near Bemis Heights, worsening supply and weather, and surrender after relief did not arrive.
- Diplomatic effect: [[349-the-birth-of-the-united-states-part-3-glt9804967639]] connects the victory to European confidence in the rebellion and France's open entry into the war.
