# Shibusawa Eiichi

## Overview

Shibusawa Eiichi is the Meiji business organizer described in [[277-japan-samurai-and-shoguns-glt3851919583]] as a leading architect of Japanese capitalism.

## Current Profile

Shibusawa helped normalize the joint-stock company and participated in a wide business network during Japan's rapid response to Western pressure. The episode also attributes to him a Confucian ideal of cooperation among investors, managers, and workers. [[MeijiCapitalismImperialEntanglement]] qualifies the modernization story because his opportunities and profits also extended into colonized Korea.

## Key Characteristics

- Promoted joint-stock corporate organization in Meiji Japan.
- Connected industrial development to national defense and catch-up with Western powers.
- Sought to give capitalism a Confucian ethical frame.
- Participated in a broad network of companies and infrastructure projects.
- Benefited from economic opportunities created by Japanese imperial expansion.

## Evidence

- Corporate development: [[277-japan-samurai-and-shoguns-glt3851919583]] links Shibusawa to joint-stock diffusion and firms including Sapporo, Tokyo Electric Light, and Nippon Railway.
- Ethical framing: [[277-japan-samurai-and-shoguns-glt3851919583]] describes cooperation rather than pure shareholder antagonism as his stated ideal.
- Imperial connection: [[277-japan-samurai-and-shoguns-glt3851919583]] notes his gains from Korea after Japanese annexation.

## Qualifications

The “father of Japanese capitalism” label, company counts, personal influence, ethical intent, and Korean profit claims remain episode-scoped. The source does not establish whether Confucian rhetoric reliably governed actual labor or colonial practice.

## What Changed

- Created a profile that keeps corporate institution-building and imperial benefit in the same assessment.
