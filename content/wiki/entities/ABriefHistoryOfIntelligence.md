# A Brief History of Intelligence

## Overview
*A Brief History of Intelligence* is [[MaxBennett]]'s book used in the wiki to connect biological intelligence, reinforcement learning, simulation, mentalizing, language, and AI-era self-understanding. It appears first as a technical-culture reading recommendation and then as E46's scaffold for a relationship and Buddhist-inflected cognition essay.

## Current Profile
The book's wiki role has broadened. In the Neihe Konghuang source, it helps compare biological intelligence stages with current AI limits, especially [[WorldModels]] and mentalizing. In E46, [[MengYan]] rereads the same evolutionary arc as a "natural history" of the mechanisms that make people fail to see one another: valuation, wanting, predictive simulation, social projection, and language compression.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames intelligence as an evolutionary progression rather than only a contemporary AI capability set.
- Connects biological learning, simulation, and mentalizing to present AI questions.
- Gives E46 a five-layer structure for understanding how adaptive cognition can create suffering and misrecognition.
- Supports a bridge between AI-world-model discussions and human relationship perception.

- Model-boundary example: [[biancheng-de-neiranji-shidai-neihe-konghuang-71-1-71-1]] describes [[WuTao]] asking [[ChatGPT]] where it would place itself in the book's progression, with the answer treated as interesting but not proof of genuine mentalizing.

## Qualifications
The wiki's E46 reading is Meng Yan's interpretation of the book, not necessarily Bennett's own framing. The book page should keep AI-model comparison and interpersonal misrecognition distinct unless a source explicitly joins them.
