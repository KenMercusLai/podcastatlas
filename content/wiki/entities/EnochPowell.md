# Enoch Powell

## Overview
Enoch Powell appears as a former Conservative frontbencher whose anti-immigration notoriety, free-market opposition to [[EdwardHeath|Edward Heath]], and hostility to European integration gave him unusual leverage in the [[February1974UKGeneralElection|February 1974 election]].

## Current Profile
[[418-britain-in-1974-the-crisis-election-part-2-glt9690615062]] presents Powell as an insurgent attacking Heath from the right. Sacked after his 1968 “Rivers of Blood” speech, he also opposed Heath's wage and price controls and believed European Community membership threatened parliamentary sovereignty. Days before polling, he declined to stand again as a Conservative and urged voters to support [[LabourPartyUK|Labour]] because it promised a referendum on Europe.

## Key Characteristics
- Combined intellectual authority and populist appeal with inflammatory anti-immigration politics.
- Opposed Heath personally after his dismissal from the shadow cabinet.
- Rejected Heathite corporatism and income controls from a free-market position.
- Treated European integration as a sovereignty threat.
- Used a late campaign intervention to weaken his former party and elevate Labour's referendum pledge.

## Evidence
- Conservative rupture: [[418-britain-in-1974-the-crisis-election-part-2-glt9690615062]] connects Powell's hostility to Heath's decision to sack him after the 1968 speech.
- Economic difference: [[418-britain-in-1974-the-crisis-election-part-2-glt9690615062]] contrasts Powell's free-market politics with Heath's corporatist wage and price controls.
- Electoral intervention: [[418-britain-in-1974-the-crisis-election-part-2-glt9690615062]] says Powell urged voters to reject the Conservatives and vote Labour to secure a referendum on Europe.

## Qualifications
The profile is limited to Powell's role in one election episode. It does not assess his full political career, the wider reception and consequences of his immigration rhetoric, or independently measure how many votes his intervention changed.
