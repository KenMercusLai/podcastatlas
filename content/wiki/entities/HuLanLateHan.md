## Overview
Hu Lan is the Guiyang bandit leader whose alliance with [[ZhuGaiLateHan|Zhu Gai]] expands the 165 CE Jingzhou rebellion. His force pressures [[GuiyangCommanderyLateHan|Guiyang]] and [[LinglingCommanderyLateHan|Lingling]], then his remnants become the hinge of the later [[ZhangPanLateHan|Zhang Pan]] false-accusation case.

## Current Profile
Hu Lan functions as the connective rebel actor between local banditry, regional siege warfare, and inter-provincial blame. The setup layer has Hu Lan joining Zhu Gai against Guiyang, forcing Ren Yin's flight, and moving pressure toward Lingling. At Lingling, his side attempts to use diverted water against the city, but [[ChenQiuLateHan|Chen Qiu]] drains the water out and turns the flood against the attackers.

After [[DuShangLateHan|Du Shang]] defeats the main rebel force, Hu Lan's remnants flee to [[CangwuCommanderyLateHan|Cangwu]], where Zhang Pan suppresses them. That remnant branch later becomes politically important because Du Shang can misreport the follow-on suppression.

## Key Characteristics
- Guiyang bandit leader whose force combines with a Jingzhou soldier rebellion.
- Attack participant whose force helps trigger Ren Yin's flight from Guiyang.
- Siege opponent defeated by Chen Qiu's improvisational Lingling defense.
- Residual-security actor whose remnants create the opening for Du Shang's false report against Zhang Pan.

## Qualifications
The current sources give Hu Lan's role in the rebellion but not his background or political aims. His page should remain source-scoped unless later ingests add independent information about Guiyang bandit networks or his command structure.

## What Changed
- The profile now includes the Guiyang attack and Ren Yin flight setup before the Lingling and Cangwu branches.
- Hu Lan's remnants remain the factual hinge of the Du Shang-Zhang Pan accusation.
