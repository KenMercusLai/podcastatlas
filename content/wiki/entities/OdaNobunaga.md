# Oda Nobunaga

## Overview

Oda Nobunaga is the Sengoku military leader used in [[277-japan-samurai-and-shoguns-glt3851919583]] to represent the violent transition toward Japanese political reunification.

## Current Profile

The episode presents Nobunaga as the first of three major warrior unifiers. His rule joins military innovation and ruthless attacks on armed Buddhist movements with a samurai culture that also valued hierarchy, poetry, Noh, and Zen-inflected aesthetics. His unwillingness to abolish the imperial line illustrates [[JapaneseImperialLegitimacyContinuity]].

## Key Characteristics

- Pursued reunification during prolonged civil war.
- Treated armed religious organizations as political and military rivals.
- Embodied a samurai identity combining coercion with cultivated arts.
- Preserved the imperial institution as a source of legitimacy he could not safely replace.
- Died after betrayal by Akechi Mitsuhide.

## Evidence

- Unification and force: [[277-japan-samurai-and-shoguns-glt3851919583]] places Nobunaga first in the sequence leading to Tokugawa peace.
- Religious conflict: [[277-japan-samurai-and-shoguns-glt3851919583]] attributes his attacks on armed Buddhist groups to their mobilization, wealth, and military autonomy.
- Legitimacy constraint: [[277-japan-samurai-and-shoguns-glt3851919583]] says the imperial family's sacred ancestry made abolition politically dangerous.

## Qualifications

This profile is episode-bounded. The Protestant analogy for Jodo Shinshu, Nobunaga's motives, the balance between religious and political conflict, and the episode's account of suicide require broader specialist evidence.

## What Changed

- Created a profile connecting Nobunaga's coercive unification to samurai culture and imperial legitimacy.
