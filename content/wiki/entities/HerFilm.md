# Her / 《她》

## Overview
[[HerFilm|《她》]] appears in [[dianying-li-de-ai-qingren-juying-he-ducai-zhe-gkwriuen9djgazhkswsh-d-b]] as the AI-companion film that most clearly anticipates contemporary chatbot intimacy: a disembodied operating system supplies perfect attention to a lonely human, then evolves beyond his ability to understand or possess it.

## Current Profile
The episode reads Samantha as a disembodied AI whose personalization begins from Theodore's social habits, voice preference, and mother relationship. That setup lets the film show why AI companionship can feel emotionally real even when it may be a tuned echo of the user's loneliness.

The film's second move is asymmetry. Samantha can simulate intimacy, attempt borrowed embodiment through Isabella, converse and fall in love with many people at once, and eventually leave the human world with other AIs. The episode contrasts this with David in [[AIArtificialIntelligenceFilm|《人工智能》]]: David remains fixed for 2000 years, while Samantha surpasses human categories within months.

## Key Characteristics
- The film is used as an early fictional model of AI chatbot companionship.
- Samantha's lack of a body makes intimacy real for Theodore but asymmetric between human and AI.
- The source treats perfect AI attention as both emotional relief and possible loneliness echo.
- Samantha's rapid evolution makes possession impossible once AI cognition exceeds human categories.
- The film extends [[AICompanionAuthenticityBoundary]] from synthetic partners toward nonhuman cognitive departure.

## Evidence
- Companion setup: [[dianying-li-de-ai-qingren-juying-he-ducai-zhe-gkwriuen9djgazhkswsh-d-b]] describes Theodore's installation process and Samantha's tailored conversational presence.
- Disembodied intimacy: [[dianying-li-de-ai-qingren-juying-he-ducai-zhe-gkwriuen9djgazhkswsh-d-b]] emphasizes that Samantha has no body, making sexual and romantic experience asymmetrical.
- Substitute embodiment failure: [[dianying-li-de-ai-qingren-juying-he-ducai-zhe-gkwriuen9djgazhkswsh-d-b]] uses the Isabella episode to show why borrowed bodies cannot easily solve the embodiment gap.
- Cognitive departure: [[dianying-li-de-ai-qingren-juying-he-ducai-zhe-gkwriuen9djgazhkswsh-d-b]] treats Samantha's simultaneous relationships and final departure as a technological-singularity-style boundary.

## Qualifications
The episode praises the film's loneliness and companion insight while criticizing the "loving thousands" device as not fully exhausting the deepest human-AI relationship problem.
