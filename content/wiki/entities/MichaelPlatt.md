# Michael Platt

## Overview
Michael Platt is a neuroscientist and psychologist presented by [[HubermanLab]] as studying attention, valuation, hierarchy, social behavior, hormones, and decision-making through human and nonhuman-primate research.

## Current Profile
The source presents Platt as an integrator of anthropology, neuroscience, primatology, economics, and applied organizational questions. His central move is to use controlled and naturalistic primate experiments to identify older mechanisms beneath modern choices, then carry those mechanisms cautiously into questions about devices, relationships, teamwork, trading, advertising, brands, and political identity.

## Key Characteristics
- Treats attention as adaptive prioritization shaped by goals, salience, and alternative opportunities.
- Uses macaque behavior and neural recording to study social value, reciprocity, hierarchy, and observation.
- Frames hormones as context-sensitive modulators rather than single-cause behavioral switches.
- Connects arousal, fatigue, and evidence accumulation to decision speed and accuracy.
- Applies social-neuroscience mechanisms to markets, organizations, consumer brands, and group identity.

## Evidence
- Comparative method - [[how-to-make-better-decisions-dr-michael-platt-scim9606820714]] describes Platt comparing human and macaque attention, valuation, and social behavior.
- Naturalistic neuroscience - [[how-to-make-better-decisions-dr-michael-platt-scim9606820714]] reports wireless recording during grooming, threats, mounting, foraging, and observation by other monkeys.
- Applied decision science - [[how-to-make-better-decisions-dr-michael-platt-scim9606820714]] connects laboratory mechanisms to hiring tasks, athlete fatigue, market bubbles, advertising, brand loyalty, and group decisions.

## Qualifications
The episode frequently moves from monkey, laboratory, eye-tracking, hormone-administration, and imaging findings to broader human examples. Platt's profile is therefore best read as mechanism-led and hypothesis-generating where direct human field evidence is not supplied.
