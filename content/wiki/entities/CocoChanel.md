# Coco Chanel / 香奈儿女士

Coco Chanel / 香奈儿女士 appears in [[16-shishang-shouhaizhe-jing-luse-huo-wei-zui-du-yanse-559889282]] as a contrast case inside the episode's toxic-fashion history. The source says Chanel favored black-and-white simplicity, disliked green, and is discussed by the host as a designer whose clothing had feminist implications because it simplified dress and encouraged women to wear trousers.

The episode uses Chanel less as a luxury-brand case than as a body-mobility case. Trousers matter because women's dress codes can restrict movement, safety, and everyday activity; riding horses or bicycles becomes a route around rules that otherwise make [[FemaleBodyFreedom]] dependent on legal and social permission.

## Key Claims
- The source contrasts Chanel's simple black-and-white style with the episode's arsenic-green fashion examples.
- Chanel's trousers are framed as practical bodily freedom, not only design taste.
- The episode links women's clothing reform to movement, safety, and refusal of unnecessary decorative constraint.
