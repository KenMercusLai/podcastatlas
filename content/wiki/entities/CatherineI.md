# Catherine I / 叶卡捷琳娜一世

## Overview

Catherine I appears in the episode as a young captive, probably born Marta Skowronska, whose movement through Russian commanders' households during the [[GreatNorthernWar|Great Northern War]] leads to partnership and secret marriage with [[PeterTheGreat|Peter the Great]].

## Current Profile

The Russian capture of Marienburg brings the teenage Marta into the war's system of captivity and household transfer. The episode presents her early background as uncertain: probably a Lithuanian Catholic peasant, servant, and partner of a Swedish dragoon. She passes through the households of Boris Sheremetyev and Alexander Menshikov, converts to Orthodoxy, takes the name Catherine, and becomes Peter's mistress before their reported secret marriage in 1707.

Her rise is not framed as a simple romance or merit story. It begins in conquest, vulnerability, and sparse documentation, while also showing the extraordinary social mobility that war and proximity to the ruler could produce within a personalized court.

## Key Characteristics

- Her birth identity, family background, and early relationships remain uncertain.
- War captivity is the immediate condition of her entry into Russian elite households.
- Conversion and renaming mark movement into Peter's Orthodox court.
- Her relationship with Peter crosses extreme social distance.
- The episode's account ends at the secret-marriage stage rather than covering her later reign.

## Qualifications

The episode repeatedly marks Catherine's early biography as uncertain. Names, ethnicity, class position, relationships, sequence of household transfers, and marriage details are source-scoped, and this page does not yet synthesize her later role as empress.
