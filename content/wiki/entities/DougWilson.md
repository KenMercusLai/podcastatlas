# Doug Wilson

## Overview
Doug Wilson is a pastor and co-founder of the Communion of Reformed Evangelical Churches whom the source presents as a mentor admired by [[PeteHegseth]] and the central strategist of an effort to make Moscow, Idaho, a prototype Christian town.

## Current Profile
Wilson joins religious leadership, political argument, institutional construction, and media production. He says his desired Christian nation should be reached democratically, but the destination would privilege Christianity in law and officeholding: non-Christians could vote but not hold public office, while abortion, same-sex marriage, no-fault divorce, education, and household political authority would be reordered.

His influence is not limited to a pulpit. The episode connects his church network to schools, a college, publishing, businesses, housing, podcasts, events, and a growing population of adherents. It also reports severe teachings, abuse allegations, local backlash, and former-member estrangement alongside hospitality, family stability, and certainty described by current members.

## Key Characteristics
- Advocates an explicitly Christian United States while describing democratic action as the permitted route.
- Builds influence through a linked religious, educational, commercial, residential, and media ecosystem.
- Teaches patriarchal family authority, sex-differentiated roles, and opposition to abortion, same-sex marriage, and transgender identity.
- Uses [[CanonPress]] and online media to distribute doctrine, products, and culture-war messaging nationally.
- Treats hostile attention as capable of strengthening recruitment and movement identity.
- Has gained national relevance through proximity to Hegseth and a Pentagon prayer appearance.

## Qualifications
The profile rests on one investigative episode summarized from interviews rather than the denomination's governing documents, complete local demographic or business data, police and court records, or a systematic policy analysis. Wilson's democratic-method claim and his proposed political order are both recorded; neither should be omitted to make the program appear more liberal or more immediately coercive than the supplied evidence establishes. Reports of abuse, church response, movement size, and Hegseth's degree of policy influence require primary evidence beyond this source.
