# Edward Young Clarke

## Overview

Edward Young Clarke was a Southern Publicity Association marketer who, with [[ElizabethTyler]], converted [[WilliamJosephSimmons|William Joseph Simmons's]] small second [[KuKluxKlan]] into a commission-driven national recruitment operation.

## Current Profile

Clarke's role in the bounded source is organizational rather than doctrinal originality. He and Tyler divided the country into sales territories, hired recruiters called Klegels, used theatrical titles, identified local grievances, and retained most of the initiation revenue under their agreement with Simmons. The model fused ideology with incentives: every recruited member generated money for the chain and demand for official costume purchases.

The system marketed a flexible “100% Americanism” capable of emphasizing anti-Catholicism, antisemitism, nativism, Prohibition, or local moral policing according to the constituency being approached.

## Key Characteristics

- Co-led the Southern Publicity Association's Klan marketing campaign.
- Built territorial recruitment and commissioned Klegel networks.
- Used market segmentation to translate local resentments into a national enemy story.
- Helped make membership fees and regalia into a scalable revenue system.
- Demonstrates how professional sales infrastructure can accelerate extremist organization.

## Qualifications

The source spells his surname “Clark”; this page uses the established historical spelling Clarke. The episode does not provide business records sufficient to reconstruct all commissions, costs, revenue, or decision rights. Calling the arrangement pyramid-like describes recruitment incentives and revenue flow, not a legal finding under later statutory definitions.

## What Changed

- Created Clarke as the sales-system architect behind the second Klan's rapid growth.
- Separated scalable recruitment technique from Simmons's founding mythology.
