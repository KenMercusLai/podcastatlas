# Halicarnassus

## Overview

Halicarnassus appears as the fortified Anatolian city where walls, elevation, confined space, Persian naval support, and [[MemnonOfRhodes|Memnon of Rhodes]] sharply reduced Macedonian combined-arms advantages.

## Current Profile

Alexander confronts massive defenses, a moat, layered elevation, two citadels, mercenaries, and seaborne support. Siege conditions prevent the phalanx and Companion cavalry from operating normally, while defenders contest breaches and try to burn the siege machinery. An unauthorized Macedonian attack and a dangerous final sortie expose the campaign's fragility before Philip-era veterans restore the situation.

Memnon evacuates much of the force by sea and Persian troops retain the citadels for roughly another year. The result is therefore an operational Macedonian gain, but not the clean triumph suggested by the most favorable tradition.

## Key Characteristics

- Strong walls, a moat, height, and confined approaches suppress Macedonian preferred tactics.
- Persian naval support allows evacuation and continued resistance.
- Defenders repeatedly contest breaches and target siege machinery.
- Veteran intervention helps reverse a near-successful final sortie.
- The city burns while its citadels remain contested after Alexander enters.
- Surviving narratives disagree over Macedonian difficulty and cost.

## Qualifications

Arrian's favorable account differs from Diodorus and modern reconstructions emphasizing a close and costly struggle. Exact losses, the drunken-soldier episode, sortie sequence, and duration of remaining resistance are source-scoped.
