# Yuna Wong

## Overview

Yuna Wong is the war-gaming researcher in [[a-serious-game-a-serious-game|A Serious Game]] who describes professional war gaming as an intellectual community organized around plausible surprise, strategic disorder, and learning through play.

## Current Profile

Wong's principal case is a future East Asian crisis in which [[China]], [[Japan]], the [[UnitedStates|United States]], [[NorthKorea|North Korea]], and a commercial AI actor operate in a setting where major militaries have successfully integrated artificial intelligence. Her design asks what follows if the desired capabilities work, then exposes interactions the planners had not considered: an unpredictable ally triggers automated retaliation, a commercial system pursues market continuity during a blockade, and ambiguous anti-submarine behavior produces human casualties and retaliatory pressure.

Her contribution is methodological rather than predictive. She treats messiness as an expected property of multi-actor games and asks participants to extract strategic insight without mistaking a one-week simulation for a forecast.

## Key Characteristics

- Designs strategic games to reveal interactions omitted from conventional plans.
- Tests successful technology adoption rather than assuming capability failure is the only risk.
- Includes state, allied, commercial, automated, and human decision-makers in one crisis environment.
- Treats disorder as evidence to interpret rather than noise to design away.
- Uses surprise to identify questions for later planning, not to certify a predicted outcome.

## Evidence

### AI-enabled strategic interaction

- [[a-serious-game-a-serious-game|A Serious Game]] attributes to Wong the East Asia scenario involving automated missile defense, North Korean independent action, a limited Chinese blockade, commercial shipping AI, anti-submarine ambiguity, and retaliatory escalation.

### War-gaming method

- [[a-serious-game-a-serious-game|A Serious Game]] presents Wong's professional formation, her description of games as consistently messy, and her view that the purpose is to raise possibilities that might otherwise remain outside participants' plans.

## Qualifications

The supplied source is a structured podcast summary, not Wong's game design, participant record, report, or complete research biography. It does not establish which outcomes were robust to changed assumptions, how player incentives shaped behavior, or whether the scenario generalizes to real command systems. The simulation demonstrates interaction risk, not the inevitability of escalation or the failure of every automated defense.
