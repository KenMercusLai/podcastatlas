# Michelle Wong

## Overview
Michelle Wong is a technologist and artist who frames personalized AI as a mirror and explores its reflective potential through a diary-grounded synthetic version of her 14-year-old self.

## Current Profile
Wong's work joins artistic experimentation with a cautious account of digital intimacy. She treats personal data as material through which AI can reflect selected parts of a life, not as proof that a machine knows a whole person. Her constructive-use boundary is relational: reflective AI can be useful when embedded in nourishing human ties and responsible habits, but becomes dangerous when it turns into the only outlet for distress or substitutes agreement for reciprocal care.

## Key Characteristics
- Technologist and artist working with personalized AI and autobiographical material.
- Originator in this source of the [[AIAsPersonalMirror]] framing.
- Experimenter who used childhood diaries to model a younger version of herself.
- Cautious advocate for supplementary rather than substitutive AI use.
- Emphasizes disagreement, criticism, and mutual care as features of human relationships that chatbots may not reproduce.

## Evidence
- Mirror framing - [[tech-20261026-1026-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261026-1026-mp-tech-pod-128]] reports Wong's view that AI amplifies aspects of the self that users repeatedly present.
- Earlier-self experiment - [[tech-20261026-1026-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261026-1026-mp-tech-pod-128]] describes her diary-grounded conversation with a synthetic 14-year-old self and comparison with inner-child reflection.
- Safety boundary - [[tech-20261026-1026-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261026-1026-mp-tech-pod-128]] attributes to Wong the warning that exclusive reliance on AI can become dangerous and that AI should not replace human relationships.

## Qualifications
The source summarizes rather than reproduces Wong's project methods, model design, diary corpus, privacy protections, or outcome measures. Her description of the exercise as therapeutic is a personal assessment, not clinical evidence. The phrase "AI psychosis" is a public risk label in the episode and should not be treated as a standalone clinical diagnosis or a claim of simple machine causation.
