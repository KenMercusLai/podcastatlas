# Catherine Parr

## Overview

Catherine Parr was Henry VIII's sixth wife, a twice-widowed and strongly Protestant queen who survived religious danger by combining conviction with careful management of the king's authority.

## Current Profile

The episode suggests that Catherine may have understood marriage to Henry partly as a route for advancing religious commitments. Her learning and theological discussion created influence but also exposure in a court where disagreement with the king could become dangerous.

Her survival strategy was rhetorical as well as personal: when threatened, she represented debate as a wife's attempt to learn from her husband rather than challenge sovereign authority. After Henry's death she married Thomas Seymour, whose conduct toward the young Elizabeth the episode treats as disturbing.

## Key Characteristics

- Experienced widow before becoming Henry's final queen consort.
- Strongly Protestant figure who may have linked marriage to religious purpose.
- Learned participant in theological discussion at a dangerous royal court.
- Managed a moment of acute risk through deference without abandoning intellectual agency.
- Survivor whose post-Henry marriage introduced a new threat around Elizabeth.
