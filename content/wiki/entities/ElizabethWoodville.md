# Elizabeth Woodville

## Overview

Elizabeth Woodville is the widowed commoner whose marriage to Edward IV transformed her family into a royal affinity, and the later queen mother whose negotiation and sanctuary strategies failed to protect the princes in the Tower.

## Current Profile

Elizabeth's 1464 marriage was extraordinarily unconventional for an English king: she was an English widow with two sons rather than a foreign princess carrying an alliance. The episode compares her refusal to remain a mistress with Anne Boleyn and treats the marriage as a major rupture because Woodville relatives then entered royal patronage. After Edward's death, hostile claims about the family should not erase her constrained efforts to negotiate, use sanctuary, and protect her sons against Richard's control of council, force, and access.

## Key Characteristics

- Queen dowager and mother of [[EdwardV]] and [[RichardOfShrewsbury]].
- English widow whose insistence on marriage redirected Edward IV away from the expected foreign-alliance match.
- Center of a Woodville affinity that Richard represented as dangerous to his own position.
- Sister of [[AnthonyWoodville]], whose accomplishments and guardianship complicate the image of the family as simple royal parasites.
- Participant in an attempted compromise with [[WilliamHastings]] before the arrests.
- Used Westminster sanctuary to protect her younger son and household.
- Target of witchcraft accusation and marriage-invalidity claims used during Richard's escalation.

## Qualifications

The sources do not independently establish the witchcraft allegation or invalid marriage; they treat both as politically useful claims in Richard's consolidation. The marriage was a major factional rupture, but neither Elizabeth's agency nor Woodville patronage alone explains Warwick's rebellion or the 1483 collapse.
