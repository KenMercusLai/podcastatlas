# West Germany

## Overview

West Germany appears as the post-1945 German state that restored *Das Lied der Deutschen* through a third-verse-only convention.

## Current Profile

The episode presents West Germany's anthem problem as a balance between continuity and repudiation. Public events initially lacked a settled anthem and sometimes used alternatives such as Beethoven's "Ode to Joy" or the carnival song "Trizonesia." [[KonradAdenauer]] pressed for restoring the old anthem, [[TheodorHeuss]] resisted its Nazi burden, and the eventual compromise made the third verse publicly proper while leaving the first two verses outside official practice by convention rather than legal ban.

## Key Characteristics

- Postwar German state forced to rebuild national ceremony after Nazism.
- State whose early public rituals exposed the absence of an agreed anthem.
- Political setting for the Adenauer-Heuss third-verse compromise.
- Football setting where the 1954 World Cup reopened public anxiety about which verse Germans would sing.

- Compromise: [[679-germany-the-song-hitler-stole-part-3-glt6217148052]] says [[KonradAdenauer]] and [[TheodorHeuss]] settled on *Das Lied der Deutschen* with only the third verse sung.
- Football flashpoint: [[679-germany-the-song-hitler-stole-part-3-glt6217148052]] says the 1954 World Cup final triggered concern when German fans reportedly sang the first verse.

## Qualifications

The source treats the first two verses as excluded by convention, not legally banned. It also marks some details of the 1954 broadcast reports as uncertain.
