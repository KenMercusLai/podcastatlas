# Augustus the Strong / 强力王奥古斯特

## Overview

Augustus the Strong was the Saxon ruler and elected king of Poland whose proposal to partition Swedish Baltic possessions and later attack on Livonia helped open the [[GreatNorthernWar|Great Northern War]]. His repeated survival and return made him the object of [[CharlesXII|Charles XII]]'s prolonged campaign through Poland and Saxony.

## Current Profile

The prewar episode presents Augustus as physically powerful, extravagant, and politically opportunistic. Meeting Peter during the interrupted Great Embassy, he proposes that they exploit Charles's youth and divide the [[SwedishEmpire|Swedish Baltic Empire]], helping redirect Peter from the Ottoman frontier toward the Baltic.

Augustus then joins Denmark and Russia in the anti-Swedish coalition assembled around Livonian discontent. After Denmark exits and Russia is crushed at the [[BattleOfNarva|Battle of Narva]], Charles focuses on punishing Augustus for breaking his word. Swedish victories and occupation do not immediately remove him; he evades decisive defeat, returns with Russian support, and forces Charles into years of campaigning.

Charles eventually pressures the Polish political system to depose Augustus and install Stanisław Leszczyński, then invades Saxony and compels Augustus to renounce the Polish crown and break with Russia. The victory is spectacular but strategically costly because the time spent pursuing Augustus allows [[PeterTheGreat|Peter]] to rebuild and establish [[SaintPetersburg|Saint Petersburg]].

## Key Characteristics

- He combines Saxon dynastic power with an elected Polish crown.
- His Livonian attack makes him an initiating member of the anti-Swedish coalition.
- His partition proposal helps define Peter's Baltic turn before open war.
- Evasion, return, and Russian support prolong Charles's Polish campaign.
- His deposition and Saxon surrender mark the high point of Swedish dominance.
- His defeat indirectly buys Russia the time needed for military and territorial recovery.

- Prolonged campaign: [[564-the-great-northern-war-the-battle-of-the-baltic-part-1-glt2662789070]] follows his evasion, return, deposition, Saxon defeat, and forced break with Russia.
- Strategic consequence: [[564-the-great-northern-war-the-battle-of-the-baltic-part-1-glt2662789070]] argues that Charles's fixation on Augustus gives Peter time to recover.

## Qualifications

The two sources view Augustus largely through Peter's and Charles's strategies. Polish political agency, Augustus's own aims, the constitutional process of deposition, and the wider Saxon and Polish costs require additional evidence; court descriptions and personal anecdotes remain source-scoped.
