# Augustus the Strong / 强力王奥古斯特

## Overview

Augustus the Strong was the Saxon ruler and elected king of Poland whose attack on Swedish Livonia helps open the [[GreatNorthernWar|Great Northern War]]. His repeated survival and return make him the object of [[CharlesXII|Charles XII]]'s prolonged campaign through Poland and Saxony.

## Current Profile

Augustus joins Denmark and Russia in the anti-Swedish coalition assembled around Livonian discontent. After Denmark exits and Russia is crushed at the [[BattleOfNarva|Battle of Narva]], Charles focuses on punishing Augustus for breaking his word. Swedish victories and occupation do not immediately remove him; he evades decisive defeat, returns with Russian support, and forces Charles into years of campaigning.

Charles eventually pressures the Polish political system to depose Augustus and install Stanisław Leszczyński, then invades Saxony and compels Augustus to renounce the Polish crown and break with Russia. The victory is spectacular but strategically costly because the time spent pursuing Augustus allows [[PeterTheGreat|Peter]] to rebuild and establish [[SaintPetersburg|Saint Petersburg]].

## Key Characteristics

- He combines Saxon dynastic power with an elected Polish crown.
- His Livonian attack makes him an initiating member of the anti-Swedish coalition.
- Evasion, return, and Russian support prolong Charles's Polish campaign.
- His deposition and Saxon surrender mark the high point of Swedish dominance.
- His defeat indirectly buys Russia the time needed for military and territorial recovery.

## Qualifications

This source views Augustus largely through Charles's strategy. Polish political agency, Augustus's own aims, the constitutional process of deposition, and the wider Saxon and Polish costs require additional evidence.
