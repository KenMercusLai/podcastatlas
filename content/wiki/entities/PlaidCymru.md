# Plaid Cymru

## Overview

Plaid Cymru is a Welsh nationalist political party founded in 1925, presented by the source as beginning with cultural and Welsh-language preservation before later Welsh nationalism placed greater weight on self-government and economic power.

## Current Profile

The episode treats early Plaid Cymru as a response to fear that Welsh might disappear, not chiefly as an immediate independence movement. Its initial politics could be ruralist and anti-industrial, imagining that agricultural communities offered stronger conditions for language survival than anglicized industrial society.

That cultural emphasis later existed beside demands for more Welsh control, especially as failures to resolve economic problems weakened confidence in Westminster. The party therefore marks a transition from defending a threatened language and way of life toward broader constitutional nationalism, without making independence the majority position described by the episode.

## Key Characteristics

- Welsh nationalist party founded in 1925.
- Initially centered language and cultural preservation more than immediate state independence.
- Emerged amid fears that Welsh could disappear within the following century.
- Included an early ruralist and anti-industrial strand.
- Later operated within a wider argument for Welsh self-government.

## Evidence

- Founding priority: [[267-wales-the-roar-of-the-red-dragon-glt2657098007]] says early Plaid Cymru treated Welsh-language protection as its first concern.
- Ruralist outlook: [[267-wales-the-roar-of-the-red-dragon-glt2657098007]] describes an early preference for an agricultural future thought friendlier to Welsh-language strength.
- Constitutional widening: [[267-wales-the-roar-of-the-red-dragon-glt2657098007]] connects later nationalism to dissatisfaction with Westminster's response to Welsh economic problems.

## Qualifications

The episode provides a compressed intellectual history rather than a full party history, electoral profile, or account of internal factions. Its description of early anti-industrial thinking should not be treated as a complete or timeless platform, and the source does not establish current levels of party or independence support.
