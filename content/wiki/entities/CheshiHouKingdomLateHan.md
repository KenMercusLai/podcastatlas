## Overview

车师后王国 / Later Cheshi Kingdom is the Western Regions polity in Hanji 839 where a local royal conflict becomes a Han frontier-management problem. The episode follows King [[AluoduoCheshiLateHan|阿罗多]] from conflict with Han authorities into rebellion, flight to [[Xiongnu|北匈奴]], return, and restored kingship under [[YanXiangCheshiLateHan|阎祥]]'s containment policy.

## Current Profile

The kingdom appears as a dependent frontier polity rather than a directly administered commandery. Aluoduo's conflict with Han officials escalates into an attack on Han agricultural garrison troops, causing casualties and forcing internal elites to choose sides. Houmuhou Tanzhe and some residents leave Aluoduo and surrender to Han authorities, after which Aluoduo flees north with a small cavalry group.

The settlement is pragmatic. Dunhuang administrator Song Liang proposes installing Beijun, the hostage son of the earlier king Junjiu, but Aluoduo returns and still has local support. Yan Xiang worries that excluding him may invite Northern Xiongnu intervention, so he promises restored kingship in exchange for surrender. The court accepts, restores Aluoduo, sends Beijun back to Dunhuang, and assigns three hundred households for his settlement.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Regions dependent polity whose internal succession problem affects Han frontier security.
- Local royal authority remains meaningful because Aluoduo retains support after rebellion and flight.
- Han屯田 troops and local elites are both exposed when the king rebels.
- Hostage-backed replacement is available but not automatically the best stabilizing choice.
- Restoration of the rebel king becomes a containment tool when outside Xiongnu involvement is the larger risk.
