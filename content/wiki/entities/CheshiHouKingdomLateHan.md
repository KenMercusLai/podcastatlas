## Overview

车师后王国 / Later Cheshi Kingdom is a Western Regions polity whose current profile spans a 134 CE Han-Northern Xiongnu frontier action and a later 153 CE local royal crisis. Hanji 816 shows the kingdom as an active partner and target in Han-Xiongnu pressure around Cheshi; Hanji 839 follows King [[AluoduoCheshiLateHan|阿罗多]] from conflict with Han authorities into rebellion, flight to [[Xiongnu|北匈奴]], return, and restored kingship under [[YanXiangCheshiLateHan|阎祥]]'s containment policy.

## Current Profile

The earliest current layer comes from [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-816-xiangyao-naxia-nide-lingdao-qishi-hen-jiandan-lva3y4hgu7kn1z4jmhy6jn4-oyvc|Hanji 816]]. The Cheshi Hou side cooperates with Han frontier command in a raid on Northern Xiongnu at Chanyu Lu Valley, capturing the chanyu's mother. The same source later records Northern Xiongnu retaliation through a Huyan king raid on Cheshi Hou, after which Shun orders Dunhuang troops to rescue the kingdom. This makes Cheshi Hou a live hinge between Han and Northern Xiongnu, not merely a later internal succession problem.

The kingdom also appears as a dependent frontier polity rather than a directly administered commandery. In [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-839-ni-neng-zuodao-wenxin-wukui-ma-lqvlswubf4ak5qfgzjf-owigneua|Hanji 839]], Aluoduo's conflict with Han officials escalates into an attack on Han agricultural garrison troops, causing casualties and forcing internal elites to choose sides. Houmuhou Tanzhe and some residents leave Aluoduo and surrender to Han authorities, after which Aluoduo flees north with a small cavalry group.

The settlement is pragmatic. Dunhuang administrator Song Liang proposes installing Beijun, the hostage son of the earlier king Junjiu, but Aluoduo returns and still has local support. Yan Xiang worries that excluding him may invite Northern Xiongnu intervention, so he promises restored kingship in exchange for surrender. The court accepts, restores Aluoduo, sends Beijun back to Dunhuang, and assigns three hundred households for his settlement.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Regions dependent polity whose military exposure affects Han-Northern Xiongnu frontier security.
- Active frontier partner in Hanji 816 before becoming a later restoration problem in Hanji 839.
- Local royal authority remains meaningful because Aluoduo retains support after rebellion and flight.
- Han屯田 troops and local elites are both exposed when the king rebels.
- Hostage-backed replacement is available but not automatically the best stabilizing choice.
- Restoration of the rebel king becomes a containment tool when outside Xiongnu involvement is the larger risk.

## Qualifications

This page is source-bounded to Hanji 839 and Hanji 816. The exact 134 CE Cheshi Hou king, Huyan king, office titles, the full royal genealogy around Junjiu and Beijun, and the long-term outcome of the 153 settlement remain source-scoped. The page treats "Later Cheshi Kingdom" as a route for 车师后王国 and does not attempt a full Western Regions history.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 816 as the earliest current layer: Han-aligned Cheshi action against Northern Xiongnu, Xiongnu retaliation, and Dunhuang rescue.
- Reframed Hanji 839's Cheshi rebellion, hostage-replacement, and restoration-settlement branch as a later recurrence of the same frontier hinge.
- Preserved the kingdom as a late-Han example of frontier recognition used after rebellion rather than before conflict.
