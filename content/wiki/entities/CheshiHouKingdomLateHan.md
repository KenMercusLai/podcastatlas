## Overview

车师后王国 / Later Cheshi Kingdom is a Western Regions polity whose current profile now starts with the 74 CE Han campaign that forces Cheshi Hou king An's surrender and restores Han offices in the region. Later layers show [[BanYongLateHan|Ban Yong]]'s 125 CE campaign against King Junjiu, 126-127 CE restoration of Han alignment, a 134 CE Han-Northern Xiongnu frontier action, and a 153 CE local royal crisis.

## Current Profile

Hanji 743 supplies the earliest layer. [[DouGuEasternHan|Dou Gu]], [[GengBingEasternHan|Geng Bing]], and Liu Zhang leave from Dunhuang/Kunlun Barrier with more than fourteen thousand troops. Cheshi is divided between front and rear kings. Dou Gu first wants to attack the front king, but Geng Bing argues that the rear king is the strategic hinge and leads the vanguard. Han forces kill thousands; Cheshi Hou king An surrenders by removing his cap and holding a horse's foot, and the Cheshi front king also surrenders.

The same source connects surrender to institution-building. Dou Gu petitions to restore the Western Regions Protector and Wuji Colonel offices. [[EmperorMingOfHan|Mingdi]] accepts, appoints Chen Mu as Western Regions Protector, places Geng Bing as Wu Colonel at Jinpu city in the later Cheshi area, and places Guan Chong as Ji Colonel at Liuzhong in the former Cheshi area.

The next major layer comes from [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-803-han-shundi-ruhe-zai-xuefengxueyu-zhong-dengshang-huangwei-lqvno7t-gcgxtbs3phuflr4ybe2s|Hanji 803]]. In 125 CE, Ban Yong mobilizes commandery cavalry and allied Western Regions forces against Cheshi Hou, defeats the kingdom, captures King Junjiu and a Xiongnu envoy, and executes them at Suo Ban's death site before the heads are sent to [[LuoyangLateHan|Luoyang]].

Hanji 807 then shows the kingdom's rulership being reset and bound against [[NorthernXiongnuLateHan|Northern Xiongnu]]. Ban Yong installs Jiatenu as king of Cheshi Hou, and Cheshi joins five other Western Regions polities in returning to Han alignment. Ban Yong then uses Cheshi inside the anti-Northern Xiongnu campaign: after a Xiongnu royal kinsman is captured, Jiatenu executes him personally, which the source reads as making future Cheshi-Xiongnu reconciliation harder.

Hanji 816 presents Cheshi Hou as both active partner and retaliation target. The Cheshi Hou side cooperates with Han frontier command in a raid on Northern Xiongnu at Chanyu Lu Valley, capturing the chanyu's mother. The same source later records Northern Xiongnu retaliation through a Huyan king raid on Cheshi Hou, after which Shun orders Dunhuang troops to rescue the kingdom.

The kingdom also appears as a dependent frontier polity rather than a directly administered commandery. In [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-839-ni-neng-zuodao-wenxin-wukui-ma-lqvlswubf4ak5qfgzjf-owigneua|Hanji 839]], Aluoduo's conflict with Han officials escalates into an attack on Han agricultural garrison troops, causing casualties and forcing internal elites to choose sides. Houmuhou Tanzhe and some residents leave Aluoduo and surrender to Han authorities, after which Aluoduo flees north with a small cavalry group.

The settlement is pragmatic. Dunhuang administrator Song Liang proposes installing Beijun, the hostage son of the earlier king Junjiu, but Aluoduo returns and still has local support. Yan Xiang worries that excluding him may invite Northern Xiongnu intervention, so he promises restored kingship in exchange for surrender. The court accepts, restores Aluoduo, sends Beijun back to Dunhuang, and assigns three hundred households for his settlement.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Regions dependent polity whose rulership and military exposure affect Han-Northern Xiongnu frontier security.
- Early 74 CE surrender case that helps restore Han Western Regions offices under Mingdi.
- Direct target of a 125 CE Ban Yong campaign before being restored to Han alignment in Hanji 807.
- Active frontier partner in Hanji 816 and a later restoration problem in Hanji 839.
- Local kingship can be used to bind alignment, as when Jiatenu is made to execute a captured Xiongnu royal kinsman.
- Local royal authority remains meaningful because Aluoduo retains support after rebellion and flight.
- Hostage-backed replacement is available but not automatically the best stabilizing choice.

## Qualifications

This page is source-bounded to Hanji 743, Hanji 839, Hanji 816, Hanji 807, and Hanji 803. Hanji 743 uses both Cheshi front and rear kings; this page keeps the route focused on 车师后王国 while treating the front-king material as context. Chen Mu, Guan Chong, Liu Zhang, King An's further biography, office titles, and garrison details remain source-scoped. Later notes keep Suo Ban, King Junjiu's full genealogy, Shanshan, Jiatenu, the captured Xiongnu royal kinsman, the exact 134 CE Cheshi Hou king, Huyan king, and the long-term outcome of the 153 settlement source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 743 as the earliest current layer: 74 CE Cheshi campaign, King An's surrender, and Western Regions office restoration.
- Repositioned Hanji 803 as a later recurrence rather than the beginning of the Cheshi Hou profile.
- Preserved Hanji 807 as the restoration and commitment-binding layer after Ban Yong's campaign.
- Kept Hanji 816 as the later action layer: Han-aligned Cheshi action against Northern Xiongnu, Xiongnu retaliation, and Dunhuang rescue.
- Reframed Hanji 839's rebellion, hostage-replacement, and restoration branch as a later recurrence of the same frontier hinge.
