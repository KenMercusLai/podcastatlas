## Overview

车师后王国 / Later Cheshi Kingdom is a Western Regions polity whose current profile now starts with [[BanYongLateHan|班勇]]'s 125 CE campaign against its king Junjiu, continues through 126-127 CE restoration of Han alignment, a 134 CE Han-Northern Xiongnu frontier action, and a 153 CE local royal crisis. Hanji 803 adds the conquest-and-capture layer; Hanji 807 shows the kingdom's rulership being reset and bound against [[Xiongnu|北匈奴]]; Hanji 816 shows it as both active partner and retaliation target; Hanji 839 follows King [[AluoduoCheshiLateHan|阿罗多]] from conflict with Han authorities into rebellion, flight, return, and restored kingship under [[YanXiangCheshiLateHan|阎祥]]'s containment policy.

## Current Profile

The earliest current layer comes from [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-803-han-shundi-ruhe-zai-xuefengxueyu-zhong-dengshang-huangwei-lqvno7t-gcgxtbs3phuflr4ybe2s|Hanji 803]]. In 125 CE, Ban Yong mobilizes commandery cavalry and allied Western Regions forces against Cheshi Hou, defeats the kingdom, captures King Junjiu and a Xiongnu envoy, and executes them at Suo Ban's death site before the heads are sent to [[LuoyangLateHan|Luoyang]].

The next layer comes from [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-807-banchao-zhizi-weihe-hui-hanyuan-ruyu-liiwgi77g2on1z2vnwaf1-wvph2d|Hanji 807]]. Ban Yong installs Jiatenu as king of Cheshi Hou, and Cheshi joins five other Western Regions polities in returning to Han alignment. Ban Yong then uses Cheshi inside the anti-Northern Xiongnu campaign: after a Xiongnu royal kinsman is captured, Jiatenu executes him personally, which the source reads as making future Cheshi-Xiongnu reconciliation harder.

The next layer comes from [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-816-xiangyao-naxia-nide-lingdao-qishi-hen-jiandan-lva3y4hgu7kn1z4jmhy6jn4-oyvc|Hanji 816]]. The Cheshi Hou side cooperates with Han frontier command in a raid on Northern Xiongnu at Chanyu Lu Valley, capturing the chanyu's mother. The same source later records Northern Xiongnu retaliation through a Huyan king raid on Cheshi Hou, after which Shun orders Dunhuang troops to rescue the kingdom. Together with Hanji 807, this makes Cheshi Hou a live hinge between Han and Northern Xiongnu, not merely a later internal succession problem.

The kingdom also appears as a dependent frontier polity rather than a directly administered commandery. In [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-839-ni-neng-zuodao-wenxin-wukui-ma-lqvlswubf4ak5qfgzjf-owigneua|Hanji 839]], Aluoduo's conflict with Han officials escalates into an attack on Han agricultural garrison troops, causing casualties and forcing internal elites to choose sides. Houmuhou Tanzhe and some residents leave Aluoduo and surrender to Han authorities, after which Aluoduo flees north with a small cavalry group.

The settlement is pragmatic. Dunhuang administrator Song Liang proposes installing Beijun, the hostage son of the earlier king Junjiu, but Aluoduo returns and still has local support. Yan Xiang worries that excluding him may invite Northern Xiongnu intervention, so he promises restored kingship in exchange for surrender. The court accepts, restores Aluoduo, sends Beijun back to Dunhuang, and assigns three hundred households for his settlement.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Regions dependent polity whose rulership and military exposure affect Han-Northern Xiongnu frontier security.
- Direct target of a 125 CE Ban Yong campaign before being restored to Han alignment in Hanji 807.
- Active frontier partner in Hanji 816 and a later restoration problem in Hanji 839.
- Local kingship can be used to bind alignment, as when Jiatenu is made to execute a captured Xiongnu royal kinsman.
- Local royal authority remains meaningful because Aluoduo retains support after rebellion and flight.
- Han屯田 troops and local elites are both exposed when the king rebels.
- Hostage-backed replacement is available but not automatically the best stabilizing choice.

## Qualifications

This page is source-bounded to Hanji 839, Hanji 816, Hanji 807, and Hanji 803. Hanji 803 adds the 125 CE campaign but keeps Suo Ban, King Junjiu's full genealogy, Shanshan, commandery troop details, and the Xiongnu envoy source-scoped. Jiatenu, the replaced local ruler, the captured Xiongnu royal kinsman, the exact 134 CE Cheshi Hou king, Huyan king, office titles, the full royal genealogy around Junjiu and Beijun, and the long-term outcome of the 153 settlement remain source-scoped. The page treats "Later Cheshi Kingdom" as a route for 车师后王国 and does not attempt a full Western Regions history.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 803 as the earliest current layer: Ban Yong's 125 CE campaign, King Junjiu's capture, and the Xiongnu envoy execution.
- Repositioned Hanji 807 as the next restoration layer: Ban Yong's installation of Jiatenu, renewed Han alignment, and anti-Xiongnu commitment binding.
- Kept Hanji 816 as the later action layer: Han-aligned Cheshi action against Northern Xiongnu, Xiongnu retaliation, and Dunhuang rescue.
- Reframed Hanji 839's Cheshi rebellion, hostage-replacement, and restoration-settlement branch as a later recurrence of the same frontier hinge.
- Preserved the kingdom as a late-Han example of frontier recognition used after rebellion rather than before conflict.
