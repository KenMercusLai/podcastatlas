# Walter Ong / 沃尔特·翁

## Overview
Walter Ong / 沃尔特·翁 appears in [[episode-224-bushi-bushi-bushi-bushi-8213451338-252768|Episode 224: 不是不识不时不适]] as the media theorist whose orality-literacy framework helps explain reading as a cognitive environment rather than a narrow skill.

## Current Profile
The episode uses Ong to expand literacy into three layers: decoding written signs, possessing a trained reading-and-writing ability, and living within a text-organized culture that shapes abstraction, classification, formal reasoning, definition, and self-analysis. Ong's frame lets the host treat reading decline as a possible shift in cognitive style, not merely a decline in taste or patience.

## Key Characteristics
- Turns literacy from a mechanical skill into a broad cultural-cognitive condition.
- Interprets [[AlexanderLuria]]'s fieldwork as evidence that orality and literacy organize thought differently.
- Provides the source's bridge from reading comprehension to self-analysis and abstraction.
- Supplies the term secondary orality for thinking about short video, feeds, and AI-era communication.

## Evidence
- Literacy as cognition: [[episode-224-bushi-bushi-bushi-bushi-8213451338-252768]] uses Ong to argue that text culture supports abstract categories, formal logic, definition, and reflective self-description.
- Luria interpretation: [[episode-224-bushi-bushi-bushi-bushi-8213451338-252768]] moves from Luria's cases to Ong's claim that some kinds of self-analysis are hard outside text-shaped cognition.
- Secondary orality: [[episode-224-bushi-bushi-bushi-bushi-8213451338-252768]] applies Ong's phrase to the short-video and large-language-model era.

## Qualifications
The episode uses Ong as an interpretive source rather than a complete history of literacy theory. It also refuses a simple return-to-orality argument: the closing position is that future readers and writers may need both abstract literacy and embodied experience.
