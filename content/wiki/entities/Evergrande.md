# Evergrande

## Overview
Evergrande is the Chinese property developer used by the wiki as a dense case of China's real-estate boom, debt expansion, and post-boom social damage. The earlier Dan Wang episode frames it as a systemic signal inside [[ChinaRealEstateDebtCycle]]; EP93 then supplies the detailed company story from [[XuJiayin]]'s first Guangzhou projects through the listing-credit boom, debt-stack expansion, [[ThreeRedLinesPolicy|three red lines]], [[EvergrandeWealth]], supplier commercial-paper defaults, unfinished apartments, delisting, and liquidation. The football-legacy source adds a second branch: Evergrande also used [[GuangzhouFC]] and [[EvergrandeFootballSchool]] as brand, prestige, youth-development, and national-football instruments whose afterlife cannot be reduced to the property balance sheet.

## Current Profile
The current profile is no longer just "large developer with unpaid debt." Evergrande now represents a whole operating model: [[RealEstateHighTurnoverLeverage]] built on cheap land, presale proceeds, contractor credit, fast sales, and confidence in permanent refinancing. The model could produce extreme growth while land and apartment prices were rising, but it left the company dependent on continuous sales, external credit, and belief in property appreciation.

EP93 also makes Evergrande a household and supplier exposure case. The crisis reached buyers through [[HousingPresaleFinancingRisk|presale-financed unfinished homes]], investors and employees through [[EvergrandeWealth]], and suppliers through commercial acceptance bills and unpaid project work. The company therefore remains a system signal: its collapse links household wealth, local-government land dependence, developer financing, investor expectations, and policy tightening.

The football branch makes Evergrande a legacy-splitting case. [[vol-225-hengda-de-zuqiu-yichan-ft-panweili-gkwrijeozrpkafcpbatn-ivb|Vol.225]] insists that Xu Jiayin, the property group, and illegal conduct should be clearly rejected, while football assets and damage need a separate accounting. Guangzhou's titles and academy pipeline still matter, but high spending, salary inflation, commercial-contract disregard, naturalized-player costs, and club-national-team boundary blur also belong to the legacy.

## Key Characteristics
- High-turnover property developer whose early success depended on low prices, fast sales, presale cash, land collateral, and contractor financing.
- Listed-company credit lever that turned IPO status, ratings, public markets, offshore bonds, and investor networks into expansion fuel.
- Central case in the [[ChinaRealEstateDebtCycle]] because its obligations connected buyers, suppliers, employees, local projects, banks, bondholders, and strategic investors.
- Debt-stack example where dollar bonds, perpetual bonds, commercial paper, wealth products, and strategic-investor buyback promises made simple liability measures incomplete.
- Diversified confidence-story company whose football, bottled water, auto, health, finance, insurance, film, and Faraday Future branches are source-framed as narrative support more than proven durable capability.
- Social-spillover case where corporate failure became [[GuaranteedHomeDeliveryCrisis|保交楼]], supplier distress, and wealth-product loss.
- Football legacy case whose club, academy, fan identity, salary effects, and national-team entanglement outlived the developer's crisis.

## Evidence
- Systemic real-estate role: [[building-things-and-breaking-things-in-china-summer-school-world-tour]] links Evergrande, [[XuJiayin]], prepaid apartments, local-government land dependence, speculative buying, and 2020 debt caps into a broader China boom-bust loop.
- Early high-turnover model: [[ep93-yanjian-hengda-qi-gaolou-zhuanyan-shoufu-ru-gaoqiang-lhsjpbzdfjta0phfxrtnybq57o4c|EP93]] says Jinbi Garden used cheap former-industrial land, small upfront deposits, land-backed loans, contractor advances, low pricing, and fast sellout to prove the initial model.
- Credit expansion after listing: [[ep93-yanjian-hengda-qi-gaolou-zhuanyan-shoufu-ru-gaoqiang-lhsjpbzdfjta0phfxrtnybq57o4c|EP93]] says the failed 2008 IPO was rescued by Hong Kong capital relationships and that the 2009 listing gave Evergrande stronger access to offshore bonds, syndicated loans, and partnership financing.
- Debt-stack complexity: [[ep93-yanjian-hengda-qi-gaolou-zhuanyan-shoufu-ru-gaoqiang-lhsjpbzdfjta0phfxrtnybq57o4c|EP93]] describes dollar bonds, perpetual bonds, commercial acceptance bills, [[EvergrandeWealth]], and the 1300-billion-yuan strategic-investor arrangement as distinct fragility channels.
- Social spillover: [[ep93-yanjian-hengda-qi-gaolou-zhuanyan-shoufu-ru-gaoqiang-lhsjpbzdfjta0phfxrtnybq57o4c|EP93]] connects wealth-product nonpayment, supplier bill defaults, hundreds of stalled sites, contract liabilities, and unfinished homes to ordinary households and small businesses.
- Football legacy: [[vol-225-hengda-de-zuqiu-yichan-ft-panweili-gkwrijeozrpkafcpbatn-ivb]] separates the company's collapse from the football school's survival, Guangzhou FC's titles, the [[ChineseFootballGoldenYuanEra]], and national-team boundary effects.

## Qualifications
The current profile is source-bounded. The Dan Wang source uses Evergrande as a macro-system example, while EP93 is an explanatory podcast episode that itself warns listeners that many figures come from online reference material and should be treated as approximate. Vol.225 is a football podcast with field observation and interpretive memory, not an audited institutional history. The 2025 delivery-completion figures, exact owner counts, strategic-investor side agreements, naturalization costs, and future Guangzhou team paths remain source-scoped.
