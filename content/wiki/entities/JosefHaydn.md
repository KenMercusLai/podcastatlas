# Josef Haydn

## Overview

Josef Haydn appears in the episode as the Austrian composer whose 1797 imperial tune later became the melody of the [[GermanNationalAnthem]].

## Current Profile

The source uses Haydn as the musical origin point for a song whose political identity later moved far beyond its first context. He wrote the tune as an Austrian subject of the Habsburg Empire after hearing "God Save the King" in London and wanting a comparable patriotic song for Emperor Francis II during the wars against Revolutionary France.

## Key Characteristics

- Austrian composer attached in the source to Habsburg imperial patriotism rather than German nationalism.
- Composer of the 1797 tune that later became the melody for *Das Lied der Deutschen*.
- Musical bridge showing how an anthem melody can outlive its original dynastic purpose.
- Figure whose tune was widely adapted by later European composers and political contexts.

## Evidence

- Imperial origin: [[679-germany-the-song-hitler-stole-part-3-glt6217148052]] says Haydn wrote the tune in 1797 for Emperor Francis II.
- British model: [[679-germany-the-song-hitler-stole-part-3-glt6217148052]] says Haydn had heard "God Save the King" in London and wanted Austria to have a comparable anthem.
- Later reuse: [[679-germany-the-song-hitler-stole-part-3-glt6217148052]] treats the same tune as the musical basis for the later [[GermanNationalAnthem]].
- Musical circulation: [[679-germany-the-song-hitler-stole-part-3-glt6217148052]] says the tune became popular and was adapted by composers including Beethoven, Rossini, Bruckner, and Tchaikovsky.

## Qualifications

This profile is source-scoped to Haydn's anthem role. It does not reconstruct his broader career, catalogue, or place in classical music history.
