# Bloody Knife

## Overview

Bloody Knife was an Arikara scout attached to the Seventh Cavalry whose warning to [[GeorgeArmstrongCuster]] and death beside [[MarcusReno]] connect ignored intelligence to command collapse at the [[BattleOfLittleBighorn]].

## Current Profile

The earlier episode presents Bloody Knife as Custer's friend and scout during the 1873 Yellowstone expedition, with personal reasons for opposing the Lakota. His warning that the party was being followed and might be ambushed helped Custer prepare a disciplined defense, showing intelligence that was operationally useful before the fatal campaign.

At Little Bighorn, Bloody Knife again recognized that the Native trail and encampment were much larger than Custer expected. He warned Custer that many people lay ahead and reportedly predicted that he would not see the sun again, while Custer answered with confidence in the Seventh Cavalry.

Bloody Knife later accompanied Reno's attack. He was shot in the head during the timber defense, splattering Reno with blood and brain matter. The hosts treat that moment as a severe psychological shock that helped precipitate Reno's panic, though the wider collapse also involved growing opposition, confused orders, and weak command cohesion.

## Key Characteristics

- His warnings show battlefield intelligence that Custer used effectively in 1873 but did not adequately prioritize in 1876.
- His friendship with Custer complicates treating the scout relationship as purely instrumental.
- His presence shows Native scouts serving within the U.S. force against other Native peoples.
- His death became both a personal loss and a command-shock event for Reno.
- He should not be reduced to a dramatic trigger for another officer's story.

## Qualifications

- The exact wording of his warning and death prediction is retrospective and source-scoped.
- Reno's collapse cannot be explained by one traumatic moment alone.
- The episode supplies only a battle-specific profile, not a full account of Bloody Knife's life or Arikara-U.S. relations.
- The friendship and personal-motive account comes through the hosts' compressed narrative rather than a full biography.

## What Changed

- Extended the profile backward to friendship, divided Native alignments, and effective scout warning during the Yellowstone expedition.
