## Overview

杨过 / Yang Guo is the protagonist of [[ReturnOfTheCondorHeroes|《神雕侠侣》]], read here through constancy toward [[XiaolongnuJinYong|小龙女]], charisma and unequal attachments, impulsive self-sacrifice, resistance to ritual judgment, and maturation into a public-minded hero.

## Current Profile

The bounded episodes refuse to reduce Yang Guo's romantic life to either absolute constancy or indiscriminate flirtation. His sixteen-year wait centers [[XiaolongnuJinYong|小龙女]], reinforced by love, habit, identity, hope, resistance to social opposition, and an inability to abandon an organizing promise. His later lack of other completed romances and eventual cliff leap demonstrate constancy, while his broader tendency toward impulsive risk qualifies the leap as evidence unique to romantic devotion.

His treatment of [[GuoXiangJinYong|郭襄]] is intentionally unresolved. Initial restraint gives way to three consequential promises, shared adventures, spectacular birthday gifts, and rescue. These acts show affection and generosity, but the hosts disagree whether they cross from elder-brotherly care into unspoken romance. Guo Xiang's devotion cannot establish his reciprocity.

The earlier supporting-women discussion explains why other characters can nevertheless love him. Beauty, teasing praise, rescue, willingness to answer kindness, and shared danger create intense bonds with [[LuWushuangJinYong|陆无双]], [[ChengYingJinYong|程英]], and [[GongsunLueJinYong|公孙绿萼]]. The resulting emotional weight is unequal: playful chemistry, admiration, gratitude, or guilt do not establish the same desire on both sides, and memory of Xiaolongnü repeatedly stops possible development.

[[GuoFuJinYong|郭芙]] presents a different ambiguity. Yang Guo repeatedly spares her after severe harms, which can be read as buried attachment, but her wealth, beauty, lineage, and early rejection also make her approval a symbol of the social recognition denied to him as a marginalized child. Romance, wounded pride, status, and the wish to be acknowledged remain entangled rather than settled.

The birthday sequence also joins private and public action. Destroying enemy supplies, exposing Huo Du, recovering the beggars' staff, and crediting Guo Jing's teaching turn gifts to one person into aid for Xiangyang and proof of Yang Guo's development from defensive outsider to “神雕大侠.”

## Key Characteristics

- Remains committed to Xiaolongnü across repeated separation and a sixteen-year wait.
- Rejects ritual condemnation of the teacher-student romance more directly than Xiaolongnü does.
- Combines deep attachment with a broader tendency toward impulsive, self-endangering action.
- Attracts several women through teasing attention, rescue, responsiveness, and shared danger without offering equal reciprocity.
- Maintains disputed affection, guilt, or unrealized attraction toward Lu Wushuang, Cheng Ying, Gongsun Lü'e, and Guo Fu while keeping Xiaolongnü central.
- Extends unusually consequential promises and public gestures to Guo Xiang without settled romantic reciprocity.
- Converts personal recognition and gratitude toward Guo Jing into public service at Xiangyang.

## Qualifications

This profile derives from two interpretive podcast summaries rather than an edition-specific textual study. Yang Guo's private motives, awareness that the South Sea Divine Nun story is false, feelings toward each supporting woman, and the intended meaning of his gifts, flirtation, restraint, or discarded antidote remain disputed. Constancy does not by itself prove communication quality or ideal compatibility, while another person's love, gratitude, or sacrifice does not establish his reciprocal desire.

## What Changed

- Expanded the profile from constancy and Guo Xiang to several unequal forms of attachment.
- Distinguished charisma, rescue, guilt, social recognition, and playful chemistry from reciprocal commitment.
- Added Guo Fu as a disputed mixture of affection, antagonism, status injury, and desired acknowledgment.
