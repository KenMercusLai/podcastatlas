## Overview

杨过 / Yang Guo is the protagonist of [[ReturnOfTheCondorHeroes|《神雕侠侣》]], read here through constancy toward [[XiaolongnuJinYong|小龙女]], impulsive self-sacrifice, resistance to ritual judgment, and maturation into a public-minded hero.

## Current Profile

The episode refuses to reduce Yang Guo's sixteen-year wait to one emotion. Love remains central, but habit, identity, hope, rebellion against social opposition, and an inability to abandon an organizing promise may reinforce it. His later lack of other romances and eventual leap after the deadline demonstrate constancy, while his broader tendency toward impulsive risk qualifies the leap as evidence unique to romantic devotion.

His treatment of [[GuoXiangJinYong|郭襄]] is intentionally unresolved. Initial restraint gives way to three consequential promises, shared adventures, spectacular birthday gifts, and rescue. These acts show affection and generosity, but the hosts disagree whether they cross from elder-brotherly care into unspoken romance. Guo Xiang's devotion cannot establish his reciprocity.

The birthday sequence also joins private and public action. Destroying enemy supplies, exposing Huo Du, recovering the beggars' staff, and crediting Guo Jing's teaching turn gifts to one person into aid for Xiangyang and proof of Yang Guo's development from defensive outsider to “神雕大侠.”

## Key Characteristics

- Remains committed to Xiaolongnü across repeated separation and a sixteen-year wait.
- Rejects ritual condemnation of the teacher-student romance more directly than Xiaolongnü does.
- Combines deep attachment with a broader tendency toward impulsive, self-endangering action.
- Extends unusually consequential promises and public gestures to Guo Xiang without settled romantic reciprocity.
- Converts personal recognition and gratitude toward Guo Jing into public service at Xiangyang.

## Qualifications

This profile derives from one interpretive podcast summary rather than an edition-specific textual study. Yang Guo's private motives, awareness that the South Sea Divine Nun story is false, feelings toward Guo Xiang or Guo Fu, and the intended meaning of his gifts remain disputed. Constancy does not by itself prove communication quality or ideal compatibility, and Guo Xiang's love does not establish Yang Guo's reciprocal desire.

## What Changed

- Established a focused profile separating constancy from communication quality and impulsive risk.
- Connected the Guo Xiang birthday sequence to both personal generosity and public heroic maturation.
- Preserved the ambiguity between brotherly affection and unspoken romantic feeling.
