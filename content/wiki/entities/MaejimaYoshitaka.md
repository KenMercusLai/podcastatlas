## Overview
前岛佳孝 is a Japanese historian of Western Wei and Northern Zhou whose collected topical studies examine offices, military expansion, and political figures rather than offering a continuous dynastic chronicle.

## Current Profile
In the episode, Maejima's work revises familiar narratives through close comparison of ranks, campaigns, institutions, and geography. It questions a strong emperor-versus-Yuwen-group model, reconstructs [[LiHu|Li Hu]]'s likely place among the Eight Pillars, treats Zhouli offices as innovation as well as archaism, rejects an under-evidenced fixed Liang–Eastern Wei encirclement, and uses the Li Bi–Li Mi line to test how long [[GuanlongGroup|Guanlong]] political culture persisted.

## Key Characteristics
- Organizes regime history through bounded institutional and biographical problems.
- Tests inherited rankings and narratives against offices, battle records, and new inscriptions.
- Treats archaizing institutions as possible innovations with practical administrative effects.
- Gives geography and southward expansion explanatory weight alongside battlefield performance.
- Advances motive-based interpretations while allowing later evidence to revise earlier conclusions.

## Evidence
- Topical structure: [[ltxaost4pgqhjdirwas15vizersj-ltxaost4pgqhjdirwas15vizersj]] describes the book as studies of offices, expansion, and major figures rather than a full narrative history.
- Li Hu revision: [[ltxaost4pgqhjdirwas15vizersj-ltxaost4pgqhjdirwas15vizersj]] reports Maejima's seventh-place reconstruction and the epitaph evidence qualifying a total-fabrication thesis.
- Institutional reading: [[ltxaost4pgqhjdirwas15vizersj-ltxaost4pgqhjdirwas15vizersj]] presents the Zhouli system as a new administrative architecture that could remove duplication.
- Strategic reading: [[ltxaost4pgqhjdirwas15vizersj-ltxaost4pgqhjdirwas15vizersj]] emphasizes southern civil war, Hanzhong, and Ba-Shu in the shift of power.

## Qualifications
The profile is mediated by a single Chinese podcast summary rather than a complete reading of Maejima's book and later publications. The summary does not settle how representative each interpretation is of his wider scholarship.
