# Madhusree Mukerjee

## Overview

Madhusree Mukerjee is the historian and author whose archival research in *Churchill's Secret War* anchors the episode's account of British wartime policy during the [[BengalFamine1943]].

## Current Profile

[[the-prime-minister-and-the-prof-the-prime-minister-and-the-prof]] joins family memory to archival investigation. Mukerjee recounts her mother's childhood memory of starving villagers arriving in the city, while her research uses newly available shipping records to test the claim that Britain could spare neither grain nor transport for India.

The episode credits her with identifying a change over time: shipping was constrained early in 1943, but additional American vessels reportedly created surplus capacity later, when famine mortality was severe. She also traces wheat routed through the Indian Ocean toward other stockpiles without unloading in India and argues that [[FrederickLindemann]]'s short memoranda omitted warnings and qualifications that cut against the preferred policy.

## Key Characteristics

- Connects intergenerational Bengal memory with documentary investigation of imperial policy.
- Uses shipping archives to test logistical claims rather than treating famine relief refusal as self-explanatory.
- Distinguishes early wartime shipping constraint from reported later capacity.
- Examines how ministerial memoranda selected among available evidence and consequences.
- Places colonial priorities and political choice alongside weather, war, crop, price, and market causes.

## Evidence

- Family memory: [[the-prime-minister-and-the-prof-the-prime-minister-and-the-prof]] recounts Mukerjee's description of her mother's encounter with famine refugees.
- Shipping capacity: [[the-prime-minister-and-the-prof-the-prime-minister-and-the-prof]] attributes the early-constraint and later-surplus distinction to her archival work.
- Routing priorities: [[the-prime-minister-and-the-prof-the-prime-minister-and-the-prof]] reports her finding that Australian wheat passed India en route to other destinations.
