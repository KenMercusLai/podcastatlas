# 李典 / Li Dian

## Overview
李典 / Li Dian is a Cao-side officer whose current profile combines group-scale recruitment, tactical warning and rescue, and the ability to place a common defense above personal friction. Hanji 1154 adds his cooperation with [[ZhangLiao|张辽]] at [[HefeiBattle215|Hefei]].

## Current Profile
Hanji 1104 uses Li Dian to show that talent transfer can also be organization transfer: he is said to bring his clan and several thousand retainers to [[CaoCao|曹操]], effectively adding an existing force rather than arriving alone. Hanji 1057 then places him with [[XiahouDun|夏侯惇]] and [[YuJin|于禁]] against Liu Bei's northward movement from [[LiuBiao|刘表]]'s Jingzhou base. After Liu Bei burns his own camp and retreats, Li Dian argues that an unexplained retreat plus narrow, wooded terrain signals an ambush. Xiahou Dun continues the pursuit, falls into the trap, and Li Dian's reserve movement rescues him.

Hanji 1154 places Li Dian in a different coordination problem. Cao Cao's sealed instructions assign him and Zhang Liao to attack Sun Quan's assembling force while Yue Jin holds Hefei. Although the episode reports a poor personal relationship between the two attackers, Li Dian refuses to let private resentment obstruct the defense and joins the selected assault. His contribution is therefore not only obedience or bravery but coalition discipline under a shared strategic objective.

## Key Characteristics
- Cao-side officer with better terrain and enemy-signal reading than the immediate commander in this episode.
- Group-scale recruit presented as bringing clan members and retainers into Cao Cao's organization.
- Identifies burned-camp retreat as bait rather than simple weakness.
- Preserves force continuity by rescuing Xiahou Dun after the ambush begins.
- Functions as the successful counterpart to Xiahou Dun's overconfident pursuit.
- Subordinates reported personal hostility to coordinated action when Hefei's defense requires joint command.

## Qualifications
This remains a partial biography built from three compressed branches. Hanji 1104's count and “entire army” analogy require narrower textual verification, while Hanji 1057 covers one tactical episode. Hanji 1154's personal hostility, exact role, dialogue, selected-force size, and battle effects remain source-scoped; cooperation in this crisis does not establish consistently harmonious relations.

## What Changed

- Connected Li Dian's Hefei role to explicit division of labor between attack and fortress defense.
