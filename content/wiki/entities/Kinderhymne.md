# Kinderhymne

## Overview

Kinderhymne appears as Bertolt Brecht's proposed alternative to the old German anthem tradition.

## Current Profile

The episode treats Kinderhymne as an intellectually attractive but politically unsuccessful alternative. Brecht wrote it in response to [[KonradAdenauer]]'s restoration of the old West German anthem, and Hanns Eisler set it to music. Its lyrics are presented as more self-critical about Germany's crimes than *Das Lied der Deutschen*, but reunified [[Germany]] kept the third verse of the [[GermanNationalAnthem]] instead.

## Key Characteristics

- Alternative German anthem text associated with Brecht's postwar critique.
- Song set by Hanns Eisler, linking it musically to the East German anthem branch.
- Self-critical proposal that acknowledged other countries' fear of Germany.
- Reunification-era alternative favored by some intellectual and Green Party voices but not adopted.

## Evidence

- Origin: [[679-germany-the-song-hitler-stole-part-3-glt6217148052]] says Brecht wrote Kinderhymne in response to Adenauer restoring the old anthem in West Germany.
- Musical setting: [[679-germany-the-song-hitler-stole-part-3-glt6217148052]] says Hanns Eisler also set the text to music.
- Political tone: [[679-germany-the-song-hitler-stole-part-3-glt6217148052]] describes the lyrics as self-critical and aware of other countries recoiling from Germany's crimes.
- Non-adoption: [[679-germany-the-song-hitler-stole-part-3-glt6217148052]] says reunified Germany kept the third verse of *Das Lied der Deutschen* instead.
