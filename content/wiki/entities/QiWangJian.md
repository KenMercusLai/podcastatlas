# 齐王建 / 田建 / King Jian of Qi

齐王建 / 田建 / King Jian of Qi enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-91-chulong-ruhe-qiaokai-shougua-zhaotaihou-de-men-2-liy5yulg2ysop-jwfigvdkub1pk7]] as the son of [[QiXiangWang|齐襄王]] and [[JunWangHou|君王后]]. The source identifies him as the crown prince who succeeds after Qi Xiang Wang dies in the same 265 BCE frame that resolves the Zhao hostage crisis.

The episode presents his accession as a Qi-side parallel to [[ZhaoDan|赵丹 / 赵孝成王]] in [[ZhaoState|赵国]]: both are young rulers whose mothers become the practical governing authorities. In Qi, Jun Wang Hou's regency is described as cautious and peace-oriented, creating a court style in which a forceful figure such as [[TianDan|田单]] may not fit easily.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-92-lishishang-di-yi-wei-zhuming-tiangou-shi-shui-1-lmwf0q0-3hxb6d4hro8fdmc3zdxp]] adds a naming note. The episode says the narration uses Qi Wang Jian's personal name because his later status as conquered final ruler means [[QinState|秦国]]'s unification leaves no normal posthumous-title path for him. The point turns his accession into a countdown marker for the final decades of the [[WarringStatesPeriod|战国时期]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-94-shashen-baiqi-changping-zhi-zhan-zhong-de-caobao-mingjiang-2-lvqctrazn8ddkcnp-lhspzzmzmns]] gives Qi Wang Jian a first major non-intervention case. Zhao asks Qi for grain during the Changping encirclement, but the court refuses; the episode says the practical decision lies with Jun Wang Hou. The source reads the refusal as short-term avoidance of helping an old rival, but one that weakens the larger anti-Qin shield around Qi.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-95-changping-zhi-zhan-hou-qinguo-weishenme-meiyou-mie-le-zhaoguo-2-lutsvwvnwzsezoorhe-vu7yjthyx]] makes Qi Wang Jian's weakened-state context matter to [[YuQing|虞卿]]'s post-Changping proposal. Yu Qing says [[ZhaoState|赵国]] should give six cities to [[QiState|齐国]] rather than [[QinState|秦国]] to create anti-Qin pressure, but the host doubts that Qi under Qi Wang Jian has enough strength or ambition to make that plan reliable.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-102-2-kongzi-zai-luguo-de-shenshi-zhimi-lphy12exg4u7f-30qkir96gv634r]] extends that late-Qi weakness into the Lu question. The episode says Qi is the nearest state to [[LuState|鲁国]], but Qi Wang Jian's court is kept from active intervention by [[HouSheng|后胜]], whom Qin repeatedly bribes to advise non-concern for other states. Qi Wang Jian therefore becomes part of the explanation for why [[ChuState|楚国]], not Qi, destroys Lu.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-119-3-lishishang-di-yi-wei-zhenzheng-yiyishang-chuilian-tingzheng-taihou-liq9d3itmbar00ij43zzed-mulul]] makes his later continuity with [[JunWangHou|君王后]] explicit. After his mother's death, Qi Wang Jian continues the cautious, pro-Qin, non-aligned line that has kept Qi peaceful for more than forty years. The source then moves toward his final decision environment: he accepts [[HouSheng|后胜]]'s proposal to go personally to Qin, and [[YongmenSima|雍门司马]] begins a remonstrance by asking whether the king exists for the state or the state exists for the king.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-120-1-shui-shi-huangdi-zhong-de-mabaonan-lhw8vsj6ta6rjdkugcvfohaqqxc0]] gives Qi Wang Jian's endpoint in 221 BCE. [[WangBenQin|王贲]] attacks Qi from the north and reaches [[Linzi|临淄]] quickly; Qin then promises Qi Wang Jian five hundred li of land if he surrenders. He accepts, but the promise is not honored: the episode says he is placed at Gong and starves among pines and cypresses.

The same source sharpens the evaluation tension around him. [[SimaGuang|司马光]]'s criticism makes him a weak final ruler who trusted ministers and missed the common interest behind [[HezongAlliance|合纵]], while the host also says his surrender spared Qi's soldiers and people from a large final war. His page therefore now belongs to both [[DistantStateAppeasementTrap|远国安抚误读]] and [[CasualtySparingSurrender|保全生灵式投降]].

The page is source-scoped. Later sources may need to revise the balance between this cautious-regency inheritance, the final surrender, and later memory of Qi's fall.
