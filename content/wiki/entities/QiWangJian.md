# 齐王建 / 田建 / King Jian of Qi

## Overview
齐王建 / 田建 / King Jian of Qi is the final ruler of [[QiState|齐国]], succeeding young under his mother [[JunWangHou|君王后]] and later surrendering to Qin in 221 BCE. The source set presents him neither as a simple monster nor as a successful peace ruler, but as a weak decision-maker formed inside a long-lived policy that preserved his people while progressively shrinking his strategic options.

## Current Profile
Qi Wang Jian inherits a cautious, non-aligned court and more than forty years of relative peace. That settlement protects Qi in the short term but leaves the state outside the rescue of Zhao, unreliable for later coalition plans, vulnerable to [[HouSheng|后胜]]'s Qin-funded advice, and militarily unprepared when Qin's conquest sequence finally reaches it. [[YongmenSima|雍门司马]] can stop his personal journey to Qin, and [[JimoDafuLateQi|即墨大夫（齐末）]] can offer a restoration coalition, but he chooses neither submission nor active preparation. Qin later enters from the north; he surrenders under a land promise, is confined at Gong, and starves. The resulting judgment must hold together state failure, adviser capture, personal indecision, long peace, and the lives spared by avoiding a final large battle.

## Key Characteristics
- Inherits rule while young and depends first on Jun Wang Hou's regency and later on a compromised advisory environment.
- Continues non-intervention even as Qin removes the states that shield Qi.
- Mistakes sustained Qin friendliness and domestic peace for evidence that urgent preparation is unnecessary.
- Can accept a compelling remonstrance in the moment but does not convert correction into a coherent alternative strategy.
- Ends the state through surrender after Qin reaches Linzi, while avoiding a destructive final campaign.

## Evidence
- **Young succession and inherited court:** [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-91-chulong-ruhe-qiaokai-shougua-zhaotaihou-de-men-2-liy5yulg2ysop-jwfigvdkub1pk7]] records his accession under Jun Wang Hou's regency, while [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-92-lishishang-di-yi-wei-zhuming-tiangou-shi-shui-1-lmwf0q0-3hxb6d4hro8fdmc3zdxp]] explains the source tradition's use of his personal name.
- **Non-intervention and coalition weakness:** [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-94-shashen-baiqi-changping-zhi-zhan-zhong-de-caobao-mingjiang-2-lvqctrazn8ddkcnp-lhspzzmzmns]] places him in Qi's refusal of grain to Zhao at Changping, and [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-95-changping-zhi-zhan-hou-qinguo-weishenme-meiyou-mie-le-zhaoguo-2-lutsvwvnwzsezoorhe-vu7yjthyx]] doubts that his weakened court could anchor Yu Qing's anti-Qin plan.
- **Captured advice and terminal indecision:** [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-102-2-kongzi-zai-luguo-de-shenshi-zhimi-lphy12exg4u7f-30qkir96gv634r]] links Hou Sheng's influence to non-action around Lu; [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-119-3-lishishang-di-yi-wei-zhenzheng-yiyishang-chuilian-tingzheng-taihou-liq9d3itmbar00ij43zzed-mulul]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-119-4-shishang-neng-cheng-juying-de-junwang-qiwangjian-livcg5uz60ne-6oje-qzjfpghhph]] carry that pattern into the proposed Qin visit, successful remonstrance, rejected restoration plan, and failure to rearm.
- **Fall and mixed legacy:** [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-120-1-shui-shi-huangdi-zhong-de-mabaonan-lhw8vsj6ta6rjdkugcvfohaqqxc0]] records Qin's northern entry, the five-hundred-li promise, his surrender and starvation at Gong, Sima Guang's criticism, and the host's casualty-sparing qualification.

## Qualifications
The insulting “巨婴” and “妈宝男” labels are episode frames, not neutral historical classifications. The deathbed handoff, bribery network, force estimates, and reported dialogue remain source-scoped. Qi's surrender spared a final battle but does not erase the earlier choices that helped produce strategic isolation, and the humanitarian outcome does not establish that all prior non-intervention was optimal.

## What Changed
- His late reign is now framed as strategic suspension after two competing interventions, not only passive continuity with his mother's policy.
- Long peace now carries an explicit military-readiness cost alongside its civilian benefit.
- Hou Sheng's influence is clarified as an information-channel problem involving rewarded guests.
- Yongmen Sima's remonstrance now has a recorded successful outcome.
- The late-Qi restoration proposal is preserved as courageous but operationally doubtful.

## Relationships
- [[JunWangHou|君王后]] - mother, regent, and source of the peace policy he inherits.
- [[HouSheng|后胜]] - chancellor who reinforces Qin-friendly non-intervention and discourages preparation.
- [[YongmenSima|雍门司马]] - remonstrant who prevents his personal journey to Qin.
- [[JimoDafuLateQi|即墨大夫（齐末）]] - adviser whose exile-led restoration proposal he does not adopt.
- [[DistantStateAppeasementTrap|远国安抚误读]] - diplomatic mechanism behind his false security.
- [[LongPeaceReadinessAtrophy|长期和平战备退化]] - capacity loss that narrows his late options.
- [[CasualtySparingSurrender|保全生灵式投降]] - humanitarian qualification on his final state failure.
