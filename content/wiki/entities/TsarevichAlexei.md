# Tsarevich Alexei / 阿列克谢皇太子

## Overview

Tsarevich Alexei was [[PeterTheGreat|Peter the Great]]'s son and heir, presented by the episode as the central victim of Peter's final years. His religious conservatism, attachment to old Moscow, anxious temperament, and association with opponents of reform made him both personally incompatible with his father and politically usable as an alternative future for the [[RussianEmpire|Russian Empire]].

## Current Profile

Alexei's story is a case of dynastic role becoming coercive. The prewar episode places the rupture early: after the Great Embassy, Peter forces his mother Eudokia into a convent and transfers Alexei to the care of Peter's sister. Neglected by Peter and marked by that removal, he grows into a studious but frightened heir who prefers Orthodox ritual to his father's military and technical world. The later episode does not idealize him: it also describes heavy drinking, verbal abuse of his wife Charlotte, neglect, and a relationship with Afrosenia.

The decisive political sequence begins when Peter demands that Alexei become worthy of succession or enter a monastery. Alexei renounces the throne, hesitates over monastic life, flees to Habsburg protection, and returns only after promises of pardon and a quiet private life. Peter then converts the failed flight into an expanding investigation. Alexei names associates, Afrosenia testifies about his anti-reform hopes, and a trial produces a conspiracy confession under torture. The episode concludes that renewed torture killed him and that the official account concealed the violence.

## Key Characteristics

- He is an unwilling heir whose religious and cultural preferences represent the old order Peter seeks to subordinate.
- Fear of his father shapes his education, self-injury, renunciation, attempted monastic exit, and eventual flight.
- His personal conduct toward Charlotte and his heavy drinking prevent the source from presenting him as a simple innocent ideal.
- His political danger comes largely from symbolic availability to conservative clergy, relatives, and critics of reform rather than a demonstrated organized conspiracy.
- His return under promised pardon becomes the entry point for interrogation, collective punishment, torture, and death.

## Qualifications

This profile follows two narrative podcast episodes rather than a full biography or independent review of the surviving records. The later source distinguishes Alexei's anti-reform wishes and politically dangerous associations from proof of an organized conspiracy, but both episodes' reconstruction of family motive, interrogation, torture, and cause of death remains source-scoped.
