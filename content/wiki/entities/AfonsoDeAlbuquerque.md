# Afonso de Albuquerque

## Overview

Afonso de Albuquerque was a Portuguese imperial commander and viceroy associated with the seizure of Goa and Malacca and the consolidation of a fortified Asian maritime network.

## Current Profile

The episode presents Albuquerque as the figure who turned route discovery into durable strategic control. Capturing and fortifying major ports, sending embassies across South and Southeast Asia, and dispatching mapping voyages eastward allowed [[Portugal]] to pursue commerce and coercive leverage across a region it lacked the manpower to occupy territorially.

His triumphal titles and reported boasts also make the violence explicit. The source ties the capture of Goa to mass killing and the conquest of Malacca to a large force imbalance, so administrative and diplomatic reach cannot be separated from [[PortugueseCrusadingCommercialExpansion|crusading-commercial warfare]].

## Key Characteristics

- Portuguese viceroy and military organizer in the Indian Ocean.
- Captor of Goa and Malacca in the episode's expansion sequence.
- Builder of a coastal fort-and-port system rather than an extensive inland empire.
- Diplomatic actor sending embassies toward Burma, Thailand, and Sumatra.
- Commander whose prestige language and reported boasts foreground anti-Muslim violence.

## Evidence

- Goa: [[228-portugal-the-golden-age-of-discovery-part-2-glt7949934800]] associates Albuquerque with Goa's capture and a reported boast of 6,000 Muslims killed.
- Malacca: [[228-portugal-the-golden-age-of-discovery-part-2-glt7949934800]] describes the Portuguese taking the port in 1511 despite being heavily outnumbered.
- Regional reach: [[228-portugal-the-golden-age-of-discovery-part-2-glt7949934800]] connects his administration to embassies, eastern Indonesian mapping, Canton, and prestige gifts sent to Lisbon.

## Qualifications

The episode uses Portuguese titles, boasts, and conquest narratives and gives limited attention to local political actors. Casualty totals, force ratios, exact administrative reach, and the degree of Albuquerque's personal direction remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Established Albuquerque as the consolidator of Portugal's fortified maritime-node strategy in Asia.
- Preserved violence and diplomacy as simultaneous parts of his profile.
