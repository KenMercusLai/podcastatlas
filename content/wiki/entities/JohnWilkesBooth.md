# John Wilkes Booth

## Overview

John Wilkes Booth is Abraham Lincoln's assassin, presented as an actor who heard Lincoln support Black voting rights, cast the president as a tyrant, and violently altered the leadership context in which Reconstruction began.

## Current Profile

[[203-american-civil-war-aftermath-legacy-part-4-glt3990109453]] connects Booth's act directly to the racial politics of the war's end. Lincoln's limited endorsement of Black suffrage intensified Booth's hostility, and the assassination on Good Friday in 1865 removed the president days after Confederate surrender.

The political consequence cannot be measured as a simple alternative history. The episode judges Lincoln more capable than [[AndrewJohnson]] while acknowledging that transforming Southern racial order would still have required extraordinary sustained force and Northern commitment.

## Key Characteristics

- Actor and Confederate sympathizer identified as Lincoln's assassin.
- Opponent of Black political rights in the episode's account.
- Tyrannicide self-image used to justify political murder.
- Catalyst of a leadership rupture at Reconstruction's opening.
- Indirect creator of Lincoln's martyr memory.

## Evidence

### Racial and political motive

- [[203-american-civil-war-aftermath-legacy-part-4-glt3990109453]] says Booth heard Lincoln endorse Black voting rights and regarded the president as a tyrant.

### Reconstruction rupture

- [[203-american-civil-war-aftermath-legacy-part-4-glt3990109453]] links the assassination to Johnson's succession and to the loss of Lincoln's greater political skill.

### Memory consequence

- [[203-american-civil-war-aftermath-legacy-part-4-glt3990109453]] argues that the killing preserved Lincoln as a martyr outside the compromises of later Reconstruction.

## Qualifications

This page is limited to one episode's concise account. It does not reconstruct the wider assassination conspiracy, Booth's complete biography, escape and death, or the full documentary basis for his motives. The claim that Lincoln's survival would have improved Reconstruction remains counterfactual.
