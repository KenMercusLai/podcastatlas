# John of Westwick

## Overview

John of Westwick was a late-fourteenth-century English monk associated with St Albans, Tynemouth, the 1383 Bishop's Crusade, and an equatorium design for calculating planetary positions.

## Current Profile

John matters here as a representative knowledge worker rather than a canonical scientific hero. The source portrays him copying, editing, glossing, commenting on, and translating texts, then joining textual knowledge to the practical geometry and construction of an astronomical instrument.

His mobile and varied life also complicates the image of the cloister as intellectually sealed. Monastic institutions, coastal travel, military expedition, London craft activity, and translated learning all meet in the surviving reconstruction of his work.

## Key Characteristics

- Benedictine monk associated with St Albans and its Tynemouth daughter house.
- Probable birth in the 1350s, with substantial biographical uncertainty.
- Participant in the failed 1383 Bishop's Crusade to Flanders.
- Copyist, editor, commentator, translator, and adapter of learned texts.
- Associated with an equatorium that mechanically computed celestial longitude.

## Evidence

- Monastic and military life: [[112-medieval-science-glt3839934134]] places John at St Albans, Tynemouth, and the Bishop's Crusade.
- Textual practice: [[112-medieval-science-glt3839934134]] describes copying, editing, glossing, commentary, and translation as his normal knowledge work.
- Instrument construction: [[112-medieval-science-glt3839934134]] associates him in early-1390s London with plans for a brass-and-wood equatorium using dials and strings.

## Qualifications

John's birth date, reason for transfer to Tynemouth, and some stages of his biography are uncertain. The source says he “appears” in connection with the equatorium, so attribution and reconstruction should not be expressed with greater confidence than the evidence permits.

## What Changed

- Established John as an ordinary-practitioner case for medieval knowledge-making.
- Connected his textual labor to astronomical computation and skilled construction.
- Preserved uncertainty around biography and instrument attribution.
