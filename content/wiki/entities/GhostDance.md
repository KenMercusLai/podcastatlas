# Ghost Dance

## Overview

The Ghost Dance was a millenarian Native religious movement that the episode presents as gaining force among the [[LakotaPeople|Lakota]] amid dispossession and despair, then becoming the object of press panic and military suppression in 1890.

## Current Profile

In the source, the movement expresses hope during social collapse rather than functioning as proof of a coordinated military uprising. [[SittingBull]] showed interest and presided over dances but is not said to have received visions or commanded a Ghost Dance rebellion. Nevertheless, newspapers and reservation authorities treated him as a dangerous ringleader, helping turn religious activity into a pretext for arrest and intervention.

After the [[WoundedKneeMassacre]], the movement rapidly receded under tighter military control, food, gifts, leniency, and incentives. Its leaders were then absorbed into [[FrontierViolenceSpectacle]]: Kicking Bear and Short Bull joined Buffalo Bill's European tour, while advertising falsely connected performers to Wounded Knee.

## Key Characteristics

- It offered spiritual hope amid severe material and political dislocation.
- Authorities and newspapers interpreted it through an uprising frame not established by the episode's evidence.
- Its association with Sitting Bull amplified fear even though he was not presented as its prophetic or military commander.
- Suppression combined coercive control with food, leniency, and incentives.
- Its leaders and imagery were commercialized almost immediately after defeat.

## Evidence

- Social and religious context: [[456-fall-of-the-sioux-the-massacre-at-wounded-knee-part-3-glt7996658277]] places the movement amid bison loss, drought, ration pressure, settlement, and reservation confinement.
- Panic and attribution: [[456-fall-of-the-sioux-the-massacre-at-wounded-knee-part-3-glt7996658277]] contrasts press claims about Sitting Bull with the absence of a coordinated uprising under his command.
- Suppression and spectacle: [[456-fall-of-the-sioux-the-massacre-at-wounded-knee-part-3-glt7996658277]] describes the movement's decline and the commutation of arrested dancers' sentences through participation in Buffalo Bill's show.

## Qualifications

- The episode provides a Lakota-centered final-chapter account rather than a complete intertribal theology or history of the movement.
- “Millenarian” is the hosts' comparative historical frame and should not substitute for Indigenous theological accounts.
- Motives attributed to individual participants, including Sitting Bull, remain interpretive.
- The exact content of Kicking Bear's untranslated Glasgow speech is unknown.

## What Changed

- Established the Ghost Dance as both a hope-bearing religious movement and an object of official panic.
- Distinguished spiritual participation from evidence of coordinated insurrection.
- Added the movement's rapid conversion from suppressed practice into commercial performance.
