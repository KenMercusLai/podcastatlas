# J. L. Austin / 奥斯丁

## Overview
J. L. Austin / 奥斯丁 appears in the wiki through a 迟早更新 episode on birdsong playback, ritual speech, sincerity, and performative utterances.

## Current Profile
Austin is used as the language-philosophy hinge for [[SpeechActRitual]]. The episode presents his insight that some utterances do not primarily describe facts; saying them can itself perform an act, such as promising or completing a wedding formula.

The source distinguishes Austin's social-institutional account from Japanese kotodama, but uses both to reduce the episode's dependence on inner sincerity. If saying "I promise" can bind a person even when the person's later feeling is unstable, then saying thanks and apology after disturbing a bird may also matter as a practice that shifts responsibility and attention.

## Key Characteristics
- Enters the wiki as a philosopher of ordinary-language action.
- Provides the performative-utterance frame for ritual speech.
- Helps separate speech validity from truth-description.
- Supports the episode's claim that awkward or weakly felt ritual words can still bind behavior.
