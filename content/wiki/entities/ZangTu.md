# 臧荼 / Zang Tu

臧荼 / Zang Tu enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-143-xiangyu-shi-ruhe-feijin-xinji-nongsi-chuhuaiwang-mixin-lstfzfvbd2h9i39f7y5dxrbxxwv2|Hanji 143]] as the Yan-side general rewarded in [[XiangYu|项羽]]'s post-Qin settlement. The episode says he followed the Chu army to rescue Zhao and then entered the pass, so Xiang Yu makes him Yan king while moving the earlier Yan king [[HanGuang|韩广]] to Liaodong.

Zang Tu's placement shows the settlement's preference for commanders who joined Xiang Yu's victorious line over old kings who held titles before the final westward entry. It is a concrete case of military participation outranking prior royal status.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-148-2-peng-le-wangling-muqin-xiangyu-sharen-zhuxin-ljyv8bow4rj1l5ths3znx2evgvtm|Hanji 148-2]] gives the enforcement sequel. [[HanGuang|韩广]] refuses to accept the Liaodong relocation and stay out of Yan's old center, so Zang Tu attacks and kills him. The source keeps this as part of the wider backlash from Xiang Yu's settlement: reward promotion creates a direct fight between new and displaced Yan rulers.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-169-pandian-liubang-dengjiqian-buweirenzhi-de-gushi-1-liwkaygjtyafxxpdwzq9pdhuzroi|Hanji 169]] places Zang Tu among the rulers who urge [[LiuBang|刘邦]] to accept the imperial title after Xiang Yu's death. The source does not narrate a full Yan transition, but his presence in the petition shows a former Xiang-settlement king participating in the public recognition of the Han founding.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-171-zhangliang-jiao-ni-ruhe-mingli-shuangshou-2-lm1iff0cadjapwf2mggwyo8wcvxn|Hanji 171 part 2]] gives Zang Tu's terminal early-Han branch. In the seventh Han year, the source says he rebels in July, is defeated and captured by Liu Bang in October, and is replaced in Yan by [[LuWan|卢绾]]. The host treats his rebellion as likely driven less by a realistic bid for empire than by pressure from the central court and fear that a former Xiang-side different-surname king could not remain trusted. His son Zang Yan's later flight to [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] keeps the Yan problem connected to the northern frontier.
