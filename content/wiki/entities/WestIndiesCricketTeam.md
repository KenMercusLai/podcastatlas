# West Indies Cricket Team

## Overview

The West Indies cricket team is a transnational Caribbean sporting institution represented here as a source of shared regional and diasporic identity and a vehicle for postcolonial reversal.

## Current Profile

Unlike most Test teams, the West Indies does not correspond to one sovereign state. The episode argues that cricket, together with a regional university, gives institutional form to a collective identity spanning autonomous islands and extending into the diaspora, including the Windrush generation in Britain.

That identity carries a difficult history. Caribbean cricket grounds were created within plantation society, and Black labor and practice served colonial masters before wider populations appropriated the game. The 1950 Test victory in England and the dominant 1970s side transformed mastery of cricket into evidence against inherited racial assumptions. Tony Greig's 1976 promise to make the team “grovel” caused particular anger because it activated the language of slavery and mastery.

The team's dominance was not permanent. India's underdog victory in the 1983 World Cup final marks both the strength of the West Indian standard and a further transfer of cricketing authority within the postcolonial world.

## Key Characteristics

- Team representing multiple Caribbean territories rather than one sovereign state.
- Institution of regional and diasporic identity.
- Product of colonial cricket that became a vehicle of symbolic resistance.
- Dominant 1970s side associated with fast bowling, athleticism, and Viv Richards.
- Established champion against which India's 1983 breakthrough gained meaning.

## Qualifications

This profile compresses many islands, communities, players, and political settings into one episode's regional argument. It does not establish uniform Caribbean identification with the team or provide a complete sporting history, and the political readings attached to playing style remain interpretive.
