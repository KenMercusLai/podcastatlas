# Nupedia

## Overview
Nupedia was the expert-led free online encyclopedia project that preceded [[Wikipedia]] and supplied the clearest contrast for redesigning online contribution.

## Current Profile
The episode describes Nupedia as an open-source-inspired encyclopedia written by volunteers but governed through a seven-stage qualification and review process. It produced 21 articles in its first year, intimidated even [[JimmyWales]] when he tried to contribute, and still published an article later found to contain plagiarism. A wiki was introduced as a faster collaboration layer for the Nupedia community, but Wikipedia's lower threshold quickly overtook the original project.

## Key Characteristics
- Sought freely accessible expert knowledge produced by qualified volunteers.
- Required contributor credentials and multiple expert and copy-editing stages.
- Used emailed Word documents, creating version conflicts among collaborators.
- Produced slowly despite its elaborate quality-control process.
- Failed to prevent every serious error, including reported plagiarism.
- Became the institutional and conceptual predecessor from which Wikipedia diverged.

## Evidence
- Review design - [[vmp5702291377-vmp5702291377]] describes contributor qualification and a seven-stage publication process.
- Scale limit - [[vmp5702291377-vmp5702291377]] reports 21 articles in the first year and Wales's own reluctance to endure the process.
- Quality limit - [[vmp5702291377-vmp5702291377]] says a published article was later found to contain plagiarism after wider readership.
- Transition - [[vmp5702291377-vmp5702291377]] describes the wiki as a collaboration aid that became a separate, faster-growing encyclopedia.

## Qualifications
The account is retrospective and compressed. It supports a comparison of contribution architectures but does not establish that expert review is generally inferior, reconstruct every Nupedia participant's role, or independently verify the article and plagiarism totals.

## What Changed
- Established Nupedia as the bounded predecessor case for strict review, slow output, and the move to wiki collaboration.
