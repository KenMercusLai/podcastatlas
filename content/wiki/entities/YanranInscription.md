## Overview

燕然勒石 / Yanran Inscription enters the wiki as Hanji 763's memorial marker for [[DouXianEasternHan|Dou Xian]]'s Northern Xiongnu campaign. The episode says Dou Xian follows the precedent of earlier frontier commemoration, climbs Yanran Mountain after the [[JiluoshanBattleLateHan|Jiluoshan victory]], and orders [[BanGuEasternHan|Ban Gu]] to inscribe the campaign's achievement.

## Current Profile

The inscription is not treated here as a freestanding literary text or archaeological object. In the current bounded source, it functions as a symbol of achieved frontier power: the battle has broken Northern Xiongnu force, and the commander wants the victory fixed publicly through mountain inscription.

Hanji 763 also gives the symbol an ambivalent political setting. The commemorated commander is not a clean loyal hero; he is an outer-relative who had killed Liu Chang, escaped punishment by asking for war, and later appears in adjacent sources as arrogant and politically dangerous. Yanran commemoration therefore records real military success while also feeding the larger Dou Xian profile in which achievement and court danger coexist.

## Key Characteristics

- Commemorative marker following Dou Xian's Northern Xiongnu victory.
- Tied directly to the Jiluoshan campaign sequence in the source.
- Attributed to Ban Gu's role as writer of the inscribed achievement.
- Works as a public memory symbol, not merely a battlefield detail.
- Carries the source's moral ambiguity because the honored commander is politically dangerous.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 763. It does not reconstruct the inscription's full text, later literary reception, exact site, archaeology, or all classical precedents. The source's comparison with Huo Qubing is treated as a commemoration precedent inside the episode, not as a complete study of Han frontier inscription culture.
