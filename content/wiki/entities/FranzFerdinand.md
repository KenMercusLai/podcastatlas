# Franz Ferdinand

## Overview

Franz Ferdinand was the Habsburg heir who entered the succession path after [[CrownPrinceRudolf]]'s death and whose assassination at Sarajevo opened the [[JulyCrisis|July Crisis]].

## Current Profile

The Mayerling episode establishes Franz Ferdinand as the long-shadow consequence of Rudolf's death: succession moves through [[KarlLudwigOfAustria]] toward the branch whose later destruction becomes another Habsburg crisis. The July Crisis episode adds his political significance in 1914. It presents him as an opponent of war with [[Serbia]] and a spokesman for a peace party inside Vienna, so his murder and Sophie's removed a restraining figure just as [[FranzConradVonHotzendorf|Franz Conrad von Hötzendorf]] and other hard-liners pressed for action.

## Key Characteristics

- Franz Ferdinand appears as the later heir created by the post-Mayerling succession path.
- His role links a private 1889 catastrophe to the 1914 succession and diplomatic crisis.
- He opposed preventive war against Serbia and served as an internal restraint on military advocacy.
- His assassination removed that restraint while also giving punishment advocates an emotionally and politically powerful cause.

## Evidence

- Succession placement: [[690-a-murderous-affair-death-at-mayerling-part-2-glt7203783068]] says the line moves toward [[KarlLudwigOfAustria]] and then Franz Ferdinand after Rudolf's death.
- Later tragedy frame: [[690-a-murderous-affair-death-at-mayerling-part-2-glt7203783068]] closes by looking toward the crisis after Franz Ferdinand's 1914 assassination.
- Anti-war role: [[469-the-road-to-the-great-war-countdown-to-armageddon-part-1-glt3669608632]] describes Franz Ferdinand as resisting war with Serbia and speaking for the peace party.
- Removal effect: [[469-the-road-to-the-great-war-countdown-to-armageddon-part-1-glt3669608632]] connects his death to the strengthening of immediate-war advocates without treating that effect as sufficient by itself to cause general war.

## Qualifications

The profile remains selective. The sources establish succession, an anti-war position, assassination, and the removal of an internal restraint, but do not provide a full account of Franz Ferdinand's politics, his relationship with Sophie, the Sarajevo plot, or his counterfactual ability to prevent war.

## What Changed

- Added Franz Ferdinand's opposition to war with Serbia and the restraining role removed by his assassination.
- Preserved the post-Mayerling succession path that made him the Habsburg heir.
