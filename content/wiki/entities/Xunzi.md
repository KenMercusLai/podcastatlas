# 荀子 / Xunzi

荀子 / Xunzi enters this wiki branch through [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-70-xihuan-huayang-zuosi-de-qiguo-junzhu-luuyasp5-nhnikdcozzaueosb-md]], where [[SimaGuang|司马光]] is said to quote the Wangba chapter after the death of [[QiMinWang|齐湣王]]. That source uses him as the philosophical bridge from a ruler-collapse story into explicit "资治" reflection.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-71-xunzide-xingbenelun-lnyfxoxauexhwbrwkdevs8m4mr2j]] supplies the promised development. The episode introduces Xunzi as Xun Kuang, courtesy name Qing, a Zhao figure associated with repeated leadership at [[JixiaAcademy|稷下学宫]], later service as Lanling magistrate, and authorship tied to [[XunziText|《荀子》]]. It positions him as a major Confucian thinker who combines kingly and hegemonic statecraft, ritual and law, and whose [[XingETheory|性恶论]] is commonly contrasted with [[Mengzi|孟子 / Mencius]].

The main political frame is [[XunzianWangbaThreePaths|荀子王霸三分法]]. In the episode's reading, Xunzi does not treat state power as automatically good or bad; he asks what a ruler does with it. Ritual and righteousness can create kingly order, credible promises and punishments can create hegemony, and profit-first deception produces distrust, isolation, and ruin.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-74-huanyuan-wanbi-guizhao-zhenxiang-lg1irvbbc1wp8oph2kmpatfoxebk]] adds a more compact ruler-evaluation passage from the Wangzhi frame. The episode has Xunzi rank ritual-righteousness, effective administration, and people-support above wealth extraction: ritual-righteousness can produce kingly rule, administration can make a state strong, people-support can make it secure, and mere gathering of wealth leads toward ruin.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-75-1-qiongshe-jichi-de-zushiye-guanzhong-lmeeh8t6dobgzrkoouato9vhjjuj]] returns to that evaluative scale through [[ZiChan|子产]] and [[GuanZhong|管仲]]. Zi Chan's kindness is valuable but must become public governance, while Guan Zhong's administration and economic imagination are treated as real "能为政" achievement that still falls short of "修礼." The episode therefore makes Xunzi's hierarchy concrete: political competence can strengthen a state without creating durable ritual order.
