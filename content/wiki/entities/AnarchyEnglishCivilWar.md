# The Anarchy

## Overview

The Anarchy appears as the civil war between [[StephenOfBlois|Stephen of Blois]] and [[EmpressMatilda|Empress Matilda]] that followed the unresolved succession after [[HenryIOfEngland|Henry I]]'s death.

## Current Profile

The episode uses the Anarchy to show why medieval political society prized a ruler who could impose order, justice, and military defense. Once Stephen's seizure failed to end the succession question and Matilda landed in England in 1139, baronial violence, local coercion, and torture became the episode's evidence for royal authority breaking down.

The conflict also reveals Matilda's gendered military problem. She could lead a political cause, but the episode says she could not personally perform battle leadership in the expected kingly mode; Robert of Gloucester had to serve as her champion. The war ends not with Matilda's coronation but with the dynastic compromise that Stephen remains king and [[HenryIIOfEngland|Henry]] inherits.

## Key Characteristics

- Succession civil war rooted in the Matilda-Stephen legitimacy dispute.
- Remembered in the episode through the phrase that "Christ and his saints slept."
- Shows baronial violence when royal order is contested.
- Makes warrior-kingship expectations central to legitimacy.
- Gives Matilda temporary advantage after Stephen's capture at Lincoln.
- Ends in a succession settlement rather than Matilda's personal coronation.
