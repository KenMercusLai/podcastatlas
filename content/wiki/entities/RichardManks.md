# Richard Manks

## Overview

Richard Manks was a Solihull bricklayer and professional pedestrian, nicknamed the Warwickshire Antelope, whose 1851 walk of 1,000 miles in 500 hours at [[TheOval|the Oval]] became a major Victorian sporting spectacle.

## Current Profile

Manks's public identity joined manual labor, running experience, extreme endurance, and commercial showmanship. The episode treats his bricklaying background as part of the explanation and mythology of his stamina, then follows increasingly elaborate challenges in which distance, hourly schedules, food, sleep, weather, and timekeeping became measurable components of performance.

His October 1851 Oval walk made possible failure central to spectator interest. Press reports tracked diet, rain, pain, fatigue, emotional collapse, and the discovery that the course had made him walk farther than required. Lamps, packed seating, outside crowds, and intense reporting turned the final night into evidence that pedestrianism could combine athletic ordeal with modern event production.

## Key Characteristics

- Working-class endurance athlete whose bricklaying background became part of his sporting persona.
- Former runner known publicly as the Warwickshire Antelope.
- Specialist in precisely scheduled, escalating walking challenges.
- Performer whose sleep, meals, pain, weather exposure, and mental resistance became spectator drama.
- Central figure in the Oval's early success as a commercial multi-sport venue.
