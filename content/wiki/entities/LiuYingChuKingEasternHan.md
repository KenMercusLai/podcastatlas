## Overview

[[LiuYingChuKingEasternHan|刘英 / Liu Ying]] is the Eastern Han Chu king whose rebellion case becomes the origin point for a Mingdi-era treason panic in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-740-rang-ni-tihuguanding-de-zhichang-zhihui-zhichang-bibei-lih5b-ighkyoycawm5c-dbrnuiew|Hanji 740]]. The episode presents him as a royal figure tied to Buddhist practice, magical or auspicious objects, and accusations of covert rebellion; after his fall and suicide, the case keeps expanding through torture and implication.

## Current Profile

The current profile is not a full biography. Liu Ying matters here because his case reveals how royal suspicion, religious anxiety, and ruler anger can turn a single accusation into a broad court and local-elite purge. The episode's emphasis is less on battlefield rebellion than on the downstream legal and organizational damage after the ruler keeps searching for hidden accomplices.

Hanji 740 also makes Liu Ying a disambiguation case. The wiki already has [[LiuYing|刘盈 / Emperor Hui of Han]], so this page keeps the Eastern Han Chu king on a separate route. The source's Buddhist angle is important but source-scoped: it signals how private religious organization could alarm an imperial court, not that Buddhist practice itself is treated as proven treason.

## Key Characteristics

- Eastern Han royal kin whose accusation occurs under [[EmperorMingOfHan|汉明帝]].
- Chu-king figure whose case escalates after accusations involving rebellion, religious symbols, and hidden organization.
- Political trigger rather than only a personal actor: his fall produces a wider prison case involving royal relatives, local elites, and officials.
- Source-scoped early Buddhist figure whose religious practice becomes part of the host's explanation of imperial suspicion.
- Disambiguated Liu Ying route kept separate from the Western Han emperor [[LiuYing|刘盈]].

- Religious-political suspicion: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-740-rang-ni-tihuguanding-de-zhichang-zhihui-zhichang-bibei-lih5b-ighkyoycawm5c-dbrnuiew|Hanji 740]] frames Liu Ying as an early Buddhist prince and treats private religious organization as one reason Mingdi fears covert mobilization.
- Case-expansion trigger: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-740-rang-ni-tihuguanding-de-zhichang-zhihui-zhichang-bibei-lih5b-ighkyoycawm5c-dbrnuiew|Hanji 740]] says the investigation expands into widespread torture, implication, death, exile, and imprisonment.

## Qualifications

The page treats Liu Ying as the source's case trigger, not as a fully reconstructed rebel organization. The episode's Buddhist-secret-society explanation remains interpretive and should not be generalized beyond this source without more evidence. Details about his objects, followers, exact charges, and legal sequence remain source-scoped.
