## Overview

王太后（王娡） / Empress Dowager Wang is [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s mother and the maternal authority who monitors the Dou Ying-Guan Fu debate, directly presses the emperor on behalf of her brother [[TianFenWesternHan|田蚡]], and shapes his room for maneuver.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-265-dongchaotingbian-ruhe-yanbian-cheng-jieduandazhan-1-lil81vjxn5mait2wapdx_t4wy5hr|Hanji 265 part 1]] gives that backing a direct scene. Wang has the audience monitored, refuses food after hearing how Tian Fen was attacked, and asks how her brother could be treated this way while she remained alive. She also challenges whether the emperor can decide for himself and whether ministers who behave this way can be trusted after his death. The episode thus depicts maternal authority not as background affinity alone but as active pressure after formal deliberation.

Han Wudi reportedly answers that he convened debate because both Tian Fen and [[DouYingWesternHan|窦婴]] were imperial in-laws; an ordinary case could have been resolved by edict. That explanation preserves an attempt at public judgment while showing how family status and Wang's intervention narrow the separation between court procedure and household power.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-265-dongchaotingbian-ruhe-yanbian-cheng-jieduandazhan-2-lvu23osgv46nn3rbgque1mbhmpgw|Hanji 265 part 2]] shows the consequence: Tian Fen's access to her support makes intervention for Dou Ying costly for a Han Wudi whom the episode describes as not yet fully controlling court politics.

The same source says Tian Fen's later death removes her principal political helper and leads her to withdraw completely from politics. That consequence is useful for the episode's centralization narrative, but the source does not document the precise institutional form, timing, or completeness of her political retreat.

## Key Characteristics

- Mother of Han Wudi and senior maternal authority at court.
- Sister and political protector of Tian Fen in the episode's account.
- Uses information from the court audience to intervene directly after formal debate.
- Her support changes the relative vulnerability of Tian Fen and Dou Ying.
- Reportedly loses her principal political helper when Tian Fen dies.

## Qualifications

The page is grounded in two connected episode summaries and does not supply Wang Zhi's wider biography or an institutional account of her influence. Her reported speech demonstrates intervention inside the narrative but does not establish how much it caused the later punishment. Exact wording, information channels, emotional conduct, and “complete withdrawal” after Tian Fen's death remain episode-attributed.
