# Leopoldo Galtieri

## Overview

Leopoldo Galtieri was the Argentine junta leader whose government invaded the [[FalklandIslands]] and whose authority collapsed after defeat in the [[FalklandsWar]].

## Current Profile

The source presents Galtieri as the leader of a dictatorship that converted a sovereignty claim into a regime-survival gamble. The junta planned for seizure more effectively than for defense against a British counterattack, so military defeat destroyed its claim to competence and accelerated a democratic transition already developing within a wider regional change.

Galtieri resigned within days, was later prosecuted in Argentina, lost his military rank, and was pardoned by Carlos Menem. The episode mentions this trajectory as aftermath rather than supplying a complete account of his role in the [[ArgentineDirtyWar]] or the invasion decision.

## Key Characteristics

- Military ruler governing without democratic legitimacy.
- Leader who tied regime prestige to recovering the Falkland Islands.
- Associated with inadequate preparation for a British counterattack.
- Rapidly removed after defeat destroyed the junta's competence claim.
- Later prosecuted, stripped of rank, and pardoned.

## Evidence

- Regime gamble: [[172-the-falklands-war-afterlife-part-4-glt5779306068]] says the junta focused on taking the islands without adequately planning to hold them against Britain.
- Collapse: [[172-the-falklands-war-afterlife-part-4-glt5779306068]] connects surrender to Galtieri's resignation and the military government's loss of legitimacy.
- Legal aftermath: [[172-the-falklands-war-afterlife-part-4-glt5779306068]] reports later charges, loss of rank, and pardon.

## Qualifications

The page is based on one retrospective British podcast episode. It does not provide a complete biography, command history, account of Dirty War responsibility, or full chronology of Argentina's democratic transition. The war accelerated the junta's fall but was not the only force moving South American military regimes toward democracy.
