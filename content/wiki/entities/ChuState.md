# 楚国 / Chu State

楚国 / Chu State appears in [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-06-weiwenhou-xuan-shui-dang-zaixiang-ne-2-lmfzo4sl9nd5kz3mrxdl1dl9efei]] through the death of [[ChuShengWang|楚声王]] and the accession of [[ChuDaoWang|楚悼王]]. The source makes Chu the main example of a compact annalistic phrase that may hide more political complexity than it states.

The episode says Chu Sheng Wang was killed by "bandits," then treats that wording as possibly opaque. That makes Chu a bridge between ordinary succession chronology and [[BanditAssassinationAttribution]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-07-liangqi-cisha-1-lvkp-ju7pf5z5cz3thojv3s2fkyx]] adds Chu as a great-power pressure source against [[ZhengState|郑国]]. The episode infers from [[QinghuaJianXinian|清华简《系年》]] that Zheng's Jin alignment, including help around [[WangziDing|王子定]], likely explains Chu's siege of Xinzheng.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-08-yidai-mingjiang-wuqi-cansi-2-lvaqlgyccrugfo0l-wuwf1mt1kcm]] makes Chu the arena for [[WuQi|吴起]]'s reform career after he flees [[WeiState|魏国]]. With [[ChuDaoWang|楚悼王]]'s support, Wu Qi centralizes law and resources, strips privileges from nobles and distant royal kin, removes idle officials, strengthens military training, and opposes peace with [[QinState|秦国]]. Chu becomes stronger, but the episode treats elite hatred of Wu Qi as the core case of [[WarringStatesReformBacklash]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-08-yidai-mingjiang-wuqi-cansi-3-ltca-ikuzo9ej7a0lfrgwv2-wulb]] completes that branch. When [[ChuDaoWang|楚悼王]] dies, nobles immediately attack Wu Qi; after Wu Qi is killed against the ruler's corpse, [[ChuSuwang|楚肃王]] punishes the attackers because their arrows also damaged the dead king's body. Chu therefore becomes the setting where reform backlash, ruler protection, aristocratic violence, and legal retaliation all collapse into one succession moment.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-09-zhanguo-shidai-de-shijie-dazhan-losmum91vjpqfymywelj60o7-vbl]] moves Chu into the interstate-war frame under [[ChuSuwang|楚肃王]]. Zhao seeks Chu help after being attacked by Wei and Qi, but Chu attacks Wei's border instead, making Chu's intervention both relief for Zhao and opportunistic pressure on Wei. The source also records a brief notice of Shu attacking Chu and occupying Zifang.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-10-yi-jiazu-danwei-you-mayi-chi-daxiang-de-jingshen-2-lmnqfjmydygqnfec6xhzpixralvi]] adds two later Chu notices: [[WeiState|魏国]] attacks Chu and takes Luyang in 371 BCE, and [[ChuSuwang|楚肃王]] dies without a son, so his younger brother [[ChuXuanwang|楚宣王]] succeeds.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-14-qiweiwang-buming-zeyi-yiming-jingren-lrs6rbifyiqrojxg0mccpg7sf9rv]] adds a Qin-Chu marriage notice. [[YouyinHei|右尹黑]], a senior Chu official, goes to [[QinState|秦国]] to receive a bride, and the episode reads the bride as likely intended for [[ChuXuanwang|楚宣王]]. The notice becomes a case of [[WarringStatesMarriageDiplomacy|战国联姻外交]] whose limits are clear because marriage ties do not prevent later conflict.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-15-jiu-bu-chun-yinfa-de-shijie-dazhan-li-iojxthq8komhlhyumho3yvtem]] makes Chu the state whose honor reaction opens the Handan chain. [[ChuXuanwang|楚宣王]] resents [[LuGonggong|鲁共公]]'s late attendance and poor wine, attacks [[LuState|鲁国]] with [[QiState|齐国]], and then sends [[Jingshe|景舍]] to aid [[ZhaoState|赵国]] after [[WeiState|魏国]] surrounds [[Handan|邯郸]]. The source uses this to explain how ritual insult, alliance behavior, and strategic distraction produce [[LuWineHandanWarCascade|鲁酒薄而邯郸围]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-16-sunbin-pangjuan-enyuan-jiuge-guiling-zhi-zhan-lryhzkc1-xsvfjhvicu8yxa9d2jr]] adds Chu's chancellor branch. The ruler appoints [[ZhaoXixu|昭奚恤]] as chancellor, and [[JiangYi|江乙]] then uses stories against him, making Chu a court-rhetoric case through [[ChancellorCenteredGovernance]] and [[PoliticalParableWeaponization]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-17-hanzhaohou-he-shenbuhai-lghjmjhq9qs4se96c5ofgz6caqof]] adds [[Jingshe|景舍]] as a Chu-associated channel in a Wei-Qi peace notice from [[ZhushuJinian|《竹书纪年》]]. The episode does not develop Chu policy here; it uses Jing She to show how diplomacy around [[XianglingWarringStates|襄陵]] can route through a third-party figure.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-21-zouji-xia-yinzhao-tianji-guowai-binan-lkvmjoxolgfgw-ig7ir-ngvjifij]] makes Chu the refuge for [[TianJi|田忌]] after the [[MalingBattle|马陵之战]] aftermath. The episode does not develop Chu's reception of him; it uses Tian Ji's flight to show how valuable displaced military talent could move between Warring States courts.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-23-weihuiwang-hui-buyong-gongshu-zhi-yan-lveaqklo7oncmqudgmdk7l03qhsf]] adds a compact succession notice: [[ChuXuanwang|楚宣王]] dies and [[ChuWeiwang|楚威王]] succeeds. The source does not make Chu part of the main causal chain, but the notice keeps Chu in the same crowded 340 BCE annalistic field.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-28-hanzhaohou-de-kuaile-ni-xiangxiang-budao-ln2tbxniz4kvwogbwp32exmdokz8]] develops Chu Wei Wang's active role. Chu defeats [[YueState|越国]] in the 334 BCE cluster without immediately destroying it, then [[ChuWeiwang|楚威王]] attacks [[QiState|齐国]] at [[Xuzhou|徐州]] after [[XuzhouMutualKingship|徐州相王]]. The episode makes Chu the leading power enforcing [[StatusOrderBacklash|名分秩序反弹]] against Qi's elevated title posture.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-29-suqin-lizhi-shuangwen-zhishi-gaibian-mingyun-lrf5cqvoriyep2lkbozju-lyjaxv]] continues Chu's leverage after the attack. Chu Wei Wang demands that [[TianYing|田婴]] be removed from Qi, but [[ZhangChou|张丑]] redirects him by arguing that an expulsion could let [[TianBan|田斑]] or another stronger Qi figure rebuild resistance.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-32-shishang-shouwei-lianheguo-mishuzhang-suqin-lgfkk45yq1c0yblequ-d1t7mol3]] makes Chu the final state in [[SuQin|苏秦]]'s completed [[HezongAlliance|合纵]] circuit. The episode summarizes the Chu persuasion more briefly than Han, Wei, or Qi, but [[ChuWeiwang|楚威王]]'s acceptance completes the six-state chain and lets Su Qin become the covenant leader.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-34-qinguo-zongli-ruhe-wanzhuan-liuguo-lsenzwp0kr5wscb0btvuzmkibriq]] adds the next succession turn: Chu Wei Wang dies in 329 BCE, and [[ChuHuaiwang|楚怀王]] succeeds. The episode keeps the notice compact but previews Chu Huai Wang as a more dramatic ruler, widening the branch beyond the Qin-Wei pressure story.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-39-lishishang-zhangyi-shi-zenyang-nudui-qiangdi-de-lv2gc2wtcxuhugfgixk7rbbhpa6x]] makes Chu a receiving and return court in the Zhang Yi branch. [[HuiShi|惠施 / 惠师]] flees there after losing to Zhang Yi in [[WeiState|魏国]], and [[FengHaoWarringStates|冯浩]] advises [[ChuHuaiwang|楚怀王]] to send him to [[SongState|宋国]] rather than offend Zhang Yi. Later, [[ChenZhenWarringStates|陈枕]] uses [[ZuoShuang|左爽]]'s advice to regain Chu favor, making Chu the court where a blocked Wei path can be repurposed.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-45-yi-shi-wei-jian-junwang-daodi-gai-bu-gai-sharen-chengxing-lo3cok7cfwzrxesp-i8miz1fc1db]] makes Chu the named leader of the 318 BCE anti-Qin coalition. [[ChuHuaiwang|楚怀王]] is alliance head when Chu, Zhao, Wei, Han, and Yan reach [[HanguPass|函谷关]], but Qin cuts the Chu army's grain route, Chu withdraws first, and the rest collapse. The same episode later makes Chu both a target of Song Kang Wang's expansion and a participant in Song's destruction.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-50-zhangyi-shi-zenyang-ba-chuhuaiwang-huyou-que-de-loxg3ylhmskvdm-axtco5y-acege]] turns Chu from failed coalition leader into the direct target of Qin's alliance-breaking diplomacy. [[ZhangYiStrategist|张仪]] promises [[ChuHuaiwang|楚怀王]] [[Shangyu|商於]] if Chu breaks with [[QiState|齐国]], while [[ChenZhenWarringStates|陈枕]] warns that the Qi tie is exactly what makes Chu valuable to Qin. Chu breaks with Qi first, receives only the six-li reversal, and then attacks [[QinState|秦国]] from anger rather than from a protected alliance position.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-51-chuhuaiwang-qin-xiao-er-kan-jiujiu-ruhe-mie-le-ni-ltfkdlivomnvux5xodh2rlfqf6ac]] follows Chu's resulting military collapse. [[DanyangBattle|丹阳之战]] costs Chu eighty thousand soldiers and [[QuGai|屈匄]] is captured; [[Hanzhong|汉中]] is lost to Qin. Rather than making Hanzhong the recovery objective, Chu Huaiwang mobilizes again toward Shangyu, is drawn through [[Wuguan|武关]], and loses near [[LantianBattle|蓝田之战]] while [[HanState|韩国]] and [[WeiState|魏国]] threaten Chu's rear. The source makes Chu the clearest state-level case for [[AngerDrivenWarDecision|因怒兴师]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-53-qinhuiwang-yong-zhangyi-huan-dipi-lvmo2cwbafcg2gnxliuo18fewimk]] shows Chu trying to convert that anger into personal revenge against [[ZhangYiStrategist|张仪]]. [[ChuHuaiwang|楚怀王]] refuses a land exchange for [[Qianzhong|黔中]] and asks [[QinState|秦国]] to hand over Zhang Yi instead, then imprisons him. Chu's court does not process the decision only through formal state interest: a favorite and [[ZhengXiu|郑袖]] make [[CourtBackchannelSurvival|宫廷后门自救]] decisive, while fear of Qin retaliation and the [[EnvoyKillingTaboo|不斩来使]] norm make release the less costly exit.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-60-zhongguo-di-yi-wei-bachi-chaozheng-de-taihou-jingshi-ta-ltazis9qo0dwvwmzxikk8mmpvosc]] moves Chu from Zhang Yi aftermath into a temporary Qin-aligned phase. [[XuanTaihou|芈八子 / 宣太后]] arranges a Chu bride for [[QinZhaoxiangwang|秦昭襄王]], Qin and Chu meet at [[Huangji|黄棘]], and Qin returns [[Shangyong|上庸]] to Chu. That shift makes Chu a target for [[QiState|齐国]], [[HanState|韩国]], and [[WeiState|魏国]], so [[ChuHuaiwang|楚怀王]] sends crown prince [[MiHeng|芈横]] as a hostage and receives Qin help through [[KeqingTong|客卿通]]. The alliance then breaks when Mi Heng kills a Qin official and escapes, making Chu the central case for [[HostageDiplomacyRupture|质子外交破裂]] and [[WarringStatesAllianceSwitching|战国同盟转向]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-62-ji-er-kuang-ni-jiujiu-de-tui-a-lumch-ibj8m94abjh5nvr3qhiqff]] shows Chu's leadership crisis after that rupture. [[QinZhaoxiangwang|秦昭襄王]] traps Chu Huaiwang at [[Wuguan|武关]] and detains him in [[Xianyang|咸阳]], demanding [[WuJunWarringStates|巫郡]] and [[Qianzhong|黔中]]. Chu's internal response centers on succession because [[MiHeng|芈横 / 楚顷襄王]] is in [[QiState|齐国]]; [[ZhaoJu|昭雎]] opposes installing a different prince, and Chu asks Qi to send Mi Heng home. The episode makes Chu a case where diplomatic isolation and [[CourtFeedbackCollapse|君臣反馈失灵]] leave the state able to continue the throne but unable to rescue the trapped king.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-63-degao-wangzhong-mengchangjun-shi-weijunzi-lmd0owe3rq-faponbwzhkaigprin]] records the next consequence. Chu sends envoys to Qin announcing that it already has a new king, making [[ChuHuaiwang|楚怀王]] less useful as a hostage. Qin then attacks through [[Wuguan|武关]], killing fifty thousand Chu troops and occupying sixteen cities. Chu therefore survives the succession problem but still pays a military price for Qin's failed coercive plan.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-70-xihuan-huayang-zuosi-de-qiguo-junzhu-luuyasp5-nhnikdcozzaueosb-md]] adds Chu indirectly to the fall of [[QiState|齐国]]. The episode says Chu does not join the main anti-Qi coalition but sends [[ZhuoChi|卓齿]] toward Ju as an apparent rescue force for [[QiMinWang|齐湣王]]. Zhuo Chi then arrests and kills Qi Min Wang, and the host leaves open whether Chu's role reflects righteous punishment, opportunistic spoils, or a tacit understanding with Yan.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-74-huanyuan-wanbi-guizhao-zhenxiang-lg1irvbbc1wp8oph2kmpatfoxebk]] adds an origin branch rather than a policy branch. [[BianHe|卞和]] discovers the raw stone that becomes [[HeShiBi|和氏璧]], but successive Chu courts misjudge it until the jade is finally cut open and named after him. Chu's role here is the setting for [[TreasureRecognitionRisk|识宝风险]] before the object later moves into Zhao-Qin diplomacy.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-75-3-gudai-de-jingji-zhanzheng-neng-you-duo-kepa-liybgoz8lspypax6dhuke2js0pxf]] adds a Spring-Autumn economic-war anecdote against Chu. [[QiHuanGong|齐桓公]] wants to punish Chu, but [[GuanZhong|管仲]] proposes buying Chu live deer at extremely high prices. The episode says Chu people shift effort from farming to catching deer while [[QiState|齐国]] quietly accumulates grain; when Qi later closes the border, Chu's food prices rise and people flee, making Chu submit without a direct campaign.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-76-shui-geile-chuqingxiangwang-de-zixin-lsut4cfic7xbbrmbmimrruf7n6y0]] returns to Chu's late-Warring-States position under [[MiHeng|楚顷襄王]]. The notice says Chu wants to join [[QiState|齐国]] and [[HanState|韩国]] against [[QinState|秦国]] and may also plot against the [[ZhouRoyalHouse|周王室]], but the host doubts the plausibility of such boldness after Chu's earlier fear of Qin. [[DongzhouWugong|东周武公]] then persuades Chu that Zhou land, people, and [[Jiuding|九鼎]] are not worth the symbolic cost, so Chu abandons the Zhou target.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-77-lianpo-yu-linxiangru-shishang-zui-zhuming-de-yidui-hao-jiyou-lmelnhhtfuzezvmmsuw4yf10plsb]] follows with a concrete Qin southern-pressure sequence. The episode says [[SimaCuo|司马错]] moves Longxi forces through [[BaShu|巴蜀]] toward Chu's Qianzhong region, forcing Chu to give up territory around the Han River and [[Shangyong|上庸]], while [[BaiQi|白起]] later attacks and takes places rendered as Yan, Deng, and Xiling. Chu's role here is mainly as the target Qin chooses when attacking Wei might reawaken eastern coalition rescue.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-82-daqin-de-hewuqi-baiqi-laile-lo0w8mifkijhzmx7fdwcimmv7ade]] turns that pressure into a capital-region catastrophe. Bai Qi takes the Chu capital rendered as Ying, burns Yiling, and Qin establishes [[NanCommandery|南郡]] in the conquered area; in the following year Qin also takes [[WuJunWarringStates|巫郡]] and [[Qianzhong|黔中]]. The episode then qualifies Chu's collapse by showing [[MiHeng|楚顷襄王]] moving toward Chen, recalling [[ZhuangXin|庄辛]], and recovering some territory through the space-and-time logic of [[StrategicDepthDefense|战略纵深防御]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-84-fangzhi-qinguo-miechu-jing-yong-zhe-zhao-llgh12eistejijsl6yildzkixocm]] gives Chu a diplomatic rescue after that strategic-depth survival. [[Chunshenjun|黄歇 / 春申君]] does not claim Chu can defeat Qin directly; he argues that attacking Chu would make Qin depend on resentful Han-Wei routes or accept poor gains through difficult terrain. Chu's survival therefore comes from converting its geography and Qin's corridor risk into [[InterestReframingPersuasion|利益重构式说服]], after which Qin stops [[BaiQi|白起]] and seeks Qin-Chu friendship.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-85-xuantaihou-weihe-sitong-30-nian-hou-ansha-le-laoqingren-lg7zy6vqtdjcufou4-mox5qkg2ct5]] gives the immediate price of that friendship. [[MiHeng|楚顷襄王]] sends the Chu crown prince to [[QinState|秦国]] as hostage, and [[Chunshenjun|黄歇 / 春申君]] accompanies him. The episode treats this as a familiar but risky Warring States guarantee: it stabilizes the Qin-Chu relationship for the moment, while Huang Xie's proximity to the hostage prince becomes the source of his later rise.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-92-lishishang-di-yi-wei-zhuming-tiangou-shi-shui-1-lmwf0q0-3hxb6d4hro8fdmc3zdxp]] returns to that hostage cost nine years later. Mi Heng is gravely ill while [[MiWan|太子完]] remains in [[QinState|秦国]], so Chu's continuity depends on whether the hostage heir can return before another prince is installed. [[Chunshenjun|黄歇 / 春申君]] first tries to persuade [[FanJu|范雎]] that returning Mi Wan serves Qin's interest, then proposes a disguised escape when Qin insists on verification.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-92-lishishang-di-yi-wei-zhuming-tiangou-shi-shui-2-lt1nwraiuuvpy2tzo-zwaoqsajzx]] completes the succession repair. Mi Wan reaches Chu, succeeds as [[MiWan|楚考烈王]], and turns Huang Xie's risk into chancellorship and the Chunshenjun title. The same episode also turns Chu from a military-diplomatic case into a literary-memory case through [[SongYu|宋玉]], [[WushanGoddess|巫山神女]], and [[XiangwangYouyiShennvWuxin|襄王有意，神女无心]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-93-zai-mengli-cangzhe-toutian-gaiming-1-ljshdryvf9qgabtn-siontthzsok]] makes the succession repair look limited. In [[MiWan|楚考烈王]]'s first year, Chu cedes substantial territory to [[QinState|秦国]] without fighting. The episode uses this as another sign that [[Chunshenjun|黄歇 / 春申君]]'s rise and the successful return of the hostage prince do not halt Chu's downward strategic trend.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-96-maosui-zijian-1-lvqbmaabsvbdz2zibgmseidx5phe]] brings Chu back as the requested rescue state during the [[Handan|邯郸]] siege. [[ZhaoState|赵国]] turns to Chu because Wei and Qi are unreliable, sending [[Pingyuanjun|平原君]] with a retainer delegation. The episode stops before Chu's court response, so Chu's role here is source-scoped setup for rescue diplomacy rather than a completed intervention.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-96-maosui-zijian-2-ltlobdujy-hdipimfdupvwod2ech]] completes that court response. [[Pingyuanjun|平原君]] cannot move [[MiWan|楚考烈王]] after a full day of argument, but [[MaoSui|毛遂]] presses the king through proximity, Chu's memory of [[BaiQi|白起]]'s devastation, and [[DignityBasedPersuasion|dignity-based persuasion]]. Chu agrees to [[HezongAlliance|合纵]] and sends [[Chunshenjun|黄歇 / 春申君]] north with troops.

The source then immediately limits Chu's rescue capacity. Chu does not border Zhao, so aid to [[Handan|邯郸]] depends on passage through [[WeiState|魏国]]; when [[JinBi|晋鄙]] stops at [[YeWarringStates|邺城]], the episode turns Chu from willing ally into distant ally constrained by [[CoalitionTransitDependency|transit dependency]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-97-1-baiqi-zhisi-lrqoq1sbxljbvuayq3o1hxebxhu6]] records the practical release of that constraint after [[Xinlingjun|信陵君]] seizes Wei command. Once Wei's army moves, Chu's aid can contribute to the relief of [[Handan|邯郸]], making Chu part of the working anti-Qin coalition rather than only a pledged but blocked ally. The episode's strategic reading treats this as one reason Zhao survives as a barrier against [[QinState|秦国]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-99-2-mingming-yilian-baixiang-de-caize-ping-shenme-baixiang-lhfyb6hzcqkpbzdrwsxajb7hhmhe]] adds Chu as an occupier of culturally important former [[LuState|鲁国]] territory. [[Chunshenjun|黄歇 / 春申君]] appoints [[Xunzi|荀子]] to [[LanlingWarringStates|兰陵 / Lanling]], making Chu's post-conquest problem administrative and cultural as well as military.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-99-4-shui-shi-kongzi-hou-zui-weidade-sixiangjia-lr4phoq9chc4tz0yb8y95gy21qdd]] continues that Chu-Lu-Xunzi branch. The source says Chu occupies Lu more fully, exiles Lu's ruler to Ju, and leaves Lu only nominally present. It also dates Xunzi's return to Lanling to 251 BCE under [[MiWan|楚考烈王]]'s reign, then says [[Chunshenjun|黄歇 / 春申君]] is killed during Chu turmoil in 238 BCE, ending Xunzi's ability to hold the office.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-102-1-lvbuwei-shi-jiangziya-houren-lveg-wsmggroaxjsnxopaevexqod]] gives the terminal Lu notice in sharper political language. Chu destroys [[LuState|鲁国]], moves Lu Qing Gong from Ju to Biancheng, and makes him a commoner. The episode uses the notice to stress Lu's surprisingly late survival rather than to develop new Chu strategy, so Chu's role here remains the final absorber of an old Zhou enfeoffed state.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-102-2-kongzi-zai-luguo-de-shenshi-zhimi-lphy12exg4u7f-30qkir96gv634r]] supplies that missing strategic explanation. The episode argues that Chu has both the ability and the attention to destroy Lu because Qin and the Three Jin are absorbed by western pressure, Qi is weakened and isolationist, and Yan is too distant. Chu's final absorption of Lu therefore becomes a case of [[SmallStateSurvivalByDistraction|小国因大国无暇而存续]] ending when one great power has room to move.

The same source then turns inward to Chu's own elite geography. [[Chunshenjun|黄歇 / 春申君]] asks [[MiWan|楚考烈王]] to move his fief from the Huai north border to [[JiangdongWarringStates|江东]], presenting the old borderland as too exposed to [[QiState|齐国]] while the host questions whether Chu's true main danger is still [[QinState|秦国]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-104-yinmou-shisuan-rang-qinguo-bantu-you-you-you-pengzhang-le-lgnyhbduqutegtksrjwcvhmn8-t6]] adds Chu as the final refuge of [[LianPo|廉颇]] after his exclusion from [[ZhaoState|赵国]] and uneasy residence in [[WeiState|魏国]]. The source treats his move to Chu less as a Chu strategic initiative than as evidence that late Warring States talent can survive physically outside its home state while losing the political fit needed to act.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-107-zuihou-yici-hezong-faqin-fa-le-ge-jimo-lrzq1qks7z-oromd-5f-dgqjwjhw]] gives Chu the aftermath of the final anti-Qin [[HezongAlliance|合纵]] attempt. In the Chunshenjun-version tradition, [[MiWan|楚考烈王]] is nominal leader and [[Chunshenjun|黄歇 / 春申君]] commands the coalition, but the campaign fails and damages Chunshenjun's standing. [[ZhuYingWarringStates|朱英]] then argues that Qin's pressure through weakened Wei makes the Chen-centered capital geography unsafe, leading Chu to move to [[ShouchunWarringStates|寿春]] and call it Ying.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-109-1-lishishang-zui-zhuming-de-huangdi-bei-lv-le-liawtywj4c9n0zpii-rxl7cguzyy]] turns Chu's late pressure from external defense to internal succession. [[MiWan|楚考烈王]] still has no son, [[Chunshenjun|黄歇 / 春申君]] fears the loss of his long-time patron's protection, and [[LiYuanWarringStates|李源]] plus [[LiYuanSister|李源妹妹]] exploit that fear through [[PregnancySuccessionSubstitution|孕身继承替换]]. Chu's problem is therefore no longer only that Qin pushes from outside; the court's own unresolved heir question lets private paternity, ministerial fear, and royal-in-law ambition reorganize the future throne.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-109-2-chunshenjun-bei-huangdi-dajiuge-quanzu-miemen-locma2plu7v-vsssm5dr5frc3szw]] completes that internal collapse. [[ZhuYingWarringStates|朱英]] sees that the succession will give [[Chunshenjun|黄歇 / 春申君]] both regency opportunity and mortal danger, but Chunshenjun ignores him. When [[MiWan|楚考烈王]] dies, [[LiYuanWarringStates|李源 / 李元]] kills Chunshenjun at the palace gate, destroys his household, places [[ChuYouwang|楚幽王]] on the throne, and lets the Li siblings control court politics. The source treats this as the point where Chu's decline shifts from external Qin pressure plus defensive relocation into a succession regime compromised at birth.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-111-2-wo-yu-lvbuwei-de-gekong-duihua-ltpbkg1echouxwjyhsfax2swfzch]] adds a terse external-pressure notice after that succession collapse. In a year also marked by drought in the host's reading, [[WeiState|魏国]] attacks Chu and [[QinState|秦国]] supports Wei with four armies. Chu is not the episode's main subject, but the notice reinforces the late-Warring-States pattern that even vulnerable states keep fighting one another instead of rebuilding effective [[HezongAlliance|合纵]] discipline.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-112-1-yaojia-shi-ruhe-ping-yizhangzui-po-siguo-hezong-lthw0fvvinmf6ghxpqut60agsueg]] keeps Chu in the post-terminal-hezong coalition residue. The source names Chu as one of the four states in the Zhao-Han-Wei-Chu anti-Qin plan that [[YaoJia|姚贾]] breaks. It does not add a new Chu ruler or internal episode, but it shows that Chu remains necessary to any late anti-Qin alignment while also vulnerable to Qin-backed bribery and court-level division.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-114-1-jintian-liaoliao-yingzheng-dou-you-naxie-canren-shouduan-lmqky6g3qi9nklpdnye4lk5rrbz6]] returns Chu to internal succession instability while Qin is destroying Zhao. [[ChuYouwang|楚幽王]] dies, Chu people install his younger brother [[ChuAiwang|熊昊 / 熊犹 / 楚哀王]], and then the older half-brother [[ChuFuchu|熊负刍 / 楚王负刍]] has him killed and takes the throne. The source also preserves a name variant, 熊昊 in one Shiji table tradition and 熊犹 in the Chu hereditary-house tradition, so the event belongs to both late Chu succession collapse and [[TextualVariantPoliticalStakes|textual-variant caution]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-116-shishang-zuican-taizi-bei-fuwang-qinshou-zhansha-lthruqxtd4iqofdxzvim58ph8rlk]] makes Chu the next major Qin target after Yan is largely broken. [[WangBenQin|王贲]] has already taken more than ten Chu cities, and [[QinShiHuang|嬴政 / 秦始皇]] asks [[LiXinQin|李信]] and [[WangJianQin|王翦]] how many troops are needed for conquest. The source leaves the campaign result for later, but frames the initial decision through [[AnchoringEffect|锚定效应]]: Li Xin's 200,000-person answer makes Wang Jian's 600,000-person requirement look excessive.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-117-1-papa-dalian-lishishang-shuangwen-de-kaishan-bizu-lr0h-kfeguvatattnj-mz8fgvnc]] keeps Chu as the concurrent southern front rather than the episode's main battlefield. The source says [[LiXinQin|李信]] and [[MengWu|蒙武]] go south against Chu while [[WangBenQin|王贲]] attacks [[WeiState|魏国]] and destroys it. Chu therefore remains the large unfinished conquest problem beside the completed Wei endpoint.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-117-2-xuezhedian-qinchao-wujiang-jingran-ruci-gaoqingshang-lsyw3eitcnzfexl1l6vfh2gbtaoy]] gives Chu a successful defensive counter inside that unfinished problem. [[XiangYan|项燕]] turns Qin's first 200,000-person campaign into a trap by using peace signals and a diversion against [[NanCommandery|南郡]], defeating Li Xin and [[MengTian|蒙恬]]. Chu remains large enough, and its command active enough, that Qin must return to [[WangJianQin|王翦]]'s 600,000-person scale for the next phase.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-118-1-wangjian-lv-liushiwan-dajun-duizhan-xiangyan-haoxi-kaichang-le-lsf2puinwg0m3bgyi25znof7aznu]] follows that next phase to Chu's terminal battlefield collapse. Chu mobilizes nationally under Xiang Yan, but Wang Jian and [[MengWu|蒙武]] refuse to fight until Chu's provisions and morale fall. The source then has Xiang Yan defeated near [[QinanChuBattlefield|蕲南]], [[ChuFuchu|熊负刍 / 楚王负刍]] captured in 223 BCE, and [[ChangpingJun|昌平君]] reopened as a source-critical problem through [[LiKaiyuan|李开元]]'s [[QinMi|《秦谜》]] reconstruction.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-118-2-chi-gua-le-wangshi-de-gua-bao-tian-lns7jt-kbv3qunm66hzltdgugbco]] completes that collapse as both family history and territorial conversion. It reads Changping Jun / Xiong Qi through [[PreQinSurnameClanIdentity|先秦姓氏身份]] as a Chu royal figure inside Qin, challenges the earlier claim that [[MiWan|楚考烈王]] had no sons, and narrates Xiang Yan's retreat, Changping Jun's brief kingship, Changping Jun's death, and Xiang Yan's suicide. Qin then creates a temporary Chu commandery before later dividing the region, while officials and noble families from Chu and the Three Jin are imagined as possible refugees into [[QiState|齐国]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-119-2-zanmen-dou-yuanwang-le-beifu-2000-duonian-heiguo-de-yegong-haolong-lh-h-gezpqtvnyq40zlqac8s-5e]] jumps back to a Spring-and-Autumn Chu backstory used by the Qinji 119 moral comparison. In 479 BCE, [[ZiXiChu|子西]] recalls [[BaiGongSheng|白公胜]] despite [[YeGong|叶公]]'s warning, Bai uses a victory over [[WuState|吴国]] to enter the capital armed, kills Zi Xi, seizes [[ChuHuiwang|楚惠王]], and is then defeated in the [[BaiGongShengRebellion|白公胜之乱]]. For Chu's page, the episode adds an older personnel-judgment and court-violence case rather than a new step in the late-Qin conquest sequence.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-120-1-shui-shi-huangdi-zhong-de-mabaonan-lhw8vsj6ta6rjdkugcvfohaqqxc0]] adds Chu to Qin's post-unification justification list. The source-scoped Qin story says Chu broke covenant and attacked [[NanCommandery|南郡]], recoding the southern conquest as punishment and self-defense within [[ConquestSelfDefenseNarrative|兼并自卫叙事]] after Chu had already fallen militarily.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-122-3-qinshihuang-jing-he-shenxian-si-qilai-le-ljnw5estg6dvgfllm3kfcq6ulhxu]] adds a post-conquest Chu sacred-geography aftershock. Although Chu has already been conquered, Qin Shi Huang's reaction at [[XiangshanShrine|湘山祠]] treats [[XiangJun|湘君]] and the surrounding landscape as a local authority that can be humiliated. The episode's contrast with Qin's respect for Qi rites makes Chu not only a military opponent but also a conquered cultural world where Qin rule can create resentment through religious insult.
