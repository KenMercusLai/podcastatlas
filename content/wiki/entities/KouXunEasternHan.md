## Overview

寇恂 / Kou Xun is a Guangwu-era commander and official whose current profile combines hard surrender coercion with local trust-based pacification. Hanji 709 uses him as the minister who tells [[LiuXiu|刘秀]] not to expose himself at [[GaoJunEasternHan|高峻]]'s Gaoping stronghold and then as the envoy-commander who executes [[HuangfuWenEasternHan|皇甫文]] to force Gao Jun's surrender. Hanji 707 adds the Yingchuan layer, where his presence helps calm local disorder strongly enough that residents ask Liu Xiu to leave him there longer.

## Current Profile

Hanji 709 gives Kou Xun two connected profiles. The first is strategic restraint: when Liu Xiu wants to travel from Chang'an toward Gaoping, Kou Xun argues that Gao Jun is not worth imperial exposure and that Liu Xiu's presence would overvalue a holdout whose submission can be handled by a lesser mission.

The second is coercive surrender management. Kou Xun accepts the mission, meets Gao Jun's adviser Huangfu Wen, judges Huangfu Wen's hard line as the real center of resistance, and kills him despite objections that the act may violate negotiation norms. He then sends Huangfu Wen's deputy back with a stark choice: surrender quickly or prepare for assault. Gao Jun surrenders the same day.

Hanji 707 supplies the softer local-governance side. When Yingchuan bandits rise while Liu Xiu is campaigning west, Kou Xun diagnoses the disorder as opportunistic rather than deeply rooted. Liu Xiu's southern movement and Kou Xun's local role are enough for the bandits to submit. Residents then block Liu Xiu's road and ask to "borrow Kou Jun for one year," so Liu Xiu keeps Kou Xun at Changshe to pacify the area.

The current synthesis treats Kou Xun as a flexible stabilizer. He can perform [[MouDestructionStrike|伐谋式斩首]] when a holdout's planning center must be broken, but he can also become a trusted local presence whose credibility reduces the need for heavy fighting.

## Key Characteristics

- Guangwu-era adviser who discourages Liu Xiu from personally risking himself at Gaoping.
- Field envoy sent with imperial authority after Liu Xiu rejects his initial caution.
- Hard coercive negotiator who executes Huangfu Wen rather than continue a stalled persuasion sequence.
- Tactical reader of morale who treats Gao Jun's adviser as the resistance's key planning node.
- Yingchuan stabilizer whose presence helps convert opportunistic bandit disorder into surrender.
- Public-trust figure whom local residents explicitly ask Liu Xiu to retain.

## Qualifications

This page is still bounded to two source notes and does not reconstruct Kou Xun's full career. The transcript form 寇勋 is normalized to 寇恂 for the Guangwu-era context. Huangfu Wen's killing is preserved as an extreme forced-surrender case, not as a general rule that envoys or advisers can be killed without strategic or moral cost. The Yingchuan branch shows public confidence in Kou Xun, but the source does not give a detailed administrative program beyond his retention at Changshe.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 707 as Kou Xun's Yingchuan pacification and popular-retention layer.
- Reframed Kou Xun as a flexible stabilizer whose methods range from coercive adviser killing to trust-bearing local reassurance.
- Preserved Hanji 709 as the Gaoping surrender and "上兵伐谋" case.
