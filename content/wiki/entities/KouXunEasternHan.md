## Overview

寇恂 / Kou Xun is a Guangwu-era commander and official whose current profile now combines local legal enforcement, conflict de-escalation, hard surrender coercion, and trust-based pacification. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-686-liuxiu-fashi-zhi-ai-yinlihua-weihe-li-guoshi-wei-hou-lnqlqgq54srb1zzzgemfwuwujhvc|Hanji 686]] adds the earliest current Yingchuan law layer: Kou executes [[JiaFuEasternHan|贾复]]'s murderer-subordinate, avoids a direct clash with Jia Fu, and lets [[LiuXiu|刘秀]] convert private anger into public command discipline. Hanji 707 and Hanji 709 then show the softer public-trust side and the harder Gaoping surrender mission.

## Current Profile

Hanji 686 gives Kou Xun the legal-order and de-escalation layer. As Yingchuan administrator, he arrests a Jia Fu subordinate who has killed someone and executes him publicly in the market. When Jia Fu treats the act as an insult and threatens to kill him, Kou Xun refuses armed escalation, invokes the Lin Xiangru/Lian Po precedent, supplies Jia Fu's army generously, personally welcomes him, and sends Gu Chong to report the case to Liu Xiu. Liu Xiu then summons Kou, seats him beside Jia Fu, and says generals cannot fight privately while the realm remains unsettled.

Hanji 707 supplies the softer local-governance side. When Yingchuan bandits rise while Liu Xiu is campaigning west, Kou Xun diagnoses the disorder as opportunistic rather than deeply rooted. Liu Xiu's southern movement and Kou Xun's local role are enough for the bandits to submit. Residents then block Liu Xiu's road and ask to "borrow Kou Jun for one year," so Liu Xiu keeps Kou Xun at Changshe to pacify the area.

Hanji 709 gives Kou Xun two connected profiles. The first is strategic restraint: when Liu Xiu wants to travel from Chang'an toward Gaoping, Kou Xun argues that Gao Jun is not worth imperial exposure. The second is coercive surrender management: Kou accepts the mission, kills [[HuangfuWenEasternHan|皇甫文]], releases Huangfu Wen's deputy with a stark surrender choice, and forces [[GaoJunEasternHan|高峻]] to surrender the same day.

The current synthesis treats Kou Xun as a flexible stabilizer. He can enforce law against a powerful commander's subordinate, de-escalate among founding generals, perform [[MouDestructionStrike|伐谋式斩首]] when a holdout's planning center must be broken, and become a trusted local presence whose credibility reduces the need for heavy fighting.

## Key Characteristics

- Yingchuan administrator who publicly executes a murderer protected by Jia Fu's command prestige.
- De-escalator who avoids personal combat with Jia Fu while preserving the legal result.
- Public-order official whose case depends on Liu Xiu backing discipline over private military anger.
- Local stabilizer whose presence helps convert opportunistic bandit disorder into surrender.
- Guangwu-era adviser who discourages Liu Xiu from personally risking himself at Gaoping.
- Hard coercive negotiator who executes Huangfu Wen rather than continue a stalled persuasion sequence.
- Flexible stabilizer whose methods range from hospitality and avoidance to force-backed surrender pressure.

## Qualifications

This page is still bounded to three source notes and does not reconstruct Kou Xun's full career. Hanji 686's Jia Fu conflict shows legal authority surviving military anger, but it also shows that the outcome depends on Kou Xun's de-escalation and Liu Xiu's final mediation, not on an independent court process. Huangfu Wen's killing remains an extreme forced-surrender case, not a general rule that envoys or advisers can be killed without strategic or moral cost. The transcript form 寇勋 is normalized to 寇恂 for the Guangwu-era context.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 686 as Kou Xun's earliest current layer: Yingchuan legal enforcement, Jia Fu de-escalation, and Liu Xiu mediation.
- Reframed Kou Xun as a flexible stabilizer whose methods include public law enforcement, hospitality-based conflict control, local pacification, and hard surrender coercion.
- Preserved Hanji 707 as the Yingchuan pacification and popular-retention layer.
- Preserved Hanji 709 as the Gaoping surrender and "上兵伐谋" case.
