## Overview

寇恂 / Kou Xun is a Guangwu-era commander and adviser currently grounded in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-709-mian-dui-bangjia-an-guren-weihe-buguan-renzhi-sihuo-luknor8yw839qwxogeyzwluo6qe2|Hanji 709]]. The episode first uses him as the minister who tells [[LiuXiu|刘秀]] not to personally go to [[GaoJunEasternHan|高峻]]'s Gaoping stronghold, then as the envoy-commander who executes [[HuangfuWenEasternHan|皇甫文]] and forces Gao Jun's surrender.

## Current Profile

Hanji 709 gives Kou Xun two connected profiles. The first is strategic restraint: when Liu Xiu wants to travel from Chang'an toward Gaoping, Kou Xun argues that Gao Jun is not worth imperial exposure and that Liu Xiu's presence would overvalue a holdout whose submission can be handled by a lesser mission.

The second is coercive surrender management. Kou Xun accepts the mission, meets Gao Jun's adviser Huangfu Wen, judges Huangfu Wen's hard line as the real center of resistance, and kills him despite objections that the act may violate negotiation norms. He then sends Huangfu Wen's deputy back with a stark choice: surrender quickly or prepare for assault. Gao Jun surrenders the same day.

The current synthesis treats Kou Xun as the operational actor behind [[MouDestructionStrike|伐谋式斩首]]: he reads a rebel position through its adviser and morale structure, then removes the planning center to make surrender the rational move.

## Key Characteristics

- Guangwu-era adviser who discourages Liu Xiu from personally risking himself at Gaoping.
- Field envoy sent with imperial authority after Liu Xiu rejects his initial caution.
- Hard coercive negotiator who executes Huangfu Wen rather than continue a stalled persuasion sequence.
- Tactical reader of morale who treats Gao Jun's adviser as the resistance's key planning node.
- Forced-surrender actor whose action turns threat into immediate capitulation.
- Norm-straining figure because Huangfu Wen is killed in an envoy/adviser context.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 709 and does not reconstruct Kou Xun's full career. The transcript form 寇勋 is normalized to 寇恂 for the Guangwu-era context. Huangfu Wen's killing is preserved as an extreme forced-surrender case, not as a general rule that envoys or advisers can be killed without strategic or moral cost.
