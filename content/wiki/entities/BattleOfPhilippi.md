# Battle of Philippi / 腓立比战役

## Overview

The Battle of Philippi / 腓立比战役 is the Macedonian campaign in which [[MarkAntony]] and [[OctavianAugustus|Octavian]] defeat the armies of [[GaiusCassiusLonginus|Cassius]] and [[MarcusBrutus|Brutus]], ending the main organized military attempt to restore the [[RomanRepublic|Roman Republic]] after Caesar's assassination.

## Current Profile

[[305-the-fall-of-the-roman-republic-glt6995332578]] presents both coalitions as mass war machines. Brutus and Cassius raise money, troops, cavalry, and approximately twenty legions in the East; Antony and Octavian cross the Adriatic with a roughly comparable legionary force. The liberators hold a strong supply position near Philippi and the Via Egnatia, while Antony takes the more active battlefield role and Octavian is ill.

The battle unfolds unevenly. Brutus's forces take Octavian's camp while Antony defeats Cassius's wing. Cassius kills himself before learning of Brutus's success, and the episode treats that misjudgment as a possible turning point rather than a settled single cause. Brutus waits several weeks, fights again, loses, and dies. Later traditions disagree about whether Antony honors his body or Octavian has it beheaded.

## Key Characteristics

- It pits two large commander-centered coalitions against each other rather than a constitutional state against a small conspiracy.
- Supply position, marshland maneuver, divided wings, and incomplete battlefield information shape the outcome.
- Cassius's suicide breaks the republican partnership before Brutus's final defeat.
- Brutus's death makes the battle a political endpoint as well as a military loss.
- Later ghost stories and the phrase “last of the Romans” turn the campaign into a tragedy of republican memory.

## Evidence

- Force and position: [[305-the-fall-of-the-roman-republic-glt6995332578]] describes roughly twenty legions on each side, the liberators' Via Egnatia position, and their port access.
- First engagement: [[305-the-fall-of-the-roman-republic-glt6995332578]] says Brutus captures Octavian's camp while Antony breaks Cassius's forces.
- Command collapse: [[305-the-fall-of-the-roman-republic-glt6995332578]] reports Cassius's premature suicide and Brutus's later decision to fight again after a three-week delay.
- Memory: [[305-the-fall-of-the-roman-republic-glt6995332578]] presents competing treatment-of-the-body traditions, hostile captive reactions to Octavian, and later praise of Brutus and Cassius.

## Qualifications

Legion and troop totals, marsh maneuvers, the effect of Octavian's illness, Cassius's information failure, and the competing accounts of Brutus's body are source-scoped. The battle ends the principal liberator armies, but later resistance and the hunt for Caesar's assassins continue.
