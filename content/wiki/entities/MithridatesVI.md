# Mithridates VI / 米特里达梯六世

## Overview

Mithridates VI / 米特里达梯六世 is the king of Pontus whose invasion of Roman Asia creates the lucrative eastern command contested by [[GaiusMarius]] and [[LuciusCorneliusSulla]], then forces Sulla to fight abroad while Rome collapses into armed politics.

## Current Profile

Episode 702 presents Mithridates as prepared, informed, and alert to Greek resentment against Roman rule in Asia. While Rome is still strained by the [[SocialWar]], [[ManiusAquillius]] helps provoke conflict, Mithridates invades Asia, Roman power collapses locally, and Aquillius flees. Roman elites then treat the eastern campaign as both revenge and opportunity because glory, money, and prestige are expected to flow to whoever receives the command.

Episode 703 deepens Mithridates from command-prize trigger into anti-Roman imperial challenger. He uses eastern prophecies to cast himself as a great king punishing Roman overreach, executes Aquillius with molten gold as propaganda against Roman greed, orders massacres of Romans and Italians in Asian cities, and supports [[Athens]] after [[AristionAthens]]'s revolt. His armies are eventually defeated by Sulla in Greece, and he accepts a treaty returning Asia and conquered territory to Roman oversight. The settlement is generous because Sulla's enemies in Rome are now the more urgent threat.

## Key Characteristics

- Mithridates is portrayed as a prepared ruler who understands Roman weakness during and after the Social War.
- His invasion succeeds partly because Greek communities in Asia resent Roman and Italian exploitation.
- He turns anti-Roman resentment into prophecy, punishment theater, massacre, and alliance-building.
- The war against him is treated in Rome as attractive because it promises glory and wealth.
- His campaign turns a foreign war into the immediate object of Marius-Sulla rivalry.
- His defeat does not produce total destruction because Sulla prioritizes returning to Italy over finishing the eastern war.

## Qualifications

The current page remains centered on Mithridates as the Roman Civil War sequence uses him. The reported 80,000 massacre figure is treated by episode 703 as inflated but powerful because Romans believed it. The wider chronology of the Mithridatic Wars and Pontic policy remains outside the current synthesis.

## What Changed

- Added Mithridates's anti-Roman prophecy use, Aquillius execution, Asian massacre order, Athens alliance, defeat by Sulla, and pragmatic treaty.
- Reframed him from command-prize trigger into an imperial challenger whose eastern campaign and Roman domestic crisis mutually shape each other.

## Relationships

- [[ManiusAquillius]] - Roman figure whose provocation and execution become part of Mithridates's anti-Roman propaganda.
- [[Athens]] - Greek city whose revolt Mithridates supports against Roman influence.
- [[AristionAthens]] - Athenian revolutionary-tyrant who seeks alliance with Mithridates.
- [[LuciusCorneliusSulla]] - consul and commander who marches on Rome to keep the Mithridatic command and later defeats Mithridates in Greece.
- [[GaiusMarius]] - rival claimant who tries to take the Mithridatic command through Sulpicius.
- [[RepublicanCommandTransferCrisis]] - domestic crisis created when the Mithridatic command is reassigned.
- [[RomanExpansionMoralAnxiety]] - adjacent mechanism because Asian wealth, tax extraction, and resentment shape the war's background.
- [[LateRomanRepublicCrisis]] - broader Roman crisis intensified when a foreign war becomes a civil command prize.
