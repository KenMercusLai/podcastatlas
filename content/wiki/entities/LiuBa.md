# 刘巴 / Liu Ba

## Overview
刘巴 / Liu Ba is presented as a prestigious late-Han scholar who repeatedly resists alignment with [[LiuBei|刘备]], enters his regime only after the conquest of Yizhou, and then becomes useful in addressing the treasury crisis after taking Chengdu.

## Current Profile
Hanji 1145 supplies the political and interpersonal prehistory. It says Liu Ba chooses Cao Cao while other Jingzhou figures move toward Liu Bei, rejects Zhuge Liang's invitation, reaches Jiaozhi and then Yizhou, and repeatedly warns Liu Zhang against admitting Liu Bei. After Chengdu falls, Liu Ba apologizes and enters the victorious regime. His later refusal to converse with Zhang Fei makes the tension public: Zhang Zhao calls the behavior narrow, while Sun Quan interprets accommodation to Zhang Fei as possible flattery of Liu Bei.

Hanji 1146 then supplies the capability payoff. Liu Ba recommends issuing “值百钱,” channeling important goods through the new currency, establishing an official market, and using the money to purchase grain and cloth. Together, the episodes portray a technically capable but proud adviser whose usefulness survives prior opposition and personal friction.

The profile remains bounded: the supplied summaries do not establish Liu Ba's exact offices, motives, complete travel chronology, the monetary policy's full institutional design, its longer-run effects, or how much of the result should be attributed to him alone.

## Key Characteristics
- Repeatedly opposes or avoids Liu Bei before Yizhou's conquest, including warnings to Liu Zhang.
- Maintains status-conscious independence after entering Liu Bei's regime, most sharply in his treatment of Zhang Fei.
- Offers a concrete fiscal response to an immediate post-conquest treasury shortage.
- Combines coin denomination with market rules and official procurement rather than treating minting as an isolated act.
- Becomes organizationally useful through problem-solving capacity rather than personal accommodation.

## Qualifications
The sources are structured episode summaries rather than verified transcripts or primary texts. Liu Ba's family standing, Cao-side mission, Jiaozhi and Yizhou route, warnings, invitation, apology, dialogue, treatment of Zhang Fei, offices, and motives require broader historical verification. The coin standard, exchange rate, monopoly scope, official-market design, fiscal proceeds, price effects, and civilian burden require numismatic and institutional evidence. A short-run treasury gain does not by itself establish sustainable monetary success, while scholarly independence does not by itself justify status contempt.

## What Changed
- Added the pre-conquest opposition, Yizhou incorporation, and Zhang Fei conflict that make Liu Ba's later fiscal use a case of capability surviving personal friction.
- Preserved Zhang Zhao and Sun Quan's competing judgments rather than reducing Liu Ba to either principled independence or simple arrogance.
