# George Kurtz

## Overview
George Kurtz is the CEO of [[CrowdStrike]] and an operator-focused commentator on AI-enabled cyber offense, identity compromise, and enterprise defense.

## Current Profile
Across the bounded interviews, Kurtz's central position is that AI compresses discovery and attack timelines while widening the population able to conduct sophisticated operations. His examples move from adaptive malware and agent permission chains to old vulnerability exploitation, rushed patches, stolen sessions, and fraudulent employees. He argues that defenders need comparable AI access, broad telemetry, continuous attack-path testing, and practiced response relationships, while treating offensive action as primarily a government function.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames AI cyber risk through operational timelines and attacker-defender asymmetry rather than model capability alone.
- Connects software vulnerabilities to identity, browsers, sessions, help desks, hiring, and delegated agent authority.
- Uses concrete incident accounts to argue for AI-native defensive detection and response.
- Treats safe patch deployment and legacy modernization as bottlenecks after accelerated discovery.
- Separates corporate defense and law-enforcement coordination from private offensive cyber action.

## Qualifications
Both sources are executive interviews rather than independent technical audits. Incident counts, attack-framework performance, vulnerability-growth figures, employee-attribution cases, and causal interpretations are not reproducible from the supplied notes. Kurtz's role at a security vendor also gives him a commercial interest in AI-native defense, although that does not negate the operational mechanisms he describes.

## What Changed
- Added the “break or breach” patching dilemma and legacy modernization as central parts of Kurtz's profile.
- Expanded his threat model from adaptive malware and agent permissions to exploit chaining, session theft, and autonomous attack scale.
- Added his distinction between corporate defense and government-led offensive cyber operations.
