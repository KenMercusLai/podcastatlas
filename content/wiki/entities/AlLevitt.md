# Al Levitt

## Overview

Al Levitt was an aspiring Hollywood screenwriter whose military service placed him in the Army Air Forces' [[FirstMotionPictureUnit|First Motion Picture Unit]] during the Second World War. The bounded source emphasizes the contrast between his youthful communist affiliation and his work beside the future anti-communist president [[RonaldReagan|Ronald Reagan]].

## Current Profile

[[teaser-the-compromises-of-al-levitt-teaser-the-compromises-of-al-levitt]] presents Levitt's early path as closely shared with his wife Helen: both grew up in New York, joined the Young Communist League in college, married, and drove to Hollywood. He hoped to become a screenwriter, but the draft interrupted that ambition just as a job appeared close.

Levitt then spent about eighteen months at the former Hal Roach Studios in Culver City, where the First Motion Picture Unit produced military training films and newsreels. His surviving recollection focuses less on his own production role than on Reagan's conspicuous arrival from cavalry service, which Levitt says made Reagan a laughingstock at the post.

## Key Characteristics

- A New Yorker whose college years included membership in the Young Communist League.
- A married migrant to Hollywood pursuing screenwriting before military conscription interrupted the plan.
- A wartime member of the [[FirstMotionPictureUnit|First Motion Picture Unit]] at the former Hal Roach Studios.
- A firsthand observer who later recalled Reagan through the sharp contrast between awkward military presentation and subsequent political stature.
