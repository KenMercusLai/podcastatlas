# Wovoka

## Overview

Wovoka, also known to white acquaintances as Jack Wilson, was the Paiute religious teacher whose teachings became the basis of the [[GhostDance]] movement described in the source.

## Current Profile

The episode presents Wovoka as an English-speaking Paiute man who worked for a rancher, absorbed Christian ideas, and reportedly experienced a vision during the 1889 solar eclipse. His message emphasized peace, love, nonviolence, communal dancing, contact with the dead, and an approaching renewal in which Native people and the lost world could return.

The source reaches him mainly through transmission. [[LakotaPeople|Lakota]] emissaries including Kicking Bear and Short Bull carried the teaching east, and the hosts caution that their reports may have adapted or distorted it. Claims about bulletproof shirts and the removal of white settlers therefore cannot be assigned straightforwardly to Wovoka's own message.

## Key Characteristics

- He combined Paiute religious practice with ideas the episode associates with Christianity and possibly Mormon influence.
- His teaching centered on dance, peace, reunion with the dead, and collective renewal.
- His authority reached the Lakota through emissaries rather than direct control of their reservation movement.
- Later militant claims may reflect transmission, local adaptation, or retrospective distortion.
- He functioned as a hope-bearing religious figure rather than a military commander in the episode's account.

## Evidence

- Biography and vision: [[455-fall-of-the-sioux-the-ghost-dance-part-2-glt2320133118]] identifies Jack Wilson/Wovoka, his ranch work and English, his exposure to Christianity, and the reported eclipse vision.
- Teaching: [[455-fall-of-the-sioux-the-ghost-dance-part-2-glt2320133118]] associates his message with peace, love, dancing, the dead, and Native renewal.
- Transmission boundary: [[455-fall-of-the-sioux-the-ghost-dance-part-2-glt2320133118]] says Lakota emissaries may have altered the teaching and treats the bulletproof-shirt claim cautiously.

## Qualifications

- The page reflects a short podcast account rather than a complete Paiute or intertribal history of Wovoka's movement.
- The reported vision, exact theology, Christian and Mormon influences, and route of transmission remain source-scoped.
- Distinguishing Wovoka's message from Lakota adaptations should not imply that later participants merely misunderstood a fixed original doctrine.

## What Changed

- Established Wovoka as the religious source of the Ghost Dance teaching in the episode.
- Separated his reported peaceful message from uncertain later militant adaptations.
- Added emissary transmission as a central qualification on claims about doctrine and intent.
