# Monopoly (board game)

## Overview

Monopoly is a property-trading board game used in the source to show how historical entertainment can outlive and reverse its originating political lesson.

## Current Profile

[[40-history-as-entertainment-glt9519276470]] traces Monopoly to an early-twentieth-century Georgist design intended to demonstrate how land ownership enriches landlords at tenants' expense. Through circulation, patent disputes, and commercialization, that critical lesson became a competitive mass-market game in which accumulation and opponents' bankruptcy supply the objective and pleasure.

The episode also mentions communist and dissident adaptations, making Monopoly less a fixed political statement than a reusable rules system whose local names and variants could criticize capitalism, bureaucracy, or household scarcity.

## Key Characteristics

- Began, in the episode's account, as a model of landlord accumulation influenced by Henry George.
- Converts ownership, rent, scarcity, and bankruptcy into visible and repeatable game mechanics.
- Acquired a dominant commercial meaning different from its stated reformist origin.
- Supports ideological adaptation because the rules can be renamed and reframed across political systems.

## Evidence

- **Origin and reversal:** [[40-history-as-entertainment-glt9519276470]] describes the early game as anti-landlord or Georgist and its later commercial form as a capitalist competition.
- **Political adaptation:** [[40-history-as-entertainment-glt9519276470]] mentions East German and Hungarian variants as examples of reuse under communist systems.

## Qualifications

The episode supplies a conversational history rather than documentary analysis of designers, patents, editions, or international variants. The current page therefore preserves the origin-to-commercialization interpretation as source-bounded rather than treating every play session or edition as carrying one political message.

## What Changed

- Created a bounded profile of Monopoly as an example of ideological reversal through game commercialization.
