# 希拉里·尼尔森 / Hilaree Nelson

## Overview
Hilaree Nelson was a climber and ski mountaineer represented in the episode as [[JimMorrisonSkiMountaineer|吉姆·莫里森]]'s partner, a mother of two, and a central presence in [[EverestBeyondTheCloudsFilm|《珠穆朗玛：云中彼岸》]]. She died in a 2022 avalanche while descending Manaslu.

## Current Profile
The source's current profile is organized around a conflict that should not be flattened. Through Morrison's recollection, Nelson appears as someone who loved her children and her mountain vocation, understood that her work carried fatal risk, and resisted the idea that motherhood required abandoning her professional and personal self. Her story therefore supplies a difficult case of authenticity, example, dependency, and transferred consequence rather than simple evidence for or against adventure.

## Key Characteristics
- Combined elite mountain and ski practice with parenting two children.
- Treated continued vocation as part of being fully herself rather than as indifference to family.
- Accepted knowingly that repeated mountain exposure could have fatal consequences.
- Hoped, in Morrison's account, to model an authentic and purpose-led life for her children.
- Remains present through family grief, memory, and the documentary's attempt to preserve her complexity.

## Evidence
- Mountain and family commitments: [[83178c10-cba6-4869-8b69-559da582009c-83178c10-cba6-4869-8b69-559da582009c]] reports Morrison's view that Nelson held both commitments intensely.
- Risk and example: [[83178c10-cba6-4869-8b69-559da582009c-83178c10-cba6-4869-8b69-559da582009c]] attributes to Morrison the claim that she understood the danger yet wanted her life to show her children that a person could pursue a true calling.
- Death and aftermath: [[83178c10-cba6-4869-8b69-559da582009c-83178c10-cba6-4869-8b69-559da582009c]] reports her death during a Manaslu descent and the severe grief experienced by her children and Morrison.

## Qualifications
This profile is based on a short structured interview summary and largely on recollections by Nelson's partner and friend. It is not her direct account, a complete biography, an expedition investigation, or a basis for judging what all parents, athletes, or bereaved families should choose.
