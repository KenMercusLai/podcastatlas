# Venezuela

## Overview
Venezuela is a core wiki case for how oil wealth can build infrastructure, state ambition, and international bargaining power while also creating dependence, institutional fragility, monetary breakdown, disaster-response weakness, and foreign leverage. The current synthesis treats the country less as a single collapse story than as a repeated struggle over who controls oil value and whether that control can maintain production, legitimacy, and state capacity.

## Current Profile
The oil-history branch begins with [[chevron-venezuela-and-the-paradox-of-plenty]], where [[LakeMaracaibo]], foreign concessions, [[DutchDisease]], [[OilConcessionBargaining]], [[OPEC]], [[OilNationalization]], and [[PDVSA]] make Venezuela an early [[Petrostate]] and a central [[PoliticalResourceCurse]] case. [[the-secret-meeting-that-launched-opec]] adds Venezuela to producer-state coordination against [[SevenSistersOilMajors|Seven Sisters]] pricing power.

The economic-collapse branch comes from [[venezuelas-recent-economic-history-update]], where [[HugoChavez]]'s oil-funded spending, post-2003 exchange controls, and import reliance become a [[CurrencyControlTrap]] once prices fall under [[NicolasMaduro]]. The same branch includes [[ImportApprovalBottleneck]] and partial [[DollarizedStabilization]], while [[tech-20260212-0212-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260212-0212-mp-tech-pod-128]] adds a separate [[Stablecoins]] financial-access and sanctions-evasion angle.

The Pereira episode, [[trump-drinks-venezuelas-milkshake]], adds the reopening cycle through [[JoseAngelPereira|José Ángel Pereira]]. It shows that national oil control did not remove the need for foreign capital and technology: Apertura helped revive production in the 1990s, backlash helped reverse it, and a Trump-era proposal may again restart investment while raising constitutional, sovereignty, and executive-safety concerns.

The state-capacity and external-pressure branch comes from [[fault-lines-venezuelas-paltry-earthquake-response-6a44e38375e7a3e96111931b]] and [[caracas-under-pressure-democracy-in-venezuela-6a7d8bbe7d85cbdf96e00822]], where earthquakes, aid, U.S. intervention, oil-revenue permissions, institutions, and possible elections make recovery inseparable from political transition. [[dark-times-for-cubas-economic-experiment]] and [[153-quanqiu-hongguan-he-ziben-shichang-2026-zhanwang-da-nian-zhihou-reng-shi-da-nian-lupeqjdszon-wp5zdq06w3ustw2d]] add Venezuela's role as an external risk to [[Cuba]] and [[China]].

## Key Characteristics
- Oil-sovereignty case where foreign concessions, producer coordination, nationalization, and reopening all recur.
- Petrostate whose public spending, imports, currency regime, and political support depend heavily on oil income.
- State-company capacity case in which [[PDVSA]] control does not guarantee reinvestment, technical retention, or production stability.
- Foreign-leverage target where sanctions, licenses, oil-revenue permissions, and possible U.S.-backed oil rights shape domestic institutions.
- Disaster-response stress test where weak fuel, machinery, medical, and aid capacity turn earthquakes into governance crises.
- Geopolitical spillover source for Cuba's oil dependence, China's overseas-resource exposure, and U.S. pressure strategy.
- Financial-adaptation case through dollarization and stablecoins, both of which can support access while creating unequal or dual-use effects.

## Evidence
Oil sovereignty and producer bargaining:
- [[chevron-venezuela-and-the-paradox-of-plenty]] traces Venezuela from the 1922 Lake Maracaibo gusher through concessions, 50-50 bargaining, nationalization, and PDVSA.
- [[the-secret-meeting-that-launched-opec]] adds Venezuelan officials to the secret 1959 meeting that helped produce OPEC.
- [[trump-drinks-venezuelas-milkshake]] adds Apertura and the Trump-era oil reopening as later turns in the same bargaining cycle.

Petrostate dependence and monetary crisis:
- [[venezuelas-recent-economic-history-update]] links oil-funded imports and spending to exchange controls, government dollar rationing, hyperinflation, and partial dollarized stabilization.
- [[chevron-venezuela-and-the-paradox-of-plenty]] connects oil wealth to Dutch disease, weak diversification, and later crisis.

PDVSA capacity and reopening pressure:
- [[chevron-venezuela-and-the-paradox-of-plenty]] presents PDVSA as the national oil company at the center of state control and foreign-company bargaining.
- [[trump-drinks-venezuelas-milkshake]] adds underinvestment, field decline, heavy-oil technology needs, Chávez-era firings, production-capacity loss, and foreign-investment hesitation.

Disaster response and transition pressure:
- [[fault-lines-venezuelas-paltry-earthquake-response-6a44e38375e7a3e96111931b]] uses major earthquakes to show shortages of machinery, fuel, medical capacity, trained personnel, and organized aid.
- [[caracas-under-pressure-democracy-in-venezuela-6a7d8bbe7d85cbdf96e00822]] adds talks over aid, political rights, electoral institutions, courts, and oil-revenue permissions under U.S. pressure.

Geopolitical spillovers:
- [[dark-times-for-cubas-economic-experiment]] presents Venezuela as Cuba's later oil lifeline and as a trigger for Cuba's blackout risk when Venezuelan support weakens.
- [[153-quanqiu-hongguan-he-ziben-shichang-2026-zhanwang-da-nian-zhihou-reng-shi-da-nian-lupeqjdszon-wp5zdq06w3ustw2d]] treats an extreme Venezuela deterioration as a China-facing risk to loans, resource interests, infrastructure projects, and South America positioning.

Financial adaptation:
- [[venezuelas-recent-economic-history-update]] describes remittances and cash dollars supporting partial stabilization after hyperinflation.
- [[tech-20260212-0212-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260212-0212-mp-tech-pod-128]] cites Venezuela as a stablecoin-access example while warning about bad-actor use outside traditional finance.
