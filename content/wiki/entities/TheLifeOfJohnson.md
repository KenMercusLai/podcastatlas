# The Life of Samuel Johnson

## Overview

*The Life of Samuel Johnson* is [[JamesBoswell]]’s 1791 biography of [[SamuelJohnson]], presented by the episode as a landmark work that made recorded conversation, contradiction, intimate detail, and psychological presence central to biography.

## Current Profile

Boswell’s seven-year struggle to complete the book combined delay and personal disorder with an unusually ambitious evidentiary and literary project. He recorded Johnson’s conversation, revisited places, questioned associates, checked facts, and treated apparently small details as significant. The resulting work makes Johnson feel present through scenes, speech, conflict, eccentricity, fear, tenderness, and company rather than through a uniformly exemplary life.

That immediacy is constructed rather than transparent. Boswell selected and shaped material, brought his own needs and jealousy to the portrait, and had only partial access to the Johnson known within [[HesterPiozzi|Hester Thrale]]’s household. Its achievement therefore lies in combining documentary appetite with literary judgment, not in eliminating mediation.

## Key Characteristics

- Biography built from sustained observation, recorded conversation, interviews, travel, recollection, and factual checking.
- Psychological portrait that preserves contradiction instead of resolving Johnson into a single moral label.
- Social biography that renders a person through friends, dependents, households, arguments, habits, and scenes.
- Immersive literary construction whose apparent immediacy still reflects authorial selection and rivalry.
- Influential model treated by the episode as a major origin point for modern biography.

## Evidence

- Archive and method: [[653-londons-golden-age-the-shadow-of-the-madhouse-part-4-glt2240052230]] describes Boswell gathering conversations, Oxford memories, testimony, and checked facts across years.
- Psychological form: [[653-londons-golden-age-the-shadow-of-the-madhouse-part-4-glt2240052230]] emphasizes Johnson’s inner conflict, religious fear, tenderness, need, brilliance, and flaws as the book’s moral force.
- Publication and reception: [[653-londons-golden-age-the-shadow-of-the-madhouse-part-4-glt2240052230]] dates publication to 1791 and presents the work as immediately extraordinary and formally transformative.

## Qualifications

The episode’s “modern biography descends from Boswell” claim is a broad genealogy rather than a comparative survey of earlier and later biographical traditions. Boswell’s vivid dialogue and scenes are not raw documentary footage, and Hester’s different access reveals omissions and rivalry within his account.

## What Changed

- Created the book as a documentary-literary method joining conversation, contradiction, social setting, and psychological presence.

## Relationships

- [[JamesBoswell]] - author, witness, researcher, and literary constructor.
- [[SamuelJohnson]] - central subject whose contradictions organize the work.
- [[HesterPiozzi]] - competing witness whose domestic knowledge marks the biography’s limits.
- [[PsychologicalBiographyThroughConversation]] - biographical method the book exemplifies.
- [[PsychologicalRealism]] - neighboring literary practice of making inner conflict causally and morally significant.
