# The Life of Samuel Johnson

## Overview

*The Life of Samuel Johnson* is [[JamesBoswell]]’s 1791 biography of [[SamuelJohnson]], built from a conversational archive that began with personal fascination and grew through sustained observation, travel, testimony, checking, and literary scene construction.

## Current Profile

The 1763 Covent Garden bookshop meeting supplies the archive’s origin. Boswell approached Johnson as a nervous celebrity seeker, but his existing habit of intensive journal writing let him preserve the talk through which Johnson’s humor, learning, prejudice, roughness, force, and sociability became publicly memorable. The biography’s later method therefore began in repeated attention to conversation before it became a formal research project.

The 1773 Hebridean tour shows that method developing in action. Boswell positioned Johnson in dramatic locations, recorded quarrels and reconciliations, preserved comic performances, and sometimes provoked him for memorable material. [[JournalOfATourToTheHebrides]] made those habits public before the mature biography joined them to a much larger archive.

Boswell’s seven-year struggle to complete the book combined personal disorder with an ambitious evidentiary project. He revisited places, questioned associates, checked facts, and treated small details as significant. The result renders Johnson through speech, conflict, eccentricity, fear, tenderness, travel, work, and company rather than a uniformly exemplary life.

That immediacy is constructed rather than transparent. Boswell selected and shaped material, brought his own needs and jealousy to the portrait, and had only partial access to the Johnson known within [[HesterPiozzi|Hester Thrale]]’s household.

## Key Characteristics

- Biography whose core archive began in Boswell’s habitual recording of Johnson’s conversation.
- Work built from observation, interviews, travel, recollection, and factual checking.
- Method developed through friendship and the 1773 journey before formal completion.
- Psychological portrait preserving contradiction rather than resolving Johnson into one moral label.
- Social biography rendering a person through labor, friends, dependents, households, arguments, and places.
- Immersive literary construction whose apparent presence still reflects selection, rivalry, and provocation.

## Qualifications

The “modern biography descends from Boswell” claim is a broad genealogy rather than a comparative survey. His vivid dialogue and scenes are not raw documentary footage; the first meeting, remembered speech, and deliberate-provocation claim all pass through selection and literary reconstruction. Hester’s different access reveals omissions and rivalry without making either witness complete.

## What Changed

- Added Boswell’s 1763 meeting and preexisting journal habit as the beginning of the biography’s conversational archive.
- Connected Johnson’s public talk and sociability to the evidentiary form later expanded through travel, interviews, and checking.
