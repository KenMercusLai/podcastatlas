# The Life of Samuel Johnson

## Overview

*The Life of Samuel Johnson* is [[JamesBoswell]]’s 1791 biography of [[SamuelJohnson]], built from sustained observation, travel, recorded conversation, testimony, and literary scene construction.

## Current Profile

The 1773 Hebridean tour shows the method forming in real time. Boswell positioned Johnson in dramatic locations, recorded quarrels and reconciliations, preserved comic performances, and sometimes provoked him for memorable material. [[JournalOfATourToTheHebrides]] made those habits public before the mature biography joined them to a much larger archive.

Boswell’s seven-year struggle to complete the book combined personal disorder with an ambitious evidentiary project. He recorded conversation, revisited places, questioned associates, checked facts, and treated small details as significant. The result renders Johnson through scenes, speech, conflict, eccentricity, fear, tenderness, travel, and company rather than a uniformly exemplary life.

That immediacy is constructed rather than transparent. Boswell selected and shaped material, brought his own needs and jealousy to the portrait, and had only partial access to the Johnson known within [[HesterPiozzi|Hester Thrale]]’s household.

## Key Characteristics

- Biography built from observation, recorded conversation, interviews, travel, recollection, and factual checking.
- Method partly developed through Boswell’s 1773 journey and prior travel journal.
- Psychological portrait that preserves contradiction instead of resolving Johnson into one moral label.
- Social biography rendering a person through friends, dependents, households, arguments, places, and scenes.
- Immersive literary construction whose apparent immediacy still reflects selection, rivalry, and provocation.
- Influential model treated by the episodes as a major origin point for modern biography.

## Evidence

- Travel archive and scene formation: [[652-londons-golden-age-the-ghosts-of-culloden-part-3-glt2422514848]] shows Boswell recording Johnson, arranging charged encounters, and returning with material for the planned biography.
- Archive and method: [[653-londons-golden-age-the-shadow-of-the-madhouse-part-4-glt2240052230]] describes Boswell gathering conversations, memories, testimony, and checked facts across years.
- Psychological form and reception: [[653-londons-golden-age-the-shadow-of-the-madhouse-part-4-glt2240052230]] emphasizes inner conflict, tenderness, fear, flaws, publication in 1791, and formal influence.

## Qualifications

The “modern biography descends from Boswell” claim is a broad genealogy rather than a comparative survey. His vivid dialogue and scenes are not raw documentary footage, the Hebridean episode’s deliberate-provocation claim is interpretive, and Hester’s different access reveals omissions and rivalry.

## What Changed

- Added the 1773 Hebridean tour as a formative archive and workshop for Boswell’s later biographical method.
- Connected travel scene-building, quarrel, provocation, and reconciliation to the biography’s mature sense of presence.
