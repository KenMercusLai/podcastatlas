## Overview

贾复 / Jia Fu is synthesized as an Eastern Han founding general whose current profile now joins post-unification withdrawal with later death-memory. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-715-shishang-zui-zhihui-de-yici-duoquan-loqkawl247maw9fn-nomaw5gzkgu|Hanji 715]] presents him as a Jiaodong marquis who understands [[LiuXiu|光武帝刘秀]]'s postwar civilian turn and steps away from military authority; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-730-zhongguo-baijiaxing-juran-you-diwu-xing-lsm3xean8fzidhhhxi6-ppphexxh|Hanji 730]] remembers him at his 55 CE death as a quiet, wounded, high-merit commander.

## Current Profile

Hanji 715 supplies the settlement layer. After unification, Liu Xiu rewards Jia Fu as Jiaodong marquis, and the episode says Jia Fu and [[DengYuEasternHan|Deng Yu]] perceive that Liu Xiu wants to set aside weapons and govern through ritual and civilian instruction. Jia Fu therefore gives up military authority and withdraws into honored status.

The same source gives his postwar posture a self-cultivation shape. Jia Fu is described as firm and upright, staying home and cultivating himself after withdrawal. When Zhu Hu and others recommend returning him to the chancellorship, Liu Xiu does not use founding commanders as governing ministers because he is trying to regularize official administration.

Hanji 730 then presents Jia Fu as the Jiaodong Ganghou whose death falls amid the late Guangwu notices of 54-55 CE. The episode emphasizes his undefeated fighting record and twelve wounds, then says Liu Xiu reduced his exposure to dangerous distant campaigns because Jia Fu fought too fiercely. Its interpretive use is about recognition and merit: when generals discuss achievements, Jia Fu keeps his head down and does not advertise himself, while Liu Xiu says he knows Jia Fu's merit.

## Key Characteristics

- Eastern Han founding commander rewarded as Jiaodong marquis after unification.
- High-merit actor who surrenders military authority without being disgraced.
- Representative of [[HonorsWithoutOfficeSettlement|honors without office]] through retained title, honor, and limited participation.
- Postwar self-cultivation figure who does not push back into office after withdrawal.
- Martial figure whose undefeated record and many wounds embody high-risk battlefield service.
- Quiet merit case: he does not boast in achievement discussions, while Liu Xiu claims to know his contribution.

## Qualifications

This page remains bounded to two source notes rather than a complete campaign biography. Hanji 715 gives Jia Fu's post-unification withdrawal and non-return-to-office layer, while Hanji 730 gives death memory, battlefield risk, and quiet merit. Exact battles, formal rank chronology, and the full Yuntai-general tradition remain outside the current bounded evidence.
