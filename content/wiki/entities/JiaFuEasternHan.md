## Overview

贾复 / Jia Fu enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-730-zhongguo-baijiaxing-juran-you-diwu-xing-lsm3xean8fzidhhhxi6-ppphexxh|Hanji 730]] as an Eastern Han founding general remembered at his 55 CE death. The source uses him less as a full campaign biography than as a compact example of martial merit, bodily risk, and quiet service under [[LiuXiu|光武帝刘秀]].

## Current Profile

Hanji 730 presents Jia Fu as the Jiaodong Ganghou whose death falls amid the late Guangwu notices of 54-55 CE. The episode emphasizes his undefeated fighting record and twelve wounds, then says Liu Xiu reduced his exposure to dangerous distant campaigns because Jia Fu fought too fiercely.

The source's interpretive use is about recognition and merit. When generals discuss achievements, Jia Fu keeps his head down and does not advertise himself; Liu Xiu says he knows Jia Fu's merit. The page therefore keeps Jia Fu's current role narrow: a founding-warrior memory that supports the episode's Liu Xiu personnel-judgment theme.

## Key Characteristics

- Eastern Han founding commander remembered in this source at his 55 CE death notice.
- Martial figure whose undefeated record and many wounds embody high-risk battlefield service.
- Quiet merit case: he does not boast in achievement discussions, while Liu Xiu claims to know his contribution.
