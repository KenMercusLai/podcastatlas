# Amy Choi

## Overview

Amy Choi is the [[Playdate]] buyer whose separately itemized tariff charge anchors [[should-we-all-be-getting-tariff-refunds-right-now]].

## Current Profile

Choi paid $14.02 as a distinct tariff line when buying the console from [[PanicSoftware|Panic]]. After Panic recovered the invalidated duty, the company returned the same amount automatically to her original payment card, making her purchase a concrete example of traceable [[ConsumerTariffRefundAllocation]].

## Key Characteristics

- Consumer who indirectly bore an import-duty cost through an explicit checkout charge.
- Customer whose order-level record made the passed-through amount identifiable.
- Recipient of an automatic company refund rather than a direct government tariff claim.

## Evidence

- **Recorded payment:** [[should-we-all-be-getting-tariff-refunds-right-now]] reports the $14.02 tariff line on Choi's Playdate order.
- **Refund path:** [[should-we-all-be-getting-tariff-refunds-right-now]] says Panic returned that amount to her original card after receiving its own refund.
- **Consumer response:** [[should-we-all-be-getting-tariff-refunds-right-now]] presents her reaction as evidence that unexpected repayment can strengthen customer trust.

## Qualifications

Choi is one reported customer in an unusually well-documented direct-sales case. Her refund does not establish a general consumer right, typical company practice, or the feasibility of tracing tariff costs embedded in ordinary retail prices.

## What Changed

- Established Choi as the consumer case connecting tariff pass-through to a completed order-level refund.
