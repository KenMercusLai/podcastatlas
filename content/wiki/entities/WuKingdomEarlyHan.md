## Overview

吴国 / early Han Wu was a large Western Han kingdom created after [[YingBu|英布]]'s defeat and assigned to [[LiuBiWuKing|刘濞]] as part of [[SameSurnameKingEnfeoffment|same-surname enfeoffment]]. The current evidence follows it from a dynastic security replacement into the resource base and semi-autonomous political field behind the [[RebellionOfTheSevenStatesWesternHan|Rebellion of the Seven States]].

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-189-liubang-siqian-zuihou-yibo-lllydj2xyk4cr7b9d2qx0hvfw-dz|Hanji 189]] presents the founding move. After suppressing Ying Bu, [[LiuBang|刘邦]] converts the former [[JingKingdomEarlyHan|荆国]] field into Wu and appoints his nephew Liu Bi over three commanderies and fifty-three counties. The measure uses royal kin to secure territory made dangerous by a different-surname king's rebellion.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-231-hanjingdi-jiuhou-jing-zaojiu-wulong-chuanwei-shijian-2-lg4ybjhj0w8azd-h_krzhibc0clg|Hanji 231 part 2]] supplies the later reversal. The episode attributes to Wu a copper-minting and salt-producing economy strong enough to reduce ordinary taxation, subsidize military service, make festival distributions, reward talent, and protect fugitives from outside arrest. These claims do not establish a complete fiscal system, but they explain how a kingdom founded as a trust substitute could acquire material capacity, local allegiance, and practical resistance to central enforcement.

The joined profile is therefore institutional rather than deterministic. Size and resources make autonomy possible, while Liu Bi's grievance, court policy, coalition work, and later military choices turn that capacity into rebellion. The source does not show that every large same-surname kingdom followed the same path.

## Key Characteristics

- Post-Ying-Bu territorial reorganization granted to Liu-family kin as a security measure.
- Large kingdom described at foundation as three commanderies and fifty-three counties.
- Copper- and salt-based resource center attributed with exceptional fiscal capacity.
- Polity said to exchange tax relief, service subsidies, gifts, and talent patronage for local attachment.
- Kingdom whose refusal to surrender fugitives indicates resistance to external enforcement.
- Dynastic security instrument that later becomes Liu Bi's base for confrontation with the center.

## Evidence

Foundation and scale:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-189-liubang-siqian-zuihou-yibo-lllydj2xyk4cr7b9d2qx0hvfw-dz|Hanji 189]] says Liu Bang replaces Jing with Wu and appoints Liu Bi over three commanderies and fifty-three counties.

Resource and fiscal capacity:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-231-hanjingdi-jiuhou-jing-zaojiu-wulong-chuanwei-shijian-2-lg4ybjhj0w8azd-h_krzhibc0clg|Hanji 231 part 2]] attributes copper mining, minting, salt production, tax relief, service payments, and distributions to Wu.

Practical autonomy:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-231-hanjingdi-jiuhou-jing-zaojiu-wulong-chuanwei-shijian-2-lg4ybjhj0w8azd-h_krzhibc0clg|Hanji 231 part 2]] says Wu protects fugitives from outside officials and connects that practice to Liu Bi's capacity to resist the court.

## Qualifications

The current profile rests on two compact popular-history notes, not a complete administrative history of Wu. The founding geography, commandery and county totals, mine ownership, minting authority, salt system, tax incidence, military-service rules, distribution practices, talent patronage, fugitive policy, public support, and degree of autonomy require primary-source comparison. Material capacity is treated as an enabling condition rather than a sufficient cause of rebellion.

## What Changed

- Recast Wu from a founding-era territorial replacement into a kingdom with an attributed fiscal, social, and enforcement base for autonomy.
- Added copper, salt, tax relief, service subsidy, patronage, and fugitive protection as linked capacities rather than isolated anecdotes.
- Qualified the causal judgment: resources enable resistance but do not make rebellion automatic.

- [[JingKingdomEarlyHan|荆国]] - predecessor territory reorganized after Ying Bu's defeat.
- [[YingBu|英布]] - defeated different-surname king whose rebellion precedes Wu's creation.
