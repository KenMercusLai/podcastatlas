# 三庄汉墓群 / Sanzhuang Han Tomb Complex

## Overview

三庄汉墓群 / Sanzhuang Han Tomb Complex is the Western Han burial landscape in Siyang that [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-345-2-qiannian-wangling-de-guiyi-xianxiang-daodi-zhenxiang-ruhe-ljkkhfbumlsgf7ppz96qvys_ok3d|Hanji 345-2]] associates with the royal cemetery of [[SishuiKingdomWesternHan|泗水国]].

## Current Profile

The episode describes many differently sized mounds whose funerary nature became visible when land leveling exposed coffins, skeletal material, coins, iron, and jade. A later remote-sensing survey reportedly found tombs organized along a north-south axis, supporting a planned cemetery rather than an accidental cluster.

That landscape-level interpretation does not assign one identity to every grave. [[ChendunHanTomb|陈墩汉墓]] is presented as an elite burial with a female skeleton and a “张廷意” seal, while nearby [[DaqingdunHanTomb|大青墩汉墓]] is the larger candidate whose excavation later supplied direct “泗水王冢” text. The complex can therefore include royal, family, household, or accompanying burials without making all occupants Liu kings.

## Key Characteristics

- Multi-mound Western Han cemetery near the historical setting of Sishui Kingdom.
- Tombs reported as arranged along a north-south planning axis.
- Contains monuments of very different scale and likely different rank or function.
- Includes both Chendun and Daqingdun in the episode's excavation sequence.
- Cemetery attribution is stronger than a universal claim about the surname, ethnicity, or office of every occupant.

## Evidence

- Discovery and extent: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-345-2-qiannian-wangling-de-guiyi-xianxiang-daodi-zhenxiang-ruhe-ljkkhfbumlsgf7ppz96qvys_ok3d|Hanji 345-2]] reports numerous mounds and funerary materials exposed during land leveling.
- Planned landscape: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-345-2-qiannian-wangling-de-guiyi-xianxiang-daodi-zhenxiang-ruhe-ljkkhfbumlsgf7ppz96qvys_ok3d|Hanji 345-2]] attributes the north-south alignment and family-cemetery interpretation to remote sensing and archaeological assessment.
- Internal diversity: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-345-2-qiannian-wangling-de-guiyi-xianxiang-daodi-zhenxiang-ruhe-ljkkhfbumlsgf7ppz96qvys_ok3d|Hanji 345-2]] contrasts the smaller elite Chendun tomb with the much larger Daqingdun mound.

## Qualifications

The page rests on a short podcast note rather than a cemetery catalogue or excavation report. The total number of mounds, mapped area, chronology, axial organization, family structure, status hierarchy, and relationship of unexcavated graves to the Sishui kings require direct archaeological documentation. A planned cemetery may contain more than rulers and their agnatic relatives.

## What Changed

- Added a landscape-level entity distinct from its individual tombs.
- Recorded planning and scale variation without assigning all graves to one occupant class.
- Separated royal-cemetery association from universal Liu-family identification.

## Relationships

- [[SishuiKingdomWesternHan]] - polity with which the cemetery is archaeologically and historically associated.
- [[ChendunHanTomb]] - smaller elite tomb whose body and named seal complicate a simple royal-family reading.
- [[DaqingdunHanTomb]] - larger tomb whose later chamber-board labels directly name the Sishui royal context.
- [[ArchaeologicalInterpretationUnderSparseEvidence]] - method for distinguishing a planned burial landscape from claims about every tomb and occupant.
