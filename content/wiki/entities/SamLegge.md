# Sam Legge

## Overview

Sam Legge was a conscientious objector and [[MinnesotaStarvationExperiment]] volunteer whose self-injury, determination to continue, and later testimony make the conflict between severe harm and chosen service unusually concrete.

## Current Profile

During starvation-induced delirium, Legge cut off some of his fingers. The episode says he still asked to remain because completing the study would provide an honorable answer to what he had done during the war. His decision arose within stigma against conscientious objectors, yet it also expressed a religious and moral effort to serve without killing.

Decades later, Legge described the experiment as a way to build up the world while war destroyed it. His case resists a clean label: the injury demonstrates impaired judgment and the limits of anticipated consent, while his sustained explanation and retrospective endorsement remain evidence of purpose that ethical analysis should not simply erase.

## Key Characteristics

- Refused military service on moral grounds and sought useful nonviolent contribution.
- Volunteered for prolonged controlled starvation under wartime social pressure.
- Suffered delirium and serious self-injury during the experiment.
- Asked to continue because participation carried moral and social meaning.
- Later described the study as constructive service and did not reduce himself to victimhood.

### Ethical tension

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to a structured podcast summary rather than Legge's full oral history, medical record, writings, or independent biography. His wish to continue during delirium cannot by itself demonstrate contemporaneous decision-making capacity, while his later pride cannot establish that every risk was proportionate or every volunteer shared his judgment.
