# Sam Legge

## Overview

Sam Legge was a conscientious objector and [[MinnesotaStarvationExperiment]] volunteer whose self-injury, determination to continue, and later testimony make the conflict between severe harm and chosen service unusually concrete.

## Current Profile

During severe starvation-related deterioration, Legge lost several fingers while chopping wood. One source describes the event as starvation-induced self-injury; the earlier episode summary preserves Legge's own uncertainty, reporting that weakness and slowed reactions could explain the accident but that he could not categorically exclude deliberate harm. He still asked to remain because completing the study would provide an honorable answer to what he had done during the war. His decision arose within stigma against conscientious objectors, yet it also expressed a religious and moral effort to serve without killing.

Before the injury, he experienced mood swings, solitary eating, intense food rituals, hostility toward a child he imagined going home to supper, and the frightening sense that his character was slipping away. Decades later, Legge described the experiment as a way to build up the world while war destroyed it. His case resists a clean label: the injury and behavioral change demonstrate impaired judgment and the limits of anticipated consent, while his sustained explanation and retrospective endorsement remain evidence of purpose that ethical analysis should not simply erase.

## Key Characteristics

- Refused military service on moral grounds and sought useful nonviolent contribution.
- Volunteered for prolonged controlled starvation under wartime social pressure.
- Suffered severe mood, behavior, and identity disruption during the experiment.
- Lost several fingers in an injury whose accidental or deliberate character remained uncertain in his own later account.
- Asked to continue because participation carried moral and social meaning.
- Later described the study as constructive service and did not reduce himself to victimhood.

### Moral purpose

### Ethical tension

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to two structured podcast summaries rather than Legge's full oral history, medical record, writings, or independent biography. The sources differ in compression: one calls the injury self-injury, while the other preserves Legge's uncertainty about whether it was deliberate. His wish to continue amid severe deterioration cannot by itself demonstrate contemporaneous decision-making capacity, while his later pride cannot establish that every risk was proportionate or every volunteer shared his judgment. The source title uses “Sam Legg”; the wiki retains the established “Sam Legge” canonical identity.
