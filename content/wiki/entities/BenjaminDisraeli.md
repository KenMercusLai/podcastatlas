# Benjamin Disraeli

## Overview

Benjamin Disraeli was a British Conservative prime minister, novelist, and political performer whose wit, flamboyance, imperial associations, and rivalry with Gladstone give him an unusually vivid public afterlife.

## Current Profile

[[35-the-prime-ministers-world-cup-glt6124252189]] presents Disraeli less as a complete governing record than as the clearest example of personality defeating administrative weight in popular memory. His literary career, humor, theatrical style, association with empire, and role in popular Tory identity helped him defeat [[LordSalisbury|Lord Salisbury]] by 79% despite the hosts' judgment that Salisbury was the more substantial prime minister.

The comparison suggests that Disraeli survives as both politician and character. That vividness made him legible to a modern voting audience and prepared a quarterfinal against [[WilliamEwartGladstone|William Ewart Gladstone]], but it does not establish that his government was more consequential or successful than Salisbury's.

## Key Characteristics

- Conservative prime minister remembered through wit, style, and theatrical personality.
- Novelist whose cultural identity extends beyond formal political office.
- Strongly associated in the source with imperialism and popular Tory tradition.
- Durable rival of Gladstone in British political memory.
- Example of vivid reputation outperforming administrative substance in a public poll.
