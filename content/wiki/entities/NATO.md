# NATO

## Overview
NATO appears in the wiki as a security alliance whose credibility, European capability demands, Arctic access, Ukraine support, and technology agenda are being tested by Russia's war, American commitment uncertainty, and U.S.-European responsibility rebalancing.

## Current Profile
NATO summit politics often involve projecting unity while avoiding the hardest question: whether the alliance can survive weaker American commitment. [[NATOAllianceCredibility]] depends not only on spending but on trust, command integration, intelligence, satellites, deep precision strike, and response confidence under [[RussianHybridPressure]].

NATO also serves as the defense arena where the [[UnitedKingdom]] may still matter after [[Brexit]], and as the alliance context for [[Greenland]]'s existing defense access through [[Denmark]] and the [[UnitedStates]]. In those cases, strategic value does not automatically imply annexation or total sovereignty transfer.

The alliance-rebalancing profile is now sharper. [[SunChenghao|孙承昊]] argues that the 2026 summit is about converting commitments into spending, defense-industrial capacity, procurement, Ukraine aid, and high-end capabilities. [[TransatlanticBurdenShifting]] captures the resulting pressure: the United States still values NATO but increasingly wants Europe to absorb more regional security responsibility.

NATO's technology profile also expands into long-range precision strike, integrated air and missile defense, unmanned systems, intelligence, operational cloud, and strong AI models, connecting the alliance to [[MilitarizedAITechnologyBlocRisk]].

[[XiangLanxin|Xiang Lanxin]] reinforces responsibility transfer but adds a historical-functional claim: NATO's classic purposes—containing the Soviet Union, keeping the United States engaged, and constraining Germany—have changed enough that command responsibility and forward deployment may be reorganized. That is a transformation thesis, not evidence that NATO is already dissolving.

## Key Characteristics
- Collective-defense alliance anchored by Article 5 credibility and U.S.-European coordination.
- European security platform shaped by [[Russia]], [[Ukraine]], American commitment risk, and frontline member reassurance.
- Spending and capacity conversion problem where budgets must become munitions, procurement, industry, readiness, and command capability.
- Strategic access context for [[Greenland]], [[Denmark]], and the [[UnitedStates]] without requiring territorial ownership.
- Burden-shifting arena where the United States tries to keep NATO useful while transferring more regional responsibility to Europe.
- Technology-security platform increasingly connected to AI, operational cloud, drones, intelligence, and precision strike.
- Alliance whose inherited functions and command arrangements may change without formal U.S. withdrawal.

## Evidence
- Arctic defense access: [[how-to-get-what-greenland-has-with-permission]] says Greenland is not geopolitically empty because it is tied to Denmark and NATO while the United States already has defense access there.
- Post-Brexit defense role: [[keep-qualms-and-carry-on-a-decade-after-brexit-6a3a53028a3c8822ab345d4b]] says defense is one area where Britain may still contribute to European security after Brexit.
- Alliance credibility and U.S. integration: [[continental-rift-natos-tense-summit-6a4cc6b0c4772b27e88e898e]] argues that NATO credibility depends on American command, intelligence, satellites, and deep-strike integration as much as spending.
- Burden shifting and capability conversion: [[397-cong-zeren-fendan-dao-zeren-zhuanyi-ouzhou-weihe-nanyi-zhenzheng-yu-meiguo-fendao-yangbiao-31c69f95-d493-47a1-a6f8-905eb22c3e41]] adds the 5% target, Ukraine aid institutionalization, U.S. burden-shifting pressure, and AI/cloud military capability agenda.
- Functional transformation: [[508-xiang-lanxin-tan-guoji-guanxi-shiye-xia-de-zhongmeiou-jinxi-cong-sitanfo-xiaojituan-dao-junshi-kaiensizhuyi-lqrjnxctet30isfmgt-wyavdwffu]] interprets Trump's pressure as transfer of conventional defense and regional responsibility rather than necessary alliance exit.

## Qualifications
The evidence does not establish that NATO is collapsing or that the United States will leave. The current synthesis preserves congressional constraints on unilateral U.S. withdrawal and the American strategic mainstream's view that NATO remains useful. European members are not one actor: France, Germany, southern Europe, Canada, Turkey, and frontline states face different threat perceptions and industrial interests. Xiang's “fundamental transformation” remains a forecast.
