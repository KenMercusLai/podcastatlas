# Pheidippides

## Overview

Pheidippides appears in the wiki as the Athenian runner whose Sparta mission and later victory-run legend show how Marathon history, religion, and civic myth overlap.

## Current Profile

The source distinguishes two traditions. In the main pre-battle story, Pheidippides runs roughly 140 miles from Athens to Sparta in two days to request help. Sparta promises aid but is delayed by the Carnea festival. On his return, Pheidippides is said to encounter [[PanGreekGod]], who complains about Athenian neglect and promises help if properly honored.

The famous story that a runner goes from Marathon to Athens, announces victory, and dies is treated differently. The episode says that version is not in [[Herodotus]] and appears in later sources. Pheidippides therefore becomes a useful case for [[MythAsHistoricalEvidence]]: a historically important military message can accrete divine encounter, panic explanation, and later athletic/civic symbolism.

## Key Characteristics

- Pheidippides is the runner sent from Athens to Sparta for help before Marathon.
- His Sparta mission reveals that Athens expects Spartan aid but must survive alone for crucial days.
- His reported encounter with Pan gives religious explanation to later Persian panic.
- The famous death-after-victory run is treated as later tradition, not Herodotus' version.
- The episode uses him to separate recoverable source evidence from Marathon's afterlife.
