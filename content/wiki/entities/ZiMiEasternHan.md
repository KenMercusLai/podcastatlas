## Overview
Zi Mi is presented as the close household slave who kills [[PengChongEasternHan|Peng Chong]] and Peng Chong's wife, sends their heads to [[LiuXiu|Liu Xiu]], and is rewarded with the title "不义侯". His profile matters less as an independent statesman than as a compact case where household access, rebel collapse, and the moral disorder of rewarding useful betrayal converge.

## Current Profile
In [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-693-panchu-liuxiu-de-pengchong-ruhe-yin-beipan-er-wang-lqler4fu9wqztsrgzsxhs1519nm]], Zi Mi exploits his place inside Peng Chong's household while Peng Chong is resting during a period of ritual fasting. With three associates, he binds Peng Chong, controls access to officials by claiming the king is fasting and the servants have been granted leave, binds the household slaves and Peng Chong's wife, and prevents a guard from accepting Peng Chong's offer of treasure and his daughter Peng Zhu.

At night, Zi Mi forces Peng Chong to write an order letting the conspirators go to Hou Lanqing and open the gates. Zi Mi then kills Peng Chong and Peng Chong's wife, takes their heads and treasure, and sends the heads to Luoyang. Liu Xiu rewards Zi Mi with the title "不义侯", a title that acknowledges the unrighteousness of the act while still granting public rank for its usefulness against a rebel enemy.

## Key Characteristics
- Close-access conspirator: Zi Mi's decisive advantage is proximity inside Peng Chong's household rather than military strength.
- Deceptive gate-and-command operator: he uses claims about fasting, servant leave, forced written orders, and gate access to neutralize normal palace controls.
- Master-killer against a rebel enemy: his act removes Peng Chong but remains framed as morally and legally polluted.
- Title-problem exemplar: the "不义侯" reward makes him a key case for how official rewards can damage the meaning of meritorious titles.
- Evidence for inner-security fragility: his success shows that a rebel court can collapse through trusted domestic infrastructure.

## Evidence
- Household proximity and deception: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-693-panchu-liuxiu-de-pengchong-ruhe-yin-beipan-er-wang-lqler4fu9wqztsrgzsxhs1519nm]] describes Zi Mi binding Peng Chong during ritual fasting, shutting out officials, controlling household slaves, and using a forced order to open the gates.
- Moral and title ambiguity: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-693-panchu-liuxiu-de-pengchong-ruhe-yin-beipan-er-wang-lqler4fu9wqztsrgzsxhs1519nm]] records Liu Xiu's "不义侯" reward and argues that the label recognizes unrighteousness without resolving the problem of rewarding a master-killer.
- Rebel-regime collapse: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-693-panchu-liuxiu-de-pengchong-ruhe-yin-beipan-er-wang-lqler4fu9wqztsrgzsxhs1519nm]] makes Peng Chong's death and the later surrender of his remaining structure follow from Zi Mi's inner assault.

## Qualifications
- The profile is currently source-bound to one episode and should not be treated as a full biography.
- Zi Mi's act helps Liu Xiu strategically, but the page preserves the source's moral distinction between useful anti-rebel violence and lawful merit.
- The romanization and role are normalized from the episode's transcript context; minor household details remain source-scoped.
