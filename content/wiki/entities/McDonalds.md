# McDonald's

[[fuzhuang-pinpai-a-f-xunzhao-zhongguo-hezuo-huoban-fufei-tiqian-kan-telangpu-tiewen-fuwu-shangxian-1004810677]] adds a company-specific operations update. The episode says McDonald's U.S. same-store sales grew only 0.8% in the second quarter, traffic continued to fall, franchisee pricing compliance was uneven, and dense new-product rollout added store burden, reinforcing [[FastFoodDemandReset]] as both a demand and execution problem.

McDonald's appears in [[155-meimao-neng-dang-fan-chi-ma-xiang-zhuanqian-gai-zuo-dian-sha-jieju-shi-ying-bimian-shenme-xingwei-jingjixue-siwei-you-shenme-yong-920167210]] as the fast-food context for [[CocaCola|Coca-Cola]]'s unlimited-refill example. The episode uses the pairing to explain [[MarginalAnalysis]], [[PriceElasticity]], and targeted marketing: a drink refill can feel like a large consumer benefit while the restaurant expects limited actual consumption and gains from food traffic.

[[caracas-under-pressure-democracy-in-venezuela-6a7d8bbe7d85cbdf96e00822]] adds McDonald's as a current demand-pressure case. The source says U.S. sales were weak, traffic fell in the first half of the year, and shares had fallen by about 10% that year, making the chain a visible anchor for [[FastFoodDemandReset]].

[[145-gaijia-zhongzi-de-canyin-yangpinpai-feng-tou-quan-1-145-1]] adds McDonald's China as the earliest template for [[ForeignRestaurantBrandLocalControl]]. The episode says the 2017 China transaction put [[CITICCapital]] at 52% and brought in [[CarlyleGroup]] and McDonald's global as minority holders; after McDonald's global later increased its China stake and Carlyle exited, CITIC still controlled 52%. The source treats this as the proof case that a foreign restaurant brand can grow under local control rather than disappear from China.

This global company page is separate from [[McDonaldsNetherlands]], which is a specific country-unit case in an AI-generated advertising episode.
