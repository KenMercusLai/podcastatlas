# Harold Macmillan

## Overview

Harold Macmillan appears as the British prime minister whose “winds of change” position represented official acceptance that African decolonisation could not be resisted indefinitely.

## Current Profile

In [[232-queen-elizabeth-ii-part-2-glt5667149245]], Macmillan is part of an [[ElizabethII|Elizabeth II]]-centered interpretation of imperial contraction. The episode says the Queen supported the direction of his “winds of change” speech, using that alignment to argue that the Crown did not make itself the center of conservative resistance to decolonisation.

## Key Characteristics

- Served as the British governmental reference point for accepting African political change.
- His “winds of change” position is linked to the monarchy's post-imperial adjustment.
- Appears through one retrospective episode rather than a complete account of his premiership.

## Evidence

- Decolonisation stance: [[232-queen-elizabeth-ii-part-2-glt5667149245]] cites Macmillan's “winds of change” speech as the direction Elizabeth II supported.
- Crown-government alignment: [[232-queen-elizabeth-ii-part-2-glt5667149245]] uses that support to contrast accommodation with possible monarchical resistance to imperial dissolution.

## Qualifications

The source does not analyze the speech in full, Macmillan's broader African policy, the pace and violence of decolonisation, colonial perspectives, or the complete record of his government. Royal support is presented as the hosts' interpretation rather than an independent policy act.

## What Changed

- Created the profile from the episode's decolonisation and Crown-alignment discussion.
