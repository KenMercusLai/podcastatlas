# Operation Uranus

## Overview

Operation Uranus was the November 1942 Soviet counteroffensive that broke the Axis flanks around Stalingrad and encircled the German Sixth Army.

## Current Profile

The plan answered the urban stalemate by attacking the wider operational system. German units concentrated toward the Volga while Romanian forces, with other Axis allies elsewhere on the flanks, held long sectors without adequate food, equipment, or anti-tank weapons. Soviet planners exploited that asymmetry with coordinated artillery, armour, infantry, and air power.

The northern attack began on 19 November and the southern attack on 20 November. Their link at Kalach on 23 November closed a pocket roughly 40–50 kilometres behind the German front. In [[215-stalingrad-and-the-red-army-part-2-glt4269997308]], the operation marks the point where Soviet tactical survival inside the city became strategic initiative and where the German problem changed from taking Stalingrad to supplying or escaping an encircled army.

## Key Characteristics

- Planned during the earlier crisis while German forces pressed into Stalingrad.
- Targeted extended and under-equipped Axis-allied flanks rather than the strongest urban concentration.
- Used coordinated air, artillery, armour, infantry, and mobile forces.
- Opened from the north on 19 November 1942 and the south on 20 November.
- Linked at Kalach on 23 November behind German lines.
- Encircled the Sixth Army and transformed the campaign into a relief, supply, and breakout crisis.

## Evidence

- Vulnerability selection: [[215-stalingrad-and-the-red-army-part-2-glt4269997308]] describes German concentration toward the Volga and shortages on Romanian-held flanks.
- Operational sequence: [[215-stalingrad-and-the-red-army-part-2-glt4269997308]] dates the two attacks and their 23 November meeting at Kalach.
- Combined arms: [[215-stalingrad-and-the-red-army-part-2-glt4269997308]] emphasizes rebuilt Soviet air power and coordination across artillery, armour, infantry, and air attack.
- Strategic result: [[215-stalingrad-and-the-red-army-part-2-glt4269997308]] connects encirclement to Hitler's hold order, the airlift claim, and the later Winter Storm relief attempt.

## Qualifications

The episode offers an accessible operational outline rather than a complete order of battle. Its troop, tank, aircraft, distance, equipment, and timing claims should be checked against specialist military histories. Describing Romanian and other Axis-allied formations as weakly equipped should not be mistaken for denying their agency, resistance, losses, or the effects of German command and allocation decisions.

## What Changed

- Established Operation Uranus as a flank-exploitation and combined-arms solution to the Stalingrad urban stalemate.
