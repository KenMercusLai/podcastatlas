# Jean-Baptiste Carrier

## Overview

Jean-Baptiste Carrier was a representative on mission associated in the source with mass drownings, blasphemous naming, and violent repression at Nantes during the [[FrenchRevolution|French Revolution]].

## Current Profile

The episode uses Carrier as a limit case in [[MaximilienRobespierre|Robespierre]]'s distinction between centralized revolutionary terror and cruelty driven by appetite, ambition, or local power. Carrier was accused of drowning bound prisoners in sabotaged barges and calling the killings “Republican baptisms”; the most lurid details are reported allegations rather than independently established here. Robespierre's February 1794 recall of Carrier demonstrates opposition to some decentralized atrocities without resolving the legitimacy of state terror itself.

## Key Characteristics

- He exercised coercive authority as a representative on mission at Nantes.
- He was accused of organizing mass drowning as an instrument of revolutionary repression.
- Blasphemous labels attached civic-revolutionary language to the alleged killings.
- His conduct became an example of provincial violence exceeding Robespierre's claimed rules of terror.
- His recall shows that revolutionary leaders disputed methods and scope inside the coercive system.

## Qualifications

- The episode distinguishes disputed or lurid allegations from better-documented events; this page preserves that source boundary.
- It does not provide a complete account of Carrier's mission, command chain, victims, trial, or later fate.
- Robespierre's recall of Carrier does not erase Robespierre's own support for execution and exceptional government.

## What Changed

- A canonical profile now records Carrier's role as a disputed boundary case between revolutionary government and provincial atrocity.
