# British Army

## Overview
The British Army is the state military force whose Northern Ireland role moves from emergency separator in 1969 to contested counterinsurgency actor by 1972.

## Current Profile
The current profile is a rapid legitimacy reversal. The army enters [[Derry]] and [[Belfast]] in August 1969 after the [[BattleOfTheBogside]] and is initially welcomed by many Catholics as protection from loyalists and the [[RoyalUlsterConstabulary|RUC]]. By 1970-1971, the [[FallsCurfew]], soldier deaths, house searches, and [[OperationDemetrius]] have made the army a direct participant in the conflict. By [[BloodySunday]], fear, bitterness, aggressive unit culture, and command failure turn an arrest operation into civilian killings that shatter much remaining Catholic trust.

## Key Characteristics
- Emergency deployment force first presented as a buffer during the 1969 street-war crisis.
- Visible executor of searches, curfews, internment, and detention operations.
- Institution exposed to soldier deaths, fear of snipers, and hardening attitudes on the ground.
- Central actor in the [[CounterinsurgencyLegitimacyTrap]] because coercive operations often produced political blowback.
- Institution implicated in Bloody Sunday through 1 Para's advance, shootings, and later disputed claims.
- Reputation marker for Britain's wider legitimacy in Ireland and America.

## Evidence
- Initial deployment: [[698-the-troubles-the-brink-of-civil-war-part-2-glt2308408988]] says troops entered Derry and then Belfast after Stormont and Westminster could no longer contain street violence.
- Legitimacy erosion: [[699-the-troubles-return-of-the-ira-part-3-glt9519811642]] links Falls Curfew, Robert Curtis's killing, the Royal Highland Fusiliers murders, and Operation Demetrius to the army's shift from buffer to combatant.
- Bloody Sunday operation: [[700-the-troubles-bloody-sunday-part-4-glt3909829334]] says paratroopers were selected as an arrest force, exceeded the limited operation's boundary, fired more than 100 rounds, and killed 13 unarmed civilians.
- Strategic consequence: [[700-the-troubles-bloody-sunday-part-4-glt3909829334]] says Bloody Sunday destroyed the army's reputation among many Catholics and strengthened Provisional IRA recruitment.

## Qualifications
The sources keep two time periods distinct. The army's initial 1969 welcome by many Catholics is not erased, but it no longer defines the institution's later role after curfews, internment, deaths, and Bloody Sunday.

## What Changed
- Added Bloody Sunday as the decisive endpoint of the army's legitimacy reversal.
- Added fear of snipers, bitter soldiers, and aggressive arrest-force selection as conditions around the failure.
- Qualified the earlier protector profile with the 1972 civilian-killing evidence.
