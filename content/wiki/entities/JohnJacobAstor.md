# John Jacob Astor IV

## Overview

John Jacob Astor IV was the wealthiest passenger aboard [[RMSTitanic|RMS Titanic]] and a representative of Gilded Age New York wealth in [[429-titanic-countdown-to-disaster-part-3-glt1273657377]].

## Current Profile

The episode presents Astor as a concentrated example of the ship's connection to American fortunes, social scandal, luxury hospitality, and extreme inequality. His family's Waldorf Astoria helped define the hotel environment that Titanic imitated, while his voyage with his much younger pregnant wife Madeleine, staff, and dog Kitty made his household part of the ship's elaborate first-class world. His wealth makes him central to the popular plutocrat narrative, but the wider passenger portrait prevents him from standing in for everyone aboard.

## Key Characteristics

- Heir to a dominant Gilded Age New York fortune and described as Titanic's richest passenger.
- Linked through the Waldorf Astoria to the luxury-hotel model reproduced aboard Titanic.
- Traveled with his pregnant wife Madeleine, household staff, and dog Kitty.
- Passenger whose wealth and marriage made him a figure of contemporary status and scandal.
- Representative of first-class inequality, but not of the ship's complete social composition.

## Evidence

- Wealth and status: [[429-titanic-countdown-to-disaster-part-3-glt1273657377]] identifies Astor as the richest passenger and situates him within a powerful New York dynasty.
- Luxury model: [[429-titanic-countdown-to-disaster-part-3-glt1273657377]] connects the Waldorf Astoria to the hotel-like standard embodied by Titanic.
- Traveling household: [[429-titanic-countdown-to-disaster-part-3-glt1273657377]] describes Madeleine, staff, and Kitty as part of Astor's party.
- Social interpretation: [[429-titanic-countdown-to-disaster-part-3-glt1273657377]] uses Astor to ground wealth and inequality while arguing against reducing the full passenger list to plutocrats and poor migrants.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to one retrospective episode rather than a full biography or economic history of the Astor fortune. Wealth rankings, private motives, social reactions to the marriage, and household details remain episode-attributed. The profile does not yet incorporate the later evacuation account beyond relationships supplied by the broader wiki.
