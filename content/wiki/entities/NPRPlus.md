# NPR+

## Overview

NPR+ is [[NPR]]'s network-wide podcast subscription product, described by [[LedaMeritz]] in [[the-economics-of-podcasting-and-npr-plus|The economics of podcasting and NPR+]] as the successor for new enrollment to separate show products such as Planet Money+.

## Current Profile

The product combines sponsor-free listening, bonus material, archive access, early access, and selected non-audio benefits. Existing Apple show subscribers were moved into the broader bundle at their old price, while new supporters could enroll through Apple or directly through NPR.

NPR+ is not presented as a ring-fenced fund for whichever show generated a subscription. Its revenue supports the wider public-radio system, including NPR and member stations, while show-level attribution still informs investment decisions. The episode frames the product as both paid audio access and an emerging membership/community relationship.

## Key Characteristics

- Bundles benefits across NPR's podcast network rather than selling only one show.
- Supports NPR and public-radio stations instead of reserving revenue for the originating show.
- Offers sponsor-free feeds plus bonus, archive, and early-access material.
- Uses both platform-mediated and direct enrollment paths with different economics and data access.
- Seeks to convert a small share of podcast listeners into recurring supporters.
- Treats retention, failed-payment recovery, feedback, and community as operating concerns.

## Evidence

- Bundle and migration - [[the-economics-of-podcasting-and-npr-plus|The economics of podcasting and NPR+]] says existing Apple subscribers moved from individual show products to NPR+ at their original price and gained network-wide benefits.
- System funding - [[the-economics-of-podcasting-and-npr-plus|The economics of podcasting and NPR+]] says subscription value flows across NPR and public radio, while show attribution remains an input into resource decisions.
- Product and relationship - [[the-economics-of-podcasting-and-npr-plus|The economics of podcasting and NPR+]] lists audio benefits and says direct supporters can receive event invitations and an NPR Shop discount.
- Growth and retention - [[the-economics-of-podcasting-and-npr-plus|The economics of podcasting and NPR+]] reports sub-2% podcast support and distinguishes voluntary from failed-payment churn.

## Qualifications

The source is an institutional explanation and fundraising argument, not audited product reporting. It does not disclose subscriber counts, show-level revenue, allocation formulas, operating costs, retention rates, benefit usage, station outcomes, or the incremental effect of bundling.
