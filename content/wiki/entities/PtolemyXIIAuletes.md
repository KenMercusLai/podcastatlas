# Ptolemy XII Auletes

## Overview

Ptolemy XII Auletes was the father of [[CleopatraVII|Cleopatra VII]] and a late ruler of [[PtolemaicEgypt|Ptolemaic Egypt]] whose dependence on Roman recognition, credit, and military restoration exposed the kingdom's constrained sovereignty.

## Current Profile

[[195-young-cleopatra-part-1-glt1502910094]] presents Auletes as a vulnerable monarch between Alexandrian opposition and Roman competition. He paid figures including [[Pompey]] and [[JuliusCaesar|Julius Caesar]] for recognition, financed those payments through taxation and borrowing, and fled after an Alexandrian revolt in 58 BCE.

A Roman expedition led by Aulus Gabinius and including the young [[MarkAntony|Mark Antony]] restored him. Auletes then executed his daughter [[BereniceIV|Berenice IV]], who had ruled during his absence, and appointed his creditor Rabirius to recover debt from Egypt. His death in 51 BCE transferred the throne to Cleopatra and [[PtolemyXIII|Ptolemy XIII]] as Roman civil conflict approached.

## Key Characteristics

- Late Ptolemaic king formally sovereign but heavily dependent on Roman favor.
- Debtor ruler who converted Egyptian taxation into payments to Roman politicians and financiers.
- Exiled monarch restored by a Roman-funded military expedition.
- Dynastic survivor who executed Berenice IV after recovering the throne.
- Father whose settlement placed teenage Cleopatra in a dangerous joint succession.

## Evidence

- Roman dependence: [[195-young-cleopatra-part-1-glt1502910094]] connects Auletes's recognition strategy to Pompey, Caesar, bribery, debt, and fear of annexation.
- Exile and restoration: [[195-young-cleopatra-part-1-glt1502910094]] follows the Alexandrian revolt, flight to Italy, Gabinius's expedition, and Roman military return.
- Fiscal aftermath: [[195-young-cleopatra-part-1-glt1502910094]] links Rabirius's appointment and extraction to the costs of restoration.
- Succession: [[195-young-cleopatra-part-1-glt1502910094]] connects Auletes's death to the joint accession of Cleopatra and Ptolemy XIII.

## Qualifications

The episode is a narrative introduction rather than a full reign. The meaning of Auletes's flute-playing nickname, the religious character of his Dionysian performance, exact financial arrangements, popular responsibility for his expulsion, and Cleopatra's private loyalty remain source-scoped or uncertain.

## What Changed

- Established Auletes as the fiscal and diplomatic link between Ptolemaic decline, Roman informal control, and Cleopatra's accession.

## Relationships

- [[CleopatraVII]] - daughter and successor presented as loyal during his restoration.
- [[BereniceIV]] - daughter who replaced him and was executed after his return.
- [[PtolemyXIII]] - younger son and co-successor with Cleopatra.
- [[PtolemaicEgypt]] - kingdom whose revenues supported his Roman recognition and restoration strategy.
- [[MarkAntony]] - young cavalry commander in the expedition that restored him.
- [[RomanInformalImperialControl]] - governing pattern expressed through recognition, debt, commanders, and intervention.
