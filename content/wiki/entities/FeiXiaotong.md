# 费孝通 / Fei Xiaotong

## Overview

[[FeiXiaotong|费孝通]] is presented as a Chinese sociologist and anthropologist whose village research, rural-reform thinking, and rediscovered English fiction grew from the social crises and intellectual debates of the 1930s.

## Current Profile

The source joins three roles that are often separated: empirical researcher, reform-oriented public intellectual, and literary writer. Fei moved from medicine into sociology, trained across Yenching and [[TsinghuaUniversity|清华大学]], and used investigation to test sweeping claims about Chinese society. [[JiangcunEconomy|《江村经济》]] examines a bounded village economy, while [[CocoonFeiXiaotong|《茧》]] combines wider Jiangnan experience, silk-industry experiments, and social relationships in fictional form.

His practical preference was to increase rural income through industry, technology, and organization, but the episode does not turn that preference into a universal solution. Fei acknowledged real rural crisis and revolutionary pressure, while [[RegionalVariationInRuralChina]] and world-market exposure limited what a Jiangnan model could establish for China as a whole.

## Key Characteristics

- Grounded general social questions in community study and concrete investigation.
- Combined academic analysis with a practical concern for rural livelihoods.
- Treated rural industry and technical improvement as possible responses to land and income pressure.
- Used literary fiction as another way to represent social relations, institutional experiments, and moral conflict.
- Worked within but did not simply reproduce the revolutionary and social-nature debates of his generation.
- Was intellectually and practically connected to women researchers and reformers whose contributions require independent recognition.

## Qualifications

The episode relies on a structured retrospective and repeatedly distinguishes fact from inference. Fei's later autobiographical explanations were shaped by later political circumstances; the exact reason he wrote *Cocoon*, the mapping of fictional characters onto real people, and the balance between early conviction and retrospective narration remain uncertain. A Jiangnan reform program also cannot by itself settle national debates about revolution, land, poverty, or industrialization.

## What Changed

- Created a profile integrating Fei's sociology, rural reform, and rediscovered fiction.
- Separated the bounded village study in *Peasant Life in China* from the broader composite world of *Cocoon*.
- Made the regional and distributional limits of his rural-industry program explicit.
