## Overview
曹腾 / Cao Teng is now tracked in three bounded roles: first as the eunuch actor whose personal slight from [[LiuSuanLateHan]] makes him oppose Liu Suan after [[EmperorZhiOfHan]]'s death, then as the adviser who helps [[LiangJiLateHan]] choose [[EmperorHuanOfHan]] in the 146 CE succession crisis, and later as the eunuch-line ancestor whose adoption of [[CaoSong]] shapes [[CaoCao]]'s inherited reputation problem. The profile therefore connects palace access, personal grievance, succession self-protection, and later elite stigma around eunuch family background.

## Current Profile
[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-827-yi-ju-hua-yinfa-de-diwang-zhisi-lnf6rz1i2vmgsclgiejbh0qevzc2|Hanji 827]] gives Cao Teng his earliest current wiki role. After senior officials recommend Liu Suan, Cao Teng opposes him because Liu Suan once failed to salute or show him deference. The succession objection is therefore introduced as a personal access grievance before it becomes a full factional-security argument.

In [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-828-da-jiangjun-liangji-shanquan-feili-de-donghan-jianchen-llqnqijagtmdlgitulddxspncu0e|Hanji 828]], the strategic rationale becomes explicit. Cao Teng warns Liang Ji that Liu Suan is strict and clear enough to threaten Liang Ji's guests and eunuch fortunes; he advises installing Liwu Hou Liu Zhi instead, because that choice would better preserve the incumbent bloc's wealth and status.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-904-juezhao-neiguan-huode-xinling-da-ziyou-de-mijue-lopn-okbzr4ljvqmgzcsb7qav0n2|Hanji 904]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-926-zhenzheng-de-qiangzhe-dou-dongde-zaoshi-lssmabzoku4ue28irdmfn0-d-0dc|Hanji 926]] supply the later Cao-family memory layer. They present Cao Teng as the adoptive father of Cao Song, making him the eunuch-connected ancestor through whom Cao Cao's early reputation is judged and partially recoded by later anti-eunuch signaling.

## Key Characteristics
- Eunuch power broker whose palace position gives him decisive advisory access during a succession vacuum.
- Personal-grievance actor whose opposition to Liu Suan begins from perceived disrespect.
- Self-preservation strategist who frames ruler choice around the security of Liang Ji's clients and eunuch fortunes.
- Enabler of Liu Zhi's accession as Emperor Huan in the current evidence base.
- Adoptive father of Cao Song and ancestral link in Cao Cao's eunuch-family reputation problem.
- Figure whose legacy shows how eunuch status can matter both as immediate court power and as later elite stigma.

## Evidence
- Personal grievance against Liu Suan: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-827-yi-ju-hua-yinfa-de-diwang-zhisi-lnf6rz1i2vmgsclgiejbh0qevzc2|Hanji 827]] says Cao Teng opposes Liu Suan because Liu Suan had not properly saluted or acknowledged him.
- Succession advice: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-828-da-jiangjun-liangji-shanquan-feili-de-donghan-jianchen-llqnqijagtmdlgitulddxspncu0e|Hanji 828]] says Cao Teng persuades Liang Ji to avoid Liu Suan and choose Liu Zhi because the stricter candidate would threaten existing palace and Liang-family interests.
- Eunuch interest alignment: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-828-da-jiangjun-liangji-shanquan-feili-de-donghan-jianchen-llqnqijagtmdlgitulddxspncu0e|Hanji 828]] explicitly links Cao Teng's argument to the eunuch bloc's desire to preserve wealth and status.
- Cao family genealogy: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-926-zhenzheng-de-qiangzhe-dou-dongde-zaoshi-lssmabzoku4ue28irdmfn0-d-0dc|Hanji 926]] treats Cao Teng as the eunuch ancestor whose adoption of Cao Song makes Cao Cao vulnerable to reputational attack.
- Career-entry setup: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-904-juezhao-neiguan-huode-xinling-da-ziyou-de-mijue-lopn-okbzr4ljvqmgzcsb7qav0n2|Hanji 904]] adds the earlier Xiaolian and first-entry context in which Cao Cao benefits from the Cao family channel while also carrying its stigma.
- Reputation recoding: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-926-zhenzheng-de-qiangzhe-dou-dongde-zaoshi-lssmabzoku4ue28irdmfn0-d-0dc|Hanji 926]] connects Cao Cao's later hard line against Jian Shuo's uncle to the need to offset inherited eunuch associations.

## Qualifications
The current profile is not a full biography of Cao Teng. Hanji 827 gives the personal-grievance layer behind his opposition to Liu Suan; Hanji 828 captures the decisive strategic succession intervention; Hanji 904 and Hanji 926 use Cao Teng primarily to explain Cao Song and Cao Cao's reputation pathway. The source set does not yet establish every office, patronage tie, or later evaluation attached to Cao Teng in broader historical tradition.

## What Changed
- Added Hanji 827 as Cao Teng's earliest current succession layer: personal resentment toward Liu Suan after a failed deference encounter.
- Reframed Hanji 828 as the factional-security argument that extends this opposition into Liang Ji's ruler choice.
- Preserved Cao Teng as both an active eunuch strategist and a genealogical source of later Cao Cao stigma.

## Relationships
- [[LiangJiLateHan]] - receives Cao Teng's advice and acts on the self-protective succession argument.
- [[EmperorHuanOfHan]] - installed as Liu Zhi after Cao Teng argues he is safer for the incumbent power bloc.
- [[LiuSuanLateHan]] - rejected candidate whose earlier failure to defer and later strictness make him dangerous to Cao Teng's personal and factional interests.
- [[LiGuLateHan]] and [[DuQiaoLateHan]] - ministerial opponents of the succession outcome Cao Teng helps enable.
- [[CaoSong]] - adopted son who carries Cao Teng's eunuch-family connection into the Cao lineage.
- [[CaoCao]] - later figure whose early reputation is marked by Cao Teng's eunuch status.
- [[SelfProtectiveAccessionChoice]] - conceptual frame for Cao Teng's succession logic.
- [[PalaceProximityPower]] - Cao Teng's influence depends on palace access in a moment of dynastic vacancy.
- [[EliteEndorsementReputationMaking]] - later Cao Cao reputation work responds partly to Cao Teng-linked stigma.
