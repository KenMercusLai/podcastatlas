# Gallipoli Campaign

## Overview

The Gallipoli Campaign is the [[FirstWorldWar|First World War]] operation treated across [[675-the-first-world-war-slaughter-at-gallipoli-part-5-glt4394132715]] and [[676-the-first-world-war-churchills-calamity-part-6-glt1275431911]] as [[WinstonChurchill|Winston Churchill]]'s Dardanelles shortcut becoming an attritional battlefield, political disaster, and national-memory source.

## Current Profile

The campaign began as a failed attempt to use the [[DardanellesStrategicShortcut]] to bypass Western Front stalemate, reach Constantinople, open a route to Russia, and force the [[OttomanEmpire|Ottoman Empire]] out of the war. [[675-the-first-world-war-slaughter-at-gallipoli-part-5-glt4394132715]] stresses how the plan moved from naval optimism to landings after the March 1915 naval failure, while [[676-the-first-world-war-churchills-calamity-part-6-glt1275431911]] follows the beachheads into stalemate, reinforcement failure, and evacuation.

The campaign's profile in the wiki is not only tactical. It joins battlefield limits, diseased trench conditions, political crisis in London, failed reinforcement choices, and later memory. Its military failure damaged Churchill, strengthened [[MustafaKemalAtaturk|Mustafa Kemal / Ataturk]], and became foundational for [[AnzacSpirit]] in [[Australia]] and [[NewZealand]].

## Key Characteristics

- The campaign was imagined as a strategic shortcut through the Dardanelles rather than a conventional Western Front offensive.
- The March 1915 naval failure produced escalation into landings rather than termination.
- The April 1915 landings produced beachhead survival but not operational breakout.
- Modern defensive firepower, rough terrain, and trench systems turned offensive plans into costly attrition.
- Sanitation collapse, heat, unburied bodies, flies, inadequate latrines, and dysentery made the campaign a disease burden as well as a combat burden.
- Westminster's coalition crisis, Churchill's removal, and delayed reinforcement decisions made the campaign a political accountability case.
- The final evacuation was tactically successful but did not reverse the strategic failure, while later memory turned Gallipoli into Churchill's calamity, Ataturk's reputation-maker, and an Anzac identity story.

## Evidence

- Strategic-shortcut failure: [[675-the-first-world-war-slaughter-at-gallipoli-part-5-glt4394132715]] explains the initial promise of forcing the Dardanelles, opening the Black Sea route, and threatening Constantinople, while [[676-the-first-world-war-churchills-calamity-part-6-glt1275431911]] argues even a successful peninsula campaign would not necessarily have secured those outcomes.
- Escalation and landing failure: [[675-the-first-world-war-slaughter-at-gallipoli-part-5-glt4394132715]] tracks War Council approval, Fisher's warnings, the March 18 naval losses, Hamilton's rushed planning, Anzac Cove, Cape Helles, and the River Clyde disaster.
- Attrition and disease: [[676-the-first-world-war-churchills-calamity-part-6-glt1275431911]] follows Krithia, Suvla Bay, Lone Pine, the Nek, trench systems, dead bodies, flies, inadequate sanitation, and dysentery as linked reasons Gallipoli became especially destructive.
- Political and memory consequences: [[675-the-first-world-war-slaughter-at-gallipoli-part-5-glt4394132715]] anchors the first-day ANZAC and Mustafa Kemal memory, while [[676-the-first-world-war-churchills-calamity-part-6-glt1275431911]] connects Churchill's Admiralty fall, Hamilton's removal, evacuation, and later Anzac public memory.
