# Gallipoli Campaign

## Overview

The Gallipoli Campaign is the [[FirstWorldWar|First World War]] operation treated in [[676-the-first-world-war-churchills-calamity-part-6-glt1275431911]] as [[WinstonChurchill|Winston Churchill]]'s Dardanelles shortcut becoming an attritional battlefield and political disaster.

## Current Profile

The episode frames Gallipoli as a failed attempt to use the [[DardanellesStrategicShortcut]] to bypass Western Front stalemate, reach Constantinople, and force the [[OttomanEmpire|Ottoman Empire]] out of the war. The naval failure led to landings on 25 April 1915, but Allied forces at Anzac Cove and Cape Helles were quickly trapped in difficult terrain and trench warfare.

The campaign's profile in the wiki is not only tactical. It joins battlefield limits, diseased trench conditions, political crisis in London, failed reinforcement choices, and later memory. Its military failure damaged Churchill, strengthened [[MustafaKemalAtaturk|Mustafa Kemal / Ataturk]], and became foundational for [[AnzacSpirit]] in [[Australia]] and [[NewZealand]].

## Key Characteristics

- The campaign was imagined as a strategic shortcut through the Dardanelles rather than a conventional Western Front offensive.
- The April 1915 landings produced beachhead survival but not operational breakout.
- Modern defensive firepower, rough terrain, and trench systems turned offensive plans into costly attrition.
- Sanitation collapse, heat, unburied bodies, flies, inadequate latrines, and dysentery made the campaign a disease burden as well as a combat burden.
- Westminster's coalition crisis, Churchill's removal, and delayed reinforcement decisions made the campaign a political accountability case.
- The final evacuation was tactically successful but did not reverse the strategic failure.
- Later memory turned Gallipoli into Churchill's calamity, Ataturk's reputation-maker, and an Anzac identity story.

## Evidence

- Strategic-shortcut failure: [[676-the-first-world-war-churchills-calamity-part-6-glt1275431911]] says the campaign was intended to force the Dardanelles, threaten Constantinople, and knock the Ottoman Empire out of the war, but even a successful peninsula campaign would not necessarily have secured those outcomes.
- Attrition and disease: [[676-the-first-world-war-churchills-calamity-part-6-glt1275431911]] follows Krithia, Suvla Bay, Lone Pine, the Nek, trench systems, dead bodies, flies, inadequate sanitation, and dysentery as linked reasons Gallipoli became especially destructive.
- Political and memory consequences: [[676-the-first-world-war-churchills-calamity-part-6-glt1275431911]] connects Churchill's Admiralty fall, Hamilton's removal, evacuation, Mustafa Kemal's rising reputation, and Anzac memory in Australia and New Zealand.
