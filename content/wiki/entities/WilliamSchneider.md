# William Schneider

## Overview

William Schneider is presented as a former Fort Detrick plant virologist who moved from comparing detection methods to developing synthetic scent aids for training disease-detection dogs.

## Current Profile

In [[the-dog-will-see-you-now-the-dog-will-see-you-now]], Schneider says dogs outperformed available laboratory approaches in a plum pox virus comparison. He then describes producing inert, stable, shippable materials intended to reproduce characteristic pathogen-associated odors without requiring handlers to work with infectious clinical material.

His COVID-19 account supplies the episode's strongest performance claim: dogs trained after publication of the SARS-CoV-2 genome reportedly reached 99% accuracy and detected signals across several sample types. The summary does not include the validation design needed to interpret that figure, so his durable contribution here is the training-aid and deployment model rather than an independently established accuracy estimate.

## Key Characteristics

- Brings virology and assay-design experience into working-dog training.
- Compares canine detection with instrument and nucleic-acid methods.
- Develops inert, stable, reproducible scent-training materials.
- Treats safe distribution and training scale as part of biodetection infrastructure.
- Makes high reported performance claims whose methods are absent from the supplied source.

## Evidence

### Comparative detection work

- [[the-dog-will-see-you-now-the-dog-will-see-you-now]] attributes to Schneider a plum pox virus comparison in which dogs outperformed the laboratory methods available to him.

### Synthetic training aids

- [[the-dog-will-see-you-now-the-dog-will-see-you-now]] describes his use of pathogen information to create inert, stable material that can be produced in quantity and shipped to dog teams.

### COVID-19 training

- [[the-dog-will-see-you-now-the-dog-will-see-you-now]] reports training after the SARS-CoV-2 genome release and later checking dogs against confirmed-positive clinical samples.

## Qualifications

This profile relies on one structured episode summary rather than Schneider's publications, protocols, patents, or independent evaluations. The source does not show which odorants the aids reproduce, whether they generalize to natural infection, how contamination and handler cueing were controlled, or how the reported accuracy divides into sensitivity and specificity across prevalence and sample types.
