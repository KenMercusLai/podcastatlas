# Tony Benn

## Overview
Tony Benn appears in this wiki as a Labour-left democratic-sovereignty critic of European Community membership and a later critic of [[JimCallaghan|Jim Callaghan]] and [[DenisHealey]] during [[Britain1976IMFCrisis|Britain's 1976 IMF crisis]].

## Current Profile
The sources present Benn as the clearest Labour-left figure joining constitutional democracy, socialism, and economic sovereignty. [[663-britain-in-the-70s-the-brexit-that-never-was-part-2-glt2237203377]] makes him a prominent 1975 No campaigner who argued that European authority could block democratic socialism and remove power from voters. [[665-britain-in-the-70s-the-bailout-from-hell-part-4-glt9598017819]] then uses Benn to represent Labour's alternative economic strategy: import controls, capital controls, bank nationalisation, higher taxes on the rich, and departure from the Common Market.

## Key Characteristics
- He framed European Community membership as a democratic-sovereignty problem because voters could not easily remove European power.
- He linked anti-EC politics to democratic socialism, worker control, industrial planning, and suspicion of capitalist constraints.
- He represented Labour-left resistance to Healey's spending cuts and IMF-backed discipline.
- He promoted an alternative economic strategy centered on controls, nationalisation, and withdrawal from European constraints.
- He remained alienated from Callaghan's line even after the cabinet accepted the deal.

## Evidence
- Democratic sovereignty: [[663-britain-in-the-70s-the-brexit-that-never-was-part-2-glt2237203377]] says Benn opposed Europe because he saw it as a capitalist cartel and argued that power should remain removable by voters.
- Industrial planning: [[663-britain-in-the-70s-the-brexit-that-never-was-part-2-glt2237203377]] describes Benn's planning agreements, National Enterprise Board ambitions, emergency-powers thinking, and worker-cooperative experiments.
- Alternative program: [[665-britain-in-the-70s-the-bailout-from-hell-part-4-glt9598017819]] lists Benn's preferred import controls, capital controls, nationalisation, higher taxes, and Common Market exit.
- Party conflict: [[665-britain-in-the-70s-the-bailout-from-hell-part-4-glt9598017819]] places Benn's argument alongside Labour conference anger and the cabinet split over IMF terms.
- Cabinet outcome: [[665-britain-in-the-70s-the-bailout-from-hell-part-4-glt9598017819]] says Callaghan's side prevailed while Benn remained unhappy but did not stop the deal.
