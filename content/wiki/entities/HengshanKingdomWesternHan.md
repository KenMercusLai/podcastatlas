## Overview

衡山国 / Hengshan Kingdom is the Western Han vassal kingdom ruled by [[LiuCiHengshanKingWesternHan|刘赐]] after his transfer from Lujiang. Hanji 295 adds its recruitment, weapons, forged seals, and secret alliance with Huainan; Hanji 304-2 shows that the king shares jurisdiction with centrally appointed officials and can lose appointment powers; Hanji 305-2 treats abolition as the endpoint of a royal-house crisis that exposes those preparations.

## Current Profile

The kingdom appears not as a complete administrative history but as a constrained royal jurisdiction. Hanji 295 says Liu Ci recruits retainers, directs preparation of chariots, swords, and forged imperial and military seals, places Liu Xiao over recruitment and matériel, and secretly agrees with Liu An on joint action. Hanji 304-2 says its 内史 can reject Liu Ci's unlawful death sentence and report royal misconduct, while the imperial court can remove the king's authority to appoint more senior local officials. Hanji 305-2 adds that Liu Ci must seek imperial approval to replace crown prince [[LiuShuangHengshanCrownPrince|刘爽]]. Rival reports then give the emperor grounds to investigate; the episode says the royal principals are ordered to die and the kingdom becomes directly administered territory.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han vassal kingdom dependent on imperial approval for a formal heir change.
- Royal jurisdiction supervised by centrally appointed officials and subject to imperial restriction of appointment power.
- Royal jurisdiction in which household succession conflict intersects with alleged military preparation.
- Kingdom whose ruler turns independent preparation into a secret alliance with neighboring Huainan.
- Polity exposed to central investigation through reciprocal denunciation by its ruling family.
- Abolished kingdom whose territory the source says becomes a commandery under direct court control.

## Qualifications

This page is intentionally disambiguated from [[HengshanState|恒山国]], the pre-Qin polity in Guan Zhong's economic-war story. The profile rests on three short narrative episodes from one series and does not establish the kingdom's full chronology, geography, offices, population, fiscal organization, exact division of royal and central authority, alliance mechanics, precise investigative procedure, or the administrative sequence and boundaries behind the stated conversion to 衡山郡. Hanji 295's “刘刺” and Hanji 304-2's “恒山国” are treated as transcript errors for the contextually identified king and polity.

## What Changed

- The kingdom's military preparation now includes recruitment, chariots, swords, forged seals, and delegated authority before the later denunciations.
- Its external position now includes a secret joint-rising pact with Huainan after Liu Ci's earlier independent preparation.
