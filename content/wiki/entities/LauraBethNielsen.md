# Laura Beth Nielsen

## Overview

Laura Beth Nielsen is a Northwestern University law professor and social scientist presented in [[little-mermaid-part-1-the-golden-contract-little-mermaid-part-1-the-golden-contract|Little Mermaid Part 1: The Golden Contract]] as a scholar of how ordinary people understand liberty, fairness, justice, and the proper role of the state.

## Current Profile

Nielsen applies sociolegal analysis to children's media after repeated family viewing made the legal structure of Disney's *The Little Mermaid* newly visible to her. Her central distinction is between a document that looks formally authoritative and an agreement that law should actually recognize. Because Ariel is a minor, surrenders her voice, risks enslavement, and faces interference from Ursula, Nielsen treats the golden contract as an example of form being allowed to defeat justice.

Her broader media argument concerns institutional imagination. If law is controlled by the villain, cannot prevent exploitation, and offers no corrective procedure, the story leaves lethal force as the working remedy. Nielsen therefore reads entertainment not as a literal legal textbook but as one place where children encounter models of obligation, authority, and conflict resolution.

## Key Characteristics

- Sociolegal scholar concerned with everyday understandings of law, fairness, liberty, and state authority.
- Reads children's stories as possible contributors to early moral and legal frameworks.
- Distinguishes written form from substantive validity and justice.
- Treats exploitative bargaining and bad-faith interference as reasons law should refuse enforcement.
- Critiques narratives in which institutions disappear and violence becomes the only effective remedy.

## Qualifications

This profile is based on one podcast's presentation of Nielsen and is not a complete account of her scholarship, publications, teaching, or legal views. The source offers interpretive media criticism rather than direct causal evidence about how a particular film changes children's beliefs. Its contract analysis does not specify a real jurisdiction or exhaust the doctrines that might govern capacity, bodily autonomy, unconscionability, interference, slavery, or remedy.
