# Odysseus

## Overview

Odysseus is the central hero of [[TheOdyssey]], defined in the current wiki as a cunning, morally difficult returner whose goal is [[HomericNostos]] and restored household recognition rather than endless adventure.

## Current Profile

EP269 establishes the main corrective: Odysseus' route matters because it returns him to [[Ithaca]], the social world where identity, household order, ritual, and [[AncientGreekHonorCommunity|honor]] can be recognized. The Rest Is History first-half retelling makes his mixed character concrete before the homecoming even begins. Odysseus is credited with the [[TrojanHorse]], tries unsuccessfully to avoid the war in a non-Homeric story, raids Ismarus, uses wine and the "No One" trick against [[Polyphemus]], reveals his identity out of pride, survives the collapse of crew trust around [[Aeolus]] and [[Helios]], receives guidance through [[Circe]] and [[Tiresias]], and reaches [[Calypso]] only after every shipmate has died.

The second-half retelling then completes the return. Odysseus refuses Calypso's immortality, survives Poseidon's storm with [[Athena]]'s help, becomes his own narrator among the [[Phaeacians]], lies even when back home, enters his palace as a beggar, tests loyalties, strings the bow, kills the suitors, and is finally accepted by [[Penelope]] through the marriage-bed secret.

The adaptation branch keeps a warning attached to him. Modern retellings may want a psychologically legible and morally simplified hero, but the wiki's source set treats Odysseus' deception, brutality, longing, dependency on others, and desire for recognition as part of the ancient figure's force.

## Key Characteristics

- Odysseus is goal-directed toward mortal homecoming, but the first-half voyage shows that desire under pressure from pleasure, hunger, delay, fear, and divine hostility.
- His intelligence is tactical and narrative: he devises the Trojan Horse, names himself "No One," lies, disguises himself, reads situations, and tells his own adventures.
- His brilliance has limits because pride, secrecy, sleep, and weak crew trust repeatedly turn successes into renewed danger.
- His return depends on allies and guides, including Athena, Circe, Tiresias, Telemachus, Penelope, Eumaeus, Philoteus, Eurycleia, and the Phaeacians.
- His restoration of order is violent and morally difficult, centered on killing the suitors and punishing disloyal household figures.
- He is hard to modernize cleanly because ancient heroism, cunning, cruelty, grief, and social recognition do not map onto a simple good-person protagonist.

- Fixed homecoming goal: [[ep269-cong-manwei-yuzhou-dao-aodesai-weihe-zongyao-fanpai-yingxiong-shishi-ltklotheqpup6oamyflfzpxtipsw]] says Odysseus is not pursuing indefinite adventure but return to a community of recognition, [[687-the-odyssey-hero-of-the-trojan-horse-part-1-glt2393125423]] defines that desire as nostos under repeated pressure, and [[688-the-odyssey-return-of-the-king-part-2-glt2882541084]] shows him refusing Calypso's immortality.
- Tactical cunning: [[687-the-odyssey-hero-of-the-trojan-horse-part-1-glt2393125423]] credits him with the Trojan Horse and the "No One" escape from Polyphemus, while [[688-the-odyssey-return-of-the-king-part-2-glt2882541084]] has him lie to Athena on Ithaca, claim to be a Cretan to Eumaeus, describe himself convincingly to Penelope, and enter the palace in disguise.
- Limits and failure: [[687-the-odyssey-hero-of-the-trojan-horse-part-1-glt2393125423]] shows the Ismarus losses, the Polyphemus boast, Aeolus' opened bag, Scylla's six victims, and Helios' cattle as cases where command, trust, or circumstance defeat clean success.
- Dependence on allies and guides: [[687-the-odyssey-hero-of-the-trojan-horse-part-1-glt2393125423]] gives Circe and Tiresias key advisory roles, and [[688-the-odyssey-return-of-the-king-part-2-glt2882541084]] gives Athena, the Phaeacians, Telemachus, Eumaeus, Philoteus, Eurycleia, and Penelope distinct roles in the return's completion.
- Moral difficulty and adaptation risk: [[in-it-to-bin-it-nigel-farage-v-count-binface-6a58abcdc152a357db17c20e]] frames modern smoothing of Odysseus as a [[HomericAdaptationModernization]] problem, while [[688-the-odyssey-return-of-the-king-part-2-glt2882541084]] keeps the slaughter and punishments explicit.
