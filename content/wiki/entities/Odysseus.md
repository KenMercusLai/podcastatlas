# Odysseus

## Overview

Odysseus is the central hero of [[TheOdyssey]], defined in the current wiki as a cunning, morally difficult returner whose goal is restored household recognition rather than endless adventure.

## Current Profile

EP269 establishes the main corrective: Odysseus' route matters because it returns him to [[Ithaca]], the social world where identity, household order, ritual, and [[AncientGreekHonorCommunity|honor]] can be recognized. The new Rest Is History retelling makes that concrete. Odysseus refuses [[Calypso]]'s immortality, survives Poseidon's storm with [[Athena]]'s help, becomes his own narrator among the [[Phaeacians]], lies even when back home, enters his palace as a beggar, tests loyalties, strings the bow, kills the suitors, and is finally accepted by [[Penelope]] through the marriage-bed secret.

The adaptation branch keeps a warning attached to him. Modern retellings may want a psychologically legible and morally simplified hero, but the wiki's source set treats Odysseus' deception, brutality, longing, dependency on others, and desire for recognition as part of the ancient figure's force.

## Key Characteristics

- Odysseus is goal-directed toward mortal homecoming, even when offered immortality away from Penelope and Ithaca.
- His intelligence is tactical and narrative: he lies, disguises himself, reads situations, and tells his own adventures.
- His return depends on allies, including Athena, Telemachus, Penelope, Eumaeus, Philoteus, Eurycleia, and the Phaeacians.
- His restoration of order is violent and morally difficult, centered on killing the suitors and punishing disloyal household figures.
- He is hard to modernize cleanly because ancient heroism, cunning, cruelty, grief, and social recognition do not map onto a simple good-person protagonist.

## Evidence

- Fixed homecoming goal: [[ep269-cong-manwei-yuzhou-dao-aodesai-weihe-zongyao-fanpai-yingxiong-shishi-ltklotheqpup6oamyflfzpxtipsw]] says Odysseus is not pursuing indefinite adventure but return to a community of recognition, and [[688-the-odyssey-return-of-the-king-part-2-glt2882541084]] shows him refusing Calypso's immortality.
- Tactical cunning: [[688-the-odyssey-return-of-the-king-part-2-glt2882541084]] has Odysseus lie to Athena on Ithaca, claim to be a Cretan to Eumaeus, describe himself convincingly to Penelope, and enter the palace in disguise.
- Dependence on allies: [[688-the-odyssey-return-of-the-king-part-2-glt2882541084]] gives Athena, the Phaeacians, Telemachus, Eumaeus, Philoteus, Eurycleia, and Penelope distinct roles in the return's completion.
- Moral difficulty and adaptation risk: [[in-it-to-bin-it-nigel-farage-v-count-binface-6a58abcdc152a357db17c20e]] frames modern smoothing of Odysseus as a [[HomericAdaptationModernization]] problem, while [[688-the-odyssey-return-of-the-king-part-2-glt2882541084]] keeps the slaughter and punishments explicit.
