# Battle of Liège (1914)

## Overview

The Battle of Liège was the opening major battle of the [[GermanInvasionOfBelgium1914|German invasion of Belgium]], where Belgian troops and a ring of forts imposed unexpected resistance before German forces captured the city and reduced the remaining defenses.

## Current Profile

[[594-the-first-world-war-the-invasion-of-belgium-part-1-glt4855256129]] presents Liège as a collision between Germany's need for speed and Belgian defensive capacity. The assault began on 5 August against a garrison the episode places at roughly 40,000. Early infantry attacks suffered heavily under Belgian fire, previewing the mismatch between massed advance and modern defensive weapons that would recur on the Western Front.

Erich Ludendorff helped seize the city and citadel on 7 August, but the surrounding forts continued to resist for roughly another ten or eleven days. The distinction matters: capture of the urban center did not mean the whole fortress system had immediately ceased to obstruct German movement.

Liège also became the setting for the invasion's first large cycle of civilian fear and reprisal. German troops interpreted shots and rumored ambushes as francs-tireurs activity, while some fire may have come from Belgian regulars using cover. The resulting shootings of suspected civilians made tactical uncertainty, inherited memory, and collective punishment inseparable from the battle's history.

## Key Characteristics

- Opened on 5 August 1914 against a larger and more resilient garrison than Germany expected.
- Exposed attacking infantry to destructive modern defensive fire.
- Separated capture of the city and citadel from the later reduction of surrounding forts.
- Delayed a campaign governed by a tightly compressed western timetable.
- Included an early Zeppelin attack that killed civilians.
- Became a setting for mass reprisals driven by real, mistaken, or rumored civilian-fire claims.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to one narrative source and does not reconstruct every fort, unit, artillery action, or command decision. The approximate garrison, timing, civilian death totals, individual stories, and the precise amount of operational delay remain episode-attributed. Distinguishing regular soldiers from civilians in particular incidents may explain German perception but does not validate collective punishment.

## What Changed

- Created Liège as the opening battle where fortress resistance, modern firepower, timetable pressure, and civilian reprisals converged.
