# Guthrum

## Overview

Guthrum appears in the wiki as the Danish leader whose surprise seizure of Chippenham nearly destroyed [[AlfredTheGreat|Alfred the Great]]'s kingship before defeat in 878 led to withdrawal, baptism, and a new form of Christian royal legitimacy.

## Current Profile

Guthrum led the later “great summer army” and remained a central threat after Alfred's accession. In early 878, despite a treaty and hostage exchange, his force attacked Chippenham during the Christmas season, exploiting surprise and assumptions about winter and sacred time to drive Alfred into flight.

After Alfred's recovery and victory at the [[BattleOfEdington]], Guthrum's army withdrew to Chippenham and endured a siege of roughly two weeks before accepting terms. Guthrum was baptized at Aller with Alfred as sponsor, took the Christian name Athelstan, and was joined in baptism by close companions.

The episode treats the settlement as more than forced religious theater: it marks a movement from mobile raiding toward territorial rule that could borrow authority and status from Anglo-Saxon Christian kingship. That interpretation leads into the later [[DanelawSettlement]], although the source does not establish Guthrum's inner belief or make conversion a sudden end to conflict.

## Key Characteristics

- Danish military leader whose 878 campaign brought West Saxon kingship close to collapse.
- Commander who used treaty-breaking, winter timing, and surprise at Chippenham.
- Defeated opponent who negotiated withdrawal after Edington and the siege of Chippenham.
- Baptized ruler who took the name Athelstan under Alfred's sponsorship.
- Transitional figure between Viking warfare and territorially grounded Christian kingship.

- Defeat and negotiation - [[251-alfred-the-great-return-of-the-king-part-2-glt9621369023]] follows Guthrum from Edington to the Chippenham siege and accepted terms.
- Surprise offensive - [[250-alfred-the-great-fury-of-the-vikings-part-1-glt7924523463]] follows Guthrum's force from renewed Viking pressure to the January 878 seizure of Chippenham and Alfred's escape.
- Baptism and sponsorship - [[251-alfred-the-great-return-of-the-king-part-2-glt9621369023]] places his baptism at Aller and identifies Alfred as sponsor.
- Political transition - [[251-alfred-the-great-return-of-the-king-part-2-glt9621369023]] interprets baptism as access to Christian royal legitimacy during a shift toward settled rule.

## Qualifications

The two sources are consecutive episodes in one podcast series rather than independent confirmation. They do not independently establish Guthrum's motives, personal belief, exact authority over all Danish forces, treaty obligations, or the direct legal path from the 878 terms to the later Danelaw boundary. Baptism should not be read as immediate cultural assimilation or an end to Anglo-Danish warfare.

## What Changed

- Established Guthrum as a negotiated Christian ruler rather than only Alfred's defeated enemy.
- Added his surprise winter seizure of Chippenham as the high point preceding Edington.

## Relationships
