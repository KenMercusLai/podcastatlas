# Guthrum

## Overview

Guthrum appears in the wiki as the Danish leader defeated by [[AlfredTheGreat|Alfred the Great]] in 878 who then accepted baptism, withdrawal, and a new form of Christian royal legitimacy.

## Current Profile

After Alfred's victory at the [[BattleOfEdington]], Guthrum's army withdrew to Chippenham and endured a siege of roughly two weeks before accepting terms. Guthrum was baptized at Aller with Alfred as sponsor, took the Christian name Athelstan, and was joined in baptism by close companions.

The episode treats the settlement as more than forced religious theater: it marks a movement from mobile raiding toward territorial rule that could borrow authority and status from Anglo-Saxon Christian kingship. That interpretation leads into the later [[DanelawSettlement]], although the source does not establish Guthrum's inner belief or make conversion a sudden end to conflict.

## Key Characteristics

- Danish military leader whose 878 campaign brought West Saxon kingship close to collapse.
- Defeated opponent who negotiated withdrawal after Edington and the siege of Chippenham.
- Baptized ruler who took the name Athelstan under Alfred's sponsorship.
- Transitional figure between Viking warfare and territorially grounded Christian kingship.

## Evidence

- Defeat and negotiation - [[251-alfred-the-great-return-of-the-king-part-2-glt9621369023]] follows Guthrum from Edington to the Chippenham siege and accepted terms.
- Baptism and sponsorship - [[251-alfred-the-great-return-of-the-king-part-2-glt9621369023]] places his baptism at Aller and identifies Alfred as sponsor.
- Political transition - [[251-alfred-the-great-return-of-the-king-part-2-glt9621369023]] interprets baptism as access to Christian royal legitimacy during a shift toward settled rule.

## Qualifications

This is a single-source profile. The episode does not independently establish Guthrum's motives, personal belief, exact authority over all Danish forces, or the direct legal path from the 878 terms to the later Danelaw boundary. Baptism should not be read as immediate cultural assimilation or an end to Anglo-Danish warfare.

## What Changed

- Established Guthrum as a negotiated Christian ruler rather than only Alfred's defeated enemy.
