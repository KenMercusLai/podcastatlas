## Overview

施丹 / Shi Dan enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-626-waren-fenmu-wangmang-jing-ruci-sangxinbingkuang-lotwk6rbtl5pvbrnlfrysu5ol6rz|Hanji 626]] as a Western Han official retrospectively rewarded after [[WangMang|王莽]]'s posthumous prosecution of [[FuTaihouWesternHan|傅太后]] and [[DingJiWesternHan|丁姬]]. His role is narrow but useful: he shows how grave and title correction also reorders living officials' reputations.

## Current Profile

The source says Shi Dan had previously opposed Fu Taihou and had been removed from office and stripped of ducal standing. After Wang Mang's 5 CE campaign against Fu Taihou and Ding Ji, Shi Dan is summoned to Chang'an, made a guannei marquis, restored to his former income, later made Yiyang marquis, and then dies soon afterward.

Shi Dan therefore functions as a posthumous-merit-vindication case. The reward does not come from a new achievement in the episode; it comes from Wang Mang recoding an earlier stance as correct once the court officially condemns Fu Taihou's old status.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han official whose earlier opposition to Fu Taihou becomes retroactively rewarded.
- Example of Wang Mang turning posthumous ritual correction into living political sorting.
- Restored and re-titled shortly before his death.
- Narrow source figure rather than a fully synthesized statesman profile.

## Qualifications

Hanji 626 does not reconstruct the full earlier debate in which Shi Dan opposed Fu Taihou. The page therefore treats him as source-scoped evidence for retrospective reward, not as a complete biography or independent judgment on the earlier dispute.
