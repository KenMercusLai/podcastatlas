## Overview
The Antarctic Treaty appears in [[the-continent-nobody-owns-everyone-benefits-from-summer-school]] as the governance arrangement that keeps [[Antarctica]] oriented toward peace and science rather than ordinary territorial exploitation.

## Current Profile
The treaty's role in the source is institutional and economic. It helps explain why a highly valuable continent can be governed without normal private ownership, military bases, or mining companies in the episode's account. That makes the treaty a concrete example of [[TrustBasedCommonsGovernance]].

## Key Characteristics
- It frames Antarctica as a protected scientific and peaceful-use zone in the source.
- It depends on cooperation among many countries rather than one owner or sovereign market regulator.
- It makes restraint economically significant because the continent's largest value comes from preserved climate services.
- It relies on trust, collaboration, respect, and sanctions according to Natalie Stokel's account.

## Evidence
- Peace and science function: [[the-continent-nobody-owns-everyone-benefits-from-summer-school]] says the treaty reserves Antarctica for peaceful purposes and scientific research.
- Non-ownership governance: [[the-continent-nobody-owns-everyone-benefits-from-summer-school]] uses the treaty to explain how Antarctica can remain outside ordinary military and mining development.
- Trust mechanism: [[the-continent-nobody-owns-everyone-benefits-from-summer-school]] describes the system as relying on trust, collaboration, respect, and sanctions.

## Qualifications
The source is not a treaty-law explainer. Its signatory count and restrictions are preserved as episode-level claims, not independently updated legal status.
