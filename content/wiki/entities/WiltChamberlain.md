# Wilt Chamberlain

## Overview

Wilt Chamberlain is the record-setting basketball player at the center of [[the-big-man-can-t-shoot-the-big-man-can-t-shoot|The Big Man Can’t Shoot]]. The episode uses his brief success with underhand free throws and later abandonment of the technique to examine the gap between knowing that a method works and accepting its social cost.

## Current Profile

The episode presents Chamberlain as exceptionally large, athletic, and prolific. On March 2, 1962, he scored 100 points for the Philadelphia Warriors against the New York Knicks and made 28 of 32 free throws. Those free throws matter to the episode because he had previously been a roughly 40-percent shooter and improved above 60 percent during the 1961–62 season after adopting an underhand motion.

Chamberlain nevertheless returned to the conventional overhand technique. His own reported explanation emphasizes feeling silly and unmanly rather than doubting the result. Gladwell therefore interprets him through the [[GranovetterThresholdModel]] as a high-threshold adopter: personal evidence and competitive incentive were insufficient without visible peer acceptance.

## Key Characteristics

- Extraordinary scorer whose 100-point game anchors the episode.
- Episode-reported 28-for-32 free-throw performance during that game.
- Improved substantially while using an underhand free-throw technique.
- Faced strong competitive incentives to improve because opponents frequently fouled him.
- Abandoned an effective experiment because of perceived ridicule and gendered stigma.
- Serves as the episode’s case of unrealized performance under social pressure.
