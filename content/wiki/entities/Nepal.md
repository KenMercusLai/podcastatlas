# Nepal

## Overview
Nepal is a Himalayan country that appears in the wiki through mountaineering, rescue, and climate-disaster exposure. Current evidence connects elite high-altitude climbing to broader national vulnerability: the same mountains that shape [[NirmalPurja]] and [[ProjectPossible]] also expose river valleys, hydropower tunnels, pilgrims, villages, and rescue, identification, warning, and recovery systems to extreme events.

## Current Profile
The current profile is no longer only a mountaineering context. The earlier source uses Nepal as Nirmal Purja's birthplace and as the social setting for Sherpa labor, oxygen debates, and high-altitude rescue ethics. Three accounts of the August 26, 2026 flood make Nepal a disaster-response, infrastructure-capacity, and corridor-governance case: a fast surge from Tibet entered Rasuwa, damaged concentrated hydropower and transport infrastructure, dispersed bodies far downstream, and left families navigating repeated displacement, delayed identification, body-absent mourning, and inadequate aid. The newest account places those losses inside the longer history of [[GyirongPort]], where Nepal's effort to reduce transport dependence on India also creates [[StrategicBorderCorridorDependence]] and [[CumulativeRiverValleyDevelopmentRisk]].

## Key Characteristics
- Himalayan geography makes Nepal both a high-altitude achievement setting and an acute climate-hazard exposure zone.
- Rescue and recognition recur across sources, from climber rescue ethics to disaster teams searching hydropower tunnels.
- Hydropower and river-valley infrastructure can convert mountain hazards into energy, economic, and recovery shocks.
- Incomplete reconstruction can preserve exposure across disasters, as people displaced by the 2015 earthquake were struck again in riverside temporary settlements.
- Nepal's disaster burden is amplified by poverty, limited delivery capacity, and insufficient or delayed external finance.
- Foreign climbers, pilgrims, trade dependence, neighboring terrain, and cross-border rivers make Nepal's risk profile transnational rather than purely domestic.

## Evidence
- Mountain identity and climbing ethics: [[afghanistan-five-years-on-our-correspondent-visits-6a7ee547049c692a6b3ee0a9]] ties Nepal to [[NirmalPurja]], [[ProjectPossible]], Sherpa recognition, bottled oxygen, helicopters, and high-altitude rescue debates.
- Climate-hazard exposure: [[after-the-flood-nepals-ongoing-rescue-6a969d560cefab2730527078]] says a glacier and ice-rock avalanche in Tibet dammed and then released a flood surge into Nepal, with villages destroyed and large numbers dead or missing.
- Infrastructure concentration and energy shock: [[after-the-flood-nepals-ongoing-rescue-6a969d560cefab2730527078]] reports rapid generation loss, while [[niboer-teda-hongzai-xianchang-yichang-meiyou-yiti-de-zangli-cd38365e015f056efaa8b0506c123504]] says twelve plants were destroyed and connects the damage to electricity trade with India and Bangladesh.
- Repeated displacement and recovery capacity: [[niboer-teda-hongzai-xianchang-yichang-meiyou-yiti-de-zangli-cd38365e015f056efaa8b0506c123504]] describes 2015 earthquake survivors still living in temporary riverside housing, road-blocked aid, and a centralized distribution system unable to reach everyone.
- Identification and mourning: [[niboer-teda-hongzai-xianchang-yichang-meiyou-yiti-de-zangli-cd38365e015f056efaa8b0506c123504]] describes temporary burial with retained forensic samples and kush-grass figures used for funerals when bodies remained missing.
- Transnational warning and finance: [[after-the-flood-nepals-ongoing-rescue-6a969d560cefab2730527078]] recommends regional monitoring, while [[niboer-teda-hongzai-xianchang-yichang-meiyou-yiti-de-zangli-cd38365e015f056efaa8b0506c123504]] says an earlier Nepal-China warning proposal remained unimplemented and an emergency loss-and-damage request produced no timely support.
- Corridor dependence and cumulative exposure: [[niboer-teda-hongzai-weihe-jiang-kouan-jian-zai-zaihai-pinfa-de-shangu-fe064a422e32872349638d39a97d8c19]] says Gyirong gives Nepal an alternative to Indian transport routes while concentrating ports, roads, hydropower, businesses, and settlements in one hazardous valley whose access remains partly controlled from across the border.

## Qualifications
The current evidence is episode-scoped and does not provide a full country profile. The flood episodes provide different dated casualty and missing-person snapshots, while the September 16 transcript contains questionable names and inconsistent climate-fund arithmetic. The newest episode reports local perceptions of Chinese control and environmental-review gaps but does not supply complete bilateral operating records or engineering assessments. The mountaineering and disaster sources also use Nepal for different analytic purposes, so the page should not collapse elite climbing labor, pilgrimage, hydropower policy, mourning practice, corridor dependence, and national governance into one simple national trait.

## What Changed
- Added Gyirong as a trade-diversification corridor with unequal-access and disaster-concentration risks.
- Distinguished missing bilateral data sharing from proof that the sudden ice-rock collapse was predictable.
- Kept casualty and missing-person figures as dated source snapshots rather than a final ledger.
