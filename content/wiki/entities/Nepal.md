# Nepal

## Overview
Nepal is a Himalayan country that appears in the wiki through mountaineering, rescue, and climate-disaster exposure. Current evidence connects elite high-altitude climbing to broader national vulnerability: the same mountains that shape [[NirmalPurja]] and [[ProjectPossible]] also expose river valleys, hydropower tunnels, pilgrims, villages, and rescue systems to extreme events.

## Current Profile
The current profile is no longer only a mountaineering context. The earlier source uses Nepal as Nirmal Purja's birthplace and as the social setting for Sherpa labor, oxygen debates, and high-altitude rescue ethics. The new flood episode makes Nepal a disaster-response and infrastructure-capacity case: a glacier-and-rock avalanche in Tibet caused a fast downstream surge into Nepal's Rasua district, where deaths, missing people, hydropower damage, and rescue in mud-filled tunnels test state capacity and external aid.

## Key Characteristics
- Himalayan geography makes Nepal both a high-altitude achievement setting and an acute climate-hazard exposure zone.
- Rescue and recognition recur across sources, from climber rescue ethics to disaster teams searching hydropower tunnels.
- Hydropower and river-valley infrastructure can convert mountain hazards into energy, economic, and recovery shocks.
- Nepal's disaster burden is amplified by political instability, poverty, and shrinking international aid.
- Foreign climbers, pilgrims, tourists, and neighboring terrain make Nepal's risk profile transnational rather than purely domestic.

## Evidence
- Mountain identity and climbing ethics: [[afghanistan-five-years-on-our-correspondent-visits-6a7ee547049c692a6b3ee0a9]] ties Nepal to [[NirmalPurja]], [[ProjectPossible]], Sherpa recognition, bottled oxygen, helicopters, and high-altitude rescue debates.
- Climate-hazard exposure: [[after-the-flood-nepals-ongoing-rescue-6a969d560cefab2730527078]] says a glacier and ice-rock avalanche in Tibet dammed and then released a flood surge into Nepal, with villages destroyed and large numbers dead or missing.
- Infrastructure concentration and energy shock: [[after-the-flood-nepals-ongoing-rescue-6a969d560cefab2730527078]] says Nepal placed too many hydropower plants along single rivers and that about 10% of generation was knocked out within hours.
- Recovery-capacity constraint: [[after-the-flood-nepals-ongoing-rescue-6a969d560cefab2730527078]] frames Nepal as poor, politically unstable, and facing a possible $5 billion recovery burden while aid pledges and climate-finance capacity fall short.
- Transnational exposure: [[after-the-flood-nepals-ongoing-rescue-6a969d560cefab2730527078]] reports hundreds of missing foreigners, many pilgrims from India, and recommends cross-border Himalayan monitoring.

## Qualifications
The current evidence is episode-scoped and does not provide a full country profile. The disaster numbers are presented as the episode's September 1, 2026 snapshot and may change as rescue and recovery continue. The mountaineering source and flood source also use Nepal for different analytic purposes, so the page should not collapse elite climbing labor, pilgrimage, hydropower policy, and national disaster governance into one simple national trait.
