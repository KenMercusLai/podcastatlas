## Overview

朱家 / Zhu Jia is a Lu-region youxia whose current profile centers the rescue of [[JiBu|季布]]. He shelters the fugitive former Chu general, persuades [[XiahouYing|夏侯婴]] to present a loyalty-based pardon argument to [[LiuBang|刘邦]], and refuses a later relationship with Ji Bu that might turn rescue into repayment or client-building.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-170-liubang-weisha-cong-luoyang-banjia-dao-changan-1-lpioq8izitba7uhbdybp7i03djdy|Hanji 170 part 1]] introduces Zhu Jia after Liu Bang places Ji Bu under bounty and collective-punishment threat. Zhu recognizes Ji Bu despite a shaved head, chains, and slave disguise, shelters him, and uses Xiahou Ying as the channel for a political argument: service to [[XiangYu|项羽]] was loyal fulfillment of a former duty, not a reason to destroy useful defeated talent after unification.

The argument also reframes Liu Bang's interest. Killing Ji Bu for earlier battlefield hostility would look like personal resentment, advertise narrowness, and risk driving capable former enemies toward [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] or [[Nanyue|南越]]. Liu Bang's pardon and appointment then convert defeated-enemy loyalty into a virtue usable by the new order through [[FormerEnemyLoyaltyReframing|旧敌忠义重释]].

[[33-dongxifang-de-xiake-cong-chunqiu-zhanguo-dao-zhongshiji-ouzhou-lhvdbhuubif6tt1qwsxnehp1y7cl|Weird History episode 33]] places Zhu Jia within [[SimaQian|司马迁]]'s favorable youxia ideal. It adds that he sheltered fugitives, relieved poverty, dispersed his wealth, and lived frugally, then treats his refusal to meet Ji Bu after the latter's advancement as evidence that aid was not performed for fame or return. In the episode's wider comparison, Zhu is a cleaner case of reputation-backed rescue than [[GuoJieWesternHan|郭解]], whose network also carried coercive and violent risk.

## Key Characteristics

- Lu-region youxia whose authority operates through shelter, reputation, access, and persuasion rather than formal office.
- Protector of Ji Bu during Liu Bang's postwar pursuit of former enemies.
- Strategist who reframes enemy service as loyalty and pardon as an interest of the founding regime.
- Figure associated with aid to fugitives and the poor, personal frugality, and dispersal of wealth.
- Self-effacing rescuer who avoids later contact with the beneficiary and therefore limits the appearance of repayment-seeking.

## Qualifications

The two supplied sources are podcast summaries rather than a complete biography or primary-text collation. They do not independently establish Zhu Jia's wealth, full rescue record, exact words, route to Xiahou Ying, political influence, private motives, or the complete causal chain behind Liu Bang's decision. His refusal to meet Ji Bu supports a self-effacing interpretation but cannot prove the absence of every political or reputational interest. The contrast with Guo Jie identifies different risks in the bounded accounts; it does not establish that Zhu Jia's wider network was wholly non-coercive.
