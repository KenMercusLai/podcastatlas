## Overview

《寻梦》 is a [[NiKuang|倪匡]] novel in the [[WesleyNiKuang|卫斯理]] series that turns recurring dreams, bodily traces, shared memory, and an old murder into a tragic inquiry into personal continuity, betrayal, and fate.

## Current Profile

The bounded episode presents the novel as a mature middle-period work from 1980 and reports that Ni Kuang regarded it as a personal favorite. Yang Liqun repeatedly dreams that he is Xiaozhan, a man beaten by accomplices and stabbed by his beloved Cuilian. Liu Liling dreams the same events from Cuilian's viewpoint. Their pain, birthmarks, dialect behavior, mutual recognition, and Yang's discovery of a corresponding oil mill make the two accounts function as a coordinated mystery rather than isolated nightmares within the fiction.

The speculative explanation replaces a conventional immortal soul with a “memory group”: an organized set of memories may survive death and enter another brain. This lets the novel join supernatural reincarnation to an information model without using the series' familiar extraterrestrial solution. The mechanism, however, does not explain how memories are stored, selected, transmitted, or assigned, and the episode does not treat it as empirical science.

The present romance briefly promises that love can override inherited betrayal. That possibility narrows as Hu Xiecheng and Kong Yuzhen are identified with earlier accomplices and die through Yang's actions. When Liu again kills Yang and invokes an even earlier debt, repetition wins over reconciliation. The hosts accept tragedy as a valid destination but argue that the widening reincarnation network and previously undeveloped debt make the causal and emotional ending less convincing than the suspense that precedes it.

## Key Characteristics

- Reveals one past killing through two recurring dreams with opposed participant viewpoints.
- Uses pain, birthmarks, dialect, place recognition, and historical correspondence as mutually reinforcing fictional clues.
- Recasts survival after death as persistence and reassignment of organized memory rather than a separable soul.
- Places present love against inherited betrayal and resentment without allowing romance to end the cycle.
- Expands the mystery by identifying present characters with participants in the earlier crime.
- Avoids an extraterrestrial explanation while retaining a quasi-informational speculative mechanism.
- Sustains suspense more successfully than it justifies the final karmic extension, according to the hosts.

## Qualifications

This profile is based on one conversational retelling rather than direct comparison of the complete novel or its editions. The reported 1980 date, authorial preference, plot chronology, quotations, historical correspondence, place identification, dialect, birthmarks, psychological effects, and intended genre remain source-scoped. The “memory-group” model is a fictional explanation and does not establish reincarnation, consciousness upload, memory transfer, or a scientific mechanism of personal identity.
