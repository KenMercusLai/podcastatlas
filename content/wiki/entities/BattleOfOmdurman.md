# Battle of Omdurman

## Overview

The Battle of Omdurman was the 2 September 1898 clash in Sudan where an Anglo-Egyptian army under [[HerbertKitchener|Herbert Kitchener]] defeated Dervish forces with overwhelming technological superiority.

## Current Profile

For this source, Omdurman is both the climax and breakdown of [[WinstonChurchill|Winston Churchill]]'s youthful war fantasy. Riding with the 21st Lancers and firing a pistol because of his injured shoulder, he experienced the old-style cavalry spectacle he had imagined. Yet machine-powered slaughter, enemy courage, treatment of prisoners, and desecration after victory led him to describe the defeated as brave men destroyed by machinery and to say he was sickened by blood.

## Key Characteristics

- It was framed as a climactic late-Victorian imperial battle and a response to Gordon's death at Khartoum.
- It combined a cavalry charge with a much larger disparity in industrial firepower.
- It supplied Churchill with both heroic material and a direct encounter with mass killing.
- Its aftermath prompted criticism of Kitchener's conduct toward the defeated.

## Evidence

- Battlefield experience: [[240-young-churchill-soldier-of-empire-part-2-glt4710302249]] follows Churchill in the 21st Lancers' charge using a pistol rather than a sword.

## Qualifications

The account is organized around Churchill and British imperial memory. It provides little Sudanese or Egyptian perspective and does not offer a complete operational, political, casualty, or Mahdist history.

## What Changed

- Created a battle profile centered on the collision between romantic cavalry imagery and industrial imperial violence.
