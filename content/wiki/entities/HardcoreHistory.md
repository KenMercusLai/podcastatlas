# Hardcore History

## Overview

Hardcore History is [[DanCarlin]]'s long-form history podcast, represented here through a two-part crossover with [[TheRestIsHistory]].

## Current Profile

The show's production prehistory begins with a 2006 launch and a 16-minute first episode before the later development of multi-hour work from extensive reading, marked quotations, prior subject grounding, and free-form recording. Carlin describes the original premise as history for listeners who already knew the basic narrative and wanted unusual or difficult questions. [[183-historys-biggest-questions-with-dan-carlin-part-1-glt3395544177]] is the bounded source for that account.

Three *Mania for Subjugation* installments supply direct long-form examples. [[dchha72-mania-for-subjugation-ii-dchha72-mania-for-subjugation-ii]] moves from assassination and contested succession through northern campaigning to Thebes, using ancient-source conflict to keep tactical achievement, terror, and moral responsibility in the same frame. [[dchha73-mania-for-subjugation-iii-dchha73-mania-for-subjugation-iii]] continues through campaign finance, Persian institutional revision, ancient-number criticism, Homeric ritual, competing Granicus accounts, leader exposure, and massacre. [[dchha74-mania-for-subjugation-iv-dchha74-mania-for-subjugation-iv]] develops army–commander trust, siege engineering, logistics, terrain, morale, source contradiction, post-rout killing, royal propaganda, and the psychology of power. All distinguish firmer event claims from speculation, although extended analogies and counterfactual reconstruction remain central to the format.

Across both crossover parts, Hardcore History is associated with comparison rather than one compressed chronology: siege survival, twentieth-century causal hinges, lost ancient perspectives, Confederate endurance, whether history improves life, how to judge compromised figures, how contingent the Cold War was, why leaders miscalculate, and what source mediation permits historians to know.

## Key Characteristics

- Uses broad historical comparison and counterfactual questions to test present assumptions.
- Developed from short-form podcasting into multi-hour episodes supported by extensive reading and quotation gathering.
- Emphasizes contingency, uncertainty, political psychology, and the lived force of historical belief.
- Connects ancient source problems to modern propaganda and intelligence failure.
- Uses military narrative to join tactical sequence with logistics, political legitimacy, violence, and qualified psychological interpretation.
- Revises inherited civilizational stereotypes by comparing propaganda with institutional and logistical evidence.

## Qualifications

Two crossover episodes and two structured episode summaries cannot establish the format, range, or editorial method of the entire Hardcore History catalogue. Carlin's account of his own workflow is high-level, while the historical answers are conversational and often counterfactual. The Alexander episodes' careful labels do not independently validate their numbers, causal claims, analogies, or psychological possibilities.

## What Changed

- Added the accession, northern-campaign, and Theban sequence as an earlier direct long-form example.
- Made contested ancient testimony and the strategic-versus-moral judgment boundary more explicit.
- Extended the show's Alexander coverage from campaign systems back to violent consolidation of rule.
