# Hardcore History

## Overview

Hardcore History is [[DanCarlin]]'s long-form history podcast, represented here through a two-part crossover with [[TheRestIsHistory]].

## Current Profile

The show's production prehistory begins with a 2006 launch and a 16-minute first episode before the later development of multi-hour work from extensive reading, marked quotations, prior subject grounding, and free-form recording. Carlin describes the original premise as history for listeners who already knew the basic narrative and wanted unusual or difficult questions. [[183-historys-biggest-questions-with-dan-carlin-part-1-glt3395544177]] is the bounded source for that account.

Across both crossover parts, Hardcore History is associated with comparison rather than one compressed chronology: siege survival, twentieth-century causal hinges, lost ancient perspectives, Confederate endurance, whether history improves life, how to judge compromised figures, how contingent the Cold War was, why leaders miscalculate, and what source mediation permits historians to know.

## Key Characteristics

- Uses broad historical comparison and counterfactual questions to test present assumptions.
- Developed from short-form podcasting into multi-hour episodes supported by extensive reading and quotation gathering.
- Emphasizes contingency, uncertainty, political psychology, and the lived force of historical belief.
- Connects ancient source problems to modern propaganda and intelligence failure.

- Comparative method: [[183-historys-biggest-questions-with-dan-carlin-part-1-glt3395544177]] and [[184-historys-biggest-questions-with-dan-carlin-part-2-glt1976082798]] present Carlin in rapid sequences of ethical, strategic, causal, and epistemological questions.
- Historiographical position: [[184-historys-biggest-questions-with-dan-carlin-part-2-glt1976082798]] supports qualified confidence that the past is knowable without being perfectly objective.

## Qualifications

Two crossover episodes cannot establish the format, range, or editorial method of the entire Hardcore History catalogue. Carlin's account of his own workflow is high-level, while the historical answers are conversational and often counterfactual.
