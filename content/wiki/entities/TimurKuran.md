# Timur Kuran

## Overview
Timur Kuran is the economist and political scientist whose account of [[PreferenceFalsification]] structures [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-2e8bbfb73b37c052c8149a42261bc57f-58412302-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-2e8bbfb73b37c052c8149a42261bc57f-58412302|A Conspiracy of Silence]].

## Current Profile
In the current bounded evidence, Kuran connects ordinary tact and workplace conformity to authoritarian stability, religious identity, democratic party discipline, elections, polarization, humor, and sudden political change. His central distinction is between private preference and public performance: when people anticipate punishment or stigma, visible agreement may cease to be reliable evidence of genuine support.

Kuran also limits the theory. A regime's fall cannot reveal with certainty who previously believed, complied, or later rewrote their history; democratic institutions reduce some coercion without eliminating reputational and professional pressure; and concealment can preserve civility when it protects relationships rather than withholding information needed for collective judgment.

## Key Characteristics
- Defines preference falsification as socially pressured misrepresentation of wants or beliefs, including active public support for a privately rejected position.
- Uses examples across interpersonal, organizational, religious, authoritarian, and democratic settings.
- Treats public conformity as social information that can reinforce other people's conformity.
- Explains abrupt dissent cascades through heterogeneous thresholds and newly visible support.
- Preserves uncertainty about private motives, retrospective claims, and election-specific causal interpretation.

## Qualifications
This profile rests on one supplied episode summary, not Kuran's books, papers, survey instruments, complete interview transcript, or independent evaluation of the historical and electoral cases. The episode's examples make the theory legible but do not measure the prevalence of concealed preferences or prove it caused any particular election, religious shift, regime collapse, or workplace decision. Kuran's cautions about retrospective self-presentation and mixed motives remain part of the profile rather than exceptions to it.
