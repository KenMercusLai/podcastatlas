# Jack Prager

## Overview
Jack Prager was a British physician whose free pavement clinic in Kolkata developed into [[CalcuttaRescue]], an organization offering medical and social support to poor residents without distinction of religion or caste.

## Current Profile
The source presents Prager's work as pragmatic, frugal, and institution-building. He entered medicine in adulthood, worked in Bangladesh, and began treating patients beneath a Kolkata flyover in 1979 before formalizing Calcutta Rescue and securing lawful status. His model widened from clinical treatment to HIV care, education, meals, and identity documents, recognizing that health depends partly on material and administrative access.

## Key Characteristics
- Entered medical training at 34 after studying politics and economics rather than science.
- Directed his career toward poor communities in Bangladesh and Kolkata.
- Began with free pavement treatment and later built a broader service organization.
- Prioritized practical hygiene, medicine, and non-sectarian access.
- Lived simply and judged limited action preferable to paralysis before overwhelming need.

## Evidence
### Late vocational transition
- [[invidious-position-finding-jensen-huangs-successor-d443e5022186571d4b5372ea9e5287f5]] says Prager retrained as a doctor after experiencing an internal instruction to enter medicine and graduated from the Royal College of Surgeons in Dublin.

### Pragmatic service
- [[invidious-position-finding-jensen-huangs-successor-d443e5022186571d4b5372ea9e5287f5]] reports that he left work with Mother Teresa after disagreeing with the balance between prayer and practical medical and hygiene measures, then opened a free pavement clinic.

### Institution and legacy
- [[invidious-position-finding-jensen-huangs-successor-d443e5022186571d4b5372ea9e5287f5]] attributes to Prager the growth of Calcutta Rescue, West Bengal's first HIV clinic, and services extending beyond medicine to schools, meals, and identity documents.
