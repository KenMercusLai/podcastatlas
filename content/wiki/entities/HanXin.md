# 韩信 / Han Xin

韩信 / Han Xin enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-144-xiangyu-jituan-fumie-de-yuanyin-2-lut3jiatchla3pfxpkznsrs7wkzu|Hanji 144 part 2]] as one representative of the disappointed people who follow [[LiuBang|刘邦]] into [[Hanzhong|汉中]]. The episode has not yet developed his later command career; it uses his appearance to show that Xiang Yu's settlement leaves capable but unrewarded or under-placed people searching for another patron.

His source role therefore belongs to the transition from forced retreat to latent opportunity. Liu Bang's move toward Hanzhong looks like confinement under [[ThreeQinContainment|三秦压制]], but the migrating personnel field means the same retreat can absorb future talent while [[XiangYu|项羽]] is pulled east by [[TianRong|田荣]], [[PengYue|彭越]], and [[ChenYu|陈馀]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-145-junshi-qicai-hanxin-bolan-zhuangkuo-de-yisheng-1-lj7u4nluimuzapgqhl-hrfx-dqce|Hanji 145]] rewinds from that Hanzhong appearance into Han Xin's early biography. The episode presents him as a [[Huaiyin|淮阴]] native, probably born around 228 BCE in Chu territory, with family details largely absent from [[SimaQian|司马迁]]'s account in [[Shiji|《史记》]]. It notes possible old Han-state royal descent but keeps that as speculation because the source gives no settled genealogy.

The episode's Han Xin is poor, socially isolated, and difficult to place in ordinary status categories. He cannot get office, does not make a living through trade, sometimes relies on others for food, and still carries a sword in a way that makes market people resent him. The Piaomu feeding story and the later street challenge both make this shame public: he is helped, rebuked, provoked, and laughed at before his future military ability is visible.

The key interpretive addition is [[GreatCourageUnderHumiliation|忍辱大勇]]. Han Xin's choice to crawl under the aggressor rather than kill him is framed as preserving life and future scale rather than proving cowardice. The episode uses [[SuShi|苏轼]]'s 《留侯论》 language about "大勇" to turn the "胯下之辱" from an anecdote of humiliation into a test of whether a person can refuse a meaningless status fight.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-145-junshi-qicai-hanxin-bolan-zhuangkuo-de-yisheng-2-npgife56ltayww2wt4wo-fiwo-ep|Hanji 145 part 2]] extends that setup into a military-character explanation. The source reconstructs Han Xin as possibly descended from the old [[HanState|韩国]] royal line, with a father who fled after Qin's conquest and trained him in military learning; the wiki keeps this as inference rather than settled genealogy. The episode then links his later command talent to early study, admiration for [[JiangTaigong|姜太公]], and long familiarity with [[SunziBingfa|《孙子兵法》]].

That second episode also makes Han Xin's restraint more explicitly strategic. His refusal to spend his life on low-value conflict is read through [[AngerDrivenWarDecision|因怒兴师]] and the Sunzi rule that action should follow advantage rather than anger. In this frame, Han Xin's "能忍" is not social weakness but the ability to protect the future battlefield from the present insult.
