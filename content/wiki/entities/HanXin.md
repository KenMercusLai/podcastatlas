# 韩信 / Han Xin

韩信 / Han Xin enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-144-xiangyu-jituan-fumie-de-yuanyin-2-lut3jiatchla3pfxpkznsrs7wkzu|Hanji 144 part 2]] as one representative of the disappointed people who follow [[LiuBang|刘邦]] into [[Hanzhong|汉中]]. The episode has not yet developed his later command career; it uses his appearance to show that Xiang Yu's settlement leaves capable but unrewarded or under-placed people searching for another patron.

His source role therefore belongs to the transition from forced retreat to latent opportunity. Liu Bang's move toward Hanzhong looks like confinement under [[ThreeQinContainment|三秦压制]], but the migrating personnel field means the same retreat can absorb future talent while [[XiangYu|项羽]] is pulled east by [[TianRong|田荣]], [[PengYue|彭越]], and [[ChenYu|陈馀]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-145-junshi-qicai-hanxin-bolan-zhuangkuo-de-yisheng-1-lj7u4nluimuzapgqhl-hrfx-dqce|Hanji 145]] rewinds from that Hanzhong appearance into Han Xin's early biography. The episode presents him as a [[Huaiyin|淮阴]] native, probably born around 228 BCE in Chu territory, with family details largely absent from [[SimaQian|司马迁]]'s account in [[Shiji|《史记》]]. It notes possible old Han-state royal descent but keeps that as speculation because the source gives no settled genealogy.

The episode's Han Xin is poor, socially isolated, and difficult to place in ordinary status categories. He cannot get office, does not make a living through trade, sometimes relies on others for food, and still carries a sword in a way that makes market people resent him. The Piaomu feeding story and the later street challenge both make this shame public: he is helped, rebuked, provoked, and laughed at before his future military ability is visible.

The key interpretive addition is [[GreatCourageUnderHumiliation|忍辱大勇]]. Han Xin's choice to crawl under the aggressor rather than kill him is framed as preserving life and future scale rather than proving cowardice. The episode uses [[SuShi|苏轼]]'s 《留侯论》 language about "大勇" to turn the "胯下之辱" from an anecdote of humiliation into a test of whether a person can refuse a meaningless status fight.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-145-junshi-qicai-hanxin-bolan-zhuangkuo-de-yisheng-2-npgife56ltayww2wt4wo-fiwo-ep|Hanji 145 part 2]] extends that setup into a military-character explanation. The source reconstructs Han Xin as possibly descended from the old [[HanState|韩国]] royal line, with a father who fled after Qin's conquest and trained him in military learning; the wiki keeps this as inference rather than settled genealogy. The episode then links his later command talent to early study, admiration for [[JiangTaigong|姜太公]], and long familiarity with [[SunziBingfa|《孙子兵法》]].

That second episode also makes Han Xin's restraint more explicitly strategic. His refusal to spend his life on low-value conflict is read through [[AngerDrivenWarDecision|因怒兴师]] and the Sunzi rule that action should follow advantage rather than anger. In this frame, Han Xin's "能忍" is not social weakness but the ability to protect the future battlefield from the present insult.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-146-qiannian-jingdian-xiaohe-yuexia-zhui-hanxin-lq0oc3vqr-tsr0r-iwztifxeezl|Hanji 146]] turns that latent preparation into a recognition and retention crisis. Han Xin first joins [[XiangLiang|项梁]] with military learning already in hand, survives [[Dingtao|定陶]], serves [[XiangYu|项羽]] only as a langzhong, and repeatedly fails to have his advice adopted. The source treats possible objections to Xiang Yu's [[Xianyang|咸阳]] violence and partition choices as host inference, not settled record.

After following [[LiuBang|刘邦]] into [[Hanzhong|汉中]], Han Xin remains underused as a minor or mid-level officer and nearly dies under military law before [[XiahouYing|夏侯婴]] notices him. The grain-supply commandant post then places him near [[XiaoHe|萧何]], whose stronger judgment converts Han Xin from a replaceable officer into a "国士无双" figure. When Han Xin flees [[Nanzheng|南郑]], Xiao He's pursuit and insistence on full appointment ceremony make Han Xin the source case for [[RitualizedCommandAppointment|仪式化拜将留才]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-147-pimei-zhugeliang-longzhongdui-de-hanzhongdui-lqucctzhipgtcvtjlixvj5qbdeai|Hanji 147]] gives the immediate payoff of that appointment. Han Xin opens by making Liu Bang admit that he is weaker than Xiang Yu in visible force, then turns the comparison around: Xiang Yu's bravery lacks delegation, his kindness lacks reward execution, and his settlement choices have alienated [[GuanzhongRegion|关中]]. This becomes [[HanzhongDui|汉中对]], a plan to use Liu Bang's prior [[YuefaSanzhang|约法三章]] and the weakness of [[ThreeQinContainment|三秦压制]] to take the Three Qin before reopening the eastward contest.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-148-1-hanxin-yupan-le-zhanghan-de-yupan-lodhpuxpossjno87gorjkfrtitbe|Hanji 148-1]] turns that plan into execution. The episode gives Han Xin two connected achievements: he first pushes Liu Bang's army through [[HanInheritsQinSystem|汉承秦制式军制改造]] by "申军法," then uses [[LayeredFeintCentralBreakthrough|多路佯动中央突破]] against [[ZhangHanQin|章邯]]. In this reading, Han Xin's genius lies in joining institutional preparation, subordinate deployment through [[GuanYing|灌婴]], [[FanKuai|樊哙]], and [[CaoShen|曹参]], and the central strike through [[ChenCang|陈仓]] rather than only in a single surprise route.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-148-2-peng-le-wangling-muqin-xiangyu-sharen-zhuxin-ljyv8bow4rj1l5ths3znx2evgvtm|Hanji 148-2]] uses that campaign as the new starting condition rather than repeating the tactical detail. Han Xin's Guanzhong success has changed [[LiuBang|刘邦]]'s perceived strength, allowing Liu Bang to contact [[WangLingHanMinister|王陵]] and attempt to recover his family while [[XiangYu|项羽]] is pulled toward wider settlement crises.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-149-xichu-bawang-xiangyu-weihe-shijun-2-lpohbb3prsgvokpjipfd7i1ntiwn|Hanji 149 part 2]] uses Han Xin mainly as Liu Bang's military rear anchor after Guanzhong recovery, while explicitly distinguishing the general from [[HanWangXin|韩王信]], the old Han royal descendant whom Liu Bang installs as king of Han.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-150-chenping-weihe-bei-cheng-zhiwei-chenggong-zhengzhijia-yangban-4-lhhprrkjcuzijgndi2uuazaalern|Hanji 150 part 4]] uses Han Xin as a contrast case for [[ChenPing|陈平]]. The host presents Han Xin as brilliant in war but weak in court-survival intelligence, so his fate becomes the negative comparison for Chen Ping's [[PoliticalSurvivalFirst|安全第一政治生存]] and [[WorldlyPoliticalCultivation|入世政治修行]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-152-xiangyu-3wanren-ruhe-wansheng-liubang-56wan-dajun-1-lrtpxlak0dlyej8bozmmhdtfczno|Hanji 152 part 1]] returns to Han Xin from the [[PengchengBattle|彭城之战]] angle. The episode argues that Liu Bang's largest Pengcheng mistake was not putting Han Xin in command of the main army while Han Xin stayed in [[GuanzhongRegion|关中]] continuing pressure on [[ZhangHanQin|章邯]]. The later "多多益善" exchange is therefore used to make Han Xin not only a clever campaign planner but a rare commander whose ability fits [[AncientLargeArmyCommandLimits|古代大兵团指挥极限]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-153-xiayi-huace-canbai-hou-liubang-ruhe-qisi-huisheng-1-lgvjuxuskyyfmyeeqm9y8aeln-hu|Hanji 153]] turns that negative lesson into a positive assignment. In [[ZhangLiang|张良]]'s [[XiayiPlan|下邑画策]], Han Xin is the one Liu Bang-side general who can be entrusted with major independent work. The point is not only that Han Xin was absent at Pengcheng, but that Liu Bang's recovery now depends on letting him open a separate front rather than keeping all decisive force under Liu Bang's own command.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-153-xiayi-huace-canbai-hou-liubang-ruhe-qisi-huisheng-2-ll-rvx07pyw-qg1jofkjro-qfllu|Hanji 153 part 2]] gives that recovery a more immediate defensive role. After news of Pengcheng reaches [[GuanzhongRegion|关中]], [[XiaoHe|萧何]] raises emergency troops and has Han Xin lead them east. Han Xin defeats Chu pursuers south of [[YingyangWarringStates|荥阳]], allowing the Han side to stabilize the emerging [[XingyangStandoff|荥阳相持]] rather than continuing the rout.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-155-1-lishi-jizai-shenme-qingkuang-hui-fasheng-ren-chi-ren-canju-lrwz-prnxd8hzchagyrbywkux7lh|Hanji 155-1]] invokes Han Xin mainly as the setup for a tactical question: how he used "声东击西" against [[WeiBao|魏豹]]. The available transcript names the frame but does not yet narrate the full West Wei campaign, so this page records it as a source-scoped bridge back to [[LayeredFeintCentralBreakthrough|多路佯动中央突破]] and [[BattlefieldInformationControl|战场信息控制]] rather than as a new detailed battle account.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-155-2-zhuan-gei-ni-piqi-da-de-pengyou-weiwang-panhan-jing-shi-liubang-ai-maren-ls5nvunmhakvefd2oxn4zdyoob4l|Hanji 155-2]] supplies that campaign detail. Han Xin first asks whether Wei has entrusted command to [[ZhouShu|周叔]], then exploits Wei Bao's actual deployment by staging a visible crossing at [[LinjinWarringStates|临晋]] / [[PubanWarringStates|蒲阪]] and secretly crossing at [[XiayangChuHan|下阳]]. The resulting strike on [[Anyi|安邑]] captures Wei Bao and turns the episode into a specific [[RiverCrossingDeception|渡河欺敌]] case.

The same source then expands Han Xin's independent-command role beyond Wei. He requests thirty thousand troops to take [[ZhaoState|赵国]], [[YanState|燕国]], and [[QiState|齐国]] before turning south against Chu's grain route, while [[LiuBang|刘邦]] sends [[ZhangEr|张耳]] to assist but repeatedly draws elite troops away for [[XingyangStandoff|荥阳相持]]. Han Xin still defeats Dai around [[EyuBattle|阏与]] and takes [[Jinyang|晋阳]], but the episode treats the unfinished grain-route cutoff as a result of Liu Bang's simultaneous trust and fear.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-156-bingxian-hanxin-de-fengshen-zhizhan-1-ltkz7bwqgkcqessdfbn9unf5e05l|Hanji 156 part 1]] moves that northern campaign into [[JingxingPass|井陉口]]. Han Xin and [[ZhangEr|张耳]] now face [[ChenYu|陈馀]] and [[ZhaoXie|赵歇]] across a narrow pass where Han's improvised army, long supply route, and stretched column create exactly the weakness [[LiZuoChe|李左车]] wants to exploit. The source shows Han Xin halting thirty li from the pass mouth, feeding the troops, then moving at midnight with flags lowered and drums silent, making the campaign a new [[BattlefieldInformationControl|战场信息控制]] case before the full "背水一战" result is narrated.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-156-bingxian-hanxin-de-fengshen-zhizhan-2-li2c9d04jpepy4t0mquwjt-bli4|Hanji 156 part 2]] completes that result as [[BackwaterBattle|背水一战]]. Han Xin confirms through spies that Chen Yu has rejected Li Zuoche's plan, hides two thousand red-flag cavalry near the pass exit, forms a forward force with water at its back, then uses flags, drums, and a feigned defeat with Zhang Er to pull Zhao out of camp. The victory is presented as a chain of [[BattlefieldInformationControl|战场信息控制]], forced fighting resolve, and enemy morale collapse rather than as a simple lucky gamble.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-157-hanxin-beishuiyizhan-bingfa-jiedu-1-loiz8ge-a39tmigajool2spzri5i|Hanji 157 part 1]] turns that completed result into explicit doctrine. Han Xin's explanation to his officers draws on [[SunziBingfa|《孙子兵法》]]'s death-ground logic, but the episode stresses that he could use it only because he first knew his own soldiers were undertrained, used spies to confirm Chen Yu's rejected plan and the absence of a pass ambush, and paired the water-backed array with hidden cavalry. The result extends Han Xin from tactical executor to a case of [[DeathGroundTacticalPreconditions|死地战术前提]]: morale pressure works because intelligence and surprise have already shaped the field.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-157-hanxin-beishuiyizhan-bingfa-jiedu-2-lowgtomc-sfmdnshzlscm26d-d3|Hanji 157 part 2]] shifts Han Xin's Jingxing result from battlefield doctrine into post-victory strategy. After ordering [[LiZuoChe|李左车]] spared and treating him with teacher-like courtesy, Han Xin asks how to move against [[YanState|燕国]] and [[QiState|齐国]]. He accepts the advice to rest the exhausted army, pacify Zhao's people, display northward pressure, and send envoys, turning victory into [[VictoryDeterrenceDiplomacy|胜势威慑外交]] rather than immediate assault. The same source adds the political consolidation step: Han Xin reports to [[LiuBang|刘邦]] and requests [[ZhangEr|张耳]] as Zhao king to hold the new territory.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-159-2-zhangliang-shuole-sha-jiuxia-hanchao-lvs9elvrjp-ypz63w4czpuhbsfb|Hanji 159-2]] reuses Han Xin's Jingxing victory as a strategy-transfer warning. The episode contrasts Han Xin's [[BackwaterBattle|背水一战]] with Liu Bang's river-backed disaster at the [[PengchengBattle|彭城之战]]: Han Xin's soldiers fight because no retreat, prior intelligence, and camp-seizure shock align, while Liu Bang's troops at Pengcheng are complacent and Xiang Yu's army is fighting with emergency fury.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-162-1-ta-ti-liubang-qu-si-rujin-chengle-zhengzhou-de-shouhushen-li3su2zlmbm1ift9nibujgtcrxwm|Hanji 162-1]] keeps Han Xin mostly offstage but makes his northern consolidation one reason for [[YuanShengChuHan|袁先生 / 袁生]]'s advice. If [[LiuBang|刘邦]] lures [[XiangYu|项羽]] south and avoids battle, the [[XingyangStandoff|荥阳相持]] and [[Chenggao|成皋]] front gain rest while Han Xin gets time to digest Zhao and extend pressure toward [[YanState|燕国]] and [[QiState|齐国]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-162-2-liubang-ruhe-shoushi-wei-gao-quan-zhong-de-xiashu-lqvye2zmqhu3otk0uwu7lakf2prz|Hanji 162-2]] turns that offstage consolidation into a command-boundary scene. After Liu Bang loses Chenggao again, he arrives at Han Xin and [[ZhangEr|张耳]]'s camp in Zhao before dawn, takes their seals and military tokens, and uses Han Xin's seal to summon the army. Han Xin remains crucial, but his role changes: Liu Bang names him chancellor and sends him to gather unused Zhao manpower for the [[QiState|齐国]] campaign, making him the central case for [[CommandAuthorityRecapture|统帅权再收束]] rather than simple trust or simple purge.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-162-3-zuipao-wang-lishiqi-budong-yibing-naxia-70-yu-cheng-lq88alghxqxxcddk4zzqodkezipx|Hanji 162-3]] puts that Qi assignment into conflict with [[LiShiqi|郦食其]]'s envoy success. Han Xin hears that Li Shiqi has persuaded [[TianGuangQiKing|田广]] and initially prepares to stop, but [[KuaiChe|蒯彻 / 蒯通]] argues that Han Xin's original order was to attack Qi and that Liu Bang has not sent a stop order. Kuai Che then turns the issue into merit competition: if Li Shiqi can gain more than seventy cities by speech, Han Xin's hard-won military record risks looking smaller. Han Xin accepts the argument and attacks Qi secretly, making him no longer just the executor of [[VictoryDeterrenceDiplomacy|胜势威慑外交]] but also the commander whose pursuit of merit can destroy a successful diplomatic outcome.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-163-liubang-shouxia-moushi-ruhe-bei-hanxin-neijuan-cansi-lubinu6hoihu59knmlixmaryhnq7|Hanji 163]] records that threat becoming action. Han Xin attacks Qi in the tenth month of 203 BCE after Qi has accepted Li Shiqi's persuasion, quickly breaks the relaxed defenses near Linzi, and gains the campaign advantage that Kuai Che urged him to seek. The cost is that Li Shiqi is executed by Qi, making Han Xin's success a concrete case of [[CommandSynchronizationFailure|军令同步失灵]] rather than only aggressive battlefield initiative.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-164-1-liubang-geqiang-chaofeng-qi-de-xiangyu-shejian-touxi-lhs6miq2aa3avnny-prxtvkfxgvy|Hanji 164-1]] shows the strategic payoff of that same Qi attack. While [[LiuBang|刘邦]] and [[XiangYu|项羽]] are fixed at [[GuangwuChuHan|广武]], Han Xin's eastern victories over Wei, Zhao, Yan, and Qi change the broader balance. After Qi asks Chu for help, [[LongJuChuGeneral|龙且]] arrives to support [[TianGuangQiKing|田广]], and Han Xin begins another [[BattlefieldInformationControl|战场信息控制]] sequence by crossing to attack and then feigning defeat so Long Ju's contempt can pull him forward.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-164-2-xiangyu-touhao-dajiang-zenme-shu-gei-hanxin-de-lhe-lmb1zrzipbongg02gmnmb4l|Hanji 164-2]] completes the same Qi-front sequence as [[WeishuiBattle|潍水之战]]. Han Xin dams the upstream Weishui with sand-filled cloth bags, crosses to attack, pretends to lose, and lets Long Ju pursue into the river channel before releasing the water. The result extends Han Xin's page from Jingxing and West Wei deception into a hydraulic river trap: he wins because he understands Long Ju's contempt, controls what the enemy thinks he sees, and times terrain as part of command.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-165-1-liubang-zheju-hua-chadian-nongdiu-dahan-jiangshan-llxgisomsdo8qjeecgdg6frri-xq|Hanji 165-1]] shows the political consequence of that victory. Han Xin asks [[LiuBang|刘邦]] to let him act as king of [[QiState|齐国]], arguing that Qi is unstable and close to Chu. Liu Bang, pushed by [[ZhangLiang|张良]] and [[ChenPing|陈平]], grants him real Qi kingship instead, which turns Han Xin from delegated commander into a possible third pole in the Chu-Han field.

The same source makes Han Xin's political limitation explicit. [[WuShe|武涉]] argues for [[TripartiteBalanceStrategy|三分天下式制衡]] by warning that Liu Bang will eventually target Han Xin once [[XiangYu|项羽]] is gone, but Han Xin refuses because Xiang Yu ignored him while Liu Bang trusted, fed, clothed, and promoted him. The host reads the answer as gratitude rather than mature power calculation, setting up [[KuaiChe|蒯彻 / 蒯通]]'s next attempt to warn him.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-165-2-hanxin-weihe-zhisi-ye-bu-beipan-liubang-lrgtvbdyc9cwbfhaix1aabsmqhhb|Hanji 165-2]] completes that warning. Kuai Che tells Han Xin that his choice now determines Chu-Han victory and that his safest route is to keep Qi, make Zhao and Yan follow, and form a three-way order rather than deliver uncontested victory to either side. Han Xin does not answer by denying the strategic danger; he answers by invoking Liu Bang's earlier chariot, clothing, food, and status as a debt that makes betrayal into见利忘义.

The source then makes Han Xin's dilemma sharper through precedent. Kuai Che cites [[ZhangEr|张耳]] and [[ChenYu|陈馀]] as proof that intimate friendship can become lethal hostility, and [[WenZhongYue|文种]] under [[Goujian|越王勾践]] as proof that completed victory can destroy an overmighty minister. Han Xin is moved and disturbed but delays his answer, leaving the page's current endpoint as hesitation between [[RetainerReciprocityEthic|士为知己者死]] and [[PowerExitTrap|权力退场困境]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-166-jiran-xiangyu-bujiang-wude-kan-liubang-ruhe-fuchou-ltwwbn9rzeqzzimmxxfihb9-drj1|Hanji 166]] closes the refusal. Han Xin remains unwilling to betray Liu Bang and trusts that his own merit will preserve his expected position, so Kuai Che has no workable path and withdraws by pretending madness and becoming a diviner. The host also argues that the Wu She / Kuai Che persuasion sequence likely belongs before the acting-Qi-king request, which this page preserves as source-critical reconstruction rather than settled chronology.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-167-jianzheng-xiangyu-zuizhong-zhizhan-1-lm6nwhjjuguhrmwzoz0sqawgxwuv|Hanji 167]] shows that Han Xin's refusal to betray Liu Bang does not mean unconditional obedience without concrete reward. He initially fails to join the final pursuit, and Zhang Liang argues that Liu Bang must promise the Chen-to-sea territory before Han Xin will fight for the final result. The episode therefore carries Han Xin from third-pole refusal into territorial-stake mobilization on the road to Gaixia.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-167-jianzheng-xiangyu-zuizhong-zhizhan-2-li14pizmfrzhpg8m5bmxdx8ibw7p|Hanji 167 part 2]] gives Han Xin the direct confrontation with [[XiangYu|项羽]] that the source treats as the missing peak of his military career. With [[LiuBang|刘邦]] yielding coalition command, Han Xin arranges the sixty-ten-thousand army into three lines: his own first line with [[KongXi|孔熙]] and [[ChenHe|陈贺]] on the wings, Liu Bang's second line, and a third line under [[ZhouBo|周勃]] and [[ChaiWu|柴武]]. His controlled retreat turns Xiang Yu's breakthrough into overextension, making Gaixia the clearest source case for [[LayeredDepthDefense|纵深分层防御]] and a positive answer to [[AncientLargeArmyCommandLimits|古代大兵团指挥极限]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-169-pandian-liubang-dengjiqian-buweirenzhi-de-gushi-1-liwkaygjtyafxxpdwzq9pdhuzroi|Hanji 169]] gives the immediate post-victory turn that earlier warning pages had anticipated. After Gaixia and Xiang Yu's death, Liu Bang goes directly into Han Xin's camp at [[Dingtao|定陶]] and removes his military command, repeating the [[CommandAuthorityRecapture|统帅权再收束]] pattern under far more final conditions. Liu Bang then changes Han Xin from Qi king to Chu king, with territory north of the Huai River and capital at [[Xiapi|下邳]], so the episode converts Han Xin's victory-making merit into both reward and containment inside the new [[WesternHanDynasty|西汉]] order.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-169-pandian-liubang-dengjiqian-buweirenzhi-de-gushi-2-lqq8qhramfzii5o5icesd-uagwkc|Hanji 169 part 2]] deepens the Chu kingship reading. The stated reason is that Han Xin knows Chu customs, but the episode treats the placement as [[HostileTerritoryRoyalContainment|敌意封国安置]] because Chu people may resent the commander who helped force [[XiangYu|项羽]]'s death. Han Xin's reward to the old woman who fed him and appointment of the man who humiliated him as zhongwei are therefore read not only as magnanimity, but as a way to build personally loyal security inside a politically hostile kingdom.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-171-zhangliang-jiao-ni-ruhe-mingli-shuangshou-1-lptgy7wxwsve8ljt79asetubin41|Hanji 171 part 1]] uses Han Xin as the negative comparison to [[ZhangLiang|张良]]'s post-founding exit. The host argues that Han Xin's danger is not mainly a moral failure to know when to stop; it is the structural danger created when a young, brilliant, admired commander has more battlefield capacity than [[LiuBang|刘邦]] and remains difficult for the future [[LuZhi|吕雉]] / [[LiuYing|刘盈]] succession to control. The episode therefore reinforces Han Xin's [[PowerExitTrap|权力退场困境]] while warning against turning his later destruction into simple hindsight blame.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-171-zhangliang-jiao-ni-ruhe-mingli-shuangshou-2-lm1iff0cadjapwf2mggwyo8wcvxn|Hanji 171 part 2]] makes that danger concrete through [[ZhongliMo|钟离眜]]. Han Xin shelters the former Chu general after Xiang Yu's death, which the host reads as both personal friendship and a pragmatic attempt to use old Chu relationships in a hostile Chu kingdom. From Liu Bang's side, however, the same act makes Han Xin look like a young, brilliant, popular king with independent military memory and Chu-side networks, exactly the kind of postwar figure who is hard to leave alone.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-172-liubang-yangyou-yunmengze-zhiqin-hanxin-lrpkye8y6ibqdqs9pz7artk-6chv|Hanji 172]] turns that suspicion into demotion. Once someone reports Han Xin for rebellion, [[LiuBang|刘邦]] accepts [[ChenPing|陈平]]'s [[RitualTourSummonsEntrapment|ritual-tour summons trap]] instead of the generals' direct-attack advice. Han Xin tries to solve the [[ZhongliMo|钟离眜]] problem by presenting Zhongli Mo's head at [[ChenCountyChuHan|陈县]], but the act removes one of his remaining deterrents and lets Liu Bang arrest him without testing Han Xin's battlefield superiority. The episode's endpoint is no longer hostile Chu kingship but loss of kingship itself: Han Xin is spared from death and reduced to a marquis title associated with [[Huaiyin|淮阴]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-173-hanxin-taoguo-kuaxia-zhiru-mei-duoguo-niaojin-gongcang-1-ljowpzxo9u0evorhzbdyi2o0shlx|Hanji 173]] follows Han Xin inside [[ChangAnHanCapital|长安]] after that demotion. The source stresses his isolation: Zhongli Mo is dead, [[LiZuoChe|李左车]] has left, [[XiaoHe|萧何]] is too prudent to approach freely, and Han Xin can only pass time compiling military writings and visiting old acquaintances. His visit to [[FanKuai|樊哙]] produces the famous "生乃与哙等为伍" line, but the episode reads the scene in two layers. It records Han Xin's wounded pride, while also treating the insult as possible [[PublicDisavowalSelfProtection|公开切割式自保]] because visible friendship with Fan Kuai, a Pei/Feng veteran with [[LuZhi|吕雉]] ties, could make Liu Bang suspect a new dangerous combination.

The same source reopens the earlier [[TripartiteBalanceStrategy|三分天下式制衡]] question. The host argues that Han Xin had at least two major opportunities to break with Liu Bang, and that after conquering [[QiState|齐国]] he held the leverage to decide whether Han or Chu would win. Han Xin had criticized [[XiangYu|项羽]] for "妇人之仁" and indecision over rewards, but Hanji 173 turns that critique back on Han Xin: decisive battlefield judgment did not become decisive political self-preservation.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-174-liubang-weihe-dafeng-zijide-chouren-1-lnzzvpieqtgatmvgogz3msyhs60u|Hanji 174]] keeps Han Xin mostly offstage but makes his former royal base central. After his reduction to Huaiyin marquis, Liu Bang splits the old Chu kingdom: the Huai River southeast becomes [[JingKingdomEarlyHan|荆国]] for [[LiuJia|刘贾]], while Xue, Donghai, and Pengcheng form [[ChuKingdomEarlyHan|楚国]] for [[LiuJiao|刘交]]. The move shows that Han Xin's title loss is followed by territorial dismantling, not merely personal demotion.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-182-weishenme-shuo-chengye-xiaohe-baiye-xiaohe-1-lhe3h27wdbju3cgsvukdvgkri1a9|Hanji 182]] reopens Han Xin after that post-demotion isolation and gives the first part of the "成也萧何败也萧何" branch. Before [[ChenXi|陈豨]] leaves for his frontier command, Han Xin privately proposes an inside-outside rebellion: Chen Xi should wait until repeated reports make [[LiuBang|刘邦]] personally campaign, while Han Xin can respond from the capital. This does not erase the earlier Chu-Han-period refusal in Hanji 165-166; it marks a later political stage after Han Xin has lost kingship, command, and usable outside deterrents.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-182-weishenme-shuo-chengye-xiaohe-baiye-xiaohe-2-lqk-jifupm9lbghfzaf09igfufgb|Hanji 182 part 2]] completes that branch. The source says Han Xin uses illness to avoid Liu Bang's campaign, waits for Chen Xi's reply, and plans to release convicts and light-crime slaves for an attack on [[LuZhi|吕雉]] and the crown prince. A retainer's younger brother reports the plot, after which Lu Zhi and [[XiaoHe|萧何]] use false victory news and court congratulations to draw Han Xin into [[ChanglePalace|长乐宫]], where he is bound, killed, and exterminated by three clans.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-182-weishenme-shuo-chengye-xiaohe-baiye-xiaohe-3-lq9fif0ow90zsyrmovhwxb7-qicr|Hanji 182 part 3]] returns to the same death as interpretation. It repeats the Changle Palace trap but adds the source-scoped "three not die" lore around Liu Bang's supposed promise and a bamboo-killing method, while keeping the killing inside [[CelebratorySummonsEntrapment|报捷祝贺式诱捕]] rather than making it a separate mechanism. The more important addition is retrospective: Han Xin regrets not listening to [[KuaiChe|蒯彻 / 蒯通]], so the earlier [[TripartiteBalanceStrategy|三分天下式制衡]] warning is vindicated only after leverage, command, and allies are gone.

The episode also sharpens the page's political-ability contrast. Han Xin remains a military genius and a figure of [[GreatCourageUnderHumiliation|忍辱大勇]], but the host reads his end as a failure to study politics, history, motive, and changing human incentives. In this source, trusting [[LiuBang|刘邦]] and Xiao He after class position and succession danger have changed becomes part of the same [[PowerExitTrap|权力退场困境]] as refusing Kuai Che at peak leverage and allegedly rebelling only after the useful moment had passed.
