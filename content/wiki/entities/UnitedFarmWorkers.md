# United Farm Workers

## Overview

The United Farm Workers is a U.S. labour organization co-founded by [[DoloresHuerta]] and [[CesarChavez]]. The source presents it as a vehicle for major farm-worker gains and as an institution whose public history must also confront gendered credit, sexism, alleged sexual violence by its celebrated leader, and pressure to protect movement reputation.

## Current Profile

The movement joined Mexican and Filipino farm workers in a 1965 vineyard strike near Delano and adapted civil-rights tactics, especially consumer boycotts, to agricultural labour. By 1970, the source says growers had agreed to wage increases and union recognition, and California later expanded unionization protection, health coverage, and other benefits.

Its achievements were collective and organizational. Huerta's negotiation and legislative work mattered alongside worker participation, while Chavez became the dominant public symbol. That unequal attribution is part of the union's history, not merely a later publicity problem.

The source also describes pervasive sexism and episode-reported allegations of sexual violence by Chavez. The union therefore grounds [[MovementAchievementAbuseAccountability]]: preserving worker gains and tactical innovation requires neither leader sanctification nor silence about institutional harm.

## Key Characteristics

- Organized farm workers facing long hours, poor sanitation, weak benefits, and limited remedies against abusive employers.
- Joined worker organizing, strikes, negotiation, legislation, and consumer boycotts.
- Helped win wage, recognition, health, and legal-protection gains.
- Relied on [[DoloresHuerta]]'s often underrecognized negotiating and organizational labour.
- Built a public identity around [[CesarChavez]] that the source says obscured sexism and serious abuse allegations.

## Qualifications

The page rests on one structured episode summary rather than union archives, contracts, legislation, worker oral histories, a transcript, or a comprehensive institutional history. Dates, participant numbers, causal attribution, benefit scope, internal culture, and allegations remain source-scoped. The union's gains should not be reduced to Chavez, and its harms should not be assigned to every worker or organizer.

## Relationships

- [[DoloresHuerta]] - co-founder, negotiator, and organizer whose underrecognized work is central to the union's achievements.
- [[CesarChavez]] - co-founder and public leader whose achievements and alleged abuse shape the union's contested legacy.
- [[MovementAchievementAbuseAccountability]] - framework for preserving collective gains while confronting leadership and institutional harm.
