# John Dickey

## Overview

John Dickey is a historian and the author of *The Craft: How the Freemasons Made the Modern World*, presented in this source as a scholar of both Italian organized crime and [[Freemasonry]].

## Current Profile

Dickey approaches Freemasonry between apologetic and conspiratorial accounts. He treats it as a varied historical institution whose ritual, secrecy, fraternity, welfare, politics, and network form could support civic belonging or controlled equality while also enabling exclusion, mythmaking, imperial service, and occasional corrupt capture.

## Key Characteristics

- His route into Masonic history came through research on the mafia and a provocative comparison between criminal organization and Freemasonry.
- He rejects both the total hidden-control story and the claim that Freemasonry is only misunderstood charity.
- He emphasizes ritual and the sacred treatment of death rather than reducing lodges to ordinary sociability.
- He uses comparative cases across Europe, the United States, empire, authoritarian repression, P2, and Prince Hall Masonry.
