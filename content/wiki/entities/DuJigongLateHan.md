## Overview

杜季贡 / Du Jigong is the Hanji 789 source case for a Han civilian pushed into Qiang-aligned rebellion by coercive frontier relocation. His profile matters less as a full biography than as evidence that state protection can create enemy manpower when it destroys the protected population's livelihood.

## Current Profile

Hanji 789 places Du Jigong in the 116-117 CE Qiang crisis under the Deng regency. The court tries to move frontier civilians inland so rebels cannot use them, but the relocation is implemented with crop destruction, burned housing, hunger, and forced movement. Du Jigong, his relatives, and other local people join Qiang forces, seize Shanggui, and then flee toward the Qiang leader Dianling after defeat.

Du remains militarily consequential. When Dianling dies and [[LinChangQiang|Lin Chang]] succeeds, Du operates in the same Qiang-side field. [[SimaJunLateHan|Sima Jun]] briefly defeats Du and takes Dingxi, but a subordinate disobeys the order to cut enemy grain and is ambushed, allowing Du to retake the city. Later, [[RenShangLateHan|Ren Shang]] uses the surrendered Qiang Yu Gui to assassinate Du, treating proxy killing as a way to close a problem that direct operations had not settled.

## Key Characteristics

- Han civilian turned rebel by coercive relocation rather than presented as an originally external enemy.
- Military organizer around Shanggui and Dingxi after joining Qiang forces.
- Central evidence for [[CoerciveFrontierRelocationBackfire|coercive frontier relocation backfire]].
- Survives initial defeats by attaching to the wider Qiang command field around Dianling and Lin Chang.
- Removed through proxy assassination after conventional operations prove costly and incomplete.
