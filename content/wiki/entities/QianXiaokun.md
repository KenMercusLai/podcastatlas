# 钱小坤 / Qian Xiaokun

## Overview
钱小坤 appears in [[ep291-meikong-shengbing-de-women-ruhe-guoshang-ruxian-youhao-de-shenghuo-lmzevsk2atc-6psjqeogg-qhuyq2|EP291]] as a furniture designer and male breast-cancer survivor.

## Current Profile
He uses his experience of noticing a lump, receiving an invasive hormone-receptor-positive diagnosis, entering a predominantly female breast ward, undergoing surgery and rehabilitation, and returning to work to make male breast cancer more visible. His account joins symptom recognition to physical recovery and ordinary social participation without presenting work or optimism as treatment.

## Key Characteristics
- Makes the nonzero possibility of male breast cancer concrete through lived experience.
- Describes self-noticing as an entry to clinical assessment rather than self-diagnosis.
- Discusses gendered awkwardness in a breast-care environment designed mainly around women.
- Treats rehabilitation and social contact as parts of recovery while respecting medical limits.

## Evidence
- Recognition and treatment: [[ep291-meikong-shengbing-de-women-ruhe-guoshang-ruxian-youhao-de-shenghuo-lmzevsk2atc-6psjqeogg-qhuyq2]] describes a self-noticed lump, biopsy, invasive hormone-receptor-positive diagnosis, surgery, and upper-limb rehabilitation.
- Gendered care setting: [[ep291-meikong-shengbing-de-women-ruhe-guoshang-ruxian-youhao-de-shenghuo-lmzevsk2atc-6psjqeogg-qhuyq2]] records his discomfort as a man in a predominantly female ward and the encouragement he drew from other patients.
- Recovery: [[ep291-meikong-shengbing-de-women-ruhe-guoshang-ruxian-youhao-de-shenghuo-lmzevsk2atc-6psjqeogg-qhuyq2]] connects return to work and social contact with psychological and functional recovery.

## Qualifications
This is one survivor's retrospective account, not evidence about male breast-cancer prevalence, biology, prognosis, or the correct rehabilitation and return-to-work schedule for another patient.

## What Changed
- Created a profile distinguishing male breast-cancer visibility from sex-based risk overgeneralization.
