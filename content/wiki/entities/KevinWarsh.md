The episode uses him less as a biography subject than as the next test of whether [[CentralBankIndependence]] survives leadership change.

## Source Position
- The source says Warsh previously served on the Fed board, which [[LaelBrainard]] treats as a reason to give him the benefit of the doubt.
- Brainard's argument is that board service exposes officials to the Fed's dual mandate and the need to avoid political judgments.
- Warsh is connected to the episode's broader question: independence is not settled by Powell's behavior if future chairs face similar pressure.
- Episode 155 adds the "seemingly hawkish dove" interpretation: Warsh may preserve independent-sounding language while still fitting Trump's practical preference for rate cuts.
- Episode 155 also treats his appointment as a market trigger for gold, while arguing that the deeper selloff came from [[LiquidityDrivenVolatilityCascade|liquidity and model-driven volatility]].
