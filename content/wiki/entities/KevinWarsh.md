# Kevin Warsh

## Overview
Kevin Warsh is a former [[FederalReserve]] board member and [[DonaldTrump]] nominee who succeeded [[JeromePowell]] as Fed chair in 2026.

## Current Profile
Warsh's early record resists a one-dimensional label. Before his chairmanship, sources variously treated him as an experienced, productivity-aware institutional insider, a politically flexible apparent hawk, or a source-scoped governance-risk silhouette. His first rate increase—against Trump's stated preference for cuts—provides evidence of operational independence, but market pressure, committee unanimity, terse communication, and an unfinished policy review leave his long-term doctrine unsettled. The earliest All-In source is useful as nomination-era expectation, not evidence about later conduct.

## Key Characteristics
- Has prior Fed-board experience and an institutional understanding of the dual mandate.
- Entered office under strong presidential pressure for lower interest rates.
- Was initially read through conflicting hawk, dove, and political-operator narratives.
- Raised rates early in his chairmanship after signaling the move at Jackson Hole.
- Emphasizes growth, productivity, investment, and labor strength alongside inflation.
- Communicates tersely and has not yet articulated a settled long-run framework.
- Was expected by supportive investors to distinguish productivity-driven growth from inflation and to modernize the Fed's use of economic data.

## Qualifications
- One rate increase does not establish durable independence or a complete monetary doctrine.
- The 1929 analogy is explicitly a source-scoped watchlist, not an allegation of misconduct.
- The “hawkish dove” label came before the observed rate increase and remains provisional rather than predictive.
- The economic and market conditions are dated to September 2026.
- The February All-In assessment is friendly nomination-era commentary and cannot establish integrity, independence, or policy skill on its own.

## What Changed
- Warsh moved from incoming-chair speculation to an observable governing record.
- His first increase weakens the simplest claim that he would mechanically follow Trump's rate preference.
- Market discipline and committee unanimity now appear alongside presidential pressure as constraints on his choices.
- Added the earlier productivity-and-data-modernization expectation while keeping it subordinate to later observed conduct.
