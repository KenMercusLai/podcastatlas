# John Dickie

## Overview

John Dickie is a historian of Italy and professor of Italian studies at University College London whose food-history argument anchors [[484-the-food-that-changed-the-world-glt2872429496|The Food that Changed the World]].

## Current Profile

In the current evidence, Dickie challenges the idea that Italian cuisine simply descends from timeless rural household practice. Drawing on *Delizia: The Epic History of Italians and Their Food*, he instead presents cities, trade, courts, mass provisioning, migration, industry, politics, and marketing as the forces that made Italian food both coherent and changeable.

## Key Characteristics

- Treats Italian cuisine as a dialogue among cities rather than a fixed map of modern regions.
- Distinguishes documented food history from clear, attractive, but weakly supported origin legends.
- Connects food to political economy, class, religion, monarchy, fascism, migration, industry, and national identity.
- Defends Italian cuisine as historically real while rejecting claims of unchanged peasant authenticity.

## Evidence

- Urban thesis: [[484-the-food-that-changed-the-world-glt2872429496]] attributes to Dickie the claim that Italy's network of strong cities is the main historical structure behind its cuisine.
- Myth criticism: [[484-the-food-that-changed-the-world-glt2872429496]] presents his rejection of the Marco Polo pasta story and the Raffaele Esposito pizza Margherita legend.
- Political and commercial history: [[484-the-food-that-changed-the-world-glt2872429496]] follows his account through Renaissance courts, Neapolitan mass food, fascist self-sufficiency, postwar brands, and Slow Food.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded by one interview summarizing a very large subject. The episode does not expose the full documentary basis for every chronology, statistic, or authenticity judgment, so those details remain source-scoped rather than independently verified here.
