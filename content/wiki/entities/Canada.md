# Canada

## Overview
Canada is represented as a federation where American trade dependence, provincial energy and identity politics, infrastructure delivery, Indigenous land and housing, media financing, travel, and wildfire risk intersect.

## Current Profile
The current trade branch presents Canada as an exposed ally of the [[UnitedStates]]. [[MarkCarney]] wants greater export and energy independence, but tariff conflict can reduce the stability and investment needed to diversify. The new episode sharpens this into a unity problem: [[Alberta]]’s oil and fiscal grievances and [[Quebec]]’s cultural-national movement have different causes, yet both can intensify when American pressure produces uneven regional losses.

Domestic capacity is therefore political as well as economic. The [[BuildingCanadaAct]] and [[MajorProjectsOfficeCanada]] aim to accelerate nationally important infrastructure, while the housing branch uses the [[SquamishNation]] project to show how federal finance, sovereign land, and municipal supply constraints interact. The energy branch adds Alberta oil-sands concentration and [[TransMountainExpansion]] capacity; the media branch shows how public support, tax credits, distribution advances, and retained IP can produce internationally legible television.

The remaining sources widen rather than unify the profile. Canada functions as a restorative travel setting through nature-city proximity, and as a climate-risk case through the 2023 wildfire season and smoke burden. These strands show national capacity under different pressures, but they do not amount to a complete country survey.

## Key Characteristics
- Highly exposed to U.S. trade pressure while seeking export, energy, and infrastructure diversification.
- Federally constrained by distinct Alberta resource grievances and Quebec language, culture, and independence politics.
- Uses tax policy and faster project approvals as part of its response to trade and unity pressure.
- Relies on federal finance and public policy in housing, infrastructure, and media-production outcomes.
- Carries an oil-sands boom branch tied to producer profits, pipeline capacity, environmental cost, and consultation politics.
- Functions as a travel and recovery setting through proximity between cities, nature, local life, and long vacations.
- Carries major wildfire-climate evidence through the 2023 fire season and smoke-health burden.

## Qualifications
The profile is source-scoped and uneven. Referendum thresholds, export shares, tariff effects, oil claims, wildfire estimates, and approval-speed expectations are time-bounded to their episodes. The sources do not establish that separatism will succeed, diversification will offset American losses, or faster approvals will resolve environmental, Indigenous consultation, cost, and legitimacy constraints.

## What Changed
- Added the simultaneous Alberta and Quebec unity test to the trade and energy profile.
- Added the Building Canada Act, tax response, and wider export-market outreach.
- Reframed infrastructure delivery as part of both economic diversification and federal cohesion.
