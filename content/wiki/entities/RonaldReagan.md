# Ronald Reagan / 罗纳德·里根

## Overview
Ronald Reagan was president of the [[UnitedStates]] from 1981 to 1989 and is represented in the wiki through Soviet threat perception, domestic political economy, presidential performance, the [[IranContraAffair|Iran-Contra affair]], and late-Cold-War arms control.

## Current Profile
[[81-jiandie-yu-pantu-shuwozhiyan-007-de-gushi-ruobaole-xia-693711209]] emphasizes how Reagan's rhetoric, Pershing II deployment, and Strategic Defense Initiative entered a Soviet war-scare system already primed by [[OperationRYAN]]. [[312-reagan-iran-contra-and-the-cold-war-part-3-glt4042202229]] broadens the profile: optimism, humor, ceremony, and national storytelling restored confidence in presidential authority, but [[ReaganomicsPoliticalEconomy|Reaganomics]], confrontation with labor, silence during the AIDS crisis, and Iran-Contra keep the substantive record contested. The same source presents nuclear policy as internally complex: Reagan combined hard-line pressure with horror at nuclear war, sensitivity to Soviet fear after Able Archer, and ambitious bargaining with [[MikhailGorbachev|Mikhail Gorbachev]].

## Key Characteristics
- A skilled [[PresidentialSymbolicLeadership|symbolic leader]] who used performance, optimism, formal ceremony, humor, and emotionally resonant stories as political instruments.
- A conservative economic reformer whose tax, deregulation, spending, defense, and labor choices remain contested in their distributional and fiscal effects.
- A confrontational anti-communist whose posture could intensify [[ColdWarNuclearMisperception|adversary misperception]].
- A nuclear abolitionist in aspiration who nevertheless refused to abandon strategic defense at Reykjavik.
- A president whose personal concern for hostages contributed to approval of the Iran arms channel and whose administration pursued covert Contra support despite congressional limits.
- A leader whose political resilience and public appeal outlasted recession, policy failures, scandal, and questions about factual precision.

## Evidence
### Strategic posture and adversary perception
- [[81-jiandie-yu-pantu-shuwozhiyan-007-de-gushi-ruobaole-xia-693711209]] connects Reagan's posture to Operation RYAN and Soviet fear of a first strike.
- [[312-reagan-iran-contra-and-the-cold-war-part-3-glt4042202229]] connects Able Archer and Reagan's reaction to *The Day After* to a later effort to reassure and negotiate.

### Presidential authority and domestic policy
- [[312-reagan-iran-contra-and-the-cold-war-part-3-glt4042202229]] uses the 1984 campaign, inauguration, assassination attempt, debate humor, and farewell address to ground symbolic leadership, while pairing them with tax cuts, deficits, recession, PATCO, and AIDS-response failures.

### Scandal and diplomacy
- [[312-reagan-iran-contra-and-the-cold-war-part-3-glt4042202229]] says Reagan approved the Iran arms channel and encouraged a route around Contra-funding restrictions, then follows the scandal into denial and concealment.
- [[312-reagan-iran-contra-and-the-cold-war-part-3-glt4042202229]] follows the Geneva, Reykjavik, Washington, and Moscow summits into the INF Treaty and strategic-arms-reduction process.

## Qualifications
These sources emphasize interpretation rather than a complete presidency. Economic recovery has competing explanations, the episode's figures and dialogue remain source-scoped, and presidential intent should not be inferred from Soviet perception alone. Reagan's exact knowledge of operational details in Iran-Contra is less certain than his support for its two underlying objectives. Symbolic effectiveness does not excuse policy harm, and anti-nuclear aspiration does not erase escalation risk.

## What Changed
- Expanded Reagan from an adversary-perception figure into a multidimensional presidential profile.
- Added symbolic leadership as a substantive source of authority rather than a purely cosmetic trait.
- Added domestic-policy, AIDS-response, labor, and Iran-Contra qualifications.
- Reconciled hard-line nuclear posture with later reassurance and arms-control ambition without flattening the tension.
