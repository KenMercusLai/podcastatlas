## Overview

殷皇后 / Empress Yin enters the wiki as the Emperor-He-era empress whose jealousy and reported threat against the Deng family make [[EmpressDowagerDengLateHan|Deng Sui]]'s pre-regency restraint politically consequential. Her current role is source-scoped: she is less a full biography than the harem-power figure whose fall clears the way for Deng's appointment as empress.

## Current Profile

Hanji 775 portrays Empress Yin as the formal empress before Deng's rise. The source contrasts her with Deng through conduct and status management: Yin is described as small in stature and sometimes mocked by attendants for behavior that fails court etiquette, while Deng quietly covers for her rather than joining the ridicule.

The conflict intensifies when [[EmperorHeOfHan|Emperor He]] increasingly favors Deng. Yin's jealousy is presented not as private resentment only, but as potential lethal family politics. During Emperor He's illness, she reportedly says that if she gains power she will not leave the Deng family alive. Deng hears this, despairs, and nearly kills herself before a palace maid's false report of the emperor's recovery interrupts the attempt.

After Emperor He recovers, Yin's conduct has become known at court. The source says Emperor He deposes her and appoints Deng as empress, making Yin the direct predecessor whose fall marks the transition from harem rivalry to Deng's formal political elevation.

## Key Characteristics

- Formal empress in Emperor He's court before Deng Sui's appointment.
- Rival whose jealousy turns Deng's palace favor into Deng-family survival risk.
- Negative foil for Deng's self-effacing etiquette and restraint in Hanji 775.
- Deposed after Emperor He's recovery and the exposure of her hostility toward Deng.
- Source-scoped harem actor whose broader life, family network, and later fate are not yet developed in the wiki.

## Qualifications

The current page is bounded to one podcast source note and preserves its negative portrayal cautiously. It does not reconstruct Empress Yin's full biography, the formal legal grounds for deposition, her family network, or her later fate. The jealousy and threat are important because they explain Deng's danger in this source, not because they settle every historical claim about Yin independently.
