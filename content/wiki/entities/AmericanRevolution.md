# American Revolution

## Overview

The American Revolution appears in the wiki as the colonial break with Britain whose success depended on military endurance, institutional coordination, and French-backed diplomacy.

## Current Profile

The current synthesis now has two complementary Founding Fathers routes. The Franklin source explains how a loyal British imperial identity ruptured into organized independence and how [[BenjaminFranklin|Benjamin Franklin]]'s French diplomacy internationalized the war. The Washington source adds the military survival problem: [[GeorgeWashington|George Washington]] had to keep the [[ContinentalArmy|Continental Army]] alive, avoid decisive defeat, survive [[ValleyForge|Valley Forge]], and hold legitimacy long enough for British logistical strain and the [[FrancoAmericanAlliance1778|Franco-American alliance]] to change the war's balance.

## Key Characteristics

- Political rupture from British loyalty to American independence.
- Institutionalized through the [[ContinentalCongress|Continental Congress]] and the [[DeclarationOfIndependence|Declaration of Independence]].
- Depended on [[StrategicEnduranceMilitaryCommand|strategic endurance military command]] as well as diplomatic breakthrough.
- Turned on foreign support, especially the [[FrancoAmericanAlliance1778|Franco-American alliance]].
- Produced a peace settlement in the [[TreatyOfParis1783|Treaty of Paris]] recognizing independence.
- Carried slavery contradictions because liberty advocates included enslavers and because Black soldiers served while slavery persisted.

## Evidence

- Rupture sequence: [[684-franklin-revenge-of-the-american-genius-part-2-glt6013835340]] follows Franklin from loyal Englishman to independence supporter after the Hutchinson letters, Cockpit humiliation, and outbreak of fighting.
- Institutional independence: [[684-franklin-revenge-of-the-american-genius-part-2-glt6013835340]] places Franklin in the Continental Congress and Declaration committee, while [[683-washington-hero-of-the-revolution-part-1-glt3752138067]] shows the Congress selecting Washington as commander.
- Military endurance: [[683-washington-hero-of-the-revolution-part-1-glt3752138067]] says Washington's task was to keep the Continental Army alive, avoid decisive defeat, and wait for Britain to tire under distance, terrain, and cost.
- Valley Forge crisis: [[683-washington-hero-of-the-revolution-part-1-glt3752138067]] treats Valley Forge as a supply, disease, discipline, inoculation, training, and political-legitimacy crisis rather than only patriotic tableau.
- Internationalization: [[684-franklin-revenge-of-the-american-genius-part-2-glt6013835340]] argues that American victory required European support, and [[683-washington-hero-of-the-revolution-part-1-glt3752138067]] adds that news of French recognition and alliance changed British priorities by making the war global.
- Slavery contradiction: [[684-franklin-revenge-of-the-american-genius-part-2-glt6013835340]] records Samuel Johnson's critique of slaveholding Americans demanding liberty and Franklin's own slaveholding before his late abolitionist turn, while [[683-washington-hero-of-the-revolution-part-1-glt3752138067]] adds Washington's slaveholding and Black soldiers' service in the army.

## Qualifications

This page is still source-scoped to the Franklin and Washington episodes. It does not yet synthesize Loyalist politics beyond William Franklin and the Gates pressure signal, Indigenous diplomacy beyond the Oneida scouts mention, the southern campaign, wartime finance, ordinary civilian experience, or the full peace settlement.

## What Changed

- Added the Washington and Valley Forge military-survival branch to the prior Franklin diplomacy account.
- Reframed the Revolution as an endurance-and-internationalization problem rather than only a diplomatic rupture.
- Added Black military service and Washington's slaveholding to the founding liberty-slavery contradiction.
