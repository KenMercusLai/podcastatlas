# American Revolution

## Overview

The American Revolution appears in the Franklin episode as the colonial break with Britain whose success depended not only on battlefield resistance but also on Franklin's diplomacy in France.

## Current Profile

The episode approaches the Revolution through Benjamin Franklin's transformation. Franklin's 1770 parable warns Britain not to assume America will remain weak; the Hutchinson letters and Cockpit humiliation make imperial repression feel personal and political; Lexington and Boston fighting make neutrality less plausible; and the French mission turns American independence from rebellion into an international war Britain can lose. The source's strongest claim is therefore diplomatic: French support, secured partly through Franklin's fame and performance, tipped the balance toward American victory.

## Key Characteristics

- Political rupture from British loyalty to American independence.
- Institutionalized through the [[ContinentalCongress|Continental Congress]] and the [[DeclarationOfIndependence|Declaration of Independence]].
- Depended on foreign support, especially the [[FrancoAmericanAlliance1778|Franco-American alliance]].
- Produced a peace settlement in the [[TreatyOfParis1783|Treaty of Paris]] recognizing independence.
- Carried a slavery contradiction because many advocates of liberty also owned enslaved people.

## Evidence

- Rupture sequence: [[684-franklin-revenge-of-the-american-genius-part-2-glt6013835340]] follows Franklin from loyal Englishman to independence supporter after the Hutchinson letters, Cockpit humiliation, and outbreak of fighting.
- Institutional independence: [[684-franklin-revenge-of-the-american-genius-part-2-glt6013835340]] places Franklin in the Continental Congress and Declaration committee.
- Internationalization: [[684-franklin-revenge-of-the-american-genius-part-2-glt6013835340]] argues that American victory required European support and that Franklin was the obvious figure to seek it.
- French support: [[684-franklin-revenge-of-the-american-genius-part-2-glt6013835340]] says France entered the war and tipped the balance in America's favor.
- Slavery contradiction: [[684-franklin-revenge-of-the-american-genius-part-2-glt6013835340]] records Samuel Johnson's critique of slaveholding Americans demanding liberty and Franklin's own slaveholding before his late abolitionist turn.
