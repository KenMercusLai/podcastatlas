# Interventions Testing Program

## Overview
The Interventions Testing Program is the mouse-longevity research project used in [[supplements-for-longevity-their-efficacy-dr-peter-attia-scim7110594668]] as a comparatively rigorous test of whether candidate interventions change lifespan rather than only pathways or biomarkers.

## Current Profile
Within the sources, the program matters less as a catalog of compounds than as an evidence standard. Survival results in mice are used to challenge claims built from mechanism, unusual disease models, observational associations, or subjective response. [[PeterAttia]] contrasts negative reported results for NR, resveratrol, and metformin with positive results for rapamycin, canagliflozin, acarbose, and 17-alpha estradiol; the Journal Club also highlights rapamycin benefit when started late in mouse life.

The program is still preclinical. A mouse lifespan result can strengthen or weaken a longevity hypothesis, but it does not establish human benefit, dose, safety, indication, or treatment eligibility.

## Key Characteristics
- Tests candidate longevity interventions against survival and healthspan-relevant outcomes in mice.
- Is presented in the source as a stronger filter than mechanism-only reasoning or a single unusual disease model.
- Distinguishes interventions that change mouse lifespan from those that do not in the tested conditions.
- Produces preclinical evidence that still requires human translation.

## Evidence
- Design role: [[supplements-for-longevity-their-efficacy-dr-peter-attia-scim7110594668]] describes the program as the strongest available non-human testing system for candidate lifespan interventions.
- Discriminating results: [[supplements-for-longevity-their-efficacy-dr-peter-attia-scim7110594668]] reports negative results for NR and resveratrol and positive results for rapamycin, canagliflozin, acarbose, and 17-alpha estradiol.
- Metformin filter: [[journal-club-with-dr-peter-attia-metformin-for-longevity-the-power-of-belief-effects-scim5394566255]] reports a negative metformin lifespan result and uses it to qualify general geroprotection claims without denying the drug's established metabolic indication.
- Evidence boundary: [[supplements-for-longevity-their-efficacy-dr-peter-attia-scim7110594668]] uses those results to reject a direct jump from pathway plausibility to human longevity advice.

## Qualifications
This profile is bounded to two podcast sources and is not an independent review of program protocols, every tested dose, sex-specific result, interaction, replication record, healthspan measure, or publication. Mouse survival evidence is informative but cannot by itself establish human clinical efficacy, safety, dose, or indication.
