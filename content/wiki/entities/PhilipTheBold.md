# Philip the Bold

## Overview

Philip the Bold is the first Valois duke in the episode's Burgundian sequence, joining a French princely inheritance to the wealth of [[Flanders]] through marriage.

## Current Profile

Philip won a reputation for bravery beside his father, John II of France, at the [[BattleOfPoitiers|Battle of Poitiers]]. After the previous Burgundian line ended without an heir, he received the Duchy of Burgundy and made it the southern base of a larger dynastic project.

His marriage to Margaret of Flanders was more consequential than battlefield reputation alone. It brought the prospect of Flemish territories, cities, trade, and cloth wealth into the dynasty. Philip then directed northern artistic resources toward Champmol near Dijon, making patronage part of the political bridge between the two regions.

## Key Characteristics

- French royal son whose standing was shaped by Poitiers.
- Founder of the Valois Burgundian ducal sequence.
- Dynastic strategist whose marriage connected Burgundy with Flanders.
- Power broker who benefited from French royal weakness.
- Patron linking Flemish wealth and artists to Dijon.
- Ruler associated in the episode with regulation of Burgundian wine and mustard production.

## Qualifications

The episode emphasizes the dramatic combination of war, plague, and marriage rather than providing a complete administrative biography. Its claims about grape policy, mustard improvement, city wealth, and artistic origins are retained as episode-level claims.

## What Changed

- Created Philip as the dynastic founder whose marriage gave Burgundy its durable north-south structure.
