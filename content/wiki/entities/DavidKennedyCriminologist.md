# David M. Kennedy

## Overview

David M. Kennedy is the criminologist and violence-reduction strategist presented across the two *Wolfgang's Law* episodes as a principal architect of [[FocusedDeterrence]] and [[OperationCeasefireBoston|Boston's Operation Ceasefire]].

## Current Profile

The episodes portray Kennedy as moving from network diagnosis to an integrated intervention: identify the small connected group driving serious violence, communicate directly, offer practical help and community support, and make continued violence trigger predictable enforcement. Part One broadens the concentration finding through a Stockholm case in which an initially feared group of 30 was reassessed as six principal drivers. His Baltimore experience also makes him a witness to the human cost of delayed and incomplete adoption.

## Key Characteristics

- Uses tightly scoped network analysis rather than treating an entire neighborhood or demographic group as equally dangerous.
- Combines support, community legitimacy, direct communication, and enforcement in one strategy.
- Treats faithful local implementation and durable institutional ownership as essential to results.
- Frames Baltimore's multi-decade delay as preventable harm rather than a neutral diffusion lag.
- Uses social-network reassessment to narrow diffuse community fear into a more specific intervention problem.

## Evidence

### Strategy design

- [[wolfgang-s-law-part-two]] credits Kennedy and colleagues with identifying Boston's concentrated violence network and developing the intervention later known as Operation Ceasefire.
- [[wolfgang-s-law-part-one]] uses his Stockholm example to show that local fear may initially overstate the number of principal violence drivers.

### Implementation judgment

- [[wolfgang-s-law-part-two]] says Kennedy observed early replications weaken after external experts departed and Baltimore postpone comprehensive adoption for more than two decades.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to two structured episode summaries. It does not provide Kennedy's full scholarship, the wider authorship of focused deterrence, program evaluation methods, or the views of affected communities.
