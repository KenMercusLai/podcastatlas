## Overview

窦武 / Dou Wu (late Han) is a late-Han outer-relative and anti-eunuch official whose wiki profile now begins with the failed 168 CE coup itself. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-887-dang-zhuduizhang-pengshang-zhuduiyou-jieguo-taican-lvyh9a-kvsdqaokmylgg139bvhfk|Hanji 887]] presents him as the formal powerholder whose incomplete action gives [[CaoJieLateHan|曹节]], [[WangFuLateHan|王甫]], and the eunuch side time to reverse the field. Later sources preserve the memory of his defeat through [[EmpressDowagerDouMiao|窦妙 / 窦皇太后]]'s treatment, Cao/Wang's old grievance, and [[DongZhuo|董卓]]'s later rehabilitation politics.

## Current Profile

The current synthesis separates Dou Wu's action, defeat, and afterlife. In the coup layer, Dou has status and military access but does not convert the plan into fast removal of the eunuch threat. [[ChenFanLateHan|陈蕃]] tries to rescue the situation by entering the palace and publicly defending Dou, but Wang Fu counters with rebellion and corruption accusations, Chen Fan is killed, Zhang Huan's uninformed return is used by the eunuch side, and Dou's troops defect after Wang Fu promises rewards. Dou then flees with his nephew, chooses suicide, and has his head displayed.

The later memory layer shows why Dou's defeat does not disappear. [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]] still remembers Dou Wu and Dou Miao as accession supporters, while Cao Jie and Wang Fu remember Dou as an old enemy. The 172 CE burial dispute over Dou Miao indirectly reopens the question of whether Dou Wu and Chen Fan were unjustly destroyed, and Dong Zhuo later turns rehabilitation of Dou, Chen Fan, and other Party Prohibition figures into legitimacy packaging for his own coercive takeover.

## Key Characteristics

- Dou Wu is a defeated anti-eunuch powerholder whose failure is framed as a mismatch between formal authority and executable crisis command.
- His 168 CE collapse turns on hesitation, secrecy failure, and the eunuch side's faster command of palace information and troop persuasion.
- His defeat creates immediate collateral punishment for family, clients, followers, recommended officials, and old subordinates.
- His memory remains politically active because Dou Miao's status and later rehabilitation debates reopen the moral meaning of the coup.
- Later actors can use the real injustice memory around Dou Wu for opposing purposes: repair, factional defense, or legitimacy cover.

## Evidence

- Death and public humiliation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-887-dang-zhuduizhang-pengshang-zhuduiyou-jieguo-taican-lvyh9a-kvsdqaokmylgg139bvhfk|Hanji 887]] says Dou flees, dies by suicide, and has his head displayed at Luoyang's douting.
- Accession and old-grievance memory: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-898-renzei-zuofu-kelian-zhiren-neng-you-duo-huangtang-lgfup6tyv85zhvnmzf6xlesmblcj|Hanji 898]] says Emperor Ling remembers Dou and Dou Miao as accession supporters while Cao Jie and Wang Fu resent Dou's earlier attempt to remove them.
- Burial dispute as indirect repair: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-901-jixingzi-de-ren-hui-shi-shenme-xiachang-lh1yfyjzqptxvtnv9taiqms9yjud|Hanji 901]] says Dou Miao's posthumous status threatens to revive the injustice memory around Dou Wu and Chen Fan.
- Continuing eunuch-power memory: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-921-jinqian-ru-shui-que-ze-ke-si-tan-ze-yan-si-lj1dk4qdgcago9elr7ctuvre3zic|Hanji 921]] pairs Dou Wu with Chen Fan as an older victim in Cao Jie's negative legacy.
- Rehabilitation as political cover: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-948-dongzhuo-feidi-quanchen-ruhe-gaibian-huangquan-youxi-ltekg1a-5b5u37aeekjm3dm2m6kk|Hanji 948]] uses Dou Wu and Chen Fan as restored Party Prohibition-linked victims in Dong Zhuo's justice-packaging move.

## Qualifications

- Hanji 887 gives a podcast-summary version of the coup, so its claim that Dou's hesitation was decisive remains source-scoped rather than a complete reconstruction of every military and palace channel.
- The page preserves Dou Wu's later moral grievance without treating every later appeal to his memory as sincere repair; Dong Zhuo's rehabilitation branch is explicitly coercive legitimacy packaging.
- Minor figures attached to Dou's final flight and punishment are left source-scoped until stronger evidence supports stable pages.

## What Changed

- Hanji 887 moves Dou Wu from remembered victim and rehabilitation symbol to the actual failed-coup actor whose decision tempo shaped the disaster.
- The page now separates the 168 CE execution failure from the later Dou Miao burial and Dong Zhuo rehabilitation memory branches.
- The hesitation and secrecy diagnosis is retained as source-scoped interpretation, not as a claim that violence alone would have guaranteed success.
