# Epicurus / 伊壁鸠鲁

## Overview

Epicurus / 伊壁鸠鲁 is a Hellenistic philosopher represented here through two sharply different uses: a comic office cameo and a doctrine in which gods exist but do not govern human fate, while natural understanding aims at tranquility.

## Current Profile

[[605-greek-myths-jason-the-quest-for-the-golden-fleece-part-4-glt3067620137]] places Epicurus inside changing Hellenistic responses to inherited religion. He is not presented as an atheist: the gods exist, but they do not intervene in ordinary life or control destiny. The study of nature therefore seeks freedom from fear and *ataraxia*, not modern experimental science for its own sake.

[[92-bailatu-shangbanji-yong-zhexue-gaoxiao-zhichang-716822350]] uses Epicurus much more lightly as part of the philosophical cast of *Plato Goes to Work*. Copier trouble, drinking, farewell rituals, and everyday routines turn famous thinkers into embodied office characters. This comic reception is evidence of later reuse, not a doctrinal account.

## Key Characteristics

- Hellenistic philosopher associated with tranquility and freedom from supernatural fear.
- Believer in non-intervening gods rather than a simple atheist in the episode's account.
- Thinker for whom natural explanation has an ethical purpose.
- Historical figure reusable as a recognizable character in modern philosophical satire.

## Evidence

- Hellenistic theology: [[605-greek-myths-jason-the-quest-for-the-golden-fleece-part-4-glt3067620137]] distinguishes divine existence from providential intervention.
- Ethical aim: [[605-greek-myths-jason-the-quest-for-the-golden-fleece-part-4-glt3067620137]] names *ataraxia* as the purpose of understanding nature.
- Comic reception: [[92-bailatu-shangbanji-yong-zhexue-gaoxiao-zhichang-716822350]] places Epicurus among philosophers translated into office equipment, parties, and routine.

## Qualifications

Neither source is a systematic treatment of Epicurean physics, epistemology, ethics, community, pleasure, or the surviving texts. The Hellenistic episode compresses doctrine for a broader argument about religion, while the workplace episode discusses a satirical book. The profile should not be treated as a complete account of Epicureanism.

## What Changed

- Added the non-intervening-gods and *ataraxia* account while preserving the earlier comic-reception context.
- Distinguished source-backed doctrine from a modern satirical cameo.
