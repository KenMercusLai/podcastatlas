# Lookie / Loki

Lookie, also called Loki in the source transcript, is the wearable [[AIContextMachine|context-machine]] product discussed by [[WangTianfan]] in [[reai-yige-hangye-15-nian-de-liyou-shi-shenme-duitan-wang-tianfan-woyao-tou-zhenzheng-de-kuaile-tou-zui-chun-de-yuanjing-tou-renxing-de-guanghui-gonglu-boke-lu98aa1byafbbljyjrn8oquiezk]]. This page uses the `Lookie` filename to avoid collision with the existing mythological [[Loki]] page.

Wang says [[BAICapital|B.A.I Capital]] became interested in a pendant-like form after studying [[Limitless]] and after concluding that many AI-glasses pitches misunderstood the near-term readiness of supply chain, chips, connectivity, battery, and user adoption. In his account, Lookie mattered because the team had soft/hard integration experience from autonomous-driving backgrounds and because the product could capture meetings, speech, and daily life more continuously than a phone-only app.

The episode's strongest product point is that the hardware is not the real moat by itself. Wang highlights an AI comic feature that turns a user's day into passive, shareable comic output, using it as evidence that interaction design and emotional output can create user joy beyond efficiency.

## Key Claims
- Pendant-like AI hardware can be useful when it captures context that phones, laptops, or one-off prompts miss.
- The source treats recording as shallow unless it becomes reflection, feedback, recall, or delight.
- [[PersonalAIMemory]] products face privacy and bystander-trust constraints, but the source is more focused on whether the output makes the user's life more present and memorable.
- Copyable form factors do not settle the product question; user understanding, software loops, and non-productivity value matter more.
