# CoreWeave

## Overview
CoreWeave is a specialized AI cloud and neocloud provider that combines GPU fleets, data-center capacity, operating software, and long-term customer contracts to supply training and inference infrastructure.

## Current Profile
CoreWeave developed from GPU-based crypto mining into rendering, scientific compute, and neural-network workloads, then specialized in operating Nvidia clusters for model companies and hyperscalers. Its financing model packages a customer contract, GPUs, and a data-center agreement into a project-like box whose customer payments cover operating costs, power, interest, and principal before residual cash reaches the company.

That structure makes scarce capacity financeable but does not remove leverage. Recent sources report bonds yielding roughly 15%, stock weakness, and expectations that a large unfinanced buildout will require further debt. Better-than-expected quarterly revenue and raised 2026 guidance support near-term demand, while the newest episode sharpens the timing problem: construction and interest costs arrive before power connection and customer cash flow, and secured claims can sit ahead of unsecured creditors. Existing fixed-rate coupons do not rise when secondary-market yields rise, yet stressed pricing can make future borrowing more expensive. Continuing demand, backlog, and guidance therefore do not settle refinancing, customer concentration, construction, contract, energization, chip-collateral, residual-value, or payback-period risk.

## Key Characteristics
- Specialized AI cloud built around purpose-operated GPU clusters rather than generic cloud breadth.
- Reused crypto-mining GPU operations across rendering, research, model training, and inference.
- Uses customer contracts, accelerators, and data-center obligations as a project-like financing package.
- Depends strategically on Nvidia supply, ecosystem support, accelerator generations, and residual-value assumptions.
- Fleet economics depend on utilization, contract durability, powered capacity, rental pricing, deployment timing, and payback period.
- Long fixed-price contracts improve bankability and downside protection but can forgo spot-market upside.
- Strong revenue, guidance, and backlog can coexist with negative cash timing, pre-energization liquidity risk, and unsecured-creditor exposure when data centers must be financed before they generate rental income.

## Qualifications
CoreWeave's operating and financing accounts are partly company-led, while the market-stress accounts are commentator-led and do not audit the full capital structure. A 15%-plus yield or costly CDS signals perceived risk, not a finding of default. Older-GPU demand weakens a universal rapid-obsolescence claim but does not prove every fleet earns through its depreciation life. Better revenue, guidance, and backlog do not establish positive free cash flow or self-funded expansion. Debt requirements, loan plans, payback periods, bond prices, creditor priority, contract economics, chip values, and spot-price claims remain attributed estimates.

## What Changed
- Made grid connection and the construction-to-cash timing gap explicit alongside demand and refinancing risk.
- Added secured-versus-unsecured priority as a reason identical operating performance can affect creditor classes differently.
- Preserved strong backlog and current compute demand as qualifications rather than treating stressed bond pricing as proof of default.
