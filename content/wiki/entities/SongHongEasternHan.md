## Overview

宋弘 / Song Hong is an Eastern Han official whose current wiki profile joins a primary Jianwu 2 court-restraint source with a later coerced-marriage comparison. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-685-donghan-dachen-songhong-weihe-buken-zuo-guangwudi-jiefu-ljafg8giyhg5wmumv-g-09lywcu2|Hanji 685]] presents him as the Da Sikong who corrects a music-favor problem around [[HuanTanEasternHan|桓谭]] and then refuses [[HuyangPrincessEasternHan|湖阳公主]] through the maxim "糟糠之妻不下堂." [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-878-bu-shou-yueshu-de-quanli-hairen-buqian-lvevnahcxshgbz5zyqbaf36pa7nk|Hanji 878]] later uses that same refusal as the positive contrast to [[YangQiaoLateHan|杨桥]]'s fatal forced-marriage case under [[EmperorHuanOfHan|汉桓帝]].

## Current Profile

Hanji 685 gives Song Hong his active court role. [[LiuXiu|刘秀 / 光武帝]] appoints him Da Sikong, and Song recommends Huan Tan for office. When Liu Xiu enjoys Huan's music, Song does not treat the problem as harmless entertainment: he rebukes Huan for pleasing the ruler through sound and then, at court, blames himself for recommending someone who has drawn the ruler toward Zheng-style music rather than loyal and righteous assistance. Liu Xiu is ashamed and apologizes.

The same episode then turns Song into a marriage-boundary case. Huyang Princess, Liu Xiu's widowed sister, praises Song's bearing and virtue. Liu Xiu tests Song indirectly by quoting the idea that status and wealth make it natural to change friends or wives. Song answers that companions from poverty should not be forgotten and the wife who shared hardship should not be cast out. Liu Xiu understands and tells the princess the matter cannot be arranged.

Hanji 878 later uses this as a restraint benchmark. The comparison matters because Song is not forced into the match. Liu Xiu raises the possibility, receives a principled refusal, and withdraws. Song's profile therefore separates imperial marriage interest from coercive imperial marriage pressure: the subject's existing moral obligation remains a real boundary only if the ruler accepts it.

## Key Characteristics

- Da Sikong whose current profile centers on court correction and marital obligation rather than administrative biography.
- Recommender who remains responsible for whether his recommended talent serves governance or merely pleases the ruler.
- Face-preserving remonstrant who criticizes ruler behavior through self-blame and correction of Huan Tan.
- Loyal existing-husband figure in the "贫贱之知" and "糟糠之妻" moral frame.
- Positive contrast case showing that imperial marriage interest need not become coercion when the ruler accepts refusal.

## Qualifications

This page remains bounded to Hanji 878 and Hanji 685. It should not infer Song Hong's full career, family background, or complete Da Sikong administration. Hanji 685 makes him more than a later comparison case, but only within two bounded scenes: correction of Huan Tan's court entertainment role and refusal of a princess marriage. Huan Tan's later anti-chenwei conflict in Hanji 731 belongs to Huan Tan's separate profile and should not be folded back into Song's responsibility.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 685 as Song Hong's primary Jianwu 2 source.
- Expanded Song from a late-Han comparison figure into a Da Sikong who combines recommendation accountability, face-preserving remonstrance, and marital-boundary refusal.
- Preserved Hanji 878 as the later forced-marriage contrast rather than treating Song's case as coercive.
