# Akron, Ohio

## Overview
Akron, Ohio is the former rubber-industry center used by [[MarketplaceTech]] as a historical warning for AI-era labor and tax disruption.

## Current Profile
In the source, Akron matters less as a full city history than as a concentrated industrial ecosystem. Its tire factories provided difficult but well-paid union work and helped fund local public services, while the radial tire helped undermine the production base. The episode uses that history to ask whether AI could similarly weaken labor income and public revenue in places or sectors dependent on older forms of work.

## Key Characteristics
- Akron is framed as a place where consumer-benefiting technology produced concentrated local harm.
- Its rubber-industry identity depended on major tire companies and union manufacturing jobs.
- Its decline makes tax-base loss visible, especially through nearby [[BarbertonOhio|Barberton]].
- The city functions as the historical anchor for [[LaborTaxBaseAIErosion]] rather than only as local color.

## Evidence
- Industrial identity: [[tech-20260903-0903-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260903-0903-mp-tech-pod-128]] describes Akron as once known as the rubber capital of the world and names major tire companies around the city.
- Job-quality and cost evidence: [[tech-20260903-0903-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260903-0903-mp-tech-pod-128]] says the old rubber industry created poor air quality but also good union jobs.
- Technology-disruption evidence: [[tech-20260903-0903-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260903-0903-mp-tech-pod-128]] presents the radial tire as a central reason rubber work left Akron.
- Fiscal-analogy evidence: [[tech-20260903-0903-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260903-0903-mp-tech-pod-128]] connects the Akron-area factory decline to lost municipal income-tax revenue and then to AI labor-tax risk.

## Qualifications
The page is source-bounded to one Marketplace Tech episode. It does not reconstruct Akron's full industrial history, company chronology, migration patterns, or later economic development.
