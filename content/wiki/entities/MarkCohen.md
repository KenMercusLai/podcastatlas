# Mark Cohen

## Overview

Mark Cohen is a philosopher interviewed in [[a-good-walk-spoiled-a-good-walk-spoiled|A Good Walk Spoiled]] to explain why gradual replacement creates a genuine institutional-identity problem rather than an answer supplied automatically by the Ship of Theseus.

## Current Profile

Cohen contrasts a component-based or mereological view, under which replacing every part can produce a different object, with a spatiotemporal-continuity view, under which an object persists through gradual change. He emphasizes that counterexamples trouble both theories, leaving no simple resolution that can decide public policy by itself.

Gladwell applies that ambiguity to member-owned golf clubs whose equity shares turn over one at a time. Cohen's contribution supports the narrower conclusion that the county must choose which form of continuity matters for reassessment; it does not establish that one legal rule is philosophically mandatory.

## Key Characteristics

- Philosopher used as an expert on identity through gradual change.
- Explains component-based and continuity-based accounts of persistence.
- Treats the Ship of Theseus as genuinely unresolved by a universal intuition.
- Helps separate philosophical explanation from legal and policy choice.
