# Mark Cohen

## Overview

Mark Cohen is a philosopher interviewed by [[MalcolmGladwell]] to explain identity through change. The season-two trailer introduces the Heraclitean river and [[ShipOfTheseus|Ship of Theseus]] as open philosophical problems; [[a-good-walk-spoiled-a-good-walk-spoiled|A Good Walk Spoiled]] later applies that framework to gradual institutional turnover.

## Current Profile

Cohen first appears in [[introducing-revisionist-history-season-two]] as the expert who moves from [[Heraclitus]]' changing river to a ship whose boards are replaced one at a time. The preview uses the puzzle tentatively, asking whether identity can persist while material composition changes.

In the later golf episode, Cohen contrasts a component-based or mereological view, under which replacing every part can produce a different object, with a spatiotemporal-continuity view, under which an object persists through gradual change. He emphasizes that counterexamples trouble both theories, leaving no simple resolution that can decide public policy by itself.

Gladwell applies that ambiguity to member-owned golf clubs whose equity shares turn over one at a time. Cohen's contribution supports the narrower conclusion that the county must choose which form of continuity matters for reassessment; it does not establish that one legal rule is philosophically mandatory.

## Key Characteristics

- Philosopher used as an expert on identity through gradual change.
- Connects the Heraclitean river image to the Ship of Theseus persistence problem.
- Explains component-based and continuity-based accounts of persistence.
- Treats the Ship of Theseus as genuinely unresolved by a universal intuition.
- Helps separate philosophical explanation from legal and policy choice.
