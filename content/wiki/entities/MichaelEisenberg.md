# Michael Eisenberg

## Overview
Michael Eisenberg is the urologist, clinician, and researcher interviewed by [[AndrewHuberman]] about male sexual function, fertility, urinary health, prostate health, and reproductive aging.

## Current Profile
In the available source, Eisenberg's clinical frame joins reproductive and general health without reducing one to the other. He treats semen analysis, erectile function, hormone testing, urinary symptoms, medication exposure, physical findings, age, sleep, heat, substance use, and cardiometabolic risk as inputs that may justify evaluation. He repeatedly distinguishes population association from individual prediction and lifestyle foundations from prescription treatment.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses objective testing and clinical history to counter appearance-based or internet-driven assumptions about hormones, fertility, and sexual function.
- Frames male fertility evaluation as relevant both to conception and to detecting broader health problems.
- Treats persistent erectile dysfunction as commonly vascular or neurologic and therefore potentially useful for broader risk assessment.
- Preserves uncertainty around sperm trends, chemical and phone exposure, cannabis effects, paternal-age risk, and several treatment mechanisms.
- Distinguishes exogenous testosterone from fertility-preserving endocrine approaches through their different effects on LH, FSH, intratesticular testosterone, and sperm production.

## Evidence
- Assessment style: [[improving-male-sexual-health-function-fertility-dr-michael-eisenberg-scim8414501670]] emphasizes semen analysis, hormone testing, symptom history, vascular screening, examination, and clinician review rather than visual prediction or self-diagnosis.
- Fertility and whole-body health: [[improving-male-sexual-health-function-fertility-dr-michael-eisenberg-scim8414501670]] connects lower semen quality with later disease and healthcare use while describing cases in which fertility evaluation revealed other conditions.
- Sexual and urinary medicine: [[improving-male-sexual-health-function-fertility-dr-michael-eisenberg-scim8414501670]] covers erectile therapies, prostate-related urinary symptoms, pelvic-floor treatment matching, varicocele, Peyronie's disease, and testicular warning signs.
- Uncertainty discipline: [[improving-male-sexual-health-function-fertility-dr-michael-eisenberg-scim8414501670]] labels sperm decline controversial and qualifies evidence on phones, cannabis, paternal age, penile-length trends, and post-finasteride symptoms.
