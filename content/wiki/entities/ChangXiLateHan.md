# 昌豨 / Chang Xi (late Han)

## Overview
昌豨 / Chang Xi is an East Sea / Donghai local armed leader whose profile runs from support for [[LiuBei|刘备]] after Liu Bei retakes [[Xuzhou|徐州]], through surrender under [[ZhangLiao|张辽]]'s guarantee, to repeated rebellion and execution by his old acquaintance [[YuJin|于禁]].

## Current Profile
Hanji 1040 makes Chang Xi evidence that Liu Bei's breakaway created a wider regional realignment rather than only one city seizure. His support helps explain why Liu Bei's force can expand to tens of thousands and why Cao Cao's advisers were right to fear releasing Liu Bei.

Hanji 1054 turns Chang Xi into a more unstable local-force case. [[CaoCao|曹操]] sends [[XiahouYuan|夏侯渊]] and Zhang Liao to besiege him in Donghai; after months of resistance and Cao-side grain pressure, Zhang Liao reads his hesitation, negotiates through old ties and guarantee, and brings him to surrender. Cao appoints Chang Xi as Donghai taishou.

Hanji 1076 supplies the previously summarized endpoint. The episode presents Chang Xi as repeatedly treating surrender as a bargain that preserves his followers and leverage. When Yu Jin and Xiahou Yuan break his position, he again relies on an old relationship and surrenders to Yu Jin. Yu Jin applies the stated non-pardon rule for surrender after encirclement and executes him, showing that personal trust could open negotiations earlier but could not indefinitely override enforcement after repeated revolt.

## Key Characteristics
- East Sea / Donghai armed leader associated first with Liu Bei's post-Che-Zhou Xuzhou shift.
- Represents local-force adhesion around Liu Bei's renewed autonomy in Hanji 1040.
- Appears later as a repeatedly shifting, opportunistic armed actor under Cao-side pressure.
- Surrenders when siege pressure, prior acquaintance, promised office, and Zhang Liao's personal guarantee make submission credible.
- Does not become a stable loyalty case; later rebellion and execution qualify the incorporation.
- Misjudges an old friendship as protection against a standing surrender rule after encirclement.

## Qualifications
The name normalization remains source-scoped because Hanji 1040 and Hanji 1076 render the name as "昌熙" in places. Hanji 1054's claim about Chang Xi's exact kinship with Lü Bu is also kept source-bounded; the stable wiki claim is that prior acquaintance with Zhang Liao made persuasion easier. Hanji 1076's characterization of three rebellions, preservation of his own troops as a repeated bargaining demand, exact camp totals, and brick-inscription economic explanation remain source-scoped. His appointment after surrender should not be read as durable pacification because the later source records renewed rebellion.

## What Changed
- Added Hanji 1076's final siege, surrender to Yu Jin, and execution under the non-pardon rule.
- Clarified the difference between Zhang Liao's earlier guarantee-backed incorporation and the later enforcement boundary after renewed revolt.
