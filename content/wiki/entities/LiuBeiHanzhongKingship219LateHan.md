## Overview

The 219 CE post-Hanzhong kingship settlement is the episode's account of how [[LiuBei|刘备]] converts territorial victory into royal title, succession, offices, military commands, and further northern expansion while inheriting new coordination and status risks.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1165-zhangji-zhuan-didiao-caishi-dingji-shengcun-zhihui-lr7x5qltfi_ikktqabowt1garphl|Hanji 1165]] places Liu Bei's declaration as King of Hanzhong at Mianyang in the seventh lunar month of Jian'an 24 / 219 CE. Because the title is self-established rather than granted by Emperor Xian, the episode emphasizes a memorial to the emperor and the return of seals for the Left General and Yicheng village marquis offices associated with Cao Cao's earlier authority.

The settlement then distributes responsibility. Liu Shan becomes heir, [[WeiYan|魏延]] receives Hanzhong, Fa Zheng becomes shangshuling, and Guan Yu, Zhang Fei, Ma Chao, and Huang Zhong receive senior generalships. At the same time, the Fangling–Shangyong advance enlarges a polity already divided among Yizhou, Hanzhong, and Jingzhou. The episode therefore treats the moment as both Liu Bei's political high point and the start of a support-and-status problem that becomes visible around Guan Yu.

## Key Characteristics

- Converts the Hanzhong campaign victory into a new royal claim and internal political hierarchy.
- Uses memorial, seal return, succession, office, and command allocation to express legitimacy and authority.
- Extends territorial pressure through Fangling and Shangyong while retaining a submitting local administrator.
- Entrusts Hanzhong to Wei Yan and Jingzhou to Guan Yu, distributing major theaters among strong commanders.
- Creates rank-comparison tension around Huang Zhong and structural support risk across separated territories.

## Qualifications

The page rests on one structured episode summary rather than the royal memorial, appointment documents, or a full institutional study. The exact ceremony, legal meaning of returned seals, rank equivalence, territorial control, troop allocation, and motives behind appointments remain source-scoped. Calling the moment the realization of the Longzhong Plan or Liu Bei's career peak is an interpretive compression; it does not establish operational integration or durable control.

## What Changed

- Created an event profile joining kingship, legitimacy, succession, command allocation, and expansion.
- Preserved the episode's contrast between visible political high point and hidden coordination risk.
