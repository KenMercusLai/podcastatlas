## Overview

Pope Sixtus IV was the pope whose conflict with [[LorenzoDeMedici|Lorenzo de' Medici]] over finance, Imola, and ecclesiastical appointment aligned papal interests with the [[PazziFamily|Pazzi family]] before the 1478 conspiracy and the subsequent war against Florence.

## Current Profile

Sixtus initially confirmed the [[MediciBank|Medici Bank]] as papal banker but broke with Lorenzo after the Medici refused to finance Imola's purchase for Girolamo Riario. The Pazzi supplied the money and received the papal account, while Sixtus's appointment of Francesco Salviati as archbishop of Pisa deepened conflict.

The episode preserves ambiguity about authorization. Sixtus reportedly insisted that he did not want killing while strongly demanding political change in Florence; the mercenary Montesecco nevertheless treated the exchange as papal approval. After Florentines killed Salviati and other clergy, Sixtus excommunicated Lorenzo and senior officials, imposed an interdict, expelled the Medici bank, seized assets, and supported military pressure with Naples.

## Key Characteristics

- Papal patron whose family and territorial interests affected Florentine politics.
- Reassigned papal banking business from the Medici to the Pazzi.
- Used ecclesiastical appointment as part of a widening political conflict.
- Gave an ambiguous directive interpreted by a conspirator as authorization.
- Escalated the failed coup into spiritual, financial, and military punishment.

## Qualifications

The source does not settle whether Sixtus authorized assassination. His reported words, Montesecco's testimony, Girolamo Riario's agency, and the legal and canonical grounds for papal retaliation require comparison with primary and specialist sources. This profile does not cover Sixtus's broader papacy or patronage.
