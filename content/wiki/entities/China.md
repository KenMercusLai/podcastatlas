# China

[[vol-120-riben-yiliao-tixi-de-bengkui-yu-zhongsheng-yiyuan-yaopin-yibao-sanjiao-kunju-de-yizhong-jiefa-lp-p5p1cybbldphe0qajteggsitt]] adds China as the comparison case for Japanese healthcare reform. The episode argues that [[Japan]]'s aging, public insurance pressure, drug procurement, tiered diagnosis, community care, and state-led reform path are relevant because China faces similar hospital, drug, and insurance tensions.

[[165-yinfa-chuanliu-yong-youmo-miandui-laonian-zanmen-lai-xie-dayoushi-944213646]] adds China as the historical and contemporary comparison point for [[RespectForEldersTradition]]. The episode moves from [[Japan]]'s [[YinfaChuanliu|《银发川柳》]] into Chinese elder-respect language, early filial/old-age characters, [[Mengzi|孟子 / Mencius]], [[Confucius|孔子]], tax and corvee relief for elders, attendants, care institutions, and ceremonial elder banquets, while also warning that contemporary China needs practical care and technology access rather than slogans alone.

[[vol-118-danshen-renshi-yanglao-zhinan-bushi-gudu-de-daimingci-shi-gengzao-zhangwo-rensheng-de-zhudongquan-lhvkuveznxd4citlwf5marzaj-wt]] adds China as the future-facing comparison case for single aging. The episode treats China's changing family structure, uncertain retirement cash-flow calculations, urban service gaps, property and reverse-mortgage constraints, and uneven home-care capacity as reasons to prepare [[SoloAgingPlanning]] before the high-care stage arrives.

[[no-201-zhongguo-gaotie-jianshi-976314772]] adds China as the main case for [[ChineseHighSpeedRail]]. The episode connects the late-1970s railway gap, [[JinghuHighSpeedRail]] demand, [[WheelRailVsMaglev]], [[ProcurementLedTechnologyTransfer]], [[HighSpeedRailSystemIntegration]], [[Hexiehao]], [[Fuxinghao]], and [[CR450]] to a broader industrial-capacity story, while also emphasizing [[StrongmanInfrastructureGovernance]], [[InfrastructureLaborBurden]], corruption, and the [[WenzhouTrainCollision]] as costs of fast buildout.

[[119-anlesi-xianchang-zhengyin-shengming-ruci-zhengui-770771087]] adds China as the [[WangMingchen]] / [[PuLiansheng]] assisted-death case setting in [[AnlesiXianchang|《安乐死现场》]]. The episode says Wang requested help for his mother's painful late-stage illness in 1986, then later wanted euthanasia for himself after developing cancer but faced the absence of relevant legislation and hospital refusal, making China a case for [[AssistedDyingLaws]], [[LawMoralityBoundary]], and [[EndOfLifeAutonomyAndDignity]].

[[building-things-and-breaking-things-in-china-summer-school-world-tour]] adds China as the main case for [[DanWang]]'s [[EngineeringState]] thesis. The episode links rapid construction, manufacturing power, poverty reduction, [[Guizhou]] bridges and airports, [[ChinaRealEstateDebtCycle]], [[ChinaYouthUnemployment]], low fertility pressure, and [[ChinaLowRedistributionState]] into one political-economy frame: state capacity can build astonishing physical systems while still misallocating capital, debt, and social support.

[[why-economists-got-free-trade-with-china-so-wrong]] adds China as the source of import competition behind the U.S. [[ChinaShock]]. The [[PlanetMoney]] episode treats China's post-2001 trade growth as a case where aggregate free-trade gains coexisted with concentrated U.S. manufacturing loss, then links the repair debate to [[TradeAdjustmentAssistance]], [[BlanketTariffLimit]], and targeted [[StrategicIndustrialPolicy]] for future sectors.

[[dark-times-for-cubas-economic-experiment]] adds a narrower China-Cuba support detail. The [[PlanetMoney]] episode says [[Cuba]] developed its relationship with China during fuel shortages and received about a million bikes, placing China inside Cuba's search for socialist or communist allies after the [[SovietUnion]] stopped supplying cheap oil.

[[tech-20260312-0312-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260312-0312-mp-tech-pod-128]] adds China as a comparison case for [[CrossBorderCryptoCapitalFlight]]. [[GilLuria]] says people have used [[Bitcoin]] to get around restrictions on moving capital out of China, linking the episode's Iran-focused crisis discussion to the wiki's existing [[CapitalAccountInvestmentRestrictions]] branch.

[[tech-20260710-tech-pod-128-tech-20260710-tech-pod-128]] adds China as an advanced-model access-control case. The episode says China has reportedly considered restrictions on foreign access to its most powerful AI models and names [[Alibaba]] and [[ByteDance]] as companies involved in discussions reported by [[ThomsonReuters|Reuters]].

China appears in [[latin-lessons-the-donroe-doctrine-boost-6a4233e26b6690f46aae4e3d]] as the external power whose earlier investment in [[LatinAmerica]] helps explain renewed [[UnitedStates]] attention to the region. The episode says Chinese investment rose until around 2018 and then flattened, but Chinese firms and projects remain central to the regional influence contest.

In the source, China is less a domestic-policy subject than a strategic counterparty. [[BYD]]'s Brazil factory, mining ventures, and a proposed Chile-Hong Kong undersea cable show how industrial projects, infrastructure, and [[CriticalMineralsGeopolitics]] become instruments of influence.

[[how-to-get-what-greenland-has-with-permission]] adds China as the rare-earth processing benchmark behind U.S. concern over [[Greenland]]. The [[PlanetMoney]] episode says China processes roughly 90 percent of rare earths, tried to enter Greenland through airports, an abandoned naval station, and a rare-earth mining investment, and exposed U.S. industrial dependence when export restrictions disrupted heavy rare-earth supply. This extends [[CriticalMineralsGeopolitics]] into [[RareEarthProcessingBottleneck]] and [[StrategicAccessWithoutAnnexation]].

[[battlefield-rare-earths-how-the-us-lost-to-china]] adds China as the long-run rare-earth industrial-policy case behind that benchmark. The source says China moved from a dirty, small-scale industry around a rare-earth office in 1975 into global dominance by training scientists and engineers, offering low-cost finance, encouraging domestic competition, restricting ore exports, and pushing refining and processing inside China. The episode also adds the 2010 [[Japan]] export shock and the 2025 U.S. tariff response as [[RareEarthExportLeverage]] cases.

[[fear-jerker-americas-ai-backlash-6a3cf783d760508ebaecd9fd]] adds China as a domestic family-law and demography case. The episode says the state wants to keep people married amid population decline and falling birth rates, but [[ChinaDivorceRestrictions]] and [[MarriageExitFriction]] can make young people, especially women, less willing to enter marriage.

[[peace-fire-further-us-iran-strikes-6a4f7acffe878dc8e27e6fbf]] adds China as an early [[FilialPietyLaws]] case. The episode says China passed a law after [[Singapore]] and later required children to visit elderly parents "often", with possible fines or jail.

[[vol-262-qu-xibanya-mai-zuqiu-julebu-yichang-huangdan-de-shangye-maoxian-992614191]] adds China as a football-development and sports-investment case. The source says the Chinese football gold-money cycle made [[ChinesePlayerOverseasArbitrage]] look plausible, but China's smaller registered-player base and thinner competition system made the underlying [[YouthFootballDevelopmentSystem]] gap hard to overcome through overseas placement alone.

[[no-203-businiao-lanshili-979295393]] adds China as a private-aviation and early private-enterprise case. [[ChinesePrivateAirlineOpening]] created room for companies such as [[EastStarAirlines]] and [[SpringAirlines]], but the source emphasizes that entry still depended on regulation, local-government support, aircraft financing, and the ability to survive [[PrivateAirlineFailureModes]] when shocks arrived.

[[142-chanpin-tiyan-xue-riben-quanqiu-yingxiao-xue-hanguo-feng-tou-quan-1-142-1]] adds China as the efficiency and infrastructure side of an East Asian consumer comparison. The episode says China is stronger in internet ordering, instant retail, hardware, fast construction, and digital service efficiency, but weaker in [[AuthenticNeighborhoodExperience]] and [[LongTermPlaceOperation]] when districts become over-marketed, homogeneous, or too focused on check-in traffic. It also raises [[InboundTourismBrandDiscovery]] as a possible consumer-brand opportunity as foreign tourists encounter Chinese products and stores directly.

[[e232-canyin-chuhai-you-xin-zhaor-ma-cong-dingtaifeng-yu-gongcha-naxia-meiguo-chudai-xiaoguan-liaoqi-3bb14dd4-0b13-4173-9d5b-0e63bf98ae1d]] adds China as the origin market whose restaurant and tea-drink speed can become misleading abroad. The source argues that Chinese consumer-chain strengths in digital operations, menu speed, and dense domestic competition do not automatically translate to the [[UnitedStates]], where [[USRestaurantRealEstateConstraint]], local labor rules, and [[RestaurantCulturalLegibility]] matter more.

[[tech-20260211-0211-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260211-0211-mp-tech-pod-128]] adds China as a lunar-race actor. The episode says China aims to land humans on the [[Moon]] by 2030 and has not signed the [[ArtemisAccords]], placing it inside [[LunarResourceGovernance]] debates over safety zones, transparency, resource access, and side-by-side operation with U.S.-led partners.

[[e229-cong-shougong-zuofang-dao-quanqiu-diyi-zhongguo-dongli-dianchi-nixi-shi]] adds China as the main case in power-battery industrialization. The source argues that China's battery advantage came from [[TenCitiesThousandVehicles]], [[PowerBatteryWhitelist]], [[LocalBatteryIndustrialClusters]], dense suppliers, and factory-level [[BatteryManufacturingKnowHow]], with [[CATL]], [[BYD]], and [[ATL]] as central company examples.

[[tech-20260407-0407-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260407-0407-mp-tech-pod-128]] adds China as the EV price-parity comparison case. [[ColinMcCarrick]] of [[BloombergNEF]] says EVs in China are now sticker-price competitive with internal-combustion vehicles, partly because integrated supply chains and improved [[LithiumIronPhosphateBatteries|LFP batteries]] lowered costs enough to affect consumer prices. This extends the battery branch from industrial capability into [[ElectricVehiclePriceParity]].

[[tech-20251231-1231-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20251231-1231-mp-tech-pod-128]] adds a later global-market comparison. The source says EV growth in China has been huge and supported by subsidies that helped make EVs cheaper, while Chinese companies are exporting EVs to other parts of the world. It also notes that Chinese EV sales growth is slowing somewhat, keeping the transition strong but not frictionless.

[[65-longwang-zhinu-1931-nian-de-changjiang-hongshui-658100922]] adds China as a Republican-era disaster-history case through the 1931 [[YangtzeRiver|Yangtze]] flood. The source connects [[Hubei]], [[Wuhan]], [[Hankou]], [[Gaoyou]], the [[NationalistGovernment]], and relief institutions to [[NaturalHazardSocialDisaster]], showing how flood risk became mass suffering through wetland loss, dikes, war, famine, disease, religion, and relief mismatch.

[[vol-113-cong-jiqian-ye-zhiku-wenjian-zhong-goule-telangpu-2-0-zhizheng-jihua-beihou-de-ren-jigou-sixiang-he-mailuo-lihnknc-3om5evexocb6a6zdwueu]] adds China as a trade and industrial-competition reference inside the Trump 2.0 policy discussion. The episode argues that once U.S. hawks frame trade deficits, offshoring, and manufacturing loss as [[SupplyChainSovereignty]] and defense-industrial problems, China becomes harder to engage through ordinary business logic alone.

[[vol-123-telangpu-de-duideng-guanshui-an-buzhi-shi-yi-chang-daxing-fucongxing-ceshi-lm6bkiqgyyefwompxon7lo6cnqp6]] adds China as the exposed export and policy-response case in the April 2025 tariff shock. The source argues that China is hit through direct U.S.-bound exports, East/Southeast Asian transshipment chains such as [[Vietnam]], and possible third-market compliance with U.S. pressure; it then shifts the practical market question toward [[ChinaPolicyEasingPivot]], [[ChinaFiscalExpansionChannels]], and [[RMBExchangeRatePolicy]].

[[vol-112-yici-feigongshi-de-2024-fansi-he-2025-zhanwang-duihua-lanxiaokang-x-mouyiling-lkasjtodqgmnbpyambqoxl-fcsyv]] adds China as the active policy and asset-pricing subject in a 2025 investment debate. The episode argues that China should not be reduced to a Japan-style stagnation analogy if it can build a different economic circle, alter distribution through [[LaborShareConsumptionRebalancing]], repair industrial ecology through [[ChinaSupplySideClearing]], maintain agency over [[RMBExchangeRatePolicy]], and create external demand through [[BeltAndRoadExternalDemand]].

[[vol-111-guanyu-2025-nian-de-si-ge-caixiang-lgaira5qw1fhic4qwihrimed6q9g]] adds China as the center of three annual guesses: [[ChinaLocalDebtResolution]] as a possible way to loosen the local-fiscal and confidence loop, [[ChinaCorporateAntiInvolution]] as a policy response to overcapacity and global resistance, and [[ChinaDivergentTechnologyRoute]] as a path through semiconductors, electric vehicles, and AI. The episode's broader frame is [[InternalStabilityConfidenceRepair]]: domestic confidence and institutional room matter before external strategy can stabilize.

[[vol-115-quanqiu-hongguan-he-ziben-shichang-2025-zhanwang-duanqi-wenti-bu-jiejue-jiu-meiyou-zhongqi-he-changqi-le-lrfl2e5nuf7-ubq-shybqytjhuhx]] adds China as the central macro and asset-pricing case in a 2025 outlook. The source connects [[ChinaPolicyEasingPivot]], [[ShortTermDemandBeforeLongTermReform]], [[ChinaFiscalExpansionChannels]], [[HouseholdBalanceSheetRepair]], [[LaborShareConsumptionRebalancing]], [[ChinaNarrativeSplit]], and [[AHShare2025Barbell]] to the claim that policy must repair near-term demand before long-term reform or productivity narratives become durable market support.

[[vol-102-aoguo-jiuye-bingheqi-de-riben-nianqingren-qu-nali-xunzhao-xingfugan-chuantai-qingdao-kuaima-lmpluij7rcfq49cxcyb65x3f7zfn]] adds China as the comparison side of a Japan-youth discussion. The episode uses Chinese university confidence surveys, mortgage prepayment, social-media labels, and "上岸" pressure to connect [[MacroNarrativeAnxiety]], [[GraduationAnxiety]], [[CareerShoreMyth]], [[HouseholdBalanceSheetRepair]], and [[AlgorithmicLabeling]] to [[YouthHappinessAfterGrowth]].

[[vol-108-riben-wuda-zonghe-shangshe-zhongfan-wutai-zhongyang-lhamp3hn14iihcicpa8po5dvh6gq]] adds China as the comparison case for failed or incomplete [[JapaneseSogoShosha|sogo shosha]] replication. The source says China experimented with comprehensive trading-company models in the 1990s, but argues that the Japanese model could not be copied by importing the business shell alone because it depended on surrounding finance, industry, government boundaries, [[KeiretsuBusinessGroups|keiretsu]]-like trust, and long-term commercial relationships.
