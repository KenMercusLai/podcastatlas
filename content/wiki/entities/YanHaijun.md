## Overview
严海军 / 阎海军 / Yan Haijun enters the wiki as the [[HuzuoHuyou|忽左忽右]] guest whose new book on [[LongshanGansuShaanxi|陇山 / 龙山]] turns a familiar northwest region into a historical-geography problem. The source title uses 阎海军, while the body repeatedly uses 严海军; the page preserves both forms as a source-scoped name variance.

## Current Profile
Yan is presented as a non-fiction writer who had previously worked on rural China and local society through books such as 《牙边报告》 and 《陇中手艺》. In this episode, his method shifts toward historical geography: starting from personal and regional memory, he asks how mountains, railways, ancient roads, passes, local names, poems, and field walking can explain large historical processes.

His central contribution in this source is not a complete biography but an interpretive frame. He argues that [[LongshanGansuShaanxi|陇山 / 龙山]] should be read as a key east-west hinge for Qin expansion, Western Han movement toward Hexi and the [[WesternRegionsProtectorate|Western Regions]], later Guanlong route systems, and enduring transport corridors in [[Gansu|甘肃]] and Shaanxi.

## Key Characteristics
- Non-fiction writer whose prior work focused on rural China, local society, and northwest field observation.
- Historical-geography interpreter who moves from personal railway memory into a larger account of mountains, rivers, roads, and state formation.
- Source speaker for the claim that [[LongshanGansuShaanxi|陇山 / 龙山]] is a durable east-west interaction axis.
- Field-oriented author who treats walking route traces, finding stone-paved road remains, and speaking with local people as evidence-shaping experiences.
- Cautious narrator where the evidence is thin, especially around Zhang Qian place memory and Qin-origin debates.
