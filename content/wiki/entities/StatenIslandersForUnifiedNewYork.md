# Staten Islanders for Unified New York

## Overview

Staten Islanders for Unified New York, or SIFUNY, was the local organization opposing [[StatenIsland|Staten Island's]] proposed departure from New York City.

## Current Profile

The group organized the remain side of the 1993 campaign. Through figures including [[DavidGoldfarb]], it argued that a new city would inherit expensive obligations for schools, police, detention, water, and other public services, and that the cited fiscal evidence showed Staten Island receiving more from New York City than it contributed.

Its difficulty was political as well as informational. Secessionists framed detailed cost warnings as fear and asked residents to trust neighbors over remote officials. The episode therefore treats SIFUNY as a case in which stronger material evidence did not supply an equally compelling story of local identity or future self-government.

## Key Characteristics

- Local organization campaigning to keep Staten Island in New York City.
- Advanced fiscal, service-capacity, and administrative arguments against independence.
- Raised concerns about minority political power and internal borough division.
- Faced a secession campaign organized around optimism, proximity, and local trust.
- Illustrates the persuasive limits of facts without a resonant affirmative identity.

## Qualifications

This profile relies on one structured episode summary, not organizational records, campaign literature, polling, or a complete debate transcript. It does not show that every remain voter shared the same fiscal, racial, or institutional reasons.

## What Changed

- Established SIFUNY as the organized remain campaign in the referendum debate.
- Distinguished the strength of its material evidence from the weakness of its affirmative political story.
