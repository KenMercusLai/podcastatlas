## Overview
The Federal Trade Commission is the U.S. regulator represented in the bounded sources through advertising enforcement, antitrust investigation, and the application of existing consumer-protection law to emerging products and markets.

## Current Profile
Across supplements, housing, and AI, the FTC acts after products, claims, or market structures have already developed. Its authority can reach deceptive advertising and anticompetitive conduct without a sector-specific statute, but enforcement speed, evidentiary access, institutional independence, and remedial power constrain its practical effect.

The new AI inquiry sharpens an unresolved dual-use problem. Existing law can provide a legitimate route to examine autonomous agents, marketing, and data handling, yet broad standards such as consumer harm can also create uncertainty or political leverage when agency composition and executive pressure are contested.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses existing consumer-protection authority to scrutinize potentially deceptive claims and unsafe or misleading product practices.
- Shares regulatory space with specialized agencies such as the [[FoodAndDrugAdministration|FDA]] and with antitrust enforcement by the [[USDepartmentOfJustice|U.S. Department of Justice]].
- Often intervenes after commercial activity has scaled, making enforcement delay part of the consumer-protection outcome.
- Can investigate new technology categories without waiting for AI-specific legislation.
- Depends on institutional independence, evidence access, and effective remedies for public credibility.

### Antitrust investigation
- [[can-trump-make-buying-a-home-more-affordable]] places the FTC beside the Justice Department in a presidential directive to investigate large institutional home purchases, while noting that the policy's national market reach may be limited.

### AI consumer protection
- [[tech-20261002-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261002-mp-tech-pod-128]] describes an inquiry into major AI labs covering agents, marketing, and data practices, while preserving uncertainty about ordinary enforcement versus political leverage.

## Qualifications
The sources do not establish that the AI targets violated law, that the inquiry is politically motivated, or that existing consumer-protection doctrine is sufficient for frontier-AI risk. The episode's account of two Republican commissioners after Democratic commissioners were fired is institutionally relevant but does not by itself prove the purpose or outcome of a specific investigation.

## What Changed
- Extended the FTC profile from supplements and housing into frontier-AI consumer protection.
- Added evidence access and executive testimony as potential benefits of an existing-law investigation.
- Added institutional independence and political leverage as explicit qualifications.
