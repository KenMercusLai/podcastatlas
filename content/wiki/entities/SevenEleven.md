# 7-Eleven

## Overview

7-Eleven is a global convenience-store chain used by the bounded source as a case of a proven retail format failing to reproduce its network economics in India.

## Current Profile

The source describes 7-Eleven as the world's largest convenience-store chain by store count, with roughly 87,000 stores at the cited year-end, but its Indian rollout never approached comparable density. A major local franchise partner contributed retail, warehouse, procurement, and distribution assets, yet 7-Eleven reportedly peaked at a little over 60 stores and closed all of them after five years.

The current judgment is that the failure cannot be reduced to weak localization. Local drinks and snacks were present, but [[KiranaRetail|kirana stores]] already offered cheap, relational neighborhood access and [[InstantRetail]] offered home delivery. Regional assortment fragmentation and high urban operating costs then made [[ConvenienceStoreDensityEconomics|store density]] more necessary while losses made continued expansion harder.

## Key Characteristics

- Global convenience-store brand dependent on frequent visits, rapid stock turns, and dense logistics networks.
- Entered India through a large local franchise partner rather than wholly owned expansion.
- Faced incumbent neighborhood retail and quick-commerce substitutes for immediate purchases.
- Tried localized drinks and snacks without establishing sufficient product differentiation.
- Failed to reach the store density needed to spread rent, logistics, inventory, and management costs.

## Evidence

- Indian entry and exit: [[gkwridoovnyyahvaqqtzrpda-gkwridoovnyyahvaqqtzrpda]] reports a 2021 first store in Mumbai, a peak above 60 stores, and closure of the entire network after five years.
- Partner capacity: [[gkwridoovnyyahvaqqtzrpda-gkwridoovnyyahvaqqtzrpda]] says the franchise partner already operated more than 10,000 retail stores and more than 200 warehouse and distribution facilities.
- Competitive format: [[gkwridoovnyyahvaqqtzrpda-gkwridoovnyyahvaqqtzrpda]] contrasts 7-Eleven with millions of kirana stores and a rapidly growing quick-commerce market.
- Operating economics: [[gkwridoovnyyahvaqqtzrpda-gkwridoovnyyahvaqqtzrpda]] connects high urban rent and store-level costs with a network too sparse to share logistics and management efficiently.

## Qualifications

The bounded source is a structured podcast summary, not a 7-Eleven filing, franchise contract, audited unit-economics record, store-level dataset, or account from Indian customers and operators. Store counts, partner assets, losses, market shares, and causal weights remain episode-scoped.
