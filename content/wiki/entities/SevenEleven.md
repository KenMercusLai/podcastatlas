# 7-Eleven

## Overview

7-Eleven is a global convenience-store chain represented by the bounded sources through two contrasting network cases: a sparse Indian rollout that closed after five years and an established Guangdong business planning another thousand stores plus differentiated private-label products.

## Current Profile

The India source shows why global brand scale and a large local partner do not automatically reproduce local convenience economics. Kirana stores already supplied cheap neighborhood access, [[InstantRetail]] supplied delivery, regional assortment was complex, and a peak of a little more than 60 stores could not spread logistics, rent, labor, inventory, electricity, and management costs.

An established Guangdong network shows the opposite side of the density question. 7-Eleven reportedly plans to grow from about 2,000 to 3,000 stores over four years while expanding private-label beverages and supplier-developed channel exclusives. The plan operates inside strong local franchise competition, so density and differentiated assortment remain coupled rather than interchangeable growth levers.

## Key Characteristics

- Global convenience-store brand dependent on frequent visits, rapid stock turns, and dense logistics networks.
- Franchise-led international operator whose partner assets do not guarantee local demand or network utilization.
- Competitor facing neighborhood stores, delivery platforms, and mature regional franchise systems.
- Retailer using private labels and supplier co-development to create channel-specific products.
- Chain with sharply different local positions: failed sparse entry in India and planned densification from an established Guangdong base.

## Evidence

- Indian exit: [[gkwridoovnyyahvaqqtzrpda-gkwridoovnyyahvaqqtzrpda]] reports a 2021 entry, a peak above 60 stores, and closure of the entire Indian network after five years.
- India mechanism: [[gkwridoovnyyahvaqqtzrpda-gkwridoovnyyahvaqqtzrpda]] connects kirana and quick-commerce substitutes, regional assortment complexity, urban operating cost, and insufficient density.
- Guangdong expansion: [[yumaoqiu-jiage-huiluo-wuyinliangpin-jiang-xiang-oumei-fuzhi-zhongguo-jingyan-gkwriueovjpsaflmdqt0zl6p]] reports a four-year plan to increase stores from about 2,000 to 3,000.
- Product differentiation: [[yumaoqiu-jiage-huiluo-wuyinliangpin-jiang-xiang-oumei-fuzhi-zhongguo-jingyan-gkwriueovjpsaflmdqt0zl6p]] says 7-Eleven is expanding private-label beverages and co-developing channel-specific products with suppliers.

## Qualifications

Both sources are structured podcast summaries rather than filings, franchise contracts, audited unit economics, store datasets, or customer and operator research. Store counts, partner capacity, losses, targets, private-label plans, and causal weights remain source-scoped. Guangdong expansion is a plan, and more stores improve density economics only if they add productive demand rather than duplicated cost.

## What Changed

- Added Guangdong expansion as a contrasting established-network case.
- Added private-label beverages and supplier co-development as assortment differentiation.
- Clarified that density and differentiated local demand must develop together.
