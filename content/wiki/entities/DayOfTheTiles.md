# Day of the Tiles

## Overview

The Day of the Tiles was the 7 June 1788 Grenoble uprising in which resistance to royal court reform became a general strike, street battle, and temporary collapse of local authority before the [[FrenchRevolution|French Revolution]]'s better-known events of 1789.

## Current Profile

The source connects the event to Grenoble's parlement-dependent legal economy, regional identity, and a downturn in glove-making. When royal troops moved to remove judges under [[EtienneCharlesDeLomenieDeBrienne|Brienne]]'s reforms, bells and protest escalated into a general strike and riot; residents threw roof tiles, troops fired and killed civilians, and the crowd seized the city and sacked the governor's house. The event matters in the episode because an elite constitutional dispute escaped elite control and generated the later [[AssemblyOfVizille|Assembly of Vizille]] program.

## Key Characteristics

- Began from enforcement of royal measures against the Grenoble parlement.
- Joined court-based livelihoods and regional identity to wider economic distress.
- Escalated from protest and strike to improvised urban combat and lethal troop fire.
- Left judges less in command of the crowd than carried along by it.
- Created the political opening for Mounier and the Assembly of Vizille.

## Evidence

### Local causes and escalation

- [[477-the-french-revolution-the-violence-begins-part-3-glt6535380991]] connects the parlement, legal occupations, glove-industry downturn, troop action, roof tiles, deaths, seizure of the city, and attack on the governor's house.

### Revolutionary threshold

- [[477-the-french-revolution-the-violence-begins-part-3-glt6535380991]] presents the event as the point where elite constitutional resistance was overtaken by mass urban action and followed by a representative program.

## Qualifications

- Treating the event as the beginning of the French Revolution is a useful but contested periodization rather than an uncontested date.
- Casualties, crowd intentions, the judges' degree of control, dialogue, and the precise balance of constitutional and economic motives remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Created an event profile distinguishing the Grenoble uprising from the constitutional program developed at Vizille.

## Relationships

- [[EtienneCharlesDeLomenieDeBrienne]] - minister whose court reform triggered the enforcement crisis.
- [[JeanJosephMounier]] - younger judge who converted the aftermath into organized constitutional politics.
- [[AssemblyOfVizille]] - meeting that followed the uprising and broadened its representative demands.
- [[RevolutionaryCrisisConvergence]] - wider interaction of economic distress, institutional conflict, political language, and coercive breakdown.
