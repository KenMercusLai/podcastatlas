## Overview

Cen Peng / 岑彭 is the Eastern Han commander whose Shu campaign profile currently rests on [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-711-ta-zhu-liuxiu-da-xia-banbi-jiangshan-weihe-si-fa-biequ-liib38zub0-tgrbtycmlqvohv-w|Hanji 711]]. He breaks the Shu defensive line through fast long-distance movement, captures Wuyang, threatens Guangdu and Chengdu, maintains strict anti-plunder discipline, and is assassinated by Gongsun Shu before the final victory he made likely.

## Current Profile

Hanji 711 presents Cen Peng as the commander who changes the western campaign's tempo. While [[ZangGongEasternHan|Zang Gong]] contains the Fu River line with surrendered Ba-Shu troops, Cen Peng leads elite Han forces by water from Jiangzhou, breaks Hou Dan's defense, turns through a long detour, and reaches Wuyang before Shu can realign.

His effect is strategic as much as tactical. Once Wuyang is captured and light cavalry moves rapidly toward Guangdu, [[GongsunShuEasternHan|Gongsun Shu]] can no longer rely on mountains, rivers, and long defensive distance to protect Chengdu. The source treats this as a "兵贵神速" case: speed makes a geography-based defense collapse from inside.

Cen Peng's death gives the episode its central irony. After Liu Xiu judges the Shu campaign largely settled, Gongsun Shu sends assassins again; Cen Peng stays overnight at Pengwang/Peng亡 because the hour is late and the troops are tired, and he is killed before the final Chengdu outcome. The same source stresses that his troops had not plundered and that Shu people later remembered him through submission tribute, family compensation, a Wuyang temple, and Pengshan place-memory.

## Key Characteristics

- Eastern Han commander whose rapid Shu breakthrough changes the campaign before Chengdu.
- Operational tempo actor who uses water movement, detour, surprise, and cavalry pressure rather than attrition alone.
- Disciplined conquest commander whose troops are remembered for not plundering local communities.
- Assassination victim whose death occurs after operational success but before final victory.
- Local-memory figure in Shu, tied to Ren Gui's tribute, family compensation, Wuyang temple memory, and Pengshan naming.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 711 and does not reconstruct Cen Peng's full career before the Shu campaign. The source's force counts, place-name forms, and timing should be treated as episode-bounded campaign evidence. Cen Peng's humane reputation is a source-supported contrast with Wu Han's later Chengdu conduct, not proof that all Han conquest armies behaved similarly.
