## Overview

Cen Peng / 岑彭 is an Eastern Han commander whose current profile now combines southern diplomatic pacification, a Jianwu 8 western-campaign retreat role, a Jianwu 11 water-front and bridge-breaking prelude, and the later Shu breakthrough and assassination. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-699-gan-yu-liuxiu-zhengxiong-de-ta-weihe-candan-shouchang-lth-8mndudj3khf9cnsouj86c1-z|Hanji 699]] shows him using friendship correspondence and proclamations to help bring [[JiaozhiCommandery|Jiaozhi]] and Jiangnan actors toward Liu Xiu. Hanji 707 then shows him in the Wei Xiao war, where his Xicheng siege pressure nearly succeeds and his rearguard preserves Han forces after [[WuHanEasternHan|吴汉]]'s food-short withdrawal. Hanji 710 shows [[LiuXiu|刘秀]] making Cen Peng the water-war authority at Jingmen, and Hanji 711 presents him as the commander who breaks the Shu defensive line through fast long-distance movement, strict discipline, and pressure toward Chengdu before [[GongsunShuEasternHan|公孙述]] has him assassinated.

## Current Profile

Hanji 699 gives Cen Peng a pre-campaign diplomatic layer. He writes to Jiaozhi Mu Deng Rang through personal friendship, and Qu Chong circulates Liu Xiu's orders across Jiangnan and the southern field. Deng Rang and other regional actors send tribute, and Liu Xiu enfeoffs them. This makes Cen Peng a conduit for pacification by letters and proclamations before the better-known siege and water-command profile.

Hanji 707 gives Cen Peng an earlier western-front layer. Liu Xiu sends Wu Han and Cen Peng against Xicheng after [[WeiXiaoEasternHan|隗嚣]] refuses surrender. Cen Peng's water attack raises pressure on the city, but Gongsun Shu-backed reinforcements break into Xicheng and rescue Wei Xiao. When food shortage and desertion force Han withdrawal, Cen Peng covers the rear so the commanders can retreat intact.

Hanji 710 supplies the missing water-front prelude. When Wu Han wants to dismiss water troops because they consume grain, Cen Peng argues that Gongsun Shu remains strong and that the water force should not be dispersed. Liu Xiu backs Cen Peng, saying Wu Han understands infantry and cavalry better than water warfare and that Jingmen affairs should follow Cen Peng's command.

The same source shows why that authority matters. Cen Peng recruits volunteers for the floating-bridge attack, promises top rewards for the first to mount the bridge, and uses Lu Qi's ship assault plus wind-driven fire to break the defensive works. After Ren Man is killed and Cheng Fan captured, Cen Peng enters deeper Shu territory, orders no plunder, wins local surrender, receives acting Yizhou authority, bypasses difficult Jiangzhou, seizes Pingqu grain, and prepares the split movement toward Chengdu.

Hanji 711 presents Cen Peng as the commander who later completes the tempo shift. While [[ZangGongEasternHan|Zang Gong]] contains the Fu River line with surrendered Ba-Shu troops, Cen Peng leads elite Han forces by water from Jiangzhou, breaks Hou Dan's defense, turns through a long detour, and reaches Wuyang before Shu can realign.

His effect is strategic as much as tactical. Once Wuyang is captured and light cavalry moves rapidly toward Guangdu, Gongsun Shu can no longer rely on mountains, rivers, and long defensive distance to protect Chengdu. The source treats this as a "兵贵神速" case: speed makes a geography-based defense collapse from inside.

Cen Peng's death gives Hanji 711 its central irony. After Liu Xiu judges the Shu campaign largely settled, Gongsun Shu sends assassins again; Cen Peng stays overnight at Pengwang/Peng亡 because the hour is late and the troops are tired, and he is killed before the final Chengdu outcome. The same source stresses that his troops had not plundered and that Shu people later remembered him through submission tribute, family compensation, a Wuyang temple, and Pengshan place-memory.

## Key Characteristics

- Diplomatic pacification conduit whose letters and proclamations help bring southern actors toward Liu Xiu before his later battlefield profile.
- Siege-pressure actor who can use terrain and water but remains vulnerable to outside relief forces.
- Rearguard commander who preserves retreat after food shortage and desertion undo a siege.
- Specialist water-front commander whose Liu Xiu-backed authority, reward incentives, fire attack, and pursuit open the Jingmen route.
- Eastern Han commander whose rapid Shu breakthrough changes the campaign before Chengdu.
- Disciplined conquest commander whose troops are remembered for not plundering local communities.
- Assassination victim whose death occurs after operational success but before final victory.

## Qualifications

This page does not reconstruct Cen Peng's full career before Xicheng or every step between southern pacification, Xicheng, and the Shu campaign. Hanji 699 gives the Jiaozhi/Jiangnan letter-and-proclamation layer but leaves Deng Rang, Qu Chong, Hou Deng, Wang Tang, Han Fu, Zhang Long, Tian Xi, Du Mu, and other southern actors source-scoped. Hanji 707 gives the western-front siege and retreat layer but not a full command hierarchy for every force at Xicheng and Shanggui. Hanji 710 supplies the Jingmen water-front, bridge attack, no-plunder discipline, and split-route setup, but its transcript stops before Cen Peng's own assassination is completed. Hanji 711 remains the stronger source for the Wuyang/Guangdu breakthrough, death, and local memory. Cen Peng's humane reputation is a source-supported contrast with Wu Han's later Chengdu conduct, not proof that all Han conquest armies behaved similarly.
