# William McKinley

## Overview

William McKinley is presented as a U.S. president whose policy bundle joined protective tariffs and industrial development to overseas expansion, with [[152-american-crusades-glt4400059090]] adding a religious interpretation of his decision to annex the [[Philippines]].

## Current Profile

[[vol-113-cong-jiqian-ye-zhiku-wenjian-zhong-goule-telangpu-2-0-zhizheng-jihua-beihou-de-ren-jigou-sixiang-he-mailuo-lihnknc-3om5evexocb6a6zdwueu]] uses McKinley as a historical analogy for [[DonaldTrump|Donald Trump]], emphasizing high tariffs, industrial protection, gold-standard commitments, attraction of skill and technology, weak domestic regulation, and territorial or political expansion. The analogy is a source interpretation rather than a settled equivalence between administrations.

[[152-american-crusades-glt4400059090]] focuses on the Philippines. It treats McKinley's reported prayerful deliberation and commitment to educate, uplift, civilize, and “Christianize” Filipinos as evidence that his Methodist faith may have helped him understand annexation as duty. Because Filipinos were already Catholic, the episode interprets “Christianize” as Protestant remaking. Religion therefore joins strategy, markets, race, and power without removing imperial coercion or Filipino resistance.

## Key Characteristics

- President associated with protective tariffs, industrial development, and a large domestic-market strategy.
- Historical reference point for a modern protectionist policy bundle rather than a simple tariff-only analogy.
- Expansionist leader whose presidency included U.S. acquisition and rule of the Philippines.
- Devout Methodist in the episode's account, with professed belief treated as a plausible influence on imperial reasoning.
- Advocate of a paternalist Protestant uplift frame toward an already Christian population.

## Evidence

### Protectionist policy bundle

- [[vol-113-cong-jiqian-ye-zhiku-wenjian-zhong-goule-telangpu-2-0-zhizheng-jihua-beihou-de-ren-jigou-sixiang-he-mailuo-lihnknc-3om5evexocb6a6zdwueu]] connects McKinley to high tariffs, industrial protection, gold-standard discipline, skill attraction, light domestic regulation, domestic-market leverage, and expansion.

### Religious and imperial reasoning

- [[152-american-crusades-glt4400059090]] presents McKinley's reported prayer, Methodist commitment, refusal of Filipino independence, Protestant meaning of “Christianize,” and annexation rationale as a case for taking religious motive seriously.

## Qualifications

The modern tariff analogy comes through one podcast's reading of outside material and does not establish institutional or historical equivalence with Trump. McKinley's reported private deliberation, its sincerity, and religion's causal weight remain source-scoped; prayer and uplift language neither exclude strategic or economic motives nor justify annexation, racial hierarchy, or the violent suppression of Filipino resistance.

## What Changed

- Reframed McKinley from a tariff analogy into a profile joining protectionism, territorial expansion, and religious imperial reasoning.
- Added the Philippines and Protestant uplift as qualified parts of his policy worldview.
