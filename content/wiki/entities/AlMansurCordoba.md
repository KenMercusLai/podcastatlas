# Al-Mansur / Almanzor (Cordoba)

## Overview

Al-Mansur, also known as Almanzor, is the Cordoban vizier Ibn Amir who exercised effective power under Hisham II and combined military success, religious repression, and political control with policies that increased the caliphate's later fragility.

## Current Profile

After the teenage Hisham II succeeded al-Hakam in 976, Ibn Amir captured effective rule and adopted the title al-Mansur in 981. The episode portrays him as a capable political and military operator hostile to Cordoba's perceived decadence. He purged books and scholars judged heretical and repeatedly campaigned against Christian territories, including Barcelona, Leon, and Santiago de Compostela.

His reliance on Berber forces brought battlefield capacity but also taxation, billeting, and ethnic resentment. The collapse came after his death rather than during his rule: succession through his sons, Sanchuelo's attempt to become heir, an Umayyad revolt, anti-Berber massacre, siege, and sack converted managed coercion into civil war.

## Key Characteristics

- Vizier and de facto ruler under the sidelined caliph Hisham II.
- Successful campaigner who adopted the title “the victorious one.”
- Sponsor of austere religious reform, censorship, and repression.
- Relied increasingly on Berber military forces from North Africa.
- Left a succession structure unable to contain court, dynastic, military, and ethnic conflict.
