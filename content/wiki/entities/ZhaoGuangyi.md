# 赵光义 / Song Taizong

Zhao Guangyi / 赵光义 is [[ZhaoKuangyin|赵匡胤]]'s younger brother and later Song Taizong. In [[169-songtaizu-zhisi-zhuying-fusheng-yinmoulun-zhong-de-renxing-mituan-shang-957052909]], he matters less as a fully resolved actor in [[ChenQiaoMutiny|陈桥兵变]] than as a problem for later historical memory.

The episode notes that some Taizong-era or later official accounts give Zhao Guangyi and [[ZhaoPu|赵普]] a larger role in planning Chenqiao and restraining violence. The hosts treat that emphasis cautiously, suggesting that it may have served Zhao Guangyi's later succession legitimacy by making him appear present at the dynasty's founding.

Zhao Guangyi therefore connects the episode's founding coup to its announced death-and-succession mystery. The issue is not only whether he personally plotted at Chenqiao, but how a later ruler's need for political capital can affect [[EvidenceBoundHistoricalRevision|source criticism]] and [[HistoricalMemoryContest|historical memory]].

[[170-songtaizu-zhisi-zhuying-fusheng-yinmoulun-zhong-de-renxing-mituan-xia-958158115]] moves him from memory problem to succession beneficiary. The source emphasizes his long control of Kaifeng politics, his Jin王 status, and the sudden sequence in which [[SongEmpress|宋皇后]] expects [[ZhaoDefang|赵德芳]] but [[WangJien|王继恩]] brings Zhao Guangyi into the palace.

The episode's portrait is mixed. Taizong has political skill and helps consolidate a civil-official order, but [[CandleShadowAxeSound|烛影斧声]], [[GoldenCabinetPledge|金匮之盟]], the deaths of [[ZhaoDezhao|赵德昭]] and Zhao Defang, and the fall of [[ZhaoTingmei|赵廷美]] make his rule look shaped by continuing insecurity over how he gained power.
