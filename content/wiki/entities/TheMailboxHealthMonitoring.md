# The Mailbox (Health Monitoring System)

## Overview

The Mailbox is a remote health-monitoring system developed by [[AziziSeixas]]'s [[UniversityOfMiami]] team as a distributed “physical in a box” for use in participants' homes.

## Current Profile

The system combines roughly seven relatively affordable devices that were not originally designed to communicate. APIs connect the devices, a provided phone transmits measurements, and a university command center reviews data and can arrange clinical escalation.

Reported examples include documenting mold with an air-quality sensor and detecting repeated overnight oxygen saturation below 80%. These cases illustrate potential value but do not establish population-level effectiveness or diagnostic validity.

## Key Characteristics

- Collects physiological and environmental data outside clinical facilities.
- Combines multiple devices through API-based interoperability.
- Uses a provided phone as the transmission link to a command center.
- Places clinical review and referral behind continuous sensing.
- Favors an affordable multi-vendor system over a single expensive proprietary device.

## Evidence

### Environmental observation

- [[exploring-life-saving-ai-tech-with-t-mobile-for-business]] reports an air-quality sensor being used to document mold after a participant's respiratory concerns had been dismissed.

### Physiological escalation

- [[exploring-life-saving-ai-tech-with-t-mobile-for-business]] reports repeated low oxygen-saturation readings prompting rapid physician and cardiology connection.

### System integration

- [[exploring-life-saving-ai-tech-with-t-mobile-for-business]] describes roughly seven devices, API “handshakes,” a provided phone, and University of Miami command-center review.

## Qualifications

The episode does not provide device names, validation data, false-alert rates, missing-data rates, clinical endpoints, costs, privacy and security architecture, or evidence of routine deployment beyond the study. Sensor signals can support screening and escalation but do not by themselves establish diagnosis or treatment need.

## What Changed

- Established the project profile as a multi-device home-monitoring and escalation system.
- Distinguished reported individual cases from validated population outcomes.
- Made interoperability, oversight, and access part of the system rather than treating sensors alone as the intervention.
