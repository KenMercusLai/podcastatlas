# The American Way of Killing

## Overview

*The American Way of Killing: The Invention of an Epidemic* is [[MalcolmGladwell]]'s book, presented in the interview as an argument that exceptionally high US gun violence was produced by historical, institutional, and policy choices rather than national inevitability.

## Current Profile

The interview describes a deliberately cross-cutting argument. The book disputes both a simple over-policing story and reliance on gun control as an adequate near-term answer. It joins conditional police-capacity expansion, community legitimacy, the history of gun rights and regulation, the expressive character of much homicide, and individualized intervention among a small at-risk network.

The later episode develops the book's constitutional-history branch through the [[StonoRebellion]], the [[SlavePatrolMilitiaSystem]], [[CarrieSullivan]]'s analysis of [[JamesMadison]]'s grammar, and [[CarlBogus]]'s account of Virginia ratification. This makes the slavery-linked claim more specific without turning a contested origin interpretation into settled history.

## Key Characteristics

- Treats American gun violence as historically made rather than inevitable.
- Shifts attention from mass shootings toward recurrent community gun violence.
- Makes a conditional case for more relational and preventive police capacity.
- Connects contemporary gun politics to slavery and frontier regulation.
- Distinguishes expressive violence from planned instrumental violence.
- Pairs long-horizon gun control with immediate individualized prevention.
- Develops its constitutional claim through combined institutional, linguistic, and ratification evidence.

## Qualifications

The page reflects two episode summaries, not a complete reading of the book. Comparative statistics, historical interpretations, causal claims, and the balance among policing, regulation, and social intervention remain episode-attributed. The detailed Second Amendment account is strongly framed and does not offer a balanced survey of competing scholarship.
