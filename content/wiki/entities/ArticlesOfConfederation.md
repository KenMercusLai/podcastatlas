# Articles of Confederation

## Overview

The Articles of Confederation are presented as the weak first governing framework of the independent United States, exposing the state-building problem left after victory in the [[AmericanRevolution|American Revolution]].

## Current Profile

In the episode, the Articles preserve a loose union but deny the center a proper executive and reliable tax-raising power. That weakness helps explain the constitutional turn associated with Hamilton and Madison: Americans who had resisted distant government increasingly wanted effective government of their own, while opponents feared that national authority and a powerful president could recreate monarchy.

## Key Characteristics

- First confederal framework discussed for the post-independence United States.
- Preserved strong state autonomy and weak national capacity.
- Lacked a proper national executive in the episode's summary.
- Did not give the center dependable tax-raising power.
- Became the institutional contrast motivating the Constitution.

## Evidence

- Weak center: [[350-the-triumph-of-george-washington-part-4-glt1619660676]] describes the new country as an uneasy confederation without a proper executive or national taxing capacity.
- Constitutional reaction: [[350-the-triumph-of-george-washington-part-4-glt1619660676]] connects Hamilton and Madison's push for stronger government to the shortcomings of the Articles.
- Authority tension: [[350-the-triumph-of-george-washington-part-4-glt1619660676]] frames the resulting debate as national capacity versus state autonomy and fear of elective monarchy.

## Qualifications

The source provides a compressed constitutional interpretation. It does not summarize the Articles article by article, distinguish congressional requests from direct taxation in detail, or cover their diplomatic, territorial, and administrative achievements.

## What Changed

- Created the Articles as the institutional bridge between independence and constitutional redesign.

## Relationships

- [[AmericanRevolution]] - conflict whose victory created the need for a durable national framework.
- [[UnitedStatesConstitution]] - stronger successor framework motivated partly by confederal weakness.
- [[GeorgeWashington]] - national figure whose authority helped stabilize the constitutional transition.
- [[RepublicanRestraintLeadership]] - leadership model that made stronger executive power less threatening.
