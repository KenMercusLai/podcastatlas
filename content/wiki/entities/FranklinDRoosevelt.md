# Franklin D. Roosevelt

## Overview

Franklin D. Roosevelt was the United States president whose wartime role in the supplied sources spans public morale leadership, responsibility for strategic choices and their indirect costs, and the political opposition directed at him by [[EzraPound]].

## Current Profile

[[dchha65-supernova-in-the-east-iv-dchha65-supernova-in-the-east-iv|Supernova in the East IV]] presents Roosevelt in early 1942 as a leader managing cumulative defeat after Pearl Harbor. His radio address acknowledged likely losses while maintaining confidence that the United States would move to the offensive and win.

The episode also places leadership inside moral and institutional limits. West Coast fear accompanied the incarceration of Japanese Americans, while the Doolittle Raid lifted American morale but exposed scarce carriers and preceded devastating Japanese retaliation in China. Strategic action and inaction both carried risk, but explaining the dilemma does not excuse rights violations or erase displaced civilian costs.

In a separate literary context, [[i-robot-ai-and-consciousness-6a880840b0d5e24ea40c7015]] identifies Roosevelt as the American president attacked in Pound's wartime Italian radio broadcasts, part of the conduct behind Pound's treason indictment.

## Key Characteristics

- Used radio to combine acknowledgment of bad news with confidence in eventual victory.
- Treated public morale as a strategic requirement during military setbacks.
- Exercised wartime authority within a democracy vulnerable to fear-driven rights violations.
- Faced moral responsibility for indirect and displaced consequences of symbolic military action.
- Served as a political target for Ezra Pound's pro-fascist wartime propaganda.

## Evidence

- Wartime reassurance: [[dchha65-supernova-in-the-east-iv-dchha65-supernova-in-the-east-iv]] describes Roosevelt's February 1942 broadcast, anticipated losses, and promise of eventual offensive victory.
- Strategic and moral responsibility: [[dchha65-supernova-in-the-east-iv-dchha65-supernova-in-the-east-iv]] connects his administration's wartime setting to Japanese American incarceration, the Doolittle Raid, scarce-carrier risk, and reported Japanese retaliation in China.
- Propaganda target: [[i-robot-ai-and-consciousness-6a880840b0d5e24ea40c7015]] identifies Roosevelt and American entry into the Allied war as targets of Pound's Italian radio broadcasts.

## Qualifications

These sources cover one 1942 narrative and one literary-obituary segment, not Roosevelt's full presidency. The episode does not independently establish the Doolittle Raid's complete decision record, the exact Chinese civilian toll, a direct causal chain from coastal attacks to incarceration, or Roosevelt's private reasoning. Describing morale leadership does not excuse rights violations or make indirect consequences uniquely attributable to one person.

## What Changed

- Expanded Roosevelt from a propaganda target into a wartime leader managing defeat, reassurance, strategic risk, and moral responsibility.
- Added the tension between defending democratic institutions abroad and violating citizens' rights under domestic war fear.
