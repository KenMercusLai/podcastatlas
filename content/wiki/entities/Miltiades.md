# Miltiades

## Overview

Miltiades appears in the wiki as the Athenian commander whose experience with Persian warfare shapes the defensive containment and offensive gamble at Marathon.

## Current Profile

The source presents Miltiades as the strategic mind behind Athens' response to the Persian landing. He argues that the Athenians should march quickly to block the road from Marathon to [[Athens]], not because battle is immediately desirable, but because containment prevents the Persians from moving freely on the city.

His second decision is riskier. When the Athenians learn that Persian cavalry has left the plain and the force may be divided, Miltiades argues for a dawn attack. The vote is close, with Callimachus as the decisive supporter. Miltiades then thins the center, strengthens the wings, and accepts the danger that the center may buckle so that the wings can win the flanks and turn inward.

## Key Characteristics

- Miltiades is presented as experienced in Persian warfare and therefore unusually able to anticipate Persian options.
- His first aim is containment: block the road from Marathon to Athens and deny the Persians an easy march.
- His offensive decision depends on the reported absence of Persian cavalry.
- His tactical design uses strong wings and a thin center to match the Persian frontage.
- The episode frames his plan as successful but extraordinarily risky.

The profile is source-scoped to Marathon Part 2. It does not cover Miltiades' wider career, later trial, or all debates over his exact tactical authorship. The cavalry absence and battle vote are preserved as this source's reconstruction.
