## Overview

应奉 / Ying Feng is now a two-source 165 CE memorialist under [[EmperorHuanOfHan|汉桓帝]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-868-ying-le-ye-shi-shu-jia-donghan-hougong-zhengyan-ji-lmmjgx23nlkeqr3mkzliij-zwe2v|Hanji 868]] shows him blocking Huan's plan to make [[TianShengLateHan|田圣 / 田胜]] empress by arguing from formal status and precedent, while [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-869-liying-weihe-bei-chengwei-tianxia-diyi-kaimo-lltckcioe1n9v91yy4c9vvwmz4mo|Hanji 869]] shows him persuading Huan to pardon [[LiYingLateHan|李膺]] and other punished officials by translating injustice into court security, talent retention, and frontier-risk language.

## Current Profile

Ying Feng's current profile is a compact intercession profile rather than a full biography. Hanji 868 shows him using formal-rank and precedent language against a ruler's private favorite: Tian Sheng may be loved by Huan, but Ying argues that low origin and insufficient status make the empress title improper. [[ChenFanLateHan|陈蕃]] supports the objection, and Dou Miao receives the title instead.

Hanji 869 then shows Ying Feng using state-capacity language in a punishment review. He submits a memorial arguing that loyal officials and capable generals are the state's vital support. He does not rely only on sympathy for the accused; he lists prior service, invokes earlier Han precedents for reusing flawed but useful officials, and stresses that three frontier directions are unstable enough to make competent personnel a matter of imperial safety.

The two episodes therefore use Ying Feng as a communication and decision-field example. In one case he converts harem desire into formal-status limits; in the other he converts punishment review into personnel security. Both arguments work because they address what Huan's decision must publicly mean, not only what the speaker personally feels.

## Key Characteristics

- Memorialist who reframes punishment review as a state-security and personnel-capacity problem.
- Formal-status defender who blocks a favored consort's elevation to empress.
- Official who succeeds after an earlier tearful petition fails to move Emperor Huan.
- Precedent user who cites earlier Han rulers' willingness to recall or promote imperfect but useful officials.
- Frontier-risk interpreter who argues that capable officials are needed because border conditions may change suddenly.
- Narrow but consequential actor whose argument enables Li Ying's restored enforcement role.
- Narrow but consequential actor whose argument also clears the formal path for Dou Miao's empress title.

## Qualifications

This page is not a full biography of Ying Feng. The current evidence comes from two short episode summaries and uses him as an intercession case. The exact office title, the complete list of officials he defended, and the broader career context should remain source-scoped until additional source notes require expansion.

## What Changed

- Hanji 868 adds an earlier harem-status memorial in which Ying Feng blocks Tian Sheng's elevation and helps Dou Miao receive the empress title.
- Hanji 869 preserves Ying Feng's role as the effective memorialist behind Li Ying's 165 CE pardon and restoration.
