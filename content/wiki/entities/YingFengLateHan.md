## Overview

应奉 / Ying Feng enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-869-liying-weihe-bei-chengwei-tianxia-diyi-kaimo-lltckcioe1n9v91yy4c9vvwmz4mo|Hanji 869]] as the official whose memorial persuades [[EmperorHuanOfHan|汉桓帝]] to pardon [[LiYingLateHan|李膺]] and other punished officials. His role is source-scoped but important: he succeeds where a prior emotional petition fails by translating injustice into court security, talent retention, and frontier-risk language.

## Current Profile

Ying Feng's current profile is a single-source intercession case. The source says he submits a memorial arguing that loyal officials and capable generals are the state's vital support. He does not rely only on sympathy for the accused; he lists prior service, invokes earlier Han precedents for reusing flawed but useful officials, and stresses that three frontier directions are unstable enough to make competent personnel a matter of imperial safety.

The episode therefore uses Ying Feng as a communication and decision-field example. His memorial does not deny that officials can have faults, but it argues that remembered service, state need, and impending danger should govern punishment. In the source's sequence, this framing immediately changes Emperor Huan's decision and opens the way for Li Ying's return as Sili xiaowei.

## Key Characteristics

- Memorialist who reframes punishment review as a state-security and personnel-capacity problem.
- Official who succeeds after an earlier tearful petition fails to move Emperor Huan.
- Precedent user who cites earlier Han rulers' willingness to recall or promote imperfect but useful officials.
- Frontier-risk interpreter who argues that capable officials are needed because border conditions may change suddenly.
- Narrow but consequential actor whose argument enables Li Ying's restored enforcement role.
