# Seventh Generation

## Overview

Seventh Generation is the responsible household-products company built by [[AlanNewman]] and [[JeffreyHollender]] from an abandoned environmental mail-order catalog and later acquired by [[Unilever]].

## Current Profile

The company combined environmental principles with practical customer education, product performance, health and safety claims, and retailer economics. Rapid catalog growth exposed it to recession and operating fragility, after which Jeffrey led a capital-intensive shift toward wholesale paper, cleaning, and diaper products. The company is also a dual founder-governance case: Alan and Jeffrey were removed in different periods after relationship and board alignment failed, even though the business survived and grew.

## Key Characteristics

- Early environmental brand that made recycled or unbleached attributes visible rather than concealing them.
- Customer-value translator that sold savings, health, safety, and performance alongside environmental benefit.
- Company that moved from catalog dependence to wholesale retail distribution despite selling the catalog when it still represented most revenue.
- Purpose-driven business that openly acknowledged the limits of making disposable consumption merely less harmful.
- Governance case in which complementary founders and later board relationships broke down under stress.

## Qualifications

The founder episode is not a complete institutional history and gives little direct voice to employees, retailers, investors, directors, or later executives. Reported revenue, order, valuation, acquisition, and ownership figures remain source-scoped. Greener product design should not be treated as proof that disposable consumption becomes environmentally harmless.
