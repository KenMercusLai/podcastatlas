# Hébertists

## Overview

The Hébertists were the radical faction around [[JacquesHebert|Jacques Hébert]] that pressed for stronger economic controls, surveillance, de-Christianization, denunciation, and punishment of alleged food profiteers.

## Current Profile

The faction tried to outflank central revolutionary government through Parisian militancy. It connected scarcity and popular distress to demands for executions and supported a de-Christianization campaign that [[MaximilienRobespierre|Robespierre]] regarded as both religiously wrong and politically dangerous. Hébert's call for insurrection on 4 March 1794 failed when the Commune, National Guard, and most sections withheld support; the government then assembled the faction with unlike defendants as a supposed foreign network and executed its leaders.

## Key Characteristics

- It claimed to defend working people against scarcity and withholding merchants.
- It favored expanded surveillance, denunciation, price control, and execution.
- It promoted de-Christianization and Temples of Reason.
- It challenged the Convention from a more radical Parisian position.
- Its failed insurrection exposed the growing capacity of central authority to defeat pressure from the left.

## Evidence

### Radical economic and religious program

- [[710-the-terror-death-at-the-guillotine-part-4-glt5038102951]] links the faction to food politics, surveillance, denunciation, executions, priestly renunciation, and the Festival of Reason.

### Failed insurrection and execution

- [[710-the-terror-death-at-the-guillotine-part-4-glt5038102951]] records the limited response to Hébert's March call, the faction's arrest, its constructed foreign-network trial, and execution on 24 March.

## Qualifications

- The source does not establish a disciplined party, complete membership, or uniform agreement on every radical demand.
- Food scarcity and public anger were real even when the conspiracy explanation attached to them was exaggerated.
- The exact popular reach of Hébertism and causal role of de-Christianization remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- A canonical profile now records both the Hébertists' radical program and the institutional failure of their final insurrection.
