# Hyde Parker

## Overview

Sir Hyde Parker was the overall British fleet commander during the 1801 [[BattleOfCopenhagen1801|Battle of Copenhagen]], with [[HoratioNelson]] leading the attack.

## Current Profile

Parker's profile is defined by the limits of command visibility. He entrusted Nelson with the assault but, observing from farther from the main action as British ships grounded and losses mounted, hoisted Signal 39 to discontinue. The source presents the order as a judgment intended to give Nelson a route out of a worsening battle, not simply as cowardice. Nelson did not repeat it, whereas [[EdwardRiou]] eventually obeyed and was killed during withdrawal. Parker therefore anchors a case in which a formal commander's incomplete view, a subordinate's local judgment, and another subordinate's rank-based obligation produced divergent decisions.

## Key Characteristics

- Overall commander who delegated the Copenhagen attack to Nelson.
- Distant observer whose view of the action differed from that of the ships engaged.
- Issuer of the disputed withdrawal signal.
- Example of how humane or cautious intent can produce dangerous ambiguity in battle.

## Qualifications

The bounded source centers Nelson and gives limited access to Parker's reasoning. The order's exact intent, Parker's information, how withdrawal would have unfolded, and later judgments of his command remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Established Parker as a distinct commander rather than only the sender of Nelson's famous ignored signal.
- Framed the withdrawal order through information limits and delegated command.
