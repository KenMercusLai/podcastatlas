## Overview

樛太后 / Queen Dowager Jiu is the Han-born queen of [[ZhaoYingqiNanyue|赵婴齐]] and mother of [[ZhaoXingNanyue|赵兴]]. Her court-recognized position gives Han a possible channel inside [[Nanyue|南越]], but her later attempt to move the kingdom toward closer incorporation is defeated by [[LyuJiaNanyue|吕嘉]]'s locally rooted power and coup.

## Current Profile

Hanji 349-2 supplies the succession prequel. Zhao Yingqi asks the Han court to recognize Lady Jiu as queen and Zhao Xing as heir. After Zhao Yingqi dies, Zhao Xing succeeds and she becomes queen dowager. The host calls her a person sent by [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] and an internal ally for eventual incorporation, but the source does not independently establish a formal agent commission.

Hanji 352-1 shows the later policy conflict. Queen Dowager Jiu is more willing than her son and the Han envoys to remove Lü Jia when he appears at a royal banquet. The attempt fails: the envoys hesitate, Zhao Xing restrains her, and Lü Jia leaves. The scene makes her an emblem of forceful action in the episode, while the operational balance of palace power remains unclear.

Once the Han mission under [[HanQianqiuWesternHan|韩千秋]] and her brother [[JiuLeNanyue|樛乐]] enters Nanyue, Lü Jia alleges that her Han origin, sexual relationship with an envoy, and wish to transfer treasure and people prove betrayal. His faction kills her alongside Zhao Xing and the Han envoys. Those sexual and enslavement claims remain hostile mobilization rhetoric rather than neutral biography.

## Key Characteristics

- Han-born Nanyue queen, later queen dowager, and mother of Zhao Xing.
- Court-recognized participant in Zhao Yingqi's succession arrangement.
- Potential channel for closer Han influence inside the Nanyue royal center.
- Royal advocate of closer submission to Han.
- Intended initiator of a direct move against Lü Jia at the banquet.
- Coup victim whose origin and intimate life are weaponized in hostile rhetoric.

## Qualifications

The supplied summaries write her name as “纠氏／纠太后”; the wiki normalizes it to the received form 樛氏／樛太后. Hanji 349-2's claim that Wudi “sent” her and used her as an inside agent is stronger than the later source's evidence of Han origin and pro-incorporation policy, so formal agent status remains source-scoped. Neither source supplies her personal name, full marriage history, independent court network, or direct perspective. The failed banquet does not prove that killing Lü Jia was feasible or sufficient, and his sexual and enslavement allegations require independent evidence before use as fact.

## What Changed

- Added her court-recognized marriage and queen-to-queen-dowager succession before the later coup.
- Qualified the claim that she was a formally planted Han agent while retaining her demonstrated pro-incorporation alignment.
