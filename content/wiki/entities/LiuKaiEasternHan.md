## Overview

刘恺 / Liu Kai enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-796-donghan-mingchen-vs-diwang-shenbianren-shui-neng-ying-lkyzp2v-cjysd65syoyuldlbdb5p|Hanji 796]] as the former situ appointed taiwei under [[EmperorAnOfHan|汉安帝]]. His durable significance here is the accepted memorial against extending a corrupt official's punishment to innocent descendants, which grounds [[PunishmentPersonalResponsibilityLimit|刑罚个人责任边界]].

## Current Profile

Hanji 796 places Liu Kai in the same troubled An-era court that ignores Yang Zhen and Zhai Gui on private favor. After Ma Ying dies, Liu Kai is appointed taiwei despite the episode noting that he had previously spoken of retirement.

His substantive intervention concerns the Fan Bin case. The court considers following the Shusun Guang precedent, under which descendants of a corrupt official are barred from office for two generations. Liu Kai argues from Spring and Autumn moral reasoning that rewards for goodness may extend to descendants, but punishment for evil should stop with the offender.

The intervention succeeds. [[ChenZhongLateHan|陈忠]] agrees with Liu Kai, and Emperor An accepts the taiwei's position. This makes Liu Kai a useful contrast inside Hanji 796: An can ignore broad correction against favorite households while still accepting a bounded legal argument when it is framed as cautious punishment.

## Key Characteristics

- Former situ appointed taiwei in the early An-era sequence.
- Anti-corruption voice who rejects turning punishment into hereditary office exclusion.
- Legal remonstrant who distinguishes reward extension from punishment extension.
- Actor whose memorial succeeds where Yang Zhen and Zhai Gui's broader anti-favorite memorials fail.
- Disambiguated Eastern Han figure kept separate from the modern Liu Kai page.
