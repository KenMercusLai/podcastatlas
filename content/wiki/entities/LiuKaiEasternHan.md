## Overview

刘恺 / Liu Kai is an Eastern Han royal-collateral figure and senior official whose current profile now begins before office: he refuses hereditary succession so his younger brother [[LiuXianEasternHan|Liu Xian]] can inherit, turns the refusal into public reputation through flight and risk, and only later appears in An-era office movement, mourning defense, and punishment-limit memorials.

## Current Profile

Hanji 772 supplies the earliest current layer. Liu Kai refuses to inherit Liu Ban's marquisate because Liu Xian is the son of the principal wife. The episode presents the refusal as legally awkward and reputation-making: Liu Kai leaves home and risks becoming a fugitive rather than accept succession, [[JiaKuiEasternHan|Jia Kui]] defends the act as exceptional moral yielding, and [[EmperorHeOfHan|Emperor He]] allows Liu Xian to inherit while later drawing Liu Kai into office.

Hanji 786 supplies the next current office layer. After disasters and court turnover under [[EmpressDowagerDengLateHan|Empress Dowager Deng]] and [[EmperorAnOfHan|Emperor An]], Zhang Min is removed from sikong and Liu Kai moves from taichang to sikong. This places Liu inside the regency's early disaster-and-office pressure before his later policy interventions.

Hanji 788 adds the next office-transition layer. After Situ Xia Qin is removed in the source's office sequence, Liu Kai moves from sikong to situ, while [[YuanChangLateHan|Yuan Chang]] moves into sikong. The paired notices make Liu a senior court actor across multiple regency crisis years rather than only a named memorialist.

Hanji 789 places Liu Kai under Deng in 116-117 CE. Amid deaths of high officials and court instability, he argues that senior officials should be allowed to complete three-year mourning rather than be forced immediately back into office. The court accepts the proposal, creating an earlier pro-ritual layer before the later Hanji 797 mourning cancellation debate.

Hanji 796 places Liu Kai as former situ appointed taiwei under An. His substantive intervention concerns the Fan Bin case. The court considers following a precedent that bars descendants of a corrupt official from office for two generations. Liu Kai argues that rewards for goodness may extend to descendants, but punishment for evil should stop with the offender. [[ChenZhongLateHan|Chen Zhong]] agrees, and Emperor An accepts the narrower punishment rule.

## Key Characteristics

- Royal-collateral figure whose public profile begins with hereditary-title refusal rather than appointment.
- Converts ritual yielding into political reputation only after Jia Kui and Emperor He treat the case as exceptional.
- Senior court official whose An-era profile includes taichang, sikong, situ, and later taiwei movement.
- Appears in office through disaster-era replacement before later legal and ritual interventions.
- Uses ritual and moral precedent rather than factional leverage as his visible argument style.
- Defends three-year official mourning before the later court attempts to cancel it.
- Rejects hereditary extension of corruption punishment to innocent descendants.

## Qualifications

The current profile is bounded to five source notes. Hanji 772 adds an earlier Emperor-He title-refusal episode, while Hanji 786, 788, 789, and 796 supply the later An-era office, mourning, and punishment layers. The source's motive reading is preserved as interpretation rather than proof of Liu Kai's inner intent, and the transcript name 刘凯 is normalized here to 刘恺. Zhang Min and Xia Qin remain source-scoped because neither is promoted to a standalone route here.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 772 as the earliest current layer: Liu Kai's hereditary-title refusal, flight, Jia Kui defense, and Emperor He's succession exception.
- Added Hanji 786's taichang-to-sikong appointment before Liu Kai's later sikong-to-situ movement.
- Reframed Liu Kai as a figure whose public capital begins in ritualized refusal before developing through senior office, mourning policy, and punishment-limitation counsel.
