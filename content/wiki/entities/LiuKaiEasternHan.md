## Overview

刘恺 / Liu Kai is an Eastern Han senior official whose An-era profile now includes two successful bounded interventions: an earlier defense of three-year official mourning and a later punishment-limit memorial protecting innocent descendants from hereditary exclusion.

## Current Profile

Hanji 789 places Liu Kai under the Deng regency in 116-117 CE. Amid deaths of high officials and court instability, he argues that senior officials should be allowed to complete three-year mourning rather than be forced immediately back into office. The court accepts the proposal, creating an earlier pro-ritual layer before the later Hanji 797 mourning cancellation debate.

Hanji 796 places Liu Kai as former situ appointed taiwei under [[EmperorAnOfHan|Emperor An]]. His substantive intervention concerns the Fan Bin case. The court considers following a precedent that bars descendants of a corrupt official from office for two generations. Liu Kai argues that rewards for goodness may extend to descendants, but punishment for evil should stop with the offender. [[ChenZhongLateHan|Chen Zhong]] agrees, and Emperor An accepts the narrower punishment rule.

## Key Characteristics

- Senior court official whose arguments are framed through ritual and moral precedent.
- Defends three-year official mourning before the later court attempts to cancel it.
- Rejects hereditary extension of corruption punishment to innocent descendants.
- Succeeds in bounded legal-ritual arguments even while broader An-era political correction often fails.
- Disambiguated Eastern Han figure kept separate from the modern Liu Kai page.

## Qualifications

The current profile is bounded to two An-era source notes. It does not yet provide a full biography of Liu Kai's earlier career or later service; it preserves the two episodes where his memorials succeed.
