## Overview

刘恺 / Liu Kai is an Eastern Han senior official whose An-era profile now begins with a 112 CE taichang-to-sikong appointment, then continues through sikong-to-situ movement, three-year mourning defense, and a punishment-limit memorial protecting innocent descendants from hereditary exclusion.

## Current Profile

Hanji 786 supplies Liu Kai's earliest current office layer. After disasters and court turnover under [[EmpressDowagerDengLateHan|Empress Dowager Deng]] and [[EmperorAnOfHan|Emperor An]], Zhang Min is removed from sikong and Liu Kai moves from taichang to sikong. This places Liu inside the regency's early disaster-and-office pressure before his later policy interventions.

Hanji 788 adds the next office-transition layer. After Situ Xia Qin is removed in the source's office sequence, Liu Kai moves from sikong to situ, while [[YuanChangLateHan|Yuan Chang]] moves into sikong. The paired notices make Liu a senior court actor across multiple regency crisis years rather than only a named memorialist.

Hanji 789 places Liu Kai under Deng in 116-117 CE. Amid deaths of high officials and court instability, he argues that senior officials should be allowed to complete three-year mourning rather than be forced immediately back into office. The court accepts the proposal, creating an earlier pro-ritual layer before the later Hanji 797 mourning cancellation debate.

Hanji 796 places Liu Kai as former situ appointed taiwei under An. His substantive intervention concerns the Fan Bin case. The court considers following a precedent that bars descendants of a corrupt official from office for two generations. Liu Kai argues that rewards for goodness may extend to descendants, but punishment for evil should stop with the offender. [[ChenZhongLateHan|Chen Zhong]] agrees, and Emperor An accepts the narrower punishment rule.

## Key Characteristics

- Senior court official whose An-era profile now includes taichang, sikong, situ, and later taiwei movement.
- Appears first through disaster-era office replacement before later legal and ritual interventions.
- Uses ritual and moral precedent rather than factional leverage as his visible argument style.
- Defends three-year official mourning before the later court attempts to cancel it.
- Rejects hereditary extension of corruption punishment to innocent descendants.
- Succeeds in bounded legal-ritual arguments even while broader An-era political correction often fails.

## Qualifications

The current profile is bounded to four An-era source notes. It does not yet provide a full biography of Liu Kai's earlier career or later service; it preserves the office transitions and the two episodes where his memorials succeed. Zhang Min and Xia Qin remain source-scoped because neither is promoted to a standalone route here.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 786's taichang-to-sikong appointment before Liu Kai's later sikong-to-situ movement.
- Reframed Liu Kai as a senior officeholder whose visible An-era profile starts inside disaster-era replacement and then develops through ritual and legal counsel.
