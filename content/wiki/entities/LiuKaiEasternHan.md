## Overview

刘恺 / Liu Kai is an Eastern Han senior official whose An-era profile combines high-office movement with two successful bounded interventions: defense of three-year official mourning and a later punishment-limit memorial protecting innocent descendants from hereditary exclusion.

## Current Profile

Hanji 788 adds Liu Kai's office-transition layer in the Deng-regency crisis field. After Situ Xia Qin is removed in the source's office sequence, Liu Kai moves from sikong to situ, while [[YuanChangLateHan|Yuan Chang]] moves into sikong. This confirms Liu Kai as a senior court actor before the later policy arguments.

Hanji 789 places Liu Kai under [[EmpressDowagerDengLateHan|Empress Dowager Deng]] in 116-117 CE. Amid deaths of high officials and court instability, he argues that senior officials should be allowed to complete three-year mourning rather than be forced immediately back into office. The court accepts the proposal, creating an earlier pro-ritual layer before the later Hanji 797 mourning cancellation debate.

Hanji 796 places Liu Kai as former situ appointed taiwei under [[EmperorAnOfHan|Emperor An]]. His substantive intervention concerns the Fan Bin case. The court considers following a precedent that bars descendants of a corrupt official from office for two generations. Liu Kai argues that rewards for goodness may extend to descendants, but punishment for evil should stop with the offender. [[ChenZhongLateHan|Chen Zhong]] agrees, and Emperor An accepts the narrower punishment rule.

## Key Characteristics

- Senior court official whose profile now includes sikong, situ, and later taiwei movement across the An-era sources.
- Uses ritual and moral precedent rather than factional leverage as his visible argument style.
- Defends three-year official mourning before the later court attempts to cancel it.
- Rejects hereditary extension of corruption punishment to innocent descendants.
- Succeeds in bounded legal-ritual arguments even while broader An-era political correction often fails.
- Disambiguated Eastern Han figure kept separate from the modern Liu Kai page.

## Qualifications

The current profile is bounded to three An-era source notes. It does not yet provide a full biography of Liu Kai's earlier career or later service; it preserves the office transitions and the two episodes where his memorials succeed. The Xia Qin name in Hanji 788 remains source-scoped because no standalone route is created here.
