# Xu Jiayin

## Overview
Xu Jiayin is the [[Evergrande]] founder used by the wiki to personify China's property boom, high-leverage developer finance, and the collapse of boom-era wealth confidence. The earlier Dan Wang episode uses him as a symbol of developer excess inside [[ChinaRealEstateDebtCycle]]; EP93 adds the life-and-company arc from rural Henan, steel-factory work, Shenzhen sales, and Guangzhou development to the 2017 richest-person peak and the episode's reported 2026 first-instance life sentence.

## Current Profile
The current profile is a founder-operating-model profile rather than a complete biography. EP93 presents Xu as someone who learned low-price, fast-sales property development through the Pengda project, then built Evergrande around speed, presale cash, debt, strict internal control, aggressive marketing, and confidence management. His strength in the source is not a single invention; it is the ability to assemble land, contractors, buyers, creditors, strategic investors, and public narratives into a rolling growth machine.

The same profile becomes a failure case when the assumptions reverse. Xu's 2008 survival depended on Hong Kong capital relationships, the 2009 listing enlarged the credit lever, and the 2017-2020 return-to-A-share plan added high-stakes strategic-investor obligations. When financing rules tightened and sales confidence broke, the source uses Xu's public promises to [[EvergrandeWealth]] investors and the later reported criminal punishment to mark the collapse of the "too big to fail" belief.

## Key Characteristics
- Founder associated with transforming a small Guangzhou developer into a national property conglomerate through speed, low pricing, presales, and leverage.
- Operator whose early career supplied the template for [[RealEstateHighTurnoverLeverage]]: lower entry price, fast sellout, and repeated capital turnover.
- Boom-era status figure who reached the 2017 richest-person peak while Evergrande's balance sheet was becoming more fragile.
- Confidence manager who used listing status, Hong Kong investor relationships, diversification stories, football spending, and political/business status to sustain belief in Evergrande.
- Crisis figure whose public wealth-product promises and reported 2026 first-instance sentence are source-framed as the end point of the same leverage cycle.

## Qualifications
This page remains source-bounded. EP93 supplies a narrative biography and legal endpoint but also cautions that many data points are online reference figures rather than audited research. The reported first-instance sentence, fines, asset figures, owner counts, and liquidation details are preserved as episode claims, not independently verified legal findings. The page does not attempt to settle Xu's complete culpability or the full court record.
