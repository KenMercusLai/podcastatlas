# Xu Jiayin

## Overview
Xu Jiayin is the [[Evergrande]] founder used by the wiki to personify China's property boom, high-leverage developer finance, and the collapse of boom-era wealth confidence. The earlier Dan Wang episode uses him as a symbol of developer excess inside [[ChinaRealEstateDebtCycle]]; EP93 adds the life-and-company arc from rural Henan, steel-factory work, Shenzhen sales, and Guangzhou development to the 2017 richest-person peak and the episode's reported 2026 first-instance life sentence. The Evergrande football source extends Xu's profile into sports: his scale-first style shaped [[GuangzhouFC]], [[EvergrandeFootballSchool]], the "全华班" promise, and the limits of using football as corporate and national prestige infrastructure.

## Current Profile
The current profile is a founder-operating-model profile rather than a complete biography. EP93 presents Xu as someone who learned low-price, fast-sales property development through the Pengda project, then built Evergrande around speed, presale cash, debt, strict internal control, aggressive marketing, and confidence management. His strength in the source is not a single invention; it is the ability to assemble land, contractors, buyers, creditors, strategic investors, and public narratives into a rolling growth machine.

The same profile becomes a failure case when the assumptions reverse. Xu's 2008 survival depended on Hong Kong capital relationships, the 2009 listing enlarged the credit lever, and the 2017-2020 return-to-A-share plan added high-stakes strategic-investor obligations. When financing rules tightened and sales confidence broke, the source uses Xu's public promises to [[EvergrandeWealth]] investors and the later reported criminal punishment to mark the collapse of the "too big to fail" belief.

In football, Xu is presented as both builder and distortion source. The source credits Evergrande with long-horizon academy investment and historic club achievement, while connecting Xu's maximalist style to an oversized early academy, salary escalation, diversification overreach, and national-team-linked spending that ordinary club economics cannot fully explain.

## Key Characteristics
- Founder associated with transforming a small Guangzhou developer into a national property conglomerate through speed, low pricing, presales, and leverage.
- Operator whose early career supplied the template for [[RealEstateHighTurnoverLeverage]]: lower entry price, fast sellout, and repeated capital turnover.
- Boom-era status figure who reached the 2017 richest-person peak while Evergrande's balance sheet was becoming more fragile.
- Confidence manager who used listing status, Hong Kong investor relationships, diversification stories, football spending, and political/business status to sustain belief in Evergrande.
- Crisis figure whose public wealth-product promises and reported 2026 first-instance sentence are source-framed as the end point of the same leverage cycle.
- Football patron whose "biggest and most" operating style produced both [[GuangzhouFC]] success and early [[EvergrandeFootballSchool]] scale errors.
- Boundary-blurring sports actor tied by the source to "全华班" ambition, "为国养士" logic, and naturalized-player spending.

- Systemic-symbol role: [[building-things-and-breaking-things-in-china-summer-school-world-tour]] places Xu Jiayin after the Desmond Shum property story and connects him to [[Evergrande]], debt, fraud allegations, prepaid apartments, and China's broader property cycle.
- Early life and operating template: [[ep93-yanjian-hengda-qi-gaolou-zhuanyan-shoufu-ru-gaoqiang-lhsjpbzdfjta0phfxrtnybq57o4c|EP93]] says Xu moved from Henan poverty and Wuyang Steel Factory work into Shenzhen and Guangzhou, where the Zhudao Garden project taught him small-unit, low-price, fast-turnover property sales.
- Evergrande formation and scaling: [[ep93-yanjian-hengda-qi-gaolou-zhuanyan-shoufu-ru-gaoqiang-lhsjpbzdfjta0phfxrtnybq57o4c|EP93]] describes Xu using Jinbi Garden, advertising, militarized management, nationwide expansion, the 2008 Hong Kong capital rescue, and the 2009 listing to build scale.
- Peak and belief environment: [[ep93-yanjian-hengda-qi-gaolou-zhuanyan-shoufu-ru-gaoqiang-lhsjpbzdfjta0phfxrtnybq57o4c|EP93]] says Xu reached the 2017 Hurun richest-person rank while Evergrande hired [[RenZeping]] and benefited from strong property-price optimism.
- Crisis endpoint: [[ep93-yanjian-hengda-qi-gaolou-zhuanyan-shoufu-ru-gaoqiang-lhsjpbzdfjta0phfxrtnybq57o4c|EP93]] says Xu promised Evergrande Wealth investors they should not lose everything, while the episode opens with the reported first-instance life sentence and company fines.
- Football patronage: [[vol-225-hengda-de-zuqiu-yichan-ft-panweili-gkwrijeozrpkafcpbatn-ivb]] links Xu's long-cycle ambition to the academy and "全华班" idea while criticizing the early mass-scale school model.
- Overextension lesson: [[vol-225-hengda-de-zuqiu-yichan-ft-panweili-gkwrijeozrpkafcpbatn-ivb]] uses football, autos, finance, water, film, and other diversification to support the lesson that Evergrande's reach exceeded its capacity.

## Qualifications
This page remains source-bounded. EP93 supplies a narrative biography and legal endpoint but also cautions that many data points are online reference figures rather than audited research. Vol.225 supplies football interpretation and reporting memory. The reported first-instance sentence, fines, asset figures, owner counts, liquidation details, naturalization costs, and football-policy motives are preserved as episode claims, not independently verified legal findings.
