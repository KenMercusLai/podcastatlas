# Galba

## Overview

Galba was the Roman emperor whose replacement of [[Nero]] failed to secure the Rhine armies and helped open the [[YearOfTheFourEmperors|Year of the Four Emperors]].

## Current Profile

[[12-days-neros-succession-and-the-fall-of-the-byzantine-empire-glt6148714296]] presents Galba as a ruler with formal senatorial recognition but fragile military legitimacy. He had declared against Nero in Spain and became emperor after Nero's suicide, yet his authority depended on armies whose expectations he did not satisfy.

The New Year oaths of AD 69 exposed that weakness. Rhine soldiers resented his refusal to let them plunder Gaul after their action against Julius Vindex and disliked a command style remembered as stern and ungenerous. Lower German troops swore reluctantly; Upper German soldiers rejected his images and redirected their oath to the Senate and Roman people while seeking another emperor. Galba's murder soon afterward confirms that ritual recognition without military allegiance could not stabilize succession.

## Key Characteristics

- Spanish governor who rebelled against Nero and received senatorial recognition as emperor.
- Ruler whose formal accession did not resolve the post-Neronian succession crisis.
- Severe commander unable to convert discipline into loyalty among the Rhine armies.
- Emperor rejected during the New Year oath ritual of AD 69.
- Early casualty of the military competition defining the Year of the Four Emperors.
