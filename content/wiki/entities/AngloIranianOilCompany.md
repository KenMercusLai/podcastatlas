# Anglo-Iranian Oil Company

## Overview

The Anglo-Iranian Oil Company was the British-linked oil enterprise that grew from the D'Arcy concession and made Iranian petroleum central to British naval and wartime strategy.

## Current Profile

[[259-iran-england-the-little-satan-glt8347276204]] presents the company as both the foundation of Iran's first major modern industry and a concentrated source of inequality and political resentment. Its Abadan-centered operations created industrial capacity, but profit-based royalties without Iranian inspection rights, preferential Royal Navy pricing, and board resistance to renegotiation made the company a symbol of compromised sovereignty before nationalization.

## Key Characteristics

- Developed from the D'Arcy oil concession after the 1908 discovery.
- Became strategically important when the Royal Navy shifted from coal to oil.
- Built large-scale industrial and refinery capacity around Abadan.
- Operated through disputed profit-based royalties and asymmetric access to company accounts.
- Resisted revisions that parts of the British Foreign Office considered necessary after the Second World War.
