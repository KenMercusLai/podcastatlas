# Brixton

## Overview

Brixton is a south London district represented here through postwar Caribbean settlement, entertainment culture, Black British publishing and activism, the 1981 riots, cricket memory, and C. L. R. James's final residence.

## Current Profile

[[211-london-people-part-3-glt1562041230]] links Caribbean settlement in Brixton to nearby arrival processing and comparatively affordable housing. It also places that migration within an older local ecology of music hall, performers, clubs, and entertainment rather than treating the district as culturally empty before Windrush-era change.

Railton Road later became both the *Race Today* base and a focal point in the 1981 riots. [[CLRJames]]'s move into a small flat above the magazine's offices made his final years part of this political geography. Cricket at the Oval and in local parks supplies a further, qualified memory of Caribbean presence whose later weakening the episode attributes partly to poor facilities.

## Key Characteristics

- Affordable postwar housing context for Caribbean settlement in London.
- District shaped by overlapping entertainment, migration, political, and community histories.
- Railton Road setting for *Race Today*, Black British activism, and the 1981 riots.
- Final home of C. L. R. James and site of his commemorative blue plaque.
- Place where West Indies cricket support and local sporting memory expressed Caribbean British belonging.

## Evidence

- Settlement geography: [[211-london-people-part-3-glt1562041230]] connects postwar Caribbean arrival routes and affordable housing to Brixton community formation.
- Cultural layering: [[211-london-people-part-3-glt1562041230]] places migration alongside older entertainment, club, and performance associations.
- Political geography: [[211-london-people-part-3-glt1562041230]] identifies 165 Railton Road as the *Race Today* base and situates the street within the 1981 riots.
- James and cricket memory: [[211-london-people-part-3-glt1562041230]] connects James's upstairs flat, the Oval, local West Indies support, and later deterioration of cricket facilities.

## Qualifications

This is a selective district profile from one conversational episode, not a full social, demographic, political, architectural, or sporting history. Housing affordability, migration routes, the meaning of “frontline,” riot geography, the scale and decline of cricket, facility conditions, and the representativeness of personal memory remain source-scoped.
