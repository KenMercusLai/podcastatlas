## Current Profile

The current source set gives Jing Fang a two-layer profile. In the religious-history source, he matters because Han astrology and omen interpretation can make Heaven's signs legible as political evidence. That page presents him as part of a broader field linking [[ChenweiPolitics|谶纬政治]], [[PortableDivinationSystems|portable divination systems]], and court criticism.

Hanji 488 adds that Jing Fang's danger is not only that he reads signs politically. He also proposes an administrative system that would assess officials and potentially remove incumbents. In the host's interpretation, this makes his death structurally overdetermined: a lower-positioned figure tries to advance a high-stakes accountability reform while [[HanYuandi|汉元帝]] trusts Shi Xian and the court has many reasons to resist assessment.

The episode's direct case sequence is private and procedural. Jing Fang tells Zhang Bo about his imperial conversations; Zhang Bo secretly records those words and suggests routing the reform through Liu Qing's petition. Once the plan leaks, Shi Xian can frame Jing Fang and Zhang Bo as conspiring, slandering state policy, and improperly involving a regional king. Yuan then orders their execution, while [[ZhengHongWesternHan|郑弘]] is also removed.

Jing Fang therefore now functions in the wiki as more than an astrologer. He is a case where omen-trained political diagnosis, reform ambition, private-speech exposure, weak coalition-building, and ruler-favorite power converge.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han Yi-learning and omen-politics figure whose technique makes celestial signs politically meaningful.
- Political critic who uses hidden or indirect signs to diagnose ruler and minister failure.
- Reform proposer whose kaogong plan threatens incumbent officials by turning performance into measurable evidence.
- Poor secrecy manager whose private disclosures to Zhang Bo become hostile political material.
- Lower-power reformer whose proposal lacks the protection, coalition, and timing needed for a system-level assessment reform.
- Execution victim in a case the source calls tragic but not a simple clean miscarriage of justice.

## Qualifications

This page preserves the older astrology layer without treating every technical claim about Jing Fang's influence as settled intellectual history. Hanji 488 is source-scoped to the host's reading of Jing Fang's death: the episode calls it tragic but not simply a wrongful case, and it does not supply a complete legal dossier, full received text of the kaogong proposal, or independent proof of Liu Qing's exact leak motive. The page also does not settle whether Jing Fang should have withdrawn in a "否" environment; it records that as the episode's interpretive frame.
