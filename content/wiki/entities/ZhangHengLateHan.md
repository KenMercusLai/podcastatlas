## Overview

张衡 / Zhang Heng now enters this wiki through two distinct layers. The 133-134 CE Shun-era sources present him as a learned adviser criticizing personnel criteria, mass removals, divided authority, and unreliable tuchen/chenwei evidence. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1128-meiyou-heikeji-guren-ruhe-diaocha-nimingxin-lqdm57jzw9wqo_umlasz2j3vlzqh|Hanji 1128]] adds a later-memory layer in which his *Er Jing Fu*, scientific reputation, and Taishi Ling career become cultural knowledge used inside [[GuoYuanInvestigatorLateHan|国渊]]'s investigation.

## Current Profile

In [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-815-kebei-gudai-zhongchen-de-jiafeng-zhishang-llkhbxo-63pjw-gk9pihnbfr94v|Hanji 815]], Zhang Heng argues that the Xiaolian route has drifted away from filial conduct toward scripture phrase-reading and memorial-writing. He warns that even an extremely filial person could be excluded if he lacks the newly emphasized literary and administrative techniques. The same source has him criticize the mass dismissal of commandery and kingdom administrators, arguing that officials selected through long effort should not be thrown away for small faults when disasters and unusual signs are already warning the ruler to correct policy.

In [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-816-xiangyao-naxia-nide-lingdao-qishi-hen-jiandan-lva3y4hgu7kn1z4jmhy6jn4-oyvc|Hanji 816]], Zhang Heng memorializes after the previous year's Luoyang earthquake and ground splitting. He interprets split earth as an image of divided authority and earthquake as an image of popular alarm, then warns Shun not to let decision power drift away from the throne through neglected government or private favor.

The same source makes him a critic of tuchen/chenwei texts. Zhang Heng points to anachronisms, mixed references, and the absence of such material from the cataloging work associated with Liu Xiang and Liu Xin. His position is not simple rejection of every omen language: he still uses earthquake as political warning, but he rejects uncontrolled prophetic texts as unreliable evidence.

Hanji 1128 does not add a new Zhang Heng action in the investigation's own time. It supplies a reception and biography layer: the anonymous letter's apparent connection to *Er Jing Fu* helps narrow possible authors; the episode also identifies Zhang Heng as a Nanyang scholar summoned to court in 111 CE, later Taishi Ling, inventor of the seismoscope, and one of four major Han fu writers. These details widen the page beyond court remonstrance but remain source-scoped until directly corroborated.

## Key Characteristics

- Learned official who treats personnel criteria as a state-capacity problem and distinguishes Xiaolian's moral basis from later text-and-writing filters.
- Remonstrant who uses unusual signs, earthquake, and ground-splitting imagery to criticize policy drift and divided authority.
- Source-critical opponent of tuchen/chenwei texts and their court authority.
- Distinguishes structured omen warning from fabricated or late prophetic books.
- Early Shun-era feedback actor whose warnings broaden the 133-134 CE court field beyond single-policy advice.
- Model of learned self-positioning: Hanji 815 praises his broad talent while the host treats his low-ambition restraint as survival wisdom.
- Literary-scientific reference point whose *Er Jing Fu* and technical reputation remain recognizable in later historical memory.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to three source notes. Hanji 815 and Hanji 816 establish Zhang Heng's selection, dismissal, authority, omen, and tuchen arguments; Hanji 1128 supplies a compressed retrospective rather than direct evidence for the investigation-era Zhang Heng. It still does not reconstruct his full scientific, literary, or official biography. The Hanji 816 transcript writes 张恒; the wiki normalizes to 张衡 because the Taishi Ling, earthquake memorial, and anti-tuchen critique match the received late-Han figure. The 111 CE summons, seismoscope attribution, “four fu masters” grouping, and asteroid reference remain source-scoped.
