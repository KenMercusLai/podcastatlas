# RMS Titanic

## Overview

RMS Titanic was the passenger liner whose third-class migrant world, iceberg collision, sinking, evacuation, rescue aftermath, inquiries, and cultural afterlife are examined across [[430-titanic-the-iceberg-strikes-part-4-glt2129072034]], [[431-titanic-nightmare-at-midnight-part-5-glt2941711597]], and [[432-titanic-the-survivors-part-6-glt4613169046]].

## Current Profile

The three episodes present Titanic as four connected historical objects: an unusually comfortable but class-ordered [[TransatlanticMigrantPassage|migrant passage]], a warning and collision failure, an evacuation and mass-casualty disaster, and a cultural story repeatedly made to carry meanings about gender, class, nation, technology, capitalism, hubris, and chance. Before impact, [[MaritimeWarningFragmentation|fragmented ice warnings]], mixed wireless traffic, accepted speed practice, and an exceptionally calm sea prevented available information from becoming effective avoidance. After impact, [[ThomasAndrews]]'s damage assessment, [[EdwardSmith]]'s ambiguous priority order, and the contrasting practices of [[WilliamMurdoch]] and [[CharlesLightoller]] show how design limits, incomplete belief, and local judgment shaped the final hours. [[RMSCarpathia|Carpathia]]'s rescue and the inquiries ground the aftermath materially, while [[BruceIsmay]], [[MasabumiHosono]], bereaved families, and film retellings show how moral memory exceeded the technical causes.

## Key Characteristics

- Passenger liner carrying a socially and nationally diverse migrant population in unusually comfortable contemporary third-class accommodation.
- Ship lost after fragmented ice warnings, continued speed, poor visual conditions, and a glancing iceberg collision converged on 14-15 April 1912.
- Disaster with disputed official death totals above 1,500 and especially heavy crew losses.
- Evacuation case structured by a short damage deadline, delayed risk recognition, lifeboat shortage, ambiguous orders, class access, and inconsistent procedures.
- Trigger for inquiry findings about ice warnings, speed, lifeboats, training, and industry norms.
- Catalyst for international maritime safety reform.
- Immediate object of mourning, blame, survivor stigma, and an enduring cultural afterlife amplified by [[JamesCameron]]'s 1997 film.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to three retrospective podcast episodes rather than passenger manifests, company archives, or technical inquiry records. Passenger totals, migration motives, warning transmission, collision mechanics, damage, timings, boat occupancy, casualty totals, class mechanisms, individual motives, reported stories, and metaphorical readings remain disputed or source-scoped. Comparative comfort before the collision does not negate unequal emergency access afterward.

## What Changed

- Added the third-class migrant world and the pre-collision chain linking fragmented warnings, normal speed practice, environmental visibility, and bridge response.
- Added the collision-to-plunge sequence, including progressive flooding, evacuation delay, ambiguous command, underfilled boats, and exposure deaths.
- Qualified simple class-cruelty and individual-cowardice stories without denying unequal access.
