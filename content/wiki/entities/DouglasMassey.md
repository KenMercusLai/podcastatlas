# Douglas Massey

## Overview

Douglas Massey is a sociologist presented in [[general-chapman-s-last-stand-general-chapman-s-last-stand|General Chapman’s Last Stand]] as a founder of the [[MexicanMigrationProject]] and a leading proponent of the claim that militarized border enforcement helped turn circular Mexican migration into longer-term settlement.

## Current Profile

The episode says Massey and [[JorgeDurand]] began systematically interviewing migrant households in Mexico during the 1970s and built detailed histories of first, last, and total United States trips. That longitudinal approach supports [[CircularMigration]] by treating returns as analytically important rather than counting arrivals alone.

Massey is also presented as a policy dissenter. He reportedly warned congressional committees that raising the cost and danger of crossing would suppress return trips and enlarge the settled undocumented population. The episode attributes to him a preference for less enforcement, expanded legal status, and credible permission to return after visiting Mexico, but those prescriptions and the associated counterfactual estimates remain bounded to the supplied summary.

## Key Characteristics

- Studies migration through household histories and repeated movement rather than entry events alone.
- Co-developed a long-running empirical project on movement between Mexico and the United States.
- Treats crossing cost as a behavioral variable that can change length of stay and family settlement.
- Challenged invasion framing and argued that more enforcement could increase the resident undocumented population.

## Evidence

- Research design: [[general-chapman-s-last-stand-general-chapman-s-last-stand]] describes household interviews and detailed trip histories accumulated over decades.
- Central finding: [[general-chapman-s-last-stand-general-chapman-s-last-stand]] attributes the circular-migration interpretation to the project’s data.
- Enforcement mechanism: [[general-chapman-s-last-stand-general-chapman-s-last-stand]] reports Massey’s claim that route closure and higher crossing costs reduced return movement.
- Policy intervention: [[general-chapman-s-last-stand-general-chapman-s-last-stand]] says he presented the argument to congressional committees and proposed greater legal mobility.

## Qualifications

The bounded source is a structured podcast summary, not Massey’s publications, datasets, testimony, or the later literature evaluating them. It does not establish how representative particular households were, how undocumented entries and departures were estimated, or how enforcement effects were separated from other economic, demographic, legal, and social causes.

- Created a focused profile around longitudinal migration measurement and the enforcement-settlement hypothesis.
