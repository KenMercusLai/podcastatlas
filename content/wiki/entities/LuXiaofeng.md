## Overview

陆小凤 / Lu Xiaofeng is [[GuLong|古龙]]'s investigator and social protagonist in [[DuelBeforeAndAfterGuLong|《决战前后》]], where he investigates deaths and a palace plot surrounding the duel between [[XimenChuixue|西门吹雪]] and [[YeGucheng|叶孤城]].

## Current Profile

The bounded source presents Lu as a contrast to the two sword masters. He drinks, forms friendships, retains ordinary desires, and still possesses exceptional martial skill, complicating the claim that mastery requires emotional exclusion. He opposes the duel's conversion into gambling and spectacle and treats death for an invisible ranking as potentially empty, while the duelists understand the same encounter as fidelity to their art and self-testing.

His strongest relational moment comes when he protects 卜巨 from humiliation. Rather than mock the man's inability to reach the roof, Lu uses a humorous tiger-and-sparrow story to preserve dignity. The source nevertheless questions a recurring gap between the novel's declarations of deep friendship and the limited shared history or abrupt attitude changes that are shown.

## Key Characteristics

- Combines investigation, high martial ability, appetite, humor, friendship, and emotional openness.
- Serves as the humanly engaged counterpoint to the duelists' pursuit of technical purity.
- Questions fame, ranking, gambling, and spectatorship as reasons to risk life.
- Attempts to prevent the duel even while understanding both participants.
- Uses humor and attention to protect another person's dignity.
- Attracts loyalty, though some relationships are more asserted than behaviorally developed in the bounded reading.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to one novel as discussed in one structured podcast summary, not the full Lu Xiaofeng series. His intelligence, confusion, romantic relationships, friendships, motives, exact investigation, dialogue, and martial standing remain source-scoped. The hosts' criticism of asserted friendship and inconsistent characterization is a literary judgment rather than a settled account of the text's reception.
