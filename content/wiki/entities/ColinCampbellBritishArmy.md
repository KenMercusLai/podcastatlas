# Colin Campbell (British Army officer)

## Overview

Sir Colin Campbell was an experienced British Army officer who commanded the Highland Brigade in the [[CrimeanWar|Crimean War]] after being passed over for supreme command.

## Current Profile

[[722-the-charge-of-the-light-brigade-the-tsars-terrible-siege-part-2-glt7837711407]] presents Campbell as the strongest independently experienced British command candidate discussed in the episode. His service included extensive fighting and command in India and the conquest of the Punjab, giving him practical qualifications that exceeded those of several socially better-connected appointees.

The source argues that this experience did not overcome class hierarchy. As the son of a Glasgow carpenter, without great wealth or patrons and still formally a colonel, Campbell was not considered for the highest post. He instead received the three Highland regiments of the Highland Brigade, making his case an example of how rank, money, and patronage could separate demonstrated command experience from appointment authority.

[[723-the-charge-of-the-light-brigade-crushing-the-russians-part-3-glt9985161615]] supplies battlefield evidence for that experience. At the Alma, Campbell's Highland regiments maintained discipline under fire and used the new Minié rifle effectively against Russian troops with inferior muskets, helping force the Russian retreat. The episode credits the combination of French flank movement and British rifle fire rather than making Campbell the sole cause of victory.

[[724-the-charge-of-the-light-brigade-into-the-valley-of-death-part-4-glt1598624240]] extends that record at Balaclava. Facing Russian cavalry near the road to the port, Campbell formed the heavily outnumbered 93rd Highlanders in two lines rather than a conventional square, maximizing the fire of their rifles. Repeated volleys stopped the advance before contact. Russell's description became the “Thin Red Line,” turning a tactically effective adaptation into one of the war's principal memory images.

## Key Characteristics

- Possessed extensive combat and independent command experience from India and the Punjab before the Crimean War.
- Lacked the wealth, aristocratic origin, and patrons associated with senior appointment.
- Remained formally a colonel despite his experience.
- Received command of the three-regiment Highland Brigade rather than the army.
- Led disciplined, rifle-armed Highland regiments effectively at the Alma.
- Used a two-deep rifle line to stop Russian cavalry at Balaclava and became associated with the reported “Thin Red Line.”

## Qualifications

The episodes' class-prejudice explanation is plausible but source-scoped and does not reconstruct the complete appointment process, formal seniority rules, political deliberation, or every candidate. Greater prior experience and effective performance at the Alma and Balaclava do not prove how Campbell would have performed as coalition commander; the exact formation, cavalry intent, range, timing, rifle effect, unit discipline, terrain, and enemy decisions also mattered.

## What Changed

- Added Campbell's two-deep Balaclava formation and its transformation through reporting into the “Thin Red Line.”
