# Gleiwitz Incident

## Overview

The Gleiwitz Incident was an SS-organized false-flag attack on a German radio station on 31 August 1939, staged to support [[NaziGermany|Nazi Germany]]'s claim that Polish aggression justified the invasion beginning the next morning.

## Current Profile

The episode places Gleiwitz at the endpoint of diplomacy that had become theatrical. German authorities were already completing military preparations and isolating the Polish embassy when reports of frontier incidents supplied a public pretext for action already decided.

SS personnel performed the attack, fired blanks, and left bodies dressed or positioned to support the fabricated account. The episode identifies one victim as a local Polish farmer and German citizen targeted for Polish patriotism, who was arrested, drugged, brought to the site, shot, and used as physical evidence. The incident therefore joined propaganda deception to the killing and instrumentalization of a civilian.

## Key Characteristics

- State-organized false flag presented as spontaneous enemy aggression.
- Propaganda pretext produced after the strategic and military decision for invasion.
- SS operation attributed by the episode to planning under [[ReinhardHeydrich]].
- Violent staging that used a murdered civilian as fabricated evidence.

## Evidence

- Timing and purpose: [[531-hitlers-war-on-poland-the-pact-with-stalin-part-2-glt5437884494]] places the incident on the evening before the invasion and inside a wider set of alleged border attacks.
- Operational staging: [[531-hitlers-war-on-poland-the-pact-with-stalin-part-2-glt5437884494]] describes SS personnel, blank fire, staged roles, and bodies used to substantiate the story.
- Victimization: [[531-hitlers-war-on-poland-the-pact-with-stalin-part-2-glt5437884494]] recounts the arrest, drugging, transport, and killing of a Polish-identifying German citizen.

## Qualifications

This page reflects one episode's concise reconstruction rather than an independent archival investigation. The wider false-flag program, chain of command, number and identities of victims, exact staging, reported orders, and contemporary propaganda effect require broader evidence. The operation supplied a pretext; it did not cause a military decision that had already been made.

## What Changed

- Established Gleiwitz as a planned propaganda operation embedded in the final invasion timetable.
- Made the coercion and murder of a civilian part of the incident's profile rather than treating it only as media deception.
