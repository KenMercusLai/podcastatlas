# Workshop

## Overview
Workshop is an internal communications SaaS company founded by [[RickKnudtson]]. In the available source, it serves large organizations that need to create, distribute, manage, and measure employee communications across channels.

## Current Profile
Workshop's current wiki profile is as an enterprise communications platform that found product-market fit by narrowing from a modern-intranet thesis to email-centered infrastructure. The product is presented as multi-channel, but its breakthrough came from treating internal email as a complex enterprise workflow rather than as a generic marketing-newsletter problem.

## Key Characteristics
- Serves communications teams, leadership, departments, and executives that need to reach employees at scale.
- Supports [[ChannelNativeInternalCommunication]] across email, SMS, Slack, Microsoft Teams, SharePoint, and related employee channels.
- Centers on [[EnterpriseInternalEmailInfrastructure]], including deliverability, security review, analytics, governance, brand control, and permissions.
- Emerged through a [[CustomerSignalDrivenPivot]] after an initial intranet concept failed to produce strong sales traction.
- Uses an audience-and-channel pricing model rather than strict per-seat pricing, reducing friction when more internal users collaborate on communications.
- Uses [[HappyMondayClub]] and practitioner education as a community and demand-generation surface.

## Evidence
- Product scope and customer profile: [[rick-knudtson-workshop-the-email-signal-he-ignored-for-9-months]] describes Workshop as a multi-channel internal communications platform for organizations with hundreds or thousands of employees.
- Pivot evidence: [[rick-knudtson-workshop-the-email-signal-he-ignored-for-9-months]] reports the contrast between roughly three intranet customers in nine months and about 10 email customers in 30 days.
- Enterprise email complexity: [[rick-knudtson-workshop-the-email-signal-he-ignored-for-9-months]] lists deliverability, spam filters, security tools, analytics, governance, and brand consistency as reasons email became the product core.
- Pricing and go-to-market: [[rick-knudtson-workshop-the-email-signal-he-ignored-for-9-months]] explains the audience/channel pricing model and Happy Monday Club audience strategy.

## Qualifications
The available evidence comes from a founder interview, so reported ARR, headcount, customer count, and early-customer conversion metrics should be treated as source-scoped. The episode presents Workshop as multi-channel, but the strongest product-market-fit evidence in the source centers on email.
