# University of Chicago / 芝加哥大学

## Overview
The University of Chicago appears in the source as an analogy for open intellectual fields with looser disciplinary boundaries.

## Current Profile
The episode uses the University of Chicago to compare Dartmouth-style innovation fields with other environments where important ideas emerged from cross-boundary conversation. Yang Bin emphasizes a "wild" western feeling, less rigid disciplinary separation, Robert Hutchins's general-education reforms, and Friedrich Hayek's placement outside a standard economics-department home.

Its current wiki role is therefore a university-culture comparison, not a full institutional profile.

## Key Characteristics
- Used as an analogy for open and cross-disciplinary intellectual fields.
- Associated in the source with looser historical disciplinary boundaries.
- Linked to Robert Hutchins's general-education push.
- Linked to Hayek's move into the Committee on Social Thought after economics-department resistance.

## Evidence
- Cross-field analogy: [[401-bianyu-manju-manzou-women-haineng-yinglai-lingwai-yige-datemao-si-de-xiatian-ma-f7495e05]] compares Dartmouth with University of Chicago reading and discussion fields.
- Openness claim: [[401-bianyu-manju-manzou-women-haineng-yinglai-lingwai-yige-datemao-si-de-xiatian-ma-f7495e05]] says Chicago's disciplinary boundaries were less clear and had a "wild" feeling.
- Education reform context: [[401-bianyu-manju-manzou-women-haineng-yinglai-lingwai-yige-datemao-si-de-xiatian-ma-f7495e05]] mentions Hutchins and general education as part of the example.

## Qualifications
