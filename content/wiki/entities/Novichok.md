# Novichok

## Overview
Novichok is the widely used name for a family of highly toxic nerve agents associated in this source with a secret Soviet weapons programme and with A234 used in Salisbury in 2018.

## Current Profile
The episode treats Novichok as a capability whose consequences persisted beyond the laboratory and the state programme's official denial. It connects A234 to the poisoning of Sergei and Yulia Skripal, the later exposure of Charlie Rowley and Dawn Sturgess, and [[VladimirUglev]]'s technical identification and public warnings. Persistence is both chemical and institutional: discarded material can harm unintended victims, while undeclared knowledge or stockpiles can outlast the system that created them.

## Key Characteristics
- It is presented as a Soviet-developed family of long-lasting, highly effective nerve agents.
- A234 is identified as the substance used in the Salisbury poisoning.
- Residual material can expose unintended victims after an initial attack.
- Identification can draw on molecular structure, spectrometry, and purity signatures.
- Programme secrecy complicated declaration, control, and accountability.

## Qualifications
This page is grounded only in a structured episode summary. It does not establish chain of custody, full toxicology, programme inventory, individual criminal liability, or Uglev's cause of death. The name “Novichok” can cover multiple compounds, so claims about A234 should not automatically be generalized to every agent in the family.
