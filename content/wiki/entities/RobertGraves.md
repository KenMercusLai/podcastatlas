# Robert Graves

## Overview

Robert Graves is the British soldier-writer whose early Western Front experience in [[671-the-first-world-war-blood-in-the-trenches-part-1-glt2309179565]] gives the wiki its most detailed source voice for [[TrenchLifeWesternFront|trench life]] as comic, frightening, young, class-mixed, routine-bound, and brutal.

## Current Profile

The episode introduces Graves as a Charterhouse-educated junior officer born in 1895 who joined the Royal Welch Fusiliers and reached France in May 1915. His platoon included working-class men, older men, and underage boys, making the trench line a forced social mixture.

Graves's value in this source is observational. He records troop trains, night marches, frightened hymn singing, the sudden nearness of shells, slippery clay trenches, trapped mice and frogs, cosy dugouts, trench routines, gas fear, shelling fear, and the idea that everyone has a breaking point. The source uses him to complicate both romantic battle expectation and later flattened anti-war memory.

## Key Characteristics

- British junior officer and later memoir voice for the Western Front.
- Observes class and age mixing inside trench units.
- Records both domestic trench routine and lethal randomness.
- Supports the episode's argument that trench life contained comedy, boredom, food, fear, and trauma together.
- Later fear of gas and shelling grounds the shell-shock breaking-point claim.

## Evidence
