# Paul Keating

## Overview

Paul Keating was Bob Hawke's treasurer and successor, presented as a sharp parliamentary combatant who combined economic reform with republicanism, Indigenous reconciliation, and an Asian strategic orientation.

## Current Profile

The episode makes [[PaulKeating|Keating]] the intellectual and rhetorical counterweight to [[BobHawke|Hawke]]. It contrasts his working-class, Catholic, Irish-Australian origins with his enthusiasm for Mahler and French clocks, then places him at the center of the [[HawkeKeatingReformSettlement]]. After Hawke did not honor their reported handover agreement, Keating failed in one 1991 challenge and won a second. As prime minister he delivered the Redfern Park speech, promoted an Australian republic, prioritized engagement with Asia and [[Indonesia]], won the 1993 election despite recession, and later criticized AUKUS as dependence on a fading Anglosphere.

## Key Characteristics

- Treasurer and reform partner to Bob Hawke before becoming prime minister in 1991.
- Working-class politician with conspicuously cosmopolitan cultural tastes.
- Exceptionally aggressive and memorable parliamentary rhetorician.
- Advocate of Aboriginal reconciliation and the Redfern Park speech.
- Republican critic of British imperial and monarchical inheritance.
- Proponent of an Australian strategic and economic pivot toward Asia.
