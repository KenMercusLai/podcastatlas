# The Birthday Party (Charles Randolph screenplay)

## Overview

[[BirthdayPartyFilm|The Birthday Party]] is [[CharlesRandolph]]'s unproduced adaptation of Stanley Alpert's account of being kidnapped in New York in 1998 by young people seeking access to his bank account.

## Current Profile

The screenplay shifts the source from procedural survival toward mutual misrecognition. Fictional Stanley remains a victim who helps bring his captors to justice, but his liberal self-image is tested against racial assumptions; the captors remain responsible while receiving background, dependence, fear, and a choice over whether they can become killers.

Its central wager is that invention can produce emotional truth without factual equivalence. That wager also created its central liability: altering a living victim and staging young Black men preying on a white captive made the work difficult for filmmakers to defend, finance, and cast as responsible representation.

## Key Characteristics

- Uses a blindfold as the organizing image of literal and moral nonrecognition.
- Invents a gunpoint confrontation to test claims about opportunity, racism, and choice.
- Retains justice while complicating punishment and empathy.
- Gives both victim and captors internal as well as external conflict.
- Remained unproduced after repeated representational concern and Forman's withdrawal for health reasons.
