# Moby-Dick / 《白鲸》

## Overview
*Moby-Dick* is [[HermanMelville|Herman Melville]]'s maritime novel, read by [[CultureLimited|文化有限]] as an encyclopedic inquiry into industry, labor, religion, nature, obsession, and human finitude.

## Current Profile
The episode treats the pursuit of the white whale as the novel's narrative spine rather than its whole substance. [[IshmaelMobyDick|Ishmael]] opens the world through curiosity, labor, technical explanation, and unstable narration; [[CaptainAhab|Ahab]] narrows that world into one totalizing target. The book's unusual scale comes from allowing classifications, production processes, documents, sermons, drama, and poetry to interrupt plot and reveal the wider systems carried by the Pequod.

## Key Characteristics
- A revenge voyage supports a much wider study of a nineteenth-century industrial and social world.
- Technical digressions function as world-building, historical record, and philosophical method.
- Multiple genres and viewpoints approach the whale and sea without claiming final mastery.
- The Pequod's diverse crew becomes a compact social and political model.
- The contrast between Ishmael and Ahab opposes widening curiosity to identity-consuming purpose.
