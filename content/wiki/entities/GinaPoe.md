# Gina Poe

## Overview
Gina Poe is presented in the bounded source as the Huberman Lab Essentials guest explaining sleep stages, memory consolidation, creativity, and emotional-memory processing.

## Current Profile
The current wiki evidence for Gina Poe is limited to [[essentials-use-sleep-to-enhance-learning-memory-emotional-state-dr-gina-poe-scim7156610982]]. In that source, Poe distinguishes wakefulness, non-REM sleep, and REM sleep as different brain states with different rhythms and chemistry. Her main contribution is a staged sleep model: early-night slow-wave sleep supports restoration and memory transfer, later REM-rich cycles support creative association and emotional-memory processing, and pre-sleep arousal or alcohol can interfere with adaptive sleep.

The episode also positions Poe as a mechanistic explainer. She moves between brain rhythms such as spindles and P-waves, neuromodulators such as norepinephrine, and practical boundaries such as consistent bedtime, normal nighttime waking, imperfect sleep trackers, and calming routines before sleep.

## Key Characteristics
- Explains sleep as active staged brain work rather than passive shutdown or quiet wakefulness.
- Links early-night slow-wave sleep to growth hormone, energy rebuilding, protein synthesis, cleanup, and memory transfer.
- Links stage-two spindles, P-waves, and later REM sleep to schema formation, insight, and creativity.
- Uses the locus coeruleus and norepinephrine to explain why REM may help separate traumatic memories from overwhelming physiological charge.
- Keeps practical guidance bounded by context, including circadian regularity, alcohol effects, tracker limits, and pre-sleep arousal.
