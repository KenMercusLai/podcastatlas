# George Harrison

## Overview

George Harrison was a member of [[TheBeatles|the Beatles]] whose profile in this source centers spiritual exploration, skepticism toward commercialized flower-power imagery, and continued creative work beyond the band.

## Current Profile

Harrison's visit to Haight-Ashbury is presented as a disillusioning encounter with a scene whose idealized image exceeded the reality he saw. His turn toward Indian spirituality offered a different response to psychedelic and countercultural searching and helped bring the Beatles into contact with Maharishi Mahesh Yogi.

The 1968 Rishikesh retreat mattered differently to each member. For Harrison it was described as life-changing rather than merely fashionable, while the wider episode keeps the retreat connected to the acoustic writing conditions behind much of the White Album. His solo album *All Things Must Pass* is named as evidence that significant work continued after the breakup.

## Key Characteristics

- Pursued Indian spirituality as more than a temporary visual fashion.
- Helped connect the Beatles with Maharishi Mahesh Yogi and the Rishikesh retreat.
- Became skeptical of the press-amplified idealization of Haight-Ashbury.
- Represents divergent personal responses within the band's shared countercultural image.
- Continued major creative work after the Beatles ended.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to one compressed group-history episode and is not a full biography, musical analysis, or account of Harrison's spiritual development. Private motives, the depth and continuity of belief, the press-driven character of the Summer of Love, and the retreat's precise effect on songwriting remain source-scoped.
