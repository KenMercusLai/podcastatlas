## Overview

海大富 / Hai Dafu is a [[LuDingJi|《鹿鼎记》]] character interpreted as [[WeiXiaobao|韦小宝]]'s first consequential teacher despite the absence of a formal master-disciple relationship.

## Current Profile

Hai and Wei initially depend on and deceive each other. Hai poisons him and uses him in the search for the *Forty-Two Chapter Sutra*, while Wei relies on Hai's protection and knowledge to survive the palace. Inside that coercive relationship, Hai teaches rudimentary martial techniques and the use of corpse-dissolving powder. Their value lies less in elegance than in immediate application, making him a strong case of [[InformalSelectiveLearning|informal selective learning]].

His most durable lesson is political. By warning Wei not to mistake the emperor for an equal friend, Hai makes hierarchy visible beneath intimacy. Wei can continue to feel friendship toward Kangxi while outwardly maintaining the posture of a servant; survival depends on recognizing that private feeling does not suspend sovereign power.

## Key Characteristics

- Functions as a teacher in practice without formal discipleship.
- Combines instruction, protection, coercion, poisoning, and mutual use.
- Teaches techniques whose immediate utility outweighs their martial refinement.
- Makes imperial status inequality explicit beneath personal familiarity.
- Leaves Wei with a durable method of political self-protection.

## Qualifications

This profile follows one podcast's literary interpretation rather than a complete edition-based character study. Calling Hai a teacher describes functional influence, not ethical approval: coercion, poisoning, and instrumental use sharply limit any claim that the relationship is good mentorship.

## What Changed

- Created a profile separating effective informal instruction from benevolent or legitimate teaching.
- Added recognition of imperial hierarchy as Hai's most durable lesson.
