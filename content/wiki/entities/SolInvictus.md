# Sol Invictus

## Overview

Sol Invictus, "the unconquered sun," is a Roman solar divine title or cult figure whose possible December 25 festival is central to arguments that Christmas adopted a pre-existing pagan date.

## Current Profile

[[402-christmas-pagan-or-christian-glt8329172184]] accepts a probable December 25 celebration of "the unconquered one" but treats its interpretation cautiously. The Calendar of Philocalus does not prove that the figure was Sol, that the observance predated Christmas, or that Christians borrowed from it; other recorded Sol festivals also appear more prominent.

## Key Characteristics

- A Roman solar cult with evidence predating Emperor Elagabalus.
- Distinct from [[Mithras]] despite shared solar language and association.
- Probably connected to a December 25 chariot-race observance in the Calendar of Philocalus.
- More securely associated in the episode with substantial festivals on August 28 and October 22.

## Evidence

- Independent identity: [[402-christmas-pagan-or-christian-glt8329172184]] cites images in which Sol and Mithras appear as separate figures.
- Long Roman presence: [[402-christmas-pagan-or-christian-glt8329172184]] rejects the claim that Elagabalus introduced sun worship to Rome from nothing.
- Calendar evidence: [[402-christmas-pagan-or-christian-glt8329172184]] reports 30 chariot races for the December 25 birthday of "the unconquered one," alongside 36 for Sol on October 22.

## Qualifications

The December 25 entry is ambiguous in identity, priority, and causal significance. A shared date can establish coexistence without establishing that one festival produced the other.

## What Changed

- Created a profile separating a probable December observance from claims of direct influence on Christmas.

## Relationships

- [[December25ChristmasDating]] - chronological debate in which the Sol festival is important but inconclusive.
- [[Saturnalia]] - separate December festival sometimes combined with Sol in pagan-Christmas narratives.
