# Antoine Galland

## Overview

Antoine Galland was the translator-editor whose early-eighteenth-century French Arabian Nights made the corpus a major European success while reshaping what European readers understood it to contain.

## Current Profile

Galland began with Sinbad in 1701 and produced a twelve-volume Nights over sixteen years. His work was not a neutral transfer of one complete Arabic original: he softened or omitted sexual material, drew on a fourteenth-century manuscript, and incorporated stories from beyond that manuscript tradition.

Aladdin and Ali Baba were reportedly supplied orally by a Syrian Maronite Christian storyteller, while Sinbad had circulated separately. Galland therefore occupies several roles at once—translator, adapter, editor, collector, and canon maker.

## Key Characteristics

- First major translator of the Arabian Nights into a European language.
- Editor who bowdlerized parts of the material for his audience.
- Publishing intermediary who turned the collection into a European success.
- Collector who incorporated oral and separately circulating stories.
- Canon-making figure whose choices altered the perceived boundaries of the Nights.

## Evidence

- **Publication:** [[379-baghdad-the-arabian-nights-part-4-glt8529456010]] dates the Sinbad work from 1701 and describes the larger translation as a twelve-volume, sixteen-year success.
- **Editorial intervention:** [[379-baghdad-the-arabian-nights-part-4-glt8529456010]] says Galland bowdlerized the tales and added material not present in the Arabic manuscript he used.
- **Oral acquisition:** [[379-baghdad-the-arabian-nights-part-4-glt8529456010]] attributes Aladdin and Ali Baba to a Syrian Maronite Christian storyteller who told them to Galland.
