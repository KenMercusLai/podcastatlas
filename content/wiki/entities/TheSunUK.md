# The Sun (UK)

## Overview

The Sun is a British tabloid represented here through its highly influential, pro-Thatcher, and aggressively patriotic coverage of the [[FalklandsWar|Falklands War]].

## Current Profile

The episode describes The Sun as a mass-readership newspaper that joined support for British forces to jokes, slogans, promotional stunts, attacks on rival outlets, and sexualized material sent toward the task force. Its “Gotcha” treatment after the [[GeneralBelgrano|General Belgrano]] attack became the clearest symbol of a tone that celebrated military success while news of casualties and survivors was still emerging.

The paper's importance in this source is therefore not proof that it created public support for the war. It shows how a popular outlet can intensify [[WartimeMediaLoyaltyPressure|wartime media loyalty pressure]], harden moral camps, and make the style of reporting part of an event's later memory.

## Key Characteristics

- High-circulation British tabloid with substantial 1980s popular influence.
- Strong supporter of Margaret Thatcher and the British war effort.
- Used jokes, slogans, stunts, and ridicule as part of patriotic mobilization.
- Attacked rival outlets and dissenting voices as disloyal.
- Became inseparable from Belgrano memory through the “Gotcha” headline.

## Evidence

- Campaign style: [[171-the-falklands-war-battle-for-the-islands-part-3-glt4679725480]] describes the paper's pro-Thatcher nationalism, anti-Argentine jokes, stunts, and attacks on critics.
- Belgrano memory: [[171-the-falklands-war-battle-for-the-islands-part-3-glt4679725480]] treats “Gotcha” as a major reason the sinking remained morally and politically bitter.
- BBC conflict: [[171-the-falklands-war-battle-for-the-islands-part-3-glt4679725480]] links press attacks on detached language and dissent to accusations of treachery.

## Qualifications

This profile is limited to one podcast's account of Falklands coverage. It does not provide a full ownership, editorial, circulation, audience-reception, or accuracy history, and selected notorious examples cannot establish the tone of every report or the independent effect of the newspaper on public opinion.
