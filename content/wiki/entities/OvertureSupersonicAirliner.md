# Overture Supersonic Airliner

## Overview
Overture is [[BoomSupersonic]]'s planned supersonic passenger airliner and the commercial target behind its market modeling, [[XB1SupersonicDemonstrator|XB-1]] prototype work, engine program, and [[BoomlessCruise]] strategy.

## Current Profile
The current source synthesis presents Overture as a premium first-generation aircraft intended to fly around Mach 1.7 over water and, under suitable conditions, near Mach 1.3 over land without a boom reaching the ground. [[BlakeScholl]] argues that a roughly three-and-a-half-hour Atlantic trip and a claimed break-even round-trip fare near $3,500 could make it more accessible than Concorde. Part 2 adds a product-experience goal—no middle seats, rapid turns, and integrated connectivity—and a co-design example in which [[Makeboom]] helped search for an engine configuration after a better cabin reduced simulated range. Overture nevertheless remains a planned aircraft, and neither simulation, XB-1 flight, nor ground-engine use resolves full-airliner certification and production.

## Key Characteristics
- Overture's initial economics rely on the [[AllBusinessClassSupersonicModel|premium time-saving market]], not immediate mass-market pricing.
- It is designed around differentiated operating regimes: faster over water and boomless-profile flight over populated land.
- Boom's internal engine path is intended to align propulsion, range, operations, and airframe choices more closely than the former supplier plan.
- Ground use of the engine core is proposed as a source of revenue and high-duty-cycle reliability evidence before passenger service.
- Cabin, connectivity, airframe, and propulsion are being treated as one [[AircraftPropulsionCoDesign|coupled design problem]] rather than sequential supplier interfaces.
- The aircraft still faces certification, manufacturing-scale, fuel, environmental, airline-adoption, fare, and schedule uncertainty.

- Product and market origin: [[tsr-s5-blakescholl-v3-finalaudio-tsr-s5-blakescholl-v3-finalaudio]] describes premium-cabin economics, early airline intent, XB-1 learning, and the change from a supplier to an internal engine path.

- Engine validation path: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-blake-scholl-why-plane-speed-stalled-supersonic-commercial-flight-revolutionizing-the-engine-42987238]] argues that the [[GroundPowerEngineValidationFlywheel|ground-power derivative]] could accumulate extensive operating data before Overture carries passengers.
- Cabin and system design: [[tsr-s6-blakescholl-v3-tsr-s6-blakescholl-v3]] describes no-middle-seat and rapid-turn goals, integrated Starlink hardware, and a Makeboom configuration search that Scholl says recovered most of a source-reported 1,000-mile simulated range loss.

## Qualifications
All performance, fare, cabin, boarding, range-recovery, and timing figures are founder projections. Break-even fare is not an airline ticket commitment, and airline pricing could be higher. Atmospheric cutoff depends on conditions, while ground power, simulation, and XB-1 evidence do not substitute for certification of the passenger airframe-engine system. Environmental impact and total operating economics receive little independent scrutiny in the bounded sources.
