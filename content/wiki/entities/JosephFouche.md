# Joseph Fouché

## Overview

Joseph Fouché was a Convention deputy and representative on mission whose de-Christianization and mass violence in Lyon were followed by an agile political defence and coalition role in the overthrow of [[MaximilienRobespierre|Robespierre]].

## Current Profile

The sources present Fouché as a former Oratorian physics teacher who moved from institutional religion into radical politics, systematic de-Christianization, and provincial terror. In Nièvre he closed churches, compelled clerical renunciations, seized precious metals, and promoted cults of reason. In Lyon he joined iconoclasm and parody ritual to executions by grapeshot. Recalled to Paris, he defended the violence as revolutionary humanity, won the [[JacobinClub|Jacobin Club]] presidency, survived Robespierre's attack, spread the claim that a new victim list existed, and helped align endangered deputies before the [[ThermidorianCoup|Thermidorian coup]].

## Key Characteristics

- He converted an Oratorian teaching background into radical anti-clerical politics.
- His Nièvre mission made de-Christianization systematic through church closure, clerical renunciation, asset seizure, and cults of reason.
- His Lyon mission joined spectacular blasphemy to mass execution and a public defence of terror.
- He survived recall by seizing the initiative in the Convention and Jacobin Club.
- He turned personal vulnerability into coalition building among deputies who feared prosecution.
- He helped make uncertain exposure actionable by circulating warnings about an intended purge.

## Evidence

### De-Christianization and Lyon

- [[711-the-terror-killing-god-part-5-glt6491220072]] traces his Nièvre campaign, influence on Parisian de-Christianization, Lyon iconoclasm, grapeshot executions, and claimed responsibility for more than two thousand deaths.

### Contest with Robespierre

- [[711-the-terror-killing-god-part-5-glt6491220072]] records his public defence after recall, private confrontation with Robespierre, election and removal as Jacobin president, expulsion from the club, and discreet organizing among threatened deputies.
- [[712-the-terror-the-fall-of-robespierre-part-6-glt1154404105]] presents him spreading rumors of a victim list and encouraging threatened deputies to cooperate before Thermidor.

## Qualifications

- The source supplies a concentrated 1793–94 profile rather than Fouché's full career or later state service.
- The death total, reported dialogue, exact operational responsibility, and interpretation of his motives remain source-scoped.
- Political agility explains survival without making his defence of violence factually or morally credible.
- The existence of a specific Robespierre victim list is presented as rumor rather than established documentary fact.

## What Changed

- Extended Fouché's pre-Thermidor organizing into the successful coalition against Robespierre.
- Added the rumored victim list as a mobilizing device without treating it as established fact.

## Relationships

- [[MaximilienRobespierre]] - rival who condemned his provincial violence and later tried to isolate him.
- [[JacobinClub]] - institution whose presidency he briefly won before expulsion.
- [[LawOf22Prairial]] - legal escalation that increased his incentive to organize against Robespierre.
- [[RevolutionaryPoliticalReligion]] - conflict between his cults of reason and Robespierre's deist civic religion.
- [[RevolutionaryPurgeSecurityDilemma]] - mechanism through which his personal danger became coalition pressure.
- [[JeanBaptisteCarrier]] - fellow representative whose provincial atrocities also provoked recall and condemnation.
- [[ThermidorianCoup]] - overthrow his coalition work helped prepare.
