# Bay of Naples

## Overview

The Bay of Naples is the densely settled Roman coastal region around [[MountVesuvius]] that connected [[Pompeii]], [[Herculaneum]], Naples, Puteoli, Misenum, Stabiae, Baiae, villas, ports, naval infrastructure, and an elite resort economy.

## Current Profile

The sources present the bay as both luxury landscape and working imperial infrastructure. Fashionable villas and resorts coexisted with agriculture, grain-supply ports, the Misenum fleet, and production of volcanic material useful for marine concrete. Baiae concentrated sulphur baths, domed pools, spectacular building, yachts, privacy, and a reputation for decadence; Puteoli combined the grain port with hotels, brothels, and entertainment serving a broader public; Sergius Orata's oyster beds, heated pools, and property development show entrepreneurs creating demand around elite taste.

During the eruption the same connected coast became a field of sharply unequal exposure. Wind, distance, shore access, pumice on water, darkness, ash, and successive flows determined who could observe, flee, sail, shelter, or survive.

## Key Characteristics

- Maritime region joining cities, ports, villas, farmland, and Roman naval power.
- Fashionable elite landscape with major economic and logistical functions.
- Resort system combining bathing, dining, property, lodging, sex work, entertainment, and maritime display.
- Volcanic environment whose soils and materials supported prosperity.
- Shared disaster field in which local timing and hazard differed substantially by place.
- Refuge and dispersal region after Pompeii and Herculaneum vanished.

## Evidence

- Regional economy: [[355-roman-apocalypse-pompeii-79-ad-glt6664993493]] links the bay to cities, resorts, villas, ports, grain supply, agriculture, and marine concrete.
- Naval geography: [[355-roman-apocalypse-pompeii-79-ad-glt6664993493]] follows Pliny's fleet from Misenum toward Pompeii and Stabiae.
- Unequal exposure: [[355-roman-apocalypse-pompeii-79-ad-glt6664993493]] contrasts Herculaneum, Pompeii, Misenum, and Stabiae across the eruption sequence.
- Aftermath: [[355-roman-apocalypse-pompeii-79-ad-glt6664993493]] describes refugees spreading around the bay and toward Rome and Ostia.
- Resort concentration: [[224-roman-holidays-glt9939678940]] identifies Baiae as a luxury and spa center and Puteoli as both major port and broader entertainment city.
- Leisure enterprise: [[224-roman-holidays-glt9939678940]] links Sergius Orata to Lucrine oysters, heated pools, gourmands, and coastal property development.

## Qualifications

This remains an episode-bounded regional profile, not a complete economic or urban history. The holiday source is elite-heavy even when Puteoli broadens the social range, and modern resort comparisons should not erase Roman slavery, status hierarchy, infrastructure, or moral categories. Engineering attribution, port functions, population, evacuation capacity, ash effects, and modern exposure figures remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Added the bay's resort economy and its split between Baian elite privacy and Puteolan port entertainment.
