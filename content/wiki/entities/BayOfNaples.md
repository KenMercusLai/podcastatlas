# Bay of Naples

## Overview

The Bay of Naples is the densely settled Roman coastal region around [[MountVesuvius]] that connected [[Pompeii]], [[Herculaneum]], Naples, Puteoli, Misenum, Stabiae, Baiae, villas, ports, and naval infrastructure.

## Current Profile

The source presents the bay as both luxury landscape and working imperial infrastructure. Fashionable villas and resorts coexisted with agriculture, grain-supply ports, the Misenum fleet, and production of volcanic material useful for marine concrete. During the eruption it became a field of sharply unequal exposure: wind, distance, shore access, pumice on water, darkness, ash, and successive flows determined who could observe, flee, sail, shelter, or survive.

## Key Characteristics

- Maritime region joining cities, ports, villas, farmland, and Roman naval power.
- Fashionable elite landscape with major economic and logistical functions.
- Volcanic environment whose soils and materials supported prosperity.
- Shared disaster field in which local timing and hazard differed substantially by place.
- Refuge and dispersal region after Pompeii and Herculaneum vanished.

## Evidence

- Regional economy: [[355-roman-apocalypse-pompeii-79-ad-glt6664993493]] links the bay to cities, resorts, villas, ports, grain supply, agriculture, and marine concrete.
- Naval geography: [[355-roman-apocalypse-pompeii-79-ad-glt6664993493]] follows Pliny's fleet from Misenum toward Pompeii and Stabiae.
- Unequal exposure: [[355-roman-apocalypse-pompeii-79-ad-glt6664993493]] contrasts Herculaneum, Pompeii, Misenum, and Stabiae across the eruption sequence.
- Aftermath: [[355-roman-apocalypse-pompeii-79-ad-glt6664993493]] describes refugees spreading around the bay and toward Rome and Ostia.

## Qualifications

This is an episode-bounded regional profile, not a complete economic or urban history. Port functions, population, evacuation capacity, ash effects, and modern exposure figures remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Created a regional profile joining volcanic productivity, Roman connectivity, and unequal disaster exposure.
