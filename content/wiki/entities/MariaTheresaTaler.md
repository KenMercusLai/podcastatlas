# Maria Theresa Taler

## Overview

The Maria Theresa taler enters the wiki through [[706-queen-victorias-revenge-storming-the-citadel-part-2-glt3285474568]] as the Austrian silver coin that the [[AbyssinianExpedition1868|Abyssinian expedition]] had to buy in bulk because it was the currency local suppliers and warlords actually accepted.

## Current Profile

The episode uses the coin to show that an imperial campaign's decisive supply problem can be monetary. Napier's force needed cash to buy food and pay local rulers on the route to [[Magdala]], and the preferred medium in the region was the Maria Theresa taler, first minted by Maria Theresa in 1741, with the 1780 edition especially prized. British agents bought up old coins across Central Europe, and the official mint in Vienna produced roughly 20,000 talers a week for the expedition.

The detail also outlives the campaign. The episode notes that these coins were still circulating as currency in East Africa in the 1950s, which makes the taler an example of a trade currency that kept working long after the empire that minted it had stopped being relevant in the region.

## Key Characteristics

- It is a foreign silver coin that became the accepted trade currency of the Red Sea and Ethiopian interior.
- Its value depends on trust in the metal and the familiar 1780 design rather than on the current Austrian state.
- It makes the expedition dependent on European mints and coin dealers as much as on rifles and transport.
- It long outlasted its issuing monarchy, remaining in use in East Africa into the 1950s.

## Qualifications

The episode gives the coin three sentences of colour rather than monetary history: the spellings taler and tala both appear, the 20,000-a-week output and the 1950s circulation date are source-scoped, and the wiki has no separate coverage of Austrian minting, Indian Ocean trade money or Ethiopian currency history.
