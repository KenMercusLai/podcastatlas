## Overview

李广利 / Li Guangli enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-389-2-lishi-jiemi-shui-shi-xie-jiantaoshu-zuiduo-de-wang-lvnamm5dza3s9ao8ofcshhiynsu1|Hanji 389-2]] as the Western Han Ershi General whose failed campaign against [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] becomes one of the background failures behind [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s [[LuntaiSelfCriticismPolicyTurn|Luntai self-criticism policy turn]]. The episode presents him less as an independent strategist than as the human and political cost marker of late Han Wudi frontier ambition.

## Current Profile

The source says Li Guangli is sent after Xiongnu provocation and the detention of Han envoys, while court figures, local officials, fangshi, and astrology readers encourage Han Wudi with auspicious interpretations. Later captured Xiongnu testimony reverses the sign: the tied horses were allegedly a curse against Han troops rather than proof of Xiongnu weakness.

Li Guangli's defeat then becomes central to the edict's self-criticism. His soldiers are described as killed, captured, or scattered, and the later narrative says that after capture he receives respect in Xiongnu territory until [[WeiLvXiongnu|魏律]] grows jealous. Wei Lü uses the illness of the chanyu's mother and shamanic speech to demand Li Guangli's sacrifice; Li Guangli curses Xiongnu before being killed.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han general sent against Xiongnu in a prestige-and-envoy crisis.
- Campaign decision is framed by misread signals and overly favorable auspicious interpretation.
- Defeat produces deaths, captures, and flight, making him a cost marker in Han Wudi's later self-criticism.
- Captured by Xiongnu and initially treated with honor in the episode's account.
- Dies after Wei Lü manipulates ritual illness language and has him killed as a sacrifice.

## Qualifications

The current role is source-scoped: failed expedition, cost signal, and sacrificial death.
