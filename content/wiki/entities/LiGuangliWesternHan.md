## Overview

李广利 / Li Guangli enters the wiki as the Western Han Ershi General whose failed campaign against [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] becomes one of the background failures behind [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s [[LuntaiSelfCriticismPolicyTurn|Luntai self-criticism policy turn]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-388-1-shaqi-shazi-zhihou-hanwudi-weihe-zumie-dajiuzi-liguangli-lkkwpivpwqpeg5kd7azu-wmdqmgb|Hanji 388-1]] now supplies the missing causal prequel: Li's surrender follows not only battlefield defeat but also succession plotting around [[LiuBoChangyiKingWesternHan|昌邑王刘髆]], the execution of [[LiuQuliWesternHan|刘屈氂]], and the arrest of Li's own wife and children.

## Current Profile

Hanji 388-1 places Li Guangli in the 90 BCE three-route expedition against Xiongnu after attacks on Five Plains and Jiuquan. Li initially wins: he defeats Xiongnu forces near Fuyang and advances to Fan Furen City. The same episode then shifts from battlefield capacity to succession exposure. Li is the brother of [[LadyLiWesternHan|李夫人]], whose son Liu Bo is Changyi king; because Li's daughter is Liu Quli's daughter-in-law, Li and the chancellor have a shared interest in asking Han Wudi to make Liu Bo crown prince.

That family-political line becomes fatal when Guo Rang reports curse-and-prayer charges against Liu Quli's household and links Li Guangli to prayers for Liu Bo's accession. Liu Quli is executed and Li Guangli's wife and children are arrested while Li is still at the front. The source reads Li's continued advance as a desperate attempt to trade new military merit for family rescue. Once army officers suspect that he has private motives, Li kills a changshi, loses confidence inside the army, faces Xiongnu pursuit and prepared traps, and surrenders because returning to Han appears fatal.

Hanji 389-2 then treats the failed expedition as central to the Luntai edict's self-criticism. Its version emphasizes misread signals, auspicious interpretation, and disastrous casualties. After capture, Li receives respect in Xiongnu territory until [[WeiLvXiongnu|魏律]] grows jealous. Wei Lü uses the illness of the chanyu's mother and shamanic speech to demand Li Guangli's sacrifice; Li curses Xiongnu before being killed.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han general sent against Xiongnu in a prestige-and-envoy crisis.
- Maternal-uncle supporter of Liu Bo's succession claim through Lady Li's family line.
- Politically exposed by his in-law alignment with Liu Quli and alleged prayers for Liu Bo's accession.
- Battlefield choices are shaped by family arrest, fear of return, and the hope that new merit can reverse punishment.
- Campaign failure produces deaths, captures, flight, and surrender, making him a cost marker in Han Wudi's later self-criticism.
- Captured by Xiongnu and initially treated with honor in Hanji 389-2's account.
- Dies after Wei Lü manipulates ritual illness language and has him killed as a sacrifice.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 388-1 and Hanji 389-2. It does not reconstruct Li Guangli's full Dayuan campaigns, complete title history, primary-source chronology, the full legal record of Liu Quli's case, or every military decision in the 90 BCE expedition. Hanji 388-1 supplies the family-politics and surrender chain; Hanji 389-2 supplies the Luntai-cost and sacrificial-death aftermath.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 388-1's prequel: Li Guangli's succession alignment with Liu Quli around Liu Bo, the arrest of Li's family, the desperate deep advance, army suspicion, surrender, and Wudi's family extermination.
- Preserved Hanji 389-2's role for Li Guangli as a failed-expedition cost marker behind Han Wudi's Luntai self-criticism and as the captured general later killed through Wei Lü's ritual manipulation.
