# Ishmael / 以实玛利（《白鲸》）

## Overview
Ishmael is the narrator of [[MobyDick|《白鲸》]], represented here as a curious outsider who goes to sea to escape depression and ordinary life and learns through labor, difference, and uncertainty.

## Current Profile
The episode reads “Call me Ishmael” as an uncertain self-presentation rather than a fully secured identity. His departure joins economic need, melancholy, curiosity, and a desire to hand himself over to the unknown. Unlike [[CaptainAhab|Ahab]], who forces the world into one purpose, Ishmael allows one whale and one voyage to keep opening into bodies, tools, industries, religions, peoples, stories, and questions.

## Key Characteristics
- He treats departure as both escape and a method of self-renewal.
- Curiosity lets technical and ordinary labor become education.
- His uncertain naming and shifting narrative position resist a single stable viewpoint.
- He values practical maritime experience alongside book knowledge.
- His expansive attention contrasts with Ahab's narrowing obsession.
