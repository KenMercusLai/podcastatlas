# Farinelli

## Overview

Farinelli was an eighteenth-century Italian castrato whose exceptional European celebrity and earnings reveal both the musical opportunities and the childhood bodily harm built into the castrato system.

## Current Profile

[[45-top-ten-eunuchs-glt5221212176]] presents Farinelli as the outstanding winner within a much larger and harsher labor market. Born in southern Italy in 1705, he performed across Italy, Vienna, London, Versailles, and Madrid and earned extraordinary sums.

His success cannot represent the typical outcome. The episode says poor families authorized the castration of thousands of boys in hopes that rare vocal talent would produce economic mobility. Farinelli therefore embodies both artistic achievement and selection bias: the famous survivor makes visible the system's prize while the many failed investments largely disappear.

## Key Characteristics

- Southern Italian singer born in 1705 and trained as a castrato.
- European celebrity who performed at leading courts and commercial venues.
- Exceptionally highly paid artist within an unequal musical labor system.
- Survivor whose fame can obscure the many boys harmed without comparable careers.
