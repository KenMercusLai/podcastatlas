## Overview

朱买臣 / Zhu Maichen is a Western Han reader and official whose wiki profile spans an impoverished marriage, assistance from his former wife, patronage-mediated entry into court, policy debate, later grievance, accusation politics, and execution. [[CuiShiZhuMaichenWife|崔氏]] leaves during his years as a woodcutter but later feeds him when he is destitute; [[YanZhuWesternHan|严助]] then helps him reach [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]. Much later, Zhu becomes a principal architect of the three-clerk counterattack against [[ZhangTangWesternHan|张汤]], who names him in the final accusation preceding Zhu's execution.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-286-canbei-wujie-qiannian-zhumaichen-xiuqi-de-fanzhuan-zhenxiang-1-lpvzvzomh6u6zbr1ispaiycenl3f|Hanji 286 part 1]] supplies the earliest phase. It presents Zhu as poor into his forties, without substantial property, cutting and selling firewood while continuing to read and recite. Cui experiences his conduct as impractical and publicly embarrassing. When she seeks to leave, Zhu predicts prosperity at fifty and promises to repay her years of hardship, but she does not accept the forecast. The episode reads their separation through household pressure and incompatible life judgments while warning that the familiar “马前泼水” opera plot is a later reputational layer.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-286-canbei-wujie-qiannian-zhumaichen-xiuqi-de-fanzhuan-zhenxiang-2-lrgdpir3ez-hk_eptcq8q3-pzozr|Hanji 286 part 2]] complicates both abandonment and self-made-rise narratives. Cui, now with another husband, reportedly sees Zhu hungry and cold and buys him a meal. Zhu later travels to Chang'an with a local reporting party, exhausts his provisions while waiting for a summons, and receives access through fellow townsman Yan Zhu. The episode attributes his appointment as zhong dafu to his display of learning in the [[Chunqiu|《春秋》]] and *Chuci*. It then gives Zhu an early policy role: his ten-point defense of retaining Shuofang defeats [[GongsunHong|公孙弘]]'s objection, although the arguments, title, chronology, and policy effect remain source-scoped.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-339-1-sanrenchenghu-ruhe-bisi-xihan-diyi-kuli-lowphplbvgugdyfag-sczpgggyrv|Hanji 339-1]] says Zhu, [[WangChaoWesternHan|王朝]], and [[BianTongWesternHan|边通]] were once senior officials before Zhang's rapid promotion and later endured repeated humiliation under him. When Zhang presses a knowing-nonreporting case against Chancellor [[ZhuangQingdiWesternHan|庄青翟]], they join Zhuang both from resentment and because they fear becoming Zhang's next targets.

Zhu's deepest personal grievance is attributed to Yan Zhu's death. Yan had recommended the struggling Zhu Maichen to [[HanWudi|汉武帝]], but Zhang allegedly insisted on Yan's execution after the Huainan case despite the emperor's reluctance. Zhu then watches Zhang for misconduct and selects merchant [[TianXinWesternHan|田信]] as the route for alleging policy leakage, official-commercial collusion, and private profit. The incomplete transcript preserves the construction of the charge but not proof of its claims.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-339-2-yi-si-sharen-kuli-zhangtang-shi-ruhe-zuodao-de-luq3jaa9iruhiqiqlduewbj-luca|Hanji 339-2]] supplies the reversal. Zhang's final letter identifies Zhu and the other two clerks as those who caused his death. After Zhang's estate and his mother's sparse funeral alter the emperor's reading, all three are executed and associated with Zhuang.

## Key Characteristics

- Poor but committed reader who supports himself through cutting and selling firewood in the episode's early-life account.
- Reaches court only after material hardship, aid from Cui, and Yan Zhu's personal introduction.
- Is credited with winning a court debate for retaining Shuofang before his later service as one of three chief clerks opposing Zhang Tang.
- Joins Zhuang Qingdi under a combination of shared threat and accumulated humiliation.
- Holds a personal grievance over the execution of benefactor Yan Zhu.
- Selects Tian Xin as the bridge for constructing an allegation against Zhang.
- Is named in Zhang's final written accusation and executed after Han Wudi reopens the case.

## Qualifications

The sources still do not reconstruct Zhu's continuous career or an independent case file. Hanji 286 parts 1 and 2 are structured retellings rather than quoted primary texts; household finances, Cui's motives and remarriage, the exact divorce procedure, the promised age of advancement, meal scene, Chang'an waiting conditions, Yan Zhu's role, learning displayed, appointment title, ten-point argument, and the opera's transmission remain source-scoped. Hanji 339-1's claims about humiliation, self-protection, Yan Zhu, Tian Xin, leakage, bribery, and collusion are likewise episode-attributed, and its transcript ends before the accusation is complete. Coalition activity is clearer than adjudicated guilt; Hanji 339-2 does not independently supply the clerks' exact acts or legal charges.
