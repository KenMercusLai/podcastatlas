## Overview

Frederick II appears in the source as a thirteenth-century Holy Roman emperor and king of Sicily whose learning, Mediterranean court culture, and long conflict with the papacy produced sharply opposed memories of heretic tyrant and world-renewing ruler.

## Current Profile

The episode uses Frederick primarily to explain why a dead ruler can remain politically available. His death did not close his public identity: stories said he had survived or would return, impostors issued commands and gathered followers in his name, and at least one claimant was burned. Later tradition blended him with his grandfather Frederick Barbarossa in the Kyffhäuser “sleeping emperor” story. The durable point is not which return story was literally believed, but how longing for peace and order preserved an idealized sovereign role that another person could try to occupy.

## Key Characteristics

- Holy Roman emperor and king of Sicily presented as culturally multilingual and intellectually curious.
- Ruler whose conflict with the papacy produced both demonizing and salvific political memories.
- Dead sovereign around whom survival, return, and imposture stories accumulated.
- Figure later blended with Frederick Barbarossa in a sleeping-emperor tradition.

## Qualifications
