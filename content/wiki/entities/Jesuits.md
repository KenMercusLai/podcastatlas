# Jesuits / 耶稣会

[[ep259-yu-miao-shifu-mantan-xu-guangqi-sibainian-qian-ruhe-rangren-biancongming-lkirczxnx8skbbfpmz5-m-mivs4e]] adds the China science-history branch through [[MatteoRicci|利玛窦]] and [[JohannAdamSchallVonBell|汤若望]]. The episode presents Jesuits as carriers of maps, geometry, astronomy, and calendar technique, while keeping their missionary strategy visible: elite access, useful knowledge, and religious goals were intertwined in [[LateMingWesternLearning|晚明西学东渐]].

Jesuits / 耶稣会 appear in [[14-wushi-weilian-dahanghai-shidai-de-riben-he-xifang-555771285]] as the Catholic missionary network already established in Japan before [[WilliamAdams|William Adams / 三浦按针]] arrives. The episode says missionaries and Iberian merchants had built influence through trade and conversion, and it gives a source figure of about 150,000 Japanese Catholics at the time.

In the source, Jesuits initially become Adams's adversaries: after learning that the stranded sailors are English and Dutch rather than Iberian Catholics, they describe them as pirates and push for execution. Later they try unsuccessfully to convert Adams. Their expulsion under [[TokugawaIeyasu|Tokugawa Ieyasu]] in 1614 marks the policy turn from selective openness toward control of Christian risk.

## Key Claims
- Jesuit influence makes Japan a contested contact zone before Adams arrives.
- Their conflict with Adams is not only doctrinal; it is bound to trade rivalry, information control, and [[ConfessionalTradeConflict]].
- The source uses Jesuit expulsion to show [[TokugawaForeignTradeAmbivalence]] hardening under security pressure.
- In EP259, Jesuit knowledge is both a public-science channel and a political vulnerability: [[CalendarReformLegitimacy|calendar usefulness]] creates access, while religious foreignness creates backlash.
