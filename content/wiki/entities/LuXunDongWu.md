## Overview

[[LuXunDongWu|陆逊]] is an Eastern Wu commander whose early profile joins field expansion and critic-responsive personnel judgment to perceptive, low-profile information warfare against Guan Yu.

## Current Profile

Lu Xun first appears as a title-history comparator: [[LuFan|吕范]]'s early `都督` is a lower-status discipline office, not the later high command associated with Lu Xun. His substantive profile credits him with campaigns against mountain forces in Kuaiji, cooperation with He Qi, growing military capacity, [[SunQuan|孙权]]'s patronage, and marriage to [[SunCe|孙策]]'s daughter.

The military-growth story carries a civilian-cost qualification. [[ChunyuShiLateHan|淳于式]] complains that Lu Xun's requisitions burden local people. Lu Xun acknowledges the disruption, credits Chunyu Shi's role in supporting operations, and recommends that Sun Quan use him. The episode treats this as public-interest judgment under criticism, not as proof that the original civilian burden was harmless.

Hanji 1168 adds the 219 Jingzhou operation. Lu Xun independently recognizes that [[GuanYu|关羽]]'s recent success, contempt for lesser-known officers, and [[LyuMeng|吕蒙]]'s apparent illness create an opening. Lyu Meng keeps the existing plan secret but later recommends Lu Xun precisely because his ability is not yet matched by threatening public reputation. After succession, Lu Xun sends a deferential letter that fits Guan Yu's expectations and reportedly helps induce further transfer of rear-defense troops toward Fancheng.

This page remains distinct from [[LuXun|鲁迅]], the modern writer, because both normalize to `LuXun`.

## Key Characteristics

- Eastern Wu commander associated with a later, higher-scope use of `都督`.
- Field officer whose campaigns expand his force and standing.
- Sun Quan protégé joined to the ruling family through marriage.
- Commander whose operations are reported to impose civilian burdens.
- Critic-responsive leader who separates personal offense from institutional usefulness.
- Perceptive low-profile officer whose apparent harmlessness and deferential communication become operational assets.

## Qualifications

Hanji 994 supplies no biography beyond title comparison. Hanji 1161 is a structured summary rather than a cited historical study and does not establish campaign scale, the identity and status of the mountain groups, requisition mechanics, civilian harm, marriage chronology, complaint wording, or whether Chunyu Shi was promoted. Hanji 1168 likewise does not independently establish the conversations, secrecy, letter wording, Guan Yu's psychology, troop movement, or causal effect of praise. The favorable moral frame does not erase the burdens attributed to Lu Xun's operations, and the later deception should not be reduced to personality without the surrounding strategic conditions.

## What Changed

- Added Lu Xun's independent recognition of the 219 Jingzhou opening.
- Added low public profile and deferential signaling as operational assets.
- Preserved uncertainty around letters, psychology, and the causal effect on troop allocation.
