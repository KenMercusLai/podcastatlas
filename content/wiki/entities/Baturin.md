# Baturin / 巴图林

## Overview

Baturin was [[IvanMazepa|Ivan Mazepa]]'s fortified Cossack capital and the supply base that [[CharlesXII|Charles XII]] expected to anchor his turn into Ukraine. Its rapid capture and destruction by Menshikov's forces removed food, powder, shelter, and political momentum from the Swedish-Cossack alliance.

## Current Profile

Mazepa leaves Baturin with a small loyal retinue to join Charles, expecting the fortress to support the arriving Swedes. Peter instead orders Menshikov to prevent other Cossacks from joining the defection. Russian dragoons storm the city, destroy its stores and defenses, and kill soldiers and civilians in what the episode presents as a massacre.

The city's fall matters on several levels at once: it denies the Swedes an operational base, demonstrates that Russia can move faster than the alliance, and supplies a terror signal reinforced by Mazepa's public denunciation, ritual deposition, and excommunication. When Charles arrives, the Ukrainian option remains geographically open but materially hollow.

## Key Characteristics

- It is simultaneously a fortress, supply depot, administrative capital, and political rallying point.
- Its value rises sharply after Charles abandons the Moscow route and turns south.
- Russian speed converts Mazepa's promised base into a destroyed resource before the Swedes can use it.
- Violence against soldiers and civilians serves both immediate military denial and wider deterrence.
- Its destruction links logistics warfare to the coercive containment of political defection.

## Qualifications

The episode's figure of six thousand dead and its characterization of the killings remain source-scoped pending broader evidence. The destruction is presented here both as military supply denial and political terror; the relative weight of those purposes, the assault sequence, survivor experience, and later memory require specialized sources.
