# Citadel Securities

## Overview
Citadel Securities is an electronic market maker appearing in the wiki both as a recipient of retail brokerage order flow and as one of the specialized quantitative firms reshaping modern trading.

## Current Profile
The GameStop episode says Robinhood sent customer orders to market makers such as Citadel Securities and earned routing revenue. This makes the firm part of the economic chain behind “zero commission”: retail flow can be valuable to an execution venue even when the customer pays no explicit ticket charge. E259 broadens that narrow routing profile by placing Citadel Securities among machine- and model-driven firms active in stocks, ETFs, and related assets, where profitability depends on the integration of data, models, low-latency infrastructure, execution, and risk control.

## Key Characteristics
- Market maker receiving retail brokerage order flow.
- Named counterparty in the episode's payment-for-order-flow explanation.
- Part of the market structure connecting customer trades, spreads, execution, and broker revenue.
- Distinct subject from the investment firm represented by [[Citadel]].
- Member of the source's broader class of specialized quantitative and electronic trading firms.
- Operates in a domain where automated scale increases the importance of execution and hard risk controls.

## Qualifications
The GameStop source uses the shorter name “Citadel” in a market-maker discussion; this page maps that reference to Citadel Securities to avoid conflating it with the separate investment firm. Neither source supplies comparative execution-quality data, proprietary strategies, audited performance, or evidence of customer harm or improper conduct.
