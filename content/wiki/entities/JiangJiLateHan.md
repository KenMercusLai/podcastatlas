# 蒋济 / Jiang Ji (late Han)

## Overview
蒋济 / Jiang Ji is a Cao-side official whose current wiki profile combines information deception at Hefei with an accurate warning against coercive population movement on the Yangtze frontier.

## Current Profile
Hanji 1112 places Jiang Ji at [[HefeiLateHan|合肥]] when rain, damaged defenses, a prolonged siege, and an epidemic-obstructed relief force narrow the defenders' options. He proposes replacing unavailable manpower with a credible information effect: announce that Zhang Xi has arrived with forty thousand troops and send multiple letters so that enemy interception appears to reveal rather than transmit the claim.

The result is a bounded case of [[FalseReinforcementDeception|虚构援军欺敌]]. Jiang Ji does not prove the false number through combat; he makes the opposing side infer that it has discovered private coordination and may soon face an inner-outer attack. The source says [[SunQuan|孙权]] accepts that inference and withdraws.

Hanji 1134 adds a policy-advice case. When [[CaoCao|曹操]] proposes moving civilians away from the Sun-controlled frontier, Jiang Ji distinguishes the current setting from the earlier Yan-Baima evacuation: Cao Cao has already defeated Yuan Shao, the population is settled and not disloyal, and coercion may create the flight it is meant to prevent. Cao Cao hears but does not adopt the advice; the episode reports that more than one hundred thousand households cross the Yangtze into Sun territory.

Cao Cao later acknowledges that Jiang Ji's forecast was correct and appoints him administrator of [[DanyangCommandery|丹阳郡]]. Because Danyang is under Sun control in the episode's account, the appointment is better treated as recognition of judgment than evidence that Jiang Ji exercised ordinary territorial administration there.

## Key Characteristics
- Diagnoses an enemy-estimation problem when direct reinforcement is unavailable.
- Uses apparently intercepted communication to authenticate a false troop claim.
- Converts uncertainty about relief timing into pressure on a besieger.
- Distinguishes a superficially similar precedent from changed political and civilian conditions.
- Predicts that coercion can turn a protective relocation order into mass flight toward an adversary.
- Receives retrospective recognition after advice is ignored and the predicted harm occurs.

- Operational result: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1112-bei-digu-de-caowei-mingjiang-zhanchang-shang-you-duo-meng-lkqecaphucolkdcsutsvjyxounln|Hanji 1112]] says deliberately intercepted messages persuade Sun Quan to burn siege equipment and withdraw.
- Context-sensitive warning: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1134-caocao-meiyou-cuanhan-chengdi-zhi-xin-llg1ubbjxxuwjyuoallhd8mkecod|Hanji 1134]] says Jiang Ji rejects direct transfer of the Yan-Baima precedent because the strategic situation and population's settled condition have changed.
- Forecast and recognition: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1134-caocao-meiyou-cuanhan-chengdi-zhi-xin-llg1ubbjxxuwjyuoallhd8mkecod|Hanji 1134]] reports mass southward flight after the order and Cao Cao's later acknowledgment and appointment of Jiang Ji.

## Qualifications
This is not a full biography. Jiang Ji's titles, the exact number and route of messengers, the role of the provincial inspector, the real relief force's condition, Sun Quan's complete decision process, troop and household figures, exact wording of the advice, and the legal or practical meaning of the Danyang appointment remain source-scoped.

## What Changed
- Added Jiang Ji's context-sensitive warning against coercive frontier relocation.
- Added the reported mass flight that validates his forecast and Cao Cao's retrospective recognition.
- Qualified the Danyang appointment as recognition rather than demonstrated territorial control.
