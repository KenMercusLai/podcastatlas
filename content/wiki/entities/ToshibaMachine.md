# Toshiba Machine / 东芝机械

## Overview
Toshiba Machine is the company at the center of the episode's account of an illicit 1980s machine-tool sale to the Soviet Union and the resulting U.S.-Japan export-control dispute.

## Current Profile
[[2b2e96d8aea7-2b2e96d8aea7]] says Toshiba Machine supplied large precision machine tools through intermediaries using false specifications and end-use descriptions. The United States argued the tools could improve submarine propeller production; U.S. legislators dramatized the dispute by smashing consumer Toshiba radios even though the alleged transaction involved industrial machinery. The episode uses the mismatch between public theater and the eventual penalty to illustrate weakened late-stage [[CoCom]] enforcement.

## Key Characteristics
- Industrial machinery company distinguished from the consumer radios used in the political protest.
- Allegedly supplied controlled precision machine tools to a Soviet importer through Japanese intermediaries.
- Appears with Norway's Kongsberg in the episode's hardware-and-software transaction chain.
- Became a symbol of U.S.-Japan trade and security tension beyond the specific transaction.

## Evidence
Transaction account:
- [[2b2e96d8aea7-2b2e96d8aea7]] describes eight large machine tools, false export descriptions, intermediaries, Soviet delivery, and software supplied by Kongsberg.

Political and enforcement aftermath:
- [[2b2e96d8aea7-2b2e96d8aea7]] recounts the congressional radio-smashing protest, a Japanese apology, limited export sanctions, and prosecution of managers.
