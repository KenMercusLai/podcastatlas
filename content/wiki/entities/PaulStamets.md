# Paul Stamets

## Overview
Paul Stamets is a mycologist and mushroom-product entrepreneur who appears on [[HubermanLab]] to discuss fungal ecology, medicinal-mushroom research, psilocybin, mycoremediation, and speculative models of mycelial communication.

## Current Profile
The source presents Stamets as both a field collector and culture-library builder who moves between ecological observation, laboratory screening, commercial product development, clinical collaboration, and public scientific imagination. His strongest contribution is to make fungi legible as organisms and networks with ecological, biochemical, and research value. His claims require unusually visible evidence boundaries because the discussion moves quickly among controlled trials, cell culture, observational studies, anecdotes, traditional use, commercial products, and openly speculative hypotheses.

Stamets discloses that he founded a company selling mushroom supplements and says sales help fund research. That disclosure is relevant but does not resolve the conflict: independent replication and product-specific characterization remain necessary, especially for [[MedicinalMushroomImmuneModulation]] and [[ComplexNaturalProductSynergy]].

## Key Characteristics
- Treats applied mycology as a bridge among ecosystem science, natural-product research, health, and environmental remediation.
- Maintains fungal cultures and describes collaborations involving antiviral screening and early clinical research.
- Advocates studying whole fungal preparations rather than assuming one isolated molecule explains every effect.
- Supports regulated, screened, and integrated psychedelic research while acknowledging psychiatric risk and uncertain microdosing evidence.
- Uses “natural intelligence” as a metaphor for distributed biological responsiveness, sometimes extending into speculative communication hypotheses.
- Combines research advocacy with ownership of a mushroom-supplement company, creating a material conflict-of-interest boundary.
