# Barbara Underwood

## Overview

Barbara Underwood was New York's advocate in the *New York State Rifle & Pistol Association v. Bruen* oral argument discussed by [[guns-part-2-getting-out-of-dodge]]. Her response to the justices centers the system-level risks of widespread firearm carrying in crowded public space.

## Current Profile

Underwood argues that the safety question changes when many licensed carriers share an enclosed subway car or dense public area. Fear, imperfect marksmanship, fights, misidentification, and simultaneous reactions can turn one threat into crossfire or accelerating violence. Arriving police may also be unable to distinguish the initiating aggressor from carriers responding to the incident.

The episode contrasts this interaction model with [[SamuelAlito]]'s image of one law-abiding commuter defending against a criminal. It also emphasizes Underwood's long professional and personal experience in New York, including riding the subway, while criticizing the justices' portrayal of ordinary city movement as a frontier confrontation.

## Key Characteristics

- Represents New York's defense of restricted handgun licensing in the episode's account of *Bruen*.
- Treats density and enclosure as central variables in public-carry safety.
- Emphasizes mistakes and escalation among lawful carriers, not only malicious misuse.
- Identifies armed-bystander ambiguity as a danger to responding police.
- Brings direct New York and subway experience to the factual dispute highlighted by the episode.
