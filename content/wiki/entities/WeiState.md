# 魏国 / Wei State

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-04-yurang-wei-zhiyao-fuchou-ln34tydha6iqwlrgmw7iugqfgfik]] adds Wei's pre-rise legitimacy branch. Wei participates with Han and Zhao in dividing [[ZhiYao|智瑶]]'s territory, and [[WeiWenhou|魏斯 / 魏文侯]] is later named as one of the three Jin leaders formally recognized by [[ZhouWeilieWang|周威烈王]]. The episode also previews Wei Wenhou as the strongest and most prestigious of the three recognized leaders.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-05-guangcai-duomu-de-weiwenhou-1-lmkxzbmwhw3nhurape-eebydqcdl]] explains that prestige through ruler conduct. Under [[WeiWenhou|魏文侯]], Wei becomes attractive to scholars and worthy people through [[TalentAttractionThroughRespect|礼贤下士式人才吸附]], builds credibility through [[SmallTrustBuildsLargeTrust|小信诚则大信立]], and gains Han-Zhao trust through [[SymmetricInterstateCredibility|对称外交信用]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-06-weiwenhou-xuan-shui-dang-zaixiang-ne-1-lk-6ejgmjhvukqgtlz2lowu8qx-v]] adds Wei's internal personnel-selection branch. [[WeiWenhou|魏文侯]] asks [[LiKe|李克]] to help choose between [[WeiCheng|魏成]] and [[DiHuang|翟黄]], making Wei a case where early state strength depends on [[SituationalCharacterTests|character testing]] and [[TalentReferralQuality|referral quality]] as well as battlefield command.

魏国 / Wei State appears in [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-06-weiwenhou-xuan-shui-dang-zaixiang-ne-2-lmfzo4sl9nd5kz3mrxdl1dl9efei]] through the episode title's [[WeiWenhou|魏文侯]] frame and through [[WuQi|吴起]]'s military effectiveness. The source says Wei became one of the fastest-rising strong states in the early [[WarringStatesPeriod|战国时期]] because of Wu Qi's command.

In this page, Wei is not yet a full institutional history. Its wiki role is to host the episode's claim that a state can convert discipline, training load, commander credibility, and morale into interstate power.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-07-liangqi-cisha-1-lvkp-ju7pf5z5cz3thojv3s2fkyx]] adds Wei to two new early [[ZhouAnWang|周安王]] threads: [[QinState|秦国]] attacks Wei in 401 BCE, and [[WangYinglin|王应麟]] later reads the Guo mountain landslide as a [[NaturalDisasterPoliticalOmen]] for Wei's eventual disaster.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-07-liangqi-cisha-2-lhngst3xsnyjrmzh1cvheocvvsqm]] adds the death of [[WeiWenhou|魏文侯]] and accession of [[WeiWuhou|魏武侯]], turning Wei from a military-rise case into a succession case as well.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-08-yidai-mingjiang-wuqi-cansi-1-lq5r3xe-3y7al9nkbc8kyhntipll]] turns that succession into an internal-governance problem. [[WeiWuhou|魏武侯]] praises West River terrain, but [[WuQi|吴起]] argues for [[VirtueOverNaturalBarriers]]. The chancellor dispute between Wu Qi and [[TianWenWeiChancellor|田文]], followed by [[GongshuWeiChancellor|公叔]]'s trap, makes Wei a case where state strength depends on ruler-minister trust and transition management, not only on armies.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-08-yidai-mingjiang-wuqi-cansi-2-lvaqlgyccrugfo0l-wuwf1mt1kcm]] closes the Wei portion by having Wu Qi realize the court trap has made him unsafe. He flees to [[ChuState|楚国]], so Wei's branch now also explains how internal suspicion can export a talented but dangerous minister to a rival state.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-08-yidai-mingjiang-wuqi-cansi-3-ltca-ikuzo9ej7a0lfrgwv2-wulb]] adds a brief 383 BCE notice: Wei attacks [[ZhaoState|赵国]] and defeats Zhao at Tutai. The episode says [[ZizhiTongjian|《资治通鉴》]] gives little motive or aftermath, but reads the event as evidence that tension within the former Jin-derived states is now visible.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-09-zhanguo-shidai-de-shijie-dazhan-losmum91vjpqfymywelj60o7-vbl]] returns Wei to the center of interstate war. Wei joins Han and Zhao to rescue [[YanState|燕国]] from [[QiState|齐国]], is attacked by Zhao, fights alongside Qi against Zhao, is struck opportunistically by [[ChuSuwang|楚肃王]], loses control of [[ZhongshanState|中山国]] after [[WeiZhi|魏挚]] is driven out, and finally joins Han and Zhao in ending [[JinState|晋国]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-10-yi-jiazu-danwei-you-mayi-chi-daxiang-de-jingshen-1-lq3t7lwpmq1oyb8zmmmn2tywnu8d]] adds a 372 BCE Zhao-Wei exchange: [[ZhaoState|赵国]] takes seventy-three "du-bi" settlements from Wei, but Wei defeats Zhao at Beiling. The episode uses the wording to caution that the captured places were not seventy-three major cities.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-10-yi-jiazu-danwei-you-mayi-chi-daxiang-de-jingshen-2-lmnqfjmydygqnfec6xhzpixralvi]] adds Wei's next cluster of pressure: in 371 BCE Wei attacks [[ChuState|楚国]] and takes Luyang, then after [[WeiWuhou|魏武侯]] dies the unclear succession between [[LiangHuiWang|魏罃]] and [[GongzhongHuan|公中缓]] creates internal disorder. The same source later notes Wei defeating [[ZhaoState|赵国]] at Huai.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-10-yi-jiazu-danwei-you-mayi-chi-daxiang-de-jingshen-3-lmhj4le2mwu1yhhl-qmuoqkfmhjf]] shows that the succession disorder nearly destroys Wei. [[HanState|韩国]] and [[ZhaoState|赵国]] defeat Wei at [[ZhuozeBattle|浊泽之战]] and besiege [[Anyi|安邑]], but Wei survives because the victors split over whether to partition the state or install [[GongzhongHuan|公中缓]] under land concessions. [[LiangHuiWang|魏罃]] then kills Gongzhong Huan and consolidates rule.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-11-shijie-fengqi-yunyong-1-lt60b2gddiosf5kade35uopwohi5]] shows the immediate aftermath of that survival. [[QiState|齐国]] attacks Wei in 368 BCE, and [[LiangHuiWang|魏罃 / 梁惠王]] seeks peace to stabilize internal rule. Wei then joins [[HanState|韩国]] against [[QinState|秦国]] but loses at Luoyang Jinjiao, attacks [[SongState|宋国]] in 365 BCE, and faces continued Qin attack at Shaoliang in 363 BCE.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-11-shijie-fengqi-yunyong-2-llzzalowv5vmbvgd6l-jujltkbyf]] extends the same pressure into 362 BCE. Wei defeats [[HanState|韩国]] and [[ZhaoState|赵国]] at Hui, then loses to [[QinState|秦国]] at [[Shaoliang|少梁]], where a Wei figure rendered as [[GongshuWeiChancellor|公孙措 / 公叔痤]] is captured. The episode reads this as a puzzling Three Jin pattern: Wei can still fight former Jin-derived rivals even when the western Qin threat remains obvious.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-12-gongsunyang-qiuzhi-lu-shang-de-la-guanxi-zou-houmen-lvv4-bz8sm9scezci-o-msapkvil]] turns Wei's pressure into a talent-retention failure. [[GongshuWeiChancellor|公叔痤]] recognizes [[ShangYang|公孙鞅 / 商鞅]] and warns [[LiangHuiWang|魏惠王 / 梁惠王]] either to use him or kill him, but Wei does neither. Shang Yang's later Qin career therefore becomes an exported-risk case: a state that ignores a dangerous talent can lose him to the rival already applying [[QinEastwardPressure|pressure]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-14-qiweiwang-buming-zeyi-yiming-jingren-lrs6rbifyiqrojxg0mccpg7sf9rv]] adds Wei's 358-357 BCE defensive-diplomatic response. After Qin defeats Han at West Mountain, [[LiangHuiWang|魏惠王]] sends [[LongJia|龙甲]] west to build a wall, making [[WeiGreatWallAntiQinDefense|魏长城防秦]] a concrete extension of [[QinEastwardPressure|Qin eastward pressure]]. The next year, Wei and Han hold a high-level meeting, probably between Liang Hui Wang and [[HanZhaohou|韩昭侯]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-15-jiu-bu-chun-yinfa-de-shijie-dazhan-li-iojxthq8komhlhyumho3yvtem]] adds Wei at a hinge between display, diplomacy, and war. [[LiangHuiWang|魏惠王]] first contrasts himself with [[QiWeiwang|齐威王]] by boasting about luminous pearls; he then meets [[QinXiaogong|秦孝公]] at Duping in a negotiation the episode treats as unsuccessful because Qin soon defeats Wei at Yuanli and occupies [[Shaoliang|少梁]]. The same source makes Wei's later siege of [[Handan|邯郸]] an opportunistic reading of Chu's distraction inside [[LuWineHandanWarCascade|鲁酒薄而邯郸围]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-16-sunbin-pangjuan-enyuan-jiuge-guiling-zhi-zhan-lryhzkc1-xsvfjhvicu8yxa9d2jr]] adds Wei as both oppressor and vulnerable target. [[PangJuan|庞涓]] serves Wei and traps [[SunBin|孙膑]] there, while the Wei army's siege of [[Handan|邯郸]] leaves [[Daliang|大梁]] exposed to Qi's [[EncirclingWeiToRescueZhao|围魏救赵]] plan and defeat at [[GuilingBattle|桂陵之战]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-17-hanzhaohou-he-shenbuhai-lghjmjhq9qs4se96c5ofgz6caqof]] shows Wei reacting to the aftermath of that pressure. [[ShangYang|公孙鞅 / 商鞅]] attacks Wei at [[Anyi|安邑]] and [[Guyang|固阳]], while coalition pressure around [[XianglingWarringStates|襄陵]] keeps the eastern front unstable. [[LiangHuiWang|魏惠王]] returns [[Handan|邯郸]] to [[ZhaoState|赵国]] and joins the [[ZhangshuiAlliance|漳水之盟]], making reconciliation with former enemies a response to [[QinEastwardPressure|Qin pressure]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-19-maling-zhi-zhan-lhbapt09x2ptdqx0uopkp8apbada]] returns Wei to the eastern war field. [[PangJuan|庞涓]] attacks [[HanState|韩国]], but [[QiState|齐国]]'s delayed intervention toward [[Daliang|大梁]] forces him to leave the Han front. Wei then raises additional troops under [[PrinceShenWei|太子申]], making the campaign a case of both battlefield overconfidence and [[CrownPrinceCommandRisk|crown-prince command risk]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-20-pangjuan-si-ci-shu-xia-ls-8-3jhz5q1c5ca11mhdzmz4wg4]] completes that eastern-war failure. [[PangJuan|庞涓]] misreads [[SunBin|孙膑]]'s [[DecreasingStovesDeception|reduced-stove deception]], abandons the slower main force, and dies in the Maling ambush. Wei's army then falls into disorder, Qi captures [[PrinceShenWei|太子申]], and the episode frames the loss as a major blow after which Wei's power declines sharply.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-22-gongsunyang-zha-wei-gongzi-ang-lss1l7lojfwgwdtcje7m5ijodpxi]] follows that decline onto the western front in 340 BCE. [[ShangYang|公孙鞅 / 商鞅]] argues that Wei is now vulnerable enough for Qin to attack, and [[LiangHuiWang|魏惠王]] sends another royal-family commander, [[GongziAng|公子卬 / 公子昂]], to respond. Gongzi Ang's capture by [[PeaceParleyEntrapment|和谈诱捕]] turns Wei's military problem into command vulnerability: after losing [[PrinceShenWei|太子申]] at Maling, Wei again exposes a high-status family member to battlefield consequences.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-23-weihuiwang-hui-buyong-gongshu-zhi-yan-lveaqklo7oncmqudgmdk7l03qhsf]] gives the material consequence. Qin breaks Wei after Gongzi Ang is taken, Wei cedes Hexi, [[LiangHuiWang|魏惠王]] leaves [[Anyi|安邑]] for [[Daliang|大梁]], and the king regrets ignoring [[GongshuWeiChancellor|公叔痤]]'s advice. The same source also makes Wei's later refusal to admit Shang Yang a credibility verdict on the original deception.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-27-weiguan-weihuiwang-he-qiweiwang-shi-za-shangye-hupeng-lminlxhrm5bethub9kyy-8blimrb]] adds Wei's eastern diplomatic repair after [[MalingBattle|马陵之战]]. [[LiangHuiWang|魏惠王 / 梁惠王]] wants revenge against [[QiState|齐国]], but [[HuiShi|惠施]] argues for [[LowPostureStatecraft|low-posture statecraft]] instead. By proposing [[QiWeiwang|齐威王]]'s kingly title and accepting reciprocal recognition at [[XuzhouMutualKingship|徐州相王]], Wei trades immediate pride for face-saving strategic room.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-28-hanzhaohou-de-kuaile-ni-xiangxiang-budao-ln2tbxniz4kvwogbwp32exmdokz8]] shows that Wei's low posture can externalize risk. [[ChuWeiwang|楚威王]] attacks Qi after the title exchange, while Wei stands aside despite being the other party to [[XuzhouMutualKingship|徐州相王]]. The episode therefore treats Wei's tactic as effective for breathing room but costly to Qi through [[StatusOrderBacklash|名分秩序反弹]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-29-suqin-lizhi-shuangwen-zhishi-gaibian-mingyun-lrf5cqvoriyep2lkbozju-lyjaxv]] makes that non-rescue explicit: Wei had wanted Chu to pressure Qi and therefore has little reason to save Qi from the backlash. The same source also records a compact Wei succession notice, with [[WeiChenghou|魏成侯]] dying and [[WeiPinghou|魏平侯]] succeeding.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-31-hezong-lianheng-weiou-qin-lncpgmxrojc0y1meg9a3ul-o9nih]] returns Wei to the western-pressure line. Qin attacks Wei, defeats more than forty thousand troops, captures [[LongJia|龙甲]], and takes Diaoyin. The episode uses Wei's defeat as the event that threatens [[SuQin|苏秦]]'s [[HezongAlliance|合纵]] schedule before the coalition is built.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-32-shishang-shouwei-lianheguo-mishuzhang-suqin-lgfkk45yq1c0yblequ-d1t7mol3]] moves Wei from the timing problem to the persuasion circuit. Su Qin's Wei pitch includes a large army list with different infantry categories, chariots, and cavalry. The host warns that the figures may be inflated, but reads the list as evidence for [[WarringStatesMilitaryTransformation|Warring States military transformation]]: chariot warfare is no longer the center, infantry scale has grown, and cavalry is present but not yet dominant.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-34-qinguo-zongli-ruhe-wanzhuan-liuguo-lsenzwp0kr5wscb0btvuzmkibriq]] shows how fragile that position remains once Qin resumes pressure. Wei is maneuvered into anti-Zhao action with [[QiState|齐国]], then seeks peace with [[QinState|秦国]] by ceding [[Yinjin|殷晋]]. Qin later presses [[Jiaoyi|焦邑]] and [[QuwoWarringStates|曲沃]], receives [[Shaoliang|少梁]] and [[HexiWarringStates|河西]] land, and finally turns the returned [[PuyangWarringStates|蒲阳]] into the loss of [[Shangjun|上郡]]'s fifteen counties through [[ZhangYiStrategist|张仪]]'s [[StrategicConcessionDiplomacy|欲擒故纵式让利外交]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-35-lishishang-diyi-qibing-jiaofu-shi-ta-lnxgnu1czkl-ftfb3gq9kzxwnxdk]] qualifies that pressure by showing Qin giving some land back. The episode says [[QinState|秦国]] returns [[Jiaoyi|焦邑]] and [[QuwoWarringStates|曲沃]] to Wei, but reads the move as [[ZhangYiStrategist|张仪]]'s "半拉半打" strategy rather than a clean reversal of Qin expansion. Wei is still being managed through bilateral pressure and reassurance.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-36-weisijun-de-diwang-zhishu-lt5cpdm9yqdj4erwykd0kubfpucz]] gives Wei two scenes as the larger neighbor of [[WeyState|卫国]]. [[LiangHuiWang|魏惠王 / 梁惠王]] initially refuses to return [[WeySijun|卫嗣君]]'s escaped prisoner, then returns him after hearing the [[NoSmallMattersInGovernance|治无小乱无大]] argument. The [[Zhanguoce|《战国策》]] supplement also has [[WuxiaWarringStates|武夏]] press Wei Hui Wang to receive a Wey envoy by comparing the neglect to Wei's need to maintain relations with [[QinState|秦国]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-37-suqin-liuguo-jituan-ceo-de-bukan-qingshi-fj2kdqlaxzyy2nqtcutktuwrq5fo]] returns Wei to the center of strategist movement. [[ZhangYiStrategist|张仪]] attacks Wei for [[QinState|秦国]] and takes [[ShanchengWarringStates|陕城]], but the episode also says [[GongsunYan|犀首公孙衍]] has left Qin for Wei and begins contacting the lords for anti-Qin alliance work. After the [[Niesang|啮桑]] meeting, Zhang Yi himself comes to Wei as chief minister, turning Wei into the court where rival Qin-facing and anti-Qin strategies overlap.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-38-gao-jianzhi-de-zhangyi-bu-yiban-lobhcpz679psy54k0iduccqkfcrn]] explains why that overlap is so unstable. [[ZhangYiStrategist|张仪]] asks Wei to lead other states in serving Qin; when [[LiangHuiWang|魏惠王 / 梁惠王]] refuses, [[QinState|秦国]] attacks and takes [[QuwoWarringStates|曲沃]] and [[PingzhouWarringStates|平州]]. The episode also asks why Wei would trust Zhang Yi and [[GongsunYan|犀首公孙衍]] at all, answering that weakened Wei may need mobile specialists who know Qin even if their personal alignments are unclear. Wei therefore becomes the test case for [[WarringStatesCareeristMobility|乱世职业经理人式流动]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-39-lishishang-zhangyi-shi-zenyang-nudui-qiangdi-de-lv2gc2wtcxuhugfgixk7rbbhpa6x]] turns that instability inward. Zhang Yi and [[HuiShi|惠施 / 惠师]] compete over Wei's grand alignment: Zhang Yi pushes a Qin-Han-Wei attack on Qi and Chu, while Hui Shi urges a Qi-Chu line and non-action. Zhang Yi wins more ministers, Hui Shi flees, and [[ChenZhenWarringStates|陈枕]] then finds the Wei court pre-framed against him. Wei is therefore not only a weak state using mobile specialists; it is also a court where those specialists can reshape internal factional reality.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-44-yi-ge-gongsi-weishenme-buneng-you-liang-wei-ceo-lsz0xk6-0wwznkc62k-xkoxgz-r]] cites Wei retrospectively through [[MiuLiu|谬刘]]'s warning to [[HanXuanhuiwang|韩宣惠王]]. The episode names Wei's paired use of [[ZhangYiStrategist|张仪]] and [[GongsunYan|犀首公孙衍]] as an example of [[DualExecutivePowerRisk|双首长权力风险]], tying the earlier Wei court-mobility branch to the danger of rival high ministers and foreign leverage.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-45-yi-shi-wei-jian-junwang-daodi-gai-bu-gai-sharen-chengxing-lo3cok7cfwzrxesp-i8miz1fc1db]] gives Wei a succession and alliance role at the opening of 周纪三. The source says Wei lowers its title posture in 320 BCE, [[LiangHuiWang|魏惠王 / 梁惠王]] dies in 319 BCE, and [[WeiXiangwang|魏襄王]] succeeds. Wei also joins the 318 BCE five-state attack on Qin, but the coalition collapses at [[HanguPass|函谷关]] through [[CoalitionSelfPreservationFailure|联盟自保失灵]]. Later in the same episode, Song Kang Wang's expansion and final destruction include Wei as both victim and attacker.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-46-ma-ni-shi-ge-erbaiwu-de-lishi-laiyuan-lkz5hiamaojtvanllbunecy3g3ff]] adds the military cost of that failed anti-Qin posture. The episode says [[QinState|秦国]] pursues Han and Wei eastward under [[Chulizi|樗里子]], and that [[XiuyuBattle|修鱼]] becomes unavoidable because the fighting has reached central-plains terrain close to Wei's capital space. Wei is not named as the defeated army in every detail, but it is part of the retreat and pressure field that leads Qin from Xiuyu toward [[ZhuozeBattle|浊泽]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-51-chuhuaiwang-qin-xiao-er-kan-jiujiu-ruhe-mie-le-ni-ltfkdlivomnvux5xodh2rlfqf6ac]] shows Wei as an opportunistic pressure actor during Chu's overextension. As [[ChuHuaiwang|楚怀王]] follows [[QinState|秦国]] through [[Wuguan|武关]] and [[Shangyu|商於]] toward [[LantianBattle|蓝田之战]], Wei and [[HanState|韩国]] threaten the [[ChuState|楚国]] capital region and rear. Wei's role is not the main campaign, but it helps make Chu's retreat urgent.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-55-zhangyi-tongshi-guiren-qinhuiwang-lsclfbwn06fgzooipf5up9sdkedy]] returns Wei to Zhang Yi's final refuge. After [[YingSi|秦惠文王]] dies, Zhang Yi persuades [[QinWuwang|秦武王]] to send him to Wei on the claim that [[QiState|齐国]] will attack any state hosting him. Wei is then attacked by Qi, [[WeiXiangwang|魏襄王]] becomes afraid, and Zhang Yi's disclosure channel causes [[QiXuanWang|齐宣王]] to withdraw. Wei becomes the court where [[SelfPreservationAsStateStrategy|以自保包装国策]] and [[YinmouYangmouConversion|阴谋阳谋转换]] produce Zhang Yi's final year in office.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-60-zhongguo-di-yi-wei-bachi-chaozheng-de-taihou-jingshi-ta-ltazis9qo0dwvwmzxikk8mmpvosc]] adds a 303-302 BCE reversal. Qin attacks Wei and takes [[PubanWarringStates|蒲阪]], [[YangjinWarringStates|阳晋]], and [[FenglingWarringStates|封陵]], all treated by the episode as important Hexi-Hedong crossing points. Wei then joins [[QiState|齐国]] and [[HanState|韩国]] in attacking [[ChuState|楚国]] after Chu moves toward Qin. Once the [[MiHeng|芈横]] hostage incident breaks Qin-Chu relations, [[WeiXiangwang|魏襄王]] meets [[QinZhaoxiangwang|秦昭襄王]] and [[HanYing|韩婴]] at [[LinjinWarringStates|临晋]], and Qin returns Puban to Wei.
