## Overview
李纪 / Li Ji is the historian interviewed in [[100-nian-qian-dongbei-nvhai-xie-gei-bali-de-xin-duitan-lishixuezhe-liji-putongren-ruhe-chuanguo-da-lishi-3d36f181-94ad-4883-8db3-5f040b5ed45b]] about Northeast Catholic villages, missionary archives, ordinary women, and how small traces can open large historical questions.

## Current Profile
The source presents Li Ji as a professional historian shaped by Chinese humanistic training, U.S. doctoral archival training, and long work across English and Chinese scholarly worlds. Her research began with Chinese letters found in the [[ParisForeignMissionsSociety|巴黎外方传教会]] archive and expanded through maps, genealogies, field visits to [[SantaiZiCatholicVillage|三台子]], descendants, and hundreds of missionary family letters. Her method is defined by patient evidence work and a refusal to reduce historical actors to single labels.

## Key Characteristics
- She uses small, concrete archival traces to reconstruct the world around ordinary people who rarely enter formal history.
- Her Northeast work connects migration, Catholic villages, treaty-era mission networks, gender, literacy, and local society.
- She combines archive work with field visits, descendant contact, genealogy, and oral-history listening.
- Her writing trajectory moves from English academic form toward more public Chinese storytelling without abandoning source discipline.
- She treats AI-era education as a shift from fact delivery toward question formation, judgment, and experiential understanding.
- She resists ending a historical life with a totalizing label, especially for figures such as [[FatherGaoNortheastMission|高神父]].
