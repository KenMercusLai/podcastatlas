# Great Fire of Rome

## Overview

The Great Fire of Rome was the nine-day urban disaster under [[Nero]] whose destruction, relief, rebuilding, Golden House development, and persecution of Christians became inseparable from the emperor's historical reputation.

## Current Profile

[[56-nero-glt7241559003]] distinguishes the fire from its most famous accusation. Nero was away from Rome when it began, returned to organize relief and rebuilding, and probably did not order the fire. Suspicion nevertheless persisted because the destruction enabled urban redesign and the construction of his Golden House on cleared central land.

The episode also makes the fire a case in political scapegoating and source criticism. Nero punished Christians through theatrical executions, but why this obscure group was selected and how the emperor encountered it remain unclear. Tacitus supplies the central narrative, whose passage and details continue to be debated.

## Key Characteristics

- Nine-day urban fire that destroyed a large part of Rome.
- Disaster to which Nero returned with relief and rebuilding measures.
- Event followed by the Golden House and therefore by durable accusations of intentional destruction.
- Crisis in which Christians became targets of spectacular imperial punishment.
- Source-critical problem shaped by hostile texts, later Christian memory, archaeology, and political reputation.

## Evidence

- Fire and response: [[56-nero-glt7241559003]] places Nero outside Rome at ignition and describes his return for relief and reconstruction.
- Motive accusation: [[56-nero-glt7241559003]] links claims of arson to beautification plans and the Golden House while judging intentional starting unlikely.
- Christian punishment: [[56-nero-glt7241559003]] describes crucifixion and burning as theatrical penalties imposed after blame shifted to Christians.
- Evidentiary boundary: [[56-nero-glt7241559003]] identifies Tacitus as the key textual source and preserves debate around the passage and Nero's awareness of Christians.

## Qualifications

The page follows one interpretive source and does not independently establish ignition, exact destruction, relief measures, rebuilding chronology, responsibility, or the scale and identity of those punished. Rejecting confidence in Neronian arson does not establish that every part of his response was benign or that later construction had no political effect.

## What Changed

- Created an event profile separating likely non-responsibility for ignition from the relief, rebuilding, scapegoating, and reputation consequences that followed.
