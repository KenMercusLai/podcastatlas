# Assembly of Vizille

## Overview

The Assembly of Vizille was the 21 July 1788 meeting organized by [[JeanJosephMounier|Jean-Joseph Mounier]] after Grenoble's [[DayOfTheTiles|Day of the Tiles]], turning provincial resistance into a program of elections, Third Estate representation, and single-chamber deliberation.

## Current Profile

The source treats Vizille as a bridge from defence of inherited liberties toward constituent politics. The meeting called for free elections and a provincial Estates-General for Dauphine, equal Third Estate numbers to clergy and nobility combined, and deliberation in one chamber. Its patriotic language of sacrifice and general will enlarged the claim beyond restoration of a court, but the source also warns that identifying one program with the nation can recode opponents as traitors.

## Key Characteristics

- Followed mass unrest in Grenoble rather than preceding it.
- Demanded free elections and a provincial representative assembly.
- Sought numerical parity for the Third Estate with both privileged orders combined.
- Favored one chamber, giving majority rule practical force.
- Joined constitutional demands to patriotic, sacrificial, and general-will rhetoric.

## Qualifications

- The source does not reproduce the full proceedings, delegate composition, or later positions of all participants.
- Calling Vizille a seismic beginning of the Revolution and assigning proto-totalitarian implications to its rhetoric remain source-scoped interpretations.

## What Changed

- Created a profile separating Vizille's constitutional program from the preceding Grenoble uprising.
