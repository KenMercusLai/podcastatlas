# Panera Bread

## Overview
Panera Bread is a U.S. bakery-cafe company developed from the St. Louis Bread Company concept under [[RonShaich|Ron Shaich]]. The bounded source presents it as a fast-casual and third-place format built around fresh bread, prepared food, quick service, and room to linger.

## Current Profile
Au Bon Pain acquired the 19-store St. Louis Bread Company in 1993 as a suburban complement to its urban stores. Larger, less expensive sites supported meetings and social use alongside food purchases, while the Panera name allowed expansion outside St. Louis without a regional identity. By 1998, Shaich judged the concept capable of national category leadership and sold the company's other divisions to direct money, people, and management toward it.

The source later frames Panera as a case of restraint before the Great Recession and accelerated investment during it. It also presents the 2017 sale to JAB Holding as an attempt to protect long-term transformation from quarterly pressure, but provides no post-acquisition evidence to establish that result.

## Key Characteristics
- Bakery-cafe format combining bread, sandwiches, soups, rapid service, and a comfortable gathering environment.
- Suburban concept derived from St. Louis Bread Company and renamed for broader national expansion.
- Beneficiary of concentrated capital and leadership after the sale of Au Bon Pain and other divisions.
- Company presented as investing countercyclically after avoiding expensive pre-recession expansion.
- Public company taken private by JAB Holding near the end of Shaich's leadership.

## Qualifications
The profile rests on one founder-centered retrospective. Store counts, sales, stock returns, cost savings, and causal explanations are source-reported. The evidence does not independently compare Panera with competitors, evaluate franchisee or employee outcomes, or establish the effects of JAB ownership after the transaction.
