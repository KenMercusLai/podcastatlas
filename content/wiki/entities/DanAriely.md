# Dan Ariely

## Overview

Dan Ariely is a behavioral economist represented in the supplied source through an experiment comparing externally imposed, self-selected, and single final deadlines.

## Current Profile

[[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-2fc443663e259ff389aeeb81792c6086-50338835-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-2fc443663e259ff389aeeb81792c6086-50338835|You, But Better]] uses Ariely's deadline study to show that unrestricted flexibility can worsen procrastination. Evenly spaced external deadlines reportedly produced the best student performance, self-imposed interim deadlines came second, and one final deadline performed worst.

## Key Characteristics

- Studies predictable departures from idealized self-control.
- Uses deadline structure to examine procrastination and precommitment.
- Shows that constraints can sometimes improve execution rather than merely reduce freedom.

## Evidence

- Deadline comparison - [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-2fc443663e259ff389aeeb81792c6086-50338835-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-2fc443663e259ff389aeeb81792c6086-50338835]] reports the three-condition student experiment and its performance ordering.

## Qualifications

The supplied episode summary does not provide the study's sample, assignment details, outcome measures, effect sizes, or replication evidence. The result does not establish that external deadlines are optimal for every task, learner, workplace, disability, or caregiving context.
