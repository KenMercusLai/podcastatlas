## Overview

鲜卑 / Xianbei is a northern frontier people whose current bounded profile now begins in 汉纪 759 as an already active pressure on [[NorthernXiongnuLateHan|Northern Xiongnu]] and a potential future resentment problem if Han restrains them for [[SouthernXiongnu|Southern Xiongnu]]'s northern return. 汉纪 765 then makes Xianbei a diplomatic-risk audience in [[YuanAnEasternHan|Yuan An]]'s warning against recognizing [[YuChuNorthernXiongnu|Yu Chu / 于除]]. Later listed sources move into recurring late-Emperor-Ling raids, [[TanshihuaiXianbei|Tanshihuai]]'s military strength, family-hostage leverage, succession weakening after Tanshihuai, [[LiuYuLateHan|Liu Yu]]'s outreach, and the anti-[[GongsunZan|Gongsun Zan]] coalition.

## Current Profile

汉纪 759 supplies the earliest current bounded layer. [[SongYiEasternHan|Song Yi]] says Xianbei have already attacked Northern Xiongnu, helping produce the weakness that [[GengBingEasternHan|Geng Bing]] wants Han to exploit. Song's objection is that if Southern Xiongnu return north and Han then restrains Xianbei for them, Xianbei may become a new border danger.

汉纪 765 supplies the next recognition-risk layer. Yuan An says Wuhuan and Xianbei had killed Youliu chanyu, so Han recognition of Youliu's brother Yu Chu as Northern Xiongnu chanyu could provoke groups that Han also needed to manage. Xianbei are not yet a campaign actor in this source; their significance is that earlier frontier violence constrains what kind of recognition package Han can credibly offer.

The 173-180 CE source sequence then presents Xianbei as recurring pressure on Han's northern and northwestern edges. 汉纪 903 through 汉纪 907 record repeated incursions into Bingzhou, Youzhou, Beidi, Hongnong, and the Guanzhong field. 汉纪 913, 汉纪 918, and 汉纪 936 add further Jiuquan, Youzhou, and Bingzhou pressure markers, often beside disasters, office churn, or court dysfunction rather than as self-contained Xianbei narratives.

汉纪 909 gives the strongest policy episode. Xianbei raiding leads Xia Yu to ask for a deep offensive, but Cai Yong warns that Xianbei had grown stronger after occupying former Xiongnu lands, gaining horses, weapons, Han fugitives, and battlefield knowledge. Tanshihuai concentrates eastern, central, and western forces and destroys the three Han columns, making Xianbei the key example for overextended frontier expedition risk. 汉纪 910 then adds a personal coercion layer when Xianbei capture Zhao Bao's mother and wife and use them as battlefield leverage.

汉纪 920 turns from pressure to leadership transition. Tanshihuai dies, He Lian succeeds with weaker capacity, He Lian is killed attacking Beidi, and Kuitou takes power because He Lian's son is too young. Later contention between Kuitou and He Lian's grown son disperses the group. 汉纪 942 and 汉纪 998 then show Xianbei as a frontier audience and coalition force: Liu Yu uses envoys and incentive-resetting during the Zhang Chun-Zhang Ju crisis, while Xianbei riders later join Xianyu Fu, Wuhuan, Liu He, and Yuan Shao's side against Gongsun Zan.

## Key Characteristics

- Frontier group whose significance begins here as anti-Northern-Xiongnu pressure and recognition-risk audience before later appearing as raider, hostage-taker, expedition opponent, pacification audience, and coalition cavalry.
- Xianbei pressure is recurrent and geographically broad across Youzhou, Bingzhou, Beidi, Jiuquan, Hongnong, and Guanzhong-related fields.
- Tanshihuai-era strength comes from former Xiongnu territory, material capture, Han fugitives, cavalry knowledge, and centralized coordination.
- Han overextension against Xianbei can convert border-defense anxiety into catastrophic expedition failure.
- Xianbei cohesion weakens after Tanshihuai through weaker succession, child-heir constraints, and later contestation.
- Han relations with Xianbei can shift through incentive-setting and coalition politics, not only battlefield outcomes.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to the listed source notes and does not provide a full Xianbei history. 汉纪 759 and 汉纪 765 use Xianbei mainly as a Han-facing balance and recognition-risk factor, not as an internal Xianbei narrative. The earlier legacy page text cited 汉纪 898, but that slug was not part of the existing frontmatter source list, so this synthesis-v1 migration preserves the canonical source inventory and does not use 汉纪 898 as evidence. The sources emphasize Han-facing frontier consequences more than Xianbei internal society, and most incursion notices are brief annal markers.
