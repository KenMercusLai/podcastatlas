# Jerusalem (Hymn)

## Overview

Jerusalem appears in this source as the strongest possible English alternative to [[GodSaveTheKing|God Save the King]] because it is specifically about [[England]] rather than the whole [[UnitedKingdom]].

## Current Profile

The episode presents Jerusalem as unusually flexible for an English anthem debate. Its text begins as a William Blake poem, with music by Hubert Parry and orchestration by Elgar, but its later public associations stretch across landscape, Glastonbury, sport, chapel, public-school culture, and film. The hosts stress that Blake's revolutionary reputation could let the song appeal to anti-monarchists as well as monarchists, even while its class and institutional associations may limit consensus.

## Key Characteristics

- English anthem alternative because its imagery is England-specific rather than broadly British.
- Blake-origin text that can be read through revolutionary as well as patriotic associations.
- Public song whose later meaning spans landscape, religion, sport, film, schools, and ceremony.
- Potential compromise alternative to [[GodSaveTheKing|God Save the King]] for [[EnglandNationalFootballTeam|England football]].
- Limited by class-coded and institutional associations that may make it less universally acceptable.
