## Overview

Huangdi Neijing / 《黄帝内经》 enters the wiki as a classical Chinese medical text invoked by [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-651-hetu-luoshu-zhi-mi-ni-neng-pojie-ma-lvkgovzsk0yqjb-vbvfxl0ikx2u9|Hanji 651]] during its setup for the next episode's Wang Mang dissection branch. The source uses the text to argue that ancient Chinese medicine already contained anatomy-facing interest in organs, vessels, blood, qi, and observable bodily structure.

## Current Profile

The page currently has a narrow source role. Hanji 651 does not offer a full history of the Huangdi Neijing; it cites the text as evidence that Chinese medical discourse could talk about internal bodily structures before the episode turns to [[PunitiveAnatomyPoliticalTerror|punitive anatomy as political terror]] in Hanji 652.

That use creates an important boundary. The source treats anatomical attention as real intellectual background, but it does not make all later body-opening acts ethically neutral. The contrast helps distinguish medicine as inquiry from Wang Mang's later punitive use of anatomy against a defeated rebel.

## Key Characteristics

- Classical Chinese medical text used in Hanji 651 as anatomy-history background.
- Associated in the source with internal organs, vessels, blood, qi, and bodily observation.
- Functions as setup for distinguishing medical knowledge from punitive political violence.
