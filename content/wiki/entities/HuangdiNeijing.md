## Overview

Huangdi Neijing / 《黄帝内经》 is synthesized as a classical Chinese medical text that podcast sources use for two body-knowledge functions: anatomy-facing attention to organs and vessels, and life-cycle explanations of menstruation, "天癸," and reproductive capacity. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-651-hetu-luoshu-zhi-mi-ni-neng-pojie-ma-lvkgovzsk0yqjb-vbvfxl0ikx2u9|Hanji 651]] invokes it during its setup for the next episode's Wang Mang dissection branch. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-627-1-nvxing-biting-dute-shijiao-jingqi-yinyu-beihou-de-lishi-zhihui-lqomjfejmglm9hylkomktx6rtbtt|Hanji 627-1]] invokes it for "月事," "月信," and gendered seven-year/eight-year life rhythms.

## Current Profile

The page remains narrow but now has two grounded uses. Hanji 651 cites the text as evidence that Chinese medical discourse could talk about internal bodily structures before the episode turns to [[PunitiveAnatomyPoliticalTerror|punitive anatomy as political terror]] in Hanji 652.

Hanji 627-1 uses the text differently: it makes classical medical vocabulary part of the history of menstruation's naming. The episode links "月事" and "月信" to regularity, then explains "女子十四而天癸至" and the seven-year female cycle alongside the eight-year male cycle. This adds reproductive life-stage language without turning the page into modern clinical guidance.

## Key Characteristics

- Classical Chinese medical text used as anatomy-history and reproductive-life-cycle background.
- Associated in the source with internal organs, vessels, blood, qi, and bodily observation.
- Associated in Hanji 627-1 with "月事," "月信," "天癸," female seven-year cycles, and male eight-year cycles.
- Functions as setup for distinguishing medical knowledge from punitive political violence.
- Functions as historical-cultural vocabulary for menstruation and reproductive capacity.

## Qualifications

This page is intentionally narrow. Hanji 651 and Hanji 627-1 are not comprehensive sources for the Huangdi Neijing's composition, dating, textual layers, medical theory, or later reception. Hanji 627-1's menstruation and life-cycle material is historical-cultural context, not modern medical advice.
