## Overview

国渊 / Guo Yuan is a late-Han official represented in the wiki through an anonymous-letter investigation, promotion under [[CaoCao|曹操]], and a source-scoped portrait of austere personal conduct. The semantic suffix in this page key distinguishes him from [[GuoYuanLateHan|郭援 / 郭元]].

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1128-meiyou-heikeji-guren-ruhe-diaocha-nimingxin-lqdm57jzw9wqo_umlasz2j3vlzqh|Hanji 1128]] says Guo Yuan infers from an anonymous accusation's use of [[ZhangHengLateHan|张衡]]'s *Er Jing Fu* that the author probably belongs to a relatively small literate set. He sends three people to find someone capable of explaining the text; they pose as students and ask the target to write a letter. Guo Yuan then compares that sample with the accusation, identifies a suspect, and orders arrest and interrogation.

The episode says Cao Cao promotes him to taipu after the case. It also portrays him as wearing plain cloth, eating simple food, distributing salary and rewards to relatives and old associates, and dying in office. These biographical details add a frugality-and-service profile but remain bounded to this popular-history source.

## Key Characteristics

- Investigator who begins with what the document reveals about its author's knowledge.
- Uses a pretext to obtain a comparison sample before coercive action.
- Treats handwriting resemblance as corroboration within a narrowed candidate field.
- Cao-side official rewarded with promotion after solving the case.
- Source-scoped model of plain living and redistribution of official income.

## Qualifications

The source summary does not supply the anonymous letter's full text, the number of plausible candidates, comparison criteria, independent corroboration, false-positive controls, or interrogation record. The case should not be projected backward as validated modern forensic handwriting analysis. The office chronology and virtue portrait also require direct historical-text corroboration.
