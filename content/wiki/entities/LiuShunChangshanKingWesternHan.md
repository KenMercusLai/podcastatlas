## Overview

常山王刘顺 / Liu Shun is presented in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-342-1-waihao-bie-luan-jiao-you-de-mingzi-hui-bei-duzhou-yisheng-lpehgfgtkbed4vg3z7yegsqalll1|Hanji 342-1]] as [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]]'s favored youngest son and the comparatively fortunate Western Han King of Changshan who ruled for thirty-two years.

## Current Profile

The episode gives Liu Shun a deliberately ambivalent profile. It says he was arrogant, idle, sexually disorderly, and repeatedly in violation of rules, yet paternal favor repeatedly secured forgiveness. That protection distinguishes his long reign from the early deaths, killing, or deposition used to characterize other holders of the Changshan title.

His relative fortune ends at the level of posthumous memory. The episode identifies [[GaozhuangHanTomb|高庄汉墓]] as his burial and describes it as looted and burned so heavily that stone structures collapsed. This turns Liu Shun into both the exception to and final exhibit in the source's [[CursedTitleNarrative|“cursed title” narrative]].

## Key Characteristics

- Identified as Han Jingdi's youngest and especially favored son.
- Enfeoffed as King of [[ChangshanKingdomWesternHan|Changshan]].
- Described by the episode as indulgent, idle, sexually disorderly, and repeatedly in breach of prohibitions.
- Repeatedly forgiven because of paternal affection.
- Holds the Changshan title for thirty-two years, much longer than the other examples emphasized by the episode.
- Succeeded by his son Liu Bo, who is soon deposed.
- Proposed as the occupant of the robbed and burned Gaozhuang tomb.

## Qualifications

This is a source-bounded profile, not a complete biography. The episode does not provide Liu Shun's dates, mother, precise offenses, legal records, administration, court role, kingdom governance, burial evidence, or the grounds and chronology of Liu Bo's deposition. Its moral language about sexual and personal conduct is not independently assessed, and the tomb attribution remains a reported conclusion without its archaeological argument.

## What Changed

- Created a bounded profile that separates Liu Shun's long protected reign from the posthumous fate used by the episode.
- Preserved paternal favor as the episode's explanation rather than a complete institutional account.
