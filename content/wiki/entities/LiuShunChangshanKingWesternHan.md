## Overview

常山宪王刘舜 / Liu Shun is presented as [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]]'s favored youngest son, the long-reigning Western Han King of Changshan, and the father whose divided household produces a succession crisis after his death.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-342-1-waihao-bie-luan-jiao-you-de-mingzi-hui-bei-duzhou-yisheng-lpehgfgtkbed4vg3z7yegsqalll1|Hanji 342-1]] gives Liu Shun a deliberately ambivalent profile. It says he was arrogant, idle, sexually disorderly, and repeatedly in violation of rules, yet paternal favor repeatedly secured forgiveness. That protection distinguishes his long reign from the early deaths, killing, or deposition used to characterize other holders of the Changshan title.

His relative fortune ends at the level of posthumous memory. The episode identifies [[GaozhuangHanTomb|高庄汉墓]] as his burial and describes it as looted and burned so heavily that stone structures collapsed. This turns Liu Shun into both the exception to and final exhibit in the source's [[CursedTitleNarrative|“cursed title” narrative]].

The later household account explains his son's removal through the order Liu Shun leaves behind. He favors several consorts while [[QueenXiuChangshanWesternHan|王后脩]] and crown prince [[LiuBoChangshanKingWesternHan|刘勃]] allegedly neglect him during his final illness. He also excludes his elder son [[LiuZhuoChangshanWesternHan|刘棁]] from affection and property. After Liu Shun dies, that unequal household order becomes denunciation, investigation, deposition, and abolition of the Changshan kingship.

## Key Characteristics

- Identified as Han Jingdi's youngest and especially favored son.
- Enfeoffed as King of [[ChangshanKingdomWesternHan|Changshan]] and holds the title for thirty-two years.
- Described by the episode as indulgent, idle, sexually disorderly, and repeatedly in breach of prohibitions.
- Repeatedly forgiven because of paternal affection.
- Leaves a divided household in which Queen Xiu and Liu Bo hold succession advantage while Liu Zhuo is excluded.
- Succeeded by Liu Bo, whose mourning case and obstruction lead to deposition.
- Proposed as the occupant of the robbed and burned Gaozhuang tomb.

## Qualifications

This is a source-bounded profile, not a complete biography. The episodes do not provide Liu Shun's full dates, administration, court role, kingdom governance, precise offenses, or burial argument. Hanji 342-2 gives allegations from an interested household conflict rather than an independent record of every private interaction. Its transcript form “刘顺” is normalized to the received name 刘舜, while the stable page key is preserved.
