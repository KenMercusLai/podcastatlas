## Overview

郭昌 / Guo Chang is a Western Han general whose current profile joins frontier defense to hydraulic mobilization. He is appointed 拔胡将军 when failed negotiations with Xiongnu are followed by renewed raids, and Hanji 363 also names him as a supervisor of the Huzi breach repair.

## Current Profile

The current evidence introduces Guo Chang only at the transition from diplomacy to military preparedness. His appointment does not begin a narrated offensive campaign; it supplies command structure for a frontier concentration intended to deter or answer Xiongnu raids.

The episode interprets the deployment as proof that bargaining power rests partly on military capability, but the underlying event supports the narrower conclusion that Han responded to raids by visibly preparing the frontier. Guo Chang's wider career and his individual contribution remain outside the source.

Hanji 363 adds a separate state-capacity role in 109 BCE. Guo Chang and Ji Ren are said to mobilize tens of thousands to close the [[YellowRiver|Yellow River]] breach at Huzi before [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] visits the site, court attendants carry brushwood, channels are cut, and [[XuanfangPalace|宣房宫]] is built. The source establishes supervisory association, not Guo Chang's personal engineering expertise.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han commander appointed with the title 拔胡将军.
- Enters the record after diplomatic assurances fail and border raids resume.
- Deploys east of Shuofang alongside Zhao Ponu.
- Represents organized defensive readiness rather than a completed offensive victory in this source.
- Named co-supervisor of a large Huzi flood-repair mobilization.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to two short episodes. They do not establish Guo Chang's full career, exact authority, operational decisions, engineering training, or whether his individual actions caused either the reported Xiongnu withdrawal or the success of the Huzi repair. The broad deterrence claim and the hydraulic details remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Added Guo Chang's separate Huzi flood-repair supervisory role while avoiding an unsupported claim of personal engineering expertise.
