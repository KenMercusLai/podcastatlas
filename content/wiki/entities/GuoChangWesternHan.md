## Overview

郭昌 / Guo Chang is a Western Han general appointed 拔胡将军 when failed negotiations with Xiongnu are followed by renewed border raids. He deploys with [[ZhaoPonuWesternHan|赵破奴]] east of Shuofang as a defensive response.

## Current Profile

The current evidence introduces Guo Chang only at the transition from diplomacy to military preparedness. His appointment does not begin a narrated offensive campaign; it supplies command structure for a frontier concentration intended to deter or answer Xiongnu raids.

The episode interprets the deployment as proof that bargaining power rests partly on military capability, but the underlying event supports the narrower conclusion that Han responded to raids by visibly preparing the frontier. Guo Chang's wider career and his individual contribution remain outside the source.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han commander appointed with the title 拔胡将军.
- Enters the record after diplomatic assurances fail and border raids resume.
- Deploys east of Shuofang alongside Zhao Ponu.
- Represents organized defensive readiness rather than a completed offensive victory in this source.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to one short episode. It does not establish Guo Chang's earlier or later career, exact authority, force size, operational decisions, or whether his individual appointment caused the reported Xiongnu withdrawal. The broad claim that Xiongnu could not risk full war is the host's interpretation rather than a demonstrated fact in the supplied note.

## What Changed

- Created the canonical Guo Chang entity around his 拔胡将军 appointment and Shuofang deployment.
- Kept the evidence at the level of defensive preparedness rather than attributing a standalone battlefield victory.
