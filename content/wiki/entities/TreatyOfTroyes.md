# Treaty of Troyes

## Overview

The Treaty of Troyes is the 1420 agreement that kept [[CharlesVIOfFrance|Charles VI]] as king but made [[HenryVOfEngland|Henry V]] regent and heir to France.

## Current Profile

The settlement arose from English conquest and Burgundian alignment after Montereau. Henry's marriage to [[CatherineOfValois|Catherine of Valois]] supplied its dynastic mechanism, while [[CharlesVIIOfFrance|the Dauphin Charles]] was effectively disinherited.

Its governing design was an [[AngloFrenchDualMonarchy]]: England and France would remain separate kingdoms under one crown. Continued Dauphinist resistance and Henry's death before Charles VI made the agreement dependent on an infant heir and exposed its fragility.

## Key Characteristics

- Ratified on 21 May 1420 after English-Burgundian negotiations.
- Preserved Charles VI's title during his lifetime.
- Made Henry regent and successor to the French crown.
- Joined succession to Henry's marriage with Catherine.
- Excluded the Dauphin Charles from inheritance.
- Designed separate kingdoms under one crown rather than formal annexation.

## Qualifications

The episode presents the treaty as Henry's extraordinary high point without treating it as a settled peace. Its legitimacy remained disputed, and the source ends before testing its longer-term institutional viability.

## What Changed

- Created Troyes as the constitutional mechanism translating conquest and alliance into a disputed dynastic succession.
