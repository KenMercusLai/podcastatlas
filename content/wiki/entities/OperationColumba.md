# Operation Columba

## Overview

Operation Columba was a British Second World War intelligence project that dropped homing pigeons into occupied Europe so civilians could send reports back to Britain.

## Current Profile

The project, described in [[216-pigeons-glt2770080110]] as the Special Continental Pigeon Service run by MI14 subsection D, packaged pigeons with instructions, proof of British origin, and questionnaires about German troops, morale, food, BBC reception, and unusual structures. British pigeon fanciers supplied many of the birds, while civilians in France, Belgium, and the Netherlands accepted the risk of writing and returning reports.

The operation joined a centralized intelligence requirement to decentralized civilian observation and an animal return channel. Its importance lies not in replacing radio or agents, but in creating another path across occupied and monitored territory when ordinary reporting was dangerous or unavailable.

## Key Characteristics

- British wartime intelligence collection system using dropped homing pigeons.
- Depended on donated birds, air delivery, fixed British lofts, and willing civilian respondents.
- Used structured questionnaires to turn local observation into reportable intelligence.
- Collected both short replies and detailed reports, including a Belgian message shown to Churchill.
- Reportedly contributed observations of German radar and V-weapon sites.

## Evidence

- Operating design: [[216-pigeons-glt2770080110]] describes canisters, authentication material, questionnaires, and return to Britain.
- Civilian participation: [[216-pigeons-glt2770080110]] reports returned messages from occupied France, Belgium, and the Netherlands, including the detailed Belgian “message 37.”
- Technical intelligence: [[216-pigeons-glt2770080110]] credits questionnaire responses with identifying rotating radar structures and helping locate V1 and V2 sites.

## Qualifications

The current evidence is one popular-history interview based on Corera's archival research. The exact number, operational impact, targeting contribution, and representativeness of successful returns require comparison with the underlying records and broader intelligence history. Civilian participation should not be romanticized without acknowledging the risks and unknown response rate.

## What Changed

- Established Operation Columba as a distributed civilian-intelligence and fallback-communication case.
