## Overview

Shanghai Disneyland is a Disney theme park in Shanghai used by [[BobIger]] to explain culturally adapted global expansion and the attempt to recreate Disneyland's aspirational public role in a different national setting.

## Current Profile

In [[rihc-disneys-legacy-with-bob-iger-glt6989183827]], Iger says the project followed the brief “authentically Disney, but distinctly Chinese.” He compares its televised opening and intended beacon effect with the original [[Disneyland]], while presenting lessons from Disney's Paris experience as reasons to take local cultural distinctions seriously.

The case supports a bounded localization principle: a globally recognizable story-and-experience system can preserve core identity while changing how it is expressed locally. The interview does not provide design-level evidence, local visitor perspectives, financial results, labor conditions, or independent measures of cultural fit.

## Key Characteristics

- A Disney-branded park designed for a Chinese setting rather than as an unchanged American export.
- Framed by Iger through the joint requirement of authentic Disney identity and distinct Chinese expression.
- Intended to serve as an aspirational destination, partly through televised public spectacle.
- Presented as evidence that Disney learned from earlier international park mistakes.

## Qualifications

The profile rests on Iger's retrospective account in a friendly interview. His claims about ranking, cultural respect, lessons learned, audience response, and successful adaptation are not independently tested here. The source does not represent Chinese visitors, workers, regulators, designers, or critics.
