# Battle of Agincourt

## Overview

The Battle of Agincourt is the 1415 English victory whose military destruction, human suffering, religious interpretation, and political afterlife drive the episode.

## Current Profile

The battle killed members of the French royal family, high command, aristocracy, and local Picard governing class while sending hundreds or perhaps thousands into captivity. The source keeps looting, stripped bodies, grief, shame, and the long imprisonment of the Duke of Orleans beside the English triumph.

For Henry, the victory validated tactics and kingship under divine favor. Its London celebration strengthened [[LancastrianLegitimacy]] and helped create a durable national myth that exceeded the popular resonance of earlier English victories.

## Key Characteristics

- Catastrophic loss of French military, aristocratic, and administrative leadership.
- Much smaller English loss credited partly to Henry's command decisions.
- Battlefield followed by captivity, looting, grief, and memorial poetry.
- Victory interpreted by Henry and English ceremony as divine favor.
- Political resource stabilizing the Lancastrian regime.
- National myth promoted through saints, propaganda, and public celebration.

## Evidence

- French destruction: [[490-hundred-years-war-england-triumphant-part-4-glt2692942762]] lists dead dukes, royal cousins, commanders, officials, and Picard seigneurs.
- Human aftermath: [[490-hundred-years-war-england-triumphant-part-4-glt2692942762]] records prisoners, battlefield stripping, burial preparation, and Alain Chartier's grieving women.
- English interpretation: [[490-hundred-years-war-england-triumphant-part-4-glt2692942762]] links divine favor, London ritual, and saint-day promotion to political legitimacy.

## Qualifications

Prisoner and casualty estimates vary, and the episode's claims about relative losses are not independently audited here. National myth should not erase either French suffering or the practical military and political choices behind the victory.

## What Changed

- Created Agincourt as both a battle and a transformation of regime legitimacy and historical memory.
