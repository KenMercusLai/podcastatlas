# Herman Melville / 赫尔曼·梅尔维尔

## Overview
Herman Melville is the American author of [[MobyDick|《白鲸》 / Moby-Dick]], represented here through a [[CultureLimited|文化有限]] discussion of seafaring experience, encyclopedic form, and delayed literary recognition.

## Current Profile
The source presents Melville as a writer whose commercial, whaling, island, and naval experience gave technical and sensory pressure to a novel of philosophy and formal experiment. His case supports neither simple autobiography nor the claim that experience writes itself: lived maritime knowledge becomes literature through classification, voice, drama, essay, poetry, and imaginative transformation.

## Key Characteristics
- He is presented as converting maritime labor into literary form rather than merely recording adventure.
- His writing joins technical particularity to religion, philosophy, industry, and nature.
- The episode treats his mixed forms as an attempt to approach an object too large for one viewpoint.
- His represented reception moves from weak initial sales and altered editions to posthumous canonization.
