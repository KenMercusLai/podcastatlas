# René Schneider

## Overview
René Schneider was commander-in-chief of the Chilean army and a defender of military obedience to the constitution during the disputed ratification of [[SalvadorAllende]]'s 1970 election.

## Current Profile
[[370-the-1973-chilean-coup-allende-nixon-and-the-cia-part-1-glt6405070034]] presents Schneider as the command-level obstacle to efforts to prevent Allende taking office through military intervention. He held that the armed forces should protect rather than override the constitutional process. Plotters attempted to kidnap him before Congress voted; he was shot during the third attempt and died, after which public reaction and Allende's constitutional pledge helped consolidate congressional ratification.

## Key Characteristics
- He treated military non-intervention as an affirmative professional duty rather than passive neutrality.
- His command position made one officer's constitutional commitment materially important to democratic succession.
- His attempted kidnapping linked foreign encouragement, domestic plotting, and violence without making every participant part of one fully controlled operation.
- His death produced an outcome opposite to the plotters' immediate objective by strengthening support for constitutional ratification.

## Evidence
- Constitutional stance: [[370-the-1973-chilean-coup-allende-nixon-and-the-cia-part-1-glt6405070034]] says Schneider insisted that the armed forces defend the constitution and stay outside political succession.
- Obstacle and attack: [[370-the-1973-chilean-coup-allende-nixon-and-the-cia-part-1-glt6405070034]] places three kidnapping attempts before the congressional vote and reports that he was shot during the third.
- Political consequence: [[370-the-1973-chilean-coup-allende-nixon-and-the-cia-part-1-glt6405070034]] connects his death to a constitutional reaction that helped Allende's ratification.

## Qualifications
The page reflects one narrative podcast episode, not a complete military biography or archival reconstruction. The source distinguishes broad U.S. encouragement of a coup from simple operational control of every Chilean plotter; the exact chain of responsibility for the kidnapping operation remains source-scoped here.

- Added the kidnapping and death as evidence that failed coercion can strengthen the norm it sought to bypass.
