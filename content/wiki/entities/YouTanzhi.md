## Overview

游坦之 / You Tanzhi is a character in [[TianLongBaBu|《天龙八部》]] whose attraction to [[Azi|阿紫]] becomes an increasingly self-destructive dependency after the destruction of his family and former life.

## Current Profile

The bounded episode places You Tanzhi's first attraction before A Zi's abuse, rejecting an easy Stockholm-syndrome description. Beauty, catastrophe, loss of purpose, and the arrival of a new emotional object are offered as overlapping conditions. Once attached, he accepts the iron mask, mauling, poison and martial experiments, repeated danger, and finally the donation of both eyes.

The hosts interpret physical suffering as a possible substitute for grief and service to A Zi as a replacement purpose for failed revenge. That reading remains speculative, but it explains why increasing cost strengthens rather than weakens commitment. His martial power gives him possible exits, yet he allows A Zi, 丁春秋, and 全冠清 to direct his life until he follows A Zi over the cliff.

## Key Characteristics

- Attaches to A Zi after family catastrophe and loss of a stable life direction.
- Accepts escalating humiliation, injury, experimentation, and danger to remain near her.
- Converts sacrifice into proof of devotion despite minimal reciprocity.
- Gains considerable martial power without developing comparable independence or judgment.
- Continues investing after each loss, making withdrawal psychologically and narratively harder.
- Donates both eyes and ultimately follows A Zi into death.

## Qualifications

This profile rests on one conversational literary discussion, not clinical evidence. Stockholm syndrome, masochism, trauma conversion, sunk cost, and emotional dependency are analogies proposed or debated by the hosts, not diagnoses. The serial version reportedly gives A Zi a more reciprocal response to the disguised “王星天,” so the claim of complete nonreciprocity is edition-specific.

## What Changed

- Created a profile centered on catastrophic loss, replacement purpose, escalating sacrifice, and lost autonomy.
- Explicitly rejected a casual captor-bond label unsupported by the episode's chronology.
- Preserved edition differences in whether A Zi ever responds to him romantically.
