# Susanna Søberg

## Overview
Susanna Søberg is presented as a researcher of deliberate cold and heat exposure, brown adipose tissue, thermoregulation, and human metabolism.

## Current Profile
Across the full interview and its later Essentials edit, Søberg translates a proof-of-concept comparison of experienced male winter swimmers and matched controls into a conservative thermal-stress framework. She emphasizes short, repeatable exposure, natural rewarming, and variation rather than extreme cold endurance, while distinguishing measured findings from unanswered questions about cold showers, women, children, Raynaud's syndrome, sleep timing, fasting, and mental-health outcomes.

Her practical “end on cold” principle is mechanistic rather than universal: ending a contrast session with cold requires the body to reheat through vascular, brown-fat, and muscle responses. The episode attributes approximately 11 weekly cold minutes and 57 sauna minutes to the observed winter-swimmer pattern, but its design does not establish those totals as an optimum for every person.

## Key Characteristics
- Studies human responses to deliberate cold and heat exposure, with emphasis on brown fat and metabolism.
- Uses controlled measurements including PET-MRI, infrared thermography, cooling days, glucose challenge, insulin, and cardiovascular markers.
- Frames useful exposure as brief hormetic stress rather than a test of maximum tolerance.
- Distinguishes plausible mechanisms and anecdotes from direct evidence.
- Treats individual adaptation, population differences, afterdrop, and hypothermia as important protocol limits.

## Qualifications
This profile is bounded to two overlapping structured episode notes rather than Søberg's complete publication record. The described study involved experienced male winter swimmers, so its observational group differences and weekly exposure totals do not establish causality, universal benefit, or individualized safety. The Essentials edit gives less attention to contraindications and open-water safety than the full interview. All medical, metabolic, cardiovascular, pediatric, pregnancy-related, open-water, and extreme-temperature applications require context beyond these sources.
