## Overview

穆先生 / Master Mu is an adviser in the Chu court whose decision to leave after a small ritual omission becomes the episode's case for recognizing political danger before overt coercion.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-232-wuwang-liubi-pingshenme-gan-faqi-qiguo-zhiluan-lqtwfjr6oknd9pgdtvnnz61rs1f1|Hanji 232]] says Mu studied the *Book of Songs* with Liu Jiao, Shen, and Bai under Fuqiu Bo. Because Mu did not drink alcohol, Liu Jiao regularly arranged sweet wine for him; [[LiuWuChuKingWesternHan|刘戊]] initially preserves but later forgets the accommodation.

Mu interprets the omission as evidence that the inherited basis of respect has ended. He withdraws despite Shen and Bai's objection, arguing from “知几” and “见几而作” that action should precede fully visible danger. Their later public humiliation supplies narrative confirmation of his risk judgment while leaving the broader predictive rule qualified.

## Key Characteristics

- Learned adviser connected to the early Chu court and its ruler-minister ethical order.
- Observer who treats a small courtesy change as evidence about the underlying relationship rather than as an isolated insult.
- Early exiter whose decision is retrospectively contrasted with advisers who remain and are abused.

## Qualifications

The current profile depends on one episode's retelling. Names, teacher-student relations, office, ritual details, quoted classical language, timing, and punishments require primary-text comparison. Later confirmation inside a narrative can sharpen the lesson through hindsight; the page therefore does not generalize one omitted courtesy into a reliable standalone forecast.

## What Changed

- Established Mu as the episode's central observer of an incipient shift in political safety.
- Separated his inference about a relationship from a universal claim about etiquette.
