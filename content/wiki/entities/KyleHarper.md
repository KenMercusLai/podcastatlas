# Kyle Harper

## Overview

Kyle Harper is a historian of the ancient world who appears in the source as a University of Oklahoma professor and the author of *The Fate of Rome*.

## Current Profile

Harper's contribution is a multi-causal environmental history of Roman transformation. He treats climate evidence, pathogen ecology, ancient DNA, urban demography, and transport networks as additions to the historical archive, not replacements for texts or for political, military, and social explanation.

His central argument is that Roman success carried biological costs. Dense cities, long-distance exchange, grain supply, armies, roads, shipping, and animal contact increased disease exposure; pandemic mortality could then reduce taxpayers and recruits, disrupt food systems, weaken frontiers, and intensify crises already produced by war and institutional fragility.

## Key Characteristics

- Historian connecting ancient history with climate science, epidemiology, demography, and ancient DNA.
- Advocate of disease and climate as major historical forces within a plural causal model.
- Interpreter of Roman connectivity as both imperial capacity and pathogen infrastructure.
- Cautious about unknown pathogens, disputed mortality totals, and incomplete geographic evidence.
- Analyst of the Antonine, Cyprian, and Justinianic pandemics as distinct shocks rather than one undifferentiated plague story.

## Qualifications

This profile is based on one podcast interview rather than Harper's full scholarship. Claims about Roman life expectancy, stature, specific mortality levels, disease origins, migrant transmission, Britain's transformation, and the scale of plague effects remain the episode's interpretations unless independently supported elsewhere.
