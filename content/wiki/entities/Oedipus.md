# Oedipus / 俄狄浦斯

## Overview

Oedipus is a Greek mythic ruler represented here both as psychoanalytic shorthand for taboo family desire and as [[Sophocles]]' unwitting parricide, incestuous husband, plague investigator, and tragic self-discoverer.

## Current Profile

[[29-qianmian-yingxiong-edipusi-zhengzai-xiaban-ji-ditie-590112891]] uses Oedipus briefly inside a Campbellian comparison of myth, dream, family desire, taboo, and recurring symbolic patterns. In that frame he belongs to a useful but contested psychological vocabulary: resemblance can generate interpretation without proving one universal subconscious mechanism.

[[603-greek-myths-the-riddle-of-the-sphinx-part-2-glt9108845593]] restores the figure's narrative and historical density. Exposed after an oracle says he will kill his father, Oedipus is raised in Corinth, leaves to protect the people he believes are his parents, kills Laius in a road dispute, defeats the Sphinx, marries Jocasta, and rules Thebes. When plague leads him to investigate Laius's murder, prophecy, witness memory, and messenger evidence reveal that he has fulfilled the prediction precisely while trying to avoid it.

The resulting guilt is objective but unwitting. Oedipus has committed the acts and Thebes suffers pollution, yet he did not know the identities involved. His self-blinding gives bodily form to the play's contrast between confident sight and absent knowledge, while his movement from solver of the Sphinx to the riddle he cannot solve makes inquiry itself tragic.

## Key Characteristics

- Mythic ruler whose identity is shaped by abandonment, attempted prophecy avoidance, the Sphinx, kingship, and catastrophic recognition.
- Investigator who discovers that he is the offender he publicly curses and seeks.
- Guilty of parricide and incest without conscious knowledge of the relevant kinship.
- Figure through whom plague, pollution, family history, and public rule become inseparable.
- Later psychoanalytic symbol whose universal use requires historical and evidentiary qualification.

## Evidence

- Psychological shorthand: [[29-qianmian-yingxiong-edipusi-zhengzai-xiaban-ji-ditie-590112891]] places Oedipus among Freudian, Jungian, and Campbellian readings of myth and dream while warning against treating resemblance as proof.
- Mythic and tragic sequence: [[603-greek-myths-the-riddle-of-the-sphinx-part-2-glt9108845593]] follows exposure, Corinthian adoption, Delphi, Laius's death, the Sphinx, marriage, plague, investigation, recognition, and self-blinding.
- Unwitting guilt and civic pollution: [[603-greek-myths-the-riddle-of-the-sphinx-part-2-glt9108845593]] holds his lack of knowledge beside the public consequences of the acts.
- Historical interpretation: [[603-greek-myths-the-riddle-of-the-sphinx-part-2-glt9108845593]] places Sophocles' version within Athenian plague, war, democracy, and rivalry with Thebes.

## Qualifications

Oedipus has no single canonical biography: Homeric, tragic, epic, psychoanalytic, and modern versions select and alter different material. The current account is dominated by Sophocles as summarized by one podcast episode. The detective analogy, political reception, and critique of Freud are interpretations, while the Campbell episode's symbolic use does not establish a universal developmental or clinical law.

## What Changed

- Expanded Oedipus from psychoanalytic shorthand into a full mythic and Sophoclean profile.
- Distinguished unwitting action from absence of guilt or civic consequence.
- Added investigation, recognition, plague, kingship, and historically specific Athenian reception.
