## Overview

第一次河西之战 / First Hexi Campaign is presented as [[HuoQubing|霍去病]]'s spring 121 BCE cavalry attack from Longxi into the western [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] field. Across two compact episode summaries, the operation moves from light equipment, penetration, political separation, conditional surrender, and campaign replenishment into sustained pursuit and a closing engagement near Gaolan Mountain against forces associated with [[HunyeKingWesternHan|浑邪王]] and [[XiutuKingWesternHan|休屠王]]. It begins the military weakening of Xiongnu power in the [[HexiCorridorWesternHan|河西走廊]] but does not by itself establish permanent Han control.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-307-1-buxuewushu-de-huoqubing-ping-shenme-hengsao-xiongnu-lus99k4vphqaj8xofkm0rqi1nga9|Hanji 307-1]] says Huo leads ten thousand cavalry from Longxi, crosses the Yellow River, attacks a group rendered as Suopu, and moves through several western groups over six days and more than one thousand li. Its operational explanation is tempo: a lightly moving force avoids extended looting or positional delay, penetrates between political units, and attacks before they can organize a joint response.

The opening phase is also presented as political disaggregation. Huo allegedly tells peripheral groups that submission will protect their people and property, giving up some immediate captive or plunder credit in exchange for less resistance and greater isolation of stronger Xiongnu centers. “以战养战” is part of the same account, but the episode does not reconstruct quantities, acquisition rules, animal supply, or longer-term sustainability.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-307-2-huoqubing-gujun-yizhan-zhanshen-neng-you-duo-biantai-ll-egjuu-gn-s-_mazrbbarfmb02|Hanji 307-2]] supplies the pursuit and battle conclusion. It says Huo moves north and south around Yanzhi Mountain while chasing Hunye King and Xiutu King, compressing their force toward Gaolan Mountain, the Yellow River, and the Han frontier until they turn to fight. The episode makes speed the bridge between maneuver and decision: deep penetration and pursuit create contact, while close combat and a claimed Han weapons advantage produce the battlefield result.

That result is reported as 8,900 opposing dead from a 13,000-person force, rulers and officials killed or captured, the Xiutu group's sacrificial golden figure taken as booty, and only a small remnant escaping with the two kings. Wudi is then said to add 2,200 households to Huo's fief, while Huo builds a Yellow River fort called Jincheng. The source does not establish the names, figures, trophy meaning, fort identity, or exact causal sequence independently.

The campaign remains costly and internally difficult to reconcile. Hanji 307-1's opening claims seven thousand losses from a ten-thousand force, while Hanji 307-2 again describes a ten-thousand-person Han army after prolonged fighting over two thousand li. The two compact accounts do not explain the accounting. Later campaigning, the rulers' eventual surrender crisis, population settlement, and administrative development are still required to explain durable change in Hexi.

## Key Characteristics

- High-tempo cavalry operation that joins deep penetration and repeated pursuit to a forced closing engagement.
- Penetration-and-separation method aimed at preventing western groups from coordinating resistance.
- Conditional surrender policy that exchanges some immediate plunder or captive credit for political fragmentation and lighter movement.
- Campaign-replenishment claim whose exact logistical mechanism and sustainability remain unestablished.
- Major claimed Gaolan victory with elite captives and symbolic booty, bounded by unresolved Han loss accounting.
- Incomplete first-stage disruption before the second campaign and later surrender-and-settlement sequence.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to two compact podcast summaries rather than a primary-source campaign reconstruction. Date, route, Suopu, Yanzhi and Gaolan identifications, force balances, six-day, thousand-li and two-thousand-li figures, number and status of defeated groups, casualty accounting, supply capture, surrender promises, battle mechanics, weapons comparison, names and ranks, reward, golden-figure meaning, and Jincheng fort remain episode-attributed. The host's inference that Huo consulted Zhang Qian is not direct evidence of a meeting. Inner-state claims about Huo, his soldiers, and the opposing rulers are narrative interpretation. The title's “横扫匈奴” and “战神” language must not be read as defeat of Xiongnu as a whole, and this first campaign must remain distinct from the second Hexi operation begun in Hanji 308.

## What Changed

- Extended the campaign from its opening penetration-and-surrender method through the pursuit and claimed Gaolan battlefield conclusion.
- Connected tempo to the creation of decisive contact while keeping close-combat and weapons explanations source-scoped.
- Added the reported elite captives, sacrificial golden figure, imperial reward, and Jincheng fort without treating them as independently verified.
- Made the unreconciled ten-thousand-force and severe-loss accounting explicit.
