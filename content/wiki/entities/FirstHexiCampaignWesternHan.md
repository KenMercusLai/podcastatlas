## Overview

第一次河西之战 / First Hexi Campaign is presented as [[HuoQubing|霍去病]]'s spring 121 BCE cavalry attack from Longxi into the western [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] field. The source explains the operation through light movement, high tempo, penetration and separation of local groups, campaign replenishment, and conditional surrender rather than through a single frontal battle. It begins the military weakening of Xiongnu power in the [[HexiCorridorWesternHan|河西走廊]] but does not by itself establish permanent Han control.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-307-1-buxuewushu-de-huoqubing-ping-shenme-hengsao-xiongnu-lus99k4vphqaj8xofkm0rqi1nga9|Hanji 307-1]] says Huo leads ten thousand cavalry from Longxi, crosses the Yellow River, attacks a group rendered as Suopu, and moves through several western groups over six days and more than one thousand li. The source's operational explanation is tempo: a lightly moving force avoids extended looting or positional delay, penetrates between political units, and attacks before they can organize a joint response.

The campaign is also presented as political disaggregation. Huo allegedly tells peripheral groups that submission will protect their people and property, giving up some immediate captive or plunder credit in exchange for less resistance and greater isolation of stronger Xiongnu centers. “以战养战” is part of the same account, but the episode does not reconstruct the quantities, acquisition rules, animal supply, or longer-term sustainability of campaign replenishment.

The campaign's result remains costly and bounded. The opening claims seven thousand losses from the ten-thousand force, while the later account emphasizes rapid destruction or submission of several groups. The source does not resolve the accounting, and the later [[SecondHexiCampaignWesternHan|第二次河西之战]], Hunye surrender, settlement, and administrative development are still required to explain durable change in the corridor.

## Key Characteristics

- High-tempo cavalry operation presented as crossing more than one thousand li in six days.
- Penetration-and-separation method aimed at preventing western groups from coordinating resistance.
- Conditional surrender policy that exchanges some immediate plunder or captive credit for political fragmentation and lighter movement.
- Campaign-replenishment claim whose exact logistical mechanism and sustainability remain unestablished.
- Costly but incomplete first-stage disruption before the second campaign and later surrender-and-settlement sequence.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to one compact podcast summary rather than a primary-source campaign reconstruction. Date, route, Suopu identification, force balance, six-day and thousand-li figures, number and status of defeated groups, casualty accounting, supply capture, surrender promises, and strategic effects remain episode-attributed. The host's inference that Huo consulted Zhang Qian is not direct evidence of a meeting. The title's “横扫匈奴” must not be read as defeat of Xiongnu as a whole, and this first campaign must remain distinct from the second Hexi operation begun in Hanji 308.

## What Changed

- Created a distinct first-Hexi campaign page rather than folding its operation into the later desert-envelopment campaign.
- Joined battlefield tempo to political separation and conditional surrender as one operational method.
- Preserved the severe claimed loss, logistics, route, totals, and Zhang Qian consultation as unresolved source-scoped evidence.
