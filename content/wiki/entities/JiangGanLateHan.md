## Overview

蒋干 / Jiang Gan is the Cao-side visitor whom Hanji 1113 presents as attempting to sound out or persuade [[ZhouYuLateHan|周瑜]] after [[RedCliffsBattle|赤壁之战]].

## Current Profile

The episode identifies Jiang Gan as a Jiujiang man and Zhou Yu's fellow commandery man, giving [[CaoCao|曹操]] a preexisting social channel into the Wu commander's camp. Jiang Gan approaches under the language of a private visit, but Zhou Yu immediately frames the possibility that he has come as a persuader.

His importance lies in the mission's failure. Zhou Yu shows him the camp, stores, weapons, banquet treatment, and the trust binding him to [[SunQuan|孙权]]. Jiang Gan returns with the judgment that Zhou Yu's resolve cannot be moved by argument or money. The source therefore uses him less as a deceitful mastermind than as the observer who verifies the limits of transactional recruitment.

## Key Characteristics

- Cao-side visitor using a same-region social connection to approach Zhou Yu.
- Possible persuader whose purpose is recognized before he can develop the pitch.
- Witness to Wu military resources, elite treatment, and leader-subordinate trust.
- Returns with an unfavorable but usable assessment that Zhou Yu cannot be bought or argued away.
- Source role is narrower than later fictional depictions associated with elaborate deception.

- Controlled reception: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1113-liubei-qu-sunquan-meimei-guocheng-zhong-you-duo-beiwei-lqvpwr7tkvzlg2o3-m46srk98cnm|Hanji 1113]] has Zhou Yu answer indirectly through a tour, banquet, and explicit loyalty statement.
- Mission result: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1113-liubei-qu-sunquan-meimei-guocheng-zhong-you-duo-beiwei-lqvpwr7tkvzlg2o3-m46srk98cnm|Hanji 1113]] says Jiang Gan reports that Zhou Yu is too resolute to be moved.

## Qualifications

The exact commission, chronology, dialogue, camp display, and report are bounded to one popular-history account. This page does not import later Romance of the Three Kingdoms plots into the evidence. A failed persuasion mission does not prove that Jiang Gan was incompetent; his report may still have reduced uncertainty for Cao Cao.

## What Changed

- Created a source-bounded profile centered on reconnaissance and failed persuasion.
- Distinguished the episode's Jiang Gan from later fictional trickery narratives.
- Treated the negative report as information production rather than zero-value activity.
