# Ithaca

## Overview

Ithaca is [[Odysseus]]' home island in [[TheOdyssey]], treated in [[688-the-odyssey-return-of-the-king-part-2-glt2882541084]] as the place where homecoming still requires recognition, secrecy, and political restoration.

## Current Profile

The episode makes Ithaca more than a destination. Odysseus reaches it while asleep on a [[Phaeacians|Phaeacian]] ship but does not recognize it at first, and [[Athena]] immediately makes the island into a tactical field. The suitors occupy the palace, [[Penelope]] delays them, [[Telemachus]] returns under divine protection, and loyal household figures help Odysseus retake the hall.

## Key Characteristics

- Ithaca is the goal that gives Odysseus' wandering a fixed direction rather than an endless adventure logic.
- Arrival alone is insufficient; Odysseus has to recover recognition, property, marriage, and command.
- The palace is a contested household-political space because suitors consume resources and pressure Penelope.
- Ithaca's restoration depends on both high-status recognition and lower-status loyalty from servants such as [[Eumaeus]].

## Evidence

- Goal of return: [[688-the-odyssey-return-of-the-king-part-2-glt2882541084]] keeps Odysseus' desire for Ithaca and Penelope central against Calypso's offer.
- Unrecognized arrival: [[688-the-odyssey-return-of-the-king-part-2-glt2882541084]] has Odysseus wake on Ithaca without immediately knowing where he is.
- Occupied household: [[688-the-odyssey-return-of-the-king-part-2-glt2882541084]] presents 108 suitors as consuming the estate and pressuring Penelope.
- Restoration network: [[688-the-odyssey-return-of-the-king-part-2-glt2882541084]] describes Athena, Telemachus, Eumaeus, Philoteus, Eurycleia, and Penelope as stages in the return's completion.

## Qualifications
