# Apollo (god)

## Overview

Apollo is a Greek god joining beauty, plague, healing, prophecy, Delphi, Delos, and an oracular style that demands interpretation rather than offering simple clarity.

## Current Profile

[[59-the-world-cup-of-gods-part-2-glt6474253297]] treats Apollo as archetypically Greek but internally double-sided: plague and healing, radiance and obliqueness, classical prestige and distance. His epithet Loxias marks the oracle as indirect, while Ovid's later organization of metamorphosis stories helps shape the mythology through which Apollo reaches later readers.

## Key Characteristics

- Associated with beauty and a highly classicized Greek image.
- Joins plague and healing rather than belonging to only one side of disease.
- God of prophecy whose oracles require careful interpretation.
- Linked to the sacred centers of Delphi and Delos.
- Reaches later culture partly through Ovid's literary arrangement of myth.

## Evidence

- Divine range: [[59-the-world-cup-of-gods-part-2-glt6474253297]] connects Apollo with beauty, plague, healing, Delphi, and Delos.
- Oracular ambiguity: [[59-the-world-cup-of-gods-part-2-glt6474253297]] uses the epithet Loxias to stress oblique communication.
- Literary reception: [[59-the-world-cup-of-gods-part-2-glt6474253297]] presents Ovid's metamorphosis collection as influential packaging for later mythology.

## Qualifications

This is a compact reception-aware profile rather than a full account of Apollo's cults, local forms, music, archery, or Roman adoption. The suggestion that “chilly classicism” explains his tournament loss is playful audience interpretation rather than a historical finding.
