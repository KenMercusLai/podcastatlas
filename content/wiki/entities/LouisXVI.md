# Louis XVI

## Overview

Louis XVI was the deposed Bourbon king tried and executed by the French Republic on 21 January 1793.

## Current Profile

The source presents Louis simultaneously as an accused individual and a political symbol. His indictment concerned treasonous conduct, but his naming, seating, clothing, restraint, execution, and burial were organized to destroy sacred royal exception; his composed final conduct then gave royalists material for a rival martyr memory.

## Key Characteristics

- He rejected “Louis Capet” and continued to understand himself through Bourbon identity and legitimacy.
- He chose a point-by-point factual defence rather than making constitutional inviolability his main strategy.
- He denied the charges calmly but showed indignation at the accusation that he had shed French blood.
- He requested time for spiritual preparation, freedom for his family, a final family meeting, and a non-juring priest.
- He attempted a final public statement before drums drowned him out and was guillotined at the Place de la Révolution.
- His death became both republican anti-royal spectacle and royalist martyr narrative.

## Qualifications

- The source is a dramatic narrative rather than a comprehensive legal record of the trial.
- The “one vote” margin, exact dialogue, final words, private beliefs, and eyewitness scenes remain source-scoped.
- Composure at death does not decide the truth of the indictment or restore monarchical legitimacy.
