# Larry David

## Overview

Larry David is a comedian, writer, and co-creator of [[SeinfeldTVSeries|Seinfeld]] whose grievances, social observations, and structural instincts supplied much of the show's early creative foundation.

## Current Profile

Before *Seinfeld*, David had worked in stand-up, television writing, and briefly at *Saturday Night Live* without achieving stable commercial success. [[JerrySeinfeld|Jerry Seinfeld]] brought him into the [[NBC]] opportunity because observational material needed a partner able to turn it into scripted stories.

The source portrays David as uncompromising about comedy, resistant to network notes, and central to the rule against sentimental growth. He also helped develop the show's interlocking plot form and intense rewriting system. He left after the first major syndication cycle, when creative burnout and backend income made continued production less necessary.

## Key Characteristics

- Converts personal grievances and social friction into scripted comedy.
- Prioritizes comic structure over character likability or moral uplift.
- Strongly resists notes perceived to weaken the work.
- Helps interweave independent character plots into convergent episodes.
- Combined creative exhaustion with an ownership position that could earn after departure.
