# Beijing

## Overview
Beijing is represented through two distinct lenses: a public stage for China's humanoid-robot push and a lived city whose heritage, streets, waterways, commerce, and planning changed substantially across the preceding decade.

## Current Profile
The robotics source uses Beijing as the setting for a spectacle that makes China's humanoid-robot momentum visible, including both athletic demonstrations and public failure modes.

The cycling source supplies a broader urban profile. A route from Jingshan to Sanlitun connects heritage restoration, road greening, active historic infrastructure, hutong commerce, ecological river repair, stalled construction, and premium retail rebuilding. It presents the 2016-era capital-function and master-plan shift as a major organizing context while qualifying any simple progress-or-decline account: public space and ecology can improve while informal use, local shops, and subculture are displaced or reorganized.

## Key Characteristics
- Beijing functions as a public stage for China's humanoid robotics ambitions and their current reliability limits.
- Historic places remain embedded in transport, tourism, memory, and ordinary public use.
- Recent road and river projects shift some value from vehicle throughput toward greenery, walking, ecology, and public life.
- Hutong and nightlife governance can displace commerce before it returns in altered locations, scales, and operating conditions.
- The 2016-era planning direction helps explain later spatial change, but long-range forecasts remain vulnerable to shocks.

## Qualifications
This remains a bounded profile, not a general history of Beijing politics, economy, demographics, or planning. The robotics source is a short international briefing; the urban source relies on a selected route, participant memory, and field interpretation. Exact dates, policy causation, design intent, visitor patterns, and commercial effects remain source-scoped.
