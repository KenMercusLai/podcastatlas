# Hindu Kush Himalaya

## Overview
The Hindu Kush Himalaya is the regional mountain system used in the Nepal flood episode to connect warming, glacier melt, unstable slopes, monsoon dynamics, and cross-border river hazards. In the current wiki it matters as the climate and geography context behind [[Nepal]]'s flood exposure rather than as a complete regional profile.

## Current Profile
The source presents the region as warming faster than the rest of the world, with glaciers melting twice as fast as they did in 2000. That physical change compounds older hazards: monsoon air, cold winds, tectonic instability, steep valleys, landslides, and river systems that cross national borders. The profile therefore sits between [[ClimateAdaptation]], [[HimalayanGlacialFloodRisk]], and [[DisasterResponseStateCapacity]].

## Key Characteristics
- The region is treated as a fast-warming mountain system with accelerating glacier melt.
- River hazards can begin on one side of a border and become a disaster downstream in another country.
- Climate risk interacts with monsoon weather, tectonics, unstable slopes, and river-valley settlement.
- Monitoring and warning are regional public goods because downstream communities may have only minutes to respond.

## Evidence
- Warming and glacier change: [[after-the-flood-nepals-ongoing-rescue-6a969d560cefab2730527078]] says the Hindu Kush Himalaya is heating faster than the rest of the world and that its glaciers are melting twice as fast as in 2000.
- Cross-border hazard chain: [[after-the-flood-nepals-ongoing-rescue-6a969d560cefab2730527078]] describes a glacier and ice-rock avalanche in Tibet that dammed the Lenda River before floodwaters moved into Nepal.
- Exposure mix: [[after-the-flood-nepals-ongoing-rescue-6a969d560cefab2730527078]] connects monsoon air, cold Himalayan winds, unstable slopes, and plate-boundary geology to Nepal's recurring flood vulnerability.
- Regional monitoring need: [[after-the-flood-nepals-ongoing-rescue-6a969d560cefab2730527078]] recommends better cross-border monitoring among Himalayan nations after the disaster.

## Qualifications
The source uses the Hindu Kush Himalaya mainly to explain Nepal's flood disaster. It does not provide a full country-by-country comparison, glacier inventory, or governance map for the entire region, and its glacier-melt figures remain source-scoped until reinforced by additional evidence.
