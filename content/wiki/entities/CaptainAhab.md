# Ahab / 亚哈船长

## Overview
Ahab is the Pequod's captain in [[MobyDick|《白鲸》]], interpreted here as a tragic figure who turns bodily injury and a sacrificed working life into a collective revenge mission.

## Current Profile
The episode's Ahab is not driven by hatred alone. After losing a leg to Moby Dick, he assigns cosmic intention to injury and makes revenge the final proof that decades of danger, absence, limited reward, and aging have a meaning. This gives him dramatic grandeur while making his authority politically destructive: the crew must bear the risk of a private purpose. Because the pursuit organizes his identity, failure perpetuates it and imagined victory threatens emptiness.

## Key Characteristics
- He converts accidental injury into a personalized struggle against a meaningful enemy.
- He uses command authority to impose private revenge on a whole crew.
- His stage-like speech gives obsession tragic and theatrical force.
- His target compensates for feared failure, aging, and a life without adequate return.
- He cannot easily survive psychologically beyond the purpose that sustains him.

- Collective risk - [[vol-356-baijing-yongchang-de-shenghuo-yishougou-qing-dahai-daizou-wode-aichou-gkwriw4oms-ma1lixgtjj3um]] asks whether an individual's dream can legitimately make everyone else pay.
- Victory counterfactual - [[vol-356-baijing-yongchang-de-shenghuo-yishougou-qing-dahai-daizou-wode-aichou-gkwriw4oms-ma1lixgtjj3um]] suggests success could remove the structure that made Ahab's life coherent.

## Qualifications
This is a source-bounded character interpretation, not a definitive account of the novel. Psychological motive, religious symbolism, and counterfactual post-victory life remain interpretive.

## What Changed
- Created the profile around revenge as attempted meaning repair and collective-risk imposition.
