## Overview

陈胜 / Chen Sheng is the initiating political leader of the [[DazexiangUprising|Dazexiang uprising]] and founder of [[ZhangChuRegime|Zhang Chu]]. His record combines extraordinary mobilizing importance with a rapid failure to turn revolt into trusted, coordinated government.

## Current Profile

Chen Sheng and [[WuGuang|吴广]] convert a conscript deadline crisis into revolt by killing their officers, invoking [[FuSu|扶苏]] and [[XiangYan|项燕]], and manufacturing signs of mandate. Rapid recruitment carries them to Chen County, where Chen Sheng rejects [[ZhangEr|张耳]] and [[ChenYu|陈馀]]'s advice to delay kingship and restore the six old royal houses. He instead becomes Chu king and creates Zhang Chu.

Qinji 128-4 clarifies the regime's early scale and trust problem. Chen Sheng disperses forces across several theaters, but [[GeYing|葛婴]]'s independent installation of [[XiangJiang|襄疆]] as Chu king becomes unforgivable even after Ge Ying kills the claimant. The execution removes a commander and signals that initiative under weak communication can become retroactively fatal. Later sources extend the same pattern through overconfidence after [[ZhouWenRebel|周文]]'s advance, partial loss of Zhao, the unpunished killing of Wu Guang, harsh inspection, estrangement from intimates, and Chen Sheng's own murder by his driver.

His regime collapses, but his political afterlife remains substantial. Later rebels still use his name for legitimacy, Liu Bang maintains his tomb rites, and later strategic argument treats the restoration plan he rejected as potentially suitable to the anti-Qin moment even though it would be dangerous in Liu Bang's different situation.

## Key Characteristics

- Crisis mobilizer who turns shared punishment risk into a politically legible rebellion.
- Legitimacy entrepreneur who combines Chu memory, false signs, and royal naming with military expansion.
- Premature central claimant who rejects a broader restoration coalition and concentrates authority in Zhang Chu.
- Suspicious ruler whose handling of Ge Ying, returning commanders, Wu Guang's killers, and inspectors makes initiative and intimacy unsafe.
- Historically durable founder whose symbolic authority survives military defeat and personal governing failure.

## Qualifications

The sources are podcast interpretations of transmitted historical narratives. The fatal-deadline law, force totals, dialogue, private motives, and direct causal weight assigned to character remain source-scoped. Chen Sheng's possible prior approval of Wu Guang's killing is inferred from non-punishment and promotion, not established by a direct order. His execution of Ge Ying shows severe mistrust, but the sources do not reconstruct the authorization rules or information delays facing detached commanders. Founder significance and governing failure should therefore remain separate judgments.
