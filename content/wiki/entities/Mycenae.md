# Mycenae

## Overview

Mycenae is the Bronze Age citadel and later mythic royal seat associated with [[Agamemnon]] and [[Clytemnestra]], where [[HeinrichSchliemann]] excavated elite shaft graves, treasure, bodies, and gold masks.

## Current Profile

The episode uses Mycenae to separate a real elite and fortified Bronze Age center from literal identification with later epic characters. The Lion Gate, massive walls, rich burials, weapons, textiles, and evidence of women inside the citadel make the social world behind Homer less purely imaginary, but the famous mask is centuries earlier than the conventional Trojan War date and cannot be Agamemnon's face.

Mycenae therefore supports cultural and institutional continuity more strongly than biographical continuity: archaeology can illuminate elite rulership, defense, production, and gendered display without naming the dead through myth.

## Key Characteristics

- It was a wealthy, fortified Bronze Age center with elite shaft graves.
- Its later literary identity is tied to Agamemnon's cursed royal household.
- Its material culture supports a warrior and palace society without reproducing Homer's plot.
- Female burials and palace-space evidence complicate a simple picture of secluded women.
- Its chronology falsifies Schliemann's most famous individual identification.

## Evidence

- Fortification and elite burial: [[458-helen-of-troy-a-family-of-blood-part-2-glt7255356131]] describes Cyclopean walls, the Lion Gate, shaft graves, treasure, weapons, bodies, and masks.
- Gender and production: [[458-helen-of-troy-a-family-of-blood-part-2-glt7255356131]] connects female burials, gold diadems, weaving, and weapons within palace space.
- Dating boundary: [[458-helen-of-troy-a-family-of-blood-part-2-glt7255356131]] places the celebrated mask roughly four centuries earlier than Schliemann expected.

## Qualifications

- Material remains do not verify Agamemnon, Clytemnestra, or Helen as historical individuals.
- The episode compresses complex archaeological chronology and debates into a narrative overview.
- Associations among rooms, artifacts, gender, and social roles remain interpretive rather than self-evident.

## What Changed

- Established Mycenae as strong evidence for a Bronze Age palace and warrior world but weak evidence for named Homeric biographies.

## Relationships
