# Gilgamesh / 吉尔伽美什

## Overview

Gilgamesh is the king and epic hero of [[TheEpicOfGilgamesh|The Epic of Gilgamesh]], remembered through friendship, grief, failed immortality, finite kingship, and the city of [[Uruk]].

## Current Profile

[[ep269-cong-manwei-yuzhou-dao-aodesai-weihe-zongyao-fanpai-yingxiong-shishi-ltklotheqpup6oamyflfzpxtipsw]] presents Gilgamesh as superhuman in strength and status but immature in self-knowledge. [[Enkidu]]'s death, repeated mourning, the failed sleep test, and the lost rejuvenation plant turn an overwhelming ruler into a case for [[EpicHeroHumanLimits]]: greatness can consist in accepting the boundary between human life and divine permanence.

[[519-the-worlds-first-city-glt2603135981]] restores the urban ground beneath that literary arc. Gilgamesh may preserve the memory of a real king of Uruk around 2900 BC, but that identification and date remain qualified. The source stresses a twofold dependence: Uruk supplies the royal subject, walls, gardens, and city memory, while its [[AdministrativeWritingInfrastructure]] ultimately makes epic literature preservable. The king's afterlife therefore joins political memory to the transformation of accounting media into poetry.

## Key Characteristics

- King and epic hero associated with Uruk.
- Begins as an overpowering ruler whose strength exceeds his self-knowledge.
- Friendship with Enkidu makes loss the turning point of his character.
- Failed immortality quests teach the limits of kingship and human life.
- Literary survival depends on the writing culture that grew from urban administration.

## Evidence

- Heroic and moral arc: [[ep269-cong-manwei-yuzhou-dao-aodesai-weihe-zongyao-fanpai-yingxiong-shishi-ltklotheqpup6oamyflfzpxtipsw]] links Enkidu's death, repeated grief, sleep, the lost plant, and return to human limits.
- Urban identity: [[519-the-worlds-first-city-glt2603135981]] connects Gilgamesh to Uruk's walls, gardens, clay pits, Inanna precinct, and possible early kingship.
- Media dependence: [[519-the-worlds-first-city-glt2603135981]] argues that Uruk matters both as the hero's city and as an origin point for writing that could later preserve literature.

## Qualifications

The sources are public interpretations of fragmentary, layered traditions. A historical king may stand behind the epic figure, but the episode does not independently establish his date, deeds, or continuity with the literary hero. City descriptions within epic are culturally important without functioning as direct archaeological surveys. The transformation from administrative marks to literary cuneiform was long and should not be reduced to one inventor or moment.

## What Changed

- Added Uruk as the political, spatial, and media setting of Gilgamesh's afterlife.
- Distinguished possible historical kingship from the layered literary hero.
- Connected epic preservation to the earlier administrative development of writing.

## Relationships

- [[Uruk]] - city, kingship setting, and enduring object of the hero's memory.
- [[TheEpicOfGilgamesh]] - literary tradition through which the figure survives.
- [[Enkidu]] - friend whose death transforms Gilgamesh's story.
- [[EpicHeroHumanLimits]] - interpretive concept for mortality, sleep, loss, and failed immortality.
- [[OralEpicRepetition]] - repeated grief speech as emotional accumulation.
- [[AdministrativeWritingInfrastructure]] - urban record medium that later supports written epic preservation.
