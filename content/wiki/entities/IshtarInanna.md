# Ishtar / Inanna

## Overview

Ishtar / Inanna is an ancient Mesopotamian goddess whose accumulated identities join love, sexuality, warfare, kingship, ritual boundary-crossing, death, and seasonal return.

## Current Profile

[[58-the-world-cup-of-gods-part-1-glt8884399326]] places Inanna at Uruk and follows the figure's later Ishtar identity into Akkadian, Assyrian, and Babylonian royal worlds. Association with Sargon of Akkad and later kings makes the goddess political as well as cultic, while the combination of love and war shows divine roles accumulating rather than remaining within modern functional categories.

The descent to the underworld supplies the episode's central mythic sequence. Ishtar passes gates that remove her attributes, dies, and leaves earthly sexuality suspended until a substitute is found; Dumuzid then occupies the underworld for part of the year. Debated sacred-prostitution claims, gender-crossing priesthood, and ritual suspension of ordinary norms are retained as contested rather than flattened into a single claim about Mesopotamian sexuality.

## Key Characteristics

- Very ancient urban and royal goddess associated with Uruk and later Mesopotamian empires.
- Combines love, sexuality, warfare, and political sovereignty.
- Central figure in an underworld descent joining loss of power, death, libido, and seasonal substitution.
- Associated in the episode with disputed sexual rites and gender-crossing priestly practice.
- Later received as a case of female divinity diminished or reinterpreted under changing religious and gender orders.

## Evidence

- Historical layering: [[58-the-world-cup-of-gods-part-1-glt8884399326]] connects Inanna, Uruk, Ishtar, Sargon, and later Assyrian and Babylonian kingship.
- Love-war synthesis: [[58-the-world-cup-of-gods-part-1-glt8884399326]] emphasizes roles that accumulate and change over time.
- Underworld myth: [[58-the-world-cup-of-gods-part-1-glt8884399326]] recounts the gates, loss of attributes, death, earthly sexual suspension, revival, and Dumuzid's substitution.
- Contested ritual and reception: [[58-the-world-cup-of-gods-part-1-glt8884399326]] discusses sacred prostitution, gender-crossing priests, later religious decline, and Simone de Beauvoir's feminist reuse.

## Qualifications

The episode compresses a long, multilingual history into one profile. Inanna and Ishtar should not be assumed identical in every period or city, sacred prostitution remains debated, and reported ritual roles require text-, place-, and date-specific evidence. The possible Yazidi echo through Shamash and Beauvoir's account of patriarchal erasure are reception claims rather than proof of uninterrupted cultic continuity.

## What Changed

- Created a layered profile connecting urban cult, royal power, love, war, and underworld mythology.
- Preserved contested ritual and later feminist reception as qualifications rather than settled history.
