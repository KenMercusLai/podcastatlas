# Ivan Mazepa / 伊万·马泽帕

## Overview

Ivan Mazepa was the Cossack hetman whose 1708 defection from [[PeterTheGreat|Peter the Great]] to [[CharlesXII|Charles XII]] made Ukraine appear to offer the Swedish invasion food, shelter, and military support. The episode presents him neither as a simple traitor nor as an uncomplicated nationalist hero, but as a political broker whose attempt to preserve [[CossackHetmanate|Cossack autonomy]] failed when Russia rapidly destroyed [[Baturin]] and deterred wider support.

## Current Profile

Mazepa's education, multilingual court experience, and diplomatic work prepare him to mediate between Polish, Russian, Ottoman, and Cossack worlds. After Russian backing helps make him hetman in 1687, he must satisfy Moscow while retaining legitimacy among Cossacks wary of becoming imperial subjects.

His switch to Charles in October 1708 is therefore presented as a high-risk response to tightening Russian control and wartime demands. The timing is disastrous: Mazepa reaches the Swedes with only about two thousand retainers, while Menshikov captures Baturin before it can become a Swedish base. Russian denunciation, massacre, ritual deposition, and church excommunication isolate him, and the expected Ukrainian option largely collapses.

## Key Characteristics

- He is a multilingual courtier and diplomat as well as a Cossack military leader.
- His authority depends on balancing Moscow's demands against Cossack expectations of autonomy.
- His defection is a strategic bid for political survival and leverage, not merely a change of battlefield allegiance.
- He overestimates or cannot mobilize the scale and speed of support needed to make the Swedish alliance viable.
- His memory remains polarized between Russian treason narratives and more favorable Ukrainian national interpretations.

## Qualifications

This profile rests on one narrative podcast episode. Mazepa's private motives, the depth of Cossack support, the balance between elite autonomy and broader Ukrainian aims, biographical anecdotes, and later nationalist memory require wider evidence. The page records the episode's interpretive framing rather than resolving the treason-versus-liberation dispute.
