# Neil Price

## Overview

Neil Price is an archaeologist at Uppsala University whose work on Viking Age Scandinavia emphasizes lived worldview, ritual, magic, violence, and the limits of modern categories.

## Current Profile

In [[358-viking-sorcery-glt1200485419]], Price argues that much of the evidence was not unknown so much as underused in conventional accounts. His method joins archaeology, texts, landscape, comparative circumpolar material, and experiential attention while warning that imaginative proximity can become romanticization.

## Key Characteristics

- Reconstructs the Viking world as stranger and less doctrinally organized than later religious categories suggest.
- Uses multiple evidence types while keeping late texts, analogy, and ritual interpretation qualified.
- Treats violence, slavery, human sacrifice, and animal slaughter as indispensable to moral and historical interpretation.
- Prefers uneven social transformation over one universal Viking Age start or end date.
- Distinguishes fascination with Viking history from admiration for Viking actors.

## Evidence

- Worldview and method: [[358-viking-sorcery-glt1200485419]] presents gods, spirits, landscapes, death, and magic as parts of lived reality while warning against romantic landscape identification.
- Source discipline: [[358-viking-sorcery-glt1200485419]] repeatedly qualifies Christian influence, comparative shamanism, narrative parallels, berserkers, and ritual reconstruction.
- Moral boundary: [[358-viking-sorcery-glt1200485419]] foregrounds enslaved funerary victims and mass animal killing against heroic reception.

## Qualifications

This profile is based on one interview-led episode rather than Price's full scholarly corpus. The episode's accounts of his books, views, and preferred interpretations are source-scoped and should not replace direct consultation of his publications.
