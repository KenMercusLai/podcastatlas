# 赵俨 / Zhao Yan (late Han)

## Overview
赵俨 / Zhao Yan is a late-Han Yingchuan refugee turned Cao Cao official whose current profile combines camp-choice timing, law-over-kinship enforcement, and fiscal restraint under wartime local pressure.

## Current Profile
Hanji 1022 presents Zhao Yan as one of three displaced Yingchuan figures who initially use [[LiuBiao|刘表]]'s [[Jingzhou|荆州]] as shelter. After [[CaoCao|曹操]] receives [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]] and builds the [[Xudu|许都]] court position, Zhao Yan treats the field as changed: he moves north with his family and enters Cao Cao's service. As Langling magistrate, he then arrests and executes the lawbreaking uncle of [[LiTongLateHan|李通]]'s wife, making his official role a visible case of public law overriding private appeals.

Hanji 1046 adds the other side of the same capacity. In [[YangAnLateHan|阳安]], with surrounding areas unstable and Li Tong anxious to prove loyalty by sending cotton-silk to Xudu, Zhao Yan argues that forced household collection could create the very disturbance Cao Cao needs to avoid. He persuades Li Tong to pause, writes upward through [[XunYu|荀彧]], and helps turn a local burden warning into Cao Cao's order returning the levied goods to the people.

## Key Characteristics
- Moves from refugee self-preservation to active service only after the political center around Cao Cao and Xudu becomes legible.
- Reads patron choice as a timing problem: hospitality from Liu Biao is different from durable restoration capacity.
- Exercises county-level legal authority against a socially dangerous target connected to a Cao-aligned military actor's household.
- Becomes credible because Li Tong accepts the public-duty frame rather than converting wife-side kinship into exemption.
- Treats fiscal extraction as a political-risk problem when urgent collection could destabilize a loyal but exposed locality.
- Uses an appropriate adviser channel, not public defiance, to convert local information into an acceptable central decision.
- Links Cao Cao's camp-building to enforceable rules and adjustable local administration, not only talent recruitment or battlefield success.

## Qualifications
The Hanji 1022 transcript renders the name as "赵衍" and the office as "朗领长"; the wiki normalizes to 赵俨 / Zhao Yan and Langling magistrate from the historical sequence. The source's claim that Zhao Yan saw Cao Cao as able to unify the realm is treated as Zhao Yan's judgment in the episode, not as independent proof of inevitability. Hanji 1046's later-career sketch is retained as a source-scoped profile, not a complete biography.
