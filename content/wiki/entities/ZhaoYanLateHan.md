## Overview

赵俨 / Zhao Yan is a late-Han Yingchuan refugee turned Cao Cao official whose bounded profile combines camp-choice timing, law-over-kinship enforcement, fiscal restraint, delegated coordination, selective mutiny settlement, and staged battlefield relief.

## Current Profile

Hanji 1022 presents Zhao Yan moving from [[LiuBiao|刘表]]'s Jingzhou refuge into [[CaoCao|曹操]]'s service after the Xudu court position becomes legible. As Langling magistrate, he executes the lawbreaking uncle of [[LiTongLateHan|李通]]'s wife, making public law override private appeals.

Hanji 1046 adds fiscal judgment. In unstable [[YangAnLateHan|阳安]], Zhao Yan argues that forced cotton-silk collection could create the disorder Cao Cao needs to avoid. He persuades Li Tong to pause, writes through [[XunYu|荀彧]], and helps produce an order returning the goods. Hanji 1088 then extends this channel-oriented capacity into delegated military coordination between the mutually hostile [[YuJin|于禁]] and [[LeJinLateHan|乐进]] commands.

Hanji 1155 adds staged mutiny containment. Zhao Yan secures the small group around him, punishes leaders, allows ordinary participants to return, and later separates and rotates forces when immediate pardon does not restore sensitive-command trust.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1169-2026-gai-zenme-shou-zhu-zishen-de-fuqi-lu-3dpvj0nbqhise2aqx-a3zi3kp|Hanji 1169]] applies the same sequencing logic at [[FanchengBattle219LateHan|Fancheng]]. Rather than answer pressure with an understrength immediate attack, Zhao Yan argues that [[XuHuangCaoGeneral|徐晃]] should move close enough to signal relief, raise the besieged garrison's morale, and wait until reinforcement permits coordinated action with Cao Ren. He reportedly accepts responsibility for delay, joining caution to accountable execution rather than passive postponement.

## Key Characteristics

- Changes allegiance only after Cao Cao's court-backed position becomes legible.
- Enforces public law against socially dangerous private connections.
- Treats fiscal extraction as a political-stability problem when timing and local conditions are adverse.
- Uses adviser and delegated-authority channels to coordinate actors without direct troop ownership.
- Separates ringleaders from ordinary participants and immediate de-escalation from restored trust.
- Treats delay as active preparation when proximity, morale, reinforcement, and coordination improve the later action.
- Accepts responsibility for timing advice rather than shifting risk entirely to field commanders.

## Qualifications

Hanji 1022 and Hanji 1088 render the name as “赵衍”; the wiki normalizes it to 赵俨 / Zhao Yan from the historical sequences. The claim that Zhao Yan saw Cao Cao as destined to unify the realm remains source judgment. Exact offices, titles, garrisons, troop totals, commander personalities, levy mechanics, mutiny chronology, relief positions, morale effects, dialogue, and responsibility language require direct textual verification. Delay is beneficial here only if it improves the future position; it is not a general rule against urgent action.

## What Changed

- Added staged Fancheng relief as a later expression of Zhao Yan's sequencing and coordination capacity.
- Added explicit responsibility-taking to the profile of cautious action.
- Connected morale signaling to reinforcement and inside-outside coordination.
