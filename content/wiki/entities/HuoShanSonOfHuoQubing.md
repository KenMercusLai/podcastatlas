## Overview

霍嬗 / 霍子侯 / Huo Shan is presented in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-361-1-hanwudi-liuche-mixin-guishen-de-gushi-lpgclrt_xauneyhm0ri9bi-uenje|Hanji 361-1]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-361-2-taishan-jisi-hou-huoqubing-duzi-liqi-baobi-jiemi-lmixjdfqycjyv_lki1quiq_ox96x|Hanji 361-2]] as the son of [[HuoQubing|霍去病]], successor to his father's marquisate, a close attendant of [[HanWudi|汉武帝]], and the sole named companion in a restricted [[Taishan|泰山]] ascent. The continuation then records his sudden death from an unspecified one-day illness. This page uses `HuoShanSonOfHuoQubing` to distinguish 霍嬗 from the later Huo-family figure 霍山, whose name has the same modern romanization.

## Current Profile

The sources give Huo Shan only a compact court, ritual, and death profile. After Huo Qubing's early death, the son inherits the title and remains close enough to Han Wudi to join a secret mountain rite that excludes other participants. The emperor and Huo Zihou ascend after the altar ceremony, spend the night on the mountain, and descend the following day.

Hanji 361-2 says Huo Zihou remains continuously near the emperor and then dies after a “暴病” that the episode interprets as sudden onset and death within one day. The terse notice supplies no disease, precipitating event, investigation, or explanation. His death follows the Taishan rite in the narrative, but sequence alone does not establish ritual causation or foul play.

## Key Characteristics

- Son and marquisate successor of Huo Qubing in the supplied episode.
- Close imperial attendant identified as 侍中 and 奉车都尉 in the episode's Taishan branch.
- Sole named companion admitted with Han Wudi to the hidden summit rite and overnight stay.
- Subject of a terse sudden-death notice that establishes abruptness but not cause.
- Identity requiring separation from the later 霍山 associated with Huo Guang's household.

- Restricted ritual access: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-361-1-hanwudi-liuche-mixin-guishen-de-gushi-lpgclrt_xauneyhm0ri9bi-uenje|Hanji 361-1]] names Huo Zihou as the only companion who ascends Taishan with the emperor, stays overnight, and returns the next day.
- Narrative role: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-361-1-hanwudi-liuche-mixin-guishen-de-gushi-lpgclrt_xauneyhm0ri9bi-uenje|Hanji 361-1]] moves from the Huo family's early bereavement into the emperor's sacred and immortality-oriented actions.
- Sudden death: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-361-2-taishan-jisi-hou-huoqubing-duzi-liqi-baobi-jiemi-lmixjdfqycjyv_lki1quiq_ox96x|Hanji 361-2]] reports “奉车子侯暴病，一日死” after the Taishan sequence and provides no medical or investigative detail.

## Qualifications

The current page is bounded to two short structured podcast summaries. They do not supply a full biography, clarify the relation between 嬗 and 子侯, establish the reason for Huo Shan's selection, or prove that he independently shaped the ceremony. “暴病，一日死” does not identify a cause and offers no basis for poisoning, murder, ritual consequence, or a specific diagnosis. The grief-to-immortality connection is an episode-level narrative interpretation rather than evidence of Huo Shan's psychology.

## What Changed

- Added the continuation's terse one-day sudden-death notice while explicitly rejecting an unsupported causal explanation.
- Clarified that his ritual proximity and subsequent death are sequentially linked in the narrative, not proven causally linked.
