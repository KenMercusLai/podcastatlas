## Overview

霍嬗 / 霍子侯 / Huo Shan is presented in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-361-1-hanwudi-liuche-mixin-guishen-de-gushi-lpgclrt_xauneyhm0ri9bi-uenje|Hanji 361-1]] as the son of [[HuoQubing|霍去病]], successor to his father's marquisate, and the sole named attendant accompanying [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] during a restricted ascent of [[Taishan|泰山]]. This page uses `HuoShanSonOfHuoQubing` to distinguish 霍嬗 from the later Huo-family figure 霍山, whose name has the same modern romanization.

## Current Profile

The source gives Huo Shan only a compact court-and-ritual profile. After Huo Qubing's early death, the son inherits the title and remains close enough to Han Wudi to join a secret mountain rite that excludes other participants. The emperor and Huo Zihou ascend after the altar ceremony, spend the night on the mountain, and descend the following day.

The episode uses the father's early death and the son's presence to open a reflection on bereavement before moving into imperial pursuit of gods and immortals. That narrative juxtaposition may illuminate the episode's mortality theme, but it does not establish Huo Shan's own beliefs, agency, or reason for selection.

## Key Characteristics

- Son and marquisate successor of Huo Qubing in the supplied episode.
- Close imperial attendant identified as 侍中 and 奉车都尉 in the episode's Taishan branch.
- Sole named companion admitted with Han Wudi to the hidden summit rite and overnight stay.
- Identity requiring separation from the later 霍山 associated with Huo Guang's household.

## Evidence

- Succession and proximity: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-361-1-hanwudi-liuche-mixin-guishen-de-gushi-lpgclrt_xauneyhm0ri9bi-uenje|Hanji 361-1]] says Huo Qubing's son inherits the marquisate and serves near Han Wudi.
- Restricted ritual access: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-361-1-hanwudi-liuche-mixin-guishen-de-gushi-lpgclrt_xauneyhm0ri9bi-uenje|Hanji 361-1]] names Huo Zihou as the only companion who ascends Taishan with the emperor, stays overnight, and returns the next day.
- Narrative role: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-361-1-hanwudi-liuche-mixin-guishen-de-gushi-lpgclrt_xauneyhm0ri9bi-uenje|Hanji 361-1]] moves from the Huo family's early bereavement into the emperor's sacred and immortality-oriented actions.

## Qualifications

The current page is bounded to one short structured podcast summary. It does not supply a full biography, clarify the relation between 嬗 and 子侯, establish the reason for Huo Shan's selection, or prove that he independently shaped the ceremony. The grief-to-immortality connection is the episode's narrative interpretation rather than evidence of Huo Shan's psychology.

## What Changed

- Created a distinct canonical identity for Huo Qubing's son and separated him from the later 霍山.
- Recorded his inherited title, court proximity, and exclusive role in Han Wudi's Taishan ascent.
