# Caroline Walker Bynum

## Overview

Caroline Walker Bynum is a historian of medieval Christianity whose work on food, embodiment, and women's religious practice provides the episode's main scholarly framework for interpreting Catherine of Siena.

## Current Profile

In [[356-the-blood-drinking-bride-of-christ-glt8496536394]], Bynum's *Holy Feast and Holy Fast* helps explain why [[CatherineOfSiena|Catherine]]'s fasting cannot be understood through a modern eating-disorder label alone. Food scarcity, women's work in preparing and distributing food, Eucharistic presence, bodily suffering, and images of Christ's humanity made eating and refusal religious languages with specific medieval meanings.

## Key Characteristics

- Historian used to contextualize medieval women's food practices.
- Connects embodiment and gender to Christian devotion.
- Supports interpretation without eliminating medical or skeptical questions.
- Reads Catherine's blood imagery as identification with Christ's humanity.

## Evidence

- Food and gender frame: [[356-the-blood-drinking-bride-of-christ-glt8496536394]] cites *Holy Feast and Holy Fast* when explaining scarcity, domestic food roles, fasting, and Eucharistic meaning.
- Blood interpretation: [[356-the-blood-drinking-bride-of-christ-glt8496536394]] attributes to Bynum the reading of Catherine's craving for blood as identification with Christ's humanity.
