# John Rizzo

## Overview

John Rizzo was a senior [[CIA]] lawyer whose memoir supplies the institutional account at the center of [[the-road-to-damascus-the-road-to-damascus|The Road to Damascus]].

## Current Profile

Rizzo describes an unnamed former terrorist who volunteered intelligence as atonement, became one of the agency's most valuable sources in a terrorist organization, and helped locate [[CarlosTheJackal|Carlos the Jackal]]. He also acknowledges the procedural failure exposed by the post-[[AldrichAmes]] source review: the CIA had begun using the man despite attacks on Americans and without first consulting the [[FederalBureauOfInvestigation|FBI]] or [[USDepartmentOfJustice|Justice Department]].

His account does not resolve the agency's conflict by denying accountability. Rizzo disclosed the source's history to Justice, while Congress was briefed. Yet his memoir's framing implies that publication of the asset's distinctive operational role led to identification and death, making broken faith with a source the case's final institutional failure.

## Key Characteristics

- Senior CIA legal official positioned between clandestine operations and public-law accountability.
- Memoirist whose overlooked anecdote made the unnamed asset's story reconstructable.
- Acknowledges that consultation with law-enforcement authorities should have preceded use of the asset.
- Participated in disclosure of the asset's history to the Justice Department.
- Frames the later killing as a source-protection and institutional-trust failure.

## Evidence

### Asset value and motive

- [[the-road-to-damascus-the-road-to-damascus]] attributes to Rizzo the account of voluntary, minimally paid cooperation motivated by conscience and atonement.

### Legal-process failure

- [[the-road-to-damascus-the-road-to-damascus]] records his acknowledgment that FBI or Justice consultation should have occurred before the agency used a source implicated in attacks on Americans.

### Consequence and broken faith

- [[the-road-to-damascus-the-road-to-damascus]] presents his memoir as implying that publication exposed the asset and led to his death.

## Qualifications

The profile is bounded to one episode's use of Rizzo's memoir. It does not independently test the memoir, reconstruct the internal legal advice, establish what every CIA official knew, or settle the causal dispute with [[TimWeiner]]. Acknowledging a procedural error does not by itself allocate responsibility for the later disclosure and killing.
