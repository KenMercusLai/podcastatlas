## Overview

季布 / Ji Bu is a former [[XiangYu|项羽]] general whose hostile service to [[LiuBang|刘邦]] is later reframed as loyal duty, enabling pardon and Han office. Under [[LuZhi|吕雉]] he uses the [[BaidengEncirclement|白登之围]] precedent to stop an honor-driven war proposal; as Hedong governor he later challenges [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]]'s praise-and-slander-driven appointment reversal. A separate covert-order theory involving [[ZhouBo|周勃]] remains speculative.

## Current Profile

The two Hanji 170 notes establish Ji Bu's secure current profile. Liu Bang first seeks him under severe bounty after Chu's defeat, but [[ZhuJiaLu|朱家]] shelters him and persuades [[XiahouYing|夏侯婴]] to distinguish faithful service to a former ruler from personal evil. Liu Bang pardons and appoints Ji Bu. Paired with [[DingGong|丁公]], who is executed after helping Liu Bang by betraying his old duty, Ji Bu becomes the positive case in [[FormerEnemyLoyaltyReframing|旧敌忠义重释]].

Hanji 197 places that incorporated former enemy inside early Han strategic deliberation. After [[MaoDunChanyu|冒顿单于]] sends Lu Zhi a provocative letter, [[FanKuai|樊哙]] asks for troops and promises to sweep away the Xiongnu. Ji Bu answers that Liu Bang's much larger Baideng force had still been trapped and that Fan Kuai had failed to resolve that crisis. He also urges the court not to let Xiongnu praise or insult govern its emotions. Lu Zhi accepts the advice and de-escalates, making Ji Bu a successful capacity-based remonstrant rather than only a beneficiary of pardon.

Hanji 215 places Ji Bu as Hedong commandery governor when Zhou Bo fears visits from local officials. The host infers from Ji Bu's courage, independence, and an earlier summons by [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]] that he might have been considered for covert violence against Zhou Bo. That inference is not supported by a direct order, attempt, witness, or independent corroboration and therefore does not alter Ji Bu's established profile as fact.

Hanji 215 part 1 supplies the earlier summons itself. Liu Heng reportedly considers Ji Bu for御史大夫, then changes course after hearing that he is forceful, fond of drink, and hard to approach. Ji Bu answers that recall after one person's praise and dismissal after another person's criticism lets the realm measure the ruler's judgment. The episode therefore extends his profile from loyal former enemy to an official willing to expose [[CourtHearsayAppointmentVolatility|appointment volatility]] directly to the emperor.

## Key Characteristics

- Former Chu commander whose attacks on Liu Bang are reclassified as office-bound loyalty.
- Fugitive protected by Zhu Jia and defended through Xiahou Ying before receiving pardon and office.
- Positive counterpart to Ding Gong in the new dynasty's public definition of loyalty.
- Capacity-based remonstrant who uses remembered defeat to separate injured dignity from feasible frontier policy.
- Hedong governor who directly criticizes unstable ruler judgment in a high-office summons.
- Later Hedong governor whose proximity to Zhou Bo becomes material for a speculative covert-action theory.

## Qualifications

The current evidence consists of five popular-history episode notes rather than a full biography. The rescue route, dialogue, appointment details, endorsers, critics, Ding Gong comparison, Baideng force figures, court debate, and exact diplomatic sequence remain source-scoped. Hanji 197 supports a capacity warning, not a universal claim that war with Xiongnu was always impossible. Hanji 215 part 1 does not prove that a formal御史大夫 appointment had already been made or that one reversal defines Liu Heng's whole personnel system. Most importantly, office, temperament, summons, and temporal proximity do not establish an assassination instruction; the part 2 theory must not be used as factual evidence that Ji Bu planned or attempted to kill Zhou Bo.

## What Changed

- Added Ji Bu's successful Baideng-based warning against an honor-driven Xiongnu war.
- Expanded his profile from incorporated loyal enemy and personnel critic to a capacity-based frontier remonstrant.
