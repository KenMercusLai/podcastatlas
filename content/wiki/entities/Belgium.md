# Belgium

## Overview

Belgium enters the wiki as both a modern assisted-death comparison case and a [[FirstWorldWar]] occupation setting where [[EdithCavell|Edith Cavell]]'s nursing, resistance assistance, execution, and martyr memory became internationally significant.

## Current Profile

In the modern legal-ethics branch, Belgium is an assisted-death law case where euthanasia can extend to psychiatric patients and minors under complex processes. This makes the hard edge of [[AssistedDyingSafeguards]] visible: terminal physical illness is not the only possible eligibility boundary.

In the wartime branch, Belgium is the invaded and occupied country whose refusal of German passage, civilian reprisals, Louvain's destruction, economic dismantling, labor deportations, shortages, and resistance networks made Cavell's clinic and escape-network aid politically explosive.

## Key Characteristics

- Belgium functions as a European legal comparison case for assisted dying.
- The assisted-death material emphasizes psychiatric suffering, minors, safeguards, and clinical responsibility.
- German-occupied Belgium functions as the wartime setting for Cavell's nursing and resistance assistance.
- The First World War material connects occupation violence, resistance networks, and propaganda memory.

## Evidence

- Assisted-death boundary: [[119-anlesi-xianchang-zhengyin-shengming-ruci-zhengui-770771087]] contrasts Belgian mental-illness and minor eligibility with Swiss, Dutch, U.S., Spanish, Japanese, and Chinese cases.
- Assisted-death disagreement: [[119-anlesi-xianchang-zhengyin-shengming-ruci-zhengui-770771087]] presents both a bereaved father's view that prohibition can be inadequate and a bioethics opponent's argument that psychiatric patients need support, care, and treatment rather than assisted death.
- Occupation setting: [[674-the-first-world-war-the-spy-who-took-on-the-germans-part-4-glt1889262052]] describes German occupation after August 1914, civilian executions, Louvain's burning, economic coercion, deported workers, shortages, and resistance networks.
- Cavell branch: [[674-the-first-world-war-the-spy-who-took-on-the-germans-part-4-glt1889262052]] locates Cavell's Brussels clinic inside the Belgian escape network that moved men toward the [[Netherlands]].

## Qualifications

The assisted-death source is a cross-cultural ethical discussion rather than a full legal history of Belgium. The First World War source is centered on Cavell and uses occupation context to explain her case; it should not be treated as a complete account of the Belgian occupation.
