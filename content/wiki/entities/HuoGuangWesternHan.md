## Overview

霍光 / Huo Guang enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-425-huoguang-sihou-canzao-miemen-simaguang-weisha-fenfenbuping-lmz7bdfy8anhkuq24l-f2ulkolbu|Hanji 425]] as the Western Han regent whose loyal service and posthumous family destruction become a test case for [[SimaGuang|司马光]]'s thinking about authority, timing, and punishment. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-401-suihong-zhiming-hanzhaodi-tuiwei-rangxian-huoguang-zaile-ll6pyzpjl0mlcfn0g1zjot2wf8s8|Hanji 401]] now supplies an earlier Han Zhao layer: Huo dominates appointments and policy review, while [[TianQianqiuWesternHan|田千秋]] remains cautious and [[SuiHongWesternHan|眭弘]]'s abdication memorial is punished as a legitimacy threat. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-402-1-sanghongyang-ceng-bang-hanwudi-zhifu-weihe-zuihou-canzao-miezu-ltpcos20d5mafcoywmfcb6iifwsv|Hanji 402-1]] adds a purge-and-law layer: Huo destroys [[SangHongyangWesternHan|桑弘羊]] after the Liu Dan rebellion and then rejects a narrow amnesty reading in the [[HouShiWuWesternHan|侯史吴]] case, turning harboring and lenient judgment into rebellion-adjacent liability. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-402-2-shudiwushu-de-kuli-duzhou-weihe-zuizhong-deyi-shanzhong-lqrunv51se0w7_2fi2ifs_f7zub4|Hanji 402-2]] adds a frontier-command layer in the same regency zone: Huo weighs Zhao Chongguo's caution, appoints his son-in-law [[FanMingyouWesternHan|范明友]], and turns an aborted Xiongnu interception into punishment of Wuhuan. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-404-hanzhaodi-liufuling-zhendeshi-bei-huoguang-haiside-lu3adnqvomeco0tsoviuqwedjpfs|Hanji 404]] supplies the pre-Liu-He death setting: Huo is not treated as proven murderer of [[EmperorZhaoOfHan|汉昭帝刘弗陵]], but he is presented as the regent who constrained the emperor's private life, harem access, and successor environment. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-407-zaiwei-jin-27-tian-han-feidi-liuhe-ruhe-bei-huoguang-feichu-lpob_9jkyomzrbeqnbjekqbpjeog|Hanji 407]] supplies the private prelude to Liu He's deposition: Huo moves from disappointment to secret planning with [[TianYannianWesternHan|田延年]] and [[ZhangAnshiWesternHan|张安世]], accepts the Yi Yin precedent, fears leakage after [[XiahouShengWesternHan|夏侯胜]]'s warning, and opens the Weiyang Palace pressure meeting. Later sources add his inner-circle discipline, family concealment, death honors, Huo-family danger, conspiracy, and retrospective judgment.

## Current Profile

The sources present Huo Guang as a loyal minister who served Han, removed an unsuitable emperor, and helped bring [[EmperorXuanOfHan|汉宣帝]] to the throne. Hanji 401 makes his Han Zhao regency already visible before the later succession crisis: he promotes Zhang Anshi and Du Yannian, lets Du supply a lenient review channel under his harsh enforcement style, and treats Sui Hong's omen-based abdication advice as a capital threat to the dynasty. Hanji 402-1 adds that Huo's regency also polices defeated rivals and legal interpretation after rebellion: old fiscal merit does not save Sang Hongyang once he is tied to Liu Dan's anti-Huo action, and Huo's review in the Hou Shiwu case overrules Wang Ping and Xu Ren's amnesty judgment. Hanji 402-2 adds that the same regency operates across frontier calculation: Huo first hears Zhao Chongguo's argument not to help Wuhuan against Xiongnu, then backs Fan Mingyou with a fallback order that the army should not return empty-handed if Xiongnu cannot be intercepted. Hanji 404 adds that his regency over Han Zhao should be separated from a direct murder accusation: the episode reads illness and long pressure as more plausible than assassination, while still making Huo's control over harem access and childbearing politically consequential. Hanji 407 emphasizes the decision-and-alignment phase before procedure: Huo looks for historical legitimacy, uses Tian Yannian and Zhang Anshi as close planners, and begins turning elite silence into a forced collective posture. Hanji 408 and Hanji 409 emphasize the procedural side of removal: shared ministerial responsibility, empress-dowager authorization, isolation of Liu He's followers, public indictment, seal removal, palace exit, and a settlement that avoids immediate martyrdom. Hanji 410-1 adds the constructive half of the same authority: Huo accepts a hidden but legitimate candidate, convenes officials in the Ye Ting setting, petitions the empress dowager, and has Liu Bingyi brought through washing, zongzheng preparation, Yangwu Hou designation, and seal transfer. Hanji 418 makes Xuan's later gratitude visible in material and ritual form. Sima Guang's later criticism is therefore not that Huo Guang was simply a traitor; it is that he failed to return authority after Xuan was already nineteen, capable, resolute, and familiar with popular hardship.

That delay made Huo Guang's family politically unsafe. Hanji 423 shows why unsafe became desperate: Huo family members might have expected demotion or political exclusion, but Huo Xian's admission that she killed Empress Xu makes them fear that Xuan's eventual move will mean extinction. Hanji 424 gives the later narrative details: after Huo dies, Huo Xian's affair, burial overreach, palace access, and attempted harm to crown prince [[HanYuandi|刘奭]] make the household look undisciplined, while Huo family members plan to kill ministers through an empress-dowager banquet, depose Xuan, and install Huo Yu. Hanji 425 then supplies Sima Guang's retrospective judgment: Huo's descendants had real guilt, but Xuan's delayed removal and total extinction of Huo Guang's sacrificial line remain excessive.

Huo Guang's profile therefore holds achievement, overstay, concealment, and information discipline together. Hanji 412-1 shows that inside his own patronage system he still needs truthful reporting from trusted subordinates: Tian Yannian's corruption might be negotiable, but Tian's false denial makes protection dangerous to Huo's command order. Hanji 414 then shows the darker family-protection side during Huo's lifetime: once the physician arrests threatened to expose Huo Xian, he chose family survival over an imperial murder investigation. Ban Gu's assessment in Hanji 424 praises Huo's immense service in stabilizing Han, but criticizes his lack of learning, private desire, concealment of his wife's evil plot, and placement of his daughter as empress. Sima Guang's judgment in Hanji 425 preserves the same tension by refusing to erase either Huo's loyalty or the political danger his household became.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han regent remembered as loyal to Han, central to Han Zhao's court control, legitimacy policing, anti-rival cleanup, legal classification pressure, frontier-command fallback planning, constrained late reign, Liu He's planning-stage and procedurally staged deposition, and Xuan's accession.
- Deceased meritorious minister whom Xuan honors lavishly before beginning to roll back the family's practical control.
- Overmighty minister whose continued control after Xuan's maturity violated Sima Guang's ruler-authority standard.
- Patriarch whose failure to restrain, expose, and reposition his family exposed descendants to resentment, succession danger, conspiracy, and purge.
- Posthumous source of political restraint because Xuan's gratitude for Huo Guang helps explain why early leaks do not immediately produce full punishment.
- Posthumous merit case because Sima Guang argues his sacrificial line should not have been completely cut off.
- Warning figure for the danger of converting public regency service into enduring family office concentration.

## Qualifications

This page is source-scoped to Hanji 401, Hanji 402-1, Hanji 402-2, Hanji 404, Hanji 407-410-1, Hanji 412-1, Hanji 414, Hanji 418, and Hanji 423-425. It does not yet reconstruct Huo Guang's full career, the complete primary-source record for Han Zhao's reign and death, Sui Hong's legal case, the Sang Hongyang/Liu Dan rebellion case, the Hou Shiwu amnesty dispute, the Wuhuan campaign's complete military record, Liu He's accession and deposition, Tian Yannian's corruption case, Empress Xu's poisoning, the exact legal charges against every Huo household member, or all offices held by Huo descendants.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 402-1's purge-and-law backfill: Huo Guang destroys Sang Hongyang after the Liu Dan rebellion and rejects a narrow amnesty reading in the Hou Shiwu case.
