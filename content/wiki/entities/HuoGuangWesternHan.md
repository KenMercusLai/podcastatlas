## Overview

霍光 / Huo Guang enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-425-huoguang-sihou-canzao-miemen-simaguang-weisha-fenfenbuping-lmz7bdfy8anhkuq24l-f2ulkolbu|Hanji 425]] as the Western Han regent whose loyal service and posthumous family destruction become a test case for [[SimaGuang|司马光]]'s thinking about authority, timing, and punishment. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-394-2-jiemi-huoguang-chengwei-xihan-diyi-quanchen-de-xinji-fmmyov0ecqykq37zkphi40o52bc6|Hanji 394-2]] now supplies the earliest current regency-stabilization layer: after being warned not to repeat the Lu-family mistake of excluding Liu royal kin, Huo appoints [[LiuBijiangWesternHan|刘碧江]] and [[LiuChangleWesternHan|刘长乐]] as a visible royal-kin inclusion move. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-396-1-12-sui-huangdi-yu-6-sui-huanghou-shui-ganchu-de-huangtang-shier-lnq-puxt0zdxhfm7pwilxellccdr|Hanji 396-1]] supplies the next Shangguan rupture layer: Huo refuses to support six-year-old [[ShangguanTaihouWesternHan|上官氏]]'s palace entry, then [[ShangguanJieWesternHan|上官桀]] and [[ShangguanAnWesternHan|上官安]] bypass him through [[DingWairenWesternHan|丁外人]] and [[GaiChanggongzhuWesternHan|鄂邑 / 盖长公主]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-399-hanwudi-tuogu-dachen-shengsi-neidou-mubiao-gandiao-huoguang-lgiwuhupmd0dox-oaoxjfj-smlw5|Hanji 399]] supplies the next danger layer: Shangguan, princess, Sang Hongyang, and Liu Dan-aligned actors first try a forged memorial accusing Huo of quasi-imperial display and possible treason. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-400-1-taidula-hanzhaodi-jing-bipo-gege-yanwang-liudan-zisha-ln0-b64ip2kwpcpen_65e4du3vqc|Hanji 400-1]] then shows the same anti-Huo field escalating into a banquet assassination plan before deposing [[EmperorZhaoOfHan|汉昭帝刘弗陵]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-401-suihong-zhiming-hanzhaodi-tuiwei-rangxian-huoguang-zaile-ll6pyzpjl0mlcfn0g1zjot2wf8s8|Hanji 401]] supplies another Han Zhao layer: Huo dominates appointments and policy review, while [[TianQianqiuWesternHan|田千秋]] remains cautious and [[SuiHongWesternHan|眭弘]]'s abdication memorial is punished as a legitimacy threat. Later sources add purge-and-law, frontier-command, Liu He deposition, Xuan accession, inner-circle discipline, family concealment, death honors, Huo-family danger, conspiracy, and retrospective judgment.

## Current Profile

The sources present Huo Guang as a loyal minister who served Han, survived slander and assassination attempts, removed an unsuitable emperor, and helped bring [[EmperorXuanOfHan|汉宣帝]] to the throne. Hanji 394-2 adds the earliest current managerial layer: Huo recognizes that regency dominance becomes safer when royal kin receive office and cooperative benefit, so his power is not presented as pure exclusion at the beginning. Hanji 396-1 adds the first family-politics complication inside that loyal-regent profile: Huo resists the Shangguan plan to send his six-year-old granddaughter into Han Zhao's palace, but the Shangguan side bypasses him through the princess's intimate access channel. Hanji 399 and Hanji 400-1 together then show the danger his regency attracts: opponents first try to convert forged accusation into imperial investigation, then escalate toward banquet assassination and deposition. Hanji 401 makes his Han Zhao regency already visible before the later succession crisis: he promotes Zhang Anshi and Du Yannian, lets Du supply a lenient review channel under his harsh enforcement style, and treats Sui Hong's omen-based abdication advice as a capital threat to the dynasty. Hanji 402-1 adds that Huo's regency also polices defeated rivals and legal interpretation after rebellion: old fiscal merit does not save Sang Hongyang once he is tied to Liu Dan's anti-Huo action, and Huo's review in the Hou Shiwu case overrules Wang Ping and Xu Ren's amnesty judgment. Hanji 402-2 adds that the same regency operates across frontier calculation. Hanji 404 separates his regency over Han Zhao from a direct murder accusation while still making Huo's control over harem access and childbearing politically consequential. Hanji 407-410-1 then follow his Liu He deposition and Xuan accession authority. Hanji 418 makes Xuan's later gratitude visible in material and ritual form. Sima Guang's later criticism is therefore not that Huo Guang was simply a traitor; it is that he failed to return authority after Xuan was already nineteen, capable, resolute, and familiar with popular hardship.

That delay made Huo Guang's family politically unsafe. Hanji 423 shows why unsafe became desperate: Huo family members might have expected demotion or political exclusion, but Huo Xian's admission that she killed Empress Xu makes them fear that Xuan's eventual move will mean extinction. Hanji 424 gives the later narrative details: after Huo dies, Huo Xian's affair, burial overreach, palace access, and attempted harm to crown prince [[HanYuandi|刘奭]] make the household look undisciplined, while Huo family members plan to kill ministers through an empress-dowager banquet, depose Xuan, and install Huo Yu. Hanji 425 then supplies Sima Guang's retrospective judgment: Huo's descendants had real guilt, but Xuan's delayed removal and total extinction of Huo Guang's sacrificial line remain excessive.

Huo Guang's profile therefore holds achievement, overstay, concealment, and information discipline together. Hanji 412-1 shows that inside his own patronage system he still needs truthful reporting from trusted subordinates: Tian Yannian's corruption might be negotiable, but Tian's false denial makes protection dangerous to Huo's command order. Hanji 414 then shows the darker family-protection side during Huo's lifetime: once the physician arrests threatened to expose Huo Xian, he chose family survival over an imperial murder investigation. Ban Gu's assessment in Hanji 424 praises Huo's immense service in stabilizing Han, but criticizes his lack of learning, private desire, concealment of his wife's evil plot, and placement of his daughter as empress. Sima Guang's judgment in Hanji 425 preserves the same tension by refusing to erase either Huo's loyalty or the political danger his household became.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han regent remembered as loyal to Han, central to Han Zhao's court control, early royal-kin benefit sharing, initial resistance to Shangguan Shi's child palace entry, later forged accusation and anti-Huo assassination planning, legitimacy policing, anti-rival cleanup, legal classification pressure, frontier-command fallback planning, constrained late reign, Liu He's deposition, and Xuan's accession.
- Deceased meritorious minister whom Xuan honors lavishly before beginning to roll back the family's practical control.
- Overmighty minister whose continued control after Xuan's maturity violated Sima Guang's ruler-authority standard.
- Patriarch whose failure to restrain, expose, and reposition his family exposed descendants to resentment, succession danger, conspiracy, and purge.
- Posthumous source of political restraint because Xuan's gratitude for Huo Guang helps explain why early leaks do not immediately produce full punishment.
- Posthumous merit case because Sima Guang argues his sacrificial line should not have been completely cut off.
- Warning figure for the danger of converting public regency service into enduring family office concentration.
