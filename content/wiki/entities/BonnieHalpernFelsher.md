# Bonnie Halpern-Felsher

## Overview
Bonnie Halpern-Felsher is presented in [[vaping-alcohol-use-other-risky-youth-behaviors-dr-bonnie-halpern-felsher-scim9155116429]] as a Stanford professor of pediatrics and adolescent medicine and a developmental psychologist studying adolescent risk behavior.

## Current Profile
Halpern-Felsher's role in the wiki is to connect youth substance use with development, product design, commercial targeting, peers, family relationships, stress, and addiction rather than treating it as a simple failure of judgment. She keeps parents involved while recognizing adolescent autonomy and uses youth advisory input to make prevention and quitting messages more credible.

Her practical stance is a continuum: prevent initiation where possible, support reduction and quitting when use has begun, and provide harm-reduction tools for ongoing risk. She favors accurate, non-moralizing conversations tied to immediate goals and industry incentives, while preserving clinical and evidentiary boundaries around prevalence, nicotine replacement, cannabis psychosis, pouches, Narcan, and fentanyl test strips.

## Key Characteristics
- Interprets adolescent risk through autonomy, identity, peer influence, development, and continuing adult support.
- Treats nicotine initiation as partly engineered through flavor, design, concealability, marketing, and social context.
- Replaces fear-only messaging with accurate information, immediate relevance, and repeated conversation.
- Includes youth perspectives in prevention and cessation design.
- Joins primary prevention, quitting support, and harm reduction without presenting any one layer as sufficient.
- Maintains uncertainty around changing prevalence, emerging products, off-label treatment, and psychiatric causality.
