## Overview
刘平 / Liu Ping is treated by the current wiki route as an Eastern Han official whose evidence now has two moralized governance layers. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-693-panchu-liuxiu-de-pengchong-ruhe-yin-beipan-er-wang-lqler4fu9wqztsrgzsxhs1519nm]] shows a Liu Ping protecting his superior Sun Meng with his own body during [[PangMengEasternHan|Pang Meng]]'s rebellion, while [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-735-13-sui-rugong-zhongsheng-weiyu-tade-xianming-zhen-hanjian-lj2lyie73xk9fubebzwogd-w7hrq|Hanji 735]] shows Liu Ping's later Quanjiao governance producing voluntary trust signals.

## Current Profile
Hanji 693 places Liu Ping inside a crisis of rebel violence. Pang Meng captures Pengcheng and wants to kill Chu commandery's Sun Meng. Liu Ping lies over Sun Meng, weeps, asks to die in his place, receives seven wounds, and then gives Sun Meng blood from his own wounds when Sun Meng revives thirsty. The episode uses the scene to show loyalty as bodily substitution rather than verbal allegiance.

Hanji 735 gives the local-administration layer. Liu Ping governs Quanjiao in a way that leaves unusually strong civic trust signals: people voluntarily increase their reported property in order to pay more tax, lower their declared age so they can serve, and leave prisons nearly empty. Zhongli Yi and another official recommend him, and he is appointed as a Lang. The combined profile links personal loyalty under coercion with later benevolent administration that makes subjects voluntarily cooperate with government.

## Key Characteristics
- Self-substituting subordinate who physically shields an endangered superior during rebellion.
- Loyalty exemplar whose wounds become evidence of commitment rather than just rhetoric.
- Local administrator remembered through voluntary tax and service behavior by the people under his rule.
- Low-coercion governor whose prisons are described as nearly empty.
- Beneficial official whose local record is legible enough to be recommended for central service.

## Qualifications
The page remains bounded to the listed source notes. The wiki currently keeps Hanji 693 and Hanji 735 under the existing LiuPingEasternHan route because both source notes concern an Eastern Han Liu Ping remembered through exemplary official conduct; if later evidence proves distinct biographies, the route should be split with stable semantic suffixes. Hanji 693 is a loyalty crisis scene and does not prove Liu Ping's whole early career. Hanji 735's reported popular behavior is best treated as a moralized historiographical signal of trust, not as audited administrative data.

## What Changed
- Added Hanji 693 as a self-sacrificial loyalty layer around Pang Meng's rebellion.
- Preserved Hanji 735 as the Quanjiao local-governance and central-recommendation layer.
- Reframed Liu Ping's profile around exemplary conduct under both coercive danger and ordinary administration.
