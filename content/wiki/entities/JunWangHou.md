# 君王后 / Jun Wang Hou

## Overview
君王后 / Jun Wang Hou is [[TaishiJiao|太史角]]'s daughter, the crisis partner and later queen of [[TianFazhang|田法章 / 齐襄王]], and the regent for their son [[QiWangJian|齐王建 / 田建]]. Her profile joins personal autonomy, ritual conflict, competent low-posture rule, costly non-intervention, and an incomplete final transfer of trusted personnel knowledge.

## Current Profile
She first appears helping the hidden Tian Fazhang in Ju and entering a relationship before formal dynastic arrangement. After his restoration, she becomes queen; after his death, she governs cautiously for their young son. Her Qin-facing neutrality and trust toward other lords preserve Qi for decades and include dignity-preserving resistance such as the jade-ring response. Yet the same line refuses grain to Zhao at Changping, leaves Qi outside collective defense, and is later inherited as a false sense of security. On her deathbed she tries to name loyal and capable ministers, but the transfer fails before the names can be recorded, exposing a succession-support weakness without erasing the real peace her policy bought.

## Key Characteristics
- Forms a crisis-based relationship with Tian Fazhang outside normal arranged-marriage procedure.
- Maintains respect toward her estranged father despite his ritual objection to the marriage.
- Governs as a cautious regent who combines low posture toward Qin with defense of Qi's dignity.
- Preserves decades of peace while contributing to non-intervention and coalition weakness.
- Fails to complete a final handoff of reliable ministers to Qi Wang Jian.

- **Ritual-family tension:** [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-79-shishang-yong-gengniu-da-shengzhang-diyi-ren-lsrl0posblloqwugox160xsicp2p]] records Taishi Jiao's refusal to see her and her continued respect toward him.
- **Regency and non-intervention:** [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-91-chulong-ruhe-qiaokai-shougua-zhaotaihou-de-men-2-liy5yulg2ysop-jwfigvdkub1pk7]] establishes her cautious regency, while [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-94-shashen-baiqi-changping-zhi-zhan-zhong-de-caobao-mingjiang-2-lvqctrazn8ddkcnp-lhspzzmzmns]] places her in the refusal of Zhao's grain request.
- **Peace, dignity, and strategic trap:** [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-119-3-lishishang-di-yi-wei-zhenzheng-yiyishang-chuilian-tingzheng-taihou-liq9d3itmbar00ij43zzed-mulul]] credits decades of peace, recounts the jade linked-rings response, and situates her policy under Qin's far-friend/near-attack sequencing.
- **Succession handoff and later judgment:** [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-119-4-shishang-neng-cheng-juying-de-junwang-qiwangjian-livcg5uz60ne-6oje-qzjfpghhph]] records the failed deathbed naming of ministers; [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-120-1-shui-shi-huangdi-zhong-de-mabaonan-lhw8vsj6ta6rjdkugcvfohaqqxc0]] weighs long peace and casualties avoided against terminal strategic isolation.

## Qualifications
Claims that Qin “did not dare” attack during her rule, the exact duration and form of her control, the jade-ring story, and the deathbed exchange remain source-scoped. The first “true curtain regency” title also remains in tension with earlier court-power claims for [[XuanTaihou|宣太后]]. Her policy's civilian benefits and strategic costs should not be collapsed into either uncomplicated praise or blame.

## What Changed
- Her final succession-support effort is now visible as an incomplete transfer of trusted personnel knowledge.
- The long-peace synthesis now includes military-readiness erosion in the next reign.
- Her legacy is more clearly separated into immediate peace, preserved dignity, coalition cost, and fragile succession.
