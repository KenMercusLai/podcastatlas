# 君王后 / Jun Wang Hou

君王后 / Jun Wang Hou appears in [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-73-lishishang-ziyou-lianai-de-kaichuangzhe-shi-shui-lkumfsfialqgdl6qpb3c-hnz7h9u]] first as [[TaishiJiao|太史角]]'s daughter in [[JuWarringStates|莒城]]. She notices that the household servant [[TianFazhang|田法章]] has unusual appearance and bearing, treats him with sympathy, and learns that he is the son of [[QiMinWang|齐湣王]].

The episode uses her relationship with Tian Fazhang as the title's "free love" case. Their private bond forms while he is a fugitive and before he can offer a formal dynastic match; after he is restored as [[QiXiangWang|齐襄王]], he brings her out and makes her queen.

The source also projects her later political importance, saying she governed with her son for more than forty years and that Qin did not dare attack during that period. The wiki keeps that as a source-scoped claim until a later source develops the post-restoration reign.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-79-shishang-yong-gengniu-da-shengzhang-diyi-ren-lsrl0posblloqwugox160xsicp2p]] adds the family-ritual cost of that crisis relationship. Her father [[TaishiJiao|太史敫 / 太史角]] refuses to recognize or see her because the marriage did not pass through parental command and matchmaker procedure. The episode uses [[MenciusText|《孟子》]] to frame that as [[MarriageRitualOrthodoxy|marriage ritual orthodoxy]], while still praising Jun Wang Hou for continuing to treat her father with respect after becoming queen.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-91-chulong-ruhe-qiaokai-shougua-zhaotaihou-de-men-2-liy5yulg2ysop-jwfigvdkub1pk7]] moves her from queen into regent. After Qi Xiang Wang dies, her son [[QiWangJian|齐王建 / 田建]] succeeds while young, and Jun Wang Hou governs in a cautious, peace-oriented style. The episode contrasts that posture with [[TianDan|田单]]'s forceful military profile and treats it as part of why he may not have remained comfortable in Qi.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-94-shashen-baiqi-changping-zhi-zhan-zhong-de-caobao-mingjiang-2-lvqctrazn8ddkcnp-lhspzzmzmns]] makes that cautious posture costly. The episode says Zhao's Changping army asks Qi for grain after being cut off, and that Jun Wang Hou is the actual decision-maker who refuses. Her peace-oriented rule therefore becomes part of [[ShieldStateRescueFailure|屏障国救援失灵]]: avoiding war with Qin in the short term helps leave Zhao's army starving.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-119-3-lishishang-di-yi-wei-zhenzheng-yiyishang-chuilian-tingzheng-taihou-liq9d3itmbar00ij43zzed-mulul]] returns to the same policy from Qi's own late-unification vantage point. The source describes her line as cautious toward Qin, trustworthy toward the other lords, neutral, and non-aligned, crediting it with more than forty years of peace for [[QiState|齐国]]. It also calls the policy a form of [[AppeasementLogic|appeasement]], because Qin's friendliness can be read as part of [[YuanjiaoJingongStrategy|远交近攻]] rather than as proof that Qi is permanently safe. The jade linked-rings story complicates the image: Jun Wang Hou avoids direct war, but still answers a Qin diplomatic challenge in a way that preserves state dignity.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-120-1-shui-shi-huangdi-zhong-de-mabaonan-lhw8vsj6ta6rjdkugcvfohaqqxc0]] adds the posthumous outcome of that line. After [[WangBenQin|王贲]] enters Qi and [[QiWangJian|齐王建 / 田建]] surrenders, the host says Jun Wang Hou's cautious course can still be credited with preserving decades of peace and preventing mass casualties in the final campaign, even though it also left Qi without a viable anti-Qin coalition. This makes her page part of [[CasualtySparingSurrender|保全生灵式投降]] as well as the earlier appeasement warning.
