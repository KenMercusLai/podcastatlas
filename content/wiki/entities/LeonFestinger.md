# Leon Festinger / 利昂·费斯廷格

## Overview

Leon Festinger was a social psychologist represented in the wiki through work associated with deindividuation and cognitive dissonance, plus a popular reaction-choice maxim whose attribution is not treated as settled scholarship.

## Current Profile

[[123-renxing-shiyan-dangxin-ni-ziji-779479569]] places Festinger and colleagues in the history of [[Deindividuation]], describing the idea that immersion in a group can reduce individual self-awareness and self-control. [[defaultmp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-3bb43a3a1bc3b7d73f72cf6318a8ff07-56990038-defaultmp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-3bb43a3a1bc3b7d73f72cf6318a8ff07-56990038|When You Need It To Be True]] provides the stronger cognitive-dissonance profile: Festinger predicted that [[DorothyMartin|Dorothy Martin]]'s followers might preserve or intensify belief after a failed prophecy when prior sacrifice made admitting error extremely costly.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-161-caiyun-he-renji-guanxi-de-liangzi-jiuchan-2-lttnuxlypca4pafz1mzdr3aizfcv|Hanji 161 part 2]] invokes “费斯丁格法则” as a popular 10/90 rule in which response matters more than the uncontrollable initiating event. The wiki records that source's usage as part of [[ReactionChoiceFrame|reaction-choice framing]], not as verified authorship or a complete statement of Festinger's research.

## Key Characteristics

- Social psychologist associated in the supplied sources with group behavior and belief-preserving rationalization.
- Linked to an early deindividuation account about diminished individual presence inside groups.
- Predicted that costly commitment could make failed prophecy strengthen proselytizing or reinterpretation rather than end belief.
- Used field observation of the Seekers as a real-world test of cognitive-dissonance theory.
- Frequently invoked in popular psychology beyond the claims that these sources securely attribute to his scholarship.

## Qualifications

The three sources are podcast summaries, not Festinger's publications, complete field records, or a scholarly biography. The failed-prophecy episode cannot establish the internal state of every group member, and later retellings may simplify the observation and theory. The 10/90 maxim remains a source-scoped popular attribution. Dissonance is an explanatory mechanism, not proof of dishonesty, pathology, or inevitable resistance to evidence.
