# Roger / 罗杰（《蝇王》）

## Overview
Roger / 罗杰 is the more directly violent boy in [[LordOfTheFlies|《蝇王》]] as discussed in [[vol-351-yingwang-21shiji-de-huangdao-gushi-haineng-zenme-kai-naodong-1983-nuojiang-dezhu-gkwriueodap1a3taaattkgol]].

## Current Profile
The episode uses Roger to separate organized factional power from a more immediate appetite for violence. If Jack turns hunting and fear into political control, Roger is treated as closer to direct cruelty: he needs less justification and therefore sharpens the book's question of where evil sits once ordinary restraint weakens.

## Key Characteristics
- He represents a more direct violence than Jack's political-rivalry role.
- The source treats him as close to "pure" evil within the symbolic cast.
- His role helps the episode avoid explaining all harm through leadership competition alone.
- He belongs to the broader breakdown in which restraint, rule, and empathy fail.

- Direct violence: [[vol-351-yingwang-21shiji-de-huangdao-gushi-haineng-zenme-kai-naodong-1983-nuojiang-dezhu-gkwriueodap1a3taaattkgol]] records ChaoGe's view that Roger is closer to pure evil because he needs less political excuse.
- Symbolic cast: [[vol-351-yingwang-21shiji-de-huangdao-gushi-haineng-zenme-kai-naodong-1983-nuojiang-dezhu-gkwriueodap1a3taaattkgol]] places Roger among the boys carrying violence within the allegory.
- Beyond rivalry: [[vol-351-yingwang-21shiji-de-huangdao-gushi-haineng-zenme-kai-naodong-1983-nuojiang-dezhu-gkwriueodap1a3taaattkgol]] distinguishes Jack's power and hunting logic from Roger's more immediate cruelty.
- Restraint failure: [[vol-351-yingwang-21shiji-de-huangdao-gushi-haineng-zenme-kai-naodong-1983-nuojiang-dezhu-gkwriueodap1a3taaattkgol]] treats the island's violence as emerging after rules, fear control, and civil restraint fail.
