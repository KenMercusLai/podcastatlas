## Overview

马武 / Ma Wu is synthesized as an Eastern Han commander whose current evidence now begins with a Jianwu 5 battlefield reversal beside [[WangBaEasternHan|王霸]], then continues through later Guangwu and Mingdi frontier pressure. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-692-donghan-kaiguo-dajiang-wangba-de-rensheng-you-duo-baqi-lmqqtsxwemetqqae-qdierska9pl|Hanji 692]] shows him defeated by [[SuMaoEasternHan|苏茂]] and [[ZhouJianEasternHan|周建]], forced into desperate resistance while Wang Ba waits for the right rescue window. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-714-shi-shui-di-yi-ge-han-chu-nan-er-dang-ma-ge-guo-shi-lpkqxrl3dsok4ui0lij4c3x8zfpi|Hanji 714]] keeps him in a Guangwu-era Hutuo defensive deployment against Xiongnu pressure, while [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-733-1-lishi-suo-yiwang-de-diwang-zhishi-mingjun-liuzhuang-lor7vhzzpwz1rv9tijksogukjqvr|Hanji 733-1]] presents him as the commander who later suppresses the early Mingdi [[ShaodangQiang|Shaodang Qiang]] revolt after earlier Han responses fail.

## Current Profile

Hanji 692 supplies Ma Wu's earliest current role. In Jianwu 5 / 29 CE, Su Mao and Zhou Jian defeat Ma Wu, and Ma Wu flees to Wang Ba's camp asking for rescue. Wang Ba refuses immediate aid because the enemy is strong and Han troops are fearful; the refusal forces Ma Wu's troops to fight for survival instead of passively depending on relief. Ma Wu is therefore not the planner of the victory, but he becomes the tactical pressure point that makes Wang Ba's later rear-cavalry strike work.

Hanji 714 supplies Ma Wu's later Guangwu frontier role. In Jianwu 13 / 37 CE, Liu Xiu sends the Bulu General Ma Wu to station troops at the Hutuo River to guard against Xiongnu disturbance. The episode does not make Ma Wu the branch's decisive attacker; it presents him as part of a defensive containment layer while [[LuFangEasternHan|Lu Fang]] and Xiongnu forces pressure Yunzhong and then fracture through [[SuiYuEasternHan|Sui Yu]]'s defection.

Hanji 733-1 places Ma Wu beside [[DouGuEasternHan|Dou Gu]] when Mingdi sends a major force against the Shaodang Qiang crisis. Zhang Hong had been defeated at Yunwu, and the new reign needed a stronger military response after Dian Wu's advance into Longxi.

Ma Wu's distinct action comes later in the same year, when he attacks the Qiang and achieves suppression. The page's current evidence is therefore narrow but clear: Ma Wu is the successful field resolver of this early frontier test.

## Key Characteristics

- Eastern Han commander whose earliest current scene is a defeated ally whose forced resistance helps create Wang Ba's battle window.
- Battlefield actor whose crisis illustrates why rescue timing and morale can matter more than immediate reinforcement.
- Guangwu-era frontier commander later stationed defensively against Xiongnu pressure.
- Military actor in Mingdi's first Shaodang Qiang crisis.
- Sent with Dou Gu as part of a larger escalated response under Mingdi.
- Receives the clearest current credit for the later Shaodang Qiang suppressive attack.
- Functions as a contrast to the failed initial command response.

## Qualifications

Hanji 692 does not make Ma Wu the strategist of Wang Ba's victory; it uses him as the allied force whose crisis changes the field. Hanji 714 does not describe a battle result for Ma Wu's Hutuo deployment, so the page should not infer victory from that notice alone. Hanji 733-1 does not provide Ma Wu's broader career, nor does it detail the tactics of the final suppression. Dou Gu's role in the final stage is less specified in Hanji 733-1 than Ma Wu's.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 692 as Ma Wu's earliest current battlefield layer, where defeat and delayed rescue become part of Wang Ba's morale-tempo victory.
- Reframed Ma Wu as both a battlefield pressure point in Guangwu's eastern fighting and a later frontier commander.
- Preserved Hanji 714 as Ma Wu's Guangwu-era Hutuo defensive deployment against Xiongnu pressure.
- Preserved Hanji 733-1 as the early-Mingdi Shaodang Qiang suppression layer.
