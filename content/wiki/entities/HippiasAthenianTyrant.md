# Hippias / Athenian Tyrant

## Overview

Hippias is the Peisistratid successor whose increasingly oppressive rule helps end Athenian tyranny and whose later return with Persia turns Marathon into a political-restoration threat as well as an invasion.

## Current Profile

The origin source places Hippias inside dynastic decline. He inherits power with Hipparchus after [[Peisistratus]] dies; after Hipparchus is assassinated following a personal dispute, Hippias becomes increasingly oppressive. [[Cleisthenes]] then uses Alcmaeonid influence at Delphi to encourage Sparta to intervene, and Cleomenes forces Hippias into exile in 510 BC.

The Marathon source shows the exile returning with the Persians in 490 BC. Persian hopes for internal division and betrayal make him dangerous because he offers a local alternative to the existing Athenian order. The episode even notes that Hippias believed he was helping Athens by bringing it into submission to Persia.

Hippias therefore personifies the fear that Athens might fall from within. Marathon is not only about hoplites against Persian infantry; it is also about whether Athenian factions, exiles, and betrayal could undo the city's fragile autonomy.

## Key Characteristics

- Hippias is an exiled Athenian tyrant attached to the Persian expedition.
- His repression is presented as a post-assassination turn rather than the whole Peisistratid style of rule.
- Spartan intervention, encouraged through Cleisthenes' Delphic strategy, drives him into exile.
- He makes the invasion a possible restoration of tyranny rather than only foreign conquest.
- His presence links Persian strategy to Athenian internal division.
- The source treats him as sincerely self-justifying rather than only as a flat villain.

## Evidence

- Dynastic decline and exile: [[334-athens-and-the-birth-of-democracy-glt2496707123]] links Hipparchus' assassination to Hippias' increased repression and says Cleomenes forced him out in 510 BC.
- Exile and return: [[669-greece-vs-persia-the-battle-of-marathon-part-2-glt2696648260]] says Hippias was expelled in 510 BC and accompanied the Persians back to Attica.
- Betrayal risk: [[669-greece-vs-persia-the-battle-of-marathon-part-2-glt2696648260]] says the Persians expected internal divisions in Athens to create opportunities.
- Self-understanding: [[669-greece-vs-persia-the-battle-of-marathon-part-2-glt2696648260]] says Hippias believed he was helping Athens by submitting it to Persia.

## Qualifications

The sources provide a compressed political arc rather than a full chronology of Hippias' rule, the assassination conspiracy, Spartan motives, exile, or Persian relationship. His supposed belief that Persian submission would help Athens remains the Marathon episode's interpretation.

## What Changed

- Extended Hippias backward from Marathon to dynastic succession, repression, Spartan intervention, and exile.

## Relationships

- [[Athens]] - city Hippias seeks to re-enter under Persian backing.
- [[Peisistratus]] - father whose more stable tyranny Hippias inherits.
- [[Cleisthenes]] - rival whose Delphic strategy helps bring Spartan intervention.
- [[AchaemenidEmpire]] - imperial force with which Hippias returns.
- [[BattleOfMarathon]] - crisis in which his presence raises the internal-collapse threat.
- [[AthenianDemocracy]] - political order threatened by possible tyrant restoration.
