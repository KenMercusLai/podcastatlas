# Mobutu Sese Seko

## Overview

Mobutu Sese Seko appears as the Congolese official who emerged from the Congo Crisis, seized the presidency in 1965, renamed the country Zaire, and combined Western alignment with authoritarian “authenticity” politics.

## Current Profile

The episode first introduces Mobutu as Joseph-Désiré Mobutu, a secretary of state during [[PatriceLumumba]]'s short premiership. After Lumumba's murder and further civil war, Mobutu removed President Joseph Kasavubu and the prime minister in November 1965 and declared himself president.

His later regime is presented through a political contradiction. Mobutu relied on American and wider Western support while using renaming, dress, personal titulature, and restrictions on European Christian names to perform a break with colonial culture. The source treats that symbolic decolonization as compatible with personal dictatorship, executions, and large-scale plunder.

## Key Characteristics

- Rose from the early post-independence Congo government and crisis.
- Took presidential power in a November 1965 coup.
- Consolidated personal authoritarian rule after civil war and secession.
- Renamed Congo as Zaire and promoted “authenticity” and Mobutuism.
- Combined anti-European symbolism with Western geopolitical backing.

## Evidence

- Early role and takeover: [[100-decolonising-africa-glt2427735251]] follows Mobutu from Lumumba's secretary of state to the 1965 removal of civilian leaders.
- Authoritarian consolidation: [[100-decolonising-africa-glt2427735251]] cites public executions and personal rule after the Congo crises.
- Authenticity politics: [[100-decolonising-africa-glt2427735251]] describes the Zaire renaming, changed names and symbols, and restrictions on European baptismal names.

## Qualifications

The episode supplies a compressed moral and political portrait, not a full institutional history of Mobutu's rule. Claims about wealth extraction, Western sponsorship, ideology, repression, and comparisons with other postcolonial rulers require deeper country-specific evidence.

## What Changed

- Created Mobutu's profile as a case of authoritarian consolidation joining Western dependence to symbolic anti-colonial authenticity.

## Relationships

- [[PatriceLumumba]] - prime minister whose fall and murder preceded Mobutu's consolidation.
- [[DemocraticRepublicOfCongo]] - state he ruled and renamed Zaire.
- [[BelgianCongo]] - colonial predecessor whose institutional afterlife framed the early crisis.
- [[AfricanDecolonization]] - process whose unresolved aftermath his regime exemplifies.
