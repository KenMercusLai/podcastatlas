## Overview

任安 / Ren An is presented as a Western Han official and associate of [[WeiQingHanGeneral|卫青]] who remains connected to Wei after political favor shifts toward [[HuoQubing|霍去病]]. The current evidence uses that persistence as a moral contrast with retainers who follow the stronger patron, while leaving Ren An's complete offices, chronology, and later biography outside the supplied source.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-327-1-wanwan-meixiangdao-hanwudi-ye-ai-chi-xiangcai-lv_ed7hfumn80ypmwq8nfdpdfwcv|Hanji 327-1]] identifies Ren An as the “长安使” present when Wei Qing asked advisers how to handle 苏建 after a battlefield defeat. The episode then places him in the aftermath of the Mobei campaign, when Wei's influence declines and other friends or retainers reportedly move toward Huo Qubing for appointments and benefit.

Ren An's durable importance in this source is relational rather than administrative. He continues to enter Wei Qing's household after the incentive field changes, and the host treats that behavior as evidence that he would not bend simply toward current power. The source links him to [[SimaQian|司马迁]], Tian Ren, and the appended biographies associated with the *Tian Shu liezhuan*, but it does not provide enough detail to reconstruct those relationships fully.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han official identified by the episode as a Chang'an envoy in Wei Qing's earlier command setting.
- Associate who continues visiting Wei Qing after Wei's favor and household attraction decline.
- Contrast case to retainers who move toward Huo Qubing when appointments and benefits appear more available there.
- Figure whom the host connects to Sima Qian's praise of resistance to powerful influence.
- Friend of Sima Qian and Tian Ren in the episode's compressed biographical account, with the exact relationship chronology left source-scoped.

## Qualifications

This page rests on one short podcast summary rather than a complete Ren An biography or primary-text collation. His exact title, the “长安使” identification, role in the Su Jian consultation, frequency of visits, claim that he alone remained, friendship network, Sima Qian's precise evaluation, and the role of Chu Shaosun in appending biographies all require fuller textual verification. Continued contact supports a contrast with advantage-seeking patronage, but it does not reveal every private motive or establish that all other associations were insincere.
