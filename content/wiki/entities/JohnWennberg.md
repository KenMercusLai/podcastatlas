# John Wennberg

## Overview

John Wennberg is the physician-researcher in [[the-tipping-point-revisited-an-excerpt|The Tipping Point Revisited: An Excerpt]] whose mapping of medical care in Vermont anchors the episode's account of [[SmallAreaVariation]].

## Current Profile

The source says Wennberg began mapping local treatment and spending in 1967 expecting differences to track wealth, access, or illness. Instead, neighboring hospital districts showed large and stable differences in procedures such as tonsillectomy. The episode attributes much of that variation to locally shared physician judgment, making his work a bridge from health-services research to [[BoundedSocialContagion]].

## Key Characteristics

- Maps healthcare use geographically rather than assuming that national or state averages describe local practice.
- Distinguishes differences in treatment intensity from differences in underlying illness.
- Treats local clinical norms as a candidate explanation for persistent variation.

## Evidence

### Geographic treatment variation

- [[the-tipping-point-revisited-an-excerpt]] reports three- to fivefold procedure differences across service areas and large community differences in childhood tonsillectomy rates.

### Local professional norms

- [[the-tipping-point-revisited-an-excerpt]] says later work found stable geographic clusters that payment systems, patient demand, and random physician distribution did not fully explain.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to one structured episode summary rather than Wennberg's publications, datasets, institutional biography, or later research. The episode's use of his work as an analogy for social contagion is Gladwell's synthesis; geographic variation alone does not identify peer transmission, prove inappropriate care, or exclude patient mix, capacity, referral patterns, policy, measurement, and other local causes.

## What Changed

- Established Wennberg as the source's bridge between mapped healthcare variation and bounded community norms.
