# Arline Bronzaft

## Overview

Arline Bronzaft is the environmental psychologist in [[running-hot|Running Hot]] who places emergency sirens inside the broader health and learning consequences of environmental noise.

## Current Profile

Bronzaft connects intrusive noise with impaired learning, stress, poor sleep, mental-health effects, and increased heart-disease risk. She also points to less intrusive European sirens and reverses the burden of proof by asking whether quieter warning systems have been shown to cause more deaths.

Her role is narrower than the episode's EMS evidence: she establishes why noise is not a trivial inconvenience, while the crash, response-time, triage, and intervention claims come from other researchers and practitioners.

## Key Characteristics

- Environmental psychologist focused on noise as a health and learning exposure.
- Treats sleep and stress effects as part of siren policy rather than private annoyance.
- Uses international comparison to question the assumed necessity of extreme loudness.
- Separates the need for warning from the assumption that louder is always safer.

## Evidence

### Health and learning effects

- [[running-hot]] attributes to Bronzaft the links among noise, learning disruption, stress, sleep, mental health, and cardiovascular risk.

### Comparative policy question

## Qualifications

This profile rests on one structured podcast summary rather than Bronzaft's publications or a systematic review. The source does not provide study designs, effect sizes, exposure thresholds, or a controlled comparison of siren technologies.
