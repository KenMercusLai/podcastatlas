# Jean-Joseph Mounier

## Overview

Jean-Joseph Mounier was a young Grenoble judge and organizer of the [[AssemblyOfVizille|Assembly of Vizille]] whom the source presents as a possible first leader of the [[FrenchRevolution|French Revolution]].

## Current Profile

The episode presents Mounier as an upwardly mobile lawyer, cloth merchant's son, and purchased royal judge at the lower edge of nobility. After the [[DayOfTheTiles|Day of the Tiles]] displaced cautious senior judges as the drivers of events, he convened the July 1788 Assembly of Vizille. Its program moved beyond restoring a local parlement: it demanded free elections, a Dauphine Estates-General, Third Estate numbers equal to the privileged orders combined, and deliberation in a single chamber. His importance in this source lies in translating crowd-backed institutional breakdown into a program of representation and majority rule.

## Key Characteristics

- Combined middling commercial origins, legal training, purchased office, and minor noble status.
- Emerged politically when older Grenoble judges retreated from crowd mobilization.
- Converted provincial court resistance into a wider representative program.
- Demanded stronger Third Estate representation and a single deliberative chamber.
- Used patriotic and general-will language that could delegitimize opponents as enemies of the nation.
