## Overview

左悺 / Zuo Guan is a source-bounded late-Han eunuch in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-857-xuehui-gongying-cai-shi-zhenzheng-de-yingjia-lh3zx6pw-4ib7owiz8c68bv-9htn|Hanji 857]]. The episode pairs him with [[XuHuangLateHan|徐璜]] as an inner-court actor who uses [[HuangfuGuiLateHan|皇甫规]]'s expected frontier reward to solicit gifts and then turns refusal into renewed vulnerability.

## Current Profile

Hanji 857 gives Zuo Guan a narrow but important role. Huangfu Gui's frontier service should have positioned him for marquisate-level recognition, but Zuo Guan and Xu Huang repeatedly send guests to ask about his merit in a way the source reads as a request for gifts. Huangfu Gui refuses to pay or apologize.

That refusal does not remain private. The same source says the eunuch side becomes angry and revives accusations against Huangfu Gui, after which his subordinates try to settle the matter through payments and apology while Huangfu himself will not bend. Zuo Guan therefore matters less as an independently developed biography than as one named gatekeeper in the late-Han reward and accusation channel.

## Key Characteristics

- Late-Han eunuch named in a source-scoped Huan-era reward-gatekeeping case.
- Paired with Xu Huang in gift-solicitation pressure around Huangfu Gui's military merit.
- Uses intermediary guests rather than formal public accusation at the first stage.
- Helps turn refusal to pay or apologize into renewed slander and punishment exposure.
- Functions as evidence that military reward decisions can be shaped by palace-access actors.
