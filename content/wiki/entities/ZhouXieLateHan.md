## Overview

周燮 / Zhou Xie enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-799-donghan-mingchen-yangzhen-ruhe-jujue-zou-houmen-lmufb7lvb-kzsvjtn6iu-zrpiptf|Hanji 799]] as a Runan scholar recommended by [[ChenZhongLateHan|陈忠]] and summoned by [[EmperorAnOfHan|汉安帝]]. He refuses office by returning to his county and claiming illness, making him an early evidence point for [[PrincipledOfficeRefusal|原则性拒仕]] before the later Huan-era recluse cluster.

## Current Profile

Hanji 799 describes Zhou Xie as a man of learning and upright conduct with rural prestige. Chen Zhong recommends him together with [[FengLiangLateHan|冯良]], and Emperor An summons them with black silk and lambskin gifts. The source frames the summons as a genuine recognition of public reputation rather than a private-favor appointment.

Zhou Xie's refusal turns on timing. When clan members ask why a person with high virtue and conduct would remain on the eastern hillside rather than serve the state, Zhou replies that people who cultivate virtue and learning must wait for the right time; acting before the time has arrived will not work. He and Feng Liang then return to their county offices, say they are sick, and lie at home rather than take office.

## Key Characteristics

- Runan scholar known for learning, upright conduct, and rural prestige.
- Recommended through a public reputation channel rather than private palace favor.
- Refuses office despite formal imperial summons and gifts.
- Frames refusal as a judgment about timing, not rejection of all public obligation.
- Source-bounded precursor to later late-Han reclusion patterns.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 799. It does not reconstruct Zhou Xie's full family background, teaching network, later reputation, or whether his "time has not arrived" statement refers to a specific factional danger beyond the host's interpretation of An-era court sickness.
