## Overview

虚竹 / Xuzhu is a late-entering protagonist of [[TianLongBaBu|《天龙八部》]] whose compassion, honesty, and weak attachment to victory repeatedly turn accidents into life-changing outcomes. The bounded episode treats his rise from Shaolin monk to Xiaoyao leader, Lingjiu Palace master, and Western Xia prince-consort as neither pure merit nor pure luck.

## Current Profile

Xuzhu begins with a complete identification as an ordinary monk and no knowledge that [[Xuanci|玄慈]] and [[YeErniang|叶二娘]] are his parents. Once outside Shaolin, other people repeatedly move him into situations he did not seek. His agency appears less in choosing the situation than in responding to it: he intervenes to save 段延庆 at the 珍珑棋局, rescues [[TianshanTonglao|天山童姥]] when she appears to be a threatened child, and answers the Western Xia princess without constructing a winning persona.

Those choices cost him the continuity he values. He loses his Shaolin martial foundation, breaks dietary and sexual vows, receives unwanted power and office, and learns his parentage only to witness both parents die. Yet the ice-cell relationship with 梦姑 also gives him a remembered experience of joy and desire outside monastic identity. His final accommodation to secular life can therefore be read as compromised discipline, human integration, or both.

The source distinguishes woodenness from incapacity. Xuzhu has strong memory and martial comprehension, and [[TianshanTonglao|天山童姥]] rather than 无崖子 provides much of his systematic instruction. Earlier and later editions reportedly rebalance talent, awkwardness, and the happiness of his marriage, so the profile remains version-sensitive.

## Key Characteristics

- Acts from compassion even when he lacks a plan or personal stake in the outcome.
- Tells the truth when strategic self-presentation would be more advantageous.
- Has little initial desire for status, martial supremacy, wealth, or marriage.
- Learns complex martial systems despite social awkwardness and apparent woodenness.
- Experiences gains as mixed because they displace the monastic identity he actually wanted.
- Moves from rule-based renunciation toward an uneasy acceptance of desire, relationship, and secular responsibility.

## Qualifications

This profile rests on one conversational literary discussion. Xuzhu's inner motives, degree of consent, martial aptitude, philosophical symbolism, and final happiness remain interpretive and edition-dependent. The ice-cell relationship is engineered through deception and coercion by Tonglao; later desire does not make its initiation ethically uncomplicated. Compassion and honesty help explain how Xuzhu responds to chance, but they do not make every reward deserved or every outcome predictable.

## What Changed

- Created a profile separating contingent opportunity from the dispositions that shape Xuzhu's response.
- Made unwanted gain and loss of monastic identity part of the fortune judgment.
- Added desire and secular attachment as both ethical complication and possible human integration.
