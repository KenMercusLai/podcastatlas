# Temple University

## Overview

Temple University appears in the bounded sources as both [[LauraMcGrath]]'s academic affiliation for bestseller-list research and the institutional setting of an adjudicated business-school ranking fraud case involving [[MoshePorat]].

## Current Profile

Temple's bounded profile has two distinct ranking branches. Its academic branch comes through [[LauraMcGrath]], whose affiliation anchors [[how-to-make-a-book-into-a-bestseller]]'s explanation of the [[NewYorkTimesBestsellerList|New York Times bestseller list]] as an opaque cultural and economic institution.

Its accountability branch concerns the online business school's reported false claim of universal GMAT participation, first-place ranking, and almost $40 million in additional tuition. [[the-pushkin-prize-for-egregiously-deceptive-self-promotion-the-pushkin-prize-for-egregiously-deceptive-self-promotion]] pairs that misconduct with an outside investigation, compensation for affected students, and referral of former dean Moshe Porat to authorities. Those actions make Temple a contrast with Columbia's response without making the underlying cases equivalent.

## Key Characteristics

- Provides academic context for research on bestseller-list history and opacity.
- Is the institutional setting of a reported and legally adjudicated ranking-data fraud case.
- Responded in the episode's account through external investigation, compensation, and law-enforcement referral.
- Shows why misconduct and post-discovery institutional response should be evaluated separately.

## Qualifications

The ranking case is summarized by one podcast source that does not reproduce the court record, investigation, settlement, financial analysis, or Temple's complete response. It should not be generalized to Temple as a whole or treated as equivalent to Columbia's unresolved responsibility and intent.
