## Overview

田畴 / Tian Chou is a late-Han frontier figure whose current profile joins loyal risk-taking, local order-building, campaign route expertise, and principled refusal of earned reward. He first appears as [[LiuYuLateHan|刘虞]]'s covert envoy and later helps [[CaoCao|曹操]] approach [[Wuhuan|乌桓]] through [[LulongRoadLateHan|卢龙道]], but refuses to turn that contribution into a marquisate.

## Current Profile

Hanji 972 introduces Tian Chou as a young [[YoubeipingCommandery|右北平]] talent trusted with Liu Yu's secret memorial to [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]]. He rejects visible envoy display, travels with twenty riders through dangerous frontier routes, and refuses a qiduwei appointment because accepting office while the emperor is displaced and humiliated would violate the meaning of his mission. After Liu Yu's death, he reads the court reply at the tomb, rebukes [[GongsunZan|公孙瓒]], and builds a law-, rite-, farming-, and schooling-based settlement at [[XuWuMountain|徐无山]].

Hanji 1110 extends this profile from loyal non-service and local governance into strategic cooperation with Cao Cao. Tian Chou's Lulong Road advice contributes to victory over Wuhuan, yet he repeatedly refuses the resulting tinghou title and five-hundred-household fief because he regards the advice as service to the state rather than a bid for rank. Even Xiahou Dun's persuasion fails; after Tian Chou threatens death, Cao Cao changes the recognition to yilang. Across both sources, refusal is selective rather than passive: Tian Chou will carry a dangerous mission, organize a frontier community, and give decisive military advice, but resists offices or rewards he judges inconsistent with the purpose of those acts.

## Key Characteristics

- Loyal mission execution under frontier danger and weak central authority.
- Direct moral courage toward Gongsun Zan after Liu Yu's killing.
- Practical local-order builder using farming, law, rites, education, and frontier relations.
- Route expert whose Lulong Road advice materially assists Cao Cao's Wuhuan campaign.
- Selective refusal of office and earned reward when acceptance would distort the meaning of service.

## Qualifications

The sources emphasize different moments and do not supply a complete biography. Hanji 972's Tian Chou display form was previously written “田筹” in the wiki; Hanji 1110 supports normalizing it to the received “田畴” without changing the stable route. Hanji 1110's exact reward sequence, office interpretation, move to Ye, and age at death remain source-scoped, and the episode's modern management language should not replace the late-Han political context.

## What Changed

- Normalized the display name from “田筹” to the received “田畴” while preserving the stable page identity.
- Added Lulong Road route expertise and contribution to Cao Cao's Wuhuan victory.
- Reframed the two refusal episodes as selective service boundaries rather than generalized withdrawal.
- Added the four-order marquisate refusal and yilang compromise.
