## Overview

田畴 / Tian Chou is a late-Han frontier figure whose current profile joins loyal risk-taking, local order-building, campaign route expertise, and principled refusal of earned reward. He first appears as [[LiuYuLateHan|刘虞]]'s covert envoy and later helps [[CaoCao|曹操]] approach [[Wuhuan|乌桓]] through [[LulongRoadLateHan|卢龙道]], but refuses to turn that contribution into a marquisate.

## Current Profile

Hanji 972 introduces Tian Chou as a young [[YoubeipingCommandery|右北平]] talent trusted with Liu Yu's secret memorial to [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]]. He rejects visible envoy display, travels with twenty riders through dangerous frontier routes, and refuses a qiduwei appointment because accepting office while the emperor is displaced and humiliated would violate the meaning of his mission. After Liu Yu's death, he reads the court reply at the tomb, rebukes [[GongsunZan|公孙瓒]], and builds a law-, rite-, farming-, and schooling-based settlement at [[XuWuMountain|徐无山]].

Hanji 1078 supplies the campaign mechanics that Hanji 1110 only recalls. Wuhuan raids against Youbeiping give Tian Chou a practical reason to cooperate with Cao Cao after refusing Yuan Shao; when rain and a guarded coastal road stop the army, he proposes a feigned withdrawal and the long-disused Lulong Road. He then guides the force through Xu Wu Mountain and a difficult road-opening march toward the Wuhuan rear. Hanji 1110 extends the sequence into reward politics: Tian Chou repeatedly refuses the resulting tinghou title and five-hundred-household fief because he regards the advice as service to the state rather than a bid for rank. Even Xiahou Dun's persuasion fails; after Tian Chou threatens death, Cao Cao changes the recognition to yilang.

Across the three sources, refusal is selective rather than passive. Tian Chou will carry a dangerous mission, organize a frontier community, choose the patron capable of meeting a frontier threat, and give decisive military advice, but resists offices or rewards he judges inconsistent with the purpose of those acts.

## Key Characteristics

- Loyal mission execution under frontier danger and weak central authority.
- Direct moral courage toward Gongsun Zan after Liu Yu's killing.
- Practical local-order builder using farming, law, rites, education, and frontier relations.
- Selective coalition partner who links service to the capacity to protect Youbeiping from Wuhuan raids.
- Route expert whose feigned-withdrawal and Lulong Road advice materially assists Cao Cao's Wuhuan campaign.
- Selective refusal of office and earned reward when acceptance would distort the meaning of service.

## Qualifications

The sources emphasize different moments and do not supply a complete biography. Hanji 972's Tian Chou display form was previously written “田筹” in the wiki; Hanji 1110 supports normalizing it to the received “田畴” without changing the stable route. Hanji 1078's motive reconstruction, detailed dialogue, route distances, engineering scale, and claim that only Cao Cao could defeat Wuhuan remain source-scoped. Hanji 1110's exact reward sequence, office interpretation, move to Ye, and age at death also remain bounded to that episode.

## What Changed

- Added the home-region and anti-Wuhuan logic behind Tian Chou's choice to cooperate with Cao Cao after refusing Yuan Shao.
- Expanded Lulong Road merit from a retrospective attribution into the feigned-withdrawal, route-selection, and guide sequence.
- Strengthened the synthesis of selective service: capacity and public purpose, not generalized withdrawal, govern Tian Chou's choices.
