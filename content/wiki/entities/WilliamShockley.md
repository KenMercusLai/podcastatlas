## Overview

William Shockley was a physicist and semiconductor entrepreneur whose scientific prestige helped bring a consequential group of researchers to Mountain View, while his management failures helped disperse them into new firms.

## Current Profile

[[silicon-valley-on-the-couch]] presents Shockley as both a powerful technical attractor and an organizational hazard. His Bell Labs transistor work, wartime consulting, rapid problem solving, and interest in silicon made him credible enough to recruit exceptional scientists to an unlikely location. The same source describes severe suspicion, coercive employee testing, grandiosity, and destructive conflict that culminated in the departure of the [[TraitorousEight]].

The episode also refuses a sanitized genius story. It records Shockley's later racist eugenic campaign, family breakdown, Russian roulette and a suicide note, and increasing isolation. Its claims about bipolar disorder and his unconscious need for maternal proximity remain retrospective hypotheses, not established diagnoses or motives.

## Key Characteristics

- Combined elite scientific ability with strong practical and recruiting influence.
- Chose Mountain View despite stronger apparent institutional alternatives elsewhere.
- Pursued silicon semiconductor devices while much contemporary work used germanium.
- Turned personal suspicion into coercive management practices that damaged trust and retention.
- Helped assemble the talent base whose departure accelerated regional company formation.
- Publicly advocated racist eugenic policies later in life.

## Evidence

- Scientific and recruiting force - [[silicon-valley-on-the-couch]] connects Shockley's Bell Labs transistor work, wartime consulting, Nobel Prize, problem solving, and direct recruitment to the laboratory's initial talent concentration.
- Technical direction - [[silicon-valley-on-the-couch]] says his Mountain View laboratory pursued silicon when many researchers still used germanium.
- Organizational damage - [[silicon-valley-on-the-couch]] describes extensive psychological screening, the broken-thumbtack sabotage episode, attempted lie-detector testing, and the coordinated departure of eight leading employees.
- Public extremism and private crisis - [[silicon-valley-on-the-couch]] reports his eugenic advocacy, first-marriage collapse, Russian roulette, suicide note, and later isolation.

## Qualifications

The source is a structured episode summary, not a comprehensive biography. The proposed bipolar diagnosis was made retrospectively without an examination, and proximity to [[MayBradfordShockley]] does not prove why Shockley selected Mountain View. Wartime impact, private motives, remembered behavior, and causal credit for Silicon Valley remain source-attributed.
