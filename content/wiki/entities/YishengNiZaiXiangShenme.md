# 《医生，你在想什么》

## Overview
《医生，你在想什么》 is [[WangXingDoctor|王兴医生]]'s medical-popularization book about clinicians' reasoning, concerns, behavior, and institutional context from the patient's side of the encounter.

## Current Profile
The 蜜獾吃书 episode presents the book as a practical bridge: it explains why doctors structure history-taking, interrupt to clarify a diagnostic thread, hesitate in remote advice, rely on treatment feedback, protect the integrity of medical records, and use tests, consultation, and review to reduce rather than eliminate error.

VOL.110 confirms that the book is not primarily a manual for registration, insurance, or tactical hospital navigation. Its distinctive role is to reveal what clinicians need, what they do not want, and what risks or rules shape their behavior. Read together, the sources support cooperation without medical worship: patients can prepare useful information, ask questions, compare qualified opinions, and make goals visible while accepting that medicine remains uncertain.

## Key Characteristics
- Translates clinical reasoning and hospital behavior into ordinary language.
- Helps patients prepare symptoms, duration, history, allergies, tests, and treatment feedback.
- Explains why online or casual medical advice can be brief and conservative.
- Treats consultation, review, tests, records, and second opinions as error-reduction tools rather than guarantees.
- Rejects both blind obedience and adversarial self-diagnosis from fragmented search results.
- Adds patient goals and acceptable burden to the comparison of treatment plans.

## Evidence
- Diagnostic and communication framework: [[70-yisheng-ni-zai-xiang-shenme-shao-kan-baidu-yiji-chiyao-shi-bie-chi-xiyou-a-668238394]] uses the book to explain visit preparation, structured questioning, uncertainty, medical records, feedback, interactions, screening, and second opinions.
- Authorial purpose: [[vol-110-shanzhong-shi-weile-genghao-de-huozhe-shenglaobingsi-6-wei-yisheng-shijiao-zenme-kan-shijie-dushuri-lklwhndplyk73rpqxuraocs4cii5]] has Wang describe the book as an account of clinicians' logic, concerns, and hospital rules rather than only procedural tips.
- Remote and preference-sensitive care: [[vol-110-shanzhong-shi-weile-genghao-de-huozhe-shenglaobingsi-6-wei-yisheng-shijiao-zenme-kan-shijie-dushuri-lklwhndplyk73rpqxuraocs4cii5]] connects incomplete-information caution with the patient's pain, cost, reassurance, and treatment preferences.

## Qualifications
The wiki derives this profile from two podcast discussions rather than a full independent textual analysis of the book. Concrete examples remain public medical literacy, not individualized diagnostic, medication, online-consultation, screening, or referral advice.
