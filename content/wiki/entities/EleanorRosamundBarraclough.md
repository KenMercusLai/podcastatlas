## Overview

Eleanor Rosamund Barraclough is a scholar and author whose work connects Viking travel, Old Norse literature, landscape, and the historical interpretation of medieval Icelandic storytelling.

## Current Profile

In [[103-the-norse-sagas-glt8280648151]], Barraclough guides a wide account of Icelandic settlement, Greenland and Vinland travel, saga genres, Christianisation, the Eddas, women, oral performance, and 13th-century politics. Her recurring contribution is evidentiary calibration: archaeology, manuscripts, genetics, landscape, and literary form can illuminate one another, but no single layer makes the sagas transparent records of the Viking Age.

## Key Characteristics

- Interprets Viking mobility through both material evidence and Old Norse narrative.
- Treats sagas as mediated historical evidence rather than either chronicle or fantasy.
- Connects literary transmission to Christianisation and 13th-century Icelandic politics.
- Uses landscape, domestic society, and women characters to widen Viking history beyond raiding and kingship.

## Evidence

- Evidentiary method: [[103-the-norse-sagas-glt8280648151]] compares saga claims with volcanic dating, archaeology, genetics, runes, picture stones, and manuscript history.
- Literary history: [[103-the-norse-sagas-glt8280648151]] distinguishes saga genres from the Poetic and Prose Eddas and emphasizes oral-to-written transmission.
- Social range: [[103-the-norse-sagas-glt8280648151]] discusses farms, law speaking, winter storytelling, feud, Gudrid, Freydis, and household structure.
