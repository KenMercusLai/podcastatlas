# Hephaestion

## Overview

Hephaestion appears as [[AlexanderTheGreat|Alexander the Great's]] close companion in the famous, uncertain story of the captured Persian royal household after the [[BattleOfIssus]].

## Current Profile

The episode recounts the tradition that [[DariusIII|Darius III's]] mother mistook the taller Hephaestion for Alexander and that Alexander excused her by saying Hephaestion was also Alexander. The scene condenses intimacy, aristocratic courtesy, and political theater: the conqueror protects the royal women, preserves their status, and presents generosity as part of victory.

## Key Characteristics

- Close companion publicly associated with Alexander.
- Participant in a royal-family anecdote central to the “good Alexander” tradition.
- His resemblance in status to the king becomes part of a story about intimacy and magnanimity.

## Evidence

- Issus aftermath: [[dchha74-mania-for-subjugation-iv-dchha74-mania-for-subjugation-iv]] recounts the mistaken-identity scene while warning that ancient authors themselves questioned its truth.

## Qualifications

The episode does not independently establish the anecdote. Its survival and polished moral structure make it evidence for Alexander's favorable tradition as much as for the event itself.
