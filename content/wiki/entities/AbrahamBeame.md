# Abraham Beame

## Overview

Abraham Beame was mayor of New York City during the mid-1970s fiscal crisis and the 1977 blackout.

## Current Profile

In [[360-fear-city-new-york-in-the-1970s-glt7107047696]], Beame inherits a city already borrowing heavily to meet operating costs. State and federal assistance eventually prevents default, but the rescue is paired with mass public-sector layoffs that reduce teaching, hospital, police, and fire capacity. His administration also grants the long tax concession attached to [[DonaldTrump|Donald Trump]]'s Commodore Hotel redevelopment, linking emergency-era fiscal weakness to the next decade's private-development model.

## Key Characteristics

- He entered office in 1974 amid deep borrowing and budget pressure.
- His administration sought state and federal help to prevent municipal collapse.
- Fiscal retrenchment included large layoffs across essential city services.
- The resulting service cuts intensified public fear and union conflict.
- His administration approved a major tax concession for the Commodore Hotel redevelopment.

## Evidence

- Fiscal emergency: [[360-fear-city-new-york-in-the-1970s-glt7107047696]] says New York borrowed at high rates to pay workers and needed external assistance by 1975.
- Austerity: [[360-fear-city-new-york-in-the-1970s-glt7107047696]] reports layoffs across education, hospitals, policing, and firefighting.
- Redevelopment: [[360-fear-city-new-york-in-the-1970s-glt7107047696]] attributes a 40-year Commodore Hotel tax break to the Beame administration.
