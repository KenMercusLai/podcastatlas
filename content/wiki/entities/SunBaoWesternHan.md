## Overview

孙宝 / Sun Bao enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-611-lishi-wuming-de-sunbao-you-duo-yemen-lhlc5v3ndnivv9j_cb9w65v4xutw|Hanji 611]] as a late [[WesternHanDynasty|Western Han]] Grand Minister of Agriculture whose public objection to praise of [[WangMang|王莽]] exposes the danger of truth-telling inside an omen-heavy court.

## Current Profile

Hanji 611 presents Sun Bao as a rare direct-speaking official. When ministers use a reported yellow dragon to praise Wang Mang's virtue and compare him to the Duke of Zhou, Sun Bao refuses the consensus frame. He argues that Zhougong and Shaogong could disagree without losing moral standing, then points to the concrete condition of the Han state: treasury resources are depleted and common people lack clothing and food.

The source's profile is not that Sun Bao leads a faction or blocks Wang Mang. His importance is diagnostic. By forcing the court to acknowledge the gap between auspicious praise and public hardship, he makes the praise environment visible. The aftermath then shows the cost: [[ChenChongXinOfficial|陈崇]] impeaches him over alleged failure to support his mother after she falls ill during relocation, and Sun Bao accepts guilt because he reads the political context as unwinnable. He is removed and later dies at home.

## Key Characteristics

- Late-Western-Han Grand Minister of Agriculture active under [[HanPingdi|汉平帝]].
- Direct-speaking official who publicly resists court praise of Wang Mang.
- Uses classical comparison to argue that disagreement need not destroy virtue.
- Shifts debate from auspicious signs to fiscal depletion and commoner hardship.
- Becomes exposed after his speech and is removed through a filial-duty impeachment.
- Source-bounded figure whose current wiki profile rests on one focused episode.

## Qualifications

The source does not reconstruct Sun Bao's full career, factional ties, earlier policy record, or private intention. The episode strongly implies political retaliation after direct speech, but the formal charge concerns filial support; the wiki records the retaliation reading as source-backed interpretation rather than independent proof of Chen Chong's private motive.
