## Overview

叶明琛 / Ye Mingchen (1809–1859) was the Qing governor-general of Guangdong and Guangxi captured after Guangzhou fell to British and French forces and sent to Calcutta, where he died in confinement.

## Current Profile

The source rejects reduction of Ye to the famous “不战、不和、不守，不死、不降、不走” verdict. He appears as a rapidly promoted, personally austere official with real administrative capacity: he organized militia, identity controls, intelligence, maritime food supply, and concentrated force to survive the Red Turban siege of Guangzhou. That victory, however, included mass execution and left forces dispersed across Guangdong.

Earlier British retreats and repeated false intelligence then strengthened Ye's belief that firmness would again prevail. He underestimated British capacity and resolve during the [[SecondOpiumWar|Second Opium War]], became isolated as subordinates moved toward negotiation, refused to flee Guangzhou, and was captured. British officials removed him to India because his continuing prestige could obstruct occupation; accounts of his death by illness, acclimatization failure, or deliberate starvation remain unresolved.

## Key Characteristics

- Qing scholar-official whose early promotion and crisis administration complicate the stereotype of pure incompetence.
- Organizer who combined militia, provisioning, surveillance, and limited regular forces during the Red Turban revolt.
- Repressor associated by the episode with executions on a very large scale.
- Decision-maker whose successful local experience and confirmation-seeking intelligence fed [[ColonialPowerMiscalculation]].
- Captive who maintained official dress and ritual bearing after practical authority disappeared.
- Historical reputation reshaped by hostile “six noes,” loyalist memory, nationalism, and disputed poems.

## Qualifications

This profile rests on one comparative podcast summary. It neither converts structural military imbalance into personal innocence nor lets Ye's competence erase responsibility for brutal repression and strategic failure. Execution totals, private motives, intelligence pathways, British impressions, the Arrow incident, death causation, and poem authorship require primary and specialist comparison.

## What Changed

- Created a layered profile separating administrative capacity, coercive violence, strategic misjudgment, captivity, and contested memory.
