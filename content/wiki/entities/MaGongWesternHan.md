## Overview

马公 / Ma Gong enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-624-du-lishi-wu-xingfu-ni-shi-rensheng-yingjia-haishi-yongren-zirao-daibiao-lpavicltc7nducbxiqgpr6t7iczd|Hanji 624]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-626-waren-fenmu-wangmang-jing-ruci-sangxinbingkuang-lotwk6rbtl5pvbrnlfrysu5ol6rz|Hanji 626]] as a Western Han high official whose late rise and later exposure both depend on [[WangMang|王莽]]'s court position. He is appointed Taishi after [[KongGuangWesternHan|Kong Guang]] dies, then becomes dangerous to Wang Mang's retrospective Fu Taihou title prosecution because he had participated in the earlier settlement.

## Current Profile

Hanji 624 says Ma Gong is appointed Taishi after Kong Guang's death in 5 CE. In that source, the appointment is part of the high-office background around Wang Mang's Nine Bestowments sequence rather than a biographical focus.

Hanji 626 then supplies the reversal. Ma Gong had served as Guanglu xun in the [[HanAidi|Han Aidi]] period and joined the chancellor and censor in settling [[FuTaihouWesternHan|Fu Taihou]]'s posthumous title. When Wang Mang later prosecutes people tied to that decision, Ma Gong is exposed. Because he has a close private relationship with Wang Mang, he avoids death or severe ruin, but he becomes fearful and ashamed enough to request surrender of title, income, and seals.

Wang Mang's reply keeps Ma Gong's marquis rank and income while requiring him to return the seals and ribbons of Taishi and Da Situ and go home as a marquis. The profile is therefore neither exoneration nor full purge: Ma Gong loses high office but not noble survival.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han high official implicated by an earlier Fu Taihou title decision.
- Taishi appointee after Kong Guang's death in 5 CE.
- Protected in part by personal closeness to Wang Mang.
- Self-accusing petitioner who tries to surrender title, income, and high-office symbols.
- Case of removal from top office without loss of marquis status.

## Qualifications

The sources do not settle Ma Gong's exact responsibility for the earlier title decision, the full list of other officials punished, or whether his self-accusation was voluntary conscience, survival strategy, or both. Hanji 624 also does not explain why he is chosen to succeed Kong Guang. The wiki records both appointment and limited removal as source-backed while keeping inner motive source-scoped.
