# Fredegund

## Overview

Fredegund was a sixth-century Frankish queen who rose from likely servile origins to become Chilperic's wife, queen mother, and regent of Neustria, sustaining a decades-long dynastic struggle with [[Brunhilde]].

## Current Profile

[[521-warlords-of-the-west-killer-queens-part-2-glt5666966864]] presents Fredegund's ascent as unusually insecure. She entered Chilperic's household, became his leading concubine, and married him soon after the strangling of his Visigothic wife Galswintha. Her dependence on royal favor did not prevent political agency: the episode attributes to her acute observation, information gathering, alliance management, intimidation, and a readiness to use covert violence.

Her later position depended increasingly on motherhood and survival. Child deaths intensified the succession struggle; after Chilperic's murder in 584, she appealed to Guntram for protection of her infant son Clothar and governed Neustria as regent. She also appears as a military actor in the episode's account of Doisy, where disguise, surprise, and a smaller Neustrian force defeated an Austrasian army.

Fredegund's reputation cannot be detached from [[GregoryOfTours|Gregory of Tours]]. He links her to assassination, torture, witchcraft accusations, and household murder, yet motive and political benefit do not independently prove her responsibility for every death. The episode therefore retains both her demonstrable political capacity and the uncertainty surrounding its most sensational stories.

## Key Characteristics

- Queen who rose from likely servile household status rather than foreign royal birth.
- Political survivor skilled in information, patronage, tactical submission, intimidation, and covert action.
- Dynastic mother whose authority and security depended on producing and protecting an heir.
- Regent who governed Neustria after Chilperic's death.
- War leader credited with a surprise victory at Doisy.
- Historical figure whose violent reputation is heavily mediated by a hostile principal narrator.

## Evidence

- Ascent and insecurity - [[521-warlords-of-the-west-killer-queens-part-2-glt5666966864]] traces her movement from household service to concubinage and queenship after Galswintha's murder.
- Dynastic survival - [[521-warlords-of-the-west-killer-queens-part-2-glt5666966864]] connects child deaths, rival heirs, Chilperic's murder, and the appeal to Guntram with her regency for Clothar.
- Political and military capacity - [[521-warlords-of-the-west-killer-queens-part-2-glt5666966864]] describes revolt-incitement, coercive order-making, and the surprise tactics used at Doisy.
- Hostile-source reputation - [[521-warlords-of-the-west-killer-queens-part-2-glt5666966864]] repeatedly distinguishes Gregory's accusations and common suspicion from direct proof.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to one narrative podcast summary and its treatment of Gregory of Tours. Galswintha's murder, Sigebert's assassination, Bishop Praetextatus's killing, Chilperic's death, alleged witchcraft, reported speeches, and banquet or battlefield details vary in evidentiary security. Rejecting automatic acceptance of hostile accusations does not establish Fredegund's innocence or erase the violence conducted under her authority.

## What Changed

- Established Fredegund as a regent and military-political actor rather than only a legendary murderess.
- Made the dependence of her authority on royal favor, maternity, guardianship, and aristocratic support explicit.
- Separated documented political outcomes from Gregory's accusations and later inference.
