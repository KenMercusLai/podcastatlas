# Antipater

## Overview

Antipater appears as a senior [[PhilipIIMacedon|Philip II]]-era commander whom [[AlexanderTheGreat|Alexander the Great]] consulted before leaving him to guard Macedonia and manage the risk of renewed Greek resistance.

## Current Profile

During the winter of 335-334 BCE, Antipater and [[Parmenion]] reportedly advised Alexander to marry and secure an heir before invading the [[AchaemenidEmpire]]. Alexander refused to postpone. Antipater then remained in Europe with a substantial force because Macedonian control over the subordinated Greek states could not be assumed to survive the king's absence unaided.

The profile therefore connects dynastic continuity to strategic depth. The Asian expedition depended not only on the invasion army but also on a trusted senior commander, retained troops, and coercive capacity behind it.

## Key Characteristics

- Senior commander inherited from Philip II's regime.
- Associated with advice to secure dynastic succession before a high-risk invasion.
- Left in Europe while Alexander campaigned in Asia.
- Anchors rear-area security against possible Greek revolt.

## Evidence

- Dynastic counsel: [[dchha73-mania-for-subjugation-iii-dchha73-mania-for-subjugation-iii]] attributes the marriage-and-heir recommendation to Antipater and Parmenion.
- European command: [[dchha73-mania-for-subjugation-iii-dchha73-mania-for-subjugation-iii]] connects his retained force to Alexander's distrust of Greek political stability.

## Qualifications

The source provides a campaign-level role rather than a full biography. The exact deliberations, wording, force retained in Europe, distribution of authority, and Antipater's independent reasoning remain source-scoped.
