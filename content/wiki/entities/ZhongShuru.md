## Overview

钟淑如 / Zhong Shuru is an anthropologist represented in the wiki through long-term research on Chinese wet markets, seafood supply chains, food knowledge, ecological agriculture, and the relationship between rural production and urban consumption.

## Current Profile

The bounded source presents Zhong as a participant observer rather than a distant commentator. Beginning with Hainan fieldwork in 2014, she moved from unsuccessful customer interviews into unpaid fish-stall work, accompanied vendors to docks, and followed how catch, price, preservation, credit, reputation, season, and customer knowledge shape daily trade.

Her analysis treats the wet market as a food system rather than a nostalgic place type. Smallholders and small-scale fishers need flexible routes to market; vendors classify and translate irregular supply; consumers need enough sensory and cooking knowledge to recognize value; and managers determine whether these relationships can remain embedded in neighborhood life. Her later work extends that inquiry toward ecological agriculture, compost, soil, and food origins.

## Key Characteristics

- Anthropologist using long-duration participant observation in markets and supply chains.
- Researcher connecting food retail to agriculture, fishing, urban planning, labor, trust, and local culture.
- Analyst of freshness as a plural standard rather than a synonym for shelf appearance or cold-chain control.
- Critic of both romanticizing wet markets and reducing them to disorder or inefficiency.
- Advocate for community-specific management involving vendors and everyday users.

## Qualifications

The profile is based on one edited episode summary rather than Zhong's dissertation, publications, full interview transcript, or independent biography. Dates, market counts, institutional examples, and claims about consumer generations or platform effects remain source-scoped. Her analysis does not establish that every wet market has the same supply structure or that direct trade is automatically safer, fairer, or more sustainable.
