# David Floyd

## Overview

David Floyd is the named plaintiff whose Stop-and-Frisk experience and federal class action anchor [[MalcolmGladwell]]'s reassessment of [[BrokenWindowsPolicing]].

## Current Profile

The episode says police stopped and frisked Floyd in 2008 while he helped a neighbor unlock an apartment. His account makes the cost of suspicion concrete: the encounter was humiliating and frightening and carried the possibility of losing freedom despite an ordinary explanation for his presence.

Floyd later became the named plaintiff in the class action that produced a 2013 federal ruling against the NYPD's use of Stop and Frisk at its prior scale. The episode treats the sharp post-ruling reduction in stops and continued homicide decline as a test of the prediction that mass stops were necessary for public safety.

## Key Characteristics

- Named plaintiff in a federal challenge to NYPD Stop-and-Frisk practices.
- Connects aggregate policy debate to the dignity and liberty costs of an intrusive encounter.
- Supplies the institutional turning point for Gladwell's natural-experiment argument.
