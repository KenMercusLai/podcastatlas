## Overview

Liu An / 刘安 is a Western Han prince whose current wiki profile holds together an intellectual afterlife as patron of the [[Huainanzi|《淮南子》]] circle and a political memory as a defeated royal conspirator. The rebellion case now includes rivalry with his brother [[LiuCiHengshanKingWesternHan|刘赐]], insider denunciation by [[LeiBeiWesternHan|雷被]] and [[WuBeiWesternHan|伍被]], and a downstream investigation that spreads risk to other royal relatives.

## Current Profile

[[87-guonian-zui-shihe-liao-xuanxue-tantao-shenxian-fangshu-fazhanshi-wei-wan-daixu-704051997|Mihuan Chishu 87]] presents Liu An's fangshu interest as textual and encyclopedic rather than only miracle seeking. His circle gathers philosophy, astronomy, calendars, technical lore, and alchemical recipes, while later legend turns the politically defeated prince into an immortal figure.

The political layer remains distinct from that intellectual legacy. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-304-2-yin-tiaocao-yinfa-de-zhuhouwang-moufan-lnav5gprm-mmsbbugeflghixplf2|Hanji 304-2]] says Liu An and Liu Ci are hostile brothers and that news of Liu An's preparations leads Liu Ci to recruit independently rather than report him. Once Liu An's plot is exposed, investigation and testimony spread to [[LiuJiJiaodongKingWesternHan|胶东王刘寄]], showing how one royal rebellion case widens through knowledge, non-reporting, and suspected preparation. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-316-1-ta-gong-yue-bai-wang-weihe-zhi-xia-baixing-yuelaiyue-qiong-lu_tr4m36rmz9sx38j4nvlmof5am|Hanji 316-1]]

The internal disclosure layer further separates early denunciation from late confession. Lei Bei reports Liu An and crown prince [[LiuQianHuainanCrownPrinceWesternHan|刘迁]] early enough to survive, while Wu Bei advises the prince before confessing after Liu Qian's arrest. The episode uses Liu An's association with a `Huainanzi` warning against desire and fame to frame a knowledge-action contradiction, but neither that moral contrast nor the host's reconstruction of the retainers' motives substitutes for a complete rebellion dossier. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-304-1-gudai-ruhe-zuo-shuangmian-jiandie-lu05uaxp5b_buvl5go2bn7iy9q_x|Hanji 304-1]]

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han prince associated with a large knowledge-compilation circle.
- Patron figure linked to philosophy, astronomy, calendars, technical lore, fangshu, and alchemy.
- Political loser under a rebellion charge whose case spread risk to other royal relatives.
- Rival elder brother whose alleged preparations prompt parallel recruitment by Liu Ci in Hanji 304-2.
- Prince whose intimate circle produces both early denunciation and late confession before the case's terminal punishment phase.
- Later immortalization figure in religious and folk storytelling.

## Evidence

Knowledge compilation and legend:
- [[87-guonian-zui-shihe-liao-xuanxue-tantao-shenxian-fangshu-fazhanshi-wei-wan-daixu-704051997|Mihuan Chishu 87]] connects Liu An's circle to the Huainanzi's mixed philosophical, technical, astronomical, and alchemical material and to later ascent legends.

Rebellion-case aftershock:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-304-1-gudai-ruhe-zuo-shuangmian-jiandie-lu05uaxp5b_buvl5go2bn7iy9q_x|Hanji 304-1]] says Lei Bei reports Liu An and Liu Qian, while Wu Bei later discloses the conspiracy after having advised it; the source keeps its explanation of Wu Bei's strategy explicitly conjectural.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-304-2-yin-tiaocao-yinfa-de-zhuhouwang-moufan-lnav5gprm-mmsbbugeflghixplf2|Hanji 304-2]] says Liu Ci learns of Liu An's preparations and begins recruiting because he fears absorption rather than reporting the plan.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-316-1-ta-gong-yue-bai-wang-weihe-zhi-xia-baixing-yuelaiyue-qiong-lu_tr4m36rmz9sx38j4nvlmof5am|Hanji 316-1]] says exposure of Liu An's conspiracy led to investigation and testimony implicating Liu Ji.

## Qualifications

The current sources do not reconstruct Liu An's full biography, the composition and authorship layers of the Huainanzi, the complete rebellion case, or the evidentiary and legal procedure used against implicated kings and retainers. The immortality material is later legend; Hanji 304-1 uses a Huainanzi maxim as moral framing and explicitly treats Wu Bei's double-agent motive as conjecture, Hanji 304-2 supplies Liu Ci's perspective on the alleged preparations and brotherly hostility, and Hanji 316-1 supplies only the case's downstream effect on Liu Ji.

## What Changed

- Added the internal disclosure sequence through Lei Bei's early denunciation and Wu Bei's late confession.
- Distinguished the episode's knowledge-action moral contrast and double-agent interpretation from established case facts.
