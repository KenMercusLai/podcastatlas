## Overview

Liu An / 刘安 is a Western Han prince whose current wiki profile joins an intellectual afterlife as patron of the [[Huainanzi|《淮南子》]] circle to a political memory as a defeated royal conspirator. The rebellion case now has a continuous source-scoped chain: weak support and proliferating schemes, failed internal mobilization, insider disclosure, suicide before arrest, household punishment, abolition of Huainan, and investigation spreading to other royal relatives.

## Current Profile

[[87-guonian-zui-shihe-liao-xuanxue-tantao-shenxian-fangshu-fazhanshi-wei-wan-daixu-704051997|Mihuan Chishu 87]] presents Liu An's circle as a knowledge-compilation world spanning philosophy, astronomy, calendars, technical lore, fangshu, and alchemy. Later storytelling converts the politically defeated prince into an immortal figure, so textual patronage, political catastrophe, and religious memory remain related but distinct layers.

The political layer begins from a poor strategic base. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-303-1-huainanwang-ruhe-bei-jiujiexin-bisi-de-ljtqn-nrxyf8yqx-yowsainkzmjc|Hanji 303-1]] says [[WuBeiWesternHan|伍被]] saw neither princely support nor popular grievance and rated even a manufactured-crisis scheme at only one chance in ten. Liu An allegedly multiplied alternatives—false orders and seals, assassination, palace fire, a fabricated border emergency, and an official roundup—without forming a viable coalition or a settled operation. When only the kingdom chancellor answered the final summons, Liu An let him leave rather than begin an unsupported rising.

Disclosure then overtakes planning. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-304-1-gudai-ruhe-zuo-shuangmian-jiandie-lu05uaxp5b_buvl5go2bn7iy9q_x|Hanji 304-1]] contrasts [[LeiBeiWesternHan|雷被]]'s early denunciation with Wu Bei's late confession after crown prince [[LiuQianHuainanCrownPrinceWesternHan|刘迁]] becomes vulnerable to arrest. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-303-2-saiweng-shima-de-zuozhe-shi-zenme-si-de-lp9u6yu1la2w6sfqwqfikcvgq9pt|Hanji 303-2]] supplies the terminal phase: arrests and a wide inquiry follow, Liu An dies before the imperial arrest mission arrives, his household is punished, and Huainan is converted into Jiujiang Commandery.

The aftershock extends beyond his own kingdom. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-304-2-yin-tiaocao-yinfa-de-zhuhouwang-moufan-lnav5gprm-mmsbbugeflghixplf2|Hanji 304-2]] says hostile brother [[LiuCiHengshanKingWesternHan|刘赐]] recruits independently after learning of Liu An's preparation, while [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-316-1-ta-gong-yue-bai-wang-weihe-zhi-xia-baixing-yuelaiyue-qiong-lu_tr4m36rmz9sx38j4nvlmof5am|Hanji 316-1]] says the exposed case reaches [[LiuJiJiaodongKingWesternHan|胶东王刘寄]]. The current synthesis therefore treats Liu An's failure as both operational and networked: the scheme lacked a workable base, yet its evidence created escalating danger for his household and royal kin.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han prince associated with a large knowledge-compilation circle.
- Patron figure linked to philosophy, astronomy, calendars, technical lore, fangshu, and alchemy.
- Conspirator whose current source set depicts little princely or popular support before action.
- Planner who accumulates forged-authority, assassination, diversion, and mobilization schemes without a stable execution rule.
- Royal leader unable to secure attendance from his own senior officials at the final mobilization attempt.
- Prince whose inner circle produces both early denunciation and late confession before terminal punishment.
- Later immortalization figure whose religious memory differs from the rebellion record.

## Qualifications

The current sources do not reconstruct Liu An's full biography, the Huainanzi's composition layers, or a primary-text legal dossier for the rebellion. Hanji 303-1's dialogue, schemes, corruptibility judgments, motives, and odds remain episode-attributed, and its excerpt stops before Liu An's death. Its evidence of weak support qualifies the later claim that faster action might have saved him: a lost window and a viable path to victory are not the same. Hanji 304-1's double-agent theory, Hanji 303-2's singular “author of 塞翁失马” title, and later immortality traditions likewise remain bounded interpretations rather than settled biography.

## What Changed

- The profile now distinguishes failure to decide from the more basic absence of political support and a credible operational plan.
- The final failed summons shows that scheme proliferation had not produced control over Liu An's own senior officials.
- The knowledge-action contrast now includes the source's ironic use of a Huainanzi teaching about capacities and limits.
