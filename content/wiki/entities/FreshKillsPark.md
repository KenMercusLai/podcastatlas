# Fresh Kills Park

## Overview

Fresh Kills Park is the landscape being created over Staten Island's former Fresh Kills landfill, used by the episode as both a material policy achievement and a metaphor for buried civic memory.

## Current Profile

The final regular barge of New York City trash left Fresh Kills on 22 March 2001 after [[RudyGiuliani]] and [[GuyMolinari]] worked to end what they described as an inequitable burden on Staten Island. The former landfill is being converted into one of the city's largest parks, with methane infrastructure, grass, paths, benches, and open space covering buried waste. Deep-rooted trees remain unsafe because of the site's subsurface conditions.

Anthropologist Robin Nagle describes the buried material as a record of people who lived in or passed through the city between 1948 and 2001. The episode turns that observation into a civic metaphor: places and histories can remain structurally present even when ordinary public life no longer sees them. The site's later [[FreshKillsRecoveryOperation|World Trade Center recovery operation]] adds a separate memorial and forensic layer not developed in detail by this episode.

## Key Characteristics

- Former municipal landfill on Staten Island.
- Closed to regular city trash in March 2001.
- Undergoing long-term conversion into a major urban park.
- Engineered landscape constrained by buried waste and methane management.
- Symbol of both material redress and the persistence of forgotten histories.

## Qualifications

This page reflects one episode's political and symbolic treatment, not an engineering history, environmental assessment, construction schedule, or complete account of the site after September 11. The link between buried waste and forgotten communities is an explicit narrative metaphor rather than evidence that the histories are equivalent.
