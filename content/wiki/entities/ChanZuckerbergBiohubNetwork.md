# Chan Zuckerberg Biohub Network

## Overview
The Chan Zuckerberg Biohub Network is the [[ChanZuckerbergInitiative|CZI]] research model described in the source for independent, focused teams that collaborate with multiple universities on large interdisciplinary biomedical problems.

## Current Profile
The episode describes Biohubs as a complement to traditional independent-investigator science. Each hub can sustain a roughly fifty-person focused effort while retaining university partnerships for scientists who remain in academia. Proposals must involve at least three institutions and explain why the problem requires cross-disciplinary and cross-university organization.

The San Francisco Biohub joins Stanford, UC Berkeley, and UCSF around cell biology and infectious-disease work. Chicago is described as engineering tissue with thin sensors to observe cell communication, secretion, and inflammation. New York is described as engineering immune cells that can report internal body states and may eventually act on them.

## Key Characteristics
- Organizes focused research teams outside normal departmental and single-lab boundaries.
- Requires multi-institution collaboration justified by the scientific problem.
- Retains university partnerships rather than demanding that all investigators leave academia.
- Combines biology, engineering, instrumentation, and computation.
- Uses iterative multi-year programs whose continuation depends on what the network learns.

## Qualifications
The episode describes proposals and ambitions rather than independently evaluated research outcomes. Team size, applications, and future acting-cell capabilities remain source-scoped, and the network is presented by its founders rather than external investigators or critics.
