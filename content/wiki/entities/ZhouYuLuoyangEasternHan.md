## Overview

周纡 / Zhou Yu enters the wiki as the Zhang-era Luoyang magistrate whose enforcement focus turns from ordinary local strongmen toward imperial relatives such as the Ma and Dou households. Hanji 752 uses him as a high-status enforcement case: real order in the capital requires disciplining the people with background, but touching those people also exposes the official.

## Current Profile

The source says Zhou Yu asks subordinates to identify the true powerful households in Luoyang. When they name ordinary strongmen, he redirects them toward royal and outer-relative families like the Ma and Dou households. His administration is described as hard-edged enough that powerful relatives become afraid and capital disorder decreases.

The risk appears in the Dou Du incident. Dou Du travels at night and is stopped by the station chief Huo Yan. Huo Yan's aggressive enforcement leads Dou Du to report the matter to Zhangdi, and Zhou Yu is arrested and sent to the tingwei. Zhangdi releases him a few days later, but the episode keeps the danger visible: enforcing against protected people may work locally while still triggering imperial or kinship backlash.

## Key Characteristics

- Zhang-era Luoyang magistrate focused on powerful households rather than only ordinary strongmen.
- Official who treats imperial relatives as the real test of capital order.
- Enforcement figure whose subordinates' toughness makes elite misconduct visibly costly.
- Exposed actor after Huo Yan blocks Dou Du and the Dou side reports upward.
- Disambiguated from the later Three Kingdoms Zhou Yu / 周瑜.

## Qualifications

The source transcript writes 周瑜, but the Zhangdi-era Luoyang magistrate is normalized as 周纡 / Zhou Yu and kept distinct from [[ZhouYuLateHan|周瑜 / Zhou Yu]] of the late-Han/Sanguo military field. Huo Yan remains source-scoped because this episode does not give him an independent arc beyond the Dou Du stop.
