# Matthew Hill

## Overview
Matthew Hill is the University of Calgary professor and cannabinoid researcher interviewed in [[how-cannabis-impacts-health-the-potential-risks-dr-matthew-hill-scim3230094101]] on [[HubermanLab]]. The source presents his work across endocannabinoid signaling, stress, feeding, developmental exposure, cannabis effects, and public-health interpretation.

## Current Profile
Hill's defining role in the wiki is as a scientific corrector who resists both cannabis alarmism and casual safety claims. He distinguishes the precise homeostatic activity of endogenous cannabinoids from broad THC-driven CB1 activation, then repeatedly separates association from causation, commercial labels from chemistry, anecdotes from blinded evidence, and clinical dosing from retail marketing.

His practical frame is conditional rather than binary. Dose, route, potency, tolerance, timing, product composition, psychiatric vulnerability, pregnancy, cardiovascular status, and the treatment indication all change the risk-benefit picture. He treats productive disagreement as part of science: the episode begins with his criticism of Huberman and turns it into explicit revisions around psychosis and indica/sativa claims.

## Key Characteristics
- Explains cannabis through endocannabinoid physiology rather than a generic psychoactive-drug label.
- Separates population association, biological plausibility, genetic vulnerability, and causal proof.
- Emphasizes dose, route, potency, tolerance, and user context in harm assessment.
- Challenges commercial strain categories and low-dose CBD claims that outrun blinded or clinical evidence.
- Recognizes selected medical uses while preserving psychiatric, cardiovascular, reproductive, pulmonary, and use-disorder boundaries.
- Models public scientific correction through direct, evidence-focused disagreement.
