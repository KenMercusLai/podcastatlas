## Overview

年羹尧 / Nian Gengyao appears in the bounded sources as a successful Qing commander whose subsequent purge shaped official fear under [[YongzhengEmperor|雍正]], and as a negative example of failing to reduce sovereign threat perception after victory.

## Current Profile

The earlier source approaches Nian through his political afterlife. His fall made former subordinate [[YueZhongqi|岳钟琪]] especially vulnerable to fear when [[ZhangXi|张熙]] delivered an anti-Qing letter: succession to a sensitive Sichuan-Shaanxi command after a major purge made the approach look like a possible trap or chain of implication. Nian's case therefore helps explain why later “逆书” material was read inside an atmosphere of literary, military, and regional suspicion.

Hanji 1142 supplies the episode's retrospective explanation for the fall. It says that after victory in Qinghai, Nian treated imperial reliance as personal indispensability, monopolized authority, and displayed etiquette that challenged the ruler-minister hierarchy. Yongzheng's later ninety-two charges and order of suicide are presented as the endpoint of that misreading. This sharpens Nian's role in [[HighMeritThreatManagement|功高不震主]], while leaving his motives, the complete charge record, and the weight of etiquette source-scoped.

## Key Characteristics

- Qing military official whose successful service preceded a politically consequential purge.
- Former superior of Yue Zhongqi, making his fall part of a successor's risk environment.
- Negative example, in Hanji 1142, of confusing sovereign favor with equal partnership.
- Figure through whom military merit, etiquette, independent authority, and imperial insecurity intersect.
- Part of the wider anti-“逆书” and disloyalty atmosphere surrounding the Zeng Jing affair.

## Evidence

- Purge afterlife and successor fear: [[11-yongzheng-wangchao-zhi-dayi-juemi-shijingqian-bi-xia-de-qiangu-qi-an-547265513]] links Nian's fall to Yue Zhongqi's anxiety, sensitive command, and reading of Zhang Xi's approach.
- Post-victory threat signals: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1142-caocao-ruhe-dapo-sibainian-hanshi-lifa-ljxatz6lgnc47viery3wuswxi7rx|Hanji 1142]] attributes Nian's danger to monopolized authority, arrogance, and ruler-minister etiquette after Qinghai success.
- Punitive endpoint: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1142-caocao-ruhe-dapo-sibainian-hanshi-lifa-ljxatz6lgnc47viery3wuswxi7rx|Hanji 1142]] reports ninety-two charges and compelled suicide under Yongzheng.

## Qualifications

The bounded evidence is selective and podcast-based rather than a complete biography or legal reconstruction. Hanji 1142's language about arrogance, partnership, indispensability, etiquette, and imperial psychology is interpretive; it does not independently establish Nian's inner beliefs, the validity of every charge, or the relative weight of military, factional, fiscal, ceremonial, and personal causes. The earlier source uses his purge mainly as context for Yue Zhongqi and the Zeng Jing affair.

## What Changed

- Added the Qinghai-victory and postwar-threat account behind Nian's later purge.
- Connected authority, etiquette, and perceived indispensability to the existing record of his political afterlife.
- Preserved the ninety-two charges and psychological diagnosis as source-scoped rather than settled fact.
