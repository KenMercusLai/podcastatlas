# Ban Hai / 半海

## Overview

Ban Hai / 半海 is the young baijiu brand discussed by co-founder [[SofiaAlcoholCreator|索菲亚]] in [[ep143-dangdai-nianqingren-de-yinjiu-wenhua-shui-shuo-baijiu-buneng-zuo-jiweijiu-cmcoor4dajwhbj_a0gbgtuyr|EP143]]. The episode uses its first product and planned second product to examine how an unfamiliar brand can make baijiu legible outside gift, status, and compulsory-banquet contexts.

## Current Profile

The first product is described as a thirty-year strong-aroma baijiu offered in 41% and 53% alcohol versions at prices in the RMB 300-400 range. Rather than lead with luxury gifting, the small team targeted drinkers roughly 25 to 35 years old who already had some baijiu experience, and sought trust through premium restaurants and cocktail bars.

The early launch exposed a positioning tension. The team expected consumers to learn about production and pay for quality before the new brand had earned trust; some buyers instead read the price as high. For a planned second product, the team considered a lower entry barrier, simpler story, wider life-state identification, and package formats that would work for both individuals and bar channels.

## Key Characteristics

- Strong-aroma baijiu brand positioned away from conventional high-end gifting.
- Early product strategy combining aged liquid, mid-tier pricing, and two alcohol-strength options.
- Channel strategy using cocktail bars and higher-end restaurants to create trial occasions and credibility.
- Founder-led attempt to make baijiu compatible with voluntary, social, and mixed-drink use.
- Iterative brand strategy that treats first-product friction as evidence for revising story, packaging, and audience assumptions.

## Qualifications

The evidence is a co-founder's October 2020 account of a very small and early-stage team. The source gives no audited sales, margins, distribution breadth, blind sensory comparison, repeat-purchase data, or later outcome for the planned second product. Age targeting, consumer gender, price resistance, and channel reception are therefore founder observations rather than market-wide proof.
