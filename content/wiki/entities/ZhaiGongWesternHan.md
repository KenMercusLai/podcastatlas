## Overview

翟公 / Zhai Gong is the Western Han official whose crowded residence in office, empty gate after dismissal, and returning visitors after reinstatement form the current source's story behind “门可罗雀.”

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-333-1-men-ke-luoque-de-youlai-ni-zhidao-ma-lkoajhp2b_k32aurnk8rs-ukxaib|Hanji 333-1]] says Zhai Gong serves as 廷尉 in the fifth year of Yuanguang. Visitors crowd his gate while he holds office, but after dismissal the entrance becomes quiet enough to set a net for sparrows. When he returns to office, the visitors return too.

The episode says Zhai responds by writing at his gate that life and death reveal friendship, poverty and wealth reveal social conduct, and high and low rank reveal the relationship. This turns his residence into a test of [[PowerContingentSocialTies|权势依附型交往]] rather than merely a scene of personal disappointment.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han official identified by the episode as 廷尉 in Yuanguang 5.
- Receives crowds of visitors while in office.
- Experiences an almost empty gate after dismissal.
- Sees former visitors return after restoration to office.
- Answers the reversal with an inscription about friendship under death, poverty, wealth, rank, and disgrace.

## Qualifications

The source note is a short podcast summary and explicitly lacks rigorous textual collation. It does not reconstruct Zhai Gong's full biography, the exact dates of dismissal and restoration, the physical state of his gate, or the wording and earliest manuscript form of the inscription. The page treats “门可罗雀” as the episode's later idiom connection, not as the verified exact phrase of the underlying Han account.
