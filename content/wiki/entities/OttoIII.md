# Otto III

## Overview

Otto III was the son of [[OttoII]] and [[Theophano]], crowned as a child and protected by his mother's regency before developing a universal and apocalyptic Roman imperial vision.

## Current Profile

In this episode Otto III first appears as the vulnerable heir whose timely coronation and Theophano's government preserve the dynasty after 983. He later embodies a fusion of Saxon and Byzantine influences and imagines a renewed Roman empire spanning the old eastern-western divide.

The source associates this program with last-emperor expectations, Jerusalem, Golgotha, and the Antichrist. It treats Theophano as a plausible channel for those traditions, while leaving the causal link unproven.

## Key Characteristics

- Child heir whose coronation and regency insulated the succession from crisis.
- Dynast formed by both Saxon and Byzantine court traditions.
- Ruler attracted to a universal restoration of Roman empire.
- Subject of an apocalyptic interpretation involving the last emperor and Jerusalem.
- Example of maternal political protection outlasting a regent's lifetime.

## Evidence

- Succession - [[460-the-empress-of-the-apocalypse-glt9103397168]] says Theophano brought Otto north, secured his coronation at Aachen, and defended his position after Otto II's death.
- Cultural formation - [[460-the-empress-of-the-apocalypse-glt9103397168]] describes him as combining Saxon and Byzantine influences.
- Imperial eschatology - [[460-the-empress-of-the-apocalypse-glt9103397168]] connects his Roman ambitions to last-emperor and Antichrist traditions.

## Qualifications

The episode offers a compact interpretive ending rather than a full account of Otto III's reign. His precise beliefs, the institutional content of his imperial program, and Theophano's role in forming them require evidence beyond this source.
