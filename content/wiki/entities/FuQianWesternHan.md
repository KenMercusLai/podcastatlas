## Overview

傅迁 / Fu Qian is a late [[WesternHanDynasty|Western Han]] court attendant and relative of [[FuTaihouWesternHan|傅太后]] whose retention against [[HanAidi|汉哀帝]]'s wishes makes him a compact case of outer-relative patronage overriding nominal imperial personnel authority.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-569-nianhui-buneng-ting-doushi-jiade-zhichang-zhandui-caishi-zhende-lvjg3qcsgkq4ltai8mqt6p26smk6|Hanji 569]] says Fu Taihou placed her clan relative Fu Qian near Aidi. Aidi disliked him, removed him, and ordered him home, but Fu Taihou's anger forced the emperor to reverse course and retain him as shizhong. [[KongGuangWesternHan|孔光]] and [[ShiDanWesternHan|师丹 / 施丹]] warned that the opposed edicts would confuse the realm and weaken imperial credibility.

Fu Qian matters here less as an independently documented policymaker than as the protected subordinate through whom the court's real hierarchy becomes visible. Once Fu Taihou can veto his dismissal, Aidi's formal superiority no longer guarantees practical command, and Fu Qian's incentives point toward the patron who secured his office.

## Key Characteristics

- Fu-family relative placed near Aidi through Fu Taihou's patronage.
- Court attendant whom Aidi attempts to dismiss and send home.
- Beneficiary of a higher patron's intervention, which reverses the emperor's personnel decision.
- Shizhong whose retention becomes evidence of weakened imperial command credibility.

## Qualifications

Hanji 569 is a short popular-history episode centered on the personnel reversal and its modern workplace analogy. It does not provide Fu Qian's full office chronology, independent actions, home commandery, later career, exact kinship degree beyond the episode's description, or the received text of the edicts and memorial. The claim that protection made him dismissive of Aidi is the host's organizational inference, not a preserved statement by Fu Qian.

## What Changed
