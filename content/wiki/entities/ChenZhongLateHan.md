## Overview
陈忠 / Chen Zhong now appears as an An-era adviser whose current profile begins before the post-Deng accusation phase. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-794-yidai-nvjun-dengsui-qushi-hou-jing-quanjia-zaoyang-llmlhabdd09opca6-pr80q4mfoxi|Hanji 794]] shows him preparing [[EmperorAnOfHan|Emperor An]] to tolerate sharp criticism during worthy-and-upright selection; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-795-dengjia-miewang-hou-weihe-juchao-wei-tamen-mingyuan-lqvmbqrrookphb4becaqbp7h-wi1|Hanji 795]] then complicates him as a restored anti-Deng accuser. Hanji 797-799 preserve the later pattern: he loses some domestic correction but wins selected mourning, frontier, and personnel arguments.

## Current Profile
Chen's earliest current role is constructive court speech. Before the worthy-and-upright recommendations, he warns An that talented candidates may offer fierce criticism and that a ruler must reward useful bluntness rather than punish offense. The result is limited but real: [[ShiYanLateHan|Shi Yan]] performs exceptionally and receives appointment as shizhong.

Hanji 795 complicates any purely virtuous reading. After [[EmpressDowagerDengLateHan|Empress Dowager Deng]] falls, Chen is restored and repeatedly impeaches the [[DengFamilyAnEraLateHan|Deng family]]. The source ties this stance to his father Chen Chong's earlier resistance to special honors for Deng Xun and to Chen Zhong's frustration under Deng dominance. Chen is therefore both a channel for correction and a beneficiary of changed power.

Hanji 797 gives Chen's ritual and frontier profile. He defends three-year mourning for senior officials as institutional precedent and moral modeling, but eunuchs block the memorial before Emperor An cancels the policy. He then applies mourning logic outward after [[GoguryeoLateHan|Goguryeo]]'s king dies, rejecting opportunistic attack and recommending condolences, rebuke, and pardon. An accepts this frontier-restraint advice.

Hanji 798 makes Chen the diagnostic voice for domestic institutional burden. He names disasters, poverty, empty treasury, and Qiang pressure before criticizing palace-linked missions through [[BoRongLateHan|Bo Rong]] as local labor, road, station, supply, and gift extraction. He also argues that Shangshu channels hold practical authority while the Three Excellencies carry disaster blame. An does not accept these warnings.

Hanji 799 shows Chen succeeding again on frontier policy. He argues that abandoning Shanshan and the Western Regions would push states toward Northern Xiongnu, strengthen Xiongnu resources, and expose the Hexi corridor; An accepts the advice by sending [[BanYongLateHan|Ban Yong]] to Liuzhong. The same source shows Chen recommending Zhou Xie and Feng Liang, identifying moral prestige even though both refuse service.

## Key Characteristics
- Open-remonstrance advocate who urges Emperor An to tolerate sharp criticism during worthy-and-upright selection.
- Post-Deng accuser restored after the regency family falls, with a stance shaped by family memory.
- Ritual-policy remonstrant who defends three-year official mourning as institutional precedent and moral formation.
- Frontier restraint adviser who rejects attacking Goguryeo during royal mourning as unrighteous.
- Court critic who diagnoses palace-proximity missions as local labor and supply burden during public crisis.
- Institutional critic who identifies Three Excellencies blame after practical authority has moved into Shangshu channels.
- Advice-giver whose domestic correction often fails under Emperor An even when selected frontier advice is accepted.

## Qualifications
This page is bounded to Hanji 799, Hanji 798, Hanji 797, Hanji 795, and Hanji 794. It does not settle Chen Zhong's full office history, complete family background, later career, or whether his anti-Deng accusations, mourning arguments, domestic memorials, and frontier advice were part of a continuous reform program. The current sources caution against flattening him into either a failed or successful adviser: he can prepare open criticism and win some frontier arguments, but he also helps the post-Deng purge climate and loses domestic correction when access is blocked or ignored.

## What Changed
- Added Hanji 794 as Chen Zhong's earliest current layer around open-remonstrance preparation and Shi Yan's appointment.
- Reframed Chen as an uneven adviser before, during, and after the Deng transition rather than only a post-Deng accuser or later principled remonstrator.
- Preserved Hanji 797 as the official-mourning and Goguryeo mourning-restraint layer.
- Preserved Hanji 798 as the domestic-remonstrance layer around palace-channel burden and authority-blame mismatch.
- Preserved Hanji 799's anti-abandonment and talent-recommendation branches as later adviser roles.
