## Overview
陈忠 / Chen Zhong now appears as an An-era adviser whose current profile begins at the first post-Deng personnel restoration. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-793-lishi-zhuming-nvhuang-dengsui-zenme-si-de-lszfgj7waiuusrddzjmv5mbrp1i|Hanji 793]] shows him recommending upright men after [[EmpressDowagerDengLateHan|Empress Dowager Deng]]'s death; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-794-yidai-nvjun-dengsui-qushi-hou-jing-quanjia-zaoyang-llmlhabdd09opca6-pr80q4mfoxi|Hanji 794]] shows him preparing [[EmperorAnOfHan|Emperor An]] to tolerate sharp criticism during worthy-and-upright selection; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-795-dengjia-miewang-hou-weihe-juchao-wei-tamen-mingyuan-lqvmbqrrookphb4becaqbp7h-wi1|Hanji 795]] then complicates him as a restored anti-Deng accuser. Hanji 797-799 preserve the later pattern: he loses some domestic correction but wins selected mourning, frontier, and personnel arguments.

## Current Profile
Chen's earliest current role is post-regency personnel repair. After Deng's death and Emperor An's personal-rule opening, Chen recommends Yin Yi, [[DuGenLateHan|Du Gen]], and [[ChengYishiLateHan|Cheng Yishi]] as upright figures. An accepts the recommendation, and Du Gen and Cheng Yishi are summoned and appointed after earlier punishment for urging the return of power.

Chen then becomes a constructive court-speech adviser. Before the worthy-and-upright recommendations, he warns An that talented candidates may offer fierce criticism and that a ruler must reward useful bluntness rather than punish offense. The result is limited but real: [[ShiYanLateHan|Shi Yan]] performs exceptionally and receives appointment as shizhong.

Hanji 795 complicates any purely virtuous reading. After Deng falls, Chen is restored and repeatedly impeaches the [[DengFamilyAnEraLateHan|Deng family]]. The source ties this stance to his father Chen Chong's earlier resistance to special honors for Deng Xun and to Chen Zhong's frustration under Deng dominance. Chen is therefore both a channel for correction and a beneficiary of changed power.

Hanji 797 gives Chen's ritual and frontier profile. He defends three-year mourning for senior officials as institutional precedent and moral modeling, but eunuchs block the memorial before Emperor An cancels the policy. He then applies mourning logic outward after [[GoguryeoLateHan|Goguryeo]]'s king dies, rejecting opportunistic attack and recommending condolences, rebuke, and pardon. An accepts this frontier-restraint advice.

Hanji 798 makes Chen the diagnostic voice for domestic institutional burden. He names disasters, poverty, empty treasury, and Qiang pressure before criticizing palace-linked missions through [[BoRongLateHan|Bo Rong]] as local labor, road, station, supply, and gift extraction. He also argues that Shangshu channels hold practical authority while the Three Excellencies carry disaster blame. An does not accept these warnings.

Hanji 799 shows Chen succeeding again on frontier policy. He argues that abandoning Shanshan and the Western Regions would push states toward Northern Xiongnu, strengthen Xiongnu resources, and expose the Hexi corridor; An accepts the advice by sending [[BanYongLateHan|Ban Yong]] to Liuzhong. The same source shows Chen recommending Zhou Xie and Feng Liang, identifying moral prestige even though both refuse service.

## Key Characteristics
- Post-Deng personnel recommender who helps restore upright or previously punished remonstrance figures.
- Open-remonstrance advocate who urges Emperor An to tolerate sharp criticism during worthy-and-upright selection.
- Post-Deng accuser restored after the regency family falls, with a stance shaped by family memory.
- Ritual-policy remonstrant who defends three-year official mourning as institutional precedent and moral formation.
- Frontier restraint adviser who rejects attacking Goguryeo during royal mourning as unrighteous.
- Court critic who diagnoses palace-proximity missions and authority-blame mismatch during public crisis.
- Advice-giver whose domestic correction often fails under Emperor An even when selected frontier advice is accepted.

## Qualifications
This page is bounded to Hanji 799, Hanji 798, Hanji 797, Hanji 795, Hanji 794, and Hanji 793. It does not settle Chen Zhong's full office history, complete family background, later career, or whether his personnel recommendations, anti-Deng accusations, mourning arguments, domestic memorials, and frontier advice were part of a continuous reform program. The current sources caution against flattening him into either a failed or successful adviser: he can restore and recommend useful people and win some frontier arguments, but he also helps the post-Deng purge climate and loses domestic correction when access is blocked or ignored. Yin Yi remains source-scoped.

## What Changed
- Added Hanji 793 as Chen Zhong's earliest current layer around post-Deng personnel restoration and the reuse of Du Gen and Cheng Yishi.
- Reframed Chen as an uneven adviser before, during, and after the Deng transition rather than only a post-Deng accuser or later principled remonstrator.
- Preserved Hanji 794 as the open-remonstrance preparation layer.
- Preserved Hanji 797-799 as the mourning, domestic-burden, Western Regions, and talent-recommendation layers.
