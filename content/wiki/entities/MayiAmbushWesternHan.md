## Overview

马邑之谋 / Mayi Ambush is presented as a Western Han attempt to turn a strategic policy dispute into a bounded deception operation: lure [[JunchanChanyu|军臣单于]] toward [[Mayi|马邑]] with a credible insider-defection story, then use concealed massed forces to encircle him near the frontier.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-260-mayi-zhimou-hanwudi-fanji-xiongnu-diyizhan-luji7sarjbc2y4l8ljsnilld8xjb|Hanji 260]] places the plan after decades in which the post-[[BaidengEncirclement|白登之围]] [[HanXiongnuHeqinPolicy|汉匈和亲政策]] reduced but did not eliminate frontier pressure. [[WangHuiWesternHan|王恢]] argues that stronger Han capacity should now support active deterrence. [[HanAnguoWesternHan|韩安国]] replies that deep advance creates supply, timing, and exhaustion risks.

The resulting proposal is a design compromise rather than a rejection of those operational risks. [[NieWengyiWesternHan|聂翁壹]] will appear to betray the city, promise its people and wealth, and display condemned prisoners' heads as evidence. The chanyu's expected movement lets Han choose the ground, conceal the force, and attack from several directions without sustaining a long pursuit into steppe territory.

The episode says [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] mobilizes three hundred thousand troops under five generals and places Han Anguo in senior command despite his opposition. It ends after Junchan Chanyu reportedly advances with ten thousand cavalry, leaving execution and outcome open.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-261-sishiwanren-de-zhanzheng-weihe-da-le-chang-jimo-lkd8xg1rwv3f5wpmnzu1ygw-7hk|Hanji 261]] supplies the collapse. To leave the target an apparently open road, Han removes visible forces; the resulting emptiness and unattended livestock instead appear abnormal. Junchan Chanyu attacks a watch post, captures a visiting censor, learns the plan, and withdraws before the intended encirclement or a mass battle occurs.

[[WangHuiWesternHan|王恢]] is then the nearest forward commander, with thirty thousand troops intended to strike the Xiongnu rear and baggage after entry into the trap. He decides that attacking the retreating main force without timely support would expose his detachment to destruction. Wudi rejects that risk judgment after a nationwide mobilization has produced no engagement, and Wang dies after a capital sentence. The episode interprets the punishment as a signal favoring initiative and combat over visible inaction; it does not establish that Wang caused the trap's discovery.

## Key Characteristics

- Policy-turn operation intended to move from accommodation toward active deterrence.
- Border ambush designed to answer objections about deep advance and supply.
- Insider-deception plan combining promised gain, apparent grievance, and visible proof.
- Large mobilization placed under a cautious dissenter despite adoption of the aggressive proposal.
- Self-defeating concealment in which the absence of normal defenses becomes evidence of a trap.
- Bloodless operational collapse followed by punitive accountability focused on a forward commander who did not pursue.
- Political aftermath interpreted as a command-culture signal, with imperial motive and later behavioral effects remaining source-scoped.

## Qualifications

The two installments now supply a complete internal arc, but not an independently verified reconstruction. Force totals differ between the approach and collapse accounts: Hanji 260 describes ten thousand Xiongnu cavalry committing toward the bait, while Hanji 261 describes a ten-wan-person Xiongnu force; these may be stage-specific or variant numbers. The censor's disclosure is the reported immediate intelligence breach, yet the emptied route also contributes to suspicion, so responsibility cannot be reduced to one captive or to Wang Hui. Exact command structure, deployments, support feasibility, judicial procedure, speeches, motives, evidence display, bribe amount, later commander behavior, and the label “first battle” remain episode-attributed. The plan's border location reduces some logistics problems but does not by itself prove feasibility or soundness.

## What Changed

- Completed the event arc with abnormal-route detection, captive disclosure, and withdrawal before mass battle.
- Distinguished Wang Hui's decision not to pursue from the intelligence failure that exposed the trap.
- Added punishment as a claimed command-culture signal while keeping imperial motive and downstream effects source-scoped.
- Preserved Han Anguo's logistics objections as design constraints rather than flattening the debate into pro-war versus anti-war temperament.
