# Henry V of England

## Overview

Henry V is the English king whose battlefield command at [[BattleOfAgincourt|Agincourt]], conquest of [[Normandy]], and [[TreatyOfTroyes|Treaty of Troyes]] created an unfinished claim to rule both [[England]] and [[France]].

## Current Profile

The sources present Henry as a highly capable but morally complicated soldier, diplomat, propagandist, and administrator. Before Agincourt he held together an exhausted army through calm command, confession, masses, patriotic appeal, and confidence that divine justice favored his small force. He then used [[TerrainConstrainedCombinedArms|terrain, archers, stakes, formation, and initiative]] to force battle on terms that degraded the larger French army.

The victory also exposes the limits of heroic kingship. When Henry believed French forces might renew the attack and his baggage train was under threat, he ordered prisoners killed despite his soldiers' ransom interest and the order's conflict with chivalric and Christian norms. He nevertheless treated Agincourt as divine confirmation, used it to reinforce [[LancastrianLegitimacy]], and assembled taxation, naval security, imperial diplomacy, and Burgundian cooperation for a larger conquest.

His 1420 settlement was more ambitious than holding Normandy: marriage to [[CatherineOfValois|Catherine of Valois]] made him regent and heir to [[CharlesVIOfFrance|Charles VI]]. Yet continued resistance and Henry's death in 1422 left this [[AngloFrenchDualMonarchy|dual-monarchy]] project to an infant.

## Key Characteristics

- Battlefield commander who sustained cohesion under exhaustion and used formation, archers, stakes, terrain, and initiative at Agincourt.
- Religious ruler who interpreted victory as evidence of divine favor.
- Commander whose fear of renewed attack produced the controversial order to kill prisoners.
- Regime builder who converted military success into domestic Lancastrian legitimacy.
- Diplomat who used imperial and Burgundian relationships to isolate his French opponents.
- Conqueror who established substantive control over Normandy and Rouen.
- Political communicator who used both English and French identities and advanced English-language administration.

## Evidence

- Command and cohesion: [[489-hundred-years-war-bloodbath-at-agincourt-part-3-glt5834061192]] presents Henry maintaining silence and readiness, scouting at night, hearing masses, addressing the line, and forcing action through an advance.
- Tactical system: [[489-hundred-years-war-bloodbath-at-agincourt-part-3-glt5834061192]] connects Henry's thin deployment, archers, stakes, terrain use, and visible place in the center to the English survival and counterattack.
- Religious authority: both [[489-hundred-years-war-bloodbath-at-agincourt-part-3-glt5834061192]] and [[490-hundred-years-war-england-triumphant-part-4-glt2692942762]] show Henry treating victory as divine favor.
- Prisoner killing: [[489-hundred-years-war-bloodbath-at-agincourt-part-3-glt5834061192]] records Henry's order after reports of renewed French action and a baggage attack.
- State and diplomatic preparation: [[490-hundred-years-war-england-triumphant-part-4-glt2692942762]] links taxation, Sigismund, Harfleur, and Burgundy to the 1417 campaign.
- Territorial and dynastic ambition: [[490-hundred-years-war-england-triumphant-part-4-glt2692942762]] traces Normandy's conquest and the Treaty of Troyes settlement.
- Administrative legacy: [[490-hundred-years-war-england-triumphant-part-4-glt2692942762]] highlights separate kingdoms under one crown and Henry's use of English documents.

## Qualifications

The sources do not resolve whether Henry could have sustained the French settlement had he lived. Admiration for his competence is balanced by prisoner killing, battlefield mass death, looting, taxation, continuing resistance, and the possibility that seeking all France was strategic overreach. Exact Agincourt forces and the motives behind particular decisions remain disputed or reconstructed.

## What Changed

- Added the prisoner-killing order as a moral qualification to the ideal-command narrative.
