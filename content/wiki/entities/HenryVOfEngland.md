# Henry V of England

## Overview

Henry V is the English king whose victory at [[BattleOfAgincourt|Agincourt]], conquest of [[Normandy]], and [[TreatyOfTroyes|Treaty of Troyes]] created an unfinished claim to rule both [[England]] and [[France]].

## Current Profile

The episode presents Henry as a highly capable soldier, diplomat, propagandist, and administrator. He treated Agincourt as divine confirmation, used the victory to reinforce [[LancastrianLegitimacy]], and assembled taxation, naval security, imperial diplomacy, and Burgundian cooperation for a larger conquest.

His 1420 settlement was more ambitious than holding Normandy: marriage to [[CatherineOfValois|Catherine of Valois]] made him regent and heir to [[CharlesVIOfFrance|Charles VI]]. Yet continued resistance and Henry's death in 1422 left this [[AngloFrenchDualMonarchy|dual-monarchy]] project to an infant.

## Key Characteristics

- Battlefield commander credited with army composition, marching decisions, tactics, and leadership at Agincourt.
- Religious ruler who interpreted victory as evidence of divine favor.
- Regime builder who converted military success into domestic Lancastrian legitimacy.
- Diplomat who used imperial and Burgundian relationships to isolate his French opponents.
- Conqueror who established substantive control over Normandy and Rouen.
- Political communicator who used both English and French identities and advanced English-language administration.

## Evidence

- Military and religious authority: [[490-hundred-years-war-england-triumphant-part-4-glt2692942762]] credits Henry's choices at Agincourt and records his belief that God granted victory.
- State and diplomatic preparation: [[490-hundred-years-war-england-triumphant-part-4-glt2692942762]] links taxation, Sigismund, Harfleur, and Burgundy to the 1417 campaign.
- Territorial and dynastic ambition: [[490-hundred-years-war-england-triumphant-part-4-glt2692942762]] traces Normandy's conquest and the Treaty of Troyes settlement.
- Administrative legacy: [[490-hundred-years-war-england-triumphant-part-4-glt2692942762]] highlights separate kingdoms under one crown and Henry's use of English documents.

## Qualifications

The episode does not resolve whether Henry could have sustained the French settlement had he lived. Its admiration for his competence is balanced by mass killing, looting, taxation, continuing resistance, and the possibility that seeking all France was strategic overreach.

## What Changed

- Created a profile that joins Henry's military success to legitimacy, diplomacy, administration, and an inherently fragile succession settlement.

## Relationships

- [[HundredYearsWar]] - conflict in which Henry temporarily secured an English dynastic triumph.
- [[BattleOfAgincourt]] - victory that transformed Henry's authority and reputation.
- [[Normandy]] - conquered territorial base for his advance toward Paris.
- [[CatherineOfValois]] - French princess whose marriage to Henry anchored the Troyes settlement.
- [[TreatyOfTroyes]] - agreement naming Henry regent and heir to France.
- [[CharlesVIIOfFrance]] - disinherited rival whose supporters continued resistance.
