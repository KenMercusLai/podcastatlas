# Andrew Palmer

## Overview

Andrew Palmer is a management and workplace voice in [[TheIntelligence]], using comic examples to extract practical organizational lessons.

## Current Profile

Across the bounded sources, Palmer's role is to make workplace dysfunction legible through satire. [[settler-bet-a-west-bank-flashpoint-6a842bb539e73ea8f3c13a2d]] turns disguised public controversies into advice about meetings, unclear authority, career transitions, flattery, legality, and thinking time. [[islamism-in-britain-what-is-the-threat-6aa9164ffca3d5c77b4b383a]] shifts from famous-person dilemmas to mandatory corporate training, arguing that dated videos and infantilized assessments can satisfy compliance while producing little learning.

## Key Characteristics

- Uses humor to reveal management and workplace design failures.
- Converts public or exaggerated scenarios into recognizable organizational problems.
- Distinguishes useful institutional purpose from ineffective delivery.
- Focuses on meetings, authority, pacing, judgment, training, and employee response.

## Evidence

- Advice satire: [[settler-bet-a-west-bank-flashpoint-6a842bb539e73ea8f3c13a2d]] uses a Bartleby segment to treat public controversies as workplace dilemmas about pre-meetings, conflicting bosses, career change, boss criticism, and time to think.
- Compliance satire: [[islamism-in-britain-what-is-the-threat-6aa9164ffca3d5c77b4b383a]] uses dated training videos, weak acting, predictable quizzes, badges, and confetti to expose completion without retention.
- Qualified purpose: [[islamism-in-britain-what-is-the-threat-6aa9164ffca3d5c77b4b383a]] still credits training with useful skills, legal protection, and compliance value before criticizing its design.

## Qualifications

Both contributions are comic commentary rather than systematic organizational research. Their examples identify plausible mechanisms and recognizable failure modes, but do not measure the average effectiveness of advice columns or corporate training.
