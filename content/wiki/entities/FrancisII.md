# Francis II

## Overview

Francis II is the French dauphin and brief king whose marriage to [[MaryQueenOfScots|Mary, Queen of Scots]] joined French royal power to Scottish rule and Mary's contested English claim.

## Current Profile

Francis and Mary were raised together at the French court and are presented as personally affectionate despite contrasts in health, stature, and courtly accomplishment. Their April 1558 marriage placed Mary beside the heir to France while secret instruments sought to transfer Scotland to Francis and future French kings if she died without an heir.

Francis became king after Henry II's death in July 1559. Under strong Guise influence, the royal couple's arms incorporated Mary's claim to England and the French government supported the Scottish position of [[MaryOfGuise|Mary of Guise]]. The defeat of that project in the [[TreatyOfEdinburgh|Treaty of Edinburgh]] preceded Francis's death in December 1560, which ended Mary's French queenship and redirected her toward Scotland.

## Key Characteristics

- Childhood companion and first husband of Mary, Queen of Scots.
- Dauphin whose marriage made Mary the prospective and then actual Queen of France.
- Dynastic bridge joining France, Scotland, and a contested claim to England.
- Young king operating within the political ascendancy of Mary's Guise relatives.
- Early death whose consequences ended Mary's viable future at the French court.
