## Overview

来歙 / Lai Xi is synthesized as an Eastern Han western-campaign planner and commander whose evidence now runs from [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-708-jizun-bingshi-weihe-yinfa-baiguan-jingkong-lhfuvjyiiirlaf-ugrufkaspuauq|Hanji 708]]'s Longxi/Tianshui plan to [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-710-shui-si-le-jiemi-donghan-zui-zhuming-de-ansha-shijian-lok1aczap1u-tmtyutx-upokaplz|Hanji 710]]'s road-opening success and assassination. He matters because he identifies the western shield protecting [[GongsunShuEasternHan|Gongsun Shu]], converts that diagnosis into supply-backed campaigning, and then preserves command continuity after a fatal assassin strike ordered from the Shu side.

## Current Profile

Hanji 708 says Liu Xiu orders Lai Xi to command the generals stationed at Chang'an and appoints [[MaYuanEasternHan|Ma Yuan]] as his assistant. Lai Xi then memorializes that Gongsun Shu depends on Longxi and Tianshui as a screen; if the two commanderies are pacified, Gongsun Shu will be left without a good answer.

The source makes the plan logistical as well as military. Lai Xi requests additional troops, stored grain, and money or food to attract exhausted and hungry local soldiers and civilians. Liu Xiu accepts the plan and orders grain for sixty thousand households to be stored. In autumn, Lai Xi leads Feng Yi and other generals into Tianshui against Wei Chun.

Hanji 710 shows the plan becoming effective. Lai Xi and other generals take Luomen, the remaining Wei-line position collapses, Wang Yuan flees to Gongsun Shu, and Liu Xiu later resettles and punishes the Wei household after renewed flight plans. Lai Xi then fights Qiang forces with Gai Yan and others, uses opened granaries to relieve disaster victims, and helps make Longyou stable enough for the Liangzhou road to open.

The same source gives Lai Xi his final command profile. In 35 CE, he recommends Ma Yuan for Longxi commandery after Xianling Qiang pressure, defeats Wang Yuan and [[HuanAnEasternHan|Huan An]] near Hechi, captures Xiabian, and keeps advancing. Huan An responds by proposing assassination and Qiang agitation. The assassin enters the Han camp at night and wounds Lai Xi, but Lai Xi remains alive long enough to summon Gai Yan, stop his panic, assign military responsibility, write a final memorial, recommend a blunt and upright official, and ask Liu Xiu to watch over his brothers before he dies.

## Key Characteristics

- Guangwu-era western campaign planner assigned to command Chang'an-stationed generals.
- Strategist who reads Longxi and Tianshui as the shield behind Gongsun Shu's continued resistance.
- Logistics-minded commander who asks for troops, grain, money, and inducements before attack.
- Field commander who turns the plan into Luomen collapse, Longyou stabilization, Qiang suppression, relief, and Liangzhou road-opening.
- Assassination victim whose deathbed actions preserve military continuity rather than allowing immediate command panic.
- Cousin and childhood companion whose death produces unusually personal grief from Liu Xiu.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 708 and Hanji 710. The source notes do not reconstruct Lai Xi's full career before the Longxi/Tianshui plan, and Hanji 710 leaves several operational details source-scoped: Gai Yan, Wang Yuan, the unnamed official recommended in the final memorial, exact place variants, and the formal scope of Ma Cheng's succession. The assassination evidence supports command-targeting and continuity claims, but it does not prove that Shu's wider Qiang-agitation plan succeeded.

## What Changed

- Created the page from Hanji 708 as a western-campaign planner linking barrier reduction, supply, and local inducement.
- Added Hanji 710 as the execution and death layer: Luomen, Longyou stabilization, Hechi advance, assassination, deathbed handoff, final memorial, and Liu Xiu's grief.
- Reframed Lai Xi from a planner with unknown outcome into a western commander whose success provokes command-target assassination.
