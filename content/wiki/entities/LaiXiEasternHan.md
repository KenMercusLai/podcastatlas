## Overview
来歙 / Lai Xi is synthesized as an Eastern Han western-campaign envoy, planner, and commander. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-701-liuxiu-de-diwang-quanshu-zhezhong-ren-bixu-sha-lmsoxwg1k3k931ccnpeaomxfcu3q|Hanji 701]] now supplies his earliest current layer: he carries Liu Xiu's order to [[WeiXiaoEasternHan|隗嚣]], rebukes Wei Xiao's hesitation, nearly becomes the target of an envoy-killing crisis, and survives because his status and western reputation still matter. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-705-1-diwang-moulue-liuxiu-ruhe-yi-sheling-nizhuan-caiyuan-fengyun-lq2m06p7eczj9ebysxpl84bkzzi|Hanji 705-1]] follows with the surprise seizure of Lüeyang, [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-708-jizun-bingshi-weihe-yinfa-baiguan-jingkong-lhfuvjyiiirlaf-ugrufkaspuauq|Hanji 708]] continues through the Longxi/Tianshui plan, and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-710-shui-si-le-jiemi-donghan-zui-zhuming-de-ansha-shijian-lok1aczap1u-tmtyutx-upokaplz|Hanji 710]] ends in road-opening success and assassination. He matters because he repeatedly converts western geography and personal credibility into actionable pressure against the Wei Xiao-Gongsun Shu field while preserving command continuity under mortal danger.

## Current Profile
Hanji 701 gives Lai Xi a diplomatic-risk opening before his later field-command profile. Liu Xiu sends him to Wei Xiao with an imperial order explaining the western campaign against [[GongsunShuEasternHan|Gongsun Shu]]. Lai Xi rebukes Wei Xiao for listening to smaller men and warns that his course looks like a plan for clan destruction. The confrontation nearly becomes violent: Lai Xi draws his sword, Wei Xiao retreats inside, and Wei Xiao's camp surrounds Lai Xi's carriage before Wang Zun and western gentry argue that killing a solitary imperial envoy and Liu Xiu's cousin would bring worse disaster.

Hanji 705-1 gives Lai Xi the earlier operational breakthrough. In 32 CE he leads two thousand men through difficult western routes, cuts through Fanxu and Huizhong, reaches Lüeyang unexpectedly, kills Wei Xiao's defending general Jin Liang, and takes the position. Liu Xiu reads Lüeyang as Wei Xiao's key shield and predicts that Wei Xiao will attack it in anger; Lai Xi then holds the fortress against months of pressure from Wei Xiao, Wang Yuan, Xing Xun, Wang Meng, Niu Han, and Gongsun Shu-backed troops.

Hanji 708 expands the same western logic into campaign planning. Liu Xiu orders Lai Xi to command the generals stationed at Chang'an and appoints [[MaYuanEasternHan|Ma Yuan]] as his assistant. Lai Xi memorializes that Gongsun Shu depends on Longxi and Tianshui as a screen; if the two commanderies are pacified, Gongsun Shu will be left without a good answer. His plan asks for troops, stored grain, and money or food to attract exhausted and hungry local soldiers and civilians.

Hanji 710 shows the plan becoming effective and then costly. Lai Xi and other generals take Luomen, the remaining Wei-line position collapses, Wang Yuan flees to Gongsun Shu, and Liu Xiu later resettles and punishes the Wei household after renewed flight plans. Lai Xi then fights Qiang forces, opens granaries to relieve disaster victims, helps stabilize Longyou, recommends Ma Yuan for Longxi, defeats Wang Yuan and [[HuanAnEasternHan|Huan An]] near Hechi, captures Xiabian, and keeps advancing until Huan An's side targets him by assassination. Even then he summons Gai Yan, stops panic, delegates military affairs, writes a final memorial, and dies only after command continuity is secured.

## Key Characteristics
- Guangwu-era envoy whose first current evidence is a dangerous rebuke of Wei Xiao before open rebellion.
- Western actor whose personal reputation and imperial-envoy status help keep a crisis from turning into execution.
- Western commander whose main operational evidence is the small-force surprise seizure of Lüeyang.
- Strategist who reads Longxi, Tianshui, and Lüeyang as shields protecting Wei Xiao and Gongsun Shu.
- Logistics-minded planner who asks for troops, grain, money, and inducements before deeper attack.
- Field commander who turns planning into Luomen collapse, Longyou stabilization, Qiang suppression, relief, and Liangzhou road-opening.
- Assassination victim whose deathbed actions preserve military continuity rather than allowing immediate command panic.

## Qualifications
This page is bounded to Hanji 701, Hanji 705-1, Hanji 708, and Hanji 710. Hanji 701 supports Lai Xi's envoy-risk and reputation layer, but Wang Zun, Niu Han, the western gentry, and the precise confrontation wording remain source-scoped. Hanji 705-1 backfills the Lüeyang operation but leaves exact route geography, Jin Liang, Wang Yuan, Xing Xun, Wang Meng, Niu Han, Li Yu, Tian Yan, and improvised weapon details source-scoped. Hanji 710 supports command-targeting and continuity claims, but it does not prove that Shu's wider Qiang-agitation plan succeeded.

## What Changed
- Added Hanji 701 as Lai Xi's earliest current layer: the dangerous envoy confrontation with Wei Xiao before open rebellion.
- Preserved Hanji 705-1 as Lai Xi's first western-operation layer: the surprise capture and defense of Lüeyang.
- Connected the Lüeyang holdout to the later Longxi/Tianshui plan as a repeated shield-removal strategy against Wei Xiao and Gongsun Shu.
- Preserved Hanji 710 as the execution and death layer: Luomen, Longyou stabilization, Hechi advance, assassination, deathbed handoff, final memorial, and Liu Xiu's grief.
