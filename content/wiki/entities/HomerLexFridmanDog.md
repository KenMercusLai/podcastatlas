# Homer (Lex Fridman's dog)

## Overview
Homer was [[LexFridman]]'s Newfoundland dog and long-term companion in the United States. This disambiguated page separates him from [[Homer]], the ancient Greek poet.

## Current Profile
In [[essentials-machines-creativity-love-dr-lex-fridman-scim3392253065]], Fridman describes Homer as an exceptionally large, kind, clumsy dog who shared lonely nights, difficult periods, and successes with him. Homer's cancer, decline, euthanasia, and death become the conversation's concrete evidence that accumulated shared time can create profound attachment and that caregiving can include uncertainty about whether suffering continued too long.

## Key Characteristics
- Newfoundland companion dog described as weighing more than 200 pounds.
- Remembered as kind, clumsy, and present through loneliness and success.
- Shared routines and moments exemplify the episode's account of attachment through time.
- Final illness raised questions about suffering, euthanasia, and caregiver responsibility.

## Evidence
- Shared-life evidence: [[essentials-machines-creativity-love-dr-lex-fridman-scim3392253065]] describes Homer accompanying Fridman through difficult and successful periods and many nights together.
- End-of-life evidence: [[essentials-machines-creativity-love-dr-lex-fridman-scim3392253065]] describes cancer, slow decline, inability to rise, hospital euthanasia, and Fridman's uncertainty about whether Homer suffered too long.
- Mortality evidence: [[essentials-machines-creativity-love-dr-lex-fridman-scim3392253065]] says witnessing Homer's death made life's brevity feel real to Fridman.

## Qualifications
The page reflects Fridman's personal recollection rather than veterinary documentation. The episode does not provide enough clinical information to judge the timing of treatment or euthanasia.
