## Overview

裴遵 / Pei Zun is synthesized as a Dunhuang frontier official who reports Western Regions pressure to [[LiuXiu|光武帝刘秀]] and helps narrow Han commitments. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-719-guangwudi-liuxiu-suanbusuan-zhanan-lk8eme3wwyk5qkjjfxkp0zsc77ix|Hanji 719]] has him object to giving [[ShacheKingdomLateHan|Shache]] king Xian protectorate-level authority; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-722-rang-liuxiu-xiansi-de-toutongbing-zenme-huishi-logwccdsca7vm2zzssu8ubm8rwra|Hanji 722]] has him report smaller Western Regions states' request to leave hostages in Han territory as a deterrent signal.

## Current Profile

Hanji 719 supplies Pei Zun's first current role. After Shache King Xian asks Han to restore Western Regions authority through him, Liu Xiu initially grants a protectorate-level seal, ribbon, vehicle, flags, and gifts. Pei Zun objects that giving such authority to Shache would elevate a frontier ruler too far and disappoint other states. Liu Xiu accepts the objection, downgrades Xian to Great General, and Pei Zun retrieves the stronger seal.

Hanji 722 supplies the next role. Smaller Western Regions polities fear Shache pressure and ask that hostage princes be allowed to remain in Han territory so they can warn Shache that Han backing may arrive. Pei Zun reports the request, and Liu Xiu accepts the narrower signal without restoring a full protectorate.

Pei Zun therefore appears less as a conquest commander than as a credibility filter. He helps the center distinguish symbolic deterrence from a recognition package that would create wider expectations.

## Key Characteristics

- Dunhuang official positioned between Luoyang and Western Regions polities.
- Warns against overrecognizing Shache King Xian with protectorate-level authority.
- Executes the practical retrieval of the stronger seal after Liu Xiu downgrades the title.
- Reports smaller-state hostage requests as a possible deterrent signal against Shache.
- Embodies the difference between narrow frontier signaling and unsustainable commitment.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Pei Zun's Western Regions reporting and objection role. It does not reconstruct his full office history, clan background, or later career. Hanji 719 and Hanji 722 create a chronological tension: in 41 CE he narrows an excessive Shache-centered recognition, while in 45 CE he reports a smaller-state signal meant to restrain Shache without full commitment. The page keeps those as a coherent frontier-credibility profile rather than as a contradiction.
