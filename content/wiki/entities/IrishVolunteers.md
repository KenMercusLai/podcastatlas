# Irish Volunteers

## Overview

The Irish Volunteers were a nationalist armed organization founded under [[EoinMacNeill]] during the Home Rule crisis and later divided over service in the First World War.

## Current Profile

The Volunteers arose after Ulster mobilization, gun-running, and the Curragh incident had normalized organized force in Irish constitutional politics. MacNeill's “The North Began” argued for a nationalist counterpart. When John Redmond urged members to join the British Army, the organization split, leaving a smaller body more open to [[IrishRepublicanBrotherhood|IRB]] influence.

That overlap did not eliminate strategic disagreement. MacNeill required defensive necessity and German arms before supporting revolt, whereas the IRB military council planned an offensive rising. His countermand after the arms plan failed reduced and confused Easter mobilization, showing that the Volunteers were a contested institution rather than a single conspiratorial instrument.

## Key Characteristics

- Nationalist volunteer force created in response to Ulster armed organization.
- Product of the prewar normalization of paramilitary politics.
- Movement divided by John Redmond's call to join the British war effort.
- Public organization containing a clandestine IRB current.
- Force whose leadership split over the threshold for rebellion.
- Principal manpower pool reduced by MacNeill's countermand.

## Qualifications

The source does not supply a complete organizational genealogy, regional account, or precise membership history. Militarization created capacity for rebellion but did not make all volunteers separatists or guarantee that armed organization would culminate in the Rising.
