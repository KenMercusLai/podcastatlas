# Black Hills

## Overview

The Black Hills are the contested northern Plains region where [[GeorgeArmstrongCuster]]'s 1874 military expedition, gold publicity, settler intrusion, and failed federal purchase talks intensified conflict with the [[LakotaPeople|Lakota]].

## Current Profile

The episode places the hills outside the Great Sioux Reservation but within unceded territory protected from white intrusion. It rejects a simple claim of timeless Lakota possession, noting that Lakota power in the region was comparatively recent, while also stressing that conquest, bison, timber, political prestige, and spiritual meaning made surrender unacceptable to influential leaders.

Custer's large 1874 expedition combined cavalry, Gatling guns, scouts, scientists, journalists, and President Grant's son. A small gold find became sensational national news, and miners entered faster than [[PhilipSheridan]] and other officials could or would exclude them. In 1875 a commission sought purchase, but [[CrazyHorse]] stayed away, [[SittingBull]] rejected sale, and Spotted Tail demanded terms far beyond the commission's authority. The failure made the hills the hinge between treaty enforcement, coercion, and the war sequence later described by [[ManufacturedWarPretext]].

## Key Characteristics

- The hills joined territorial, subsistence, resource, political, and spiritual value.
- Military exploration and press publicity helped convert uncertain mineral evidence into a gold rush.
- Miner intrusion exposed the gap between formal territorial protection and practical enforcement.
- Purchase negotiations failed because federal and Lakota valuations reflected incompatible political stakes.
- The region became a causal bridge from railroad expansion and economic crisis to the 1876 war.

- Status and value: [[450-custers-last-stand-death-in-the-black-hills-part-5-glt1497339770]] places the hills in unceded territory and connects them to bison, timber, prestige, and sacred meaning.
- Expedition and gold: [[450-custers-last-stand-death-in-the-black-hills-part-5-glt1497339770]] describes Custer's mixed military-scientific-media expedition, the small find, sensational coverage, and miner influx.
- Failed sale: [[450-custers-last-stand-death-in-the-black-hills-part-5-glt1497339770]] records Crazy Horse's absence, Sitting Bull's refusal, Spotted Tail's high demand, and the commission's limited offer.

## Qualifications

- The episode's challenge to timeless sacredness does not show that the hills lacked sacred meaning in the 1870s.
- Territorial history and spiritual significance require Lakota accounts and specialist scholarship beyond this popular-history source.
- The precise gold quantity, purchase figures, legal status, and motives of officials remain source-scoped.
- Miner intrusion, military sponsorship, press amplification, and federal policy involved distinct actors whose effects converged without requiring one unified plan.

## What Changed

- Created a profile linking territorial meaning, gold publicity, failed purchase, and movement toward war.
