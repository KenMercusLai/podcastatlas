# Brazilian Independence

## Overview

Brazilian Independence appears across the episodes as the 1822 dynastic break with Portuguese authority led by [[DomPedroI]], enabled by the court's earlier move to Rio and later remembered through the Ipiranga reference in the [[BrazilianNationalAnthem]].

## Current Profile

The episodes present independence as a dynastic and colonial rupture rather than a clean republican founding. [[DomJoaoVI]]'s [[ImperialCenterRelocation|relocation of the court and government to Rio]] had already made Brazil unusually central inside the Portuguese monarchy. When Lisbon tried to reverse that elevation and pressed Pedro to return, Brazilian supporters rallied around the regent; he rejected the order, declared independence in 1822, and became constitutional emperor rather than president of a republic.

Portugal lacked the forces to restore the old hierarchy, while Britain mediated recognition in pursuit of commercial access and pressure against the slave trade. That diplomatic settlement did not end slavery: the episode uses Brazil's weakly enforced 1831 law and the phrase “a law for the British to see” to distinguish formal concession from implementation.

## Key Characteristics

- Triggered by Portuguese pressure for Pedro to return to Lisbon after Brazil had been elevated inside the monarchy.
- Led by [[DomPedroI]], who became constitutional emperor and "perpetual defender" in the episode's account.
- Sustained by Portugal's limited coercive capacity and British mediation shaped by commercial and anti-slave-trade goals.
- Remembered through the Ipiranga site named in the anthem's opening.
- Produced an early independence anthem closely tied to Pedro I's personal image.
- Opened the [[BrazilianEmpire]] rather than an immediate republic.
- Left the slaveholding order intact despite later formal pressure against the trade.

## Evidence

- Dynastic precondition: [[681-brazil-the-emperors-anthem-part-5-glt5596929385]] says [[DomJoaoVI]] left Pedro as regent and suggested that if Brazil broke away, Pedro should lead the break.
- Imperial reversal: [[229-portugal-gold-earthquakes-and-brazil-part-3-glt8317395817]] connects the court transfer, Brazil's elevated status, and Lisbon's attempt at recentralization.
- Ipiranga break: [[681-brazil-the-emperors-anthem-part-5-glt5596929385]] places Pedro's declaration at the Ipiranga on 7 September 1822.
- Diplomatic settlement: [[229-portugal-gold-earthquakes-and-brazil-part-3-glt8317395817]] says Portugal could not prevent separation militarily and Britain mediated while seeking commerce and slave-trade restrictions.
- Anthem memory: [[681-brazil-the-emperors-anthem-part-5-glt5596929385]] says the anthem's opening reference points back to the Ipiranga independence moment.

## Qualifications

The page records two episodes' court-transfer and anthem-centered version of independence. It does not reconstruct the full military, regional, constitutional, or diplomatic history. British motives, Masonic organization, the independence speech, the 1831 law's enforcement, and the timing and terms of recognition remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Added court transfer and failed metropolitan recentralization as structural preconditions.
- Added the qualified role of British mediation and the persistence of slavery after independence.
