# Chris Palmer

## Overview
Chris Palmer is the psychiatrist and central guest in [[essentials-diet-nutrition-for-mental-health-dr-chris-palmer-scim2465421786]], where he argues for a metabolic and mitochondrial approach to some mental illness cases.

## Current Profile
The source presents Palmer through a personal-to-clinical arc: childhood mental illness, later metabolic syndrome during Harvard internship and residency, a strong response to the Atkins diet, and cautious clinical use of dietary intervention with treatment-resistant patients. His profile in this wiki is not "diet cures mental illness"; it is a supervised metabolic psychiatry stance that combines clinical anecdotes, epilepsy precedent, mitochondrial mechanisms, and explicit evidence limits.

## Key Characteristics
- Palmer links his interest in dietary psychiatry to his own metabolic syndrome and mood/energy improvements after trying the Atkins diet.
- He reports mixed clinical response: some treatment-resistant patients improved, others did not benefit or could not follow the diet.
- He treats ketosis as a meaningful treatment variable, especially in the source's schizoaffective disorder case and in different ketone targets for depression versus psychotic or bipolar disorders.
- He uses epilepsy's ketogenic-diet history to argue that diet can plausibly alter brain function.
- He proposes mitochondria as a unifying mechanism across neurotransmitters, stress hormones, inflammation, gene expression, and mental health.
- He explicitly qualifies the psychiatric evidence base because large randomized controlled trials for mental disorders are not yet available.

## Evidence
- Personal entry point - [[essentials-diet-nutrition-for-mental-health-dr-chris-palmer-scim2465421786]] says Palmer had childhood mental illness, later metabolic syndrome, and marked metabolic and mental-state improvements after the Atkins diet.
- Clinical intervention stance - [[essentials-diet-nutrition-for-mental-health-dr-chris-palmer-scim2465421786]] describes treatment-resistant patients, mixed response, ketosis dependence, and a detailed schizoaffective-disorder case.
- Evidence boundary - [[essentials-diet-nutrition-for-mental-health-dr-chris-palmer-scim2465421786]] records his warning that psychiatric medication changes can be difficult and dangerous and that large psychiatric RCTs are not yet available.
- Mechanism model - [[essentials-diet-nutrition-for-mental-health-dr-chris-palmer-scim2465421786]] connects Palmer's model to mitochondria, mitophagy, mitochondrial biogenesis, neurotransmitters, stress response, steroid hormones, and inflammation.

## Qualifications
The profile is bounded to this episode note and relies on Palmer's reported clinical cases and interpretation. The source does not establish completed large-scale psychiatric trial evidence, and the medication and severe-disorder advice is explicitly supervision-bound.
