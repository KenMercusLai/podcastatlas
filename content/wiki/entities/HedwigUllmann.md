# Hedwig Ullmann

## Overview

Hedwig Ullmann was a German Jewish art collector whose forced departure from Nazi Europe dispersed a family collection that included [[VaseWithCarnations|Vincent van Gogh's Vase with Carnations]].

## Current Profile

[[hedwig-s-lost-van-gogh-hedwig-s-lost-van-gogh|Hedwig’s Lost Van Gogh]] presents Ullmann as born into a wealthy German family in 1872 and as a collector, with her husband Albert, of paintings, medieval sculpture, porcelain, and silver. Nazi persecution progressively constrained her life in Frankfurt. She sold or consigned as much of the collection as possible before fleeing to Milan around the end of 1938, then escaped with her sons and their families to Australia as Fascist Italy became more dangerous for Jews.

Ullmann died in Melbourne in May 1945, four days before the war in Europe ended. Her later importance to the wiki lies not only in ownership history but in how forced migration changes the meaning of a transaction: the family's dispersed works became evidence of lost continuity, and descendant-led recovery became part of [[HolocaustEraArtRestitution|restitution and healing]].

## Key Characteristics

- German Jewish collector whose family assembled a broad decorative- and fine-art collection.
- Persecuted owner who sold or consigned works while preparing to flee Nazi Germany.
- Refugee who escaped through Milan to Australia with her family.
- Historical center of a descendant-led effort to reconstruct a dispersed collection.
