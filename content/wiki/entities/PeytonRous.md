# Peyton Rous

## Overview
Peyton Rous is the physician-researcher whose chicken-sarcoma transmission experiments provide the earliest case in [[the-obscure-virus-club-the-obscure-virus-club|The Obscure Virus Club]] for a virus causing cancer.

## Current Profile
The episode presents Rous as an early experimental precursor to [[LudwigGross]] and [[HowardTemin]]. In 1911 he transferred material from a chicken sarcoma into healthy chickens and observed similar tumors, leading him to infer a viral cause. The finding mattered less as immediate consensus than as a durable anomaly: critics doubted whether the tumors were truly cancer or whether a chicken result could illuminate human disease, but later retrovirus work made the question mechanistically productive.

## Key Characteristics
- Used transmissible tumor material to argue for a viral cause of chicken sarcoma.
- Supplied an experimental system that later researchers could revisit mechanistically.
- Faced skepticism about disease classification and animal-to-human relevance.
- Functions in the episode as the first link in a long, discontinuous retrovirus research lineage.

## Evidence
- Tumor transmission: [[the-obscure-virus-club-the-obscure-virus-club]] reports that Rous transferred chicken-sarcoma material into healthy chickens, which then developed similar tumors.
- Viral inference: [[the-obscure-virus-club-the-obscure-virus-club]] says Rous concluded that a virus caused the tumors.
- Reception and legacy: [[the-obscure-virus-club-the-obscure-virus-club]] describes contemporary doubts and connects Rous sarcoma virus to Temin's later work on permanent cellular transformation.

## Qualifications
This profile rests on one retrospective podcast summary rather than Rous's papers, contemporary criticism, or a full history of tumor virology. The experiment's protocol, reception, terminology, and place within the wider cancer-research field remain source-scoped.
