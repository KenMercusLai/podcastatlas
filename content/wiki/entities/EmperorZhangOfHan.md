## Overview

汉章帝 / Emperor Zhang of Han now has six bounded wiki layers. Hanji 754 adds the earliest current layer: he refuses to punish fact-based Taixue criticism, loosens some speech-linked implication bans, recognizes [[MaoYiEasternHan|Mao Yi]] and [[ZhengJunEasternHan|Zheng Jun]] as local integrity figures, and approves the [[NorthernXiongnuLateHan|Northern Xiongnu]] trade contact that later becomes a restitution problem. Later layers present him as a household-aware ruler who encourages childbirth, values low-disturbance county administration, performs ritual travel, honors teachers and Confucian descendants, remembers [[LiuCangDongpingKing|Dongping King Liu Cang]], preserves ministerial speech, chooses restitution, protects spring farming, sponsors legal and ritual reform, responds to Qiang-frontier failure, accepts omen warnings, and reaches a late-reign weakness when generosity toward royal kin outruns fiscal and ritual boundaries. Hanji 770 keeps him as a deceased emperor whose tomb and spouse order frame the later [[EmperorHeOfHan|Emperor He]] birth-mother settlement.

## Current Profile

Hanji 754 supplies the earliest current governing layer. Zhangdi receives a Taixue speech case after [[LiangYuEasternHan|Liang Yu]] reports [[KongXiEasternHan|Kong Xi]] and [[CuiYinEasternHan|Cui Yin]] for discussing Emperor Wu's record. Kong Xi argues that fact-based historical criticism is not fabricated slander, and Zhangdi stops the case, appoints Kong Xi as Lantai lingshi, and issues a limited release from earlier `yaoyan` implication bans. This gives Zhangdi a speech-preserving layer before Hanji 756's later ministerial-disagreement case.

This early layer also connects integrity recognition with frontier contact. Zhangdi honors Mao Yi and Zheng Jun, asking local officials to visit them annually and provide sheep and wine; the anecdotes frame Mao's apparent office joy as filial and Zheng's anti-corruption advice as action-backed. Zhangdi also approves Meng Yun's Wuwei memorial supporting Northern Xiongnu mutual trade before Southern Xiongnu raiding disrupts the contact.

Hanji 755 then supplies the household-statecraft layer. Zhangdi formalizes a birth-encouragement package: pregnant women receive fetal-nurture grain, the husband receives a one-year poll-tax exemption, and the episode places this beside older and later dynastic measures that lowered household burdens to increase population. The source's political premise is material rather than sentimental: population means future tax, labor, and soldiers, so childbirth policy becomes state-capacity policy through [[FertilityBurdenReliefPolicy|fertility burden relief]].

The same source gives Zhangdi a local-governance standard. His instruction to the Three Excellencies praises Xiangcheng magistrate Liu Fang for frugal, low-disturbance rule and criticizes officials who treat harshness, heavy punishment, and displays of severity as administrative brilliance. This links Zhangdi to [[LocalGovernanceAsCare|local governance as care]] through non-harassment rather than through public works.

The ritual and learning layer is broad. Zhangdi orders calendrical correction from the old Taichu calendar to the Sifen calendar, performs the ploughing rite at Dingtao, sacrifices at Taishan and to the Five Emperors, visits Qufu to sacrifice to [[Confucius|Confucius]] and his disciples, and treats Kong Xi's modest answer as evidence of worthy descent. He also honors [[ZhangPuEasternHan|Zhang Pu]] as his former `Shangshu` teacher by first taking student protocol and then resuming ruler-minister protocol. Zheng Jun's "white-clothed Shangshu" honor and old anti-corruption story add a private-morality layer to the same classical-order field.

The frontier layer begins before the later campaign debates. Northern Xiongnu weakness is visible through defections and attacks from Southern Xiongnu, Dingling, Xianbei, and Western Regions polities. Hanji 754 gives the trade-opening version of that weakness, and Hanji 755 gives the later raid-and-restitution version. When Southern Xiongnu troops seize people and livestock, Meng Yun warns that Han credibility may suffer if a temporarily reconciled Northern Xiongnu side reads the attack as Han-backed bad faith. Zhangdi receives a divided court: [[ZhengHongEasternHan|Zheng Hong]] and [[FifthLunEasternHan|Fifth Lun]] oppose restitution, while [[HuanYuEasternHan|Huan Yu]] and [[YuanAnEasternHan|Yuan An]] support it.

Hanji 756 resolves that dispute into the forbearance-and-court-speech layer. Zhangdi refuses to let angry ministers turn policy disagreement into resignation; he says silent ministers are not a blessing and sends Zheng Hong, Fifth Lun, and Wei Biao back into deliberation. He then pays the Southern Xiongnu side for the seized people and livestock and returns them to Northern Xiongnu, while preserving precedent rewards for killings. The same source adds a spring inspection-tour rule: officials should detour rather than trample fields, release spare horses, and keep escort arrangements from burdening farming season.

Hanji 756 also makes Zhangdi the ruler who receives Zheng Hong's early warnings that [[DouXianEasternHan|Dou Xian]] has too much power. Zhangdi strips Zheng Hong after Dou's counteraccusation, but later sends a physician when Zheng Hong submits a final anti-Dou memorial during terminal illness. The evidence therefore does not make Zhangdi a consistent anti-Dou ruler; it shows that warning reached him before the later He-era crisis.

Hanji 757 supplies the institutional-action layer. Zhangdi appoints the Yingchuan jurist [[GuoGongEasternHanJurist|Guo Gong]] as tingwei and accepts his proposal to lighten forty-one heavy-punishment provisions. He also responds to [[CaoBaoEasternHan|Cao Bao]] by choosing execution over broad scholar deliberation: despite status and consultation objections, Zhangdi assigns Cao Bao alone and gives him [[ShusunTong|Shusun Tong]]'s twelve `Han Yi` chapters as the base for revising Han rites. After [[FuYuEasternHan|Fu Yu]] dies in a failed Qiang-frontier expedition, Zhangdi appoints [[ZhangYuEasternHan|Zhang Yu]] as Protector of the Qiang. Later auspicious signs prompt the Zhanghe era change, while [[HeChangEasternHan|He Chang]] warns that strange signs can also signal danger.

Hanji 758 supplies the final-year governing profile. Zhangdi travels through southern regions, receives large Northern Xiongnu submissions, accepts Cao Bao's Han ritual code, and presides over a frontier environment in which [[BanChaoEasternHan|Ban Chao]] defeats Shache. The same source praises his broad, kind, kin-attached temperament, but the weakness is proportion. Royal kin receive long capital residence, high treatment, heavy gifts, palace access, and protocol privileges while disasters, Qiang pressure, and commandery strain make restraint necessary. [[SongYiEasternHan|Song Yi]] gives the sharper hierarchy argument: royal uncles and brothers remain subjects in relation to the emperor. Zhangdi dies before the remonstrances become durable correction.

The death scene complicates the judgment rather than reversing it. Zhangdi's final edict asks that no separate temple or sleeping hall be built at the tomb and that earlier imperial precedent be followed. Fan Ye's assessment, as presented by the episode, praises Zhangdi as loyal, humane, filial toward Ma Taihou, lighter in taxes and labor, and oriented toward loyalty, forgiveness, rites, and music. Hanji 770 then supplies the later posthumous layer: after [[EmpressDowagerDouHeLateHan|Dou Taihou]] dies and the hidden [[LiangGuirenHeLateHan|Liang Guiren]] birth-mother case reaches Emperor He, officials ask to remove Dou's title and deny burial with Zhangdi. Emperor He refuses, preserving the deceased emperor's burial order while redressing Liang Guiren west of Zhangdi's tomb.

## Key Characteristics

- Late Eastern Han emperor whose current profile starts with speech tolerance, local integrity recognition, frontier trade contact, household fertility relief, low-disturbance local administration, ritual-classical travel, and Xiongnu restitution debate.
- Ruler who treats population, farming, and household burden as state-capacity concerns rather than separate private matters.
- Sponsor of Cao Bao's Han-rites project from single-compiler commission through later acceptance.
- Frontier decision maker whose credibility choices run from Meng Yun's warning and the Hanji 756 restitution decision to Zhang Yu's appointment after Fu Yu's failed Qiang campaign.
- Humane and ritually minded ruler whose legal leniency, teacher respect, Confucian sacrifice, and royal-kin memory coexist with incomplete correction of Dou Xian warning.
- Ruler whose royal-kin favoritism becomes a case of blurred public hierarchy and fiscal overextension despite an otherwise positive late-reign assessment.
- Deathbed frugality and posthumous spouse-order figure whose simple-burial wish and tomb pairing shape later legitimacy settlements.

## Qualifications

This page is still source-bounded and does not attempt a full Zhangdi reign biography. Hanji 754 gives a selective layer of speech tolerance, integrity recognition, and trade approval before the Hanji 755 restitution setup. Hanji 755 gives population policy, local administration, ritual travel, personal honors, and frontier-diplomacy setup. Hanji 756 supplies the resolution of the Xiongnu restitution dispute plus farming-care travel and Zheng Hong's anti-Dou warning. Hanji 757 gives a selective law, ritual, frontier replacement, and omen layer. Hanji 758 gives a late-reign synthesis filtered through the host's "分寸感" frame, not a complete institutional history. Its royal-kin criticism is compatible with Fan Ye's positive assessment because the source itself holds both points together: humane governance still needs fiscal and ritual boundaries. Hanji 770 remains a posthumous-status layer rather than evidence for Zhangdi's own decision-making.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 754 as Zhangdi's earliest current layer: historical-critique tolerance, limited speech-linked release, Mao Yi and Zheng Jun honors, and Northern Xiongnu trade approval.
- Reframed Zhangdi's living profile around speech capacity and household-aware statecraft before the existing court-speech, law, ritual, frontier, and kinship-boundary layers.
- Preserved Hanji 755 as the population, local governance, ritual, and Xiongnu restitution setup layer.
- Preserved Hanji 756's restitution decision as the resolution of the debate Hanji 755 sets up.
- Preserved Hanji 758's bounded-virtue judgment and Hanji 770's posthumous burial-order role.
