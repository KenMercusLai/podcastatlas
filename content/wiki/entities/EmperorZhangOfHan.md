# 汉章帝 / Emperor Zhang of Han

## Overview

Emperor Zhang of Han is the Eastern Han ruler whose bounded profile spans ritual and classical ambition, household-centered population policy, frontier restraint, legal leniency, evidence-sensitive accusation handling, and partial tolerance for remonstrance. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-753-banchao-beigao-heizhuang-lt-sksxrz30sawit81eqbeonhzv|Hanji 753]] now adds the earliest layer in this current corpus: Zhangdi rejects [[LiYiEasternHan|Li Yi]]'s slander against [[BanChaoEasternHan|Ban Chao]], accepts [[ZhengHongEasternHan|Zheng Hong]]'s burden-relief advice, hears [[WeiBiaoEasternHan|Wei Biao]] on appointments, restrains interrogation tools, tries to limit southern-tour disturbance, and ultimately accepts [[ZhuHuiZhangEraEasternHan|Zhu Hui]]'s refusal to endorse salt and junshu restoration.

## Current Profile

Zhangdi is presented as a ruler who wants humane, ritualized, textually grounded government, but whose rule works best when that aspiration becomes concrete procedural restraint. In Hanji 753, this takes the form of rejecting a slander by checking troop behavior, reducing Jiaozhi transport and tribute burdens, warning against wrong office fit, banning unauthorized torture devices, and trying to prevent imperial travel from becoming a local extraction event.

The later Zhangdi layers deepen and complicate that picture. Hanji 754-758 show a ruler who promotes fertility, local non-harassment, teacher respect, ritual order, kinship memory, and some legal leniency, while also exposing anxiety over Taixue speech, limits in frontier foresight, and vulnerabilities around Dou Xian. Hanji 770 adds that the Hou clan memory and the silence around his biological mother remain politically meaningful after his reign, tying Zhangdi's ritual household order to later questions of legitimacy.

## Key Characteristics

- Evidence-testing ruler: Zhangdi rejects Li Yi's accusation against Ban Chao because it conflicts with the costly behavior of troops still following Ban Chao.
- Burden-aware administrator: fertility policy, Jiaozhi route reform, tribute relief, local non-harassment, and southern-tour orders all treat household burden as a test of governance.
- Procedural and legal restrainer: he limits interrogation devices and also appears in the Guo Gong episode as a ruler open to mercy and process.
- Appointment-quality ruler: he accepts Wei Biao's warning that seniority, legal rote, and quick answers do not equal office fit.
- Ritual and classical emperor: he invests in calendar correction, sacrifice, teacher respect, kinship memory, and structured imperial symbolism.
- Frontier decision maker with mixed results: he can trust Ban Chao against slander and debate border costs, yet his broader frontier and Dou-clan environment remains unstable.
- Humane but uneven sovereign: the corpus shows real responsiveness to dissent and public burden alongside coercive authority, court sensitivity, and incomplete control of later clan risk.

## Evidence

- Accusation verification and command trust: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-753-banchao-beigao-heizhuang-lt-sksxrz30sawit81eqbeonhzv|Hanji 753]] has Zhangdi reject Li Yi's slander by asking why homesick Han soldiers still follow Ban Chao if the accusation is true.
- Southern logistics and public burden: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-753-banchao-beigao-heizhuang-lt-sksxrz30sawit81eqbeonhzv|Hanji 753]] records Zhangdi accepting Zheng Hong's tribute-relief advice and ordering localities not to pre-stock supplies or send movement-inquiry envoys for his southern travel.
- Personnel and office fit: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-753-banchao-beigao-heizhuang-lt-sksxrz30sawit81eqbeonhzv|Hanji 753]] shows Zhangdi accepting Wei Biao's advice on selecting capable officials and not confusing Shangshu capacity with langguan legal familiarity.
- Legal restraint and remonstrance tolerance: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-753-banchao-beigao-heizhuang-lt-sksxrz30sawit81eqbeonhzv|Hanji 753]] records Zhangdi forbidding cruel interrogation tools and later releasing and rewarding Zhu Hui after initially reacting angrily to opposition against salt and junshu restoration.
- Speech discipline and court anxiety: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-754-gudai-ban-qieting-fengyun-jieju-shuangwen-ltvymh-2r-ic5diqfh-2ic9cudq2|Hanji 754]] places Zhangdi inside the Taixue speech case, showing how imperial order can criminalize or discipline uncontrolled public talk.
- Population and household policy: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-755-zhege-chaodai-huangshang-jiu-kaishi-guli-shengwa-le-lj7bdkxkdtjhpo469yxhskjyfdco|Hanji 755]] shows Zhangdi encouraging births and attempting to stabilize household reproduction as a governing concern.
- Frontier, leniency, and burden debates: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-756-weishenme-quan-ni-de-rao-ren-chu-qie-rao-ren-ltn4tqzy1d-sodhjidncv8dyqxpj|Hanji 756]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-757-rensheng-xiang-shao-zou-wanlu-xian-zhaozhun-dingwei-lri1zyiq8210ymqjakznyp3v4k6x|Hanji 757]] show the Guo Gong mercy case, Xiongnu-policy debates, and the ruler's attempt to match law, pardon, and frontier cost.
- Kinship and forbearance boundary: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-758-suoyou-gaoqingshang-beihou-doushi-fencungan-lr-6tzffmwdtzidkf3y8ule35qf7|Hanji 758]] tests Zhangdi's household and kinship politics against the dangers of excessive clan indulgence.
- Posthumous household legitimacy: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-770-chenmo-de-zhenxiang-huangdi-de-shengmu-jing-shi-ta-lu5dqu8uuw277ikzxkblvqdbpl3q|Hanji 770]] preserves the Hou-clan and biological-mother memory problem that shapes how Zhangdi's family order is remembered after his reign.

## Qualifications

- Zhangdi's profile should not be flattened into either benevolent ritualism or court fragility alone; the source set shows both.
- Hanji 753 adds several discrete policy notices, but Liu Chang of Jiyin, Liu Shang of Rencheng, Liu Fu of Pei, Zhang Lin, exact torture-device details, and exact southern route geography remain source-scoped.
- Zhu Hui in Hanji 753 is the Zhang-era remonstrant page [[ZhuHuiZhangEraEasternHan]], not the later He-era frontier figure [[ZhuHuiHeEraLateHan]].
- Accepting a remonstrance or edicting restraint does not prove perfect enforcement; the current evidence records imperial posture and decisions, not long-term administrative compliance.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 753 as the earliest current Zhangdi layer.
- Added evidence-based rejection of Li Yi's slander against Ban Chao.
- Added Zheng Hong's route and tribute relief, Wei Biao's personnel advice, interrogation limits, southern-tour burden restraint, and Zhu Hui's monopoly-policy remonstrance.
- Reframed Zhangdi's humane profile around concrete verification, burden control, and office-fit decisions as well as ritual ideals.
