# Chuck Yeager

## Overview

Chuck Yeager was the West Virginia test pilot who broke the sound barrier in 1947 and became the central figure in Tom Wolfe's origin story for the American [[PilotVoice]].

## Current Profile

[[this-is-your-captain-speaking]] presents Yeager less as the direct acoustic model for every modern airline pilot than as a durable professional ideal. Wolfe associated his Appalachian drawl, folksiness, and calm under danger with a style that other pilots adopted, but the episode notes that many contemporary pilots have barely heard him speak.

The stronger bounded judgment is therefore cultural rather than genealogically exact: aviation retained an image of effortless command associated with Yeager even if no continuous chain of literal vocal imitation can be demonstrated.

## Key Characteristics

- Test pilot associated with breaking the sound barrier in 1947.
- West Virginia and Appalachian speech background central to Wolfe's account.
- Symbol of calmness and command under dangerous conditions.
- Cultural reference point for the American pilot stereotype rather than a proven universal acoustic template.

## Evidence

### Historical origin story

- [[this-is-your-captain-speaking]] attributes the pilot-voice genealogy to Tom Wolfe's account of pilots adopting Yeager's drawl, folksiness, and composure.

### Surviving professional ideal

- [[this-is-your-captain-speaking]] argues that Yeager's attitude may have survived more clearly than his exact sound because many current pilots have little direct exposure to his voice.

## Qualifications

The page is based on one structured episode summary rather than Yeager's recordings, biography, flight records, Wolfe's full text, or a historical acoustic study. It does not establish that Yeager originated every feature of airline speech or that pilots consciously imitated him.
