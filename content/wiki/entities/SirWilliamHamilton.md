# Sir William Hamilton

## Overview

Sir William Hamilton was the British envoy to Naples whose diplomatic household joined court access, antiquarian celebrity, devotion to Emma Hamilton, and an increasingly paternal attachment to Horatio Nelson.

## Current Profile

Hamilton's sober, scholarly reputation joined diplomacy to collecting, volcanology, and classical antiquities. He met the young Emma through his nephew [[CharlesGreville|Charles Greville]], received her in Naples after Greville deceptively sent her there, and supported French lessons, Italian fluency, interest in Vesuvius and antiquity, and the salon performances later known as the attitudes. Their 1791 marriage made Emma Lady Hamilton and secured her formal place in Neapolitan society; the source emphasizes affection, intellectual partnership, and safety without erasing the unequal circumstances of her arrival.

As Britain's long-serving envoy, he and Emma later received [[HoratioNelson|Horatio Nelson]] after the Nile and formed a household in which Hamilton's admiration resembled a father-mentor bond. He joined the royal flight to Palermo, feared death during the storm crossing, and remained inside Nelson and Emma's deepening intimacy. Emma's later affair with Nelson exposed him to gossip and mockery, yet the comic source interprets his continued attachment as loyalty stronger than pride or conventional jealousy.

## Key Characteristics

- British diplomat associated with the court of Naples.
- Collector, volcanologist, classicist, and cultural partner as well as diplomat.
- Husband, educator, and social sponsor of Emma Hamilton.
- Diplomatic host and older mentor figure for Nelson after the Battle of the Nile.
- Participant in the royal court's evacuation from Naples to Palermo.
- Participant in a publicly scandalous emotional triangle with Emma and Nelson.
- Cast as “the devoted one” because attachment outweighed reputation and humiliation.

## Qualifications

The comic source compresses a long diplomatic career and complicated marriage into a dating-show category, while the Nelson episodes center Emma or Nelson rather than Hamilton's independent perspective. His role in Greville's arrangement, Emma's constrained choices, the transition to intimacy, marriage motives, educational influence, diplomatic work, emotional response, public humiliation, and the exact household structure remain source-scoped.
