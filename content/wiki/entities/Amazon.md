# Amazon

[[tech-20260731-0731-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260731-0731-mp-tech-pod-128]] uses Amazon as a peer cloud provider in the AI capex comparison. The source's point is comparative rather than Amazon-specific: [[Meta]] faces a harder [[AIRevenueLegibility]] question because [[Microsoft]], [[Google]], and Amazon have existing cloud-compute businesses that make third-party AI infrastructure demand easier for investors to understand.

[[aodesai-ba-baomihua-zhuangjin-teluoyi-muma-haolaiwu-dapian-weihe-zhadui-dingzhi-baomihuatong-1004599628]] adds a source-dated market and retail update. The episode says Amazon's market value crossed $3 trillion after cloud growth improved, while its physical grocery strategy shifted toward [[WholeFoodsMarket|Whole Foods]] price cuts and private labels after Amazon Fresh supermarkets underperformed.

[[174-women-hai-neng-gei-suanfa-dang-duojiu-de-pinwei-laoshi-duitan-yamaxun-agi-cha-sheng-lrs0qgmr9gy1nbdtrsvn2lx5dxza]] adds Amazon through [[AmazonAGI]] and [[ChaSheng]]'s foundation-model research perspective. The source extends Amazon's existing cloud, AI-infrastructure, and [[Anthropic]] threads into model training itself: open-source strategy, [[AIDataFlywheel|data flywheels]], synthetic-data filtering, [[ModelValueEmbedding]], enterprise-owned models, and AGI limits are discussed from inside a team that works on large-model training.

[[tech-20260721-0721-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260721-0721-mp-tech-pod-128]] adds Amazon to the AI bond-market story. [[JulieOsk|Julie Osk]] says major technology companies are issuing bonds to finance AI infrastructure as data-center capex expands beyond earlier cash-flow norms, while the episode cites [[Amazon]], [[Nvidia]], and [[SpaceX]] as accounting for a large share of one month of recent bond issuance. For Amazon, the source extends the existing hyperscaler capex thread from cloud demand into [[AIInfrastructureDebtFinancing]] and bond-supply risk.

[[159-suanfa-de-liufu-miankong-ta-shi-ruhe-cong-chuli-shuju-biancheng-dingyi-women-shi-shui-de-lh5ng5mbvkworfxlq9l-coscepc7]] adds Amazon through the "Amazon Fail" example in [[AlgorithmicInclusionPatterns]]. The episode uses the 2009 same-sex-book visibility failure to show how category rules, adult-content flags, and ranking eligibility can become public-facing power even when they appear to be internal data operations.

[[146-meiguo-jingji-zheme-cha-meigu-hai-neng-jixu-zhang-ma-chuantai-meilun-meihuan-lrshkxgofprel-e-7lkiqu-en8gu]] adds Amazon as the strong Big Tech earnings example in a U.S. equities discussion. The source says Amazon's quarterly results beat expectations and helped explain why U.S. stocks could rise despite weak economic sentiment in many labor and consumer contexts.

[[tech-20260422-0422-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260422-0422-mp-tech-pod-128]] adds Amazon as the positive profitability comparison inside [[SarahKunst]]'s discussion of [[Snap]]. Kunst says Amazon's financial story changed as [[AmazonWebServices]] became a major part of the business, contrasting higher-margin cloud revenue with lower-margin marketplace, warehouse, logistics, and shipping operations. The source uses Amazon to show why investors may tolerate losses when a credible future profit engine makes the [[PathToProfitability]] legible.

[[tech-20251229-1229-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20251229-1229-mp-tech-pod-128]] adds Amazon through [[Zoox]], the autonomous-vehicle company it acquired. [[KirstenKorosek]] presents Zoox as one of the robotaxi companies that survived industry consolidation, making Amazon a parent-company context for the capital, regulation, and operations needed to move from demonstrations to commercial robotaxi service.

[[tech-20260417-0417-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260417-0417-mp-tech-pod-128]] adds Amazon through its announced purchase of [[Globalstar]], described by [[MarketplaceTech]] as a stock-and-cash deal valued at about $11.6 billion. The source frames the acquisition as part of Amazon's push into low-earth-orbit satellite networks, [[DirectToDeviceSatelliteConnectivity]], and competition with [[SpaceX]] for hard-to-reach internet access.

[[live-anthropic-co-founder-on-ai-and-jobs]] adds Amazon as [[DarylFairweather]]'s former corporate-economist setting and as a consumer-facing dynamic-pricing example. In the source, Fairweather says she worked on employee engagement for frontline call-center workers, while the hosts use Amazon price changes to illustrate [[DynamicPricingFairness]].

[[tsr-s5-blakescholl-v3-finalaudio-tsr-s5-blakescholl-v3-finalaudio]] adds Amazon as part of [[BlakeScholl]]'s founder formation before [[BoomSupersonic]]. Scholl says he worked at Amazon in 2001 as a software engineer on early scalable customer-acquisition systems, including buying Google ads before an ads API existed. In the source, Amazon is a training context rather than the product insight for Boom.

[[tech-20260320-0320-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260320-0320-mp-tech-pod-128]] adds Amazon's ultra-fast delivery push. The episode says Amazon announced one-hour delivery in hundreds of U.S. cities and three-hour delivery in more than 2,000 places for supercenter-like goods, with non-Prime customers paying about $20 for one-hour delivery. [[AnitaRamaswamy]] frames the move as direct competition with [[Walmart]], a continuation of consumer willingness to pay for convenience through [[UberEats|Uber Eats]] and [[DoorDash]], and a case where AI can help both fulfillment and recommendations.

[[tech-20260313-0313-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260313-0313-mp-tech-pod-128]] adds Amazon as the company case behind [[AICodingGuardrails]]. The episode says the [[FinancialTimes|Financial Times]] reported that Amazon engineers met about recent website outages, including incidents discussed in relation to AI use; Amazon told the show that only one discussed incident was AI-related and that none involved AI-written code. [[JewelBurkeSolomon]] uses the story to argue that AI coding and deployment workflows need review, controls, and uptime-oriented oversight.

[[socialradarspod-emmettshear-v2]] adds Amazon as the acquirer of [[Twitch]]. [[EmmettShear]] describes Amazon as a good acquirer for founders who want to keep running their company, because its decentralized model can let acquired businesses preserve brand, management, and operating independence. In this source, Amazon is therefore a [[PostAcquisitionFounderIdentity]] case as much as a media or cloud company.

Amazon appears in [[ep39-feng-man-lou-xiaji-quanqiu-shuaitui-manman-bijin-yanfang-sishou-bubu-weiying-manliao-xiabannian-meigu-meizhai-huilv-lgz718un4sjvgxqbydfikyxz0hcj]] as one of the large cloud buyers whose AI-infrastructure budget helps shape [[Nvidia]] demand. The episode groups Amazon with [[Microsoft]] and [[Google]] to make a customer-concentration and capex-cycle point rather than a detailed Amazon thesis.

[[ep77-si-shi-wan-nian-xin-fu-ye-zhuan-le-san-shi-si-yi-te-lang-pu-jiao-ni-ruhe-gao-qian-lnrx8vkdjedxbmmcwxlnwow-pcab]] adds Amazon as the buyer-side example in [[MelaniaTrump]]'s documentary deal. [[vol-162-keji-kuaile-xingqiu-44-xin-moxing-sotamen-qihe-xinchun-1-6628-1]] adds Amazon as a frontier AI infrastructure partner. [[chule-shiyou-he-haixia-zhejie-yilang-zhanzheng-kaishi-suanji-nide-fuwuqi-le-keji-luandun]] adds Amazon through AWS-style cloud-region exposure in the Middle East. [[stock-options-how-to-hedge-an-ai-bubble-698efe7e7301331f1f674bd5]] adds Amazon to the [[AIBubbleHedging]] discussion. [[dhaka-matters-an-election-for-bangladesh-698c5a3afeb59e13a3b8a94d]] adds Amazon as a concrete [[CandidateIdentityFraud]] case. [[144-dui-yang-meng-de-4-xiaoshi-fangtan-xiaofei-dianzi-si-yu-sheng-di-san-lei-gongsi-duan-ce-moxing-chanpin-fangfa-youxi-moshi-lnjleqjgjo1txupouvygmdv7oo8b]] adds Amazon as the early marketplace context for [[AnkerInnovations]]. [[1-ren-gongsi-kang-5-ge-ren-de-huo-hai-yao-guan-50-ge-agents-s10e18-e3a21dde-0bba-4ec2-bf12-5043500ae5c6]] adds an AWS startup-program angle through [[AmazonWebServices]] and [[FromIdeaToFrontier]]. [[shaoyou-de-shendu-canyu-guo-zijie-meituan-zuzhi-jianshe-de-ren-duitan-ai-chuangyezhe-wei-xiaokang-lof0mb7u5buzec1qtxqrt9kraiud]] adds Amazon as the operating-plan comparison in [[BusinessModelOrganizationFit]]. [[WeiXiaokang]] uses Amazon to represent long-chain, lower-margin businesses where goals, budget, headcount, role level, and bar-raising processes are planned before hiring rather than relying mainly on talent-first team matching.

[[no-200-dianshang-sanguo-zhi-qunxiong-zhulu-yaogua-gongzhang-chijian-juqian-yiji-108-zhong-sifa-974601500]] adds Amazon's China ecommerce branch through [[JoyoAmazonChina]]. [[tech-20251218-1218-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20251218-1218-mp-tech-pod-128]] adds Amazon as an incumbent advertising competitor for [[AISearchAdvertising]]. [[GarrettJohnson]] groups Amazon with [[Google]] and [[Meta]] as mature platforms that already have advertiser relationships, targeting systems, and conversion expectations, making the AI-search ad market harder for new entrants.

[[tech-20260220-0220-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260220-0220-mp-tech-pod-128]] adds Amazon through [[Ring]], its video doorbell and camera subsidiary. The episode uses [[RingSearchParty|Ring Search Party]] to show how an AI-enabled consumer safety feature can raise [[ConsumerCameraSurveillance]] concerns when connected-camera networks, law-enforcement relationships, and partnerships such as [[FlockSafety]] enter the story.

[[e244-duan-dao-duan-vs-shangxia-fenceng-jiqiren-lujing-zhizheng-zhengzai-zhuanxiang-fc9a3737-81a9-49cf-a7d6-530c77df836e]] adds Amazon as the scene-owner and vertical-application robotics case. [[HanZheng]] notes Amazon's long investment in warehouses, Kiva-style logistics, robotic arms, humanoid pilots, and partnerships such as Agility or Covariant-like attempts; the source frames Amazon's strength as deployment environment and business loop rather than necessarily the most general robot foundation model.

[[tech-20260106-0106-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260106-0106-mp-tech-pod-128]] adds [[BlueJ]] as another source-scoped warehouse robotics example. [[AmyWebb]] describes BlueJ as overhead robotic arms that move packages faster and at lower cost than a human workforce, making Amazon a concrete case for [[PhysicalAI]] becoming a labor and operations issue.

[[would-you-trust-an-economist-with-your-economy]] adds Amazon as a return-to-office example in [[NickBloom]]'s [[WorkFromHomeEvidence]] discussion. The source says Bloom suspects some mandates can function as headcount reduction without severance, while noting that Amazon denied this was its motivation.

[[costco-1]] adds Amazon as the strategic contrast for [[Costco]]. The source frames Amazon as convenience-first retail, while Costco's [[RetailCounterPositioning]] depends on warehouse visits, limited SKUs, pallet logistics, low markups, and member trust. Costco's e-commerce extensions through [[CostcoLogistics]] and [[CostcoNext]] are treated as Costco-flavored rather than Amazon imitation.

[[sweetwater-chuck-surack-how-a-customer-service-strategy-built-a-billion-dollar-online-pro-audio-and-music-company-50c2b5e3-3bc3-4039-a92d-26228b8ac908]] adds Amazon as the general-marketplace contrast for [[Sweetwater]]. [[ChuckSurack]] says Sweetwater may sell on Amazon for reach, but the company's direct advantage depends on [[ServiceLedRetailMoat]], dedicated [[SalesEngineerModel|sales engineers]], advice, support, and fulfillment speed for customers who need help choosing music gear.

[[the-nfl-1]] adds Amazon as a live-sports streaming buyer through Thursday Night Football on Prime. The source treats Amazon's NFL rights as evidence that digital distributors need scarce live attention and that the [[NationalFootballLeague|NFL]] can use [[LiveSportsStreamingTransition]] without becoming a streaming platform itself.
