# William Adams / 三浦按针

William Adams / 三浦按针 is the English navigator at the center of [[14-wushi-weilian-dahanghai-shidai-de-riben-he-xifang-555771285]] and [[SamuraiWilliam|《武士威廉》]]. The episode presents him as a lower-status English seaman shaped by naval war, Dutch employment, and failed route searches before a disastrous Pacific voyage leaves him among the few survivors to reach [[Japan]] in 1600.

In Japan, Adams becomes valuable to [[TokugawaIeyasu|Tokugawa Ieyasu]] because he can explain European geography, [[ConfessionalTradeConflict]], navigation, shipbuilding, and the political difference between Iberian Catholic powers and Protestant English/Dutch traders. The episode says Ieyasu grants him land, a Japanese name, and samurai status, but it keeps his role bounded: Adams helps with diplomacy, shipbuilding, and trade access; he does not redirect the [[TokugawaShogunate]] away from later restriction.

## Key Claims
- Adams's story is useful because it joins biography to [[EarlyModernJapanEuropeContact]], not because one foreigner single-handedly changes Japanese history.
- His voyage makes [[OceanicVoyageAttrition]] concrete: the source tracks shipwreck, scurvy, hunger, violence, capture, and very low survival.
- Adams's political value depends on being a practical mediator rather than a missionary; he can oppose [[Jesuits|Jesuit]] accusations without turning the episode into a simple Protestant hero story.
- His shipbuilding and navigation knowledge matter to [[TokugawaForeignTradeAmbivalence]] because the regime wants technique and trade while fearing Christian political risk.
- [[Nioh|《仁王》 / Nioh]] amplifies Adams's legend through fantasy, while the episode returns him to early-modern maritime and diplomatic history.
