# Engelbert Dollfuss

## Overview

Engelbert Dollfuss was the Catholic-authoritarian Austrian chancellor killed during the failed Austrian Nazi coup of 1934, when Italian opposition still constrained [[AdolfHitler]] from absorbing [[Austria]].

## Current Profile

The episode places Dollfuss in an Austria divided among socialist Vienna, conservative Catholic rule, pan-German feeling, and an Austrian Nazi movement. His government was authoritarian, but it also resisted takeover by Hitler's movement. Austrian Nazis murdered him during their 1934 coup attempt.

The coup failed partly because [[BenitoMussolini]] moved forces toward the Brenner Pass and signaled that Italy would not accept German intervention. That contrast makes Dollfuss's death a marker of what changed by 1938: Austrian resistance remained, but the external deterrent had disappeared.

## Key Characteristics

- Catholic-authoritarian ruler of interwar Austria.
- Opponent of Austrian Nazi seizure of power.
- Victim of the failed 1934 Nazi coup.
- Historical marker for Mussolini's earlier defense of Austrian independence.

## Qualifications

Resistance to Nazism did not make Dollfuss a democratic ruler. This source offers a compressed prehistory of his government and death rather than a full account of Austrofascism, the Austrian civil conflict, or the coup's organization.
