# Brazilian Empire

## Overview

The Brazilian Empire appears in the episode as the monarchy created after [[BrazilianIndependence]], first under [[DomPedroI]] and then under [[DomPedroII]], before abolition and republican pressure ended it.

## Current Profile

The episode uses the empire to explain why Brazil's national anthem and flag could not be understood as timeless patriotic symbols. Pedro I's personalist independence anthem became politically vulnerable after his decline and abdication. Under Pedro II, Brazil built a more distinct tropical national identity, but the same imperial period also carried war, rebellion, slavery, abolition, and the republican turn that forced national symbols to detach from one ruling house.

## Key Characteristics

- Monarchical independence settlement rather than an immediate republic.
- Initially centered on [[DomPedroI]], whose image dominated the first independence anthem.
- Continued under [[DomPedroII]], who is linked by the episode to stronger tropical national identity.
- Marked by regional revolt, the War of the Triple Alliance, slave revolts, and abolition in 1888.
- Ended as coffee planters, intellectuals, and the army turned against the monarchy.
- Left symbols, especially the anthem tune and flag colors, that the republic could reuse.

## Evidence

- Pedro I phase: [[681-brazil-the-emperors-anthem-part-5-glt5596929385]] says Pedro became constitutional emperor after independence and later abdicated in 1831.
- Pedro II phase: [[681-brazil-the-emperors-anthem-part-5-glt5596929385]] links Pedro II's reign to a more distinct Brazilian identity, continued political turbulence, the War of the Triple Alliance, and abolition in 1888.
- Republican transition: [[681-brazil-the-emperors-anthem-part-5-glt5596929385]] says anti-monarchical opinion grew among coffee planters, intellectuals, and the army before [[DeodoroDaFonseca]]'s republic kept the old anthem tune.

## Qualifications

This page is not a full imperial Brazil history. It preserves the episode's anthem-centered account of monarchy, identity, slavery pressure, and symbolic afterlife.
