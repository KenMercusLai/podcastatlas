## Overview

王皇后（汉平帝皇后） / Empress Wang of Han Pingdi is [[WangMang|王莽]]'s daughter and the former empress of [[HanPingdi|汉平帝]], now synthesized as a palace figure whose selection, reproductive body, marriage identity, and death are repeatedly used to stage dynastic meaning. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-615-chengmu-jieshe-gudai-caili-zheme-gao-lkllbe015xkb7wlhkobtastkifdo|Hanji 615]] adds the earliest current layer: officials inspect and praise her suitability, divination and ancestral notification make the marriage public, and the imperial bride-price ties her body and natal household to Han ritual legitimacy. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-627-1-nvxing-biting-dute-shijiao-jingqi-yinyu-beihou-de-lishi-zhihui-lqomjfejmglm9hylkomktx6rtbtt|Hanji 627-1]] adds the next layer: Wang Mang invokes her "子孙瑞," read by the episode as menstruation and fertility maturity, to announce [[Ziwudao|子午道]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-640-qipa-wangmang-ruhe-chedi-bifan-xiongnu-lhxbx6keibmfe8z6bcsmlpnr6eht|Hanji 640]] presents her as Ding'an Taihou / Huanghuang Shizhu, refusing Wang Mang's remarriage plan. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-666-xihan-zuihou-yiwei-huanghou-weihe-touhuo-zifen-lrjurgjzvwezkppvgeupu6ormmzw|Hanji 666]] later centers her last act during the burning of [[WeiyangPalace|未央宫]].

## Current Profile

The current evidence covers an arc from selection and bride-price to early sign use, coercive recoding, and terminal collapse. Hanji 615 says the marriage is built through official inspection, divination, sacrifice, and a huge bride-price; her empress status is therefore a dynastic office and Wang-family power channel before she appears as an individual actor. Hanji 627-1 then says Wang Mang uses her "子孙瑞" as auspicious public language for a road project. The page treats this as [[ReproductiveBodyOmenPolitics|reproductive body omen politics]]: the empress is symbolically valuable because her body can be read as dynastic fertility, but she is not shown as choosing the political use of that sign.

Hanji 640 says that after Western Han has fallen, she often claims illness and avoids court attendance. Wang Mang wants to use remarriage to link her to [[SunJianXinGeneral|孙建]]'s family, but she refuses to become a political instrument and does not want to betray the Liu house through her father's arrangement.

Hanji 640 also shows Wang Mang changing her title from Ding'an Taihou to Huanghuang Shizhu, then sending Sun Jian's son in a staged visit with doctors. Her anger and illness make refusal a bodily and palace-bound form of opposition. Hanji 666 presents the endpoint: as Xin collapses and Weiyang Palace burns, she says she has no face to see the Han house and enters the fire.

The page therefore reads her not as a policy actor but as a dynastic-identity rupture case. Her father uses Xin authority to rename, remarry, and position her, while her former Han empress status makes full assimilation impossible.

## Key Characteristics

- Wang-family daughter and empress candidate whose official inspection, divination, ancestral notification, and bride-price tie her status directly to Wang Mang's political project.
- Former Western Han empress whose reproductive maturity is used as auspicious political language before Wang Mang's usurpation.
- Former Western Han empress whose identity remains bound to the Han house after Wang Mang's usurpation.
- Refusal figure who resists Wang Mang's attempt to remarry her as a political instrument.
- Title-recoded palace woman moved from Ding'an Taihou to Huanghuang Shizhu, whose end is narrated inside Weiyang Palace's burning rather than battlefield combat.
- Figure of dynastic identity rupture: reproductive symbolism, family loyalty, marriage status, and restored-Han legitimacy become mutually unbearable.

## Qualifications

This page should not be expanded into a full biography without more source notes. Hanji 615 supports the selection, divination, bride-price, and redistribution layer, but her own voice, age, daily palace life, and inner experience of the marriage are absent. Hanji 627-1 supports only the "子孙瑞" and Ziwudao framing; it does not independently establish her inner experience of menstruation, fertility, or the road announcement. Hanji 640 supports the Ding'an Taihou/Huanghuang Shizhu title branch and refusal of Wang Mang's remarriage plan, but her exact legal title mechanics, daily court position, and inner motives beyond the host's interpretation remain source-scoped. Hanji 666 supports her identity as Wang Mang's daughter and Han Pingdi's former empress, her residence inside the palace-fire field, and the episode's shame-centered interpretation of her death.
