# Brian Klaas

## Overview
Brian Klaas is a political scientist represented here through a [[HiddenBrain]] episode on chance, chaos, causal explanation, optimization, and resilience.

## Current Profile
In [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-0a3c8347ab08972a4dce8dba455358cf-53039697-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-0a3c8347ab08972a4dce8dba455358cf-53039697|Fluke: How Chance and Chaos Shape Our Lives]], Klaas argues that social science and ordinary hindsight often label contextual detail as noise even when weather, clothing, timing, travel, or material accidents become decisive. His examples move from personal survival and family history to atomic targeting, coup failure, evolutionary contingency, and chaos theory, supporting [[ChanceSensitiveCausality]] without claiming that events are literally uncaused.

His practical response is resilience rather than perfect prediction. Hyperconnected systems transmit small disruptions widely, and relentless optimization removes the buffers that might contain them. Klaas therefore favors slack, decoupling, scenario thinking, intrinsic sources of meaning, and exploration. The stance is not fatalism: limited control reduces claims of sole credit or blame while leaving every action capable of influencing later outcomes.

## Key Characteristics
- Treats contextual details and chance encounters as potentially decisive causal inputs rather than disposable noise.
- Uses historical, political, scientific, and personal cases to make causal sensitivity concrete.
- Separates causal contribution from moral responsibility.
- Critiques [[MagnitudeBias]] and confident retrospective storytelling.
- Connects hyperconnectivity and near-total optimization to cascading fragility.
- Replaces complete-control goals with resilience, influence, curiosity, and exploration.

## Qualifications
This profile rests on one structured public-education episode summary rather than Klaas's complete scholarship or the underlying historical, scientific, and engineering evidence. The examples illustrate sensitivity and contingency but do not quantify each cause's contribution, establish every counterfactual, or prove that all optimization is harmful. Reduced sole-authorship does not remove responsibility for foreseeable choices or institutional safeguards.
