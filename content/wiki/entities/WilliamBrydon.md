# William Brydon

## Overview
William Brydon was the army doctor whose arrival at Jalalabad became the iconic image of the 1842 retreat from Kabul during the [[FirstAngloAfghanWar|First Anglo-Afghan War]].

## Current Profile
[[88-the-first-anglo-afghan-war-glt5156371854]] recounts Brydon surviving a sword blow because reading material in his cap softened it, then reaching Jalalabad wounded and exhausted. His image later supported the claim that he was the sole survivor of the retreat.

The episode explicitly qualifies that legend. Other soldiers and stragglers survived or emerged later, while hostages also lived. Brydon therefore matters both as an individual survivor and as a case of [[MilitaryDisasterMemory]].

## Key Characteristics
- Army doctor in the retreating Kabul column.
- Reached Jalalabad after the column's destruction.
- Survived a head strike through an improvised layer in his cap, according to the source.
- Became the visual and narrative center of British disaster memory.
- Was not literally the only survivor.

## Evidence
- Survival narrative: [[88-the-first-anglo-afghan-war-glt5156371854]] recounts his wounded arrival and the material in his cap.
- Memory formation: [[88-the-first-anglo-afghan-war-glt5156371854]] connects him to *Remnants of an Army* and the sole-survivor image.
- Qualification: [[88-the-first-anglo-afghan-war-glt5156371854]] names other survivors and later returnees.

## Qualifications
The precise cap anecdote and survivor accounting are transmitted through a popular historical narrative. The episode's firmest contribution is the correction that the iconic image simplified a more complex survival record.

## What Changed
- Added Brydon as both a real survivor and a vehicle for simplified imperial memory.
