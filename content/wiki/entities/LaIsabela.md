# La Isabela

## Overview

La Isabela was the Spanish settlement founded by [[ChristopherColumbus]] on [[Hispaniola]] during the second voyage and presented in the episode as an early demonstration of colonial planning and governance failure.

## Current Profile

After finding La Navidad destroyed, Columbus moved rather than retaliating immediately and founded La Isabela. [[308-columbus-death-in-the-caribbean-part-3-glt3250512968]] emphasizes its poor site and harbor, food shortages, illness, internal conflict, and Columbus's limited capacity as administrator and town planner.

The settlement's weakness mattered beyond local hardship. Expectations of gold and return on investment survived even as productive capacity collapsed, helping push the project toward enslavement, captive export, tribute, and coercive labor. La Isabela is therefore a concrete institutional link between maritime arrival and violent colonial extraction.

## Key Characteristics

- Second-voyage Spanish settlement founded after La Navidad's destruction.
- Poorly sited town with a weak harbor and severe supply problems.
- Community damaged by disease, hunger, faction, and administrative failure.
- Base from which unmet commercial expectations intensified coercive extraction.
- Early test of Columbus's capacity to govern rather than navigate.

## Evidence

- Foundation and site: [[308-columbus-death-in-the-caribbean-part-3-glt3250512968]] describes Columbus's move after La Navidad and historians' judgment of the new town's poor location.
- Administrative failure: [[308-columbus-death-in-the-caribbean-part-3-glt3250512968]] connects the settlement to shortages, illness, conflict with Bernardo Bruil, and collapsing confidence in Columbus.
- Extractive pressure: [[308-columbus-death-in-the-caribbean-part-3-glt3250512968]] links absent gold and investor expectations to the turn toward enslaved captives and tribute.

## Qualifications

The episode gives a high-level narrative rather than an archaeological or comprehensive settlement history. Population, mortality, supply, construction, site-selection motives, and responsibility for particular failures are not independently established.

## What Changed

- Established La Isabela as a distinct early colonial settlement and governance test.
- Connected material settlement failure to the escalation of coercive extraction.
