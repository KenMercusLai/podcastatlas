# 果同学 / Guo Music Therapy Guest

## Overview
果同学 is the music-therapy guest in [[vol-210-wo-zhen-bushi-qiao-songbo-de-jiemi-linchuang-yinyue-zhiliao-si-xia-xuanxue-liaoyu-biaoqian-lvumxyev-szqtsxmqepc6lcg2m0]], a [[ZheBingShuoLaiHuaChang|这病说来话长]] episode explaining clinical music therapy to non-specialist listeners.

## Current Profile
The source presents 果同学 as a graduate student in music therapy at Ewha Womans University and as a practitioner-in-training with experience in [[SouthKorea]], a one-month [[UnitedArabEmirates|UAE]] internship in Sharjah, and domestic hospital settings. Her role in the episode is explanatory: she separates [[ClinicalMusicTherapy|clinical music therapy]] from white noise, spa music, singing bowls, and generalized "music healing," then grounds the distinction in assessment, treatment goals, clinical relationship, music skill, and concrete cases.

Her cases make small clinical changes visible. She describes autistic children learning to sit, attend, ring bells, or notice a therapist's musical following; hearing-impaired children practicing stop-and-go auditory discrimination; older adults singing remembered songs and having their own life stories rewritten into lyrics; and hospitalized children, families, or surgery patients using music to tolerate fear, waiting, or loss of control.

## Key Characteristics
- Explains [[ClinicalMusicTherapy]] as an interdisciplinary clinical profession rather than a relaxation product or performance.
- Uses internship and hospital cases to show how music can serve cognition, language, movement, emotion, and social participation goals.
- Treats music ability as a clinical tool: guitar, voice, percussion, rewritten lyrics, and digital timbre tools are used to support the client rather than display the therapist.
- Emphasizes assessment, individualized goals, caregiver feedback, treatment relationship, and measurable change.
- Keeps severe distress, sleep problems, somatization, disability support, and hospital care inside professional intervention boundaries.
- Positions China-facing music therapy as early in public understanding and institutional adoption compared with the Korean ecosystem she describes.
