## Overview

明定陵 / Ming Dingling is a Ming imperial tomb represented in the current wiki through one popular-history episode as both a major archaeological recovery and a warning about excavation undertaken without adequate conservation readiness.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-311-wenxuejia-guomoruo-ruhe-niangjiu-kaogu-dabeiju-lpq9bozhrdxqn7tiotyx3j-epm9l|Hanji 311]] says opening the underground palace changed its long-isolated environment and exposed vulnerable objects to oxygen. It associates the excavation with damaged paintings, calligraphy, and textiles, as well as later breakage or loss, while also reporting more than three thousand finds including ritual objects, personal goods, precious-metal objects, jade, jewelry, crowns, and phoenix crowns.

The profile therefore resists a simple success-or-disaster binary. Excavation can recover an extraordinary assemblage and still cause irreversible information and material loss when stabilization, documentation, custody, and long-term preservation lag behind exposure. The episode centers [[GuoMoruo|郭沫若]] in this story but does not reconstruct the complete institutional chain needed to assign individual responsibility.

## Key Characteristics

- Ming imperial burial site opened through deliberate archaeological excavation.
- Reported source of more than three thousand artifacts across ritual, daily-life, and luxury categories.
- Presented as a case where environmental change after opening endangered vulnerable materials.
- Used as a cautionary example of conservation capacity lagging behind excavation.
- Institutional authorization, field responsibility, object-by-object loss, and custody history remain outside the current evidence.

## Qualifications

The only current source is a short derivative episode summary, not an excavation dossier or conservation study. Its environmental mechanism, instantaneous-loss language, object count and classifications, missing-object claims, and narrative of Guo Moruo's role require primary documentation before being treated as a complete history. The reported recovery of many artifacts does not cancel preventable loss, and conservation failure does not imply that every recovered item was destroyed.

## What Changed

- Created a bounded profile separating archaeological recovery from conservation readiness and loss.
