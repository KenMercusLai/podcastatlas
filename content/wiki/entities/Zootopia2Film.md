# 《疯狂动物城2》 / Zootopia 2

## Overview
《疯狂动物城2》 / Zootopia 2 is an animated film interpreted in the wiki through three podcast sources: an adult-psychology reading and a two-part investigation of authorship acknowledgment, institutional self-critique, and excluded-group restoration.

## Current Profile
The psychology source uses Gary, Judy, and a Lynxley family member to discuss inherited missions, self-blame, loyalty, progress pressure, and becoming oneself. [[zootopia-exposed-part-one-zootopia-exposed-part-one|Part One]] establishes the authorship mystery: Gary De'Snake resembles [[GaryGoldman]] in name, appearance, manner, Louisiana association, and above all a grievance that powerful actors stole the invention underlying Zootopia. [[zootopia-exposed-part-two-zootopia-exposed-part-two|Part Two]] adds dense Disney self-reference and the return of reptiles, proposing that [[JaredBush]] may be acknowledging Goldman while extending his family's history of defending outsiders.

Together the sources make the film a vehicle for questions about who must carry family history, whether belonging requires sameness, how excluded members are restored, and whether fiction can recognize a claimed originator without legal remedy. None supplies the complete film, screenplay, production archive, or confirmed authorial intent.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses inherited family burden to separate chosen responsibility from imposed over-responsibility.
- Makes staying with family and becoming different competing but potentially autonomous forms of belonging.
- Centers reptiles' exclusion and restoration to the animal community.
- Mirrors a stolen-origin dispute through Gary De'Snake and the Lynxley family's control of credit.
- Supports a theory of corporate self-reference and symbolic acknowledgment of an allegedly wronged creator.
- Leaves competing explanations—coincidence, mockery, apology, or a deniable internal maneuver—open before Part Two advances its preferred theory.

### Exclusion and restoration
- [[zootopia-exposed-part-two-zootopia-exposed-part-two]] says the film restores reptiles to Zootopia and interprets that arc through the Bush family's challenges to racial, religious, and sexual exclusion.

### Authorship and institutional critique
- [[zootopia-exposed-part-one-zootopia-exposed-part-one]] says Gary's family invention was stolen by wealthy “fat cats” and joins that plot to the character's name, resemblance, and Louisiana-associated setting as a possible Goldman allusion.
- [[zootopia-exposed-part-two-zootopia-exposed-part-two]] maps Gary De'Snake's stolen weather-wall invention onto Goldman's dispute and joins that parallel to Lynxley Manor's castle imagery, Disney jokes, Bob Iger's cameo, and Judy's line that the matter matters to Gary.

## Qualifications
All three sources are interpretive podcast notes rather than the film, screenplay, production archive, court record, or direct testimony from the filmmakers. The psychology source explicitly extends themes rather than proving intended meaning. Both Revisionist History sources offer a cumulative but circumstantial Goldman theory; deliberate animated detail does not establish that referent. “Pawbert” in the psychology source and “Paubert” in Part Two remain a source-level spelling difference.
