# Kiku Sharda

## Overview
Kiku Sharda is an Indian actor represented here through a [[TomorrowToday]] interview about family expectations, theatre and screen craft, television comedy, public controversy, grief, and AI-era performance.

## Current Profile
The source presents Sharda as a reserved person whose professional range is enabled by preparation and transformation rather than by an always-extroverted private identity. An MBA and family-business fallback gave him unusual security, but he still needed *Hatim* to demonstrate that acting could supply income and recognition. His durable comic method combines writers' premises with costume, physicality, imagined biography, scene-partner chemistry, timing, and improvisation.

He prefers the broader identity of actor because he wants non-comic and darker work, though he also describes ethical and emotional limits on what he can embody. His AI position is similarly conditional: learn and use the tools, but preserve consent, human authorship, lived struggle, real presence, and the imperfect choices through which performance gains meaning.

## Key Characteristics
- Moves between private introversion and expansive performance through costume, appearance, movement, and character logic.
- Treats education and financial proof as enabling conditions without presenting them as substitutes for acting craft.
- Combines theatre discipline, camera technique, backstory, improvisation, and ensemble response.
- Credits writers and collaborators rather than locating comedy in one performer's jokes or timing.
- Treats laughter as connection and relief while retaining boundaries around deliberate harm and public consequence.
- Accepts AI adaptation while resisting the reduction of acting to reproducible likeness alone.
