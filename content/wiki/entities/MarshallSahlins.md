# Marshall Sahlins

## Overview

Marshall Sahlins was an anthropologist whose interpretation of [[JamesCook]]'s Hawaiian reception links the expedition's timing, route, material appearance, and ceremonies to the Makahiki return of Lono.

## Current Profile

In the supplied episode, Sahlins represents the strongest version of the ritual-structural reading. Cook arrived during Makahiki, circled Hawaii along a route resembling Lono's ritual journey, reached its ceremonial destination, and was received amid white cloth, offerings, anointing, prostration, and language heard as “Orono.” Sahlins treats these correspondences as evidence that Cook entered the role of the returning god.

The episode takes this argument seriously but not as settled fact. [[GananathObeyesekere]] disputes literal god-identification, and the surviving archive does not allow modern readers to translate Hawaiian categories with certainty. Sahlins's value within the wiki is therefore as an influential interpretation of patterned evidence, not as the final recovered voice of every Hawaiian participant.

## Key Characteristics

- Anthropologist associated with a ritual-structural interpretation of Cook's Hawaiian reception.
- Reader of route, timing, symbols, language, and ceremony as a connected Makahiki pattern.
- Central participant in the contested [[CookLonoDebate]].
- Scholar presented through a popular-history summary rather than direct study of his full work.

## Evidence

- Ritual alignment: [[726-the-death-of-captain-cook-massacre-on-the-beach-part-2-glt2770007115]] attributes to Sahlins the connection among Makahiki timing, Cook's circuit, sails and cloth, “Orono,” and the reception at Kealakekua.
- Interpretive dispute: [[726-the-death-of-captain-cook-massacre-on-the-beach-part-2-glt2770007115]] places his argument against Obeyesekere's colonial-projection critique and leaves the precise Hawaiian category unresolved.

## Qualifications

This profile represents Sahlins only within one episode's account of a major anthropological dispute. It does not reconstruct his broader scholarship, the development of his thesis, responses to critics, or the Hawaiian-language and archival evidence in full. Agreement between ritual pattern and voyage sequence supports interpretation but does not prove uniform belief.
