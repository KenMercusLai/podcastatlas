# Rithy Panh / 潘礼德

## Overview

Rithy Panh / 潘礼德 is a Cambodian filmmaker whose documentary practice confronts Khmer Rouge violence through testimony, propaganda footage, places, clay figures, reenactment, and explicit attention to how historical images are made or missing.

## Current Profile

[[20-shiji-zhuanzhi-shi-dianying-xian-qingsuan-yi-bufen-gkwriaiosoyxcck-awttzvx8]] presents Panh as a central model for cinema's role in incomplete transitional justice. Works including *S-21: The Khmer Rouge Killing Machine*, *The Missing Picture*, and *Meeting with Pol Pot* do not treat an archive as a transparent store of facts. They place testimony beside perpetrator behavior, propaganda, physical sites, constructed models, and reenactment so that each form reveals the limits of the others.

His method retains moral judgment about mass violence while resisting a frictionless good-versus-evil narrative. By showing the filmmaker's organization of material, it turns documentary from passive storage into an inquiry into how people remember, perform, deny, and inherit violence.

## Key Characteristics

- Uses multiple evidence forms to address severe gaps in surviving historical imagery.
- Makes construction and directorial intervention visible rather than claiming unmediated access to the past.
- Places archive, testimony, propaganda, material objects, and reenactment in critical relation.
- Preserves clear judgment about Khmer Rouge violence without eliminating ambiguity in memory and representation.
- Treats documentary as both historical preservation and active questioning.
