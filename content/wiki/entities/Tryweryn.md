# Tryweryn

## Overview

Tryweryn is the Welsh valley and community flooded for a reservoir serving Liverpool, remembered through “Cofiwch Dryweryn” as a symbol of lost landscape, Welsh-language community, and constitutional inequality.

## Current Profile

The episode uses Tryweryn to make modern Welsh-English asymmetry concrete. Most Welsh MPs opposed the reservoir plan, but the measure passed because English MPs greatly outnumbered them. The resulting memory does not require an intentional English campaign to destroy Wales; it shows how a larger nation's ordinary parliamentary weight can override a smaller nation's collective objection.

“Remember Tryweryn” consequently carries several losses at once: home, land, community, language, and political efficacy. It turns a specific infrastructure decision into a durable warning within Welsh national culture.

## Key Characteristics

- Welsh-speaking community displaced by reservoir construction for Liverpool.
- Decision opposed by most Welsh MPs in the source's account.
- Example of demographic and parliamentary power overriding territorial opposition.
- Symbol joining landscape loss, community loss, language pressure, and national inequality.
- Preserved through the “Cofiwch Dryweryn” remembrance slogan.

## Evidence
