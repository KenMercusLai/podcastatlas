# John Puskas

## Overview

John Puskas is the cardiac surgeon featured in [[wolfgang-s-law-part-two|Wolfgang's Law - Part Two]] as an advocate and practitioner of minimally invasive coronary-bypass surgery.

## Current Profile

Puskas presents smaller-incision, camera-guided, beating-heart bypass as potentially easier on selected patients but substantially harder for practitioners to adopt. The episode uses his account to show that technical innovation can require an entire team's retraining, retention, coordination, and identity change rather than only a surgeon learning a new maneuver.

## Key Characteristics

- Advocates minimally invasive bypass around patient recovery and complication burden rather than novelty alone.
- Describes the method as a team sport requiring tightly coordinated specialized roles.
- Treats professional retraining as an ethical obligation when better patient care may be available.
- Expects younger surgeons to be more receptive than established peers to the new operating model.

## Evidence

### Clinical and organizational case

- [[wolfgang-s-law-part-two]] attributes to Puskas shorter hospitalization and recovery, and lower infection, bleeding, and intensive-care burden, while emphasizing the coordination needed to operate through small access points.

### Adoption judgment

- [[wolfgang-s-law-part-two]] reports his estimate that only two or three percent of US bypass operations use the minimally invasive method and his decision to focus more on younger surgeons than on persuading peers.

The supplied summary does not include comparative studies, patient-selection criteria, complication definitions, center volume, learning curves, or Puskas's full professional record. The clinical claims remain episode-attributed and do not establish universal superiority.
