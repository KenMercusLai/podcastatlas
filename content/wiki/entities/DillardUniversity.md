# Dillard University

## Overview

Dillard University is a historically Black liberal arts university in New Orleans presented by [[project-dillard-project-dillard|Project Dillard]] as a case where access, student development, and Black STEM attainment are poorly represented by conventional college rankings.

## Current Profile

The episode describes a small institution serving an overwhelmingly Black student body with high Pell Grant eligibility, low average family income, and substantial first-generation enrollment. These starting conditions expose a measurement problem: completion, test-score, alumni-giving, and reputation measures partly reflect the resources students and institutions already possess.

Dillard's reported alternative strength is developmental. Students describe advisers and faculty noticing absence, peers helping one another through bereavement, and direct access to president [[WalterKimbrough]]. The episode also reports an unusually strong record of producing Black physics graduates. Together these claims present support relationships as educational infrastructure rather than a soft supplement to instruction.

[[lord-of-the-rankings-lord-of-the-rankings|Lord of the Rankings]] supplies the preceding reputational frame. It reports Dillard's peer-assessment score as 2.6 and presents Kimbrough's view that the formula measures privilege more readily than the institution's work. Its comparison with higher HBCU bond spreads does not prove why Dillard received that score, but it shows why inherited institutional legitimacy can have consequences beyond a symbolic rank.

The episode's counterfactual Dillard becomes wealthy, selective, smaller, and highly ranked by changing its financial and student profile. That institution would no longer serve the same mission. Dillard therefore anchors the difference between selecting students with strong conventional inputs and helping students develop capability under less advantaged conditions.

## Key Characteristics

- Historically Black liberal arts institution with an access-oriented mission.
- Serves many Pell-eligible, first-generation, and lower-income students.
- Uses close faculty, adviser, peer, and leadership relationships as developmental support.
- Reports strong production of Black undergraduate physics graduates relative to its size.
- Faces ranking incentives that would reward greater wealth, selectivity, and exclusion.

## Evidence

- Access mission - [[project-dillard-project-dillard]] reports that about three-quarters of students were Pell-eligible, roughly one-third were first generation, average family income was slightly above $30,000, and virtually all students were Black.
- Developmental support - [[project-dillard-project-dillard]] describes faculty contact after missed classes, attentive advisers, peer support after bereavement, and direct presidential access.
- STEM outcomes - [[project-dillard-project-dillard]] reports 13 Black physics graduates in 2019 and 35 over the preceding decade, while preserving those figures as episode-attributed.
- Ranking conflict - [[project-dillard-project-dillard]] shows a simulated ranking climb produced by wealth, test scores, shrinking enrollment, and other ranked inputs rather than a demonstrated change in teaching.
- Reputational constraint - [[lord-of-the-rankings-lord-of-the-rankings]] reports a 2.6 peer-assessment score and Kimbrough's argument that this makes a top ranking effectively unreachable.

## Qualifications

This profile rests on two aligned structured podcast summaries, not Dillard's institutional records, audited outcomes, student-level comparison data, rater-level peer assessments, or current ranking methodology. The episodes' comparisons do not isolate Dillard's causal effect from selection, field mix, student goals, local conditions, or other institutions' practices. Strong physics outcomes and supportive anecdotes are important signals but do not establish performance across every program or student group, while the bond-market analogy does not directly explain Dillard's rank.

## What Changed

- Created a focused profile of Dillard's access mission, developmental support, and reported Black-physics outcomes.
- Distinguished institutional value added from the wealth and student inputs rewarded by rankings.
- Added the earlier peer-assessment constraint and kept the HBCU financing comparison explicitly analogical.
