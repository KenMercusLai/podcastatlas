# Rizzio Murder

## Overview

The Rizzio murder was the 9 March 1566 killing of [[DavidRizzio]] inside Holyrood and an attempted palace coup against the government of the pregnant [[MaryQueenOfScots|Mary, Queen of Scots]].

## Current Profile

The conspiracy joined [[HenryStuartLordDarnley|Darnley]] to [[JamesDouglasEarlOfMorton|Morton]], [[WilliamMaitlandOfLethington|Maitland]], and the exiles defeated in the [[ChaseaboutRaid]]. Darnley expected support for coronation and fuller royal authority; the lords expected pardon, return, and protection of Protestant power. Rizzio's foreignness, Catholicism, and closeness to Mary supplied a convenient explanation for Darnley's reversal and a vulnerable target.

Armed men entered Mary's private rooms during supper, restrained her, threatened her pregnancy, seized Rizzio, and killed him nearby. Darnley's dagger and signed bond marked his participation. The conspirators then guarded Mary and accused her circle of Catholic restoration, but did not secure the settlement they wanted.

Mary reopened communications, manipulated Darnley's fear of his allies, and escaped with him to Dunbar. Her selective decision to pardon Moray's earlier rebels but outlaw Rizzio's killers split the coalition and restored royal control, while leaving Darnley isolated and exposed as a betrayer.

## Key Characteristics

- Palace murder used as the operational center of a wider political coup.
- Coalition between a frustrated royal consort and previously exiled Protestant lords.
- Scapegoating of a foreign Catholic secretary through unsupported sexual and political allegations.
- Direct armed coercion against a pregnant sovereign inside her private rooms.
- Failed seizure of power reversed by Mary's escape, coalition-splitting, and loyalist mobilization.
- Betrayal that supplied later motives for the [[KirkOFieldMurder|Kirk o' Field murder]].

## Evidence

- Conspiracy and bond: [[587-mary-queen-of-scots-murder-most-foul-part-4-glt2035316933]] connects Darnley's royal demands, the exiles' restoration, Morton's organization, Maitland's propaganda, and the signed commitment.
- Killing and coercion: [[587-mary-queen-of-scots-murder-most-foul-part-4-glt2035316933]] grounds the armed entry, Mary's restraint, Rizzio's seizure and stabbing, and the threat to her pregnancy.
- Failed aftermath: [[587-mary-queen-of-scots-murder-most-foul-part-4-glt2035316933]] follows Mary's messages, deception of Darnley, escape to Dunbar, selective pardons, and punishment of the killers.
- Later motive: [[588-mary-queen-of-scots-the-mystery-of-the-exploding-mansion-part-5-glt2383249995]] shows Darnley's abandonment of the conspirators becoming part of Morton's motive for his own murder.

## Qualifications

The source clearly places Darnley, Morton, and Maitland within the plot, but exact authorship, English involvement, individual actions, private motives, dialogue, and the reported 57 wounds remain source-scoped. Political purpose does not reduce Rizzio's death to symbolism or erase personal hatred.

## What Changed

- Established the murder as a failed coup whose reversal restored Mary temporarily while creating the revenge coalition later directed at Darnley.

## Relationships

- [[DavidRizzio]] - secretary selected as the coup's scapegoat and murder victim.
- [[MaryQueenOfScots|Mary, Queen of Scots]] - sovereign physically threatened and temporarily confined.
- [[HenryStuartLordDarnley|Henry Stuart / Lord Darnley]] - husband whose signed involvement and later betrayal isolated him.
- [[JamesDouglasEarlOfMorton|James Douglas / Earl of Morton]] - organizer driven into exile after the coup failed.
- [[WilliamMaitlandOfLethington|William Maitland of Lethington]] - political operator who helped construct the case against Rizzio.
- [[JamesHepburnEarlOfBothwell|James Hepburn / Earl of Bothwell]] - loyalist who escaped Holyrood and mobilized at Dunbar.
- [[KirkOFieldMurder|Kirk o' Field murder]] - later conspiracy partly enabled by Darnley's betrayal of the Rizzio plotters.
