## Overview

Dongqiang / Eastern Qiang are the frontier group whose late-Han wiki evidence now begins with [[ZhangHuanLateHan|张奂]] separating Qiang groups from a [[SouthernXiongnu|Southern Xiongnu]] rebellion in 155 CE, then runs through the later 168-169 CE Eastern Qiang suppression, policy dispute, campaign, settlement, and ethical critique.

## Current Profile

Although Hanji 840 is appended to the source inventory by ingest order, it now supplies the earliest current chronological layer. In [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-840-yanguang-changyuan-de-ren-you-duo-lihai-lvzfg8zbp0nugjg0iybgukz-cxgv|Hanji 840]], Eastern Qiang groups initially respond to a Southern Xiongnu rebellion. Zhang Huan arrives with only 200 soldiers, strengthens key points, recruits more men, and sends Wang Wei to persuade Eastern Qiang groups away from the Xiongnu alignment. The episode then has Qiang chiefs cooperate against the Xiongnu side and later offer gifts that Zhang refuses. This makes Eastern Qiang visible not only as a later suppression target, but as a trust and alliance-management field.

The later 168-169 evidence begins with [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-880-weihe-zhichang-laoyoutiao-rezhong-yu-chengbing-lnzfgwghrxrpltsmb8godap-nyzw|Hanji 880]]: after Western Qiang are described as pacified, [[XianlingQiang|先零羌]] and other Eastern Qiang groups are still not submitted. [[DuanJiongLateHan|段熲]] acknowledges Zhang Huan's fear that renewed attack could unsettle already-submitted Qiang, but argues that Eastern Qiang groups repeatedly surrender under pressure and rebel after Han armies withdraw. He turns that diagnosis into a [[BudgetedFrontierSuppression|budgeted suppression plan]] with cavalry, infantry, vehicle units, a multi-year duration, and an estimated cash cost.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-881-shi-shui-zai-fa-zhanzheng-hengcai-lipglslijnbp0cuqfpps88q5wit6|Hanji 881]] supplies the first battle-and-reward continuation: Duan defeats Xianling Qiang forces at [[FengyishanBattleLateHan|冯翊山之战]], and [[EmpressDowagerDouMiao|窦妙 / 窦皇太后]] defers final reward until the Eastern Qiang are fully pacified while still giving Duan military funding and the `破羌将军` title. The page treats that as a campaign-context connection, not as a reason to merge all Qiang labels.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-883-laoban-yao-fansi-ruhe-bimian-gaoguan-neihong-lph-fycnqas6hztjq1jbsisvylid|Hanji 883]] then shows remaining Qiang forces as a policy problem after military defeat: Zhang Huan argues they are hard to eliminate and should be induced to surrender, while Duan treats them as a recurrent frontier threat that must be crushed before they recover. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-891-gao-duanwei-de-muqin-shi-jia-de-dinghai-shenzhen-lmqkzsnhtiwc9wzcee869kuu57m5|Hanji 891]] shows a later surrender mission that Duan rejects on food, timing, and reliability grounds; the episode then follows a Han surprise attack, Qiang retreat, valley defense, and reported mass killing. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-892-xiu-zhiyu-guren-youshenme-jiejing-lnobbkp6mj4xyb9jeuqsiuqogct|Hanji 892]] gives the post-campaign settlement and ethical interpretation: some surrendered people are resettled, while [[SimaGuang|司马光]] and the host frame Qiang rebellion as socially caused by harsh commandery exploitation rather than inherent hostility to Han rule.

## Key Characteristics

- Frontier population whose role shifts between rebel-aligned group, potential ally, surrender-policy object, and military target depending on Han governance and command credibility.
- Early 155 CE case where Zhang Huan separates Eastern Qiang groups from Southern Xiongnu rebels and turns them into cooperative pressure.
- Object of later budgeted hard suppression that defines troops, duration, terrain assumptions, and state cost before the campaign.
- Campaign frame that connects the earlier Fengyishan victory to a promised final reward after full pacification.
- Policy-dispute object between surrender-first conciliation and hard-line elimination.
- Surrender-policy test case where hunger, pressure, and provisioning needs make submission appear potentially unstable.
- Subject of Sima Guang's anti-extermination critique and of the host's root-cause reading of local official extraction.

## Evidence

- Early alliance-management case: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-840-yanguang-changyuan-de-ren-you-duo-lihai-lvzfg8zbp0nugjg0iybgukz-cxgv|Hanji 840]] says Eastern Qiang groups respond to a Southern Xiongnu rebellion before Zhang Huan sends Wang Wei to persuade them away from that alignment.
- Trust after Zhang Huan's victory: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-840-yanguang-changyuan-de-ren-you-duo-lihai-lvzfg8zbp0nugjg0iybgukz-cxgv|Hanji 840]] says Qiang chiefs cooperate in the attack and later respect Zhang enough to offer horses and gold earrings, which he refuses.
- Pre-campaign budget and approval: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-880-weihe-zhichang-laoyoutiao-rezhong-yu-chengbing-lnzfgwghrxrpltsmb8godap-nyzw|Hanji 880]] says Emperor Huan approves Duan Jiong's argument that remaining Eastern Qiang groups can be suppressed with a defined mix of cavalry, infantry, vehicle units, time, and cash.
- Recurrent-surrender diagnosis: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-880-weihe-zhichang-laoyoutiao-rezhong-yu-chengbing-lnzfgwghrxrpltsmb8godap-nyzw|Hanji 880]] presents Duan's claim that repeated temporary submission followed by renewed rebellion makes conciliation unreliable.
- Early campaign and reward horizon: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-881-shi-shui-zai-fa-zhanzheng-hengcai-lipglslijnbp0cuqfpps88q5wit6|Hanji 881]] places Duan Jiong's Fengyishan victory over Xianling Qiang forces inside an Eastern Qiang pacification horizon by having Dou Miao delay final reward until all Eastern Qiang are pacified.
- Policy split before the later campaign: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-883-laoban-yao-fansi-ruhe-bimian-gaoguan-neihong-lph-fycnqas6hztjq1jbsisvylid|Hanji 883]] says Zhang Huan urges surrender and peace because residual Qiang groups are hard to eliminate, while Duan Jiong argues they are already weakened enough to suppress.
- Surrender-risk setup: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-891-gao-duanwei-de-muqin-shi-jia-de-dinghai-shenzhen-lmqkzsnhtiwc9wzcee869kuu57m5|Hanji 891]] says Feng Chan is sent to persuade remaining Qiang forces to surrender, while Duan Jiong argues that hunger and local provisioning costs make the surrender path unreliable.
- Tactical defeat: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-891-gao-duanwei-de-muqin-shi-jia-de-dinghai-shenzhen-lmqkzsnhtiwc9wzcee869kuu57m5|Hanji 891]] describes a surprise cavalry attack, Qiang retreat to Shehu Valley, east-west Han pressure, and reported killing of more than 19,000 Qiang.
- Military pacification, resettlement, and critique: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-892-xiu-zhiyu-guren-youshenme-jiejing-lnobbkp6mj4xyb9jeuqsiuqogct|Hanji 892]] says the rebellion is considered pacified after Duan's campaign, four thousand surrendered Qiang are settled across Anding, Hanyang, and Longxi, and Sima Guang rejects treating Qiang and other frontier peoples as killable animals.

## Qualifications

This page is not an ethnic history of Qiang peoples. It records only the Eastern Qiang role in six short Hanji episodes, all mediated through the host's summary of the Zizhi Tongjian tradition. Hanji 840 contains unstable ASR renderings for some names and places, so the page uses only the clear source-level relationship: Eastern Qiang groups are separated from the Southern Xiongnu side and later become a trust-repair field under Zhang Huan. Hanji 880 and Hanji 881 connect the Xianling Qiang and Eastern Qiang campaign fields, but the named [[XianlingQiang|先零羌]] opponent still keeps its own page. Duan Jiong's troop, duration, and cost figures are preserved as his court proposal, not treated as audited campaign accounts.

- Added Hanji 840 as the earliest current Eastern Qiang layer, before the 168-169 Duan Jiong campaign material.
- Reframed Eastern Qiang as an alliance-management and trust-repair field, not only a later suppression target.
