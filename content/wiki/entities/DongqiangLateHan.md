# 东羌 / Eastern Qiang (late Han)

## Overview

Dongqiang / Eastern Qiang are the frontier group whose late-Han conflict now begins with Hanji 880's pre-campaign budget and approval, then runs through Hanji 881's Fengyishan reward horizon, Hanji 883's policy dispute, Hanji 891's tactical campaign narrative, and Hanji 892's critique of border governance.

## Current Profile

The current evidence is source-scoped to Hanji 880, Hanji 881, Hanji 883, Hanji 891, and Hanji 892. Hanji 880 adds the earliest layer: after Western Qiang are described as pacified, [[XianlingQiang|先零羌]] and other Eastern Qiang groups are still not submitted. [[DuanJiongLateHan|段熲]] acknowledges [[ZhangHuanLateHan|张奂]]'s fear that renewed attack could unsettle already-submitted Qiang, but argues that Eastern Qiang groups repeatedly surrender under pressure and rebel after Han armies withdraw. He turns that diagnosis into a [[BudgetedFrontierSuppression|budgeted suppression plan]] with cavalry, infantry, vehicle units, a multi-year duration, and an estimated cash cost.

Hanji 881 supplies the first battle-and-reward continuation: Duan defeats Xianling Qiang forces at [[FengyishanBattleLateHan|冯翊山之战]], and [[EmpressDowagerDouMiao|窦妙 / 窦皇太后]] defers final reward until the Eastern Qiang are fully pacified while still giving Duan military funding and the `破羌将军` title. The page treats that as a campaign-context connection, not as a reason to merge all Qiang labels.

Hanji 883 then shows remaining Qiang forces as a policy problem after military defeat: Zhang Huan argues they are hard to eliminate and should be induced to surrender, while Duan treats them as a recurrent frontier threat that must be crushed before they recover. Hanji 891 shows a later surrender mission that Duan rejects on food, timing, and reliability grounds; the episode then follows a Han surprise attack, Qiang retreat, valley defense, and reported mass killing. Hanji 892 gives the post-campaign settlement and ethical interpretation: some surrendered people are resettled, while [[SimaGuang|司马光]] and the host frame Qiang rebellion as socially caused by harsh commandery exploitation rather than inherent hostility to Han rule.

## Key Characteristics

- Late-Han frontier population treated in the sources as participants in the Eastern Qiang rebellion.
- Object of a budgeted hard-suppression strategy that defines troops, duration, terrain assumptions, and state cost before the campaign.
- Campaign frame that connects the earlier Fengyishan victory to a promised final reward after full pacification.
- Policy-dispute object between surrender-first conciliation and hard-line elimination.
- Military target of Duan Jiong's pursuit, surprise attack, valley encirclement, and later court reward.
- Surrender-policy test case where hunger, pressure, and provisioning needs make submission appear potentially unstable.
- Subject of Sima Guang's anti-extermination critique and of the host's root-cause reading of local official extraction.

## Evidence

- Pre-campaign budget and approval: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-880-weihe-zhichang-laoyoutiao-rezhong-yu-chengbing-lnzfgwghrxrpltsmb8godap-nyzw|Hanji 880]] says Emperor Huan approves Duan Jiong's argument that remaining Eastern Qiang groups can be suppressed with a defined mix of cavalry, infantry, vehicle units, time, and cash.
- Recurrent-surrender diagnosis: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-880-weihe-zhichang-laoyoutiao-rezhong-yu-chengbing-lnzfgwghrxrpltsmb8godap-nyzw|Hanji 880]] presents Duan's claim that repeated temporary submission followed by renewed rebellion makes conciliation unreliable.
- Early campaign and reward horizon: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-881-shi-shui-zai-fa-zhanzheng-hengcai-lipglslijnbp0cuqfpps88q5wit6|Hanji 881]] places Duan Jiong's Fengyishan victory over Xianling Qiang forces inside an Eastern Qiang pacification horizon by having Dou Miao delay final reward until all Eastern Qiang are pacified.
- Policy split before the later campaign: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-883-laoban-yao-fansi-ruhe-bimian-gaoguan-neihong-lph-fycnqas6hztjq1jbsisvylid|Hanji 883]] says Zhang Huan urges surrender and peace because residual Qiang groups are hard to eliminate, while Duan Jiong argues they are already weakened enough to suppress.
- Surrender-risk setup: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-891-gao-duanwei-de-muqin-shi-jia-de-dinghai-shenzhen-lmqkzsnhtiwc9wzcee869kuu57m5|Hanji 891]] says Feng Chan is sent to persuade remaining Qiang forces to surrender, while Duan Jiong argues that hunger and local provisioning costs make the surrender path unreliable.
- Tactical defeat: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-891-gao-duanwei-de-muqin-shi-jia-de-dinghai-shenzhen-lmqkzsnhtiwc9wzcee869kuu57m5|Hanji 891]] describes a surprise cavalry attack, Qiang retreat to Shehu Valley, east-west Han pressure, and reported killing of more than 19,000 Qiang.
- Military pacification, resettlement, and critique: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-892-xiu-zhiyu-guren-youshenme-jiejing-lnobbkp6mj4xyb9jeuqsiuqogct|Hanji 892]] says the rebellion is considered pacified after Duan's campaign, four thousand surrendered Qiang are settled across Anding, Hanyang, and Longxi, and Sima Guang rejects treating Qiang and other frontier peoples as killable animals.

## Qualifications

This page is not an ethnic history of Qiang peoples. It records only the Eastern Qiang role in five short Hanji episodes, all mediated through the host's summary of the Zizhi Tongjian tradition. Hanji 880 and Hanji 881 connect the Xianling Qiang and Eastern Qiang campaign fields, but the named [[XianlingQiang|先零羌]] opponent still keeps its own page. Duan Jiong's troop, duration, and cost figures are preserved as his court proposal, not treated as audited campaign accounts.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 880's pre-campaign budget, Emperor Huan approval, and recurrent-surrender diagnosis before the Hanji 881 battle continuation.
- Reframed Eastern Qiang as the object of a force-time-cost proposal before they become the reward horizon after Fengyishan.
- Clarified that Hanji 880 and Hanji 881 connect Xianling Qiang to the Eastern Qiang campaign field without merging the labels.
- Preserved Hanji 892's ethical and root-cause qualification around later military success.
