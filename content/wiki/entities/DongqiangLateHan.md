# 东羌 / Eastern Qiang (late Han)

## Overview

Dongqiang / Eastern Qiang are the frontier group whose 169 CE pacification anchors Hanji 892's critique of late-Han border governance.

## Current Profile

Current evidence is narrow and source-scoped to Hanji 892. The episode presents the Eastern Qiang as a rebel frontier population defeated by [[DuanJiongLateHan|段熲]] after a long, costly campaign, with some surrendered people resettled in Anding, Hanyang, and Longxi. More important for the wiki, the host and [[SimaGuang|司马光]] frame their rebellion as socially caused by harsh commandery exploitation rather than as inherent hostility to Han rule.

## Key Characteristics

- Late-Han frontier population treated in the source as participants in the 169 CE Dongqiang rebellion.
- Military target of Duan Jiong's long campaign and postwar court reward.
- Subject of Sima Guang's anti-extermination critique: non-Han peoples still value life and safety.
- Example of rebellion rooted in local official extraction and harsh governance.
- Memory field in which Duan Jiong's name still carries later fear or prestige through the Jia Xu anecdote.

## Evidence

- Military pacification: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-892-xiu-zhiyu-guren-youshenme-jiejing-lnobbkp6mj4xyb9jeuqsiuqogct|Hanji 892]] says the Eastern Qiang rebellion is considered pacified after Duan Jiong's campaign and Feng Chan's receipt of surrendered Qiang.
- Resettlement: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-892-xiu-zhiyu-guren-youshenme-jiejing-lnobbkp6mj4xyb9jeuqsiuqogct|Hanji 892]] says four thousand surrendered Qiang are settled across Anding, Hanyang, and Longxi.
- Ethical critique: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-892-xiu-zhiyu-guren-youshenme-jiejing-lnobbkp6mj4xyb9jeuqsiuqogct|Hanji 892]] uses Sima Guang's comment to reject treating Qiang and other frontier peoples as animals or grasses to be killed without moral distinction.
- Governance cause: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-892-xiu-zhiyu-guren-youshenme-jiejing-lnobbkp6mj4xyb9jeuqsiuqogct|Hanji 892]] attributes rebellion to long-accumulated resentment from commandery exploitation and harsh administration.
- Reputation afterlife: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-892-xiu-zhiyu-guren-youshenme-jiejing-lnobbkp6mj4xyb9jeuqsiuqogct|Hanji 892]] says Jia Xu later survives Qiang robbers by claiming connection to Duan Gong.

## Qualifications

This page is not an ethnic history of Qiang peoples. It records only the Eastern Qiang role in Hanji 892's 169 CE military and governance argument. Later Western Qiang or Xianling Qiang pages should stay separate unless a source explicitly connects those groups.
