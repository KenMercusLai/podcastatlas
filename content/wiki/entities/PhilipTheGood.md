# Philip the Good

## Overview

Philip the Good is the Burgundian duke whose political alignment helped make the [[TreatyOfTroyes|Treaty of Troyes]] possible and whose later court employed [[JanVanEyck|Jan van Eyck]] across painting, diplomacy, mapping, and spectacle.

## Current Profile

After [[JohnTheFearless|John the Fearless]] was killed at Montereau, Philip treated punishment of the Dauphin as compatible with alliance with [[HenryVOfEngland|Henry V]]. By December 1419, the two rulers publicly framed cooperation as a means to punish the killing and restore French peace.

Philip's alignment supplied crucial French elite support for a settlement that displaced [[CharlesVIIOfFrance|the Dauphin]] and redirected succession through Henry and [[CatherineOfValois|Catherine]].

The Van Eyck source extends his profile from war and dynastic settlement into court culture. Philip recruited Van Eyck into Burgundian service, used him on the Portuguese marriage embassy, received a portrait of Isabella of Portugal, and involved him in maps and elaborate banquets. The court's Order of the Golden Fleece also supplies one of the episode's secondary readings of the [[GhentAltarpiece]].

## Key Characteristics

- Burgundian successor shaped by his father's killing.
- Political opponent of the Dauphin after Montereau.
- French princely ally lending legitimacy and leverage to Henry.
- Participant in the coalition leading to the Treaty of Troyes.
- Patron whose court integrated art with marriage diplomacy, mapping, banquets, and dynastic display.
- Founder of the Order of the Golden Fleece in the episode's symbolic account of Burgundian culture.

## Evidence

- Succession and grievance: [[490-hundred-years-war-england-triumphant-part-4-glt2692942762]] says Philip moved toward England after his father's death.
- Public coalition: [[490-hundred-years-war-england-triumphant-part-4-glt2692942762]] describes Philip and Henry presenting their cause as punishment and peace restoration.
- Treaty outcome: [[490-hundred-years-war-england-triumphant-part-4-glt2692942762]] connects the alliance to the Dauphin's disinheritance.
- Artistic service: [[271-belgium-historys-greatest-artist-glt1331750756]] places Van Eyck in Philip's service as painter, traveler, marriage envoy, mapmaker, and spectacle designer.
- Court symbolism: [[271-belgium-historys-greatest-artist-glt1331750756]] links the Order of the Golden Fleece and Burgundian display to a layered reading of the Ghent Altarpiece.

## Qualifications

The war source explains Philip's immediate alignment but does not cover the later limits of Anglo-Burgundian cooperation. The art source is centered on Van Eyck rather than a full study of Philip's court. Revenge, factional interest, peace rhetoric, patronage, and cultural ambition should not be reduced to a single motive, while the Golden Fleece reading remains interpretive.
