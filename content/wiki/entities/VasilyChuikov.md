# Vasily Chuikov

## Overview

Vasily Chuikov was the Soviet 62nd Army commander who took charge inside the [[BattleOfStalingrad]] and organized a ruthless close-range defence intended to survive German firepower and attrition until time and winter altered the campaign.

## Current Profile

[[214-the-battle-of-stalingrad-part-1-glt4564292022]] introduces Chuikov as a committed communist, Russian Civil War veteran, former military attaché in China, and hard commander. He assumed command of the 62nd Army on 12 September 1942 under orders to hold Stalingrad or die, with Andrey Yeryomenko and Nikita Khrushchev overseeing the defence.

The episode associates Chuikov with fighting at extreme proximity: rooms, floors, cellars, rubble, and improvised strongpoints narrowed the usefulness of German aircraft and artillery. Soviet positions remained linked to reinforcements and supplies crossing the Volga even when the bridgehead was only hundreds of metres deep. His reported phrase “time is blood” captures an attritional logic in which every day of survival cost lives but brought colder weather and further German exhaustion.

## Key Characteristics

- Took command of the Soviet 62nd Army during the opening urban crisis in September 1942.
- Was described as politically committed, experienced, physically forceful, and ruthless.
- Used close-range combat and ruined urban terrain to reduce German firepower advantages.
- Held a shallow Volga bridgehead dependent on concealed, repaired, and heavily contested crossings.
- Accepted severe attrition to deny a quick German victory and gain time for changing operational conditions.
