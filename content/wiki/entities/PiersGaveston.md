## Overview

Piers Gaveston was the prominent favourite of [[EdwardIIOfEngland|Edward II of England]] whose courtly elevation became a focus of aristocratic hostility.

## Current Profile

[[97-top-ten-mistresses-glt8855582591|Top Ten Mistresses]] makes Gaveston its male entry while refusing certainty about whether his relationship with Edward II was sexual. His political importance lies in visible favour, display, and status: royal intimacy elevated him within court and helped turn resentment among established magnates into a wider conflict over access, rank, and kingship.

## Key Characteristics

- Royal favourite elevated through exceptional closeness to Edward II.
- Showy court figure whose conduct and standing angered traditional aristocrats.
- Male case that exposes the instability of the episode's “mistress” category.
- Politically consequential whether or not the relationship was sexual.

## Evidence

- Royal closeness: [[97-top-ten-mistresses-glt8855582591]] presents Gaveston as Edward II's favourite and a conspicuous presence at court.
- Aristocratic reaction: [[97-top-ten-mistresses-glt8855582591]] connects his elevation and style to baronial anger.
- Sexual uncertainty: [[97-top-ten-mistresses-glt8855582591]] explicitly says the exact sexual character of the relationship cannot be known.

## Qualifications

The episode supplies a compressed portrait rather than a full political biography. It does not reconstruct Gaveston's offices, exile, military role, execution, or the separate interests of Edward's opponents. Political intimacy should not be used as proof of a sexual relationship, and baronial conflict cannot be reduced to private jealousy alone.

## What Changed
