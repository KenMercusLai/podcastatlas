# 《茧》 / Cocoon (Fei Xiaotong)

## Overview

[[CocoonFeiXiaotong|《茧》]] is an English novella by [[FeiXiaotong|费孝通]], rediscovered in an LSE archive in 2016 and later translated into Chinese, that fictionalizes Jiangnan rural crisis, silk production, factory organization, intellectual debate, and women's social work.

## Current Profile

The novel shares the 1930s rural crisis represented in left-wing literature but does not resolve it through the same revolutionary trajectory. Its factory tries to use technology and organization while returning gains to farmers, making the book a test of [[RuralIndustryLedImprovement]] rather than a simple celebration of industrial capital.

The episode argues against treating the work as merely a fictional [[JiangcunEconomy|《江村经济》]]. Its characters and institutions draw on several places, family experience, industrial experiments, social-work careers, and intellectual controversies. It therefore belongs between literary camps as well as between fiction, social inquiry, and reform imagination.

## Key Characteristics

- Represents commercialized Jiangnan sericulture as exposed to credit, finance, industrial processing, and world demand.
- Acknowledges rural breakdown while imagining cooperative and distributive reform rather than only revolution.
- Combines village, town, factory, finance, craft, romance, and intellectual life in one social field.
- Draws on multiple real institutions and biographies without permitting a one-to-one prototype reading.
- Uses women's work, ambition, disappointment, and self-reflection as substantive parts of modernization.

## Qualifications

The source does not provide the manuscript, publication scholarship, or archival correspondence directly. The London literary agent's role is incompletely verified, and proposed links between characters and [[FeiDasheng|费达生]], Zhu Yubao, or private romantic relationships are hypotheses rather than established identifications.

## What Changed

- Created a profile treating the novella as a composite literary inquiry rather than a fictionalized duplicate of one ethnography.
- Preserved uncertainty around publishing causation and real-person prototypes.
