# National Convention

## Overview

The National Convention was the republican assembly that tried [[LouisXVI|Louis XVI]] and voted on his guilt, a proposed popular referendum, punishment, and reprieve.

## Current Profile

In this source the Convention appears as court, legislature, and arena of revolutionary legitimacy at once. Its public roll-call votes and ritual treatment of Louis asserted equal republican authority, but petitions, factional suspicion, correspondence disclosures, and the narrow and contested sentencing result show that the judgment was never merely routine adjudication.

## Key Characteristics

- It summoned Louis as “Louis Capet” and withheld the bodily courtesies associated with kingship.
- It allowed legal representation and heard a defence plea for mercy.
- It found Louis guilty without a vote against guilt, though abstentions were recorded.
- It rejected the Girondin proposal to refer the verdict to the people.
- It debated death, exile, imprisonment, transportation, and forced labor before imposing death.
- It rejected both delay and reprieve while granting access to a priest and a final family meeting.

## Qualifications

- The episode does not supply a comprehensive institutional history or the full voting record.
- “The Convention” should not imply unanimity about procedure, sentence, mercy, or the execution's meaning.
- Vote totals and the “one vote” description remain bounded to the source's presentation.

## What Changed

- The Convention now has a canonical page focused on its simultaneous judicial, legislative, ritual, and factional roles in the regicide.
