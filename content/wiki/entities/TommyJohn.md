# Tommy John

## Overview

Tommy John is a Major League Baseball pitcher whose successful ligament reconstruction became the episode's standard case for accepted restorative sports medicine.

## Current Profile

[[the-standard-case-the-standard-case|The Standard Case]] recounts John's 1974 ulnar collateral ligament tear, then commonly a career-ending injury. Surgeon Frank Jobe reconstructed the elbow by grafting a forearm tendon onto the damaged ligament, after which John returned to Major League Baseball and pitched until age 46.

The operation later became known as Tommy John surgery. Jonah Keri characterizes it as restorative because it enables a pitcher to use existing strength and mechanics rather than manufacturing an entirely new level of performance; the episode uses that accepted case to reassess [[AndyPettitte]]'s HGH use.

## Key Characteristics

- Sustained a severe elbow-ligament injury in 1974.
- Underwent an experimental reconstruction by Frank Jobe and returned to professional pitching.
- Gave his name to a procedure that became widely accepted in baseball.
- Functions in the episode as the standard case for technologically assisted restoration.

## Qualifications

This profile is based on one structured podcast summary rather than surgical records, medical literature, biography, or performance analysis. Calling the operation restorative does not show that all recipients return only to a prior baseline, that access is equal, or that every medically assisted recovery has the same ethical status.

## What Changed

- Established John and his surgery as the episode's accepted restorative comparator.
- Added the distinction between recovering existing mechanics and creating materially new capacity.
