## Overview

东平王刘苍 / Liu Cang, Dongping King enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-755-zhege-chaodai-huangshang-jiu-kaishi-guli-shengwa-le-lj7bdkxkdtjhpo469yxhskjyfdco|Hanji 755]] as [[EmperorZhangOfHan|Emperor Zhang]]'s deceased uncle and object of mourning during the 85 CE tour. The source uses Liu Cang to show how kinship memory, palace emotion, tomb ritual, and long-serving retainers become part of Zhangdi's public conduct.

## Current Profile

The source says Zhangdi reaches Dongping, speaks to Liu Cang's sons about missing him, and weeps when seeing the former residence still present while Liu Cang is gone. He then sacrifices at Liu Cang's tomb and in the ancestral temple.

The episode also names retainers Ding Mu and Zhou Xu as men who had followed Liu Cang back from the capital and continued serving Liu Cang's descendants for decades. Zhangdi promotes them to yilang because he pities their long low status and values their loyalty and virtue.

Liu Cang's current page role is therefore posthumous and relational. The source does not develop him as an independent political actor; it uses his absence to reveal Zhangdi's royal-family affection and to reward durable service within a royal household.

## Key Characteristics

- Deceased Eastern Han royal uncle remembered by Emperor Zhang during the 85 CE tour.
- Royal-family memory figure whose house, tomb, and temple become public ritual sites.
- Source context for Zhangdi's visible grief and kinship attachment.
- Household-labor and retainer-loyalty node through Ding Mu and Zhou Xu's promotions.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 755 and does not reconstruct Liu Cang's full royal career. Ding Mu and Zhou Xu remain source-scoped because this single note uses them mainly as evidence of service continuity within Liu Cang's household.
