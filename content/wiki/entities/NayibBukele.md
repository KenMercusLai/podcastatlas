## Overview
Nayib Bukele is El Salvador's president, represented in the current evidence through his domestic anti-gang record and his decision to deploy Salvadoran forces into Haiti.

## Current Profile
[[08cf7dbf07d967235ec2004c6e8b93f5-08cf7dbf07d967235ec2004c6e8b93f5mp3aid6af5967413c30781a320173dchidd556eb54-6160-4]] presents Bukele as a politically popular security hard-liner whose administration sharply reduced El Salvador's murder rate while using mass imprisonment on weak evidence and committing serious human-rights abuses. His dispatch of about 2,000 soldiers after four Salvadoran technicians disappeared in Haiti extends that security identity abroad.

The operation is not treated as a proven export of the Salvadoran model. Haiti's institutions, armed actors, territorial control, and international missions differ substantially, while the operation's scope and end point remain unclear. The source therefore keeps rescue, security, international prestige, and electoral image as overlapping possible motives rather than collapsing them into one settled explanation.

## Key Characteristics
- Builds political authority around visible, forceful anti-gang action and a large reported reduction in homicide.
- Couples security results with mass detention, weak-evidence imprisonment, and serious human-rights concerns.
- Seeks an international security role by deploying Salvadoran forces beyond a domestic context.
- Uses an independently organized Haiti intervention whose relationship to the existing UN-backed mission is uncertain.
- Faces a transfer problem because Haiti's gangs, institutions, armed forces, and operational environment differ from El Salvador's.

## Qualifications

A domestic security outcome does not establish causal attribution to one policy, legal proportionality, or transferability to Haiti.
