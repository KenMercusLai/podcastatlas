# Nguyễn Văn Thiệu

## Overview

Nguyễn Văn Thiệu was South Vietnam's president during the Paris settlement and most of the state's final military collapse.

## Current Profile

The episode portrays Thiệu as an autocratic and reportedly corrupt ally who nevertheless had a substantive grievance about American promises. Nixon had assured him of severe retaliation if North Vietnam violated the peace agreement, but Watergate and congressional restrictions made that assurance ineffective.

Thiệu's spring 1975 decision to abandon northern territory and consolidate nearer Saigon became the episode's pivotal South Vietnamese error. A difficult strategic withdrawal turned into rout, refugee congestion, territorial loss, and accelerating institutional collapse. He resigned on 21 April while accusing the United States and Kissinger of betrayal.

## Key Characteristics

- Led South Vietnam through the Paris agreement and the 1975 offensive.
- Relied on American assurances, aid, and military support.
- Ordered a strategic territorial withdrawal that became chaotic.
- Resigned as North Vietnamese forces approached Saigon.
- Combined compromised rule with a partly credible accusation of allied abandonment.
