# Minamoto no Yoshitsune

## Overview
Minamoto no Yoshitsune is the victorious Minamoto commander in [[661-dawn-of-the-samurai-the-shogun-triumphant-part-4-glt7837412375]], where his military brilliance destroys the [[TairaClan]] but also makes him politically dangerous to [[MinamotoNoYoritomo]].

## Current Profile
The episode presents Yoshitsune as both a historical actor and a legendary hero. His childhood stories, bond with [[Benkei]], and later survival myths belong to legend, but his campaign record at Ichinotani, Yashima, and [[DanNoUraBattle|Dan-no-ura]] makes him the decisive battlefield figure in the [[GenpeiWar]].

His tragedy is that tactical victory does not translate into regime control. Once Kyoto honors and battlefield fame give him an independent reputation, Yoritomo treats him as a rival, strips his lands, and drives him into outlawry and death.

## Key Characteristics
- Yoshitsune is framed as a commander who turns impossible terrain and weather into tactical opportunities.
- His victories at Ichinotani, Yashima, and [[DanNoUraBattle|Dan-no-ura]] make him the military destroyer of Taira power.
- His legend blends verifiable military success with stories of supernatural training, heroic retainers, and survival after death.
- His fame becomes politically lethal because [[MinamotoNoYoritomo]] cannot allow an alternative Minamoto loyalty pole.

## Evidence
- Tactical audacity: [[661-dawn-of-the-samurai-the-shogun-triumphant-part-4-glt7837412375]] describes the cliff descent above Ichinotani, the gale crossing to Shikoku, phantom banners at Yashima, and the final naval decision at [[DanNoUraBattle|Dan-no-ura]].
- Military result: [[661-dawn-of-the-samurai-the-shogun-triumphant-part-4-glt7837412375]] credits Yoshitsune with the decisive run of victories from Kyoto through the Taira's destruction.
- Political danger: [[661-dawn-of-the-samurai-the-shogun-triumphant-part-4-glt7837412375]] says Yoritomo saw Yoshitsune as the only figure who could rival him and refused to receive him in Kamakura.

## Qualifications
The source treats Yoshitsune's campaign as historical but his childhood training, Benkei material, final-stand details, and later Genghis Khan survival legend as culturally powerful stories rather than fully verifiable reportage.
