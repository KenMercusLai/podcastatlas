# Minamoto no Yoshitsune

## Overview
Minamoto no Yoshitsune is the brilliant Minamoto commander whose legend and campaign record drive the late [[GenpeiWar]] narrative.

## Current Profile
The episodes present Yoshitsune as both a historical actor and a legendary hero. [[660-dawn-of-the-samurai-japans-greatest-warrior-part-3-glt3581345898]] follows the uncertain childhood material: spared after his father Yoshitomo's defeat, sent to a monastery near Mount Kurama, later imagined as training with Tengu and defeating [[Benkei]] on a Kyoto bridge. Clearer historical action begins when he joins [[MinamotoNoYoritomo]] in 1180 and then moves rapidly to relieve [[GoShirakawa]] in Kyoto.

[[661-dawn-of-the-samurai-the-shogun-triumphant-part-4-glt7837412375]] extends that profile into the decisive campaigns at Ichinotani, Yashima, and [[DanNoUraBattle|Dan-no-ura]], making Yoshitsune the military destroyer of Taira power.

His tragedy is that tactical victory does not translate into regime control. Once Kyoto honors and battlefield fame give him an independent reputation, Yoritomo treats him as a rival, strips his lands, and drives him into outlawry and death.

## Key Characteristics
- Yoshitsune is framed as a commander of speed, aggression, and tactical boldness.
- His victories at Ichinotani, Yashima, and [[DanNoUraBattle|Dan-no-ura]] make him the military destroyer of Taira power.
- His legend blends verifiable military success with stories of hidden origins, supernatural training, heroic retainers, and survival after death.
- His fame becomes politically lethal because [[MinamotoNoYoritomo]] cannot allow an alternative Minamoto loyalty pole.

## Evidence
- Early profile: [[660-dawn-of-the-samurai-japans-greatest-warrior-part-3-glt3581345898]] contrasts Yoshitsune's legendary youth with the firmer 1180 record of joining Yoritomo and later racing to Kyoto after the Uji crossing.
- Tactical audacity: [[661-dawn-of-the-samurai-the-shogun-triumphant-part-4-glt7837412375]] describes the cliff descent above Ichinotani, the gale crossing to Shikoku, phantom banners at Yashima, and the final naval decision at [[DanNoUraBattle|Dan-no-ura]].
- Military result: [[661-dawn-of-the-samurai-the-shogun-triumphant-part-4-glt7837412375]] credits Yoshitsune with the decisive run of victories from Kyoto through the Taira's destruction.
- Political danger: [[661-dawn-of-the-samurai-the-shogun-triumphant-part-4-glt7837412375]] says Yoritomo saw Yoshitsune as the only figure who could rival him and refused to receive him in Kamakura.

## Qualifications
The sources treat Yoshitsune's campaign as historical but his childhood training, Benkei material, final-stand details, and later survival legends as culturally powerful stories rather than fully verifiable reportage.
