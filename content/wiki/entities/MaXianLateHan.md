## Overview

马贤 / Ma Xian is a Shun-era late-Han commander whose current profile is defined by the 141 CE Western Qiang campaign failure. He matters less as a full biography than as the appointment-quality and command-collapse case: [[EmperorShunOfHan|汉顺帝]] insists on using him, [[MaRongLateHan|马融]] and [[HuangfuGuiLateHan|皇甫规]] warn that his conduct will fail, and he dies with his sons at Shegu Mountain.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-821-budong-laoban-xinsi-haixiang-jinsheng-meimen-lmjxr8fdxgw-colrlinmujba74fh|Hanji 821]] places Ma Xian at the center of Shun's Qiang-frontier decision. Liang Shang opposes his appointment, but Shun still assigns him to suppress the Western Qiang.

The source then presents Ma Xian's command style as slow and indulgent at the wrong moment. Ma Rong argues that Ma Xian is enjoying feasts, clothing, chariots, and horses instead of striking before Qiang forces unite, and Huangfu Gui independently predicts that Ma Xian will lose.

The warnings are ignored. On 141-01-21, Ma Xian and his two sons are defeated and killed at Shegu Mountain, leaving the frontier situation worse and making his command the episode's negative example of bad fit, bad tempo, and ignored counsel.

## Key Characteristics

- Shun-era Qiang-front commander chosen despite high-level objection.
- Negative appointment-fit case whose command was criticized before defeat.
- Commander associated with delayed action while Qiang forces gained cohesion.
- Battlefield failure whose defeat widened pressure on Longxi, Guanzhong, and the tomb-security field.
- Source-bounded example of how rejected remonstrance can become military catastrophe.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 821. It does not reconstruct Ma Xian's full office career, earlier service, or every tactical detail of the Shegu Mountain battle. The source presents Ma Rong's and Huangfu Gui's warnings as vindicated by the outcome, but the current wiki does not yet have an independent multi-source comparison of Ma Xian's whole command record.

## What Changed
