# George Romney

## Overview

George Romney was a leading British portrait painter whose repeated images of [[EmmaHamilton|Emma Hamilton]] helped turn her face and performed identities into widely circulated celebrity imagery.

## Current Profile

The episode presents Romney as artistically fascinated by Emma after [[CharlesGreville|Charles Greville]] introduced them. He painted her in numerous roles and classical guises, using her capacity for expression and transformation as a source of invention. Prints after these portraits carried her image beyond the original canvases and their owners, linking portraiture to [[EmbodiedClassicalPerformance]] and [[FashionCirculationInfrastructure]].

This relationship was collaborative in effect but unequal in documentation. Romney's name and paintings survive as the formal art record, while Emma supplied the face, poses, costume intelligence, and performative range that made the series distinctive.

## Key Characteristics

- Prominent Georgian portrait painter.
- Artist strongly associated with repeated portraits of Emma Hamilton.
- Used Emma's expressive and theatrical range across classical and dramatic identities.
- Contributor to print-mediated celebrity through reproductions of his portraits.
- Example of formal artistic authorship resting partly on a model's embodied creative labor.
