# 韩国 / Han State

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-04-yurang-wei-zhiyao-fuchou-ln34tydha6iqwlrgmw7iugqfgfik]] adds Han's role in the first partition threshold and formal recognition frame. Han joins Zhao and Wei in dividing [[ZhiYao|智瑶]]'s territory after Jinyang, and [[HanJinghou|韩虔 / 韩景侯]] later becomes one of the three Jin leaders recognized by [[ZhouWeilieWang|周威烈王]] through [[ThreeJinVassalRecognition|三晋受封]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-05-guangcai-duomu-de-weiwenhou-1-lmkxzbmwhw3nhurape-eebydqcdl]] adds a diplomatic trust scene. Han asks [[WeiState|魏国]] to join an attack on [[ZhaoState|赵国]], but [[WeiWenhou|魏文侯]] refuses by calling Zhao a brother state; when Han later learns that Wei also refused Zhao's anti-Han request, Han accepts Wei's consistency through [[SymmetricInterstateCredibility|对称外交信用]].

韩国 / Han State appears in [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-07-liangqi-cisha-1-lvkp-ju7pf5z5cz3thojv3s2fkyx]] as one of the early Warring States powers formed from the Jin world. The episode places Han in two linked events in 400 BCE: Han joins [[ZhaoState|赵国]] and [[WeiState|魏国]] in attacking [[ChuState|楚国]], and then suffers [[ZhengState|郑国]]'s attack on Yangdi after [[HanJinghou|韩景侯]] dies.

The source uses Han less as a full institutional history than as a succession and vulnerability case. [[HanLiehou|韩烈侯]]'s accession matters because the episode treats ruler death as a moment when rivals can test whether a new polity's chain of command is intact.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-07-liangqi-cisha-2-lhngst3xsnyjrmzh1cvheocvvsqm]] adds two Han stress points: [[XiaLei|侠累]], Han's chancellor, is killed by [[NieZheng|聂政]], and [[FushuCity|负黍]] defects back from [[ZhengState|郑国]] to Han before being taken by [[ChuState|楚国]] soon after.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-08-yidai-mingjiang-wuqi-cansi-2-lvaqlgyccrugfo0l-wuwf1mt1kcm]] adds the death of [[HanLiehou|韩烈侯]] in 387 BCE and the accession of his son [[HanWenhou|韩文侯]]. Han remains part of the source's clustered early Warring States succession pattern.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-08-yidai-mingjiang-wuqi-cansi-3-ltca-ikuzo9ej7a0lfrgwv2-wulb]] adds a 385 BCE attack on [[ZhengState|郑国]] in which Han takes Yangcheng. The episode presents it as one more step in the pressure that later culminates in Han's absorption of Zheng, rather than as an isolated border raid.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-09-zhanguo-shidai-de-shijie-dazhan-losmum91vjpqfymywelj60o7-vbl]] keeps Han in both war and succession roles. Han joins Zhao and Wei to attack Qi in support of Yan, later joins Zhao and Wei again against Qi at Lingqiu, sees [[HanWenhou|韩文侯]] die and [[HanAihou|韩哀侯]] succeed, and participates in the final deposition of [[JinJinggong|晋静公]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-10-yi-jiazu-danwei-you-mayi-chi-daxiang-de-jingshen-1-lq3t7lwpmq1oyb8zmmmn2tywnu8d]] turns Han from one actor in interstate churn into the episode's main long-strategy case. It traces Han from [[HanKangzi|韩康子]] and [[HanWuzi|韩武子]] through [[HanJinghou|韩景侯]], [[HanLiehou|韩烈侯]], [[HanWenhou|韩文侯]], and [[HanAihou|韩哀侯]], arguing that the absorption of [[ZhengState|郑国]] in 375 BCE was built through [[GenerationalFamilyStrategy|generational family strategy]] and [[StrategicCapitalRelocation|strategic capital relocation]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-10-yi-jiazu-danwei-you-mayi-chi-daxiang-de-jingshen-2-lmnqfjmydygqnfec6xhzpixralvi]] adds Han's immediate post-conquest instability. The episode records [[HanAihou|韩哀侯]] dying in the [[YanZhongzi|严遂]] / [[HanGui|韩廆]] assassination crisis and [[HanYihou|韩懿侯]] succeeding, while flagging the relation between this account and the earlier [[NieZheng|聂政]] / [[XiaLei|侠累]] variant.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-10-yi-jiazu-danwei-you-mayi-chi-daxiang-de-jingshen-3-lmhj4le2mwu1yhhl-qmuoqkfmhjf]] returns Han to strategic expansion. [[WangChuo|王绰]]'s flight from Wei and [[GongsunQi|公孙齐]]'s advice lead [[HanYihou|韩懿侯]] to join [[ZhaoChenghou|赵成侯]] against Wei. Han then favors partitioning Wei into smaller states, but withdraws when Zhao prefers installing [[GongzhongHuan|公中缓]] and taking territory.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-11-shijie-fengqi-yunyong-1-lt60b2gddiosf5kade35uopwohi5]] shifts Han from attacking Wei to coordinating with it under [[QinEastwardPressure|Qin eastward pressure]]. In 366 BCE, Han and [[WeiState|魏国]] meet at Zaiyang, but [[QinState|秦国]] defeats their joint force at Luoyang Jinjiao.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-11-shijie-fengqi-yunyong-2-llzzalowv5vmbvgd6l-jujltkbyf]] adds the unstable sequel in 362 BCE. Han and [[ZhaoState|赵国]] are defeated by [[WeiState|魏国]] at Hui, showing that anti-Qin coordination does not remove immediate rivalry among the Three Jin successor states.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-14-qiweiwang-buming-zeyi-yiming-jingren-lrs6rbifyiqrojxg0mccpg7sf9rv]] adds a later pressure point. Qin defeats Han at West Mountain in 358 BCE, helping explain why [[WeiState|魏国]] builds western defenses and why the 357 BCE Wei-Han meeting, probably involving [[HanZhaohou|韩昭侯]], belongs inside [[QinEastwardPressure]] rather than ordinary court ceremony.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-16-sunbin-pangjuan-enyuan-jiuge-guiling-zhi-zhan-lryhzkc1-xsvfjhvicu8yxa9d2jr]] adds Han attacking [[EasternZhouPolity|东周国]] and taking small places in 353 BCE. The episode uses the notice to show that Han's expansion pressure reaches even the reduced [[ZhouRoyalHouse|周王室]] domain after [[EasternWesternZhouSplit|东周西周分裂]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-17-hanzhaohou-he-shenbuhai-lghjmjhq9qs4se96c5ofgz6caqof]] adds Han's internal-strengthening branch under [[HanZhaohou|韩昭侯]] and [[ShenBuhai|申不害]]. The episode says Shen Buhai's chief-minister work helps make Han ordered and militarily strong, while Han Zhaohou's refusal of private office requests and careful use of rewards become [[MeritBasedRewardPunishment|因功赏罚]]. Han also appears as both a force in the [[XianglingWarringStates|襄陵]] fighting and a refuge for [[GongziXie|公子泄]] after Zhao's succession conflict.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-19-maling-zhi-zhan-lhbapt09x2ptdqx0uopkp8apbada]] makes Han the endangered state whose crisis triggers [[MalingBattle|马陵之战]]. [[WeiState|魏国]] attacks under [[PangJuan|庞涓]], Han asks [[QiState|齐国]] for help, and [[QiWeiwang|齐威王]] privately promises rescue while following [[SunBin|孙膑]]'s [[DelayedRescueStatecraft|delayed-rescue]] advice. Han keeps fighting, loses repeatedly, and ends up dependent on Qi's intervention.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-26-mengzi-laile-ln4twwp2frmr-xpumu0rqg-2gpyn]] adds Han's post-[[ShenBuhai|申不害]] personnel line. After Shen dies, [[ZhangKaidi|张开地]] succeeds as chief minister, and his son [[ZhangPingHan|张平]] later continues the office; the episode uses this family continuity to explain why [[ZhangLiang|张良]]'s later anti-Qin action is remembered as Han-restoration loyalty.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-27-weiguan-weihuiwang-he-qiweiwang-shi-za-shangye-hupeng-lminlxhrm5bethub9kyy-8blimrb]] adds two Han-facing branches. First, Qin takes [[YiyangWarringStates|宜阳]] from Han in 335 BCE, extending [[QinEastwardPressure|Qin eastward pressure]] against Han directly. Second, the episode explains why Han is not onstage at [[XuzhouMutualKingship|徐州相王]]: [[ShenBuhai|申不害]] had already advised [[HanZhaohou|韩昭侯]] into a deferential posture toward [[LiangHuiWang|魏惠王 / 梁惠王]], making Han's low posture earlier and less ceremonially visible in the Qi-Wei meeting.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-28-hanzhaohou-de-kuaile-ni-xiangxiang-budao-ln2tbxniz4kvwogbwp32exmdokz8]] uses that Qin seizure as the context for judging Han's internal conduct. After losing [[YiyangWarringStates|宜阳]] and suffering drought, [[HanZhaohou|韩昭侯]] builds a high gate; [[QuYijiu|屈宜臼]] criticizes this as [[UntimelyExtravagance|时绌举赢]], making Han a case where external pressure and domestic hardship should change ruler behavior.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-29-suqin-lizhi-shuangwen-zhishi-gaibian-mingyun-lrf5cqvoriyep2lkbozju-lyjaxv]] records Han's next compact succession notice. [[HanZhaohou|韩昭侯]] dies, and [[HanXuanhuiwang|韩宣惠王]] succeeds in the same 333 BCE cluster that also records Qi, Yan, and Wei transitions.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-32-shishang-shouwei-lianheguo-mishuzhang-suqin-lgfkk45yq1c0yblequ-d1t7mol3]] makes Han the first new stop in [[SuQin|苏秦]]'s completed [[HezongAlliance|合纵]] tour after Zhao authorization. Su Qin tells [[HanXuanhuiwang|韩宣惠王]] that serving [[QinState|秦国]] will only produce repeated land concessions because Han's territory is finite and Qin's demands are not, then converts the strategic point into [[DignityBasedPersuasion|dignity-based persuasion]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-44-yi-ge-gongsi-weishenme-buneng-you-liang-wei-ceo-lsz0xk6-0wwznkc62k-xkoxgz-r]] adds a Han internal-governance case. [[HanXuanhuiwang|韩宣惠王]] considers making [[GongzhongHan|公众]] and [[GongshuHan|公叔]] joint chief ministers, and [[MiuLiu|谬刘]] argues that two top ministers could produce domestic factions, foreign backing, and territorial concessions. The episode makes Han a case for [[DualExecutivePowerRisk|双首长权力风险]] rather than only an object of Qin-facing persuasion.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-45-yi-shi-wei-jian-junwang-daodi-gai-bu-gai-sharen-chengxing-lo3cok7cfwzrxesp-i8miz1fc1db]] continues Han's Qin-facing vulnerability. Qin attacks Han and takes Yanling in 319 BCE. Han then joins Chu, Zhao, Wei, and Yan in the 318 BCE anti-Qin attack, but the army stalls at [[HanguPass|函谷关]] and collapses through [[CoalitionSelfPreservationFailure|联盟自保失灵]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-46-ma-ni-shi-ge-erbaiwu-de-lishi-laiyuan-lkz5hiamaojtvanllbunecy3g3ff]] follows that vulnerability into disaster. After the failed Hangu Pass campaign, [[QinState|秦国]] pursues Han and [[WeiState|魏国]] east under [[Chulizi|樗里子]], defeats Han badly at [[XiuyuBattle|修鱼之战]], and then captures Han generals rendered in the source as Wei Sou and Shen Chai at [[ZhuozeBattle|浊泽]]. Han therefore becomes the front-line state that pays for a coalition failure it could not solve alone.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-51-chuhuaiwang-qin-xiao-er-kan-jiujiu-ruhe-mie-le-ni-ltfkdlivomnvux5xodh2rlfqf6ac]] gives Han a smaller but strategically important role in the Qin-Chu sequel. While [[ChuHuaiwang|楚怀王]] drives the [[ChuState|楚国]] army deep toward [[LantianBattle|蓝田之战]], Han and [[WeiState|魏国]] move against Chu's capital region and rear. Han is therefore no longer only a state exposed to Qin pressure; it can also exploit another state's overextension.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-52-chenzhuang-panqin-duanqi-wan-chifan-fangxia-wan-ma-niang-ls13ixmbhflepjgpelxgu6idjqa2]] adds Han's next compact succession notice. The episode says [[HanXuanhuiwang|韩宣惠王 / 韩惠王]] dies and his son Cang succeeds as [[HanXiangwang|韩襄王]], keeping Han inside the annalistic rhythm while the main narrative weight shifts to Yan recovery and Qin's Ba-Shu rebellion.
