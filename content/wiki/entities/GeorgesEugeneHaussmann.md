# Georges-Eugène Haussmann

## Overview

Georges-Eugène Haussmann was the administrator selected by Napoleon III to remake [[Paris]] through boulevards, coordinated buildings, utilities, water, and sewers.

## Current Profile

[[57-paris-glt6186083108]] presents Haussmann as the highly capable executor of Napoleon III's ambition to make Paris breathe. His program addressed congestion and severe sanitary weakness, symbolized by the 1832 cholera epidemic, and helped create the recognizable modern city associated with the Belle Époque.

The profile is deliberately double-sided. Haussmann's infrastructure improved circulation, hygiene, lighting, water, and sewerage, but the work also demolished large areas of old Paris. His legacy therefore exemplifies [[HaussmannianUrbanModernization]]: durable public capacity and visual coherence achieved through disruptive urban destruction.

## Key Characteristics

- Efficient Second Empire administrator chosen to execute large-scale urban transformation.
- Builder of boulevards and coordinated streetscapes that shaped the recognizable modern city.
- Modernizer of gas, electricity, water, and sewer infrastructure.
- Actor responding to congestion and lethal public-health conditions in older Paris.
- Destructive remaker whose achievements depended on erasing substantial parts of the inherited city.

## Evidence

- Administrative role: [[57-paris-glt6186083108]] attributes Haussmann's selection to efficiency, talent, and capacity for sustained work under Napoleon III.
- Infrastructure: [[57-paris-glt6186083108]] credits the transformation with boulevards, uniform buildings, lighting, water, and sewers.
- Public-health setting: [[57-paris-glt6186083108]] connects the program to severe hygiene problems and the 1832 cholera disaster.
- Destructive cost: [[57-paris-glt6186083108]] emphasizes that much of old Paris disappeared in the process.

## Qualifications

This single episode does not reconstruct the program's finance, legal powers, displacement patterns, political opposition, military implications, engineering chronology, or Haussmann's complete career. Its account is a high-level interpretation of the city's physical transformation.
