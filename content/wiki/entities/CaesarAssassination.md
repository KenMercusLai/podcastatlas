# Assassination of Julius Caesar / 恺撒遇刺

## Overview

The assassination of Julius Caesar / 恺撒遇刺 is the Ides of March killing framed as republican tyrannicide but followed by a failed settlement, popular backlash, renewed civil war, triumviral dictatorship, and the defeat of the liberators at [[BattleOfPhilippi|Philippi]].

## Current Profile

The Caesar source places the killing at Pompey's Curia and treats the location, [[MarcusBrutus]]'s anti-king ancestry, [[GaiusCassiusLonginus]]'s organization, and [[DecimusJuniusBrutus]]'s access as central to its republican public meaning. The conspiracy is principled and compromised at once: resistance to personal rule coexists with fear, resentment, rivalry, and weak post-assassination planning. The Marat source turns it into a comparative case in which an unlawful killing may preserve political refusal without repairing the system that produced it.

[[305-the-fall-of-the-roman-republic-glt6995332578]] supplies the missing aftermath. The conspirators do not immediately control the Senate, streets, armies, or succession. [[Cicero]]'s amnesty creates a temporary compromise, but [[MarkAntony]] uses Caesar's funeral and will to mobilize public anger. [[OctavianAugustus|Octavian]] then converts Caesar's name and fortune into an armed following, consulship, and the [[SecondTriumvirate]]. Proscriptions reverse the amnesty, and Philippi destroys the principal republican armies. The murder removes Caesar but accelerates the military concentration it was meant to prevent.

## Key Characteristics

- Elite conspiracy presented through the language of tyrannicide and republican restoration.
- Politically ambiguous act joining constitutional principle to resentment, rivalry, and fear.
- Deliberately limited killing whose refusal of a broader purge preserves moral meaning but leaves powerful opponents intact.
- Immediate governance failure because the conspirators do not control institutions, crowds, succession, or armies.
- Catalyst for Caesar's martyrdom, Octavian's inheritance politics, triumviral terror, and further civil war.
- Durable memory event whose political meaning changes across ancient history, Shakespeare, and later revolutionary analogy.

## Evidence

- Plot and setting: [[124-kaisa-zhisi-lishi-de-yunjiao-shifou-yi-shixiao-781894361]] describes Pompey's Curia, Brutus's symbolism, Cassius's organization, Decimus's access, and refusal to kill Antony.
- Comparative ethics: [[125-mala-zhisi-beibi-de-ansha-huo-zhengyi-de-chujue-783972516]] uses Caesar's murder to qualify the idea that political assassination can restore law or stop terror.
- Failed settlement: [[305-the-fall-of-the-roman-republic-glt6995332578]] follows the Capitol retreat, amnesty, funeral backlash, Octavian's rise, Lex Pedia, proscriptions, and Philippi.
- Imperial result: [[124-kaisa-zhisi-lishi-de-yunjiao-shifou-yi-shixiao-781894361]] and [[305-the-fall-of-the-roman-republic-glt6995332578]] connect the failure of restoration to Octavian's later Augustan order.

## Qualifications

The sources do not settle every conspirator's motive or make the republic's collapse inevitable. Funeral staging, crowd causation, last words, apparition stories, death details, and later dramatic interpretations remain source-scoped. The assassination is a major accelerant and failed repair, not the sole origin of the [[LateRomanRepublicCrisis|late-republic crisis]].

## What Changed

- Extended the event through amnesty, funeral backlash, Octavian's ascent, triumviral proscriptions, and Philippi.
- Made the distinction between removing a ruler and controlling the post-assassination order explicit.

## Relationships

- [[JuliusCaesar]] - target whose will, funeral, and political memory survive the murder.
- [[MarcusBrutus]] - symbolic republican leader whose restraint and later defeat shape the event's meaning.
- [[GaiusCassiusLonginus]] - practical organizer and later eastern commander.
- [[MarkAntony]] - survivor who reverses the conspirators' public position through funeral and military politics.
- [[OctavianAugustus]] - heir who turns the event into a path toward imperial rule.
- [[SecondTriumvirate]] - dictatorship formed by Caesar's avengers after the senatorial settlement fails.
- [[TyrannicideRepublicanDilemma]] - conceptual relation between morally legible killing and failed restoration.
