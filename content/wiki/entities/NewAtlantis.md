## Overview

*New Atlantis* is [[FrancisBacon|Francis Bacon]]'s unfinished utopian narrative about travelers reaching Bensalem, an island whose prosperity and technical capacity center on [[SalomonsHouse|Salomon's House]].

## Current Profile

The island combines selective hospitality with political closure. Foreign visitors are restricted, but knowledge is not: agents periodically travel abroad to collect books, instruments, inventions, and scientific findings. Bensalem therefore separates openness to information from openness to migration, political exchange, or social customs.

The technological catalogue matters less than the institutional architecture behind it. Laboratories, experimental environments, specialized roles, cumulative records, secrecy, forecasting, and public advice make discovery a coordinated social system. Yet the text leaves the political settlement incomplete: Salomon's House can choose what to reveal and may withhold discoveries even from government, while the public depends chiefly on the scientists' virtue.

## Key Characteristics

- Unfinished early-modern island utopia published after Bacon's death.
- Narrative of travelers admitted into the hidden society of Bensalem.
- Political closure combined with systematic international knowledge collection.
- Advanced research across medicine, agriculture, optics, sound, flight, navigation, and environmental manipulation.
- Scientific organization built on specialization, experiment, analysis, invention, and cumulative learning.
- Social conservatism and religious authority coexisting with technical innovation.
- Open-ended governance problem concerning secrecy, autonomy, expertise, and public accountability.

## Qualifications

The supplied episode is a structured interpretive summary rather than the primary text. It does not establish which imagined devices were historically original, which later institutions directly drew from Bacon, or whether Bensalem should be read as endorsement, warning, satire, or an intentionally unresolved design. The Silicon Valley parallel is a modern comparison rather than literal prediction.

## What Changed

- Established the work as an institutional thought experiment rather than only a catalogue of predicted inventions.
- Made political closure, scientific openness, secrecy, and incomplete oversight central to its profile.
