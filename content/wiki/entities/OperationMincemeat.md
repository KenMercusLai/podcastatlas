# Operation Mincemeat

## Overview

Operation Mincemeat was an Allied wartime deception that placed false documents on a dead body given a fabricated identity in order to make Greece appear to be the invasion target instead of Sicily.

## Current Profile

[[37-spies-with-ben-macintyre-glt4382644548]] uses the operation as a concrete case in which [[EspionageFictionWorldbuilding|constructed narrative]] entered military strategy. The body, identity, personal detail, official papers, neutral-Spain route, and anticipated German access had to form one believable world. Intercepts then gave the Allies feedback that the planted information had traveled toward Berlin and Hitler's headquarters, allowing the episode to argue for strategic effect rather than merely an ingenious plan.

## Key Characteristics

- Built deception around a fabricated human identity rather than one isolated false document.
- Used neutral Spain as the route by which German intelligence could acquire the material.
- Targeted German expectations about Allied landings in Greece and Sicily.
- Included an intelligence-feedback loop through intercepted communications.
- Is presented as a rare operation with observable military consequences.

## Evidence

## Qualifications

The episode summarizes the operation and attributes troop movement to the deception but does not expose the full intercept record, German command debate, other deception measures, or a counterfactual estimate of what would have happened without it.
