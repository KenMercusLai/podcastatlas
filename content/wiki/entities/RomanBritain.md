# Roman Britain

## Overview

Roman Britain is the provincial order produced after the [[RomanConquestOfBritain|Roman conquest]] through client alliances, military occupation, extraction, urban development, and selective cultural incorporation under the [[RomanEmpire]].

## Current Profile

Episode 500 adds the province's invasion foundation. Britain was already tied to Rome through trade, diplomacy, coinage, hostages, and client rulers when [[Caratacus]] displaced [[Verica]]. [[Claudius]] used that crisis and preparations inherited from Caligula to authorize the AD 43 invasion; [[AulusPlautius]] won at the Medway and Thames before the emperor arrived to claim Camulodunon and stage submission. The first settlement joined client rewards and new towns to garrisons, veteran colonization, resource extraction, and prolonged Welsh war.

Episodes 501 and 502 show the province nearly collapsing under the contradictions of that early rule and then being rebuilt. Before the [[BoudiccanRevolt]], Rome combined client rulers, rewards for collaborators, urban settlement, debt and taxation with spectacular punishment. The seizure of the [[Iceni]] kingdom, abuse of [[Boudicca]] and her daughters, and grievance against Camulodunum triggered a revolt while [[SuetoniusPaulinus]] and much of the army were in Wales. The destruction of Camulodunum, London, and Verulamium exposed how much Roman power depended on being able to protect local allies.

Paulinus preserved the province through concentration, terrain, and Roman discipline, but his reprisals became too destructive for continued occupation. Episode 502 then follows repair through administrative restraint and London's rebuilding, followed by expansion under Kerialis, Frontinus, and [[GnaeusJuliusAgricola|Agricola]]. [[RomanProvincialConsolidation]] joined forts, roads, towns, fleet logistics, census, taxation, and elite incorporation, yet its northern endpoint remained reversible: Inchtuthil could not survive the Danubian crisis and changing imperial priorities.

## Key Characteristics

- Provincial system founded by invasion but prepared by earlier trade, diplomacy, client politics, and tribal rivalry.
- Early order dependent on uneven client autonomy, local collaboration, extraction, and credible military protection.
- Province nearly lost during Boudicca's revolt and rebuilt through a combination of coercion, restraint, and financial recovery.
- Highly militarized territory organized around legionary bases, forts, roads, and fleet support.
- Administrative system that linked census-taking, towns, elite incorporation, and taxation.
- Unevenly Romanized landscape, with stronger urban and material integration in some southern areas than in Caledonia.
- Northern frontier whose furthest conquests were strategically reversible.

## Evidence

- Conquest foundation: [[500-the-roman-conquest-of-britain-the-empire-strikes-back-part-2-glt3137178317]] links Verica's exile, Claudius's legitimacy needs, Caligulan preparations, and Plautius's campaign to the AD 43 invasion.
- Unequal settlement: [[500-the-roman-conquest-of-britain-the-empire-strikes-back-part-2-glt3137178317]] contrasts rewards for collaborators with veteran colonization, occupation abuse, resource extraction, and Welsh resistance.
- Pre-revolt vulnerability: [[501-the-roman-conquest-of-britain-boudiccas-reign-of-blood-part-3-glt3128836892]] links Iceni annexation, debt, elite abuse, urban vulnerability, and the army's Welsh concentration to provincial crisis.
- Alliance credibility: [[501-the-roman-conquest-of-britain-boudiccas-reign-of-blood-part-3-glt3128836892]] shows the burning of Verulamium threatening Rome's ability to retain British collaborators.
- Post-revolt repair: [[502-the-roman-conquest-of-britain-to-the-ends-of-the-earth-part-4-glt1970351222]] describes Classicanus and Petronius Turpilianus restraining reprisals and rebuilding London after Boudicca.
- Military infrastructure: [[502-the-roman-conquest-of-britain-to-the-ends-of-the-earth-part-4-glt1970351222]] connects York, Carlisle, Caerleon, Chester, Welsh fort networks, northern roads, and Inchtuthil to successive expansion.
- Fiscal and cultural administration: [[502-the-roman-conquest-of-britain-to-the-ends-of-the-earth-part-4-glt1970351222]] links towns, census, taxation, Latin education, dress, dining, and elite competition to provincial rule.
- Geographic reach: [[502-the-roman-conquest-of-britain-to-the-ends-of-the-earth-part-4-glt1970351222]] describes Mount Graupius, Orkney, the fleet's circumnavigation, and confirmation that Britain was an island.
- Strategic limit: [[502-the-roman-conquest-of-britain-to-the-ends-of-the-earth-part-4-glt1970351222]] says the Danubian crisis pulled a legion away and made permanent Caledonian occupation expendable.

## Qualifications

These episodes cover the Claudian invasion, the revolt of AD 60, and the decades through Agricola rather than the full history of Roman Britain. Tacitus is both a central literary source for conquest and revolt and a familial source for Agricola; Dio is later and rhetorical; and archaeological destruction or military sites do not verify every narrative detail. Roman claims about Druids and submission are interested, collaboration was not necessarily voluntary, and temporary penetration into Caledonia should not be confused with permanent conquest of the whole island.

## What Changed

- Added the AD 43 invasion and its prehistory in cross-Channel diplomacy and client politics.
- Added Claudius's legitimacy theater and Plautius's operational role to the province's foundation.
- Extended early rule through collaboration, colonization, extraction, and prolonged Welsh resistance.

## Relationships

- [[RomanEmpire]] - imperial polity that conquered, administered, and reprioritized the province.
- [[RomanConquestOfBritain]] - invasion and prolonged occupation process that created the province.
- [[Claudius]] - emperor who authorized and claimed the foundational invasion.
- [[AulusPlautius]] - commander who won the initial campaign.
- [[Boudicca]] - rebel leader whose campaign exposed its early political and military fragility.
- [[Iceni]] - client polity whose coercive annexation helped trigger the provincial crisis.
- [[SuetoniusPaulinus]] - governor who saved the province militarily before reprisals endangered recovery.
- [[GnaeusJuliusAgricola|Agricola]] - governor associated with its furthest northern advance and civil administration.
- [[Tacitus]] - principal literary interpreter of Agricola's Britain.
- [[RomanProvincialConsolidation]] - mechanism that converted military presence into governable territory.
- [[AncientImperialCritiqueLimits]] - interpretive frame for the source's Roman criticism of conquest and luxury.
