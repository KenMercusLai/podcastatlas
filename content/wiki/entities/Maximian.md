# Maximian

## Overview

Maximian was the Illyrian soldier-emperor whom [[Diocletian]] elevated first as Caesar and then as co-Augustus, making him the senior western ruler in the system that became the [[Tetrarchy]].

## Current Profile

The episode treats Maximian as a capable, trusted complement to Diocletian. He suppressed the Bagaudae in Gaul and defended the Rhine, although the breakaway ruler Carausius initially kept Britain beyond his reach because he lacked control of the Channel.

As Herculius, associated with Hercules, Maximian occupied a subordinate symbolic relationship to Diocletian's Jovius while remaining Augustus. He accepted the 305 abdication unwillingly and later urged Diocletian to return, exposing the difference between Diocletian's retirement and Maximian's continuing attachment to power.

## Key Characteristics

- Illyrian military colleague selected as Diocletian's trusted western partner.
- Caesar promoted to Augustus in 286.
- Campaigner against the Bagaudae and Rhine-frontier enemies.
- Herculius counterpart to Diocletian's Jovius identity.
- Reluctant retiree whose response exposed the fragility of planned abdication.

- Abdication tension: [[260-croatia-the-man-who-saved-the-roman-empire-glt9631298578]] says he retired under Diocletian's plan despite reluctance and later proposed their return.

## Qualifications

This is a source-bounded profile centered on Maximian's role in Diocletian's system, not a complete account of his later restorations, alliances, or death. The episode's judgment that Diocletian chose able but unimaginative partners is interpretation rather than a measurable trait.
