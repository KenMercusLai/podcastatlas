# Zou Yan / 邹衍

Zou Yan / 邹衍 appears in [[87-guonian-zui-shihe-liao-xuanxue-tantao-shenxian-fangshu-fazhanshi-wei-wan-daixu-704051997]] as the [[WarringStatesPeriod|Warring States]] thinker used to anchor the episode's [[YinYangFivePhasesPoliticalTheory|阴阳五行]] branch. The source presents him as a figure who helped join yin-yang reasoning, five-phase cycles, and dynastic succession into a language that later fangshu and imperial legitimacy could reuse.

The episode's interest in Zou Yan is structural: once nature, history, and rule can be explained through cyclical correspondences, political change can look like cosmic sequence rather than only human seizure.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-64-gudai-gangjing-gongsunlong-yangchengji-lkrchesllfi9ipgfapft7av2mnh5]] adds a different Zou Yan role. When he passes through [[ZhaoState|赵国]], [[Pingyuanjun|平原君]] asks him to debate [[GongsunLong|公孙龙]] on "白马非马." Zou Yan refuses and instead explains what debate should do: clarify categories, principles, and purposes so people do not lose the thread of truth. The episode therefore makes him a source for [[TruthSeekingDebateNorm|求明理的辩论规范]] as well as for cosmological political theory.

## Key Claims
- Zou Yan is used to show that five-phase theory was not born as a fully unified system.
- The source treats yin-yang and five-phase reasoning as gradually assembled, then politically amplified.
- Five-virtue dynastic language becomes one bridge between cosmology and legitimacy.
- Later fangshu gains authority when it can borrow this wider explanatory grammar.
- Zhouji 64 uses Zou Yan as a critic of debate that relies on name-definition tricks and verbal pressure rather than clarification.
- In that episode, his refusal to debate "白马非马" is itself a judgment about proper argumentative purpose.
