# Yao Song / 姚颂

Yao Song is the central guest in [[173-duihua-yao-song-shenjian-dongfang-kongjian-zaichufa-tiancai-shaonian-shinian-hou-1-173-1]], where [[LateTalk]] presents him as a 1992-born continuous hard-tech founder. His path runs from [[TsinghuaUniversity|清华大学]] electronic engineering and [[WangYu]]'s lab to [[DeePhiTech]], [[Orienspace]], and [[StridingAI]].

The source uses Yao as a decade-long sample of Chinese hard-tech entrepreneurship. His first startup gave him [[HardTechFundraising]] and [[StrategicAcquirerFit]] experience; the [[Xilinx]] acquisition then created a [[PostAcquisitionFounderIdentity]] problem because financial success did not give him a durable operating mission. [[Orienspace]] added large-project strategy and Chinese commercial-space execution, while [[StridingAI]] is his attempt to build a long-lived company around [[PhysicalIntelligenceSystemStack|physical intelligence as a system stack]].

## Key Claims
- Yao says he chooses directions by combining important human problems with personal enjoyment in turning a technical starting point into useful products.
- His [[FounderRiskCalibration]] is bounded: he supports going all in with time and attention, but not destroying the option to keep acting if the first attempt fails.
- He sees physical intelligence as more complex than a model or robot body because data, compute, hardware, scenes, remote operation, and product delivery all have to work together.
- He treats transparency around robot demos as a long-term operating boundary, especially when industry videos blur teleoperation and autonomy.
