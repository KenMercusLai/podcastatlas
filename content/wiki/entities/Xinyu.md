# 《新语》 / Xinyu

《新语》 / Xinyu enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-176-shusuntong-ruhe-wei-dahan-zhiding-liyi-zhidu-loeoqccrfwbmtr634ukyau9xykqy|Hanji 176]] as the text [[LuJia|陆贾]] writes for [[LiuBang|刘邦]] after military conquest. The episode uses it to make a founding-order point: arms can win the realm, but durable rule needs ritual, learning, and civil order.

The text is not presented as an abstract classic in this source. It matters because Liu Bang is willing to hear Lu Jia after initially bristling at Confucian talk. In the episode's reading, 《新语》 helps mark the turn from wartime victory into [[LiAsPoliticalOrder|礼制政治秩序]] and [[BattlefieldVictoryToFoundingOrder|战场胜利到建国秩序]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-185-yi-zhang-li-zui-shoufu-zhongguo-wanli-jiangtu-lps0moshdfvosnyhtdhrkdvswibh|Hanji 185]] repeats and sharpens the scene after [[LuJia|陆贾]] returns from [[Nanyue|南越]]. Liu Bang initially rejects Lu Jia's references to [[Shijing|《诗经》]] and [[Shangshu|《尚书》]] by saying he won the realm on horseback, but Lu Jia asks whether he can govern it on horseback. The book's twelve chapters then become a compact explanation of why Qin failed, why Han succeeded, and why conquest has to be followed by civil order.

## Key Claims
- 《新语》 is used as Lu Jia's post-conquest lesson for Liu Bang.
- The source presents its central warning as anti-militarist: ruling cannot remain only an extension of battlefield force.
- The text connects Shusun Tong's court ritual to a broader early-Han civil-governance turn.
- Hanji 185 adds the "horseback" contrast more explicitly and links the text to Lu Jia's successful frontier mission, making speech useful both outside the border and inside the court.
