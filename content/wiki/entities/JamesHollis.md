# James Hollis

## Overview
James Hollis is the Jungian psychoanalyst and author interviewed by [[AndrewHuberman]] about the Self, unconscious complexes, shadow, relationships, suffering, adulthood, and mortality.

## Current Profile
The bounded source presents Hollis as a depth-psychology clinician who translates Jungian ideas into recurring questions rather than fixed protocols. He asks what patterns repeat, what a behavior serves, what a symptom may be requesting, and whether a path enlarges or diminishes a person psycho-spiritually. His account emphasizes responsibility for what spills into relationships and society while preserving uncertainty about dreams, diagnosis, gender, and death.

## Key Characteristics
- Distinguishes socially formed ego consciousness from a deeper capital-S Self.
- Explains disproportionate reactions and repeated behavior through unconscious complexes and inherited stories.
- Uses dreams, feelings, energy, meaning, solitude, and trusted feedback as materials for reflection.
- Frames shadow ownership as both personal work and a social responsibility.
- Treats suffering as potentially task-bearing while retaining the need for differential diagnosis.
- Connects relationship maturity to otherness, dialogue, renegotiation, and commitment to a shared project.
- Treats aging and mortality as pressures toward integrity, letting go, and meaningful choice.
