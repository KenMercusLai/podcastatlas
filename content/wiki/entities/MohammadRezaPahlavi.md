# Mohammad Reza Pahlavi / 穆罕默德·礼萨·巴列维

## Overview

Mohammad Reza Pahlavi / 穆罕默德·礼萨·巴列维 appears as both a ruler challenged by [[OrianaFallaci|Oriana Fallaci]] and the shah whose 1971 [[Persepolis]] celebration later became a symbol of monarchical excess.

## Current Profile

[[77-ta-shi-ziyou-de-yegou-ta-shi-falaqi-685710187]] uses Pahlavi less as a full Iran-history subject than as a power-interview case. Fallaci's sharp criticism of the shah later made [[RuhollahKhomeini|Khomeini]]'s side think she might be useful or sympathetic, but her subsequent challenge to Khomeini supports [[JournalisticIndependenceAgainstFaction]]: opposition to one ruler did not become loyalty to the next power center.

The 1971 commemoration at Persepolis adds a monarchical-spectacle layer. The shah invoked 2,500 years associated with [[CyrusTheGreat|Cyrus the Great]] through foreign dignitaries, luxury arrangements, and imperial continuity. [[154-the-most-disastrous-party-in-history-glt6506112996]] describes how the event became a compact story of elite expense and political distance after the Iranian Revolution.

## Key Characteristics

- Iranian shah subjected to confrontational power journalism.
- Monarch who used Persepolis and Cyrus-centered antiquity for dynastic legitimation.
- Associated with a luxury international spectacle later reinterpreted by revolutionary memory.
- Case where criticism of monarchy did not require endorsement of its revolutionary successor.

## Evidence

- Power-interview role: [[77-ta-shi-ziyou-de-yegou-ta-shi-falaqi-685710187]] connects Fallaci's criticism of the shah to Khomeini's mistaken expectation of sympathy.
- Gender and monarchy: [[77-ta-shi-ziyou-de-yegou-ta-shi-falaqi-685710187]] places Pahlavi beside [[SorayaEsfandiaryBakhtiary]] in a branch about elite power and fertility pressure.
- Imperial commemoration: [[154-the-most-disastrous-party-in-history-glt6506112996]] links the shah to the 1971 Persepolis celebration and its later reputation for excess.

## Qualifications

The Fallaci source is a journalist-biography episode rather than a balanced political history of the shah. The Persepolis source offers a retrospective symbolic reading rather than a full account of the event's spending, logistics, domestic reception, reforms, repression, foreign relations, or causal role in the revolution. The celebration's later notoriety should not be treated as a complete explanation of monarchical collapse.

## What Changed

- Added the 1971 Persepolis celebration as a concrete case of Pahlavi dynastic legitimation and retrospective excess.
- Reframed the profile from a single interview reference into a bounded political-memory synthesis.
