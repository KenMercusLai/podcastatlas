## Overview

阎昌 / Yan Chang is the Hanji 787 source case of a weakly rooted Eastern Han official who tries to change family status through his daughter's palace career. The page treats him less as a full biography than as the planner behind [[EmpressDowagerYanLateHan|阎姬]]'s entry into [[EmperorAnOfHan|汉安帝]]'s harem and 115 CE elevation to empress.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-787-chuanqi-huanghou-yanji-shi-ruhe-yibubu-shangwei-de-lslngjnedmqgh8h5nfc-qmyahqnm|Hanji 787]] presents Yan Chang as an official whose court base is not deep enough to rely on ordinary career advancement. He sees his daughter Yan Ji's beauty as an opening, but the source insists that appearance alone is not enough. Yan Chang therefore prepares her from childhood for palace selection, treating harem entry as a demanding family project rather than a lucky encounter.

The source's political point is that this strategy succeeds in an unusually difficult field. Eastern Han empress positions are described as normally dominated by great founding families tied to Liu Xiu's regime and marriage network, while Emperor An still governs under Empress Dowager Deng's real authority. Yan Chang's achievement is therefore not just sending a daughter into the palace; it is getting her accepted by the emperor and the regency environment strongly enough to make her empress and make the Yan household outer relatives.

## Key Characteristics

- Weakly rooted official whose family strategy compensates for limited independent court standing.
- Father-planner who treats daughter education and palace selection as long-term family investment.
- Uses Yan Ji's beauty as an entry condition but not as a complete strategy.
- Converts imperial affection and regency acceptance into family status mobility.
- Opens the Yan household's later outer-relative position before Yan Xian and Yan Taihou become direct succession actors.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to one source note. It does not reconstruct Yan Chang's full office record, household resources, training methods, kin network, or later political influence after Yan Ji becomes empress. The source's evaluation of him as farsighted and strategic is preserved as episode interpretation, not independent proof of private motive.
