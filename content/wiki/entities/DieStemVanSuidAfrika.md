# Die Stem van Suid-Afrika

## Overview

Die Stem van Suid-Afrika is presented as the apartheid-era anthem tradition that [[NelsonMandela]] refused to discard outright because it carried emotional meaning for white South Africans inside the transition.

## Current Profile

The episode makes Die Stem a test of recognition. Black South Africans came to hate the song because of its apartheid-state association, while many white South Africans attached memory and belonging to it. Mandela's temporary decision to keep both Die Stem and [[NkosiSikelelIAfrika|Nkosi Sikelel' iAfrika]] made the anthem question a political exercise in including people the [[AfricanNationalCongress|ANC]] did not yet fully represent.

## Key Characteristics

- Anthem of the apartheid state in the episode's account.
- Song hated by many black South Africans because of political association rather than only literal wording.
- Symbol carrying white South African and [[Afrikaners|Afrikaner]] emotional attachment.
- Temporarily retained by Mandela in early post-apartheid anthem practice.
- Later fused with [[NkosiSikelelIAfrika|Nkosi]] into South Africa's multilingual national anthem.

## Evidence

- Apartheid-era role: [[682-south-africa-mandela-and-the-death-of-apartheid-part-6-glt6535658080]] says the apartheid state had its own anthem, Die Stem van Suid-Afrika.
- Opposed meanings: [[682-south-africa-mandela-and-the-death-of-apartheid-part-6-glt6535658080]] says white South Africans distrusted the ANC anthem while black South Africans came to hate Die Stem.
- Literal versus emotional meaning: [[682-south-africa-mandela-and-the-death-of-apartheid-part-6-glt6535658080]] says the literal lyrics of both songs were less aggressive than their political associations.
- Mandela's intervention: [[682-south-africa-mandela-and-the-death-of-apartheid-part-6-glt6535658080]] says Mandela argued that Die Stem carried the emotions of people the ANC did not yet represent.
- Fused anthem: [[682-south-africa-mandela-and-the-death-of-apartheid-part-6-glt6535658080]] says a later committee fused Die Stem with [[NkosiSikelelIAfrika|Nkosi]].
