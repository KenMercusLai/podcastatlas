## Overview

窦婴 / Dou Ying is a Western Han outer relative whose defense of [[GuanFuWesternHan|灌夫]] moves from a plea for proportionate judgment into open conflict with [[TianFenWesternHan|田蚡]], then ends with an accusation of deceiving the emperor, failure of an exculpatory edict, and execution after Dou-family protection has faded.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-265-dongchaotingbian-ruhe-yanbian-cheng-jieduandazhan-1-lil81vjxn5mait2wapdx_t4wy5hr|Hanji 265 part 1]] presents Dou Ying first as an advocate for proportion. He argues that Guan Fu's immediate wrongdoing is drunken speech and that Tian Fen has attached unrelated accusations. The court audience then becomes a contest over political character and security: Dou attacks Tian Fen's weaknesses, while Tian Fen depicts Dou and Guan Fu's guest networks and commentary on the two palaces as preparation for disorder. The episode does not establish that accusation as true.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-265-dongchaotingbian-ruhe-yanbian-cheng-jieduandazhan-2-lvu23osgv46nn3rbgque1mbhmpgw|Hanji 265 part 2]] presents Dou Ying's legal vulnerability as inseparable from a changed court structure. He is no longer a serving central official and can no longer rely on Empress Dowager Dou, while Tian Fen has the support of [[EmpressDowagerWangWesternHan|王太后]]. His attempt to save Guan Fu therefore moves from advocacy and mutual exposure into an accusation that he misled [[HanWudi|汉武帝]].

The episode says Dou Ying then produces an edict from Han Jingdi that could exculpate him, but no corresponding palace copy can be found after Tian Fen allegedly destroys it. The source interprets the resulting forgery charge and execution as political choice under unequal protection rather than law operating in isolation. The archival mechanism and Tian Fen's intervention remain source-attributed, not independently verified here.

## Key Characteristics

- Dou-family outer relative whose influence has declined by the time of the case.
- Political ally and defender of Guan Fu.
- Begins with a proportionality argument but participates in mutual exposure once the dispute reaches court.
- Becomes vulnerable when his testimony conflicts with the checked court record.
- Relies unsuccessfully on an edict attributed to Han Jingdi.
- Functions as Tian Fen's rival and counterweight before his execution.

## Qualifications

The profile rests on two connected podcast summaries and does not reconstruct the underlying memorials, trial procedure, transmitted versions of the edict, or the full earlier careers of Dou Ying and Guan Fu. Tian Fen's security accusation and alleged destruction of an archival copy remain episode-attributed. The page also does not treat political calculation and legal process as mutually exclusive merely because the program emphasizes the former.
