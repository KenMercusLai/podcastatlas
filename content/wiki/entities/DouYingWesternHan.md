## Overview

窦婴 / Dou Ying is a Western Han outer relative whose attempt to defend [[GuanFuWesternHan|灌夫]] ends, in the current bounded evidence, with an accusation of deceiving the emperor, failure of an exculpatory edict, and execution after the political protection of the Dou family has faded.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-265-dongchaotingbian-ruhe-yanbian-cheng-jieduandazhan-2-lvu23osgv46nn3rbgque1mbhmpgw|Hanji 265]] presents Dou Ying's legal vulnerability as inseparable from a changed court structure. He is no longer a serving central official and can no longer rely on Empress Dowager Dou, while his rival [[TianFenWesternHan|田蚡]] has the support of [[EmpressDowagerWangWesternHan|王太后]]. His attempt to save Guan Fu therefore moves from advocacy into an accusation that he misled [[HanWudi|汉武帝]].

The episode says Dou Ying then produces an edict from Han Jingdi that could exculpate him, but no corresponding palace copy can be found after Tian Fen allegedly destroys it. The source interprets the resulting forgery charge and execution as political choice under unequal protection rather than law operating in isolation. The archival mechanism and Tian Fen's intervention remain source-attributed, not independently verified here.

## Key Characteristics

- Dou-family outer relative whose influence has declined by the time of the case.
- Political ally and defender of Guan Fu.
- Becomes vulnerable when his testimony conflicts with the checked court record.
- Relies unsuccessfully on an edict attributed to Han Jingdi.
- Functions as Tian Fen's rival and counterweight before his execution.

## Evidence

- Declining protection: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-265-dongchaotingbian-ruhe-yanbian-cheng-jieduandazhan-2-lvu23osgv46nn3rbgque1mbhmpgw|Hanji 265]] says Dou Ying has lost Empress Dowager Dou's protection and no longer holds decisive office.
- Guan Fu defense: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-265-dongchaotingbian-ruhe-yanbian-cheng-jieduandazhan-2-lvu23osgv46nn3rbgque1mbhmpgw|Hanji 265]] makes his effort to save Guan Fu the immediate route into an accusation of deceiving the emperor.
- Edict failure: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-265-dongchaotingbian-ruhe-yanbian-cheng-jieduandazhan-2-lvu23osgv46nn3rbgque1mbhmpgw|Hanji 265]] reports that his Jingdi edict cannot be matched to a palace copy and is recast as a forgery.
- Counterweight role: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-265-dongchaotingbian-ruhe-yanbian-cheng-jieduandazhan-2-lvu23osgv46nn3rbgque1mbhmpgw|Hanji 265]] uses Dou Ying and Tian Fen to illustrate rival outer relatives constraining one another before one side is removed.

## Qualifications

The profile rests on one podcast summary and does not reconstruct the underlying memorials, trial procedure, transmitted versions of the edict, or the full earlier careers of Dou Ying and Guan Fu. The claim that Tian Fen burned an archival copy is retained as the episode's assertion. The page also does not treat political calculation and legal process as mutually exclusive merely because the episode emphasizes the former.

## What Changed

- Created the canonical entity around Dou Ying's failed defense of Guan Fu, lost patronage, and counterweight role against Tian Fen.

## Relationships

- [[GuanFuWesternHan|灌夫]] - ally whose defense draws Dou Ying into the fatal court case.
- [[TianFenWesternHan|田蚡]] - rival outer relative who benefits from Dou Ying's loss of protection and removal.
- [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] - emperor who ultimately authorizes the punishment in the episode's account.
- [[EmpressDowagerWangWesternHan|王太后]] - maternal authority backing Dou Ying's rival Tian Fen.
- [[OuterRelativeBalanceUnderImperialPower|皇权下的外戚制衡]] - political structure in which Dou Ying serves as one of two competing kin-power centers.
