## Overview

窦婴 / Dou Ying is a Western Han outer relative whose current profile spans institutional remonstrance, power, patronage, and decline: an early objection to [[LiuWuLiangKingWesternHan|梁孝王刘武]]'s succession route costs him [[EmpressDowagerDouWesternHan|窦太后]]'s favor; his later military prestige and large guest household give [[TianFenWesternHan|田蚡]] a route upward before policy conflict and patronage reversal leave Dou Ying exposed.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-231-hanjingdi-jiuhou-jing-zaojiu-wulong-chuanwei-shijian-1-lluz1oxfqrazahrdas5sl5ybouwj|Hanji 231 part 1]] supplies the earliest current layer. When [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]] reportedly promises the throne to Liu Wu during a banquet before naming a crown prince, Dou Ying objects that the realm belongs to the Han dynastic order and that succession should pass from father to son. The intervention treats imperial speech as institutionally consequential even when delivered while drinking. The episode says Dowager Dou then has Dou Ying removed from office and barred from palace access, making correct procedure costly when it blocks a senior patron's family preference.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-263-weile-shengguan-ren-keyi-duo-beiwei-1-lgewf4qfarqfjfgp50nss2vf_7y|Hanji 263]] supplies Dou Ying's high-status starting point. During Han Jingdi's early reign, the episode credits him with military merit, prominence in Chang'an, proximity to Dowager Dou, and a large household of guests and specialists. Tian Fen is initially only one undistinguished attendant among them. His extreme service to Dou Ying works because Dou is both a patron and an access bridge to the senior dowager's network.

The episode says Dou Ying recognizes Tian Fen's distinctive attentiveness even while understanding it as flattery. After Wudi's accession, Dou Ying becomes chancellor and Tian Fen grand commandant. The source presents their joint elevation inside a layered Dou-Wang court structure, but does not establish the exact appointment process or how much Dou Ying sponsored Tian Fen personally.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-263-weile-shengguan-ren-keyi-duo-beiwei-2-lvwimzpphquwycrfu-2r6z_r3mqu|Hanji 263 part 2]] explains the reversal. Dou Ying and Tian Fen both lose office after a Confucian-policy challenge to the senior dowager's authority, but Dou's earlier resistance to making the Liang king heir and the new dispute are said to leave him estranged from his aunt. After her death, Tian Fen returns as chancellor through Wang-family protection while Dou remains excluded. Dou then bonds with the similarly demoted Guan Fu, accepts his offer to rebuild elite contacts, prepares an overnight reception after Tian Fen's casual promise to visit, and later refuses Tian Fen's request for southern land.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-264-tianfen-hunyan-xianchang-ruhe-chengle-guanfu-de-duantoutai-lux-kqkfsqpjldtocrk65j5pvl8p|Hanji 264]] shows the reversal in public ritual. Dou presses Guan Fu to attend Tian Fen's wedding and sees guests respond weakly to him but fully to Tian Fen. When Guan Fu is detained, Dou accepts responsibility for bringing him, seeks private intercession, and petitions Wudi after that fails.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-265-dongchaotingbian-ruhe-yanbian-cheng-jieduandazhan-1-lil81vjxn5mait2wapdx_t4wy5hr|Hanji 265 part 1]] presents Dou's proportionality argument and the subsequent mutual exposure in court. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-265-dongchaotingbian-ruhe-yanbian-cheng-jieduandazhan-2-lvu23osgv46nn3rbgque1mbhmpgw|Part 2]] places his vulnerability inside changed patronage: he no longer holds decisive office or enjoys Dowager Dou's protection, while Tian Fen retains Wang's support. His Jingdi edict cannot be matched to a palace copy after Tian Fen allegedly destroys it, and the resulting forgery accusation ends in execution.

## Key Characteristics

- Dou-family outer relative whose father-to-son succession objection protects dynastic procedure while beginning a damaging break with the senior dowager.
- Patron whose large guest network gives the initially obscure Tian Fen access and political familiarity.
- Serves as chancellor alongside Tian Fen's early grand-commandant appointment.
- Presses Guan Fu to attend the later wedding and accepts responsibility after the resulting arrest.
- Moves from private intercession and proportionality argument into mutual court exposure.
- Becomes vulnerable when his protection, office, and archival support fail together.
- Functions as Tian Fen's rival counterweight before execution.

## Qualifications

The six connected summaries do not reconstruct Dou Ying's full military career, household composition, appointment process, patronage of Tian Fen, policy position, relationship with the Liang king, trial procedure, or the transmitted edict record. Hanji 231's banquet words, formal succession rule, office, dismissal mechanism, and palace-access ban remain episode-attributed; the page does not prove that the single objection alone determined his entire later relationship with the dowager. Early attentiveness may be strategic without proving that every part of the Tian Fen relationship was insincere. Dou's loyalty to Guan Fu does not settle whether seeking renewed access or urging wedding attendance was prudent, and political calculation does not exclude legal process. Guest conduct, land terms, reconciliation, spending, motives, security accusations, and alleged archival destruction remain episode-attributed.

## What Changed

- Backfilled Dou Ying's earliest current remonstrance: he treats a drunken succession promise as a dynastic-order problem rather than private family speech.
- Connected Dowager Dou's initial retaliation to the later evidence that his opposition to Liu Wu leaves their patronage relationship broken.
- Added Dou Ying's earlier position as Tian Fen's patron and indirect bridge to Dowager Dou.
- Turned the later status contrast into a full power reversal from Tian Fen's dependence to Dou Ying's vulnerability.
- Preserved the later rescue, archival-failure, and execution sequence as a separate phase rather than reading the early dispute as a complete biography.
