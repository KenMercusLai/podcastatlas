## Overview

窦婴 / Dou Ying is a displaced Western Han outer relative who presses [[GuanFuWesternHan|灌夫]] to attend [[TianFenWesternHan|田蚡]]'s wedding, accepts responsibility when the visit becomes an arrest, and moves from private intercession and a plea for proportionate judgment into open court conflict, an accusation of deceiving the emperor, and execution after Dou-family protection has faded.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-264-tianfen-hunyan-xianchang-ruhe-chengle-guanfu-de-duantoutai-lux-kqkfsqpjldtocrk65j5pvl8p|Hanji 264]] begins with the visible reversal between Dou Ying and Tian Fen. Dou rejects Tian Fen's request for land, while Guan Fu abuses the messenger in his defense. When Guan Fu later declines Tian Fen's wedding because he expects drunken conflict, Dou says the breach has been repaired and presses him to attend. Guests' weak response to Dou's toast, compared with full deference to Tian Fen, then helps produce the resentment Guan Fu acts out.

After Guan Fu is detained, Dou leaves but interprets the outcome as his responsibility because he brought Guan Fu to the banquet. He spends heavily seeking private intercession and, when that fails, accepts the possible loss of his marquisate and petitions [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]. The emperor initially treats the proportionality argument as plausible and sends the matter to public debate.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-265-dongchaotingbian-ruhe-yanbian-cheng-jieduandazhan-1-lil81vjxn5mait2wapdx_t4wy5hr|Hanji 265 part 1]] presents that debate. Dou argues that Guan Fu's immediate wrongdoing is drunken speech and that Tian Fen has attached unrelated accusations. The court audience then becomes a contest over political character and security: Dou attacks Tian Fen's weaknesses, while Tian Fen depicts Dou and Guan Fu's guest networks and commentary on the two palaces as preparation for disorder. The episode does not establish that accusation as true.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-265-dongchaotingbian-ruhe-yanbian-cheng-jieduandazhan-2-lvu23osgv46nn3rbgque1mbhmpgw|Hanji 265 part 2]] presents Dou Ying's legal vulnerability as inseparable from a changed court structure. He is no longer a serving central official and can no longer rely on Empress Dowager Dou, while Tian Fen has the support of [[EmpressDowagerWangWesternHan|王太后]]. His attempt to save Guan Fu therefore moves from advocacy and mutual exposure into an accusation that he misled [[HanWudi|汉武帝]].

The episode says Dou Ying then produces an edict from Han Jingdi that could exculpate him, but no corresponding palace copy can be found after Tian Fen allegedly destroys it. The source interprets the resulting forgery charge and execution as political choice under unequal protection rather than law operating in isolation. The archival mechanism and Tian Fen's intervention remain source-attributed, not independently verified here.

## Key Characteristics

- Dou-family outer relative whose influence has declined by the time of the case.
- Political ally who presses a reluctant Guan Fu to attend the wedding and then accepts responsibility for the resulting danger.
- Moves from failed private intercession to an imperial petition before the case becomes a public court dispute.
- Begins with a proportionality argument but participates in mutual exposure once the dispute reaches court.
- Becomes vulnerable when his testimony conflicts with the checked court record.
- Relies unsuccessfully on an edict attributed to Han Jingdi.
- Functions as Tian Fen's rival and counterweight before his execution.

## Qualifications

The profile rests on three connected podcast summaries and does not reconstruct the underlying memorials, trial procedure, transmitted versions of the edict, or the full earlier careers of Dou Ying and Guan Fu. The reconciliation claim, invitation pressure, banquet gestures, intercession spending, private motives, Tian Fen's security accusation, and alleged destruction of an archival copy remain episode-attributed. Dou's loyalty and sense of responsibility do not settle whether bringing Guan Fu was prudent, and political calculation and legal process are not treated as mutually exclusive merely because the program emphasizes the former.

## What Changed

- Added the pre-debate chain: Dou Ying presses Guan Fu to attend, recognizes responsibility after the arrest, fails at private intercession, and petitions the emperor.
- Made the banquet's unequal ritual deference an observable sign of Dou Ying's loss of standing without treating every guest motive as known.

## Relationships

- [[GuanFuWesternHan|灌夫]] - ally whose defense draws Dou Ying into the fatal court case.
- [[TianFenWesternHan|田蚡]] - rival outer relative who benefits from Dou Ying's loss of protection and removal.
- [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] - emperor who ultimately authorizes the punishment in the episode's account.
- [[EmpressDowagerWangWesternHan|王太后]] - maternal authority backing Dou Ying's rival Tian Fen.
- [[OuterRelativeBalanceUnderImperialPower|皇权下的外戚制衡]] - political structure in which Dou Ying serves as one of two competing kin-power centers.
