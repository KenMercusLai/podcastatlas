## Overview

程不识 / Cheng Bushi is a Western Han general presented through a contrast with [[LiGuangHanGeneral|李广]]. His army maintains ordered movement, camps, watches, signals, and documentation. The burden is high and his troops prefer Li Guang's easier service, but [[SimaGuang|司马光]] treats Cheng's method as the safer model for ordinary commanders because readiness is carried by routines rather than exceptional personal perception.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-257-handai-dagangjing-jiujing-you-duogang-2-lnqd8g2sh79s1uywcbyn56n_vmmv|Hanji 257]] places Cheng in command toward Yanmen while Li Guang operates toward Yunzhong. Both return without attack, so the episode does not demonstrate a battlefield victory for Cheng's system. Its contribution is comparative. Cheng accepts that Li Guang's soldiers are comfortable and willing to die for him, yet argues that a loosely organized camp would be difficult to recover if surprised. His own force imposes exhausting watch and administrative work but keeps a reproducible state of preparation.

The source therefore gives Cheng a bounded institutional role rather than a complete biography. He represents the cost and value of ordinary military procedure: more friction in daily service, less reliance on one commander's unusual awareness, and a stronger floor against avoidable collapse. This does not prove that paperwork volume is inherently useful or that rigid procedure always defeats flexible command.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han general paired with Li Guang as a contrasting command model.
- Maintains tightly ordered marches and encampments rather than loosely following water and grass.
- Uses continuous night-watch signals and extensive military administration to sustain readiness.
- Imposes a daily burden that officers and soldiers reportedly find exhausting.
- Recognizes Li Guang's popularity while judging his loose system vulnerable to surprise.
- Represents procedure-backed reliability rather than a promise of battlefield distinction.

## Qualifications

The current profile rests on one short podcast summary and does not reconstruct Cheng Bushi's wider career, battles, appointments, later reputation, or the full textual context of Sima Guang's judgment. The episode does not show his force winning an engagement, measure whether every administrative task improved readiness, or establish that strict routine alone prevents defeat. Exact offices, routes, dates, unit size, camp mechanics, quoted dialogue, and soldier sentiment remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Created Cheng Bushi as the procedure-backed side of the episode's paired command comparison.
- Preserved troop burden and absent battlefield testing as limits on a simple endorsement of strictness.
- Distinguished reproducible readiness from guaranteed military success.
