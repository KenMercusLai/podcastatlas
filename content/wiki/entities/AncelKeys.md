# Ancel Keys

## Overview

Ancel Keys was the physiologist who directed the [[MinnesotaStarvationExperiment]], a controlled study intended to improve rehabilitation after severe wartime malnutrition.

## Current Profile

In the episode, Keys first works with conscientious objectors on vitamin-deficiency studies and regards them as committed and dependable. The familiar origin story has him proposing deliberate starvation to learn how undernourished people could recover, but [[SarahTracy]]'s archival evidence complicates sole authorship: objectors in the earlier studies sought a peacetime humanitarian experiment, brought church support and grant funding, and helped define its relief purpose.

Keys retained scientific authority over a protocol that produced profound physical and psychological harm. The resulting profile is neither lone visionary nor simple exploiter: he directed consequential research whose feasibility and moral purpose depended materially on volunteer collaborators while retaining distinct institutional duties toward them.

## Key Characteristics

- Directed controlled starvation and rehabilitation research during the final year of World War II.
- Recruited conscientious objectors after earlier vitamin-deficiency work established their reliability.
- Pursued practical knowledge for treating anticipated mass postwar malnutrition.
- Shared the experiment's origin story with volunteers who helped formulate and fund a humanitarian study.
- Held researcher authority and duties that participant enthusiasm could not transfer away.

## Evidence

### Earlier work and recruitment

- [[the-rise-of-the-guinea-pigs]] reports that Keys worked with conscientious objectors in vitamin studies before recruiting from that population for starvation research.

### Scientific purpose

- [[the-rise-of-the-guinea-pigs]] attributes to Keys the goal of understanding both severe undernutrition and rehabilitation after wartime deprivation.

### Volunteer contribution

- [[the-rise-of-the-guinea-pigs]] summarizes [[SarahTracy]]'s correspondence evidence that objectors proposed the peacetime direction and brought church-supported grant funding.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to a structured podcast summary, not Keys's papers, correspondence, protocol, biography, or the complete experiment archive. The episode's recovery of volunteer initiative qualifies a Keys-centered narrative but does not establish an exact division of intellectual credit, funding authority, protocol control, or responsibility.
