# Dan Singletary

## Overview

Dan Singletary was an artist and grassroots organizer who turned Staten Island quality-of-life grievances into an organized secession campaign.

## Current Profile

Singletary and his wife, Alice, moved to [[StatenIsland]] seeking a good community for their children. The Fresh Kills landfill, weak public services, dense development, inadequate sewers, and homelessness near the ferry terminal accumulated into what they called a repeated “sandpapering” of local sensitivities.

He made those complaints political. Singletary argued that poor city management of schools and services justified local control, helped form an official secession party, and organized volunteers who collected and checked the signatures needed to place candidates on its ballot line. His speeches made Fresh Kills a concrete symbol of a borough on which the rest of the city could impose unwanted burdens.

Later living on an upstate New York farm, Singletary still described Staten Island as the smallest borough and vulnerable to being overruled by the rest of the city. The episode treats him as a folk hero of the movement without claiming that his account represented every island resident.

## Key Characteristics

- Artist who became a prominent Staten Island secession advocate.
- Connected service and infrastructure failures to demands for local control.
- Used Fresh Kills as a visible symbol of unequal city burden.
- Organized an official party and ballot-signature campaign.
- Converted cumulative everyday grievances into coordinated political action.

## Qualifications

This profile rests on one structured episode summary and its presentation of archival footage and later recollection. It does not supply the complete petition record, campaign organization, independent evaluation of city services, or the views of residents who opposed secession. Singletary's memories explain his motivation but cannot alone establish the movement's representative cause.

## What Changed

- Established Singletary as the grassroots organizer who converted diffuse complaints into an electoral campaign.
- Preserved the distinction between his movement narrative and the full range of Staten Island opinion.
