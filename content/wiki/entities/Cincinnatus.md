# Cincinnatus

## Overview

Cincinnatus is a Roman republican exemplar remembered for accepting emergency authority, saving the polity, relinquishing command, and returning to his farm.

## Current Profile

The episode uses Cincinnatus primarily as political memory rather than as a fully reconstructed ancient biography. His significance lies in the sequence of reluctance, service, victory, and voluntary withdrawal, which made him a model for [[GeorgeWashington|George Washington]] and for an American ideal in which power becomes more legitimate when its holder visibly gives it up.

## Key Characteristics

- Embodies emergency leadership bounded by voluntary relinquishment.
- Links agrarian simplicity and reluctance to republican legitimacy.
- Supplied the classical model through which Washington's resignation could be understood.

## Qualifications

The source uses the traditional exemplary story and does not test the ancient evidence, chronology, or later embellishment. The page therefore records Cincinnatus's political-memory function, not every element of the story as verified biography.

## What Changed

- Created a canonical profile for the Roman model behind Washington's voluntary-withdrawal symbolism.
