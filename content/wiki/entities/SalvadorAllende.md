# Salvador Allende

## Overview
Salvador Allende was Chile's democratically elected socialist president, overthrown in the military coup of September 11, 1973.

## Current Profile
[[371-the-1973-chilean-coup-general-pinochet-seizes-power-part-2-glt3740766328]] presents Allende as a democratic symbol whose reforms redistributed land, nationalized copper and banks, expanded welfare, and gave many poor and rural Chileans dignity, while inflation, shortages, constitutional conflict, and fear of a more radical left alienated other groups. During the coup he refused escape or exile, defended his constitutional mandate in a final broadcast, and died by suicide as La Moneda fell.

## Key Characteristics
- He pursued socialist transformation through an elected presidency and remained symbolically committed to constitutional legitimacy.
- His reforms generated material and dignitary support among poorer Chileans.
- Inflation, shortages, black markets, and institutional confrontation made his government frightening to many middle-class and conservative Chileans.
- He misjudged Pinochet's loyalty and the military's willingness to overthrow him.
- His last speech and death became competing objects of democratic and revolutionary memory.

## Evidence
- Reform and polarization: [[371-the-1973-chilean-coup-general-pinochet-seizes-power-part-2-glt3740766328]] links redistribution, nationalization, and welfare expansion to support, while also recording inflation and scarcity.
- Constitutional stand: [[371-the-1973-chilean-coup-general-pinochet-seizes-power-part-2-glt3740766328]] says Allende refused exile because he was the elected president and would stand by the constitution.
- Death and memory: [[371-the-1973-chilean-coup-general-pinochet-seizes-power-part-2-glt3740766328]] favors the eyewitness and 2011 forensic suicide account over the later story that Allende died fighting.

## Qualifications
The source rejects both a flat saint-versus-villain story and any equivalence between contested government policy and military dictatorship. The hypothetical referendum announcement, precise economic causation, constitutional claims by opposing institutions, and Allende's private expectations remain uncertain or source-scoped.

## What Changed
- Created a profile holding democratic legitimacy, redistributive support, economic crisis, and institutional conflict together.
- Distinguished the forensic account of suicide from the later combat-death myth.
- Added Allende's final constitutional refusal of exile as central to his historical memory.
