## Overview
[[HeQiLateHan]] is a Jiangdong officer under [[SunQuan]] whose current profile joins terrain-led mountain warfare to the conversion of pacification into troops, counties, and commandery administration.

## Current Profile
In [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1061-yuanshao-zhizi-he-shafu-chouren-jiecheng-qinjia-lnsnj6ipiyscw-vopnjjgoxpnt5r|Hanji 1061]], Sun Quan sends He Qi against Jian'an, Hanxing, and Nanping county rebellions, each described as involving more than ten thousand people. He Qi suppresses them, rebuilds county seats, selects more than ten thousand elite troops, and is promoted to Pingdong colonel.

A second operation reveals the method behind another pacification success. Facing a steep stronghold that has defeated earlier attacks, He Qi spends more than a month examining the terrain, identifies a hidden climbing route from traces on a cliff, draws defenders toward a noisy frontal threat, and sends selected troops up rope ladders at night. The rear attack collapses the position, after which Sun Quan creates Xindu commandery and appoints He Qi administrator. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1111-caocao-zui-tong-de-yinian-erzi-yaozhe-qiu-lianyin-ai-dui-lr5tvu8-kti41ezapvq2vz2wfjh6|Hanji 1111]]

## Key Characteristics
- Serves as a pacification officer in the interior Jiangdong problem created by [[Shanyue]] resistance.
- Refuses predictable frontal assault when terrain makes conventional strength costly.
- Combines prolonged reconnaissance, attention-fixing display, and a selected force on a hidden route.
- Converts rebellion suppression into administrative reconstruction, new troops, and commandery government.
- Strengthens Sun Quan's authority through practical local control rather than battlefield victory alone.

## Evidence
- Rebellion suppression and reconstruction: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1061-yuanshao-zhizi-he-shafu-chouren-jiecheng-qinjia-lnsnj6ipiyscw-vopnjjgoxpnt5r|Hanji 1061]] names He Qi as Sun Quan's officer for the Jian'an, Hanxing, and Nanping rebellions and credits him with pacification, reconstruction, troop selection, and promotion.
- Terrain-led deception: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1111-caocao-zui-tong-de-yinian-erzi-yaozhe-qiu-lianyin-ai-dui-lr5tvu8-kti41ezapvq2vz2wfjh6|Hanji 1111]] says He Qi reconnoiters for more than a month, fixes defenders at the front, and sends selected troops up an unexpected cliff route.
- Administrative consolidation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1061-yuanshao-zhizi-he-shafu-chouren-jiecheng-qinjia-lnsnj6ipiyscw-vopnjjgoxpnt5r|Hanji 1061]] links victory to rebuilt counties and troop extraction, while [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1111-caocao-zui-tong-de-yinian-erzi-yaozhe-qiu-lianyin-ai-dui-lr5tvu8-kti41ezapvq2vz2wfjh6|Hanji 1111]] links it to the creation of Xindu commandery and He Qi's appointment.

## Qualifications
The two sources do not provide a complete career. Hanji 1111's assailant labels, force and household counts, month-long reconnaissance, cliff traces, rope-ladder mechanics, exact place readings, and Xindu administrative sequence remain source-scoped. The tactics show a successful local case rather than a general rule that high-risk climbing attacks are normally preferable.

## What Changed
- Added prolonged reconnaissance and deceptive cliff ascent as the operational layer behind He Qi's pacification profile.
- Added the Xindu commandery appointment as a second example of victory becoming administration.
