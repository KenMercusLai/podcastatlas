# Samer Hattar

## Overview
Samer Hattar is a neuroscientist presented in the source as chief of the Section on Light and Circadian Rhythms at the U.S. National Institute of Mental Health.

## Current Profile
Hattar studies how light reaches beyond conscious vision to regulate biological clocks, sleep, mood, learning, feeding, and activity. In the bounded interview, he uses melanopsin-containing retinal ganglion cells to connect environmental brightness with brain timing systems, then broadens the explanation through a tripartite model combining circadian phase, homeostatic drives, and direct environmental effects.

His practical stance emphasizes coordinated signals rather than one universal schedule. Outdoor morning light, dimmer evenings, regular sleep and meals, and individually workable exercise timing are presented as alignment tools, while personal routines, exact thresholds, and emerging mood pathways are kept distinct from settled clinical prescriptions.

The Essentials edit preserves that profile while making its everyday mismatch argument more explicit: an indoor routine can create jet-lag-like delay without travel, and a late schedule may reinforce itself when it removes morning light. This is a condensed release of the same interview, so it adds emphasis and accessibility rather than independent confirmation.

## Key Characteristics
- Explains light as a physiological signal as well as a source of conscious vision.
- Uses melanopsin retinal pathways to connect ambient brightness with circadian entrainment.
- Integrates circadian clocks, homeostatic need, and immediate environmental inputs in one model.
- Treats light, sleep, food, and exercise timing as interacting rather than independent variables.
- Distinguishes mechanistic evidence, personal practice, and unresolved human measurement questions.

## Qualifications
This profile is bounded to one public interview and its condensed edit, not two independent interviews, and is not a complete biography, institutional record, publication history, or priority account of melanopsin research. Hattar's personal sleep, meal, exercise, and lighting routine is illustrative rather than a universal prescription, and the episode's animal, mechanistic, and observational claims do not by themselves establish clinical treatment.
