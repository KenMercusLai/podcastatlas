# 徐阶 / Xu Jie

## Overview

徐阶 / Xu Jie appears as the Ming court rival who displaced [[YanSongMing|严嵩]] by supplying better practical advice, cultivating trusted intermediaries, and waiting until [[JiajingEmperor|嘉靖帝]] was ready to accept an attack on the Yan household.

## Current Profile

The source presents Xu Jie's victory as a contest in ruler-centered access rather than an institutional defeat of personal rule. His promise to rebuild Wanshou Palace quickly answered an immediate imperial need better than Yan's proposal to move the emperor to the politically charged Southern City.

Xu then used the Daoist Lan Daoxing and a bribed eunuch to make hostile judgments about Yan legible inside Jiajing's own spiritual and informational world. Zou Yinglong's corruption memorial became effective only after that shift in imperial receptivity. Xu therefore exemplifies tactical patience and coalition work under concentrated personal authority, not an escape from it.

## Key Characteristics

- Court rival who waited through Yan Song's period of protection.
- Practical adviser who gained trust through the Wanshou Palace rebuilding proposal.
- User of religious and palace intermediaries to shape information reaching Jiajing.
- Coordinator of timing between changed imperial mood and formal accusation.
- Beneficiary of Yan's aging, bad advice, and dependence on Yan Shifan.

## Evidence

- Practical displacement: [[44-jianchen-ziji-tiao-chulaile-yansong-vs-pengbaer-houjue-lmx4poxetwxy625kriodizxlubou]] contrasts Yan's Southern City proposal with Xu's rapid rebuilding promise.
- Intermediary strategy: [[44-jianchen-ziji-tiao-chulaile-yansong-vs-pengbaer-houjue-lmx4poxetwxy625kriodizxlubou]] says Xu worked through Lan Daoxing and a bribed eunuch to alter Jiajing's view of Yan.
- Attack timing: [[44-jianchen-ziji-tiao-chulaile-yansong-vs-pengbaer-houjue-lmx4poxetwxy625kriodizxlubou]] links Zou Yinglong's memorial and Yan's retirement to the prior change in imperial confidence.

## Qualifications

The source gives Xu mainly the role of Yan Song's successful opponent. It does not provide a complete administrative biography or establish the reliability of every intermediary and prophecy story. The account's bribery, motive, and coordination claims remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Established Xu Jie as the rival who converted practical advice, intermediaries, and timing into the collapse of Yan Song's protection.

## Relationships

- [[JiajingEmperor|嘉靖帝]] - ruler whose confidence Xu had to win before formal accusations could succeed.
- [[YanSongMing|严嵩]] - incumbent rival displaced after his usefulness and access declined.
- [[YanShifan|严世蕃]] - vulnerable household actor whose conduct helped make the final attack sustainable.
- [[CourtDependentMinisterialPower|君主依附型权臣权力]] - system Xu navigated and ultimately inherited rather than abolished.
