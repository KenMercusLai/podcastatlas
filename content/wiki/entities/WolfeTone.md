# Theobald Wolfe Tone

## Overview

Theobald Wolfe Tone was a founder of the [[UnitedIrishmen]] and a central advocate of cross-confessional Irish reform who later sought French military support for a self-determining republic.

## Current Profile

The sources place Tone at the transition from reform to revolution. The United Irishmen began in October 1791 around parliamentary reform and Catholic representation rather than immediate insurrection. Suppression and the failure of legal routes pushed the organization underground, while Tone went first to the United States and then to France seeking the external force needed to overthrow British rule.

In France he became a citizen and an officer. The failed 1796 expedition and the 1798 rebellion show both the strategic logic and danger of that choice: a movement claiming national liberation could use foreign alliance to pursue sovereignty, while British authorities could treat the same conduct as direct treason.

## Key Characteristics

- Founding figure in the United Irishmen's 1791 reform coalition.
- Advocate of cooperation across Catholic, Protestant, and dissenter lines.
- Exile who sought French support after Irish prosecution and suppression.
- French citizen and military officer in the source's account.
- Revolutionary whose republican project made British and Irish allegiance claims collide.
