# Elizabeth Sennett

## Overview

Elizabeth Sennett is the murder victim whose death begins the case examined by [[TheAlabamaMurders]] and whose family's long grief remains part of its final episode.

## Current Profile

The sources say Sennett's husband arranged her murder and recruited two troubled young men, including [[KennethSmith]], to carry it out. Their focus is Smith's trauma and the later punishment process rather than a biography of Sennett, but they preserve her death, Smith's intended apology, and her sons' decades-long wait as essential context. The series' psychological account and criticism of Alabama do not erase the murder or treat the losses of victim and condemned families as interchangeable.

## Key Characteristics

- Murder victim at the origin of the Alabama case.
- Wife and mother whose sons waited roughly thirty-five years for Smith's execution.
- Essential moral context for a series otherwise focused on punishment institutions and execution trauma.
- Person to whose family Smith reportedly intended to apologize during the failed execution.
- Represented only sparsely in the supplied final-episode summary.

## Evidence

### Case origin and family aftermath

- [[the-alabama-murders-part-7-the-second-warrant]] identifies Sennett's murder, her husband's role, the recruited killers, and her sons' presence at the post-execution press conference.
- [[the-alabama-murders-part-6-the-porterfield-sessions]] reports that Smith rehearsed an apology to Sennett's family while awaiting their entry to the witness area.

## Qualifications

This is deliberately not a full biography or reconstruction of the murder. The supplied episode summaries center Smith and the execution, giving little information about Sennett's life, family perspectives, investigation, or trial evidence. Smith's intended apology is reported intention, not evidence of how Sennett's relatives received it; the page does not infer views for her or her sons beyond what the sources report.
