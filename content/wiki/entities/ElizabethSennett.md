# Elizabeth Sennett

## Overview

Elizabeth Sennett was a homemaker and mother whose planned assault and death begin the case examined by [[TheAlabamaMurders]]. Her life, reported fear of [[CharlesSennett]], fatal injuries, sons' abrupt loss, and family's long grief remain essential to the series.

## Current Profile

Part 2 remembers Sennett through her sons as a kind and consistently present mother. It reconstructs how Parker and Smith gained her trust, attacked her, staged a burglary, and left her critically wounded; paramedics found a faint pulse, but she died in hospital. The episode says Charles arranged the attack, while Part 3 complicates the fatal-conduct account: an unrecovered smaller knife appeared to cause the lethal chest wounds, the timeline places Parker and Smith away before those wounds, and nurse Susan Moseley recalled Sennett reporting severe bruising, fear that Charles would kill her, and a belief that she could not escape the marriage. Part 3 therefore advances Charles as the most plausible person to have completed the killing, but the bounded sources do not independently establish that theory.

Later episodes center the men's trials, trauma, punishment, and executions, while preserving Sennett's death, her sons' grief, admissions of involvement, and apologies as moral limits. Criticism of Alabama's sentencing and execution systems does not erase the assault or make the losses of victim and condemned families interchangeable.

## Key Characteristics

- Homemaker and nurturing mother remembered by her sons as consistently present for them.
- Murder victim whose sons lost both parents within one week and later waited roughly thirty-five years for Smith's execution.
- Woman whom a nurse recalled as bruised, afraid Charles would kill her, and unable to see a way out of the marriage.
- Victim whose fatal chest wounds were attributed by the episode to a smaller knife that investigators never recovered.
- Person whom Parker and Smith admitted assaulting while disputing or leaving unresolved who inflicted the fatal wounds.
- Person to whose family Smith reportedly intended to apologize and Parker apologized for becoming involved.
- Essential moral context for a series focused heavily on punishment institutions and execution trauma.

### Life, abuse, and fatal injuries

- [[the-alabama-murders-part-3-a-peculiar-institution]] reports Moseley's account of bruising, fear, saved money, and attempted help, along with the weapon mismatch, survival timeline, and alternative-assailant theory.

### Case origin and responsibility boundary

- [[the-alabama-murders-part-7-the-second-warrant]] identifies Sennett's murder, her husband's role in arranging the attack, the recruited men, and her sons' presence after Smith's execution.
- [[the-alabama-murders-part-4-the-protocol]] reports Parker's admitted assault, denial that he killed Sennett, separate jury recommendation, and final apology for his involvement.

### Family and later punishment

- [[the-alabama-murders-part-6-the-porterfield-sessions]] reports that Smith rehearsed an apology to Sennett's family while awaiting their entry to the witness area.
- [[the-alabama-murders-part-5-cruel-and-unusual]] places her murder before Smith's reported post-crime statements, conviction, 11-1 life recommendation, and judicially imposed death sentence.

## Qualifications

This remains an incomplete biography filtered through a series centered on the defendants and Alabama's punishment system. Her sons' memories, witness accounts, Moseley's recollections, and the episode's medical timeline require the underlying testimony, medical records, trial exhibits, and fuller family perspectives. Part 2's attack reconstruction and Part 3's claim that Charles inflicted the fatal wounds are related but not identical accounts; the latter remains a circumstantial episode conclusion, not an independent adjudication. The men's apologies and claims do not establish how Sennett's relatives interpreted them.
