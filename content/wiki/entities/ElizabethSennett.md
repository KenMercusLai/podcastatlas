# Elizabeth Sennett

## Overview

Elizabeth Sennett is the murder victim whose death begins the case examined by [[TheAlabamaMurders]] and whose life, reported fear of her husband, fatal injuries, and family's long grief remain essential to the series.

## Current Profile

The sources say Sennett's husband arranged an attack and recruited [[JohnForrestParker]] and [[KennethSmith]], who admitted violent participation and were convicted of her murder. Part 3 complicates the fatal-conduct account: an unrecovered smaller knife appeared to cause the lethal chest wounds, the episode's timeline places Parker and Smith away before those wounds, and nurse Susan Moseley recalled Sennett reporting severe bruising, fear that Charles would kill her, and a belief that she could not escape the marriage. The episode therefore advances Charles Sennett as the most plausible person to have completed the killing, but the bounded sources do not independently establish that theory.

Later episodes center the men's trials, trauma, punishment, and executions, while preserving Sennett's death, her sons' grief, admissions of involvement, and apologies as moral limits. Criticism of Alabama's sentencing and execution systems does not erase the assault or make the losses of victim and condemned families interchangeable.

## Key Characteristics

- Murder victim at the origin of the Alabama cases.
- Wife and mother whose sons waited roughly thirty-five years for Smith's execution.
- Woman whom a nurse recalled as bruised, afraid Charles would kill her, and unable to see a way out of the marriage.
- Victim whose fatal chest wounds were attributed by the episode to a smaller knife that investigators never recovered.
- Person whom Parker and Smith admitted assaulting while disputing or leaving unresolved who inflicted the fatal wounds.
- Person to whose family Smith reportedly intended to apologize and Parker apologized for becoming involved.
- Essential moral context for a series focused heavily on punishment institutions and execution trauma.

## Evidence

### Life, abuse, and fatal injuries

- [[the-alabama-murders-part-3-a-peculiar-institution]] reports Moseley's account of bruising, fear, saved money, and attempted help, along with the weapon mismatch, survival timeline, and alternative-assailant theory.

### Case origin and responsibility boundary

- [[the-alabama-murders-part-7-the-second-warrant]] identifies Sennett's murder, her husband's role in arranging the attack, the recruited men, and her sons' presence after Smith's execution.
- [[the-alabama-murders-part-4-the-protocol]] reports Parker's admitted assault, denial that he killed Sennett, separate jury recommendation, and final apology for his involvement.

### Family and later punishment

- [[the-alabama-murders-part-6-the-porterfield-sessions]] reports that Smith rehearsed an apology to Sennett's family while awaiting their entry to the witness area.
- [[the-alabama-murders-part-5-cruel-and-unusual]] places her murder before Smith's reported post-crime statements, conviction, 11-1 life recommendation, and judicially imposed death sentence.

## Qualifications

This remains an incomplete biography filtered through a series centered on the defendants and Alabama's punishment system. Moseley's recollections and the episode's medical timeline materially deepen Sennett's profile but require the underlying testimony, medical records, trial exhibits, and family perspectives. The claim that Charles inflicted the fatal wounds is a circumstantial episode conclusion, not an independent adjudication. The men's apologies and claims do not establish how Sennett's relatives interpreted them.

## What Changed

- Added Sennett's reported bruising, fear of Charles, savings for her sons, and perceived inability to leave.
- Added the smaller-knife evidence and survival timeline to the account of her fatal injuries.
- Reframed her husband's role from arranging the attack alone to the episode's disputed theory that he completed the killing.
