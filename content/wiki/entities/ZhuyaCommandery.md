## Overview

珠崖郡 / Zhuya Commandery enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-472-cong-qizhi-zhuya-dao-zairu-bantu-jiemi-hainan-lishi-lhvp37w_0z8rebkka6squgfgledp|Hanji 472]] as the Western Han commandery on Hainan whose repeated unrest forces [[HanYuandi|汉元帝]] to choose between suppression, garrisoning, and abolition. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-471-qizhuya-yi-beihou-hanchao-weihe-fangqi-zhengfuyu-lqf0rzjmdzvcjvbtrnrrjmenoy6l|Hanji 471]] supplies the policy-argument layer behind that choice: [[JiaJuanzhiWesternHan|贾捐之]] treats Zhuya and Dan'er as remote, high-cost, low-stability direct-rule commitments.

## Current Profile

Hanji 472 treats Zhuya as a test of how far direct frontier administration should extend when state capacity is strained. The episode says local disorder had repeatedly killed officials and commoners, while the wider empire faced Guandong famine, thin stores, and commoner hunger. Hanji 471 adds Jia Juanzhi's argumentative machinery: ancient sage-kings ruled only where governance could take hold, Qin-style and Han Wudi-style expansion could hollow the interior, and a humid island campaign would consume troops before producing durable order. Yuan therefore abolishes the commandery and allows willing people to attach to the interior without forcing the unwilling.

The profile is not one of simple abandonment or simple restoration. Hanji 472 says some scholars read post-abolition Hainan as attached to Hepu Commandery, but it also stresses that the move from two commanderies and sixteen counties to one commandery/one county and then county abolition marks a major loss of effective control. Later Eastern Han references to Ma Yuan and Zhuya are treated as restored contact and tribute, with the Zhuya county seat on the mainland at Xuwen rather than a restoration of direct island rule. Hanji 471 also keeps a counterweight visible: the host's modern commentary warns that territory, population, and strategic depth are major state assets, so Zhuya's abolition remains a source-scoped capacity judgment rather than a universal rule that distant territory is dispensable.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han island-frontier commandery associated with Hainan.
- Repeatedly troubled by local violence and rebellion against Han officials and people.
- Abolished by Han Yuandi in 46 BCE amid famine, fiscal-capacity pressure, and Jia Juanzhi's anti-expedition argument.
- Continued as a name and loose attachment problem after formal commandery abolition.
- Later Eastern Han contact is framed as tribute and reception rather than restored direct island administration.

## Qualifications

This page is source-scoped to Hanji 471 and Hanji 472. It does not settle the full administrative geography of early Hainan, the exact chronology of every county-level change, local Hainan social structures, the original wording of Jia Juanzhi's memorial, or the scholarly debate over Hepu jurisdiction. "Zhuya" is treated here as an administrative and memory label, not as proof of continuous direct rule.
