## Overview

孙寿 / Sun Shou is [[LiangJiLateHan|Liang Ji]]'s wife and a late-Han household power actor whose profile now extends from 150 CE privilege and estate luxury through the 159 CE collapse. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-833-tanguan-neng-you-duo-lipu-kanwan-dadieyanjing-lqudlijjyqmcro-wsmrvwkodgt72|Hanji 833]] presents her as receiving a revenue-bearing title with high insignia, sharing Liang's mansion and garden extravagance, and standing near the household access world represented by [[QinGongLateHan|Qin Gong]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-847-nvren-biande-haoming-cong-dongde-zhe-liang-zi-kaishi-lrlov43jtz0vq-o-5m8r2cqo0etx|Hanji 847]] presents her as controlling Liang at home, punishing his outside mistress, setting Luoyang beauty fashions, and helping send [[EmpressDengHuanLateHan|Deng Meng]] into [[EmperorHuanOfHan|Emperor Huan]]'s harem. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-848-shunjian-kuatai-wo-weihe-shuo-ta-si-you-yu-gu-lgqvgmxlwga2fknmtxpj3ya9chk0|Hanji 848]] then shows her dying with Liang and being swept into the Liang-Sun purge.

## Current Profile

Hanji 833 supplies the earliest current Sun Shou layer. After Liang dowager's death, Sun Shou is granted a title, rent income, and insignia that the episode compares to long-princess-level status. The same source places her beside Liang Ji in the construction of mansions, gardens, ponds, hills, and entertainment spaces, making her household power material and fiscal before the later harem and fashion stories.

The episode also links Sun Shou to the household access field. Qin Gong can freely enter her residence while provincial and commandery officials visit him before taking office. This does not make Sun Shou the sole controller of those officials, but it shows that the Liang-Sun household has become a political threshold through which public officeholders seek access.

Hanji 847 gives Sun Shou active power before her death. In the household layer, Liang Ji dominates emperors and court officials but fears Sun Shou at home. When she discovers his outside mistress, she beats the woman, shaves her head, disfigures her face, and forces Liang to seek mediation through Sun Shou's mother. This makes Sun Shou more than a passive spouse: she disciplines the household ruler who otherwise appears politically untouchable.

The same source gives her cultural and harem-channel influence. Sun Shou is credited with fashions such as sorrowful brows, tear makeup, falling-horse hair, bent-waist walking, and toothache smiling, which Luoyang women imitate. She also helps send Deng Meng into Huan's palace through kinship and remarriage ties, making her household influence part of the political chain that leads to Liang's failed absorption of Deng Meng and the Xuan crisis.

Hanji 848 supplies the collapse. Once Huan's side controls palace access, seals, tallies, and force, Liang's general-in-chief seal is removed and Liang plus Sun Shou kill themselves on the same day. The source then names the Sun family in the wider punishment: Huan arrests Liang and Sun relatives, executes family members publicly, removes dependent officials, and dismisses hundreds of former clerks and guests.

## Key Characteristics

- Liang Ji's wife and domestic counterweight inside a household that otherwise projects overwhelming court power.
- Revenue-bearing titled spouse whose status and estate luxury make Liang-family power visible through marriage and household wealth before the coup.
- Violent household enforcer who punishes Liang's outside mistress and compels mediation.
- Luoyang fashion setter whose embodied style becomes a social signal beyond the household.
- Harem-channel actor who helps move Deng Meng into Emperor Huan's palace orbit.
- Sun-family node in the wider Liang-Sun purge after the coup.
- Source-bounded figure whose current significance is household power, cultural imitation, harem placement, and purge scope.

## Qualifications

The page remains source-bounded. Hanji 833 adds an earlier fiscal and estate layer, but its income, title, and household-access details are not a complete account of Sun Shou's independent authority. Qin Gong's access to her residence shows household political threshold power, not necessarily that she personally controlled every official who visited him. Hanji 847 expands Sun Shou's profile sharply, but its fashion anecdotes and mistress-punishment scene should be treated as the episode's transmitted characterization rather than a complete biography or independent policy record. Hanji 848 still makes her final political role part of Liang's household collapse and the breadth of the Liang-Sun punishment after Huan's coup.

## What Changed

- Hanji 833 moves Sun Shou's profile earlier, adding titled revenue, insignia, estate luxury, and household access around Qin Gong before the harem-placement and collapse layers.
- Hanji 847 expands Sun Shou from a narrow joint-suicide figure into a household enforcer, fashion setter, and harem-placement conduit.
- Hanji 848 remains the evidence for her final death and the wider Liang-Sun family purge.
