## Overview

济东王刘彭离 / Liu Pengli is a Western Han vassal king and son of [[LiuWuLiangKingWesternHan|梁孝王刘武]]. He receives Jidong when [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]] divides Liang among five brothers, then later loses title and territory after a verified pattern of robbery and killing.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-242-cong-chengxiang-dao-mianguan-zhouyafu-fanle-zhichang-daji-2-lvwwjv0w-widjjzafmweqdoepydg|Hanji 242]] supplies Liu Pengli's accession setting. After Liu Wu dies and [[EmpressDowagerDouWesternHan|窦太后]] blames Jingdi, the emperor grants kingships to all five sons; Liu Pengli receives Jidong. The episode interprets this as family consolation that also prevents one heir from inheriting all of Liang's strength.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-338-1-mei-tianli-shasi-shangbairen-ta-jing-bu-bei-pan-si-lggp0q9ka9jhb9pej9z7w1ue5lod|Hanji 338-1]] presents Liu Pengli's later twenty-nine-year reign as a local breakdown of accountability. He allegedly goes out after dark with servants and fugitive youths, attacks travelers, kills for pleasure, and takes property; more than one hundred victims can eventually be named, while fear suppresses accusation inside the kingdom.

A victim's son succeeds in reaching the central court. After investigation overcomes [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s initial suspicion that the accusation may be slander against the imperial house, officials request execution. Wudi instead reduces Liu Pengli to commoner status and exiles him to Shangyong. The abolition of Jidong and reorganization of its territory as Dahe Commandery remove his territorial power, but the refusal to execute him makes the case an example of [[RoyalKinshipPenaltyMitigation|宗室亲缘减刑]].

## Key Characteristics

- Son of Liang Xiao Wang Liu Wu and a Western Han imperial kinsman.
- One of five brothers enfeoffed when Jingdi divides Liang after their father's death.
- Rules Jidong for a reported twenty-nine years.
- Leads servants and fugitive youths in repeated night robbery and killing.
- Benefits from local fear that prevents ordinary accusation and night travel.
- Exposed after a victim's son gets a complaint to the central court.
- Spared execution but stripped of kingship, reduced to commoner status, and exiled.

## Qualifications

The page is grounded in two short popular-history episodes. “刘鹏黎” is normalized to 刘彭离 from the Jidong, parentage, reign-length, exile, and commandery context. Hanji 242's exact date, sibling titles, dowager dialogue, and claim that division was designed to weaken Liang require primary-text comparison. Hanji 338-1 supplies a moralized narrative rather than a complete legal dossier: exact victim count beyond “more than one hundred,” investigative procedure, statutory charge, Wudi's private motive, and exile conditions remain uncertain. The claim that inherited merit caused the commutation is the episode's interpretation of Wudi's refusal, not a fully documented sentencing opinion.

## What Changed

- Added Liu Pengli's initial enfeoffment through the five-way division of Liang.
- Connected the anti-concentration logic of his accession to the later abolition of Jidong after his crimes.
- Preserved the distinction between severe punishment and equal punishment under royal kinship.

## Relationships

- [[LiuWuLiangKingWesternHan|梁孝王刘武]] - father whose rebellion-era service becomes part of the explanation for clemency.
- [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]] - uncle and ruler who creates Jidong in the posthumous division of Liang.
- [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] - imperial cousin and sentencing ruler who refuses the requested execution.
- [[RoyalKinshipPenaltyMitigation|宗室亲缘减刑]] - governance pattern illustrated by Liu Pengli's commuted punishment.
- [[RuleOverKinshipPower|法度压过亲缘特权]] - contrast case showing that enforcement can reach royal kin without eliminating privilege.
- [[WesternHanDynasty|西汉]] - dynasty that abolishes Jidong and reabsorbs its territory after his deposition.
