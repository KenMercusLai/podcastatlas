# Apollonius of Rhodes

## Overview

Apollonius of Rhodes was the Hellenistic poet of the [[Argonautica]], represented by the source as an Alexandrian scholar who reshaped the voyage of [[Jason]] and [[MedeaCharacter|Medea]] for a post-Alexander literary world.

## Current Profile

[[605-greek-myths-jason-the-quest-for-the-golden-fleece-part-4-glt3067620137]] places Apollonius around 250 BCE, working in the cultural orbit of [[Alexandria]] and the [[LibraryOfAlexandria]]. His epic depends on Homer, Hesiod, Pindar, Euripides, local traditions, and scholarly comparison, but its recombination of those materials is creative rather than merely antiquarian.

The episode frames his method through a modern shared-universe analogy: familiar characters, monsters, places, and prehistories reward recognition while a new plot reorganizes them. The supposed conflict with Callimachus represents a debate about scale, genre, and whether long epic remained viable, but the biographical details are not independently established here.

## Key Characteristics

- Hellenistic epic poet associated with Alexandria and Rhodes.
- Author who reorganizes inherited Greek stories rather than claiming a single canonical version.
- Scholar-poet whose intertextual detail depends on knowledge of earlier literature.
- Adapter who makes Medea's love and magic propel an adventure whose tragic future remains visible.
- Figure through whom literary preservation and creative transformation become inseparable.
