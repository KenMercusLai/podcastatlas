# Greek War of Independence

## Overview

The Greek War of Independence appears in the wiki as the revolutionary conflict that drew [[LordByron|Lord Byron / 拜伦]] from Italian exile to Cephalonia and Missolonghi in 1823–24.

## Current Profile

In [[443-lord-byron-death-of-a-vampire-part-4-glt3247503776]], the war is less a full military history than the setting in which European philhellenism, money, celebrity, factional liaison, and bodily risk meet. The London Greek Committee and Hobhouse encouraged Byron, while Greek leaders treated his international fame as potentially useful to the cause.

Byron's contribution combined fundraising and asset liquidation, purchase of powder and uniforms, mediation among London supporters, Greek rebels, and British actors, and willingness to remain at unhealthy Missolonghi. His death made him a martyr-like European symbol, but the episode does not establish how decisive his practical contribution was to eventual independence.

## Key Characteristics

- National struggle attracting European Romantic and philhellenic support.
- Conflict in which foreign celebrity could function as diplomatic and fundraising capital.
- Politically fragmented theater requiring liaison as well as military resources.
- Material environment of blockade, hunger, rain, mosquitoes, and disease at Missolonghi.

## Evidence

- Mobilization: [[443-lord-byron-death-of-a-vampire-part-4-glt3247503776]] links Byron's decision to Hobhouse and the London Greek Committee.
- Material contribution: [[443-lord-byron-death-of-a-vampire-part-4-glt3247503776]] describes asset sales, gunpowder purchases, uniforms, and liaison work from Cephalonia.
- Missolonghi: [[443-lord-byron-death-of-a-vampire-part-4-glt3247503776]] records Byron's January 1824 welcome, worsening illness, refusal to leave, and death on 19 April.
