# Pompeii

## Overview

Pompeii is the Roman city south of [[MountVesuvius]] reconstructed in the source through its rebuilding after the 62 CE earthquake, its final hours during the [[EruptionOfVesuvius79|79 CE eruption]], and the archaeological traces of inhabitants who fled, sheltered, or died.

## Current Profile

Pompeii's destruction unfolded over time. Darkness and wind-driven pumice obstructed streets, overloaded roofs, and made both flight and shelter dangerous, yet the city initially escaped the surge that destroyed [[Herculaneum]]. A temporary easing near dawn created movement toward the gates before the eruption column's final collapse sent a lethal flow across the city. Its remains preserve both mass catastrophe and particular lives through bodies, clothing, medical tools, sacred objects, household goods, and animal casts.

## Key Characteristics

- Rebuilding city still repairing earthquake damage seventeen years after the 62 CE shock.
- Urban community continuing ordinary milling, harvest, wine production, commerce, and worship amid poorly understood warnings.
- City first endangered by darkness, pumice accumulation, blocked routes, and roof collapse.
- Site of a temporary escape window before a later fatal pyroclastic flow.
- Archaeological record preserving differentiated people, possessions, animals, buildings, and volcanic deposits.

## Evidence

- Pre-disaster continuity: [[355-roman-apocalypse-pompeii-79-ad-glt6664993493]] describes rebuilding, food preparation, harvest, wine making, and business before the eruption.
- Pumice phase: [[355-roman-apocalypse-pompeii-79-ad-glt6664993493]] links darkness, street obstruction, roof loading, and failed sea escape to the city's first crisis.
- Final movement and death: [[355-roman-apocalypse-pompeii-79-ad-glt6664993493]] describes survivors moving toward gates before the later flow.
- Material traces: [[355-roman-apocalypse-pompeii-79-ad-glt6664993493]] cites victims, a doctor's instruments, sacred objects, household possessions, shackles, and the dog cast.

## Qualifications

The episode synthesizes archaeology, Plinian testimony, and volcanology; not every named person's movement or motive is directly recoverable. Victim totals, flow timing, exact depth, sea conditions, and the interpretation of individual finds remain source-scoped.
