# Bogside

## Overview
The Bogside is the Catholic district of [[Derry]] that anchors two major Troubles turning points in the current sources: the 1969 [[BattleOfTheBogside]] and the 1972 [[BloodySunday]] shootings.

## Current Profile
The current profile is a district whose meaning changes with the conflict. In 1969, the Bogside is a self-defending Catholic area where barricades, petrol bombs, first aid stations, RUC force, and British Army deployment mark the move from protest to street battle. In 1972, it sits inside [[FreeDerry]] as a no-go area where a civil-rights march, limited arrest plan, armoured personnel carriers, and 1 Para's advance end in Bloody Sunday. The place therefore tracks the reversal from temporary Catholic welcome for British troops to deep distrust.

## Key Characteristics
- Catholic district in Derry central to the conflict's street-war geography.
- Site of barricaded self-protection during the 1969 Battle of the Bogside.
- Area initially entered by the [[BritishArmy]] as a welcomed buffer by many Catholics.
- Part of [[FreeDerry]] by the 1971-1972 no-go-area phase.
- Operational space where the 30 January 1972 arrest plan becomes Bloody Sunday.

## Evidence
- 1969 street battle: [[698-the-troubles-the-brink-of-civil-war-part-2-glt2308408988]] describes barricades, petrol bombs, first aid stations, water cannon, tear gas, burning shops, and clashing interpretations in the Bogside.
- Initial army welcome: [[698-the-troubles-the-brink-of-civil-war-part-2-glt2308408988]] says many Catholics welcomed British troops entering the area after the Battle of the Bogside.
- No-go status: [[700-the-troubles-bloody-sunday-part-4-glt3909829334]] describes Free Derry and the Bogside as barricaded spaces guarded by masked vigilantes.
- Bloody Sunday operation: [[700-the-troubles-bloody-sunday-part-4-glt3909829334]] says armoured personnel carriers entered the Bogside during the limited arrest operation before soldiers fired on civilians.

## Qualifications
The Bogside's initial welcome for troops and later hostility are not contradictory when kept chronological. The current page treats them as evidence of a changed security relationship after curfews, internment, troop deaths, and Bloody Sunday.

## What Changed
- Added Free Derry and Bloody Sunday as the district's 1972 layer.
- Reframed the British Army welcome as a temporary phase rather than a stable relationship.
