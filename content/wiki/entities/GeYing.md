## Overview

葛婴 / Ge Ying is an early [[DazexiangUprising|Dazexiang uprising]] commander whose operational initiative helps expand [[ChenSheng|陈胜]]'s movement but whose unauthorized installation of a Chu king ends in his own execution.

## Current Profile

After the rebels take Qixian, Chen Sheng sends Ge Ying outward to capture more territory. Qinji 128-4 identifies his direction as Jiujiang and says that he installs the claimed Chu royal descendant [[XiangJiang|襄疆]] as king while away from the center. When Ge Ying learns that Chen Sheng has already declared himself king, he kills Xiang Jiang in an attempted correction, but Chen Sheng still arrests and executes him.

The sources make his death more important than an isolated punishment. It removes a commander credited with early gains and teaches subordinates that initiative taken under uncertain communication can later be read as disloyalty. The episode also supplies a source-scoped genealogy connecting his descendants, the Zhuge surname, and [[ZhugeLiang|诸葛亮]].

## Key Characteristics

- Early field commander trusted with expansion beyond the main rebel force.
- Autonomous political actor who installs a Chu claimant without central authorization.
- Failed reconciler who kills his own claimant after learning of Chen Sheng's kingship.
- Victim of a command environment where initiative, communication delay, and ruler suspicion become lethal.
- Genealogical ancestor of the Zhuge line only within the episode's explicitly bounded claim.

- [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-128-4-ta-jingran-shi-zhugeliang-de-zuxian-lkof-myhhubvksvl67i5myj1v3he|Qinji 128-4]] links an alleged later Zhu County title for Ge Ying's descendants to the formation of the Zhuge surname and then to Zhuge Liang.

## Qualifications

The sources do not establish Ge Ying's private motive for installing Xiang Jiang, the exact communication available to him, or whether killing the claimant was a voluntary repair or an act under pressure. The Zhuge genealogy is an oral-history claim in this episode and is not independently verified here. Qinji 128-3's transcript writes the name as 葛英; the canonical page retains 葛婴.
