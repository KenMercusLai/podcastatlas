# Polyphemus

## Overview

Polyphemus is the one-eyed Cyclops whose cave episode gives the first-half Odyssey branch its clearest anti-hospitality test and its curse on [[Odysseus]].

## Current Profile

In [[687-the-odyssey-hero-of-the-trojan-horse-part-1-glt2393125423]], Polyphemus traps Odysseus and twelve men in a cave, eats several of them, is blinded through the "No One" trick, and then calls on his father [[Poseidon]] to make Odysseus' return late, lonely, and troubled. The hosts also use him as a boundary figure for Greek fear of non-Greek peoples who do not observe agriculture, wine dilution, or guest-host norms.

## Key Characteristics

- Polyphemus inverts hospitality by treating strangers as food rather than guests.
- He forces Odysseus into indirect strategy because killing him immediately would leave the cave sealed.
- His humiliation becomes dangerous only after Odysseus reveals his real name, lineage, and home.
- His curse turns a successful escape into the source of Odysseus' longer suffering.
- He anchors the episode's discussion of mythic geography, Sicily, elephant-skull speculation, and Greek colonial anxiety.

## Evidence

- Anti-hospitality: [[687-the-odyssey-hero-of-the-trojan-horse-part-1-glt2393125423]] says Polyphemus seals the cave, kills Odysseus' men, and eats them.
- Strategic escape: [[687-the-odyssey-hero-of-the-trojan-horse-part-1-glt2393125423]] has Odysseus use wine, the "No One" name, blinding, and sheep to get out alive.
- Curse and pride: [[687-the-odyssey-hero-of-the-trojan-horse-part-1-glt2393125423]] shows Odysseus revealing himself after escape, allowing Polyphemus to pray to Poseidon against him.
- Fantasy geography: [[687-the-odyssey-hero-of-the-trojan-horse-part-1-glt2393125423]] connects Polyphemus to Sicilian localization, ancient elephant skulls, and Greek worries about peoples beyond familiar norms.
