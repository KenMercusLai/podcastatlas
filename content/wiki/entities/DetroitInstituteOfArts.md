# Detroit Institute of Arts

## Overview

The Detroit Institute of Arts is the museum holding [[VaseWithCarnations|Vase with Carnations]], used in the bounded source as a test case for authentication, collection retention, and the difference between legal title and moral restitution.

## Current Profile

Katherine Kresge bequeathed the painting to the museum without restrictions in 1990, after which it remained in storage for roughly two decades. [[hedwig-s-lost-van-gogh-hedwig-s-lost-van-gogh|Hedwig’s Lost Van Gogh]] reports that the institution later accepted it as an authentic Vincent van Gogh, displayed it, lent it to an exhibition, and sold image-based merchandise.

The museum also appears in the Ullin family's wider restitution history. Although the family is not pursuing this painting because it sees no viable claim, the episode argues that the institution's legal position does not answer whether retaining and commercializing the work is consistent with its values. This profile records that source-framed controversy rather than adopting a final institutional judgment.

## Key Characteristics

- Public art museum holding an authenticated but comparatively minor Van Gogh still life.
- Recipient of the work through an unrestricted 1990 bequest.
- Institution whose authentication converted uncertainty into substantial market and exhibition value.
- Restitution case study where lawful possession and asserted moral responsibility diverge.
