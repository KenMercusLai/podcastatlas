# Jaco Macaco

## Overview

Jaco Macaco was a celebrated fighting primate associated with London's Westminster Pit in the 1820s and later used in parliamentary argument against cruelty to animals.

## Current Profile

The episode presents Jaco less as a recoverable animal biography than as a collision between blood-sport advertising, gambling, eyewitness claim, reform rhetoric, and later retelling. Accounts disagree over where he came from, how he entered the fighting pits, whether he was a macaque, baboon, mandrill, or gibbon, and when he died.

His advertised fight with the dog Puss became politically consequential when Richard Martin cited it in Parliament. Yet Martin's claim that Jaco's jaw was torn off and that he died conflicts with proprietor Charles Aistrop's claim that the animal survived another fifteen months.

## Key Characteristics

- Regency London fighting animal promoted through sensational advertising.
- Species, origin, career, injuries, and death remain disputed.
- Associated with Westminster Pit and a heavily publicized fight against Puss.
- Entered animal-welfare politics through Richard Martin's parliamentary account.
- Biography survives through interested and conflicting narrators rather than stable records.
