# Edward Said

## Overview

Edward Said was a Palestinian American literary critic whose reading of [[HeartOfDarkness|Heart of Darkness]] argues that [[JosephConrad]] exposes imperial corruption without escaping imperialism's limits.

## Current Profile

The episode uses Said to sharpen the difference between criticizing a brutal colonial regime and imagining a post-imperial political future. Conrad can reveal hypocrisy, profit, and violence while still treating European rule as the available horizon and Africans as insufficiently autonomous subjects. Said's broader connection between knowledge and control also informs the episode's reading of Kurtz's report, maps, categories, and ledgers.

## Key Characteristics

- Distinguishes exposure of imperial abuse from a complete rejection of imperialism.
- Argues that Conrad does not finally imagine African liberation or self-rule.
- Connects colonial knowledge-making to political control over the people and places being described.
- Helps the episode preserve the novella's complexity without treating ambiguity as political innocence.
