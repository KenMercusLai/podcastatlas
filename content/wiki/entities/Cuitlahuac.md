## Overview

Cuitlahuac was Moctezuma's successor as ruler of the [[MexicaEmpire|Mexica Empire]], associated here with resistance after the [[NocheTriste|Noche Triste]] and an approximately eighty-day reign ended by smallpox.

## Current Profile

During the palace crisis, [[HernanCortes|Cortes]] releases Cuitlahuac, [[Moctezuma]]'s brother, to obtain supplies. The episode says he instead mobilizes resistance, making the release a major Spanish miscalculation. After the [[NocheTriste|Noche Triste]], Cuitlahuac tries to convert victory into political isolation of the survivors by urging [[Tlaxcala]] to abandon and destroy them. Tlaxcalan leaders instead preserve the alliance with Cortes. As the coalition regroups, smallpox spreads into the Valley of Mexico and kills Cuitlahuac, creating another succession during a rapidly changing military and epidemiological crisis. [[Cuauhtemoc]] then inherits leadership as Cortes seizes routes and prepares for siege.

## Key Characteristics

- Mexica ruler who succeeded Moctezuma during the campaign crisis.
- Royal figure whose release from the Spanish palace helped turn siege conditions into organized resistance.
- Opponent of the Spanish-led coalition who sought to detach Tlaxcala from Cortes.
- Short-reigned leader whose strategy was overtaken by Tlaxcalan refusal and epidemic disruption.
- Smallpox victim whose death made disease a direct factor in political succession.

## Evidence

- Anti-coalition strategy: [[389-the-fall-of-the-aztecs-the-night-of-tears-part-6-glt2744313815]] reports Cuitlahuac's appeal to Tlaxcala after the Spanish defeat.
- Reign and death: [[389-the-fall-of-the-aztecs-the-night-of-tears-part-6-glt2744313815]] describes an approximately eighty-day reign ending in smallpox.
- Succession consequence: [[389-the-fall-of-the-aztecs-the-night-of-tears-part-6-glt2744313815]] places Cuauhtemoc's accession amid coalition recovery and preparations to isolate Tenochtitlan.
- Release and mobilization: [[388-the-fall-of-the-aztecs-the-festival-of-blood-part-5-glt8784071073]] says Cortes released Cuitlahuac for supplies and that he instead encouraged resistance.

## Qualifications

The episodes provide only a narrow campaign profile and do not reconstruct Cuitlahuac's earlier life, government, full military role, or exact chronology. His purpose on release, reported appeal, the approximately eighty-day reign, and the private reasoning of all parties remain source-scoped. His death demonstrates epidemic political impact but does not make disease the sole explanation for later Mexica defeat.

## What Changed

- Established Cuitlahuac as the short-reigned political link between the Noche Triste and Cuauhtemoc's siege leadership.
- Connected smallpox to succession without reducing conquest to epidemic disease alone.
- Added his release from the palace and immediate mobilization role before the Noche Triste.
