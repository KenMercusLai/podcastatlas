## Overview

Cuitlahuac was Moctezuma's successor as ruler of the [[MexicaEmpire|Mexica Empire]], associated here with resistance after the [[NocheTriste|Noche Triste]] and an approximately eighty-day reign ended by smallpox.

## Current Profile

After the Spanish-led retreat from Tenochtitlan, Cuitlahuac tries to convert victory into political isolation of the survivors by urging [[Tlaxcala]] to abandon and destroy them. Tlaxcalan leaders instead preserve the alliance with [[HernanCortes|Cortes]]. As the coalition regroups, smallpox spreads into the Valley of Mexico and kills Cuitlahuac, creating another succession during a rapidly changing military and epidemiological crisis. [[Cuauhtemoc]] then inherits leadership as Cortes seizes routes and prepares for siege.

## Key Characteristics

- Mexica ruler who succeeded Moctezuma during the campaign crisis.
- Opponent of the Spanish-led coalition who sought to detach Tlaxcala from Cortes.
- Short-reigned leader whose strategy was overtaken by Tlaxcalan refusal and epidemic disruption.
- Smallpox victim whose death made disease a direct factor in political succession.
- Predecessor who left Cuauhtemoc to face the approaching siege.

## Evidence

- Anti-coalition strategy: [[389-the-fall-of-the-aztecs-the-night-of-tears-part-6-glt2744313815]] reports Cuitlahuac's appeal to Tlaxcala after the Spanish defeat.
- Reign and death: [[389-the-fall-of-the-aztecs-the-night-of-tears-part-6-glt2744313815]] describes an approximately eighty-day reign ending in smallpox.
- Succession consequence: [[389-the-fall-of-the-aztecs-the-night-of-tears-part-6-glt2744313815]] places Cuauhtemoc's accession amid coalition recovery and preparations to isolate Tenochtitlan.

## Qualifications

The episode provides only a narrow campaign profile and does not reconstruct Cuitlahuac's earlier life, government, full military role, or exact chronology. His reported appeal, the approximately eighty-day reign, and the private reasoning of all parties remain source-scoped. His death demonstrates epidemic political impact but does not make disease the sole explanation for later Mexica defeat.

## What Changed

- Established Cuitlahuac as the short-reigned political link between the Noche Triste and Cuauhtemoc's siege leadership.
- Connected smallpox to succession without reducing conquest to epidemic disease alone.

## Relationships

- [[MexicaEmpire]] - polity he led after Moctezuma's death.
- [[Tlaxcala]] - rival polity he reportedly tried to draw away from Cortes.
- [[HernanCortes]] - enemy whose surviving coalition Cuitlahuac sought to dismantle.
- [[Cuauhtemoc]] - successor after Cuitlahuac's death from smallpox.
- [[ColumbianExchange]] - process whose epidemic dimension ended his reign.
- [[SiegeOfTenochtitlan]] - campaign prepared after his death and conducted under his successor.
