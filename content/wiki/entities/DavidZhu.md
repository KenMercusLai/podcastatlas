# David Zhu

## Overview

David Zhu is the co-founder and CEO of [[Revo]], an AI-native sales platform. In the available source, he is a technical founder describing how an ambitious integrated product survived only after its commercial packaging became more incremental.

## Current Profile

Zhu presents himself as a lifelong builder and fifth-time founder whose strongest advantage is recruiting engineers rather than inheriting a prominent sales reputation. His account combines technical conviction with commercial correction: he kept Revo's shared platform architecture, acknowledged that the original full-stack replacement pitch failed with mid-market buyers, and shifted toward outcome-based entry points. He also treats founder participation in discovery and sales as necessary learning, even when a complementary sales leader is better at closing.

## Key Characteristics

- Builds around technical depth and an unusually large engineering-heavy founding team.
- Separates durable product architecture from commercial packaging after early buyer resistance.
- Treats direct founder discovery as necessary for learning the ideal customer profile, objections, and positioning.
- Uses complementary roles by opening conversations while a stronger sales operator closes and codifies the motion.
- Frames innovation as uncertain work and warns against mistaking politeness or adjacent-segment adoption for validation.

## Evidence

Technical-founder advantage:
- [[2bbd2ffd]] reports Zhu's emphasis on recruiting 12 senior or staff engineers into Revo's 14-person founding team and building a shared data, semantic, and memory foundation.

Commercial adaptation:
- [[2bbd2ffd]] records his account of 200-300 rejected full-stack pitches, roughly a year of weak results, and the later decision to sell individual workflows without abandoning the integrated architecture.

Founder learning and role design:
- [[2bbd2ffd]] says Zhu opposes outsourcing early customer discovery and describes a division in which he initiated conversations while a founding sales leader handled closing and later trained the team.
