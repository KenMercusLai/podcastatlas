# Bloody Friday

## Overview
Bloody Friday is the 21 July 1972 Provisional IRA bombing attack in central [[Belfast]]. The episode uses it as the moment when post-[[BloodySunday]] republican escalation damaged the [[ProvisionalIRA]]'s own support among moderate nationalists and in America.

## Current Profile
The episode frames Bloody Friday as a strategic backfire inside a broader 1972 escalation. The Provisionals planted 22 bombs, warnings were hoax-like or confusing enough that evacuation failed, and the blasts killed nine people and seriously injured around 130. The attack shows that atrocity-driven recruitment gains could reverse when violence made civilian exposure too visible.

## Key Characteristics
- Large coordinated bombing attack by the [[ProvisionalIRA]] in [[Belfast]].
- Civilian-casualty event that damaged republican political legitimacy.
- Late stage of the failed June-July 1972 ceasefire and talks sequence.
- Counterpoint to [[BloodySunday]] because state violence and paramilitary violence each reshaped legitimacy in different directions.
- Example of the [[AtrocityRetaliationCycle]] becoming strategically self-defeating.

## Evidence
- Attack scale: [[700-the-troubles-bloody-sunday-part-4-glt3909829334]] says the Provisionals planted 22 bombs in central Belfast on 21 July 1972.
- Casualties and warnings: [[700-the-troubles-bloody-sunday-part-4-glt3909829334]] says hoax and confusing warnings prevented effective evacuation, with nine killed and around 130 seriously injured.
- Political backfire: [[700-the-troubles-bloody-sunday-part-4-glt3909829334]] says Bloody Friday damaged IRA support among moderate nationalists and in America.
- Ceasefire context: [[700-the-troubles-bloody-sunday-part-4-glt3909829334]] places the bombing shortly after failed contacts between the IRA and Willie Whitelaw's side.
