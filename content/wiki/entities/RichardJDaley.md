# Richard J. Daley

## Overview

Richard J. Daley was Chicago's Democratic mayor and machine leader during the [[Chicago1968DemocraticConvention|1968 Democratic National Convention]].

## Current Profile

Daley appears as a powerful municipal partisan who wanted the convention's prestige and economic benefits but treated protest as a threat to both civic order and his personal authority. His loyalty to [[LyndonBJohnson]], hope that Johnson might be drafted again, and determination to prevent visible disorder helped close space for compromise. Once police violence drew criticism, Daley defended the force and escalated his rhetoric rather than concede loss of control.

## Key Characteristics

- Controlled a powerful Chicago Democratic machine and maintained a close alliance with Johnson.
- Sought the convention for prestige, economic benefit, and possible influence over the nomination.
- Interpreted public order as a test of his authority and Chicago's institutional legitimacy.
- Prepared police and National Guard forces for severe disorder while resisting protest permits.
- Defended police conduct even after reporters, delegates, and television viewers witnessed violence.

## Qualifications

This profile is drawn from one retrospective podcast episode focused on convention violence. It explains Daley's political incentives but does not provide a full history of his mayoralty, machine governance, urban policy, or relations with Black Chicago.
