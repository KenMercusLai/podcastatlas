# Ray Kroc

## Overview

Ray Kroc is the McDonald’s expansion figure whom the episode portrays as treating French-fry preparation, consistency, and flavor as foundational to the company’s identity.

## Current Profile

The source says Kroc encountered the McDonald brothers’ restaurant in 1954 while selling milkshake machines and recognized its potential for global scale. Gladwell emphasizes that Kroc was especially impressed by the fries, not only the hamburgers.

Kroc’s product discipline centered on Russet potatoes, the beef-tallow mixture known as Formula 47, and a dedicated frying process protected from contamination by other foods. In the episode’s argument, this attention made the fry a designed product rather than a generic potato cooked in an incidental medium. The account is useful for product-history synthesis but remains Gladwell’s selective reconstruction rather than a complete corporate biography.

## Key Characteristics

- Expansion entrepreneur associated with turning the McDonald brothers’ restaurant system into a global chain.
- Product operator who treated French-fry quality as strategically central.
- Advocate of standardized ingredients and tightly controlled preparation.
- Founder figure used to contrast product stewardship with later corporate reformulation.
