## Overview

Studio 54 was a New York nightclub that became a symbol of [[Disco]]'s celebrity glamour, gatekeeping, spectacle, cocaine-era excess, and commercial mainstreaming.

## Current Profile

In [[439-disco-sex-and-race-in-seventies-america-glt8206137792|the source]], Studio 54 marks the distance between early mixed underground clubs and disco as elite spectacle. Its selective door, famous guests, and theatrical imagery made it globally legible as disco while representing only one phase and social layer. Criticism of celebrity worship and exclusion was not necessarily prejudice, but treating Studio 54 as the whole genre erases disco's community-oriented formation.

## Key Characteristics

- Became a visible symbol of celebrity glamour and excess.
- Used selective admission and social gatekeeping.
- Joined nightlife, spectacle, cocaine culture, fashion, and famous guests.
- Represented commercial mainstreaming more than underground origin.
