# Rudyard Kipling

## Overview

Rudyard Kipling appears in [[671-the-first-world-war-blood-in-the-trenches-part-1-glt2309179565]] as the patriotic writer whose use of influence to get [[JohnKipling|his son John Kipling]] commissioned makes the [[BattleOfLoos|Battle of Loos]] a family grief case as well as a military disaster.

## Current Profile

The source frames Kipling through the cost of patriotic pressure. John Kipling had been rejected because of severe short-sightedness, but Rudyard Kipling used connections to secure him a commission in the Irish Guards. John went missing during Loos after losing his glasses in mud, and Rudyard was devastated.

Kipling's current wiki role is therefore not a full literary biography. He functions as a bridge between public war language and private guilt: a father and celebrated imperial writer whose later epitaphs focused on dead sons and bereaved parents.

## Key Characteristics

- Patriotic literary figure tied in this source to recruitment pressure.
- Father whose influence helped John Kipling enter military service despite eyesight problems.
- Bereaved parent after Loos.
- Provides a private grief ending to an episode otherwise focused on collective trench experience.
