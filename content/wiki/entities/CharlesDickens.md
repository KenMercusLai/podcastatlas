# Charles Dickens

## Overview

Charles Dickens is a British novelist whose 1842 American tour is used here to show how literary celebrity, reform hopes, slavery, copyright conflict, and national stereotype could turn Anglo-American admiration into resentment.

## Current Profile

Dickens arrived in the United States as an intensely celebrated British author. The episode describes large receptions in Boston and New York and meetings with American writers, but constant public attention, resistance to international copyright protection, slavery, treatment of Native Americans, and aspects of American public life progressively alienated him.

His response joined moral criticism to national caricature. *American Notes* attacked slavery but also portrayed Americans as vulgar and overbearing, while the American chapters of *Martin Chuzzlewit* sharpened the image of a society dominated by dollars. The result illustrates [[AngloAmericanPowerReversal]] before the later reversal: a British cultural authority judged an American society still highly attentive to British opinion.

## Key Characteristics

- British literary celebrity received with extraordinary enthusiasm in the United States.
- Advocate of international copyright protection for British authors.
- Explicit critic of American slavery and treatment of Native Americans.
- Visitor whose frustration with intrusive attention intensified during the tour.
- Author who converted disappointment into travel writing and satirical fiction.
- Case of moral criticism becoming entangled with broad national stereotype.

## Evidence

- Celebrity reception: [[263-usa-vs-england-the-200-year-rivalry-glt7546211559]] describes celebrations in Boston and New York, including a large Boz Ball and meetings with American writers.
- Copyright conflict: [[263-usa-vs-england-the-200-year-rivalry-glt7546211559]] says reform was one purpose of the tour and that U.S. resistance became a major grievance.
- Moral criticism: [[263-usa-vs-england-the-200-year-rivalry-glt7546211559]] emphasizes Dickens's opposition to slavery and his criticism of Native American treatment.
- Literary backlash: [[263-usa-vs-england-the-200-year-rivalry-glt7546211559]] connects *American Notes* and *Martin Chuzzlewit* to collapsing goodwill and caricatures of American vulgarity and money-mindedness.

## Qualifications

This profile is limited to one episode's use of the 1842 tour. It does not summarize Dickens's wider life, fiction, reform activity, later American visit, or the full legal history of international copyright. Reported reactions, private motives, and generalizations about American behavior remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Established Dickens as a case where transatlantic literary admiration became moral and commercial conflict.
- Kept his anti-slavery criticism distinct from his broader national caricatures.
