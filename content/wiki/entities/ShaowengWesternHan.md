## Overview

少翁 / Shaoweng is a Western Han fangshi presented by Hanji 331-1 as gaining [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s trust through an extreme-age claim, historical storytelling, and a screened apparition of the emperor's deceased consort before losing that trust through a forged physical sign.

## Current Profile

The current evidence supports a court-authority sequence, not a verified supernatural biography. Shaoweng claims to be two hundred years old and able to call spirits; a restricted-view appearance of Wang夫人 turns bereavement into apparent proof; Wudi then makes him 文成将军 and accepts sacred-building advice. When divine visitors fail to arrive, the episode says Shaoweng plants a written silk text inside a cow, but recognizable handwriting converts the miracle into an inspectable fabrication. His execution later becomes the dangerous precedent that [[LuanDaWesternHan|栾大]] invokes while bargaining for patience and protected status.

## Key Characteristics

- Court fangshi who combines age, historical knowledge, and spirit access into a credibility package.
- Performer whose strongest early proof depends on darkness, a screen, distance, and a ban on inspection.
- Beneficiary of imperial grief and immortality desire, receiving military-style title and architectural patronage.
- Failed claimant who answers nonarrival with a planted physical object that leaves a human trace.
- Punished predecessor whose death changes how a later fangshi negotiates the same imperial demand.

## Evidence

Court entry and screened apparition:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-331-1-200sui-de-ta-ruhe-ba-huangdi-pian-de-tuantuanzhuan-lvn0xka_dxl88qo5iroseks852ha|Hanji 331-1]] says Shaoweng claims two hundred years of age, tells Qin-Han stories, and stages a screened appearance of Wang夫人 that Wudi may observe but not approach.

Patronage, nondelivery, and exposure:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-331-1-200sui-de-ta-ruhe-ba-huangdi-pian-de-tuantuanzhuan-lvn0xka_dxl88qo5iroseks852ha|Hanji 331-1]] links the performance to the 文成将军 title and sacred construction, then says prolonged divine nonarrival leads to the ox-belly silk forgery, handwriting recognition, confession, and execution.

## Qualifications

The only canonical source is a short podcast summary, not a primary-source dossier. It does not verify Shaoweng's age, supernatural claims, performance mechanism, exact title chronology, construction role, handwriting evidence, interrogation, manner of death, or the horse-liver cover story. The host's shadow-play explanation and inventor attribution remain speculation or reception history rather than established biography.

## What Changed

- Created a canonical profile separating the observable court-authority sequence from supernatural and performance-mechanism claims.
- Connected Shaoweng's own rise and fall to the later protection strategy used by Luan Da.
