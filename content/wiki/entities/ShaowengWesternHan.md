## Overview

少翁 / Shaoweng is a Western Han fangshi presented by Hanji 331-1 as gaining [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s trust through an extreme-age claim, historical storytelling, and a screened apparition of the emperor's deceased consort before losing that trust through a forged physical sign. Hanji 331-2 adds a posthumous reversal in Wudi's perception: an alleged sighting and empty grave make the emperor reinterpret the executed claimant as an immortal who escaped.

## Current Profile

The current evidence supports a court-authority and reception sequence, not a verified supernatural biography. Shaoweng claims to be two hundred years old and able to call spirits; a restricted-view appearance of Wang夫人 turns bereavement into apparent proof; Wudi then makes him 文成将军 and accepts sacred-building advice. When divine visitors fail to arrive, Hanji 331-1 says Shaoweng plants a written silk text inside a cow, but recognizable handwriting converts the miracle into an inspectable fabrication.

Hanji 331-2 shows that even execution does not settle the ruler's interpretation. While ill, Wudi hears that Shaoweng has been seen alive; an opened grave allegedly contains a bamboo slip but no body. Wudi reads the report as immortal escape, regrets the killing, and becomes receptive to another supernatural healer. This episode supplies no custody record, independent witness, or causal evidence, so the durable profile change is posthumous authority in Wudi's belief system rather than established survival. That regret later becomes the precedent [[LuanDaWesternHan|栾大]] invokes while bargaining for patience and protected status.

## Key Characteristics

- Court fangshi who combines age, historical knowledge, and spirit access into a credibility package.
- Performer whose strongest early proof depends on darkness, a screen, distance, and a ban on inspection.
- Beneficiary of imperial grief and immortality desire, receiving military-style title and architectural patronage.
- Failed claimant who answers nonarrival with a planted physical object that leaves a human trace.
- Posthumously reinterpreted claimant whose alleged empty grave reverses the patron's judgment without verifying survival.
- Punished predecessor whose death and the emperor's regret change how a later fangshi negotiates the same imperial demand.

## Evidence

Court entry and screened apparition:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-331-1-200sui-de-ta-ruhe-ba-huangdi-pian-de-tuantuanzhuan-lvn0xka_dxl88qo5iroseks852ha|Hanji 331-1]] says Shaoweng claims two hundred years of age, tells Qin-Han stories, and stages a screened appearance of Wang夫人 that Wudi may observe but not approach.

Patronage, nondelivery, and exposure:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-331-1-200sui-de-ta-ruhe-ba-huangdi-pian-de-tuantuanzhuan-lvn0xka_dxl88qo5iroseks852ha|Hanji 331-1]] links the performance to the 文成将军 title and sacred construction, then says prolonged divine nonarrival leads to the ox-belly silk forgery, handwriting recognition, confession, and execution.

Posthumous reinterpretation:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-331-2-hunjun-haishi-mingjun-hanwudi-de-yumei-yimian-fl-a4uchkdjzcfmfs-itb2swgi_t|Hanji 331-2]] says an attendant reports Shaoweng alive in Guandong and that Wudi finds no body when the grave is opened, leading the ill emperor to infer immortal escape, regret the execution, and accept another ritual healer.

## Qualifications

The two canonical sources are short, sequential podcast summaries, not independent primary-source dossiers. They do not verify Shaoweng's age, supernatural claims, performance mechanism, exact title chronology, construction role, handwriting evidence, interrogation, manner of death, or the horse-liver cover story. Nor does the later episode establish who reported the sighting, how the grave was secured, why the body was absent, what the bamboo slip contained, or whether Wudi's illness had any connection to Shaoweng. The host's shadow-play explanation and inventor attribution remain speculation or reception history rather than established biography, and the “绍翁” spelling in Hanji 331-2 is normalized to this canonical identity.

## What Changed

- Added Wudi's posthumous reversal after the alleged Guandong sighting and empty grave.
- Distinguished evidence of renewed belief from evidence that Shaoweng survived or became immortal.
- Made the emperor's regret an explicit bridge from Shaoweng's execution to Luan Da's later protection strategy.
