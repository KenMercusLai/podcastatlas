## Overview

郭圣通 / Guo Shengtong is now synthesized as both a founding political-marriage empress and a displaced maternal-line figure. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-719-guangwudi-liuxiu-suanbusuan-zhanan-lk8eme3wwyk5qkjjfxkp0zsc77ix|Hanji 719]] adds the origin and deposition layer: her marriage to [[LiuXiu|刘秀]] is tied to [[LiuYangZhendingKingEasternHan|真定王刘杨]]'s Hebei alliance value, but that value declines after consolidation and she is replaced by [[YinLihua|阴丽华]]. Hanji 720, Hanji 722, and Hanji 732 show the afterlife: [[LiuQiangDonghaiKing|刘强]] withdraws from heirship, the Guo branch receives compensation, and her removal remains usable as accession grievance.

## Current Profile

Hanji 719 gives Guo Shengtong's earliest current profile. The episode frames her marriage to Liu Xiu as a political alliance during the Wang Lang crisis in Hebei, when Liu Yang's support mattered to Liu Xiu's survival and growth. Guo's empress status is therefore not merely a household romance story; it is attached to a founding coalition.

The same source explains her fall through changing power conditions. Liu Xiu retains deep attachment to Yin Lihua and eventually deposes Guo after her complaints and resentment are framed as queenly failure under solar-eclipse language. Zhi Yun warns that empress deposition is a state-foundation matter. Liu Xiu then orders no congratulations, makes Yin empress, gives Guo Zhongshan Taihou status, enlarges the Zhongshan arrangement through Liu Fu, and enfeoffs the other sons. In this source, Guo loses central status but avoids extermination.

Hanji 720 supplies the decisive succession layer. Once Guo is deposed, Liu Qiang no longer has the maternal status that made his crown-prince position secure. Zhi Yun's advice to Liu Qiang treats the changed mother-son field as both a danger and a filial problem, so Liu Qiang's withdrawal becomes a way to protect himself and serve his mother from a safer position.

Hanji 722 gives the next Guo-branch layer. After Empress Guo's displacement, Liu Xiu changes [[LiuFuPeiKingEasternHan|刘辅]] from Zhongshan king to Pei king and rewards Guo Kuang with office, visits, and large gifts. The episode presents this as continued compensation after the 41 CE soft landing.

Hanji 732 shows why the deposition remains politically live. After Liu Xiu dies, [[LiuJingGuanglingKingEasternHan|刘荆]] sends Liu Qiang an anonymous letter arguing that Liu Qiang had been wrongly displaced and that Queen Guo had been wrongly removed. The letter tries to turn Guo's earlier status loss into accession grievance, but Liu Qiang reports it and [[EmperorMingOfHan|Mingdi]] handles the matter quietly.

## Key Characteristics

- Founding political-marriage empress whose original value is tied to Liu Yang's Hebei alliance.
- Deposed Guangwu empress whose lost status changes her son's crown-prince security.
- Maternal-line node whose fall helps explain Liu Qiang's voluntary withdrawal in 43 CE.
- Displaced consort whose branch receives titles, fiefs, and later compensation rather than immediate destruction.
- Later grievance figure invoked at Mingdi's accession.
- Transcript-normalized figure: the source form 郭盛通 is routed to the established 郭圣通 page.

## Qualifications

Hanji 719 adds Guo's origin and deposition but still does not make this page a complete biography. The episode includes source-scoped commentary about Guo's complaints and resentment; the wiki treats the durable claim as political-marriage depreciation, maternal-status loss, and managed compensation. Guo Kuang remains source-scoped until a separate source makes his profile durable. The page also preserves the established route spelling 郭圣通 even though this source uses 郭盛通.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 719 as Guo's political-marriage origin, deposition, and soft-landing layer.
- Reframed Guo from a mainly afterlife/succession node into a founding alliance figure whose value changes after Liu Xiu consolidates.
- Connected the 41 CE deposition directly to Hanji 720's Liu Qiang withdrawal and Hanji 722's later Guo-branch compensation.
- Preserved Hanji 732 as the later accession-grievance layer.
