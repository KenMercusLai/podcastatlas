## Overview

郭圣通 / Guo Shengtong is synthesized as both the first empress produced by Liu Xiu's founding settlement and a later displaced maternal-line figure. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-686-liuxiu-fashi-zhi-ai-yinlihua-weihe-li-guoshi-wei-hou-lnqlqgq54srb1zzzgemfwuwujhvc|Hanji 686]] now supplies the initial 26 CE layer: [[YinLihua|阴丽华]] declines the empress position because Guo has already borne a son, so [[LiuXiu|刘秀]] makes Guo empress and makes [[LiuQiangDonghaiKing|刘强]] crown prince. Hanji 719, Hanji 720, Hanji 722, and Hanji 732 then show how that same Guo-Liu Qiang status line is later weakened, compensated, and invoked as accession grievance.

## Current Profile

Hanji 686 gives Guo Shengtong's earliest current palace-status layer. The source says Liu Xiu had long wanted Yin Lihua as empress, but Yin refused because Guo had already given birth to a son. On 26 CE-06-07, Liu Xiu makes Guo empress and makes her son Liu Qiang crown prince. The source also identifies Guo as [[LiuYangZhendingKingEasternHan|真定王刘杨]]'s niece, keeping her position tied to a politically charged Hebei kinship field rather than only to Liu Xiu's private preference.

Hanji 719 gives the origin-and-deposition explanation. It frames Guo's marriage to Liu Xiu as a political alliance during the [[WangLangHebeiClaimant|王郎]] crisis, when Liu Yang's support mattered to Liu Xiu's survival and growth. After consolidation, that alliance value declines. Liu Xiu later deposes Guo, makes Yin empress, gives Guo Zhongshan Taihou status, and gives her sons titles or enlarged fiefs rather than eliminating the branch.

Hanji 720 supplies the decisive succession consequence. Once Guo is deposed, Liu Qiang no longer has the maternal status that originally made his crown-prince position secure. [[ZhiYunEasternHan|郅恽]] advises Liu Qiang that withdrawal is safer and better for serving his mother, so Liu Qiang repeatedly asks to resign and eventually becomes Donghai King.

Hanji 722 gives the next Guo-branch compensation layer. After Empress Guo's displacement, Liu Xiu changes [[LiuFuPeiKingEasternHan|刘辅]] from Zhongshan king to Pei king and rewards Guo Kuang with office, visits, and large gifts. Hanji 732 shows why the deposition remains politically live: after Liu Xiu dies, [[LiuJingGuanglingKingEasternHan|刘荆]] sends Liu Qiang an anonymous letter arguing that both Liu Qiang and Queen Guo had been wrongly displaced.

## Key Characteristics

- First Guangwu empress in the current evidence, installed in 26 CE after Yin Lihua declines and Guo's son is made crown prince.
- Founding political-marriage figure whose original value is tied to Liu Yang's Hebei alliance.
- Maternal-line node whose status initially secures and later destabilizes Liu Qiang's crown-prince position.
- Deposed Guangwu empress whose branch receives titles, fiefs, and compensation rather than immediate destruction.
- Later grievance figure invoked at Mingdi's accession.
- Transcript-normalized figure: source forms 郭氏 / 郭贵人 / 郭盛通 are routed to the established 郭圣通 page.

## Qualifications

The page remains bounded to the listed source notes. Hanji 686 establishes the initial appointment and heir-status logic but does not reconstruct Guo's independent biography or household politics. Hanji 719 includes source-scoped commentary about Guo's complaints and resentment; the durable claim is political-marriage depreciation, maternal-status loss, and managed compensation. Guo Kuang remains source-scoped until a separate source makes his profile durable. The established route remains 郭圣通 even when individual source notes use 郭氏, 郭贵人, or 郭盛通.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 686 as Guo's earliest current layer: initial empress appointment and Liu Qiang's crown-prince foundation in 26 CE.
- Reframed Guo's profile as initial maternal-status success before later political-marriage depreciation and deposition.
- Connected Hanji 686 directly to Hanji 719's 41 CE deposition and Hanji 720's Liu Qiang withdrawal.
- Preserved Hanji 722 as compensation evidence and Hanji 732 as later accession-grievance evidence.
