## Overview

窦融 / Dou Rong is synthesized as an Eastern Han high-status affiliate whose current profile spans early western submission under [[LiuXiu|光武帝刘秀]], Hexi integration, post-unification exposure, and later patriarchal prestige under [[EmperorMingOfHan|汉明帝]]. Hanji 707 now supplies the earliest current layer: Dou Rong arrives with large western forces, asks how to perform imperial audience ritual, and tries to convert regional authority into household security while Liu Xiu keeps him in place as a stabilizer. Hanji 714 follows with Luoyang-entry ritual and Hexi personnel redistribution; Hanji 715 presents his later high-office anxiety and repeated resignation attempts; Hanji 736 shows how his aged prestige temporarily shields, but cannot discipline, the Dou family.

## Current Profile

Hanji 707 frames Dou Rong's first current submission as a mutual-security exchange. He leads tens of thousands of troops and a large baggage train into Liu Xiu's camp, but before audience he asks for the correct court protocol. Liu Xiu praises this because the ritual makes the new hierarchy legible to everyone around him.

The same source stresses that Dou Rong knows what he wants: family safety rather than continued independent rule. He is willing to hand over authority and change office, but Liu Xiu does not let him withdraw at once. Because the west remains unsettled, Liu Xiu needs Dou Rong's local standing to reassure士人 and commoners. Dou Rong repeatedly asks to be replaced; Liu Xiu answers that their relationship is like right and left hands and keeps him as a pacifying presence.

Hanji 714 supplies the later court-entry layer. Liu Xiu calls Dou Rong and the five commandery governors to Luoyang. They arrive with officials, military clients, more than one thousand carts, and livestock across the fields, making the scale of the Hexi power field visible at the capital.

The Luoyang ritual converts that visible power into submission and reuse. Dou Rong surrenders seals and ribbons before entering, but Liu Xiu returns the marquis seals, personally receives him, appoints him Yizhou governor, and appoints associated officials such as [[LiangTongEasternHan|Liang Tong]], [[KongFenEasternHan|Kong Fen]], and [[RenTingEasternHan|Ren Ting]] into new roles.

Hanji 715 supplies Dou Rong's post-unification high-office layer. Liu Xiu appoints him to very high office, but Dou Rong does not treat the promotion as simple security. Because he was not an old Liu Xiu follower and now sits above earlier meritorious men, he behaves humbly in meetings, gains Liu Xiu's respect, and repeatedly tries to resign office or even title. Liu Xiu blocks the resignation and eventually tells him not to raise it again.

Hanji 736 presents Dou Rong at the end of a long elite life. He is old enough that he can no longer control his descendants, and the source says those descendants commit many unlawful acts. The central incident is carried by his eldest son [[DouMuEasternHan|Dou Mu]], who falsely invokes [[YinLihua|Yin Taihou]]'s authority to pressure Liu Xu of Liu'an to divorce his original wife and marry Dou Mu's daughter.

When the wife's family reports the matter, Mingdi is angry but does not destroy the entire family. The source interprets the initial punishment as partially restrained because of Dou Rong's face and standing. Mingdi removes Dou Mu and his brothers from office and sends the officeholding Dou relatives with their households back to the original commandery, leaving only Dou Rong in the capital. After Dou Rong dies, Dou Mu and his sons are later accused, imprisoned, and executed.

## Key Characteristics

- Regional power-holder whose first submission is formalized by visible ritual, troops, and material scale.
- Security-seeking affiliate willing to surrender practical authority but retained by Liu Xiu for western stabilization.
- Hexi power-holder whose Luoyang retinue, seal submission, returned marquis honor, and new appointment turn regional power into continued integration.
- Newly attached high official whose status above old followers creates humility and resignation anxiety that Liu Xiu refuses to let become withdrawal.
- Confucian household instructor who wants sons to be cautious and proper rather than exceptional.
- Aged Eastern Han Dou-family patriarch and Anfeng Dai marquis whose standing initially limits punishment of descendants.
- Prestige-bearing but vulnerable ancestor whose inability to discipline descendants exposes the limits of personal protection.

## Qualifications

The source transcripts contain route-sensitive name and office details, so this page normalizes the 斗荣 transcript form to 窦融 / Dou Rong while preserving source-bound scope. The preserved frontmatter source order follows the page's provenance history rather than chronology. Hanji 707 gives the initial submission and security-exchange frame but not a full Hexi administrative history. Hanji 714 gives the Luoyang-entry and appointment scene but not a full administrative record for the Yizhou post or every member of the five-commandery party. Hanji 715's provincial label is not treated as a durable geography claim here; the durable point is Dou Rong's new-affiliate status, high office, humility, and rejected resignation. Hanji 736 does not give a full account of Dou Rong's earlier career, his complete genealogy, or every charge against later family members.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 707 as Dou Rong's earliest current submission and security-for-authority exchange layer.
- Reframed Hanji 714's Luoyang entry and Hanji 715's resignation anxiety as later repetitions of the same controlled-integration problem.
- Preserved Hanji 736 as the late patriarchal protection and family-misconduct layer.
- Kept the page distinct from later He-era Dou-family dominance.
