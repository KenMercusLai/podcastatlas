## Overview

窦融 / Dou Rong is the aged Anfeng Dai marquis in Hanji 736 whose personal prestige partly shields the Dou family when his descendants break court order. The page treats him as an early Dou-family patriarchal node, distinct from the later [[DouXianEasternHan|Dou Xian]] and [[EmpressDowagerDouHeLateHan|Dou Taihou]] power field.

## Current Profile

Hanji 736 presents Dou Rong at the end of a long elite life. He is old enough that he can no longer control his descendants, and the source says those descendants commit many unlawful acts.

The central incident is carried by his eldest son [[DouMuEasternHan|Dou Mu]]. Dou Mu, as husband of Neihuang Princess, falsely invokes [[YinLihua|Yin Taihou]]'s authority to pressure Liu Xu of Liu'an to divorce his original wife and marry Dou Mu's daughter. When the wife's family reports the matter, Mingdi is angry but does not destroy the entire family.

The source interprets the initial punishment as partially restrained because of Dou Rong's face and standing. Mingdi removes Dou Mu and his brothers from office and sends the officeholding Dou relatives with their households back to the original commandery, leaving only Dou Rong in the capital. After Dou Rong dies, Dou Mu and his sons are later accused, imprisoned, and executed.

## Key Characteristics

- Aged Eastern Han Dou-family patriarch and Anfeng Dai marquis.
- Personal standing still matters enough that punishment of descendants is initially limited.
- Unable to discipline descendants whose unlawful conduct creates court danger.
- Connected to the forged-dowager-order marriage coercion case through Dou Mu.
- Early warning node for how Dou-family prestige can coexist with household misconduct.

## Qualifications

The source transcript writes 窦荣, but this page normalizes the Anfeng Dai marquis route to 窦融 / Dou Rong while preserving the source-bound scope. Hanji 736 does not give a full account of Dou Rong's earlier career, his complete genealogy, or every charge against later family members.
