## Overview

窦融 / Dou Rong is synthesized as an Eastern Han high-status affiliate whose current profile spans early Hexi integration under [[LiuXiu|光武帝刘秀]] and later patriarchal prestige under [[EmperorMingOfHan|汉明帝]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-714-shi-shui-di-yi-ge-han-chu-nan-er-dang-ma-ge-guo-shi-lpkqxrl3dsok4ui0lij4c3x8zfpi|Hanji 714]] now adds the Luoyang-entry layer: Dou Rong arrives with the five-commandery retinue, surrenders seals at the gate, receives his marquis seals back, and is reappointed while connected officials are redistributed. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-715-shishang-zui-zhihui-de-yici-duoquan-loqkawl247maw9fn-nomaw5gzkgu|Hanji 715]] then presents him as a newly attached senior actor who feels exposed and repeatedly tries to resign; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-736-shengzai-huangquan-xia-ta-pingshenme-neng-andu-wannian-lisegz1ia5najewghxaqirkafvup|Hanji 736]] later presents him as the aged Anfeng Dai marquis whose prestige partly shields the Dou family when his descendants break court order. The page keeps him distinct from the later [[DouXianEasternHan|Dou Xian]] and [[EmpressDowagerDouHeLateHan|Dou Taihou]] power field.

## Current Profile

Hanji 714 supplies the first current court-entry layer. Liu Xiu calls Dou Rong and the five commandery governors to Luoyang. They arrive with officials, military clients, more than one thousand carts, and livestock across the fields, making the scale of the Hexi power field visible at the capital.

The ritual at the gate converts that visible power into submission and reuse. Dou Rong surrenders seals and ribbons before entering, but Liu Xiu returns the marquis seals, personally receives him, appoints him Yizhou governor, and appoints associated officials such as [[LiangTongEasternHan|Liang Tong]], [[KongFenEasternHan|Kong Fen]], and [[RenTingEasternHan|Ren Ting]] into new roles.

Hanji 715 supplies Dou Rong's earlier court-integration layer. After unification, Liu Xiu appoints him to very high office, but Dou Rong does not treat the promotion as simple security. Because he was not an old Liu Xiu follower and now sits above earlier meritorious men, he behaves humbly in meetings, gains Liu Xiu's respect, and repeatedly tries to resign office or even title.

The core exchange is a failed withdrawal. Dou Rong teaches his sons through Confucian classics and says he wants them to be respectful, careful, and proper rather than extraordinary. He seeks a private audience to return status, but Liu Xiu repeatedly blocks the resignation and eventually tells him not to raise it again. In this source, Dou Rong is not a commander being pushed out; he is a powerful new affiliate whom Liu Xiu keeps integrated.

Hanji 736 presents Dou Rong at the end of a long elite life. He is old enough that he can no longer control his descendants, and the source says those descendants commit many unlawful acts.

The central incident is carried by his eldest son [[DouMuEasternHan|Dou Mu]]. Dou Mu, as husband of Neihuang Princess, falsely invokes [[YinLihua|Yin Taihou]]'s authority to pressure Liu Xu of Liu'an to divorce his original wife and marry Dou Mu's daughter. When the wife's family reports the matter, Mingdi is angry but does not destroy the entire family.

The source interprets the initial punishment as partially restrained because of Dou Rong's face and standing. Mingdi removes Dou Mu and his brothers from office and sends the officeholding Dou relatives with their households back to the original commandery, leaving only Dou Rong in the capital. After Dou Rong dies, Dou Mu and his sons are later accused, imprisoned, and executed. The combined evidence makes Dou Rong a case where personal prestige is useful but unstable: it can integrate a new affiliate, shield descendants temporarily, and then disappear as protection once the patriarch dies.

## Key Characteristics

- Hexi power-holder whose large Luoyang retinue, seal submission, returned marquis honor, and new appointment turn regional power into continued integration.
- Newly attached high official whose status above old followers creates humility and resignation anxiety that Liu Xiu refuses to let become withdrawal.
- Confucian household instructor who wants sons to be cautious and proper rather than exceptional.
- Aged Eastern Han Dou-family patriarch and Anfeng Dai marquis.
- Personal standing still matters enough that punishment of descendants is initially limited.
- Unable to discipline descendants whose unlawful conduct creates court danger.
- Early warning node for how Dou-family prestige can coexist with household misconduct.

## Qualifications

The source transcripts contain route-sensitive name and office details, so this page normalizes the Anfeng Dai marquis route to 窦融 / Dou Rong while preserving source-bound scope. Hanji 714 gives the Luoyang-entry and appointment scene but not a full administrative record for the Yizhou post or every member of the five-commandery party. Hanji 715's provincial label is not treated as a durable geography claim here; the durable point is Dou Rong's new-affiliate status, high office, humility, and rejected resignation. Hanji 736 does not give a full account of Dou Rong's earlier career, his complete genealogy, or every charge against later family members.
