## Overview

严助 / Yan Zhu is an early [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] recruit who argues for relieving [[DongouWesternHan|东瓯]], attempts to mobilize Kuaiji troops without the normal tiger tally, later brokers [[ZhuMaichenWesternHan|朱买臣]]'s entry to court, and becomes the execution victim whose death gives Zhu's opposition to [[ZhangTangWesternHan|张汤]] a personal foundation.

## Current Profile

Hanji 252 supplies Yan's earliest current role. He argues that a capable empire should not ignore a smaller polity's aid request, wins Wudi's support against Tian Fen's nonintervention position, and goes to Kuaiji to mobilize relief forces. The commandery initially refuses because Yan lacks a tiger tally; the episode says he kills an opposing military official, conveys imperial authority, and secures the force before Minyue withdraws. This makes him both an intervention advocate and a case of imperial purpose overriding ordinary command authentication.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-339-1-sanrenchenghu-ruhe-bisi-xihan-diyi-kuli-lowphplbvgugdyfag-sczpgggyrv|Hanji 339-1]] says Yan recommends the struggling Zhu Maichen to Wudi, after which Zhu receives a court appointment. The episode later places Yan in the political fallout from his friendship with Huainan king Liu An: Wudi reportedly regards the offense as insufficient for death, while Zhang Tang insists on execution. Zhu thereafter watches Zhang for actionable misconduct.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-286-canbei-wujie-qiannian-zhumaichen-xiuqi-de-fanzhuan-zhenxiang-2-lrgdpir3ez-hk_eptcq8q3-pzozr|Hanji 286 part 2]] supplies the earlier access mechanism. It calls Yan a favored fellow townsman already close to Wudi and says he obtains an audience for Zhu after Zhu has exhausted his provisions while waiting in Chang'an. Zhu's learning then earns appointment in the episode's account, making Yan an access broker rather than a substitute for demonstrated ability.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han court figure in the episode's account.
- Early recruit who advocates relief for Dongou and is entrusted with the Kuaiji mobilization.
- Envoy whose tally-less demand meets lawful refusal before coercive imperial enforcement succeeds.
- Patron and fellow townsman who reportedly secures Zhu Maichen's first imperial audience.
- Friend of Huainan king Liu An.
- Executed after the Huainan case in the episode's reconstruction.
- Becomes the personal grievance linking Zhu Maichen to Zhang Tang's downfall.

## Qualifications

Hanji 339-1 renders the name as “严柱”; this page normalizes it to 严助 because the Zhu Maichen recommendation and Huainan Liu An context identify that figure, while Hanji 286 part 2 independently uses 严助 in the same patronage sequence. Hanji 252 does not establish the exact wording of the intervention debate, the identity or office of the person killed, the legal basis for Yan's action, or the troop movement's causal effect because Minyue withdraws before arrival. The sources also do not reconstruct Yan's full career, the complete case against him, Zhang Tang's formal authority, Wudi's decision process, or whether Zhu's later conduct can be reduced to this grievance alone.
