# Durrington Walls

## Overview

Durrington Walls is a Neolithic site near [[Stonehenge]] represented in the wiki as a settlement and feasting location within the wider Stonehenge landscape.

## Current Profile

[[199-stonehenge-glt3612348466]] uses Durrington Walls to move interpretation beyond the stone monument alone. Evidence of pigs and feasting supports large gatherings, and the site may have accommodated people participating in the major sarsen-building phase.

Mike Parker Pearson's contrast between Durrington Walls as a landscape of life and Stonehenge as a landscape of death captures the settlement-burial difference, but [[MikePitts|Mike Pitts]] treats it as significant rather than exhaustive. The sites may have joined domestic activity, communal eating, construction, ritual, and mortuary practice within one changing [[StonehengeRitualLandscape|ritual landscape]].

## Key Characteristics

- A nearby Neolithic settlement used to interpret Stonehenge in its broader landscape.
- Associated in the episode with pig remains, feasting, and large communal gatherings.
- A possible residence for people involved in constructing the large sarsen monument.
- Contrasted with Stonehenge's cremation and mortuary evidence without being reduced to a simple life/death binary.

## Evidence

- Gathering and food: [[199-stonehenge-glt3612348466]] cites pig and feasting evidence at Durrington Walls.
- Construction connection: [[199-stonehenge-glt3612348466]] presents the site as a possible home for sarsen-phase builders.
- Interpretive contrast: [[199-stonehenge-glt3612348466]] describes and qualifies the landscape-of-life versus landscape-of-death theory.

## Qualifications

The episode does not establish that every resident built Stonehenge, that all feasts were construction events, or that Durrington Walls represented only life while Stonehenge represented only death. The site's detailed chronology, architecture, animal provenance, and relationship to other local monuments require specialist evidence beyond this source.

## What Changed

- Established Durrington Walls as the settlement and feasting counterpart that situates Stonehenge within a lived regional landscape.
