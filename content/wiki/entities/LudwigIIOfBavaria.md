# Ludwig II of Bavaria

## Overview

Ludwig II of Bavaria was the young king whose intense admiration for [[RichardWagner]] became decisive material patronage for Wagnerian performance and theatre-building.

## Current Profile

The source introduces Ludwig in 1864 as a newly crowned eighteen-year-old and an exceptional Wagner enthusiast. His significance in the bounded evidence is institutional: admiration became money, enabling the first production of [[TristanAndIsolde|Tristan and Isolde]] in 1865 and supporting Wagner's theatre at [[BayreuthFestival|Bayreuth]].

Ludwig therefore represents patronage as productive infrastructure rather than passive appreciation. The source does not survey his reign or private life; it uses him to explain how artistically ambitious work that exceeded ordinary market and court arrangements acquired a stage.

## Key Characteristics

- Became king of Bavaria at eighteen in the source's account.
- Held an unusually intense admiration for Wagner and his music.
- Converted personal enthusiasm into direct financial patronage.
- Enabled the delayed first production of *Tristan and Isolde*.
- Helped finance the purpose-built Wagnerian theatre at Bayreuth.

## Evidence

- Royal encounter: [[631-wagner-live-at-the-royal-albert-hall-glt7214350401]] dates Wagner's meeting with the newly crowned Ludwig to 1864.
- Production patronage: [[631-wagner-live-at-the-royal-albert-hall-glt7214350401]] credits Ludwig with paying for the 1865 first production of *Tristan and Isolde*.
- Institutional patronage: [[631-wagner-live-at-the-royal-albert-hall-glt7214350401]] connects Ludwig's funding to Wagner's theatre at Bayreuth.

## Qualifications

This profile is narrowly bounded to Ludwig's Wagner patronage and is not a general biography or evaluation of his reign. The source's description of him as the ultimate Wagner fan is rhetorical. It does not quantify his funding, separate phases of support, or examine political opposition and financial consequences.
