# Thomas Foley

## Overview

Thomas Foley was the Royal Navy captain whose initiative aboard *Goliath* opened the landward attack at the 1798 [[BattleOfTheNile|Battle of the Nile]].

## Current Profile

The episode presents Foley as the captain who converted [[HoratioNelson|Nelson]]'s shared objective into a decisive local maneuver. Leading the British approach through shoal-filled Aboukir Bay, he used an older French chart and reasoned that the anchored French ships must have navigable water on their landward side. *Goliath* passed around the French van, raked the leading ship, anchored inside the line, and began the double-sided concentration that following British ships extended. His action exemplifies delegated initiative: Nelson did not need to prescribe the exact route because his captains understood the aim and could exploit an opening.

## Key Characteristics

- Royal Navy captain commanding *Goliath* at the Battle of the Nile.
- Used chart evidence, anchorage logic, and local judgment in unfamiliar coastal water.
- Led the landward passage that exposed the French van to fire from both sides.
- Represents captain-level initiative inside Nelson's shared command intent rather than isolated individual genius.
