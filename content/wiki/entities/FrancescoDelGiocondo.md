# Francesco del Giocondo

## Overview
Francesco del Giocondo appears in [[667-the-mystery-of-the-mona-lisa-glt1735270133]] as [[LisaGherardini|Lisa Gherardini]]'s husband and the likely social context for [[MonaLisa|the Mona Lisa]] commission.

## Current Profile
The source describes Francesco as an upwardly mobile Florentine businessman. His family moved from barrel-making into textiles, silks, moneylending, sugar, and land. He married Lisa as his second wife, had children with her, and described her warmly in his 1537 will.

## Key Characteristics
- Francesco anchors the Mona Lisa in ordinary Florentine family and business networks.
- His family mobility helps explain why a portrait commission would make social sense.
- His name supplies the "del Giocondo" and "La Gioconda" connection.
- His legal network may have connected the commission to Leonardo's father.
- His will provides one of the source's strongest personal details about Lisa.

## Evidence
- Business and status - [[667-the-mystery-of-the-mona-lisa-glt1735270133]] describes the family's movement into textiles, silks, moneylending, sugar, and land.
- Marriage and household - [[667-the-mystery-of-the-mona-lisa-glt1735270133]] says he married Lisa when she was fifteen, became father to six children with her, and had a son from a first marriage whom Lisa helped raise.
- Commission network - [[667-the-mystery-of-the-mona-lisa-glt1735270133]] says Leonardo's father was Francesco's lawyer, making the commission plausible through local networks.

## Qualifications
The source does not settle whether Francesco paid for or received the painting. It uses his profile to explain a plausible commission setting, not to reconstruct the complete transaction.

## What Changed
- Created Francesco del Giocondo as the Florentine household and commission-context node.
