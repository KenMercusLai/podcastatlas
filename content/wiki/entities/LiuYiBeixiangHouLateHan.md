## Overview

北乡侯刘裔 / Liu Yi (Beixiang Hou) appears in the wiki as the short-lived Yan-backed child ruler whose illness and death trigger [[EmperorShunOfHan|汉顺帝 / Liu Bao]]'s restoration crisis. The page uses `LiuYiBeixiangHouLateHan` to distinguish him from [[LiuYiLateHan|刘翊 / Liu Yi]], the later Zhang Miao-side actor.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-803-han-shundi-ruhe-zai-xuefengxueyu-zhong-dengshang-huangwei-lqvno7t-gcgxtbs3phuflr4ybe2s|Hanji 803]] supplies the decisive succession trigger. After Liu Yi becomes gravely ill, [[SunChengLateHan|孙程]] tells Changxing Xu that Liu Bao is the prior emperor's legitimate son and has no true offense; if Liu Yi cannot recover, Sun Cheng wants to eliminate Jiang Jin and [[YanXianLateHan|阎显]] and restore Liu Bao. When Liu Yi dies on 125-10-27, [[EmpressDowagerYanLateHan|阎太后]] conceals the death, summons royal descendants, closes palace gates, and tries to preserve Yan-family control over the next selection.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-804-gei-ni-tang-de-ren-bu-yiding-shi-guiren-lqongdqg-hgetoegpylv-os86buh|Hanji 804]] uses Liu Yi mainly as a legitimacy foil. [[CuiYuanLateHan|崔瑗]] reads Beixiang Hou's position as weak because he is not the prior emperor's legitimate son, which makes the Yan succession route vulnerable once Liu Bao has a palace-backed restoration path.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-805-tongtou-renxing-de-nvren-jing-neng-yuce-jixiong-ll3bzoyrkbtnvt-aqu9nqelqonew|Hanji 805]] supplies the post-restoration settlement. After Shun is restored, Liu Yi is buried with princely rites rather than as a lasting imperial predecessor, making his public status part of Shun's legitimacy cleanup.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-825-lishi-gaosu-ni-dang-ni-meiyong-shi-renxing-zui-zisi-luv-8z9axuejnfhlkgsuxee3uked|Hanji 825]] shows Liu Yi's death becoming a later warning precedent. When [[EmpressDowagerLiangNaLateHan|梁太后]] considers delaying [[EmperorChongOfHan|汉冲帝]]'s death announcement, [[LiGuLateHan|李固]] invokes the Beixiang Hou case as a reason not to repeat concealed-death transition control.

## Key Characteristics

- Short-lived Yan-backed successor whose illness exposes the fragility of the Yan succession plan.
- Legitimacy foil to Liu Bao because later sources present Liu Bao as the prior emperor's legitimate son.
- Trigger for a concealed-death succession race when Yan Taihou hides his death and summons royal candidates.
- Post-restoration status problem resolved by burial with princely rites rather than durable imperial elevation.
- Later precedent for why hiding an emperor's death is politically dangerous.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 803, Hanji 804, Hanji 805, and Hanji 825. It does not reconstruct Liu Yi's full lineage, palace life, enthronement procedure, or biological cause of death. Hanji 803 supplies the death-concealment trigger, Hanji 804 supplies the legitimacy-weakness reading, Hanji 805 supplies burial status, and Hanji 825 supplies later precedent value.
