## Overview

北乡侯刘裔 / 刘懿 / Liu Yi (Beixiang Hou) appears in the wiki as the short-lived Yan-backed child ruler whose accession and death bracket the first phase of [[EmperorShunOfHan|汉顺帝 / Liu Bao]]'s restoration crisis. Hanji 802 supplies his selection after [[EmperorAnOfHan|Emperor An]]'s death; Hanji 803 supplies his illness and hidden death; Hanji 805 and Hanji 825 show how his status is later neutralized and remembered as a warning precedent. The page keeps the stable route `LiuYiBeixiangHouLateHan` to distinguish him from [[LiuYiLateHan|刘翊 / Liu Yi]], while preserving the 刘裔 / 刘懿 source-name tension.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-802-andi-zhisi-donghan-wangchao-quanli-da-xipai-lgvne92e0jjzjnznzfkz7nvztnbj|Hanji 802]] supplies Liu Yi's accession layer. After Emperor An dies and [[EmpressDowagerYanLateHan|Yan Taihou]] returns secretly to Luoyang, she and [[YanXianLateHan|Yan Xian]] choose the young Beixiang Hou as emperor. The episode states the control logic directly: a young ruler lets the Yan family hold court power behind him. Liu Bao, the demoted former crown prince and An's son, is excluded despite visible mourning.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-803-han-shundi-ruhe-zai-xuefengxueyu-zhong-dengshang-huangwei-lqvno7t-gcgxtbs3phuflr4ybe2s|Hanji 803]] supplies the decisive succession trigger after that accession. After Liu Yi becomes gravely ill, [[SunChengLateHan|Sun Cheng]] tells Changxing Xu that Liu Bao is the prior emperor's legitimate son and has no true offense; if Liu Yi cannot recover, Sun Cheng wants to eliminate Jiang Jin and Yan Xian and restore Liu Bao. When Liu Yi dies on 125-10-27, Yan Taihou conceals the death, summons royal descendants, closes palace gates, and tries to preserve Yan-family control over the next selection.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-804-gei-ni-tang-de-ren-bu-yiding-shi-guiren-lqongdqg-hgetoegpylv-os86buh|Hanji 804]] uses Liu Yi mainly as a legitimacy foil. [[CuiYuanLateHan|Cui Yuan]] reads Beixiang Hou's position as weak because he is not the prior emperor's legitimate son, which makes the Yan succession route vulnerable once Liu Bao has a palace-backed restoration path.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-805-tongtou-renxing-de-nvren-jing-neng-yuce-jixiong-ll3bzoyrkbtnvt-aqu9nqelqonew|Hanji 805]] supplies the post-restoration settlement. After Shun is restored, Liu Yi is buried with princely rites rather than as a lasting imperial predecessor, making his public status part of Shun's legitimacy cleanup.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-825-lishi-gaosu-ni-dang-ni-meiyong-shi-renxing-zui-zisi-luv-8z9axuejnfhlkgsuxee3uked|Hanji 825]] shows Liu Yi's death becoming a later warning precedent. When [[EmpressDowagerLiangNaLateHan|梁太后]] considers delaying [[EmperorChongOfHan|Emperor Chong]]'s death announcement, [[LiGuLateHan|Li Gu]] invokes the Beixiang Hou case as a reason not to repeat concealed-death transition control.

## Key Characteristics

- Young Yan-backed successor chosen after Emperor An's death because his age makes behind-the-throne control easier.
- Legitimacy foil to Liu Bao because later sources present Liu Bao as the prior emperor's legitimate son.
- Short-lived ruler whose illness exposes the fragility of the Yan succession plan.
- Trigger for a second concealed-death succession race when Yan Taihou hides his death and summons royal candidates.
- Post-restoration status problem resolved by burial with princely rites rather than durable imperial elevation.
- Later precedent for why hiding an emperor's death is politically dangerous.
- Disambiguation case with a source-name tension between 刘裔 and 刘懿.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 802, Hanji 803, Hanji 804, Hanji 805, and Hanji 825. It does not reconstruct Liu Yi's full lineage, palace life, enthronement procedure, or biological cause of death. Hanji 802 renders him as 刘懿, while earlier wiki pages used 刘裔 from source-note normalization; the current route remains stable until a broader identity cleanup resolves the display name.
