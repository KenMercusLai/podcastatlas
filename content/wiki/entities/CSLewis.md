# C. S. Lewis / C.S. 刘易斯

## Overview

C. S. Lewis was Tolkien's postwar friend, intellectual companion, candid critic, and Christian literary counterpart, used by the current sources to distinguish shared inheritance from artistic agreement and direct allegory from embedded religious structure.

## Current Profile

[[225-j-r-r-tolkien-glt9725776786]] places the men's 1926 meeting after Tolkien's wartime loss of nearly all his closest school friends. Language, Norse myth, Anglo-Saxon material, private worldbuilding, and the practice of reading work aloud gave the friendship its intellectual base.

Lewis and [[JRRTolkien|Tolkien]] appear as affectionate, intellectually serious friends who could praise one another's character while criticizing one another's writing. Tolkien's dislike of *Narnia* and Lewis's resistance to some of Tolkien's fantasy habits keep the friendship from becoming a polished legend of automatic agreement. [[60-xianliao-weida-zuojiamen-de-bagua-di-yi-dan-648693204]]

The relationship also supplies a literary-theological contrast. Lewis's *The Lion, the Witch and the Wardrobe* represents comparatively direct Christian allegory, while [[TheLordOfTheRings]] contains no explicit church, God, or Christ and instead embeds sacrifice, pity, relinquished power, and hope within the story's structure. [[226-the-lord-of-the-rings-glt1788342017]]

## Key Characteristics

- Tolkien's close friend, intellectual companion, and candid literary critic.
- Postwar companion linked to Tolkien through language, northern myth, and mutual reading.
- Christian writer used as a contrast between direct allegory and embedded theology.
- Example of friendship that accommodates serious artistic disagreement.

## Evidence

- Friendship formation: [[225-j-r-r-tolkien-glt9725776786]] supplies the 1926 meeting and shared interests in language, Norse myth, Anglo-Saxon material, and reading one another's work.
- Friendship and criticism: [[60-xianliao-weida-zuojiamen-de-bagua-di-yi-dan-648693204]] supplies the mutual respect, teasing, praise, and dissatisfaction with each other's fiction.
- Christian literary contrast: [[226-the-lord-of-the-rings-glt1788342017]] contrasts Lewis's direct Narnia allegory with Tolkien's implicit Catholic structure.

## Qualifications

These sources use Lewis primarily as a comparison within Tolkien's profile rather than providing a full account of Lewis's theology, scholarship, fiction, conversion, or career. The biographical sequence does not make Lewis a replacement for the TCBS friends Tolkien lost, and the direct-versus-embedded distinction does not describe every work by either author.

## What Changed

- Added the 1926 meeting and the shared philological and northern-literary basis of the friendship.
- Placed the friendship after Tolkien's wartime loss without reducing it to replacement psychology.
