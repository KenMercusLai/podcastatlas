# Ghana

## Overview

Ghana is a West African country represented across the sources through precolonial [[Asante]] history, contemporary radio access, food-price comparison, and regional cocaine-route displacement.

## Current Profile

The historical source places modern Ghana at a crossroads between Atlantic coastal commerce, inland Akan state formation, gold, slavery, and northern Islamic literacy. [[Asante]], [[Kumasi]], and the [[GoldenStool]] supply a long political-history branch in which conquest and British colonial pressure do not erase institutional continuity.

The contemporary sources show different systems operating across the same national setting. [[JoyFM]] and digital-radio trials make Ghana part of [[AfricanRadioResilience]]; the [[JollofIndex]] segment uses lower inflation and a stronger currency to contrast Ghanaian food-price transmission with [[Nigeria]]; and a trafficking report names Ghana as one destination when scrutiny displaces cocaine routes within [[WestAfrica]].

## Key Characteristics

- Modern national setting for the continuing Asante polity and capital at [[Kumasi]].
- Historical Gold Coast crossroads joining coastal European commerce and inland African and Islamic networks.
- Food-price contrast case where macroeconomic stability can cushion household costs.
- Radio market where established national news and DAB trials coexist.
- Regional route-displacement destination in the West African cocaine trade.

## Qualifications

These four sources cover distinct topics and do not form a comprehensive national profile. The Asante episode is a brisk popular-history narrative and should not make one polity stand for every Ghanaian history or identity. Inflation, currency, radio, and trafficking claims are time-bound episode reports rather than current national statistics.
