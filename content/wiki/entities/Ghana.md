# Ghana

## Overview

Ghana is a West African country represented across the sources through [[Asante]] state history, Gold Coast decolonization under [[KwameNkrumah]], and contemporary media, food-price, and trafficking systems.

## Current Profile

The historical sources now supply two connected but distinct political layers. The Asante episode places modern Ghana at a crossroads among Atlantic coastal commerce, inland Akan state formation, gold, slavery, and northern Islamic literacy. [[Asante]], [[Kumasi]], and the [[GoldenStool]] show institutional continuity through conquest and British colonial pressure. The decolonization episode then follows the Gold Coast from British cooperation with educated African elites to Nkrumah's mass demand for immediate independence and Ghana's 1957 sovereignty.

Independence made Accra an early center of [[PanAfricanism]], including the 1958 conference at which [[FrantzFanon]] connected Algerian liberation with struggles elsewhere on the continent. The contemporary sources then show different systems operating across the same national setting: [[JoyFM]] and digital-radio trials, the [[JollofIndex]] food-cost comparison with [[Nigeria]], and cocaine-route displacement within [[WestAfrica]].

## Key Characteristics

- Modern national setting for the continuing Asante polity and capital at [[Kumasi]].
- Historical Gold Coast crossroads joining coastal European commerce and inland African and Islamic networks.
- Early sub-Saharan independence case where elite nationalism broadened into mass politics.
- Pan-African convening center under Kwame Nkrumah.
- Food-price contrast case where macroeconomic stability can cushion household costs.
- Radio market where established national news and DAB trials coexist.
- Regional route-displacement destination in the West African cocaine trade.

## Evidence

- Historical setting: [[269-ghana-the-ashanti-empire-glt3638102749]] places Asante formation, gold, Atlantic trade, northern literacy, British war, and symbolic continuity within what is now Ghana.
- Independence pathway: [[100-decolonising-africa-glt2427735251]] connects Gold Coast elite politics, Nkrumah's split and imprisonment, mass support, elections, and 1957 independence.
- Pan-African center: [[100-decolonising-africa-glt2427735251]] places Fanon's 1958 continental appeal at an Accra conference under Nkrumah's leadership.
- Food affordability: [[latin-lessons-the-donroe-doctrine-boost-6a4233e26b6690f46aae4e3d]] uses Ghana as a lower-inflation, stronger-currency contrast to Nigeria's jollof costs.
- Media access: [[in-arms-way-gaza-deal-sticking-points-6a71b49647235c354d3c9b74]] identifies Joy FM and a Ghanaian DAB trial within Africa's durable radio ecology.
- Trafficking displacement: [[taking-the-shine-off-albedo-and-global-temperatures-6a730fdaca067b295bab9760]] names Ghana among destinations that receive greater pressure when other West African routes face scrutiny.

## Qualifications

These five sources cover distinct topics and do not form a comprehensive national profile. The Asante episode should not make one polity stand for every Ghanaian history or identity, and the decolonization episode focuses on Nkrumah's rise rather than his complete governing record or the participation of all regions and social groups. Inflation, currency, radio, and trafficking claims are time-bound episode reports rather than current national statistics.

## What Changed

- Added Gold Coast independence and Pan-African Accra as a second major historical layer beside Asante continuity.
- Connected Nkrumah's elite breakaway strategy to workers, unions, teachers, elections, and mass legitimacy.

- [[PanAfricanism]] - transnational movement for which Accra became an early postcolonial center.
- [[AfricanDecolonization]] - comparative process in which Ghana supplies a relatively organized independence pathway.
