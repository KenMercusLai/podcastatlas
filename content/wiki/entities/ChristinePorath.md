# Christine Porath

## Overview
Christine Porath is a behavioral scientist represented here through a [[HiddenBrain]] episode on the cognitive, relational, organizational, and network costs of incivility.

## Current Profile
Porath presents disrespect as more than an etiquette failure. Her account connects threat arousal and rumination to lost attention, memory and calculation errors, reduced creativity, weaker trust, less information sharing, customer withdrawal, job exit, and patient-safety risk. She also argues that targets and witnesses can pass negativity forward unintentionally, making civility a performance and systems issue rather than a purely private virtue.

Her practical framework combines self-awareness, candid but caring feedback, respectful culture, resilience-building activity, and [[SelfDistancingRecovery]]. The account preserves an important boundary: recovery can restore a target's agency, but it should not make the target responsible for fixing repeat offenders or unsafe institutions.

## Key Characteristics
- Studies incivility as a measurable cognitive and organizational exposure.
- Emphasizes witness effects and the social transmission of negative behavior.
- Connects respect and trust to information sharing, creativity, help-seeking, and safety.
- Treats self-awareness and behavioral feedback as correctives to unintentional harm.
- Recommends personal recovery practices without denying contextual or institutional responsibility.

## Qualifications
This profile is based on one structured episode summary rather than Porath's publications, datasets, full interviews, or a systematic review. Reported percentages, experimental effects, long-term memories, workplace outcomes, medical-team effects, cultural comparisons, and intervention benefits remain source-scoped. Defining incivility partly through recipient perception supports attention to impact but does not by itself resolve intent, severity, power, evidence, or accountability.
