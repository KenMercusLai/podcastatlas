## Overview
精工足球队 / Seiko Football Club is the Hong Kong club case the source uses to explain the capital-backed peak of postwar Hong Kong professional football. It is deliberately disambiguated from [[Seiko|the Japanese watchmaker]] already present in the wiki.

## Current Profile
The source presents Seiko as a club built from Huang-family capital connected to Thai Chinese business and watch agency activity. Its importance is not only the title count: from 1979 to 1985 it won seven consecutive league championships, upgraded import-player expectations, drew leading local players such as Hu Kwok Hung, and made Hong Kong football's early golden-yuan dynamic visible.

Seiko also marks a limit. The episode says its Asian club competition run included beating Liaoning home and away in 1985, but that it did not continue to the final stage because travel costs to West Asia were high and Asian football prize money or sponsorship did not cover the burden. The club's later exit is framed less as a direct all-Chinese-squad-policy effect and more as an owner decision after a long operating cycle and impending local-star retirement.

## Key Characteristics
- Capital-backed Hong Kong football dynasty associated with the Huang family.
- Seven-season league champion from 1979 to 1985 in the source's account.
- Import-player escalator that helped normalize higher-profile foreign recruitment.
- Local-star aggregator associated with Hu Kwok Hung and owner-player relationship management.
- Asian-competition near-miss whose withdrawal shows the limits of regional club economics.
