# Oakley

## Overview

Oakley appears in the wiki as the outdoors and adventure eyewear collaborator named in the [[MarketplaceTech]] episode on [[Meta]]'s smart-glasses strategy. The episode uses the collaboration to show how [[RayBanSmartGlasses|Ray-Ban smart glasses]] moved from gadget positioning toward activity-specific and style-coded adoption.

## Current Profile

In this source, Oakley is not profiled as a standalone eyewear company. It functions as a positioning partner: its outdoors and point-of-view adventure associations help Meta test whether smart glasses can feel natural in sports, travel, and creator contexts.

That makes Oakley relevant to [[AIGlassesProductFit]] because a form factor may succeed where the activity already justifies eyewear, hands-free capture, and first-person perspective.

## Key Characteristics

- Oakley is used as an activity-coded extension of Meta's smart-glasses push.
- Its source role is tied to outdoor, adventure, and point-of-view content rather than general social-platform use.
- The collaboration helps frame smart glasses as situational gear, not only as consumer electronics.

## Evidence

- **Activity-coded positioning:** [[tech-20260826-0826-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260826-0826-mp-tech-pod-128]] says Meta expanded into outdoors and point-of-view adventure culture through an Oakley collaboration in 2025.
- **Smart-glasses product fit:** [[tech-20260826-0826-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260826-0826-mp-tech-pod-128]] pairs the Oakley collaboration with travel and hands-free recording use cases.
- **Marketing normalization:** [[tech-20260826-0826-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260826-0826-mp-tech-pod-128]] places Oakley alongside fashion and celebrity promotion as part of Meta's broader adoption strategy.

## Qualifications

- The source does not describe Oakley's commercial terms, technical contribution, or independent smart-glasses strategy.
- The collaboration is evidence of positioning, not proof that outdoor users have broadly adopted the glasses.

## What Changed

- Oakley was added as an activity-positioning partner in Meta's smart-glasses mainstreaming strategy.
