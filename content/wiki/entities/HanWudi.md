# Emperor Wu of Han / 汉武帝

Emperor Wu of Han / 汉武帝 appears in [[87-guonian-zui-shihe-liao-xuanxue-tantao-shenxian-fangshu-fazhanshi-wei-wan-daixu-704051997]] as the episode's most extensive case of imperial fangshu consumption. The source presents his求仙 as deeper and more expensive than [[QinShiHuang|秦始皇]]'s: Li Shaojun, Shaoweng, Luan Da, spirit-summoning displays, sea voyages, palaces, platforms, and alchemical promises all feed a long search for immortality.

The same source also makes Han Wudi central to [[WuguPoliticalPanic|巫蛊之乱]]. His late-life illness, suspicion, and willingness to accept curse accusations allow denunciation, torture, palace rivalry, and succession fear to escalate into mass death and the suicide of the crown prince and empress.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-105-zhanzheng-huayang-zhaoguo-limu-zui-hui-shua-ljtqdg46a8o13zzsjv-p-fpol5oh]] adds a narrow military comparison. The episode contrasts [[LiMu|李牧]]'s constrained Zhao frontier strategy with Han Wudi's later ability to fight [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] through deep, expensive cavalry campaigns after Western Han accumulation. This page keeps that as a source-scoped resource comparison, separate from Han Wudi's fangshu and Wugu branch.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-150-plus-zaiye-bujian-2022-qing-duo-guanzhao-2023-fjhgtqac9r-1rpjh6oubmspqywue|Hanji 150 PLUS New Year special]] adds a calendar-and-festival role. The source says Han Wudi fixed the Xia calendar's first day of the first month as 元旦, making him an anchor for [[YuanDanFestivalLayering|元旦节日层累]] and [[CalendarReformLegitimacy|历法改革与政治合法性]] rather than only for fangshu, war resources, or late-life political panic.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-178-maodun-weihe-yao-fangguo-liubang-zhe-cai-shi-zhenxiang-2-loluh53iyrzo4ucf3ttzmn3-otkv|Hanji 178 part 2]] adds Han Wudi as Sima Guang's negative afterexample in the [[WeiyangPalace|未央宫]] discussion. Sima Guang argues that early Western Han palace grandeur helped set a precedent whose later expansion under Han Wudi exhausted the people.

## Key Claims
- The source treats Han Wudi's求仙 as a political and fiscal system, not only a private obsession.
- Fangshi repeatedly convert failure into claims of hidden success, distance, obstruction, or the practitioner's own transcendence.
- The emperor's late disillusionment does not undo the earlier waste and violence attached to his belief.
- Wugu accusations show the darker side of the same interpretive environment: hidden ritual action can be treated as evidence of political danger.
- Qinji 105 uses Han Wudi only as a later resource comparison: Western Han can sustain expensive anti-Xiongnu offensives that Zhao in Li Mu's time could not.
- Hanji 150 PLUS uses Han Wudi as a calendar-standardization anchor for the traditional naming of 元旦.
- Hanji 178 part 2 uses Han Wudi as a later warning about palace-building precedent and the fiscal-moral cost of imperial grandeur.
