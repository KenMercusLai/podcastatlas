# Christian X of Denmark

## Overview

Christian X was Denmark's king during the German occupation and became a public symbol of national continuity and Jewish civic inclusion.

## Current Profile

The episode emphasizes symbolic rather than operational leadership. Christian remained in [[Denmark]], rode publicly through Copenhagen, and joined the broader political insistence that Danish Jews were Danish citizens who could not be separated from the nation.

His curt 1942 birthday reply to Hitler intensified the “telegram crisis,” but the source rejects the famous story that Christian himself wore a yellow star. His place in the rescue history therefore rests on public legitimacy and civic belonging, not that invented gesture or direct management of escape boats and hiding networks.

## Key Characteristics

- Resident monarch who maintained visible national continuity during occupation.
- Symbolic defender of Jewish citizens' inclusion within the Danish nation.
- Participant in a public rupture with Hitler through the 1942 telegram crisis.
- Subject of a durable yellow-star myth that the episode explicitly rejects.

## Evidence

- Public continuity: [[284-denmark-the-great-escape-glt4809968647]] describes Christian remaining in Denmark and riding through Copenhagen.
- Civic inclusion: [[284-denmark-the-great-escape-glt4809968647]] attributes to the king and Danish politicians the position that Jewish citizens could not be singled out.
- Myth correction and conflict: [[284-denmark-the-great-escape-glt4809968647]] rejects the yellow-star story and recounts Hitler's anger over Christian's brief telegram reply.

## Qualifications

The episode does not establish that Christian directed the [[RescueOfTheDanishJews]] or that symbolism alone explains it. The exact wording and setting of his statements, the political meaning of his rides, and the telegram crisis require documentary corroboration beyond the source.

## What Changed

- Created Christian X as a symbol of civic inclusion while separating documented posture from the yellow-star myth.
