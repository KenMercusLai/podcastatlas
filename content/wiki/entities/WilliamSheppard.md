# William Sheppard

## Overview

William Sheppard was a missionary whose observations supplied early evidence of killing and severed-hand accounting in the [[CongoFreeState|Congo Free State]].

## Current Profile

The episode centers Sheppard as a witness who encountered smoked right hands being kept as evidence of killings. A chief reportedly explained that the hands demonstrated to state agents how many people had been killed, connecting bodily mutilation to the accountability practices of the [[ForcePublique|Force Publique]].

## Key Characteristics

- Worked in the Congo as a missionary.
- Observed preserved severed hands rather than merely repeating a distant rumor.
- Recorded an explanation linking the hands to proof of killings for the colonial state.
- Became part of the cumulative missionary and eyewitness record that challenged denial of Congo atrocities.

## Evidence

- Direct observation: [[539-horror-in-the-congo-the-crimes-of-empire-part-2-glt5467841243]] says Sheppard found right hands being smoked and preserved.
- Colonial accounting: [[539-horror-in-the-congo-the-crimes-of-empire-part-2-glt5467841243]] reports a chief's explanation that the hands showed the state how many people had been killed.
- Evidentiary role: [[539-horror-in-the-congo-the-crimes-of-empire-part-2-glt5467841243]] places his observation within a wider cumulative case built from agents, missionaries, and other observers.

## Qualifications

The episode does not provide Sheppard's full biography, mission context, original written account, or a complete chain of command for the killings. It notes that many hands were removed from corpses, that living amputees also appear in the wider record, and that the custom's pre-Leopold origins are disputed; the institutional use of severed hands under the Congo regime remains the relevant claim.

## What Changed

- Established Sheppard's role in connecting atrocity testimony to the regime's systems of proof and accountability.

## Relationships

- [[ForcePublique|Force Publique]] - armed institution whose killing and ammunition discipline contextualizes Sheppard's evidence.
- [[CongoFreeState|Congo Free State]] - colonial regime in which he made the observation.
- [[LeopoldII|Leopold II]] - sovereign whose regime was implicated by the cumulative evidence.
- [[EvidenceDrivenHumanRightsCampaign]] - later reform process that depended on corroborating missionary and eyewitness evidence.
- [[CoerciveCommodityExtraction]] - production system within which killing became an accountable output.
