# Red Cloud

## Overview

Red Cloud was an Oglala [[LakotaPeople|Lakota]] leader whose campaign against the Bozeman Trail forts helped produce the 1868 settlement and whose later acceptance of reservation life contrasted with [[SittingBull]] and [[CrazyHorse]].

## Current Profile

The episode presents Red Cloud's war as a successful defense of Lakota power in the Powder River country. The [[FettermanFight]] intensified U.S. shock, and the government ultimately closed the Bozeman Trail and abandoned its forts before negotiating the [[GreatSiouxReservation]] and associated unceded territory.

After visiting Washington and meeting President Ulysses S. Grant, Red Cloud concluded that the scale of U.S. population and power made accommodation the realistic course. Most Lakota, together with many Cheyenne and Arapaho people, followed the reservation path. The source treats this as a constrained strategic judgment rather than proof that Red Cloud accepted the justice or clarity of U.S. policy.

## Key Characteristics

- His military leadership helped force closure of the Bozeman Trail forts.
- The Fetterman victory strengthened his bargaining position and Lakota military reputation.
- His Washington visit changed his assessment of what resistance could achieve.
- He accepted reservation accommodation under unequal demographic and military pressure.
- His choice became a political contrast with Sitting Bull's continued refusal.

## Evidence

- Military success: [[449-custer-vs-crazy-horse-rise-of-sitting-bull-part-4-glt2415947095]] connects Red Cloud's war, the Fetterman defeat, and U.S. abandonment of the Bozeman Trail forts.
- Treaty settlement: [[449-custer-vs-crazy-horse-rise-of-sitting-bull-part-4-glt2415947095]] places him within the 1868 reservation and unceded-territory settlement.
- Accommodation: [[449-custer-vs-crazy-horse-rise-of-sitting-bull-part-4-glt2415947095]] says his Washington visit persuaded him that reservation life was the realistic option.

## Qualifications

- This profile is grounded in one popular-history episode rather than Red Cloud's own speeches, Lakota oral history, treaty records, or a full biography.
- The episode compresses a collective war and treaty process around prominent leaders.
- Accommodation under severe pressure should not be equated with free consent or cultural surrender.
- The motives attributed to Red Cloud after Washington remain the source's interpretation.

## What Changed

- Created the profile around military success, treaty settlement, and constrained accommodation.
