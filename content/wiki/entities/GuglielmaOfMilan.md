# Guglielma of Milan

## Overview

Guglielma was a thirteenth-century religious woman in Milan whose charitable reputation and posthumous following developed into claims about a female incarnation of the Holy Spirit.

## Current Profile

[[403-the-mystery-of-the-pregnant-pope-glt1981561492]] presents Guglielma as living in poverty and charity before her death around 1280, after which her Chiaravalle tomb attracted pilgrimage and miracle claims. Surviving inquisitorial fragments attribute to her or her movement a program for women's redemption and a coming female age, but hostile evidence makes it difficult to separate her own teaching from later followers' development.

## Key Characteristics

- Remembered locally for poverty, charity, sanctity, and miracles.
- Became the posthumous center of a movement anticipating a female age of the Spirit.
- Was identified in surviving hostile records as the Holy Spirit incarnate for women's redemption.
- Had her tomb destroyed, body burned, and ashes scattered by inquisitors in 1300.

## Evidence

- Local sanctity: [[403-the-mystery-of-the-pregnant-pope-glt1981561492]] describes pilgrimage and miracle traditions at Chiaravalle.
- Female spiritual program: [[403-the-mystery-of-the-pregnant-pope-glt1981561492]] reports claims about resurrection, women's redemption, and a new age.
- Suppression: [[403-the-mystery-of-the-pregnant-pope-glt1981561492]] records the destruction of her tomb and remains.

## Qualifications

The evidence is fragmentary and filtered through inquisitors. The source does not establish which claims came from Guglielma herself and which were elaborated by [[MaifredaDaPirovano]] or other followers.

## What Changed

- Created a profile distinguishing Guglielma's charitable reputation from the more radical posthumous program attributed to her movement.
