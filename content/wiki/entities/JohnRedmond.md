# John Redmond

## Overview

John Redmond was leader of the reunited Irish Parliamentary Party whose support for Liberal constitutional reform helped bring Irish Home Rule onto the statute book before the First World War.

## Current Profile

Redmond remained loyal to [[CharlesStewartParnell]] during the party split and later reunited the Irish Parliamentary Party. After the 1910 elections left Liberals and Conservatives closely balanced, his MPs became decisive at Westminster. He supported the People's Budget and the Parliament Act even where doing so carried costs with parts of his Irish base.

The Parliament Act curtailed the House of Lords' veto and opened the legislative route for the third Home Rule bill. Redmond's achievement was therefore both Irish and British constitutional politics: he traded parliamentary support for a rule change that made [[IrishHomeRule|Home Rule]] enactable. The episode also stresses the limit of that achievement, because Redmond and the Liberals underestimated Ulster unionist resistance and could not turn statute into accepted settlement before war intervened.

## Key Characteristics

- Parnell loyalist who later reunited the Irish Parliamentary Party.
- Parliamentary leader whose MPs became kingmakers after the 1910 elections.
- Supporter of the People's Budget and the Parliament Act of 1911.
- Central actor in bringing the third Home Rule bill through its statutory route.
- Constitutional nationalist whose achievement was overtaken by unionist mobilization and war.

## Qualifications

The episode ends before a full account of Redmond's First World War policy, the Home Rule suspension, the Easter Rising, or his later political eclipse. Claims about how Home Rule would have operated, whether exclusion or partition could have produced consent, and how much responsibility belonged to Redmond, the Liberals, Conservatives, or unionist leaders remain counterfactual and source-scoped.
