# Malcolm Gaskell

## Overview

Malcolm Gaskell is the historian interviewed about seventeenth-century New England witchcraft and the Springfield case behind *The Ruin of All Witches*.

## Current Profile

Gaskell interprets witchcraft through social, religious, economic, legal, and household history rather than as a synonym for irrationality. He reconstructs a world in which supernatural causation was credible while preserving the actors' uncertainty about perception, testimony, proof, and guilt.

## Key Characteristics

- He uses [[SpringfieldMassachusetts|Springfield]] as a microhistory of Puritan settlement and neighbor conflict.
- He treats religion and economic ambition as compatible motives in early New England.
- He locates accusation in converging pressures rather than in belief alone.
- He resists translating every seventeenth-century experience directly into a modern diagnosis.
- He emphasizes that belief in witchcraft could coexist with judicial skepticism and acquittal.
