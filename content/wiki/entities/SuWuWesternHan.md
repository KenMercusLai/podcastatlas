## Overview

苏武 / Su Wu is synthesized as the Western Han chief envoy whose mission crisis, coerced detention, long exile, and return form one extended loyalty case. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-376-1-lishishang-zhenshi-de-suwu-muyang-shi-shenmeyang-lidxs1d66m5q2hegjteq1lgra5fp|Hanji 376-1]] supplies the opening: [[ZhangShengSuWuMissionWesternHan|张胜]]'s unauthorized involvement in [[YuChangWesternHan|虞常]]'s plot exposes the mission, after which Su Wu twice seeks death rather than let coercion publicly degrade his office. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-398-ruguo-ni-shi-liling-hui-ti-xiongnu-quanjiang-suwu-ma-lrvkyakuzpnv77uoq4wkrt7vcuzy|Hanji 398]] supplies the later North Sea exile, refusal of [[LiLingWesternHan|李陵]]'s persuasion, mourning for [[HanWudi|汉武帝]], [[ChangHuiWesternHan|常惠]]'s rescue stratagem, and return under [[EmperorZhaoOfHan|汉昭帝刘弗陵]].

## Current Profile

The current evidence presents Su Wu less as a complete biographical subject than as a test of office identity under escalating coercion. Before exile, he has not joined or known about Zhang Sheng's private agreement, yet he recognizes that the chief envoy and entire mission will bear the consequences. His first response is to seek death before hostile treatment can dishonor the mission; after the chanyu moves from contemplated execution to coerced surrender, he stabs himself and later remains unmoved by Wei Lü's sword threat.

The later exile turns that immediate crisis response into durable identity. Su Wu is cut off from Chang'an, told that his family has suffered, placed under Xiongnu pressure, and made to face the possibility that no one will ever know whether he held out. He keeps the Han envoy staff, rejects surrender even when Li Ling's arguments are emotionally and politically realistic, and turns service to the ruler into an obligation he treats like filial duty.

The return branch complicates a simple glory story. Su Wu's rescue depends on Chang Hui's tactical proof-of-life story to the Han envoy, not on Xiongnu generosity alone. When he returns, he has survived nineteen years and receives honor, office, and reward, but he also leaves behind a Xiongnu wife and child. The episode therefore frames his loyalty as costly, embodied, and publicly honored only after long private suffering.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han chief envoy whose completed peace mission is compromised by his deputy's unauthorized plot support.
- Treats bodily survival after coerced humiliation as a possible betrayal of the mission and twice seeks death before yielding.
- Rejects collective guilt for Zhang Sheng's conduct while refusing Wei Lü's sword threat and surrender demand.
- Keeps the Han envoy staff through hunger, cold, and abandonment, making office identity physically visible.
- Refuses Li Ling's surrender argument despite family disaster, political danger under Han Wudi, and the apparent uselessness of unseen loyalty.
- Returns through Chang Hui's diplomatic ruse and Han pressure but carries private cost because his Xiongnu wife and child remain behind.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to two compact podcast sources. It now joins the mission crisis to the later exile but does not reconstruct Su Wu's complete chronology, every companion, all stages of coercion, later office-holding, the textual history of the wild-goose letter, or the historicity of every quoted line. Hanji 376-1 stops before North Sea exile, while Hanji 398 starts from the later detention and return branch; the synthesis connects them without treating either installment as a complete biography.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 376-1's mission-compromise origin, two suicide attempts, and refusal of guilt by association before the North Sea exile.
- Reframed the loyalty case as continuity between immediate defense of mission dignity and later endurance through ritual token, mourning, and refusal.
