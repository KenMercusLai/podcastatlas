## Overview

苏武 / Su Wu enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-398-ruguo-ni-shi-liling-hui-ti-xiongnu-quanjiang-suwu-ma-lrvkyakuzpnv77uoq4wkrt7vcuzy|Hanji 398]] as the Western Han envoy detained by [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] whose long exile becomes a concentrated loyalty case. The episode follows him at the North Sea, his refusal of [[LiLingWesternHan|李陵]]'s persuasion, his mourning for [[HanWudi|汉武帝]], [[ChangHuiWesternHan|常惠]]'s rescue stratagem, and his return under [[EmperorZhaoOfHan|汉昭帝刘弗陵]].

## Current Profile

The current evidence presents Su Wu less as a full biographical subject than as a test of identity under prolonged isolation. He is cut off from Chang'an, told that his family has suffered, placed under Xiongnu pressure, and made to face the possibility that no one will ever know whether he held out. His answer is to keep the Han envoy staff, reject surrender even when Li Ling's arguments are emotionally and politically realistic, and turn service to the ruler into an obligation he treats like filial duty.

The return branch complicates a simple glory story. Su Wu's rescue depends on Chang Hui's tactical proof-of-life story to the Han envoy, not on Xiongnu generosity alone. When he returns, he has survived nineteen years and receives honor, office, and reward, but he also leaves behind a Xiongnu wife and child. The episode therefore frames his loyalty as costly, embodied, and publicly honored only after long private suffering.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han envoy whose detention by Xiongnu becomes a canonical loyalty-under-exile case in this source.
- Keeps the Han envoy staff through hunger, cold, and abandonment, making office identity physically visible.
- Refuses Li Ling's surrender argument despite family disaster, political danger under Han Wudi, and the apparent uselessness of unseen loyalty.
- Treats ruler-service through a father-son analogy, framing surrender as identity rupture rather than only strategic choice.
- Mourns Han Wudi from exile, showing affective and ritual attachment to Han after years of detention.
- Returns through Chang Hui's diplomatic ruse and Han pressure, then receives Han Zhao's public honors.
- Carries private cost into return because his Xiongnu wife and child remain behind.

## Evidence

- Exile identity: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-398-ruguo-ni-shi-liling-hui-ti-xiongnu-quanjiang-suwu-ma-lrvkyakuzpnv77uoq4wkrt7vcuzy|Hanji 398]] says Su Wu is sent to the North Sea, suffers intermittent supplies, survives through desperate food sources, and continues holding the Han envoy staff until its yak-tail ornaments fall away.
- Refusal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-398-ruguo-ni-shi-liling-hui-ti-xiongnu-quanjiang-suwu-ma-lrvkyakuzpnv77uoq4wkrt7vcuzy|Hanji 398]] says Li Ling argues from hopeless return prospects, family disasters, and Han Wudi's late unpredictability, but Su Wu answers that service to ruler is like service to father and threatens death if surrender is pressed.
- Return and cost: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-398-ruguo-ni-shi-liling-hui-ti-xiongnu-quanjiang-suwu-ma-lrvkyakuzpnv77uoq4wkrt7vcuzy|Hanji 398]] says Chang Hui's wild-goose message story forces acknowledgement that Su Wu is alive, after which Su Wu returns to Chang'an in 81 BCE and leaves a Xiongnu wife and newborn child behind.

## Qualifications

This page is source-scoped to a podcast summary of Hanji 398. It does not reconstruct Su Wu's complete mission chronology, all companions, later office-holding, the textual history of the wild-goose letter, or the historicity of every quoted line. The current synthesis treats Su Wu as the episode presents him: a loyalty and identity figure under extreme exile pressure.
