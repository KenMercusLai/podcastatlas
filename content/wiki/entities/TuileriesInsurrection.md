# Tuileries Insurrection of 10 August 1792

## Overview

The Tuileries insurrection of 10 August 1792 was the armed Parisian confrontation that destroyed the effective constitutional monarchy, led to the massacre of the Swiss Guards, and caused the suspension and imprisonment of [[LouisXVI|Louis XVI]].

## Current Profile

The episode treats the insurrection as the culmination of a distributed mobilization rather than a single leader's plot. Paris sections, provincial fédérés, radical clubs, the insurrectionary Commune, and defecting National Guardsmen converged after war failure and the Brunswick Manifesto had made royal authority appear incompatible with national survival. Unclear first gunfire then became reciprocal battle and massacre, while the Assembly converted the palace's defeat into suspension of the king and a call for a National Convention.

## Key Characteristics

- It followed section-level demands to remove the king and the arrival of armed provincial fédérés.
- The insurrectionary Commune displaced the existing Paris municipal authority during the night of 9-10 August.
- The killing of the Tuileries commander and replacement of National Guard command weakened palace defence.
- Louis left for the Legislative Assembly after being advised that fighting was hopeless.
- Uncertain initial gunfire escalated into battle, palace destruction, and the killing of captured or fleeing Swiss Guards.
- The Assembly suspended Louis and called a new National Convention.

### Battle, massacre, and regime change

- [[506-the-french-revolution-massacre-at-the-palace-part-4-glt9954902163]] records uncertain first shots, combat with the Swiss Guards, killings after capture or flight, suspension of the king, and the call for a Convention.

## Qualifications

- The exact sequence and responsibility for the first shots remain unclear.
- “Massacre” describes the killing of captured and fleeing Swiss Guards as well as the broader revolutionary memory of the battle; it does not establish a single prearranged plan.
- Bottom-up section mobilization and coordination by prominent radicals can both be true.
- Crowd, defender, casualty, and weapon totals remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Added the 10 August insurrection as the missing institutional and violent break between wartime monarchical crisis and the later Republic.
