# Armed Forces Movement (Portugal)

## Overview

The Armed Forces Movement was the junior-officer organization that overthrew Portugal's [[EstadoNovoPortugal|Estado Novo]] in the [[CarnationRevolution]].

## Current Profile

The movement grew from the professional resentment of academy-trained officers into a coalition demanding withdrawal from Africa, elections, and abolition of the secret police. Its success depended on military coordination and rapid defections, but its ideological breadth also carried conflict into the [[PortugueseRevolutionaryProcess|post-coup revolutionary process]].

## Key Characteristics

- Originated among younger officers known as "the captains."
- Converted career and status grievances into opposition to dictatorship and colonial war.
- Used coded radio songs to coordinate the 24-25 April 1974 operation.
- Won quickly as most government forces joined or declined to resist.
- Contained conservative, democratic-socialist, and revolutionary tendencies rather than one stable ideology.

## Evidence

- Formation evidence: [[273-portugal-the-carnation-revolution-glt3297148398]] links the captains' organization to the 1973 promotion reform and repeated African service.
- Program evidence: [[273-portugal-the-carnation-revolution-glt3297148398]] gives withdrawal from Africa, elections, and abolition of the secret police as early movement demands.
- Coalition evidence: [[273-portugal-the-carnation-revolution-glt3297148398]] contrasts [[AntonioDeSpinola|Spinola]], [[OteloSaraivaDeCarvalho|Otelo]], and the later Group of Nine.

## Qualifications

The episode compresses the movement's internal organization and changing program. Its members should not be treated as ideologically uniform before or after the coup.

## What Changed

- Added the officer movement as the institutional bridge between colonial war, professional grievance, coup, and disputed transition.
