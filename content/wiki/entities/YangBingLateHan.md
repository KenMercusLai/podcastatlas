## Overview
Yang Bing was a late-Han minister and son of [[YangZhenLateHan|Yang Zhen]] whose current wiki role is the clean-office counterpoint to eunuch-dominated court politics. He is remembered here for refusing corrupt incentives, restricting personal desire, and recommending Liu Yu as a remonstrant voice after court feedback channels had weakened.

## Current Profile
Yang Bing's profile is built around disciplined public service rather than factional spectacle. Even after reaching high office late in life, he keeps compensation clean, refuses gifts, and cooperates with other senior officials to punish corrupt actors under Emperor Huan.

The episode's "three nots" formulation turns his integrity into a practical boundary system: no attachment to wine, sexual entanglement, or money. That boundary system is not merely private morality; it preserves the credibility needed for anti-corruption judgment and for recommending figures such as Liu Yu.

## Key Characteristics
- Late-Han senior minister whose reputation continues the clean-family style associated with Yang Zhen.
- Public official who treats clean pay and gift refusal as institutional safeguards.
- Anti-corruption actor working with Zhou Jing against corrupt officials despite eunuch ascendancy.
- Model of "three refusals" toward wine, sexual entanglement, and money.
- Recommender of Liu Yu, linking personal integrity to the restoration of court criticism.
