## Overview

Yang Bing was a late-Han minister and son of [[YangZhenLateHan|杨震]] whose current wiki role now begins in 151 CE as a Shangshu using disaster, ritual, hierarchy, palace defense, and seal security to criticize [[EmperorHuanOfHan|Emperor Huan]]'s private outing to Liang Yin's household. The same early layer backfills his classical family formation, reluctance to chase office before age forty, clean pay practice, gift refusal under poverty, and "three refusals" integrity before later sources show him punished, restored, and used in eunuch-linked office review, appointment restraint, and [[HouLanLateHan|侯览]] impeachment.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-836-xiongdi-fanmu-beihou-ruzhe-he-jianchen-de-jiaofeng-lq9wjn5c8mwt-onrtslum4fw53eq|Hanji 836]] supplies the earliest current Yang Bing layer. After Emperor Huan privately leaves the palace for the home of [[LiangJiLateHan|梁冀]]'s sixteen-year-old son Liang Yin, Yang Bing reads violent wind, daytime darkness, and uprooted trees as Heaven's warning. He argues that imperial movement should be reserved for major rites and constrained by hierarchy, palace defense, and jade-seal security. Huan does not take the warning seriously, but the episode makes Yang Bing an early carrier of [[OmenCodedRemonstrance|omen-coded remonstrance]] and [[ImperialRitualDistance|imperial ritual distance]] before the later eunuch and anti-corruption cases.

The same Hanji 836 layer gives the personal formation behind that public posture. Yang Bing is presented as Yang Zhen's son, trained in the Ouyang Shangshu and Jing-family Changes traditions, first teaching rather than seeking office, then entering official life after forty. The source also emphasizes clean living: he takes only enough salary for basic subsistence, refuses extra rewards and family bribery, remains unmoved by wine, sexual entanglement, and money, and closes his door to a former subordinate bringing a large cash gift when he is poor enough to eat every other day.

The next current Yang Bing layer is a failed Henan Yin enforcement move in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-851-hanhuandi-weihe-zhuding-yichou-wannian-ltgm1ix6cgqb-kqo9bztfrzjgeun|Hanji 851]]. He acts after [[FifthZhongLateHan|第五种]] and Wei Yu expose Shan Kuang's corruption and Shan Kuang hires [[RenFangLateHan|任芳]] against Wei Yu. Yang Bing asks that Shan Kuang be sent to Luoyang for confrontation, but the court punishes Yang Bing with left-workshop labor instead of confronting the protected offender.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-858-weishenme-quan-ni-yiding-yao-huanwei-sikao-lobr-xznqo-ctjpc-ytpstubmahl|Hanji 858]] later shows him restored as taiwei and paired with [[ZhouJingLateHan|周景]]. Together they turn anti-eunuch criticism into a concrete review of eunuch relatives, guests, and unqualified officeholders through named reporting channels, after which Yang Bing impeaches more than fifty officials.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-863-yi-ba-huo-shaochu-de-zhandouli-ljpfmj8pphunruioce64ywjk7yhb|Hanji 863]] shows Yang Bing applying the same office discipline to casual 郎 appointments during Emperor Huan's southern tour. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-865-zhichang-boss-ye-rongyi-bei-zhezhong-ren-beici-ljfhfevljqnx6zceakjfbw5gwfma|Hanji 865]] gives his strongest successful case: he uses [[HouCanLateHan|侯参]]'s corruption to pressure Hou Lan and defends Three Excellencies oversight of inner-court eunuchs through precedent. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-866-zhenzheng-huomingbai-de-ren-dongde-sanbuzhan-lhhmmyjfo6mkf-qs6bhhzu8hhln6|Hanji 866]] then makes the personal discipline behind that public role explicit through clean compensation, gift refusal, anti-corruption action, and restraint from wine, sexual entanglement, and money.

## Key Characteristics

- Disaster-remonstrance official who uses weather anomaly, ritual order, palace security, and seal control to criticize Emperor Huan's private outing.
- Yang Zhen's son and classics-trained official whose authority rests on family learning, teaching, delayed entry into office, and clean personal discipline.
- Model of clean compensation, gift refusal under scarcity, and "three refusals" toward wine, sexual entanglement, and money.
- Henan Yin punished after seeking formal confrontation in Shan Kuang's witness-suppression case.
- Taiwei who later converts anti-eunuch criticism into an accepted review of eunuch-linked and unqualified officeholders.
- Appointment-restraint voice who treats casual 郎 grants as a palace-security and local-governance risk.
- Impeachment actor and jurisdiction defender who turns Hou Can's corruption into wider pressure against Hou Lan's palace access.

## Qualifications

Yang Bing is disambiguated from [[YangBing|杨冰 / Yang Bing]], the modern Hupu and Dewu founder. Hanji 836 gives the earliest current source layer, but it does not show that Huan accepted the warning or that Yang Bing had durable leverage over imperial movement. The "three nots" appear as both a Hanji 836 biographical profile and a Hanji 866 clean-office concept; the overlap strengthens continuity rather than creating a separate person. Hanji 851 creates a later punishment layer, not a contradiction of subsequent restoration: the current synthesis treats the taiwei, southern-tour, and Hou Lan cases as restored or later roles after the Shan Kuang reversal. Hanji 858 proves a bounded cleanup order, not a permanent purge of eunuch-linked appointments. Hanji 865 remains an anti-Hou Lan success, but later source notes may still show Hou Lan or his network active.

## What Changed

- Hanji 836 moves Yang Bing's current profile back to 151 CE with the ignored disaster-and-ritual warning against Huan's private outing.
- Hanji 836 adds family learning, teaching before office, delayed service, clean pay, gift refusal under poverty, and an earlier "three nots" integrity layer.
- Hanji 851 remains the punishment layer for trying to confront Shan Kuang in Luoyang.
- The page now treats later Yang Bing successes as restorations after a prior enforcement reversal, not as evidence that he was always institutionally protected.
- Hanji 858, Hanji 863, Hanji 865, and Hanji 866 remain later layers of executable review, appointment restraint, jurisdictional impeachment, and clean-office discipline.
