## Overview
Yang Bing was a late-Han minister and son of [[YangZhenLateHan|杨震]] whose current wiki role is the clean-office counterpoint to eunuch-dominated court politics. He is remembered here for restraining casual 郎 appointments during [[EmperorHuanOfHan|汉桓帝]]'s southern tour, impeaching [[HouCanLateHan|侯参]] and [[HouLanLateHan|侯览]], defending senior-minister oversight of inner-court eunuchs, refusing corrupt incentives, restricting personal desire, and recommending Liu Yu as a remonstrant voice after court feedback channels had weakened.

## Current Profile
Yang Bing's profile is built around disciplined public service rather than factional spectacle. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-863-yi-ba-huo-shaochu-de-zhandouli-ljpfmj8pphunruioce64ywjk7yhb|Hanji 863]] adds an earlier Huan-era appointment-restraint layer. During Emperor Huan's southern tour, eunuchs and favorites use proximity to gain appointments for many people as 郎. Yang Bing warns that 郎 officials guard the palace and can later administer local populations, so imperial softness toward followers creates real public risk.

Hanji 865 gives Yang Bing's most forceful active case: he impeaches Hou Can for corruption as Yizhou inspector, then widens the accusation to Hou Lan's proximity to Emperor Huan. When the shangshu side questions whether the Three Excellencies can impeach palace eunuchs, Yang Bing's officials answer through classical reasoning and Han precedent, helping force Hou Lan's removal.

Hanji 866 then makes the personal discipline behind that public role explicit. Even after reaching high office late in life, Yang Bing keeps compensation clean, refuses gifts, and cooperates with other senior officials to punish corrupt actors under Emperor Huan. The episode's "three nots" formulation turns his integrity into a practical boundary system: no attachment to wine, sexual entanglement, or money. That boundary system is not merely private morality; it preserves the credibility needed for anti-corruption judgment and for recommending figures such as Liu Yu.

## Key Characteristics
- Late-Han senior minister whose reputation continues the clean-family style associated with Yang Zhen.
- Appointment-restraint voice who treats casual 郎 grants as a palace-security and local-governance risk.
- Impeachment actor who turns Hou Can's corruption into a wider attack on Hou Lan's palace access.
- Defender of senior-minister jurisdiction over inner-court eunuchs through precedent-backed argument.
- Public official who treats clean pay and gift refusal as institutional safeguards.
- Anti-corruption actor working with Zhou Jing against corrupt officials despite eunuch ascendancy.
- Model of "three refusals" toward wine, sexual entanglement, and money.

## Qualifications
Yang Bing is disambiguated from [[YangBing|杨冰 / Yang Bing]], the modern Hupu and Dewu founder. The current page only covers the late-Han official 杨秉 and should not absorb modern-business evidence. Hanji 863 adds an appointment-restraint notice but does not prove how long Huan's halt on casual 郎 appointments lasted. Hanji 865 is treated as an earlier anti-Hou Lan removal or demotion, not as a final settlement of Hou Lan's career, because later source notes still show Hou Lan or his network active.
