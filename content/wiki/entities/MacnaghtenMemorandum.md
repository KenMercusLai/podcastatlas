# Macnaghten Memorandum

## Overview

The Macnaghten Memorandum is an 1894 Scotland Yard document discussed in [[628-jack-the-ripper-the-killer-unmasked-part-5-glt3796910737]] as a retrospective list of three men whom Sir Melville Macnaghten considered more likely suspects in the [[JackTheRipper|Jack the Ripper]] murders than Thomas Cutbush.

## Current Profile

The document names Aaron Kosminski, Montague John Druitt, and Michael Ostrog, but its importance lies as much in its limit as in its names: Macnaghten stated that no proof could be attached to any one suspect. The episode treats it as evidence of police suspicion, not evidence that Scotland Yard possessed a secret definitive solution.

The source also tests the memorandum against surviving records. Kosminski's asylum file does not clearly support Macnaghten's violent characterization; Druitt's alleged insanity and family suspicion remain poorly evidenced; and Ostrog appears more like a con man and thief than a homicidal anatomist and was probably imprisoned in France during the murders.

## Key Characteristics

- It is a retrospective 1894 police memorandum rather than a contemporaneous conviction record.
- It names Aaron Kosminski, Montague John Druitt, and Michael Ostrog as comparatively plausible suspects.
- It expressly concedes that proof could not be attached to any named suspect.
- Its character descriptions and implications require checking against asylum, biographical, sporting, prison, and location records.
- It preserves institutional suspicion without resolving the Whitechapel murders.

## Evidence

### Named suspects and explicit evidentiary limit

- [[628-jack-the-ripper-the-killer-unmasked-part-5-glt3796910737]] reports the memorandum's three names and its statement that none could be proved responsible.

### Conflict with surviving records

- [[628-jack-the-ripper-the-killer-unmasked-part-5-glt3796910737]] contrasts Macnaghten's descriptions with Kosminski's largely passive asylum record, Druitt's weakly supported family-suspicion story and cricket commitments, and Ostrog's probable French imprisonment.

## Qualifications

The source is a podcast synthesis rather than a direct archival edition of the memorandum. Its account establishes the episode's interpretation of the document but does not independently settle manuscript variants, Macnaghten's information channels, the precise identity intended by “Kosminski,” or every suspect's chronology.
