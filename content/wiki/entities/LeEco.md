# LeEco / 乐视

## Overview
LeEco / 乐视 was a Chinese online-video company that expanded into televisions, smartphones, sports, film, mobility, overseas operations, and electric vehicles under [[JiaYueting|贾跃亭]]. It is both a real product-and-platform innovator and a major cautionary case about trying to finance many unproven businesses through one interconnected ecosystem.

## Current Profile
LeEco's early model had operating substance. Licensed-content accumulation, operator relationships, audiovisual permissions, copyright distribution, direct hardware sales, memberships, and advertising helped it build a video platform and large smart-TV business; its smartphones also reached substantial reported volume. The stronger “ecosystem” claim was harder to validate: vertical links among content, platform, terminal, and services were plausible, but sports, phones, cars, overseas launches, property projects, and peripheral ventures created funding needs far beyond internally generated cash.

The group therefore belongs in both innovation and governance history. Its direction of travel anticipated later practices in smart television, hardware-linked services, sports memberships, and electric vehicles, yet the company did not survive long enough to consolidate those advantages. Supplier arrears exposed hidden fragility, cross-guarantees and related entities transmitted stress, and later regulatory findings about Le.com's historical reporting made financial quality a central—not incidental—part of the failure.

## Key Characteristics
- Built meaningful businesses in licensed online video, smart televisions, smartphones, film, and sports media rather than existing only as a promotional story.
- Used low-margin hardware, memberships, content, advertising, and internet distribution as an early integrated consumer-media model.
- Extended a plausible vertical-integration thesis into seven simultaneous ecosystems whose horizontal synergies were not adequately proven.
- Depended on continuing external finance while multiple businesses lost cash and auto, property, and overseas ambitions raised capital intensity.
- Suffered system-wide collapse when supplier arrears broke confidence and cross-linked financing turned local shortfalls into group contagion.
- Became a market caution after regulators found years of financial misstatement and the listed company was ultimately delisted.

## Qualifications
- The source's company-level figures remain episode-scoped because LeEco used a complex entity structure and regulators later found historical disclosures unreliable.
- Later adoption of similar television, membership, service, sports, or EV models does not prove that LeEco itself could have executed them profitably.
- Liquidity stress explains the speed of collapse but does not reduce the case to an unlucky run: the underlying scope, cash-flow, accounting, and governance weaknesses preceded the confidence break.
