# The President's Cake / 《总统的蛋糕》

## Overview

*The President's Cake* / 《总统的蛋糕》 is an Iraqi film, set around 1995, in which a poor girl must obtain scarce ingredients and make a cake for Saddam Hussein's official birthday celebration.

## Current Profile

[[20-shiji-zhuanzhi-shi-dianying-xian-qingsuan-yi-bufen-gkwriaiosoyxcck-awttzvx8]] uses the film as the entry point to a larger discussion of authoritarian history and transitional justice. The child's task converts a national loyalty ritual into household scarcity, fear of informing, coerced compliance, and danger. The cake's eventual irrelevance beside an air-raid alarm makes the political ceremony absurd without making its costs unreal.

The film's political force is double rather than symmetrical: it criticizes Saddam Hussein's dictatorship and informer culture while showing that international sanctions and war costs fall heavily on ordinary people rather than rulers. The child's perspective keeps both pressures visible through daily necessity rather than geopolitical exposition.

## Key Characteristics

- Uses a child's constrained task to translate authoritarian ritual into everyday coercion.
- Connects political loyalty performance to poverty, scarcity, and informer culture.
- Criticizes dictatorship without hiding civilian harm from international sanctions.
- Ends by contrasting an unwanted ceremonial object with the immediate danger of war.
- Preserves the girl's agency and innocence while showing how adult political systems exploit both.
