# Mark Carney

## Overview
Mark Carney is represented in the wiki as Canada's prime minister in sources about U.S. tariff pressure, trade diversification, provincial constraints, and infrastructure approval speed.

## Current Profile
Carney enters the wiki through a renewed [[UnitedStates]] trade confrontation. The earlier source says he called off talks that had seemed close to agreement, promised to match [[DonaldTrump]]'s tariffs dollar for dollar, and then set a September 8 waiting period before matching American tariffs. His governing problem is [[TradeDiversificationStabilityParadox]]: Canada wants less U.S. dependence, but tariff war can reduce the stability needed to attract diversification investment.

The oil-sands episode extends that profile from retaliation to delivery. It says Canadians wanted greater energy independence from America because the trading relationship had become hard to predict, and that Carney opened [[MajorProjectsOfficeCanada]] to speed nationally important infrastructure projects through approvals. In the current synthesis, Carney is therefore a trade leader and an infrastructure-speed leader, with [[Alberta]]'s oil leverage making both roles politically constrained.

## Key Characteristics
- Canadian prime minister figure in the wiki's U.S.-Canada trade-war branch.
- Uses retaliation threats and timing as bargaining tools against Trump-era tariffs.
- Faces provincial constraints from Alberta's energy leverage and Quebec's tariff/language exposure.
- Wants diversification from the United States but needs enough stability to attract investment.
