# Mark Carney

## Overview
Mark Carney is represented as Canada’s prime minister confronting U.S. tariff pressure, trade dependence, infrastructure delay, and simultaneous separatist pressure in Alberta and Quebec.

## Current Profile
Carney first enters through renewed [[UnitedStates]] trade conflict: retaliation can defend domestic legitimacy, but prolonged uncertainty undermines the investment required for diversification. The oil-sands source extends that profile into project delivery through [[MajorProjectsOfficeCanada]]. The newest episode adds a wider supply-side program—tax reductions, an investment deduction, and the [[BuildingCanadaAct]]—alongside outreach to China, India, and the European Union.

His governing problem is now also federal cohesion. [[Alberta]]’s resource grievances and non-binding referendum differ from [[Quebec]]’s culturally grounded independence movement under the [[PartiQuebecois]], but both test whether economic competence and infrastructure delivery can contain [[CanadianSeparatism]] during an external trade shock.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses retaliation threats and timing as bargaining tools in U.S.-Canada tariff conflict.
- Pursues export diversification while still depending on access and stability in the American market.
- Uses tax and infrastructure-approval policy to encourage domestic investment.
- Faces conflicting provincial exposure, resource leverage, and independence pressures.
- Is presented as personally popular and competent in the newest source, without independent polling validation here.
