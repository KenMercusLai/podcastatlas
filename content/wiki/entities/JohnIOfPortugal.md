# John I of Portugal / 若昂一世

## Overview
John I of Portugal / 若昂一世 was the Aviz monarch whose English alliance, marriage to [[PhilippaOfLancaster|Philippa of Lancaster]], and 1415 conquest of [[Ceuta]] connected Portuguese dynastic consolidation to maritime expansion.

## Current Profile
The sources present John I's court as newly ambitious after Portugal's mainland Reconquista and the establishment of the Aviz dynasty. The Treaty of Windsor and Philippa marriage gave this monarchy an English diplomatic and family connection, while their “illustrious generation” included [[HenryTheNavigator|Henry the Navigator]]. The 1415 campaign required large-scale preparation, taxation, requisition, and secrecy; its result became a founding episode in later Portuguese overseas-expansion memory even though Ceuta disappointed commercially.

## Key Characteristics
- Aviz monarch attached to the 1415 conquest of Ceuta.
- Dynastic partner in the formalization of the Anglo-Portuguese alliance.
- Represents Portugal's shift from mainland consolidation toward North African and Atlantic horizons.
- Father of the “illustrious generation” whose sons joined the Ceuta campaign.

## Evidence
- Campaign evidence: [[496-yiminchao-beihou-de-xiuda-cong-gudian-shidai-manzu-duhai-yibiliya-shoufu-shidi-yundong-dao-putaoya-dahanghai-lu8pawnkiselmmrgubrey4pq-4fw]] describes Portuguese preparations, fiscal measures, and fleet movement before the attack.
- Victory evidence: [[496-yiminchao-beihou-de-xiuda-cong-gudian-shidai-manzu-duhai-yibiliya-shoufu-shidi-yundong-dao-putaoya-dahanghai-lu8pawnkiselmmrgubrey4pq-4fw]] says Portuguese forces captured Ceuta on 21 August 1415.
- Memory evidence: [[496-yiminchao-beihou-de-xiuda-cong-gudian-shidai-manzu-duhai-yibiliya-shoufu-shidi-yundong-dao-putaoya-dahanghai-lu8pawnkiselmmrgubrey4pq-4fw]] links the campaign to later Portuguese Age of Discovery narratives.
- Alliance evidence: [[227-portugal-on-the-edge-of-the-world-part-1-glt1834337589]] links João to the Treaty of Windsor, Philippa's marriage, and their dynastic legacy.
- Commercial qualification: [[227-portugal-on-the-edge-of-the-world-part-1-glt1834337589]] says Ceuta's trade shifted toward Tangier after conquest.

## Qualifications
The sources do not provide a full reign profile. They emphasize alliance and Ceuta, while the claim that 1415 inaugurated a coherent global project remains qualified and the commercial outcome of conquest was disappointing.

## What Changed
- Added the Treaty of Windsor, Philippa marriage, illustrious generation, and Ceuta's commercial disappointment.
