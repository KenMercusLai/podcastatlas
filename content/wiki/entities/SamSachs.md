## Overview
Sam Sachs is a senior fellow at [[NewAmerica|New America]] who appears in [[MarketplaceTech]] to discuss U.S.-China AI governance, semiconductor restrictions, and technical cooperation.

## Current Profile
In the bounded source, Sachs combines geopolitical realism with a narrow cooperation strategy. She does not expect a presidential bargain to halt frontier development; instead, she argues that expert dialogue and private technical coordination can reduce cross-border risks without requiring either country to abandon competition.

## Key Characteristics
- Analyzes Chinese technology regulation and its interaction with U.S.-China competition.
- Distinguishes genuine public-safety rules in China from censorship and party-control objectives.
- Treats chip export controls as both a constraint and an incentive for domestic Chinese substitution.
- Favors expert, academic, engineering, and private-sector channels when formal diplomacy cannot deliver broad agreement.

## Evidence
- Competition and restraint: [[tech-20260924-0924-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260924-0924-mp-tech-pod-128]] records Sachs explaining why shared AI risk does not remove fears of unilateral disadvantage.
- Regulation and innovation: [[tech-20260924-0924-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260924-0924-mp-tech-pod-128]] uses continued Chinese AI development to challenge a categorical opposition between safeguards and innovation.
- Practical cooperation: [[tech-20260924-0924-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260924-0924-mp-tech-pod-128]] cites track-two dialogue and U.S.-Tencent work on a WeChat vulnerability as narrower paths to common security.
