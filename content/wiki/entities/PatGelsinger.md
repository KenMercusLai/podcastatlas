# Pat Gelsinger

## Overview
Pat Gelsinger is presented in this wiki as the former [[Intel]] CEO interviewed by [[AllIn|All-In]] about Intel's strategic decline, U.S. semiconductor resilience, AI infrastructure, token economics, and the future mix of classical, AI, and quantum computing.

## Current Profile
The bounded source uses Gelsinger primarily as a technical executive interpreting Intel's history. His profile is not a general biography; it is a source-bounded role as a semiconductor operator who argues that technology companies lose position when financial leadership displaces technical judgment, when long-cycle manufacturing bets are delayed, and when platform shifts such as [[CUDA]], [[TSMC]]'s foundry model, and [[Apple]]'s full-stack silicon strategy are underestimated.

## Key Characteristics
- Diagnoses Intel's decline through technical leadership, capital allocation, manufacturing underinvestment, and missed platform shifts.
- Treats U.S. semiconductor capacity and the [[CHIPSAct|CHIPS Act]] as industrial-resilience issues rather than only company-specific subsidies.
- Interprets the AI buildout through power availability, token cost, energy per token, and Jevons-style demand expansion.
- Presents quantum computing as part of a longer computing stack alongside classical and AI computing, while keeping scale-up as the unresolved engineering challenge.

## Qualifications
This page is based on a friendly podcast interview and should not be read as independent verification of Gelsinger's figures or as a full career biography. The Intel account is also partly retrospective and may understate alternative explanations such as market timing, execution quality, competitive capability, board incentives, customer demand, and macro capital cycles.
