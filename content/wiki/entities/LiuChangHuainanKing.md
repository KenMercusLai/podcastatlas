## Overview

刘长 / Liu Chang is the early Western Han Huainan king first recorded as the Liu-family replacement for rebel king [[YingBu|英布 / 黥布]] and later remembered in Hanji 304-2 as the father of [[LiuAn|刘安]] and [[LiuCiHengshanKingWesternHan|刘赐]], whose own failed rebellion becomes part of the sons' political background. This page is distinct from [[LiuChang|刘畅 / Liu Chang]], the modern podcast speaker.

## Current Profile

The two bounded sources show an institutional reversal across generations. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-187-yingbu-panluan-liubang-weihe-tuobingti-qinzheng-lvjafotlnvmyqmwaeav3novz1dox|Hanji 187]] says [[LiuBang|刘邦]] abolishes Ying Bu's title and makes Liu Chang king of [[HuainanKingdomEarlyHan|淮南国]], extending [[SameSurnameKingEnfeoffment|同姓王分封]] as a trust-based replacement strategy. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-304-2-yin-tiaocao-yinfa-de-zhuhouwang-moufan-lnav5gprm-mmsbbugeflghixplf2|Hanji 304-2]] then briefly says Liu Chang himself dies after a failed rebellion and makes that precedent part of the background to Liu An's and Liu Ci's later anti-court preparations.

## Key Characteristics

- Liu-family replacement appointed to Huainan after Ying Bu's rebellion.
- Same-surname king whose title illustrates the court's attempt to replace distrusted non-kin territorial power with royal kin.
- Later failed rebel in the compressed retrospective supplied by Hanji 304-2.
- Father whose sons Liu An and Liu Ci are both presented as later royal conspirators.

## Evidence

Appointment and institutional role:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-187-yingbu-panluan-liubang-weihe-tuobingti-qinzheng-lvjafotlnvmyqmwaeav3novz1dox|Hanji 187]] says Liu Bang cancels Ying Bu's title and appoints Liu Chang as Huainan king before personally campaigning.

Later rebellion memory:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-304-2-yin-tiaocao-yinfa-de-zhuhouwang-moufan-lnav5gprm-mmsbbugeflghixplf2|Hanji 304-2]] identifies Liu Chang as Liu Bang's son, says he dies after rebellion fails, and frames Liu An and Liu Ci as sons who later repeat conflict with the center.

## Qualifications

Neither source gives a full biography. Hanji 187 records only Liu Chang's appointment, while Hanji 304-2 compresses his rebellion and death into family background without reconstructing charges, procedure, chronology, or causation. The episode's implied family pattern does not establish inherited psychology or make the sons' later choices inevitable.
