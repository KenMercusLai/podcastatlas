## Overview

刘长 / Liu Chang is the early Western Han Huainan king first recorded as the Liu-family replacement for rebel king [[YingBu|英布 / 黥布]] and later remembered as a royal ruler in conflict with central law whose failed rebellion and death become grievance material around sons [[LiuAn|刘安]] and [[LiuCiHengshanKingWesternHan|刘赐]]. This page is distinct from [[LiuChang|刘畅 / Liu Chang]], the modern podcast speaker.

## Current Profile

The bounded sources show an institutional reversal across generations. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-187-yingbu-panluan-liubang-weihe-tuobingti-qinzheng-lvjafotlnvmyqmwaeav3novz1dox|Hanji 187]] says [[LiuBang|刘邦]] abolishes Ying Bu's title and makes Liu Chang king of [[HuainanKingdomEarlyHan|淮南国]], extending [[SameSurnameKingEnfeoffment|同姓王分封]] as a trust-based replacement strategy. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-294-2-yi-chang-bijian-yinbao-de-jingtian-daan-lpqd6by5nn9k8p28m30yrzxanxql|Hanji 294-2]] says Liu Chang later governs with rules outside Han law and that people around Liu An repeatedly invoke his death as an injustice, while [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-304-2-yin-tiaocao-yinfa-de-zhuhouwang-moufan-lnav5gprm-mmsbbugeflghixplf2|Hanji 304-2]] compresses his death as the outcome of a failed rebellion. The joined evidence makes his memory part of the sons' political background without making their choices hereditary or inevitable.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-220-minjian-geyao-fengci-wendi-jia-renyi-liuheng-fanying-guguai-1-liuajl1w1byd6m_hjy0obirtmlu8|Hanji 220]] fills in the political afterlife of the death without resolving its medical or legal history. A popular song presents [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]] as a brother unable to tolerate his own kin. Liu Heng then grants marquis titles to Liu Chang's four sons, while [[JiaYi|贾谊]] argues that the act can validate the wrongful-death narrative and give the sons resources for revenge. The source alternately describes Liu Chang as starving himself and reports Jia Yi's defense that exile spared his life and illness caused the death; both remain competing narratives.

## Key Characteristics

- Liu-family replacement appointed to Huainan after Ying Bu's rebellion.
- Same-surname king whose title illustrates the court's attempt to replace distrusted non-kin territorial power with royal kin.
- Royal ruler described source-scopedly as rejecting Han law in favor of his own rules.
- Later failed rebel in the compressed retrospective supplied by Hanji 304-2.
- Father whose death becomes an anti-court grievance around Liu An and whose sons Liu An and Liu Ci are both presented as later royal conspirators.
- Dead prince whose treatment becomes public song, reputational pressure on Liu Heng, and the setting for grants to four sons.

## Evidence

Appointment and institutional role:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-187-yingbu-panluan-liubang-weihe-tuobingti-qinzheng-lvjafotlnvmyqmwaeav3novz1dox|Hanji 187]] says Liu Bang cancels Ying Bu's title and appoints Liu Chang as Huainan king before personally campaigning.

Later rebellion memory:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-294-2-yi-chang-bijian-yinbao-de-jingtian-daan-lpqd6by5nn9k8p28m30yrzxanxql|Hanji 294-2]] says Liu Chang rejects Han law in his kingdom and that his death is repeatedly invoked around Liu An as an injustice.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-304-2-yin-tiaocao-yinfa-de-zhuhouwang-moufan-lnav5gprm-mmsbbugeflghixplf2|Hanji 304-2]] identifies Liu Chang as Liu Bang's son, says he dies after rebellion fails, and frames Liu An and Liu Ci as sons who later repeat conflict with the center.

Death narrative and public afterlife:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-220-minjian-geyao-fengci-wendi-jia-renyi-liuheng-fanying-guguai-1-liuajl1w1byd6m_hjy0obirtmlu8|Hanji 220]] records popular song framing the death as failed brotherly tolerance and says Liu Heng subsequently grants titles to all four sons.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-220-minjian-geyao-fengci-wendi-jia-renyi-liuheng-fanying-guguai-1-liuajl1w1byd6m_hjy0obirtmlu8|Hanji 220]] preserves both the program's self-starvation account and Jia Yi's illness-after-commuted-exile defense.

## Qualifications

None of the sources gives a full biography. Hanji 187 records only Liu Chang's appointment, while Hanji 294-2 and Hanji 304-2 compress his autonomous rule, rebellion, and death into family background without reconstructing charges, procedure, chronology, or causation. Hanji 220 adds exile, public song, competing death explanations, and the sons' grants, but does not resolve the underlying legal record or Liu Heng's motive. Hanji 294-2's claim that Liu Chang was enfeoffed under Emperor Wen conflicts with Hanji 187's explicit initial appointment under Liu Bang; the latter remains canonical. The implied family pattern and later rebellions do not establish inherited psychology or make the grants a sufficient cause.

## What Changed

- Added the popular-song response, grants to four sons, and competing starvation-versus-illness accounts.
- Reframed the death as both a legal-political event and a contested public memory without resolving causation.

## Relationships

- [[LiuBang|刘邦]] - father and grantor who installs Liu Chang after Ying Bu's rebellion.
- [[YingBu|英布 / 黥布]] - different-surname king whose removal creates Liu Chang's Huainan title.
- [[HuainanKingdomEarlyHan|淮南国]] - kingdom assigned to Liu Chang.
- [[LiuAn|淮南王刘安]] - son later associated with an exposed rebellion plan.
- [[LiuCiHengshanKingWesternHan|衡山王刘赐]] - son whose anti-court posture is developed in Hanji 304-2.
- [[SameSurnameKingEnfeoffment|同姓王分封]] - institutional strategy illustrated by Liu Chang's appointment and qualified by his later rebellion memory.
- [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]] - brother whose punishment, exile settlement, and later grants to the sons define the source's legitimacy conflict.
- [[JiaYi|贾谊]] - adviser who defends the original commutation yet warns that later grants validate the hostile narrative.
- [[ReputationalRepairSecurityTradeoff|声誉修复与安全风险权衡]] - concept linking the sons' compensation to unresolved grievance and future capacity.
