## Overview

灌夫 / Guan Fu is a Western Han military and court figure whose shared displacement strengthens his alliance with [[DouYingWesternHan|窦婴]]. His prior hostility toward [[TianFenWesternHan|田蚡]] and acknowledged tendency to offend while drunk then converge at Tian Fen's wedding, triggering a larger conflict over punishment, local power, guest networks, and court security that also destroys his defender.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-263-weile-shengguan-ren-keyi-duo-beiwei-2-lvwimzpphquwycrfu-2r6z_r3mqu|Hanji 263 part 2]] supplies Guan Fu's entry into the conflict. The episode presents him as a militarily promoted but repeatedly demoted official who bonds with the excluded Dou Ying and offers to reconnect him with powerful people. When Tian Fen forgets a promised visit, Guan Fu treats the wasted overnight preparations as an insult; he later drinks, invites Tian Fen to dance, and attacks him verbally after refusal. Guan Fu also denounces Tian Fen's land request and thereby turns loyalty to Dou into direct hostility toward the chancellor.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-264-tianfen-hunyan-xianchang-ruhe-chengle-guanfu-de-duantoutai-lux-kqkfsqpjldtocrk65j5pvl8p|Hanji 264]] carries that hostility to the wedding. Guan Fu recognizes that his earlier drunken offenses and the resulting grievance make attendance dangerous and initially refuses to go, but Dou Ying presses him. Unequal guest deference toward Tian Fen and Dou Ying sharpens Guan Fu's resentment; successive toasts become insinuation and public abuse, and he refuses to apologize after Tian Fen orders his detention.

Tian Fen then treats the banquet's dowager-backed attendance as the basis for a grave-disrespect accusation and orders investigation of Guan Fu's earlier conduct and relatives. Drinking is therefore an immediate disinhibitor, not a complete cause: prior loyalty, anger, rank reversal, ritual comparison, and an empowered opponent determine why the outburst becomes lethal.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-265-dongchaotingbian-ruhe-yanbian-cheng-jieduandazhan-1-lil81vjxn5mait2wapdx_t4wy5hr|Hanji 265 part 1]] supplies the competing profiles later used at court. Dou Ying treats Guan Fu as a drunken offender whose immediate conduct does not warrant appended capital accusations. [[HanAnguoWesternHan|韩安国]] cites the death of Guan Fu's father in state service and Guan Fu's own early entry into battle and multiple wounds, but also acknowledges allegations that Guan Fu gathered dangerous associates, oppressed local people, accumulated great wealth, dominated Yingchuan, and intruded on royal interests. The source does not adjudicate those allegations.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-265-dongchaotingbian-ruhe-yanbian-cheng-jieduandazhan-2-lvu23osgv46nn3rbgque1mbhmpgw|Hanji 265 part 2]] treats his insult of Tian Fen at a politically sensitive banquet as an offense that [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] could have regarded as limited, but which becomes lethal once Dou Ying's rescue attempt enters a court struggle between declining Dou-family influence and Wang-family protection.

The episode says Guan Fu receives clan extermination. His role is therefore less a standalone legal case in this source than the initial node in a chain linking public insult, factional defense, maternal pressure, and unequal access to sovereign protection.

## Key Characteristics

- Ally of Dou Ying formed through shared displacement, attempted elite-network rebuilding, the land dispute, and resentment of Dou's public status loss.
- Foresees the danger of attending Tian Fen's wedding because of prior drunken offenses but yields to Dou Ying's request.
- Turns ritual and status resentment into public insult during successive toasts and refuses a coerced apology.
- Possesses a military-merit defense based on family service, early combat, wounds, and courage.
- Faces unresolved accusations of coercive associates, local predation, wealth accumulation, and infringement on royal interests.
- Serves as the immediate object of Dou Ying's unsuccessful defense.
- Receives collective punishment extending beyond the individual.

## Qualifications

The four supplied sources do not establish Guan Fu's broader career, the exact causes and sequence of his demotions, the wording and legal classification of his conduct, whether the adverse local allegations were proven, the membership covered by the reported clan punishment, or the complete earlier causes of his conflict with Tian Fen. The networking offer, failed visit, land dispute, guest gestures, motives, intoxication sequence, arrest procedure, and investigation remain episode-attributed. Military merit does not by itself exonerate later conduct, drinking does not erase agency, and the claim that the insult alone was a small matter is an argument inside the episode rather than a legal finding.

## What Changed

- Added Guan Fu's prior warning that drunken offense and existing hostility made the wedding dangerous.
- Recast the banquet as a chain joining loyalty, status comparison, intoxication, refusal to apologize, and prosecutorial expansion rather than a single stray remark.
- Added shared displacement, attempted network rebuilding, the failed visit, and the land dispute as the alliance and grievance layer before the wedding.
