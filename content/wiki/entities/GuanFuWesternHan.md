## Overview

灌夫 / Guan Fu is a Western Han military and court figure whose drunken denunciation of [[TianFenWesternHan|田蚡]] becomes the trigger for a larger conflict over punishment, local power, guest networks, and court security that also destroys his defender [[DouYingWesternHan|窦婴]].

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-265-dongchaotingbian-ruhe-yanbian-cheng-jieduandazhan-1-lil81vjxn5mait2wapdx_t4wy5hr|Hanji 265 part 1]] supplies the competing profiles used at court. Dou Ying treats Guan Fu as a drunken offender whose immediate conduct does not warrant appended capital accusations. [[HanAnguoWesternHan|韩安国]] cites the death of Guan Fu's father in state service and Guan Fu's own early entry into battle and multiple wounds, but also acknowledges allegations that Guan Fu gathered dangerous associates, oppressed local people, accumulated great wealth, dominated Yingchuan, and intruded on royal interests. The source does not adjudicate those allegations.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-265-dongchaotingbian-ruhe-yanbian-cheng-jieduandazhan-2-lvu23osgv46nn3rbgque1mbhmpgw|Hanji 265 part 2]] treats his insult of Tian Fen at a politically sensitive banquet as an offense that [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] could have regarded as limited, but which becomes lethal once Dou Ying's rescue attempt enters a court struggle between declining Dou-family influence and Wang-family protection.

The episode says Guan Fu receives clan extermination. His role is therefore less a standalone legal case in this source than the initial node in a chain linking public insult, factional defense, maternal pressure, and unequal access to sovereign protection.

## Key Characteristics

- Ally of Dou Ying in the episode's court-conflict sequence.
- Publicly attacks or insults Tian Fen at a politically sensitive banquet.
- Possesses a military-merit defense based on family service, early combat, wounds, and courage.
- Faces unresolved accusations of coercive associates, local predation, wealth accumulation, and infringement on royal interests.
- Serves as the immediate object of Dou Ying's unsuccessful defense.
- Receives collective punishment extending beyond the individual.

## Evidence

- Banquet conflict: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-265-dongchaotingbian-ruhe-yanbian-cheng-jieduandazhan-2-lvu23osgv46nn3rbgque1mbhmpgw|Hanji 265]] identifies Guan Fu's attack on Tian Fen as the opening offense in the closing phase of the case.
- Merit and proportionality: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-265-dongchaotingbian-ruhe-yanbian-cheng-jieduandazhan-1-lil81vjxn5mait2wapdx_t4wy5hr|Hanji 265 part 1]] says Han Anguo uses Guan Fu's family sacrifice, battlefield wounds, and courage to argue against turning drunken speech alone into death.
- Adverse allegations: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-265-dongchaotingbian-ruhe-yanbian-cheng-jieduandazhan-1-lil81vjxn5mait2wapdx_t4wy5hr|Hanji 265 part 1]] also records claims of violent association, local oppression, wealth, and intrusion on royal interests.
- Alliance: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-265-dongchaotingbian-ruhe-yanbian-cheng-jieduandazhan-2-lvu23osgv46nn3rbgque1mbhmpgw|Hanji 265]] says Dou Ying risks his own position in an effort to save Guan Fu.
- Punishment: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-265-dongchaotingbian-ruhe-yanbian-cheng-jieduandazhan-2-lvu23osgv46nn3rbgque1mbhmpgw|Hanji 265]] reports that Han Wudi orders Guan Fu's clan exterminated.

## Qualifications

The two supplied sources do not establish Guan Fu's broader career, the exact wording and legal classification of his conduct, whether the adverse local allegations were proven, the membership covered by the reported clan punishment, or the complete earlier causes of his conflict with Tian Fen. Military merit does not by itself exonerate later conduct, and the claim that the insult alone was a small matter is an argument inside the episode rather than a legal finding.

## What Changed

- Added the court's competing account of Guan Fu: military merit and proportionality on one side, unresolved local-power allegations on the other.
