## Overview

灌夫 / Guan Fu is a Western Han military and court figure whose loyalty to [[DouYingWesternHan|窦婴]], prior hostility toward [[TianFenWesternHan|田蚡]], and acknowledged tendency to offend while drunk converge at Tian Fen's wedding, triggering a larger conflict over punishment, local power, guest networks, and court security that also destroys his defender.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-264-tianfen-hunyan-xianchang-ruhe-chengle-guanfu-de-duantoutai-lux-kqkfsqpjldtocrk65j5pvl8p|Hanji 264]] supplies the immediate prehistory. Guan Fu attacks Tian Fen's land request on Dou Ying's behalf, recognizes that his earlier drunken offenses and the resulting grievance make the wedding dangerous, and initially refuses to attend. Dou Ying nevertheless presses him to go. At the banquet, unequal guest deference toward Tian Fen and Dou Ying sharpens Guan Fu's resentment; successive toasts become insinuation and public abuse, and he refuses to apologize after Tian Fen orders his detention.

Tian Fen then treats the banquet's dowager-backed attendance as the basis for a grave-disrespect accusation and orders investigation of Guan Fu's earlier conduct and relatives. Drinking is therefore an immediate disinhibitor, not a complete cause: prior loyalty, anger, rank reversal, ritual comparison, and an empowered opponent determine why the outburst becomes lethal.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-265-dongchaotingbian-ruhe-yanbian-cheng-jieduandazhan-1-lil81vjxn5mait2wapdx_t4wy5hr|Hanji 265 part 1]] supplies the competing profiles later used at court. Dou Ying treats Guan Fu as a drunken offender whose immediate conduct does not warrant appended capital accusations. [[HanAnguoWesternHan|韩安国]] cites the death of Guan Fu's father in state service and Guan Fu's own early entry into battle and multiple wounds, but also acknowledges allegations that Guan Fu gathered dangerous associates, oppressed local people, accumulated great wealth, dominated Yingchuan, and intruded on royal interests. The source does not adjudicate those allegations.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-265-dongchaotingbian-ruhe-yanbian-cheng-jieduandazhan-2-lvu23osgv46nn3rbgque1mbhmpgw|Hanji 265 part 2]] treats his insult of Tian Fen at a politically sensitive banquet as an offense that [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] could have regarded as limited, but which becomes lethal once Dou Ying's rescue attempt enters a court struggle between declining Dou-family influence and Wang-family protection.

The episode says Guan Fu receives clan extermination. His role is therefore less a standalone legal case in this source than the initial node in a chain linking public insult, factional defense, maternal pressure, and unequal access to sovereign protection.

## Key Characteristics

- Ally of Dou Ying who intervenes angrily in the earlier land dispute and later resents Dou Ying's public loss of status.
- Foresees the danger of attending Tian Fen's wedding because of prior drunken offenses but yields to Dou Ying's request.
- Turns ritual and status resentment into public insult during successive toasts and refuses a coerced apology.
- Possesses a military-merit defense based on family service, early combat, wounds, and courage.
- Faces unresolved accusations of coercive associates, local predation, wealth accumulation, and infringement on royal interests.
- Serves as the immediate object of Dou Ying's unsuccessful defense.
- Receives collective punishment extending beyond the individual.

## Qualifications

The three supplied sources do not establish Guan Fu's broader career, the exact wording and legal classification of his conduct, whether the adverse local allegations were proven, the membership covered by the reported clan punishment, or the complete earlier causes of his conflict with Tian Fen. Hanji 264's land dispute, guest gestures, motives, intoxication sequence, arrest procedure, and investigation remain episode-attributed. Military merit does not by itself exonerate later conduct, drinking does not erase agency, and the claim that the insult alone was a small matter is an argument inside the episode rather than a legal finding.

## What Changed

- Added Guan Fu's prior warning that drunken offense and existing hostility made the wedding dangerous.
- Recast the banquet as a chain joining loyalty, status comparison, intoxication, refusal to apologize, and prosecutorial expansion rather than a single stray remark.
