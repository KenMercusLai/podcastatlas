# Ben Naddaff-Hafrey

## Overview

Ben Naddaff-Hafrey is a reporter, producer, and musician whose work for [[RevisionistHistory]] ranges from Staten Island's secession politics and Revolutionary-era Loyalism through film-production oral history, experimental food reporting, emergency siren overuse, and autobiographical songwriting.

## Current Profile

The trailer identifies Naddaff-Hafrey as the host of *The Staten Island Problem* and presents the project as his deep investigation of a largely forgotten four-year secession battle. Its broad claim is interpretive: the local breakdown of trust anticipated forces dividing the United States, including an outer-borough political trajectory associated with [[RudyGiuliani]] and [[DonaldTrump]].

In the crossover source, Naddaff-Hafrey explains that the historical material grew out of reporting for *The Staten Island Problem*, a series about the borough's attempt to leave New York City. A section on opposition to the [[AmericanRevolution|American Revolution]] was cut from that project and became the basis for a longer discussion with [[TheDollop]].

His contribution is both archival and interpretive: he credits historian Phil Pappas, preserves uncertainty around releases and the peace conference, and argues that commerce and fear of disruption made early Loyalism intelligible before occupation produced the losses residents had hoped rebellion would avoid.

In the *Christmas in Connecticut* episode, Naddaff-Hafrey moves from political history to production history. His long attachment to the 1945 original creates the mystery, while interviews with writers, producers, executives, and crew reconstruct how [[ArnoldSchwarzenegger]] reshaped [[StanBrooks]]'s modest remake. He does not defend the finished film as good. Instead, he treats its strangeness, commitment, friendships, and visible pleasure of production as evidence that making can retain value despite artistic failure.

In [[the-formula-the-formula|The Formula]], he turns a legal and branding claim into a practical experiment. After treating [[BimboBakeries]]' suit against [[ChrisBotticella]] as a reason to test the mystique around [[ThomasEnglishMuffins|Thomas' English muffins]], he recruits [[RachelWyman]] and follows iteration, blind testing, and a comparison with [[Chartreuse]]. The result preserves failure as evidence: the team matches the nooks-and-crannies structure but not perceptual identity, then discovers that testers prefer Wyman's sourdough alternative.

In [[running-hot|Running Hot]], a personal noise complaint becomes a systems investigation. Naddaff-Hafrey counts sirens near his Brooklyn apartment, consults [[ArlineBronzaft]] on environmental-noise harms, then follows [[JonathanBayer]] and [[JeffJarvisEMS|Jeff Jarvis]] from crash and response-time evidence into dispatch reform. The reporting abandons an emotionally convenient mascot when the howling dog appears to enjoy sirens, while preserving the stronger evidence-led case for selective use.

In [[malcolm-breaks-down-the-perfect-break-up-song-malcolm-breaks-down-the-perfect-break-up-song|Malcolm Breaks Down the Perfect Break Up Song]], he appears as a songwriter rather than a reporter. “If I Didn't Know You By Now” grows from gratitude toward Julia, concrete changes to his Brooklyn room and routines, repeated exchange with bandmate Adam, and a darker final verse. The delayed lyric-sheet admission also shows how a song can be shared before its autobiographical origin is openly acknowledged.

## Key Characteristics

- Reporter linking local political identity to deeper historical structure.
- Host and investigative reporter for Revisionist History's Staten Island series.
- Preserves uncertainty, conflicting memory, and failed tests rather than smoothing them into a cleaner story.
- Uses practical experiments to pressure-test legal and cultural mythology.
- Turns a neighborhood complaint into a risk, triage, and institutional-history investigation while discarding a weak emotional frame.
- Writes autobiographically through domestic detail, gratitude, collaboration, and delayed disclosure.
