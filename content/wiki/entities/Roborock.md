# Roborock / 石头科技

Roborock / 石头科技 appears in [[vol-127-nianbaoji-zhong-de-zhenshi-zhongguo-2025-lqjy2pnfy09zyewj0ljxjuc3gxqv]] as a smart-appliance example of [[TechnologyInnovationAsScaleEconomy]]. The source cites Roborock's annual-report framing of intelligent cleaning appliances as a technology-intensive field involving AI, optics, electromechanics, acoustics, mechanics, materials, and fluid dynamics.

The episode uses Roborock to explain why newer Chinese consumer-hardware expansion is not only low-cost category copying. Algorithmic navigation, lidar, vision obstacle avoidance, mechanical integration, and reusable technology platforms can let a company expand from robot vacuums into floor washers, washer-dryers, and other smart appliances.

[[142-riben-xiaofeizhe-weihe-ruci-chimi-jijie-xianding-chuantai-kuahai-dianbo-lgbjiahlutyyf4paylnyxy7hv0iq]] adds a cautionary after-sales example. [[DavidWeng|大卫翁]] uses a Roborock support experience to explain why [[JapanMarketEntryTrust]] may require more visible escalation paths than form-and-email support alone, especially for Japanese consumers who expect someone to be reachable when FAQ material fails.

## Source Position
- Roborock is used to show that algorithmic and engineering capability can become a product moat.
- The source treats smart cleaning as a multi-discipline systems problem rather than a simple appliance category.
- Category expansion is framed as technology-platform reuse, not just channel extension.
- Roborock sits next to [[MideaGroup]] and [[AnkerInnovations]] in the source's hardware-technology branch.
- The Japan-market source treats Roborock's support example as a warning that technical product value can be weakened if after-sales trust does not match local expectations.
