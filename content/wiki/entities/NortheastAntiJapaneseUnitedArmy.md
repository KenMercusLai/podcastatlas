# Northeast Anti-Japanese United Army / 东北抗日联军

## Overview

The Northeast Anti-Japanese United Army was an armed resistance movement against Japanese occupation and puppet-state rule in Northeast China, represented in the bounded source through [[YuTianfang]]'s clandestine work, mobile unit, capture, and escape.

## Current Profile

[[lsehilqpxbu9hutqm87jyfejx6wy]] presents the movement through a biographical rather than comprehensive institutional lens. Yu combined teaching cover, underground organization, and armed leadership; the small unit he led from 1942 to 1944 is described as the only Anti-Japanese United Army force then remaining inside Heilongjiang. His arrest through betrayal shows the movement's exposure to surveillance and local informants.

The prison and escape narrative extends resistance beyond battlefield combat. Knowledge of terrain, covert identity confirmation, a shared escape plan, and help from rural civilians allowed resistance to persist within and beyond confinement. Zhao Zhongliang's death and Yu's survival also show how the same networked struggle produced sharply unequal individual outcomes.

## Key Characteristics

- Combined armed struggle with clandestine political and educational work.
- Operated under Japanese occupation and puppet-state policing in Northeast China.
- Relied on small mobile units, local terrain knowledge, secrecy, and civilian relationships.
- Remained vulnerable to betrayal, specialized interrogation, torture, and informant systems.
- Extended organizational identity into prison through recognition and cooperation among captured fighters.

## Evidence

- Organization and field conditions: [[lsehilqpxbu9hutqm87jyfejx6wy]] describes Yu's underground activity and leadership of a small force in Heilongjiang.
- Repression: [[lsehilqpxbu9hutqm87jyfejx6wy]] connects betrayal, arrest, inducement, torture, and specialized detention to the occupation system.
- Prison continuity and civilian support: [[lsehilqpxbu9hutqm87jyfejx6wy]] describes Yu and Zhao Zhongliang identifying each other, coordinating escape, and receiving uneven public protection afterward.

## Qualifications

The source centers one fighter's memoir and does not supply a full account of the movement's command, ethnic and political composition, Soviet connections, regional campaigns, civilian relations, or changing names and structures. The claim that Yu's unit was the only force remaining within Heilongjiang in 1942–1944 is retained as source-scoped, as are details of betrayal, interrogation, escape, local commemoration, and helpers' motives.

## What Changed

- Added the movement through Yu Tianfang's linked clandestine, armed, prison, and escape experience.
- Treated confinement as a continuation of resistance organization rather than its automatic end.
- Made civilian assistance and local discretion visible alongside armed action.
