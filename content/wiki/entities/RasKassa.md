# Ras Kassa

## Overview

Ras Kassa enters the wiki through [[706-queen-victorias-revenge-storming-the-citadel-part-2-glt3285474568]] as the ruler of Tigray and a rival of [[TeodrosII]] whose territory contained the road the [[AbyssinianExpedition1868|British expedition]] needed to reach [[Magdala]].

## Current Profile

The episode uses Kassa to show how the expedition's survival depended on local politics rather than on firepower alone. At the end of February 1868 [[RobertNapier|Napier]]'s column entered Tigray and stopped to deal with him directly: full military honours, gifts including an Arabian horse, a rifle and glassware, an assurance that British troops would not loot or steal, payment for every sack of grain, and a promise of the friendship of [[QueenVictoria]]. Kassa gave Napier a lion-skin cloak and agreed to send weekly supplies, which he did.

The source reads the exchange as an unwritten bargain. Kassa supplied safe passage and food for an army moving toward his own rival, and in return expected the British to deal with Teodros once and for all. The arrangement therefore makes Kassa an early example inside this branch of the local ruler who chooses a foreign force as the instrument of a regional rivalry.

## Key Characteristics

- He controls the ground between the coast and Magdala, which makes him a gatekeeper rather than a bystander.
- His relationship with the expedition is transactional and reciprocal: passage and food against payment, respect and a promise of protection.
- He is a rival of [[TeodrosII]], which aligns his interest with the expedition's objective.
- The British approach to him is deliberately ceremonial and legalistic, with honours, gifts and a no-looting pledge rather than requisition.
- The source presents him as a reliable supplier once the bargain is made.

## Evidence

- Passage and supply deal: [[706-queen-victorias-revenge-storming-the-citadel-part-2-glt3285474568]] says Napier greeted Kassa with full military honours, offered gifts, promised that his troops would pay for every sack of grain, and received a lion-skin cloak and weekly supplies in return.
- Rivalry as motive: [[706-queen-victorias-revenge-storming-the-citadel-part-2-glt3285474568]] describes Tigray as the kingdom of Theodros's rival Ras Kassa and reads the arrangement as an unwritten bargain that the British would settle his rival.
- Reliability: [[706-queen-victorias-revenge-storming-the-citadel-part-2-glt3285474568]] records that Kassa agreed to send weekly supplies and did so.

## Qualifications

Kassa appears inside a British campaign narrative, so his own aims, title, territory and later career are not developed: the source gives no Ethiopian-side account of the bargain, no detail on what he gained after Magdala, and no discussion of whether the arrangement matched his expectations. Treat the figure as a source-scoped case of local alliance-making inside the expedition branch rather than as a full biography.
