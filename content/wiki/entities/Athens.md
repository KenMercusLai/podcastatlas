# Athens / 雅典

## Overview

Athens / 雅典 is the Greek city whose revolt under [[AristionAthens]] lets episode 703 show how Roman imperial pressure abroad and Roman republican crisis at home become connected during [[LuciusCorneliusSulla]]'s eastern campaign.

## Current Profile

The source presents Athens as formally outside direct Roman provincial rule but already inside Rome's sphere of influence. Pro-Roman oligarchs benefit from Roman favor, while broader Athenian resentment is stirred by [[MithridatesVI]]'s successes in Asia and his claim to punish Roman greed. The city therefore becomes a symbolic test of Greek freedom from Roman dominance.

That test fails. Aristion's revolution massacres or expels plutocrats and brings in Mithridatic support, but Sulla's arrival traps the city. Athens starves, diplomatic appeals to ancient glory fail, and Roman troops sack the city in March 86 BC. Sulla spares the city itself out of respect for its dead, yet Piraeus is flattened, democracy effectively ends, pro-Roman businessmen return, and later humiliation includes the removal of temple columns and libraries.

## Key Characteristics

- Athens is not presented as a sovereign equal to Rome, but as a culturally prestigious city under Roman shadow.
- Internal class and collaboration resentment make the city receptive to Mithridates's anti-Roman campaign.
- The revolt turns Greek freedom language into alliance with an eastern king and local tyranny under Aristion.
- Sulla's siege breaks Athens through starvation, sack, and restoration of pro-Roman interests.
- The episode treats 86 BC as the real end of Athens as a meaningful independent political power.

- Roman shadow: [[703-roman-civil-war-marching-on-the-senate-part-3-glt3125075511]] says Greece is not formally part of Rome's empire, but Athens is under Roman influence.
- Oligarchic resentment: [[703-roman-civil-war-marching-on-the-senate-part-3-glt3125075511]] says pro-Roman oligarchs benefit from Roman favor while the mass of Athenians resent wealthy collaborators.
- Revolt and alliance: [[703-roman-civil-war-marching-on-the-senate-part-3-glt3125075511]] says Aristion's revolution asks Mithridates for alliance and receives an expeditionary force.
- Siege and sack: [[703-roman-civil-war-marching-on-the-senate-part-3-glt3125075511]] describes starvation, Sulla's rejection of antiquarian appeals, and the March 86 BC sack.
- Political aftermath: [[703-roman-civil-war-marching-on-the-senate-part-3-glt3125075511]] says Piraeus is flattened, Athens loses remaining independence, democracy is effectively abandoned, and pro-Roman businessmen are restored.

## Qualifications

The current page is source-scoped to the Sullan siege and Mithridatic revolt. It does not synthesize earlier classical Athens, the philosophical schools, or later Roman-era cultural life beyond the episode's claim that political independence is broken.

## What Changed

- Created Athens as the Greek city node tying Mithridatic anti-Roman revolt to Sulla's eastern campaign and Rome's wider crisis.
