## Overview

齐厉王 / King Li of Qi is the Western Han king whose alleged relationship with his sister becomes the basis for [[ZhuFuYan|主父偃]]'s memorial and coercive investigation in Hanji 284.

## Current Profile

The episode presents the Qi king as ruler of a wealthy, populous fief and as a more distant imperial relative than the emperor's sons or brothers. After Empress Dowager Ji rejects Zhufu Yan's proposed marriage connection, Zhufu Yan reports both the political importance of Linzi and the king's alleged relationship with Princess Ji Weng. Appointed Qi chancellor, Zhufu Yan tortures palace eunuchs for testimony; when the evidence reaches the king, he drinks poison and dies.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han king of the wealthy Qi fief centered on Linzi.
- Son of Empress Dowager Ji in the episode's household frame.
- Accused of an incestuous relationship with Princess Ji Weng.
- Investigated by Zhufu Yan after a rejected marriage proposal creates an alleged private grievance.
- Dies by poison after coerced testimony implicates him.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to one compact episode. The private-grievance explanation is source-attributed, the testimony is described as coercively obtained, and the page does not independently settle the allegation, legal procedure, or the exact relationship between central territorial interest and personal motive.
