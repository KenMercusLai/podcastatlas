# Brazilian National Anthem

## Overview

The Brazilian National Anthem appears in the wiki as the national song whose tune, lyrics, and performance history carry [[Brazil]]'s monarchy, republic, slavery-shadowed identity, and democratic-protest memory.

## Current Profile

The episode treats the anthem as a political archive rather than a settled patriotic object. The first independence anthem praised [[DomPedroI]] too directly to survive his decline, while [[FranciscoManuelDaSilva]]'s 1831 tune endured through imperial and republican change. [[OsorioDuqueEstrada]]'s lyrics were settled around the 1922 independence centenary, but later uses by protest movements, dictatorship opponents, and right-wing crowds show that the anthem's emotional authority can be reused without a single fixed politics.

## Key Characteristics

- National song whose durable tune came from [[FranciscoManuelDaSilva]] after Pedro I's 1831 abdication.
- Lyrics by [[OsorioDuqueEstrada]] were officially settled only around the 1922 independence centenary.
- References to the Ipiranga and Southern Cross tie the song to independence geography and Brazilian celestial symbolism.
- Political meaning shifts across monarchy, republic, dictatorship-era protest, land-reform activism, and Bolsonaro-era right-wing use.
- [[FafaDeBelem]]'s [[DiretasJa]] performances turned the anthem into an anti-dictatorship democratic sound.
- The anthem carries [[SlaveryNationalIdentityContradiction]] because patriotic liberty language sits beside Brazil's long slavery history.

## Evidence

- Tune and lyrics: [[681-brazil-the-emperors-anthem-part-5-glt5596929385]] says [[FranciscoManuelDaSilva]] composed the tune after Pedro I's abdication and [[OsorioDuqueEstrada]]'s lyrics were bought and settled for the 1922 centenary.
- Independence symbolism: [[681-brazil-the-emperors-anthem-part-5-glt5596929385]] links the anthem's Ipiranga reference to [[DomPedroI]]'s 1822 independence declaration and the Southern Cross to early Portuguese observation and flag symbolism.
- Regime survival: [[681-brazil-the-emperors-anthem-part-5-glt5596929385]] says the republic held a new anthem contest but [[DeodoroDaFonseca]] preferred the older tune.
- Protest reuse: [[681-brazil-the-emperors-anthem-part-5-glt5596929385]] describes land reform protesters, Bolsonaro supporters, and [[FafaDeBelem]] at [[DiretasJa]] rallies claiming the anthem in different political registers.
