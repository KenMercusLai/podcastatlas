# David DeSteno

## Overview
David DeSteno is a Northeastern University psychology professor presented in the supplied [[HubermanLab]] interview as a researcher of morality, religion, ritual, gratitude, and the behavioral and health effects of spiritual practice.

## Current Profile
DeSteno draws a firm boundary between metaphysical truth and experimentally tractable consequences. Science cannot manipulate God as a variable and therefore cannot settle God's existence, but it can examine what prayer, meditation, gratitude, mourning, community worship, mortality contemplation, and surrender do to physiology, behavior, relationships, and wellbeing.

His organizing proposal is [[ReligiousPracticeAsSpiritualTechnology]]. Ancient practices deserve neither automatic acceptance nor reflexive dismissal: they can be investigated like inherited behavioral tools, with attention to mechanism, outcome, confounding, and misuse. In this account, practice matters more than identity alone, embodied ritual can join meaning to regulation and social connection, and prosocial effects remain conditional rather than guaranteed.

## Key Characteristics
- Separates scientifically testable consequences of belief and practice from untestable claims about God's existence.
- Advocates “religio-prospecting”: rigorous study of inherited religious practices for useful psychological, physiological, and social mechanisms.
- Treats repeated practice, not nominal belief alone, as the main candidate pathway for measurable benefit.
- Connects prayer and ritual to breathing, bodily safety, synchrony, memory, community, grief, and continuing bonds.
- Studies moral variability, cheating, rationalization, gratitude, and the situational conditions that change conduct.
- Describes himself in the episode as an agnostic shaped by Catholic upbringing who personally uses gratitude, meditation, and occasional prayer.
- Warns that religious mechanisms can be directed toward aggression, leader worship, exploitation, or institutional harm as well as flourishing.

## Evidence
- Scientific boundary: [[science-health-benefits-of-belief-in-god-religion-dr-david-desteno-scim1533978257]] has DeSteno say that science lacks empirical proof of God but also cannot prove nonexistence through experiment.
- Practice-centered method: [[science-health-benefits-of-belief-in-god-religion-dr-david-desteno-scim1533978257]] introduces religio-prospecting and repeatedly distinguishes belief from participation in prayer, meditation, ritual, gratitude, mourning, and community.
- Embodied mechanisms: [[science-health-benefits-of-belief-in-god-religion-dr-david-desteno-scim1533978257]] connects repeated prayer to respiration and ritual synchrony to connection and helping.
- Moral psychology: [[science-health-benefits-of-belief-in-god-religion-dr-david-desteno-scim1533978257]] describes cheating, self-protective rationalization, religious setting, and gratitude induction as situational influences on conduct.
- Personal stance and caution: [[science-health-benefits-of-belief-in-god-religion-dr-david-desteno-scim1533978257]] presents DeSteno as agnostic and records his warning that spiritual technologies can be used for good or ill.

## Qualifications
This profile is bounded to one public interview and is not a complete biography, publication record, or independent audit of the studies discussed. Epidemiological health effects remain associational, experimental summaries omit important methodological detail, and DeSteno's personal agnosticism does not verify or invalidate any religious doctrine.
