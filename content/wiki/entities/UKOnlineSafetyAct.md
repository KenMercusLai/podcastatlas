# UK Online Safety Act

The UK Online Safety Act appears in [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-under-secretary-of-state-sarah-b-rogers-on-dismantling-the-censorship-industrial-complex-39828360]] as one of the foreign online-safety regimes [[SarahBRogers|Sarah B. Rogers]] criticizes. In the episode's account, the law imposes age-gating and risk-assessment obligations and can require platforms to remove material considered illegal under UK law.

The source keeps two issues adjacent but distinct. Rogers says many UK speech arrests discussed in the episode are not directly under the Online Safety Act, but under older statutes applied online. The act still matters in her account because it creates a broader compliance environment in which platforms may remove more content than a specific case requires.

## Key Claims
- Age-gating can become a speech and privacy burden when lawful users must prove age or identity before participating online.
- Risk assessments can push platforms toward over-removal when penalties are high and legal categories are vague.
- A law aimed at UK users can become a U.S. foreign-policy issue if it affects American platforms, American users, or American speech norms.
- The source treats the act as part of [[CrossBorderPlatformSpeechRegulation]], not just domestic UK child-safety policy.
