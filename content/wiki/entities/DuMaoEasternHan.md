## Overview

杜茂 / Du Mao is currently synthesized as an Eastern Han frontier commander whose current profile runs from northern defensive infrastructure to later command accountability. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-714-shi-shui-di-yi-ge-han-chu-nan-er-dang-ma-ge-guo-shi-lpkqxrl3dsok4ui0lij4c3x8zfpi|Hanji 714]] now adds his earlier role in guarding against Lu Fang, Xiongnu, and Wuhuan pressure through Feihu-route repair, forts, and beacon stations. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-717-13-ge-zi-yinfa-de-diwang-zhinu-liz3jsbvw3snfapzsrqw917fqv7v|Hanji 717]] then marks a command-accountability point when he is removed and replaced by [[MaChengEasternHan|Ma Cheng]], while [[ZhangKanEasternHan|Zhang Kan]] later uses his former troops at Gaoliu.

## Current Profile

Hanji 714 supplies Du Mao's earlier current role. Liu Xiu sends the Piaoji General Du Mao and others to defend the northern frontier while Lu Fang, Xiongnu, and Wuhuan forces repeatedly raid. The source emphasizes preparation and containment rather than decisive victory: Feihu route repair, forts, beacon stations, and repeated fighting without final resolution.

Du Mao's page remains intentionally narrow. Hanji 717 does not develop him as a full founding general biography; it records the accountability event and the reuse of his force structure. His importance is that Guangwu-era frontier correction reaches command rank rather than stopping at local disorder.

The reassignment branch also matters. Du Mao's removal does not make his troops useless: Liu Xiu sends Zhang Kan with Du Mao's troops, and Zhang Kan defeats Xiongnu at Gaoliu. Du Mao therefore connects punishment, personnel replacement, and continued operational use of frontier manpower.

## Key Characteristics

- Eastern Han frontier commander removed because misconduct by a subordinate commander is attributed upward.
- Earlier northern frontier defender linked to route repair, forts, and beacon infrastructure.
- Commander in a long-consumption pressure field rather than a decisive victory notice in Hanji 714.
- Accountability case showing that command responsibility could reach senior military office.
- Personnel bridge between Ma Cheng's replacement command and Zhang Kan's later use of former troops.
- Source-bounded figure currently known through one 39 CE frontier notice.

## Qualifications

The current sources do not establish Du Mao's full earlier service, rank history beyond these frontier notices, or the legal details of the subordinate's killing. Hanji 714 should not be read as a victory claim; it emphasizes long-running northern pressure and defensive preparation. Hanji 717 should not infer personal culpability beyond the source's removal-and-replacement sequence.
