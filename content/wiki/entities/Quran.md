# Qur'an

## Overview

The Qur'an is Islam's scripture, understood by Muslims as divine revelation to [[Muhammad]] and treated by the episode as the earliest indispensable textual witness to his movement and religious world.

## Current Profile

The episode keeps theological and historical questions distinct. For Muslim belief, the Qur'an comes from God; for secular historical inquiry, it is a text associated with Muhammad whose biblical figures, disputes, landscape, apocalyptic intensity, and post-biblical claims preserve evidence about late antiquity.

The source rejects an eighth- or ninth-century origin for the whole text, but it does not replace that rejected model with certainty. Early manuscript fragments constrain chronology without dating ink or settling origin, language, audience, transmission, or codification. The Qur'an is therefore early enough to discipline reconstruction but too complex to yield a self-explanatory biography.

## Key Characteristics

- Sacred revelation and foundational scripture in Muslim belief.
- Earliest central text associated by the episode with Muhammad's prophetic movement.
- Deeply engaged with Abraham, Moses, Jesus, angels, monotheism, and earlier scriptural traditions.
- Apocalyptic text reflecting a world of religious conflict and anticipated judgment.
- Historical witness whose geography, opponents, audience, and transmission remain debated.
- Manuscript tradition early enough to rule out very late composition of the whole text.

## Evidence

- **Early association:** [[60-muhammad-glt7621065751]] treats the Qur'an as early and connected to someone called Muhammad.
- **Religious world:** [[60-muhammad-glt7621065751]] uses biblical figures, Abrahamic disputes, and apocalyptic language to situate the text in late antiquity.
- **Material chronology:** [[60-muhammad-glt7621065751]] discusses Birmingham and Tübingen fragments while distinguishing parchment dating from ink dating.
- **Interpretive limits:** [[60-muhammad-glt7621065751]] reports uncertainty over origin, language, audience, transmission, and codification.

## Qualifications

The episode is a conversational historical treatment, not a comprehensive account of Quranic studies, manuscript science, Islamic theology, Arabic linguistics, or textual transmission. Its northern-geography and intra-Abrahamic-dispute readings are hypotheses. Historical analysis of the text neither verifies nor disproves the Muslim claim of divine revelation.

## What Changed

- Established the Qur'an as both sacred scripture and an early but difficult historical source.
- Distinguished manuscript-age constraints from certainty about composition and codification.
