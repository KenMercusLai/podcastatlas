# Edward II of England

## Overview

Edward II of England appears as the weak predecessor whose military defeat, favorite-driven domestic instability, Aquitaine crisis, and deposition leave [[EdwardIIIOfEngland|Edward III]] with humiliation and strategic constraints to reverse.

## Current Profile

The episode uses Edward II primarily as prehistory to the [[HundredYearsWar|Hundred Years' War]]. He is described as weak and unsuccessful, marked by defeat at Bannockburn and by domestic turmoil around favorites such as Piers Gaveston and the Despensers.

His weakness lets [[France]] pressure [[Aquitaine]]. In 1324 French forces overrun most of the duchy except Bordeaux and demand homage on forfeiture terms. [[IsabellaOfFrance|Isabella]]'s Paris negotiation then becomes the path through which she and [[RogerMortimer|Roger Mortimer]] keep young Edward in France, invade England, and force Edward II's abdication.

## Key Characteristics

- English king used by the source as a prewar weakness marker.
- Defeated at Bannockburn and domestically weakened by favorites.
- Unable to prevent French pressure and invasion in Aquitaine.
- Married to Isabella, daughter of Philip IV of France.
- Deposed by Isabella and Mortimer's invasion.
- His fall shapes Edward III's later need to restore authority and prestige.
