## Current Profile

The sources give Du Qin as a Wang Feng-side adviser rather than a complete biography. In Hanji 504, he diagnoses Chengdi's famous desire as a political problem and recommends a "nine wives" rule: enough consorts for heir production, but no indefinite harem expansion. The proposal fails because the dowager says Han has no such precedent and Wang Feng cannot create law by himself, but it reveals Du Qin's characteristic method: he turns private conduct into public risk through ritual, precedent, and succession language.

In Hanji 505, the same harem-and-heir logic appears inside disaster interpretation. Du Qin and Gu Yong answer omen pressure by naming palace jealousy and heir risk rather than the Wang-family power field. In Hanji 510, the advisory style shifts to frontier diplomacy: Du Qin and Gu Yong argue that accepting one Xiongnu minister's defection could damage the trust of an entire submitted chanyu relationship.

Hanji 517 adds the inverse diplomatic calculation. Instead of paying a small reception cost to protect a high-value frontier relationship, Du Qin argues against a dangerous full escort for a remote, unreliable polity whose submission has little effect on regional security. His compromise—escort Jibin's merchant-like mission only to Pishan—matches ceremony to practical reach rather than choosing either unlimited protection or no contact.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han adviser in Wang Feng's orbit.
- Uses ritual precedent to frame ruler desire as a harem and succession risk.
- Uses disaster-omen interpretation to discuss harem favoritism and heir danger.
- Helps redirect pressure from outer relatives to consorts in the Chengdi omen field.
- Helps argue for diplomatic restraint when accepting a defector could damage a larger frontier relationship.
- Distinguishes strategically important diplomatic trust from low-value prestige expenditure.
- Uses a geographically bounded escort as a compromise between ritual reception and operational risk.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 504, Hanji 505, Hanji 510, and Hanji 517 and does not reconstruct Du Qin's full biography, office sequence, complete memorial texts, or all recommendations to Wang Feng. Hanji 504's "nine wives" proposal is source-scoped as an episode summary and is not treated here as proof that the policy was legally feasible in Han practice. Hanji 517's supplied transcript writes `杜清`; the wiki routes the adviser to Du Qin from the Chengdi-era Wang Feng context, while preserving the transcription uncertainty here.
