# Hinton Ampner

## Overview

Hinton Ampner is the English house used in the source as an unusually well-documented eighteenth-century haunting case.

## Current Profile

[[66-ghosts-glt2309926019]] presents Mary Ricketts's 1760s family account of banging doors, apparent intrusions, apparitions, frightened children, and departing servants. The house was eventually abandoned and demolished, while the written record continued to support literary and supernatural interpretation.

The episode values the duration and witness documentation without establishing a paranormal cause. [[RogerClarke]] proposes that nearby Civil War violence may echo through the noisy narrative and argues that the account reached Henry James through Bishop Benson, but both links remain interpretive.

## Key Characteristics

- Long-duration domestic disturbance recorded in family testimony.
- Case shaped by household fear, children, servants, and absence of the husband.
- House said to have become unlivable before demolition.
- Later connected to nearby battle memory and Gothic literature.
- Strong documentation relative to many ghost stories, but no settled cause.

## Evidence

- Household record - [[66-ghosts-glt2309926019]] describes Mary Ricketts's written account of repeated disturbances and the family's departure.
- Historical interpretation - [[66-ghosts-glt2309926019]] connects the site tentatively to the Battle of Cheriton and Civil War memory.
- Literary afterlife - [[66-ghosts-glt2309926019]] reports Clarke's proposed transmission from Bishop Benson to Henry James and [[TheTurnOfTheScrew]].

## Qualifications

The source does not reproduce the original documents or independently test the battle-memory and literary-influence hypotheses. Stronger witness documentation does not by itself prove supernatural causation.
