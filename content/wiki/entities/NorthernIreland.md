# Northern Ireland

## Overview
Northern Ireland is the central place and constitutional setting for the wiki's [[TheTroubles]] branch. The current evidence treats it as a statelet whose institutions, communal fears, and relationship to Westminster made ordinary policing and reform politically explosive.

## Current Profile
The strongest current profile is Northern Ireland as a legitimacy-crisis system. Partition created a Protestant majority state with a large Catholic minority; Stormont, housing, ward boundaries, policing, and commemorative politics made many Catholics experience the state as partisan while many Protestants feared being absorbed into an Ireland-wide minority position. By 1972, that structure had produced barricaded spaces such as [[FreeDerry]], British military operations, [[BloodySunday]], direct rule from Westminster, and competing republican and loyalist armed mobilization.

## Key Characteristics
- Constitutional unit created by partition with Protestant-majority institutions and a large Catholic minority.
- Site of gerrymandered local power, housing grievance, Protestant-dominated policing, and civil-rights demands.
- Security field where the [[RoyalUlsterConstabulary|RUC]], [[BritishArmy]], republican groups, and loyalist groups all became politically coded.
- Geography of local flashpoints: [[Derry]], [[Bogside]], [[Belfast]], [[ShortStrand]], [[FallsCurfew]], [[FreeDerry]], and [[BloodySunday]].
- Governance crisis that moves from Stormont rule to [[NorthernIrelandDirectRule]] after March 1972.
- Example of how communal fear and state legitimacy failure can reinforce armed organization.

## Evidence
- Foundational state structure: [[697-the-troubles-murder-in-belfast-part-1-glt4877520736]] presents partition, James Craig's Protestant-state language, Derry housing and boundary manipulation, and Protestant-dominated policing as core preconditions.
- Protest-to-street-war shift: [[698-the-troubles-the-brink-of-civil-war-part-2-glt2308408988]] connects civil-rights marches, Burntollet, the Battle of the Bogside, Belfast violence, and British deployment.
- Military and detention escalation: [[699-the-troubles-return-of-the-ira-part-3-glt9519811642]] adds the Provisional IRA, Falls Curfew, internment, detention abuse claims, and loyalist mobilization.
- 1972 crisis peak: [[700-the-troubles-bloody-sunday-part-4-glt3909829334]] adds Bloody Sunday, Stormont's suspension, direct rule, failed IRA talks, loyalist killings, and Bloody Friday.

## Qualifications
The wiki's Northern Ireland page is source-bounded to conflict-era podcast summaries. It is not a full social, economic, or post-Good-Friday-Agreement account, and it keeps unionist fear and Catholic grievance visible together rather than reducing either side to a single motive.

## What Changed
- Added Free Derry and Bloody Sunday as 1972 evidence of territorial and legitimacy breakdown.
- Added direct rule as the point where Westminster takes over from Stormont.
- Added the failed talks and Bloody Friday as evidence that 1972 violence also constrained political openings.
