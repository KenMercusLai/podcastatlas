# 孟姜女 / Meng Jiangnü

## Overview

Meng Jiangnü is the legendary grieving wife whose familiar connection to Qin forced labor and a collapsing section of the [[GreatWallOfChina|Great Wall]] developed through successive reinterpretations of a much earlier account about Qiliang's widow.

## Current Profile

[[17-dongxifang-de-changcheng-lvlaqbfmfxbvytpsimiknt0km-xp]] begins with the *Zuo Zhuan* account of Qiliang's widow refusing an improper roadside condolence and insisting on correct household ritual. Later texts emphasize her lament, relocate the grief to a city wall, transfer the story to the Qin period, add a husband buried in the wall and motifs such as wall collapse and blood identifying bones, and eventually connect the couple to Great Wall conscription.

The resulting figure is therefore evidence for [[HistoricalLegendAccretion|legend accretion]], not a transparent biography. Her afterlife preserves grief and protest while also allowing later moral systems to recast the story as instruction in a wife's fidelity and dependence.

## Key Characteristics

- Legendary wife formed from an earlier ritual-remonstrance story about Qiliang's widow.
- Figure whose lament becomes progressively more central across later retellings.
- Character relocated from an unspecified city-wall setting into Qin and Great Wall history.
- Carrier of forced-labor, burial, bodily recognition, grief, and wall-collapse motifs.
- Flexible cultural symbol used for protest, love, fidelity, and moral instruction.

## Evidence

- **Ritual core:** [[17-dongxifang-de-changcheng-lvlaqbfmfxbvytpsimiknt0km-xp]] describes the earliest bounded account as a widow's insistence that condolence follow proper ritual at home.
- **Grief and wall attachment:** [[17-dongxifang-de-changcheng-lvlaqbfmfxbvytpsimiknt0km-xp]] traces later emphasis on lament and the addition of crying at the wall where her husband died.
- **Qin narrative expansion:** [[17-dongxifang-de-changcheng-lvlaqbfmfxbvytpsimiknt0km-xp]] attributes the husband-in-the-wall, wall collapse, blood recognition, and Great Wall conscription complex to later literary development.
- **Moral reframing:** [[17-dongxifang-de-changcheng-lvlaqbfmfxbvytpsimiknt0km-xp]] says Song and later Ming-Qing retellings increasingly organize the story around marital fidelity.

## Qualifications

The episode is a popular-history survey rather than a complete textual genealogy. It does not settle the dating, transmission, regional variants, names, or exact sequence of every motif, and a later written form may preserve older oral material. The page therefore distinguishes recoverable textual layers from claims about a historical Meng Jiangnü.

## What Changed

- Established the distinction between the early ritual-remonstrance core and the later Great Wall legend.
- Added grief, Qin relocation, wall collapse, bodily recognition, and marital instruction as successive narrative layers.
