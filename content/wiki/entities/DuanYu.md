## Overview

段誉 / Duan Yu is a protagonist of [[TianLongBaBu|《天龙八部》]] whom the bounded episode reads as a humane, classically educated Dali prince, an initially unwilling martial artist, an obsessive lover, and the [[NarrativeBridgeProtagonist|narrative bridge]] linking the novel's major story regions.

## Current Profile

Duan Yu enters without martial skill and opposes killing, yet repeatedly intervenes when stronger people threaten others. His fear of pain and death does not cancel courage; it makes action costly. The episode distinguishes his principled “呆” from affective “痴”: he can resist wealth, coercion, and immoral allegiance, but desire—especially the image of the “神仙姐姐”—also drives exception-making and self-justification.

His philosophical meaning is composite. Classical citation, filial behavior, humane concern, and entry into worldly affairs support a Confucian reading; 北冥神功 and 凌波微步 carry Daoist language; Dali, egalitarian sympathy, “无相,” and eventual renunciation supply Buddhist associations. His later adaptability prevents any of these labels from exhausting the character.

Structurally, he opens the novel and moves between Dali, the Murong line, Qiao Feng's story, the Beggars' Sect crisis, the Zhenlong board, and Xuzhu. He connects the sworn brothers and returns as viewpoint near the close. This supports a “first protagonist” argument based on linkage and duration, not a settled hierarchy over [[XiaoFeng|萧峰]] or [[Xuzhu|虚竹]].

His relationships expose the limits of generosity and intensity as knowledge. [[ZhongLingTianLongBaBu|钟灵]] offers a light, disputed romance; [[MuWanqing|木婉清]] develops fierce mutual attachment amid likely incompatibility; and [[WangYuyan|王语嫣]] becomes inseparable from the jade-statue ideal in his imagination. The revised ending can therefore be read as release from projection rather than simple romantic defeat.

## Key Characteristics

- Intervenes against harm before possessing the force needed to protect himself.
- Grounds conduct in learning, filial respect, anti-killing principle, and sympathy across status boundaries.
- Distinguishes principled persistence from emotional obsession but embodies both.
- Learns tactical flattery and interpretive flexibility without wholly abandoning moral limits.
- Carries Confucian, Daoist, and Buddhist motifs without reducing to one doctrine.
- Links semi-independent plot regions and characters across the novel.
- Loves intensely while sometimes mistaking an idealized image for the other person.

## Qualifications

The profile rests on one conversational literary discussion rather than an edition-specific close reading of the entire novel. “First protagonist,” “无相,” doctrinal mixture, courage, flexibility, compatibility, and projection are interpretive judgments. The episode does not fully cover Duan Yu's martial development or father-son relationships, and old and revised endings produce different romantic trajectories. Intense rescue and sacrifice do not by themselves establish accurate perception, reciprocity, or compatibility.

## What Changed

- Created a profile joining moral courage, worldly adaptation, philosophical mixture, structural linkage, and romantic projection.
- Kept the “first protagonist” claim criterion-dependent rather than treating it as a settled ranking.
