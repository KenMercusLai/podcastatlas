# Bloody Sunday

## Overview
Bloody Sunday is the 30 January 1972 killing of 13 unarmed Catholic civilians by British paratroopers during a civil-rights march in [[Derry]]. The episode treats it as both a moral atrocity and a strategic failure inside [[TheTroubles]].

## Current Profile
The strongest current wiki judgment is that Bloody Sunday converted an already failing security strategy into a legitimacy catastrophe. The march began as an anti-internment protest, most marchers were moving toward [[FreeDerry]] rather than forcing the city centre, and the [[SavilleInquiry]] later found no evidence supporting the paratroopers' claim that they had faced sniper fire. The killings shattered Catholic trust in the [[BritishArmy]], strengthened [[ProvisionalIRA]] recruitment, and intensified pressure on [[Stormont]] and Westminster.

## Key Characteristics
- Civilian massacre during a civil-rights march against [[InternmentWithoutTrial]].
- State-violence event later reassessed through the [[SavilleInquiry]] after the earlier [[WidgeryInquiry]] had largely protected the soldiers' account.
- Crucial escalation point in the [[CounterinsurgencyLegitimacyTrap]] because the security force's conduct strengthened the insurgent narrative.
- Local [[Derry]] and [[Bogside]] event with system-wide consequences for [[NorthernIreland]] and the [[UnitedKingdom]].
- Recruitment and reputation shock that benefited the [[ProvisionalIRA]] even while deepening wider suffering.

## Evidence
- Civilian-march setting: [[700-the-troubles-bloody-sunday-part-4-glt3909829334]] describes a 30 January 1972 NICRA anti-internment march in Derry whose initial atmosphere was relaxed before confrontation at barricades escalated.
- Shooting account: [[700-the-troubles-bloody-sunday-part-4-glt3909829334]] says paratroopers fired more than 100 rounds, hit 26 people, and killed 13, many while victims were running away or helping others.
- Inquiry reassessment: [[700-the-troubles-bloody-sunday-part-4-glt3909829334]] reports Saville's finding that there was no supporting evidence for the soldiers' sniper-fire claim.
- Strategic consequence: [[700-the-troubles-bloody-sunday-part-4-glt3909829334]] ties the killings to Catholic alienation from the British Army, Provisional IRA recruitment, and damage to Britain's reputation in Ireland and America.

## Qualifications
The episode is a narrative synthesis, not a victim-by-victim legal record. It repeatedly leans on Saville's later findings and contrasts them with Widgery, so the page's judgment is source-grounded to that episode's reconstruction rather than a complete independent inquiry file.
