# Andrew Lange

## Overview

Andrew Lange appears in [[BrianKeating]]'s account as a cosmologist, mentor, father figure, and major influence on experimental work concerning the geometry and early history of the universe.

## Current Profile

The episode credits Lange with scientific and institutional mentorship that helped make Keating's South Pole work possible. His research is associated with evidence that the universe has flat spatial geometry, while his relationship with Keating provides the personal bridge between technical ambition and scientific belonging.

Lange's death by suicide is discussed with deliberate uncertainty. The speakers reflect on scientific pressure, crisis, risk-taking, and dopamine, but Keating says the death cannot be fully explained. The wiki therefore records the loss and its meaning to Keating without assigning a single cause or retrospective diagnosis.

## Key Characteristics
- Cosmologist associated with observational evidence about spatial geometry.
- Mentor and father figure in Keating's scientific formation.
- Institutional supporter of South Pole experimental cosmology.
- Personal loss through which the episode discusses the human costs surrounding scientific work.

## Qualifications

The episode offers Keating's personal retrospective rather than a complete biography or research history. It does not support diagnosing Lange or attributing his death to scientific pressure, personality, dopamine, a single crisis, or any one cause. Readers affected by the subject should treat the account as bereavement testimony, not mental-health guidance.
