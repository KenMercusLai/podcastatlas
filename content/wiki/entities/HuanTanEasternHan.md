## Overview

桓谭 / Huan Tan is an Eastern Han scholar-official whose current wiki profile has two bounded layers. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-685-donghan-dachen-songhong-weihe-buken-zuo-guangwudi-jiefu-ljafg8giyhg5wmumv-g-09lywcu2|Hanji 685]] shows him earlier as [[SongHongEasternHan|宋弘]]'s recommended talent whose music pleases [[LiuXiu|光武帝刘秀]] enough to trigger Song's correction. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-731-jinhu-wanmei-de-liuxiu-zui-da-quedian-shi-ta-lrhwupjmjm-hch3usx26jfxkhoma|Hanji 731]] later presents him as the direct anti-chenwei voice whose open rejection of prophetic books nearly becomes fatal.

## Current Profile

Hanji 685 gives the earlier court-favor layer. Song Hong recommends Huan Tan for office, Liu Xiu becomes fond of hearing him play, and Song rebukes Huan for winning attention through music rather than assisting the ruler with loyalty and righteousness. Huan Tan apologizes in that scene, and Liu Xiu also accepts Song's correction after seeing Huan's fear at court.

Hanji 731 supplies the later and more central intellectual-risk layer. Huan Tan argues that people often neglect ordinary, visible things while chasing strange reports; proper rulers rely on benevolence, righteousness, and the Five Classics rather than fabricated prophetic books. The target is not only superstition in general, but court use of forged texts that can deceive unprincipled people.

The decisive episode comes during discussion of the Lingtai site. Liu Xiu asks whether chenwei can resolve the question, and Huan Tan eventually says he does not read such books because they are not classics. Liu Xiu treats that answer as slander against sages and contempt for law, orders execution, then relents after Huan Tan apologizes. Huan Tan is sent out as Liu'an commandery assistant and dies before taking office.

Fan Ye's comparison, as reported in the episode, turns Huan Tan into one pole of a three-part contrast. [[ZhengXingEasternHan|郑兴]] survives by saying he does not understand chenwei while not rejecting it, and [[JiaKuiEasternHan|贾逵]] is remembered as someone who could attach chenwei to classical interpretation. Huan Tan therefore represents truthful refusal whose intellectual clarity exceeds its political protection.

## Key Characteristics

- Recommended talent whose early court access is first framed through musical skill and ruler favor.
- Direct critic of chenwei and fuming books under Guangwu.
- Frames anti-chenwei criticism through classical authority and righteous governance.
- Risks execution because Liu Xiu treats chenwei rejection as an attack on sacred legitimacy.
- Serves as the high-risk contrast to Zheng Xing's ambiguity and Jia Kui's accommodation.
- His death after demotion keeps the source's moral judgment tied to personal cost rather than abstract debate.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 731 and Hanji 685 and does not provide a complete biography of Huan Tan, his writings, musical practice, or broader intellectual career. Hanji 685 should not be read as evidence that Huan Tan's career was mainly musical; it is an early court-favor scene used to show Song Hong's corrective role. The strongest current claim remains narrower: in Hanji 731, evidence-based and canonical criticism of chenwei becomes politically dangerous because it touches the emperor's legitimacy commitments.
