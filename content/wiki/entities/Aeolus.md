# Aeolus

## Overview

Aeolus is the wind-god host whose bag of winds almost returns [[Odysseus]] to [[Ithaca]] before crew mistrust reverses the voyage.

## Current Profile

In [[687-the-odyssey-hero-of-the-trojan-horse-part-1-glt2393125423]], Aeolus receives Odysseus and his men hospitably for a month and gives Odysseus a bag containing contrary winds. Ithaca comes nearly into view, but Odysseus falls asleep and his men open the bag because they suspect hidden treasure. The released winds drive them back to Aeolus, who refuses further help.

## Key Characteristics

- Aeolus represents successful divine hospitality before the crew breaks the conditions of return.
- His bag of winds makes homecoming depend on trust, secrecy, and disciplined waiting.
- The episode uses his refusal of second aid to mark the reversal from near-success into renewed wandering.
- His scene shows that Odysseus' problem is not only external monsters but fragile authority inside his own crew.

## Evidence

- Hospitable aid: [[687-the-odyssey-hero-of-the-trojan-horse-part-1-glt2393125423]] says Aeolus hosts Odysseus for a month and gives him the wind bag.
- Near return: [[687-the-odyssey-hero-of-the-trojan-horse-part-1-glt2393125423]] has Ithaca almost in sight after Odysseus keeps watch for nine days and nights.
- Trust failure: [[687-the-odyssey-hero-of-the-trojan-horse-part-1-glt2393125423]] says the men think the bag holds treasure, open it, and release the winds.
- Refused reset: [[687-the-odyssey-hero-of-the-trojan-horse-part-1-glt2393125423]] has Aeolus reject them when they return.
