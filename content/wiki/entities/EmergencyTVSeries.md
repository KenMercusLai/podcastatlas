# Emergency! (television series)

## Overview

*Emergency!* is the 1972 NBC television series that [[running-hot|Running Hot]] presents as both a successful campaign for paramedicine and a source of the enduring expectation that every emergency call is time-critical.

## Current Profile

The episode says the series launched when the United States had only 12 paramedic units, was developed with EMS advocate James Page, reached roughly 30 million viewers, influenced future paramedics and lawmakers, and coincided with 46 states legalizing paramedicine during its early years.

Its cultural benefit and distortion are linked. By dramatizing nearly every call as life-threatening and sending responders with lights and sirens, the show made a then-new service legible and desirable while normalizing universal urgency after emergency medicine, dispatch, and system capacity had changed.

## Key Characteristics

- 1970s drama used to build public support for paramedicine.
- Presented emergency medical work through vivid rescue narratives.
- Depicted nearly every call as life-threatening and warning-device worthy.
- Supplies a media explanation for public expectations of speed and noise.
- Illustrates how a useful founding story can outlast its original conditions.

## Evidence

### Paramedicine advocacy

- [[running-hot]] attributes the show's Page collaboration, audience reach, professional influence, and legalization context to the episode's historical account.

### Universal-urgency narrative

- [[running-hot]] says the series used lights and sirens for every response and represented nearly every call as a race against time.

## Qualifications

The page is based on one structured podcast summary rather than episodes, production archives, audience research, legislative histories, or a causal media-effects study. Temporal coincidence and participant testimony do not isolate the show's effect on legalization or current EMS culture.
