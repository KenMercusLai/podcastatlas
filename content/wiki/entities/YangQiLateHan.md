# 杨琦 / Yang Qi (late Han)

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-914-xinren-de-ren-weihe-zong-shang-ni-zui-shen-lvwit-rzrkscn5a3h6wblntxppso|Hanji 914]] backfills Yang Qi's earlier service under [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]]. When Ling asks how he compares with Emperor Huan, Yang Qi answers by comparing the pair to Shun and Yao; the episode reads the answer as too straight and insufficiently flattering for Ling's desired self-image.

Ling responds by calling Yang Qi a stubborn descendant of [[YangZhenLateHan|杨震]] and invoking the large-bird omen associated with Yang Zhen's burial. The Hanji 914 transcript renders the name as "杨齐"; this page keeps the existing Yang Qi route because the later Hanji 985 source already uses 杨琦 for a late-Han shizhong whose career continues into the Emperor Xian crisis.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-985-fanmu-chengchou-liguo-zhiluan-de-xijie-tanjiu-lt-t2un0zcudkige9ucl1txa2hhk|Hanji 985]] later shows Yang Qi as the shizhong who urges [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]] not to rebuke [[LiJueLateHan|李傕]] after Li Jue sends rotten beef bones instead of usable food. Yang Qi warns that Li Jue already knows he has committed a grave rebellion and may move the emperor toward Chiyang's Huangbai city, so anger could worsen captivity rather than correct it.

His page is source-scoped. Yang Qi matters as a survival adviser: under ordinary sovereignty the emperor's anger could punish disrespect, but under Li Jue's control it becomes a bodily risk to be suppressed.
