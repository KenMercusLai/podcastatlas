## Overview

胡斐 / Hu Fei is the protagonist of [[FeihuWaizhuan|《飞狐外传》]]. The bounded sources read his relationships with [[YuanZiyi|袁紫衣]] and [[ChengLingsu|程灵素]] as a test of the difference among desire, admiration, reciprocity, care, and belated recognition.

## Current Profile

Hu Fei is immediately drawn to Yuan Ziyi's beauty, vitality, martial competence, and unpredictable interaction. With Cheng Lingsu, his initial pity develops into respect, reliance, and shared侠义 purpose, but he categorizes the bond as sibling-like even while understanding that this hurts her. The result is not simple ignorance: he recognizes her attachment and wants to retain her companionship without offering the response she wants.

The revised edition prevents that position from being treated as permanent indifference. Hu Fei notices Cheng Lingsu as attractive and imagines marriage and a peaceful household, suggesting that familiarity and recognition may be changing desire. Her death ends that development, and his later grief becomes inseparable from regret that his emotional understanding arrived too late.

The relationship-ranking source supplies an ethical boundary. Cheng Lingsu's extraordinary competence, protection, and sacrifice make the missed bond painful, but they do not make romantic reciprocity a debt. Hu Fei can be criticized for clumsy boundary-setting and for wanting continued care after refusal without being judged obligated to love the person who saves him.

## Key Characteristics

- Responds strongly to beauty, novelty, play, martial parity, and uncertain availability.
- Develops trust, respect, and value alignment with Cheng Lingsu before clear romantic attraction.
- Understands enough of Cheng Lingsu's feelings to know that siblinghood will wound her.
- Wants to preserve companionship after refusing romance, creating an ethically ambiguous boundary.
- Shows possible movement toward domestic and romantic imagination in the revised edition.
- Reinterprets Cheng Lingsu only after irreversible loss, turning growth into regret rather than relationship.

## Qualifications

The sources are conversational literary readings, not diagnoses or a full edition study. The claim that Hu Fei feels threatened by Cheng Lingsu's intelligence is explicitly disputed, and his marriage fantasy supports possible change rather than proving a completed love. Criticism of his boundary-setting should remain distinct from a claim that another person's merit or sacrifice entitles them to romance.

## What Changed

- Created a profile distinguishing immediate attraction from slowly developing admiration and possible desire.
- Preserved both criticism of his ambiguous boundaries and the principle that love is not repayment for sacrifice.
