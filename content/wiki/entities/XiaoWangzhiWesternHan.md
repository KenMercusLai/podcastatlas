## Overview

萧望之 / Xiao Wangzhi enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-501-hanyuandi-jing-ba-taijian-dang-fuqin-lml1qwuw3iujobnltbjymeyojd4g|Hanji 501]] as [[HanYuandi|汉元帝]]'s teacher and an early opponent of Yuan's favor toward [[ShiXianWesternHan|石显]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-451-ruzhe-xiaowangzhi-shui-youxiu-wo-jiu-nongsi-shui-lo20q2_kdbmsw2zfhehoanceo2oa|Hanji 451]] now backfills an earlier negative Xuan-era case: Xiao pursues [[HanYanshouWesternHan|韩延寿]] over old East Commandery spending, resists [[BingJiWesternHan|丙吉]]'s attempt to drop the matter after amnesty, and reframes Han's counteraccusation as resistance to lawful supervision. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-449-yichu-huangdan-naoju-xiongnu-wuda-chanyu-duoquan-huopin-lo39-mygg6xxo8cwcyvpqzcpzhrm|Hanji 449]] adds a different early Xuan-era layer: during the Xiongnu five-chanyu crisis, Xiao rejects most ministers' proposal to attack and instead recommends mourning, comfort, and aid as a way to turn disorder into gratitude and future submission. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-462-kanbi-dayuebing-xiongnu-xianghan-changmian-you-duo-niu-lnfkacjnat32wi-akpwdggvljkxk|Hanji 462]] adds the later high-statecraft layer: under [[EmperorXuanOfHan|汉宣帝]], Xiao argues that [[HuhanyeChanyuWesternHan|呼韩邪单于]] should be received as an elevated guest rather than ranked below internal kings, preserving both Han magnanimity and future diplomatic flexibility. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-465-cong-qiutu-dao-mingjun-hanxuandi-liubingyi-shi-zenme-si-de-lguhdrq8lfw2wfykuteib-sucwyt|Hanji 465]] adds his transition-office baseline: Xuan appoints him as Qian Jiangjun Guanglu Xun and includes him among the entrusted deathbed officials. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-468-yan-duo-bi-shi-xiao-wangzhi-ruhe-yin-zui-chi-da-kui-losjg52ruc7ov6k3uvmxvhxlzvjw|Hanji 468]] adds the setup: Xiao, [[ZhouKanWesternHan|周堪]], [[LiuXiang|刘向 / 刘更生]], and Jin Chang form a Confucian adviser bloc; Xiao directly attacks Zhongshu eunuch power; and he initially encourages [[ZhengPengWesternHan|郑朋]] after Zheng accuses the Xu-Shi outer-relative side. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-469-weishenme-junzi-dou-buguo-xiaoren-kankan-xiaowangzhi-de-xiachang-lj8iji-c3scj6vznntavm6awolho|Hanji 469]] adds the first-stage fall: Xiao misjudges Zheng, cuts him off too late, and is pulled into a Tingwei-custody and pardon sequence that damages his reputation. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-470-xiao-wangzhi-shasi-wode-chule-taijian-haiyou-wode-yufu-lof1c5nctali_zsy0hotqyocjgoe|Hanji 470]] then gives the fuller Chuyuan 2 death case: Xiao is restored to honor, but his son [[XiaoJiWesternHan|萧吉]] asks for vindication, [[HongGongWesternHan|弘恭]] and Shi Xian turn the request into a punitive arrest, and Xiao commits suicide after his residence is surrounded.

## Current Profile

Hanji 449, Hanji 451, Hanji 462, and Hanji 465 show why Xiao's later loss matters and why the wiki should keep his profile morally mixed. Before the Yuan-era factional struggle, he is already a high-level adviser trusted with difficult frontier policy, protocol, and succession transition, but he is also shown as a dangerous investigator-rival in the Han Yanshou case. His Xiongnu advice is flexible rather than rigid. In Hanji 449, he treats Xiongnu disorder as a chance to build dependence through benevolent restraint rather than as a simple invitation to conquest; in Hanji 462, he treats the chanyu as a guest above princes, letting Han stage strength and generosity without pretending Xiongnu has become an ordinary domestic subject. In the Han Yanshou case, however, Xiao's skill is adversarial: he takes a real old-account case, refuses de-escalation, and uses [[NarrativeReframingAsPoliticalAttack|narrative reframing]] to make Han's failed counteraccusation look like resistance to lawful authority. Hanji 501 then treats him as a high-capacity but temperamentally vulnerable counterweight to Shi Xian. The episode says Shi Xian regards him as dangerous because Xiao has ability and will not easily spare people he dislikes. Hanji 468-470 fill in the earlier case behind that summary: Xiao's anti-eunuch memorial threatens Zhongshu power, his circle rewards Zheng Peng's anti-Shi-Gao accusations with encouragement, then that same attachment line becomes a breach point through Zheng Peng, the Tingwei procedure, and a face-saving pardon. Yuan later grants Xiao Guannei Hou status and contemplates high office, yet the hostile inner-court field remains intact.

The current source set makes Xiao's death a shared-failure case. Hong Gong and Shi Xian exploit Xiao's pride and Yuan's uneasy responsibility for the earlier case; Xiao Ji's vindication petition gives them an opening; Yuan allows a "temporary lesson" to become a public humiliation; Xiao's wife urges endurance, but [[ZhuYunWesternHan|朱云]] counsels suicide; Xiao chooses reputation over survival. The host still grants Xiao major status as an imperial teacher, Confucian minister, and Qilin Pavilion figure, but also stresses earlier conduct against Zhang Chang, Han Yanshou, and Bing Ji to keep him from becoming a spotless martyr.

## Key Characteristics

- Xuan-era frontier-policy and protocol adviser, severe investigator, and Yuan-era teacher whose authority makes him both useful and dangerous.
- Leader within a Confucian adviser bloc that tries to guide Yuan through ancient institutions and to push eunuchs out of Zhongshu.
- Presented as capable and unwilling to let disliked actors off easily.
- Restored to honor under Yuan but still exposed because hostile inner-court actors control access and procedure.
- Poorly served by late personnel boundary-setting: he first encourages Zheng Peng's anti-Shi-Gao attack, then identifies Zheng as corrupt only after Zheng has entered the circle and gathered usable information.
- Vulnerable because pride, moral reputation, and public humiliation make him easier for Hong Gong and Shi Xian to manipulate.
- His death marks the weakening of organized resistance to Shi Xian in Yuan's court while also showing the limits of reputation-first politics.

## Qualifications

This page is source-scoped to Hanji 501, Hanji 470, Hanji 469, Hanji 468, Hanji 465, Hanji 462, Hanji 451, and Hanji 449. It does not settle Xiao Wangzhi's complete career, exact legal cases, factional network, responsibility distribution among Zheng Peng, Shi Xian, Hong Gong, Yuan, Zhu Yun, Xiao Ji, and Xiao himself, or the full historical record behind the host's negative references to Xiao's earlier conduct toward Zhang Chang, Han Yanshou, and Bing Ji. Hanji 449 supplies a policy argument against opportunistic war, not proof that restraint was the only viable strategy. Hanji 451 now grounds the Han Yanshou reference, but the host's psychological claims about jealousy and insecurity remain source-scoped. Hanji 462's praise of Xiao's frontier protocol is preserved alongside, not replaced by, later sources' criticism of his pride, severity, and political rigidity.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 451's Han Yanshou case, grounding the earlier negative reference to Xiao's treatment of Han and adding narrative reframing as one of Xiao's dangerous political skills.
- Added Hanji 449's earlier Xiongnu-civil-war advice, showing Xiao as a legitimacy-minded strategist before the later Huhanye protocol scene.
- Added Hanji 465's transition-office baseline: Xiao is one of Xuan's entrusted deathbed officials before the later Yuan-era conflict.
