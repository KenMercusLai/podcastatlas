## Overview

萧望之 / Xiao Wangzhi enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-501-hanyuandi-jing-ba-taijian-dang-fuqin-lml1qwuw3iujobnltbjymeyojd4g|Hanji 501]] as [[HanYuandi|汉元帝]]'s teacher and an early opponent of Yuan's favor toward [[ShiXianWesternHan|石显]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-462-kanbi-dayuebing-xiongnu-xianghan-changmian-you-duo-niu-lnfkacjnat32wi-akpwdggvljkxk|Hanji 462]] adds an earlier high-statecraft layer: under [[EmperorXuanOfHan|汉宣帝]], Xiao argues that [[HuhanyeChanyuWesternHan|呼韩邪单于]] should be received as an elevated guest rather than ranked below internal kings, preserving both Han magnanimity and future diplomatic flexibility. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-465-cong-qiutu-dao-mingjun-hanxuandi-liubingyi-shi-zenme-si-de-lguhdrq8lfw2wfykuteib-sucwyt|Hanji 465]] adds his transition-office baseline: Xuan appoints him as Qian Jiangjun Guanglu Xun and includes him among the entrusted deathbed officials. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-468-yan-duo-bi-shi-xiao-wangzhi-ruhe-yin-zui-chi-da-kui-losjg52ruc7ov6k3uvmxvhxlzvjw|Hanji 468]] adds the setup: Xiao, [[ZhouKanWesternHan|周堪]], [[LiuXiang|刘向 / 刘更生]], and Jin Chang form a Confucian adviser bloc; Xiao directly attacks Zhongshu eunuch power; and he initially encourages [[ZhengPengWesternHan|郑朋]] after Zheng accuses the Xu-Shi outer-relative side. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-469-weishenme-junzi-dou-buguo-xiaoren-kankan-xiaowangzhi-de-xiachang-lj8iji-c3scj6vznntavm6awolho|Hanji 469]] adds the first-stage fall: Xiao misjudges Zheng, cuts him off too late, and is pulled into a Tingwei-custody and pardon sequence that damages his reputation. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-470-xiao-wangzhi-shasi-wode-chule-taijian-haiyou-wode-yufu-lof1c5nctali_zsy0hotqyocjgoe|Hanji 470]] then gives the fuller Chuyuan 2 death case: Xiao is restored to honor, but his son [[XiaoJiWesternHan|萧吉]] asks for vindication, [[HongGongWesternHan|弘恭]] and Shi Xian turn the request into a punitive arrest, and Xiao commits suicide after his residence is surrounded.

## Current Profile

Hanji 462 and Hanji 465 show why Xiao's later loss matters: before the Yuan-era factional struggle, he is already a high-level adviser trusted with difficult frontier protocol and succession transition. His Huhanye advice is flexible rather than rigid. By treating the chanyu as a guest above princes, Xiao lets Han stage strength and generosity without pretending Xiongnu has become an ordinary domestic subject. Hanji 501 then treats him as a high-capacity but temperamentally vulnerable counterweight to Shi Xian. The episode says Shi Xian regards him as dangerous because Xiao has ability and will not easily spare people he dislikes. Hanji 468-470 fill in the earlier case behind that summary: Xiao's anti-eunuch memorial threatens Zhongshu power, his circle rewards Zheng Peng's anti-Shi-Gao accusations with encouragement, then that same attachment line becomes a breach point through Zheng Peng, the Tingwei procedure, and a face-saving pardon. Yuan later grants Xiao Guannei Hou status and contemplates high office, yet the hostile inner-court field remains intact.

The current source set makes Xiao's death a shared-failure case. Hong Gong and Shi Xian exploit Xiao's pride and Yuan's uneasy responsibility for the earlier case; Xiao Ji's vindication petition gives them an opening; Yuan allows a "temporary lesson" to become a public humiliation; Xiao's wife urges endurance, but [[ZhuYunWesternHan|朱云]] counsels suicide; Xiao chooses reputation over survival. The host still grants Xiao major status as an imperial teacher, Confucian minister, and Qilin Pavilion figure, but also stresses earlier conduct against Zhang Chang, Han Yanshou, and Bing Ji to keep him from becoming a spotless martyr.

## Key Characteristics

- Xuan-era protocol adviser and Yuan-era teacher whose authority makes him an early opponent of Shi Xian's favor.
- Leader within a Confucian adviser bloc that tries to guide Yuan through ancient institutions and to push eunuchs out of Zhongshu.
- Presented as capable and unwilling to let disliked actors off easily.
- Restored to honor under Yuan but still exposed because hostile inner-court actors control access and procedure.
- Poorly served by late personnel boundary-setting: he first encourages Zheng Peng's anti-Shi-Gao attack, then identifies Zheng as corrupt only after Zheng has entered the circle and gathered usable information.
- Vulnerable because pride, moral reputation, and public humiliation make him easier for Hong Gong and Shi Xian to manipulate.
- His death marks the weakening of organized resistance to Shi Xian in Yuan's court while also showing the limits of reputation-first politics.

## Qualifications

This page is source-scoped to Hanji 501, Hanji 470, Hanji 469, Hanji 468, Hanji 465, and Hanji 462. It does not settle Xiao Wangzhi's complete career, exact legal case, factional network, responsibility distribution among Zheng Peng, Shi Xian, Hong Gong, Yuan, Zhu Yun, Xiao Ji, and Xiao himself, or the historical record behind the host's negative references to Xiao's earlier conduct toward Zhang Chang, Han Yanshou, and Bing Ji. Hanji 462's praise of Xiao's frontier protocol is preserved alongside, not replaced by, later sources' criticism of his pride and political rigidity.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 465's transition-office baseline: Xiao is one of Xuan's entrusted deathbed officials before the later Yuan-era conflict.
- Added Hanji 462's Huhanye protocol recommendation, showing Xiao's capacity for flexible frontier statecraft before the later court struggle.
