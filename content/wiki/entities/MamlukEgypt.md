# Mamluk Egypt

## Overview

Mamluk Egypt was the late-eighteenth-century governing order in which Egypt remained nominally Ottoman while a military elite rooted in a slave-soldier system exercised effective local power.

## Current Profile

The episode introduces the Mamluks mainly as the political and military order encountered by the French invasion. Their cavalry confronted a modern French army near Giza, and defeat at the Battle of the Pyramids opened Cairo while surviving forces retreated up the Nile.

## Key Characteristics

- Combined nominal [[OttomanEmpire|Ottoman]] sovereignty with effective local military rule.
- Drew elite identity from a slave-soldier institutional tradition.
- Relied visibly on cavalry in the episode's battle comparison.
- Was displaced from Cairo by French victory without representing Egyptian society as a whole.

## Qualifications

This is a campaign-centered profile, not a full social or institutional history. The episode's modern-army-versus-cavalry contrast risks compressing Mamluk organization, Egyptian participation, and Ottoman authority.
