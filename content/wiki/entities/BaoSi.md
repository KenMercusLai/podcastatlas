# 褒姒 / Bao Si

## Overview
褒姒 / Bao Si is the Western Zhou consort whose later memory is dominated by the “一笑亡国” and “烽火戏诸侯” stories around [[ZhouYouwang|周幽王]], although the complete familiar beacon-fire narrative is not present in the earliest bounded accounts.

## Current Profile
[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-601-2-fenghuo-xi-zhuhou-yixiao-tianxia-wang-baosi-shi-hefang-yaonie-lkirbrvonyiyaousoblcd|Hanji 601-2]] presents Bao Si less as a recoverable court actor than as a blurred figure produced by legend, moral blame, and dynastic-collapse storytelling. It retells a supernatural birth account from the [[LienuZhuan|《列女传》]] tradition, then explicitly warns that this material is myth-like and should not be treated as firm history.

A narrative-layering account in [[17-dongxifang-de-changcheng-lvlaqbfmfxbvytpsimiknt0km-xp]] separates the succession conflict and Quanrong invasion from a later false-alarm story, distinguishes an earlier drum version from the beacon-fire version in Sima Qian's narrative, and connects that substitution to the mature Qin-Han signaling system. Bao Si's profile therefore remains intentionally qualified: neither source establishes that she personally caused the dynasty's fall.

## Key Characteristics
- Legendary consort whose public memory is attached to the fall of Western Zhou.
- Figure filtered through mythic birth narrative, abandonment, adoption, and ominous signs.
- Target of a gendered "妖姬" blame frame that the episode repeats but does not fully endorse.
- Central figure in a false-alarm legend whose drum and beacon-fire forms belong to different recoverable narrative layers.
- Historically obscure person whose moral responsibility remains source-scoped and disputed.

## Evidence
- **Legendary consort:** [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-601-2-fenghuo-xi-zhuhou-yixiao-tianxia-wang-baosi-shi-hefang-yaonie-lkirbrvonyiyaousoblcd]] says Bao Si is remembered through Zhou Youwang's attempt to win her smile and the later collapse at Lishan.
- **Mythic birth narrative:** [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-601-2-fenghuo-xi-zhuhou-yixiao-tianxia-wang-baosi-shi-hefang-yaonie-lkirbrvonyiyaousoblcd]] retells the dragon-saliva box, black-lizard, miraculous pregnancy, abandonment, and Bao adoption story while marking it as myth-like.
- **Gendered blame frame:** [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-601-2-fenghuo-xi-zhuhou-yixiao-tianxia-wang-baosi-shi-hefang-yaonie-lkirbrvonyiyaousoblcd]] groups Bao Si with Mei Xi, Da Ji, and Li Ji as “四大妖姬,” but its closing qualification keeps the label from becoming a settled causal claim.
- **Narrative layering:** [[17-dongxifang-de-changcheng-lvlaqbfmfxbvytpsimiknt0km-xp]] says the complete false-beacon story is absent from the earliest bounded history, identifies a drum-alarm intermediate version, and places the beacon substitution in later historiography.
- **Historical obscurity:** [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-601-2-fenghuo-xi-zhuhou-yixiao-tianxia-wang-baosi-shi-hefang-yaonie-lkirbrvonyiyaousoblcd]] and [[17-dongxifang-de-changcheng-lvlaqbfmfxbvytpsimiknt0km-xp]] preserve the Western Zhou crisis while refusing to make Bao Si a transparent cause of dynastic collapse.

## Qualifications
Both sources are popular-history episodes. Hanji 601-2 itself says the supernatural biography “当不得真,” while the wall episode provides a compressed reception history rather than a complete textual stemma. Bao Si's precise origin, agency at court, role in alarm stories, the dating and transmission of each motif, and her fate after the Quanrong attack remain source-scoped.

## What Changed
- Added the drum-to-beacon narrative sequence and its technological-historical context.
- Strengthened the separation between the Western Zhou crisis and Bao Si's later causal blame.
