## Overview

赵禹 / Zhao Yu is presented as a clean, capable, and severe Western Han official whose independence from private requests becomes both the basis of imperial trust and a warning about judgment without flexibility. His later appointment to interrogate former legal collaborator [[ZhangTangWesternHan|张汤]] makes that severe reputation politically consequential before any questioning is described.

## Current Profile

The episode places Zhao Yu first in capital offices and then in [[ZhouYafu|周亚夫]]'s service. Zhou reportedly doubts his suitability for major responsibility because he uses legal texts strictly and treats both self and others harshly. [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] later admires his writing, appoints him to censorial and court posts, and pairs him with [[ZhangTangWesternHan|张汤]] to revise law.

Zhao Yu explains his refusal of banquets, reciprocal hospitality, and requests as a way to preserve independent action. The source therefore does not reduce him to corruption or selfishness: it treats clean distance as a real safeguard while arguing that principle without listening, context, or proportion can become severe and self-defeating.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-339-2-yi-si-sharen-kuli-zhangtang-shi-ruhe-zuodao-de-luq3jaa9iruhiqiqlduewbj-luca|Hanji 339-2]] later casts Zhao as the interrogator chosen after Zhang refuses eight accusations. Zhang's immediate despair depends on shared professional knowledge: because the two had revised law together and both were remembered as cruel officials, he expects Zhao to use coercive methods that he himself understands. The episode describes Zhang's resulting suicide, not an interrogation actually conducted by Zhao.

Zhao's late career qualifies the earlier picture of unchanging rigidity. He is described as harsher than the other Nine Ministers while serving as shaofu, then as becoming gentler and more accommodating toward colleagues in old age. After Wudi demotes him to chancellor of Yan and later removes him over a minor matter, he returns home and dies there. This supplies a source-reported safe-exit outcome, but not proof that personality change alone caused it. ## Key Characteristics

- Western Han official identified by the episode as a well-known cruel official.
- Praised within the story for cleanliness, competence, fairness, and incisive writing.
- Distrusted by Zhou Yafu for strict legal interpretation and harshness toward self and others.
- Chosen by Han Wudi for office and legal revision with Zhang Tang.
- Deliberately cuts off hospitality and requests to protect independence from relational influence.
- Chosen to investigate Zhang Tang, whose fear of Zhao's methods precedes his suicide.
- Used by the host as an example of integrity turning into rigid, one-sided action, then reportedly softening in old age and surviving dismissal to die at home.

## Qualifications

The transcripts render his name as “赵鱼” and “赵瑜”; this page normalizes both to 赵禹 from the described Western Han context. The office sequence, legal-revision wording, “一意孤行” etymology, eight charges, expected interrogation methods, late personality change, four-year interval, Yan chancellorship, dismissal cause, and death circumstances remain episode-attributed rather than independently verified. Hanji 339-2 establishes the appointment and Zhang's fearful inference, not that Zhao actually tortured or even questioned him. Refusing private requests can protect impartiality, so the sources support a qualification about inflexibility, not a conclusion that independence itself is wrong. Zhao's survival also does not prove that softening alone caused a safe exit.
