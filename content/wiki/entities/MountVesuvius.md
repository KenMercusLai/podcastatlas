# Mount Vesuvius

## Overview

Mount Vesuvius is the active volcano dominating the [[BayOfNaples|Bay of Naples]] and responsible for the [[EruptionOfVesuvius79|79 CE eruption]] that destroyed [[Pompeii]] and [[Herculaneum]].

## Current Profile

The source places Vesuvius inside a productive Roman landscape rather than outside settled life. Its soils, scenery, and volcanic materials supported agriculture, villas, cities, ports, and underwater-setting concrete, while inhabitants interpreted earthquakes, dry springs, and unusual sights without a modern volcanic-risk model. The same fertility-risk combination persists in the episode's account of later eruptions and present-day population exposure.

## Key Characteristics

- Active Campanian volcano overlooking a dense urban, maritime, agricultural, and resort landscape.
- Source of the eruption column, pumice fall, ash, surges, and flows that produced the 79 CE catastrophe.
- Geological presence interpreted through Roman giant myths as well as observation.
- Generator of fertile land and useful volcanic material alongside recurrent destructive risk.
- Volcano with a later eruption history extending well beyond antiquity in the source.

## Evidence

- Landscape and productivity: [[355-roman-apocalypse-pompeii-79-ad-glt6664993493]] links Vesuvius to Bay of Naples agriculture, settlement, villas, ports, and volcanic concrete material.
- Eruption sequence: [[355-roman-apocalypse-pompeii-79-ad-glt6664993493]] traces column growth, pumice fall, surge, and flow to the volcano.
- Myth and warning: [[355-roman-apocalypse-pompeii-79-ad-glt6664993493]] connects buried-giant traditions, earthquakes, and drying springs to premodern interpretation of the landscape.
