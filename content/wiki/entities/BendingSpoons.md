# Bending Spoons

## Overview
Bending Spoons is a Milan-based software acquirer led by [[LucaFerrari|Luca Ferrari]]. The wiki first encountered it as the buyer of [[Airtable]] and now has Ferrari's account of a broader acquisition, integration, and permanent-ownership strategy.

## Current Profile
The current evidence presents Bending Spoons as a [[SoftwareAcquisitionOperatingPlatform]] built around [[AcquiredProductMarketFit]]. It buys established products, moves them onto shared technology and operating practices, uses small selectively hired teams, and reinvests cash flow into further acquisitions. This profile broadens the earlier cost-discipline interpretation: Ferrari says procurement savings are modest compared with product, engineering, monetization, marketing, and organization changes.

## Key Characteristics
- Acquires existing users and distribution rather than relying mainly on zero-to-one product discovery.
- Integrates acquired products through shared technology, talent, experimentation, payments, recruiting, and operating rules.
- Treats small teams, high hiring selectivity, and individual ownership as performance advantages.
- Screens for sizeable, predictable businesses with multiple improvement paths.
- Uses free cash flow and moderate debt to compound acquisitions under a long holding horizon.
- Distinguishes itself from resale-oriented private equity through deeper operational integration.

- Airtable reset: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-googles-ai-brain-drain-spacexs-huge-quarter-airtables-90-collapse-us-data-fuels-china-ai-42362555]] frames the company as a disciplined acquirer that could simplify Airtable's cost structure and restore [[ProductLedGrowth|product-led growth]].
- Founding and acquisition thesis: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-luca-ferrari-bending-spoons-ceo-the-40k-origin-story-buying-product-market-fit]] traces the company from a failed AI startup and $40,000 restart to the decision to buy existing traction.
- Operating platform: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-luca-ferrari-bending-spoons-ceo-the-40k-origin-story-buying-product-market-fit]] describes more than 50 shared technologies, cross-product talent mobility, small teams, and limited dependence on customer-facing cross-promotion.
- Capital and ownership: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-luca-ferrari-bending-spoons-ceo-the-40k-origin-story-buying-product-market-fit]] provides the company's screening, cash-flow reinvestment, leverage, and permanent-integration rationale.

## Qualifications
The detailed operating and financial account comes from Ferrari and supportive interviewers rather than independent evaluation. The sources do not establish representative customer outcomes, failed integrations, the effect of workforce reductions, or whether historical returns will persist. Reported valuation, leverage, returns, staffing, application, revenue, and deal figures remain source-scoped.
