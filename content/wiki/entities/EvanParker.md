# Evan Parker

## Overview

Evan Parker is a legal-talent analyst whom [[the-tortoise-and-the-hare-the-tortoise-and-the-hare|The Tortoise and the Hare]] presents as applying a “Moneyball” method to law-firm hiring: begin with successful practitioners and work backward to identify which applicant traits actually predict performance.

## Current Profile

Parker argues that firms collect extensive candidate information but still default to elite-school pedigree, conventional interviews, and resemblance to existing partners. In the episode's account, law-school prestige repeatedly lacks meaningful predictive power, interview ratings perform roughly like chance and can be negatively associated with retention, and some less conventional histories—such as blue-collar work followed by later academic success—can carry useful information.

His contribution is not a universal hiring score. It is a demand that employers validate proxies against outcomes and look for traits their inherited process systematically overlooks.

## Key Characteristics

- Evaluates hiring criteria against later professional performance rather than institutional reputation alone.
- Challenges T14 attendance as a reliable predictor of law-firm success.
- Questions conventional interview ratings as stable performance evidence.
- Looks for less prestigious but potentially informative career and educational trajectories.
- Treats hiring as an empirical prediction problem while leaving role and firm context relevant.

## Evidence

### Prestige and performance

- [[the-tortoise-and-the-hare-the-tortoise-and-the-hare]] reports Parker's finding that T14 attendance repeatedly appeared insignificant in firm analyses.

### Interview validity

- [[the-tortoise-and-the-hare-the-tortoise-and-the-hare]] attributes to Parker the claim that conventional ratings were approximately coin-flip predictors and could be negatively related to retention.

### Alternative signals

- [[the-tortoise-and-the-hare-the-tortoise-and-the-hare]] reports that blue-collar experience followed by academic success predicted value in some settings and that successful rainmakers came from varied educational paths.

## Qualifications

The supplied summary does not include Parker's datasets, outcome definitions, models, effect sizes, firms, controls, uncertainty, or independent replication. Findings described as recurring may still vary by employer, role, cohort, and performance measure. Evidence-based hiring can replace one opaque shortcut with another unless criteria, validation, and governance remain inspectable.

## What Changed

- Established Parker's outcome-backward method for evaluating legal hiring signals.
- Added episode-scoped challenges to school prestige and interview ratings.
- Preserved the limits created by unavailable methods and firm-specific data.
