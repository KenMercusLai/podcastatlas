# José Ángel Pereira

## Overview
José Ángel Pereira is a Venezuelan oil executive and former [[Citgo]] president and CEO whose career tracks the major swings in [[Venezuela]]'s oil politics. In [[trump-drinks-venezuelas-milkshake]], he functions as both technical witness and personal-risk witness for the country's cycle of foreign investment, national control, reopening, and backlash.

## Current Profile
Pereira is presented as a third-generation Venezuelan oilman. His grandfather arrived as a Dutch drilling engineer during the early foreign-investment era, his parents trained in petroleum engineering in the United States before working for [[PDVSA]], and Pereira himself worked on the 1990s Apertura before later managing nationalized oil companies and leading [[Citgo]]. His imprisonment after a 2017 trip to Caracas turns the oil-sector story from institutional risk into direct executive risk.

## Key Characteristics
- Biographical bridge across Venezuela's foreign-concession, nationalization, Apertura, renationalization, and Citgo eras.
- Technical and managerial witness for why heavy-oil production requires sustained capital, technology, and operational discipline.
- Qualified critic of both sides of the oil-sovereignty cycle: he sees foreign investment as useful but criticizes imposed or constitutionally suspect terms.
- Personal-risk witness whose imprisonment shows how oil executives can become bargaining chips in U.S.-Venezuela conflict.

## Evidence
Biographical bridge:
- [[trump-drinks-venezuelas-milkshake]] traces Pereira's family from foreign-run drilling and oil-company institutions through PDVSA professionalization, then follows Pereira through Apertura, nationalized-company management, Citgo leadership, and imprisonment.

Technical and managerial witness:
- [[trump-drinks-venezuelas-milkshake]] uses Pereira to explain that oil fields decline without continuing investment and that foreign companies supplied heavy-oil dilution and transport technology during the 1990s opening.

Qualified critic:
- [[trump-drinks-venezuelas-milkshake]] records Pereira's view that Chávez had some valid criticisms of PDVSA and foreign contracts, while also treating the new Trump-era deal as economically useful but politically and legally troubling because it was imposed.

Personal-risk witness:
- [[trump-drinks-venezuelas-milkshake]] recounts Pereira's 2017 arrest in Caracas, months in solitary confinement, nearly five years in prison, and release through a prisoner swap, making personal safety part of the oil-investment calculus.

## Qualifications
The current profile is based on one source that foregrounds Pereira's perspective. It should not be treated as a complete biography, a legal account of his Citgo case, or a representative survey of Venezuelan views on foreign oil investment.
