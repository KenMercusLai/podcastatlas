# Louis the Pious

## Overview

Louis the Pious was [[Charlemagne]]'s son and imperial successor, whose inheritance preserved the title for one generation but whose sons' later partition exposed the fragility of Carolingian unity.

## Current Profile

Charlemagne crowned Louis joint emperor before his own death and did so without papal involvement, asserting dynastic control over succession. Louis then inherited both the realm and imperial title in 814.

After Louis, partible inheritance and conflict among his sons produced the 843 Treaty of Verdun. The episode uses that settlement to explain western, eastern, and central divisions that contributed to later France, Germany, and Lotharingia, while avoiding the claim that those modern states appeared fully formed in the ninth century.

## Key Characteristics

- Sole surviving son who inherited Charlemagne's empire and title.
- Joint emperor crowned by his father without papal participation.
- Transitional ruler between personal Carolingian unity and partition among heirs.
- Father of the rulers whose 843 settlement divided the imperial realm.

## Evidence

- Managed succession - [[525-charlemagne-emperor-of-the-west-part-3-glt6103469677]] says Charlemagne crowned Louis joint emperor before dying in 814.
- Partition after Louis - [[525-charlemagne-emperor-of-the-west-part-3-glt6103469677]] connects his sons' conflict and the Treaty of Verdun to the empire's western, eastern, and central divisions.

## Qualifications

The source compresses Louis's reign into the succession and partition story. It does not provide a full account of his government, religious policy, family conflicts, depositions, restoration, or the detailed formation of the Verdun settlement.

## What Changed

- Created the profile to distinguish Charlemagne's controlled succession from the fragmentation that followed Louis.
