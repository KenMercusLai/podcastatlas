## Overview
陇山 / 龙山 / Longshan enters the wiki as the northwest China mountain system that [[YanHaijun|严海军 / 阎海军]] treats as an east-west historical hinge. The source uses 龙山 in the body while the title and terms such as 陇坂 point to 陇山; this page uses a disambiguated key to avoid confusing it with other Longshan subjects.

## Current Profile
In [[499-fanyue-longban-yanhaijun-tan-zuowei-gudai-zhongguo-dongxi-hudong-zhouxin-de-longshan-lrm9qbtncvuh2utckq7hogpoxhay|499 翻越陇坂]], Longshan is described as a mountain system roughly 240 kilometers long and not especially high, with a highest point around 2,900 meters in the source's account. Its importance comes from position rather than height: it sits between [[GuanzhongRegion|关中]], Longyou, [[Gansu|甘肃]], Hexi, and the [[WesternRegionsProtectorate|Western Regions]] route world.

The source presents Longshan as both barrier and passage. When a central dynasty is strong, it can function as part of a defensive screen for the Guanzhong-centered order; when dynastic capacity weakens, it can become a border condition close enough to threaten Chang'an. The same geography also supports movement: Qin eastward expansion, Han Wudi's western strategy, Silk Road passage, Guanlong roads, and modern railway, expressway, national-road, and pipeline routes are all treated as evidence that the corridor remains convenient across technological periods.

## Key Characteristics
- Modest-height but high-consequence mountain system between Guanzhong and the northwest route world.
- Place where barrier, pass, road, and state-security roles overlap.
- Geographic hinge for Qin eastward movement and Western Han westward expansion in the source's interpretation.
- Terrain remembered through names such as 陇坂, 陇坻, 陇首, Liupan Mountain, Guan Mountain, and Xiao Longshan.
- Fieldwork object whose old-road traces, villages, place names, and poetry help turn historical geography into embodied evidence.

## Qualifications
This page records Longshan as used in one source, not a complete physical geography entry. The source's 龙山 / 陇山 wording is normalized here because the episode context, title, and route vocabulary point to the Gansu-Shaanxi Longshan system. Specific place-name legends, Zhang Qian associations, and ancient road routes remain source-scoped.
