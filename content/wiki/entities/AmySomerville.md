# Amy Somerville

## Overview
Amy Somerville is a psychologist represented here through a [[HiddenBrain]] episode on regret, counterfactual thinking, responsibility, rumination, and decision strategy.

## Current Profile
Somerville presents regret as potentially hopeful because comparing reality with an imagined alternative can reveal what matters and change later behavior. Her account is functional rather than uniformly positive: review becomes harmful when it turns into intrusive repetition without new understanding, and counterfactual thought can distort responsibility when a person treats one imagined intervention as proof that they controlled a complex tragedy.

She also distinguishes the functions of comparison. Upward counterfactuals can expose an improvement path, downward counterfactuals can provide relief, and satisficing can reduce prospective regret by ending search once an option meets meaningful standards.

## Key Characteristics
- Treats regret as a learning signal rather than an emotion that is inherently useful or harmful.
- Separates constructive reflection from repetitive, intrusive rumination.
- Connects imagined alternatives to learning, comfort, causal judgment, and perceived control.
- Balances personal accountability with situational and multi-actor causation.
- Uses satisficing to limit maximizing, comparison, and choice overload.
