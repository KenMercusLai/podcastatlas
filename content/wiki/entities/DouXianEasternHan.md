## Overview

窦宪 / Dou Xian is the Eastern Han outer-relative powerholder whose wiki profile now begins before Emperor He's accession. Hanji 752 shows him already using palace-backed prestige under [[EmperorZhangOfHan|Emperor Zhang]] to seize [[QinshuiPrincessEasternHan|Qinshui Princess]]'s estate and suppress truthful reporting; Zhangdi returns the estate but does not punish him. Hanji 756 then shows [[ZhengHongEasternHan|Zheng Hong]] warning that Dou's power is too great. Hanji 759-767 carry the same danger through child-emperor regency, private revenge, the Liu Chang murder, campaign command, Jiluoshan-Yanran achievement, family abuse, Yu Chu policy, palace removal, and post-Dou aftershocks.

## Current Profile

Hanji 752 supplies the new seed layer. Dou Xian and [[DouDuEasternHan|Dou Du]] are already close to the palace as Empress Dou's brothers, receiving favor and gathering guests. Fifth Lun warns Zhangdi that this access is dangerous. Dou then forces a low-price acquisition of Qinshui Princess's estate and tries to keep the answer hidden when Zhangdi asks about it. The emperor's rebuke is severe, but the lack of formal punishment creates a durable [[KnownMisconductNonPunishment|deterrence failure]].

Hanji 756 shows the same problem becoming a ministerial warning. Zheng Hong repeatedly tells Zhangdi that Dou Xian's power is excessive, then is counteraccused after impeachment material against Dou-linked officials leaks. Zheng's final memorial frames Dou as a state-survival danger, so the source treats Dou risk as visible before the regency crisis.

Hanji 759-760 show escalation after Zhangdi's death. Child [[EmperorHeOfHan|Emperor He]] succeeds under [[EmpressDowagerDouHeLateHan|Empress Dowager Dou]], Dou gains privileged access, attracts [[CuiYinEasternHan|Cui Yin]] and [[BanGuEasternHan|Ban Gu]], uses [[DengBiaoEasternHan|Deng Biao]] as a formal procedure channel, converts old resentment into private revenge, murders [[LiuChangDuxiangHouEasternHan|Duxiang marquis Liu Chang]], frames [[LiuGangEasternHan|Liu Gang]], and then turns capital-crime liability into a Northern Xiongnu campaign request.

Hanji 761-767 preserve the complexity. Officials oppose the campaign on mourning, farming, logistics, and legitimacy grounds, and [[ZhiShouEasternHan|Zhi Shou]] is nearly killed after refusing Dou's private request. The campaign nevertheless succeeds at [[JiluoshanBattleLateHan|Jiluoshan]] and is commemorated by [[YanranInscription|Yanran]]. The later sources then show rank elevation, household and guard-command abuse, appointment capture, Yu Chu recognition despite objections, Emperor He's palace operation against the Dou bloc, and aftershocks including Ban Gu's prison death and frontier arrangements unraveling.

## Key Characteristics

- Outer-relative actor whose coercive property abuse and truth suppression are visible already under Zhangdi.
- Protected kin whose early non-punishment weakens deterrence before later regency power matures.
- Powerholder who attracts talent and builds procedural channels while using authority for private revenge.
- Murder-case offender who converts death-penalty liability into frontier military command.
- Capable commander whose Jiluoshan-Yanran achievement remains real despite the political origin of the campaign.
- Family-bloc leader whose brothers' mansions, guard commands, household abuse, and private request channels make court capture concrete.
- Frontier-policy sponsor whose Yu Chu recognition plan prevails despite credibility, fiscal, and alliance objections.

## Qualifications

This page remains bounded to the listed source notes and does not reconstruct Dou Xian's full biography, exact campaign route, or complete office chronology. Hanji 752 adds a Zhangdi-era estate and truth-suppression case, not a claim that this single non-punishment mechanically caused every later Dou abuse. Hanji 763 and Hanji 764 associate Northern Xiongnu displacement with later Eurasian migration pressure; the page treats that as a qualified source association rather than proof that Dou Xian directly destroyed Rome. Hanji 766 contains a local tension around the Dou brothers' deaths and Dou Gui's survival; the wiki records [[DouGuiEasternHan|Dou Gui]] as the specific resolved mercy case.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 752 as the earliest current Dou Xian layer: palace access, Fifth Lun's warning, Qinshui Princess estate coercion, truth suppression, and non-punishment.
- Reframed Dou's later rise as an escalation from known but incompletely sanctioned misconduct rather than a danger visible only after Emperor He's accession.
- Preserved the Zheng Hong warning, Liu Chang murder, punishment-to-campaign conversion, Jiluoshan-Yanran achievement, Yu Chu policy, palace removal, and post-Dou aftermath.
- Added explicit contrast between military competence and accountability failure.
