## Overview

窦宪 / Dou Xian now appears as the He-era outer-relative powerholder whose dominance first requires a palace operation and then produces a wide aftermath. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-766-lishi-jiaogei-ni-weishenme-yao-guyi-fa-piqi-lrs-mguyfmnxe970hdwjvmevl8oc|Hanji 766]] supplies the pre-collapse layer: household intimidation, anti-Dou ministerial resistance, a reported assassination plot, and [[EmperorHeOfHan|Emperor He]] stripping Dou's general-in-chief seal. Hanji 767 then shows the consequences: [[LiHeEasternHan|Li He]] avoids a gift connection, [[BanGuEasternHan|Ban Gu]] dies during the Dou-family cleanup, and [[YuChuNorthernXiongnu|Yu Chu]] abandons a Northern Xiongnu return arrangement after Dou loses power.

## Current Profile

Hanji 766 presents Dou Xian before and during the collapse. His outer-relative status and Northern Xiongnu military achievement have made his household a shadow authorization center: new governors and two-thousand-shi officials visit the Dou residence before taking office. Opposition from [[YuanAnEasternHan|Yuan An]], [[RenKuiEasternHan|Ren Kui]], [[ZhouRongEasternHan|Zhou Rong]], and [[DingHongEasternHan|Ding Hong]] shows that the court can still name the danger, but the emperor cannot safely reach outside ministers and must plan through [[ZhengZhongEasternHan|Zheng Zhong]] and [[LiuQingQingheKing|Liu Qing]].

The same source makes the fall operational. Emperor He closes palace gates, mobilizes palace guards and northern army command, executes immediate palace conspirators, removes Dou Xian's general-in-chief seal, demotes him to Champion Marquis, and sends the Dou brothers to their fiefs. [[ZhangPuEasternHan|Zhang Pu]] later persuades the emperor to spare [[DouGuiEasternHan|Dou Gui]], making the cleanup severe but not wholly indiscriminate.

Hanji 767 then presents the social and frontier aftershocks. Commanderies and kingdoms had been sending gifts when Dou took a concubine, and the Hanzhong administrator is afraid not to participate. Li He judges Dou as arrogant and unvirtuous, predicts disaster, and delays the gift mission until Dou's family falls. Ban Gu is swept into prison after an older private grievance becomes actionable during the arrest of Dou-family guests. On the frontier, Dou had installed Yu Chu as a Northern Xiongnu remnant leader and planned to escort him back to the royal court, but Yu Chu repudiates the arrangement after learning Dou has been removed.

## Key Characteristics

- Outer-relative and military powerholder whose household becomes an unofficial authorization center.
- Target of ministerial opposition, omen-coded warning, and Emperor He's secret palace planning.
- Fallen general-in-chief whose seal removal turns formal title into unusable authority.
- Patron whose collapse triggers a purge of officials and guests linked to him.
- Court danger node through which private grievance can become public punishment.
- Northern-frontier actor whose earlier Yu Chu arrangement unravels after his removal.
- Negative reference point for judging strategic timing, from Zhou Rong's deterrent anger to Li He's delayed gift mission.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 766 and Hanji 767 and still does not reconstruct Dou Xian's full biography, earlier Northern Xiongnu campaigns, family rise, or relationship with Empress Dowager Dou. Hanji 766 contains a local tension: it compresses the Dou brothers as secretly forced to die after reaching their fiefs and later says Dou Gui survives after Zhang Pu's memorial. The wiki records Dou Gui's survival as the specific resolved case.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 766 as the pre-collapse and palace-operation layer before Hanji 767's aftermath.
- Reframed Dou Xian from a mostly fallen patron into the active outer-relative threat that Emperor He removes.
- Added the distinction between severe bloc liquidation and Zhang Pu's evidence-specific defense of Dou Gui.
