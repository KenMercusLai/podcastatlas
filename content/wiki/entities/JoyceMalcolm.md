# Joyce Malcolm

## Overview

Joyce Malcolm is a constitutional historian whose research on the English right to arms helped make [[SirJohnKnight]]'s 1686 acquittal important to modern American gun-rights scholarship and litigation.

## Current Profile

According to [[guns-part-1-the-sudden-celebrity-of-sir-john-knight]], Malcolm encountered the short reported account of *Rex v. Knight* while researching historical casebooks for *To Keep and Bear Arms*. She interpreted the acquittal as evidence that English common law did not impose a broad prohibition on individuals peacefully carrying ordinary firearms.

Malcolm contests the episode's revisionist reconstruction. She emphasizes a reported judicial statement that gentlemen could carry arms if the weapons were not unusual or dangerous, doubts that Bristol broadly prohibited carrying, and argues that modern British assumptions about state protection can make historians less attentive to an older right of self-defense.

The disagreement leaves her as both an important transmitter of the Knight precedent and an advocate for one disputed interpretation. The supplied source does not include her full scholarship, underlying citations, or a complete exchange with the historians she challenges.

## Key Characteristics

- Constitutional historian specializing in the historical right to keep and bear arms.
- Researcher who recovered *Rex v. Knight* from later legal casebooks for modern debate.
- Interpreter of Knight's acquittal as evidence against a broad ban on peaceful public carry.
- Defender of a reported distinction between ordinary arms and unusual or dangerous weapons.
- Critic of the revisionist claim that Knight's conduct and Bristol's local rules undermine the public-carry interpretation.
