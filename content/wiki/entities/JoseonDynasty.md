# Joseon Dynasty

## Overview

The Joseon dynasty is the Korean kingdom dated by [[266-south-korea-the-riddle-of-hwang-jini-glt1910695360]] from 1392 to 1897 and used as the Confucian political and social setting for [[HwangJini|Hwang Jini]], the [[Gisaeng|gisaeng institution]], and [[SijoPoetry|sijo poetry]].

## Current Profile

The episode contrasts Joseon with the preceding Buddhist Goryeo kingdom and stresses Chinese cultural influence, but it does not attempt a full dynastic history. Its contribution is narrower: Joseon combined restrictive hereditary and gender hierarchy with state-supported artistic training for some low-status women whose work moved through elite social spaces.

That contradiction should not be read as equality. Gisaeng education and cultural mobility operated inside structures of sexual service, patronage, hereditary status, and limited career options. Hwang's later fame shows how a person constrained by that order could nonetheless become part of Korean literary memory.

## Key Characteristics

- Korean dynasty presented as lasting from 1392 to 1897.
- Political and cultural order strongly associated in the episode with Confucianism and Chinese influence.
- Setting for hereditary social hierarchy and gendered restrictions.
- State context in which gisaeng could receive artistic training while remaining low-status service workers.
- Literary setting for Hwang Jini's sijo and later cultural reputation.

## Evidence

- Chronology and ideology: [[266-south-korea-the-riddle-of-hwang-jini-glt1910695360]] dates Joseon to 1392-1897 and contrasts its Confucian orientation with Goryeo Buddhism.
- Social structure: [[266-south-korea-the-riddle-of-hwang-jini-glt1910695360]] places gisaeng in the chonmin hereditary stratum.
- Cultural institution: [[266-south-korea-the-riddle-of-hwang-jini-glt1910695360]] describes government training in music, dance, poetry, needlework, and other arts.
- Literary afterlife: [[266-south-korea-the-riddle-of-hwang-jini-glt1910695360]] locates Hwang and gisaeng sijo within lasting Korean cultural memory.

## Qualifications

This profile derives from a short episode centered on Hwang Jini, not from a general history of Joseon institutions. The chronology, social categories, government-training system, Chinese influence, and gender comparisons are retained as source-scoped summaries requiring specialist expansion.
