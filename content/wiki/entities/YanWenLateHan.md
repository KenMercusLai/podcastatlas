## Overview

阎温 / Yan Wen is the Ji official sent by [[WeiKangLateHan|韦康]] through [[MaChao|马超]]'s siege lines to request help from [[XiahouYuan|夏侯渊]]. Captured before completing the mission, he turns a forced surrender appeal into a public claim that relief is imminent and is killed after refusing to reverse that message.

## Current Profile

Hanji 1136 presents Yan Wen through one bounded siege episode. He swims out of Ji at night but leaves water traces that allow Ma Chao's troops to find him. Ma Chao then orders him to announce that no relief will come and to persuade the city to surrender. Yan Wen instead tells the defenders that a large army will arrive within three days and urges them to hold.

The episode treats the claim as morale management rather than verified intelligence. Yan Wen does not bring relief, but he preserves resistance temporarily by denying the besieger control over the message delivered to the city. His profile is therefore one of failed operational transmission but successful symbolic resistance under captivity.

## Key Characteristics

- Ji official entrusted with a covert request for military relief.
- Attempts a night escape by water through Ma Chao's siege lines.
- Is captured after physical traces reveal his route.
- Reverses a coerced surrender message into encouragement for continued defense.
- Is portrayed as accepting death rather than publicly betraying his superior and the defenders.

## Qualifications

The current profile rests on one structured popular-history summary rather than a transcript or primary-source comparison. Yan Wen's exact office, route, timing, words, access to real relief information, and death circumstances require broader verification. Calling his false arrival date successful refers only to short-term morale preservation, not completion of the relief mission or prevention of Ji's surrender.

## What Changed

- Established Yan Wen as a disambiguated late-Han entity from the Ji siege.
- Distinguished operational failure from his source-attributed success in resisting coerced messaging.
