# Dominic Sandbrook

## Overview

Dominic Sandbrook is a historian and co-host of [[TheRestIsHistory|The Rest Is History]] whose contribution to this source links [[VladimirPutin]]'s authority to post-Soviet instability, public demand for order, national grievance, and the political difficulty of ending the war in [[Ukraine]].

## Current Profile

In [[162-putins-russia-glt4575290976]], Sandbrook emphasizes the social setting in which toughness could become politically attractive. He treats the “wild” 1990s—oligarchic enrichment, collapsing living standards, poverty, and extremist politics—as essential context for understanding why economic stabilization, action against oligarchs, and martial strength supported Putin's appeal.

His endgame analysis is structural rather than predictive. A ruler who cannot admit error may prefer continued fighting, Western fatigue, or a frozen conflict to withdrawal that looks like defeat. The episode records this as contemporary interpretation in March 2022, not privileged knowledge of Russian decision-making.

## Key Characteristics

- Uses social and political context to explain why authoritarian strength can acquire popular legitimacy.
- Treats the 1990s as a formative background of disorder, inequality, grievance, and radical politics.
- Connects national humiliation and populations beyond state borders to restoration politics while marking the analogy as interpretive.
- Frames the Ukraine-war endgame through regime survival and the inability to admit error.
- Uses counterfactual and comparative history to test claims without presenting them as settled fact.

## Evidence

- Stability and grievance: [[162-putins-russia-glt4575290976]] records Sandbrook's account of post-Soviet chaos, economic recovery, masculine political imagery, and nationalist restoration.
- War exit: [[162-putins-russia-glt4575290976]] presents his argument that continued fighting, Western fatigue, or a frozen conflict may look like Putin's least bad options.
- Historical judgment: [[162-putins-russia-glt4575290976]] uses Putin to question how distance can turn conquest and urban destruction into evidence of “greatness.”

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to one conversational episode and does not summarize Sandbrook's wider books, journalism, or positions across the show. The Weimar comparison, Soviet-collapse counterfactual, public-opinion claims, and endgame possibilities are analytical prompts rather than demonstrated causal models or forecasts.
