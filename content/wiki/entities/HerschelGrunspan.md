# Herschel Grünspan

## Overview

Herschel Grünspan was a young Polish Jewish refugee living in Paris who shot German diplomat [[ErnstVomRath|Ernst vom Rath]] on 7 November 1938 after his family was expelled from [[NaziGermany|Nazi Germany]].

## Current Profile

Grünspan's parents had settled in Hanover but were caught in a German roundup after Poland moved to strip Jews abroad of citizenship. German authorities arrested them, seized their property, and transported them toward the Polish border. In Paris, Grünspan bought a revolver, entered the German embassy, and shot vom Rath five times while carrying a note framing the act as revenge for his parents and thousands of expelled Jews.

His individual act did not cause Nazi antisemitism or independently produce [[Kristallnacht]]. Hitler, Goebbels, Heydrich, party networks, and police converted vom Rath's death into a pretext for violence already consistent with an escalating program of exclusion, dispossession, coerced emigration, and war preparation.

## Key Characteristics

- He was separated from a family rendered vulnerable by conflicting citizenship and expulsion policies.
- His shooting of vom Rath was explicitly framed as retaliation for the mass expulsion of Jews.
- His act gave the Nazi regime a propaganda opportunity rather than originating its anti-Jewish program.

## Qualifications

This profile rests on one narrative-history episode and addresses Grünspan chiefly as part of the immediate Kristallnacht sequence. It does not provide a complete biography, legal history, or account of his later fate. His responsibility for the shooting should not be conflated with Nazi responsibility for the independently chosen and coordinated persecution that followed.
