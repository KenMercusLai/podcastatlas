# Minamoto no Yorimasa

## Overview
Minamoto no Yorimasa is the former samurai turned monk who joins [[PrinceMochihito]]'s anti-Taira revolt and becomes an early model for samurai death memory.

## Current Profile
[[659-dawn-of-the-samurai-bloodbath-at-the-bridge-part-2-glt3420393571]] presents Yorimasa as the military partner in Mochihito's revolt. He helps send the appeal against [[TairaNoKiyomori]], escorts Mochihito out of Kyoto, and tries to reach Nara after limited monastic support at Miidera.

At the [[UjiBridgeBattle]], Yorimasa orders bridge planks removed to slow Taira cavalry, but the defenders are overwhelmed. The episode presents his death as an archetypal samurai seppuku scene: invocation of the Buddha, a death poem, self-disembowelment, and beheading by a servant.

## Key Characteristics
- Yorimasa links Minamoto opposition, monastic identity, and anti-Taira rebellion.
- His Uji tactic turns infrastructure into a temporary defensive advantage.
- His defeat becomes more culturally important than militarily successful.
- His death scene is treated as a later model for samurai seppuku memory.

## Evidence
- Revolt role: [[659-dawn-of-the-samurai-bloodbath-at-the-bridge-part-2-glt3420393571]] names Yorimasa as Mochihito's partner in the anti-Taira plot.
- Battle role: [[659-dawn-of-the-samurai-bloodbath-at-the-bridge-part-2-glt3420393571]] describes his bridge-plank order at Uji and the Taira crossing in force.
- Death memory: [[659-dawn-of-the-samurai-bloodbath-at-the-bridge-part-2-glt3420393571]] presents his prayer, poem, abdomen cutting, and beheading as archetypal seppuku material.

## Qualifications
The source treats Yorimasa's death through literary memory; the ritual details should remain source-scoped rather than generalized to all earlier samurai practice.
