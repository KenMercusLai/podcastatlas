## Overview

南匈奴 / Southern Xiongnu is the late-Han frontier polity whose wiki profile now starts with the 158 CE [[CheerChanyu|车儿]] surrender-and-retention dispute before the later Huzheng, Qiangqu, Yufuluo, Qubei, and Huchuquan branches. Across the current sources, Southern Xiongnu politics are repeatedly shaped by Han frontier command, chanyu legitimacy, requisition pressure, and opportunistic movement through a collapsing central-plains order.

## Current Profile

The earliest chronological layer is the 158 CE Che'er crisis in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-844-ruhe-liyu-bubaizhidi-lsp49uw8atyiufrf-ryo-9ctos5z|Hanji 844]]. [[ZhangHuanLateHan|张奂]] answers a Southern Xiongnu and [[Wuhuan|Wuhuan]] frontier crisis by secretly reconciling with Wuhuan and using them against the Xiongnu side. After the Xiongnu submit, Zhang wants to detain Che'er and install the Left Guli King, but [[EmperorHuanOfHan|汉桓帝]] restores Che'er on hereditary and surrender-management grounds.

The 178 CE source layers make succession and Han intervention the recurring problem. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-903-weishenme-quan-ni-yuanyuan-xiangbao-heshi-le-lokpt93muc8kasvn44vgsjki8bom|Hanji 903]] records an earlier group-level chanyu succession marker without resolving it to a later named branch. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-914-xinren-de-ren-weihe-zong-shang-ni-zui-shen-lvwit-rzrkscn5a3h6wblntxppso|Hanji 914]] names [[HuzhengChanyu|呼征]] as successor, and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-917-ruhe-xunsu-zhaodao-shengzhi-jiaxin-de-pojudian-lk2zt5c3onvui9a4ppbowshy66tg|Hanji 917]] shows [[ZhangXiuProtectorOfXiongnu|张脩]] killing Huzheng without authorization and installing [[QiangquChanyu|羌渠]], after which the court punishes Zhang Xiu.

The 188-191 CE layers turn Southern Xiongnu into both frontier partner and central-plains armed actor. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-939-donghan-luanju-de-fengkuang-sanguo-xuqu-lthmvw8ihk96fuez9vamlryzcngy|Hanji 939]] says requisition for Han campaigning helps trigger [[XiluoSouthernXiongnu|醯落]] and [[TugeHu|屠各胡]] to kill Qiangqu and install [[YufuluoChanyu|于扶罗]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-949-lishishang-de-dongzhuo-daodi-duo-canbao-lk6idogwmc54nk1sdfc5wonyvgbb|Hanji 949]] then places Yufuluo in a stranded force-market role after the Han court cannot settle his rival-succession appeal during [[DongZhuo|董卓]]'s takeover. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-957-ta-ceng-xiongba-yifang-weihe-ruonuo-dao-zai-cesuo-zijin-lnkhah144822mc4aa9bnezklgvoo|Hanji 957]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-961-neihuang-zhizhan-caocao-chulu-fengmang-ljdlt6kiu8tg0qws-jlyej-yhgxr|Hanji 961]] preserve Yufuluo's links to [[ZhangYangLateHan|张杨]], [[YuanShao|袁绍]], [[PuyangLateHan|濮阳]], and [[CaoCao|曹操]]'s early campaign field.

The later notes preserve Southern Xiongnu utility in emperor-escort and succession contexts. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-991-zhengduo-xiandi-zhizhan-shui-neng-zuizhong-qusheng-lkbsl5uzqxouzm6mlewrnuns0x6p|Hanji 991]] has [[QubeiSouthernXiongnu|去卑]] bring cavalry into the emergency coalition protecting [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-998-baima-jiangjun-gongsunzan-cong-sanqian-baima-dao-kunshou-gucheng-lp-1l2gvbsbfnyznutdghxwwvqna|Hanji 998]] closes the current branch with Yufuluo's death, [[HuchuquanChanyu|呼厨泉]]'s succession, and stationing at Pingyang.

## Key Characteristics

- Frontier polity whose Han-facing order depends on chanyu legitimacy, submission terms, and the credibility of court recognition.
- Recurrent succession field where Han commanders and court decisions can stabilize, override, or destabilize internal leadership.
- Partner and threat at the same time: Southern Xiongnu actors appear as submitted rulers, requisitioned auxiliaries, rebels, hostages, and campaign participants.
- Vulnerable to requisition and supply pressure, especially when Han use of Southern Xiongnu cavalry makes local leaders fear repeated extraction.
- Often connected through alliances or fractures with Wuhuan, Tuge Hu, Han commanders, Baibo forces, Yuan Shao, and Cao Cao rather than as an isolated actor.
- Late-Han chronology is tracked through named leaders: Che'er, Huzheng, Qiangqu, Yufuluo, Qubei, and Huchuquan.

## Evidence

- 158 CE surrender and retention: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-844-ruhe-liyu-bubaizhidi-lsp49uw8atyiufrf-ryo-9ctos5z|Hanji 844]] says Zhang Huan fractures the Wuhuan-Xiongnu alignment, induces Xiongnu submission, and is overruled when he tries to detain Che'er.
- Succession and unauthorized intervention: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-903-weishenme-quan-ni-yuanyuan-xiangbao-heshi-le-lokpt93muc8kasvn44vgsjki8bom|Hanji 903]], [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-914-xinren-de-ren-weihe-zong-shang-ni-zui-shen-lvwit-rzrkscn5a3h6wblntxppso|Hanji 914]], and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-917-ruhe-xunsu-zhaodao-shengzhi-jiaxin-de-pojudian-lk2zt5c3onvui9a4ppbowshy66tg|Hanji 917]] show group-level succession, Huzheng's succession, and Zhang Xiu's unauthorized killing-and-installation of Qiangqu.
- Requisition backlash: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-939-donghan-luanju-de-fengkuang-sanguo-xuqu-lthmvw8ihk96fuez9vamlryzcngy|Hanji 939]] links Han military requisition, Xiluo and Tuge rebellion, Qiangqu's death, and Yufuluo's installation.
- Central-plains armed drift: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-949-lishishang-de-dongzhuo-daodi-duo-canbao-lk6idogwmc54nk1sdfc5wonyvgbb|Hanji 949]], [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-957-ta-ceng-xiongba-yifang-weihe-ruonuo-dao-zai-cesuo-zijin-lnkhah144822mc4aa9bnezklgvoo|Hanji 957]], and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-961-neihuang-zhizhan-caocao-chulu-fengmang-ljdlt6kiu8tg0qws-jlyej-yhgxr|Hanji 961]] place Yufuluo across failed appeal, Baibo association, Zhang Yang hostage movement, and Cao Cao's Neihuang campaign field.
- Emperor-escort utility and later succession: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-991-zhengduo-xiandi-zhizhan-shui-neng-zuizhong-qusheng-lkbsl5uzqxouzm6mlewrnuns0x6p|Hanji 991]] uses Qubei's cavalry in Emperor Xian's emergency coalition, and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-998-baima-jiangjun-gongsunzan-cong-sanqian-baima-dao-kunshou-gucheng-lp-1l2gvbsbfnyznutdghxwwvqna|Hanji 998]] records Yufuluo's death and Huchuquan's succession.

## Qualifications

This page remains bounded to the listed source notes and does not reconstruct a full Southern Xiongnu history. Hanji 903's succession notice stays at group level because the source does not resolve its named-chanyu identity against the later Huzheng-Qiangqu-Yufuluo branch. Hanji 844's Che'er layer is chronologically earliest but appears last in the frontmatter evidence list because the list preserves source-ingest order.
