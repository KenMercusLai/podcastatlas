# Rough Wooing

## Overview

The Rough Wooing was the English war intended to force the [[TreatyOfGreenwich1543|Treaty of Greenwich]] marriage after Scottish resistance prevented [[MaryQueenOfScots|Mary, Queen of Scots]] from being sent to England.

## Current Profile

[[HenryVIII|Henry VIII]] answered the failed settlement by ordering destructive invasion in 1544. The campaign continued beyond his death and used burning, military occupation, and repeated pressure to make Scotland surrender Mary for marriage to Edward.

The coercion did not create one uniform Scottish response. It deepened much anti-English resistance and strengthened reliance on the [[FrancoScottishAlliance|Franco-Scottish alliance]], yet some Protestants still preferred confessional alignment with England. The later English victory at [[BattleOfPinkie|Pinkie]] remained strategically insufficient because France could intervene and remove Mary beyond English reach.

## Key Characteristics

- War used to compel a dynastic marriage.
- Destructive campaign extending beyond Henry VIII's reign.
- Pressure that strengthened rather than eliminated much Scottish resistance.
- Conflict complicated by a Protestant pro-English Scottish faction.
- Military escalation ending in French intervention rather than English control of Mary.

## Qualifications

The episode provides a strategic narrative rather than a complete eight-year military history. Campaign chronology, destruction totals, local responses, force sizes, and the later label's framing remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Established the Rough Wooing as a coercive marriage war whose violence helped defeat its political purpose.

- [[BattleOfPinkie|Battle of Pinkie]] - major English victory that failed to produce submission.
