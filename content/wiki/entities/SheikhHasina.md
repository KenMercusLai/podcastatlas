# Sheikh Hasina

## Overview

Sheikh Hasina is the ousted Bangladeshi leader whose 15-year rule is represented in the bounded sources through institutional weakening, politically connected lending, violent protest suppression, exile, and continuing influence over [[Bangladesh]]'s transition.

## Current Profile

The first source places Hasina's removal in a Gen Z-led uprising that grew from campus protests into a mass movement and forced her departure to [[India]]. Her absence did not remove the transition problem: banning the [[AwamiLeague]] from the immediate election left many of its potential voters unrepresented and strained relations with India.

Her current profile also includes financial and legal consequences. The bounded evidence attributes a large share of non-performing bank loans to credit extended to government-connected figures during her rule and reports that Hasina, convicted in absentia and facing a death sentence for the violent suppression surrounding her overthrow, has said she wants to return in December.

## Key Characteristics

- Led Bangladesh for 15 years before a youth-led uprising removed her in 2024.
- Is associated in the sources with weakened institutions and violent suppression of protest.
- Remains tied to the Awami League's exclusion and the legitimacy of the successor political order.
- Is linked by the later episode to politically connected lending and the banking system's bad-loan burden.
- Lives in exile in India while her proposed return raises renewed confrontation risk.

## Qualifications

These are podcast summaries rather than court records, bank audits, or testimony from Hasina and the Awami League. The causal share of political direction in bank losses, the fairness and finality of the conviction, the precise conditions of exile, and the feasibility or timing of any return remain source-scoped.
