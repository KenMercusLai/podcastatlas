# 《论李维》 / Discourses on Livy

## Overview
《论李维》 / Discourses on Livy is Machiavelli's republican and Roman-historical text in the wiki's current Machiavelli branch.

## Current Profile
The current synthesis treats Discourses on Livy as the necessary counterweight to a thin "Machiavelli advises tyrants" reading. [[72-junzhulun-du-ta-shi-yichang-weixian-de-maoxian-673440837]] uses the work to show that [[NiccoloMachiavelli]] can value republican institutions while also analyzing what princely rule requires once that regime exists.

[[350-majiyaweli-weihe-renwei-renmin-zai-chun-ye-bi-jingying-kekao-fqgvbt9naq3u9dkdabvczvw-3-u5]] gives the text a second role: it proves that Machiavelli's modernity is not a clean escape from antiquity. His political reasoning still unfolds through commentary on Roman history, especially the [[RomanRepublic]]'s wars, institutions, conflicts, laws, and political actors.

## Key Characteristics
- Republican companion to The Prince inside the wiki's Machiavelli branch.
- Uses Roman historical experience rather than purely abstract deduction.
- Preserves dialogue with authority texts, historical cases, and earlier political experience.
- Complicates the image of Machiavelli as only a cynical adviser to rulers.
- Shows how modern political questions can still be answered through classical material.

## Evidence
- Republican counterweight: [[72-junzhulun-du-ta-shi-yichang-weixian-de-maoxian-673440837]] says Discourses on Livy praises republican institutions and complicates a tyrant-adviser reading.
- Classical dependency: [[350-majiyaweli-weihe-renwei-renmin-zai-chun-ye-bi-jingying-kekao-fqgvbt9naq3u9dkdabvczvw-3-u5]] says Machiavelli keeps returning to Roman republican wars, institutions, conflicts, laws, and people to understand general political regularities.
- Method contrast: [[350-majiyaweli-weihe-renwei-renmin-zai-chun-ye-bi-jingying-kekao-fqgvbt9naq3u9dkdabvczvw-3-u5]] contrasts the text's ancient style of reasoning with The Prince's more modern direct entry into political problems.

## Qualifications
The current sources use Discourses on Livy mainly to establish interpretive position and method. They do not reconstruct the whole book, Livy's original history, or the full substance of Machiavelli's republican theory. The tension with The Prince is kept productive rather than flattened into a contradiction.
