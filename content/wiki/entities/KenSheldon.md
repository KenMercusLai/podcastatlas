# Ken Sheldon

## Overview
Ken Sheldon is a psychologist represented here through a [[HiddenBrain]] episode about choosing personally fitting goals, motivation under external control, creative self-discovery, and value-based persistence.

## Current Profile
[[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-db5f9d45fb650a533486e6eb38780ebf-49695184-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-db5f9d45fb650a533486e6eb38780ebf-49695184|Wellness 2.0: Who Do You Want To Be?]] connects Sheldon's own movement from an unsuccessful band and an abandoned psychotherapy program into goal research with his account of [[SelfConcordantGoalSelection]]. He treats personal direction as a testable alignment problem: conscious plans can inherit status, family, educational, and cultural demands, while deeper interests and values may appear only through reflection, incubation, and experiment.

His motivation account separates enjoyment from endorsement. External control can weaken voluntary interest, while [[IdentifiedMotivationPersistence]] can sustain a difficult path because the person still considers it important and expressive of their values. [[CommitmentDeliberationRubicon]] adds a revision boundary: commitment helps implementation, but discomfort and dissonance can sometimes justify reopening the choice.

## Key Characteristics
- Studies how goals differ in their fit with a person's values and deeper inclinations.
- Distinguishes intrinsic enjoyment, externally controlled action, and personally endorsed importance.
- Treats self-knowledge as an iterative process of questioning, incubation, insight, and verification.
- Uses education, legal careers, sport, and endurance activity to examine motivation across contexts.
- Preserves reconsideration as a necessary boundary on persistence and commitment.
