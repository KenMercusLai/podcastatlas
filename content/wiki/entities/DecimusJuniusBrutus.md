# Decimus Junius Brutus / 德奇姆斯

## Overview

Decimus Junius Brutus / 德奇姆斯 was the trusted Caesarian commander whose personal access made his betrayal operationally decisive in the [[CaesarAssassination|assassination of Julius Caesar]].

## Current Profile

The sources distinguish Decimus from the better-known [[MarcusBrutus]]. Decimus had served [[JuliusCaesar|Caesar]], held military standing, possessed a gladiator force, and remained close enough to dine with him on the eve of the Ides. When Caesar reportedly hesitated after warnings and Calpurnia's dream, Decimus persuaded him not to slight the Senate and escorted him to Pompey's complex.

His role makes the conspiracy depend on the same personal loyalty networks it claimed to resist. Republican principle alone did not place Caesar in the room; trusted access converted elite opposition into a successful ambush. His later defeat and death, covered by the continuation source rather than this bounded evidence set, also show that operational success in the murder did not create durable political control.

## Key Characteristics

- Trusted Caesarian insider rather than a distant ideological opponent.
- Conspirator whose access helped overcome Caesar's reported hesitation.
- Operational bridge between republican plotting and the target's physical arrival.
- Example of personal military and patronage loyalty reversing into political betrayal.

## Evidence

- Insider standing: [[124-kaisa-zhisi-lishi-de-yunjiao-shifou-yi-shixiao-781894361]] presents Decimus as a Caesarian confidant with military merit and an armed following.
- Eve of the murder: [[304-the-murder-of-julius-caesar-glt3561951089]] places Decimus at Lepidus's dinner with Caesar and in the next morning's effort to secure Caesar's attendance.
- Operational role: both [[124-kaisa-zhisi-lishi-de-yunjiao-shifou-yi-shixiao-781894361]] and [[304-the-murder-of-julius-caesar-glt3561951089]] make his trusted access the link between conspiracy and assassination.

## Qualifications

The warning stories, Calpurnia's dream, Caesar's degree of hesitation, and Decimus's exact argument are transmitted through dramatic ancient narratives and remain source-scoped. Trusted access is clearly important in the episode's account, but private motive and the relative weight of principle, ambition, and resentment are not settled.
