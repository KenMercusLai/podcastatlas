## Overview
褒斜道 / Baoxie Road is a Qinling corridor running through the Bao and Xie river valleys and connecting northern approaches to [[Hanzhong|汉中]] and the wider [[ShuRoadNetwork|古蜀道网络]].

## Current Profile
The road is defined by both state engineering and strategic vulnerability. Narrow valleys required timber and stone trestle construction, but flood, fire, war, and repeated use frequently destroyed the roadway. Surviving cliff sockets, inscriptions, and later cliff-side road sections therefore reveal a history of recurrent construction rather than uninterrupted preservation.

Its early profile was strongly military because the northern valleys offered little settlement, lodging, or supply outside official posts. Later rerouting through Liuba and Fengxian toward the Chencang system supported more ordinary official and civilian travel.

## Key Characteristics
- One of the major northern Qinling approaches to Hanzhong.
- Trestle-engineering corridor preserved mainly through sockets and inscriptions.
- Strategic road repeatedly built, blocked, burned, and repaired.
- Early military route constrained by thin settlement and difficult resupply.
- Later route shifts increased ordinary travel and reduced dependence on flood-prone sections.

## Evidence
- Route reconstruction: [[ep285-tafang-gushudao-ganwen-lu-zai-hefang-li20senvn3oelioproznnykdajvt|EP285]] says recent survey connected formerly isolated remains into a more continuous alignment.
- Engineering and loss: [[ep285-tafang-gushudao-ganwen-lu-zai-hefang-li20senvn3oelioproznnykdajvt|EP285]] describes trestle sockets as the common surviving trace after repeated flood and wartime damage.
- State project: [[ep285-tafang-gushudao-ganwen-lu-zai-hefang-li20senvn3oelioproznnykdajvt|EP285]] cites 《石门颂》 as evidence for imperial orders, labor, and completion of road work.
- Functional shift: [[ep285-tafang-gushudao-ganwen-lu-zai-hefang-li20senvn3oelioproznnykdajvt|EP285]] characterizes later rerouting as a partial “military-to-civilian” transition.

## Qualifications
Famous narratives such as “明修栈道，暗度陈仓” refer to a wider field of Qinling routes, and the precise itinerary remains disputed. Surviving sockets do not by themselves establish one continuous road surface or a single date.
