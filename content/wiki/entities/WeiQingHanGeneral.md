## Overview
卫青 / Wei Qing is synthesized as a low-status palace entrant who becomes a high-merit Western Han commander through the interaction of kin-enabled access, mounted skill, imperial trust, and demonstrated judgment. His earliest current layer is the contested 129 BCE first command, where Hanji 274 places him inside Wudi's new cavalry establishment and an intelligence-poor four-route probe toward Xiongnu territory. Later Gaoque, Dingxiang, and Mobei evidence combines guided deep pursuit, battlefield command, information-bound restraint, and restraint in punishment. Across the record, humility, credit-sharing, court deference, and source-attributed concern for soldiers make him both a safer powerful subordinate and a source-scoped stabilizing counterweight.

## Current Profile
The earliest current layer comes from [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-274-cong-qinu-dao-jiangjun-weiqing-shi-ruhe-nixi-de-llgairjhid8rskv4jzx0yyijhmnc|Hanji 274]]. It presents Wei Qing as a poor dependent and rider whose expectations are initially limited to avoiding abuse. [[EmpressWeiZifuWesternHan|卫子夫]]'s palace rise brings him into Jianzhang Palace and creates proximity to [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] and the developing cavalry system. That access is indispensable in the episode's account, but it is not the whole explanation: the host also attributes Wudi's confidence to Wei's riding experience, composure, modesty, unconventional judgment, and persistence.

The same source supplies only the pre-battle stage of Wei's first command. In 129 BCE he receives one of four reported ten-thousand-cavalry forces amid court objections to an untested outer relative. With poor knowledge of Xiongnu terrain and movement, he reportedly continues searching deep into the grassland and reaches Longcheng, but the episode stops before combat. The evidence therefore establishes a contested appointment and high-uncertainty approach, not yet the victory later remembered as his breakthrough.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-276-baling-heye-jiangjun-liguang-weihe-bei-xiaobing-qiling-lq813ahfpxsjjy7qoq90b4cnvyzx|Hanji 276]] supplies the next bounded campaign layer. It says Wudi uses Li Guang's eastern pressure to hold Xiongnu attention while Wei Qing leads thirty thousand cavalry from Yanmen and Li Xi blocks eastern reinforcement. Wei reportedly kills several thousand opponents and returns intact. The episode reads the operation as reconnaissance-led escalation and evidence of rising trust, but the exact battle name, geometry, totals, objectives, and chronology remain source-scoped.

The earliest completed campaign layer now comes from [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-291-hengsao-xiongnu-youxianwang-weiqing-gongbai-dajiangjun-lm1nilz4omifoepxdcg5rj5ynrxu|Hanji 291]], which closes the approach narrated in Hanji 290-1 and 290-2. Wei Qing's night attack fails to capture the Right Wise King, who escapes north with several hundred guards, but reportedly captures more than ten lesser kings, over fifteen thousand people, and nearly one million livestock. The distinction matters: the campaign is a major force- and resource-neutralizing victory without literal decapitation.

The same episode adds the clearest direct case behind the existing humility and credit-sharing profile. Han Wudi sends the grand-general seal to the army, increases Wei's fief, and grants marquisates to his three sons; Wei objects that the sons have no merit, attributes success to the ruler and fighting commanders, and prompts a broader reward distribution. The speech and reward details remain source-scoped, but they materially strengthen the judgment that Wei separates personal command success from hereditary entitlement.

The earliest current campaign-judgment layer comes from [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-296-1-weiqing-guashuai-chuzheng-wei-hanchao-diaoda-xiongnu-lkr5lcogip_nx5uoycleuqzp9you|Hanji 296-1]]. [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] gives Wei Qing a command team combining maneuver leaders, [[ZhaoXinWesternHan|赵信]]'s Xiongnu knowledge, [[ZhangQianWesternHan|张骞]]'s grassland guidance, and [[LiJuWesternHan|李沮]]'s crossbow force. After that force rapidly destroys a rear guard, Wei Qing does not equate the local result with the campaign objective. He infers that the chanyu's main body remains elsewhere, returns to the frontier commanderies, rests, and sends scouts. This supports [[LocalVictoryCampaignUncertainty|local victory and campaign uncertainty]], while the exact deployment, intent, and private reasoning remain source-scoped.

The earliest direct command-law layer comes from [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-297-gonggao-zhubuyi-mingjiang-weiqing-de-weiguan-zhihui-lg2zmxdwvx_ydqfqsme_hdcswtwn|Hanji 297]]. After winning his own engagement, Wei Qing checks the other formations and learns that Su Jian's force has been destroyed after Zhao Xin's surrender. One adviser recommends execution to establish Wei's authority, while Ren An and others stress hard fighting, return without defection, and the danger of teaching future defeated commanders not to come back. Wei neither acquits nor kills Su Jian: he imprisons him and refers final judgment to Wudi. The source interprets this as power-boundary awareness and earned authority, though the legal details and Wei's complete motives remain source-scoped.

The earliest mentorship and delegation layer presents Wei Qing as Huo Qubing's model and says he authorizes the seventeen-year-old's first independent detachment because he knows the preparation behind the request, then lets Huo choose eight hundred cavalry. This supports Wei as a commander willing to delegate to demonstrated readiness, but it does not prove that preparation alone displaced kinship, favor, or campaign need, and it stops before the detachment's battle result. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-298-shaonian-zhanshen-huoqubing-dengchang-lhwmfj24jl0ywwpcknytor9ujksh|Hanji 298]]

Hanji 1065 uses Wei Qing as one of the safer high-merit models in [[HighMeritThreatManagement|high merit threat management]]. His battlefield success is not the main claim there; the source emphasizes the posture after success. Wei Qing remains deferential toward [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] and shares credit with subordinates, making him a contrast to figures who turn merit into public superiority or independent threat.

Hanji 384-1 does not revise that personal profile, but it qualifies its durability. The episode says Han Wudi honored Wei Qing at death, yet later the Wugu case destroys part of the Wei-family support field: [[WeiKangWesternHan|卫伉]], Wei Qing's son and Changping marquis, is executed because the host treats his title and military capacity as useful to [[EmpressWeiZifuWesternHan|卫子夫]] and Liu Ju. Wei Qing's other sons, Wei Buyi and Wei Deng, are said to avoid implication because they do not participate in court affairs.

The marriage-history branch gives Wei Qing a narrow role outside military and succession politics. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-616-plus-pan-dian-shi-shang-de-hao-se-nv-ren-men-lgsf8_asfrictdmsptsyfizykpon|Hanji 616 plus]] says the widowed [[PingyangPrincessWesternHan|平阳公主]] married him and uses that union to illustrate elite female remarriage in the Western Han; it does not expand his campaign biography.

The transition evidence adds only a narrow role outside those established branches. Wei Qing's death marks the moment when Han Wudi sees the senior civil and military cohort as depleted and orders broad recommendation of unconventional people fit for high command, the chancellorship, or distant missions in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-369-2-gaiming-bikan-yinji-faze-yyds-foitbkrmhmc1xgeu0tk4btyvmi98|Hanji 369-2]]. This confirms Wei Qing's importance to the personnel field but does not add campaign detail or establish that his death alone caused the shortage.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-369-1-weiqing-zhisi-jing-rang-hanwudi-ruci-fengkuang-lljxwjp9qxvpog9q2dh2kiyyccer|Hanji 369-1]] gives that transition a stronger but explicitly interpretive political meaning. It says Wei Qing remained important to imperial military standards and court stability after active campaigning, received an honored tomb and the posthumous title Lie, and functioned like a stabilizing weight on the polity. The host then argues that his death removed a person able to balance Wudi's authority, increasing imperial insecurity and control-seeking. This extends Wei Qing from a vacancy marker into a proposed counterweight, but the causal chain is not independently demonstrated.

A conflict-containment case extends that profile. After [[LiGuangHanGeneral|李广]] dies following a missed movement and inquiry, Li Gan blames and assaults Wei Qing. Wei conceals the assault instead of pursuing punishment in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-335-dangzhe-wudi-de-mian-huoqubing-weihe-gan-sha-liguang-zhi-zi-lupxhu64fi0d0xl223zq9pwenmy1|Hanji 335]]. That restraint is consistent with the page's low-threat, non-escalatory profile, but the source does not establish whether it came from guilt, political judgment, compassion, or another motive. The later killing of Li Gan by Wei's nephew Huo Qubing also shows that one actor's forbearance does not settle a grievance across a kin network.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-325-2-liguang-weihe-hui-zisha-ta-fanle-shenme-cuo-lsrkaep1np1bugqae8ujie2ttqpe|Hanji 325-2]] expands the disputed campaign prehistory without settling culpability. It says Wei Qing cites a secret instruction associated with [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] when moving Li Guang from the vanguard to an eastern route with [[ZhaoShiqiWesternHan|赵食其]]; the force then lacks a guide, becomes lost, and misses the battle. The host suspects political maneuvering or deliberate guide removal, but the episode supplies no direct evidence that Wei planned the failure, knew why the guide was absent, or intended Li Guang's death.

The disputed Mobei deployment joins imperial constraint, personal patronage, and operational execution. After intelligence redirects Wei toward the chanyu, he rejects Li Guang's repeated request for the vanguard, orders Li and Zhao Shiqi onto the eastern flank, and is said to want [[GongsunAoWesternHan|公孙敖]] to lead the direct arm so his friend can regain rank through merit. Wei's army then reaches the enemy in fighting condition because logistics defeats the expected desert attrition, forms a wagon perimeter, and opens the cavalry battle before a sandstorm. The patronage motive is episode-attributed and does not by itself establish sabotage or sole causation. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-324-2-liguang-yu-weiqing-liangwei-mingjiang-de-aihen-qingchou-lqejjd0rqvm7lj59w8v1s0pvpwpd|Hanji 324-2]]

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-325-1-weiqing-ruhe-yizhan-daduan-xiongnu-de-jiliang-lkt-_jefjsdyijozqxqnh2od9liq|Hanji 325-1]] supplies the battle that Li Guang misses. Wei Qing orders left and right wings around the chanyu's main force, learns after dark that the chanyu has escaped, sends close cavalry to pursue, and presses the force left behind. The episode treats the flight as the point at which closely matched fighting turns into dispersal, then reports nineteen thousand killed or captured and seized supplies. This adds operational detail without resolving the reassignment controversy.

The immediate post-Mobei evidence adds an evaluation and relationship aftermath. Although Wei's arm wins, the chanyu escapes and neither Wei nor his surrounding commanders receives a comparable reward because the annihilation objective remains incomplete. As Huo Qubing's prestige rises, former friends and retainers reportedly move toward Huo for office or benefit, while Ren An continues entering Wei's household. The result qualifies a simple victory biography: battlefield success, imperial reward, and household attraction can diverge when the ruler judges the unmet objective more heavily than the achieved result. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-327-1-wanwan-meixiangdao-hanwudi-ye-ai-chi-xiangcai-lv_ed7hfumn80ypmwq8nfdpdfwcv|Hanji 327-1]]

A comparative welfare layer rather than a new campaign comes from [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-328-2-gudai-zuqiu-ye-fengkuang-huoqubing-jing-ye-chimi-lh5o5cgy9kg7kd_vl-wvvetsmvsn|Hanji 328-2]]. The host attributes Wei's understanding of hardship, kindness toward soldiers and civilians, humility, and conciliatory manner to his poor upbringing, then uses that profile to contrast Huo's discarded provisions and cuju under food shortage. This is consistent with the existing humility and conflict-containment synthesis, but upbringing as cause and the full character contrast remain the episode's interpretation.

The post-Huo transition remains disputed. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-336-2-huoqubing-yunluo-wudi-zhen-shi-muhou-heishou-ltfkwalc48bj1hav65bg-8qotrf5|Hanji 336-2]] distinguishes Wei and Huo Qubing personally from the groups around them, then reads title losses after Huo's death and Wei's long absence from active command as deliberate imperial weakening. This can coexist with Hanji 369-1's claim that Wei retained stabilizing prestige, but the intent, faction boundary, and causal chain remain the host's reconstruction.

## Key Characteristics
- Low-status rider whose palace access through Wei Zifu creates opportunity and who becomes a high-merit Western Han commander; the first-command, Yanmen, Gaoque, Dingxiang, and Mobei evidence joins source-attributed capability, search, diversion, reconnaissance, movement, formation, encirclement, and pursuit.
- Restrained, humble, deferential, and credit-sharing commander who refuses hereditary reward without merit and preserves accountability through detention and higher review rather than execution for personal display.
- Posthumous family-risk case showing that a commander's prior favor does not necessarily protect descendants when succession politics turn.
- Husband in the episode's elite-remarriage example around Pingyang Princess.
- Senior-command vacancy marker whose death helps trigger a broad Han Wudi recruitment response.
- Source-scoped stabilizing counterweight whose death is used to explain a later turn toward greater imperial suspicion and control.
- Final-campaign commander whose victory does not bring comparable new reward after the chanyu escapes, and whose declining household attraction exposes power-contingent ties while Ren An remains.

## Qualifications
This page remains source-scoped to eighteen compact podcast notes. It does not provide a full military biography, reconstruct every campaign, assess every interpretation of Wei Qing's relationship with Emperor Wu, or supply complete genealogical and legal records for his descendants. Hanji 274 ends before the Longcheng battle and does not independently establish the physiognomy story, Wudi's private reasoning or reply to ministers, exact force totals and routes, absence of guides, distance marched, pledge, or discovery mechanics. It shows kin-enabled access and a source-attributed capability judgment together; it does not prove either pure nepotism or purely meritocratic selection. Hanji 276 does not independently establish the “燕门之战” label, Li Guang's diversion role, Li Xi's blocking mission, force and casualty totals, intact return, or Wudi's private reconnaissance objective. Hanji 291 does not independently establish the attack geometry, escape party, captured rulers, population and livestock totals, reward amounts, appointment ceremony, exact speeches, or the wider strategic claim that one of three Xiongnu power groupings was removed. The Right Wise King's escape requires separating force neutralization from literal decapitation. Hanji 296-1 does not independently establish the first Dingxiang route, offices, force size, casualty total, exact pincer and crossbow geometry, chanyu plan, withdrawal destinations, scouting sequence, or Wei's private reasoning. Hanji 297 does not independently establish the later force sizes, battlefield geometry, adviser titles and wording, scope of field execution power, Su Jian's palace office, Wei's exact motives, or the modern formal-versus-natural-authority framework; referral preserved the possibility of survival, but Han Wudi's later judgment and redemption mechanism also determined the result.
