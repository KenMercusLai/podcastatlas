# Kamehameha IV

## Overview

Kamehameha IV was a Hawaiian king presented as a well-traveled monarch who favored British royal and Anglican connections while governing amid intensifying American influence.

## Current Profile

[[12-days-reconquest-of-spain-and-the-old-queen-of-hawaii-glt9407596415]] uses Kamehameha IV to show that the Hawaiian monarchy was an active diplomatic institution rather than a passive object of colonization. Before and during his reign, travel brought him into contact with Prince Albert, Lord Palmerston, Napoleon III, and U.S. political leaders, giving him a comparative view of monarchy, race, and foreign power.

His reported mistreatment by an American train conductor sharpened a pro-British orientation that also appeared in Anglican institution-building with [[QueenEmmaOfHawaii|Queen Emma]]. The death of their son Albert in 1862 and Kamehameha IV's death the following year ended that partnership early, leaving Emma to carry its philanthropic and religious legacy.

## Key Characteristics

- Hawaiian king using travel and court diplomacy to navigate foreign pressure.
- Monarch with strong cultural and political affinity for Britain.
- Critic of racial humiliation experienced in the United States.
- Partner with Queen Emma in Anglican and philanthropic institution-building.
- Ruler embedded in a Christian monarchy shaped by, but not reducible to, missionary influence.

## Evidence

- Diplomatic formation: [[12-days-reconquest-of-spain-and-the-old-queen-of-hawaii-glt9407596415]] describes meetings with Prince Albert, Lord Palmerston, Napoleon III, and President Taylor.
- Racial comparison: [[12-days-reconquest-of-spain-and-the-old-queen-of-hawaii-glt9407596415]] recounts his mistreatment on an American train and his favorable comparison with British public courtesy.
- Religious and royal partnership: [[12-days-reconquest-of-spain-and-the-old-queen-of-hawaii-glt9407596415]] connects him and Emma to Anglican institution-building and Queen Victoria through their son Albert.
- Dynastic loss: [[12-days-reconquest-of-spain-and-the-old-queen-of-hawaii-glt9407596415]] dates Albert's death to 1862 and the king's death to 1863.

## Qualifications

The source provides a compressed profile and does not cover the reign's complete domestic politics, public-health policy, constitutional setting, land system, U.S. relations, or Native Hawaiian opposition and support. Its British-American moral contrast rests heavily on a reported personal anecdote and should not become a general comparison of the two empires.

## What Changed

- Established Kamehameha IV as a traveling, British-facing Hawaiian monarch whose racial experience and Anglican policy shaped a distinct foreign orientation.
