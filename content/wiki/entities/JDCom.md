# JD.com / 京东

## Overview
JD.com / 京东 is a major Chinese ecommerce and logistics platform. In the current wiki it functions as a durable platform benchmark, a foodservice-platform experimenter, a possible robotics data-collection actor, and an operator testing how online assortment and rapid fulfillment can connect to physical shopping spaces.

## Current Profile
The bounded sources present JD.com less as a single-topic company profile than as a platform with logistics depth and operational reach. Ecommerce-history sources use it as one of the surviving winners against which failed Chinese ecommerce businesses are compared. Retail and food-court sources place it in platform canteen and instant-retail experiments, while a robotics episode describes its workforce and outside participant network as a possible [[EgocentricRobotData]] collection surface. The newest episode adds JD Tiandi and JD 101 Home: physical venues where product experience, online catalog access, and JD Miaosong delivery are meant to reinforce one another. That combination is strategically coherent, but the source explicitly leaves physical-store operations and format replication unproven.

## Key Characteristics
- JD.com is treated as a surviving Chinese ecommerce platform whose logistics, category breadth, and credibility pressured earlier vertical or book-retail businesses.
- The company appears in foodservice and instant-retail contexts as platforms explore canteen-like or food-court-like spaces beyond classic ecommerce.
- Recent operating coverage presents a mixed business profile: revenue pressure paired with profit improvement and shifting new-business spending.
- Its logistics workforce and ecosystem can potentially be repurposed as physical-world data infrastructure for [[RealRobotDataStrategy]].
- JD Tiandi and JD 101 Home test whether physical product discovery can feed online ordering and [[InstantRetail|rapid fulfillment]].
- The transfer of ecommerce efficiency into shopping-center and showroom operations remains an execution question.

## Evidence
- Ecommerce benchmark evidence: [[no-200-dianshang-sanguo-zhi-qunxiong-zhulu-yaogua-gongzhang-chijian-juqian-yiji-108-zhong-sifa-974601500]] uses JD.com beside Taobao and Pinduoduo as a surviving platform whose logistics and category expansion made many older ecommerce models harder to defend.
- Platform foodservice evidence: [[vol-270-da-shi-dai-liu-zai-le-ta-de-shi-dai-1006063835]] names JD.com beside Meituan in the platform-canteen discussion as food-court-like functions move to new operators and subsidy logics.
- Current operating evidence: [[vans-kuangwei-fengguang-buzai-jingdian-fanbuxie-weishenme-maibudongle-1006742982]] reports revenue decline, profit improvement, and new-business spending shifting away from food delivery toward international business.
- Robotics data evidence: [[bots-on-the-ground-china-leads-humanoid-race-6a91564cee851f3f31239bbf]] says JD.com is recruiting 100,000 staff and 500,000 outside participants to wear sensor gear, with a possible target of 10 million hours of movement data over two years.
- Physical-retail evidence: [[jingdong-kaichu-gouwuzhongxin-weilai-yu-jili-tuijin-chongdian-huandian-hezuo-1019779523]] describes the first JD Tiandi in Suqian and JD 101 Home in Shanghai as links among in-person experience, online inventory, scanning and ordering, and rapid delivery.

## Qualifications
The robotics claim is source-scoped and does not establish useful robot-training data or a robot product. The operating metrics in the Vans/Converse source are not a full financial analysis. The store sizes, expansion plans, assortment links, and delivery claims in the newest episode are also source-scoped; opening a venue does not establish repeat traffic, store-level economics, or successful replication in other cities.

## What Changed
- Added JD Tiandi and JD 101 Home as physical-retail tests.
- Connected those formats to JD's online assortment and rapid-fulfillment capabilities.
- Preserved physical-store operation and replication as unresolved constraints.
