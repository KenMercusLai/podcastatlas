## Overview

管敢 / Guan Gan is the Western Han military officer whose defection becomes the decisive intelligence break in Hanji 377-2's account of [[LiLingWesternHan|李陵]]'s battle. After being humiliated by a commandant, he joins [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] and reveals the Han force's lack of reinforcement, depleted arrows, forward deployment, and flag identifiers.

## Current Profile

Guan Gan's role is causal rather than biographically broad. Before his defection, the Xiongnu leadership is uncertain whether Li Ling is drawing it toward a Han ambush and considers ending the pursuit before open ground. Guan Gan tells them that no rescue force exists and that the Han arrows are nearly gone, then identifies Li Ling and [[HanYannianWesternHan|韩延年]]'s forward elements and recommends concentrated attack.

The source therefore treats his disclosure as the turn from costly Xiongnu hesitation to renewed pressure. The information does not create Han weakness, but it removes uncertainty about weakness that already exists.

## Key Characteristics

- Military officer inside Li Ling's force before defection.
- Defector whose stated grievance is humiliation by a commandant.
- Insider source on reinforcement, ammunition, formation, leadership, and visual identifiers.
- Turning-point figure whose disclosure changes Xiongnu risk assessment and targeting.

## Qualifications

The supplied episode summary writes the name as “管感”; this page normalizes it to the received-history 管敢. The source does not identify the commandant, independently verify the grievance, reconstruct Guan Gan's later life, or establish that disclosure alone caused the defeat; ammunition loss, casualties, blocked terrain, and sustained numerical pressure were already severe.
