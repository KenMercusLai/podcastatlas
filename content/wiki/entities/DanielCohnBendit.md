# Daniel Cohn-Bendit

## Overview

Daniel Cohn-Bendit, known as “Danny the Red,” was a Nanterre student activist whom the source presents as a visible, media-capable catalyst in the opening phase of May 1968.

## Current Profile

[[353-paris-1968-the-students-revolt-part-1-glt6124707164]] situates Cohn-Bendit in a German Jewish refugee family and distinguishes his libertarian politics from conventional communism. His January 1968 challenge to the youth and sports minister over sexuality and his role in the 22 March occupation made him a recognizable representative of a movement that combined concrete campus grievances with anti-authoritarian performance.

His prominence did not make him the commander of a unified revolt. The episode uses his later acknowledgment that students and workers were never one force to clarify the crisis's coalition limits: students sought broad changes in authority and everyday life, while organized labor pursued material workplace demands.

## Key Characteristics

- Became a highly visible spokesman for the Nanterre student revolt.
- Combined libertarian anti-authoritarianism with humor, provocation, and media fluency.
- Helped connect residence rules and university bureaucracy to a wider challenge to paternal authority.
- Was associated publicly with the radical left without fitting a conventional communist profile.
- Later emphasized that student and worker mobilization overlapped without becoming a unified movement.
