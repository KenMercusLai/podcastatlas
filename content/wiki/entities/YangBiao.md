# 杨彪 / Yang Biao

## Overview
杨彪 / Yang Biao is a late-Han high minister whose value and danger come from the same source: old-court prestige, family status, and a record of direct remonstrance under captured courts. Across the wiki, he moves from local evidence-gatherer against eunuch-linked extraction to Three Excellencies officeholder, emergency court protector, and finally a symbolic target for [[CaoCao|曹操]] after the court is moved into the Xu order.

## Current Profile
Yang Biao's current profile is not a simple loyal-minister biography. Earlier sources show him supplying evidence, resisting forced relocation, judging supply and route risks, and naming the illegitimacy of hostage politics. Hanji 1020 and 1021 then recast that accumulated prestige as political exposure: his marriage tie to [[YuanShu|袁术]], old-Han reputation, and court visibility make him a useful warning target, but [[KongRong|孔融]] and [[ManChong|满宠]] make clear that weak evidence cannot safely sustain punishment against such a famous figure. Cao Cao's release-and-removal outcome leaves Yang alive but sidelines him, and Yang's later withdrawal turns survival into exit from active court politics.

## Key Characteristics
- Evidence-bearing local official who helps expose eunuch-retainer extraction.
- Prestige-bearing Three Excellencies figure whom coercive rulers can use for legitimacy while risking his remonstrance.
- Old-Han minister who keeps speaking and judging under Li Jue/Guo Si-era captivity and flight conditions.
- Reputation-protected prosecution target after Cao Cao controls the emperor's court.
- Survival actor whose final response is withdrawal from the captured court rather than renewed confrontation.

## Evidence
- Evidence channel against palace-linked extraction: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-915-shishang-meiyou-zhenzheng-de-kouwu-zhehua-dui-ma-lgxcodjxnaozvyegxqurgzji5gus|Hanji 915]] has Yang Biao report Wang Fu-retainer seizure of public wealth, giving [[YangQiuLateHan|阳球]] an evidentiary opening.
- Elite office and relocation remonstrance: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-948-dongzhuo-feidi-quanchen-ruhe-gaibian-huangquan-youxi-ltekg1a-5b5u37aeekjm3dm2m6kk|Hanji 948]], [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-951-tianjiang-hengcai-jiujing-shi-haoshi-haishi-huaishi-lovwgng2wrtfno-s9ttpybbhksvh|Hanji 951]], and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-979-zaoyu-tongliao-beici-ta-ruhe-wei-caocao-liwan-kuanglan-luu58a2ww11g-radh-5g4l7fcis|Hanji 979]] show Dong Zhuo using Yang's prestige, Yang opposing the forced Luoyang-to-Chang'an relocation, and Yang later returning to taiwei-level office amid weakening court order.
- Captive-court speech and emergency judgment: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-985-fanmu-chengchou-liguo-zhiluan-de-xijie-tanjiu-lt-t2un0zcudkige9ucl1txa2hhk|Hanji 985]], [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-989-ningwei-taipingquan-buzuo-luanshi-ren-lkoycygmjnltuvpq-6wjv-ls61s6|Hanji 989]], and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-991-zhengduo-xiandi-zhizhan-shui-neng-zuizhong-qusheng-lkbsl5uzqxouzm6mlewrnuns0x6p|Hanji 991]] show Yang rebuking hostage politics, defending [[DuanWeiLateHan|段煨]] against unproven rebellion claims, and arranging a safer night crossing for [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]].
- Prosecution constrained by reputation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1020-sanguo-baijiazi-yuanshu-ruhe-ba-hao-pai-da-de-xilan-lhwr9hfmanr-zktleufy3kvl38u0|Hanji 1020]] gives the arrest, Yuan Shu marriage-tie suspicion, Kong Rong's protest, Man Chong's evidence warning, and Yang's release.
- Release as sidelining rather than reconciliation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1021-caocao-shouwo-zhongquan-weishenme-rongbuxia-yangbiao-lr-y4xqanwjyslvf4fbwxjuymtun|Hanji 1021]] adds that Cao Cao releases Yang Biao but removes him from taiwei, using the case to warn old-Han loyalists, weaken Emperor Xian's practical authority, and pressure great clans while avoiding execution's reputation cost.

## Qualifications
The accusation against Yang Biao remains source-scoped. The wiki treats the Cao-side case as a political prosecution constrained by evidence and reputation, not as proof that Yang was innocent in every private connection or guilty of Yuan Shu's rebellion. Hanji 1021's "open break with old-Han ministers" reading is preserved as the episode's political interpretation, while the concrete outcome is narrower: release, removal from office, and later withdrawal.
