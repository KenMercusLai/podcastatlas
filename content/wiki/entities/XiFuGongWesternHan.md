## Overview

息夫躬 / Xi Fu Gong is synthesized as a late [[HanAidi|汉哀帝]] adviser whose current wiki role combines Dongping-case reward, feared accusation-channel behavior, and frontier escalation. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-586-weile-aiqing-gudai-huangdi-you-duo-chongdong-lizfxc8a4do88e6vyrcupyet3vs|Hanji 586]] makes him one of the Dongping king Liu Yun case beneficiaries: he is rewarded in the same package that gives [[DongXian|董贤]] fabricated or contested merit, and after favor grows he repeatedly reports to Aidi and denounces high officials. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-591-1-yichang-bingjia-ruhe-xianxie-yinbao-liangguo-zhizhan-lrq-xdhvrnuarzwgrykp7j-x8j-a|Hanji 591-1]] then shows him transforming a [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] chanyu's illness-delayed visit into a proposed security operation involving speculative threat assessment, covert diplomacy, deliberate information leakage, disaster-omen pressure, military inspection, and punitive spectacle.

## Current Profile

Xi Fu Gong's current profile is defined by escalation and accusation. Hanji 586 supplies the court-politics layer before the Xiongnu crisis: he and Sun Chong are treated as meritorious in the Liu Yun case, receive reward, and Xi Fu Gong later uses frequent access to Aidi to report matters and attack high officials. The source says other officials become afraid of him, making him a near-court intimidation figure rather than only a frontier adviser.

Hanji 591-1 begins from uncertainty: the chanyu was expected to enter the frontier in the eleventh month but reports illness instead. Xi Fu Gong treats that delay as possibly fraudulent and connects it to [[Wusun|乌孙]] internal weakness and the risk that Xiongnu could absorb western influence.

His concrete diplomatic proposal is manipulative rather than verification-based. He suggests using surrendered western Hu people as false envoys connected to Wusun, having them ask the Han emperor to pressure the chanyu over hostages, and leaking the petition to Xiongnu envoys to break trust between Xiongnu and Wusun.

The proposal then expands inside the imperial audience. After [[GongsunLuWesternHan|公孙禄]] objects, Xi Fu Gong argues that he is eliminating danger before rebellion happens. In private discussion with Aidi, he invokes repeated disasters, proposes sending a great general to inspect the frontier, and suggests killing a commandery governor to create fear. [[WangJiaWesternHan|王嘉]]'s later objection makes Xi Fu Gong the episode's main example of how ambition, disaster interpretation, and security language can turn a small diplomatic delay into war pressure.

## Key Characteristics

- Late Western Han adviser whose plan begins from a chanyu illness delay.
- Dongping-case reward beneficiary who later becomes a feared near-court accuser.
- Converts uncertainty into a broad frontier-security narrative involving Wusun and Xiongnu.
- Proposes false-envoy diplomacy and intentional leakage as a divide-and-pressure tactic.
- Uses "preventive" language to defend escalation before rebellion has occurred.
- Connects disasters and border anxiety to military inspection and exemplary killing.
- Functions as the source's foil for Gongsun Lu's trust-preserving diplomacy and Wang Jia's procedural remonstrance.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 586 and Hanji 591-1. It does not establish Xi Fu Gong's full biography, later fate, precise role in the original Liu Yun accusation, or whether the chanyu illness had any hidden strategic content. The sources present his conduct through critical framing, so the page treats his accusation and security strategies as source-grounded proposals and behaviors rather than verified diagnoses of others' intent.
