## Overview

[[CaoPi]] is a son and eventual successor of [[CaoCao|曹操]] whose current evidence connects the conversion of Cao-family power into formal rule with earlier succession contingency and a pre-accession lesson in delegated force, captive treatment, and paternal authority.

## Current Profile

In [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-24-shangyang-yue-wo-za-lie-le-lt4yd6pwlezqia5yljg-gankyhut|Zhouji 24]], Cao Pi appears as the endpoint of a Cao Cao analogy: after the family monopolizes effective power, [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]] abdicates to Cao Pi. The comparison is used to show one possible escape from an irreversible power position—formalize succession—while [[ShangYang|公孙鞅 / 商鞅]] lacks any equivalent route to the throne.

Hanji 1111 adds a much earlier family vulnerability. When Cao Pi tries to comfort Cao Cao after Cao Chong's death, Cao Cao reportedly says the loss is his own disaster but good fortune for the other sons. Cao Pi is later said to acknowledge that the realm might not have come to him had Cao Chong survived. The source therefore treats Cao Pi's later succession not as inevitable but as partly reordered by the disappearance of a younger favored contender.

Hanji 1127 adds a 212 CE governing test. When rebellion breaks out in Hejian, [[ChangLinLateHan|常林]] persuades Cao Pi that personally pursuing a limited victory would expose a politically important person to needless risk, so he sends Jia Xin instead. After the rebels' defeat, Cao Pi does not stretch general military discretion into authority to execute more than one thousand controlled surrenderers under an older wartime order. He asks Cao Cao, who grants pardon. The episode presents restraint here as bounded judgment rather than softness: delegation fits the threat, and post-capture punishment remains answerable to the superior whose order is being interpreted.

## Key Characteristics

- Son who ultimately converts Cao-family command into formal dynastic succession.
- Beneficiary of Cao Chong's early death in the source's counterfactual succession frame.
- Participant in a father-son exchange that exposes rivalry behind family grief.
- Pre-accession administrator who delegates a limited revolt and refers a disputed mass execution upward.
- Contrast case for political actors whose accumulated power cannot safely be surrendered but can be inherited and formalized.

## Evidence

- Formal succession: [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-24-shangyang-yue-wo-za-lie-le-lt4yd6pwlezqia5yljg-gankyhut|Zhouji 24]] says Emperor Xian abdicates to Cao Pi after the Cao family monopolizes power.
- Counterfactual rivalry: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1111-caocao-zui-tong-de-yinian-erzi-yaozhe-qiu-lianyin-ai-dui-lr5tvu8-kti41ezapvq2vz2wfjh6|Hanji 1111]] reports Cao Cao calling Cao Chong's death fortunate for the surviving sons and Cao Pi later acknowledging that he might otherwise not have received the realm.
- Emotional exposure: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1111-caocao-zui-tong-de-yinian-erzi-yaozhe-qiu-lianyin-ai-dui-lr5tvu8-kti41ezapvq2vz2wfjh6|Hanji 1111]] places the succession remark inside Cao Pi's attempt to console his grieving father.
- Proportional delegation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1127-caocao-cuanwei-sanjian-ta-zhide-shi-shenme-livrh3obobkcfhj_ngnhe-kgv2ls|Hanji 1127]] says Cao Pi accepts Chang Lin's advice not to risk personal command against a limited Hejian revolt.
- Captive decision boundary: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1127-caocao-cuanwei-sanjian-ta-zhide-shi-shenme-livrh3obobkcfhj_ngnhe-kgv2ls|Hanji 1127]] says he requests Cao Cao's ruling rather than execute controlled surrenderers through an expansive reading of wartime discretion.

## Qualifications

The current page is not a complete biography of Cao Pi. Zhouji 24 uses him briefly as a later analogy, while Hanji 1111 reports a succession counterfactual rather than a formal designation record. Hanji 1127 does not establish how independently Cao Pi designed the Hejian response, the exact legal force of Cao Cao's earlier order, or the identities and number of captives. Dialogue, physical reactions, and motives remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Added the Hejian response as evidence of proportional delegation and bounded authority over punishment.
- Reframed the profile to include pre-accession governing judgment as well as succession contingency.
