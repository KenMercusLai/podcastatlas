## Overview

袁涣 / Yuan Huan is a late-Han official whose current profile joins independence under coercion, gratitude across regime change, material restraint, and selective refusal of court sentiment. His central continuity is that current power does not grant him permission to rewrite a former patron's moral record.

## Current Profile

Hanji 1009 introduces the defining refusal. [[LyuBu|吕布]] pressures Yuan Huan at sword point to write an abusive letter against [[LiuBei|刘备]], who had earlier recommended him as a maocai. Yuan Huan argues that insults cannot manufacture moral shame and refuses to convert former service into retrospective slander. The scene makes him the person who blocks [[MoralDisengagementRationalization|道德推脱式自我合理化]] rather than helping a superior launder betrayal.

Hanji 1138 extends that refusal into [[CaoCao|曹操]]'s order. When a report says Liu Bei has died and others offer congratulations, Yuan Huan reportedly declines to celebrate because he will not forget Liu Bei's earlier recommendation. The same episode describes him distributing rewards rather than accumulating wealth, taking books instead of plunder after Lü Bu's defeat, and holding high Wei offices. These anecdotes support a coherent profile of gratitude and restraint, while the exact offices, death-report scene, and material practices remain source-scoped.

## Key Characteristics

- Refuses to write abuse against a former patron even under armed threat.
- Treats moral judgment as independent of whichever ruler he currently serves.
- Preserves gratitude to Liu Bei after entering Cao Cao's political order.
- Is portrayed as distributing rewards and preferring books to captured wealth.
- Converts personal consistency into a public contrast with opportunistic praise and blame.

## Evidence

- Coerced refusal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1009-liubei-touben-caocao-xianxie-langru-hukou-llm0ds4krkptjdtb71hbusnpzbdc|Hanji 1009]] says Lü Bu threatens Yuan Huan while demanding an abusive letter against Liu Bei, and Yuan Huan refuses.
- Cross-regime gratitude: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1138-luanshi-sanguo-zhaoang-de-zhongcheng-yu-xisheng-lmnxifntpnaat1q4wmx043joh8hg|Hanji 1138]] says Yuan Huan does not join congratulations after a report of Liu Bei's death because Liu Bei had recommended him.
- Material restraint: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1138-luanshi-sanguo-zhaoang-de-zhongcheng-yu-xisheng-lmnxifntpnaat1q4wmx043joh8hg|Hanji 1138]] reports that Yuan Huan distributes rewards, keeps little accumulated wealth, and takes books rather than treasure after Lü Bu's defeat.

## Qualifications

The current evidence consists of two structured popular-history summaries, not a full biography or primary-source comparison. The exact sword scene, dialogue, Liu Bei death report, court response, offices, rewards, book count, household wealth, and Cao Cao's grief require verification. The consistent pattern supports a bounded judgment about refusal and remembered obligation; it does not establish that every action in Yuan Huan's career followed one motive or that austerity alone proves public virtue.
