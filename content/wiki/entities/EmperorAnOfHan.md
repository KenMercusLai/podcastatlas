## Overview
汉安帝 / Emperor An of Han now appears in the wiki through a 121-125 CE sequence in which post-regency insecurity never becomes stable public order. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-794-yidai-nvjun-dengsui-qushi-hou-jing-quanjia-zaoyang-llmlhabdd09opca6-pr80q4mfoxi|Hanji 794]] makes his fear of replacement by [[LiuYiPingyuanWangLateHan|Pingyuan Wang Liu Yi]] the opening layer; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-795-dengjia-miewang-hou-weihe-juchao-wei-tamen-mingyuan-lqvmbqrrookphb4becaqbp7h-wi1|Hanji 795]] shows the Deng purge's partial correction and replacement by new favorites; Hanji 796-802 carry the same reign through private-favor appointment erosion, uneven advice acceptance, Yang Zhen's destruction, Liu Bao's deposition, and concealed death on tour.

## Current Profile
An's personal rule begins under the shadow of [[EmpressDowagerDengLateHan|Empress Dowager Deng]] rather than with a clean transfer. Deng had installed him, delayed returning authority, and kept collateral royal sons in Luoyang. [[WangShengPalaceActorLateHan|Wang Sheng]], [[LiRunLateHan|Li Run]], and [[JiangJingLateHan|Jiang Jing]] help turn the fear that An may be deposed into palace slander; after Deng dies, the alleged plan to install Pingyuan Wang Liu Yi becomes the trigger for a rapid purge of the [[DengFamilyAnEraLateHan|Deng family]].

The Deng purge defines An's legitimacy problem. He can punish forcefully, but [[ZhuChongLateHan|Zhu Chong]] later exposes the weakness of proof, interrogation, and charge clarity. An partially retreats by blaming local over-enforcement, permitting Deng corpses to return for burial, and allowing surviving cousins back to Luoyang. The correction is limited: the defeated Deng network is replaced by Geng, Song, Yan, eunuch, wet-nurse, and favorite-household networks rather than by impersonal appointment order.

An is not portrayed as unable to hear any advice. Hanji 794 shows [[ChenZhongLateHan|Chen Zhong]] warning him to tolerate sharp criticism during worthy-and-upright selection, after which [[ShiYanLateHan|Shi Yan]] is appointed shizhong. Hanji 796 shows him accepting [[LiuKaiEasternHan|Liu Kai]] and Chen Zhong's narrow punishment-limit argument; Hanji 797 shows him accepting Chen's advice not to attack [[GoguryeoLateHan|Goguryeo]] during royal mourning; Hanji 799 shows him accepting Chen's Western Regions retention argument by appointing [[BanYongLateHan|Ban Yong]] to Liuzhong.

The advice he repeatedly fails to convert is domestic correction against palace access. [[YangZhenLateHan|Yang Zhen]] and [[ZhaiGuiLateHan|Zhai Gui]] warn against Wang Sheng's household, [[BoRongLateHan|Bo Rong]], [[LiuGuiLateHan|Liu Gui]]'s title path, and the loss of public control over offices and rewards. Chen Zhong warns that Bo Rong's missions burden local people and that the Three Excellencies bear blame without real authority. An instead exposes memorials, ignores broad warnings, funds Wang Sheng's mansion, executes [[ZhaoTengLateHan|Zhao Teng]], and lets Fan Feng's group turn the forged-edict case against Yang Zhen.

The reign closes by repeating the succession-insecurity pattern. In Hanji 801, An treats broad support for his son [[EmperorShunOfHan|Liu Bao]] as a threat and deposes him after palace accusation. In Hanji 802, An dies away from [[LuoyangLateHan|Luoyang]], letting [[EmpressDowagerYanLateHan|Yan Hou / Yan Taihou]], [[YanXianLateHan|Yan Xian]], and [[FanFengEunuchLateHan|Fan Feng]] hide the death, return to the capital, and install [[LiuYiBeixiangHouLateHan|Beixiang Hou Liu Yi]] while excluding Liu Bao again.

## Key Characteristics
- Ruler whose post-regency personal rule begins with fear of replacement and a severe Deng-family purge.
- Partial corrector who limits Deng-family over-punishment only after public mourning and Zhu Chong's coffin memorial.
- Selectively receptive ruler who accepts bounded legal, personnel, and frontier advice while ignoring broader domestic institutional correction.
- Ruler whose court converts kinship, accession service, wet-nurse status, palace marriage, and favorite access into title and office pressure.
- Executor or punisher of correction figures including Zhao Teng, Yang Zhen, and Lai Li.
- Father of Liu Bao whose 124 CE deposition and 125 CE tour death create the succession legitimacy wound.
- Reign marker for court-feedback collapse under continuing disaster, frontier, and fiscal pressure.

## Qualifications
This page is bounded to Hanji 802, Hanji 801, Hanji 800, Hanji 799, Hanji 798, Hanji 797, Hanji 796, Hanji 795, and Hanji 794. It does not reconstruct Emperor An's full reign, complete family politics, full frontier strategy, or all palace actors. The current synthesis should stay two-sided: An can accept narrow or external correction, but he repeatedly fails to repair domestic palace-access problems. Hanji 794 makes the Pingyuan Wang Liu Yi accusation consequential without proving the alleged Deng plan; it is kept separate from the later Beixiang Hou Liu Yi route. Transcript variants such as 李闰/李润, 王胜/王圣, 薄荣/伯荣, 刘归/刘瑰, 翟规/翟瑰, 刘凯/刘恺, and 冯石/冯师 remain normalized cautiously.

## What Changed
- Added Hanji 794 as the new earliest layer: Deng regency tension, replacement fear, anti-Deng slander, first Deng purge wave, Chen Zhong's open-remonstrance warning, and Shi Yan's appointment.
- Separated Pingyuan Wang Liu Yi from the later Beixiang Hou Liu Yi succession route.
- Reframed An's reign around selective advice acceptance versus repeated domestic palace-access non-correction.
- Preserved Hanji 795-802 as the escalation from purge repair and favorite replacement to Yang Zhen's death, Liu Bao's deposition, and concealed succession control.
