## Overview

汉安帝 / Emperor An of Han now appears in the wiki through the prelude and trigger of the Shun succession crisis. Hanji 801 shows him as the active ruler who deposes the ten-year-old crown prince [[EmperorShunOfHan|刘保]] after palace slander and ministerial remonstrance, while Hanji 802 shows his sudden 125 CE death creating the information vacuum that lets [[EmpressDowagerYanLateHan|阎皇后 / 阎太后]], [[YanXianLateHan|阎显]], and palace allies exclude Liu Bao again and install [[LiuYiBeixiangHouLateHan|北乡侯刘懿 / 刘裔]].

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-801-han-andi-weishenme-henxin-fei-taizi-lgnhbsqnp6vdigsmqfkxiqmx2vdy|Hanji 801]] places Emperor An in 124 CE, before the death crisis. Wang Sheng, [[FanFengEunuchLateHan|樊丰]], and others first attack Liu Bao's wet nurse Wang Nan and kitchen supervisor Bing Ji; Fan Feng then works with Yan Hou to fabricate evidence against Liu Bao and his palace officers. An becomes angry, convenes senior ministers, and pushes toward deposition despite objections from [[LaiLiLateHan|来历]], [[HuanYanLateHan|桓焉]], and [[ZhangHaoRemonstratorLateHan|张皓]].

The same source makes An's ruler-security anxiety visible. After Liu Bao is formally demoted to Jiyin Wang on 124-09-07, more than ten officials remonstrate at Hongdu Gate that the child is blameless. The host reads An's reaction as fear that a ten-year-old prince with so much official sympathy could become an alternative political center. An's Zhongchangshi edict then threatens the remonstrants, accuses them of hidden motive, and punishes Lai Li's office, family, and fief position after he persists.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-802-andi-zhisi-donghan-wangchao-quanli-da-xipai-lgvne92e0jjzjnznzfkz7nvztnbj|Hanji 802]] then places Emperor An in 125 CE, his Yan Guang fourth year. During a southern tour, he becomes ill at [[WanCounty|宛城]], continues traveling while sick, and dies near Ye County at age thirty-two.

The political consequence is immediate. Because An dies away from [[LuoyangLateHan|洛阳]], Yan Hou, Yan Xian, and [[FanFengEunuchLateHan|樊丰]] can hide the death, keep routine food and medicine appearances, and return to the capital before public announcement. Once inside the palace, Yan Hou becomes empress dowager, Yan Xian receives military authority, and the Yan side chooses a young Beixiang Hou instead of An's son Liu Bao.

An's death therefore becomes an accession-control event whose vulnerability was made earlier by An himself. Hanji 801 explains why Liu Bao is no longer crown prince; Hanji 802 shows how that demotion lets the Yan side treat him as an excluded Jiyin Wang even though he remains the more emotionally and genealogically compelling heir. The Yan family's first post-death settlement then turns An-era allies such as [[GengBaoGrandGeneralLateHan|耿宝]] and Wang Sheng into purge targets.

## Key Characteristics

- Active ruler who converts palace slander and ministerial support for Liu Bao into a succession-security decision.
- Father of Liu Bao, later Emperor Shun, whose 124 CE deposition of the crown prince creates the crisis's legitimacy wound.
- Threatened ruler whose edict recodes remonstrance as hidden factional motive.
- Dead ruler whose 125 CE tour death creates a temporary information vacuum.
- Patron whose body and routines are used to preserve the appearance of continuity during the return to Luoyang.
- Predecessor whose court networks become vulnerable once the Yan-backed succession is announced.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 801 and Hanji 802. It does not reconstruct Emperor An's full reign, earlier relationship with the Yan family, complete palace-building policy, or the complete background of Geng Bao, Fan Feng, Wang Sheng, Wang Nan, and Bing Ji. Hanji 801's specific motive claim about An fearing Liu Bao's support is the host's interpretation of the recorded support field and threat edict.
