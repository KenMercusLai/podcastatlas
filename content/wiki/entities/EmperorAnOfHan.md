## Overview
汉安帝 / Emperor An of Han now appears in the wiki through a 120-125 CE sequence in which regency constraint and post-regency restoration never become stable public order. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-792-dengkang-renu-taihou-dagou-weishenme-yao-kan-zhuren-lh3xkjneqfd9sqijqo3rgzsa5vwn|Hanji 792]] opens with Liu Bao's crown-prince appointment, Qiang-front pressure, disasters, and [[DengKangLateHan|Deng Kang]]'s warning against Deng-family power while An is still under [[EmpressDowagerDengLateHan|Empress Dowager Deng]]'s regency; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-793-lishi-zhuming-nvhuang-dengsui-zenme-si-de-lszfgj7waiuusrddzjmv5mbrp1i|Hanji 793]] makes Deng's death, the restoration of return-power remonstrants, and An's natal-lineage honors the next layer; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-794-yidai-nvjun-dengsui-qushi-hou-jing-quanjia-zaoyang-llmlhabdd09opca6-pr80q4mfoxi|Hanji 794]] turns replacement fear around [[LiuYiPingyuanWangLateHan|Pingyuan Wang Liu Yi]] into purge; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-795-dengjia-miewang-hou-weihe-juchao-wei-tamen-mingyuan-lqvmbqrrookphb4becaqbp7h-wi1|Hanji 795]] shows partial correction and new favorites; Hanji 796-802 carry the reign through private-favor appointment erosion, uneven advice acceptance, Yang Zhen's destruction, Liu Bao's deposition, and concealed death on tour.

## Current Profile
Hanji 792 supplies the pre-personal-rule layer. In 120 CE, An's only son Liu Bao is made crown prince while Liu Chong, Liu Chang, and Liu Yi receive royal titles; the year also contains Qiang-front fighting around Zhangye and Jincheng, flood and earthquake notices, and major office turnover. Deng Kang's memorials show that even before Deng dies, the court-authority problem is visible inside the Deng family.

An's personal rule begins with a restoration signal. Hanji 793 says Empress Dowager Deng becomes ill and dies in 121 CE; after that, An accepts [[ChenZhongLateHan|Chen Zhong]]'s recommendation of upright men, summons [[DuGenLateHan|Du Gen]] and [[ChengYishiLateHan|Cheng Yishi]], appoints them, and honors his father Liu Qing, birth mother Zuo Shi, and grandmother Song Guiren. The moment distinguishes personal rule from the regency's suppression of return-power remonstrance, but it does not yet show stable governance.

Hanji 794 then makes the transfer unstable rather than clean. Deng had installed An, delayed returning authority, and kept collateral royal sons in Luoyang. [[WangShengPalaceActorLateHan|Wang Sheng]], [[LiRunLateHan|Li Run]], and [[JiangJingLateHan|Jiang Jing]] help turn the fear that An may be deposed into palace slander; after Deng dies, the alleged plan to install Pingyuan Wang Liu Yi becomes the trigger for a rapid purge of the [[DengFamilyAnEraLateHan|Deng family]].

The Deng purge defines An's legitimacy problem. He can punish forcefully, but [[ZhuChongLateHan|Zhu Chong]] later exposes the weakness of proof, interrogation, and charge clarity. An partially retreats by blaming local over-enforcement, permitting Deng corpses to return for burial, and allowing surviving cousins back to Luoyang. The correction is limited: the defeated Deng network is replaced by Geng, Song, Yan, eunuch, wet-nurse, and favorite-household networks rather than by impersonal appointment order.

An is not portrayed as unable to hear any advice. Hanji 793 shows him accepting Chen Zhong's early personnel recommendations; Hanji 794 shows Chen warning him to tolerate sharp criticism during worthy-and-upright selection, after which [[ShiYanLateHan|Shi Yan]] is appointed shizhong. Hanji 796 shows him accepting [[LiuKaiEasternHan|Liu Kai]] and Chen Zhong's narrow punishment-limit argument; Hanji 797 shows him accepting Chen's advice not to attack [[GoguryeoLateHan|Goguryeo]] during royal mourning; Hanji 799 shows him accepting Chen's Western Regions retention argument by appointing [[BanYongLateHan|Ban Yong]] to Liuzhong.

The advice he repeatedly fails to convert is domestic correction against palace access. [[YangZhenLateHan|Yang Zhen]] and [[ZhaiGuiLateHan|Zhai Gui]] warn against Wang Sheng's household, [[BoRongLateHan|Bo Rong]], [[LiuGuiLateHan|Liu Gui]]'s title path, and the loss of public control over offices and rewards. Chen Zhong warns that Bo Rong's missions burden local people and that the Three Excellencies bear blame without real authority. An instead exposes memorials, ignores broad warnings, funds Wang Sheng's mansion, executes [[ZhaoTengLateHan|Zhao Teng]], and lets Fan Feng's group turn the forged-edict case against Yang Zhen.

The reign closes by repeating the succession-insecurity pattern. In Hanji 801, An treats broad support for his son [[EmperorShunOfHan|Liu Bao]] as a threat and deposes him after palace accusation. In Hanji 802, An dies away from [[LuoyangLateHan|Luoyang]], letting [[EmpressDowagerYanLateHan|Yan Hou / Yan Taihou]], [[YanXianLateHan|Yan Xian]], and [[FanFengEunuchLateHan|Fan Feng]] hide the death, return to the capital, and install [[LiuYiBeixiangHouLateHan|Beixiang Hou Liu Yi]] while excluding Liu Bao again.

## Key Characteristics
- Ruler whose profile now begins under Deng regency in 120 CE before personal rule opens with restored remonstrants and natal-lineage honors after Deng's death.
- Post-regency ruler whose restoration signal quickly gives way to replacement fear and a severe Deng-family purge.
- Partial corrector who limits Deng-family over-punishment only after public mourning and Zhu Chong's coffin memorial.
- Selectively receptive ruler who accepts bounded legal, personnel, and frontier advice while ignoring broader domestic institutional correction.
- Ruler whose court converts kinship, accession service, wet-nurse status, palace marriage, and favorite access into title and office pressure.
- Executor or punisher of correction figures including Zhao Teng, Yang Zhen, and Lai Li.
- Father of Liu Bao whose 124 CE deposition and 125 CE tour death create the succession legitimacy wound.

## Qualifications
This page is bounded to Hanji 802, Hanji 801, Hanji 800, Hanji 799, Hanji 798, Hanji 797, Hanji 796, Hanji 795, Hanji 794, Hanji 793, and Hanji 792. It does not reconstruct Emperor An's full reign, complete family politics, full frontier strategy, or all palace actors. The current synthesis should stay two-sided: An can accept narrow, personnel, or external correction, but he repeatedly fails to repair domestic palace-access problems. Hanji 792 makes Liu Bao's crown-prince appointment and Deng Kang's warning part of the pre-personal-rule field, not proof that An controlled the regency setting. Hanji 794 makes the Pingyuan Wang Liu Yi accusation consequential without proving the alleged Deng plan; it is kept separate from the later Beixiang Hou Liu Yi route. Liu Qing, Zuo Shi, Song Guiren, Liu Chong, Liu Chang, and the Hanji 792 disaster/tribute notices are kept source-scoped here. Transcript variants such as 李闰/李润, 王胜/王圣, 薄荣/伯荣, 刘归/刘瑰, 翟规/翟瑰, 刘凯/刘恺, and 冯石/冯师 remain normalized cautiously.

## What Changed
- Added Hanji 792 as the new earliest layer: 120 CE crown-prince appointment, regency crisis setting, Deng Kang's warning, and frontier/disaster pressure before An's personal rule.
- Clarified that restoration begins before, but does not prevent, the later Deng purge and favorite-household replacement field.
- Separated Pingyuan Wang Liu Yi from the later Beixiang Hou Liu Yi succession route.
- Reframed An's reign around selective advice acceptance versus repeated domestic palace-access non-correction.
- Preserved Hanji 794-802 as the escalation from Deng-purge fear to Yang Zhen's death, Liu Bao's deposition, and concealed succession control.
