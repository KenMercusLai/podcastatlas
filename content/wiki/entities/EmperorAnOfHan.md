## Overview

汉安帝 / Emperor An of Han appears in the wiki through the 125 CE succession crisis created by his sudden death on tour. This source uses An less as an active ruler than as the dead patron whose absence lets [[EmpressDowagerYanLateHan|阎皇后 / 阎太后]], [[YanXianLateHan|阎显]], and palace allies control information, exclude [[EmperorShunOfHan|刘保]], and install [[LiuYiBeixiangHouLateHan|北乡侯刘懿 / 刘裔]].

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-802-andi-zhisi-donghan-wangchao-quanli-da-xipai-lgvne92e0jjzjnznzfkz7nvztnbj|Hanji 802]] places Emperor An in 125 CE, his Yan Guang fourth year. During a southern tour, he becomes ill at [[WanCounty|宛城]], continues traveling while sick, and dies near Ye County at age thirty-two.

The political consequence is immediate. Because An dies away from [[LuoyangLateHan|洛阳]], Yan Hou, Yan Xian, and [[FanFengEunuchLateHan|樊丰]] can hide the death, keep routine food and medicine appearances, and return to the capital before public announcement. Once inside the palace, Yan Hou becomes empress dowager, Yan Xian receives military authority, and the Yan side chooses a young Beixiang Hou instead of An's son Liu Bao.

An's death therefore becomes an accession-control event. The source emphasizes that Liu Bao's prior demotion makes him vulnerable even though he remains the more emotionally and genealogically compelling heir, while the Yan family's first post-death settlement turns An-era allies such as [[GengBaoGrandGeneralLateHan|耿宝]] and Wang Sheng into purge targets.

## Key Characteristics

- Dead ruler whose tour death creates a temporary information vacuum.
- Father of Liu Bao, later Emperor Shun, whose exclusion becomes the crisis's legitimacy wound.
- Patron whose body and routines are used to preserve the appearance of continuity during the return to Luoyang.
- Predecessor whose court networks become vulnerable once the Yan-backed succession is announced.
- Source-bounded figure here; the page does not yet synthesize An's full reign.

## Evidence

- Sudden death on tour: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-802-andi-zhisi-donghan-wangchao-quanli-da-xipai-lgvne92e0jjzjnznzfkz7nvztnbj|Hanji 802]] says An becomes ill at Wan, travels onward, and dies near Ye County at age thirty-two.
- Concealed return: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-802-andi-zhisi-donghan-wangchao-quanli-da-xipai-lgvne92e0jjzjnznzfkz7nvztnbj|Hanji 802]] says the Yan side hides the death with food, medicine, and rest routines while returning to Luoyang.
- Succession exclusion: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-802-andi-zhisi-donghan-wangchao-quanli-da-xipai-lgvne92e0jjzjnznzfkz7nvztnbj|Hanji 802]] says Liu Bao is excluded from the accession arrangement despite being An's son and wanting to mourn.
- Network aftermath: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-802-andi-zhisi-donghan-wangchao-quanli-da-xipai-lgvne92e0jjzjnznzfkz7nvztnbj|Hanji 802]] says Geng Bao, Fan Feng, Wang Sheng, and others from the old An-era power field are removed after the Yan-backed accession.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 802. It does not reconstruct Emperor An's full reign, earlier treatment of Liu Bao, earlier relationship with the Yan family, or the complete background of Geng Bao, Fan Feng, and Wang Sheng. The source's main value is the transition mechanism after An's death, not a full biography.
