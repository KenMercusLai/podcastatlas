# Roger Barnsley

## Overview

Roger Barnsley is a psychologist associated with foundational research on birth-month distributions in elite Canadian youth hockey and the naming of the [[RelativeAgeEffect]].

## Current Profile

In [[outliers-revisited-outliers-revisited|Outliers, Revisited]], Barnsley and his wife Paula notice that competitive Canadian hockey rosters contain many players born early in the eligibility year and few born late. Barnsley interprets this as a selection-system effect: January 1 cutoffs make relatively older children look more capable at ages when a few months can materially affect size, coordination, and confidence.

The episode returns to him decades later and reports that the skew remains visible in a contemporary Canadian junior national roster. His role in the source is therefore both empirical and institutional: identifying the pattern did not by itself make hockey redesign its talent pipeline.

## Key Characteristics

- Psychologist associated with the early hockey research behind the relative-age effect.
- Connects calendar cutoffs to unequal access to elite teams, practice, coaching, and competition.
- Used by the episode to show the gap between a known selection bias and durable institutional correction.

## Evidence

### Birth-month pattern

- [[outliers-revisited-outliers-revisited]] attributes the roughly 40–30–20–10 quarterly birth distribution in elite Canadian hockey to Barnsley's research.

### Institutional persistence

- [[outliers-revisited-outliers-revisited]] reports that Barnsley and Gladwell found the early-year skew still present on a later junior national roster.

## Qualifications

The source is a structured podcast summary rather than Barnsley's paper, roster dataset, or a review of the later literature. Exact distributions, replication boundaries, causal estimates, and Barnsley's complete scholarly position remain source-scoped.
