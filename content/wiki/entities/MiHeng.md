# 芈横 / 楚顷襄王 / Mi Heng

芈横 / Mi Heng appears in [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-60-zhongguo-di-yi-wei-bachi-chaozheng-de-taihou-jingshi-ta-ltazis9qo0dwvwmzxikk8mmpvosc]] as the [[ChuState|楚国]] crown prince sent to [[QinState|秦国]] as hostage when [[QiState|齐国]], [[HanState|韩国]], and [[WeiState|魏国]] attack Chu for moving toward Qin. His hostage status is part of the Qin-Chu rescue bargain: [[ChuHuaiwang|楚怀王]] asks for help, and Qin sends [[KeqingTong|客卿通]] to aid Chu.

The same episode turns Mi Heng into the hinge of alliance collapse. After a private quarrel with a Qin official, he kills the official and escapes back to Chu without an apology. The host reads this as the event that breaks [[XuanTaihou|芈八子 / 宣太后]]'s Qin-Chu alignment and gives [[QinZhaoxiangwang|秦昭襄王]] a later pretext to attack Chu.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-61-shui-shi-lishishang-daner-zui-fei-de-diwang-lvpoaszanascnlrmu2atinlxzmk1]] follows the consequences. Qin joins Han, Wei, and Qi in attacking Chu after Mi Heng's killing and escape, and the next year Qin's Mi Rong again defeats Chu. [[ChuHuaiwang|楚怀王]] responds by sending Mi Heng to [[QiState|齐国]] as hostage to seek peace, so the same crown prince moves from breaking one hostage arrangement to becoming the object of another.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-62-ji-er-kuang-ni-jiujiu-de-tui-a-lumch-ibj8m94abjh5nvr3qhiqff]] makes Mi Heng the answer to Chu's succession emergency after [[QinZhaoxiangwang|秦昭襄王]] detains [[ChuHuaiwang|楚怀王]] in [[Xianyang|咸阳]]. [[ChuState|楚国]] asks [[QiState|齐国]] to return him by falsely saying the old king has died; [[QiMinWang|齐湣王]] debates whether to hold him for land north of the Huai River, then releases him. Mi Heng returns to Chu and succeeds as Chu Qingxiang Wang, turning hostage return into royal-continuity repair.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-63-degao-wangzhong-mengchangjun-shi-weijunzi-lmd0owe3rq-faponbwzhkaigprin]] shows the external consequence of that succession repair. Chu sends envoys to [[QinState|秦国]] to announce that it already has a new king, which makes Qin's detention of Chu Huaiwang less useful as coercive leverage. Qin responds by attacking Chu through [[Wuguan|武关]], killing fifty thousand troops and taking sixteen cities.
