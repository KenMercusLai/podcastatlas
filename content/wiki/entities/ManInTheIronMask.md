# The Man in the Iron Mask

## Overview

The Man in the Iron Mask is a documented masked state prisoner whose sparse record became a legend of royal birth, hidden identity, and absolutist secrecy.

## Current Profile

[[411-the-man-in-the-iron-mask-glt3787137343]] separates a firm evidentiary core from its literary afterlife. Contemporary notices place an unnamed masked prisoner under [[SaintMars]] in 1687, at the Bastille in 1698, and at death in 1703; the mask appears as steel in one report and black velvet in another. The episode identifies [[EustacheDege]] as the strongest candidate through earlier correspondence, status, chronology, and transfers. [[Voltaire]] and [[AlexandreDumas]] then convert uncertainty into royal-brother and twin narratives whose cultural force exceeds their documentary basis.

## Key Characteristics

- Real masked imprisonment supported by multiple contemporary records.
- Literal iron construction uncertain and contradicted by the black-velvet notice.
- Most plausibly identified with a valet rather than royalty.
- Cultural identity shaped by Voltaire and Dumas more than by the sparse archive.

## Qualifications

- The archive supports a masked prisoner more strongly than an iron mask.
- The Degé identification is the best fit presented by the source, not a final explanation of his imprisonment.
- Exceptional treatment and royal identity derive from later reports and fiction rather than contemporary proof.
