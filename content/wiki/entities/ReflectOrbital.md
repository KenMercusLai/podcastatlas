# Reflect Orbital

## Overview
Reflect Orbital is a California startup proposing mirror-equipped satellites that redirect sunlight toward selected ground areas after sunset.

## Current Profile
The source presents Reflect Orbital as an early-stage [[OrbitalReflectedSunlight]] project rather than a proven energy provider. Its planned Earendil-1 test would place an aluminium-coated mirror roughly the size of a volleyball court in low Earth orbit and create a broad, moonlike patch of light. That test would not power solar panels; the commercial proposal depends on combining beams from a much larger constellation around dusk.

The intended market is hybrid rather than battery-free. Reflected light would extend solar-panel operation while batteries handle ordinary time shifting, with the constellation competing mainly against expensive reserve capacity for longer periods of poor weather. Astronomical interference, ecological disturbance, launch scale, targeting reliability, and cost remain central constraints.

## Key Characteristics
- Proposes controllable reflected sunlight from low-Earth-orbit mirror satellites.
- Treats Earendil-1 as a low-intensity technical test rather than commercial proof.
- Targets solar-farm operating-hour extension as the main economic use described by the episode.
- Positions orbital light alongside batteries, not as a universal storage replacement.
- Faces scale, cost, astronomy, ecology, targeting, and governance constraints.

## Evidence
- Demonstration profile: [[valley-of-the-shadow-of-debt-bond-market-jitters-69104aa52182b702669d450d025a7dac]] describes a planned 600-kilometre orbit, a mirror roughly the size of a volleyball court, and a light patch about 5.5 kilometres across.
- Commercial model: [[valley-of-the-shadow-of-debt-bond-market-jitters-69104aa52182b702669d450d025a7dac]] says many satellites would combine beams around dusk and complement batteries by targeting costly reserve needs.
- Externalities and mitigation: [[valley-of-the-shadow-of-debt-bond-market-jitters-69104aa52182b702669d450d025a7dac]] compares the astronomy risk with [[Starlink]] and records the company's openness to telescope exclusion zones.

## Qualifications
The source does not establish that Earendil-1 has launched, that multiple beams can be combined reliably, that the system can activate solar farms economically, or that telescope exclusion zones would address ecological and wider sky-brightness effects. Satellite counts, launch economics, maintenance, liability, consent, and regulation remain unresolved.
