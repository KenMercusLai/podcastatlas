# 小海 / Xiaohai (poet)

## Overview
小海 / Xiaohai is a Chinese poet and memoirist represented here through his account of factory labor, migration, rock music, poetry, secondhand-clothing work, and the bilingual memoir [[YiKuaiGunShiBook|《一块滚石》 / A Drift in the South]].

## Current Profile
The episode presents Xiaohai as a worker whose artistic life grew inside, not after, repeated factory and service jobs. Writing first gave form to alienation during night shifts; Tang poetry expanded his mental world beyond output and wages; Hai Zi helped make worker life writable; and rock, Ginsberg, and Dylan supplied rhythm, companionship, and models of artistic persistence. His later move to Beijing and relatively flexible secondhand-clothing-shop work widened the time and community available for writing without ending economic pressure.

The profile therefore resists making “migrant worker poet” either an embarrassment or a complete identity. Labor is indispensable context, but Xiaohai's work also records pleasure, curiosity, friendship, performance, ambition, family support, and a changing understanding of freedom. The “rolling stone” is not frictionless mobility: it names repeated obstruction, continued labor, and movement without a guaranteed destination.

## Key Characteristics
- Developed a writing practice during factory labor and used poetry to preserve perception, memory, and self-recognition.
- Draws artistic resources from classical Chinese poetry, modern Chinese poetry, rock music, Beat poetry, and singer-songwriters.
- Treats worker experience as legitimate poetic material without accepting that social identity alone establishes literary value.
- Uses voice, rhythm, guitar, song, and bodily performance to carry poetry beyond silent reading.
- Values partial freedom over conventional status markers while acknowledging money, aging, marriage, and family expectations as persistent constraints.
- Understands publication and international readers as relationships created by literature rather than proof that practical insecurity has disappeared.
