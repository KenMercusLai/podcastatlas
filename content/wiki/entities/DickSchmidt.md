# Dick Schmidt

## Overview

Dick Schmidt is presented as a UCLA human-movement researcher whose work on driver foot movement helps explain sudden-acceleration reports as errors of action rather than necessarily as vehicle defects.

## Current Profile

In [[blame-game-blame-game|Blame Game]], Schmidt broadens the inquiry beyond [[Toyota]]. Sudden-acceleration complaints appeared across manufacturers and clustered around conditions that can destabilize learned movement: unfamiliar vehicles, parking or low-speed maneuvers, altered footwell geometry, shorter or older drivers, and moments soon after entering a car.

His key contribution is “impulse variability,” the gap between a movement a person intends and the movement the body executes. Applied to driving, a person can sincerely experience themselves as braking while their foot is pressing the accelerator. That account supports [[PedalMisapplicationUnderStress]] without implying carelessness or dishonesty.

## Key Characteristics

- UCLA professor associated with human movement and driver foot use.
- Studied sudden-acceleration reports across vehicle manufacturers.
- Connected incidents to unfamiliarity, driver geometry, and transitional driving contexts.
- Used impulse variability to distinguish intended from executed movement.
- Supplies the episode's main bridge between vehicle evidence and non-negligent human error.
