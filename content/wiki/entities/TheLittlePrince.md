# 《小王子》 / The Little Prince

[[07-yong-rongge-lilun-fenxi-xiaowangzi-yongheng-shaonian-ji-qita-539091165]] turns [[TheLittlePrince|《小王子》 / The Little Prince]] from a comparison point into a central source branch. [[MihuanChishu|蜜獾吃书]] rereads the book through [[CarlJung|Carl Jung / 荣格]], [[MarieLouiseVonFranz|Marie-Louise von Franz / 玛丽-路易丝·冯·弗兰兹]], and [[TheEternalYouth|《永恒少年：我们为何拒绝长大》]], treating the little prince as a [[PuerAeternus|puer aeternus / 永恒少年]] figure whose beauty and danger have to be held together.

The episode's darker claim is that the little prince preserves imagination and spiritual protest against adult utility, but repeatedly fails at relation. He leaves the rose, is taught by the fox, leaves the fox too, and then uses the snake as an exit that may be less a responsible return than [[RomanticDeathEscape|romanticized disappearance]]. This extends the page beyond melancholy comparison into [[ResponsibilityAgainstRomanticEscape]] and [[StatisticalPoisoningOfTheSoul]].

[[TheLittlePrince|《小王子》 / The Little Prince]] appears in [[180-ailisi-mengyou-xianjing-shijie-duo-huangdan-wo-yeshi-ziji-de-zhuzai-xia-975539611]] as the main comparison point for [[AliceInWonderland|《爱丽丝梦游仙境》]]. The episode says both works involve a childlike traveler, strange worlds, and episodic encounters, but it separates their emotional centers.

The source reads [[AliceWonderlandCharacter|Alice / 爱丽丝]] as more active, embodied, and life-oriented, while The Little Prince is framed as closer to adult projection, melancholy, return, and death-shadow. The comparison strengthens the wiki's [[FemaleSelfPossession]] and [[ChildrensLiteratureComplexity]] branches by showing that child protagonists can carry very different theories of agency.

## Key Claims
- The book can be read as both a children's classic and an adult psychological self-portrait about refusal, projection, death, and responsibility.
- The little prince's innocence is morally unstable: he sees what adults miss, but he also converts relations with the rose and fox into stages of his own revelation.
- The snake ending is not treated by episode 07 as pure transcendence; it may be the extreme form of eternal-youth escape.
- The five thousand roses scene supports [[StatisticalPoisoningOfTheSoul]] because it contrasts interchangeable quantity with one relation made unique by care.
- The comparison clarifies Alice by contrast: she survives, argues, adjusts, and wakes rather than longing to leave life behind.
- A children's classic can feel philosophical without giving the same emotional instruction as another children's classic.
- The episode values Alice's practical vitality over a more elegiac child-as-adult-symbol mode.
