## Overview

Pope Clement VII / 克莱曼特七世, born Giulio de' Medici, was the family's second pope in the episode and a ruler whose attempt to maneuver between France and Charles V ended in the 1527 Sack of Rome and an imperial-backed restoration of Medici power in Florence.

## Current Profile

Giulio was presented as the posthumous son of Giuliano, killed in the Pazzi conspiracy, and was raised within the Medici system before entering the church. Elected pope in 1523, he inherited a debt-heavy papacy and a European balance dominated by France and Charles V.

His decision to join an anti-imperial alliance exceeded the papacy's reliable fiscal and military capacity. Unpaid imperial troops entered Rome in 1527, producing mass violence, plunder, damage, and Clement's flight into fortified refuge. The episode then follows compromise with Charles V: imperial force helped restore Medici control in Florence, and Clement used marriage and appointments to secure the family through Alessandro and Catherine de' Medici. His career therefore joins strategic miscalculation, institutional weakness, survival, and dynastic recovery.

## Key Characteristics

- Medici churchman formed by the family's recovery after the Pazzi crisis.
- Pope inheriting debt and intense Franco-imperial competition.
- Alliance-maker whose anti-imperial choice outran papal military capacity.
- Survivor and loser of the 1527 Sack of Rome.
- Dynastic broker using imperial compromise, Florentine restoration, appointments, and marriage.
- Transitional figure between informal republican dominance and hereditary Medici rule.

## Qualifications

The source is a compressed narrative, not a complete diplomatic or military history. It does not make Clement solely responsible for the unpaid imperial army, the Sack of Rome, or every later Florentine settlement. Giulio's legitimacy, Alessandro's parentage, alliance causation, negotiations, troop control, and marriage motives remain disputed or source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Created a profile linking papal alliance failure to institutional incapacity and the Sack of Rome.
- Added imperial compromise and marriage as recovery mechanisms after military catastrophe.
- Positioned Clement at the transition from informal Medici control to hereditary rule.
