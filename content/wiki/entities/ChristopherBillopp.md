# Christopher Billopp

## Overview

Christopher Billopp (1738–1827) was a wealthy [[StatenIsland|Staten Island]] landowner, colonial legislator, and Loyalist militia commander whose repeated capture, property loss, exile, and later New Brunswick service expose the civil-war stakes of the [[AmericanRevolution|American Revolution]].

## Current Profile

Billopp inherited the 932-acre Bentley Manor estate and used political and militia authority to oppose the Continental boycott and support British forces. His prominence made him a target for [[NathanielFitzRandolph|Nathaniel Fitz Randolph]] and other Patriot raiders, who abducted him more than once for use in prisoner exchanges. He refused George Washington's offer of protected return in exchange for resigning his command.

His local authority nevertheless eroded under occupation. During the frozen American incursion of January 1780, no militia member followed his call to defend Richmond. New York confiscated his estate and banished him; after the war he sought compensation in Britain, moved to Nova Scotia, and served in the first New Brunswick Assembly and provincial council.

## Key Characteristics

- Prominent hereditary landowner at Bentley Manor on Staten Island's southern tip.
- New York Assembly member who resisted Continental organization and boycott enforcement.
- Loyalist militia commander who materially supported British occupation forces.
- Repeated kidnapping and exchange target in the New Jersey–Staten Island border war.
- Uncompromising Crown loyalist even after imprisonment and local militia collapse.
- Exile who rebuilt a public career in British North America.

## Qualifications

The source leaves Billopp's first release uncertain and treats Bentley Manor as the probable rather than certain site of the September 1776 peace conference. His gravestone's claim of leaving without compensation conflicts with the episode's account of a lump-sum payment and annual allowance. Property valuations, motives, and exact exchange mechanics remain episode-reported.
