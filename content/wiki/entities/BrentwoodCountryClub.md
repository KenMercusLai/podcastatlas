# Brentwood Country Club

## Overview

Brentwood Country Club is a private Los Angeles golf club used by [[MalcolmGladwell]] as the physical starting point for an argument about scarce urban land, public exclusion, and preferential property-tax treatment.

## Current Profile

The bounded source contrasts the club's mature trees and extensive green space with a narrow, rocky running path outside its fence. Landscape architect Dai Zock describes the grounds as a potential public park and says that making it welcoming would begin with removing the barbed wire.

Gladwell and Zock later observe more people using the exterior path on a Saturday afternoon than the course itself. The scene does not establish a complete usage study, but it makes the distributive question concrete: who can use scarce open space when the public helps subsidize its current form?

## Key Characteristics

- Large private golf property in land-scarce Los Angeles.
- Mature, park-like landscape physically separated from surrounding residents.
- Central observational case for the episode's land-use and access argument.
- Candidate for scheduled public access under a reciprocity model.
