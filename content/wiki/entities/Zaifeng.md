# Zaifeng / 载沣

## Overview

Zaifeng was the late Qing prince who became regent for his young son Puyi after the deaths of the Guangxu Emperor and Empress Dowager Cixi. The source presents him as a reluctant dynastic insider whose effort to restore imperial-clan control deepened the court's political isolation.

## Current Profile

Zaifeng inherited rank and proximity to the throne but had little experience of sustained political competition or top-level administration. Once made regent, he treated recovery of military and administrative power by the Aisin Gioro clan as a priority. He dismissed Yuan Shikai without dismantling Yuan's Beiyang relationships, marginalized experienced or competing figures, assumed supreme military titles, and promoted imperial relatives.

The prince-heavy cabinet made the contradiction visible: a constitutional reform expected to broaden participation instead looked like dynastic concentration. Zaifeng thus gained nominal control while losing coalition support and reliable command leverage. After the 1911 Revolution he left the regency and did not attempt to recover supreme power; his later life, anti-collaboration posture, educational donation, and comparatively peaceful death contrast with his failed political tenure.

## Key Characteristics

- Dynastic legitimacy and fatherhood to the child emperor supplied formal authority.
- Limited preparation for crisis government and high-level coalition management.
- Sought to restore military and administrative control to the imperial clan.
- Removed Yuan Shikai without taking control of the Beiyang network.
- Withdrew from supreme politics after the regency collapsed.

## Qualifications

The source's portrait of Zaifeng as gentle, weak, cautious, or relieved to resign depends partly on retrospective family testimony and interpretation. It does not establish that his decisions alone caused Qing collapse. Precise cabinet counts, personal motives, military control, and later-life anecdotes remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Established a focused profile of Zaifeng as the Qing dynasty's last regent.
- Distinguished possession of court titles from control over military and political networks.
- Connected clan concentration and constitutional disappointment to regency legitimacy loss.
