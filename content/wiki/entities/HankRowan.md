# Hank Rowan

## Overview

Henry “Hank” Rowan was the founder of Inductotherm and the philanthropist whose $100 million gift to a resource-constrained New Jersey public college anchors [[my-little-hundred-million-my-little-hundred-million|My Little Hundred Million]].

## Current Profile

The episode presents Rowan as an engineer-industrialist shaped by manufacturing rather than prestige consumption. He founded Inductotherm in a garage in 1953 and built it into an international maker of metal-melting furnaces. When Glassboro State College approached him for modest support, he redirected the conversation toward a much larger engineering and production investment.

Rowan's allocative judgment is the durable part of the profile. Although he graduated from MIT, he believed another building there would produce less change than an engineering school at Glassboro. His gift therefore exemplifies [[PhilanthropicNeglectedness]]: the recipient's starting resources and capacity to convert money into broad access matter to the value of the next dollar.

## Key Characteristics

- Engineer and manufacturing entrepreneur who founded Inductotherm in 1953.
- Operationally demanding leader described by his daughter as a “benevolent dictator.”
- Personally frugal industrialist with little interest in conspicuous consumption.
- Donor who redirected a small solicitation toward a $100 million engineering investment.
- MIT alumnus who chose a less-resourced regional public college for greater marginal effect.
- Philanthropist whose local institutional impact produced an unusually visible community legacy.

## Evidence

### Engineering and manufacturing orientation

- [[my-little-hundred-million-my-little-hundred-million]] connects Rowan's garage-founded furnace company, work ethic, and production values to his insistence on funding engineering rather than a business-school proposal.

### Transformative recipient choice

- [[my-little-hundred-million-my-little-hundred-million]] reports that Glassboro's endowment was about $787,000 when Rowan made the gift and contrasts the resulting engineering school with the smaller effect he expected at MIT.

### Local legacy

- [[my-little-hundred-million-my-little-hundred-million]] opens with students commemorating Rowan around his campus statue after his 2015 death and uses that response as evidence of a transformation visible to one community.

## Qualifications

This profile rests on one admiring, argumentative episode summary rather than company records, the gift agreement, audited university outcomes, or a critical biography. The source supports Rowan's recipient-selection logic but does not establish the gift's causal effect against a counterfactual, compare every alternative recipient, or show that regional institutions always outperform elite ones.
