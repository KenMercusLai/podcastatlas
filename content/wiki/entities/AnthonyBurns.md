# Anthony Burns

## Overview

Anthony Burns is the fugitive from slavery whose 1854 arrest and forced return from Boston made federal slave-catching visible inside a city associated with revolutionary liberty.

## Current Profile

[[200-american-civil-war-the-causes-part-1-glt1124511401]] treats Burns's rendition as a radicalizing public event. Arrested in Boston, he was marched in chains to the harbor amid tolling church bells and returned south aboard a federal ship. The spectacle showed that slavery's legal reach did not stop at the borders of a free state.

Burns was later purchased by Northern antislavery supporters and died in 1862. His case matters here less as an isolated biography than as an example of how the [[FugitiveSlaveAct1850|Fugitive Slave Act]] converted an abstract constitutional dispute into witnessed coercion.

## Key Characteristics

- Fugitive from slavery arrested in Boston in 1854.
- Subject of a highly visible federal rendition to the South.
- Individual whose treatment exposed slavery's reach into a free-state community.
- Later purchased by antislavery supporters before his death in 1862.
