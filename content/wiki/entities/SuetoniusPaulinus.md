# Suetonius Paulinus

## Overview

Suetonius Paulinus was the Roman governor who returned from campaigning in Wales, concentrated an outnumbered force, and defeated [[Boudicca]]'s revolt in AD 60 before excessive reprisals led to his replacement.

## Current Profile

Episode 501 presents Paulinus as a ruthless but effective crisis commander. His assault on Anglesey had drawn Roman forces away from the southeast when revolt began. Returning rapidly, he assessed London but sacrificed it and Verulamium rather than divide his army defending unwalled settlements, then sought a battlefield where terrain and Roman formation could neutralize the rebels' numbers.

Victory preserved [[RomanBritain]] and restored the credibility on which Rome's collaborators depended. Yet Paulinus's subsequent devastation threatened the province's recovery. His removal in AD 61 marks the limit of punitive command: the violence that suppresses a revolt can become an obstacle to durable rule.

## Key Characteristics

- Aggressive governor whose Welsh campaign created both military achievement and temporary vulnerability elsewhere.
- Commander willing to abandon valuable cities to preserve a force for decisive battle.
- User of roads, concentration, terrain, armor, formation, and discipline against a larger rebel host.
- Victor whose reprisals ended Iceni autonomy but became excessive even within Roman strategic judgment.
- Transitional figure between emergency military survival and post-revolt administrative repair.

## Evidence

- Strategic absence: [[501-the-roman-conquest-of-britain-boudiccas-reign-of-blood-part-3-glt3128836892]] places him in Anglesey while the southeast was treated as secure.
- Operational choices: [[501-the-roman-conquest-of-britain-boudiccas-reign-of-blood-part-3-glt3128836892]] describes his rapid return, troop concentration, and evacuation of London and Verulamium.
- Victory: [[501-the-roman-conquest-of-britain-boudiccas-reign-of-blood-part-3-glt3128836892]] attributes the rebel defeat to chosen terrain and Roman tactical discipline.
- Reprisal limit: [[501-the-roman-conquest-of-britain-boudiccas-reign-of-blood-part-3-glt3128836892]] says his post-battle severity contributed to his replacement in 61.

## Qualifications

The battle's location is unknown, with Mancetter and Windridge Farm among proposed sites. Force sizes, casualty counts, his movements, the reason the Second Legion failed to join, and the precise administrative grounds for his replacement remain dependent on incomplete literary and archaeological evidence.

## What Changed

- Created a profile joining tactical success to the strategic failure of excessive reprisal.
- Added his evacuation decisions as force-preservation choices rather than simple abandonment.
- Added provincial credibility and collaborator protection as political stakes of his victory.

## Relationships

- [[Boudicca]] - rebel leader whose coalition he defeated.
- [[BoudiccanRevolt]] - crisis that defines his governorship in the source.
- [[RomanBritain]] - province he preserved militarily but endangered through reprisals.
- [[Iceni]] - polity whose independence ended after his victory.
- [[RomanProvincialConsolidation]] - longer process that required restraint after his emergency campaign.
