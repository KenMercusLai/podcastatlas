# Suetonius Paulinus

## Overview

Suetonius Paulinus was the Roman governor who completed a destructive assault on Anglesey, returned to confront [[Boudicca]]'s revolt in AD 60, and preserved the province before excessive reprisals led to his replacement.

## Current Profile

Episode 500 supplies the campaign prehistory. Sent under Nero to resolve the prolonged Welsh war, Paulinus defeated the Silures and Ordovices, built northern Welsh forts, and crossed the Menai Strait with a large force. The Romans killed and enslaved people on Anglesey and destroyed sacred groves in a scene reported by [[Tacitus]] through the imagery of armed defenders, women with firebrands, and Druids. News of revolt arrived immediately after this victory.

Episode 501 presents Paulinus as a ruthless but effective crisis commander. His assault on Anglesey had drawn Roman forces away from the southeast when revolt began. Returning rapidly, he assessed London but sacrificed it and Verulamium rather than divide his army defending unwalled settlements, then sought a battlefield where terrain and Roman formation could neutralize the rebels' numbers.

Victory preserved [[RomanBritain]] and restored the credibility on which Rome's collaborators depended. Yet Paulinus's subsequent devastation threatened the province's recovery. His removal in AD 61 marks the limit of punitive command: the violence that suppresses a revolt can become an obstacle to durable rule.

## Key Characteristics

- Aggressive governor whose Welsh campaign created both military achievement and temporary vulnerability elsewhere.
- Commander whose Anglesey victory combined amphibious logistics, mass violence, enslavement, and religious destruction.
- Commander willing to abandon valuable cities to preserve a force for decisive battle.
- User of roads, concentration, terrain, armor, formation, and discipline against a larger rebel host.
- Victor whose reprisals ended Iceni autonomy but became excessive even within Roman strategic judgment.
- Transitional figure between emergency military survival and post-revolt administrative repair.

## Qualifications

Tacitus's Anglesey tableau may preserve testimony connected to Agricola but is also highly literary, and claims about Druidic sacrifice served Roman conquest narratives. The Boudiccan battle's location is unknown, with Mancetter and Windridge Farm among proposed sites. Force sizes, casualty counts, movements, the Second Legion's failure to join, and the precise grounds for Paulinus's replacement remain dependent on incomplete evidence.
