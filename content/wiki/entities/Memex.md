# Memex

## Overview
Memex is the conceptual information device discussed in [[episode-224-bushi-bushi-bushi-bushi-8213451338-252768|Episode 224: 不是不识不时不适]] through [[VannevarBush]]'s "As We May Think." The source uses it to explain associative knowledge organization before AI.

## Current Profile
Within the episode, Memex matters because it imagines knowledge tools organized around trails of association rather than alphabetical order, library categories, or isolated documents. It becomes a predecessor image for hypertext, Engelbart-style knowledge work, and the web, while also marking a difference from contemporary AI: Memex preserves and presents human association, whereas AI systems increasingly infer associations from large-scale text patterns.

## Key Characteristics
- Functions as the episode's historical model for associative knowledge storage and retrieval.
- Shifts the knowledge problem from possession of documents to usable connections among documents.
- Links print-era limitations to later hypertext and web imaginaries.
- Serves as a contrast with AI systems that generate associations without direct bodily experience.

## Evidence
- Associative storage: [[episode-224-bushi-bushi-bushi-bushi-8213451338-252768]] describes Memex as a system aligned with how memory follows associations.
- Media lineage: [[episode-224-bushi-bushi-bushi-bushi-8213451338-252768]] connects Memex to Ted Nelson, Douglas Engelbart, and Tim Berners-Lee as later realizations of related ideas.
- AI-era contrast: [[episode-224-bushi-bushi-bushi-bushi-8213451338-252768]] uses Memex to distinguish human-made trails from machine-generated statistical links.
