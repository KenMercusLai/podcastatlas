# Helen Duncan

## Overview

Helen Duncan was a British medium whose wartime seances became a government concern after she appeared to reveal the sinking of HMS Barham before the information was public.

## Current Profile

The source presents Duncan as the modern endpoint of British occult politics. Her claimed materialization of a drowned sailor alarmed an attending intelligence officer because the military information was sensitive, not because officials necessarily accepted spiritualism.

Duncan was prosecuted in 1944 under the Witchcraft Act 1735, which allowed imprisonment. The episode's cautious interpretation is that wartime officials could treat seances as careless talk, rumor transmission, or possible leakage during preparations for D-Day. That security logic did not require a settled view about whether Duncan was fraudulent, unusually informed, or supernaturally gifted.

## Key Characteristics

- Portsmouth spiritualist medium associated with materialization seances.
- Publicized sensitive information about HMS Barham's sinking.
- Prosecuted during wartime under the Witchcraft Act 1735.
- Case where an occult claim became an information-security concern.
- Subject of unresolved debate about official belief and prosecutorial motive.

## Evidence

- Barham episode: [[174-merlin-magic-and-the-british-glt5141910845]] says Duncan claimed to materialize a sailor from the recently sunk ship while the loss remained sensitive.
- Prosecution: [[174-merlin-magic-and-the-british-glt5141910845]] dates the Witchcraft Act case to 1944 and contrasts its custodial reach with the Vagrancy Act 1824.
- Security interpretation: [[174-merlin-magic-and-the-british-glt5141910845]] presents D-Day secrecy and careless-talk risk as the likely institutional concern.

## Qualifications

The source does not establish how Duncan learned of HMS Barham, whether intelligence personnel believed any supernatural claim, or whether D-Day security was the decisive reason for prosecution. Statutory details and the views of “several historians” remain episode-attributed pending legal and archival sources.

## What Changed

- Created a profile distinguishing claimed mediumship from the government's practical information-risk problem.
