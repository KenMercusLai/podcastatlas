# Hope Forward

## Overview

Hope Forward is [[HopeCollege]]'s tuition-free experiment: participating students sign a covenant to take their education seriously and give something annually after graduation, while the college sets no required amount and retains no legal repayment claim.

## Current Profile

The program is designed to replace a debtor relationship with [[VoluntaryPayItForwardEducation]]. Its intended benefit is not only affordability but greater freedom to choose study, service, and work without tuition debt steering decisions toward near-term income.

Its defining feature is also its principal uncertainty. Because a contribution must remain voluntary to count as a gift, the model cannot guarantee that alumni giving will recover educational cost. Scaling therefore depends on transition capital, institutional performance, durable belonging, and a culture in which recipients accept responsibility for future students.

## Key Characteristics

- Removes tuition for participants rather than deferring it as a conventional loan.
- Uses a moral covenant rather than a legally enforceable repayment contract.
- Requires annual giving after graduation but specifies neither a minimum nor a recommended amount.
- Transfers financial uncertainty from individual students toward the college and donor community.
- Treats gratitude, belonging, service, and monetary giving as forms of continuing reciprocity.

## Evidence

### Covenant design

- [[a-good-circle-a-good-circle]] reports the reciprocal promises and the deliberate absence of a prescribed donation amount.

### Choice and purpose

- [[a-good-circle-a-good-circle]] uses student testimony and Scogin's argument to connect reduced debt with freer educational and career choice.

### Scale and uncertainty

- [[a-good-circle-a-good-circle]] reports 58 participants after two years, an interim 100-student annual goal, and the much larger capital requirement for full expansion.

## Qualifications

Hope Forward is an early institutional experiment, not a demonstrated sector-wide replacement for tuition. The cohort is small, alumni repayment has not matured, selection effects are unresolved, and Hope College's Christian culture may be an enabling condition rather than an incidental feature.

## What Changed

- Established the program's covenant, risk transfer, and intended freedom effects.
- Made the absence of enforceable repayment both the program's moral center and its financial uncertainty.
