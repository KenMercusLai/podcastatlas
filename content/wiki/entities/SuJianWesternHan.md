## Overview

苏建 / Su Jian is presented as a Western Han general whose force is destroyed after hard fighting and [[ZhaoXinWesternHan|赵信]]'s surrender, but who returns rather than defecting. [[WeiQingHanGeneral|卫青]] rejects immediate execution as a display of authority, imprisons him, and sends him for capital review; Su Jian then redeems a death sentence, loses status, and is later restored as governor of Dai Commandery. He is also identified as the father of [[SuWuWesternHan|苏武]], but the current page keeps his own command-accountability and return-to-office trajectory central.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-297-gonggao-zhubuyi-mingjiang-weiqing-de-weiguan-zhihui-lg2zmxdwvx_ydqfqsme_hdcswtwn|Hanji 297]] supplies the defeat and field-review stage. It says Su Jian's roughly three thousand troops are surrounded by a much larger [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] force, fight for a day, and collapse after [[ZhaoXinWesternHan|赵信]] surrenders. Su Jian returns alone. One adviser urges execution to establish Wei Qing's authority, while [[RenAnWesternHan|任安]] and others argue that killing a commander who fought to exhaustion and still returned would make future defeated officers afraid to come back.

Wei Qing does not acquit Su Jian. He imprisons him and returns the case to [[HanWudi|汉武帝]], preserving accountability while declining an irreversible field execution. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-299-2-xihan-caizheng-da-weiji-hanwudi-ruhe-gao-qian-fqf4vv3uhemqey3qqk6cdiht_2cc|Hanji 299-2]] then says the emperor spares Su Jian from death after payment of redemption money and reduces him to commoner status.

The episode then gives the punishment a nonfinal endpoint: Su Jian later becomes governor of Dai Commandery and dies in office. The sequence makes him a compact case of severe military accountability coexisting with financial redemption and later reuse, while the current evidence does not explain the legal mechanism or the reasons for restoration.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han commander serving under Wei Qing in war against Xiongnu.
- Returned officer whose hard fighting, isolation, and refusal to defect weigh against immediate execution without erasing responsibility for losing his force.
- Defeated officer returned to the capital for judgment rather than executed in the field.
- Beneficiary of death-penalty redemption who is reduced to commoner status.
- Later appointee as governor of Dai Commandery, showing restoration after punishment.
- Father of Su Wu in the episode's biographical bridge.

## Qualifications

The current evidence consists of two compact structured summaries rather than a full biography or legal study. The exact force sizes, encirclement, Zhao Xin's effect, Su Jian's escape, legal exposure, advisers' wording, Wei Qing's authority, review procedure, original sentence, redemption amount, interval before restoration, Dai Commandery record, death circumstances, and complete family remain source-scoped. Calling Wei Qing Su Wu's “half savior of his father” is a counterfactual interpretation: referral preserved the possibility of survival, while Han Wudi's decision and redemption mechanism also determined the outcome. Later appointment shows reuse after punishment but does not by itself establish why the court restored him or whether the earlier judgment was reconsidered.

## What Changed

- Added the direct defeat and field-review stage before the previously recorded redemption and restoration.
- Distinguished return after hard fighting from acquittal: Su Jian remains imprisoned and subject to capital judgment.
- Added the organizational risk that automatic execution could deter later defeated officers from returning.
