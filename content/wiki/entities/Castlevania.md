## Overview

Castlevania / 恶魔城 is a game series used in the episode to examine how franchise identity emerges from the interaction of combat, role-playing progression, weapons, level structure, music, art, and atmosphere.

## Current Profile

The host's comparison separates competent 2D action from a convincing Castlevania whole. The recent “Belmont's Curse” is described as enjoyable in movement, difficulty, and level design but thin in the RPG, weapon, music, and visual qualities the host associates with “Metroidvania” Castlevania. By contrast, the Nintendo DS title “Order of Ecclesia” is praised for three switchable glyph setups, hand-specific configuration, attribute matching, and a difficulty curve shaped by more linear progression, while its fragmented maps and true-ending village tasks remain weaknesses.

## Key Characteristics

- Series identity is treated as an integrated aesthetic and systems judgment, not a logo or movement model alone.
- RPG growth and strategically distinct weapons are central to the host's expected “Metroidvania” form.
- Music and visual art materially shape whether a game feels like Castlevania.
- Order of Ecclesia rewards tactical switching among glyph combinations and enemy weaknesses.
- Linear structure can improve difficulty control even when it weakens the feeling of one monumental castle.
