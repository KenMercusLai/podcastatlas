## Overview

稽落山之战 / Jiluoshan Battle is the main battlefield marker in the wiki's account of [[DouXianEasternHan|Dou Xian]] breaking [[NorthernXiongnuLateHan|Northern Xiongnu]] power. Hanji 761 now supplies the route and field-command details, while Hanji 763 gives the broader crime-to-campaign and migration-memory framing that connects the victory to [[YanranInscription|Yanran commemoration]] and the qualified [[TransEurasianMigrationCascade|trans-Eurasian migration-cascade]].

## Current Profile

Hanji 761 places the battle inside a controversial campaign. Officials had opposed the expedition on mourning, farming, logistics, and legitimacy grounds, and [[HeChangEasternHan|He Chang]] had tied those objections to Dou-family luxury. The campaign nevertheless proceeds in summer with Dou Xian and Geng Bing moving from Jilu Pass, the Southern Xiongnu chanyu from Manyi Valley, and [[DengHongHeEraLateHan|Deng Hong]] from Guyang Pass toward Zhuoye Mountain.

The source gives the battle's tactical node to Dou's delegated commanders. Dou sends Yan Pan, [[GengKuiLateHan|Geng Kui]], and Geng Tan with more than ten thousand Southern Xiongnu cavalry against the Northern Xiongnu chanyu at Jiluoshan. They win, pursue to Siqu Beihai, record large casualties and captured livestock, and receive surrender from many tribes.

Hanji 763 supplies the moral and historical frame. Dou's campaign is a punishment-avoidance path after killing Liu Chang, yet the episode still treats the victory as real military competence. Jiluoshan therefore connects three layers: contested campaign authorization, battlefield success, and public commemoration at Yanran.

## Key Characteristics

- Campaign centerpiece in Dou Xian's Northern Xiongnu offensive.
- Victory achieved through delegated field commanders and Southern Xiongnu cavalry in Hanji 761.
- Presented as decisive enough to produce pursuit, large captures, and mass submission.
- Leads directly to Yanran commemoration through Ban Gu's inscription.
- Carries moral ambiguity because the campaign is politically contested and led by a dangerous outer-relative.
- Functions as the local battlefield node behind the source's larger migration-cascade argument.

## Qualifications

This page is not a complete military reconstruction of the campaign. Hanji 761 supplies route, cavalry, pursuit, and submission details; Hanji 763 provides broader moral and migration framing. Exact geography, casualty and captive numbers, the relationship between Jiluoshan and later Jinwei Mountain operations, and the long-range European migration chain remain source-scoped.
