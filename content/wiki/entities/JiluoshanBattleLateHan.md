## Overview

稽落山之战 / Jiluoshan Battle enters the wiki as the main battlefield marker in Hanji 763's account of [[DouXianEasternHan|Dou Xian]] breaking [[NorthernXiongnuLateHan|Northern Xiongnu]] power. The source treats the victory as both a real military success and the immediate precondition for [[YanranInscription|Yanran commemoration]], while keeping the longer migration consequences under the qualified [[TransEurasianMigrationCascade|trans-Eurasian migration-cascade]] frame.

## Current Profile

Hanji 763 places the battle inside Dou Xian's redemption-through-war path. After Dou kills Liu Chang and is jailed by [[EmpressDowagerDouHeLateHan|Empress Dowager Dou]], he asks to campaign north against the Xiongnu. The source says Dou leads Han troops together with submitted western Qiang and Xianbei forces in three routes across the desert field.

The battlefield result is presented as decisive in the episode's narrative. Dou Xian does not stop after the first success; he pursues the beaten Northern Xiongnu and first defeats the main force at Jiluoshan. The source reports large casualties and captures, then links the victory to Yanran Mountain, where [[BanGuEasternHan|Ban Gu]] is ordered to inscribe the achievement.

The battle should therefore be read narrowly as Hanji 763's campaign centerpiece. It is not yet a complete military reconstruction of routes, forces, or chronology. Its durable role in the wiki is to connect Dou Xian's military competence, Northern Xiongnu collapse, and the later commemorative and migration-memory layers.

## Key Characteristics

- Campaign centerpiece in Dou Xian's Northern Xiongnu offensive.
- Presented as a decisive blow against the Northern Xiongnu main force.
- Built around pursuit after initial victory rather than a single contained clash.
- Leads directly to Yanran commemoration through Ban Gu's inscription.
- Functions as the local battlefield node behind the source's larger migration-cascade argument.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 763. The exact force composition, route mapping, geography of Jiluoshan, casualty and captive figures, and relationship between this battle and the later Jinwei Mountain operation remain source-scoped until additional notes make them durable. The page also does not treat the later European migration chain as settled proof; it only records that Hanji 763 makes Jiluoshan one starting point for that interpretation.
