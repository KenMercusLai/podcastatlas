## Overview

鲍宣 / Bao Xuan is synthesized as a Western Han remonstrant and enforcement official whose current wiki profile joins two episodes: in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-588-laoban-bu-chenzhi-xiashu-neng-you-duo-can-lovz7epf8qs0atyr3njy2kvblqpv|Hanji 588]], he warns [[HanAidi|汉哀帝]] that popular suffering, official predation, and favorite or outer-relative privilege have made punishment an inadequate answer to disorder; in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-599-gan-dui-qianguize-shuo-bu-chengxiang-wangjia-you-duo-yong-lpptx-fzls9xbakw52b8n1n6xow0|Hanji 599]], he later enforces road rules against [[KongGuangWesternHan|孔光]]'s entourage and is punished after colliding with elite privilege.

## Current Profile

Hanji 588 gives Bao Xuan's earlier ruler-facing critique. In Jianping 4 / 3 BCE, he submits a memorial that organizes commoner suffering through "seven harms" and "seven deaths": disaster, tax and corvee pressure, official greed, powerful-household land seizure, theft, harsh punishment, wrongful cases, famine, and disease. The source's durable point is that people are not merely unlucky; they are being damaged by a governing order in which officials enrich families and the court continues to reward outer relatives and [[DongXian|董贤]].

Bao Xuan's advice is severe but practical. He says criminal law cannot bring peace when people are already in extreme distress, and he tells Aidi to remove glib or harmful insiders, keep untrained outer-relative children out of office, and restore or rely on experienced figures such as [[FuXiWesternHan|傅喜]], [[HeWuWesternHan|何武]], Shi Dan, Kong Guang, and [[PengXuanWesternHan|彭宣]]. The episode treats Aidi's decision not to punish Bao Xuan as limited tolerance, not as evidence of durable reform.

Hanji 599 presents Bao Xuan after Wang Jia's death and Kong Guang's restoration. When Kong Guang travels as chancellor, his accompanying officials use the emperor's central roadway, which the episode treats as illegal. Bao Xuan orders the offending entourage arrested and confiscates the carriage and horses.

The later punishment reverses the moral direction of the enforcement scene. Bao Xuan is accused of humiliating the chancellor, closing gates against messengers, and violating ministerial etiquette. His death sentence is reduced, but he is still sentenced to shaved-head penal labor with restraints. The source therefore makes him a law-enforcement countercase inside an elite-protection court.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han Sili official in the immediate post-Wang-Jia court sequence.
- Earlier remonstrant who uses the people's "seven harms" and "seven deaths" to diagnose late-Aidi governance failure.
- Treats criminal law as ineffective when disaster, extraction, official violence, hunger, and disease are already destroying household life.
- Links ruler responsibility to personnel correction, restraint of outer relatives, and limits on Dong Xian-centered privilege.
- Enforces rules against Kong Guang's entourage for using the imperial central roadway.
- Treats elite proximity as insufficient to exempt officials from road rules.
- Becomes vulnerable to charges of humiliating the chancellor and violating court etiquette.

## Qualifications

This page is still not a full biography of Bao Xuan. Hanji 588 supports the remonstrance, livelihood diagnosis, punishment warning, and personnel proposal, but not the full original memorial or every named actor's career. Hanji 599 supports the road-rule enforcement and punishment case but not Bao Xuan's later fate, family network, or the exact legal basis for the sentence. Later source notes already mention Bao Xuan as a victim in Wang Mang-era purge contexts, but those mentions have not yet been integrated into this page as bounded evidence.
