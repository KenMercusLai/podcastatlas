## Overview

鲍宣 / Bao Xuan is synthesized as a Western Han remonstrant and enforcement official whose current wiki profile joins three episodes. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-588-laoban-bu-chenzhi-xiashu-neng-you-duo-can-lovz7epf8qs0atyr3njy2kvblqpv|Hanji 588]] gives his diagnosis of popular suffering and official predation; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-596-baoxuan-quanjian-aidi-you-yizhong-ai-jiao-fangshou-lpfmgwzjvqgoybs0q572ufo4hhka|Hanji 596]] shows him packaging withdrawal of [[DongXian|董贤]] as protection for the favorite and winning a partial personnel response from [[HanAidi|汉哀帝]]; and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-599-gan-dui-qianguize-shuo-bu-chengxiang-wangjia-you-duo-yong-lpptx-fzls9xbakw52b8n1n6xow0|Hanji 599]] shows his later enforcement against [[KongGuangWesternHan|孔光]]'s entourage colliding with elite privilege.

## Current Profile

Hanji 588 gives Bao Xuan's earlier ruler-facing critique. In Jianping 4 / 3 BCE, he submits a memorial that organizes commoner suffering through "seven harms" and "seven deaths": disaster, tax and corvee pressure, official greed, powerful-household land seizure, theft, harsh punishment, wrongful cases, famine, and disease. The source's durable point is that people are not merely unlucky; they are being damaged by a governing order in which officials enrich families and the court continues to reward outer relatives and [[DongXian|董贤]].

Bao Xuan's advice is severe but practical. He says criminal law cannot bring peace when people are already in extreme distress, and he tells Aidi to remove glib or harmful insiders, keep untrained outer-relative children out of office, and restore or rely on experienced figures such as [[FuXiWesternHan|傅喜]], [[HeWuWesternHan|何武]], Shi Dan, Kong Guang, and [[PengXuanWesternHan|彭宣]]. The episode treats Aidi's decision not to punish Bao Xuan as limited tolerance, not as evidence of durable reform.

Hanji 596 adds Bao Xuan's hierarchy-aware method and a more positive reception case. He recognizes Aidi's initial self-correction, uses eclipse, earthquake, cloud, drought, and public unease to show that the cause remains, then identifies Dong Xian's treasury, residence, land, construction, and tribute advantages. His decisive move is to call dismissal and return to the fief an act of protection: continued favor makes Dong Xian a public target, while withdrawal may preserve his family. The episode says Aidi recalls He Wu and Peng Xuan and makes Bao Xuan Sili, but later Dong Xian sources prevent treating the proposed dismissal as implemented.

Hanji 599 presents Bao Xuan after Wang Jia's death and Kong Guang's restoration. When Kong Guang travels as chancellor, his accompanying officials use the emperor's central roadway, which the episode treats as illegal. Bao Xuan orders the offending entourage arrested and confiscates the carriage and horses.

The later punishment reverses the moral direction of the enforcement scene. Bao Xuan is accused of humiliating the chancellor, closing gates against messengers, and violating ministerial etiquette. His death sentence is reduced, but he is still sentenced to shaved-head penal labor with restraints. The source therefore makes him a law-enforcement countercase inside an elite-protection court.

## Key Characteristics

- Earlier remonstrant who uses the people's "seven harms" and "seven deaths" to diagnose late-Aidi governance failure.
- Treats criminal law as ineffective when disaster, extraction, official violence, hunger, and disease are already destroying household life.
- Links ruler responsibility to personnel correction, restraint of outer relatives, and limits on Dong Xian-centered privilege.
- Uses acknowledgment, omen-coded diagnosis, and protection language to make a severe favorite-removal proposal more acceptable to the ruler.
- Enforces rules against Kong Guang's entourage for using the imperial central roadway.
- Treats elite proximity as insufficient to exempt officials from road rules.
- Becomes vulnerable to charges of humiliating the chancellor and violating court etiquette.

## Qualifications

This page is still not a full biography of Bao Xuan. Hanji 588 supports the livelihood diagnosis and personnel proposal; Hanji 596 supports the rhetorical sequence and named personnel follow-up but does not prove that Aidi actually dismissed Dong Xian; and Hanji 599 supports the road-rule enforcement and punishment case but not Bao Xuan's later fate, family network, or the exact legal basis for the sentence. Later source notes already mention Bao Xuan as a victim in Wang Mang-era purge contexts, but those mentions have not yet been integrated into this page as bounded evidence.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 596's acceptance-path layer: Bao Xuan turns withdrawal of Dong Xian into protection, and Aidi answers with a limited personnel response.
- Preserved Hanji 588's earlier remonstrance layer: the "seven harms" and "seven deaths" turn commoner suffering into a ruler-facing diagnosis.
- Preserved Hanji 599's later enforcement layer against Kong Guang's entourage.
