## Overview

张厂 / Zhang Chang is the Nanyang gongcao in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-868-ying-le-ye-shi-shu-jia-donghan-hougong-zhengyan-ji-lmmjgx23nlkeqr3mkzliij-zwe2v|Hanji 868]] who persuades [[WangChangShumaoLateHan|王畅]] to abandon overly harsh action against powerful households and govern through a broader humane strategy.

## Current Profile

Zhang Chang's role is source-scoped but conceptually important. Wang Chang has arrested many powerful-household figures in Nanyang and seems inclined toward punitive domination. Zhang Chang reframes the problem: Nanyang is not an ordinary commandery but the imperial-origin region of Eastern Han, so spectacular harshness risks damaging public meaning as well as local trust.

His advice combines precedent, local status, and Confucian governance theory. He points to earlier administrators remembered for education and transformation, then cites the idea that upright personal conduct can move people without constant orders. In the episode's interpretation, Zhang Chang gives Wang Chang the decisive "转一念" that turns enforcement capacity into a humane local-governance case.

## Key Characteristics

- Gongcao whose advice redirects a superior's punitive local policy.
- Adviser who treats Nanyang's symbolic status as an administrative constraint.
- Precedent user who invokes educative officials rather than only legal punishment.
- Confucian governance interpreter who makes official self-correction part of public order.
