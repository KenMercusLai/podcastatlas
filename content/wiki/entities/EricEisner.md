# Eric Eisner

## Overview

Eric Eisner is the former entertainment lawyer presented in [[carlos-doesn-t-remember-carlos-doesn-t-remember|Carlos Doesn’t Remember]] as founder of the [[YESProgram|YES program]] and a long-term educational advocate for [[CarlosStudent|Carlos]].

## Current Profile

Eisner's role goes beyond finding academically gifted children. The episode shows him tutoring, noticing material barriers, using social connections to secure school placements, monitoring students over time, and intervening again after disruption. His discovery that Carlos avoided recess because his shoes were several sizes too large makes the program's operating premise concrete: admission does not remove the everyday constraints surrounding a low-income child.

He also supplies the episode's strongest institutional criticism. Colleges that begin searching at age seventeen are reaching the pipeline too late; in Lennox, he argues, support may need to begin around fourth grade before confidence and school attachment deteriorate. His work is therefore evidence for [[EarlyOpportunityIntervention]], while its dependence on unusual personal commitment reveals the limits of advocate-dependent access.

## Key Characteristics

- Former entertainment lawyer who founded an educational support program.
- Searches for gifted students in under-resourced Los Angeles schools.
- Combines tutoring, placement, material support, relationship leverage, and follow-through.
- Treats early confidence and continuing support as prerequisites for later college access.
- Acts as an advocate for Carlos across multiple educational disruptions.

## Evidence

### Early identification and placement

- [[carlos-doesn-t-remember-carlos-doesn-t-remember]] credits Eisner with identifying Carlos in fifth grade and helping place him in a Brentwood private school.

### Whole-context support

- [[carlos-doesn-t-remember-carlos-doesn-t-remember]] recounts Eisner discovering and replacing Carlos's oversized shoes and later finding another school closer to home.

### Pipeline diagnosis

- [[carlos-doesn-t-remember-carlos-doesn-t-remember]] attributes to Eisner the argument that college recruitment at seventeen comes too late for children whose confidence and safety are already at risk.

## Qualifications

The source is sympathetic to Eisner and does not independently evaluate the program's selection process, scale, outcomes, costs, safeguarding, attrition, or counterfactual impact. Personal networks can produce valuable access while remaining uneven, discretionary, difficult to reproduce, and dependent on the judgment of a powerful advocate.
