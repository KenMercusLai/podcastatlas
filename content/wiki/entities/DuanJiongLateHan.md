## Overview

段熲 / Duan Jiong is a veteran late-Han frontier commander whose current wiki profile now begins with restoration after accusation and local petition, then develops into a hard-line anti-Qiang military career. Hanji 859 shows why the court returns to him under Liangzhou pressure; later sources show the tactical competence, shared hardship, ethical critique, and coercive enforcement that made his reputation both useful and troubling.

## Current Profile

Duan Jiong first appears here as a commander the frontier still wants after a false accusation. In Hanji 859, western-region officials and civilians plead for him at the palace gate, and strengthening Qiang pressure in Liangzhou forces the court to reappoint him as Protector of the Qiang. This establishes public demand and emergency practicality before the later record of campaigns.

The main military profile then intensifies. Hanji 867 presents Western Qiang victory, and Hanji 880-883 extends the portrait through battlefield leadership, campaign finance, and an increasingly harsh anti-Qiang line. Hanji 891-904 places him within promotion, court factional danger, and the later costs of coercive power. Hanji 915 and 917 preserve the afterlife of that model: rapid breakthrough and strong leadership can solve one problem while leaving moral and political damage unresolved.

The resulting profile is deliberately ambivalent. Duan is not a mere villain or simple savior; he is a commander whose tactical skill, endurance with soldiers, and frontier credibility make him indispensable under pressure, while his hard-line method and later political entanglements make him a warning about what emergency usefulness can license.

## Key Characteristics

- Frontier commander restored to office after local officials and civilians petition for him during Qiang pressure.
- Associated with Western Qiang and Liangzhou campaigns where emergency conditions favor proven military capacity.
- Tactically aggressive and capable of rapid battlefield breakthroughs.
- Shares hardship and command discipline in ways that can strengthen military credibility.
- Pursues a hard-line anti-Qiang policy that attracts later ethical critique.
- Moves from frontier usefulness into court and factional risk, where coercive skill becomes politically dangerous.
- Embodies the late-Han tension between practical indispensability and moral-political cost.

## Qualifications

The page remains bounded to the listed podcast source notes and does not resolve all historical debates about Duan Jiong's full biography or policy legacy. The transcript form "段炯" is normalized to 段熲 because the Protector of the Qiang and Liangzhou sequence matches the established late-Han frontier commander. The source's false-accusation and palace-gate petition details are kept as source-scoped claims.
