## Overview
段熲 / Duan Jiong is a late-Han frontier commander whose current wiki profile now begins with a 160 CE western-Qiang pursuit and then runs through repeated restoration, hard-line Qiang policy, costly battlefield success, and eventual palace-politics collapse. He is one of the clearest examples of a capable but morally ambivalent crisis specialist: the court repeatedly needs his frontier capacity, yet the same aggressiveness later attaches him to eunuch and court-violence risk.

## Current Profile
[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-854-lishi-gaosu-ni-xuanguan-yongguan-hen-zhongyao-lhin-iwk6y1xr8vvzdmnjch3s2um|Hanji 854]] supplies the earliest active layer in the current bounded record: Duan Jiong fights western Qiang forces from morning to noon, continues after weapons and arrows are exhausted, pursues for more than forty days beyond the frontier toward Jishi Mountain, kills a major Shaohe leader, and receives or captures more than five thousand people. The same source also has him defeat Qiang forces around Yunje, making his first appearance here a successful but physically extreme campaign rather than the later obstruction case.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-855-ni-you-duo-wuqing-jiu-you-duo-qiangda-lulbpnufi8xfijrviufo6biuy-h1|Hanji 855]] then changes the problem from battlefield endurance to bureaucratic interference: Guo Hong's merit-seeking obstruction delays the campaign, creates desertion, and pushes wrongful punishment onto Duan. Later sources restore him because renewed Qiang pressure makes his military value difficult to replace. The mature profile is not simply "strong general"; it is a progression from needed frontier competence into a hard-line elimination strategy, then into court dependency and factional contamination.

## Key Characteristics
- Frontier commander whose current profile now starts with a high-endurance 160 CE pursuit against western Qiang forces.
- Repeatedly restored or retained because frontier crises make his military capacity hard for the court to replace.
- Shares danger and hardship with troops, using proximity to suffering as part of command legitimacy.
- Prefers aggressive, annihilating solutions to Qiang conflict once he argues that appeasement is strategically futile.
- Operates in a state where success can be undermined by jealous supervisors, court bargaining, and eunuch-adjacent incentives.
- Becomes a morally ambivalent figure: effective at restoring order, but later implicated in coercive court power and lethal factional violence.

## Evidence
- Early high-endurance field success: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-854-lishi-gaosu-ni-xuanguan-yongguan-hen-zhongyao-lhin-iwk6y1xr8vvzdmnjch3s2um|Hanji 854]] presents Duan fighting until weapons fail, eating meat and snow during pursuit, crossing beyond the frontier, and killing the Shaohe leader near Jishi Mountain.
- Continued early Qiang suppression: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-854-lishi-gaosu-ni-xuanguan-yongguan-hen-zhongyao-lhin-iwk6y1xr8vvzdmnjch3s2um|Hanji 854]] also has Duan defeat Qiang forces besieging Yunje, reinforcing that the court's later reliance on him follows visible field results.
- Obstructed but still central commander: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-855-ni-you-duo-wuqing-jiu-you-duo-qiangda-lulbpnufi8xfijrviufo6biuy-h1|Hanji 855]] shows Guo Hong withholding supplies and access to frustrate Duan's campaign, which reframes Duan as effective enough to threaten a superior's credit.
- Replacement failure and restoration: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-859-gudai-haoren-weishenme-nan-dang-haoguan-llkyx18ssvserxncvrtkw7xfduad|Hanji 859]] restores Duan after Hu Hong cannot contain renewed Qiang pressure, making capacity rather than clean reputation the decisive variable.
- Hard-line policy logic: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-880-weihe-zhichang-laoyoutiao-rezhong-yu-chengbing-lnzfgwghrxrpltsmb8godap-nyzw|Hanji 880]], [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-881-shi-shui-zai-fa-zhanzheng-hengcai-lipglslijnbp0cuqfpps88q5wit6|Hanji 881]], and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-883-laoban-yao-fansi-ruhe-bimian-gaoguan-neihong-lph-fycnqas6hztjq1jbsisvylid|Hanji 883]] build the later extermination-versus-pacification debate around Duan, Feng Chan, and the Qiang front.
- Court-politics contamination: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-891-gao-duanwei-de-muqin-shi-jia-de-dinghai-shenzhen-lmqkzsnhtiwc9wzcee869kuu57m5|Hanji 891]], [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-892-xiu-zhiyu-guren-youshenme-jiejing-lnobbkp6mj4xyb9jeuqsiuqogct|Hanji 892]], and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-897-zhichang-bidu-shenme-cai-jiao-lingdaoli-lvrrmksykazlsb7w8abbdmnqfzqq|Hanji 897]] show the later Duan participating in coercive court power and suffering reputational damage from eunuch-aligned politics.

## Qualifications
The source set is episodic and not a full biography. It is especially strong on crisis scenes where Duan's capacity is visible, but weaker on ordinary administration, motivations beyond memorialized policy, and how much later blame belongs to personal ambition versus structural dependence on violent specialists. ASR variants such as 段炯 are normalized to 段熲 within the bounded Hanji corpus.

## What Changed
- Added Hanji 854 as the new earliest active source layer for Duan, before the Guo Hong obstruction and punishment sequence.
- Reframed Hanji 855 as a bureaucratic-interference episode following already demonstrated frontier competence.
- Added Duan's 160 CE Jishi Mountain pursuit and Yunje relief to the evidence base for why later rulers keep returning to him.
- Narrowed the overall judgment: Duan is neither merely brutal nor merely competent, but a crisis-capacity figure whose usefulness makes later moral and political risks harder to avoid.
