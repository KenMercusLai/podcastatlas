## Overview

段熲 / Duan Jiong is a late-Han frontier commander whose current wiki profile now begins with a 156 CE Xianbei ambush that succeeds through deception while creating legal liability, then runs through western-Qiang pursuit, repeated restoration, hard-line Qiang policy, costly battlefield success, and eventual palace-politics collapse. He is one of the clearest examples of a capable but morally and legally ambivalent crisis specialist: the court repeatedly needs his violence-management capacity, yet that usefulness also shields or delays accountability.

## Current Profile

The earliest active layer in the current bounded record is Hanji 842. As a Duliao defense figure in Liaodong, Duan Jiong avoids a frontal encounter with Xianbei forces by staging a false imperial recall. He sends a station officer to pretend that an edict has summoned him back to Luoyang for reward, visibly withdraws, secretly returns, and ambushes the Xianbei after they enter. The tactic works, but the false edict is a capital offense. The court commutes the penalty because of his military merit, later restores him as yilang, and then uses him again as zhonglangjiang against the Gongsun Ju and Dongguo Dou rebellion, where he wins, kills many rebels, and receives marquis status.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-854-lishi-gaosu-ni-xuanguan-yongguan-hen-zhongyao-lhin-iwk6y1xr8vvzdmnjch3s2um|Hanji 854]] supplies the next active layer: Duan fights western Qiang forces from morning to noon, continues after weapons and arrows are exhausted, pursues for more than forty days beyond the frontier toward Jishi Mountain, kills a major Shaohe leader, and receives or captures more than five thousand people. The same source also has him defeat Qiang forces around Yunje, making his early profile a sequence of successful but physically extreme campaigns.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-855-ni-you-duo-wuqing-jiu-you-duo-qiangda-lulbpnufi8xfijrviufo6biuy-h1|Hanji 855]] then changes the problem from battlefield endurance to bureaucratic interference: Guo Hong's merit-seeking obstruction delays the campaign, creates desertion, and pushes wrongful punishment onto Duan. Later sources restore him because renewed Qiang pressure makes his military value difficult to replace. The mature profile is not simply "strong general"; it is a progression from needed frontier competence into a hard-line elimination strategy, then into court dependency and factional contamination.

Across the later Qiang and court-politics layers, Duan's usefulness remains inseparable from risk. He shares hardship with troops and wins difficult campaigns, but he also argues for annihilating solutions to Qiang conflict and later participates in coercive court power. The page therefore treats him as an appointment-quality stress test: a state under pressure may keep reusing a commander whose methods solve immediate crises while accumulating legal, moral, and factional cost.

## Key Characteristics

- Tactical deception commander whose 156 CE false-recall ambush defeats Xianbei but creates legal liability for forged imperial order.
- Reused after punishment because the court still needs his military talent against frontier and internal rebellion.
- Frontier commander whose profile includes a high-endurance 160 CE pursuit against western Qiang forces.
- Shares danger and hardship with troops, using proximity to suffering as part of command legitimacy.
- Prefers aggressive, annihilating solutions to Qiang conflict once he argues that appeasement is strategically futile.
- Operates in a state where success can be undermined by jealous supervisors, court bargaining, and eunuch-adjacent incentives.
- Becomes a morally ambivalent figure: effective at restoring order, but later implicated in coercive court power and lethal factional violence.

## Qualifications

The source set is episodic and not a full biography. It is especially strong on crisis scenes where Duan's capacity is visible, but weaker on ordinary administration, motivations beyond memorialized policy, and how much later blame belongs to personal ambition versus structural dependence on violent specialists. Hanji 842's forged-order episode should be treated as tactical success plus legal liability, not as an endorsement of fabricating imperial authority. ASR variants such as 段炯 are normalized to 段熲 within the bounded Hanji corpus.

## What Changed

- Hanji 842 adds the new earliest active source layer: a 156 CE Xianbei ambush won through false-recall information control.
- The profile now starts with legal-risk victory before the 160 CE western-Qiang pursuit and Guo Hong obstruction sequence.
- Added Duan's merit-shielded sentence reduction, later reappointment, eastern rebel suppression, and marquis reward to the evidence base.
- Narrowed the overall judgment: Duan's usefulness repeatedly protects him, but the same pattern exposes the court's dependence on legally and morally risky crisis specialists.
