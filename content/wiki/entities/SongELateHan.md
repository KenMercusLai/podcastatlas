# 宋娥 / Song E (late Han)

## Overview

Song E is Emperor Shun's wet nurse, a palace-proximity figure whose removal in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-815-kebei-gudai-zhongchen-de-jiafeng-zhishang-llkhbxo-63pjw-gk9pihnbfr94v|Hanji 815]] triggers retaliatory accusation against [[LiGuLateHan|Li Gu]]. The later [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-818-guanchang-weishenme-yao-duo-jiaoyou-neng-jiuming-liyziudpezaoa4l6pbsq0wk2qdjdr|Hanji 818]] note had kept Song E source-scoped in a corruption-case caution; this page promotes her because Hanji 815 makes her a causal actor.

## Current Profile

Song E's current profile is built around palace access and retaliation. In 133 CE, [[EmperorShunOfHan|Emperor Shun]] accepts Li Gu's advice and orders Song E out of the palace, while eunuchs kneel and ask pardon. That correction briefly makes the court serious and restrained.

The correction does not end the palace network's ability to strike back. Song E and eunuchs resent Li Gu and forge anonymous letters or charges against him; Shun then orders Li Gu investigated by direct edict, bypassing the Shangshu Tai. Song E therefore marks a recurrent Shun-era problem: intimate palace service can become political leverage even when formal advisers momentarily prevail.

## Key Characteristics

- Holds influence through wet-nurse proximity rather than formal office.
- Becomes the target of Li Gu's successful corrective advice in 133 CE.
- Participates in or anchors retaliation through forged anonymous accusation after losing palace access.
- Illustrates how palace proximity can distort regular bureaucratic review channels.
- Remains thinly profiled outside the Li Gu and Shun court episodes.
