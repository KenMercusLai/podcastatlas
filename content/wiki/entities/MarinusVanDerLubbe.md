# Marinus van der Lubbe

## Overview

Marinus van der Lubbe was a Dutch building worker and political radical whom the episode identifies as the lone perpetrator of the [[ReichstagFire1933|Reichstag fire]] on 27 February 1933.

## Current Profile

Van der Lubbe came from Leiden and had moved through communist and anarcho-syndicalist circles. In Berlin, he believed a dramatic act of arson could awaken unemployed workers against fascism; after unsuccessful attempts elsewhere, he entered the Reichstag and ignited material in the debating chamber.

The political consequence far exceeded his intended act. Nazi leaders immediately represented the fire as evidence of a communist conspiracy, arrested opponents from prepared lists, and secured the [[ReichstagFireDecree|Reichstag Fire Decree]]. His case therefore separates authorship of a catalytic act from the state's opportunistic construction and use of its meaning.

## Key Characteristics

- He was a Dutch worker shaped by communist and anarcho-syndicalist political circles.
- He treated exemplary arson as a hoped-for signal for mass anti-fascist action.
- The episode identifies him as acting alone rather than as an agent of a Nazi or communist plot.
- His arrest gave Nazi leaders a usable individual suspect for a much broader conspiracy narrative.
- The regime's exploitation of the fire mattered more politically than his intended revolutionary effect.

- Political background and intent: [[298-the-nazis-total-power-part-4-glt6097237943]] traces his Leiden background, radical affiliations, travel, and belief that arson could mobilize the unemployed.

The episode's reconstruction of van der Lubbe's route, failed attempts, exact ignition method, intentions, and lone responsibility remains source-attributed.
