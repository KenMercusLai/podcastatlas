# Edward Coke

## Overview

Edward Coke was an English jurist whose seventeenth-century interpretation made [[MagnaCarta|Magna Carta]] a central statement of fundamental law against royal overreach.

## Current Profile

In the episode, Coke is a decisive mediator between medieval charter and modern constitutional memory. His legal interpretation treated Magna Carta as the fountain of fundamental laws and supplied later opponents of arbitrary monarchy with a usable historical language of inherited rights.

That interpretation was politically powerful but not simply neutral recovery. Coke folded the charter into the historically false Norman-yoke idea that it restored older Anglo-Saxon liberties. His importance therefore lies in joining genuine legal continuity to a persuasive historical reconstruction that later radicals and Americans could reuse.

## Key Characteristics

- English common-law jurist and interpreter of Magna Carta.
- Framed the charter as a source of fundamental law.
- Helped make medieval precedent usable in resistance to Stuart royal power.
- Combined genuine legal inheritance with a historically inaccurate restoration narrative.
- Influenced later radical and transatlantic rights arguments.
