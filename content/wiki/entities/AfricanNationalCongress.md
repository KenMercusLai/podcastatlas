# African National Congress

## Overview

The African National Congress is presented as the anti-apartheid movement and governing party whose anthem, election victory, and internal debates frame South Africa's post-apartheid symbol settlement.

## Current Profile

The ANC profile runs from liberation symbolism into the accountability burden of long incumbency. [[NkosiSikelelIAfrika|Nkosi Sikelel' iAfrika]] moves from Christian hymn to congress song and official ANC anthem. After the 1994 election, the ANC under [[NelsonMandela]] faces a governing problem: symbols that had expressed liberation also have to coexist with the emotions of former opponents, especially around the [[Springboks]] and [[DieStemVanSuidAfrika|Die Stem]].

As governing party, the ANC is also portrayed as deeply implicated in patronage and criminality. Presidential cleanup efforts and the coming leadership transition therefore become decisive tests of whether inquiry findings can become institutional reform.

## Key Characteristics

- Anti-apartheid organization rooted in black South African political mobilization.
- Successor name of the South African Native National Congress, which the episode says was founded in 1912 and became the ANC in 1923.
- Political home of [[NkosiSikelelIAfrika|Nkosi Sikelel' iAfrika]] after its adoption as official anthem in 1925.
- Governing party after the 1994 multiracial election that made [[NelsonMandela]] president.
- Internal coalition where some radicals wanted to abolish Springbok symbols, while Mandela chose incorporation.
- Long-governing party now judged not only by liberation legitimacy but by responsibility for patronage, criminal penetration, and whether succession sustains reform.

## Evidence

- Organizational origin: [[682-south-africa-mandela-and-the-death-of-apartheid-part-6-glt6535658080]] says [[NkosiSikelelIAfrika|Nkosi]] was sung at the 1912 inaugural meeting of the South African Native National Congress, which became the ANC in 1923.
- Anthem adoption: [[682-south-africa-mandela-and-the-death-of-apartheid-part-6-glt6535658080]] says [[NkosiSikelelIAfrika|Nkosi]] became the ANC's official anthem in 1925.
- Electoral transition: [[682-south-africa-mandela-and-the-death-of-apartheid-part-6-glt6535658080]] says the ANC won South Africa's first multiracial election in 1994.
- Symbolic disagreement: [[682-south-africa-mandela-and-the-death-of-apartheid-part-6-glt6535658080]] says some ANC radicals wanted the Springbok name and green jersey banned.
- Governing compromise: [[682-south-africa-mandela-and-the-death-of-apartheid-part-6-glt6535658080]] says Mandela kept both anthem traditions temporarily and left the final fusion to a committee.
- Incumbency and reform test: [[the-mob-rule-criminality-consumes-south-africa-6ab24a5a1f5b87e5353dbc86]] describes the ANC as implicated in the criminality under inquiry and warns that leadership succession may narrow the cleanup window.

## Qualifications

The page remains source-bounded. It does not cover the ANC's full liberation history, armed wing, exile politics, negotiations, factional structure, or post-1994 governance record. Attributions of criminality and reform intent are journalistic and do not assign individual legal guilt.

## What Changed

- Created the ANC as the institutional bridge between liberation anthem and governing compromise.
- Extended the profile from reconciliation-era legitimacy into long-incumbency accountability and leadership-succession risk.
