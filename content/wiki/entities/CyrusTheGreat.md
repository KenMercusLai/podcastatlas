# Cyrus the Great

## Overview

Cyrus the Great appears in the wiki as the founder figure whose conquests create the imperial inheritance that [[DariusI]] later stabilizes and turns toward the Aegean.

## Current Profile

The episode uses Cyrus to explain why Darius is not merely an ambitious king but the heir to a new kind of imperial scale. Cyrus defeats the Medes, incorporates them as junior partners, conquers Lydia and [[Sardis]], brings the Ionian Greeks under Persian rule, and takes Babylon in 539 BC.

This page is therefore not a full Cyrus biography. It records the role Cyrus plays in the Marathon prehistory: he transforms Persia from provincial obscurity into the core of the greatest empire yet seen, making later Ionian subjection and Athenian-Persian collision possible.

## Key Characteristics

- Cyrus is presented as the founder of the Persian imperial tradition inherited by Darius.
- His conquest of Lydia and Sardis brings Ionian Greek cities into the Persian orbit.
- His conquest of Babylon marks the decisive proof of Persian imperial scale.
- The episode emphasizes Cyrus as background for Darius's legitimacy and ambition rather than as its main subject.

## Evidence

- Imperial foundation: [[668-greece-vs-persia-the-rise-of-the-first-superpower-part-1-glt8965800182]] says Cyrus transformed the Persians into rulers of the greatest empire yet seen.
- Ionian incorporation: [[668-greece-vs-persia-the-rise-of-the-first-superpower-part-1-glt8965800182]] links Cyrus's conquest of Lydia and Sardis to Persian rule over Ionian Greeks.
- Babylonian conquest: [[668-greece-vs-persia-the-rise-of-the-first-superpower-part-1-glt8965800182]] identifies Cyrus's 539 BC conquest of Babylon as definitive for his rise.

## Qualifications

The page is source-scoped to Cyrus's role in a Marathon setup episode. It does not synthesize Cyrus's full reign, religion, imperial policy, or later reception.

## What Changed

- Created Cyrus as the founder-context node for the Achaemenid-Marathon branch.
