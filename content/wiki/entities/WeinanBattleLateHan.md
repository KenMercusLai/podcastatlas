## Overview

渭南之战 / Battle of Weinan is the 211 CE Guanzhong campaign in which [[CaoCao|曹操]] defeats a coalition led in the current evidence by [[MaChao|马超]] and [[HanSui|韩遂]]. The sources now frame its two hinges as the political sequencing that turns western fear into an apparent rebellion and the later exploitation of coalition fragility.

## Current Profile

Hanji 1120 supplies the political opening. Cao Cao announces a campaign against [[ZhangLuFivePecks|张鲁]], but reaching Hanzhong places Cao-side forces across the Guanzhong commanders' sphere. [[GaoRouLateHan|高柔]] warns that they will suspect an attack and unite; the episode interprets Cao Cao's refusal to change course, and his later instruction not to attack first, as a deliberate effort to make the coalition initiate hostilities and surrender the legitimacy advantage.

Hanji 1105 supplies the compressed outcome. It uses the campaign to demonstrate Cao Cao's opponent assessment and adviser use, presenting the coalition as numerous but divisible and [[JiaXu|贾诩]]'s counsel as the later decision hinge. Together the sources describe a campaign won first in political classification and then through coalition division, while leaving most battlefield mechanics outside the evidence set.

## Key Characteristics

- Begins with an announced Hanzhong expedition whose route creates coercive pressure in Guanzhong.
- Pits Cao Cao against a coalition represented by Ma Chao and Han Sui.
- Makes the order of mobilization and attack politically consequential because the first mover can be classified as a rebel.
- Turns a self-protective coalition's cohesion into its later strategic vulnerability.
- Uses Jia Xu's divisive counsel as the retrospective account's battlefield decision hinge.

## Qualifications

Both episodes summarize and interpret rather than fully reconstruct the campaign. Exact force composition, maneuver sequence, correspondence, casualties, appointments, and dialogue remain source-scoped. Hanji 1120's claim that Cao Cao deliberately intended to provoke rebellion is a coherent reading of the sequence, but intention is not independently settled by the current source set; fear-driven preemption by the Guanzhong commanders remains part of the same account.

## What Changed

- Added the declared Hanzhong campaign, Gao Rou's warning, and Guanzhong threat perception as the campaign opening.
- Added first-move legitimacy as a distinct political mechanism before the later coalition-division phase.
- Qualified deliberate provocation as the episode's interpretation rather than a settled account of Cao Cao's intent.
