## Overview

渭南之战 / Battle of Weinan is the 211 CE Guanzhong campaign in which [[CaoCao|曹操]] defeats a coalition led in the current evidence by [[MaChao|马超]] and [[HanSui|韩遂]]. The three-source synthesis now connects political sequencing, route control, engineered distrust, operational delay, and concentrated attack rather than treating the victory as a single battlefield stratagem.

## Current Profile

Hanji 1120 supplies the opening. Cao Cao announces a campaign against [[ZhangLuFivePecks|张鲁]], but the route toward Hanzhong threatens the Guanzhong commanders. The episode interprets Cao Cao's refusal to change course and his instruction not to attack first as a deliberate effort to make the coalition initiate hostilities and lose the public legitimacy advantage.

Hanji 1105 identifies the coalition as numerous but divisible and credits [[JiaXu|贾诩]]'s counsel as the decision hinge. Hanji 1122 then expands both the division and battlefield sequence: Cao Cao fixes the coalition at Tong Pass while [[XuHuangCaoGeneral|徐晃]] and [[ZhuLingLateHan|朱灵]] secure the western route, crosses and fortifies by stages, feigns weakness, accepts peace terms to gain rest and lower vigilance, and uses Cao-Han familiarity plus an altered letter to intensify Ma Chao's suspicion. Light troops engage first; elite cavalry then attacks the flanks after coalition morale and coordination have deteriorated.

## Key Characteristics

- Begins with an announced Hanzhong expedition whose route creates coercive pressure in Guanzhong.
- Makes the order of mobilization and attack politically consequential because the first mover can be classified as a rebel.
- Uses route control, protected movement, camps, and delay to create a favorable operational position before decisive combat.
- Converts existing coalition fragility into active distrust through social and documentary signals.
- Uses apparent peace and weakness as a temporary lull for rest and enemy complacency rather than as a durable settlement.
- Concentrates a divided multi-leader coalition into one target and attacks after its usable cohesion has fallen.

## Qualifications

These podcast summaries do not independently establish exact force totals, every maneuver, the authorship and wording of advice, correspondence details, cavalry identification, casualties, or private motives. Hanji 1120's deliberate-provocation reading and Hanji 1122's unified-plan reconstruction are coherent interpretations, not settled proof that Cao Cao predesigned every reaction. “潼关之战” and “渭南之战” are treated here as labels within the same campaign account, while precise phase boundaries remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Added route control, protected movement, fortification, and operational delay to the campaign model.
- Added the public conversation and altered letter as the concrete coalition-division mechanism.
- Reframed feigned peace as a rest-and-vigilance tactic inside a wider sequence.
- Added the light-troop engagement and flank attack as the reported decisive phase.
