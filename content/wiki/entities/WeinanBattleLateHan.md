## Overview

渭南之战 / Battle of Weinan is the 211 CE Guanzhong campaign in which [[CaoCao|曹操]] defeats a coalition led in the current evidence by [[MaChao|马超]] and [[HanSui|韩遂]]. The four-source synthesis connects political sequencing, crossing-point deception, route protection, logistics, engineered distrust, operational delay, and concentrated attack rather than treating the victory as a single battlefield stratagem.

## Current Profile

Hanji 1120 supplies the opening. Cao Cao announces a campaign against [[ZhangLuFivePecks|张鲁]], but the route toward Hanzhong threatens the Guanzhong commanders. The episode interprets Cao Cao's refusal to change course and his instruction not to attack first as a deliberate effort to make the coalition initiate hostilities and lose the public legitimacy advantage.

Hanji 1121 supplies the central maneuver phase. Cao Cao fixes the coalition at Tong Pass while [[XuHuangCaoGeneral|徐晃]] and [[ZhuLingLateHan|朱灵]] secure Pujin, then survives a cavalry attack during the main Yellow River crossing with protection from [[XuChu|许褚]] and a diversion by [[DingFeiLateHan|丁斐]]. Protected corridors, a night Wei River crossing, improvised fortification, and the coalition's weaker food base gradually turn geographic bypass into sustainable position.

Hanji 1105 identifies the coalition as numerous but divisible and credits [[JiaXu|贾诩]]'s counsel as the decision hinge. Hanji 1122 completes the sequence: Cao Cao feigns weakness, accepts peace terms to gain rest and lower vigilance, and uses Cao-Han familiarity plus an altered letter to intensify Ma Chao's suspicion. Light troops engage first; elite cavalry then attacks the flanks after coalition morale and coordination have deteriorated.

## Key Characteristics

- Begins with an announced Hanzhong expedition whose route creates coercive pressure in Guanzhong.
- Makes the order of mobilization and attack politically consequential because the first mover can be classified as a rebel.
- Uses route control, protected movement, camps, and delay to create a favorable operational position before decisive combat.
- Turns a concealed crossing into durable access through rearguard protection, defended corridors, river movement, and field fortification.
- Converts existing coalition fragility into active distrust through social and documentary signals.
- Uses apparent peace and weakness as a temporary lull for rest and enemy complacency rather than as a durable settlement.
- Concentrates a divided multi-leader coalition into one target and attacks after its usable cohesion has fallen.

## Qualifications

These podcast summaries do not independently establish exact force totals, every maneuver, the authorship and wording of advice, correspondence details, cavalry identification, casualties, construction methods, or private motives. Hanji 1120's deliberate-provocation reading and Hanji 1122's unified-plan reconstruction are coherent interpretations, not settled proof that Cao Cao predesigned every reaction. Hanji 1121's morale reading of Cao Cao's personal exposure must be weighed against the command-continuity risk created by the same act. “潼关之战” and “渭南之战” are treated here as labels within the same campaign account, while precise phase boundaries remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Distinguished the concealed Pujin crossing from the later work of keeping the new route open and supplied.
- Added the contested main crossing, Xu Chu's protection, and Ding Fei's diversion to the operational sequence.
- Added protected corridors, the Wei River night crossing, improvised fortification, and coalition food pressure.
- Qualified the morale value of Cao Cao's personal exposure with command-continuity risk.
