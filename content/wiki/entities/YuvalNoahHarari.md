# Yuval Noah Harari

## Overview
Yuval Noah Harari is the historian and philosopher interviewed in [[an-interview-with-yuval-noah-harari-6a872b94c71d02aaba56ca7c]] about AI, information networks, trust, consciousness, politics, and human agency. The episode presents him as the author of [[NexusHarariBook|Nexus]], a recent book about information networks from the Stone Age to AI.

## Current Profile
In this wiki, Harari functions as a civilizational AI-risk thinker rather than a technical AI builder. His profile is defined by connecting AI to social trust, legal categories, intimacy, war, geopolitics, democratic accountability, and the moral status of conscious beings. He accepts severe AI risk as plausible on current trajectories, but his distinctive stance is anti-fatalistic: humans are still making choices in 2026, and those choices should be governed openly.

## Key Characteristics
- Harari treats AI as a general institutional force that can reshape trust, finance, intimacy, war, and self-understanding.
- He rejects inevitability language when it converts present-day AI decisions into supposedly unavoidable destiny.
- He uses historical and philosophical framing, especially information networks and consciousness, to make AI governance intelligible to nontechnical publics.
- He favors bans or hard rules around AI legal personhood and AI systems pretending to be human or conscious.
- He argues from moral standing through suffering: intelligence and agency alone do not make an entity a moral subject.
