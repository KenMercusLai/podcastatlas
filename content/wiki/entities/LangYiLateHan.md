## Overview

郎顗 / Lang Yi is a Shun-era omen specialist, remonstrant, and recommender. Hanji 812 adds his summons after a tomb-building fire and his policy-heavy disaster answer; Hanji 813 shows him using drought and wind anomalies to recommend [[HuangQiongLateHan|黄琼]] and [[LiGuLateHan|李固]]. The source transcripts write 郎乙 and 郎毅, but the Beihai yin-yang specialist, disaster-remonstrance, and recommendation context support normalizing him to 郎顗 while preserving the spelling caution.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-812-guren-40-sui-caineng-dangguan-lordzuaqhzghyd93tpm0fql1cigv|Hanji 812]] gives Lang Yi a direct disaster-inquiry role. After a fire at Emperor An's Gongling tomb buildings, [[EmperorShunOfHan|汉顺帝]] hears that Lang Yi of Beihai knows yin-yang learning and summons him by official carriage in 133 CE to explain celestial and disaster anomalies. Lang Yi uses the opening to criticize the Three Excellencies: high office carries responsibility for recommendation and governance, but the court punishes commandery and kingdom officials more strictly than the senior ministers who recommended them.

The same source makes Lang Yi's memorial practical rather than only omen language. He recommends easing labor after the tomb fire, choosing good ministers, preparing for drought and flood, releasing eligible palace women for marriage, warning frontier commanderies against Western Qiang danger, delaying lawsuits until autumn, and simplifying accumulated laws so ordinary people can avoid punishment more realistically.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-813-gudai-de-gaoguan-jing-ye-hui-bei-ouda-ln-dw3xohnmpavkpu-bdvasvietv|Hanji 813]] places Lang Yi in the same 133 CE court-repair field, when drought and unseasonable west winds let him speak about political failure through a Heaven-and-human-affairs frame. He argues that sincere self-correction matters more than repeated sacrificial performance, and he recommends [[HuangQiongLateHan|黄琼]] and [[LiGuLateHan|李固]] as people Shun should bring into use.

Lang Yi is not presented as a durable office-seeker in these sources. Shun appoints him langzhong after receiving the Hanji 813 memorial, but Lang Yi feigns illness and does not take up the post. His wiki significance is therefore concentrated in three roles: omen expert summoned into crisis explanation, disaster-policy remonstrant who turns signs into governance remedies, and recommender who helps bring Li Gu and Huang Qiong into the 133 CE court-repair field.

## Key Characteristics

- Yin-yang and omen specialist summoned by Shun after a tomb-building fire.
- Disaster-remonstrance speaker who translates fire, drought, wind, and seasonal anomalies into political criticism.
- Talent recommender who helps bring Huang Qiong and Li Gu into Shun's attention.
- Critic of empty ritual performance when it is not joined to real self-correction.
- High-office accountability critic who asks why local recommenders are punished while the Three Excellencies' recommendation responsibility is spared.
- Reluctant office-holder who avoids the langzhong post after being appointed.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 812 and Hanji 813. The transcript spellings 郎乙 and 郎毅 are preserved as cautions, and the page does not yet establish Lang Yi's broader biography, writings, offices, or later career beyond the Shun-era disaster and recommendation setting.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 812 as Lang Yi's summons, high-office accountability, and seven-proposal disaster-policy layer.
- Reframed Lang Yi from recommendation-only remonstrant into omen expert, practical policy critic, and talent recommender.
- Preserved Hanji 813 as the drought/wind recommendation layer that brings Huang Qiong and Li Gu into Shun's attention.
- Normalized the transcript forms 郎乙 and 郎毅 cautiously to 郎顗 while keeping the uncertainty visible.
