# The Knights of the Cross / 《十字军骑士》

## Overview

*The Knights of the Cross* is [[HenrykSienkiewicz|Henryk Sienkiewicz]]'s historical novel about medieval Poland's conflict with the [[TeutonicOrder|Teutonic Order]], culminating in the 1410 Polish-Lithuanian victory at Grunwald.

## Current Profile

The episode judges the novel unevenly. Its romance is long, its characters often schematic, and its early pacing slow; its account of gender and its Polish-centered moral world also show their age. Yet estates, food, equipment, hospitality, court ritual, captivity, dueling, and war give the novel unusual material density, while the final battle produces a strong sense of physical presence.

The novel is therefore both a medieval narrative and a late-nineteenth-century national artifact. Its historical victory helped preserve Polish confidence under Russian rule, but it also maps modern national opposition onto a period structured by dynasty, confession, estate, lordship, and mixed military coalitions.

## Key Characteristics

- Historical adventure moving from romance and captivity toward large-scale war.
- Rich description of medieval material life, social ritual, estate management, and combat.
- Polish-centered portrayal of the Teutonic conflict with some sympathetic German characters.
- Traditional plotting and characterization that may feel slow or schematic to modern readers.
- Cultural-national work written under foreign rule.
