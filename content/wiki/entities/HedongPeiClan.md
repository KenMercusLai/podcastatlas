## Overview

河东裴氏 / Hedong Pei Clan enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-723-bi-kongjia-gengniu-de-jiazu-pingsha-shi-peishi-lqti3stvdcw7utgm6uzwv5giazhj|Hanji 723]] as an elite lineage used to ask why some families can convert local origin, officeholding, and family rules into unusually long durability. The episode locates the clan in Wenxi within [[HedongCommandery|河东郡]] and treats Pei Bai village's "Chancellor Village" memory as shorthand for that afterlife.

## Current Profile

The episode presents the Pei clan as beginning to rise in the Qin-Han world, reaching peak prominence in the Sui-Tang period, declining after the Five Dynasties without disappearing, and still producing jinshi in Ming-Qing memory. Its scale is expressed through official and genealogical counts: more than six hundred people recorded or given biographies in standard histories, more than three thousand officials above seventh rank, and a claimed fifty-nine chancellors and fifty-nine great generals.

The source's interpretation is institutional rather than purely genealogical. The host argues that the clan's endurance depended on family rules divided into twelve instructions and ten admonitions. Because the transcript names but does not enumerate those rules, the durable wiki claim is narrower: this source uses Hedong Pei as a case for [[LineageRuleDurability|family-rule durability]], not as evidence for the exact content of the Pei corpus.

## Key Characteristics

- Wenxi/Hedong-origin elite lineage presented as a rare long-duration Chinese family case.
- Source says the family rises from Qin-Han roots, peaks under Sui-Tang, and retains later traces after decline.
- Official scale is represented through very large source-scoped counts of biographies, officials, chancellors, and great generals.
- Pei Bai village functions in the episode as the place-memory anchor behind the "Chancellor Village" label.
- The source explains durability through family instructions and admonitions, while leaving their contents undeveloped.
- The page currently treats the Pei clan as a lineage-governance case rather than a complete genealogy.

## Qualifications

This page is source-bounded to one podcast summary and should not be treated as a complete Pei genealogy. The transcript provides headline counts and a family-rule explanation but does not enumerate the twelve instructions or ten admonitions. Wenxi/闻喜 spelling is normalized from the transcript's 文喜 form by context but remains tied to this source until additional evidence sharpens the place history.
