## Overview

郅恽 / Zhi Yun is synthesized as an Eastern Han rule-bound officer and adviser whose remonstrance profile begins before the succession cases. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-714-shi-shui-di-yi-ge-han-chu-nan-er-dang-ma-ge-guo-shi-lpkqxrl3dsok4ui0lij4c3x8zfpi|Hanji 714]] now adds the Shangdong Gate scene: Zhi Yun refuses to open the gate when he cannot identify the emperor's late hunting party, then criticizes [[LiuXiu|光武帝刘秀]]'s hunting, receives reward, and sees the more compliant gate officer demoted. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-719-guangwudi-liuxiu-suanbusuan-zhanan-lk8eme3wwyk5qkjjfxkp0zsc77ix|Hanji 719]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-720-donghan-kaiguo-taizi-weihe-fanfu-da-cizhi-baogao-lslizspo8rywnf3cebyhincdhvz0|Hanji 720]] then place him in the Guo Shengtong and Liu Qiang succession-risk field.

## Current Profile

Hanji 714 supplies Zhi Yun's earliest current role. As Shangdong Gate officer, he refuses to open the gate at night because the lamps are too far away for him to identify the arriving party. Liu Xiu enters by another gate, but the next day Zhi Yun submits a criticism of excessive hunting and misplaced pleasure.

The outcome makes the case a rule-and-remonstrance signal. Liu Xiu rewards Zhi Yun with cloth and demotes the Dongzhong Gate officer who opened the other gate. The episode therefore presents Zhi Yun as someone whose procedural refusal becomes a successful political admonition, not merely as a stubborn gatekeeper.

Hanji 719 supplies Zhi Yun's first current role. As Liu Xiu prepares to depose Guo Shengtong and make [[YinLihua|阴丽华]] empress, Liu Xiu initially treats the matter as private family affairs. Zhi Yun counters that an empress's elevation or deposition concerns the foundation of the state and must be handled carefully.

Hanji 720 supplies the downstream succession role. After Guo's deposition, Liu Qiang becomes uneasy because his mother's status had supported his crown-prince position. Zhi Yun advises him that remaining crown prince is dangerous and does not help him serve his mother; retiring to a fief is safer and more filial.

Across the two episodes, Zhi Yun functions as a boundary adviser. He tells the ruler that palace change is public politics, then tells the endangered heir that moral language and withdrawal may preserve life and kinship under the changed order.

## Key Characteristics

- Adviser who reframes palace-household decisions as state-foundation matters.
- Rule-bound officer who refuses a nighttime gate request when identification is uncertain.
- Remonstrant whose criticism of imperial hunting is rewarded rather than punished.
- Succession-risk diagnostician after Guo Shengtong's deposition.
- Links empress status, crown-prince safety, and filial service as one political field.
- Offers cautionary advice without becoming an independent power center in the current evidence.

## Qualifications

The page is source-bounded to the gate-remonstrance scene and two succession-advice scenes. Hanji 714's `志恽` transcript form is normalized to the established 郅恽 / Zhi Yun route. The page does not reconstruct Zhi Yun's full official biography, offices, family line, or later reputation. The Hanji 719 and Hanji 720 scenes should be read together because the second is the direct consequence field of the first, but the sources do not prove Zhi Yun controlled the final imperial decision.
