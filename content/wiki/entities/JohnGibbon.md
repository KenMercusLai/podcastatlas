# John Gibbon

## Overview

John Gibbon was a U.S. Army colonel whose explicit sympathy for Native resistance coexisted with command of a Montana column in the campaign preceding the [[BattleOfLittleBighorn]].

## Current Profile

The episode opens with Gibbon asking white Americans to imagine how they would react if subjected to the dispossession imposed on Native peoples. That warning gives him unusual moral clarity within the narrative, but it did not remove him from the military system carrying the policy forward.

Gibbon's winter advance failed in severe weather. In the summer he moved east from Fort Ellis along the Yellowstone and met [[AlfredTerry]] on the steamer Far West. He reported signs of a large Native village near the Rosebud or Little Bighorn and remained part of the intended convergence as Terry released [[GeorgeArmstrongCuster]] ahead.

## Key Characteristics

- He articulated the moral logic of resistance to dispossession.
- His empathy coexisted with active participation in the campaign.
- Weather defeated his winter advance.
- His summer reconnaissance contributed to locating the Native village.
- His column was one component of a communication-poor convergence.
