# Hans Jonas / 汉斯·约纳斯

## Overview
Hans Jonas / 汉斯·约纳斯 appears in the wiki through [[episode-227-ershiyishiji-qingchun-wuyu-8211230332-278487]], where [[RenNing]] uses him to explain why irreversible modern technologies require precaution before harm is fully knowable.

## Current Profile
The episode presents Jonas as a philosopher whose ethics were shaped by twentieth-century catastrophe and by technologies whose consequences exceed ordinary trial-and-error learning. His book [[ThePrincipleOfResponsibility]] becomes the bridge between nuclear or genetic risk and the episode's diagnosis of [[YouthPrecautionaryCulture]]: when decisions feel irreversible, people treat prevention as the moral default.

## Key Characteristics
- Represents responsibility ethics under modern technological power.
- Supplies an intellectual source for precaution before irreversible damage.
- Is framed biographically through exile, war experience, and the loss of his mother in Auschwitz.
- Helps the episode explain why younger people may apply large-scale risk ethics to ordinary life decisions.

## Evidence
- Responsibility ethics: [[episode-227-ershiyishiji-qingchun-wuyu-8211230332-278487]] says Jonas is invoked through [[ThePrincipleOfResponsibility]] to argue that irreversible decisions cannot be handled by ordinary trial and error.
- Biographical seriousness: [[episode-227-ershiyishiji-qingchun-wuyu-8211230332-278487]] emphasizes that Jonas had personally seen irreversible historical harm rather than merely imagining distant future risk.
- Personal-life extension: [[episode-227-ershiyishiji-qingchun-wuyu-8211230332-278487]] treats Jonas's principle as unexpectedly migrating from states, nuclear power, and biotechnology into personal choices.
