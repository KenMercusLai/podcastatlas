# Perplexity Computer

## Overview
Perplexity Computer is Perplexity's agent product for orchestrating models, sub-agents, tools, files, connectors, browsers, and computer environments around a user's objective.

## Current Profile
The first source identifies Perplexity Computer as an agent product associated with reported revenue acceleration. The CEO interview supplies the operating model: different models are assigned to different specialties; sub-agents and tools execute parts of a job; and the system can return completed artifacts such as memos, decks, or research rather than only an answer. Perplexity also describes a future hybrid architecture in which private local data stays on user-controlled hardware while heavier or longer-running work moves to a private server-side environment.

## Key Characteristics
- Orchestrates multiple external models rather than relying on one universal model.
- Uses sub-agents, tools, skills, files, command-line operations, and connectors to complete multi-step work.
- Extends from cloud research into browser and computer control through Perplexity and Comet surfaces.
- Supports a proposed local/server split for private context and heavier remote execution.
- Aims to hide API-key, billing, and provider-management complexity from users.
- Requires verification, permissions, recovery, and platform access to make autonomous execution trustworthy.

## Qualifications
Revenue, valuation, investment, customer, and productivity claims are source-attributed rather than independently verified. Multi-model orchestration can add routing and review complexity, and browser or computer access creates credential, transaction, privacy, and recovery risk. The hybrid personal-computer architecture is described as work in progress, not a demonstrated general solution.

## What Changed
- Added the product's multi-model orchestration, sub-agent, file, tool, and computer-use architecture.
- Added the proposed local/private and server-side execution split.
- Added platform access, permissions, and verification as core product boundaries.
