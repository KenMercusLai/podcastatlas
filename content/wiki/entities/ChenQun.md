## Overview

陈群 / Chen Qun is a late-Han official presented in the current evidence first as [[ChenJiLateHan|陈纪]]'s son within [[CaoCao|曹操]]'s post-[[Xiapi|下邳]] incorporation of elite families, and later as an advocate of limited restoration of mutilating punishment.

## Current Profile

Chen Qun's current profile has an incorporation branch and a penal-policy branch. After Xiapi, he appears with Chen Ji among the socially usable actors whom Cao Cao treats courteously after executing [[LyuBu|吕布]], [[ChenGongLateHan|陈宫]], and [[GaoShun|高顺]]. The scene supports an incorporation profile rather than an independent biography: family standing and administrative usefulness survive a defeated regime's collapse. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1026-jiemi-sanguo-di-yi-mengjiang-lvbu-de-luomu-lhjf-agujuliglqchumtff3tftxg1|Hanji 1026]]

In the penal-policy branch, Chen Qun relays Chen Ji's claim that abolition of mutilation had produced replacement flogging that could be lighter in name but deadlier in execution, then supports limited castration or foot mutilation for serious offenses below the death threshold. The evidence does not establish that this comparison was empirically correct or that restoration would have reduced total harm; it shows Chen Qun reasoning from outcome, deterrence, and sentence gradation while accepting irreversible bodily injury. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1139-jiemi-gudai-rouxing-yige-bi-yige-xiaren-lgnj-8smphspf8nud-2o3xh_lggk|Hanji 1139]]

## Key Characteristics

- Enters Cao Cao's order through an elite-family channel after the fall of Xiapi.
- Is treated as administratively incorporable rather than identified with Lü Bu's personal unreliability.
- Relays Chen Ji's execution-focused critique of nominally humane penal reform.
- Supports a graded punishment tier between flogging and death, even at the cost of irreversible mutilation.

## Qualifications

The current evidence consists of two popular-history episode summaries, not an independent biography, legal text, or primary-source collation. Chen Qun's exact proposal, the mapped offenses, comparative mortality, and the degree to which his father's view is preserved verbatim remain source-scoped. Supporting mutilation should not be recoded as simple humanitarianism merely because the argument invokes survival.

## What Changed

- Added Chen Qun's reported support for limited restoration of mutilating punishment.
- Reframed his profile from elite-family incorporation alone to include outcome-focused but irreversible penal reasoning.
