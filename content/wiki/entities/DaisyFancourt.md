# Daisy Fancourt

## Overview
Daisy Fancourt is the psychobiologist interviewed by [[HiddenBrain]] about the emotional, social, physiological, and cognitive pathways through which artistic engagement may support health.

## Current Profile
The two supplied episodes trace Fancourt from a musical upbringing and Oxford hospital-arts work into research prompted by observations of singing, theatre, and familiar music changing patient behavior. Her public explanation moves from clinical scenes to reward and emotion mechanisms, group-singing studies, longitudinal cohorts, neuroimaging, public investment, and practical habit design. She presents arts participation as a potentially useful health practice while distinguishing participation from excellence and association or possibility from guaranteed treatment outcomes.

## Key Characteristics
- Connects artistic practice with psychobiology, public health, and social connection.
- Uses hospital observations as questions for research rather than treating anecdotes as sufficient proof.
- Studies both receptive engagement and active creation across music, visual art, movement, craft, and storytelling.
- Emphasizes participation and sustained attention over talent or professional status.
- Recommends consistency, variety, novelty, and social scaffolding for everyday practice.
- Treats access to community arts as a public-health and social-infrastructure question, not only an individual hobby choice.

## Qualifications
This profile is based on two overlapping structured episodes from the same program, not Fancourt's publications, institutional biography, study protocols, or a systematic review. Their agreement is repeated provenance rather than independent confirmation. Named effect sizes, biomarkers, imaging results, basic-income outcomes, health-economic returns, causal interpretations, and practical dose suggestions therefore remain episode-reported rather than independently verified or individualized medical guidance.
