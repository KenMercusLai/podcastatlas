# Hawaii / 夏威夷

Hawaii enters the wiki through [[168-yin-yu-xin-yibu-riben-laogong-yimin-de-kuayang-huikuan-shi-lmgfmmgkawoa-6xyucnm1ljebzwu]] as the first major destination in the episode's [[JapaneseLaborMigrationRemittances]] story. Before Hawaii became a U.S. territory, Hawaiian sugar plantations needed inexpensive labor and helped draw contract workers from East Asia, including Japanese workers sent under Meiji-era official arrangements.

The source uses Hawaii as a labor and financial node rather than only a destination. Japanese migrants worked under plantation discipline, saved under government rules, sent money home through postal and bank channels, and later sometimes moved onward to California. Hawaii therefore sits between [[Japan]], the [[UnitedStates]], [[YokohamaSpecieBank|Yokohama Specie Bank]], and [[StateMediatedRemittanceSystem]].

## Key Claims
- Hawaiian sugar production created demand for large numbers of East Asian contract workers.
- Early poor treatment of Japanese workers helped push the Meiji state to control later migration and remittance systems more tightly.
- Hawaii became a bridge from Japan to the U.S. West Coast after it entered the U.S. territorial system.
