# The Jeremy Vine Show

## Overview

The *Jeremy Vine Show* is the BBC Radio 2 current-affairs and phone-in programme used by the episode as a window into everyday British concerns.

## Current Profile

The programme's distinctive role in this source comes from mixing serious public affairs with ordinary, comic, or culturally specific topics. That tonal range allows it to draw a broad audience into discussion without requiring every item to fit a conventional political-news agenda.

Listener calls and rapid editorial response make the show a possible early signal of public concern. The episode cites fly-tipping and parental concern about online harms as issues discussed on air before later formal political action. This does not make the programme a representative survey, but it can reveal salience, vocabulary, and lived experience that slower institutions or fragmented online spaces miss.

## Key Characteristics

- Combines current affairs, listener phone-ins, and lighter cultural topics.
- Uses BBC Radio 2's broad reach and low-friction listening format.
- Moves between serious analysis and deliberate silliness without treating them as separate audiences.
- Can surface emerging concerns before they become formal parliamentary or legislative subjects.
- Offers qualitative public-mood evidence rather than representative measurement.

- **Editorial range:** [[school-of-shock-teenagers-get-dimmer-6a9fd55a89f0f48b8810a1bb]] lists topics from social housing, pornography, and war to vertical drinking, garden centres, and ten-pin bowling.
- **Public response:** [[school-of-shock-teenagers-get-dimmer-6a9fd55a89f0f48b8810a1bb]] describes phone-in radio as responsive to audience mood and contrasts its shared listening experience with an atomized online environment.
- **Early signals:** [[school-of-shock-teenagers-get-dimmer-6a9fd55a89f0f48b8810a1bb]] says the programme covered mass fly-tipping before a House of Lords inquiry and heard parental concern about online harms before the Online Safety Act became law.

## Qualifications

The examples establish editorial responsiveness, not predictive accuracy or population representativeness. Callers are self-selecting, producers choose and frame topics, and the source supplies no systematic comparison with polling, search data, local journalism, or other radio programmes. Weekly British radio-listening totals and Radio 2's ranking remain episode-attributed.
