# Emma Hamilton

## Overview

Emma Hamilton was a self-made celebrity performer, model, and court intermediary whose classically draped “attitudes” made antiquity visible and imitable before her relationship with [[HoratioNelson|Horatio Nelson]] brought greater scandal without durable financial security.

## Current Profile

Hamilton performed poses in simple drapery for visitors in Naples, presenting herself as a sequence of classical figures or statues. The performance turned antiquity into a living bodily image rather than an archaeological reference. British travellers, including Lady Charlotte Campbell, carried impressions of that image home and reproduced aspects of the style, making performance, celebrity, travel, observation, memory, and imitation part of the broader [[FashionCirculationInfrastructure]].

Her path to that salon was marked by poverty and unequal patronage. Born Amy Lyon in 1765 and raised after her father's early death, she moved from domestic service into London's theatrical and sexual economy. Pregnancy and abandonment left her dependent on [[CharlesGreville|Charles Greville]], whose protection required separation from her child, a new name, broken ties, and a performed respectable identity. Greville introduced her to [[GeorgeRomney|George Romney]]; repeated portraits and prints made her face widely recognizable, while also recording the expressive range that later powered her [[EmbodiedClassicalPerformance|attitudes]]. Greville then deceptively sent her to Naples, where [[SirWilliamHamilton|Sir William Hamilton]] became her partner, supported her education, and eventually married her.

Her life also shows the reach and limits of celebrity as informal power. Friendship with [[MariaCarolina|Maria Carolina]] created an unusual channel into the Neapolitan court: the new source credits her with helping Nelson obtain troops in 1793 and supplies at Syracuse in 1798, before she celebrated the Nile victory and received him in Naples. In 1798-1799 she staged Nelson's welcome, assisted the royal family's escape and treasure transfer, and cared for the dying Prince Carlo Alberto during the storm passage to Palermo. She also nursed Nelson, amplified his heroic identity, and became his emotional refuge. The sources resist a Lady Macbeth explanation of his later conduct: gendered and class hostility made her an easy cause of command failure, while her reported appeals for mercy sit uneasily beside an alleged toast to Caracciolo's execution.

The Palermo and return journey material makes her more than a later Merton companion: pregnancy, travel with both men, aristocratic ridicule, public appearances with [[FannyNelson|Fanny Nelson]], Horatia's concealed birth, and mutual jealousy made the relationship simultaneously intimate, theatrical, and harmful. Palermo remained connected to Naples, Sicily, and Malta strategy, so her presence cannot by itself explain Nelson's uneven command.

After the [[BattleOfCopenhagen1801|Battle of Copenhagen]], Nelson immediately returned in writing to jealousy over her association with the Prince of Wales and then sent her a poem, evidence of emotional dependence filtered entirely through his perspective. Merton was their attempted domestic refuge with Horatia, but expansion, heavy hospitality and decoration, jewelry spending, praise of Nelson, social exclusion, and conflict with Fanny made it both a household and a public theater of their relationship. Sir William's death removed the triangle without securing Emma financially; his limited provision and Nelson's renewed command left the household dependent on patronage and promises. Hamilton later waited through Nelson's pursuit of Villeneuve and experienced his September 1805 return and departure as a compressed reunion and final separation. After Trafalgar, celebrity did not prevent debt, the sale of Merton, imprisonment, and death in Calais.

## Key Characteristics

- Survivor of poverty, sexual dependence, child separation, and controlling patronage whose ascent did not erase vulnerability.
- Celebrity performer whose technically demanding “attitudes” animated classical figures for elite spectators.
- Model and fashion intermediary whose image travelled through portraits, prints, visitors, and imitation.
- Household builder whose Merton refuge also magnified spending, scandal, flattery, and social exclusion.
- Participant in a publicly exposed relationship triangle whose pregnancy, travel, and concealed parenthood intensified Fanny's harm.
- Informal court intermediary whose care, access, mercy appeals, and disputed influence resist a single seductress or avenger role.
- Partner of Nelson and cautionary case in which public fame and intimate access outlasted financial protection.

## Qualifications

The Regency source uses Hamilton to explain fashion transmission, while the Nelson episodes often center his emotions and command; his jealous accusations should not be treated as evidence of her conduct. The new biographical source recovers more of Emma's independent life but still relies on uncertain childhood reconstruction, letters, biographers, elite witnesses, and modern comparisons. Her early sexual labor, work for James Graham, relationship motives, Romney portrait count, audience reactions, celebrity rank, fashion influence, language fluency, and exact causal role in obtaining troops or Syracuse supplies remain source-scoped. Contemporary hostility mixed criticism of adultery and public behavior with class, gender, and anti-Italian prejudice, yet identifying those biases does not remove the harm to Fanny or settle Emma's later royalist conduct. Claims that she drove the Naples repression, appealed for mercy, or toasted Caracciolo's death require stronger evidence and should not transfer Nelson's agency to her. Precise finances, household labor, responsibility for spending, Horatia's treatment, and responsibility for Hamilton's decline also require fuller evidence.
