# Emma Hamilton

## Overview

Emma Hamilton appears as a celebrity performer whose classically draped “attitudes” helped make Romantic antiquity visible, embodied, and imitable in British fashion culture.

## Current Profile

In [[238-the-regency-revolution-glt7671750288]], Hamilton performs poses in simple drapery for visitors in Naples, presenting herself as a sequence of classical figures or statues. The performance turns antiquity into a living bodily image rather than an archaeological reference. British travellers, including Lady Charlotte Campbell, carry impressions of that image home and reproduce aspects of the style.

Hamilton therefore functions as a pre-mass-media fashion intermediary. Performance, celebrity, travel, observation, memory, and imitation connect her to the broader [[FashionCirculationInfrastructure]] through which Regency style moved.

## Key Characteristics

- Celebrity performer associated with classically inspired “attitudes.”
- Embodied Romantic and neoclassical drapery for elite spectators.
- Fashion intermediary whose image travelled through visitors and imitation.
- Example of influence operating through performance before modern broadcast media.

## Evidence

- Classical performance: [[238-the-regency-revolution-glt7671750288]] describes Hamilton posing in simple drapery as figures legible to spectators through classical art.
- Travel transmission: [[238-the-regency-revolution-glt7671750288]] presents British visitors as observers who carried the image into other social settings.
- Imitation: [[238-the-regency-revolution-glt7671750288]] names Lady Charlotte Campbell as an elite adopter of Hamilton's classically inflected dress.
