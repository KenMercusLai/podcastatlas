## Overview

殷梨亭（金庸小说） / Yin Liting (Jin Yong) is a Wudang disciple in [[HeavenSwordDragonSaber|《倚天屠龙记》]] whose lost betrothal to [[JiXiaofuJinYong|纪晓芙]] and later marriage to her daughter [[YangBuhuiJinYong|杨不悔]] create an unusual age and relational-history problem.

## Current Profile

The episode does not deny that Yin initially mistakes Yang Buhui for her mother while injured, but argues that the later relationship rests on prolonged contact and care with Yang herself. His earlier devotion to Ji, Yang's resemblance, and the plot's wish to repair an old emotional debt remain relevant without wholly defining the marriage.

The relationship gains audience sympathy through Yin's vulnerability, Yang's initiative, and the stability they offer one another. It nevertheless carries a stronger ethical taboo than the narrative fully explores: Yang is the daughter of his former fiancée and substantially younger, even without blood relation.

## Key Characteristics

- Carries long-standing grief from Ji Xiaofu's broken betrothal and death.
- Encounters Yang Buhui while severely injured and dependent on care.
- Initially confuses Yang with her mother, making resemblance ethically relevant.
- Develops a later relationship that the hosts distinguish from simple substitution.
- Occupies the older and historically entangled position in a large age-gap marriage.
- Receives narrative and community acceptance more quickly than the taboo might predict.

## Qualifications

The source does not establish the characters' full internal motives or provide a comprehensive ethical defense of the marriage. Care, mutual choice, and lack of blood relation matter, but do not erase age, dependency, resemblance, or inherited relational history. “Generational compensation” is a narrative interpretation, not proof that either character acts to discharge Ji Xiaofu's debt.
