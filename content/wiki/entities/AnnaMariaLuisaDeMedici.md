## Overview

Anna Maria Luisa de' Medici / 安娜·玛丽亚·路易萨 was the last member of the main Medici line and the dynastic heir whose family pact transferred collections to Tuscany on the condition that they remain in Florence.

## Current Profile

The episode places Anna Maria at the end of a failed succession system. Her brothers left no continuing legitimate line, proposed alternatives did not preserve the dynasty, and Medici rule ended in 1737. As the surviving heir, she held palaces, villas, paintings, sculpture, jewelry, furniture, books, and manuscripts accumulated across generations.

Her 1743 settlement gave that property to the succeeding rulers while restricting removal from Florence. The episode treats this condition as the dynasty's most durable public act: objects accumulated through wealth, devotion, display, propaganda, and government remained concentrated in the city and later supported its museum landscape. Her legacy is therefore not that she saved every work by herself, but that inheritance law redirected dynastic assets toward place-bound civic continuity.

## Key Characteristics

- Last member of the main Medici dynastic line.
- Heir to a large, heterogeneous family collection and property portfolio.
- Party to a transfer conditioned on the collections remaining in Florence.
- Bridge between private dynastic ownership and later public cultural access.
- Memorial figure in Florence after the family's political extinction.

## Qualifications

The source is a podcast summary, not the legal instrument or a provenance study. The exact title of the agreement, ownership categories, enforceability, later transfers, conservation, museum access, and causal share in Florence's present collections require primary and specialist evidence. Keeping objects in the city did not automatically make them immediately public or resolve how they had been acquired.

- Distinguished place-bound preservation from immediate public ownership or universal access.
