# Aldi / 奥乐齐

[[zhizhuxia-xinpian-naxia-jinban-guonei-piaofang-ai-moxing-baofa-jiagezhan-1004403588]] adds Aldi / 奥乐齐 as the episode's concrete [[HardDiscountRetail|hard-discount retail]] case in China. The source says Aldi had created a Hangzhou company and was preparing to enter that market, while its China CEO had stated a plan to open 50 new domestic stores that year.

The wiki relevance is less a full corporate history than a retail-format signal. Aldi is used to show how low-price supermarket competition can depend on sourcing, private-label capability, factory cooperation, store rollout, and whether customers trust discounted goods as deliberately efficient rather than merely cheap.

## Source Position
- Aldi is framed as pushing beyond its earlier China footprint into Hangzhou.
- The episode places Aldi inside a broader Chinese hard-discount wave, not as an isolated foreign-retailer story.
- The source says more retailers are experimenting with direct sourcing or factory cooperation to reduce supply-chain layers and price.
- The same segment warns that many supermarket private-label goods may still come from the same contract-manufacturing base, leaving product-development capability as a weak point.
