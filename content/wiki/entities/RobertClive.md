# Robert Clive

## Overview

Robert Clive was an [[EastIndiaCompany|East India Company]] servant and soldier whose recapture of Calcutta and intervention against Siraj ud-Daulah made him a central operator in the Company's acquisition of Bengal.

## Current Profile

[[75-the-east-india-company-glt7809915873]] presents Clive as a failed accountant who proved unusually effective as a soldier. After returning to India on mistaken intelligence about a French fleet, he retook Calcutta following Siraj ud-Daulah's seizure of the city and the Black Hole of Calcutta episode.

His subsequent advance on Murshidabad depended on collaboration with [[JagatSeth|Jagat Seth]] and other Indian interests that opposed Siraj. Both sources therefore treat Plassey not as Clive's solitary national victory but as a financed Anglo-Indian coup involving bribery, whose treasure strengthened both Clive personally and the Company institutionally. The Seven Years' War account places that locally driven operation inside a wider but loosely coordinated Anglo-French global struggle.

## Key Characteristics

- Company servant whose military ability contrasted with his earlier failure in accountancy.
- Commander associated with the recapture of Calcutta and the campaign against Siraj ud-Daulah.
- Beneficiary of Indian banking support and elite conflict in Bengal.
- Personal recipient of payments within a wider transfer of wealth to the Company.
- Contingent actor whose presence helped turn commercial competition into territorial power.

## Evidence

- Career turn: [[75-the-east-india-company-glt7809915873]] describes Clive as an unsuccessful accountant who became an effective soldier and returned to India after false intelligence about a French fleet.
- Bengal campaign: [[75-the-east-india-company-glt7809915873]] connects the recapture of Calcutta to the move against Siraj ud-Daulah and the seizure of treasure at Murshidabad.
- Indian financing: [[75-the-east-india-company-glt7809915873]] says Jagat Seth encouraged the campaign and offered payments to Clive and the Company.
- Global-war context: [[47-the-seven-years-war-glt8800494407]] treats Bengal as a partly autonomous theater and emphasizes bribery and Company interests rather than a simple British battlefield victory.

## Qualifications

These are two related popular-history accounts of Clive's role in Bengal, not independent specialist reconstructions of Plassey. Their language of coup, bribery, corruption, mistaken intelligence, and personal enrichment is retained as interpretation; exact payments, motives, responsibility for Siraj's death, and the Black Hole narrative require wider evidence.

## What Changed

- Added a global-war context while preserving Plassey as a locally financed, bribery-dependent Company operation rather than an isolated national victory.

## Relationships

- [[EastIndiaCompany]] - employer whose commercial power his campaigns helped turn into territorial rule.
- [[JagatSeth]] - banking power presented as financing and encouraging the move against Siraj ud-Daulah.
- [[CharteredCompanySovereignty]] - institutional transition his Bengal campaign accelerated.
- [[MughalEmpire]] - fragmented imperial order within which Company expansion became possible.
- [[SevenYearsWar]] - wider conflict within which his Bengal campaign remained partly autonomous.
