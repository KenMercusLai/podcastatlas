# 《螺丝在拧紧》 / The Turn of the Screw

## Overview

*The Turn of the Screw* is represented as Henry James's Gothic novella of disputed haunting, perception, repression, social position, and narrative reliability.

## Current Profile

The novella sits near detective-fiction history because readers must first decide what kind of signs its apparitions are before deciding what happened. Its ambiguity precedes clean rational detection and makes interpretation itself part of the mystery. [[69-xianliao-tuili-wenxue-lishixuezhe-ke-bu-jiu-shi-zhentan-ma-666244465]]

[[RogerClarke]] proposes a source lineage from Mary Ricketts's written [[HintonAmpner]] account through Bishop Benson to Henry James. The claim gives the novella a possible basis in documented British haunting tradition, but the episode does not establish that transmission independently. [[66-ghosts-glt2309926019]]

## Key Characteristics

- Gothic novella organized around ambiguous supernatural signs.
- Narrative in which perception and reliability cannot be cleanly separated.
- Work methodologically adjacent to mystery fiction without becoming a conventional detective case.
- Possible literary afterlife of the Hinton Ampner haunting record.

## Evidence

- Interpretive ambiguity - [[69-xianliao-tuili-wenxue-lishixuezhe-ke-bu-jiu-shi-zhentan-ma-666244465]] connects ghosts, repression, position, perception, and unreliable narration to mystery method.
- Proposed source lineage - [[66-ghosts-glt2309926019]] reports Clarke's argument connecting [[HintonAmpner]], Bishop Benson, and Henry James.

## Qualifications

The wiki's evidence does not resolve whether the apparitions are supernatural or psychological, and a Gothic text need not yield a single detective-style answer. The Hinton Ampner influence claim is source-scoped and requires literary-historical corroboration before being treated as established.
