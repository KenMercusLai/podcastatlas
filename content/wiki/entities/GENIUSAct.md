# GENIUS Act

## Overview
The GENIUS Act is the U.S. stablecoin-law framework discussed by [[Circle]] and [[Coinbase]] executives in the supplied All-In sources. It is presented as a credibility layer for regulated payment stablecoins and as a contested boundary between issuers, platforms, and banks.

## Current Profile
The sources describe a framework in which regulated stablecoins hold fully reserved, short-duration Treasury assets and issuers cannot pay holder interest directly, while platforms may offer rewards or loyalty incentives. That distinction can support adoption without simply reproducing bank-deposit economics, but it also creates conflict with banks and trade groups concerned about deposit competition and payment control.

## Key Characteristics
- Establishes a U.S. compliance path for payment stablecoins.
- Is described as requiring U.S.-regulated stablecoins to hold fully reserved short-term Treasury assets.
- Restricts direct issuer interest while leaving room for platform rewards and incentives.
- Gives regulated issuers and distributors a claimed level-playing-field advantage.
- Remains contested by banking interests and crypto platforms over how its rules should be implemented or changed.
