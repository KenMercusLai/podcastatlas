## Overview

张汤 / Zhang Tang is a Western Han cruel-official figure in the current evidence. One source uses his closeness to power, resentment from peers, and suicide as the family precedent behind [[ZhangAnshiWesternHan|张安世]]'s low-profile survival; another places him beside [[ZhaoYuWesternHan|赵禹]] in an imperial legal-revision program.

## Current Profile

The current profile is a bounded combination of family precedent and institutional role rather than a full career. Zhang Tang is remembered as Zhang Anshi's capable but harsh father: a court actor close to imperial power who draws ministerial resentment and dies by suicide. That family memory helps explain why Zhang Anshi hides credit, refuses excessive favor, and avoids converting public decisions into private obligation.

Zhang Tang is also placed with Zhao Yu in revising and supplementing law under Han Wudi to constrain officials. The evidence does not distinguish Zhang Tang's initiative, legal theory, or responsibility for particular provisions, so the joint assignment cannot support a more specific allocation of praise or blame.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han official remembered by both episodes within the cruel-official field.
- Father of Zhang Anshi and a negative family precedent for visible closeness to imperial power.
- Associated by Hanji 431 with harsh methods, peer resentment, and death by suicide.
- Paired with Zhao Yu by Hanji 347-1 in revising and supplementing law under Han Wudi.

## Evidence

Cruel-official and family-precedent role:

Legal-revision role:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-347-1-gudai-shi-ruhe-yong-lijiao-pua-zhenjie-lienv-de-lt39fogfivnipellq6y2xl6ctrzq|Hanji 347-1]] says Zhang Tang and Zhao Yu jointly revise law under Han Wudi to regulate officeholders more strictly.

## Qualifications

Neither current source supplies a full biography, case record, or primary-text comparison. Hanji 431's moral language about harshness and Hanji 347-1's joint-revision notice do not by themselves allocate responsibility for specific laws or prove that Zhang Tang and Zhao Yu held identical legal views.
