# Khorasan

## Overview

Khorasan appears as the northeastern imperial region spanning parts of present-day Iran, Afghanistan, and Central Asia where distance, mixed populations, military resources, and anti-Umayyad discontent gave the [[AbbasidRevolution]] its decisive base.

## Current Profile

The episode places Arab conquest of the region around 705-706 and notes that remote areas retained Zoroastrian institutions longer than western centers. By the 740s, Abbasid agents worked especially intensively there, turning social and religious plurality into an organizing opportunity.

Part 1 establishes the opening rather than narrating the rising: failed Umayyad legitimacy, non-Arab Muslim grievances, scholarly criticism, and rival sacred claims made the east receptive. The next episode follows [[AbuMuslim]] converting those conditions into a military coalition.

## Key Characteristics

- Northeastern frontier of the early Arab empire.
- Region with Iranian, Central Asian, Muslim, and Zoroastrian historical layers.
- Distant military and political base less controlled by Damascus than the Syrian core.
- Major field of Abbasid underground organization.
- Launch region for the coalition that defeated the Umayyads.

## Evidence

- **Conquest and regional scope:** [[376-baghdad-the-forging-of-islam-part-1-glt9529602805]] locates Khorasan across northeastern Iran, Afghanistan, and Central Asia and dates conquest to roughly 705-706.
- **Religious persistence:** [[376-baghdad-the-forging-of-islam-part-1-glt9529602805]] contrasts destruction in western centers with some Zoroastrian survival in remote Khorasan.
- **Revolutionary opening:** [[376-baghdad-the-forging-of-islam-part-1-glt9529602805]] identifies Khorasan as the Abbasids' principal field of pre-revolutionary organization.
