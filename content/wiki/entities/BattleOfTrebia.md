# Battle of Trebia / 特雷比亚河战役

## Overview

The Battle of Trebia / 特雷比亚河战役 is [[HannibalBarca]]'s late-218 BC victory in northern Italy, where a cavalry provocation draws a Roman army into cold, hunger, an icy river crossing, flank pressure, and a concealed rear attack.

## Current Profile

The episode treats Trebia as a prepared trap. Roman consular urgency and fear that more Gauls will join Hannibal make an early battle attractive. Hannibal reads that pressure, feeds and warms his army, then sends Numidian cavalry to attack the Roman camp at dawn and withdraw across the river.

Roman infantry still fights effectively in the center and roughly 10,000 men reportedly break through. Defeat comes from the combined system around that center: Roman troops enter cold and unfed, Carthaginian cavalry and elephants clear the wings, flank attacks compress the infantry, and Mago's concealed detachment emerges behind them. The result terrifies Rome and accelerates Gallic alignment with Hannibal.

## Key Characteristics

- Begins with a provocative cavalry raid and controlled withdrawal.
- Uses cold, hunger, and a river crossing to degrade Roman readiness.
- Preserves a strong infantry center while cavalry and elephants win the wings.
- Adds a concealed rear force to convert pressure into collapse.
- Produces recruitment and terror effects as well as battlefield casualties.

## Evidence

- Roman urgency: [[570-hannibal-the-invasion-of-italy-part-3-glt2952414067]] links consular pressure and possible Gallic reinforcement to the choice for battle.
- Preparation asymmetry: [[570-hannibal-the-invasion-of-italy-part-3-glt2952414067]] contrasts fed, warmed Carthaginians with unfed Romans crossing the icy Trebia.
- Combined trap: [[570-hannibal-the-invasion-of-italy-part-3-glt2952414067]] describes wing pressure, Roman success in the center, and Mago's hidden troops attacking from behind.
- Aftermath: [[570-hannibal-the-invasion-of-italy-part-3-glt2952414067]] says the victory spreads terror in Rome and brings more Gauls to Hannibal.

## Qualifications

The battle is reconstructed through ancient narratives. Exact troop counts, weather, motives, casualties, and the number and placement of Mago's force remain source-scoped. The survival of a substantial Roman central formation qualifies any account of effortless or total encirclement.

## What Changed

- Established Trebia as a combined logistical, psychological, and tactical trap.
- Preserved Roman infantry success in the center as a qualification.
- Added the victory's alliance and terror effects in northern Italy.
