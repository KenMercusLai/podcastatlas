## Overview

萧贤 / Xiao Xian enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-601-1-chule-yuhui-tade-wenhua-shuiping-ye-neng-gaiming-lnt4p2mo2nwfm3xqhg0dmfwiip1n|Hanji 601-1]] as a late [[WesternHanDynasty|Western Han]] official and descendant of Xiao Wangzhi whose classical literacy becomes a survival tool. His central act is refusing a Dong-family marriage proposal after reading "云止其中" in Dong-family appointment language as a sign that [[DongXian|董贤]]'s favor has crossed from office promotion toward abdication-coded danger.

## Current Profile

The source identifies Xiao Xian as father-in-law to [[WangHongWesternHan|王洪]] and as a figure whose household is attractive to Dong Xian's father. When Dong's father proposes that Dong Kuanxin marry Xiao Xian's daughter, Xiao Xian reacts with fear rather than eagerness, because he has already noticed abnormal language in the appointment of Dong Xian's father as Da Sima.

Xiao Xian's political judgment turns on a textual detail. He says "云止其中" belongs to the language of Yao yielding to Shun rather than to ordinary Three Excellencies appointment. The source presents this not as antiquarian cleverness but as risk detection: if the court is using abdication-coded language around Dong Xian's family, a marriage tie could bind Xiao's household to a favorite network already approaching a constitutional boundary.

## Key Characteristics

- Late Western Han official connected to the Xiao Wangzhi lineage.
- Father-in-law of Wang Hong and target of a proposed Dong-family marriage alliance.
- Classical-literacy case whose risk judgment depends on recognizing allusive appointment language.
- Cautious family protector who refuses a marriage that would bind his household to Dong Xian's rise.
- Source-bounded example of cultural learning as political survival rather than ornamental education.

## Qualifications

This page does not reconstruct Xiao Xian's full biography, exact office record, or the full Xiao family genealogy. The source's key value is the episode-level interpretation that textual and ritual literacy can expose political risk before open crisis.
