# Nelson Mandela

## Overview

Nelson Mandela is presented as the [[SouthAfrica|South African]] leader who converted forgiveness, strategic empathy, and public symbolism into a practical post-[[Apartheid|apartheid]] reconciliation program.

## Current Profile

The episode frames Mandela as both moral actor and political operator. His prison years matter because they sharpen his understanding of violence, enemies, language, and Afrikaner history. As president, he applies that understanding to rugby and anthem politics: he embraces the [[Springboks]], works with [[FrancoisPienaar]], keeps [[DieStemVanSuidAfrika|Die Stem]] temporarily, and turns the [[RugbyWorldCup1995|1995 Rugby World Cup]] final into a ceremony of inclusion rather than victory over the old order.

## Key Characteristics

- Former [[AfricanNationalCongress|ANC]] leader and prisoner whose authority was rooted in sacrifice and moral discipline.
- Strategist who studied Afrikaans, [[Afrikaners|Afrikaner]] history, and rugby as tools for understanding former opponents.
- Reconciliation leader who refused simple symbolic purges when they threatened to alienate white South Africans.
- Public performer of inclusion who wore [[FrancoisPienaar]]'s number-six [[Springboks|Springbok]] shirt at [[EllisPark]].
- State-builder who treated anthem compromise as recognition of people the [[AfricanNationalCongress|ANC]] did not yet fully represent.

## Evidence

- Prison and political formation: [[682-south-africa-mandela-and-the-death-of-apartheid-part-6-glt6535658080]] says Mandela spent 27 years in prison after his 1964 conviction and reflected there on the limits of violence.
- Enemy knowledge: [[682-south-africa-mandela-and-the-death-of-apartheid-part-6-glt6535658080]] says Mandela learned Afrikaans, studied Afrikaner history, and learned rugby from prison guard Christo Brand.
- Springbok decision: [[682-south-africa-mandela-and-the-death-of-apartheid-part-6-glt6535658080]] says Mandela resisted ANC radicals who wanted the Springbok name and green jersey banned.
- Rugby final performance: [[682-south-africa-mandela-and-the-death-of-apartheid-part-6-glt6535658080]] describes Mandela wearing Pienaar's jersey number and receiving the crowd's chant at Ellis Park.
- Anthem compromise: [[682-south-africa-mandela-and-the-death-of-apartheid-part-6-glt6535658080]] says Mandela argued that [[DieStemVanSuidAfrika|Die Stem]] carried the emotions of people the ANC did not yet represent.

## Qualifications

This profile is source-scoped to Mandela's rugby and anthem reconciliation strategy. It does not cover his full biography, presidency, negotiations, economic choices, or later South African political debates.
