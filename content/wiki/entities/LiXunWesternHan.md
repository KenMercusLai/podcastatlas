## Overview

李寻 / Li Xun is synthesized as a Western Han court figure whose authority comes from omen and disaster interpretation joined to practical political realism. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-559-mingming-wuzui-weihe-tianxiaren-dou-xiang-ta-si-lu3ckuxwujdbj4zdoqhvo2cg1rs0|Hanji 559]] first shows him warning [[ZhaiFangjinWesternHan|翟方进]] that an 荧惑守心 crisis may require a fatal substitute. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-566-buxiang-jianglin-weihe-guren-weiju-tiangou-shiyue-lkukbe4hu71ti_lmasca-mpbffxj|Hanji 566]] then shows him giving [[HanAidi|汉哀帝]] a broad disaster diagnosis after earthquake, lunar eclipse, and flood signs. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-579-zoujin-kexue-guyao-shi-yao-ma-lof7pvrnlzv2lmbzjshbx7vi2c2n|Hanji 579]] later shows him reading a palace sound as Hongfan "鼓妖" and advising Aidi to dismiss the newly appointed [[ZhuBoWesternHan|朱博]] and [[ZhaoXuanWesternHan|赵璇]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-581-han-aidi-zai-guishen-zhidao-you-duo-yuchun-ltok7kbzmuf-os84spseww0qjbrm|Hanji 581]] shows the same omen-literate credibility helping [[XiaHeliangWesternHan|夏贺良]] reach Aidi's illness-and-renewed-mandate decision environment.

## Current Profile

Hanji 559 gives Li Xun an earlier and harsher omen-advice role. Serving in the chancellor's office, he warns Zhai Fangjin that the celestial crisis may be redirected toward him and proposes selecting someone among the chancellor's staff to die in his place. The page treats this as evidence of Li's political realism inside omen culture, not as a morally endorsed recommendation.

Hanji 566 gives Li Xun a fuller Aidi-era disaster-remonstrance role before the later "鼓妖" episode. Asked about earthquake damage, earlier lunar eclipse, floods, and other ominous events, he maps the sun to imperial conduct, the moon to yin-associated powers such as empresses and ministers, water to fairness and political order, and earthquakes to yin affairs losing their proper quietness. The advice is concrete despite its cosmological packaging: strengthen imperial will, keep law, resist private palace influence, restrain outer relatives, select worthy ministers, and end informal requests.

Hanji 579 gives Li Xun a direct court-interpretation role. When a bell-like sound is heard in the palace as Zhu Bo and Zhao Xuan are about to receive appointment documents, Aidi asks Li Xun and Yang Xiong about it. Li Xun identifies the phenomenon as "鼓妖," a formless sound category in which the sign is heard but not physically located. He reads the event as warning that the ruler has admitted improper or empty-name figures into important office, then narrows the timing to an immediate personnel recommendation: dismiss the chancellor and Yushi Dafu.

The episode presents Li Xun as a more institutionally credible bridge than Xia Heliang alone. He is said to understand astronomy and disasters and to have earned influence through effective water-control work. That standing makes his alignment with Gan Zhongke's renewed-mandate method politically consequential: Aidi's search for cure and order reaches Xia through an adviser who already has court weight.

Li Xun later becomes one of the proposed beneficiaries when Xia's group argues that existing ministers cannot understand Heaven's intention. Aidi spares Li from execution but exiles him to Dunhuang with [[JieGuangWesternHan|解光]].

## Key Characteristics

- Combines omen expertise with practical political credibility from water-control success.
- Earlier appears as an omen-literate chancellor's-office adviser who anticipates scapegoat logic around Zhai Fangjin.
- Turns Aidi-era earthquake, eclipse, and flood signs into a systematic diagnosis of imperial conduct, court influence, outer relatives, and minister quality.
- Interprets the "鼓妖" sound as a personnel warning against the new chancellor and Yushi Dafu.
- Functions as the bridge between Aidi's problem-seeking and Xia Heliang's renewed-mandate method.
- Shows how technical prestige can lend court force to unverifiable ritual claims.
- Becomes a proposed governing assistant after the symbolic cure fails.

## Qualifications

Li Xun's full administrative record, the exact nature of his water-control achievement, and how much he personally shaped Xia Heliang's proposals remain source-scoped. Hanji 559 supports his warning and substitution advice, but not the full moral psychology behind it. Hanji 566 supports his disaster interpretation and corrective advice, but not the literal causal claim that earthquake, lunar eclipse, or flood were produced by court disorder. Hanji 579 supports his interpretation of the palace sound and dismissal advice, but not the physical cause of the sound, the full Hongfan tradition, or his private motive.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 566's broad Aidi-era disaster diagnosis, making Li Xun's omen expertise visible as a full political-remonstrance system before the later "鼓妖" personnel attack and Xia Heliang access route.
