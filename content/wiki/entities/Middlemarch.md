# Middlemarch / 《米德尔马契》

## Overview

*Middlemarch* is [[GeorgeEliot|George Eliot's]] novel of provincial English life, read by the bounded source through marriage, vocation, class, religion, and reform around 1829–1832.

## Current Profile

The episode treats the novel's length and slow pace as both obstacle and method. Elections, medical practice, railways, agriculture, estate management, industry, banking, and theological change acquire force through accumulated encounters among ordinary people. Dorothea and Casaubon, Lydgate and Rosamond, Dorothea and Will, and Mary and Fred then test different relations among idealization, compatibility, work, money, responsibility, forgiveness, and agency.

## Key Characteristics

- Builds emotional force through dense ordinary detail rather than rapid dramatic incident.
- Makes provincial households a field for political, economic, medical, religious, and class history.
- Treats failed ideals as products of character, institutions, money, and relationships rather than one villain.
- Distinguishes affection and admiration from practical knowledge of a prospective spouse.
- Gives women meaningful judgment and refusal within historically constrained forms of agency.
- Maps class through land, title, occupation, family history, and the source of wealth.
