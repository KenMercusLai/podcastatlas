# Isabella I of Castile

## Overview

Isabella I of Castile was a Spanish monarch who completed the conquest of Granada, sponsored [[ChristopherColumbus]], backed Caribbean settlement and conversion, and later objected to the killing and enslavement of Indigenous people she regarded as royal subjects and potential Christians.

## Current Profile

[[306-columbus-the-adventure-begins-part-1-glt6089901915]] places Isabella's initial caution and eventual reversal inside the Granada war, crusading ambition, financial pressure, Portuguese rivalry, and [[LuisDeSantangel|Santángel's]] argument that the proposed voyage was inexpensive relative to its possible return.

[[308-columbus-death-in-the-caribbean-part-3-glt3250512968]] presents Isabella as both an architect and a critic of the emerging colonial project. With [[FerdinandIIOfAragon]], she confirmed Columbus's position, sought papal authority, sent colonists, demanded conversion, and protected Spanish claims against Portuguese competition. Her instruction to treat Indigenous people well remained paternal and conversionary, not anticolonial.

Yet the instruction created a real limit that colonial practice violated. Reports of killing, rape, captivity, and enslavement provoked royal alarm; Isabella opposed treating people she claimed as subjects as exportable property. Her position therefore embodies rather than resolves [[ColonialConversionEnslavementContradiction]].

## Key Characteristics

- Royal sponsor of Columbus's voyages and Caribbean settlement.
- Monarch whose court rejected Columbus's geography before accepting a limited strategic gamble.
- Catholic monarch who joined territorial claim to conversion of Indigenous subjects.
- Political actor concerned with Portuguese rivalry and control of distant governors.
- Opponent of at least some Indigenous enslavement within a still-expansionist framework.
- Source of royal intervention when reports of colonial abuse and disorder reached Spain.

## Evidence

- Pre-voyage decision: [[306-columbus-the-adventure-begins-part-1-glt6089901915]] connects Granada's surrender, expert rejection, Santángel's intervention, Portuguese rivalry, and the April 1492 agreement.
- Sponsorship and claim: [[308-columbus-death-in-the-caribbean-part-3-glt3250512968]] connects Isabella to Columbus's confirmed titles, the second voyage, papal backing, settlement, and conversion instructions.
- Protective-subject language: [[308-columbus-death-in-the-caribbean-part-3-glt3250512968]] reports her instruction that Indigenous people be treated well and lovingly.
- Opposition and oversight: [[308-columbus-death-in-the-caribbean-part-3-glt3250512968]] describes her anger over killing, rape, and enslavement and the monarchy's investigation and limitation of Columbus's monopoly.

## Qualifications

The episode does not make Isabella an opponent of conquest or coerced conversion. Royal protection, Christian paternalism, territorial appropriation, and imperial management coexist in her position. Her private motives, the practical reach of her objections, the legal details of subject status, and the balance of moral concern against crown control remain source-scoped.

## Relationships

- [[FerdinandIIOfAragon]] - co-monarch and partner in sponsorship, diplomacy, investigation, and imperial control.
- [[ChristopherColumbus]] - navigator and governor she backed, instructed, and increasingly scrutinized.
- [[TainoPeople]] - Indigenous subjects and potential converts whose mistreatment triggered royal alarm.
- [[ColonialConversionEnslavementContradiction]] - tension her policy made explicit without solving.
- [[ColumbusSecondVoyageColonization]] - enlarged settlement project authorized under the Catholic Monarchs.
- [[LuisDeSantangel]] - treasury official whose low-cost competitive argument helped secure approval.
