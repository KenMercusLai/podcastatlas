# General Post Office, Dublin

## Overview

The General Post Office in Dublin was the headquarters, proclamation site, besieged stronghold, and later commemorative center of the [[EasterRising|Easter Rising]].

## Current Profile

Rebels entered the GPO shortly after noon on Easter Monday 1916, took control, raised the Irish Republic flag and tricolour, and heard [[PatrickPearse]] proclaim a republic outside. The building became the command center for Pearse, [[JamesConnolly]], and other leaders even though control of a symbolically powerful headquarters did not secure Dublin's approaches, communications, castle, or strategically overlooking buildings.

British artillery and incendiary fire made the GPO untenable by Friday. Heat, hunger, fire, and structural collapse forced an evacuation toward Moore Street. Its rubble then became a site of republican return: on Easter Monday 1917 crowds gathered there to declare that the republic had not died. The GPO therefore joins material defeat to durable symbolic legitimacy.

## Key Characteristics

- Central Dublin public building seized as the Rising's headquarters.
- Site where Pearse read the republican proclamation.
- Stronghold flying both the Irish Republic flag and the tricolour.
- Fixed position subjected to artillery and incendiary fire.
- Ruined building that became a commemorative claim on continuing republican legitimacy.

## Evidence

- Seizure and proclamation: [[339-ireland-the-easter-rising-1916-part-4-glt7095481415]] follows the entry, shooting of a sergeant, flag raising, and public declaration.
- Siege and evacuation: [[339-ireland-the-easter-rising-1916-part-4-glt7095481415]] describes bombardment, Sackville Street destruction, and retreat toward Moore Street.
- Commemoration: [[339-ireland-the-easter-rising-1916-part-4-glt7095481415]] records the 1917 gathering at the rubble as an assertion that the republic survived military defeat.

## Qualifications

The GPO's symbolic centrality should not be mistaken for command over the whole rebellion. Other garrisons experienced very different levels of fighting, some remained relatively intact at surrender, and the building's later national meaning was produced through commemoration rather than fixed at the moment of occupation.

## What Changed

- Established the GPO as both an operational headquarters and a post-defeat memory site.
