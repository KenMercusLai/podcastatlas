# Eric Masanet

## Overview
Eric Masanet is the UC Santa Barbara professor cited in [[tech-20261127-1127-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261127-1127-mp-tech-pod-128]] on the difficulty of estimating AI-related emissions from incomplete and inconsistent data-center reporting.

## Current Profile
Masanet's contribution is an evidence-boundary argument. AI uses substantial energy, but researchers cannot turn that fact into a reliable per-query or industry-wide carbon figure when operators do not separate AI from other workloads and use reporting methods that are difficult to compare. He also preserves the possibility that AI may generate wider efficiencies while requiring concrete data before concluding that those benefits will offset infrastructure emissions.

## Key Characteristics

- Studies the energy and emissions implications of AI and data-center activity in the episode's account.
- Uses indirect estimates when operator reporting does not provide AI-specific figures.
- Emphasizes workload attribution and method comparability as separate measurement problems.
- Treats claimed AI-enabled efficiency gains as unresolved rather than inherently false or proven.

## Evidence

- Estimation constraint - [[tech-20261127-1127-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261127-1127-mp-tech-pod-128]] says Masanet estimates AI emissions indirectly because many data-center operators do not report them.
- Attribution boundary - [[tech-20261127-1127-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261127-1127-mp-tech-pod-128]] records his concern that researchers cannot distinguish AI computation from services such as video streaming.
- Comparability boundary - [[tech-20261127-1127-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261127-1127-mp-tech-pod-128]] says company reporting methods vary.
- Net-benefit test - [[tech-20261127-1127-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261127-1127-mp-tech-pod-128]] says Masanet considers it unknown whether AI's claimed efficiencies will materialize or offset data-center and infrastructure emissions.
