# Committee of General Security

## Overview

The Committee of General Security was the policing and internal-security body that operated beside the [[CommitteeOfPublicSafety|Committee of Public Safety]] during the Terror.

## Current Profile

By late 1793, the sources assign the committee responsibility for policing and link it to the [[RevolutionaryTribunal]]. In March 1794 it joined the Committee of Public Safety in approving [[GeorgesDanton|Danton's]] arrest, although two members refused to sign. Rivalry then intensified when Robespierre created an independent police bureau and blocked open prosecution of Catherine Théot after Marc-Guillaume Vadier publicized claims connecting her religious circle to him. The dispute shows that revolutionary repression was distributed across overlapping bodies whose competition could become a struggle for political survival.

## Key Characteristics

- It handled policing and internal security within revolutionary government.
- It operated alongside rather than inside the Committee of Public Safety.
- It was institutionally connected to the Revolutionary Tribunal.
- It participated in the decision to arrest Danton and his allies.
- Internal refusal to sign shows that committee action was not unanimous.
- Its jurisdictional rivalry with Robespierre's police bureau helped turn institutional overlap into open conflict.

## Evidence

### Police and tribunal role

- [[710-the-terror-death-at-the-guillotine-part-4-glt5038102951]] distinguishes the committee's policing and tribunal responsibilities from the policy direction of the Committee of Public Safety.

### Danton arrest

- [[710-the-terror-death-at-the-guillotine-part-4-glt5038102951]] says the two governing committees approved Danton's arrest on 29 March while recording two refusals to sign.

### Police rivalry and Catherine Théot

- [[712-the-terror-the-fall-of-robespierre-part-6-glt1154404105]] connects resentment of Robespierre's police bureau, Vadier's Catherine Théot campaign, and blocked prosecution to near-collapse in relations between the governing committees.

## Qualifications

- The source supplies a focused institutional role, not a complete history of membership, jurisdiction, or rivalry between committees.
- Participation in the arrest decision does not establish identical motives or equal responsibility for every member.
- The exact allocation of control over the tribunal remains source-scoped.
- Alleged forged or coerced evidence in the Théot affair, Vadier's intent, and Robespierre's reasons for intervention remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Extended the profile from shared repression into police-jurisdiction conflict and the Catherine Théot affair.
- Added institutional rivalry as a direct precursor to Thermidor.

## Relationships

- [[CommitteeOfPublicSafety]] - parallel governing body that joined it in approving Danton's arrest.
- [[RevolutionaryTribunal]] - political court linked to its internal-security function.
- [[GeorgesDanton]] - former revolutionary leader whose arrest it approved.
- [[EmergencyInstitutionsToTerror]] - institutional accumulation illustrated by overlapping executive, police, and judicial bodies.
- [[RevolutionaryPurgeSecurityDilemma]] - later fear generated inside the governing apparatus by repeated elite purges.
- [[ThermidorianCoup]] - confrontation accelerated by the committee's rivalry with Robespierre and his allies.
