## Overview

Deng Yu / 邓禹 is synthesized as an Eastern Han founding minister and commander whose current wiki role spans three layers of the founding generation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-690-jiaoyou-bushen-you-duo-kepa-kankan-tianrong-de-jingli-lpbms8pebzdo2ldiwho1cba81njk|Hanji 690]] shows him defeating [[YanCenEasternHan|Yan Cen]] at Shunyang during Jianwu 4, [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-715-shishang-zui-zhihui-de-yici-duoquan-loqkawl247maw9fn-nomaw5gzkgu|Hanji 715]] presents him as a high-merit commander who accepts post-unification withdrawal into honored status, and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-733-1-lishi-suo-yiwang-de-diwang-zhishi-mingjun-liuzhuang-lor7vhzzpwz1rv9tijksogukjqvr|Hanji 733-1]] presents him as the elder founding minister whom [[EmperorMingOfHan|Han Mingdi]] places in the Taifu role at accession.

## Current Profile

Hanji 690 supplies the early field-command layer now visible in the wiki. Yan Cen attacks Shunyang, but Deng Yu defeats him, pushing Yan Cen toward Hanzhong and Gongsun Shu's title system. This makes Deng Yu part of the early military pressure that channels displaced rivals into Shu's orbit before the later Shu campaign.

Hanji 715 supplies the postwar Guangwu layer. After unification, [[LiuXiu|光武帝刘秀]] rewards Deng Yu as Gaomi marquis, and the episode says Deng Yu understands Liu Xiu's desire to set aside warfare and govern through ritual and civilian order. Deng Yu therefore gives up military authority while retaining rank, income, honor, and special court access. The source also uses his household as a restrained elite model: he has thirteen sons, has each learn a skill, maintains strict family discipline, and relies on fief income rather than side business.

Hanji 733-1 frames Deng Yu through Mingdi's first political self-presentation. Mingdi says he is young, lacks a previous emperor to consult, and needs a virtuous elder. Naming Deng Yu as Taifu therefore does more than distribute office: it gives the new reign a visible elder support structure after [[LiuXiu|Guangwu]]'s death.

Deng Yu's death later in Hanji 733-1 closes that immediate accession layer. The page now holds a longer arc: early campaign pressure under Guangwu, postwar withdrawal, then symbolic elder support at Mingdi's accession.

## Key Characteristics

- Early Eastern Han field commander whose defeat of Yan Cen at Shunyang redirects a rival toward Hanzhong and Shu.
- Founding minister rewarded as Gaomi marquis after Liu Xiu completes unification.
- High-merit actor who accepts military authority withdrawal without public disgrace.
- Representative of [[HonorsWithoutOfficeSettlement|honors without office]] through rank, fief income, and limited court access.
- Household-discipline model whose sons are trained in skills and whose estate avoids added commercial accumulation.
- Elder founding minister used to stabilize Mingdi's accession.
- Death under Mingdi marks the thinning of direct Guangwu-era elder support.

## Qualifications

This page still does not reconstruct Deng Yu's full military or ministerial career. Hanji 690 gives one early battlefield result, not a complete campaign biography. Hanji 715 gives a post-unification settlement and household-discipline layer, while Hanji 733-1 gives his late symbolic role in Mingdi's accession. Exact campaign chronology, office history before the settlement, and later family consequences remain outside the current bounded evidence.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 690 as an early military layer: Deng Yu defeats Yan Cen at Shunyang before Yan Cen enters Gongsun Shu's orbit.
- Preserved Hanji 715 as the Guangwu postwar withdrawal and honor-settlement case.
