## Overview

Deng Yu / 邓禹 is synthesized as an Eastern Han founding minister and commander with a mixed arc: an early [[ChimeiRebellion|赤眉]] failure under status pressure, a later Shunyang victory against [[YanCenEasternHan|Yan Cen]], post-unification honored withdrawal, and Mingdi-era elder support. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-688-cong-yongbing-baiwan-dao-kuibuchengjun-dengyu-jingli-le-shenme-lpabdvtmhtrf37puwvgi4c9ajp60|Hanji 688]] now supplies the negative command layer: Deng Yu misreads Chimei movement, pushes hungry troops into repeated attacks, rejects [[FengYiEasternHan|冯异]]'s caution and rest advice, and suffers a rout before [[LiuXiu|光武帝刘秀]] reassigns but does not discard him. Existing later sources then show recovery and different roles: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-690-jiaoyou-bushen-you-duo-kepa-kankan-tianrong-de-jingli-lpbms8pebzdo2ldiwho1cba81njk|Hanji 690]] has him defeating Yan Cen at Shunyang, [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-715-shishang-zui-zhihui-de-yici-duoquan-loqkawl247maw9fn-nomaw5gzkgu|Hanji 715]] has him surrendering command into honors after unification, and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-733-1-lishi-suo-yiwang-de-diwang-zhishi-mingjun-liuzhuang-lor7vhzzpwz1rv9tijksogukjqvr|Hanji 733-1]] has [[EmperorMingOfHan|Han Mingdi]] making him Taifu at accession.

## Current Profile

Hanji 688 backfills Deng Yu's Chimei campaign as a command-failure case. Deng Yu holds major responsibility but lacks matching achievement, and the host reads his behavior through face and status pressure. He interprets Chimei movement as a sign that his pressure is working, attacks with hungry troops, and ignores Feng Yi's advice to induce surrender gradually while Liu Xiu pressures from the east and their army presses from the west.

The failure intensifies when Deng Yu and Deng Hong continue into battle despite exhausted soldiers. Feng Yi advises rest, but Deng Yu insists on fighting. Chimei deception and Han-side hunger compound the problem, and Deng Yu's force is badly beaten; the source says more than 3,000 die and Deng Yu returns to Yiyang with twenty-four cavalry. Deng Yu submits the Da Situ and Lianghou seals, but Liu Xiu removes the Da Situ role, appoints him Right General, and returns the Lianghou seal. The judgment is negative, but it is not terminal exile.

Hanji 690 supplies the later field-recovery layer now visible in the wiki. Yan Cen attacks Shunyang, but Deng Yu defeats him, pushing Yan Cen toward Hanzhong and [[GongsunShuEasternHan|Gongsun Shu]]'s title system. This keeps Deng Yu in the founding-war field after the Chimei rout and prevents the page from collapsing his whole career into one early mistake.

Hanji 715 supplies the postwar Guangwu layer. After unification, Liu Xiu rewards Deng Yu as Gaomi marquis, and the episode says Deng Yu understands Liu Xiu's desire to set aside warfare and govern through ritual and civilian order. Deng Yu therefore gives up military authority while retaining rank, income, honor, and special court access. The source also uses his household as a restrained elite model: he has thirteen sons, has each learn a skill, maintains strict family discipline, and relies on fief income rather than side business.

Hanji 733-1 frames Deng Yu through Mingdi's first political self-presentation. Mingdi says he is young, lacks a previous emperor to consult, and needs a virtuous elder. Naming Deng Yu as Taifu therefore gives the new reign a visible elder support structure after Liu Xiu's death. Deng Yu's later death in the same source closes that immediate accession layer.

## Key Characteristics

- Founding commander whose early Chimei campaign becomes a warning about status-driven attack under poor troop conditions.
- High-responsibility minister whom Liu Xiu demotes from Da Situ after failure but retains through reassignment and restored marquisate seal.
- Early Eastern Han field commander whose later defeat of Yan Cen at Shunyang redirects a rival toward Hanzhong and Shu.
- Founding minister rewarded as Gaomi marquis after Liu Xiu completes unification.
- High-merit actor who accepts military authority withdrawal without public disgrace.
- Household-discipline model whose sons are trained in skills and whose estate avoids added commercial accumulation.
- Elder founding minister used to stabilize Mingdi's accession.

## Qualifications

This page still does not reconstruct Deng Yu's full military or ministerial career. Hanji 688 gives a vivid command-failure layer, but its "face" diagnosis is the host's interpretation of events rather than an independent psychological record. Hanji 690 gives one later battlefield result, not a complete recovery biography. Hanji 715 gives a post-unification settlement and household-discipline layer, while Hanji 733-1 gives his late symbolic role in Mingdi's accession. Exact campaign chronology, office history before the Chimei defeat, and later family consequences remain outside the current bounded evidence.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 688 as Deng Yu's earlier Chimei overreach and rout layer.
- Reframed Deng Yu from a mostly positive three-stage founding profile into a mixed four-stage arc.
- Preserved Hanji 690 as later field success rather than letting the Chimei failure dominate the entire profile.
- Preserved Hanji 715 and Hanji 733-1 as postwar honor and accession-elder layers.
