# Billy Garland

## Overview

Billy Garland was a California real-estate developer and Olympic promoter who secured the 1932 Games for Los Angeles and represented the episode's commercially pragmatic approach to sustaining the Olympic movement.

## Current Profile

[[hitler-s-olympics-part-9-a-plague-on-both-your-houses-hitler-s-olympics-part-9-a-plague-on-both-your-houses|Hitler’s Olympics, Part 9]] follows Garland to the International Olympic Committee's 1923 meeting in Rome, where he sold Los Angeles, the Pacific coast, and nearby Hollywood as the face of an expanding America. His bid succeeded unanimously.

Garland's model joined city promotion to practical delivery. The Coliseum, an athletes' village, Hollywood celebrity support, tourism, investment, and a reported profit made Los Angeles 1932 a successful spectacle after war and pandemic disruption. The episode treats that achievement ambivalently: commercial realism kept the Games viable, but the same techniques supplied Nazi organizers with a model for Berlin while leaving American racial injustice outside the event's moral accounting.

## Key Characteristics

- California real-estate developer who promoted Los Angeles as an Olympic host.
- Secured unanimous approval for the city's 1932 Games bid at the IOC's 1923 Rome meeting.
- Joined sport to Hollywood, tourism, investment, urban promotion, and purpose-built accommodation.
- Helped deliver a reportedly profitable Games amid difficult economic conditions.
- Represents realism that solves institutional delivery while bracketing moral conflict.
