# Lester Bush Jr.

## Overview
Lester Bush Jr. was a physician, former CIA medical director, Mormon historian, and father of [[JaredBush]] who is presented in [[zootopia-exposed-part-two-zootopia-exposed-part-two]] as challenging the church's claimed historical basis for excluding Black members from priesthood ordination.

## Current Profile
After eight years of archival work, Lester reportedly documented Black ordinations under Joseph Smith and located the ban's origin under Brigham Young, reframing it as a historically contingent policy influenced by American racism rather than immutable founding revelation. His 1973 publication is presented as giving the church historical room to reverse the restriction five years later. The episode also stresses institutional cost: attempted suppression, shunning, family withdrawal, and only much later recognition.

## Key Characteristics
- Physician and former CIA medical director who pursued sustained historical research outside his main profession.
- Archival critic of the Mormon priesthood restriction on Black members.
- Reframed an exclusionary rule from foundational doctrine to historically contingent policy.
- Experienced social and institutional costs before receiving delayed acknowledgment.
- Central family precedent in the episode's interpretation of Jared Bush's inclusion themes.

## Evidence
### Historical intervention
- [[zootopia-exposed-part-two-zootopia-exposed-part-two]] says Lester found Black men ordained during Joseph Smith's leadership and traced the restriction to Brigham Young.
- [[zootopia-exposed-part-two-zootopia-exposed-part-two]] dates his publication to 1973 and the church's reversal to five years later.

### Cost and recognition
- [[zootopia-exposed-part-two-zootopia-exposed-part-two]] reports attempts by church leaders to block the essay, later shunning, and withdrawal by Lester and his children.
- [[zootopia-exposed-part-two-zootopia-exposed-part-two]] reports a late honorary lecture and private thanks from a senior church official.

## Qualifications
The episode supplies a strong causal narrative but not Lester's article, archival documents, church deliberations, or evidence sufficient to isolate his influence on the 1978 change. “Provided justification” is therefore retained as the episode's assessment rather than a settled sole-cause claim. The exact scope of opposition, shunning, withdrawal, and later recognition also requires primary verification.

- Distinguished the chronology and relevance of his work from a proven exclusive causal role in church reform.
