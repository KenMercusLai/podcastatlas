# Aurel Popovici

## Overview

Aurel Popovici was a political thinker whose proposed United States of Greater Austria supplied one federal model considered in [[FranzFerdinand|Franz Ferdinand]]'s reform circle.

## Current Profile

The episode associates Popovici with a plan to replace or deeply restructure the [[AustroHungarianDualMonarchy|dual monarchy]] as a federation of sixteen states. The proposal matters less as proof of Franz Ferdinand's final program than as evidence that his Belvedere circle was considering structural alternatives to Austrian-German and Hungarian dominance over the empire's other nationalities.

## Key Characteristics

- Political theorist associated with federal reconstruction of [[AustriaHungary]].
- Proposed a United States of Greater Austria composed of sixteen states.
- Provided one option within Franz Ferdinand's wider but unsettled reform thinking.

## Evidence

- Federal design: [[467-the-murder-of-franz-ferdinand-the-victim-part-3-glt9071060087]] attributes the sixteen-state United States of Greater Austria plan to Popovici.
- Political setting: [[467-the-murder-of-franz-ferdinand-the-victim-part-3-glt9071060087]] places this proposal beside trialism and Hungarian suffrage reform in Franz Ferdinand's search for alternatives to the existing dual structure.

## Qualifications

The source gives only a compact description of Popovici and his plan. It does not establish that Franz Ferdinand had adopted the sixteen-state model as a final program or explain its constitutional details and political feasibility.

## What Changed

- Established Popovici as one source of the federal alternatives circulating around Franz Ferdinand.

## Relationships

- [[FranzFerdinand]] - heir whose reform circle considered Popovici's proposal.
- [[AustriaHungary]] - empire Popovici proposed to reorganize.
- [[AustroHungarianDualMonarchy]] - existing constitutional structure the plan would replace or transform.
- [[HabsburgFederalReform]] - broader reform family to which the sixteen-state proposal belongs.
