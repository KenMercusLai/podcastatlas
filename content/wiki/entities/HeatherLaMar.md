# Heather LaMar

## Overview

Heather LaMar is the communications scholar whom [[the-satire-paradox-the-satire-paradox|The Satire Paradox]] credits with studying how politically different audiences interpreted [[StephenColbertSatiricalPersona|Stephen Colbert's satirical persona]].

## Current Profile

The episode presents LaMar's work as its central empirical challenge to the assumption that shared laughter implies shared political understanding. Liberal viewers generally recognized a liberal comedian parodying a conservative television host, while conservative viewers often treated the performed host as sympathetic to conservatism and hostile to liberals.

LaMar names this pattern motivated cognition or biased perception in the supplied summary. Her role in the episode is to distinguish a remarkable cross-partisan comic achievement from evidence that the performance won a common ideological argument.

## Key Characteristics

- Communications scholar focused here on satire and audience interpretation.
- Uses Colbert's performed ideology to test whether audiences identify the same target.
- Connects divergent reception to motivated cognition or biased perception.
- Separates enjoyment of a performance from agreement about its political meaning.
