# Carrie Sullivan

## Overview

Carrie Sullivan is the linguist whose corpus research the episode uses to test whether the Second Amendment's militia clause could have been independent of its arms clause in [[JamesMadison]]'s English.

## Current Profile

Sullivan examined historical sentences beginning with “being” clauses and a large set of examples from Madison's writings. The episode reports that she found no case in which Madison used that construction to express a thought independent of the adjoining operative clause. Her work supplies the episode's most specific empirical challenge to [[DistrictOfColumbiaVHeller|Heller]]'s treatment of the militia language as prefatory and non-limiting.

## Key Characteristics

- Linguist applying corpus evidence to constitutional interpretation.
- Studies historical syntax rather than relying on modern grammatical intuition.
- Tests Madison's individual usage across many examples.
- Supplies evidence about clause relationship, not a complete account of legal consequence or political motive.

## Evidence

### Historical-corpus analysis

- [[the-invention-of-an-epidemic-the-invention-of-an-epidemic]] reports her analysis of 1,363 “being” sentences, including 976 examples in Madison's writings, with no independent-clause use by Madison.

## Qualifications

The corpus sizes and conclusion are reported by the episode summary; the underlying dataset, coding choices, publication, and scholarly responses were not supplied. A grammatical relationship can constrain interpretation without independently proving why Madison chose the provision or what modern doctrine must be.
