## Overview

费兴 / Fei Xing enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-654-jiemi-xihan-yangxiong-de-chuanqi-rensheng-lq3o-13h2in89qwh0dvchczlvrqk|Hanji 654]] as the Xin official whom [[WangMang|王莽]] appoints to govern Jingzhou and then removes after hearing an unwelcome livelihood diagnosis. His role is narrow but important because it adds an 18 CE example of root-cause advice being punished before later late-Xin crisis episodes.

## Current Profile

The episode says Wang Mang asks Fei Xing how to govern Jingzhou. Fei Xing answers from local livelihood: Jingzhou and Yangzhou people rely on mountains, forests, rivers, and marshes, but the Xin control system taxes those resources and combines with drought to push people into banditry.

Fei Xing's proposed remedy is practical relief rather than more coercion. He suggests letting bandits return home to farm, providing tools, cattle, seed, and grain, and reducing taxes so rural life can restart. Wang Mang becomes angry and removes him, turning Fei Xing into another bounded case of [[SuppressedRootCauseReporting|root-cause reporting suppression]].

## Key Characteristics

- Xin official appointed as Jingzhou governor in the 18 CE setting of Hanji 654.
- Diagnoses banditry through livelihood damage, resource taxation, and drought.
- Proposes agricultural resettlement, relief inputs, and lighter taxes.
- Punished by removal after presenting an unwelcome policy diagnosis.
- Bridges Hanji 653's reform-control layer and Hanji 656's later indirect-remonstrance layer.

## Qualifications

The source does not give a full biography of Fei Xing, his later career, or detailed Jingzhou implementation history. The durable claim is limited: the episode uses him as an official who correctly names livelihood causes and is punished because the diagnosis implicates Wang Mang's own policy design.
