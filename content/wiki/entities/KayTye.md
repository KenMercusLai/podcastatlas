# Kay Tye

## Overview
Kay Tye is the neuroscientist interviewed by [[AndrewHuberman]] in a [[HubermanLab]] episode on emotional valence, social need, hierarchy, empathy, psychedelic research, mentorship, and sustainable academic life.

## Current Profile
In the bounded source, Tye connects projection-specific circuit neuroscience with naturalistic social behavior. She presents the amygdala as a system for assigning motivational significance, interprets dorsal-raphe dopamine activity after isolation through [[SocialHomeostasis]], and distinguishes experimental findings from hypotheses about social media, empathy, hierarchy, and psychedelics. Her scientific profile is paired with a leadership view that values trainee independence, diverse prior experience, candid feedback, and a life supported by several domains of meaning.

## Key Characteristics
- She studies how neural circuits assign positive, negative, and socially relevant meaning.
- Her lab's isolation work frames loneliness as an aversive need state that can motivate social seeking.
- She distinguishes acute isolation from chronic adaptation and possible anti-social re-entry.
- She uses animal behavior, circuit recording, causal manipulation, and hidden-state modeling to study difficult social questions.
- She marks social-media, empathy, rank-development, and psychedelic interpretations as provisional when evidence is incomplete.
- She advocates early trainee ownership and a diversified life beyond work achievement.
