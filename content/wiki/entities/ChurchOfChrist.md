# Church of Christ

## Overview

The Church of Christ is the Christian tradition that forms the religious setting of Part 1 of [[TheAlabamaMurders]], especially in the Shoals region of northwestern Alabama.

## Current Profile

The episode portrays the tradition through insiders rather than as a uniform institution. Its simple worship, a cappella singing, close biblical study, intellectual seriousness, and tight relationships could provide love, belonging, and clarity. The same restored-church certainty and rule-centered discipline could also make divorce, exclusion, public failure, and possible damnation overwhelming. That tension supplies context for [[CharlesSennett]]'s concealment but does not make the church the cause of his conduct.

## Key Characteristics

- Presented as a dense network of congregations and relationships in the Shoals.
- Associated with simple architecture, a cappella worship, biblical study, and recurring devotional language.
- Historically understood by many adherents as the restored New Testament or “true” church.
- Offered intellectual formation, love, discipline, belonging, simplicity, and moral clarity.
- Enforced strict positions on divorce, worship, gender roles, dancing, swimming, dress, and fellowship in the episode's historical account.
- Could turn public correction, disfellowshipping, and fear of damnation into powerful pressures.

### Certainty, discipline, and fear

- [[the-alabama-murders-part-1-the-true-church]] reports restored-church certainty, behavioral restrictions, disfellowshipping, fear of damnation, and insider criticism of an inadequate practical account of grace.

## Qualifications

This profile derives from one episode focused on a 1980s Alabama murder case, not a comprehensive history, doctrinal statement, congregational survey, or current assessment of Churches of Christ. [[LeeCamp]] and [[GrantAsbell]] speak as insiders while also criticizing parts of their tradition. Practices and emphases may differ across periods and congregations. The source does not establish that doctrine caused Sennett's abuse, concealment, mental illness, or later alleged crime.

## What Changed

- Created a bounded profile preserving both the tradition's reported gifts and its reported harms.
- Separated religious and social pressure from any claim that the church caused Sennett's conduct.
