## Overview

桓荣 / Huan Rong enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-734-huanrong-cheng-daru-yinwei-mingdi-shige-hao-penggen-lhwbxrqocf102or9k0nb6z93gzx9|Hanji 734]] as [[EmperorMingOfHan|汉明帝]]'s former `Shangshu` teacher. The source treats him as a learned Eastern Han classicist whose late-life reputation becomes public because Mingdi repeatedly stages teacher respect before officials, students, and the wider court.

## Current Profile

Hanji 734 says Huan Rong was from a farming background and was not recognized by [[LiuXiu|Emperor Guangwu]] until he was already in his sixties. Liu Xiu appointed him `yilang` and brought him into the palace to teach Crown Prince Liu Zhuang, the future Mingdi.

The episode's main profile is not a full intellectual biography. It shows Huan Rong as the beneficiary and object of imperial teacher protocol. In Yongping 2 / 59 CE, Mingdi appoints Li Gong as `sanlao` and Huan Rong as `wugeng` during the Biyong elder-care ceremony, arranges robes, staff, seating, meat-cutting, sauce-offering, and wine rites, and then publicly lectures and discusses classics around Huan Rong and his students.

Mingdi's later conduct deepens that profile. At the Taichang office he seats Huan Rong facing east, gives him a desk and staff, summons officials and hundreds of students, and listens while holding the text. When a student asks Mingdi to explain a point, Mingdi declines because the teacher is present. During Huan Rong's illness, Mingdi sends food and physicians, refuses his request to return title and land, personally walks from the street entrance with the text in hand, cries at the sickbed, and gives burial honors after Huan Rong dies.

The current synthesis is that Huan Rong's standing is produced by the combination of scholarly accumulation and imperial recognition. His learning matters, but the episode emphasizes that Mingdi's repeated public gestures make that learning legible as court authority.

## Key Characteristics

- Eastern Han classicist and former `Shangshu` teacher of the crown prince Liu Zhuang.
- Late-recognized scholar whose palace teaching role begins under Guangwu.
- Publicly honored as `wugeng` in Mingdi's Biyong elder-care ceremony.
- Teacher figure whose authority Mingdi protects through seating, listening, and refusal to answer while the teacher is present.
- Sickbed and funeral beneficiary of unusual imperial respect, medical care, gifts, mourning, and burial grant.
- Example of how classical authority can depend on ruler-backed ritual staging as well as learning.

## Qualifications

The bounded evidence gives Huan Rong's teacher role, ritual elevation, late recognition, illness treatment, and family succession issue, but not his full scholarly works, teaching lineage, or political career. The host's "good supporting actor" reading is preserved as an interpretation of Mingdi's public role in Huan Rong's fame, not as proof that Huan Rong's learning was secondary or manufactured.

## What Changed

- Created Huan Rong as Mingdi's former teacher and a source case for court-backed classical authority.
- Located Huan Rong's reputation at the intersection of scholarship, public ceremony, and imperial example.
