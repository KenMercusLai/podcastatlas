## Overview

桓荣 / Huan Rong appears in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-728-weihe-mingchen-nan-shanzhong-beihou-de-yuanyin-ni-gai-dong-ltjl2bgpyyz1bmwe-4mfcyd9e6cf|Hanji 728]] as one of [[LiuXiu|光武帝刘秀]]'s appointed crown-prince teachers and in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-734-huanrong-cheng-daru-yinwei-mingdi-shige-hao-penggen-lhwbxrqocf102or9k0nb6z93gzx9|Hanji 734]] as [[EmperorMingOfHan|汉明帝]]'s former teacher whose classical authority becomes public through imperial honor ritual.

## Current Profile

Hanji 728 supplies the appointment-and-learning signal. After [[ZhangYiEasternHan|张义]] objects to making the crown prince's teacher a maternal-family appointment, Liu Xiu appoints Zhang Yi as crown-prince taifu and Huan Rong as crown-prince shaofu. Liu Xiu gives both men carriages and horses. Huan Rong gathers students, shows them the imperial gifts and seals, and says his honor comes from studying ancient books, urging them to study.

Hanji 734 then gives the fuller teacher-honor profile. It says Huan Rong was from a farming background and was not recognized by Liu Xiu until he was already in his sixties. Liu Xiu appointed him `yilang` and brought him into the palace to teach Crown Prince Liu Zhuang, the future Mingdi.

The episode's main profile is not a full intellectual biography. It shows Huan Rong as the beneficiary and object of imperial teacher protocol. In Yongping 2 / 59 CE, Mingdi appoints Li Gong as `sanlao` and Huan Rong as `wugeng` during the Biyong elder-care ceremony, arranges robes, staff, seating, meat-cutting, sauce-offering, and wine rites, and then publicly lectures and discusses classics around Huan Rong and his students.

Mingdi's later conduct deepens that profile. At the Taichang office he seats Huan Rong facing east, gives him a desk and staff, summons officials and hundreds of students, and listens while holding the text. When a student asks Mingdi to explain a point, Mingdi declines because the teacher is present. During Huan Rong's illness, Mingdi sends food and physicians, refuses his request to return title and land, personally walks from the street entrance with the text in hand, cries at the sickbed, and gives burial honors after Huan Rong dies.

The current synthesis is that Huan Rong's standing is produced by the combination of scholarly accumulation, crown-prince education, and ruler-backed public recognition. His learning matters, but the episodes emphasize that imperial appointment and repeated public gestures make that learning legible as court authority.

## Key Characteristics

- Eastern Han classicist and former `Shangshu` teacher of the crown prince Liu Zhuang.
- Appointed crown-prince shaofu under Liu Xiu after the tutor decision is redirected toward public talent.
- Uses imperial gifts in Hanji 728 to tell students that study of ancient books produced his honor.
- Late-recognized scholar whose palace teaching role begins under Guangwu.
- Publicly honored as `wugeng` in Mingdi's Biyong elder-care ceremony.
- Teacher figure whose authority Mingdi protects through seating, listening, and refusal to answer while the teacher is present.
- Sickbed and funeral beneficiary of unusual imperial respect, medical care, gifts, mourning, and burial grant.

## Qualifications

The bounded evidence gives Huan Rong's teacher appointment, public honor, late recognition, illness treatment, and family succession issue, but not his full scholarly works, teaching lineage, or political career. The host's "good supporting actor" reading in Hanji 734 is preserved as an interpretation of Mingdi's public role in Huan Rong's fame, not as proof that Huan Rong's learning was secondary or manufactured.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 728 as Huan Rong's earlier crown-prince shaofu appointment and student-facing learning exhortation.
- Reframed Hanji 734's Mingdi honor scenes as later public elevation of an already appointed crown-prince teacher.
- Preserved Huan Rong's reputation as the intersection of scholarship, court appointment, ceremony, and imperial example.
