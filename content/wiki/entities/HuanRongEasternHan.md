## Overview

桓荣 / Huan Rong is synthesized as an Eastern Han classicist whose authority develops through scholarship, crown-prince teaching, and ruler-backed public recognition. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-721-ta-sha-jianu-ma-gongzhu-weihe-fanbei-shang-lryzcejdnrm4-92esnddfvyjkqli|Hanji 721]] now adds his earliest current layer as yilang and crown-prince classics teacher under [[LiuXiu|光武帝刘秀]], before Hanji 728's crown-prince shaofu appointment and Hanji 734's later [[EmperorMingOfHan|Mingdi]] teacher-honor rituals.

## Current Profile

Hanji 721 supplies Huan Rong's earliest current court profile. Liu Xiu appoints him yilang and has him teach Crown Prince Liu Zhuang Confucian classics. In the Taixue discussion scene, Huan Rong is recognized for deep learning and courteous conduct, receives reward, and becomes part of a broader Guangwu personnel lesson about choosing people for character and ability rather than assuming education alone makes them usable.

Hanji 728 supplies the appointment-and-learning signal. After [[ZhangYiEasternHan|张义]] objects to making the crown prince's teacher a maternal-family appointment, Liu Xiu appoints Zhang Yi as crown-prince taifu and Huan Rong as crown-prince shaofu. Liu Xiu gives both men carriages and horses. Huan Rong gathers students, shows them the imperial gifts and seals, and says his honor comes from studying ancient books, urging them to study.

Hanji 734 then gives the fuller teacher-honor profile. It says Huan Rong was from a farming background and was not recognized by Liu Xiu until he was already in his sixties. Liu Xiu appointed him `yilang` and brought him into the palace to teach Crown Prince Liu Zhuang, the future Mingdi, matching and expanding the Hanji 721 teaching layer.

The episode's main profile is not a full intellectual biography. It shows Huan Rong as the beneficiary and object of imperial teacher protocol. In Yongping 2 / 59 CE, Mingdi appoints Li Gong as `sanlao` and Huan Rong as `wugeng` during the Biyong elder-care ceremony, arranges robes, staff, seating, meat-cutting, sauce-offering, and wine rites, and then publicly lectures and discusses classics around Huan Rong and his students.

Mingdi's later conduct deepens that profile. At the Taichang office he seats Huan Rong facing east, gives him a desk and staff, summons officials and hundreds of students, and listens while holding the text. When a student asks Mingdi to explain a point, Mingdi declines because the teacher is present. During Huan Rong's illness, Mingdi sends food and physicians, refuses his request to return title and land, personally walks from the street entrance with the text in hand, cries at the sickbed, and gives burial honors after Huan Rong dies.

The current synthesis is that Huan Rong's standing is produced by the combination of scholarly accumulation, crown-prince education, and ruler-backed public recognition. His learning matters, but the episodes emphasize that imperial appointment and repeated public gestures make that learning legible as court authority.

## Key Characteristics

- Eastern Han classicist and former `Shangshu` teacher of the crown prince Liu Zhuang.
- Appointed yilang under Liu Xiu and selected to teach the crown prince classics.
- Recognized in a Taixue discussion for learning and courteous conduct.
- Appointed crown-prince shaofu under Liu Xiu after the tutor decision is redirected toward public talent.
- Uses imperial gifts in Hanji 728 to tell students that study of ancient books produced his honor.
- Publicly honored as `wugeng` in Mingdi's Biyong elder-care ceremony.
- Teacher figure whose authority Mingdi protects through seating, listening, sickbed care, and funeral honor.

## Qualifications

The bounded evidence gives Huan Rong's teacher appointment, public honor, late recognition, illness treatment, and family succession issue, but not his full scholarly works, teaching lineage, or political career. Hanji 721 and Hanji 734 overlap on Liu Xiu appointing him yilang and teacher to Liu Zhuang; the new source sharpens the Taixue recognition and reward scene rather than overturning the later Mingdi-centered teacher-honor synthesis. The host's "good supporting actor" reading in Hanji 734 is preserved as an interpretation of Mingdi's public role in Huan Rong's fame, not as proof that Huan Rong's learning was secondary or manufactured.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 721 as Huan Rong's earlier yilang, Taixue-recognition, and crown-prince classics-teaching layer.
- Preserved Hanji 728 as Huan Rong's crown-prince shaofu appointment and student-facing learning exhortation.
- Reframed Hanji 734's Mingdi honor scenes as later public elevation of an already appointed crown-prince teacher.
- Preserved Huan Rong's reputation as the intersection of scholarship, court appointment, ceremony, and imperial example.
