## Overview

伯荣 / Bo Rong enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-798-donghan-shiqi-de-baixing-you-duo-ku-loolveumdtv4eo-omubfi5z2p2r5|Hanji 798]] as the palace emissary whose trips between Ganling and the capital become [[ChenZhongLateHan|陈忠]]'s main example of court-proximity power burdening local society under [[EmperorAnOfHan|汉安帝]].

## Current Profile

The source does not give Bo Rong an independent biography. It uses him as the visible carrier of an institutional problem: because he travels as a palace-linked emissary, commanderies and counties treat his passage as politically dangerous. The source says princes, marquises, and senior officials bow before him, while local administrations mobilize roads, stations, supplies, gifts, and labor for his entourage.

Chen Zhong's criticism makes Bo Rong more than a corrupt individual. The memorial argues that if Emperor An does not correct the over-honoring of such emissaries, outsiders will read the behavior as the emperor's will. Bo Rong therefore functions as the concrete case behind [[PalaceEnvoyLocalExtraction|宫使扰民式地方抽取]]: ambiguous imperial proximity turns into real extraction from commoners.

## Key Characteristics

- Palace emissary whose authority derives from proximity to Emperor An's court.
- Trigger case for Chen Zhong's critique of excessive ritual reception and local burden.
- Figure through whom officials' fear of guessed imperial intent becomes visible.
- Source-bounded actor currently known only through the Ganling mission controversy.

## Qualifications

Bo Rong is source-bounded to Hanji 798. The page does not settle his office, family background, later career, or personal intent. The current claim is narrower: the source uses his passage to show how palace emissary status can make local officials over-mobilize people and resources.
