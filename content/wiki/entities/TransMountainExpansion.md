# Trans Mountain Expansion

## Overview
Trans Mountain Expansion is represented in the wiki as the Canadian pipeline-capacity project that came online just as oil-sands producers benefited from stronger global demand and trade uncertainty.

## Current Profile
The episode treats the Trans Mountain Expansion as an infrastructure timing case. Pipeline construction in Canada is described as difficult because environmental policy, Indigenous consultations, and jurisdictional disputes slow projects. Kinder Morgan abandoned the expansion in 2018, after which Justin Trudeau's government bought the pipeline for $4.5 billion and spent a further $34 billion to get it operating.

The project came online in 2024. In the episode's synthesis, that timing matters because [[Canada]] wanted greater energy independence from the [[UnitedStates]], producers had stronger price incentives, and [[MarkCarney]] later opened [[MajorProjectsOfficeCanada]] to speed nationally important infrastructure approvals.

## Key Characteristics
- Pipeline-capacity project used to explain why Canadian oil producers had more export optionality.
- Shows how infrastructure delays can become economically important when market conditions change.
- Connects oil-sands production to federal ownership, environmental consultation, and jurisdictional politics.
- Functions as a precedent for later Canadian project-approval acceleration arguments.
