# Cleomenes I

## Overview

Cleomenes I was a Spartan king presented as the earliest major Spartan political personality recoverable through [[Herodotus]], combining forceful intervention abroad with disputed conduct and a violent death at home.

## Current Profile

The episode places Cleomenes at the intersection of Spartan kingship and the emergence of [[AthenianDemocracy]]. He helped drive tyrants from [[Athens]] but then opposed the new democratic settlement, illustrating that Spartan intervention could be framed as liberation without accepting popular government.

His daughter [[Gorgo]] appears as a perceptive counselor who warns him against an Ionian attempt to purchase Spartan participation in revolt against Persia. Cleomenes later crushes Argos and burns a sacred grove, before reportedly becoming mad, being confined, and dying through self-mutilation.

The account does not resolve that death. The hosts preserve the possibility that he was murdered and note that his half-brother [[Leonidas]] benefited from the succession, but they do not present suspicion as established fact.

## Key Characteristics

- Spartan king known chiefly through an external Greek narrative tradition.
- Intervened against Athenian tyranny before resisting the democratic order that followed.
- Rejected an Ionian appeal after Gorgo warned him about escalating bribery.
- Defeated Argos in a campaign associated with sacrilege and severe violence.
- Died in custody under circumstances remembered as madness, suicide, or possible murder.

## Evidence

- Athenian intervention: [[82-sparta-glt1755395266]] connects Cleomenes to the removal of tyrants and a later attempt to block democracy.
- Royal counsel: [[82-sparta-glt1755395266]] recounts Gorgo's warning during an Ionian appeal for Spartan aid.
- Argive campaign and death: [[82-sparta-glt1755395266]] describes the sacred-grove burning, confinement, violent death, and murder speculation.

## Qualifications

This profile depends on one episode's compressed use of Herodotean tradition. Motives, chronology, the Argive campaign, the diagnosis of madness, the mechanics of death, and any role for Leonidas remain uncertain or literary. Succession benefit is not evidence of murder.

## What Changed

- Established a bounded profile that keeps political action separate from speculation about Cleomenes's death.

## Relationships

- [[Sparta]] - polity he ruled as one of its hereditary kings.
- [[Gorgo]] - daughter remembered as a politically perceptive adviser.
- [[Leonidas]] - half-brother who succeeded him and benefited from the succession.
- [[Athens]] - city where his interventions first opposed tyranny and then democracy.
- [[Herodotus]] - principal surviving narrative channel for his profile.
- [[AthenianDemocracy]] - political settlement his later intervention attempted to prevent.
