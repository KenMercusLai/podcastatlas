## Overview

Li Zibiao was a Chinese Christian educated in Europe who served as an interpreter for the [[MacartneyEmbassy|Macartney Embassy]].

## Current Profile

The episode describes Li as a Gansu-born student who learned Latin and Italian in Europe and became the only one of four returning Chinese clerics willing to remain with the British mission. Because he did not know English, communication still required a relay rather than direct English-Chinese interpretation.

His difficulties show that multilingual ability is not identical to diplomatic fluency. The source says he lacked familiarity with Qing official prose and court implication, struggled to explain a warship model to [[QianlongEmperor|乾隆]], and rendered an indirect suggestion that the embassy depart as an invitation to stay. An attempt to dress as a British soldier did not erase his Qing identity or vulnerability: a senior official reportedly recognized him and invoked the safety of his family.

Li's role is therefore neither that of a transparent conduit nor an all-powerful manipulator. His language repertoire made communication possible, while court conventions, technical vocabulary, identity, coercive exposure, and institutional pressure constrained what he could understand and safely say.

## Key Characteristics

- Chinese Christian with Latin and Italian training acquired through European study.
- Relay interpreter rather than a direct English-Chinese translator.
- Linguistically capable intermediary with limited familiarity with Qing diplomatic and bureaucratic language.
- Technical interpreter challenged by specialized models and scientific explanation.
- Politically vulnerable subject whose family ties could be used as leverage.

## Qualifications

This profile comes from one episode. Li's precise education, clerical status, language proficiency, motivations, exact translations, clothing episode, family threat, and responsibility for specific misunderstandings remain episode-attributed. The profile should not make him solely responsible for institutional incompatibility between the British mission and Qing court.

## What Changed

- Established a bounded profile centered on relay interpretation and court-language competence.
- Added political vulnerability and technical explanation as constraints on diplomatic translation.
- Distinguished individual mistakes from the larger incompatibility of diplomatic systems.
