# Lancelot

## Overview

Lancelot is the Arthurian knight whose supreme prowess is bound to service of [[Guinevere]], making him both an exemplar of courtly chivalry and an adulterer whose love helps destroy Camelot.

## Current Profile

[[714-king-arthur-lancelot-and-the-round-table-part-2-glt6459272632]] presents [[ChretienDeTroyes|Chrétien de Troyes]] as the first known author to name Lancelot. In *The Knight of the Cart*, Lancelot accepts pain, public shame, and Guinevere's commands; his momentary hesitation before entering a criminal's cart establishes that loving submission costs him real knightly honor. The romance thereby makes devotion a source of martial excellence rather than its opposite.

[[ThomasMalory|Malory]] preserves the contradiction rather than resolving it. Lancelot is merciful, loyal, courteous, and capable of grace, yet his affair enables the exposure and civil conflict that break the Round Table. His accidental killing of Gareth and Gaheris turns private love into political catastrophe, while repentance and the healing of Sir Urry prevent sin from exhausting his moral identity.

## Key Characteristics

- Earliest known literary appearance in Chrétien's *Knight of the Cart*.
- Knight whose martial excellence is represented as strengthened by service to Guinevere.
- Courtly lover willing to exchange public honor for obedience and rescue.
- Father and moral counterpoint of the perfectly chaste [[Galahad]].
- Malorian ideal of mercy, loyalty, courtesy, repentance, and divine grace.
- Adulterer whose relationship helps fracture Arthur's fellowship.

- [[714-king-arthur-lancelot-and-the-round-table-part-2-glt6459272632]] follows the affair's exposure, Guinevere's rescue, the accidental deaths of Gareth and Gaheris, and Gawain's vengeance into Camelot's collapse.

- [[714-king-arthur-lancelot-and-the-round-table-part-2-glt6459272632]] uses Sir Urry's healing, Lancelot's tears, priesthood, peaceful death, and Ector's tribute to preserve both guilt and exemplary stature.

## Qualifications

The page describes a changing literary character, not a historical knight or a single stable medieval text. Chrétien's precise degree of invention, the name's origin, direct influence on real knightly conduct, and Malory's theological or autobiographical intentions remain source-scoped. Calling Lancelot a moral center does not excuse adultery or erase the deaths and civil conflict that follow it.

## What Changed

- Established Lancelot as a durable chivalric ideal whose loving service, sin, consequence, repentance, and grace cannot be separated.

## Relationships

- [[Guinevere]] - beloved queen whose service ennobles him and whose affair with him destabilizes Camelot.
- [[ChretienDeTroyes]] - earliest known author to name and define him as the knight of loving submission.
- [[Galahad]] - chaste son who revises and surpasses his father's sacred qualifications.
- [[ThomasMalory]] - author who makes Lancelot's virtue and destructive sin central to Arthurian tragedy.
- [[CourtlyLoveChivalricIdentity]] - cultural synthesis his humiliation and prowess most clearly embody.
- [[RoundTableFellowship]] - fellowship he exemplifies, divides, and survives.
