# 王绾 / Wang Wan (Qin)

王绾 / Wang Wan (Qin) enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-89-qinwang-jing-bei-ta-dui-mabaonan-ljugedx8qxhtuzd1ja-xiwxgse19]] as the likely identity behind the episode's "武大夫绾" notice. In 268 BCE, [[QinZhaoxiangwang|秦昭襄王]] sends Wudafu Wan to attack [[WeiState|魏国]] and take Huai.

The host suggests this figure may be the later Qin minister Wang Wan, implying a possible career path from mid-ranking military command to higher civil office. The page records that as a source-scoped inference; it does not merge him with the existing modern author page [[WangWan|王婉 / Wang Wan]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-120-2-qinchao-di-yi-tao-vi-sheji-linian-lno-aeljks2vt1k3wjrizsanirtq]] adds the clearer later-minister role. In the episode's post-unification discussion, Wang Wan appears with [[LiSi|李斯]] in the court deliberation over using "朕" as [[QinShiHuang|嬴政 / 秦始皇]]'s exclusive self-reference. His source role therefore shifts from a possible earlier campaign notice to a named participant in Qin's imperial-language standardization.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-120-3-jiemi-qinshihuang-weihe-faming-junxianzhi-li4bzz54trstteapodnonxzdzkuv]] makes Wang Wan the advocate for limited post-unification enfeoffment. As chancellor, he argues that former [[YanState|燕国]], [[QiState|齐国]], and [[ChuState|楚国]] territory is too distant from [[Xianyang|咸阳]] to hold securely without placing imperial sons there. The page records the argument as a serious distance-and-control proposal, even though the source ultimately sides with Li Si's [[CommanderyCountyAntiEnfeoffment|anti-enfeoffment]] answer.
