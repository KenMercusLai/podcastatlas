## Overview

钟瑾 / Zhong Jin enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-832-wei-lingdao-beiguo-ta-shi-sha-haishi-zhi-lmfpuixmi4odrf6lu31rf6-66-lq|Hanji 832]] as [[ZhongHaoYingchuanLateHan|钟皓]]'s nephew and a reputation peer of [[LiYingLateHan|李膺]]. The source uses him to contrast Li Ying's forceful right-wrong judgment with Zhong Hao's counsel that ambiguity, humility, and self-protection may be wiser in a dangerous age.

## Current Profile

Zhong Jin is introduced as book-loving, ancient-minded, modest, and naturally inclined toward yielding. His family position connects him to both Zhong Hao and Li Ying: the source says his mother is Li Ying's aunt, and Zhong Jin and Li Ying become locally famous for talent and conduct.

The interpretive center is the disagreement over judgment. Li Ying cites the Mencian idea that a person without a sense of right and wrong is not fully human, then criticizes Zhong Jin for failing to distinguish clear moral lines. Zhong Jin feels wronged. Zhong Hao later explains that Li Ying's family power gives him confidence to speak sharply, while Zhong Jin's restraint may better protect him in a world where absolute black and white are difficult and dangerous to assert.

## Key Characteristics

- Late-Han Yingchuan-adjacent reputation figure linked by kinship to Zhong Hao and Li Ying.
- Portrayed as book-loving, modest, and inclined toward deference.
- Serves as the episode's case for prudent ambiguity rather than sharp public judgment.
- Functions as a foil to Li Ying's Mencian right-wrong clarity.
- Receives Zhong Hao's counsel that self-protection can be wisdom in a dangerous political environment.

## Qualifications

The page is source-bounded to Hanji 832 and does not yet establish Zhong Jin's full career, exact family genealogy beyond the episode, or later political conduct. The transcript form 钟谨 is normalized here to 钟瑾 because the late-Han Zhong Hao and Li Ying context supports that received identity.
