# House Select Committee on Assassinations

## Overview

The House Select Committee on Assassinations was the later U.S. congressional investigation that revisited the Kennedy assassination and reported in 1979 that acoustic evidence suggested a possible conspiracy.

## Current Profile

The episode presents the committee as a qualified official reopening rather than a wholesale rejection of the [[WarrenCommission|Warren Commission]]. It praised much of the earlier inquiry and did not find the CIA, KGB, mafia, or comparable organizations complicit, but its interpretation of a Dallas police dictabelt appeared to indicate shots from more than one direction.

That narrow acoustic claim had an outsized afterlife because it let conspiracy suspicion acquire official language. The hosts say later specialists discredited the analysis, making the committee a case in how a technically fragile finding can survive culturally after the evidentiary basis weakens.

## Key Characteristics

- Congressional reinvestigation convened in the 1970s.
- Reported in 1979 on the Kennedy assassination.
- Relied partly on disputed dictabelt acoustic evidence.
- Suggested a possible conspiracy while rejecting major named institutions as participants.
- Simultaneously praised and qualified the Warren Commission.

## Qualifications

This page records one podcast's compressed account rather than the committee's complete mandate, hearings, membership, findings, or later scientific reviews. “Debunked” is the hosts' summary and is not independently demonstrated here. A failed acoustic inference would weaken that particular basis for multiple shooters without validating every other official conclusion.

## What Changed

- Added the later congressional inquiry that qualified the Warren Commission's public legacy.
- Isolated the dictabelt finding from broader claims about institutional complicity.
