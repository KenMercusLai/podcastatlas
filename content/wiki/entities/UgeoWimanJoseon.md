## Overview

卫右渠 / King Ugeo is the grandson of [[WimanJoseonFounder|卫满]] and the ruler of [[WimanJoseon|卫氏朝鲜]] during its final conflict with Western Han. His inherited position combines regional strength with control over neighboring rulers' court access before diplomacy collapses into retaliation, war, aborted submission, and internal fracture.

## Current Profile

Hanji 364-2 says Ugeo inherits rule through Wiman's son after the polity has absorbed nearby groups and attracted more Han migrants. He does not attend the Han court and blocks neighboring small states from sending petitions or appearing there. When [[SheHeWesternHan|涉何]] is sent to reopen compliance, Ugeo refuses.

Hanji 366-1 continues the sequence: She He kills a Joseon escort officer, and Joseon retaliates by killing the envoy in Liaodong. Ugeo then defeats [[YangPuWesternHan|杨仆]]'s early force and resists Xun Zhi at the Pei River. Under renewed military pressure he offers submission, horses, provisions, and his son, but the prince turns back when the Han side demands disarmament.

Ugeo then holds [[WangxianCity|王险城]] while the divided Han command delays decisive action. Hanji 366-2 says that once Xun Zhi receives the combined force and intensifies the attack, several senior officials defect but Ugeo still refuses surrender. The administrator Sam has him killed and submits to Han. Resistance continues under Seong Gi until Ugeo's son and another defector's son help induce the city's final submission.

## Key Characteristics

- Final Wiman Joseon king who inherits Wiman's enlarged and Han-recognized position before facing escalation, negotiation, siege, and collapse.
- Intermediary ruler who blocks neighboring polities' direct access to the Han court.
- Ruler who offers submission after initial resistance but does not complete it after the crown prince's security fears.
- Defender whose position later benefits from division between Han commanders.
- Ruler who refuses surrender after senior officials judge the war unwinnable.
- King killed through internal elite defection rather than direct capture in battle.
- Political center whose death does not by itself end all resistance.

## Qualifications

This is a source-bounded profile, not a complete biography or a full account of Wiman Joseon's institutions. The sources give little access to Ugeo's reasoning, the identities and motives of the blocked small states, or his internal coalition, and the failed submission does not prove settled trust or a durable political agreement. Names and titles are normalized to received-text forms where the summaries diverge.

## What Changed

- Added Ugeo's dynastic inheritance and court-access blockade as the political prehistory of She He's mission.
- Reframed the campaign opening as the escalation of an older frontier-hierarchy dispute.
