## Overview

皇后张嫣 / Zhang Yan is presented as [[LuYuanPrincess|鲁元公主]]'s daughter, [[LiuYing|汉惠帝刘盈]]'s niece and empress, and the assigned mother of [[LiuGongYoungEmperorWesternHan|前少帝刘恭]] during [[LuZhi|吕雉]]'s regency.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-198-jiu-qu-waishengnv-zhe-wei-huangdi-shenshang-shier-bu-jiandan-lkgewcb8avfq_qy0jpqkdfm5t6wi|Hanji 198]] moves Zhang Yan backward into the marriage arrangement. It says Lu Zhi has the adult Liu Ying marry his eleven-year-old niece Zhang Yan, that Liu Ying never consummates the marriage, and that Zhang Yan is later found to have remained a virgin. These intimate claims are source-scoped, but the kinship structure makes her empresshood part of Lu Zhi's attempt to keep household and succession control inside the same family network.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-200-lishishang-diyici-tonghuopengzhang-lgcmvklcdkv-mtxv_bei2wclvuqw|Hanji 200]] and Hanji 198 then place Zhang Yan inside Liu Gong's concealed-parentage crisis. The young emperor is reportedly assigned to her as a son after his birth mother's death, and his later discovery that Zhang Yan was not his biological mother helps trigger his threat of revenge and removal. Neither source establishes Zhang Yan's consent, knowledge, authority, or responsibility for the birth mother's death.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han empress placed inside a regency-controlled succession arrangement.
- Niece of Liu Ying and granddaughter of Lu Zhi in the reported kin marriage.
- Public mother of Liu Gong in the episode's account.
- Not Liu Gong's biological mother according to the same source.
- Figure whose personal agency is not established by the supplied evidence.

## Evidence

- Marriage and kinship: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-198-jiu-qu-waishengnv-zhe-wei-huangdi-shenshang-shier-bu-jiandan-lkgewcb8avfq_qy0jpqkdfm5t6wi|Hanji 198]] identifies Zhang Yan as Lu Yuan Princess's daughter and says Lu Zhi arranges her marriage to her maternal uncle Liu Ying.
- Maternal role and identity boundary: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-200-lishishang-diyici-tonghuopengzhang-lgcmvklcdkv-mtxv_bei2wclvuqw|Hanji 200]] says Liu Gong was treated as Zhang Yan's son but later learned that she was not his biological mother.
- Assigned motherhood before Liu Gong's accession: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-198-jiu-qu-waishengnv-zhe-wei-huangdi-shenshang-shier-bu-jiandan-lkgewcb8avfq_qy0jpqkdfm5t6wi|Hanji 198]] links Zhang Yan's public motherhood to Lu Zhi's post-Hui succession arrangement.
- Succession consequence: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-200-lishishang-diyici-tonghuopengzhang-lgcmvklcdkv-mtxv_bei2wclvuqw|Hanji 200]] places that discovery within the chain leading to Liu Gong's threat, confinement, deposition, and death.

## Qualifications

The sources do not independently establish Zhang Yan's exact age, consent, intimate marital history, reported virginity, biological motherhood, role in selecting Liu Gong, knowledge of his birth mother's fate, authority under Lu Zhi, response to his discovery, or later life. They support a kin marriage and public maternal role in a succession narrative, not a judgment of Zhang Yan's responsibility.

## What Changed

- Extended Zhang Yan from assigned motherhood into the reported niece marriage that precedes it.
- Preserved the distinction between her formal roles and unsupported claims about consent, intimacy, knowledge, or responsibility.

## Relationships
