## Overview

皇后张嫣 / Zhang Yan enters the bounded evidence as the empress publicly presented as [[LiuGongYoungEmperorWesternHan|前少帝刘恭]]'s mother during [[LuZhi|吕雉]]'s regency, although the episode says she was not his biological mother.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-200-lishishang-diyici-tonghuopengzhang-lgcmvklcdkv-mtxv_bei2wclvuqw|Hanji 200]] uses Zhang Yan only inside Liu Gong's concealed-parentage crisis. The young emperor is reportedly assigned to her as a son after his birth mother's death, and his later discovery that Zhang Yan was not his biological mother helps trigger his threat of revenge and removal. The source does not describe Zhang Yan's consent, knowledge, conduct, or wider biography.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han empress placed inside a regency-controlled succession arrangement.
- Public mother of Liu Gong in the episode's account.
- Not Liu Gong's biological mother according to the same source.
- Figure whose personal agency is not established by the supplied evidence.

## Evidence

- Maternal role and identity boundary: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-200-lishishang-diyici-tonghuopengzhang-lgcmvklcdkv-mtxv_bei2wclvuqw|Hanji 200]] says Liu Gong was treated as Zhang Yan's son but later learned that she was not his biological mother.
- Succession consequence: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-200-lishishang-diyici-tonghuopengzhang-lgcmvklcdkv-mtxv_bei2wclvuqw|Hanji 200]] places that discovery within the chain leading to Liu Gong's threat, confinement, deposition, and death.

## Qualifications

This one-source profile does not establish Zhang Yan's age, marriage history, biological motherhood, role in selecting Liu Gong, knowledge of his birth mother's fate, authority under Lu Zhi, response to his discovery, or later life. The source supports a public maternal role in a succession narrative, not a judgment of Zhang Yan's responsibility.

## What Changed

- Created a bounded profile separating Zhang Yan's assigned maternal status from unsupported claims about her agency or responsibility.
