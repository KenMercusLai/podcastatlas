## Overview

Hako X is an Android emulator for the original [[Xbox]], presented in the bounded episode as a striking but still incomplete example of AI-era emulator development.

## Current Profile

The episode reports that version 1.2 is optimized for Mali GPUs and can run selected games smoothly on lower-powered hardware. Its significance is comparative rather than universal: good results for two tested Xbox games coexist with compatibility and graphics defects, and the episode does not provide a systematic benchmark or independent account of how AI was used in development.

## Key Characteristics

- Targets original Xbox software on Android devices.
- Reportedly includes Mali GPU optimization in version 1.2.
- Runs selected titles well on the [[PocketAirMini]] in the host's tests.
- Retains visible compatibility and rendering problems.
- Is presented as suggestive, not conclusive, evidence of [[AIAssistedGameReverseEngineering|AI-assisted reverse engineering]].

## Evidence

- Performance profile: [[gkwrijiovcomarlvvwt0f8r9-gkwrijiovcomarlvvwt0f8r9]] reports smooth play for Ninja Gaiden Black and Panzer Dragoon Orta on Pocket Air Mini.
- Remaining limits: [[gkwrijiovcomarlvvwt0f8r9-gkwrijiovcomarlvvwt0f8r9]] explicitly notes compatibility and image defects despite the strong result.
- Development interpretation: [[gkwrijiovcomarlvvwt0f8r9-gkwrijiovcomarlvvwt0f8r9]] says the host saw extensive signs of AI involvement in project documentation, without establishing a verified development history.

## Qualifications

All technical and provenance claims come from one episode summary. Two favorable game tests do not establish broad compatibility, and apparent traces in documentation do not quantify AI's contribution or identify which components it affected.
