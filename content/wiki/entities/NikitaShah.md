# Nikita Shah

[[NikitaShah]] appears in [[tech-20260819-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260819-mp-tech-pod-128]] as a cybersecurity analyst at the [[CenterForStrategicAndInternationalStudies|Center for Strategic and International Studies]] explaining cyberattacks on U.S. water systems. Her contribution is to turn the incident into a broader [[IndustrialControlSystemCyberRisk]] and [[WaterSystemCyberResilience]] case rather than a narrow report about one utility sector.

Shah emphasizes that many failures are basic: default passwords, missing multi-factor authentication, and internet-exposed operational technology can create avoidable exposure. She also connects water security to [[StateCyberActorThreatModel]], [[AICyberDefenseUtility]], [[ElectionInformationOperations]], and [[PrivateSectorOffensiveCyberAuthority]], making the episode a bridge between local utility operations and national cyber policy.

## Key Claims
- Attribution should stay cautious when a group such as [[CyberAvengers]] claims responsibility before official U.S. assessment.
- Infrastructure resilience depends on money, technical personnel, basic controls, and the ability to operate manually after an attack.
- AI can help defenders discover vulnerabilities first, but it can also help threat actors improve phishing, sorting, scripting, and future exploitation.
- Election cyber threats often aim to create distrust and division through information operations rather than only direct vote manipulation.
