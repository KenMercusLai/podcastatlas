# 《孔雀翎》 / The Peacock Feather

## Overview

《孔雀翎》 is the second story in [[GuLong|古龙]]'s 《七种武器》 sequence and the work discussed in [[121-quhua-qizhongwuqi-zhi-kongqueling-gulong-xiegei-chengnianrende-xinxin-yuyan-lixp1oc4wx0wiksvzo4zehqj45b4]]. It follows killers 高立 and the disguised Peacock Manor heir 秋凤梧 through betrayal of their organization, friendship, love, pursuit, and the secret that the manor's invincible weapon was lost long ago.

## Current Profile

The source reads the story as a confidence allegory complicated by moral cost. 高立 believes he carries the Peacock Feather and therefore defeats 麻锋 without using it; the borrowed object is later revealed as a counterfeit, while the real device survives chiefly as a reputation protecting the manor. The same secret makes 秋凤梧's friendship with 高立 incompatible with his claimed duty to hundreds of dependents, producing an ending that strongly implies murder without narrating it directly.

The episode values the reciprocal love between 高立 and 双双, but questions whether exaggerated physical description instrumentalizes 双双 for reversal. It likewise treats the story's theme as powerful while judging several causal steps underprepared.

## Key Characteristics

- Replaces the titular physical weapon with [[ConfidenceAsWeapon|confidence]] as the effective source of victory.
- Uses the Peacock Feather's reputation both to empower its supposed holder and to deter attacks on a community.
- Builds its emotional center around [[ReciprocalCareAndDignity|mutual recognition and care]] between 高立 and 双双.
- Turns friendship into a conflict with inherited responsibility and institutional secrecy.
- Relies on concealed identity, counterfeit objects, delayed revelation, and an implied rather than explicit death.
- Leaves a gap between thematic clarity and the hosts' judgment of causal plausibility.

## Qualifications

This page records one podcast's reading rather than an independent textual edition or publication history. The death of 高立 is presented as strongly implied, not explicitly narrated. The theft and secret-identity theories about 双双 are reader speculation that the source itself rejects as ungrounded. Judgments about disability representation, friendship, and plot credibility are interpretations on which the hosts disagree.
