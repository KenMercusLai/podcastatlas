## Overview

越繇王丑 / Chou is the uninvolved descendant of [[MinyueKingYing|闽越王郢]] whom [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] recognizes after Ying's death to continue the Minyue ancestral sacrifices.

## Current Profile

Hanji 256 gives Chou a legitimacy role rather than a military one. After [[YuShanDongyueKing|余善]] kills Ying and the Han army withdraws, Han Wudi does not allow the killer to inherit the whole settlement automatically. He instead recognizes Chou, described as Ying's descendant and uninvolved in the offense, as Yueyao king responsible for the ancestral rites.

Recognition does not give Chou uncontested effective control. Yu Shan's local威势 attracts followers and prevents Chou from governing alone. Han Wudi then recognizes Yu Shan as Dongyue king rather than launch another costly expedition, leaving Chou's inherited and ritual legitimacy beside Yu Shan's practical power.

## Key Characteristics

- Descendant of Ying whom the episode treats as uninvolved in the Nanyue conflict.
- Han-recognized Yueyao king with responsibility for ancestral sacrifice.
- Holder of inherited and ritual legitimacy after a violent succession crisis.
- Ruler unable to displace Yu Shan's local following and effective authority.
- One side of a dual recognition settlement rather than sole successor.

## Qualifications

The transcript gives “洛丑” and a title rendered “岳瑶王.” This page cautiously normalizes the figure as 丑 and the title as 越繇王 from the recognizable event sequence. Exact genealogy, title wording, territory, residence, governing authority, ritual duties, and later fate require primary-text verification. A balancing effect is visible in the outcome, but deliberate imperial counterweight design remains an inference.
