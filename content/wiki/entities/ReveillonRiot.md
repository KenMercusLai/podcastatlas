# Reveillon Riot

## Overview

The Réveillon riot was the 28 April 1789 attack on wallpaper manufacturer Jean-Baptiste Réveillon's Paris house and factory after a false wage rumor fused with unemployment, food anxiety, and political tension.

## Current Profile

The source treats the riot as evidence that real deprivation did not require an accurate immediate accusation to become explosive. Réveillon was presented as a comparatively well-paying and paternalistic employer who had supported workers during the winter, but a claim that he wanted wages reduced to fifteen sous drew a crowd that destroyed his property. Guard fire then caused a large but uncertain death toll and the city became more visibly militarized before the Estates-General opened.

## Key Characteristics

- Triggered by a false or distorted statement about subsistence wages.
- Occurred amid high bread prices, severe unemployment, and winter hardship.
- Targeted an employer whom the source describes as comparatively paternalistic.
- Ended in lethal confrontation with guards.
- Encouraged troop patrols and fear of foreign soldiers in Paris.
- Foreshadowed the interaction of rumor, deprivation, repression, and street politics.

## Qualifications

- The reported death toll of around 300 is presented as an estimate and remains source-scoped.
- Correcting the rumor does not make the crowd's material distress unreal or settle individual motives and responsibility.

## What Changed

- Created the profile as the pre-Estates-General example of scarcity and false attribution producing lethal political mobilization.
