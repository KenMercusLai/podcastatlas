## Overview

范生 / Fan Sheng enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-656-zhichang-buhui-shuohua-de-ren-hen-nan-hunchutou-lpn15smz7r1glekugzwtm2tpw-tl|Hanji 656]] as a late-[[XinDynasty|新朝]] official from Dai Commandery who tries to warn the court about domestic crisis without directly confronting [[WangMang|王莽]]. His role is narrow: he writes a petition to the Grand Minister over Works [[WangYiXinGeneral|王邑 / 王毅]], frames the warning in loyal language, and asks for a chance to speak in person.

## Current Profile

Fan Sheng's importance is the combination of content and route. The content is severe: he says disorder is already obvious, criticizes mistimed spring labor mobilization, points to hunger, abandoned fields, high grain prices, and rebellion, and argues that popular resentment inside the realm is more dangerous than border trouble. That makes him a source case for [[RootCauseRemonstrance|根源诊断式进谏]].

The route is cautious. Fan Sheng does not submit the warning straight to Wang Mang; he sends it through Wang Yi, using loyalist preface language before turning to criticism. The petition fails because Wang Yi ignores it, but the episode contrasts that outcome with harsher punishments faced by more direct critics. Fan Sheng therefore becomes a limited case of [[ObliqueRemonstranceSelfProtection|迂回进谏自保]]: self-protection succeeds better than policy correction.

## Key Characteristics

- Late-Xin lower official who sees domestic grievance as the central danger.
- Root-cause critic who connects mistimed conscription, hunger, abandoned farming, grain prices, and rebellion.
- Hierarchy-aware speaker who packages criticism in loyalist language and routes it through a superior.
- Ineffective policy influencer whose warning is ignored before it reaches usable decision.
- Survival case whose indirectness reduces personal risk without repairing the regime.

## Qualifications

This page is source-bounded to Hanji 656. It does not reconstruct Fan Sheng's complete office history, later career, exact wording, or relationship to Wang Yi. The page treats the host's workplace analogy as an interpretive frame, not as proof that every indirect warning is wiser than direct remonstrance.
