## Overview

杨逍（金庸小说） / Yang Xiao (Jin Yong) is a Ming Cult leader in [[HeavenSwordDragonSaber|《倚天屠龙记》]] whose charisma, power, love for [[JiXiaofuJinYong|纪晓芙]], and admitted “forcing” remain in unresolved moral tension.

## Current Profile

The episode presents Yang as attractive through appearance, martial ability, experience, and force of personality, while refusing to convert attraction into moral exoneration. Ji Xiaofu names their daughter “Buhui,” refuses to kill him, and Yang collapses on learning of her death, all of which support later mutual attachment.

None of that determines what the earlier “forcing” consisted of. The source preserves competing readings ranging from coercion after an existing attachment to affection emerging after coercion, and it lacks the scene detail needed to choose among sexual violence, broader domination, or another form of imposed conduct.

## Key Characteristics

- Holds high status and formidable ability within Ming Cult.
- Exercises charisma and asymmetrical power in his relationship with Ji Xiaofu.
- Is associated by both characters with an act described as “forcing.”
- Inspires lasting love and loyalty from Ji without thereby resolving the consent issue.
- Shows profound grief at Ji's death.
- Retains moral ambiguity that adaptations may soften through appearance and heroic presentation.

## Qualifications

This source cannot settle the nature, sequence, or legal and ethical classification of the “forcing,” and modern categories should not be imposed without textual evidence. The opposite error is also excluded: later affection, loyalty, a shared child, or Yang's grief cannot retroactively establish consent. Adaptation-based impressions and claims about charisma, reputation, or motive remain interpretive.

## What Changed

- Made later love evidentially distinct from earlier consent.
