# Nicholas I of Russia

## Overview

Nicholas I was the Russian emperor whose claims to protect Orthodox Christians under Ottoman rule and occupation of Moldavia and Wallachia helped precipitate the [[CrimeanWar|Crimean War]].

## Current Profile

[[721-the-charge-of-the-light-brigade-outbreak-of-the-crimean-war-part-1-glt2909363211]] presents Nicholas as a ruler who joined religious conviction, imperial strategy, and a self-image as defender of the post-Napoleonic European order. He regarded protection of Orthodox Christians as divinely grounded and viewed the weakening [[OttomanEmpire|Ottoman Empire]] as a succession problem that great powers should settle in advance.

His central failure in the episode is political misreading. After a cordial 1844 visit to Britain, Nicholas believed elite conversation amounted to a durable Anglo-Russian understanding about Ottoman decline. He underestimated Parliament, the press, public opinion, anti-autocratic sentiment, Polish memory, and British anxiety over India. His 1853 protectorate demand and occupation of Ottoman territories therefore encountered a Britain much more hostile than he expected.

## Key Characteristics

- Understood monarchy and Orthodox protection as divinely authorized duties.
- Took pride in Russia's defeat of Napoleon and defense of the post-1815 order.
- Treated Ottoman decline as an imperial succession problem requiring advance agreement.
- Mistook cordial elite diplomacy for a binding national understanding with Britain.
- Underestimated British political pluralism, public opinion, and Russophobia.
- Escalated the crisis through protectorate demands and territorial occupation.

## Evidence

- Religious-imperial role: [[721-the-charge-of-the-light-brigade-outbreak-of-the-crimean-war-part-1-glt2909363211]] connects Nicholas's Orthodox protector identity to demands on the Ottoman government.
- Ottoman calculation: [[721-the-charge-of-the-light-brigade-outbreak-of-the-crimean-war-part-1-glt2909363211]] attributes the “sick man” frame and partition planning to his view of Ottoman decline.
- British misreading: [[721-the-charge-of-the-light-brigade-outbreak-of-the-crimean-war-part-1-glt2909363211]] contrasts his personal-diplomatic assumptions with Parliament, press, and public hostility.
- Escalation: [[721-the-charge-of-the-light-brigade-outbreak-of-the-crimean-war-part-1-glt2909363211]] links Russian demands, occupation of Moldavia and Wallachia, Ottoman war, and Sinope to British and French intervention.

## Qualifications

This episode emphasizes the road to war rather than Nicholas's full reign or Russian archival perspectives. The claimed 1844 understanding, his inner religious conviction, the seriousness of partition proposals, Russian military aims, and the relative importance of British public opinion remain episode-framed interpretations. British fear of an immediate Russian invasion of India is explicitly treated as disproportionate to Russian capability.

## What Changed

- Established Nicholas's role as a religious-imperial claimant whose misreading of British politics helped turn pressure on the Ottoman Empire into great-power war.

## Relationships

- [[NapoleonIII]] - rival ruler whose Catholic prestige politics intersected with Nicholas's Orthodox claims.
- [[OttomanEmpire]] - state pressured by his protectorate demand and territorial occupation.
- [[Russia]] - empire whose Orthodox and geopolitical claims he embodied.
- [[CrimeanWar]] - war produced partly by his demands, occupation, and diplomatic miscalculation.
- [[UnitedKingdom]] - power whose political system and public mood he misread.
