## Overview

杨仆 / Yang Pu is the Western Han tower-ship general whose profile runs from purchased military rank and harsh local enforcement through waterborne command in the conquest of [[Nanyue|南越]], victory and imperial rebuke, renewed service against Dongyue, a boundary proposal, and defeat and punishment in [[WimanJoseon|卫氏朝鲜]]. His career tests how [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] combines specialized military usefulness with repeated accountability.

## Current Profile

Hanji 353-1 fills the operational gap left by Hanji 352-2. It says Yang Pu began with a purchased “thousand-man” martial rank but demonstrated ability, acted severely against powerful local families, and was later grouped among harsh officials. Wudi appoints him tower-ship general and sends his specially developed waterborne force from [[YuzhangCommandery|豫章郡]] as one of five routes converging on Panyu. The episode calls him China's first “naval commander,” but the wiki retains that as a retrospective analogy grounded in his title and [[TowerShipWaterborneWarfare|tower-ship command]], not a settled historical superlative.

Hanji 356-1 supplies the aftermath. After victory, Yang Pu is accused of inflating battlefield credit, failing to pursue fully, boasting, delaying after recall, and not reporting promptly. Wudi rebukes him but accepts his offer to fight for redemption and sends him against [[YuShanDongyueKing|余善]]. Hanji 356-2 then attributes to Yang Pu the proposal to move [[HanguPass|函谷关]] east to Xin'an, aligning personal status with enlargement of the supervised imperial core.

Hanji 366-1 and 366-2 show a harsher endpoint in Wiman Joseon. Yang Pu advances with seven thousand Qi troops, is defeated, hides and regroups, then favors surrender while [[XunZhiWesternHan|荀彘]] favors assault. Missed dates and separate channels deepen suspicion until [[GongsunSuiWesternHan|公孙遂]] arrests Yang Pu and transfers his force. After conquest, Yang Pu redeems a death penalty but becomes a commoner for unauthorized advance and heavy losses.

## Key Characteristics

- Specialized tower-ship commander in the multi-route conquest of Nanyue.
- Forceful official whose purchased entry rank does not exhaust the sources' account of his capability.
- High-merit commander whose achievement does not prevent scrutiny of credit, reporting, pursuit, and obedience.
- Officer repeatedly allowed to seek redemption through service or legal commutation.
- Commander whose defeat and separate surrender channel contribute to fragmented command in Wiman Joseon.
- Proposer, in Hanji 356-2, of moving Hangu Pass eastward to Xin'an.

## Qualifications

“China's first naval commander” modernizes an ancient title and is not demonstrated through comparison with earlier waterborne warfare. Hanji 353-1 does not give the campaign's battle sequence or final allocation of credit, and its rank, service, route, ship, and personality details remain episode-attributed. Hanji 356-1's five accusations and Hanji 356-2's private-motive reading are likewise attributed. The Joseon episodes do not make caution equivalent to treason: premature advance, prior losses, separate diplomacy, and missed coordination create risk, but Hanji 366-1 says Xun Zhi lacks evidence for a larger plot. Transcript forms 杨溥 and 杨浦 are normalized to 杨仆.

## What Changed

- Added Yang Pu's pre-command background and the Yuzhang tower-ship route toward Panyu.
- Reframed the “first naval commander” label as a source-scoped analogy rather than a settled office classification.
- Connected specialized waterborne command to the later victory-and-accountability sequence without inventing missing battle details.
