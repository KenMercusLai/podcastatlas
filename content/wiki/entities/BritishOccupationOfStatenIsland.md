# British Occupation of Staten Island

## Overview

The British occupation of [[StatenIsland|Staten Island]] lasted from the July 1776 landing through evacuation on 3 December 1783 and converted an initially welcoming Loyalist base into a community marked by extraction, institutional breakdown, militia refusal, and resistance.

## Current Profile

The island supplied British forces with landing space, defensive positions, food, water, transport, fuel, horses, and wagons. Early enthusiasm and a large Loyalist mobilization made it appear a secure base near New York and New Jersey.

Over time, soldiers stole supplies, dismantled fences, damaged crops and houses, consumed or destroyed churches, removed records, and operated amid closed courts and a burned courthouse. Prices rose while complaints could worsen treatment. Patriot raids and the January 1780 frozen invasion added looting, abduction, and enslavement. The occupation therefore produced [[LoyalistSupportErosionUnderOccupation|support erosion]] without making the Patriot side benign.

## Key Characteristics

- Long-duration occupation of a strategically placed harbor island.
- Initially supported by strong local Loyalist mobilization and supply networks.
- Dependent on civilian food, transport, property, and institutional cooperation.
- Progressively associated with theft, inflation, destruction, and failed civil government.
- Contested by raids, kidnapping, intelligence, ambushes, and a winter invasion across ice.
- Ended with public celebration by at least some residents who had endured the occupation.

## Qualifications

The current account comes from one podcast summary and centers civilian experience rather than a complete operational history. Reported troop, price, duration, property-loss, and participation figures remain episode-scoped. Local resistance was uneven, and occupation harm does not establish uniform Patriot allegiance.

## What Changed

- Added the occupation as a distinct event rather than treating Staten Island only as a Loyalist label.
- Connected military dependence on local support to the behavior that eroded that support.
- Preserved Patriot looting and enslavement as a qualification to any liberation narrative.
