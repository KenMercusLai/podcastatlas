# 昌平君 / Changping Jun

昌平君 / Changping Jun enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-108-1-lishi-gan-dangmian-dingzhuang-qinshihuang-diyi-ren-lg0dssyicp387foiraohkv1x5aij]] as one of the Qin figures [[QinShiHuang|嬴政 / 秦始皇]] sends against [[LaoAi|嫪毐]] after Lao Ai's rebellion begins. The source says the rebel force is defeated around the Xianyang-side fighting, after which Lao Ai flees and is captured.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-118-1-wangjian-lv-liushiwan-dajun-duizhan-xiangyan-haoxi-kaichang-le-lsf2puinwg0m3bgyi25znof7aznu|Qinji 118-1]] turns that earlier stub into a source-critical problem. The host says the compact [[ZizhiTongjian|《资治通鉴》]] account records [[WangJianQin|王翦]] and [[MengWu|蒙武]] capturing [[ChuFuchu|楚王负刍]] in 223 BCE and Changping Jun dying, but leaves many questions unresolved.

The episode then introduces [[LiKaiyuan|李开元]]'s [[QinMi|《秦谜》]] reconstruction. As relayed by the host, Changping Jun is Xiong Qi, a son of [[MiWan|楚考烈王]] born from a Qin royal woman, with [[QinZhaoxiangwang|秦昭襄王]] as the maternal-line frame. This makes him a Qin court figure with a Chu royal identity problem rather than only a Qin official.

In that source-scoped reconstruction, Changping Jun's rebellion at Chenying is also used to explain why [[LiXinQin|李信]]'s earlier Chu campaign collapsed after withdrawal. The wiki keeps this as a cited reconstruction, not as an independently settled biography.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-118-2-chi-gua-le-wangshi-de-gua-bao-tian-lns7jt-kbv3qunm66hzltdgugbco|Qinji 118-2]] pushes the same reconstruction more confidently. It opens with [[PreQinSurnameClanIdentity|先秦姓氏身份]], using the pre-Qin 姓/氏 distinction to read "熊启" as a Chu royal identity marker, then ties Changping Jun to [[HuayangFuren|华阳夫人]], [[QinZhaoxiangwang|秦昭襄王]], and [[MiWan|楚考烈王]] through Qin-Chu marriage politics.

The episode also gives him a fuller late sequence. After the Lao Ai crisis and Lu Buwei's implication, Changping Jun becomes Qin chancellor, but the host infers that his Chu blood later makes [[QinShiHuang|嬴政 / 秦始皇]] distrust him. He is dismissed or displaced, rebels at Chenying during the Chu campaign, is later enthroned by [[XiangYan|项燕]] as Chu king, and dies when [[WangJianQin|王翦]] and [[MengWu|蒙武]] finish the final Chu resistance.
