# George B. McClellan

## Overview

George B. McClellan is the Union commander whose 1862 Peninsula Campaign failed before Richmond and whose intelligence advantage at [[BattleOfAntietam|Antietam]] helped check [[RobertELee|Robert E. Lee]]'s Maryland invasion without destroying his army.

## Current Profile

In [[201-american-civil-war-outbreak-part-2-glt4100428000]], McClellan anchors the contrast between Union resources and uneven eastern performance. He moved toward Richmond by the James River and peninsula but was defeated during the Seven Days Battles. During Lee's subsequent Maryland campaign, lost Confederate orders gave him a major opportunity; Antietam nevertheless remained a costly draw before Lee withdrew into Virginia.

The episode therefore presents McClellan less through a complete command biography than through two linked limits: Union material advantage did not automatically produce decisive battlefield results, and even incomplete military success could create a major political opening for emancipation.

## Key Characteristics

- Union commander in the eastern theater during 1862.
- Leader of the failed Peninsula Campaign against Richmond.
- Recipient of a major intelligence advantage before Antietam.
- Commander whose army checked Lee without securing a decisive destruction.
- Figure showing the gap between structural capacity and operational outcome.

## Evidence

### Peninsula Campaign

- [[201-american-civil-war-outbreak-part-2-glt4100428000]] says McClellan approached Richmond through the peninsula before Lee defeated him in the Seven Days Battles.

### Antietam

- [[201-american-civil-war-outbreak-part-2-glt4100428000]] connects lost Confederate orders to McClellan's advantage and describes the battle as a bloody draw followed by Lee's retreat.

## Qualifications

This is a bounded 1862 campaign profile, not a full assessment of McClellan's army-building, politics, relations with Lincoln, removal from command, or 1864 presidential candidacy. The source does not settle whether different speed or decisions would have destroyed Lee's army, and its account of the lost orders is compressed.

## What Changed

- Established McClellan's profile around the Peninsula failure and Antietam's incomplete but politically consequential success.
