# 成吉思汗 / Genghis Khan

## Overview

Genghis Khan was the Mongol ruler who unified steppe forces in 1206, campaigned across northern China and Central Asia, and created the conquest network from which the [[MongolEmpire]] developed.

## Current Profile

[[166-genghis-khan-lord-of-the-mongols-glt4398796320]] presents him as a leader whose aims widened through success. His first requirements were to consolidate nomadic groups and win plunder from northern China to reward a coalition. Campaigns against the Tanguts and Jin, followed by the destruction of the [[KhwarazmianEmpire]], transformed that coalition into a [[NomadicImperialFormation|nomadic imperial formation]] with composite forces, siege capacity, and Eurasian reach.

His rule combined sophisticated coalition politics and military adaptation with [[MongolStrategicTerror|conditional strategic terror]]. Submission could bring survival, while resistance, rebellion, or mistreatment of envoys could bring exemplary destruction. The episode refuses both romantic rehabilitation and context-free caricature: political purpose does not diminish the severity of the massacres, while unreliable casualty totals and legendary speeches still require source criticism.

At his death in 1227, authority remained concentrated in family, personal followers, commanders, and charisma more than a mature bureaucracy. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-131-3-liqingzhao-zhongnian-fuqi-libie-hejiu-jie-xiangsi-lnzxhrj2iulahccy2-pkm-wphdei|Qinji 131-3]] supplies a separate moral afterlife, using him as an example of drinking restraint in a feast culture; the newer episode calls him reportedly teetotal, so both claims remain attributed rather than definitive biography.

## Key Characteristics

- Unified Mongol and allied steppe groups under paramount leadership in 1206.
- Expanded from coalition maintenance and northern Chinese plunder toward imperial conquest.
- Incorporated non-Mongol manpower, siege skills, and tributary relationships into military power.
- Used conditional submission and exemplary punishment as instruments of authority.
- Governed a vast but lightly bureaucratized realm through kinship, commanders, loyalty, and charisma.
- Became a modern national, commercial, sacred, genealogical, and environmental symbol far beyond the secure evidence of his life.

## Qualifications

Casualty totals, the Great Yasa, the Bukhara speech, death accounts, burial-cortege stories, and the Tangut genocide or ethnocide label remain uncertain, exaggerated, or contested. The source's description of targeted violence explains a method but does not soften its moral or demographic consequences. Drinking restraint and teetotalism are compatible possibilities but not independently verified here. Genetic descent, atmospheric-carbon effects, plague transmission, and climate-assisted expansion are indirect or source-scoped claims rather than settled explanations of his success.
