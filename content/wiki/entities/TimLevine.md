# Tim Levine

## Overview

Tim Levine is a psychologist represented in [[the-queen-of-cuba-the-queen-of-cuba|The Queen of Cuba]] through deception experiments and [[TruthDefaultTheory]], his account of why people generally presume honesty until doubt crosses a trigger threshold.

## Current Profile

Levine's quiz experiments exposed participants to an opportunity to cheat and then asked observers to judge recorded denials. Some liars displayed familiar evasive cues, but others appeared convincing; observers therefore performed only slightly above chance at identifying deception in the episode's reported results. The important pattern was not random classification but a truth bias: observers tended to label both truthful and deceptive speakers as truthful.

Levine uses that tendency to explain why a person can notice inconsistencies without fully abandoning belief. His reinterpretation of [[StanleyMilgram]]'s experiment makes the same distinction: many participants entertained doubts about the staged shocks, but few became certain enough to reject the experiment's apparent reality.

## Key Characteristics

- Psychologist studying deception, truth judgments, and the conditions that trigger disbelief.
- Designer of quiz experiments contrasting actual cheating with observers' judgments of denials.
- Formulator of [[TruthDefaultTheory]] as presented by the episode.
- Emphasizes the difference between possessing doubts and exiting a default assumption of honesty.
- Skeptical that professional experience alone makes demeanor-based lie detection reliable.

## Evidence

### Deception experiments

- [[the-queen-of-cuba-the-queen-of-cuba]] describes Levine's staged cheating opportunity, recorded denials, and observer judgments as evidence of a strong tendency to classify speakers as truthful.

### Trigger threshold

- [[the-queen-of-cuba-the-queen-of-cuba]] attributes to Levine the claim that people leave truth-default mode only after accumulated doubt becomes a sufficiently strong trigger.

### Milgram reinterpretation

- [[the-queen-of-cuba-the-queen-of-cuba]] uses Levine's reading of Milgram to distinguish partial suspicion from certainty that the apparent situation is false.

## Qualifications

The bounded source is a podcast summary rather than Levine's publications, methods, datasets, replications, or a systematic review. Reported accuracy and cheating rates, sample composition, recording selection, professional-group performance, and the reinterpretation of Milgram remain source-scoped. Truth-default describes a general tendency, not a rule that every person trusts every speaker in every context.
