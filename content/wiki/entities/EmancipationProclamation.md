# Emancipation Proclamation

## Overview

The Emancipation Proclamation is Abraham Lincoln's wartime measure presented as a decisive bridge between Union military policy, the destruction of slavery, and Black enlistment.

## Current Profile

[[202-american-civil-war-gettysburg-part-3-glt5256515778]] treats the proclamation not as the whole of emancipation but as a policy change that enabled wider [[BlackUnionMilitaryServiceCitizenship|Black military service]] and clarified slavery's destruction as necessary to defeating the rebellion. The episode keeps this federal action beside [[EnslavedSelfEmancipation|enslaved people's flight and resistance]], which had already forced Union commanders to decide whether escapees would be returned.

The proclamation therefore changed both military capacity and the meaning of the war. Its practical reach depended on Union arms, Black service, and the ongoing collapse of Confederate control.

## Key Characteristics

- Wartime executive measure associated with Abraham Lincoln.
- Policy bridge from Union preservation toward slavery's destruction.
- Enabler of expanded Black enlistment in the Union armed forces.
- Measure whose enforcement depended on military advance and local action.
- Part of, rather than a substitute for, enslaved people's agency in emancipation.

## Evidence

### Enlistment and military transformation

- [[202-american-civil-war-gettysburg-part-3-glt5256515778]] says Black soldiers did not serve from the war's beginning and that the proclamation helped open the path to large-scale enlistment.

### War aims and slavery

- [[202-american-civil-war-gettysburg-part-3-glt5256515778]] attributes to Lincoln the view that saving the Union required attacking slavery as the rebellion's root.

### Policy and bottom-up action

- [[202-american-civil-war-gettysburg-part-3-glt5256515778]] places formal policy beside escape to Union lines, the contraband category, and weakening plantation discipline.

## Qualifications

This bounded source does not detail the proclamation's text, exemptions, constitutional theory, preliminary version, international effects, or geographic limits. It should not be described as a single act that independently freed every enslaved person; the episode instead supports a combined account of policy, military enforcement, Black service, and self-emancipation.

## What Changed

- Established the proclamation as a policy hinge connecting Union war aims, Black enlistment, and slavery's destruction.
