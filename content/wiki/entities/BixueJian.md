## Overview

《碧血剑》 / Sword Stained with Royal Blood is a [[JinYong|金庸]] wuxia novel represented here through two [[ZhiZuiJinMiFM|纸醉金迷FM]] readings of love, revenge, martial inheritance, historical power, and revision.

## Current Profile

The relationship discussion treats the novel as a system of mismatched feeling and constrained action. [[YuanChengzhi|袁承志]] protects more readily than he declares; [[WenQingqing|温青青]] acts more readily than she trusts; [[AjiuBixueJian|阿九]] and Yuan share feeling without converting it into a future; and [[HeHongyao|何红药]] turns total sacrifice into destructive possession.

The previous generation makes romance inseparable from revenge. [[XiaXueyi|夏雪宜]] and [[WenYiBixueJian|温仪]] nearly interrupt a family vendetta, but greed and suspicion destroy the exit. Because Xia dies before the main action yet continues to shape treasure, martial inheritance, love, and retaliation, the novel also supplies a strong case of [[AbsentPresenceCharacterDesign]].

The historical discussion broadens the novel from private attachment and vengeance into judgment of regimes. Chongzhen, Hong Taiji, and [[LiZicheng|李自成]] are evaluated through consequences for ordinary people rather than legitimacy or ethnicity alone. 袁崇焕 and 李岩 form a pair of loyal servants destroyed by suspicious rulers, while 张朝唐's robbery by both Ming and rebel troops shows political turnover reproducing popular suffering. This gives “碧血” a tragic political meaning and links the novel to [[PeopleCenteredHistoricalJudgment]].

The same discussion treats Yuan's progression as structurally weak but thematically revealing. He inherits multiple martial systems, protective equipment, treasure, and a coalition of heroes without acquiring the command, discipline, statecraft, or institutional leverage needed to change war and government. His overseas departure closes the novel through [[MartialHeroHistoricalAgencyLimits]] rather than victory.

## Key Characteristics

- Romantic choice is divided among passion, responsibility, gratitude, promise, security, and viable action.
- Family status and violent institutions shape attachment without eliminating individual responsibility.
- Love may interrupt revenge temporarily but cannot by itself overcome organized greed and coercion.
- A dead predecessor continues to govern the living plot through memory, objects, skills, and unfinished relationships.
- Edition changes alter emotional explicitness and the credibility of character motivation.
- Regimes and rebels are judged by their treatment of ordinary people rather than origin, slogan, or ethnic identity alone.
- Martial mastery can win encounters and enable rescue without supplying military command, political institutions, or historical control.

## Qualifications

The current profile derives from two adjacent podcast discussions rather than a complete edition-by-edition textual or historical study. Claims about psychology, authorial intention, martial-lineage continuity, historical causation, and which relationship constitutes the deepest love remain interpretive. The historical installment's nearly flawless and indistinct Yuan is qualified by the relationship installment's stronger account of romantic ambivalence.

## What Changed

- Extended the profile from romantic and revenge structure into popular-welfare judgment and repeated political betrayal.
- Distinguished martial or rescue capacity from military, institutional, and historical agency.
- Qualified the “indistinct protagonist” judgment through the adjacent episode's account of romantic ambivalence.
