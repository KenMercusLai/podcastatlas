# Richard II of England

## Overview

Richard II was the English king whose conflict with [[HenryIVOfEngland|Henry of Lancaster]], confiscation of the Lancastrian inheritance, deposition, and death created the founding legitimacy crisis of the Lancastrian regime.

## Current Profile

The episode presents Richard and Henry as cousins born months apart but placed in a structurally tense relationship by royal succession. Richard inherited through Edward the Black Prince, while Henry's descent through John of Gaunt and the competing Mortimer line kept alternatives near the throne. Henry's popularity, chivalric reputation, and noble standing sharpened the political contrast between them.

In 1398 Richard exiled Henry for ten years. After John of Gaunt's death, he confiscated Henry's inheritance, turning a personal rivalry into a wider threat to aristocratic property and succession. Henry returned claiming restoration of his lands, but Richard was rapidly abandoned, captured, deposed, and confined at Pontefract. His subsequent death burdened the new king with suspicion of regicide and allowed rebels to invoke Richard's survival or restoration against [[LancastrianLegitimacy|Lancastrian legitimacy]].

## Key Characteristics

- Hereditary king whose succession remained politically complicated by Lancaster and Mortimer claims.
- Rival of a popular, chivalric cousin close to the royal line.
- Ruler whose exile and confiscation of Henry's inheritance alienated the nobility.
- Deposed monarch whose imprisonment and death became a lasting political liability for Henry IV.
- Posthumous legitimacy symbol used by restoration plots, rebels, and imposture rumors.

## Evidence

- Succession and rivalry: [[485-henry-iv-the-usurper-king-part-1-glt8868267122]] connects Richard's descent, Henry's Lancastrian position, the Mortimer line, and their close ages.
- Exile and confiscation: [[485-henry-iv-the-usurper-king-part-1-glt8868267122]] identifies Henry's extended exile and the seizure of his inheritance as the immediate political rupture.
- Deposition and death: [[485-henry-iv-the-usurper-king-part-1-glt8868267122]] traces Richard's abandonment, capture, removal, confinement, and death.
- Political afterlife: [[485-henry-iv-the-usurper-king-part-1-glt8868267122]] links the Epiphany Rising and later survival rumors to continuing opposition against Henry IV.

## Qualifications

The episode is organized around Henry IV and does not provide a complete assessment of Richard's reign. Its language of tyranny, jealousy, abandonment, and responsibility for death reflects a mixture of political interpretation and reported evidence. The exact hereditary hierarchy among the competing lines, Henry's original intentions on landing, and the circumstances and agency behind Richard's death remain qualified.

## What Changed

- Added Richard as the deposed ruler whose confiscation policy and death made Lancastrian legitimacy a continuing problem rather than a settled transfer of power.
