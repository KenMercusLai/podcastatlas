## Overview

邓训 / Deng Xun enters the wiki as the Protector of the Qiang whose death in 92 CE reveals unusually deep frontier legitimacy. Hanji 767 contrasts his cross-community mourning and household shrines with [[NieShangLateHan|Nie Shang]]'s failed attempt to succeed him through quick conciliation.

## Current Profile

The source records Deng Xun's death as a political event, not only an obituary. Han officials, commoners, Qiang, Hu, and former Wuhuan-protector subordinates mourn him in large numbers. Some frontier mourners cut themselves or slaughter animals as a sign of wanting to follow him in death, while households later build shrines and pray to him during epidemics.

The mourning evidence makes Deng a standard of local trust. His successor Nie Shang also tries conciliation, but Mi Tang's renewed rebellion and killing of escorts makes the contrast sharp: Deng's legitimacy appears rooted in accumulated credibility, while Nie's posture lacks the same trust base.

## Key Characteristics

- Eastern Han frontier official whose death draws mourning from Han, Qiang, Hu, and former Wuhuan-linked subordinates.
- Protector of the Qiang remembered through public grief, private household shrines, and epidemic prayers.
- Example of frontier legitimacy that outlasts office and military command.
- Contrast figure for Nie Shang's failed, impatient conciliation of Mi Tang.
- Source-bounded model of people-based political security in a frontier setting.

## Qualifications

This page is not a full biography of Deng Xun. Hanji 767 records posthumous legitimacy and a contrast with Nie Shang, but it does not yet explain the policies or earlier actions that created that frontier trust.
