# Herbert Kitchener

## Overview

Herbert Kitchener was the British commander whose Sudan campaign culminated in the [[BattleOfOmdurman|Battle of Omdurman]] and whose conduct [[WinstonChurchill|Winston Churchill]] both sought access to and later condemned.

## Current Profile

The source places Kitchener at the center of a contradiction in imperial war. He initially resisted Churchill's attempt to join the campaign, apparently distrusting his medal hunting and self-advertisement. His army's technological superiority then produced decisive victory at Omdurman, but Churchill criticized Kitchener's callousness toward prisoners and the desecration of the Mahdi's tomb after the battle.

## Key Characteristics

- He commanded the Anglo-Egyptian reconquest campaign presented as avenging Gordon and Khartoum.
- He did not welcome Churchill's self-promoting presence in the force.
- His army possessed overwhelming technological advantages over the Dervish forces.
- His treatment of the defeated became the object of Churchill's moral criticism.

## Qualifications

The episode provides a Churchill-centered portrait rather than a complete account of Kitchener, the Anglo-Egyptian army, Sudanese politics, or the campaign's long-term consequences. Churchill's criticism is important evidence within the source, not a comprehensive verdict.
