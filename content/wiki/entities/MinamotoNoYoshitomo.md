# Minamoto no Yoshitomo

## Overview
Minamoto no Yoshitomo is the defeated Minamoto leader whose failed 1159 coup creates the exile-and-survival conditions for [[MinamotoNoYoritomo]].

## Current Profile
[[659-dawn-of-the-samurai-bloodbath-at-the-bridge-part-2-glt3420393571]] presents Yoshitomo as initially victorious alongside [[TairaNoKiyomori]] in the 1156 Kyoto crisis, then increasingly resentful of Kiyomori's superior rewards. In 1159, while Kiyomori is away on pilgrimage, Yoshitomo attempts a coup, seizes the young emperor, and kills Kiyomori's allies.

The coup fails when Kiyomori returns to Kyoto, the emperor escapes, and Taira forces defeat the Minamoto. Yoshitomo flees and is murdered by an old ally while bathing, turning his children into either victims, exiles, or politically dangerous survivors.

## Key Characteristics
- Yoshitomo represents the Minamoto side of warrior intervention in Kyoto succession politics.
- His resentment over unequal rewards helps trigger the 1159 coup.
- His defeat allows Kiyomori to dominate court politics.
- His surviving sons become the future Minamoto threat to Taira power.

## Evidence
- Warrior entry into Kyoto: [[659-dawn-of-the-samurai-bloodbath-at-the-bridge-part-2-glt3420393571]] includes Yoshitomo among the victors of the 1156 conflict and the participants in public executions.
- Failed coup: [[659-dawn-of-the-samurai-bloodbath-at-the-bridge-part-2-glt3420393571]] describes his 1159 seizure of the emperor, Kiyomori's return, imperial escape, and Yoshitomo's defeat.
- Aftermath: [[659-dawn-of-the-samurai-bloodbath-at-the-bridge-part-2-glt3420393571]] links his death to Kiyomori's killing or sparing of Yoshitomo's sons, including Yoritomo.
