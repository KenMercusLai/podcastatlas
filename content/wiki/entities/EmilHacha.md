# Emil Hácha

## Overview

Emil Hácha was the Czech president coerced in Berlin immediately before German forces occupied Prague and established the Protectorate of Bohemia and Moravia in March 1939.

## Current Profile

The episode presents Hácha as an elderly lawyer and intellectual leading the diminished post-Munich Czech state. Summoned to Berlin with his foreign minister and daughter, he was kept waiting and then confronted after midnight by Hitler and senior Nazi officials.

Hitler announced that German troops would cross the border within hours and demanded an order against Czech resistance; Göring added the threat that Prague could be bombed. Hácha collapsed during the meeting, was revived by Hitler's doctor, contacted Prague, and signed a declaration accepting German protection under conditions that make the act evidence of coercion rather than voluntary alignment.

## Key Characteristics

- Lawyer and intellectual serving as president of the post-Munich Czech state.
- Summoned into a deliberately intimidating overnight Berlin meeting.
- Faced a pre-scheduled invasion and threats against Prague.
- Collapsed during the confrontation and signed under acute coercion.
- Figure through whom Nazi force was given the appearance of formal consent.
