# Jean-Lambert Tallien

## Overview

Jean-Lambert Tallien was a National Convention deputy and former representative on mission who became a leading parliamentary attacker of [[MaximilienRobespierre|Robespierre]] on 9 Thermidor.

## Current Profile

The source presents Tallien as a politically exposed former practitioner of provincial violence whose own survival and concern for his imprisoned lover, Thérésa Cabarrus, drew him into the anti-Robespierre coalition. He spent the night before 9 Thermidor encouraging deputies to resist, interrupted [[LouisAntoineSaintJust|Saint-Just]] in the Convention, denounced Robespierre as a tyrant, and displayed a dagger as a theatrical sign that he would act if the assembly did not.

## Key Characteristics

- His provincial record made a new purge personally dangerous.
- Thérésa Cabarrus's imprisonment added a close personal motive for action.
- He helped organize moderate and threatened deputies before the decisive sitting.
- He used interruption, accusation, and theatrical threat to break Robespierre's control of debate.
- His role exemplifies coalition through survival rather than shared innocence of terror.

## Qualifications

- The source supplies Tallien's role in Thermidor rather than a full biography or audited account of his earlier mission.
- Personal rescue, political survival, factional rivalry, and opposition to Robespierre can coexist; the source does not isolate one motive.
- Reported dialogue, private organizing, and the precise effect of the dagger display remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- A canonical profile now records Tallien's exposure, coalition work, and decisive Convention intervention.
