# John Bradshaw

## Overview

John Bradshaw was the Cheshire lawyer who presided over the specially created High Court of Justice at the trial of [[CharlesIOfEngland|Charles I]].

## Current Profile

Bradshaw appears as the public voice of an extraordinary tribunal whose commissioners combined judging and jury functions. His authority depended on the court's revolutionary claim that a king could be called to account in the people's name, a jurisdiction Charles rejected by refusing to plead.

The episode uses his confrontation with Charles to show both the trial's formality and its political inversion. Bradshaw delivered or enforced the court's authority even when Charles sought to speak after sentence, but the famous story that he protected himself with a bulletproof hat lacks evidence in Vallance's account.

## Key Characteristics

- English lawyer selected to preside over Charles I's High Court of Justice.
- Embodied the court's disputed claim to judge an anointed monarch.
- Operated within a commission that combined judicial and jury functions.
- Became attached to later legend as well as documented revolutionary procedure.

- Legend boundary: [[144-the-trial-of-charles-i-part-2-glt2968292003]] reports Vallance's conclusion that there is no evidence for the bulletproof-hat story.

This is a trial-bounded profile, not a full biography of Bradshaw or account of his later career. Commissioner attendance, individual influence, dialogue, motive, and later anecdotes require fuller primary and specialist evidence.
