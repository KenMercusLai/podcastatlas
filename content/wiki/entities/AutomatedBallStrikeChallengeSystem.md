# Automated Ball Strike Challenge System

The Automated Ball Strike Challenge System, or ABS, appears in [[tech-20260330-0330-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260330-0330-mp-tech-pod-128]] as [[MajorLeagueBaseball]]'s camera-supported system for reviewing balls and strikes. The source describes it as a challenge system: after a human umpire's call, a batter, catcher, or pitcher can ask for review by tapping his hat.

ABS connects [[Hawkeye]] tracking to [[BaseballStrikeZoneAutomation]]. The source says MLB calibrates the top and bottom of the zone to percentages of each player's height and uses a two-dimensional strike zone positioned at the middle of the plate, even though the rulebook strike zone is three-dimensional.

The episode frames ABS as a bounded form of [[SportsOfficiatingAutomation]]. It can improve fairness and trust, but it does not erase umpires: if a pitch is not correctly tracked or recorded, the umpire's original call stands.

## Key Claims
- ABS changes the review process before it changes who officially works the game.
- Player challenge limits and gestures make the system part of game procedure, not a separate broadcast graphic.
- The most important design choice is not only camera accuracy but also where the measured strike-zone plane sits relative to the plate.
- The system creates data that could affect umpire training, evaluation, and future rule changes.
