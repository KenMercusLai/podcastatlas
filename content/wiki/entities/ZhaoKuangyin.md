## Overview

赵匡胤 / Zhao Kuangyin is the founding emperor of the [[NorthernSong|北宋]]. The current evidence follows his rise through Later Zhou military networks, his politically packaged accession at [[ChenQiaoMutiny|陈桥兵变]], his attempt to replace Five Dynasties military turnover with central control, his southern-first unification, and the succession uncertainty that followed his death. A newer source adds official chronology to that profile: his era names projected founding ambition, but the reported 乾德 collision shows that symbolic state language could fail when historical review was weak.

## Current Profile

Zhao Kuangyin's rise is cumulative rather than miraculous in the current evidence. Family military background, hardship, [[GuoWei|郭威]] and [[ChaiRong|柴荣]]'s Later Zhou order, the Battle of Gaoping,禁军 reform, oath-brother networks, and [[ZhaoPu|赵普]]'s counsel create the power base from which Chenqiao becomes possible. Soldier acclamation, omen talk, frontier alarms, capital access, and abdication ritual then make a prepared seizure appear compelled, supporting a judgment of politically managed legitimacy rather than either pure accident or an entirely transparent plot.

As ruler, Zhao Kuangyin combines unification with institutional redesign. [[CupWineDisarmsGenerals|杯酒释兵权]], [[SongMilitaryCentralization|宋初军事集权]], and court-controlled appointments seek to prevent another cycle of military succession. [[SongUnificationStrategy|先南后北]] shows strategic restraint, but [[SichuanPostConquestResistance|川蜀征服后反抗]] shows that conquest under his commanders could still produce plunder, coercion, and local violence.

Hanji 301 adds symbolic administration. 建隆 and 开宝 are interpreted as assertive founding names, while 乾德 becomes a case of institutional embarrassment when a mirror allegedly reveals that [[WangYanFormerShu|前蜀王衍]] had already used the name. The account fits Zhao Kuangyin's broader legitimacy concerns, but the inscription, palace route, reaction, and causal link to the later era-name change remain source-scoped.

His death leaves an unresolved succession field. [[CandleShadowAxeSound|烛影斧声]], [[SongEmpress|宋皇后]]'s reported attempt to summon [[ZhaoDefang|赵德芳]], [[WangJien|王继恩]]'s turn to [[ZhaoGuangyi|赵光义]], and the later [[GoldenCabinetPledge|金匮之盟]] make Zhao Kuangyin the absent center of a legitimacy struggle that the current sources do not conclusively resolve.

## Key Characteristics

- Military-network founder whose accession joined accumulated command power to managed legitimacy performance.
- Institution-builder who reduced warlord autonomy through bargains, surveillance, personnel control, and military centralization.
- Pragmatic unifier whose southern-first strategy coexisted with severe conquest violence by subordinates.
- Ruler who used era names as founding symbolism but reportedly suffered a historical-review failure over 乾德.
- Succession focal point whose death and intended inheritance remain contested rather than settled.

## Evidence

### Rise and accession

- [[169-songtaizu-zhisi-zhuying-fusheng-yinmoulun-zhong-de-renxing-mituan-shang-957052909|Song Taizu 169]] reconstructs the Later Zhou military field, Zhao Kuangyin's command network, the “点检做天子” rumor, Chenqiao mobilization, and the ritual packaging of accession.

### Centralization and unification

- [[169-songtaizu-zhisi-zhuying-fusheng-yinmoulun-zhong-de-renxing-mituan-shang-957052909|Song Taizu 169]] treats disarmament and military redesign as a prolonged institutional process rather than a single banquet.
- [[170-songtaizu-zhisi-zhuying-fusheng-yinmoulun-zhong-de-renxing-mituan-xia-958158115|Song Taizu 170]] adds southern-first strategy, Sichuan conquest violence, later command discipline, and the limits of a morally clean unification story.

### Era-name symbolism and collision

- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-301-tai-liqi-jiemi-nianhao-zui-da-wulong-shijian-lrsbd_9zfkcofnosolvrjvfcdfyg|Hanji 301]] reads 建隆 and 开宝 as founding-era signals and attributes the 乾德 embarrassment to the discovery of [[WangYanFormerShu|王衍]]'s earlier use through a mirror associated with [[LadyHuaruiLaterShu|花蕊夫人]].

### Death and succession shadow

- [[170-songtaizu-zhisi-zhuying-fusheng-yinmoulun-zhong-de-renxing-mituan-xia-958158115|Song Taizu 170]] joins the death-night story, palace transfer sequence, alternative heirs, and competing Golden Cabinet versions without claiming that Zhao Guangyi's guilt is proven.

## Qualifications

- The two long-form death-series episodes explicitly interpret later official history through succession interests; their revisionist account is evidence-bounded and does not make every counter-story true.
- Chenqiao planning, participant roles, omen manufacture, and Zhao Guangyi's early involvement remain disputed.
- The civil-military redesign reduced one class of coup risk but may also have contributed to later border weakness; the current sources do not isolate its long-run causal weight.
- Hanji 301 is a compact summary. The 乾德 mirror's provenance, [[LadyHuaruiLaterShu|Lady Huarui]] identification, Zhao Kuangyin's reaction, and the claim that the incident caused the switch to 开宝 require independent verification.
- The 烛影斧声, Golden Cabinet pledge, alternative-heir sequence, and Zhao Kuangyin's intended successor remain unresolved.

## What Changed

- Added era-name selection to Zhao Kuangyin's legitimacy and state-symbolism profile.
- Added 乾德 reuse as a source-scoped historical-review failure rather than a settled causal explanation for the switch to 开宝.
- Integrated the new chronology evidence without changing the unresolved judgment on his death and succession.

- [[ChenQiaoMutiny|陈桥兵变]] - accession event joining military coercion to legitimacy performance.
- [[SongMilitaryCentralization|宋初军事集权]] - institutional answer to the military turnover that enabled his own rise.
- [[SongUnificationStrategy|先南后北]] - strategic sequence used to consolidate the south before harder northern campaigns.
