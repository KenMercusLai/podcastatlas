# 赵匡胤 / Song Taizu

Zhao Kuangyin / 赵匡胤 is the founding emperor of the [[NorthernSong|北宋]] and the central figure in [[169-songtaizu-zhisi-zhuying-fusheng-yinmoulun-zhong-de-renxing-mituan-shang-957052909]] and [[170-songtaizu-zhisi-zhuying-fusheng-yinmoulun-zhong-de-renxing-mituan-xia-958158115]]. The first episode follows him from a declining military family and early hardship into [[LaterZhou|后周]] service under [[GuoWei|郭威]] and [[ChaiRong|柴荣]], where the Battle of Gaoping,禁军 reform, oath-brother networks, and [[ZhaoPu|赵普]]'s advice become his political base.

The source treats Zhao Kuangyin as neither an innocent accidental ruler nor a crude usurper alone. His role in [[ChenQiaoMutiny|陈桥兵变]] is read as a politically packaged seizure of power: soldiers, omens, frontier alarms, capital access, and abdication ritual all make him appear compelled while making the transfer workable.

After founding the Song, Zhao Kuangyin's importance shifts from coup-maker to institution-builder. [[CupWineDisarmsGenerals|杯酒释兵权]], [[SongMilitaryCentralization|宋初军事集权]], relatively generous treatment of surrendered rulers, and the [[KaifengCapitalTradeoff|开封定都取舍]] all show a ruler trying to end Five Dynasties-style military turnover through bargain, surveillance, and central control.

The second episode adds Taizu's governing and succession shadow. [[SongUnificationStrategy|先南后北]] shows him as a practical strategist who avoids premature northern war, while [[SichuanPostConquestResistance|川蜀征服后反抗]] shows that unification under him could still produce severe local violence when commanders such as [[WangQuanbin|王全斌]] abused victory.

Taizu's death becomes the hinge to [[SongSuccessionLegitimacyCrisis|宋初继承合法性危机]]. [[CandleShadowAxeSound|烛影斧声]], [[SongEmpress|宋皇后]]'s expected summons for [[ZhaoDefang|赵德芳]], and the later [[GoldenCabinetPledge|金匮之盟]] make him not only a founder but the absent center around whom [[ZhaoGuangyi|赵光义 / 宋太宗]], [[ZhaoDezhao|赵德昭]], [[ZhaoDefang|赵德芳]], and [[ZhaoTingmei|赵廷美]] are repositioned.
