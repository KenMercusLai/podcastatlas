## Overview

[[CaoZhi|曹植]] is Cao Cao's poet son, presented in Hanji 1149 as a serious but ultimately weakened succession contender whose literary brilliance does not compensate for drinking, rule-breaking, and unreliable self-command.

## Current Profile

The episode says [[CaoCao|曹操]] leaves Cao Zhi at Ye and assigns [[XingYongLateHan|刑颙]] as a strict household counselor, implying that Cao Zhi once received deliberate political formation. Cao Zhi instead prefers more congenial literary company, including [[LiuZhenLateHan|刘桢]], although Liu Zhen himself warns that ornate talent is not a substitute for Xing Yong's practical counsel.

The succession judgment turns on reliability. The reported Sima Gate violation and Cao Zhi's drinking make talent hard to trust with power, while [[CaoPi|曹丕]] is portrayed as more controlled, politically legible, and able to manage officials. This is a source-scoped succession interpretation, not a complete literary or political biography.

## Key Characteristics

- Exceptional literary ability and expressive temperament.
- Receives strict political mentorship but resists its style.
- Prefers congenial talent while underweighting corrective counsel.
- Loses political trust through drinking and boundary violations.
- Illustrates the gap between brilliance and dependable governing capacity.

## Qualifications

The source does not establish that one gate incident alone decided the succession, nor that poetic temperament and political capacity are inherently opposed. Exact appointments, dialogue, chronology, motives, and the Cao Pi contrast require direct textual comparison.

## What Changed

- Established Cao Zhi as a succession contender evaluated through self-command and receptivity to correction.
- Separated literary achievement from reliable political delegation.

## Relationships

- [[CaoCao|曹操]] - father and succession evaluator who assigns Xing Yong to Cao Zhi.
- [[CaoPi|曹丕]] - brother and succession rival presented as more politically controlled.
- [[XingYongLateHan|刑颙]] - strict counselor whose useful advice Cao Zhi undervalues.
- [[LiuZhenLateHan|刘桢]] - literary companion who nevertheless urges respect for Xing Yong.
- [[PoliticalSelfControlTrust|政治托付中的自制力]] - concept explaining why talent without reliable conduct loses delegability.
