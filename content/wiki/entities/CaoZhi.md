## Overview

[[CaoZhi|曹植]] is Cao Cao's poet son, presented in Hanji 1149 as a serious but ultimately weakened succession contender whose literary brilliance does not compensate for drinking, rule-breaking, and unreliable self-command.

## Current Profile

The episode says [[CaoCao|曹操]] leaves Cao Zhi at Ye and assigns [[XingYongLateHan|刑颙]] as a strict household counselor, implying that Cao Zhi once received deliberate political formation. Cao Zhi instead prefers more congenial literary company, including [[LiuZhenLateHan|刘桢]], although Liu Zhen himself warns that ornate talent is not a substitute for Xing Yong's practical counsel.

The succession judgment turns on reliability. The reported Sima Gate violation and Cao Zhi's drinking make talent hard to trust with power, while [[CaoPi|曹丕]] is portrayed as more controlled, politically legible, and able to manage officials.

Hanji 1156 adds a factional layer through [[DingYiLateHan|丁仪]], described as a Cao Zhi adviser with a grievance against Cao Pi. The episode places the likely accusation of [[CuiYanLateHan|崔琰]], a public Cao Pi supporter, inside that contest. This establishes political alignment and a plausible interest structure, not Cao Zhi's direction of the accusation or knowledge of it.

## Key Characteristics

- Exceptional literary ability and expressive temperament.
- Receives strict political mentorship but resists its style.
- Prefers congenial talent while underweighting corrective counsel.
- Loses political trust through drinking and boundary violations.
- Illustrates the gap between brilliance and dependable governing capacity.
- Attracts advisers whose rivalry with Cao Pi makes the succession struggle a factional risk field.

- Succession reliability: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1149-jiemi-sanguo-dingji-moushi-xunyou-de-guanchang-shengcunshu-ljv1fwephe86-qczfy0o1n2yh7um|Hanji 1149]] uses the Sima Gate episode and Cao Zhi's drinking to explain declining trust relative to Cao Pi.
- Factional alignment: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1156-caocao-weihe-bi-chu-cuiyan-zhe-mian-daode-daqi-lpmr4ep1uk9hjmwteosbusxukjgo|Hanji 1156]] identifies Ding Yi as a Cao Zhi adviser and reconstructs his possible interest in removing the Cao Pi-aligned Cui Yan.

## Qualifications

The sources do not establish that one gate incident alone decided the succession, nor that poetic temperament and political capacity are inherently opposed. Hanji 1156 does not show Cao Zhi directing, approving, or knowing of the accusation against Cui Yan; it places a member of his camp in a plausible but indirectly reconstructed role.

- Kept Cao Zhi's personal responsibility outside the current evidence.
