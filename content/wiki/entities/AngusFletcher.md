# Angus Fletcher

## Overview
Angus Fletcher is a narrative scholar and friend of [[GaryGoldman]] who brings Goldman's Zootopia dispute to [[MalcolmGladwell]] in [[zootopia-exposed-part-one-zootopia-exposed-part-one]].

## Current Profile
In the Zootopia source, Fletcher serves as advocate, narrative analyst, and hypothesis builder. He treats Goldman's proposed animal world as a myth-scale concept and reads Gary De'Snake's stolen family invention in [[Zootopia2Film|Zootopia 2]] as an allegory of Goldman's allegation against Disney. His interpretation organizes the episode's mystery but is not independent evidence of copying or filmmaker intent.

In [[in-defense-of-paw-patrol-in-defense-of-paw-patrol|In Defense of PAW Patrol]], he becomes a critic of over-resolved children's narrative. His term “vacuous agon” describes conflict that generates little genuine anxiety because viewers know a competent authority will supply the answer. He argues that immediate explanation can interrupt active thought, reduce counterfactual problem-solving, and deny children the imaginative invitation created by unresolved stories.

## Key Characteristics
- Narrative scholar who frames the dispute as a layered story requiring extended investigation.
- Friend and advocate who introduces Goldman's account to Gladwell.
- Interpreter of Goldman's original concept as a world capable of supporting many stories.
- Proponent of the theory that the sequel allegorizes Goldman's dispute.
- Source of hypotheses rather than a participant in Disney's production or the underlying litigation.
- Critic of formulaic narrative when predetermined solutions leave audiences little reason to generate alternatives.
- Treats a varied story diet as more important than an absolute ban on reassuring entertainment.

## Evidence
### Investigative role
- [[zootopia-exposed-part-one-zootopia-exposed-part-one]] says Fletcher contacted Gladwell on Goldman's behalf and argued that the story's layers required two episodes.

### Narrative interpretation
- [[zootopia-exposed-part-one-zootopia-exposed-part-one]] attributes to Fletcher the view that Goldman's concept could sustain a mythic universe and that the sequel turns his dispute into allegory.
- [[zootopia-exposed-part-one-zootopia-exposed-part-one]] also records Fletcher's suggestion that a favorable portrayal of Gary somehow passed through Disney's legal and corporate systems.

### Children's narrative and imaginative exercise
- [[in-defense-of-paw-patrol-in-defense-of-paw-patrol]] records Fletcher's claim that PAW Patrol's immediately explained rescues leave too little uncertainty for counterfactual thought.
- [[in-defense-of-paw-patrol-in-defense-of-paw-patrol]] also preserves his diet analogy: occasional formula need not be damaging, but exclusive reliance on it may be developmentally thin.

## Qualifications
The profile is limited to two structured podcast summaries. Friendship and advocacy give Fletcher access to Goldman's perspective but do not establish access to Disney's internal decisions. His claims about allegory, corporate review, and concealed intent remain interpretations rather than production evidence. The PAW Patrol source supplies no developmental study, exposure threshold, or child-outcome data; a predictable ending does not by itself prove passive viewing.

## What Changed
- Added Fletcher's critique of predetermined children's narrative and its counterfactual opportunity cost.
- Preserved his media-diet qualification rather than turning the critique into an absolute prohibition.
- Kept both the Zootopia and child-development interpretations distinct from direct causal evidence.
