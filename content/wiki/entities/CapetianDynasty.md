# Capetian Dynasty

## Overview

The Capetian Dynasty appears as the French royal house whose long father-to-son succession stabilizes royal France before the failure of direct male heirs creates the succession crisis behind the [[HundredYearsWar|Hundred Years' War]].

## Current Profile

The sources present the Capetian line as unusually stable by medieval standards. Hugh Capet is elected in 987, and fourteen Capetian kings succeed one another from father to son. This succession stability helps French kings avoid repeated inheritance conflict while expanding the royal domain beyond the original Paris-Orleans base.

The [[AlbigensianCrusade]] supplies a concrete mechanism for that expansion. [[SimonDeMontfort]]'s heirs could not hold the southern conquests, so Louis VIII accepted Amaury de Montfort's claimed lands and led a royal campaign. The 1229 settlement reduced Toulouse's territory, preserved Béziers and Carcassonne for the crown, and used dynastic marriage to prepare the eventual 1271 absorption of Toulouse.

That success makes the later rupture more dramatic. After [[PhilipIVOfFrance|Philip IV]] dies, his three sons succeed in rapid sequence without leaving surviving sons. The direct male line ends, allowing [[EdwardIIIOfEngland|Edward III]] to assert a claim through [[IsabellaOfFrance|Isabella]] while French elites select [[PhilipVIOfFrance|Philip VI]] of the [[ValoisDynasty|Valois]] line.

## Key Characteristics

- French royal dynasty descended from Hugh Capet's 987 election.
- Presented as unusually stable through fourteen father-to-son successions.
- Initially rules a smaller royal domain around Paris and Orleans.
- Expands royal authority by reducing Angevin power and absorbing other territories.
- Converts unstable private crusading conquests in Languedoc into durable royal territory.
- Reaches an episode-framed peak under Philip IV.
- Fails in direct male succession after Philip IV's sons die without surviving sons.

## Evidence

- Origin and stability: [[318-hundred-years-war-a-game-of-thrones-part-1-glt5584156008]] says Hugh Capet was elected in 987 and fourteen Capetian kings followed father to son.
- Domain expansion: [[318-hundred-years-war-a-game-of-thrones-part-1-glt5584156008]] says French kings expanded royal authority by reducing Angevin power, absorbing lands after the Albigensian Crusade, and annexing Champagne and Brie.
- Southern mechanism: [[303-the-bloodiest-crusade-glt7887097153]] traces Amaury's failed inheritance, Louis VIII's intervention, the Treaty of Paris, and Toulouse's dynastic transfer to the crown.
- Peak power: [[318-hundred-years-war-a-game-of-thrones-part-1-glt5584156008]] uses Philip IV to mark French confidence and strength.
- Succession failure: [[318-hundred-years-war-a-game-of-thrones-part-1-glt5584156008]] says Philip IV's three sons do not leave surviving sons.
- War relevance: [[318-hundred-years-war-a-game-of-thrones-part-1-glt5584156008]] links the end of direct Capetian male succession to Edward III's rejected claim and Philip VI's selection.

## Qualifications

The page tracks only selected mechanisms of Capetian consolidation and succession. The crusade source does not reduce the war to royal planning: papal aims, regional rivalry, belief, and private conquest preceded direct crown control. Collateral branches, legal doctrine, and broader state formation remain incomplete.

## What Changed

- Created the Capetian Dynasty as the stable French succession line whose rupture enables the 1328 crisis.
- Added the conversion of Albigensian crusading conquest into royal territorial consolidation.
