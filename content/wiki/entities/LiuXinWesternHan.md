## Overview

刘歆 / Liu Xin (Western Han) enters the wiki as a late [[WesternHanDynasty|Western Han]] scholar-official whose importance rests on the combination of court office, inherited textual work, and system-building bibliography. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-564-gudai-xueshu-dalao-bi-xiangxiang-de-gengniu-ljf28ze9-p218vuzjicjcwutvrhl|Hanji 564]] presents him as the son and successor of [[LiuXiang|刘向]], appointed by [[HanAidi|汉哀帝]] to audit and collate texts gathered in the capital and to complete the imperial cataloging project that becomes [[Qilue|《七略》]].

## Current Profile

Hanji 564 gives Liu Xin a dual identity. Politically, he is recommended by [[WangMang|王莽]], becomes shizhong, and rises to Guanglu Dafu with imperial favor. Intellectually, he becomes the visible finisher of Liu Xiang's unfinished work: he reviews the collected texts, organizes the material, and submits Qilue to the throne.

The episode also marks the name-taboo layer. After Liu Xin's namesake, Liu Xin of Dingtao, becomes [[HanAidi|汉哀帝]], the scholar changes his name to Liu Xiu. That detail matters because it ties a bibliographic career to ordinary court rules about imperial names and rank.

Liu Xin's intellectual profile is not merely archival. Through Qilue's classification of books and its account of the nine schools, the source presents him as someone who turns scattered texts into a theory of how knowledge traditions arise, diverge, and can be recombined for governance.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han scholar-official recommended by Wang Mang and trusted by Han Aidi.
- Son and successor of Liu Xiang in the imperial text-collation project.
- Compiler who submits Qilue after auditing capital-held texts.
- Name-taboo case who changes his name to Liu Xiu after Han Aidi's accession.
- Bibliographic thinker whose school classification treats rival traditions as historically situated and politically usable.

## Qualifications

This page is source-scoped to Hanji 564. The episode summarizes Liu Xin's office, name change, and Qilue compilation, but it does not reconstruct the full catalog text, the complete mechanics of imperial library collation, Liu Xin's later career under Wang Mang, or the scholarly disputes around old-text and new-text classics. The source transcript appears to render his name as "刘兴"; the canonical entity is Liu Xin because the described biography and Qilue attribution match Liu Xin.
