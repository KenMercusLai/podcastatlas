# Great Northern War / 大北方战争

## Overview

The Great Northern War was the 1700-1721 struggle in which Sweden faced a changing coalition led by Russia, Denmark, and Saxony-Poland. In the episode, the war turns at the [[BattleOfPoltava|Battle of Poltava]] from [[CharlesXII|Charles XII]]'s attempt to break Russia into a prolonged dismantling of the [[SwedishEmpire|Swedish Empire]] and consolidation of the [[RussianEmpire|Russian Empire]] as a Baltic great power.

## Current Profile

The war begins as a test of Sweden's established military dominance against multiple rivals. Charles's 1707 Russian invasion seeks decisive victory, but logistics, winter, the turn toward Ukraine, and Russian reform erode the Swedish advantage. Poltava destroys the invading field army in 1709 and restores the anti-Swedish coalition, yet Charles's survival and refusal to cede territory keep the conflict open.

The later war spreads through Ottoman diplomacy, northern Germany, Finland, Sweden, and Norway. Peter suffers a setback against the Ottomans and temporarily gives up Azov, but Sweden cannot turn that episode into strategic recovery. Charles's death in 1718 removes the strongest barrier to settlement, and the Treaty of Nystad in 1721 transfers key Baltic territories to Russia while leaving Sweden territorially intact but no longer dominant.

## Key Characteristics

- It is a coalition war whose participants and incentives shift after battlefield signals alter expectations.
- Logistics, winter, fortified positions, state finance, and military reform matter alongside battlefield courage.
- Poltava is decisive without being terminal: the war continues for twelve more years.
- The conflict links Baltic power, Polish subordination, Ukrainian alignment, Ottoman intervention, and northern European diplomacy.
- Its settlement formalizes the movement of imperial primacy from Sweden toward Russia.

## Qualifications

This page currently rests on one episode centered on Poltava and the war's final arc, not a complete campaign history. Coalition motives, Polish and Ukrainian agency, Ottoman decision-making, civilian losses, territorial details, and counterfactual outcomes require broader evidence. The episode's statement that Poltava created Russian great-power status is best read as a hinge claim, not a single-cause explanation.
