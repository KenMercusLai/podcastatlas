## Overview
汉质帝 / Emperor Zhi of Han is the child emperor whose public naming of [[LiangJiLateHan|梁冀]] as a "跋扈将军" exposes Liang's overreach and triggers a lethal response. The current evidence now starts with [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-827-yi-ju-hua-yinfa-de-diwang-zhisi-lnf6rz1i2vmgsclgiejbh0qevzc2|Hanji 827]]'s direct poisoning narrative, then follows Hanji 828's burial and succession aftermath and Hanji 835's later cautionary interpretation.

## Current Profile
[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-827-yi-ju-hua-yinfa-de-diwang-zhisi-lnf6rz1i2vmgsclgiejbh0qevzc2|Hanji 827]] supplies the direct death scene. The episode says the child emperor sees Liang Ji's court behavior clearly, calls him "跋扈将军" in front of officials, and thereby makes his hostility visible. Liang concludes that such a perceptive emperor will become hard to control, bribes an attendant, and has poison mixed into soup cake.

The same source makes the deathbed scene politically important. Emperor Zhi asks for [[LiGuLateHan|李固]] after abdominal pain and says water might save him; Liang Ji blocks the water request, and the emperor dies soon afterward. Li Gu's grief and questioning after the death make him one of the first visible dangers to Liang's cover-up.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-828-da-jiangjun-liangji-shanquan-feili-de-donghan-jianchen-llqnqijagtmdlgitulddxspncu0e|Hanji 828]] supplies the immediate political result. After Zhi dies, Liang Ji, [[CaoTengLateHan|曹腾]], Li Gu, [[DuQiaoLateHan|杜乔]], [[LiuSuanLateHan|刘蒜]], and [[EmpressDowagerLiangNaLateHan|梁妠 / 梁太后]] are drawn into a succession contest; Zhi is buried at Jingling as Xiao Zhi emperor at age nine.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-835-donghan-diyi-quanchen-weihe-yao-gui-ta-lpwezt-z-2qloitwd3t4gyxznqbo|Hanji 835]] supplies the retrospective moral-political layer. It recalls the poisoning after the "跋扈将军" remark and uses Fan Ye and Kangxi's comments to frame Zhi's intelligence as dangerous when exposed under an overmighty powerholder, but this remains interpretation rather than proof that a child ruler had a realistic path out of Liang Ji's control.

## Key Characteristics
- Child emperor whose formal title does not protect him from the powerholder who had helped shape the throne around him.
- Dangerous truth-speaker in the source's reading: the "跋扈将军" label names Liang Ji's overreach but also exposes the emperor's hostility.
- Poisoning victim whose deathbed request for water is blocked by Liang Ji in the episode's direct narrative.
- Political trigger whose death opens the Liu Zhi versus Liu Suan succession contest.
- Posthumous reference point: buried at Jingling and remembered in Hanji 828 as Xiao Zhi emperor.
- Figure used by later interpreters to discuss exposed intelligence, patience, and self-concealment under threat.

## Evidence
- Direct trigger and poisoning: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-827-yi-ju-hua-yinfa-de-diwang-zhisi-lnf6rz1i2vmgsclgiejbh0qevzc2|Hanji 827]] says Emperor Zhi publicly calls Liang Ji "跋扈将军" and is later poisoned through soup cake; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-835-donghan-diyi-quanchen-weihe-yao-gui-ta-lpwezt-z-2qloitwd3t4gyxznqbo|Hanji 835]] recalls the same label as the remembered cause of Liang's lethal response.
- Deathbed scene and Li Gu link: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-827-yi-ju-hua-yinfa-de-diwang-zhisi-lnf6rz1i2vmgsclgiejbh0qevzc2|Hanji 827]] says Zhi asks for Li Gu, describes pain after eating soup cake, asks for water, and dies after Liang Ji blocks the request.
- Accession and death ages: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-835-donghan-diyi-quanchen-weihe-yao-gui-ta-lpwezt-z-2qloitwd3t4gyxznqbo|Hanji 835]] says Emperor Zhi took the throne at eight and was poisoned at nine after one year in power; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-828-da-jiangjun-liangji-shanquan-feili-de-donghan-jianchen-llqnqijagtmdlgitulddxspncu0e|Hanji 828]] also gives age nine at death.
- Succession aftermath: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-827-yi-ju-hua-yinfa-de-diwang-zhisi-lnf6rz1i2vmgsclgiejbh0qevzc2|Hanji 827]] closes with senior officials recommending Liu Suan; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-828-da-jiangjun-liangji-shanquan-feili-de-donghan-jianchen-llqnqijagtmdlgitulddxspncu0e|Hanji 828]] continues into Cao Teng's advice, Liang Ji's coercive meeting, and Liu Zhi's installation as Emperor Huan.
- Burial and posthumous framing: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-828-da-jiangjun-liangji-shanquan-feili-de-donghan-jianchen-llqnqijagtmdlgitulddxspncu0e|Hanji 828]] says Zhi is buried at Jingling as Xiao Zhi emperor.
- Later interpretation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-835-donghan-diyi-quanchen-weihe-yao-gui-ta-lpwezt-z-2qloitwd3t4gyxznqbo|Hanji 835]] cites Fan Ye and Kangxi to frame Zhi's intelligence as politically dangerous when prematurely exposed.

## Qualifications
This remains a bounded profile rather than a full reign narrative. Hanji 827 gives the direct poisoning account, Hanji 828 gives immediate succession and burial details, and Hanji 835 supplies retrospective moral-political interpretation. The eight-versus-nine wording across source notes is treated as a counting or narration variation unless a later source makes it analytically important.

## What Changed
- Added Hanji 827 as the direct poisoning, deathbed, and Li Gu witness layer.
- Re-anchored Hanji 828 as the succession aftermath rather than the first current evidence for Emperor Zhi's death.
- Preserved Hanji 835's concealment lesson as retrospective interpretation rather than settled causal proof.
