# Kebra Nagast

## Overview

The Kebra Nagast is the Ethiopian dynastic and religious text that links the Queen of Sheba, Solomon, Menelik, and the [[ArkOfTheCovenant|Ark of the Covenant]] to Ethiopia and the legitimacy of the Solomonic monarchy.

## Current Profile

In the episode's account, the Queen of Sheba, called Makeda, visits Solomon, adopts the worship of Israel's God, and bears Menelik. When Menelik later visits Solomon, Ethiopian companions secretly remove the Ark from the Temple, and the Archangel Michael carries the company through Egypt to Ethiopia. Menelik becomes king, and the Ark becomes both sacred inheritance and source of victory.

The text is commonly dated in this discussion to the early 14th century and interpreted as legitimation for a newly established Solomonic dynasty. The hosts also point to possible older material, including features they associate with the 6th century. The current profile therefore treats the work as a layered tradition whose political use is clear even though its date, sources, and historical Ark claim remain unresolved.

## Key Characteristics

- Joins biblical genealogy, Ethiopian Christianity, kingship, and sacred geography.
- Makes Menelik the son of Solomon and Makeda and the vehicle of the Ark's transfer.
- Legitimates the Solomonic dynasty through possession of Israelite ancestry and sacred authority.
- Combines a likely medieval political setting with claims or motifs that the episode considers potentially older.

## Evidence

- Transfer narrative: [[345-raiders-of-the-lost-ark-part-1-glt1189467433]] recounts Makeda's visit, Menelik's return to Solomon, the companions' removal of the Ark, and the journey to Ethiopia.
- Dynastic authority: [[345-raiders-of-the-lost-ark-part-1-glt1189467433]] connects the text to Ethiopian emperors and a Solomonic line ending with Haile Selassie.
- Dating debate: [[345-raiders-of-the-lost-ark-part-1-glt1189467433]] gives the early 14th century as a common theory while noting arguments for older layers.
- Eschatological frame: [[345-raiders-of-the-lost-ark-part-1-glt1189467433]] says the text expects the Ark to return to Zion only with Christ's return.

## Qualifications

This is a source-scoped profile of the text as presented in one podcast episode, not a manuscript history or independent dating study. Political utility does not prove late invention of every element, while possible older elements do not verify Menelik's genealogy or the physical Ark's transfer.
