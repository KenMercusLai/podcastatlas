# Scarlet Sunrise

## Overview
Scarlet Sunrise is a bicolor grape tomato developed at [[RutgersUniversity|Rutgers University]] and used by [[PlanetMoney]] as the central example of branded produce beyond apples.

## Current Profile
The episode frames Scarlet Sunrise as a small but revealing crop-commercialization case. Breeders crossed a sweet but fragile cherry tomato with a hardier grape tomato, selected across years for taste, appearance, firmness, plant structure, and resistance, then passed the result to Rutgers' business office for naming and trademarking. Its market promise is real but bounded: the tomato impressed a skeptical taster, yet it is still expensive to grow and may remain more specialty seed product than mass-market produce brand.

## Key Characteristics
- Bicolor grape tomato selected for sweetness, acidity, firmness, appearance, plant habit, and disease or insect resistance.
- Originated from a cross involving the sweet, crack-prone Isis Candy cherry tomato and a hardier red grape tomato.
- Became a named and trademarked Rutgers product in 2019.
- Serves as the episode's produce-aisle analogue to [[Honeycrisp]]-style branding.
- Has commercial constraints because it remains difficult or expensive to grow at scale.

## Evidence
- Breeding path: [[branded-fruit-how-produce-got-honeycrisp-ified|Branded fruit]] describes a six-year, roughly twelve-generation selection process from the tomato cross to a stable grape tomato.
- Product traits: [[branded-fruit-how-produce-got-honeycrisp-ified|Branded fruit]] emphasizes sweetness, bicolor appearance, firmness, plant structure, and resistance as simultaneous breeding targets.
- Commercial identity: [[branded-fruit-how-produce-got-honeycrisp-ified|Branded fruit]] says Rutgers' business office named, trademarked, and marketed the variety as Scarlet Sunrise in 2019.
- Market constraint: [[branded-fruit-how-produce-got-honeycrisp-ified|Branded fruit]] says the variety may remain a specialty seed product because production is expensive.

## Qualifications
Scarlet Sunrise is not presented as another Honeycrisp-scale success. The source uses it to show the mechanism of branded produce, while preserving uncertainty about whether this tomato will reach broad supermarket distribution.
