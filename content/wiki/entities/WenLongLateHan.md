## Overview

文龙 / Wen Long enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-809-weishenme-quan-ni-buyao-luan-pai-mapi-lnv2l-ehkzmf2ifz3semscaqftku|Hanji 809]] as the [[GuiyangCommanderyLateHan|桂阳郡]] administrator whose large-pearl tribute is rejected by [[EmperorShunOfHan|汉顺帝]] after the emperor publicly orders frugality and bans treasured-object offerings.

## Current Profile

Wen Long's profile is source-bounded. In 129 CE, Shun has just completed his coming-of-age capping and responds to disasters with a public frugality line: the imperial diet is reduced and local governments are told not to present珍玩 or unusual treasures. Wen Long nevertheless presents a large pearl from Guiyang.

The source frames the act as a misread hierarchy signal. Wen Long appears to offer what a local official imagines will please the throne, but Shun's public instruction has made luxury tribute politically wrong. Shun returns the pearl sealed and calls the act flattery, so Wen Long becomes an example of how status-directed gifts can fail when they contradict the ruler's declared governance posture.

## Key Characteristics

- Guiyang administrator active in Emperor Shun's early personal-rule moment.
- Local official who presents a large pearl after the court has banned treasured-object tribute.
- Negative example of flattery because the gift contradicts the ruler's public frugality signal.
