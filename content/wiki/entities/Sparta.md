# Sparta

## Overview

Sparta was an ancient Greek polity whose military citizen elite, conquest of Messenia, dependence on helot labor, and austere civic discipline produced both real regional power and a durable political myth.

## Current Profile

The episode roots Spartan power in a fertile, defensible territory and in the conquest of neighboring Messenia. Subordinated helots worked the land, while perioikoi supplied crafts and production; this division enabled full Spartan citizens to train, dine, and campaign as a warrior body rather than as part-time soldiers. [[SpartanSocialOrder]] therefore joined military excellence to institutionalized coercion.

Citizen formation reportedly began with public scrutiny at birth and continued through the agoge, mess membership, property qualification, and competitive military service. Ephors, an assembly, a council of elders, and two hereditary kings distributed authority, but equality among citizens was incomplete. Land concentration progressively reduced the number able to maintain full status.

Sparta's external power rose through Messenian conquest and leadership in Greek war, including the Persian Wars and victory over [[Athens]] in the Peloponnesian War. Its order was less adaptable as an expanding empire: a narrow citizen body had to dominate a much larger coerced population. Theban victories and the liberation of Messenia removed the labor and territorial foundation of supremacy.

The [[SpartaMirage]] makes every institutional portrait provisional. Sparse Spartan-authored evidence leaves the city filtered through admirers, critics, myth, and later political projects. [[Leonidas]], Victorian schooling, fascist admiration, and *300* each select a different version of Sparta from that unstable inheritance.

## Key Characteristics

- A defensible and fertile Greek polity whose conquest of Messenia transformed its social and military organization.
- A citizen warrior elite materially supported by helot agriculture and perioikoi production.
- Civic discipline organized through education, common messes, property qualification, competition, and military obligation.
- A mixed political structure involving ephors, assembly, gerousia, and two hereditary kings.
- Persistent insecurity produced by the domination of helots and the danger of revolt.
- A declining citizen body whose land concentration and rigidity limited lasting imperial expansion.
- An unusually powerful afterlife in heroic, educational, egalitarian, authoritarian, and popular-cultural imagination.

## Evidence

- Material and coercive foundation: [[82-sparta-glt1755395266]] connects fertile territory, Messenian conquest, helot labor, and perioikoi production to full-time citizen warfare.
- Civic institutions: [[82-sparta-glt1755395266]] describes the agoge, common messes, property qualification, ephors, assembly, gerousia, and dual kingship.
- Instability and decline: [[82-sparta-glt1755395266]] links helot fear, the post-earthquake revolt, citizen contraction, Theban victory, and Messenian liberation.
- Historical afterlife: [[82-sparta-glt1755395266]] follows Spartan imagery through Thermopylae, Roman commemoration, Victorian schools, Nazi admiration, and *300*.

## Qualifications

The profile is bounded to one conversational episode rather than a specialist reconstruction. Spartan institutions changed over time, and surviving evidence is unusually dependent on outsiders, later writers, idealization, and hostile memory. Claims about infant exposure, education, the Krypteia, gender, equality, Lycurgus, and daily life should not be treated as uniformly documented practice across all Spartan history.

## What Changed

- Established Sparta as a polity whose military reputation depended on a coercive labor system and narrowing citizen elite.
- Made source scarcity and later ideological reuse part of the profile rather than treating the legendary image as transparent evidence.
