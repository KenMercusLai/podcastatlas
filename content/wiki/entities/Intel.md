# Intel

## Overview
Intel is presented in this wiki as a strategically important U.S. semiconductor company whose role spans advanced process competition, foundry ambition, AI-era missed turns, and industrial-policy leverage.

## Current Profile
Across the bounded sources, Intel is both an anchor of U.S. semiconductor capability and a cautionary case about incumbent erosion. Earlier sources place it among the few firms still relevant to advanced process nodes and as a policy target in U.S. semiconductor rebuilding. The new All-In episode sharpens the profile by treating Intel's decline as a long-horizon technical leadership failure: missed EUV purchases, a factory pause, capital returned to shareholders, a passed iPhone chip opportunity, underestimated GPU software ecosystems, and a late response to [[TSMC]]'s foundry model.

## Key Characteristics
- Remains a strategic U.S. semiconductor asset because advanced manufacturing, packaging, foundry capacity, and supply-chain resilience depend on a small set of firms.
- Functions as a benchmark for the difficulty of maintaining process leadership when manufacturing, tool purchases, and customer trust require decade-scale technical judgment.
- Serves as a cautionary example of incumbent platform blindness toward [[Apple]]'s full-stack silicon strategy, [[Nvidia]]'s GPU/CUDA ecosystem, and [[TSMC]]'s standardized foundry model.
- Sits inside U.S. industrial policy debates where grants, tariffs, customer contracts, foundry scale, and taxpayer return are used to rebuild domestic chip capacity.
- Shows how capital allocation and leadership composition can become technical outcomes when buybacks or finance-led decisions crowd out fabs, EUV equipment, or speculative architecture bets.

## Qualifications
The Intel record is source-bounded and mixes market analysis, Chinese-language technology commentary, policy interviews, and a retrospective from former CEO [[PatGelsinger|Pat Gelsinger]]. Exact figures on buybacks, wafer-volume ratios, U.S. leading-edge production share, and fab-recovery timing are attributed claims rather than independently verified facts. The retrospective diagnosis also may underweight execution details, board incentives, customer pricing, software ecosystem inertia, macro demand, and competitors' positive capabilities.
