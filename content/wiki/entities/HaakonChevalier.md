# Haakon Chevalier

## Overview

Haakon Chevalier was the Berkeley French professor and friend of [[JRobertOppenheimer|J. Robert Oppenheimer]] whose left-wing gatherings influenced Oppenheimer's political world and whose suggestion about sharing information through a Soviet contact became the core of the Chevalier affair.

## Current Profile

The episode separates Chevalier's approach from Oppenheimer's response. Chevalier raised the possibility that wartime information might reach Soviet representatives in the name of Allied unity; Oppenheimer rejected passing secrets but failed to report the conversation promptly.

The later security problem came from both the approach and Oppenheimer's handling of it. Delayed disclosure, vagueness, and an inaccurate account gave investigators reason to doubt his candor even though the episode does not present him as accepting the proposal.

## Key Characteristics

- Berkeley academic whose social circle introduced Oppenheimer to organized left-wing discussion.
- Friend associated with the proposal to pass wartime information through Soviet channels.
- Origin point of a rejected approach that became damaging because of late and inconsistent reporting.
- Figure whose name became shorthand for the boundary between association, candor, and treachery.

## Qualifications

The source gives the conversation through a compressed secondary narrative and does not reconstruct every version, date, participant claim, or archival dispute. It supports a distinction between a reported approach and demonstrated espionage.
