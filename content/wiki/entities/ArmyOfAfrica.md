# Army of Africa

## Overview

The Army of Africa appears as Spain's colonial military formation in Morocco and as the institutional bridge between the [[RifWar]] and the [[SpanishCivilWar]].

## Current Profile

After Annual exposed the weakness of Spain's conscript army, Spain relied more heavily on Moroccan regulares commanded by Spanish officers and on its Foreign Legion. These forces offered professional cohesion and combat experience while embedding Reconquista language, racialized colonial violence, and loyalty to Africanist commanders.

The episode treats this formation as politically consequential. Officers including [[FranciscoFranco]] gained authority in Morocco, and Franco later commanded the Army of Africa when it became the strongest initial military base of the nationalist rebellion.

## Key Characteristics

- Combined Moroccan regulares under Spanish officers with the Spanish Foreign Legion.
- Developed in response to the failures and humiliations of Spain's earlier conscript campaign.
- Concentrated professional combat experience and Africanist officer identity.
- Used crusading and Reconquista imagery to frame colonial war.
- Later supplied troops, commanders, and prestige to the nationalist rebellion in Spain.

## Evidence

- Force composition: [[282-morocco-the-rif-war-glt7389759975]] describes Moroccan regulares and the Foreign Legion under José Millán Astray and Franco.
- Ideological culture: [[282-morocco-the-rif-war-glt7389759975]] connects the Legion to Reconquista and Christian crusading imagery.
- Political transfer: [[282-morocco-the-rif-war-glt7389759975]] identifies Mola, Sanjurjo, and Franco with the Moroccan officer world and makes the Army of Africa central to Franco's later rise.

## Qualifications

The page is grounded in one narrative source and does not yet provide an organizational history, distinguish every unit and command phase, or represent Moroccan soldiers' motives and experiences directly. Institutional continuity does not make the Rif War a single cause of the Civil War.

## What Changed

- Established the Army of Africa as the institutional link between colonial Morocco and Spain's later rebellion.
- Distinguished its mixed force composition from the weak conscript army defeated at Annual.
