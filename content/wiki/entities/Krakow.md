# Krakow

## Overview

Kraków is a former Polish capital whose main square and Wawel complex join medieval commerce, royal government, Catholic memory, Jewish history, occupation, and tourism.

## Current Profile

The episode presents Kraków's large medieval square, cloth hall, town-hall tower, guild culture, and routes to Kyiv, Lithuania, Hungary, Germany, and the Baltic as evidence of a commercially connected European city. Wawel adds coronations, royal burials, national poets and leaders, and the later career of Pope John Paul II.

Preserved architecture coexists with radical human loss. The episode foregrounds the city's large prewar Jewish population, synagogues, ghetto, Holocaust history, and association with Oskar Schindler and Roman Polanski. Nazi use of Wawel further turned national heritage into a theater of occupation.

## Key Characteristics

- Medieval and Hanseatic-linked commercial center organized around a monumental square.
- Former capital whose political primacy declined after the court moved to Warsaw.
- Royal and Catholic memory center anchored by Wawel.
- Major prewar Jewish city transformed by the Holocaust.
- Preserved tourist landscape whose beauty does not erase occupation and genocide.

## Qualifications

The source is a brief landmark-led account rather than a full urban history. Its square-size superlative, synagogue and population estimates, and causal description of John Paul II's 1979 visit remain source-scoped. Architectural survival must not be treated as survival of the prewar social world.
