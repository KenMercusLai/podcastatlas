## Overview

刘贺 / Liu He enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-430-chigua-qunzhong-qianren-yu-xianren-de-gongdou-daxi-lkfcjewfhmhfgw39vqfqkpa9umzq|Hanji 430]] as the former Changyi king and twenty-seven-day Western Han emperor whom [[EmperorXuanOfHan|汉宣帝]] continues to monitor after deposition. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-409-hanxuandi-liubingyi-cong-yuzhong-zouchulai-de-chuanqi-huangdi-ljyxm9nd5rvheusna29wkxuraqux|Hanji 409]] supplies the immediate deposition aftermath: [[HuoGuangWesternHan|霍光]] removes the imperial seal, sends Liu He out of the palace, lets him return to Changyi with support, and dissolves Changyi as a kingdom while most followers are punished.

## Current Profile

The episodes present Liu He less as an active plotter than as a legitimacy problem produced by failed accession. Hanji 409 shows the first containment layer: Liu He is stripped of the seal and imperial status, accepts blame for not being able to handle Han affairs, loses Changyi's royal standing, but is not killed or sent into total destitution. The punishment falls harder on his entourage, while figures with an admonition record, including [[WangShiChangyiTeacher|王式]], survive.

Hanji 430 gives the later surveillance layer. Xuan reportedly judges him "not worth worrying about," but privately orders [[ZhangChangShanyangWesternHan|张厂]] to watch his visitors, conduct, speech, body, health, wives, and children. That gap between verbal dismissal and hidden surveillance is the page's core profile: Liu He is politically weakened, but not politically irrelevant.

In 63 BCE Xuan grants him the [[HaihunMarquisate|Haihun marquisate]] while stripping away royal privileges such as ancestral-temple sacrifice and direct court audience. The settlement preserves a reduced noble status while containing the symbolic danger of a former emperor.

## Key Characteristics

- Former Western Han emperor whose twenty-seven-day reign ends in ritualized deposition and continuing succession-legitimacy residue.
- Deposed ruler punished through loss of throne, seal, kingdom, and royal privilege rather than immediate execution.
- Subject of sustained confidential surveillance under Emperor Xuan.
- Described by Zhang Chang as bodily and mentally diminished in the episode's report.
- Father of a large household that the surveillance report counts and describes.
- Reassigned into a reduced marquisate that grants status without restoring court access.

## Qualifications

This page is source-scoped to Hanji 430 and Hanji 409. It does not reconstruct Liu He's full biography, the complete Changyi accession and deposition process, archaeological evidence from the Haihun tomb, or the full Han Shu account. Hanji 409's moral reading of Liu He's downfall is preserved as episode interpretation, and Zhang Chang's "白痴儿" style assessment is treated as an official surveillance report in the episode, not as an independent medical diagnosis.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 409's immediate deposition aftermath before Hanji 430's later surveillance and Haihun settlement.
- Preserved the page's distinction between Western Han Liu He / Haihun Hou and late-Han figures with similar names.
