## Overview

刘贺 / Liu He enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-430-chigua-qunzhong-qianren-yu-xianren-de-gongdou-daxi-lkfcjewfhmhfgw39vqfqkpa9umzq|Hanji 430]] as the former Changyi king and twenty-seven-day Western Han emperor whom [[EmperorXuanOfHan|汉宣帝]] continues to monitor after deposition. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-408-feidi-jie-tianhuaban-han-feidi-liuhe-xiagang-la-lgqd0gddiz5plifsvwteaub2emug|Hanji 408]] supplies the deposition proceeding itself: [[HuoGuangWesternHan|霍光]] isolates Liu He from the Changyi followers, has the indictment read before [[ShangguanTaihouWesternHan|上官太后]] and the court, and turns alleged ritual, palace, seal, reward, and administrative disorder into the case for removal. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-409-hanxuandi-liubingyi-cong-yuzhong-zouchulai-de-chuanqi-huangdi-ljyxm9nd5rvheusna29wkxuraqux|Hanji 409]] supplies the immediate aftermath: Huo Guang removes the imperial seal, sends Liu He out of the palace, lets him return to Changyi with support, and dissolves Changyi as a kingdom while most followers are punished.

## Current Profile

The episodes present Liu He less as an active plotter than as a legitimacy problem produced by failed accession. Hanji 408 shows the accusation layer before the formal exit: the court case against him is built from alleged mourning impropriety, misuse of the imperial seal and tally, entry of Changyi followers into palace space, entertainment before Han Zhaodi's coffin, palace sexual misconduct, improper distribution of honors and goods, irregular ancestral sacrifice to his father, and 1,127 commands or requisitions in only twenty-seven days. Hanji 409 then shows the first containment layer: Liu He is stripped of the seal and imperial status, accepts blame for not being able to handle Han affairs, loses Changyi's royal standing, but is not killed or sent into total destitution. The punishment falls harder on his entourage, while figures with an admonition record, including [[WangShiChangyiTeacher|王式]], survive.

Hanji 430 gives the later surveillance layer. Xuan reportedly judges him "not worth worrying about," but privately orders [[ZhangChangShanyangWesternHan|张厂]] to watch his visitors, conduct, speech, body, health, wives, and children. That gap between verbal dismissal and hidden surveillance is the page's core profile: Liu He is politically weakened, but not politically irrelevant.

In 63 BCE Xuan grants him the [[HaihunMarquisate|Haihun marquisate]] while stripping away royal privileges such as ancestral-temple sacrifice and direct court audience. The settlement preserves a reduced noble status while containing the symbolic danger of a former emperor.

## Key Characteristics

- Former Western Han emperor whose twenty-seven-day reign is publicly recoded through an indictment of ritual, palace, and administrative disorder before continuing as succession-legitimacy residue.
- Deposed ruler punished through loss of throne, seal, kingdom, and royal privilege rather than immediate execution.
- Subject of sustained confidential surveillance under Emperor Xuan.
- Described by Zhang Chang as bodily and mentally diminished in the episode's report.
- Father of a large household that the surveillance report counts and describes.
- Reassigned into a reduced marquisate that grants status without restoring court access.

## Qualifications

This page is source-scoped to Hanji 408, Hanji 409, and Hanji 430. It does not reconstruct Liu He's full biography, the complete Changyi accession and deposition process, archaeological evidence from the Haihun tomb, or the full Han Shu account. Hanji 408's indictment is treated as the episode's presentation of Huo Guang's and the court's accusation logic, Hanji 409's moral reading of Liu He's downfall is preserved as episode interpretation, and Zhang Chang's "白痴儿" style assessment is treated as an official surveillance report in the episode, not as an independent medical diagnosis.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 408's deposition-indictment layer before Hanji 409's immediate aftermath and Hanji 430's later surveillance and Haihun settlement.
- Preserved Hanji 409's immediate post-deposition containment: seal removal, Changyi settlement, and follower punishment.
- Preserved the page's distinction between Western Han Liu He / Haihun Hou and late-Han figures with similar names.
