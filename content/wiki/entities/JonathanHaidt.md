# Jonathan Haidt

## Overview
Jonathan Haidt is a social psychologist and public advocate whose argument in [[TheAnxiousGeneration|The Anxious Generation]] connects youth mental health, phone-based childhood, adolescent development, and coordinated social-media restrictions.

## Current Profile
Across [[how-smartphones-social-media-impact-mental-health-the-realistic-solutions-dr-jonathan-haidt-scim1870541944]] and [[351-xifang-zhengfu-jinzhi-qingshaonian-shiyong-shejiao-meiti-zoudao-nabu-le-luvc8m2kuqkyv2hddfcse-6ogxym]], Haidt is presented as both a developmental diagnostician and a policy translator. His "great rewiring" account links the early-2010s move from play-based to phone-based childhood with rising adolescent distress, while emphasizing displacement of sleep, autonomy, embodied skill, courtship, and face-to-face conflict resolution alongside harmful or reinforcing content.

His four-part response is consistent across both sources: delay smartphones until high school, delay social-media accounts until 16, make schools phone-free, and restore real-world independence, free play, and responsibility. The policy source adds institutional boundaries: account creation is treated as a data contract with a platform, age checks should disclose only the minimum necessary eligibility signal, and regulation should focus on manipulative design rather than government judgment about lawful speech.

[[302-guojia-yinggai-lifa-jinzhi-qingshaonian-shiyong-shejiao-meiti-ma-lljqs1xddfaoul1cd1ejvy-i2w3d]] supplies an earlier movement-building snapshot. It presents Haidt's package as collective action rather than private abstinence and connects childhood restoration to adult modeling, accessible offline alternatives, and class inequality. Its optimistic Australian implementation claims are historical evidence about the movement's early expectations, not the current verdict on effectiveness.

## Key Characteristics
- Public advocate connecting youth mental health, smartphones, social media, and childhood independence.
- Frames digital harm as developmental displacement and medium design, not only exposure to bad content.
- Proposes age and institutional defaults because peer networks make isolated family discipline costly.
- Distinguishes restrictions on social-media accounts from restrictions on public internet access.
- Favors privacy-preserving age assurance over broad identity disclosure.
- Keeps sensitive-period, sex-difference, and causal claims qualified where the supplied evidence does not settle them.
- Treats real-world replacement activities and shared adult norms as necessary complements to device and account restrictions.

## Evidence
- Childhood diagnosis: [[how-smartphones-social-media-impact-mental-health-the-realistic-solutions-dr-jonathan-haidt-scim1870541944]] presents Haidt's shift from play-based to phone-based childhood and the associated loss of sleep, autonomy, embodied feedback, and real-world social practice.
- Developmental mechanism: [[how-smartphones-social-media-impact-mental-health-the-realistic-solutions-dr-jonathan-haidt-scim1870541944]] connects puberty, culture learning, reward reinforcement, identity formation, and superstimuli while acknowledging that parts of the account remain speculative.
- Youth policy package: both [[how-smartphones-social-media-impact-mental-health-the-realistic-solutions-dr-jonathan-haidt-scim1870541944]] and [[351-xifang-zhengfu-jinzhi-qingshaonian-shiyong-shejiao-meiti-zoudao-nabu-le-luvc8m2kuqkyv2hddfcse-6ogxym]] summarize the same four proposals: no smartphones before high school, no social-media accounts before 16, phone-free schools, and more real-world free play and responsibility.
- Account and age-assurance boundary: [[351-xifang-zhengfu-jinzhi-qingshaonian-shiyong-shejiao-meiti-zoudao-nabu-le-luvc8m2kuqkyv2hddfcse-6ogxym]] says Haidt treats youth account creation as a question of whether children can consent to complex data contracts.
- Design-versus-content boundary: [[351-xifang-zhengfu-jinzhi-qingshaonian-shiyong-shejiao-meiti-zoudao-nabu-le-luvc8m2kuqkyv2hddfcse-6ogxym]] presents Haidt as rejecting government content regulation while supporting rules against manipulative platform design for children.
- Early movement translation: [[302-guojia-yinggai-lifa-jinzhi-qingshaonian-shiyong-shejiao-meiti-ma-lljqs1xddfaoul1cd1ejvy-i2w3d]] links the four norms to Australia's first implementation phase, international political outreach, parent coordination, adult example, and unequal household access to offline substitutes.

## Qualifications
The wiki currently knows this branch through three episode summaries rather than a complete review of Haidt's scholarship or its critics. Cross-national timing, a proposed ages 9 to 15 culture-learning window, reward-driven rewiring, sex-differentiated platform attraction, and intervention effects remain source-scoped or contested. The new source's account-removal totals, low false-positive claim, youth support, and seven-part AI-era diagnosis are not independently verified. Later evidence on widespread Australian evasion means early implementation optimism cannot be treated as an effectiveness finding.

## What Changed
- Added the early movement-building bridge from the four norms to Australia's implementation and international policy outreach.
- Made adult modeling, offline substitutes, and unequal family capacity explicit parts of the intervention account.
- Reclassified encouraging Australian account-removal claims as an early snapshot qualified by later use evidence.
