## Overview

莱纳·布朗 / Reiner Braun is a warrior and infiltrator in [[AttackOnTitan|《进击的巨人》]] whose indoctrination, intimacy with designated enemies, divided identity, guilt, and search for accountability make him one of the episode's most highly regarded characters.

## Current Profile

Reiner begins inside a national story that turns the people within the Walls into devils and violence against them into duty. Long residence among them makes that abstraction untenable: comradeship and ordinary life reveal people rather than a single enemy category. His “warrior” and “soldier” selves split because he both performs the mission and belongs to the community it attacks.

The episode contrasts his response with Annie's detachment and Bertholdt's avoidance. Reiner integrates more fully into the group while continuing to demand a definite judgment and action from himself, producing the sharpest psychic conflict. This makes him a concrete case of how propaganda can enable harm and how contact can defeat dehumanization without undoing what has already been done.

His arc offers a limited answer to [[InheritedHistoricalGuilt]]: accountability begins neither with denying context nor with treating assigned identity as destiny, but with recognizing the enemy's humanity and facing one's own acts. Indoctrination explains how he entered violence; it does not erase responsibility or guarantee redemption.

## Key Characteristics

- Internalizes a national mission that casts the people inside the Walls as morally disposable enemies.
- Forms genuine bonds that make the propaganda category impossible to sustain.
- Splits between warrior duty and soldier belonging rather than resolving the contradiction cleanly.
- Carries guilt because explanation by indoctrination does not cancel participation in harm.
- Embodies recognition of shared humanity as the beginning, not completion, of accountability.

## Qualifications

The profile comes from one podcast summary rather than a complete character study. “Split personality” is retained as a narrative description used in the discussion, not a clinical diagnosis. The degree of indoctrination, voluntariness, guilt, redemption, and comparison with Annie or Bertholdt remains interpretive; recognition and suffering do not compensate victims or by themselves complete repair.

- Treated human recognition as necessary but insufficient for redemption.
- Preserved causal explanation without converting it into moral acquittal.
