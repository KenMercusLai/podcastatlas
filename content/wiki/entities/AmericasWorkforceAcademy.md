# America's Workforce Academy

## Overview

America's Workforce Academy is the paid skilled-trades training program [[DinaPowellMcCormick|Dina Powell McCormick]] describes as [[Meta]]'s response to fiber-technician and related labor shortages at data-center sites. The name is deliberately broader than Meta because the source says the program is intended for an industry alliance rather than one employer alone.

## Current Profile

In the bounded source, the academy is a five-week fast-track program combining safety training, JobSiteReady certification, full wage replacement during training, and a guaranteed job at a Meta data-center site after graduation. Its design addresses two barriers simultaneously: employers cannot find enough trained workers, while potential trainees living paycheck to paycheck cannot risk unpaid training without a clear job at the end.

Powell McCormick reports 40,000 applicants, 250 graduates, and 90 percent retention after roughly four months. She also describes partnerships with CBRE, building-trades unions, the Association of Builders and Contractors, community colleges, VFW halls, and a wider alliance convened by [[RuthPorat|Ruth Porat]] with participation from Meta and [[BlackRock]]. These figures and arrangements remain source-attributed.

## Key Characteristics

- Pays trainees what they would earn on the job rather than requiring unpaid or low-paid preparation.
- Combines short-cycle safety and work-readiness credentials with a guaranteed employment destination.
- Targets fiber, welding, electrical, construction, and adjacent roles needed for data-center expansion.
- Uses an employer-neutral name and distributed training venues to support cross-company scaling.
- Treats access and retention, not only curriculum, as workforce-pipeline design problems.

## Qualifications

The bounded source provides company-reported figures rather than an independent evaluation. It does not define the retention window, applicant selection rate, total program cost, credential portability, wage distribution, geographic coverage, or how many graduates would remain employed after construction demand changes. A Meta-site job guarantee also demonstrates a hiring pipeline more directly than it demonstrates a durable, employer-independent career ladder.

## What Changed

- Established the academy as a canonical workforce project.
- Identified paid training plus job certainty as its central access mechanism.
- Preserved measurement gaps around scale, selection, portability, cost, and long-term retention.
