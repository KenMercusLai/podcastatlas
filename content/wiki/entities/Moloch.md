# Moloch

## Overview

Moloch is a disputed ancient Levantine religious term or figure associated in biblical and later reception with child sacrifice, fire, and extreme cruelty.

## Current Profile

[[58-the-world-cup-of-gods-part-1-glt8884399326]] keeps Moloch's identity unresolved. Levitical prohibition and later literary tradition can be read as referring to a deity, but an alternative interpretation treats *molek* as a type of sacrifice or ritual rather than a divine proper name. The episode places this ambiguity beside Gehenna, tophets, Baal language, classical accusations against Carthage, and archaeological evidence.

The profile therefore separates three layers: ancient ritual vocabulary, disputed sacrificial practice, and the later demonic image. [[CarthaginianChildSacrificeEvidence]] makes a propaganda-only dismissal difficult, but neither burned remains nor hostile texts independently establish one uniform theology, one divine recipient, or the exact meaning of Moloch. Milton's literary reuse then helps make Moloch a durable emblem of monstrous worship beyond what the ancient evidence alone can support.

## Key Characteristics

- Ambiguous identity that may designate a deity, an offering category, or a sacrificial rite.
- Strong association with biblical prohibitions and later child-sacrifice memory.
- Entangled with contested Carthaginian and Phoenician evidence rather than securely defined by it.
- Later transformed into a literary and cultural emblem of destructive power.

## Qualifications

The episode is a rapid comparative discussion, not a full philological or archaeological treatment. It does not settle the word's grammar, the relationship among Israelite, Canaanite, Phoenician, and Carthaginian practices, the frequency or institutional form of sacrifice, or whether all evidence concerns the same ritual complex. Comparisons with Abraham and Isaac or Christian sacrifice are thematic and do not prove transmission.
