# Paul Ekman

## Overview
Paul Ekman is referenced in the Huberman Lab emotion episode as the influential facial-expression researcher whose basic-emotion work Adolphs both acknowledges and qualifies.

## Current Profile
The source places Ekman in the history of emotion-recognition research. Adolphs treats the basic-expression tradition as highly influential, but uses it to show why posed faces and forced-choice labels can make real social emotion reading look easier than it is.

## Key Characteristics
- He is associated in the source with basic-emotion facial-expression research.
- His work is treated as influential for emotion science.
- The episode uses his legacy to introduce limits of static, posed, label-driven emotion inference.

## Evidence
- Influence - [[neuroscience-of-emotions-tools-for-improving-emotion-regulation-dr-ralph-adolphs-scim1222961716]] says Ekman's basic-emotion facial-expression work had major influence.
- Qualification - [[neuroscience-of-emotions-tools-for-improving-emotion-regulation-dr-ralph-adolphs-scim1222961716]] argues that posed expressions and forced-choice labels inflate apparent accuracy compared with real-world social reading.

## Qualifications
This page records the source's treatment of Ekman's work. It does not adjudicate the full facial-expression debate.
