# Paul Ekman

## Overview
Paul Ekman is a facial-expression researcher represented here through the influence of his basic-emotion work, its scientific qualifications, and its later use as inspiration for the fictional perception premise in [[BlinkFilm|Blink]].

## Current Profile
The Adolphs source places Ekman in the history of emotion-recognition research. It treats the basic-expression tradition as highly influential, but uses it to show why posed faces and forced-choice labels can make real social emotion reading look easier than it is.

[[blink-with-stephen-gaghan-development-hell-blink-with-stephen-gaghan-development-hell|Blink]] shows a separate cultural afterlife: [[StephenGaghan]] and [[MalcolmGladwell]] turned research associated with Ekman into a protagonist who can reliably identify lies and feelings. That dramatic premise extends far beyond what the bounded scientific source supports and should remain classified as fiction rather than evidence for dependable deception detection.

## Key Characteristics
- He is associated in the source with basic-emotion facial-expression research.
- His work is treated as influential for emotion science.
- The episode uses his legacy to introduce limits of static, posed, label-driven emotion inference.
- His research also supplies cultural material for fictional stories of exceptional truth and emotion detection.

## Qualifications
This page records two sources' treatment of Ekman's work and does not adjudicate the full facial-expression or deception-detection literature. The *Blink* episode reports artistic inspiration, not validation of Teddy's capacity. Neither bounded source supplies Ekman's own account, complete methods, or the wider scholarly debate.
