# 黑山军 / Heishan Army

## Overview
黑山军 / Heishan Army is the late-Han mountain-force network centered in the current wiki on [[ZhangYanLateHan|张燕]], [[YuDuLateHan|于毒]], and related armed groups. Across the source notes it functions as insurgent disorder, court-recognized semi-administration, warlord resource, threat to [[YuanShao|袁绍]]'s base, and finally a remaining mountain asset that Zhang Yan can bring toward [[CaoCao|曹操]].

## Current Profile
The earliest layers make the Black Mountain field a product of late-Han disorder after [[ZhangJiaoLateHan|张角]]'s uprising. Hanji 932 names [[ZhangNiujiaoLateHan|张牛角]] and Chu Feiyan / Zhang Yan in the armed field, while Hanji 933 explains the transition from Zhang Niujiao and Chu Yan into Zhang Yan's large northern mountain network. The court cannot suppress it directly, so Zhang Yan receives office and delegated order functions.

The group then appears as a recurring pressure field rather than a single army with one fixed campaign. Hanji 941 shows [[WangFenLateHan|王芬]] using Black Mountain attacks as a mobilization pretext for a failed imperial-seizure plot. Hanji 957 and Hanji 961 put [[YuDuLateHan|于毒]], [[BaiRaoLateHan|白绕]], and [[SuiGuLateHan|眭固]] in the East Commandery pressure that helps Cao Cao gain and defend an early foothold. Hanji 969 shows Black Mountain remnants supporting [[YuanShu|袁术]] while Cao Cao attacks him.

Hanji 970 gives the strongest Yuan Shao confrontation. Wei Commandery soldiers join Yu Du's Black Mountain-linked force, attack [[YeLateHan|邺城]], and create family-security panic before [[TaoShengLateHan|陶生]] protects Yuan Shao's household. Yuan Shao then attacks Yu Du, Zhang Yan, and other mountain leaders, temporarily cooperating with [[LyuBu|吕布]] against Zhang Yan.

Hanji 1030 adds a late external-help role: [[GongsunZan|公孙瓒]] seeks Black Mountain support from [[YijingFortress|易京]] after his own rescue network fails. Hanji 1062 then closes Zhang Yan's current arc by making the Black Mountain base a preserved bargaining asset. The host emphasizes mountain protection, farming-fighting durability, and Zhang Yan's effort to remain both hard to destroy and worth incorporating when Cao Cao becomes the stronger patron.

## Key Characteristics
- Late-Han armed mountain network rooted in Yellow Turban aftermath and Zhang Yan's consolidation.
- Semi-legal order actor after the Han court grants office to a force it cannot suppress.
- Recurring military pressure field around Jizhou, Dong Commandery, Ye, and northern campaigns.
- Flexible resource in warlord competition, appearing as threat, ally, pretext, or hoped-for rescue depending on the actor using it.
- Terrain-backed survival structure whose mountain base gives leaders bargaining value across regime shifts.
- Source-scoped organization rather than a fully reconstructed institution; different episodes foreground different leaders and fragments.

## Qualifications
The page is a synthesis of episodic source notes, not a full institutional history. Black Mountain appears under different names, fragments, leaders, and political uses; the wiki should not flatten every occurrence into Zhang Yan's direct command. Hanji 1062's farming-fighting account and Taihang-base interpretation strengthen the terrain-backed survival picture but remain source-scoped where exact organization, troop counts, geography, and titles are uncertain.
