# Argonautica

## Overview

The *Argonautica* is [[ApolloniusOfRhodes|Apollonius of Rhodes]]' Hellenistic epic about [[Jason]], the Argonauts, [[MedeaCharacter|Medea]], and the quest for the [[GoldenFleece|Golden Fleece]].

## Current Profile

[[605-greek-myths-jason-the-quest-for-the-golden-fleece-part-4-glt3067620137]] presents the poem as an adventure epic written around 250 BCE but built from much older material. Jason's tests include yoking fire-breathing bulls and sowing dragon's teeth; Medea supplies magical protection and the strategy that turns the earth-born warriors against one another.

The poem is also a learned map of Greek literary memory. It places figures and motifs known from Homer, Hesiod, Pindar, Euripides, the Cadmus cycle, and the *Odyssey* inside one voyage, while its unusual return route relocates wonder beyond increasingly familiar seas. That method makes it a central case for [[MythToMythologyTransition]] without reducing it to a catalogue: recognition, love, danger, travel, and suspense still drive the story.

## Key Characteristics

- Quest epic centered on obtaining the Golden Fleece from Colchis.
- Literary prehistory that places older heroic and Homeric materials before the Trojan War generation.
- Story in which Medea's magic and counsel are necessary to Jason's success.
- Learned recombination of variant myths, recognizable characters, and inherited places.
- Hellenistic relocation of marvels in response to expanding geographical knowledge.
- Ancient source for major set pieces adapted by the 1963 *Jason and the Argonauts* film.
