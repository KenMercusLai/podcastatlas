# Sir John Oldcastle

## Overview

Sir John Oldcastle was [[HenryVOfEngland|Prince Henry's]] soldier-friend who later became a [[Lollardy|Lollard]] rebel and an early namesake for Shakespeare's [[Falstaff]].

## Current Profile

The episode uses Oldcastle to separate theatrical character from historical person. He was a comrade-in-arms of Prince Henry rather than a cowardly tavern companion, and his later rebellion and execution made him a politically and religiously charged name. Pressure associated with his descendants helped prompt Shakespeare to replace the character name Oldcastle with Falstaff.

Oldcastle also complicates Henry's religious development. Friendship with a prominent Lollard does not prove that Henry shared Oldcastle's beliefs, but it places the prince's later orthodoxy beside earlier Lancastrian and personal contact with Wycliffite reform currents.

## Key Characteristics

- Soldier and personal associate of Prince Henry.
- Adherent associated with Lollard religious dissent.
- Later rebel against Henry V.
- Executed through hanging and burning.
- Historical name initially attached to Shakespeare's comic knight.
- Evidence of proximity between Lancastrian circles and reformist networks, not proof of shared belief.

## Evidence

- Military and personal relationship: [[486-henry-iv-warrior-princes-and-fat-knights-part-2-glt8391310611]] describes Oldcastle as Henry's friend and comrade-in-arms.
- Religious and political rupture: [[486-henry-iv-warrior-princes-and-fat-knights-part-2-glt8391310611]] links him to Lollardy, rebellion, and execution under Henry V.
- Theatrical afterlife: [[486-henry-iv-warrior-princes-and-fat-knights-part-2-glt8391310611]] says Shakespeare's character was first called Oldcastle before being renamed.

## Qualifications

The source does not give a full account of Oldcastle's theology, trial, revolt, descendants, or the precise mechanism behind Shakespeare's name change. His friendship with Henry supports religious complexity in the prince's network but cannot establish Henry's private doctrine.

## What Changed

- Established Oldcastle as the historical link among Henry's military circle, Lollard rebellion, and Falstaff's naming history.
