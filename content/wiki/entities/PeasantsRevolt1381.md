# Peasants’ Revolt of 1381

## Overview

The Peasants’ Revolt of 1381 was a coordinated English rising against servitude, taxation, labor controls, legal and ecclesiastical power, corrupt officials, and economic privilege whose regional origins and London climax joined radical demands to loyalty toward [[RichardIIOfEngland|Richard II]].

## Current Profile

The sources reject a simple picture of the poorest peasants spontaneously confronting nobles. Post-Black Death labor scarcity raised wages and aspirations, while the Statute of Labourers, serfdom, manorial dues, and legal records attempted to preserve older controls. The third [[PollTax|poll tax]] supplied the immediate trigger, but the movement grew most strongly in commercially connected Kent, Essex, Suffolk, and the Home Counties among mobile and assertive rural communities as well as poorer laborers.

Resistance at Brentwood spread through horse-borne organizers, village networks, and the Bocking oath before rebels captured strongpoints in Rochester and Canterbury and converged on London. There a divided city widened the coalition to townspeople, merchants, political rivals, prisoners, religious radicals, and opportunists. Rebels attacked prisons, Lambeth Palace, the Savoy, the Hospitallers, the Tower, monopolists, foreigners, and above all the documents that made obligations enforceable.

The program was similarly composite. Mile End demands included the end of serfdom, fixed rents, freedom from forced labor, removal of supposed traitors, and abolition of monopolies, tolls, and privileges. [[WatTyler|Tyler’s]] later Smithfield demands extended toward free hunting, the destruction of manorial jurisdiction, and a reduced church hierarchy. Yet this social and religious radicalism coexisted with [[RebelRoyalism|royalism]]: rebels asked the king to free them from intermediate lords rather than to abolish monarchy.

The London phase ended after [[WilliamWalworth]] struck Tyler and Richard claimed leadership of the armed crowd, drawing it toward Clerkenwell until loyal forces surrounded it. Suppression and executions followed elsewhere, and the king eventually revoked his concessions. The movement failed to achieve its stated institutional program, but poll taxes vanished for centuries and the rising became both a warning about popular limits and a contested symbol of later radical traditions.

## Key Characteristics

- Networked multi-regional rising driven by poll-tax enforcement, post-plague labor conflict, servitude, lordship, official corruption, and economic restrictions.
- Royalist revolt that attacked ministers and intermediaries while affirming the young king’s legitimacy.
- Coalition combining rural grievance, religious radicalism, London rivalries, elite opportunism, and prison-breaking.
- Movement that targeted records and property as instruments of authority rather than treating destruction as simple looting.
- Socially mixed movement in which frustrated aspiration and mobility mattered alongside poverty and coercion.
- Violent uprising that included executions, xenophobic killing, sanctuary violations, and disputed acts of assistance.
- Immediate political failure with a narrower durable legacy in taxation and institutional memory.

## Qualifications

The sources cover the revolt's origins, southeastern escalation, London climax, and suppression but do not supply a complete national or archival history. Exact social composition, the degree of preplanning, reported oaths and speeches, insider assistance at London Bridge and the Tower, Richard’s sincerity, the intent behind Tyler’s death, and direct Wycliffite influence remain uncertain. Later readings of the revolt as progressive, Protestant, American, libertarian, or Thatcherite are retrospective interpretations and do not resolve its ideological mixture or violence.

## What Changed

- Added the failed-war, poll-tax, post-plague labor, and manorial-control causes beneath the London crisis.
- Reframed the rising as an organized movement of frustrated aspiration as well as deprivation.
- Extended the narrative backward through Brentwood, Bocking, Rochester, and Canterbury.
