# M*A*S*H

## Overview

*M*A*S*H* was a long-running American television series set during the Korean War and used by the bounded source to examine how universal anti-war storytelling can obscure historically specific responsibility.

## Current Profile

[[goodbye-farewell-and-amen-goodbye-farewell-and-amen]] treats the series as a major American memory of the Korean War. Its 1983 finale, “Goodbye, Farewell, and Amen,” uses an incendiary attack to force the medical unit's evacuation but leaves the attacking side unstated.

[[MalcolmGladwell]] argues that the ambiguity reverses the historical position of Americans from users of napalm into apparent victims. Alan Alda's reported explanation is different: the fire entered the story after a real wildfire destroyed the set, and identifying a side was unnecessary to the show's anti-war purpose. The case therefore preserves a genuine tension between universal moral messaging and assignment of historical responsibility.

## Key Characteristics

- Korean War-set television series that became a durable American cultural memory.
- Eleven-season program whose finale drew a reported audience of 106 million.
- Used an incendiary attack in its finale without identifying the attacking force.
- Pursued a universal anti-war message that the episode argues weakened specific accountability.
