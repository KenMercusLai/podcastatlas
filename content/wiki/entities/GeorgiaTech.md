# Georgia Tech

## Overview

Georgia Tech is an Atlanta university presented through a sharp historical contrast: its segregated handling of a visiting professor in 1959 and its later efforts to widen access to computer-science and engineering education.

## Current Profile

[[return-to-the-404-return-to-the-404|Return to the 404]] recounts how administrators, responding to Graham Gladwell's request to use the library, telephoned to determine whether a professor from the University of the West Indies was white. The story uses his eventual visit and photograph of his Black wife, Joyce, to make the institution's racial boundary concrete.

Six decades later, [[CharlesIsbell]] describes a different institutional direction: a lower-cost online computer-science master's, recruitment of talent in sub-Saharan Africa, substantial enrollment growth, and strong representation in engineering education. The contrast supports a judgment of meaningful change, but the episode does not treat one program or university record as evidence that wider racial inequity has ended.

## Key Characteristics

- Historically segregated institution whose 1959 visitor screening made race a condition of academic access.
- Large-scale provider of online graduate computer-science education at a reported price far below the campus program.
- Institution presented as actively recruiting geographically and demographically underrepresented technical talent.
- Case in which real institutional transformation coexists with unresolved social racism and incomplete outcome evidence.

## Evidence

### Expanded computing education

- [[return-to-the-404-return-to-the-404]] reports Isbell's description of the online master's scale, price, academic equivalence, and sub-Saharan African outreach.

### Representation and remaining limits

- [[return-to-the-404-return-to-the-404]] combines claims about educating Black, Hispanic, and Asian engineers with Isbell's experiences of continuing racism.

## Qualifications

The source provides a retrospective family anecdote and an interview with a university dean, not archival segregation policy, audited enrollment and completion data, comparative learning outcomes, or an independent equity assessment. Reported price and participation describe access conditions but do not by themselves establish equal support, completion, career outcomes, or institutional transformation across every domain.

## What Changed

- Established Georgia Tech as a bounded case of movement from racial exclusion toward wider technical-education access.
- Kept program reach and demographic representation distinct from proof of complete racial equality.
