# Kate Sweeny

## Overview

Kate Sweeny is a psychologist represented in the supplied source through research on waiting, uncertainty, and the emotional forms relief can take when danger passes or a prolonged ordeal ends.

## Current Profile

In [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-fb27a2f1edb36120fa77e5b7b54ac6de-51580587-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-fb27a2f1edb36120fa77e5b7b54ac6de-51580587|Waiting Games]], Sweeny connects personal experiences with reported experiments and studies. Her model distinguishes high-arousal near-miss relief from exhausted task-completion relief, describes waiting as especially difficult at its beginning and end, and treats uncertainty plus low control as a particularly stressful combination.

Her practical approach preserves optimism for much of a wait while adding late-stage preparation, contingency planning, possible silver linings, and flow-producing activity. The framework does not promise control over the result; it reallocates attention toward emotional preparation and the limited actions still available.

## Key Characteristics

- Studies the emotional experience of waiting for consequential outcomes.
- Distinguishes near-miss relief from relief after completing a sustained ordeal.
- Treats optimism and pessimism as strategies whose value changes across the waiting timeline.
- Uses contingency planning and anticipated silver linings to restore limited agency under uncertainty.
- Presents flow as a particularly useful response to open-ended waiting.
