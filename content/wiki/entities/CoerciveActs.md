# Coercive Acts

## Overview

The Coercive Acts, called the Intolerable Acts by American colonists, were Britain's punitive 1774 response to the [[BostonTeaParty|Boston Tea Party]].

## Current Profile

The measures were intended to restore authority in Massachusetts but instead helped turn a local confrontation into an intercolonial constitutional crisis. The episode highlights the closing of Boston's port, the overriding of the Massachusetts Bay Charter, tighter control of town meetings, and changed legal arrangements for royal officials. Together these measures made compromise harder and encouraged coordinated resistance through the [[ContinentalCongress|Continental Congress]].

## Key Characteristics

- Punitive legislative response to destroyed tea.
- Concentrated coercive pressure on Massachusetts and Boston.
- Reduced charter self-government and local meeting autonomy.
- Joined commercial punishment to constitutional control.
- Helped unite colonial resistance rather than isolate Massachusetts.

## Evidence

- Legislative scope: [[348-the-boston-tea-party-part-2-glt5189620267]] identifies port closure, charter override, town-meeting limits, and changed legal protections as central measures.
- Escalation effect: [[348-the-boston-tea-party-part-2-glt5189620267]] argues that the response broadened rather than contained the crisis.
- Coordinating response: [[348-the-boston-tea-party-part-2-glt5189620267]] links the acts to the First Continental Congress and Suffolk Resolves.

## Qualifications

The source summarizes a legislative package rather than analyzing each statute separately. Its claim that Britain overreacted is an interpretation of political effect, not evidence that London regarded punishment as irrational or that every colony responded identically.

## What Changed

- Created the legislation as the punishment-to-coordination hinge in the revolutionary sequence.
