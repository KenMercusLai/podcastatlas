# Agojie

## Overview

The Agojie are the Dahomey women warriors the episode uses to examine female military power, European fascination, spectacle, and slavery controversy.

## Current Profile

The source starts and ends with the Agojie. They are described through harsh training, executions, reported battlefield rituals, and European accounts that often praised their bravery and effectiveness compared with male soldiers. The episode says they began largely as the king's palace bodyguard and became a more formal army unit under [[Ghezo]]. It also qualifies the record: violence may be real, staged, exaggerated, or observer-shaped, and captives taken in Dahomey wars could be retained as serfs or sold to Europeans.

## Key Characteristics

- Female warrior regiment associated with [[Dahomey]].
- Initially connected to palace protection in the episode's account.
- Formalized as a fighting unit under [[Ghezo]].
- Known through European reports mixing fear, admiration, spectacle, and possible exaggeration.
- Central to the [[TheWomanKing]] controversy and modern memory of "African Amazons."
- Key case for distinguishing [[AfricanFemaleAuthorityInstitutions]] from [[EuropeanAmazonProjection]].

## Evidence

- Opening description: [[317-african-amazons-glt5973082485]] opens with a vivid description of the Agojie, including harsh training, executions, and reported battlefield rituals.
- Palace origin: [[317-african-amazons-glt5973082485]] says Dahomey's women warriors began largely as the king's palace bodyguard.
- Naming: [[317-african-amazons-glt5973082485]] says names included Ahose or Ahosi, meaning king's wives, and Mino, meaning our mothers.
- Formalization and numbers: [[317-african-amazons-glt5973082485]] says they became more formal under [[Ghezo]] and reportedly grew from about 800 to around 3,000.
- Source caution: [[317-african-amazons-glt5973082485]] says violent demonstrations may have been staged for European observers and that European descriptions were shaped by fear and fascination.

## Qualifications

The current profile treats violent and numerical details as source-scoped. The source itself does not decide how much of the reported ritual violence was battlefield fact, court spectacle, propaganda, or European exaggeration.

## What Changed

- Created the Agojie as the episode's primary Dahomey women-warrior institution.
