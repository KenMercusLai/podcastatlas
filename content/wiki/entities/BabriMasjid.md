# Babri Masjid

## Overview

The Babri Masjid was a mosque in Ayodhya whose disputed relationship to an earlier temple and to the claimed birthplace of Rama made it a central site of Indian religious and political conflict.

## Current Profile

[[110-history-of-india-in-10-buildings-glt7972990176]] presents the mosque as allegedly built by Babur on an older temple site and records its demolition by Hindu right-wing groups in 1992. The episode treats that destruction as a national political rupture: control over a building site became control over historical legitimacy, challenged the idea of secular India, and culminated in [[NarendraModi]] laying a foundation stone for a new Rama temple.

## Key Characteristics

- A mosque associated in tradition with the Mughal founder Babur.
- Located at a site claimed by Hindu groups as Rama's birthplace and as an earlier temple location.
- Demolished by Hindu right-wing mobilization in 1992.
- A focal point where archaeology, devotion, law, nationalism, and public memory became politically inseparable.
- Replaced within a new temple project supported at the highest level of Indian government.

## Evidence

- Origin dispute: [[110-history-of-india-in-10-buildings-glt7972990176]] describes the mosque as allegedly built by Babur on an older temple site.
- Demolition: [[110-history-of-india-in-10-buildings-glt7972990176]] attributes the 1992 destruction to Hindu right-wing groups who identified the place with Rama's birthplace.
- Political transformation: [[110-history-of-india-in-10-buildings-glt7972990176]] connects the demolition to a challenge to secular India and notes Modi's role in the new temple foundation.

## Qualifications

The source note compresses a long legal, archaeological, religious, and political dispute. “Allegedly” is retained because the episode summary does not establish the older-site claim, and the page does not independently adjudicate competing title, excavation, or devotional claims.

## What Changed

- Established Babri Masjid as a built-heritage case where demolition changed national politics as well as a physical site.
- Preserved the evidentiary distinction between a reported origin claim and the documented political power of that claim.
