# Franco-American Alliance of 1778

## Overview

The Franco-American Alliance of 1778 is the treaty relationship that turned the [[AmericanRevolution|American Revolution]] from colonial rebellion into a global war Britain had to reprioritize.

## Current Profile

The sources present the alliance as a staged and transformative development. France secretly supplied the Americans before formal entry, including support associated with Beaumarchais and covert royal funding. The [[BattleOfSaratoga|Saratoga]] victory made the rebellion look viable, while [[BenjaminFranklin|Benjamin Franklin]] made independence politically attractive in France. Formal entry in 1778 changed British priorities; the closing episode completes the chain at [[Yorktown]], where French troops helped trap Cornwallis and French sea power prevented relief. The alliance therefore joins covert aid, diplomatic persuasion, endurance, and coalition warfare inside [[AmericanRevolutionGlobalWar|a global war]].

## Key Characteristics

- Diplomatic objective of Franklin's mission to France.
- Military and financial backing relationship for American independence.
- Product of strategic opportunity as well as Franklin's personal popularity.
- Internationalized the [[AmericanRevolution|American Revolution]] and weakened Britain's position.
- Strategic turning point for [[GeorgeWashington|George Washington]]'s army after the Valley Forge winter.
- Operational coalition whose land and naval power was decisive at Yorktown.
- Preceded by covert French material and financial support before open war.

## Evidence

- Mission objective: [[684-franklin-revenge-of-the-american-genius-part-2-glt6013835340]] says Franklin's mission in Paris was to secure French military and financial backing for American independence.
- French hesitation: [[684-franklin-revenge-of-the-american-genius-part-2-glt6013835340]] says France was indebted, not yet at war with Britain, and widely expected Britain to win.
- Franklin's leverage: [[684-franklin-revenge-of-the-american-genius-part-2-glt6013835340]] says Franklin's popularity at Versailles and in French public opinion positioned him to exploit the changing war.
- Valley Forge timing: [[683-washington-hero-of-the-revolution-part-1-glt3752138067]] says news reached Valley Forge that Franklin had secured French recognition of the American Republic and a military alliance at Versailles.
- War effect: [[684-franklin-revenge-of-the-american-genius-part-2-glt6013835340]] says France entered the war and tipped the balance in America's favor, while [[683-washington-hero-of-the-revolution-part-1-glt3752138067]] says war between Britain and France globalized the conflict and changed British priorities toward the Caribbean.
- Yorktown effect: [[350-the-triumph-of-george-washington-part-4-glt1619660676]] says French land forces joined Washington's encirclement while the French fleet prevented British relief.
- Covert-to-open sequence: [[349-the-birth-of-the-united-states-part-3-glt9804967639]] describes secret French supply and royal funding before formal entry in February 1778.
- Saratoga signal: [[349-the-birth-of-the-united-states-part-3-glt9804967639]] connects Burgoyne's surrender to European confidence that the American rebellion was worth backing.

## Qualifications

The sources establish covert aid, diplomatic formation, strategic internationalization, and Yorktown effect but do not independently evaluate all French motives, financial flows, naval operations, troop contributions, or alliance tensions. The exact covert funding and Beaumarchais mechanisms remain source-scoped. France's war costs are presented as contributing to later fiscal crisis, not as a complete causal explanation of the French Revolution.

## What Changed

- Added covert pre-alliance support and Saratoga as the credibility signal before formal French entry.
