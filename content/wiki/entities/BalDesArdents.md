# Bal des Ardents

## Overview

The Bal des Ardents was a January 1393 French court entertainment in which flammable wild-man costumes caught fire, killing several dancers and intensifying anxiety around [[CharlesVIOfFrance|Charles VI]] and his court.

## Current Profile

[[154-the-most-disastrous-party-in-history-glt6506112996]] places the masquerade after Charles's earlier mental collapse, when entertainment was meant to improve his spirits. Charles and five companions wore pitch-covered costumes decorated with branches and hair. When a torch carried by the Duke of Orléans ignited them, several men burned to death; Charles survived after his aunt shielded him with her dress.

## Key Characteristics

- Court entertainment organized partly around a vulnerable king's recovery.
- Costume design that turned proximity to flame into lethal risk.
- Disaster that made royal instability visible through spectacle.
- Survival story centered on rapid protection by Charles's aunt.
