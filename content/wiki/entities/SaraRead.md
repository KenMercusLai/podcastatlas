# Sara Read

## Overview

Sara Read is a literary historian specializing in early-modern culture, women's bodies, and reproductive health who appears as the guest in [[191-childbirth-glt5119375504|191. Childbirth]].

## Current Profile

Read approaches [[ChildbirthHistory|childbirth history]] by reading prescriptive medical literature against household practice and women's lived experience. She uses manuals, oaths, parish structures, letters, diaries, poetry, and later cultural representations to recover birth as a social and religious event rather than only a medical procedure.

Her interpretation emphasizes evidence discipline. She distinguishes prescription from practice, treats the Agnodice story as probably mythical, rejects the generic “midwife as witch” story, and resists attributing supine birth practice to Louis XIV. At the same time, she argues that [[EarlyModernMidwifery]] combined practical apprenticeship with theoretical knowledge and that [[MedicalizationOfChildbirth|medicalisation]] changed both safety and authority.

## Key Characteristics

- Literary historian working at the intersection of early-modern culture, medicine, and women's reproductive experience.
- Reads male-authored medical texts critically because they can obscure female practical authority.
- Reconstructs childbirth through household, parish, religious, and community institutions.
- Uses popular myths as opportunities to separate evidence from retrospective storytelling.
- Frames birth through bodily continuity and cultural change rather than a simple progress narrative.
