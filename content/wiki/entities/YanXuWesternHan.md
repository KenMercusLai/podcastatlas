## Overview

严徐 / Yan Xu is the [[WesternHanDynasty|Western Han]] Yingchuan governor used in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-619-fumuguan-zhege-ci-shi-zenme-laide-lmluscbtl3faczssz5didybawa1r|Hanji 619]] to explain why a good local official could be remembered as a "父母官." The source presents him as a filial, restrained, self-reflective administrator whose transfer from [[YingchuanCommandery|颍川郡]] makes local people vulnerable to harsher rule.

## Current Profile

Yan Xu's profile is built around moral conduct rather than policy detail. He enters office through filial reputation, treats subordinates as teachers or friends, responds to their faults by examining himself, and is remembered for never speaking loudly. The episode treats these traits as evidence that local authority can be judged through self-command and respect for those below.

The strongest scene is his departure from Yingchuan. When [[WangMang|王莽]] has him brought to the capital, hundreds of commandery officials hold a farewell banquet. Yan Xu sits on the ground and weeps, explaining that he is grieving for Yingchuan's people because a severe replacement may kill without restraint. [[HeBingWesternHan|何病]]'s later executions make the warning concrete.

## Key Characteristics

- Filial-reputation entrant into official service.
- Restrained local official who treats subordinates with unusual respect.
- Self-blaming superior who reads subordinate error as a prompt for his own reflection.
- "父母官" exemplar whose concern is the people's safety rather than personal promotion.
- Transfer-risk figure whose departure exposes how much local order can depend on appointment fit.

## Evidence

- Filial and restrained conduct: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-619-fumuguan-zhege-ci-shi-zenme-laide-lmluscbtl3faczssz5didybawa1r|Hanji 619]] says Yan Xu was recommended for filial conduct, treated officials as teachers or friends, reflected on himself after subordinate errors, and never spoke loudly.
- People-first farewell: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-619-fumuguan-zhege-ci-shi-zenme-laide-lmluscbtl3faczssz5didybawa1r|Hanji 619]] says Yan Xu cried at the farewell banquet because he feared what would happen to Yingchuan's people after his transfer.
- Replacement contrast: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-619-fumuguan-zhege-ci-shi-zenme-laide-lmluscbtl3faczssz5didybawa1r|Hanji 619]] says He Bing soon replaced Yan Xu and killed Zhong Wei, Zhao Ji, Li Kuan, and others, confirming the episode's contrast between restrained and severe local rule.

## Qualifications

The source gives a moral portrait, not an administrative audit. It does not specify Yan Xu's full policy record, the scale of Yingchuan's prior disorder, the legal basis for his transfer, or whether all later violence resulted from He Bing's temperament rather than court instruction or local conditions.
