# St Bartholomew the Great

## Overview

St Bartholomew the Great is a surviving church of the medieval Smithfield priory whose fabric and reused spaces connect devotion, institutional continuity, Reformation change, printing, folklore, and screen culture.

## Current Profile

The episode traces the church to Rahere's twelfth-century foundation of a priory and hospital after illness, pilgrimage, recovery, and a reported vision of St Bartholomew. Romanesque and early Gothic fabric, a Tudor gatehouse over an older arch, Rahere's tomb, and the hospital preserve parts of this medieval institutional world.

Continuity did not mean unchanged use. The Reformation dissolved the priory; the Lady Chapel passed into accommodation, workshops, printing, and a dancing school, with Benjamin Franklin working there in 1724. Apparition and stolen-relic stories, Hogarth's baptism, medical associations, Sherlock Holmes, and film locations add later layers without carrying equal evidentiary status.

## Key Characteristics

- Surviving church from Rahere's medieval priory and hospital foundation at Smithfield.
- Built fabric combining Romanesque, early Gothic, later gatehouse, tomb, chapel, and restored institutional space.
- Religious site altered by the Reformation but not erased from London's institutional landscape.
- Lady Chapel whose secular reuse included housing, workshops, printing, and dancing.
- Place where devotion, medicine, biography, folklore, fiction, and film accumulate around surviving architecture.

## Qualifications

The episode does not supply a full architectural survey or institutional history. Claims of being London's oldest surviving parish church, the city's only Marian apparition site, exact building dates, liturgical details, ghost stories, and the complete list of screen appearances remain source-scoped.
