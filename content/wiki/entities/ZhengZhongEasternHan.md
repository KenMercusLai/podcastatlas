## Overview

郑众 / Zheng Zhong enters the wiki as the eunuch whose reward refusal after the Dou purge deepens [[EmperorHeOfHan|Emperor He]]'s trust. Hanji 767 treats his promotion to da changqiu and repeated political conversations with the emperor as the source's beginning point for eunuch power.

## Current Profile

After Emperor He removes the Dou family, he rewards those connected to the successful consolidation. Zheng Zhong is promoted to da changqiu, but the source stresses his modesty: he refuses rewards and behaves humbly enough that Emperor He judges him virtuous.

That virtue reading matters because it turns service into access. The source says Emperor He often discusses government with Zheng Zhong afterward, and the host explicitly identifies this as the beginning of eunuch power. Zheng's current profile is therefore not one of coercive dominance yet; it is a formation layer where trusted inner-court service becomes a political channel.

## Key Characteristics

- Eunuch promoted to da changqiu after the Dou-family purge.
- Reward refuser whose humility strengthens Emperor He's trust rather than reducing his influence.
- Inner-court actor whose access expands through personal confidence and repeated political discussion.
- Early formation case for eunuch power before later institutionalized dominance.
- Counterpoint to outer-relative power because the emperor moves from Dou-family danger toward trusted palace servants.

## Qualifications

The current evidence is a formation layer, not a complete account of Eastern Han eunuch politics. Zheng Zhong is not yet shown in this page as running a faction or controlling appointment machinery; the sourced claim is that humility, reward refusal, and trusted access create the opening.
