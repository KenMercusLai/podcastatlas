# Battle of Gettysburg

## Overview

The Battle of Gettysburg was the three-day July 1863 battle in Pennsylvania that ended Robert E. Lee's northern invasion and became the symbolic “high watermark” of the Confederacy.

## Current Profile

[[202-american-civil-war-gettysburg-part-3-glt5256515778]] treats Gettysburg as an unintended meeting battle rather than a preselected showdown or a search for shoes. Roads brought the armies together, weak Confederate cavalry intelligence limited [[RobertELee|Robert E. Lee]]'s knowledge, and Union withdrawal after the first day placed its army on strong high ground.

The second day's attacks failed to break the Union line. On the third, Confederate artillery and a roughly mile-long infantry advance culminated in Pickett's Charge, which briefly reached the stone wall but could not hold it. Lee retreated, and the episode pairs the defeat with [[SiegeOfVicksburg|Vicksburg]] to mark a strategic change rather than an automatic end to the war.

## Key Characteristics

- Unplanned meeting battle shaped by road convergence and incomplete intelligence.
- Three-day escalation from early Confederate success to Union defense of strong terrain.
- Extremely costly clash on free Northern soil with major political stakes for Lincoln.
- Site of Pickett's Charge and the failure of Lee's final central assault.
- Battlefield later made a national interpretive site through the Gettysburg Address.

## Evidence

### Encounter, intelligence, and terrain

- [[202-american-civil-war-gettysburg-part-3-glt5256515778]] rejects the shoe story and emphasizes roads, Jeb Stuart's absence, imperfect Union intelligence, and the defensive value of Cemetery Hill and Cemetery Ridge.

### Three-day battle

- [[202-american-civil-war-gettysburg-part-3-glt5256515778]] describes Confederate success on July 1, failed attacks on July 2, and the artillery-supported central assault on July 3.

### Strategic and symbolic consequence

- [[202-american-civil-war-gettysburg-part-3-glt5256515778]] connects the retreat, unequal replacement capacity, paired Vicksburg victory, cemetery dedication, and Lincoln's speech.

## Qualifications

The episode provides a compressed popular-history reconstruction, not a complete tactical history. Troop totals, artillery effects, command intentions, the possibility of trapping Lee's retreat, and the counterfactual chance that Pickett's Charge could have succeeded remain approximate or interpretive. “High watermark” is a memory frame, not proof that every later Confederate possibility vanished here.

## What Changed

- Established Gettysburg as an intelligence-and-terrain encounter, paired strategic turning point, and later site of national meaning.
