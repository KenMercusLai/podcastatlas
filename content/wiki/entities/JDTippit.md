# J. D. Tippit

## Overview

J. D. Tippit was the Dallas police officer shot dead in Oak Cliff shortly after the [[KennedyAssassination|assassination of John F. Kennedy]], a killing the sources attribute to [[LeeHarveyOswald|Lee Harvey Oswald]].

## Current Profile

Tippit's role in these sources is brief but structurally important. Part 4 places him inside the citywide suspect search: after seeing a pedestrian who appeared to match the broadcast description, Tippit stopped him and was shot in front of multiple witnesses. The killing produced a second urgent crime scene and a suspect trail that led police to the Texas Theatre, where Oswald was arrested with a revolver after resisting officers.

The later episodes treat Tippit's murder as part of the cumulative case against Oswald rather than as a separate biographical story. The Warren Commission's conclusion that Oswald killed both Tippit and Kennedy and the public contrast between Tippit's attended funeral and Oswald's difficult burial also made the officer part of the case's legal and moral aftermath.

## Key Characteristics

- Dallas police officer killed in Oak Cliff on 22 November 1963.
- Patrol officer who stopped a man matching the assassination suspect broadcast.
- Victim whose shooting generated eyewitness evidence and an immediate theater search.
- Second homicide victim in the sources' cumulative case against Oswald.
- Publicly mourned officer whose funeral contrasted with Oswald's burial.

## Evidence

### Stop, shooting, and suspect trail

- [[395-jfk-hunt-for-a-killer-part-4-glt1345279222]] says Tippit stopped a suspicious pedestrian matching the broadcast description, that multiple witnesses saw the man shoot him, and that the subsequent search led to Oswald's arrest in the Texas Theatre.

### Place in the Oswald case

- [[398-jfk-the-mystery-is-solved-part-7-glt6409828878]] treats Tippit's killing, Oswald's armed resistance, and his flight as mutually reinforcing consciousness-of-guilt evidence.
- [[397-jfk-a-conspiracy-unmasked-part-6-glt4318220159]] reports the Warren Commission's conclusion that Oswald killed both Kennedy and Tippit.

### Public mourning

- [[396-jfk-the-second-assassin-strikes-part-5-glt7803941061]] contrasts Tippit's heavily attended funeral with Kennedy's national funeral and Oswald's difficult burial.

## Qualifications

These sources focus on Tippit's death as evidence in the Kennedy case and do not provide a full biography, policing record, independent witness audit, or complete forensic reconstruction. The page records the episodes' attribution of the shooting to Oswald while keeping exact witness reliability, timing, and ballistic details source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Established Tippit as the second homicide victim and the bridge between the assassination search and Oswald's theater arrest.
- Separated eyewitness and suspect-trail evidence from the later commission conclusion and public memory.
