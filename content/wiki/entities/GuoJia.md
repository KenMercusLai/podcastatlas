# 郭嘉 / Guo Jia

## Overview

郭嘉 / Guo Jia is a [[CaoCao|曹操]] strategist whose current profile centers on bold condition-reading inside Cao Cao's wider [[AdviserStateArchitecture|谋士政权架构]]. Across the bounded sources, he evaluates patron quality, reputation costs, siege timing, target order, comparative leadership, succession fractures, and the interaction between enemy incentives and operational surprise; Hanji 1081 adds his death after the Wuhuan campaign and a heavily qualified claim about the judgment capacity Cao Cao then lost.

## Current Profile

The early sources place Guo Jia inside a system rather than presenting him as a solitary genius. Hanji 976, Hanji 1005, and Hanji 1006 show an Yingchuan-linked adviser who leaves [[YuanShao|袁绍]] after judging that courtesy and family prestige cannot compensate for weak personnel use and poor priority discipline. Through [[XunYu|荀彧]]'s recommendation, he enters Cao Cao's camp and becomes the bold, risk-tolerant counterpart to [[XunYou|荀攸]]'s secrecy and caution.

Hanji 1009, Hanji 1014, Hanji 1015, and Hanji 1025 define that style in decisions. Guo Jia opposes killing [[LiuBei|刘备]] because it would damage Cao Cao's recruitment reputation, joins the [[TenVictoriesTenDefeats|十胜十败论]] comparison against Yuan Shao, recommends removing [[LyuBu|吕布]] before Yuan Shao is free to intervene, and urges continued pressure at [[Xiapi|下邳]] before Lü Bu's command can recover. Hanji 1058 adds the post-Guandu case: relaxed pressure will let [[YuanTan|袁谭]] and [[YuanShang|袁尚]] turn an unresolved succession into open conflict.

Hanji 1077 supplies the campaign decision behind that final arc. Against officers' warnings about distance, supply, and an exposed Xu capital, Guo Jia argues that Wuhuan's confidence in remoteness creates unpreparedness and that [[LiuBiao|刘表]] will hesitate to empower [[LiuBei|刘备]] enough to attack Cao Cao's rear. He then recommends shedding baggage for speed. The rain-blocked advance does not invalidate his reasoning, but it makes the tradeoff explicit: surprise is purchased by accepting route and logistics exposure.

Hanji 1081 closes the bounded arc with Guo Jia's death during the return from Cao Cao's Wuhuan campaign. Its useful addition is functional: the episode understands Guo Jia as a specialist in reading hesitation, fear, appetite, and internal fracture. Its stronger claim—that his survival would have exposed every Red Cliffs deception and enabled Cao Cao to prevent tripartite division—is an untestable counterfactual and is not part of the current factual profile.

## Key Characteristics

- Cao-side adviser whose value depends on fit within a multi-role decision system.
- Patron evaluator who distinguishes receiving talent from converting counsel into action.
- Bold strategist contrasted with Xun You's discretion and caution.
- Reputation-sensitive counselor who protects Cao Cao's talent-market promise in the Liu Bei case.
- Sequencing adviser who relates target choice to flank risk, enemy distraction, and timing.
- Pressure, incentive, and surprise analyst who detects breaking points and connects enemy complacency or third-party authority fears to campaign timing.
- Distinctive judgment specialist whose death creates a capability-loss question, without proving any single later defeat.

## Qualifications

The page remains a synthesis of selected strategy scenes, not a complete biography. Several episodes praise Guo Jia through modern leadership language, compressed metaphors, and retrospective outcome knowledge. Hanji 1077's “risk investment” and “prisoner's dilemma” language is modern analogy, while the transcript ends before Tian Chou's solution or the campaign result; later success must not be used to erase the expedition's real supply and rear-security risks. Hanji 1081's claims that Guo Jia would have detected the Red Cliffs fire plan, linked-ships stratagem, or borrowed east wind—and that his death caused Cao Cao's strategic decline or the eventual tripartite order—cannot be tested and are preserved only as source-scoped counterfactuals. His value should therefore remain attached to the specific judgments the bounded sources actually describe.

## What Changed

- Added the Wuhuan expedition decision as a concrete case of reading both target complacency and third-party incentives.
- Added the baggage-for-speed recommendation while preserving terrain and supply exposure as qualifications.
- Kept Guo Jia's later Red Cliffs counterfactual outside settled causal history.
