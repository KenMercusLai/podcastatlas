## Overview

淖姬 / Nuo Ji enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-382-2-yisheng-zuoguo-sanwei-zhuhou-feizi-gudai-chaomei-nuoji-de-beiqing-rensheng-lt06-as48q9qlpt_-dfdx_jsidjy|Hanji 382-2]] as a Western Han consort associated successively with [[LiuFeiJiangduKingWesternHan|江都王刘非 / 刘飞]], [[LiuJianJiangduKingWesternHan|江都王刘建]], and [[LiuPengzuZhaoKingWesternHan|赵敬肃王刘彭祖]]. The episode presents her as beautiful and repeatedly favored, but it frames that favor as exposure inside male royal succession and desire structures rather than as durable power.

## Current Profile

Nuo Ji's profile is built around movement between princely households. With Liu Fei, the episode gives her the most protective setting: Liu Fei is described as a heroic Jiangdu king, and an excavated lacquer plate bearing Nuo Ji's name is used to suggest she remained emotionally significant enough for her object to accompany his burial.

After Liu Fei dies, Nuo Ji's position becomes more coercive. Liu Jian, Liu Fei's son and successor, is said to seize her even before Liu Fei is buried. When Liu Jian later kills himself after a rebellion accusation, Zhao king Liu Pengzu obtains Nuo Ji and fathers Liu Naozi with her. The end of the episode shows that even motherhood does not secure her branch: her brother's warning that Liu Naozi has too many desires helps block his succession.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han consort whose source role crosses Jiangdu and Zhao princely households.
- Valued for beauty and favor, but not shown as independently controlling her own marital or political fate.
- Linked positively to Liu Fei through the episode's affectionate tomb-object interpretation.
- Exposed after Liu Fei's death when Liu Jian seizes her during the burial interval.
- Later attached to Liu Pengzu and becomes mother of Liu Naozi.
- Her son's succession failure makes her case a source for consort vulnerability rather than simple palace romance.

## Qualifications

The page is bounded to one compact podcast source. It does not settle Nuo Ji's full biography, family origin, precise legal status, personal consent, inner feelings, burial-object interpretation, or whether other records characterize her differently.
