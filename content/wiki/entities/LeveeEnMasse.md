# Levée en masse

## Overview

The levée en masse was the National Convention's 23 August 1793 mobilization of French society for the Republic's war effort.

## Current Profile

The decree assigned young men to battle and distributed supporting wartime duties among married men, women, children, and the elderly. The source presents it as an early modern breakthrough in mass national mobilization that strengthened revolutionary armies and helped lay foundations for later French and Napoleonic military dominance.

## Key Characteristics

- It was enacted on 23 August 1793.
- It mobilized military service and civilian production across age and gender roles.
- It tied citizenship and social labor to national survival.
- It expanded state capacity during simultaneous foreign and civil war.
- It is presented as a precursor to modern mass conscription.

## Qualifications

- The source emphasizes institutional novelty and military capacity but does not provide a full account of enforcement, resistance, local variation, or troop totals.
- Mobilization contributed to survival alongside strategy, organization, repression, and changing battlefield conditions; it was not the sole cause.

## What Changed

- A canonical page now records the levée en masse as a whole-society mobilization rather than only a conscription measure.
