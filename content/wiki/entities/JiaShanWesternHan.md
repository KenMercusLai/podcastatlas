## Overview

贾山 / Jia Shan is a Western Han remonstrant who argues that private minting distributes part of the ruler's power over wealth and rank to ordinary producers of money.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-216-hanchao-diyi-shoufu-jingran-shi-wendi-de-nanchong-ls2ebkz64ax0oywszpjrndfn3-k4|Hanji 216]] preserves a compact political argument. Money may have little value as material, but it can purchase wealth and status; allowing ordinary people to make it therefore lets them share an authority that Jia Shan presents as properly imperial. He asks [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]] to end private minting, but the emperor rejects the memorial.

The argument complements rather than duplicates [[JiaYi|贾谊]]'s warning in the same episode. Jia Yi emphasizes incentives, coin quality, criminalization, labor allocation, and copper control, whereas Jia Shan makes authority over monetary creation part of sovereign control over social distribution.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han court remonstrant represented through a monetary-sovereignty argument.
- Treats money's exchange power, not its material substance, as politically decisive.
- Connects the ability to mint with control over wealth and rank.
- Advocates ending private minting.
- Adviser whose warning Liu Heng declines.

## Qualifications

The current profile rests on one short popular-history note rather than Jia Shan's complete biography, memorial text, or a study of early-Han monetary law. The wording, constitutional meaning of “shared authority,” exact policy proposal, and reason Liu Heng rejected it require comparison with transmitted sources. The argument should not be treated as a complete economic theory or as proof that all noncentral minting necessarily destroys sovereignty.

## What Changed

- Created a bounded profile distinguishing Jia Shan's sovereignty argument from Jia Yi's incentive-and-material-control analysis.
