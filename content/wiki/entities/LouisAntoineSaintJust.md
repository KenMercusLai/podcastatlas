# Louis Antoine de Saint-Just

## Overview

Louis Antoine de Saint-Just was a Montagnard deputy who argued that [[LouisXVI|Louis XVI]] should be condemned because kingship itself placed him outside legitimate republican political order.

## Current Profile

In this source Saint-Just appears at the opening of the king's trial debate as an advocate of political judgment beyond ordinary constitutional procedure. His argument shifts the issue from proving discrete offences to treating the former king's existence as structurally incompatible with a virtuous republic.

## Key Characteristics

- He belonged to the radical Montagnard side of the Convention.
- He treated kingship itself, not only particular alleged crimes, as the basis of guilt.
- He rejected the old constitution as an adequate limit on republican judgment.
- He argued that a virtuous republic could not safely preserve a living former king.

### Republican necessity

- [[546-the-french-revolution-the-monarchy-falls-part-3-glt3779628370]] places the speech between natural-law arguments for trial and Robespierre's claim that the homeland required Louis's death.

## Qualifications

- The source supplies one decisive intervention rather than a full biography or account of Saint-Just's later career.
- The necessity of execution is his political argument as presented by the episode, not a settled legal conclusion.
- His claim should not be treated as the unanimous position of the Convention or even every Montagnard.

## What Changed

- A canonical page now records Saint-Just's role in redefining Louis's case as a judgment on kingship itself.
