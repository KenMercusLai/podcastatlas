# Louis Antoine de Saint-Just

## Overview

Louis Antoine de Saint-Just was a Montagnard deputy who argued that [[LouisXVI|Louis XVI]] should be condemned because kingship itself placed him outside legitimate republican political order.

## Current Profile

The sources trace Saint-Just from the king's trial into the leadership of revolutionary government. His argument against Louis shifted judgment from discrete offences to kingship's incompatibility with a virtuous republic; as a close ally of [[MaximilienRobespierre|Robespierre]], he later helped draft the [[ConstitutionOf1793|Constitution of 1793]] while defending the exclusion of revolutionary opponents from citizenship and ordinary legal protection.

## Key Characteristics

- He belonged to the radical Montagnard side of the Convention.
- He treated kingship itself, not only particular alleged crimes, as the basis of guilt.
- He rejected the old constitution as an adequate limit on republican judgment.
- He argued that a virtuous republic could not safely preserve a living former king.
- He became a disciplined and influential ally of Robespierre on the Committee of Public Safety.
- He helped join democratic constitutional promises to exceptional revolutionary government.
- He treated active opposition to revolutionary government as grounds for exclusion from citizenship protections.

## Qualifications

- The sources supply two decisive political phases rather than a full biography or account of Saint-Just's later career.
- The necessity of execution is his political argument as presented by the episode, not a settled legal conclusion.
- His claim should not be treated as the unanimous position of the Convention or even every Montagnard.
- The “Archangel of Terror” label, personality portrait, later ideological comparisons, and exact division of authorship remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Extended Saint-Just from regicide theory into committee leadership, constitutional drafting, and emergency citizenship exclusion.
