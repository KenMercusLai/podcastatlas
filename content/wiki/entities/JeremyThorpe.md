# Jeremy Thorpe

## Overview
Jeremy Thorpe appears as the Liberal leader whose party's February 1974 surge denied either major party a majority and briefly made him the pivotal figure in coalition talks.

## Current Profile
[[418-britain-in-1974-the-crisis-election-part-2-glt9690615062]] portrays Thorpe as personally attracted to coalition and a possible Home Secretary role, but unable to carry his party into an agreement with [[EdwardHeath|Edward Heath]]. Liberal support for Europe overlapped with Conservative policy, yet distrust of Heath's confrontational record and the absence of Conservative support for voting reform prevented a deal.

## Key Characteristics
- Led a Liberal campaign built around Europe, proportional representation, and moderate alternatives to the two major parties.
- Benefited from voter dissatisfaction with both Heath and [[HaroldWilson|Harold Wilson]].
- Won substantial national vote share but few seats under first-past-the-post.
- Was personally open to coalition with Heath.
- Could not bridge Liberal demands for electoral reform and Conservative resistance.

## Evidence
- Electoral surge: [[418-britain-in-1974-the-crisis-election-part-2-glt9690615062]] gives the Liberals 19.3% of votes but only 14 seats.
- Coalition opportunity: [[418-britain-in-1974-the-crisis-election-part-2-glt9690615062]] says Thorpe liked the idea of coalition and might have become Home Secretary.
- Deal failure: [[418-britain-in-1974-the-crisis-election-part-2-glt9690615062]] links failure to Liberal distrust, demands for voting reform, and weak Conservative enthusiasm for an agreement.

## Qualifications
This is an election-specific profile rather than a full biography of Thorpe or the Liberal Party. The source does not isolate whether leadership, policy, protest voting, or local organization best explains the Liberal surge.
