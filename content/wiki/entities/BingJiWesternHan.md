## Overview

丙吉 / Bing Ji enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-454-yi-zhi-niao-ganfan-yige-chengxiang-huangba-you-duo-can-lrjgft-s8oi3yt3lrnsh0_m20sxj|Hanji 454]] as the Western Han chancellor whose death precedes [[HuangBaWesternHan|黄霸]]'s appointment. The episode uses him as a contrast figure: a minister tied to [[EmperorXuanOfHan|汉宣帝]]'s survival and the Xiaoxuan revival who does not turn merit into public self-advertisement. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-409-hanxuandi-liubingyi-cong-yuzhong-zouchulai-de-chuanqi-huangdi-ljyxm9nd5rvheusna29wkxuraqux|Hanji 409]] supplies the original rescue scene: as tingwei jian during the witchcraft-disaster aftermath, Bing Ji protects the imprisoned infant Liu Bingyi, arranges nursing care, spends personal resources, and blocks a prison-killing order. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-452-xiaowangzhi-bei-jiangzhi-ni-zaizhichang-fanguo-zhezhong-cuo-ma-likmpw-97b-o3p5qaxgyyourkldn|Hanji 452]] makes that tie politically active: [[XiaoWangzhiWesternHan|萧望之]]'s contempt toward Bing Ji offends Xuan because Bing Ji had protected and cared for the future emperor as an infant. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-430-chigua-qunzhong-qianren-yu-xianren-de-gongdou-daxi-lkfcjewfhmhfgw39vqfqkpa9umzq|Hanji 430]] adds the illness-and-reward layer: Xuan wants Bing Ji to receive his marquis seal even while gravely ill, and [[XiahouShengWesternHan|夏侯胜]] interprets Bing Ji's recovery through [[YindeRewardCausality|阴德福报因果]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-429-jiaobingbibai-de-gushi-zenme-laide-lokha4dmrik4ez4vtpunb9rwhzav|Hanji 429]] backfills the later discovery scene: Bing Ji stays silent until the bondwoman Ze's claim forces a report, then redirects credit toward Hu Zu and Guo Zhengqing while explaining the prison-era care network.

## Current Profile

The sources give Bing Ji a compact evaluative role rather than a full biography. They identify him as a figure who served from prison/censorial responsibility into chancellorship, became one of the Qilin Pavilion merit figures, and helped enable Emperor Xuan's rule without publicly boasting that he had once protected the future emperor. Hanji 409 now makes the concrete rescue mechanism visible: Bing Ji reads the crown-prince witchcraft case as unjust, treats the infant Liu Bingyi as an innocent victim rather than a political contaminant, hires or retains caregivers with his own resources, and resists an imperial prison-killing order until the command collapses into amnesty. Hanji 452 adds why his status cannot be read only from old age or current office: he carries Xuan's survival memory, so disrespect toward him can become disrespect toward the emperor's gratitude order. Hanji 429 shows the mechanism of that hiddenness. Even after Xuan's accession, Bing Ji does not volunteer the story; only Ze's petition brings him into the record, and he responds by correcting the merit distribution toward Hu Zu and Guo Zhengqing before submitting the full account.

Hanji 430 makes the same hidden-service profile explicitly moral: Bing Ji's unadvertised merit is treated as hidden virtue that deserves repayment and, in Xiahou Sheng's interpretation, protects him before death. Hanji 429 then sharpens the moral reading through Laozi's "上德不德," accumulated hidden virtue, and the warning that public self-advertisement can reduce the moral meaning of good action.

Within Hanji 454, Bing Ji matters because he sets the standard Huang Ba fails to meet. Bing Ji's quiet merit, restraint, and reputation are used to frame what a chancellor's interior steadiness can look like; Huang Ba's later search for auspicious proof and visible recognition becomes more conspicuous against that background.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han chancellor whose death opens the episode's court transition.
- Meritorious official connected to Emperor Xuan's rescue and reign legitimacy in the host's account.
- Prison-era protector who uses office authority and personal resources to keep the infant Liu Bingyi alive.
- Quiet-credit contrast who does not advertise his role in Xuan's survival.
- Hidden-virtue example whose illness and recovery are interpreted as deferred moral reward.
- Credit-distribution corrector who names Hu Zu and Guo Zhengqing when Ze overstates her own nursing merit.
- Protected symbolic elder whose hidden relationship weight makes Xiao Wangzhi's contempt politically costly.

## Qualifications

This page is source-scoped to Hanji 454, Hanji 452, Hanji 430, Hanji 429, and Hanji 409. It does not reconstruct Bing Ji's complete official career, the full original prison records, his full policy record, or the original Ban Gu evaluation beyond the episodes' summaries. Hanji 409's prison-gate account and Hanji 430 and Hanji 429's hidden-virtue interpretation are preserved as the episodes' moral and narrative frame, not as complete legal reconstruction, a medical explanation for illness and recovery, or a universal law of reward.
