# Túpac Amaru I

## Overview

Túpac Amaru I was the final ruler of the independent Neo-Inca state in Vilcabamba, captured by Spanish forces and executed in Cusco in 1572.

## Current Profile

In [[649-the-fall-of-the-incas-the-last-emperor-part-6-glt4505534064]], Túpac Amaru succeeds Tito Cusi after the latter's sudden illness and death. He blames Christians, kills a friar, prohibits Christianity, and tries to close [[NeoIncaState|Vilcabamba]] to outside influence, reversing the preceding ruler's cautious religious and diplomatic accommodation.

After a Spanish envoy is murdered, Viceroy Francisco de Toledo declares him an apostate, homicide, rebel, and tyrant and sends forces into the jungle. Túpac Amaru is captured, tried in Cusco in a proceeding the hosts compare to Atahualpa's show trial, converts before death, and is beheaded on 24 September 1572 amid public mourning. His execution closes the episode's line of independent Inca kings.

## Key Characteristics

- Final independent ruler of the Neo-Inca state at Vilcabamba.
- Successor who rejected Tito Cusi's limited accommodation with missionaries and envoys.
- Ruler associated with religious exclusion and the killing of a Spanish envoy.
- Captive subjected to a rapid colonial rebellion trial in Cusco.
- Convert to Christianity immediately before execution.
- Symbolic endpoint of the episode's Inca royal narrative.

## Evidence

- Succession and reversal: [[649-the-fall-of-the-incas-the-last-emperor-part-6-glt4505534064]] links Tito Cusi's death to Túpac Amaru's anti-Christian policy.
- Spanish intervention: [[649-the-fall-of-the-incas-the-last-emperor-part-6-glt4505534064]] connects the envoy's murder to Toledo's declaration and military expedition.
- Trial and execution: [[649-the-fall-of-the-incas-the-last-emperor-part-6-glt4505534064]] describes capture, rapid adjudication, conversion, public address, beheading, and mass mourning.

## Qualifications

The episode supplies a dramatic narrative rather than a full legal, political, or religious study. Túpac Amaru's responsibility for each killing, the trial record, the precise meaning of his conversion and final Quechua address, and Viceroy Toledo's motives remain source-scoped. Calling him the “last Inca king” refers to the independent Vilcabamba line and does not imply the disappearance of Inca descendants, identity, or political memory.

## What Changed

- Established the policy reversal that preceded the final Spanish expedition.
- Qualified “last Inca” as the end of an independent royal line rather than a people or identity.
