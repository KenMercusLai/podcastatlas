## Overview

申公 / Shen Gong, identified in Hanji 249 as 申培, is the elderly teacher of [[ZhaoWanWesternHan|赵绾]] and [[WangZangWesternHan|王臧]] whose ceremonial summons to [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s court joins scholarly authority, teacher honor, and early ritual reform.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-249-dongzhongshu-wo-bushi-rujia-de-zuiren-livm-uxnbsm0mkbrm-lvnozqnail|Hanji 249]] presents Shen Gong as a scholar from Lu who has lived in seclusion and is already over eighty when his students recommend him. Wudi sends gifts and a four-horse carriage with its wheels wrapped or cushioned to reduce jolting, making the journey itself a public act of respect for age and learning.

At court, Shen Gong answers Wudi's question about order and disorder by saying government depends less on abundant speech than on effective action. The episode says the plain answer does not suit the young ruler's taste for literary display, yet Shen Gong still becomes a court grandee and joins discussion of Mingtang construction, imperial-tour rules, the calendar, and official colors. His profile therefore preserves both ritual recognition and a possible gap between honoring a teacher and embracing his governing style.

## Key Characteristics

- Elderly scholar from Lu, also identified as Shen Pei.
- Teacher of Zhao Wan and Wang Zang.
- Summoned through an unusually careful and honor-laden imperial transport protocol.
- Gives an action-over-speech answer to Wudi's question about government.
- Receives office despite the episode's claim that his plain style disappoints the emperor.
- Participates in ritual and institutional planning associated with the Mingtang program.

## Qualifications

The supplied episode does not establish Shen Gong's full scholarly lineage, writings, exact age, official title, travel protocol, or independent role in each reform project. Its claim about Wudi's disappointment interprets silence and literary preference as inner motive. Public honor demonstrates political recognition but does not prove that Shen Gong controlled policy.

## What Changed

- Created a focused profile distinguishing ceremonial scholarly authority from demonstrated policy influence.
