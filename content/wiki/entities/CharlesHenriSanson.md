# Charles-Henri Sanson

## Overview

Charles-Henri Sanson was Paris's hereditary executioner under both monarchy and revolution, making his career a point of continuity across a major regime break.

## Current Profile

Sanson belonged to a dynasty whose necessary public role carried social pollution, distinctive dress, and unusual privileges. He performed branding, pillorying, shackling, torture, and execution before becoming a normalized revolutionary functionary. His practical warning that manual beheading could fail, exhaust the executioner, and spill uncontrolled blood helped move the Assembly toward machinery; he then operated it for [[NicolasJacquesPelletier|Pelletier]] and later revolutionary victims.

## Key Characteristics

- He inherited a stigmatized but indispensable public office.
- He served both royal and revolutionary authority rather than embodying a clean regime rupture.
- His practical expertise shaped the move from manual to mechanical beheading.
- The source attributes roughly 3,000 executions to his own later claim.
