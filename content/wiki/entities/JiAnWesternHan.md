## Overview

汲黯 / Ji An is a Western Han official whom the current source presents as upright, clean, highly placed, and willing to confront [[HanWudi|汉武帝]], but abandoned by former visitors after losing office and money.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-333-1-men-ke-luoque-de-youlai-ni-zhidao-ma-lkoajhp2b_k32aurnk8rs-ukxaib|Hanji 333-1]] pairs Ji An with [[ZhengDangshiWesternHan|郑当时]] as worthy senior ministers. Their authority and reputation draw respect and frequent visitors while they hold power; dismissal removes both political usefulness and, in the episode's telling, financial means, after which previous associates stop coming.

The episode uses Ji An's reputed frankness toward Han Wudi to add a second interpretation: incorruptibility and refusal to conform can make an official difficult for a political system to retain. That systemic claim is commentary, while the more securely bounded contribution is the social contrast between office and dismissal.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han senior official in the episode's Han Wudi setting.
- Presented as upright, clean, and willing to remonstrate directly.
- Respected and surrounded by visitors while powerful.
- Abandoned by previous visitors after dismissal and impoverishment.
- Paired with Zheng Dangshi in Sima Qian's attributed reflection on status-dependent relationships.

## Qualifications

The supplied note explicitly warns that names and details were not textually collated. It transcribes Ji An as “济暗/齐暗”; this page normalizes the name to 汲黯. The source does not establish his full biography, precise offices, dismissal sequence, wealth, every exchange with Han Wudi, or how many former associates abandoned him. The claim that clean officials are structurally excluded remains the host's generalization.
