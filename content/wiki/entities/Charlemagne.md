# Charlemagne

## Overview

Charlemagne was the expansionist Frankish ruler whose protection of the papacy and coronation in Rome revived a competing western Roman imperial claim.

## Current Profile

The sources treat Charlemagne's coronation in Rome as a political and ideological transfer rather than a straightforward restoration of the ancient state. The Carolingians had gained papal recognition after displacing the Merovingians, while conquest gave Charlemagne territorial power across much of the former western empire. Papal need, Frankish protection, Roman location, Christian authority, and the language of imperium therefore reinforced one another.

The Christmas episode adds the immediate settlement. After [[PopeLeoIII|Pope Leo III]] escaped a violent Roman challenge, Charlemagne received him at Paderborn, entered Rome with armed power, restored him through a synod, and was crowned on 25 December 800. [[IreneOfAthens|Irene of Athens]]'s contested sole rule, Rome's prestige, Charlemagne's territories, and a Jerusalem delegation supplied further legitimation, although the source treats Charlemagne's supposed surprise and the delegation's timing skeptically.

The claim remained competitive. Byzantines continued to understand themselves as Romans and resented a western coronation that challenged their exclusive imperial identity. Charlemagne therefore anchors the distinction between continuing eastern Roman self-identification and a later western institution's recognized claim to Roman authority.

## Key Characteristics

- Frankish ruler crowned emperor in Rome.
- Protector whose military capacity helped answer papal security needs.
- Carolingian ruler whose conquests made the imperial title plausible in territorial as well as sacred terms.
- Founder figure for a western Roman imperial revival.
- Competitor with continuing Byzantine Roman claims.

## Qualifications

The sources do not provide a full account of Charlemagne's reign, administration, wars, theology, or the later constitutional development of the Holy Roman Empire. “Revival” does not imply uninterrupted identity with the ancient Roman state. Einhard's surprise story, Charlemagne's prior agreement with Leo, and the political staging of the Jerusalem delegation remain source-dependent or interpretive.

## What Changed

- Established Charlemagne as the papally recognized western competitor in the episode's map of Roman succession.
- Added the coronation's immediate papal crisis, territorial, Byzantine, and sacred-symbolic foundations.
