# Charlemagne

## Overview

Charlemagne appears in this source as the western ruler whose papal coronation revived a competing Roman imperial claim when Constantinople could no longer reliably protect the pope.

## Current Profile

The episode treats Charlemagne's coronation in Rome as a political and ideological transfer rather than a straightforward restoration of the ancient state. Papal need, Frankish protection, Roman location, Christian authority, and the language of imperium combined to produce a credible western afterlife of Rome.

That claim remained competitive. Byzantines continued to understand themselves as Romans and resented a western coronation that challenged their exclusive imperial identity. Charlemagne therefore anchors the episode's distinction between continuous Roman self-identification and a later institution's recognized claim to Roman authority.

## Key Characteristics

- Frankish ruler crowned emperor in Rome.
- Protector whose military capacity helped answer papal security needs.
- Founder figure for a western Roman imperial revival.
- Competitor with continuing Byzantine Roman claims.

## Qualifications

The episode uses Charlemagne to frame imperial afterlife and does not provide a full account of his reign, administration, wars, theology, or the later constitutional development of the Holy Roman Empire. “Revival” does not imply uninterrupted identity with the ancient Roman state.

## What Changed

- Established Charlemagne as the papally recognized western competitor in the episode's map of Roman succession.
