# Charlemagne

## Overview

Charlemagne was the expansionist Frankish ruler whose protection of the papacy and coronation in Rome revived a competing western Roman imperial claim.

## Current Profile

The sources treat Charlemagne's coronation in Rome as a political and ideological transfer rather than a straightforward restoration of the ancient state. The Carolingians had gained papal recognition after displacing the Merovingians, while conquest gave Charlemagne territorial power across much of the former western empire. Papal need, Frankish protection, Roman location, Christian authority, and the language of imperium therefore reinforced one another.

The Christmas episodes add the immediate settlement. After [[PopeLeoIII|Pope Leo III]] escaped a violent Roman challenge, Charlemagne received him at Paderborn, entered Rome with armed power, restored him through a synod, and was crowned on 25 December 800. [[IreneOfAthens|Irene of Athens]]'s contested sole rule, Rome's prestige, Charlemagne's territories, and a Jerusalem delegation supplied further legitimation. The fuller account adds [[Alcuin]]'s advice, Charlemagne's itinerary through old political and sacred centers, legal preparation for Leo's restoration, and careful post-coronation titulature, making a wholly spontaneous crown unlikely.

The claim remained competitive. Byzantines continued to understand themselves as Romans and resented a western coronation that challenged their exclusive imperial identity. Charlemagne therefore avoided simply calling himself emperor of the Romans; Byzantine envoys recognized his imperial rank in 812 without surrendering their exclusive Roman title. Gifts from [[HarunAlRashid|Harun al-Rashid]] and the palace at [[Aachen]] nevertheless placed the court inside a wider imperial diplomatic and material language.

The succession clarifies both achievement and limit. Charlemagne crowned [[LouisThePious|Louis the Pious]] joint emperor without papal involvement, but the realm later divided and weaker rulers increasingly depended on papal coronation. [[CarolingianImperialFragmentation]] did not erase the learned network or western Roman claim; it made them available for later renewal by the Saxon [[OttoTheGreat|Otto the Great]].

## Key Characteristics

- Frankish ruler crowned emperor in Rome.
- Protector whose military capacity helped answer papal security needs.
- Carolingian ruler whose conquests made the imperial title plausible in territorial as well as sacred terms.
- Deliberate image-builder whose itinerary, titulature, [[Aachen]] palace, and classical biography projected imperial status.
- Diplomatic peer recognized in qualified ways by Baghdad and Constantinople.
- Competitor with continuing Byzantine Roman claims.
- Founder figure whose imperial memory outlasted the political unity of his realm.

## Qualifications

The sources do not provide a full account of Charlemagne's reign, administration, wars, theology, or the later constitutional development of the Holy Roman Empire. “Revival” does not imply uninterrupted identity with the ancient Roman state. [[Einhard]]'s surprise story and character portrait, Charlemagne's exact prior agreement with Leo, the political staging of the Jerusalem delegation, diplomatic motives, and the depth of Carolingian institutions remain source-dependent or interpretive. Later France, Germany, and the Holy Roman Empire should not be projected fully formed into Charlemagne's realm.

## What Changed

- Strengthened the judgment that the coronation was prepared while preserving uncertainty about Charlemagne's exact prior knowledge.
- Added careful titulature, Aachen, and foreign recognition as tools for managing a competitive imperial claim.
- Extended the profile through planned succession, Carolingian fragmentation, and Saxon renewal.
