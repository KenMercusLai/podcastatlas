## Overview

梁松 / Liang Song, Linxiang Marquis is now synthesized as an Eastern Han elite whose danger appears twice: first as the figure who helps dishonor [[MaYuanEasternHan|Ma Yuan]] after death, and later as the Mingdi-era patron whose resentment, anonymous slander, and implicated guests reveal the exposure created by private access networks around royal power.

## Current Profile

Hanji 726 gives the earlier Ma Yuan branch that Hanji 736 only alluded to. [[LiuXiu|Liu Xiu]] sends Liang Song to rebuke Ma Yuan and act in a monitoring role during the Wuling campaign. After Ma Yuan dies, Liang finds the opening to accuse him: coix seeds brought from Jiaozhi are treated as alleged pearls and rhinoceros horn, Liu Xiu grows angrier, and Ma Yuan's Xinxihou seal is seized.

Hanji 736 then supplies Liang's later court-risk branch under [[EmperorMingOfHan|Emperor Ming]]. Liang, now remembered as having previously slandered Ma Yuan, resents the court, hangs anonymous defamatory writing, is arrested, imprisoned, and sentenced to death. The same source uses him as the negative counterpart to [[ZhengZhongFrontierEasternHan|Zheng Zhong]]: when Liang had carried silk to recruit Zheng for the crown prince and [[LiuJingGuanglingKingEasternHan|Liu Jing]], Zheng refused and therefore remains clean when Liang's guests are later implicated.

## Key Characteristics

- Linxiang Marquis whose current evidence combines a 49 CE Ma Yuan slander branch and a later Mingdi-era fall.
- Imperial monitor/rebuker who uses Ma Yuan's death to press an accusation over baggage.
- Court-risk figure whose accusation harms a dead official's title, burial order, and family security.
- Private recruitment intermediary whose mission to Zheng Zhong later becomes evidence of dangerous princely access networks.
- Patron whose own resentment and anonymous slander lead to imprisonment, death sentence, and implication of guests.

## Qualifications

The page does not identify Liang Song with later Liang-family name variants outside this source chain. Hanji 726 clarifies the Ma Yuan accusation but still does not prove all of Liang's motives or every political connection behind the case. Hanji 736 explains Liang's later fall through resentment and anonymous slander, not through a full biography.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 726 to supply the concrete Ma Yuan slander episode that Hanji 736 only referenced.
- Reframed Liang Song as a two-branch court danger: posthumous accusation first, private-retainer exposure later.
- Connected Liang's accusation to Ma Yuan's family crisis and Zhu Bo's later corrective memorial.
