## Overview

梁松 / Liang Song, Linxiang Marquis is a Mingdi-era elite whose current wiki profile is bounded to Hanji 736. He matters less as a full biographical subject than as the patron whose resentment, anonymous slander, and implicated guests reveal how private access networks around royal power could become dangerous under [[EmperorMingOfHan|汉明帝]].

## Current Profile

Hanji 736 introduces Liang Song as a marquis with earlier reputational damage: the source says he had previously spread false accusations against Ma Yuan. In the 61-63 CE layer, he resents the court, hangs anonymous defamatory writing, is arrested, imprisoned, and sentenced to death.

The source also uses Liang's older role to explain [[ZhengZhongFrontierEasternHan|郑众]]'s later safety. When Mingdi was still crown prince, the crown prince and [[LiuJingGuanglingKingEasternHan|Shanyang/Guangling King Liu Jing]] sent Liang with silk to recruit Zheng as a private guest. Zheng refused because crown princes should not casually build outside ties and royal princes should not privately gather retainers.

When Liang falls, many of his guests are implicated, while Zheng remains clean precisely because he had refused the recruitment. Liang's profile therefore turns on the conversion of elite access into exposure: a tie that looks advantageous before the patron's fall becomes legal and political danger afterward.

## Key Characteristics

- Mingdi-era marquis whose current profile is source-bounded to Hanji 736.
- Earlier reputation includes the source's claim that he slandered Ma Yuan.
- Acts as a private recruitment intermediary for the crown prince and Liu Jing before Mingdi's reign.
- Falls after anonymous defamatory writing against the court.
- Becomes the negative counterpart to Zheng Zhong's refusal of risky client status.
