# Catherine Eddowes

## Overview

Catherine Eddowes was killed and mutilated in Mitre Square in the City of London early on September 30, 1888, less than an hour after [[ElizabethStride|Elizabeth Stride]] was found dead. Her murder anchors the second half of the “double event” associated with [[JackTheRipper|Jack the Ripper]].

## Current Profile

[[626-jack-the-ripper-the-killer-strikes-again-part-3-glt7980605076]] reconstructs Eddowes as a Wolverhampton-born woman who had lived for many years with Thomas Conway, had three children, later partnered with John Kelly, and experienced severe poverty and housing insecurity. False names on pawn tickets and her use of “Kate Conway” made initial identification harder. She had been arrested for drunkenness and was released around 1 a.m., shortly before her death.

Her throat was cut, her face and abdomen were mutilated, and her left kidney and part of her uterus were removed at the scene within a narrow time window. The injuries imply speed and practical bodily familiarity, but they do not prove formal medical training. A piece of her apron found on Goulston Street establishes a trail away from the scene; nearby anti-Jewish graffiti does not establish who wrote it or why.

## Key Characteristics

- Wolverhampton-born woman whose biography included long partnerships, children, poverty, pawning, and unstable housing.
- Released from police custody shortly before her murder in Mitre Square.
- Killed and mutilated rapidly within the City of London police jurisdiction.
- Anatomical injuries support bodily familiarity but not a specific profession or suspect.
- Apron evidence is stronger than the adjacent graffiti, letters, or later kidney attribution.

## Qualifications

The episode does not independently verify every biographical, medical, or timing detail. Witness descriptions, exact anatomical competence, the route to Goulston Street, authorship of the graffiti, and the provenance of the “From Hell” kidney remain uncertain. Poverty and alcohol use explain exposure and identification difficulty, not culpability for her death.

- Separated evidence of practical anatomical familiarity from claims of formal medical training.
- Distinguished the apron trail from uncertain graffiti and letter provenance.
