# Cassivellaunus

## Overview

Cassivellaunus was the principal British opponent of [[JuliusCaesar]] during the larger expedition of 54 BC.

## Current Profile

The episode presents Cassivellaunus as a powerful ruler north of the Thames whose earlier defeat of the Trinovantian king drove [[Mandubracius]] into exile. Caesar's return therefore intersected with an existing dynastic struggle: the [[Trinovantes]] and five other groups submitted or allied with Rome as Caesar advanced.

Cassivellaunus used chariot warfare and indirect pressure but could not prevent the Thames crossing or the attack on his stronghold. He negotiated through [[Commius]], accepted Mandubracius's restoration, provided hostages, and promised tribute. The settlement limited him without converting his territory into a Roman province.

## Key Characteristics

- Leading British opponent of Caesar in 54 BC.
- Rival whose expansion created the Trinovantian exile that Caesar could exploit.
- Commander associated with chariot warfare and resistance beyond the Thames.
- Negotiating ruler who retained power under a settlement rather than suffering annexation.

## Evidence

- British political rivalry: [[499-the-roman-conquest-of-britain-julius-caesars-invasion-part-1-glt9770560726]] connects him to the overthrow of Mandubracius's father and the prince's appeal to Caesar.
- Military resistance: [[499-the-roman-conquest-of-britain-julius-caesars-invasion-part-1-glt9770560726]] describes opposition at the Thames, chariot tactics, and the storming of a stronghold.
- Negotiated outcome: [[499-the-roman-conquest-of-britain-julius-caesars-invasion-part-1-glt9770560726]] records mediation by Commius, hostages, promised tribute, and Mandubracius's restoration.

## Qualifications

The surviving narrative is Caesar's interested account, mediated here through the episode. Cassivellaunus's exact territorial authority, the location of his stronghold, the scale of coalition shifts, casualties, and whether annual tribute was subsequently delivered remain uncertain.

## What Changed

- Established Cassivellaunus as both Caesar's military opponent and a participant in a negotiated, non-provincial settlement.

## Relationships

- [[JuliusCaesar]] - invading commander who defeated and negotiated with him.
- [[Mandubracius]] - dynastic rival restored under Roman protection.
- [[Trinovantes]] - polity whose submission weakened his coalition.
- [[Commius]] - intermediary in his settlement with Caesar.
- [[CaesarsBritishExpeditions]] - campaign context for his resistance and accommodation.
