## Overview

江都王刘建 / Liu Jian is synthesized as [[LiuFeiJiangduKingWesternHan|江都王刘非 / 刘飞]]'s son and successor, the coercive household actor in [[NuoJiWesternHan|淖姬]]'s story, and the father of [[LiuXijunPrincessWesternHan|刘细君]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-382-2-yisheng-zuoguo-sanwei-zhuhou-feizi-gudai-chaomei-nuoji-de-beiqing-rensheng-lt06-as48q9qlpt_-dfdx_jsidjy|Hanji 382-2]] centers his seizure of Nuo Ji and death after a rebellion accusation; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-370-1-jiemi-hanchao-diyiwei-yu-xiongnu-heqin-de-gongzhu-lgetvmacjnoxhqfzymupxsk7fknk|Hanji 370-1]] adds how that death reshapes his daughter's childhood and later diplomatic vulnerability.

## Current Profile

The episode portrays Liu Jian as a negative contrast to his father. Where Liu Fei is described as heroic and martial, Liu Jian is described as sexually predatory and politically reckless. His seizure of Nuo Ji during the burial interval is the source's sharpest sign that a consort protected by one king could become exposed as soon as household authority changed.

Liu Jian's later behavior escalates the risk field from household coercion to state crime. The source says he makes an imperial seal privately, is accused of rebellion, faces Han Wudi's investigation, and kills himself out of fear. His death then opens the way for Zhao king Liu Pengzu to obtain Nuo Ji.

Hanji 370-1 adds the descendant cost. It identifies Xijun as Liu Jian's daughter and says his suicide causes the family to fall from its former status while she is still a child. The host says Han does not kill the household and leaves it in Jiangdu; that account helps explain Xijun's combination of authentic royal lineage and weak protection, but the stronger claim that this vulnerability caused her later selection remains interpretive.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han Jiangdu king and successor of Liu Fei.
- Source's negative filial contrast to Liu Fei's hero-like image.
- Seizes Nuo Ji before Liu Fei's burial, showing coercive household succession exposure.
- Described as dissolute and politically reckless.
- Privately makes an imperial seal and later kills himself after a rebellion accusation.
- His death enables Nuo Ji's later transfer into Liu Pengzu's Zhao household.
- Father of Liu Xijun, whose childhood status collapses after his death.

## Qualifications

This page is source-bounded. It does not settle Liu Jian's full reign, the legal file behind the rebellion accusation, the exact object described as an imperial seal, or Nuo Ji's own testimony. Hanji 370-1's account of Xijun's age, the household's post-case legal position, and the inference that family weakness caused her selection for Wusun remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Created from Hanji 382-2 as the coercive Jiangdu successor whose seizure of Nuo Ji and rebellion accusation make princely-household succession dangerous.
