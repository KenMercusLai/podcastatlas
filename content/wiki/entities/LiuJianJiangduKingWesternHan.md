## Overview

江都王刘建 / Liu Jian enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-382-2-yisheng-zuoguo-sanwei-zhuhou-feizi-gudai-chaomei-nuoji-de-beiqing-rensheng-lt06-as48q9qlpt_-dfdx_jsidjy|Hanji 382-2]] as [[LiuFeiJiangduKingWesternHan|江都王刘非 / 刘飞]]'s son and successor. His source role is to mark the collapse of [[NuoJiWesternHan|淖姬]]'s protection after Liu Fei's death: he seizes her before Liu Fei is buried and later kills himself after a rebellion accusation.

## Current Profile

The episode portrays Liu Jian as a negative contrast to his father. Where Liu Fei is described as heroic and martial, Liu Jian is described as sexually predatory and politically reckless. His seizure of Nuo Ji during the burial interval is the source's sharpest sign that a consort protected by one king could become exposed as soon as household authority changed.

Liu Jian's later behavior escalates the risk field from household coercion to state crime. The source says he makes an imperial seal privately, is accused of rebellion, faces Han Wudi's investigation, and kills himself out of fear. His death then opens the way for Zhao king Liu Pengzu to obtain Nuo Ji.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han Jiangdu king and successor of Liu Fei.
- Source's negative filial contrast to Liu Fei's hero-like image.
- Seizes Nuo Ji before Liu Fei's burial, showing coercive household succession exposure.
- Described as dissolute and politically reckless.
- Privately makes an imperial seal and later kills himself after a rebellion accusation.
- His death enables Nuo Ji's later transfer into Liu Pengzu's Zhao household.
