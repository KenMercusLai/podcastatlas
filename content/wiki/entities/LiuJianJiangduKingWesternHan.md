## Overview

江都王刘建 / Liu Jian is synthesized as [[LiuFeiJiangduKingWesternHan|江都王刘非 / 刘飞]]'s son and successor, the coercive household actor in [[NuoJiWesternHan|淖姬]]'s story, and the father of [[LiuXijunPrincessWesternHan|刘细君]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-382-2-yisheng-zuoguo-sanwei-zhuhou-feizi-gudai-chaomei-nuoji-de-beiqing-rensheng-lt06-as48q9qlpt_-dfdx_jsidjy|Hanji 382-2]] centers his seizure of Nuo Ji and death after a rebellion accusation; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-309-2-huangyin-zhi-ji-de-ta-jing-hai-guinv-yuanjia-80sui-laotou-lpya7pfxrss9_qwcbcx_3hnliuyl|Hanji 309-2]] expands the episode-attributed cruelty and rebellion preparations; and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-370-1-jiemi-hanchao-diyiwei-yu-xiongnu-heqin-de-gongzhu-lgetvmacjnoxhqfzymupxsk7fknk|Hanji 370-1]] adds how his death reshapes his daughter's childhood and later diplomatic vulnerability.

## Current Profile

The episode portrays Liu Jian as a negative contrast to his father. Where Liu Fei is described as heroic and martial, Liu Jian is described as sexually predatory and politically reckless. His seizure of Nuo Ji during the burial interval is the source's sharpest sign that a consort protected by one king could become exposed as soon as household authority changed.

Liu Jian's later behavior escalates the risk field from household coercion to repeated lethal violence and state crime. Hanji 309-2 says he lets two guards drown for amusement, forbids their rescue, wrongfully kills nearly thirty-five people, joins his queen in curse ritual, gathers weapons, and fabricates imperial and military instruments. Alongside Hanji 382-2's shorter account of a privately made seal, the sources converge on investigation for rebellion preparations and suicide from fear. Hanji 309-2 then says the queen is executed and Jiangdu is abolished, while Hanji 382-2 follows the separate household consequence in which Zhao king Liu Pengzu obtains Nuo Ji.

Hanji 370-1 adds the descendant cost. It identifies Xijun as Liu Jian's daughter and says his suicide causes the family to fall from its former status while she is still a child. The host says Han does not kill the household and leaves it in Jiangdu; that account helps explain Xijun's combination of authentic royal lineage and weak protection, but the stronger claim that this vulnerability caused her later selection remains interpretive.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han Jiangdu king and successor of Liu Fei, used as a negative filial contrast to his father's hero-like image.
- Seizes Nuo Ji before Liu Fei's burial, showing coercive household succession exposure.
- Described as sexually predatory, deliberately cruel, and politically reckless.
- Accused of repeated wrongful killing, including forbidding rescue of two drowning guards.
- Prepares forged instruments and weapons before killing himself under rebellion investigation.
- His death enables Nuo Ji's later transfer into Liu Pengzu's Zhao household.
- Father of Liu Xijun, whose childhood status collapses after his death.

## Qualifications

This page is source-bounded. It does not settle Liu Jian's full reign, the legal file behind the rebellion accusation, the exact objects described as forged instruments, or Nuo Ji's own testimony. Hanji 309-2's sexual allegations, lake narrative, victim count, queen's role and punishment, administrative conversion, and tomb correction remain episode-attributed pending primary-text and archaeological comparison. Hanji 370-1's account of Xijun's age, the household's post-case legal position, and the inference that family weakness caused her selection for Wusun remain source-scoped; Hanji 309-2's stronger claim that her tragedy was caused by her father is treated as commentary rather than a settled causal chain.

## What Changed

- Added the episode-attributed pattern of deliberate cruelty, wrongful killing, curse ritual, forged state instruments, the queen's execution, and Jiangdu's abolition.
- Narrowed the father-to-daughter causal claim: Liu Jian's fall weakens Xijun's position, but it does not by itself establish every cause of her selection, illness, or death.
- Added Hanji 370-1's daughter and descendant-consequence layer through Liu Xijun.
- Created from Hanji 382-2 as the coercive Jiangdu successor whose seizure of Nuo Ji and rebellion accusation make princely-household succession dangerous.
