# 吴氏（刘备妻） / Lady Wu (wife of Liu Bei)

## Overview
吴氏 / Lady Wu is presented in Hanji 1146 as [[WuYiLateHan|吴懿]]'s sister, the widow of Liu Mao, and the woman whom [[LiuBei|刘备]] marries after taking Yizhou; she is later elevated to empress and empress dowager.

## Current Profile
The episode places Lady Wu at the intersection of household status and regime consolidation. Her prior marriage connects her to Liu Yan's family, while her brother Wu Yi connects her to an established Yizhou network. Liu Bei's marriage to her is therefore read as political integration. The episode also reports a physiognomic prediction of high status, but that motif is retained as source narrative rather than evidence of causation or character.

Lady Wu later becomes empress and, during the Later Lord's reign, empress dowager. The available source says she dies naturally, but it gives little evidence about her own preferences, actions, or influence.

## Key Characteristics
- Connects Liu Bei by marriage to Wu Yi and an incumbent Yizhou elite network.
- Carries a prior marital connection to Liu Mao and the Liu Yan household.
- Becomes the subject of a kinship and ritual objection addressed through historical precedent.
- Is later elevated from political spouse to empress and empress dowager.

- Family and widowhood: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1146-liubei-qu-guafu-beihou-de-suanji-taishenle-lv4if2rvouqruziv8pt-klrcasfp|Hanji 1146]] identifies Lady Wu as Wu Yi's sister and Liu Mao's widow.
- Political marriage: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1146-liubei-qu-guafu-beihou-de-suanji-taishenle-lv4if2rvouqruziv8pt-klrcasfp|Hanji 1146]] interprets her marriage to Liu Bei as a link to Yizhou's former ruling network.
- Ritual debate: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1146-liubei-qu-guafu-beihou-de-suanji-taishenle-lv4if2rvouqruziv8pt-klrcasfp|Hanji 1146]] says Liu Bei worries about shared imperial-clan kinship and Fa Zheng answers with the Duke Wen precedent.
- Later rank: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1146-liubei-qu-guafu-beihou-de-suanji-taishenle-lv4if2rvouqruziv8pt-klrcasfp|Hanji 1146]] says Lady Wu later becomes empress and then empress dowager.

## Qualifications
The source is a structured popular-history summary and is strongly state-centered. It does not recover Lady Wu's consent, preferences, household agency, political activity, or assessment of the marriage. The physiognomy story, kinship classification, negotiation, titles, timing, and later-life details require textual verification. Her political usefulness should not be mistaken for a complete biography.

## What Changed
- Established the first canonical profile for Lady Wu while making the limits of the state-centered marriage account explicit.
