# Robert Blum

## Overview

Robert Blum was a German radical, parliamentarian, and revolutionary figure presented in [[326-the-year-of-revolutions-1848-glt9746323554]] as a human face of the [[RevolutionsOf1848]].

## Current Profile

Blum represents the parliamentary strand of German revolution. He remained committed to debate in the [[FrankfurtParliament]] while [[FriedrichHecker]] chose armed insurrection. After going to Vienna with fraternal greetings and taking part in the city's defense, Blum was arrested and executed on November 9, 1848 despite invoking parliamentary immunity.

His path from spring enthusiasm to autumn execution compresses the revolutionary year's emotional and political arc. The source uses his farewell to his wife and his death to show how state recovery could destroy individual revolutionaries even when some constitutional and national claims survived them.

## Key Characteristics

- German radical associated with parliamentary rather than premature insurrectionary strategy.
- Member and symbolic figure of the Frankfurt Parliament.
- Participant in the Vienna revolutionary crisis.
- Executed after the Austrian army retook Vienna.
- Biographical bridge between revolutionary euphoria, fragmentation, repression, and memory.

## Qualifications

The source provides a compressed political biography, not a full account of Blum's ideas, organizing, family life, or conduct in Vienna. His representative role is Clark's narrative choice and should not imply that one life captures every current within 1848.

## What Changed

- Established Blum as the source's principal biographical case for parliamentary revolution and repression.
