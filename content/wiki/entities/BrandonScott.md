# Brandon Scott

## Overview

Brandon Scott is the Baltimore mayor presented in [[wolfgang-s-law-part-two|Wolfgang's Law - Part Two]] as the political leader who made comprehensive violence reduction a governing priority after growing up amid both persistent violence and aggressive policing.

## Current Profile

The episode ties Scott's policy commitment to lived experience. He contrasts broad stop-and-arrest practices that did not make his neighborhood feel safer with a coordinated strategy directed at the people and networks most exposed to serious violence. His role in the account is not merely to endorse a program but to require agencies and service providers to work as one prevention system.

## Key Characteristics

- Brings personal experience of violence and repeated police detention to public-safety policy.
- Rejects arrest volume as a sufficient proxy for safety or precise violence prevention.
- Prioritizes comprehensive intervention that combines support, prevention, community participation, and enforcement.
- Uses mayoral authority to demand cooperation across institutions with different professional identities and incentives.

## Evidence

### Lived policy context

- [[wolfgang-s-law-part-two]] says Scott grew up in Baltimore, experienced aggressive policing, and was detained and handcuffed without cause seven times between adolescence and early adulthood.

### Governing approach

- [[wolfgang-s-law-part-two]] contrasts his comprehensive violence-reduction priority with earlier mass-arrest policy and an incomplete punishment-heavy version of focused deterrence.

## Qualifications

This profile is limited to the episode's account and does not independently evaluate Scott's full administration, Baltimore crime data, program budgets, implementation record, or competing interpretations of the city's violence decline.
