## Overview

法雄 / Fa Xiong is presented as the Eastern Han Qingzhou inspector whose response to [[ZhangBailuLateHan|Zhang Bailu]]'s coastal rebellion makes surrender credibility, timing after amnesty, and local command discipline central to pacification.

## Current Profile

Fa Xiong enters the current evidence when the court appoints him to Qingzhou after Zhang Bailu and associated pirate groups kill senior local officials and gather several thousand followers. The local military impulse is pursuit: Wang Zong leads troops, and Donglai forces remain ready to chase the rebels. Fa Xiong instead argues that once an imperial amnesty has been issued, immediate pursuit would undercut the credibility of surrender and push rebels toward distant island refuges.

His approach initially works. Zhang Bailu's group returns to prior settlements and sends back captives, suggesting that the rebels are testing whether the state's amnesty promise is real. The failure lies in synchronization: Donglai troops do not disarm and do not fully align with Fa Xiong's timing, so the rebels panic and flee to Liaodong islands. Fa Xiong therefore becomes a case of sound pacification logic constrained by local execution.

## Key Characteristics

- Qingzhou inspector charged with responding to a dispersed coastal rebellion.
- Treats amnesty as a credibility instrument that must change military timing after proclamation.
- Understands terrain and mobility risk: frightened rebels can escape into harder-to-reach island zones.
- His policy shows partial success when rebels return captives and reappear near former settlements.
- His limits are revealed by subordinate or neighboring troops whose posture makes surrender unsafe in practice.

- Amnesty timing: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-785-donghan-dengsui-zhangkong-yuwang-caineng-xiaodao-zuihou-llfg4kjaautqlfewhplikvu3ry1u|Hanji 785]] reports Fa Xiong arguing against immediate pursuit after the imperial amnesty.
- Rebel response: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-785-donghan-dengsui-zhangkong-yuwang-caineng-xiaodao-zuihou-llfg4kjaautqlfewhplikvu3ry1u|Hanji 785]] says Zhang Bailu's followers returned captives, indicating that surrender credibility had some effect.
- Execution failure: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-785-donghan-dengsui-zhangkong-yuwang-caineng-xiaodao-zuihou-llfg4kjaautqlfewhplikvu3ry1u|Hanji 785]] attributes the renewed flight to Donglai troops failing to disarm and align with the pacification posture.
- Later endpoint: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-786-guren-weishenme-paichi-fanji-shuiguo-lmcwqwdxwe0ehuusfeoaqk82msm7|Hanji 786]] records the rebellion's later Liaodong defeat and Zhang Bolu/Bailu's death, showing that the failed surrender window moved the problem rather than resolving it.

## Qualifications

The current evidence does not yet give Fa Xiong's full biography, earlier offices, or later career. The spelling and naming of Zhang Bailu/Bolu vary across adjacent source notes, so Fa Xiong's page treats them as the same rebellion sequence while leaving the variation explicit.
