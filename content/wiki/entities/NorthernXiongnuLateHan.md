## Overview

北匈奴 / Northern Xiongnu is the late Eastern Han frontier field where pastoral resource pressure, renewed raids, Western Regions contestation, Han offensive return, envoy intimidation, border attacks, weakness, Southern Xiongnu opportunism, court credibility debates, [[DouXianEasternHan|Dou Xian]]'s contested campaign, battlefield defeat, Yu Chu recognition failure, and Xianbei succession pressure connect. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-741-weishenme-gudai-xiongnu-zongyao-ruqin-zhongyuan-lllpgozgi6yd5pscw8z5ofidk5rp|Hanji 741]] now supplies the backfilled starting layer: Northern Xiongnu raids are placed inside the broader [[SteppePastoralResourcePressure|steppe pastoral resource pressure]] frame, and Mingdi's 73 CE campaign begins the concrete Eastern Han answer.

## Current Profile

Hanji 741 reframes the opening problem. The episode first explains [[Xiongnu|Xiongnu]] raids through low rainfall, water and pasture movement, overgrazing risk, winter pressure, and the attraction of agrarian stores. For late Eastern Han, this broad ecological logic becomes a political problem because Northern Xiongnu raids threaten border confidence in the court. [[EmperorMingOfHan|Mingdi]] consults officials, hears [[GengBingEasternHan|Geng Bing]] argue for recovering the Western Regions, and authorizes a four-route offensive.

The 73 CE campaign is uneven but important. [[DouGuEasternHan|Dou Gu]]'s route reaches Tianshan, attacks the Huyan king, kills more than one thousand, takes [[YiwuGarrisonLateHan|Yiwu]], and stations soldiers. Geng Bing's route crosses desert but finds no decisive enemy, while [[ZhaiRongEasternHan|Zhai Rong]]'s route fails after questionable Southern Xiongnu guidance and ends in punishment. Northern Xiongnu is therefore a mobile opponent whose pressure can force Han action even when not every route can find or fix the target.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-742-banchao-chushi-xiyu-37-ren-da-sha-si-fang-ltutsinxtciar0tmpxkge0mnsnp1|Hanji 742]] supplies the next layer. Northern Xiongnu appears as a rival diplomatic and military pressure system. Its envoys reach [[ShanshanKingdomLateHan|Shanshan]] and make King Guang waver after initially receiving [[BanChaoEasternHan|Ban Chao]] respectfully. Its supervisory presence also shapes [[KhotanKingdomLateHan|Khotan]], where King Guangde treats Ban Chao coldly before the shaman incident. In the same year, Northern Xiongnu attacks Yunzhong before [[LianFanEasternHan|Lian Fan]]'s torch deception and dawn ambush.

The retrenchment layer is visible in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-747-banchao-gaosu-ni-zhenzheng-de-congming-ren-dou-you-shiminggan-lj5z9tayvosa-jcufyxwuc35dzvi|Hanji 747]]. The Gao Lin Wenyu Du king of Northern Xiongnu returns to Zhuoye Mountain, where Southern Xiongnu remnants, Han border soldiers, and Wuhuan jointly attack and defeat the Northern Xiongnu side. The same episode later says [[EmperorZhangOfHan|Zhangdi]] withdraws Yiwu Lu tuntian troops, after which Northern Xiongnu occupies the position again.

The next current layer is 85-88 CE weakness and restitution politics. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-755-zhege-chaodai-huangshang-jiu-kaishi-guli-shengwa-le-lj7bdkxkdtjhpo469yxhskjyfdco|Hanji 755]] says Northern Xiongnu groups defect to Han in many batches while [[SouthernXiongnu|Southern Xiongnu]], Dingling, [[Xianbei|Xianbei]], and Western Regions actors attack from different directions. After Southern Xiongnu seizes Northern Xiongnu captives and livestock, Meng Yun warns that failure to return them could make Northern Xiongnu think Han had deceived them after reconciliation. Hanji 759 then says famine and internal disorder send thousands to Southern Xiongnu each year, prompting an attack request that Geng Bing supports and [[SongYiEasternHan|Song Yi]] resists on cost, Xianbei, and outer-screen grounds.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-761-cong-sixingfan-dao-shachang-yingxiong-ta-shi-ruhe-zuodao-de-lmcllzgpj19dstnzfztstrkawuex|Hanji 761]] makes the moral ambiguity sharper once Dou Xian's campaign is proposed. Officials argue that attacking a far-off, already pressured enemy during mourning and spring farming is not righteous and will burden the people. [[HeChangEasternHan|He Chang]] adds that Northern Xiongnu has not committed a new offense requiring revenge. Those objections fail, and the campaign proceeds to [[JiluoshanBattleLateHan|Jiluoshan]], mass surrender, pursuit, captured livestock, and the [[YanranInscription|Yanran inscription]].

Hanji 763 and Hanji 764 deepen the collapse and its memory. Han forces and Southern Xiongnu pressure wound the Northern Xiongnu chanyu, take royal family members, and later defeat him at Jinwei Mountain. The sources associate westward Northern Xiongnu remnants with later Huns, Gothic displacement, and pressure on the [[RomanEmpire|Roman Empire]], but the current judgment stays cautious: the chain is interpretive and qualified, not a proven continuous identity or monocausal explanation.

Hanji 765 and Hanji 767 shift from defeat to failed settlement. After the Northern Xiongnu chanyu disappears, [[YuChuNorthernXiongnu|Yu Chu]] asks Han to recognize him as chanyu. Dou Xian supports recognition, but [[YuanAnEasternHan|Yuan An]] and [[RenKuiEasternHan|Ren Kui]] warn that copying the Southern Xiongnu arrangement would damage older commitments, anger Wuhuan and Xianbei, and cost too much. After Dou falls, Yu Chu abandons the return arrangement, and Han cavalry under Wang Fu and [[RenShangLateHan|Ren Shang]] kill him and destroy his force. Xianbei then occupy abandoned Northern Xiongnu lands, and many former Xiongnu households begin calling themselves Xianbei.

## Key Characteristics

- Late-Han steppe opponent whose raids are now linked to a broader pastoral resource-pressure explanation.
- Mobile frontier force that can trigger Han credibility problems even when Han commanders struggle to find decisive contact.
- Western Regions pressure actor whose envoys and supervision shape Shanshan and Khotan before Ban Chao's coercive reversals.
- Border attacker and route-node occupier, including the Yunzhong attack and later reoccupation of Yiwu after Han withdrawal.
- Weakening polity whose famine, defections, and multi-sided attacks create both opportunity and diplomatic risk for Han.
- Campaign target whose Jiluoshan/Jinwei defeats become both military success and morally contested state action.
- Collapsing population field whose abandoned lands and households feed Xianbei expansion and migration-memory claims.

## Qualifications

The page treats Hanji 741's pastoral pressure explanation as an upstream frame, not as a complete explanation for every Northern Xiongnu decision. Later evidence still shows diplomacy, leadership, court faction, Southern Xiongnu incentives, Xianbei incentives, and Han campaign cost shaping outcomes. The Northern-Xiongnu-to-European-Huns chain remains a qualified memory layer rather than a settled identity proof.

Route commanders, exact force counts, Huyan king identity, Zhuoye/Sanmulou geography, and commandery-name details remain source-scoped where Hanji 741's transcript is noisy.
