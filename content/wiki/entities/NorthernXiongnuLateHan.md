## Overview

北匈奴 / Northern Xiongnu is the late Emperor-He-era frontier field where [[DouXianEasternHan|Dou Xian]]'s campaign, Southern Xiongnu opportunism, claimant recognition, surrender reversal, Xianbei succession pressure, and later migration memory connect. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-763-gaixie-lishi-donghan-douxian-po-bei-xiongnu-zhi-zhan-ltlctmb-iprvggoit46-qohm0tb|Hanji 763]] now supplies the detailed collapse layer: Southern Xiongnu forces wound the chanyu, Geng Kui and [[RenShangLateHan|Ren Shang]] defeat him at Jinwei Mountain, Dou's larger campaign wins at [[JiluoshanBattleLateHan|Jiluoshan]], and [[YanranInscription|Yanran]] turns the victory into public memory. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-764-ta-weile-huoming-jing-jianjie-mie-le-luoma-diguo-lpk0c6muneabhk1oonzyjvawwf4|Hanji 764]] preserves the same defeat as a qualified [[TransEurasianMigrationCascade|trans-Eurasian migration-cascade]] starting point, while [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-765-donghan-fengyun-nengchen-nengbuneng-gandiao-bahu-waiqi-ltnvdqnyofuelaklssbquguwivan|Hanji 765]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-767-congming-ren-dou-shanchang-yanxi-lq3ol7y-jdh7dnnfkmtfdg4ibryq|Hanji 767]] track the failed [[YuChuNorthernXiongnu|Yu Chu]] recognition arrangement after the chanyu disappears.

## Current Profile

Hanji 763 makes Northern Xiongnu weakness visible before the recognition dispute. Dou Xian first applies pressure by retaking [[Yiwu|Yiwu]], causing nearby Cheshi polities to submit hostages. The Northern Xiongnu chanyu then seeks court access after Han returns his hostage brother, but the [[SouthernXiongnu|Southern Xiongnu]] chanyu attacks first, sending Shizi and allied cavalry through Jilu frontier routes, wounding the chanyu, taking royal family members, and killing or capturing large numbers. The source treats this as internal Xiongnu competition as well as Han frontier pressure.

The 91 CE layer deepens the collapse. Dou Xian reads Northern Xiongnu power as thin and sends Geng Kui and Ren Shang from Juyan toward Jinwei Mountain, where they surround and defeat the chanyu. The chanyu escapes and disappears, but the source says Han troops had advanced farther beyond the frontier than any previous Han anti-Xiongnu operation. The broader campaign then centers on Jiluoshan, where Dou defeats the main force, records large casualties and captures, and commemorates the victory through Ban Gu's Yanran inscription.

Hanji 763 and Hanji 764 both turn this defeat into a long-range historical interpretation. The current judgment stays cautious: the sources associate westward Northern Xiongnu remnants with later Huns, Gothic displacement, and pressure on the [[RomanEmpire|Roman Empire]], but they do not prove a continuous identity chain or make Rome's fall monocausal. Hanji 763 is especially explicit that the European-evidence portion is deferred to the next installment.

Hanji 765 and Hanji 767 shift from defeat to failed settlement. After the Northern Xiongnu chanyu disappears, Yu Chu, identified as the chanyu's younger brother and right Guli king, asks Han to recognize him as chanyu. Dou Xian supports recognition, but Yuan An and Ren Kui warn that copying the Southern Xiongnu arrangement would damage older commitments, anger [[Wuhuan|Wuhuan]] and [[Xianbei|Xianbei]], and cost too much. Emperor He accepts Dou's proposal anyway. After Dou falls, Yu Chu abandons the return arrangement, and Han cavalry under Wang Fu and Ren Shang kill him and destroy his force.

The aftereffect is a steppe-transition story. Hanji 763 says Northern Xiongnu westward movement leaves a grassland power vacuum, Xianbei fill it, and Southern Xiongnu and other Hu groups move inward under pressure. Hanji 767 gives the tighter Northern Xiongnu-to-Xianbei version: Xianbei occupy abandoned Northern Xiongnu lands, and many former Xiongnu households begin calling themselves Xianbei.

## Key Characteristics

- Remnant frontier power weakened by Southern Xiongnu attack, Han pursuit, and leadership disappearance.
- Main opponent in Dou Xian's Jiluoshan victory and Yanran commemoration.
- Migration-memory starting point for a qualified chain from steppe displacement to European Hun and Roman pressure.
- Recognition field where Yu Chu's submission request threatens older Southern Xiongnu commitments and neighboring Wuhuan-Xianbei relations.
- Surrender field whose arrangements are sensitive to Han court stability and Dou Xian's political survival.
- Steppe vacuum whose collapse makes Xianbei expansion and remnant identity shift visible in the source sequence.
- Source-bounded field where casualty, captive, distance, and migration-chain details remain evidentiary cautions.

## Qualifications

This is a narrow late-Han remnant page, not a complete Xiongnu history. Hanji 763 and Hanji 764 preserve the Northern-Xiongnu-to-Hun-to-Rome chain as a qualified source association, not as settled ethnogenesis, direct causation, or a complete explanation for Rome's fall. Yu Chu's name is normalized under this stable route even though Hanji 765 uses 于除 and Hanji 767 uses 俞厨. The sources' large figures, exact route geography, Puli Sea, Jinwei Mountain, Jiluoshan, the mechanism of mass identity shift, and the European migration connection remain source-scoped pending additional evidence.
