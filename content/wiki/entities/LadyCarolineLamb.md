# Lady Caroline Lamb

## Overview

Lady Caroline Lamb was an aristocratic writer and the wife of William Lamb whose affair with [[LordByron|Lord Byron / 拜伦]] became one of the central scandals of his early celebrity.

## Current Profile

[[442-lord-byron-dangerous-liaisons-part-3-glt1176702097]] presents Caroline as restless, theatrical, romantically intense, and already captivated by Byron's poetry before their affair. Her page-boy clothing played with gender and matched Byron's attraction to youthful masculine figures, while her social arrangements accommodated his physical discomfort and helped her monopolize his attention.

After Byron withdrew, her behavior became increasingly exposing and self-destructive: the episode reports intrusion into his rooms, an attempted elopement, intimate correspondence, public self-wounding, and later circulation of allegations about Byron and [[AugustaLeigh|Augusta Leigh]]. She is therefore neither a disposable comic pursuer nor a neutral witness; she was harmed within the relationship and also became an active force in the scandal that followed.

## Key Characteristics

- Aristocratic writer and society figure married to William Lamb.
- Early reader and admirer whose attachment joined poetry, celebrity, and erotic projection.
- Gender-nonconforming performer within the affair through page-boy dress and role play.
- Rejected partner whose pursuit became intrusive, public, and self-destructive.
- Important but interested transmitter of severe allegations about Byron.

## Evidence

- Attraction and performance: [[442-lord-byron-dangerous-liaisons-part-3-glt1176702097]] describes Caroline's fascination with Byron's poetry, social pursuit, and page-boy presentation.
- Escalation and rupture: [[442-lord-byron-dangerous-liaisons-part-3-glt1176702097]] reports room intrusion, an attempted elopement, the intimate hair cutting, and Byron's consultation with [[LadyMelbourne|Lady Melbourne]].
- Public scandal: [[442-lord-byron-dangerous-liaisons-part-3-glt1176702097]] connects her public self-wounding and later allegations to the widening social crisis around Byron.

## Qualifications

The episode is a narrative synthesis, and Caroline's motives, mental state, reported gestures, and role in spreading specific allegations depend on mediated testimony. Her conduct should not be used to erase Byron's cruelty or to convert an interested participant into an unimpeachable witness.
