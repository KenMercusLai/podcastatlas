## Overview

耿恭 / Geng Gong is an Eastern Han frontier officer whose current profile now has two contrasting layers: Hanji 746 records an earlier survival-and-rescue episode against [[NorthernXiongnuLateHan|Northern Xiongnu]], while Hanji 749 records later Qiang-frontier effectiveness followed by politically distorted accountability after he offends [[MaFangEasternHan|Ma Fang]].

## Current Profile

Hanji 746 gives Geng Gong the heroic endurance layer. By the time [[FanQiangEasternHan|Fan Qiang]] reaches the isolated city, Geng and the remaining defenders have only twenty-six people left and are still preparing for battle. Fan's arrival turns a doomed holdout into a rescue scene, but the retreat remains brutal: they fight while withdrawing, and only thirteen of the original group reach Yumen.

[[ZhengZhongFrontierEasternHan|Zheng Zhong]] then turns the event into recognized service. After seeing the survivors' condition and hearing the story, he memorializes that Geng Gong held an isolated city with tiny numbers against a much larger Xiongnu force and preserved Han honor. After Geng reaches Luoyang, he is appointed cavalry commandant.

Hanji 749 gives the later Qiang campaign layer. The court sends Ma Fang as acting chariot-and-cavalry general and includes Geng Gong among the commanders moving against Mi Wu and Buqiao's coalition. Ma Fang's victory and Buqiao's later surrender reduce the rebellion's main pressure.

After Ma Fang returns to court, Geng Gong remains in the field with other forces. He attacks Qiang groups that had not yet submitted, kills more than a thousand people according to the source, and receives the surrender of thirteen Qiang tribes with tens of thousands of people. On field performance alone, the source presents him as effective.

The ending changes the meaning of that success. Because Geng Gong had previously offended Ma Fang, the campaign monitor guesses Ma Fang's preference and impeaches him. Geng Gong is summoned back, jailed, and removed from office. The page therefore treats him as both a rescued loyalist hero and a later case where outer-relative command can distort accountability.

## Key Characteristics

- Eastern Han frontier officer whose current evidence spans Northern Xiongnu siege survival and Qiang-frontier command.
- Isolated defender whose endurance becomes meaningful because Fan Qiang attempts rescue rather than leaving him forgotten.
- Recognition case because Zheng Zhong's memorial converts extreme survival into official merit.
- Zhangdi-era Qiang-frontier commander operating inside Ma Fang's campaign structure.
- Effective field officer who secures additional Qiang submissions after the main commander is recalled.
- Subordinate power-risk case because prior offense against Ma Fang becomes relevant after victory.
- Example of accountability distortion when monitors anticipate the preference of an imperial in-law commander.

## Qualifications

The current page is bounded to Hanji 749 and Hanji 746. Hanji 746 supplies the rescue endpoint but does not reconstruct the full siege chronology, exact fortress geography, or complete Geng-family background. The "Shule city" in the rescue episode is kept source-scoped and is not merged with the existing [[ShuleKingdom|Shule Kingdom]] route. Hanji 749 supports an accountability-risk reading, but it does not prove that Ma Fang personally ordered the impeachment.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 746 as Geng Gong's earlier isolated-defense, Fan Qiang rescue, Zheng Zhong memorial, and cavalry-commandant recognition layer.
- Reframed Geng Gong as both a rescued loyalist hero and a later Qiang-frontier commander.
- Preserved Hanji 749's accountability-distortion judgment while narrowing it away from any claim that Ma Fang personally ordered removal.
