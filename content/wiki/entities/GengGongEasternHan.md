## Overview

耿恭 / Geng Gong is an Eastern Han frontier officer whose Hanji 749 profile is a compact case of battlefield effectiveness followed by politically distorted accountability. He fights under the Ma Fang-led Qiang campaign, secures major submissions after [[MaFangEasternHan|Ma Fang]] is recalled, and is then impeached after having offended Ma Fang earlier.

## Current Profile

Hanji 749 first places Geng Gong inside Zhangdi's response to Qiang rebellion. The court sends Ma Fang as acting chariot-and-cavalry general and includes Geng Gong among the commanders moving against Mi Wu and Buqiao's coalition. Ma Fang's victory and Buqiao's later surrender reduce the rebellion's main pressure.

After Ma Fang returns to court, Geng Gong remains in the field with other forces. He attacks Qiang groups that had not yet submitted, kills more than a thousand people according to the source, and receives the surrender of thirteen Qiang tribes with tens of thousands of people. On field performance alone, the source presents him as effective.

The ending changes the meaning of that success. Because Geng Gong had previously offended Ma Fang, the campaign monitor guesses Ma Fang's preference and impeaches him. Geng Gong is summoned back, jailed, and removed from office. The page therefore treats him as a case where outer-relative command can make subordinate accountability depend on court power signals rather than campaign results alone.

## Key Characteristics

- Zhangdi-era Qiang-frontier commander operating inside Ma Fang's campaign structure.
- Effective field officer who secures additional Qiang submissions after the main commander is recalled.
- Casualty-and-submission figure whose reported success remains source-bounded.
- Subordinate power-risk case because prior offense against Ma Fang becomes relevant after victory.
- Example of accountability distortion when monitors anticipate the preference of an imperial in-law commander.

## Qualifications

The current page is bounded to Hanji 749. It does not reconstruct Geng Gong's full career or merge him with other Geng-family figures until additional source notes warrant that expansion. The source supports an accountability-risk reading, but it does not prove that Ma Fang personally ordered the impeachment.

## What Changed

- Created Geng Gong as a standalone page because Hanji 749 gives him a complete success-and-removal arc.
- Framed his removal as a source-bounded accountability distortion rather than a proven private order from Ma Fang.
