## Overview

眭弘 / Sui Hong enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-401-suihong-zhiming-hanzhaodi-tuiwei-rangxian-huoguang-zaile-ll6pyzpjl0mlcfn0g1zjot2wf8s8|Hanji 401]] as the Western Han scholar-official who reads a cluster of anomalies under [[EmperorZhaoOfHan|汉昭帝刘弗陵]] as a sign that a nonordinary successor should rise, then urges the emperor to abdicate to a worthy person.

## Current Profile

The source presents Sui Hong as a learned but politically fatal omen interpreter. His reasoning draws on classical models: stones standing by themselves, revived shrine wood, revived willows, white crows, and leaves marked like "公孙病已立" become signs that Heaven points beyond the present ruler. He then links those signs to Yao's abdication to Shun and asks Han Zhao to seek a worthy person, yield the throne, and withdraw to a small fief.

That move crosses the court's hardest boundary. In the source, [[HuoGuangWesternHan|霍光]] treats the memorial not as ordinary remonstrance but as dangerous false omen speech and great rebellion. Sui Hong and the friend who forwards the memorial are executed.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han scholar-official whose classical omen reading becomes a succession intervention.
- Uses Yao-Shun abdication precedent to argue that Han Zhao should yield the empire.
- Treats natural and shrine anomalies as signs of political transfer rather than as isolated portents.
- Does not know who or where the "Gongsun" figure is, leaving the interpretation speculative inside the source.
- Becomes an example of how omen language can become capital politics when it names an alternative holder of the throne.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 401. It does not reconstruct Sui Hong's full biography, original memorial text, Gongyang learning, the primary-source legal wording, or later historiographical treatments of the "公孙病已立" sign. The anomaly reports are preserved as source claims and political language, not as verified supernatural events.
