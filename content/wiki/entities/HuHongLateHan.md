# 胡闳 / Hu Hong (late Han)

## Overview

Hu Hong is introduced in Hanji 855 as the Jinan chancellor appointed to replace [[DuanJiongLateHan|段熲]] as Protector of the Qiang after Duan is blamed and punished.

## Current Profile

The current evidence presents Hu Hong mainly through incapacity under frontier pressure. After [[GuoHongLateHan|郭闳]]'s blame-shifting removes Duan Jiong from the Qiang-front office, the court appoints Hu Hong as the replacement. The source says Hu Hong lacks the reputation and planning capacity needed to restrain the Qiang groups.

His role is therefore transitional but important. Under his watch, the Qiang side becomes more aggressive, attacking military camps, frontier forts, and commandery areas. This failed succession helps explain why [[HuangfuGuiLateHan|皇甫规]]'s later memorial can argue that frontier disorder is not just a matter of enemy strength but of the governing side's personnel and credibility.

## Key Characteristics

- Jinan chancellor appointed to the Protector of the Qiang role.
- Replacement for Duan Jiong after Duan is blamed and punished.
- Lacks the reputation and strategic capacity the source says the frontier office requires.
- Presides over continued Qiang attacks on camps, forts, and commanderies.
- Functions as a negative personnel-choice bridge before Huangfu Gui's intervention.

## Evidence

- Appointment path: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-855-ni-you-duo-wuqing-jiu-you-duo-qiangda-lulbpnufi8xfijrviufo6biuy-h1|Hanji 855]] says the court appoints Hu Hong after recalling and punishing Duan Jiong.
- Capacity problem: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-855-ni-you-duo-wuqing-jiu-you-duo-qiangda-lulbpnufi8xfijrviufo6biuy-h1|Hanji 855]] says Hu Hong has no prestige or planning ability sufficient to resist the Qiang threat.
- Frontier consequence: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-855-ni-you-duo-wuqing-jiu-you-duo-qiangda-lulbpnufi8xfijrviufo6biuy-h1|Hanji 855]] says Qiang forces become more arrogant, take camps and defensive points, and raid multiple commanderies.

## Qualifications

The source gives Hu Hong as a personnel and frontier-capacity case, not as a developed biography. His Jinan role is preserved as source wording; no separate Jinan commandery page is created from this brief notice.
