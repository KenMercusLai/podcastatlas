# David Sinclair

## Overview
David Sinclair is presented in the source as a Harvard genetics professor researching why aging occurs and whether aspects of it can be slowed or reversed.

## Current Profile
In the bounded episode, Sinclair is both a mechanistic aging researcher and a public interpreter of longevity interventions. He advances [[EpigeneticAgingInformationTheory]], describes experimental work on aging clocks and retinal-neuron reprogramming, and links fasting, exercise, sirtuins, mTOR, NAD, and cellular stress responses. He also discloses personal use of fasting, NMN, resveratrol, metformin, and a statin, while acknowledging that animal findings, unpublished observations, epidemiology, and personal response do not have the same evidentiary weight as established human outcomes.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames aging as an upstream biological driver of multiple age-related diseases.
- Prioritizes loss of epigenetic information and cell identity within the hallmarks of aging.
- Uses animal manipulation and biological-age measurement to test aging mechanisms.
- Connects intermittent adversity with cellular defense and repair pathways.
- Combines research interpretation with disclosure of personal longevity practices.
- Distinguishes experimental promise from completed clinical validation, though several claims remain optimistic and source-scoped.

## Qualifications
This profile is bounded to one 2021 public interview and does not independently verify Sinclair's institutional role, study details, unpublished trial observations, supplement formulations, clinical timelines, or personal outcomes. His practices are anecdotes rather than prescriptions. The later [[NADTherapyEvidenceBoundary]] and [[MechanismOutcomeEvidenceHierarchy]] materially limit translation from pathway, mouse, biomarker, or self-report evidence to human healthspan or lifespan.
