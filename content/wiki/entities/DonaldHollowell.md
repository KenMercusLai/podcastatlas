# Donald L. Hollowell

## Overview

Donald L. Hollowell was a Black civil-rights attorney in segregated Georgia whose courtroom preparation, controlled manner, and insistence on dignity made legal advocacy a form of both case work and public leadership.

## Current Profile

[[mr-hollowell-didn-t-like-that-mr-hollowell-didn-t-like-that|Mr. Hollowell Didn’t Like That]] portrays Hollowell as operating against hostile judges, all-white juries, police violence, coerced confessions, rushed convictions, and the practical restrictions of segregation. His response combined meticulous factual challenge with symbolic authority: he corrected degrading language, continued speaking when a judge turned away, and cross-examined white officials without adopting their provocations.

The episode’s strongest profile claim is not that Hollowell always won. It is that his visible competence made defeat less total by showing Black spectators that official stories could be tested and authority could be exercised from a position the system expected to remain subordinate. His work for [[WillieNash|Willie Nash]], A.C. Hall, James Fair, and [[MartinLutherKingJr|Martin Luther King Jr.]] joins legal remedy, community confidence, and movement continuity while keeping those outcomes analytically distinct.

## Key Characteristics

- Civil-rights lawyer practicing under openly segregated and racially hostile institutions.
- Meticulous advocate who tested physical evidence, witness conditions, police accounts, and confession procedures.
- Controlled courtroom presence who used formal speech and dignity as strategic resources.
- Public leader addressing both legal decision-makers and the Black community observing the proceeding.
- Mentor and employer of [[VernonJordan|Vernon Jordan]] at the beginning of Jordan’s legal career.
- Persistent practitioner whose importance included both concrete releases and meaningful demonstrations of resistance after defeat.

## Evidence

### Evidentiary and courtroom discipline

- [[mr-hollowell-didn-t-like-that-mr-hollowell-didn-t-like-that]] describes Hollowell’s challenges to the alleged Nash murder weapon, conflicting police stories, coercion, eyewitness visibility, and the unsupported rape allegation.
- [[mr-hollowell-didn-t-like-that-mr-hollowell-didn-t-like-that]] reports that he exposed contradictions in the officer’s A.C. Hall account through methodical questioning without shouting.

### Dignity as public leadership

- [[mr-hollowell-didn-t-like-that-mr-hollowell-didn-t-like-that]] recounts his correction of a judge’s language, measured response when another judge turned away, and calm public explanation after King’s secret prison transfer.
- [[mr-hollowell-didn-t-like-that-mr-hollowell-didn-t-like-that]] places these acts before Black spectators who later repeated courtroom exchanges and treated visible legal authority as politically significant.

### Mentorship and endurance

- [[mr-hollowell-didn-t-like-that-mr-hollowell-didn-t-like-that]] says Jordan joined Hollowell directly after law school, helped release demonstrators on his first day, and traveled with him through segregated Georgia.
