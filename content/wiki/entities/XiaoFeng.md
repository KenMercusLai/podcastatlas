## Overview

萧峰 / Xiao Feng is a central character in [[TianLongBaBu|《天龙八部》]]. The bounded sources present him both as 阿朱's bereaved lover and [[Azi|阿紫]]'s nonreciprocating protector, and, in a short character-study preview, as a non-Han tragic hero admired for chivalric concern, boldness, integrity, and composure.

## Current Profile

The episode treats Xiao Feng's grief after accidentally killing 阿朱 as the scene through which A Zi first recognizes both his emotional depth and the security represented by his strength. He accepts responsibility for A Zi because she is A Zhu's sister and needs protection, but care never becomes romantic consent.

This boundary remains explicit. A Zi invokes A Zhu's death, demands continued companionship, and seeks the love attached to her sister's place, while Xiao Feng repeatedly affirms that A Zhu is the person he loves. His suicide at Yanmen Pass therefore cannot be read as fulfillment of A Zi's romance; her leap with his body imposes a final physical union he did not choose.

The separate Xiao Feng episode opening widens the intended frame from this relationship to heroism, ethnic identity, unusual origins, and tragic fate. Because the supplied excerpt ends just as the main discussion begins, its character labels are an agenda rather than a demonstrated full-life analysis.

## Key Characteristics

- Carries profound grief and responsibility after A Zhu's death.
- Is framed as a non-Han tragic hero whose identity and origins belong to the promised wider study.
- Is admired for chivalric concern, boldness, integrity, and composure under pressure.
- Provides A Zi with physical protection and a form of family security.
- Distinguishes care for a dependent relative from romantic reciprocity.
- Remains emotionally committed to A Zhu despite A Zi's persistence and sacrifice.
- Becomes the object onto which A Zi projects strength, safety, rivalry, and possession.

## Qualifications

This remains a deliberately narrow profile. The A Zi-centered episode develops grief, protection, and nonreciprocity, while the Xiao Feng-centered source is only a three-minute opening and does not deliver its promised analysis. The profile therefore does not yet synthesize his full biography, leadership, martial career, political tragedy, relationship with A Zhu, or reasons for suicide. Claims about what attracts A Zi and what his care means remain literary interpretations; the new source's canon-wide uniqueness claims are unverified, and its “肖峰” spelling is treated as a transcript error for 萧峰.
