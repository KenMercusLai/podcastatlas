# Keith Joseph

## Overview
Keith Joseph appears as the Conservative politician whose turn toward free-market argument helped prepare the ground for [[MargaretThatcher|Margaret Thatcher]] but whose own leadership hopes collapsed before the 1975 contest.

## Current Profile
In [[662-britain-in-the-70s-the-rise-of-thatcher-part-1-glt1785737730]], Joseph is the expected challenger to [[EdwardHeath|Ted Heath]] after Heath's two 1974 election defeats. The episode presents him as an important carrier of the emerging free-market mood, but also as politically self-wounding: a disastrous Birmingham speech about working-class mothers and birth control made him decide not to stand, prompting Thatcher to tell him that she would run if he would not.

## Key Characteristics
- Conservative free-market voice in the post-Heath ideological turn.
- Expected leadership challenger before his Birmingham speech damaged his viability.
- Political precursor whose ideas mattered even though he did not become leader.
- Contingency point in Thatcher's rise: his withdrawal opened the space she occupied.

## Evidence
- Ideological role: [[662-britain-in-the-70s-the-rise-of-thatcher-part-1-glt1785737730]] links Joseph to the rightward, free-market critique of Heathite policy.
- Collapse: [[662-britain-in-the-70s-the-rise-of-thatcher-part-1-glt1785737730]] says Joseph damaged himself with a Birmingham speech and then chose not to stand.
- Thatcher opening: [[662-britain-in-the-70s-the-rise-of-thatcher-part-1-glt1785737730]] reports Thatcher telling Joseph that if he would not stand, she would.
