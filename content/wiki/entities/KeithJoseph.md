# Keith Joseph

## Overview
Keith Joseph appears as the Conservative politician whose turn from Heathite corporatism toward free-market argument helped prepare the Thatcher turn, but whose own leadership prospects collapsed before the 1975 contest.

## Current Profile
Both sources make Joseph the expected ideological challenger to [[EdwardHeath|Ted Heath]] after the two 1974 election defeats. [[420-britain-in-1974-thatcher-enters-the-ring-part-4-glt2799978515]] emphasizes the immediate sequence: his Birmingham speech about the "human stock" and working-class birth control shocked contemporaries, and on 21 November he told [[MargaretThatcher|Margaret Thatcher]] he would not stand; she answered that she would. [[662-britain-in-the-70s-the-rise-of-thatcher-part-1-glt1785737730]] places that collapse inside the broader free-market and party realignment that Thatcher later carried.

## Key Characteristics
- He moved from Heathite corporatism toward a more radical free-market critique.
- He was the expected leadership challenger after Heath's two 1974 defeats.
- His Birmingham speech damaged his political viability and remains central to the sources' account of his withdrawal.
- His ideas mattered to the Thatcher turn even though he did not lead it.
- His withdrawal was the contingency that converted Thatcher from ally and potential supporter into candidate.

## Evidence
- Ideological role: [[662-britain-in-the-70s-the-rise-of-thatcher-part-1-glt1785737730]] links Joseph to the emerging free-market critique of Heathite policy.
- Speech and withdrawal: [[420-britain-in-1974-thatcher-enters-the-ring-part-4-glt2799978515]] describes the Birmingham speech, public shock, and Joseph's 21 November decision not to stand.
- Thatcher opening: both [[420-britain-in-1974-thatcher-enters-the-ring-part-4-glt2799978515]] and [[662-britain-in-the-70s-the-rise-of-thatcher-part-1-glt1785737730]] report Thatcher saying she would stand if Joseph would not.

## Qualifications
This is not a full Joseph biography. It covers his role in the free-market turn and leadership succession, while the interpretation of his speech, beliefs, and withdrawal remains episode-scoped and requires broader primary and biographical evidence.
