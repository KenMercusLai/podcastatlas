# Pedro de Ursúa

## Overview

Pedro de Ursúa was the Spanish commander appointed to lead the 1560 expedition from Peru toward [[ElDorado]] before mutineers aligned with [[LopeDeAguirre|Lope de Aguirre]] murdered him on 1 January 1561.

## Current Profile

Ursua's command combined imperial reward with colonial risk management. The viceroy promised him government of any conquered lands while using the expedition to move unemployed and potentially dangerous soldiers out of Peru. Once underway, leaking vessels, scarce food, rain, uncertain geography, and preferential treatment for his mistress Dona Ines weakened his authority.

The source does not make Ursua's failures sufficient justification for mutiny. Instead, it shows how logistical breakdown and visible command privilege gave Aguirre and Salduendo grievances they could convert into a coup framed as resistance to tyranny.

## Key Characteristics

- Crown-appointed expedition leader promised governorship of conquered territory.
- Commander of a large mixed force of Spaniards and Indigenous Peruvians entering Amazonia.
- Logistically compromised leader whose ships and rafts failed early.
- Politically weakened by his relationship with Dona Ines and perceived command privilege.
- Victim of a coup that initially used loyalist language while killing the king's governor.

## Evidence

- Appointment and mission: [[543-death-in-the-amazon-aguirre-the-wrath-of-god-glt3694360248]] connects Ursua's command to the viceroy's treasure ambitions and desire to remove dangerous soldiers.
- Command failure: [[543-death-in-the-amazon-aguirre-the-wrath-of-god-glt3694360248]] describes unusable vessels, scarcity, a reserved cabin, and falling confidence.
- Overthrow: [[543-death-in-the-amazon-aguirre-the-wrath-of-god-glt3694360248]] records his New Year's Day murder and the mutineers' language of liberty, kingship, and tyranny.

## Qualifications

The episode explains how grievances accumulated but does not establish that Ursua alone caused the expedition's failure. Amazonian distance, weather, provisioning, vessel construction, conquest expectations, and factional ambition all constrained his command.

## What Changed

- Created a profile separating Ursua's command failures from the mutineers' later attempt to legitimize murder.
