# Jamie Siminoff

## Overview
Jamie Siminoff is [[Ring]]'s founder and the central company-side voice in the wiki's Ring privacy branch. He appears first through reporting about the broader ambition behind [[RingSearchParty|Search Party]], then directly in a [[MarketplaceTech]] interview explaining [[RingTakeEncryption|TAKE encryption]], law-enforcement access, AI features, and the canceled [[FlockSafety|Flock Safety]] integration.

## Current Profile
Siminoff's current profile is not just founder biography; it is a source-position inside the privacy debate around Ring. In the earlier coverage, reported internal language tied Search Party to broader crime-reduction ambitions and helped explain why a pet-finding feature could look like surveillance infrastructure. In the later interview, Siminoff argues that Ring can keep AI-enabled safety features while making users the holders of the key to their own video.

His statements also narrow the Flock timeline. He says Ring announced it would look at integrating community requests with Flock, but the work never started and was canceled before it began. He frames backlash to Search Party as a future-misuse anxiety rather than opposition to finding lost dogs.

## Key Characteristics
- Siminoff is Ring's founder and a company-side interpreter of Ring's AI, privacy, and public-safety strategy.
- Earlier coverage ties him to reported internal language about using Search Party for broader crime reduction.
- Later coverage has him present TAKE encryption as default-on user key control for Ring video.
- He describes police community requests as local, anonymous, optional, and dependent on user unlocking.
- He frames public backlash as anxiety about future surveillance misuse that companies must answer with clearer controls and guardrails.
