# War in the Vendée

## Overview

The War in the Vendée was the counter-revolutionary and civil conflict ignited in 1793 by conscription and deepened by rural opposition to clerical policy, taxation, royal execution, and intervention from Paris.

## Current Profile

The episode presents the conflict as both a military emergency and a conceptual crisis for revolutionary claims about "the people." Peasants, priests, and landowners formed the Catholic and Royal Army, while Parisian radicals dismissed the insurgents as tools of aristocracy and religion; massacres, reprisals, and scorched-earth warfare then made the region a central driver and product of terror politics.

## Key Characteristics

- The February 1793 recruitment order for 300,000 soldiers was the immediate trigger.
- Religious, fiscal, political, and center-periphery grievances predated conscription.
- The insurgency joined peasants, priests, and landowners under a Catholic and royal banner.
- The conflict exposed the gap between abstract celebration of the people and rejection of actual dissenting peasants.
- Republican repression and insurgent violence escalated through massacres, reprisals, and scorched earth.

### Causes and mobilization

- [[707-the-terror-an-assassination-in-paris-part-1-glt6580043397]] connects conscription to accumulated resentment over clergy, taxes, regicide, and rule from Paris.

### Revolutionary category conflict

- [[707-the-terror-an-assassination-in-paris-part-1-glt6580043397]] notes that radicals treated rural insurgents as manipulated reactionaries despite revolutionary ideology's appeal to ordinary people.

### Violence and scale

- [[707-the-terror-an-assassination-in-paris-part-1-glt6580043397]] describes guerrilla war, massacre, reprisal, and scorched earth, while reporting an episode-level estimate of about 200,000 deaths.

## Qualifications

- The approximate death toll and proportion of regional population are source-scoped estimates.
- Whether republican repression should be classified as genocide remains an unresolved historiographical dispute in the episode.
- The source is a high-level account and does not reconstruct regional variation or the full chronology of the war.

## What Changed

- The Vendée is now represented as a distinct civil-war front in the 1793 crisis system.
- The wiki records its challenge to revolutionary claims about who counted as "the people."
