# Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart

## Overview

Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart was a Salzburg-born composer and former child prodigy whose movement from family-managed touring and court service toward Viennese freelancing made artistic freedom, patronage dependence, family conflict, and the modern idea of genius visible in one career.

## Current Profile

[[LeopoldMozart|Leopold Mozart]] recognized and promoted his son's unusual musical ability through European court and concert travel. As the marketability of childhood precocity declined, Wolfgang's career shifted toward composition, including an opera commission in Milan at fourteen. The same family enterprise that provided training, access, and reputation also created a conflict over adulthood: Leopold sought a secure court post, while Wolfgang increasingly wanted to test his powers outside Salzburg's servant hierarchy.

The 1781 break with the Archbishop of Salzburg moved Mozart into a more autonomous but unstable Viennese career. He could earn and display more than a Salzburg salary, marry Constanze Weber, and pursue ambitious opera, yet remained exposed to commissions, patrons, illness, war, audience demand, and credit. The episode reads *The Marriage of Figaro* through social hierarchy, resists treating *Don Giovanni* as a transparent self-portrait, and rejects the later story of the poisoned, forgotten pauper writing a supernatural Requiem for himself.

## Key Characteristics

- Exceptionally precocious performer and composer whose childhood ability became a European public phenomenon.
- Son within a family-managed career that joined education, travel, promotion, money, and parental control.
- Salzburg court musician who rejected being confined to aristocratic servant status.
- Viennese freelancer whose higher earning potential coexisted with recurring material insecurity.
- Opera composer whose formal ambition and social themes cannot be reduced to direct autobiography.
- Early-dead artist whose Requiem and financial difficulties were later absorbed into a misleading doom narrative.

## Qualifications

This is a cultural-biographical profile based on one live public-history episode, not a complete catalogue or technical musical study. Childhood dates and feats, income and expense comparisons, reported dialogue, family motives, the meaning of particular works, the anonymous commissioner's behavior, illness, burial circumstances, and cause of death remain episode-attributed or source-scoped. The note's claim that Mozart died at 36 conflicts with its own 1756-1791 chronology and is not adopted here. Correcting Romantic exaggeration does not make Mozart fully independent, financially secure, or free from family and patronage pressures.

## What Changed

- Created Mozart as the transitional composer between family-managed prodigy, court service, freelance ambition, and later genius mythology.
- Distinguished increased agency and income potential from material independence.
- Separated interpretations of the operas from claims that a work transparently reveals its creator's personality.
- Preserved late success and public recognition against the total-failure death myth.
