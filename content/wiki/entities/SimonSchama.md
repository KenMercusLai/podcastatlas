# Simon Schama

## Overview

Simon Schama is a historian whose *The Embarrassment of Riches* supplies the principal interpretive framework for the episode's account of Dutch cleanliness, material abundance, morality, and domestic art.

## Current Profile

In [[276-netherlands-the-maid-of-holland-glt8981353378]], Schama matters as an intellectual source rather than an interview participant. His framing helps the hosts read the [[DutchRepublic]] as a wealthy commercial society attempting to reconcile material prosperity with Calvinist seriousness and civic virtue. Household order, paintings of domestic work, and the [[MaidOfHolland]] become evidence within that interpretation.

## Key Characteristics

- Historian used by the hosts to interpret Dutch Golden Age material and moral culture.
- Associated here with *The Embarrassment of Riches*.
- Connects commercial abundance to anxiety about discipline, restraint, and virtue.
- Encourages a cultural-history reading of domestic images and ordinary practices as political evidence.

## Evidence

- Intellectual influence: [[276-netherlands-the-maid-of-holland-glt8981353378]] states that Schama's book shaped the episode's approach to the Dutch Republic.
- Interpretive contribution: [[276-netherlands-the-maid-of-holland-glt8981353378]] uses his materialism-and-morality frame to explain the symbolic importance of housework and domestic art.

## Qualifications

This page records Schama only through one podcast episode's use of one major work. It does not summarize his wider scholarship, public career, or debates over *The Embarrassment of Riches*. The episode's reliance on his frame is evidence of influence, not independent confirmation of every causal claim.
