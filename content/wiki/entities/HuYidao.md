## Overview

胡一刀 / Hu Yidao is the dead martial hero at the center of [[XueshanFeihu|《雪山飞狐》]]. Although he appears mainly through retrospective testimony, the episode treats him as the novel's clearest embodiment of chivalry and possibly its spiritual protagonist.

## Current Profile

Hu Yidao's stature is produced through small choices rather than prolonged exposition. He treats an innkeeper, servants, and the low-status Ping A-si as equals; learns Miao-family swordsmanship and immediately uses it to avenge the Miao family; refuses the advantage of a superior weapon; and withholds a potentially lethal final kick against [[MiaoRenfeng|苗人凤]]. These acts join power to fairness rather than letting victory define heroism.

His private fear adds rather than subtracts from the portrait. When Yan Ji sees him weeping, the apparent fear of death is later reinterpreted as concern that his wife will die with him and leave their infant son unprotected. Responsibility, tenderness, and mortality therefore complicate the heroic surface.

The effects of his conduct outlive him. Ping A-si devotes his life to protecting [[HuFei|胡斐]] because Hu Yidao recognized and aided him when hierarchy made that recognition unusual. The remembered consequence makes equality a practiced relation rather than a declared virtue.

## Key Characteristics

- Treats low-status people with equality and personal respect.
- Refuses unfair advantage even in a lethal duel.
- Converts newly learned skill into aid for his opponent's family.
- Combines martial courage with responsibility for spouse and child.
- Exercises restraint at the moment he could kill Miao Renfeng.
- Continues to shape later loyalty and moral judgment after death.

## Qualifications

This profile follows one podcast's reconstruction from a structured summary. The claim that Hu Yidao exceeds Miao Renfeng in martial skill, the comparison to Qiao Feng, and the designation "spiritual protagonist" are interpretations. Admiration for honorable combat does not remove the broader question of why inherited grievance is settled through lethal violence.

## What Changed

- Established Hu Yidao as an absent but causally active moral center.
- Grounded his chivalry in equality, fair means, restraint, and family responsibility.
- Preserved the distinction between admirable conduct and the violent feud containing it.
