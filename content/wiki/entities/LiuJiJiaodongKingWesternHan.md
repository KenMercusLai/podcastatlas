## Overview

胶东康王刘寄 / Liu Ji is a Western Han royal relative whose current wiki profile joins his implication in the aftermath of [[LiuAn|淮南王刘安]]'s rebellion case, the preservation of his princely line after his sudden death, and a later Shandong treasure legend provisionally attached to his tomb.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-316-1-ta-gong-yue-bai-wang-weihe-zhi-xia-baixing-yuelaiyue-qiong-lu_tr4m36rmz9sx38j4nvlmof5am|Hanji 316-1]] says Liu Ji knew something about Liu An's plan but did not promptly report it and instead secretly prepared military equipment. After the Huainan case is exposed, testimony reaches Liu Ji; fear and illness then precede his death before he designates an heir.

The episode also makes kinship politically decisive. It presents Liu Ji as a childhood companion and close maternal relative of [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]. Wudi's grief and pity are used to explain why the elder son Liu Xian succeeds in Jiaodong and the favored younger son Liu Qing receives the new Liu'an kingdom over former Hengshan territory. The durable point is not that the accusation was disproved, but that death, affection, and royal kinship prevented the case from extinguishing the line.

Liu Ji's current afterlife also includes a source-scoped tomb legend. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-310-1-weishenme-shuo-kangwangfen-neng-yanghuo-yige-sheng-lnau07_cmw4u08tyonm8tqjtfbwl|Hanji 310-1]] treats him as the likely “康王” behind “打开康王坟，山东不受贫” and connects that saying to a reported attempted tomb robbery around 2000 in the 六曲山 area near Pingdu. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-310-2-gumu-zhong-de-jinlvyuyi-zhen-neng-qisihuisheng-lj_f2c88afcbjraslzodaisgkqbq|Hanji 310-2]] makes extraordinary wealth more generally plausible by comparing excavated royal burials associated with Liu Sheng, the Shizishan Chu king, and Liu He. Those analogies do not identify or inventory Liu Ji's tomb: neither episode supplies excavation evidence proving its location, ownership, or contents.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han king of Jiaodong and close royal relative of Han Wudi.
- Implicated through testimony after the Huainan rebellion plot was exposed.
- Accused in the episode of knowing without reporting and of making military preparations.
- Died from fear-associated illness before naming an heir in the source's account.
- Left a line that Han Wudi preserved through Liu Xian's Jiaodong succession and Liu Qing's Liu'an grant.
- Later proposed as the Kang king behind a Shandong tomb-wealth proverb and a reported modern robbery attempt, with other rich royal tombs used only as analogies.

## Qualifications

This profile rests on three short structured episode summaries. They do not establish the full evidence against Liu Ji, whether military preparation amounted to a settled rebellion decision, the legal status of the case at his death, the precise genealogy, or the formal procedure and territorial consequences of his sons' enfeoffment. Fear is reported as preceding illness and death, not established as a medical cause. All three summaries' 刘继 is normalized to 刘寄: Hanji 316-1 supplies the matching Jiaodong, Huainan, Liu Xian, Liu Qing, and Liu'an identity chain, while Hanji 310-1 and 310-2 add the 康王 title and tomb legend. Analogies from other excavated royal tombs do not prove Liu Ji's tomb location, identity, contents, proverb history, or the details of the reported robbery case.
