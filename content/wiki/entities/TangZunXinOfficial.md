## Overview

Tang Zun / 唐尊 is a late-[[XinDynasty|新朝]] classicist-official who appears in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-644-shui-gan-he-huangdi-shizhe-taojiahuanjia-lkgn0idimbu6twz-symr5012f8sn|Hanji 644]] among famous scholars who serve Wang Mang and is developed in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-657-wangmang-fei-taizi-you-duo-erxi-lklx1rd39kgz-by4tbcpaagx4z-l|Hanji 657]] as a taifu after Ping Yan's death. The wiki treats him as the serving and performative-austerity qualification to the Xin-era refusal pattern.

## Current Profile

Hanji 644 first places Tang Zun among famous classicists who study deeply, act cautiously, and become honored under Wang Mang's regime. That placement matters because the same episode centers on [[GongShengXinScholar|龚胜]] and [[XueFangXinRecluse|薛方]] refusing service: Tang Zun shows that scholarly reputation did not produce one uniform anti-Xin response.

Hanji 657 then gives Tang Zun's fuller profile. He diagnoses state poverty and commoner hardship as linked to elite extravagance and poor management. His response is performative austerity: small sleeves, short clothes, a plain carriage, straw mats, pottery food vessels, and symbolic gifts of humble goods to high officials. He also polices gender separation in public by marking people with red-earth water, turning moral order into everyday visible discipline.

## Key Characteristics

- Taifu appointed during late-Xin crisis.
- Famous classicist whose service under Xin contrasts with Gong Sheng's and Xue Fang's refusal.
- Austerity exemplar whose plain consumption is made into a court model.
- Moral-discipline actor who turns ritual separation norms into public shaming.
- Official whose behavior Wang Mang praises as a standard for ministers.

## Qualifications

The page remains source-scoped because the episodes provide Tang Zun mainly as an exemplar, not as a full career biography. His austerity and service may represent sincere critique, court performance, accommodation, or some mix; the sources do not settle motive.
