# 韩愈 / Han Yu

## Overview

Han Yu was a [[TangDynasty|Tang]] writer and official whom the current sources present through literary influence, blunt remonstrance, repeated demotion, personal loss, continued local service, and a dangerous mission to the mutinous Chengde command.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-341-3-chuangzuo-331-ge-chengyu-de-dalao-you-duo-lihai-lpxxovwptomp2mmt64o5armp8xrk|Hanji 341-3]] constructs Han Yu's significance from continuity across reversals. His early examination failures do not prevent entry into office; punishment for criticizing extraction and the 819 Buddha-relic campaign does not end his willingness to serve; and the journey to [[Chaozhou|Chaozhou]], including severe family loss in the episode's telling, does not become permission for administrative withdrawal.

The same source joins this official ethic to literary memory through *Shishuo*, the *Memorial on the Buddha's Relic*, the “Blue Pass” poem, and the *Sacrificial Text to the Crocodiles*. Its closing judgment is therefore broader than fame: Han's profile is that of a writer-official whose prose, corrective speech, and conduct under demotion are treated as mutually revealing.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-341-4-bei-digu-de-ta-kancheng-zhongguo-zui-yinghe-de-shiren-lv78zkmugscl4fwhts-rak6mhyep|Hanji 341-4]] extends that continuity into direct physical risk. After [[WangTingcouTang|王廷凑]]'s Chengde seizure and a failed punitive campaign, Han accepts a pacification mandate, enters despite warnings, and addresses assembled troops with examples of earlier rebels' ruin. The episode then places *Early Spring Presented to Zhang Shiba* beside this mission, making close observation and lyrical delicacy part of the same profile rather than an exception to its “hardcore” political courage.

## Key Characteristics

- Influential prose writer represented by *Shishuo* and several works tied to political or administrative action.
- Persistent examination candidate whose early failures complicate a simple success narrative.
- Direct remonstrant willing to criticize local extraction and imperial religious policy despite career risk.
- Punished official whose rapid demotions make the cost of blunt correction concrete.
- Local administrator portrayed as continuing public work in Chaozhou rather than converting grievance into neglect.
- Commissioned persuader portrayed as entering a coercive military setting and addressing mutinous troops without armed force.
- Later moral exemplar whose reputation combines literary impact with [[ConscienceThroughDuty|duty under constraint]].

## Evidence

- Literary influence: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-341-3-chuangzuo-331-ge-chengyu-de-dalao-you-duo-lihai-lpxxovwptomp2mmt64o5armp8xrk|Hanji 341-3]] foregrounds *Shishuo*, multiple remembered phrases, and four works associated with teaching, remonstrance, exile, and local action.
- Persistence through failure: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-341-3-chuangzuo-331-ge-chengyu-de-dalao-you-duo-lihai-lpxxovwptomp2mmt64o5armp8xrk|Hanji 341-3]] presents repeated jinshi and boxue hongci failures before Han's official career.
- Remonstrance and punishment: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-341-3-chuangzuo-331-ge-chengyu-de-dalao-you-duo-lihai-lpxxovwptomp2mmt64o5armp8xrk|Hanji 341-3]] connects criticism of extraction to Yangshan and the Buddha-relic memorial to threatened execution and Chaozhou demotion.
- Duty after demotion: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-341-3-chuangzuo-331-ge-chengyu-de-dalao-you-duo-lihai-lpxxovwptomp2mmt64o5armp8xrk|Hanji 341-3]] says Han continued practical local work and uses the crocodile text as its vivid example.
- Mission under threat: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-341-4-bei-digu-de-ta-kancheng-zhongguo-zui-yinghe-de-shiren-lv78zkmugscl4fwhts-rak6mhyep|Hanji 341-4]] says Han accepts the Chengde mission, refuses to stop at the frontier, and answers military intimidation with a speech about rebellion's consequences.
- Literary range and later memory: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-341-4-bei-digu-de-ta-kancheng-zhongguo-zui-yinghe-de-shiren-lv78zkmugscl4fwhts-rak6mhyep|Hanji 341-4]] quotes Han's early-spring poem and uses later judgments by Su Shi and Zhang Zai to join literary, political, and moral reputation.

## Qualifications

This profile rests on two short, admiring public-history episode summaries, not a complete biography or textual study. The number and attribution of idioms, examination sequence, causal accounts of demotion, journey and family details, crocodile outcome, Chengde dialogue, troop response, released captives, and causal success of the pacification speech all remain source-scoped. Later praise by Su Shi and the application of Zhang Zai's maxim describe reception and moral framing rather than independently verifying the mission.

## What Changed

- Added the Chengde mission as a distinct form of public duty under bodily and military threat.
- Added the early-spring poem to prevent political hardness from flattening Han's literary range.
- Preserved the mission's success claims and later praise as source-bounded rather than independent verification.
