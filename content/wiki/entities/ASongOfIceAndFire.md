# 《冰与火之歌》 / A Song of Ice and Fire

[[ASongOfIceAndFire|《冰与火之歌》 / A Song of Ice and Fire]] enters the wiki through [[31-bing-yu-huo-zhi-ge-suiran-cong-bu-zicheng-lishi-594509848]], where [[MihuanChishu|蜜獾吃书]] reads it as a historically saturated fantasy world rather than as disguised history. The source emphasizes [[GeorgeRRMartin|George R. R. Martin / 乔治·R·R·马丁]]'s method of breaking apart and recombining European history, religion, myth, geography, and court politics.

The episode's strongest claim is not that Westeros equals medieval England or that one character equals one historical figure. It argues that the series feels historical because it uses [[HistoricalFantasySourceLayering]]: the [[WarsOfTheRoses|Wars of the Roses / 玫瑰战争]], Norman conquest, Roman and Greek analogies, crusading orders, Celtic forest religion, [[NorseMythology]], disease stigma, and city history all become source material for a world with its own institutions and memories.

The work also anchors [[FictionalHistoriography]]. The source highlights Samwell Tarly's archive problems to show that even inside the story world, old records, heroic tales, commander lists, and knightly legends can be wrong, partial, or anachronistic. That makes Martin's worldbuilding historical in texture because it simulates how societies misremember their own past.
