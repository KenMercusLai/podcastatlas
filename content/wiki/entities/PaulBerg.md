## Overview
Paul Berg is presented as the molecular biologist who stopped [[JanetMertz]]'s proposed recombinant-DNA experiment after colleagues raised an escape risk, then helped organize the voluntary pause and Asilomar safety process.

## Current Profile
In the source, Berg turns a very low-probability but potentially severe concern into collective scientific action. He consults colleagues, halts work with substantial scientific value, joins other laboratory leaders in calling for a temporary moratorium, and helps seek rules under which research can resume. His role illustrates responsible leadership but also the asymmetry between senior rule-makers and junior researchers who bore the immediate cost.

## Key Characteristics
- Senior scientist willing to halt promising work under severe uncertainty.
- Organizer of a temporary collective pause rather than an advocate for ending recombinant-DNA research.
- Participant in expert-led rule design that ultimately depended on external funding enforcement.
- Decision-maker whose safety action imposed concentrated costs on a junior researcher.

## Qualifications
The source does not reconstruct Berg's full reasoning, distinguish his individual proposals from the conference's collective output, or provide archival evidence for every reported motive. His role should not be reduced either to heroic precaution or to senior exclusion; the episode supports both responsibility and distributional scrutiny.
