# Charles VI of France

## Overview

Charles VI is the French king whose weakened government and divided kingdom became the constitutional center of the [[TreatyOfTroyes|Treaty of Troyes]].

## Current Profile

The sources depict Charles less as an active strategist than as the monarch around whom competing Armagnac, Burgundian, Dauphinist, and English claims operated. His recurring mental crises from the 1390s weakened his capacity to contain competition between [[LouisOfOrleans|Louis of Orléans]] and [[JohnTheFearless|John the Fearless]], while royal pardons and forced reconciliations failed to prevent civil war.

After Paris became unsafe, he, Queen Isabeau, and [[CatherineOfValois|Catherine]] withdrew to Troyes.

The 1420 treaty kept Charles as king while making [[HenryVOfEngland|Henry V]] regent and heir, thereby preserving formal continuity while disinheriting Charles's surviving son.

## Key Characteristics

- French king presiding over a collapse of coherent royal authority.
- Monarch whose recurring incapacity opened space for factional competition around the council and Dauphin.
- Dynastic focus of rival factional and English programs.
- Monarch retained by the Treaty of Troyes rather than immediately deposed.
- Father whose son Charles was displaced in favor of an English son-in-law.

## Evidence

- Incapacity and court competition: [[487-hundred-years-war-henry-v-s-invasion-of-france-part-1-glt7554933021]] recounts Charles's recurring breakdowns and connects them to rivalry between Orléans and Burgundy.
- Failed containment: [[487-hundred-years-war-henry-v-s-invasion-of-france-part-1-glt7554933021]] describes Charles pardoning John after Orléans's murder in an unsuccessful effort to avoid civil war.
- Political weakness: [[490-hundred-years-war-england-triumphant-part-4-glt2692942762]] places repeated dauphin deaths and factional violence around Charles's court.
- Flight and settlement: [[490-hundred-years-war-england-triumphant-part-4-glt2692942762]] records the royal family's move to Troyes and Charles's retention as king under the treaty.
- Succession rupture: [[490-hundred-years-war-england-triumphant-part-4-glt2692942762]] says Henry became heir while the Dauphin was effectively disinherited.

## Qualifications

The episodes center Henry's strategy and French factional collapse. Reported episodes of Charles believing himself glass or Saint George illustrate incapacity in the podcast but are not independently audited here, and the sources supply little analysis of his lucid intervals, agency, treatment, or personal view of the Troyes settlement.

## What Changed

- Added Charles's recurring incapacity as an enabling condition for factional capture and civil war.
- Created Charles VI as the continuity point through which the Troyes regime attempted to transfer the French succession.
