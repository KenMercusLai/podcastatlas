# Kristallnacht / Night of Broken Glass

## Overview

Kristallnacht was the coordinated anti-Jewish pogrom across [[NaziGermany|Nazi Germany]] on and after 9 November 1938, presented by the regime as spontaneous anger after [[ErnstVomRath|Ernst vom Rath]]'s death but enabled by decisions and instructions from Nazi leaders.

## Current Profile

[[AdolfHitler]] and [[JosephGoebbels]] used vom Rath's death after his shooting by [[HerschelGrunspan|Herschel Grünspan]] as a political opportunity. Party members were encouraged to act in civilian clothes, police were told not to protect Jewish people or property, and jails prepared for mass arrests. The result joined central authorization to local violence, cheering, looting, humiliation, and material benefit.

Synagogues, cemeteries, businesses, homes, and an orphanage were attacked across cities, towns, and villages. Roughly 30,000 Jewish men were arrested and sent to concentration camps. The regime then imposed collective financial punishment, accelerated [[EconomicAryanization|economic Aryanization]], and closed more of public life to Jews, making the pogrom a transition from exclusion and dispossession toward openly organized mass violence.

## Key Characteristics

- It was state-enabled violence disguised as an unofficial popular outburst.
- It spread across the Reich through central permission, party networks, police nonintervention, and local participation.
- It attacked religious, commercial, domestic, educational, and bodily security at once.
- It combined destruction and mass arrest with subsequent financial expropriation.
- It intensified Jewish emigration under coercion while foreign borders remained restrictive.
- It served as a watershed on the path toward genocidal bureaucracy and war.

## Evidence

- Political pretext and direction: [[410-the-nazis-in-power-the-night-of-broken-glass-part-7-glt5771265943]] connects vom Rath's death, the Munich gathering, Goebbels's speech, and police instructions.
- Reich-wide violence: [[410-the-nazis-in-power-the-night-of-broken-glass-part-7-glt5771265943]] describes attacks on synagogues, shops, homes, an orphanage, and Jewish residents in multiple localities.
- Arrest and dispossession: [[410-the-nazis-in-power-the-night-of-broken-glass-part-7-glt5771265943]] reports about 30,000 arrests, a one-billion-Reichsmark fine, asset seizure, and exclusion from economic and public life.
- Historical transition: [[410-the-nazis-in-power-the-night-of-broken-glass-part-7-glt5771265943]] links the pogrom to forced emigration, Heydrich's 1939 office, international revulsion, and Hitler's annihilation threat.

## Qualifications

The episode gives a range rather than a settled death toll and distinguishes immediate deaths from later consequences. Its account of local participation does not imply identical behavior in every place or equal responsibility among leaders, organizers, perpetrators, beneficiaries, bystanders, and victims under coercion. The name “Night of Broken Glass” can foreground property damage; the current profile instead centers attacks on people, communities, worship, livelihood, and freedom.

## What Changed

- Established Kristallnacht as a coordinated, state-enabled pogrom rather than spontaneous public anger.
- Connected the violence to prior dispossession and later bureaucratic genocide.
- Added local participation, mass arrest, collective punishment, and refugee barriers to the event profile.
