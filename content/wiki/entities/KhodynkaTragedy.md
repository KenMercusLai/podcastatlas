# Khodynka Tragedy

## Overview

The Khodynka tragedy was a mass-casualty crowd crush during the May 1896 coronation celebrations of [[NicholasII|Nicholas II]] and Alexandra near Moscow.

## Current Profile

[[154-the-most-disastrous-party-in-history-glt6506112996]] describes a public celebration offering food, beer, and commemorative cups on rough ground broken by ditches and ravines. Rumors that supplies might run out and that cups could contain gold coins helped produce a fatal surge. At least 1,200 people died in the episode's account. Nicholas's attendance at a later French-embassy ball then made the catastrophe a symbol of monarchical distance rather than an isolated crowd-management failure.

## Key Characteristics

- Coronation generosity undermined by crowd scale, rumor, scarcity fear, and dangerous terrain.
- Mass death at the opening of a reign.
- Later royal attendance interpreted as indifference or political misjudgment.
- Retrospective omen attached to Nicholas's rule and the empire's social divisions.

## Evidence

- Crowd mechanism: [[154-the-most-disastrous-party-in-history-glt6506112996]] connects gifts, rumors, rough ground, and crowd movement to the disaster.
- Human cost: [[154-the-most-disastrous-party-in-history-glt6506112996]] reports at least 1,200 deaths and many injuries.
- Political afterlife: [[154-the-most-disastrous-party-in-history-glt6506112996]] links Nicholas's later social attendance to the label "Bloody Nicholas" and an ominous reading of his reign.

## Qualifications

The casualty floor, rumor mechanics, responsibility, Nicholas's decision-making, and the origin or reach of the nickname are source-scoped. A tragic opening does not by itself prove or cause the later collapse of the monarchy.

## What Changed

- Established Khodynka as a canonical crowd-disaster and legitimacy case.
