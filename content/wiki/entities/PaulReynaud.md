# Paul Reynaud

## Overview

Paul Reynaud was the French prime minister during the final phase of the [[FallOfFrance1940]], who opposed a separate armistice and sought to continue the war from abroad.

## Current Profile

Before the German western offensive, [[621-the-nazis-at-war-blitzkrieg-part-2-glt8711886512]] presents Reynaud as replacing Daladier and supporting more active, risky Allied operations in Scandinavia. Once the Ardennes breakthrough became clear, he telephoned Churchill on 15 May to report that France was defeated and replaced Gamelin with [[MaximeWeygand]].

[[622-the-nazis-at-war-the-fall-of-france-part-3-glt6447940829]] follows the later cabinet struggle. Reynaud supported continued resistance from North Africa, maintained France's undertaking not to seek a separate peace, and accepted the proposed Franco-British union as an alternative to capitulation. When ministers rejected that course on 16 June, he resigned; he then enabled [[CharlesDeGaulle]] to leave for London with money from secret funds.

## Key Characteristics

- Opposed a separate French armistice with Germany.
- Entered office favoring more active Allied operations and accepted strategic risk in Norway.
- Recognized the scale of the German breakthrough early enough to warn Churchill on 15 May.
- Sought to preserve French belligerence from overseas territory.
- Supported the proposed Franco-British union during the Bordeaux crisis.
- Lost the cabinet struggle to the armistice faction.

## Qualifications

The sources are narrative episodes rather than a full biography or cabinet-record study. Exact dialogue, vote dynamics, personal motives, responsibility for the Norway operation, the timing and meaning of “defeat,” the feasibility of overseas resistance, and the purpose and amount of secret funds remain source-scoped.
