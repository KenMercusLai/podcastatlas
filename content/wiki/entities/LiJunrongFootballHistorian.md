## Overview
李峻嵘 / 李俊仁 / Li Junrong is the guest voice in the [[Budongqiu|不懂球]] episode on postwar [[HongKong|Hong Kong]] football history. The source uses him as a historical interpreter of professionalization, paid-player ambiguity, foreign-player recruitment, commercial teams, and traditional sports-club governance.

## Current Profile
Within this wiki, Li's role is evidence-provider and historical synthesizer rather than a full public biography. He emphasizes that Hong Kong's 1968 professionalization was driven by the mismatch between Olympic amateur eligibility and already-paid football labor, not by a comprehensive reform plan. He then follows the consequences through semi-professional employment, the [[SeikoFootballClub|Seiko]] dynasty, foreign-player escalation, and later membership-borrowing arrangements.

Li's interpretation is strongest where it connects football facts to institutional incentives. Owners, in his telling, want control because traditional sports associations can subject the football team to elections, elders, and unclear authority; league rules also have to keep rivals alive because football competition needs opponents.

## Key Characteristics
- Historical commentator on postwar Hong Kong football professionalization and club mechanisms.
- Source voice who treats 1968 professionalization as reactive rather than planned modernization.
- Interpreter of semi-professional player economics, including club wages plus arranged non-football work.
- Analyst of import-player escalation around [[SeikoFootballClub|Seiko]] and Bulova.
- Governance commentator who contrasts owner-controlled teams with traditional sports-club mechanisms.
