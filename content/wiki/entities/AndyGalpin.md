# Andy Galpin

## Overview
Andy Galpin is the exercise physiologist and [[HubermanLab]] guest in this Essentials episode on how to assess every major aspect of fitness. He is presented as the source voice for an assessment-first approach: identify which capacity is actually limiting a goal, test it with either a gold-standard method or a cheap substitute, and only then spend training effort where it matters.

## Current Profile
In this conversation Galpin organizes fitness into two goal buckets, appearance and functionality, and nine [[NinePhysiologicalAdaptations|physiological adaptations]] that can each be assessed separately. His contribution is less a training program than a diagnostic system: [[MovementScreenScoring]] as a gate, accessible strength, endurance, anaerobic, aerobic, and long-duration tests with concrete minimums, [[FatFreeMassIndex]] as the health floor for muscle, and [[FitnessAssessmentTestingWeek]] as the roughly annual protocol that runs everything in a fatiguing-order-safe sequence.

He is also consistently explicit about test quality. He flags the 220-minus-age maximum-heart-rate formula as extremely generic, refuses universal standards for anaerobic work because the modality changes the result, notes that dead-hang and grip standards scale imperfectly with body size, and requires technique comfort and a standardized warm-up before maximal strength testing. The episode closes with him deferring speed, strength, and hypertrophy programming to a later conversation, which keeps this appearance as an assessment foundation rather than a complete training guide.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames fitness assessment as a prioritization problem about appearance versus functionality rather than a single fitness score.
- Supplies a nine-part adaptation taxonomy with both laboratory or specialist methods and low-cost do-it-yourself substitutes for each item.
- Gives concrete numeric minimums while repeatedly naming the conditions that make those numbers unreliable.
- Treats movement quality as a gate before maximal strength testing and treats severe [[FitnessPerformanceAnchors|performance anchors]] as the first thing to fix.
- Separates the aesthetic part of hypertrophy from the health-relevant amount of muscle and aerobic capacity.
- Defers programming to a follow-up conversation, which keeps this source deliberately diagnostic.

- Test-quality caveats: [[essentials-how-to-assess-improve-all-aspects-of-your-fitness-dr-andy-galpin-scim5641095924]] has him call 220-minus-age extremely generic, avoid kettlebell swings for anaerobic testing, and refuse universal standards because modality changes the result.
- Testing week: [[essentials-how-to-assess-improve-all-aspects-of-your-fitness-dr-andy-galpin-scim5641095924]] has him recommend running the full battery about once a year across roughly three days with non-fatiguing tests first and maximal tests last.
