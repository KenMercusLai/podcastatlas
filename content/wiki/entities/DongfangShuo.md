## Overview

东方朔 / Dongfang Shuo enters the wiki through Hanji 392 as a Western Han court wit and praise-speaker whom the episodes present near [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s most sensitive decisions. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-392-2-xiangshengjie-zushiye-dongfangshuo-shi-ge-zenyang-de-qiren-lnhc_lsj53gy-yqmwqobnw5y37g9|Hanji 392-2]] frames him as an ancestor-like figure for later comic performance, while [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-392-1-hanwudi-shoule-meimei-de-qian-weihe-reng-yao-shasi-qinwaisheng-lhey4utgx3njqea-suiqohjybrpa|Hanji 392-1]] shows him congratulating Wudi after the execution of [[ZhaoPingjunWesternHan|赵平君]].

## Current Profile

The episode's Dongfang Shuo is a figure of comic intelligence rather than a developed official biography. When a fangshi flatters [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] by saying a long philtrum predicts long life, Dongfang Shuo laughs and redirects the possible offense away from the emperor toward Peng Zu. He then follows the premise mechanically: if one inch means one hundred years, Peng Zu's eight hundred years would require an absurdly long face.

The current synthesis is that Dongfang Shuo's wit functions as indirect correction. He does not openly accuse the fangshi of deception or the emperor of gullibility; he makes the flattering logic ridiculous from inside. That lets the episode connect him to [[HumorousRemonstrance|滑稽讽谏]] and to later traditions that remember him as a comic or xiangsheng-adjacent ancestor.

Hanji 392-1 adds a more straight-faced but still tactically precise court-speech role. After Wudi executes a close relative, Dongfang Shuo congratulates him by turning the decision into kingly impartiality: reward should not avoid enemies, and punishment should not spare bone-and-flesh kin. The episode says Wudi is pleased and appoints Dongfang Shuo as zhonglang, so the new evidence expands him from comic refuter of flattery into a broader figure of timing, framing, and speech near power.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han court figure remembered here through wit, eccentricity, and comic proximity to power.
- Uses absurd extrapolation to expose a fangshi's flattering physiognomy claim.
- Protects himself by turning apparent laughter at the emperor into laughter at the logical consequence of the claim.
- Represents humor as a possible remonstrance channel when direct correction would be socially or politically risky.
- Uses praise after a painful decision to affirm the ruler's public-law self-image and convert legal severity into moral legitimacy.
- The page is currently a source-scoped profile, not a complete biography.
