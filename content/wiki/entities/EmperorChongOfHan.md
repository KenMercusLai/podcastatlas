# 汉冲帝 / Emperor Chong of Han

## Overview

Emperor Chong of Han, personal name Liu Bing, is represented here as a child ruler whose political significance lies less in personal agency than in the succession machinery around him. Hanji 824 adds the crown-prince designation and palace credential crisis; Hanji 825 then records his death in 145 and the regime's pivot to Liu Suan versus Liu Zuan under Empress Dowager Liang Na and Li Gu.

## Current Profile

In 144 CE, [[EmperorShunOfHan|Emperor Shun]] designates Liu Bing as crown prince and places him in Chengguang Palace under [[ZhongHaoLateHan|Zhong Hao]]'s supervision. When [[GaoFanLateHan|Gao Fan]] tries to fetch him without an edict credential, Zhong Hao blocks the transfer until formal authorization is produced. After Shun dies, Liu Bing succeeds at age two as Emperor Chong.

In 145 CE, Emperor Chong dies young. His death exposes the gap between a child emperor's nominal sovereignty and the real decision space held by Empress Dowager Liang Na, Li Gu, Liang Ji, and other succession actors.

## Key Characteristics

- Child successor whose agency is minimal but whose person anchors the succession order.
- Crown prince whose physical custody requires strict credential control before accession.
- Short-reign emperor whose death triggers the Liu Suan versus Liu Zuan succession debate.
- Figure whose reign illustrates the dependence of imperial legitimacy on regents, ministers, and palace access.
- Bridge between Shun's final-year succession setup and the later Emperor Zhi/Emperor Huan transition.

## Qualifications

The current evidence does not support a substantive policy profile for Emperor Chong. The page should treat him primarily as a succession node until sources supply independent actions or decrees.
