# Gary Steinberg

## Overview
Gary Steinberg is the Stanford neurosurgeon and neuroscience and neurology professor interviewed by [[AndrewHuberman]] about cerebral blood flow, stroke, traumatic brain injury, neurosurgery, and experimental recovery treatments.

## Current Profile
The bounded source presents Steinberg as a clinician-researcher who combines vascular prevention, acute neurological distinction, function-preserving surgery, and cautious neurorestoration. His stance is neither fatalistic nor promotional: damaged brains may recover through surviving circuits and rehabilitation, but stem cells, cooling, stimulation, and screening must be separated by indication, evidence stage, and risk.

## Key Characteristics
- Explains ischemic stroke, hemorrhage, TIA, aneurysm, spinal-cord stroke, concussion, and traumatic brain injury through blood flow, anatomy, symptoms, and urgency.
- Describes modern neurosurgery as increasingly function-mapped and minimally invasive.
- Connects brain-health prevention to ordinary cardiovascular and lifestyle risk management.
- Treats adult plasticity and rehabilitation as real recovery mechanisms after injury.
- Investigates experimental stem-cell approaches while warning against unproven commercial clinics.
- Presents preventive imaging and implanted stimulation as clinical tradeoffs rather than universal upgrades.
