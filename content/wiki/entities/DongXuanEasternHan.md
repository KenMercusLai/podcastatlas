## Overview

董宣 / Dong Xuan enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-721-ta-sha-jianu-ma-gongzhu-weihe-fanbei-shang-lryzcejdnrm4-92esnddfvyjkqli|Hanji 721]] as the Luoyang magistrate who executes [[HuyangPrincessEasternHan|湖阳公主]]'s murderous slave and refuses to abase himself before the princess. His importance is not only personal severity; the episode makes him a visible test of whether [[LiuXiu|光武帝刘秀]] will let royal-household privilege override local legal authority.

## Current Profile

Hanji 721 gives Dong Xuan one focused profile. A slave protected by Princess Huyang kills someone and hides in the princess household, where ordinary officials cannot safely enter. Dong Xuan waits until the princess travels with the slave in her carriage, stops the vehicle, rebukes the princess for sheltering a killer, orders the slave down, and has him killed.

The palace aftermath makes Dong Xuan politically legible. Liu Xiu first plans to beat him to death, but Dong Xuan argues that a ruler who restored Han through virtue cannot govern the realm while allowing slaves to murder under royal protection. When Liu Xiu tries to force him to apologize, Dong Xuan braces himself and will not bow. Liu Xiu releases him, calls him hard-necked, and rewards him with money that Dong Xuan distributes to subordinates.

The episode's synthesis treats Dong Xuan as an enforcement signal. His act is severe and personalized, but Liu Xiu's final backing turns it into a warning to Luoyang nobles and relatives that household rank will not automatically defeat magistrate authority.

## Key Characteristics

- Luoyang magistrate whose defining source case is enforcement against a princess-protected household slave.
- Uses timing and public interception to avoid the protected residence while still reaching the offender.
- Reframes private royal anger as an imperial governance question.
- Refuses both execution intimidation and forced apology.
- Becomes a high-status law-enforcement signal after Liu Xiu releases and rewards him.
- Shares the reward with subordinates, tying personal vindication back to office staff.

## Qualifications

This page is source-bounded to one episode and does not reconstruct Dong Xuan's complete official career. The episode praises his legal courage, but the synthesis keeps the coercive severity visible: the case is about ruler-backed enforcement credibility inside Eastern Han imperial politics, not modern due-process equality. The transcript form 董轩 is normalized here to 董宣 because the Luoyang magistrate, Princess Huyang, hard-necked refusal, and reward sequence identify the established figure.
