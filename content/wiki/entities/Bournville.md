# Bournville

## Overview

Bournville is the rural factory site and model village through which [[Cadbury]] linked chocolate manufacturing to housing, welfare, recreation, and a deliberately shaped company community.

## Current Profile

[[425-the-history-of-chocolate-glt9203655246]] says Cadbury bought farmland outside Birmingham by the Bourn in 1878 and named the site Bournville. George Cadbury later expanded it with land for a model village intended to relieve cramped urban conditions; by 1900, the episode reports more than 300 cottages and houses.

The site also organized working life through holidays, a shorter week, classes, sport, paid exercise, benefits, pensions, and the *Bournville Works Magazine*. These provisions make Bournville a significant industrial-welfare case, but [[QuakerIndustrialPaternalism]] keeps employer authority visible alongside material improvement.

## Key Characteristics

- Rural factory site established outside Birmingham from 1878.
- Model-village project intended to offer healthier housing than crowded industrial districts.
- Worker-welfare system combining time off, education, sport, exercise, benefits, and pensions.
- Company-culture project promoting a shared "Bournville spirit" of community and service.
- Paternalist environment in which employer provision and employer influence operated together.

## Evidence

- Site formation: [[425-the-history-of-chocolate-glt9203655246]] describes the 1878 farmland purchase by the Bourn and the naming of Bournville.
- Housing: [[425-the-history-of-chocolate-glt9203655246]] says George Cadbury bought 120 acres in the 1890s and that the village had more than 300 homes by 1900.
- Welfare: [[425-the-history-of-chocolate-glt9203655246]] lists holidays, a five-and-a-half-day week, evening classes, sport, exercise, benefits, and pensions.
- Culture: [[425-the-history-of-chocolate-glt9203655246]] describes the 1902 works magazine and its promotion of communal service.

## Qualifications

The episode judges the project broadly favorably by industrial-Britain standards but acknowledges paternalism. It does not provide worker counter-testimony, comparative housing or wage evidence, exclusion criteria, or a full account of governance and discipline. The episode's Cadbury sponsorship also makes independent corroboration important.

## What Changed

- Created Bournville as the place-based case joining industrial production, worker welfare, housing reform, and corporate culture.

## Relationships

- [[Cadbury]] - company that built its works and model village at the site.
- [[JohnCadbury]] - founder of the earlier Birmingham business from which the later Bournville project developed.
- [[Quakers]] - religious and philanthropic context invoked for the site's social program.
- [[QuakerIndustrialPaternalism]] - framework describing its combination of provision and employer control.
- [[ChocolateDrinkToSolidTransition]] - industrial product shift whose scale helped make the site possible.
