# Winston Churchill

## Overview

Winston Churchill enters the wiki through [[676-the-first-world-war-churchills-calamity-part-6-glt1275431911]] as First Lord of the Admiralty and principal political sponsor of the [[GallipoliCampaign|Gallipoli campaign]].

## Current Profile

The episode presents Churchill as both scapegoat and responsible actor. He was not the only person behind Gallipoli, and wartime politics used the campaign against him, but he kept defending the Dardanelles strategy after repeated failure made withdrawal more plausible.

Churchill's profile here is therefore not a complete biography. It is a source-bounded picture of brilliant strategic imagination turning destructive when joined to persistence, vanity, and reluctance to abandon a failing operation.

## Key Characteristics

- Churchill advocated the Dardanelles route as a way to bypass deadlock and pressure the Ottoman Empire.
- He remained publicly committed to the campaign as battlefield evidence and political criticism mounted.
- His conflict with Jackie Fisher and criticism from Lloyd George, newspapers, and naval officers made Gallipoli a Cabinet crisis.
- He lost the Admiralty after Asquith formed a coalition government, receiving the lesser Duchy of Lancaster post.
- He tried to defend his reputation while troops remained trapped in deteriorating conditions.
- He opposed evacuation and then left government, later serving on the Western Front.
- The episode treats him as a qualified scapegoat: blamed heavily, but not innocent of major responsibility.

## Evidence

- Campaign sponsorship: [[676-the-first-world-war-churchills-calamity-part-6-glt1275431911]] presents Churchill's plan as forcing the Dardanelles, reaching the Black Sea, threatening Constantinople, and removing the Ottoman Empire from the war.
- Political fall: [[676-the-first-world-war-churchills-calamity-part-6-glt1275431911]] links Fisher's resignation, the shell shortage scandal, Asquith's coalition, naval-officer hostility, and Churchill's removal from the Admiralty.
- Responsibility judgment: [[676-the-first-world-war-churchills-calamity-part-6-glt1275431911]] says Churchill was the chief scapegoat but deserved major responsibility because he would not abandon the campaign once it was clearly failing.

## Qualifications

This page covers Churchill only through the Gallipoli episode. It does not assess his later premiership, World War II leadership, imperial politics, writings, or broader career.
