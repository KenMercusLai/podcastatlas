## Overview

咸丰帝 / Xianfeng Emperor enters the wiki through Hanji 422-2 as the Qing ruler whose resistance to foreign resident envoys illustrates tribute-order diplomatic rigidity.

## Current Profile

The episode portrays Xianfeng as interpreting the Treaty of Tianjin's Beijing envoy provision through imperial face and kneeling ritual. Foreign ambassadors in Beijing were not only a security or administrative issue in this source; they threatened the visible hierarchy of "天朝上国." Xianfeng therefore considers extreme fiscal concession, then war, to avoid a diplomatic practice that would expose non-subordinate foreign states at the capital.

## Key Characteristics

- Qing emperor used by the source as the late tribute-order rigidity case.
- Treats foreign envoy residence in Beijing as a ritual hierarchy problem.
- Fears that non-kneeling foreigners could damage imperial face and weaken other tribute relationships.
- Briefly considers removing customs duties to keep foreign envoys out of Beijing.
- Ends the episode's arc as a ruler whose status defense carries military and personal costs.

- Kneeling and face: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-422-2-gudai-waiguoren-weisha-ai-dao-zhongguo-chaogong-yuanyin-papadalian-lr3erfvroy4b_ryu2sfsdgd-e4m1|Hanji 422-2]] says he feared foreigners would not kneel and would cause a cascade of disrespect from other tribute states.
- Tariff proposal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-422-2-gudai-waiguoren-weisha-ai-dao-zhongguo-chaogong-yuanyin-papadalian-lr3erfvroy4b_ryu2sfsdgd-e4m1|Hanji 422-2]] says he considered using full customs-duty exemption as a bargain to prevent envoy residence.
- War and retreat: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-422-2-gudai-waiguoren-weisha-ai-dao-zhongguo-chaogong-yuanyin-papadalian-lr3erfvroy4b_ryu2sfsdgd-e4m1|Hanji 422-2]] links the failed bargain to renewed conflict, Yuanmingyuan's burning, flight to Chengde, and his death there.

## Qualifications

The page preserves the episode's interpretation of Xianfeng's motives but does not treat it as a full account of Qing policy, foreign coercion, or the Second Opium War. The tariff idea, motive attribution, and causal chain need comparison with future sources.
