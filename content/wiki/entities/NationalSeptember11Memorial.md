# National September 11 Memorial

## Overview

The National September 11 Memorial is the Ground Zero memorial whose “Reflecting Absence” design preserves the Twin Towers' footprints through two voids, waterfalls, reflecting pools, victims' names, and a tree-filled plaza.

## Current Profile

[[a-memorial-for-the-living-a-memorial-for-the-living]] presents the memorial as an unusually exact and resource-intensive system of public memory. Location, dimensions, opening date, name relationships, tactile materials, lighting, temperature control, infrastructure, and landscaping were all treated as meaningful parts of commemoration.

Its role in the episode is comparative rather than simply architectural. The precision devoted to 2,982 dead people becomes the standard against which [[ChangingHomelessness]]'s materially constrained care for living people is judged.

## Key Characteristics

- Its two pools occupy the footprints of the destroyed Twin Towers.
- Victims' names are cut into touchable brass panels and arranged through meaningful adjacency.
- The plaza was constructed above active rail and transit infrastructure under a fixed tenth-anniversary deadline.
- Trees, panel conditioning, fountains, security, and maintenance make remembrance an ongoing operating system rather than a one-time monument.
- The episode uses its scale and cost to frame [[MemorialCareAllocation]].

## Qualifications

The source is a structured episode summary, not an audited project history. Its reported costs and engineering details remain source-scoped. The comparison with homelessness does not show that memorial and housing budgets were interchangeable or governed by the same institutions.
