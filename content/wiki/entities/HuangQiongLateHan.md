## Overview

黄琼 / Huang Qiong is a late-Han high official whose bounded profile now combines three layers: an ignored 159 CE illness memorial diagnosing [[EmperorHuanOfHan|汉桓帝]]'s post-Liang court inversion, 161 CE high-office churn under disaster pressure, and a later funeral scene where [[XuZhiLateHan|徐稚]] and [[GuoTaiLateHan|郭泰]] turn Huang Qiong into a reputation marker.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-851-hanhuandi-weihe-zhuding-yichou-wannian-ltgm1ix6cgqb-kqo9bztfrzjgeun|Hanji 851]] now gives Huang Qiong his earliest current wiki role. Sick and unable to correct the court, he memorializes Huan with a blunt diagnosis: Liang-family dominance has not been replaced by superior governance, eunuchs fill the court, loyal officials such as [[LiGuLateHan|李固]], [[LiYunLateHan|李云]], and [[DuZhongLateHan|杜仲]] have been harmed, and reward no longer separates loyal from corrupt actors. Huan's lack of visible response makes Huang Qiong a senior witness to [[CourtFeedbackCollapse|court feedback collapse]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-854-lishi-gaosu-ni-xuanguan-yongguan-hen-zhongyao-lhin-iwk6y1xr8vvzdmnjch3s2um|Hanji 854]] then places Huang Qiong in 161 CE Three Excellencies turnover: he is removed as taiwei after palace fire, epidemic, and administrative pressure, later appointed sikong after Yu Fang's removal, and removed again before [[LiuChongLateHanMinister|刘宠]] enters the post. The source uses him less as a policy actor than as evidence that Huan-era high office is unstable under disaster and personnel pressure.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-860-jingwei-tianren-donghan-guotai-you-duoshen-ltihxtkdp08eemcvhhxnpubykruv|Hanji 860]] gives Huang Qiong a posthumous reputation role. His funeral becomes the scene where Xu Zhi mourns selectively and Guo Tai interprets the act for a wider public, linking Huang Qiong's death to late-Han reputation discernment.

## Key Characteristics

- Senior minister whose illness memorial diagnoses Huan's post-Liang reward and feedback inversion.
- High official moved through taiwei and sikong roles in a year marked by disaster pressure and administrative churn.
- Reputation-bearing elder whose funeral is meaningful enough to attract Xu Zhi and Guo Tai's interpretive attention.
- Institutional bridge between failed court correction, high-office instability, and later public character reading.
