## Overview

黄琼 / Huang Qiong is a late-Han high official whose bounded profile now begins in 127 CE with summons to [[EmperorShunOfHan|汉顺帝]]'s court, [[LiGuLateHan|李固]]'s warning against reputation-only reclusion, and Huang Qiong's later competence in Shangshu work. Later layers show him limiting [[LiangJiLateHan|梁冀]]'s reward expansion, participating in the short post-Liang reform window, recognizing [[FanPangLateHan|范滂]] as an enforcement talent, memorializing [[EmperorHuanOfHan|汉桓帝]] against reward and feedback inversion, moving through high-office churn, and becoming a posthumous reputation marker.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-809-weishenme-quan-ni-buyao-luan-pai-mapi-lnv2l-ehkzmf2ifz3semscaqftku|Hanji 809]] gives Huang Qiong the earliest current layer. In 127 CE, Shun summons Yang Hou and Huang Qiong to court; Huang Qiong is introduced as the son of Huang Xiang, whose filial reputation and Shangshu career already attach the family to learned official service. When Huang Qiong arrives in Luoyang, Li Gu writes to him and warns that high reputation without practical contribution can become a liability. The advice pushes Huang Qiong away from sterile purity and toward service in an imperfect court.

The same source turns reputation into capacity rather than ornament. Huang Qiong is appointed yilang and later Shangshu puye. Because he had grown up around the Shangshu office through Huang Xiang, he understands its procedures and can discuss state affairs calmly; the source says his memorials are often accepted. This early layer makes his later reputation rest not just on rank or moral aura, but on institutional fluency.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-838-tanlan-de-youhuo-rang-ta-kuangbiao-zai-zuosi-de-lushang-lrydilyni6o6yzhk1-e74m6aigwr|Hanji 838]] gives Huang Qiong the first current Liang Ji reward-restraint role. When Huan asks high officials to discuss added rewards for Liang Ji, many speakers support extreme honors. Huang Qiong does not attack Liang directly, but he narrows the reward by arguing that Liang already holds substantial food-estate households and that Liang's son has also been enfeoffed. The result is partial restraint rather than full merit discipline: land expansion is limited, but Liang still receives exceptional privilege and remains dissatisfied.

Hanji 848 gives Huang Qiong the first post-Liang office-repair signal. After Huan destroys Liang Ji, people expect the court to improve; Huang Qiong is appointed taiwei and impeaches long-abusive or greedy provincial and commandery officials. The episode says more than ten are executed or exiled and that local people praise the action, making Huang Qiong part of the short reform window opened by Liang's fall.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-849-sushi-ziyou-du-de-fanpangzhuan-jiujing-shuo-de-shui-ltquglqzvezeyp0mqxwgwnlp12lf|Hanji 849]] gives Huang Qiong an earlier personnel role. While serving as sikong in 159 CE, he recruits Fan Pang as Qingzhao envoy, so Huang Qiong appears as a senior minister able to identify a severe but principled anti-corruption inspector before the later Party Prohibition crisis fixes Fan Pang's martyr profile.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-851-hanhuandi-weihe-zhuding-yichou-wannian-ltgm1ix6cgqb-kqo9bztfrzjgeun|Hanji 851]] then gives Huang Qiong his direct remonstrance role. Sick and unable to correct the court, he memorializes Huan with a blunt diagnosis: Liang-family dominance has not been replaced by superior governance, eunuchs fill the court, loyal officials such as [[LiGuLateHan|李固]], [[LiYunLateHan|李云]], and [[DuZhongLateHan|杜仲]] have been harmed, and reward no longer separates loyal from corrupt actors. Huan's lack of visible response makes Huang Qiong a senior witness to [[CourtFeedbackCollapse|court feedback collapse]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-854-lishi-gaosu-ni-xuanguan-yongguan-hen-zhongyao-lhin-iwk6y1xr8vvzdmnjch3s2um|Hanji 854]] places Huang Qiong in 161 CE Three Excellencies turnover: he is removed as taiwei after palace fire, epidemic, and administrative pressure, later appointed sikong after Yu Fang's removal, and removed again before [[LiuChongLateHanMinister|刘宠]] enters the post. The source uses him less as a policy actor than as evidence that Huan-era high office is unstable under disaster and personnel pressure.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-860-jingwei-tianren-donghan-guotai-you-duoshen-ltihxtkdp08eemcvhhxnpubykruv|Hanji 860]] gives Huang Qiong a posthumous reputation role. His funeral becomes the scene where Xu Zhi mourns selectively and Guo Tai interprets the act for a wider public, linking Huang Qiong's death to late-Han reputation discernment.

## Key Characteristics

- Summoned scholar-official whose 127 CE profile converts high family and personal reputation into court service.
- Shangshu-trained official whose institutional familiarity makes his state-affairs memorials usable under Emperor Shun.
- Senior minister who limits the 151 CE proposal to expand Liang Ji's reward beyond an already large privilege base.
- Senior minister appointed taiwei in the short reform window after Liang Ji's fall and whose early post-Liang impeachments produce visible local praise.
- Senior minister who recognizes Fan Pang as a Qingzhao-envoy enforcement talent.
- Senior remonstrant whose illness memorial diagnoses Huan's post-Liang reward and feedback inversion.
- High official and reputation-bearing elder whose later office churn and funeral scene mark both political stress and moral standing.

## Qualifications

The page remains bounded to the listed podcast notes, not a full biography. Hanji 809 supplies an early summons, Li Gu advice, and Shangshu-competence layer, but it does not reconstruct Huang Qiong's full youth, family network, or exact office chronology. Hanji 838 shows Huang Qiong limiting one reward proposal, but the resulting settlement still leaves Liang Ji with extraordinary privilege, so this is not evidence of durable court correction. Hanji 848 shows Huang Qiong acting in a short post-Liang cleanup window, but later sources show that window did not become durable institutional recovery. Hanji 849 shows Huang Qiong recognizing Fan Pang for a specific inspection role, but it does not prove a broader patronage network or explain how far Huang Qiong controlled Fan Pang's later reputation. Hanji 851 strengthens Huang Qiong's direct policy voice, but the current source set still does not prove how his memorial affected later appointments or enforcement. ASR forms such as 黄穹 are normalized to 黄琼 in this route.

## What Changed

- Hanji 809 adds Huang Qiong's earliest current layer: 127 CE summons, Li Gu's warning against reputation-only purity, and later Shangshu competence.
- Hanji 838 remains the first Liang Ji reward-restraint layer, but it is no longer the beginning of Huang Qiong's bounded profile.
- Hanji 848 and Hanji 849 keep Huang Qiong as a short-window reform actor and talent recognizer after the early Shun-era service layer.
- Hanji 851 through Hanji 860 continue the sequence from ignored remonstrance to office churn and funeral reputation.
- The current judgment now treats Huang Qiong's reputation as grounded partly in practical institutional skill, not only senior moral standing.
