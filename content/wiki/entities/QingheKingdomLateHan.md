# 清河王国 / Qinghe Kingdom (late Han)

## Overview

清河王国 / Qinghe Kingdom enters the current wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-829-junzi-weishenme-doubuguo-xiaoren-lthvnstkfu8n9thx1sz6yezl4yqq|Hanji 829]] as the royal setting attached to [[LiuSuanLateHan|刘蒜]] and the failed attempt by [[LiuWenQingheRebelLateHan|刘文]] and [[LiuWeiNanjunRebelLateHan|刘伟]] to install him as emperor. The source makes Qinghe a succession-memory site rather than a developed territorial history.

## Current Profile

Hanji 829 uses Qinghe in two linked ways. First, Liu Suan is identified as Qinghe king, which matters because earlier officials such as [[LiGuLateHan|李固]] judged him a stronger accession candidate than the later [[EmperorHuanOfHan|汉桓帝]]. Second, the plotters' abduction of [[XieHaoQingheLateHan|谢浩]] shows that Qinghe office and royal status could become leverage in a wider succession claim.

The incident ends with state suppression rather than a new regime. Liu Wen and Liu Wei are executed, Liu Suan is demoted to Weishi Hou, exiled to Guiyang, and dies by suicide. Qinghe therefore appears as the local stage where residual succession legitimacy is converted into a chargeable rebellion.

## Key Characteristics

- Late-Han royal kingdom attached to Liu Suan in the Hanji 829 evidence.
- Succession-memory site because Liu Suan had been treated as an alternative to Emperor Huan.
- Local office field where Xie Hao becomes valuable to rebels seeking legitimacy.
- Failed-rebellion setting that ends in execution, demotion, exile, and suicide.
- Source-bounded place page without a reconstructed full institutional history.

## Qualifications

This page is not a full history of Qinghe's administrative geography, royal line, or later officeholders. It records only the source-bounded succession and rebellion context. The source does not specify how Qinghe's institutions respond beyond Xie Hao's refusal and death.

## What Changed

- Framed Qinghe as a succession-memory site rather than as a general territorial page.
