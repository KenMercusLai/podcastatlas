# Buzz (Heavyweight)

## Overview

Buzz is [[JonathanGoldstein]]'s father and the younger brother of [[SheldonHeavyweight|Sheldon]]. The bounded source follows him into a reunion after roughly four decades of estrangement and examines how a childhood belief about being the child their mother did not choose organized later resentment.

## Current Profile

Buzz begins by describing indifference, but his anxiety during the trip and his behavior with Sheldon reveal continuing attachment. At the reunion he becomes recognizably the younger brother—affectionate, deferential, competitive, and sensitive to teasing—while still disputing memories and protecting himself from renewed humiliation.

His central change is interpretive. Learning more about the violence Sheldon experienced allows Buzz to consider that their mother may have tried to protect Sheldon rather than simply preferred him. That possibility does not settle the past, but it supports a practical shift toward birthday contact, reduced formality, concern, and willingness to help.

## Key Characteristics

- Carries a long-standing abandonment interpretation into later family conflict.
- Alternates among affection, rivalry, anxiety, resentment, and protective detachment.
- Reads ordinary teasing through a history of perceived rejection.
- Becomes willing to revise a central grievance when given previously unavailable experience.
- Expresses repair through practical promises more readily than through overt emotional display.

## Evidence

### Abandonment interpretation

- [[welcoming-heavyweight-welcoming-heavyweight]] reports that Buzz treated his mother's attempt to take Sheldon, but not him, as proof of unequal love.

### Continuing attachment beneath detachment

- [[welcoming-heavyweight-welcoming-heavyweight]] contrasts Buzz's claim of apathy with his changing mood before the visit, younger-brother behavior, and relief afterward.

### Perspective revision and practical repair

- [[welcoming-heavyweight-welcoming-heavyweight]] connects Sheldon's account of paternal violence to Buzz's revised understanding and his promises of birthday contact and practical care.

## Qualifications

The source supplies Jonathan's narrated interpretation and a structured summary rather than a transcript, clinical account, or independent family history. Buzz's reactions do not prove the mother's motive, and relief immediately after the meeting does not establish durable reconciliation. Sensitivity to rejection explains part of his pattern without excusing every slight or assigning him sole responsibility for the estrangement.

## What Changed

- Replaced a single favoritism explanation with a qualified protection hypothesis grounded in Sheldon's experience.
- Shifted the current profile from claimed apathy toward unresolved attachment, practical concern, and cautious renewed contact.
