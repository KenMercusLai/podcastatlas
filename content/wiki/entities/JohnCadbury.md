# John Cadbury

## Overview

John Cadbury appears in [[425-the-history-of-chocolate-glt9203655246]] as the Quaker founder who opened the Birmingham shop from which [[Cadbury]] developed.

## Current Profile

The episode dates Cadbury's shop at 93 Bull Street to 1824 and says it sold tea, coffee, and cocoa before he opened a factory in 1831. It places his business inside a wider [[Quakers|Quaker]] commercial pattern: exclusion from conventional elite institutions encouraged trade, while temperance made cocoa attractive as a sober alternative to alcohol.

John Cadbury is therefore the founder of the episode's company story, but the later industrial scale, advertising, model-village welfare, and consumer culture are associated mainly with Richard and George Cadbury. The profile should not transfer every later Cadbury institution back to the founder.

## Key Characteristics

- Quaker shopkeeper and chocolate-business founder in Birmingham.
- Opened a tea, coffee, and cocoa shop at 93 Bull Street in 1824.
- Moved into factory production in 1831.
- Positioned in an emerging market where cocoa could be sold as wholesome and temperate.

## Evidence

- Founding: [[425-the-history-of-chocolate-glt9203655246]] identifies the 1824 Bull Street shop and its tea, coffee, and cocoa offering.
- Manufacturing: [[425-the-history-of-chocolate-glt9203655246]] dates the factory opening to 1831 and connects British producers to Dutch cocoa-processing advances.
- Religious-business context: [[425-the-history-of-chocolate-glt9203655246]] links Quaker commerce to institutional exclusion, sobriety, pacifism, equality, and philanthropy.

## Qualifications

The source provides only a compact founder narrative. It does not establish a full biography, isolate John Cadbury's personal motives from broader Quaker and market conditions, or attribute the later Bournville system solely to him. Dates, addresses, technical influence, and motive claims remain episode-attributed.

## What Changed

- Created the founder profile and separated John Cadbury's early shop and factory role from later family management.
