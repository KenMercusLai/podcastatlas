# 东海恭王刘强 / Liu Qiang, Donghai King

## Overview

Liu Qiang appears in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-733-1-lishi-suo-yiwang-de-diwang-zhishi-mingjun-liuzhuang-lor7vhzzpwz1rv9tijksogukjqvr|Hanji 733-1]] as Donghai Gong King, a royal relative whose final illness and death give [[EmperorMingOfHan|Mingdi]] a scene of kinship grief, deference, and burial restraint.

## Current Profile

The source emphasizes Liu Qiang's final memorial rather than his earlier political background. During Liu Qiang's illness, Mingdi sends imperial envoys, physicians, and princes to attend him. Liu Qiang then thanks the emperor, asks to return his fief to commandery status, and urges Mingdi to care doubly for the empress dowager.

Mingdi's reaction confirms Liu Qiang as a positive royal-kin example. The emperor weeps, mourns deeply, and sends gifts beyond ordinary funeral practice, but then orders burial goods kept simple because Liu Qiang had been known for modesty and frugality.

## Key Characteristics

- Royal-kin figure framed through deferential final speech.
- His illness triggers unusually attentive imperial care.
- Requests return of his fief to commandery status, marking self-limitation rather than territorial assertiveness.
- Functions as a contrast case to more dangerous royal kin in Mingdi's reign.
- His burial becomes an early Mingdi example of grief constrained by frugality.
