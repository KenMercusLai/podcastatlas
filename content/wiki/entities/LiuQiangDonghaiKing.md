## Overview

Liu Qiang appears across Hanji 719, Hanji 720, Hanji 728, Hanji 732, and Hanji 733-1 as a displaced former-heir figure whose survival depends on turning succession loss into controlled royal honor. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-719-guangwudi-liuxiu-suanbusuan-zhanan-lk8eme3wwyk5qkjjfxkp0zsc77ix|Hanji 719]] now supplies the root setup: he is [[GuoShengtong|郭圣通]]'s son, and Guo's deposition breaks the maternal-status basis for his crown-prince security. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-720-donghan-kaiguo-taizi-weihe-fanfu-da-cizhi-baogao-lslizspo8rywnf3cebyhincdhvz0|Hanji 720]] then gives the repeated resignation, Donghai settlement, and replacement by Liu Yang / Liu Zhuang, the later [[EmperorMingOfHan|Mingdi]].

## Current Profile

Hanji 719 supplies the pre-withdrawal layer. Liu Qiang's mother Guo Shengtong is deposed and replaced by [[YinLihua|阴丽华]] in 41 CE. The source does not yet narrate Liu Qiang's resignation, but it makes clear why his status becomes vulnerable: a crown prince attached to the deposed empress no longer stands on the same maternal foundation.

Hanji 720 gives Liu Qiang the crown-prince withdrawal layer. After Guo's deposition, the source says Liu Qiang becomes uneasy because his mother's status had been his support. [[ZhiYunEasternHan|郅恽]] advises that remaining in an unstable heir position is dangerous and does not help him serve his mother, so Liu Qiang repeatedly asks through trusted attendants and royal kin to resign and retire to a fief. [[LiuXiu|刘秀]] hesitates for years, then in 43 CE makes Liu Qiang Donghai King and makes Liu Yang crown prince, renaming him Liu Zhuang.

The same source preserves the institutional objection through Yuan Hong's comment. Yuan Hong argues that crown-prince status is meant to settle order and should not be changed if the heir has no grave fault. Liu Qiang's withdrawal therefore solves a family danger without making the original succession change institutionally clean.

Hanji 728 gives Liu Qiang the later Guangwu-era honor layer. In 52 CE, Liu Xiu changes Lu King Liu Xing's fief to Beihai and merges the former Lu territory into Donghai. The source says Liu Qiang is very proper in ritual conduct, and Liu Xiu treats him exceptionally by expanding his fief and granting huben guards, cavalry, and ritual-music equipment. The same episode later lists Liu Qiang among the princes sent back to their fiefs after the prince-guest disaster.

Hanji 732 gives Liu Qiang an accession-risk role. After Guangwu dies, [[LiuJingGuanglingKingEasternHan|刘荆]] sends an anonymous letter arguing that Liu Qiang had been wrongly displaced as heir and that Queen Guo had been wrongly removed. Liu Qiang does not take the bait: he panics, secures the messenger figure, seals the letter, and reports the matter to Mingdi. Mingdi keeps Liu Jing's role private, has Liu Qiang leave the capital first, and then reassures the Guo/Liu Qiang branch.

Hanji 733-1 then emphasizes Liu Qiang's final memorial rather than a full earlier biography. During his illness, Mingdi sends imperial envoys, physicians, and princes to attend him. Liu Qiang thanks the emperor, asks to return his fief to commandery status, and urges Mingdi to care doubly for the empress dowager. Mingdi mourns deeply but orders burial goods kept simple because Liu Qiang had been known for modesty and frugality.

## Key Characteristics

- Established crown prince whose security collapses after his mother's deposition.
- Guo-line son whose vulnerability begins in the 41 CE empress change.
- Uses repeated resignation to turn forced succession loss into morally legible withdrawal.
- Recast as Donghai King when Liu Yang / Liu Zhuang becomes crown prince.
- Receives exceptional Guangwu-era honor without becoming an unbounded capital presence.
- Royal kinsman who refuses to let later grievance language become rebellion.
- Final memorial, fief-return request, and frugal burial extend the same self-limiting profile.

## Qualifications

Hanji 719 adds the setup for Liu Qiang's vulnerability but not the resignation itself; Hanji 720 remains the direct source for withdrawal and replacement. The combined page remains source-bounded rather than a full biography. Hanji 720 presents his motive as a survival and filial calculation after Guo Shengtong's deposition; that is stronger than a pure virtue reading, but still an interpretation of compressed chronicle material. Yuan Hong's criticism should stay attached to the synthesis: Liu Qiang's peaceful withdrawal reduced conflict without proving that replacing an established heir was sound institutional practice. Queen Guo's death notice and many details of Liu Qiang's fief administration remain outside the current evidence.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 719 as the 41 CE setup: Guo Shengtong's deposition makes Liu Qiang's later crown-prince insecurity intelligible.
- Preserved Hanji 720 as Liu Qiang's root succession layer: repeated resignation, Donghai King settlement, and Liu Zhuang's installation.
- Reframed the profile around maternal-status loss before withdrawal, not only withdrawal itself.
- Preserved Hanji 728 as preferential treatment and post-guest-disaster fief-return evidence.
- Preserved Hanji 732 and Hanji 733-1 as the loyal former-heir and frugal final-memory layers.
