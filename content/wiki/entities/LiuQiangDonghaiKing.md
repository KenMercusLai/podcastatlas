## Overview

Liu Qiang appears across Hanji 732 and Hanji 733-1 as a displaced former-heir figure who becomes a positive royal-kin contrast under [[EmperorMingOfHan|Mingdi]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-732-donghan-guangwudi-liuxiu-jiabeng-bingsi-de-lnsu1le2x-cj75cjslysvbndeuvr|Hanji 732]] shows him rejecting an anonymous succession provocation, while [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-733-1-lishi-suo-yiwang-de-diwang-zhishi-mingjun-liuzhuang-lor7vhzzpwz1rv9tijksogukjqvr|Hanji 733-1]] shows his final illness, deferential memorial, and frugal burial.

## Current Profile

Hanji 732 gives Liu Qiang an accession-risk role. After [[LiuXiu|Guangwu]] dies, [[LiuJingGuanglingKingEasternHan|Liu Jing]] sends an anonymous letter arguing that Liu Qiang had been wrongly displaced as heir and that Queen Guo had been wrongly removed. Liu Qiang does not take the bait: he panics, secures the messenger figure, seals the letter, and reports the matter to Mingdi.

Mingdi's response confirms why Liu Qiang is treated as a stabilizing royal kinsman. The emperor keeps Liu Jing's role private, has Liu Qiang leave the capital first, and then reassures the Guo/Liu Qiang branch. Liu Qiang's loyal report lets the new reign contain a dangerous grievance without making the former heir into a rival center.

Hanji 733-1 then emphasizes Liu Qiang's final memorial rather than a full earlier biography. During his illness, Mingdi sends imperial envoys, physicians, and princes to attend him. Liu Qiang thanks the emperor, asks to return his fief to commandery status, and urges Mingdi to care doubly for the empress dowager. Mingdi weeps, mourns deeply, and sends gifts beyond ordinary funeral practice, but then orders burial goods kept simple because Liu Qiang had been known for modesty and frugality.

## Key Characteristics

- Displaced former-heir figure whose loyal report helps stabilize Mingdi's accession.
- Royal kinsman who refuses to let grievance language become rebellion.
- Positive contrast to Liu Jing's more dangerous succession and treason signals.
- His illness triggers unusually attentive imperial care.
- Requests return of his fief to commandery status, marking self-limitation rather than territorial assertiveness.
- His burial becomes an early Mingdi example of grief constrained by frugality.

## Qualifications

This page is not a full succession-history account of Liu Qiang. Hanji 732 gives the accession-provocation layer, and Hanji 733-1 gives the final illness and burial layer. Queen Guo's full biography and Liu Qiang's earlier deposition remain source-scoped until added by separate evidence.

- Reframed Hanji 733-1's final illness as the later positive-kinship memory rather than Liu Qiang's first visible role.
- Preserved his profile as a loyal displaced-kin contrast to Liu Jing.
