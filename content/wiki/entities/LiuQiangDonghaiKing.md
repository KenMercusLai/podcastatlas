## Overview

Liu Qiang appears across Hanji 728, Hanji 732, and Hanji 733-1 as a displaced former-heir figure whose treatment tests how royal honor can coexist with political containment. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-728-weihe-mingchen-nan-shanzhong-beihou-de-yuanyin-ni-gai-dong-ltjl2bgpyyz1bmwe-4mfcyd9e6cf|Hanji 728]] shows Guangwu favoring him and then sending him back to his fief after the guest disaster, [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-732-donghan-guangwudi-liuxiu-jiabeng-bingsi-de-lnsu1le2x-cj75cjslysvbndeuvr|Hanji 732]] shows him rejecting an anonymous succession provocation, and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-733-1-lishi-suo-yiwang-de-diwang-zhishi-mingjun-liuzhuang-lor7vhzzpwz1rv9tijksogukjqvr|Hanji 733-1]] shows his final illness, deferential memorial, and frugal burial.

## Current Profile

Hanji 728 gives Liu Qiang the earlier Guangwu-era honor layer. In 52 CE, Liu Xiu changes Lu King Liu Xing's fief to Beihai and merges the former Lu territory into Donghai. The source says Liu Qiang is very proper in ritual conduct, and Liu Xiu treats him exceptionally by expanding his fief and granting huben guards, cavalry, and ritual-music equipment. The same episode later lists Liu Qiang among the princes sent back to their fiefs after the prince-guest disaster.

Hanji 732 gives Liu Qiang an accession-risk role. After [[LiuXiu|Guangwu]] dies, [[LiuJingGuanglingKingEasternHan|Liu Jing]] sends an anonymous letter arguing that Liu Qiang had been wrongly displaced as heir and that Queen Guo had been wrongly removed. Liu Qiang does not take the bait: he panics, secures the messenger figure, seals the letter, and reports the matter to Mingdi.

Mingdi's response confirms why Liu Qiang is treated as a stabilizing royal kinsman. The emperor keeps Liu Jing's role private, has Liu Qiang leave the capital first, and then reassures the Guo/Liu Qiang branch. Liu Qiang's loyal report lets the new reign contain a dangerous grievance without making the former heir into a rival center.

Hanji 733-1 then emphasizes Liu Qiang's final memorial rather than a full earlier biography. During his illness, Mingdi sends imperial envoys, physicians, and princes to attend him. Liu Qiang thanks the emperor, asks to return his fief to commandery status, and urges Mingdi to care doubly for the empress dowager. Mingdi weeps, mourns deeply, and sends gifts beyond ordinary funeral practice, but then orders burial goods kept simple because Liu Qiang had been known for modesty and frugality.

## Key Characteristics

- Displaced former-heir figure who receives exceptional Guangwu-era honor without being allowed to become an unbounded capital presence.
- Granted expanded Donghai territory, guards, cavalry, and ritual-music equipment in Hanji 728.
- Sent back to his fief after the prince-guest disaster, making honor compatible with territorial distance.
- Royal kinsman who refuses to let grievance language become rebellion in Hanji 732.
- His illness triggers unusually attentive imperial care in Hanji 733-1.
- Requests return of his fief to commandery status, marking self-limitation rather than territorial assertiveness.
- His burial becomes an early Mingdi example of grief constrained by frugality.

## Qualifications

This page is not a full succession-history account of Liu Qiang. Hanji 728 gives the pre-accession honor and fief-distance layer; Hanji 732 gives the accession-provocation layer; Hanji 733-1 gives the final illness and burial layer. Queen Guo's death notice and Liu Qiang's earlier deposition remain source-scoped until added by separate evidence.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 728 as Liu Qiang's earlier Guangwu-era preferential treatment and post-guest-disaster fief-return layer.
- Reframed the profile around the tension between exceptional royal honor and territorial-political containment.
- Preserved Hanji 732 and Hanji 733-1 as the loyal former-heir and frugal final-memory layers.
