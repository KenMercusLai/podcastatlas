## Overview

Liu Qiang appears across Hanji 686, Hanji 719, Hanji 720, Hanji 728, Hanji 732, and Hanji 733-1 as a crown-prince-turned-Donghai-king figure whose survival depends on turning succession loss into controlled royal honor. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-686-liuxiu-fashi-zhi-ai-yinlihua-weihe-li-guoshi-wei-hou-lnqlqgq54srb1zzzgemfwuwujhvc|Hanji 686]] now supplies the root setup: after [[YinLihua|阴丽华]] declines the empress position because [[GuoShengtong|郭圣通]] has already borne a son, Liu Xiu makes Guo empress and Liu Qiang crown prince in 26 CE. Hanji 719 and Hanji 720 then show how Guo's later deposition breaks that maternal-status foundation and leads to Liu Qiang's repeated resignation.

## Current Profile

Hanji 686 supplies Liu Qiang's initial crown-prince layer. The source says Guo has already given Liu Xiu a son, Yin Lihua refuses the empress position for that reason, and Liu Xiu makes Guo empress and her son Liu Qiang crown prince on 26 CE-06-07. The page therefore now treats Liu Qiang's later vulnerability as the reversal of an earlier mother-son settlement, not merely as a sudden problem after 41 CE.

Hanji 719 supplies the pre-withdrawal shock. Guo Shengtong is deposed and replaced by Yin Lihua in 41 CE. The source does not yet narrate Liu Qiang's resignation, but it makes clear why his status becomes vulnerable: a crown prince attached to the deposed empress no longer stands on the same maternal foundation.

Hanji 720 gives Liu Qiang the crown-prince withdrawal layer. After Guo's deposition, the source says Liu Qiang becomes uneasy because his mother's status had been his support. [[ZhiYunEasternHan|郅恽]] advises that remaining in an unstable heir position is dangerous and does not help him serve his mother, so Liu Qiang repeatedly asks through trusted attendants and royal kin to resign and retire to a fief. Liu Xiu hesitates for years, then in 43 CE makes Liu Qiang Donghai King and makes Liu Yang crown prince, renaming him Liu Zhuang.

The same source preserves the institutional objection through Yuan Hong's comment. Yuan Hong argues that crown-prince status is meant to settle order and should not be changed if the heir has no grave fault. Liu Qiang's withdrawal therefore solves a family danger without making the original succession change institutionally clean.

Hanji 728 gives Liu Qiang the later Guangwu-era honor layer. In 52 CE, Liu Xiu changes Lu King Liu Xing's fief to Beihai and merges the former Lu territory into Donghai. The source says Liu Qiang is very proper in ritual conduct, and Liu Xiu treats him exceptionally by expanding his fief and granting huben guards, cavalry, and ritual-music equipment. Hanji 732 and Hanji 733-1 then show him as a loyal former heir who reports Liu Jing's anonymous letter and closes his life with a deferential fief-return request and frugal burial memory.

## Key Characteristics

- Crown prince first established through Guo Shengtong's empress status in 26 CE.
- Guo-line son whose security collapses after his mother's deposition in 41 CE.
- Uses repeated resignation to turn forced succession loss into morally legible withdrawal.
- Recast as Donghai King when Liu Yang / Liu Zhuang becomes crown prince.
- Receives exceptional Guangwu-era honor without becoming an unbounded capital presence.
- Royal kinsman who refuses to let later grievance language become rebellion.
- Final memorial, fief-return request, and frugal burial extend the same self-limiting profile.

## Qualifications

Hanji 686 gives the initial crown-prince appointment but not Liu Qiang's later conduct. Hanji 719 adds the setup for Liu Qiang's vulnerability but not the resignation itself; Hanji 720 remains the direct source for withdrawal and replacement. The combined page remains source-bounded rather than a full biography. Yuan Hong's criticism should stay attached to the synthesis: Liu Qiang's peaceful withdrawal reduced conflict without proving that replacing an established heir was sound institutional practice. Queen Guo's death notice and many details of Liu Qiang's fief administration remain outside the current evidence.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 686 as Liu Qiang's initial 26 CE crown-prince appointment and mother-son status foundation.
- Reframed the profile around an arc from initial maternal-status security to later maternal-status loss.
- Preserved Hanji 720 as Liu Qiang's root succession-withdrawal layer.
- Preserved Hanji 728, Hanji 732, and Hanji 733-1 as honor, loyal reporting, and final-memory layers.
