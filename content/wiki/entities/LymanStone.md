# Lyman Stone

## Overview
Lyman Stone is the demographer in [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-58f17fc3725ef3102277ae962324f7dd-59875813-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-58f17fc3725ef3102277ae962324f7dd-59875813]] who defines pronatalism as helping people achieve the family size they want rather than pressuring unwilling people to have children.

## Current Profile
Stone places the emotional cost of unrealized family goals ahead of replacement-rate targets or fiscal alarm. He argues that falling exposure to marriage is a major recent U.S. fertility mechanism and connects delayed marriage to later earnings stability and reduced face-to-face social life.

He is more optimistic than [[MelissaKearney]] and [[NickBloom]] about financial incentives producing additional births, while acknowledging that a replacement-level effect could require very high public expenditure. He also favors more abundant family-suitable housing as a tractable policy direction.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames pronatalism through voluntary preference realization.
- Emphasizes marriage exposure, social interaction, and relationship formation.
- Treats financial incentives as effective at the margin but potentially very costly.
- Favors housing abundance as a promising family-policy lever.
