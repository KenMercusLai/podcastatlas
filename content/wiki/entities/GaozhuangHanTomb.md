## Overview

高庄汉墓 / Gaozhuang Han Tomb is the large, robbed, and burned Western Han royal tomb that [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-342-1-waihao-bie-luan-jiao-you-de-mingzi-hui-bei-duzhou-yisheng-lpehgfgtkbed4vg3z7yegsqalll1|Hanji 342-1]] attributes to [[LiuShunChangshanKingWesternHan|常山王刘顺]].

## Current Profile

The episode uses Gaozhuang to contrast fictional tomb defenses with archaeological disturbance. It says ancient builders mainly relied on sealing a burial, that robbery shafts are common, and that Gaozhuang's robbers not only removed valuable objects but set an unusually destructive fire that caused stone chamber elements to collapse.

The site's narrative role is stronger than its documented archaeological profile in the supplied material. The episode reports that archaeologists identify it as a Changshan royal tomb and specifically as Liu Shun's burial, but it does not reproduce the dating, inscriptions, spatial context, grave inventory, human remains, or candidate comparison behind that identification.

## Key Characteristics

- Large Western Han tomb presented as a vassal king's burial.
- Extensively looted, with the grave inventory described as having been stripped of precious objects.
- Subjected to a rare post-robbery fire said to have damaged or collapsed stone chamber structures.
- Associated with [[ChangshanKingdomWesternHan|西汉常山国]] in the episode.
- Personally attributed to Liu Shun without the supporting excavation argument being supplied.

## Qualifications

The supplied source is a compact episode summary, not an excavation report.
