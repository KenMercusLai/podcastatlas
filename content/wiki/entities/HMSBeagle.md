# HMS Beagle

## Overview

HMS Beagle was the British survey ship on whose 1831-1836 circumnavigation [[CharlesDarwin|Charles Darwin]] gathered geological, biological, and social observations later important to his intellectual development.

## Current Profile

[[283-ecuador-darwins-adventure-to-the-galapagos-glt2992613399]] presents the Beagle as more than transport to the [[GalapagosIslands|Galapagos Islands]]. Its extended South American survey exposed Darwin to marine life, rainforest, fossils, mountains, plains, violence, and changing landscapes before the island specimens entered the story. The voyage joined field collection to later metropolitan analysis: specimens gathered aboard the ship acquired new meaning when specialists such as [[JohnGould]] examined them in Britain.

## Key Characteristics

- Royal Navy survey vessel assigned to map South America.
- Voyage planned for roughly two years but extended into a five-year circumnavigation.
- Commanded by [[RobertFitzRoy|Robert FitzRoy]].
- Platform for Darwin's geological observation, specimen collection, and comparative natural history.
- Link between field evidence in South America and the Galapagos and later expert analysis in London.

## Evidence

- Survey and voyage role - [[283-ecuador-darwins-adventure-to-the-galapagos-glt2992613399]] dates departure from Plymouth to 27 December 1831 and describes the survey mission.
- Scientific platform - [[283-ecuador-darwins-adventure-to-the-galapagos-glt2992613399]] follows Darwin's geology, fossils, wildlife, and specimen collection across the voyage.
- Evidence transport - [[283-ecuador-darwins-adventure-to-the-galapagos-glt2992613399]] connects shipboard collection to Gould's later classification of the birds.

## Qualifications

The current profile comes from an episode focused on Darwin and the Galapagos, not a complete naval or hydrographic history of the vessel. The source calls Darwin a naturalist, geologist, and gentleman companion; the exact institutional terms of his role and the work of other crew members remain outside this bounded account.

## What Changed

- Established the Beagle as the infrastructure connecting distributed observation, specimen collection, and later interpretation.
- Emphasized the wider South American voyage rather than reducing the ship to its Galapagos stop.

## Relationships

- [[CharlesDarwin]] - naturalist and geologist whose later thinking was shaped by the voyage.
- [[RobertFitzRoy]] - captain and shipboard political and social counterpart to Darwin.
- [[GalapagosIslands]] - September 1835 field site within the larger circumnavigation.
- [[EvolutionaryTheoryFormation]] - gradual knowledge process supported by voyage observations and preserved specimens.
- [[ObservationBeforeInference]] - method illustrated by evidence gathered before its full significance was understood.
