# HMS Beagle

## Overview

HMS Beagle was the British survey ship on whose 1831-1836 circumnavigation [[CharlesDarwin|Charles Darwin]] gathered geological, biological, and social observations later important to his intellectual development.

## Current Profile

The difficult departure sits behind the ship's later scientific importance. The small Beagle required [[CharlesDarwin|Charles Darwin]] and [[RobertFitzRoy|Robert FitzRoy]] to share cramped cabin space; storms delayed its planned November sailing, a sailor died overboard, two December departures failed, and the final 27 December departure began a voyage Darwin endured with severe seasickness.

The ship was more than transport to the [[GalapagosIslands|Galapagos Islands]]. Its extended South American survey exposed Darwin to marine life, rainforest, fossils, mountains, plains, violence, and changing landscapes before the island specimens entered the story. The voyage joined field collection to later metropolitan analysis: specimens gathered aboard the ship acquired new meaning when specialists such as [[JohnGould]] examined them in Britain.

## Key Characteristics

- Royal Navy survey vessel assigned to map South America.
- Voyage planned for roughly two years but extended into a five-year circumnavigation.
- Commanded by [[RobertFitzRoy|Robert FitzRoy]].
- Platform for Darwin's geological observation, specimen collection, and comparative natural history.
- Link between field evidence in South America and the Galapagos and later expert analysis in London.
- Difficult shipboard environment whose delays, danger, confinement, and motion shaped Darwin's experience.

## Qualifications

The current profile comes from two Darwin-centered episodes, not a complete naval or hydrographic history of the vessel. They call Darwin a naturalist, geologist, and gentleman companion; the exact institutional terms of his role and the work of other crew members remain outside this bounded account. Ship dimensions, planned and attempted departure dates, the accident narrative, and Darwin's private reactions remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Added the storm-delayed and physically difficult departure behind the later scientific voyage.
- Connected the ship's cramped, dangerous environment to Darwin's lived experience without reducing the voyage to hardship.
