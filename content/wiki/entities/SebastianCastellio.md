# Sebastian Castellio

## Overview

Sebastian Castellio is presented as a Reformation scholar and critic of [[JohnCalvin|John Calvin]] who turned [[MichaelServetus|Michael Servetus]]'s execution into an argument against persecuting alleged heretics.

## Current Profile

In [[274-switzerland-calvins-cancel-culture-glt2874543265]], Castellio belongs to the scholarly opposition that condemned the Servetus execution and compared Calvin's conduct to the persecuting authorities Protestants had resisted. The episode associates him with the 1554 work *Concerning Heretics and Whether They Should Be Persecuted* and with the argument that Christians should not kill alleged heretics because Christ himself suffered persecution.

His importance in this bounded profile is genealogical. The source connects the argument to later free-thinking and Enlightenment toleration, including Voltaire, without claiming that Castellio articulated a complete modern regime of equal citizenship or secular religious neutrality.

## Key Characteristics

- Reformation-era scholar associated with opposition to Calvin's coercive authority.
- Critic of executing people for alleged heresy.
- Interpreter of Servetus's death as a warning against reproducing persecution within Protestant reform.
- Contributor to a longer intellectual history of liberty of conscience and toleration.

## Evidence

- Servetus response - [[274-switzerland-calvins-cancel-culture-glt2874543265]] places Castellio among critics who condemned Calvin after the execution.
- Anti-persecution argument - [[274-switzerland-calvins-cancel-culture-glt2874543265]] associates the 1554 toleration text and its Christological argument with Castellio.
- Later influence - [[274-switzerland-calvins-cancel-culture-glt2874543265]] links this reasoning to later free thinkers and Voltaire.

## Qualifications

The source document renders the name “Sebastian Castello”; this page uses the standard English form Sebastian Castellio. The episode compresses authorship, pseudonymous publication, intellectual context, and later reception, so exact bibliographic attribution and lines of influence remain source-scoped. Opposition to execution for heresy should not automatically be expanded into every modern conception of free speech, secularism, or equal religious citizenship.

## What Changed

- Established Castellio as the episode's bridge from the Servetus execution to an explicit anti-persecution argument.
- Bounded the claimed afterlife as an intellectual lineage rather than a complete modern rights doctrine.
