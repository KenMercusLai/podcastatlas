# Gregory VII

## Overview

Gregory VII enters the wiki as the reforming pope who made clean shaving part of a wider program of Latin clerical purification and discipline.

## Current Profile

[[491-historys-greatest-beards-from-egyptian-queens-to-medieval-conquerors-part-1-glt6058938613]] places Gregory's grooming demand alongside clerical celibacy and other Gregorian reforms. In this account, requiring priests as well as monks to shave drew on an older association between hairlessness and holiness while distinguishing Latin clergy from pagan hairiness and the bearded practice retained in Orthodox Christianity.

## Key Characteristics

- Extended clean-shaving expectations from monks toward priests.
- Joined bodily presentation to institutional purification.
- Reinforced a Latin Christian code distinct from Orthodox bearded clergy.

## Evidence

- Reform package: [[491-historys-greatest-beards-from-egyptian-queens-to-medieval-conquerors-part-1-glt6058938613]] places clerical shaving beside celibacy within Gregorian reform.

## Qualifications

This profile is confined to the episode's treatment of grooming within Gregorian reform. The suggested role of Viking pagan hairiness in medieval Christian attitudes is interpretive and source-scoped, and clerical practice was not necessarily uniform.
