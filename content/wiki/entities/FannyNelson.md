# Fanny Nelson

## Overview

Fanny Nelson was [[HoratioNelson|Horatio Nelson]]'s wife and caregiver, whose support during his disabling 1797 recovery preceded abandonment during his relationship with [[EmmaHamilton|Emma Hamilton]].

## Current Profile

The earlier bounded source presents Fanny caring closely for Nelson after the Tenerife wound cost him an arm and left him in pain, unable to manage ordinary tasks, and afraid of becoming professionally useless. Nelson credited her tenderness with helping save his life, and the couple appeared close enough to buy a house near Ipswich before his renewed Mediterranean command.

The later source presents Fanny waiting for Nelson after more than two years away while his increasingly sparse and cold letters had already made her anxious. His return with the pregnant Emma and [[SirWilliamHamilton|Sir William Hamilton]] forced Fanny into repeated public appearances inside the relationship triangle; witnesses saw her cry and faint, while Nelson chose Christmas with the Hamiltons rather than with her and his father.

Their marriage effectively ended in January 1801 when Fanny asked Nelson to choose between her and Emma and he refused to renounce Emma. Nelson later offered an allowance while rejecting Fanny's care, leaving her socially unable to form another partnership even though her position as his legal widow eventually gave her more financial protection than Emma received.

## Key Characteristics

- Abandoned spouse whose marital crisis unfolded under intense public attention.
- Caregiver during Nelson's disabling injury, pain, dependency, and fear of professional uselessness.
- Recipient of increasingly cold correspondence and a later rejection of her care.
- Socially constrained wife unable to move on openly while Nelson lived.
- Financially protected relative to Emma through legal status and Nelson's later pension.

## Qualifications

The bounded evidence consists of two Nelson-centered popular-history episodes and does not recover Fanny's full life or independent voice. Reported dialogue, the medical effect of her care, emotional states, social options, finances, responsibility for particular misunderstandings, and the long-term meaning of her widow's pension require broader evidence.

## What Changed

- Added Fanny's close caregiving during Nelson's post-Tenerife disability and pain.
- Established that gratitude, apparent marital closeness, and a shared house preceded the later abandonment.
