# Fanny Nelson

## Overview

Fanny Nelson was [[HoratioNelson|Horatio Nelson]]'s wife, whose abandonment during his relationship with [[EmmaHamilton|Emma Hamilton]] made her private distress a public consequence of his naval celebrity.

## Current Profile

The bounded source presents Fanny waiting for Nelson after more than two years away while his increasingly sparse and cold letters had already made her anxious. His return with the pregnant Emma and [[SirWilliamHamilton|Sir William Hamilton]] forced Fanny into repeated public appearances inside the relationship triangle; witnesses saw her cry and faint, while Nelson chose Christmas with the Hamiltons rather than with her and his father.

Their marriage effectively ended in January 1801 when Fanny asked Nelson to choose between her and Emma and he refused to renounce Emma. Nelson later offered an allowance while rejecting Fanny's care, leaving her socially unable to form another partnership even though her position as his legal widow eventually gave her more financial protection than Emma received.

## Key Characteristics

- Abandoned spouse whose marital crisis unfolded under intense public attention.
- Recipient of increasingly cold correspondence and a later rejection of her care.
- Socially constrained wife unable to move on openly while Nelson lived.
- Financially protected relative to Emma through legal status and Nelson's later pension.

## Qualifications

The bounded evidence is a Nelson-centered popular-history episode and does not recover Fanny's full life or independent voice. Reported dialogue, emotional states, social options, finances, responsibility for particular misunderstandings, and the long-term meaning of her widow's pension require broader evidence.

## What Changed

- Established Fanny as a historical actor affected by Nelson's celebrity rather than an unnamed obstacle in his relationship with Emma.
- Joined public humiliation, legal constraint, emotional abandonment, and relative financial protection in one profile.
