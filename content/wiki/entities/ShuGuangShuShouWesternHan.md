## Overview

疏广与疏受 / Shu Guang and Shu Shou enter the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-431-zhichang-bidu-kuli-zhizi-zhanganshi-de-zhichang-zhihui-lmxey8uoliwynimxcnh1khjefenl|Hanji 431]] as Western Han crown-prince teachers who retire together after reaching rank, reputation, and reward sufficiency. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-419-nongxu-zuojia-de-hanchen-wangcheng-ruhe-ba-hanxuandi-pian-de-tuantuanzhuan-lim7jqyvestgoqb68pxhyuwiuhyd|Hanji 419]] backfills their appointment and shows Shu Guang's earlier usefulness: he can turn [[XuGuanghanWesternHan|徐广汉]]'s family request around crown prince [[HanYuandi|刘奭]] into a public-interest rule for heir education. The Hanji 431 transcript writes their names as 叔广 and 叔寿; this canonical page uses the standard historical names 疏广 and 疏受.

## Current Profile

Their profile now has three linked lessons. Hanji 419 first makes Shu Guang a high-trust tutor and court interpreter. After [[EmperorXuanOfHan|汉宣帝]] appoints him crown-prince taifu and Shu Shou shaofu, Xu Guanghan asks to have [[XuShunWesternHan|徐顺]] oversee the crown prince's household. Xuan asks Shu Guang for his view, and Shu Guang reads the political need: the request should be refused, but through a principled explanation rather than a blunt personal rejection. He says the crown prince is the state's heir, that his teachers and companions should be outstanding people of the realm, and that the prince should not be especially tied to the Xu maternal family.

Second, Hanji 431 shows the pair's exit judgment. Shu Guang tells Shu Shou that satisfaction prevents humiliation and knowing where to stop prevents danger. They decide that reaching two-thousand-shi rank, honor, and wealth is enough, so they ask to retire by claiming illness. Xuan approves and grants money; the crown prince and public send-off turn the departure into a praised example rather than disgrace.

Third, the pair's retirement is not private hoarding. Back home, they host neighbors, kin, friends, and guests, dispersing reward wealth instead of buying more property for descendants. Shu Guang argues that existing land and houses are enough if descendants are diligent, while too much wealth can blunt worthy children and empower foolish ones. The page therefore treats them as a joint case of [[CrownPrinceTutorPublicInterest|太子师傅公器化]], [[TimelyRetirementRiskAvoidance|知足知止式退场]], and [[WealthTransmissionRestraint|传财有度]].

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han crown-prince teachers whose timing makes retirement appear wise rather than evasive.
- Shu Guang is trusted to voice a public-interest refusal of a maternal-family request around the crown prince.
- Separate crown-prince education from outer-relative household convenience.
- Treat sufficient rank, merit, and wealth as the signal to leave office.
- Receive imperial and crown-prince gifts plus public honor at departure.
- Spend reward wealth socially after returning home rather than concentrating it as descendant property.
- Frame excess inheritance as a risk to both worthy and foolish descendants.

## Qualifications

This page is source-scoped to Hanji 419 and Hanji 431. Hanji 419 supplies appointment, Xu-family refusal, and crown-prince education boundary material; it does not reconstruct the complete original court debate, Shu Guang's full teaching record, or Xu-family intention beyond the episode's interpretation. Hanji 431 supplies the retirement and wealth-transmission lesson; it does not reconstruct Shu Guang's or Shu Shou's complete biographies or the full original wording of their retirement exchange.
