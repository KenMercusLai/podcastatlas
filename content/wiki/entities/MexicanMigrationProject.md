# Mexican Migration Project

## Overview

The Mexican Migration Project is a long-running household research program presented in [[general-chapman-s-last-stand-general-chapman-s-last-stand|General Chapman’s Last Stand]] as the empirical foundation for understanding Mexican migration to the [[UnitedStates]] as historically circular.

## Current Profile

The episode says [[DouglasMassey]] and [[JorgeDurand]] began interviewing migrant families in [[Mexico]] during the 1970s and collected detailed histories of first, last, and total United States trips. After decades of accumulation, the resulting dataset could compare arrivals with returns and examine how crossing conditions changed repeated movement.

Its key contribution in this source is conceptual as well as empirical. By measuring departures and return probabilities, the project challenges a permanent-settlement model imported from other immigration histories. The episode uses its findings to argue that enforcement increased crossing cost without removing labor demand, thereby encouraging longer stays, family reunification, and [[BorderEnforcementSettlementBackfire|settlement]].

## Key Characteristics

- Builds migration evidence from household interviews and individual trip histories.
- Tracks repeated movement rather than treating every entry as a separate permanent immigrant.
- Makes departures, return probabilities, crossing cost, and family location part of migration measurement.
- Supplies the episode’s evidence for a shift from circulation toward settlement as enforcement intensified.

## Evidence

- Data collection: [[general-chapman-s-last-stand-general-chapman-s-last-stand]] describes family interviews and first, last, and total trip histories begun in the 1970s.
- Historical pattern: [[general-chapman-s-last-stand-general-chapman-s-last-stand]] presents circular labor movement as the project’s principal finding.
- Behavioral change: [[general-chapman-s-last-stand-general-chapman-s-last-stand]] attributes declining return probability and family settlement to rising crossing cost and danger.
- Counterfactual analysis: [[general-chapman-s-last-stand-general-chapman-s-last-stand]] cites a 2016 study associated with project researchers estimating a smaller undocumented population under lower enforcement.

## Qualifications

The supplied document summarizes the project through a narrative podcast episode. It does not provide sampling frames, questionnaires, weighting, missing-data treatment, model specifications, confidence intervals, or competing estimates. The project’s findings are therefore evidence for a mechanism in this wiki, not a license to treat every migrant, period, route, or policy environment as identical.

## What Changed

- Created a project profile distinguishing repeated-trip measurement from arrival-only immigration counts.
- Preserved the difference between observed histories and model-based enforcement counterfactuals.
