## Overview

王章 / Wang Zhang is the Jingzhao Yin remembered as plain and upright, then killed after criticizing [[WangFengWesternHan|王凤]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-519-aiguozhe-wangzhang-de-si-yuanyugen-cuo-le-zhuzi-lt5hzlh9t-gryds1lpn0ieqw3cvn|Hanji 519]] now supplies the missing confrontation: he persuades [[HanChengdi|汉成帝]] that Wang Feng's concentrated power, not the Dingtao king, is the eclipse's political warning; recommends [[FengYewangWesternHan|冯野王]] as replacement; and is prosecuted after the emperor reverses course. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-520-daji-duishou-weishenme-yao-xian-faqi-yulunzhan-lh1ibhc9xsihtrh6mhzznqq9ioso|Hanji 520]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-544-yuangong-yue-zhongcheng-laoban-yue-huangkong-lk52zm46585z2noqxmtfn9kpwek2|Hanji 544]] trace the resulting public suspicion and court silence.

## Current Profile

The three sources make Wang Zhang both an upright remonstrant and a political-risk failure case. Hanji 519 says his diagnosis is strong enough to move Chengdi: Wang Feng has used disaster interpretation, palace access, and personnel control to isolate the ruler, so the remedy must be replacement rather than another warning. Wang Zhang's vulnerability lies in treating Chengdi's private agreement as durable authority. Once the plan leaks, Wang Feng's resignation and [[WangZhengjun|王政君]]'s pressure cause Chengdi to protect the family alliance and sacrifice his adviser.

Hanji 520 makes the reputational aftermath explicit. Because the formal charges are not publicly disclosed, people read Wang Zhang as a man punished for direct remonstrance rather than transparently convicted of a known crime. Hanji 544 carries that interpretation forward through Mei Fu. Wang Zhang therefore functions as a negative remonstrance precedent: his death teaches officials that correct advice may be fatal when the ruler cannot protect the adviser, gives Du Qing grounds to call for visible speech repair, and gives Mei Fu grounds to warn that blocking correction lets outer-relative power grow unchecked.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han official presented as plain, loyal, and upright.
- Diagnoses Wang Feng's concentrated power and asks Chengdi to replace him.
- Recommends Feng Yewang after Chengdi privately accepts the plan.
- Misjudges Chengdi's ability to withstand Wang-family and maternal pressure.
- Dies in prison while his wife and children are exiled.
- Becomes a punished-speaker precedent that produces public suspicion and wider official silence.
- Gives Du Qing and Mei Fu grounds to demand protection for remonstrance.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 544, Hanji 520, and Hanji 519 and does not resolve Wang Zhang's full prosecution record, precise charges, biography, or the complete Wang-family factional context. Hanji 519 supplies a strongly moralized causal account in which Chengdi's weakness and Wang-family pressure decide the outcome; it does not independently establish every motive or legal detail. Hanji 520 preserves the gap between secret formal charges and public interpretation, so the wiki treats wrongful-death belief as politically important without independently settling legal guilt.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 519's confrontation, Feng Yewang recommendation, imperial reversal, prison death, and family-exile sequence.
- Reframed Wang Zhang as both an upright remonstrant and a subordinate exposed by weak authority.
- Preserved Hanji 520 and Hanji 544 as the public-trust and court-silencing aftermath.
