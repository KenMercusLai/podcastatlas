# Neko Health

## Overview
Neko Health is a preventive-health company co-founded by [[DanielEk|Daniel Ek]] and [[HjalmarNilsonne|Hjalmar Nilsonne]] that combines diagnostic equipment, software, AI, laboratory testing, imaging, and clinician consultation in one examination.

## Current Profile
The source presents Neko as a vertically integrated attempt to make broad preventive assessment fast enough and inexpensive enough to become a repeated consumer service. Its U.S. product is described as a $499, roughly one-hour examination that combines blood markers, high-resolution skin imaging, cardiovascular measures, circulation measures, grip strength, and a clinician review.

Neko's distinctive claim is longitudinal rather than merely comprehensive: indexed findings can be compared across annual visits, while wearable data can supplement clinical measurements. The model remains early. Founder-reported detection and behavior-change signals are promising, but they do not establish that annual broad scanning improves population outcomes or reduces total healthcare spending.

## Key Characteristics
- Vertically integrated ownership of facilities, diagnostic equipment, software, and clinical staff.
- Multimodal examination combining laboratory, imaging, cardiovascular, circulation, and functional measures.
- Longitudinal indexing intended to reveal change across repeat annual visits.
- AI-assisted triage followed by clinician and specialist review rather than autonomous diagnosis.
- Consumer price and time design intended to lower the access burden of preventive assessment.
- International rollout beginning in Sweden before expansion to the United Kingdom and United States.

## Evidence
- Integrated service design: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-daniel-ek-life-after-spotify-broken-healthcare-incentives-catching-di]] describes the facilities, equipment, software, staff, measurements, and consultation.
- Longitudinal and AI workflow: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-daniel-ek-life-after-spotify-broken-healthcare-incentives-catching-di]] describes skin indexing, annual comparison, automated flagging, and clinician review.
- Early operating signals: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-daniel-ek-life-after-spotify-broken-healthcare-incentives-catching-di]] reports more than 100,000 scans, a roughly 1% serious-condition detection rate, and positive unit economics.
- Expansion model: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-daniel-ek-life-after-spotify-broken-healthcare-incentives-catching-di]] reports launches in Sweden and the United Kingdom followed by New York and planned U.S. expansion.

## Qualifications
Scan volume, detection rates, clinical-trial counts, profitability, and expansion plans are founder-reported. The source does not provide study methods, comparator groups, false-positive rates, downstream procedures, representativeness, privacy analysis, or evidence of reduced morbidity, mortality, or total spending. The $499 price also limits access.

## What Changed
- Created the company profile and separated its product design from unproven population-level outcome claims.
