## Overview

[[TheLastEmperorFilm|《末代皇帝》]] is Bernardo Bertolucci's film about Puyi, invoked by [[WushichaYanjiusuo|无时差研究所]] as a positive counterexample to the claim that foreign filmmakers are inherently unable to tell Chinese stories.

## Current Profile

The bounded source praises the film for a grounded story, layered characters, historical research, and use of Puyi's autobiography as an important basis. Access to the Forbidden City is treated as part of its material credibility, while the characters' different values and responses to political change give the historical setting human complexity.

Its English dialogue remains a qualification rather than a fatal flaw. The hosts find the delivery and emotion more natural than in [[Mulan2020Film|《花木兰》(2020)]], but they also suggest that a film released in a later climate might face stronger criticism for language choice and perspective. The comparison supports an open authorship standard based on research and craft rather than nationality.

## Key Characteristics

- Uses Puyi's autobiography as an important source foundation in the episode's account.
- Connects individual character conflict to historical transformation rather than using history only as scenery.
- Benefits from material access to the Forbidden City and a strong sense of place.
- Uses English dialogue in a Chinese story, but is praised for comparatively natural delivery and emotional credibility.
- Functions as the episode's positive cross-cultural filmmaking counterexample.

## Qualifications

This profile records one podcast episode's retrospective judgment rather than independent film scholarship. The source acknowledges a possible time filter around canonical reputation, and it does not fully assess historical accuracy, production politics, adaptation choices, Puyi's autobiography as evidence, or the range of Chinese and international reception. Praise relative to [[Mulan2020Film|《花木兰》(2020)]] does not settle every question about representation or language.
