## Overview

翟戎 / Zhai Rong is an Eastern Han frontier commander whose current wiki profile is bounded to the 73 CE anti-[[NorthernXiongnuLateHan|Northern Xiongnu]] campaign in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-741-weishenme-gudai-xiongnu-zongyao-ruqin-zhongyuan-lllpgozgi6yd5pscw8z5ofidk5rp|Hanji 741]]. He is remembered less for battlefield success than for a failed route, formal punishment, shame-driven deathbed instruction, and posthumous frontier respect.

## Current Profile

Hanji 741 places Zhai Rong inside [[EmperorMingOfHan|Mingdi]]'s four-route 73 CE campaign. Zhai and Wu Tang lead a force from the high gate route with Southern Xiongnu Left Worthy King Xin. Xin reportedly points to a hill as Zhuoye Mountain, but Zhai and Wu cannot find the Northern Xiongnu main force and return without result.

The court treats the failure seriously. Zhai and Wu are both imprisoned and dismissed, and Zhai is later released only after losing his official post. The episode then shifts from formal judgment to reputation: Zhai is ashamed enough to become ill, cough blood, and die. Before death, he tells his son not to accept the rewards and support that Mingdi had given him, and instead to return them to the state and serve in the army.

The same source says Mingdi had already recognized Zhai's talent and intended to use him again, while Wuhuan and Xianbei envoys, Liaodong officials, and local people mourn him and build commemorative worship. Zhai's durable profile is therefore an honor-pressure case: a failed campaign can end a post, but remembered frontier service can outlast that sentence.

## Key Characteristics

- Frontier commander in Mingdi's four-route 73 CE anti-Northern-Xiongnu campaign.
- Failed-route figure whose column returns without finding the enemy after relying on Southern Xiongnu route guidance.
- Punished official who is imprisoned, dismissed, and released without office.
- Shame-bound commander whose deathbed instruction asks his son to return imperial support and serve militarily.
- Reputation case whose talent is still recognized by Mingdi and whose memory draws frontier mourning.
