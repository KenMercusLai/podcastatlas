# Beth Shapiro

## Overview
Beth Shapiro is an evolutionary biologist represented in the wiki through a [[HubermanLab]] interview on ancient DNA, [[FunctionalDeExtinction]], and genetic tools for conservation.

## Current Profile
Shapiro's account joins evolutionary history to applied biotechnology. She uses ancient DNA and comparative genomics to reconstruct extinct lineages, while emphasizing that an engineered animal cannot be reduced to a recovered sequence: development, epigenetics, microbiome, behavior, present pathogens, habitat, and social organization also shape what the animal becomes.

Her practical position is interventionist but staged. She treats conservation as an already active human practice, argues that declining species may not adapt quickly enough without assistance, and counts inaction as a consequential choice. At the same time, she calls for welfare safeguards, regulatory review, long-term observation, ecological planning, and participation by local communities and long-term stewards.

## Key Characteristics
- Evolutionary biologist working across ancient DNA, comparative genomics, and conservation biotechnology.
- Defines de-extinction by selected traits and ecological function rather than exact genomic identity.
- Connects extinct-species research to genetic rescue of threatened living populations.
- Treats intervention and inaction as competing risk-bearing choices.
- Advocates staged experiments, animal-welfare safeguards, regulation, monitoring, and community participation.
