## Overview

《圣主得贤臣颂》 enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-433-xihan-wangbao-de-shengzhu-de-xianchen-song-shuo-le-sha-lmaah4tjg_gsewzb6izhojtommlx|Hanji 433]] as [[WangBaoWesternHan|王褒]]'s ode written for [[EmperorXuanOfHan|汉宣帝]]. The episode reads it less as generic praise and more as a compact theory of [[ShengzhuXianchenMutualRecognition|圣主贤臣互相成就]]: a ruler's achievement depends on worthy ministers, while worthy ministers need a ruler whose trust lets their counsel become action.

## Current Profile

The ode's current wiki role is political-literary. Its analogies make personnel statecraft visible through common experience: dull tools make artisans labor in vain, sharp tools enable fine work, good drivers make powerful horses useful, and the right clothing answers heat or cold. In the episode's reading, those images all support one thesis: talent is state capacity when placed, trusted, and used well.

The ode also elevates the relationship emotionally and cosmologically. By invoking "flying dragon," "great person," wind, feathers, whales, and harmonious ruler-minister trust, it turns political employment into a near-ideal meeting of timing, virtue, skill, and mutual recognition.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han court ode attributed in the episode to [[WangBaoWesternHan|王褒]] and prompted by [[EmperorXuanOfHan|汉宣帝]].
- Uses practical analogies to argue that good governance requires suitable tools, roles, and people.
- Makes the ruler's active search for talent a precondition of later ease and effective rule.
- Makes the minister's obscurity before recognition a structural problem rather than merely personal failure.
- Presents ideal ruler-minister fit as mutual trust, lack of suspicion, shared timing, and amplified public order.
