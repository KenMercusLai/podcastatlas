## Overview

田邝 / Tian Kuang enters the wiki as a complicated late-[[XinDynasty|新朝]] official rather than a clean hero or villain. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-656-zhichang-buhui-shuohua-de-ren-hen-nan-hunchutou-lpn15smz7r1glekugzwtm2tpw-tl|Hanji 656]] shows him winning reward by helping [[WangMang|王莽]] extract more tax from stressed civilians; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-659-youxie-hexin-rencai-buyao-qingyi-dong-lg1nzal53ihdw-krici2thek-ij|Hanji 659]] later shows him briefly making a collapsing local field governable before Wang Mang removes him.

## Current Profile

Hanji 656 supplies the earlier fiscal branch. Tian Kuang reports that local property estimates contain major concealment, meaning the state is collecting too little. Wang Mang accepts the report, imposes another one-thirtieth levy, promotes Tian Kuang to a noble rank, and rewards him with money. The result is not social repair: Qingzhou and Xuzhou civilians curse Tian Kuang, flee, die on the road, or join bandit groups under tax and labor pressure. This makes Tian Kuang part of [[FiscalExtractionRebellionSpiral|财政压榨叛乱螺旋]] before his later command episode.

Hanji 659 then presents Tian Kuang as an emergency field actor rather than a normal court official. When local order is threatened, he calls up adult men, distributes stored weapons, and publishes military rules openly. The action violates central control, but it works: armed groups avoid Yiping, and Wang Mang temporarily tolerates the offense because Tian Kuang promises results.

His memorial gives the page's most durable content. Tian Kuang argues that small rebel groups could initially have been handled by local order if counties had reported honestly and responded early. Instead, counties deceived commanderies, commanderies deceived the court, commanders arrived late and ineffectively, officials spent their time answering envoys, and hungry civilians lost trust in surrender offers.

Wang Mang's later replacement of Tian Kuang turns competence into danger. Hanji 659 says Wang Mang fears Tian Kuang's ability and merit, secretly sends an imperial edict to replace him, and recalls him to Chang'an as palace staff. The result is renewed disorder in Qi, making Tian Kuang the episode's key case of [[KeyOperatorRemovalRisk|关键执行者移除风险]].

## Key Characteristics

- Fiscal informant whose undercount report helps trigger another levy and earns personal reward.
- Ambiguous crisis actor whose advice can worsen social burden in one episode and improve field control in another.
- Emergency local commander who acts before permission when the formal command system is too slow.
- Practical stabilizer whose mobilization and public rules deter rebel entry into Yiping.
- Field diagnostician who identifies underreporting, official delay, failed command, and surrender distrust as systemic causes.
- Planner who favors local defense, food storage, movement of vulnerable civilians, clear rewards and punishments, and fewer disruptive envoys.
- Politically vulnerable competent actor removed because his merit threatens Wang Mang.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 656 and Hanji 659. The two source notes create a more mixed profile: Tian Kuang can be read as socially harmful fiscal informer in the earlier tax branch and as genuinely useful field stabilizer in the later command branch. The page does not reconstruct his full office history, later career, exact geography of Yiping, Qingzhou, Xuzhou, and Qi, inner motives, exact campaign record, or troop numbers.
