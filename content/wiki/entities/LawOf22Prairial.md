# Law of 22 Prairial

## Overview

The Law of 22 Prairial was a June 1794 measure that compressed Revolutionary Tribunal procedure, greatly broadened political offences, and made death the only guilty sentence.

## Current Profile

Introduced by [[GeorgesCouthon|Georges Couthon]] on 10 June and presented by the sources as largely written by [[MaximilienRobespierre|Robespierre]], the law removed defence counsel and ordinary evidentiary testimony. Jurors could convict through inner conviction, while the category “enemies of the people” absorbed criticism, false news, moral insult, official abuse, interference with food supply, and broadly hostile conduct. Because acquittal or death became the available outcomes, the law accelerated executions during the Great Terror and anticipatory fear among deputies. Its repeal after the [[ThermidorianCoup|Thermidorian coup]] made it an early target in dismantling Robespierrist emergency government.

## Key Characteristics

- It removed defence counsel for accused persons.
- It displaced testimony and ordinary evidence with jurors' inner conviction.
- It broadened enemy categories across speech, morality, office, supply, and conduct.
- It made death the only sentence after conviction.
- Its combination of vague liability and Robespierre's unnamed warnings made insiders unable to infer safety.
- Its execution surge and post-Thermidor repeal connected procedural design directly to political backlash.

## Evidence

### Procedural compression

- [[711-the-terror-killing-god-part-5-glt6491220072]] records the removal of counsel and evidentiary testimony and the substitution of juror conviction.

### Broad liability and political effect

- [[711-the-terror-killing-god-part-5-glt6491220072]] lists the expanded offence categories, death-only sentencing, accelerating executions, and shared fear among threatened deputies.
- [[712-the-terror-the-fall-of-robespierre-part-6-glt1154404105]] estimates about 1,400 executions during the following seven weeks and identifies repeal as part of the immediate Thermidorian dismantling of terror.

## Qualifications

- The episode's account of drafting responsibility is source-scoped and does not substitute for a full legislative history.
- A broad law does not prove that every listed category was enforced identically or that every later execution followed the same factual pattern.
- Fear around the law contributed to coalition formation but was not the only cause of Robespierre's fall.
- The execution total, causal share of the law, and timing and effect of repeal remain source-scoped rather than audited legal findings.

## What Changed

- Extended the law from procedural design and coalition pressure through the Great Terror execution surge and post-Thermidor repeal.
