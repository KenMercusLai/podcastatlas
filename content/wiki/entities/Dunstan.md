# Dunstan

## Overview

Dunstan appears as the Archbishop of Canterbury who likely shaped [[EdgarThePeaceful|Edgar's]] 973 coronation and helped establish the ecclesiastical pattern inherited by later English coronations.

## Current Profile

The episode gives Dunstan both liturgical and political importance. Edgar's delayed coronation at Bath staged a consolidated kingdom through Pentecost, Roman-imperial language, oath, prostration, crowning, and the presence of bishops; Dunstan is presented as the principal designer or organizer of that synthesis.

His role also becomes institutional precedent. The ceremony helps explain why the Archbishop of Canterbury normally crowns later English and British monarchs, although the episode acknowledges exceptions. Dunstan's reported tears during Edgar's prostration reinforce the rite's theological claim that royal elevation begins with humility and service before God.

## Key Characteristics

- Archbishop associated with the design of Edgar's 973 coronation order.
- Churchman who joined English state-building to Christian consecration and inherited imperial form.
- Precedent-setter for the Archbishop of Canterbury's normal coronation role.
- Interpreter of kingship as oath-bound service rather than power without restraint.

## Evidence

- Liturgical design: [[327-coronations-the-deep-history-part-1-glt4746607803]] attributes the likely shaping of Edgar's Bath ceremony to Dunstan.
- Sacred-political synthesis: [[327-coronations-the-deep-history-part-1-glt4746607803]] links Pentecost, prostration, oath, crown, bishops, and imperial language in the rite he supervised.
- Institutional precedent: [[327-coronations-the-deep-history-part-1-glt4746607803]] connects Dunstan's role to the later norm that Canterbury crowns the monarch.
- Theology of service: [[327-coronations-the-deep-history-part-1-glt4746607803]] reports his tears as Edgar humbled himself before the crown on the altar.
