# Pentagon Papers

## Overview

The Pentagon Papers were a classified documentary history of U.S. involvement in Vietnam whose disclosure by [[DanielEllsberg]] exposed discrepancies between official claims and prior policy-making.

## Current Profile

In this episode, the papers matter less as a complete history of Vietnam than as the trigger for the Nixon administration's leak-control response. The documents primarily concerned earlier administrations, yet [[RichardNixon|Nixon]] and his aides treated their release as evidence of a wider threat from insiders and the press. The resulting [[WhiteHousePlumbers]] used burglary against Ellsberg, establishing personnel and methods that fed into the [[WatergateScandal]].

## Key Characteristics

- Large classified history of U.S. Vietnam decision-making.
- Disclosed to demonstrate a pattern of official deception about the war.
- Concerned earlier administrations more directly than Nixon's own initial decisions.
- Intensified White House fear of leaks and unauthorized disclosure.
- Prompted an illegal response that prefigured Watergate operations.

## Evidence

### Leak and White House response

- [[106-watergate-part-1-glt6675580126]] connects Ellsberg's disclosure to the creation of the plumbers and their break-in at his psychiatrist's office.

## Qualifications

The episode does not reconstruct the papers' contents, publication sequence, litigation, or full effect on Vietnam debate. Its emphasis is causal and political: how the Nixon White House reacted to the leak.

## What Changed

- Established the papers as the disclosure event connecting Vietnam secrecy to Watergate's illegal leak-control culture.
