## Overview

管霸 / Guan Ba is a late-Han court figure whose current wiki profile now starts with a 159 CE judicial role in the [[LiYunLateHan|李云]] and [[DuZhongLateHan|杜仲]] case, then includes property privilege under [[EmperorHuanOfHan|汉桓帝]] and later execution in [[EmpressDowagerDouMiao|窦妙 / 窦皇太后]]'s limited anti-eunuch purge. The earlier layers make his later punishment more concrete: before being singled out as powerful and capable, he is already shown inside judgment, property, and palace-protection fields.

## Current Profile

Guan Ba's current profile is no longer only an execution note. In the 159 CE layer, he serves as commandant of justice in the Li Yun and Du Zhong case. After interrogation and torture, he reports the sentence and still tries a narrowing mitigation: Li Yun is a rural scholar, Du Zhong is a low official, and their words should not be treated as grounds for severe punishment. Huan rejects the appeal and Li Yun and Du Zhong die in prison, so Guan Ba appears first as a failed institutional mitigator inside a closed punishment decision.

In the 165 CE layer, he and [[SuKangLateHan|苏康]] obtain good land and residences cheaply, and [[LiuYouLateHan|刘祐]] orders confiscation. The court response punishes Liu You, not Guan Ba, which makes Guan Ba a concrete beneficiary of the same eunuch-protection field that later reformers try to cut back.

In the 168 CE layer, Dou Miao's first action against the eunuch field does not begin with the broader targets Cao Jie and Wang Fu; it begins with selective punishment of someone described as especially powerful and strategically able among the zhongchangshi.

The result clarifies the compromise structure of the 168 CE decision window. Guan Ba and [[SuKangLateHan|苏康]] are arrested and executed, but the source does not show this as a completed removal of the palace proximity network around Dou Miao.

## Key Characteristics

- Guan Ba first appears here as commandant of justice trying and failing to mitigate Li Yun and Du Zhong's punishment.
- Guan Ba is later a powerful zhongchangshi whose privilege includes underpriced property acquisition before his execution.
- Liu You's failed confiscation order shows Guan Ba's property position had palace protection.
- Guan Ba is singled out in the first purge step after the later reform coalition gains authority.
- The source describes him as both influential and capable, making him more than a generic eunuch name.
- His execution represents selective punishment rather than acceptance of a blanket eunuch purge.

## Qualifications

The current sources do not supply Guan Ba's full biography, family network, complete office sequence, or role after execution. Hanji 850 presents him as commandant of justice, while later pages present him in the eunuch-power field; this page records the sequence without filling the institutional gap between those notices. His profile should remain centered on failed judicial mitigation, property privilege, palace protection, and selective purge status. Transcript 管爸 is normalized to 管霸 based on the late-Han context.

## What Changed

- The profile now starts with Guan Ba's 159 CE commandant-of-justice role and failed mitigation attempt in the Li Yun and Du Zhong case.
- The profile now includes Guan Ba's earlier property-acquisition privilege and Liu You's failed confiscation order.
- The later execution remains selective punishment rather than proof that the broader palace-access field was disabled.
