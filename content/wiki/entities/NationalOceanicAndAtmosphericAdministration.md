# National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration

## Overview

The National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) is a U.S. federal agency whose weather and fish-stock data support marine safety and fisheries management. The source uses Alaska to show how scientific capacity becomes a direct local political issue.

## Current Profile

In the episode's Alaska segment, fishermen, scientists, and officials rely on NOAA information to monitor weather, assess stocks, and set quotas. Reported federal workforce cuts therefore matter not only as an administrative change but as a possible reduction in the reliability of safety and resource-management decisions.

## Key Characteristics

- Provides weather information used by fishermen operating in hazardous conditions.
- Supplies evidence used to track fish stocks and inform catch quotas.
- Connects federal scientific capacity to local livelihoods and public safety.
- Becomes politically salient when staffing or service reductions threaten data quality.

- Weather and safety: [[bf8d29dbc7b83c7daefff88e8666657c]] reports complaints about insufficient weather updates and explains fishermen's reliance on federal data.

## Qualifications

The source does not provide NOAA staffing records, service metrics, forecast audits, or causal evidence that a particular cut produced a particular failure. The workforce and reliability claims remain episode-attributed concerns.

## What Changed

- Added NOAA as the institutional bridge between federal capacity and Alaska fishing safety.
- Connected weather services and stock assessment to the same local political dispute.
- Preserved reduced reliability as a reported concern rather than an established measured outcome.
