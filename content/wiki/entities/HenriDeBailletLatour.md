# Henri de Baillet-Latour

## Overview

Henri de Baillet-Latour is presented as president of the [[InternationalOlympicCommittee]] during the dispute over Jewish exclusion and American participation in the 1936 Berlin Olympics.

## Current Profile

[[hitler-s-olympics-part-3-mustache-to-mustache-hitler-s-olympics-part-3-mustache-to-mustache|Hitler’s Olympics, Part 3]] places Baillet-Latour inside the patrician Olympic world shared by [[CharlesHitchcockSherrill]]. He reportedly expressed personal dislike of Jews while maintaining a formal boundary that Jewish athletes could not be excluded from Olympic competition.

After Sherrill's inconclusive meeting with [[AdolfHitler]], Sherrill warned Baillet-Latour that the earlier Vienna assurances might be collapsing and urged intervention. The episode therefore uses Baillet-Latour to show how prejudiced officials could defend a narrow procedural minimum without challenging the wider system of persecution surrounding the Games.

## Key Characteristics

- IOC president during the Berlin participation dispute.
- Member of the elite social and institutional world shaping Olympic diplomacy.
- Personally prejudiced official who nevertheless defended formal Jewish eligibility.
- Recipient of Sherrill's warning that German assurances might fail.
- Representative of the gap between narrow sporting procedure and wider political equality.

- Prejudice and procedural line: [[hitler-s-olympics-part-3-mustache-to-mustache-hitler-s-olympics-part-3-mustache-to-mustache]] reports both Baillet-Latour's dislike of Jews and his view that they could not be barred from Olympic competition.
- Diplomatic role: [[hitler-s-olympics-part-3-mustache-to-mustache-hitler-s-olympics-part-3-mustache-to-mustache]] describes Sherrill urging him to intervene after the Hitler meeting.

## Qualifications

This page is limited to one episode summary and does not provide a complete presidency, correspondence record, or account of Baillet-Latour's authority over German selection. The distinction between personal prejudice and formal eligibility should not be mistaken for proof that the IOC secured equal preparation, selection, or safety.
