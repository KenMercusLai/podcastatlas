# Seinfeld

## Overview

*Seinfeld* is the sitcom created by [[JerrySeinfeld|Jerry Seinfeld]] and [[LarryDavid|Larry David]] that the current source examines as both a creative work and a long-lived media asset.

## Current Profile

The series built comedy from minor frustrations, awkward social rules, self-interested characters, and four plots that often converged. Its refusal of sentimental learning made it abrasive by conventional sitcom standards but also kept episodes largely self-contained. [[NBC]] supplied patient early support and later mass Thursday-night distribution, while [[CastleRockEntertainment|Castle Rock]] and related rights arrangements enabled backend value beyond first-run broadcasting.

The completed catalog remained commercially useful through local syndication, cable, digital sales, and streaming. The source's exact lifetime figures are estimates, but its stronger conclusion is that creative consistency, accessibility, ubiquity, and cultural familiarity made the library repeatedly licensable after production ended.

## Key Characteristics

- Observational comedy grounded in small social frictions rather than conventional family or workplace plots.
- Permanently flawed central characters governed by “no hugging, no learning.”
- Dense construction in which separate character stories often converge.
- Largely self-contained episodes that can be watched out of order.
- Long commercial afterlife across syndication, cable, digital, and streaming windows.
