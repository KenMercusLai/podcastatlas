## Overview

刘肥 / Liu Fei is [[LiuBang|刘邦]]'s eldest son and the first early-Han Qi king in this wiki's evidence. His large eastern grant supplies both a regional military resource during Liu Bang's reign and the dynastic-territorial foundation later inherited by [[LiuXiangQiKingWesternHan|刘襄]].

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-174-liubang-weihe-dafeng-zijide-chouren-1-lnzzvpieqtgatmvgogz3msyhs60u|Hanji 174]] places Liu Fei in Liu Bang's founding settlement. Because the emperor has few adult close male relatives available, Qi is assigned to his eldest son as part of a wider conversion from dangerous different-surname regional power to Liu-family kingship. The source describes the grant broadly through the population speaking the Qi language.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-188-gudai-zhongguo-de-tianxia-wushui-xian-lnoib0bvz8w0ihvhxmtx19tr4jdz|Hanji 188]] makes the kingdom operational. Liu Bang waits behind fortifications against [[YingBu|英布 / 黥布]] until Liu Fei and [[CaoShen|曹参]] arrive with Qi forces; their reinforcement enables the coordinated attack rather than a premature personal confrontation.

The succession evidence identifies Liu Fei as Liu Bang's eldest son, assigns him seventy-three cities at age twenty-one, and says he rules thirteen years before Liu Xiang succeeds. These numbers remain episode-scoped, but the larger synthesis is stable: Liu Fei's grant created a powerful hereditary base whose later territorial reduction becomes part of Liu Xiang's grievance and mobilization capacity. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-205-nvhuangdi-lvzhi-jingran-yin-gou-er-si-2-ltgwfdhn9cdwphudy0blyh36fge1|Hanji 205 part 2]]

This page is intentionally named `LiuFeiQiKing` rather than `LiuFei`: the wiki already has [[LiuFei|刘飞]], a modern podcast host with a different Chinese character and subject.

## Key Characteristics

- Eldest son of Liu Bang in the supplied source set.
- First Liu-family ruler of the large early-Han Qi grant.
- Regional reinforcement provider in the final campaign against Ying Bu.
- Father and predecessor of Liu Xiang.
- Dynastic founder of the Qi branch later active in the post-Lü succession crisis.

## Evidence

Founding grant and regional settlement:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-174-liubang-weihe-dafeng-zijide-chouren-1-lnzzvpieqtgatmvgogz3msyhs60u|Hanji 174]] assigns Qi to Liu Fei as part of Liu Bang's same-surname royal settlement.

Military support:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-188-gudai-zhongguo-de-tianxia-wushui-xian-lnoib0bvz8w0ihvhxmtx19tr4jdz|Hanji 188]] says Liu Fei and Cao Shen bring Qi forces before Liu Bang orders the multi-sided attack on Ying Bu.

Succession and inherited capacity:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-205-nvhuangdi-lvzhi-jingran-yin-gou-er-si-2-ltgwfdhn9cdwphudy0blyh36fge1|Hanji 205 part 2]] supplies the eldest-son claim, age, city count, reign length, succession by Liu Xiang, and later reduction of Qi's territory.

## Qualifications

The page synthesizes three structured podcast notes rather than a full biography. Liu Fei's maternal status, exact age, seventy-three-city count, language-based border description, reign chronology, military command, and the legal-administrative reality of Qi require comparison with transmitted histories. Hanji 205 part 2 announces source problems without resolving them, and later territorial grievance should not be projected backward as Liu Fei's own political position.
