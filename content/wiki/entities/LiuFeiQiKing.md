## Overview

刘肥 / Liu Fei is [[LiuBang|刘邦]]'s eldest son and the first early-Han Qi king in this wiki's evidence. His profile joins three roles: recipient of a large same-surname royal grant, provider of regional military support, and threatened court prince who reportedly trades territory and rank for survival under [[LuZhi|吕雉]].

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-174-liubang-weihe-dafeng-zijide-chouren-1-lnzzvpieqtgatmvgogz3msyhs60u|Hanji 174]] places Liu Fei in Liu Bang's founding settlement. Because the emperor has few adult close male relatives available, the broad Qi-speaking region is assigned to his eldest son as part of the conversion from dangerous different-surname regional power to Liu-family kingship. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-188-gudai-zhongguo-de-tianxia-wushui-xian-lnoib0bvz8w0ihvhxmtx19tr4jdz|Hanji 188]] then makes that kingdom operational: Liu Bang waits against [[YingBu|英布 / 黥布]] until Liu Fei and [[CaoShen|曹参]] arrive with Qi forces, after which a coordinated Han attack becomes possible.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-195-lvhou-zhangquan-liubang-zhangzi-jimang-renmei-wei-niang-lokajlf0bhft1b7fo-hwazyqretn|Hanji 195]] changes the profile from capacity to vulnerability. At a Chang'an banquet, [[LiuYing|汉惠帝刘盈]] reportedly seats his elder half-brother in the honored place; Lu Zhi responds with poisoned wine, and Liu Fei escapes after Liu Ying's intervention reveals the danger. Liu Fei then gives [[ChengyangCommandery|城阳郡]] to [[LuYuanPrincess|鲁元公主]] as a [[TangmuYi|汤沐邑]] and honors his younger half-sister as queen mother. The episode reads this as [[StatusReversalSurvivalBargain|名分倒置式求生]]: a powerful regional king makes himself visibly subordinate and pays a territorial price to survive.

The succession evidence identifies Liu Fei as Liu Bang's eldest son, assigns him seventy-three cities at age twenty-one, and says he rules thirteen years before [[LiuXiangQiKingWesternHan|刘襄]] succeeds. These numbers remain episode-scoped, but the joined synthesis is stable: Liu Fei's large grant creates a hereditary base, while his court survival shows that territorial capacity under same-surname kingship does not remove dependence on the palace center. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-205-nvhuangdi-lvzhi-jingran-yin-gou-er-si-2-ltgwfdhn9cdwphudy0blyh36fge1|Hanji 205 part 2]]

This page is intentionally named `LiuFeiQiKing` rather than `LiuFei`: the wiki already has [[LiuFei|刘飞]], a modern podcast host with a different Chinese character and subject.

## Key Characteristics

- Eldest son of Liu Bang in the supplied source set.
- First Liu-family ruler of the large early-Han Qi grant.
- Regional reinforcement provider in the final campaign against Ying Bu.
- Court-vulnerable prince whose kingdom does not shield him from the central household hierarchy.
- Territorial and symbolic concession-maker in the reported settlement with Lu Zhi and Princess Lu Yuan.
- Father and predecessor of Liu Xiang, whose later mobilization depends on the Qi branch's inherited base.

## Qualifications

The page synthesizes four structured podcast notes rather than a full biography. Liu Fei's maternal status, exact age, seventy-three-city count, language-based border description, reign chronology, military command, and the legal-administrative reality of Qi require comparison with transmitted histories. Hanji 195 does not independently establish poisoned wine, Lu Zhi's motive, Liu Ying's knowledge, the ceremony, or the exact reason Liu Fei was released; its “recognizing his sister as mother” language describes a queen-mother title rather than biological kinship. Hanji 205 part 2 announces source problems without resolving them, and later territorial grievance should not be projected backward as Liu Fei's own political position.
