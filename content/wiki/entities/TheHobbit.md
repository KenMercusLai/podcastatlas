# The Hobbit

## Overview

*The Hobbit* is [[JRRTolkien|J.R.R. Tolkien]]'s children's quest story about Bilbo Baggins leaving domestic comfort for danger, fellowship, treasure, and a permanently altered return, written after decades of language-first legendarium development.

## Current Profile

[[225-j-r-r-tolkien-glt9725776786]] places the book's famous opening around 1930, when Tolkien wrote “In a hole in the ground, there lived a hobbit” while marking examination papers. The apparently sudden sentence arrived after long work on languages, names, Earendel, and early legendarium stories such as [[TheFallOfGondolin|The Fall of Gondolin]], so it marks a new entry point into an older invented world rather than creation from nothing.

[[226-the-lord-of-the-rings-glt1788342017]] presents the book's distinctive force as a collision between the modern and archaic. Bilbo resembles a respectable interwar Englishman—attached to tea, clocks, gardens, routine, and noninvolvement—yet travels through a world built from northern myth, Anglo-Saxon poetry, dragons, dwarves, mountains, maps, keys, and lost kingdoms.

The episode treats the work as a durable children's adventure in its own right, not merely a prelude to [[TheLordOfTheRings]]. Its changed homecoming supplies an early version of the later epic's stronger argument that experience and fellowship make a complete return to one's former self impossible.

## Key Characteristics

- Children's quest narrative centered on Bilbo's reluctant departure and changed return.
- Book whose famous first sentence opened a new narrative path into a pre-existing legendarium.
- Modern domestic protagonist placed inside an archaic mythic environment.
- Layered use of northern myth, philology, dragons, maps, keys, and lost kingdoms.
- Independent literary appeal as well as narrative foundation for *The Lord of the Rings*.

## Evidence

- Origin and chronology: [[225-j-r-r-tolkien-glt9725776786]] connects the examination-paper sentence around 1930 to Tolkien's earlier language and legendarium work.
- Modern-archaic contrast: [[226-the-lord-of-the-rings-glt1788342017]] compares Bilbo's habits with interwar English respectability and northern legendary material.
- Quest construction: [[226-the-lord-of-the-rings-glt1788342017]] follows Gandalf, the dwarves, Gollum, Mirkwood, spiders, the dragon's mountain, and the return home.
- Literary status: [[226-the-lord-of-the-rings-glt1788342017]] argues that the book would remain loved even without its sequel.

## Qualifications

The sources offer broad historical interpretation rather than a close textual or publication study. The remembered origin sentence does not explain the book's full composition or revision history. The “modern hobbit” frame is illuminating but does not exhaust Bilbo or the book's humor, and the list of mythic and imperial-adventure ingredients does not establish a one-to-one source for every element.

## What Changed

- Added the examination-paper origin sentence and its place after years of legendarium development.
- Distinguished a new narrative entry point from creation of the wider world from nothing.
