# 支付宝 / Alipay

## Overview
Alipay / 支付宝 is the Ant Group app and service network that [[273-guangwan-waitan-dahui-faxian-mayi-zhaodaole-xin-weizhi-lotxogfoqqwcjigxihtixvncbfc3]] treats as the base for Ant's agent-era repositioning.

## Current Profile
The sources present Alipay less as a standalone payment wallet than as a service graph containing payment, orders, bills, mini-programs, insurance, medical access, merchant touchpoints, and other daily-life workflows. That makes it a plausible base for [[AntAbao|阿宝]], [[AntAfu|阿福]], [[AlipayTapNetwork|碰一下]], and [[AgentPaymentInfrastructure|AI payment]]. The roundtable adds a trust-provider role: authorization, identity, anti-fraud, audit, and compensation can make an agent purchase feel bounded even when the user does not approve every mechanical step.

## Key Characteristics
- Serves as Ant Group's main consumer-facing app and daily-life service shelf.
- Provides payment, order, bill, mini-program, and account context that can support agentic task routing.
- Anchors Ant's move from traffic competition toward intent recognition and fulfillment.
- Connects AI payment to established trust, risk-control, and dispute-handling infrastructure.
- Extends into health and offline merchant scenes through 阿福 and 碰一下.
- Provides a possible recourse and risk-control backstop for progressively larger delegated payments.

## Evidence
- Service-shelf evidence: [[273-guangwan-waitan-dahui-faxian-mayi-zhaodaole-xin-weizhi-lotxogfoqqwcjigxihtixvncbfc3]] describes 阿宝 as Alipay's existing service shelf made more agentic through natural-language intent.
- Trust evidence: [[273-guangwan-waitan-dahui-faxian-mayi-zhaodaole-xin-weizhi-lotxogfoqqwcjigxihtixvncbfc3]] links AI payment to Alipay's accumulated payment, risk-control, authorization, refund, and liability boundary experience.
- Offline evidence: [[273-guangwan-waitan-dahui-faxian-mayi-zhaodaole-xin-weizhi-lotxogfoqqwcjigxihtixvncbfc3]] says 碰一下 gives Alipay a dense offline interaction network that can support merchant-facing intelligence.
- Delegated-payment evidence: [[waitan-dahui-xianxia-yuanzhuo-gan-ba-qianbao-jiaogei-ai-ma-liaoliao-agent-jiaoyi-baofa-qianye-de-xinren-jijian-78ec2d74-0ad7-4c6f-9764-728eec0e6e87]] records one panelist accepting a 1,000-yuan delegated amount if Alipay provides a backstop, while Han Xinyi emphasizes compensation after safe-execution controls.

## Qualifications
The sources are strategic discussions rather than adoption studies. They do not prove that users will prefer Alipay as a personal-agent entrance or that compensation alone resolves misranking, privacy, merchant, and authorization risks, especially when discovery begins on other platforms.

## What Changed
- Added Alipay's proposed role as a risk-control and recourse backstop for delegated agent payments.
