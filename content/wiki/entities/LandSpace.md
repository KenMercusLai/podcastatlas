# LandSpace / 蓝箭航天

[[zhongting-zhuque-sanhao-fuzongshi-huojian-huishou-zhiyou-cheng-yu-bai-meiyou-zhongjiantai-s9e41-d9e11e3b-7c7e-4beb-b19f-6f90fff38714]] adds [[DongKai|董凯]]'s internal engineering account of LandSpace's reusable-rocket strategy. The episode frames the company's goal as large payload, high launch frequency, and lower cost, with reuse as a capacity multiplier rather than a substitute for manufacturing capacity.

Dong's account also gives the company a more operational profile: LandSpace prepared around a 2026 target of ten rockets and roughly one hundred engines, while still treating public safety and regulator scrutiny as hard boundaries. The episode's long-term branch points from [[Zhuque3]] toward a larger fully reusable rocket, a 220-tonne full-flow engine, more launch positions, more recovery directions, and possible [[Starship]]-style catch architecture at superheavy scale.

[[lanjian-hangtian-wancheng-zhongguo-shouci-ludi-huojian-huishou-yushu-keji-shizhi-chaoguo-3000-yi-1007302506]] adds a reported milestone after the earlier route-comparison source. The episode says LandSpace's [[Zhuque3]] Yao-2 launched at 7:35 a.m. on August 19, 2026 and its first stage landed in [[Gansu]] roughly eight minutes later, making LandSpace the first Chinese private company in the source's account to complete land recovery of a carrier rocket.

The same update frames the landing as a financing signal: the source says LandSpace was preparing a listing and planned to raise 7.5 billion RMB for R&D and reusable launch-service expansion. The wiki keeps this as a source-dated capital-market and technical-validation claim.

LandSpace, or 蓝箭航天, appears in [[ruhe-douzhu-yike-huojian-s10e21-b66fdf0f-d428-4f0c-8412-b0c7581132d0]] as one of the Chinese private space companies working toward reusable rockets. The episode discusses its [[Zhuque3]] program as a commercial-space comparison to the state-led [[LongMarch10B]] sea-net recovery.

The source does not treat LandSpace as following the same route as Long March 10B. It uses the company to distinguish private rocket economics and risk tolerance from resource-concentrated, multi-institute recovery systems such as [[SeaNetRocketRecovery]].
