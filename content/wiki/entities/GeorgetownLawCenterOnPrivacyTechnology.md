# Georgetown Law Center on Privacy and Technology

## Overview
The Georgetown Law Center on Privacy and Technology appears in [[tech-20260831-0831-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260831-0831-mp-tech-pod-128]] as the organization behind an analysis of DHS DNA collection from detainees.

## Current Profile
In this wiki branch, the center functions as the research and legal-analysis source connecting [[USDepartmentOfHomelandSecurity|DHS]] and [[USImmigrationAndCustomsEnforcement|ICE]] collection practices to [[CODIS]], [[CivilLibertiesSurveillanceRisk]], and [[CompelledDNASamplingFourthAmendment]]. Its analysis supplies the episode's scale figures, retention concern, child-sampling concern, and distinction from [[MarylandVKing|Maryland v. King]].

## Key Characteristics
- Privacy and technology legal center used as the episode's main evidentiary source.
- Analyst of DHS DNA collection scale and CODIS growth after the 2020 policy change.
- Civil-liberties critic of turning immigration detention into criminal DNA database infrastructure.
- Observer of early litigation over DHS DNA collection through [[BriggsVMullen|Briggs v. Mullen]].

## Evidence
- Scale analysis - [[tech-20260831-0831-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260831-0831-mp-tech-pod-128]] attributes the nearly one million last-year collection figure and 3.3 million CODIS additions to Georgetown's analysis.
- Legal interpretation - [[tech-20260831-0831-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260831-0831-mp-tech-pod-128]] presents the center's view that immigration-detainee sampling lacks the probable-cause footing of criminal-arrestee DNA collection.
- Litigation watch - [[tech-20260831-0831-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260831-0831-mp-tech-pod-128]] says the center is watching Briggs v. Mullen as an early challenge.

## Qualifications
The episode relies heavily on Georgetown's analysis and does not include the underlying report text or a detailed agency rebuttal. Figures, projections, and legal conclusions should therefore stay attributed to this source until corroborated by later material.
