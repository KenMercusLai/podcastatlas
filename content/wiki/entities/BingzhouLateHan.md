## Overview

并州 / Bingzhou (Late Han) is a northern province whose current wiki profile moves from exposed frontier administration under Emperor Ling to Gao Gan's rebellion and Liang Xi's coercive Cao-side consolidation.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-898-renzei-zuofu-kelian-zhiren-neng-you-duo-huangtang-lgfup6tyv85zhvnmzf6xlesmblcj|Hanji 898]] first marks Bingzhou through a [[Xianbei|鲜卑]] attack during [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]]'s reign. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-939-donghan-luanju-de-fengkuang-sanguo-xuqu-lthmvw8ihk96fuez9vamlryzcngy|Hanji 939]] deepens the crisis: inspector [[ZhangYiLateHan|张懿]] is killed by the [[TugeHu|屠各胡]], while Baibo and Southern Xiongnu disturbances expose the weakness of provincial authority and help make stronger provincial commands politically plausible.

The post-Yuan settlement changes the province from an exposed rebellion field into a stronger Cao-side administrative presence. After [[GaoGanLateHan|高干]] loses [[HuguanLateHan|壶关]] and dies in flight, [[CaoCao|曹操]] appoints [[LiangXiLateHan|梁习]] with civil and military authority. Liang uses courtesy and office, troop dispersal, family relocation to [[YeLateHan|邺城]], and force to break local armed networks. The resulting stabilization is inseparable from coercion and population control, as recorded in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1073-huguan-zhi-zhan-caocao-de-tucheng-jihua-haican-le-shui-lspli7p63ouktn6k0zbgvggj59di|Hanji 1073]].

## Key Characteristics

- Northern frontier province exposed to Xianbei, Tuge Hu, Baibo, and Southern Xiongnu pressure.
- Provincial office is visibly unsafe during the late Emperor Ling crisis.
- Becomes Gao Gan's rebellion base and includes the Shangdang-Huguan route field.
- Requires both civil and military authority in Cao Cao's post-rebellion settlement.
- Local order is rebuilt by decomposing strongman networks rather than by appointment alone.
- Stabilization is credited with taxation, service, agriculture, and law, but includes forced relocation and killing.

## Evidence

- Early frontier pressure: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-898-renzei-zuofu-kelian-zhiren-neng-you-duo-huangtang-lgfup6tyv85zhvnmzf6xlesmblcj|Hanji 898]] places an Xianbei attack on Bingzhou inside the wider 171 CE crisis field.
- Provincial office collapse: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-939-donghan-luanju-de-fengkuang-sanguo-xuqu-lthmvw8ihk96fuez9vamlryzcngy|Hanji 939]] says Tuge Hu kill inspector Zhang Yi amid renewed regional disorder.
- Rebellion and succession: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1073-huguan-zhi-zhan-caocao-de-tucheng-jihua-haican-le-shui-lspli7p63ouktn6k0zbgvggj59di|Hanji 1073]] closes Gao Gan's Huguan resistance and places Liang Xi in charge of settlement.
- Consolidation method: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1073-huguan-zhi-zhan-caocao-de-tucheng-jihua-haican-le-shui-lspli7p63ouktn6k0zbgvggj59di|Hanji 1073]] reports strongman appointments, youth recruitment and dispersal, mass family relocation, suppression, and frontier submission.

## Qualifications

This page is a bounded late-Han provincial profile, not a complete geography or social history of Bingzhou. The sources compress distinct frontier peoples and long intervals. Hanji 1073's claims about quick pacification, popular praise, exact migration and casualty totals, and the behavior of every Xiongnu or tribal leader require independent verification; they do not erase the coercive means described in the same episode.
