## Overview

Lu Qianshou / 陆谦受 is presented as an architect associated with modern [[BankOfChina]] buildings in Shanghai, Hong Kong, and Singapore.

## Current Profile

In [[gkwriaiorn5rawbazwtstr0k-gkwriaiorn5rawbazwtstr0k|Vol.278]], Lu's work provides a second path for bank symbolism alongside the [[HSBCLions|HSBC lions]]. The Shanghai Bank of China building combined an Art Deco mass with a glazed-tile roof, Confucius relief, ruyi-cloud motifs, and entrance beasts identified by the cited restoration team as winged bixie rather than ordinary stone lions. The Hong Kong building likewise used abstract mythical beasts before more conventional Chinese lions later replaced those on the harbor side.

The episode therefore uses Lu as evidence that modern bank architecture could express Chinese institutional identity through selective synthesis rather than by attaching one fixed traditional form to every branch.

## Key Characteristics

- Specialized in or repeatedly worked on bank architecture across several Chinese commercial centers.
- Combined modern architectural language with selected Chinese roofs, motifs, reliefs, and mythical animals.
- Treated symbolic form as part of institutional identity rather than as a universal bank template.
- Appears in the episode through building history, not through a full biographical or professional survey.

## Qualifications

The source does not provide a complete attribution record, chronology, project list, or account of collaborators. Its explanation for the Shanghai tower's reduced scale includes silver-market, property-bubble, and Bank of China liquidity pressures while rejecting a single popular obstruction legend; those causal claims remain source-scoped pending architectural and archival evidence.

## What Changed

- Established Lu as the episode's architect linking modern bank design to selective Chinese symbolic expression.
- Distinguished the Bank of China mythical-beast lineage from direct copying of HSBC's reclining lions.
