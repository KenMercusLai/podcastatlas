# Boston Corbett

## Overview

Boston Corbett was a Union soldier and former hatter best known for killing [[JohnWilkesBooth|John Wilkes Booth]], whose self-castration is presented as an act of extreme religious literalism amid possible mercury exposure and mental instability.

## Current Profile

[[45-top-ten-eunuchs-glt5221212176]] distinguishes Corbett from court eunuchs and economically selected castrati. After an encounter with prostitutes, he reportedly used scissors to castrate himself as a response to sexual temptation, then sought medical treatment and continued his life.

The episode connects his intense religion, hat-making exposure, military insubordination, and claim to divine instruction without proving a single medical explanation. His later killing of Booth made him historically famous, but the castration episode belongs to a broader profile of volatile religious conviction rather than public office enabled by eunuch status.

[[591-the-assassination-of-abraham-lincoln-manhunt-for-the-killer-part-2-glt7618197031]] supplies the operational setting. During the Garrett-farm standoff, Corbett saw Booth through the tobacco-barn timbers and fired through the wall, striking Booth in the neck and damaging his spinal column. That account identifies his act and its consequence without settling whether the shot complied with orders or proving that religious conviction caused it.

## Key Characteristics

- Former hatter for whom mercury exposure is raised as a possible mental-health factor.
- Religious literalist who reportedly castrated himself after confronting sexual temptation.
- Union soldier repeatedly in conflict with military authority.
- Union cavalryman who shot Booth through the Garrett barn during the final standoff.

## Evidence

- Self-castration and religion - [[45-top-ten-eunuchs-glt5221212176]] recounts the scissors operation and presents it as an attempt to remove sexual temptation.
- Occupational and behavioral context - [[45-top-ten-eunuchs-glt5221212176]] connects hat-making, possible mercury poisoning, religious intensity, and clashes with command.
- Historical fame - [[45-top-ten-eunuchs-glt5221212176]] identifies Corbett as the soldier who shot Booth.
- Garrett-farm action - [[591-the-assassination-of-abraham-lincoln-manhunt-for-the-killer-part-2-glt7618197031]] places Corbett with the 16th New York Cavalry and describes the shot through the barn wall and Booth's fatal spinal injury.

## Qualifications

Mercury poisoning and mental instability are plausible episode-level interpretations, not established diagnoses here. The sources compress chronology and do not justify reducing Corbett's religion, military conduct, later deterioration, or decision to fire to one cause. The assassination episode describes the fatal shot but does not provide a complete military inquiry or settle compliance with orders.
