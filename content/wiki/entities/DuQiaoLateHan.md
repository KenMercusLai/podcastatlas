## Overview
杜乔 / Du Qiao is the late-Han upright official whose current profile now begins in the 146 CE succession dispute after [[EmperorZhiOfHan]]'s death. He resists [[LiangJiLateHan]]'s move to install [[EmperorHuanOfHan]], later becomes taiwei and criticizes reward inversion, then dies in prison as the [[LiGuLateHan]]-paired target of Liang Ji's persecution.

## Current Profile
[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-828-da-jiangjun-liangji-shanquan-feili-de-donghan-jianchen-llqnqijagtmdlgitulddxspncu0e|Hanji 828]] supplies the earliest current layer. As dahonglu, Du Qiao stands with Li Gu against Liang Ji's effort to install Liu Zhi; when most senior officials submit to Liang Ji's pressure, Du Qiao remains one of the holdouts. After Li Gu is removed, the episode says Du Qiao continues to hold himself upright, foreshadowing later conflict.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-829-junzi-weishenme-doubuguo-xiaoren-lthvnstkfu8n9thx1sz6yezl4yqq|Hanji 829]] supplies the office-and-advice layer. Du Qiao becomes taiwei in 147 CE and memorializes that appointing worthies plus matching rewards and punishments to merit are core state tasks; he criticizes rewards flowing to Liang-family and eunuch actors without merit, and Huan gives no visible answer.

The same source shows why Du Qiao becomes personally dangerous to Liang Ji. After [[EmpressLiangYingLateHan]] is appointed empress, Du Qiao refuses Liang Ji's attempt to use empress-level wedding rites beyond established institutions. He also refuses to recommend a corrupt candidate for shangshu. Liang Ji interprets both refusals as disobedience, and [[TangHengLateHan]] plus [[ZuoGuanLateHan]] later recode Du Qiao and Li Gu's earlier succession stance as personal opposition to Huan.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-830-donghan-ligu-wo-sui-si-yourong-lt-j7n9gotiw-negqxojoacf6ji|Hanji 830]] gives the final persecution layer. [[EmpressDowagerLiangNaLateHan]] initially refuses to approve Du Qiao's arrest because she regards him as loyal and upright, but Liang Ji later threatens him with suicide as the price of preserving wife and children. Du Qiao refuses, enters the prison process, dies in prison, and has his displayed body guarded and returned for burial by [[YangKuangDuQiaoRetainer]].

## Key Characteristics
- Dahonglu who resists Liang Ji's preferred succession outcome in 146 CE.
- Taiwei whose 147 CE memorial criticizes unmerited reward and bad appointments around Liang-family and eunuch power.
- Procedural official who refuses Liang Ji's empress-rite overreach and corrupt shangshu recommendation.
- Loyal late-Han official paired with Li Gu in Liang Ji's accusation campaign.
- Initially shielded by Liang Na's confidence in his uprightness.
- Refuses coerced suicide even when family safety is used as leverage.
- Dies in prison under Liang Ji's political pressure, with posthumous reputation preserved by Yang Kuang's guarded burial action.

## Qualifications
This page is bounded to Hanji 828, Hanji 829, and Hanji 830 and does not reconstruct Du Qiao's full career, family, or all office history. Hanji 828 supplies the direct accession-resistance layer; Hanji 829 supplies the taiwei, remonstrance, ritual, recommendation, and slander layer; Hanji 830 supplies the final persecution, prison death, and posthumous burial-defense layer.

## What Changed
- Added Hanji 828 as Du Qiao's earliest current layer: dahonglu, succession objector, and upright holdout after Li Gu's removal.
- Reframed Hanji 829's memorial and institutional refusals as continuations of an already visible resistance profile.
- Preserved Hanji 830 as the death-and-reputation layer.
