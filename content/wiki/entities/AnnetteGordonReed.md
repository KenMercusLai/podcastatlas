# Annette Gordon-Reed

## Overview
Annette Gordon-Reed appears as the historian whose archival work helped shift the Jefferson-Hemings question from dismissed scandal toward modern consensus.

## Current Profile
The episode credits Gordon-Reed with showing that [[ThomasJefferson|Thomas Jefferson]] was at [[Monticello]] nine months before each of [[SallyHemings|Sally Hemings]]'s children was born. Her contribution matters because it paired chronology and archival method with testimony and later DNA evidence, undermining older biographical refusals to accept the relationship because it conflicted with Jefferson's moral image.

## Key Characteristics
- Historian associated in the episode with archival reassessment of Jefferson and Hemings.
- Provided chronological evidence connecting Jefferson's presence at Monticello to Hemings's pregnancies.
- Helped move the debate from character-based denial toward evidence-based consensus.
- Functions as a modern corrective voice in [[FoundingFatherReputationRevision|founding-father reputation revision]].
- Links Jefferson memory to method: records, testimony, and institutional reassessment.

## Evidence
- Chronology: [[686-jefferson-the-betrayal-of-liberty-part-4-glt6535571769]] says Gordon-Reed's work showed Jefferson was at Monticello nine months before each Hemings child was born.
- Consensus shift: [[686-jefferson-the-betrayal-of-liberty-part-4-glt6535571769]] places her archival work alongside DNA evidence and the Thomas Jefferson Foundation's acceptance of Jefferson paternity.
- Historiographical correction: [[686-jefferson-the-betrayal-of-liberty-part-4-glt6535571769]] contrasts modern evidence with 20th-century biographers who rejected the allegations as inconsistent with Jefferson's character.
