# Georgi Markov / 格奥尔基·马尔科夫

## Overview

Georgi Markov / 格奥尔基·马尔科夫 was a Bulgarian writer and émigré broadcaster whose 1978 death in London became a defining case of suspected ricin assassination and covert intelligence delivery.

## Current Profile

The episode presents Markov as a defector who criticized Bulgaria’s government through outlets including [[BBC]]. In September 1978 he felt a sharp pain in his thigh near a London bus stop, developed fever, and died four days later. Investigators reportedly recovered a perforated metal pellet about 1.7 millimeters wide; its design, clinical course, and later defector accounts led investigators to infer ricin delivery.

The case’s historical significance lies in method and evidentiary boundary. A minute implant can join a lethal biological agent to a delivery system that looks accidental and preserves state deniability. Later testimony reportedly attributed the operation to Bulgarian security with [[KGB]] assistance, but no one was prosecuted, so the page distinguishes a strongly supported assassination account from adjudicated responsibility.

## Key Characteristics

- Bulgarian writer and émigré critic who broadcast against his former government.
- Target of a 1978 London attack associated with a tiny perforated pellet and suspected ricin.
- Case linking toxicology, micro-engineering, intelligence cooperation, and political deniability.
- Death supported by physical and clinical evidence but followed by no criminal conviction.
- Public memory often compresses the delivery method into a “poison umbrella,” although the exact device remains less certain than the recovered pellet.
