## Overview
三台子 / Santai Zi is the Northeast Catholic village at the center of the letters and fieldwork discussed in [[100-nian-qian-dongbei-nvhai-xie-gei-bali-de-xin-duitan-lishixuezhe-liji-putongren-ruhe-chuanguo-da-lishi-3d36f181-94ad-4883-8db3-5f040b5ed45b]].

## Current Profile
The source presents Santai Zi as a village whose importance depends on the knowledge system being used. In ordinary administrative geography it was not necessarily prominent, but on Catholic missionary maps it appeared as a significant church and believer center. The village becomes a microhistorical site where migration, Catholic mission infrastructure, women's literacy, Boxer-era violence, descendant memory, and family continuity intersect.

## Key Characteristics
- It is treated as a Northeast immigrant Catholic village rather than an old lineage village with deep local clan institutions.
- It became highly visible in [[ParisForeignMissionsSociety|巴黎外方传教会]] mapping and records because of Catholic population and church infrastructure.
- It produced or preserved the social world around the [[DuFamilyGirls|杜氏女孩]] letters.
- It remained connected to Catholic memory through descendants, genealogy, crosses, images, and local recollection.
- It experienced violent twentieth-century disruptions, including the Boxer-era siege described around [[FatherGaoNortheastMission|高神父]].
