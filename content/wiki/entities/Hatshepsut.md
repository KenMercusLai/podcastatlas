# Hatshepsut

## Overview

Hatshepsut enters the wiki as an Egyptian female pharaoh whose artificial beard expressed kingship within an established court code.

## Current Profile

[[491-historys-greatest-beards-from-egyptian-queens-to-medieval-conquerors-part-1-glt6058938613]] places Hatshepsut's false beard in a system where pharaohs used visibly artificial facial hair to combine royal and martial authority with the bodily shaving associated with purity. Her presentation is therefore treated as an adoption of pharaonic regalia, not simply an imitation of ordinary male grooming.

## Key Characteristics

- Female ruler using established pharaonic regalia.
- Combined the signs of royal power and ritual purity.
- Demonstrates that facial hair can encode office rather than biological sex alone.

## Evidence

- Royal convention: [[491-historys-greatest-beards-from-egyptian-queens-to-medieval-conquerors-part-1-glt6058938613]] says artificial beards were already normal components of Egyptian pharaonic presentation.
- Symbolic combination: [[491-historys-greatest-beards-from-egyptian-queens-to-medieval-conquerors-part-1-glt6058938613]] interprets shaving and the false beard as simultaneously communicating purity and kingship.

## Qualifications

The page reflects one podcast's cultural interpretation and is not a complete profile of Hatshepsut's reign. The source does not establish that every Egyptian viewer assigned one fixed meaning to royal false beards.

## What Changed

- Created a source-bounded profile of Hatshepsut's false beard as office-bearing regalia.
