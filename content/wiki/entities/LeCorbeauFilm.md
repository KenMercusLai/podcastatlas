# Le Corbeau

## Overview

Le Corbeau is Henri-Georges Clouzot's 1943 film about anonymous poison-pen letters spreading accusation, exposure, and mistrust through a French town.

## Current Profile

[[242-french-history-on-film-glt2672520929]] reads the film as an Occupation allegory whose social paranoia matters more than explicit depiction of Germans or the Gestapo. Anonymous informing exposes adultery, abortion, illegitimacy, and other secrets until collaboration appears as a community pathology. Its refusal to offer a flattering France made it objectionable to Vichy and controversial again after Liberation.

## Key Characteristics

- Converts anonymous accusation into a mechanism of social surveillance.
- Addresses Occupation behavior indirectly rather than through an explicit wartime plot.
- Challenges Vichy ideals of family, Catholic morality, and womanhood.
- Resists post-Liberation pressure for a morally reassuring picture of France.

## Evidence

- Allegory: [[242-french-history-on-film-glt2672520929]] connects the letters to délation encouraged by German and Vichy authorities.
- Cross-regime controversy: [[242-french-history-on-film-glt2672520929]] explains why both Vichy and later resistance-aligned critics found the film objectionable.
- Moral refusal: [[242-french-history-on-film-glt2672520929]] highlights sympathy for a sexually transgressive woman and suspicion toward an apparently idealized woman.

## Qualifications

The informing allegory is a critical interpretation; the plot is based on an earlier real poison-pen case and does not explicitly stage Occupation institutions. Claims about the motives of whole political camps remain source-scoped.
