## Overview

徐广汉 / Xu Guanghan enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-419-nongxu-zuojia-de-hanchen-wangcheng-ruhe-ba-hanxuandi-pian-de-tuantuanzhuan-lim7jqyvestgoqb68pxhyuwiuhyd|Hanji 419]] as [[HanYuandi|刘奭]]'s maternal grandfather and [[EmperorXuanOfHan|汉宣帝]]'s father-in-law. After Liu Shi is installed as crown prince, Xuan honors Xu Guanghan as Ping'en Hou, but Xu's later request to place [[XuShunWesternHan|徐顺]] near the crown prince triggers the episode's public-interest tutor boundary.

## Current Profile

Hanji 419 presents Xu Guanghan as a valued relative whose political request becomes delicate because of prior personal kindness and plausible family concern. The episode says Xuan cannot easily reject him face to face: Liu Shi is young, Xu Guanghan is the crown prince's maternal grandfather, and the Xu family had treated Xuan well before he became emperor.

The same source treats the proposal as more than household help. Xu Guanghan asks that his younger brother Xu Shun oversee the crown prince's household, which the host reads as family positioning around the next generation. Xuan answers indirectly by asking [[ShuGuangShuShouWesternHan|疏广]], and Shu Guang reframes the issue: the crown prince is the state's heir, so his teachers and associates should be worthy people of the realm rather than a narrow circle of maternal relatives.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han outer relative linked to Liu Shi through maternal kinship.
- Father-in-law whom Emperor Xuan is reluctant to reject bluntly because of past kindness and plausible concern.
- Recipient of Ping'en Hou status after Liu Shi is made crown prince.
- Actor whose request for Xu Shun's crown-prince household role tests the boundary between kinship care and political access.
- Source case showing why outer-relative requests can require mediated refusal rather than direct confrontation.

## Qualifications

This page is source-scoped to Hanji 419. It does not reconstruct Xu Guanghan's full biography, exact title history, relationship with Xu Pingjun, or the complete court procedure behind the crown-prince household request. The claim that the request was next-generation political positioning is preserved as the host's interpretation.
