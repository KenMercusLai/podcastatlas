# 《长生剑》 / The Longevity Sword

## Overview

《长生剑》 / The Longevity Sword is the opening story of [[GuLong|古龙]]'s [[SevenWeaponsGuLong|《七种武器》]] cycle. In the bounded episode, Bai Yujing and Yuan Zixia move through a struggle over a stolen Peacock Feather blueprint in which nearly every apparent ally, identity, treasure, and winning position can be false.

## Current Profile

The plot's operational center is not a fair contest of martial skill but a competition to shape what others believe. The blueprint is counterfeit, yet secrecy, bloodshed, and difficulty make rivals treat it as authentic. Fang Longxiang trusts a hiding place more readily when he thinks he inferred it himself; competing factions wait to exploit one another and become victims of the same pattern; Yuan Zixia's affection and allegiance remain unresolved because every intimate gesture may also be strategic.

The story closes by naming “笑” rather than the sword as the first true weapon. The episode grants that smile can stand for charm, apparent harmlessness, intimacy, and emotional influence, but judges desire and scheming to be more continuous with the actual plot. This makes the work a strong case for separating a memorable final proposition from the mechanisms most consistently demonstrated by the preceding action.

## Key Characteristics

- Organizes the plot around a counterfeit treasure whose believed value is enough to cause real violence.
- Uses nested betrayal so each supposed manipulator may occupy someone else's trap.
- Makes inference, vanity, trust, and romantic ambiguity more decisive than direct combat.
- Keeps Bai Yujing and Yuan Zixia's identities and final motives deliberately unresolved.
- Names smiling as its governing allegory while supplying mixed support for that conclusion.
- Combines compact pacing and repeated reversal with a risk of audience fatigue.

## Qualifications

This profile is based on one podcast's retelling and criticism rather than an independent collation of the novel. The identities of Bai Yujing and Yuan Zixia, her motive for changing sides, and theories linking either protagonist to Green Dragon Society leadership remain unconfirmed. Strategic objections to selling the blueprint and aesthetic judgments about reversal density or thematic fit are source-scoped readings.
