# John Adams

## Overview

John Adams appears as a revolutionary observer, political correspondent, diplomat, and foil whose reactions connect the Boston Tea Party, founding debate, Franklin's French methods, women's claims, and early Anglo-American reconciliation.

## Current Profile

The sources do not build a full Adams biography. The 1770 episode places him before settled independence, defending British soldiers after the [[BostonMassacre|Boston Massacre]] to demonstrate due process even while using racialized and ethnic descriptions of the crowd. The 1773-1775 episode then uses his approval of the tea destruction to capture revolutionary escalation while qualifying his later claim that colonial opinion divided neatly into thirds. Later sources contrast his objections with Franklin's socially effective French diplomacy, use Abigail Adams's appeal to “remember the ladies” to show an intellectually serious political partnership, and present his meeting with George III as an emotionally charged gesture toward postwar friendship.

## Key Characteristics

- Founding-era American figure used as a diplomatic comparison with Franklin.
- Early interpreter of the Boston Tea Party as a historic revolutionary act.
- Critic of Franklin's aristocratic and social methods in France.
- Foil for [[DiplomaticPersonaAsStrategicAsset|diplomatic persona as strategic asset]].
- Political correspondent addressed by Abigail Adams on women's claims.
- Diplomat whose meeting with George III represented postwar Anglo-American repair.
- Lawyer whose Boston Massacre defense joined due process to racialized courtroom rhetoric.

## Qualifications

The page remains selective. It does not cover Adams's presidency, full diplomatic service, constitutional thought, family politics, or role in the [[FirstPartySystem]] beyond the supplied episodes. The Boston Massacre discussion does not provide the complete trial record; his retrospective population estimate is contested within the source, and Abigail Adams's appeal did not itself produce broad women's enfranchisement.
