# John Adams

## Overview

John Adams appears as both the foil whose discomfort with Franklin's French methods clarifies Franklin's diplomatic effectiveness and the political correspondent and diplomat connecting women's claims to early Anglo-American reconciliation.

## Current Profile

The sources do not build a full Adams biography. One uses him comparatively, contrasting his objections with Franklin's socially effective French diplomacy. The other uses Abigail Adams's appeal to “remember the ladies” to show an intellectually serious political partnership, then presents John Adams's later meeting with George III as an emotionally charged gesture toward friendship after independence.

## Key Characteristics

- Founding-era American figure used as a diplomatic comparison with Franklin.
- Critic of Franklin's aristocratic and social methods in France.
- Foil for [[DiplomaticPersonaAsStrategicAsset|diplomatic persona as strategic asset]].
- Political correspondent addressed by Abigail Adams on women's claims.
- Diplomat whose meeting with George III represented postwar Anglo-American repair.
