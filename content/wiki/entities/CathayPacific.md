# Cathay Pacific / 国泰航空

## Overview
Cathay Pacific / 国泰航空 is the Hong Kong-based airline used by the Shengdong Jixi roundtable as an 80-year lens on aviation, city networks, cargo, and passenger experience.

## Current Profile
The current wiki evidence treats Cathay less as a standalone financial company than as an anchor for Hong Kong's aviation-hub role. Its history links early flights through Shanghai's Longhua Airport, jet and Boeing 747-era long-haul expansion, Hong Kong's airport migration, cargo strength, and the design of mainland China connections.

The episode also makes Cathay a passenger-experience case. Details such as long-haul transfer routing, remote check-in from mainland cities, pricing structures for mainland-origin itineraries, and even economy-cabin metal cutlery become examples of how an airline converts a hub into a remembered travel product.

## Key Characteristics
- Hong Kong-based airline anchoring the episode's aviation-history frame.
- Long-haul and cargo actor tied to Hong Kong's role as an East-West connection point.
- Example of a hub airline extending demand through mainland-city access, remote check-in, and transfer pricing.
- Passenger-experience case where service details, meals, and routing affect brand memory after ticket purchase.

## Qualifications
The source comes from a Cathay 80th-anniversary branded live recording. It is useful for the airline's role in the episode's infrastructure story, but it does not independently verify Cathay's market share, financial performance, customer satisfaction, or route economics.
