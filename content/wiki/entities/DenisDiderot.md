# Denis Diderot

## Overview

Denis Diderot appears as an overtly atheist Enlightenment writer whose intellectual range, sociability, and humane curiosity represent the movement at its most generous in the episode's account.

## Current Profile

The source contrasts Diderot with both religious Enlightenment figures and deists who retained God for social order. It presents him as believing that people are corrupted less by innate evil than by bad education, unjust law, and social pressure. Tom Holland also chooses him as a favorite Enlightenment thinker, emphasizing kindness, generosity, breadth of interest, and concern about the destruction of beaver populations by North American trapping.

## Key Characteristics

- Overt atheist within an Enlightenment in which atheists remained a minority.
- Thinker who linked wrongdoing to education, institutions, and unjust law rather than fixed natural evil.
- Participant in the sociable and publishing world of the Enlightenment.
- Presented by the episode as humane, generous, intellectually wide-ranging, and environmentally attentive.
