## Overview
南华 / South China Athletic Association appears in the source as the traditional Hong Kong sports-club contrast to owner-controlled or corporatized football teams. The episode treats its brand as historically strong but its current professional-football participation as institutionally constrained.

## Current Profile
South China's wiki role is a governance case rather than a full club history. The source says it has a long history and strong brand recognition, yet is not currently playing at the top professional level. The guest does not claim to know the association's internal leadership thinking, but he treats the apparent lack of interest in professional-league participation as part of a broader mechanism problem: traditional sports associations may not give football patrons the direct control, stable authority, and long planning horizon they want.

The source also uses South China in earlier player-history context, including players who moved between traditional clubs, corporate teams, and other Hong Kong football institutions. That makes the club both a prestige memory and an example of why prestige alone does not settle operating governance.

## Key Characteristics
- Traditional Hong Kong sports association with a major football brand in the source's account.
- Historical prestige case whose brand recognition does not guarantee top-level professional participation.
- Governance contrast to independent or corporatized football operations.
- Example of how association leadership, elections, and internal priorities can matter to football planning.
