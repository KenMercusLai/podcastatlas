# Vasan Kesavan

## Overview

Vasan Kesavan is the co-author of the state-subdivision argument featured in [[divide-and-conquer-divide-and-conquer|Divide and Conquer]].

## Current Profile

Kesavan and [[MichaelStokesPaulsen]] develop a textual and historical reading of Article IV, Section 3. They conclude that the final consent requirement modifies both the state-subdivision and state-combination language, preserving the constitutionality of [[WestVirginia|West Virginia]] and allowing subdivision when the relevant state and Congress consent.

That framework supports the episode's Texas argument: an 1845 congressional resolution may constitute continuing advance permission for [[Texas]] to create up to four additional states.

## Key Characteristics

- Co-develops a text-and-history interpretation of Article IV's state-creation clause.
- Reads the disputed semicolon within the full grammatical construction.
- Uses West Virginia's formation as a constitutional test case.
- Connects general doctrine to Texas's specific admission terms.

## Evidence

### Textual and historical method

- [[divide-and-conquer-divide-and-conquer]] says Kesavan and Paulsen examined drafts, constitutional history, and founding-era records before concluding that the consent language applies across the semicolon.

### Statehood applications

- [[divide-and-conquer-divide-and-conquer]] attributes to the co-authors both the defense of West Virginia's formation and the Texas advance-consent theory.
