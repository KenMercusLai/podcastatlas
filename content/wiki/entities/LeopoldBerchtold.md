# Leopold Berchtold

## Overview

Leopold Berchtold was Austria-Hungary's foreign minister during the [[JulyCrisis|July Crisis]] and a principal advocate of coercive action against [[Serbia]].

## Current Profile

Berchtold helped turn personal shock and public outrage after Sarajevo into a policy designed to produce either war or a humiliating Serbian submission. Previously regarded as relatively moderate and personally close to Franz Ferdinand and Sophie, he returned quickly to Vienna, met [[FranzConradVonHotzendorf|Franz Conrad von Hötzendorf]], and accepted the case for punishing Serbia as urgent and defensive. German backing strengthened that course, but internal opposition and practical constraints delayed it. He and [[AlexanderHoyos|Alexander Hoyos]] eventually presented the harsh ultimatum to [[FranzJosephI|Franz Joseph I]], who approved it despite recognizing the danger of Russian resistance.

## Key Characteristics

- Senior diplomatic advocate of punishing Serbia after the assassination of Franz Ferdinand and Sophie.
- Moderate reputation and personal grief did not prevent a rapid shift toward coercive punishment.
- Participant in designing an ultimatum Serbia was unlikely to accept fully.
- Worked within a slow dual-monarchy system rather than commanding a unified war machine.
- Advanced the ultimatum despite an unresolved Russian contingency and Serbian end state.

## Qualifications

The sources show Berchtold as a central advocate, not an unconstrained author. His sincere grief and defensive self-description do not change the aggressive effect of his policy. Tisza's resistance, military logistics, imperial approval, and German support all shaped its timing and risk.

## What Changed

- Added Berchtold's moderate reputation, personal grief, meeting with Conrad, and decision to send Hoyos.
- Preserved his role as the foreign-ministerial link from Sarajevo through the blank cheque to the harsh ultimatum.

## Relationships

- [[FranzJosephI]] - emperor whose approval Berchtold obtained for the ultimatum.
- [[AlexanderHoyos]] - envoy and hard-line collaborator in securing German support.
- [[FranzConradVonHotzendorf]] - chief of staff whose immediate-war demand shaped Berchtold's opening response.
- [[IstvanTisza]] - Hungarian prime minister who delayed immediate war.
- [[AustriaHungary]] - dual monarchy whose diplomacy Berchtold directed.
- [[JulyCrisis]] - escalation in which his ultimatum strategy became decisive.
