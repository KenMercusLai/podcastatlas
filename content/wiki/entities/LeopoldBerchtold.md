# Leopold Berchtold

## Overview

Leopold Berchtold was Austria-Hungary's foreign minister during the [[JulyCrisis|July Crisis]] and a principal advocate of coercive action against [[Serbia]].

## Current Profile

Berchtold helped turn outrage after Sarajevo into a policy designed to produce either war or a humiliating Serbian submission. German backing strengthened that course, but internal opposition and practical constraints delayed it. He and [[AlexanderHoyos|Alexander Hoyos]] eventually presented the harsh ultimatum to [[FranzJosephI|Franz Joseph I]], who approved it despite recognizing the danger of Russian resistance.

## Key Characteristics

- Senior diplomatic advocate of punishing Serbia after the assassination of Franz Ferdinand and Sophie.
- Participant in designing an ultimatum Serbia was unlikely to accept fully.
- Worked within a slow dual-monarchy system rather than commanding a unified war machine.
- Advanced the ultimatum despite an unresolved Russian contingency and Serbian end state.

## Qualifications

The source shows Berchtold as a central advocate, not an unconstrained author. Tisza's resistance, military logistics, imperial approval, and German support all shaped the timing and risk of the policy.

## What Changed

- Created Berchtold as the foreign-ministerial link between the blank cheque and the harsh ultimatum.

## Relationships

- [[FranzJosephI]] - emperor whose approval Berchtold obtained for the ultimatum.
- [[AlexanderHoyos]] - envoy and hard-line collaborator in securing German support.
- [[IstvanTisza]] - Hungarian prime minister who delayed immediate war.
- [[AustriaHungary]] - dual monarchy whose diplomacy Berchtold directed.
- [[JulyCrisis]] - escalation in which his ultimatum strategy became decisive.
