# Thomas Andrews

## Overview

Thomas Andrews was the [[RMSTitanic|Titanic]] designer who diagnosed the collision damage and concluded that the ship would sink, as reconstructed in [[431-titanic-nightmare-at-midnight-part-5-glt2941711597]].

## Current Profile

The episode presents Andrews as the crucial bridge between physical damage and human response. After inspecting the flooding, he judged that six forward compartments had been breached and that water would pass over bulkheads that did not extend high enough to contain it. His estimate of perhaps two hours transformed an apparently modest collision into a terminal emergency, although that knowledge could not eliminate disbelief, communication delay, limited boat capacity, or inconsistent evacuation practice.

## Key Characteristics

- Ship designer with detailed knowledge of Titanic's compartment system.
- Early technical interpreter of damage that many aboard did not initially perceive as catastrophic.
- Concluded that flooding exceeded the vessel's survivable compartment limit.
- Estimated that the ship had at most roughly two hours remaining.
- Figure whose diagnosis exposed the gap between expert knowledge and coordinated emergency action.

## Evidence

- Damage diagnosis: [[431-titanic-nightmare-at-midnight-part-5-glt2941711597]] says Andrews identified six breached forward compartments and progressive overflow across insufficiently high bulkheads.
- Time horizon: [[431-titanic-nightmare-at-midnight-part-5-glt2941711597]] attributes to Andrews an estimate of no more than about two hours before sinking.
- Operational significance: [[431-titanic-nightmare-at-midnight-part-5-glt2941711597]] contrasts his diagnosis with continued reassurance, passenger disbelief, and delayed evacuation.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to one retrospective episode rather than a full biography or naval-architecture assessment. The exact inspection sequence, damage geometry, reported wording, and time estimate are presented through the episode and are not independently established here.
