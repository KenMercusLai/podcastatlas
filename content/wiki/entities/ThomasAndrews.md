# Thomas Andrews

## Overview

Thomas Andrews was the [[HarlandAndWolff]] ship designer who developed and inspected [[RMSTitanic|Titanic]], then diagnosed the collision damage that made sinking inevitable.

## Current Profile

The three episodes present Andrews across the full arc from apprentice to emergency diagnostician. He entered Harland & Wolff as a teenager, learned across departments, became a draftsman and designer, and specialized in large, comfortable, steady, safety-conscious ships rather than speed record seekers. He and [[BruceIsmay]] used experience with Olympic to inspect and refine Titanic's design and service details. After the collision, Andrews judged that six forward compartments had been breached and that water would pass over bulkheads that did not extend high enough to contain it. His estimate of perhaps two hours transformed an apparently modest collision into a terminal emergency, although that knowledge could not eliminate disbelief, communication delay, limited boat capacity, or inconsistent evacuation practice.

## Key Characteristics

- Harland & Wolff apprentice turned ship designer with cross-department production knowledge and detailed command of Titanic's compartment system.
- Designer oriented toward size, comfort, steadiness, and contemporary safety rather than maximum speed.
- Pre-disaster inspector concerned with design and service standards aboard the new liner.
- Early technical interpreter of damage that many aboard did not initially perceive as catastrophic.
- Concluded that flooding exceeded the vessel's survivable compartment limit.
- Estimated that the ship had at most roughly two hours remaining.
- Figure whose diagnosis exposed the gap between expert knowledge and coordinated emergency action.
