# John Bull

## Overview

John Bull is a personification of [[England]] created by John Arbuthnot in 1712 and developed in eighteenth-century print culture into a stout, blunt, beef- and beer-consuming Englishman.

## Current Profile

In [[286-england-beef-and-liberty-glt5964129806]], John Bull is the human form of [[RoastBeefEnglishIdentity]]. His body, dress, appetite, plain speech, scepticism, and anti-intellectual manner convert a food symbol into a claim about national character: materially comfortable, liberty-minded, unpretentious, stubborn, and opposed to French refinement or revolutionary abstraction.

The profile is deliberately double-edged. John Bull could affirm abundance and independence, but his fatness, roughness, and predictability also made him a caricature. That capacity to be both patriotic ideal and joke helped the figure survive as the eighteenth-century political urgency of roast beef declined.

## Key Characteristics

- Embodies England through a recognizable male body rather than an abstract constitutional argument.
- Joins roast beef, pudding, beer, plain dress, physical solidity, and bluff manners into one national type.
- Opposes French luxury, thinness, Catholicism, and later Jacobin rationalism in the episode's visual-political field.
- Carries liberty as inherited habit and temperament more than systematic political philosophy.
- Remains usable both sincerely and satirically because the stereotype contains pride and self-mockery.

## Evidence

- Origin and peak use: [[286-england-beef-and-liberty-glt5964129806]] dates Arbuthnot's creation to 1712 and places the figure's strongest roast-beef use from the 1760s through the French Revolution.
- Bodily and culinary profile: [[286-england-beef-and-liberty-glt5964129806]] describes John Bull as stout, waistcoated, beer-drinking, pudding- or beef-eating, bluff, sceptical, and anti-intellectual.
- Political contrast: [[286-england-beef-and-liberty-glt5964129806]] contrasts him with skinny, enthusiastic, rationalist, atheistical Jacobins during the French Revolution.
- Comic durability: [[286-england-beef-and-liberty-glt5964129806]] links the figure to a national stereotype people could perform, mock, and later recognize in modern personalities.

## Qualifications

This page covers John Bull only through one episode's food-history argument. It does not establish a complete iconography, chronology, political reception, or comparison with Britannia. The figure represents a gendered and selective ideal of Englishness, not the population's actual diet, body, politics, or social diversity.

## What Changed

- Created the entity as the personified endpoint of the episode's roast-beef politics.
- Preserved the figure's simultaneous use as patriotic ideal and self-caricature.
