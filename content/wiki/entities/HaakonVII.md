# Haakon VII

## Overview

Haakon VII was Norway's king during the 1940 German invasion and became a focal point of legitimate resistance by refusing to accept a collaborationist government.

## Current Profile

[[621-the-nazis-at-war-blitzkrieg-part-2-glt8711886512]] presents Haakon's agency as dependent on military delay. The sinking of the Blücher and resistance by local riflemen bought time for the king, ministers, and national gold reserves to leave Oslo ahead of German forces.

When Germany demanded approval of a government led by [[VidkunQuisling]], Haakon said he would abdicate rather than give it constitutional legitimacy. The episode therefore treats royal refusal as a political and moral complement to armed resistance, not as a substitute for it.

## Key Characteristics

- Constitutional monarch whose escape preserved a center of legitimate Norwegian authority.
- Beneficiary of the strategically important delay created at Oscarsborg Fortress.
- Refused to authorize Quisling's collaborationist government.
- Willing to put abdication ahead of lending royal legitimacy to occupation.

## Evidence

- Escape and continuity: [[621-the-nazis-at-war-blitzkrieg-part-2-glt8711886512]] connects the Blücher's sinking to the flight of the king, government, and gold reserves.
- Collaboration refusal: [[621-the-nazis-at-war-blitzkrieg-part-2-glt8711886512]] presents Haakon as rejecting Quisling and preferring abdication to approval.
- Resistance setting: [[621-the-nazis-at-war-blitzkrieg-part-2-glt8711886512]] places the king's decision within a broader Norwegian campaign lasting roughly two months.

## Qualifications

The source is a campaign narrative rather than a full biography or constitutional study. Exact dialogue, the distribution of authority between king and ministers, the handling of the gold reserves, and the independent effect of Haakon's refusal remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Created the profile around the interaction between military delay, constitutional legitimacy, and refusal to collaborate.
