# 寇荣 / Kou Rong (late Han)

## Overview

寇荣 / Kou Rong enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-864-mingchen-zhihou-weihe-cansi-yu-diwang-zhishou-lqwexx6nchx10roxsrvh8c0ktkfq|Hanji 864]] as a late-Han meritorious-family figure who is removed, pursued, and finally killed under [[EmperorHuanOfHan|汉桓帝]] after eunuch-side resentment and a failed imperial petition converge. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-863-yi-ba-huo-shaochu-de-zhandouli-ljpfmj8pphunruioce64ywjk7yhb|Hanji 863]] now supplies the prelude: Kou Rong's aloof style, imperial marriage ties, and court-favorite jealousy are visible before the full accusation and petition disaster.

## Current Profile

Kou Rong's profile is about status without protection. Hanji 863 introduces the risk before the event unfolds: Kou Rong is described as aloof and self-regarding, with powerful people disliking him. His clan also has imperial marriage ties: a younger clan relative marries Huan's sister, and Huan takes Kou Rong's granddaughter as a consort. Hanji 864 develops the same ties as visibility without safety, especially because Kou Rong is described as aloof toward eunuchs.

After eunuch and court-favorite accusation, Kou Rong is removed and sent back to his home commandery with his kin. Local officials continue pressure, and his attempt to go to Luoyang for palace-gate self-defense is blocked. His final memorial tries to argue innocence, expose the excesses of pursuit, request public review by senior officials, and preserve his family line. The appeal fails because Huan reads its historical analogies as an accusation of imperial injustice, and Kou Rong is executed.

## Key Characteristics

- Meritorious-family late-Han figure whose lineage and imperial marriage ties create visibility but not durable protection.
- Anti-eunuch or eunuch-disdaining actor whose posture makes him vulnerable inside Huan's court.
- Aloof court figure whose social isolation and high status make jealousy politically actionable.
- Accused and displaced official whose attempted direct petition is blocked before reaching ordinary public review.
- Fugitive petitioner who treats amnesty without exoneration as insufficient because the accusation remains active.
- Source case for self-vindicating speech becoming dangerous when historical analogy implies ruler wrongdoing.

## Qualifications

The page is bounded to Hanji 863 and Hanji 864 and does not reconstruct Kou Rong's full genealogy, offices, legal charges, or the independent documentary text of his memorial beyond the source summaries. Hanji 863 is a prelude and cliffhanger; Hanji 864 supplies the fatal petition sequence. The sources treat Kou Rong sympathetically, but the wiki keeps the causal judgment narrow: his death follows accusation, failed access, fugitive status, and offensive petition language as these sources present them.
