# 寇荣 / Kou Rong (late Han)

## Overview

寇荣 / Kou Rong enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-864-mingchen-zhihou-weihe-cansi-yu-diwang-zhishou-lqwexx6nchx10roxsrvh8c0ktkfq|Hanji 864]] as a late-Han meritorious-family figure who is removed, pursued, and finally killed under [[EmperorHuanOfHan|汉桓帝]] after eunuch-side resentment and a failed imperial petition converge.

## Current Profile

Kou Rong's profile is about status without protection. The source presents him as a high-lineage figure connected to the Eastern Han meritorious tradition and to imperial marriage ties: a younger clan relative marries Huan's sister, and Huan takes Kou Rong's granddaughter as a consort. Those ties increase visibility and resentment rather than safety, especially because Kou Rong is described as aloof toward eunuchs.

After eunuch and court-favorite accusation, Kou Rong is removed and sent back to his home commandery with his kin. Local officials continue pressure, and his attempt to go to Luoyang for palace-gate self-defense is blocked. His final memorial tries to argue innocence, expose the excesses of pursuit, request public review by senior officials, and preserve his family line. The appeal fails because Huan reads its historical analogies as an accusation of imperial injustice, and Kou Rong is executed.

## Key Characteristics

- Meritorious-family late-Han figure whose lineage and imperial marriage ties create visibility but not durable protection.
- Anti-eunuch or eunuch-disdaining actor whose posture makes him vulnerable inside Huan's court.
- Accused and displaced official whose attempted direct petition is blocked before reaching ordinary public review.
- Fugitive petitioner who treats amnesty without exoneration as insufficient because the accusation remains active.
- Source case for self-vindicating speech becoming dangerous when historical analogy implies ruler wrongdoing.

## Qualifications

The page is source-bounded to Hanji 864 and does not reconstruct Kou Rong's full genealogy, offices, legal charges, or the independent documentary text of his memorial beyond the episode's summary. The source treats Kou Rong sympathetically, but the wiki keeps the causal judgment narrow: his death follows accusation, failed access, fugitive status, and offensive petition language as this source presents them.
