# Kutná Hora / 库特纳霍拉

## Overview

Kutná Hora is a Czech city east of [[Prague]] whose medieval rise, coinage, churches, and later decline were organized around silver mining.

## Current Profile

The source presents Kutná Hora as an extraction-built city. Rich silver output supported royal revenue, mining law, centralized minting, the Prague groschen, church construction, and civic ambitions that once made the city second only to Prague in importance. St. Barbara's Church makes this labor visible through mining and minting imagery, while its patronage expresses both wealth and a wish for autonomy from monastic control.

Prosperity depended on dangerous depth. A preserved mine route makes the narrow, wet, dark working environment tangible; relatively high income and legal privilege did not remove mortal risk. Depletion, deeper shafts, and rising drainage costs then weakened the industry after the sixteenth century. Sedlec Ossuary adds a different material history in which plague, famine, war, sacred burial prestige, and nineteenth-century bone arrangement turn accumulated death into a *memento mori*.

## Key Characteristics

- Medieval Bohemian silver-mining center whose wealth rivaled Prague's importance.
- Site of royal mining regulation and stable silver coin production.
- City where churches and civic buildings convert extractive wealth into monumental form.
- Labor environment combining comparatively high reward and privilege with severe underground danger.
- Urban economy vulnerable to depletion, shaft depth, drainage cost, and post-boom decline.
