# Robert Bork

## Overview
Robert Bork was a Supreme Court nominee whose disclosed video-rental history became the historical catalyst for the 1988 [[VideoPrivacyProtectionAct|Video Privacy Protection Act]].

## Current Profile
In this wiki, Bork's role is narrow and historical rather than a general judicial biography. [[tech-20261116-1116-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261116-1116-mp-tech-pod-128]] uses the publication of his rental history to explain why Congress protected identifiable video-viewing records and why applying that remedy to analytics and advertising now creates a statutory-translation problem.

## Key Characteristics
- Supreme Court nominee at the center of the disclosure that prompted the statute.
- Historical privacy case showing that viewing records can reveal personal interests.
- Link between a videotape-era scandal and present internet-data litigation.

## Evidence
- Legislative catalyst - [[tech-20261116-1116-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261116-1116-mp-tech-pod-128]] says Congress passed the law after a reporter published Bork's video-rental history.
- Modern relevance - [[tech-20261116-1116-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261116-1116-mp-tech-pod-128]] contrasts that original transaction with analytics and targeted advertising on contemporary websites.
