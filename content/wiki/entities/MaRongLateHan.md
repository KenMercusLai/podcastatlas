## Overview

马融 / Ma Rong is a late-Han scholar-official introduced in Hanji 836 as both a learned儒者 and a victim of Liang Ji's retaliatory politics. His visit to [[LiangBuyiLateHan|梁不疑]] brings punishment, but his later recall and classical work keep him from being reduced to a single persecution episode.

## Current Profile

Hanji 836 presents Ma Rong as a scholar with elite literary and rhetorical ability, trained in Confucian classics under a respected Jingzhao teacher who valued his talent. His office path includes posts such as xiaoshu lang, langzhong, yilang, Liang Shang's subordinate, Wudu taishou, and later Nan commandery taishou under Emperor Huan.

The source's central Ma Rong event occurs when he visits Liang Buyi while traveling to a new post. Liang Ji's side accuses him of corruption in Nan commandery, and he receives hair-removal punishment, flogging, and exile to Shuofang. He is later recalled and continues scholarly work by helping collate Confucian classics and extending Hanji.

The current profile therefore treats Ma Rong as a scholar-official whose public dignity becomes a target. The punishment matters not only as administrative discipline but as [[PunitiveHairRemovalHumiliation|髡刑式身份羞辱]] imposed on someone whose status rested on learning, reputation, and office.

## Key Characteristics

- Learned late-Han儒者 whose reputation combines classical study, literary ability, and official service.
- Official punished after visiting Liang Buyi, making social contact with a monitored household politically dangerous.
- Victim of hair-removal and flogging punishment whose humiliation carries special force because of scholar-official status.
- Exile survivor who is later recalled and continues textual and historical work.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 836 and does not reconstruct Ma Rong's full intellectual lineage, teaching network, or complete bibliography. The source treats Liang Ji's accusation as retaliatory, but it does not independently adjudicate every administrative detail of the corruption charge. Later wiki sources may add Ma Rong's teacher role if they supply direct evidence.
