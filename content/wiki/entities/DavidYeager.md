# David Yeager

## Overview
David Yeager is a psychology professor at the University of Texas at Austin whose research examines motivation, growth mindset, stress appraisal, purpose, adolescent development, and mentor feedback.

## Current Profile
Across the sources, Yeager's central contribution is a context-sensitive account of motivation. [[GrowthMindset]] is not a promise that effort can achieve anything; it is a belief that capacity can change when strategy, opportunity, and support make change possible. [[MentorMindsetFeedback]] applies the same logic interpersonally by pairing high standards with honest feedback, confidence, and practical help.

Yeager also distinguishes a stressor from its appraisal and response, links purpose to both self-development and contribution, and treats adolescent status-seeking as something institutions can channel through multiple valued niches. The earlier Alpha School source applies part of this framework but does not independently validate the school's wider claims.

The earlier solo Huberman synthesis adds Yeager's combined adolescent intervention. It presents growth mindset and stress-is-enhancing appraisal as mutually reinforcing and reports improved self-regard, difficult-course passing, and later hard-challenge choice after a brief tutorial. The later direct interview supplies the necessary boundary: short interventions can start a recursive process, but durable outcomes depend on environments that make improvement and support real.

## Key Characteristics
- Defines [[GrowthMindset]] as conditional changeability rather than effort-only optimism.
- Frames stress arousal as challenge or threat partly through perceived demands and resources.
- Develops the mentor-mindset combination of high standards, honest criticism, belief, and support.
- Connects sustained motivation to personal growth, social status, and contribution beyond the self.
- Uses field experiments and brief interventions while emphasizing recursive environmental effects.
- Combines growth and stress mindsets while keeping their effects conditional on credible opportunity and resources.

## Qualifications
The episodes summarize studies conversationally and do not provide complete methods, effect sizes, or replication histories. Some longer-term school results are described as unpublished, and applied examples do not prove that mindset or incentive design alone caused later outcomes. The brief tutorial's numerical outcomes remain source-scoped and should not be generalized beyond its studied populations and settings. The Alpha School source borrows Yeager's framework but should not be treated as Yeager's endorsement or independent validation of that school's claims.
