## Overview

Gai Yan / 盖延 enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-690-jiaoyou-bushen-you-duo-kepa-kankan-tianrong-de-jingli-lpbms8pebzdo2ldiwho1cba81njk|Hanji 690]] as the Eastern Han commander whose Lanling response illustrates failed indirect relief. When [[DongXianEasternHan|董宪]] surrounds Lanling after Ben Xiu offers the county to Han, [[LiuXiu|光武帝刘秀]] tells Gai Yan to attack Tan so the Lanling siege will lift. Gai Yan instead rushes to the besieged city, enters after Dong Xian feigns retreat, then breaks out in fear and fails to save Ben Xiu.

## Current Profile

The source gives Gai Yan a compact but clear command problem. His instinct is direct rescue: Lanling is in danger, so he wants to move toward Lanling. Liu Xiu's answer is indirect pressure: strike Dong Xian's own base at Tan and the besieger will be forced to leave.

Gai Yan's failure comes from choosing the urgent visible site over the must-save site. Dong Xian's feigned retreat lets him enter Lanling, but the next day's encirclement changes the rescue into a trap. Gai Yan breaks out, then goes toward Tan too late. Liu Xiu's rebuke frames the lesson: the indirect strike mattered before Dong Xian's plan settled, not after Lanling had been re-enclosed.

## Key Characteristics

- Eastern Han commander in the Jianwu 4 Dong Xian/Lanling branch.
- Direct-rescue actor who overrides Liu Xiu's indirect-relief instruction.
- Commander deceived by Dong Xian's feigned retreat into entering a vulnerable city.
- Negative case for tactical urgency outrunning strategic target selection.
- Figure whose failure leads to Lanling's fall and Ben Xiu's death.
