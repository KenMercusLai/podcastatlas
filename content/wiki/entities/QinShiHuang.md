# Qin Shi Huang / 秦始皇

Qin Shi Huang / 秦始皇 appears in [[87-guonian-zui-shihe-liao-xuanxue-tantao-shenxian-fangshu-fazhanshi-wei-wan-daixu-704051997]] as the first major imperial case in the episode's history of fangshu. The source presents him as a ruler whose unification project and death anxiety made [[ImmortalityQuestPolitics|求仙政治]] unusually consequential: immortal islands, hidden palaces, water-phase symbolism, omen fear, and punishment of fangshi all enter the same political field.

The episode's Qin Shi Huang is not only a credulous individual. He is a case where cosmic language can support court secrecy, symbolic order, harsh rule, and large-scale search projects, while failed miracle claims can trigger violent backlash.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-97-3-lvbuwei-lishishang-fengtou-di-yi-ren-lswa9b864oc-s8vaxtdu15arydu2]] adds Qin Shi Huang's birth setup before his imperial identity. The episode says [[LuBuwei|吕不韦]] gives his beloved woman to [[QinZhuangxiangwang|异人 / 子楚]], and she later gives birth to 嬴政. This page records that as a source-scoped Qin succession origin point tied to [[PoliticalVentureInvestment|political venture investment]], not as a full biography of Ying Zheng's later rise.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-97-4-shishang-wei-shiye-song-laopo-diyi-ren-lo8omg01gkwlojkpiflaep99x-vz]] names the woman as [[ZhaoJi|赵姬]] and then treats Ying Zheng's paternity as a source-critical problem. The episode compares [[ZizhiTongjian|《资治通鉴》]] and [[Shiji|《史记》]], notes the tradition that Zhao Ji may already have been pregnant by Lu Buwei, and then says modern scholars increasingly doubt or reject the claim. [[GuoMoruo|郭沫若]] is cited as a representative skeptic because the story is isolated, internally strained, and similar to the [[Chunshenjun|春申君]] succession scandal.

For this page, the result is a guarded origin note: Ying Zheng is linked to Zhao Ji, Yiren, and Lu Buwei's political investment, but the wiki does not treat Lu Buwei paternity as established. The episode's stronger point is about reception: later hostility to Qin made a story of merchant-and-dancer ancestry feel satisfying to repeat.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-109-1-lishishang-zui-zhuming-de-huangdi-bei-lv-le-liawtywj4c9n0zpii-rxl7cguzyy]] reinforces that caution from the [[Chunshenjun|春申君]] side. The host compares the real Chu succession-substitution story with the rumor that [[LuBuwei|吕不韦]] fathered Ying Zheng, but explicitly says she believes Ying Zheng was [[QinZhuangxiangwang|异人 / 子楚]]'s son and treats the Lu Buwei claim as likely slander from the six states or Qin political enemies. The page therefore keeps [[PregnancySuccessionSubstitution|孕身继承替换]] as a motif comparison, not proof about Qin Shi Huang's birth.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-98-lishishang-shouge-laolai-dengchang-le-luxsxepdedsxd-zprezfg3u3bsnu]] adds a narrow early-life context. While [[ZhouNanwang|周赧王]]'s final year closes 周纪 and the narrative prepares to enter Qin-centered chronology, Ying Zheng is still a small child in [[Handan|邯郸]] with [[ZhaoJi|赵姬]] after Yiren's escape. The source uses that contrast to show that Qin's future unifier is present but not yet the political subject of the chronicle.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-100-1-huangdi-zhong-de-chaochang-daiji-wang-guoshi-le-lt75scavlm8qjn6ulpd-b2kwzwjf]] moves that childhood context forward. The episode says young Ying Zheng is in [[Handan|邯郸]] alongside [[YanTaiziDan|燕太子丹]] in 252 BCE, then returns to Qin with [[ZhaoJi|赵姬]] after [[QinZhuangxiangwang|子楚]] becomes crown prince. The host reads his later treatment of Handan enemies after Zhao's fall as a memory-and-revenge branch rooted in this early danger.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-103-1-simaqian-kanzhong-de-xinlingjun-suan-shi-xiaoren-ma-lvty1pdnibvqpk0m16kkxzw4t-ck]] gives the accession notice. In the source's Qin Zhuangxiang Wang third-year frame, Ying Zheng is thirteen and becomes Qin king while Qin is already expanding through [[Shangdang|上党]], [[TaiyuanWarringStates|太原]], and the Wei front under [[MengAo|蒙敖]]. The episode still treats him mainly as a chronological marker; the active story remains [[Xinlingjun|信陵君]]'s resistance to Qin.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-103-2-simaqiande-idol-weihe-siyu-jiuse-lvmqg1ojmka9pqagfhgvo4albpjx]] repeats the accession but makes the governance consequence explicit. At thirteen, Ying Zheng inherits after [[QinZhuangxiangwang|秦庄襄王]]'s death, while state affairs are decided by [[LuBuwei|吕不韦]] as "仲父." The source uses this to set up a paradox: Qin faces internal unrest after the succession, yet is approaching the rapid conquest of the six states.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-104-yinmou-shisuan-rang-qinguo-bantu-you-you-you-pengzhang-le-lgnyhbduqutegtksrjwcvhmn8-t6]] develops that paradox through the [[ZhengGuoCanal|郑国渠]]. Ying Zheng is still young and Lu Buwei remains the active governing figure, but the source has Ying Zheng ultimately accept [[ZhengGuoHydraulicEngineer|郑国]]'s argument after Han's plot is exposed: finishing the canal benefits [[QinState|秦国]] more than abandoning it, and the attempted [[SelfDefeatingResourceDrainStrategy|resource-drain strategy]] becomes Qin capacity.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-106-jingren-faxian-xinlingjun-de-feizhengchang-siwang-lsj3kd2tb4tqtuvhwvsdwkh-h5el]] keeps Ying Zheng in the young-ruler frame during 243-242 BCE. At seventeen, his Qin faces "locusts and epidemic"; the episode says Qin responds with [[RankSaleDisasterFinance|鬻爵赈灾融资]], exchanging grain for the lowest rank. At eighteen, Qin still sends [[MengAo|蒙敖]] against [[WeiState|魏国]], takes twenty cities, and creates [[DongCommandery|东郡]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-107-zuihou-yici-hezong-faqin-fa-le-ge-jimo-lrzq1qks7z-oromd-5f-dgqjwjhw]] carries the young-ruler chronology into Ying Zheng's sixth to eighth years, 241-239 BCE. The sixth year brings the final anti-Qin [[HezongAlliance|合纵]] campaign and its failure; the seventh year records Qin attacking [[WeiState|魏国]] again and the deaths of [[XiaTaihou|夏太后]] and Meng Ao; the eighth year records Wei giving Ye to Zhao and [[HanHuanhuiwang|韩桓惠王]] dying before Han An succeeds.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-108-1-lishi-gan-dangmian-dingzhuang-qinshihuang-diyi-ren-lg0dssyicp387foiraohkv1x5aij]] moves Ying Zheng into his ninth year and personal-rule threshold. At twenty-two, he goes to [[YongchengQin|雍城]] for the crown ceremony, while [[YangDuanhe|杨端和]] continues Qin's eastward attack on [[WeiState|魏国]]. The same episode makes his court crisis central: [[LaoAi|嫪毐]]'s rebellion and [[ZhaoJi|赵姬]]'s confinement expose Ying Zheng's rage, family humiliation, and legitimacy problem until [[MaoJiao|茅焦]]'s [[DeathDefyingRemonstrance|赴死式直谏]] persuades him to bring Zhao Ji back to [[Xianyang|咸阳]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-108-2-laoai-de-hougong-mishi-lof4xuuxfkehm9igpdvihv7exqdy]] backfills the emotional setting behind that crisis. The episode repeats Ying Zheng's hostage-childhood danger in [[Handan|邯郸]], his father's escape to Qin, and his long minority under [[LuBuwei|吕不韦]] and [[ZhaoJi|赵姬]], then reads the Lao Ai affair as a humiliating exposure of family disorder just as he is preparing to rule personally. This page keeps that as the host's psychological interpretation of Ying Zheng's severity, not as a complete explanation for Qin's later imperial violence.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-108-3-huangtaihou-he-qingfu-laoai-qingse-beihou-de-qishi-lthiqkpc0nbnlkxhcmivufvgl25b]] turns the same crisis into a power-consolidation sequence. After Lao Ai is exposed and rebels, Ying Zheng destroys Lao Ai's faction, kills the two sons, confines [[ZhaoJi|赵姬]], and initially wants to kill [[LuBuwei|吕不韦]] before settling for dismissal and exile from the capital. The episode therefore extends his early-rule profile through [[PalaceScandalPowerConsolidation|宫廷丑闻式收权]] while also noting that [[MaoJiao|茅焦]]'s advice leads him to restore Zhao Ji and "复为母子如初."

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-110-1-lisi-micang-laoshu-he-xiashuidao-laoshu-ni-zenme-xuan-lmv-fw-kgsuokonssq8bkbfvdsb]] moves into Ying Zheng's tenth year, 237 BCE, and shows the policy aftershock of that consolidation. With Lu Buwei removed from central office and [[ZhengGuoHydraulicEngineer|郑国]]'s canal mission usable as proof of foreign danger, Qin royal-clan ministers persuade Ying Zheng to issue the [[GuestExpulsionOrder|逐客令]]. The source therefore adds a talent-policy crisis to his early-rule profile: suppressing insider threats can create a new temptation to purge useful outsiders such as [[LiSi|李斯]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-110-2-qinshihuang-de-cp-weihe-bei-quzhu-chuguo-lj0222vw8ijbvj25iwfzh-qhvzr9]] gives the correction to that crisis. Ying Zheng first treats the [[ZhengGuoHydraulicEngineer|郑国]] affair as reason to distrust foreign guest officials, but [[LiSi|李斯]]'s [[JianZhuKeShu|《谏逐客书》]] persuades him that expulsion would contradict Qin's own talent history and send useful people to enemy states. The episode therefore presents Ying Zheng as capable of reversing a sweeping security decision when the argument is tied to unification strategy.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-111-1-ruoda-de-zhaoguo-jing-wang-yu-yige-jinv-zhi-shou-lhrqb8-swlkuyoydgjyn8ldnlkzc]] moves him into the eleventh year, 236 BCE. A Yan envoy warns that [[ZhaoState|赵国]] will become more dangerous if it swallows [[YanState|燕国]], and the episode says Ying Zheng accepts the reasoning and sends [[WangJianQin|王翦]], [[HuanYiQin|桓齮]], and [[YangDuanhe|杨端和]] against Zhao. This adds a practical post-guest-expulsion example of Qin turning rival-state conflict into conquest timing.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-111-2-wo-yu-lvbuwei-de-gekong-duihua-ltpbkg1echouxwjyhsfax2swfzch]] keeps him in the twelfth year, 235 BCE, but turns from external conquest to cleanup after [[LuBuwei|吕不韦]]'s removal. The episode says foreign envoys continue visiting Lu Buwei in Luoyang, so Ying Zheng writes a letter denying Lu Buwei's merit, blood relation, and "仲父" status and orders him to move with his family to Shu. The source reads Lu Buwei's suicide as Ying Zheng's successful removal of a residual power center after the [[LaoAi|嫪毐]] scandal and [[GuestExpulsionOrder|逐客令]] crisis.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-112-1-yaojia-shi-ruhe-ping-yizhangzui-po-siguo-hezong-lthw0fvvinmf6ghxpqut60agsueg]] adds a diplomatic-use case after that cleanup. When a Zhao-Han-Wei-Chu [[HezongAlliance|合纵]] plan worries Ying Zheng and the court hesitates, [[YaoJia|姚贾]] asks to go as Qin envoy. Ying Zheng gives him carriages, horses, money, clothes, and a sword, making Yao Jia's royal backing visible to the target courts. The episode therefore adds a decision style: Ying Zheng uses material support and public status to turn a low-origin persuader into an instrument of [[InterestBasedCoalitionSabotage|coalition sabotage]]. It also opens, but does not complete, the branch in which [[LiSi|李斯]] attacks [[HanFei|韩非]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-112-2-qinshihuang-yeshi-tade-shumi-llkoy-zvhjjr6af9tflcw1xvogwz]] adds the reader-admirer side of that Han Fei branch. Ying Zheng reads [[HanFei|韩非]]'s [[Gufen|《孤愤》]] and [[Wudu|《五蠹》]], is deeply moved, and wants to meet and befriend the author. The source makes this admiration politically unstable: when [[HanAn|韩王安 / Han An]] sends Han Fei to Qin as Qin prepares to attack Han, Ying Zheng's desire to keep him collides with [[LiSi|李斯]]'s conquest-first objection and becomes [[AuthorAdmirationPowerRisk|author admiration power risk]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-112-3-hanfei-yu-yaojia-lunzhan-qinwang-dang-caipan-ljxxlok4uh-ajjirmolybodlspv9]] adds the decision failure inside that admiration. Ying Zheng rewards [[YaoJia|姚贾]] after the three-year anti-[[HezongAlliance|合纵]] mission, hears [[HanFei|韩非]]'s attack on Yao Jia, and accepts Yao Jia's defense that low origin and contested biography do not disqualify useful talent. But when Li Si frames Han Fei's [[HanState|韩国]] royal identity as an unavoidable loyalty problem, Ying Zheng has Han Fei imprisoned for review. His later regret and pardon arrive only after Li Si's poison has already killed Han Fei.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-113-limu-ruhe-yong-yiji-zhili-zu-qinguo-tongyi-luymqpcolsvyz5miodit3wdlx0ob]] moves Ying Zheng from court and talent crises into the visible unification-war phase. In his fifteenth year, Qin's Zhao campaign still has to reckon with [[LiMu|李牧]]. By his seventeenth year, Qin captures [[HanAn|韩王安 / Han An]] and destroys [[HanState|韩国]], and by his eighteenth year Qin attacks disaster-weakened [[ZhaoState|赵国]] while using rumor and bribery to remove Li Mu and [[SimaShang|司马尚]]. The source therefore makes Ying Zheng's conquest style a combination of military pressure, opportunistic timing, and enemy-court manipulation.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-114-1-jintian-liaoliao-yingzheng-dou-you-naxie-canren-shouduan-lmqky6g3qi9nklpdnye4lk5rrbz6]] gives the nineteenth-year Zhao endpoint and returns to the childhood Handan memory first foreshadowed in Qinji 100-1 and 108-2. After [[Handan|邯郸]] falls, Ying Zheng personally goes there and has old enemies connected to his childhood danger killed or, in the [[Shiji|《史记》]] wording reported by the host, buried. The source therefore adds a sharper case of [[PersonalRevengeThroughStatePower|personal revenge through state power]] to his conquest profile, while also noting that [[ZhaoYoumiuWang|赵迁 / 赵幽缪王]] is left alive in exile and that [[ZhaoJi|赵姬]] dies soon after Ying Zheng returns to Xianyang.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-114-2-yanguo-cisha-qinshihuang-weihe-hui-xuan-jingke-lsewjrmgup1yanf8ipn-i0ykqwjx]] makes Ying Zheng mostly the offstage target of Yan's desperation. The source's title uses "秦始皇," but the plot belongs to the pre-imperial Qin king stage: [[YanTaiziDan|燕太子丹]] tries to convert Qin's state-level pressure into a plan against Ying Zheng's body, using [[JingKe|荆轲]] and [[FanWuji|樊无忌]] as access devices.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-114-3-jingke-ciqin-weihe-jiujiu-bu-xingdong-zai-deng-shui-liihe-kgwigu3bggxmyofgvotged]] keeps Ying Zheng offstage but clarifies the access assumptions around him. Jing Ke believes the Qin king can be approached only if the envoy brings gifts that match Qin's immediate desires: [[FanWuji|樊无忌]]'s head and the [[DuKangMap|督亢地图]]. The episode therefore presents Ying Zheng less as a speaking actor than as a ruler whose appetite for fugitive punishment and territorial gain shapes the assassination design.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-115-1-jingke-ciqin-sihu-you-dianer-caoshuai-le-lgz1adsn1t8jjiaobmtfedpr-ixn]] brings Ying Zheng into the reception scene. Through [[MengJia|蒙嘉]], [[JingKe|荆轲]] presents [[YanState|燕国]] as willing to submit, offer [[FanWuji|樊无忌]]'s head, and surrender the [[DuKangMap|督亢地图]]. Ying Zheng is pleased enough to stage a high-ceremony palace audience, which creates the pressure under which [[QinWuyang|秦武阳]] freezes.

The same source adds a counterfactual limit to Ying Zheng's biographical importance. It says that if Jing Ke had killed him, Qin would likely have seen political shock, as after [[QinWuwang|秦武王]]'s sudden death, but that Qin's larger institutional momentum probably would not have reversed. Ying Zheng remains the mission's target, but the episode refuses to reduce Qin unification to one ruler's life.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-115-2-qinwang-danan-busi-zhihou-ganle-jian-ni-buzhidao-de-shi-lidfjb6-nggubw8vpz5fvnfacrqu]] completes the assassination scene and makes Ying Zheng's survival depend on a series of tactical contingencies. [[JingKe|荆轲]] reveals the dagger at the end of the [[DuKangMap|督亢地图]], misses the first strike, tears Ying Zheng's sleeve, and forces him to run around a pillar before a court reminder to carry the sword on his back lets him draw the long weapon and wound Jing Ke. After the failed throw of the dagger, Ying Zheng turns survival into retaliation by adding troops in the occupied Zhao area and sending [[WangJianQin|王翦]] against [[YanState|燕国]], where Wang Jian defeats Yan and [[DaiState|代国]] forces under [[ZhaoJia|赵嘉]] west of the Yi River.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-116-shishang-zuican-taizi-bei-fuwang-qinshou-zhansha-lthruqxtd4iqofdxzvim58ph8rlk]] shows Ying Zheng refusing to stop at personal revenge against [[YanTaiziDan|燕太子丹]]. [[YanWangXi|燕王喜]] kills Dan under [[ZhaoJia|赵嘉]]'s advice, but the source says Ying Zheng's goal is not only Dan's head; it is the whole conquest of the remaining states. The same episode then gives him a command-selection error frame: after [[LiXinQin|李信]] says 200,000 troops can conquer [[ChuState|楚国]], Ying Zheng treats [[WangJianQin|王翦]]'s 600,000 estimate as excessive and sends Li Xin with [[MengTian|蒙恬]] instead.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-117-1-papa-dalian-lishishang-shuangwen-de-kaishan-bizu-lr0h-kfeguvatattnj-mz8fgvnc]] adds two linked conquest-era roles. Offstage, Ying Zheng's Qin destroys [[WeiState|魏国]] through [[WangBenQin|王贲]]'s water attack on [[Daliang|大梁]]. Onstage in the [[Zhanguoce|《战国策》]] branch, he tries to exchange a large territory for [[AnlingJun|安陵君]]'s small domain and is answered by [[TangJuEnvoy|唐雎]]'s [[ClothCladAngerPersuasion|布衣之怒]]. The page keeps the title wording and the court scene source-scoped because these events are pre-221 BCE and the episode itself questions the Tang Ju story's literal reliability.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-117-2-xuezhedian-qinchao-wujiang-jingran-ruci-gaoqingshang-lsyw3eitcnzfexl1l6vfh2gbtaoy]] shows Ying Zheng correcting the Chu command error. After [[LiXinQin|李信]] and [[MengTian|蒙恬]] are defeated by [[XiangYan|项燕]], he is angry but the host emphasizes that he accepts his own mistaken judgment, goes to [[WangJianQin|王翦]], and grants the 600,000 troops plus [[MengWu|蒙武]] deputy request. The same source also marks Ying Zheng as a suspicious ruler whose possible fear of a commander with the whole army forces Wang Jian into [[GreedPersonaSelfProtection|贪财人设式自保]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-118-1-wangjian-lv-liushiwan-dajun-duizhan-xiangyan-haoxi-kaichang-le-lsf2puinwg0m3bgyi25znof7aznu]] gives that correction its campaign result. Ying Zheng's willingness to lower posture and accept Wang Jian's full troop requirement lets Qin use not only scale but patience: Wang Jian refuses premature decision, waits behind fortifications, and attacks [[ChuState|楚国]] only after [[XiangYan|项燕]]'s army begins to withdraw. The episode therefore adds a leadership correction case to Ying Zheng's page, while preserving the source's pre-imperial title wording as a naming convention rather than a chronological claim.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-118-2-chi-gua-le-wangshi-de-gua-bao-tian-lns7jt-kbv3qunm66hzltdgugbco]] adds a personnel-trust and post-conquest version of the same late-unification problem. The host infers that Ying Zheng's distrust of [[ChangpingJun|昌平君]] comes from Changping Jun's Chu royal blood, making Qin court kinship a possible security risk during the Chu campaign. The episode also frames Ying Zheng's next choice after Chu's fall as strategic restraint versus pursuit: if Qin presses remnants too hard, nobles and officials from destroyed states may concentrate in still-intact [[QiState|齐国]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-119-3-lishishang-di-yi-wei-zhenzheng-yiyishang-chuilian-tingzheng-taihou-liq9d3itmbar00ij43zzed-mulul]] keeps Ying Zheng mostly offstage but shows his unification project entering its final geometry. With [[WangBenQin|王贲]] able to attack [[DaiState|代国]] and threaten [[QiState|齐国]] from the north, and [[WangJianQin|王翦]] extending Qin control into Jiangnan, [[Baiyue|百越]], and [[KuaijiCommandery|会稽郡]], the episode frames the "天下大酺" celebration as a Qin-centered announcement of near-completion rather than a literal all-under-heaven event including Qi.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-120-1-shui-shi-huangdi-zhong-de-mabaonan-lhw8vsj6ta6rjdkugcvfohaqqxc0]] moves Ying Zheng across the 221 BCE threshold. With [[WangBenQin|王贲]] pressuring [[QiState|齐国]] and [[QiWangJian|齐王建 / 田建]] surrendering, the episode treats 221 BCE as both the last Warring States year and the first Qin imperial year. It then adds a legitimacy layer: Ying Zheng needs to calm postwar opinion, so Qin's account of the six destroyed states presents conquest as a series of justified responses to betrayal, rebellion, assassination, and treachery.

The transcript closes as the episode begins discussing the imperial language around "朕." This page records only the opening of that language-order branch, because the provided transcript stops before the institutional discussion is complete.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-120-2-qinchao-di-yi-tao-vi-sheji-linian-lno-aeljks2vt1k3wjrizsanirtq]] completes that language-order branch. After unification, Ying Zheng rejects "王" as too small for the new order and adopts "皇帝"; the court distinguishes imperial "制" and "诏"; and [[LiSi|李斯]] with [[QinWangWan|王绾]] help make "朕" the emperor's exclusive self-reference. The same episode links his title design to [[PosthumousNamePolitics|谥号政治]] by abolishing posthumous names, honoring [[QinZhuangxiangwang|嬴异人 / 子楚 / 秦庄襄王]] as 太上皇, and imagining the dynasty as 始皇帝, 二世, 三世, and so on.

The episode also adds the [[YinYangFivePhasesPoliticalTheory|阴阳五行]] and water-virtue layer to his imperial identity. Ying Zheng accepts a Qi-linked theory associated with [[ZouYan|邹衍]], treats Qin as water virtue overcoming Zhou fire, and turns that claim into black as dynastic color, October as year-start, six as symbolic number, 德水 naming, 黔首 language for the people, and a law-and-punishment style. This page records that as [[ImperialSymbolicStandardization|帝国符号标准化]]: a ruler biography turning into a standardized imperial vocabulary and legitimacy system.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-120-3-jiemi-qinshihuang-weihe-faming-junxianzhi-li4bzz54trstteapodnonxzdzkuv]] completes the administrative branch after the symbolic one. When [[QinWangWan|王绾]] proposes enfeoffing imperial sons in distant former Yan, Qi, and Chu territory, Ying Zheng accepts [[LiSi|李斯]]'s warning that renewed hereditary states would plant future war. The episode treats this as [[CommanderyCountyAntiEnfeoffment|郡县制反分封定局]]: Qin Shi Huang preserves the conquest settlement by extending direct commandery-county administration to the whole empire.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-120-4-bing-liuguo-hou-qinshihuang-jiao-ni-ruhe-shou-jiangshan-lgwsd0c3wkl-cphma8sm3ftkowwr]] adds the ruler's post-unification control package. Ying Zheng orders weapons collected and melted into palace figures, verifies weights and measures through official standards, relocates 120,000 powerful households to [[Xianyang|咸阳]], and uses construction and direct local administration to make conquered society answer to the center. The episode treats these measures as [[ImperialHomogenization|帝国整齐划一]] with coercive teeth: Qin Shi Huang wants commands to reach the empire's edges, but the same program deepens resentment among six-state populations already afraid of Qin.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-121-yingzheng-zao-shijie-zuizao-gaosu-gonglu-2200nian-huangcao-busheng-ls1u8dmuvs5bbrzadf2dtd00js-p]] moves the ruler from stationary post-conquest control to ritual travel and transport infrastructure. The episode reads Ying Zheng's first post-unification departure from [[Xianyang|咸阳]] west through [[LongxiCommandery|陇西郡]] and [[BeidiCommandery|北地郡]] less as ordinary inspection than as [[HighTempleRite|高庙礼]] in Qin old lands around [[YongchengQin|雍城]]. It then connects him to [[QinJimiao|极庙]], inferred [[Taiyi|太一]] worship, [[QinCosmicCapitalPlanning|秦代宇宙化都城规划]], and [[QinChidao|驰道]], making imperial movement a ritual, cosmological, and infrastructural problem.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-122-1-jiemi-qinshihuang-fengchan-weishenme-xuan-taishan-lkcyayuqrtsgy-k4bhdvy-l-lapo]] continues that movement into the 219 BCE eastern tour. Qin Shi Huang now travels through former six-state territory, using presence, road preparation, inscriptions, and spectacle as [[ImperialTourPoliticalTheater|帝国巡游政治剧场]]. The source treats his ceremony at [[Taishan|泰山]] and [[LiangfuMountain|梁父山]] as [[FengshanRitualLegitimacy|封禅礼制合法性]]: conquest is recast as [[MandateOfHeavenLegitimacy|天命认可]], even though the ritual procedure is uncertain enough that the consulted Qi-Lu scholars disagree and Qin ultimately relies heavily on its own heaven-sacrifice practice.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-122-2-ribenren-daodi-shi-bushi-xufu-de-houdai-lhgbbhg3zw8rlx1jjb7lpb5ihihh]] continues the same tour into coastal former-Qi territory. Qin Shi Huang worships the [[QiEightSpirits|齐地八神]], stays at [[LangyaMountain|琅琊山]], relocates 30,000 households, and uses the [[LangyaInscriptionPoliticalProgram|琅琊刻石政治纲领]] to proclaim agriculture, law, unified standards, peace, and universal authority. The episode then turns him from public legitimacy-maker into patron of [[XuFu|徐福]]'s [[Penglai|蓬莱]] search, making [[ImmortalityQuestPolitics|求仙政治]] and [[FangshiFraudAndAuthority|方士骗术与权威]] the darker sequel to the sacred-tour program.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-122-3-qinshihuang-jing-he-shenxian-si-qilai-le-ljnw5estg6dvgfllm3kfcq6ulhxu]] follows the return leg of that second tour. After the Langya/Xu Fu branch, Qin Shi Huang fails to recover [[Jiuding|九鼎]] at [[Xuzhou|彭城 / 徐州]], then interprets the storm at [[XiangshanShrine|湘山祠]] as [[XiangJun|湘君]]'s obstruction and orders convicts to strip the mountain. The episode therefore sharpens his profile: the same ruler who can incorporate former-Qi gods can also answer a local Chu sacred obstacle through punitive landscape violence, while the closing [[ZhangLiang|张良]] preview turns imperial travel into the prelude to anti-Qin assassination.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-123-1-shen-zhi-yuyan-wangqin-zhe-hu-jing-shi-zhi-lg7m3vfdbcweb4joxe4jvhxjx6y8]] adds the immediate sequel. In 218 BCE Qin Shi Huang survives [[ZhangLiang|张良]]'s [[Bolangsha|博浪沙]] attack after the assassin hits an accompanying carriage. Rather than stop touring, he continues eastward to Zhifu and Langya, and by 215 BCE he travels to [[Jieshi|碣石]], sends [[LuSheng|卢生]] to seek immortals, removes large local defensive works under [[StrongTrunkWeakBranchCentralization|强干弱枝式集权]], and then reads "亡秦者胡" as a northern threat requiring [[MengTian|蒙恬]]'s campaign against [[Xiongnu|匈奴]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-123-2-gudai-shiqi-de-daochamen-daodi-you-duo-weixian-lgcknwubwm3uzqmzgbgdlxlvcwxy]] reframes Qin Shi Huang's thirty-third year, 214 BCE, through institutions rather than only imperial temperament. The episode says Qin's southern attack on [[Baiyue|百越]] drafts捕亡人,赘婿, and merchants, then creates [[GuilinCommandery|桂林郡]], [[NanhaiCommandery|南海郡]], and [[XiangCommandery|象郡]], while [[MengTian|蒙恬]] defeats [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] in the north, recovers Hetao, links older walls, and sits at [[Shangjun|上郡]]. The page records this as [[PostUnificationWarInertia|统一后战争惯性]]: Qin Shi Huang is acting inside a [[FarmingWarStatecraft|耕战逻辑]] and [[MilitaryMeritRankSystem|军功爵制]] order that still needs military outlets after unification.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-124-fenshu-kengru-wo-zai-bzhan-wei-qinshihuang-pingfan-ljnhwcouqu35rmvb-cnrzyzj2j6l]] moves Qin Shi Huang into the 213 BCE book-burning branch. The source stresses that he approves [[LiSi|李斯]]'s proposal with a terse "可" after [[ChunyuYue|淳于越]] argues for restoring enfeoffment and ancient models. This page records the episode's double framing: the order is a coercive [[QinBookBurningPolicy|秦代焚书令]] that damages culture, especially six-state historical memory, and also an attempt to extend [[ImperialHomogenization|帝国整齐划一]] and [[OfficialLearningMonopoly|学在王官式知识垄断]] into texts, teaching, and political interpretation.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-125-1-qinshihuang-xiujian-shishang-zuida-lanweilou-afanggong-lhqc6jw4jzoyrg7jzc2mbxdebj4]] adds Qin Shi Huang's thirty-fifth-year construction and secrecy branch. The source connects [[QinZhidao|秦直道]], [[AfangPalace|阿房宫]], [[QinShiHuangMausoleum|秦始皇陵]], forced labor, palace networks, and [[Xianyang|咸阳]] expansion into [[ImperialConstructionOverload|帝国工程过载]]. It then returns to [[LuSheng|卢生]]: Qin Shi Huang follows advice to hide his residence, calls himself "真人," and turns immortal-seeking into [[ImperialItinerarySecrecy|帝王行踪保密]], including lethal punishment for leaks. The source stops as he prepares to investigate slanderous儒生, so this page records the坑儒 prelude rather than the completed event.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-125-2-lishishang-qinshihuang-weihe-yao-fenshu-kengru-lulaivzp1xqs71nbaltr-ygpjihw]] completes that branch. After [[HouShengFangshi|侯生]] and [[LuSheng|卢生]] flee and criticize his self-willed, punitive, overcentralized rule, Qin Shi Huang responds by ordering interrogation of Xianyang scholars, personally judging more than 460 people, and having them坑杀 at [[Xianyang|咸阳]]. The episode also adds [[FuSu|扶苏]]'s remonstrance and punishment: when Fusu warns that heavy law against Confucius-following scholars will unsettle the realm, Qin Shi Huang sends him to [[Shangjun|上郡]] under [[MengTian|蒙恬]]'s frontier command.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-126-qinshihuang-siyin-zhimi-1-lgtlhau6ixqglk1ict1fs0jexjwe]] moves Qin Shi Huang into his thirty-sixth year, 211 BCE, and frames his late rule through [[QinLateOmenCrisis|秦始皇晚年异象危机]]. The episode links [[DongCommandery|东郡]]'s meteor inscription, [[ChineseAstrologicalPolitics|荧惑守心]], the returned jade bi, and "今年祖龙死" to public resentment, court anxiety, and the final-tour prelude. It also keeps the source-critical problem visible: the host doubts whether the "始皇" wording should be treated as a direct contemporary phrase, even though the omen narrative remains politically meaningful.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-126-qinshihuang-siyin-zhimi-2-ln15p5qb7lc20goni1ibcpmn3wca]] turns that prelude into Qin Shi Huang's response. The host reads his surface interpretation of "祖龙" as ancestor language against the stronger implication that he knew it meant himself, then connects his divination, the "游徙吉" result, the thirty-thousand-household northern relocation, and the fifth tour into [[OmenCountermeasureMobility|游徙避命]]. [[ZhaoZhengShu|《赵正书》]] is used to support the idea that the last tour was not merely inspection, but an attempt to change fate after a predicted death.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-127-3-haiwai-huigui-yipi-zhujian-jiekai-huhai-lidi-zhi-mi-lja0pankcx4ugtylbxxqkwuavdnw]] returns to Zhao Zheng Shu for Qin Shi Huang's final political decision. In the alternate account, he knows he is dying, tells trusted officials to keep the illness quiet, worries that his sons will be too weak against powerful ministers, hears [[LiSi|李斯]]'s loyalty declaration, and accepts the argument from Li Si and [[FengQuji|冯去疾]] that the nearby [[QinErshi|胡亥 / 秦二世]] should be established as heir. This makes Qin Shi Huang's deathbed a contested source problem: either his will is simulated after death, or he makes an emergency choice before death under severe distance and timing pressure.

## Key Claims
- The source links Qin Shi Huang's pursuit of immortality to eastern-sea immortal-island lore and fangshi promises.
- His use of water-phase symbolism is treated as a political style, not only as private belief.
- Fangshi could gain access and resources from his fear of death, but they also risked punishment when promises failed.
- Omen language around his late reign shows how prophecy could intensify court anxiety.
- Zhouji 98 keeps Ying Zheng as a young child under danger in Handan while the historical narrative shifts from Zhou to Qin.
- Qinji 100-1 records Ying Zheng's return from Handan to Qin after Zichu becomes crown prince and treats later Handan revenge as source-scoped memory politics.
- Qinji 103-1 records Ying Zheng's accession at thirteen inside Qin's continuing late-Warring-States expansion.
- Qinji 103-2 adds that Lu Buwei decides affairs after the accession and frames Qin's internal disorder as the next problem before unification.
- Qinji 104 shows early Ying Zheng preserving the Zhengguo Canal despite its hostile origin, making infrastructure completion part of Qin's road from succession instability to conquest capacity.
- Qinji 106 shows early Ying Zheng's state absorbing locust and epidemic pressure through rank-for-grain mobilization, then resuming expansion into Wei with Dong Commandery.
- Qinji 107 shows the last anti-Qin coalition failing during Ying Zheng's sixth year, with Qin continuing pressure through later attacks and succession notices around Wei, Han, Xia Taihou, and Meng Ao.
- Qinji 108-1 shows Ying Zheng's transition to personal rule colliding with the Lao Ai rebellion, Zhao Ji's punishment, and Mao Jiao's remonstrance, making court feedback and royal-family legitimacy part of his early-rule profile.
- Qinji 108-2 adds a psychological prehistory to the Lao Ai crisis: hostage childhood, minority-rule frustration, and palace humiliation are used to explain why the scandal would feel politically and personally intolerable.
- Qinji 108-3 adds the power-consolidation consequence of the Lao Ai crisis: Ying Zheng removes Lao Ai's faction and Lu Buwei's central authority, then repairs the mother-son rupture after Mao Jiao's advice.
- Qinji 109-1 reinforces the source-critical paternity caution by contrasting a Chu hidden-pregnancy succession plot with the host's explicit rejection of the Lu Buwei paternity rumor.
- Qinji 110-1 adds the tenth-year guest-expulsion crisis, where Ying Zheng accepts a sweeping anti-outsider order after Lu Buwei's removal and Zheng Guo's exposure intensify court-security anxiety.
- Qinji 110-2 adds the reversal: Ying Zheng accepts Li Si's state-interest argument, cancels guest expulsion, restores Li Si, and turns the pair toward the conquest project.
- Qinji 111-1 adds the eleventh-year Zhao campaign: Ying Zheng accepts Yan's warning about Zhao's growth and attacks Zhao while Zhao is occupied against Yan.
- Qinji 111-2 adds the twelfth-year Lu Buwei endpoint: Ying Zheng's relocation order to Shu converts residual reputation into political danger and ends with Lu Buwei's poisoned-wine suicide.
- Qinji 112-1 adds the anti-hezong envoy case: Ying Zheng backs Yao Jia visibly and materially so a mobile persuader can break a four-state coalition plan before it becomes effective.
- Qinji 112-2 adds Ying Zheng as Han Fei's admiring reader, showing how appreciation for Legalist writing can create political access without making the author safe.
- Qinji 112-3 adds that Ying Zheng's admiration and later regret do not protect Han Fei once Yao Jia's defense succeeds and Li Si turns Han Fei's identity into loyalty suspicion.
- Qinji 113 adds the 232-229 BCE escalation from Zhao-front pressure to Han's destruction and Zhao's Li Mu crisis, showing conquest through armies, disaster timing, rumor, bribery, and command replacement.
- Qinji 114-1 adds the 228 BCE Handan endpoint: Ying Zheng captures Zhao's capital, conducts source-scoped revenge against old Handan enemies, leaves Zhao Qian alive in exile, and sends Wang Jian forward against Yan.
- Qinji 114-2 adds the Yan-facing assassination setup: Ying Zheng is treated as the single-person target through whom Yan hopes to disrupt Qin's otherwise overwhelming state pressure.
- Qinji 114-3 adds the access logic around that target: Fan Wuji's head and the Du Kang map are treated as the gifts that could bring Jing Ke physically close to the Qin king.
- Qinji 115-1 adds the palace-reception phase and the strategic counterfactual: Ying Zheng's death might have created turmoil, but the source doubts it would have undone Qin's larger unification machinery.
- Qinji 115-2 adds the survival sequence itself: Ying Zheng is nearly trapped by palace protocol and weapon constraints, survives through small tactical turns, and then converts the failed assassination into an attack on Yan and Dai.
- Qinji 116 adds the post-assassination refusal of appeasement and the Chu troop-estimate decision: Ying Zheng accepts Li Xin's 200,000-person answer over Wang Jian's 600,000-person caution.
- Qinji 117-1 adds Wei's destruction under his conquest project and the Tang Ju-Anling story as a source-critical case of Ying Zheng being rhetorically checked by a weak-side envoy.
- Qinji 117-2 adds the correction after Li Xin's Chu defeat: Ying Zheng returns to Wang Jian's larger troop requirement while Wang Jian manages royal suspicion through performed property desire.
- Qinji 118-1 adds the payoff of that correction: Ying Zheng's lowered posture and acceptance of Wang Jian's scale requirement enable the fortified attrition campaign against Chu.
- Qinji 118-2 adds the personnel-security and remnant-management problem after Chu: Changping Jun's Chu identity becomes suspect, and Qin must decide how hard to pursue displaced elites before they gather in Qi.
- Qinji 119-3 adds the final-geometry frame: Yan/Dai pressure from the north, Jiangnan-Kuaiji expansion in the south, and a Qin celebration that excludes still-independent Qi.
- Qinji 120-1 adds the 221 BCE threshold: Qi surrenders, the Warring States period closes, and Qin begins narrating conquest as justified self-defense and punishment.
- Qinji 120-2 adds the post-unification title and symbol package: Ying Zheng becomes 皇帝, monopolizes imperial language, abolishes谥法, and links Qin rule to water-virtue standards.
- Qinji 120-3 adds the commandery-county settlement: Ying Zheng rejects Wang Wan's princely-enfeoffment proposal and accepts Li Si's direct-administration argument.
- Qinji 120-4 adds the coercive implementation layer: disarmament, measurement inspection, elite relocation, capital display, and forced uniformity become tools for holding the unified empire.
- Qinji 121 adds the first post-unification westward journey, source-scoped as ancestral reporting rather than ordinary inspection, and links Qin Shi Huang's later tours to chidao, ritual architecture, and cosmic capital planning.
- Qinji 122-1 adds the eastern-tour and fengshan branch: Qin Shi Huang claims former six-state space through visible travel, [[Taishan|Taishan]] ritual, heavenly approval, and Qin-shaped ritual improvisation.
- Qinji 122-2 adds the Langya and Xu Fu branch: Qin Shi Huang turns former Qi coastal space into inscription, settlement, local-god sacrifice, and a resource-consuming search for immortal medicine.
- Qinji 122-3 adds the Xiangshan branch: failed Jiuding recovery, anger at Xiangjun, and the cutting of Xiangshan trees show Qin Shi Huang's sacred-political touring can move from reverent incorporation into coercive humiliation of a local Chu deity space.
- Qinji 123-1 adds Bolangsha survival, continued eastern touring, the Jieshi-Lu Sheng immortal search, local fortification removal, and the "亡秦者胡" reading that points him toward a northern campaign.
- Qinji 123-2 adds the 214 BCE southern/northern campaign frame: Baiyue commandery setup, marginal-status conscription, Meng Tian's Xiongnu victory and wall integration, and the institutional claim that Qin's war engine could not easily switch to peace.
- Qinji 124 adds the 213 BCE book-burning branch: Qin Shi Huang accepts Li Si's plan after a revived enfeoffment critique, making textual control part of post-unification rule while the source keeps cultural loss visible.
- Qinji 125-1 adds the 212 BCE late-rule construction and secrecy branch: Afang Palace, the Lishan mausoleum, Qin Straight Road, hidden movement, leak executions, and fangshi accountability pressure lead into the坑儒 prelude.
- Qinji 125-2 adds the completed坑儒 branch: fangshi flight, court feedback collapse, Xianyang interrogation, 460-plus executions, Fusu's remonstrance, and Fusu's dispatch to Meng Tian at Shangjun.
- Qinji 126 part 1 adds the 211 BCE omen crisis: meteor inscription, 荧惑守心, returned jade bi, and "祖龙" prophecy turn public resentment and death anxiety into the setup for Qin Shi Huang's final tour.
- Qinji 126 part 2 adds the response layer: Qin Shi Huang's "游徙吉" divination links northern household relocation and the fifth tour to a source-scoped attempt to alter the death prophecy.
