# Chen Tianqiao / 陈天桥

[[no-202-shengdade-shengda-chuanqide-chuanqi-zhongguo-hulianwang-gushi-20-977833518]] adds Chen Tianqiao / 陈天桥 as the founder of [[Shanda|盛大]] and the key operator behind [[LegendOfMir|《传奇》]]'s China breakout. The episode follows him from Fudan, Lujiazui Group, and Jin Xin Trust into a 1999 startup with [[ChenDanian]] and [[LuoQianqian]], then through the Legend agency bet, [[InternetCafePointCardDistribution]], Shanda's Nasdaq listing, and his rise as a young Chinese internet billionaire.

The source presents Chen as a founder with unusually strong timing instincts and resource-integration ability, but not as a simple genius narrative. [[ShandaBox|盛大盒子]], the [[Sina|新浪]] stake purchase, [[ShandaLiterature|盛大文学]], [[ShandaGames|盛大游戏]], and [[ShandaInnovationInstitute|盛大创新院]] show a repeated [[FounderVisionTimingMismatch]] pattern: many directions were early or directionally right, but infrastructure, regulation, product fit, channel control, and organization did not always support them.

The same episode adds Chen's post-Shanda arc through [[FounderHealthStopSign]]. Panic disorder, cancer, relocation to Singapore, Buddhist practice, investment work, and neuroscience philanthropy are treated as a forced interruption of the high-speed founder life rather than just a retirement story.

Chen Tianqiao also appears in [[e242-zuikuai-bannian-ai-paotong-zi-jinhua-yu-chen-tianqiao-shouxi-kexuejia-liaoliao-guigu-moxing-bi-zheng-zhi-di]] as the strategic figure behind [[Apodex]]. The source says he funded, founded, and actively directs the company, with a strong preference for Heavy Duty Solver work over consumer chat, image generation, or video generation.

The episode presents Chen's influence as both organizational and model-level. [[LiBeibin]] says Chen frequently discusses strategy with the team and corrects research drift when academic instincts diverge from the company's broader direction. He also says Apodex's model constitution is largely shaped by Chen, including an emphasis on truthfulness and on decomposing hard problems before rushing into search.
