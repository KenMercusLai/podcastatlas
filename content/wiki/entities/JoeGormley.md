# Joe Gormley

## Overview
Joe Gormley appears as the pragmatic miners' leader caught between government wage restraint and members demanding a major pay rise before the 1974 strike.

## Current Profile
[[417-britain-in-1974-state-of-emergency-part-1-glt6761791949]] uses Gormley to challenge the assumption that a union leader could privately accept the national-interest case and then deliver compliance. His members sought a 35% rise after the miners' 1972 victory, while the oil shock increased coal's strategic importance and made their bargaining position stronger.

## Key Characteristics
- Presented as pragmatic rather than ideologically maximalist.
- Accountable to a large membership expecting recovery in miners' pay.
- Led during an overtime ban that escalated into a national energy emergency.
- Illustrates the fragmented authority within British labour bargaining.

## Evidence
- Member constraint: [[417-britain-in-1974-state-of-emergency-part-1-glt6761791949]] says miners demanded a 35% rise and emphasizes that union leaders had to answer to those members.
- Strategic leverage: [[417-britain-in-1974-state-of-emergency-part-1-glt6761791949]] links the oil-price shock to coal's rising value and to the government's declaration of emergency after the overtime ban.

## Qualifications
The episode focuses on the confrontation as seen through Heath's political crisis. It does not provide a full institutional history of the National Union of Mineworkers or distinguish all regional and rank-and-file positions.

## What Changed
- Created Gormley's profile as a constrained intermediary rather than an all-powerful union commander.
