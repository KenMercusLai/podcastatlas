# Jason Cowley

## Overview

Jason Cowley is the editor of *The New Statesman* and author of *Who Are We Now? Stories of Modern England*, presented in the source as an interpreter of modern English identity.

## Current Profile

In [[180-england-englishness-glt7388885434]], Cowley argues that Englishness has become more visible since devolution while remaining institutionally ambiguous inside the [[UnitedKingdom]]. He resists treating it as inherently reactionary and looks for a progressive English identity capable of holding historical continuity, immigration, black English belonging, landscape, football, and democratic conflict together.

His method is organized around paradox. [[London]] is both national capital and global city; Britishness can be plural and inclusive while leaving some English places unrepresented; countryside imagery remains powerful in a suburban and urban nation; and [[Brexit]] can be disruptive while also registering democratic pressure from below. His phrase “changing changelessness” summarizes an identity altered by history yet connected to a recognizable past.

## Key Characteristics

- Editor and writer focused here on modern England and national identity.
- Treats Englishness as contested, layered, and capable of progressive reinterpretation.
- Uses reporting, place, cultural symbols, electoral change, and personal anecdote rather than a single institutional definition.
- Reads football and the England team as unusually visible expressions of national change.
- Preserves tensions between Englishness and Britishness rather than collapsing them.
