# Nanjing Massacre / 南京大屠杀

## Overview

The Nanjing Massacre was the mass murder, sexual violence, plunder, arson, and terror committed by Japanese forces after they occupied Nanjing in December 1937.

## Current Profile

[[236-china-and-world-war-ii-part-2-glt5290070743]] treats Nanjing as one of the most horrific war crimes of the modern era and as an early demonstration of the wider brutality that would mark the Second World War. The violence lasted roughly six weeks, while civilians who remained after the Chinese military withdrawal did not anticipate its scale.

The wider profile includes the collapse after Shanghai, the retreat and abandonment of Nanjing, execution of captured or suspected soldiers, and the documentary role of the Nanking International Safety Zone. [[JohnRabe]] and other foreign residents left diaries, letters, telegrams, appeals, and reports that support the wider record of killing, rape, torture, looting, and arson. [[dchha63-supernova-in-the-east-ii-dchha63-supernova-in-the-east-ii|Supernova in the East II]] is the bounded source for those details.

Both sources discuss military brutalization, hierarchy, internal physical abuse, resentment after unexpectedly hard Chinese resistance, and command permission as possible contributors. These explanations do not displace responsibility or establish a single sufficient cause. Disagreement over exact totals, duration, terminology, or particular stories also does not make denial of the central atrocity record reasonable.

## Key Characteristics

- It followed the Japanese occupation of Nanjing in December 1937.
- It involved extensive murder, sexual violence, plunder, and terror against civilians and prisoners.
- Contemporary Safety Zone records document violence and repeated efforts to protect civilians.
- The sources treat the atrocity as Japanese military responsibility while discussing several possible enabling conditions.
- Exact casualty totals remain uncertain and should not be collapsed into one settled number.
- Evidentiary qualification does not justify minimization or denial of the wider event.

## Evidence

- Violence and duration: [[236-china-and-world-war-ii-part-2-glt5290070743]] describes roughly six weeks of mass killing, rape, plunder, and extreme brutality from mid-December 1937 into January 1938.
- Safety Zone documentation: [[dchha63-supernova-in-the-east-ii-dchha63-supernova-in-the-east-ii]] identifies diaries, letters, telegrams, reports, and appeals from [[JohnRabe]] and other foreign residents.
- Responsibility and explanation: both [[236-china-and-world-war-ii-part-2-glt5290070743]] and [[dchha63-supernova-in-the-east-ii-dchha63-supernova-in-the-east-ii]] place responsibility on Japanese forces while considering brutalization, hierarchy, frustration, abuse, and command license.
- Historical significance: [[236-china-and-world-war-ii-part-2-glt5290070743]] and [[dchha63-supernova-in-the-east-ii-dchha63-supernova-in-the-east-ii]] present Nanjing as a central Chinese wartime trauma and a window onto the wider mass violence of the Second World War.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to two narrative podcast episodes rather than an archive or systematic atrocity study. The sources stress uncertainty and the wiki does not adopt a precise casualty total. The reported beheading contest, duration, terminology, command mechanisms, and individual allegations remain source-scoped; comparative references to other armies are context, not equivalence or mitigation.
