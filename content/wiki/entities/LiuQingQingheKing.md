## Overview

清河王刘庆 / Liu Qing now appears as a displaced crown prince whose later caution has a concrete origin. Hanji 751 shows him born to [[SongGuirenZhangHeLateHan|Song Guiren]], deposed from crown prince to Qinghe king after [[EmpressDowagerDouHeLateHan|Empress Dou]] moves succession toward [[EmperorHeOfHan|Liu Zhao]], and then surviving through silence and careful brotherly conduct. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-766-lishi-jiaogei-ni-weishenme-yao-guyi-fa-piqi-lrs-mguyfmnxe970hdwjvmevl8oc|Hanji 766]] shows him helping Emperor He during the anti-Dou operation; Hanji 767 presents him as a rewarded but careful ally after the purge; Hanji 770 shows him requesting ritual support for Song Guiren after Liang birth-mother redress. The page is intentionally disambiguated from the modern [[LiuQing|刘擎]] page.

## Current Profile

Hanji 751 supplies the origin layer. Liu Qing is the son of Song Guiren and is first established as crown prince. Empress Dou has no son and raises Liu Zhao, the son of [[LiangGuirenHeLateHan|Liang Guiren]], so Liu Qing's position becomes the obstacle to a Dou foster-maternal succession. After Empress Dou's accusation against the Song sisters succeeds, [[EmperorZhangOfHan|Emperor Zhang]] removes Liu Qing from the crown-prince palace, demotes him to Qinghe king, and makes Liu Zhao crown prince.

The same source makes Liu Qing's later caution legible. Liu Qing is young but knows to avoid suspicion and not speak about the case. Zhangdi gives him clothing like Liu Zhao's, and Liu Zhao remains friendly with him, sharing room and carriage, but the episode stresses that equal clothing and brotherly closeness do not restore equal political status.

Hanji 766 supplies the later operational layer. Liu Qing is close to Emperor He from childhood and often enters the palace, but his own mother's forced death under Dou Taihou teaches him restraint under palace danger. When Emperor He fears that ordinary attendants may leak the anti-Dou plan, he asks Liu Qing to borrow historical material from Liu Kang and later summons Liu Qing privately to pass Zheng Zhong the task of gathering precedents about emperors punishing maternal uncles.

Hanji 767 supplies the post-operation political-survival layer. Liu Qing has helped Emperor He remove the Dou family and receives large rewards in slaves, carriages, horses, gold, silver, and valuables. When he is ill, Emperor He sends people morning and evening to ask after him and provides food and medicine. Yet Liu Qing remains cautious because childhood upheaval, earlier displacement, and his mother's death under Dou power taught him how easily favor can become danger.

Hanji 770 places Liu Qing after [[LiangGuirenHeLateHan|Liang Guiren]] has been posthumously redressed. The source says Liu Qing's mother Song Guiren had also been forced to death by [[EmpressDowagerDouHeLateHan|Dou Taihou]], but only after the Liang case is recognized does Liu Qing dare to request sacrifices and grave care for his mother.

[[EmperorHeOfHan|Emperor He]] immediately grants the request and orders the imperial kitchen to provide seasonal offerings in spring, summer, autumn, and winter. Liu Qing is deeply moved because he had been unable to care for his mother while she was alive but can now finally sacrifice to her. The source also says he wants to build a shrine for Song Guiren but never asks, fearing that it might look like self-comparison with Liang's higher maternal status.

Liu Qing's current role is therefore a secondary but important witness to [[ImperialBirthMotherRecognition|imperial birth-mother recognition]]. His case shows how one redress can create a permission structure for another suppressed maternal grievance while still leaving status anxieties and limits.

## Key Characteristics

- Displaced crown prince whose demotion to Qinghe king begins under Empress Dou's succession pressure.
- Son of Song Guiren, whose accusation and death shape his political caution.
- Weakly backed imperial child whose removal models vulnerability under stronger maternal houses.
- Qinghe king and imperial relative trusted by Emperor He during the secret anti-Dou operation.
- Book-borrowing and relay actor who helps turn history into crisis planning.
- Rewarded ally after the Dou-family purge who remains cautious despite Emperor He's visible favor.
- Requests and receives official seasonal sacrifices for Song Guiren only after Liang Guiren's redress creates an opening.

## Qualifications

This page is source-bounded to Hanji 751, Hanji 766, Hanji 767, and Hanji 770 and does not reconstruct Liu Qing's full royal career, relation to later emperors, or Qinghe Kingdom's full history. Hanji 751 supplies the original deposition and survival posture; Hanji 766 and Hanji 767's psychological reading of childhood trauma is kept source-scoped, while Hanji 770's ritual-redress case remains the concrete later action. The route uses a semantic suffix because `LiuQing.md` is already occupied by the modern scholar 刘擎.
