## Overview

清河王刘庆 / Liu Qing enters the wiki as the imperial kinsman whose caution links the fall of the Dou family to later maternal ritual redress. Hanji 767 presents him as a rewarded but careful ally after [[EmperorHeOfHan|Emperor He]] removes the Dou clan; Hanji 770 then shows him using the Liang birth-mother redress opening to request ritual support for his own mother [[SongGuirenZhangHeLateHan|Song Guiren]]. The page is intentionally disambiguated from the modern [[LiuQing|刘擎]] page.

## Current Profile

Hanji 767 supplies the earlier political-survival layer. Liu Qing has helped Emperor He remove the Dou family and receives large rewards in slaves, carriages, horses, gold, silver, and valuables. When he is ill, Emperor He sends people morning and evening to ask after him and provides food and medicine. Yet Liu Qing remains cautious because his childhood upheaval, earlier displacement, and his mother's death under Dou power taught him how easily favor can become danger.

Hanji 770 places Liu Qing after [[LiangGuirenHeLateHan|Liang Guiren]] has been posthumously redressed. The source says Liu Qing's mother Song Guiren had also been forced to death by [[EmpressDowagerDouHeLateHan|Dou Taihou]], but only after the Liang case is recognized does Liu Qing dare to request sacrifices and grave care for his mother.

[[EmperorHeOfHan|Emperor He]] immediately grants the request and orders the imperial kitchen to provide seasonal offerings in spring, summer, autumn, and winter. Liu Qing is deeply moved because he had been unable to care for his mother while she was alive but can now finally sacrifice to her. The source also says he wants to build a shrine for Song Guiren but never asks, fearing that it might look like self-comparison with Liang's higher maternal status.

Liu Qing's current role is therefore a secondary but important witness to [[ImperialBirthMotherRecognition|imperial birth-mother recognition]]. His case shows how one redress can create a permission structure for another suppressed maternal grievance while still leaving status anxieties and limits.

## Key Characteristics

- Qinghe king and imperial relative whose mother Song Guiren had been forced to death by Dou Taihou in Hanji 770.
- Rewarded ally after the Dou-family purge who remains cautious despite Emperor He's visible favor.
- Trauma-shaped survivor whose childhood displacement and mother's death inform his later restraint.
- Requests seasonal sacrifices for Song Guiren only after Liang Guiren's redress creates an opening.
- Receives Emperor He's immediate approval for official offerings to his mother.
- Shows how maternal-status repair can spread from an emperor's birth mother to a royal relative's mother.
- Keeps a self-limiting boundary by not asking to build a shrine that might imply parity with Liang Guiren.

## Qualifications

This page is source-bounded to Hanji 767 and Hanji 770 and does not reconstruct Liu Qing's full royal career, relation to later emperors, or Qinghe Kingdom's full history. Hanji 767's psychological reading of childhood trauma is kept source-scoped, while Hanji 770's ritual-redress case remains the concrete later action. The route uses a semantic suffix because `LiuQing.md` is already occupied by the modern scholar 刘擎.
