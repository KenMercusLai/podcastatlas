## Overview

Charles VIII was the French king whose 1494 invasion of Italy toppled Medici dominance in Florence and appeared to fulfill [[GirolamoSavonarola|Savonarola]]'s warnings of divine punishment.

## Current Profile

The episode grounds Charles's campaign in an Angevin claim to Naples and encouragement from Pope Innocent VIII and Ludovico Sforza. His large army and powerful artillery exposed the vulnerability of Italian states accustomed to a different scale and tempo of war.

Charles used commercial pressure against Florence after [[PieroDeMedici|Piero]] refused safe passage. As French forces approached, Piero conceded fortresses, Pisa, Livorno, and money. Savonarola addressed Charles as an instrument of God's justice and sought leniency; Charles entered Florence, accepted a subsidy, recognized the new regime, and continued toward Naples.

His significance exceeded his military presence. Savonarola identified him with a new Cyrus who would renew Italy, but Charles neither completed that renewal nor restored Pisa. His retreat and truce with the Holy League left Florence isolated and weakened the prophecy that had made him providential.

## Key Characteristics

- French monarch claiming Naples through Angevin ancestry.
- Invader whose army and artillery intensified the Italian Wars.
- User of commercial exclusion as well as military pressure.
- Immediate external cause of Piero's capitulation and Medici expulsion.
- Negotiator who spared Florence in return for recognition and subsidy.
- Unwilling or unable to fulfill the role Savonarola assigned him.

## Qualifications
