# United States Senate

## Overview
The United States Senate enters the wiki as the institutional setting for the All-In episode's discussion of shutdown votes, filibuster rules, bipartisan relationships, and extremely expensive statewide races.

## Current Profile
The source portrays the Senate as a strained but still useful cross-party institution. [[JohnFetterman|John Fetterman]] and [[DaveMcCormick|Dave McCormick]] argue that keeping the government open is a basic duty, while both treat the filibuster as frustrating but protective because it forces negotiation in a polarized chamber. The episode also shows the Senate as an expensive reputational battleground where hundreds of millions of dollars can be spent in a single race.

## Key Characteristics
- Chamber where government-shutdown votes become a test of basic institutional function.
- Filibuster rule makes majority action harder while preserving minority leverage.
- Statewide representation can force senators from opposing parties to cooperate on local priorities.
- Senate races can become extremely expensive media and reputation contests.
- Institutional legitimacy depends on visible problem-solving amid low public trust.
