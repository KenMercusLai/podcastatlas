# Shire folk

## Overview

Shire folk is the Economist audio investigation introduced by [[trailer-shire-folk-6aa271d1a6e9aef4cc74ef3b]] and developed in [[the-weekend-intelligence-shire-folk-6aa44797a6e9aef4ccfde9bc]].

## Current Profile

The trailer positions the series as [[GeorgiaBanjo]]'s six-month investigation into [[TommyRobinson]] and his influence beyond British mainstream media. The first full episode, reported by Banjo and Sam Westron, supplies the origin layer: it moves between [[Texas]] and [[Luton]] to show how American sentimental attachment to Britain, local British decline, Islamist fringe activism, football-hooligan culture, and the War on Terror converged around Robinson.

As a wiki node, the series matters because it links a British far-right figure to American political attention without flattening the local evidence. It frames [[UnitedKingdom|Britain]] as a cautionary tale in a wider battle to save "the West," while the full episode argues that Robinson's power comes from [[SelectiveThreatNarrativeEditing]]: he uses real problems but turns them into a narrower anti-Muslim story.

## Key Characteristics

- Economist audio investigation introduced through a short trailer and expanded in a three-part Weekend Intelligence series.
- Reported by [[GeorgiaBanjo]].
- Focused on [[TommyRobinson]]'s influence, media exclusion, and American symbolic value.
- Opens with a London rally where [[ElonMusk]] speaks with Robinson about coming violence and fighting back.
- Uses Luton as the first full episode's origin setting for Robinson, the [[EnglishDefenceLeague]], and selective anti-Muslim storytelling.
- Uses Britain as the national case for a wider Western-decline warning narrative.

## Evidence

- Series premise: [[trailer-shire-folk-6aa271d1a6e9aef4cc74ef3b]] says Banjo spent six months investigating Robinson's rise as a prophet for some powerful Americans.
- Opening scene: [[trailer-shire-folk-6aa271d1a6e9aef4cc74ef3b]] begins at a London rally where Musk appears on screen with Robinson.
- Origin layer: [[the-weekend-intelligence-shire-folk-6aa44797a6e9aef4ccfde9bc]] reconstructs Luton's industrial decline, organised crime, Muslim anti-war protest, Al-Muhajiroun presence, and EDL formation.
- Transatlantic bridge: [[the-weekend-intelligence-shire-folk-6aa44797a6e9aef4ccfde9bc]] uses Brent Money in Texas as an example of an American right-wing audience imagining Britain through Islam-threat language.

## Qualifications

The first full episode is still part one of a larger series. Later instalments are needed for the grooming-gangs branch, Robinson's later legal and political comeback, and the American network beyond the Brent Money example. The title is now treated as "Shire folk" because both the trailer metadata and full episode title use that form.
