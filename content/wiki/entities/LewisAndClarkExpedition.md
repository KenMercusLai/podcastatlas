# Lewis and Clark Expedition

## Overview
The Lewis and Clark expedition appears as the exploration project [[ThomasJefferson|Thomas Jefferson]] commissioned after the [[LouisianaPurchase|Louisiana Purchase]] to reach westward toward the Pacific.

## Current Profile
The episode treats the expedition as an extension of Jefferson's mixed intellectual and political character. It expresses scientific curiosity, geographical ambition, and Enlightenment habits, but it also belongs to a presidential expansion project with imperial consequences. Within the Jefferson branch, the expedition is therefore not only exploration; it is the outward movement of a republic whose liberty language coexisted with conquest and slavery.

## Key Characteristics
- Jefferson-commissioned westward exploration project.
- Connected to the Louisiana Purchase and the enlarged U.S. territorial horizon.
- Presented as combining scientific inquiry with imperial ambition.
- Part of Jefferson's presidential legacy rather than only his intellectual interests.
- Helps show how Enlightenment curiosity and state expansion can reinforce each other.
