# Bill Maris

[[BillMaris|Bill Maris]] is the guest in [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-bill-maris-how-google-could-crush-ai-competitors-why-small-funds-win-and-ais-atari-stage-41586010]]. The source presents him as the founding CEO of [[GoogleVentures|Google Ventures]], founder of [[Section32|Section 32]], and a venture investor whose philosophy combines founder contrarianism, computer-science methods, fund-size discipline, and interest in AI and life sciences.

The episode uses Maris's early data-center company to explain his founder filter: people who see a future early may look strange before the market catches up. It then uses his Google and Section 32 experience to argue that venture returns depend on concentrated attention, fund construction, and willingness to apply machine learning or simulation to investment questions.

## Source Position
- Maris says venture investors should look for founders who know a future secret most people do not believe yet.
- He treats [[VentureComputerScienceEdge]] as a practical advantage, not a slogan: portfolio construction, fund sizing, and deal analysis can be modeled and backtested.
- He argues that [[VentureFundSizeDiscipline]] matters because fund size determines check size, stage focus, required exit value, and GP/LP incentive alignment.
- In AI, he sees [[GoogleAITokenPriceLeverage]] as a plausible competitive move because [[Google]] has cash, infrastructure, and [[Gemini]] distribution.
- In biology, he is optimistic about [[ComputationalBiology]] but cautious about claims that AI removes clinical, regulatory, and human-safety bottlenecks.
