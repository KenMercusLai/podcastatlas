# Netherlands

## Overview

The Netherlands appears in the wiki as a modern country whose sources connect early-modern revolt, republican symbolism, maritime trade and finance, assisted-death law, and the dangers of administrative expertise under occupation.

## Current Profile

The early-modern branch is the strongest historical layer. One sequence follows the [[LowCountries|Low Countries]], [[WilliamOfOrange|William of Orange]], [[HetWilhelmus|Het Wilhelmus]], [[LoyalRebellion]], and the [[DutchRevolt]] that produces the [[DutchRepublic]]. The Elizabethan sources then show the same revolt from England's strategic angle: the [[ActOfAbjuration]], Anjou's failed French solution, the [[FrenchFury]], William's assassination, Farnese's southern recovery, Antwerp's fall, and the [[TreatyOfNonsuch]] all make the Dutch theatre part of Anglo-Spanish escalation.

The cultural-identity layer centers on the [[MaidOfHolland|Maid of Holland]]. The fenced garden, liberty cap, Dutch lion, spear, and mop connect independence to protected territory and domestic order. [[DutchDomesticCleanlinessCivicOrder]] then links housework, streets, cities, dikes, and religious discipline, while [[CommercialWealthDomesticMoralization]] explains how disciplined households could make commercial abundance appear compatible with virtue.

Other sources keep the country from collapsing into one origin story. The maritime Japan source presents Dutch sailors and traders as Protestant rivals to Iberian Catholic powers and later beneficiaries of Japan's narrow Dutch trading window. The assisted-death source uses the Netherlands as a physician-administered voluntary euthanasia comparison case. The IBM/Nazi source presents the Netherlands as a highly recorded occupation setting where population-registration expertise becomes part of [[DataEnabledPersecution|data-enabled persecution]].

## Key Characteristics

- Modern country whose early state-formation story is rooted in the Dutch Revolt and the northern outcome of the Low Countries crisis.
- National-symbol branch anchored by Het Wilhelmus, William of Orange, and the Maid of Holland's gendered liberty-and-housekeeping imagery.
- Commercial and urban branch connecting Amsterdam, finance, overseas trade, domestic art, and the moral representation of prosperity.
- Early-modern maritime branch linking Dutch Protestant sailors, Iberian rivalry, and the later [[SakokuDutchWindow]] in Japan.
- Assisted-death branch contrasting Dutch voluntary euthanasia with Swiss self-administered assisted suicide.
- Occupation-data branch treating Dutch registration capacity as a warning about administrative expertise under violent rule.

## Qualifications

The page is a cross-domain country synthesis, not a complete Netherlands history. The revolt, anthem, and Maid branches are early-modern narrative and cultural history; the assisted-death, maritime, and occupation-data branches come from different shows, periods, and analytic concerns. Claims about exceptional cleanliness, its Calvinist cause, and domestic art's moral function remain interpretive and source-scoped; they should not be generalized to every Dutch household or used to hide colonial wealth, class difference, or unequal domestic labor.

## What Changed

- Added the Maid of Holland as a second national-symbol branch alongside Het Wilhelmus.
- Connected domestic cleanliness and housekeeping to civic order, water defense, commercial prosperity, and gendered identity.
- Added explicit limits on exceptionalist, religious-causal, colonial, and household-generalization claims.
