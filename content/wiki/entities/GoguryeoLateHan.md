## Overview

高句丽 / Goguryeo enters the wiki as a late-Han northeastern frontier polity whose 121-122 CE attack on Xuantu becomes a test case for ritualized restraint after royal death. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-797-gudai-zhongxiao-zhende-wufa-liangquan-ma-lru9shbwfwwr9lluhhd7-xjuall6|Hanji 797]] shows Goguryeo threatening Han territory, suffering defeat after Buyeo assists Han, undergoing succession, and then submitting after [[ChenZhongLateHan|陈忠]] persuades [[EmperorAnOfHan|汉安帝]] not to attack during mourning.

## Current Profile

The source's first Goguryeo layer comes in the twelfth month of 121 CE. The Goguryeo king leads Mahan, Mo, and other cavalry to surround Xuantu commandery, but the Buyeo king sends his son Weichoutai with more than twenty thousand troops to support Han. Goguryeo is defeated, and the king later dies.

After the king's death, Suicheng succeeds him. [[YaoGuangLateHan|姚光]], the Xuantu administrator, proposes attacking Goguryeo during the royal mourning period, and many court discussants support the opportunity. Chen Zhong rejects the plan as unrighteous and advises condolence, rebuke for earlier offenses, and pardon. Emperor An accepts that advice.

The same source later records that Suicheng returns captured people and livestock and submits to Xuantu, while Mo also submits. The episode does not prove that restraint alone produced submission, but it makes ritual diplomacy part of the pacification sequence.

## Key Characteristics

- Northeastern frontier opponent able to attack Xuantu with allied cavalry.
- Polity whose succession and mourning create a strategic choice for Han.
- Object of both opportunistic attack proposals and ritual-diplomatic restraint.
- Later recorded as returning captives and livestock and submitting to Xuantu.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 797. It does not reconstruct Goguryeo's full dynastic history, the identity of Mahan and Mo in broader Korean-Manchurian history, Buyeo's longer relationship with Han, or the later durability of Suicheng's submission. The source supports treating Goguryeo as an active frontier actor and a ritual-diplomacy test case, not as a passive object of Han policy.

## What Changed

- Created a source-bounded Goguryeo entity for the 121-122 CE Xuantu attack, succession, and submission sequence.
- Distinguished Goguryeo from broader generic frontier pressure by linking it to mourning-restraint diplomacy.
