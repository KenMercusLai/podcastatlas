## Overview

高句丽 / Goguryeo enters the wiki as a late-Han northeastern frontier polity whose current evidence now begins with a 33 CE tribute-and-title notice under [[LiuXiu|Liu Xiu]], then moves to a 105 CE Liaodong raid and Han counterattack under [[EmperorHeOfHan|Emperor He]], and later develops into a 121-122 CE Xuantu attack and mourning-diplomacy test under [[EmperorAnOfHan|Emperor An]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-708-jizun-bingshi-weihe-yinfa-baiguan-jingkong-lhfuvjyiiirlaf-ugrufkaspuauq|Hanji 708]] shows recognition-backed status management, Hanji 776 shows Goguryeo as an active raider defeated by [[GengKuiLateHan|Geng Kui]], and Hanji 797 shows a later succession moment where [[ChenZhongLateHan|陈忠]] blocks opportunistic attack during royal mourning.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-708-jizun-bingshi-weihe-yinfa-baiguan-jingkong-lhfuvjyiiirlaf-ugrufkaspuauq|Hanji 708]] supplies the earliest current Goguryeo layer. In 33 CE, Goguryeo offers tribute, and Liu Xiu restores its royal title. The notice is compact, but it makes Goguryeo part of the early Guangwu recognition order rather than only a later raiding opponent.

Hanji 776 supplies the next Goguryeo layer. In 105 CE, Goguryeo king Gong invades the Liaodong frontier and plunders six subordinate counties. Liaodong administrator Geng Kui counterattacks in autumn and defeats the Goguryeo force. The source does not yet turn this into a diplomatic settlement; it is a compact raid-and-response notice.

Hanji 797 then gives the fuller 121-122 CE layer. The Goguryeo king leads Mahan, Mo, and other cavalry to surround Xuantu commandery, but the Buyeo king sends his son Weichoutai with more than twenty thousand troops to support Han. Goguryeo is defeated, and the king later dies.

After the king's death, Suicheng succeeds him. [[YaoGuangLateHan|姚光]], the Xuantu administrator, proposes attacking Goguryeo during the royal mourning period, and many court discussants support the opportunity. Chen Zhong rejects the plan as unrighteous and advises condolence, rebuke for earlier offenses, and pardon. Emperor An accepts that advice.

The same source later records that Suicheng returns captured people and livestock and submits to Xuantu, while Mo also submits. The episode does not prove that restraint alone produced submission, but it makes ritual diplomacy part of the pacification sequence.

## Key Characteristics

- Northeastern frontier opponent able to raid Liaodong and later attack Xuantu with allied cavalry.
- Polity whose current evidence begins with tribute and restored royal title under Liu Xiu.
- Polity whose current evidence includes both military counterattack and later ritual-diplomatic restraint.
- Polity whose succession and mourning create a strategic choice for Han.
- Object of both opportunistic attack proposals and ritual-diplomatic restraint.
- Later recorded as returning captives and livestock and submitting to Xuantu.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to the listed source notes. Hanji 708 gives a tribute/title notice, not a full account of Goguryeo's early royal hierarchy or later allegiance. It does not reconstruct Goguryeo's full dynastic history, the identity of king Gong, Mahan and Mo in broader Korean-Manchurian history, Buyeo's longer relationship with Han, or the later durability of Suicheng's submission. The sources support treating Goguryeo as an active frontier actor and a ritual-diplomacy test case, not as a passive object of Han policy.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 708 as the 33 CE tribute-and-title layer under Liu Xiu.
- Added Hanji 776 as the earliest current Goguryeo layer: king Gong's 105 CE Liaodong raid and Geng Kui's counterattack.
- Preserved Hanji 797 as the fuller 121-122 CE Xuantu, succession, mourning-restraint, and submission layer.
- Distinguished immediate military response from later ritual-diplomatic restraint.

## Relationships

- [[LiuXiu|光武帝刘秀]] - ruler who restores Goguryeo's royal title after tribute in Hanji 708.
- [[EmperorHeOfHan|汉和帝]] - ruler whose late reign includes the 105 CE Goguryeo raid and Han counterattack.
- [[GengKuiLateHan|耿夔]] - Liaodong administrator who defeats Goguryeo after the 105 CE raid.
- [[EmperorAnOfHan|汉安帝]] - ruler who accepts Chen Zhong's advice not to attack Goguryeo during royal mourning.
- [[ChenZhongLateHan|陈忠]] - adviser who reframes the Goguryeo response from opportunistic attack to condolence-backed rebuke.
- [[YaoGuangLateHan|姚光]] - Xuantu administrator who wants to attack Goguryeo during mourning.
- [[MourningDiplomaticRestraint|趁丧不伐]] - concept illustrated by Han's decision not to exploit Goguryeo royal mourning militarily.
- [[SmallStateBufferDiplomacy|小国缓冲外交]] - adjacent frontier pattern because Goguryeo, Buyeo, Mahan, and Mo appear as linked peripheral actors around Han commanderies.
- [[RecognitionBackedFrontierDiplomacy|名义承认支撑边疆外交]] - related pattern for using status gestures and submission rather than conquest alone.
