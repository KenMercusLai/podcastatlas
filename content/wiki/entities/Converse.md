# Converse / 匡威

Converse / 匡威 is the classic canvas-footwear brand analyzed in [[vans-kuangwei-fengguang-buzai-jingdian-fanbuxie-weishenme-maibudongle-1006742982]] alongside [[Vans]]. The source traces Converse from a 1908 American rubber-shoe company into canvas rubber shoes, early basketball footwear, the 1917 All Star predecessor, and the later youth-culture afterlife of its flat canvas silhouette.

[[ChuckTaylor|Chuck Taylor]] is central to the source's Converse history. The episode says he wore the shoes through basketball training camps, brought player feedback into product improvements, and had his signature added to the shoe patch in 1934.

[[Nike]] acquired Converse in 2003 after bankruptcy restructuring. The source says Converse later grew to more than $2.4 billion in fiscal 2023 revenue, helped in China by demand for Chuck 70, but then declined for 12 consecutive quarters and fell below $1.2 billion in the most recent fiscal year described by the episode.

## Key Claims
- Converse moved from basketball utility into beach, rock, skate, street, and youth identity.
- Its cultural value depended partly on being cheap, durable, worn-in, and available for reinterpretation by musicians and young consumers.
- The same broad mainstream adoption now weakens the brand's distinction because buyers compare it against ordinary basics on comfort, price, and use case.
- The source says Nike management acknowledged heavy dependence on Chuck Taylor All-Star while other product lines have been harder to grow.
