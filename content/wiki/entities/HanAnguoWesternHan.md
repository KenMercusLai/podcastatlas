## Overview

韩安国 / Han Anguo is presented as a Western Han official whose career joins high-skill court mediation, a famous reversal-of-fortune anecdote, and a late frontier intelligence failure. His durable profile is not simply success followed by incompetence: he can interpret family, legal, and sovereign interests together, yet his earlier political balancing does not substitute for verifying military intelligence or fitting [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s more aggressive frontier order.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-266-sihui-furan-yu-yi-pao-niao-de-gushi-1-lrdhzy9cykdnjgoi90h95atugnxw|Hanji 266]] supplies the earlier Liang court layer. After [[LiuWuLiangKingWesternHan|梁孝王刘武]]'s service in the Rebellion of the Seven States, his prestige reportedly expands into emperor-like protocol and angers [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]]. When both Jingdi and Empress Dowager Dou refuse to receive the envoy, Han Anguo reaches the imperial family through [[LiuPiaoWesternHan|馆陶长公主刘嫖]]. He reframes Liu Wu's excess as a boastful display of being loved by emperor and dowager rather than disloyal ambition. The account treats this interpretation as sufficient to lower Jingdi's threat perception and reopen direct contact.

That defense is not a denial of fault. Han also warns Liu Wu that continued reliance on brotherly affection can bring ruin because a vassal prince remains bound by rank, law, and duty. His political strength therefore lies in holding several truths together: the prince needs protection from an escalatory reading, the emperor needs reassurance about loyalty, and the family relationship needs a public boundary. The same episode links him to “死灰复燃” through a reported prison exchange in which jailer Tian Jia answers Han's threat of revived fortunes by saying he would urinate on the rekindled ash.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-275-dapo-xiongnu-bubai-shenhua-weiqing-ruhe-zuodao-de-2-lpn6t8p8d0iumsd7gabohtevxxqo|Hanji 275]] preserves the later and less successful side. It characterizes Han as calm, shrewd, politically flexible, and able to balance competing forces, while also describing him as money-seeking and willing to cultivate powerful people. A captured Xiongnu soldier reportedly tells him that the opposing force has withdrawn; Han accepts the report, releases soldiers for agricultural work, and is left with a small remnant when the [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] return.

Wudi's transfer of Han to [[YoubeipingCommandery|右北平]], alongside [[WeiQingHanGeneral|卫青]]'s rise, makes operational failure political. The episode treats his final illness and death as the joint result of error, marginalization, and [[PoliticalMilitaryEraFit|political-military era mismatch]]. Across both sources, the more cautious conclusion is that mediation talent and past service are real but domain-specific: they do not remove the need for evidence checks, operational readiness, or adaptation to a changed office.

## Key Characteristics

- Court mediator who can reduce perceived threat by separating improper conduct from alleged disloyal motive.
- Adviser who protects Liu Wu while still insisting that royal affection cannot cancel law, rank, and princely duty.
- Official linked to the “死灰复燃” reversal-of-fortune idiom through a reported prison exchange with Tian Jia.
- Morally mixed political actor portrayed as steady and calculating but also money-seeking and willing to cultivate powerful patrons.
- Frontier commander whose acceptance of a captive's report leads to premature demobilization and renewed Xiongnu exposure.
- Older political-military figure contrasted with Wei Qing's stronger fit with Wudi's frontier priorities.

## Qualifications

These two short podcast summaries do not form a complete biography. Hanji 266's exact prison dialogue, first-use attribution for the idiom, audience sequence, tears, speeches, rewards, and private motives require comparison with the transmitted records; its supplied text ends before the full rehabilitation sequel. The claim that Liu Wu lacked dangerous ambition is a persuasive reframing inside the story, not independently established intent. Hanji 275's deception, demobilization mechanics, roughly seven-hundred-man remnant, losses, emotions, reassignment purpose, illness, death timing, and Sima Qian characterization remain episode-attributed. “Being discarded by the era” is an interpretive frame, not proof that cautious administration or political balancing became useless throughout Wudi's reign.
