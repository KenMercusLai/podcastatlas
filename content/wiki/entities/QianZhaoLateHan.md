## Overview

牵招 / Qian Zhao is a late-Han frontier official whose bounded wiki profile now has two Jian'an 9 layers: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1064-xuyou-zhisi-gonggao-zhenzhu-yu-xingge-quexian-de-beige-lswmvj55p7ip97ts5e30xfdubwth|Hanji 1064]] shows him leaving the collapsing Yuan-family side for Cao Cao's Jizhou administration, while [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1066-sanguo-zui-manheng-de-waijiaoguan-pingshenme-shi-qianzhao-ll17spch6x4zyo61vno-oi0lk7ft|Hanji 1066]] shows him using local frontier experience and public coercion to block Wuhuan aid for [[YuanTan|袁谭]].

## Current Profile

Hanji 1066 says Qian Zhao had previously managed Wuhuan cavalry for [[YuanShao|袁绍]], so [[CaoCao|曹操]] chooses him for a mission to Liucheng when Wuhuan leaders are preparing five thousand cavalry to rescue Yuan Tan. The mission places him in a three-way authority contest: Yuan Shao had previously issued titles in the emperor's name, Cao Cao now claims the court-facing right to renew recognition, and [[GongsunKangLateHan|公孙康]]'s Liaodong envoy [[HanZhongLiaodongLateHan|韩中]] arrives with a chanyu seal.

Qian Zhao's method is deliberately public. He argues that Yuan Shao's former authority has passed to Cao Cao because Yuan Shao opposed the emperor's will and was destroyed, while Liaodong is too remote and minor to grant offices by itself. When Han Zhong boasts of Liaodong's strength and questions why Cao Cao should be uniquely honored, Qian Zhao violently seizes him, presses his head down, and draws a sword. The Wuhuan leader intervenes to save Han Zhong, but the public balance has shifted: Wuhuan dismisses the Liaodong envoy, accepts the court command, and disbands the cavalry.

Hanji 1064 backfills the immediate route into Cao-side service. Yuan Shang had sent Qian Zhao to Shangdang to supervise grain transport, but Yuan Shang fled toward Zhongshan before Qian returned. Qian Zhao urges [[GaoGanLateHan|Gao Gan]] to receive Yuan Shang and wait on events; when Gao Gan refuses, Qian Zhao goes east to Cao Cao, who keeps him as a Jizhou official. The host's biographical aside emphasizes Qian Zhao's later northern-frontier value as stable commandery and border administration, not only the dramatic Wuhuan envoy scene.

## Key Characteristics

- Former Yuan-side frontier handler whose Wuhuan-cavalry experience makes him useful to Cao Cao after the Yuan camp fractures.
- Yuan-side transport supervisor whose failed appeal to Gao Gan precedes his submission to Cao Cao.
- Wartime envoy operating in a contested recognition field between Cao Cao, Yuan-family remnants, Liaodong, and Wuhuan.
- Uses argument about legitimate title authority before turning to bodily intimidation.
- Treats the public meeting as the decisive theater: the point is to make Wuhuan leaders see the cost of hedging.
- Converts coercive diplomacy into battlefield effect by preventing five thousand Wuhuan cavalry from joining Yuan Tan.
- Compared by the host with Ban Chao and Sui He as an operator who grasps timing, fractures alliances, and forces choices.

## Qualifications

This page is still source-scoped and should not be treated as a full biography. Hanji 1064's later-career praise is a host summary rather than detailed narrative evidence. Hanji 1066's "most domineering diplomat" label is likewise interpretive; the durable claim is narrower: Qian Zhao combines frontier experience, public argument, and coercive theater to move from Yuan-side collapse into Cao-side service and then block a specific Yuan Tan-Wuhuan-Liaodong alignment.
