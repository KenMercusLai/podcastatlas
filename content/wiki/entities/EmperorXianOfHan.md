# 汉献帝 / Emperor Xian of Han

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-947-dongzhuo-shoufu-lvbu-kaiqi-chengba-zhilu-lvr-24l6j8f6zvpfcmqgknmgywa1|Hanji 947]] backfills Emperor Xian before he is emperor. In this source he is Liu Xie / Chenliu Wang, fleeing with [[LiuBianHongnongWang|刘辩 / 弘农王]] and [[MinGongLateHan|闵贡]] after the Luoyang disorder, then answering [[DongZhuo|董卓]] clearly when Liu Bian cannot.

The episode makes his later title begin as someone else's strategic reading. Dong Zhuo's preference for Liu Xie creates the precondition for Hanji 948's installation; the title has not yet shifted, but the future [[MingqiLegitimacy|名器合法性]] center is already being selected by an armed court entrant.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-948-dongzhuo-feidi-quanchen-ruhe-gaibian-huangquan-youxi-ltekg1a-5b5u37aeekjm3dm2m6kk|Hanji 948]] adds Emperor Xian's installation before the later forced-relocation and captured-court branches. In this source he is still Liu Xie / Chenliu Wang when [[DongZhuo|董卓]] argues that he should replace [[LiuBianHongnongWang|刘辩 / 弘农王]], then forces the court ceremony that makes him emperor.

The episode shows the title's value from the beginning. Emperor Xian is not yet an active ruler, but the office Dong Zhuo gives him is still powerful enough to require edict language, seal transfer through [[YuanWeiLateHan|袁隗]], and court ceremony. That makes Hanji 948 the earliest local case of [[MingqiLegitimacy|名器合法性]] under Dong Zhuo's armed capture.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-951-tianjiang-hengcai-jiujing-shi-haoshi-haishi-huaishi-lovwgng2wrtfno-s9ttpybbhksvh|Hanji 951]] adds the forced-relocation layer before the later alternative-sovereign and Chang'an captivity branches. [[DongZhuo|董卓]] moves Emperor Xian from [[LuoyangLateHan|洛阳]] toward [[ChangAnHanCapital|长安]], and the emperor's recognized person makes the move politically decisive even though he is not shown as choosing it.

The source matters because it shows the title's weakness and value at the same time. Officials such as [[YangBiao|杨彪]] can oppose the move by invoking ancestral and institutional order, while [[HuangfuSongLateHan|皇甫嵩]] hesitates to attack Dong Zhuo because Dong Zhuo still operates through the court's formal surface. Emperor Xian is therefore already a [[MingqiLegitimacy|名器合法性]] center before later sources show warlords fighting to possess or protect him.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-954-renxingbenshan-haishi-renxingbene-lkurz9dbb25jrjyl5c-rrahda39h|Hanji 954]] adds the earliest replacement-pressure layer in this late-Han branch. The anti-[[DongZhuo|董卓]] leaders recognize that Emperor Xian is young, displaced to Chang'an, and controlled by Dong Zhuo, so [[YuanShao|袁绍]] and [[HanFuLateHan|韩馥]] try to install [[LiuYuLateHan|刘虞]] as an alternative sovereign.

The episode's importance for this page is that Emperor Xian remains weak but not empty. [[CaoCao|曹操]] refuses replacement because the emperor has not forfeited the throne, and Liu Yu refuses the title rather than damage the recognized Han order. Hanji 954 therefore shows [[MingqiLegitimacy|名器合法性]] operating even before the later route-control, Anyi, Luoyang, and Xudu episodes: the title is captured, but it still constrains what rivals can safely claim.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-958-jiemi-shishang-zui-duanming-de-tongchoudikai-lve41q9gyijbop8wk-b7e6shogju|Hanji 958]] adds an early trapped-court rescue attempt before the later Chang'an captivity and flight branch. Emperor Xian, stuck under [[DongZhuo|董卓]]'s control and hoping to return toward [[LuoyangLateHan|洛阳]], sends [[LiuYuLateHan|刘虞]]'s son out through [[Wuguan|武关]] to ask Liu Yu for troops.

The attempt shows both the residual value and practical weakness of the imperial name. The request carries enough legitimacy that Liu Yu prepares cavalry, but once the envoy reaches [[NanyangCommandery|南阳郡]], [[YuanShu|袁术]] can detain him and try to redirect the rescue force into his own calculations. Hanji 958 therefore prefigures the later [[MingqiLegitimacy|名器合法性]] pattern: the emperor's title can still summon claims of protection, but armed intermediaries decide whether those claims become rescue, leverage, or captivity.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-963-shishang-bei-diantian-deng-de-diyiren-shi-ta-lkpkhlpkv3xpw-hur4b7sh-3druk|Hanji 963]] adds the court-meeting setting that makes [[DongZhuo|董卓]]'s assassination possible. After the emperor's illness improves, the court gathers at [[WeiyangPalace|未央宫]], and [[WangYunLateHan|王允]] plus [[LyuBu|吕布]] use an edict to frame Dong Zhuo's killing as imperial punishment.

This source makes Emperor Xian's authority thin but still useful. The emperor is not shown as directing the plot himself, yet the edict form and later rewards to Wang Yun and Lü Bu show that his title remains the legal surface through which armed actors try to make violence legible as court action.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-966-meiyou-yexin-budong-quanmou-jiu-bie-dang-lingdao-lldn3hi1gl5v3axvklwakgf6vx3u|Hanji 966]] backfills the first Li-Guo capture layer before Hanji 967 and the later 195 CE hostage sequence. As [[LiJueLateHan|李傕]] and [[GuoSiLateHan|郭汜]] enter [[ChangAnHanCapital|长安]], Emperor Xian is a twelve-year-old ruler whom [[WangYunLateHan|王允]] refuses to abandon. The court can still issue amnesty and offices, but the emperor's name is quickly folded into coercive demands for Wang Yun and summonses to [[SongYiLateHan|宋翼]] and [[WangHongLateHan|王宏]].

The source therefore gives an early case of [[MingqiLegitimacy|名器合法性]] under armed capture. The title still matters enough that Wang Yun treats abandonment as morally impossible and Li-Guo commanders use court form to legitimate actions, yet the emperor cannot prevent plunder, executions, or the destruction of the outside deterrent.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-967-ta-canyu-mousha-dongzhuo-jingneng-taoguo-sishen-weilie-lv24biyswj9ljdld5htynhauhph3|Hanji 967]] backfills the first post-[[DongZhuo|董卓]] Chang'an capture layer before the later Li-Guo captivity sequence. [[JiaXu|贾诩]]'s advice helps [[LiJueLateHan|李傕]], [[GuoSiLateHan|郭汜]], and other Dong Zhuo remnant commanders take [[ChangAnHanCapital|长安]], after which the court can still appoint [[LiuBiao|刘表]] and remove [[HuangfuSongLateHan|皇甫嵩]] while the emperor's position is militarily constrained. The source also says Jia Xu later obstructs direct harm to Emperor Xian and secretly helps his escape, making the emperor both a legitimacy center and a physically exposed captive.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-969-yuanshi-xiongdi-neidou-shui-neng-jianlou-lsgj6sewo35jpsgfm-koivijtr6r|Hanji 969]] adds an early appointment-and-title use of Emperor Xian's court before the later captivity and relocation sequence. [[ZhongYao|钟繇]] persuades [[LiJueLateHan|李傕]] and [[GuoSiLateHan|郭汜]] to release [[CaoCao|曹操]]'s appointment, while Li Jue later grants [[YuanShu|袁术]] left-general, marquis, and `假节` recognition. The source shows the court's residual naming power and its contamination at the same time: titles still help warlords, but the institution issuing them is already under coercive military control.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-972-sanguo-liuyu-zhisi-taisha-taitianzhen-lvawrcandbh628rckukosgarh7v2|Hanji 972]] adds an early Youzhou-facing limit of Emperor Xian's authority. The court can send [[DuanXunLateHan|段迅]] with edicts expanding [[LiuYuLateHan|刘虞]]'s status and rewarding [[GongsunZan|公孙瓒]], and it can appoint [[TianChou|田筹]] after he delivers Liu Yu's memorial, but the emperor's name cannot stop Gongsun Zan from coercing the envoy or killing Liu Yu. Tian Chou's refusal of office makes the weakness personal: accepting rank feels improper while the ruler is displaced and humiliated.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-973-changpingguan-zhizhan-shengsi-zhidi-zhanhou-jing-chonggui-yuhao-lgazc9dewfayb8qnkncfkfa12yet|Hanji 973]] adds the 194 CE coming-of-age and maternal-rite prelude before the later captivity branch. Emperor Xian formally reaches the age at which he can handle government, but the source treats this as ritual without power because [[ChangAnHanCapital|长安]] remains controlled by armed actors around [[LiJueLateHan|李傕]].

The same episode gives a small but important court-ritual scene. When officials ask him to choose an empress, he says he cannot discuss selection before [[WangMeirenLateHan|王美人]]'s burial is settled, and the Three Excellencies request her reburial and posthumous honor as 灵怀皇后.

汉献帝 / Emperor Xian of Han appears in [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-24-shangyang-yue-wo-za-lie-le-lt4yd6pwlezqia5yljg-gankyhut]] inside the episode's analogy between [[CaoCao|曹操]] and [[ShangYang|公孙鞅 / 商鞅]]. The source says [[CaoPi|曹丕]] later forces or receives his abdication after the Cao family has already monopolized power.

The page is source-scoped. Its role is not to narrate the late Han in full, but to mark the endpoint of the analogy: some political actors escape a no-retreat condition by turning de facto control into dynastic replacement, while Shang Yang's Qin position does not offer that viable path.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-979-zaoyu-tongliao-beici-ta-ruhe-wei-caocao-liwan-kuanglan-luu58a2ww11g-radh-5g4l7fcis|Hanji 979]] adds an earlier Chang'an court-capacity scene before the hostage-and-flight branch. [[LiJueLateHan|李傕]], [[GuoSiLateHan|郭汜]], [[FanChouLateHan|樊稠]], and later [[YangDingLateHan|杨定]] use titles and kaifu-style privileges to crowd the appointment system, while Emperor Xian's own authority remains strong enough to issue offices but too weak to keep selection channels orderly.

The same source shows the emperor's residual administrative judgment during famine. When [[HouWenLateHan|侯汶]]'s rice-bean gruel relief fails to reduce deaths in [[ChangAnHanCapital|长安]], Emperor Xian has the ingredients measured in front of him and exposes diversion or dilution. The audit works locally, but it does not undo the broader military capture of court power.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-984-liangzhouxi-junfa-neidou-ta-weihe-di-yige-daoxia-lurb2qfzsa7e-owh-dl-m-sccze5|Hanji 984]] backfills the first direct seizure before Hanji 985's dual hostage stage. Emperor Xian sends attendants and ministers to mediate between [[LiJueLateHan|李傕]] and [[GuoSiLateHan|郭汜]], but neither armed leader obeys. After Guo Si's abduction plan leaks, Li Jue preempts through [[LiXianLateHan|李暹]], who surrounds the palace on 195-03-25 and moves the emperor to Li Jue's camp in carts while [[YangBiao|杨彪]] objects that a ruler should not live in a subject's house.

This source shows [[ProtectorCaptorAmbiguity|护驾与挟持模糊性]] before the road-flight episodes: the emperor's body is valuable enough to trigger a race between warlords, yet his orders cannot settle their conflict or protect the palace from plunder and fire.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-985-fanmu-chengchou-liguo-zhiluan-de-xijie-tanjiu-lt-t2un0zcudkige9ucl1txa2hhk|Hanji 985]] backfills Emperor Xian's captivity before the later mediation and flight sources. [[LiJueLateHan|李傕]] holds the emperor while [[GuoSiLateHan|郭汜]] holds ministers such as [[YangBiao|杨彪]], so the ruler and court officials are simultaneously trapped as military bargaining assets. Even in that setting, the court still appoints [[FuShouEmpress|伏寿]] as empress and [[FuWanLateHan|伏完]] as zhijinwu, showing that formal title practice continues under coercion.

The episode also makes the emperor's bodily vulnerability explicit before the road-flight crisis. Guo Si's night attack sends arrows into the emperor's tent area; Li Jue later moves him into Beiwu under guard; hungry attendants depend on the emperor asking Li Jue for rice and bones, but the answer is rotten beef bones. [[YangQiLateHan|杨琦]] then advises the emperor to suppress anger because open rebuke could make Li Jue's retaliation worse.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-986-tianyu-qiwang-bixian-lingqi-kuang-lvlvu057-tnz3hal6oodhydvzvbd|Hanji 986]] adds Emperor Xian as a trapped mediator before the later departure sequence. He tries again to settle the [[LiJueLateHan|李傕]]-[[GuoSiLateHan|郭汜]] conflict by sending [[HuangfuLiLateHan|皇甫郦]], but his practical authority cannot make Li Jue obey. The same source shows him managing Li Jue's confused private complaints by answering along with him, a small survival performance under coercive hosting.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-988-shoujin-lingru-ta-shi-hanmo-beican-de-huangdi-lsdkpakvk5u-omegcri-wstup-zl|Hanji 988]] backfills the immediate Chang'an exit prelude before Hanji 989. Emperor Xian is housed under [[LiJueLateHan|李傕]]'s pressure while [[LiJueLateHan|李傕]] and [[GuoSiLateHan|郭汜]] fight, and Qiang-Hu soldiers can approach his lodging to demand palace women promised by Li Jue. [[JiaXu|贾诩]]'s intervention through promised titles and goods removes that harassment, while [[LiuAiLateHan|刘艾]] later makes the emperor visible at the bridge when Guo Si's soldiers block the carriage. The episode shows [[MingqiLegitimacy|名器合法性]] reduced to a narrow but real force: the title cannot secure route or food, yet the emperor's face and rebuke still make armed men step aside.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-989-ningwei-taipingquan-buzuo-luanshi-ren-lkoycygmjnltuvpq-6wjv-ls61s6|Hanji 989]] backfills the route-control crisis immediately before Hanji 990-991. Emperor Xian grants titles to [[ZhangJiLateHan|张济]], [[GuoSiLateHan|郭汜]], [[YangDingLateHan|杨定]], [[YangFengLateHan|杨奉]], and [[DongChengLateHan|董承]], but ceremonial recognition does not give him command over their soldiers. His refusal to eat when Guo Si blocks the route, his escape from fire at Xinfeng, and his hesitation to enter [[DuanWeiLateHan|段煨]]'s camp at [[HuayinLateHan|华阴]] show a sovereign whose title remains valuable while physical survival depends on food, rumor, and armed escorts.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-990-lijue-guosi-shengsi-yuanjia-weihe-turan-lianshou-lmop5mcrsjuq-ixuv0sfho5mvc-f|Hanji 990]] backfills the decision room immediately before the bloody flight in Hanji 991. When [[YangDingLateHan|杨定]], [[DongChengLateHan|董承]], and [[YangFengLateHan|杨奉]] seek an order against [[DuanWeiLateHan|段煨]], Emperor Xian refuses because Duan Wei has not shown rebellion; the episode then shows how little that refusal can control the armed field, because the attack proceeds and [[LiJueLateHan|李傕]] plus [[GuoSiLateHan|郭汜]] reunite to recover the emperor as a shared political prize.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-991-zhengduo-xiandi-zhizhan-shui-neng-zuizhong-qusheng-lkbsl5uzqxouzm6mlewrnuns0x6p|Hanji 991]] backfills the physical danger before the Anyi stage. Emperor Xian is pursued by [[LiJueLateHan|李傕]], [[GuoSiLateHan|郭汜]], and [[ZhangJiLateHan|张济]], protected unevenly by [[DongChengLateHan|董承]], [[YangFengLateHan|杨奉]], [[BaiboArmy|白波军]] commanders, and [[QubeiSouthernXiongnu|去卑]], and forced into a night river crossing where only a small party, including [[FuShouEmpress|伏皇后]] and [[YangBiao|杨彪]], gets across. The episode makes his person the center of [[ProtectorCaptorAmbiguity|护驾与挟持模糊性]]: his title is still worth fighting over, but neither title nor ritual prevents the loss of records, officials, guards, and followers.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-992-luanshi-you-duo-kepa-jiemi-changan-de-xuese-mengyan-ltuxzrb-1x5y2ctwo4drggxn5stz|Hanji 992]] adds the Anyi stage before the later Luoyang-return branch. Emperor Xian arrives at [[Anyi|安邑]] in a rough ox cart, receives offerings through the [[HedongCommandery|河东郡]] governor, and still grants titles while the court lacks proper seals, food, and secure court space. The episode makes this a compressed image of [[MingqiLegitimacy|名器合法性]] under material collapse: the emperor's title can still authorize offices, but the institution has to carve emergency seals and conduct ceremonies behind thorn fences.

The same source shows why his body remains strategically valuable despite that weakness. [[JuShou|沮授]] urges [[YuanShao|袁绍]] to welcome him and use the Han name to command rival lords, while Yuan Shao's refusal leaves the later opening for [[CaoCao|曹操]]. Emperor Xian is therefore not an empty symbol; he is a vulnerable center whose recognized title becomes a decisive [[LegitimacyWindowSeizure|合法性窗口抢占]] for whoever can protect and operationalize it.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-999-sanguo-luanshi-renxing-you-duome-cuiruo-li8tz30elca7bnipei6dfqdxmmeu|Hanji 999]] adds the pre-Luoyang-return danger before the Hanji 1000 court branch. [[LiJueLateHan|李傕]] and [[GuoSiLateHan|郭汜]] fight over him, [[DongChengLateHan|董承]] works with [[YangFengLateHan|杨奉]] and [[HanXianLateHan|韩暹]] to escort him, and [[ZhangYangLateHan|张杨]] plus [[LiuBiao|刘表]] help repair or supply the route toward [[LuoyangLateHan|洛阳]]. The episode emphasizes that escaping one set of captors does not restore freedom when every protector has its own interest.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1000-hao-meng-panluan-jingbi-lvbu-fanqiang-taopao-lluthd9hv-0nqbot4fuctjcuka68|Hanji 1000]] adds Emperor Xian's immediate return to [[LuoyangLateHan|洛阳]] before the Cao Cao intervention sequence. [[YangFengLateHan|杨奉]] and [[HanXianLateHan|韩暹]] escort him east, [[ZhangYangLateHan|张杨]] supplies grain and meets the court, and the emperor reaches a capital whose palaces, food supply, and official living conditions are already broken.

The episode shows why his title remains potent but his material position is weak. He can issue a general amnesty and confer high ranks, but he depends on armed protectors and a ruined city, so the court becomes a legitimacy object others can either rescue, control, or claim to defend.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1001-weishenme-quan-ni-buyao-fanzhongnu-lolurkcjpie14nnczk0tu7iqerke|Hanji 1001]] adds Emperor Xian as the weak but still blocking sovereign in [[YuanShu|袁术]]'s pre-coronation calculation. Yuan Shu hears that the emperor has been defeated at Cao Yang and treats that weakness as an opening, but [[YanXiangLateHan|阎象]] and [[ZhangChengLateHan|张承]] argue that Han has not become a tyrannical order whose replacement can be justified.

This makes Emperor Xian's legitimacy negative before it becomes Cao Cao's opportunity. Even when exposed, the emperor's title still prevents an unsupported claimant from making simple succession claims through prophecy, five phases, or the [[HeirloomImperialSeal|传国玉玺]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1002-yuanshu-chengdi-hou-sunce-chengwei-zui-da-ying-llya3c2-pefujwxdxefvqxwxjizjo|Hanji 1002]] supplies the legitimacy-window stage before Cao Cao's entry and relocation mechanics. [[SunCe|孙策]] uses Emperor Xian's still-recognized standing to reject [[YuanShu|袁术]]'s planned imperial claim: the emperor is young and constrained, not a tyrant whose overthrow can be justified. [[XunYu|荀彧]] then uses the same recognized title positively, arguing that [[CaoCao|曹操]] should welcome the emperor before another actor captures the public-order advantage.

This makes Emperor Xian weak but not empty. His person is exposed, and actors around him are difficult to control, but the title still carries enough public force to create [[LegitimacyWindowSeizure|合法性窗口抢占]] for Cao Cao and [[AntiUsurpationCoalitionPressure|讨逆同盟压力]] against Yuan Shu.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1003-sanguo-zui-bei-digu-de-moushi-dongzhao-llc7q-loennxw26v3u5cbhitikw1|Hanji 1003]] supplies the immediate prelude to Cao Cao's court access. Emperor Xian has returned toward [[LuoyangLateHan|洛阳]] under the protection of actors such as [[HanXianLateHan|韩暹]] and [[YangFengLateHan|杨奉]], but those protectors also become obstacles around the court. [[DongZhaoLateHan|董昭]]'s letter to Yang Feng and [[DongChengLateHan|董承]]'s appeal against Han Xian give [[CaoCao|曹操]] the path and pretext to enter.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1004-caocao-yingfeng-hanxiandi-shi-shui-de-zhuyi-lrrga-flmx5ajnhu1c42x7kooe94|Hanji 1004]] adds the concrete relocation from [[LuoyangLateHan|洛阳]] to [[Xudu|许县 / 许都]]. The source credits [[HanXianLateHan|韩暹]], [[YangFengLateHan|杨奉]], and other protection forces with helping Emperor Xian escape Li Jue and Guo Si and return to Luoyang, but it also says the court remains materially unsafe and politically exposed.

[[DongZhaoLateHan|董昭]]'s advice to [[CaoCao|曹操]] turns that weakness into a move: the emperor and officials can be persuaded by food and shelter needs, while relocation gives Cao Cao a stronger setting for court control. The source therefore shows Emperor Xian as both a suffering displaced ruler and a still-potent legitimacy center around which armed actors compete.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1005-sunce-zhuding-zaoshi-shi-xingge-jueding-mingyun-lpqnere2cwcrngggyezd-bsqahx4|Hanji 1005]] adds Emperor Xian as the legitimating surface of Cao Cao's new [[Xudu|许都]] court. The source says the court reproaches [[YuanShao|袁绍]] for failing to rescue the emperor, then appoints him taiwei and Marquis of Ye. When Yuan Shao resents [[CaoCao|曹操]]'s superior great-general rank, the emperor's name is again used to reshuffle titles: Yuan Shao receives great general, while Cao Cao becomes sikong and acting chariots-and-cavalry general.

This branch shows the early form of the later pattern. Emperor Xian's court can still assign recognized titles and issue reproaches, but effective initiative already lies with Cao Cao, who uses title allocation to manage a dangerous rival through [[StrategicTitleConcession|战略性名位让步]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1020-sanguo-baijiazi-yuanshu-ruhe-ba-hao-pai-da-de-xilan-lhwr9hfmanr-zktleufy3kvl38u0|Hanji 1020]] adds an earlier court-control use of Emperor Xian. After [[CaoCao|曹操]] brings him to Xu, the court still contains old-Han ministers whose prestige and networks matter. The arrest of [[YangBiao|杨彪]] is formally reported through imperial channels, but [[KongRong|孔融]]'s reply treats Cao Cao as the actor who cannot hide behind the emperor's name.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1015-sanguo-luanshi-weishenme-yuanshu-gan-chengdi-lim0jemkwyjcesj2nvsr3jndhxfk|Hanji 1015]] supplies the direct anti-usurpation use of Emperor Xian's position. [[YuanShu|袁术]]'s self-coronation is treated as a challenge to the still-recognized Han title, creating [[AntiUsurpationCoalitionPressure|讨逆同盟压力]] for actors who need to preserve their own public legitimacy by opposing the claim.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1016-sanguo-luanshi-li-chengui-jiazu-de-mingyun-jueze-ltuckdcxpyxk-xmzp8rt-2gjfzcs|Hanji 1016]] adds the appointment side of the same legitimating surface. The source says Kong Rong is sent with a tally to [[YuanShao|袁绍]], who receives the great-general title and four-province oversight, while Cao Cao uses the Han-court position to oppose [[YuanShu|袁术]]'s unsupported imperial claim.

The addition therefore makes Emperor Xian's page less purely endpoint-focused. He is also a living legitimating surface in Cao Cao's court: accusations can be framed as the emperor's will, while real accountability remains contested among ministers who understand where effective power sits.
