# 汉献帝 / Emperor Xian of Han

汉献帝 / Emperor Xian of Han appears in [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-24-shangyang-yue-wo-za-lie-le-lt4yd6pwlezqia5yljg-gankyhut]] inside the episode's analogy between [[CaoCao|曹操]] and [[ShangYang|公孙鞅 / 商鞅]]. The source says [[CaoPi|曹丕]] later forces or receives his abdication after the Cao family has already monopolized power.

The page is source-scoped. Its role is not to narrate the late Han in full, but to mark the endpoint of the analogy: some political actors escape a no-retreat condition by turning de facto control into dynastic replacement, while Shang Yang's Qin position does not offer that viable path.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-991-zhengduo-xiandi-zhizhan-shui-neng-zuizhong-qusheng-lkbsl5uzqxouzm6mlewrnuns0x6p|Hanji 991]] backfills the physical danger before the Anyi stage. Emperor Xian is pursued by [[LiJueLateHan|李傕]], [[GuoSiLateHan|郭汜]], and [[ZhangJiLateHan|张济]], protected unevenly by [[DongChengLateHan|董承]], [[YangFengLateHan|杨奉]], [[BaiboArmy|白波军]] commanders, and [[QubeiSouthernXiongnu|去卑]], and forced into a night river crossing where only a small party, including [[FuShouEmpress|伏皇后]] and [[YangBiao|杨彪]], gets across. The episode makes his person the center of [[ProtectorCaptorAmbiguity|护驾与挟持模糊性]]: his title is still worth fighting over, but neither title nor ritual prevents the loss of records, officials, guards, and followers.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-992-luanshi-you-duo-kepa-jiemi-changan-de-xuese-mengyan-ltuxzrb-1x5y2ctwo4drggxn5stz|Hanji 992]] adds the Anyi stage before the later Luoyang-return branch. Emperor Xian arrives at [[Anyi|安邑]] in a rough ox cart, receives offerings through the [[HedongCommandery|河东郡]] governor, and still grants titles while the court lacks proper seals, food, and secure court space. The episode makes this a compressed image of [[MingqiLegitimacy|名器合法性]] under material collapse: the emperor's title can still authorize offices, but the institution has to carve emergency seals and conduct ceremonies behind thorn fences.

The same source shows why his body remains strategically valuable despite that weakness. [[JuShou|沮授]] urges [[YuanShao|袁绍]] to welcome him and use the Han name to command rival lords, while Yuan Shao's refusal leaves the later opening for [[CaoCao|曹操]]. Emperor Xian is therefore not an empty symbol; he is a vulnerable center whose recognized title becomes a decisive [[LegitimacyWindowSeizure|合法性窗口抢占]] for whoever can protect and operationalize it.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-999-sanguo-luanshi-renxing-you-duome-cuiruo-li8tz30elca7bnipei6dfqdxmmeu|Hanji 999]] adds the pre-Luoyang-return danger before the Hanji 1000 court branch. [[LiJueLateHan|李傕]] and [[GuoSiLateHan|郭汜]] fight over him, [[DongChengLateHan|董承]] works with [[YangFengLateHan|杨奉]] and [[HanXianLateHan|韩暹]] to escort him, and [[ZhangYangLateHan|张杨]] plus [[LiuBiao|刘表]] help repair or supply the route toward [[LuoyangLateHan|洛阳]]. The episode emphasizes that escaping one set of captors does not restore freedom when every protector has its own interest.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1000-hao-meng-panluan-jingbi-lvbu-fanqiang-taopao-lluthd9hv-0nqbot4fuctjcuka68|Hanji 1000]] adds Emperor Xian's immediate return to [[LuoyangLateHan|洛阳]] before the Cao Cao intervention sequence. [[YangFengLateHan|杨奉]] and [[HanXianLateHan|韩暹]] escort him east, [[ZhangYangLateHan|张杨]] supplies grain and meets the court, and the emperor reaches a capital whose palaces, food supply, and official living conditions are already broken.

The episode shows why his title remains potent but his material position is weak. He can issue a general amnesty and confer high ranks, but he depends on armed protectors and a ruined city, so the court becomes a legitimacy object others can either rescue, control, or claim to defend.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1001-weishenme-quan-ni-buyao-fanzhongnu-lolurkcjpie14nnczk0tu7iqerke|Hanji 1001]] adds Emperor Xian as the weak but still blocking sovereign in [[YuanShu|袁术]]'s pre-coronation calculation. Yuan Shu hears that the emperor has been defeated at Cao Yang and treats that weakness as an opening, but [[YanXiangLateHan|阎象]] and [[ZhangChengLateHan|张承]] argue that Han has not become a tyrannical order whose replacement can be justified.

This makes Emperor Xian's legitimacy negative before it becomes Cao Cao's opportunity. Even when exposed, the emperor's title still prevents an unsupported claimant from making simple succession claims through prophecy, five phases, or the [[HeirloomImperialSeal|传国玉玺]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1002-yuanshu-chengdi-hou-sunce-chengwei-zui-da-ying-llya3c2-pefujwxdxefvqxwxjizjo|Hanji 1002]] supplies the legitimacy-window stage before Cao Cao's entry and relocation mechanics. [[SunCe|孙策]] uses Emperor Xian's still-recognized standing to reject [[YuanShu|袁术]]'s planned imperial claim: the emperor is young and constrained, not a tyrant whose overthrow can be justified. [[XunYu|荀彧]] then uses the same recognized title positively, arguing that [[CaoCao|曹操]] should welcome the emperor before another actor captures the public-order advantage.

This makes Emperor Xian weak but not empty. His person is exposed, and actors around him are difficult to control, but the title still carries enough public force to create [[LegitimacyWindowSeizure|合法性窗口抢占]] for Cao Cao and [[AntiUsurpationCoalitionPressure|讨逆同盟压力]] against Yuan Shu.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1003-sanguo-zui-bei-digu-de-moushi-dongzhao-llc7q-loennxw26v3u5cbhitikw1|Hanji 1003]] supplies the immediate prelude to Cao Cao's court access. Emperor Xian has returned toward [[LuoyangLateHan|洛阳]] under the protection of actors such as [[HanXianLateHan|韩暹]] and [[YangFengLateHan|杨奉]], but those protectors also become obstacles around the court. [[DongZhaoLateHan|董昭]]'s letter to Yang Feng and [[DongChengLateHan|董承]]'s appeal against Han Xian give [[CaoCao|曹操]] the path and pretext to enter.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1004-caocao-yingfeng-hanxiandi-shi-shui-de-zhuyi-lrrga-flmx5ajnhu1c42x7kooe94|Hanji 1004]] adds the concrete relocation from [[LuoyangLateHan|洛阳]] to [[Xudu|许县 / 许都]]. The source credits [[HanXianLateHan|韩暹]], [[YangFengLateHan|杨奉]], and other protection forces with helping Emperor Xian escape Li Jue and Guo Si and return to Luoyang, but it also says the court remains materially unsafe and politically exposed.

[[DongZhaoLateHan|董昭]]'s advice to [[CaoCao|曹操]] turns that weakness into a move: the emperor and officials can be persuaded by food and shelter needs, while relocation gives Cao Cao a stronger setting for court control. The source therefore shows Emperor Xian as both a suffering displaced ruler and a still-potent legitimacy center around which armed actors compete.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1005-sunce-zhuding-zaoshi-shi-xingge-jueding-mingyun-lpqnere2cwcrngggyezd-bsqahx4|Hanji 1005]] adds Emperor Xian as the legitimating surface of Cao Cao's new [[Xudu|许都]] court. The source says the court reproaches [[YuanShao|袁绍]] for failing to rescue the emperor, then appoints him taiwei and Marquis of Ye. When Yuan Shao resents [[CaoCao|曹操]]'s superior great-general rank, the emperor's name is again used to reshuffle titles: Yuan Shao receives great general, while Cao Cao becomes sikong and acting chariots-and-cavalry general.

This branch shows the early form of the later pattern. Emperor Xian's court can still assign recognized titles and issue reproaches, but effective initiative already lies with Cao Cao, who uses title allocation to manage a dangerous rival through [[StrategicTitleConcession|战略性名位让步]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1020-sanguo-baijiazi-yuanshu-ruhe-ba-hao-pai-da-de-xilan-lhwr9hfmanr-zktleufy3kvl38u0|Hanji 1020]] adds an earlier court-control use of Emperor Xian. After [[CaoCao|曹操]] brings him to Xu, the court still contains old-Han ministers whose prestige and networks matter. The arrest of [[YangBiao|杨彪]] is formally reported through imperial channels, but [[KongRong|孔融]]'s reply treats Cao Cao as the actor who cannot hide behind the emperor's name.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1015-sanguo-luanshi-weishenme-yuanshu-gan-chengdi-lim0jemkwyjcesj2nvsr3jndhxfk|Hanji 1015]] supplies the direct anti-usurpation use of Emperor Xian's position. [[YuanShu|袁术]]'s self-coronation is treated as a challenge to the still-recognized Han title, creating [[AntiUsurpationCoalitionPressure|讨逆同盟压力]] for actors who need to preserve their own public legitimacy by opposing the claim.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1016-sanguo-luanshi-li-chengui-jiazu-de-mingyun-jueze-ltuckdcxpyxk-xmzp8rt-2gjfzcs|Hanji 1016]] adds the appointment side of the same legitimating surface. The source says Kong Rong is sent with a tally to [[YuanShao|袁绍]], who receives the great-general title and four-province oversight, while Cao Cao uses the Han-court position to oppose [[YuanShu|袁术]]'s unsupported imperial claim.

The addition therefore makes Emperor Xian's page less purely endpoint-focused. He is also a living legitimating surface in Cao Cao's court: accusations can be framed as the emperor's will, while real accountability remains contested among ministers who understand where effective power sits.
