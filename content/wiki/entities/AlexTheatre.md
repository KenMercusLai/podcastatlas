## Overview
The Alex Theatre is the historic Glendale, California movie palace used in the Marketplace Tech source as a case of reviving large-format analog exhibition for [[TheOdyssey]].

## Current Profile
The source presents the Alex Theatre as both a preservation setting and a technical relaunch. It had operated as a first-run movie palace until 1991, then rebuilt enough projection, sound, and booth infrastructure to screen [[TheOdyssey]] in 70mm during a highly compressed preparation window.

The theater matters in the wiki because it turns [[SeventyMillimeterFilmProjection]] from a format preference into an operating problem. A venue needs a usable screen, expert projection and acoustics support, working legacy equipment, trained operators, and audience-facing experience design before the old technology can become a modern draw.

## Key Characteristics
- Historic 1920s movie palace with a first-run exhibition past that the source says ended in 1991.
- Rapid 70mm retrofit site, with roughly eight weeks to complete work Williams says should have taken much longer.
- Venue-level example of [[SeventyMillimeterFilmProjection]] constraints around projectors, sound, clarity, booth design, and specialist labor.
- Audience-experience case because the theater made the projection booth visible through picture windows.
- Local analog revival signal, described by the source as becoming the highest-grossing 70mm cinema in the country for the release.

## Evidence
- Historic role and relaunch - [[tech-20260827-0827-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260827-0827-mp-tech-pod-128]] describes the Alex Theatre as a 1920s Glendale movie palace whose last first-run studio film before this summer was Terminator 2 in 1991.
- Retrofit and operating constraints - [[tech-20260827-0827-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260827-0827-mp-tech-pod-128]] says the theater brought in projection and acoustics specialists, kept its existing screen, spent about half a million dollars, and finished work in about eight weeks.
- Visible projection as experience design - [[tech-20260827-0827-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260827-0827-mp-tech-pod-128]] says the theater added picture windows so patrons could see projectionists and projectors at work.
- Market signal - [[tech-20260827-0827-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260827-0827-mp-tech-pod-128]] reports that the Alex Theatre became the country's highest-grossing 70mm cinema for the release.

## Qualifications
