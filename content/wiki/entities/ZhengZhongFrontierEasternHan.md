## Overview

郑众 / Zheng Zhong (Eastern Han frontier commander) is now synthesized as a non-eunuch frontier official whose profile begins with high-risk envoy refusal under [[EmperorMingOfHan|Mingdi]] and later continues with recognition of [[GengGongEasternHan|Geng Gong]]'s survivors under [[EmperorZhangOfHan|Zhangdi]]. Hanji 738 makes him a source case for [[DiplomaticRitualDignity|diplomatic ritual dignity]] and [[FrontierRecognitionCredibilityTradeoff|frontier recognition credibility tradeoff]], while Hanji 746 makes him the official who turns frontier endurance into court-recognized merit.

## Current Profile

Hanji 738 supplies Zheng Zhong's earlier diplomatic layer. In 65 CE, [[NorthernXiongnuLateHan|Northern Xiongnu]] sends tribute while still raiding the border, and Mingdi considers a return mission. Zheng argues against it. His warning is strategic rather than fearful: a Han envoy could let the chanyu advertise reconciliation, reinforce Western Regions loyalty, shake [[SouthernXiongnu|Southern Xiongnu]] confidence, and encourage [[Wuhuan|Wuhuan]] distance from Han.

The same source turns ritual into statecraft. Zheng says his previous mission to Northern Xiongnu had already led to a confrontation over kowtowing, after which he was surrounded and denied food. He refuses to carry Han credentials and bow to the chanyu, because coerced submission by the envoy would damage Han prestige. Mingdi first treats this persistence as defiance, recalls him from the road, and imprisons him; after an amnesty, Zheng returns home.

Zheng's rehabilitation comes through hostile-side testimony. When Northern Xiongnu visitors later describe how Zheng had risked death rather than bow, Mingdi reinterprets the same conduct as courage and loyalty, then recalls him and appoints him military sima. This episode makes Zheng's profile depend on the unstable interpretation of loyal refusal: inside the court it can look like disobedience until the ritual context is understood.

Hanji 746 adds the later recognition layer. After [[FanQiangEasternHan|Fan Qiang]] rescues Geng Gong and the remaining defenders fight their way back east, only thirteen of the original twenty-six survivors reach Yumen. Zheng has them bathe, changes their clothes and caps, hears their account, and memorializes Geng's isolated defense as a preservation of Han honor. After Geng reaches Luoyang, he is appointed cavalry commandant.

## Key Characteristics

- Non-eunuch Eastern Han Zheng Zhong, explicitly distinct from the later eunuch Zheng Zhong.
- Frontier diplomat who reads Northern Xiongnu envoy exchange as a credibility and alliance-signaling problem.
- Refuses coerced kowtow because envoy ritual would publicly represent Han dignity.
- Suffers imprisonment when persistent warning is first read as disobedience.
- Is later rehabilitated when outside testimony confirms the loyalty behind his refusal.
- Receives and dignifies Geng Gong's survivors, then memorializes their merit for court recognition.

## Qualifications

This page exists to avoid an identity collision with [[ZhengZhongEasternHan|郑众 / Zheng Zhong (Eastern Han eunuch)]]. Hanji 738's transcript gives 郑仲/正宗-like forms, while Hanji 746 uses 郑众; the wiki normalizes both to the non-eunuch frontier commander because the envoy, military sima, and Geng Gong recognition context align. The page does not claim a full biography outside these source-bounded frontier roles.
