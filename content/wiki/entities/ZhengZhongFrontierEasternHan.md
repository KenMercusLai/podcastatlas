## Overview

郑众 / Zheng Zhong (Eastern Han frontier commander) is now synthesized as a non-eunuch official whose profile begins before the frontier, with a refusal to enter a crown-prince and royal-prince private retainer network. Hanji 737 then gives his 65 CE mission to [[NorthernXiongnuLateHan|Northern Xiongnu]], refusal to kowtow, and detection of a [[SouthernXiongnu|Southern Xiongnu]] defection plot; Hanji 738 shows him warning against a return mission and being misread by [[EmperorMingOfHan|Mingdi]], while Hanji 746 continues with recognition of [[GengGongEasternHan|Geng Gong]]'s survivors under [[EmperorZhangOfHan|Zhangdi]].

## Current Profile

Hanji 736 supplies Zheng Zhong's earliest current layer. Before Mingdi's reign, the crown prince and [[LiuJingGuanglingKingEasternHan|Liu Jing]] send [[LiangSongLinxiangMarquis|Liang Song]] with silk to recruit Zheng as a private guest. Zheng refuses because the crown prince should not casually form outside relationships and a royal prince should not privately gather retainers. When Liang later falls and his guests are implicated, Zheng's refusal becomes evidence of political judgment as well as formal rectitude.

Hanji 737 supplies Zheng Zhong's first frontier layer. Sent as Yueqi sima to Northern Xiongnu, he refuses the chanyu's demand that he kneel, suffers imprisonment and starvation, draws a knife when pressured further, and eventually returns to Luoyang with a Northern Xiongnu envoy. The same source then gives his intelligence role: while crossing the frontier, he detects [[XuguDuhouSouthernXiongnu|Xugu Duhou]] secretly seeking Northern Xiongnu help, captures the messenger, and memorializes the plot. Han creates [[DuliaoCampEasternHan|Duliaoying]] after this warning.

Hanji 738 supplies the later return-mission dispute. Northern Xiongnu sends tribute while still raiding the border, and Mingdi considers a return mission. Zheng argues against it. His warning is strategic rather than fearful: a Han envoy could let the chanyu advertise reconciliation, reinforce Western Regions loyalty, shake Southern Xiongnu confidence, and encourage [[Wuhuan|Wuhuan]] distance from Han.

Hanji 738 turns the earlier ritual confrontation into statecraft. Zheng says his previous mission to Northern Xiongnu had already led to the kowtow crisis recorded more directly in Hanji 737. He refuses to carry Han credentials and bow to the chanyu, because coerced submission by the envoy would damage Han prestige. Mingdi first treats this persistence as defiance, recalls him from the road, and imprisons him; after an amnesty, Zheng returns home.

Zheng's rehabilitation comes through hostile-side testimony. When Northern Xiongnu visitors later describe how Zheng had risked death rather than bow, Mingdi reinterprets the same conduct as courage and loyalty, then recalls him and appoints him military sima. This episode makes Zheng's profile depend on the unstable interpretation of loyal refusal: inside the court it can look like disobedience until the ritual context is understood.

Hanji 746 adds the later recognition layer. After [[FanQiangEasternHan|Fan Qiang]] rescues Geng Gong and the remaining defenders fight their way back east, only thirteen of the original twenty-six survivors reach Yumen. Zheng has them bathe, changes their clothes and caps, hears their account, and memorializes Geng's isolated defense as a preservation of Han honor. After Geng reaches Luoyang, he is appointed cavalry commandant.

## Key Characteristics

- Non-eunuch Eastern Han Zheng Zhong, explicitly distinct from the later eunuch Zheng Zhong, whose earliest current layer is risky private-retainer recruitment refusal.
- Frontier diplomat who refuses Northern Xiongnu ritual coercion, then later warns against return-envoy credibility damage.
- Intelligence actor whose detection of Xugu Duhou's northern contact helps create Duliaoying.
- Refuses coerced kowtow because envoy ritual publicly represents Han dignity.
- Suffers imprisonment when persistent warning is first read as disobedience.
- Is later rehabilitated when outside testimony confirms the loyalty behind his refusal.
- Receives and dignifies Geng Gong's survivors, then memorializes their merit for court recognition.

## Qualifications

This page exists to avoid an identity collision with [[ZhengZhongEasternHan|郑众 / Zheng Zhong (Eastern Han eunuch)]]. Hanji 736 uses 郑仲 in the private-recruitment refusal branch; Hanji 737 and Hanji 738 preserve envoy and military-sima contexts; Hanji 746 uses 郑众 in the Geng Gong-survivor recognition branch. The wiki normalizes these to the same non-eunuch frontier commander because the bounded source chain treats Zheng as an officeholding court/frontier actor, not the later eunuch access figure. The page does not claim a full biography outside these source-bounded roles.
