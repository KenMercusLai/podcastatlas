# Mai Elliott

## Overview

Mai Elliott is presented as a Vietnamese interviewer and interpreter in [[RANDCorporation]]'s [[VietCongMotivationAndMoraleProject]], whose family displacement, field encounters, and later reassessment make her a central case of evidence challenging belief without producing immediate revision.

## Current Profile

Elliott grew up in a privileged northern Vietnamese family connected to the French colonial administration. Her family fled south after the communist victory, leaving home and possessions because her father feared retaliation. That history made communist victory an immediate family threat rather than an abstract analytical possibility.

RAND recruited her in Saigon to interview Viet Cong prisoners and defectors. A captured communist officer whom she found principled and committed disrupted her earlier image of communists as simply violent and inhuman. The encounter created uncertainty, but fear for her family constrained what she could initially conclude. After moving to the United States, she eventually judged North Vietnamese determination stronger than American policy assumed and the war wrong and unwinnable.

## Key Characteristics

- Vietnamese interviewer working inside a large American-funded morale study.
- Displaced member of a northern family whose anti-communism was tied to concrete fears of retaliation.
- Direct observer who found a captured communist officer more principled and committed than her prior schema allowed.
- Analyst whose uncertainty preceded rather than instantly produced political revision.
- Later critic of the American war and of confidence that North Vietnamese determination could be broken.
