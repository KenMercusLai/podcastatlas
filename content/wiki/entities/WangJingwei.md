# Wang Jingwei / 汪精卫

## Overview

Wang Jingwei appears as the former Nationalist leader who broke with continued resistance, negotiated with Japan, and headed a Japanese-controlled Republic of China from Nanjing.

## Current Profile

[[236-china-and-world-war-ii-part-2-glt5290070743]] compares Wang to Laval or Pétain: he claimed that collaboration could spare Chinese lives and preserve order, but the regime installed in Nanjing in 1940 remained subordinate to Japanese power. His government did not present itself as abandoning Chinese nationalism; it claimed to be the authentic Republic of China and heir of Sun Yat-sen.

The episode also separates Wang's elite political decision from the mixed calculations of people living under occupation. Acceptance, accommodation, or work with local institutions could reflect coercion, property, routine, order, or survival without amounting to wholehearted ideological support.

## Key Characteristics

- He was a senior Nationalist figure before choosing collaboration with Japan.
- He left secretly in 1938 and became head of the Japanese-backed Nanjing regime in March 1940.
- He justified collaboration partly as a way to reduce bombing and civilian suffering.
- His regime competed with Chiang's government for the symbols and legitimacy of the Republic of China.
- He died in Nagoya in 1944, before the regime's collapse in August 1945.

## Evidence

- Political break: [[236-china-and-world-war-ii-part-2-glt5290070743]] describes Wang's 1938 flight, negotiations with Japan, and 1940 installation in Nanjing.
- Claimed rationale: [[236-china-and-world-war-ii-part-2-glt5290070743]] records the argument that cooperation could reduce bombing and save lives in occupied cities.
- Legitimacy claim: [[236-china-and-world-war-ii-part-2-glt5290070743]] says the regime claimed continuity with Sun Yat-sen and the Republic rather than open rejection of Nationalist identity.

## Qualifications

The episode clearly identifies the regime as Japanese-controlled while resisting a uniform account of everyone who lived or served under it. Its discussion of local attitudes is interpretive and does not establish the motives of every official or resident.

## What Changed

- Established Wang's profile as a Nationalist collaborator competing over Republican legitimacy under Japanese domination.
- Distinguished his elite program from the varied survival calculations of occupied populations.
