# Hetton Railway

## Overview

The Hetton Railway was a colliery line near Sunderland built to carry coal from pithead to the River Wear and presented in [[140-the-birth-of-the-railways-glt2177757841]] as [[GeorgeStephenson]]'s first major railway breakthrough.

## Current Profile

In the episode's progression, Hetton is the point where mine transport becomes a complete locomotive system. The eight-mile route is described as the first railway operated entirely by locomotive power rather than horses, tying steam engineering directly to the commercial pressure of moving coal toward river export.

Its importance is therefore systemic rather than merely commemorative. Hetton joins coal extraction, mine engineering, rails, locomotives, and navigable waterways before the better-known passenger lines. George and [[RobertStephenson]] are also linked to mining engineering at the site, reinforcing the overlap between extracting coal and transporting it.

## Key Characteristics

- It was designed around coal haulage from mine to river.
- It is presented as an eight-mile line near Sunderland.
- The episode treats it as the first railway powered entirely by locomotives rather than horses.
- It links Stephenson locomotive work to a complete commercial transport route.
- Its 1822 opening makes it an earlier milestone than the mixed-traffic public lines that followed.

## Evidence

- Purpose and route: [[140-the-birth-of-the-railways-glt2177757841]] says the line moved coal from pithead to the River Wear over roughly eight miles.
- Power system: [[140-the-birth-of-the-railways-glt2177757841]] describes it as the first railway to use locomotive power throughout rather than horsepower.
- Engineering network: [[140-the-birth-of-the-railways-glt2177757841]] connects both Stephensons to railway and mine-engineering work at Hetton.
- Historical placement: [[140-the-birth-of-the-railways-glt2177757841]] identifies 2022 as the railway's bicentenary and treats it as a predecessor to Stockton-Darlington and Liverpool-Manchester.

## Qualifications

The line's length, all-locomotive characterization, geological detail, and priority status follow the episode and remain source-scoped. The source does not provide a full operational history, ownership structure, route survey, or comparison with every contemporary colliery railway.
