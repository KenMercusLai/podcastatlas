# Karolina Westlund

## Overview
Karolina Westlund is presented in [[what-pets-actually-want-need-dr-karolina-westlund-scim2878719314]] as an animal ethologist and educator focused on animal behavior, emotional states, learning, and welfare.

## Current Profile
Westlund's method starts with the animal rather than the owner's preferred story. She asks how a species evolved to live, what sensory information it uses, whether it is predator or prey, how social it is, and which motivated behaviors captivity or domestic life permits or blocks. She then translates that comparison into practical changes in touch, enrichment, housing, introductions, feeding, and veterinary decisions.

Her stance also rejects two opposite interpretive errors. Anthropomorphism can impose human motives where context offers a simpler explanation, while anthropodenial can erase real continuities in emotion, attachment, play, fairness sensitivity, or social regulation. The working position is comparative and qualified: recognize shared processes without assuming identical minds.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses ethology and species history as the starting point for animal-care judgments.
- Separates observable behavior and plausible function from confident claims about private motive.
- Emphasizes animal choice, consent tests, emotional safety, and opportunities for natural behavior.
- Treats breed, sex, age, individual history, and environment as modifiers rather than minor details.
- Marks uncertain interpretations and emerging evidence instead of presenting every practical idea as settled science.

## Evidence
- Comparative method - [[what-pets-actually-want-need-dr-karolina-westlund-scim2878719314]] compares predator-prey status, social organization, sensory ecology, foraging time, and domestication history before interpreting behavior.
- Practical welfare - [[what-pets-actually-want-need-dr-karolina-westlund-scim2878719314]] recommends consent-based petting, nose work, scatter feeding, gradual cat introductions, food-litter separation, and veterinary consultation on sterilization.
- Interpretive restraint - [[what-pets-actually-want-need-dr-karolina-westlund-scim2878719314]] challenges broad dominance stories, gift-giving assumptions about cat prey, and automatic happiness readings of tail wagging.
- Scientific qualification - [[what-pets-actually-want-need-dr-karolina-westlund-scim2878719314]] explicitly leaves some claims about chattering, early weaning, socialization, and attachment as guesses or incompletely supported hypotheses.
