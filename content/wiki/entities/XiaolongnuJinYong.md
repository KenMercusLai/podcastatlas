## Overview

小龙女 / Xiaolongnü is a central [[ReturnOfTheCondorHeroes|《神雕侠侣》]] character whose love for [[YangGuoJinYong|杨过]] is shaped by isolation, sexual violence, social stigma, repeated withdrawal, and a final attempt to preserve his life.

## Current Profile

The episode treats Xiaolongnü's four departures differently rather than calling all of them sacrifice. Early separations grow from sexual violence, misunderstanding, difficulty expressing desire, ritual condemnation, and belief in false information. Her seclusion in the Ancient Tomb helps explain limited social and romantic experience, but the hosts dispute whether it fully explains her shifting perceptiveness and repeated refusal to ask Yang Guo what he wants.

Her final departure has a distinct ethical structure. Believing herself fatally injured, she leaves a sixteen-year promise so Yang Guo can continue living rather than die with her. This is presented as the clearest case of deliberate care, even though it again withholds information and chooses for him.

The episode also challenges how the novel uses her body. Pairing sexual violation with Yang Guo's amputation can create shared stigma and relational equality, yet the equivalence risks accepting a traditional chastity logic in which violence against a woman becomes a loss requiring narrative compensation. Her eventual survival enables reunion but is judged less persuasive than the emotional architecture of waiting.

## Key Characteristics

- Loves Yang Guo while repeatedly responding to conflict through withdrawal rather than joint deliberation.
- Has limited social experience that explains some, but not all, communication failures.
- Bears sexual violence whose plot function must remain separate from any implication of blame or moral diminishment.
- Resists and internalizes social judgment in different moments, especially around teacher-student marriage.
- Uses the sixteen-year promise to preserve Yang Guo's hope and life under expected death.

## Qualifications

The page reflects a conversational literary reading rather than a direct comparison of novel editions. Character consistency, inferred motives, the psychology of trauma, authorial intention, and the healing mechanism remain interpretive. Sexual violence is harm inflicted on Xiaolongnü, not evidence of consent, fault, impurity, or reduced worth; the episode's plot-function analysis does not override that boundary.
