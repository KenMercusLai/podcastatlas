# Dominic de Guzmán

## Overview

Dominic de Guzmán is the preacher presented as developing a mobile, propertyless religious response to alleged heresy before and during the [[AlbigensianCrusade]], laying the foundation for the Dominican order.

## Current Profile

The episode contrasts Dominic's preaching model with both wealthy clerical display and the authority of local good men and women. His friars owned no property, begged, studied, and preached in the world, making institutional Catholicism more capable of competing for lay trust.

Dominic is also described as an intense ascetic who practiced repeated self-flagellation. The source links his model to later inquisitors who treated persuasion, interrogation, education, and pressure as compassionate efforts to rescue people from error. That genealogy does not make Dominic personally responsible for every later inquisitorial practice.

## Key Characteristics

- Preached against alleged heresy before and during the Albigensian conflict.
- Founded a mendicant order organized around poverty, mobility, study, and preaching.
- Offered an institutional rival to the local authority of good men and good women.
- Practiced severe personal asceticism in the episode's account.
- Connected to, but not equated with, later inquisitorial methods.

## Evidence

- Preaching role: [[303-the-bloodiest-crusade-glt7887097153]] places Dominic's anti-heresy preaching before and during the war.
- Institutional model: [[303-the-bloodiest-crusade-glt7887097153]] describes friars who owned no property, begged, and preached among laypeople.
- Later development: [[303-the-bloodiest-crusade-glt7887097153]] connects Dominican practice to [[InquisitorialPastoralCoercion]].

## Qualifications

The episode gives a compressed institutional genealogy. It distinguishes medieval inquisitors from the later early-modern Inquisition and does not establish that Dominic designed the full later procedure.

## What Changed

- Established Dominic as the source's bridge from competitive mendicant preaching to later coercive pastoral investigation.

## Relationships

- [[Cathars]] - contested religious population his preaching sought to reclaim.
- [[AlbigensianCrusade]] - violent setting in which his alternative strategy developed.
- [[InquisitorialPastoralCoercion]] - later method connected to Dominican preaching, study, and salvation claims.
- [[FourthLateranCouncil]] - adjacent institutional reform that tightened clerical and lay religious obligations.
