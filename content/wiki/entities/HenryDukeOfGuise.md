# Henry, Duke of Guise

[[692-elizabeth-i-vs-the-catholics-a-massacre-in-paris-part-2-glt4665799025]] adds Guise before the later Catholic League conspiracy role. In the source's account of the [[StBartholomewsDayMassacre]], Guise has a personal grudge against [[GaspardColigny]] and his men kill Coligny after an earlier failed assassination attempt.

The episode also keeps Guise's role from becoming a complete explanation of the massacre. Coligny's murder is a trigger, but the wider bloodbath also involves royal calculation, armed guards, neighborhood violence, and local grievances. For the English branch, however, Guise becomes part of the Catholic-threat memory that [[WilliamCecil|William Cecil / Lord Burleigh]] later reads as international conspiracy.

Henry, Duke of Guise, appears in [[694-elizabeth-i-vs-the-catholics-a-treacherous-conspiracy-part-4-glt1627515087]] as the Catholic leader whose ambitions connect French factional politics to [[MaryQueenOfScots|Mary, Queen of Scots]]. The episode places him as one of the three Henrys shaping [[France]] after the [[StBartholomewsDayMassacre]].

Guise dominates the [[CatholicLeague]] side of French politics and leads the June 1583 Paris meeting behind the [[ThrockmortonPlot]]. The invasion plan would send troops toward Catholic areas of [[England]], replace [[ElizabethI|Elizabeth I]] with Mary, and draw money from Guise, [[PhilipII|Philip II]], and the papacy.

The episode presents Guise's project as dangerous because it was not only a fantasy. He had support from [[BernardinoDeMendoza]], Catholic exiles such as [[WilliamAllen]], and preparations in Normandy for a Channel crossing before the plot was exposed and French civil war intervened.
