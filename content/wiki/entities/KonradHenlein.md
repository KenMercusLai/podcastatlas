# Konrad Henlein

## Overview

Konrad Henlein was the Sudeten German party leader whose autonomy campaign became an instrument of [[AdolfHitler]]'s engineered crisis against [[Czechoslovakia]] in 1938.

## Current Profile

After the [[Anschluss]], Henlein met Hitler and advanced demands for Sudeten German autonomy. The episode acknowledges economic distress and growing German nationalism in the border regions, but presents the demand as tactical rather than a good-faith endpoint: rejection could justify escalation, while any Czech concession could be declared untrustworthy or insufficient.

When [[EdvardBenes|Edvard Beneš]] offered greater autonomy and federalization, Berlin instructed Henlein's movement to reject the proposal. In September, supporters staged demonstrations and violence that drew Czech police action, allowing Nazi propaganda to recast a deliberately intensified confrontation as evidence of Czech oppression.

## Key Characteristics

- Leader of the principal Sudeten German nationalist party in the 1938 crisis.
- Intermediary between local German grievances and Hitler's expansion strategy.
- Advanced autonomy as an escalating demand rather than a stable settlement.
- Rejected Czech concessions under Berlin's direction.
- Helped turn organized disorder and state response into propaganda for intervention.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to one episode on the opening Sudeten crisis, not a complete biography, organizational history, or account of Sudeten German opinion. Economic depression and minority grievance do not establish that all German speakers supported Henlein or that his party acted independently of Berlin. Precise orders, membership, public attitudes, reported dialogue, and individual responsibility for particular violent acts remain source-scoped.
