# Charles de Gaulle

## Overview

Charles de Gaulle was the French president whose authority appeared close to collapse during May 1968 before an army consultation, parliamentary dissolution, radio address, counter-demonstration, and election restored his political control.

## Current Profile

[[354-paris-1968-the-return-of-de-gaulle-part-2-glt6246374874]] places the crisis against de Gaulle's longer symbolic career: Verdun, Free French leadership, liberation, the Fifth Republic, and an idea of the presidency approaching monarchical embodiment of the nation. That accumulated authority initially became a liability when he dismissed unrest, delivered an ineffective speech, and privately doubted his capacity to govern.

His unannounced trip to Baden-Baden tested whether the French army would remain loyal and restored his confidence, but it also caused panic among officials left in Paris. On returning, he accepted Georges Pompidou's proposal to dissolve the National Assembly, used radio to evoke his wartime broadcasts, framed the contest as order against communist intimidation, and benefited from a large Gaullist demonstration and landslide election result.

## Key Characteristics

- Embodied a personalized and highly presidential idea of French national authority.
- Drew legitimacy from wartime resistance, liberation, and the creation of the Fifth Republic.
- Initially misread and dismissed the student challenge before losing confidence as strikes spread.
- Treated army loyalty as the decisive backstop of state authority during the crisis.
- Recovered politically by shifting the contest from street mobilization to broadcast leadership and elections.
- Used historical memory and anti-communist framing to assemble a public constituency for order.

- Military backstop: [[354-paris-1968-the-return-of-de-gaulle-part-2-glt6246374874]] presents the Baden-Baden meeting with Jacques Massu as a test of army loyalty and a turning point in de Gaulle's confidence.
- Political recovery: [[354-paris-1968-the-return-of-de-gaulle-part-2-glt6246374874]] supports the sequence of return, dissolution, radio address, Gaullist march, and election landslide.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to one narrative episode about May 1968 rather than a full assessment of de Gaulle's career, Algeria policy, constitutional legacy, foreign policy, or resignation in 1969. The episode reconstructs private emotion and counterfactual danger dramatically; the army's actual response to an order for force and the precise causal weight of the Baden-Baden meeting cannot be established from this source alone.
