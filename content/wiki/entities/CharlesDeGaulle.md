# Charles de Gaulle

## Overview

Charles de Gaulle was the 1940 advocate of continued French resistance who later founded and became the symbolic benchmark of the [[FrenchFifthRepublic]]; wartime legitimacy, Algerian crisis leadership, a near-monarchical presidency, independent foreign policy, and recovery during May 1968 created the demanding model against which later presidents are judged.

## Current Profile

[[622-the-nazis-at-war-the-fall-of-france-part-3-glt6447940829]] supplies the origin of that wartime legitimacy. As a junior general and minister, de Gaulle led an unsuccessful but delaying counterattack at Abbeville, supported [[WinstonChurchill|Churchill's]] proposal to continue fighting, accepted the proposed Franco-British union, and opposed the armistice faction. After [[PaulReynaud]] resigned, he flew to London and issued an appeal for resistance that the episode says almost nobody heard at the time. His later authority therefore grew from a politically marginal refusal rather than immediate mass leadership.

[[353-paris-1968-the-students-revolt-part-1-glt6124707164]] presents de Gaulle decades later as the aging embodiment of France who initially misread student unrest and moved unevenly between concession and repression. [[354-paris-1968-the-return-of-de-gaulle-part-2-glt6246374874]] shows that authority nearly failing before his Baden-Baden consultation, return to Paris, dissolution of the Assembly, radio address, counter-demonstration, and election victory restored political control.

[[179-french-presidents-1981-2022-part-2-glt2206497694]] turns de Gaulle's role into a longer institutional legacy. Later presidents are judged not only by policy but by whether they can rise above party, embody France, and perform the grandeur of an office designed around his authority.

[[178-french-presidents-1958-1981-part-1-glt1396593896]] supplies the founding sequence: the Algerian crisis brought de Gaulle back in 1958, constitutional redesign concentrated executive authority, and an exceptionalist foreign policy projected autonomy within the Western alliance. It also distinguishes recovery from final exit: de Gaulle survived May 1968 politically but resigned after losing the 1969 regionalization referendum.

## Key Characteristics

- Turned a little-heard 1940 appeal and rejection of armistice into the wartime legitimacy later reinforced by liberation and the creation of the Fifth Republic.
- Used the Algerian crisis to establish a presidency designed to stand above unstable party politics.
- Embodied a personalized and almost monarchical idea of national authority.
- Pursued visible French autonomy inside the Western alliance and European order.
- Expected French youth to remain disciplined and initially misread the May 1968 revolt.
- Treated army loyalty as the decisive backstop of state authority during the crisis.
- Recovered in 1968 but bound continued rule to referendums strongly enough to resign after defeat in 1969.

## Qualifications

These episodes do not provide a full assessment of de Gaulle's wartime organization, Algeria policy, constitutional design, decolonization, foreign policy, or referendum strategy. The 1940 source compresses his military role, legal position, audience reach, and later Free French development. The May 1968 sources reconstruct private emotion and counterfactual danger dramatically, while both presidential surveys treat grandeur and French autonomy as interpretive standards rather than measurable constitutional or geopolitical facts.
