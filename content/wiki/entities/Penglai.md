## Overview

Penglai / 蓬莱 is the current wiki's maritime immortal destination whose political effect comes from being vivid enough to fund but distant enough to resist verification. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-122-2-ribenren-daodi-shi-bushi-xufu-de-houdai-lhgbbhg3zw8rlx1jjb7lpb5ihihh|Qinji 122-2]] attaches it to [[XuFu|徐福]] and [[QinShiHuang|秦始皇]]; the Western Han sources trace [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] from an early desire to reach it through [[LiShaojunWesternHan|李少君]]'s claimed access to [[AnQisheng|安期生]] and then to mass maritime search.

## Current Profile

In Qinji 122-2, Xu Fu says he encountered a sea deity and saw Penglai but could not retrieve immortal medicine because Qin's offerings were insufficient. The promise converts failure into a request for children, craftsmen, grain, seeds, ships, and further patronage, while later Japan stories remain reception history rather than proof of his destination or of broad descent.

Hanji 259 part 1 supplies an earlier Western Han stage. During a 133 BCE tour toward Bohai, Wudi longs to reach Penglai; Li Shaojun then claims to have met An Qisheng at sea and turns the island into one stage of a longer program joining stove sacrifice, material transformation, gold vessels, immortal encounter, and fengshan. After Li dies, Wudi sends [[KuanShuWesternHan|宽舒]] to keep searching, so death and failed access do not close the premise.

Hanji 361-1 later removes the island from a single-specialist story without making it more verifiable. During Han Wudi's coastal tour, thousands of people claiming knowledge of sea mountains are sent out first, additional vessels are prepared, and later divine reports prompt thousands more to go to sea. Across both reigns, Penglai functions less as mapped geography than as a repeatable horizon for court-funded search.

## Key Characteristics

- Named immortal destination in both current Qin and Western Han source layers.
- Sacred geography that converts imperial mortality anxiety into maritime personnel, ships, supplies, and repeated missions.
- Verification-resistant destination whose nonarrival can sustain rather than automatically end patronage.
- Authority multiplier for specialists who claim personal access to named immortals associated with the island.
- Reception-history node whose later Japan associations must remain separate from evidence for the early voyages.

## Evidence

- Specialist promise and resource escalation: [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-122-2-ribenren-daodi-shi-bushi-xufu-de-houdai-lhgbbhg3zw8rlx1jjb7lpb5ihihh|Qinji 122-2]] says Xu Fu invokes Penglai and insufficient offerings after failing to bring back immortal medicine, leading to further material support.
- Early Han personalization and persistence: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-259-hanwudi-wo-ba-ni-dang-dashi-ni-ba-wo-dang-shazi-1-lqczbezelhdjrtxdrgsfcnmpoklx|Hanji 259 part 1]] says Li Shaojun claims to have met An Qisheng at sea and that Wudi continues the search through Kuan Shu after Li's death.
- Multi-person Han search: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-361-1-hanwudi-liuche-mixin-guishen-de-gushi-lpgclrt_xauneyhm0ri9bi-uenje|Hanji 361-1]] says Han Wudi prepares more ships and sends thousands of sea-mountain claimants out to seek Penglai.
- Persistence after ambiguous signs: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-361-1-hanwudi-liuche-mixin-guishen-de-gushi-lpgclrt_xauneyhm0ri9bi-uenje|Hanji 361-1]] links a footprint report and a vanished-elder story to continued coastal waiting and another large dispatch.

## Qualifications

None of the sources establishes Penglai as a mapped historical island or verifies an immortal encounter. Qinji 122-2 preserves tension between an early account in which Xu Fu returned and the later rhetorical “一去不复返” summary; Japanese shrines and legends show reception, not ancestry. Hanji 259 part 1 does not verify Li Shaojun's meeting, An Qisheng, or Kuan Shu's route and outcome. Hanji 361-1 provides group scale but no voyage outcome, route, casualty total, or independent confirmation of the supernatural reports.

## What Changed

- Added the 133 BCE desire and Li Shaojun-An Qisheng claim as an early Western Han stage.
- Clarified that a named immortal and a patron's posthumous reinterpretation can sustain search before the later mass-dispatch phase.
- Preserved the separation between political effects and verified geography or encounter.
