# Empire Windrush

## Overview

Empire Windrush was the ship whose June 1948 arrival at Tilbury with almost 500 Caribbean migrants became a foundational public image of postwar Black British history.

## Current Profile

[[401-windrush-the-story-of-black-britain-glt4826407019]] treats the voyage as both a documented migration event and a later national symbol. The passengers were British subjects, many with wartime connections to Britain, and their motives included ambition, work, adventure, remittances, and eventual return. The ship's symbolic force comes partly from surviving film and testimony, especially the formal clothing and gangplank images that condensed dignity, expectation, and movement into a memorable scene.

## Key Characteristics

- Left Kingston on 8 June 1948 and arrived at Tilbury on 22 June.
- Carried almost 500 Caribbean migrants within an imperial British-subject citizenship framework.
- Included people with prior wartime service or residence in Britain.
- Became visually iconic through documented arrival footage and passengers' formal clothing.
- Functions as a landmark in [[PostwarCaribbeanBritishSettlement]] and [[WindrushNationalMemory]], not a complete origin story for Black Britain.

## Evidence

- Voyage and legal context: [[401-windrush-the-story-of-black-britain-glt4826407019]] dates the crossing and stresses that passengers moved as British subjects before later immigration barriers.
- Passenger agency: [[401-windrush-the-story-of-black-britain-glt4826407019]] describes wartime service, adventure, work, money, remittances, and intended return as plural motives.
- Symbolic afterlife: [[401-windrush-the-story-of-black-britain-glt4826407019]] explains why film, interviews, formal dress, political reaction, anniversary work, and the later Windrush scandal made the ship a durable public marker.

## Qualifications

The ship did not begin Black presence in Britain, and its passengers do not represent every Caribbean migrant or every later migration wave. Exact passenger categories, official reactions, and the claim that a warship shadowed the voyage remain source-scoped here.

## What Changed

- Created a profile separating the 1948 voyage from its later national-memory role.
