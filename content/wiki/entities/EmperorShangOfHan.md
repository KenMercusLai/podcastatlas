## Overview

汉殇帝 / Emperor Shang of Han is Liu Long, the infant Eastern Han emperor installed by [[EmpressDowagerDengLateHan|Empress Dowager Deng]] after Emperor He died in 106 CE. His reign is brief but structurally important because his mortality makes Deng's succession management, Liu Hu's palace custody, and [[EmperorAnOfHan|Emperor An]]'s installation part of the same regency-security sequence.

## Current Profile

Hanji 781 presents Emperor Shang primarily as a succession hinge rather than an active ruler. Deng selects him over Liu Sheng because Liu Sheng is said to have chronic illness, even though the infant Liu Long is only about one hundred days old at accession. The decision gives Deng immediate regency authority but also exposes the court to the foreseeable risk that an infant emperor may not survive.

The source says Deng had already brought Liu Hu, son of the former deposed crown prince Liu Qing, into the palace because she worried about Liu Long's survival. When Emperor Shang dies after a reign of about eight months, Deng can quickly enthrone Liu Hu as Emperor An. Emperor Shang therefore anchors the transition from post-Emperor He uncertainty into Deng's long regency over An.

## Key Characteristics

- Infant ruler whose accession gives Deng formal regency authority after Emperor He.
- Succession choice made against Liu Sheng, who is treated in the source as unsuitable because of chronic illness.
- Short-lived emperor whose death after about eight months validates Deng's earlier concern about succession fragility.
- Political bridge between Emperor He and Emperor An rather than an independent governing actor in the current evidence.
- Part of the wider pattern where child accession can stabilize a transition while deepening regency dependence.
