# Pigeon

## Overview

The pigeon, presented in [[216-pigeons-glt2770080110]] primarily through the rock dove, is a long-domesticated bird whose homing behavior, proximity to humans, symbolic associations, and shifting reputation made it useful across religion, food production, sport, news, war, rescue, and intelligence.

## Current Profile

Rock doves' preference for cliffs and ledges translated readily to dovecots and cities. Humans then selectively bred pigeons for speed, distance, appearance, and reliability, turning a return-to-home behavior into [[CarrierPigeonCommunication]]. That system was not general two-way messaging: a displaced bird could return to its trained loft, so people had to position birds in advance and design networks around fixed homes.

The episode's cultural history is equally important. Pigeons and doves could signify peace, fidelity, fertility, sacrifice, or divine presence, yet feral urban descendants of domesticated birds later became despised as dirty pests. The contrast suggests that human use and cultural framing, not a simple change in the animal, shaped the reputational reversal.

## Key Characteristics

- Rock-dove ancestry suited to cliffs, architectural ledges, and close human cohabitation.
- Strong homeward orientation exploitable for fast one-way communication.
- Subject to selective breeding for racing, message carrying, and other valued traits.
- Historically useful for food, sport, commerce, military communication, rescue, and intelligence.
- Symbolically associated with peace and sacred themes while urban feral populations attract contempt.

## Qualifications

“Pigeon” and “dove” are culturally loaded common names rather than a clean moral or biological division. The episode's account of navigation acknowledges uncertainty, and its ancient anecdotes, performance numbers, and causal claims should remain source-scoped.
