# Ashok Natarajan

## Overview
Ashok Natarajan is the brother of [[ShekharNatarajan]] in a short [[TomorrowToday]] narrative about cognitive difference, family misunderstanding, associative creativity, reconciliation, and a source-scoped AI approach called “Ashok's Sight.”

## Current Profile
The bounded source presents Ashok as a child whose unusual lunar drawings and calculations were treated as evidence of a broken mind. Decades later, Shekhar narratively reinterprets the markings as an early recognition of craters later catalogued by [[NASA]] and uses Ashok's associative style as inspiration for an AI system.

The durable profile is therefore ethical rather than biographical or technical: Ashok represents the person harmed when unfamiliar cognition is named defective before it is understood. His request to be remembered, rather than merely reproduced in code, makes dignity and recognition more central than the alleged prediction.

## Key Characteristics
- Presented as [[ShekharNatarajan]]'s older brother in the episode.
- Described as making remote associations and perceiving patterns that others did not initially recognize.
- Carries childhood shame after his cognition is treated as broken.
- Becomes the namesake and narrative inspiration for “Ashok's Sight.”
- Asks for personal recognition and social meaning rather than only technical imitation.

## Qualifications
This profile rests on one highly compressed, dramatized episode note. It does not independently establish Ashok's identity, diagnosis, childhood calculations, the alleged match to NASA data, quoted dialogue, or involvement in an implemented AI system. The episode uses “manic episode,” “madness,” and “genius” rhetorically; this page does not infer a clinical diagnosis or generalize a relationship between mental illness and extraordinary ability.
