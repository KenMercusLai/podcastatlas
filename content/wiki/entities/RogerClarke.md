# Roger Clarke

## Overview

Roger Clarke is presented as the author of *Ghosts: A Natural History, 500 Years of Searching for Proof* and as a cultural historian of ghost belief.

## Current Profile

[[66-ghosts-glt2309926019]] centers Clarke's shift from asking whether ghosts exist to asking what people in different periods want ghosts to do. His account compares theology, class, literature, investigation, publicity, and technology while preserving a distinction between the historical value of testimony and proof of supernatural causation.

Clarke is neither presented as a simple debunker nor as an unqualified believer. He treats the [[CockLaneGhost]] and parts of [[BorleyRectory]] as fraud or publicity, yet says the length and witness quality of the [[HintonAmpner]] record still trouble him.

## Key Characteristics

- Studies ghost traditions as historically changing cultural forms.
- Compares religious doctrine, social hierarchy, folklore, literature, and media.
- Distinguishes documented testimony from verified supernatural explanation.
- Accepts hoax and commercial incentives as major parts of haunting history.
- Retains uncertainty where incidents resist a complete account.

## Evidence

- Historical method - [[66-ghosts-glt2309926019]] follows ghost forms from classical burial stories through medieval warnings, Reformation conflict, Methodism, psychical research, and digital media.
- Evidentiary stance - [[66-ghosts-glt2309926019]] contrasts documented reports with hoax, fraud, class bias, and unresolved incidents.
- Comparative cases - [[66-ghosts-glt2309926019]] uses [[HintonAmpner]], the [[CockLaneGhost]], and [[BorleyRectory]] to separate different mixtures of testimony, publicity, and explanation.

## Qualifications

This profile derives from one podcast conversation rather than a full biography or assessment of Clarke's published scholarship. His literary-source claims, readings of particular hauntings, national comparisons, and judgments about fraud remain source-scoped.
