# Oded Rechavi

## Overview
Oded Rechavi is a neurobiology scientist and [[HubermanLab]] guest whose current wiki profile centers on genes, RNA, epigenetic inheritance, and whether acquired information can pass across generations.

## Current Profile
In the current wiki evidence, Rechavi functions as a careful boundary-setter for popular claims about inherited memories and acquired traits. He explains ordinary inheritance through DNA, RNA, proteins, somatic cells, and germ cells, then identifies the [[WeismannBarrier]] and [[EpigeneticReprogrammingBoundary]] as major reasons learned knowledge, muscle gain, and most other acquired states do not become hereditary. The full interview adds [[TransgenerationalInheritanceEvidenceDesign]]: direct prenatal exposure, parental physiology, and shared environment must be separated from germline transmission by generation counting and designs such as IVF or embryo transfer.

His distinctive contribution is the C. elegans evidence. Rechavi presents worms as the place where acquired-trait inheritance can be shown cleanly: small RNAs can cross generations, carry antiviral sequence information, connect brain-produced RNAs with descendant behavior, and have their persistence changed by amplification and inheritance-duration regulators. He repeatedly limits the inference to worms, distinguishes within-life cold/lithium memory work from transgenerational effects, and treats mammal or human intergenerational-memory claims as unresolved.

## Key Characteristics
- Explains inheritance through molecular flow from DNA to RNA to protein rather than through vague family resemblance.
- Separates somatic experience from germline transmission using the [[WeismannBarrier]].
- Uses epigenetic reprogramming to explain why many inherited molecular marks are reset before development.
- Treats RNA, especially small RNAs, as a plausible carrier for intergenerational information in specific organisms.
- Grounds the strongest acquired-trait inheritance claims in [[CElegansModelOrganism]] experiments.
- Uses generation-aware experimental design to distinguish direct exposure from true transgenerational inheritance.
- Explicitly cautions against extending worm evidence or unfinished memory experiments into human claims.

- Two-barrier explanation - [[essentials-genes-inheritance-memories-across-generations-dr-oded-rechavi-scim1624813352]] identifies soma-germline separation and epigenetic reprogramming as core barriers.
- Worm evidence - [[essentials-genes-inheritance-memories-across-generations-dr-oded-rechavi-scim1624813352]] describes inherited antiviral resistance and brain-derived small RNA effects in C. elegans.
- Study-design boundary - [[genes-the-inheritance-of-memories-across-generations-dr-oded-rechavi-scim1011684384]] has Rechavi distinguish directly exposed descendants from paternal F2 and maternal F3 tests, with IVF and embryo transfer as possible controls.
- Inheritance duration - [[genes-the-inheritance-of-memories-across-generations-dr-oded-rechavi-scim1011684384]] connects small-RNA amplification and duration-regulating genes to effects lasting several or, in mutants, many generations.
- Human and unpublished-work qualification - [[genes-the-inheritance-of-memories-across-generations-dr-oded-rechavi-scim1011684384]] says human transmission remains unknown and labels the cold/lithium worm-memory work unfinished and unreviewed.
