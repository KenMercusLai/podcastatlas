# 《情深深雨濛濛》 / Romance in the Rain

## Overview

[[QingShenShenYuMengMeng|《情深深雨濛濛》 / Romance in the Rain]] is [[QiongYao|琼瑶]]'s television reworking of [[YanYuMengMeng|《烟雨濛濛》]], represented here through a source-novel comparison focused on character agency, wartime setting, tonal change, and the ethics of a brighter ending.

## Current Profile

The television version does not simply retain the novel's events with a happier conclusion. It strengthens character charm, adds Du Fei, makes Rǔping more educated and active, and relocates the family story to wartime Shanghai. In the episode's reading, those changes rebuild the causal path: the revised Rǔping can direct courage outward, confront invasion, and continue living rather than follow the novel character's passivity toward death.

The anti-Japanese-war material is interpreted as more than an audience-facing addition because Qiong Yao's own childhood displacement gives wartime memory a biographical connection. Even so, the hosts preserve objections to padding, comic excess, and sentimental dialogue. Their qualified judgment is that adaptation succeeds when a new outcome fits the person the new version has actually constructed.

## Key Characteristics

- Rebuilds rather than merely copies the novel's principal characters.
- Moves a private Taiwan-adjacent family tragedy into wartime Shanghai and a larger historical frame.
- Gives Rǔping greater education, initiative, and capacity to survive loss.
- Adds comic and audience-friendly material while retaining romance and family conflict.
- Uses a brighter ending whose plausibility depends on the revised character trajectory.

## Qualifications

The profile is bounded to one comparative podcast episode rather than a full scene-by-scene study of novel, screenplay, and broadcast version. Claims about the reasons for wartime relocation, the expression of Qiong Yao's memories, and the adaptation's mass appeal remain interpretive. Praise for revised causality coexists with criticism of padding, farce, and sentimental dialogue; a happier ending is not inherently more truthful or less serious than tragedy.

## What Changed

- Established character reconstruction as the basis for the adaptation's different ending.
- Connected wartime relocation with both expanded historical stakes and source-scoped biographical memory.
- Preserved the hosts' mixed judgment of adaptation skill, tonal excess, and popular accessibility.
