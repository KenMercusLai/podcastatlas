# 张既 / Zhang Ji (Cao official, late Han)

## Overview

张既 / Zhang Ji is a Cao-side official whose current wiki profile rests on the 208 mission that converts [[MaTeng|马腾]]'s tentative agreement to enter court into an increasingly difficult-to-reverse public journey.

## Current Profile

Hanji 1088 presents Zhang Ji as the intermediary selected by [[CaoCao|曹操]] before the Jingzhou campaign because he can combine personal access with regional administrative and logistical leverage. After Ma Teng agrees to become weiwei but hesitates, Zhang Ji arranges provisions and official receptions along the route. The public preparations change the decision environment: withdrawal would no longer be a private change of mind but a visible refusal of the court-facing settlement.

The result separates Ma Teng from direct western command while [[MaChao|马超]] retains the force. The source reads this as soft coercion rather than free ceremonial movement, but Zhang Ji's ancestry, family connections, exact grain leverage, and dialogue remain source-scoped.

## Key Characteristics

- Cao-side intermediary in the western settlement before the 208 Jingzhou campaign.
- Combines relationship access with the shadow of administrative and logistical capacity.
- Converts a private promise into a public route of reception and supply.
- Uses ceremony and expectations to raise the political cost of reversal.
- Helps separate Ma Teng's court position from Ma Chao's continuing military command.

- Settlement result: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1088-junying-yeluan-ta-dai-jishiren-jiu-zhenzhule-lu8ocnihz6arqllfcc3ujok6ujil|Hanji 1088]] connects the mission to Ma Teng's weiwei office and Ma Chao's continued command.

## Qualifications

The transcript calls this figure “张继,” a form that would collide with the distinct [[ZhangJiLateHan|张济]]. The surrounding Ma Teng, weiwei, and Guanzhong sequence supports 张既, so this page uses a semantic suffix and does not merge the two men. Claims about inherited Qiang-Di ties, ancestral horse trade, “one hundred thousand” tuntian troops, total control of Sanfu grain, and a hidden threat to cut supplies remain source-scoped.
