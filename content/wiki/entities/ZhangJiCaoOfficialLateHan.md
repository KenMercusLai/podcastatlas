# 张既 / Zhang Ji (Cao official, late Han)

## Overview

张既 / Zhang Ji is a Cao-side intermediary in two western settlements: seeking [[MaTeng|马腾]]'s military help for the Hedong crisis and later converting Ma Teng's tentative agreement to enter court into an increasingly difficult-to-reverse public journey.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1072-duji-danqi-shou-hedong-jing-shi-baixing-yuan-wei-ta-fusi-ltiuwt7v-rjcjpjpovqf9ffl88hu|Hanji 1072]] gives Zhang Ji an earlier relief-diplomacy role. After [[DuJiLateHan|杜畿]] contains the Hedong rebel coalition but cannot finish it alone, Cao Cao sends Zhang Ji west to seek Ma Teng's help. The resulting Ma Teng-associated attack defeats [[ZhangShengLateHan|张盛]]'s side, making Zhang Ji a connector between internal local defense and external western force.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1088-junying-yeluan-ta-dai-jishiren-jiu-zhenzhule-lu8ocnihz6arqllfcc3ujok6ujil|Hanji 1088]] presents Zhang Ji as the intermediary selected by [[CaoCao|曹操]] before the Jingzhou campaign because he can combine personal access with regional administrative and logistical leverage. After Ma Teng agrees to become weiwei but hesitates, Zhang Ji arranges provisions and official receptions along the route. The public preparations change the decision environment: withdrawal would no longer be a private change of mind but a visible refusal of the court-facing settlement.

The result separates Ma Teng from direct western command while [[MaChao|马超]] retains the force. The source reads this as soft coercion rather than free ceremonial movement, but Zhang Ji's ancestry, family connections, exact grain leverage, and dialogue remain source-scoped.

## Key Characteristics

- Cao-side intermediary in the western settlement before the 208 Jingzhou campaign.
- Earlier envoy who obtains Ma Teng-associated help for the Hedong rebellion.
- Combines relationship access with the shadow of administrative and logistical capacity.
- Converts a private promise into a public route of reception and supply.
- Uses ceremony and expectations to raise the political cost of reversal.
- Helps separate Ma Teng's court position from Ma Chao's continuing military command.

## Qualifications

Hanji 1088 calls this figure “张继,” a form that would collide with the distinct [[ZhangJiLateHan|张济]]. The surrounding Ma Teng, weiwei, and Guanzhong sequence supports 张既, so this page uses a semantic suffix and does not merge the two men. Hanji 1072 compresses the earlier mission and does not identify the exact relief contingent or battle mechanics. Claims about inherited Qiang-Di ties, ancestral horse trade, “one hundred thousand” tuntian troops, total control of Sanfu grain, and a hidden threat to cut supplies remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Added the earlier Hedong relief mission through Ma Teng.
- Retained the 208 Ma Teng mission as a ceremony-backed commitment mechanism rather than neutral message delivery.

## Relationships

- [[CaoCao|曹操]] - principal who sends Zhang Ji to reduce western uncertainty.
- [[MaTeng|马腾]] - regional leader whose agreement Zhang Ji turns into a public journey.
- [[DuJiLateHan|杜畿]] - Hedong administrator whose internal defense Zhang Ji's relief mission supports.
- [[MaChao|马超]] - military successor left with the western force after Ma Teng enters court.
- [[GuanzhongRegion|关中]] - regional power and logistics field behind the mission.
- [[ZhangJiLateHan|张济]] - distinct late-Han military figure with a similar romanized name.
