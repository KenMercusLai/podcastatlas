## Overview

张既 / Zhang Ji is a Cao-side western intermediary and administrator whose current profile joins outside-force coordination in Hedong, ceremony-backed movement of [[MaTeng|马腾]] into court, and a post-Hanzhong proposal to reshape the Guanzhong approach through civilian relocation and supply denial.

## Current Profile

Zhang Ji's relief-diplomacy role connects local defense to outside western force. After [[DuJiLateHan|杜畿]] contains the Hedong rebel coalition but cannot finish it alone, [[CaoCao|曹操]] sends Zhang Ji west to seek Ma Teng's help. The resulting Ma Teng-associated attack defeats [[ZhangShengLateHan|张盛]]'s side.

His later Ma Teng mission combines personal access with administrative and logistical leverage. After Ma Teng agrees to become weiwei but hesitates, Zhang Ji arranges provisions and official receptions along the route. The public preparations make withdrawal a visible refusal rather than a private change of mind, separating Ma Teng from direct western command while [[MaChao|马超]] retains the force.

The post-Hanzhong proposal extends the same administrative logic into border security. After Cao Cao withdraws, Zhang Ji reportedly argues that Liu Bei is not yet able to attack Guanzhong but recommends moving residents eastward toward the Fufeng–Tianshui area. A less populated corridor would reduce local supplies available to a future advance. The episode calls for facilitation rather than direct force, but does not establish how voluntary or humane implementation was.

## Key Characteristics

- Cao-side intermediary connecting internal defense to external western reinforcement.
- Combines relationship access with administrative and logistical leverage.
- Uses public ceremony and route preparation to raise the cost of reversing a political commitment.
- Helps separate Ma Teng's court position from Ma Chao's continuing military command.
- Treats population and supply geography as components of post-withdrawal border defense.
- Pairs near-term threat assessment with preparation against a longer-term advance.

## Qualifications

Hanji 1088 calls this figure “张继,” a form that would collide with the distinct [[ZhangJiLateHan|张济]]. The surrounding Ma Teng, weiwei, and Guanzhong sequence supports 张既, so this page uses a semantic suffix and does not merge the two men. Hanji 1072 compresses the relief mission and does not identify the exact contingent or battle mechanics. Claims about family ties, grain leverage, and dialogue remain source-scoped. Hanji 1164 does not verify relocation scale, exact geography, legal authority, free consent, property treatment, destination support, civilian hardship, or military effectiveness.

## What Changed

- Extended Zhang Ji's profile from western mediation into post-withdrawal border planning.
- Added population-and-supply geography as a third mode of administrative leverage.
- Preserved the distinction between reported facilitation and demonstrated voluntary consent.
