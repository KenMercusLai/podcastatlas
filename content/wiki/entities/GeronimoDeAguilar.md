## Overview

Geronimo de Aguilar was a Spanish shipwreck survivor whose knowledge of a Maya language supplied the first Spanish-facing link in [[HernanCortes|Hernan Cortes]]' interpretation chain.

## Current Profile

The episode says Aguilar reached Yucatan after a shipwreck, survived captivity and enslavement, and later joined Cortes at Cozumel. His Maya-language knowledge was strategically valuable because the expedition previously lacked a Spaniard able to speak a local language.

His importance became transitional when [[Malinche]] interpreted between Nahuatl and Maya, allowing Aguilar to relay her words into Spanish. Once Malinche learned Spanish, his role diminished. The sequence shows that conquest depended on multilingual intermediaries before Cortes could understand local diplomacy or rivalry.

## Key Characteristics

- Spanish shipwreck survivor whose captivity story remains dependent on his own account.
- Maya-language speaker who gave Cortes's expedition its first capable Spanish-local interpreter.
- Middle link in the early Nahuatl-Maya-Spanish translation chain.
- Transitional intermediary whose importance declined as Malinche learned Spanish.

## Evidence

- Survival and recruitment: [[385-the-fall-of-the-aztecs-the-woman-who-changed-the-world-part-2-glt5124604457]] describes Aguilar's reported shipwreck, captivity, arrival by canoe, and reunion with Spaniards.
- Interpretation: [[385-the-fall-of-the-aztecs-the-woman-who-changed-the-world-part-2-glt5124604457]] connects his Maya-language skill to Malinche's Nahuatl and the expedition's first workable translation chain.

## Qualifications

The episode explicitly cautions that Aguilar's survival and captivity story comes from Aguilar and cannot be fully verified. It does not identify the Maya language precisely in the supplied account or reconstruct how fluently, neutrally, or accurately the translation chain worked.

## What Changed

- Established Aguilar as translation infrastructure rather than a marginal castaway.
- Located his importance within a chain that became less necessary after Malinche learned Spanish.

## Relationships

- [[Malinche]] - Nahuatl-Maya interpreter whose work he initially relayed into Spanish.
- [[HernanCortes]] - commander who used his language knowledge for coastal diplomacy.
- [[SpanishConquestOfMexico]] - campaign whose political intervention depended on multilingual mediation.
