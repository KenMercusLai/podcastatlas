## Overview

庞统 / Pang Tong is a Jingzhou strategist whose current evidence joins early elite recognition, repeated underuse, and eventual reassessment by [[LiuBei|刘备]] through recommendations from trusted advisers.

## Current Profile

Hanji 1027 introduces Pang Tong as part of the younger Jingzhou talent field that Liu Biao's weak correction and retention channels fail to convert into durable service. Hanji 1083-1084 supply the reputational prehistory: [[PangDegongLateHan|庞德公]] and [[SimaHuiLateHan|司马徽]] make Pang Tong legible through elite appraisal, while the hosts point to sustained conversation and later strategic proposals as evidence that endorsement amplified preparation rather than replacing it.

Hanji 1118 adds the transition from reputation to appointment. The episode says Pang Tong had worked around [[ZhouYuLateHan|周瑜]], was not retained after Zhou Yu's death, and then received only a county post from Liu Bei before being dismissed. [[LuSu|鲁肃]] and [[ZhugeLiang|诸葛亮]] separately recommend a deeper evaluation; after discussing wider affairs with Pang Tong, Liu Bei appoints him as `治中` and later `军师中郎将`. The source therefore shows endorsement correcting a poor initial placement rather than merely creating fame.

Hanji 1125 supplies the first current source in which Pang Tong's advice directly shapes Liu Bei's territorial expansion. He argues that constrained Jingzhou cannot support the larger project, presents populous Yizhou as a necessary base, answers Liu Bei's stated concern for loyalty and benevolence with a disorder-era case for irregular action followed by compensation, and later supports capturing Liu Zhang at the Fu meeting. Liu Bei accepts the territorial objective but rejects the immediate seizure.

Hanji 1131 supplies the three-plan sequence after open conflict approaches. Pang Tong's upper plan is a rapid elite strike on Chengdu; the middle plan uses a feigned return to lure Yang Huai and Gao Pei, remove them, absorb their forces, and advance; the lower plan withdraws toward Baidi before attempting Yizhou again. Liu Bei chooses the middle plan, making Pang Tong's role one of option design across speed, deception, force acquisition, and retreat rather than advocacy of only the fastest seizure.

Hanji 1144 carries the profile from advice into loss. In the episode's account, Pang Tong directs an assault during the prolonged fighting around 雒城 and dies after being struck by arrows at age thirty-six. The source contrasts his rapid, breakthrough-oriented, risk-accepting style with [[ZhugeLiang|诸葛亮]]'s system-building and stable-governance role. The strongest current judgment is organizational rather than counterfactual: Pang Tong's death removes one complementary decision style, increases Liu Bei's reliance on [[FaZheng|法正]], and contributes to Zhuge Liang's entry into Yizhou. The later loss of Jingzhou and death of Guan Yu cannot be assigned to this personnel loss alone.

## Key Characteristics

- Emerges from the Jingzhou scholar and talent network.
- Receives early reputation certification from Pang Degong and Sima Hui.
- Illustrates how a region can contain talent socially without integrating it politically.
- Is initially underused even after reputation has circulated, showing that recognition does not guarantee role fit.
- Reaches a larger Liu Bei role only after trusted recommendations trigger substantive reassessment.
- Converts that role into territorial advice by advocating a Yizhou base and designing routes across rapid seizure, deception, force absorption, and withdrawal.
- Embodies a breakthrough-oriented, higher-risk advisory role whose death redistributes decision and command functions inside Liu Bei's organization.

## Qualifications

The sources still provide a selective rather than complete career. Kinship details, evaluator wording, the exact “南州士之冠冕” exchange, his role under Zhou Yu, Sun Quan's alleged appearance-based rejection, deliberate neglect of county duty, and exact offices remain source-scoped pending narrower evidence. Hanji 1125's dialogue, moral reasoning, capture proposal, and claim of an easy bloodless seizure also require direct textual corroboration. Hanji 1131 supplies the three-plan sequence only through a modern summary; wording, route details, the exact roles of Yang Huai and Gao Pei, and Liu Bei's reasons for selecting the middle plan require direct textual comparison. Hanji 1144's siege duration, arrow sequence, age, burial and shrine details, and risk-preference psychology likewise need narrower verification. “落凤坡” is retained as later place attachment rather than the verified death site, while the chain from Pang Tong's death to Jingzhou's later loss remains a qualified strategic inference rather than settled causation. Hanji 1083's “近日不错” explanation is textually unstable and is not treated as a verified idiom claim.

## What Changed

- Extended the profile through Pang Tong's death during the assault on 雒城.
- Distinguished Pang Tong's breakthrough-oriented style from Zhuge Liang's system-building role without ranking them absolutely.
- Added the redistribution of advisory capacity after Pang Tong's death while qualifying the claimed Jingzhou–Guan Yu causal chain.
- Separated the later “落凤坡” place tradition from verified event history.
