## Overview

马王爷 / Ma Wangye enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-390-2-shi-shen-shi-gui-jinridi-zhen-zhangle-san-zhi-yanjing-ljk1eueoe8tclfoajkat5y1igqwt|Hanji 390-2]] as the three-eyed horse deity invoked to explain later folklore around [[JinMidiWesternHan|金日磾]]. The source uses the folk saying about Ma Wangye's three eyes to frame a historical question: why later imagination could turn Jin's alert palace service into a supernatural ability to see hidden danger.

## Current Profile

The page is currently source-scoped to one podcast note. Hanji 390-2 describes Ma Wangye as a horse god, gives "水草马明王" as a fuller name, and mentions multiple origin explanations: a star deity, Huaguang from `Nanyouji`, Yin Jiao from `Fengshen Yanyi`, and a tradition connecting Ma Wangye to Jin Midi. The episode's historical use of the figure is metaphorical: the "third eye" stands for detecting malicious intent and seeing a palace plot early, not for proving a literal supernatural claim.

## Key Characteristics

- Folk horse deity associated in the source with a three-eyed image.
- Figure whose origin is presented as plural and tradition-dependent rather than settled.
- Moralized deity image: the third eye recognizes malicious intent and punishes evil.
- Folklore bridge used to explain why Jin Midi could be mythologized after detecting palace danger.
- Source-scoped cultural reference rather than an independently reconstructed religious history.

## Qualifications

This page does not reconstruct Ma Wangye worship, ritual geography, iconographic history, temple practice, or the textual history of the cited origin traditions. It preserves Hanji 390-2's use of the deity as folklore attached to Jin Midi and should not be read as settling whether Ma Wangye "is" Jin Midi in all traditions.
