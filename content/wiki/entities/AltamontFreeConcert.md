# Altamont Free Concert

## Overview

The Altamont Free Concert was the Rolling Stones' December 1969 free concert near San Francisco. Poor siting, rushed organization, intoxication, an unsafe stage boundary, and Hells Angels security accompanied repeated violence, including Meredith Hunter's fatal stabbing, and made the event a symbol of countercultural rupture.

## Current Profile

The concert answered criticism of high ticket prices but moved to a weak venue after permit difficulties. Grateful Dead management helped arrange local Hells Angels as security, compensated with beer. About 300,000 attended in the episode's account, many quickly becoming intoxicated, while Angels carried cues and chains.

[[TheRollingStones|The Stones]] stopped “Sympathy for the Devil” amid fighting. Hunter, an eighteen-year-old Black attendee who apparently drew a revolver, was repeatedly stabbed by a Hells Angel. Two others were run over and another drowned. The band completed its set without understanding the full scale and left by helicopter.

Contrasted with Woodstock, Altamont became a narrative endpoint for 1960s optimism, though the episode argues that war, riots, political reaction, and a darker American mood had already changed the culture.

## Key Characteristics

- It answered criticism over commercial ticket prices.
- Site selection and rushed logistics created safety weaknesses.
- Beer-paid Hells Angels blurred security and participation in violence.
- Hunter's killing made race, weapons, and disorder central to memory.
- Multiple deaths prevent reduction to one filmed stabbing.
- Its end-of-sixties meaning is retrospective symbolism, not a precise switch.
