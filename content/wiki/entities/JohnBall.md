# John Ball

## Overview

John Ball was the radical English preacher who gave Christian egalitarian and anticlerical language to the [[PeasantsRevolt1381|Peasants’ Revolt of 1381]].

## Current Profile

Ball was probably from Colchester and trained as a priest in York. He repeatedly clashed with [[SimonSudbury|Simon Sudbury]], who condemned him as a vagabond preacher of doctrines contrary to church teaching. His attacks on wealthy landowners, clerical hierarchy, and the pope drew on Christian equality, most memorably in the question attributed to him: “When Adam delved, and Eve span, who then was a gentleman?”

The sources associate Ball with the movement’s religious radicalism and with [[WatTyler|Wat Tyler’s]] Smithfield demands to reduce church hierarchy and property. Possible overlap with John Wycliffe’s ideas is discussed, but the relationship is not established as a direct line of influence.

After the revolt spread beyond London, Ball was captured, tried, and executed during the wider suppression. His role helps explain why the rising cannot be reduced to taxation or labor grievance alone: arguments about Christian truth, clerical wealth, hierarchy, and justice interacted with material demands and [[RebelRoyalism|royalist allegiance]].

## Key Characteristics

- Radical preacher associated with egalitarian and anticlerical strands of the rising.
- Cleric repeatedly disciplined by Simon Sudbury for itinerant and heterodox preaching.
- Ideological figure linking material grievance to religious claims about truth and justice.
- Influence invoked to interpret Tyler’s church-related demands at Smithfield.
- Captured and executed during the post-London suppression.

- Chronology and hostile memory: [[413-the-peasants-revolt-england-erupts-part-1-glt8198769595]] doubts Walsingham's Blackheath sermon scene while retaining Ball's wider ideological influence.
- Religious program: [[414-the-peasants-revolt-londons-burning-part-2-glt6726049231]] connects Ball to Tyler’s attack on church hierarchy and property.
- Wider ideological setting: [[414-the-peasants-revolt-londons-burning-part-2-glt6726049231]] discusses proto-Protestant currents and possible Wycliffite affinities without treating them as settled.
- Fate: [[414-the-peasants-revolt-londons-burning-part-2-glt6726049231]] records Ball’s capture, trial, and execution during suppression.

## Qualifications

The sources do not reconstruct Ball’s full teaching, writings, or movements. Walsingham was a hostile elite narrator, and the episode argues that Ball could not have delivered the attributed Blackheath sermon on the reported date. Proto-Protestant language is retrospective, and any direct intellectual dependency on John Wycliffe remains possible rather than demonstrated.

## What Changed

- Added Ball's clerical formation, conflict with Sudbury, and Christian-egalitarian critique of wealth and servitude.
- Separated his wider influence from the chronologically doubtful Blackheath sermon scene.
