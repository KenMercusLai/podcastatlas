# Nicolas de Condorcet

## Overview

Nicolas de Condorcet was an Enlightenment thinker and revolutionary politician presented by the episode as a notable male supporter of women's suffrage.

## Current Profile

The episode places Condorcet inside a minority current willing to extend revolutionary political rights to women. It connects his position to [[OlympeDeGouges|Olympe de Gouges]] and his wife [[SophieDeGrouchy|Sophie de Grouchy]], while using his puzzlement at women's admiration for anti-suffrage leaders to expose the limits of a purely interest-based model of political identification.

## Key Characteristics

- He supported female suffrage.
- He belonged to a more female-inclusive Girondin intellectual milieu in the episode's account.
- His position is linked to de Gouges and de Grouchy without being reduced solely to their influence.
- He was puzzled that women could admire Robespierre despite his opposition to their suffrage.

- The influence attributed to de Gouges and de Grouchy is suggestive and source-scoped, not a complete causal explanation of his views.
- His reaction to women who admired Robespierre does not establish what most women believed.
