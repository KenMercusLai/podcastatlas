## Overview

符融 / Fu Rong enters the wiki through Hanji 862 as a late-Han reputation critic. His key role is to warn [[LiYingLateHan|李膺]] that [[HuangYunLateHan|黄允]] and [[JinWenjingLateHan|晋文经]] are being treated as great men before their conduct and achievements have been publicly established.

## Current Profile

The source uses Fu Rong as a corrective observer inside a prestige-heavy late-Han field. Huang Yun and Jin Wenjing have made themselves scarce while officials and disciples orbit them; appointment decisions are affected by their praise or criticism. Fu Rong's warning identifies the risk: an empty reputation can distort public values and administrative judgment when it is treated as evidence.

Fu Rong's significance is therefore diagnostic. He does not punish the two men directly; he makes their reputation field visible to Li Ying, after which their fame and visitors decline. In this source, his value is the ability to tell the difference between reputation as evidence and reputation as performance.

## Key Characteristics

- Late-Han scholar-observer whose source role is reputation correction.
- Critic of fame that lacks visible conduct and achievement.
- Intermediary who brings the concern to Li Ying.
- Counterweight to Huang Yun and Jin Wenjing's scarcity-based prestige.
- Source case for identifying empty reputation before it damages selection norms further.
