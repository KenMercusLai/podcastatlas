# David Linden

## Overview
David Linden is a neuroscientist and Johns Hopkins professor presented in the source as a researcher of cerebellar neuroplasticity and an interpreter of sensory variation, human individuality, brain-body biology, and mortality.

## Current Profile
In the bounded source, Linden's characteristic move is to replace binaries with interacting biological mechanisms. Nature versus nurture becomes [[DevelopmentalIndividuality]]; mind versus body becomes [[BiologicalMindBodyMechanisms]]; and a simple synapse-centered account of change becomes a plural view of [[Neuroplasticity]]. He separates evidence levels carefully, distinguishing mouse mechanisms, human association, speculation, and clinical inference.

His scientific stance also shapes his response to rare cardiac synovial sarcoma. Curiosity about his illness and mental states gave him some agency during difficult treatment, but it did not remove anger, suffering, or the relational pain of leaving loved ones. The result is a mortality account centered on gratitude for sentience, creative life, and love rather than a claim that illness reliably produces wisdom.

## Key Characteristics
- Reframes individuality as heritability interacting with broad experience through stochastic development.
- Uses sensory systems to show that different nervous systems can construct different experienced realities.
- Interprets cerebellar function through prediction rather than balance or motor coordination alone.
- Treats mind-body claims as testable biological hypotheses involving neural, hormonal, immune, and vascular pathways.
- Maintains explicit boundaries between animal evidence, human association, mechanistic plausibility, and treatment claims.
- Uses scientific curiosity as a personal source of agency while resisting a universal prescription for facing mortality.
