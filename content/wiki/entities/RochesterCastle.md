# Rochester Castle

## Overview

Rochester Castle is a Norman fortification overlooking the River Medway whose strategic purpose was to control a major crossing and the approach toward London.

## Current Profile

The episode situates the castle beside the road and bridge that made [[Rochester]] strategically important. An early wooden fortification is associated with Odo of Bayeux, later stone construction with Gundulf, and the surviving keep with the twelfth century.

Its significance comes less from height alone than from route control. The castle appears in narratives of sieges, captured kings, the Peasants' Revolt, and royal movement because authority over Rochester could shape access between Kent, the Channel coast, and London.

Within the joined high-street walk, the castle and [[RochesterCathedral]] embody medieval military and ecclesiastical authority before the route reaches Chatham's naval-industrial system.

## Key Characteristics

- Fortification positioned to command the Medway crossing and London route.
- Norman site with successive wooden and stone phases.
- Monumental keep associated with twelfth-century castle building.
- Political landscape connected to siege, rebellion, captivity, and royal travel.

## Qualifications

This profile relies on one popular-history episode. Construction attribution, architectural superlatives, exact phases, and the castle's role in individual political events require archaeological and documentary verification. Route control explains strategic relevance but does not by itself explain every siege or occupation.
