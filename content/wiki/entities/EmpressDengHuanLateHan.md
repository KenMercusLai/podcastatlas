## Overview

邓皇后 / Empress Deng is the Huan-era empress whose bounded profile now begins before the anti-[[LiangJiLateHan|Liang Ji]] coup. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-847-nvren-biande-haoming-cong-dongde-zhe-liang-zi-kaishi-lrlov43jtz0vq-o-5m8r2cqo0etx|Hanji 847]] shows Deng Meng entering [[EmperorHuanOfHan|Emperor Huan]]'s palace through a [[SunShouLateHan|Sun Shou]] channel and becoming the object of Liang Ji's adoption or surname strategy. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-848-shunjian-kuatai-wo-weihe-shuo-ta-si-you-yu-gu-lgqvgmxlwga2fknmtxpj3ya9chk0|Hanji 848]] records her post-coup elevation and surname restoration. Hanji 865 adds the later deposition notice and Deng Meng / three-surname identity detail, while Hanji 868 places her case inside the broader palace sequence before [[EmpressDowagerDouMiao|Dou Miao]] and [[TianShengLateHan|Tian Sheng]].

## Current Profile

Hanji 847 gives the earliest Deng layer. Deng Meng's father Deng Xiang is dead, and her mother [[XuanDengMotherLateHan|Xuan]] has a remarriage-linked connection to Sun Shou's circle. Sun Shou sends Deng Meng into the palace, Huan favors her, and Deng becomes guiren. Liang Ji then tries to make her a Liang-family asset through adoption or surname change, but [[BingZunLateHan|Bing Zun]] warns Xuan not to accept the plan. Liang kills Bing Zun and tries to kill Xuan, making Deng's natal household the crisis that pushes Huan toward action.

Hanji 848 supplies the post-coup turn. After the Liang purge, Huan installs the consort then described as Liang Guiren as empress, demotes the former Liang empress's tomb identity because of hatred toward the Liang family, and later restores Deng Meng's Deng surname when he learns she is Deng Xiang's daughter.

The later pages show that elevation does not become stable protection. After the Liang empress is removed from the story, Emperor Huan favors Empress Deng, then [[GuoGuirenHuanLateHan|郭贵人]] becomes her rival. Hanji 865 presents the rivalry as mutual accusation and court struggle: Deng fails, is deposed on the second-month date preserved by the source, is confined in the baoshi, and dies in grief. The same notice says 邓万世 and 邓会 are executed, making her fall a household-punishment event rather than only a private emotional defeat.

In the episode's structure, Empress Deng provides the first example in a chain where success inside palace rivalry does not equal stable security. Her case sets up the later Dou Miao and Tian Sheng contrast between title, affection, lineage, and post-patron vulnerability.

## Key Characteristics

- Huan-era consort whose palace entry is routed through Sun Shou before the anti-Liang coup.
- Favorite whom Liang Ji tries to absorb through adoption or surname change.
- Consort whose mother Xuan and brother-in-law Bing Zun become trigger figures in the Liang crisis.
- Post-coup empress whose surname and status are used to remove Liang-family symbolic traces.
- Huan-era empress whose later position depends on palace favor and rivalry.
- Rival of Guo Guiren in the episode's harem sequence.
- Deposed consort whose death is framed as emotional, political, and confinement-related defeat.

## Qualifications

The page does not reconstruct Empress Deng's full lineage, office chronology, or independent factional base. Hanji 847 supplies the fuller pre-coup adoption and attempted-Xuan-assassination layer, while Hanji 848 compresses or reports the trigger as Xuan being killed. The page keeps both evidence statements visible until another source resolves the tension. Hanji 868 presents her as part of Emperor Huan's harem-politics sequence, so claims about her private motives remain source-scoped. Hanji 865's Deng Meng and three-surname explanation is retained as the episode's identity note, not expanded into a complete prosopography.
