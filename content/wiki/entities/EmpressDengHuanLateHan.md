## Overview

邓皇后 / Empress Deng is the Huan-era empress whose bounded profile now begins with the 159 CE anti-[[LiangJiLateHan|梁冀]] coup and ends with palace rivalry, deposition, confinement, and death. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-848-shunjian-kuatai-wo-weihe-shuo-ta-si-you-yu-gu-lgqvgmxlwga2fknmtxpj3ya9chk0|Hanji 848]] ties Deng Meng's mother Xuan to the trigger for [[EmperorHuanOfHan|汉桓帝]]'s move against Liang and records Deng's post-coup elevation and surname restoration. Hanji 865 adds the later deposition notice and Deng Meng / three-surname identity detail, while Hanji 868 places her case inside the broader palace sequence before [[EmpressDowagerDouMiao|窦妙]] and [[TianShengLateHan|田圣 / 田胜]].

## Current Profile

Hanji 848 gives the earliest Deng layer. The episode says Liang Ji has Deng Meng's mother Xuan assassinated, making Deng's natal side part of the immediate trigger for Huan's purge of Liang. After the coup, Huan installs the consort then described as Liang Guiren as empress, demotes the former Liang empress's tomb identity because of hatred toward the Liang family, and later restores Deng Meng's Deng surname when he learns she is Deng Xiang's daughter.

The later pages show that elevation does not become stable protection. After the Liang empress is removed from the story, Emperor Huan favors Empress Deng, then [[GuoGuirenHuanLateHan|郭贵人]] becomes her rival. Hanji 865 presents the rivalry as mutual accusation and court struggle: Deng fails, is deposed on the second-month date preserved by the source, is confined in the baoshi, and dies in grief. The same notice says 邓万世 and 邓会 are executed, making her fall a household-punishment event rather than only a private emotional defeat.

In the episode's structure, Empress Deng provides the first example in a chain where success inside palace rivalry does not equal stable security. Her case sets up the later Dou Miao and Tian Sheng contrast between title, affection, lineage, and post-patron vulnerability.

## Key Characteristics

- Huan-era consort whose mother Xuan's assassination helps trigger Huan's anti-Liang coup.
- Post-coup empress whose surname and status are used to remove Liang-family symbolic traces.
- Huan-era empress whose later position depends on palace favor and rivalry.
- Rival of Guo Guiren in the episode's harem sequence.
- Deposed consort whose death is framed as emotional, political, and confinement-related defeat.
- Palace actor identified by the source as Deng Meng and as a woman whose surname/status shifted through court adoption and imperial favor.
- Fall case whose relatives are punished after her deposition.

## Qualifications

The page does not reconstruct Empress Deng's full lineage, office chronology, or independent factional base. Hanji 848 provides the earliest mother-assassination, elevation, and surname-restoration context, but it does not settle every adoption or title detail. Hanji 868 presents her as part of Emperor Huan's harem-politics sequence, so claims about her private motives remain source-scoped. Hanji 865's Deng Meng and three-surname explanation is retained as the episode's identity note, not expanded into a complete prosopography.

## What Changed

- Hanji 848 adds the Xuan assassination trigger, post-coup empress elevation, Liang tomb demotion, and Deng surname restoration.
- Added Hanji 865's deposition, baoshi confinement, death, and household-punishment details.
- Preserved Empress Deng as an earlier Huan-era example of palace status failing to secure durable protection.
