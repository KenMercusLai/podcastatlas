## Overview

颜异 / Yan Yi is a Western Han official whose current wiki evidence centers on one lethal court case: after opposing a high-valued white-deer-skin currency proposal, he is later investigated by [[ZhangTangWesternHan|张汤]] and executed when silence plus a slight lip movement is treated as inward defamation of an imperial edict.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-337-gudai-zuiming-keyi-you-duo-huangtang-lqoqdtg35ua6m9y4shcz9u8zygp1|Hanji 337]] presents Yan as clean, upright, and capable enough to rise to the Nine Ministers. His policy role is concrete but narrow: when [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] asks about a fiscal measure associated with Zhang Tang, Yan objects that a forty-wan skin token used as tribute padding is disproportionate to tribute worth only several thousand cash.

The later case makes him a boundary figure between remonstrance and silence. A guest criticizes a new edict; Yan does not assent aloud, but “微反唇” is observed. Zhang argues that a Nine Minister who sees an inconvenient edict should speak, and that failing to do so while inwardly criticizing it deserves death. Yan's profile therefore does not show direct speech alone becoming dangerous: it shows political prosecution claiming authority over an ambiguous expression and an inferred inner judgment.

## Key Characteristics

- Clean and upright official presented as rising to Nine Minister rank.
- Fiscal dissenter who argues that the skin-currency valuation reverses principal and accessory.
- Existing opponent of Zhang Tang before the terminal investigation.
- Silent listener whose slight lip movement is treated as evidence of inward criticism.
- Capital defendant whose case becomes the episode's emblem of 腹诽 and court-wide chilling effects.

- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-337-gudai-zuiming-keyi-you-duo-huangtang-lqoqdtg35ua6m9y4shcz9u8zygp1|Hanji 337]] says Yan does not answer a guest's criticism of an edict but slightly moves his lips, after which Zhang Tang characterizes the conduct as unspoken inward defamation and secures a death judgment.

Political aftereffect:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-337-gudai-zuiming-keyi-you-duo-huangtang-lqoqdtg35ua6m9y4shcz9u8zygp1|Hanji 337]] interprets the case as teaching ministers to flatter and remain silent rather than transmit criticism upward.

## Qualifications

The source does not provide a full biography, office chronology, independent legal record, or primary-text comparison. Its “严义” form is normalized here to 颜异 because the distinctive “异不应，微反唇” and 腹诽 sequence identifies the received Yan Yi account. The exact currency mechanics, Yan's words, Zhang's private motive, trial procedure, statutory basis, final punishment, and scale of the later chilling effect remain episode-attributed. The page treats 腹诽 as the case's transmitted characterization, not as proof of a fully reconstructed codified offense.

## What Changed

- Created a canonical Yan Yi identity while preserving “严义” as the supplied transcript's variant.
- Separated Yan's documented policy objection and ambiguous nonverbal conduct from claims about his unobservable inner thought.
- Framed the case as a speech-and-silence prosecution whose legal classification remains source-scoped.
