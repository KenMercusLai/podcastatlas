# Sophie Scholl

## Overview

Sophie Scholl was a German student and member of the [[WhiteRose|White Rose]] who joined its anti-Nazi leaflet campaign, was arrested with her brother [[HansScholl|Hans]], and was executed in February 1943.

## Current Profile

The episode presents Sophie as someone whose opposition grew through family influence, religious conviction, reading, work, and resistance to enforced conformity. She had participated in Nazi youth culture but challenged its gendered expectations, read banned authors, and criticized the elevation of force over thought while completing labor and munitions assignments during the war.

After arriving in Munich in 1942, Sophie recognized that Hans and his friends were behind the White Rose leaflets and chose to join them. She took part in the final distribution at Munich University on February 18, 1943. During interrogation, she rejected an invitation to portray herself as merely misled by her brother and accepted responsibility for the action; she was tried and executed with Hans and Christoph Probst four days after the arrest.

## Key Characteristics

- Early participant in Nazi youth culture who later rejected its discipline and worldview.
- Independent reader who resisted gendered and intellectual conformity.
- White Rose participant who knowingly joined an existing resistance network.
- Defendant who refused an exculpatory story based on her brother's influence.
- Executed resister whose postwar memory became a durable symbol of conscience and freedom.

## Evidence

- Formation of judgment - [[256-germany-the-white-rose-glt6296738472]] connects family opposition, religious life, banned reading, munitions work, and criticism of unquestioning belief in force to Sophie's development.
- Entry into resistance - [[256-germany-the-white-rose-glt6296738472]] says she recognized material from Hans's books in the leaflets and then chose to join the group.
- Responsibility and death - [[256-germany-the-white-rose-glt6296738472]] recounts the university distribution, interrogation, refusal to blame Hans, trial, final family meeting, communion, and execution.

## Qualifications

The source is a compressed narrative centered on the siblings and does not provide a complete documentary biography. Its account of Sophie's thoughts and interrogation depends on the episode's selection and retelling, and her later iconic status should not erase the other White Rose members or turn gradual formation into a claim of innate heroism.

## What Changed

- Created a profile joining Sophie's intellectual formation, chosen participation, and acceptance of responsibility.
- Qualified the heroic afterlife by preserving the wider network and the gradual nature of her opposition.
