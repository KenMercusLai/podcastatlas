## Overview

窦妙 / Empress Dowager Dou Miao is a late-Han empress dowager whose page now spans the 168 CE anti-eunuch coup failure, her confinement after [[DouWuLateHan|窦武]]'s defeat, her later living recognition by [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]], and the 172 CE burial-status dispute. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-887-dang-zhuduizhang-pengshang-zhuduiyou-jieguo-taican-lvyh9a-kvsdqaokmylgg139bvhfk|Hanji 887]] adds the crisis layer: the host says Dou Miao failed to accept Dou Wu's proposed removal of Wang Fu and other eunuchs, then was moved to Nangong after the coup collapsed.

## Current Profile

Dou Miao's profile is a case of status without secure power. In the 168 CE crisis, her formal position does not protect the Dou family once the eunuch side wins. The source's interpretation is that she enjoys the appearance of authority but does not grasp the lethal stakes quickly enough; after Dou Wu's death, she is confined in Nangong and the remaining Dou family is exiled far away.

The later sources show that her status remains contested rather than erased. Emperor Ling remembers that Dou Miao and Dou Wu helped his accession and temporarily improves her treatment. After her death, Cao Jie and [[WangFuLateHan|王甫]] try to lower her funeral and burial status, while [[ChenQiuLateHan|陈球 / 陈求]] and [[LiXianTaiweiLateHan|李贤]] argue that she should share [[EmperorHuanOfHan|汉桓帝]]'s burial and sacrificial order. The final burial decision becomes a narrow ritual repair that does not undo the wider Dou/Chen disaster.

## Key Characteristics

- Dou Miao holds high formal status but lacks the secure command and information position needed to protect the Dou family during the coup.
- Her confinement after the failed coup makes her a living reminder of the Dou/Chen anti-eunuch grievance.
- Emperor Ling's later support shows that accession memory still matters even after the eunuch side wins.
- Her death turns family guilt, ritual role, and political memory into a public burial-status dispute.
- The final recognition is real but narrow: it repairs her posthumous status without fully rehabilitating the destroyed faction.

## Qualifications

- Hanji 887's claim that Dou Miao misread the situation is the host's interpretation of her political judgment, not a complete reconstruction of her private reasoning.
- The page distinguishes Dou Miao's own ritual status from Dou Wu's political failure; the final burial decision does not prove that the court fully accepted the anti-eunuch side's case.
- Her confinement, living support, funeral treatment, and burial status belong to related but distinct stages and should not be compressed into one simple rise-or-fall story.

## What Changed

- Hanji 887 adds the crisis-stage Dou Miao before her later living-support and burial-dispute branches.
- The page now treats her as a status-without-secure-power case during the coup, not only as a posthumous ritual case.
- The later burial recognition is framed as narrow repair after earlier confinement and family disaster.
