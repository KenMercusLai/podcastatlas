# Scottsboro Boys

## Overview

The Scottsboro Boys were nine young Black men whose 1931 Alabama prosecution for an alleged rape became an international symbol of racial injustice and, in the current source, a challenge to the moral exceptionalism surrounding Olympic Berlin.

## Current Profile

[[hitler-s-olympics-part-9-a-plague-on-both-your-houses-hitler-s-olympics-part-9-a-plague-on-both-your-houses|Hitler’s Olympics, Part 9]] presents the prosecution as a system failure rather than an isolated bad trial. The defendants met their attorney only hours before proceedings, faced all-white juries drawn from a voting system designed to exclude Black Alabamians, and received death sentences despite the episode's claim that no rape occurred and one accuser later withdrew her allegation.

Appeals, retrials, organizing, and international protest carried the case to the United States Supreme Court and made it a global indictment of American white supremacy. The episode's Olympic argument turns on the resulting contrast: German demonstrators protested the case, yet the United States faced no comparable movement to boycott Los Angeles 1932.

## Key Characteristics

- Group of nine young Black defendants arrested in Alabama in 1931.
- Subjected to rushed proceedings and all-white juries within a disfranchising legal order.
- Initially faced multiple death sentences despite severely deficient evidence and process.
- Became an international civil-rights cause through appeals and public campaigning.
- Used in the episode to expose unequal scrutiny of Los Angeles 1932 and Berlin 1936.

Ages, evidence, procedural chronology, prison totals, activist roles, and the episode's causal explanation for the absence of a boycott require primary and specialist verification.
