# Spencer Kaplan

## Overview
Spencer Kaplan is presented as a Yale PhD candidate who conducted two years of anthropological fieldwork in San Francisco's AI research community.

## Current Profile
Kaplan's role in the source is interpretive rather than predictive. He reports how AI safety workers form and act on catastrophic-risk beliefs, connecting their choices to firsthand model work, [[EffectiveAltruism]], and a Bay Area norm of personal agency while declining to validate any particular extinction estimate.

## Key Characteristics
- Conducted two years of embedded fieldwork among San Francisco AI researchers.
- Observed online spaces, group houses, parties, reading groups, and salon-style discussions.
- Treats AI-risk belief as sincere enough to shape careers, savings, and ordinary life for some workers.
- Explains both public resignation and continued lab employment as possible impact-maximizing choices.
- Distinguishes the social practice of numerical risk estimation from the accuracy of any particular estimate.
