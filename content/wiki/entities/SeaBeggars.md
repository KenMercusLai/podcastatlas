# Sea Beggars

## Overview

The Sea Beggars appear in the wiki as Calvinist-leaning privateer forces funded by [[WilliamOfOrange|William of Orange]] during the [[DutchRevolt]], most important here for turning the accidental capture of Brill into a wider revolt opening.

## Current Profile

The episode treats the Sea Beggars as irregular maritime resistance rather than a tidy state navy. William funds them as part of a broader anti-Spanish campaign, while his German mercenary offensives fail to force decisive battle. By 1572 the revolt seems close to defeat, but the Sea Beggars arrive at Brill, find the garrison absent, occupy the port, sack Catholic churches, and create a rebel stronghold that helps trigger uprisings in Holland and Zeeland.

## Key Characteristics

- Irregular maritime rebel force operating against Spanish Habsburg power.
- Funded by [[WilliamOfOrange|William of Orange]] during a desperate phase of resistance.
- Calvinist-leaning and anti-Spanish, but attached to a revolt the source calls national as well as religious.
- Capture Brill on 1 April 1572 after finding the garrison absent.
- Turn a near-defeated rebellion into a practical foothold for wider uprisings.

## Evidence

- Funding and role: [[680-the-netherlands-the-revolt-that-made-the-modern-world-part-4-glt4209356543]] says William funded the Sea Beggars as privateers while also raising German mercenary armies.
- Confessional and national mix: [[680-the-netherlands-the-revolt-that-made-the-modern-world-part-4-glt4209356543]] describes them as Calvinist-leaning pirates but says the revolt was national rather than purely religious.
- Brill opening: [[680-the-netherlands-the-revolt-that-made-the-modern-world-part-4-glt4209356543]] says their occupation of Brill triggered uprisings across Holland and Zeeland.

## Qualifications

The source emphasizes the Sea Beggars' catalytic role at Brill. It does not settle their full command structure, legality, social composition, or later naval history.
