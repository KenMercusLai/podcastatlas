## Overview

Zhang Bu / 张步 is synthesized as a Qi-region adversary whose current profile now begins before open campaign defeat: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-688-cong-yongbing-baiwan-dao-kuibuchengjun-dengyu-jingli-le-shenme-lpabdvtmhtrf37puwvgi4c9ajp60|Hanji 688]] shows Liu Yong trying to make him a Qi king and [[FuLongEasternHan|Fu Long]] failing to pull him fully toward [[LiuXiu|刘秀 / 光武帝]]. Later source notes then follow outer-network destabilization, tactical vulnerability at [[Linzi|临淄]], battlefield defeat, conditional surrender, controlled settlement, late loss of room, flight, and execution.

## Current Profile

Hanji 688 adds the title-politics prelude. Liu Yong makes Dong Xian Haixi king and wants to make Zhang Bu Qi king, but Fu Long argues that Han norms reserve kingship for the Liu clan and that Zhang Bu should expect at most a large marquisate if he submits to Liu Xiu. Zhang Bu wants Fu Long to stay and help hold Qing and Xu; Fu Long refuses, sends a secret memorial asking Liu Xiu to advance and not spare him, and is later killed by Zhang Bu. This makes Zhang Bu's later campaign profile begin with failed persuasion and overlarge-title temptation rather than only with battlefield defeat.

Hanji 695 opens Zhang Bu's campaign profile through his defensive perimeter. After hearing [[GengYanEasternHan|Geng Yan]] is coming, Zhang Bu sends Fei Yi to Lixia and places forces at Zhu'a and Zhongcheng. Geng Yan's fast capture of Zhu'a, panic transmission to Zhongcheng, and later killing of Fei Yi at Juli show Zhang Bu's network being destabilized before the Linzi battle.

Hanji 696 then places Zhang Bu in the Qi/Linzi campaign environment at the point of battlefield failure. He guards the region through split deployments: Zhang Lan holds Xi'an with elite troops, while Linzi is defended by commandery forces. Geng Yan attacks the weaker city after falsely announcing Xi'an as the target, captures Linzi quickly, then provokes Zhang Bu into coming with Zhang Lan, Zhang Hong, Zhang Shou, and Chong Yi. Zhang Bu arrives confident against what he reads as a smaller, tired Han force, but Geng Yan has already planned around Zhang Bu's march fatigue, attack impulse, and retreat route.

Hanji 733-2 generalizes that campaign environment. Geng Yan attacks in ways designed to force Zhang Bu-linked forces to move under unfavorable conditions: announcing one target while striking another, fighting before Liu Xiu's arrival rather than waiting, and using enemy anger and retreat paths as campaign assets. Zhang Bu ultimately surrenders in the episode's telling, making him an adversary who marks the transition from local resistance to Liu Xiu's consolidation.

Hanji 697 makes that first settlement more coercive and more precise. Zhang Bu retreats to Pingshou, receives Su Mao's rescue force, then kills Su Mao after Liu Xiu secretly promises immediate enfeoffment to whichever man kills the other and submits. Geng Yan sends Zhang Bu to Liu Xiu, returns to Pingshou, disperses Zhang Bu's more than 100,000 troops and more than 7,000 wagons, while Liu Xiu pardons Zhang Bu's brothers, makes Zhang Bu Anqiu Marquis, and keeps Zhang Bu's wife and children in Luoyang as a control mechanism.

Hanji 707 supplies the later collapse of that controlled settlement. As land and officials increasingly attach to Liu Xiu, Zhang Bu can no longer operate as a durable regional actor. He flees with wife and children, joins brothers Zhang Hong and Zhang Lan, and tries to gather followers for a sea escape. Chen Jun catches and beheads him, turning Zhang Bu into a case where an initial [[SecurityForAuthorityExchange|security-for-authority exchange]] survives long enough for pardon and title but does not create stable loyalty.

## Key Characteristics

- Qi-region actor whose early profile includes Liu Yong's overlarge kingship lure and Fu Long's failed persuasion mission.
- Adversary who rejects or cannot accept a realistic marquisate path before later coercive defeat.
- Opponent whose outer defensive network is destabilized before his own Linzi field defeat.
- Target of subordinate-focused deception and lure tactics rather than a purely defensive city holder.
- Field commander whose overconfidence and response to provocation expose him to fortified defense and retreat-path ambush.
- Conditional surrender actor whose survival begins through rival-killing inducement and submission.
- Former regional actor whose later flight shows the danger of trying to recover political space after surrender.

## Qualifications

The source transcripts include ASR-sensitive name forms and battlefield details. Hanji 688 strengthens the early title-politics and Fu Long mission layer but keeps Liu Yong, Dong Xian, Ju, Qing/Xu defense specifics, and Fu Long's full career source-scoped. Hanji 695 adds the outer-network prelude but keeps Fei Yi, Fei Gan, Zhu'a, Zhongcheng, Juli, Lixia, and Jinan camp counts source-scoped; its Fei Yi is not the existing Warring States [[FeiYi|肥义]] page. Hanji 696 clarifies the Linzi defeat but not the full geography or exact status of Zhang Lan, Zhang Hong, Zhang Shou, Chong Yi, Chen Jun, Liu Xing, Ju, Xi'an, and Pingshou. Hanji 697 clarifies Zhang Bu's first settlement, but the full legal-political terms of pardon, family retention, and later revocation remain source-scoped. Hanji 707 clarifies the later endpoint but not every intervening trigger between Anqiu marquisate and flight. The transcript form 张布 is normalized to 张步 because the Anqiu Marquis, brothers, flight, and Guangwu consolidation context match the established Eastern Han route.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 688 as Zhang Bu's Liu Yong title-lure, Fu Long mission, detention, and envoy-killing layer.
- Reframed Zhang Bu's profile so overlarge title temptation precedes later battlefield defeat and controlled surrender.
- Preserved Hanji 695 and Hanji 696 as the pre-Linzi and Linzi defeat layers.
- Preserved Hanji 697 as first controlled surrender and Hanji 707 as later flight and execution.
