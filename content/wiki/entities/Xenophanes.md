# Xenophanes

## Overview

Xenophanes is an ancient Greek critic of anthropomorphic religion who, in the current source, exposes how communities project their own bodies and identities onto gods while still advancing a non-human-like account of divinity.

## Current Profile

[[604-greek-myths-sex-drugs-tragedy-part-3-glt7123647684]] places Xenophanes inside philosophy's challenge to Homeric and Hesiodic authority. The episode uses his thought experiment that cattle or horses would picture gods in their own form to show that divine images can reveal their makers as much as independent divine reality.

This critique does not make him a straightforward atheist in the source. Xenophanes still proposes one god unlike mortals, making him a case of theological revision: mythic anthropomorphism is rejected, but reflection on divinity continues.

## Key Characteristics

- Critiques human projection in traditional images of the gods.
- Uses cross-species comparison to expose the contingency of anthropomorphic divinity.
- Challenges poetic authority without abandoning theological speculation.
- Represents the overlap rather than a clean break between myth criticism and new divine accounts.

## Evidence

- Anthropomorphic projection: [[604-greek-myths-sex-drugs-tragedy-part-3-glt7123647684]] summarizes the cattle-and-horses thought experiment.
- Revised theology: [[604-greek-myths-sex-drugs-tragedy-part-3-glt7123647684]] presents Xenophanes as believing in one god unlike mortals.
- Intellectual transition: [[604-greek-myths-sex-drugs-tragedy-part-3-glt7123647684]] places him among thinkers who contest inherited myth while philosophy develops new accounts of the cosmos and divine.

## Qualifications

The page is based on one broad podcast synthesis rather than surviving fragments, their textual context, or specialist debate. “One god” should not be mapped automatically onto later monotheism, and the episode's placement of Xenophanes inside a mythos-to-logos movement is an interpretive simplification.
