# The Alabama Murders

## Overview

*The Alabama Murders* is a [[RevisionistHistory]] series described as beginning with trauma psychology and developing into an investigation of Alabama's death-penalty system.

## Current Profile

The project follows the killing of [[ElizabethSennett]] and the treatment of John Parker and [[KennethSmith]], the two men convicted of killing her. Its wider inquiry moves through lethal injection, nitrogen hypoxia, judicial override, racial history, secrecy, trauma, and the moral limits of state punishment. The final episode tests Alabama's first-use nitrogen protocol against courtroom admissions, animal research, expert warnings, eyewitness observation, and the state's success narrative.

## Key Characteristics

- Began from Kate Porterfield's trauma work rather than a preselected death-penalty thesis.
- Uses individual cases to examine institutional procedure and hidden suffering.
- Connects execution methods to courts, bureaucracy, racial history, and public perception.
- Develops an abolitionist argument that survives hypothetical procedural perfection.
- Preserves the grief of the victim's family while examining trauma imposed on the condemned person, his family, clinicians, and counsel.
- Uses conflict among protocol claims, scientific warnings, witness observations, and official interpretation to test institutional accountability.

- [[the-alabama-murders-part-7-the-second-warrant]] follows Smith's second warrant, reported PTSD, the nitrogen-hypoxia challenge, the execution, and conflicting witness and state accounts.

## Qualifications

This profile derives from one interview summary and the structured summary of the final episode rather than the complete series, transcripts, production archive, court files, medical evidence, execution records, or a developed defense of Alabama's method and capital punishment. Witness movement cannot establish Smith's subjective awareness or pain, and the animal study cannot be transferred directly to a human execution without fuller evidence.

## What Changed

- Added the final episode's test of nitrogen protocol design against expert, animal, courtroom, eyewitness, and official accounts.
- Extended the series profile from institutional secrecy to execution-induced trauma and plural, non-equivalent grief.
