## Overview

士燮 / Shi Xie is a late-Han Jiaozhou elite and long-serving administrator of [[JiaozhiCommandery|交趾郡]] whose authority combined family roots, classical learning, local governance, and accommodation with stronger outside regimes.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1119-ta-shengqian-guishun-sunquan-sihou-weihe-jiazu-bei-ganjin-shajue-ll4mblhk7ydn3pdxg-5zucstokzz|Hanji 1119]] presents Shi Xie as the descendant of a family established in [[Jiaozhou|交州]] for six generations. His father's service in Rinan and his own study of the *Zuo Commentary* in Luoyang joined local influence to classical and official credentials. Appointed to Jiaozhi in 187 CE, he later placed brothers over three neighboring commanderies and became the central broker in a region whose nominal inspectors could not govern around the family.

His strategy was to preserve practical power without claiming the province's highest formal office. He cooperated with successive outside inspectors, accepted [[SunQuan|孙权]]'s authority when Bu Zhi arrived, sent a son as hostage, and maintained tribute and gift exchanges. The source credits this bargain with allowing him to die in office at ninety in 226 CE, but the posthumous replacement of his family shows that personal accommodation was neither immunity nor a hereditary settlement.

## Key Characteristics

- Southern local elite whose authority rested on six generations of family establishment.
- Classically educated official whose learning added prestige to material local power.
- Family-network builder whose brothers held several Jiaozhou commanderies.
- Broker who kept practical authority while leaving the inspector's title to outsiders.
- Adaptive subordinate who used cooperation, a family hostage, tribute, and gift exchange.
- Long-lived officeholder whose personal settlement did not survive succession.

## Qualifications

This profile rests on one podcast source. Its praise of broad stability, humane government, and cultural flourishing, as well as its interpretation of office avoidance as deliberate political design, requires comparison with primary histories. Submission to Sun Quan may combine choice, coercion, and geographic necessity; longevity alone does not prove the wisdom of every policy. The fate of [[ShiHui|士徽]] also should not be read as punishment mechanically transferred backward onto Shi Xie's intentions.

## What Changed

- Created Shi Xie as the broker joining local lineage power, scholarship, administration, and formal submission.
- Distinguished his lifetime accommodation from any hereditary right to Jiaozhi.
