# Sergiu Pașca

## Overview
Sergiu Pașca is the Stanford psychiatry and neuroscience researcher interviewed by [[AndrewHuberman]] about human brain development, neurodevelopmental disorders, patient-derived stem-cell models, organoids, assembloids, transplantation, and therapeutic translation.

## Current Profile
In the current source, Pașca addresses the inaccessibility and slow development of the human brain by reprogramming patient cells, guiding them into neural lineages, and assembling region-specific tissue into experimentally tractable circuits. His central case is Timothy syndrome, where a causal calcium-channel mutation provides a path from cellular phenotype to a nucleic-acid therapeutic. He pairs that optimism with explicit limits: behavioral diagnoses are heterogeneous, organoids are not miniature brains, cross-species models are incomplete, and unregulated stem-cell injections are not evidence-based autism treatment.

## Key Characteristics
- Builds patient-derived human neural models to study development and disease mechanisms.
- Developed organoid and assembloid approaches for region-specific tissue, cell migration, and functional circuits.
- Uses rare genetic disorders as causal entry points into broader neurodevelopmental questions.
- Treats model-to-patient translation as a staged process requiring complementary validation.
- Rejects misleading “mini-brain” language and unsupported commercial stem-cell claims.
- Integrates science with prospective ethics, animal-welfare, nomenclature, and consent review.
