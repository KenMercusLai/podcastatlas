# Low Countries

[[694-elizabeth-i-vs-the-catholics-a-treacherous-conspiracy-part-4-glt1627515087]] adds the Low Countries before the [[TreatyOfNonsuch]], as the strategic focus behind [[ElizabethI|Elizabeth I]]'s Anjou diplomacy. The episode explains why English Protestants viewed Spanish repression there with horror, why Dutch rebels sought a replacement for [[PhilipII|Philip II]], and why [[FrancoisDukeOfAnjou|Francois, Duke of Anjou]] looked useful despite being Catholic.

The source also adds the [[ActOfAbjuration]] and [[FrenchFury]] sequence. Anjou's failed Antwerp coup collapses the French solution and leaves the Low Countries problem unresolved before the later Spanish recovery and English intervention in episode 695.

The Low Countries enter the wiki through [[695-elizabeth-i-vs-the-catholics-the-shadow-war-part-5-glt5637968455]] as the strategic theatre where [[Spain]], the [[DutchRevolt]], [[England]], and Protestant-Catholic geopolitics converge. The episode explains that Spanish victory there would give [[PhilipII|Philip II]] a stronger base for threatening England and would damage Protestant resistance and English trade.

The source uses the region to explain the later distinction between a Protestant north and Catholic south. [[AlexanderFarnese|Alexander Farnese / Duke of Parma]] recovers much of the south; [[Antwerp]] falls after a long siege; Protestant residents and commercial elites move north; and [[Amsterdam]] gains from the shift.

For [[ElizabethI|Elizabeth I]], the Low Countries become the setting of reluctant intervention. The [[TreatyOfNonsuch]] lets England send troops and hold cautionary towns without formally accepting sovereignty, a compromise that reveals the [[StrategicInterventionThreshold|strategic intervention threshold]] rather than full imperial ambition.
