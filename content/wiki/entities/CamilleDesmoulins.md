# Camille Desmoulins

## Overview

Camille Desmoulins was a revolutionary speaker and journalist whose 1789 call to arms helped ignite the mobilization preceding the [[StormingOfTheBastille|storming of the Bastille]] and whose later clemency campaign in *[[LeVieuxCordelier|Le Vieux Cordelier]]* led to his execution with [[GeorgesDanton|Georges Danton]].

## Current Profile

The sources trace Desmoulins from catalytic street mobilizer to dissenting revolutionary journalist. In July 1789 he interpreted Necker's dismissal and troop concentration as imminent counterrevolution, urged the Palais Royal crowd to arm, and supplied it with a temporary shared sign. By late 1793, the former schoolmate and friend of [[MaximilienRobespierre|Robespierre]] used *Le Vieux Cordelier* first against [[JacquesHebert|Hébert]] and then against terror itself. His call for a Committee of Clemency won a public audience but crossed the governing boundary of permissible criticism; Robespierre failed to separate him from the paper, and he was tried and executed with the [[Indulgents]].

## Key Characteristics

- He converted political news and fear into immediate collective action in July 1789.
- His friendship with Robespierre spanned school ties, revolutionary ascent, and later fatal disagreement.
- He used journalism to make factional argument accessible to a broad café-reading public.
- His newspaper moved from an approved attack on Hébert to a direct critique of terror.
- He advocated clemency for prisoners who had committed no punishable offence.
- He refused symbolic submission when Robespierre proposed destroying the paper while sparing its author.

## Qualifications

- His speech mattered within an already volatile crisis of hunger, troops, finance, and distrust; it did not create that crisis alone.
- The new source covers the final clemency campaign rather than a complete account of his intervening career.
- Public readings and applause show meaningful reception but do not establish national opinion.
- Exact wording, circulation, private motives, final letters, and courtroom conduct remain source-scoped reconstructions.

## What Changed

- Extended Desmoulins from the 1789 call to arms through the Indulgent clemency campaign and execution.
- Added the shift from government-tolerated factional attack to punishable criticism of terror itself.
- Connected his political fate to the failed effort to separate a former friend from his publication.

## Relationships

- [[JacquesNecker]] - dismissed minister whose removal structured his appeal.
- [[MaximilienRobespierre]] - friend named as part of his early revolutionary network.
- [[GeorgesDanton]] - close ally and co-defendant in the Indulgent trial.
- [[LeVieuxCordelier]] - newspaper through which he attacked Hébert and then demanded clemency.
- [[Indulgents]] - faction whose opposition to continued terror he articulated.
- [[LucileDesmoulins]] - wife arrested during his trial and executed one week after him.
- [[StormingOfTheBastille]] - event preceded by the mobilization he helped ignite.
- [[FrenchNationalGuard]] - institutional force that emerged as elites tried to order the armed city.
- [[RevolutionaryCrowdVengeance]] - later violent theatre foreshadowed by the wax-head procession.
- [[RevolutionaryFactionalOutflanking]] - ratchet that turned an approved attack into condemned moderation.
