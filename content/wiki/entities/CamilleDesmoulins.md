# Camille Desmoulins

## Overview

Camille Desmoulins was a young revolutionary speaker whose call to arms at the Palais Royal helped turn news of [[JacquesNecker|Jacques Necker]]'s dismissal into the street mobilization preceding the [[StormingOfTheBastille|storming of the Bastille]].

## Current Profile

The source places the 26-year-old friend of [[MaximilienRobespierre|Maximilien Robespierre]] at the catalytic moment of 12 July 1789. Standing on a table at the Cafe Foy, he interpreted Necker's removal and royal troop concentration as an imminent attack, urged the crowd to arm, and proposed green cockades as the color of hope. The crowd then disrupted theatres, entered a wax museum, and carried likenesses of Necker and the Duke of Orleans through Paris. Desmoulins's importance here lies in converting rumor, fear, and political news into a visible collective identity and immediate action.

## Key Characteristics

- Young revolutionary orator operating in the Palais Royal political crowd.
- Friend of Robespierre before both became major revolutionary figures.
- Interpreter who framed ministerial dismissal as imminent counterrevolution.
- Mobilizer whose “to arms” appeal accelerated collective action.
- Promoter of a cockade that gave the crowd a temporary shared sign.

## Qualifications

- The episode covers one catalytic intervention rather than Desmoulins's full journalistic and revolutionary career.
- His speech mattered within an already volatile crisis of hunger, troops, finance, and distrust; it did not create that crisis alone.
- Exact wording, crowd response, and individual motives remain source-scoped reconstructions.

## What Changed

- Establishes Desmoulins as the speaker who converted the Necker crisis into an armed and symbolically marked street movement.
