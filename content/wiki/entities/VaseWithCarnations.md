# Vase with Carnations

## Overview

*Vase with Carnations* is a small Vincent van Gogh still life from his experimental Paris period whose authentication, price, and ownership history connect art attribution to [[HolocaustEraArtRestitution|Holocaust-era restitution ethics]].

## Current Profile

[[hedwig-s-lost-van-gogh-hedwig-s-lost-van-gogh|Hedwig’s Lost Van Gogh]] describes the painting as a tentative flower study rather than a major or stylistically representative Van Gogh. A stamp on a later backing once contributed to authenticity doubts; removal of that added layer resolved the discrepancy, and institutional acceptance transformed the work from effectively valueless to worth millions without changing the object itself.

The painting passed from [[HedwigUllmann]]'s consignment through later owners before Katherine Kresge bequeathed it without restrictions to the [[DetroitInstituteOfArts]] in 1990. Because the dealer sold it only after the war, the family is presented as lacking a viable legal claim. The episode nevertheless treats it as a moral test of whether lawful possession can be separated from the coercive conditions under which Hedwig relinquished control.

## Key Characteristics

- Small experimental flower still life associated with Van Gogh's Paris period.
- Minor work whose market value changed sharply after authentication.
- Former Ullmann-family object consigned during flight from Nazi persecution.
- Detroit museum holding that functions as the episode's narrative “MacGuffin.”
- Artwork whose legal chain and moral provenance point toward different judgments.
