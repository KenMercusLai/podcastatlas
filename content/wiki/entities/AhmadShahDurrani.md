# Ahmad Shah Durrani

## Overview
Ahmad Shah Durrani was an eighteenth-century Pashtun ruler whom the source presents as the founder of the [[DurraniEmpire|Durrani Empire]] and a central figure in the emergence of an Afghan polity.

## Current Profile
[[87-afghanistan-part-1-glt7464087190]] places Ahmad Shah in Nadir Shah's military and political world. After Nadir Shah's assassination in 1747, Ahmad Shah secured treasure, built support among regional power-holders, adopted the title Durr-i-Durran, or "Pearl of Pearls," and established a realm larger than modern [[Afghanistan]].

The episode treats his achievement as coalition-based state formation rather than the creation of a nation-state in its later borders. His realm sat between Persia, the declining Mughal world, Russian expansion, and the political systems of the Indian subcontinent.

## Key Characteristics
- Pashtun leader from the Kandahar region.
- Former participant in Nadir Shah's imperial system.
- Founder and namesake of the Durrani ruling formation.
- Coalition-builder who used portable wealth and political bargaining to consolidate rule.
- Precursor figure in the formation of modern Afghanistan.

## Evidence
- Rise after Nadir Shah: [[87-afghanistan-part-1-glt7464087190]] links the 1747 assassination, access to treasure, and Ahmad Shah's consolidation among warlords.
- Dynastic identity: [[87-afghanistan-part-1-glt7464087190]] explains his title Durr-i-Durran and the subsequent Durrani name.
- Historical significance: [[87-afghanistan-part-1-glt7464087190]] presents the resulting polity as an early outline of the later Afghan state.

## Qualifications
The source is a rapid narrative survey rather than a specialist biography. Its emphasis on treasure and personal coalition-building does not exhaust the military, tribal, fiscal, regional, or institutional foundations of Durrani rule.
