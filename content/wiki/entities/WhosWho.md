# Who’s Who

## Overview
*Who’s Who* is a British biographical reference annual first published in 1849 whose long-running, consistently structured entries now form a historical record of elite selection and self-description.

## Current Profile
The episode describes 179 editions and more than 125,000 entries covering careers, addresses, hobbies, and changing personal identifiers. Its value lies in continuity rather than representativeness: current entrants are reported as 80% male and 97% white and are disproportionately London-based, privately educated, and educated at Oxford or Cambridge. [[Bloomsbury]] is selling the publication, leaving its ownership and continuation uncertain.

## Key Characteristics
- Combines automatic inclusion for some offices with opaque board selection for other prominent figures.
- Uses sufficiently consistent fields to support comparison across more than a century and a half.
- Records incidental details that reveal changing elite technologies, habits, and self-presentation.
- Represents a narrow status population rather than Britain as a whole.
- Preserves contemporaneous reputations before later events reshape historical memory.

## Evidence
- **Longitudinal continuity:** The episode reports 179 editions, more than 125,000 entries, and stable categories that permit comparisons from the 1870s to the present. [[all-eyes-on-eisenkot-israels-could-be-prime-minister-6af19d18e7c6d309d1116743]]
- **Selection and bias:** Some offices confer entry automatically, while actors, writers, and business figures face a secretive selection board; present membership is heavily male, white, London-centered, privately educated, and Oxbridge-educated. [[all-eyes-on-eisenkot-israels-could-be-prime-minister-6af19d18e7c6d309d1116743]]
- **Historical texture:** Telegram addresses, vehicle registrations, hobbies, pets, jokes, and social-media handles make successive editions evidence about elite life as well as individual biography. [[all-eyes-on-eisenkot-israels-could-be-prime-minister-6af19d18e7c6d309d1116743]]
- **Continuity risk:** Bloomsbury’s planned sale leaves both the buyer and the annual’s future unresolved. [[all-eyes-on-eisenkot-israels-could-be-prime-minister-6af19d18e7c6d309d1116743]]

## Qualifications
The episode supplies aggregate counts and examples rather than the underlying directory data or a full history of selection rules. The publication’s bias is analytically useful for studying status but prevents treating its entrants as representative of British society. Its possible discontinuation was unresolved when the source was produced.

- Made selection bias and preservation uncertainty part of the current judgment.
