## Overview

《论积贮疏》 / Memorial on Accumulation and Storage is the memorial through which [[JiaYi|贾谊]] connects agricultural production and stored grain to livelihood, public order, disaster response, military supply, and regime survival in the current wiki evidence.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-212-jiayi-lun-jizhu-shu-yu-zhongnong-zhuzhang-2-lpdh1bl4etefwusbbl2q4ytmrda1|Hanji 212]] presents the memorial as a chain of political risk. Food and clothing make moral and ritual expectations practicable; slow production, luxury, and a shrinking producer base erode surplus; thin household and state stores then leave drought or war capable of producing displacement, predation, and rebellion. Its positive prescription joins farming, accumulation, imperial example, and lower tax burden, while its attempt to move craft and commercial labor into agriculture inherits the limits of [[HeavyAgricultureLightCommercePolicy|agrarian hierarchy]].

## Key Characteristics

- Treats food and clothing as material preconditions of stable moral and political order.
- Defines accumulation as resilience against harvest failure, war, and frontier-supply pressure.
- Connects household scarcity to family separation and state scarcity to military and regime weakness.
- Places agriculture above crafts and commerce in its proposed labor hierarchy.
- Enters the episode's policy narrative through imperial plowing and partial land-tax remission.

## Qualifications

The current page is grounded in one structured podcast summary rather than a textual edition of the memorial. Exact wording, textual divisions, dates, intended audience, policy sequence, and the relationship between Jia Yi's argument and Liu Heng's measures require comparison with the transmitted text and histories. The memorial's classification of crafts and commerce as secondary does not account for their roles in toolmaking, transport, storage, and exchange, and the episode does not measure whether occupational transfer increased output or reserves.
