# Richard of York

## Overview

Richard of York is the English magnate whose service as lord protector, hereditary claim, and attempt to redirect the succession made him a central Yorkist challenger before his death at the [[BattleOfWakefield]] in 1460.

## Current Profile

The episode presents Richard as a credible administrator and soldier operating against the incapacity of Henry VI. His descent from Edward III made a claim available, but not automatically acceptable: even Yorkist allies remained attached to the sacred standing of an anointed king. The compromise that preserved Henry while naming York's line as successor displaced the king's son and made renewed Lancastrian resistance likely.

Richard then moved north to defend his estates and followers. Trapped and outnumbered at Sandal Castle, he launched an unexplained sortie before expected reinforcements arrived. His death, Edmund's killing, and the paper crown placed on his displayed head made Wakefield both a military disaster and a deliberate attack on his royal ambition; his son [[EdwardIV]] later converted the surviving Yorkist cause into kingship.

## Key Characteristics

- Plantagenet magnate with a hereditary claim through descent from Edward III.
- Lord protector during Henry VI's incapacity and a stronger administrator in the episode's comparison.
- Yorkist leader allied with the Neville family in recurring conflict with the Lancastrian court.
- Claimant whose direct bid for the crown exceeded what even some allies would accept.
- Father whose settlement elevated his line while disinheriting Henry VI's son.
- Defeated commander killed after an unexplained sortie from Sandal Castle.

## Qualifications

This profile rests on one brief popular-history episode rather than a full biography. The balance among hereditary law, administrative competence, ambition, self-defense, and factional escalation remains interpretive. The source does not resolve why Richard left Sandal Castle, whether the action should be called a conventional pitched battle, or how much Shakespeare's later scene reflects contemporary events.

## What Changed

- Established Richard's profile from protectorship through the succession compromise and Wakefield.
- Distinguished a legally available dynastic claim from sufficient political acceptance or military capacity.
