## Overview

*Mary Poppins* is a Disney musical adaptation whose critical and commercial success is paired in the source with P. L. Travers's resistance and a biographical reading of Mr. Banks as an echo of [[WaltDisney]].

## Current Profile

[[614-walt-disney-the-great-american-storyteller-glt5465018421]] emphasizes the disagreement between Travers and Disney over sentimentality, songs, animation, and Americanization. The episode then makes a qualified interpretive move: Mr. Banks's absorption in work, relation to banking pressure, distance from his children, and recovery through joy resemble tensions in Disney's own life, making the film a possible late self-reflection rather than a documented confession.

## Key Characteristics

- Adaptation shaped by conflict between author and studio preferences.
- Combination of music, sentiment, animation, and live-action storytelling.
- Major critical and commercial success in the episode's account.
- Possible indirect self-portrait of Walt Disney through Mr. Banks.

## Evidence

### Adaptation conflict and self-portrait reading

## Qualifications

The Mr. Banks comparison is interpretive and does not establish authorial intent. The source does not provide a complete adaptation, production, reception, or Travers biography, and its Americanization judgment remains source-scoped.
