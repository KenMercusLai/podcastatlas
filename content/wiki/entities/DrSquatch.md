## Overview
Dr. Squatch is a men's personal-care brand founded by [[JackHalldrup]], originally launched as Sasquatch Soap and acquired by [[Unilever]] in 2025 for a reported $1.5 billion. [[dr-squatch-jack-halldrup-from-a-closet-full-of-soap-to-a-1-5-billion-brand-vmp1750831437]] traces its path from closet fulfillment and online soap sales to owned manufacturing and majority-retail distribution.

## Current Profile
The company's profile joins product reframing, character-led branding, performance marketing, repeat purchase, and channel expansion. Dr. Squatch did not invent cold-process soap; it made the product legible and entertaining for men who were unlikely to encounter artisan soap at farmers markets. Facebook demand generation and a 2018 humorous video created a [[PerformanceCreativeScaleLoop]], while later product expansion, manufacturing capacity, and retail distribution moved the company beyond its DTC origin.

## Key Characteristics
- Positions cold-process soap and adjacent personal-care products specifically for men through humor, outdoor imagery, and a recurring character.
- Began as a capital-light e-commerce brand using outsourced production, family fulfillment, and paid social discovery.
- Built repeatable demand through integrated advertising, subscriptions, merchandising, email, and retention.
- Used a forced trademark-driven rebrand to strengthen the Dr. Squatch character and brand voice.
- Expanded from an almost entirely DTC model into majority-retail sales while retaining meaningful e-commerce.
- Built owned cold-process manufacturing capacity before its reported 2025 acquisition by Unilever.

## Evidence
- **Product and audience reframing:** [[dr-squatch-jack-halldrup-from-a-closet-full-of-soap-to-a-1-5-billion-brand-vmp1750831437]] says Halldrup recognized that men might value artisan soap but were unlikely to discover it through existing farmers-market channels.
- **Brand and acquisition engine:** [[dr-squatch-jack-halldrup-from-a-closet-full-of-soap-to-a-1-5-billion-brand-vmp1750831437]] describes the Sasquatch-to-Dr. Squatch rebrand, early Facebook experiments, and a $15,000 humorous video that reportedly reduced some customer acquisition costs to $3 to $4.
- **Operating scale:** [[dr-squatch-jack-halldrup-from-a-closet-full-of-soap-to-a-1-5-billion-brand-vmp1750831437]] reports growth from roughly $5 million in 2018 revenue to $113 million in 2020, supported by product expansion, advertising reinvestment, hiring, and pandemic tailwinds.
- **Channel and manufacturing shift:** [[dr-squatch-jack-halldrup-from-a-closet-full-of-soap-to-a-1-5-billion-brand-vmp1750831437]] says Dr. Squatch later produced about five million bars per month in its own facility and generated a majority of sales through retailers including Target and Walmart.
- **Ownership transition:** [[dr-squatch-jack-halldrup-from-a-closet-full-of-soap-to-a-1-5-billion-brand-vmp1750831437]] reports a 2021 majority private-equity investment and Unilever's June 2025 acquisition for approximately $1.5 billion.
