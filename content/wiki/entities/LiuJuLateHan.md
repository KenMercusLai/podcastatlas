## Overview
刘矩 / Liu Ju is a late-Han official introduced in Hanji 854 as the Taichang appointed taiwei after Huang Qiong's removal. The episode's main interest is not the appointment title alone, but the remembered Yongqiu magistrate story: Liu Ju governed through ritual yielding, persuasion, and dispute prevention.

## Current Profile
[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-854-lishi-gaosu-ni-xuanguan-yongguan-hen-zhongyao-lhin-iwk6y1xr8vvzdmnjch3s2um|Hanji 854]] places Liu Ju in the 161 CE office-turnover sequence, after palace fire and epidemic pressure remove Huang Qiong from the taiwei post. It then backfills why Liu Ju matters: when he had governed Yongqiu, litigants who came to the county office were brought forward and gently instructed in ritual yielding, until they withdrew and resolved their disputes outside punitive administration.

## Key Characteristics
- High official appointed taiwei in the Huan-era disaster and office-turnover layer.
- Former Yongqiu magistrate remembered for teaching-based dispute resolution.
- Humane-governance exemplar whose method prevents small conflicts from becoming formal cases.
- Appointment-quality case: central elevation is explained through local administrative character.

## Qualifications
The source is one episode and does not yet provide Liu Ju's broader career, factional position, or later policy record as taiwei. The transcript says 刘举 in places, but the received Tongjian passage supports normalizing the route to 刘矩.
