# Carl Webb

## Overview

Carl Webb, also called Charles Webb in the source, is the Australian man whom hair-DNA and genealogical research reportedly identified as the deceased in the [[SomertonManCase|Somerton Man case]]. Identification supplies a biographical name and a possible explanation for the “T Keane” clothing markings, but not a cause of death.

## Current Profile

The episode describes Webb as the sixth child of an Australian-German couple from Melbourne. It says he enjoyed poetry and horse betting, had separated from his wife in April 1947, and left no later known record before the unidentified man's death in late 1948. His wife reportedly began divorce proceedings for desertion in 1951.

Webb's brother-in-law Thomas Keane provides a plausible family route for clothes marked “T Keane.” His interest in poetry makes possession of a *Rubaiyat of Omar Khayyam* less surprising, while horse betting supplies one possible interpretation of the book's unexplained letter sequences. Neither point proves what happened at Somerton Park Beach.

## Key Characteristics

- Australian man reportedly identified through hair DNA and genetic genealogy.
- Biographical subject previously absent from the public case under his own name.
- Family connection that can contextualize the “T Keane” clothing clue.
- Poetry and horse-betting interests used to reassess once-exotic clues.
- Person whose identity is more certain in the episode than his final movements or cause of death.

## Qualifications

The page follows a single podcast's account of recently announced research and does not independently verify the genetic chain, identity records, marriage history, or reported interests. “Carl” and “Charles” are both retained because the episode uses both. Poetry and betting can make clues less exotic without proving Webb owned the recovered book, wrote its letters, intended suicide, or died through gambling-related events.
