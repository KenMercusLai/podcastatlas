# Quebec

## Overview
Quebec is represented as the Canadian province where high tariff exposure, French-language protection, cultural distinctiveness, civil law, and separatist politics intersect.

## Current Profile
The earlier trade source presents Quebec as highly exposed to American tariffs and politically unable to treat demands affecting French-language and culture protections as ordinary commercial concessions. The newest episode extends that distinctiveness into an independence movement led by the elected [[PartiQuebecois]]. Unlike Alberta’s resource and fiscal grievance, Quebec separatism is framed primarily through language, culture, and legal tradition.

The episode places current independence support around 30%, making growth rather than immediate majority support the party’s challenge. Conflict with Ottawa may therefore become a strategy for changing political conditions, particularly while trade pressure damages aluminium and other provincial industries.

## Key Characteristics
- High tariff exposure in the earlier Canada trade comparison.
- Strong political sensitivity around French-language and culture protections.
- Cultural-national and civil-law basis for separatist politics.
- Governed by the Parti Québécois in the newest source’s time frame.
- Minority independence support that may still shape federal-provincial bargaining.
