## Overview

祭遵 / Ji Zun is synthesized as an Eastern Han commander whose current evidence is concentrated in Hanji 708. The episode remembers him less through a campaign result than through disciplined command, clean handling of rewards, thin-burial instructions, and the political aftereffect of [[LiuXiu|Liu Xiu]]'s intense public mourning.

## Current Profile

Hanji 708 says Ji Zun dies in camp in Jianwu 9 / 33 CE. His profile in the source is a model-official profile: clean, cautious, duty-bound, strict with troops, generous with rewards, and restrained in death arrangements. The episode emphasizes that armies under him are so orderly that local officials hardly feel a large force has arrived.

His death then becomes a court-management event. Liu Xiu wears mourning clothes, personally mourns, cries over the coffin, conducts sacrifice with full animal offerings, and repeatedly asks in court how he can obtain another person so patriotic and public-minded. The praise is not presented as Ji Zun's fault, but it makes him the standard against which living officials feel measured.

## Key Characteristics

- Eastern Han commander whose current wiki profile is grounded in disciplined camp conduct rather than battle narrative.
- Clean and careful official who distributes received rewards to officers and soldiers.
- Commander whose army is remembered as locally non-disruptive because discipline restrains troop impact.
- Confucian personnel selector and thin-burial requester.
- Posthumous model whose praise creates pressure on living ministers.
- Source-bounded figure whose wider career remains outside the current wiki evidence.

## Qualifications

The source does not supply a full biography, battle record, family background, or exact office sequence for Ji Zun. The court panic after his death should be read as an effect of imperial praise and comparison, not as evidence that Ji Zun had harmed the ministers while alive.
