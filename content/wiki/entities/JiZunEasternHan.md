## Overview

祭遵 / Ji Zun is synthesized as an Eastern Han commander whose current evidence now begins with strict enforcement against [[LiuXiu|Liu Xiu]]'s own household, then continues into western-campaign victory and later model-official memory. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-670-juejing-qiusheng-liuxiu-xianru-shengsi-da-weiji-ltz1nciofev4cqjkod-p8mmwovhe|Hanji 670]] shows Ji Zun executing a young Liu-household servant under military law, surviving Liu Xiu's first angry reaction, and then being promoted after advisers frame the act as proof that Liu Xiu's discipline order has force.

## Current Profile

Hanji 670 supplies the earliest current Ji Zun layer. A young servant from Liu Xiu's household violates military law and Ji Zun executes him. Liu Xiu initially orders Ji Zun's arrest, but advisers argue that Liu Xiu has demanded strict discipline and that Ji Zun's refusal to exempt the ruler's own household shows the order is being carried out. Liu Xiu accepts the correction, pardons and promotes Ji Zun, and warns the generals that if even his household servant can be executed, others should not expect favoritism.

Hanji 702 places Ji Zun inside the active western counterblow against [[WeiXiaoEasternHan|隗嚣]]. After Feng Yi secretly occupies the disputed city and breaks Xing Xun's force, Ji Zun also defeats Wang Yuan on another line. The source links these results to Beidi local strongmen turning away from Wei Xiao and to later submission by Beidi, Shangjun, and Anding.

Hanji 708 says Ji Zun dies in camp in Jianwu 9 / 33 CE. His profile in that source is a model-official profile: clean, cautious, duty-bound, strict with troops, generous with rewards, and restrained in death arrangements. The episode emphasizes that armies under him are so orderly that local officials hardly feel a large force has arrived.

His death then becomes a court-management event. Liu Xiu wears mourning clothes, personally mourns, cries over the coffin, conducts sacrifice with full animal offerings, and repeatedly asks in court how he can obtain another person so patriotic and public-minded. The praise is not presented as Ji Zun's fault, but it makes him the standard against which living officials feel measured.

## Key Characteristics

- Strict military-law officer whose earliest current signal is punishment of a Liu-household servant.
- Eastern Han commander whose current wiki profile now includes active western-campaign victory before later memorialization.
- Clean and careful official who distributes received rewards to officers and soldiers.
- Commander whose army is remembered as locally non-disruptive because discipline restrains troop impact.
- Confucian personnel selector and thin-burial requester.
- Posthumous model whose praise creates pressure on living ministers.
- Source-bounded figure whose wider career remains outside the current wiki evidence.

## Qualifications

The sources do not supply a full biography, family background, or exact office sequence for Ji Zun. Hanji 670 supplies the household-servant execution and promotion scene but leaves the servant's name, exact offense, and office-title rendering source-scoped. Hanji 702 supplies one campaign result but leaves the exact place name, Wang Yuan's force details, and Ji Zun's tactical method source-scoped. The court panic after his death should be read as an effect of imperial praise and comparison, not as evidence that Ji Zun had harmed the ministers while alive.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 670 as Ji Zun's earliest current layer: enforcement against Liu Xiu's own household and ruler-backed promotion after correction.
- Preserved Hanji 702 as a pre-death western-campaign victory layer against Wei Xiao's Wang Yuan line.
- Reframed Hanji 708 as later model-commander memory and court comparison-pressure rather than the whole current profile.
