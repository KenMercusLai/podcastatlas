# 项羽 / Xiang Yu

Xiang Yu is the dominant host and failed would-be ruler in the episode's reading of [[HongmenYan|《鸿门宴》]] in [[164-guwen-de-liliang-qing-shou-xia-zhe-fen-qiannian-wei-bian-de-shenqing-dongjian-he-yongqi-940236576]]. The source presents him as powerful, proud, susceptible to recognition, and politically dangerous because he can humiliate [[LiuBang|刘邦]] without finally killing him.

The episode's larger judgment is ambivalent. QinZong says a harder, more shameless Xiang Yu might have won, but she also connects his residual need to "要脸" with [[SimaQian|司马迁]]'s decision to place him in imperial annals. Xiang Yu therefore anchors [[MoralFaceUnderPowerCompetition]] rather than a simple lesson that kindness loses.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-45-yi-shi-wei-jian-junwang-daodi-gai-bu-gai-sharen-chengxing-lo3cok7cfwzrxesp-i8miz1fc1db]] uses Xiang Yu in a different contrast. In the host's response to [[Mengzi|孟子 / Mencius]]'s [[NonKillingUnification|不爱杀人者能一之]] claim, Xiang Yu is framed as the more killing-oriented Chu-Han contender, while [[LiuBang|刘邦]] becomes the less-killing figure who founds a durable Han order. This source therefore makes Xiang Yu a negative durability case, not only a banquet power-scene actor.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-128-6-dingji-tianshilun-touziren-lvgong-lhqqja27zxdiypgf6wuv1abgkw3j|Qinji 128-6]] names Xiang Yu as one of the real protagonists about to enter after [[ChenSheng|陈胜]] and [[WuGuang|吴广]] open the anti-[[QinState|秦国]] rebellion. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-128-7-liubang-he-xiangyu-youhe-butong-llwcqatndck4zita2piufwhjpxp1|Qinji 128-7]] then supplies his early profile: a [[ChuState|楚国]] aristocratic descendant, [[XiangYan|项燕]]'s grandson, and [[XiangLiang|项梁]]'s nephew who wants to study the "万人敌" rather than mere literacy or one-on-one swordsmanship.

The same source presents Xiang Yu's gifts and dangers together. He is physically imposing, unusually strong, and terrifying in the [[KuaijiCommandery|会稽郡]] office when he kills [[YinTong|殷通]] and fights resisting guards. But the episode also marks impatience: he likes military study at first, then refuses to go deeply into it. Xiang Yu's anti-Qin entry is therefore framed through martial charisma and [[KuaijiCommanderyCoup|会稽郡守府夺权]], sharply contrasting with Liu Bang's [[PeiCountyUprisingMobilization|沛县起义动员]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-130-1-guailaotou-yiqiao-san-shua-zhangliang-chuanshou-shenshu-lqwjo13zzfgtndpajzpuvrb2h4l2|Qinji 130-1]] keeps Xiang Yu in his uncle's command but moves him onto the broader campaign road. After [[ZhaoPingRebel|昭平]]'s false appointment gives [[XiangLiang|项梁]] a Chu title, Xiang Yu crosses the Yangtze with him and eight thousand [[KuaijiCommandery|会稽郡]] troops.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-130-2-lishi-jilu-shouci-tucheng-lm4kuzdpnqn-hicyuvvb4p2wdudm|Qinji 130-2]] adds the first explicit massacre marker to Xiang Yu's branch. Xiang Liang sends him against [[XiangchengQin|襄城]] in Yingchuan commandery; after the city resists and falls, Xiang Yu kills the city's military and civilian population. The episode uses this as a source-scoped first recorded "屠城" and as evidence for the later judgment that Xiang Yu's cruelty exceeds [[LiuBang|刘邦]]'s, tying him more tightly to [[WarringStatesWarBrutalization|战国战争残酷化]], [[PostBattleTerrorSignaling|战后恐怖震慑]], and the negative side of [[NonKillingUnification|不爱杀人者能一之]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-132-5wan-wanbao-40wan-xiangyu-cizhan-fengshen-lk87nb3fskg5zh6fcgptypixhzuw|Qinji 132]] turns that brutality marker into a concrete assignment problem. After [[ZhangHanQin|章邯]] kills [[XiangLiang|项梁]] at [[Dingtao|定陶]], Xiang Yu joins Liu Bang and [[LuChenQinRebel|吕臣]] in moving [[MiXin|芈心 / 楚怀王]] to [[Xuzhou|彭城]]. Mi Xin then gives Xiang Yu the Chang'an Hou title and Lu Gong designation.

Xiang Yu asks to accompany Liu Bang west against Qin because he wants revenge for Xiang Liang's death. The older Chu generals block the request by citing his "狡诈残暴" profile and the [[XiangchengQin|襄城]] massacre: they argue that Qin's overburdened population needs a more generous pacifier. Xiang Yu is therefore assigned as second general under [[SongYi|宋义]] for the [[Julu|巨鹿]] relief mission, with [[FanZeng|范增]] also in the command structure, while Liu Bang receives the westward [[GuanzhongRegion|关中]] route under [[HuaiwangAgreement|怀王之约]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-133-songyi-zhan-xiangyu-weihe-bei-fansha-lms7noyrm5omedv77gqfhjeu53zx|Qinji 133]] turns that subordinate assignment into Xiang Yu's takeover moment. When Song Yi stops at [[AnyangQinRebellion|安阳]], Xiang Yu urges immediate crossing and a combined inside-outside attack with Zhao. The episode frames him as impatient and violent, but also as strategically alert to the possibility that Qin might grow stronger if it destroys Zhao and absorbs its supplies.

Song Yi's insult and targeted discipline order make Xiang Yu's conflict personal, while Song Yi's [[WuyanCounty|无盐县]] banquet lets Xiang Yu appeal to the soldiers' hardship. Xiang Yu then enters the command tent and kills Song Yi, converting command grievance into [[WartimeCommandDecapitation|战时统帅斩首]]. This is a major step in [[MilitaryPersonalization|军队私人化]]: formal appointment still belongs to Mi Xin's Chu court, but actual army power shifts to Xiang Yu through violence and camp legitimacy.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-134-ni-buzhidao-de-pofu-chenzhou-banben-ltnry1fva2hzplw4ft8nrfpoiolw|Qinji 134]] turns Xiang Yu's seized command into his [[JuluBattle|巨鹿之战]] victory. The episode stresses sequence: Xiang Yu first sends [[YingBu|英布]] and [[PuGeneral|蒲将军]] to break Qin's protected supply corridor from [[Aocang|敖仓]] toward [[Julu|巨鹿]], then crosses the [[ZhangRiver|漳水]] with the main force and uses [[BreakingCauldronsAndSinkingBoats|破釜沉舟]] to force death-ground morale.

The victory gives Xiang Yu more than battlefield fame. Other lords had watched from their camps until Chu broke [[WangLiQin|王离]]'s army; after the battle, they submit to Xiang Yu as coalition commander. This makes Julu the moment when his personal military authority begins to dominate the anti-Qin coalition, not just a tactical success.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-146-qiannian-jingdian-xiaohe-yuexia-zhui-hanxin-lq0oc3vqr-tsr0r-iwztifxeezl|Hanji 146]] adds a talent-use failure to the post-Julu Xiang Yu branch. After [[HanXin|韩信]] moves from [[XiangLiang|项梁]]'s army into Xiang Yu's service, Xiang Yu appoints him only as a langzhong / close attendant and repeatedly ignores his advice. The source treats possible Han Xin objections to Xiang Yu's [[Xianyang|咸阳]] violence and partition choices as inference, but the outcome is clear: Xiang Yu's camp loses a future commander because it cannot recognize or use him.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-147-pimei-zhugeliang-longzhongdui-de-hanzhongdui-lqucctzhipgtcvtjlixvj5qbdeai|Hanji 147]] turns Xiang Yu into the negative model inside [[HanXin|韩信]]'s [[HanzhongDui|汉中对]]. Han Xin concedes that Xiang Yu looks stronger than [[LiuBang|刘邦]], but calls his courage "匹夫之勇" because he cannot delegate to able commanders and his kindness "妇人之仁" because he hesitates over concrete rewards. The same diagnosis folds together Xiang Yu's refusal to base himself in [[GuanzhongRegion|关中]], breach of the [[HuaiwangAgreement|怀王之约]], treatment of [[MiXin|芈心 / 楚怀王]], and destructive violence into one loss of people-support.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-137-shendu-jiedu-zhiluweima-zhong-de-quanli-boyi-lgkjmddvrcelw2ltdwt92e4poguj|Qinji 137]] gives the immediate post-Julu sequel. Xiang Yu accepts [[ZhangHanQin|章邯]]'s surrender partly because Chu supplies are strained, swears with him south of Huan water, keeps him near headquarters, appoints [[SimaXin|司马欣]] as upper general, and puts the absorbed Qin troops in the vanguard. The episode sharply criticizes the settlement decision to make Zhang Han 雍王: because 雍州 effectively means the Qin/[[GuanzhongRegion|关中]] core, the appointment undercuts the [[HuaiwangAgreement|怀王之约]] and shows Xiang Yu's weak postwar design.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-139-ta-zaici-yingxiang-hanchao-lp1zujuq0o3ohxc79xqi1phpb5zo|Hanji 139]] turns that post-Julu settlement weakness into the [[XinanMassacre|新安坑降]]. While leading the coalition and surrendered Qin troops west toward [[HanguPass|函谷关]], Xiang Yu treats the large, resentful Qin force as a mutiny risk and consults [[YingBu|英布]] and [[PuGeneral|蒲将军]]. The episode says the resulting night killing of surrendered soldiers leaves only Zhang Han, Sima Xin, and [[DongYiQin|董义]] alive among the senior surrendered leaders, making Xinan the clearest source case where Xiang Yu's military risk management destroys his ability to win [[GuanzhongRegion|关中]] people-support.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-140-xiangyu-yu-liubang-shi-zhen-youqing-haishi-xianghu-liyong-1-lmmhoq2wo3bs6b1johpc0ixuf662|Hanji 140 part 1]] moves Xiang Yu from that damaged Guanzhong position into the pre-[[HongmenYan|鸿门宴]] confrontation. After learning that [[LiuBang|刘邦]] has entered Guanzhong first and closed the entry routes, Xiang Yu orders [[YingBu|英布]] and others to attack the pass. [[CaoWushang|曹无伤]]'s leak then triggers another angry response: Xiang Yu decides to attack Liu Bang the next morning, while the host criticizes him for lacking a full settlement plan after taking the "toy" back.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-140-xiangyu-yu-liubang-shi-zhen-youqing-haishi-xianghu-liyong-2-lvdgirxmwxcckh2loh-yk2zzer2e|Hanji 140 part 2]] shows why that attack order is cancelled. [[XiangBo|项伯]] returns from [[Bashang|霸上]] and argues that Liu Bang's prior entry into [[GuanzhongRegion|关中]] helped the whole coalition, so attacking him would be "不义." The episode stresses Xiang Bo's family standing after [[XiangLiang|项梁]]'s death and reads Xiang Yu as emotionally responsive to kinship, old Chu-camp comradeship, and the language of义.

The source therefore sharpens Xiang Yu's profile as a powerful commander whose decisions remain vulnerable to affective frames. He is not reduced to pure stupidity or pure interest calculation; he can be moved by an elder's moral narrative even after anger and Fan Zeng's threat diagnosis have already pointed him toward killing Liu Bang.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-141-huanyuan-lishi-zhong-de-hongmenyan-lpmb-bcoocngtqjvtnpj0elm-siy|Hanji 141]] shows that vulnerability continuing inside the banquet. Xiang Yu accepts [[LiuBang|刘邦]]'s apology posture, casually exposes [[CaoWushang|曹无伤]] as Liu Bang's informer, and then ignores [[FanZeng|范增]]'s repeated jade-ring signals to kill Liu Bang. The episode reads his hesitation partly through pride and [[MoralFaceUnderPowerCompetition|moral face]]: if he meant to kill Liu Bang, open attack would fit his warrior identity better than a covert banquet assassination.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-142-zhechang-juhui-xiangyu-shudiao-le-yiqie-ln7dv2jxrk6lt1c4szylsrb41k3r|Hanji 142]] completes the Hongmen failure and then broadens it into statecraft failure. Xiang Yu lets Liu Bang leave after Liu Bang's apparent submission, sends [[ChenPing|陈平]] to search only after the escape is underway, and can do little when Zhang Liang returns with the gifts. Afterward, he enters [[Xianyang|咸阳]], kills [[ZiyingQin|子婴]], burns Qin palace space, plunders goods and women, and rejects [[HanSheng|韩生]]'s advice to make [[GuanzhongRegion|关中]] his strategic base.

The source's sharpest judgment is that Xiang Yu has enough force to decide the post-Qin order but not enough political imagination or self-control to stabilize it. His desire to return home in glory wins over Guanzhong state-building, and Han Sheng's death after the "沐猴而冠" insult shows criticism becoming a trigger for lethal anger rather than a route to corrected strategy.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-150-chenping-weihe-bei-cheng-zhiwei-chenggong-zhengzhijia-yangban-2-lkbo2kwkn6aexyyygv-m84fnqx2h|Hanji 150 part 2]] reopens the Chen Ping thread from Xiang Yu's side. Xiang Yu first trusts Chen Ping enough to send him against [[SimaAng|司马卬]] and rewards him with office and gold, but Sima Ang's later turn toward [[LiuBang|刘邦]] makes Chen Ping fear punishment. The episode therefore adds a talent-retention failure: a capable but exposed subordinate concludes that returning seals and fleeing is safer than remaining under Xiang Yu's anger.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-143-xiangyu-shi-ruhe-feijin-xinji-nongsi-chuhuaiwang-mixin-lstfzfvbd2h9i39f7y5dxrbxxwv2|Hanji 143]] makes Xiang Yu's settlement authority explicit. After [[MiXin|芈心 / 楚怀王]] refuses to abandon the [[HuaiwangAgreement|怀王之约]], Xiang Yu argues that Mi Xin has no real merit and that the generals who fought for three years should divide the world. He virtually honors Mi Xin as 義帝, moves him to [[ChenxianChangsha|郴县]], makes himself West Chu hegemon-king at Pengcheng, assigns [[LiuBang|刘邦]] to [[Nanzheng|南郑]] as Han king, and uses [[ThreeQinContainment|三秦压制]] around [[GuanzhongRegion|关中]]. The episode reads this as [[ZhouQinHybridOrder|周秦混合制]]: constrained and more sophisticated than simple feudal nostalgia, but still an unstable extension of his post-Hongmen statecraft gap.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-144-xiangyu-jituan-fumie-de-yuanyin-1-lr0ft8-jupxgvutn6c89mfs8r4wb|Hanji 144]] evaluates that settlement from the backlash side. The episode says Xiang Yu divides the nineteen kingdoms according to personal preference, turning Liu Bang, [[ZhaoXie|赵歇]], the Qi Tian line, [[HanGuang|韩广]], [[WeiBao|魏豹]], and [[HanCheng|韩成]] into a grievance-bearing "倒项派" while relying on a larger "拥项派" to contain them. It also adds an internal incentive failure: Xiang Yu rewards many allied or surrendered commanders but does not properly settle his own direct generals, making the partition a source of [[RewardAllocationBacklash|分配反噬]] inside his camp as well as outside it.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-144-xiangyu-jituan-fumie-de-yuanyin-2-lut3jiatchla3pfxpkznsrs7wkzu|Hanji 144 part 2]] shows the backlash becoming immediate military disorder. [[TianRong|田荣]] attacks the Qi settlement, uses [[PengYue|彭越]] as an armed partner, and helps [[ChenYu|陈馀]] reopen the Zhao field against [[ZhangEr|张耳]]. Xiang Yu must defend his own new order in the east just as [[LiuBang|刘邦]] hides his western return capacity behind [[BurningGalleryRoads|burned gallery roads]], so the partition's eastern rebellion creates the strategic window that the Hanzhong assignment was supposed to prevent.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-148-2-peng-le-wangling-muqin-xiangyu-sharen-zhuxin-ljyv8bow4rj1l5ths3znx2evgvtm|Hanji 148-2]] adds a coercion backfire to this failing-order branch. After Liu Bang recovers Guanzhong access and tries to contact [[WangLingHanMinister|王陵]], Xiang Yu captures [[WangLingMother|王陵母亲]] and stages respectful treatment to induce Wang Ling's surrender. Her suicide removes the hostage leverage, and Xiang Yu's order to cook the corpse becomes a cruelty signal that pushes Wang Ling fully toward Liu Bang in the episode's reading.

The same source keeps Xiang Yu under settlement overload. Liu Bang claims only to restore the [[HuaiwangAgreement|怀王之约]], while [[TianRong|田荣]], [[PengYue|彭越]], Yan's [[HanGuang|韩广]] / [[ZangTu|臧荼]] conflict, and [[MiXin|芈心 / 楚怀王]]'s forced removal pull Xiang Yu toward other fires. His violence against Wang Ling's mother therefore sits beside the larger problem: coercive responses create enemies while unresolved partition grievances consume attention.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-149-xichu-bawang-xiangyu-weihe-shijun-1-lsqxs2hzu0ngnrpmzsklkqhtyuvw|Hanji 149]] turns the unresolved Mi Xin pressure into open regicide. Xiang Yu has [[YingBu|英布]], [[WuRui|吴芮]], and [[GongAo|共敖]] pursue and kill Yi Emperor [[MiXin|芈心 / 楚怀王]] near [[ChenxianChangsha|郴县]], despite their reluctance to attack a former anti-Qin盟主. The episode says Xiang Yu wants to remove both an old grudge from the [[HuaiwangAgreement|怀王之约]] and a possible political banner for Liu Bang.

The result is another backfire in Xiang Yu's statecraft branch. By killing the symbolic ruler he cannot use, Xiang Yu gives [[LiuBang|刘邦]] a public accusation with which to call lords against him. The source therefore deepens Xiang Yu's pattern: short-term coercive clarity converts into longer-term legitimacy loss.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-149-xichu-bawang-xiangyu-weihe-shijun-2-lpohbb3prsgvokpjipfd7i1ntiwn|Hanji 149 part 2]] gives the battlefield-administration consequence. Xiang Yu appoints [[ZhengChangHanKing|郑昌]] after killing [[HanCheng|韩成]], but Zheng Chang surrenders to Liu Bang; [[ShenYang|申阳]], [[WeiBao|魏豹]], and [[SimaAng|司马卬]] also show how Xiang Yu's partition can be converted into Liu Bang-side commanderies or defections. In Qi, Xiang Yu defeats [[TianRong|田荣]] but continues punitive killing and plunder after local submission becomes possible, creating a larger [[AtrocityBackfireMobilization|暴行反噬式动员]] problem.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-151-huanyuan-pengcheng-zhizhan-xiangyu-zai-fengshen-1-lspx-bqe5kczq6bmn6thlerfbrmu|Hanji 151 part 1]] shows that Qi backfire becoming the immediate precondition for [[PengchengBattle|彭城之战]]. [[TianHeng|田横]] gathers Qi remnants, installs [[TianGuangQiKing|田广]] as king, and holds around [[JuWarringStates|莒城 / 城阳]], so Xiang Yu remains away from [[Xuzhou|彭城]] while [[LiuBang|刘邦]] assembles a coalition and takes the Chu capital. The episode ends with Xiang Yu hearing the news, becoming furious, and moving at once, but it has not yet narrated the counterattack.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-151-huanyuan-pengcheng-zhizhan-xiangyu-zai-fengshen-2-lqy1uu29xbfipo9e0kq6obkl3oay|Hanji 151 part 2]] turns that reaction into Xiang Yu's tactical peak. He blocks information, keeps the visible Qi operation in place, secretly selects about thirty thousand elite mounted and chariot troops, takes [[XiaoCounty|萧县]] instead of first attacking Pengcheng, and then strikes [[LiuBang|刘邦]]'s coalition from the west. The source presents his victory as [[CavalryShockCommandStrike|骑兵奇袭指挥中枢]]: intelligence, terrain familiarity, route cutting, and direct command-center pressure let a small force destroy a much larger but poorly coordinated army.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-152-xiangyu-3wanren-ruhe-wansheng-liubang-56wan-dajun-1-lrtpxlak0dlyej8bozmmhdtfczno|Hanji 152 part 1]] keeps Xiang Yu's Pengcheng victory but shifts part of the explanation to the enemy side. The episode argues that the thirty-thousand-force success worked because Liu Bang personally handled a coalition beyond his command ceiling while [[HanXin|韩信]] was left in Guanzhong. Xiang Yu's tactical brilliance therefore meets [[AncientLargeArmyCommandLimits|古代大兵团指挥极限]] on the Han side.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-152-xiangyu-3wanren-ruhe-wansheng-liubang-56wan-dajun-2-lq9ecpjb4r-ij0-e-iu-ezfavowj|Hanji 152 part 2]] adds the victory's political payoff. Xiang Yu captures [[LiuTaigong|刘太公]] and [[LuZhi|吕雉]] as hostages, and his restored strength pulls multiple lords away from [[LiuBang|刘邦]] under [[PowerBasedAllianceRepricing|实力重估式阵营转向]]. The Qi branch also bends back toward him: after [[TianHeng|田横]] defeats [[TianJia|田假]], Tian Jia flees to Xiang Yu and is killed, while Tian Heng reaches a settlement with Xiang Yu.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-153-xiayi-huace-canbai-hou-liubang-ruhe-qisi-huisheng-1-lgvjuxuskyyfmyeeqm9y8aeln-hu|Hanji 153]] shows the hidden cost of that restored strength. [[ZhangLiang|张良]]'s [[XiayiPlan|下邑画策]] treats [[YingBu|英布]] as Xiang Yu's vulnerable southern-side commander because Ying Bu has already claimed illness instead of fully supporting the Qi and Pengcheng campaigns. Xiang Yu rebukes and summons him through envoys but does not repair the relationship face to face, and his earlier reward of Jiujiang kingship has given Ying Bu a separate power base to protect.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-153-xiayi-huace-canbai-hou-liubang-ruhe-qisi-huisheng-2-ll-rvx07pyw-qg1jofkjro-qfllu|Hanji 153 part 2]] adds the first limit to the Pengcheng advantage. Xiang Yu's cavalry remains the force Liu Bang is trying to answer, but [[HanXin|韩信]] blocks Chu pursuit south of [[YingyangWarringStates|荥阳]] and [[GuanYing|灌婴]]'s new cavalry defeats Chu cavalry east of the city. The episode therefore shifts Xiang Yu from total tactical dominance at Pengcheng into the beginning of [[XingyangStandoff|荥阳相持]], where Han logistics and cavalry learning keep him from simply rolling the defeat forward.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-155-2-zhuan-gei-ni-piqi-da-de-pengyou-weiwang-panhan-jing-shi-liubang-ai-maren-ls5nvunmhakvefd2oxn4zdyoob4l|Hanji 155-2]] shows Xiang Yu's restored pull working indirectly through [[WeiBao|魏豹]] and the northern states. Wei Bao's return to Chu alignment creates the immediate campaign that [[HanXin|韩信]] answers at [[XiayangChuHan|下阳]] and [[Anyi|安邑]], while Han Xin's later proposal to take Zhao, Yan, and Qi aims to cut Xiang Yu's grain route from the north and east. Xiang Yu is therefore less a battlefield actor in this source than the pressure system Han strategy is trying to isolate.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-158-koucai-hao-de-tianhuaban-yiren-ding-baiwan-dajun-lnup-vmw-ff-nqmuh6-x8si0k8zu|Hanji 158]] turns the earlier [[YingBu|英布]] trust fracture into an open strategic loss. [[SuiHe|随何]] persuades Ying Bu at [[JiujiangChuHan|九江国]] and then forces the break by publicly declaring the defection before Xiang Yu's envoy. For Xiang Yu, the episode shows a former reward recipient and execution instrument becoming a new enemy, forcing Chu to divert troops and weakening the pressure on [[LiuBang|刘邦]] around [[XingyangStandoff|荥阳相持]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-159-1-liubang-xijiao-de-yuren-zhishu-lga3puexunjzeu3s8pvt8saaa8n3|Hanji 159-1]] shows the counterpressure after that loss. [[LongJuChuGeneral|龙且]] defeats Ying Bu's remaining Jiujiang position and returns the region to Chu control, then reinforces Xiang Yu near Xingyang. The episode says Chu captures [[Aocang|敖仓]], controls Yellow River transport, and attacks Han's food corridors, so Xiang Yu's camp regains enough pressure to make Liu Bang consider the dangerous six-kingdom restoration proposal.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-159-2-zhangliang-shuole-sha-jiuxia-hanchao-lvs9elvrjp-ypz63w4czpuhbsfb|Hanji 159-2]] makes Xiang Yu the external attractor behind Zhang Liang's warning. If Liu Bang restores six kingdoms while Xiang Yu remains militarily powerful, the new kings may look to West Chu rather than Han. Xiang Yu's strength therefore turns Li Shiqi's proposed fragmentation from an anti-Chu device into a possible pro-Chu alignment channel.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-160-chenping-gongxinji-bi-zhenhuanchuan-jingcai-1-lujcothn8aunydmznta1tkglqt-q|Hanji 160 part 1]] turns that military pressure into a vulnerability inside Xiang Yu's own camp. He nearly accepts Liu Bang's peace request after [[XiangBo|项伯]] advises him, but [[FanZeng|范增]] persuades him to press the siege. [[ChenPing|陈平]] then reads Xiang Yu's personal pattern against him: respectful treatment can attract clean men, but stingy reward distribution leaves commanders such as [[ZhongliMo|钟离眜]] exposed to grievance rumors, and Xiang Yu's suspicious temperament makes those rumors usable.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-160-chenping-gongxinji-bi-zhenhuanchuan-jingcai-2-lmjiydb2weahtauisouw5mdiwtoo|Hanji 160 part 2]] makes the vulnerability structural. Chen Ping's envoy-feast performance causes Xiang Yu to doubt Fan Zeng, but the host argues that this only works because Xiang Yu already fails to balance [[XiangCampFactionalCleavage|项羽阵营派系裂缝]]: Xiang-family figures such as Xiang Bo, [[XiangZhuang|项庄]], and [[XiangTuoChuHan|项佗]] sit beside old Chu merit commanders such as Fan Zeng, [[LongJuChuGeneral|龙且]], [[ZhongliMo|钟离眜]], and [[ZhouYin|周殷]]. Xiang Yu's bias toward kin and confidence in his own force make Fan Zeng's withdrawal possible, leaving military strength without the same adviser check.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-162-1-ta-ti-liubang-qu-si-rujin-chengle-zhengzhou-de-shouhushen-li3su2zlmbm1ift9nibujgtcrxwm|Hanji 162-1]] shows Xiang Yu winning the immediate siege pressure but losing the reaction game. He burns [[JiXin|纪信]] after discovering the false surrender, then follows [[LiuBang|刘邦]] south toward [[WanCounty|宛县]] when Liu Bang uses [[Wuguan|武关]] as a diversion route. The host criticizes this as reactive strategy: instead of turning [[Aocang|敖仓]] and the central front into continuous pressure, Xiang Yu gets fixed by Liu Bang's fortified refusal to fight while [[PengYue|彭越]] cuts logistics around the [[SuiRiver|睢水]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-162-2-liubang-ruhe-shoushi-wei-gao-quan-zhong-de-xiashu-lqvye2zmqhu3otk0uwu7lakf2prz|Hanji 162-2]] sharpens that reactive-strategy diagnosis. Xiang Yu leaves [[Chenggao|成皋]] to deal with Peng Yue around [[Xiapi|下邳]], returns to retake Xingyang and Chenggao, and kills [[ZhouKe|周苛]] and [[CongGong|枞公]] after Zhou Ke refuses a high-rank offer. The host treats these as tactical wins with strategic cost: Xiang Yu can still defeat enemies personally, but the need to answer every front himself leaves the Chu army tired and gives Liu Bang openings to regroup.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-162-3-zuipao-wang-lishiqi-budong-yibing-naxia-70-yu-cheng-lq88alghxqxxcddk4zzqodkezipx|Hanji 162-3]] continues that strain. When [[PengYue|彭越]]'s reinforced raids intensify, Xiang Yu leaves [[CaoJiu|曹咎]] to hold [[Chenggao|成皋]] and turns east again, showing that even a recovered central-front position still cannot free him from rear logistics pressure. The same episode also turns Xiang Yu into the negative comparison in [[LiShiqi|郦食其]]'s persuasion of [[TianGuangQiKing|田广]]: broken promises, Yi Emperor's death, and weak reward allocation make Chu look less reliable than Han.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-163-liubang-shouxia-moushi-ruhe-bei-hanxin-neijuan-cansi-lubinu6hoihu59knmlixmaryhnq7|Hanji 163]] shows Xiang Yu returning to a worse central front: Cao Jiu has ignored his order, lost Chenggao and [[Aocang|敖仓]], and left Chu short of food. At [[GuangwuChuHan|广武]], Xiang Yu threatens to cook [[LiuTaigong|刘太公]], is talked down by [[XiangBo|项伯]], and then challenges Liu Bang to a duel that Liu Bang refuses. The episode preserves his personal intimidation through the [[Loufan|楼烦]] archers while showing that personal force no longer solves Chu's strategic position.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-164-1-liubang-geqiang-chaofeng-qi-de-xiangyu-shejian-touxi-lhs6miq2aa3avnny-prxtvkfxgvy|Hanji 164-1]] makes that personal-force limit sharper. At Guangwu, Liu Bang's [[LegitimacyIndictmentWarfare|正当性控诉战]] turns Xiang Yu's duel frame into a public accusation over broken promises, palace burning, surrendered-prisoner killing, unfair partition, and Yi Emperor [[MiXin|芈心 / 楚怀王]]'s murder. Xiang Yu cannot answer the indictment verbally, so he answers with a hidden crossbow shot; the source reads this as force interrupting speech without restoring political initiative. The later Qi-front section also shows him forced to answer [[TianGuangQiKing|田广]]'s appeal by sending [[LongJuChuGeneral|龙且]], exposing Chu to another theater while Guangwu remains unresolved.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-164-2-xiangyu-touhao-dajiang-zenme-shu-gei-hanxin-de-lhe-lmb1zrzipbongg02gmnmb4l|Hanji 164-2]] turns that Qi rescue into a deeper Xiang Yu weakness. Long Ju is presented as one of the few trusted and capable Chu commanders, yet his role has remained under Xiang Yu's shadow long enough that the episode reads him as proud, under-tested, and prone to "匹夫之勇." His death at [[WeishuiBattle|潍水之战]] therefore damages Xiang Yu materially and interpretively: Chu loses a major commander, and the source uses the loss to contrast Xiang Yu's personal-force, low-delegation style with [[LiuBang|刘邦]]'s specialist-use pattern.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-165-1-liubang-zheju-hua-chadian-nongdiu-dahan-jiangshan-llxgisomsdo8qjeecgdg6frri-xq|Hanji 165-1]] records the strategic fear created by that loss. After Long Ju dies and [[HanXin|韩信]] holds [[QiState|齐国]], Xiang Yu sends [[WuShe|武涉]] to [[Linzi|临淄]] to argue that Han Xin should at least stay neutral and ideally preserve a Chu-Han-Qi division. Xiang Yu's branch therefore shifts from trying to solve every front through force to trying, late, to recover leverage through [[TripartiteBalanceStrategy|三分天下式制衡]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-165-2-hanxin-weihe-zhisi-ye-bu-beipan-liubang-lrgtvbdyc9cwbfhaix1aabsmqhhb|Hanji 165-2]] makes Xiang Yu one half of [[KuaiChe|蒯彻 / 蒯通]]'s balance-of-power warning rather than an active battlefield actor. Kuai Che says Chu and Han have exhausted each other around the central front and that Han Xin now decides which side wins. But he also tells Han Xin that returning to Chu is not a safe exit because Xiang Yu would not trust a former Han-side king of Qi; preserving Chu as a counterweight is safer than submitting to Chu.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-166-jiran-xiangyu-bujiang-wude-kan-liubang-ruhe-fuchou-ltwwbn9rzeqzzimmxxfihb9-drj1|Hanji 166]] turns that strategic weakness into negotiated withdrawal. Lacking reinforcements and expecting Han Xin to join Liu Bang, Xiang Yu first rejects [[LuJia|陆贾]] but accepts [[HouGongChuHan|侯公]]'s negotiation, returns [[LiuTaigong|刘太公]] and [[LuZhi|吕雉]], and agrees to the [[HonggouSettlement|鸿沟议和]]. The episode's closing judgment makes his personal heroism part of the tragedy: it sustains the image of a hero, but cannot replace durable alliance, supply, and timing advantages.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-167-jianzheng-xiangyu-zuizhong-zhizhan-1-lm6nwhjjuguhrmwzoz0sqawgxwuv|Hanji 167]] moves Xiang Yu from negotiated withdrawal into the Gaixia endpoint. Liu Bang's initial pursuit is not enough by itself, but once Han Xin and Peng Yue receive explicit territorial promises and Zhou Yin defects in Huainan, Xiang Yu is pressed from several sides. The source preserves his confidence in open-field shock and the disciplined Jiangdong core, while ending with the first charge failing to throw the allied army into disorder.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-167-jianzheng-xiangyu-zuizhong-zhizhan-2-li14pizmfrzhpg8m5bmxdx8ibw7p|Hanji 167 part 2]] turns that failed first charge into the final battlefield explanation. Xiang Yu still uses the only tactic available to a smaller, hungry Chu army: a rapid shock attack meant to break the coalition's command center, as at [[PengchengBattle|彭城之战]]. At Gaixia, however, [[HanXin|韩信]]'s [[LayeredDepthDefense|纵深分层防御]] absorbs the blow. Xiang Yu breaks through the first two lines but cannot create panic, fails at the [[ZhouBo|周勃]] / [[ChaiWu|柴武]] third line, and is then surrounded by converging Han forces.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-168-bawangbieji-qian-fashengle-shenme-1-lowxwc8oq8as1trjo-gjimmuhnr3|Hanji 168 part 1]] follows Xiang Yu after the field defeat rather than as an active battlefield commander. [[FourSidedChuSongs|四面楚歌]] turns encirclement into perceived homeland collapse, [[GaixiaSong|《垓下歌》]] exposes his command despair, and [[YuJi|虞姬]]'s suicide resolves the personal dilemma he cannot state directly. The source then makes his breakout morally and politically stark: after burying Yu Ji he leaves most of the remaining Chu army, escapes with eight hundred men, is pursued by [[GuanYing|灌婴]], and finally reads the narrowing road as Heaven wanting to destroy him, while the host answers through [[PeopleBasedPoliticalSecurity|民心型政治安全]] that Heaven appears as lost people-support.
