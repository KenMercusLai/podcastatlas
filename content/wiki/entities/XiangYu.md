# 项羽 / Xiang Yu

Xiang Yu is the dominant host and failed would-be ruler in the episode's reading of [[HongmenYan|《鸿门宴》]] in [[164-guwen-de-liliang-qing-shou-xia-zhe-fen-qiannian-wei-bian-de-shenqing-dongjian-he-yongqi-940236576]]. The source presents him as powerful, proud, susceptible to recognition, and politically dangerous because he can humiliate [[LiuBang|刘邦]] without finally killing him.

The episode's larger judgment is ambivalent. QinZong says a harder, more shameless Xiang Yu might have won, but she also connects his residual need to "要脸" with [[SimaQian|司马迁]]'s decision to place him in imperial annals. Xiang Yu therefore anchors [[MoralFaceUnderPowerCompetition]] rather than a simple lesson that kindness loses.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-45-yi-shi-wei-jian-junwang-daodi-gai-bu-gai-sharen-chengxing-lo3cok7cfwzrxesp-i8miz1fc1db]] uses Xiang Yu in a different contrast. In the host's response to [[Mengzi|孟子 / Mencius]]'s [[NonKillingUnification|不爱杀人者能一之]] claim, Xiang Yu is framed as the more killing-oriented Chu-Han contender, while [[LiuBang|刘邦]] becomes the less-killing figure who founds a durable Han order. This source therefore makes Xiang Yu a negative durability case, not only a banquet power-scene actor.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-128-6-dingji-tianshilun-touziren-lvgong-lhqqja27zxdiypgf6wuv1abgkw3j|Qinji 128-6]] names Xiang Yu as one of the real protagonists about to enter after [[ChenSheng|陈胜]] and [[WuGuang|吴广]] open the anti-[[QinState|秦国]] rebellion. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-128-7-liubang-he-xiangyu-youhe-butong-llwcqatndck4zita2piufwhjpxp1|Qinji 128-7]] then supplies his early profile: a [[ChuState|楚国]] aristocratic descendant, [[XiangYan|项燕]]'s grandson, and [[XiangLiang|项梁]]'s nephew who wants to study the "万人敌" rather than mere literacy or one-on-one swordsmanship.

The same source presents Xiang Yu's gifts and dangers together. He is physically imposing, unusually strong, and terrifying in the [[KuaijiCommandery|会稽郡]] office when he kills [[YinTong|殷通]] and fights resisting guards. But the episode also marks impatience: he likes military study at first, then refuses to go deeply into it. Xiang Yu's anti-Qin entry is therefore framed through martial charisma and [[KuaijiCommanderyCoup|会稽郡守府夺权]], sharply contrasting with Liu Bang's [[PeiCountyUprisingMobilization|沛县起义动员]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-130-1-guailaotou-yiqiao-san-shua-zhangliang-chuanshou-shenshu-lqwjo13zzfgtndpajzpuvrb2h4l2|Qinji 130-1]] keeps Xiang Yu in his uncle's command but moves him onto the broader campaign road. After [[ZhaoPingRebel|昭平]]'s false appointment gives [[XiangLiang|项梁]] a Chu title, Xiang Yu crosses the Yangtze with him and eight thousand [[KuaijiCommandery|会稽郡]] troops.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-130-2-lishi-jilu-shouci-tucheng-lm4kuzdpnqn-hicyuvvb4p2wdudm|Qinji 130-2]] adds the first explicit massacre marker to Xiang Yu's branch. Xiang Liang sends him against [[XiangchengQin|襄城]] in Yingchuan commandery; after the city resists and falls, Xiang Yu kills the city's military and civilian population. The episode uses this as a source-scoped first recorded "屠城" and as evidence for the later judgment that Xiang Yu's cruelty exceeds [[LiuBang|刘邦]]'s, tying him more tightly to [[WarringStatesWarBrutalization|战国战争残酷化]], [[PostBattleTerrorSignaling|战后恐怖震慑]], and the negative side of [[NonKillingUnification|不爱杀人者能一之]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-132-5wan-wanbao-40wan-xiangyu-cizhan-fengshen-lk87nb3fskg5zh6fcgptypixhzuw|Qinji 132]] turns that brutality marker into a concrete assignment problem. After [[ZhangHanQin|章邯]] kills [[XiangLiang|项梁]] at [[Dingtao|定陶]], Xiang Yu joins Liu Bang and [[LuChenQinRebel|吕臣]] in moving [[MiXin|芈心 / 楚怀王]] to [[Xuzhou|彭城]]. Mi Xin then gives Xiang Yu the Chang'an Hou title and Lu Gong designation.

Xiang Yu asks to accompany Liu Bang west against Qin because he wants revenge for Xiang Liang's death. The older Chu generals block the request by citing his "狡诈残暴" profile and the [[XiangchengQin|襄城]] massacre: they argue that Qin's overburdened population needs a more generous pacifier. Xiang Yu is therefore assigned as second general under [[SongYi|宋义]] for the [[Julu|巨鹿]] relief mission, with [[FanZeng|范增]] also in the command structure, while Liu Bang receives the westward [[GuanzhongRegion|关中]] route under [[HuaiwangAgreement|怀王之约]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-133-songyi-zhan-xiangyu-weihe-bei-fansha-lms7noyrm5omedv77gqfhjeu53zx|Qinji 133]] turns that subordinate assignment into Xiang Yu's takeover moment. When Song Yi stops at [[AnyangQinRebellion|安阳]], Xiang Yu urges immediate crossing and a combined inside-outside attack with Zhao. The episode frames him as impatient and violent, but also as strategically alert to the possibility that Qin might grow stronger if it destroys Zhao and absorbs its supplies.

Song Yi's insult and targeted discipline order make Xiang Yu's conflict personal, while Song Yi's [[WuyanCounty|无盐县]] banquet lets Xiang Yu appeal to the soldiers' hardship. Xiang Yu then enters the command tent and kills Song Yi, converting command grievance into [[WartimeCommandDecapitation|战时统帅斩首]]. This is a major step in [[MilitaryPersonalization|军队私人化]]: formal appointment still belongs to Mi Xin's Chu court, but actual army power shifts to Xiang Yu through violence and camp legitimacy.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-134-ni-buzhidao-de-pofu-chenzhou-banben-ltnry1fva2hzplw4ft8nrfpoiolw|Qinji 134]] turns Xiang Yu's seized command into his [[JuluBattle|巨鹿之战]] victory. The episode stresses sequence: Xiang Yu first sends [[YingBu|英布]] and [[PuGeneral|蒲将军]] to break Qin's protected supply corridor from [[Aocang|敖仓]] toward [[Julu|巨鹿]], then crosses the [[ZhangRiver|漳水]] with the main force and uses [[BreakingCauldronsAndSinkingBoats|破釜沉舟]] to force death-ground morale.

The victory gives Xiang Yu more than battlefield fame. Other lords had watched from their camps until Chu broke [[WangLiQin|王离]]'s army; after the battle, they submit to Xiang Yu as coalition commander. This makes Julu the moment when his personal military authority begins to dominate the anti-Qin coalition, not just a tactical success.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-137-shendu-jiedu-zhiluweima-zhong-de-quanli-boyi-lgkjmddvrcelw2ltdwt92e4poguj|Qinji 137]] gives the immediate post-Julu sequel. Xiang Yu accepts [[ZhangHanQin|章邯]]'s surrender partly because Chu supplies are strained, swears with him south of Huan water, keeps him near headquarters, appoints [[SimaXin|司马欣]] as upper general, and puts the absorbed Qin troops in the vanguard. The episode sharply criticizes the settlement decision to make Zhang Han 雍王: because 雍州 effectively means the Qin/[[GuanzhongRegion|关中]] core, the appointment undercuts the [[HuaiwangAgreement|怀王之约]] and shows Xiang Yu's weak postwar design.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-139-ta-zaici-yingxiang-hanchao-lp1zujuq0o3ohxc79xqi1phpb5zo|Hanji 139]] turns that post-Julu settlement weakness into the [[XinanMassacre|新安坑降]]. While leading the coalition and surrendered Qin troops west toward [[HanguPass|函谷关]], Xiang Yu treats the large, resentful Qin force as a mutiny risk and consults [[YingBu|英布]] and [[PuGeneral|蒲将军]]. The episode says the resulting night killing of surrendered soldiers leaves only Zhang Han, Sima Xin, and [[DongYiQin|董义]] alive among the senior surrendered leaders, making Xinan the clearest source case where Xiang Yu's military risk management destroys his ability to win [[GuanzhongRegion|关中]] people-support.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-140-xiangyu-yu-liubang-shi-zhen-youqing-haishi-xianghu-liyong-1-lmmhoq2wo3bs6b1johpc0ixuf662|Hanji 140 part 1]] moves Xiang Yu from that damaged Guanzhong position into the pre-[[HongmenYan|鸿门宴]] confrontation. After learning that [[LiuBang|刘邦]] has entered Guanzhong first and closed the entry routes, Xiang Yu orders [[YingBu|英布]] and others to attack the pass. [[CaoWushang|曹无伤]]'s leak then triggers another angry response: Xiang Yu decides to attack Liu Bang the next morning, while the host criticizes him for lacking a full settlement plan after taking the "toy" back.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-140-xiangyu-yu-liubang-shi-zhen-youqing-haishi-xianghu-liyong-2-lvdgirxmwxcckh2loh-yk2zzer2e|Hanji 140 part 2]] shows why that attack order is cancelled. [[XiangBo|项伯]] returns from [[Bashang|霸上]] and argues that Liu Bang's prior entry into [[GuanzhongRegion|关中]] helped the whole coalition, so attacking him would be "不义." The episode stresses Xiang Bo's family standing after [[XiangLiang|项梁]]'s death and reads Xiang Yu as emotionally responsive to kinship, old Chu-camp comradeship, and the language of义.

The source therefore sharpens Xiang Yu's profile as a powerful commander whose decisions remain vulnerable to affective frames. He is not reduced to pure stupidity or pure interest calculation; he can be moved by an elder's moral narrative even after anger and Fan Zeng's threat diagnosis have already pointed him toward killing Liu Bang.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-141-huanyuan-lishi-zhong-de-hongmenyan-lpmb-bcoocngtqjvtnpj0elm-siy|Hanji 141]] shows that vulnerability continuing inside the banquet. Xiang Yu accepts [[LiuBang|刘邦]]'s apology posture, casually exposes [[CaoWushang|曹无伤]] as Liu Bang's informer, and then ignores [[FanZeng|范增]]'s repeated jade-ring signals to kill Liu Bang. The episode reads his hesitation partly through pride and [[MoralFaceUnderPowerCompetition|moral face]]: if he meant to kill Liu Bang, open attack would fit his warrior identity better than a covert banquet assassination.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-142-zhechang-juhui-xiangyu-shudiao-le-yiqie-ln7dv2jxrk6lt1c4szylsrb41k3r|Hanji 142]] completes the Hongmen failure and then broadens it into statecraft failure. Xiang Yu lets Liu Bang leave after Liu Bang's apparent submission, sends [[ChenPing|陈平]] to search only after the escape is underway, and can do little when Zhang Liang returns with the gifts. Afterward, he enters [[Xianyang|咸阳]], kills [[ZiyingQin|子婴]], burns Qin palace space, plunders goods and women, and rejects [[HanSheng|韩生]]'s advice to make [[GuanzhongRegion|关中]] his strategic base.

The source's sharpest judgment is that Xiang Yu has enough force to decide the post-Qin order but not enough political imagination or self-control to stabilize it. His desire to return home in glory wins over Guanzhong state-building, and Han Sheng's death after the "沐猴而冠" insult shows criticism becoming a trigger for lethal anger rather than a route to corrected strategy.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-143-xiangyu-shi-ruhe-feijin-xinji-nongsi-chuhuaiwang-mixin-lstfzfvbd2h9i39f7y5dxrbxxwv2|Hanji 143]] makes Xiang Yu's settlement authority explicit. After [[MiXin|芈心 / 楚怀王]] refuses to abandon the [[HuaiwangAgreement|怀王之约]], Xiang Yu argues that Mi Xin has no real merit and that the generals who fought for three years should divide the world. He virtually honors Mi Xin as 義帝, moves him to [[ChenxianChangsha|郴县]], makes himself West Chu hegemon-king at Pengcheng, assigns [[LiuBang|刘邦]] to [[Nanzheng|南郑]] as Han king, and uses [[ThreeQinContainment|三秦压制]] around [[GuanzhongRegion|关中]]. The episode reads this as [[ZhouQinHybridOrder|周秦混合制]]: constrained and more sophisticated than simple feudal nostalgia, but still an unstable extension of his post-Hongmen statecraft gap.
