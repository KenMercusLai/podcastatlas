# Dan Snow

## Overview

Dan Snow is a historian and broadcaster who presents the [[SevenYearsWar|Seven Years' War]] as a globally decisive conflict linking Britain and France, Prussia, North America, India, naval power, public finance, empire, and revolution.

## Current Profile

[[47-the-seven-years-war-glt8800494407]] introduces Snow through his work on Quebec and the birth of empire. His interpretation places the war inside a long eighteenth century and a prolonged Anglo-French rivalry rather than isolating it as a 1756-1763 European contest. He combines military narrative with institutional explanation: Cook's charts, coalition warfare, blockade, credit, borrowing, and subsidy matter alongside Quebec, Quiberon Bay, and Plassey.

Snow's account is willing to call the war foundational for British global power while qualifying patriotic memory. Caribbean wealth depended on enslaved labor, West African acquisitions connected to slave trading, Plassey involved bribery and Indian elite interests, and territorial success produced debt and resistance that helped fracture the empire.

## Key Characteristics

- Public historian who interprets a complex war through global connections rather than battle chronology alone.
- Advocate of a long-eighteenth-century frame running from the post-1688 order to 1815.
- Emphasizes naval, fiscal, logistical, cartographic, and coalition capacity as causes of British success.
- Connects military settlement to British identity, imperial memory, and the spread of Anglophone institutions.
- Qualifies heroic imperial narrative through slavery, corruption, Native resistance, and political blowback.

## Evidence

- Global framing: [[47-the-seven-years-war-glt8800494407]] joins North America, Europe, India, the Caribbean, West Africa, and the Atlantic within one interpretive account.
- Institutional explanation: [[47-the-seven-years-war-glt8800494407]] emphasizes public credit, low-interest borrowing, subsidy, naval supply, blockade, and charting.
- Critical qualification: [[47-the-seven-years-war-glt8800494407]] places slavery, slave trading, bribery, territorial expense, and resistance inside the victory narrative.
- Long consequences: [[47-the-seven-years-war-glt8800494407]] links the settlement to British global power, Prussia, and the fiscal-political background of two revolutions.

## Qualifications

This profile rests on one summarized podcast appearance rather than Snow's full published work. Its large causal claims, the precise boundaries of his long eighteenth century, and the balance between his interpretation and the hosts' contributions remain source-scoped. The episode's British center gives Indian and Native actors less independent treatment than its global ambition would ideally require.
