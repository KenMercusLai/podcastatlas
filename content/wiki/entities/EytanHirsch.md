# Eytan Hirsch

## Overview
Eytan Hirsch is the political scientist represented in a [[HiddenBrain]] episode through his distinction between [[PoliticalHobbyism]] and organized attempts to acquire and exercise political power.

## Current Profile
Hirsch evaluates political engagement by its intended effect rather than by the intensity of feeling or quantity of information consumed. News-following, partisan discussion, memes, donations, and election enthusiasm may support participation, but they remain hobbyism when emotional gratification, identity, access, or spectacle is the endpoint. Political work instead tries to move voters, policy, institutions, or officials through repeated organization.

His positive model begins locally. The “Ukrainian boss” case shows practical help with citizenship and neighborhood problems becoming trust, unusually high turnout, and leverage with politicians. Deep canvassing adds difficult persuasion through vulnerability and personal connection. Hirsch's examples therefore connect immediate service, relationship-building, local institutional attention, and sustained mobilization while warning that the same methods can build immoral movements.

## Key Characteristics
- Defines political engagement by attempts to influence elections, policy, institutions, or other people.
- Diagnoses national news, scandal, partisan conflict, and candidate celebrity as possible forms of entertainment rather than power.
- Treats anger as politically useful only when it is converted into organized action.
- Emphasizes local participation, repeated relationship-building, and practical service over episodic excitement.
- Uses turnout, donation, petition, media, and community-leader examples to separate symbolic expression from strategic effect.
- Presents persuasion across disagreement as difficult political labor rather than betrayal of identity.

## Qualifications
This profile is bounded to one structured episode summary rather than Hirsch's complete book, scholarship, datasets, or later views. The episode emphasizes individual and organizational practice more than time scarcity, unequal resources, institutional exclusion, access barriers, repression, or unresponsive government. Its election, survey, demographic, donation, petition, media, organizing, and persuasion claims require underlying evidence before magnitudes or generality can be established. Calling an activity hobbyism does not prove it has no indirect value; information, emotion, donation, voting, and public expression can support genuine power-building when connected to a strategy.
