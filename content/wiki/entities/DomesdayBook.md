# Domesday Book

## Overview

Domesday Book appears in the wiki as the 1086 land survey that made [[WilliamTheConqueror]]'s new property order legible while revealing the strength of the English administrative system he inherited.

## Current Profile

The episode presents Domesday as both conquest record and state-capacity artifact. Royal agents asked who held land at Edward the Confessor's death and who held it in 1086, thereby treating [[HaroldGodwinson]]'s reign as legally absent while producing an unusually detailed fiscal map useful for taxation, military preparation, and royal control.

## Key Characteristics

- Comprehensive royal survey of landholding and resources completed through a short, intensive inquiry.
- Legal-memory device comparing Edward's death with William's present and bypassing Harold's reign.
- Proof of the scale of [[NormanEliteReplacement]].
- Product of inherited Anglo-Saxon fiscal and administrative capacity.
- Instrument connected in the episode to taxation and renewed Scandinavian invasion risk.

## Evidence

- Survey method - [[557-1066-the-norman-conquest-part-4-glt5156716826]] says royal agents recorded who held land at Edward's death and who held it in 1086.
- Political effect - [[557-1066-the-norman-conquest-part-4-glt5156716826]] argues that this comparison erased Harold's reign from the operative legal record and confirmed the Norman order.
- Administrative continuity - [[557-1066-the-norman-conquest-part-4-glt5156716826]] treats the survey as evidence that William exploited sophisticated English fiscal machinery.

## Qualifications

The episode uses Domesday chiefly to interpret conquest and state capacity. This page does not reconstruct the survey's complete manuscript history, regional omissions, assessment methods, or later legal reception.
