# Casey Halpern

## Overview
Casey Halpern is the neurosurgeon and Huberman Lab guest who explains how functional neurosurgery studies and modulates circuits involved in movement disorders, compulsive behavior, craving, and severe psychiatric symptoms.

## Current Profile
Across [[essentials-compulsive-behaviors-deep-brain-stimulation-dr-casey-halpern-scim9347675622]] and the full [[biology-treatments-for-compulsive-behaviors-binge-eating-dr-casey-halpern-scim4911670131]] interview, Halpern describes his subspecialty as stereotactic functional neurosurgery, especially [[DeepBrainStimulationPsychiatry]] and [[FocusedUltrasoundNeuromodulation]]. His role is both clinical and translational: movement-disorder surgery shows that stimulation can rapidly change symptoms, while emotional or behavioral spillover demonstrates why target location and timing matter.

The full interview adds the design of an early human study in severe obesity with binge eating disorder. Halpern distinguishes [[LossOfControlEating]] from body weight and formal binge criteria, then combines personally relevant food cues, single-unit recordings, local field potentials, imaging, and connectomics to identify candidate craving signals. [[ResponsiveDeepBrainStimulation]] uses that logic to deliver seconds of stimulation only when a selected signal appears rather than continuously.

The profile centers on severe refractory cases rather than ordinary self-help. Halpern repeatedly distinguishes awareness, behavioral therapy, and medication from patients who remain unable to control dangerous urges despite those supports. Invasive research is therefore framed as a discovery engine for better TMS, ultrasound, physiological monitoring, or other scalable tools, not as a population-scale surgical answer.

## Key Characteristics
- Explains DBS as a therapeutic electrical intervention targeted to small brain regions.
- Uses movement-disorder surgery as a template for finding psychiatric and craving-related signals.
- Treats compulsive behavior as urge pursuit despite risk while preserving diagnosis-specific differences.
- Distinguishes loss-of-control eating from obesity and from a formal binge defined partly by food quantity.
- Tests responsive, signal-triggered stimulation as an alternative to continuous stimulation in episodic symptoms.
- Frames invasive neurosurgery as a discovery path that could later make scalable non-invasive treatments more rigorous.
