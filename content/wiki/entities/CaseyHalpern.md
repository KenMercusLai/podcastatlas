# Casey Halpern

## Overview
Casey Halpern is the neurosurgeon and Huberman Lab guest who explains how functional neurosurgery studies and modulates circuits involved in compulsive behavior, craving, and severe psychiatric symptoms.

## Current Profile
In [[essentials-compulsive-behaviors-deep-brain-stimulation-dr-casey-halpern-scim9347675622]], Halpern describes his subspecialty as stereotactic functional neurosurgery, especially [[DeepBrainStimulationPsychiatry]] and [[FocusedUltrasoundNeuromodulation]]. His role in the source is both clinical and translational: he explains how DBS can treat motor symptoms, how psychiatric effects reveal limbic circuitry, and how invasive human recordings may identify craving- or obsession-related signals that later guide less invasive tools.

The profile centers on severe refractory cases rather than ordinary self-help. Halpern repeatedly distinguishes awareness, behavioral therapy, and medication from patients who remain unable to control dangerous urges despite those supports. His research direction links [[UrgeDespiteRiskCircuit]], [[IntracranialCircuitMapping]], binge eating, OCD, addiction, obesity, depression, and suicidality while preserving the need for careful target selection and clinical oversight.

## Key Characteristics
- Explains DBS as a therapeutic electrical intervention targeted to small brain regions.
- Uses movement-disorder surgery as a template for finding psychiatric and craving-related signals.
- Treats compulsive behavior as urge pursuit despite risk rather than as mere weak motivation.
- Emphasizes that powerful tools such as focused ultrasound still depend on knowing the right circuit target.
- Frames invasive neurosurgery as a discovery path that could later make scalable non-invasive treatments more rigorous.
