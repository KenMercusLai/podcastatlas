# Puy du Fou

## Overview

Puy du Fou is a French historical theme park used in the source as a case of spectacular public history organized around national and martial imagery.

## Current Profile

[[40-history-as-entertainment-glt9519276470]] describes the park as combining Vikings, medieval settings, Roman arena entertainment, musketeers, kings, battles, and Verdun. The episode links its creation to politician Philippe de Villiers and interprets its selection of episodes as compatible with a right-wing French nationalist and conservative historical imagination.

The case shows that immersive scale does not eliminate interpretation. Architecture, casting, chronology, spectacle, and the choice of conflicts produce a legible national story even when visitors primarily seek entertainment.

## Key Characteristics

- Uses large-scale performance and themed environments to make selected historical worlds physically present.
- Privileges spectacular conflict, rulers, warriors, and national memory in the episode's description.
- Blends tourism, theatre, reenactment, and public historical interpretation.
- Demonstrates how selection and spectacle can carry ideology without functioning as a formal political argument.

## Evidence

- **Historical repertoire:** [[40-history-as-entertainment-glt9519276470]] lists Roman, Viking, medieval, musketeer, royal, and Verdun attractions.
- **Political framing:** [[40-history-as-entertainment-glt9519276470]] connects the park to Philippe de Villiers and reads its historical emphasis as nationalist and conservative.

## Qualifications

The episode offers a brief critical portrait, not a systematic study of the park's changing productions, governance, visitor reception, or educational materials. Its popularity ranking and ideological characterization remain source-scoped.
