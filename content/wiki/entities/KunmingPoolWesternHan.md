## Overview

昆明池 / Kunming Pool was a large artificial water body southwest of [[ChangAnHanCapital|Western Han Chang'an]] attributed to [[HanWudi|Emperor Wu]]. The current source presents it not as a single-purpose lake but as infrastructure joining military preparation, water management, production, ritual provision, court recreation, and celestial symbolism.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-317-niulang-zhinv-fayuandi-wo-zai-changan-zhaodao-le-lidupukyt45k8buwneivkmgs3u89|Hanji 317]] says Han Wudi commissioned the pool after learning of the southwestern Kunming polity and Dianchi, making imitation of a distant landscape part of capital construction and frontier imagination. Its practical profile includes naval training, irrigation, grain transport, storage, linked channels, drainage, fish cultivation, sacrificial supply, and imperial boating.

The same source makes the pool an imperial cosmogram. Stone figures of [[CowherdAndWeaverGirl|牵牛 and 织女]] stood on opposite sides while the pool represented the Milky Way. That layout is material evidence that the celestial pair had entered monumental landscape design, but it cannot alone establish that the full later romance originated at Kunming Pool.

The source attributes the pool's eventual disappearance after the Tang to sedimentation, upstream tree loss, population pressure, and inadequate dredging. These causes remain an episode-level synthesis pending comparison with archaeological and environmental-history evidence.

## Key Characteristics

- A large Western Han artificial water project integrated into Chang'an's southwest landscape.
- Served military, irrigation, transport, storage, drainage, productive, ritual, and recreational functions in the episode's account.
- Connected distant southwestern geography to imperial construction through its Dianchi/Kunming naming story.
- Used opposed 牵牛 and 织女 figures to turn water into a terrestrial representation of the Milky Way.
- Outlived its original engineering life through surviving sculptures and later religious memory.

## Qualifications

The current profile rests on one short structured episode summary, not a transcript, excavation report, hydrological reconstruction, or primary-source edition. The pool's dimensions, exact date, channel arrangement, claimed status as China's first large artificial lake, economic output, military effectiveness, statue dating and original placement, later temple history, and precise decline chronology remain source-scoped. The episode describes many benefits without accounting for construction labor, fiscal cost, displacement, ecological impact, or opportunity cost. Its Jade-Emperor analogy and its claim that the site is the birthplace of the Cowherd-Weaver story are interpretations rather than settled conclusions.

## What Changed

- Created a focused profile separating the pool's infrastructure functions from its celestial and folkloric afterlife.
- Preserved the stone figures as material evidence while narrowing the episode's single-birthplace claim.
