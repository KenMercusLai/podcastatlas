# Garnet Wolseley

## Overview

Garnet Wolseley was the senior British officer, friend of [[CharlesGeorgeGordon|General Gordon]], and advocate of military intervention who eventually commanded the unsuccessful Khartoum relief expedition.

## Current Profile

The episode treats Wolseley's concern for Gordon as compatible with institutional and personal ambition. He backed the demand to send Gordon and wanted a Sudan expedition partly because it would strengthen his military position. When [[WilliamEwartGladstone|Gladstone]] finally authorized relief, logistical constraints slowed the force, and its steamers reached Khartoum two days after the city fell.

Wolseley then helped define the public meaning of failure. His furious message to Queen Victoria blamed Gladstone, while his description of Gordon as an embodiment of manly Christian virtue contributed to the martyr image that displaced attention from the expedition's own timing, military limits, and the wider Sudanese war.

## Key Characteristics

- Gordon's friend and influential advocate for intervention.
- Senior officer whose strategic preference aligned with institutional and personal military interest.
- Commander associated with the delayed Khartoum relief expedition.
- Active maker of Gordon's Christian and masculine heroic memory.
