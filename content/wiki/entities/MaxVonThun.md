## Overview

Max von Thun appears as a technology-policy advocate at [[OpenMarketsInstituteEurope|Open Markets Institute Europe]] who defends European privacy, safety, interoperability, and AI rules while disputing regulation-only explanations of the region's technology gap.

## Current Profile

In the bounded [[MarketplaceTech]] interview, von Thun treats delayed European product launches as a mixture of compliance work and corporate leverage. His broader position joins [[PrecautionaryTechnologyRegulation|precautionary safeguards]] to competitive markets: significant risks should be examined before release, but regulation need not preclude innovation. He assigns substantial causal weight to Europe's language, national-market, and capital fragmentation and recommends transatlantic learning rather than isolated policy camps.

## Key Characteristics

- Defends privacy, safety, and interoperability rules as consumer protections with real short-term access costs.
- Distinguishes necessary compliance from strategic product withholding by regulated companies.
- Rejects a single-cause account in which recent regulation principally explains Europe's weak technology sector.
- Favors precaution where emerging AI harms may be severe or irreversible.
- Treats European and American technology risks as overlapping evidence bases for policy learning.
