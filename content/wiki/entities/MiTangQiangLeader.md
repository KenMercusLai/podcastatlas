## Overview

弥唐 / Mi Tang enters the wiki as the [[ShaodangQiang|Shaodang Qiang]] leader whose late Emperor-He-era pressure begins with a 97 CE attack on Longxi, then moves through surrender, unstable incorporation, renewed rebellion, defeat, relocation of surrenderers, and the collapse of his remaining group.

## Current Profile

Hanji 770 supplies the current earliest battlefield layer. Mi Tang leads more than eight thousand people into [[LongxiCommandery|Longxi]], coerces other Qiang groups, gathers a much larger foot-and-cavalry force, and defeats the local Longxi troops. [[EmperorHeOfHan|Emperor He]] sends Liu Shang as acting western campaign commander with Zhao Shi as deputy; their deployment makes Mi Tang flee, and Liu Shang defeats him after pursuit into high mountains, but heavy Han losses stop the chase before Mi Tang is captured.

Hanji 771 then shows Mi Tang again becoming afraid and asking Han forces for surrender. Wang Xin and Geng Tan accept him, and in the twelfth month Mi Tang leads his people to the capital for court audience and tribute.

Hanji 772 then says Mi Tang comes to Luoyang after earlier pressure, but his people are fewer than two thousand and hungry. He wants to move them near the capital; [[EmperorHeOfHan|Emperor He]] instead orders him back to the large and small Yu valleys. Mi Tang stalls, citing hunger, difficult roads, troop movement, and distance, while the Han frontier official Wu Zhi gives him valuables so he can buy grain and livestock and leave the frontier interior.

The settlement fails. Local Qiang groups suspect that Mi Tang's large following will cause trouble, and he rebels again, drawing Huangzhong Hu groups into raids and killings. Hanji 772 then has Zhou Wei and Hou Ba defeat his Shaodang forces; more than six thousand surrenderers are moved to Hanyang, Anding, Longxi, and related areas. Mi Tang's strength declines, he flees to the Fa Qiang, dies of illness, and his son eventually submits with only a few dozen households.

## Key Characteristics

- Shaodang Qiang leader whose current profile begins with military pressure on Longxi before later surrender.
- Battlefield actor whose force can coerce adjacent Qiang groups and defeat local Han troops before imperial counter-deployment.
- Surrender figure whose incorporation is unstable because hunger, location, and return orders are unresolved.
- Tests the limits of imperial incorporation when a frontier group cannot or will not return to its assigned zone.
- Rebellion draws in other Huangzhong Hu groups, making local settlement risk contagious.
- Defeat leads to relocation of surrenderers and the collapse of his remaining leadership line.
- Serves as the immediate prelude to Cao Feng's later resource-and-tuntian policy direction.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 770, Hanji 771, and Hanji 772. Liu Shang, Zhao Shi, Wang Xin, Geng Tan, Wu Zhi, Zhou Wei, Hou Ba, the Fa Qiang, exact Huangzhong groups, and route geography remain source-scoped here. The current evidence makes Mi Tang important as a frontier-pressure, frontier-incorporation, and policy-prelude case, not as a complete biography of Shaodang leadership.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 770 as the early attack-and-counterattack layer before Mi Tang's later surrender.
- Reframed Mi Tang's case as a three-step frontier sequence: costly military pressure, accepted surrender, then unstable return order and renewed rebellion.
- Preserved Hanji 771 and Hanji 772 as the surrender, failed incorporation, relocation, and collapse evidence set.
