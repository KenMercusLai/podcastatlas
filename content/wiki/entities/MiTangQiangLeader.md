## Overview

弥唐 / Mi Tang enters the wiki as the [[ShaodangQiang|Shaodang Qiang]] leader whose late Emperor-He-era surrender fails to become stable incorporation. Hanji 771 supplies the surrender-and-tribute prelude, while Hanji 772 follows him from Luoyang submission through refusal to return, renewed rebellion, defeat, relocation of surrenderers, and the collapse of his remaining group.

## Current Profile

Hanji 771 first shows Mi Tang again becoming afraid and asking Han forces for surrender. Wang Xin and Geng Tan accept him, and in the twelfth month Mi Tang leads his people to the capital for court audience and tribute.

Hanji 772 then says Mi Tang comes to Luoyang after earlier pressure, but his people are fewer than two thousand and hungry. He wants to move them near the capital; [[EmperorHeOfHan|Emperor He]] instead orders him back to the large and small Yu valleys. Mi Tang stalls, citing hunger, difficult roads, troop movement, and distance, while the Han frontier official Wu Zhi gives him valuables so he can buy grain and livestock and leave the frontier interior.

The settlement fails. Local Qiang groups suspect that Mi Tang's large following will cause trouble, and he rebels again, drawing Huangzhong Hu groups into raids and killings. Hanji 772 then has Zhou Wei and Hou Ba defeat his Shaodang forces; more than six thousand surrenderers are moved to Hanyang, Anding, Longxi, and related areas. Mi Tang's strength declines, he flees to the Fa Qiang, dies of illness, and his son eventually submits with only a few dozen households.

## Key Characteristics

- Shaodang Qiang leader whose surrender is unstable because hunger, location, and return orders are unresolved.
- First appears in the current sequence through a surrender accepted by Wang Xin and Geng Tan before his Luoyang audience.
- Tests the limits of imperial incorporation when a frontier group cannot or will not return to its assigned zone.
- Rebellion draws in other Huangzhong Hu groups, making local settlement risk contagious.
- Defeat leads to relocation of surrenderers and the collapse of his remaining leadership line.
- Serves as the immediate prelude to Cao Feng's later resource-and-tuntian policy direction.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 771 and Hanji 772. Wang Xin, Geng Tan, Wu Zhi, Zhou Wei, Hou Ba, the Fa Qiang, exact Huangzhong groups, and route geography remain source-scoped here. The current evidence makes Mi Tang important as a frontier-incorporation and policy-prelude case, not as a complete biography of Shaodang leadership.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 771 as the surrender-and-tribute prelude before Hanji 772's failed incorporation sequence.
- Reframed Mi Tang's case as a two-step incorporation failure: accepted surrender first, unstable return order and renewed rebellion second.
