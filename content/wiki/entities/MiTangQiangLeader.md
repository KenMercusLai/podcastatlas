## Overview

弥唐 / Mi Tang enters the wiki as the [[ShaodangQiang|Shaodang Qiang]] leader whose current profile now begins with the death of his father [[MiWuQiangLeader|Mi Wu]] after [[ZhangYuEasternHan|Zhang Yu]]'s poisoned-banquet breach. Hanji 757 shows Mi Tang responding by reconciling groups, intermarrying, exchanging hostages, and gathering a stronger rebel coalition in the large and small Yu valleys. The later arc includes threats against Hu groups, loss of adherents after [[DengXunEasternHan|Deng Xun]] protects them, an early defeat and westward displacement, [[NieShangLateHan|Nie Shang]]'s failed 92 CE conciliation attempt, [[GuanYouLateHan|Guan You]] splitting his coalition, a 97 CE Longxi attack, surrender, unstable incorporation, renewed rebellion, defeat, relocation of surrenderers, and the collapse of his remaining group.

## Current Profile

Mi Tang's current evidence begins in Hanji 757 after Zhang Yu kills Mi Wu and many Qiang leaders under banquet and surrender-contact conditions. Mi Tang, identified as Mi Wu's son, ends feuds with other groups, creates marriage alliances, exchanges hostages, and gathers in the large and small Yu valleys with enough combined strength that Zhang Yu cannot quickly subdue him. This makes Mi Tang's later profile begin as an aftereffect of trust breach and coalition consolidation, not only as a free-standing rebel line.

The next layer shows pressure against small Yuezhi and Hu groups. Mi Tang leads more than ten thousand people toward the frontier and prepares to coerce those groups into submission. Deng Xun protects the threatened Hu families, wins their allegiance, and then uses surrendered Qiang intermediaries to draw other groups away from Mi Tang. Mi Tang's uncle Hao Wu brings more than eight hundred households over to Han, and Deng defeats Mi Tang with a mixed Han, Hu, and Qiang force.

The early defeat produces a displacement pattern before the later incorporation crisis. In 89 CE, Mi Tang tries to return to his former area, but Deng sends forces across the river on leather boats and rafts, kills and captures large numbers, seizes livestock, and pushes Mi Tang's remaining people more than a thousand li westward. Smaller groups that had followed Mi Tang submit to Han, while Mi Tang retreats toward later remnant territory.

After Deng Xun dies, Nie Shang tries to summon Mi Tang back to the large and small Yu valleys through a conciliatory approach. Mi Tang sends his grandmother Bi Que, receives escort support for her return, then rebels again, kills Tian Si and the escort party, and attacks Jincheng. This makes the Nie Shang episode a post-Deng failed succession test rather than Mi Tang's first current layer.

Guan You later sends interpreters and envoys with money to split other Qiang groups from Mi Tang, then attacks in the large and small Yu valley area, kills or captures a reported 800-plus people, seizes wheat, builds fortifications, creates boats, bridges the Fengliu river crossing, and causes Mi Tang to flee farther away.

Mi Tang later leads more than eight thousand people into [[LongxiCommandery|Longxi]], coerces other Qiang groups, gathers a much larger foot-and-cavalry force, and defeats the local Longxi troops. [[EmperorHeOfHan|Emperor He]] sends Liu Shang as acting western campaign commander with Zhao Shi as deputy; their deployment makes Mi Tang flee, and Liu Shang defeats him after pursuit into high mountains, but heavy Han losses stop the chase before Mi Tang is captured.

Mi Tang again becomes afraid and asks Han forces for surrender. Wang Xin and Geng Tan accept him, and in the twelfth month Mi Tang leads his people to the capital for court audience and tribute.

Mi Tang comes to Luoyang after earlier pressure, but his people are fewer than two thousand and hungry. He wants to move them near the capital; [[EmperorHeOfHan|Emperor He]] instead orders him back to the large and small Yu valleys. Mi Tang stalls, citing hunger, difficult roads, troop movement, and distance, while the Han frontier official Wu Zhi gives him valuables so he can buy grain and livestock and leave the frontier interior.

The settlement fails. Local Qiang groups suspect that Mi Tang's large following will cause trouble, and he rebels again, drawing Huangzhong Hu groups into raids and killings. Zhou Wei and Hou Ba defeat his Shaodang forces; more than six thousand surrenderers are moved to Hanyang, Anding, Longxi, and related areas. Mi Tang's strength declines, he flees to the Fa Qiang, dies of illness, and his son eventually submits with only a few dozen households.

## Key Characteristics

- Shaodang Qiang leader whose current profile begins after his father Mi Wu is killed under surrender-contact conditions.
- Coalition builder who turns reconciliation, marriage ties, and hostage exchange into renewed rebel strength.
- Frontier coercer whose later threats let Deng convert protection into allegiance against him.
- Battlefield actor whose force can coerce adjacent Qiang groups and defeat local Han troops before imperial counter-deployment.
- Surrender figure whose incorporation is unstable because hunger, location, and return orders are unresolved.
- Tests the limits of imperial incorporation when a frontier group cannot or will not return to its assigned zone.
- Rebellion draws in other Huangzhong Hu groups, making local settlement risk contagious.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 757, Hanji 760, Hanji 767, Hanji 768, Hanji 770, Hanji 771, and Hanji 772. Hanji 757 gives the origin layer after Mi Wu's death and uses the source spelling 迷雾 for Mi Wu. Deng Xun, Nie Shang, Guan You, Liu Shang, Zhao Shi, Wang Xin, Geng Tan, Wu Zhi, Zhou Wei, Hou Ba, the Fa Qiang, exact Huangzhong groups, and route geography remain source-scoped here except where they have standalone pages. Hao Wu, Bi Que, and Tian Si remain source-scoped figures unless additional notes make them durable. The current evidence makes Mi Tang important as a frontier trust, frontier-pressure, frontier-incorporation, and policy-prelude case, not as a complete biography of Shaodang leadership.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 757 as Mi Tang's origin layer: son of Mi Wu, coalition builder after Zhang Yu's surrender-trust breach.
- Moved the starting point earlier to include Hu coercion, Deng Xun's protective counterstrategy, Hao Wu's submission, early defeat, and westward displacement.
- Repositioned the Nie Shang episode as the post-Deng failed-conciliation layer rather than the starting point.
- Preserved the Guan You coalition-fracture layer and the attack, surrender, failed incorporation, relocation, and collapse evidence set.
