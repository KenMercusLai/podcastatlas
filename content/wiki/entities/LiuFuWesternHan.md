## Overview

刘辅 / Liu Fu enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-534-zhichang-li-weishenme-yao-tuichong-zhanan-lpaup86hls0kp7hc3vd2msjfuyw3|Hanji 534]] as a Western Han remonstrant under [[HanChengdi|汉成帝]]. After Chengdi elevates [[ZhaoFeiyanWesternHan|赵飞燕]] and favors the Zhao sisters, Liu Fu submits a fierce memorial against imperial indulgence and is secretly imprisoned before senior officials force a more public handling.

## Current Profile

Liu Fu's source role is not a full biography but a feedback-channel stress test. The episode says he had received Chengdi's trust and served as Jian Dafu, then used that duty to criticize the ruler's pleasure, female favor, low-born empress elevation, and failure of open speech at court.

The punishment sequence matters more than the wording alone. Chengdi reacts with anger and places Liu Fu in a secret palace prison. [[XinQingjiWesternHan|辛庆忌]], Lian Bao, [[ShiDanWesternHan|施丹]], [[GuYongWesternHan|谷永]], and others do not repeat the insulting content directly; instead, they ask Chengdi to make the charge public and transfer the case to ordinary office procedure if a real crime exists. Chengdi releases Liu Fu from the secret prison and spares his life, but still sentences him to three years of labor.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han Jian Dafu whose office made direct correction part of his public role.
- Critic of Chengdi's indulgence, Zhao-sister favor, and disorderly elevation of low-origin palace women.
- Secretly imprisoned after his memorial touches the ruler's private desire and political face.
- Saved from death by collective official pressure for public, procedural handling.
- Case shows that partial rescue can still chill later remonstrance when punishment remains.
