## Overview

魏文侯 / Marquis Wen of Wei, identified as Wei Si, is the early [[WeiState|魏国]] ruler whose recognition by [[ZhouWeilieWang|周威烈王]] places him in the [[ThreeJinVassalRecognition|三晋受封]] settlement. The bounded evidence presents him as a builder of credibility and talent networks whose strengths coexist with anger at criticism, family-centered frontier control, and caution toward subordinates whose service becomes politically or morally threatening.

## Current Profile

Wei Wenhou makes relationships usable without dissolving hierarchy. He honors [[ZiXia|子夏]], [[TianZifang|田子方]], and [[DuanGanmu|段干木]] as teachers; keeps small promises and applies the same rationale to Han and Zhao; asks [[LiKe|李克]] to compare chancellor candidates and evaluate [[WuQi|吴起]]; and lets specialists operate while retaining appointment and review authority.

The Zhongshan cluster makes that profile less idealized. Wei Wenhou sustains [[LeYang|乐羊]] through a long campaign, plans to assign the detached conquest to his son, becomes angry when [[RenZuoWarringStates|任作]] calls the choice unworthy of a humane ruler, and then accepts [[DiHuang|翟黄]]'s face-saving route to recall the critic. He can therefore reopen a damaged feedback channel without conceding that the policy changes. After victory he shows Le Yang the hostile memorials to restore hierarchy, then rewards the commander's achievement while withdrawing trust because the son's killing and broth episode make Le Yang's lack of visible restraint appear dangerous.

## Key Characteristics

- Builds authority through credible small commitments and symmetrical interstate conduct.
- Treats eminent scholars as teachers, making respectful access part of Wei's talent attraction.
- Uses comparative personnel judgment and role-fit employment of flawed specialists.
- Can react angrily to direct criticism but accept an intermediary's face-preserving route back to dialogue.
- Protects a commander through a long campaign, then separates military reward from future trust.
- Distinguishes ruler accountability from specialist execution while preserving hierarchy after success.

## Qualifications

The corpus combines annalistic notices, supplementary stories, and modern management readings from podcast sources. Zhouji 05 part 2 does not establish whether Ren Zuo's criticism changes the appointment, whether family assignment reflects strategy or favoritism, or whether Le Yang's broth episode and resulting distrust are literal, complete, or independently corroborated. Zhouji 10 part 4 keeps the future Wei Wuhou identification and restoration story source-scoped, while Zhouji 05 part 3's exact memorials, grant terms, dialogue, and Zhongshan appointments are also unverified here. The current profile is therefore a bounded synthesis rather than a complete reign history or proof of private motive.

## What Changed

- Added the Ren Zuo—Di Huang episode as a case of anger followed by face-preserving feedback repair.
- Added the strategic-versus-favoritist ambiguity around assigning Zhongshan to the ruler's son.
- Distinguished rewarding Le Yang's achievement from trusting his future discretion.
- Qualified the earlier exemplary ruler profile with evidence about emotion, family control, and threat perception.

## Relationships

- [[WeiState|魏国]] - polity whose early authority and talent system Wei Wenhou develops.
- [[RenZuoWarringStates|任作]] - direct critic whom Wei Wenhou first rejects and then recalls.
- [[DiHuang|翟黄]] - adviser who repairs the ruler-critic relationship without erasing the criticism.
- [[LeYang|乐羊]] - commander protected during the campaign, contained after victory, and later rewarded without trust.
- [[WeiWuhou|魏武侯]] - son, Zhongshan appointee in the episode cluster, and eventual successor.
- [[FaceSavingRemonstrance|留面子式进谏包装]] - describes the route by which Wei Wenhou reopens the feedback channel.
- [[ExtremeLoyaltyThreatParadox|极端忠诚的威胁反转]] - describes why Le Yang's sacrifice weakens rather than deepens trust.
