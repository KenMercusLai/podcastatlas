## Overview

Shanghai Pudong Development Bank / 浦发银行 appears through two distinct inherited systems: legacy bank wealth-management risk and stewardship of the former Shanghai HSBC headquarters building.

## Current Profile

Pufa Wealth is a cautionary case for assets, practices, and risk cultures carried from old bank wealth management into the subsidiary era. The point is structural: separate legal entities did not begin as clean asset managers when they inherited non-standard, stock-pledge, or equity-like exposures.

The bank also has an architectural inheritance. After it obtained use of the former HSBC building, the protected original [[HSBCLions|Shanghai HSBC lions]] could not simply return as ordinary decoration; replicas were made while the originals remained museum-held cultural property. The case distinguishes current building use from ownership of the original corporate symbol.

## Key Characteristics

- Parent institution linked to the source's legacy-risk account of Pufa Wealth.
- Illustrates how bank wealth-management subsidiaries can inherit old assets and risk cultures.
- Current user of the former Shanghai HSBC headquarters in the episode's architectural history.
- Displays replicas rather than the protected original HSBC bronze lions.

## Qualifications

The wealth-management source offers a risk-mechanism case, not a current investment judgment on the bank. The architecture source does not establish ownership terms, conservation law, replica specifications, or the bank's intent in displaying the lions. Use of an inherited building and copied sculpture should not be confused with origin of the HSBC brand symbol.

## What Changed

- Added the former HSBC headquarters and replica lions as an architectural-stewardship layer.
- Distinguished present use of the symbol from ownership and authorship of the originals.
