# Alexander Haig

## Overview

Alexander “Al” Haig was the United States secretary of state who conducted shuttle diplomacy between London, Washington, and Buenos Aires during the opening [[FalklandsWar|Falklands crisis]].

## Current Profile

Haig's mission translated a divided Reagan administration into a search for an allied compromise. His proposal called for mutual withdrawal, temporary international administration, and later sovereignty negotiations. According to the episode, [[MargaretThatcher|Margaret Thatcher]] told Cabinet that Britain had to accept the plan even though it might ultimately have favored Argentine sovereignty.

The proposal failed when Argentina's junta rejected withdrawal. The case therefore presents mediation as constrained by domestic legitimacy: an arrangement that could bridge formal interstate positions could still be unacceptable to a regime that had already celebrated occupation as a nationalist triumph.

## Key Characteristics

- U.S. secretary of state during the 1982 crisis.
- Mediator between a NATO ally and an anti-communist Latin American partner.
- Used shuttle diplomacy to test compromise before major combat.
- Proposed mutual withdrawal and temporary international administration.
- Could not overcome the junta's political cost of retreat.

## Qualifications

This profile relies on one British retrospective account and does not reconstruct every draft, meeting, U.S. bureaucratic dispute, Argentine counterproposal, or legal implication. The claim that the plan probably led toward Argentine sovereignty is source interpretation rather than an established outcome.
