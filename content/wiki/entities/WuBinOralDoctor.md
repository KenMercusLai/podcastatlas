# 吴斌 / Wu Bin (口腔科)

## Overview
吴斌 is an oral-health clinician in three [[ZheBingShuoLaiHuaChang|这病说来话长]] sources, explaining orthodontic continuity, jaw dislocation caused by excessive mouth opening, and older-adult oral function and care.

## Current Profile
In the current source, 吴斌 connects laughing, eating, yawning, sneezing, and prolonged mouth opening during dental care to temporomandibular-joint dislocation. His practical emphasis is prompt oral emergency assessment when the jaw cannot return, followed by soft food and restricted opening during recovery, with extra caution after a prior dislocation or when joint clicking and pain are already present.

VOL.99 broadens his profile from acute jaw mechanics to older-adult oral function and daily care. Missing teeth and poorly functioning dentures can affect chewing and food choice; gentle temperature and texture, interdental cleaning, brushing, and correct denture cleaning support comfort and nutrition, while sharp bones or pits and using teeth as tools create avoidable injury risk.

VOL.56 adds orthodontic planning. Wu treats fixed braces and clear aligners as tools whose outcome depends on diagnosis, periodontal and bite context, clinician competence, patient cooperation, repeated review, retention, and geographic continuity. He also keeps extraction, skeletal malocclusion, and orthognathic surgery inside examination and imaging rather than answering a listener case remotely.

## Key Characteristics
- Source-scoped oral-health and oral-surgery educator.
- Explains jaw dislocation through mouth-opening mechanics and prior joint vulnerability.
- Distinguishes prevention and aftercare from self-reduction.
- Emphasizes recurrence risk after an earlier dislocation.
- Connects older-adult oral function, denture care, food texture, and daily hygiene with safer eating.
- Connects orthodontic appliance choice to multi-year review, retention, relocation planning, and possible jaw-surgery coordination.

## Qualifications
The sources do not independently verify credentials, institutional affiliation, examination findings, or complete orthodontic, dental, temporomandibular, or orthognathic treatment pathways. Prices, duration, review frequency, extraction, appliance choice, and surgical need remain source-scoped. The advice is public education, not individualized dentistry, orthodontics, prosthodontics, oral surgery, or nutrition care.
