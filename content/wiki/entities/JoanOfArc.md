# Joan of Arc

## Overview

Joan of Arc is the French military-religious figure whose intervention restored momentum to Charles VII before Burgundian forces captured her and transferred her to the English.

## Current Profile

The episode places Joan inside Burgundy's shifting position in the [[HundredYearsWar|Hundred Years' War]]. Her arrival at Chinon, role in relieving Orléans, and support for Charles VII's coronation challenged the Anglo-Burgundian settlement created after the [[TreatyOfTroyes|Treaty of Troyes]].

Burgundian troops captured her near Compiègne. [[PhilipTheGood|Philip the Good]] later transferred her to the English, whose execution of her is interpreted as an attempt to damage Charles VII's sacred and political legitimacy.

## Key Characteristics

- Religious and military supporter of Charles VII.
- Figure associated with the relief of Orléans and royal coronation.
- Challenge to the legitimacy of the Anglo-Burgundian settlement.
- Captive of Burgundian troops near Compiègne.
- Prisoner transferred to the English and executed for political as well as religious reasons.

## Qualifications

The chroniclers cited in the episode do not record what passed between Joan and Philip. Van Loo's suggestion that she confronted him is therefore speculation, while the episode's economic explanation for her transfer should not be treated as Philip's only possible motive.
