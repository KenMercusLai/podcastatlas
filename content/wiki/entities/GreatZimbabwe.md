# Great Zimbabwe

## Overview

Great Zimbabwe is a monumental African-built city whose ruins became entangled with European fantasies about Phoenicians, biblical treasure, and lost civilizations.

## Current Profile

The episode uses Great Zimbabwe as an evidentiary boundary between African history and colonial mythmaking. European observers including Carl Mauch denied or discounted African authorship and attributed the city to Phoenicians or other outsiders. [[HRiderHaggard|H. Rider Haggard]] accepted and promoted that interpretation.

Within the episode's literary argument, the false origin theory helped Victorian readers connect African grandeur to Solomon, Ophir, Sheba, and a supposedly familiar ancient world. The case therefore shows how archaeological wonder could be converted into racial denial and then into [[LostWorldFiction|lost-world fiction]].

## Key Characteristics

- Monumental African urban and architectural achievement.
- Target of European theories denying African construction.
- Attached to Phoenician, biblical, and Solomonic speculation in Victorian culture.
- Real site converted into imaginative evidence for fictional lost civilizations.

## Evidence

- African authorship and dating: [[332-king-solomons-mines-glt4960691587]] describes Great Zimbabwe as an African-built ruined city dating from the medieval period.
- Colonial misattribution: [[332-king-solomons-mines-glt4960691587]] records Mauch's and Haggard's Phoenician-origin belief and interprets it as racist denial.
- Literary reuse: [[332-king-solomons-mines-glt4960691587]] connects the site to biblical geography and Haggard's hidden-civilization imagination.

## Qualifications

This page records the episode's high-level account, not a full archaeological chronology of Great Zimbabwe. Specific ninth-century dating, Mauch's reasoning, and Haggard's later advocacy remain source-scoped here; the settled point in this source is that outsider-origin theories wrongly denied African construction.

## What Changed

- Created a profile separating African construction from colonial lost-civilization claims.
