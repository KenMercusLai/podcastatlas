# Louis Edward Nolan

## Overview

Louis Edward Nolan was a British cavalry officer, author, horse trainer, and member of [[FitzRoySomerset1stBaronRaglan|Lord Raglan]]'s staff during the [[CrimeanWar|Crimean War]].

## Current Profile

[[723-the-charge-of-the-light-brigade-crushing-the-russians-part-3-glt9985161615]] presents Nolan as a technically serious cavalry reformer whose Austrian service, European study, writing, saddle design, and humane approach to horses gave substance to his advocacy of decisive mounted action. He believed well-handled cavalry could attack infantry and artillery rather than remain an ornamental or purely screening arm.

That expertise also became a source of pressure and conflict. Nolan condemned the failure to pursue the Russians after the Alma, despised Raglan's passivity, and treated [[GeorgeBingham3rdEarlOfLucan|Lucan]] and [[JamesBrudenell7thEarlOfCardigan|Cardigan]] as products of incompetent purchased command. Because he could not openly attack Raglan, criticism focused on Lucan and was amplified through press reporting, sharpening the reputational environment before Balaclava.

## Key Characteristics

- Acquired cavalry experience in Austrian service and studied mounted forces across Europe and Russia.
- Wrote influentially on horse training and cavalry tactics.
- Advocated kinder horse treatment and designed an improved saddle.
- Believed disciplined cavalry could attack infantry and artillery decisively.
- Condemned Allied failure to pursue after the Alma.
- Opposed aristocratic and purchase-based cavalry leadership.
- Directed public criticism toward Lucan while serving on Raglan's staff.
