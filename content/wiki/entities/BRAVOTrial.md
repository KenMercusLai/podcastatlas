# BRAVO Trial

## Overview
The BRAVO Trial is the speech-neuroprosthetics clinical trial discussed by [[EddieChang]] in the Huberman Lab Essentials source, focused on decoding attempted speech from brain activity for people who cannot speak.

## Current Profile
In the source, the BRAVO Trial anchors [[SpeechNeuroprosthetics]] in a concrete clinical case. The first participant, Poncho, had long-term paralysis after a brainstem stroke and communicated through slow letter selection using limited neck movement. Chang describes implanted electrodes over speech-motor areas for the larynx, lips, tongue, jaw, and related articulators, with analog neural signals converted into digital signals and decoded by machine-learning systems.

The trial profile is promising but bounded. The early system used a small vocabulary, required weeks of training, relied on computational modeling of likely word combinations, and was not perfectly accurate. Its importance in the source is that attempted speech could be translated into words and sentences for a paralyzed person, while its limits show why restoration remains a clinical engineering problem rather than magic mind reading.

## Key Characteristics
- Tests speech decoding from cortical activity during attempted speech.
- Uses implanted electrodes over speech-related motor cortex rather than external-only sensing.
- Combines machine-learning signal decoding with context-based language correction.
- Begins with constrained vocabulary and repeated training rather than open-ended fluent conversation.
- Demonstrates communication-restoration potential while preserving hardware, accuracy, and clinical-boundary limits.

## Evidence
- Trial purpose: [[essentials-the-science-of-learning-speaking-languages-dr-eddie-chang-scim7185728420]] says Chang started BRAVO to test speech decoding from brain activity.
- Participant case: [[essentials-the-science-of-learning-speaking-languages-dr-eddie-chang-scim7185728420]] describes Poncho as paralyzed for 15 years after a brainstem stroke, with intact communication intent but no intelligible speech.
- Implant and decoding setup: [[essentials-the-science-of-learning-speaking-languages-dr-eddie-chang-scim7185728420]] describes electrodes over larynx, lips, tongue, jaw, and other speech-movement areas, connected through a skull port and processed with machine-learning algorithms.
- System boundary: [[essentials-the-science-of-learning-speaking-languages-dr-eddie-chang-scim7185728420]] reports a 50-word early vocabulary, weeks of training, context-based sentence modeling, imperfect accuracy, and interference from embodied behavior such as giggling.
