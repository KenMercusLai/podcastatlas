# BRAVO Trial

## Overview
The BRAVO Trial is the speech-neuroprosthetics clinical trial discussed by [[EddieChang]] in the full and Essentials Huberman Lab sources, focused on decoding attempted speech from brain activity for people who cannot speak.

## Current Profile
The BRAVO Trial anchors [[SpeechNeuroprosthetics]] in a concrete clinical case. The first participant, Pancho, had long-term paralysis after a brainstem stroke and communicated through slow letter selection using limited neck movement. Chang describes implanted electrodes over speech-motor areas for the larynx, lips, tongue, jaw, and related articulators, with neural signals decoded by machine-learning systems.

The trial profile is promising but bounded. The early system used a 50-word vocabulary, required weeks of training, relied on computational modeling of likely word combinations, and was not perfectly accurate. A wired skull port carried infection risk, and embodied behavior such as giggling could disrupt decoding. Its importance is proof of principle that attempted speech can become words and sentences, not evidence of an approved, wireless, open-vocabulary therapy.

## Key Characteristics
- Tests speech decoding from cortical activity during attempted speech.
- Uses implanted electrodes over speech-related motor cortex rather than external-only sensing.
- Combines machine-learning signal decoding with context-based language correction.
- Begins with constrained vocabulary and repeated training rather than open-ended fluent conversation.
- Demonstrates communication-restoration potential while preserving hardware, infection, accuracy, and clinical-boundary limits.

- Trial purpose - [[essentials-the-science-of-learning-speaking-languages-dr-eddie-chang-scim7185728420]] and [[the-science-of-learning-speaking-languages-dr-eddie-chang-scim2227065926]] say Chang's team started BRAVO to test speech decoding from brain activity.
- Participant case - [[essentials-the-science-of-learning-speaking-languages-dr-eddie-chang-scim7185728420]] and [[the-science-of-learning-speaking-languages-dr-eddie-chang-scim2227065926]] describe Pancho as paralyzed for about 15 years after a brainstem stroke, with intact communication intent but no intelligible speech.
- Implant and decoding setup - [[essentials-the-science-of-learning-speaking-languages-dr-eddie-chang-scim7185728420]] and [[the-science-of-learning-speaking-languages-dr-eddie-chang-scim2227065926]] describe electrodes over larynx, lips, tongue, jaw, and other speech-movement areas, connected through a skull port and processed with machine-learning algorithms.
- System boundary - both sources report a 50-word early vocabulary, weeks of training, context-based sentence modeling, imperfect accuracy, and giggling-related interference; [[the-science-of-learning-speaking-languages-dr-eddie-chang-scim2227065926]] also makes the wired-port infection risk explicit.
