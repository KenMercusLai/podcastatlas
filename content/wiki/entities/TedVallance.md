# Ted Vallance

## Overview

Ted Vallance is a historian of British political, religious, and constitutional culture interviewed by [[TheRestIsHistory|The Rest Is History]] about [[MagnaCarta|Magna Carta]] and the trial, execution, and political afterlife of [[CharlesIOfEngland|Charles I]].

## Current Profile

Vallance's contribution is source-critical, constitutional, and attentive to contingency. He connects Charles's policies and character to religious and fiscal distrust, then treats the Second Civil War as the decisive event hardening army opinion toward prosecution. He explains how Putney radicalization, the Remonstrance, Pride's Purge, and the Commons' sovereignty declaration created a tribunal whose authority remained inseparable from military coercion.

He treats the trial as legally extraordinary without reducing it to empty theatre, explains the prosecution's reliance on laws-of-war and command-responsibility arguments, and keeps open a narrow range of counterfactual outcomes while emphasizing army pressure for execution.

He also follows constitutional objects and political violence beyond their immediate crises. His account connects Magna Carta's elite origins and failed 1215 enforcement to reissue, legal circulation, rights-centered reinterpretation, and modern executive-power debate; his account of regicide connects royalist martyrdom, republican celebration, Restoration revenge, and later revolutionary memory. In both cases, he distinguishes real institutional inheritance from retrospective myth without dismissing the political force of either.

## Key Characteristics

- Historian of early modern British political and religious culture.
- Connects religious conflict, royal conduct, military bargaining, and institutional rupture rather than giving the Civil War one cause.
- Interprets Charles I's trial through law, war, sovereignty, public performance, and memory.
- Interprets Magna Carta through fiscal conflict, legal restraint, reissue, and expanding political use.
- Distinguishes strong historical inference from weak anecdote or interested post-Restoration testimony.
- Treats constitutional memory as a mixture of genuine continuity, selective interpretation, and politically effective myth.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to three podcast episodes rather than Vallance's complete scholarship. Causal weighting among religion, constitution, personality, taxation, and war; the army's or barons' representativeness; legal characterization; counterfactual settlements; audience reaction; the widening of medieval rights language; and long-run influence claims remain concise public-history interpretations rather than full historiographical demonstrations.

## What Changed

- Extended the profile from regicide to Magna Carta, showing a consistent method for separating constitutional inheritance from later myth while taking both seriously.
