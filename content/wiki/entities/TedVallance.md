# Ted Vallance

## Overview

Ted Vallance is the historian interviewed by [[TheRestIsHistory|The Rest Is History]] about the trial, execution, and political afterlife of [[CharlesIOfEngland|Charles I]].

## Current Profile

Vallance's contribution is source-critical and constitutional. He treats the trial as legally extraordinary without reducing it to empty theatre, explains the prosecution's reliance on laws-of-war and command-responsibility arguments, and keeps open a narrow range of counterfactual outcomes while emphasizing army pressure for execution.

He also follows regicide beyond the scaffold. His account connects royalist martyrdom, republican celebration, Restoration revenge, and later revolutionary memory, while qualifying colorful stories whose surviving tellers had reasons to minimize their responsibility.

## Key Characteristics

- Historian of early modern British political and religious culture.
- Interprets Charles I's trial through law, war, sovereignty, public performance, and memory.
- Distinguishes strong historical inference from weak anecdote or interested post-Restoration testimony.
- Treats regicide as consequential for both royalist and republican traditions.

## Evidence

- Legal interpretation: [[144-the-trial-of-charles-i-part-2-glt2968292003]] presents Vallance's account of the court's weak ordinary-law basis and its use of war and command reasoning.
- Source criticism: [[144-the-trial-of-charles-i-part-2-glt2968292003]] records his rejection of the bulletproof-hat story and caution about later coercion claims by death-warrant signers.
- Political afterlife: [[144-the-trial-of-charles-i-part-2-glt2968292003]] connects his analysis to martyrdom, republican memory, Restoration punishment, and later revolutions.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to one podcast interview rather than Vallance's complete scholarship. The episode's counterfactuals, audience estimates, legal characterization, crowd reaction, and long-run influence claims remain concise public-history interpretations rather than full historiographical demonstrations.
