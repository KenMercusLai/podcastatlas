# Elizabeth “Tetty” Porter

## Overview

Elizabeth “Tetty” Porter was the widowed woman who married [[SamuelJohnson]] in 1735 and provided affection, confidence, and some of the capital behind his unsuccessful school.

## Current Profile

The episode introduces Porter when Johnson was poor, professionally adrift, and socially disadvantaged by illness and appearance. Twenty years older than him, she judged him the most sensible man she had met. Their marriage gave Johnson emotional security and access to money, but the school funded from her resources attracted only three pupils and failed.

Porter disliked London, so Johnson’s later literary career divided their domestic life: she lived outside or at the edge of the city while he spent much of his limited income supporting her and lived precariously nearer print work. Johnson remained devoted to “Tetty,” and her death in 1752 intensified the loneliness that he managed through friendship and conversation.

## Key Characteristics

- Older widow who chose Johnson during his period of poverty and failed prospects.
- Spouse whose affection and financial resources enabled a risky school venture.
- Partner physically separated from much of Johnson’s London working life.
- Important source of emotional security whose death deepened Johnson’s loneliness.

## Qualifications

The current profile comes from one popular-history episode centered on Johnson. Porter’s perspective, finances, prior marriage, health, domestic choices, and independent life receive little attention. Her reported words, Johnson’s emotions, and the causes of their living arrangements remain mediated through retrospective biography and host interpretation.

- Connected the marriage-funded school to Johnson’s enduring relationship with David Garrick.
