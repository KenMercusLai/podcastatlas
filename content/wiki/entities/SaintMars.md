# Bénigne Dauvergne de Saint-Mars

## Overview

Saint-Mars was the royal jailer whose successive commands connect the surviving records of the [[ManInTheIronMask]].

## Current Profile

[[411-the-man-in-the-iron-mask-glt3787137343]] presents Saint-Mars as a trusted custodian within [[LouisXIV]]'s prison system. He commanded Pignerol, Exilés, Sainte-Marguerite, and eventually the Bastille, carrying an unnamed or closely guarded prisoner through the same sequence. His precautions preserved custody but also made outsiders guess that the prisoner's identity must be extraordinary.

## Key Characteristics

- Trusted royal jailer assigned to sensitive state prisoners.
- Administrative link across four prisons and more than three decades.
- Participant in an unusually short command chain joining prisoner, jailer, minister, and king.
- Security practitioner whose concealment unintentionally increased public speculation.

## Evidence

### Custodial continuity

- [[411-the-man-in-the-iron-mask-glt3787137343]] connects Saint-Mars to Pignerol, Exilés, Sainte-Marguerite, the Bastille, and all three contemporary masked-prisoner notices.

### Myth-generating secrecy

- [[411-the-man-in-the-iron-mask-glt3787137343]] reports Saint-Mars recognizing that his method of transport and guarding encouraged observers to guess the prisoner's identity.

## Qualifications

- Custodial continuity helps identify the prisoner but does not disclose the original offense.
- The source does not establish that Saint-Mars intended to create a legend or independently designed the secrecy regime.

## What Changed

- Establishes Saint-Mars as both the archival continuity point and an unintended mechanism of myth production.
