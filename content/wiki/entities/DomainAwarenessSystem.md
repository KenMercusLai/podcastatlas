# Domain Awareness System

## Overview

The Domain Awareness System is the [[NewYorkCityPoliceDepartment|NYPD]] platform that combines surveillance feeds and operational records into a searchable investigative interface.

## Current Profile

The source presents the system as the culmination of a longer integration process rather than a single camera network. A 2005 real-time crime center centralized records, a 2008 Lower Manhattan initiative added police and private camera feeds, and four years of NYPD work with [[Microsoft]] produced the Domain Awareness System launched in 2012. By 2016, the episode says the system contained billions of plate reads, tens of millions of calls and complaints, millions of arrest records, and rolling footage from thousands of cameras.

Its practical value is speed: investigators can connect footage, vehicles, identities, and records in minutes rather than assembling them over days. That same integration creates a civil-liberties problem because ordinary municipal and commercial data become searchable together, warrant requirements are disputed, retention and access boundaries matter, and AI can make much larger footage archives operationally searchable.

## Key Characteristics

- Integrates cameras, plate reads, emergency calls, complaints, arrest records, and other databases.
- Grew from earlier real-time crime-center and public-private camera initiatives.
- Was co-developed by the NYPD and Microsoft and formally launched in 2012.
- Supports large-scale daily use and mobile access with differentiated permissions.
- Converts distributed public and private observations into searchable movement and identity context.
- Makes retention, permissible purpose, auditing, procurement disclosure, and AI analysis central governance questions.

## Qualifications

The episode provides dated scale snapshots rather than a current system inventory. It does not independently test search controls, map every integrated database, measure false matches or misuse, or isolate the system's effect on crime and terrorism. Statements about safety and permissible access are department claims preserved as such.

## What Changed

- Created a focused profile separating the integrated platform from the NYPD as an institution.
