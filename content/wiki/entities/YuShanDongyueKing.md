## Overview

余善 / Yu Shan is the Dongyue ruler whose killing of Han officials and self-proclamation as “武帝” trigger a multi-route campaign ordered by [[HanWudi|汉武帝]], and whose rule ends through internal killing and surrender under Han pressure.

## Current Profile

Hanji 356-1 introduces Yu Shan as the rebel target after the Nanyue campaign. It says he kills Han officials, assumes an imperial title, and draws a multi-route force that includes [[YangPuWesternHan|楼船将军杨仆]]. That source ends at mobilization and does not establish his fate.

Hanji 356-2 supplies the outcome. Yu Shan is presented as cunning and already occupying difficult terrain, but external pressure opens an internal fracture: [[WuYangYueMarquis|吴阳]] defects with followers, and [[JianchengHouAo|建成侯敖]] joins [[JuguDongyueKing|繇王居股]] in killing Yu Shan before surrendering. His death is therefore synthesized as conquest through military pressure plus elite defection, not as proof that terrain or direct battle alone decided the campaign.

## Key Characteristics

- Dongyue ruler in open rebellion against Western Han authority.
- Challenger who is said to kill Han officials and adopt the title “武帝.”
- Campaign target of a multi-route Han mobilization that includes Yang Pu.
- Ruler whose difficult terrain does not prevent internal defections under outside pressure.
- Killed by Dongyue insiders who then surrender to Han.

- Campaign response: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-356-1-budong-yuren-ni-ping-sha-dang-lingdao-lsaa6j1jskivfhyvvpbpmnorq-jm|Hanji 356-1]] places him against forces dispatched by Han Wudi from several routes.
- Yang Pu connection: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-356-1-budong-yuren-ni-ping-sha-dang-lingdao-lsaa6j1jskivfhyvvpbpmnorq-jm|Hanji 356-1]] makes the Dongyue campaign Yang Pu's opportunity to redeem alleged faults after Nanyue.
- Defensive position and fracture: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-356-2-lishishang-dongyuewang-zheyang-si-qu-duo-biequ-lhwrrqtaejqcradjo-2ipvslml5k|Hanji 356-2]] says Yu Shan holds difficult ground while defections develop inside Dongyue.
- Death and surrender: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-356-2-lishishang-dongyuewang-zheyang-si-qu-duo-biequ-lhwrrqtaejqcradjo-2ipvslml5k|Hanji 356-2]] attributes his killing to Ao and Jugu, who then bring their followers over to Han.

## Qualifications

Both supplied transcripts corrupt the name, using “于善” or “洛于善”; this page preserves the established 余善 identity. The sources do not provide a full accession, family, or administrative biography. Hanji 356-2's claim that he killed an elder brother and its descriptions of his cunning remain episode-attributed, while the exact battlefield chronology and the private motives of the defectors are not established.

## What Changed

- Added the campaign outcome: internal defectors kill Yu Shan and surrender under Han pressure.
- Replaced the earlier open-ended endpoint with a qualified account of terrain, fracture, and death.
