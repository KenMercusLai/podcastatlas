# Storming of the Bastille

## Overview

The storming of the Bastille on 14 July 1789 was an armed seizure of a royal fortress and its gunpowder that became a foundational symbol of the [[FrenchRevolution|French Revolution]].

## Current Profile

The source presents the event as the endpoint of an escalating Paris crisis rather than a planned symbolic assault. Hunger, troop fear, [[JacquesNecker|Necker]]'s dismissal, [[CamilleDesmoulins|Desmoulins]]' mobilization, arms seizures, and military defection had already broken royal control. Attackers came for gunpowder after taking muskets at Les Invalides; negotiation and miscommunication then gave way to gunfire, cannon, surrender, and the killing of [[BernardReneDeLaunay|de Launay]]. The prison's seven inmates and mixed conditions qualify its later image, but lettres de cachet made it a credible emblem of arbitrary monarchy. [[BastilleRevolutionaryMythmaking|Mythmaking]] and [[RevolutionaryCrowdVengeance|vengeance]] therefore belong to the event's immediate history, not merely its later commemoration.

## Key Characteristics

- Practical seizure of gunpowder after a wider Parisian search for arms.
- Military confrontation enabled by royal withdrawal and Gardes Francaises defection.
- Negotiated crisis that escalated through impatience, confusion, and shooting.
- Symbolic attack on arbitrary detention and royal authority.
- Liberation memory disproportionate to the seven prisoners actually found.
- Foundational victory inseparable from lynching, decapitation, and public display.

- [[479-the-french-revolution-the-storming-of-the-bastille-part-5-glt5485693441]] connects Necker's dismissal, Parisian arming, Les Invalides, gunpowder demand, failed negotiation, cannon, and surrender.

### Prison reality and symbolic afterlife

- [[479-the-french-revolution-the-storming-of-the-bastille-part-5-glt5485693441]] contrasts seven prisoners and mixed conditions with arbitrary imprisonment, Gothic imagery, demolition, relics, and souvenirs.

### Violence after victory

- [[479-the-french-revolution-the-storming-of-the-bastille-part-5-glt5485693441]] describes the killings of de Launay and Flesselle and the display of their heads.

## Qualifications

- The event was both strategically useful and symbolically charged; neither frame alone explains it.
- Qualifying later prison imagery does not make imprisonment by lettre de cachet politically harmless.
- The source does not settle first-fire responsibility, exact crowd totals, or every participant's motive.
- Immediate violence does not prove that the later Terror was inevitable.

## What Changed

- Establishes the Bastille as a practical arms operation, a regime-breaking event, a manufactured memory object, and an early site of revolutionary vengeance at once.
