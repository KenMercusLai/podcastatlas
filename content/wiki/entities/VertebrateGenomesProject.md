# Vertebrate Genomes Project

## Overview
The Vertebrate Genomes Project is a collaborative genomics effort presented by [[ErichJarvis]] as infrastructure for producing high-quality reference genomes across vertebrate species.

## Current Profile
In the source, the project addresses a methodological bottleneck: incomplete or error-prone assemblies can create false duplications, omit difficult regulatory regions, and distort phylogenetic comparison. Better assemblies support [[ComparativeGenomicsTraitInference]] by making cross-species differences more trustworthy and connect trait research to broader conservation work through [[HighQualityGenomeInfrastructure]].

The interview locates the project within a larger consortium landscape that includes the Earth BioGenome Project and [[GenomeArk]]. It does not provide a complete institutional history, governance map, current species count, or technical production standard.

## Key Characteristics
- Coordinates high-quality reference-genome production across vertebrate species.
- Treats assembly completeness and accuracy as prerequisites for reliable comparison.
- Supports phylogeny, trait evolution, brain and vocal-learning research, and conservation.
- Operates within a wider network of biodiversity-genomics initiatives.

## Evidence
