# Joseph Gallieni

## Overview

Joseph Gallieni was the military governor of Paris whose determination to defend the capital, recognition of the German flank, and commitment of the Paris garrison contributed to the [[FirstBattleOfMarne|First Battle of the Marne]].

## Current Profile

[[596-the-first-world-war-the-miracle-on-the-marne-part-3-glt2229565266]] introduces Gallieni as an elderly and ill commander recalled during France's gravest 1914 crisis. His earlier defeat and captivity at Sedan gave the defense of Paris personal as well as national meaning, and he rejected planning that assumed the capital could simply be abandoned.

Gallieni interpreted the German First Army's movement east of Paris as an opportunity for active defense. He pressed the exposed-flank case, committed forces from the Paris garrison, and arranged emergency transport when rail congestion obstructed reinforcement. The [[TaxisOfTheMarne|Taxis of the Marne]] became the famous symbol of that effort, although their direct battlefield effect was small.

His relationship with [[JosephJoffre|Joseph Joffre]] was tense, and the episode does not reduce the victory to either man. Gallieni's distinct contribution was to convert the defense of Paris from a passive siege expectation into a component of the wider counterattack.

## Key Characteristics

- Military governor charged with defending Paris during the German advance.
- Personally shaped by the French defeat and his captivity at Sedan.
- Rejected retreat as the default plan for the capital.
- Identified the German First Army's exposed flank as an opportunity.
- Committed the Paris garrison to the wider Allied counterattack.
- Used improvised transport under rail congestion while its later legend exceeded its military effect.

## Qualifications

This is a Marne-centered profile rather than a full biography, and it does not cover Gallieni's colonial career or its violence. The source emphasizes resolve and initiative but also preserves tension with Joffre and denies that the taxi operation decided the battle. Dialogue, personal motives, and the division of operational credit remain source-scoped.
