# 张猛 / Zhang Meng (late Han)

## Overview
张猛 / Zhang Meng is the late-Han Wuwei actor who kills inspector [[HandanShangLateHan|邯郸商]], forbids public mourning, and then spares [[PangYuLateHan|庞淯]] when Pang Yu's open loyalty makes further violence reputationally costly.

## Current Profile
Hanji 1076 presents Zhang Meng through a sharp reversal. He first uses killing and a mourning ban to control the aftermath of Handan Shang's death. When Pang Yu nevertheless resigns, mourns, and comes armed for revenge, Zhang Meng refuses his soldiers' demand to seize him, permits the funeral, and argues that killing a loyal man would make honorable people cease trusting him.

Hanji 1075 supplies a heavily dramatized prequel in which Zhang Meng is Handan Shang's Taixue friend and appointee, builds an autonomous Wuwei force, clashes over grain and communications, and kills Handan Shang in a staged bridge ambush. Its chronology conflicts with Hanji 1076, and its dialogue, force totals, private motives, ritual objects, and death scene are not promoted into settled profile facts.

## Key Characteristics
- Kills Handan Shang in the episode's opening Wuwei incident.
- Tries to suppress the victim's public mourning through a death threat.
- Recognizes Pang Yu's open loyalty as a constraint on further punishment.
- Uses clemency to limit the reputational damage caused by his earlier violence.
- Is cast by Hanji 1075 as a trusted subordinate whose independent command base turns intimacy into rivalry, a source interpretation rather than verified biography.
- Remains distinct from [[ZhangMengWesternHan|张猛 / 张萌]], the Western Han official.

## Qualifications
The page is bounded to two podcast sources. Hanji 1075 dates the killing to 204 CE and Zhang Meng's death to the following year, while Hanji 1076 frames the aftermath in Jian'an 11 / 206 CE. The pre-killing friendship, Zhang Meng's appointment and force size, the bridge ambush, exact dialogue, ritual objects, death scene, and guilt-based psychological explanations require external verification and are not treated as settled profile facts.
