# General Electric

## Overview

General Electric is represented here as the industrial and media employer that gave [[RonaldReagan|Ronald Reagan]] a television role, a national factory circuit, and sustained exposure to corporate conservative arguments.

## Current Profile

[[311-reagan-the-road-to-the-white-house-part-2-glt9813084891]] treats GE as more than the sponsor of *General Electric Theater*. By employing Reagan as a host and goodwill ambassador, sending him through plants, and expecting speeches to workers, the company created a [[CorporateSpeakingPoliticalApprenticeship|corporate speaking apprenticeship]]. Its Boulwarism labor-relations philosophy emphasized free enterprise, anti-communism, low taxes, and limited government, although Reagan's later ideological development should not be reduced to corporate instruction alone. The relationship weakened as ratings fell and his increasingly explicit right-wing politics became a liability.

## Key Characteristics

- Industrial company that used television and plant visits as corporate communication.
- Employer that gave Reagan repeated contact with workers across the United States.
- Institutional carrier of Boulwarism and a free-enterprise political message.
- Provider of money, visibility, speaking practice, and material benefits during Reagan's post-Hollywood transition.
- Organization whose interests eventually diverged from Reagan's increasingly overt partisan role.

## Qualifications

This is a source-bounded profile of GE's relationship with Reagan, not a general corporate history. The episode does not isolate how much of Reagan's ideology came from GE rather than family background, anti-communism, conservative periodicals, Hollywood labor politics, or his own prior beliefs. Boulwarism is presented through the hosts' compressed account rather than a full labor-history assessment.

## What Changed

- Established GE as an institutional training ground connecting corporate communication, labor relations, television, and Reagan's political formation.
