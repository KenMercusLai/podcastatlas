## Overview

阴兴 / Yin Xing (Guangwu maternal kin) enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-722-rang-liuxiu-xiansi-de-toutongbing-zenme-huishi-logwccdsca7vm2zzssu8ubm8rwra|Hanji 722]] as [[YinLihua|阴丽华]]'s younger brother and the outer relative whom [[LiuXiu|光武帝刘秀]] trusts during a 44 CE illness crisis. His profile is defined by proximity to emergency succession risk and by refusal to convert that trust into the great marshalship.

## Current Profile

Hanji 722 gives Yin Xing a narrow but important Guangwu-era profile. When Liu Xiu suffers severe headache and dizziness in 44 CE and fears he may die, he appoints Yin Xing concurrently as shizhong and summons him into the Yuntai Guangshi to entrust affairs. The episode presents this as evidence that Yin Xing is unusually trusted because he thinks from Liu Xiu's position rather than treating maternal-family access as private opportunity.

After Liu Xiu recovers, he wants Yin Xing to replace the dead [[WuHanEasternHan|Wu Han]] as great marshal. Yin Xing refuses by kneeling, weeping, and saying that he dares not accept because it would damage Liu Xiu's sage virtue. The source therefore makes Yin Xing an outer-relative restraint case: his value comes from not taking the highest military-political office just because kinship and trust make it available.

## Key Characteristics

- Guangwu maternal relative identified as Yin Lihua's younger brother.
- Emergency trusted figure summoned when Liu Xiu fears death from severe illness.
- Temporary shizhong appointment in the illness-transfer scene.
- Refuses the great marshalship after Liu Xiu recovers.
- Outer-relative restraint figure whose political value lies in declining overlarge office.
- Distinct from same-name Yin Xing references in later Eastern Han case material unless future evidence proves identity.

## Qualifications

This page is source-bounded to Hanji 722. It does not reconstruct Yin Xing's complete biography, offices, death date, or the full Yin-family network. The route is deliberately suffixed because a later Hanji 740 source mentions a different Yin Xing context in the Chu-prison material, and this ingest does not prove those references are the same person.
