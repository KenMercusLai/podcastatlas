# Theseus

## Overview

Theseus appears in this source as Athens's remembered constitutional founder: the hero credited with uniting Attica and placing power with the demos.

## Current Profile

The episode shifts attention away from Theseus as only the Minotaur-slayer. In Athenian civic memory, he gathers the communities of Attica into one citizen body and creates an ancestral precedent for popular power.

This founder role helps later constitutional novelty look like recovery rather than invention. By associating unity and demos-rule with a deep heroic past, Athenians can understand Cleisthenic reform as continuous with who they had always been.

## Key Characteristics

- Theseus is remembered as a unifier of the peoples and territories of Attica.
- The source makes him a constitutional as well as mythological founder.
- His attributed grant of kratos to the demos gives democratic claims an ancestral pedigree.
- His memory helps radical reform present itself as restoration.

## Evidence

- Attic unification: [[334-athens-and-the-birth-of-democracy-glt2496707123]] says Athenians credited Theseus with combining Attica into one citizen body.
- Popular power: [[334-athens-and-the-birth-of-democracy-glt2496707123]] says he was remembered as founding a system in which the demos held kratos.
- Ancestral legitimation: [[334-athens-and-the-birth-of-democracy-glt2496707123]] connects heroic memory to the presentation of new institutions as ancient tradition.

## Qualifications

The page records political memory, not a historical claim that Theseus literally founded democracy. The source uses the myth to explain Athenian self-understanding while acknowledging the evidentiary limits of mythical material.

## What Changed

- Created Theseus as an Athenian unity and ancestral-democracy founder figure.
