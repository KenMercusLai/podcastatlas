# The Sorcerer's Apprentice / 《魔法师的学徒》

## Overview

*The Sorcerer's Apprentice* / 《魔法师的学徒》 is a poem by [[JohannWolfgangVonGoethe|Johann Wolfgang von Goethe]] used by the bounded source as a literary model of automation that is obedient yet difficult to stop.

## Current Profile

An apprentice commands an enchanted broom to carry water but knows only the activating spell. The broom continues after the desired bath is full, and splitting it produces two continuing carriers until the returning master restores control. The episode reads the failure as an omitted stopping condition rather than rebellion: the process follows its operational command while violating the apprentice's unstated purpose.

## Key Characteristics

- Centers a command whose literal execution diverges from the commander's intended outcome.
- Separates obedience from corrigibility and safe shutdown.
- Shows an attempted intervention multiplying rather than containing the active process.
- Retains a superior authority who knows the stopping rule, enabling comparison with systems no human fully masters.

- **Failed correction:** [[378-gede-ruhe-kandai-tongyong-rengong-zhineng-lp2zydrd_nxvglwfojhf443i_wwa]] describes the split broom as two agents continuing the same task.
- **Authority boundary:** [[378-gede-ruhe-kandai-tongyong-rengong-zhineng-lp2zydrd_nxvglwfojhf443i_wwa]] contrasts the master's successful spell with the possibility that advanced systems leave no equivalent expert.

## Qualifications

The current profile comes from one philosophical podcast summary. It uses the poem as an analogy and does not establish the behavior, architecture, or risk level of a particular AI system.
