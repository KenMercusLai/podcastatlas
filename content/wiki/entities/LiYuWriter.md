# 李渔 / Li Yu

## Overview

李渔 appears in the bounded sources as a writer and critic connecting the literary legitimation of [[JinPingMei|《金瓶梅》]] with a Chinese novel-and-drama tradition titled *The Flounder*.

## Current Profile

In the *Jin Ping Mei* source, Li Yu is important as a classifier: the episode says he grouped the novel with [[WaterMargin|《水浒传》]], [[JourneyToTheWest|《西游记》]], and *Romance of the Three Kingdoms* among the “four great strange books,” helping place a work reduced to erotic notoriety inside major vernacular-fiction criticism.

The [[TheFlounder|Grass reading]] adds Li Yu as a comparative author rather than only a reception figure. His same-titled Chinese novel and play are summarized as the story of two lower-status performers who remain faithful, die, become flounders, return to life, and achieve reunion. The episode reads their reciprocity and shared marginal status as a departure from a simpler talented-scholar/beautiful-woman hierarchy, while keeping historical and textual claims source-scoped.

## Key Characteristics

- Serves as a reception-history authority for the literary status of *Jin Ping Mei*.
- Connects criticism and classification to the legitimacy of vernacular fiction.
- Wrote across fiction and drama in the source's account of *The Flounder*.
- Uses paired-fish transformation to explore fidelity, death, revival, and comparatively reciprocal love.

## Evidence

- **Critical classification:** [[fanwai-15-banlatte-jiang-jinpingmei-la-erqie-haishi-luse-de-991508433]] cites Li Yu's “four great strange books” grouping in its defense of *Jin Ping Mei* as literature and social history.
- **Creative comparison:** [[26-junte-gelasi-bimuyu-danze-deguo-nvxing-pengren-yu-renlei-de-lishi-lnn6bsdseydyq0t98pxdttca_htz]] introduces his same-titled story and play as an East-West comparison about love and gender relations.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to two podcast summaries and does not establish Li Yu's full biography, bibliography, editions, or critical system. Attribution of the classification, plot details, social-status interpretation, and claims about gender equality require verification against primary texts and scholarship.

## What Changed

- Extended Li Yu from a reception-history classifier into a creative writer used for comparison with Grass's *The Flounder*.

## Relationships

- [[JinPingMei|《金瓶梅》]] - novel whose literary status Li Yu helps establish in the first source.
- [[GenreLiteraryLegitimacy]] - critical relationship created by placing vernacular fiction inside a major canon.
- [[TheFlounder]] - Western same-title comparison distinguished by different plots and gender arguments.
- [[LiteraryAmbiguityAsComplexity]] - related reading discipline, though the current sources do not establish that Li Yu uses Grass's polyphonic method.
