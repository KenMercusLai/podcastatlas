# Franz Conrad von Hötzendorf

## Overview

Franz Conrad von Hötzendorf was Austria-Hungary's chief of the general staff, the leading military advocate of preventive war against [[Serbia]], and a commander whose 1914-1915 Serbian and Galician failures exposed the gap between strategic ambition and Habsburg operating capacity.

## Current Profile

The episodes present Conrad as the clearest preventive-war voice in Vienna. Between 1906 and 1914 he repeatedly urged war against Serbia and several other neighbors. His conflict with [[FranzFerdinand]] was substantive: the heir repeatedly rejected Serbian war because Russian intervention could destroy Austria-Hungary. After Sarajevo, Conrad pressed [[LeopoldBerchtold]] for immediate action, converting assassination into an opportunity to remove a perceived threat without a coherent plan for Russian intervention or a general European war.

The 1914 Serbian campaign supplies the first operational test. Harvest leave, divided priorities between Serbia and Russia, weak transport and equipment, language friction, and [[OskarPotiorek]]'s repeated invasions produced humiliation rather than restored credibility. The failure cannot be assigned to Conrad alone, but it ties his demand for war to an army that had not converted manpower into readiness for simultaneous Serbian and Russian demands.

The [[Galicia]] and [[Przemysl|Przemyśl]] branch extends that pattern. Conrad attacked despite Russian numerical strength, inadequate rail capacity, supply weakness, and multilingual command friction. After defeat he ordered Przemyśl held, rejected evacuation before the second siege, and sent forces through the winter Carpathians in a failed relief attempt. Prestige and imperial honor became operational motives, while mass loss, exposure, disease, surrender, and reliance on German recapture accelerated military and political breakdown.

## Key Characteristics

- Longstanding advocate of preventive war against several neighboring states.
- Senior military participant pressing for immediate armed punishment after Sarajevo.
- Focused on Serbia and imperial credibility more than alliance consequences and readiness limits.
- Advocated war before Habsburg manpower could be coordinated for Serbian and Russian demands.
- Pursued offensives and fortress relief despite severe logistical, climatic, manpower, and command constraints.
- Made symbolic value part of operational judgment at Przemyśl.

## Qualifications

This profile remains selective. Conrad was influential but not the sole author of policy or operations; Potiorek, political leaders, rail systems, ministries, subordinate officers, and Serbian and Russian agency prevent every failure from becoming his personal act. Multiethnicity was one source of friction, not a sufficient explanation for defeat. Counts of preventive-war proposals, force and casualty totals, reported orders, romantic motives, morale effects, prestige motives, and counterfactual alternatives remain episode-attributed.

## What Changed

- Connected Conrad's preventive-war advocacy to the readiness and divided-command failures exposed in Serbia.
- Extended the command profile from political escalation through Serbian defeat, Galician collapse, and the Przemyśl relief disaster.

## Relationships

- [[LeopoldBerchtold]] - foreign minister whom Conrad pressed toward immediate action.
- [[FranzFerdinand]] - murdered heir who had resisted war with Serbia.
- [[AustriaHungary]] - empire whose army he led and whose weakness his campaigns exposed.
- [[Serbia]] - target of preventive war and the first failed campaign.
- [[OskarPotiorek]] - Serbian-campaign commander operating within the war Conrad advocated.
- [[JulyCrisis]] - escalation in which his Serbia-first logic became consequential.
- [[Przemysl]] - fortress whose defense and attempted relief became a defining failure.
- [[Galicia]] - province in which his offensive collapsed.
- [[EasternFrontFirstWorldWar]] - theater where strategic ambition met operational limits.
