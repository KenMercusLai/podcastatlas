# Vincenzo Perugia

## Overview
Vincenzo Perugia appears in [[667-the-mystery-of-the-mona-lisa-glt1735270133]] as the former Louvre worker who stole [[MonaLisa|the Mona Lisa]] in 1911 and unintentionally intensified its global fame.

## Current Profile
The source describes Perugia as an Italian painter-decorator who had worked in the [[Louvre]]. He hid the painting under his bed and later claimed patriotic motives, saying he believed it belonged in Florence. The episode treats the mismatch between his ordinary profile and the public's appetite for a glamorous mystery as part of why attention refocused on the painting itself.

## Key Characteristics
- Perugia is the thief in the 1911 Mona Lisa theft.
- His prior Louvre work made the theft practically possible.
- His patriotic claim linked the painting to contested French and Italian patrimony.
- His lack of glamour sharpened the painting's own celebrity.
- The theft turned absence, news coverage, and crowds into fame-making mechanisms.

## Evidence
- Theft event - [[667-the-mystery-of-the-mona-lisa-glt1735270133]] says the Mona Lisa was stolen from the Louvre on 21 August 1911.
- Identity and motive - [[667-the-mystery-of-the-mona-lisa-glt1735270133]] identifies Perugia as an Italian painter-decorator and former Louvre worker who claimed patriotic motives.
- Fame effect - [[667-the-mystery-of-the-mona-lisa-glt1735270133]] says the theft caused international outrage, crowds visited the empty space, and attention intensified around the painting.

## Qualifications
The source reports Perugia's patriotic motive as his claim, not as a complete explanation of the theft. It treats the fame effect as historically clear even if his inner motive remains less glamorous than public speculation expected.
