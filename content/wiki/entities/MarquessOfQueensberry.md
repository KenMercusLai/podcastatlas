# Marquess of Queensberry

## Overview

John Douglas, Marquess of Queensberry, was [[AlfredDouglas|Lord Alfred Douglas]]'s father and the antagonist whose threats and accusatory club card prompted [[OscarWilde|Oscar Wilde]]'s catastrophic libel prosecution.

## Current Profile

The episode portrays Queensberry as eccentric, aggressive, and dangerous to antagonize. After hearing rumors about Wilde and Bosie, he threatened Wilde and attempted to disrupt the opening of *The Importance of Being Earnest* before leaving an accusatory card at the Albemarle Club.

Once Wilde prosecuted him, Queensberry's lawyers pleaded justification and produced evidence connecting Wilde to multiple young men. The investigation transformed an aristocratic family conflict into a public trial, defeated the libel case, and supplied material to the Crown Prosecutor.

## Key Characteristics

- Aristocratic father violently opposed to his son's relationship with Wilde.
- Public antagonist who used threats, attempted theatrical disruption, and an accusatory club card.
- Libel defendant whose justification defense required proving the substance of his allegation.
- Client whose investigators assembled the evidence that collapsed Wilde's case.
- Political sensitivity point because his correspondence invoked Lord Rosebery and Queensberry's late eldest son.
