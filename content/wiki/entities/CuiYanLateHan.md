# 崔琰 / Cui Yan (late Han)

## Overview
崔琰 / Cui Yan (late Han) is a late-Han adviser and official whose bounded profile spans legitimacy warning, people-first remonstrance, personnel selection, succession alignment, and death after an ambiguous private letter is converted into political evidence.

## Current Profile
Cui Yan's profile is consistent across different power centers: he evaluates political action against public legitimacy and welfare rather than against the ruler's immediate sense of advantage. Under [[YuanShao|袁绍]], he joins [[JuShou|沮授]]'s cautionary side and argues that attacking while the emperor is in Xudu runs against public attachment; Yuan Shao's force, grain, and family prestige make the warning easy to ignore. Under [[CaoCao|曹操]], Cui Yan serves as Jizhou biejia and rebukes Cao for seeing 300,000 potential soldiers before seeing a war-worn population that needs consolation. Cao apologizes, making the scene an example of severe but public-interest-oriented correction rather than self-aggrandizing familiarity. These roles are grounded respectively in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1033-geju-junfa-yuanshu-zuizhong-tuxue-er-wang-lt0ibs-en0oeoqkkcxl8sevjng4|Hanji 1033]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1064-xuyou-zhisi-gonggao-zhenzhu-yu-xingge-quexian-de-beige-lswmvj55p7ip97ts5e30xfdubwth|Hanji 1064]].

The same standard-setting tendency later appears in personnel administration. Cui Yan and [[MaoJie|毛玠]] are said to favor substance, modesty, and harmony while rejecting flashy reputation, false display, and faction-building; the resulting official behavior is interpreted as [[SelectionDrivenVirtueSignaling|选官驱动的德行表演]]. Cui Yan's praise also brings [[SimaYi|司马懿]] to Cao Cao's attention.

Hanji 1156 develops the execution preview from Hanji 1087. After Cui Yan consoles Yang Xun over ridicule, a phrase about changing circumstances is reframed as dissatisfaction with Cao Cao. Cui Yan's moral reputation and explicit support for [[CaoPi|曹丕]] over [[CaoZhi|曹植]] make the accusation politically consequential, but the source does not prove that either factor alone caused his death. It identifies [[DingYiLateHan|丁仪]] as the likely accuser only indirectly and presents [[CuiLinLateHan|崔林]] as a relative who defends Cui Yan while following a more adaptive official path.

## Key Characteristics
- Warns Yuan Shao that the emperor's presence at Xudu creates a legitimacy and public-opinion constraint.
- Serves Cao Cao in Jizhou and redirects conquest-as-conscription thinking toward exhausted civilians.
- Uses severe speech as governance correction rather than public self-aggrandizement in the bounded cases.
- Jointly applies substantive and moral selection criteria with Mao Jie in the 208 CE source.
- Praises Sima Yi's intelligence and decisiveness, helping bring him to Cao Cao's attention.
- Supports Cao Pi through a primogeniture argument in the succession struggle.
- Is executed after a private letter is detached from its consoling context and treated as political hostility.

## Qualifications
This page remains narrow and source-bounded. Hanji 1087's claims about broad performative frugality and great-clan displacement remain interpretive. Hanji 1156 develops the death case but does not conclusively identify the accuser or isolate Cao Cao's motive among succession management, elite control, personal suspicion, and moral-pressure removal. The durable synthesis is that Cui Yan repeatedly appears as a severe public standard-setter whose moral authority becomes a political asset and, under concentrated power, a vulnerability.
