# Slicy

## Overview
Slicy is the MacRabbit asset-export tool discussed in [[anyway-194-anyway-194]] as a design-handoff breakthrough for Photoshop-era UI work.

## Current Profile
In the episode, Slicy matters because it turns painful manual slicing into a rule-based export workflow. Designers could name Photoshop layers or groups, drag a PSD into Slicy, and produce assets such as normal and @2x images without separate slicing labor. The hosts treat this as a precursor to later Sketch and Figma export thinking: a light naming convention could reorganize design-to-development collaboration.

## Key Characteristics
- It automated image asset export from Photoshop files through layer naming.
- It reduced manual slicing and repetitive design-handoff labor.
- It is remembered for a Mac-like interaction feel, including drag-and-drop conversion.
- It anticipates later object-level export conventions in Sketch and Figma.

## Evidence
- Export mechanism - [[anyway-194-anyway-194]] explains that designers could name Photoshop layers and drag the PSD into Slicy for automatic output.
- Workflow impact - [[anyway-194-anyway-194]] says Slicy significantly reduced cutting and exporting work for designers.
- Format convention - [[anyway-194-anyway-194]] mentions icon.png and @2x-style naming as examples of export rules.
- Later influence - [[anyway-194-anyway-194]] links Slicy's idea to later Photoshop features and Sketch/Figma export settings.
