# Ulster Defence Association

## Overview
The Ulster Defence Association is the loyalist paramilitary organization that the Troubles sequence uses to show Protestant communal fear hardening into open vigilante and military-style mobilization.

## Current Profile
The current profile is that the UDA scales loyalist paramilitarism beyond the earlier [[UlsterVolunteerForce|UVF]] branch. Part 3 introduces it as a loyalist defense group founded in autumn 1971 amid fear of civil war. Part 4 places it in the 1972 collapse after [[BloodySunday]], when [[WilliamCraig]]'s Ulster Vanguard rhetoric, unemployment, young men's need for belonging, protection rackets, and vigilante culture make military-style loyalist organization socially visible and deadly.

## Key Characteristics
- Loyalist defense organization formed amid civil-war fear.
- Openly visible military-style organization before proscription in the episode's 1972 frame.
- Closely linked to William Craig's Ulster Vanguard movement in the source account.
- Socially sustained by unemployment, young male belonging, rackets, and vigilante culture.
- Responsible in the episode's wider framing for violence overwhelmingly directed at Catholic civilians.

## Evidence
- Founding and scale: [[699-the-troubles-return-of-the-ira-part-3-glt9519811642]] describes the UDA as founded in autumn 1971 and later reaching tens of thousands of associated members.
- Loyalist mobilization context: [[699-the-troubles-return-of-the-ira-part-3-glt9519811642]] ties UDA growth to IRA attacks, British policy failures, and Protestant fear of civil war.
- 1972 visibility: [[700-the-troubles-bloody-sunday-part-4-glt3909829334]] says the UDA was not then proscribed and could march openly in military-style clothing.
- Social base and violence: [[700-the-troubles-bloody-sunday-part-4-glt3909829334]] connects loyalist mobilisation to unemployment, vigilante culture, protection rackets, and about 120 loyalist killings in 1972, mainly Catholic civilians.

## Qualifications
The sources explain why loyalist mobilization could feel defensive to some Protestants without legitimating civilian violence. The page remains source-bounded and does not yet separate all UDA factions, legal status changes, or later organizational evolution.
