# Roger Tory Peterson

## Overview

Roger Tory Peterson is the ornithologist and field-guide author used by Episode 230 to explain how diagnostic visual features made bird identification more accessible while reinforcing a type-oriented image of species.

## Current Profile

The source credits Peterson's 1934 field guide with lowering the barrier to identifying birds outdoors by emphasizing a limited set of visible marks. In the episode's critical history, that achievement also clarifies a boundary: the image needed to identify a species selects typical beak, eye-ring, wing, leg, and plumage features while suppressing much of one animal's setting and life.

## Key Characteristics

- Author associated with a 1934 bird field guide.
- Emphasized diagnostic field marks for non-specialist identification.
- Represents the democratization of bird recognition through visual simplification.
- Serves as an origin point in the episode's account of type-oriented nature imagery.
