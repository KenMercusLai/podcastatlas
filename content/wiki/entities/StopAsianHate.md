# Stop Asian Hate

## Overview
Stop Asian Hate is represented in the source as a public-action frame through which Asian Americans connected pandemic-era attacks to longer histories of bullying, exclusion, stereotyping, and conditional belonging.

## Current Profile
The episode treats Stop Asian Hate as broader than one organization or protest. Marching, speaking, sharing experiences, listening across backgrounds, and building solidarity are all presented as forms of action. Its central difficulty is that “Asian” joins people with different nationalities, migration histories, generations, and politics, while racialized hostility often ignores those distinctions.

## Key Characteristics
- Public response to anti-Asian violence and discrimination intensified during the COVID-19 pandemic.
- Umbrella language joins heterogeneous Asian communities without making their histories identical.
- Personal testimony and ordinary participation are treated as political action alongside formal organizing.
- The movement connects visible attacks to subtler exclusion, stereotyping, and belonging disputes.
- Solidarity requires acknowledging internal difference rather than replacing it with a single representative identity.

## Qualifications
This profile reflects one podcast episode's use of the phrase and does not establish the movement's organizational structure, founders, campaign chronology, geographic scope, policy program, participation levels, or later development. The coalition label should not erase differences among Asian communities or imply shared political positions.

## What Changed
- Established Stop Asian Hate as both a protest frame and a testimony-based coalition problem.
