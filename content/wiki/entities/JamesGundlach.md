# James Gundlach

## Overview

James Gundlach is a sociologist and coauthor of the 1995 paper “Damned Upon Arrival.” In the episode, his research on racially unequal victim survival is inseparable from his role sheltering vulnerable teenagers and mourning [[BrandonYoung]].

## Current Profile

Gundlach describes a childhood marked by an alcoholic father, parental absence, and material insecurity. He later served in the Army, attended graduate school, taught sociology at Auburn University, exposed an academic cheating scheme, survived encephalitis, and rebuilt much of his vocabulary after illness.

His home became a refuge after he took in a 14-year-old niece and then other teenagers who had been expelled or were living on the streets. With [[PenelopeHanke]], he studied whether differences in transport and medical care affected which assaults were prosecuted as homicides. Brandon's death in Gundlach's cabin became a footnote because it fell outside the study population but embodied the paper's central mechanism.

## Key Characteristics

- Connects his care for unsupported teenagers to his own childhood experience of parental absence.
- Combined sociology with an interest in how institutions alter apparently straightforward crime statistics.
- Rebuilt his language abilities after encephalitis and returned to academic work.
- Sheltered Brandon and encouraged his reading, vocabulary, GED completion, and possible college path.
- Used a footnote as both methodological qualification and personal dedication when Brandon's case did not fit the study sample.
