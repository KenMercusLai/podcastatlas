## Overview

王朝 / Wang Chao is one of three Western Han chief clerks who join Chancellor [[ZhuangQingdiWesternHan|庄青翟]] in an active counter-coalition against [[ZhangTangWesternHan|张汤]], are later named in Zhang's final accusation, and are executed after [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] reopens the case.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-339-1-sanrenchenghu-ruhe-bisi-xihan-diyi-kuli-lowphplbvgugdyfag-sczpgggyrv|Hanji 339-1]] places Wang with [[ZhuMaichenWesternHan|朱买臣]] and [[BianTongWesternHan|边通]] as former senior officials later subordinated to Zhang after his rapid promotion. The episode attributes their hostility to repeated humiliation and says Zhang's continuing case against Zhuang makes cooperation a matter of self-protection as well as resentment. Zhu leads the Tian Xin accusation; the source does not separately identify Wang's contribution.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-339-2-yi-si-sharen-kuli-zhangtang-shi-ruhe-zuodao-de-luq3jaa9iruhiqiqlduewbj-luca|Hanji 339-2]] says Zhang's final letter identifies the three as those who caused his death. After Zhang's estate and his mother's sparse funeral alter the emperor's reading, all three are executed and associated with Zhuang.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han official identified as one of three chief clerks in the episode.
- Presented as an opponent humiliated by Zhang Tang.
- Joins the counter-coalition from resentment and perceived future danger.
- Named in Zhang's final written accusation.
- Executed after Han Wudi reopens the case.

## Evidence

Coalition role:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-339-1-sanrenchenghu-ruhe-bisi-xihan-diyi-kuli-lowphplbvgugdyfag-sczpgggyrv|Hanji 339-1]] says Wang joins Zhu, Bian, and Zhuang when Zhang's case against the chancellor creates a shared threat.

Final accusation and punishment:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-339-2-yi-si-sharen-kuli-zhangtang-shi-ruhe-zuodao-de-luq3jaa9iruhiqiqlduewbj-luca|Hanji 339-2]] names Wang Chao with Zhu Maichen and Bian Tong in Zhang's final letter and says the emperor later has the three executed.

## Qualifications

The sources do not reconstruct Wang's wider career, individual acts, legal charges, or independent evidence of guilt. Hanji 339-1 gives a collective motive and coalition role but assigns the specific Tian Xin plan to Zhu Maichen; it does not prove equal participation by all three clerks. The generic personal name is disambiguated by the stable Western Han suffix.

## What Changed

- Added the earlier coalition, humiliation, and shared-threat context behind Wang's later appearance in Zhang's final letter.
