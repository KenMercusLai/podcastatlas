## Overview

Pope Leo X / 利奥十世, born Giovanni de' Medici, was Lorenzo's son and a cardinal who helped restore the [[HouseOfMedici|Medici]] to Florence before his 1513 election made church office the family's principal route into European dynastic power.

## Current Profile

The episode presents Leo as the clearest early case of [[EcclesiasticalDynasticConversion|ecclesiastical-dynastic conversion]]. A church career gave him relationships and resources unavailable to his exiled family, while papal and Spanish force enabled the 1512 restoration. As pope, he used office to advance relatives, arrange marriages, and seek secure territory beyond informal control of the [[FlorentineRepublic|Florentine Republic]].

That ascent carried fiscal and institutional costs. Entertainment, hunting, display, artistic patronage, and St Peter's spending joined office sales, fees, and indulgence fundraising. Martin Luther's attack on indulgences became part of the Reformation crisis, which the episode says Leo initially underestimated. His support for Raphael and other artists therefore belongs to the same papal system as debt, family advancement, and religious conflict rather than to a separate story of taste.

## Key Characteristics

- Medici cardinal who accumulated church relationships during family exile.
- Political actor associated with the papal-backed restoration of 1512.
- Pope who turned ecclesiastical office into family and territorial strategy.
- Lavish court patron joining artistic achievement to severe fiscal pressure.
- Fundraiser associated with office sales, fees, and indulgence promotion.
- Pontiff whose response to Luther became part of the early Reformation crisis.

## Qualifications

This profile rests on one broad podcast summary, not papal accounts, correspondence, theology, or specialist Reformation history. The source does not establish that Leo alone caused the Reformation, that all indulgence practice served his personal spending, or that patronage and corruption had one motive. Amounts, chronology, family intent, responsibility, and the claim about Florence's first pope remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Created a profile linking Medici restoration and papal election to a new family power base.
- Joined artistic patronage and dynastic strategy to the same fiscal and institutional system.
- Qualified a single-cause account of Reformation by retaining Leo's role without making it exclusive.
