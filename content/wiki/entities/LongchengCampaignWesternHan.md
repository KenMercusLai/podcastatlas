## Overview

龙城之战 / Longcheng Campaign is the reported 129 BCE breakthrough in which [[WeiQingHanGeneral|卫青]], commanding one of four Han cavalry columns against [[Xiongnu|匈奴]], turns an intelligence-poor search into a rapid strike on Longcheng. The current evidence comes from one compact podcast summary, so the maneuver, figures, site description, and wider turning-point interpretation remain source-scoped.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-275-dapo-xiongnu-bubai-shenhua-weiqing-ruhe-zuodao-de-1-lj6mkkcbwrfznzqpkwtkzigirotg|Hanji 275 part 1]] continues the approach left open by Hanji 274. It says Wei Qing reads the absence of enemy contact as a sign that his force has passed the expected screen, orders baggage abandoned, and advances roughly two hundred li to a lightly defended Longcheng. The reported attack kills more than seven hundred, captures more than two thousand, and takes large livestock holdings.

The result matters comparatively. [[GongsunHeWesternHan|公孙贺]] reportedly finds no opponent, [[GongsunAoWesternHan|公孙敖]] loses most of his force, and [[LiGuangHanGeneral|李广]] is defeated and captured before escaping. Wei Qing is therefore the only commander whose column gives [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] a satisfactory result, leading to his guanneihou reward. The source interprets this as a psychological break in Xiongnu invincibility and an opening for later offensive war, but a single raid does not independently establish immediate strategic reversal.

## Key Characteristics

- One successful column inside an otherwise failed or inconclusive four-route Han expedition.
- Intelligence-poor deep movement converted into surprise through wide maneuver, inference from non-contact, baggage abandonment, and acceleration.
- Attack on a target portrayed by the episode as lightly defended but politically and ritually important.
- Limited reported kill total paired with larger captive, livestock, symbolic, and morale effects.
- First-command breakthrough that differentiates Wei Qing from three more experienced parallel commanders.

## Qualifications

The current page rests on one structured popular-history summary rather than a primary-source reconstruction. It does not independently establish the campaign's exact date, routes, distances, force sizes, screen geometry, Longcheng's location and institutional status, defender strength, casualties, captives, livestock, sentence and redemption procedures, or reward. The spring-window theory and the claim that this event reversed the wider Han-Xiongnu balance remain host interpretations. The Pingxingguan analogy is a morale comparison, not an equivalence in scale or context.
