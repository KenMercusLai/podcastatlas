# 叶公 / Ye Gong

叶公 / Ye Gong enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-119-1-chaijie-jingke-ciqin-bi-bai-de-mimi-lnonhw9tlsu5dudu8750zvgeaz1p]] as the judgment figure in the [[BaiGongSheng|白公胜]] comparison. The episode uses his view to clarify why a person can look trustworthy or brave and still fail the moral standard that [[SimaGuang|司马光]] applies to [[JingKe|荆轲]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-119-2-zanmen-dou-yuanwang-le-beifu-2000-duonian-heiguo-de-yegong-haolong-lh-h-gezpqtvnyq40zlqac8s-5e]] then gives the biographical branch that Qinji 119-1 only invoked. Ye Gong is identified as Shen Zhuliang, courtesy name Zigao, a Chu official associated with Ye county. When [[ZiXiChu|子西]] wants to recall [[BaiGongSheng|白公胜]], Ye Gong warns that Bai's visible 信 and 勇 are not genuine unless governed by 仁 and 义.

The episode makes Ye Gong's judgment practical rather than abstract. Bai Gong Sheng later kills Zi Xi, seizes [[ChuHuiwang|楚惠王]], and kills [[GuanXiuChu|管修]] in the [[BaiGongShengRebellion|白公胜之乱]]. Ye Gong reads Bai's initial success as accidental and the killing of Guan Xiu as proof that the people will not attach to him, then raises troops, suppresses the rebellion, and restores Chu Hui Wang.

Qinji 119-2 also turns Ye Gong into a positive exit case. After being granted both lingyin and sima authority, he later hands the offices to successors and returns to his fief, making him an example of power used for a task and relinquished before it becomes self-protective possession.

The source contrasts this record with [[YeGongHaolong|叶公好龙]], the later idiom attributed to [[LiuXiang|刘向]]'s [[XinXu|《新序》]]. The episode argues that the idiom's hypocrite image is a reputation-distorting afterlife rather than a reliable account of Ye Gong's character.
