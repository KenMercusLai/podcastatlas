# Michael Pettit

## Overview

Michael Pettit is the historian of psychology who frames [[rat-vs-raccoon-rat-vs-raccoon|Rat vs. Raccoon]] as an inquiry into why raccoons were neglected while rats became psychology's dominant animal model.

## Current Profile

Pettit's investigation begins with a mundane Toronto encounter: a raccoon opening his compost bin. He finds that raccoons received little scientific attention compared with rats and monkeys, then recovers [[LawrenceCole]]'s 1907 puzzle-box work as a discarded alternative in comparative psychology.

His contribution is historical and methodological. He asks whether psychology's preferred animal was selected because it best represented humanity or because it was cheap, tractable, and compatible with behaviorist demands. He also connects Cole's history to [[SuzanneMacDonald]], helping reopen empirical work on Toronto's urban raccoons.

## Key Characteristics

- Historian of psychology interested in overlooked researchers and subjects.
- Uses the neglect of raccoons to question the institutional history of model selection.
- Recovers Lawrence Cole's early raccoon-cognition research.
- Separates scientific centrality from demonstrated representativeness.
- Connects historical inquiry with modern urban-animal research.
