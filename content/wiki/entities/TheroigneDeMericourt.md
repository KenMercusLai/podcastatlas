# Théroigne de Méricourt

## Overview

Théroigne de Méricourt was a revolutionary activist associated with the Women's March on Versailles, political clubs, and advocacy for an armed women's battalion.

## Current Profile

The sources present Méricourt as a visible challenge to the Revolution's gendered public boundary. One places her in the carnivalesque public world surrounding the march to Versailles, identifiable by striking riding dress; the later episode traces her Austrian imprisonment, club access, organizational experiments, and campaign for an armed women's battalion. Her visibility created political prestige without securing durable institutional inclusion.

## Key Characteristics

- She came from Liège and became a flamboyant revolutionary public figure.
- She was associated with the Women's March on Versailles and its public political theatre.
- Austrian agents arrested her in 1790 and imprisoned her until late 1791.
- Her imprisonment increased rather than destroyed her revolutionary prestige.
- She pursued women-only and mixed political clubs.
- She advocated a women's battalion to defend Paris.
