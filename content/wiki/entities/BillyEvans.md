# Billy Evans

## Overview

[[BillyEvans|Billy Evans]] is [[ElizabethHolmes|Elizabeth Holmes]]'s spouse, the primary caregiver for their two children during her imprisonment, and the founder of [[Himanthus]]. His [[AllIn|All-In]] interview is both firsthand family testimony and an explicitly interested defense of Holmes.

## Current Profile

Evans's strongest evidentiary contribution concerns his own relationship, parenting, prison visits, and startup ambitions. His explanations of [[Theranos]], Holmes's intent, the verdict, and sentencing should be read as advocacy: the episode supplies neither adversarial questioning nor the records needed to overturn the existing whistleblower and regulatory account.

## Key Characteristics

- Holmes's spouse and public advocate, presenting a humanizing counter-narrative to the scandal record.
- Parent caring for two young children and maintaining their relationship with an incarcerated mother.
- Firsthand witness to the family's pretrial, trial, sentencing, and prison-visit experience.
- Photonics entrepreneur who says he previously helped build Luminar and now leads Himanthus.
- Interested narrator whose personal knowledge is strongest on family life and weakest as independent proof of legal or technical claims.

## Evidence

- **Family witness:** [[billy-evans-ais26-audio-88-billy-evans-ais26-audio-88]] recounts the relationship from 2017 through indictment, trial, parenthood, sentencing, and regular prison visits.
- **Advocate:** [[billy-evans-ais26-audio-88-billy-evans-ais26-audio-88]] records Evans's continuing belief in Holmes's innocence, his objections to gendered media framing, and his plan to release materials that were not provided in the episode.
- **Founder:** [[billy-evans-ais26-audio-88-billy-evans-ais26-audio-88]] describes [[Himanthus]] as a Raman-spectroscopy sensor and biological-data company, while offering no external validation of its technology.

## Qualifications

Evans is not a neutral narrator of Holmes or Theranos. The interview includes no independent analysis of trial exhibits, no affected-patient or investor testimony, and no response to the detailed validation and quality-control concerns in the [[TylerShultz|Tyler Shultz]] sources. His description of federal prison rules, credits, restitution, and release timing is also personal and time-bound rather than general legal guidance.
