# August Heinrich Hoffmann von Fallersleben

## Overview

August Heinrich Hoffmann von Fallersleben appears as the liberal nationalist poet who wrote *Das Lied der Deutschen*, later the [[GermanNationalAnthem]].

## Current Profile

The episode presents Hoffmann as writing in 1841 before German unification, when the political problem was fragmented German-speaking states rather than a settled nation-state. His "Deutschland über alles" phrase is treated as a call to put German unity above local loyalties, not as an original demand for domination over other countries, though later history made that distinction politically fragile.

## Key Characteristics

- Liberal nationalist writer seeking German unity in a liberal political order.
- Lyricist of *Das Lied der Deutschen* on Heligoland in 1841.
- Author whose first-verse wording later became vulnerable to militarist and Nazi reinterpretation.
- Political exile figure after losing his professorship while the 1848 liberal revolutions failed.

## Evidence

- Authorship: [[679-germany-the-song-hitler-stole-part-3-glt6217148052]] says Hoffmann wrote *Das Lied der Deutschen* on Heligoland in 1841.
- Political intention: [[679-germany-the-song-hitler-stole-part-3-glt6217148052]] presents him as a liberal nationalist who wanted German-speaking states united in a liberal order.
- Phrase meaning: [[679-germany-the-song-hitler-stole-part-3-glt6217148052]] says "Deutschland über alles" originally meant putting Germany above local state loyalties.
- Later life: [[679-germany-the-song-hitler-stole-part-3-glt6217148052]] says Hoffmann lost his professorship, went into exile, and saw the 1848 revolutions fail.

## Qualifications

The page records the episode's distinction between original liberal-national context and later reception. It does not claim that all later listeners could or should hear the first verse in Hoffmann's 1841 sense after the twentieth century changed the song's associations.
