# Al-Mansur

## Overview

Al-Mansur appears as the second Abbasid caliph, a disciplined and violent consolidator who made the new dynasty durable and founded [[Baghdad]] as its purpose-built political center.

## Current Profile

Born Abu Jaffar and succeeding his brother as-Saffah in 753, al-Mansur combined personal austerity, fiscal and administrative attention, dynastic persecution, and deliberate political symbolism. The episode treats even probably legendary stories of his cruelty as evidence of the reputation produced by his controlled, bureaucratic violence.

His defining project was the choice and construction of [[RoundCityOfBaghdad|the Round City]]. Rejecting Damascus, Kufa, and Harran, he selected a commercially promising Mesopotamian site nearer the Abbasids' eastern power base. The central palace and mosque, controlled enclosure, gates, walls, and Khorasani settlement translated caliphal hierarchy into urban form.

## Key Characteristics

- Abbasid caliph who succeeded as-Saffah and consolidated the dynasty.
- Pious and austere ruler represented as hostile to drink and music.
- Effective economic and administrative manager in the episode's account.
- Ruthless opponent of Alid and other potential dynastic rivals.
- Founder who treated capital location and design as instruments of legitimacy.
- Ruler whose Baghdad joined commercial access, military security, Persian inheritance, and universal ambition.

## Evidence

- **Rule and reputation:** [[377-baghdad-crossroads-of-the-universe-part-2-glt9127243683]] describes al-Mansur's accession, austerity, administration, executions, and persecution of Ali's descendants.
- **Capital selection:** [[377-baghdad-crossroads-of-the-universe-part-2-glt9127243683]] explains his rejection of Damascus, Kufa, and Harran and his preference for a new Mesopotamian center.
- **Urban authority:** [[377-baghdad-crossroads-of-the-universe-part-2-glt9127243683]] connects the round plan, central palace and mosque, restricted riding, markets, and military settlement to caliphal power.

## Qualifications

The profile rests on one popular-history episode. The storeroom of labeled Alid bodies is presented as probably legendary, and exact foundation dates, design decisions, reported speeches, economic performance, and the division between al-Mansur's personal choices and broader administrative processes require corroboration.

## What Changed

- Established a profile of al-Mansur as both violent dynastic consolidator and founder of Baghdad.
- Connected his capital project to security, commerce, eastern support, Persian inheritance, and political theater.

- [[Baghdad]] - capital he founded to anchor Abbasid authority.
- [[RoundCityOfBaghdad]] - planned urban core through which his rule was spatially expressed.
- [[AbuMuslim]] - revolutionary commander whose eastern coalition helped create Abbasid power.
