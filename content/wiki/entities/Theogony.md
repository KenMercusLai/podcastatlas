# Theogony

## Overview

The *Theogony* is [[Hesiod]]' genealogical poem about the origins, generations, conflicts, and ordering of the Greek gods, culminating in [[Zeus]]' rule.

## Current Profile

[[602-greek-myths-zeus-king-of-the-gods-part-1-glt4871750364]] treats the poem as a foundational arrangement rather than a binding scripture. It moves from Gaia and Uranus through Cronos and Rhea to Zeus, the Titan war, division of the cosmos, and the containment of future succession risk. Its portable genealogy helped Greeks recognize a shared divine world while cities and poets retained variant stories.

## Key Characteristics

- Genealogical account linking divine birth, kinship, conflict, and cosmic order.
- Principal episode source for the Uranus-Cronos-Zeus [[DivineSuccessionMyth|succession sequence]].
- Literary biography of Zeus from hidden infant to king of gods and men.
- Product of oral-poetic inheritance preserved and widened through writing.
- Influential common structure that does not erase local or poetic variants.

## Evidence

- Genealogy and conflict: [[602-greek-myths-zeus-king-of-the-gods-part-1-glt4871750364]] follows Gaia, Uranus, the Titans, Cronos, Rhea, Zeus, the Cyclopes, and the Titan war through the poem.
- Cosmic settlement: [[602-greek-myths-zeus-king-of-the-gods-part-1-glt4871750364]] connects Zeus's victory to the division of sea, underworld, and heaven and to his later containment of the Metis prophecy.
- Cultural authority: [[602-greek-myths-zeus-king-of-the-gods-part-1-glt4871750364]] distinguishes the poem's panhellenic influence from priestly enforcement or scriptural exclusivity.

## Qualifications

The episode offers a broad literary history rather than textual criticism of the poem's composition, transmission, manuscript tradition, or relation to every local cult. Similarities with Near Eastern succession stories support comparison but do not by themselves establish a single direct borrowing route.

## What Changed

- Established the poem as a powerful but nonexclusive organizer of Greek divine genealogy and Zeus's rule.

## Relationships

- [[Hesiod]] - poet and narrator associated with the work.
- [[Zeus]] - central ruler whose ascent gives the genealogy its political culmination.
- [[DivineSuccessionMyth]] - repeated overthrow pattern that structures the central generations.
- [[GreekMythology]] - wider variable field that the poem organizes without fully canonizing.
