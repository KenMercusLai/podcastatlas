# Thomas Jefferson Foundation

## Overview
The Thomas Jefferson Foundation appears as the institution cited by the episode as accepting the modern consensus that [[ThomasJefferson|Thomas Jefferson]] fathered [[SallyHemings|Sally Hemings]]'s children.

## Current Profile
The foundation's role in this source is institutional rather than biographical. It marks the point at which the Jefferson-Hemings claim is no longer only a hostile allegation or academic revision but part of the mainstream interpretive frame for Jefferson and [[Monticello]]. That institutional acceptance matters because public history sites help define how founding memory is presented to visitors.

## Key Characteristics
- Institution connected to public interpretation of [[ThomasJefferson|Thomas Jefferson]] and [[Monticello]].
- Cited as accepting the modern Jefferson-Hemings paternity consensus.
- Serves as an institutional marker of historiographical change.
- Connects archival evidence to public-facing [[HistoricalMemoryContest|historical memory contest]].
- Helps make slavery central rather than peripheral to Jefferson interpretation.

## Evidence
- Consensus marker: [[686-jefferson-the-betrayal-of-liberty-part-4-glt6535571769]] says the overwhelming modern consensus that Jefferson fathered Hemings's children is accepted by the Thomas Jefferson Foundation.
- Public history role: [[686-jefferson-the-betrayal-of-liberty-part-4-glt6535571769]] links that acceptance to the wider reassessment of Jefferson's reputation since at least the 1990s.
- Monticello context: [[686-jefferson-the-betrayal-of-liberty-part-4-glt6535571769]] places the foundation's consensus inside a story where Monticello is both architectural monument and plantation.

## Qualifications
The episode uses the foundation as a consensus signal; it does not provide a detailed institutional history. The page should not infer positions beyond the episode's Jefferson-Hemings and Monticello context.
