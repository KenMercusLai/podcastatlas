# Tom Hanks

## Overview
Tom Hanks is an actor, producer, and public-history enthusiast whose Apollo-related work and astronaut conversations inform this episode's account of lunar exploration.

## Current Profile
[[438-the-moonwalkers-with-tom-hanks-glt1422432857]] presents Hanks as an interpretive witness rather than a mission participant or academic historian. He combines childhood memory of the space race, long engagement with [[ApolloProgram|Apollo]], acting research, and an accessible account of technical risk, while the anecdotal basis of some claims remains explicit.

## Key Characteristics
- Experienced the [[Apollo11]] landing at age thirteen after following the space race as current events.
- Connects engineering, science, politics, literature, film, and public memory.
- Uses Apollo-related production research and astronaut conversations to make technical stakes emotionally legible.
- Emphasizes contingency, task focus, and human judgment rather than retrospective inevitability.
- Treats history as memorable storytelling joining individual experience to public events.

## Evidence
### Apollo memory and interpretation
- [[438-the-moonwalkers-with-tom-hanks-glt1422432857]] records his memories of Apollo 8 and 11 and his description of early lunar missions as genuinely precarious.

### Research and performance
- [[438-the-moonwalkers-with-tom-hanks-glt1422432857]] links his portrayal of Jim Lovell to a more tactile understanding of Apollo 13's crisis.

### Historical interests
- [[438-the-moonwalkers-with-tom-hanks-glt1422432857]] records his interest in Calvin Coolidge, Alcoholics Anonymous, and history teaching.

## Qualifications
- Hanks speaks as an informed enthusiast and performer, not as an Apollo participant or independent technical authority.
- Astronaut dialogue, motives, risk estimates, and technical recollections remain source-attributed unless corroborated elsewhere.
- His optimism about renewed lunar travel is conditional on funding and execution.
