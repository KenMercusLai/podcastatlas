# Sayyid Qutb / Sayed Qutb

## Overview

Sayyid Qutb, spelled Sayed Qutb in one source, is represented as an ideological reference point for the [[MuslimBrotherhood]] and as an intellectual precursor to later transnational jihadism.

## Current Profile

The Brotherhood branch uses Qutb to explain a movement rooted in Islamic values, social provision, dignity politics, and community organization rather than only electoral machinery in [[spring-then-fall-a-weakened-muslim-brotherhood-6a7aef1b616564bf00d9b354]]. In that bounded account, opposition to Western civilization helps explain the Brotherhood's worldview but does not turn the movement into a synonym for al-Qaeda.

The 9/11 branch adds a more explicitly intellectual and psychological interpretation in [[95-9-11-glt8208243510]]. The hosts describe Qutb's alienation in the United States, hostility to sexual permissiveness, rejection of Western decadence, and call to return to the Prophet's example. They present his project as universalist because Islam was offered as a remedy for all humanity, and they connect his writings to the ideological lineage later intensified by [[OsamaBinLaden|Osama bin Laden]].

## Key Characteristics

- Ideological reference point for Brotherhood-style religious, social, and political organization.
- Critic of Western civilization and sexual permissiveness in the source accounts.
- Advocate of Islamic renewal framed as a universal human remedy.
- Intellectual precursor invoked to explain later jihadist ideology without collapsing distinct movements together.

## Evidence

### Brotherhood ideology and dignity

- [[spring-then-fall-a-weakened-muslim-brotherhood-6a7aef1b616564bf00d9b354]] links Qutb to Islamic values, welfare, community organization, and restored Arab and Islamic dignity.

### Universalism and jihadist lineage

- [[95-9-11-glt8208243510]] links his American experience, critique of decadence, return-to-origins program, and universal Islamic vision to the later ideology associated with bin Laden.

## Qualifications

Neither source provides a full reading of Qutb's writings, political development, imprisonment, execution, reception, or disputes among Islamists. The psychological and sexual interpretation in the 9/11 episode is explicitly speculative. Qutb's influence should not erase organizational, historical, and doctrinal differences among the Muslim Brotherhood, al-Qaeda, the Taliban, and other Islamist or jihadist movements.
