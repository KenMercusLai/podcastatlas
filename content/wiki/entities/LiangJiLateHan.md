## Overview
梁冀 / Liang Ji is the late-Eastern-Han outer-relative powerholder whose current profile now begins under [[EmperorShunOfHan|汉顺帝]] with a 141 CE office handoff after [[LiangShangLateHan|梁商]] dies. Hanji 821 records Shun appointing Liang Ji as Grand General despite Sima Guang's criticism that Liang's violent character was already known, while [[LiangBuyiLateHan|梁不疑]] becomes Henan Yin. Hanji 822 then shows protected corruption and direct but blocked impeachment: corrupt Nanyang officials bribe Liang for protection, Liang moves [[LiGuLateHan|李固]] away from the case, and [[ZhangGangLateHan|张纲]] accuses Liang and Liang Buyi while the emperor cannot immediately punish them. Later sources escalate those early office-succession, protection, succession, and retaliation patterns into the poisoning of [[EmperorZhiOfHan|汉质帝]], second succession capture, ministerial terror, honor expansion, private extraction, ignored warnings, court ritual breach, kinship surveillance, overlarge reward, frontier-official suppression, imperial marriage, household terror, harem crisis, and palace-coup liquidation.

## Current Profile
[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-821-budong-laoban-xinsi-haixiang-jinsheng-meimen-lmjxr8fdxgw-colrlinmujba74fh|Hanji 821]] supplies the earliest current Liang layer. After Liang Shang dies, Emperor Shun appoints Liang Ji as Grand General and Liang Buyi as Henan Yin. The source preserves Sima Guang's criticism that Shun entrusted top military-political office to Liang despite already knowing Liang's violent character, so Liang's profile begins with risky outer-relative promotion before the later corruption and succession-control layers.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-822-chailang-dangdao-ta-jiang-maotou-zhizhi-huangdi-yuefu-lqhpmayvd3p07byr-zoiv-jyhdv|Hanji 822]] supplies the next Liang layer. Liang is already powerful enough that corrupt Nanyang officials seek his protection through bribery; when Li Gu intensifies the investigation, Liang has Li Gu transferred to Taishan. The same source then shows Zhang Gang refusing a routine inspection circuit, burying his wheel in Luoyang, and directly impeaching Liang Ji and Liang Buyi on fifteen major charges. Emperor Shun knows the charges are true but cannot immediately punish them because Liang-family harem favor and court networks are too strong.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-825-lishi-gaosu-ni-dang-ni-meiyong-shi-renxing-zui-zisi-luv-8z9axuejnfhlkgsuxee3uked|Hanji 825]] supplies the first accession layer before the later Emperor Zhi poisoning. After Emperor Chong dies, Li Gu urges Liang to choose a mature and worthy ruler, especially the respected [[LiuSuanLateHan|刘蒜]], rather than a child who will require others to rule for him. Liang does not adopt the advice; he and Liang Na choose Liu Zuan, the eight-year-old future Emperor Zhi. When Li Gu's later cleanup removes harmful eunuchs and irregular officials, the dismissed officials' anonymous letters align with Liang's dissatisfaction and give him a pressure channel against Li Gu.

The 145 CE layer makes Liang's retaliation style visible before the Emperor Zhi crisis. [[LiuJunshiLateHan|刘君世]] presents Liang with a gold patterned snake; [[ZhongHaoLateHan|种暠]] reports the gift, and Liang resents him. When [[FuZhiLateHan|服直]] rebels in [[BaCommanderyLateHan|巴郡]], Liang uses Zhong Hao and [[YingChengLateHan|应承]]'s failed suppression as an arrest channel until Li Gu's memorial and [[EmpressDowagerLiangNaLateHan|梁妠]]'s order reduce the penalty. Liang also resents Du Qiao after Du refuses to lend the gold snake and does not attend mourning for Liang's daughter.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-827-yi-ju-hua-yinfa-de-diwang-zhisi-lnf6rz1i2vmgsclgiejbh0qevzc2|Hanji 827]] supplies the first imperial-murder layer. Liang is already described as domineering in court under empress-dowager kinship protection; when Emperor Zhi publicly calls him "跋扈将军", Liang treats the remark as evidence that the young ruler will become uncontrollable. He bribes an attendant to poison the emperor's soup cake, blocks the deathbed request for water, and then fears Li Gu's grief and questioning because they threaten to expose the murder.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-828-da-jiangjun-liangji-shanquan-feili-de-donghan-jianchen-llqnqijagtmdlgitulddxspncu0e|Hanji 828]] supplies the immediate succession layer. After Emperor Zhi dies, Cao Teng warns Liang that Liu Suan's severity and clarity would threaten Liang's guests and eunuch wealth; Liang accepts the self-protective logic, pushes the Three Excellencies and Nine Ministers toward Liu Zhi, and then uses [[EmpressDowagerLiangNaLateHan|梁妠 / 梁太后]]'s authority to remove Li Gu after Li Gu continues objecting. The episode also shows Liang ignoring [[ZhuMuLateHan|朱穆]]'s early staff advice to choose upright teachers and qualified court officials for the new emperor.

In [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-829-junzi-weishenme-doubuguo-xiaoren-lthvnstkfu8n9thx1sz6yezl4yqq|Hanji 829]], the first reward-and-resistance layer appears. Huan increases Liang Ji's enfeoffment for accession support and rewards Liang relatives, while Du Qiao criticizes precisely this inversion of merit, office, and favor. Liang clashes with Du Qiao when Du refuses expanded rites for [[EmpressLiangYingLateHan|梁盈]] and refuses to recommend a corrupt shangshu candidate.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-830-donghan-ligu-wo-sui-si-yourong-lt-j7n9gotiw-negqxojoacf6ji|Hanji 830]] gives the first terror-and-legitimacy layer after that. Liang frames Li Gu and Du Qiao as conspirators with [[LiuWenQingheRebelLateHan|刘文]] and [[LiuWeiNanjunRebelLateHan|刘伟]], is temporarily checked when petitions and Liang Na's order release Li Gu, then reasserts the case until both men die in prison. His public display of their bodies and ban on mourning aim to convert death into intimidation, but [[GuoLiangLiGuStudent|郭亮]], [[DongBanNanyangLateHan|董班]], and [[YangKuangDuQiaoRetainer|杨匡]] make the scene a reputation defeat for Liang instead.

Hanji 833 and Hanji 834 show Liang power expanding after the dowager transition. Emperor Huan increases Liang's food-estate base, [[SunShouLateHan|孙寿]] receives revenue-bearing honor, [[QinGongLateHan|秦功]] turns household service into office-access brokerage, and private estates become sites of coercion, shelter, seizure, and death-penalty danger. Zhu Mu warns Liang to reduce extraction and unfit appointments, but Liang treats the advice as insult while [[ChenFanLateHan|陈蕃]] and [[YanDuLateHan|延笃]] only achieve bounded proxy-enforcement checks.

Hanji 835, Hanji 836, Hanji 838, and Hanji 844 make the middle Liang profile one of public breach, family policing, reward appetite, and anti-Liang witness suppression. [[ZhangLingLateHan|张陵]] forces Liang to remove a sword at the 151 CE court audience, yet punishment is limited. Liang then monitors [[LiangBuyiLateHan|梁不疑]]'s household and punishes [[MaRongLateHan|马融]] and [[TianMingLateHan|田明]] for visiting him. Huan grants Liang extraordinary honors even after [[HuangQiongLateHan|黄琼]] narrows the territorial part, and [[ChenGuiDuliaoGeneralLateHan|陈龟]] later dies after an ignored anti-Liang memorial.

Hanji 845 through Hanji 848 supply the household-to-coup sequence. Liang Ying's family-backed imperial marriage places Liang power inside Huan's household; Hanji 846 records the clan's saturation of marquisates, harem titles, generals-in-chief, offices, palace surveillance, tribute priority, protected retainers, and retaliatory killings; Hanji 847 shows Sun Shou and [[EmpressDengHuanLateHan|邓皇后]] turning sexual and kinship conflict into coup opportunity; and Hanji 848 records the terminal palace move that seals communications, seizes command symbols, forces Liang's suicide, and removes the wider Liang office network.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-853-jiemi-hanchao-ban-de-zhaoshi-gu-er-lvsa2pby8iyfevfoobobpik52mij|Hanji 853]] preserves the family-aftershock of the Li Gu struggle. It shows Liang repeatedly escalating from policy defeat to imprisonment and execution once Li Gu threatens outer-relative control over succession and legitimacy, while Li Gu's surviving line later becomes an object of imperial repair.

## Key Characteristics
- Overmighty outer-relative whose Grand General appointment follows a known-risk family succession before his protection network shields corrupt officials and later imperial murder.
- Direct impeachment target whose guilt can be recognized by the ruler without producing immediate punishment.
- Serial succession operator who first chooses a controllable child after Emperor Chong's death and later turns the vacancy created by Emperor Zhi's murder into another self-protective ruler-selection decision.
- Court operator who treats succession legitimacy, upright ministers, ritual limits, and appointment quality as structural threats to Liang-family control.
- Retaliatory-terror actor who punishes corruption exposure, failed service, warning, lawful enforcement, omitted deference, property refusal, social contact, and moral criticism.
- Household ruler whose spouse, retainers, estate, access brokers, and kinship network turn private status into public coercion.
- Coup target whose defeat requires secrecy, palace communication control, command-symbol seizure, and rapid liquidation of clan offices.

## Qualifications
This profile is a bounded synthesis of the listed podcast source notes, not a complete biography or institutional monograph. Hanji 821 gives the Grand General succession and known-risk appointment layer; Hanji 822 gives the protected-corruption, Zhang Gang impeachment, and blocked-legal-response layer under Emperor Shun; Hanji 825 gives the first child-accession and anonymous-slander pressure layer after Emperor Chong's death; Hanji 826 gives the gold-snake bribery, Zhong Hao resentment, failed-service arrest, and Du Qiao omitted-deference layer; Hanji 827 gives the direct poisoning and cover-up layer; Hanji 828 gives the later accession-choice and first Li Gu removal layer; Hanji 829 and Hanji 830 follow as reward, resistance, and terror layers; Hanji 853 reconstructs family consequences. Hanji 833-846 preserve source-scoped numbers, property figures, legal forms, and victim details. Xia Mi, Gao Ci, Chong Hao, Wu Xiong, Zhang Gang's exact fifteen charges, Liu Junshi's later fate, Fu Zhi's suppression outcome, Liu Zuan's detailed lineage, and the anonymous letters' exact wording remain source-scoped. The existing timing tension remains between [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-847-nvren-biande-haoming-cong-dongde-zhe-liang-zi-kaishi-lrlov43jtz0vq-o-5m8r2cqo0etx|Hanji 847]], where Xuan survives a failed assassination attempt and helps catalyze the anti-Liang move, and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-848-shunjian-kuatai-wo-weihe-shuo-ta-si-you-yu-gu-lgqvgmxlwga2fknmtxpj3ya9chk0|Hanji 848]], where Xuan is compressed into a death-triggered lead-up.
