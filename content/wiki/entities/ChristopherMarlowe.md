## Overview

Christopher Marlowe was an English Renaissance playwright born in 1564 whose drama appears in the wiki through confessional memory and philosophy of technology.

## Current Profile

One evidence branch uses Marlowe's play about the [[StBartholomewsDayMassacre]] to show how French violence entered English Protestant memory. The drama becomes part of the warning environment around [[FrancisWalsingham]], [[WilliamCecil|William Cecil / Lord Burleigh]], and later Elizabethan anti-Catholic security practice.

The newer branch centers [[DoctorFaustusMarlowe|*Doctor Faustus*]]. It presents Marlowe as a dramatist of unlimited ambition, temptation, spectacle, and the reversal of apparent mastery into dependency. This does not turn him into a modern technology theorist, but the play supplies a durable case for distinguishing knowledge, power, purpose, desire, and freedom.

## Key Characteristics

- English Renaissance playwright working through tragic ambition, temptation, violence, and power.
- Born in 1564 and reported by the source to have died at twenty-nine after being stabbed in a dispute.
- Dramatic contributor to English Protestant memory of the St Bartholomew's Day Massacre.
- Author of a Faustus tragedy that should not be collapsed into Goethe's later account of striving.
- Literary source for examining how command over means can coexist with loss of direction and freedom.

## Qualifications

The profile remains limited to two podcast notes and is not a full literary biography, textual history, or account of Marlowe's complete dramatic corpus. The massacre-memory and technology-philosophy uses are interpretive contexts, and the modern technology analogy should not be treated as a claim that Marlowe predicted AI or biotechnology.

- Preserved the difference between Marlowe's tragic Faustus and Goethe's later tradition.
