## Overview
Vienna / 维也纳 is a recurring wiki city that now carries four distinct source roles: symbolic military memory, elite European cultural life, large-scale housing policy, and late-Habsburg court crisis.

## Current Profile
The current synthesis treats Vienna as a city where institutions make abstract historical and economic claims concrete. In one source, the 1683 siege stands for totemic memory in [[SiegeTypology]]. In another, the city is [[StefanZweig|Stefan Zweig / 茨威格]]'s cultural world in [[TheWorldOfYesterday|《昨日的世界》]]. In the Planet Money Summer School finale, Vienna becomes a housing-market example through [[ViennaSocialHousingScale]]. The Mayerling sequence now gives Vienna a fuller late-Habsburg role: Ringstrasse grandeur, the [[HofburgPalace]], public theatre, Jewish cultural and professional life, new technology, nationalism, court secrecy, Catholic ritual, public shock, and psychological language turn private tragedy into dynastic crisis.

## Key Characteristics
- Vienna can function as a totemic siege reference, where later symbolic meaning matters alongside battlefield history.
- It is a cultural setting for [[BelleEpoque]] memory, elite mobility, salons, literature, language, and the fragility of cosmopolitan Europe.
- It is also a contemporary policy case where social housing reaches enough scale to affect the wider rental market.
- Its housing model combines stability and supply with fairness concerns when higher-income residents keep very low-rent apartments.
- It is a late-Habsburg modern stage where Ringstrasse institutions, artistic modernity, Jewish professional-cultural life, and early communications technology coexist with nationalism and antisemitism.
- It is a court-crisis setting where Hofburg access, newspapers, crowds, Catholic ritual, and fin-de-siecle inner-life language shape the [[MayerlingIncident]].

## Qualifications
Each source is narrow. The siege source is classificatory, not a full military history; the Zweig source is a literary-cultural frame, not a complete city history; the housing example is a brief listener-selected policy case, not a complete fiscal or eligibility study; and the Mayerling sources are court-scandal interpretations, not a full urban history of Vienna.
