# Mary of Guise

## Overview

Mary of Guise is the French-born queen mother and later regent who first protected [[MaryQueenOfScots|Mary, Queen of Scots]] from an English-controlled marriage, secured her French future, and later lost the Franco-Scottish order to Protestant noble revolt and English intervention.

## Current Profile

The infancy episode presents Mary of Guise as an effective dynastic operator before she held the regency. After [[JamesVOfScotland|James V]]'s death, she secured her daughter at Stirling, prevented [[JamesHamiltonEarlOfArran|Arran]] from implementing the [[TreatyOfGreenwich1543|Treaty of Greenwich]], balanced Arran, [[MatthewStewartEarlOfLennox|Lennox]], and other nobles, and gained a formal place beside the regent in council and Parliament.

The [[RoughWooing|Rough Wooing]] strengthened her French strategy. She avoided blame borne by Arran, protected Mary as English armies devastated Scotland, and after [[BattleOfPinkie|Pinkie]] helped convert French military intervention into the Dauphin betrothal. Sending Mary to France removed the child queen from immediate English reach but also made her future dependent on French royal power.

The later episode presents Mary of Guise as a dynastic governor more than a simple religious zealot. She displaced Arran as regent in 1554, strengthened French influence, and governed while her daughter was being formed at the French court. French troops and centralizing policy initially secured the regime but eventually helped anti-French and Protestant opposition converge.

After [[ElizabethI|Elizabeth I]]'s accession, Scotland bordered a Protestant monarchy willing to support the Lords of the Congregation. Mary of Guise retained some loyal nobles, including [[JamesHepburnEarlOfBothwell|Bothwell]], but the English fleet and army isolated her position at Leith. Her death in June 1560 preceded the [[TreatyOfEdinburgh|Treaty of Edinburgh]], French withdrawal, and the collapse of the political order she had defended.

## Key Characteristics

- Guise dynast who protected the infant queen's custody and defeated the first English marriage project.
- Coalition-builder who moved from influence beside Arran to the Scottish regency.
- Strategist who turned English coercion and Scottish defeat into French intervention and Mary's removal to safety.
- Catholic ruler presented as prioritizing dynasty, France, and Scottish control alongside confession.
- Regent whose French troops and centralization helped unite religious and national opposition.
- Mother separated from Mary by the demands of government and Anglo-French rivalry.
- Defeated political center whose death immediately preceded the Treaty of Edinburgh settlement.

## Qualifications

The profile is grounded in two narrative podcast episodes. Its distinction between dynastic strategy and religious zeal is interpretive, and it does not settle Mary of Guise's private beliefs, the relative weight of French pressure and Scottish agency, her personal responsibility for every policy, or the motives of nobles who changed sides.

## What Changed

- Extended Mary of Guise's profile before the regency to custody politics, the defeat of Greenwich, and formal entry into government.
- Made Mary's French upbringing the result of Guise strategy under English military pressure rather than a merely cultural or familial choice.
