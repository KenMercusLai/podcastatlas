## Overview

乐恢 / Le Hui appears as an Eastern Han official whose remonstrance against [[DouXianEasternHan|Dou Xian]] turns from bureaucratic exposure into personal danger. The current wiki evidence is source-bounded to [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-764-ta-weile-huoming-jing-jianjie-mie-le-luoma-diguo-lpk0c6muneabhk1oonzyjvawwf4|Hanji 764]], where Le Hui exposes officials, advises the emperor's maternal uncles to yield power, retires after receiving no result, and dies by suicide after Dou Xian secretly pressures local authorities.

## Current Profile

Le Hui's profile is defined by the risk of honest remonstrance under outer-relative dominance. As shangshu puye, he repeatedly exposes officials and becomes hated by Dou Xian's bloc. His memorial does not attack only from a moral height; it warns the powerful uncles around the young ruler that they can preserve honor and wealth only by reducing their grip before resentment and danger accumulate.

The source then makes Le Hui a deterrent example. After the memorial has no effect, he retires sick to Changling. Dou Xian privately instructs local authorities to harass him, and Le Hui kills himself. The episode says other officials become afraid after his death, so his page belongs to both remonstrance and [[RetaliatoryPoliticalTerror|retaliatory political terror]].

## Key Characteristics

- Remonstrating official who exposes abuses inside a Dou-dominated court.
- Adviser who frames power-yielding as self-preservation for the emperor's maternal uncles.
- Victim of indirect retaliation after retiring from office.
- Negative signal to other officials that even principled withdrawal may not remove danger.
- Court-feedback figure whose death narrows the space for open criticism.

## Qualifications

This page does not reconstruct Le Hui's full career. It records only the bounded Hanji 764 layer, and treats the causal chain from Dou Xian's private pressure to Le Hui's death as the source's account rather than independently corroborated biography.
