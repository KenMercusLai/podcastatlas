# Tiresias

## Overview

Tiresias is a Greek mythic prophet represented here as the dead guide who makes [[Odysseus]]' return conditional and the blind truth-teller who exposes [[Oedipus]]' ignorance.

## Current Profile

In [[687-the-odyssey-hero-of-the-trojan-horse-part-1-glt2393125423]], Odysseus must enter the underworld and let the dead drink sacrificial blood before Tiresias can speak. The prophet warns him not to harm the cattle of [[Helios]] and foretells the late, miserable, companionless return that follows the crew's disobedience.

In [[603-greek-myths-the-riddle-of-the-sphinx-part-2-glt9108845593]], Oedipus summons Tiresias to identify Laius's killer. Tiresias initially refuses, then answers accusation by declaring that Oedipus is the offender he seeks. The scene reverses ordinary assumptions about knowledge: the physically blind prophet sees the truth, while the sighted ruler misreads prophecy as conspiracy until recognition leads him to blind himself.

## Key Characteristics

- Prophetic figure whose knowledge makes heroic outcomes conditional rather than secure.
- Underworld guide who links truth to mortality, sacrifice, taboo, and return.
- Reluctant Theban truth-teller whose disclosure threatens the ruler asking for it.
- Blind seer used to separate physical sight from knowledge and self-understanding.
- Figure whose warnings do not remove human responsibility for later choices.

## Evidence

- Underworld consultation: [[687-the-odyssey-hero-of-the-trojan-horse-part-1-glt2393125423]] sends Odysseus to Hades for Tiresias's warning about Helios's cattle and the return.
- Theban accusation: [[603-greek-myths-the-riddle-of-the-sphinx-part-2-glt9108845593]] has Tiresias identify Oedipus as Laius's killer after Oedipus accuses him of complicity.
- Sight and knowledge: [[603-greek-myths-the-riddle-of-the-sphinx-part-2-glt9108845593]] contrasts Tiresias's blindness with Oedipus's ignorance and later self-blinding.

## Qualifications

The current page covers selected Odyssean and Sophoclean roles, not every version of Tiresias's gender transformation, prophetic origin, lifespan, or Theban appearances. The blindness-versus-insight contrast is dramatically central but should not be generalized into a claim about blindness outside the mythic and literary context.

## What Changed

- Expanded Tiresias from an Odyssean underworld guide into a Theban tragic truth-teller.
- Added the sight-versus-knowledge contrast and his reluctant disclosure to Oedipus.

## Relationships

- [[TheOdyssey]] - epic where Tiresias's warning structures the cattle disaster and return.
- [[Odysseus]] - traveler who seeks his underworld guidance.
- [[Oedipus]] - ruler who demands truth and then rejects its first direct statement.
- [[OedipusTyrannos]] - tragedy where Tiresias's accusation begins the recognition sequence.
- [[Helios]] - deity whose cattle supply the taboo in Tiresias's Odyssean warning.
- [[GreekTragedy]] - dramatic field in which prophetic knowledge collides with political authority.
