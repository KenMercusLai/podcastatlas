# Lords Appellant

## Overview

The Lords Appellant were the magnate coalition that prosecuted [[RichardIIOfEngland|Richard II's]] favorites, defeated Robert de Vere's force, and imposed political control on the king during the crisis of 1387-1388.

## Current Profile

The coalition began with Thomas of Woodstock, Earl of Arundel, and the Earl of Warwick, then expanded to Thomas Mowbray and [[HenryIVOfEngland|Henry Bolingbroke]]. Their appeal against Richard's favorites followed the Wonderful Parliament's attempt to inspect royal finances and constrain government. After Bolingbroke defeated de Vere at Radcot Bridge, Richard surrendered and the Merciless Parliament executed, condemned, or removed members of his circle.

The coalition did not remain united. Military failure weakened its older leaders, while Richard rewarded Mowbray and Bolingbroke and patiently recovered authority in 1389. His later retaliation against Gloucester, Arundel, and Warwick destroyed the possibility of reconciliation and made Mowbray and Bolingbroke fear that former Appellants could also be eliminated.

## Key Characteristics

- Magnate coalition using legal appeal, armed force, and Parliament against royal favorites.
- Constitutional challenge focused on ministers, finance, and control of government rather than an immediate settled plan to abolish kingship.
- Military victor at Radcot Bridge whose dominance culminated in the Merciless Parliament.
- Internally divided alliance that Richard weakened through time, office, and selective favor.
- Object of Richard's delayed revenge, making the 1397-1399 crisis inseparable from the humiliations of 1387-1388.

## Evidence

- Coalition formation: [[415-the-murder-of-richard-ii-part-3-glt6435684837]] identifies the initial three Appellants and the later accession of Mowbray and Bolingbroke.
- Military and parliamentary control: [[415-the-murder-of-richard-ii-part-3-glt6435684837]] links Radcot Bridge, Richard's surrender, and the Merciless Parliament's punishment of his favorites.
- Fragmentation: [[415-the-murder-of-richard-ii-part-3-glt6435684837]] describes Arundel's loss of prestige and Richard's use of honors to separate the younger Appellants from the older leaders.
- Revenge cycle: [[415-the-murder-of-richard-ii-part-3-glt6435684837]] connects the 1397 arrests, executions, confiscations, and Gloucester's suspicious death to later fear among Mowbray and Bolingbroke.

## Qualifications

The episode does not establish a single coherent constitutional program shared by all five lords across the entire crisis. Their motives, any serious deposition plan in 1387, responsibility for particular sentences, and the circumstances of Gloucester's death remain qualified.

## What Changed

- Added the coalition as the institutional and military bridge between Richard's early dependence and his later revenge-driven personal rule.
