# Capitol Hill Organized Protest (CHOP)

The Capitol Hill Organized Protest, or CHOP, appears in [[20260721-pmoney-new-npr-series-we-keep-us-safe-from-the-embedded-podcast-trailer-20260721-pmoney-new-npr-series-we-keep-us-safe-from-the-embedded-podcast-trailer]] as the Seattle protest zone at the center of [[WeKeepUsSafe]]. The trailer says CHOP began in 2020 after police abandoned a precinct during a standoff with anti-police-brutality protesters.

The source describes CHOP as about eight occupied city blocks with tents, free food, mutual aid, speeches, and music. It also treats CHOP as a [[PoliceFreeProtestSpace|police-free protest space]] whose safety experiment became unstable when fear of attack, armed self-appointed security, and a Jeep entering the camp led to the shooting death of [[AntonioMaysJr|Antonio Mays Jr.]]

## Key Claims
- CHOP is framed as both protest and an attempt to model a different community after police withdrawal.
- The trailer says protesters feared right-wing attack and that some armed themselves as security.
- The Antonio Mays Jr. shooting is presented as the unresolved tragedy around which [[WillJames]] and [[SydneyBrounstone]] reopen the story.
