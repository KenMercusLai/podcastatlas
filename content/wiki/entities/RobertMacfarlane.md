# Robert Macfarlane / 罗伯特·麦克法伦

## Overview
Robert Macfarlane / 罗伯特·麦克法伦 appears in [[episode-225-yidai-yilu-8212959555-438866|Episode 225: 一袋一录]] through [[IsARiverAlive|Is a River Alive?]], which the host recommends as a river, life, and nature-writing case.

## Current Profile
The source presents Macfarlane as a writer whose work can feel bag-like rather than spear-like. His writing bends into maps, nautical knowledge, natural detail, and other materials, but the host treats those apparent digressions as part of a larger relational structure rather than as failures of plot.

## Key Characteristics
- Writes nature and landscape through associative, layered structures.
- Serves in the episode as a contemporary example of [[CarrierBagNarrative|carrier-bag narrative]].
- Uses rivers to ask whether more-than-human systems can be understood as living beings.
- Carries macro scale through geology, water bodies, mountains, and ecological time without reducing narrative to one heroic arc.

River-writing case:
- [[episode-225-yidai-yilu-8212959555-438866|Episode 225: 一袋一录]] introduces [[IsARiverAlive|Is a River Alive?]] as Macfarlane's work on whether a river is alive and strongly recommends it.

Bag-like prose:
- [[episode-225-yidai-yilu-8212959555-438866|Episode 225: 一袋一录]] says criticism of Macfarlane often treats his writing as insufficiently spear-like because it curves into map, navigation, or other materials.

Macro narrative without monopoly:
- [[episode-225-yidai-yilu-8212959555-438866|Episode 225: 一袋一录]] argues that Macfarlane can still produce grandeur through geological time, large waters, and mountains while resisting a single narrative shape.
