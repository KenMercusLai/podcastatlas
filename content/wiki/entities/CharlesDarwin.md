# Charles Darwin / 查尔斯·达尔文

## Overview

Charles Darwin is represented across the wiki as an evolutionist, experimental naturalist, scientific dissenter, influence on later hereditarian thought, and observer of continuity between humans and other animals.

## Current Profile

The sources present Darwin less through one complete biography than through several modes of inquiry. His [[HMSBeagle|Beagle]] voyage begins with geology, fossils, landscapes, and field collection; in the [[GalapagosIslands|Galapagos Islands]] he preserves island-specific evidence without immediately understanding all of it. His earthworm experiments ask narrow sensory questions and separate hearing from vibration; his encouragement of Charles Harrison Blackley's pollen work supports careful exposure and measurement; and his encounters with [[JennyOrangutan|Jenny the orangutan]] use emotion, jealousy, tools, and mirror response to test the human-animal boundary.

The Galapagos source makes Darwin's theory formation gradual and collaborative. Mockingbird differences were clearer to him than the confusing finches, [[JohnGould]] supplied later specialist classification, and Darwin's published species-modification language became more explicit over time. Darwin also functions as a public-strategy case: the principled-disobedience source emphasizes credibility, timing, accessible prose, and allies in making evolutionary dissent travel. The intelligence-testing source then draws an important boundary: Darwin influenced [[FrancisGalton]]'s hereditarian turn, but Darwin's caution should not be collapsed into later [[SocialDarwinism|social-Darwinist]] policy. The free-will source places evolution in a still broader shift toward [[BiologicalAgency]], evolved meaning, and embodied action.

## Key Characteristics

- Experimental naturalist who tested precise behavioral questions before inference.
- Field naturalist whose unresolved specimens and island labels retained value beyond his first interpretation.
- Observer of cross-species continuity in worms, orangutans, and evolutionary life.
- Strategic scientific dissenter who joined evidence to communication, timing, credibility, and allies.
- Background influence on heredity debates whose own claims remain distinct from later eugenic and social-Darwinist uses.
- Boundary figure connecting evolution to agency, embodiment, meaning, and the human-animal relationship.

## Qualifications

These sources use selected episodes from Darwin's life to support broader arguments; they do not constitute a full account of his science, correspondence, colonial setting, moral views, or intellectual development. The Galapagos source is a conversational outline and explicitly rejects a one-place, one-moment discovery story; specimen counts, taxonomy, publication wording, and the causal weight of individual observations remain source-scoped. Jenny's significance is likewise interpreted through a narrative-history episode. Distinguishing Darwin from later social Darwinism does not settle every historical debate about heredity, race, hierarchy, or the uses of evolution.

## What Changed

- Added the Beagle-to-Gould evidence chain behind Darwin's gradual evolutionary interpretation.
- Replaced the instant Galapagos discovery myth with a collaborative, multi-stage theory-formation account.
- Preserved the distinction between biological natural selection and later social-Darwinist policy.
