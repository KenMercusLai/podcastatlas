# Omar al-Bashir

## Overview

Omar al-Bashir is the Sudanese ruler whose removal anchors the episode's qualified case of successful nonviolent resistance followed by an unstable political transition.

## Current Profile

The episode connects protests against Bashir to 2018 fuel and food price increases, strikes, demonstrations, marches, sit-ins, and broader noncooperation organized in part by the [[SudaneseProfessionalsAssociation]]. His removal met one immediate campaign objective, but protesters continued demanding civilian representation from the military council that followed.

That sequence makes Bashir's case important for separating ruler removal from regime transformation. [[EricaChenoweth|Erica Chenoweth]] counts the campaign as successful under the study's near-term criteria, while renewed military control and later turmoil show that such classification does not establish durable democracy.

## Key Characteristics

- Sudanese authoritarian ruler removed amid mass civil resistance.
- Depended on institutions and constituencies vulnerable to organized noncooperation.
- Immediate removal did not itself produce stable civilian rule.
- Case exposing the difference between campaign success and democratic consolidation.
