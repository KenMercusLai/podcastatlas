# French National Guard

## Overview

The French National Guard began in the July 1789 Paris crisis as a bourgeois militia intended to give civic authorities an organized armed force between unreliable royal troops and an uncontrolled crowd.

## Current Profile

The source traces the institution from the Garde Bourgeoise formed by representatives gathering at the Hotel de Ville after royal authority collapsed in Paris. Its creation shows municipal elites attempting to channel popular arming into a civic order. The militia adopted Paris's red and blue before [[MarquisDeLafayette|Lafayette]] added Bourbon white to form the tricolour cockade and took command. Its origin was therefore double: it embodied the new nation and municipal revolution, but it also existed to contain the same street force that made its creation possible.

## Key Characteristics

- Civic militia formed during the collapse of royal military control in Paris.
- Institutional response to mass arming and urban disorder.
- Initially associated with bourgeois municipal authority.
- Adopted red and blue Paris colors before the tricolour synthesis.
- Commanded by Lafayette after the Bastille's fall.
- Joined revolutionary legitimacy to the task of preserving order.

## Qualifications

- This source establishes the Paris origin and immediate symbolic settlement, not a complete national or chronological history of the Guard.
- “Bourgeois” describes the episode's institutional formation frame and does not establish every member's class position.
- The tricolour's precise design process and contemporary meanings remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Establishes the Guard's origin as an attempt to institutionalize revolutionary arms while restraining uncontrolled street violence.
