# Frank Smith

## Overview
Frank Smith appears as the American prisoner whose more than 70 years behind bars make the episode's criminal-justice segment less about a solved case than about time, doubt, commutation, and institutionalization.

## Current Profile
Smith's profile is source-scoped to [[bargaining-chips-nvidia-is-the-bank-of-ai-6a9a86ffe7a9fe2478c4fc41]]. The episode says he was convicted in the 1949 murder of night watchman Grover Hart at the Indian Harbor Yacht Club in Greenwich, Connecticut. His death sentence was commuted in 1954 after doubts and a campaign by a prison officer, but he did not receive the second trial he wanted. The result is a life story suspended between survival from execution and unresolved exoneration.

## Key Characteristics
- Long-serving American prisoner whose incarceration lasted more than seven decades.
- Convicted in a murder case that the source presents as shadowed by factual and procedural doubt.
- Death-row prisoner whose final meal was reportedly served repeatedly before commutation.
- Recipient of mercy that did not produce a new trial or public proof of innocence.
- Person whose later escape, short parole failure, and nursing-home life show the difficulty of reentry after nearly total institutional confinement.

## Evidence
- Conviction and doubt: [[bargaining-chips-nvidia-is-the-bank-of-ai-6a9a86ffe7a9fe2478c4fc41]] recounts the 1949 Greenwich murder case, Hart's description of his attacker, Smith's disputed alibi, and the later doubts that led to commutation.
- Mercy without exoneration: [[bargaining-chips-nvidia-is-the-bank-of-ai-6a9a86ffe7a9fe2478c4fc41]] says Smith's death sentence was commuted in 1954 but his requested second trial never occurred.
- Institutionalization and reentry: [[bargaining-chips-nvidia-is-the-bank-of-ai-6a9a86ffe7a9fe2478c4fc41]] describes his 1967 escape, brief 1975 parole, later return to custody, and eventual life in a nursing home for released prisoners.

## Qualifications
The page does not decide Smith's guilt or innocence. It records how the episode frames the case: legally convicted, spared execution, denied a new trial, and eventually released into a life shaped by extreme duration of confinement.
