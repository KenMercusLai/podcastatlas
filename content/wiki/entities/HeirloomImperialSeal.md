# 传国玉玺 / Heirloom Imperial Seal

## Overview
传国玉玺 / Heirloom Imperial Seal is the late-Han imperial object whose movement through loss, recovery, coercive possession, failed usurpation, and return to court shows that symbols matter politically but cannot substitute for accepted legitimacy.

## Current Profile
The bounded sources present the seal as powerful precisely because it remains incomplete without a functioning title order. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-947-dongzhuo-shoufu-lvbu-kaiqi-chengba-zhilu-lvr-24l6j8f6zvpfcmqgknmgywa1|Hanji 947]] first marks its disappearance after the palace crisis, showing that imperial symbols can be physically exposed by disorder. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-955-guanyu-zhan-huaxiong-qishi-shi-wei-lishi-lndkrj-fnepye8iu-ifgyh-vzysf|Hanji 955]] then has [[SunJianLateHan|孙坚]] recover it in ruined [[LuoyangLateHan|洛阳]], making recovery look like ritual restoration rather than a mandate by itself. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1001-weishenme-quan-ni-buyao-fanzhongnu-lolurkcjpie14nnczk0tu7iqerke|Hanji 1001]] shows [[YuanShu|袁术]] treating the seal as support for imperial ambition, but the source's advisers insist that virtue, title order, and people-support are still missing. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1033-geju-junfa-yuanshu-zuizhong-tuxue-er-wang-lt0ibs-en0oeoqkkcxl8sevjng4|Hanji 1033]] adds the endpoint: after Yuan Shu dies, [[XuQiuGuanglingLateHan|徐秋]] sends the seal back to the court rather than turning it into a successor claim.

## Key Characteristics
- Material carrier of imperial legitimacy whose absence, possession, and return all affect political meaning.
- Object separated from stable court capacity during late-Han disorder.
- Recovered by Sun Jian after anti-Dong-Zhuo fighting and ritual cleanup at Luoyang.
- Misused by Yuan Shu as evidence for a title claim that lacks accepted moral and political backing.
- Returned to the recognized court after Yuan Shu's death, closing the source arc of his failed symbolic claim.

## Evidence
- Loss and vulnerability: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-947-dongzhuo-shoufu-lvbu-kaiqi-chengba-zhilu-lvr-24l6j8f6zvpfcmqgknmgywa1|Hanji 947]] says five seals are recovered after the emperor returns, while the Heirloom Imperial Seal remains missing.
- Ritual recovery: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-955-guanyu-zhan-huaxiong-qishi-shi-wei-lishi-lndkrj-fnepye8iu-ifgyh-vzysf|Hanji 955]] says Sun Jian finds the seal in a palace well after entering Luoyang, cleaning the imperial temples, and performing sacrifice.
- False confidence: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1001-weishenme-quan-ni-buyao-fanzhongnu-lolurkcjpie14nnczk0tu7iqerke|Hanji 1001]] says Yuan Shu combines the seal with prophecy and five-phases reasoning, while Yan Xiang and Zhang Cheng warn that symbols and force do not create legitimate rule.
- Return to court: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1033-geju-junfa-yuanshu-zuizhong-tuxue-er-wang-lt0ibs-en0oeoqkkcxl8sevjng4|Hanji 1033]] says Xu Qiu obtains the seal after Yuan Shu's death and presents it to the court.

## Qualifications
The seal is not treated as ornamental, but the bounded sources also reject seal determinism. It can strengthen claims, inspire confidence, and organize symbolic memory, yet Yuan Shu's case shows that possession becomes dangerous when it outruns consent, military capacity, and recognized title order.

## What Changed
- Hanji 1033 adds the post-Yuan-Shu endpoint: the seal returns to court instead of sustaining a Yuan-family successor claim.
- The page is migrated to synthesis-v1 while preserving the prior source order.
