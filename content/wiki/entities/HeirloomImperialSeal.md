## Overview

传国玉玺 / Heirloom Imperial Seal is the imperial object whose movement through coerced transfer, loss, recovery, coercive possession, failed usurpation, and return to court shows that symbols matter politically but cannot substitute for accepted legitimacy.

## Current Profile

The bounded sources present the seal as powerful precisely because it remains incomplete without a functioning title order. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-947-dongzhuo-shoufu-lvbu-kaiqi-chengba-zhilu-lvr-24l6j8f6zvpfcmqgknmgywa1|Hanji 947]] first marks its disappearance after the palace crisis, showing that imperial symbols can be physically exposed by disorder. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-955-guanyu-zhan-huaxiong-qishi-shi-wei-lishi-lndkrj-fnepye8iu-ifgyh-vzysf|Hanji 955]] then has [[SunJianLateHan|孙坚]] recover it in ruined [[LuoyangLateHan|洛阳]], making recovery look like ritual restoration rather than a mandate by itself. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1001-weishenme-quan-ni-buyao-fanzhongnu-lolurkcjpie14nnczk0tu7iqerke|Hanji 1001]] shows [[YuanShu|袁术]] treating the seal as support for imperial ambition, but the source's advisers insist that virtue, title order, and people-support are still missing. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1033-geju-junfa-yuanshu-zuizhong-tuxue-er-wang-lt0ibs-en0oeoqkkcxl8sevjng4|Hanji 1033]] adds the endpoint: after Yuan Shu dies, [[XuQiuGuanglingLateHan|徐秋]] sends the seal back to the court rather than turning it into a successor claim. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-633-nuza-chuanguo-yuxi-ta-jing-ruci-ganglie-lnmnwbk28ayllz5fip1a1kzm1ais|Hanji 633]] backfills an earlier transfer problem: [[WangMang|王莽]] can seize the object from [[WangZhengjun|王政君]], but the broken-corner handover dramatizes that coercive possession is not the same as accepted dynastic continuity.

## Key Characteristics

- Material carrier of imperial legitimacy whose absence, possession, and return all affect political meaning.
- Object whose forced transfer from Wang Zhengjun to Wang Mang shows symbolic continuity under coercion rather than consent.
- Object separated from stable court capacity during late-Han disorder.
- Recovered by Sun Jian after anti-Dong-Zhuo fighting and ritual cleanup at Luoyang.
- Misused by Yuan Shu as evidence for a title claim that lacks accepted moral and political backing.
- Returned to the recognized court after Yuan Shu's death, closing the source arc of his failed symbolic claim.

## Evidence

- Loss and vulnerability: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-947-dongzhuo-shoufu-lvbu-kaiqi-chengba-zhilu-lvr-24l6j8f6zvpfcmqgknmgywa1|Hanji 947]] says five seals are recovered after the emperor returns, while the Heirloom Imperial Seal remains missing.
- Ritual recovery: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-955-guanyu-zhan-huaxiong-qishi-shi-wei-lishi-lndkrj-fnepye8iu-ifgyh-vzysf|Hanji 955]] says Sun Jian finds the seal in a palace well after entering Luoyang, cleaning the imperial temples, and performing sacrifice.
- False confidence: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1001-weishenme-quan-ni-buyao-fanzhongnu-lolurkcjpie14nnczk0tu7iqerke|Hanji 1001]] says Yuan Shu combines the seal with prophecy and five-phases reasoning, while Yan Xiang and Zhang Cheng warn that symbols and force do not create legitimate rule.
- Return to court: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1033-geju-junfa-yuanshu-zuizhong-tuxue-er-wang-lt0ibs-en0oeoqkkcxl8sevjng4|Hanji 1033]] says Xu Qiu obtains the seal after Yuan Shu's death and presents it to the court.
- Coerced accession transfer: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-633-nuza-chuanguo-yuxi-ta-jing-ruci-ganglie-lnmnwbk28ayllz5fip1a1kzm1ais|Hanji 633]] says Wang Zhengjun argues Wang Mang should carve a new seal if his mandate is real, then throws the inherited seal before Wang Shun and breaks a corner.

## Qualifications

The seal is not treated as ornamental, but the bounded sources also reject seal determinism. It can strengthen claims, inspire confidence, and organize symbolic memory, yet Hanji 633 and Yuan Shu's case show that possession becomes dangerous or incomplete when it outruns consent, military capacity, and recognized title order. The Qin-origin and Li Si inscription story remains source-traditional background in Hanji 633 rather than a newly adjudicated antiquarian claim.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 633 as an earlier Xin-accession layer: Wang Mang obtains the seal, but only through Wang Zhengjun's coerced and damaged handover.
- Reframed the seal's profile from a late-Han-only object arc to a broader title-order problem across dynastic replacement and late-Han usurpation.

## Relationships

- [[WangZhengjun|王政君]] - dowager who treats the seal as Han property and damages it while surrendering under pressure.
- [[WangMang|王莽]] - usurper who needs the inherited object even after claiming new mandate signs and remaking institutions.
- [[YuanShu|袁术]] - claimant who treats the seal as support for unsupported imperial ambition.
- [[SunJianLateHan|孙坚]] - figure whose Luoyang branch recovers the seal before Yuan Shu obtains it.
- [[XuQiuGuanglingLateHan|徐秋]] - former Guangling administrator who returns the seal to court in Hanji 1033.
- [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]] - recognized sovereign whose court receives the object after Yuan Shu's collapse.
- [[MingqiLegitimacy|名器合法性]] - concept explaining why symbols matter but cannot replace accepted authority.
- [[AntiUsurpationCoalitionPressure|讨逆同盟压力]] - pressure pattern created when Yuan Shu's symbolic claim challenges the recognized imperial title.
- [[ImperialSymbolicStandardization|帝国符号标准化]] - broader symbolic-order frame for imperial objects and authority.
