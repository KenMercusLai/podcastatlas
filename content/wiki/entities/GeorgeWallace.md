# George Wallace

## Overview

George Wallace appears as the third-party candidate who carried five states and won roughly ten million votes in the [[UnitedStatesPresidentialElection1968|1968 election]].

## Current Profile

Wallace serves both as an electoral force and as the episode's contrast case for explicit racial politics. [[RichardNixon]] sought many of the same anxious Southern and working-class voters but used coded policy signals, national unity, and law-and-order language rather than Wallace's open style.

## Key Characteristics

- Third-party presidential candidate with a substantial popular and regional vote.
- Carried five states in the 1968 election.
- Represented an openly racial and confrontational alternative to the major-party candidates.
- Lost support to Nixon and Humphrey in different regions as the contest tightened.

## Evidence

- Electoral role: [[513-america-in-68-nixons-great-comeback-part-6-glt3853956463]] reports five states and about ten million votes for Wallace.
- Contrast with Nixon: [[513-america-in-68-nixons-great-comeback-part-6-glt3853956463]] distinguishes Wallace's explicit rhetoric from Nixon's coded judicial, busing, federalism, and law-and-order signals.
- Late compression: [[513-america-in-68-nixons-great-comeback-part-6-glt3853956463]] says both major candidates squeezed Wallace's support in different parts of the country.

## Qualifications

This profile is limited to Wallace's role in the Nixon-centered 1968 narrative. It does not provide a full account of his Alabama career, segregationist record, later campaigns, or voter coalition.

## What Changed

- Added Wallace as both a consequential third-party candidate and the episode's benchmark for distinguishing explicit from coded racial appeals.
