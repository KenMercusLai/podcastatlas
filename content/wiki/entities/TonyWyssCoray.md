# Tony Wyss-Coray

## Overview
Tony Wyss-Coray is presented as a Stanford neurology professor studying how circulating factors, cerebrospinal fluid, and protein signatures relate to brain and organ aging.

## Current Profile
In the bounded source, Wyss-Coray connects causal animal experiments with a measurement-and-translation program. His laboratory uses parabiosis and fluid-transfer studies to test whether age- or exercise-associated signals can change old tissues, then uses human proteomics to estimate organ and cell-type aging and future disease risk. He consistently narrows the practical claim: promising mouse results, candidate molecules, plasma fractions, and biological clocks require controlled human studies before becoming therapies or surrogate endpoints.

He also co-founded [[VeroBiosciences]], which is described as applying organ-age signatures alongside clinical and wearable data. This commercial role makes the source informative about intended translation, but it also means performance and utility claims need independent validation.

## Key Characteristics
- Studies blood-borne and cerebrospinal-fluid signals as possible drivers as well as markers of aging.
- Uses animal transfer experiments to test causation before proposing human translation.
- Develops proteomic models of organ- and cell-type aging.
- Frames preserved cognition and organ function as more meaningful than lifespan alone.
- Emphasizes blinded, controlled, sufficiently large clinical trials and warns against unproven cell therapies.
- Combines academic research with a disclosed commercial interest in organ-age measurement.
