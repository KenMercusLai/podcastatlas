# Golden Stool

## Overview

The Golden Stool, or Sika Dwa Kofi, is the sacred Asante object the episode presents as containing the soul of the people and embodying a political community above any individual ruler.

## Current Profile

In the founding tradition, [[OkomfoAnokye]] supersedes local stools and causes one supreme stool to descend to [[OseiTutu]]. Its authority rests on collective identity rather than use as an ordinary seat: nobody sits on it. That distinction explains why the British governor's demand to possess and sit on it was not a routine sovereignty claim but a fundamental insult that helped trigger the [[WarOfTheGoldenStool]]. Its concealment and later British promise of respect allowed symbolic continuity to outlast conquest.

## Key Characteristics

- Supreme sacred symbol of [[Asante]] collective identity.
- Known in the episode as Sika Dwa Kofi, the Golden Stool created on a Friday.
- Said to contain the soul of the people rather than function as an ordinary throne.
- Object that no person, including the king, may sit upon.
- Hidden from British seizure and preserved through colonial rule.

## Evidence

- Founding meaning: [[269-ghana-the-ashanti-empire-glt3638102749]] recounts its descent and says it embodies the soul of the new people.
- Political boundary: [[269-ghana-the-ashanti-empire-glt3638102749]] stresses that nobody sits on the stool, making the governor's 1900 demand a profound violation.
- Survival: [[269-ghana-the-ashanti-empire-glt3638102749]] says the stool was hidden, later found and stripped by workmen, and ultimately protected by a British promise of respect.

## Qualifications

The descent from a dark cloud is presented as sacred origin tradition. The source does not independently establish the object's early chronology, every ritual rule, the details of its concealment, or the later legal terms of protection.

## What Changed

- Created the Golden Stool as the collective-sovereignty symbol linking Asante foundation, resistance, and continuity.

- [[WarOfTheGoldenStool]] - conflict triggered by a British demand that violated the stool's meaning.
