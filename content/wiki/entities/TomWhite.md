# Tom White

## Overview

Tom White was the Bureau of Investigation agent sent in 1925 to investigate the nationally prominent [[OsageMurders]].

## Current Profile

White assembled an undercover team that included John Wren, described as the Bureau's only Native American agent, and operatives able to pose as cowboys or criminals. The group worked through missing evidence, false testimony, corrupt officials, murdered witnesses, and a double agent leaking reports.

The investigation exposed [[WilliamHale]]'s plot and helped secure convictions after bribery and mistrial. Yet the Bureau then declared victory and left, while killings and unresolved cases remained. White's success is therefore real but bounded: he solved a central conspiracy without resolving the full system described by the source.

## Key Characteristics

- Federal investigator entering after years of failed local inquiry.
- Leader of a mixed undercover team using multiple social covers.
- Investigator operating inside a corrupt and dangerous evidentiary environment.
- Builder of the case linking Hale, Ernest, insurance, wills, and headrights.
- Successful case leader whose institution closed the story too narrowly.

## Qualifications

- The source centers White during the investigation but does not provide a full biography or complete Bureau archive.
- Later Bureau institutional self-presentation may overstate finality; the episode explicitly reopens that official ending.
- Solving Hale's plot should not be treated as proving that all perpetrators shared one organization or command structure.
