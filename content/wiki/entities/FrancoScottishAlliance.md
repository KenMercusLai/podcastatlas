# Franco-Scottish Alliance

## Overview

The Franco-Scottish Alliance appears as the 1295 agreement that turns [[Scotland]] into a strategic pressure point in Anglo-French relations before the [[HundredYearsWar|Hundred Years' War]].

## Current Profile

The source presents the alliance as a two-front mechanism rather than a decorative diplomatic fact. If [[England]] attacks either France or Scotland, the other is expected to invade or pressure England. That makes Scottish war inseparable from the legal and territorial dispute over [[Gascony]] and [[Aquitaine]].

The alliance becomes active in Edward III's reign. After [[EdwardIIIOfEngland|Edward III]] supports the Balliol cause and wins at [[HallidonHill|Hallidon Hill]] in 1333, David II flees to France. [[PhilipVIOfFrance|Philip VI]] then insists that any Anglo-French treaty include Scotland and uphold Scottish rights.

## Key Characteristics

- Agreement dated by the source to 1295.
- Strategic link between French and Scottish pressure on England.
- Forces Anglo-French diplomacy to include Scottish rights.
- Makes Edward III's Scottish victories diplomatically costly.
- Connects northern war to Gascony and Aquitaine disputes.
- Helps explain why the Hundred Years' War origin is not only a French succession quarrel.

## Evidence

- Alliance terms: [[318-hundred-years-war-a-game-of-thrones-part-1-glt5584156008]] says France and Scotland agreed that either should invade England if the other was attacked by England.
- Strategic pressure: [[318-hundred-years-war-a-game-of-thrones-part-1-glt5584156008]] identifies Scotland and Gascony as the two major unresolved Anglo-French sore points.
- Hallidon Hill consequence: [[318-hundred-years-war-a-game-of-thrones-part-1-glt5584156008]] says David II flees to France after Edward's victory.
- Diplomatic condition: [[318-hundred-years-war-a-game-of-thrones-part-1-glt5584156008]] says Philip VI insists no Anglo-French treaty can exclude Scotland.
- War causality: [[318-hundred-years-war-a-game-of-thrones-part-1-glt5584156008]] uses the alliance to show how Edward's Scottish policy risks French action against Aquitaine.

## Qualifications

This page covers the alliance only through its role in the 1330s Anglo-French crisis. Later uses of the alliance, Scottish domestic politics, and French military commitments remain outside the current evidence.

## What Changed

- Created the Franco-Scottish Alliance as the two-front diplomatic mechanism in the prewar crisis.
