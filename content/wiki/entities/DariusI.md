# Darius I

## Overview

Darius I appears in the wiki as the Persian king whose desire to punish Athens and Eretria frames the expedition that ends at Marathon.

## Current Profile

The source presents Darius through the logic of imperial retaliation. Athens had supported the Ionian revolt against Persian rule, and Darius cannot ignore that challenge. The expedition destroys Eretria and enslaves many inhabitants before turning toward Athens, where the exiled tyrant [[HippiasAthenianTyrant]] helps make Persian intervention a political as well as military threat.

The episode does not portray Darius' whole reign. His role is to supply the imperial motive behind the campaign and to highlight the difference between Greek memory of existential victory and an empire's possible view of a peripheral setback.

## Key Characteristics

- Darius' Marathon role is defined by revenge for Athenian support of the Ionian revolt.
- His expedition uses punitive violence against Eretria before targeting Athens.
- The campaign combines military invasion with local-political restoration possibilities through Hippias.
- The source keeps Darius inside the larger [[AchaemenidEmpire]] rather than treating Marathon as the empire's only concern.

## Evidence

- Revenge motive: [[669-greece-vs-persia-the-battle-of-marathon-part-2-glt2696648260]] says Darius wanted revenge on Athens for supporting the Ionian revolt.
- Eretria punishment: [[669-greece-vs-persia-the-battle-of-marathon-part-2-glt2696648260]] says the expedition had destroyed Eretria and enslaved inhabitants before moving toward Athens.
- Imperial perspective: [[669-greece-vs-persia-the-battle-of-marathon-part-2-glt2696648260]] notes that Marathon may have been monumental to Greeks while looking peripheral to Persia.

## Qualifications

This profile is limited to Darius' role in the Marathon episode. It does not synthesize his wider administrative, military, or imperial history.
