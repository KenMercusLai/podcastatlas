# Darius I

## Overview

Darius I appears in the wiki as the Persian king who consolidates the [[AchaemenidEmpire]], frames rule through truth and order, suppresses the [[IonianRevolt]], and sends the expedition that ends at Marathon.

## Current Profile

The pre-Marathon source makes Darius more than a revenge figure. It presents him as a distant royal cousin and lance-bearer whose official accession story is suspicious, possibly masking a coup against the real Bardia. After taking power, he spends two years crushing revolts in Persia, Media, and Elam, using mutilation, impalement, and public terror to consolidate authority.

The same source also presents Darius as an administrator and ideological ruler. He organizes tribute, founds Persepolis, stabilizes the empire, and casts himself as the agent of Arta, or truth/order, against the lie. That frame matters for Athens: the burning of [[Sardis]] is not merely a raid but an offense against imperial and cosmic order.

The Marathon battle source then carries this logic into the 490 BC expedition. Darius cannot ignore Athenian and Eretrian support for the Ionian revolt; the expedition destroys Eretria before turning toward Athens, where [[HippiasAthenianTyrant]] makes intervention political as well as military.

## Key Characteristics

- Darius is presented as a possibly illegitimate claimant whose accession story is politically self-serving.
- His early rule depends on brutal revolt suppression and public terror.
- He is also a successful administrator who stabilizes imperial tribute, centers, and display.
- His ideology links Persian rule to truth/order against rebellion and falsehood.
- His Marathon role is defined by revenge for Athenian support of the Ionian revolt and the burning of Sardis.
- The campaign combines punitive violence, island intimidation, religious diplomacy, and local-political restoration possibilities through Hippias.

- Sardis revenge: [[668-greece-vs-persia-the-rise-of-the-first-superpower-part-1-glt8965800182]] uses the "remember the Athenians" story after the burning of Sardis.

## Qualifications

This profile remains bounded to the two Marathon episodes. Darius's accession, religious ideology, revenge psychology, and specific campaign motives are preserved as source reconstructions rather than a complete scholarly biography.

## What Changed

- Expanded Darius from a Marathon revenge node into a fuller pre-Marathon imperial ruler: contested accession, terror, administration, ideology, Sardis, and retaliation.
