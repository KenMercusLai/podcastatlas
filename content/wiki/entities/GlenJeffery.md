# Glen Jeffery

## Overview
Glen Jeffery is the University College London neuroscience professor featured in a [[HubermanLab]] interview about long-wavelength light, mitochondrial function, retinal aging, metabolism, and indoor lighting.

## Current Profile
The source presents Jeffery as an experimental vision and mitochondrial researcher who moves between basic light physics, animal models, small human studies, and built-environment proposals. His central public argument is that red and near-infrared light can support mitochondrial performance and that removing longer wavelengths from indoor environments may create a biologically consequential spectral imbalance.

His account is exploratory rather than a settled clinical protocol. He distinguishes early-stage or aging-related modulation from reversal of advanced disease, acknowledges that the mechanism behind distant effects remains unresolved, and warns against laser exposure and uncritical transfer among devices. At the same time, several population-scale claims about LEDs, architecture, myopia, and disease exceed the methodological detail available in the supplied episode note.

## Key Characteristics
- Studies retinal aging and uses the retina's high metabolic demand as a model for mitochondrial decline.
- Investigates red and near-infrared exposure across vision, glucose response, inflammation, and distant-tissue signaling.
- Proposes that mitochondrial effects may involve the surrounding water environment as well as later changes in electron-transport proteins.
- Treats mitochondria as communicating across tissues rather than acting only as isolated local organelles.
- Advocates more balanced indoor light spectra while emphasizing low intensity, early intervention, and laser safety.
