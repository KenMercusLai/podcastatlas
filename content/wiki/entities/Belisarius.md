# Belisarius

## Overview

Belisarius was [[JustinianI]]'s leading general, instrumental in suppressing the [[NikaRiots|Nika riots]], defeating the Vandals, and winning early victories in Italy while remaining vulnerable to the political suspicion created by military prestige.

## Current Profile

The sources present Belisarius first as an internal regime defender and then as the operational instrument of Justinian's western restoration. During the Nika crisis, troops under Belisarius and Mundus entered the Hippodrome from opposite directions and carried out the decisive massacre. His North African campaign then succeeded with exceptional speed, and he captured Naples and Rome in Italy, but the second campaign became a prolonged and destructive struggle beyond any single commander's control.

Success also created court danger. During Justinian's plague illness, Belisarius's glamour and reputation made him an imaginable focus for succession or opposition. [[Procopius]], who served as his secretary, provides much of the narrative while also attacking him in the Secret History.

## Key Characteristics

- Highly successful expeditionary commander in North Africa and Italy.
- Commander whose troops helped preserve Justinian's regime through mass violence in the Hippodrome.
- Central military agent of Justinian's Roman restoration project.
- Prestige figure whose victories created political as well as strategic value.
- Court dependent exposed to imperial suspicion during succession uncertainty.
- Historical subject mediated heavily through Procopius's proximity and hostility.

## Evidence

- Regime defense - [[219-justinian-making-rome-great-again-part-2-glt2369595847]] places Belisarius at one end of the Hippodrome assault that destroyed the Nika coalition.
- North Africa - [[220-justinian-theodora-the-secret-history-part-3-glt6795699757]] says Belisarius led the 533 expedition and defeated the Vandals within a year.
- Italy - [[220-justinian-theodora-the-secret-history-part-3-glt6795699757]] credits him with the captures of Naples and Rome before the war widened into attrition and devastation.
- Court risk - [[220-justinian-theodora-the-secret-history-part-3-glt6795699757]] links Justinian's illness to concern that the empire's most celebrated general might attract opposition.
- Historical record - [[220-justinian-theodora-the-secret-history-part-3-glt6795699757]] identifies [[Procopius]] as Belisarius's secretary and a major but internally divided witness.

## Qualifications

The adjacent episodes give only a compressed account of Belisarius's career. His precise conduct and responsibility in the Nika massacre, Justinian's motives in their falling-out, succession prospects, campaign responsibility, and Procopius's personal grievances remain interpretive. Strategic success in North Africa should not be generalized to the much longer Italian war.

## What Changed

- Added internal regime defense and the mass violence of the Nika suppression to the earlier expeditionary profile.

## Relationships

- [[JustinianI]] - emperor, patron, and suspicious superior.
- [[Theodora]] - empress within the court politics surrounding his command.
- [[Procopius]] - secretary and principal narrative witness.
- [[VandalKingdom]] - North African power defeated in his rapid campaign.
- [[ImperialRestorationParadox]] - strategic project in which victory produced widening costs.
- [[NikaRiots]] - urban revolt his troops helped crush.
