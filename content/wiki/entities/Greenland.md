# Greenland

## Overview

Greenland is a self-governing Arctic territory within the Kingdom of [[Denmark]] whose geography, people, land system, mineral deposits, and defense access make it central to debates about [[GreenlandStrategicAccess]] and [[CriticalMineralsGeopolitics]].

## Current Profile

Greenland matters to the [[UnitedStates]] for missile warning, space and air defense, changing Arctic routes, and possible resource access. Those interests are distinct: mineral security depends on extraction infrastructure and processing capacity, while defense access already operates through agreements with Greenland and Denmark.

The All-In source records a January 2026 argument that melting ice, shipping routes, security, and resources make acquisition strategically attractive. The later Planet Money source supplies the stronger institutional boundary: Denmark cannot simply sell a self-governing and inhabited territory, Greenlandic actors reject passive colonial treatment, and permission-based access can serve strategic aims without ownership.

## Key Characteristics

- Self-governing Arctic territory within the Kingdom of Denmark.
- Majority-Inuit political community whose consent cannot be replaced by Danish or U.S. bargaining alone.
- Strategically located for missile warning, space surveillance, air defense, and changing Arctic routes.
- Holder of mineral deposits whose commercial value depends on infrastructure, processing, capital, and local permission.
- Existing host for U.S. defense access, making cooperation and access design distinct from acquisition.

- **Defense and mineral distinction:** [[how-to-get-what-greenland-has-with-permission]] separates existing U.S. military access from the slower rare-earth mining and processing problem.

## Qualifications

Neither source establishes Greenlandic public support for expanded U.S. control, a purchase, or a specific access agreement. Climate change may increase route accessibility and strategic attention while also raising environmental and infrastructure risks. Resource estimates, shipping timelines, acquisition discussions, and proposed benefits remain source-scoped; geological endowment does not by itself create an economic mine or a secure supply chain.
