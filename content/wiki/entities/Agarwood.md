# 沉香 / Agarwood

沉香 / Agarwood enters the wiki through [[ep263-xianxiang-jueqi-faxian-zhongshi-xiangqi-de-meili-lq1lsqcprqyr7auo5gslwyymuiv]], where [[XuePeng|薛鹏]] explains it as a formed resinous material rather than a naturally fragrant piece of ordinary wood. The source says agarwood appears after a tree is injured and invaded by microbial or fungal activity; the tree secretes protective resin around the wound, and only the resin-bearing part becomes fragrant material.

The episode treats agarwood as both cultural object and supply-chain object. Its older prestige comes from rarity, long formation time, literati appreciation, Hainan aroma reputation, and high-end collection markets; its new consumer availability comes from planting, artificial结香, [[Qinan|奇楠]] grafting, and the movement from beads to incense powder, line incense, essential oil, and daily fragrance products.

## Key Claims
- Agarwood's value comes from a biological injury-response process, not from the whole tree being fragrant.
- Wild agarwood scarcity and protection pressure pushed the industry toward plantation and artificial结香 systems.
- The source presents Hainan-style aroma as cool, clear, sweet, and restrained, matching the episode's broader [[ChineseFragrance|中式香气]] preference for recognizable but non-intrusive scent.
- In the value chain, larger resinous pieces are more likely to become beads, scraps become powder for line incense, and remaining powder may be distilled into essential oil.
- Agarwood's daily-product future depends on raw-material trust, standardization, legal export proof, and consumer sensory education.
