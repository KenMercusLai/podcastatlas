# The Roll Call

## Overview

*The Roll Call* is [[ElizabethThompson|Elizabeth Thompson's]] 1874 painting of exhausted and wounded British soldiers assembling after a Crimean War battle, and the work that made her a national celebrity.

## Current Profile

The painting's force, in the episode's account, comes from treating soldiers as distinct, vulnerable people rather than anonymous emblems of military glory. Royal Collection curator Desmond Shawe-Taylor emphasizes its comradeship and restraint. The [[RoyalAcademy|Royal Academy]] displayed it at eye level and in a prized gallery, crowds made it the annual exhibition's major attraction, it toured England, and Queen Victoria acquired it after competitive bidding.

Its institutional meaning is double. The work demonstrated Thompson's ability to succeed at the center of British art, but its singular triumph did not secure her Academy membership or open a durable path for women. It therefore anchors the episode's distinction between artistic recognition and structural inclusion.

## Key Characteristics

- 1874 military painting by Elizabeth Thompson.
- Depicts depleted British soldiers after a Crimean War battle.
- Emphasizes individuality, vulnerability, restraint, and comradeship.
- Received elite placement at the Royal Academy exhibition.
- Became a national public sensation and toured England.
- Was acquired by Queen Victoria after competitive bidding.
