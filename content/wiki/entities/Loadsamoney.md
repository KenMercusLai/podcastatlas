# Loadsamoney

## Overview

Loadsamoney is Harry Enfield and Paul Whitehouse's newly wealthy construction-worker character, presented in [[the-satire-paradox-the-satire-paradox|The Satire Paradox]] as a caricature of vulgar materialism in Margaret Thatcher's Britain.

## Current Profile

The character criticizes Thatcherism by celebrating money, consumption, and status so excessively that the praise is meant to collapse into ridicule. That indirect method produced exceptional reach: the character became a cultural phenomenon and the associated song reportedly reached number two on the British charts.

Reception split the intended critique. Striking nurses used Loadsamoney as an image of what they opposed, while right-wing media treated him affectionately as evidence of working-class prosperity. Thatcher and her opponents could invoke the same character in Parliament with pride and contempt, making him a compact example of satire escaping authorial control.

## Key Characteristics

- Caricature of sudden working-class wealth and conspicuous consumption.
- Criticizes Thatcherism through exaggerated celebration rather than direct denunciation.
- Achieved mass cultural and musical visibility.
- Became politically usable by both opponents and supporters of the system being mocked.
