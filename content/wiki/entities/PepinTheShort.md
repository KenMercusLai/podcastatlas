# Pepin the Short

## Overview

Pepin the Short was the Carolingian ruler who ended [[MerovingianDynasty|Merovingian kingship]], used papal approval and anointing to make effective power sacred kingship, and bound Frankish military protection to papal territorial interests.

## Current Profile

Pepin inherited Neustria while his brother Carloman ruled Austrasia. After Carloman withdrew to monastic life in 747, Pepin became the sole effective ruler and asked Pope Zachary whether a king without power should retain the office. The pope's negative answer helped legitimate Chilperic III's deposition in 751, but the episode does not treat approval as a substitute for the military and political power already accumulated by [[CharlesMartel]] and his sons.

[[RoyalAnointingTradition|Anointing]] then gave the replacement dynasty a biblical language of consecration. Pope Stephen II crossed the Alps, appealed for the defense of Saint Peter, anointed Pepin again in 754, and praised the Franks in sacred national language. Pepin answered with campaigns against the Lombards and grants of conquered territory to Saint Peter, making reciprocal protection and recognition the basis of the [[PapalFrankishAlliance]].

At his death in 768, Pepin left a strengthened army and a realm extended through Aquitaine, across the Pyrenean frontier, and toward missionary zones in the east. The division between his sons was unstable, but Carloman's death in 771 allowed [[Charlemagne]] to inherit the whole kingdom.

## Key Characteristics

- Carolingian mayor of the palace who became king of the Franks.
- Dynastic usurper whose accession combined military control, papal approval, and anointing.
- Protector who campaigned against the Lombards on the papacy's behalf.
- Donor whose Italian territorial settlement strengthened Saint Peter's temporal position.
- Father whose enlarged and reorganized realm became Charlemagne's platform.

## Qualifications

The episode provides a high-level succession narrative rather than a full account of assemblies, elite consent, law, taxation, military organization, or the territorial details later associated with the Donation of Pepin. Papal approval and anointing strengthened an accession built on prior force and control; they did not independently create it. The sacred language used for the Franks is episode-attributed and should not be projected unchanged onto all Frankish subjects.
