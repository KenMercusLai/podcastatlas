# Drew Haven

## Overview
Drew Haven is a former technology worker whose [[MarketplaceTech]] interview describes leaving paid employment after cultural disillusionment and work-related stress, then treating control of time as more valuable than additional consumption.

## Current Profile
Haven's wiki significance is a household-scale account of [[EarlyRetirementWorkAutonomy]]. He did not begin with certainty that he would never work again; he concluded that existing resources could be enough, accepted that time away might delay conventional retirement, and gradually adopted the label "retired" while rebuilding purpose and community outside employment.

## Key Characteristics
- Left technology work after becoming disillusioned with company culture and the industry's increasing focus on profit.
- Reports that employment was harming his stress, sleep, and health.
- Treats existing resources and lower consumption as an alternative to waiting for a fixed retirement target.
- Compares discretionary purchases with the value of remaining job-free and usually chooses control of time.
- Rebuilds social connection through hobbies and relationships in his local community.

## Evidence
- Career exit and health - [[tech-20261109-1109-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261109-1109-mp-tech-pod-128]] attributes Haven's departure to cultural disillusionment and the effects of employment on stress, sleep, and health.
- Retirement and sufficiency - [[tech-20261109-1109-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261109-1109-mp-tech-pod-128]] records his changing use of the word "retired" and his conclusion that the household could make its resources enough.
- Time, spending, and community - [[tech-20261109-1109-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261109-1109-mp-tech-pod-128]] describes purchase decisions, simple living, hobby groups, and local relationship-building.

## Qualifications
The source is a short personal account and does not identify Haven's former employer, role, compensation, assets, health coverage, or precise retirement plan. His interpretation of Silicon Valley's cultural change and corporate social purpose is experience-based rather than an industry-wide measurement.
