# Alexander Burnes

## Overview
Alexander Burnes was a British traveler, intelligence gatherer, and political officer whose diplomacy and later conduct sit at the center of the episode's explanation of the [[FirstAngloAfghanWar|First Anglo-Afghan War]].

## Current Profile
In [[88-the-first-anglo-afghan-war-glt5156371854]], Burnes first appears surveying the Indus under diplomatic cover and publishing a popular travel account that also alerted Russian readers to British regional activity. He later reported that [[DostMuhammad|Dost Muhammad]] could probably be brought into a British alliance without invasion.

His advice was undermined by superiors, and the episode then gives him a damaging occupation role. Dalrymple alleges that Burnes's relationship with a woman connected to an Afghan warlord helped turn personal insult into rebellion, though that direct causal claim remains source-scoped.

## Key Characteristics
- Combined travel, diplomacy, geography, and intelligence gathering.
- Helped publicize routes and regional knowledge through a bestselling account.
- Built a small intelligence network challenged by Russian agents.
- Recommended working with Dost Muhammad rather than removing him.
- Was sidelined by hostile superiors in the policy process.
- Became implicated in occupation-era insult and the Kabul uprising.

## Evidence
- Intelligence work: [[88-the-first-anglo-afghan-war-glt5156371854]] describes his Indus journey and the Russian reading of his published account.
- Diplomatic advice: [[88-the-first-anglo-afghan-war-glt5156371854]] reports his favorable assessment of Dost Muhammad.
- Occupation conduct: [[88-the-first-anglo-afghan-war-glt5156371854]] attributes a politically explosive relationship to him.

## Qualifications
The episode compresses complex intelligence and diplomatic records into narrative. The claim that Burnes's sexual conduct directly precipitated rebellion is Dalrymple's emphasized interpretation and should not substitute for the uprising's broader political causes.

## What Changed
- Established Burnes as both the ignored advocate of diplomacy and a compromised occupation actor.
