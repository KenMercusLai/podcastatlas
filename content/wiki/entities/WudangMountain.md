# Wudang Mountain / 武当山

[[shan-shang-shan-xia-jie-renjian-wushi-xiaoyao-ji-shenxian-lhjiguq74flhatdxnbrnzhjg-acp]] turns Wudang Mountain from a tourism name into a lived Daoist institution. Through [[LiChuang|李闯]] and [[CizhiShangShan|《辞职上山》]], the source shows [[QuanzhenDaoism|全真道教]], morning and evening lessons, sweeping, incense duty, night shifts, cold, damp, animals, tourists, pilgrims, and [[DaoistTempleEverydayLabor]] as part of the mountain's reality.

The episode's main corrective is [[RetreatRomanceDeflation]]: Wudang can still be spiritually meaningful, but it is not outside "human life." The mountain is simultaneously sacred site, scenic area, workplace, refuge, and observation field where [[BeliefAsLastResortSupport]] and [[RitualTourismAuthenticityTension]] become visible.

Wudang Mountain / 武当山 appears in [[chengshi-jiushi-zheyang-21-shiyan-yizuo-yin-qiche-erqi-you-bugan-bei-qiche-paoxia-de-chengshi-1001770066]] as one of [[Shiyan]]'s major non-auto identity anchors. The episode mentions it alongside [[DanjiangkouReservoir]], water resources, Chinese medicine, turquoise, and transport improvements as part of the city's effort to avoid being defined only by automotive decline.

In the source, Wudang is not analyzed as a full tourism-industry case. Its wiki role is source-scoped: it marks the cultural and visitor-facing side of Shiyan's diversification, especially because the episode says the local airport is near Wudang and can support tourism access.
