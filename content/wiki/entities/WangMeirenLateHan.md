# 王美人 / 灵怀皇后 (late Han)

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-921-jinqian-ru-shui-que-ze-ke-si-tan-ze-yan-si-lj1dk4qdgcago9elr7ctuvre3zic|Hanji 921]] adds the earliest birth-and-poisoning layer for Wang Meiren in this wiki. The episode recounts her giving birth to Liu Xie, later [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]], and then being poisoned by [[EmpressDowagerHe|何皇后 / 何太后]], after which [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]] considers deposing He but is dissuaded by eunuch pleading.

The same source links Wang Meiren's death to Liu Xie's later succession position. Liu Xie is sent to [[EmpressDowagerDongLateHan|董太后]] in Changle Palace and raised in the Dong line, so Wang Meiren becomes the absent birth mother behind a living foster-grandmother alignment.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-943-quanshi-kaoyan-renxing-beihou-cangzhe-juda-xiongxian-lg7ia5xok-swdmhml35yv9qqkvt|Hanji 943]] backfills Wang Meiren's succession significance before the later burial and posthumous-honor branch. The source identifies her as the mother of Liu Xie, later [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]], and says Liu Xie is raised by [[EmpressDowagerDongLateHan|董太后]] after Wang Meiren's death.

This makes Wang Meiren part of the two-prince legitimacy split. [[LiuBianHongnongWang|刘辩 / 弘农王]] has the living He-maternal line through [[EmpressDowagerHe|何太后]], while Liu Xie has a dead birth mother and a powerful foster-grandmother in the Dong line. The page therefore connects Hanji 943's accession struggle to the later Hanji 973 burial-repair scene.

王美人 / 灵怀皇后 (late Han) enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-973-changpingguan-zhizhan-shengsi-zhidi-zhanhou-jing-chonggui-yuhao-lgazc9dewfayb8qnkncfkfa12yet|Hanji 973]] as [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]]'s mother. The episode says she gave birth to Liu Xie and was then poisoned by Empress He, after which she was only hastily buried outside Luoyang.

In 194 CE, when officials ask Emperor Xian to choose an empress, he refuses to discuss palace selection before his mother's burial status is settled. The Three Excellencies then request that Wang Meiren be reburied at Emperor Ling's tomb and posthumously honored as 灵怀皇后.

Her source role is less a full biography than a court-capacity marker. The late-Han court can still perform posthumous honor, burial, and title ritual, but those rites sit beside the episode's larger point that the emperor's coming-of-age does not restore practical sovereignty under [[LiJueLateHan|李傕]]-era military pressure.
