# Cicero / 西塞罗

## Overview

Cicero / 西塞罗 is the republican orator and statesman whose amnesty proposal, campaign against [[MarkAntony]], dependence on other commanders, and death in the triumviral proscriptions expose the limits of constitutional speech after Caesar's assassination.

## Current Profile

The earlier source presents Cicero as a witness to the [[LateRomanRepublicCrisis|late-republic crisis]], a suppressor of the Catiline conspiracy, and a republican not trusted with the [[CaesarAssassination|assassination plot]]. [[305-the-fall-of-the-roman-republic-glt6995332578]] begins his decisive aftermath role with the March 17 amnesty that protects the assassins while preserving much of Caesar's settlement.

As the conspirators leave Rome, Cicero becomes the strongest senatorial voice against Antony. Yet he has no army. He works through Hirtius and Pansa and persuades the Senate to legitimize [[OctavianAugustus|Octavian]]'s private force, treating the young heir as an instrument. The plan briefly defeats Antony at Mutina, but both consuls die and Octavian becomes autonomous. During the [[SecondTriumvirate|triumviral]] bargain, Octavian yields Cicero to Antony. His severed head and hands are displayed in the Forum, and the episode reads the mutilation of his speech organs as symbolic destruction of free republican politics.

## Key Characteristics

- Constitutional mediator who proposes amnesty immediately after Caesar's death.
- Leading senatorial opponent of Antony after the liberators leave Rome.
- Politician whose rhetorical authority is not matched by independent military force.
- Sponsor of Octavian's legalization who misjudges the heir's autonomy and memory of insult.
- Victim of proscription whose death becomes a symbol of speech defeated by armed rule.

## Evidence

- Earlier republican profile: [[124-kaisa-zhisi-lishi-de-yunjiao-shifou-yi-shixiao-781894361]] connects Cicero to Catiline, Caesar, compromise, and the limits of elite republican politics.
- Amnesty and leadership: [[305-the-fall-of-the-roman-republic-glt6995332578]] describes his Senate proposal, hesitation about leaving Italy, and return as the leading constitutional spokesman.
- Military dependence: [[305-the-fall-of-the-roman-republic-glt6995332578]] follows his use of Hirtius, Pansa, and Octavian against Antony.
- Death and symbolism: [[305-the-fall-of-the-roman-republic-glt6995332578]] describes his capture, killing, public display, and the reported treatment of his tongue by Fulvia.

## Qualifications

The source's details about Cicero's jokes, the transmission of private remarks, his final movements, and Fulvia's treatment of his tongue are ancient narrative material and remain source-scoped. His constitutional language should not be treated as proof that the senatorial coalition itself had a coherent postwar program.

## What Changed

- Added Cicero's amnesty, anti-Antony coalition, legalization of Octavian's force, political miscalculation, and proscription death.
- Reframed him as a test of whether republican speech can operate when every viable settlement depends on commanders.

- [[MarkAntony]] - principal opponent targeted by Cicero's speeches.
- [[OctavianAugustus]] - young commander Cicero tries to use and who later consents to his death.
- [[SecondTriumvirate]] - regime whose proscription silences Cicero.
- [[RomanRepublic]] - constitutional order Cicero tries to defend without independent force.
- [[RepublicanMemoryAgainstDictatorship]] - memory frame strengthened by Cicero's death as a symbol of lost political speech.
