# Eddie Chang

## Overview
Eddie Chang is the neurosurgeon and speech-neuroscience guest in the [[HubermanLab]] Essentials episode on speech, language, brain-machine interfaces, and stuttering.

## Current Profile
The source presents Chang as a clinician-scientist whose work links basic speech neurobiology with communication-restoration technology. He explains speech through breath, laryngeal voicing, vocal-tract shaping, and cortical motor control, then uses that model to describe [[SpeechNeuroprosthetics]] for people with severe paralysis or locked-in syndrome.

Chang's profile in the wiki centers on careful distinctions. He separates speech from broader language, learned speech from more basic vocalization, restoration from enhancement, and stuttering from intelligence or language capacity. His [[BRAVOTrial]] discussion treats brain-machine interfaces as clinically meaningful but still bounded by training, vocabulary, accuracy, hardware invasiveness, and social embodiment.

## Key Characteristics
- Explains speech as a learned motor act involving breath, vocal folds, articulators, and speech-related cortex.
- Separates speech, language, vocalization, and stuttering rather than treating all communication difficulty as one category.
- Leads the episode's account of speech neuroprosthetics for people who cannot speak because of paralysis.
- Treats machine-learning decoding and language-model-style correction as tools for restoring attempted speech, not as general mind reading.
- Keeps enhancement and augmentation claims ethically cautious, especially when invasive surgery is involved.

## Evidence
- Speech and language distinction: [[essentials-the-science-of-learning-speaking-languages-dr-eddie-chang-scim7185728420]] has Chang define speech as a physical signal and language as a broader system including pragmatics, semantics, syntax, sign, and reading.
- Vocal-tract mechanism: [[essentials-the-science-of-learning-speaking-languages-dr-eddie-chang-scim7185728420]] has Chang describe exhalation, laryngeal vocal-fold vibration, voicing, and shaping by the tongue, lips, jaw, pharynx, and oral cavity.
- Clinical neuroprosthetics: [[essentials-the-science-of-learning-speaking-languages-dr-eddie-chang-scim7185728420]] has Chang describe the BRAVO trial, implanted electrodes, attempted-speech decoding, autocorrect-like modeling, and Poncho's communication constraints.
- Boundary setting: [[essentials-the-science-of-learning-speaking-languages-dr-eddie-chang-scim7185728420]] has Chang qualify augmentation, avatar communication, and stuttering as technically and clinically nuanced rather than simple superhuman enhancement or language deficit.
