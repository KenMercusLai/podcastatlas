# Eddie Chang

## Overview
Eddie Chang is a neurosurgeon and speech-neuroscience researcher presented in two [[HubermanLab]] versions of a conversation spanning auditory development, awake brain mapping, epilepsy, speech and language, and brain-machine interfaces.

## Current Profile
The sources present Chang as a clinician-scientist whose work connects developmental neuroscience, direct human cortical mapping, neurosurgery, and communication-restoration technology. The full interview adds [[AuditoryExperienceDependentCriticalPeriods]], [[AwakeCorticalMapping]], epilepsy localization, bilingualism, reading, and [[DistributedSpeechLanguageCircuits]] to the condensed episode's account of breath, laryngeal voicing, vocal-tract shaping, and cortical motor control.

Chang's profile centers on careful distinctions and clinical boundaries. He separates speech from broader language, learned speech from more basic vocalization, restoration from enhancement, and stuttering from intelligence or language capacity. He also rejects a single-center account of speaking without denying specialized regional roles, and treats infant sound exposure, emotion-like seizures, ketogenic therapy, and brain-machine interfaces as questions whose evidence and clinical implications differ.

## Key Characteristics
- Explains speech as a learned motor act involving breath, vocal folds, articulators, and speech-related cortex.
- Uses awake stimulation, implanted recording, and clinical dissociations to explain distributed speech-language function.
- Separates speech, language, vocalization, reading, memory, and stuttering rather than treating all communication difficulty as one category.
- Leads the episode's account of speech neuroprosthetics for people who cannot speak because of paralysis.
- Treats machine-learning decoding and language-model-style correction as tools for restoring attempted speech, not as general mind reading.
- Keeps developmental, neurological, enhancement, and augmentation claims cautious when human evidence or clinical translation is incomplete.

## Evidence
- Developmental and clinical mapping - [[the-science-of-learning-speaking-languages-dr-eddie-chang-scim2227065926]] connects Chang's animal critical-period work with awake stimulation, epilepsy electrodes, and person-specific function preservation.
- Speech and language distinction - [[essentials-the-science-of-learning-speaking-languages-dr-eddie-chang-scim7185728420]] and [[the-science-of-learning-speaking-languages-dr-eddie-chang-scim2227065926]] define speech as a physical signal within the broader systems of pragmatics, semantics, syntax, sign, and reading.
- Distributed circuit account - [[the-science-of-learning-speaking-languages-dr-eddie-chang-scim2227065926]] challenges Broca-centered speaking, foregrounds precentral vocal-tract control, and preserves posterior temporal roles in comprehension and retrieval.
- Clinical neuroprosthetics - both [[essentials-the-science-of-learning-speaking-languages-dr-eddie-chang-scim7185728420]] and [[the-science-of-learning-speaking-languages-dr-eddie-chang-scim2227065926]] describe BRAVO, implanted electrodes, attempted-speech decoding, contextual modeling, and Pancho's communication constraints.
- Boundary setting - [[essentials-the-science-of-learning-speaking-languages-dr-eddie-chang-scim7185728420]] and [[the-science-of-learning-speaking-languages-dr-eddie-chang-scim2227065926]] qualify stuttering, avatars, augmentation, and device performance, while the full interview also bounds infant-white-noise, seizure, diet, dyslexia, and language-learning claims.
