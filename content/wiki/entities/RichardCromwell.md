# Richard Cromwell

## Overview

Richard Cromwell was Oliver Cromwell's designated successor whose rapid removal exposed the Protectorate's dependence on his father's personal authority and the army's continuing power.

## Current Profile

Named shortly before Oliver's death in 1658, Richard inherited an office that looked increasingly hereditary but lacked a secure constitutional and military base. The choice did not settle whether protectorship or kingship should be hereditary or elective, and Richard lacked the army support through which his father had contained those alternatives. He was quickly pushed aside, and the resulting instability reopened conflict among army leaders and parliamentary institutions.

The episode uses his failure comparatively. Anna Keay suggests that Oliver's more capable son Henry might have allowed a hereditary protectorship to take root, making Richard's selection one contingent cause of collapse rather than proof that all crownless government was impossible.

## Key Characteristics

- Son and named successor of Oliver Cromwell.
- Inherited the protectorship without equivalent personal or military authority.
- Rapidly displaced during renewed army–Parliament conflict.
- Used as evidence for the regime's unresolved succession problem.
- Demonstrates the gap between designating an heir and institutionalizing authority in an office.

## Qualifications

The Henry-versus-Richard comparison is explicitly counterfactual. Richard's weakness, the army's role, and the Protectorate's collapse require broader evidence before assigning singular causal responsibility. His rapid fall does not by itself prove that every crownless or hereditary Protectoral settlement was impossible.

## What Changed

- Clarified that four unresolved succession models surrounded Richard's designation and that army support, not heredity alone, remained decisive.
