# Haudenosaunee Confederacy

## Overview
The Haudenosaunee Confederacy is the Indigenous democratic tradition that Baratunde Thurston invokes as an alternative foundation for thinking about peace, reciprocity, authority, and future responsibility.

## Current Profile
Within the bounded source, the Confederacy functions as a living counterexample to governance stories centered on domination and ownership. Thurston highlights peace as a primary governmental purpose, women's authority, reciprocal obligation, and seven-generation thinking, then uses those features to reframe technology and ecological stewardship as responsibilities held across relationships and time.

## Key Characteristics
- Organizes governance around peace and reciprocity in the episode's account.
- Gives women central authority rather than treating political power as exclusively male.
- Extends responsibility toward future generations through a long decision horizon.
- Grounds democracy in relationship and obligation rather than isolated individual control.

## Qualifications
The page records one speaker's concise use of the Confederacy in an AI discussion. It is not a comprehensive history of Haudenosaunee nations, law, political institutions, internal diversity, colonial encounters, or present governance, and it should not collapse distinct Indigenous traditions into a generic model.
