## Overview

交州 / Jiaozhou is the southern provincial field in which the Shi family converts long residence and commandery office into regional power before Sun Wu replaces negotiated local autonomy with more direct administration.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1119-ta-shengqian-guishun-sunquan-sihou-weihe-jiazu-bei-ganjin-shajue-ll4mblhk7ydn3pdxg-5zucstokzz|Hanji 1119]] presents Jiaozhou as distant enough from the late-Han center for lineage depth and office distribution to matter heavily. [[ShiXie|士燮]]'s family had lived there for six generations, and his brothers' offices in Jiaozhi, Hepu, Jiuzhen, and Nanhai created a regional network that nominal inspectors could not ignore.

The province also shows the difference between formal jurisdiction and effective control. Shi Xie avoids taking the inspectorship yet works with successive outside appointees. After his death, [[SunQuan|孙权]] divides the territory between Guangzhou in the north and a retained Jiaozhou in the south, appoints his own officials, and sends [[LyuDai|吕岱]] against [[ShiHui|士徽]]. The source therefore makes administrative geography an instrument for replacing a personal accommodation with direct rule.

## Key Characteristics

- Distant southern province where local lineage depth can outweigh formal appointment alone.
- Multi-commandery field in which the Shi brothers build a family power network.
- Refuge and cultural zone said to receive northern scholars during central warfare.
- Province governed through coexistence between outside inspectors and a locally indispensable broker.
- Administrative territory divided after Shi Xie's death to reduce inherited local concentration.
- Centralization theater where replacement appointments lead to armed resistance and execution.

## Qualifications

This is a single-source provincial profile, not a complete geography or institutional history of Jiaozhou. The episode compresses changing provincial boundaries, offices, and external jurisdictions across decades. Its claims about peace, culture, and administrative division require comparison with primary sources, and [[JiaozhiCommandery|交趾郡]] should not be treated as interchangeable with the larger province.

## What Changed

- Created Jiaozhou as the larger provincial field distinct from Jiaozhi Commandery.
- Framed its post-226 division as both administrative change and local-power reduction.
