# Robert FitzRoy

## Overview

Robert FitzRoy is represented as the young captain of [[HMSBeagle]] and the shipboard counterpart for [[CharlesDarwin|Charles Darwin]] during the South American survey and circumnavigation.

## Current Profile

[[12-days-port-wine-and-darwin-sets-sail-glt6423200478]] explains Darwin's presence through FitzRoy's social and psychological needs as captain. The episode says he sought another gentleman because command was isolating and a previous Beagle captain had died by suicide; Darwin fit both the social role and the voyage's scientific opportunity after FitzRoy's first choice declined. The two then shared cramped quarters through delayed departures and an eventually five-year voyage.

[[283-ecuador-darwins-adventure-to-the-galapagos-glt2992613399]] contrasts FitzRoy's political conservatism and support for slavery with Darwin's progressive Whig opposition to slavery, while saying they generally managed their relationship. The departure episode adds a major quarrel over slavery without making conflict the whole relationship. FitzRoy also contributes an independent descriptive voice: his account of the [[GalapagosIslands|Galapagos Islands]] as black, dismal heaps of broken lava helps recover how far early impressions differed from the archipelago's later paradise image.

## Key Characteristics

- Young Royal Navy captain responsible for the Beagle survey voyage.
- Political conservative contrasted with Darwin's Whig and anti-slavery views.
- Host and gentleman counterpart whose relationship with Darwin carried potential tension.
- Captain whose search for companionship was shaped, in the source, by isolation and concern about depression.
- Observer whose bleak Galapagos descriptions contribute to the episode's historical contrast.

## Evidence

- Command and relationship - [[283-ecuador-darwins-adventure-to-the-galapagos-glt2992613399]] identifies FitzRoy as captain and explains Darwin's companion role.
- Political contrast - [[283-ecuador-darwins-adventure-to-the-galapagos-glt2992613399]] contrasts their slavery and political positions while avoiding a claim of constant conflict.
- Island description - [[283-ecuador-darwins-adventure-to-the-galapagos-glt2992613399]] attributes the broken-lava description to FitzRoy.
- Companion rationale - [[12-days-port-wine-and-darwin-sets-sail-glt6423200478]] links Darwin's invitation to FitzRoy's concern about isolation, depression, and the previous captain's suicide.
- Slavery conflict - [[12-days-port-wine-and-darwin-sets-sail-glt6423200478]] describes a serious quarrel arising from FitzRoy's defense of slavery and Darwin's abolitionist background.

## Qualifications

The sources offer only a narrow voyage-era portrait. They do not provide a full account of FitzRoy's naval work, later public career, religious views, scientific activity, mental health, or changing relationship with Darwin. The political characterizations, companion-role rationale, family connections, private fears, and exact form of the slavery quarrel remain episode-attributed.

## What Changed

- Added isolation, mental-health concern, and social equality to the rationale for Darwin's companion role.
- Added the slavery quarrel while preserving the distinction between serious conflict and continuous hostility.

## Relationships

- [[HMSBeagle]] - vessel he commanded during the survey voyage.
- [[CharlesDarwin]] - naturalist, gentleman companion, and political contrast aboard ship.
- [[GalapagosIslands]] - landscape he described in bleak volcanic terms.
- [[EvolutionaryTheoryFormation]] - historical process his voyage made materially possible without making him the theory's author.
