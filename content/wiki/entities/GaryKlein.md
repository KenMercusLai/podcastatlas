## Overview

Gary Klein is the decision-making specialist whose account of [[DecisionFixation]] supplies the central cognitive framework in [[memorial-day-2020-part-1-memorial-day-2020-part-1|Memorial Day, 2020 | Part 1]].

## Current Profile

Klein defines fixation as continuing down a wrong path after new evidence should prompt reassessment. His comparison between intelligence failure before the Yom Kippur War and physicians who deliberately restart analysis when unexpected information appears gives the episode both a failure mechanism and a corrective discipline.

## Key Characteristics

- Decision-making specialist focused on judgment under changing evidence.
- Defines fixation as becoming stuck on an initially plausible but failing path.
- Uses historical intelligence failure to show how assumptions can neutralize warning signs.
- Presents deliberate analytical restart as a professional defense against fixation.

## Evidence

### Fixation mechanism

- [[memorial-day-2020-part-1-memorial-day-2020-part-1]] reports Klein's definition of fixation and his account of Elie Zerah discounting evidence that conflicted with assumptions before the 1973 Yom Kippur War.

### Corrective practice

- [[memorial-day-2020-part-1-memorial-day-2020-part-1]] reports that experienced physicians can guard against fixation by starting their analysis over when unexpected evidence appears.

## Qualifications

The source uses Klein's framework interpretively and does not reproduce his underlying publications, the full historical record, or a validated assessment of Chauvin's mental process. A useful decision model is not a clinical diagnosis and does not by itself establish motive, racism, or legal responsibility.
