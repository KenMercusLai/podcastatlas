# Percy Shelley / 珀西·雪莱

## Overview

Percy Shelley was a Romantic poet, political radical, science enthusiast, partner of [[MaryShelley|Mary Shelley / 玛丽·雪莱]], and important companion and critic within [[LordByron|Lord Byron / 拜伦]]'s Geneva and Italian circles.

## Current Profile

The Frankenstein source presents Shelley as a conduit for [[RomanticScienceCulture]]: his fascination with electricity, chemistry, experiment, anti-authoritarian education, and life-principle debate helped form Mary's intellectual environment. It also keeps the damage within his intimate history visible rather than treating radical ideals as proof of responsible conduct.

[[443-lord-byron-death-of-a-vampire-part-4-glt3247503776]] extends him from Geneva to Italy. Shelley admired Byron but was disturbed by the excess of his Venetian life; at Pisa he judged Byron less dissipated and more seriously committed to poetry and politics. Their work on *The Liberal* joins literary satire to radical publishing before Shelley's death at sea in July 1822 broke up the circle.

## Key Characteristics

- Romantic poet whose radical politics included atheism, republicanism, and free-love advocacy.
- Carrier of experimental and speculative science into the Shelley household.
- Participant in the Villa Diodati creation circle.
- Admirer and moral-political critic of Byron's life in exile.
- Collaborator with Byron on radical publishing in Pisa.
- Death at sea that helped dissolve the Italian Romantic circle.

## Evidence

- Science and family context: [[141-zhizao-fulankensitan-kehuan-zhimu-he-tade-shenqi-shidai-857923931]] connects Shelley to electrical demonstrations, chemistry, anti-authoritarian education, and Mary's intellectual formation.
- Geneva encounter: [[443-lord-byron-death-of-a-vampire-part-4-glt3247503776]] places him with Mary, Claire, Byron, and Polidori at Lake Geneva in May 1816.
- Italian relationship: [[443-lord-byron-death-of-a-vampire-part-4-glt3247503776]] contrasts his unease at Byron's Venetian dissipation with his later approval of Byron's Pisan poetry and politics.
- Death and aftermath: [[443-lord-byron-death-of-a-vampire-part-4-glt3247503776]] describes his disappearance at sea, recovery of his body, cremation aftermath, and the collapse of the circle.

## Qualifications

The sources are centered on Mary Shelley, Frankenstein, or Byron rather than a complete Shelley biography or close reading of his poetry. Reported emotional reactions, private judgments, and the compressed account of his death remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Added Shelley's role as Byron's critic and radical publishing collaborator in Italy.
- Added his 1822 death as a structural break in Byron's remaining circle.
