# Keith Richards

## Overview

Keith Richards is the Rolling Stones guitarist whose Redlands country house became the setting for the 1967 drugs raid. He stands at the center of both the band's rebellious public image and the status aspirations and historical interests that complicated it.

## Current Profile

Richards was charged with allowing cannabis smoking and initially received twelve months. The episode suggests his courtroom attitude and the symbolic use of a respectable country house may have influenced the severity; his conviction was overturned on appeal. The house captures a class contradiction: apparent enemies of respectability had purchased its symbols.

Richards and [[MickJagger]] displaced founder [[BrianJones]] from the group's center. His relationship with Anita Pallenberg deepened Jones's isolation, while his partnership with Jagger supported the blues-rooted musical reboot and later rock identity.

## Key Characteristics

- Redlands made his home a stage for drug and class anxiety.
- Initial punishment was severe relative to the uncertain premises charge.
- Private historical interests complicated the decadent image.
- His partnership with Jagger became the band's governing and creative center.
- His relationship with Pallenberg intensified Jones's exclusion.
