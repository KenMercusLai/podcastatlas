# Hamilcar Barca / 哈米尔卡·巴卡

## Overview

Hamilcar Barca / 哈米尔卡·巴卡 is the Carthaginian commander whose Sicilian delaying war, suppression of the [[MercenaryWar]], and Iberian expansion connect the [[FirstPunicWar]] to the [[BarcidSpanishPowerBase|Barcid Spanish power base]] inherited by [[HannibalBarca]].

## Current Profile

Hamilcar takes command in Sicily in 247 BC with limited infantry, little cavalry, and the remaining strongholds under pressure. Positions near Palermo and Eryx let him raid and prolong resistance but not break the sieges. When Rome's final fleet destroys Carthaginian relief, Hamilcar is authorized to settle the war but, in the newer episode's account, sends Gisco to conduct the negotiation. This corrects the earlier profile's loose statement that Hamilcar personally negotiated the peace.

He then becomes central to suppressing Carthage's mercenary revolt. The profile now joins tactical energy and state rescue to escalation: after Gisco's murder, Hamilcar uses elephant executions, blockade, starvation, seizure of negotiators, and mass killing. Rome's seizure of Sardinia and Corsica during Carthaginian weakness deepens the postwar grievance attributed to him.

In 237 BC he turns to Iberia, where Phoenician settlements, mines, agriculture, and recruits offer a route to recovery. The episode portrays his expansion as both a Carthaginian rebuilding program and a possible Barcid family power base. His mix of severe punishment and rich rewards expands the army before he dies fighting in the interior.

## Key Characteristics

- Sustains a resource-poor Sicilian holding campaign without reversing strategic defeat.
- Delegates the final peace negotiation to Gisco in the newer episode's account.
- Saves Carthage during the mercenary revolt through maneuver and extreme coercion.
- Rebuilds power through Iberian resources after Carthage loses its island empire.
- Combines coercion, reward, recruitment, and dynastic continuity.

## Qualifications

The profile is bounded to two podcast reconstructions rooted in ancient and later literary traditions. The childhood oath, force totals, atrocity and cannibalism details, Hamilcar's private motives, his precise responsibility for broken negotiations, the degree of Barcid independence, and embellished accounts of his death remain source-scoped. Military effectiveness does not resolve the moral status of his punitive methods.

## What Changed

- Distinguished authorization of the peace from Gisco's personal negotiation.
- Added the resource limits of Hamilcar's Sicilian holding campaign.
- Made Mercenary War victory inseparable from coercive and atrocity-laden escalation.
