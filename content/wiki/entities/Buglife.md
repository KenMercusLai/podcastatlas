# Buglife

## Overview
Buglife is a British conservation organization presented as mapping and reconnecting high-quality insect habitats through “bug highways.”

## Current Profile
Buglife responds to habitat fragmentation by treating protected patches as a network problem. It combines historical records with landowner and expert knowledge, then participates in the Space for Nature project’s use of citizen science, satellite imagery, drone imagery, and machine learning to classify habitats and prioritize links between them.

## Key Characteristics
- Maps surviving high-quality insect habitats across Britain.
- Seeks corridors that let insects move between otherwise isolated areas.
- Combines archival, local, expert, citizen-science, and remote-sensing evidence.
- Works with Surrey Wildlife Trust and the University of Surrey on machine-assisted habitat classification.
- Plans cross-border extension through European “buzz lines.”

## Qualifications
The source reports rediscovery of rare insects and faster mapping as encouraging signs, but it does not establish population recovery, causal biodiversity gains, model accuracy, implementation cost, or landowner adoption at national scale.
