# A. Philip Randolph

## Overview

A. Philip Randolph was a labor and civil-rights leader whose long campaign for a march on Washington connected workplace equality, federal leverage, and the [[MarchOnWashington1963|1963 demonstration]].

## Current Profile

Randolph brought an older labor-organizing lineage into the coalition often remembered through [[MartinLutherKingJr|Martin Luther King Jr.]] He had proposed a Washington march in 1941 and used the threat of mass mobilization to obtain Franklin D. Roosevelt's action against discrimination in war industries, although the military remained segregated at that point.

In 1963 Randolph revived the march project, worked with [[BayardRustin|Bayard Rustin]], and helped persuade a cautious Kennedy administration that cooperation was safer than trying to stop a mobilization likely to occur anyway. He introduced King at the Lincoln Memorial, but his larger contribution was the continuity between labor organization, civil rights, and federal political pressure.

## Key Characteristics

- Labor leader associated with the Brotherhood of Sleeping Car Porters.
- Advocate of mass demonstration as leverage over federal policy.
- Originator of the 1941 and 1963 Washington march projects described by the source.
- Senior coalition leader who protected Rustin's operational role amid political attacks.
- Link between an older civil-rights generation and King's nationally visible leadership.
