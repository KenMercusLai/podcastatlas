# Aachen

## Overview

Aachen was [[Charlemagne]]'s palace center, where strategic location, thermal baths, Roman planning, and imported material culture made rulership physically legible as a western imperial project.

## Current Profile

The episode locates Aachen in the Carolingian heartland and near Charlemagne's eastern campaigns. Its palace complex used baths, a Roman-style road layout, and columns brought from Ravenna to connect Frankish rule materially to Roman prestige.

Aachen could not match the scale of Constantinople or Baghdad, but it functioned as a deliberate statement rather than a failed copy. Charlemagne died and was buried there in 814, wrapped in Byzantine silk and placed in an ancient Roman sarcophagus, joining the palace's living and funerary symbolism.

## Key Characteristics

- Carolingian palace center tied to the dynasty's heartland and eastern campaigns.
- Thermal-bath site associated with Charlemagne's courtly habits.
- Built environment using Roman planning and Ravenna columns.
- Burial place where Byzantine textile and Roman sarcophagus reinforced imperial memory.
