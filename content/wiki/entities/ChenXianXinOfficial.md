## Overview

陈贤 / Chen Xian enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-645-bidu-zhichang-quanmou-mingzhebaoshen-or-niliu-ershang-lvdqcakza0zqextrhympnpswxyjy|Hanji 645]] as a former Han legal specialist and shangshu who withdraws when [[WangMang|王莽]]'s [[XinDynasty|新朝]] turns institutional change into political danger. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-473-xihan-yongren-chenwannian-weihe-neng-bei-zairu-shice-ljxaektybs1lvddzuuqca-rpjwxy|Hanji 473]] backfills his earlier moral contrast with his father [[ChenWannianWesternHan|陈万年]]: Chen Wannian tries to teach him flattery as official survival, but Chen Xian later becomes an upright enforcement official who defends [[ZhuYunWesternHan|朱云]] against [[ShiXianWesternHan|石显]]'s accusation.

## Current Profile

Chen Xian is presented as someone close enough to law and office to understand a regime's direction. Hanji 473 gives the early character frame: despite being Chen Wannian's son, he does not inherit the father's sycophantic method. He becomes a strict official, offends powerful figures, defends Zhu Yun, and is framed by Shi Xian as leaking court secrets. The source says he avoids death only after [[ZhuBoWesternHan|朱博]] gives testimony, then suffers exile and imprisonment before later reemployment.

Hanji 645 then shows the same basic disposition under a different danger. Wang Mang changes Han institutions while in power, and the deaths of He Wu and Bao Xuan make continued service concrete risk. Chen Xian cites the timing principle "见几而作，不俟终日," asks to retire, refuses Wang Mang's later summons by claiming illness, and tells his three official sons to resign.

The profile is not simple passivity. Chen Xian keeps his household sacrifices on the former Han schedule and answers critics by saying his ancestors will not know Wang-family ritual dates. He also stores Han legal texts in a wall, making withdrawal a form of low-visibility institutional preservation rather than public collaboration with the new order.

## Key Characteristics

- Son of Chen Wannian whose career is framed as a rejection of paternal sycophancy.
- Upright official who defends Zhu Yun and suffers Shi Xian-linked accusation, imprisonment, and exile.
- Former Han legal specialist whose office experience helps him read Wang Mang's institutional rupture.
- Withdraws after politically motivated deaths show that continued service is no longer protected.
- Refuses renewed summons and extends withdrawal to his sons, treating family exposure as part of the risk.
- Preserves Han ritual timing and hides Han legal texts, making private withdrawal a form of institutional-memory preservation.

## Qualifications

The page is source-bounded to Hanji 473 and Hanji 645 and does not reconstruct Chen Xian's full career or later fate. Hanji 473 supports the father-son contrast, Zhu Yun defense, Shi Xian accusation, Zhu Bo testimony, and reemployment/refusal arc, but not the full legal record or exact procedure. Hanji 645 supports a withdrawal-and-preservation profile, but not a claim that Chen Xian coordinated resistance or openly opposed Wang Mang after retirement. This page identifies Hanji 473's Chen Xian with the later Xin-era official because the source itself continues into his Wang Mang refusal; he remains distinct from [[ChenXianWesternHan|the Chengdi-era Chen Xian]] linked to Zhai Fangjin.
