## Overview

## Current Profile
The episode presents Chongming through ordinary production details: typhoon-damaged melons, small corn, peanuts, neighboring plots, bought soil, improvised vegetable beds, river water, old furniture, rural property boundaries, and field routines. It is not used as a scenic countryside symbol; it is a working and semi-working rural setting where local knowledge, neighbor judgment, family labor, and amateur delay all matter.

## Key Characteristics
- Functions as a rural-edge setting where planting, commuting, writing, and social life overlap.
- Makes weather and timing visible through typhoons, rain, crop failure, and missed farm windows.
- Shows rural neighbor order through help, scrutiny, sharing, and strict field boundaries.
- Enables reuse practices that depend on space, storage, material familiarity, and labor.
- Gives the book a concrete local archive of what people plant, eat, repair, and improvise.
