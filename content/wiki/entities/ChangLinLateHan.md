## Overview

常林 / Chang Lin enters the wiki as a late-Han and Cao-family official whose profile joins delayed alignment, clean administration, survival outside a later regime struggle, and a concrete intervention against unnecessary command risk.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1074-quan-qing-chaoye-de-simayi-weihe-meici-jian-ta-dou-guibai-lpiw4ebdo2s5cucfcpmpkrhfnubi|Hanji 1074]] says Chang Lin grows up poor but studies persistently, refuses [[YuanShao|袁绍]]'s summons, and hides rather than bind himself to a patron he distrusts. He enters office only after [[CaoCao|曹操]] has secured Hebei, then advances through local and fiscal posts while acquiring a reputation for restraining strongmen, limiting land concentration, and avoiding personal corruption.

The episode values his trajectory through [[GoodDeathPoliticalSurvival|乱局善终]] rather than battlefield prominence. It says his age and withdrawal keep him outside the 249 CE struggle and that the Sima family respects him. That establishes the source's judgment—careful timing plus clean service can preserve both body and name—but not the title's stronger claim that [[SimaYi|司马懿]] repeatedly knelt before him.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1127-caocao-cuanwei-sanjian-ta-zhide-shi-shenme-livrh3obobkcfhj_ngnhe-kgv2ls|Hanji 1127]] supplies a narrower decision case during a Hejian revolt. When [[CaoPi|曹丕]] considers personally leading the response, Chang Lin argues that most northern people want stability, the rebels lack the base for a major movement, and victory would add little prestige while defeat or injury would impose an outsized political cost. Cao Pi accepts the advice and delegates the campaign. This turns the earlier general portrait of caution into an observable risk-asymmetry judgment.

## Key Characteristics

- Poor but persistent student in the episode's youth narrative.
- Refuses Yuan Shao and delays entry until Cao Cao controls Hebei.
- Presented as a local official who restrains strongmen and land concentration.
- Presented as a financially capable senior official who remains personally clean.
- Adviser who matches the importance of the commander to the limited value and scale of the mission.
- Avoids entanglement in the later Sima-family and Cao Shuang power struggle.
- Remembered by the host for longevity and a good end rather than military fame.

## Qualifications

This profile rests on two popular-history podcast summaries rather than direct historical texts. They give no citations for the leaf-writing story, land policy, office sequence, granary result, retirement status, age, reason for Sima-family respect, or exact Hejian exchange. The claim that Chang Lin was poor in youth sits beside an unexplained reference to his “士族背景.” Most importantly, Hanji 1074's body does not narrate the title's repeated-kneeling claim, so that detail remains unestablished.
