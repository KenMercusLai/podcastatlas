# AdButler

## Overview
AdButler is an advertising-technology SaaS company co-founded and led by [[RajivKanhaja]]. It provides infrastructure for selecting, serving, and managing advertising while generally leaving ad sales and proprietary customer data under the customer's control.

## Current Profile
The company is a long-accumulation enterprise SaaS case. AdButler began as an ad server built for Kanhaja's own web products, grew quietly through customer requests and low-priced plans, and entered the enterprise market after an inbound Costco RFP. Its source-described advantage combines broad product coverage, neutral infrastructure, live demonstrations, rapid customization, direct engineer access, and relationship-oriented commercial terms. Kanhaja reports eight-figure revenue, roughly 20–25 employees, and zero enterprise churn, but those figures remain source-scoped.

## Key Characteristics
- Neutral ad-serving infrastructure that supports web, retail-media, digital-billboard, and seat-back-display use cases without generally buying or selling the ads itself.
- Product depth accumulated through many years of customer-requested features before deliberate enterprise expansion.
- Lean enterprise motion based on inbound interest, RFP execution, real-product demonstrations, and technical specialists rather than a conventional sales organization.
- Embedded delivery model in which rapid customization and direct engineer access can make the company resemble a customer's internal ad-tech team.
- Customer-retention posture that favors value, service, and optional month-to-month terms over contractual lock-in.

## Evidence
Product and business evolution:
- [[b765334b]] describes the original self-built ad server, free inventory-share offer, later paid plans, decade-long feature accumulation, and eventual focus on AdButler as online advertising expanded.

Enterprise motion:
- [[b765334b]] identifies Costco's inbound RFP and live demonstration as the enterprise turning point, followed by an enterprise web tier and further inbound customers.
- [[b765334b]] says the platform can meet 95–99% of many enterprise requirements out of the box while the engineering team handles remaining requests quickly.

Commercial and operating model:
- [[b765334b]] reports relationship-oriented pricing, month-to-month availability, direct shared-channel access to engineers, eight-figure revenue, a roughly 20–25-person team, and zero enterprise churn.
- [[b765334b]] links small independently deployable components to parallel delivery by a lean engineering organization.

## Qualifications
The source does not disclose contract values, retention definitions, margins, customer concentration, service workload, or independently verified financials. Direct engineer access and customization can create concentration, support, security, and scaling risks even when modular architecture reduces coordination cost. Named customers establish reported use, not an endorsement of every company claim.

## What Changed
- Established AdButler as a long-horizon, product-depth-first enterprise SaaS case.
- Separated the company's stable ad-serving platform from Kanhaja's future-facing proposal for agent-generated customer software.
