## Overview

楚王刘延寿 / Liu Yanshou, Chu King enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-417-guangchuanwang-jingsong-daomu-rensheng-bi-caocao-hai-zao-ji-bai-nian-lqw0btlwi4ogmx94vnq7foyeplnv|Hanji 417]] as a Western Han royal kinsman who plots rebellion under [[EmperorXuanOfHan|汉宣帝]]. The episode presents him as a more distant royal branch actor who sees his own imperial claim as weak and therefore tries to recruit [[LiuXuGuanglingKingWesternHan|广陵王刘须]], a closer imperial relative, as the likely claimant.

## Current Profile

The source makes Liu Yanshou's plan a succession-engineering plot rather than a confident personal bid. He knows his own royal line is too distant from the imperial center to make him a persuasive emperor, so he looks for someone with stronger blood proximity and chooses Liu Xu, a son of [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]. He builds connection through marriage ties and sends Zhao Heqi to carry a message urging Liu Xu to watch the struggle for the realm and not fall behind others.

The plan fails through poor secrecy. Zhao Heqi's father Zhao Changnian reports the matter to the court. When the court asks about the case in the eleventh month, Liu Yanshou kills himself. Xuan's different handling of Liu Xu, who receives gold and gifts rather than immediate punishment in this episode, makes Liu Yanshou the punished center of the exposed plot while Liu Xu becomes the containment problem.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han Chu king and royal kinsman whose lineage is framed as too distant for a direct imperial claim.
- Succession conspirator who tries to borrow Liu Xu's closer descent from Han Wudi.
- Political operator who uses marriage connection and private messaging to approach a possible claimant.
- Poorly protected plotter whose plan is exposed through the messenger's family.
- Defeated royal offender who kills himself when the court investigates.

## Qualifications

This page preserves the episode's compact account and does not reconstruct Liu Yanshou's full genealogy, fief administration, marriage network, the exact wording of the message, or the court's formal investigative record. The claim that his imperial prospects were weak is the source's framing of his strategic calculation.
