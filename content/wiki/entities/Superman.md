# Superman

## Overview

Superman is the canonical early superhero created by Jerry Siegel and Joe Shuster and introduced in *Action Comics* in 1938, represented here as a Depression-era reformer, immigrant outsider, wartime icon, and model for the genre that followed.

## Current Profile

The episode places Superman at the junction of medium, politics, and identity. Cheap comic-book production made a new mass audience possible, while his early conflicts with corrupt businessmen, financiers, and exploiters connected exceptional power to Depression hardship and New Deal-era reform.

His arrival from Krypton and life as Clark Kent make him an assimilation story as well as a power fantasy. The interpretation gains force from Siegel and Shuster's Jewish and immigrant-family backgrounds, but it remains a cultural reading rather than proof that one biographical cause determines the character.

Wartime stories and later defense of the “American way” broadened Superman from social crusader into national emblem. That durability also exposes a central superhero problem: extraordinary protective power can symbolize hope while placing justice in the hands of a person beyond ordinary institutions.

## Key Characteristics

- First canonical superhero in the episode's genre history.
- Alien immigrant whose dual identity joins belonging to outsider status.
- Early Depression-era opponent of exploitation and corrupt wealth.
- Character created by young Jewish American writers from immigrant backgrounds.
- Wartime and postwar emblem of American ideals.

## Qualifications

This profile follows one conversational cultural history rather than the full publication record. The immigrant reading is interpretive, early political orientation changed across writers and eras, and the episode does not establish a continuous ideology from 1938 to later film and comics.

## What Changed

- Established Superman as a case linking Depression reform, immigrant identity, mass media, and national symbolism.
