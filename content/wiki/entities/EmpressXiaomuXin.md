## Overview

Empress Xiaomu of Xin / 孝睦皇后 is [[WangMang|王莽]]'s wife and first Xin empress in the Hanji 634 and Hanji 658 source set. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-634-huozai-nanren-yingzi-beihou-de-ta-can-can-can-lm3wub0l70yxwwyvwi15j93i9lwk|Hanji 634]] introduces her at Wang Mang's 9 CE accession as a title-elevated but powerless family victim whose sons [[WangHuoXinPrince|王获]] and [[WangYuXinPrince|王宇]] are already dead through Wang Mang's coercive politics. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-658-erzi-queshi-buyinggai-wangmang-nusha-taizi-ln4tu6ly8eie6jspvrurzn2rjjp|Hanji 658]] later uses her illness, grief, blindness, and death to show that Wang Mang's public moral authority has destroyed his own household before the later collapse of the [[XinDynasty|新朝]].

## Current Profile

Hanji 634 presents Empress Xiaomu as a family witness to Wang Mang's repeated killing of sons before she becomes empress. Her title rises with Wang Mang's accession, but the episode makes the elevation tragic: she has watched Wang Huo die by forced suicide and Wang Yu die after imprisonment, then is left with grief, blindness, and mental collapse rather than usable protection for the remaining sons.

Hanji 658 continues that profile near the end of her life. Wang Mang's guilt leads him to place [[WangLinXinCrownPrince|王林]] near her in the palace east so he can care for her, but her final illness and death become the trigger for Wang Lin's letter, Wang Mang's anger, funeral exclusion, and investigation.

Her attendant [[YuanBiXinPalaceAttendant|元璧]] is central to the scandal. Because Yuan Bi is also connected privately to Wang Mang, Wang Lin's involvement with her turns the queen's household into the site where sexual access, filial care, ruler suspicion, and succession danger converge.

## Key Characteristics

- Xin empress whose accession title does not translate into family security or agency.
- Mother of sons whose deaths make family grief part of early and late Xin legitimacy failure.
- Witness to the deaths of Wang Huo and Wang Yu before the later Wang Lin and Wang An succession collapse.
- Palace node linking Wang Lin's care role, Yuan Bi's position, and the funeral dispute.
- Posthumous trigger for Wang Mang's investigation and Wang Lin's coerced death.

## Evidence

- Accession elevation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-634-huozai-nanren-yingzi-beihou-de-ta-can-can-can-lm3wub0l70yxwwyvwi15j93i9lwk|Hanji 634]] says Wang Mang's wife is made empress when he becomes emperor.
- Earlier son deaths: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-634-huozai-nanren-yingzi-beihou-de-ta-can-can-can-lm3wub0l70yxwwyvwi15j93i9lwk|Hanji 634]] says Wang Huo is forced to suicide and Wang Yu dies after imprisonment.
- Grief and blindness: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-634-huozai-nanren-yingzi-beihou-de-ta-can-can-can-lm3wub0l70yxwwyvwi15j93i9lwk|Hanji 634]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-658-erzi-queshi-buyinggai-wangmang-nusha-taizi-ln4tu6ly8eie6jspvrurzn2rjjp|Hanji 658]] say she was devastated by Wang Mang's killing of sons and cried until blind.
- Wang Lin care arrangement: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-658-erzi-queshi-buyinggai-wangmang-nusha-taizi-ln4tu6ly8eie6jspvrurzn2rjjp|Hanji 658]] says Wang Mang placed Wang Lin near her in the palace east out of guilt.
- Funeral and letter trigger: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-658-erzi-queshi-buyinggai-wangmang-nusha-taizi-ln4tu6ly8eie6jspvrurzn2rjjp|Hanji 658]] says Wang Mang sees Wang Lin's fearful letter during her illness and later forbids Wang Lin from attending her funeral.
- Yuan Bi connection: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-658-erzi-queshi-buyinggai-wangmang-nusha-taizi-ln4tu6ly8eie6jspvrurzn2rjjp|Hanji 658]] identifies Yuan Bi as her attendant and as the woman whose relationships with Wang Mang and Wang Lin trigger the scandal.

## Qualifications

The source set gives a narrow profile centered on family suffering, accession title, and palace scandal. It supports her role as Wang Mang's wife, mother, empress, and late-life household context, but not her personal name, independent political agency, exact inner psychology, or a full court biography.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 634 as the earlier accession and maternal-grief layer, linking her empress title to the deaths of Wang Huo and Wang Yu.
- Preserved Hanji 658 as the later illness, Wang Lin, Yuan Bi, and funeral-investigation layer.

## Relationships

- [[WangMang|王莽]] - husband whose family punishments define her grief.
- [[WangHuoXinPrince|王获]] and [[WangYuXinPrince|王宇]] - sons whose deaths ground the earlier tragedy in Hanji 634.
- [[WangLinXinCrownPrince|王林]] - son placed near her and later excluded from her funeral.
- [[WangAnXinPrince|王安]] - remaining son bypassed for Wang Lin in the Hanji 634 succession arrangement and later restored before dying.
- [[YuanBiXinPalaceAttendant|元璧]] - attendant whose role links her household to the scandal.
- [[XinDynasty|新朝]] - regime whose palace-family collapse is made visible through her death.
