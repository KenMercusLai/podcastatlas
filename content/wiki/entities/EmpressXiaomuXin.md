## Overview

Empress Xiaomu of Xin / 孝睦皇后 is [[WangMang|王莽]]'s wife in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-658-erzi-queshi-buyinggai-wangmang-nusha-taizi-ln4tu6ly8eie6jspvrurzn2rjjp|Hanji 658]]. The episode uses her illness, grief, blindness, and death to show that Wang Mang's public moral authority has already destroyed his own household before the later collapse of the [[XinDynasty|新朝]].

## Current Profile

The source presents Empress Xiaomu as a family witness to Wang Mang's repeated killing of sons. She is said to have grieved so heavily that she went blind. Wang Mang's guilt leads him to place [[WangLinXinCrownPrince|王林]] near her in the palace east so he can care for her, but her final illness and death become the trigger for Wang Lin's letter, Wang Mang's anger, funeral exclusion, and investigation.

Her attendant [[YuanBiXinPalaceAttendant|元璧]] is central to the scandal. Because Yuan Bi is also connected privately to Wang Mang, Wang Lin's involvement with her turns the queen's household into the site where sexual access, filial care, ruler suspicion, and succession danger converge.

## Key Characteristics

- Xin empress whose suffering records the household cost of Wang Mang's political violence.
- Mother of sons whose deaths make family grief part of late-Xin legitimacy failure.
- Palace node linking Wang Lin's care role, Yuan Bi's position, and the funeral dispute.
- Posthumous trigger for Wang Mang's investigation and Wang Lin's coerced death.

## Evidence

- Grief and blindness: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-658-erzi-queshi-buyinggai-wangmang-nusha-taizi-ln4tu6ly8eie6jspvrurzn2rjjp|Hanji 658]] says she was devastated by Wang Mang's killing of sons and cried until blind.
- Wang Lin care arrangement: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-658-erzi-queshi-buyinggai-wangmang-nusha-taizi-ln4tu6ly8eie6jspvrurzn2rjjp|Hanji 658]] says Wang Mang placed Wang Lin near her in the palace east out of guilt.
- Funeral and letter trigger: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-658-erzi-queshi-buyinggai-wangmang-nusha-taizi-ln4tu6ly8eie6jspvrurzn2rjjp|Hanji 658]] says Wang Mang sees Wang Lin's fearful letter during her illness and later forbids Wang Lin from attending her funeral.
- Yuan Bi connection: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-658-erzi-queshi-buyinggai-wangmang-nusha-taizi-ln4tu6ly8eie6jspvrurzn2rjjp|Hanji 658]] identifies Yuan Bi as her attendant and as the woman whose relationships with Wang Mang and Wang Lin trigger the scandal.

## Qualifications

The source gives only a narrow final-life profile. It supports her role as Wang Mang's wife, mother, and palace-scandal context, but not a full political biography or independent account of earlier court activity.

## What Changed

- Created from Hanji 658 as the Xin empress whose death frames Wang Mang's household collapse.
