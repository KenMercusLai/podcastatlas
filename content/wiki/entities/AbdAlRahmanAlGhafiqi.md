# Abd al-Rahman al-Ghafiqi

## Overview

Abd al-Rahman al-Ghafiqi was the Umayyad governor in Toledo whose campaign through Aquitaine ended with his death against [[CharlesMartel]] at the [[BattleOfTours]].

## Current Profile

The episode places Abd al-Rahman inside the unstable politics of the western [[UmayyadCaliphate]]. In 731 he moved against the Berber leader Uthman, killed him, and broke the cross-confessional alliance Uthman had made with [[OdoOfAquitaine|Odo of Aquitaine]]. In 732, or possibly 733, his forces crossed into Aquitaine, sacked Bordeaux, defeated Odo near the Garonne, and moved north with a large baggage train of plunder.

His objective is reconstructed as the wealthy shrine of Saint Martin at Tours, valuable for booty, prestige, and religious symbolism. Charles blocked the road and forced a confrontation that favored disciplined Frankish infantry. Abd al-Rahman was killed during the fighting; his army then abandoned its camp overnight, apparently prioritizing withdrawal and the threatened plunder train over renewed battle.

## Key Characteristics

- Umayyad governor operating from Toledo within the Iberian frontier of the caliphate.
- Opponent of the Berber leader Uthman and the alliance linking Uthman to Odo.
- Commander of the destructive campaign through Bordeaux and Aquitaine.
- Leader whose mixed strategic motives are reduced poorly to either pure conquest or ordinary raiding.
- Battlefield casualty whose death contributed to Umayyad withdrawal from Tours.

## Qualifications

The source does not establish Abd al-Rahman's private intentions, the size or exact composition of his army, or whether permanent conquest would have followed victory. “Raid,” “invasion,” factional intervention, shrine attack, and imperial expansion describe overlapping aspects of the campaign rather than mutually exclusive labels. Equipment, cavalry, baggage-train, and battlefield claims remain reconstructed from limited evidence.
