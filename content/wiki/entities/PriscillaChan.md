# Priscilla Chan

## Overview
Priscilla Chan is a physician, educator, and co-founder of the [[ChanZuckerbergInitiative|Chan Zuckerberg Initiative]] who explains its biomedical strategy through basic science, shared tools, interdisciplinary institutions, and patient-centered translation.

## Current Profile
In the source, Chan is the principal voice for CZI's science program. She describes a century-scale mission to help cure, prevent, or manage disease, while narrowing the organization's operational role to enabling scientists through grants, software, hardware, data resources, and the [[ChanZuckerbergBiohubNetwork|Biohub network]]. Her account links [[SingleCellRNASequencing|single-cell biology]] to gene expression, cell identity, disease mechanisms, and computational models, but repeatedly says in-silico findings must translate into humans.

Chan also frames institutional design as part of science: Biohub proposals must combine multiple universities and disciplines, rare-disease support begins with patient organizations and registries, and CZI leaves much direct therapeutic development to startups, companies, clinicians, and other translational actors.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames biomedical philanthropy as enabling infrastructure rather than ownership of every cure.
- Connects single-cell measurement to the gap between genetic variation and disease mechanism.
- Treats computation as a faster hypothesis and experiment filter whose outputs still require biological validation.
- Supports collaboration across university, engineering, clinical, and patient communities.
- Grounds long-term optimism in practical effects on individual patients and families.

## Qualifications
This profile is based on one friendly interview with a CZI co-founder. It explains organizational intent and selected examples but does not independently evaluate CZI's research impact, governance, grant outcomes, tool adoption, or progress toward its century-scale mission.
