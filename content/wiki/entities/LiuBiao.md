# 刘表 / Liu Biao

## Overview
刘表 / Liu Biao is the late-Han Jingzhou ruler whose wiki profile combines real regional consolidation, defensive stability, shelter capacity, and later territorial expansion with a recurring strategic ceiling: slow change-reading, indecision, suspicion, ritual overreach, and fragile elite integration.

## Current Profile
The current synthesis presents Liu Biao as neither an empty figure nor a durable contender for empire. He turns a weak appointment into practical [[Jingzhou|Jingzhou]] control through [[KuaiLiang|蒯良]], [[KuaiYue|蒯越]], local-force removal, troop absorption, and the move to [[XiangyangLateHan|襄阳]]. He also preserves relative peace, attracts scholars, keeps some ritual claims bounded in earlier scenes, pressures Yuan Shu, and supplies Emperor Xian's damaged court.

Hanji 1053 adds Liu Biao's southern expansion after years of conflict with [[ZhangXianLateHan|张羡]]. Once Zhang Xian dies, local elites promote another southern leader, but Liu Biao eventually pacifies [[ChangshaCommanderyLateHan|长沙]], [[LinglingCommanderyLateHan|零陵]], and [[GuiyangCommanderyLateHan|桂阳]], giving him thousands of li of territory and more than one hundred thousand troops. This is real regional power. The same episode then sharpens the legitimacy warning: Liu Biao stops tribute, performs sacrifices to Heaven and Earth outside the normal bounds, and imitates emperor-level clothing and implements.

The same pattern has limits. [[JiaXu|贾诩]]'s judgment in Hanji 1010, Deng Xi's ignored warning in Hanji 1027, the Mi Heng transfer in Hanji 1011, the [[AnzhongLateHan|安重]] support for Zhang Xiu in Hanji 1023, and the Han Song sequence in Hanji 1036-1037 all point to a ruler who can shelter a region and create tactical danger for outsiders but struggles when court legitimacy, outside patrons, corrective advice, and Jingzhou elite opinion require decisive alignment.

Hanji 1054 adds Liu Biao's shelter role through [[LiuBei|刘备]]. After Liu Bei is defeated by [[CaoCao|曹操]], Liu Biao personally welcomes him, treats him as a guest, adds troops, and stations him at [[XinyeLateHan|新野]]. This reinforces Liu Biao's usefulness as a defensive regional host while also showing the risk: Jingzhou becomes a refuge and buffer for another ambitious actor whose [[ActivePoliticalDormancy|active dormancy]] can grow inside Liu Biao's space.

## Key Characteristics
- Converts formal appointment into regional control by combining benevolent legitimacy language, local elite counsel, and coercive removal of dangerous armed clan heads.
- Uses Han legitimacy and court tribute when useful, but Hanji 1053 shows that later territorial strength can push him into emperor-style ritual overreach.
- Provides real defensive local governance and regional military weight: peace, scholar refuge, commoner protection, troop absorption, southern expansion, support for frontier commanders, and shelter for Liu Bei.
- Shows a strategic ceiling in disorder because he reads change slowly, avoids decisive alignment, and treats correction as personal challenge.
- Tries to preserve option value between stronger powers even when advisers warn that neutrality itself is becoming a liability.
- Handles disruptive talent and court-facing advice through suspicion, coercion, or indirect displacement, as seen with Mi Heng and Han Song.
- Leaves Jingzhou vulnerable because southern commanderies, elite circles, ritual legitimacy, and hosted ambitious outsiders can attach to or empower forces beyond Liu Biao's own center.

## Qualifications
The page keeps the balanced synthesis intact. Liu Biao is not reduced to cowardice or stupidity: the sources show real state-building, local protection, ritual-political skill, territorial expansion, shelter capacity, and enough regional force to threaten Cao Cao's retreat when coordinated with Zhang Xiu. The negative judgment is narrower: those strengths fail to become durable imperial-level initiative once Guandu-era alignment, southern commandery fissures, court-Cao legitimacy pressure, hosted-rival risk, and Liu Biao's own ritual overreach intensify.

## What Changed
- Added Hanji 1053 as evidence for Liu Biao's southern pacification of Changsha, Lingling, and Guiyang.
- Added ritual overreach to the profile, distinguishing it from earlier ritual restraint rather than treating the two as a flat contradiction.
- Preserved the prior balanced judgment: Liu Biao has regional military weight, but his expansion and shelter capacity remain strategically limited.
