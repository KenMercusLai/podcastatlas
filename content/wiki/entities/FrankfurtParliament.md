# Frankfurt Parliament

## Overview

The Frankfurt Parliament was the German national assembly that met in St. Paul's Church during the [[RevolutionsOf1848]] to debate constitutional government and a German nation-state that did not yet exist.

## Current Profile

The assembly gathered overlapping liberal, democratic, social, labour, and national goals but struggled to turn debate into sovereign capacity. Existing German states retained armies and functioning administrations, while the parliament had no army and only a limited bureaucracy. Delay and disagreement therefore damaged public confidence as Prussia regained initiative.

The episode does not treat the parliament as meaningless. It made German national and constitutional questions institutionally explicit, provided the setting for figures such as [[RobertBlum]], and contributed to a political vocabulary that outlived its immediate defeat. Its weakness nevertheless shows that representative legitimacy without coercive and administrative capacity may fail against recovered state power.

## Key Characteristics

- German national assembly convened during the 1848 revolutionary crisis.
- Forum for constitutional, national, liberal, democratic, social, and labour claims.
- Institution without an independent army or substantial bureaucracy.
- Competitor to existing German states that continued to exercise power.
- Immediate failure whose constitutional and national language had a longer afterlife.

## Qualifications

The source offers a compressed institutional diagnosis rather than a complete account of elections, factions, constitutional drafting, territorial disputes, or the parliament's end. Its failure should not be attributed to capacity alone; disagreement, social division, and rival national claims also mattered.

## What Changed

- Established the parliament as a case of representative legitimacy constrained by weak coercive and administrative capacity.
