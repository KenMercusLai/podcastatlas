# Bernard-Rene de Launay

## Overview

Bernard-Rene de Launay was governor of the Bastille during its storming on 14 July 1789 and was lynched after surrendering the fortress.

## Current Profile

The source presents de Launay as an official trapped inside collapsing authority rather than as a simple embodiment of tyranny. He commanded 82 invalid soldiers and 32 Swiss guards, held 250 barrels of gunpowder, and lacked adequate food and water for a siege. He negotiated with municipal delegates but refused to surrender weapons and the fortress without instructions from Versailles. After confusion and gunfire brought defecting Gardes Francaises and cannon against him, he threatened to ignite the powder; his own men prevented it. He surrendered under white flags, then was beaten, stabbed, shot, and decapitated as legal custody gave way to [[RevolutionaryCrowdVengeance|crowd vengeance]].

## Key Characteristics

- Royal governor responsible for the Bastille's garrison and gunpowder.
- Logistically weak defender awaiting authority from Versailles.
- Negotiator who resisted transfer without formal orders.
- Threatened catastrophic destruction after the assault escalated.
- Prevented by his own soldiers from igniting the powder.
- Surrendered under promise or expectation of custody but was lynched.
