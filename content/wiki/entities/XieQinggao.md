# 谢清高 / Xie Qinggao

## Overview

谢清高 (c. 1765–1821) was a Qing-era traveler from Jiayingzhou, Guangdong, whose overseas experience was preserved in the orally composed [[HaiLuBook|《海录》]].

## Current Profile

The source's most defensible profile is narrower than the later legend. Xie probably traveled as a merchant or seafarer from about 1783 to 1787, was rescued after a maritime accident, returned to Macau, became blind, and later dictated his recollections to compatriots including Yang Bingnan. His account is valuable less because every report is accurate than because it preserves a civilian, commercial, pre–Opium War view of Asian and European societies.

Xie noticed Chinese migrant networks, colonial forts and troops, opium production, maritime commerce, London waterworks, and steam propulsion. He did not consistently assemble those observations into a systemic forecast of British power, and his relatively unawed view of Europe coexisted with Confucian moral judgment and hierarchical descriptions of some Asian societies.

## Key Characteristics

- **Merchant-seafarer observer:** attended to ports, commodities, migrant communities, labor, fortifications, shipping, and urban infrastructure.
- **Prewar comparative traveler:** viewed European countries before the Opium War transformed Chinese assessments of Western power.
- **Oral witness with possible notes:** dictated a substantial account after blindness, perhaps drawing on earlier written records or repeated recollection.
- **Mixed evidentiary reliability:** combined direct observation, mediated translation, hearsay, phonetic misunderstanding, and geographic error.
- **Information-without-system case:** recognized opium, military organization, colonial reach, and steam power without turning them into a comprehensive strategic diagnosis.

## Evidence

### Travel chronology and mediation
- [[lue3zoz87pipdfhtc8pmxj1axuyd-lue3zoz87pipdfhtc8pmxj1axuyd]] uses Macau judicial records to prefer roughly four years of travel, 1783–1787, and notes that Xie still required an interpreter in litigation.

### Commercial and migrant observation
- [[lue3zoz87pipdfhtc8pmxj1axuyd-lue3zoz87pipdfhtc8pmxj1axuyd]] attributes to Xie records of Chinese communities across the Malay Peninsula, Indonesia, Burma, Bengal, and India engaged in trade, agriculture, pepper, mining, and gold washing.

### Colonial power, opium, and technology
- [[lue3zoz87pipdfhtc8pmxj1axuyd-lue3zoz87pipdfhtc8pmxj1axuyd]] describes his attention to British troops and forts in Bengal, opium cultivation and processing, Britain's overseas possessions and disciplined forces, London water supply, and reported American steamships.

### Interpretive frame
- [[lue3zoz87pipdfhtc8pmxj1axuyd-lue3zoz87pipdfhtc8pmxj1axuyd]] presents him as relatively level toward Europe but still willing to judge some societies through a Confucian civilization-versus-barbarism hierarchy.

## Qualifications

The page relies on one secondary podcast summary rather than *Hai Lu*, the Macau files, or specialist scholarship. Xie's birthplace, precise occupation, shipwreck, blindness chronology, routes, note-taking, coauthors, and exact travel duration require primary verification. His descriptions range from likely firsthand observation to hearsay, and the source's contrast with later Qing officials risks making a complex transmission failure depend too heavily on a few anecdotes.

## What Changed

- Added Xie as a pre–Opium War civilian observer of overseas commerce, migration, empire, opium, and technology.
- Replaced the unqualified fourteen-year and multilingual legend with a source-attributed four-year reconstruction and evidence of translation dependence.
- Distinguished possession of striking information from the institutional capacity to interpret and act on it.
