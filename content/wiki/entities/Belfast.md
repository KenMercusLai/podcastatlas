# Belfast

## Overview

Belfast appears in the wiki as both the industrial city that built [[RMSTitanic|RMS Titanic]] during the Home Rule crisis and a principal urban arena of [[TheTroubles]] in 1969-1971.

## Current Profile

The Titanic episode presents Belfast in 1909-1912 as a globally significant shipbuilding center whose industrial achievement was inseparable from sectarian and constitutional conflict. [[HarlandAndWolff]] constructed Titanic while Home Rule mobilization, Lord Pirrie's support for Home Rule, Churchill's controversial visit, paramilitary drilling, and reported anti-Home Rule graffiti made the yard a political setting as well as a workplace.

The Troubles episodes show the later city at a different crisis threshold. In August 1969, loyalist attacks, IRA-police gunfire, RUC armoured-car fire into Divis flats, [[PatrickRooney]]'s death, destroyed homes, and displacement turned Belfast into open street conflict. In 1970-1971, [[ShortStrand]], the [[FallsCurfew]], the killing of [[RobertCurtis]], loyalist mobilization, and bombings culminating in [[McGurksBarBombing|McGurk's Bar]] made it a center of insurgency, counterinsurgency, and retaliation.

## Key Characteristics

- Major Belfast shipbuilding center represented through Harland & Wolff and Titanic.
- City where Home Rule politics entered industrial identity before the First World War.
- Urban setting where the 1969 Northern Ireland crisis expanded beyond Derry.
- Site of competing communal fears, street fighting, security-force violence, and displacement.
- Main setting for the 1970-1971 turn toward insurgency, curfew, bombing, and retaliation.

## Evidence

- Shipbuilding and Home Rule: [[428-titanic-kings-of-the-world-part-2-glt7773063166]] connects Titanic's construction and launch to Pirrie, Churchill, political drilling, and reported keel graffiti.
- 1969 escalation: [[698-the-troubles-the-brink-of-civil-war-part-2-glt2308408988]] traces loyalist attacks, IRA-police gunfire, RUC armoured-car fire, casualties, destroyed homes, and British troop deployment.
- 1970-1971 conflict: [[699-the-troubles-return-of-the-ira-part-3-glt9519811642]] follows Short Strand, Falls Curfew, armed-group growth, killings, bombing, and retaliatory escalation.

## Qualifications

These sources cover selected crisis periods rather than a continuous urban history. The Titanic episode's account of political graffiti and launch symbolism remains source-scoped, while the Troubles episodes do not represent every neighborhood, community perspective, casualty, policy, or cause of violence.
