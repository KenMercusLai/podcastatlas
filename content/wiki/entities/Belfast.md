# Belfast

## Overview

Belfast appears in the wiki as both the industrial city that built [[RMSTitanic|RMS Titanic]] during the Home Rule crisis and a principal urban arena of [[TheTroubles]] in 1969-1971.

## Current Profile

The Titanic episodes present Belfast in 1909-1912 as Ireland's richest and most industrial city, producing linen, rope, cigarettes, machinery, cars, and ocean liners through linked factories and suppliers. [[WilliamPirrie]] helped make [[HarlandAndWolff]] the world's largest shipbuilder, but industrial achievement remained inseparable from sectarian and constitutional conflict. An overwhelmingly Protestant yard, Orange Order influence, contested stories of workplace brutality, recurring riots, Home Rule mobilization, Pirrie's support for Home Rule, Churchill's controversial visit, paramilitary drilling, and reported anti-Home Rule graffiti made the workplace part of the city's political order.

The Troubles episodes show the later city at a different crisis threshold. In August 1969, loyalist attacks, IRA-police gunfire, RUC armoured-car fire into Divis flats, [[PatrickRooney]]'s death, destroyed homes, and displacement turned Belfast into open street conflict. In 1970-1971, [[ShortStrand]], the [[FallsCurfew]], the killing of [[RobertCurtis]], loyalist mobilization, and bombings culminating in [[McGurksBarBombing|McGurk's Bar]] made it a center of insurgency, counterinsurgency, and retaliation.

## Key Characteristics

- Irish industrial center whose shipbuilding, rope, linen, machinery, and supplier networks supported Titanic.
- Home of Harland & Wolff, presented as the world's largest shipbuilder under William Pirrie.
- City where Home Rule politics entered industrial identity before the First World War.
- City whose workplaces and social life were structured by sharp Protestant-Catholic division and class hierarchy.
- Urban setting where the 1969 Northern Ireland crisis expanded beyond Derry.
- Site of competing communal fears, street fighting, security-force violence, and displacement.
- Main setting for the 1970-1971 turn toward insurgency, curfew, bombing, and retaliation.

## Qualifications

These sources cover selected crisis periods rather than a continuous urban history. Industrial rankings, workforce totals, denominational proportions, the furnace story, political graffiti, and launch symbolism remain source-scoped. The contested violence story demonstrates the perceived plausibility of sectarian hostility but is not treated as verified, while the Troubles episodes do not represent every neighborhood, community perspective, casualty, policy, or cause of violence.
