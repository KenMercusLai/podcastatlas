# Cadbury

## Overview

Cadbury is the British chocolate company used in [[425-the-history-of-chocolate-glt9203655246]] to connect Quaker enterprise, industrial food production, branding, worker welfare, and the mass adoption of solid chocolate.

## Current Profile

The episode begins the company story with [[JohnCadbury]]'s 1824 Birmingham shop and 1831 factory, then follows Richard and George Cadbury through product-purity positioning, royal branding, Easter eggs, advertising, [[Bournville]], and Dairy Milk. Cadbury's competitive identity combined manufacturing and cocoa-processing advances with a wholesome, high-quality image that contrasted its products with adulterated cocoa and alcohol.

The same profile contains two boundaries. The welfare institutions and model village around Bournville were materially important but paternalistic, joining benefits to employer-shaped conduct and culture. The company also belonged to a chocolate economy whose cacao and sugar history was entangled with empire and enslaved plantation labor, while the episode itself was produced in partnership with Cadbury for the firm's 200th anniversary.

## Key Characteristics

- Birmingham chocolate business founded by John Cadbury in 1824 and expanded into factory production in 1831.
- Quaker-associated company positioning cocoa as sober, wholesome, pure, and eventually milk-rich.
- Early adopter of packaging, endorsements, royal-purple branding, advertising, Easter eggs, and modern consumer identity.
- Builder of Bournville as both a production site and a worker-welfare and model-housing project.
- Mass-market producer whose Dairy Milk launch, wartime supply, Fry combination, and falling prices widened chocolate consumption.

## Evidence

- Founding and production: [[425-the-history-of-chocolate-glt9203655246]] dates John Cadbury's Birmingham shop to 1824 and factory to 1831.
- Quality and brand: [[425-the-history-of-chocolate-glt9203655246]] describes royal positioning, purple packaging, the "Absolutely pure, therefore best" slogan, advertising, and the 1875 Easter egg.
- Welfare and place: [[425-the-history-of-chocolate-glt9203655246]] connects the Bournville works and village to shorter work time, holidays, classes, exercise, benefits, pensions, housing, and company culture.
- Mass consumption: [[425-the-history-of-chocolate-glt9203655246]] links Dairy Milk, wartime provisioning, the 1919 Fry combination, lower prices, and wider household purchasing.

## Qualifications

This is a source-scoped company profile, not a complete corporate history. The episode was sponsored by Cadbury, treats Indigenous cacao history and plantation labor briefly, and does not independently test its product, welfare, market-share, pricing, or consumption claims. Quaker social provision should not erase employer control, and later corporate ownership and labor history fall outside this source.
