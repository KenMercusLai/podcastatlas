## Overview

武秋尊 / Wu Qiuzun enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-572-lishishang-zhe-nanren-zhen-gou-hen-lkd0wugllcr6ewmmz6lyximyzl6c|Hanji 572]] as a former Yeting official whose conversation with [[JiWuWesternHan|纪武]] broadens [[XieGuangWesternHan|谢光]]'s report from one sealed-box case into an atmosphere of palace fear around [[HanChengdi|汉成帝]]'s possible heirs.

## Current Profile

Wu Qiuzun's page is source-scoped to a single reported conversation. He tells Ji Wu that Yeting officials are aligned with [[ZhaoHedeWesternHan|赵合德]] and cannot be trusted, that women who bore children by Chengdi had been killed, and that many palace women took medicine to abort pregnancies in order to survive. He first wants to report the matter to court officials, then while ill advises Ji Wu not to speak further.

His significance is evidentiary rather than biographical. Wu Qiuzun supplies the report's wider terror atmosphere, but his later retreat also shows how dangerous and unstable palace testimony can become when witnesses fear factional retaliation.

## Key Characteristics

- Former Yeting official appearing as a witness in the Xu Meiren branch.
- Describes the palace as controlled by Zhao Hede's people.
- Expands the accusation from one child corpse to a broader claim about killed children and forced abortions.
- Initially considers reporting the matter to higher officials, then reverses course under illness and fear.
- Illustrates both the gravity and fragility of testimony in palace accusation cases.

## Qualifications

The page does not establish Wu Qiuzun's full office history, reliability, or independent corroboration for the claims about killed children and abortions. His testimony is important to the accusation atmosphere but remains part of Xie Guang's politically consequential case record.
