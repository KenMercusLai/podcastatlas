## Overview

李固 / Li Gu is a late-Han upright minister whose current wiki profile now has three bounded layers: the 147-148 CE persecution and prison-death account in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-830-donghan-ligu-wo-sui-si-yourong-lt-j7n9gotiw-negqxojoacf6ji|Hanji 830]], the family-survival reconstruction in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-853-jiemi-hanchao-ban-de-zhaoshi-gu-er-lvsa2pby8iyfevfoobobpik52mij|Hanji 853]], and the later succession-memory phrase in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-929-duishi-buduiren-ni-xin-le-ma-lgaxj0jzopddmt3c5hibnexqlhb3|Hanji 929]]. The combined profile makes Li Gu a minister whom [[LiangJiLateHan|梁冀]] can kill but cannot strip of public moral standing.

## Current Profile

Hanji 830 is now the core death-and-reputation layer. Liang Ji accuses Li Gu and [[DuQiaoLateHan|杜乔]] of joining Liu Wen and Liu Wei in a hidden plot, but the case remains contested: Li Gu's student [[WangTiaoLiGuStudent|王调]] and the Henei petitioner [[ZhaoChengLiGuPetitioner|赵成]] help generate enough pressure for [[EmpressDowagerLiangNaLateHan|梁妠 / 梁太后]] to order Li Gu released. Liang Ji then reasserts the accusation, [[WuYouLateHan|吴祐]] defends Li Gu inside the proceeding, and Li Gu dies in prison after composing a final letter that frames death as completed loyalty.

The same source makes Li Gu's public meaning outlive the sentence. Liang Ji displays the bodies of Li Gu and Du Qiao north of Luoyang and bans mourning, but [[GuoLiangLiGuStudent|郭亮]] and [[DongBanNanyangLateHan|董班]] mourn Li Gu despite threats. Liang Na later pardons the mourners and permits the bodies to return home, so Li Gu's death becomes a case where [[RetaliatoryPoliticalTerror|报复性政治恐怖]] creates a visible counter-memory rather than silence.

Hanji 853 then supplies the family-survival branch. Li Gu anticipates that conflict with Liang Ji may destroy his household and sends his sons away. After the purge develops, his elder sons are captured and die in prison, while [[LiWenjiLateHan|李文姬]] preserves [[LiXieLateHan|李燮]] through concealment and [[WangChengLiGuStudent|王成]]'s protection. Emperor Huan's later 160 CE search for Li Gu's descendants makes the family disaster a posthumous repair problem.

Hanji 929 supplies the memory layer. The episode recalls Li Gu as the official who opposed installing the later [[EmperorHuanOfHan|汉桓帝]] and preferred [[LiuSuanLateHan|刘蒜]] as an older and more capable candidate. The Luoyang saying that the father would not establish an emperor and the son would not establish a king links Li Gu's stance to Li Xie's later objection to restoring [[LiuXuAnpingPrince|刘续]].

## Key Characteristics

- Upright late-Han minister whose opposition to Liang Ji becomes both a lethal political conflict and a posthumous reputation resource.
- Prison-death victim whose final letter presents death as loyalty fulfilled rather than disgrace.
- Public figure whose students, petitioners, defenders, and mourners make Liang Ji's accusation politically contested.
- Father whose line survives through Li Wenji's concealment of Li Xie and Wang Cheng's protection.
- Posthumous figure whom Emperor Huan later seeks through a 160 CE amnesty and descendant search.
- Succession precedent for Li Xie's later public-order judgment against restoring Liu Xu.

## Qualifications

This page remains bounded to the listed podcast source notes, not a full biography of Li Gu's career. Hanji 830 is treated as the final persecution layer; its Liu Wen, Liu Wei, Ma Rong drafting, Luan Ba, Xia Gate亭长, and exact-letter wording details remain source-scoped until more sources warrant canonical expansion. Hanji 853 gives the family-disaster branch and uses ASR-prone names for Li Xie, Liang Ji, and Li Gu's elder sons; the wiki normalizes the major routes but keeps the elder sons source-scoped. Hanji 929 gives a later memory of Li Gu through Li Xie rather than a complete account of the earlier accession dispute.
