## Overview

李固 / Li Gu is a late-Han official whose wiki profile now has two bounded layers: the earlier family disaster preserved in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-853-jiemi-hanchao-ban-de-zhaoshi-gu-er-lvsa2pby8iyfevfoobobpik52mij|Hanji 853]], and the later succession-memory phrase in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-929-duishi-buduiren-ni-xin-le-ma-lgaxj0jzopddmt3c5hibnexqlhb3|Hanji 929]]. Together, the sources make Li Gu both a victim of [[LiangJiLateHan|梁冀]]-era persecution and the father whose public-order judgment echoes through [[LiXieLateHan|李燮]].

## Current Profile

Hanji 853 now gives the survival prehistory. Li Gu offends Liang Ji, anticipates that his household may be destroyed, and sends his sons away. After the purge develops, the elder sons are captured and die in prison with Li Gu, while [[LiWenjiLateHan|李文姬]] preserves Li Xie through concealment and [[WangChengLiGuStudent|王成]]'s protection. Emperor Huan's later 160 CE search for Li Gu's descendants makes the family disaster a posthumous repair problem.

Hanji 929 supplies the memory layer. The episode recalls Li Gu as the official who opposed installing the later [[EmperorHuanOfHan|汉桓帝]] and preferred [[LiuSuanLateHan|刘蒜]] as an older and more capable candidate. The Luoyang saying that the father would not establish an emperor and the son would not establish a king links Li Gu's stance to Li Xie's later objection to restoring [[LiuXuAnpingPrince|刘续]].

## Key Characteristics

- Late-Han official remembered for resisting a disputed accession choice.
- Victim of Liang Ji-linked persecution whose household faces near-erasure.
- Father whose line survives through Li Wenji's concealment of Li Xie and Wang Cheng's protection.
- Posthumous figure whom Emperor Huan later seeks through a 160 CE amnesty and descendant search.
- Source-bounded precedent for Li Xie's later public-order judgment against restoring Liu Xu.

## Qualifications

This page remains bounded to the listed podcast source notes, not a full biography of Li Gu's career. Hanji 853 gives the family-disaster branch and uses ASR-prone names for Li Xie, Liang Ji, and Li Gu's elder sons; the wiki normalizes the major routes but keeps the elder sons source-scoped. Hanji 929 gives a later memory of Li Gu through Li Xie rather than a complete account of the earlier accession dispute.
