## Overview
李固 / Li Gu is a late-Han upright official whose current profile now begins before the 133 CE disaster-policy sequence. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-809-weishenme-quan-ni-buyao-luan-pai-mapi-lnv2l-ehkzmf2ifz3semscaqftku|Hanji 809]] supplies his learned formation and his 127 CE letter warning [[HuangQiongLateHan|黄琼]] against reputation-only reclusion. [[LangYiLateHan|郎顗]] later recommends him to [[EmperorShunOfHan|汉顺帝]] in Hanji 813, and Li Gu's first visible policy answer attacks wet-nurse title and overpowered Liang-family access before Hanji 814 broadens the same [[OmenCodedRemonstrance|天象灾异包装式进谏]] line into [[PrivateFavorAppointmentErosion|私恩任官侵蚀]], Shangshu integrity, central-court repair, and eunuch-power reduction. Hanji 815 then shows one partial implementation: his advice gets [[SongELateHan|宋娥]] removed from the palace, but Song E and eunuchs retaliate with forged anonymous charges. Hanji 817 shows him urging [[LiangShangLateHan|梁商]] to rectify court order, select loyal and worthy people, and treat disasters as warnings, though Liang Shang does not adopt the advice. Hanji 818 shows him rescuing [[WangGongTaiweiLateHan|王龚]] by writing to Liang Shang; Hanji 819 shows him rejecting a 40,000-soldier Jiaozhi expedition and replacing it with fit appointments, temporary Rinan retrenchment, rewards, and surrender policy. Hanji 822 then shows him pacifying thieves in [[Jingzhou|荆州]] and [[Taishan|泰山]] through pardon, credibility, reduced force, and anti-corruption resolve even when [[LiangJiLateHan|梁冀]] protects corrupt officials. Later notes carry him through Emperor Chong's death, failed-service clemency, [[EmperorZhiOfHan|汉质帝]]'s poisoned deathbed, resistance to Liu Zhi's accession, prison death, defiant mourning, family survival, and contested-accession memory.

## Current Profile
[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-809-weishenme-quan-ni-buyao-luan-pai-mapi-lnv2l-ehkzmf2ifz3semscaqftku|Hanji 809]] supplies the earliest current layer. Li Gu is introduced as the son of Li He, but the episode emphasizes learned formation rather than pedigree: he loves reading, changes his name while traveling with books on a donkey to seek teachers, hides his elite-family identity, and privately visits his parents while avoiding dependency on family status.

The same source gives Li Gu an early adviser role before his later court memorials. When Huang Qiong reaches Luoyang after being summoned by Shun, Li Gu writes that he should not wait for a perfect ruler or retreat into sterile purity if he is not truly a recluse. He warns that high reputation can collapse when actual contribution does not match it, using Fan Ying as the negative example. This makes Li Gu's public-duty ethic visible before the 133 CE Song E, Liang-family, and Shangshu-repair sequence.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-813-gudai-de-gaoguan-jing-ye-hui-bei-ouda-ln-dw3xohnmpavkpu-bdvasvietv|Hanji 813]] supplies the next current layer. Lang Yi recommends Li Gu and Huang Qiong after a drought-remonstrance memorial, bringing Li Gu into the Shun-era repair field. Later in the same 133 CE disaster sequence, Shun asks for policy answers after earthquakes, landslides, and fire; Li Gu criticizes the Wang Sheng precedent, Song E's title, and the concentration of Liang-family palace posts. He distinguishes repayable private service from titles, land, and office power, arguing that excessive outer-relative privilege can end badly for the favored family as well as the state.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-814-dangguan-buwei-min-zuozhu-buru-huijia-mai-hongshu-lqlrfpuenzhr1tbrdd3urtolfw2p|Hanji 814]] continues that 133 CE disaster-remonstrance layer. After the Xuande Hall ground split, [[EmperorShunOfHan|Emperor Shun]] asks senior officials and newly arrived appointees to explain the disaster and propose repairs. Li Gu uses the opening to argue that private gifts are less dangerous than granting titles and offices to wet nurses, empress-kin, attendants' children, and other near-court actors. He treats appointment as a public matter because even a small office can harm the people when its holder enters through favor rather than fitness.

The same memorial gives Li Gu's central-court theory. Shangshu offices are the ruler's throat and tongue, the court is the heart and belly, and provinces and commanderies are the limbs; if the center is diseased, outer administration will follow. Li Gu therefore asks Shun to enforce appointment rules, choose fair and upright Shangshu, and sharply reduce eunuch and inner-court power by retaining only a small number of upright close attendants and capable minor officials.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-815-kebei-gudai-zhongchen-de-jiafeng-zhishang-llkhbxo-63pjw-gk9pihnbfr94v|Hanji 815]] supplies the partial adoption and retaliation layer. In 133 CE, Shun reviews ministerial advice and adopts Li Gu's proposal to send Song E, the emperor's wet nurse, out of the palace. Eunuchs kneel and ask pardon, and the court briefly becomes serious and restrained. Song E and eunuchs then resent Li Gu, forge anonymous letters or charges, and Shun orders Li Gu investigated by direct edict outside the Shangshu Tai. Li Gu is cleared, but the episode still ends with his transfer to Luo county magistrate and resignation home to Hanzhong.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-817-kan-si-ruanruo-de-ren-shize-huode-geng-tongtou-llmgqys237de3dzb6ij-4szyetye|Hanji 817]] supplies the next current layer. After Liang Shang becomes Grand General and recruits Li Gu, Li Gu argues that repeated disasters and anomalies should prompt examination of root causes, restoration of court discipline, public uprightness, and selection of loyal and worthy people. Liang Shang does not adopt the advice. This adds an early failed-reform channel before Li Gu's more successful Jiaozhi and local-pacification work.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-818-guanchang-weishenme-yao-duo-jiaoyou-neng-jiuming-liyziudpezaoa4l6pbsq0wk2qdjdr|Hanji 818]] supplies the next layer. After Wang Gong's anti-eunuch memorial draws accusation from eunuch-linked clients, Li Gu writes urgently to Liang Shang. He argues that a Three Excellency should not be humiliated by being hauled before judicial offices unless the case is grave enough, and Liang Shang's presentation to Shun gets the inquiry canceled. This adds a court-network protection role before Li Gu's later frontier and succession profile.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-819-2-ren-gong-bi-4-wanren-tade-quanmou-lingren-paianjuejue-lsslj3y-krnd8h-c8x5pgsqeinff|Hanji 819]] supplies the next layer. During the 138 CE [[JiaozhiCommandery|交趾郡]] crisis, Li Gu opposes the court majority's plan to send a major general and 40,000 troops from Jingzhou, Yangzhou, and Yuzhou. He argues that local unrest, repeated conscription, despair at distant deployment, southern disease, long travel, food cost, and follow-up levies would deepen the crisis before solving it.

Li Gu then supplies a full alternative rather than a bare objection. He proposes choosing brave, wise, humane officials, temporarily relocating Rinan officials and civilians north when the position lacks troops and grain, using money and cloth to split hostile groups, and rewarding those who undermine or kill rebel leaders. He recommends [[ZhangQiaoLateHan|张乔]] and [[ZhuLiangLateHan|祝良]]; [[EmperorShunOfHan|汉顺帝]] accepts the plan, Zhang Qiao reassures rebels into surrender, and Zhu Liang enters the Jiuzhen rebel camp alone by carriage and wins over tens of thousands.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-822-chailang-dangdao-ta-jiang-maotou-zhizhi-huangdi-yuefu-lqhpmayvd3p07byr-zoiv-jyhdv|Hanji 822]] supplies the next local-governance layer. As Jingzhou inspector, Li Gu sends officials to comfort and pardon thieves, keeps the promise when Xia Mi and more than six hundred leaders surrender bound, and restores order within about half a year. He then impeaches corrupt Nanyang officials despite their bribing Liang Ji for protection; Liang has him moved to Taishan, but Li Gu again pacifies a long-running theft crisis by dismissing most troops, keeping a small capable force, and using reputation plus grace to induce surrender.

Li Gu's first 145 CE court-judgment layer begins after [[EmperorChongOfHan|汉冲帝]] dies. In [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-825-lishi-gaosu-ni-dang-ni-meiyong-shi-renxing-zui-zisi-luv-8z9axuejnfhlkgsuxee3uked|Hanji 825]], Li Gu persuades [[EmpressDowagerLiangNaLateHan|梁妠]] not to delay the death announcement, citing earlier concealed-death succession dangers. He then urges Liang Ji to choose the older and respected [[LiuSuanLateHan|刘蒜]] rather than the child Liu Zuan, but Liang Ji and Liang Na select Liu Zuan, the future Emperor Zhi. Li Gu's later modest-burial recommendation and cleanup of harmful eunuchs and irregular officials create the resentment that becomes [[AnonymousSlanderRetaliation|匿名诬告式报复]].

The second 145 CE layer shows Li Gu protecting failed but risk-taking officials. After [[ZhongHaoLateHan|种暠]] and [[YingChengLateHan|应承]] fail to suppress [[FuZhiLateHan|服直]] in [[BaCommanderyLateHan|巴郡]], Liang Ji has them arrested. Li Gu argues that killing officials who tried to serve but failed would teach others not to exert themselves, and Liang Na reduces the penalty to dismissal.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-827-yi-ju-hua-yinfa-de-diwang-zhisi-lnf6rz1i2vmgsclgiejbh0qevzc2|Hanji 827]] supplies the first Huan-era danger layer. When Emperor Zhi suffers abdominal pain after eating poisoned soup cake, he asks for Li Gu and says water might keep him alive. Liang Ji blocks the request, the emperor dies, and Li Gu's grief over the body plus his rebuke of the attending medical staff make him an immediate exposure risk for Liang Ji.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-828-da-jiangjun-liangji-shanquan-feili-de-donghan-jianchen-llqnqijagtmdlgitulddxspncu0e|Hanji 828]] supplies the direct accession-dispute layer. When [[CaoTengLateHan|曹腾]] persuades Liang Ji to install Liu Zhi as [[EmperorHuanOfHan|汉桓帝]], Li Gu resists alongside [[DuQiaoLateHan|杜乔]]. Even after the senior court submits to Liang Ji's pressure, Li Gu writes further to Liang Ji, warning against the chosen course. Liang Ji grows angrier and has [[EmpressDowagerLiangNaLateHan|梁妠 / 梁太后]] remove Li Gu from the taiwei office.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-829-junzi-weishenme-doubuguo-xiaoren-lthvnstkfu8n9thx1sz6yezl4yqq|Hanji 829]] shows how that earlier succession stance becomes accusation material. Li Gu's preference for Liu Suan touches Liang-family interests, and [[TangHengLateHan|唐衡]] plus [[ZuoGuanLateHan|左悺]] later tell Huan that Li Gu and Du Qiao opposed his accession, turning a public-order judgment into personal imperial resentment.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-830-donghan-ligu-wo-sui-si-yourong-lt-j7n9gotiw-negqxojoacf6ji|Hanji 830]] gives the core death-and-reputation layer. Liang Ji accuses Li Gu and Du Qiao of joining the [[LiuWenQingheRebelLateHan|刘文]] and [[LiuWeiNanjunRebelLateHan|刘伟]] plot; [[WangTiaoLiGuStudent|王调]] and [[ZhaoChengLiGuPetitioner|赵成]] generate enough pressure for Liang Na to order Li Gu released, but Liang Ji reasserts the accusation. [[WuYouLateHan|吴祐]] defends Li Gu inside the proceeding, and Li Gu dies in prison after composing a final letter that frames death as completed loyalty.

The same source makes Li Gu's public meaning outlive the sentence. Liang Ji displays the bodies of Li Gu and Du Qiao north of Luoyang and bans mourning, but [[GuoLiangLiGuStudent|郭亮]] and [[DongBanNanyangLateHan|董班]] mourn Li Gu despite threats. Liang Na later pardons the mourners and permits the bodies to return home, so Li Gu's death becomes a case where [[RetaliatoryPoliticalTerror|报复性政治恐怖]] creates visible counter-memory rather than silence.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-853-jiemi-hanchao-ban-de-zhaoshi-gu-er-lvsa2pby8iyfevfoobobpik52mij|Hanji 853]] supplies the family-survival branch. Li Gu anticipates household catastrophe and sends his sons away; his elder sons are captured and die in prison, while [[LiWenjiLateHan|李文姬]] preserves [[LiXieLateHan|李燮]] through concealment and [[WangChengLiGuStudent|王成]]'s protection. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-929-duishi-buduiren-ni-xin-le-ma-lgaxj0jzopddmt3c5hibnexqlhb3|Hanji 929]] preserves the later saying that the father would not establish an emperor and the son would not establish a king.

## Key Characteristics
- Learned public-duty adviser whose early profile combines disguised study travel, hidden pedigree, and a warning to Huang Qiong against reputation-only reclusion.
- Early reform adviser who uses disaster and anomaly warnings to urge Liang Shang toward court rectification.
- Court ally who protects Wang Gong by converting a eunuch-backed accusation into a procedural-status defense routed through Liang Shang.
- Strategic problem-solver whose Jiaozhi memorial replaces an overlarge expedition with fitted appointments, retrenchment, rewards, and surrender policy.
- Local governor whose Jingzhou and Taishan pacification combines pardon, credibility, selective force, and public trust.
- Court and institutional-risk official whose Nanyang impeachment resists Liang Ji's protection, whose Emperor Chong memorial rejects concealed death, and whose Zhong Hao memorial resists execution for failed service.
- Upright succession minister whose Emperor Zhi deathbed exposure and Liu Suan judgment threaten Liang-family and eunuch interests before removal, prison death, and posthumous reputation.
