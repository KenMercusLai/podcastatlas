# Attack on Pearl Harbor

## Overview

The attack on Pearl Harbor was Japan's surprise carrier strike on Oahu on 7 December 1941, an immediate operational success that crippled the American battleship force while bringing the United States fully into the Second World War.

## Current Profile

[[dchha63-supernova-in-the-east-ii-dchha63-supernova-in-the-east-ii|Supernova in the East II]] reconstructs the raid as the endpoint of economic pressure, failed diplomacy, mobilization deadlines, and [[IsorokuYamamoto|Yamamoto's]] attempt to prevent American interference with Japan's southern offensives. Six carriers approached by a remote northern route under radio silence, while Allied expectations remained focused on Southeast Asia, the Dutch East Indies, and the Philippines.

Warning signs did not become an effective alert. A midget submarine was detected, radar operators saw incoming aircraft, and Japanese diplomatic notice arrived too late to provide the intended warning. Two attack waves struck ships and airfields, using specially modified torpedoes in shallow water and a heavy bomb that caused the catastrophic explosion of *Arizona*.

The raid killed more than 2,400 people, wounded more than 1,000, destroyed nearly 200 aircraft, and temporarily disabled the battleship force at low immediate Japanese cost. Yet the American carriers were absent, many ships could be recovered from shallow water, and surprise attack united American opinion for war. The operation's immediate success therefore cannot be separated from its strategic limits.

## Key Characteristics

- Executed by a six-carrier force that crossed the North Pacific undetected.
- Achieved complete tactical surprise despite submarine and radar warnings.
- Struck both airfields and ships in two main waves.
- Devastated the battleship force and caused severe American casualties at low immediate Japanese cost.
- Failed to destroy American aircraft carriers because they were absent from the harbor.
- Converted American political division over intervention into determined entry into the war.

## Qualifications

The supplied source is a structured narrative summary, not an operational archive. Exact warning responsibility, the diplomatic-message delay, casualty and damage totals, whether a further strike should have been launched, and the relative strategic value of battleships, carriers, repair facilities, and fuel storage remain source-scoped or outside its full treatment.

## What Changed

- Established Pearl Harbor as the operational culmination of Japan's China-war and resource crisis rather than an isolated surprise.
- Separated immediate damage from the raid's adverse political and long-term strategic effects.
