# Warner Music Group

## Overview

Warner Music Group is a music rights holder using settlement, licensing, and opt-in experimentation as its current response to [[Suno]].

## Current Profile

[[tech-20260817-tech-pod-128-tech-20260817-tech-pod-128]] establishes Warner as the contrasting label case: it settled with Suno while [[UniversalMusicGroup]] and [[SonyMusic]] continued litigation. [[unrest-in-peace-misery-in-gaza-145c0cf22e24027ee45e02b7352ac088]] adds the proposed commercial form. Warner expects AI-partnership revenue in the current financial year, Suno says its V6 models use licensed Warner and other music, and artists and songwriters may opt into fan remixes for a share of subscription revenue. This is an operational test of licensing, not evidence that the market or compensation model has succeeded.

## Key Characteristics

- Major music rights holder pursuing a negotiated Suno relationship.
- Contrast to labels that remain in active litigation.
- Supplier of licensed catalogue material according to Suno's V6 claim.
- Sponsor of artist and songwriter opt-in rather than automatic catalogue participation.
- Early test of fan-remix demand and AI partnership revenue.

## Evidence

- Strategic contrast: [[tech-20260817-tech-pod-128-tech-20260817-tech-pod-128]] says Warner settled and began working with Suno.
- Commercial design: [[unrest-in-peace-misery-in-gaza-145c0cf22e24027ee45e02b7352ac088]] reports licensed training, opt-in participation, revenue sharing, and expected partnership revenue.
- Adoption limit: [[unrest-in-peace-misery-in-gaza-145c0cf22e24027ee45e02b7352ac088]] says no major music stars had opted in at the time discussed.

## Qualifications

Neither source provides the settlement, licences, revenue splits, audit rights, catalogue scope, or safeguards for artist identity. Warner's expected revenue and Suno's training statement are forward-looking or company-reported rather than demonstrated outcomes.

## What Changed

- Added the licensed-training, opt-in, and subscription-revenue design of the Suno partnership.
- Added near-term revenue expectations without treating them as realized success.
- Added lack of major-artist adoption as the central current qualification.
