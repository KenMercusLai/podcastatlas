# Kenji Yoshino

## Overview
Kenji Yoshino is a legal scholar presented in [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-b9a08666432cea3e0a417d731ebdaf41-104957641-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-b9a08666432cea3e0a417d731ebdaf41-104957641|Dropping the Mask]] as the developer and public interpreter of the [[Covering|covering]] framework.

## Current Profile
The episode connects Yoshino's scholarship to his experience as a gay Japanese American who moved through attempted conversion, passing, and professional covering. His current position is neither compulsory disclosure nor authenticity without limits: communities should examine the demand and its harm, weigh legitimate competing values, resist conformity imposed merely for comfort, and create conditions in which people can disclose without being forced.

Yoshino also presents covering as a bridge across groups because most people manage some non-dominant trait. He preserves differences in structural power, however, and does not equate everyday insecurity with identities shaped by long histories of subordination.

## Key Characteristics
- Distinguishes conversion, passing, and covering as different modes of assimilation and identity management.
- Grounds the framework in personal experiences of sexuality, family, race, scholarship, and professional pressure.
- Evaluates covering through harm and legitimate competing values rather than treating authenticity as absolute.
- Uses distinct and diffuse storytelling to model resistance to unjustified covering demands.
- Supports ally intervention that challenges stigma without shifting disclosure burdens onto vulnerable people.
- Treats identity as fluid and narrative rather than a fixed essence hidden beneath social performance.
