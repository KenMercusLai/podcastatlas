# 杜根 / Du Gen (late Han)

## Overview

Du Gen is used in Hanji 895 as a comparison case for surviving political danger without implicating old friends and kin.

## Current Profile

The source recalls Du Gen as an earlier Han An-di era official who hid after offending Empress Dowager Deng and later returned to office. His importance in this page is not a complete career biography; it is his explanation that he avoided friends and acquaintances while hidden because discovery would have brought disaster to them. That makes him a deliberate contrast to Zhang Jian's refuge-seeking path.

## Key Characteristics

- Political-persecution survivor whose hiding is remembered as controlled exposure rather than social flight through friends.
- Comparison figure for Zhang Jian because both face danger, but Du Gen refuses to make helpers collateral.
- Later-return figure who shows that withdrawal can preserve life without becoming permanent social death.
- Source-scoped ethical example for non-contact as care toward potential hosts.

## Evidence

- Persecution and hiding: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-895-xuexing-tudao-xia-de-gurou-qingshen-llsplyyrjxhqkpi2aiuqzulgm6w3|Hanji 895]] says Du Gen hid in mountains and forests after offending Empress Dowager Deng.
- Refusal to implicate friends: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-895-xuexing-tudao-xia-de-gurou-qingshen-llsplyyrjxhqkpi2aiuqzulgm6w3|Hanji 895]] says Du Gen later explained that identity exposure among friends would have endangered those friends and relatives.
- Later service: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-895-xuexing-tudao-xia-de-gurou-qingshen-llsplyyrjxhqkpi2aiuqzulgm6w3|Hanji 895]] notes that Du Gen was later reused by the court.

## Qualifications

The episode uses Du Gen as a contrast, not as its main subject. Details of his original remonstrance, punishment, and later official career remain outside this source note.
