## Overview
杜根 / Du Gen now has both his original An-era survival layer and his later party-prohibition comparison layer. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-793-lishi-zhuming-nvhuang-dengsui-zenme-si-de-lszfgj7waiuusrddzjmv5mbrp1i|Hanji 793]] gives the return-power remonstrance, near-execution, feigned death, fifteen-year concealment, and restoration; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-895-xuexing-tudao-xia-de-gurou-qingshen-llsplyyrjxhqkpi2aiuqzulgm6w3|Hanji 895]] later remembers him as a comparison case for surviving political danger without implicating old friends and kin.

## Current Profile
During [[EmpressDowagerDengLateHan|Empress Dowager Deng]]'s regency, Du Gen and other lang officials petition that [[EmperorAnOfHan|Emperor An]] is grown and should rule personally. Deng has them stuffed in white silk bags, beaten, and hauled out as dead. Du Gen survives by feigning death; after days of inspection and physical decay, he is believed dead and escapes the punishment field.

Du then hides for fifteen years as a waiter in a mountain tavern in Yicheng. When Emperor An begins personal rule after Deng's death, [[ChenZhongLateHan|Chen Zhong]] recommends upright people, and Du Gen is summoned and appointed shiyushi. This makes Du a restoration figure as well as a persecution survivor.

Hanji 895 preserves the later ethical memory of that survival. Du's importance there is not a complete career biography; it is his explanation that he avoided friends and acquaintances while hidden because discovery would have brought disaster to them. That makes him a deliberate contrast to [[ZhangJianLateHan|Zhang Jian]]'s refuge-seeking path.

## Key Characteristics
- Return-power remonstrant punished for urging Empress Dowager Deng to yield authority to the adult emperor.
- Political-persecution survivor whose feigned death prevents execution from becoming final.
- Long-concealment figure who hides in low-status work for fifteen years before court restoration.
- Later-return figure who shows that withdrawal can preserve life without becoming permanent social death.
- Comparison figure for Zhang Jian because both face danger, but Du Gen refuses to make helpers collateral.
- Source-scoped ethical example for non-contact as care toward potential hosts.

## Evidence
- Return-power remonstrance: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-793-lishi-zhuming-nvhuang-dengsui-zenme-si-de-lszfgj7waiuusrddzjmv5mbrp1i|Hanji 793]] says Du Gen and other lang officials petitioned that Emperor An was grown and should govern personally.
- Punishment and survival: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-793-lishi-zhuming-nvhuang-dengsui-zenme-si-de-lszfgj7waiuusrddzjmv5mbrp1i|Hanji 793]] says Deng ordered the remonstrants bagged and beaten, while Du survived by feigning death until inspectors believed he was dead.
- Concealment and restoration: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-793-lishi-zhuming-nvhuang-dengsui-zenme-si-de-lszfgj7waiuusrddzjmv5mbrp1i|Hanji 793]] says Du hid for fifteen years as a mountain tavern waiter and was later appointed shiyushi after An accepted Chen Zhong's recommendation.
- Persecution-memory use: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-895-xuexing-tudao-xia-de-gurou-qingshen-llsplyyrjxhqkpi2aiuqzulgm6w3|Hanji 895]] recalls Du Gen as an earlier official who hid after offending Empress Dowager Deng and later returned to office.
- Refusal to implicate friends: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-895-xuexing-tudao-xia-de-gurou-qingshen-llsplyyrjxhqkpi2aiuqzulgm6w3|Hanji 895]] says Du later explained that identity exposure among friends would have endangered those friends and relatives.
- Contrast with Zhang Jian: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-895-xuexing-tudao-xia-de-gurou-qingshen-llsplyyrjxhqkpi2aiuqzulgm6w3|Hanji 895]] contrasts Du's non-contact concealment with Zhang Jian's flight through host networks.

## Qualifications
The current evidence is strongest on Du Gen's remonstrance, survival, concealment ethic, and restoration. It does not settle the names and fates of all co-petitioners, the exact legal procedure behind Deng's punishment order, Du's later career after shiyushi, or whether the Hanji 895 comparison preserves Du's own words or a later moralized memory.
