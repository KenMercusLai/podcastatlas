## Overview

临江王刘荣 / Liu Rong is presented as [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]]'s former crown prince whose demotion, prosecution, imprisonment, and suicide become part of the political clearing around [[HanWudi|刘彻]]'s succession.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-240-hanjingdi-kusu-xiongnu-he-taihou-sha-le-wode-ying-lvl4t-2etswvi3ts9gbvor5dh8mi|Hanji 240]] places Liu Rong in a formally legal case over expansion of his Linjiang palace into land associated with [[LiuHeng|汉文帝]]'s ancestral temple. He is sent to the zhongwei office, where [[ZhiDuWesternHan|郅都]] imprisons him and denies him a knife and writing tablet. [[DouYingWesternHan|窦婴]] reportedly supplies them covertly; Liu Rong writes a final message and dies by suicide.

The episode interprets the case through succession politics: a living former heir remains a possible threat to Liu Che, and the host therefore attributes major responsibility to Jingdi. That inference is politically coherent within the episode but does not establish a direct imperial order to kill Liu Rong, the legal sufficiency of the land charge, or Liu Rong's private conclusion that he had to die for his younger brother.

His death changes more than the heir field. [[EmpressDowagerDouWesternHan|窦太后]]'s grief turns Zhi Du's enforcement into a dynastic grievance and later helps make the official expendable. Liu Rong therefore appears both as a removed succession claimant and as the event through which court coercion rebounds on its agent.

## Key Characteristics

- Eldest son of Han Jingdi presented as a former crown prince and later king of Linjiang.
- Royal defendant accused of palace expansion onto land linked to Emperor Wen's temple precinct.
- Prisoner denied writing tools by Zhi Du and covertly assisted by Dou Ying.
- Former heir whose suicide is interpreted by the host as clearing risk around Liu Che's accession.
- Grandson whose death produces a lasting grievance for Empress Dowager Dou.

## Qualifications

This profile rests on one compact popular-history account. It does not establish the complete demotion sequence, exact land survey or temple rule, governing statute, prison procedure, wording of the final message, means and circumstances of death, Dou Ying's access mechanism, Jingdi's direct instructions, or Liu Rong's private motive. The succession interpretation is retained as the host's judgment, not converted into proof of a planned killing.

## What Changed

- Created a bounded profile separating the reported prosecution and suicide from the host's stronger succession-murder interpretation.
- Identified Liu Rong's death as both an heir-field change and the grievance that later exposes Zhi Du.
