## Overview

临江王刘荣 / Liu Rong is presented as [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]]'s former crown prince whose lost marriage alliance and maternal-household trust precede demotion, while his later prosecution, imprisonment, and suicide become part of the political clearing around [[HanWudi|刘彻]]'s succession.

## Current Profile

The pre-demotion evidence adds a coalition sequence missing from the later custody account. [[LiuPiaoWesternHan|馆陶长公主刘嫖]] initially proposes marrying [[EmpressChenAjiaoWesternHan|陈阿娇]] to Liu Rong, but his mother [[ConsortLiJingdiWesternHan|丽姬]] rejects the match. Liu Piao then supports a Chen-Liu Che match and reportedly advocates for Liu Che with Jingdi. Jingdi's separate request that Li care for other consorts and sons after his death, followed by her refusal, makes the episode frame Liu Rong's household as a future security risk. Wang's alleged ministerial maneuver then precedes Liu Rong's deposition and reclassification as king of Linjiang. This is a coalition-and-trust explanation, not proof that one refusal or plot alone caused the outcome. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-239-bie-beipianle-jinwu-cangjiao-beihoude-yinmou-zhenxiang-1-ljdaasdscqwie1ishma3nwvgzhqp|Hanji 239 part 1]]

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-240-hanjingdi-kusu-xiongnu-he-taihou-sha-le-wode-ying-lvl4t-2etswvi3ts9gbvor5dh8mi|Hanji 240]] places the later Liu Rong in a formally legal case over expansion of his Linjiang palace into land associated with [[LiuHeng|汉文帝]]'s ancestral temple. He is sent to the zhongwei office, where [[ZhiDuWesternHan|郅都]] imprisons him and denies him a knife and writing tablet. [[DouYingWesternHan|窦婴]] reportedly supplies them covertly; Liu Rong writes a final message and dies by suicide.

The episode interprets the case through succession politics: a living former heir remains a possible threat to Liu Che, and the host therefore attributes major responsibility to Jingdi. That inference is politically coherent within the episode but does not establish a direct imperial order to kill Liu Rong, the legal sufficiency of the land charge, or Liu Rong's private conclusion that he had to die for his younger brother.

His death changes more than the heir field. [[EmpressDowagerDouWesternHan|窦太后]]'s grief turns Zhi Du's enforcement into a dynastic grievance and later helps make the official expendable. Liu Rong therefore appears both as a removed succession claimant and as the event through which court coercion rebounds on its agent.

## Key Characteristics

- Eldest son of Han Jingdi presented as a former crown prince and later king of Linjiang.
- Loses Liu Piao's proposed marriage alliance after Li rejects a match with Chen Ajiao.
- Royal defendant accused of palace expansion onto land linked to Emperor Wen's temple precinct.
- Prisoner denied writing tools by Zhi Du and covertly assisted by Dou Ying.
- Former heir whose suicide is interpreted by the host as clearing risk around Liu Che's accession.
- Grandson whose death produces a lasting grievance for Empress Dowager Dou.

## Qualifications

This profile rests on two compact popular-history accounts. They do not establish the complete demotion mechanism, the marriage proposal or children's agency, Liu Piao's exact advocacy, Jingdi's private judgment, Wang's covert instruction, exact land survey or temple rule, governing statute, prison procedure, wording of the final message, means and circumstances of death, Dou Ying's access mechanism, Jingdi's direct instructions, or Liu Rong's private motive. Coalition loss and the later succession-security interpretation are retained as host-level explanations, not converted into proof of a single plan or ordered killing.

## What Changed

- Added the rejected marriage, transferred advocacy, household-security test, and ministerial request as a bounded pre-demotion sequence.
- Kept that alliance-and-trust sequence separate from proof of a single plot or complete deposition cause.
