## Overview

沛王刘辅 / Liu Fu, Pei King is now synthesized as [[LiuXiu|光武帝刘秀]]'s son by the deposed Guo empress line whose title origin and later household-risk profile sit in different late-Guangwu layers. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-722-rang-liuxiu-xiansi-de-toutongbing-zenme-huishi-logwccdsca7vm2zzssu8ubm8rwra|Hanji 722]] gives the 44 CE re-enfeoffment from Zhongshan king to Pei king and treats it as part of Liu Xiu's compensation toward the Guo branch; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-728-weihe-mingchen-nan-shanzhong-beihou-de-yuanyin-ni-gai-dong-ltjl2bgpyyz1bmwe-4mfcyd9e6cf|Hanji 728]] later shows how Liu Fu's favor toward [[LiuLiShouguangMarquisEasternHan|寿光侯刘李]] exposes private princely households as state-security problems.

## Current Profile

Hanji 722 gives Liu Fu's earlier identity layer. In June 44 CE, Liu Xiu changes Liu Fu from Zhongshan king to Pei king. The source identifies him as Liu Xiu's second son by the first empress, Guo Shengtong, and reads the move alongside Liu Xiu's continued rewards to the Guo family after Guo's deposition.

Hanji 728 gives Liu Fu a later prince-guest-risk profile. Liu Li, son of Liu Xuan and Shouguang Marquis, is favored by Liu Fu. Because Liu Li resents Liu Penzi, he gathers guests and kills Liu Penzi's elder brother Liu Gong. Liu Xiu becomes furious, and Liu Fu is punished by being put in the imperial prison before being released three days later.

The source then places Liu Fu among the princes sent back to their fiefs after the broader guest purge. On August 19, 52 CE, Liu Qiang, Liu Fu, Liu Ying, Liu Kang, and Liu Yan each return to their fiefs. In this bounded evidence, Liu Fu matters as a prince whose household favor becomes implicated in private guest violence and then in the court's territorial-distance response.

## Key Characteristics

- Eastern Han royal prince whose Pei title is first explained by a 44 CE re-enfeoffment from Zhongshan.
- Son of Liu Xiu and the first empress Guo Shengtong in Hanji 722's bounded account.
- Favors Liu Li, whose private guests kill Liu Gong.
- Punished by brief confinement in the imperial prison after Liu Xiu becomes angry.
- Returns to his fief after the national purge of princely guests.
- Distinct from later or same-pinyin Liu Fu figures outside this source.

## Qualifications

This page remains a bounded profile rather than a full Liu Fu biography. Hanji 722 explains the Pei title and Guo-family context but does not yet develop Liu Fu's conduct. Hanji 728 does not prove Liu Fu personally ordered Liu Gong's killing; it shows that Liu Fu's favor toward Liu Li made his household politically exposed after Liu Li mobilized guests. Liu Fu remains distinct from [[LiuFuLateHan|刘馥 / Liu Fu, late Han]].
