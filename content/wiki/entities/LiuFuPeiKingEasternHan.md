## Overview

沛王刘辅 / Liu Fu, Pei King enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-728-weihe-mingchen-nan-shanzhong-beihou-de-yuanyin-ni-gai-dong-ltjl2bgpyyz1bmwe-4mfcyd9e6cf|Hanji 728]] as a royal prince whose favor toward [[LiuLiShouguangMarquisEasternHan|寿光侯刘李]] exposes how private princely households can become state-security problems.

## Current Profile

Hanji 728 gives Liu Fu a short but important prince-guest-risk profile. Liu Li, son of Liu Xuan and Shouguang Marquis, is favored by Liu Fu. Because Liu Li resents Liu Penzi, he gathers guests and kills Liu Penzi's elder brother Liu Gong. Liu Xiu becomes furious, and Liu Fu is punished by being put in the imperial prison before being released three days later.

The source then places Liu Fu among the princes sent back to their fiefs after the broader guest purge. On August 19, 52 CE, Liu Qiang, Liu Fu, Liu Ying, Liu Kang, and Liu Yan each return to their fiefs. In this bounded evidence, Liu Fu matters as a prince whose household favor becomes implicated in private guest violence and then in the court's territorial-distance response.

## Key Characteristics

- Eastern Han royal prince identified as Pei King in the 52 CE guest-disaster branch.
- Favors Liu Li, whose private guests kill Liu Gong.
- Punished by brief confinement in the imperial prison after Liu Xiu becomes angry.
- Returns to his fief after the national purge of princely guests.
- Distinct from later or same-pinyin Liu Fu figures outside this source.

## Qualifications

This page covers 沛王刘辅 from Hanji 728 only. It is distinct from [[LiuFuLateHan|刘馥 / Liu Fu, late Han]]. The source does not prove Liu Fu personally ordered Liu Gong's killing; it shows that Liu Fu's favor toward Liu Li made his household politically exposed after Liu Li mobilized guests.
