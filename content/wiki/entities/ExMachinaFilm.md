# Ex Machina / 《机械姬》

## Overview
[[ExMachinaFilm|《机械姬》]] is used by [[dianying-li-de-ai-qingren-juying-he-ducai-zhe-gkwriuen9djgazhkswsh-d-b]] as the AI film that shifts the problem from fearing or pitying artificial beings to testing whether human judgment can recognize consciousness, manipulation, programmed sexuality, or free choice.

## Current Profile
The episode treats Ava's test as more than a robot-rebellion plot. Caleb, Nathan, Ava, and the viewer occupy layered observer positions: the human characters judge Ava, the audience judges the characters' judgment, and Ava's facial expression around power cuts leaves her inner state deliberately unstable.

That instability matters because the film also attacks a simple boundary between programming and choice. Nathan's argument about sexuality, genetics, environment, and Pollock-style action suggests that human decisions are not purely unprogrammed either. Ava's behavior can therefore be read as design, deception, emergence, or something between intention and randomness.

## Key Characteristics
- The film is framed as a judgment problem rather than only an AI-threat story.
- Ava's possible consciousness, manipulation, and desire remain deliberately hard to separate.
- The source uses the film to complicate the opposition between programmed behavior and free choice.
- The viewer becomes part of the test because audience judgment mirrors Caleb's judgment.
- The film connects [[TuringTestJudgmentTrap]], [[AIConsciousnessBoundary]], and [[AIFreeWillRisk]].

## Evidence
- Judgment frame: [[dianying-li-de-ai-qingren-juying-he-ducai-zhe-gkwriuen9djgazhkswsh-d-b]] says the film asks whether human judgment about AI is reliable.
- Turing-test staging: [[dianying-li-de-ai-qingren-juying-he-ducai-zhe-gkwriuen9djgazhkswsh-d-b]] describes Caleb and the audience as judges of whether Ava has consciousness or is deceiving him.
- Performance detail: [[dianying-li-de-ai-qingren-juying-he-ducai-zhe-gkwriuen9djgazhkswsh-d-b]] notes Ava's scripted expression and gaze shifts before and after power cuts.
- Free-will ambiguity: [[dianying-li-de-ai-qingren-juying-he-ducai-zhe-gkwriuen9djgazhkswsh-d-b]] uses the Nathan-Caleb conversation and Pollock example to place Ava between setting, randomness, and choice.

## Qualifications
The source does not settle whether Ava is conscious or free. Its value is preserving the uncertainty as a test of human observers.
