## Overview
Liz Walters appears in [[the-continent-nobody-owns-everyone-benefits-from-summer-school]] as the former [[McMurdoStation]] store officer whose experience makes Antarctic economics concrete at human scale.

## Current Profile
Walters's profile in the wiki is operational rather than biographical. In the 1980s McMurdo segment, she ordered merchandise, managed a small store, set prices, removed items that did not sell, commissioned new logos, and used display and slow product release to make shopping feel like entertainment in an isolated settlement.

## Key Characteristics
- She worked at McMurdo as disbursing officer and ship store officer in the 1980s.
- Her store operated in a place where workers already received essentials, so demand centered partly on novelty and diversion.
- Her merchandising choices used scarcity, anticipation, logo identity, and window-like display to stimulate demand.
- Her case makes [[IsolatedRetailDemandManagement]] visible without turning Antarctica into a normal consumer market.

## Evidence
- Role and duties: [[the-continent-nobody-owns-everyone-benefits-from-summer-school]] says Walters ordered merchandise, organized the store, and set prices at McMurdo.
- Demand context: [[the-continent-nobody-owns-everyone-benefits-from-summer-school]] says shopping became entertainment because people had little to do outside work.
- Retail strategy: [[the-continent-nobody-owns-everyone-benefits-from-summer-school]] describes removing poor sellers, commissioning logos, introducing items slowly, and arranging the store to attract people waiting outside.

## Qualifications
The source gives a focused recollection of Walters's McMurdo store work, not a complete career profile.
