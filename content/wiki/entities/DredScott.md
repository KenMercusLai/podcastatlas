# Dred Scott

## Overview

Dred Scott is the enslaved man whose unsuccessful freedom suit became the episode's judicial emblem of slavery's possible national expansion.

## Current Profile

[[200-american-civil-war-the-causes-part-1-glt1124511401]] presents Scott as an enslaved man who sued for freedom after residence in free territory. The Supreme Court ruled against him in 1857 and treated enslaved people as property protected by the Fifth Amendment.

The decision widened the crisis beyond whether Congress would admit a particular free or slave territory. [[AbrahamLincoln|Abraham Lincoln]] feared that its constitutional logic could support enforceable slavery even in states that had prohibited it, reinforcing Northern suspicion that a “slave power” was nationalizing the institution.

## Key Characteristics

- Enslaved plaintiff who sought freedom through federal litigation.
- Central figure in the 1857 Supreme Court decision bearing his name.
- Judicial case linking property rights to slavery's territorial and potentially national reach.
- Political catalyst in Northern fears about constitutional protection for slavery.

## Evidence

### Freedom suit and ruling

- [[200-american-civil-war-the-causes-part-1-glt1124511401]] says Scott sued after being taken into free territory and that the Court treated enslaved people as Fifth Amendment-protected property.

### Political consequence

- [[200-american-civil-war-the-causes-part-1-glt1124511401]] attributes to Lincoln the fear that another ruling could make slavery enforceable within free states.

## Qualifications

This profile reflects one episode's causal summary rather than a full legal analysis of standing, citizenship, territorial power, the opinions, later repudiation, or Scott's life. The projected free-state consequence is presented as Lincoln's political fear, not as an outcome that occurred.
