# Wartburg

## Overview

The Wartburg was the castle refuge where [[FrederickTheWise|Frederick the Wise's]] agents concealed [[MartinLuther]] after the [[DietOfWorms]] under the identity of Junker Georg.

## Current Profile

The refuge solved an immediate security problem: Luther could disappear without Frederick openly admitting that he sheltered an imperial outlaw. Isolation also created a productive interval in which Luther translated the New Testament from Greek into German.

The episode does not romanticize the stay completely. Luther described anxiety, physical distress, and demonic persecution, while events in [[Wittenberg]] moved beyond his control. Wartburg therefore joined protection, translation, psychological strain, and loss of movement control.

## Key Characteristics

- Secret refuge arranged after Luther's staged abduction.
- Site of the Junker Georg disguise.
- Setting for Luther's German New Testament translation.
- Period of isolation, anxiety, and physical distress.
- Distance that allowed Wittenberg reform to accelerate without Luther's direct control.

## Evidence

- Concealment: [[436-luther-showdown-with-the-emperor-part-4-glt5159022101]] identifies Frederick's agents, the staged abduction, and the knightly disguise.
- Translation: [[436-luther-showdown-with-the-emperor-part-4-glt5159022101]] places the Greek-based German New Testament project at the castle.
- Isolation and control: [[436-luther-showdown-with-the-emperor-part-4-glt5159022101]] connects Luther's distress at Wartburg to the independent radicalization of Wittenberg.

## Qualifications

Stories about an inkpot, a dog, and demonic disturbance belong partly to Luther legend and the episode's comic treatment. The claim of an 11-week translation remains source-scoped here.

## What Changed

- Established Wartburg as both protective infrastructure and the setting for translation under enforced absence.
- Connected physical safety to loss of direct control over reform.
