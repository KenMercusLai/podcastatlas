# Vichy France

## Overview

Vichy France was the French regime led by [[PhilippePetain]] from the unoccupied southern zone after the 1940 armistice with Germany.

## Current Profile

The source introduces Vichy as the political outcome of military defeat and the cabinet victory of the armistice faction. Under the settlement, Germany occupied northern and western France while Pétain governed roughly the southern two-fifths from Vichy. This page records that founding moment; it does not yet synthesize the regime's later institutions, collaboration, persecution, empire, or dissolution.

## Key Characteristics

- Emerged from the June 1940 armistice and collapse of Reynaud's government.
- Was led by Pétain from Vichy.
- Governed the initially unoccupied southern portion of metropolitan France.
- Coexisted with German occupation of northern and western France.
- Claimed continuity of French authority under conditions imposed by defeat.

## Evidence

- Founding settlement: [[622-the-nazis-at-war-the-fall-of-france-part-3-glt6447940829]] links Pétain's accession and armistice request to the territorial division and Vichy government.
- Territorial structure: [[622-the-nazis-at-war-the-fall-of-france-part-3-glt6447940829]] describes German occupation of northern and western France and Pétain's authority in the south.

## Qualifications

One narrative source covers only the regime's creation. The exact territorial fraction, legal continuity, political motives, and later character of Vichy require broader evidence; no inference about the full regime should rest on this page alone.

## What Changed

- Created the entity to distinguish the armistice regime from France as a whole.
