# Prague Spring / 布拉格之春

## Overview

The Prague Spring was the 1968 Czechoslovak reform episode whose suppression by Soviet and Warsaw Pact forces became a political rupture shaping dissent, exile, intelligence defection, private memory, and Prague's visible urban landscape.

## Current Profile

In the espionage source, the invasion is decisive evidence in [[OlegGordievsky|Oleg Gordievsky's]] ideological defection: hope that socialism could liberalize gives way to the judgment that the Soviet system would not tolerate reform. In the Kundera source, post-1968 surveillance, professional punishment, exile, and memory revision show repression entering biography and private self-understanding.

The travel source locates this rupture in [[Prague|Prague's]] public space. Wenceslas Square becomes the site through which the host connects the invasion with the National Museum's reported impact marks and later 1989 demonstrations. This makes 1968 not only a Cold War event or literary background but a visible layer in the city's transition narrative.

## Key Characteristics

- Czechoslovak reform episode suppressed by Soviet and Warsaw Pact military intervention in 1968.
- Evidence for dissidents and defectors that the Soviet system could not tolerate internal liberalization.
- Rupture followed by surveillance, professional punishment, exile, and rewritten private memory.
- Event remembered materially and publicly through Prague, especially Wenceslas Square and the National Museum.
- Political predecessor against which the [[VelvetRevolution|1989 transition]] is understood in the source set.

- Repression and memory: [[12-man-dang-milan-kundela-yi-ju-zhengjing-meiyou-549335675]] links 1968 to surveillance, job loss, exile, and later self-mythologizing.

## Qualifications

The sources use Prague Spring through biography, fiction, espionage, and travel rather than offering a full institutional history of the reforms, invasion, resistance, normalization, casualty record, or international response. The museum-wall interpretation and physical marks are reported by the travel source and not independently verified here.
