# PREVENT Programme

## Overview

PREVENT is the British counter-radicalisation programme discussed in [[islamism-in-britain-what-is-the-threat-6aa9164ffca3d5c77b4b383a]].

## Current Profile

The episode presents PREVENT as an institution caught between discrimination concerns and a possible reporting blind spot. Islamist and civil-liberties critics argue that the programme stigmatizes Muslims, while an independent review cited by the source says the discrimination narrative contributed to hesitancy around Islamist-related referrals. The programme therefore illustrates how legitimacy affects whether a safeguarding system receives usable information.

## Key Characteristics

- Operates as a counter-radicalisation and safeguarding channel rather than only a criminal prosecution system.
- Faces sustained criticism that it discriminates against Muslims.
- May under-receive Islamist-related concerns when officials fear appearing prejudiced.
- Handles multiple extremism categories, so referral shares do not directly measure national threat shares.

## Evidence

- Political contest: [[islamism-in-britain-what-is-the-threat-6aa9164ffca3d5c77b4b383a]] describes campaigns by Islamists and others to scrap PREVENT as discriminatory.
- Reporting hesitation: [[islamism-in-britain-what-is-the-threat-6aa9164ffca3d5c77b4b383a]] cites a 2023 independent review saying the discrimination narrative had discouraged some Islamist-related reporting.
- Measurement mismatch: [[islamism-in-britain-what-is-the-threat-6aa9164ffca3d5c77b4b383a]] contrasts a low Islamist share of referrals with a much larger share of MI5's counter-terror workload.

## Qualifications

Referral proportions and security-service workload are different measures with different thresholds and denominators. The source does not establish that every unmade referral was warranted, that all criticism is Islamist-driven, or that higher referral volume would necessarily improve outcomes.
