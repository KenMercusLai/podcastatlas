# Nabonidus

## Overview

Nabonidus is the last Neo-Babylonian king, presented as an antiquarian and devotee of the moon god Sin whose long residence at Tayma disrupted Babylonian royal ritual and weakened resistance to [[CyrusTheGreat]].

## Current Profile

The episode portrays Nabonidus as an elderly general who became king after [[NebuchadnezzarII]]'s dynasty. His scholarly interest in the past and devotion to Sin distinguish him from the Marduk-centered expectations of [[Babylon]]. He spent about ten years at Tayma in northern Arabia while Belshazzar acted as regent.

Because the king was absent, the New Year ritual centered on [[Marduk]] could not operate normally. The episode treats this disruption as a source of unpopularity and as part of the context in which Cyrus could defeat Babylonian forces and represent Persian entry as respect for local gods rather than destruction of them.

## Key Characteristics

- Last king of the Neo-Babylonian Empire in the episode's transition narrative.
- Antiquarian ruler presented as unusually interested in the ancient past.
- Devotee of the moon god Sin rather than a straightforward Marduk-centered royal figure.
- Long-term resident at Tayma who left Belshazzar as regent in Babylon.
- Ritual absentee whose weakened legitimacy helped Cyrus frame conquest as restoration.

## Evidence

- Kingship and absence: [[145-babylon-glt7141377161]] describes Nabonidus as an elderly general and antiquarian who spent roughly a decade at Tayma with Belshazzar governing in Babylon.
- Religious politics: [[145-babylon-glt7141377161]] connects his devotion to Sin and absence from the Marduk-centered New Year ceremony to unpopularity.
- Persian transition: [[145-babylon-glt7141377161]] presents Babylonian unwillingness to fight for Nabonidus and Cyrus's respect-for-local-gods posture as complementary parts of the conquest story.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to one narrative episode. Nabonidus's reasons for living at Tayma, the exact duration and effects of his absence, the scale of Babylonian opposition, his religious program, and Cyrus's motives remain source-scoped. Daniel's transfer of features associated with Nabonidus to Nebuchadnezzar is treated as literary distortion, not evidence for a simple identity between the two kings.

## What Changed

- Created Nabonidus as the ritual-legitimacy and regime-transition figure preceding Persian conquest.

## Relationships

- [[Babylon]] - capital from which his prolonged absence weakened the episode's model of royal presence.
- [[Marduk]] - patron god whose New Year ritual depended on the king's participation.
- [[CyrusTheGreat]] - conqueror who displaced Nabonidus and claimed accommodation of Babylonian religion.
- [[NebuchadnezzarII]] - earlier Neo-Babylonian ruler against whose monumental and biblical profile Nabonidus is contrasted.
