# Wendy Suzuki

## Overview
Wendy Suzuki is a neuroscientist and psychology professor presented in [[boost-attention-memory-with-science-based-tools-dr-wendy-suzuki-scim2980742096]] as a researcher and public educator on learning, memory, exercise, meditation, stress, and cognitive performance.

## Current Profile
Suzuki's profile in the episode joins laboratory neuroscience to behavioral practice. She explains memory through [[MemoryEncodingLevers]] and [[HippocampalAssociativeMemory]], then uses her own move from workaholism toward regular exercise to motivate research on [[ExerciseLinkedCognitiveResilience]]. She also reports a brief body-scan study that informs [[ShortDailyMeditationAttentionTraining]]. Her strongest practical message is that exercise, meditation, and sleep can support attention and mood, but she repeatedly distinguishes accessible habits from dementia cures and acknowledges gaps in modality comparisons, mechanism, and publication status.

## Key Characteristics
- Studies learning and memory with particular emphasis on hippocampal function.
- Translates neuroscience into accessible exercise, meditation, sleep, and attention practices.
- Uses personal behavioral change as a research question rather than as proof of efficacy.
- Combines published findings, unpublished laboratory results, and population evidence with different evidentiary strength.
- Frames exercise as a possible contributor to cognitive resilience, not a cure for Alzheimer's disease or other dementias.

## Evidence
- Memory research - [[boost-attention-memory-with-science-based-tools-dr-wendy-suzuki-scim2980742096]] presents her explanations of memorability, hippocampal association, H.M., long-term storage, and future simulation.
- Exercise translation - [[boost-attention-memory-with-science-based-tools-dr-wendy-suzuki-scim2980742096]] connects her personal exercise experience to acute and three-month studies of mood, attention, and memory.
- Meditation research - [[boost-attention-memory-with-science-based-tools-dr-wendy-suzuki-scim2980742096]] reports an eight-week guided body-scan study focused on stress, mood, cognition, and adherence.
- Evidence boundaries - [[boost-attention-memory-with-science-based-tools-dr-wendy-suzuki-scim2980742096]] labels the Swedish fitness evidence correlational and identifies unpublished findings and unresolved exercise comparisons.

## Qualifications
This profile derives from one public interview rather than a comprehensive review of Suzuki's scholarship. It does not independently verify study design, sample size, effect magnitude, publication status, or replication. Claims about adult human neurogenesis, muscle- and liver-to-brain BDNF pathways, cognitive aging, affirmations, and exact meditation mechanisms remain attributed to the episode. Her personal account explains research motivation but is not causal evidence.
