# Charles Randolph

## Overview

Charles Randolph is an Oscar-winning screenwriter represented here through his unproduced adaptation [[BirthdayPartyFilm|The Birthday Party]] and his account of writing across racial and experiential boundaries.

## Current Profile

Randolph treats adaptation as an argument rather than transcription. He intensifies prejudice and invents a confrontation absent from Stanley Alpert's memoir so both the kidnapped man and his captors face internal contradiction. The method seeks moral legibility without innocence, but Randolph concedes that it also appropriates a real victim's experience and changes his ethical position.

His defense of cross-cultural authorship is conditional and collaborative. Writers may enter worlds they do not inhabit, but actors, consultation, performance, editing, and openness to correction are part of the authorship process rather than guarantees added afterward.

## Key Characteristics

- Uses invented internal conflict to move an adaptation beyond external plot.
- Resists reducing characters to victim, hero, or racial threat.
- Treats the blindfold as both plot device and moral metaphor.
- Acknowledges the real subject's legitimate objection to thematic alteration.
- Defends cross-cultural storytelling through collaborative correction rather than identity-based exclusivity.

## Qualifications

The bounded source is a friendly retrospective conversation summarized rather than transcribed. It does not provide the screenplay, Alpert's full response, the interns' reasoning, the captors' perspectives, production records, or independent evaluation of Randolph's other work. His account supports a profile of stated method and judgment, not proof that the screenplay achieved the balance he intended.
