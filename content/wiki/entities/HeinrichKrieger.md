# Heinrich Krieger

## Overview

Heinrich Krieger was a German law student and committed Nazi whose study of American race law became useful to German officials developing legally organized racial exclusion.

## Current Profile

[[hitler-s-olympics-part-8-vater-it-is-to-be-fayetteville-hitler-s-olympics-part-8-vater-it-is-to-be-fayetteville|Hitler’s Olympics, Part 8]] follows Krieger from Düsseldorf to the University of Arkansas School of Law in Fayetteville and then to research at the Library of Congress. He examined court decisions, state constitutions, criminal law, segregation, anti-miscegenation rules, and competing definitions of racial membership before producing *Race Law in America*.

Krieger's response was ideological and administrative rather than humanitarian. [[JonathanWiesen]] describes him as admiring American racial awareness while disapproving of inconsistent implementation. He rejected lynching as uncontrolled disorder, yet accepted racial domination when organized through state law. After returning to Germany, he was recruited for his knowledge of American law and later studied South African apartheid.

## Key Characteristics

- German legal researcher trained partly in Arkansas.
- Committed Nazi rather than a detached comparative scholar.
- Systematized varied American racial laws and judicial practices.
- Supplied expertise useful to German Justice Ministry deliberations.
- Preferred orderly state coercion to decentralized racial violence.
- Extended his comparative research from the United States to South Africa.
