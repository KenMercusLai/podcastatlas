# General Belgrano

## Overview

The General Belgrano was an Argentine cruiser sunk by the British submarine Conqueror during the [[FalklandsWar|Falklands War]], killing 323 sailors and servicemen and becoming one of the conflict's most disputed events.

## Current Profile

The source places the ageing cruiser, its escorts, and the aircraft carrier 25 de Mayo within an Argentine naval plan that threatened the British task force and its carriers. Although the Belgrano was outside Britain's declared exclusion zone and had turned toward the mainland while awaiting further orders, the hosts argue that neither circumstance removed it from the wider combat threat.

The sinking had both operational and political effects. More than 700 people survived, but the loss caused Argentina's surface-fleet plan to be abandoned and changed international perceptions of a conflict that had so far produced few deaths. Later allegations that [[MargaretThatcher|Margaret Thatcher]] used the attack to sabotage peace are rejected by the episode, which separates military legitimacy from the scale of human loss and the jingoistic media response.

## Key Characteristics

- An ageing Argentine cruiser operating with destroyer escorts during the Falklands War.
- Part of a wider naval threat to the British task force in the source's account.
- Sunk outside Britain's 200-mile exclusion zone after changing course toward the mainland.
- Loss site of 323 deaths, including conscripts, with more than 700 survivors.
- Operational turning point that kept Argentina's surface fleet from continuing its planned challenge.
- Enduring subject of legal, diplomatic, moral, and media controversy.

- Sinking and effect: [[171-the-falklands-war-battle-for-the-islands-part-3-glt4679725480]] reports 323 deaths, more than 700 survivors, and abandonment of the wider Argentine surface action.
