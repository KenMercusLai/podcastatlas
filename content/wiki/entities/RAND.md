# RAND

## Overview

RAND is a research organization represented in the wiki through student-AI evidence and military-strategy simulation. Across both branches, it turns broad concern into a bounded empirical or scenario-based problem while requiring careful separation between a warning signal and a proven outcome.

## Current Profile

In [[tech-20260323-0323-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260323-0323-mp-tech-pod-128]], [[HeatherSchwartz]] cites RAND findings that more than 60% of U.S. middle school, high school, and college students use AI for homework help and more than two-thirds worry about harm to critical thinking. The policy response is [[FirstDraftThinking]]: preserve the student's initial synthesis before using AI for refinement or explanation.

The military-strategy branch is represented by [[DavidShlapak]], [[YunaWong]], and [[JohnGentile]]. Repeated Baltic games, a North Korean collapse exercise, and an AI-enabled East Asia scenario use [[StrategicWarGaming]] to expose assumptions, [[OperationalFrictionInStrategicPlanning]], and [[AutomatedDeterrenceInteractionRisk]]. [[tech-20261124-1124-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261124-1124-mp-tech-pod-128]] adds [[RachelSteratore|Rachel Steratore]]'s three-stage account of AI-assisted development and her case for [[AIResearchAutomationOversight]] of internal lab systems. The common institutional pattern is calibrated warning: student concern is not proof of cognitive harm, a game outcome is not a forecast, and research automation is not proof of direct successor construction, but each can reveal a question that routine analysis should no longer ignore.

## Key Characteristics

- Produces policy research across education, technology, and national security.
- Uses survey evidence to make student AI behavior and concern measurable.
- Uses interactive simulation to expose strategic assumptions and operational constraints.
- Develops staged capability frameworks and oversight proposals for emerging AI risks.
- Separates early-warning evidence from settled causal or predictive claims.
- Translates findings into sequencing, contingency, and institutional-design questions.

## Qualifications

The student source is a short interview that does not supply the underlying survey and experimental methods, while the war-gaming source is a structured podcast summary rather than game materials, after-action reports, or validation studies. The AI-governance source does not supply its cited METR report, executive order, benchmark design, or legal-authority analysis. Student concern does not establish cognitive damage, simulated military outcomes do not establish real-world probability, and current research automation does not establish direct successor-building RSI. These sources demonstrate RAND's role in bounded cases, not the quality or conclusions of its full research portfolio.
