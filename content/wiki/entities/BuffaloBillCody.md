# Buffalo Bill Cody

## Overview

Buffalo Bill Cody was an American frontier celebrity and showman whose Wild West productions helped turn recent conquest, famous participants, horsemanship, staged conflict, and Native defeat into international mass entertainment.

## Current Profile

The sources place Cody at the hinge between lived frontier history and commercial mythology. His shows helped standardize romantic western imagery while events were still recent, drew elite and popular European audiences, and made [[SittingBull]] a paid but constrained performer whose fame, agency, humiliation, and reservation dependence cannot be separated cleanly.

The same entertainment system later absorbed imprisoned [[GhostDance]] leaders under sentence-commutation arrangements and advertised them with false Wounded Knee provenance. Cody can therefore appear as personally sympathetic or helpful in particular stories while the show itself converted conquest and unequal choices into spectacle.

## Key Characteristics

- Built international entertainment from contemporary frontier people, skills, violence, and myth.
- Helped make the Wild West a global genre before Hollywood westerns.
- Employed Sitting Bull in an exchange mixing income, travel, celebrity, supervision, and humiliation.
- Benefited from a spectacle system able to market coercion and false provenance.
- Retains a morally mixed profile that cannot be reduced to either benevolent ally or simple villain.

## Evidence

### Mythmaking and global entertainment

- [[42-the-wild-west-glt7464073840]] describes Wild West shows as active during the frontier's final decades and popular with European mass and royal audiences.

### Sitting Bull's performance

- [[455-fall-of-the-sioux-the-ghost-dance-part-2-glt2320133118]] presents Sitting Bull's participation as a mixture of income, travel, curiosity, humiliation, and agency-managed display.
- [[42-the-wild-west-glt7464073840]] also presents Cody as trying to assist Sitting Bull during later reservation conflict, while leaving the story source-scoped.

### Coercive commercial afterlife

- [[456-fall-of-the-sioux-the-massacre-at-wounded-knee-part-3-glt7996658277]] describes Ghost Dance prisoners joining the show through commuted sentences and being marketed inaccurately as Wounded Knee participants.

## Qualifications

The sources are popular-history episodes, not a complete biography or business history. Participation could provide income and mobility without becoming fully free choice, and specific claims about Cody's intentions, assistance to Sitting Bull, contracts, and performers' views require direct and specialist evidence.

## What Changed

- Created a profile that joins Cody's global mythmaking role to the constrained choices and false provenance within Wild West entertainment.
