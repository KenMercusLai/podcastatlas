# Aristides de Sousa Mendes

## Overview

Aristides de Sousa Mendes was the Portuguese consul in Bordeaux who issued visas to people escaping Hitler's Europe during the Second World War.

## Current Profile

The source places Sousa Mendes inside the ambiguity of Portuguese neutrality. While [[AntonioDeOliveiraSalazar|Salazar]] balanced alliance, trade, and strategic access, the consul enabled thousands of refugees, including Jews, to leave France. His later prominence in Portuguese public memory contrasts a rescue figure with the continuing popularity of Salazar himself.

## Key Characteristics

- Portuguese diplomat stationed in Bordeaux during the Second World War.
- Issued visas to large numbers of refugees fleeing Nazi-controlled Europe.
- Associated in the source with the escape of Jews and other threatened people from France.
- Became a major figure in later Portuguese public memory.

## Evidence

- Refugee rescue: [[230-portugal-football-fado-and-fascism-part-4-glt9147423732]] says Sousa Mendes issued thousands or possibly tens of thousands of visas in Bordeaux.
- Memory: [[230-portugal-football-fado-and-fascism-part-4-glt9147423732]] notes his third-place finish in a 2007 public vote on great Portuguese figures.

## Qualifications

The source does not reconstruct the visa chronology, recipient count, government orders, sanctions, or later rehabilitation. The wide numerical range is preserved as source uncertainty rather than resolved here.
