# Rochester Cathedral

## Overview

Rochester Cathedral is an English cathedral founded during the conversion of Anglo-Saxon Kent and later rebuilt under Norman rule, making it a durable religious anchor of [[Rochester]].

## Current Profile

The episode places the cathedral in the mission associated with Augustine and early Christian Kent. It dates the foundation to 604 under Justus and treats Rochester as England's second Anglo-Saxon cathedral center after Canterbury.

After the Norman Conquest, Gundulf renewed the cathedral beginning in 1083. The building therefore joins religious continuity to institutional and architectural reconstruction under a new ruling order. Later looting and the mixed judgments of historical visitors keep its survival from being read as uninterrupted grandeur.

Within the episode's larger geography, the cathedral represents the sacred and ancient side of Rochester, contrasted with the industrial and maritime energy of [[ChathamDockyard]].

## Key Characteristics

- Early cathedral foundation associated with the conversion of Anglo-Saxon Kent.
- Religious institution tied to Rochester's position within the Canterbury mission.
- Norman rebuilding project associated with Gundulf.
- Surviving structure marked by continuity, damage, alteration, and changing reputation.

## Evidence

- [[600-chatham-high-street-glt2782191075]] dates the cathedral's foundation to 604 under Justus and links it to the earliest Roman Christian mission in Anglo-Saxon England.

### Norman rebuilding and later history

- [[600-chatham-high-street-glt2782191075]] associates Gundulf with rebuilding from 1083 and notes later looting and qualified judgments of the building's grandeur.

## Qualifications

The foundation date, ordinal claim, building phases, attributions, and accounts of damage are drawn from one popular-history episode and require specialist ecclesiastical and architectural sources. Institutional continuity should not imply that the present fabric is unchanged from its Anglo-Saxon foundation.
