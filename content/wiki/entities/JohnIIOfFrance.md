# John II of France

## Overview

John II of France is the French king whose capture at Poitiers gave [[England]] immense bargaining power but also exposed how a captive monarch and an ungovernable kingdom could make a victorious settlement impossible to implement.

## Current Profile

John appears first as a prestigious captive living lavishly in London while France fragments under the Dauphin, [[CharlesTheBad|Charles the Bad]], Parisian revolt, the [[Jacquerie]], and [[FreeCompaniesHundredYearsWar|free companies]]. His 1358 Windsor terms promise a huge ransom and extensive sovereign concessions to [[EdwardIIIOfEngland|Edward III]], but France lacks the money and political order needed to carry them out.

The episode says John later offers still wider concessions, which the Dauphin and Estates General reject. After the [[TreatyOfBretigny|Treaty of Brétigny]], he returns voluntarily to English captivity when treaty obligations cannot be fulfilled and dies in 1364, allowing the Dauphin to become [[CharlesVOfFrance|Charles V]].

## Key Characteristics

- French king captured at Poitiers and held in London.
- Diplomatic asset whose ransom and concessions exceeded France's immediate capacity.
- Lavish captive whose courtly consumption contrasts with disorder in France.
- Signatory to proposed settlements that depended on absent fiscal and political stability.
- Voluntary returnee to captivity when treaty obligations failed.
