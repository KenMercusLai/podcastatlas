# Stuart McMillan

## Overview
Stuart McMillan is the sprint coach interviewed by [[AndrewHuberman]] in a [[HubermanLab]] episode about gait, plyometrics, sprint preparation, individualized coaching, athletic development, and movement as expression.

## Current Profile
The bounded source presents McMillan as a coach who starts from the individual rather than an idealized technical model. He distinguishes walking, jogging, running, striding, and sprinting by velocity, posture, ground contact, limb action, and force acceptance, then uses [[SkippingPlyometricProgression]] as a practical bridge for adults who are not ready to sprint.

His coaching profile joins biomechanics with observation and expression. Ground-contact sound, rhythm, posture, rotation, ease, mood, and personality can all reveal whether movement fits the athlete. Stable principles still matter, but methods are adjusted to structure, capacity, training age, and context through [[IndividualizedMovementCoaching]].

## Key Characteristics
- Treats sprinting as a distinct maximal gait rather than faster jogging.
- Uses skipping, hills, and striding as progressions toward sprint-specific demands.
- Emphasizes eccentric force acceptance, hip extension, stiffness, rhythm, and cross-body coordination.
- Lets velocity and individual structure shape mechanics rather than prescribing one universal foot strike or stride model.
- Protects athlete identity by matching cues, mood words, and technical solutions to the person.
- Favors varied youth participation before early event specialization.
- Separates stable movement principles from flexible training methods.
