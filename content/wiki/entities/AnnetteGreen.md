# Annette Green

## Overview
Annette Green was a fragrance-industry advocate and educator presented by the source as helping move American perfume culture from a single special-occasion luxury toward everyday, mood-based self-expression.

## Current Profile
The episode links Green's reportedly hyperosmic sense of smell to a career built around research, language, education, and marketing rather than perfume composition. She encouraged consumers to maintain a [[FragranceWardrobe]] selected by mood, clothing, weather, or season, preferred the lighter word “fragrance” to “perfume,” established a laboratory, and convened interdisciplinary lectures through the [[FragranceFoundation]].

Her contribution is framed as category-making: she made scent easier to discuss as emotion and identity, invited scientific and cultural disciplines into the field, and promoted repeat everyday use. The episode's dramatic market-growth figures illustrate that story but do not isolate her causal contribution.

## Key Characteristics
- Described herself as hyperosmic and used unusually acute smell perception in her work.
- Framed fragrance as romance, emotion, mood, and identity rather than only a consumer object.
- Promoted a wardrobe of multiple scents instead of one bottle reserved for special occasions.
- Built research and education infrastructure around fragrance production and effects.
- Convened neuropathology, psychology, anthropology, planning, perfumery, and beauty perspectives.
- Influenced the category without personally creating commercial perfumes.

## Evidence
- Sensory profile: [[social-insecurity-numbers-the-money-behind-the-manosphere-7eb4966be1685ee4f19adde69e240aaa]] says Green could distinguish tomato-juice brands by smell and called herself hyperosmic.
- Wardrobe advocacy: [[social-insecurity-numbers-the-money-behind-the-manosphere-7eb4966be1685ee4f19adde69e240aaa]] says she encouraged scent changes based on mood, clothing, weather, and season.
- Research and education: [[social-insecurity-numbers-the-money-behind-the-manosphere-7eb4966be1685ee4f19adde69e240aaa]] credits her with a laboratory and interdisciplinary lectures at the [[FragranceFoundation]].
- Industry recognition: [[social-insecurity-numbers-the-money-behind-the-manosphere-7eb4966be1685ee4f19adde69e240aaa]] says perfumer Frank Voelkl created “Annette's Greenhouse” for her in her tenth decade.

## Qualifications
The supplied document is a short obituary segment, not a complete biography, archive, or causal history of the fragrance market. The reported industry growth from $500,000 in 1962 to more than $6 billion annually by the 2020s is retained as episode-scoped and cannot be attributed to Green alone. Hyperosmia and sensory anecdotes are reported personal characteristics rather than independently assessed clinical findings.
