# Ghent Altarpiece

## Overview

The Ghent Altarpiece is the monumental work through which [[271-belgium-historys-greatest-artist-glt1331750756]] presents [[JanVanEyck|Jan van Eyck]] at the intersection of Christian theology, lay patronage, Burgundian politics, Flemish cloth wealth, music, and minute naturalistic observation.

## Current Profile

Installed in Ghent's Church of Saint John on 6 May 1432, the polyptych was normally closed on ordinary days and opened on Sundays and holidays. The episode emphasizes the Mystic Lamb at its open center, surrounded by Adam and Eve, angels, worshippers, vegetation, architecture, and even legible music.

Its meanings are cumulative rather than exclusive. The lamb signifies Christ's sacrifice, while the episode also reads sheep and fleece through [[Flanders|Flanders's]] cloth economy and [[PhilipTheGood|Philip the Good's]] Order of the Golden Fleece. A childless patron couple used the commission to preserve memory, making sacred imagery, civic wealth, and elite commemoration part of the same object.

## Key Characteristics

- Large devotional polyptych organized around the Mystic Lamb and Christian sacrifice.
- Work designed for alternating closed and open liturgical display.
- Monument of Van Eyck's precision, luminous surface, crowded figures, and observed detail.
- Lay patronage object securing the memory of a childless commissioning couple.
- Artwork whose religious meaning overlaps with Flemish cloth wealth and Burgundian symbolism.

## Evidence

- Installation and use: [[271-belgium-historys-greatest-artist-glt1331750756]] dates the installation and distinguishes ordinary closed display from festive opening.
- Sacred program: [[271-belgium-historys-greatest-artist-glt1331750756]] identifies the Lamb, Adam and Eve, angels, worshippers, and music.
- Patronage and memory: [[271-belgium-historys-greatest-artist-glt1331750756]] attributes the commission to a childless couple seeking commemoration.
- Regional symbolism: [[271-belgium-historys-greatest-artist-glt1331750756]] connects sheep and fleece to cloth production and the Order of the Golden Fleece.

## Qualifications

The source offers an appreciative thematic reading, not a full account of authorship, conservation, iconography, panel history, or scholarly disagreement. The economic and Golden Fleece associations are Van Loo's layered interpretation rather than proof that the lamb's primary Christian meaning should be displaced.

## What Changed

- Created the altarpiece as the principal artwork joining Van Eyck's technique to theology, patronage, and Flemish wealth.
