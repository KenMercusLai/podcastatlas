## Overview

Zhu Yun / 朱云 enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-549-ruguo-youren-ma-bifu-ni-zhidao-sha-yisi-bu-lskfkw_udnitdfzzg1lw8b1ch3si|Hanji 549]] as a former Huaili county magistrate whose direct accusation before [[HanChengdi|汉成帝]] becomes the "broken balustrade" remonstrance story. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-470-xiao-wangzhi-shasi-wode-chule-taijian-haiyou-wode-yufu-lof1c5nctali_zsy0hotqyocjgoe|Hanji 470]] adds an earlier and less flattering layer: Zhu Yun is [[XiaoWangzhiWesternHan|萧望之]]'s student who advises Xiao to commit suicide after guards surround Xiao's residence.

## Current Profile

Zhu Yun's central profile remains bounded to the Hanji 549 scene. He asks to appear before Chengdi rather than submit an ordinary written memorial, denounces high ministers for holding position without correcting the ruler or benefiting the people, and requests the imperial `shangfang` sword to execute one sycophantic minister as a warning.

When Chengdi demands a name, Zhu Yun identifies Zhang Yu, Chengdi's teacher. Chengdi treats this as intolerable insult to both court officials and his own teacher, orders Zhu Yun seized, and Zhu Yun clings to the palace railing until it breaks. Zhu Yun then compares his death-facing stance to loyal martyrs such as Long Feng and Bigan, forcing Chengdi to face the reputational meaning of killing a blunt speaker.

Zhu Yun survives because [[XinQingjiWesternHan|辛庆忌]] intercedes, arguing that upright speech should be tolerated whether the content is correct or mistaken. Chengdi later preserves the broken railing, so Zhu Yun functions in this wiki as a Western Han instance of [[DeathDefyingRemonstrance|赴死式直谏]] whose moral witness becomes a public memory object.

Hanji 470 complicates that later heroic image. The host treats Zhu Yun's advice to Xiao as "读死书" rigidity: instead of urging Xiao to endure and preserve future agency, Zhu Yun supports a reputation-first death. The page therefore keeps two source-scoped Zhu Yun layers together: later public courage before Chengdi, and earlier rigid counsel that helps make Xiao's suicide feel morally proper.

## Key Characteristics

- Low-ranking Western Han former county official who seeks face-to-face imperial speech.
- Earlier student of Xiao Wangzhi whose advice in Hanji 470 favors suicide over endurance.
- Death-facing remonstrant who names a protected court figure rather than leaving criticism abstract.
- Uses the `鄙夫` frame to connect greed, flattery, fear of losing office, and refusal to speak truth.
- Survives through Xin Qingji's intercession and Chengdi's eventual clemency.
- Becomes symbolically attached to the preserved broken railing as evidence that direct speech can be honored after imperial rage cools.

## Qualifications

This page does not reconstruct Zhu Yun's full career, evaluate whether his accusation against Zhang Yu was substantively correct, or settle whether his advice to Xiao Wangzhi reflected common elite norms or personal rigidity. It preserves the Hanji 549 court scene, the host's interpretation of `鄙夫`, the symbolic afterlife of the broken balustrade, and Hanji 470's source-scoped criticism of Zhu Yun's advice to Xiao.
