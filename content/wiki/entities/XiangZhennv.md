## Overview

向珍女 / Xiang Zhennü is presented by [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-347-2-gudai-zhenjie-lienv-qite-de-beican-mingyun-lk8of9jmcmbncl-0zoak1txx_8vs|Hanji 347-2]] as a girl in the Ming-history exemplary-women tradition who died by suicide after the death of a fiancé she had never met.

## Current Profile

The episode says Xiang was promised by her parents to a Zhou-family son in Wujiang while young. When he died before their marriage and before the two had met, she reportedly hanged herself while her parents slept and left a letter framing the death as fidelity to him.

Her durable wiki role is historiographical rather than biographical: the host uses the story to show how an arranged tie could be treated as a total moral obligation and how a girl's death could be preserved as proof of chastity. The source criticizes that exemplary framing rather than celebrating it, but it does not independently reconstruct Xiang's life, coercive setting, surviving text, or degree of agency.

## Key Characteristics

- Girl represented through an arranged betrothal to a man she reportedly never met.
- Exemplary-biography subject whose suicide is framed within the received story as fidelity to a deceased fiancé.
- Case in which parental arrangement, sexual honor, and posthumous commemoration may collapse a life into one chastity judgment.
- Historiographical figure whose surviving profile is too thin to support confident claims about motive or voluntariness.

## Qualifications

This profile rests on one short podcast retelling of an exemplary-women account. The exact historical text, name form, age, location, family identities, betrothal status, letter, death circumstances, and later commemoration require primary-source verification. Recording the story does not establish that Xiang acted freely, that her reported motive was complete, or that one biography measures the prevalence of such deaths.
