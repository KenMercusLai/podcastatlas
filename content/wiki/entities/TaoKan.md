## Overview

陶侃 / Tao Kan is an Eastern Jin general used in the current source as an example of preserving readiness through deliberate physical discipline during an inactive posting.

## Current Profile

Hanji 1083 briefly recounts the brick-carrying story: while posted in Guangzhou with little immediate work, Tao Kan repeatedly moves bricks between indoors and outdoors so comfort does not erode his will or capacity. The episode retrospectively connects the practice to his later rank and reputation as a pillar of the Eastern Jin.

The profile is intentionally narrow. It records the self-discipline anecdote and the host's modern work analogy, not a complete military or political biography.

## Key Characteristics

- Responds to forced inactivity with a repeatable physical discipline.
- Treats comfort and idleness as risks to future readiness.
- Uses self-imposed effort to preserve agency when office provides little meaningful action.
- Appears in the source as a later historical analogy rather than part of the late-Han sequence.

## Qualifications

The page rests on one short popular-history retelling. The exact count, daily routine, wording, office sequence, motive, and causal link between the exercise and later achievement require verification against the underlying *Book of Jin* tradition. The modern “搬砖” workplace contrast is the host's analogy, not a historical category.
