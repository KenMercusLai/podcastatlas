# Viet Cong Motivation and Morale Project

## Overview

The Viet Cong Motivation and Morale Project was a secret Pentagon-funded [[RANDCorporation]] study that used interviews with prisoners and defectors to assess whether American military pressure was weakening Viet Cong willingness to fight.

## Current Profile

The project addressed a strategic measurement problem. American bombing sought to break an adversary's will, but officials needed evidence about morale and had limited Vietnamese language and country expertise. RAND teams conducted extended, generally nonconfrontational interviews in prisons and rural areas, then transcribed, translated, and edited them in Saigon, producing approximately 62,000 pages according to the episode.

Its significance lies in interpretive divergence rather than collection scale alone. [[LeonGoure]] treated falling expectation of victory as evidence of collapse; [[ConradKellen]] treated continuing commitment under hardship as evidence that the war could not be won; [[MaiElliott]] remained conflicted before later revising her view. The archive therefore became a case of information abundance without analytical convergence.

## Key Characteristics

- Secret interview project commissioned to measure Viet Cong morale under American bombing.
- Relied on prisoners and defectors rather than a representative sample of all combatants or civilians.
- Used Vietnamese field teams and a translation-and-editing pipeline in Saigon.
- Produced an episode-reported archive of approximately 62,000 pages.
- Supported opposite strategic judgments because analysts emphasized different indicators and meanings.

## Qualifications

Prisoners and defectors may differ systematically from active Viet Cong members, and translation, editing, question design, timing, and analyst selection can all shape conclusions. The supplied note does not provide the corpus or methodology needed to resolve those limits, so neither archive size nor later war outcome substitutes for a full research audit.

- Created a project profile separating collection scale, sampling limits, indicator choice, and strategic interpretation.
