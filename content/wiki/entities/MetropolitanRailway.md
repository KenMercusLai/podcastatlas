# Metropolitan Railway

## Overview

The Metropolitan Railway is presented as the world's first underground railway and as a private transport company that shaped northwest London's suburban geography through line extension, surplus-land development, and lifestyle marketing.

## Current Profile

Opening in 1863 with steam-hauled, gas-lit wooden carriages, the railway expanded from central London toward Hammersmith, Richmond, Harrow, and rural Buckinghamshire. Its importance here lies less in engineering priority than in the business loop created around the line.

The company developed land it no longer needed, built estates beginning around Pinner, and publicized countryside walks, pubs, lodging, fresh air, and home ownership. Residents would commute inward, leisure travelers would ride outward, and both movements would support the railway. The 1915 Metroland guide gave this integrated corridor a durable cultural name.

## Key Characteristics

- Early underground railway connecting central London to an expanding northwest corridor.
- Private company able to develop surplus land alongside transport operations.
- Demand creator that marketed destinations and homes rather than merely serving existing journeys.
- Two-way traffic model combining daily commuting with outward leisure travel.
- Institutional origin of the Metroland brand and landscape.

## Qualifications

The episode supplies a concise cultural history rather than a full corporate, engineering, financial, or regulatory history. Route dates, distances, land powers, corporate motives, and the balance of passenger and property revenue remain source-scoped. “World's first underground railway” may depend on definitions of underground and urban rail.
