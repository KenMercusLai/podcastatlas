# Maxime Weygand

## Overview

Maxime Weygand was the French commander-in-chief during the final phase of the [[FallOfFrance1940]] and a leading advocate of an armistice.

## Current Profile

[[621-the-nazis-at-war-blitzkrieg-part-2-glt8711886512]] introduces Weygand on 19 May, when [[PaulReynaud]] replaced Gamelin after the German breakthrough. Weygand attempted to organize a counterattack against the Channel corridor, but the northern command structure was already disintegrating and General Bilotte's fatal car crash compounded the disruption.

[[622-the-nazis-at-war-the-fall-of-france-part-3-glt6447940829]] presents Weygand's later conclusion that metropolitan France had been defeated and that continuing the struggle risked destruction and internal revolution. He pressed Britain to send all available fighters to France, while [[WinstonChurchill]] refused because he expected the decisive Luftwaffe attack to fall on Britain. Weygand aligned with [[PhilippePetain]] against Reynaud's proposal to continue from abroad.

## Key Characteristics

- Judged the French military position irretrievable.
- Took command after the breakthrough and tried to organize a counterstroke under severe command disruption.
- Favored an armistice over continued resistance from overseas territory.
- Feared political disorder alongside battlefield collapse.
- Demanded greater British fighter commitment to France.
- Helped shift the civil-military balance against Reynaud's policy.
