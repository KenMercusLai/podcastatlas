# Maxime Weygand

## Overview

Maxime Weygand was the French commander-in-chief during the final phase of the [[FallOfFrance1940]] and a leading advocate of an armistice.

## Current Profile

[[622-the-nazis-at-war-the-fall-of-france-part-3-glt6447940829]] presents Weygand as convinced that metropolitan France had been defeated and that continuing the struggle risked destruction and internal revolution. He pressed Britain to send all available fighters to France, while [[WinstonChurchill]] refused because he expected the decisive Luftwaffe attack to fall on Britain. Weygand aligned with [[PhilippePetain]] against [[PaulReynaud|Reynaud's]] proposal to continue from abroad.

## Key Characteristics

- Judged the French military position irretrievable.
- Favored an armistice over continued resistance from overseas territory.
- Feared political disorder alongside battlefield collapse.
- Demanded greater British fighter commitment to France.
- Helped shift the civil-military balance against Reynaud's policy.
