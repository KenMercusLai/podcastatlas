# 华容道 / Huarong Road (Late Han)

## Overview
华容道 / Huarong Road is the marshy retreat route through which [[CaoCao|曹操]]'s damaged force moves after [[RedCliffsBattle|赤壁之战]] in the current source.

## Current Profile
Hanji 1106 treats Huarong Road as a mobility bottleneck rather than merely a famous escape setting. Rain and mud trap horses and slow wounded or sick soldiers while pursuit pressure narrows the time available. The episode says Cao Cao orders reeds laid down and prioritizes the passage of mounted core forces, turning the route into a brutal allocation problem over who and what can still be saved after defeat.

## Key Characteristics
- Marshy, rain-affected withdrawal route after Red Cliffs.
- Converts illness, wounds, animals, and terrain into a single movement crisis.
- Forces prioritization between mobile core troops and soldiers unable to keep pace.
- Connects battlefield defeat to Cao Cao's defensive regrouping around Jiangling and Xiangyang.

## Evidence
- Terrain and movement pressure: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1106-caocao-zai-huarongdao-jingli-le-zenyang-de-juewang-lpsjmdpl6-2bd1qtda-egvx0zawv|Hanji 1106]] describes mud, trapped horses, wounded and sick soldiers, and fear of pursuit.
- Improvised passage: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1106-caocao-zai-huarongdao-jingli-le-zenyang-de-juewang-lpsjmdpl6-2bd1qtda-egvx0zawv|Hanji 1106]] says reeds are cut to stabilize the route and weak soldiers are used to help mounted troops cross.
- Strategic transition: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1106-caocao-zai-huarongdao-jingli-le-zenyang-de-juewang-lpsjmdpl6-2bd1qtda-egvx0zawv|Hanji 1106]] connects survival of the retreat with later defensive deployment around [[JianglingLateHan|江陵]] and Xiangyang.

## Qualifications
The page is bounded to one popular-history episode. The exact route, force counts, sequence, and most graphic road-building details require comparison with primary chronicles and later literary tradition. The famous Guan Yu encounter is not part of this source's account and is not imported into the current profile.

## What Changed
- Created the route page from Hanji 1106's retreat sequence.
- Defined Huarong as a terrain-and-mobility crisis rather than a literary encounter alone.
