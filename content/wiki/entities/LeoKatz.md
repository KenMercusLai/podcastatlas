# Leo Katz

## Overview

Leo Katz is the law professor used in the episode to frame the legal and moral puzzle around bribery in [[USVKhoury|U.S. v. Khoury]].

## Current Profile

Katz distinguishes conduct treated as inherently wrong, or *malum in se*, from conduct prohibited by positive law, or *malum prohibitum*. His tennis-camp hypothetical asks why charging wealthy applicants for an institutionally accepted route to coach access might be lawful while a direct private payment is criminal, without claiming that the two arrangements are legally identical.

## Key Characteristics

- Supplies the episode's philosophical vocabulary for classifying wrongdoing.
- Treats bribery as conduct usually understood as inherently corrupt.
- Uses institutional form and authorization to test the boundary between accepted influence and criminal exchange.
- Offers a hypothetical rather than a holding about the facts of the Khoury case.

The source paraphrases Katz through a structured episode summary and does not provide a complete legal analysis, governing statutes, jury instructions, or his precise view of the case. The hypothetical illuminates formal boundaries but does not establish factual or moral equivalence.
