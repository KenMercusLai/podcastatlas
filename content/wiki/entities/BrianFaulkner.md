# Brian Faulkner

## Overview
Brian Faulkner is the final Stormont prime minister in the current Troubles sequence, first pressing for internment and then refusing the reduced authority offered before direct rule.

## Current Profile
The current profile is Faulkner as an ambitious law-and-order leader whose strategy collapses under its own legitimacy cost. Part 3 presents him pushing [[EdwardHeath]] to approve [[InternmentWithoutTrial]] through [[OperationDemetrius]] amid rising violence and unionist pressure. Part 4 shows the aftermath: bombings continue, [[BloodySunday]] destroys trust further, Stormont is probably doomed by February 1972, and Faulkner refuses Heath's offer of reduced authority under British control of law and order. His refusal precedes Stormont's suspension.

## Key Characteristics
- Northern Ireland prime minister identified with a hard law-and-order response.
- Political actor trying to preserve Stormont authority under worsening violence.
- Advocate for internment despite warnings about poor intelligence and Catholic alienation.
- Leader whose governing position is fatally weakened after Bloody Sunday and continued bombings.
- Refuses reduced authority, making direct rule the endpoint of his premiership in the episode sequence.

## Evidence
- Internment push: [[699-the-troubles-return-of-the-ira-part-3-glt9519811642]] says Faulkner pressed Heath to approve internment while offering limited concessions to the SDLP.
- Warning context: [[699-the-troubles-return-of-the-ira-part-3-glt9519811642]] says the army, RUC, and Jack Lynch warned internment would likely fail and drive Catholics toward the IRA.
- Stormont doom: [[700-the-troubles-bloody-sunday-part-4-glt3909829334]] says by February 1972 Stormont was probably doomed after Bloody Sunday and wider violence.
- Direct-rule refusal: [[700-the-troubles-bloody-sunday-part-4-glt3909829334]] says Faulkner refused Heath's offer of reduced authority and felt betrayed and humiliated.

## Qualifications
Faulkner is not treated as the sole cause of collapse. The sources place him under unionist pressure, IRA escalation, British policy uncertainty, poor intelligence, and institutional weakness.
