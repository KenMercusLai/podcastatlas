## Overview

艾伦·耶格尔 / Eren Yeager is the protagonist of [[AttackOnTitan|《进击的巨人》]], read here as a character whose pursuit of freedom, protective purpose, enemy-destruction logic, and personal desire converge in the Rumbling.

## Current Profile

The episode rejects the claim that Eren abruptly stops making psychological sense. His childhood killing of kidnappers already shows an uncompromising tendency to classify threats as enemies who should be eliminated. Later knowledge changes the scale and target of that impulse—from Titans to the world beyond the island—without replacing the underlying disposition.

That continuity does not justify his action. Protection of Paradis and friends is part of his motive, but the anime's added admission that he also wanted to destroy the outside world prevents collective self-defense from absorbing all personal responsibility. He is therefore neither a conventional righteous hero nor merely a neutral instrument of historical necessity.

His choice radicalizes [[ExistentialSelfDefenseEthics]] by treating possible future threat as grounds for nearly unlimited present killing. The story's refusal to show permanent peace after Titan power disappears also undermines any claim that extermination could solve conflict once and for all: even total external victory would leave internal struggles over power, identity, and rule.

## Key Characteristics

- Pursues freedom through a recurring impulse to destroy whatever he defines as confinement or enemy.
- Protects friends and Paradis while also acting from a personal desire for destruction.
- Remains impulsive, immature, and morally dangerous rather than becoming a conventional idealized hero.
- Makes an understandable survival calculation that the episode explicitly refuses to endorse.
- Converts uncertainty about future hostility into catastrophic preventive violence.

## Qualifications

This profile rests on one podcast's comparative memory of manga and anime rather than exhaustive scene analysis. Exact motive shares, foreknowledge, agency under the Founder, responsibility for particular outcomes, and the meaning of added anime dialogue remain interpretive. Psychological continuity makes the Rumbling narratively intelligible; it does not make genocide necessary, proportionate, or permissible.

## What Changed

- Established a profile centered on continuity between early enemy logic and later mass violence.
- Separated protective motive from personal destructive desire.
- Preserved the distinction between psychological explanation and ethical endorsement.
