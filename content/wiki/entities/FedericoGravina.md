# Federico Gravina

## Overview

Federico Gravina is presented as the Sicilian-born Spanish admiral who led Spain's contingent in the combined French-Spanish fleet during the closing phase of the [[TrafalgarCampaign|Trafalgar campaign]].

## Current Profile

Gravina commanded within an alliance whose governments and officers did not share the same incentives. Spanish commanders had been warned against following the French toward the Channel, while disease, manpower shortages, British pressure, and French demands constrained their choices. The campaign episode argues that [[HoratioNelson|Nelson]]'s concealment of British strength also activated honor: remaining in Cadiz against an apparently weaker enemy would have been difficult for Gravina and his officers to defend. The battle retelling places him conspicuously aboard Principe de Asturias, records that he was wounded and died in 1806, and preserves admiring reported last words about Nelson only as the testimony of a British doctor.

## Key Characteristics

- Sicilian-born aristocrat serving as a senior Spanish naval commander.
- Leader of the Spanish contingent in Villeneuve's combined fleet.
- Operated under conflicting French demands and Spanish government preferences.
- Faced manpower, disease, supply, and coalition-command constraints at Cadiz.
- Participated in a decision to sail shaped partly by honor and the apparent balance of forces.
- Wounded at Trafalgar and dead the following year, with reported last words dependent on a single British account.

### Honor and departure from Cadiz

- [[244-trafalgar-countdown-to-annihilation-part-2-glt5489746159]] argues that Nelson's reduction of his visible force made continued inaction harder for Gravina to reconcile with Spanish honor.

### Battle and death

- [[613-nelson-glory-at-trafalgar-part-6-glt7193792251]] presents Gravina as conspicuous under fire, wounded at Trafalgar, and dead in 1806, while explicitly qualifying the reported last words attributed to him.

## Qualifications

The sources provide only a brief British-centered sketch of Gravina. His own reasoning, the internal Spanish command debate, exact government orders, battlefield decisions, wound and death chronology, and the relative weight of honor, logistics, and French pressure require additional evidence. The admiring last words about Nelson depend entirely on the British medical testimony cited by the episode.
