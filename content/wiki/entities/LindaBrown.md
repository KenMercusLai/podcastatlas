# Linda Brown

## Overview

Linda Brown Thompson was the child at the center of [[BrownVBoardOfEducation|Brown v. Board of Education]] and, in the bounded source, the speaker who used [[DarlaBuchanan]]'s termination letter to redirect commemorative attention toward Black educators who paid an unequal price for integration.

## Current Profile

As a child, Brown was required to travel farther to Monroe School after nearby Sumner School refused her enrollment on racial grounds. Her mother [[LeolaBrown]] praised Monroe's education and teachers, locating the family's grievance in discriminatory restriction rather than in rejection of the Black school.

At a 2004 fiftieth-anniversary event, Linda Brown Thompson interrupted her own eyewitness account to read Buchanan's letter. That choice made a school employee's displacement part of the public memory of the case bearing Brown's name and supported the episode's shift from formal student access to the distribution of institutional costs.

## Key Characteristics

- Child whose school assignment became central to the Topeka desegregation case.
- Experienced a longer and less convenient journey because nearby enrollment was denied by race.
- Later used commemorative authority to center a dismissed Black teacher.
- Embodies both the legal achievement of Brown and the unequal burdens of implementation.
