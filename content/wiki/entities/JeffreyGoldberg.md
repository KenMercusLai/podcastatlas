# Jeffrey Goldberg

## Overview
Jeffrey Goldberg is presented in the source as a Stanford ophthalmologist, department chair, clinician, and laboratory scientist working across eye disease, visual function, and optic-nerve regeneration research.

## Current Profile
In [[how-to-improve-your-eye-health-offset-vision-loss-dr-jeffrey-goldberg-scim5633963628]], Goldberg translates ophthalmology into a prevention-and-treatment hierarchy. Correctable optics such as refractive error, presbyopia, and cataract are separated from diseases in which delayed detection can permit irreversible loss, including glaucoma, macular degeneration, retinitis pigmentosa, and diabetic retinopathy. His advice repeatedly prioritizes access to qualified eye care, screening before symptoms, eye protection, contact-lens hygiene, and treatment adherence over universal hacks.

He is receptive to emerging work on visual performance and red or near-infrared light, but keeps wavelength, brightness, dose, timing, safety, and clinical-effect questions open. The same cautious stance appears in his discussion of PRP, cannabis for glaucoma, and sleep-position adjustments.

## Key Characteristics
- Combines clinical ophthalmology with laboratory investigation of the retina and optic nerve.
- Organizes eye care around early detection, preventable trauma, and preservation of remaining function.
- Distinguishes common optical correction from treatment of progressive retinal or optic-nerve disease.
- Treats access through either optometry or ophthalmology as more important than professional-title gatekeeping for routine entry to care.
- Uses mechanism to explain tools while retaining uncertainty around emerging light, regenerative, and performance interventions.
