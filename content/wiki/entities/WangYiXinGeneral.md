## Current Profile

The source presents Wang Yi as a commander trapped by reputation and punitive imagination. When [[YanYouXinGeneral|严尤]] advises bypassing Kunyang to strike Wan, Wang Yi rejects the plan because a great army leaving a small city behind would look weak. He instead talks of taking the city and slaughtering it.

The same pattern appears inside the siege. Yan You advises leaving an opening so the defenders have a self-preservation path rather than fighting to the death. Wang Yi refuses again, maintaining a fully closed siege that intensifies defender desperation and leaves Liu Xiu's outside force room to create panic.

When Liu Xiu attacks, Wang Yi and Wang Xun underestimate the smaller Han force, engage with a limited command group, and order other camps not to move. That decision makes the Xin army's size useless at the decisive point: the command group is beaten, the wider camp cannot coordinate, and Kunyang defenders break out to join the attack.

## Key Characteristics

- Xin-side field commander at Kunyang whose large army fails to convert scale into flexible command.
- Rejects Yan You's bypass-Wan advice because face and military display outweigh strategic priority.
- Rejects siege-exit advice, creating a no-escape situation that hardens resistance.
- Underestimates Liu Xiu's smaller force and keeps other camps inactive during the decisive attack.
- Survives the rout with Yan You and Chen Mao, but only after the army's collapse.

## Qualifications

The page is source-scoped to Hanji 664 and does not reconstruct Wang Yi's full Xin career. Wang Xun, Chen Mao, exact command hierarchy, troop totals, siege-device details, and later fate remain source-scoped. The durable claim is narrower: this episode uses Wang Yi as the negative commander whose face-driven rigidity and command inactivity make numerical advantage brittle.
