# James Keenan

## Overview

James Keenan is a Jesuit moral theologian who explains casuistry's case-based origins and frames sin as a failure to exercise available love.

## Current Profile

[[the-standard-case-the-standard-case|The Standard Case]] uses Keenan to explain that “casuistry” derives from the Latin *casus* and begins with the narrative of something that happened. The method emerged from practical encounters with unfamiliar circumstances and works by comparing a disputed case with more settled standard cases.

Keenan also supplies the moral formulation that [[JamesMartinJesuit]] applies after the Pulse nightclub shooting: a person or institution with the capacity to help can fail morally by not bothering to love. This widens responsibility beyond causing harm to include culpable nonresponse to suffering.

## Key Characteristics

- Explains casuistry as reasoning that begins with narrated cases and comparison.
- Connects the method to unfamiliar circumstances in which inherited principles underdetermine judgment.
- Defines moral failure to include omitted care when help is possible.
- Influences the episode's move from analytical classification to pastoral responsibility.

### Omitted love

## Qualifications

This profile is derived from one structured podcast summary rather than Keenan's publications, lectures, or the wider history of casuistry. The concise formulation of sin should not be treated as his complete moral theology, and the episode does not define when capacity, competing duties, risk, or institutional role make nonresponse culpable.

## What Changed

- Established Keenan's role in explaining the case-based structure of casuistry.
- Connected his account of omitted love to institutional silence and pastoral response.
