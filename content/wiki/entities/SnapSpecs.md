# Snap Specs

## Overview

Snap Specs are [[Snap]]'s consumer-sold optical smart glasses for [[SpatialComputing|spatial computing]], combining display, cameras, spatial tracking, gesture input, AI, an operating system, and phone-independent networking through a connected case.

## Current Profile

The product is a strong engineering demonstration and an unresolved consumer proposition. The episode reports a roughly 40% weight reduction from the prior developer generation, a 51-degree field of view, a $2,195 pre-tax price, and about four hours of stated battery life. Those improvements make integrated spatial glasses more credible, but roughly 132-136 grams remains too heavy for ordinary all-day wear in the speakers' judgment.

Specs also lacks a settled primary job. Its demonstrations span video, documents, sport overlays, games, meditation, training, sales, and repair. That breadth proves capability but leaves it between lighter camera-and-audio glasses and more capable headset-style devices. Short-duration assembly, repair, navigation, training, or shared AR may fit better than a claim to replace the phone throughout the day.

## Key Characteristics

- Optical see-through display and spatial tracking distinguish Specs from camera-and-audio-only smart glasses.
- Gesture interaction, shared spatial content, AI, and a dedicated operating system make it a general computing experiment.
- The connected case externalizes cellular networking but adds another object to carry.
- Weight, price, battery life, field of view, heat, and interaction latency remain central adoption constraints.
- A broad demo portfolio has not yet produced one indispensable consumer use case.
- Snap's creator tooling and Lens Studio history provide an ecosystem base, but independent application depth remains uncertain.

## Evidence

- **Integrated spatial-computing design:** [[snap-zuole-shinian-yanjing-zhongyu-dengdao-ta-de-shidai-le-ma-s10e30-f98c4370-6653-4af3-9a43-386bf0ea0547]] describes optical AR, spatial positioning, hand gestures, shared experiences, an operating system, and Specs Intelligence.
- **Engineering gains and wearability limits:** [[snap-zuole-shinian-yanjing-zhongyu-dengdao-ta-de-shidai-le-ma-s10e30-f98c4370-6653-4af3-9a43-386bf0ea0547]] reports the reduction from 226 grams to roughly 132-136 grams, while judging that result far above daily-glasses norms.
- **Commercial positioning:** [[snap-zuole-shinian-yanjing-zhongyu-dengdao-ta-de-shidai-le-ma-s10e30-f98c4370-6653-4af3-9a43-386bf0ea0547]] places the $2,195 product between cheaper Meta Ray-Bans and higher-capability headsets.
- **Use-case uncertainty:** [[snap-zuole-shinian-yanjing-zhongyu-dengdao-ta-de-shidai-le-ma-s10e30-f98c4370-6653-4af3-9a43-386bf0ea0547]] lists entertainment, productivity, sports, meditation, enterprise sales, repair, and training without identifying a dominant retained use.

## Qualifications

- The evidence comes from launch material and short hands-on experience, not long-term consumer use or independent sales data.
- Official battery life was not fully tested under sustained complex computation.
- Specs Intelligence is not glasses-exclusive, so general agent ability should not be counted as unique spatial-computing value without further evidence.
- The ten-to-twenty-year phone-replacement estimate is a speaker forecast rather than a technology roadmap with verified milestones.
