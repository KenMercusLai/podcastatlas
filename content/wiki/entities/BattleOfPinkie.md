# Battle of Pinkie

## Overview

The Battle of Pinkie was the catastrophic 1547 Scottish defeat whose tactical decisiveness failed to deliver the English political objective of controlling [[MaryQueenOfScots|Mary, Queen of Scots]].

## Current Profile

During the [[RoughWooing|Rough Wooing]], Edward Seymour led another large English invasion while [[JamesHamiltonEarlOfArran|Arran]] assembled what the episode calls probably the largest Scottish army yet fielded. The Scots were defeated with very heavy losses, and [[MaryOfGuise|Mary of Guise]] moved the child queen from Stirling to Inchmahome.

The defeat did not make Scotland accept the English marriage. It produced a stronger appeal to [[France]]: [[HenryIIOfFrance|Henry II]] sent a major expedition, Mary was promised to the Dauphin, and she was taken to France in 1548. Pinkie therefore separates battlefield victory from attainment of the war's political end.

## Key Characteristics

- Major English victory during the Rough Wooing.
- Catastrophic Scottish military loss under Arran's regency.
- Immediate threat forcing Mary into greater physical security.
- Battle whose result accelerated French intervention.
- Example of tactical success failing to compel the desired dynastic settlement.

## Qualifications

The episode's army superlative, force size, death estimate of roughly 10,000 Scots, tactical account, and causal weight assigned to the battle are source-scoped rather than independently verified here.

## What Changed

- Established Pinkie as a tactical English victory that accelerated the French dynastic outcome it was meant to prevent.
