# George Salomon

## Overview
George Salomon is presented as the product innovator who moved the family workshop behind [[Salomon]] from ski edges into safety-critical ski bindings.

## Current Profile
[[vol-274-chengwei-saluomeng-1013268300]] places George Salomon inside postwar France's expansion of skiing from military skill toward mass leisure. His contribution was to focus on a concrete injury mechanism: boots tied rigidly to skis did not release reliably during a fall. The episode attributes separate toe- and heel-release binding inventions in 1955 and 1957 to him and treats that work as the technical base of Salomon's later scale.

## Key Characteristics
- Worked from a specific safety and usability problem rather than from lifestyle marketing.
- Helped move Salomon from component workshop toward core ski-equipment brand.
- Connected product innovation with the broader popularization of skiing.

## Evidence
- **Safety problem:** [[vol-274-chengwei-saluomeng-1013268300]] says early boot attachment could trap the foot during a fall and cause injury.
- **Binding innovation:** [[vol-274-chengwei-saluomeng-1013268300]] attributes releasable toe and heel binding designs in the 1950s to George Salomon.
- **Commercial consequence:** [[vol-274-chengwei-saluomeng-1013268300]] links those inventions to Salomon becoming the leading binding brand by 1972.
