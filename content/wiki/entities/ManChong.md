# 满宠 / Man Chong

满宠 / Man Chong enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1006-guojia-buman-yuanshao-weihe-que-neng-guishun-caocao-lup-p-inz1sdlhpnaggqjp0eavar|Hanji 1006]] as the Xu official who turns [[CaoHong|曹洪]]'s lawbreaking clients into a public authority test. Cao Hong first writes to Man Chong and then seeks [[CaoCao|曹操]]'s help, but Man Chong executes the offenders before kinship access can become exemption.

The episode reads Cao Cao's praise for Man Chong as more than approval of severity. It becomes [[RuleOverKinshipPower|法度压过亲缘特权]]: Cao Cao uses Man Chong's enforcement to show that clan dependents cannot openly neutralize local law, strengthening official confidence and balancing Cao-family military privilege against bureaucratic order.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1020-sanguo-baijiazi-yuanshu-ruhe-ba-hao-pai-da-de-xilan-lhwr9hfmanr-zktleufy3kvl38u0|Hanji 1020]] later adds Man Chong as the Xudu official assigned by Cao Cao to interrogate [[YangBiao|杨彪]]. [[KongRong|孔融]] and [[XunYu|荀彧]] urge him not to use torture, but the source says he proceeds and then reports that Yang Biao has provided no incriminating statement.

The episode reads Man Chong's role as more complex than simple cruelty. His final report gives Cao Cao a path to release Yang Biao: without firm evidence, punishing a famous old minister would lose public confidence. That makes Man Chong part of [[PrestigeConstrainedPoliticalProsecution|名望约束下的政治追诉]], where process, reputation, and political trust interact.

After Yang Biao is freed, Kong Rong and Xun Yu are said to understand Man Chong's intention and respect him more. The page keeps that as the source's interpretation, not as a general defense of torture.
