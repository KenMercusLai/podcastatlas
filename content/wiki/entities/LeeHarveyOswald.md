# Lee Harvey Oswald

## Overview

Lee Harvey Oswald was a former U.S. Marine and Soviet defector whom the episode identifies as the killer of [[JohnFKennedy|John F. Kennedy]] and police officer J. D. Tippit.

## Current Profile

The sources construct a cumulative profile rather than relying on one decisive clue. They link a lonely and unstable childhood, early Marxism, Marine marksmanship, failed Soviet defection, conflict with Marina Oswald, workplace difficulty, Cuba activism, a rifle bought under the A. J. Hidell alias, and the attempted shooting of Edwin Walker to a search for dignity and historical significance through politics and violence.

For the assassination itself, the hosts emphasize opportunity and conduct. Part 3 supplies the earliest event-specific observations: witnesses noticed a man with a rifle or a figure at the Depository's sixth-floor window, several people saw an object withdrawn immediately after the shots, and Officer Marion Baker briefly confronted Oswald on the second floor before superintendent Roy Truly identified him as an employee. The later episodes add that Oswald worked in the Texas School Book Depository before the motorcade route became public, made an unusual weeknight visit, left his wedding ring behind, carried a long package, was linked to the sixth floor and rifle, left immediately, collected a revolver, killed [[JDTippit|J. D. Tippit]], resisted arrest, and lied during interrogation.

Part 4 adds the chain from the broadcast description and Tippit witnesses through the Texas Theatre arrest, the A. Hidell identification, communist material found at his room, interrogation, public appearance, and rifle mail-order trace. Part 5 adds how Marina and Robert Oswald read his unusually calm demeanor as incriminating, while keeping family impressions distinct from physical proof. It also reconstructs [[JackRuby|Jack Ruby]] killing him before trial, meaning Oswald could never be publicly examined or sentenced and the apparent silencing became part of the conspiracy story. The sources treat the cumulative case as stronger than intelligence-agency explanations, but they remain podcast syntheses rather than a complete forensic adjudication.

## Key Characteristics

- Socially isolated and resentful figure whose politics became central to identity and self-worth.
- Marxist and pro-Cuba activist whose Soviet experience disappointed rather than ended his ideological ambitions.
- Marine-trained marksman with the skills the episode considers sufficient for the assassination.
- Violent actor linked by the source to the Walker attempt and Tippit killing before and after Kennedy's murder.
- Book Depository employee whose accidental workplace access created a late opportunity to attack the motorcade.
- Suspect whose flight, resistance, possession of a revolver, and false statements are treated as consciousness-of-guilt evidence.
- Defendant whose killing before trial prevented adversarial public testing of the case.

## Qualifications

The psychological and behavioral narrative is interpretive: childhood assessments, resentment, Marxism, political reading, envy, calm demeanor, small lies, and a desire for historical importance do not by themselves prove murder. Some claims depend on eyewitness identification, family impressions, later testimony, exact timing, host selection, and compressed treatment of disputed ballistic or trajectory questions. Part 3 follows the official sequence and does not independently audit whether every sixth-floor sighting referred to Oswald or whether the reported second-floor encounter occurred exactly as reconstructed. Because Oswald was killed before trial, the case also lacks the adversarial testing and defendant testimony a trial might have provided. The page records the sources' cumulative case without treating personality profiles or political ideology as substitutes for event-specific evidence.
