# Seb Falk

## Overview

Seb Falk is a historian of medieval science and the author of *The Light Ages*, presented in the source as a critic of the idea that scientific inquiry disappeared between classical antiquity and the Renaissance.

## Current Profile

Falk reconstructs medieval knowledge through ordinary practice rather than a sequence of isolated geniuses. His case study of [[JohnOfWestwick|John of Westwick]] connects textual work, travel, monastic life, astronomy, astrology, calculation, and instrument construction.

His method combines continuity with difference. He resists the “dark age” stereotype while warning that modern professional science, modern experiment, and the modern separation of science from religion should not simply be projected backward.

## Key Characteristics

- Historian specializing in medieval knowledge and scientific practice.
- Author of *The Light Ages*.
- Uses an ordinary monk and instrument maker as a microhistorical guide.
- Emphasizes material devices, calculation, textual transmission, and cross-cultural exchange.
- Rejects both scientific-dark-age mythology and anachronistic equivalence with modern science.

## Evidence

- Historical intervention: [[112-medieval-science-glt3839934134]] attributes the critique of Renaissance, Protestant, and Enlightenment dark-age narratives to Falk.
- Microhistorical method: [[112-medieval-science-glt3839934134]] follows John of Westwick through copying, translation, crusade, and equatorium construction.
- Calibrated continuity: [[112-medieval-science-glt3839934134]] distinguishes medieval *scientia*, experience, and natural philosophy from later professional and experimental institutions.

## Qualifications

The source is one podcast conversation centered on Falk's book and does not provide a complete intellectual biography or independent audit of every historical example. Broad claims about periods and institutions remain bounded by the episode's introductory scope.
