## Overview

韩棱 / Han Leng appears as an Eastern Han official who turns court etiquette into a boundary against [[DouXianEasternHan|Dou Xian]]'s ministerial overreach. In [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-764-ta-weile-huoming-jing-jianjie-mie-le-luoma-diguo-lpk0c6muneabhk1oonzyjvawwf4|Hanji 764]], he stops other officials from kneeling and shouting "wansui" to Dou Xian, arguing that no ritual permits giving a ruler's acclamation to a minister.

## Current Profile

Han Leng's current profile is compact but clear. He does not attack Dou Xian through a large policy memorial; instead, he prevents a public ritual scene that would have symbolically upgraded Dou from powerful minister to quasi-ruler. The source frames his refusal with the maxim that those above should not be arrogant and those below should not create disorder.

Because the episode later associates him with the phrase "Han Leng's sword," the page treats him as a ritual-order figure whose authority lies in drawing a line at the moment public language is about to normalize overreach.

## Key Characteristics

- Court official who blocks ruler-like acclamation for a powerful minister.
- Ritual boundary actor rather than a military or factional operator.
- Example of using etiquette to preserve hierarchy under outer-relative pressure.
- Small-action resistor whose intervention prevents public normalization of Dou Xian's dominance.
- Source of the episode's "Han Leng's sword" memory, kept here as source-scoped phrasing.
