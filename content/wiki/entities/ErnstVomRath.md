# Ernst vom Rath

## Overview

Ernst vom Rath was a junior German diplomat in Paris shot by [[HerschelGrunspan|Herschel Grünspan]] on 7 November 1938 and declared dead on 9 November, after which Nazi leaders used his death as the immediate pretext for [[Kristallnacht]].

## Current Profile

Vom Rath matters in the current wiki less as a developed biographical subject than as the hinge in a propaganda sequence. His death coincided with the fifteenth anniversary of the Beer Hall Putsch, when senior Nazis were gathered in Munich. Newspapers had already intensified antisemitic rhetoric after the shooting; the announcement of his death at the gathering helped [[AdolfHitler]] and [[JosephGoebbels]] represent coordinated violence as public retaliation.

## Key Characteristics

- He served as a junior diplomat at the German embassy in Paris.
- He died two days after Grünspan shot him five times.
- His death was politically staged as justification for violence the regime organized and permitted.

## Evidence

- Shooting and death: [[410-the-nazis-in-power-the-night-of-broken-glass-part-7-glt5771265943]] dates the shooting to 7 November and the death to 9 November 1938.
- Propaganda use: [[410-the-nazis-in-power-the-night-of-broken-glass-part-7-glt5771265943]] connects the Munich announcement to Goebbels's encouragement of supposedly spontaneous action.
- Pogrom sequence: [[410-the-nazis-in-power-the-night-of-broken-glass-part-7-glt5771265943]] details subsequent police nonintervention, mass arrest preparation, and Reich-wide attacks.

## Qualifications

The bounded source supplies little about vom Rath's life, views, or diplomatic work. Claims beyond his role in the shooting-to-pogrom chronology remain outside the current evidence inventory. Describing his death as a pretext does not deny the shooting; it distinguishes that act from the regime's separate agency in organizing mass persecution.

- Distinguished the shooting from the Nazi state's coordinated response.
