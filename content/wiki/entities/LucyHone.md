# Lucy Hone

## Overview
Lucy Hone is a resilience researcher represented here through a [[HiddenBrain]] episode about applying psychological research after the 2014 death of her 12-year-old daughter Abby in a car crash.

## Current Profile
Hone's account joins professional preparation with severe personal loss. After earlier resilience work surrounding the Christchurch earthquakes, she found conventional stage language and deterministic warnings about years of dysfunction too passive to answer what her family could do next. She instead distinguished involuntary grief reactions from the partly selectable responses that shape attention and daily life.

Her practical stance combines [[GriefResponseAgency]], limited counterfactual inquiry, a “helping or harming?” test, [[DualProcessBereavementOscillation]], positive emotion, writing, family attention, and permission for restorative breaks. She does not present agency as control over grief or as a demand for self-rescue; strategies are experiments for people who want them, with individual variation and professional-care needs left explicit.

## Key Characteristics
- Connects resilience research to bereavement without treating the two as identical.
- Separates involuntary grief reactions from partly chosen thoughts, actions, and attentional responses.
- Rejects a mandatory stage sequence and supports movement between loss and restoration.
- Uses practical questions and experiments rather than a new universal grieving formula.
- Frames agency as a source of hope while resisting blame, suppression, and compulsory positivity.

## Qualifications
This profile is based on one structured episode summary rather than Hone's scholarship, clinical records, full interview transcript, or book. Her family's decisions and coping practices do not establish a universal grief pathway or treatment. Agency language should not imply that involuntary symptoms, severe impairment, trauma, depression, or prolonged grief can be resolved through attention and willpower alone.
