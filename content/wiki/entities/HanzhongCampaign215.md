## Overview

曹操西征汉中（215年） / Cao Cao's Hanzhong Campaign is the episode-bounded campaign in which [[CaoCao|曹操]] moves west against [[ZhangLuFivePecks|张鲁]], takes the Yangping defenses after an initially planned withdrawal, occupies [[Hanzhong|汉中]], and converts military victory into negotiated incorporation.

## Current Profile

The campaign joins preparation, route choice, operational friction, contingency, and post-victory politics. Cao Cao reportedly stabilizes the court, concentrates supplies while concealing his objective, clears the Wudu-side threat, and then discovers that surrendering informants have understated Yangping's terrain and defenses. Heavy losses and low grain lead to a withdrawal order, but a lost forward unit enters the enemy camp at night; after [[XiahouDun|夏侯惇]] verifies the penetration, Cao Cao resumes the attack and breaks Zhang Wei's position.

The victory does not end with seizure. Zhang Lu leaves government stores intact and sealed, while Cao Cao protects them, offers reassurance, and later grants rank to surrendering leaders. The campaign therefore becomes both a contingency-shaped battlefield success and a case of turning an adversary's preserved bargaining signal into political absorption.

## Key Characteristics

- Begins with internal stabilization, concealed logistical concentration, and uncertainty about the true objective.
- Clears a side threat before committing fully to the Hanzhong approach.
- Exposes the danger of relying on favorable reports that do not match terrain and defensive depth.
- Turns from planned withdrawal to victory after an accidental night penetration is verified and exploited.
- Converts conquest into incorporation through preserved stores, reassurance, rank, and office.

## Qualifications

The current page rests on one structured podcast summary, not a critical campaign reconstruction. Exact routes, dates, forces, casualty and grain conditions, individual agency in the night entry, dialogue, motives, office grants, and the relative weight of chance remain source-scoped. “Campaign” here groups the episode's operations and settlement without claiming that every phase followed one stable master plan.
