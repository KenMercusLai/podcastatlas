# 南基贺 / Nan Jihe

## Overview
南基贺 is a Seoul-based psychiatrist and mental-health guest in [[ZheBingShuoLaiHuaChang|这病说来话长]] episodes on school authority conflict, workplace bullying, psychological boundaries, intimacy, shame, and Korean youth pressure.

## Current Profile
Across the current source set, 南基贺 functions as a clinical interpreter rather than a media critic. In VOL.214, he uses [[TrueEducationKDrama|《铁拳教育》/《真教育》]] to explain why scenes of parent pressure, student violence, teacher collapse, and workplace coercion feel psychologically recognizable, while repeatedly warning that real-world response cannot rely on retaliatory violence.

VOL.202 adds an intimacy branch. Using [[LoveTranslationKDrama|《爱情怎么翻译》]], he reads push-pull love as a shame and attachment problem: a person may push away first to avoid being rejected, or say "you go" while hoping to be held. He also connects relationship avoidance to Korean high-pressure life through source-scoped "three-give-up" and "five-give-up" language.

His practical frame connects individual distress to social structure. He reads "for your good" control as objectification, boss pressure as partly rooted in organizational interests and transferred accountability, relationship withdrawal as partly shaped by shame and pressure, and victim self-blame as something to interrupt through [[PsychologicalBoundaryProtection|psychological boundary protection]] or clearer self-translation.

## Key Characteristics
- Interprets drama scenes through psychiatric, developmental, and social-structure lenses.
- Keeps popular labels such as workplace PUA close to concrete behavior rather than casual diagnosis.
- Treats bullying as a power-relationship problem, not only as a personal weakness or personality flaw.
- Recommends practical coping through self-worth separation, low emotional feedback, evidence preservation, complaint routes, expression, and professional help.
- Reads intimacy difficulties through shame, attachment, indirect communication, work pressure, and the need to tolerate difference.
- Keeps Korean legal, workplace complaint, and youth-pressure examples source-scoped rather than universal.
