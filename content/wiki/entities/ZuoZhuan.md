## Overview
《左传》 / Zuo Zhuan is a pre-Qin chronological narrative classic used in the wiki both as a vivid Spring-Autumn story archive and as a source for later historical and omen interpretation.

## Current Profile
The current synthesis keeps three uses together. The Mihuan Chishu episode makes Zuo Zhuan approachable through strange, violent, comic, and ritualized Spring-Autumn anecdotes. Zhouji 07 uses it more narrowly through Wang Yinglin's citation of "国主山川" in an omen-reading context. Hanji 591-2 adds the formal source-map layer: Zuo Zhuan is introduced as a chronological history covering the Spring-Autumn world, valued for narrative art, warfare, characterization, and diplomatic speech, while also containing some source-scoped supernatural or omen-colored material.

## Key Characteristics
- Chronological pre-Qin history text associated with the Spring-Autumn period.
- Preserves vivid narrative scenes of war, ritual, courage, humiliation, and political improvisation.
- Has high literary value in the episode's account, especially in war narration, characterization, and diplomatic language.
- Can support later omen and political readings, but supernatural or omen material should remain source-scoped.

## Evidence
- Narrative openness: [[109-xianliao-zuozhuan-zhi-chunqiu-yundonghui-747508773]] treats Zuo Zhuan as a store of vivid Spring-Autumn stories about war, ritual, insult, courage, luck, and political improvisation.
- Omen-reading use: [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-07-liangqi-cisha-1-lvkp-ju7pf5z5cz3thojv3s2fkyx|Zhouji 07 part 1]] says Wang Yinglin cites the phrase "国主山川" when interpreting the Guo mountain landslide.
- Formal orientation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-591-2-ruhe-geng-gaoxiao-di-dudong-lishi-gudai-biannian-jianzan-juequ-qiannian-fengliu-yunyan-lruv48fgfpo7u0tjsqb264k0y7yq|Hanji 591-2]] introduces Zuo Zhuan as a chronological history and one of the Spring and Autumn commentarial traditions, with strong narrative and literary value.

## Qualifications
The wiki does not settle authorship or textual-formation debates around Zuo Zhuan. Hanji 591-2 explicitly notes traditional and modern uncertainty over attribution, so the page keeps authorship claims source-scoped. Sports metaphors from episode 109 remain access frames, not strict military history.
