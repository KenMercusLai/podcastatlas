## Overview
Zhu Chong is the high-risk remonstrator in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-795-dengjia-miewang-hou-weihe-juchao-wei-tamen-mingyuan-lqvmbqrrookphb4becaqbp7h-wi1|Hanji 795]]. Serving as da sinong, he bares himself, carries a coffin, and memorializes for the [[DengFamilyAnEraLateHan|Deng family]], turning a victorious anti-Deng purge into a public problem of evidence, interrogation, and proportionality.

## Current Profile
Zhu Chong's role is procedural rather than factional in the narrow sense. He does not simply praise [[EmpressDowagerDengLateHan|Empress Dowager Deng]]; he argues that a court cannot destroy a household on one-sided palace testimony when the accused are dead, dispersed, or unable to confront the claims against them.

His remonstrance does not fully reverse policy. Emperor An dismisses him and sends him toward the Tingwei process, but the episode links Zhu Chong's intervention and public sympathy to partial correction: Deng corpses can return for burial, surviving cousins can return to Luoyang, and responsibility is shifted onto local officials who over-executed the purge.

## Key Characteristics
- Da sinong who takes responsibility for an extreme public memorial defending the Deng family.
- Uses bodily abasement and coffin symbolism to show that he expects death for remonstrating.
- Frames the Deng case as a defect in proof, confrontation, and procedure rather than only a policy disagreement.
- Defends Empress Dowager Deng and her brothers as meritorious servants of Han who should not be erased through household punishment.
- Helps force limited correction without achieving full rehabilitation of the Deng family.
- Pays a career cost through dismissal, showing the narrow boundary of permissible remonstrance under Emperor An.
