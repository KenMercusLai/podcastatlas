## Overview

班伯 / Ban Bo enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-539-hanchengdi-yu-zhangfang-de-qinggan-jiuge-lk_wzo4h-c-xkipffhgikronrk3m|Hanji 539]] as the recently recovered Guanglu Dafu and palace attendant who uses a Shang Zhou and Daji image to warn [[HanChengdi|汉成帝]] against wine, disorder, and indulgent court conduct.

## Current Profile

Ban Bo's role is narrow but important. Chengdi asks whether the pictured story of Shang Zhou leaning on Daji in drunken revelry is true. Ban Bo answers by refusing the simple woman-blame frame: whether or not the image is literal, the warning is about Zhou's intoxication, day-night reversal, and loss of order. The answer lets him correct Chengdi indirectly while staying inside a classical historical analogy.

The effect is double. Chengdi receives the answer as useful speech and says he has long missed Ban Bo's counsel during illness. [[ZhangFangWesternHan|张放]], however, reacts badly and leaves. Ban Bo therefore becomes the trigger for the source's wider favorite-removal chain: honest analogy exposes the tension between correction and Chengdi's pleasure circle.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han Guanglu Dafu and attendant in Chengdi's court.
- Uses historical analogy rather than direct accusation to correct the ruler.
- Shifts the Shang Zhou/Daji example away from misogynistic blame and toward wine and disorder.
- Praised by Chengdi as a source of good counsel after illness.
- Indirectly exposes Zhang Fang's sensitivity and the favorite circle's vulnerability.
