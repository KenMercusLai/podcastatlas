# The Holocaust / 犹太人大屠杀

## Overview

The Holocaust / 犹太人大屠杀 was [[NaziGermany|Nazi Germany]]'s genocide of European Jews, in which ideological hatred became scalable through war, law, dispossession, identification, deportation, forced labor, bureaucratic coordination, concentration and extermination camps, and ordinary institutional work.

## Current Profile

The current evidence joins the genocide's later extermination machinery to its prewar escalation. [[Kristallnacht]] did not yet constitute the later industrial killing system, but it showed how central authorization, propaganda, police nonprotection, mass detention, local violence, and [[EconomicAryanization|economic dispossession]] could act together. The subsequent Central Office for Jewish Emigration under [[ReinhardHeydrich]] gave coerced removal an administrative center whose mandate the episode connects to Wannsee in 1942.

The wiki's data-infrastructure branch adds census records, punched cards, railway logistics, labor allocation, technical maintenance, and camp administration. Its Arendt branch warns against explaining perpetrators only as abnormal monsters: [[AdolfEichmann|Adolf Eichmann]]'s legalistic job language and [[BanalityOfEvil|banal role performance]] show how mass murder could be treated as official work without becoming morally neutral.

Together, these sources present the Holocaust as an ideological crime organized through modern institutions and differentiated participation. Central Nazi responsibility remains decisive, while attention to firms, experts, civil servants, police, professionals, and coerced intermediaries explains how the criminal project acquired reach.

## Key Characteristics

- Genocidal antisemitism developed through a sequence of exclusion, marking, dispossession, expulsion, detention, deportation, forced labor, and murder.
- War planning intensified the regime's internal-enemy logic and expanded its territorial and administrative reach.
- Bureaucratic records, logistics, firms, technical systems, and routine offices made persecution operational at scale.
- Open terror and apparently ordinary administrative work were complementary rather than opposing forms.
- Responsibility was differentiated across leaders, perpetrators, enablers, beneficiaries, constrained intermediaries, resisters, and victims.
- Contemporary interpretation must resist both demonic exceptionalism and explanations that dissolve agency into “the system.”

## Qualifications

The bounded sources illuminate selected mechanisms and interpretations rather than supplying a complete chronological or victim-centered history of the Holocaust. The exact path from forced removal to systematic extermination developed over time and should not be treated as a fully fixed blueprint in 1938. Corporate and bureaucratic analysis must not obscure antisemitic ideology, direct killing, victim experience, or primary responsibility; discussion of coerced Jewish Councils must preserve the difference between responsibility under duress and perpetrator guilt.
