# American Airlines

## Overview
American Airlines is a large U.S. carrier used in the wiki to illustrate incumbent fare-and-loyalty advantages and the move toward fleet-scale low-earth-orbit in-flight connectivity.

## Current Profile
The two bounded sources place American on both sides of airline competition. It can match budget-carrier headline fares while retaining network and loyalty advantages, and it is described as making a large [[Starlink]] commitment intended to turn free high-speed Wi-Fi into a service and member-acquisition layer.

## Key Characteristics
- Legacy carrier with route-network and loyalty advantages over smaller low-cost entrants.
- Participant in the basic-economy response to ultra-low-cost airline pricing.
- Large announced Starlink deployment case in the latest source.
- Uses free connectivity as both service differentiation and a possible loyalty-growth channel.

## Evidence
- Fare and loyalty competition: [[spirit-airlines-and-the-future-of-cheap-flights]] groups American with Delta and United as incumbents that copied cheaper upfront fares while retaining stronger networks and loyalty programs.
- Connectivity scale: [[haiwai-hangsi-fenfen-yongbao-xinglian-mianfei-wi-fi-weihe-zhujian-chengwei-hangsi-xin-biaopei-gkwriueoqxaeah7ykgtrgbwv]] says American planned Starlink installation across more than 1,000 aircraft.
- Indirect monetization: [[haiwai-hangsi-fenfen-yongbao-xinglian-mianfei-wi-fi-weihe-zhujian-chengwei-hangsi-xin-biaopei-gkwriueoqxaeah7ykgtrgbwv]] cites American's CEO as linking free Wi-Fi to record membership growth and places connectivity inside advertising and co-branded-card economics.

## Qualifications
The planned aircraft count, equipment and installation costs, deployment timing, membership growth, and causal role of Wi-Fi remain source-scoped. The sources do not audit American's full network strategy, service quality, contract terms, or return on connectivity investment.
