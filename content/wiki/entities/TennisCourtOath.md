# Tennis Court Oath

## Overview

The Tennis Court Oath was the 20 June 1789 pledge by deputies of the [[NationalAssembly1789|National Assembly]] not to separate until they had established a constitution for France.

## Current Profile

The oath converted an ambiguous exclusion into a durable constitutional commitment. Finding their ordinary hall closed for preparations for a royal session, the deputies moved to a nearby real-tennis court and reframed physical displacement as evidence that their authority did not depend on a room supplied by the crown. The act gave collective form to the Assembly's constituent claim before the confrontation of 23 June.

## Key Characteristics

- Triggered when deputies found their meeting hall closed.
- Held in a nearby real-tennis court suggested by Joseph-Ignace Guillotin.
- Bound deputies not to disperse before completing a constitution.
- Made collective commitment visible through a dramatic improvised setting.
- Separated national representative authority from royal control over meeting space.

## Qualifications

- The source notes that the hall closure was connected to preparations for a royal session; it does not prove that the immediate closure was a deliberate attempt to abolish the Assembly.
- The oath established a constituent commitment, not a completed constitution or settled democratic order.
