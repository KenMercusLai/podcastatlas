# Fort Sumter

## Overview

Fort Sumter is the federal installation in Charleston Harbor whose Confederate bombardment converted secession and disputed federal property into open Civil War.

## Current Profile

[[200-american-civil-war-the-causes-part-1-glt1124511401]] supplies the causal threshold. After seven slaveholding states seceded and organized the Confederacy, South Carolina demanded surrender of the federal fort. Its forces opened fire before Lincoln could resupply it and compelled surrender; Lincoln's call for volunteers then helped push Virginia and other Upper South states into the Confederacy.

[[201-american-civil-war-outbreak-part-2-glt4100428000]] treats the attack as the opening event in a war that both sides initially misunderstood. The fort therefore connects slavery-centered secession to the later contests over democratic Union, endurance, industrial capacity, and emancipation.

## Key Characteristics

- Federal fort located in Charleston Harbor after South Carolina's secession.
- Disputed federal property whose demanded surrender became a military crisis.
- Target of the Confederate bombardment that opened organized war.
- Trigger for Lincoln's volunteer call and further Upper South secession.

## Qualifications

The profile is bounded to two retrospective episodes rather than a tactical history of the siege, garrison, relief expedition, command decisions, or casualties. The distinction between resupply, reinforcement, coercion, and responsibility for escalation remains source-scoped.
