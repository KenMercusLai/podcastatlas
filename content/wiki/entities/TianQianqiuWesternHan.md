## Overview

田千秋 / Tian Qianqiu is a Western Han official whose current wiki profile now spans both his rise under [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] and his later constrained chancellorship under [[EmperorZhaoOfHan|汉昭帝刘弗陵]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-389-1-lishi-jiemi-tianqianqiu-pingshenme-keyi-dang-chengxiang-likbkdewndpp8vzridm6iymlcb50|Hanji 389-1]] explains his sudden promotion from Dahonglu to chancellor and Fumin Hou through Wudi's late-life turn against wasteful immortality-seeking: Tian proposes dismissing failed fangshi, and Wudi accepts the advice as part of a wider remorse and recovery posture.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-401-suihong-zhiming-hanzhaodi-tuiwei-rangxian-huoguang-zaile-ll6pyzpjl0mlcfn0g1zjot2wf8s8|Hanji 401]] then shows a later Tian whose chancellor title coexists with [[HuoGuangWesternHan|霍光]]'s practical dominance. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-402-1-sanghongyang-ceng-bang-hanwudi-zhifu-weihe-zuihou-canzao-miezu-ltpcos20d5mafcoywmfcb6iifwsv|Hanji 402-1]] adds a sharper legal-pressure scene: Tian tries to protect his son-in-law [[XuRenWesternHan|徐仁]] after the [[HouShiWuWesternHan|侯史吴]] case turns against Xu Ren and [[WangPingWesternHan|王平]], but he cannot move the court outside Huo Guang's will. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-403-1-xisha-xiongnu-shizhe-xihan-zui-tiexue-waijiaoguan-jingshi-ta-lum-xolrqlwjgmv5xscssenogyx|Hanji 403-1]] adds his death notice in 77 BCE, preserving him as a court-chronology figure before the episode turns to [[FuJieziWesternHan|傅介子]]'s Western Regions mission.

## Current Profile

The sources make Tian Qianqiu a case of timely remonstrance followed by constrained office. Hanji 389-1 says he has little obvious prior merit for an unprecedentedly rapid chancellorship, but his advice exactly fits Han Wudi's newly declared desire to stop harming the people and wasting state resources: dismissing ineffective fangshi gives Wudi a concrete break with the immortality projects that had marked his late reign. The same source also qualifies pure luck by describing Tian as gentle, wise, and later fitting for the office.

Under Han Zhao, the profile changes. Hanji 401 has Tian hold the chancellor title while the episode emphasizes that "政事一决大将军光": major affairs are decided by Huo Guang. Tian's defining behavior there is careful self-preservation and nonintervention in Huo's personnel choices. Hanji 402-1 shows the limit of that posture when kinship obligation pulls him into the Hou Shiwu case: Tian convenes senior officials and scholars after Huo rejects the initial amnesty reading, but the assembled court follows Huo's line and condemns Hou Shiwu as a great rebel. Du Yannian's later memorial helps keep Tian from being dragged down with Xu Ren, leaving Tian alive but politically sobered. Hanji 403-1 then closes his current profile with the notice that Fumin Hou Tian Qianqiu dies in Yuanfeng 4 / 77 BCE.

## Key Characteristics

- Promoted under Han Wudi after advising the dismissal of ineffective fangshi during Wudi's late remorse and policy turn.
- Receives the chancellorship and Fumin Hou title less for long prior merit than for matching the ruler's shift from immortality projects toward stopping waste and helping the people.
- Later Western Han chancellor under Han Zhao in a court dominated by Huo Guang.
- Presented as cautious and self-protective rather than as an independent governing center.
- Does not object when Huo Guang promotes or appoints officials.
- Functions in the source as a contrast figure who makes Huo Guang's real authority more visible.
- Attempts to protect his son-in-law Xu Ren in the Hou Shiwu case, but discovers that court consensus follows Huo Guang's harsher classification.

- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-402-1-sanghongyang-ceng-bang-hanwudi-zhifu-weihe-zuihou-canzao-miezu-ltpcos20d5mafcoywmfcb6iifwsv|Hanji 402-1]] says Du Yannian later argued that Tian usually spoke for subordinates and should not be discarded without major fault.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 389-1, Hanji 401, Hanji 402-1, and Hanji 403-1. It does not reconstruct Tian Qianqiu's full biography, the exact legal form of the Fumin Hou title, the original Shiji/Hanshu assessment of his ability, or his full relationship with Han Wudi and Han Zhao beyond these episodes' promotion, regency-context description, Hou Shiwu case involvement, and death notice.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 389-1's rise layer: Tian's dismissal-of-fangshi advice helps explain why Han Wudi rapidly promotes him to chancellor and Fumin Hou during the late-life policy turn.
- Preserved Hanji 401's contrast between chancellor title and regent-centered practical authority, Hanji 402-1's Hou Shiwu case layer, and Hanji 403-1's 77 BCE death notice.
