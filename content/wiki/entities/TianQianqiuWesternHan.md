## Overview

田千秋 / Tian Qianqiu enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-401-suihong-zhiming-hanzhaodi-tuiwei-rangxian-huoguang-zaile-ll6pyzpjl0mlcfn0g1zjot2wf8s8|Hanji 401]] as the Western Han chancellor whose cautious silence under [[HuoGuangWesternHan|霍光]] helps show how far practical decision-making had shifted toward the regent in [[EmperorZhaoOfHan|Han Zhao]]'s court. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-402-1-sanghongyang-ceng-bang-hanwudi-zhifu-weihe-zuihou-canzao-miezu-ltpcos20d5mafcoywmfcb6iifwsv|Hanji 402-1]] adds a sharper legal-pressure scene: Tian tries to protect his son-in-law [[XuRenWesternHan|徐仁]] after the [[HouShiWuWesternHan|侯史吴]] case turns against Xu Ren and [[WangPingWesternHan|王平]], but he cannot move the court outside Huo Guang's will. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-403-1-xisha-xiongnu-shizhe-xihan-zui-tiexue-waijiaoguan-jingshi-ta-lum-xolrqlwjgmv5xscssenogyx|Hanji 403-1]] adds his death notice in 77 BCE, preserving him as a court-chronology figure before the episode turns to [[FuJieziWesternHan|傅介子]]'s Western Regions mission.

## Current Profile

The sources give Tian Qianqiu a narrow but revealing profile. Hanji 401 has him hold the chancellor title while the episode emphasizes that "政事一决大将军光": major affairs are decided by Huo Guang. Tian's defining behavior there is careful self-preservation and nonintervention in Huo's personnel choices. Hanji 402-1 shows the limit of that posture when kinship obligation pulls him into the Hou Shiwu case: Tian convenes senior officials and scholars after Huo rejects the initial amnesty reading, but the assembled court follows Huo's line and condemns Hou Shiwu as a great rebel. Du Yannian's later memorial helps keep Tian from being dragged down with Xu Ren, leaving Tian alive but politically sobered. Hanji 403-1 then closes his current profile with the notice that Fumin Hou Tian Qianqiu dies in Yuanfeng 4 / 77 BCE.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han chancellor under Han Zhao in a court dominated by Huo Guang.
- Presented as cautious and self-protective rather than as an independent governing center.
- Does not object when Huo Guang promotes or appoints officials.
- Functions in the source as a contrast figure who makes Huo Guang's real authority more visible.
- Attempts to protect his son-in-law Xu Ren in the Hou Shiwu case, but discovers that court consensus follows Huo Guang's harsher classification.
- Dies as Fumin Hou in the 77 BCE court chronology before Fu Jiezi's Western Regions narrative.
