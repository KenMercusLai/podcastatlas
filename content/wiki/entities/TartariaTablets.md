# Tărtăria Tablets

## Overview

The Tărtăria tablets are three small marked artifacts found in Romania in 1961 and associated in the episode with the wider [[VincaCulture|Vinča cultural sphere]].

## Current Profile

[[280-serbia-the-birthplace-of-civilisation-glt4941340070]] describes two rectangular tablets and one round tablet, including a horned-animal image and signs resembling the broader Vinča corpus. They matter because an early secure date and a writing interpretation could place structured notation in southeastern Europe before conventionally recognized Mesopotamian writing.

Neither premise is secure in the episode's account. The tablets were baked after excavation, preserving them but preventing direct radiocarbon dating. Debate therefore includes their layer and association, possible Mesopotamian influence, authenticity, and whether the signs are writing, proto-writing, ritual imagery, decoration, or ownership marks. No accepted decipherment is presented.

## Key Characteristics

- Three small marked artifacts discovered at Tărtăria, Romania, in 1961.
- Two rectangular forms and one round form, with signs and a horned-animal image.
- Post-discovery baking that preserved the objects but blocked direct carbon dating.
- Central artifacts in disputes about chronology, authenticity, cultural contact, and the definition of writing.

## Evidence

- Discovery and form: [[280-serbia-the-birthplace-of-civilisation-glt4941340070]] records the 1961 find, three-object group, shapes, signs, and horned-animal depiction.
- Dating problem: [[280-serbia-the-birthplace-of-civilisation-glt4941340070]] says baking after discovery prevented direct radiocarbon dating.
- Interpretive range: [[280-serbia-the-birthplace-of-civilisation-glt4941340070]] presents layer confusion, Sumerian influence, forgery, decoration, ownership marking, proto-writing, and writing as competing possibilities.

## Qualifications

The tablets' archaeological context, treatment history, and dating are represented here only through one podcast note. Their association with Vinča culture and their capacity to date the wider sign corpus require specialist evidence. Toby Griffin's proposed reading quoted by the episode is speculative and is not treated as a decipherment. Similarity to later signs does not by itself demonstrate linguistic identity, transmission, or continuity.

## What Changed

- Established an artifact profile centered on unresolved dating and classification rather than a claim of deciphered early writing.
