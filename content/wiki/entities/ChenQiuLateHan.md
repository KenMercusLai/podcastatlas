# 陈球 / Chen Qiu (late Han)

## Overview
Chen Qiu is a late-Han official whose profile now begins with local crisis duty in [[LinglingCommanderyLateHan|Lingling]] before moving to central-court ritual, high office, and anti-eunuch conspiracy. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-867-wugu-bei-po-zangshui-ta-xuanze-baqi-huiji-lqenvoy7x8ic-qn3-bf93qj8tjvk|Hanji 867]] presents him refusing to evacuate his family during the [[ZhuGaiLateHan|Zhu Gai]] and [[HuLanLateHan|Hu Lan]] rebellion, improvising city defenses, and turning a flood tactic back against the attackers.

## Current Profile
Chen Qiu's current profile combines principled office-duty with uneven strategic judgment. In Hanji 867, he treats the office of commandery governor as incompatible with private household-first retreat: if the prefect abandons the city for his wife and children, court authority itself is harmed. He then converts limited resources into bows and feathered spear-arrows, studies the terrain, and drains the attackers' diverted water out of the city.

The later court evidence extends this duty posture into ritual and factional politics. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-901-jixingzi-de-ren-hui-shi-shenme-xiachang-lh1yfyjzqptxvtnv9taiqms9yjud|Hanji 901]] backfills his 172 CE role as tingwei, where he is the first senior official to argue that [[EmpressDowagerDouMiao|Dou Miao]] should be buried with [[EmperorHuanOfHan|Emperor Huan]] and retain proper sacrificial status. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-913-kan-dong-saiweng-shima-wu-tou-rensheng-fuhuo-lkfbrwtgsjma85bzugqonzk3hy4g|Hanji 913]] records his 178 CE appointment as Taiwei from Taichang and removal in November. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-918-shiqing-bu-shunli-shi-weishenme-yao-bizui-lh-tbohgo96qw8bjqu3x1yp6czv5|Hanji 918]] then shows him as Yongle shaofu urging [[LiuHeSituLateHan|Liu He]] into an anti-[[CaoJieLateHan|Cao Jie]] plot.

That final branch is morally legible but operationally weak. Chen Qiu's argument frames inaction as a betrayal of imperial-clan duty and state responsibility, but it does not solve the leakage problem Liu He explicitly identifies. The page therefore treats Chen as a duty-heavy official whose courage is real, while his later plot pressure exposes the limits of principled urgency without secure execution.

## Key Characteristics
- Duty-first local defender who refuses to protect his household before the commandery he governs.
- Practical crisis operator who improvises weapons and water-control defenses under siege.
- Ritual-legitimacy official who publicly argues for Dou Miao's posthumous burial and sacrificial status.
- Senior-office marker whose 178 CE Taiwei appointment and removal show central-court prominence before the anti-eunuch plot.
- Anti-eunuch pressure actor whose moral argument pushes Liu He toward action despite unresolved secrecy risk.

## Evidence
- Local defense under rebellion: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-867-wugu-bei-po-zangshui-ta-xuanze-baqi-huiji-lqenvoy7x8ic-qn3-bf93qj8tjvk|Hanji 867]] has Chen Qiu refuse family evacuation, threaten further evacuation advice with punishment, improvise weapons, and sustain Lingling against more than ten days of pressure.
- Terrain and water countermeasure: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-867-wugu-bei-po-zangshui-ta-xuanze-baqi-huiji-lqenvoy7x8ic-qn3-bf93qj8tjvk|Hanji 867]] credits Chen Qiu with studying local topography, draining diverted water out of the city, and flooding the rebels instead.
- Burial and ritual status: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-901-jixingzi-de-ren-hui-shi-shenme-xiachang-lh1yfyjzqptxvtnv9taiqms9yjud|Hanji 901]] says Chen Qiu argues that Dou Miao helped Emperor Ling inherit the throne, should not absorb every Dou-family offense, and has a stronger ritual claim than [[FengGuirenLateHan|Feng Guiren]].
- High-office marker: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-913-kan-dong-saiweng-shima-wu-tou-rensheng-fuhuo-lkfbrwtgsjma85bzugqonzk3hy4g|Hanji 913]] says Chen Qiu is appointed Taiwei from Taichang in 178 CE and removed before [[QiaoXuanLateHan|Qiao Xuan]] succeeds him.
- Anti-Cao Jie plot pressure: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-918-shiqing-bu-shunli-shi-weishenme-yao-bizui-lh-tbohgo96qw8bjqu3x1yp6czv5|Hanji 918]] has Chen Qiu help press Liu He toward an anti-Cao Jie plot that is later exposed through [[ChengHuangLateHan|Cheng Huang]] and punished by [[EmperorLingOfHan|Emperor Ling]].

## Qualifications
The source tradition uses variants including 陈裘 and 陈求; this page normalizes them to 陈球 while preserving the transcript forms. Hanji 913 provides a senior-office marker but little character evidence. Hanji 918 should not be read as pure recklessness or pure righteousness: Chen Qiu's anti-eunuch pressure is understandable in the source's moral frame, but Liu He's warning about many ears and eyes is a serious operational constraint.
