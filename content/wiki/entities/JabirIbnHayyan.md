## Overview

Jabir ibn Hayyan / 贾比尔·伊本·哈扬 appears as a major figure in the episode's Islamic-world alchemy branch, associated with acids, metalwork, dyes, material classification, and a sulfur-mercury theory of metals.

## Current Profile

The source uses Jabir to show that [[WesternAlchemy|Western alchemy]] was transformed through Arabic-language scholarship rather than merely stored and returned unchanged to Europe. His attributed framework holds that metals differ through combinations of sulfur, mercury, and elemental qualities, making transmutation theoretically possible if those qualities can be rearranged. The episode also associates his tradition with letter-number speculation, illustrating how laboratory practice and symbolic cosmology could coexist.

## Key Characteristics

- Represents the Islamic-world development of inherited alchemical knowledge.
- Joins practical work with acids, metallurgy, and dyes to a theory of metal composition.
- Treats transmutation as rearrangement of material qualities.
- Combines material inquiry with symbolic or letter-based interpretation.

## Qualifications

This profile follows a broad podcast summary. The Jabirian corpus has complex attribution, so the source's biographical and discovery claims should not automatically be assigned to one historical individual. The episode does not independently establish priority, recipe details, or successful transmutation.
