# Georges Danton / 丹东

## Overview

Georges Danton was a major revolutionary leader who helped coordinate the August 1792 overthrow of effective monarchy, mobilized Paris during the Prussian invasion, tolerated and justified the [[SeptemberMassacres]], initially dominated the [[CommitteeOfPublicSafety|Committee of Public Safety]], and was guillotined after becoming associated with clemency and opposition to the Terror.

## Current Profile

The sources reveal a charismatic, politically flexible, but morally compromised emergency leader. In 1792 his Cordeliers and Commune position helped mobilize the overthrow of monarchy; as Minister of Justice he supported exceptional political justice, joined military mobilization to internal-enemy rhetoric, ignored a warning about endangered prisoners, and later justified the September deaths without being shown to have commanded them. He initially dominated the public-safety committee but lost influence as pragmatism became suspect. By late 1793 he favored peace, clemency, and winding down terror, yet suspected corruption and association with [[PhilippeFabreDEglantine|Fabre d'Églantine]] made that opposition vulnerable to a conspiracy case. Arrested with [[CamilleDesmoulins|Desmoulins]], he turned trial into political theatre until the Convention changed procedure to secure conviction and execution.

## Key Characteristics

- His charisma and Cordeliers-Commune position made him an unusually effective organizer and public speaker.
- As justice minister he joined national defence to exceptional justice, surveillance, arrest, and internal-enemy rhetoric.
- He knew prisoners were endangered, declined to intervene, and later justified the deaths without a proven direct command.
- He initially dominated the Committee of Public Safety but lost influence as negotiation and compromise became suspect.
- He later sought peace, clemency, and an end to terror through the [[Indulgents]].
- Suspected corruption and corrupt associates supplied genuine vulnerabilities inside an overextended conspiracy case.
- His truncated trial and defiant execution turned political defeat into a durable image of resistance to revolutionary extremism.

## Evidence

### August insurrection and ministerial ascent

- [[506-the-french-revolution-massacre-at-the-palace-part-4-glt9954902163]] places Danton in the Cordeliers and insurrectionary Commune network, then follows his appointment as Minister of Justice and support for a political tribunal amid surveillance and mass arrest.

### September 1792 mobilization and responsibility

- [[544-the-french-revolution-the-september-massacres-part-1-glt8301042051]] links Danton's military mobilization and raids to escalating internal-enemy fear, records his dismissal of the prison inspector's warning, and reports his later “indispensable sacrifice” justification.

### Radical leadership role

- [[125-mala-zhisi-beibi-de-ansha-huo-zhengyi-de-chujue-783972516]] places Danton in the simplified Jacobin leadership cluster during the Revolution's turn toward emergency and terror.

### Committee leadership, distinction, and removal

- [[707-the-terror-an-assassination-in-paris-part-1-glt6580043397]] identifies Danton as the committee's initial dominant member, distinguishes him from Marat's extreme blood rhetoric, and links his removal to perceptions of excessive pragmatism.

### Clemency, prosecution, and execution

- [[710-the-terror-death-at-the-guillotine-part-4-glt5038102951]] connects Danton's late-1793 return to peace and clemency, the Indulgents, suspected corruption, and failed reconciliation with Robespierre.
- [[710-the-terror-death-at-the-guillotine-part-4-glt5038102951]] follows his arrest, forceful courtroom defence, procedural exclusion, conviction, and execution on 5 April 1794.

## Qualifications

- No source proves that Danton issued an operational order for the September prison killings.
- Distributed action on 10 August does not make Danton its sole commander or erase the agency of sections, fédérés, and municipal organizers.
- Military emergency helps explain his mobilization but does not settle the moral or political responsibility created by incitement and deliberate non-intervention.
- Later moderation does not erase his earlier role in exceptional justice, internal-enemy rhetoric, or the September Massacres.
- The episode treats corruption suspicions as credible while distinguishing them from the unified foreign conspiracy used to condemn him.
- Private meetings, arrest warnings, final statements, and motives remain source-scoped reconstructions.

## What Changed

- Extended Danton's profile from committee removal through the Indulgents, arrest, trial, and execution.
- Integrated late clemency with rather than substituting it for his earlier emergency violence.
- Distinguished credible corruption vulnerability from the fabricated or overextended conspiracy case.
