# Georges Danton / 丹东

## Overview

Georges Danton was a major revolutionary leader and the initially dominant member of the [[CommitteeOfPublicSafety|Committee of Public Safety]] whose pragmatic willingness to compromise became a liability during the post-Marat radicalization of 1793.

## Current Profile

The sources move Danton beyond a generic Jacobin leadership role. He participated in the politics that created emergency government, but he regarded Marat as excessively bloodthirsty and later appeared too pragmatic for a climate in which assassination, war, revolt, and fear made compromise look dangerous; his removal opened the committee to [[MaximilienRobespierre|Robespierre]].

## Key Characteristics

- He belonged to the radical revolutionary leadership but was not interchangeable with Marat or Robespierre.
- He was the dominant figure in the committee's initial nine-member form.
- He viewed Marat's blood rhetoric and personality negatively.
- His willingness to compromise became politically costly as suspicion intensified.
- His removal marked a leadership transition toward Robespierre's ascendancy.

## Evidence

### Radical leadership role

- [[125-mala-zhisi-beibi-de-ansha-huo-zhengyi-de-chujue-783972516]] places Danton in the simplified Jacobin leadership cluster during the Revolution's turn toward emergency and terror.

### Committee leadership and removal

- [[707-the-terror-an-assassination-in-paris-part-1-glt6580043397]] identifies Danton as the initial dominant committee member and links his removal to perceptions of excessive pragmatism.

### Distinction from Marat

- [[707-the-terror-an-assassination-in-paris-part-1-glt6580043397]] reports that Danton and Robespierre both considered Marat excessively bloodthirsty, preserving difference inside the radical camp.

## Qualifications

- Neither source offers a full biography or evaluates Danton's entire revolutionary record.
- Pragmatism is the episode's explanation for his loss of position, not a complete causal account of every factional decision.

## What Changed

- Danton is now represented as the committee's initial leader rather than only a supporting radical node.
- His distinction from Marat and contrast with Robespierre's rise are explicit.

## Relationships

- [[CommitteeOfPublicSafety]] - institution he initially dominated.
- [[MaximilienRobespierre]] - successor figure favored by the more uncompromising climate.
- [[JeanPaulMarat]] - fellow radical whose blood rhetoric Danton considered excessive.
- [[EmergencyInstitutionsToTerror]] - process in which Danton's emergency leadership participated.
- [[RevolutionaryTerrorPolitics]] - political environment that made his pragmatism costly.
