## Overview

Panfilo de Narvaez was the commander sent by Diego Velazquez from Cuba to arrest [[HernanCortes|Hernan Cortes]], whose larger force was defeated and absorbed into Cortes's expedition.

## Current Profile

Narvaez arrived on the Gulf coast with roughly a thousand men and established himself at Zempoala, giving him a large numerical advantage over Cortes. Cortes undermined that advantage by sending gold and promises into his camp, then attacked at night. Narvaez was wounded in the eye and captured, after which most of his men joined Cortes.

His defeat is strategically double-edged. It demonstrates Cortes's boldness and Narvaez's inability to preserve loyalty, but the reinforcements that resulted returned to a Tenochtitlan already transformed by the [[ToxcatlMassacre|Toxcatl massacre]] and siege.

## Key Characteristics

- Velazquez's enforcement commander sent to end Cortes's unauthorized campaign.
- Leader with a strong numerical advantage but vulnerable internal loyalty.
- Opponent weakened through bribery and surprise before direct defeat.
- Captive whose troops and weapons substantially enlarged Cortes's force.
- Indirect contributor to the force trapped in Tenochtitlan after the uprising.

## Qualifications

This is a narrow campaign profile rather than a full biography. Troop numbers, the loyalty shift's exact scale, individual motives, and remembered battlefield details remain source-scoped. The episode explains his defeat primarily through Cortes's actions and does not independently reconstruct Narvaez's planning or command decisions.

## What Changed

- Established Narvaez as the rival whose defeat enlarged Cortes's expedition immediately before its urban entrapment.
- Connected numerical superiority to the fragility of bought or divided loyalty.
