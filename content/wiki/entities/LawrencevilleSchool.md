# The Lawrenceville School

## Overview

The Lawrenceville School is the private school where Malcolm Gladwell asked students to compare their speech-and-election student-government system with selection by lottery.

## Current Profile

The episode uses Lawrenceville as a skeptical test site rather than as evidence that a lottery was implemented. Students defended public speaking, sociability, readiness, voter control, and earned authority, while also recognizing that campaign performance may privilege confidence and visibility. Summer's proposal—an anonymous qualification stage followed by a lottery—became the workshop's main compromise, and the group's average satisfaction score of 5.3 out of 10 weakened confidence in the status quo without resolving the design question.

## Key Characteristics

- Its student leaders were selected through speeches and open elections in the episode.
- Students articulated competence and legitimacy objections to unrestricted random selection.
- The discussion surfaced charisma and visibility as both plausible skills and possible biases.
- The workshop produced a threshold-then-lottery compromise rather than an adopted reform.
- Students extended the same reasoning to selective college admissions.

### Extension beyond student government
- [[the-powerball-revolution-the-powerball-revolution]] reports substantial student interest in considering a qualified admissions lottery.

## Qualifications

The source records a facilitated discussion, not an institutional decision, representative survey, or outcome evaluation. The episode foregrounds students who fit its argument and does not establish whether Lawrenceville later changed its constitution or selection process.

## What Changed

- Established Lawrenceville's role as the episode's test case for competence, legitimacy, and hybrid lottery design.
- Kept discussion outcomes separate from implemented policy.
