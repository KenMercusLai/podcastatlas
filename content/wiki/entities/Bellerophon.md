# Bellerophon

## Overview

Bellerophon is the Greek heroic rider whose combat with the Chimera supplies an important classical visual parallel for later mounted images of [[StGeorge|St George]] spearing a serpent or dragon.

## Current Profile

The episode uses Bellerophon riding Pegasus against the Chimera to explain why George's horseman image need not originate wholly inside the late medieval dragon narrative. Older mounted-monster combat could supply visual grammar that Christian artists and audiences reworked around a saint.

The comparison supports [[DragonSlayerMotifLayering]], not a claim that Bellerophon simply became George. Classical imagery, Christian rider figures, serpent symbolism, saint legends, and medieval conversion narrative remain distinguishable layers.

## Key Characteristics

- Greek mythic hero associated with Pegasus.
- Combatant against the Chimera.
- Classical precedent for a mounted hero attacking a monstrous enemy.
- Comparative visual source rather than a verified one-to-one origin for St George.

## Evidence

- Horseman parallel: [[462-st-george-dragon-slayer-glt5334319363]] connects George's mounted spear image partly to Bellerophon riding Pegasus against the Chimera.
- Layered transmission: [[462-st-george-dragon-slayer-glt5334319363]] places the classical comparison beside Christian serpent-slaying images and later narrative traditions.

## Qualifications

The episode offers an iconographic and mythic relationship, not proof of a single direct transmission route. Similar mounted-combat imagery can be inherited, recombined, or independently made useful in different religious settings.

## What Changed

- Created Bellerophon as a bounded classical comparison for the St George iconographic tradition.
