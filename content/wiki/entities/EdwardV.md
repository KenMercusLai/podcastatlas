# Edward V

## Overview

Edward V is the child of exile born in Westminster sanctuary who became Edward IV's carefully educated heir, then the twelve-year-old king whose seizure, delegitimation, and disappearance made him the central prince in the Tower.

## Current Profile

Edward was born in sanctuary during his father's 1470 exile, then became Prince of Wales and head of the Council of Wales and the Marches while based at Ludlow. Edward IV prescribed a disciplined household of learning, sport, and moral restraint under Anthony Woodville. That preparation could not overcome minority dependence: Richard of Gloucester and Buckingham intercepted his party after accession, arrested his protectors, postponed his coronation, and lodged him in the Tower.

## Key Characteristics

- Lawful child successor whose title depended on adults controlling access, council, forces, and coronation.
- Prince raised at Ludlow under a deliberate program of learning, physical training, household discipline, and regional preparation.
- Loyal to Anthony Woodville when Richard accused the governor of treason and moral corruption.
- Isolated in the Tower after the arrests of his maternal protectors and repeated coronation delays.
- Delegitimized with his brother before Richard III's coronation.
- Disappeared from public view and was probably killed, although no conclusive physical proof identifies his fate.

## Evidence

- Birth and formation: [[138-the-princes-in-the-tower-part-1-glt6274021473]] places Edward's birth in sanctuary and describes his offices, Ludlow base, household rules, and guardianship by Anthony Woodville.
- Heir and custody: both [[138-the-princes-in-the-tower-part-1-glt6274021473]] and [[139-the-princes-in-the-tower-part-2-glt4685691538]] place Edward at Ludlow; Part 2 records the Stony Stratford interception and removal from Woodville protection.
- Isolation and delegitimation: [[139-the-princes-in-the-tower-part-2-glt4685691538]] traces the postponed coronation, Tower lodging, invalid-marriage campaign, and declaration of illegitimacy.
- Disappearance: [[139-the-princes-in-the-tower-part-2-glt4685691538]] reports the princes' declining visibility and concludes that murder on Richard's orders is the strongest explanation.

## Qualifications

The sources cannot establish the date, method, agents, or physical remains of Edward's death. The educational program describes Edward IV's intention more clearly than its results, and Anthony's guardianship may have been both sincere formation and factional positioning. Later pretenders and survival theories show the political value of uncertainty, not proof that Edward survived.

## What Changed

- Added Edward's sanctuary birth, Ludlow offices, and planned education as the formation preceding his brief kingship.
- Clarified that preparation for rule could not solve the structural dependence of a child successor.
