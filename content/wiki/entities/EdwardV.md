# Edward V

## Overview

Edward V is the twelve-year-old heir of Edward IV whose seizure, isolation, delegitimation, and disappearance turned a lawful succession into Richard III's usurpation and the central princes-in-the-Tower mystery.

## Current Profile

Edward began the crisis at Ludlow under Anthony Woodville's protection. Richard of Gloucester and Buckingham intercepted his party, arrested his protectors, postponed his coronation, and lodged him in the Tower. His defense of Rivers shows limited but real judgment, yet his age and physical custody left him unable to convert legal title into governing control.

## Key Characteristics

- Lawful child successor whose title depended on adults controlling access, council, forces, and coronation.
- Loyal to Anthony Woodville when Richard accused the governor of treason and moral corruption.
- Isolated in the Tower after the arrests of his maternal protectors and repeated coronation delays.
- Delegitimized with his brother before Richard III's coronation.
- Disappeared from public view and was probably killed, although no conclusive physical proof identifies his fate.

## Evidence

- Heir and custody: [[139-the-princes-in-the-tower-part-2-glt4685691538]] places Edward at Ludlow, records the Stony Stratford interception, and follows his removal from Woodville protection.
- Isolation and delegitimation: [[139-the-princes-in-the-tower-part-2-glt4685691538]] traces the postponed coronation, Tower lodging, invalid-marriage campaign, and declaration of illegitimacy.
- Disappearance: [[139-the-princes-in-the-tower-part-2-glt4685691538]] reports the princes' declining visibility and concludes that murder on Richard's orders is the strongest explanation.

## Qualifications

The episode cannot establish the date, method, agents, or physical remains of Edward's death. Later pretenders and survival theories show the political value of uncertainty, not proof that he survived.

## What Changed

- Created a profile distinguishing Edward's legal kingship from the custody and recognition needed to exercise it.
