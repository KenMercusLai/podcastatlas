# Czech Republic / 捷克

## Overview

The Czech Republic, also called Czechia, enters the wiki as a Central European country whose identity is shaped by regional composition, Prague's location between simplified East-West labels, twentieth-century political rupture, literature, and older Bohemian national memory.

## Current Profile

Czech identity here spans political rupture, literature, regional geography, and national memory. Prague, 1968, repression, exile, and the [[VelvetRevolution|1989 democratic turn]] explain the political label attached to [[MilanKundera|Milan Kundera]], while *Slowness* uses geographical confusion and rewritten memory to show how abstract sympathy can erase concrete place and personhood.

The naming discussion distinguishes “Czechia” from “the Czech Republic” and explains why Bohemia alone cannot name a country also including Moravia and part of Silesia. [[WenceslasOfBohemia|Wenceslas]] adds a deeper national-memory layer: his Prague statue and return legends make the medieval ruler a symbol of protection in crisis. These strands are supplied respectively by [[12-man-dang-milan-kundela-yi-ju-zhengjing-meiyou-549335675]] and [[12-days-good-king-wenceslas-and-the-first-gilbert-sullivan-glt4255677286]].

## Key Characteristics

- Central European country whose location and history resist a generic “Eastern Europe” label.
- State also known in English by the short-form name Czechia.
- Country composed of historical regions not reducible to Bohemia alone.
- Political-cultural setting shaped by 1968, exile, 1989, and arguments over memory.
- National memory extending from modern rupture back to Wenceslas and medieval Bohemia.

## Qualifications

Both sources use the country selectively: one through a literary discussion and the other through a short festive history segment. Their comments do not constitute a full political, linguistic, regional, or constitutional history. The hosts' preference for older names such as Bohemia is aesthetic and explicitly insufficient as modern national nomenclature.
