# Madame Roland

## Overview

Madame Roland was an influential Girondin salon organizer who shaped revolutionary action while publicly defending women's indirect, domestic political role.

## Current Profile

The episode uses Roland to show both the reach and fragility of [[InformalFemalePoliticalInfluence]]. She pressed her minister husband toward stronger revolutionary action and supported recruiting fédérés, yet argued that women should inspire politics without visibly participating. Her enemies then portrayed her as a corrupting hidden favorite, repeating the same misogynistic grammar she had applied to Marie Antoinette.

## Key Characteristics

- She organized a politically influential Girondin salon.
- She is portrayed as more forceful and dynamic than her husband.
- She supported recruiting fédérés and suspending the king.
- She defended female influence exercised privately rather than open female citizenship.
- Her opponents likened her to royal mistresses and blamed her as a hidden manipulator.

## Qualifications

- The episode supplies a focused political portrait rather than a complete biography or account of Girondin salons.
- Her indirect-politics position should not be generalized to all women or all Girondins.
- The source highlights the irony in her treatment of Marie Antoinette and her own later vilification without claiming the two women's political positions were identical.
