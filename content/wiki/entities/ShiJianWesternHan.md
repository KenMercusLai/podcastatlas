## Overview

石建 / Shi Jian is the elder son of [[ShiFenWesternHan|石奋]], presented as a Western Han courtier whose separation of private candor from public reticence turns inherited caution into imperial trust.

## Current Profile

The episode says [[EmpressDowagerDouWesternHan|窦太后]] appoints Shi Jian 郎中令 because Shi Fen's household practices restraint rather than merely praising ritual. In service to [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]], Shi Jian reportedly waits until no one else is present before offering full advice. Before assembled officials he instead appears slow and inarticulate.

That contrast is not treated as lack of judgment. The source presents it as audience management: private access allows counsel without turning advice into public performance or challenge, while visible restraint reduces political exposure and earns the emperor's confidence.

## Key Characteristics

- Elder son of Shi Fen and member of the household behind the “万石君” label.
- Appointed 郎中令 in the episode's account.
- Offers candid and detailed advice when alone with the ruler.
- Appears reticent or inarticulate before the assembled court.
- Gains imperial trust through controlled choice of audience and setting.

## Qualifications

The page is bounded to one short episode and does not reconstruct Shi Jian's complete offices, advice, policy effect, or later life. Public reticence may reflect strategy, temperament, etiquette, or retrospective moral framing; the source does not let those explanations be separated. Imperial trust is reported but not measured through independent decisions or outcomes.
