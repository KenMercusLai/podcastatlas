# Hamad bin Khalifa Al Thani

## Overview

Hamad bin Khalifa Al Thani is the Qatari ruler whom the episode places at the center of the country's effort to convert hydrocarbon wealth into strategic autonomy after taking power in 1995.

## Current Profile

The episode describes Hamad as a Sandhurst-trained military figure who deposed his father, Khalifa bin Hamad, in a bloodless palace coup while the latter was abroad. His governing project sought to loosen [[Qatar]]'s dependence on [[SaudiArabia]] by building institutions and relationships that made the state useful, visible, and costly to isolate.

That project included [[AlJazeera]], Education City, Doha's aviation role, sovereign investment, liquefied-natural-gas exports, diplomacy across rival camps, and construction of Al Udeid Air Base for American and allied forces. The profile is therefore less a full biography than an account of state strategy under his rule.

## Key Characteristics

- Military-trained ruler who took power in a 1995 intra-family coup.
- Advocate of a Qatari foreign policy less subordinate to Saudi Arabia.
- Builder of media, education, aviation, investment, energy, and military-security leverage.
- Practitioner of diplomatic flexibility across rival regional relationships.
- Leader whose internationalization strategy created both protection and reputational exposure.

## Qualifications

This is a source-bounded strategic profile rather than a complete reign history. The episode compresses decision-making across Hamad, other members of the ruling family, state institutions, commercial organizations, and foreign partners. It does not independently establish his motives, the internal politics of the 1995 coup, or the relative causal weight of each policy.
