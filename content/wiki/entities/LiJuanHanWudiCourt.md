## Overview

丽娟 / Li Juan is a palace favorite associated with [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] in later literary anecdotes cited by [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-365-2-wan-de-hua-jiemi-shishang-zuihaose-de-huangdi-lu3xsuqwx0ibjdk6t2glls389vkd|Hanji 365-2]]. Her current wiki profile is deliberately source-bounded: it records how the episode presents her while distinguishing marvel-tale imagery from a verified Western Han biography.

## Current Profile

The episode introduces Li Juan through 《汉武洞冥记》 as a fourteen-year-old palace woman of extraordinary beauty and lightness. When she sings 《回风之曲》 at Zhisheng Hall, flowers supposedly fall; Wudi then places her behind protective curtains so dust cannot touch her. A separate amber ornament story adds material luxury and a self-sounding marvel.

The most developed anecdote is attributed through 《琅嬛记》 to 《甲子说林》. While Wudi and Li Juan view roses, he says the flower surpasses a beautiful woman's smile. Li Juan asks whether laughter can be bought, has attendants bring one hundred jin of gold as “买笑钱,” and makes the emperor laugh; the episode connects this exchange to the name “卖笑花.”

The source reads her wit as affectionate intelligence, then emphasizes her weak historical visibility. It says standard history does not mention her and speculates that a palace woman who bore no child carried little dynastic importance. That explanation is plausible only as the episode's interpretation: the present evidence does not establish her full life, political influence, reproductive history, or even the historical reliability of the anecdotes.

## Key Characteristics

- Palace favorite represented primarily through later literary anecdotes rather than a secure administrative or annalistic record.
- Beauty-and-performance figure whose singing is surrounded by falling-flower and dustless-body imagery.
- Luxury figure connected to protective curtains, amber ornament, and a large gold gift.
- Witty intimate in the “卖笑花” exchange, where verbal play becomes reward and shared laughter.
- Example of how a palace woman's memory may depend on marvel literature when she lacks office, heir, or durable family power.

## Qualifications

This profile depends on one short popular-history source summarizing later literary works. It does not establish when the underlying texts were composed, how their versions differ, whether Li Juan was historical, or whether the quoted details preserve Western Han court practice. “Fourteen,” the bodily descriptions, flower miracle, dust protection, sounding amber, one-hundred-jin payment, “卖笑花” etymology, and childlessness remain source-scoped. Literary prominence should not be mistaken for political power, and absence from standard histories should not be explained by childlessness alone without further evidence.
