## Overview

Francis Bacon (1561–1626) was an English statesman and philosopher represented here through his reform of knowledge, his political career, and the unfinished scientific utopia [[NewAtlantis|*New Atlantis*]].

## Current Profile

The episode treats Bacon's intellectual and political roles as inseparable. He criticizes learning organized around inherited authority, formal disputation, and unsupported intuition, then advocates observation, experiment, comparison, exclusion, and induction. Because inquiry is vulnerable to prejudice, habit, and language, method must discipline both evidence collection and inference.

His formula that knowledge becomes power is institutional as well as philosophical. Reliable discovery needs money, facilities, specialized labor, cumulative records, and decisions about what to investigate or disclose. *New Atlantis* turns that program into [[SalomonsHouse|Salomon's House]], where organized science can improve life while acquiring unusual autonomy and authority.

## Key Characteristics

- Early-modern English thinker joining natural philosophy to practical improvement.
- Critic of authority-centered learning and premature generalization.
- Advocate of empirical, experimental, comparative, and inductive inquiry.
- Statesman whose career included service as lord chancellor and ended after bribery charges and impeachment.
- Author of an unfinished utopia centered on organized scientific research.
- Thinker for whom knowledge expands human capacity and therefore raises questions of institutional power.

## Qualifications

This page reflects one podcast's philosophical reading rather than a complete biography or history of science. Dates, offices, bribery proceedings, terminology, authorial intention, and influence require primary or specialist corroboration. Calling Bacon a founder of modern scientific method does not make him its sole originator, and the episode's Silicon Valley comparison is retrospective.

- Connected empirical method to the organizational and governance consequences of knowledge production.
