# Nord Stream

## Overview

Nord Stream is the Baltic Sea gas-pipeline project connecting Russian supply more directly to Germany. In the source account, its strategic significance lies as much in bypassing Ukrainian transit as in delivering gas.

## Current Profile

[[168-oil-conflict-chaos-and-climate-change-glt4572551005]] places Nord Stream inside a longer history of European purchases of Soviet and Russian energy. Whereas the Friendship Pipeline crossed Ukraine and Belarus, the Baltic route reduced Ukraine's transit role and therefore altered revenue, bargaining power, and the security relationship among [[Russia]], [[Germany]], and [[Ukraine]].

The project exemplifies [[EuropeanEnergySecurityDependence]] because an infrastructure choice optimized supply ties while accumulating a route-specific strategic exposure. The 2022 invasion shattered the political assumptions behind that relationship, but the episode notes that immediate dependence persisted after the judgment changed.

## Key Characteristics

- Direct Baltic Sea gas route between Russia and Germany.
- Infrastructure designed in part to reduce reliance on Ukrainian transit.
- Continuation of a longer European-Russian energy relationship rather than an isolated decision.
- Source of asymmetrical leverage involving supplier, buyer, and bypassed transit state.
- Central example of the tension between commercial energy supply and security policy.

## Evidence

Route and leverage:

Historical continuity:
- [[168-oil-conflict-chaos-and-climate-change-glt4572551005]] connects Nord Stream to earlier U.S.-European disputes over the Soviet Friendship Pipeline.

Policy rupture:
- [[168-oil-conflict-chaos-and-climate-change-glt4572551005]] treats Russia's invasion of Ukraine as a break in decades of German energy and foreign policy while noting continued gas purchases at the time of recording.

## Qualifications

The source gives a strategic history rather than a technical, financial, legal, or environmental account of the pipeline system. It was recorded in March 2022 and therefore does not cover subsequent physical disruption, investigations, trade reorientation, or later policy outcomes.

## What Changed

- Established Nord Stream as a route-specific redistribution of energy leverage, not merely a bilateral gas purchase.

## Relationships

- [[Russia]] - supplier whose pipeline strategy sought to reduce dependence on transit states.
- [[Germany]] - principal buyer whose energy policy accumulated strategic exposure.
- [[Ukraine]] - transit state whose route position the project weakened.
- [[EuropeanEnergySecurityDependence]] - broader vulnerability made concrete by the pipeline.
- [[EnergyTransitionGeopolitics]] - transition pressure partly motivated by exposure to imported gas.
