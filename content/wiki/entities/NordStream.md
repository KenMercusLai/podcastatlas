# Nord Stream

## Overview

Nord Stream is the Baltic Sea gas-pipeline project connecting Russian supply more directly to Germany. In the source account, its strategic significance lies as much in bypassing Ukrainian transit as in delivering gas.

## Current Profile

The sources place Nord Stream inside a longer history of European purchases of Soviet and Russian energy and a specific German political lineage. Whereas the Friendship Pipeline crossed Ukraine and Belarus, the Baltic route reduced Ukraine's transit role and therefore altered revenue, bargaining power, and the security relationship among [[Russia]], [[Germany]], and [[Ukraine]]. [[GerhardSchroder]]'s advocacy and later Russian corporate ties connect the project to elite relationships as well as infrastructure, while [[AngelaMerkel]]'s governments inherited the dependence.

The project exemplifies [[EuropeanEnergySecurityDependence]] because an infrastructure choice optimized supply ties while accumulating a route-specific strategic exposure. The 2022 invasion shattered the political assumptions behind that relationship, but the episode notes that immediate dependence persisted after the judgment changed.

## Key Characteristics

- Direct Baltic Sea gas route between Russia and Germany.
- Infrastructure designed in part to reduce reliance on Ukrainian transit.
- Continuation of a longer European-Russian energy relationship rather than an isolated decision.
- Source of asymmetrical leverage involving supplier, buyer, and bypassed transit state.
- Central example of the tension between commercial energy supply and security policy.
- Project associated politically with Schröder, Putin, and the Merkel-era dependency debate.

## Qualifications

The sources give strategic and political histories rather than a technical, financial, legal, or environmental account of the pipeline system. The 2021 episode's forecast of consequential dependence and the March 2022 source predate subsequent physical disruption, investigations, trade reorientation, and later policy outcomes.
