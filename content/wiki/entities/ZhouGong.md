## Overview

周公 / Duke of Zhou is synthesized as a classical sage-regent figure, a model of correctable leadership, and a reusable source of political legitimacy. The new regency comparison also preserves a dispute over whether he merely assisted the young king or temporarily occupied royal position or title.

## Current Profile

The page now separates Zhougong as ethical exemplar from Zhougong as political symbol. Zhouji 49 treats him as a model for correctable leadership: the Guan Shu mistake does not erase sage status because the test is whether fault can be acknowledged and repaired. Hanji 607 shows a more instrumental use. Wang Mang stages or channels a Yuechang white-pheasant tribute and has the event interpreted through the Zhou Chengwang and Zhougong story, where distant people come because a sage has appeared.

This makes Zhougong a reusable but unstable legitimacy template. The same name supports reflective leadership in Mencian argument, lets later actors launder ambition as ancient virtue, and defines the upper moral limit of substitute rule: exercise enough authority to restore order, but return it without converting delegation into usurpation. Episode 34 adds that phrases about royal dress, position, or enthronement have produced competing reconstructions of what Zhougong actually did. The wiki preserves that uncertainty rather than allowing the later loyal-regent image to settle the ancient constitutional question.

## Key Characteristics

- Classical sage-regent associated with early Zhou political virtue.
- Correctable-leadership example in Mencian argument.
- Symbolic precedent whose story can be reused by later rulers and ministers.
- White-pheasant legitimacy template invoked by Wang Mang's court.
- Disputed ancient case whose royal status, timing, and restoration mechanism remain uncertain.

- White-pheasant precedent: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-607-jiemi-wangmang-de-quanli-boyi-lgnxg-zlf5gowjbirazpijvyks78|Hanji 607]] says the Yuechang white-pheasant story is read through the Zhougong precedent of distant people visiting because a sage has appeared.
- Wang Mang comparison: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-607-jiemi-wangmang-de-quanli-boyi-lgnxg-zlf5gowjbirazpijvyks78|Hanji 607]] says ministers use the precedent to praise Wang Mang and propose Anhan Gong status.
- Regency-status dispute: [[34-congxiongyali-zhanshen-dao-daqing-modai-jiepanxia-dongxifang-de-shezhengwang-lkuu3qwavvb1yrc81cxgwwoq5y9g]] collects competing readings in which Zhougong ruled, temporarily took royal position, or remained a regent, and relates later reluctance to admit kingship to the politics of the loyal-sage model.

## Qualifications

The page records how later sources use Zhougong and one episode's account of the ancient status dispute; it does not reconstruct his full biography or resolve early textual history. Hanji 607 leaves the historicity of the Yuechang story, geography, and Wang Mang's instruction chain source-scoped. Episode 34 does not establish when, how, or whether Zhougong formally became king, and its claim about later ideological suppression remains interpretive.

## What Changed

- Added the unresolved distinction between exercising royal power, occupying royal position, and formally claiming kingship.
- Reframed Zhougong as both a loyal-restoration ideal and a politically consequential disputed precedent.
