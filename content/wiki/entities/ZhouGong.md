## Overview

周公 / Duke of Zhou is synthesized as a classical sage-regent figure whose authority can function in two different ways across the wiki. In [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-49-mengzi-yue-wo-jiao-ni-zhiguo-ni-que-ba-wo-dang-huaping-lhy14pbf9thcfdiaulcmf8dwcef8|Zhouji 49]], [[Mengzi|孟子 / Mencius]] uses him to explain that even sages can make understandable mistakes and should correct them. In [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-607-jiemi-wangmang-de-quanli-boyi-lgnxg-zlf5gowjbirazpijvyks78|Hanji 607]], [[WangMang|王莽]] uses the white-pheasant precedent around Zhougong to imply that his own regency resembles sage-era rule.

## Current Profile

The page now separates Zhougong as ethical exemplar from Zhougong as political symbol. Zhouji 49 treats him as a model for correctable leadership: the Guan Shu mistake does not erase sage status because the test is whether fault can be acknowledged and repaired. Hanji 607 shows a more instrumental use. Wang Mang stages or channels a Yuechang white-pheasant tribute and has the event interpreted through the Zhou Chengwang and Zhougong story, where distant people come because a sage has appeared.

This makes Zhougong a reusable legitimacy template. The same name that supports reflective leadership in Mencian argument can be used by later court actors to launder present ambition as continuity with ancient virtue.

## Key Characteristics

- Classical sage-regent associated with early Zhou political virtue.
- Correctable-leadership example in Mencian argument.
- Symbolic precedent whose story can be reused by later rulers and ministers.
- White-pheasant legitimacy template invoked by Wang Mang's court.

## Evidence

- Correctable mistake: [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-49-mengzi-yue-wo-jiao-ni-zhiguo-ni-que-ba-wo-dang-huaping-lhy14pbf9thcfdiaulcmf8dwcef8|Zhouji 49]] says Chen Jia cites Zhougong sending Guan Shu to oversee former Shang lands and Guan Shu later rebelling.
- Mencian correction frame: [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-49-mengzi-yue-wo-jiao-ni-zhiguo-ni-que-ba-wo-dang-huaping-lhy14pbf9thcfdiaulcmf8dwcef8|Zhouji 49]] says Mencius treats the mistake as understandable and shifts judgment toward whether one corrects faults.
- White-pheasant precedent: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-607-jiemi-wangmang-de-quanli-boyi-lgnxg-zlf5gowjbirazpijvyks78|Hanji 607]] says the Yuechang white-pheasant story is read through the Zhougong precedent of distant people visiting because a sage has appeared.
- Wang Mang comparison: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-607-jiemi-wangmang-de-quanli-boyi-lgnxg-zlf5gowjbirazpijvyks78|Hanji 607]] says ministers use the precedent to praise Wang Mang and propose Anhan Gong status.

## Qualifications

The page records how later sources use Zhougong; it does not reconstruct Zhougong's full biography. Hanji 607 supports the political use of the white-pheasant precedent but leaves the historicity of the ancient Yuechang story, the exact geography, and Wang Mang's private instruction chain source-scoped.
