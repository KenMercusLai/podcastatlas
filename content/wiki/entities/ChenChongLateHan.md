## Overview

陈宠 / Chen Chong now spans two legal-temperament layers. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-746-shenmeyang-de-lingdao-zhide-bei-sixintata-zhuisui-lvhbdgfbc69t6sdw92k4dipii-rx|Hanji 746]] presents him as the Zhangdi-era remonstrant who urges reform of inherited harsh official practice, while [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-769-gongwuyuan-gai-zenme-kao-guren-gaosu-ni-ltgxekmkpioy11b2-q2kkj93ja0i|Hanji 769]] later makes his appointment as tingwei under [[EmperorHeOfHan|Emperor He]] a personnel choice that softens court justice.

## Current Profile

Hanji 746 gives Chen Chong an earlier policy voice. The source says Mingdi-era administrative practice remains harsh, with officials tending toward severe judgments and even cruel interrogation. Chen argues that a ruler should not overuse punishment, that reward can err on the side of generosity more safely than punishment can err on severity, and that officials using harshness for private power need correction. [[EmperorZhangOfHan|Zhangdi]] accepts the advice fully.

Hanji 769 records Emperor He appointing Chen Chong, formerly dasinong, as tingwei. The source emphasizes Chen Chong's humane, composed character and says he repeatedly handled difficult cases by citing Confucian classics and following a forgiving approach.

Together the current profile is not only that one appointment can change a post's tone. Chen's legal identity begins earlier as a remonstrance against harsh institutional inheritance and later becomes embodied in judicial office.

## Key Characteristics

- Zhangdi-era remonstrant against inherited harsh administrative and penal practice.
- Treats over-punishment as more dangerous than over-reward because punishment directly harms life and trust.
- Critic of officials who use severity, torture, or public authority for private power.
- Later Emperor-He-era official appointed from dasinong to tingwei.
- Associated with humane bearing, seriousness, and lenient handling of hard legal cases.
- Uses Confucian classical reasoning as an interpretive resource in judicial work.

## Qualifications

This page remains source-bounded and does not reconstruct Chen Chong's full career. Hanji 746 supports a legal-temperament and anti-harshness reading, but it does not prove that all Zhangdi-era legal practice became lenient or that harsh enforcement was never warranted. Hanji 769 supports the later personnel-choice claim under Emperor He.
