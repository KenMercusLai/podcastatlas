# 王同惠 / Wang Tonghui

## Overview

[[WangTonghui|王同惠]] is presented as a field researcher whose work in the Dayaoshan investigation expanded access to household, women's, family, and social-organization evidence.

## Current Profile

Wang participated in fieldwork with [[FeiXiaotong|费孝通]] but should not be reduced to the role of spouse or tragic companion. Her access to homes and especially to women produced evidence that was difficult for a male researcher to obtain, and multiple pieces in the field correspondence were her own work.

Her early accidental death left an unrealized scholarly trajectory. The episode uses that loss not to define her identity but to show how academic histories can obscure women's independent contributions when they organize memory around a prominent partner and a tragedy.

## Key Characteristics

- Conducted substantive field research rather than merely accompanying another scholar.
- Expanded access to women and domestic life in the Dayaoshan field setting.
- Contributed independently authored field correspondence.
- Helped connect family evidence to wider social organization.
- Had a scholarly career cut short by an early accidental death.

## Qualifications

This profile derives from a short retrospective segment rather than Wang's complete writings, archive, or biography. It establishes independent contribution and a gendered access advantage in this field setting, not a complete account of authorship division or a universal rule about fieldwork.

## What Changed

- Created a researcher-centered profile rather than defining Wang through marriage and death.
- Identified gendered field access and independent authorship as substantive methodological contributions.
