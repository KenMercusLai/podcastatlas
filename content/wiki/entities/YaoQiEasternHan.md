## Overview

姚期 / Yao Qi is synthesized as a Guangwu-era founding commander whose current evidence now combines local pacification judgment and court remonstrance. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-683-fangniuwa-liupenzi-buxiang-dang-huangdi-dan-meiyudi-lt7hmrorydsazpkuwby1ntd7t1eh|Hanji 683]] supplies an earlier Wei commandery case: during the Tanxiang bandit threat, Yao Qi handles an internal plot by leniency, shame, proper burial, and restoration rather than immediate collective killing. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-708-jizun-bingshi-weihe-yinfa-baiguan-jingkong-lhfuvjyiiirlaf-ugrufkaspuauq|Hanji 708]] later identifies him as a Yuntai founding general and makes his main court role the warning that [[LiuXiu|Liu Xiu]]'s repeated praise of [[JiZunEasternHan|Ji Zun]] is frightening other officials.

## Current Profile

Hanji 683 puts Yao Qi in Wei commandery while Tanxiang bandits attack Wei and Qinghe. When Li Xiong's younger brother Li Lu plots to welcome the bandits into the city, Yao Qi summons Li Xiong and questions him directly. Li Xiong confesses and says he and his mother deserve death, but Yao Qi does not execute them.

The episode presents the handling as deliberately wider than punishment. Yao Qi tells Li Xiong that if his office is unsatisfactory he can take his mother and go join Li Lu, then sends them out of the city. Li Xiong brings Li Lu to the west gate of Ye; Li Lu is ashamed and kills himself. Yao Qi then arranges a proper burial and restores Li Xiong's office. The result is local stabilization by shame, kinship pressure, and selective restoration rather than blanket purge.

Hanji 708 gives Yao Qi's broader founding profile. He is the son of Guiyang administrator Yao Meng, physically imposing, serious in appearance, and known for loyalty and filiality. After recommendation by [[FengYiEasternHan|Feng Yi]], he joins Liu Xiu and contributes to the pacification of Hebei, the destruction of Wang Lang, campaigns against Tongma and Qingdu groups, and long-term defense of Wei commandery.

His later source-bounded political importance comes after Ji Zun's death. As weiwei, he tells Liu Xiu that constant public lament for Ji Zun makes ministers ashamed and afraid. Liu Xiu accepts the warning and stops repeating the comparison, making Yao Qi a case of loyal remonstrance that protects both ruler and officials from an unnecessary pressure signal.

## Key Characteristics

- Guangwu-era Wei commandery official whose local judgment appears during the Tanxiang bandit threat.
- Leniency-and-shame pacification actor who separates family connection to a plot from indiscriminate punishment.
- Restorative local governor who buries a dead conspirator properly and restores Li Xiong after the plot is neutralized.
- Yuntai Twenty-Eight General in the Guangwu founding coalition.
- Loyal and filial military actor tied to Hebei, Wang Lang, Tongma, Qingdu, and Wei commandery service.
- Weiwei who detects the political effect of Liu Xiu's repeated praise of Ji Zun.
- Remonstrance actor whose warning succeeds because Liu Xiu immediately changes behavior.

## Qualifications

Yao Qi's current page is still source-bounded. Hanji 683 gives a focused local-governance story, not a full commandery administration record or a complete Tanxiang campaign account. Hanji 708 names major career points but does not provide a full campaign chronology or title sequence. The episode summary uses the form 姚琪; this page routes it to 姚期 because the Wei commandery and Guangwu-era context match the established figure. Li Xiong, Li Lu, and the Tanxiang bandit group remain source-scoped here, and the Li Lu name is intentionally not linked to the modern investor page with the same Chinese name.
