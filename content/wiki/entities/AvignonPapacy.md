# 阿维尼翁教廷 / Avignon Papacy

## Overview

The Avignon Papacy is the 14th-century papal residence and political order centered at Avignon, represented in the wiki as both the background to Franciscan-imperial conflict and the institutional setting from which Gregory XI returned to Rome.

## Current Profile

The Avignon residence placed papal authority inside papal-imperial rivalry, Franciscan poverty controversy, French royal influence, Italian diplomacy, inquisitorial accusation, and church division. In the literary case supplied by [[54-meigui-de-mingzi-xia-zhen-yu-jia-zheng-yu-xie-quanshi-yu-guodu-quanshi-638214699]], [[BernardGui|贝尔纳 / Bernard Gui]] can make confession and heresy politically useful because the delegation needs leverage as well as truth. The Catherine biography in [[356-the-blood-drinking-bride-of-christ-glt8496536394]] follows the institutional arc toward Rome: [[CatherineOfSiena|Catherine of Siena]] wrote to [[GregoryXI|Gregory XI]] and traveled to Avignon amid diplomacy; Gregory returned in 1377, but his death was followed by the rival elections of the [[GreatWesternSchism|Great Western Schism]]. Geographic return therefore ended the residence without restoring papal unity.

## Key Characteristics

- Papal residence embedded in French, imperial, Italian, and ecclesiastical politics.
- Context for disputes over poverty, heresy, confession, and institutional authority.
- Political setting in which inquisitorial procedure could produce useful enemies.
- Target of Catherine's advocacy for a papal return to Rome.
- Prehistory to a schism in which an Avignon-backed rival obedience reappeared.

## Evidence

- Political-theological conflict: [[54-meigui-de-mingzi-xia-zhen-yu-jia-zheng-yu-xie-quanshi-yu-guodu-quanshi-638214699]] connects Avignon to imperial negotiation, Franciscan poverty, French influence, and Bernard Gui's prosecution.
- Return to Rome: [[356-the-blood-drinking-bride-of-christ-glt8496536394]] describes Catherine's letters and mission, Gregory XI's January 1377 return, and the disputed succession after his death.
- Institutional afterlife: [[54-meigui-de-mingzi-xia-zhen-yu-jia-zheng-yu-xie-quanshi-yu-guodu-quanshi-638214699]] anticipates later church division, while [[356-the-blood-drinking-bride-of-christ-glt8496536394]] names the opening rival-popes crisis directly.

## Qualifications

The current evidence consists of one literary-discussion episode and one biographical popular-history episode, not a complete institutional history. The first uses a fictional abbey plot to explain historical politics; the second foregrounds Catherine and may magnify her causal role in Gregory's decision. Neither source fully covers administration, canon law, finance, rival obediences, or the schism's resolution.
