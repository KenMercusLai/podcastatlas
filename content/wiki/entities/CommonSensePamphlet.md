# Common Sense (pamphlet)

## Overview

*Common Sense* is [[ThomasPaine|Thomas Paine]]'s January 1776 pamphlet arguing openly for American independence and republican government.

## Current Profile

The episode presents the pamphlet as mass political persuasion: subsidized, widely circulated across the thirteen colonies, written in accessible and memorable prose, and radical enough to attack monarchy rather than merely dispute policy. Its importance lies in converting a constitutional and military crisis into a universal moral cause, although [[JohnAdams|John Adams]] regarded its argument as dangerously abstract.

## Key Characteristics

- Published in January 1776 during the collapse of British authority in the colonies.
- Made explicit cases for both independence and republicanism.
- Used accessible, slogan-like prose suited to wide circulation.
- Attacked monarchy while drawing on older British radical traditions.
- Framed the American cause as relevant to all humanity.
- Divided supporters of independence over how radical the new political order should be.

## Evidence

- Circulation and purpose: [[349-the-birth-of-the-united-states-part-3-glt9804967639]] describes the pamphlet as a subsidized bestseller circulated across the thirteen colonies.
- Political argument: [[349-the-birth-of-the-united-states-part-3-glt9804967639]] says it openly advocated independence and republicanism rather than only redress within the empire.
- Universal appeal: [[349-the-birth-of-the-united-states-part-3-glt9804967639]] highlights Paine's claim that America's cause belonged to all mankind.
- Internal dispute: [[349-the-birth-of-the-united-states-part-3-glt9804967639]] reports John Adams's hostility to the pamphlet's reasoning and abstraction despite his support for independence.

## Qualifications

The source establishes political reach and rhetorical force but does not independently quantify readership, reconstruct reception in every colony, or settle how much the pamphlet changed opinions rather than expressed an accelerating movement.

## What Changed

- Created the pamphlet as the episode's bridge between imperial crisis and explicit republican independence.
