## Overview

The Zhang-era Ma family is the outer-relative household whose early honor is restrained by [[EmpressMaMingdeEasternHan|Empress Ma]] while she lives and whose protection weakens after she dies. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-750-jiemi-donghan-wanmei-nvxing-beihou-manshi-xisheng-lu-96lbdh7r0tsmiopbqqrj0q1gt|Hanji 750]] shows the family receiving marquisates but leaving office, while [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-752-shiqu-kaoshan-de-ren-gai-ruhe-chushi-lt0qb7alyh0ipf6mk8hvz92cs297|Hanji 752]] uses the family as the main example of why formerly protected people must lower display, discipline guests and juniors, and stop acting as though old patronage still holds.

## Current Profile

The family appears through several members rather than a single policy program. Hanji 750 gives the upstream restraint layer: [[EmperorZhangOfHan|Zhangdi]] grants marquisates to Ma Liao, Ma Fang, and Ma Guang because he treats Empress Ma as his mother and the Ma clan as maternal kin. Empress Ma worries that external honors will damage the family's clean reputation. The brothers try to decline the titles, then accept the titles while resigning their offices and leaving the court center as special-ranking nobles.

Hanji 752 shows the later failure to sustain that discipline after Empress Ma dies. [[MaLiaoEasternHan|Ma Liao]] is cautious and sincere but fails to restrain younger relatives. Ma Fang and Ma Guang build large residences, keep hundreds of guests, accumulate horses, and are associated with exactions from Qiang and Hu groups. Ma Yu, Ma Liao's son, becomes part of the later consequence sequence after Ma-family demotion.

The political arc is fast once the household loses protection. [[YangZhongEasternHan|Yang Zhong]] warns that the household's public standing makes irresponsible behavior dangerous. Zhangdi dislikes the display and places restrictions on the family. Once the emperor's attitude shifts, guests withdraw, officials impeach Ma Fang and Ma Guang, and the family is ordered back to its fiefs, with Ma Guang retained only because he is comparatively more cautious.

## Key Characteristics

- Outer-relative household honored because Zhangdi recognizes Empress Ma as his mother.
- Family whose first source layer tries to separate noble title from direct court office.
- Formerly protected household whose safety declines after Empress Ma's death.
- Family whose wealth, guests, residences, horses, and frontier exactions become political liabilities.
- Household where Ma Liao's personal caution does not prevent younger relatives' overreach.
- Case showing how guests and dependents can disappear when imperial favor shifts.
- Contrast family to the rising Dou household in the same Zhangdi-era court.

## Qualifications

This page aggregates the Ma household because the sources develop a family-risk pattern more than separate full biographies for Ma Fang, Ma Guang, or Ma Yu. Those individual names remain source-scoped until additional source notes give them independent arcs. Hanji 750 shows genuine early restraint, so Hanji 752 should be read as a later failure of continuity rather than proof that the family was always openly reckless.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 750 as the upstream honor-with-restraint layer before Empress Ma's death.
- Reframed the Hanji 752 decline as failure to sustain an earlier office-resignation discipline.
