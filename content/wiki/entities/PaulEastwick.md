# Paul Eastwick

## Overview
Paul Eastwick is the relationship psychologist interviewed in [[science-of-attraction-compatibility-romance-dr-paul-eastwick-scim9984287085|Science of Attraction, Compatibility & Romance | Dr. Paul Eastwick]]. In the source, [[AndrewHuberman]] introduces him as a UC Davis psychology professor whose work covers attraction, mate selection, dating, and relationship maintenance.

## Current Profile
The bounded evidence presents Eastwick as a researcher who resists one-factor stories about romance. He treats marketplace ranking, evolutionary preference narratives, dating-app filters, gender differences, income, age, similarity, attachment, sex, and social support as context-sensitive variables rather than universal scripts.

His strongest contribution to the wiki is the distinction between first-impression consensus and later pair-specific attraction. In app or stranger settings, visible desirability and peer attention can matter; with repeated contact, private moments, responsiveness, shared stories, and perceived similarity can make attraction diverge from group consensus. Eastwick's practical stance therefore favors recurring social contexts over purely app-based or interview-like dating.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames attraction as partly market-like in first-impression contexts but increasingly pair-specific with repeated interaction.
- Separates stated preferences from face-to-face attraction, especially around gendered claims about status, ambition, attractiveness, age, and similarity.
- Treats social support, attachment orientation, and relationship narratives as changeable relationship processes rather than fixed traits.
- Uses dating apps as a bounded case where unequal attention and swipe behavior can amplify marketplace dynamics.
- Recommends recurring groups and shared activities because they reveal behavior, support, and compatibility signals that trait checklists miss.
