## Overview

袁安 / Yuan An now begins in the wiki with a Mingdi-era justice-redress layer before his Zhangdi and He-era frontier and anti-[[DouXianEasternHan|Dou Xian]] profile. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-740-rang-ni-tihuguanding-de-zhichang-zhihui-zhichang-bibei-lih5b-ighkyoycawm5c-dbrnuiew|Hanji 740]] places him as the incoming Chu commandery governor who reviews unsupported cases from the [[LiuYingChuKingEasternHan|Liu Ying]] prison crisis and releases more than four hundred households. Hanji 755-766 then show him as the senior Eastern Han minister who keeps frontier credibility and anti-Dou resistance institutionally visible.

## Current Profile

Hanji 740 supplies the earliest current layer. Before entering office as Chu commandery governor, Yuan An reviews the Chu-prison files and identifies cases without clear evidence. Subordinates warn him that changing the case may be dangerous, but he says he alone will bear responsibility if the decision is wrong. [[EmperorMingOfHan|Mingdi]] approves, and more than four hundred households are released. This makes Yuan's first current profile a legal-redress official who combines evidence review with personal responsibility for subordinates.

Hanji 755 then places Yuan in a frontier-restraint debate. After [[SouthernXiongnu|Southern Xiongnu]] troops seize [[NorthernXiongnuLateHan|Northern Xiongnu]] captives and livestock, [[MengYunEasternHan|Meng Yun]] recommends restitution to prevent Northern Xiongnu from seeing Han as deceptive. Yuan and [[HuanYuEasternHan|Huan Yu]] support returning the gains, while [[ZhengHongEasternHan|Zheng Hong]] and [[FifthLunEasternHan|Fifth Lun]] oppose it. This makes Yuan's early policy pattern credibility-preserving before his later better-known objections to Dou Xian.

Hanji 760 supplies the first anti-Dou remonstrance layer. Three Excellencies and Nine Ministers object that Northern Xiongnu has not invaded the frontier and that an unprovoked expedition is not a state-minded policy. [[SongYouEasternHan|Song You]] becomes afraid and does not sign, but Yuan and [[RenKuiEasternHan|Ren Kui]] keep arguing, repeatedly submitting objections even when other officials worry for them.

Hanji 762 presents Yuan as a legal and historical-precedent remonstrator. He argues that [[DouJingEasternHan|Dou Jing]]'s unauthorized recall of border cavalry deserves capital treatment and that the Sili colonel and Henan Yin should be punished for concealing outer-relative wrongdoing. When open memorials do not work, Yuan sends a sealed memorial that reframes restraint of relatives as protection and proposes consulting [[DouGuiEasternHan|Dou Gui]] as a restrained Dou-family counterexample.

Hanji 764 then presents Yuan as a reputationally protected counterweight inside a dangerous court. Together with Ren Kui, he impeaches more than forty officials tied to Dou Xian's recommendation and extraction network. Dou's brothers hate the two ministers, but the source says they fear Yuan and Ren's public standing.

Hanji 765 supplies the policy substance behind the later [[YuChuNorthernXiongnu|Yu Chu]] dispute. Yuan argues that recognizing Yu Chu as Northern Xiongnu chanyu would betray the older Southern Xiongnu commitment, damage Han credibility with frontier groups, anger [[Wuhuan|Wuhuan]] and [[Xianbei|Xianbei]], and add heavy state cost. His opposition is therefore not only anti-Dou factional posture; it combines precedent, frontier credit, intergroup resentment, and public finance.

Hanji 766 keeps Yuan in the pre-coup visibility layer. He and Ren Kui continue opposing Dou's Northern Xiongnu arrangement after Song You retreats, and [[ZhouRongEasternHan|Zhou Rong]] drafts anti-Dou memorials through Yuan's office. Yuan does not personally overthrow the Dou bloc. His death removes one open ministerial counterweight, after which [[DingHongEasternHan|Ding Hong]] takes up the warning function through omen-coded remonstrance.

## Key Characteristics

- Evidence-review official whose earliest current layer releases unsupported Chu-prison cases while taking responsibility himself.
- Frontier-credibility minister who supports restitution to protect Han trustworthiness.
- Anti-Dou dissenter whose campaign objections continue after other high officials retreat.
- Impeacher of Dou Jing's unlawful frontier-troop mobilization and household abuse.
- Historical-allusive memorialist who frames restraint of favored relatives as protection.
- Reputation-protected bureaucratic counterweight against Dou-linked office networks.
- Frontier-policy thinker who treats title recognition as a commitment, alliance, and cost problem.

## Qualifications

Yuan An is not depicted here as part of Emperor He's secret operational planning with Zheng Zhong and Liu Qing. His importance lies in evidence review, personal responsibility for redress, open remonstrance, repeated pre-campaign objection, impeachment, sealed historical warning, policy argument, and bureaucratic resistance. Hanji 755 makes his early frontier stance pro-restitution, while Hanji 765 later has him oppose a different Northern Xiongnu arrangement; those positions are compatible because the first protects a reconciliation commitment by returning seized goods, whereas the second rejects creating a new claimant arrangement that could damage older commitments and finances. Hanji 762 also notes that Dou Xian learns of Yuan's sealed memorial before the emperor sees it, so the page treats the episode as evidence of both Yuan's warning and the vulnerability of confidential remonstrance under outer-relative power.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 740 as Yuan's earliest current layer: Chu-prison case review, personal liability, and release of more than four hundred households.
- Reframed Yuan's public identity as evidence-based redress before his later anti-Dou resistance.
- Preserved Hanji 755-766 as the main frontier credibility, remonstrance, impeachment, and pre-coup visibility sequence.
