# Matthew MacDougall

## Overview
Matthew MacDougall is presented in the bounded source as a neurosurgeon and then head neurosurgeon at [[Neuralink]], interviewed by [[AndrewHuberman]] about clinical neurosurgery and implantable neural interfaces.

## Current Profile
MacDougall’s episode role is to connect surgical constraints with Neuralink’s engineering roadmap. He emphasizes an initial assistive goal—decoding motor-cortex intention into computer control for people with severe paralysis—before more difficult restoration or augmentation goals. He also explains why very fine cortical electrodes motivate [[RoboticNeuralElectrodeInsertion]], why users and software must learn together, and why surgical bandwidth must be weighed against adoption burden through [[NeuralInterfaceBitrateAdoptionTradeoff]].

## Key Characteristics
- Frames [[BrainComputerInterface]] development around near-term assistive computer access before able-bodied augmentation.
- Describes robotics as a precision tool within a surgeon-led workflow rather than a complete replacement for the neurosurgeon.
- Separates localized implant functions from claims of broad, general-purpose [[Neuroplasticity]] enhancement.
- Uses clinical lesions and surgical cases to explain the brain through selective functional loss.
- Treats silent communication, brain–spinal bypass, and AI-linked cognition as technically imaginable but later-stage possibilities.

## Evidence
- Assistive roadmap - [[neuralink-technologies-to-enhance-human-brains-dr-matthew-macdougall-scim4147376395]] attributes to MacDougall the initial goal of cursor and keyboard control from motor intention for people with severe spinal-cord injury.
- Surgical workflow - [[neuralink-technologies-to-enhance-human-brains-dr-matthew-macdougall-scim4147376395]] explains fine-electrode placement, surface-vessel avoidance, and the continuing role of a neurosurgeon.
- Technology boundaries - [[neuralink-technologies-to-enhance-human-brains-dr-matthew-macdougall-scim4147376395]] distinguishes focused electrical interfaces from broad pharmacological modulation and near-term repair from speculative augmentation.
