# Hong Kong Chief Executive / 香港行政長官

## Overview
Hong Kong Chief Executive / 香港行政長官 appears in the Tung Chee-hwa source as the post-1997 office designed to lead Hong Kong under executive-led government.

## Current Profile
The source uses [[TungCheeHwa|董建華]]'s tenure to frame the chief executive office as structurally exposed. The office is meant to carry executive leadership after the handover, but the episode argues that without universal-suffrage legitimacy, it faced weak public recognition when economic shock, housing policy, legal interpretation, public-health crisis, and national-security legislation converged.

The office therefore becomes a node for [[HongKongChiefExecutiveLegitimacy]] rather than just a biography slot for Tung. Its early practice is tied to [[SmallCircleElectionLegitimacy]], [[BusinessEliteRuleHongKong]], and [[PostHandoverHongKongGovernance]].

## Key Characteristics
- Post-handover top executive office in Hong Kong.
- Designed around executive-led governance rather than parliamentary-party accountability.
- Initially filled through a small-circle election rather than universal suffrage.
- Depended on Beijing trust, business-elite support, and public acceptance under crisis.
- Became a visible site where personal competence and institutional design were hard to separate.

## Evidence
- Office origin through Tung: [[dong-jianhua-shishi-aiguozhe-yuanlao-de-xianggang-rensheng-7d743a8515cf65334add1a55cd20148e]] identifies Tung as Hong Kong's first chief executive.
- Selection mechanism: [[dong-jianhua-shishi-aiguozhe-yuanlao-de-xianggang-rensheng-7d743a8515cf65334add1a55cd20148e]] describes Tung's 1996 small-circle victory with 320 votes.
- Structural exposure: [[dong-jianhua-shishi-aiguozhe-yuanlao-de-xianggang-rensheng-7d743a8515cf65334add1a55cd20148e]] contrasts executive-led design with lack of universal-suffrage mandate and reliance on consultation.
