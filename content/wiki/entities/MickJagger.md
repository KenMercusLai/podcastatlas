# Mick Jagger

## Overview

Mick Jagger is the singer and public face of [[TheRollingStones|the Rolling Stones]], presented here as a target of the [[RedlandsDrugCase|Redlands prosecution]], an architect of the band's shift away from [[BrianJones]], and Hyde Park's defining performer.

## Current Profile

Jagger's celebrity made an amphetamine-possession case nationally symbolic. His three-month sentence was criticized by *The Times* and removed on appeal although the conviction stood. His television insistence that he was not a rebel and respected the wartime generation complicated his assigned public role.

Jagger and [[KeithRichards]] became the band's creative and organizational center as Jones declined. At Hyde Park, his white costume, makeup, and nervous reading from Shelley's *Adonais* turned an imperfect concert into a mass memorial. His 1976 remark that the Stones were overrated is treated as possible awareness that youth rebellion could harden into parody.

## Key Characteristics

- His persona attracted meanings larger than the conduct proved in court.
- He performed rebellion while distancing himself from a simple rebel identity.
- His partnership with Richards displaced Jones from the band's center.
- Theatrical dress and poetry helped turn Hyde Park into public ritual.
- Later self-criticism complicates triumphalist band mythology.
