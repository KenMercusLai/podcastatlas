# Mick Jagger

## Overview

Mick Jagger is the singer and public face of [[TheRollingStones|the Rolling Stones]], whose shared blues interests with [[KeithRichards]] became a songwriting and leadership partnership that displaced founder [[BrianJones]].

## Current Profile

Jagger came from a middle-class Dartford family, excelled at school, entered the London School of Economics, and reconnected with Richards through blues records in 1961. He left the LSE in 1963 as the band became viable. Lennon-McCartney's help and the later Jagger-Richards original-song run shifted the group from Jones's blues-purist model toward commercial rock.

His celebrity later made an amphetamine-possession case nationally symbolic. His sentence was removed on appeal although the conviction stood, and his insistence that he was not simply a rebel complicated the [[ManufacturedRebellionBranding|public role]] built around him. At Hyde Park, costume, makeup, and Shelley's *Adonais* turned an imperfect concert into mass memorial; his later self-criticism complicates triumphalist mythology.

## Key Characteristics

- Blues collecting joined him to Richards before fame.
- Leaving the LSE marked commitment to the band's commercial opportunity.
- His partnership with Richards displaced Jones from the creative center.
- His persona attracted meanings larger than the conduct proved in court.
- He performed rebellion while distancing himself from a simple rebel identity.
- Theatrical performance turned Hyde Park into public ritual.
