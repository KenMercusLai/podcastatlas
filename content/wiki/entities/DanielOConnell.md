# Daniel O'Connell

## Overview

Daniel O'Connell was an Irish barrister and mass political organizer whose Catholic Association campaign helped force Catholic emancipation in 1829.

## Current Profile

The episode presents O'Connell, known as “the Liberator,” as the first major constitutional answer to the Act of Union's broken emancipation expectation. He organized through counties, converted Catholic grievance into disciplined electoral power, and won the County Clare election even though Catholics could not yet sit in Parliament.

That victory made exclusion politically difficult to sustain. The Duke of Wellington's government passed emancipation despite George III's earlier oath-based opposition, establishing a pattern in which Irish mass organization used the institutions of the United Kingdom to force constitutional reform.

## Key Characteristics

- Kerry-born barrister and leading Irish politician of the early nineteenth century.
- Founder of the Catholic Association in 1823.
- Organizer who converted county networks and mass participation into parliamentary pressure.
- County Clare victor whose election exposed the instability of Catholic exclusion.
- Constitutional nationalist whose success preceded the later Home Rule strategy.
