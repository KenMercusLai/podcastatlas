# Franz von Papen

## Overview

Franz von Papen was a conservative German politician who helped construct the political arrangement that made [[AdolfHitler]] chancellor, later criticized continuing Nazi revolutionary disorder, and saw his associates targeted during the [[NightOfTheLongKnives|Night of the Long Knives]].

## Current Profile

The episode places Papen among conservative elites who believed Hitler could be contained and used inside a cabinet dependent on older institutions. That calculation helped transfer state authority to a movement whose violence and emergency government rapidly escaped conservative control.

On 17 June 1934, Papen publicly criticized the unrest and arrogance produced by the Nazi revolution. The purge then demonstrated the weakness of conservative opposition inside the regime: Papen survived, but Herbert von Bose and Edgar Jung were killed as the operation widened beyond the SA.

Papen's trajectory is therefore not simply resistance. It joins enabling miscalculation, belated criticism, and personal vulnerability within the dictatorship he had helped make possible.

## Key Characteristics

- He helped conservative elites place Hitler in the chancellorship.
- He assumed Hitler could be constrained by older political institutions.
- His June 1934 speech criticized revolutionary disorder and arrogance.
- The purge killed close associates while leaving him alive.
- His experience exposes the collapse of conservative control over Nazi power.
