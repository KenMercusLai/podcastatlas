# Franz von Papen

## Overview

Franz von Papen was a conservative German chancellor and vice chancellor whose authoritarian gambles weakened the [[WeimarRepublic|Weimar Republic]], whose bargain made [[AdolfHitler]] chancellor, and whose later criticism ended with associates killed during the [[NightOfTheLongKnives|Night of the Long Knives]].

## Current Profile

The collapse episode places Papen at the center of the authoritarian alternative to parliamentary rule. Backed by Hindenburg's circle and connected to army, political, landed, and business elites, his “cabinet of barons” lacked a Reichstag base. He and [[KurtVonSchleicher]] called another election, lifted the ban on the [[Sturmabteilung|SA]], and expected accommodation to make Hitler usable.

After deadly Altona street fighting, Papen used emergency authority to remove Prussia's elected Social Democratic government. The act damaged federalism and transferred control of Germany's largest state without armed resistance. His national government then lost a no-confidence vote 512 to 42, but continuing intrigue mattered more than parliamentary legitimacy.

Papen's decisive error came after his rivalry with Schleicher. He reopened negotiations and accepted the vice chancellorship in a cabinet headed by Hitler, believing conservative ministers and presidential authority could control the Nazi leader. That calculation transferred state authority to a movement whose violence and emergency strategy rapidly escaped conservative control.

On 17 June 1934, Papen publicly criticized the unrest and arrogance produced by the Nazi revolution. The purge then demonstrated the weakness of conservative opposition inside the regime: Papen survived, but Herbert von Bose and Edgar Jung were killed as the operation widened beyond the SA.

Papen's trajectory is therefore not simply resistance. It joins enabling miscalculation, belated criticism, and personal vulnerability within the dictatorship he had helped make possible.

## Key Characteristics

- He helped place Hitler in the chancellorship because he assumed older institutions could constrain him.
- He lifted the SA ban and used new elections to seek a mass base for authoritarian government.
- He removed Prussia's elected government by emergency decree, weakening federal and democratic restraints.
- His government lacked parliamentary legitimacy and suffered an overwhelming no-confidence defeat.
- His June 1934 speech criticized revolutionary disorder and arrogance.
- The purge killed close associates while leaving him alive.
- His experience exposes the collapse of conservative control over Nazi power.
