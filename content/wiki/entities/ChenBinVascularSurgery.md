# 陈斌 / Chen Bin (Vascular Surgery)

## Overview
陈斌 / Chen Bin is the vascular-surgery director named in the VOL.204 [[ZheBingShuoLaiHuaChang|这病说来话长]] episode on [[EconomyClassSyndromeTravelThrombosis|economy-class syndrome]], travel immobility, lower-limb venous thrombosis, pulmonary embolism risk, and emergency routing.

## Current Profile
In this source, Chen's role is to turn a viral travel-health term into a vascular-risk map. He explains why long sitting and restricted movement reduce calf-muscle pumping, raise venous pressure, promote stasis, and can allow thrombosis to become dangerous when a clot extends or embolizes. He repeatedly separates common swelling from higher-risk patterns and stresses that suspected thrombosis changes ordinary advice: walking, massage, or vigorous leg movement may become unsafe until evaluated.

He also provides the treatment-boundary frame. Anticoagulation prevents extension and new clot formation rather than dissolving an existing thrombus; thrombolysis and filter-era management belong to clinical decision-making; and aspirin or oral anticoagulants should not be treated as casual travel hacks.

## Key Characteristics
- Explains [[EconomyClassSyndromeTravelThrombosis|economy-class syndrome]] through immobility, venous return, stasis, coagulation, and pulmonary embolism risk.
- Gives lay listeners red-flag distinctions for unilateral, continuous, painful, or hard-calf swelling.
- Emphasizes transport safety when thrombosis is suspected, including avoiding self-walking and using wheelchair or stretcher routing.
- Separates prevention through activity, hydration, ankle pumping, and compression from treatment after thrombosis is suspected.
- Draws boundaries among anticoagulation, fibrinolysis, thrombolysis, aspirin, and self-medication.
