## Overview

Peng Chong / 彭宠 is synthesized as a former [[LiuXiu|刘秀 / 光武帝]]-side actor whose rebellion first tests Guangwu campaign sequencing and northern founding-coalition loyalty, then collapses through his own household. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-690-jiaoyou-bushen-you-duo-kepa-kankan-tianrong-de-jingli-lpbms8pebzdo2ldiwho1cba81njk|Hanji 690]] supplies the Jianwu 4 active-rebellion layer: [[FuZhanEasternHan|Fu Zhan]] persuades Liu Xiu not to personally prioritize distant Yuyang while closer regions remain unsettled, and the Geng family proves loyalty when [[GengShuEasternHan|Geng Shu]] defeats Peng Chong's Xiongnu-backed raid. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-733-2-gengyan-youzhizhe-shijingcheng-jiangmen-huzi-yongguan-sanjun-lnlfumtdzrigby9ox4v6pzbeogeh|Hanji 733-2]] frames him as a Yan-claiming rebel whose pressure forces the Geng family to prove alignment with Liu Xiu, while [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-693-panchu-liuxiu-de-pengchong-ruhe-yin-beipan-er-wang-lqler4fu9wqztsrgzsxhs1519nm|Hanji 693]] adds the terminal layer: Peng Chong fears betrayal but misreads its direction, and [[ZiMiEasternHan|Zi Mi]] kills him from inside the household.

## Current Profile

Hanji 733-2 describes Peng Chong as a figure who had helped Liu Xiu but later breaks with the emerging Eastern Han center after conflict with Zhu Fu and resentment over treatment. After taking Ji and claiming a Yan kingship, he tries to draw [[GengKuangEasternHan|Geng Kuang]] and [[GengYanEasternHan|Geng Yan]] into his camp. The Geng family's refusal makes Peng Chong a foil for loyal alignment and a catalyst for the Geng family's suspicion-management moves.

Hanji 690 backfills an earlier campaign-priority layer. Liu Xiu wants to personally attack Peng Chong, but Fu Zhan argues that Yan, Yu, Qing, and Xu/Yi-region affairs closer to the core remain unsettled and that chasing Peng Chong in distant Yuyang would abandon the nearer and easier for the farther and harder. Liu Xiu accepts the advice and sends Geng Yan instead. Because Geng Yan's father Geng Kuang has ties with Peng Chong, the rebellion also creates a loyalty-signaling problem: Geng Yan asks to return to Luoyang, Liu Xiu says he has no suspicion, Geng Kuang sends Geng Guo to court, and Geng Shu defeats Peng Chong's Xiongnu-backed raid.

Hanji 693 gives the end of that rebel project. Peng Chong's wife dreams of danger, and divination interprets the threat as soldiers rising from inside. Peng Chong suspects the returned hostage Hou Lanqing and pushes him outward, but the decisive threat is the household slave Zi Mi. Zi Mi binds Peng Chong during ritual fasting, blocks normal access to the court, forces a gate-opening order, kills Peng Chong and Peng Chong's wife, and sends their heads to Luoyang.

Peng Chong's death does not stabilize his camp. Han Li and others briefly set up Peng Wu Yan as king, then Han Li kills Peng Wu and surrenders to [[JiZunEasternHan|Ji Zun]], after which Peng Chong's remaining family is destroyed. Liu Xiu's reward of Zi Mi as "不义侯" shifts the final judgment from mere rebel defeat to the problem of whether a dynasty can publicly reward useful but unrighteous violence.

## Key Characteristics

- Former Liu Xiu-side actor who becomes a regional rebel after the founding coalition begins centralizing.
- Distant Yuyang rebel whose threat tests whether Guangwu should prioritize remote retaliation or nearer unsettled regions.
- Yan-claiming power holder whose rebellion tests northern family loyalty and Geng-family alignment.
- Xiongnu-backed raider whose military pressure is checked through Geng Shu's action under Geng Kuang.
- Suspicious ruler who recognizes the possibility of inner danger but misidentifies the decisive insider.
- Rebel whose regime collapses through household access, forged command control, and posthumous elite fragmentation.
- Example of how enemy removal can create a separate title-and-law problem when the method is master-killing.

## Qualifications

The page remains bounded to the listed source notes. Hanji 690 and Hanji 733-2 both cover Peng Chong as a Geng-family loyalty test, but Hanji 690 sits earlier in the campaign sequence and does not narrate Peng Chong's death. Hanji 733-2 is Geng Yan-focused and therefore preserves Zhu Fu, the exact grievance chain, and many military details as source-scoped. Hanji 693 supplies Peng Chong's terminal collapse but does not by itself reconstruct his entire prior career. Hou Lanqing, Peng Zhu, Peng Wu, Han Li, and other minor actors remain source-scoped until additional evidence justifies separate canonical pages.
