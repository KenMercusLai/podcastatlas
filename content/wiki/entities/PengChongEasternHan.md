## Overview

Peng Chong / 彭宠 is synthesized as a former [[LiuXiu|刘秀 / 光武帝]]-side actor whose profile now begins with hesitant but decisive Yuyang support during the [[WangLangHebeiClaimant|王郎]] crisis, then later turns through reward grievance, local resource autonomy, and central suspicion into rebellion. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-671-nide-lingdao-shifou-zhide-zhuisui-jinlai-duibi-xia-lrjwus-s14y1x2y4r3f-dvri1nv|Hanji 671]] supplies the positive alignment layer: [[KouXunEasternHan|Kou Xun]] negotiates Shanggu-Yuyang support, [[WuHanEasternHan|Wu Han]] pushes Peng Chong past hesitation, and Liu Xiu rewards Peng Chong after Yuyang troops arrive. The later origin layer in Hanji 685 shows why that support becomes grievance when Peng reads subsequent rewards as insufficient. Later evidence places him at Ji and Yan kingship, treats the campaign against him as a remote-priority problem, makes his rebellion a northern founding-coalition loyalty test, and ends the profile in household collapse.

## Current Profile

Hanji 671 supplies the first current Peng Chong layer before the rebellion arc. Kou Xun comes to Yuyang to coordinate Shanggu and Yuyang support for Liu Xiu. Wu Han, Ge Yan, and [[WangLiangEasternHan|Wang Liang]] urge Peng Chong to choose Liu Xiu, but Peng initially hesitates. After Wu Han gathers information about Liu Xiu's reputation and forges a Liu Xiu proclamation for a scholar to deliver, Peng Chong commits troops, sends a combined force south, and appoints Wu Han as acting zhangshi. When the northern force reaches Liu Xiu, Liu Xiu promotes Peng Chong as dajiangjun and enfeoffs him.

Peng Chong later breaks before the Yan-claim stage. He had helped Liu Xiu during the Wang Lang campaign by providing cavalry and grain movement, and he expects that contribution to be recognized. When Wu Han, Wang Liang, other high officers, and even some of Peng's own subordinates receive top status while Peng himself does not receive the reward he expects, public comparison becomes the grievance core. Yuyang's comparatively intact postwar condition and iron-office resources then make discontent actionable: Peng can exchange iron for grain, accumulate wealth, and sustain independent force.

The policy and suspicion channel hardens the grievance. Zhu Fu, as Youzhou mu, recruits local notables and former Wang Mang officials and supports their families with commandery grain. Peng refuses because he sees expanded offices as unaffordable while war continues. Zhu Fu repeatedly reports Peng's troops and grain to Liu Xiu; Liu Xiu leaks the warning, summons Peng to Luoyang, and refuses Peng's request that Zhu Fu come too. Peng's wife and retainers advise against going, so Peng detains a persuading kinsman, rebels with more than twenty thousand troops, attacks Ji, and repeatedly asks [[GengKuangEasternHan|Geng Kuang]] to join. Geng Kuang refuses and kills the envoys.

Once the revolt is public, Peng Chong looks broad but exposed. Zhu Fu's Ji city runs out of food and reaches cannibalism before Geng Kuang's rescue lets Zhu Fu escape and Ji surrenders to Peng Chong. Peng Chong then claims Yan kingship, takes Right Beiping and Shanggu, bribes Xiongnu, and connects southward with Zhang Bu and other rebel forces. Liu Xiu tells Zhu Fu that he had predicted Chimei would starve and move east, and that Peng Chong's exposed multi-front position can also be allowed to strain itself while Han capacity waits for the wheat harvest.

The campaign-priority problem gives the same logic a formal remonstrance frame. Liu Xiu wants to personally attack Peng Chong, but [[FuZhanEasternHan|Fu Zhan]] argues that Yan, Yu, Qing, and Yi-region affairs closer to the core remain unsettled and that chasing Peng Chong in distant Yuyang would abandon the nearer and easier for the farther and harder. Liu Xiu accepts the advice and sends [[GengYanEasternHan|Geng Yan]] instead.

Because [[GengKuangEasternHan|Geng Kuang]] has ties with Peng Chong, the rebellion also creates a loyalty-signaling problem. Geng Yan asks to return to Luoyang, Liu Xiu says he has no suspicion, Geng Kuang sends [[GengGuoEasternHan|Geng Guo]] to court, and [[GengShuEasternHan|Geng Shu]] defeats Peng Chong's Xiongnu-backed raid. Peng Chong tries to draw the Geng family into his camp, and their refusal makes them prove alignment with Liu Xiu.

The rebel project ends through household collapse. Peng Chong's wife dreams of danger, and divination interprets the threat as soldiers rising from inside. Peng Chong suspects the returned hostage Hou Lanqing and pushes him outward, but the decisive threat is the household slave [[ZiMiEasternHan|Zi Mi]]. Zi Mi binds Peng Chong during ritual fasting, blocks normal court access, forces a gate-opening order, kills Peng Chong and Peng Chong's wife, and sends their heads to Luoyang.

Peng Chong's death does not stabilize his camp. Han Li and others briefly set up Peng Wu Yan as king, then Han Li kills Peng Wu and surrenders to [[JiZunEasternHan|Ji Zun]], after which Peng Chong's remaining family is destroyed. Liu Xiu's reward of Zi Mi as "不义侯" shifts the final judgment from mere rebel defeat to the problem of whether a dynasty can publicly reward useful but unrighteous violence.

## Key Characteristics

- Hesitant Yuyang power holder whose initial support for Liu Xiu after Kou Xun's negotiation and Wu Han's intervention later becomes a reward-expectation baseline.
- Resource-backed Yuyang power holder whose relatively intact base and iron-for-grain capacity make resentment actionable.
- Yan-claiming rebel whose apparent strength depends on Ji, northern counties, Xiongnu bribery, and southern rebel links.
- Exposed peripheral rebel whom Liu Xiu reads as vulnerable to timing, supply limits, and multi-front pressure.
- Distant Yuyang rebel whose threat tests whether Guangwu should prioritize remote retaliation or nearer unsettled regions.
- Northern coalition pressure point whose rebellion forces Geng-family loyalty signals.
- Suspicious rebel whose regime ultimately collapses through household access, forged command control, and posthumous elite fragmentation.

## Qualifications

The page remains bounded to the listed source notes. Hanji 671 adds the initial Yuyang-support layer but keeps Ge Yan, the unnamed scholar, the exact forged proclamation, and troop counts source-scoped. The rebellion-origin layer keeps Yuyang commandery resources, Peng Chong's wife, the persuading kinsman, and exact office mechanics source-scoped. The exposed-rebel layer keeps Zhang Feng, Fuping, Huosuo, exact northern county details, and the full Ji famine sequence source-scoped. The campaign-priority and Geng-family loyalty-test layers do not narrate Peng Chong's death. The terminal-collapse layer does not by itself reconstruct his entire prior career. Hou Lanqing, Peng Zhu, Peng Wu, Han Li, and other minor actors remain source-scoped until additional evidence justifies separate canonical pages.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 671 as Peng Chong's initial Yuyang-support layer before the Hanji 685 rebellion-origin layer, sharpening the later reward-grievance contrast.
- Reframed Peng Chong as a five-stage profile beginning with discontent and local capacity before the later Yan claim.
- Preserved Hanji 688 as the Ji/Yan claim, northern county, Xiongnu, southern alliance, and exposed-position layer.
- Preserved Hanji 690 and Hanji 733-2 as the campaign-priority and Geng-family signaling layers.
- Preserved Hanji 693 as the terminal household-collapse layer.
