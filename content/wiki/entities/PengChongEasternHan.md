## Overview
Peng Chong / 彭宠 is presented in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-733-2-gengyan-youzhizhe-shijingcheng-jiangmen-huzi-yongguan-sanjun-lnlfumtdzrigby9ox4v6pzbeogeh|Hanji 733-2]] as a former Liu Xiu supporter whose rebellion turns regional alliance into a trust crisis. His challenge matters because it tests whether the Geng family will remain aligned with [[LiuXiu|刘秀 / 光武帝]] or follow old personal ties.

## Current Profile
The episode describes Peng Chong as a figure who had helped Liu Xiu but later breaks with the emerging Eastern Han center after conflict with Zhu Fu and resentment over treatment. After taking Ji and claiming a Yan kingship, he tries to draw [[GengKuangEasternHan|耿况]] and [[GengYanEasternHan|耿弇]] into his camp. The Geng family's refusal makes Peng Chong a foil for loyal alignment and a catalyst for the Geng family's suspicion-management moves.

Peng Chong's rebellion is not portrayed simply as an external enemy event. It reveals how founding coalitions remain fragile when former collaborators, local military bases, and family networks overlap. Geng Yan's later campaign against him and Peng Chong's death close the episode's trust-crisis arc.

## Key Characteristics
- Former Liu Xiu-side actor who becomes a regional rebel.
- Yan-claiming power holder whose rebellion tests northern family loyalty.
- Foil for Geng-family political judgment and loyalty signaling.
- Example of how personal ties can become suspicion risk after a founding coalition starts centralizing.
