## Overview

Peng Chong / 彭宠 is synthesized as a former [[LiuXiu|刘秀 / 光武帝]]-side actor whose rebellion moves from outwardly dangerous Yan claim to internally fragile collapse. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-688-cong-yongbing-baiwan-dao-kuibuchengjun-dengyu-jingli-le-shenme-lpabdvtmhtrf37puwvgi4c9ajp60|Hanji 688]] now backfills the exposed-claim layer: after Ji falls, Peng Chong calls himself Yan king, takes Right Beiping and Shanggu counties, bribes [[Xiongnu|匈奴]], and links south with [[ZhangBuEasternHan|张步]] plus Fuping/Huosuo forces, while Liu Xiu judges that food limits and more decisive fronts make delay preferable. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-690-jiaoyou-bushen-you-duo-kepa-kankan-tianrong-de-jingli-lpbms8pebzdo2ldiwho1cba81njk|Hanji 690]] then shows Fu Zhan formalizing that campaign-priority argument, [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-733-2-gengyan-youzhizhe-shijingcheng-jiangmen-huzi-yongguan-sanjun-lnlfumtdzrigby9ox4v6pzbeogeh|Hanji 733-2]] frames Peng Chong as a northern founding-coalition loyalty test, and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-693-panchu-liuxiu-de-pengchong-ruhe-yin-beipan-er-wang-lqler4fu9wqztsrgzsxhs1519nm|Hanji 693]] gives the terminal household-collapse layer.

## Current Profile

Hanji 688 places Peng Chong at a moment when his rebellion looks broad but Liu Xiu reads it as vulnerable. [[ZhuFuEasternHan|Zhu Fu]]'s Ji city runs out of food and reaches cannibalism before Geng Kuang's rescue lets Zhu Fu escape and Ji surrenders to Peng Chong. Peng Chong then claims Yan kingship, takes Right Beiping and Shanggu, bribes Xiongnu, and connects southward with Zhang Bu and other rebel forces. Liu Xiu tells Zhu Fu that he had predicted Chimei would starve and move east, and that Peng Chong's exposed multi-front position can also be allowed to strain itself while Han capacity waits for the wheat harvest.

Hanji 690 gives the same logic a formal remonstrance frame. Liu Xiu wants to personally attack Peng Chong, but [[FuZhanEasternHan|Fu Zhan]] argues that Yan, Yu, Qing, and Yi-region affairs closer to the core remain unsettled and that chasing Peng Chong in distant Yuyang would abandon the nearer and easier for the farther and harder. Liu Xiu accepts the advice and sends [[GengYanEasternHan|Geng Yan]] instead.

Because [[GengKuangEasternHan|Geng Kuang]] has ties with Peng Chong, the rebellion also creates a loyalty-signaling problem. Geng Yan asks to return to Luoyang, Liu Xiu says he has no suspicion, Geng Kuang sends [[GengGuoEasternHan|Geng Guo]] to court, and [[GengShuEasternHan|Geng Shu]] defeats Peng Chong's Xiongnu-backed raid. Hanji 733-2 preserves the same pressure as a founding-coalition loyalty test: Peng Chong tries to draw the Geng family into his camp, and their refusal makes them prove alignment with Liu Xiu.

Hanji 693 gives the end of the rebel project. Peng Chong's wife dreams of danger, and divination interprets the threat as soldiers rising from inside. Peng Chong suspects the returned hostage Hou Lanqing and pushes him outward, but the decisive threat is the household slave [[ZiMiEasternHan|Zi Mi]]. Zi Mi binds Peng Chong during ritual fasting, blocks normal court access, forces a gate-opening order, kills Peng Chong and Peng Chong's wife, and sends their heads to Luoyang.

Peng Chong's death does not stabilize his camp. Han Li and others briefly set up Peng Wu Yan as king, then Han Li kills Peng Wu and surrenders to [[JiZunEasternHan|Ji Zun]], after which Peng Chong's remaining family is destroyed. Liu Xiu's reward of Zi Mi as "不义侯" shifts the final judgment from mere rebel defeat to the problem of whether a dynasty can publicly reward useful but unrighteous violence.

## Key Characteristics

- Former Liu Xiu-side actor who becomes a regional rebel after the founding coalition begins centralizing.
- Yan-claiming power holder whose apparent strength depends on Ji, northern counties, Xiongnu bribery, and southern rebel links.
- Exposed peripheral rebel whom Liu Xiu reads as vulnerable to timing, supply limits, and multi-front pressure.
- Distant Yuyang rebel whose threat tests whether Guangwu should prioritize remote retaliation or nearer unsettled regions.
- Northern coalition pressure point whose rebellion forces Geng-family loyalty signals.
- Suspicious ruler who recognizes the possibility of inner danger but misidentifies the decisive insider.
- Rebel whose regime collapses through household access, forged command control, and posthumous elite fragmentation.

## Qualifications

The page remains bounded to the listed source notes. Hanji 688 strengthens the early exposed-rebel layer but keeps Zhang Feng, Fuping, Huosuo, exact northern county details, and the full Ji famine sequence source-scoped. Hanji 690 and Hanji 733-2 both cover Peng Chong as a Geng-family loyalty test, but Hanji 690 sits earlier in the campaign sequence and does not narrate Peng Chong's death. Hanji 733-2 is Geng Yan-focused and therefore preserves Zhu Fu, the exact grievance chain, and many military details as source-scoped. Hanji 693 supplies Peng Chong's terminal collapse but does not by itself reconstruct his entire prior career. Hou Lanqing, Peng Zhu, Peng Wu, Han Li, and other minor actors remain source-scoped until additional evidence justifies separate canonical pages.
