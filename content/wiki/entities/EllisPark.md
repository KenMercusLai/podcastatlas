# Ellis Park

## Overview

Ellis Park is the Johannesburg stadium presented as the charged public stage for the 1995 Rugby World Cup final and Mandela's Springbok reconciliation performance.

## Current Profile

In the episode, Ellis Park matters because it concentrates rugby's apartheid-era meaning inside a visible arena. The venue is described as a largely Afrikaner space on final day, so the crowd's acceptance of [[NkosiSikelelIAfrika|Nkosi Sikelel' iAfrika]], the [[Springboks]]' participation, and the chant for [[NelsonMandela]] make the stadium a test site for post-apartheid national recognition.

## Key Characteristics

- Johannesburg rugby stadium where the [[RugbyWorldCup1995|1995 Rugby World Cup]] final took place.
- Symbolically charged [[Afrikaners|Afrikaner]] rugby space in the episode's framing.
- Site of Mandela's number-six Springbok-shirt appearance.
- Site of the anthem ceremony where both [[NkosiSikelelIAfrika|Nkosi]] and [[DieStemVanSuidAfrika|Die Stem]] were performed.
- Located near the later memorial frame around [[EnochSontonga]]'s rediscovered grave.

## Evidence

- Final setting: [[682-south-africa-mandela-and-the-death-of-apartheid-part-6-glt6535658080]] opens with the anthem as sung at the 1995 Rugby World Cup final at Ellis Park in Johannesburg.
- Crowd meaning: [[682-south-africa-mandela-and-the-death-of-apartheid-part-6-glt6535658080]] describes the crowd as largely Afrikaner and treats the chant for Mandela as a reconciliation moment.
- Mandela's ritual action: [[682-south-africa-mandela-and-the-death-of-apartheid-part-6-glt6535658080]] says Mandela wore [[FrancoisPienaar]]'s number-six Springbok shirt at the final.
- Anthem test: [[682-south-africa-mandela-and-the-death-of-apartheid-part-6-glt6535658080]] frames the pre-kickoff anthem ceremony as a test of whether the new South Africa could be recognized in rugby's most charged space.
- Memorial geography: [[682-south-africa-mandela-and-the-death-of-apartheid-part-6-glt6535658080]] says Sontonga's memorial was about three miles from Ellis Park.
