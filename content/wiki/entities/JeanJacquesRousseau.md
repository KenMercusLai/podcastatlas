# Jean-Jacques Rousseau / 卢梭

## Overview

Jean-Jacques Rousseau was an Enlightenment thinker whose ideas appear in the wiki through workplace satire, algorithmic-media criticism, revolutionary virtue, and gendered republican citizenship.

## Current Profile

Across the sources Rousseau functions less as a complete doctrinal subject than as a portable critical vocabulary. A workplace satire turns his conflict with Voltaire into competition over credit and territory. An algorithm episode uses the Enlightenment demand for active reason to question dependence on recommendation systems. The French Revolution sources connect Rousseauian sincerity and suspicion of hidden interests to [[RevolutionaryVirtuePolitics]], while also linking his domestic division of the sexes to women's exclusion from public citizenship.

## Key Characteristics

- He is used satirically to translate Enlightenment conflict into modern workplace competition.
- His Enlightenment context helps frame the difference between active judgment and outsourced algorithmic relevance.
- Rousseau-shaped sincerity and publicity feed revolutionary suspicion of concealed interests.
- His domestic gender model helps explain the division between male public virtue and female household duty.
- The sources use him interpretively rather than offering a complete account of his philosophy.

## Qualifications

- The workplace source is a discussion of a satirical book, not a doctrinal account of Rousseau.
- The algorithm source is explicitly analogical and does not claim Rousseau analyzed modern platforms.
- The revolutionary sources identify intellectual influence and political use rather than proving that Rousseau alone caused virtue politics or women's exclusion.
- Claims about women internalizing Rousseauian gender roles remain source-scoped and cannot represent all women's motives.
