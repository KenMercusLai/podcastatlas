# Olaf II of Norway

## Overview

Olaf II of Norway, called Olaf the Stout in the episode and later Saint Olaf, appears as [[HaraldHardrada|Harald Hardrada]]'s older half-brother, a Christian king displaced by Canute, and the defeated claimant whose death at the [[BattleOfStiklestad|Battle of Stiklestad]] became both martyrdom and the starting point of Harald's exile.

## Current Profile

Olaf's career joins Viking warfare, western Christian experience, and a bid to build Norwegian royal authority. After fighting abroad and being baptized at Rouen, he became king of Norway, but conflict with Canute and Norwegian magnates drove him into Rus exile in 1028. His 1030 return gathered Swedish and upland support but faced a much larger coalition of Canute loyalists and local opponents at Stiklestad.

The episode interprets Olaf's Christianization policy as politically important but evidentially disputed. Forced baptism and white-cross shields may reflect later Christian back-projection; alternatively, Olaf's exposure to Christian monarchies could have shown him how conversion strengthened kingship. His death was subsequently recast as martyrdom, and his relics made him Norway's patron saint and a focus of pilgrimage.

## Key Characteristics

- Warrior king whose experience in England, Normandy, and Scandinavia connected rule to a wider Christian political world.
- Displaced ruler whose conflict with Canute and Norwegian magnates sent him into exile in Kievan Rus.
- Returning claimant whose small coalition was heavily outnumbered at Stiklestad.
- Christianizing monarch whose reported coercive methods remain historically disputed.
- Fallen king whose death was transformed into sainthood, pilgrimage, and dynastic memory.

## Qualifications

This profile rests on one narrative podcast episode drawing heavily on later Icelandic sagas. Olaf's broad career, defeat, death, and later cult are more secure than exact dialogue, shield markings, recruitment practices, battle wounds, supernatural protection around his opponents, or the episode's reconstruction of motives. “Olaf the Stout” is the episode's narrative label; Olaf II and Saint Olaf identify the same ruler in different contexts.
