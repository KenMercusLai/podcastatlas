# Stormont

## Overview
Stormont is the Northern Ireland parliament and government whose authority the Troubles episodes track from Protestant-majority state formation through reform failure, security crisis, internment, and suspension in March 1972.

## Current Profile
The current synthesis is that Stormont fails in stages. Its structural legitimacy is already weak for many Catholics because partition-era institutions and local power arrangements are experienced as Protestant rule. Reform efforts under [[TerenceONeill|Terence O'Neill]] cannot satisfy civil-rights demands or contain hardline unionist resistance. By the Faulkner period, internment has backfired, [[BloodySunday]] has destroyed trust further, and [[EdwardHeath]] offers [[BrianFaulkner]] either reduced authority under British control of law and order or [[NorthernIrelandDirectRule]]. Faulkner's refusal turns suspension into the outcome.

## Key Characteristics
- Devolved government identified with Protestant-majority rule in the early Northern Ireland settlement.
- Reform-constrained institution facing civil-rights demands and hardline unionist backlash.
- Security-policy actor that pushes or resists internment under Chichester-Clark and Faulkner.
- Institution increasingly dependent on Westminster and the [[BritishArmy]] once street violence and paramilitary conflict escalate.
- Suspended on 24 March 1972 after Bloody Sunday, bombings, and the collapse of Faulkner's governing authority.

## Evidence
- Protestant-state structure: [[697-the-troubles-murder-in-belfast-part-1-glt4877520736]] uses James Craig, local voting rules, boundaries, and Protestant-dominated institutions to explain why Stormont lacked neutrality for many Catholics.
- Reform limits: [[698-the-troubles-the-brink-of-civil-war-part-2-glt2308408988]] follows O'Neill's reform effort, Chichester-Clark's succession, Westminster pressure, and the civil-rights street-war threshold.
- Internment decision: [[699-the-troubles-return-of-the-ira-part-3-glt9519811642]] has Chichester-Clark resist internment because intelligence is poor, then Faulkner press Heath to approve it through Operation Demetrius.
- Suspension: [[700-the-troubles-bloody-sunday-part-4-glt3909829334]] says Heath offered reduced authority or direct rule, Faulkner refused, and Stormont was suspended on 24 March 1972.

## Qualifications
Stormont's failure is not presented as one leader's private defect. The sources show structural legitimacy problems, unionist pressure, Westminster delay, paramilitary violence, and security-policy blowback all narrowing the institution's choices before suspension.

## What Changed
- Added the March 1972 suspension as the end point of the current Stormont profile.
- Added direct rule as the institutional alternative after Faulkner rejects reduced authority.
- Reframed Stormont as a staged legitimacy failure rather than only a background parliament.
