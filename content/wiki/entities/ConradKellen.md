# Conrad Kellen

## Overview

Conrad Kellen is presented as a German Jewish refugee, Second World War intelligence veteran, and RAND analyst who read the [[VietCongMotivationAndMoraleProject]] archive as evidence of enduring commitment rather than approaching collapse.

## Current Profile

Kellen's wartime experience in intelligence and psychological warfare made him attentive to the people concealed by abstractions such as “casualties.” RAND hired him after he criticized American propaganda leaflets for asking ordinary soldiers to end a war they had neither begun nor controlled.

By late 1966, he read roughly one thousand project interviews. Where [[LeonGoure]] emphasized discouragement and declining expectation of victory, Kellen emphasized willingness to continue despite suffering. He concluded that the Viet Cong were not collapsing and that the United States could not win. The episode portrays his warning as early and unheeded, and reports his later criticism of institutions that treat agreement with established views as the mark of expertise.

## Key Characteristics

- German Jewish refugee whose war experience shaped attention to commitment and human cost.
- Intelligence and psychological-warfare veteran skeptical of propaganda that assigns strategic agency to ordinary soldiers.
- Close reader who reviewed roughly one thousand RAND interviews.
- Dissenter who treated persistence under hardship as more diagnostic than expressed optimism.
- Institutional critic who linked expertise recognition to confirmation of existing views.

## Qualifications

The source does not provide Kellen's memoranda, selection method, full interview set, or a complete biography. His eventual agreement with the war's outcome does not make every inference retrospectively correct, and the episode's biographical account should not replace methodological comparison of his analysis with Goure's.
