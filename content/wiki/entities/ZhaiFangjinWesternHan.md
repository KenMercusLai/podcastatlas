## Overview

翟方进 / Zhai Fangjin is synthesized as a Western Han chancellor whose wiki profile now has two sharply different faces: in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-558-ai-zhuang-caihuiying-wangmang-you-duo-hui-yinren-llvrlct7laohoo54s3lhzgwvsfc9|Hanji 558]] he joins [[HeWuWesternHan|何武]] in advocating the abolition of刺史 and creation of higher-ranked州牧 under [[HanChengdi|汉成帝]], while [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-559-mingming-wuzui-weihe-tianxiaren-dou-xiang-ta-si-lu3ckuxwujdbj4zdoqhvo2cg1rs0|Hanji 559]] makes him the chancellor sacrificed after an 荧惑守心 omen.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-558-ai-zhuang-caihuiying-wangmang-you-duo-hui-yinren-llvrlct7laohoo54s3lhzgwvsfc9|Hanji 558]] presents Zhai Fangjin as part of a senior-office reform voice. His argument is framed through hierarchy and古制: a low-ranking inspector supervising two-thousand-stone commandery officials looks institutionally improper, so the court should replace刺史 with州牧. This creates a contrast with [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-580-huigu-sizhou-shuishi-nide-kaoshan-liid3tymhojmajkyeqoq6vryprhz|Hanji 580]], where later officials reverse direction and restore刺史 because higher-ranked州牧 are judged too comfortable and less sharp as oversight agents.

Hanji 559 turns from Zhai's institutional voice to his political vulnerability. The source says his strict, by-the-rules style had produced many enemies: he did not look at personal ties when handling cases, had impeached many officials as丞相司直, and continued attacking factional behavior after becoming chancellor. When 荧惑守心 made Chengdi's cosmic responsibility dangerous, that resentment gave other officials a ready account in which disasters, bandits, hunger, and bureaucratic disorder could be placed on Zhai.

Zhai therefore becomes a case where office responsibility, personal enforcement style, and court resentment converge. Li Xun warns him that he may need a death-substitute, but Zhai hesitates. Once Ben Li's court advice identifies the chancellor as the right person to protect the Son of Heaven, Chengdi's courtesy-coded gifts and document leave Zhai little room except suicide. The page treats him neither as a wholly innocent martyr nor as a settled criminal; the key synthesis is that his position made him a usable vessel for blame when the ruler and officials both benefited from downward responsibility transfer.

## Key Characteristics

- Zhai Fangjin acts as a chancellor-level advocate for office-rank reform.
- His proposal treats hierarchy and ancient precedent as solutions to provincial oversight tension.
- His reform becomes unstable in later Aidi-era politics, where the same office design is judged differently.
- His strict enforcement and many impeachments create broad resentment among officials.
- His chancellorship makes him the natural target when an omen is interpreted as needing a ministerial sacrifice.
- His death is coerced through court form: condemnation, gifts, and posthumous honors preserve imperial decorum while forcing suicide.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 558 and Hanji 559, with Hanji 580 retained as a comparison for the later州牧-to-刺史 reversal. It does not reconstruct Zhai Fangjin's full biography, the independent legal truth of every charge, or the complete astrology tradition behind 荧惑守心.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 559's death layer: Zhai Fangjin becomes not only an institutional reformer but a chancellor whose harsh reputation and office liability made him a usable scapegoat in an omen crisis.
