## Overview
Han Shu / 《汉书》 is currently used in the wiki in two source-scoped ways: as a comparison text for [[HanPingdi|汉平帝]]'s death in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-627-1-nvxing-biting-dute-shijiao-jingqi-yinyu-beihou-de-lishi-zhihui-lqomjfejmglm9hylkomktx6rtbtt|Hanji 627-1]], and as one of the "前四史" orientation texts in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-591-2-ruhe-geng-gaoxiao-di-dudong-lishi-gudai-biannian-jianzan-juequ-qiannian-fengliu-yunyan-lruv48fgfpo7u0tjsqb264k0y7yq|Hanji 591-2]].

## Current Profile
The current synthesis treats Han Shu as both a Western Han official-history anchor and a source-critical comparator. Hanji 591-2 introduces it as Ban Gu's Eastern Han compilation covering Western Han history from Liu Bang's founding era through Wang Mang's final years. Hanji 627-1 then shows why the text matters methodologically: its Pingdi notices are presented as more illness-centered than Zizhi Tongjian's direct pepper-wine poisoning account, with poisoning detail entering through later annotation around the Pingdi record.

## Key Characteristics
- Western Han official-history text placed by the episode among the front-four histories.
- Associated in Hanji 591-2 with Ban Gu, long compilation labor, and a roughly Western Han-to-Wang Mang chronological scope.
- Functions in Hanji 627-1 as a comparison point for Pingdi's death tradition.
- Helps the wiki keep poisoning claims source-scoped instead of flattening later annotation and chronicle selection into one settled fact.
