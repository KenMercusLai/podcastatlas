# Leon Goure

## Overview

Leon Goure is presented as the RAND analyst who led the [[VietCongMotivationAndMoraleProject]] and interpreted falling expectations of Viet Cong victory as evidence that American bombing was breaking enemy morale.

## Current Profile

Goure came from a Russian Menshevik family that fled Russia, Germany, and France before reaching the United States. He described himself as a “professional refugee,” and the episode connects his attachment to America and experience of communist advance to a belief that communism had to be resisted wherever it moved.

At RAND he secured Pentagon support for a much larger interview program and became its prominent advocate. He emphasized a reported fall from 65 percent to 20 percent in interviewees expecting Viet Cong victory, presented his interpretation across military, intelligence, press, Pentagon, and White House settings, and supplied an influential rationale for escalation. RAND later became concerned that he had bypassed normal review, recalled him from Vietnam in April 1967, and encouraged his departure.

## Key Characteristics

- Refugee whose anti-communism and American patriotism were grounded in repeated family displacement.
- Institution builder who expanded a field-interview project into an approximately 62,000-page archive.
- Analyst who treated declining victory expectations as evidence of approaching Viet Cong collapse.
- Policy advocate who carried his interpretation to senior military, intelligence, political, and media audiences.
- Contested RAND figure whose review practices and conclusions generated internal concern.

## Qualifications

The source does not provide Goure's reports, the underlying sample, RAND's internal record, or a complete biography. His refugee history is a relevant interpretive lens but does not by itself prove why he reached his conclusion, and a conclusion that aligned with escalation policy is not thereby shown to have been insincere or knowingly distorted.
