# Mary Norris

## Overview

Mary Norris is a former *New Yorker* copy editor whom [[MalcolmGladwell]] consults in [[divide-and-conquer-divide-and-conquer|Divide and Conquer]] about constitutional grammar and punctuation.

## Current Profile

Norris explains how defining and descriptive clauses differ and identifies confusing comma use in constitutional amendments before turning to Article IV, Section 3. Her decisive contribution is grammatical: “nor” refers backward and connects with “but,” so the wording crosses the semicolon and supports reading the final consent condition as applying to both subdivision and combination.

The episode uses her analysis as independent support for [[MichaelStokesPaulsen]] and [[VasanKesavan]]'s legal interpretation, not as a substitute for their historical research.

## Key Characteristics

- Applies professional copy-editing judgment to legal text.
- Distinguishes essential clauses from parenthetical or descriptive clauses.
- Treats syntax and conjunctions as more probative than punctuation in isolation.
- Supplies an independent grammatical check on a constitutional-law argument.
