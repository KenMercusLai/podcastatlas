# Sarah Lay

## Overview

Sarah Lay is Benjamin Lay's wife and partner in [[316-the-first-abolitionist-glt2136062721|The Rest Is History episode 316]]. The source presents her as a fellow Quaker, physically similar to Benjamin Lay in stature and disability, and central to his Colchester, Barbados, and Pennsylvania life.

## Current Profile

Sarah Lay's profile is source-bounded and relational, but not incidental. In Colchester she and [[BenjaminLay]] work as drapers before moving to [[Barbados]] in 1731. There, her act of feeding starving enslaved people at the couple's shop becomes part of the episode's moral transformation sequence. The source later notes that she and Benjamin practiced simplicity, vegetarianism, boycott of goods tied to suffering, homemade clothing, and radical living in Pennsylvania.

Her death in 1735 is treated as a turning point in Benjamin Lay's public radicalism. The episode does not give Sarah the same independent activist record, but it does show her as part of the household practice that made Lay's later [[SlaveProducedGoodsBoycott|boycott]] and [[AbolitionistPublicWitness|public witness]] credible.

## Key Characteristics

- Quaker spouse and collaborator within Benjamin Lay's abolitionist household.
- Draper with Benjamin Lay in Colchester before the couple moved to Barbados.
- Direct witness to the hunger of enslaved people in Barbados and participant in feeding them at the shop.
- Practitioner of radical simplicity, vegetarianism, homemade clothing, and boycott of goods tied to suffering.
- Personal loss whose 1735 death is presented as intensifying Benjamin Lay's later public protest.

## Evidence

- Marriage and shared life: [[316-the-first-abolitionist-glt2136062721]] describes Sarah and Benjamin Lay as deeply devoted and physically similar in being short and hunchbacked.
- Colchester and Barbados: [[316-the-first-abolitionist-glt2136062721]] says the couple worked as drapers in Colchester before moving to Barbados in 1731.
- Feeding the hungry: [[316-the-first-abolitionist-glt2136062721]] says Sarah gave food to starving enslaved people who came to the couple's shop.
- Radical household practice: [[316-the-first-abolitionist-glt2136062721]] says Benjamin and Sarah Lay boycotted goods connected to suffering, remained vegetarian, made their own clothes, drank water and milk, and lived largely on vegetables.
- Death and aftermath: [[316-the-first-abolitionist-glt2136062721]] says Sarah died in 1735, after which Benjamin Lay's public radicalism intensified.

## Qualifications

The episode focuses mostly on Benjamin Lay, so Sarah's independent views, writings, and full biography are not developed. This page records only the source-supported role she plays in Lay's Barbados awakening, household discipline, and activist context.
