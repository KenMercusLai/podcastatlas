# Philo of Alexandria

## Overview

Philo of Alexandria was a Jewish Alexandrian philosopher whose favorable account of [[Tiberius]] preserves a near-contemporary alternative to the hostile Roman tradition represented by [[Tacitus]] and [[Suetonius]].

## Current Profile

[[535-emperors-of-rome-tiberius-slaughter-and-scandal-part-2-glt6029756405]] uses Philo as a counterweight rather than a decisive verdict. Having visited Rome shortly after Tiberius's death, he praised the emperor's wisdom, learning, nobility, generosity, and maintenance of peace across a twenty-three-year reign.

That testimony demonstrates that Tiberius did not possess one uncontested ancient reputation. A favorable provincial or Alexandrian memory can illuminate peace, taxation, and administrative stability that hostile senatorial biography underweights, but it cannot by itself disprove court repression or private wrongdoing.

## Key Characteristics

- Alexandrian Jewish philosopher and ancient witness.
- Preserver of a strongly favorable assessment of Tiberius.
- Near-contemporary visitor to Rome after Tiberius's death.
- Counterweight to hostile senatorial and biographical memory rather than an automatically neutral authority.

## Evidence

- Favorable assessment - [[535-emperors-of-rome-tiberius-slaughter-and-scandal-part-2-glt6029756405]] attributes to Philo praise for Tiberius's wisdom, learning, nobility, generosity, and peaceful rule.
- Evidentiary proximity - [[535-emperors-of-rome-tiberius-slaughter-and-scandal-part-2-glt6029756405]] notes Philo's presence in Rome shortly after the emperor's death.
- Competing reception - [[535-emperors-of-rome-tiberius-slaughter-and-scandal-part-2-glt6029756405]] uses his account to establish a positive tradition alongside Suetonius and Tacitus.

## Qualifications

The episode does not supply a full account of Philo's life, works, audience, political purposes, or relationship to Roman government. Proximity does not guarantee neutrality, and praise for peace and administration does not answer every allegation about dynastic repression, treason proceedings, Capri, or Sejanus.
