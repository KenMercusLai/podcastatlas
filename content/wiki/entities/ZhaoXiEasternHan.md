## Overview

赵熙 / 赵熹 / Zhao Xi appears as an Eastern Han minister whose visible value is practical order around vulnerable royal-family moments. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-680-ming-sang-chimei-zhe-wei-qiyi-huangdi-maigu-hechu-lurtvfqerb0vf3a33msmd-txr9pv|Hanji 680]] supplies the earliest current layer: after [[LiuXuanGengshiEmperor|更始帝刘玄]] is killed under [[ChimeiRebellion|赤眉]] control, Zhao Xi helps Liu Xuan's hungry relatives near Wuguan with money, food, and escort support. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-728-weihe-mingchen-nan-shanzhong-beihou-de-yuanyin-ni-gai-dong-ltjl2bgpyyz1bmwe-4mfcyd9e6cf|Hanji 728]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-732-donghan-guangwudi-liuxiu-jiabeng-bingsi-de-lnsu1le2x-cj75cjslysvbndeuvr|Hanji 732]] then show procedural order through royal fief-return advice and funeral-order repair.

## Current Profile

Hanji 680 shows Zhao Xi in a post-violence relief role. Liu Xuan's relatives are hungry near Wuguan after Liu Xuan's killing, and Zhao Xi helps them with money, food, and escort arrangements. The source uses the transcript form 赵熹; this page routes that notice to the existing Zhao Xi profile while preserving the variant in the title.

Hanji 728 shows Zhao Xi before [[LiuXiu|光武帝刘秀]]'s death. In 51 CE, Liu Xiu asks how to preserve the imperial enterprise permanently, and Zhao Xi advises that princes should return to their own fiefs. Liu Xiu begins carrying this out in winter by sending Lu King Liu Xing and Qi King Liu Shi to their fiefs.

Hanji 732 then shows Zhao Xi at the death-and-accession moment. After Liu Xiu dies, the crown prince and princes are unsure how to handle the funeral procedure because old ceremonial practice has been damaged by the Wang Mang disruption and later institutional discontinuity. Zhao Xi steps in to manage the funeral and make the hierarchy legible again.

The current synthesis is that Zhao Xi's remembered role is not policy authorship in the abstract. It is risk-reducing order: material escort for a fallen claimant's exposed relatives, territorial distance for adult princes before crisis, and ritual procedure for the heir and princes when the old ruler dies.

## Key Characteristics

- Eastern Han senior minister visible in late Guangwu and Mingdi accession-transition scenes.
- Provides material and escort support to vulnerable Liu Xuan relatives after Chimei violence.
- Advises prince-return-to-fief as a way to preserve imperial order.
- Functions as a ritual-procedure restorer after inherited Han ceremony has become uncertain.
- Stabilizes royal-family hierarchy through both territorial and ceremonial order.
- Represents ministerial memory as a practical resource when exposed kin, ruler, heir, and princes face unstable protocols.

## Qualifications

This page remains bounded to Hanji 680, Hanji 728, and Hanji 732. It does not reconstruct Zhao Xi's full career, precise office chronology across these notices, or broader political program. Hanji 680 supplies the relief-and-escort layer and uses the transcript form 赵熹; Hanji 728 supplies the prince-distance layer; Hanji 732 supplies the funeral-order layer.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 680 as Zhao Xi's earliest current Liu Xuan-family relief, money, food, and escort layer.
- Preserved Hanji 728 as Zhao Xi's royal fief-return advice layer.
- Reframed Zhao Xi's current profile around practical order across exposed kin, territorial distance, and accession ritual.
