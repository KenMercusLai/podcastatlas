# Passenger Pigeon

## Overview

The passenger pigeon was a North American pigeon species whose immense flocks and rapid extinction make it the cautionary counterpoint to the domesticated and feral pigeon history in [[216-pigeons-glt2770080110]].

## Current Profile

The episode describes passenger pigeons in billions, moving in flocks capable of darkening the sky and passing for days. That spectacle of abundance did not protect the species: mass shooting intensified in the late nineteenth century, and the population disappeared by the early twentieth century.

Within the current wiki evidence, the case demonstrates how visible abundance can obscure vulnerability when extraction scales faster than reproduction and recovery. The episode does not supply a complete ecological or economic causal history, so the profile remains a concise extinction case rather than a comprehensive species account.

## Key Characteristics

- North American pigeon distinct from domesticated homing pigeons.
- Formerly formed colonies and migratory flocks of extraordinary scale.
- Subjected to intensive mass shooting in the late nineteenth century.
- Extinct by the early twentieth century despite its earlier abundance.
- Used as a warning that abundance is not the same as resilience.

## Evidence

- Historical abundance: [[216-pigeons-glt2770080110]] invokes John James Audubon's account of a flock passing for three days and other descriptions of sky-darkening numbers.
- Destruction: [[216-pigeons-glt2770080110]] emphasizes mass killing between roughly 1870 and 1890 followed by disappearance around the turn of the century.
- Interpretive role: [[216-pigeons-glt2770080110]] places extinction beside pigeon domestication and modern urban disdain to expose unstable human-animal relationships.

## Qualifications

The episode gives a compressed popular account rather than a full analysis of habitat, breeding ecology, market hunting, technology, or final extinction chronology. Numbers, dates, and causal weighting remain source-scoped until supported by specialist evidence.
