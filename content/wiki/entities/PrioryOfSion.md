# Priory of Sion

## Overview

The Priory of Sion was a French association founded by [[PierrePlantard]] in 1956 whose later claim to be an ancient secret order guarding a sacred bloodline depended on forged documents and cumulative retelling.

## Current Profile

According to [[301-the-real-da-vinci-code-glt5654192229]], the registered organization took “Sion” from a hill near Annemasse, not from ancient Jerusalem. Its initial religious-charitable language sat beside mundane local concerns such as school-bus contracts and a housing dispute. Plantard later rebooted it as an esoteric chivalric fellowship and supplied it with fabricated genealogies and leader lists placed in the Bibliothèque nationale.

The apparent archive record then moved through Rennes-le-Château writing, Henry Lincoln's documentaries, *The Holy Blood and the Holy Grail*, and [[TheDaVinciCode|The Da Vinci Code]]. The institution's cultural importance therefore lies in how a small modern association acquired an invented medieval continuity, not in evidence for the ancient organization described by the conspiracy narrative.

## Key Characteristics

- Documented as a 1956 association rather than a secret society founded in 1099.
- Named for a hill near Annemasse rather than biblical Zion or crusader Jerusalem.
- Began with local civic and housing concerns rather than guardianship of a sacred bloodline.
- Acquired an esoteric, monarchic, and medieval identity through Plantard's later reinvention.
- Became globally recognizable after forged archival evidence was recycled through nonfiction, documentary, and fiction.

## Evidence

- Modern origin: [[301-the-real-da-vinci-code-glt5654192229]] dates the registered association to 1956 and locates its name and activities near Annemasse.
- Invented continuity: [[301-the-real-da-vinci-code-glt5654192229]] describes the early-1960s reboot, fabricated genealogies, grand-master lists, and archive deposits.
- Media transmission: [[301-the-real-da-vinci-code-glt5654192229]] traces the claim through Gérard de Sède, Henry Lincoln, *The Holy Blood and the Holy Grail*, and *The Da Vinci Code*.

## Qualifications

The source's strong conclusion about the Priory's modern origin is distinct from a complete organizational history. Membership, collaborators' precise roles, the legal character of each document, and the pathways by which each later author encountered the materials would require independent archival verification.
