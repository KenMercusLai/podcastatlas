# HeyTea / 喜茶

## Overview

HeyTea / 喜茶 is a Chinese freshly made tea-drink brand examined through overseas experience design and through its attempt to extend a store-born brand into bottled retail products.

## Current Profile

The U.S. evidence treats HeyTea's lab-style store as a localization attempt that explains tea, queue movement, drink production, space, and social use instead of assuming the category is already legible. The bottled-beverage evidence adds a different transfer problem: recognition and store-tested flavors can help a launch, but they do not guarantee that processing, packaging, price, shelf life, and supermarket demand will work.

HeyTea's early bottled sparkling-water experience is cautionary. The source says a product faced taste and packaging criticism and that its recall and destruction cost several million yuan. Later exploration moved toward fruit tea, light milk tea, and other formats. The combined profile is a brand with transferable consumer recognition but no automatic right to win when either geography or channel changes.

## Key Characteristics

- Uses store and service design to make new-style Chinese tea more understandable overseas.
- Carries brand recognition and store-tested flavors into packaged-product experiments.
- Learned from an early bottled sparkling-water attempt that lacked clear differentiation.
- Faces different translation problems in overseas stores and supermarket shelves.
- Must pair brand strength with product, supply-chain, and channel capability.

## Qualifications

The U.S. source provides practitioner interpretation rather than store-level customer research. The bottled source supplies no audited recall record, packaged-product sales, profitability, repeat-purchase, or channel inventory data. The reported several-million-yuan loss and product reception remain episode-scoped, and later category exploration does not establish commercial success.

## What Changed

- Migrated the profile to synthesis-v1 while preserving the original overseas evidence.
- Added bottled-beverage experimentation and the early product-failure boundary.
- Unified overseas and channel expansion as distinct forms of product and category translation.
