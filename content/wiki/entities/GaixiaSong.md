# 《垓下歌》 / Gaixia Song

《垓下歌》 / Gaixia Song enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-168-bawangbieji-qian-fashengle-shenme-1-lowxwc8oq8as1trjo-gjimmuhnr3|Hanji 168 part 1]] as the poem the host restores from [[Shiji|《史记》]] after noting that [[ZizhiTongjian|《资治通鉴》]] omits it from the Gaixia passage. The episode treats the poem as essential to the historical image of [[XiangYu|项羽]], because it turns the final defeat from a military result into a concentrated tragic scene.

The poem's force, in the source's reading, is contrast. It begins from extraordinary personal strength and immediately falls into failed timing, the immobility of the horse Zhui, and the impossible question of what to do with [[YuJi|虞姬]]. This contrast makes Xiang Yu look neither simply foolish nor simply heroic; it shows a commander whose personal grandeur cannot solve the political, military, and intimate consequences of defeat.

The episode also reads the poem as a command problem. Once Xiang Yu sings his despair openly and the people around him weep, the army's emotional condition becomes part of the loss. [[FourSidedChuSongs|四面楚歌]] damages morale from outside, but Gaixia Song lets the damage become visible from inside the commander himself.

## Key Claims
- The song preserves a layer of emotional and literary evidence that the compact chronicle version loses.
- It joins personal power, failed timing, the horse Zhui, and Yu Ji into one compressed image of irrecoverable defeat.
- The source treats the poem as tragic art and as a military liability: the commander's despair spreads through the camp.
- The poem is not used to exonerate Xiang Yu; it clarifies the emotional mechanism by which his force-centered world reaches its endpoint.
