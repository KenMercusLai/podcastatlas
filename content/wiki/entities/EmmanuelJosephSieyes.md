# Emmanuel-Joseph Sieyes

## Overview

Emmanuel-Joseph Sieyes was the cleric and political theorist whose pamphlet *What Is the Third Estate?* recast the common order as the complete French nation and helped turn the [[EstatesGeneral1789|Estates-General]] into a sovereignty crisis.

## Current Profile

The source presents Sieyes as the clearest theorist of the institutional break in June 1789. His argument that the Third Estate was socially everything, politically nothing, and entitled to become something rejected privileged orders as necessary components of the nation. On 10 June he proposed that the Third Estate summon the other deputies and then proceed without them; on 17 June the deputies declared themselves the [[NationalAssembly1789|National Assembly]].

## Key Characteristics

- Cleric elected as a representative of the Third Estate.
- Pamphleteer who identified productive society with the nation.
- Critic of hereditary privilege as an alien or diseased element within the body politic.
- Strategist of unilateral action after procedural deadlock.
- Central intellectual bridge from estate representation to national sovereignty.

### Nation and privilege

- [[478-the-french-revolution-showdown-in-versailles-part-4-glt4803391126]] attributes to *What Is the Third Estate?* the claim that the Third Estate formed a complete nation and that privileged people could join it only by abandoning privilege.

### Institutional break

- [[478-the-french-revolution-showdown-in-versailles-part-4-glt4803391126]] connects Sieyes's 10 June ultimatum to clerical defections and the 17 June declaration of the National Assembly.

## Qualifications

- The source supplies a narrative account rather than a complete reconstruction of Sieyes's constitutional thought or later career.
- The episode's language of disease and exclusion shows the radical edge of his rhetoric but does not establish one uncontested interpretation of the pamphlet.
