## Overview

夏侯渊 / Xiahou Yuan is a Cao-side commander whose current profile spans the Donghai surrender operation, one failed and one successful western relief response against [[MaChao|马超]], and his later death in the struggle for [[Hanzhong|汉中]]. The paired relief cases show that speed and initiative operate inside route, intelligence, local-alliance, and interception constraints rather than guaranteeing arrival.

## Current Profile

Hanji 1054 makes Xiahou Yuan the co-commander and operational frame for [[ZhangLiao|张辽]]'s surrender persuasion of Chang Xi. The detailed judgment belongs to Zhang Liao, but Xiahou Yuan participates in a force posture strong enough to make negotiation credible.

Hanji 1136 adds the failed relief of besieged Ji. [[WeiKangLateHan|韦康]] sends [[YanWenLateHan|阎温]] to seek Xiahou Yuan's help, and [[CaoCao|曹操]] reportedly orders Xiahou Yuan to move from Chang'an. The source says Ma Chao attacks the relief force and the Di leader Qianwan rises in support, after which Xiahou Yuan withdraws and Ji surrenders before help arrives. This case is not evidence of simple inactivity: the episode instead stresses distance, communication, multi-front commitments, approval delay, interception, and local force alignment.

Hanji 1140 adds a western command case. When the Qishan defenders request help against Ma Chao, Xiahou Yuan reportedly does not wait for approval from Cao Cao at Ye. He sends [[ZhangHeLateHan|张郃]] with five thousand troops, and Ma Chao withdraws before the relief force. The episode presents this as fast, delegated response; exact authority and numbers remain source-scoped.

Hanji 1105 supplies the later endpoint: Xiahou Yuan is identified as Cao Cao's long-serving kinsman and is killed during Liu Bei's Hanzhong campaign. The source treats that death as both a personal loss and a strategic reversal that Cao Cao cannot fully repair.

## Key Characteristics

- Cao-side military commander participating in the Donghai operation against Chang Xi.
- Provides the coercive command setting in which Zhang Liao's surrender persuasion becomes credible.
- Fails to reach besieged Ji after Ma Chao's interception and local support alter the relief conditions.
- Exercises or is credited with rapid western response authority during the Qishan crisis.
- Delegates the immediate relief mission to Zhang He rather than waiting for distant approval.
- Long-serving Cao family commander whose death marks the Hanzhong reversal.

- Failed Ji relief: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1136-ta-yong-ru-hanxin-que-yin-yige-jueding-quanjia-bei-sha-lky3lnnzjqlehbyyxhqfv73xpmtb|Hanji 1136]] says Cao Cao orders Xiahou Yuan to relieve Ji, but Ma Chao's attack and Qianwan's support force a withdrawal before the city surrenders.
- Rapid Qishan response: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1140-ziwo-renzhi-queshi-ruhe-huidiao-yidai-xiaoxiong-machao-lkpfzorf-o-zilyczvepedryc7ouk|Hanji 1140]] says Xiahou Yuan responds without waiting for Cao Cao and dispatches Zhang He to relieve the position.
- Hanzhong endpoint: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1105-guanyu-zhisi-maizang-le-caocao-zenyang-de-mengxiang-lgmk_dlknjpzz7fvzasno6tu7fs3|Hanji 1105]] says Xiahou Yuan is killed during Liu Bei's offensive and presents the loss as strategically and personally significant to Cao Cao.

## Qualifications

The four sources leave large chronological gaps and do not provide Xiahou Yuan's full career, exact kinship genealogy, title history, command jurisdiction, or detailed death circumstances. Hanji 1136's route, timing, authorization chain, force composition, Qianwan identification, battle mechanics, and withdrawal cause, and Hanji 1140's approval chain, relief-force total, and causal explanation for Ma Chao's withdrawal require primary-source comparison. The two relief outcomes differ in conditions and should not be reduced to a stable label of either slowness or speed.

## What Changed

- Added the unsuccessful attempt to reach Ji before Wei Kang's surrender.
- Contrasted the intercepted Ji operation with the later rapid Qishan delegation.
- Reframed relief performance as condition-sensitive rather than a fixed personal command trait.
