## Overview

鲁丕 / Lu Pi enters the wiki as an Eastern Han classicist in [[EmperorHeOfHan|Emperor He]]'s 99 CE court discussion. His current significance is a debate norm: classical disagreement should explain reasons, cite positions clearly, and avoid punishing imperfect speech when the goal is understanding.

## Current Profile

Hanji 772 says Emperor He summons scholars, including Lu Pi, [[JiaKuiEasternHan|Jia Kui]], and Huang Xiang, to discuss difficult points in the classics. Lu Pi's argument is not presented as a doctrine of a single text; it is a rule for disputation. Participants should not hide behind mutual deference, and those who question or answer should state the basis for their view.

The source says Emperor He approves Lu Pi's reasoning and rewards him after the court session. For the wiki, Lu Pi therefore supplies an Eastern Han institutional version of [[TruthSeekingDebateNorm|truth-seeking debate]]: disagreement is useful when it clarifies claims and evidence without converting every verbal error into punishment.

## Key Characteristics

- Eastern Han classicist whose current page is bounded to Emperor He's 99 CE court discussion.
- Argues that debate over classics should give reasons rather than perform empty courtesy.
- Links disagreement, source grounding, and tolerance for imperfect expression.
- Receives Emperor He's approval in the episode's account.

## Qualifications

This page is source-bounded to Hanji 772 and does not yet reconstruct Lu Pi's school affiliation, wider writings, or official career. The episode summary labels one later line "曹丕上书"; this page treats that as an ASR or shorthand error referring back to Lu Pi, not to the much later [[CaoPi|曹丕 / Cao Pi]].
