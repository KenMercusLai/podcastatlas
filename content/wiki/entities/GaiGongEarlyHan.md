# 盖公 / Gai Gong (early Han)

## Overview

盖公 / Gai Gong is the Jiaoxi-associated Huang-Lao teacher whom [[CaoShen|曹参]] consults while governing [[QiKingdomEarlyHan|齐国]].

## Current Profile

The current source presents Gai Gong through a single advice-and-reception scene. After competing proposals leave Cao Shen without a clear governing line, an unnamed recommender identifies Gai Gong as learned in Huang-Lao thought. Cao Shen sends gifts, receives him at [[Linzi|临淄]], houses him in the chancellor's residence, and treats him as a teacher.

Gai Gong's reported counsel combines quietness, natural responsiveness, non-disturbance, non-initiative, and observance of regular law. That places him behind [[HuangLaoQuietGovernance|黄老式清静治理]] as the intellectual bridge between Cao Shen's Qi administration and his later [[XiaoGuiCaoSuiInstitutionalContinuity|continuity policy]] at the Han court.

## Key Characteristics

- Identified by the episode as a Jiaoxi scholar of Huang-Lao thought.
- Consulted after Cao Shen hears many incompatible governing proposals in Qi.
- Advises quiet, non-disruptive, law-observing government responsive to the people and circumstances.
- Received with material respect, residence, and teacher status by Cao Shen.
- Presented as an intellectual source for a governing method later carried from Qi into central government.

## Evidence

Huang-Lao counsel and reception:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-196-jiexi-xiaogui-caosui-da-zhihui-lic6c_-juuvxxrpdwglnlqlgi_8o|Hanji 196]] says Cao Shen sends gifts to invite Gai Gong, receives his counsel in Linzi, houses him in the chancellor's residence, and adopts the teaching in Qi.

Influence on Cao Shen's governing line:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-196-jiexi-xiaogui-caosui-da-zhihui-lic6c_-juuvxxrpdwglnlqlgi_8o|Hanji 196]] connects the reported order in Qi with Cao Shen's later preference for restraint, continuity, and non-disturbance as Han chancellor.

## Qualifications

This is a one-source profile based on a structured popular-history summary, not a reconstructed biography or a direct reading of transmitted texts. Gai Gong's personal name, exact origin, wording, appointment-like status as “国师,” length of residence, and causal responsibility for Qi's order remain source-scoped. The page records the episode's intellectual genealogy without treating it as proof that one adviser alone designed early Han policy.
