## Overview

郑纯 / Zheng Chun enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-747-banchao-gaosu-ni-zhenzheng-de-congming-ren-dou-you-shiminggan-lj5z9tayvosa-jcufyxwuc35dzvi|Hanji 747]] as the Guanghan official whose rule in Yongchang makes frontier stability depend on clean administration and respect for local order. He is not developed as a court politician; his function is to anchor the contrast between durable local trust and the later successor who triggers Ailao rebellion.

## Current Profile

Hanji 747 says Zheng Chun first serves as western Yizhou commandant and governs cleanly while doing effective political instruction among local groups. Local Yi and Mo leaders admire him, present treasures, and submit to Han authority.

The same source says Emperor Ming had established Yongchang commandery and appointed Zheng Chun as administrator. Zheng Chun remains in office for ten years and dies there, after which a new official who lacks local understanding and relies on status display fails to hold the prior equilibrium. The contrast makes Zheng Chun a case for [[LightTouchFrontierGovernance|light-touch frontier governance]] through clean conduct, cultural knowledge, and tolerance of local power structures rather than through personal charisma alone.

## Key Characteristics

- Guanghan-origin Eastern Han frontier administrator.
- Clean official whose conduct makes submission and trust plausible in Yongchang.
- Political-instruction figure whose influence works through local recognition rather than rank alone.
- Stabilizer of a frontier commandery where local Yi and Mo leaders retain their own authority.
- Contrast case for successor failure after his death.

## Qualifications

The page is source-bounded to Hanji 747. It does not identify the successor by name, reconstruct Yongchang commandery's full history, or prove exactly which local customs Zheng Chun tolerated. The host's view that Zheng respected local traditions and implicitly allowed tribal leaders power is treated as an interpretation consistent with the episode, not as a separately verified institutional record.
