# Eric Johnson (choice researcher)

## Overview

Eric Johnson is a psychologist and decision researcher who presents [[ChoiceArchitecture]] as the unavoidable design surrounding a choice and examines how defaults, order, cues, complexity, and friction redirect behavior.

## Current Profile

In [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-434eaf8f105a4e37e33cf9afe4af65dd-52053896-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-434eaf8f105a4e37e33cf9afe4af65dd-52053896|Choose Carefully]], Johnson moves the analysis of decisions away from preference alone. Designers and institutions choose which options appear, how they are described and ordered, which option is preselected, and how much work each path requires. These decisions can influence what comes to mind, how much effort a chooser anticipates, and which strategy is used before any detailed comparison begins.

His ethical position follows from the claim that neutral non-design is impossible. Choice architecture can simplify a complex task, make a beneficial option easier, or slow an impulsive judgment; it can also exploit inattention and obstruct exit through [[DarkPatterns]]. The relevant question is therefore not whether influence exists, but whose interests the design serves, what it makes visible, and whether meaningful correction or exit remains available.

## Key Characteristics

- Defines choice architecture through option selection, order, description, defaults, cues, and action costs.
- Treats decision strategy as sensitive to anticipated effort and environmental complexity.
- Explains defaults through convenience, implied endorsement, and perceived possession rather than one universal mechanism.
- Connects cue accessibility and memory inhibition to ideas that feel self-generated.
- Argues that preferences are often constructed during the decision process.
- Treats beneficial simplification and manipulative obstruction as uses of the same general design capacity.

## Qualifications

This profile rests on one structured episode summary rather than Johnson's publications, full interview transcript, datasets, or a systematic review. The source does not establish his complete biography or research program. Reported effect sizes, costs, vote estimates, medical choices, and causal mechanisms remain episode-scoped, and examples involving performance, consumer behavior, public policy, or emergency response do not by themselves show that the same intervention will transfer across contexts.
