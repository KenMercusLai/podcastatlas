# Jeff Jarvis (EMS physician)

## Overview

Jeff Jarvis is the EMS physician and analyst in [[running-hot|Running Hot]] whose national-record analysis and local implementation case challenge routine lights-and-sirens use.

## Current Profile

Jarvis analyzes 7.5 million emergency records from the ESO dataset and compares warning-device use with potentially lifesaving interventions. The episode reports that lights and sirens were used en route for about 86 percent of calls while only 6.9 percent resulted in an intervention counted, under a generous definition, as potentially lifesaving.

His operational case complements that descriptive gap. Reducing warning-device use by about one-third reportedly increased median response time by only six seconds. He also names [[AlarmFatigue]] as a system problem: when every event is treated as an alarm, warnings lose meaning.

## Key Characteristics

- Uses large-scale EMS records to compare urgency signals with clinical intervention.
- Distinguishes dispatch categories from actual treatment delivered.
- Implements selective warning-device use and measures response-time change.
- Frames universal urgency as both a safety problem and an attention problem.
- Preserves uncertainty around whether rarer sirens would improve public response.

## Evidence

### National utilization analysis

- [[running-hot]] reports Jarvis's 7.5-million-record comparison of lights-and-sirens use with potentially lifesaving intervention.

### Local implementation

- [[running-hot]] attributes a roughly one-third reduction and six-second median response-time increase to his implementation case.

### Attention and warning value

- [[running-hot]] attributes the alarm-fatigue diagnosis to Jarvis while marking restored public responsiveness as an untested hypothesis.

## Qualifications

This page is based on one structured episode summary rather than Jarvis's full biography, the ESO analysis, intervention definitions, protocols, confidence intervals, or patient-outcome data. A small median delay does not show that every individual case was unaffected, and treatment frequency is not a complete measure of urgency.

## What Changed

- Established a bounded EMS data-and-implementation profile.
- Separated measured utilization and response-time claims from the untested attention hypothesis.
