# Catherine Khoury

## Overview

Catherine Khoury is the Georgetown student and unexpected defense witness whose admission and decision not to play tennis were central to [[USVKhoury|U.S. v. Khoury]].

## Current Profile

Catherine testified that she trained with the expectation of practicing with Georgetown's team while redshirting. That account conflicts with [[TimothyDonovan]]'s testimony that she was unqualified and never intended to play. She said she later abandoned tennis because her parents' breakup destroyed her relationship with [[AymanKhoury]], who had taught her the sport, rather than because of a planned false shoulder injury.

## Key Characteristics

- Learned tennis from her father at roughly age five or six and regarded it as a shared activity.
- Described herself as the sixth player on an eight-person high-school team.
- Expected to train and redshirt after encouragement from Georgetown coach Gordie Ernst.
- Was characterized by Donovan as unqualified for Georgetown-level tennis and never intending to compete.
- Produced contemporaneous messages documenting anger toward and separation from her father.
- Testified that emotional association with her father, not a shoulder injury, caused her to stop playing.

## Qualifications

The represented sources are sympathetic to the defense. Her testimony and messages directly challenge the injury theory, while Donovan's account conflicts with her stated expectations; the full examinations, exhibits, and opposing evidence are not available. Part 1 calls her Katherine, while Part 2 and the canonical page use Catherine.

## What Changed

- Added the explicit conflict between her testimony and Donovan's assessment of her original ability and intent.
- Preserved her role as an agent and witness rather than only the object of an admissions allegation.
