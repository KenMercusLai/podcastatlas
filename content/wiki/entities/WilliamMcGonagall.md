# William McGonagall

## Overview

William McGonagall was a Dundee hand-loom weaver, actor, and self-appointed poet remembered for verse judged unintentionally comic, especially his 1880 poem on the [[TayBridgeDisaster|Tay Bridge disaster]].

## Current Profile

The episode portrays McGonagall as both ridiculous and sad. With little formal education but strong enthusiasm for Shakespeare, he pursued acting and then experienced a poetic calling around age fifty. His walk to Balmoral and interpretation of a polite royal form letter as serious encouragement show persistence fused with grand self-misreading.

His Tay Bridge poem secured the reputation he wanted in a form he could not control. Forced rhymes, awkward meter, flat description, and a blunt recommendation for stronger construction made the poem comic precisely because the disaster killed everyone aboard. Later performances in which audiences pelted him with food complicate the joke: the public spectacle depended on humiliation, and the episode suggests he was not wholly unaware of the role he occupied.

## Key Characteristics

- Working-class Dundee weaver with limited schooling and intense Shakespearean enthusiasm.
- Performer remembered through anecdotes of theatrical self-importance.
- Late-blooming poet convinced of a calling and possible royal recognition.
- Writer whose technical weakness created durable unintended comedy.
- Public entertainment figure whose fame mixed agency, self-delusion, mockery, and humiliation.
