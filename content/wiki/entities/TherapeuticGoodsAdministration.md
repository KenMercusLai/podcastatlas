# Therapeutic Goods Administration / TGA

## Overview
The Therapeutic Goods Administration is Australia's national regulator for therapeutic goods. The source presents it as the institution that makes supplement oversight look drug-like while leaving efficacy largely unsupervised.

## Current Profile
According to the episode, Australia passed dedicated legislation in 1989 and gave the TGA responsibility for drugs and supplements together, similar in form to a combined food-and-drug authority, and every product sold, imported, or exported on the Australian market needs its own AUST number. The regulator sorts products into a listed and a registered track by efficacy claims and risk.

Most supplements are listed: they may use only TGA-permitted low-risk ingredients and low-level permitted claim language, the company applies for the number itself, and no pre-market review occurs - oversight is by post-market sampling. Prescription medicines, over-the-counter medicines, and a small number of higher-risk supplements are registered and require pre-market assessment of safety, quality, and efficacy; the source's illustration is calcium, where ordinary strength can be listed while high-concentration calcium needs a registered number. The same source reports roughly 10,000 complementary medicines in Australia by around 2010 with close to 98% listed, and relays TGA guidance that consumers who take supplements should mention them when talking to hospital staff about their medication, because supplement ingredients act on the body.

## Key Characteristics
- Regulates medicines and therapeutic supplements through one agency and one numbering system.
- Splits products into a self-assessed listed track and a pre-market-assessed registered track.
- Places the great majority of complementary medicines on the listed track.
- Controls permitted ingredients and claim wording rather than demonstrated benefit.
- Relies on post-market sampling instead of pre-market efficacy review for listed goods.
- Publishes consumer-facing warnings about supplement interactions and disclosure to clinicians.

## Qualifications
The page records a podcast-level description of the regime rather than the legislation itself, so categories, thresholds, and the L/R examples should be checked against current TGA guidance before being relied on. Complementary-medicine counts and the 98% listed share are episode figures, and the source does not describe enforcement intensity, listing-compliance failures, or reform debates.
