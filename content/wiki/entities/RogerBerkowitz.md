## Overview
Roger Berkowitz is the political theorist interviewed by [[DushuBuchenglin|独树不成林]] about his forthcoming book *A World We Share: Hannah Arendt and the Power of Friendship in a Broken World* and his reconstruction of friendship across political difference.

## Current Profile
Berkowitz reads [[HannahArendt|Hannah Arendt]] through correspondence, relationships, and political writing rather than through a single systematic doctrine. His account treats friendship as a demanding practice of conversation, time, presence, honesty, and respect, then uses that practice to criticize public denunciation, ideological sorting, expert rule, and one-directional persuasion. The episode presents his own experience of condemnation after speaking with an AfD-affiliated scholar as the immediate catalyst, so the argument is both interpretive and autobiographical.

## Key Characteristics
- Reconstructs an Arendtian theory of friendship from dispersed biographical and textual evidence.
- Defends relationships that can survive serious disagreement without reducing friendship to passive toleration.
- Distinguishes factual truth from plural political judgment and warns against political claims to final truth.
- Treats persuasion as reciprocal listening and partial revision rather than conversion of the mistaken.
- Connects private relational practices to the political task of sustaining a shared world.

## Qualifications
- The page records Berkowitz's interpretation of Arendt and his reported experience; it does not establish that this is the only defensible reading of her work.
- His critique of public denunciation does not by itself settle cases involving abuse, danger, strategic normalization, or deliberate falsehood.
- The episode names civic initiatives but supplies no comparative outcome evidence for them.
