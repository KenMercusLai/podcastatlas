# William III

## Overview

William III appears as the Dutch Protestant ruler crowned jointly with [[MaryII]] after the Glorious Revolution, when rapid coronation planning and a revised oath helped turn dynastic replacement into a public constitutional and religious settlement.

## Current Profile

The source presents William's coronation less as personal biography than as a legitimacy problem. James II had been displaced, the new monarchs ruled jointly, and the ceremony therefore had to make an unusual succession intelligible quickly. The oath's promise to defend the Protestant reformed religion established by law tied royal consecration to a confessional and legal order rather than leaving it as an unrestricted claim of sacred monarchy.

William also closes a longer episode-wide pattern: conquest, usurpation, childhood succession, Reformation, civil war, and restoration repeatedly force coronation to adapt. His ceremony retains inherited sacred form while publicly registering a changed basis of rule.

## Key Characteristics

- Dutch Protestant ruler who replaced James II alongside Mary.
- Joint monarch in an unusual double coronation.
- Beneficiary of a rapidly organized ceremony intended to stabilize a contested transfer.
- Oath-taker whose religious obligation was defined by law.

## Qualifications

The source does not provide a full account of the Glorious Revolution, William's Dutch career, parliamentary settlement, war, government, or personal relationship with Mary. Its claim that the coronation established legitimacy describes ritual purpose, not proof of universal acceptance or immediate political stability.
