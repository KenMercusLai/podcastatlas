# John Cade

## Overview
John Cade is the Australian psychiatrist and former prisoner of war used in the Huberman Lab bipolar-disorder episode to anchor the discovery of lithium's psychiatric treatment value.

## Current Profile
In the Essentials edit and full-length [[the-science-treatment-of-bipolar-disorder-scim7309473526]], Cade appears as a historically important clinician whose wartime observations and postwar experiments led toward [[LithiumBipolarTreatment]]. The episodes say he was a prisoner of war from 1942 to 1945, observed fellow prisoners moving among manic, depressed, and apparently normal states, and hypothesized that a chemical buildup might help explain those shifts. After the war, his animal and patient observations connected lithium to calming effects and reduced manic symptoms; the full episode identifies his 1949 paper on lithium salts and psychotic excitement as the publication milestone.

## Key Characteristics
- Australian psychiatrist and soldier whose wartime captivity shaped the clinical question he pursued after the war.
- Observed state shifts among prisoners and treated them as possible biological signals rather than only moral or character differences.
- Used imperfect animal experiments with patient urine, urea, uric acid, lithium urate, and lithium to search for a calming mechanism.
- Connected lithium to reduced manic symptoms in human patients after seeing lithium's calming effect in guinea pigs.
- Functions in the source as both a historical discovery figure and a reminder that psychiatric treatment clues can emerge from flawed but consequential clinical reasoning.
- Published the reported human-treatment results in 1949, according to the full episode.

## Evidence
- Wartime observation: [[essentials-the-science-treatment-of-bipolar-disorder-scim8821948371]] says Cade was a prisoner of war from 1942 to 1945 and observed prisoners shifting among manic, depressed, and normal states.
- Chemical hypothesis: [[essentials-the-science-treatment-of-bipolar-disorder-scim8821948371]] says Cade hypothesized that chemical buildup might contribute to the observed psychiatric states.
- Experimental path: [[essentials-the-science-treatment-of-bipolar-disorder-scim8821948371]] summarizes his postwar guinea-pig work using urine from manic and non-manic patients, then urea, uric acid, lithium urate, and lithium.
- Treatment signal: [[essentials-the-science-treatment-of-bipolar-disorder-scim8821948371]] says Cade found lithium alone calming in guinea pigs and later observed strong reductions in manic symptoms in human patients.
- Publication milestone: [[the-science-treatment-of-bipolar-disorder-scim7309473526]] identifies Cade's 1949 paper, “Lithium salts in the treatment of psychotic excitement,” as the report of his human results.

## Qualifications
The episodes present Cade's work as historically consequential, not as a model for modern clinical experimentation. They are derivative accounts rather than independent historical sources. Lithium's current use is treated as medically monitored because toxicity and blood-level control matter.
