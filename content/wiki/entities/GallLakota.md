# Gall

## Overview

Gall was a Hunkpapa [[LakotaPeople|Lakota]] leader presented as helping direct attacks against Calhoun Hill during the [[BattleOfLittleBighorn]] after members of his family were killed in [[MarcusReno]]'s opening assault.

## Current Profile

The episode places Gall alongside [[CrazyHorse]] as a leader who converted the encampment's numbers and confidence into coordinated pressure on Custer's separated positions. His family losses in Reno's attack give the narrative a direct personal context for his role, while the archaeological and testimonial record associates the collapse of Calhoun Hill with attacks from multiple directions.

Gall's presence also supports the episode's wider reframing: the battle was won through Native leadership and action, not merely lost through Custer's mistakes. Later reservation-era accommodation mentioned by related sources is outside this episode's main scope and should not be read backward as diminishing his battle role.

## Key Characteristics

- Hunkpapa Lakota leader within the larger battle coalition.
- Family losses during Reno's attack connected personal grief to the battle's escalation.
- Associated with attacks that helped break the Calhoun Hill position.
- Represents Native battlefield leadership in an account often organized around U.S. command failure.

## Evidence

- Personal context: [[453-custers-last-stand-the-final-showdown-part-8-glt8261745977]] says members of his family were killed in Reno's attack.
- Battlefield leadership: [[453-custers-last-stand-the-final-showdown-part-8-glt8261745977]] associates him with the assault on Calhoun Hill.
- Interpretive role: [[453-custers-last-stand-the-final-showdown-part-8-glt8261745977]] uses Gall, Crazy Horse, and Sitting Bull to reframe the result as a Native victory.

## Qualifications

- The episode does not provide a full biography or compare the complete body of testimony about Gall's movements.
- Exact tactical sequencing and the division of leadership among Native participants remain uncertain.
- Personal grief may be relevant without serving as a complete explanation of his conduct.

## What Changed

- Created a profile centered on Gall's battlefield agency rather than treating him as a background figure.
- Connected his role to the episode's Native-victory interpretation.
