# John F. Kennedy

## Overview

John F. Kennedy was the thirty-fifth president of the United States and the victim of the 22 November 1963 assassination in Dallas examined by this source.

## Current Profile

The episode uses Kennedy primarily as the victim half of a victim-killer comparison. It portrays him as a patriotic Cold War president shaped by wartime service, American global power, wealth, charisma, and political success. On that reading, the hosts find a CIA-style institutional motive to kill him less plausible than conspiracy narratives often imply.

Kennedy also matters as a symbol. His public glamour and apparently favored life sharpen the episode's speculative account of why [[LeeHarveyOswald|Lee Harvey Oswald]] might have selected him as a target, while the magnitude of his death helps explain why many later interpreters sought a cause larger than a lone alienated gunman.

## Key Characteristics

- U.S. president assassinated in Dallas on 22 November 1963.
- Cold War political leader portrayed by the episode as patriotic and committed to an American global role.
- Wealthy, charismatic public figure whose success made him a symbolically potent target.
- Victim whose stature encouraged explanations proportionate to national shock.
- Posthumous figure around whom conspiracy, cultural pessimism, and political counterfactuals accumulated.

## Qualifications

This page rests on one retrospective podcast episode focused on the assassination rather than a full presidential biography. Its description of Kennedy's politics is selective and designed mainly to test alleged assassination motives. The source does not establish what Kennedy would have done about Vietnam, civil rights, economic pressure, drugs, unrest, or later political polarization had he lived.
