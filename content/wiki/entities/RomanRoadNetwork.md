# Roman Road Network / 古罗马道路网

## Overview

The Roman road network was the engineered system of trunk roads, secondary routes, bridges, drainage, milestones, stations, and maintenance arrangements that connected Rome and the provinces of the [[RomanEmpire|Roman Empire]].

## Current Profile

The bounded episode presents the network primarily as military and administrative infrastructure. Roads enabled troops, officials, orders, and intelligence to move with greater predictability, while public-road law, graded construction standards, provincial administration, and service stations made that reach reproducible. The profile is not a claim that every Roman route was stone-paved, uniformly straight, free to use, or equally maintained.

## Key Characteristics

- Joined military mobility to provincial administration and official communication.
- Used differentiated construction, from earth and gravel routes to higher-grade stone surfaces with drainage and raised profiles.
- Combined legal minimums and access rules with route-specific engineering rather than imposing one identical road form everywhere.
- Used bridges, milestones, relay stations, lodging, horse changes, and repair services as parts of the network.
- Distributed financing and maintenance across central revenue, provincial administration, local beneficiaries, elite patronage, and selected toll points.
- Preserved route influence beyond antiquity even where surfaces, institutions, or alignments later changed.

## Evidence

- Military and administrative reach: [[8-gudai-de-gaosu-gonglu-qinhan-chidao-yu-luoma-dalu-lhldvw-jzrdg2kqafexwnzdidgli]] describes roads as a means of dispatching legions, suppressing revolt, reaching frontiers, and carrying public business.
- Engineering differentiation: [[8-gudai-de-gaosu-gonglu-qinhan-chidao-yu-luoma-dalu-lhldvw-jzrdg2kqafexwnzdidgli]] distinguishes earth, gravel, and stone roads and notes drainage, sidewalks or side paths, marsh foundations, bridges, and sometimes steep direct routes.
- Law and public access: [[8-gudai-de-gaosu-gonglu-qinhan-chidao-yu-luoma-dalu-lhldvw-jzrdg2kqafexwnzdidgli]] attributes minimum-width and failed-road access rules to the Twelve Tables while distinguishing public-road obligations from unrestricted free use.
- Measurement and services: [[8-gudai-de-gaosu-gonglu-qinhan-chidao-yu-luoma-dalu-lhldvw-jzrdg2kqafexwnzdidgli]] describes milestones, the Roman mile, official stations, horse changes, lodging, vehicle repair, and roadside private hospitality.
- Governance and finance: [[8-gudai-de-gaosu-gonglu-qinhan-chidao-yu-luoma-dalu-lhldvw-jzrdg2kqafexwnzdidgli]] describes central funding, provincial execution, elite contributions, local cost sharing, and tolls at bridges, gates, or strategic points.

## Qualifications

The total-length, paved-length, daily-speed, station-spacing, and exceptional journey figures come from one podcast summary and require specialist verification. Legal texts state norms, not uniform compliance, and surviving monuments overrepresent durable and high-status infrastructure. The comparison with [[QinChidao|秦汉驰道]] identifies shared functions but does not establish equal public access, finance, construction, or administrative organization.

## What Changed

- Created a focused profile separating the Roman road system from the [[RomanEmpire|Roman Empire]] as a whole.
- Distinguished the network from an image of universally stone-paved, free, or uniformly maintained roads.
- Integrated engineering, law, finance, milestones, relay services, and maintenance into one bounded profile.
