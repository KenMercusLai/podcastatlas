# Pasquale Paoli

## Overview

Pasquale Paoli was a Corsican nationalist and constitutional leader whose independence program, Enlightenment institutions, and paternal influence shaped the young Napoleon before their political rupture helped end the Bonapartes' future on the island.

## Current Profile

Part 1 presents Paoli as more than the opponent encountered at the end of Napoleon's Corsican career. He sought a modern [[Corsica]] with a constitution, university, and public institutions, admired the British constitutional model, resisted the French takeover, and lived in English exile before returning in 1790. [[NapoleonBonaparte|Napoleon]] revered him, tried to write Corsican history under his influence, and elevated him into an imagined father after [[CarloBonaparte|Carlo Bonaparte]] died. This personal devotion joined Paoli's political standing to [[CorsicanNationalism|Corsican national identity]].

Part 2 then makes the relationship's collapse decisive. The failed Sardinian expedition, local factional conflict, and Lucien Bonaparte's Jacobin denunciation intensified the break; Napoleon was arrested and pursued, and the family fled. Paoli thus represents both the institutional alternative that first formed Napoleon and the severed relationship that forced him toward revolutionary France.

## Key Characteristics

- Independence leader who joined national resistance to constitutional and educational institution-building.
- Admirer of the British constitutional model despite later French revolutionary hostility to Britain.
- Exile whose triumphant 1790 return reorganized Corsican revolutionary politics.
- Political and imagined paternal model for the young Napoleon.
- Later opponent whose break with the Bonapartes helped close their Corsican future.

## Qualifications

The sources remain Napoleon-centered rather than a complete biography of Paoli or audit of Corsican institutions. His constitutional program, British alignment, factional position, responsibility for acts against the Bonapartes, and private view of Napoleon require wider evidence. The father-figure framing describes Napoleon's attachment, not Paoli's own understanding of the relationship.

## What Changed

- Replaced a rupture-only profile with Paoli's earlier nationalist, constitutional, educational, and British-oriented program.
- Added the depth of Napoleon's personal and intellectual devotion before the political break.
- Preserved the later rupture as decisive without projecting it backward over the earlier relationship.
