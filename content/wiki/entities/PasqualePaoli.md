# Pasquale Paoli

## Overview

Pasquale Paoli was the Corsican leader who had served as a father figure to the young Napoleon before political conflict and the failed Sardinian expedition turned the Paolists against the Bonaparte family.

## Current Profile

In this source Paoli is the decisive figure in Napoleon's broken Corsican future. Lucien Bonaparte's Jacobin denunciation of him in Paris intensified the conflict after the failed expedition; Napoleon was arrested and pursued, and the family eventually fled the island. Paoli therefore functions both as a Corsican political actor and as the relationship whose collapse forced [[NapoleonBonaparte|Napoleon]] to seek identity and advancement in revolutionary France.

## Key Characteristics

- He represented Corsican authority and an early paternal model for Napoleon.
- He turned against the Bonapartes after the failed Sardinian expedition and growing factional conflict.
- Lucien's denunciation connected local rivalry to Jacobin politics in Paris.
- The rupture helped end the Bonaparte family's long residence and viable political future in Corsica.
