# Pasquale Paoli

## Overview

Pasquale Paoli was a Corsican nationalist and constitutional leader whose independence program, Enlightenment reputation, and personal charisma attracted European admirers including James Boswell and shaped the young Napoleon before their political rupture helped end the Bonapartes' future on the island.

## Current Profile

Paoli appears first through [[JamesBoswell]]’s 1760s visit as a revolutionary hero whose friendship, danger, classical aura, and liberty cause made Corsica emotionally and politically compelling to a British traveler. Boswell’s newspaper advocacy and successful book helped turn Paoli into a cause and public symbol in Britain, although the same activity also built Boswell’s own persona.

The Napoleon sources present Paoli as more than the opponent encountered at the end of Napoleon's Corsican career. He sought a modern [[Corsica]] with a constitution, university, and public institutions, admired the British constitutional model, resisted the French takeover, and lived in English exile before returning in 1790. [[NapoleonBonaparte|Napoleon]] revered him, tried to write Corsican history under his influence, and elevated him into an imagined father after [[CarloBonaparte|Carlo Bonaparte]] died. This personal devotion joined Paoli's political standing to [[CorsicanNationalism|Corsican national identity]].

Part 2 then makes the relationship's collapse decisive. The failed Sardinian expedition, local factional conflict, and Lucien Bonaparte's Jacobin denunciation intensified the break; Napoleon was arrested and pursued, and the family fled. Paoli thus represents both the institutional alternative that first formed Napoleon and the severed relationship that forced him toward revolutionary France.

## Key Characteristics

- Independence leader who joined national resistance to constitutional and educational institution-building.
- European liberty figure whose personal reception of Boswell helped carry the Corsican cause into British print and celebrity culture.
- Admirer of the British constitutional model despite later French revolutionary hostility to Britain.
- Exile whose triumphant 1790 return reorganized Corsican revolutionary politics.
- Political and imagined paternal model for the young Napoleon.
- Later opponent whose break with the Bonapartes helped close their Corsican future.

## Qualifications

The later sources remain Napoleon-centered, while the Boswell episode views Paoli through an admiring visitor invested in danger, liberty, classical virtue, and his own public image. His constitutional program, British alignment, factional position, responsibility for acts against the Bonapartes, and private views of Napoleon and Boswell require wider evidence. The father-figure framing describes Napoleon's attachment, not Paoli's own understanding of the relationship.

## What Changed

- Added Paoli’s role as a European liberty figure mediated to British audiences through Boswell’s visit, advocacy, book, and performance.
- Distinguished the Corsican cause from Boswell’s simultaneous self-promotion.
