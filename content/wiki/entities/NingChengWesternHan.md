## Overview

宁成 / Ning Cheng enters the wiki as an earlier Western Han harsh official whose intimidating checkpoint authority and powerful household are destroyed when [[YiZongWesternHan|义纵]] takes office in Nanyang.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-330-ta-weile-sharen-jing-xiwang-dongtian-bianchang-lluuwmkv4rjqgnvodkuzulb9ee3c|Hanji 330]] remembers Ning less through a complete career than through coercive reputation. As a checkpoint military leader, he is said to mistreat commoners and lower officials so severely that people would rather meet a mother tiger than face his anger. Yi Zong's investigation then turns the same punitive environment against Ning's family and ends in household extermination.

The bounded evidence supports a succession pattern, not a reform story: removing one feared official does not restrain coercive government when his replacement uses an even larger killing campaign elsewhere.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han harsh official presented through checkpoint authority and abusive enforcement.
- Feared by commoners and lower-ranking officials.
- Head of a locally powerful household vulnerable to a successor's investigation.
- Destroyed with his family after Yi Zong takes office in Nanyang.
- Example of coercive personnel replacement reproducing rather than ending harsh rule.

## Evidence

- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-330-ta-weile-sharen-jing-xiwang-dongtian-bianchang-lluuwmkv4rjqgnvodkuzulb9ee3c|Hanji 330]] says Ning's treatment of travelers and subordinate officials produces the mother-tiger comparison.

Household destruction:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-330-ta-weile-sharen-jing-xiwang-dongtian-bianchang-lluuwmkv4rjqgnvodkuzulb9ee3c|Hanji 330]] says Yi Zong investigates Ning's household after arriving in Nanyang and has the entire family destroyed.

## Qualifications

The current profile rests on one structured popular-history source. It does not reconstruct Ning Cheng's full office sequence, legal cases, family offenses, investigation procedure, checkpoint location, or the exact scope of the reported extermination. The mother-tiger saying is evidence of remembered reputation within the episode, not a direct measurement of everyday enforcement.
