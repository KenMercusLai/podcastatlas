# Brian Rudolph

## Overview
Brian Rudolph is the co-founder of [[Banza]], a chickpea-pasta company that began with his apartment-kitchen experiments after he associated wheat consumption with recurring sinus symptoms.

## Current Profile
Brian's profile combines high [[FounderProductFit]] with deliberate exposure to opportunity. He learned startup work at Venture for America and a Detroit technology company, developed pasta without formal food training, recruited his brother [[ScottRudolph]], and repeatedly used competitions, television, retailer meetings, and creator outreach to accelerate learning and distribution. The same account shows the cost of this intensity: failed factory runs and a later public safety allegation produced severe physical and emotional strain.

## Key Characteristics
- Turns a personal dietary constraint into iterative product development while keeping the medical inference qualified.
- Seeks rapid external feedback from operators, chefs, investors, buyers, creators, and customers.
- Accepts reputational and operating risk when an opportunity can materially increase learning or distribution.
- Treats manufacturing, packaging, retail execution, and crisis communication as founder-level work.
- Retrospectively favors tighter core-product focus and more visible communication under uncertainty.

## Qualifications
- The profile is based on Brian's retrospective founder account. It does not independently verify causation for his sinus symptoms, adjudicate the conflicting glyphosate tests, or isolate his contribution from Scott, employees, manufacturers, retailers, investors, and market tailwinds.

## What Changed
- Initial profile establishes Brian as Banza's experiment-led co-founder and chief narrator of its manufacturing and trust crises.
- Adds deliberate opportunity exposure as his explicit explanation for how effort can make favorable luck more likely.
- Adds a later-stage shift toward core-product focus and faster crisis communication.
