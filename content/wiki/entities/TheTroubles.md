# The Troubles

[[697-the-troubles-murder-in-belfast-part-1-glt4877520736]] treats the Troubles as rooted in partition, Protestant unionist state design, Catholic exclusion, rival historical memories, [[UnionistSiegeMentality|unionist siege fears]], and the 1966 loyalist killings tied to the [[UlsterVolunteerForce|UVF]]. It supplies the pre-1968 background for the later street-war threshold.

[[698-the-troubles-the-brink-of-civil-war-part-2-glt2308408988]] then treats the Troubles as entering a decisive threshold in 1968-1969, when civil rights protest, unionist backlash, police legitimacy collapse, loyalist violence, republican preparation, and British intervention combined into open conflict in [[NorthernIreland]].

[[699-the-troubles-return-of-the-ira-part-3-glt9519811642]] extends the branch into 1970-1971, when the [[ProvisionalIRA]], the [[FallsCurfew]], [[InternmentWithoutTrial]], [[OperationDemetrius]], loyalist vigilante expansion, and sectarian bombing moved the conflict into a self-reinforcing armed phase. The episode adds [[CounterinsurgencyLegitimacyTrap]] and [[AtrocityRetaliationCycle]] as the dominant mechanisms after troops had already arrived.

The three episodes do not frame the Troubles as inevitable ancient hatred. They stress that institutional discrimination and historical fear were real, but that actors such as [[TerenceONeill|Terence O'Neill]], [[IanPaisley]], [[GustySpence]], [[PeoplesDemocracyNorthernIreland|People's Democracy]], the [[RoyalUlsterConstabulary|RUC]], the [[UlsterVolunteerForce|UVF]], [[EdwardHeath]], [[BrianFaulkner]], the [[BritishArmy]], and Westminster made choices that changed the conflict's trajectory.
