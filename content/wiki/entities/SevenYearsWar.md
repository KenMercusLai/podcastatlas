# Seven Years' War

## Overview

The Seven Years' War is the global imperial conflict whose North American theater, the French and Indian War, reshaped British-colonial relations before the [[AmericanRevolution|American Revolution]].

## Current Profile

The episode treats British victory as the Revolution's structural turning point rather than a clean imperial triumph. France lost much of its North American position, but Britain inherited debt, Quebec's French Catholic population, western-security obligations, conflict involving Native nations, and the cost of regular troops. Colonists gained security from France yet became more willing to pursue western land, while Whitehall became more determined to regulate expansion, enforce trade rules, and collect revenue.

## Key Characteristics

- Global Anglo-French imperial conflict with a major North American theater.
- War in which colonial troops, British regulars, French forces, and Native alliances interacted.
- British victory that removed an external threat while enlarging administrative burdens.
- Source of postwar debt, permanent military presence, and revenue pressure.
- Precondition for the [[ProclamationLine1763|Proclamation Line of 1763]] and the later constitutional crisis.
