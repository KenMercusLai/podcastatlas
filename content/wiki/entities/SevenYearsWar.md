# Seven Years' War

## Overview

The Seven Years' War is the global imperial conflict whose North American theater, the French and Indian War, transferred Canada from French to British control and reshaped British-colonial relations before the [[AmericanRevolution|American Revolution]].

## Current Profile

The sources treat British victory as both a commercial-territorial transfer and the Revolution's structural turning point rather than a clean imperial triumph. The 1763 Treaty of Paris gave Britain legal possession of Canada and dominance over its [[BeaverFurTrade|beaver fur trade]]. France lost much of its North American position, but Britain inherited debt, Quebec's French Catholic population, western-security obligations, conflict involving Native nations, and the cost of regular troops. Colonists gained security from France yet became more willing to pursue western land, while Whitehall became more determined to regulate expansion, enforce trade rules, and collect revenue.

## Key Characteristics

- Global Anglo-French imperial conflict with a major North American theater.
- War in which colonial troops, British regulars, French forces, and Native alliances interacted.
- British victory that removed an external threat while enlarging administrative burdens.
- Transfer point for British control of Canada and the Canadian beaver trade.
- Source of postwar debt, permanent military presence, and revenue pressure.
- Precondition for the [[ProclamationLine1763|Proclamation Line of 1763]] and the later constitutional crisis.

## Evidence

- Imperial transformation: [[347-the-american-revolution-part-1-glt3042850673]] links victory over France to a larger British territorial and administrative problem.
- Colonial participation: [[347-the-american-revolution-part-1-glt3042850673]] places [[GeorgeWashington|George Washington]] and [[BenjaminFranklin|Benjamin Franklin]] inside Braddock's failed 1755 expedition.
- Postwar consequences: [[347-the-american-revolution-part-1-glt3042850673]] connects the war to debt, Pontiac's rebellion, western settlement limits, regular troops, and taxation.
- Canadian commercial transfer: [[285-canada-beaver-wars-glt6084067740]] links Wolfe's capture of Quebec and the 1763 Treaty of Paris to British possession of Canada and control of its fur trade.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to two podcast accounts of the war's North American commercial, territorial, and revolutionary consequences. It does not provide a complete European, Caribbean, Indian, maritime, French, or Native history, and the sources' fiscal totals, legal shorthand, and causal weighting remain source-scoped.
