## Overview

李轶 / Li Yi is a Gengshi-side figure in the Guangwu accession sequence whose current wiki profile is bounded to [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-676-hanguangwudi-liuxiu-jiedao-sharen-shijian-shimo-luebqlivstmkjaqqfdlcukxtnmii|Hanji 676]]. The source calls him 五阳王李毅; the wiki normalizes him as 李轶 in the Liu Xiu-Gengshi conflict and keeps him distinct from [[LiYiEasternHan|李邑 / Li Yi]], the later Western Regions envoy.

## Current Profile

Hanji 676 presents Li Yi as exposed because he had participated with [[ZhuWeiEasternHan|朱鲔]] in the killing of Liu Xiu's elder brother. When [[FengYiEasternHan|冯异]] writes to him and analyzes the collapsing Gengshi field, Li Yi answers with pro-Han-restoration language and begins corresponding with Feng Yi rather than fighting him.

That ambiguous cooperation helps Feng Yi expand, but it does not make Li Yi safe. Liu Xiu judges him crafty, circulates Li Yi's letters to commandery officials, and turns the correspondence into a warning signal. Once Zhu Wei hears of the letters, he suspects Li Yi and sends assassins against him, producing fear in [[LuoyangLateHan|洛阳]] and pushing more people toward surrender.

Li Yi therefore functions as a pivotal but unstable intermediary. His private letter channel is useful to Feng Yi, threatening to Zhu Wei, and politically usable by Liu Xiu. The page treats him as a source-scoped Gengshi general rather than a full Eastern Han official biography.

## Key Characteristics

- Gengshi-aligned figure whose past role in Liu Yan's death makes reconciliation with Liu Xiu insecure.
- Wavering correspondence partner who stops fighting Feng Yi and gives Liu Xiu's side operational space.
- Author of letters that become political evidence after Liu Xiu circulates them outside their original channel.
- Suspicion target whose exposure destabilizes Zhu Wei's Luoyang camp.
- Source-scoped identity kept separate from the later Western Regions Li Yi / 李邑.

## Qualifications

The current page is bounded to one podcast source and should not reconstruct Li Yi's full biography, title history, or exact death record. The transcript form 李毅 and title 五阳王 are treated as ASR-sensitive; the Guangwu-Gengshi context supports normalization to 李轶, while [[LiYiEasternHan|李邑 / Li Yi]] remains a distinct later figure.
