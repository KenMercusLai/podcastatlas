# U.S. Customs and Border Protection

## Overview
U.S. Customs and Border Protection is a Department of Homeland Security agency represented in the wiki through immigration-enforcement training and southern-border surveillance accountability.

## Current Profile
The ICE hiring source places CBP in a rapidly scaling federal enforcement ecosystem where classroom training, field culture, supervision, and accountability shape conduct. The border-tower investigation adds CBP as operator and steward of a multigenerational surveillance network using cameras, thermal imaging, radar, and AI classification.

The resulting profile is not simply technology adoption. CBP must connect tower placement, detection, classification, monitoring, operational decisions, response, and outcome measurement. Its reported failure to track whether towers detected people before their deaths leaves the agency unable to show whether a system promoted partly for migrant assistance delivers that benefit.

## Key Characteristics
- DHS enforcement agency adjacent to Border Patrol and ICE.
- Participant in a federal training and field-culture system where accountability extends beyond classroom hours.
- Steward of southern-border surveillance infrastructure spanning cameras, thermal imaging, radar, and AI-powered towers.
- Safety-critical operator whose humanitarian performance depends on both technology and human response workflows.
- Institution with an outcome-measurement gap around detection before death, mortality, and technology-linked apprehension results.

## Qualifications
The hiring source does not provide a standalone CBP institutional profile. The tower source cannot determine the failure mode for individual deaths, and CBP did not answer most detailed questions; its quoted position emphasizes that humans make final decisions. Range, visibility, detection, intervention feasibility, and causation must remain distinct.
