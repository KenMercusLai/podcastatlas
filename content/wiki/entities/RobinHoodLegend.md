# Robin Hood (Legend)

## Overview

Robin Hood is an English outlaw hero whose surviving medieval profile combines yeoman status, devotion, courtesy, archery, trickery, resistance to selected authorities, and extreme violence; later literature and media remake him as a dispossessed nobleman, romantic patriot, and redistributive champion.

## Current Profile

[[192-robin-hood-glt1296880830]] does not identify one historical Robin Hood. It treats *Piers Plowman*, *Robin Hood and the Monk*, *A Gest of Robin Hood*, early plays, legal names, and outlaw culture as evidence that a recognizable hero was widely known by the later Middle Ages, while preserving the gap between a circulating name and a recoverable biography.

The profile is therefore best understood through [[RobinHoodLegendAccretion]]. The medieval outlaw is not politically modern or morally clean: he may help a poor knight and oppose greedy clerics, yet his stories also contain killing, beheading, mutilation, deception, and unexplained outlawry. Early modern performance, antiquarian collection, Romantic literature, children's publishing, Hollywood, television, and political argument then add the familiar Marian romance, noble birth, royal loyalty, Saxon resistance, philanthropy, costume, and family-friendly tone.

## Key Characteristics

- Late-medieval ballad and performance figure whose exact origin remains unresolved.
- Yeoman outlaw in early material, not securely an Earl of Huntingdon or returning crusader.
- Skilled archer and trickster associated with Little John, Much, Nottingham, woodland, disguise, and contests.
- Devotee of the Virgin Mary whose religious layer complicates later secular and Protestant retellings.
- Selective defender of poor or vulnerable people rather than a documented systematic redistributor.
- Violent and morally ambivalent hero whose early actions resist modern children's-story sanitization.
- Adaptable national and global icon repeatedly recoded through literature, film, television, and politics.

## Evidence

### Medieval circulation and social identity

- [[192-robin-hood-glt1296880830]] places a familiar Robin Hood reference in *Piers Plowman*, presents *Robin Hood and the Monk* as an early surviving ballad, and describes the *Gest* as a major late-medieval synthesis.

### Trickery, devotion, and violence

- [[192-robin-hood-glt1296880830]] connects Marian devotion and Mass attendance to capture and rescue by disguise, while preserving the killings of the monk and page and the mutilation of Guy of Gisborne.

### Qualified aid to the poor

- [[192-robin-hood-glt1296880830]] distinguishes the *Gest*'s praise of doing poor men good and helping an exploited knight from the later universal slogan about robbing rich people for poor people.

### Later reconstruction

- [[192-robin-hood-glt1296880830]] traces aristocratic, romantic, national, philanthropic, visual, and family-friendly attributes through Munday, Ritson, Scott, Peacock, Pyle, Hollywood, and television.

### Historical uncertainty

- [[192-robin-hood-glt1296880830]] reviews legal-record candidates, generic outlaw naming, wider outlaw-hero traditions, and French-English literary explanations without choosing a single real person.

## Qualifications

The page describes one podcast episode's synthesis rather than a complete scholarly survey of Robin Hood studies. Text dates, candidate identities, borrowing routes, religious interpretation, political meaning, and the origins of individual motifs remain disputed or source-scoped. Calling the legend accretive does not make every later element false or culturally trivial; it distinguishes when features become visible and how later audiences reorganize them.
