## Overview

石显 / Shi Xian enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-499-liubang-fuwoqilai-dasi-liushi-zhegezeng-zengsunzi-lndfgjehpe7qc36hejxba3znlz0p|Hanji 499]], [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-500-taijian-shixian-zhisi-zhan-de-yuegao-si-de-yuecan-lvu7w8xa8mjn8_mbxew7e4afupra|Hanji 500]], [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-501-hanyuandi-jing-ba-taijian-dang-fuqin-lml1qwuw3iujobnltbjymeyojd4g|Hanji 501]], and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-502-rang-laoban-bei-heiguo-shi-shui-zai-zhaosi-ls833sgrdjz9e1tupiiqillagohb|Hanji 502]] as the [[HanYuandi|汉元帝]]-era eunuch power holder whose rise illustrates Yuan's governing weakness and whose fall creates the first [[HanChengdi|汉成帝]] cleanup and personnel-reallocation field. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-497-huangniba-diao-kudang-xihan-mingjiang-chentang-dangguan-you-duo-nan-liz2ssrmijhowebl5hhx8-61ybmp|Hanji 497]] adds a more personal pre-fall layer: Shi Xian opposes reward for [[GanYanshouWesternHan|甘延寿]] and [[ChenTangWesternHan|陈汤]] after Gan refuses to join Shi's family network through marriage.

## Current Profile

Hanji 499 supplies the broad political diagnosis: Shi Xian's fifteen-year influence under Yuan is treated as the rare Western Han case of eunuch power and as one accelerator of late-Western-Han corruption. Hanji 500 then supplies the institutional formation layer before the cleanup. Shi Xian is presented as a punished young offender who became a eunuch, started from palace message relay, and rose because he combined legal knowledge, cultural literacy, work capacity, and close information access. Hanji 501 adds the affective formation layer: Shi Xian reads Yuan's need for companionship, music, reliability, and confession-coded sincerity, then turns emotional access into political dependence. The sources therefore treat him as vicious and manipulative, but not incompetent or merely flattering.

Hanji 501 also adds the early elimination pattern. Shi Xian treats [[XiaoWangzhiWesternHan|萧望之]] as dangerous because Xiao has ability and opposes Yuan's favor toward him. In the episode's account, Shi Xian uses Xiao's pride and Yuan's resentment toward his teacher to help produce Xiao's suicide, after which Shi Xian faces less effective resistance.

Hanji 497 adds that Shi Xian's opposition can also work through personal relationship failure, not only institutional access. The host reads his resistance to Gan Yanshou and Chen Tang's fuller reward as retaliation after Gan rejects a marriage tie to Shi's family. This keeps Shi Xian's profile from becoming purely structural: private resentment and network-building ambitions sit beside document-channel power.

Once Chengdi accedes, Shi Xian's previous concentration of power, posts, and benefits becomes a vacancy field. Hanji 500 says Chengdi first moves him away from the inner document channel to Changxin zhong taipu; Kuang Heng and Zhang Tan then impeach him after the political wind changes. Hanji 502 emphasizes the downstream cleanup politics: people who could not gain under the old arrangement now have reason to support removal, while former accommodators become exposed.

Shi Xian's fall also creates a speech trap for senior ministers. Criticizing him after the fact may be politically necessary, but explaining his former dominance can make Emperor Yuan look weak or culpable. In Hanji 502, that is the opening [[WangZunWesternHan|王尊]] uses against [[KuangHengWesternHan|匡衡]] and [[ZhangTanWesternHan|张谭]].

## Key Characteristics

- Yuan-era court power holder treated by Hanji 499 as a rare Western Han eunuch-power case and sign of political decline.
- Legally trained and administratively useful eunuch whose influence rests on ability, message-channel access, and flattery.
- Emotional-access actor who builds Yuan's trust through music, companionship, reliability performance, and controlled confession.
- Opponent-removal actor whose early clash with Xiao Wangzhi shows trusted access becoming coercive power.
- Reward-opposition actor whose Gan Yanshou conflict shows private alliance failure entering court judgment.
- Former monopolizer of power, positions, and benefits whose fall marks early Chengdi cleanup.
- His ruler-dependent collapse produces a predecessor-blame problem: losing Yuan and being moved by Chengdi turns his old network into liability, while criticizing him can indirectly dishonor Emperor Yuan.

- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-500-taijian-shixian-zhisi-zhan-de-yuegao-si-de-yuecan-lvu7w8xa8mjn8_mbxew7e4afupra|Hanji 500]] says Shi Xian was literate, legally knowledgeable, capable in office, and more useful than a simple flatterer.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-500-taijian-shixian-zhisi-zhan-de-yuegao-si-de-yuecan-lvu7w8xa8mjn8_mbxew7e4afupra|Hanji 500]] says his early message-relay work mattered because it involved deciding what information and wording reached the emperor.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-501-hanyuandi-jing-ba-taijian-dang-fuqin-lml1qwuw3iujobnltbjymeyojd4g|Hanji 501]] says Shi Xian used Yuan's love of music, illness-time vulnerability, and need for understanding to build private emotional trust.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-501-hanyuandi-jing-ba-taijian-dang-fuqin-lml1qwuw3iujobnltbjymeyojd4g|Hanji 501]] says Shi Xian strengthened trust by appearing reliable in affairs and by confessing small faults in a way that made later accusations less persuasive to Yuan.

Opponent removal:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-497-huangniba-diao-kudang-xihan-mingjiang-chentang-dangguan-you-duo-nan-liz2ssrmijhowebl5hhx8-61ybmp|Hanji 497]] says Shi Xian resists fuller reward for Gan Yanshou and Chen Tang because Gan had refused a marriage alliance with Shi's family.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-501-hanyuandi-jing-ba-taijian-dang-fuqin-lml1qwuw3iujobnltbjymeyojd4g|Hanji 501]] says Shi Xian targets Xiao Wangzhi as an early opponent and exploits Xiao's pride plus Yuan's resentment to help drive Xiao's suicide.

Collapse and cleanup:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-500-taijian-shixian-zhisi-zhan-de-yuegao-si-de-yuecan-lvu7w8xa8mjn8_mbxew7e4afupra|Hanji 500]] says Chengdi moves Shi Xian to Changxin zhong taipu, after which Kuang Heng and Zhang Tan impeach him.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-500-taijian-shixian-zhisi-zhan-de-yuegao-si-de-yuecan-lvu7w8xa8mjn8_mbxew7e4afupra|Hanji 500]] says Shi Xian and his party are removed, Shi Xian's family is sent home, and he dies on the road after despair and refusing food.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-502-rang-laoban-bei-heiguo-shi-shui-zai-zhaosi-ls833sgrdjz9e1tupiiqillagohb|Hanji 502]] says his prior monopoly over power, position, and benefits left others without access, so his removal opens opportunities.

Predecessor-blame danger:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-502-rang-laoban-bei-heiguo-shi-shui-zai-zhaosi-ls833sgrdjz9e1tupiiqillagohb|Hanji 502]] says Wang Zun attacks Kuang Heng and Zhang Tan for language that makes officials' fear of Shi Xian appear greater than fear of the emperor.

## Qualifications

This page is source-scoped to Hanji 499, Hanji 500, Hanji 501, Hanji 502, and Hanji 497. It does not settle Shi Xian's full biography, exact legal record, full party network, degree of responsibility for Xiao Wangzhi's death or other loyalist deaths, personal psychology, Gan Yanshou marriage-alliance details, or the complete institutional boundaries of the Zhongshu and palace relay posts. Hanji 501's reading of Yuan's emotional dependency is treated as interpretive rather than settled biography.
