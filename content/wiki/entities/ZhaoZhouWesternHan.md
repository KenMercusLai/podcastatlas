## Overview

赵周 / Zhao Zhou is a Western Han chancellor whose current source arc runs from appointment through inherited loyalty credentials to prosecution and suicide in the [[ZhoujinForfeiture|酎金夺爵]] case.

## Current Profile

Hanji 340-2 supplies Zhao Zhou's entry into the chancellorship. It says the office conventionally requires a marquis title and explains his eligibility through family political capital: his father 赵夷吾 had opposed participation in the Rebellion of the Seven States, was killed, and was later remembered by the court as loyal. The son therefore receives a title and becomes available for appointment. The account supports an inherited-recognition mechanism, but not a claim that ancestry alone caused the appointment.

Hanji 353-2 supplies the terminal accountability profile. Zhao Zhou does not administer the ritual-gold inspection in that source's account, but his office makes knowledge and nonintervention punishable once the court treats deficient contributions as disrespect. The episode expands this into a broader interpretation: lack of initiative during the [[Nanyue|南越]] mobilization and failure to police the marquises make him vulnerable when [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] turns against elite nonresponse. The direct charge and inferred purge motive remain distinct.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han chancellor whose appointment follows enfeoffment as a marquis.
- Presented as benefiting from court recognition of his father's fatal opposition to the Rebellion of the Seven States.
- Serves through the later Nanyue mobilization and ritual-gold case.
- Held responsible for knowing that marquises' ritual contributions were underweight.
- Imprisoned after 106 marquises lose their titles.
- Dies by suicide rather than surviving the prosecution.

## Evidence

Appointment and inherited political capital:

Charge and death:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-353-2-hanwudi-zhoujin-duojue-beihou-de-yinmou-llzksgz2rjcpyjtp1fxjdtmh8q3p|Hanji 353-2]] says Zhao Zhou is accused of knowing that the marquises' ritual gold was light, is imprisoned, and dies by suicide.

Political interpretation:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-353-2-hanwudi-zhoujin-duojue-beihou-de-yinmou-llzksgz2rjcpyjtp1fxjdtmh8q3p|Hanji 353-2]] connects his fall to inactivity during the Nanyue mobilization and to Wudi's wider attack on the marquisate group.

## Qualifications

This profile rests on two short popular-history episodes. It does not reconstruct Zhao Zhou's complete career, the formal basis and timing of his title, precise duty over ritual gold, evidentiary proceedings, prison conditions, or private intention. Hanji 340-2's transcript appears to conflate several Seven States names; 赵怡武 is normalized to 赵夷吾, but the corrupt楚王刘武 form is not promoted. Hanji 353-2's political-motive explanation remains interpretation rather than a demonstrated confession or secret order. Both episodes' “赵州” is normalized to 赵周.

## What Changed

- Extended the profile backward from Zhao Zhou's prosecution to his enfeoffment and appointment as chancellor.
- Added inherited loyalty recognition as a qualification for office without treating it as the sole cause of appointment.
