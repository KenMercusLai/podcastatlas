# Robert Sheldon Brown

## Overview

Robert Sheldon Brown, known as “Casper,” is presented in [[the-tipping-point-revisited-an-excerpt|The Tipping Point Revisited: An Excerpt]] as a central organizer of Los Angeles bank robberies and an example of an unusually influential actor in a social epidemic.

## Current Profile

The episode says Brown worked with [[DonzellThompson|Donzell Thompson (“Sea Dog”)]] to choose targets, obtain vehicles, recruit crews, train participants, and divide proceeds while remaining away from the banks. This separation of planning from execution turned individual offending into a repeatable organization and supports the episode's [[BoundedSocialContagion]] argument.

## Key Characteristics

- Organized target selection, logistics, recruitment, and training rather than personally executing every robbery.
- Treated participants as replaceable crews within a scalable operating model.
- Is associated by the episode with 175 robberies over four years.

## Evidence

### Organizational scale

- [[the-tipping-point-revisited-an-excerpt]] describes Brown and Thompson building crews, arranging vehicles and hideouts, and retaining control over planning and proceeds.

### Epidemic influence

- [[the-tipping-point-revisited-an-excerpt]] links their model to later takeover crews and reports a substantial robbery decline after their arrests.

## Qualifications

This profile relies on one structured episode summary rather than court records, FBI files, interviews, or an independent criminal history. The reported robbery count, recruitment details, age of participants, division of proceeds, influence on other crews, and causal role of the arrests remain episode-scoped. “Super spreader” is an explanatory analogy, not a medical classification or proof that one person caused the wider crime trend.

## What Changed

- Established Brown's organizational role as distinct from a lone prolific robber's direct repetition.
