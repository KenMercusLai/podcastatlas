# Andrew Carnegie

## Overview

Andrew Carnegie is presented as a Scottish-born American industrialist and philanthropist whose life makes the nineteenth-to-twentieth-century transfer of economic power from Britain to the United States tangible.

## Current Profile

Born in Dunfermline in 1835, Carnegie emigrated with his family to Pittsburgh in 1848 and rose from wage labor and telegraph work into industrial capitalism. The episode characterizes him as both a major monopolist and an advocate of the “Gospel of Wealth”: private accumulation was to be followed by philanthropic expenditure intended to help others improve themselves.

After selling his companies to J. P. Morgan in 1901, he funded libraries, parks, schools, and institutions on both sides of the Atlantic. His 1905 gift of a Diplodocus cast to London's Natural History Museum became an explicit symbol of Anglo-American connection, making philanthropy part of [[AngloAmericanPowerReversal]] rather than merely a personal afterlife to wealth.

## Key Characteristics

- Scottish emigrant whose industrial career was made in the United States.
- Symbol of American economic power overtaking British leadership.
- Large-scale capitalist and monopolist rather than an uncomplicated benefactor.
- Advocate of wealth accumulation followed by improvement-oriented philanthropy.
- Funder of libraries, parks, schools, and institutions in America and Britain.
- Promoter of Anglo-American affinity and peace through symbolic gifts.

## Evidence

- Migration and ascent: [[263-usa-vs-england-the-200-year-rivalry-glt7546211559]] follows Carnegie from Dunfermline and Pittsburgh wage work to industrial dominance.
- Wealth doctrine: [[263-usa-vs-england-the-200-year-rivalry-glt7546211559]] summarizes *The Gospel of Wealth* as accumulation followed by philanthropic use.
- Transatlantic philanthropy: [[263-usa-vs-england-the-200-year-rivalry-glt7546211559]] records support for institutions in both the United States and Britain after the 1901 sale.
- Symbolic diplomacy: [[263-usa-vs-england-the-200-year-rivalry-glt7546211559]] describes the 1905 Diplodocus cast as Carnegie's emblem of connection between the two countries.

## Qualifications

The episode uses Carnegie primarily as a symbol of power transfer and does not provide a full labor, corporate, antitrust, or philanthropic history. The moral adequacy of his wealth doctrine, the effects of monopoly, and the influence of individual gifts on Anglo-American politics require evidence beyond this source.

## What Changed

- Established Carnegie as a transatlantic economic-power and philanthropy case.
- Preserved the tension between monopoly-based accumulation and public benefaction.
