## Overview

谷吉 / Gu Ji enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-474-guji-kangkai-fusi-wo-weihe-renwei-ta-yuchun-lp-07zvaxr21dt2mtva2ndp6b9by|Hanji 474]] as the Western Han escort envoy sent after [[HanYuandi|汉元帝]] agrees to return [[ZhizhiChanyu|郅支单于]]'s hostage son. Against [[GongYuWesternHan|贡禹]] and [[KuangHengWesternHan|匡衡]]'s advice to stop at the frontier, Gu Ji insists on going to the chanyu's court and is killed with his party.

## Current Profile

The episode treats Gu Ji less as a biographical subject than as a decision case. He understands that death is possible and even presents that death as a useful sacrifice, but the host argues that this confuses willingness to die with statecraft. Because Zhizhi Chanyu's hostility is already visible, Gu Ji's deeper entry is read as avoidable exposure rather than necessary service.

Gu Ji's death is also the causal bridge to the later Zhizhi-campaign branch. Hanji 489 and later episodes use the killing of the envoy party as part of Zhizhi Chanyu's threat dossier; Hanji 474 supplies the earlier decision path that made the envoy party vulnerable.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han envoy assigned to escort Zhizhi Chanyu's hostage son after Han Yuandi releases him.
- Rejects advice to stop at the frontier and insists on reaching the chanyu's court.
- Presents possible death as a sacrifice that could trade one envoy's life for wider security.
- Is killed by Zhizhi Chanyu along with his party after reaching the court.
- Functions as the episode's main example of courage without sufficient planning.
- Serves as the direct envoy-killing cause behind later Han retaliation against Zhizhi Chanyu.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 474. It does not reconstruct Gu Ji's full career, exact rank beyond the episode's escort-envoy role, original memorial wording, the full Han deliberation, or the complete diplomatic protocol around hostage return. Later sources mention the envoy killing as part of Zhizhi Chanyu's threat record, but this page currently uses Hanji 474 as the direct evidence for Gu Ji's decision and death.
