# 9to5

## Overview
9to5 is presented as the clerical-worker labor organization that turned secretaries' workplace complaints into organizing, union activity, public campaigns, and the movement base for [[NineToFiveFilm|9 to 5]].

## Current Profile
The episode describes 9to5 as an organization born from office workers comparing everyday mistreatment and seeing a shared labor pattern. Its name came from the typical clerical workday, but its demands went beyond hours: job postings, higher pay, pregnancy protection, child care, sexual-harassment recognition, paid leave, sick days, and equal pay.

Its profile combines ordinary grievance collection with strategic public performance. Contests about petty office procedures and bad bosses made disrespect discussable, while television appearances, worker rallies, and the film's release helped the movement spread. The source also ties the organization to SEIU Local 925 and later lobbying around family leave and fair pay.

## Key Characteristics
- Clerical-worker organization built around secretaries and other women office workers.
- Grievance aggregator that turned private office indignities into collective claims.
- Humor-using organizer that made bad bosses and humiliating tasks public.
- Bridge from informal worker meetings into union and legal-policy channels.
- Movement behind the [[NineToFiveFilm|9 to 5]] film's workplace-reform imagination.
- Example of unfinished labor progress because its core demands remained partly unresolved decades later.
