## Overview

袁固生 / Yuan Gusheng is the Confucian disputant in Hanji 392-3 whose defense of Tang-Wu revolution turns early Confucian ruler criticism into a live Western Han legitimacy problem.

## Current Profile

In [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-392-3-daodi-zenme-zuo-caishi-yige-hao-diwang-lgrcpyv1mf14axsq6hnqxaymrpu0|Hanji 392-3]], Yuan Gusheng argues before [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]] that [[ShangTang|商汤]]'s overthrow of Xia Jie is justified because popular support reveals Heaven's mandate. When [[HuangShengWesternHan|黄生]] insists that ministers cannot overthrow rulers, Yuan answers by asking whether [[LiuBang|刘邦]]'s replacement of Qin would also be illegitimate. The episode makes him a compact case of [[EarlyConfucianResistancePolitics|early Confucian resistance politics]] becoming politically dangerous because it can be applied to the reigning dynasty's own foundation.

## Key Characteristics

- Confucian disputant who uses ancient sage-king precedent to judge current political legitimacy.
- Defender of Tang-Wu revolution as mandate transfer grounded in popular support.
- Figure whose Liu Bang counterquestion exposes a self-reference problem inside Han dynastic legitimacy.
