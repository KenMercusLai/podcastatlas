## Overview

袁固生 / Yuan Gusheng is a Western Han Confucian whose surviving episode profile joins principled ruler criticism to a warning against bending scholarship and outward conduct toward court approval.

## Current Profile

Yuan's current profile joins intellectual resistance to personal consistency. Before [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]], he argues that [[ShangTang|商汤]]'s overthrow of Xia Jie is justified because popular support reveals Heaven's mandate; when [[HuangShengWesternHan|黄生]] insists that ministers cannot overthrow rulers, Yuan asks whether [[LiuBang|刘邦]]'s replacement of Qin would also be illegitimate. The exchange makes [[EarlyConfucianResistancePolitics|early Confucian resistance politics]] dangerous because its standard can be applied to the reigning dynasty's own foundation. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-392-3-daodi-zenme-zuo-caishi-yige-hao-diwang-lgrcpyv1mf14axsq6hnqxaymrpu0|Hanji 392-3]]

The same insistence on consistency appears in his reported encounter with [[GongsunHong|公孙弘]]. Recruited alongside Gongsun, Yuan warns him to align inner judgment, outward conduct, and correct learning rather than pursue favor through distortion. He then reportedly resigns after flattering scholars attack him as too old. The scene makes him a moral counterpoint to successful court adaptation without proving that withdrawal was the only principled option. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-272-pingjinhou-gongsunhong-ruhe-cong-zhuguan-nixi-cheng-chengxiang-2-llwabws3lkr5vittldqmmdz_hymm|Hanji 272 part 2]]

## Key Characteristics

- Confucian disputant who uses ancient sage-king precedent to judge current political legitimacy.
- Defender of Tang-Wu revolution as mandate transfer grounded in popular support.
- Figure whose Liu Bang counterquestion exposes a self-reference problem inside Han dynastic legitimacy.
- Moral counterpoint who warns another recruited scholar against separating learned conviction from court-facing conduct.
- Official whose reported withdrawal preserves integrity in the episode but does not establish that principled exit was the only available response.

## Evidence

Ancient precedent and people-based mandate:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-392-3-daodi-zenme-zuo-caishi-yige-hao-diwang-lgrcpyv1mf14axsq6hnqxaymrpu0|Hanji 392-3]] presents Yuan as saying that Jie lost the people's support and therefore could be replaced by Tang.

Han founding self-reference:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-392-3-daodi-zenme-zuo-caishi-yige-hao-diwang-lgrcpyv1mf14axsq6hnqxaymrpu0|Hanji 392-3]] has Yuan ask whether Gaozu replacing Qin was also wrong if ministerial overthrow is categorically forbidden.

Scholarship and court conduct:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-272-pingjinhou-gongsunhong-ruhe-cong-zhuguan-nixi-cheng-chengxiang-2-llwabws3lkr5vittldqmmdz_hymm|Hanji 272 part 2]] presents Yuan's warning to Gongsun Hong against bending learning toward worldly approval and his later resignation after age-based criticism.

## Qualifications

These two compact popular-history episodes do not provide a full biography, textual dossier, or independent reconstruction of Yuan's place in Han intellectual history. Hanji 272 part 2's age of ninety, service as tutor to Qinghe king Liu Cheng, exact warning, attacks by unnamed scholars, disappointment, and resignation motive remain source-scoped. The integrity contrast with Gongsun Hong is the host's moral framing, not proof that every hierarchy-aware adjustment is corrupt.

## What Changed

- Extended Yuan from a legitimacy disputant into a court-integrity counterpoint who links correct learning to consistency of speech and conduct.
- Added the reported resignation after age-based criticism while keeping its age, wording, and motive source-scoped.
