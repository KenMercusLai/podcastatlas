## Overview

孔光 / Kong Guang enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-621-diwang-xinshu-gongxin-zui-zhongyao-lgo01rnxby9ynyyzanljhtrawjck|Hanji 621]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-624-du-lishi-wu-xingfu-ni-shi-rensheng-yingjia-haishi-yongren-zirao-daibiao-lpavicltc7nducbxiqgpr6t7iczd|Hanji 624]] as a Western Han Taishi whose late-career posture shows the pressure around [[WangMang|王莽]]'s ascent. Hanji 621 presents him as a frightened senior official trying to withdraw while praise of Wang Mang intensifies; Hanji 624 then records his 5 CE death and the high-office transition to [[MaGongWesternHan|Ma Gong]].

## Current Profile

Hanji 621 says Kong Guang has already tried unsuccessfully to resign, and that the court's praise-heavy Wang Mang atmosphere makes him increasingly afraid. He uses illness to insist on leaving regular office duties. [[WangZhengjun|王政君]] cannot keep him fully in office, so the settlement reduces his obligations: he no longer has to attend ordinary court meetings, enters the palace every ten days, receives retirement implements and food gifts, and leaves Taishi-subordinate business running.

Hanji 624 then supplies the endpoint. Kong Guang dies on the first day of the fourth lunar month in 5 CE. The court grants him a rich funeral package, and the episode emphasizes the large burial procession by noting that more than ten thousand vehicles attend. Soon afterward, Ma Gong is appointed Taishi to replace him.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han Taishi active in Han Pingdi's late court.
- Holder of the Fushan marquis title in this source.
- Senior official whose illness-based withdrawal reflects fear under Wang Mang's praise-heavy power environment.
- Semi-retired court figure whose status is honored while ordinary attendance is reduced.
- Receives unusually grand funeral treatment after death.
- Office-transition figure before Ma Gong's appointment as Taishi.

## Qualifications

The sources do not provide Kong Guang's full earlier career or a direct statement of his policy position toward Wang Mang. Hanji 621 supports fear, illness-based withdrawal, and reduced attendance, but Kong Guang's inner calculation, the exact legal status of his semi-retirement, and the operation of the Taishi office after withdrawal remain source-scoped. Hanji 624 supports death, funeral scale, and succession by Ma Gong but does not explain why Ma Gong is selected.
