# Khmer Rouge

## Overview

The Khmer Rouge appears as the Communist guerrilla movement that captured Phnom Penh in 1975 and imposed a violently coercive rural revolutionary order on [[Cambodia]].

## Current Profile

The episode places the movement's rise within Cambodia's wider destabilization by North Vietnamese bases, American bombing and invasion, civil conflict, collapsing harvests, and failing law. After victory, it describes forced urban evacuation and the targeting of monks, officials, educated people, foreign-language speakers, and people associated with capitalism.

The source also uses the Khmer Rouge case to show how ideological priors can distort risk recognition: some Western journalists initially discounted warnings of mass killing as Cold War propaganda before later reporting documented the emptied capital and forced marches.

## Key Characteristics

- Emerged from Cambodia's regional-war and civil-collapse environment.
- Captured Phnom Penh in April 1975.
- Forced the urban population into the countryside.
- Targeted education, official status, religion, and perceived foreign or capitalist association.
- Exposed the danger of dismissing atrocity warnings solely as ideological messaging.

## Qualifications

This episode offers a compressed account rather than a complete history of Democratic Kampuchea. Leadership, casualty, ideology, chronology, individual atrocity, and Western-media claims remain source-scoped, and the episode explicitly distinguishes the Khmer Rouge outcome from postwar repression in Vietnam.

## What Changed

- Established the Khmer Rouge as the endpoint of Cambodia's 1975 collapse and a case of catastrophic warning failure.
- Preserved the difference between Cambodian mass killing and Vietnam's distinct postwar repression.
