# Bonar Law

## Overview

Bonar Law was the Conservative leader who made armed Ulster resistance to [[IrishHomeRule|Irish Home Rule]] a central party cause during the crisis of 1912-1914.

## Current Profile

The episode presents Law's position as both personal and strategic. Born in New Brunswick and connected to Ulster, Scotland, Glasgow, and Liverpool, he understood Protestant unionist feeling more directly than his predecessor Arthur Balfour and recognized its electoral force across Britain.

Law treated the Parliament Act and third Home Rule bill as a constitutional emergency. He backed [[EdwardCarson]] and the original [[UlsterVolunteerForce|Ulster Volunteer Force]], and his 1912 Blenheim Palace speech pledged Conservative support for resistance without defining a limit. The result was a paradox central to his profile: a leader defending union and constitution was willing to support extraparliamentary force when constitutional procedure no longer protected the desired settlement.

## Key Characteristics

- Conservative leader who made opposition to Home Rule a party-defining cause.
- Politician with personal and electoral connections to Ulster, Scotland, Glasgow, and Liverpool.
- Supporter of Carson's mass unionist campaign and the original UVF.
- Constitutional defender willing to legitimate resistance outside parliamentary procedure.
- Actor whose rhetoric increased pressure on Asquith's government and the army.

## Qualifications

This is a source-bounded profile of one constitutional crisis rather than a full political biography or premiership. The degree to which conviction, electoral calculation, party pressure, and tactical bluff separately shaped Law's conduct remains interpretive, and the episode does not establish which acts of violence or military refusal he would ultimately have endorsed.

## What Changed

- Established Law as the Conservative leader who nationalized the Ulster resistance campaign.
- Distinguished his personal unionist commitment from, without separating it cleanly from, party strategy.
