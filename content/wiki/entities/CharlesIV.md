# Charles IV / 查理四世

## Overview

Charles IV was king of Bohemia and Holy Roman emperor whose fourteenth-century rule made [[Prague]] an imperial center and gave the city much of its durable institutional and monumental framework.

## Current Profile

The source characterizes Charles as Prague's “product manager”: not because he personally designed every work, but because his reign coordinated university, urban expansion, bridge construction, cathedral patronage, and imperial status into a city-building program. Charles University, New Town, Charles Bridge, and St. Vitus Cathedral make his rule visible in present-day Prague.

This legacy is not a preserved single-author plan. Succession conflict followed his reign, St. Vitus Cathedral stopped for centuries and was completed through later national revival, and bridge sculpture accumulated long after construction. Charles therefore anchors one formative layer in a city whose landmarks were repeatedly extended and reinterpreted.

## Key Characteristics

- Bohemian king and Holy Roman emperor associated with Prague's fourteenth-century prominence.
- Founder or patron linked to Charles University, New Town, Charles Bridge, and St. Vitus Cathedral.
- Ruler whose projects joined education, urban form, royal ritual, transport, and religious authority.
- Historical predecessor to the succession conflict forming the background of [[KingdomComeDeliverance|《天国：拯救》]].

## Evidence

- Imperial and urban role: [[no-234-jieke-youji-shuyu-chengbao-yinkuang-yu-jiuguan-de-boximiya-ditu-gkwrijiovueba7xf6gt0j48r]] identifies Charles as king and emperor who made Prague a major imperial city.
- Institutional and monumental program: [[no-234-jieke-youji-shuyu-chengbao-yinkuang-yu-jiuguan-de-boximiya-ditu-gkwrijiovueba7xf6gt0j48r]] links his reign to the university, New Town, bridge, and cathedral.
- Layered afterlife: [[no-234-jieke-youji-shuyu-chengbao-yinkuang-yu-jiuguan-de-boximiya-ditu-gkwrijiovueba7xf6gt0j48r]] notes that cathedral completion and bridge sculpture belong partly to later periods, qualifying any idea of an unchanged Charles-era city.

## Qualifications

The source is a travelogue and offers a selective ruler-centered interpretation rather than a full account of fourteenth-century government, finance, labor, church politics, architectural authorship, or the Holy Roman Empire. The “product manager” label is an explanatory analogy, not a historical office.

## What Changed

- Established Charles IV as the coordinating ruler behind a durable Prague city-building layer.
- Qualified the founder story by distinguishing fourteenth-century projects from later completion and embellishment.
