# Hernando Pizarro

## Overview

Hernando Pizarro was a Spanish conquistador who commanded the defense of Cusco during Manco Inca's 1536 siege before Diego de Almagro seized the city and imprisoned him.

## Current Profile

[[648-the-fall-of-the-incas-battle-for-the-sacred-city-part-5-glt6392035011]] presents Hernando as more ceremonially respectful toward [[MancoInca]] than Juan or [[GonzaloPizarro]], but still vulnerable to the same appetite for treasure. Manco wins permission to leave Cusco by promising a golden statue, then mobilizes the uprising that leaves Hernando commanding fewer than two hundred Spaniards and roughly five hundred Indigenous auxiliaries.

During [[SiegeOfCusco1536|the siege]], Hernando concludes that the defenders must retake Sacsayhuaman and continues the assault after Juan Pizarro's fatal wound. When [[DiegoDeAlmagro]] returns, Hernando warns Manco against trusting him but cannot prevent Almagro from entering Cusco, burning Pizarro properties, and imprisoning him.

## Key Characteristics

- Pizarro-family commander responsible for Spanish-held Cusco during the 1536 siege.
- More courteous toward Manco than his brothers, without abandoning the conquest hierarchy.
- Deceived by Manco's promise to retrieve a golden statue.
- Advocate and commander of the costly operation to retake Sacsayhuaman.
- Rival of Almagro in the three-sided struggle over Cusco.

## Evidence

- Manco's escape: [[648-the-fall-of-the-incas-battle-for-the-sacred-city-part-5-glt6392035011]] says Hernando released Manco after being promised a giant golden statue.
- Siege command: [[648-the-fall-of-the-incas-battle-for-the-sacred-city-part-5-glt6392035011]] places Hernando in command of the small Spanish-Indigenous garrison and the assault on Sacsayhuaman.
- Factional defeat: [[648-the-fall-of-the-incas-battle-for-the-sacred-city-part-5-glt6392035011]] follows his warning to Manco and subsequent imprisonment when Almagro takes Cusco.

## Qualifications

This profile covers one episode and one campaign. Courtesy toward Manco is relative to more extreme abuse by other Spaniards and does not establish equal dealings or opposition to conquest. Dialogue, motives, troop totals, and the golden-statue episode remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Established Hernando as the principal Spanish commander during the siege of Cusco.
- Connected his treatment of Manco and desire for gold to the escape that enabled the revolt.

## Relationships

- [[MancoInca]] - ruler he released before commanding the defense against Manco's uprising.
- [[GonzaloPizarro]] - brother and fellow defender later imprisoned by Almagro.
- [[DiegoDeAlmagro]] - factional rival who seized Cusco and imprisoned him.
- [[SiegeOfCusco1536]] - campaign in which he commanded the Spanish-held enclave.
- [[ConquistadorCivilWarPeru]] - intra-Spanish conflict that overtook the siege.
