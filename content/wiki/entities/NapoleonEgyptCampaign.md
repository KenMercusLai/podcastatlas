# Napoleon's Egyptian Campaign

## Overview

Napoleon's Egyptian Campaign was the 1798-1801 French invasion and occupation that joined an indirect strike at British imperial power to revolutionary propaganda, scientific study, classical emulation, and colonial rule.

## Current Profile

The expedition won rapid victories over [[MamlukEgypt]] and occupied Cairo, but [[HoratioNelson]]'s victory at the [[BattleOfTheNile]] destroyed French naval mobility. Rebellion, brutal repression, the failed Syrian expedition, plague, and [[NapoleonBonaparte|Napoleon]]'s return to France made the occupation a strategic failure. Its longer afterlife ran through the savants, the [[RosettaStone|Rosetta Stone]], the [[DescriptionDeLEgypte|Description de l'Egypte]], and the emergence of [[Egyptology]].

## Key Characteristics

- Indirect anti-British strategy aimed at the route and imperial system connected to India.
- Combined army, savants, printing, survey, antiquarian study, and Suez inquiry.
- Used revolutionary liberation and Muslim-facing rhetoric alongside coercive occupation.
- Achieved land victories but lost strategic freedom after naval defeat.
- Produced a durable scholarly and collecting afterlife despite military failure.
