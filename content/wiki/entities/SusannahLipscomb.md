# Susannah Lipscomb

## Overview

Susannah Lipscomb is a historian presented by the source as a specialist in early-modern history and witchcraft who explains how religious ideas, legal procedure, gender, and local social pressure converged in witch trials.

## Current Profile

In [[26-witches-glt8850594772]], Lipscomb moves between long-run belief and historically specific prosecution. She treats witchcraft belief as recurrent but locates the major early-modern transformation in diabolic theology and legal criminalization. Her account then joins elite demonology to village grievance, subsistence crisis, reputation, gender assumptions, and changing standards of evidence.

The episode introduces her as a professor at Roehampton University and author of a Ladybird book on witchcraft. It also places her intellectual formation near the work of Robin Briggs and Lyndal Roper, while the source itself remains a conversational overview rather than a complete academic biography.

## Key Characteristics

- Historian of early-modern Europe and witchcraft.
- Interpreter who distinguishes durable supernatural belief from episodic prosecution.
- Analyst of the interaction between elite theology and local accusation.
- Historian attentive to gender patterns without treating women as the only accused group.
- Advocate of evidentiary change as a major explanation for declining prosecutions.
