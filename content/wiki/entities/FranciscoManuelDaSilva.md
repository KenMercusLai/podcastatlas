# Francisco Manuel da Silva

## Overview

Francisco Manuel da Silva appears in the episode as the composer whose 1831 tune became the durable musical core of the [[BrazilianNationalAnthem]].

## Current Profile

The episode places Francisco Manuel da Silva after [[DomPedroI]]'s abdication. His celebratory hymn associated with 7 April 1831 eventually became Brazil's de facto anthem tune, even though early anti-Portuguese lyrics and later coronation lyrics failed. The republic later kept the tune, making him central to [[RegimeSymbolContinuity]].

## Key Characteristics

- Composer of the 1831 tune that became Brazil's anthem melody.
- His tune emerged after Pedro I's abdication rather than at the 1822 independence moment.
- The melody outlasted multiple failed lyric sets.
- The republican regime kept the tune despite holding a contest for a replacement anthem.
- His role makes musical continuity stronger than textual stability in the episode's account.

## Evidence

- Composition: [[681-brazil-the-emperors-anthem-part-5-glt5596929385]] says Francisco Manuel da Silva composed a celebratory hymn after Pedro I's abdication.
- Lyric instability: [[681-brazil-the-emperors-anthem-part-5-glt5596929385]] says early words were dropped and later coronation lyrics were unsatisfactory.
- Republican reuse: [[681-brazil-the-emperors-anthem-part-5-glt5596929385]] says [[DeodoroDaFonseca]] preferred the old tune when the republic considered a new anthem.
