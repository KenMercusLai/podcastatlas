# Sean Mackey

## Overview
Sean Mackey is presented as a physician-scientist, Stanford professor, and pain-medicine division chief whose work connects pain neuroscience, whole-person clinical care, digital health, and public policy.

## Current Profile
In the episode, Mackey resists both tissue-only explanations and purely psychological dismissal. He distinguishes nociceptive signaling from the subjective experience of pain, then treats biology, emotion, cognition, memory, sleep, movement, social function, and treatment risk as parts of one clinical problem.

His practical stance is function-centered and pluralist: education, rehabilitation, pain psychology, medication, procedures, complementary approaches, and patient skills can be combined according to the person and condition. The same stance shapes his policy discussion, where he rejects simple pro- versus anti-opioid positioning and argues for better pain education, access, data, and implementation of a national pain strategy.

## Key Characteristics
- Distinguishes nociception, pain experience, tissue damage, and function rather than treating them as interchangeable.
- Uses a whole-person model joining biological, psychological, behavioral, and social contributors.
- Prioritizes quality of life, functional restoration, pacing, and durable benefit over symptom intensity alone.
- Applies risk-benefit nuance across common analgesics, opioids, cannabis, kratom, supplements, procedures, and complementary care.
- Connects individual clinical care to access, digital measurement, research, education, and public policy.
