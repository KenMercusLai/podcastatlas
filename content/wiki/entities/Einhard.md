# Einhard

## Overview

Einhard was a Carolingian courtier and biographer whose classical account shapes later memory of [[Charlemagne]] and the superseded [[MerovingianDynasty]].

## Current Profile

The episodes treat Einhard as both an informed court witness and a literary craftsman. Writing after Charlemagne's death and modeling his biography on Suetonius's life of Augustus, he opened by portraying the last Merovingian king as a ceremonial figure whose hair, beard, throne, and audiences concealed political impotence. That contrast explains the transfer of effective power to palace mayors while also supplying the victorious Carolingians with a retrospective justification for dynastic replacement.

Einhard knew Charlemagne, praised his firmness and greatness of soul, and supplied memorable details about the ruler's stature, dress, humor, swimming, and use of the baths at [[Aachen]]. His praise helps establish the courtly image of a ruler able to direct conquest and reform on an exceptional scale, but it cannot independently resolve the moral or factual disputes around those policies.

His portrait also borrows from Suetonius's imperial biographies, and his claim that Charlemagne would have avoided St Peter's had he known about the coronation sits uneasily beside evidence of preparation. His testimony therefore preserves court memory while participating in the construction of a modest, Roman-styled emperor.

## Key Characteristics

- Carolingian courtier with personal knowledge of Charlemagne.
- Biographer responsible for influential physical and behavioral details.
- Writer who adapted classical imperial biography to a medieval ruler.
- Courtly witness whose praise helped define Charlemagne's character as firm and magnanimous.
- Source for the disputed claim that Charlemagne was surprised by the coronation.
- Retrospective narrator whose powerless-Merovingian opening legitimates Carolingian succession.

## Evidence

- Dynastic framing - [[523-charlemagne-return-of-the-kings-part-1-glt5104943454|Part 1]] uses Einhard's opening portrait to contrast Merovingian ritual with palace-mayoral power and notes his Augustan literary model.
- Ruler-character praise - [[524-charlemagne-pagan-killer-part-2-glt1992648453|Part 2]] uses Einhard's language of firmness and greatness of soul when introducing Charlemagne's scale and ambition.
- Court memory and portrait - [[525-charlemagne-emperor-of-the-west-part-3-glt6103469677]] attributes details about Charlemagne's appearance, humor, dress, swimming, and baths to Einhard.
- Literary construction - [[525-charlemagne-emperor-of-the-west-part-3-glt6103469677]] notes borrowing from Suetonius and compares the surprise story with evidence of advance planning.

## Qualifications

The sources do not offer a full biography of Einhard or textual analysis of all his work. Personal access does not make every detail or character judgment transparent reportage, while classical borrowing does not make every remembered detail fictional. His description of the last Merovingians is evidence for both court politics and historical conditions, not a neutral audit of the displaced dynasty. Praise of firmness or greatness cannot by itself validate policy, campaign chronology, or ruler motive.

## What Changed

- Added the posthumous, Suetonian frame and powerless-Merovingian opening to his profile.
- Distinguished dynastic legitimation from neutral description while preserving his value as a court witness.
