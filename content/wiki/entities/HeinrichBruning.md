# Heinrich Brüning

## Overview

Heinrich Brüning was the Centre Party chancellor whose decree government, deflationary policy, and 1930 election gamble weakened parliamentary rule during the Depression.

## Current Profile

After the Müller coalition collapsed in March 1930, Brüning governed without a stable Reichstag majority through [[PaulVonHindenburg]] and Article 48 emergency decrees. The episode presents him as intelligent, austere, Catholic, monarchist, and instinctively authoritarian, with restrictions directed more heavily against the left than the right.

His spending and unemployment-benefit cuts deepened hardship, while dissolution of the Reichstag turned crisis management into an electoral gamble. The September 1930 result raised the Nazis from 12 seats to 107 and made decree government more entrenched rather than restoring a workable parliamentary order.

## Key Characteristics

- Centre Party chancellor after the collapse of the Müller coalition.
- Dependent on presidential emergency powers rather than a stable parliamentary majority.
- Advocate of deflation, spending cuts, and reduced unemployment support.
- Conservative and monarchist participant in weakening parliamentary government.
- Author of an election gamble that dramatically expanded Nazi representation.
