# Kenneth Smith

## Overview

Kenneth “Kenny” Smith is presented as one of two men convicted of killing Elizabeth Sennett and as a central subject of [[TheAlabamaMurders]].

## Current Profile

The sources use Smith's case to expose the gap between controlled execution protocols and lived procedure. Alabama's unsuccessful attempt to establish intravenous access occurred outside ordinary clinical conditions and largely outside witness view. The final episode says that attempt caused severe post-traumatic stress, after which Alabama isolated him under a second warrant and executed him with a first-use nitrogen-hypoxia protocol. The page records that institutional and human role without attempting a complete biography or case adjudication.

## Key Characteristics

- Alabama capital defendant and subject of [[TheAlabamaMurders]].
- Survived a failed lethal-injection attempt after prolonged intravenous-access efforts.
- Became a central case in the adoption of nitrogen hypoxia.
- Illustrates how restricted observation can hide execution preparation and failure.
- Was reported by [[KatePorterfield]] to have developed execution-induced nightmares, depression, nausea, and severe post-traumatic stress.
- Remained concerned for his mother, wife, sons, and grandson while preparing for the second attempt.

## Evidence

### Capital case and failed procedure

- [[understanding-the-death-penalty-malcolm-gladwell-on-the-intercept-briefing-understanding-the-death-penalty-malcolm-glad]] connects Smith to the Sennett killing, the failed intravenous-access attempt, and Alabama's later nitrogen-hypoxia method.

### Institutional meaning

- [[understanding-the-death-penalty-malcolm-gladwell-on-the-intercept-briefing-understanding-the-death-penalty-malcolm-glad]] uses the case to examine non-clinical execution practice, secrecy, and the difference between visible calm and experienced suffering.

### Second warrant, trauma, and execution

- [[the-alabama-murders-part-7-the-second-warrant]] reports Porterfield's assessment that the first attempt caused Smith's PTSD and that a second warrant, isolation, masking, and reuse of the setting would intensify it.
- [[the-alabama-murders-part-7-the-second-warrant]] records Smith's family farewells and final statement, witness descriptions of minutes of seizure-like movement and apparent gasping, and the state's claim that those reactions were expected.

## Qualifications

This is not a complete account of the murder, Smith's conduct, trial, appeals, victims, sentencing record, failed execution, PTSD diagnosis, or nitrogen execution. The two sources are related podcast material rather than independent confirmation. Witness observation cannot determine subjective awareness or pain, and the medical, legal, and procedural claims require underlying records and specialist evidence.

## What Changed

- Added the second warrant, reported execution-induced PTSD, family concerns, final statement, and disputed execution observations.
- Clarified that the state and witnesses agreed movement occurred but interpreted its meaning differently.
