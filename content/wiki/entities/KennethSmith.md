# Kenneth Smith

## Overview

Kenneth “Kenny” Smith is presented as one of two men convicted of killing Elizabeth Sennett and as a central subject of [[TheAlabamaMurders]].

## Current Profile

The sources use Smith's case to expose gaps between admitted assault, fatal-conduct evidence, jury judgment, judicial sentence, and lived execution procedure. Part 2 reports Smith's account that Billy Gray Williams offered him $1,500 to hurt someone, that he recruited [[JohnForrestParker]], and that the pair gained [[ElizabethSennett]]'s trust before Parker attacked her while Smith staged the burglary. Part 3 argues that a missing smaller knife and the survival timeline place both men away before the fatal wounds; that remains an episode theory rather than a reversal of Smith's conviction. His own jury later recommended life imprisonment without parole 11-1, but the judge imposed death. Alabama's first attempt to execute him involved hours of arm and central venous access efforts outside ordinary witness view; [[KatePorterfield]] later attributed severe post-traumatic stress and depression to that attempt. Alabama ultimately executed Smith with a first-use nitrogen-hypoxia protocol.

## Key Characteristics

- Participant who said he accepted a paid assault job, recruited Parker, staged the burglary, and whose role in the fatal stabbing is disputed by Part 3.
- Received a death sentence through judicial override after an 11-1 jury recommendation for life without parole.
- Survived a failed lethal-injection attempt after prolonged arm and central-line access efforts.
- Became a central case in the adoption of nitrogen hypoxia.
- Illustrates how restricted observation can hide execution preparation and failure.
- Was reported by [[KatePorterfield]] to have developed execution-induced nightmares, depression, nausea, and severe post-traumatic stress.
- Was described through family and clinical testimony as carrying developmental trauma while using apology, farewell, family love, and faith to orient himself during and after the failed attempt.

## Evidence

### Capital case and failed procedure

- [[the-alabama-murders-part-2-coon-dog-cemetery-road]] reports Smith's account of his recruitment, enlistment of Parker, deceptive entry, burglary staging, evidence disposal, arrest, and confession.
- [[the-alabama-murders-part-3-a-peculiar-institution]] reports Smith's admitted assault, substance use with Parker, the weapon mismatch, and the episode's conclusion that both men had left before the fatal wounds.
- [[understanding-the-death-penalty-malcolm-gladwell-on-the-intercept-briefing-understanding-the-death-penalty-malcolm-glad]] connects Smith to the Sennett killing, the failed intravenous-access attempt, and Alabama's later nitrogen-hypoxia method.
- [[the-alabama-murders-part-5-cruel-and-unusual]] reports the jury recommendation, judicial override, late litigation, repeated arm and chest access attempts, Smith's pain and panic, his post-attempt impairment, and abandonment near the warrant deadline.

### Institutional meaning

- [[understanding-the-death-penalty-malcolm-gladwell-on-the-intercept-briefing-understanding-the-death-penalty-malcolm-glad]] uses the case to examine non-clinical execution practice, secrecy, and the difference between visible calm and experienced suffering.

### Second warrant, trauma, and execution

- [[the-alabama-murders-part-7-the-second-warrant]] reports Porterfield's assessment that the first attempt caused Smith's PTSD and that a second warrant, isolation, masking, and reuse of the setting would intensify it.
- [[the-alabama-murders-part-7-the-second-warrant]] records Smith's family farewells and final statement, witness descriptions of minutes of seizure-like movement and apparent gasping, and the state's claim that those reactions were expected.

### Childhood burden and post-attempt meaning

- [[the-alabama-murders-part-6-the-porterfield-sessions]] reports testimony that Smith witnessed his father assault his mother, comforted her from early childhood, cared for her during heavy drinking, and began drinking heavily himself as a teenager.
- [[the-alabama-murders-part-6-the-porterfield-sessions]] describes his rehearsed apology to Sennett's family, farewell to his own family, bodily distress when recalling the procedure, and sustained attention to the love expressed before the attempt.

## Qualifications

This is not a complete account of the murder, Smith's conduct, trial, appeals, victims, sentencing record, childhood, substance use, failed execution, PTSD diagnosis, or nitrogen execution. The six sources are related podcast material rather than independent confirmation. Part 2's reconstruction relies substantially on Smith's account and does not independently establish each participant's conduct. Part 3 supplies a substantial reasonable-doubt argument but not an independent finding that Smith could not be legally responsible or that [[CharlesSennett]] inflicted the fatal wounds. Developmental trauma may contextualize later violence but does not establish a single causal path or diminish responsibility. Medical, legal, procedural, and clinical claims require underlying records, state responses, and specialist evidence.
