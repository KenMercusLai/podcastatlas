# Bayeux Tapestry

## Overview
The Bayeux Tapestry is the medieval embroidered artwork the episode presents as a long-surviving political narrative of [[WilliamTheConqueror|William the Conqueror]]'s 1066 invasion of [[England]].

## Current Profile
In this wiki branch, the tapestry is both historical evidence and interpretive object. It likely was stitched in England, lived near Bayeux in [[France]] for close to a millennium, and arrived at the [[BritishMuseum|British Museum]] for what the exhibition episode describes as its first display outside France. The Hastings episode adds an evidentiary boundary: the famous image associated with an arrow in [[HaroldGodwinson|Harold Godwinson]]'s eye is ambiguous, was heavily restored, and cannot by itself settle how he died.

## Key Characteristics
- Monumental medieval textile described as stretching roughly the length of a Boeing 747.
- Narrative object showing Edward, Harold, William, oath, comet, battle, death, flight, and victory scenes around the Norman Conquest.
- Visually legible artwork whose figures, hair, moustaches, arrows, bodies, horses, and margins make politics readable without modern prose explanation.
- Interpretive artifact because its ending, commission, makers, and production method remain uncertain.
- Near-contemporary battle source whose restored and ambiguous details require comparison with written accounts.
- Survival object that endured threats from the French Revolution, Napoleon, and Nazi-era seizure efforts.
- Violence-memory object because the episode closes by asking how makers and survivors process conquest through art.

## Evidence
- Conquest narrative - [[ire-and-ice-the-toll-of-americas-deportations-6a993426809ae7c2f4293df9]] says the tapestry depicts the events around William's 1066 invasion, including Edward sending Harold to Normandy, Harold's oath, Harold taking the throne, Halley's Comet, battle, and William's victory.
- Visual storytelling - [[ire-and-ice-the-toll-of-americas-deportations-6a993426809ae7c2f4293df9]] has [[MichaelLewisCurator|Michael Lewis]] compare its characters and visual sequence to cartoons or graphic novels.
- Missing and unknown evidence - [[ire-and-ice-the-toll-of-americas-deportations-6a993426809ae7c2f4293df9]] says the ending is missing, may have shown William's coronation, and that commission, makers, and production method remain unknown.
- Survival and violence - [[ire-and-ice-the-toll-of-americas-deportations-6a993426809ae7c2f4293df9]] notes the tapestry's animals, people, Latin, fables, explicit marginal detail, survival threats, and scenes of plunder, death, and suffering.
- Death-scene ambiguity - [[556-1066-the-battle-of-hastings-part-3-glt3522014441]] says the figures under the Harold death caption may not represent one continuous person, notes nineteenth-century restoration and an earlier copy suggesting a spear, and contrasts the image with early written accounts that omit an eye wound.

## Qualifications
The sources treat maker, commission, likely-English stitching, and missing-ending claims as theories rather than settled facts. The tapestry is evidence for the battle tradition, not a self-interpreting record: restoration, caption placement, and disagreement with early written sources prevent the arrow-in-the-eye scene from being treated as certain.

## What Changed
- Added the Hastings episode's source-critical boundary around the restored and ambiguous Harold death scene.
- Preserved the tapestry's broader conquest narrative, exhibition history, and violence-memory role.
