# Edith Tolkien

## Overview

Edith Tolkien was [[JRRTolkien|J.R.R. Tolkien]]'s wife and a formative emotional presence whose 1917 dance in a wood became the biographical seed of Beren and Luthien.

## Current Profile

[[225-j-r-r-tolkien-glt9725776786]] introduces Edith Bratt as a quiet, musically talented young woman who met Tolkien while both were lodging in Birmingham. Their courtship survived Father Francis Morgan's three-year prohibition on contact, and they married before Tolkien went to France.

After Tolkien returned from the Western Front with trench fever, Edith followed his changing hospital and military postings. At Roos in 1917, her dance among spring flowers entered Tolkien's imagination as the encounter of Beren and Luthien; those names were later placed on the couple's grave.

## Key Characteristics

- Musically talented young woman who met Tolkien in Birmingham lodgings.
- Romantic partner whose courtship with Tolkien endured a three-year enforced separation.
- Spouse who followed Tolkien during his wartime illness and postings.
- Biographical inspiration for Luthien through the remembered dance at Roos.

- Wartime companionship: [[225-j-r-r-tolkien-glt9725776786]] follows Edith alongside Tolkien's hospital and posting changes after trench fever.
- Literary transformation: [[225-j-r-r-tolkien-glt9725776786]] connects the 1917 dance to Beren and Luthien and to the names on their grave.

## Qualifications

The source presents Edith mainly through Tolkien's biography and literary memory, not as a complete account of her own life, views, work, or marriage. The Luthien connection is well established within the episode, but it should not reduce either Edith or the fictional character to a one-to-one identity.

- Kept the Beren-Luthien connection bounded against literal biographical reduction.
