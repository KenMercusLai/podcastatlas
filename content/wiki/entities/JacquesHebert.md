# Jacques Hébert

## Overview

Jacques Hébert was a revolutionary journalist and Paris Commune official whose *Père Duchesne* persona claimed to speak for ordinary Parisians. He joined anti-royal propaganda and custodial power to economic radicalism, de-Christianization, and a failed 1794 insurrection before being executed with the [[Hebertists]].

## Current Profile

Hébert repeatedly represented Marie Antoinette through scatological, pornographic, conspiratorial, and dehumanizing language, then used his Commune office to help remove and re-educate [[LouisCharles|Louis-Charles]] and introduce the child's incest allegation at the queen's trial. He also demanded stronger price controls, surveillance, denunciation, and executions of alleged food withholders while supporting de-Christianization. These positions helped him outflank the government from the radical left, but his March 1794 insurrection call won little institutional support. Arrested and tried with an assorted group presented as a foreign network, he was executed on 24 March.

## Key Characteristics

- He used the *Père Duchesne* persona to claim popular authenticity through aggressive vernacular speech.
- His journalism made Marie Antoinette a recurring explanation for crisis and converted sexualized fantasy into a barrier against sympathy.
- His Commune role and use of Louis-Charles's statement joined propaganda, coerced evidence, family destruction, and direct custodial power.
- He linked food scarcity to expanded economic control, surveillance, denunciation, and exemplary execution.
- His de-Christianization program conflicted with Robespierre's deism and concern about alienating Catholics.
- His failed insurrection showed that Parisian radical rhetoric no longer guaranteed Commune, section, or National Guard support.

## Qualifications

- The source's estimate that one in seven of Hébert's revolutionary articles concerned Marie Antoinette remains episode-scoped.
- His attacks did not create hostility to the queen alone; older court rumor, wartime betrayal, economic distress, and revolutionary conflict supplied an existing field of belief.
- A courtroom backfire does not establish the accusation's broader reception or mean that prior dehumanization ceased to work.
- The failed insurrection does not make the underlying food shortages or working-class distress unreal.
- The source does not establish the exact size, coherence, or popular reach of the Hébertist faction.

## What Changed

- Extended Hébert from anti-royal propaganda and custody into economic radicalism, de-Christianization, failed insurrection, and execution.
- Added the institutional withdrawal of Commune and National Guard support as the immediate limit on his radical strategy.

## Relationships

- [[MarieAntoinette]] - principal target whom he portrayed as a monstrous conspirator and accused through her son's statement.
- [[LouisCharles]] - child whose removal, re-education, and signed allegation served Hébert's anti-royal project.
- [[MaximilienRobespierre]] - revolutionary leader who criticized the incest allegation as a damaging excess.
- [[Hebertists]] - radical faction associated with his journalism and insurrectionary program.
- [[Indulgents]] - factional opponents whose newspaper initially attacked him with Robespierre's approval.
- [[LeVieuxCordelier]] - anti-Hébertist publication that later turned against terror itself.
- [[DeRoyalizationRitual]] - process his propaganda and custodial interventions extended from public language into family identity.
- [[RevolutionaryTerrorPolitics]] - coercive context in which accusation, custody, and political prosecution converged.
- [[RevolutionaryPurgeSecurityDilemma]] - mechanism intensified when his faction's destruction was followed by the Dantonists' execution.
