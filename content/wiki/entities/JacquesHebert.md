# Jacques Hébert

## Overview

Jacques Hébert was a revolutionary journalist and Paris Commune official whose *Père Duchesne* persona claimed to speak in the vulgar idiom of the Paris streets. The source presents him as both a propagandist against [[MarieAntoinette|Marie Antoinette]] and an officeholder with direct authority over the imprisoned royal family.

## Current Profile

Hébert repeatedly represented Marie Antoinette through scatological, pornographic, conspiratorial, and dehumanizing language even after her ability to shape events had greatly diminished. His political significance exceeded publication: as a Commune official he helped arrange [[LouisCharles|Louis-Charles's]] removal and re-education, sought evidence against the queen, and introduced the child's signed incest allegation at her trial. That accusation briefly generated sympathy for Marie Antoinette in court and was condemned by [[MaximilienRobespierre|Robespierre]] as a political error, while remaining legible outside court because years of propaganda had prepared audiences to believe it.

## Key Characteristics

- He used the *Père Duchesne* persona to claim popular authenticity through aggressive vernacular speech.
- He made Marie Antoinette a recurring explanation for conspiracy and crisis even when she had little operational power.
- His journalism converted sexualized fantasy into a political barrier against sympathy for an imprisoned enemy.
- His Paris Commune role joined rhetorical dehumanization to direct custodial power.
- His use of Louis-Charles's statement shows how propaganda, coerced evidence, family destruction, and political trial could reinforce one another.

### Propaganda against the queen

### Custodial intervention

- [[709-the-terror-the-execution-of-marie-antoinette-part-3-glt8774364224]] links Hébert's Commune office to Louis-Charles's removal, placement with Antoine Simon, and political re-education.

### Trial accusation and backfire

- [[709-the-terror-the-execution-of-marie-antoinette-part-3-glt8774364224]] describes his presentation of the incest statement, Marie Antoinette's appeal to mothers in the courtroom, and Robespierre's criticism of the allegation.

## Qualifications

- The source's estimate that one in seven of Hébert's revolutionary articles concerned Marie Antoinette remains episode-scoped.
- His attacks did not create hostility to the queen alone; older court rumor, wartime betrayal, economic distress, and revolutionary conflict supplied an existing field of belief.
- A courtroom backfire does not establish the accusation's broader reception or mean that prior dehumanization ceased to work.
- The source does not provide a complete political biography of Hébert or cover his later execution.

## What Changed

- A canonical profile now connects Hébert's popular-journalistic persona to his direct power over royal prisoners.

## Relationships

- [[LouisCharles]] - child whose removal, re-education, and signed allegation served Hébert's anti-royal project.
