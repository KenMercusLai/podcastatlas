# Philippe Fabre d'Églantine

## Overview

Philippe Fabre d'Églantine was a revolutionary deputy and associate of [[GeorgesDanton|Georges Danton]] whose genuine financial corruption became entangled with fabricated claims of foreign direction.

## Current Profile

The source presents Fabre as an embezzler who accused foreign financiers and agents of funding political factions in order to conceal fraud involving banks, insurers, and the French East India Company. [[MaximilienRobespierre|Robespierre]] and [[LouisAntoineSaintJust|Saint-Just]] combined Fabre's story with allegations from [[FrancoisChabot|François Chabot]], turning real corruption into apparent evidence for a single foreign conspiracy and creating a route from Fabre to Danton's prosecution.

## Key Characteristics

- He belonged to Danton's political circle.
- He was implicated in financial corruption involving the French East India Company.
- He used a foreign-plot allegation to divert attention from his own conduct.
- His accusation confirmed an existing governing belief in coordinated foreign subversion.
- His association with Danton helped make corruption evidence politically transferable.

## Evidence

### Corruption and fabricated plot

- [[710-the-terror-death-at-the-guillotine-part-4-glt5038102951]] connects Fabre's embezzlement to his claim that foreign financiers funded Hébert and other factions.

### Political incorporation

- [[710-the-terror-death-at-the-guillotine-part-4-glt5038102951]] shows Robespierre and Saint-Just merging Fabre's story with Chabot's allegation and later trying Fabre beside Dantonists and foreign bankers.

## Qualifications

- The source establishes a fabricated conspiracy claim without implying that Fabre's underlying corruption was invented.
- The episode does not supply a complete financial audit, biography, or independent judicial record.
- Exact sums, motives, transactions, and divisions of responsibility remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- A canonical profile now distinguishes Fabre's real corruption from the wider conspiracy he invented to conceal it.
