## Overview

赵佗 / Zhao Tuo is the former Qin Nanhai powerholder who uses [[RenXiao|任嚣]]'s handoff and Qin's collapse to build [[Nanyue|南越]]. His Han relationship is now a repeated cycle rather than a single submission: [[LuJia|陆贾]] first secures recognition under [[LiuBang|刘邦]], conflict under Empress Lü produces an imperial-title challenge, and [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]] later attempts a face-saving but coercively backed reconciliation.

## Current Profile

Hanji 184 and 185 trace Zhao Tuo's state-building. Ren Xiao advises him to seal the Five Ridges routes and use [[NanhaiCommandery|南海郡]]'s distance and terrain as the Qin center collapses. Zhao Tuo then blocks passes, removes Qin-appointed senior officials, installs trusted subordinates, takes [[GuilinCommandery|桂林郡]] and [[XiangCommandery|象郡]], and calls himself Nanyue Wuwang.

[[LiuBang|刘邦]] chooses recognition rather than immediate war. Lu Jia arrives with seal and tally, invokes Zhao Tuo's Central Plains origin, family and ancestral graves, and pairs those identity claims with the scale of Han victory. Zhao Tuo accepts the Han title hierarchy while retaining enough self-regard to imagine that he might have rivaled Liu Bang had he remained in the Central Plains. The settlement exemplifies [[RecognitionBackedFrontierDiplomacy|册封威慑式边疆外交]]: formal incorporation is achieved without direct occupation.

Hanji 191 qualifies the settlement further. A South Sea kingship appears to carve Nanhai out of Zhao Tuo's field on paper, but the episode treats the arrangement as possibly nominal rather than proof of effective territorial removal.

Hanji 200 supplies an earlier view of the Empress Lü rupture. Zhao Tuo reportedly reads restrictions on iron goods reaching Nanyue as hostile treatment and suspects that [[WuChenChangshaKing|长沙王吴臣]] wants Han help against him. The host instead connects a broader ban on gold, copper, and iron leaving Guanzhong to Lu Zhi's coinage and central-control policy. This makes the break partly a policy-perception conflict, but neither the measure's sole purpose nor Zhao Tuo's private reasoning is independently established.

Hanji 349-2 extends Zhao Tuo's profile into the Empress Lü and Emperor Wen eras. It says discriminatory policy and trade or technology restrictions trigger his “Nanyue Wudi” title, while Emperor Wen's conciliatory letter later obtains an external return to royal status. The source nevertheless says Zhao Tuo retains imperial-style privilege inside Nanyue and preserves a “Nanyue Wudi” seal, making formal rank and internal practice diverge.

Hanji 209 supplies the design of that reconciliation. After a failed Han expedition and Zhao Tuo's expansion and imperial display, Liu Heng repairs Zhao's parental graves, honors his relatives, withdraws a reported border force, and sends Lu Jia with a letter. The letter accepts self-government south of the Five Ridges and emphasizes the shared cost of war, but rejects the existence of two emperors. Family and ancestral ties therefore work both as reassurance and as latent leverage.

Hanji 210 part 1 supplies Zhao Tuo's answer. He attributes the rupture to Empress Lü-era trade restrictions, three detained envoys, reports of destroyed graves and killed relatives, cancellation of his royal title, and suspected Changsha instigation. He accepts Liu Heng's restoration of exchange and recognition, removes the outward imperial title and ceremony, promises submission and tribute, and invokes Liu Bang's earlier settlement. Yet he also stresses Nanyue's territory, troops, and southern relationships. The resulting repair is reciprocal but asymmetric: Han regains exclusive outward imperial rank, while Zhao Tuo preserves his local base, status dignity, and bargaining strength.

## Key Characteristics

- Former Qin regional officer who converts imperial collapse and difficult terrain into a southern polity.
- Consolidator of Nanhai, Guilin, and Xiang commandery territory under Nanyue.
- Ruler who accepts Han recognition under identity pressure and military deterrence.
- Politically self-confident subordinate whose submission does not erase local power.
- Reported adopter of a “Nanyue Wudi” title during conflict under Empress Lü.
- Negotiator whose second Han settlement exchanges outward imperial equality for local autonomy and restored relations while displaying local capacity and historical entitlement.
- Example of outward royal submission coexisting with internal imperial-style privilege.

## Qualifications

The sources are compact popular-history summaries rather than a complete biography or primary-text dossier. Hanji 191's South Sea kingship may describe nominal rather than effective territorial change. Hanji 200 does not establish the metal restriction's exact scope or sole purpose, Zhao Tuo's information, or that Changsha actually designed the policy. Hanji 349-2's discrimination, technology-barrier, title, seal, and internal-privilege claims remain episode-attributed; the evidence does not establish how consistently any dual order operated across Zhao Tuo's long reign. Hanji 209's hostage interpretation of his relatives and graves is the host's inference rather than an explicit detention or destruction threat. Hanji 210 part 1 completes the exchange but presents Zhao Tuo's own diplomatic defense: detained envoys, family harm, Changsha instigation, internal-only imperial usage, troop strength, motives, and the exact boundary between title renunciation and continuing internal privilege remain source-scoped.

- Added the initial iron-goods restriction and Zhao Tuo's suspected Changsha threat as a bounded policy-perception conflict.
- Completed the reconciliation sequence with Zhao Tuo's reply, outward title removal, renewed submission, and tribute promise.
- Added Zhao Tuo's causal defense of the rupture while keeping its allegations source-attributed.

## Relationships

- [[RenXiao|任嚣]] - predecessor whose handoff and terrain advice enable Zhao Tuo's consolidation.
- [[Nanyue|南越]] - southern polity Zhao Tuo builds and rules.
- [[LiuBang|刘邦]] - Han founder who recognizes Zhao Tuo rather than immediately invading.
- [[LuJia|陆贾]] - envoy who combines identity pressure, deterrence, and title recognition.
- [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]] - ruler who offers autonomy and restored relations while applying family, war-cost, and title pressure.
- [[ZhaoHuNanyue|赵胡]] - successor who reportedly preserves Zhao Tuo's imperial seal tradition.
- [[NanhaiCommandery|南海郡]] - territorial base whose later nominal carve-out remains uncertain.
- [[RecognitionBackedFrontierDiplomacy|册封威慑式边疆外交]] - mechanism producing Zhao Tuo's early formal submission.
- [[NominalSubmissionLocalAutonomy|名义臣服与地方实权并存]] - later synthesis separating Han-facing rank from internal political practice.
