## Overview

赵佗 / Zhao Tuo is the former Qin Nanhai powerholder who uses [[RenXiao|任嚣]]'s handoff and Qin's collapse to build [[Nanyue|南越]]. He later accepts Han recognition through [[LuJia|陆贾]]'s deterrent diplomacy, but the current evidence distinguishes that external submission from his reported continuation of imperial-style privilege inside Nanyue.

## Current Profile

Hanji 184 and 185 trace Zhao Tuo's state-building. Ren Xiao advises him to seal the Five Ridges routes and use [[NanhaiCommandery|南海郡]]'s distance and terrain as the Qin center collapses. Zhao Tuo then blocks passes, removes Qin-appointed senior officials, installs trusted subordinates, takes [[GuilinCommandery|桂林郡]] and [[XiangCommandery|象郡]], and calls himself Nanyue Wuwang.

[[LiuBang|刘邦]] chooses recognition rather than immediate war. Lu Jia arrives with seal and tally, invokes Zhao Tuo's Central Plains origin, family and ancestral graves, and pairs those identity claims with the scale of Han victory. Zhao Tuo accepts the Han title hierarchy while retaining enough self-regard to imagine that he might have rivaled Liu Bang had he remained in the Central Plains. The settlement exemplifies [[RecognitionBackedFrontierDiplomacy|册封威慑式边疆外交]]: formal incorporation is achieved without direct occupation.

Hanji 191 qualifies the settlement further. A South Sea kingship appears to carve Nanhai out of Zhao Tuo's field on paper, but the episode treats the arrangement as possibly nominal rather than proof of effective territorial removal.

Hanji 349-2 extends Zhao Tuo's profile into the Empress Lü and Emperor Wen eras. It says discriminatory policy and trade or technology restrictions trigger his “Nanyue Wudi” title, while Emperor Wen's conciliatory letter later obtains an external return to royal status. The source nevertheless says Zhao Tuo retains imperial-style privilege inside Nanyue and preserves a “Nanyue Wudi” seal, making formal rank and internal practice diverge.

## Key Characteristics

- Former Qin regional officer who converts imperial collapse and difficult terrain into a southern polity.
- Consolidator of Nanhai, Guilin, and Xiang commandery territory under Nanyue.
- Ruler who accepts Han recognition under identity pressure and military deterrence.
- Politically self-confident subordinate whose submission does not erase local power.
- Reported adopter of a “Nanyue Wudi” title during conflict under Empress Lü.
- Example of outward royal submission coexisting with internal imperial-style privilege.

## Qualifications

The sources are compact popular-history summaries rather than a complete biography or primary-text dossier. Hanji 191's South Sea kingship may describe nominal rather than effective territorial change. Hanji 349-2's discrimination, technology-barrier, title, seal, and internal-privilege claims remain episode-attributed; the evidence does not establish how consistently any dual order operated across Zhao Tuo's long reign.
