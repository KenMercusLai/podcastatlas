## Overview

Derek Chauvin is the veteran Minneapolis officer whose restraint of [[GeorgeFloyd]], authority over [[ThomasLane]], and prior conduct anchor the preventable-failure argument in [[memorial-day-2020-part-2-memorial-day-2020-part-2|Memorial Day, 2020 | Part 2]].

## Current Profile

The episode places Chauvin in policing's misconduct “fat tail”: a 19-year veteran with a complaint history, training authority, and a reported 2017 incident involving a child. His importance is therefore both direct and relational. He rejected Lane's recovery-position suggestion, modeled conduct to less experienced officers, and remained employed despite earlier warning signs that the episode argues should have prompted removal.

## Key Characteristics

- Senior officer with 19 years of experience at the time of Floyd's death.
- Former training officer whose authority intensified Lane's deference.
- Officer who rejected a proposal to roll Floyd onto his side during visible medical distress.
- Repeat-misconduct case used to illustrate concentrated institutional risk.
- Network figure whose seniority and mentorship role could influence colleagues.
- Example of failed early removal after a reported prior neck-restraint incident.

## Evidence

### Authority during Floyd's restraint

- [[memorial-day-2020-part-2-memorial-day-2020-part-2]] reports that Chauvin rejected Lane's recovery-position suggestion and that Lane deferred to Chauvin's experience and prior training role.

### Prior warning signs

- [[memorial-day-2020-part-2-memorial-day-2020-part-2]] recounts a reported 2017 incident in which Chauvin struck a child, used a chokehold, forced him down, and placed a knee on his neck.

### Concentrated and relational risk

- [[memorial-day-2020-part-2-memorial-day-2020-part-2]] uses Chauvin to connect [[PoliceMisconductConcentration]] with [[PoliceNetworkSpillover]] and failed institutional removal.

## Qualifications

This profile records the episode's analytical use of Chauvin rather than independently reconstructing his complete complaint, employment, or court record. Complaint counts require validation and context, and network position is not itself proof that a person caused another officer's conduct. The episode's broader institutional account does not diminish Chauvin's direct agency.

## What Changed

- Created a bounded profile connecting direct conduct, training authority, prior warning signs, and institutional non-removal.
