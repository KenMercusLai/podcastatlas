## Overview

Derek Chauvin is the veteran Minneapolis officer whose fatal restraint of [[GeorgeFloyd]], authority over [[ThomasLane]], and prior conduct anchor the paired *Memorial Day, 2020* episodes' account of individual and institutional failure.

## Current Profile

Part 1 interprets Chauvin's conduct through [[DecisionFixation]] and [[HostileIntentAttribution]]: Floyd's efforts to breathe, warnings from multiple observers, loss of consciousness, and an absent pulse did not dislodge the initial resistance-and-threat frame. Part 2 places Chauvin in policing's misconduct “fat tail”: a 19-year veteran with complaint history and training authority whose example could shape less experienced officers.

The reported 2017 restraint of a 14-year-old links those arguments. A similar pattern of force, prone restraint, expressed pain, and failure to reassess was recorded three years earlier, yet Chauvin remained employed. His profile therefore joins direct agency, threat interpretation, authority, repeated warning signs, peer influence, and failed institutional removal.

## Key Characteristics

- Senior officer with 19 years of experience at the time of Floyd's death.
- Former training officer whose authority intensified Lane's deference.
- Officer who rejected a proposal to roll Floyd onto his side during visible medical distress.
- Officer who continued restraint after multiple independent warnings, loss of consciousness, and an absent pulse.
- Repeat-misconduct case used to illustrate concentrated institutional risk.
- Network figure whose seniority and mentorship role could influence colleagues.
- Example of failed early removal after a recorded prior restraint of a child showed a similar pattern.

## Evidence

### Authority during Floyd's restraint

- [[memorial-day-2020-part-2-memorial-day-2020-part-2]] reports that Chauvin rejected Lane's recovery-position suggestion and that Lane deferred to Chauvin's experience and prior training role.
- [[memorial-day-2020-part-1-memorial-day-2020-part-1]] reports that Chauvin maintained the restraint despite Floyd's deterioration, warnings from bystanders and a firefighter, the recovery-position suggestion, and another officer's inability to find a pulse.

### Threat interpretation and fixation

- [[memorial-day-2020-part-1-memorial-day-2020-part-1]] applies [[GaryKlein]]'s fixation framework to Chauvin's failure to restart his interpretation as the evidence changed.
- [[memorial-day-2020-part-1-memorial-day-2020-part-1]] tentatively applies [[KennethDodge]]'s hostile-intent framework while acknowledging that Chauvin's developmental history is unknown.

### Prior warning signs

- [[memorial-day-2020-part-2-memorial-day-2020-part-2]] recounts a reported 2017 incident in which Chauvin struck a child, used a chokehold, forced him down, and placed a knee on his neck.
- [[memorial-day-2020-part-1-memorial-day-2020-part-1]] adds that the child reportedly did not physically attack officers and that Chauvin continued the restraint after the child described blacking out and paramedics arrived.

### Concentrated and relational risk

- [[memorial-day-2020-part-2-memorial-day-2020-part-2]] uses Chauvin to connect [[PoliceMisconductConcentration]] with [[PoliceNetworkSpillover]] and failed institutional removal.

## Qualifications

This profile records the episodes' analytical use of Chauvin rather than independently reconstructing his complete complaint, employment, court, medical, or disciplinary record. Complaint counts require validation and context, and network position is not itself proof that a person caused another officer's conduct. Fixation is an interpretive model rather than a diagnosis, and Gladwell explicitly lacks evidence for Chauvin's developmental history. The broader psychological and institutional accounts do not diminish Chauvin's direct agency or settle the role of racism.

## What Changed

- Added the failure to revise a threat interpretation despite multiple independent medical warnings.
- Strengthened the 2017 encounter as evidence of a repeated pattern that institutions could have acted upon.

## Relationships

- [[PoliceNetworkSpillover]] - peer-influence mechanism made more consequential by seniority and training authority.
