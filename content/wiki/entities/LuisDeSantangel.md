# Luis de Santángel

## Overview

Luis de Santángel was an Aragonese treasury official whose financial and competitive-risk argument helped secure royal support for [[ChristopherColumbus|Christopher Columbus]] in 1492.

## Current Profile

In [[306-columbus-the-adventure-begins-part-1-glt6089901915]], Santángel turns an repeatedly rejected geographic proposal into a bounded political gamble. He argues that the cost is small relative to the possible return and that refusing risks surrendering the opportunity to Portugal.

## Key Characteristics

- Aragonese treasury official connected to the Catholic Monarchs.
- Advocate for Columbus after expert and committee rejection.
- Framer of the voyage as a low-cost asymmetric opportunity.
- Link between Genoese networks, court access, finance, and royal competition.

## Evidence

- Court advocacy: [[306-columbus-the-adventure-begins-part-1-glt6089901915]] identifies Santángel as crucial to the final pitch.
- Decision frame: [[306-columbus-the-adventure-begins-part-1-glt6089901915]] attributes to him the argument that limited cost and Portuguese competitive risk favored approval.

## Qualifications

The episode emphasizes Santángel's advocacy but does not provide a full institutional or financial history of the voyage. Exact financing shares and the mechanics of Isabella's reversal remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Established Santángel as a decisive financial and political intermediary in the 1492 approval.
