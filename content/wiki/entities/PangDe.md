## Overview

庞德 / Pang De is a western military officer whose current evidence spans service in the Ma Teng-Ma Chao force field, later separation from [[MaChao|马超]] in [[Hanzhong|汉中]], and final resistance and execution during the flooded Fancheng campaign.

## Current Profile

Hanji 1057 places Pang De in [[MaTeng|马腾]]'s military line, under Ma Chao in the episode's account, during [[ZhongYao|钟繇]]'s campaign against [[GuoYuanLateHan|郭元]] and the Southern Xiongnu. Pang De kills Guo Yuan and presents the head without knowing that Guo Yuan is Zhong Yao's nephew. Zhong Yao shifts the scene from private apology to public duty by treating Guo Yuan as a court enemy.

Hanji 1140 adds a later but narrow Hanzhong transition. When Ma Chao receives troops from [[ZhangLuFivePecks|张鲁]] and moves against Qishan, Pang De reportedly remains behind with Ma Chao's family rather than joining the expedition. The source does not explain whether this reflects illness, assignment, leverage, distrust, or another constraint, but it shows that Ma Chao's surviving military and household network is already divided before the failed recovery attempt.

Hanji 1166 places Pang De in Cao-side service during the [[FanchengBattle219LateHan|Battle of Fancheng]]. After flood destroys the field position, he reportedly continues fighting, attempts to withdraw by boat, is captured after capsizing, rejects [[GuanYu|关羽]]'s offer despite the reference to his brother Pang Rou, and is executed. The episode uses the choice as a loyalty contrast with [[YuJin|于禁]]'s surrender; exact dialogue and moral ranking remain source-scoped.

## Key Characteristics

- Western military officer tied to the Ma Teng-Ma Chao force field.
- Battlefield actor credited with killing Guo Yuan at Pingyang.
- Unwitting participant in a kinship-versus-public-duty scene with Zhong Yao.
- Remains in Hanzhong when Ma Chao launches the Qishan recovery attempt.
- Marks the fragmentation of Ma Chao's retained personnel after the Weinan defeat.
- Continues resistance after the Fancheng flood and refuses Guan Yu's offer after capture.
- Is executed, becoming the episode's contrast case for loyalty under irreversible defeat.

## Qualifications

The three source notes leave major chronological gaps and do not explain Pang De's exact office, why he remained in Hanzhong, or the mechanics of his later transfer into Cao-side service. Hanji 1057's hierarchy, Hanji 1140's leverage reading, and Hanji 1166's flood sequence, dialogue, family reference, capture, and execution require broader comparison. Refusing surrender is preserved as an event, not generalized into a universal moral duty.

## What Changed

- Added the Fancheng resistance, refusal, and execution endpoint.
- Kept the episode's contrast with Yu Jin bounded rather than adopting a universal honor hierarchy.
