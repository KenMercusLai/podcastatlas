## Overview

庞德 / Pang De is a western military officer whose current evidence spans service in the Ma Teng-Ma Chao force field during the Pingyang campaign and later separation from [[MaChao|马超]] in [[Hanzhong|汉中]]. His profile remains partial, but it now connects early battlefield prominence with the fragmentation of Ma Chao's following after Weinan.

## Current Profile

Hanji 1057 places Pang De in [[MaTeng|马腾]]'s military line, under Ma Chao in the episode's account, during [[ZhongYao|钟繇]]'s campaign against [[GuoYuanLateHan|郭元]] and the Southern Xiongnu. Pang De kills Guo Yuan and presents the head without knowing that Guo Yuan is Zhong Yao's nephew. Zhong Yao shifts the scene from private apology to public duty by treating Guo Yuan as a court enemy.

Hanji 1140 adds a later but narrow Hanzhong transition. When Ma Chao receives troops from [[ZhangLuFivePecks|张鲁]] and moves against Qishan, Pang De reportedly remains behind with Ma Chao's family rather than joining the expedition. The source does not explain whether this reflects illness, assignment, leverage, distrust, or another constraint, but it shows that Ma Chao's surviving military and household network is already divided before the failed recovery attempt.

## Key Characteristics

- Western military officer tied to the Ma Teng-Ma Chao force field.
- Battlefield actor credited with killing Guo Yuan at Pingyang.
- Unwitting participant in a kinship-versus-public-duty scene with Zhong Yao.
- Remains in Hanzhong when Ma Chao launches the Qishan recovery attempt.
- Marks the fragmentation of Ma Chao's retained personnel after the Weinan defeat.

## Qualifications

The two source notes leave a major chronological gap and do not explain Pang De's exact office, why he remained in Hanzhong, or his later transfer and career. Hanji 1057's Ma Chao/Pang De hierarchy and Hanji 1140's claim that the people left behind functioned as leverage remain source-scoped pending broader textual comparison.

## What Changed

- Added his nonparticipation in Ma Chao's Qishan attempt without assigning an unsupported motive.
- Connected the episode to the fragmentation of Ma Chao's remaining military-household network.
