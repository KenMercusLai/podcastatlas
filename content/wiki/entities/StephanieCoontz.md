# Stephanie Coontz

## Overview
Stephanie Coontz is a historian presented by [[HiddenBrain]] as tracing how the purposes, expectations, labor arrangements, and gender ideology of marriage changed over time.

## Current Profile
Coontz's episode role is to challenge the idea of a single timeless traditional marriage. She describes earlier marriage as heavily shaped by production, property, obligation, and political alliance, then follows the growing legitimacy of romantic choice and the later breadwinner-homemaker ideal. The historical account makes contemporary self-development expectations intelligible as another institutional change rather than a recovery of an ancient norm.

The source also separates ideology from household practice. Men and women commonly contributed to household production before the nineteenth-century breadwinner model gained prominence, while economic dependence constrained women's freedom to choose marriage primarily for love even as romantic ideals spread.

## Key Characteristics
- Treats marriage as a changing social institution rather than a timeless private arrangement.
- Connects marital choice to property, labor, alliance, and economic dependence.
- Identifies romantic marriage as a historical development rather than a universal baseline.
- Challenges the antiquity of the male-breadwinner and female-homemaker model.
- Connects changing divorce patterns and social expectations to changing ideas of compatibility.

## Qualifications
This profile reflects one structured podcast summary rather than Coontz's full scholarship. The chronology is broad, centered largely on U.S. and European examples, and cannot represent all marital systems, classes, regions, or the coexistence of affection with economic and political motives.
