## Overview

Emperor Yuan of Han / 汉元帝 enters this wiki branch through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-592-handai-chengxiang-shi-ruhe-pingjia-qian-boss-de-lncdt3l6lk1hfxupuxti_dsjh9u5|Hanji 592]] as a precedent inside [[WangJiaWesternHan|王嘉]]'s confidential memorial to [[HanAidi|汉哀帝]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-499-liubang-fuwoqilai-dasi-liushi-zhegezeng-zengsunzi-lndfgjehpe7qc36hejxba3znlz0p|Hanji 499]] supplies the broader reign diagnosis: Yuan is personally frugal, humane, learned, and Confucian, but the source argues that he lacks practical statecraft and marks the Western Han turn toward decline. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-500-taijian-shixian-zhisi-zhan-de-yuegao-si-de-yuecan-lvu7w8xa8mjn8_mbxew7e4afupra|Hanji 500]] adds the vulnerable reign layer: Yuan's deep trust in [[ShiXianWesternHan|石显]] lets a capable eunuch in the information channel become politically dangerous. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-501-hanyuandi-jing-ba-taijian-dang-fuqin-lml1qwuw3iujobnltbjymeyojd4g|Hanji 501]] adds the source-scoped psychological layer: Yuan's illness, need for companionship, musical sensibility, and resentment toward [[XiaoWangzhiWesternHan|萧望之]] make Shi Xian's emotional support politically consequential. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-502-rang-laoban-bei-heiguo-shi-shui-zai-zhaosi-ls833sgrdjz9e1tupiiqillagohb|Hanji 502]] then adds the posthumous-reputation layer: after Shi Xian falls, [[WangZunWesternHan|王尊]] argues that [[KuangHengWesternHan|匡衡]] and [[ZhangTanWesternHan|张谭]] dishonor Yuan by making officials' fear of Shi Xian seem greater than fear of the emperor.

## Current Profile

The source set now gives two Yuan profiles that pull in different directions. Wang Jia's memorial in Hanji 592 praises Yuan as gentle, modest, without strong private greed, and fiscally restrained enough that palace treasuries and state reserves could support disaster relief and military expense during hardship. Hanji 499 agrees that Yuan had personal virtues, but uses them against the assumption that virtue equals rule: frugality, learning, and Confucian refinement did not prevent court capture or dynastic weakening.

The page therefore does not synthesize Yuan's reign as a whole. It records both how Wang Jia uses Yuan's remembered frugality to create comparative pressure before a later warning about late-Aidi private favor, and how Hanji 499 uses Yuan as a caution about [[ConfucianLegalistGovernanceBalance|儒法互补治理]]: Confucian learning can supply legitimacy language without supplying operational control.

Hanji 500, Hanji 501, and Hanji 502 give the inverse problem: not praise but blame around a trusted subordinate. Hanji 500 asks why Yuan trusted Shi Xian so deeply and answers through Shi Xian's usefulness, legal skill, and position in the ruler's information channel. Hanji 501 answers from a different angle, portraying Yuan as someone whose emotional hunger and illness-time need for attuned companionship made Shi Xian's music talk, reliability, and controlled confession feel like private safety. Hanji 502 then shows why Yuan's reputation constrains early Chengdi cleanup speech: criticizing Shi Xian too strongly can imply that the deceased emperor empowered a dangerous favorite or was feared less than his subordinate. Together the sources place Yuan inside [[CourtTransitionBlameManagement|新君清算中的责任安排]] as both the ruler whose trust made Shi Xian powerful and the predecessor whose dignity limits how far the new court can push accountability language.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han emperor used by Wang Jia as a favorable precedent for thrift, restraint, and reserve capacity.
- Functions in Hanji 592 as a rhetorical benchmark rather than as the source's main subject.
- Treated by Hanji 499 as a morally cultivated ruler whose governing weakness helps explain Western Han decline.
- Ruler whose trust in Shi Xian made eunuch information-channel power politically consequential.
- Portrayed by Hanji 501 as emotionally susceptible to Shi Xian's companionship, music, reliability, and confession tactics.
- Ruler whose teacher resentment becomes part of the source's explanation for Xiao Wangzhi's fall.
- Posthumous predecessor whose reputation constrains early Chengdi cleanup language after Shi Xian's fall.

## Evidence

- Fiscal restraint: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-592-handai-chengxiang-shi-ruhe-pingjia-qian-boss-de-lncdt3l6lk1hfxupuxti_dsjh9u5|Hanji 592]] says Wang Jia praises Yuan's modesty and lack of private greed.
- Reserve capacity: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-592-handai-chengxiang-shi-ruhe-pingjia-qian-boss-de-lncdt3l6lk1hfxupuxti_dsjh9u5|Hanji 592]] says accumulated funds helped cover disaster relief and Xiqiang-related military expense.
- Favor restraint: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-592-handai-chengxiang-shi-ruhe-pingjia-qian-boss-de-lncdt3l6lk1hfxupuxti_dsjh9u5|Hanji 592]] says Wang Jia uses Yuan's reward handling around favored palace women to stress fairness and avoidance of public resentment.
- Personal cultivation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-499-liubang-fuwoqilai-dasi-liushi-zhegezeng-zengsunzi-lndfgjehpe7qc36hejxba3znlz0p|Hanji 499]] stresses Yuan's thrift, care for people, respect for teachers, music and writing ability, and Confucian commitment.
- Governing mismatch: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-499-liubang-fuwoqilai-dasi-liushi-zhegezeng-zengsunzi-lndfgjehpe7qc36hejxba3znlz0p|Hanji 499]] argues that Yuan knew Confucian texts but not practical state governance, unlike the source's positive contrast with [[EmperorXuanOfHan|汉宣帝]].
- Shi Xian trust: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-500-taijian-shixian-zhisi-zhan-de-yuegao-si-de-yuecan-lvu7w8xa8mjn8_mbxew7e4afupra|Hanji 500]] frames the core question as why Yuan trusted Shi Xian and says Shi Xian's skill and access made him more useful than a mere flatterer.
- Information-channel risk: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-500-taijian-shixian-zhisi-zhan-de-yuegao-si-de-yuecan-lvu7w8xa8mjn8_mbxew7e4afupra|Hanji 500]] says Shi Xian's message and Zhongshu access made him a powerful court actor under Yuan.
- Emotional dependency: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-501-hanyuandi-jing-ba-taijian-dang-fuqin-lml1qwuw3iujobnltbjymeyojd4g|Hanji 501]] interprets Yuan as susceptible to Shi Xian because of early maternal loss, ill health, a need for companionship, and responsiveness to music.
- Teacher resentment: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-501-hanyuandi-jing-ba-taijian-dang-fuqin-lml1qwuw3iujobnltbjymeyojd4g|Hanji 501]] says Shi Xian exploits Yuan's dissatisfaction with Xiao Wangzhi as part of the trap that ends in Xiao's suicide.
- Predecessor-blame issue: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-502-rang-laoban-bei-heiguo-shi-shui-zai-zhaosi-ls833sgrdjz9e1tupiiqillagohb|Hanji 502]] says Wang Zun objects that Kuang Heng and Zhang Tan's criticism of Shi Xian makes officials fear Shi Xian more than the emperor.
- Transition constraint: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-502-rang-laoban-bei-heiguo-shi-shui-zai-zhaosi-ls833sgrdjz9e1tupiiqillagohb|Hanji 502]] uses Yuan's reputation as one reason early Chengdi cleanup cannot become open-ended blame transfer.

## Qualifications

This page is not a full reign profile for Emperor Yuan. Hanji 592 is focused on Wang Jia's political rhetoric, so Yuan's complete policies, fiscal numbers, consort politics, and responsibility for Western Han decline remain outside that bounded evidence. Hanji 499 adds a stronger host judgment that Yuan marks the Western Han decline turning point, but the exact causal weight of Yuan's Confucianism, Shi Xian, and broader institutions remains source-scoped. Hanji 500, Hanji 501, and Hanji 502 add Shi Xian layers, but they do not settle Yuan's actual responsibility for Shi Xian's power, the historical psychology behind Yuan's trust, or the full institutional record behind that trust.
