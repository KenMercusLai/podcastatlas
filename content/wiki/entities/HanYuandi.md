## Overview

Emperor Yuan of Han / 汉元帝 enters this wiki branch through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-592-handai-chengxiang-shi-ruhe-pingjia-qian-boss-de-lncdt3l6lk1hfxupuxti_dsjh9u5|Hanji 592]] as a precedent inside [[WangJiaWesternHan|王嘉]]'s confidential memorial to [[HanAidi|汉哀帝]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-472-cong-qizhi-zhuya-dao-zairu-bantu-jiemi-hainan-lishi-lhvp37w_0z8rebkka6squgfgledp|Hanji 472]] adds an earlier Chuyuan frontier-and-thrift layer: Yuan abolishes [[ZhuyaCommandery|珠崖郡]] rather than fight for distant prestige while Guandong famine, empty stores, and commoner hunger strain the center, and the same episode pairs that retreat with palace-cost reduction, wider Confucian education, tax relief for classic readers, and legal simplification. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-475-rexue-yushi-xueguangde-gongran-weixie-han-yuandi-lj9b1raz_rjhijw17vxgsp6racuj|Hanji 475]] adds an early Yongguang 1 conduct-and-risk layer: during famine and disaster pressure, Yuan abandons hunting after [[XueGuangdeWesternHan|薛广德]]'s warning and later accepts a safer bridge route only after [[ZhangMengWesternHan|张猛 / 张萌]] reframes Xue's harsher river-crossing protest. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-479-zhichang-qingshang-qianfei-kankan-xihan-jiajuanzhi-de-xiachang-lpsopbj1dtftnsn-kjjeyftst7el|Hanji 479]] adds an earlier Yongguang 1 personnel-authority layer: Yuan hears [[ShiXianWesternHan|石显]] report [[JiaJuanzhiWesternHan|贾捐之]] and [[YangXingWesternHan|杨兴]] after their praise-and-recommendation scheme makes senior appointments look privately bargainable. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-480-keben-bugan-ti-jiekai-zaobitouguang-de-heian-houxu-lhn89rqrj1dtntixgt5a7vd8p6ao|Hanji 480]] adds an earlier disaster-inquiry layer: after amnesty, eclipse, earthquake, and senior appointments, Yuan consults [[KuangHengWesternHan|匡衡]], whose answer turns the issue toward repeated amnesty, court example, capital customs, and personnel discipline. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-481-wei-feng-xihan-fengfengshi-yiju-mie-diguo-libgqzvquegllma9soju57k_jxue|Hanji 481]] adds an earlier frontier-crisis layer: Yuan hears [[FengFengshiWesternHan|冯奉世]] argue that the [[XianjieQiangWesternHan|贤解羌]] rebellion in [[LongxiCommandery|陇西郡]] requires sufficient initial force, but Yuan first chooses a smaller compromise before a field defeat forces major reinforcement. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-491-zhongguo-waijiao-zui-qiangying-de-shike-jing-shi-zai-xihan-lpgajp_vky9dx7jvnoahaxdfjflk|Hanji 491]] adds a late-reign mixed layer: Yuan turns the [[ZhizhiChanyu|郅支单于]] victory into public deterrence, gives first title recognition to [[ChenTangWesternHan|陈汤]] and [[GanYanshouWesternHan|甘延寿]], is shaken by disasters and illness, restores some ancestral shrines and mausoleum parks, and first sees [[HanChengdi|刘骜 / 后来的汉成帝]]'s succession vulnerability through the Zhongshan Ai King mourning incident. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-493-xiongdi-shi-hehuo-weisha-douhui-chouren-shi-sanhuo-lishi-gaosu-ni-llquknhihdms-6jyjbtfdfhvwv4g|Hanji 493]] adds a frontier-policy layer: Yuan receives [[HuhanyeChanyuWesternHan|呼韩邪单于]]'s offer to guard the Han border in exchange for removing defenses, sends it to officials, and hears [[HouYinWesternHan|侯胤]] argue against dismantling the screen. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-484-gudai-huangdi-de-hunyin-shenghuo-you-duo-mafan-lv7yfmymwhqdejvr0hxntjszlqwe|Hanji 484]] adds an earlier household-order warning: Yuan's favor toward [[FuZhaoyiWesternHan|傅昭仪]] and [[LiuKangDingtaoKingWesternHan|刘康]] weakens the [[WangZhengjun|王政君]] and Liu Ao branch enough for [[KuangHengWesternHan|匡衡]] to frame consort and嫡庶 hierarchy as state order. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-499-liubang-fuwoqilai-dasi-liushi-zhegezeng-zengsunzi-lndfgjehpe7qc36hejxba3znlz0p|Hanji 499]] supplies the broader reign diagnosis: Yuan is personally frugal, humane, learned, and Confucian, but the source argues that he lacks practical statecraft and marks the Western Han turn toward decline. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-498-taizi-liu-ao-huangyin-wudu-han-yuandi-weihe-bu-fei-le-ta-lnnuw3h3c8tqlf5ystx58gi7vvjo|Hanji 498]] adds the immediate pre-death succession layer: Yuan considers replacing Liu Ao with Liu Kang after illness, bedside intimacy, and Fu Zhaoyi's favor make Liu Kang feel closer, but [[ShiDanWesternHan|史丹 / 施丹]] blocks the change by making it an institutional-stability problem. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-500-taijian-shixian-zhisi-zhan-de-yuegao-si-de-yuecan-lvu7w8xa8mjn8_mbxew7e4afupra|Hanji 500]] adds the vulnerable reign layer: Yuan's deep trust in [[ShiXianWesternHan|石显]] lets a capable eunuch in the information channel become politically dangerous. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-501-hanyuandi-jing-ba-taijian-dang-fuqin-lml1qwuw3iujobnltbjymeyojd4g|Hanji 501]] adds the source-scoped psychological layer, and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-502-rang-laoban-bei-heiguo-shi-shui-zai-zhaosi-ls833sgrdjz9e1tupiiqillagohb|Hanji 502]] adds the posthumous-reputation layer.

## Current Profile

The source set now gives Yuan profiles that pull in different directions. Wang Jia's memorial in Hanji 592 praises Yuan as gentle, modest, without strong private greed, and fiscally restrained enough that palace treasuries and state reserves could support disaster relief and military expense during hardship. Hanji 491 shows a more event-driven Yuan: he can authorize a strong public frontier signal after the Zhizhi victory, but he also reads disasters and illness through ancestral displeasure and faces early doubts about Liu Ao's emotional fitness. Hanji 493 shows Yuan presiding over a frontier-cost question where most officials favor withdrawing defenses after Huhanye's loyalty pledge, but Hou Yin's objection keeps the issue framed as long-term state security rather than immediate savings. Hanji 499 agrees that Yuan had personal virtues, but uses them against the assumption that virtue equals rule: frugality, learning, and Confucian refinement did not prevent court capture or dynastic weakening.

Hanji 470 adds an earlier teacher-and-eunuch layer to the same mixed profile. Yuan restores [[XiaoWangzhiWesternHan|萧望之]] to honor and worries that imprisonment might kill him, but still accepts [[HongGongWesternHan|弘恭]] and [[ShiXianWesternHan|石显]]'s assurance that an arrest will only teach Xiao a lesson. After Xiao's suicide, Yuan grieves, reproaches attendants, and keeps annual sacrifices, yet does not punish Hong Gong or Shi Xian. This makes Yuan's weakness visible before the later Shi Xian trust, slander, appointment, and cleanup cases.

Hanji 472 and Hanji 481 together add two different frontier-decision versions of the same mixed profile. In the Zhuya case, Yuan narrows direct rule because a distant expedition would compete with famine relief and stores; in the Longxi Qiang case, he first undercommits before field failure forces reinforcement. Together they make Yuan neither simply pacifist nor simply strong: he is capacity-sensitive, but his calibrations can mean retreat in one frontier and underpowered initial commitment in another.

Hanji 480 adds the domestic-governance version of this mixed profile. Yuan seeks advice after disasters and amnesty, which makes him receptive enough to invite critique, but Kuang Heng's answer implies that ruler compassion and pardon must become institutional repair: court example, capital customs, official conduct, and personnel selection have to change or law will not work.

Hanji 475 adds a smaller but revealing conduct case. Yuan is not shown as refusing all correction: he gives up hunting when Xue Guangde connects leisure to famine-era public suffering, and he ultimately chooses the safer bridge route instead of a boat. But the river-crossing scene shows that reception depends on framing. Xue's threat makes Yuan angry, while Zhang Meng's praise-first, practical-risk restatement lets Yuan accept the same result. The case adds [[FaceSavingRemonstrance|留面子式进谏包装]] and [[ResponsibilityBoundRiskAvoidance|责任绑定风险回避]] to Yuan's profile.

The page therefore does not synthesize Yuan's reign as a whole. It records both how Wang Jia uses Yuan's remembered frugality to create comparative pressure before a later warning about late-Aidi private favor, and how Hanji 499 uses Yuan as a caution about [[ConfucianLegalistGovernanceBalance|儒法互补治理]]: Confucian learning can supply legitimacy language without supplying operational control. Hanji 472 and Hanji 481 add that operational weakness can appear not only in inner-court capture but in frontier calibration, where the court must decide whether direct rule, force size, and fiscal burden match actual capacity.

Hanji 484 and Hanji 498 together give the succession version of Yuan's vulnerability. Hanji 484 shows the risk before the sickroom: affection has already shifted toward Fu Zhaoyi and Liu Kang, and Kuang Heng warns that the ruler's meals, leisure, and intimacy can be exploited if empress-consort and嫡庶 order blur. Hanji 498 then shows the same pressure sharpened during Yuan's final illness. Yuan is disappointed in Liu Ao's adult indulgence and more emotionally surrounded by Fu Zhaoyi and Liu Kang. His repeated questions about Emperor Jing replacing an heir make the possible change visible, but Shi Dan's tearful remonstrance forces him to treat succession as a court-stability problem rather than a father's preference. This adds [[DeathbedHeirReplacementResistance|临终废储阻断]] to Yuan's profile: even a weak or emotionally swayed ruler can be stopped when the cost of overturning a long-established crown prince is made explicit.

Hanji 500, Hanji 501, and Hanji 502 give the inverse problem: not succession hesitation but blame around a trusted subordinate. Hanji 500 asks why Yuan trusted Shi Xian so deeply and answers through Shi Xian's usefulness, legal skill, and position in the ruler's information channel. Hanji 501 answers from a different angle, portraying Yuan as someone whose emotional hunger and illness-time need for attuned companionship made Shi Xian's music talk, reliability, and controlled confession feel like private safety. Hanji 502 then shows why Yuan's reputation constrains early Chengdi cleanup speech: criticizing Shi Xian too strongly can imply that the deceased emperor empowered a dangerous favorite or was feared less than his subordinate. Together the sources place Yuan inside [[CourtTransitionBlameManagement|新君清算中的责任安排]] as both the ruler whose trust made Shi Xian powerful and the predecessor whose dignity limits how far the new court can push accountability language.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han emperor used by Wang Jia as a favorable precedent for thrift, restraint, and reserve capacity, and in Hanji 592 as a rhetorical benchmark rather than the source's main subject.
- Ruler who can accept correction when advice is framed acceptably, from giving up famine-era hunting to choosing a safer bridge route after Zhang Meng restates Xue Guangde's warning.
- Frontier-crisis ruler who abolishes Zhuya Commandery under famine and fiscal pressure, yet initially undercommits against the Longxi Qiang rebellion before reinforcing after early defeat.
- Treated by Hanji 499 as a morally cultivated ruler whose governing weakness helps explain Western Han decline.
- Ruler whose weak handling of Xiao Wangzhi's arrest and trust in Shi Xian made eunuch information-channel power politically consequential, while his guarded personnel authority makes Jia Juanzhi and Yang Xing's appointment bargain lethal.
- Portrayed by Hanji 501 as emotionally susceptible to Shi Xian's companionship, music, reliability, and confession tactics, and by Hanji 484/498 as vulnerable to household-favor and late-life bedside succession pressure.
- Posthumous predecessor whose reputation constrains early Chengdi cleanup language after Shi Xian's fall.

## Qualifications

This page is not a full reign profile for Emperor Yuan. Hanji 470 adds the Xiao Wangzhi death case but does not settle Yuan's private motive, the legal wording of Xiao's arrest, or the exact responsibility split among Yuan, Hong Gong, Shi Xian, Zhu Yun, Xiao Ji, and Xiao himself. Hanji 592 is focused on Wang Jia's political rhetoric, so Yuan's complete policies, fiscal numbers, consort politics, and responsibility for Western Han decline remain outside that bounded evidence. Hanji 472 adds Zhuya, thrift, Confucian expansion, and legal simplification, but it does not settle Yuan's private reasoning, the full administrative geography of post-abolition Hainan, or the actual policy effects of each cost-cutting or legal measure. Hanji 475 adds conduct-and-risk cases but does not settle Yuan's private irritation, the objective danger of the boat crossing, or the exact original wording of Xue Guangde's and Zhang Meng's remonstrances. Hanji 479 adds a personnel-authority case but does not settle Yuan's private reasoning, the exact case record, or whether the punishment difference between Jia Juanzhi and Yang Xing was legally, politically, or personally driven. Hanji 480 adds a disaster-inquiry and amnesty critique but does not settle Yuan's private reasoning, the full Kuang Heng memorial, or the actual policy follow-through. Hanji 481 adds a frontier force-sizing case but does not settle the full Qiang campaign, Yuan's private reasoning, or whether Feng Fengshi's proposed forty thousand was objectively optimal. Hanji 491 adds victory-publication, disaster-repair, and early Liu Ao doubt, but it does not reconstruct the full campaign, ritual policy, medical history, or succession psychology. Hanji 493 adds a frontier-defense deliberation but does not settle Yuan's private view of Huhanye's proposal, the final procedural sequence, or the full original memorial text. Hanji 484 adds an earlier household-order warning but does not settle Yuan's private motive, the original memorial's full wording, or how direct the path is from favor to a formal heir-change plan. Hanji 499 adds a stronger host judgment that Yuan marks the Western Han decline turning point, but the exact causal weight of Yuan's Confucianism, Shi Xian, and broader institutions remains source-scoped. Hanji 498 adds succession-pressure evidence but does not settle Liu Ao's full fitness, Fu Zhaoyi's broader political role, or whether changing heirs would have improved the dynasty's later course. Hanji 500, Hanji 501, and Hanji 502 add Shi Xian layers, but they do not settle Yuan's actual responsibility for Shi Xian's power, the historical psychology behind Yuan's trust, or the full institutional record behind that trust.
