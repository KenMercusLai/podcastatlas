## Overview

Emperor Yuan of Han / 汉元帝 enters this wiki branch through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-592-handai-chengxiang-shi-ruhe-pingjia-qian-boss-de-lncdt3l6lk1hfxupuxti_dsjh9u5|Hanji 592]] as a precedent inside [[WangJiaWesternHan|王嘉]]'s confidential memorial to [[HanAidi|汉哀帝]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-502-rang-laoban-bei-heiguo-shi-shui-zai-zhaosi-ls833sgrdjz9e1tupiiqillagohb|Hanji 502]] adds an earlier posthumous-reputation layer: after [[ShiXianWesternHan|石显]] falls, [[WangZunWesternHan|王尊]] argues that [[KuangHengWesternHan|匡衡]] and [[ZhangTanWesternHan|张谭]] dishonor Yuan by making officials' fear of Shi Xian seem greater than fear of the emperor.

## Current Profile

The source's use of Emperor Yuan is narrow and rhetorical. Wang Jia praises Yuan as gentle, modest, without strong private greed, and fiscally restrained enough that palace treasuries and state reserves could support disaster relief and military expense during hardship.

The page therefore does not synthesize Yuan's reign as a whole. It records how Wang Jia uses Yuan's remembered frugality to create comparative pressure before a later warning about late-Aidi private favor.

Hanji 502 gives the inverse problem: not praise but posthumous blame containment. Yuan's reputation constrains early Chengdi cleanup speech, because criticizing Shi Xian too strongly can imply that the deceased emperor empowered a dangerous favorite or was feared less than his subordinate. The source therefore places Yuan inside [[CourtTransitionBlameManagement|新君清算中的责任安排]] as a predecessor whose dignity limits how far the new court can push accountability language.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han emperor used as a favorable precedent in Wang Jia's memorial.
- Associated in this source with thrift, restraint, and avoidance of visible private favoritism.
- Presented as leaving enough fiscal reserve to absorb disaster and frontier pressure.
- Functions as a rhetorical benchmark rather than as the source's main subject.
- Posthumous predecessor whose reputation constrains early Chengdi cleanup language after Shi Xian's fall.

## Qualifications

This page is not a full reign profile for Emperor Yuan. Hanji 592 is focused on Wang Jia's political rhetoric, so Yuan's complete policies, fiscal numbers, consort politics, and responsibility for Western Han decline remain outside that bounded evidence. Hanji 502 adds a source-scoped predecessor-dignity problem, but does not settle Yuan's actual responsibility for Shi Xian's power.
