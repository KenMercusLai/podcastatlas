## Overview

Emperor Yuan of Han / 汉元帝 enters this wiki branch through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-592-handai-chengxiang-shi-ruhe-pingjia-qian-boss-de-lncdt3l6lk1hfxupuxti_dsjh9u5|Hanji 592]] as a precedent inside [[WangJiaWesternHan|王嘉]]'s confidential memorial to [[HanAidi|汉哀帝]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-500-taijian-shixian-zhisi-zhan-de-yuegao-si-de-yuecan-lvu7w8xa8mjn8_mbxew7e4afupra|Hanji 500]] adds the vulnerable reign layer: Yuan's deep trust in [[ShiXianWesternHan|石显]] lets a capable eunuch in the information channel become politically dangerous. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-502-rang-laoban-bei-heiguo-shi-shui-zai-zhaosi-ls833sgrdjz9e1tupiiqillagohb|Hanji 502]] then adds the posthumous-reputation layer: after Shi Xian falls, [[WangZunWesternHan|王尊]] argues that [[KuangHengWesternHan|匡衡]] and [[ZhangTanWesternHan|张谭]] dishonor Yuan by making officials' fear of Shi Xian seem greater than fear of the emperor.

## Current Profile

The source's use of Emperor Yuan is narrow and rhetorical. Wang Jia praises Yuan as gentle, modest, without strong private greed, and fiscally restrained enough that palace treasuries and state reserves could support disaster relief and military expense during hardship.

The page therefore does not synthesize Yuan's reign as a whole. It records how Wang Jia uses Yuan's remembered frugality to create comparative pressure before a later warning about late-Aidi private favor.

Hanji 500 and Hanji 502 give the inverse problem: not praise but blame around a trusted subordinate. Hanji 500 asks why Yuan trusted Shi Xian so deeply and answers through Shi Xian's usefulness, legal skill, and position in the ruler's information channel. Hanji 502 then shows why Yuan's reputation constrains early Chengdi cleanup speech: criticizing Shi Xian too strongly can imply that the deceased emperor empowered a dangerous favorite or was feared less than his subordinate. Together the sources place Yuan inside [[CourtTransitionBlameManagement|新君清算中的责任安排]] as both the ruler whose trust made Shi Xian powerful and the predecessor whose dignity limits how far the new court can push accountability language.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han emperor used as a favorable precedent in Wang Jia's memorial.
- Associated in this source with thrift, restraint, and avoidance of visible private favoritism.
- Presented as leaving enough fiscal reserve to absorb disaster and frontier pressure.
- Functions as a rhetorical benchmark rather than as the source's main subject.
- Ruler whose trust in Shi Xian made eunuch information-channel power politically consequential.
- Posthumous predecessor whose reputation constrains early Chengdi cleanup language after Shi Xian's fall.

## Qualifications

This page is not a full reign profile for Emperor Yuan. Hanji 592 is focused on Wang Jia's political rhetoric, so Yuan's complete policies, fiscal numbers, consort politics, and responsibility for Western Han decline remain outside that bounded evidence. Hanji 500 and Hanji 502 add source-scoped Shi Xian layers, but they do not settle Yuan's actual responsibility for Shi Xian's power or the full institutional record behind that trust.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 500's trust-and-information-channel layer before Hanji 502's posthumous-reputation problem, clarifying why Shi Xian's cleanup could rebound onto Yuan's image.
