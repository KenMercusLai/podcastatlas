# Marcus Reno

## Overview

Marcus Reno was the Seventh Cavalry officer whose attack on the Lakota and Cheyenne village collapsed into retreat before his survivors joined [[FrederickBenteen]] in the hilltop defense during the [[BattleOfLittleBighorn]].

## Current Profile

Before the battle, the episode presents Reno as a decorated Civil War veteran with little experience fighting Native forces, heavy drinking, and unresolved personal and military trauma. [[AlfredTerry]] sent him to locate the village and report, but he extended the reconnaissance beyond his instructions and stayed away longer than expected, worsening [[GeorgeArmstrongCuster]]'s fear that the initiative might be lost.

Custer later assigned Reno about 175 men and ordered him to open the attack against a village whose scale was not yet understood. Reno crossed the river, initially charged, then halted roughly 600 yards from the lodges and formed a dismounted skirmish line. As defenders gathered, he withdrew into timber. The position held briefly, but a confused mounting order and the death of [[BloodyKnife]] beside him preceded a panic-stricken flight through a river choke point to Reno Hill.

The sources describe drinking, erratic conduct, shock, and a shouted order to run, but those details come through retrospective testimony. Reno's survivors reached the hill bloodied, disordered, and uncertain about Custer's position. When [[FrederickBenteen]] arrived, Reno argued that the combined force could not move until the ammunition packs reached them.

The episode criticizes Reno's battlefield collapse but resists making him the sole explanation for defeat. Staying on the hill helped preserve survivors, and the conditions were extreme. Afterward Reno became the subject of the only formal investigation and a major target for defenders of Custer's reputation, concentrating a system-wide command failure into a personal blame dispute.

## Key Characteristics

- He was given a critical assault role within Custer's divided command.
- His pre-battle reconnaissance exceeded Terry's locate-and-report instructions.
- His initial charge gave way to a halt, skirmish line, timber withdrawal, and disordered mounted retreat.
- Bloody Knife's death appears as a major psychological shock, though it does not alone explain the collapse.
- His ammunition concern constrained any attempt to move toward Custer.
- His hilltop command survived through improvised defense, Benteen's reinforcement, and the attackers' eventual withdrawal.
- He became the formal and public focus of blame despite Custer's wider command responsibility.

## Qualifications

- The episode criticizes his conduct while acknowledging the extraordinary pressure and preserving some sympathy for psychological collapse.
- Drinking, slurred speech, the exact mounting order, and the words attributed to him depend on retrospective and sometimes hostile testimony.
- Whether continuing the original charge could have disrupted the village or only sacrificed his command is an unresolved counterfactual.
- Counterfactual claims about whether an immediate advance could have saved Custer remain unresolvable.
- Formal investigation does not by itself establish that his responsibility exceeded Custer's or Benteen's.
- This profile is limited to one episode's reconstruction of the battle and aftermath.
- The trauma, alcohol, and personality descriptions are narrative characterizations rather than a complete medical or biographical assessment.
