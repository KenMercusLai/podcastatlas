## Overview

Shangguan Wan'er is presented across [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-536-2-wo-weihe-buyuan-chengwei-xiandai-de-shangguan-waner-lpwfoyduhwjabnvpi6zvodkbk5ll|Hanji 536-2]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-536-3-jiemi-shangguan-waner-he-ta-beihou-de-nanrenmen-lqj7rpwwfllogrxrbav-xqwwnnnm|Hanji 536-3]] as a brilliant Tang court woman whose talent, political usefulness, and cultural glamour should not be mistaken for a simple success template. The sources foreground the cost of her rise: family catastrophe, palace servitude, dependence on ruler favor, sexualized court risk, factional calculation, and final exposure during restoration politics.

## Current Profile

The current wiki evidence now covers a two-part arc. Shangguan Wan'er is the granddaughter of Shangguan Yi and daughter of an executed family, raised in palace servitude with her mother Zheng after Wu Zetian's victory in the failed anti-empress episode. Her education and writing talent let her pass Wu Zetian's test at age fourteen and enter the ruler's document apparatus, where literary skill became political utility.

The interpretive profile is deliberately double-sided. Shangguan Wan'er is not reduced to victimhood: she has unusual intelligence, literary capacity, administrative usefulness, and a quick ability to adjust when political weather changes. But the sources also resist converting her into a modern empowerment icon, because her influence depends on surviving inside coercive palace hierarchies, intimate dependency, and patron networks that can turn fatal when dynastic legitimacy hardens.

## Key Characteristics

- Exceptionally educated and literate for her court position, with talent that could be immediately used in edict and document work.
- Politically legible through service to Wu Zetian rather than through independent institutional security.
- Marked by family trauma: her grandfather and father are killed after the failed deposing-empress episode, while she and her mother enter palace servitude.
- Situated in a gendered court environment where reputation, sexuality, intimacy, and power are tightly entangled.
- Capable of factional adaptation, moving from Wu Sansi and Empress Wei toward Li-Tang royal fallback routes after the political balance shifts.
- Later admired as a talented woman and political figure, but the sources treat those labels as risky when separated from dependence, moralized reputation, and fatal factional exposure.

## Qualifications

The current page still does not settle Shangguan Wan'er's full biography. Hanji 536-3 fills in the later factional arc, but it is compressed and interpretive. Claims about the exact historicity of specific relationships, the red-plum makeup tradition, the text and legal force of the 710 edict, her literary corpus, and Li Longji's full motive for killing her should remain source-scoped unless supported by further sources.

## What Changed

- Created the page from Hanji 536-2 with a cautious early-life and Wu Zetian-entry profile.
- Added Hanji 536-3's later arc, shifting the profile from early talent under Wu Zetian to a fuller synthesis of intimacy, factional hedging, and death under Li Longji.
