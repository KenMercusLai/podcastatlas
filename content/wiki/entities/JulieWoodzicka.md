# Julie Woodzicka

## Overview
Julie Woodzicka is a psychologist represented here through a [[HiddenBrain]] episode on the gap between imagined and experienced responses to sexual harassment and on rehearsed language for confronting sexist conduct.

## Current Profile
Woodzicka's reported study compares women imagining an inappropriate job interview with women placed in a live version of the situation. Imagined participants largely predicted anger, confrontation, or departure, while live participants more often reported fear, answered the questions, and remained. Her interpretation restores the emotional state, power imbalance, social ambiguity, perceived cost, and multi-step demands that abstract predictions omit.

Her applied response is preparation rather than blame. Simple language practiced before a sexist joke or comment can reduce the number of decisions required under pressure, although rehearsal cannot eliminate retaliation risk, unequal authority, freezing, or institutional responsibility.

## Key Characteristics
- Studies divergence between hypothetical and experienced responses to harassment.
- Distinguishes predicted anger from fear reported in a live situation.
- Treats confrontation as a multi-step process rather than a single obvious choice.
- Connects response difficulty to power, social context, ambiguity, shock, and perceived cost.
- Develops simple prepared language for use under pressure.

## Qualifications
This profile is based on one supplied structured episode summary rather than the underlying papers, protocol, ethics documentation, full research program, or workplace intervention evaluation. The source reports ethical clearance, debriefing, and an option to withdraw data, but the design still involved deceptive exposure to inappropriate questions. Its results should not be generalized to every person, workplace, harassment form, culture, or power relation, and failure to confront is not consent.
