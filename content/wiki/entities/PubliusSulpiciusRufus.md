# Publius Sulpicius Rufus / 普布利乌斯·苏尔皮基乌斯·鲁弗斯

## Overview

Publius Sulpicius Rufus / 普布利乌斯·苏尔皮基乌斯·鲁弗斯 is the tribune whose citizenship bill, street following, and alliance with [[GaiusMarius]] turn Italian enfranchisement politics into the [[RepublicanCommandTransferCrisis]] that triggers [[LuciusCorneliusSulla]]'s march on Rome.

## Current Profile

Episode 702 presents Sulpicius as a tribune with strong street support and a program for full citizenship for the Italian allies. His alliance with Marius is transactional: Marius supports Sulpicius's citizenship policy, while Sulpicius supports transferring the Mithridatic command from Sulla to Marius. That bargain transforms a rights question into a coercive command dispute: Sulpicius reintroduces his bill, violence breaks out, Pompeius Rufus's son is killed, Sulla takes refuge in Marius's house, and a plebiscite strips Sulla of the eastern command.

Episode 703 adds Sulpicius's end and his role as immediate trigger. Sulla decides that the legal decision is intolerable, marches six legions on Rome, and then asks the Senate to declare Sulpicius, Marius, and their allies public enemies. Sulpicius is betrayed by a slave and murdered. His page therefore shows how popularis procedure can win a vote but still lose the military-command field when the displaced commander holds the army.

## Key Characteristics

- Sulpicius uses tribunate authority and street organization around Italian citizenship.
- His alliance with Marius joins enfranchisement policy to military command ambition.
- His supporters help create a coercive urban environment in which Sulla is forced to yield.
- The plebiscite associated with his move transfers command from Sulla to Marius.
- His success triggers Sulla's army-backed reversal and retroactive outlawry.
- His murder shows the fatal risk once republican opponents are classified as public enemies.

## Qualifications

The sources present Sulpicius through the command crisis and Sulla's retaliation. They do not provide a full independent biography or settle how much of the violence should be attributed to his direction, his supporters, rival groups, or the wider Roman street-political field.

## What Changed

- Added Sulpicius's outlawry and murder after Sulla's march on Rome.
- Reframed Sulpicius's successful command-transfer plebiscite as a trigger that cannot control the army-backed response it provokes.
