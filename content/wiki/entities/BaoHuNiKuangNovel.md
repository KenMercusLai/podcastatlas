# 《宝狐》 / The Fox Fairy (Ni Kuang novel)

## Overview

《宝狐》 is a [[NiKuang|倪匡]] novel in the [[YuanZhenxia|袁振侠]] series that recasts a fox-spirit romance as an encounter with signal-like extraterrestrial life.

## Current Profile

The bounded episode begins with a sealed coffin, an ageless woman, and her disappearance, then reconstructs [[LengZiquan|冷自泉]]'s four-decade search for [[BaoHuCharacter|宝狐]]. The younger Leng abandons military-political advancement and his elite family's plan after meeting a woman visible only to him; when she is taken by extraterrestrial pursuers, he organizes the remainder of his life around recovering her.

The final explanation changes the story's register. Bao Hu has no stable body and appears according to an observer's imagination; she represents deception or evil but is transformed by Leng's love. To join her, Leng must leave embodied human life, and the novel frames his fatal plane crash as their continued companionship in another form.

That ending supports several readings without settling them. Love may transform an apparently fixed moral nature, Leng may reject an inherited political identity, and folklore may become a speculative thought experiment about information-like life. Yet [[RomanticAutonomyUnderManipulation]] remains unresolved because Bao Hu can affect cognition, while [[SpeculativeDemystificationTradeoff]] explains why the alien mechanism may narrow rather than deepen the fox-spirit mystery.

## Key Characteristics

- Mixes romance, horror, folklore, historical suggestion, and science fiction.
- Uses a coffin mystery and forty-year separation to organize disclosure.
- Treats love as capable of changing a being identified with deception or evil.
- Makes abandonment of office, family expectation, embodiment, and ordinary life the relationship's escalating cost.
- Replaces fox-spirit ambiguity with an extraterrestrial signal-life explanation.
- Leaves the beloved's autonomy uncertain because desire may be influenced by nonhuman power.

## Qualifications

This profile is based on one conversational retelling rather than direct comparison of editions or a complete study of the series. Plot sequence, publication history, scientific mechanism, historical coding, moral symbolism, authorial intent, and the interpretation of Leng's death remain source-scoped. The episode's manipulation reading identifies an unresolved possibility inside the fiction; it does not establish a clinical condition or prove that intense devotion is inherently coerced.
