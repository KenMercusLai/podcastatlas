# Upside Foods

## Overview

Upside Foods is a cultivated-meat company represented in the wiki through a visit in which chicken cells are grown in stainless-steel tanks and served as finished restaurant-style food.

## Current Profile

[[frankenfood-the-mistakes-series-frankenfood-the-mistakes-series|Frankenfood]] uses Upside Foods as the sensory proof point for its naming argument. The company reportedly cultivates chicken cells originally taken from an egg, serves the resulting chicken in a samosa and fried-chicken sandwich, and uses “cultivated meat” rather than laboratory-centered terminology.

Its “tasting is believing” approach favors restaurant introduction so consumers can judge taste and texture before production imagery dominates the decision. In the episode, that experience supports product similarity, but it does not resolve commercial scale, cost, regulation, energy demand, or comparative environmental performance.

## Key Characteristics

- Produces chicken from cultivated animal cells in stainless-steel tanks.
- Uses “cultivated meat” as its preferred category language.
- Treats restaurant tasting as an early consumer-adoption channel.
- Presents conventional taste and texture as central product proof.
- Remains subject to unresolved scale, economics, regulatory, and lifecycle questions in the current evidence.

## Evidence

### Production and product form

- [[frankenfood-the-mistakes-series-frankenfood-the-mistakes-series|Frankenfood]] describes chicken cells originally taken from an egg being cultivated in large tanks and served in two prepared dishes.

### Sensory proof

- [[frankenfood-the-mistakes-series-frankenfood-the-mistakes-series|Frankenfood]] reports that the visiting host found both dishes delicious and indistinguishable in taste from conventional chicken.

### Consumer introduction

- [[frankenfood-the-mistakes-series-frankenfood-the-mistakes-series|Frankenfood]] attributes “tasting is believing” to the company and describes restaurant experience as its proposed adoption route.

## Qualifications

The profile relies on one structured podcast summary and one hosted tasting. It does not independently verify production methods, safety, cost, regulatory status, environmental lifecycle effects, manufacturing scale, or consumer response, and one favorable tasting cannot establish population-wide acceptance.
