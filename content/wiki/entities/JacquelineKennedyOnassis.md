# Jacqueline Kennedy Onassis

## Overview

Jacqueline Kennedy Onassis was the first lady who accompanied [[JohnFKennedy|John F. Kennedy]] on the Texas trip ending in his assassination in Dallas.

## Current Profile

The episode presents Jacqueline Kennedy's participation as both political and deeply personal. It was her first comparable campaign trip with Kennedy since 1960 and followed the death of their infant son Patrick. Her presence generated publicity, strengthened the administration's public appeal, and became part of the successful reception at Fort Worth and Love Field.

During the shooting, she turned after the first sound, witnessed the fatal head wound, cried that her husband had been shot, and moved onto the rear of the car as Clint Hill climbed aboard. Her later inability to remember that movement and the interpretation that she may have tried to retrieve part of Kennedy's head are presented as evidence of acute shock, not deliberate conduct that can be reconstructed with certainty.

## Key Characteristics

- First lady whose presence increased the Texas trip's campaign visibility and public excitement.
- Spouse returning to major political travel after the death of the Kennedys' infant son Patrick.
- Public figure whose clothing and reception became part of the trip's visual political theater.
- Immediate witness and survivor of the presidential shooting.
- Traumatized family member whose instinctive actions cannot be interpreted with certainty.

## Qualifications

This profile comes from one retrospective episode centered on the assassination, not a full biography or independent trauma study. Exact words, gaze direction, memory, intention, and the claim that she sought to retrieve part of Kennedy's head remain source-scoped. Her clothing and publicity role should not reduce her to campaign imagery or obscure her bereavement and immediate exposure to violence.
