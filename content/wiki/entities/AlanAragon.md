# Alan Aragon

## Overview
Alan Aragon is the nutrition and fitness guest in a [[HubermanLab]] interview on protein, body recomposition, meal and exercise timing, dietary fats, sweeteners, supplements, and sustainable training. The source presents him as an evidence-oriented communicator who distinguishes acute mechanisms, longer-term outcomes, meta-analytic conclusions, and personal practice.

## Current Profile
Aragon's recurring role in the source is to impose a practical hierarchy on contested nutrition questions. [[ProteinBodyCompositionLever]] puts total daily protein, resistance training, calories, and consistency above a narrow anabolic window, while [[TrainingFuelTimingContext]] separates fat burned during one fasted session from net body-fat change across time. He uses the same outcome-first method for plant proteins, ketogenic diets, sweeteners, and [[SeedOilEvidenceBoundary]], asking which intervention, comparator, dose, and outcome a claim actually concerns.

The profile is not purely minimalist. Aragon describes personal use of fish oil, creatine, collagen, vitamin D3, magnesium, vitamin C, and multivitamins, and he favors dense cluster- and superset-based resistance training. The source labels many of these as personal or partly speculative practices rather than converting them into universal prescriptions.

## Key Characteristics
- Prioritizes total daily protein, calories, resistance training, and adherence over narrow nutrient-timing rules.
- Separates acute physiology, such as substrate oxidation, from long-term outcomes such as body-fat change.
- Uses reviews, meta-analyses, intervention studies, and explicit evidence-strength distinctions to evaluate popular nutrition claims.
- Treats plant-based, ketogenic, and mixed diets as workable when protein, calories, food quality, and adherence are appropriately managed.
- Rejects categorical ingredient fear by requiring named foods, doses, comparators, and measured health outcomes.
- Clearly distinguishes personal supplement and training practices from stronger general recommendations.

## Evidence
- Protein hierarchy: [[scim8552663956-scim8552663956]] has Aragon reject a fixed 20-to-30-gram protein-use ceiling and rank total daily intake above exact timing.
- Fed-fasted distinction: [[scim8552663956-scim8552663956]] reports greater acute fat oxidation in fasted exercise without a demonstrated long-term fat-loss advantage when nutrition is equated.
- Body-composition frame: [[scim8552663956-scim8552663956]] connects recomposition to resistance training, high protein, controlled calories, and a modest rather than indiscriminate surplus when muscle gain is prioritized.
- Diet flexibility: [[scim8552663956-scim8552663956]] treats plant protein and ketogenic diets through total intake, planning, appetite, and adherence rather than ideology.
- Evidence boundary: [[scim8552663956-scim8552663956]] asks seed-oil critics to specify the oil, dose, comparator, outcome, and compelling human trial.
- Personal-practice boundary: [[scim8552663956-scim8552663956]] labels supplement choices and dense cluster- or superset-based training as personal practices whose efficiency or evidence can differ from standard hypertrophy programming.
