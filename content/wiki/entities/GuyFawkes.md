# Guy Fawkes

## Overview

Guy Fawkes was the explosives specialist captured beneath the parliamentary complex during the [[GunpowderPlot]] and later transformed into the plot's dominant public symbol.

## Current Profile

[[115-the-gunpowder-plot-glt1711431752]] describes Fawkes as a Yorkshire-born convert to Catholicism and veteran of fighting for Spain in Flanders. [[RobertCatesby]]'s circle recruited him for his military and explosives experience. Authorities found him under the alias John Johnson with fuses in the early hours of 5 November 1605, after gunpowder had been stored beneath wood in a ground-floor storeroom.

Fawkes initially resisted torture in the Tower of London but was eventually broken, as the deterioration of his signature visibly records. He was tried with the surviving conspirators and condemned to hanging, drawing, and quartering. His cultural afterlife became larger and less confessional than his operational role: nineteenth-century entertainment romanticized him, while *V for Vendetta*, Anonymous, Occupy, and other modern uses turned his mask or name into a portable anti-establishment symbol.

## Key Characteristics

- Catholic convert and veteran of military service in Flanders.
- Recruited for practical knowledge of explosives rather than leadership of the plot.
- Captured using the alias John Johnson with the means to ignite the gunpowder.
- Tortured into disclosing his identity and fellow conspirators.
- Condemned through exemplary treason punishment.
- Later recast from confessional conspirator into romantic and anti-establishment icon.

## Evidence

### Operational role and capture

- [[115-the-gunpowder-plot-glt1711431752]] distinguishes Fawkes's explosives role from Catesby's leadership and describes his discovery with fuses before the planned opening of Parliament.

### Interrogation and punishment

- [[115-the-gunpowder-plot-glt1711431752]] uses Fawkes's weakened signature as evidence of torture and places him among the surviving conspirators condemned for treason.

### Symbolic afterlife

- [[115-the-gunpowder-plot-glt1711431752]] traces his movement into nineteenth-century drama and pantomime and then into modern anti-establishment imagery.

## Qualifications

Later fame should not be mistaken for organizational primacy: the episode consistently identifies Catesby as the plot's leader. Modern Fawkes symbolism also does not preserve the conspirators' original Catholic political program, and claims about the gunpowder's condition or the authorities' handling of discovery remain disputed.
