# 小美基因编辑事件 / Xiaomei Gene-Editing Case

## Overview
The Xiaomei gene-editing case is the episode's account of a six-year-old girl with Snyder-Robinson syndrome-related developmental disability and autism-like behavior who died after receiving an immature gene-editing intervention in Shanghai.

## Current Profile
The case functions in the wiki as a source-scoped medical-ethics and disability-care event. The episode says Xiaomei's condition was not immediately fatal, that she could speak full sentences, and that animal experiments had already shown serious organ-damage signals before the human intervention. Her death is used to ask why a child whose life still had possibility became the subject of a high-risk treatment aimed at repairing her difference.

The strongest current synthesis is that the event is not reducible to one party's bad decision. The source assigns responsibility across parental desperation, research ambition, information disclosure, ethics oversight, high-cost cure markets, and [[AbleistCurePressure]] that makes disabled childhood appear to have no future.

## Key Characteristics
- Centers a child whose developmental disability was treated as an error to be corrected despite nonfatal status and remaining development potential.
- Shows how gene-editing promise can become dangerous when risk, evidence, informed consent, and necessity are weak.
- Links parental fear to a wider achievement culture that treats school success, employment, and normality as prerequisites for a valuable life.
- Exposes the gap between professional judgments about disability quality of life and disabled people's own possible assessment of their lives.
- Becomes a warning case for [[AutismCureScamRisk]] and [[GeneEditingTreatmentEthics]] rather than a general claim about all gene-editing research.
