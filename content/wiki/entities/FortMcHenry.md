# Fort McHenry

## Overview

Fort McHenry appears in the wiki as the Baltimore fort whose September 1814 bombardment and flag scene gave [[StarSpangledBanner|The Star-Spangled Banner]] its core imagery.

## Current Profile

The episode presents the fort as a symbolically decisive site even though the bombardment caused limited damage. British ships used rockets and shells during a roughly 24-hour attack, while [[FrancisScottKey]] watched from British custody. At dawn on 14 September 1814, [[GeorgeArmistead]] ordered the large flag made by [[MaryPickersgill]] raised, creating the sight that Key turned into the poem's survival image.

## Key Characteristics

- Baltimore defensive fort attacked during the [[WarOf1812]].
- Site of a British bombardment using Congreve rockets and bomb vessels.
- Flag scene behind the anthem's dawn and survival imagery.
- Associated with a large 15-star, 15-stripe flag ordered by [[GeorgeArmistead]] and made by [[MaryPickersgill]].
- Symbolically more important than tactically destructive in the source's reading.

## Evidence

- Attack setting: [[677-usa-the-star-spangled-banner-part-1-glt7890467734]] says British forces moved toward Baltimore after Washington and began bombarding the fort on 13 September 1814.
- Military imagery: [[677-usa-the-star-spangled-banner-part-1-glt7890467734]] says rockets and bomb vessels produced imagery later remembered in the anthem.
- Flag raising: [[677-usa-the-star-spangled-banner-part-1-glt7890467734]] says the large flag was raised at dawn on 14 September, after a smaller storm flag had flown during the bombardment.
- Flag makers: [[677-usa-the-star-spangled-banner-part-1-glt7890467734]] credits [[MaryPickersgill]] and helpers with making the large garrison flag.
- Tactical qualification: [[677-usa-the-star-spangled-banner-part-1-glt7890467734]] says the attack caused limited damage and may not have put the fort in serious danger.

## Qualifications

The page does not provide a full military history of Baltimore's defense. It records the fort as the anthem episode uses it: a battle scene where visual distance, limited damage, and flag survival became national-symbol material.

## What Changed

- Created Fort McHenry as the battle-site anchor for the U.S. anthem branch.
