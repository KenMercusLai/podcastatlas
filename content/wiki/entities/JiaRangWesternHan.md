## Overview

[[JiaRangWesternHan|贾让 / Jia Rang]] is recorded in the wiki as the Western Han proposer of the upper, middle, and lower Yellow River control policies discussed in Hanji 567.

## Current Profile

Hanji 567 presents Jia Rang as a hydraulic-policy thinker rather than as a biographical subject. His key contribution is a ranked diagnosis of flood control: do not keep tightening embankments around a river whose natural space has already been occupied. The upper policy relocates people out of flood-prone land and lets the [[YellowRiver|Yellow River]] resume a northern outlet; the middle policy divides water through canals, stone embankments, and sluices; the lower policy repairs old levees but keeps the recurring burden intact.

His reasoning links engineering to political economy. He argues that early settlement avoided river paths, while Warring States levees by Qi, Zhao, and Wei made local self-protection create reciprocal harm. In the episode's modernizing reading, Jia Rang becomes a model for [[FloodplainRetreatGovernance|floodplain retreat governance]]: solve the land-water structure before endlessly paying for repairs.

## Key Characteristics

- Jia Rang ranks water-control policies by whether they address the structure of flood risk rather than by short-term convenience.
- His upper policy accepts one-time relocation and loss to reduce recurring levee repair and catastrophic breach risk.
- His middle policy preserves a more engineered compromise through canals, sluices, and irrigation.
- His lower policy is criticized because repairing old levees consumes labor and money without ending the river-space conflict.
- His argument treats land use, settlement location, fiscal burden, and hydraulic danger as one governance system.
