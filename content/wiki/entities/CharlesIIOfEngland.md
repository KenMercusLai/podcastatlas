# Charles II of England

## Overview

Charles II of England is represented here as the Restoration monarch whose 1666 vest announcement gives the episode its sharpest origin point for the modern three-piece suit.

## Current Profile

[[459-the-suit-savile-row-and-smartly-dressed-men-glt8912379704]] presents Charles's clothing initiative as political as well as aesthetic. A coordinated vest, coat, and breeches answered republican criticism of court luxury, projected thrift after the Great Fire of London, supported English wool, and distinguished the court from French fashion. The episode therefore places him at an important codifying moment without proving that one decree created the suit from nothing.

## Key Characteristics

- Restoration king using dress as a language of court authority.
- Announcer of the vest fashion recorded by Samuel Pepys in October 1666.
- Promoter of a coordinated three-part male ensemble in the episode's account.
- Political appropriator of sober, anti-luxury criticism.
- Patron of a style framed as English, economical, and anti-French.

## Evidence

- Recorded announcement: [[459-the-suit-savile-row-and-smartly-dressed-men-glt8912379704]] cites Pepys's report that Charles intended to establish a lasting vest fashion on 7 October 1666.
- Political meaning: [[459-the-suit-savile-row-and-smartly-dressed-men-glt8912379704]] connects the ensemble to thrift, English wool, anti-French feeling, and a royal response to republican attacks on luxury.
- Long-term significance: [[459-the-suit-savile-row-and-smartly-dressed-men-glt8912379704]] treats the vest, coat, and breeches combination as an ancestor of the later three-piece suit.

## Qualifications

This profile covers only the episode's clothing-history argument, not Charles II's wider reign. The precise claim that he “invented” the suit on one date compresses a longer evolution in tailoring, textiles, court dress, religion, and military style.
