# Project Lightwell

## Overview
Project Lightwell is the [[IBM]] and [[RedHat|Red Hat]] initiative presented in a sponsored episode as a shared mechanism for finding, repairing, and returning security fixes across widely reused open-source software.

## Current Profile
The source describes a $5 billion commitment and an aspiration to mobilize more than 20,000 engineers. Participating organizations would submit vulnerabilities, Lightwell would develop fixes, and repairs would flow both to customers and to relevant upstream communities. The proposed value is pooled capacity: institutions avoid privately forking tens of thousands of dependencies while communities gain remediation resources. The episode does not establish operating governance, community consent, liability, prioritization rules, delivery milestones, or measured outcomes.

## Key Characteristics
- Proposed by IBM and Red Hat as a large-scale open-source security effort.
- Presented as a clearinghouse between institutional users, remediation teams, and upstream communities.
- Seeks to pool vulnerability information and repair capacity rather than duplicate private forks.
- Claims a $5 billion commitment and a potential workforce above 20,000 people.
- Counts financial institutions among early participants because they depend on shared software and face concentrated cyber risk.
