# Fetterman Fight

## Overview

The Fetterman Fight was the 1866 destruction of Captain William Fetterman's force near Fort Phil Kearny by Lakota, Cheyenne, and Arapaho fighters during [[RedCloud]]'s war over the Bozeman Trail.

## Current Profile

The episode says [[CrazyHorse]] helped lure Fetterman and roughly eighty soldiers beyond the fort's support into an ambush in which the entire U.S. detachment was killed. Mutilation of bodies intensified American outrage and the event's description as a massacre, while a Lakota perspective could frame the same encounter as a victory over an armed invading force.

The fight elevated Crazy Horse's reputation, reinforced the Lakota image as a formidable Plains opponent, and contributed to a settlement in which the United States closed the Bozeman Trail and abandoned the forts. It therefore mattered both as a battlefield result and as leverage in the road to the [[GreatSiouxReservation]].

## Key Characteristics

- It destroyed an entire U.S. Army detachment after a successful decoy and ambush.
- It formed part of a wider war over forts and travel through contested Lakota country.
- The treatment of the dead made “massacre” central to U.S. memory of the event.
- Lakota framing as victory exposes the political work done by battlefield labels.
- Its consequences helped move the United States toward fort abandonment and negotiation.

## Qualifications

- This page follows one narrative-history episode and does not independently compare military reports, archaeology, or Lakota and allied oral histories.
- The source gives approximate force size and compresses the roles of multiple Native groups and leaders.
- Calling the encounter a victory need not deny postmortem violence; calling it a massacre can obscure that the defeated force was an armed military column.
- Exact tactics, individual actions, casualty details, and responsibility for mutilation remain source-scoped.
