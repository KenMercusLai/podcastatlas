# Edward P. King

## Overview

Edward P. King was the American commander who surrendered the starving and disease-stricken Bataan force after judging further resistance militarily futile and needlessly lethal.

## Current Profile

[[dchha64-supernova-in-the-east-iii-dchha64-supernova-in-the-east-iii|Supernova in the East III]] presents King at the point where a long defense had lost its material basis. Food, medicine, ammunition, and physical strength had fallen while the final Japanese offensive broke the remaining line.

King chose capitulation despite Jonathan Wainwright's instruction to continue fighting. The episode frames his willingness to assume personal responsibility as a form of command courage distinct from the prestige attached to hopeless resistance: he accepted likely disgrace in order to reduce deaths after the outcome could no longer be changed.

## Key Characteristics

- Commanded the Bataan force at the time of its surrender.
- Faced troops depleted by hunger, disease, ammunition shortage, and final defensive collapse.
- Disobeyed a continuing-resistance order when he judged further combat futile.
- Took responsibility for capitulation rather than shifting the decision to subordinates.
- Represents a humane command choice whose consequences were overtaken by brutal captivity.

## Evidence

- Material collapse: [[dchha64-supernova-in-the-east-iii-dchha64-supernova-in-the-east-iii]] describes severe shortages, disease, malnutrition, and loss of combat capacity before the final offensive.
- Surrender judgment: [[dchha64-supernova-in-the-east-iii-dchha64-supernova-in-the-east-iii]] attributes to King the conclusion that continued resistance would cause meaningless death.
- Responsibility: [[dchha64-supernova-in-the-east-iii-dchha64-supernova-in-the-east-iii]] says King personally assumed the decision rather than passing it downward.

## Qualifications

The source offers a morally favorable interpretation of King's decision but is not a full command archive. It does not establish every alternative available, the detailed communications with Wainwright, or whether surrender terms could have changed the subsequent treatment of prisoners.
