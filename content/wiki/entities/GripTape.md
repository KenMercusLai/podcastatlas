# GripTape

## Overview
GripTape is a youth-development initiative represented here through a ten-week, self-directed learning challenge intended to broaden access to purpose exploration.

## Current Profile
The episode describes GripTape as asking young people what they want to learn and then supporting sustained action around that choice. Each participant works with an adult “champion” who need not be a subject expert; the adult checks in, listens, and learns alongside the young person rather than predetermining the project.

The model matters to the wiki less as a proven intervention than as an applied design hypothesis. It combines choice, duration, action, relationship, and low-gate support to let young people test possible identities. Its access aim challenges the idea that exploratory time, networks, and individualized encouragement should be reserved for wealthy, highly connected, or already high-performing students.

## Key Characteristics
- Centers a young person's chosen learning question or project.
- Uses a bounded ten-week challenge to turn interest into sustained action.
- Pairs participants with a supportive adult champion rather than requiring subject-matter expertise.
- Treats listening and accompaniment as meaningful forms of developmental support.
- Seeks to widen access to identity and purpose exploration beyond elite students and networks.

## Qualifications
The current evidence is one episode summary and does not provide program governance, selection criteria, participant counts, completion rates, comparison groups, outcome measures, costs, safeguarding practices, or long-term follow-up. The program is presented as being studied; the source therefore supports its design and intended mechanism, not a settled claim of effectiveness.
