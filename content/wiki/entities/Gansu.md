## Overview
Gansu / 甘肃 now appears in the wiki through two unrelated but geographically compatible branches: a modern commercial-space landing setting and a northwest historical-geography corridor. The Longshan episode shifts Gansu from a passing launch-site location into part of the Guanzhong-Longyou-Hexi-Western Regions route world.

## Current Profile
In the earlier business-news source, Gansu is the landing setting for [[LandSpace|蓝箭航天]]'s [[Zhuque3|朱雀三号]] Yao-2 first-stage recovery. The source says the booster landed there roughly eight minutes after launch on August 19, 2026.

In [[499-fanyue-longban-yanhaijun-tan-zuowei-gudai-zhongguo-dongxi-hudong-zhouxin-de-longshan-lrm9qbtncvuh2utckq7hogpoxhay|499 翻越陇坂]], Gansu is part of the larger northwest route field around [[LongshanGansuShaanxi|陇山 / 龙山]], [[GuanlongAncientRoad|关陇古道]], Longyou, Hexi, and the [[WesternRegionsProtectorate|Western Regions]]. The episode emphasizes that places such as Pingliang, Qingyang, and Tianshui can be administratively Gansu while remaining deeply connected to Shaanxi and [[GuanzhongRegion|关中]] through dialect, food, mobility, and older historical routes.

## Key Characteristics
- Modern launch and recovery setting in the commercial-space source.
- Northwest China region tied to Longshan, Longyou, Hexi, and Guanlong route history in the historical-geography source.
- Administrative province whose eastern and southeastern areas can remain culturally and economically oriented toward Shaanxi and Guanzhong.
- Fieldwork setting where mountains, villages, old-road traces, and local memory support historical interpretation.
- Route geography connecting ancient east-west movement with modern transport continuity.

## Qualifications

The Longshan source's regional claims should not be flattened into all of Gansu; they focus especially on the Longshan/Longyou/Guanlong corridor and Gansu-Shaanxi interaction.
