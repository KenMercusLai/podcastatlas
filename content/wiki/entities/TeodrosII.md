# Teodros II

## Overview

Teodros II is presented by [[705-queen-victorias-revenge-the-mad-emperor-of-abyssinia-part-1-glt8608697296]] as the [[Abyssinia|Abyssinian]] emperor whose modernizing ambitions, religious monarchy, anger at British neglect, and hostage-taking lead toward the [[AbyssinianExpedition1868|Abyssinian Expedition of 1868]].

## Current Profile

The source follows Teodros from Kassa, a successful warlord in a fragmented Abyssinia, into the messianic imperial name Teodros II in 1855. He is portrayed as energetic, tactful, charismatic, and reform-minded at first, but also proud, violent, religiously intense, and increasingly suspicious. His admiration for Britain and desire for Christian alliance turn into hostility when [[QueenVictoria]]'s answer does not arrive and when missionaries appear to insult his lineage and religious authority.

## Key Characteristics

- He combines reunification ambition with a belief that Abyssinia needs roads, justice reform, anti-slavery action, and military modernization.
- He sees diplomacy through a Christian-monarchical lens, especially under pressure from Muslim powers and European rivalry.
- His relationship with Britain is highly symbolic: recognition, reply, and respectful attention matter alongside weapons and technicians.
- His rule becomes increasingly coercive after rebellion, personal loss, drinking, missionary conflict, and distrust.
- His hostage-taking turns envoys, missionaries, and technicians into leverage over Britain.

## Evidence

- Modernizer and reunifier: [[705-queen-victorias-revenge-the-mad-emperor-of-abyssinia-part-1-glt8608697296]] says he wants to crush warlords, build roads, reform royal justice, fight slavery, modernize dress, and improve military capability.
- Religious and diplomatic worldview: [[705-queen-victorias-revenge-the-mad-emperor-of-abyssinia-part-1-glt8608697296]] says he asks [[QueenVictoria]] for Christian friendship and struggles to understand why Britain and France had allied with the [[OttomanEmpire]] against Russia in the Crimean War.
- Coercive turn: [[705-queen-victorias-revenge-the-mad-emperor-of-abyssinia-part-1-glt8608697296]] follows his anger after Tawabak's death, his punitive mutilations, artillery obsession, Stern's beating, Cameron's imprisonment, [[HormuzdRassam]]'s arrest, and the demand for technicians and guns.

## Qualifications

The source repeatedly warns against a simple caricature. Teodros is shown through European and missionary records as modernizer, Christian ruler, admirer of Britain, violent autocrat, possible sufferer of mental collapse, and hostage-taker. His inner motives remain source-scoped rather than settled.
