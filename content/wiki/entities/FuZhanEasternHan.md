## Overview

Fu Zhan / 傅湛 enters the wiki as the Eastern Han official whose remonstrance redirects [[LiuXiu|光武帝刘秀]] away from personally attacking [[PengChongEasternHan|彭宠]] in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-690-jiaoyou-bushen-you-duo-kepa-kankan-tianrong-de-jingli-lpbms8pebzdo2ldiwho1cba81njk|Hanji 690]]. He argues that closer internal regions are still unsettled and that Yuyang is a distant, harder target, making the proposed expedition a case of abandoning the near and easy for the far and hard.

## Current Profile

The source places Fu Zhan at a decision point rather than inside a long biography. Liu Xiu reaches Lu Nu and prepares to campaign personally against Peng Chong. Fu Zhan objects by comparing the strategic value and distance of targets: Yan, Yu, Qing, and Yi still suffer disorder inside the realm, while Yuyang is remote.

Fu Zhan's profile is therefore a remonstrance-and-sequencing case. He does not deny that Peng Chong is a threat; he changes the order in which Liu Xiu should spend imperial attention. Liu Xiu accepts the advice and abandons the personal expedition.

## Key Characteristics

- Guangwu-era remonstrant who changes an imperial campaign decision.
- Strategy adviser focused on target sequence, distance, and consolidation priority.
- Voice for securing closer domestic disorder before personally pursuing a remote rebel.
- Example of advice becoming policy because Liu Xiu accepts it.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to one remonstrance scene. It does not establish Fu Zhan's complete office history or all later policy influence. The source supports a target-priority claim, not a blanket claim that Peng Chong should have been ignored.
