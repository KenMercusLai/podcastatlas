# Henry Livingston Jr.

## Overview

Henry Livingston Jr. was a writer proposed by his descendants and [[DonFoster]] as the alternative author of [[AVisitFromStNicholas|“A Visit from St. Nicholas”]].

## Current Profile

[[twas-the-night-before-christmas-twas-the-night-before-christmas]] presents Livingston as a plausible stylistic comparison rather than the best-supported author. His cheerful occasional verse and use of a similar anapestic rhythm gave [[MaryVanDusen]] and Foster material for an attribution case.

The documentary basis remains weak. Livingston never publicly claimed the poem before his death in 1828, descendants' recollections were recorded across generations, and a reportedly handwritten copy could not be examined because it was said to have burned. The episode ultimately finds Moore's relevant surviving writing and the rebuttal of family chronology more persuasive.

## Key Characteristics

- Writer of convivial and festive occasional verse.
- Used rhythms comparable to the disputed poem's anapestic meter.
- Named by descendants as the poem's true author.
- Made no known public authorship claim before his death in 1828.
- Lacks a surviving manuscript tying him directly to the poem.

## Evidence

- Stylistic plausibility: [[twas-the-night-before-christmas-twas-the-night-before-christmas]] reports similarities in meter, vocabulary, and cheerful tone emphasized by Foster.
- Family claim: [[twas-the-night-before-christmas-twas-the-night-before-christmas]] describes descendants' recollections and their multigenerational research effort.
- Documentary limits: [[twas-the-night-before-christmas-twas-the-night-before-christmas]] records the absent public claim, lost-manuscript story, and chronological problems identified by Kaller.

## Qualifications

Failure to preserve a manuscript does not prove Livingston could not have written the poem, and family memory is evidence rather than nothing. It is nevertheless weaker than contemporaneous, verifiable documentation and cannot bear the attribution by itself. The episode's final judgment favors Moore.

## What Changed

- Established Livingston as a serious historical claimant without elevating the claim to parity with Moore's evidence.
- Separated stylistic resemblance from documentary proof.
