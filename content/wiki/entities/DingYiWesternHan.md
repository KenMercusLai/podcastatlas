## Overview

丁义 / Ding Yi is presented as the 乐成侯 who brings [[LuanDaWesternHan|栾大]] to [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] through a recommendation chain involving his sister, the widow of 胶东康王刘寄, and is later executed after Luan Da's supernatural claims and false report collapse.

## Current Profile

Hanji 344-1 gives Ding Yi a narrow but more concrete access role. His sister, presented as the childless widow of 胶东康王刘寄 and in conflict with the successor, wants to gain Wudi's favor after 少翁's death; Luan Da reaches the emperor through Ding Yi. The source does not clarify whether Ding Yi independently endorses Luan Da's claims, merely carries the recommendation, or knows how the “斗棋” demonstration works.

Hanji 344-2 supplies the liability endpoint. When Luan Da is executed, Ding Yi is publicly beheaded, showing that a coercive patron can extend responsibility from a failed specialist to the broker who supplied access even when the surviving source note does not establish the broker's knowledge or intent.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han marquis identified by the title 乐成侯.
- Court intermediary in a family-linked recommendation chain that brings Luan Da to Han Wudi.
- Recommender whose own knowledge and motive are not supplied.
- Subject of derivative accountability after Luan Da's claims are exposed.
