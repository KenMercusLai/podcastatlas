## Overview

刘兴居济北叛乱 / Liu Xingju's Jibei Rebellion is the failed uprising that [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-213-gei-liubang-dai-lvmao-de-shenyiqi-xiachang-ruhe-2-lntutfvfj3vdirbrjxlg1d5vsiip|Hanji 213]] links to an under-rewarded anti-Lü coalition branch, a mistaken opportunity assessment, and the Han court's combined use of military force and conditional pardon.

## Current Profile

The source places the rebellion after [[LiuXiangQiKingWesternHan|刘襄]] and [[LiuZhangChengyangKingWesternHan|刘章]] had died. [[LiuXingjuWesternHan|刘兴居]], already dissatisfied with the post-Lü settlement, reportedly believed [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]] had left to campaign personally against Xiongnu and treated the ruler's absence as a window for revolt.

The court's response separates capital security, field suppression, and political fragmentation. [[GuanYing|灌婴]] is recalled toward Chang'an, [[ChaiWu|柴武]] receives the main punitive command, another force guards Xingyang, and an edict promises pardon to Jibei officials and residents who submit before the army arrives. The episode presents this deadline as the mechanism that breaks cohesion before Liu Xingju's defeat and suicide.

## Key Characteristics

- Same-surname royal rebellion rooted in a disputed post-coalition reward settlement.
- Opportunity move based on a mistaken reading of the emperor's travel and military intentions.
- Central response divided between capital defense, blocking deployment, and offensive command.
- Pardon offer targeted at followers rather than the rebel prince.
- Rapid collapse ending in Liu Xingju's defeat and suicide.

## Qualifications

The current profile is bounded to one podcast summary. It does not independently establish the rebel coalition's size, the precise command hierarchy, force numbers, sequence of engagements, surrender volume, or whether the pardon rather than military pressure was the decisive cause of collapse. The grievance-and-fear explanation is the episode's interpretation.

- Treated the pardon as a claimed cohesion effect rather than proof that force was unimportant.
