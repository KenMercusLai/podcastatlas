# Francis Drake

[[694-elizabeth-i-vs-the-catholics-a-treacherous-conspiracy-part-4-glt1627515087]] adds Drake's return from circumnavigation in September 1580 as a national and confessional event. He brings treasure worth about 400,000 pounds, fears [[ElizabethI|Elizabeth I]] may return it to [[Spain]], and becomes the test case for whether England will appease [[PhilipII|Philip II]] or profit from anti-Spanish raiding.

The episode makes Drake central to [[PrivateeringPlausibleDeniability]]. Elizabeth quietly keeps a large share of the treasure, gives some captured silver to the Dutch rebels, denies Spanish accusations, and then has Drake knighted aboard the [[GoldenHind]] on 1 April 1581 through a French envoy rather than her own hand.

Francis Drake appears in [[695-elizabeth-i-vs-the-catholics-the-shadow-war-part-5-glt5637968455]] as the English sailor and raider whose anti-Spanish expedition runs alongside [[ElizabethI|Elizabeth I]]'s reluctant entry into the [[LowCountries|Low Countries]] crisis. The episode says he had already been knighted after circumnavigation and then spent quieter years in property, Parliament, Plymouth office, and local trade regulation before war opportunity returned.

Drake's 1585-1586 expedition attacks Spanish interests including Vigo, Santo Domingo, and Cartagena. The source says it is less lucrative than hoped because it does not capture treasure ships, but strategically important because it exposes the vulnerability of [[PhilipII|Philip II]]'s American empire and contributes to pressure on Spanish credit.

Drake also functions as a contrast to [[PhilipSidney|Sir Philip Sidney]]. Sidney tries to join Drake's expedition when Elizabeth temporarily reverses his [[Flushing]] appointment, but is recalled and sent to the Dutch theatre instead.
