# Robin Carhart-Harris

## Overview
Robin Carhart-Harris is presented in the source as a UCSF professor of neurology and psychiatry and a leading researcher on psychedelic drugs, brain networks, and psychedelic-assisted therapy.

## Current Profile
In [[the-science-of-psychedelics-for-mental-health-dr-robin-carhart-harris-scim8487331019]], Carhart-Harris links classic psychedelics' serotonin 2A pharmacology with changes in subjective experience and whole-brain organization. He argues that therapeutic research must take the experience seriously: trust, willingness to let go, supported confrontation with difficult material, emotional release, insight, and subsequent integration may be parts of the intervention rather than noise around a drug effect.

His research profile is also strongly qualified. He describes increased global connectivity and reduced modularity during psychedelic states, and reports promising depression, anorexia, fibromyalgia, cognitive-flexibility, EEG, and imaging findings. At the same time, he labels microdosing evidence thin, distinguishes published from unpublished work, cautions against overextending network measures, acknowledges relapse, and emphasizes professional safeguards and legal constraints.

## Key Characteristics
- Connects psychedelic pharmacology, subjective experience, psychotherapy, and brain-network imaging.
- Treats “trust, let go, be open” as a clinical-process hypothesis rather than a universal self-use instruction.
- Distinguishes classic psychedelics from MDMA, ketamine, cannabis, and non-hallucinogenic serotonin 2A drug development.
- Uses global connectivity and modularity as candidate correlates while warning against simple causal interpretation.
- Reports promising clinical signals alongside explicit publication, replication, durability, and access limits.
- Supports regulated, trained, professionally safeguarded care rather than general recreational or unsupervised use.
