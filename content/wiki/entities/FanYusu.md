# 范雨素 / Fan Yusu

## Overview
范雨素 / Fan Yusu is a writer used by the episode as a comparison figure for entering city life with modest expectations while sustaining learning and literary practice.

## Current Profile
The hosts present Fan as having a more concrete relation to writing than [[LiuXiaoyang|刘晓漾]] and as entering the city without the elevated expectations produced by prior fame. They mention auditing courses, attempting adult education, and valuing opportunities for conversation and respect. Her source-specific role is to show how gradual accumulation and earlier exposure to setbacks may produce a different relationship to urban life and recognition.

## Key Characteristics
- Writer used as an example of concrete and sustained practice.
- Migrant-city comparison framed around gradual accumulation rather than a high public starting point.
- Learner associated with courses, adult education, and valued intellectual exchange.
- Counterexample to treating fame as the necessary first step toward creative identity.

## Evidence
- Practice comparison: [[ep179-womende-neixin-dou-zhuzhe-yige-liuxiaoyang-zuoziji-shi-yao-fuchu-juda-daijia-ckwriaset1ykabaaaadc008h]] pairs Fan Yusu with [[YuXiuhua|余秀华]] as writers whose work preceded wider attention.
- Urban accumulation: [[ep179-womende-neixin-dou-zhuzhe-yige-liuxiaoyang-zuoziji-shi-yao-fuchu-juda-daijia-ckwriaset1ykabaaaadc008h]] attributes course auditing, adult-exam attempts, and appreciation of exchange opportunities to Fan’s path.
- Expectation contrast: [[ep179-womende-neixin-dou-zhuzhe-yige-liuxiaoyang-zuoziji-shi-yao-fuchu-juda-daijia-ckwriaset1ykabaaaadc008h]] argues that a lower public starting point may reduce the shame attached to incremental progress, while acknowledging this as interpretation.

## Qualifications
The page reflects one podcast’s compressed comparison rather than a complete biography or literary assessment. It does not establish that hardship is necessary for creativity or that Fan’s circumstances are directly comparable to Liu Xiaoyang’s.
