# Indian Muslims

## Overview

Indian Muslims are the minority community most directly affected in the source set by state uniform-civil-code changes and by identity-proxied citizenship enforcement near the Bangladesh border.

## Current Profile

The family-law source keeps a real tension visible: equal inheritance and polygamy restrictions may benefit some women, yet rules presented as universal can require Muslims to conform to majority-rooted legal norms. The border source adds a more coercive risk: Bengali language and Muslim-sounding names can be used as foreignness cues during informal pushbacks and citizenship checks.

## Key Characteristics

- Bear much of the practical change from state-level uniform civil codes.
- Include women who may gain specific legal protections even within a majoritarian policy design.
- Face surveillance concerns through mandatory relationship registration.
- Can be treated as presumptively foreign when Bengali identity and Muslim identity overlap near the border.
- Experience a legal asymmetry when neighboring-country non-Muslim minorities receive faster citizenship access.

## Qualifications

Indian Muslims are diverse, and the sources cover specific state laws and a border region rather than the community as a whole. Benefits, burdens, and wrongful-enforcement claims remain source-bounded.
