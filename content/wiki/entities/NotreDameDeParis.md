# Notre-Dame de Paris

## Overview

Notre-Dame de Paris is the medieval cathedral used in the current wiki source as a concentrated symbol of [[Paris]], French historical continuity, religious heritage, and international emotional attachment.

## Current Profile

[[57-paris-glt6186083108]] places Notre-Dame inside the sustained expansion of twelfth- and thirteenth-century Paris, when the city combined royal, episcopal, university, and commercial roles. The cathedral therefore represents not an isolated monument but an institution formed within a dense and growing medieval capital.

The episode gives Notre-Dame two modern symbolic moments. The 2019 fire produced grief well beyond practicing Catholics or people who had visited France, while [[CharlesDeGaulle|Charles de Gaulle]]'s service there during the liberation of Paris in August 1944 made the building a stage for restored French authority under continuing danger.

## Key Characteristics

- Medieval cathedral tied to the demographic and institutional expansion of Paris.
- Religious building whose public meaning extends beyond religious affiliation.
- Symbol of historical continuity made visible through worldwide reaction to the 2019 fire.
- National ceremonial stage during de Gaulle's entry into liberated Paris in 1944.
- Urban monument whose meaning joins architecture, memory, religion, and political legitimacy.

## Evidence

- Medieval formation: [[57-paris-glt6186083108]] connects the cathedral's long construction to roughly 150 years of Parisian growth.
- Transnational attachment: [[57-paris-glt6186083108]] reports that the 2019 fire caused intense responses in France and abroad, including among people who had never visited.
- Liberation ceremony: [[57-paris-glt6186083108]] describes de Gaulle entering the cathedral for a short service while shooting and fear of panic persisted.

## Qualifications

This source offers a symbolic urban history rather than a full architectural, ecclesiastical, conservation, or restoration history. Crowd sizes, the exact 1944 shooting sequence, and the breadth of global reaction remain episode-attributed.
