# Mississippi

## Overview
Mississippi appears in the wiki as both an online-regulation jurisdiction and a rural agricultural field site where white South African migrant labor exposes local race, work, and belonging tensions.

## Current Profile
One branch uses Mississippi as a state-level internet-law example: [[Bluesky]] stopped service for users there because age-verification requirements created legal exposure. In that branch, Mississippi is a case for [[AgeVerificationPatchwork]] and the uneven state-by-state effects of online compliance law.

A second branch adds a very different Mississippi. [[LiuXiaoqian|Liu Xiaoqian]] reports from the state's central agricultural region, where white South Africans gather around farm work, restaurants, gas stations, employer-sponsored visas, and local social boundaries. The state becomes a place where U.S. racial history, local Black labor questions, white rural communities, and Trump-era immigration selectivity intersect.

## Key Characteristics
- U.S. state where platform access can change because of state-level age-verification law.
- Rural agricultural setting where highly mechanized farms still depend on seasonal or specialized labor.
- Field site for [[SouthAfricanWhiteMigrantLabor]] and employer-dependent visa anxiety.
- Place where local Black unemployment, farm hiring, and alleged pay differences make migrant labor racially charged.
- Social context in which shared whiteness does not automatically make foreign white workers fully local.

## Evidence
- Age-verification jurisdiction: [[tech-20251226-1226-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20251226-1226-mp-tech-pod-128]] says [[Bluesky]] stopped service for Mississippi users because of legal-exposure concerns around age-verification requirements.
- Patchwork effect: [[tech-20251226-1226-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20251226-1226-mp-tech-pod-128]] uses Mississippi to show how lawful internet access can differ by state.
- Agricultural labor field site: [[398-yi-chang-meiguo-guancha-mantan-shijiebei-hongpai-nanfei-bairen-laogong-b28f7eaa-c801-4d5f-a6e5-35326d335df7]] says Liu traveled to central Mississippi after noticing reports of white South Africans working in U.S. agricultural regions.
- Local social boundary: [[398-yi-chang-meiguo-guancha-mantan-shijiebei-hongpai-nanfei-bairen-laogong-b28f7eaa-c801-4d5f-a6e5-35326d335df7]] describes white South African workers as visible through dress, accent, gathering habits, and a partial lack of acceptance by local white residents.
- Race and labor tension: [[398-yi-chang-meiguo-guancha-mantan-shijiebei-hongpai-nanfei-bairen-laogong-b28f7eaa-c801-4d5f-a6e5-35326d335df7]] records questions about why local unemployed workers, especially Black workers, are not hired and mentions allegations of lower pay for Black workers on some farms.
