# Michael Collins (Irish revolutionary)

## Overview

Michael Collins was an Irish revolutionary and pro-treaty leader represented here through the negotiation, ratification crisis, and violent enforcement of the Anglo-Irish settlement.

## Current Profile

The episode presents Collins as a compromise-maker caught between continued war with the [[UnitedKingdom]] and an incomplete form of Irish sovereignty. He helped negotiate dominion status, accepted the oath and probable partition as the price of a settlement, then directed pro-treaty forces against anti-treaty occupiers of Dublin's Four Courts before dying in an ambush on 22 August 1922.

## Key Characteristics

- He was central to negotiating the Anglo-Irish Treaty while Eamon de Valera remained away from the London talks.
- He accepted dominion status, the royal oath, and the settlement's partition framework as a practical path out of war.
- The narrow ratification vote left him on the pro-treaty side of a split Sinn Féin and IRA.
- Under British pressure after Henry Wilson's assassination, he ordered action against the Four Courts occupiers.
- His death during the civil war made him both a state-building actor and a casualty of the nationalist split.

## Evidence

- **Negotiator and compromiser:** [[137-1922-the-birth-of-the-modern-world-part-2-glt5281126494]] identifies Collins as a principal treaty negotiator and frames the accepted terms as a disputed compromise between sovereignty and renewed war.
- **Pro-treaty commander:** [[137-1922-the-birth-of-the-modern-world-part-2-glt5281126494]] connects his 28 June order to retake the Four Courts to the opening of sustained fighting in Dublin and the wider [[IrishTreatyCivilWarSplit]].
- **Civil-war casualty:** The episode places his 22 August death in the guerrilla phase that followed early pro-treaty territorial gains. [[137-1922-the-birth-of-the-modern-world-part-2-glt5281126494]]

## Qualifications

This is a source-bounded profile rather than a full biography. The episode compresses Collins's intelligence work, revolutionary career, private judgments, relationship with de Valera, and the institutional formation of the Irish Free State. Its assignment of pressure and motive around the Four Courts action remains episode-attributed.
