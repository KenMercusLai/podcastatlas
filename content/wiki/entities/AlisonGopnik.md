# Alison Gopnik

## Overview
Alison Gopnik is a psychologist represented here through a [[HiddenBrain]] discussion of child development, play, exploration, teaching, and the limits of outcome-driven parenting.

## Current Profile
Gopnik uses the carpenter and gardener metaphors to distinguish shaping a child toward a predetermined adult outcome from creating a protected, varied environment in which multiple outcomes remain possible. Her account treats long human childhood as an exploratory period and argues that children learn not only through instruction but also through play, participation, imitation, practice, and critique.

She does not reject teaching. Instead, [[ExplorationInstructionTradeoff]] separates efficient transmission of known solutions from broad search and transfer, while [[GardenerParenting]] makes safety and environmental design compatible with uncertainty about the child's eventual identity.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames development as reciprocal, complex, and only partly controllable by adult technique.
- Uses the carpenter-gardener contrast to challenge predetermined-outcome parenting.
- Treats play as broad model-building that becomes valuable when conditions change.
- Distinguishes direct instruction from participation and apprenticeship.
- Connects children's autonomy and exploratory freedom to adaptability under uncertainty.

## Qualifications
The profile is based on one structured episode summary rather than Gopnik's full scholarship or the underlying studies. The metaphors simplify a mixed practice in which children need safety, limits, instruction, repetition, and unequal access to exploratory time and resources. Toy and robot examples do not by themselves establish long-term human educational outcomes.
