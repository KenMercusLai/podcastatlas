## Overview
亚冠 / AFC Champions League is the continental benchmark for judging [[GuangzhouFC]]'s Evergrande-era achievement and a current test of whether Chinese clubs can convert domestic resources, supporter responsibility, and squad depth into sustained Asian competitiveness.

## Current Profile
The competition has two roles in the bounded sources. Historically, it calibrates Guangzhou FC's two titles as a rare achievement requiring club strength, high-level foreign players, regional timing, and workable tournament conditions. In the current-policy branch, it reveals how domestic title pressure, draws, travel, prize money, schedule coordination, roster depth, and club incentives influence whether Chinese teams can compete seriously.

The synthesis rejects a purely moral demand that clubs "serve the league." Clubs should first be accountable to themselves and their supporters, while coefficient points, future berths, league reputation, and stronger domestic competition emerge as wider benefits. That responsibility remains bounded by rational rotation when continental and domestic goals collide.

## Key Characteristics
- Continental club competition used to measure the peak of Evergrande-era Guangzhou football.
- Hard-to-repeat achievement context because Chinese clubs now face stronger Asian competition, especially from West Asian and Saudi clubs.
- Evidence that Guangzhou FC's legacy cannot be reduced to financial scandal or domestic spending alone.
- Current incentive test shaped by schedule, travel, prize money, squad depth, opponent draw, and domestic objectives.
- Club-and-supporter responsibility whose successful pursuit can also improve league coefficients and future berths.
