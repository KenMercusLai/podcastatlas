## Overview

石奋 / Shi Fen is a Western Han courtier presented as “万石君,” a minimally educated but exceptionally deferential patriarch whose repeated conduct shapes a household reputation for filial caution and supplies an alternative to moral instruction by speech alone.

## Current Profile

The episode defines Shi Fen through routine rather than doctrine. Even after retirement he reportedly dresses formally for court ceremonies, dismounts before the palace gate despite senior privilege, and bows when the imperial carriage passes. At home he receives descendants in formal dress and bows before using an imperial gift. These acts make rank and ritual visible inside ordinary family life.

His disciplinary method is indirect but forceful. When a descendant errs, he sits silently and refuses to eat until another senior relative corrects the offender and the offender acknowledges the fault. The same norms reportedly spread to sons, descendants, and servants. The episode treats that consistency as the reason the household's conduct impresses even scholars and attracts [[EmpressDowagerDouWesternHan|窦太后]]'s support for his sons.

## Key Characteristics

- Courtier nicknamed “万石君” from the combined two-thousand-shi status attributed to him and four sons.
- Limited formal education paired with highly consistent ritual deference.
- Maintains formal court behavior after retirement and beyond situations where privilege would permit relaxation.
- Uses modeled conduct and controlled silence rather than direct verbal rebuke to discipline descendants.
- Extends household standards across sons, later descendants, and servants.
- Father of [[ShiJianWesternHan|石建]] and [[ShiQingWesternHan|石庆]], whose cautious court behavior continues the family pattern.

## Qualifications

This profile rests on one short narrative source. The title's “four reigns” conflicts with the body's “three reigns,” and neither supplies a complete career chronology. The combined salary-rank calculation, exact rituals, household members' inner responses, and claim that Confucian scholars felt ashamed are not independently verified here. Modeled restraint may produce durable norms, but silent withdrawal of food and approval also operates as household pressure; the episode does not record dissent or harm within the family.

## What Changed

- Created a focused profile separating Shi Fen's practiced ritual discipline from a generic label of “good family tradition.”
- Preserved the coercive ambiguity of silent household correction alongside the episode's praise.
