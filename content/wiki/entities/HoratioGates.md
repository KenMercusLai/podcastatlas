# Horatio Gates

## Overview

Horatio Gates appears as the American commander associated with the [[BattleOfSaratoga|Saratoga]] victory and the alternative around whom a winter movement to replace [[GeorgeWashington|George Washington]] briefly formed.

## Current Profile

The sources join Gates's battlefield reputation to its political afterlife. New England militia met Burgoyne near Bemis Heights under Gates's command, and Burgoyne's surrender made Saratoga a major American and diplomatic success. After Washington's defeats and the British occupation of Philadelphia, Gates's association with that victory made him a possible replacement pole, but the movement to remove Washington fizzled before it became a durable command change.

## Key Characteristics

- Rival military figure whose reputation rose against Washington's low point.
- Associated in the source with the political afterlife of the American success at Saratoga.
- Became the focus of a replacement movement during the Valley Forge winter.
- Functions as evidence that Washington's authority was contested rather than automatic.
- Commander associated with the British surrender after failed attacks near Bemis Heights.

## Evidence

- Context of pressure: [[683-washington-hero-of-the-revolution-part-1-glt3752138067]] says Washington suffered defeats in 1777 while the Americans won a major propaganda success at Saratoga.
- Replacement movement: [[683-washington-hero-of-the-revolution-part-1-glt3752138067]] says a movement emerged that winter to replace Washington with Horatio Gates.
- Failure to displace Washington: [[683-washington-hero-of-the-revolution-part-1-glt3752138067]] says the movement fizzled out.
- Saratoga command: [[349-the-birth-of-the-united-states-part-3-glt9804967639]] places Gates and New England militia against Burgoyne near Bemis Heights before the British surrender.
