## Overview

*Doctor Faustus* / 《浮士德博士的悲剧》 is [[ChristopherMarlowe]]'s tragedy about a scholar who exchanges his soul for twenty-four years of service from [[MephistophelesMarlowe|Mephistopheles]].

## Current Profile

The episode reads the play as a Christian tragedy that also supports a modern philosophy-of-technology question. [[FaustusMarlowe|Faustus]] seeks knowledge as command over nature, but the bargain that expands his means narrows his future, his permissible questions, and his ability to act on what he already knows.

The play's irony lies in the gap between capability and use. Faustus imagines wealth, political power, discovery, and transcendence, yet increasingly produces pranks, humiliation, court entertainment, and convincing illusions. Power does not supply him with a worthy purpose or free him from distraction.

## Key Characteristics

- Christian tragedy organized around sin, temptation, repentance, salvation, and damnation.
- Knowledge drama contrasting understanding with the wish to dominate nature.
- Bargain structure that exchanges an entire future for bounded present service.
- Spectacle structure in which simulation and entertainment displace inquiry and achievement.
- Freedom paradox in which external options expand while self-direction contracts.
