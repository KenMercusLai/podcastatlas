## Overview

郅寿 / Zhi Shou enters the wiki as the Eastern Han official whose refusal of [[DouXianEasternHan|Dou Xian]]'s private request turns direct remonstrance into a lethal political case. The source transcript renders him as 致授 and his father as 致恽; this page normalizes the durable route to 郅寿 / 郅恽 because the Dou Xian, He Chang, and Shangshu-puye sequence matches the received account.

## Current Profile

Zhi Shou's profile begins with office responsibility. In the source, Dou Xian sends a disciple or retainer with a private letter asking Zhi, then serving in the Shangshu channel, to handle a personal matter. Zhi refuses to treat Dou's household request as legitimate business, publishes the letter in court, and sends the messenger to prison.

The refusal becomes public opposition. Zhi repeatedly attacks Dou Xian's domination, invokes [[WangMang|Wang Mang]] as a warning, and criticizes both the Northern Xiongnu expedition and mansion-building by the Dou brothers. That directness makes him dangerous to Dou because it converts private pressure into visible evidence.

Dou's counterattack turns the remonstrance into criminal liability. He accuses Zhi of buying public land and slandering the court; [[EmpressDowagerDouHeLateHan|Empress Dowager Dou]] favors Dou, and Zhi receives a death sentence. [[HeChangEasternHan|He Chang]] persuades the court to reduce the sentence to exile at Hepu, but Zhi kills himself before departure, leaving the case as an early Dou-family example of [[DirectRemonstranceCriminalization|direct remonstrance criminalization]] and [[RetaliatoryPoliticalTerror|retaliatory political terror]].

## Key Characteristics

- Shangshu-channel official who refuses to process Dou Xian's private request.
- Publicizes private pressure instead of quietly yielding to an outer-relative household.
- Direct remonstrant who attacks both frontier policy and luxury construction.
- Victim of retaliatory accusation under dowager-favored Dou power.
- Death case whose mitigation fails to restore political safety.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 761 and does not reconstruct Zhi Shou's full career beyond the source's Jizhou, Shangshu, and Jingzhao notices. The transcript's 致授 form is treated as a transcription artifact, and the specific land-purchase charge is recorded as Dou Xian's accusation rather than accepted as proven misconduct.
