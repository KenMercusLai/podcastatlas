## Overview

雅各布·富格尔 / Jacob Fugger was an Augsburg merchant-banker whose mining, metal, church-finance, and royal-credit network turned commercial capital into political leverage across early-sixteenth-century Europe.

## Current Profile

The episode follows Fugger from training in Venice into lending against mines and metal output, financing the [[HabsburgDynasty]], working with the papacy, and participating in Portuguese overseas trade. His most consequential intervention was the 1519 election of [[CharlesV]], when he supplied most of the money raised by Charles's supporters to secure the electors.

The profile is morally mixed rather than celebratory. Fugger's network helped rulers and church officials mobilize resources, but it also connected debt, office, monopoly, indulgence sales, and violent political order. He funded nobles during the [[GermanPeasantsWar]], criticized the rebels, patronized Renaissance art and architecture, and established low-rent housing in Augsburg whose residents prayed for the family.

## Key Characteristics

- Built a cross-border commercial system around mining finance, metals, banking, and elite credit.
- Converted loans to rulers and mine owners into equity, output, privilege, status, and political access.
- Supplied decisive finance for Charles V's 1519 imperial election.
- Linked archiepiscopal debt and indulgence revenue to the wider [[IndulgenceEconomy]].
- Combined support for coercive elite order with art patronage and durable social housing.

## Qualifications

This is a source-bounded profile, not a full business history or independent audit of Fugger accounts. Election sums, investment returns, degrees of mining control, papal roles, the causal weight of indulgence finance in the Reformation, and claims about motives remain attributed to the episode. “Buying an emperor” is an interpretive shorthand for electoral finance and later leverage, not proof that Fugger alone controlled Charles V.

## What Changed

- Created a profile joining Fugger's commercial network to imperial election finance, religious controversy, coercive order, patronage, and welfare.

## Relationships

- [[CharlesV]] - imperial candidate whose election Fugger substantially financed and whose debts supported later leverage.
- [[HabsburgDynasty]] - ruling family whose expansion and political projects relied partly on Fugger credit.
- [[HolyRomanEmpire]] - elective imperial order in which private finance could affect succession.
- [[MartinLuther]] - reformer who attacked indulgence practice and criticized Fugger-like merchant power.
- [[IndulgenceEconomy]] - religious-financial system connected to Fugger credit and Albrecht's office.
- [[GermanPeasantsWar]] - uprising in which Fugger supported the princely side.
- [[MerchantPoliticalPowerConversion]] - comparative framework for turning capital into political status and privilege.
