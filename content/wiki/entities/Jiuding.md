## Overview

九鼎 / Jiuding are legendary ritual vessels that the current sources treat as concentrated symbols of sovereign authority. Their political force persists whether a ruler holds, seeks, threatens to seize, or merely claims association with them.

## Current Profile

The earliest current political mechanism is symbolic deterrence. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-76-shui-geile-chuqingxiangwang-de-zixin-lsut4cfic7xbbrmbmimrruf7n6y0|Zhouji 76]] says [[DongzhouWugong|东周武公]] warns Chu that the diminished [[ZhouRoyalHouse|周王室]] offers little land or population, while moving the Jiuding would advertise an attack on the Son of Heaven and invite wider opposition. The vessels are therefore valuable and dangerous for the same reason: possession can imply transferred authority, but seizure can expose illegitimate ambition.

The terminal chronology is unstable in the supplied sources. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-102-2-kongzi-zai-luguo-de-shenshi-zhimi-lphy12exg4u7f-30qkir96gv634r|Qinji 102-2]] places Qin's destruction of Eastern Zhou and movement of the Jiuding in a Qin Zhaoxiang Wang frame that conflicts with Qinji 102-1's placement of Eastern Zhou's end under Qin Zhuangxiang Wang. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-122-3-qinshihuang-jing-he-shenxian-si-qilai-le-ljnw5estg6dvgfllm3kfcq6ulhxu|Qinji 122-3]] then says one ding was believed lost in the Si River; [[QinShiHuang|秦始皇]] fasts, prays, and sends more than a thousand people to search near [[Xuzhou|彭城 / 徐州]], but does not recover it. Imperial unification therefore fails to deliver every inherited material sign of Zhou authority.

A Western Han retelling supplies reinterpretation rather than recovery. It says Yu cast nine vessels from regional bronze and images, after which they passed through Xia, Shang, and Zhou before falling into the Si River. When a ding is later found at Fenyin under [[HanWudi|汉武帝]], officials connect it to [[XinyuanPingWesternHan|新垣平]]'s earlier Zhou-ding prediction. Inspection, ceremony, congratulations, and the “宝鼎歌” make the object politically useful, but the supplied document does not establish that it was one of the Jiuding. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-345-1-hanwudi-baoding-xuanan-zhijin-rengshi-weijie-zhimi-lhlqnresx40rqnl1pjyzvxuj-sge|Hanji 345-1]]

## Key Characteristics

- Legendary group of vessels representing ordered territory and sovereign authority.
- Politically costly trophy whose seizure can advertise an attack on the Zhou Son of Heaven.
- Mobile symbol whose reported transfer from Xia to Shang, Zhou, and Qin tracks changes in rule.
- Missing object whose failed recovery exposes a gap between imperial control and inherited legitimacy.
- Reusable interpretive category applied to later ding discoveries without establishing their identity.

## Evidence

Symbolic deterrence:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-76-shui-geile-chuqingxiangwang-de-zixin-lsut4cfic7xbbrmbmimrruf7n6y0|Zhouji 76]] says moving the Jiuding to Chu would bring little material gain and could mobilize opposition by making Chu look like an attacker of the royal house.

Transfer and chronology tension:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-102-2-kongzi-zai-luguo-de-shenshi-zhimi-lphy12exg4u7f-30qkir96gv634r|Qinji 102-2]] reports Qin taking the Jiuding while preserving an explicit conflict with the neighboring source's Eastern Zhou chronology.

Loss and failed recovery:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-122-3-qinshihuang-jing-he-shenxian-si-qilai-le-ljnw5estg6dvgfllm3kfcq6ulhxu|Qinji 122-3]] says Qin Shi Huang's large ritualized search at Pengcheng fails to recover the ding believed lost in the Si River.

Later reinterpretation:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-345-1-hanwudi-baoding-xuanan-zhijin-rengshi-weijie-zhimi-lhlqnresx40rqnl1pjyzvxuj-sge|Hanji 345-1]] uses the Yu-to-Zhou transfer legend to explain why a Fenyin ding could be celebrated as a legitimacy object while leaving its Jiuding identity unproven.

## Qualifications

The page synthesizes popular-history podcast notes, not a primary-text or archaeological dossier. The sources do not establish the vessels' manufacture, number, designs, physical continuity, transfer route, loss, or survival. Qinji 102-2's terminal chronology conflicts with Qinji 102-1 and remains unresolved. Qinji 122-3 reports a belief about one ding in the Si River and a failed search, not proof that the object was there. Hanji 345-1 verifies neither the Fenyin vessel's Zhou date nor its identity as one of the Jiuding; its own supplied document stops before the promised archaeological resolution.

## What Changed

- Added the Yu-casting and Xia-Shang-Zhou transfer legend as the episode's explanation for the vessels' sovereign meaning.
- Added the Fenyin find as a later reinterpretation rather than a confirmed recovery of the Jiuding.
- Distinguished physical-vessel verification from historical identity and sacred legitimacy.
