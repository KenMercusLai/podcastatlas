## Overview
巴黎外方传教会 / Paris Foreign Missions Society is the Catholic missionary organization whose Paris archive preserves the letters, maps, missionary records, and correspondence discussed in [[100-nian-qian-dongbei-nvhai-xie-gei-bali-de-xin-duitan-lishixuezhe-liji-putongren-ruhe-chuanguo-da-lishi-3d36f181-94ad-4883-8db3-5f040b5ed45b]].

## Current Profile
In this source, the society matters less as abstract doctrine than as a rural mission institution whose priests entered Northeast villages, mapped Catholic settlements, recorded believers, and wrote home. Its presence is inseparable from the treaty context after the Opium War, but the episode also shows missionaries becoming local actors whose records now preserve names, places, emotions, drawings, and daily life that Chinese administrative archives often missed.

## Key Characteristics
- It is presented as a Catholic overseas mission organization connected to France, Rome, and East Asian mission history.
- Its Northeast practice emphasized rural villages rather than only elite conversion or court-centered learning.
- Its archives include Chinese letters, mission maps, missionary reports, and private family correspondence.
- Its missionaries helped make [[SantaiZiCatholicVillage|三台子]] important inside a Catholic spatial imagination.
- Its work cannot be separated from treaty protection and imperial pressure, but individual missionaries also became embedded in local communities.

## Qualifications
The source is a historical interview and does not provide a full institutional history of the society. Claims here should stay bounded to the Northeast China mission and archive case unless later sources widen the profile.
