# Henry II of France / 亨利二世

## Overview

Henry II of France is the French king whose profile joins formal court combat and fatal tournament risk to military and dynastic intervention on behalf of [[MaryQueenOfScots|Mary, Queen of Scots]].

## Current Profile

The dueling source places Henry at the boundary between supervised noble violence and honor culture. A 1547 duel held in his presence used court, oath, audience, and procedure to make violence official, yet royal supervision did not reliably contain it. His own later fatal tournament injury makes the same danger personal: ceremony and aristocratic status could not make armed spectacle safe.

In Scottish statecraft, Henry's accession after Francis I strengthened the Guise position at the French court. Following the Scottish defeat at [[BattleOfPinkie|Pinkie]], he sent a large expedition, accepted [[JamesHamiltonEarlOfArran|Arran]]'s settlement, and arranged Mary's betrothal to his son [[FrancisII|Francis]]. French force and marriage thus prevented the English [[RoughWooing|Rough Wooing]] from obtaining Mary while increasing French leverage over Scotland.

## Key Characteristics

- French king presiding over formal aristocratic combat that procedure could not fully contain.
- Tournament participant whose own fatal injury exposed ceremonial violence's danger.
- Ally of the Guise family and patron of Mary Stuart's French dynastic future.
- Ruler who converted military intervention in Scotland into a royal marriage settlement.

## Evidence

- Duel and tournament branch: [[78-xifang-juedou-shi-chujian-ba-weile-rongyu-687731000]] connects Henry to the 1547 supervised duel and his fatal tournament injury.
- Scottish intervention: [[584-mary-queen-of-scots-birth-of-a-legend-part-1-glt7723962252]] links his accession, Guise ties, expedition, Arran settlement, and Mary's betrothal and transfer.

## Qualifications

The sources provide two selective episodes rather than a full reign. The duel source's institutional transition and the Mary source's troop numbers, personal closeness to the Guises, bargaining motives, and share of responsibility for the Scottish settlement remain source-scoped.
