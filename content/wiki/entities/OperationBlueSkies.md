# Operation Blue Skies

## Overview

Operation Blue Skies is the North Atlantic trial in the source testing whether air-traffic control can reduce warming aircraft contrails through small altitude changes.

## Current Profile

The operation is presented as a practical [[ContrailClimateMitigation]] test. Persistent contrails form in cold, damp ice-supersaturated layers; if pilots know where those layers are, they can often fly slightly lower and avoid producing long-lived clouds.

The trial's value comes from measurement as much as routing. The source says the North Atlantic is continuously observed by weather satellites, while [[Google]] uses machine learning to connect observed contrails to individual flights. That makes the region a concentrated test bed for a climate intervention that may be cheap in aggregate but uneven in airline-level costs.

## Key Characteristics

- North Atlantic aviation trial focused on persistent aircraft contrails.
- Test of routine, safe air-traffic-control altitude changes rather than a new aircraft technology.
- Measurement case using visible and infrared satellite observation plus Google machine learning.
- Implementation case where small affected-flight costs could create airline lobbying and cost-allocation disputes.

## Evidence

- Trial identity: [[all-right-on-the-night-afds-victory-6a9e8573fe3b7e34fd2d258f]] names the North Atlantic trial as Operation Blue Skies.
- Flight adjustment: [[all-right-on-the-night-afds-victory-6a9e8573fe3b7e34fd2d258f]] says dropping a few hundred metres can often avoid a contrail-forming layer.
- Measurement setup: [[all-right-on-the-night-afds-victory-6a9e8573fe3b7e34fd2d258f]] says weather satellites observe the region in visible and infrared wavelengths while Google uses machine learning to identify contrails from flights.
- Cost profile: [[all-right-on-the-night-afds-victory-6a9e8573fe3b7e34fd2d258f]] says affected flights may add a few minutes and 1-2% fuel cost.

## Qualifications

The source describes a trial, not a completed global system. It does not prove that the technique will scale across regions, regulators, airlines, weather forecasting systems, or safety regimes.
