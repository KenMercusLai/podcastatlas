## Overview

徐稚 / Xu Zhi is the late-Han scholar whose Hanji 861 role is to caution [[GuoTaiLateHan|郭泰]] against exhausting himself trying to hold up a collapsing order. The episode's transcript says "徐志"; this page uses 徐稚 because the received passage and surrounding names identify that figure.

## Current Profile

Xu Zhi's page is currently source-scoped to one intervention. After Guo Tai refuses office yet keeps moving around Luoyang to discover and encourage talent, Xu Zhi writes to him with the image of a great tree about to fall: a single rope cannot hold it. The counsel is not a rejection of Guo Tai's virtue; it is a judgment about scale and timing.

Guo Tai's response makes Xu Zhi more than a side name. He receives the letter as instruction, saying he accepts it as a teacherly model. The scene therefore turns the episode's teacher theme back on Guo Tai: even the teacher remains teachable when the situation exceeds his power.

## Key Characteristics

- Late-Han scholar whose current wiki role is a cautioning correspondent.
- Voice of scale judgment against overextended moral effort.
- Figure whose counsel Guo Tai receives as teacherly admonition.
- Source-scoped support for the boundary between teaching people and saving a failing order.
