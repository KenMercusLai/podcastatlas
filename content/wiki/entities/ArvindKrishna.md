# Arvind Krishna

## Overview
Arvind Krishna is the [[IBM]] executive in the All-In conversation who connects quantum-computing milestones and cryptographic migration to enterprise AI economics, software verification, national competitiveness, and vertically integrated technology strategy.

## Current Profile
Krishna's position is preparedness-oriented rather than prediction-dependent. Because encrypted data can be retained for later decryption and infrastructure replacement takes years, he argues that organizations should begin quantum-safe migration before a cryptographically relevant machine exists. He also favors task-matched model deployment, continued responsibility for agent-written code, and selective vertical integration around differentiated layers rather than ownership of every commodity component.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses error correction, independent verification, logical capacity, and executable gate growth as milestones for useful quantum computing.
- Treats cryptographic migration as a current infrastructure and procurement problem under timeline uncertainty.
- Distinguishes frontier experimentation from lower-cost, controlled production inference.
- Regards coding agents as productive but unable to eliminate verification limits for arbitrary software.
- Frames national AI competitiveness as a willingness to invest in capital while protecting worker opportunity rather than every incumbent job.
- Uses IBM's mainframe history to argue for selective control of differentiated stack layers.
