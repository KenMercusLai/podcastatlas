## Overview

鲁恭 / Lu Gong appears in the current wiki as a scholar-official under Empress Dowager Deng whose dismissal during the 109 CE famine accountability layer is paired with a teaching and recommendation case about the boundary between education, patronage, and public appointment duty.

## Current Profile

Hanji 782 presents Lu Gong as a famous scholar who rose to the office of situ before being dismissed after Luoyang famine and the Three Excellencies' ritual admission of fault. The episode uses him to explain Eastern Han office entry through xiaolian recommendation or imperial summons, and to show how those channels could be captured by great-family exchange.

The key anecdote concerns Lu Gong's students. When a student complains that Lu has not recommended many of them, Lu distinguishes his duty as teacher from his duty as court official: teaching means helping students learn, while recommendation must answer to public office. The source still treats him as understanding students' desire for advancement and helping selectively, so the page preserves a boundary rather than a refusal of all patronage.

## Key Characteristics

- Scholar-official whose current evidence joins high office, teaching, and famine-era dismissal.
- Situ removed during the 109 CE Luoyang famine accountability cycle under Deng's regency.
- Teacher who refuses to turn education automatically into mass recommendation.
- Appointment-boundary actor who separates private instruction from public recommendation duty.

## Qualifications

The source presents structural capture as a strong tendency, not a quantified access rate.
