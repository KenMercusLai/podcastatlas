# Jan van Eyck

## Overview

Jan van Eyck is the fifteenth-century painter whom [[271-belgium-historys-greatest-artist-glt1331750756]] presents as a master of luminous oil technique, a court servant of [[PhilipTheGood|Philip the Good]], and a central figure in the artistic history of the Burgundian Low Countries.

## Current Profile

The episode reconstructs Van Eyck from sparse evidence: a likely origin in Maaseik, possible training in Liège, documented employment in The Hague by 1425, service in Lille and Bruges, and death in 1441. His work for Philip included travel, marriage diplomacy in Portugal, a lost world map, and design work for court spectacles, placing painting within a wider system of [[BurgundianCourtArt|Burgundian court art]].

His artistic importance lies not in inventing oil paint but in controlling translucent layers, light, texture, and minute detail with exceptional force. Portrait likeness, inscriptions, reflected self-presence, and the monumental [[GhentAltarpiece]] also make him a bridge between medieval sacred art and a Renaissance culture of individual visibility.

## Key Characteristics

- Burgundian court painter whose service extended into diplomacy, travel, mapping, and spectacle.
- Technical master who intensified oil painting through translucent layering and close observation.
- Portraitist associated with unusually vivid material texture and individual presence.
- Artist who made authorship visible through signatures, mottoes, and implied self-representation.
- Painter of major works tied to Bruges, Ghent, court patronage, and wealthy urban Flanders.

## Evidence

- Career and service: [[271-belgium-historys-greatest-artist-glt1331750756]] follows Van Eyck from The Hague into Philip's service, Lille, Portugal, and Bruges.
- Technical achievement: [[271-belgium-historys-greatest-artist-glt1331750756]] distinguishes invention of oil paint from Van Eyck's mastery of luminous layered effects.
- Portrait and authorship: [[271-belgium-historys-greatest-artist-glt1331750756]] discusses the red-turban portrait, Arnolfini inscription, reflected figure, and personal motto.
- Monumental work: [[271-belgium-historys-greatest-artist-glt1331750756]] treats the Ghent Altarpiece as the fullest convergence of his realism, symbolism, patronage, and regional setting.
