# Labor of Love (M. Night Shyamalan screenplay)

## Overview

*Labor of Love* is [[MNightShyamalan|M. Night Shyamalan]]'s unproduced 1994 screenplay about an older widower who walks across the United States to demonstrate the love he failed to make recognizable to his wife before her death.

## Current Profile

The screenplay begins from a marital communication failure rather than an absence of love. Its protagonist answers loss through an extreme physical journey which, in a pre-smartphone and early-internet setting, grows from private proof into a publicly supported vision quest.

Its development history is inseparable from Shyamalan's career. A bidding war and studio purchase validated his inspiration, but he was removed as director and later rewrites for other filmmakers felt to him like damage. Opportunities involving a celebrated unnamed actor repeatedly failed to align, while another respected director's interest has forced the unresolved question of whether personal authorship requires personal direction.

The screenplay now occupies several forms at once: period story, declaration to Shyamalan's wife, evidence of youthful inspiration, source of regret, and ideal protected from the compromises and verdicts of production. Shyamalan says he would retain its 1994 setting and original emotional character rather than modernize it.

## Key Characteristics

- Centers on the difference between feeling love and making that love legible to another person.
- Uses a cross-country walk as grief ritual, proof of devotion, and personal vision quest.
- Depends partly on 1994's slower and less pervasive communications environment.
- Contains slight spiritual or magical feeling without the conventional genre framework associated with Shyamalan's later work.
- Remains artistically potent because its unproduced status preserves both possibility and uncertainty.
