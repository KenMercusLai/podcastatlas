# Edward VI

## Overview

Edward VI is the young Protestant king whose succession plan attempts to preserve religious reform by excluding his half-sisters [[MaryI|Mary I]] and [[ElizabethI|Elizabeth I]] and placing [[LadyJaneGrey|Lady Jane Grey]] next in line.

## Current Profile

The episode treats Edward's plan as the legal-documentary foundation of Jane's accession but not as a self-executing settlement. Its confessional aim collides with the stronger public intelligibility of Mary's hereditary position, while the exclusion of Protestant Elizabeth reveals that legitimacy categories cannot be adjusted only against Catholic Mary without broader dynastic consequences.

Edward's role also qualifies a simple theory that [[JohnDudleyDukeOfNorthumberland|Northumberland]] designed everything. Northumberland's failure to secure Mary and the improvised collapse of the regime suggest a succession project with multiple authors, weak operational planning, and insufficient acceptance.

## Key Characteristics

- Edward seeks to preserve Protestant succession beyond his own death.
- His plan excludes both Mary and Elizabeth rather than only the Catholic heir.
- The plan supplies Jane with a documentary claim but cannot by itself create political recognition.
- Its success depends on adult councillors, military control, and neutralizing rival claimants.
- The episode leaves the exact division of authorship between Edward and Northumberland open.

## Evidence

- Confessional design: [[294-lady-jane-grey-the-axe-falls-part-2-glt7286066638]] describes the succession plan as a route around Catholic Mary through Jane.
- Dynastic consequence: [[294-lady-jane-grey-the-axe-falls-part-2-glt7286066638]] notes that the reasoning excluding Mary also excludes Protestant Elizabeth.
- Implementation failure: [[294-lady-jane-grey-the-axe-falls-part-2-glt7286066638]] shows that proclamation, force, and council backing collapse when Mary remains free and gathers support.

## Qualifications

This source is a continuation centered on Jane's fall, so it does not provide a complete reconstruction of Edward's drafting process, health, advisers, or theological development. It also does not settle how much initiative belongs to Edward, Northumberland, or other councillors.

## What Changed

- Established Edward's succession plan as the documentary basis of the nine-day crisis.
- Clarified that excluding Mary also threatened Elizabeth's place in the succession.
- Separated the plan's confessional logic from the weak political machinery available to enforce it.

## Relationships

- [[LadyJaneGrey]] - chosen Protestant successor under his plan.
- [[MaryI]] - Catholic half-sister whom the plan attempts to exclude.
- [[ElizabethI|Elizabeth I]] - Protestant half-sister also displaced by the plan's logic.
- [[JohnDudleyDukeOfNorthumberland]] - councillor and military backer associated with implementation.
- [[NineDaysQueenSuccessionCrisis]] - failed accession settlement created by the plan.
