## Overview

Jibin / 罽宾 is a distant Western Regions polity in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-516-gudai-zhongguo-weihe-hui-yu-zhege-guojia-duanjiao-llfmuseg49szpxvypvfsp4naimiy|Hanji 516]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-517-gudai-waijiao-zhongguo-weisha-ai-da-zhonglian-chong-pangzi-liemyvmwxw5_yd-trusbuqqhsbng|Hanji 517]]. The episodes route it through the transcript forms `季宾` and `继宾` and use its conflict with Western Han envoys to show how nominal recognition and tribute ceremony can exceed enforceable diplomatic reach.

## Current Profile

Hanji 516 presents Jibin as confident that distance protects it from Han military punishment. Its rulers repeatedly attack envoys despite Han intervention in a local succession and the granting of a royal seal to [[YinmofuJibinKing|阴末赴 / 殷墨傅]]. When the recognized ruler later attacks another mission, the court chooses rupture and exclusion of the apology envoy rather than a distant punitive expedition.

Hanji 517 adds the later recontact decision. [[DuQinWesternHan|杜钦]] argues that Jibin's delegation is made up of merchants rather than royal kin or senior ministers, that Jibin cannot materially stabilize or threaten the wider Western Regions, and that a full escort would impose severe supply, robbery, terrain, and mortality risks. [[HanChengdi|汉成帝]] adopts the compromise of escorting the mission only as far as [[PishanKingdomWesternHan|皮山国]]. The resulting profile is neither total engagement nor permanent isolation: Han accepts limited contact while refusing a commitment beyond its practical reach.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Regions polity treated by the episode as too distant for reliable Han military enforcement.
- Diplomatic actor whose rulers repeatedly attack Han envoys.
- Site of a Han-backed local regime change carried out through an envoy and a subordinate prince.
- Example of recognition and investiture failing to create durable compliance.
- Tribute participant whose low-status envoys are interpreted as merchants seeking rewards and trade access.
- Counterpart for which Han adopts a geographically bounded escort rather than full protection.

## Qualifications

The supplied sources are short podcast summaries, not a full reconstruction of Jibin's geography, dynastic sequence, relations with neighboring polities, route conditions, or the original `Hanshu` text. Their transcripts use `季宾` and `继宾`; the canonical page uses 罽宾 / Jibin to avoid treating likely transcription forms as separate polities. The exact identity of Rongqu, sequence of attacks, status of the later envoys, route names, risk frequency, and court treatment of the apology mission remain source-scoped.
