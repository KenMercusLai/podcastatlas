# Irish Republican Brotherhood

## Overview

The Irish Republican Brotherhood was a clandestine transatlantic republican organization rooted in the post-famine Fenian tradition and later central to planning the [[EasterRising|Easter Rising]].

## Current Profile

The IRB was founded in Dublin and New York within a post-famine diaspora world. Its 1867 rebellion failed badly and was compromised by informers, after which the revolutionary current acknowledged that war required a meaningful national decision and a plausible chance of success. Republicanism persisted, but majority nationalism generally moved toward [[IrishConstitutionalNationalism]] and [[IrishHomeRule|Home Rule]].

By the early twentieth century the Brotherhood still carried American links, money, and access to guns, though it had not launched a serious revolution since 1867 and could appear obsolete. [[TomClarke]]'s return and younger organizers converted that inherited network into a military council active from spring 1915.

The Brotherhood worked through rather than simply replacing public organizations. It gained influence inside the [[IrishVolunteers|Irish Volunteers]], coordinated with [[JamesConnolly]] and the [[IrishCitizenArmy|Irish Citizen Army]], sought German arms, and deceived [[EoinMacNeill]] about imminent suppression. Its practical work complicates a martyrdom-only reading, even though the final military design proved inadequate.

The organization remained relevant after independence politics split over the Anglo-Irish Treaty. Ronan McGreevey argues that IRB president [[MichaelCollinsIrishRevolutionary|Michael Collins]] alone could sanction the June 1922 assassination of [[HenryWilson|Henry Wilson]], and reconstructs a chain through Liam Tobin and Sam Maguire to Reggie Dunne. That interpretation shows how a secret revolutionary command could overlap with emerging state authority, but the episode does not provide a surviving written order and the attribution remains contested.

## Key Characteristics

- Secret republican organization rooted in the Fenian tradition.
- Transatlantic organization founded through Dublin and New York networks.
- Movement chastened by the failed and informer-compromised 1867 rebellion.
- Institution renewed through cooperation between older and younger revolutionaries.
- Military-council center for planning the 1916 rebellion.
- Clandestine current operating inside broader volunteer organization.
- Coalition builder and clandestine command network willing to use deception, foreign wartime assistance, and allegedly targeted violence.

## Evidence

- Origins and failed rebellion: [[337-ireland-union-famine-and-parnell-part-2-glt4375415331]] connects the IRB to famine-era diaspora formation, Dublin-New York organization, the 1867 failure, and a higher threshold for renewed war.
- Organizational inheritance: [[339-ireland-the-easter-rising-1916-part-4-glt7095481415]] traces post-famine roots, the 1867 failure, American links, and Clarke's role.
- Planning: [[339-ireland-the-easter-rising-1916-part-4-glt7095481415]] dates active military-council work to spring 1915 and records fundraising and arms efforts.
- Coalition and deception: [[339-ireland-the-easter-rising-1916-part-4-glt7095481415]] follows IRB influence in the Volunteers, coordination with Connolly, and the altered Dublin Castle document shown to MacNeill.
- Post-treaty clandestine authority: [[580-the-irish-civil-war-the-assassination-of-sir-henry-wilson-part-1-glt8285525213]] reports McGreevey's case that Collins sanctioned Wilson's assassination through an IRB chain involving Tobin, Maguire, and Dunne.

## Qualifications

The sources do not reconstruct the IRB's complete founding, membership, command structure, transatlantic finances, informer problem, or internal debate. Its survival does not mean revolutionary republicanism was the majority post-famine position; its centrality in 1916 does not make the Rising an IRB-only event, and practical preparation does not prove that its battlefield plan offered a plausible route to victory. The Wilson attribution is McGreevey's argued conclusion rather than a settled documentary fact, and it should not be generalized into a complete account of IRB policy in 1922.

## What Changed

- Added the Dublin-New York founding context and the strategic lesson drawn from the failed 1867 rebellion.
- Clarified that organizational continuity coexisted with constitutional nationalism's wider support.
- Extended the profile into 1922 through a contested assassination-authorization chain that overlapped with Collins's emerging state power.
