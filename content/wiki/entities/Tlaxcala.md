## Overview

Tlaxcala was an independent Nahua confederation that first fought [[HernanCortes|Hernan Cortes]], then made and later renewed a mutually instrumental alliance against the [[MexicaEmpire|Mexica Empire]].

## Current Profile

The episodes treat Tlaxcala as an independent strategic actor, not a passive Spanish auxiliary. Surrounded by lands of the Mexica-led alliance but outside its tribute system, Tlaxcala first resists Cortes's force for several weeks. Spanish cannon and punitive night attacks inflict severe harm, yet the later agreement can be read in both directions: Cortes gains troops, food, and local knowledge, while Tlaxcalan leaders gain useful foreign fighters for a regional struggle. That balance makes “Tlaxcala recruited the Spaniards” a valuable corrective to a Spanish-centered story, though not a settled description of equal power.

Tlaxcalan participation in the [[CholulaMassacre|Cholula massacre]] then shows the alliance serving local rivalry as well as Cortes's advance. After the shattered column later retreats from the [[NocheTriste|Noche Triste]], the new Mexica ruler [[Cuitlahuac]] urges Tlaxcala to destroy the survivors. Xicotencatl the younger reportedly favors that course, while his father supports continued cooperation as a way to break Mexica power. Tlaxcalans feed and treat the defeated Spaniards and then supply the renewed campaign with political backing, warriors, labor, and logistical depth.

The alliance remains mutually instrumental and unequal in its later memory. Indigenous allies outnumber Spaniards in the campaign, and Tlaxcalan porters carry brigantine components overland, yet Cortes reduces their causal role in his own narrative. Smallpox also kills heavily in Tlaxcala, showing that alliance did not protect the polity from the biological catastrophe of conquest.

## Key Characteristics

- Autonomous Nahua confederation pursuing advantage against Mexica power.
- Former military opponent whose alliance with Cortes followed weeks of resistance and Spanish punitive violence.
- Divided leadership faced with a real choice between destroying Cortes's survivors and renewing alliance.
- Provider of refuge, food, treatment, warriors, porters, and a territorial base for the renewed campaign.
- Numerically central coalition partner whose role was minimized in Spanish-centered accounts.
- Community severely affected by smallpox despite its alliance with the Spaniards.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to two campaign episodes and does not comprehensively reconstruct Tlaxcala's government, internal factions, flower wars, or later colonial history. The balance of initiative in alliance-making, battle details, dialogue, factional motives, the alleged Cholulan warning, and the claimed mortality fraction are source-scoped. “Alliance” does not imply shared goals, equal power, or freedom from later Spanish domination, and “Tlaxcalans” should not flatten disagreement among leaders and communities or collective responsibility for coalition violence.

## What Changed

- Extended the profile backward from alliance renewal to Tlaxcala's armed resistance and first negotiated partnership with Cortes.
- Reframed the alliance as mutually instrumental while preserving coercion, unequal power, and uncertainty about who recruited whom.
- Added Tlaxcalan participation in the Cholula massacre as evidence of both agency and coalition violence.

## Relationships

- [[HernanCortes]] - defeated ally whom Tlaxcalan leaders chose to support again.
- [[Cuitlahuac]] - Mexica ruler who reportedly tried to detach Tlaxcala from the Spanish alliance.
- [[MexicaEmpire]] - regional rival whose defeat offered Tlaxcala strategic opportunity.
- [[NocheTriste]] - disaster after which Tlaxcala's decision determined whether the coalition survived.
- [[SiegeOfTenochtitlan]] - campaign supplied by Tlaxcalan warriors, transport, and political support.
- [[ColumbianExchange]] - process through which smallpox devastated Tlaxcala as well as its enemies.
