# 江陵 / Jiangling (late Han)

## Overview
江陵 / Jiangling (late Han) is the Nanjun seat whose middle-Yangtze position links the military, fiscal, political, and upstream-expansion stakes of [[Jingzhou|荆州]].

## Current Profile
Hanji 969 introduces Jiangling as a corridor node from which a power can move upstream toward [[YizhouLateHan|益州]] or downstream toward [[YangzhouLateHan|扬州]]. Hanji 1108 makes that geography operational after Red Cliffs. [[CaoCao|曹操]] leaves [[CaoRen|曹仁]] and [[XuHuangCaoGeneral|徐晃]] to hold the city while [[ZhouYuLateHan|周瑜]] and Cheng Pu confront them.

The source argues that loss would push Cao-side defense toward Xiangyang-Fancheng, weaken southern political and economic influence, improve [[SunQuan|孙权]]'s middle-Yangtze position, and give [[LiuBei|刘备]] a possible approach toward Yizhou. [[GanNing|甘宁]]'s seizure of [[YilingLateHan|夷陵]] matters because it opens a nearby pressure point.

## Key Characteristics
- Nanjun seat and middle-Yangtze corridor node.
- Connects downstream Yangzhou and upstream Yizhou directions.
- Holds military, fiscal, political-alignment, and legitimacy value.
- Loss would shift Cao-side defense toward Xiangyang-Fancheng.
- Gains meaning through linked positions such as Yiling.

## Qualifications
This captures strategic function in two popular-history episodes, not a complete urban or campaign history. Elite realignment, fiscal effects, legitimacy, and Liu Bei's route are strategic interpretations, not automatic consequences.
