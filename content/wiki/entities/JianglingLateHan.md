# 江陵 / Jiangling (late Han)

## Overview
江陵 / Jiangling (late Han) is the Nanjun seat whose middle-Yangtze position links military movement, fiscal resources, political alignment, and pressure on neighboring regimes.

## Current Profile
Hanji 969 introduces Jiangling as a corridor node pointing upstream toward [[YizhouLateHan|益州]] and downstream toward [[YangzhouLateHan|扬州]]. Hanji 1095 places that geography into the 208 CE crisis: Cao Cao's movement toward Jiangling after taking [[Jingzhou|荆州]] brings strategic pressure materially closer to [[SunQuan|孙权]]'s Jiangdong base.

Hanji 1108 makes the position operational after Red Cliffs. Cao Cao leaves [[CaoRen|曹仁]] and [[XuHuangCaoGeneral|徐晃]] to hold it while [[ZhouYuLateHan|周瑜]] and Cheng Pu press from the opposing side; [[GanNing|甘宁]]'s seizure of [[YilingLateHan|夷陵]] opens a nearby pressure point. Hanji 1112 shows how that pressure reaches the city wall: [[NiuJinLateHan|牛金]]'s small detachment is surrounded by a larger Wu vanguard, and Cao Ren's mounted extraction becomes a short-term recovery of troops and morale inside a campaign that remains strategically adverse.

Hanji 1113 closes this phase with Cao Ren's withdrawal and Zhou Yu's appointment over Nanjun. The result shifts the corridor from Cao-side delay to Sun-side administration, while Liu Bei remains based separately at [[GonganLateHan|公安]] rather than controlling the entire strategic node.

## Key Characteristics
- Nanjun seat and middle-Yangtze corridor node.
- Connects downstream Yangzhou and upstream Yizhou directions.
- Holds military, fiscal, political-alignment, and legitimacy value.
- Turns Jingzhou's fall into direct strategic pressure on Jiangdong.
- Gains meaning through linked positions such as Yiling.
- Functions as a morale theater where visible command action can stabilize a pressured garrison without settling the longer siege.

## Qualifications
This captures strategic function in five popular-history episodes, not a complete urban or campaign history. Travel-time estimates, elite realignment, fiscal effects, legitimacy, siege duration, rescue and army sizes, casualties, appointment sequence, and future routes remain episode-framed interpretations. The successful rescue in Hanji 1112 does not contradict Hanji 1113's later withdrawal.

## What Changed
- Added the Niu Jin encirclement and Cao Ren extraction as the garrison-level crisis inside the longer siege.
- Distinguished a local morale success from the later loss of the position.
