# Jennifer Groh

## Overview
Jennifer Groh is presented as a Duke professor of psychology and neuroscience whose laboratory studies how the brain represents space and combines hearing, vision, eye movements, and other sensory signals.

## Current Profile
In [[scim2057835571-scim2057835571]], Groh moves between experimental sensory neuroscience and cautious everyday interpretation. Her central contribution is to reject static, isolated sensory maps: eye position changes auditory coding, saccades are accompanied by tiny ear signals, visual evidence can relocate a perceived sound source, and architecture changes the reflections available to hearing. She also proposes, more tentatively, that thought may reuse sensory and motor systems.

Her practical advice follows the same context-sensitive frame. She changes location when writing stalls, uses familiar or non-lyrical music when it does not capture too much attention, builds project playlists as cues, and prefers phone activities with explicit purposes and endpoints. These are personal strategies and hypotheses, not universal protocols.

## Key Characteristics
- Studies neural representations of space across hearing, vision, and eye movement.
- Treats perception as active remapping and source inference rather than passive recording.
- Connects laboratory findings to ordinary cases such as ventriloquism, recorded voices, room acoustics, music, driving, and screen use.
- Distinguishes measured results from first-principles predictions and broader theories.
- Uses environment, familiar sound, and bounded technology as conditional supports for focused work.
