# Rezső Kasztner

## Overview

Rezső Kasztner was a Hungarian Jewish leader whose access to the [[VrbaWetzlerReport]], selective rescue activity, and failure to warn Hungarian Jews broadly make him a contested figure in the source's account of [[TheHolocaust]].

## Current Profile

[[292-the-shadow-of-the-holocaust-glt7756076339]] says Kasztner received the report earlier than Allied leaders but put it away rather than distributing its warning widely. [[RudolfVrba]] later treated that failure as a central betrayal because Hungarian Jews continued boarding trains without being told clearly that Auschwitz meant death.

The same account credits Kasztner with rescuing about 1,600 Jews, preventing a simple classification as either rescuer or collaborator. He therefore functions in this wiki as a case of morally constrained action, selective rescue, information control, and retrospective judgment—not as evidence that Jewish victims or intermediaries shared perpetrator guilt.

## Key Characteristics

- Occupied a leadership and intermediary position within Hungarian Jewry in 1944.
- Received direct warning about Auschwitz before the report reached some foreign leaders.
- Did not broadly distribute the warning according to the episode's account.
- Participated in negotiations that enabled a selective rescue of about 1,600 Jews.
- Became a major target of Vrba's postwar accusations.
- Remains morally and historically contested rather than reducible to one label.

## Evidence

- Information control: [[292-the-shadow-of-the-holocaust-glt7756076339]] says Kasztner received the report and did not pass it to Hungarian Jews at large.
- Rescue and controversy: [[292-the-shadow-of-the-holocaust-glt7756076339]] places the rescue of about 1,600 people beside the much larger deportation and frames judgment as unresolved.

## Qualifications

The page preserves the episode's framing rather than adjudicating the full Kasztner historiography. His options, motives, negotiations, risks, actual capacity to warn, and responsibility remain contested. Nazi perpetrators and collaborating state authorities retain primary guilt; assessing an intermediary under extreme coercion requires a differentiated account of agency.

## What Changed

- Created the page as a contested selective-rescue and information-dissemination case.
- Explicitly separated responsibility under coercion from perpetrator guilt.
