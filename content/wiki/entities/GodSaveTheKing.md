# God Save the King / Queen

## Overview

God Save the King / Queen appears in the wiki as [[UnitedKingdom|Britain]]'s national anthem, presented by the source as an unusually durable song because its origin is obscure, its ruler reference is adaptable, and its meaning can shift across dynastic, constitutional, cultural, and sporting contexts.

## Current Profile

The episode treats the anthem as a politically flexible inherited symbol rather than a fixed patriotic text. Its public breakthrough comes during the 1745 [[Jacobitism|Jacobite]] crisis, when performance at Drury Lane helped turn it into a Hanoverian loyalist song. At the same time, the source keeps older Stuart/Jacobite use plausible but unsettled, making the anthem a case of [[NationalAnthemPoliticalPlasticity]] and [[RegimeSymbolContinuity]] through repurposing rather than clean invention.

The anthem later becomes part of the British constitutional and sporting problem. The hosts read its laws verse as compatible with monarchy under law, while its use by [[England]] in football raises questions because [[Scotland]] can be British, monarchical, and still prefer [[FlowerOfScotland|Flower of Scotland]] for sporting identity.

## Key Characteristics

- British national anthem whose official force is presented as conventional rather than statute-based.
- Song of uncertain authorship and date, making origin ambiguity part of its national usefulness.
- Politically repurposed anthem associated with possible Stuart/Jacobite use before Hanoverian loyalist adoption.
- Monarchical symbol that can move between king and queen forms without changing the core formula.
- Constitutional symbol whose law-defending verse lets the source read it as monarchy under law rather than absolute monarchy.
- Sporting identity problem because [[England]] uses it while other UK constituent nations may prefer separate anthems.

## Evidence

- Conventional anthem status: [[678-britain-god-save-the-king-part-2-glt2603959218]] says the song became established as Britain's anthem during the Napoleonic period by convention rather than statute.
- Origin ambiguity: [[678-britain-god-save-the-king-part-2-glt2603959218]] contrasts the anthem's unclear author, date, and purpose with more event-specific anthems such as La Marseillaise and The Star-Spangled Banner.
- Jacobite-Hanoverian repurposing: [[678-britain-god-save-the-king-part-2-glt2603959218]] follows Percy Scholes's account of the 1745 Drury Lane breakthrough and his caution that the song may have older Stuart associations.
- Constitutional meaning: [[678-britain-god-save-the-king-part-2-glt2603959218]] says the law-defending verse fits the Hanoverian settlement and the idea of monarchy under law.
- Cultural afterlife: [[678-britain-god-save-the-king-part-2-glt2603959218]] uses [[GeorgeOrwell]], Jimi Hendrix, and punk parody to show that dislike or mockery still depends on the anthem's recognizability.
- Sporting tension: [[678-britain-god-save-the-king-part-2-glt2603959218]] says [[England]] continues to use the anthem in football while [[Scotland]] uses [[FlowerOfScotland|Flower of Scotland]].

## Qualifications
