# Battle of Poltava / 波尔塔瓦战役

## Overview

The Battle of Poltava in 1709 was the decisive battlefield reversal of the [[GreatNorthernWar|Great Northern War]]. An exhausted Swedish army under [[CharlesXII|Charles XII]] attacked [[PeterTheGreat|Peter the Great]]'s fortified, numerically superior, artillery-supported Russian force; broken coordination and overwhelming firepower destroyed Swedish field power and altered the Baltic balance.

## Current Profile

The episode treats Poltava as the endpoint of accumulated Swedish disadvantage rather than a single unlucky engagement. Charles had lost his supply column, diverted into Ukraine, endured a catastrophic winter, and suffered a severe wound. The attack still relied on surprise and disciplined infantry closing rapidly without artillery support, but newly built redoubts, rival commanders, dispersed units, and failed communication disrupted the plan before the main clash.

Roos's separated battalions were destroyed, Lewenhaupt drifted away from the main force, and Rehnskiöld waited for units that did not return. Peter then brought his army out of the fortified camp. The surviving Swedish infantry briefly struck the Russian line, but artillery, reserves, widened gaps, and encirclement caused collapse. The defeat made Charles a fugitive, encouraged Sweden's enemies to re-enter the war, and secured Russia's great-power and Baltic trajectory.

## Key Characteristics

- It concentrates prior logistical attrition, winter exposure, numerical inferiority, injury, and alliance weakness into one battle.
- The Swedish plan depends on surprise, speed, cohesion, and close combat because artillery and material parity are absent.
- Redoubts and divided command turn a high-risk maneuver into fragmented local actions.
- Russian artillery, reserves, and improved infantry translate defensive preparation into battlefield decision.
- Its political effect exceeds the immediate casualties by reversing coalition expectations and imperial credibility.

## Evidence

- Pre-battle disadvantage: [[566-the-great-northern-war-slaughter-on-the-steppes-part-3-glt4679437587]] connects the lost supply column, Ukrainian diversion, winter, wound, and force imbalance to the attack decision.
- Coordination failure: [[566-the-great-northern-war-slaughter-on-the-steppes-part-3-glt4679437587]] traces the changed redoubt plan, Roos's isolation, Lewenhaupt's displacement, and Rehnskiöld's uncertainty.
- Russian battle system: [[566-the-great-northern-war-slaughter-on-the-steppes-part-3-glt4679437587]] describes fortified positions, cannon, infantry depth, reserves, and the opening of Peter's camp for the main engagement.
- Geopolitical result: [[566-the-great-northern-war-slaughter-on-the-steppes-part-3-glt4679437587]] links the Swedish collapse to Charles's flight, renewed coalition war, Russian influence in Poland, and Saint Petersburg's security.

## Qualifications

The account is a narrative synthesis with source-scoped troop totals, casualty estimates, dialogue, command motives, and near-miss anecdotes. Poltava is decisive in the episode's geopolitical interpretation, but the war continues until 1721; battlefield victory does not by itself explain the entire imperial transition.
