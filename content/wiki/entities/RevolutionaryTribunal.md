# Revolutionary Tribunal

## Overview

The Revolutionary Tribunal was the special court created in 1793 to try political crimes during the escalating emergency of the [[FrenchRevolution|French Revolution]].

## Current Profile

The sources trace the tribunal from an emergency court with rapid, non-appealable death sentences into a centralized instrument of the Terror. [[JeanPaulMarat|Marat]]'s April acquittal showed early unpredictability; division into four chambers and committee influence over personnel later made acquittal rarer. Girondin procedure yielded to a politically required result, Marie Antoinette's trial mixed real treason with weak and inflammatory accusations, and Danton's case went further by grouping unlike defendants into one conspiracy and removing them when their defence threatened conviction.

## Key Characteristics

- It exercised special jurisdiction over political crimes.
- Its non-appealable death sentences were to be carried out within twenty-four hours.
- It formed part of a larger emergency apparatus rather than operating alone.
- Marat's acquittal showed early unpredictability before restructuring expanded throughput and committee influence over personnel.
- Politically assembled defendant groups made association perform the work of proving a unified plot.
- The Danton trial preserved judicial form only until adversarial defence endangered the required verdict.

## Evidence

### Compressed political justice

- [[707-the-terror-an-assassination-in-paris-part-1-glt6580043397]] identifies rapid execution and absence of appeal as defining features of the tribunal's political-crime jurisdiction.

### Part of an accumulating system

- [[707-the-terror-an-assassination-in-paris-part-1-glt6580043397]] groups the tribunal with surveillance committees, deputies on mission, and the [[CommitteeOfPublicSafety]].

### Marat trial

- [[707-the-terror-an-assassination-in-paris-part-1-glt6580043397]] describes Marat's April trial and acquittal as a Girondin defeat that converted prosecution into popular triumph.

### Expansion and committee influence

- [[708-the-terror-the-reign-of-robespierre-part-2-glt2099451333]] describes four chambers, committee influence over judges and jurors, and a later decline in acquittals.

### Girondin trial

- [[708-the-terror-the-reign-of-robespierre-part-2-glt2099451333]] shows Robespierre replacing an open-ended defence with a jury judgment about whether conscience had heard enough, after which all twenty-one defendants were convicted.

### Dantonist trial and removal of the defence

- [[710-the-terror-death-at-the-guillotine-part-4-glt5038102951]] describes Dantonists, corrupt deputies, and foreign bankers being tried together to imply one conspiracy.
- [[710-the-terror-death-at-the-guillotine-part-4-glt5038102951]] records the decree permitting disruptive defendants to be removed, after which Danton and his co-defendants were condemned without completing their defence or calling witnesses.

## Qualifications

- The sources cover selected politically important trials rather than the tribunal's full caseload or regional operation.
- Marat's acquittal complicates any simple description of the tribunal as a mechanically predictable instrument of one faction at this early stage.
- The reported reduction in acquittals and the hosts' interpretation of Robespierre's intervention are source-scoped rather than a quantitative judicial history.
- Danton's corruption vulnerability does not validate a unified foreign plot or cure the denial of defence rights.
- Saint-Just's prison-break allegation and claim that courtroom resistance proved guilt are presented as political devices rather than independently established facts.

## What Changed

- Extended the tribunal through the Dantonist trial and explicit removal of defendants from their own defence.
- Added defendant grouping as a way to manufacture the appearance of one conspiracy.
- Sharpened the distinction between legal form and politically guaranteed outcome.
