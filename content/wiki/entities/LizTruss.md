# Liz Truss

## Overview

Liz Truss was the British prime minister whose resignation after 45 days is analyzed in [[246-the-fall-of-liz-truss-glt3484252092]].

## Current Profile

The source presents Truss as an extreme case of rapid governing failure: selected by Conservative members, she attempted a deregulatory, tax-cutting post-[[Brexit]] programme with [[KwasiKwarteng]], lost economic and parliamentary authority after the mini-budget, dismissed Kwarteng, accepted reversal under Jeremy Hunt, and resigned. Her importance here lies less in the raw tenure record than in what the collapse reveals about [[PartyMembershipLeadershipSelection]] and [[PoliticalAuthorityCollapseCascade]].

## Key Characteristics

- Conservative leader selected by party members rather than the wider electorate.
- Advocate of a low-tax, deregulatory post-Brexit programme.
- Prime minister for 45 days, shorter than George Canning's conventionally recognized record.
- Lost authority after market turmoil, policy reversal, ministerial departures, and parliamentary disorder.

## Evidence

- Selection and programme: [[246-the-fall-of-liz-truss-glt3484252092]] links Truss's victory among Conservative members to a "Singapore on the Thames" Brexit vision.
- Collapse: [[246-the-fall-of-liz-truss-glt3484252092]] treats Kwarteng's dismissal, Hunt's policy reversal, and breakdown of party discipline as stages in her loss of control.
- Historical distinction: [[246-the-fall-of-liz-truss-glt3484252092]] contrasts her politically caused 45-day tenure with short premierships ended by illness or failure to form a ministry.
