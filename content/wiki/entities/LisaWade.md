# Lisa Wade

## Overview
Lisa Wade is the sociologist whose research and interpretation structure the [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-b22429cd47d6a37db02cc28d3ef377e1-25960294-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-b22429cd47d6a37db02cc28d3ef377e1-25960294|Hidden Brain episode “Just Sex”]] on college hookup culture.

## Current Profile
Wade's contribution is to separate behavior frequency from cultural authority. She argues that students need not hook up often, or at all, for an alcohol-centered and status-conscious sexual script to shape campus expectations. Her account follows that script through initiation, peer approval, the performance of meaninglessness, unequal access, coercion risk, gendered emotional rules, and pandemic-era disruption.

The analysis is critical without treating all casual sex as intrinsically harmful. Wade preserves the possibility of wanted experimentation while asking whether students can also choose care, commitment, repetition, sobriety, kindness, and emotional honesty without social penalty.

## Key Characteristics
- Distinguishes reported sexual frequency from the rules that govern how encounters are interpreted.
- Treats hookup culture as a status system shaped by peers, alcohol, gender, race, class, sexuality, and campus context.
- Connects performed indifference to emotional misunderstanding and unequal social power.
- Preserves a boundary between chosen experimentation and coercive or exclusion-driven participation.
- Interprets pandemic disruption as a possible change in conditions rather than proof of permanent cultural reform.

## Qualifications
This profile is bounded to one structured episode summary, not Wade's books, complete research record, underlying datasets, or a verbatim interview. Numerical participation claims, demographic comparisons, causal explanations, student testimony, and pandemic changes remain source-scoped. A dominant cultural script does not describe every campus or individual, and criticism of that script does not establish that casual sex is inherently harmful.
