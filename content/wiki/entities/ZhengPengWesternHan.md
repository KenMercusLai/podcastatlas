## Overview

郑朋 / Zheng Peng enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-478-chushi-zhihui-zhezhong-ren-tai-jiduan-yudao-qing-yuanli-lq3hhg58lohdi-tbpmcuhwajjo7h|Hanji 478]] as a Western Han court actor whose failed attachment to [[XiaoWangzhiWesternHan|萧望之]] turns into resentment and then accusation through [[ShiGaoWesternHan|史高]]'s outer-relative side. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-468-yan-duo-bi-shi-xiao-wangzhi-ruhe-yin-zui-chi-da-kui-losjg52ruc7ov6k3uvmxvhxlzvjw|Hanji 468]] supplies the first attachment moment: Zheng attacks the Xu-Shi side, wins encouragement from Zhou Kan and Xiao, and receives a promise of future benefit. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-469-weishenme-junzi-dou-buguo-xiaoren-kankan-xiaowangzhi-de-xiachang-lj8iji-c3scj6vznntavm6awolho|Hanji 469]] then shows Zheng learning enough to become dangerous and defecting after Xiao cuts him off.

## Current Profile

The source set gives Zheng Peng a compact but sharp profile: he has connections to Zhou Kan, uses the rivalry between Xiao's side and Shi Gao's side to enter Xiao's circle, and initially gains validation by accusing Shi Gao and the Xu-Shi outer-relative network of dangerous private patronage. He is later rejected when Xiao judges him corrupt or untrustworthy. Zheng then carries disappointed expectation and private resentment into factional politics, moving toward Shi Gao and [[XuZhangWesternHan|许章]] and supplying accusations against Xiao Wangzhi, Zhou Kan, and Liu Gengsheng.

Zheng Peng therefore matters less as an independent policy figure than as a mechanism of political injury. The source uses him to show how a private slight can become public accusation when an ambitious person finds a hostile faction willing to use his grievance.

## Key Characteristics

- Former friend or associate of Zhou Kan in the episode's account.
- Attempts to attach himself to Xiao Wangzhi by attacking the Xu-Shi side, earns encouragement and expectations of reward, but is later rejected as untrustworthy.
- Turns resentment against Xiao Wangzhi into factional alignment with Shi Gao's side.
- Supplies accusations used against Xiao Wangzhi, Zhou Kan, and Liu Gengsheng.
- Helps show how a weak boundary around an ambitious attaché can become a later procedural weapon.
- Illustrates how private grievance can become political weaponry.

## Qualifications

This page is source-scoped to Hanji 478, Hanji 469, and Hanji 468. It does not settle Zheng Peng's full offices, exact relationship to Zhou Kan, the content of his accusations, his later fate, or the broader legal record around Xiao Wangzhi's fall.
