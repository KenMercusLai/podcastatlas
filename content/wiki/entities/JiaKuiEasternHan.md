## Overview

贾逵 / Jia Kui enters the current wiki in an Emperor-He-era role, distinct from the later [[JiaKuiLateHan|late-Han Jia Kui]] of the Yuan-family war. In Hanji 772 he is the shizhong who defends [[LiuKaiEasternHan|Liu Kai]]'s refusal of hereditary title and also participates in the court classics discussion with [[LuPiEasternHan|Lu Pi]] and Huang Xiang.

## Current Profile

Jia Kui's first source-bounded function is legal-moral framing. Officials want to handle Liu Kai's refusal and flight as a succession violation, but Jia Kui cites Confucian approval of governing through ritual yielding. That argument lets [[EmperorHeOfHan|Emperor He]] permit [[LiuXianEasternHan|Liu Xian]] to inherit while still declaring the case exceptional.

The same episode places Jia Kui in the 99 CE classics discussion. Emperor He summons scholars to debate difficult points in the classics; Lu Pi, Jia Kui, Huang Xiang, and others challenge and answer each other. Jia Kui therefore appears not only as a memorialist but also as part of the court's textual-reasoning environment.

## Key Characteristics

- Eastern Han shizhong and classicist in the Emperor He source layer.
- Converts Liu Kai's legally suspect flight into a case for completing ritual yielding.
- Participates in court debate over difficult classical questions.
- Kept distinct from the later Wei/Hedong-era Jia Kui route already present in the wiki.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 772 and does not yet attempt a full biography of the Eastern Han classicist. The source summary writes 贾奎, but the role and context are normalized here as 贾逵. This page is deliberately separate from [[JiaKuiLateHan|贾逵 / Jia Kui (late Han)]], whose current evidence concerns Guo Yuan's Hedong campaign more than a century later.
