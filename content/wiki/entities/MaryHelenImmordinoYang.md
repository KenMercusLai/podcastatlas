# Mary Helen Immordino-Yang

## Overview
Mary Helen Immordino-Yang is presented as a USC professor studying how emotion, social interaction, culture, development, and brain-body processes shape learning and meaning.

## Current Profile
Across the full interview and its Essentials edit, Immordino-Yang integrates neuroscience, developmental psychology, and education around one organizing position: emotion is part of cognition because it determines what matters enough to receive sustained thought. Her examples move from children's increasingly abstract emotional concepts and bodily feeling to narrative inference, adolescent ethical reflection, culturally shaped perception, inquiry-based schooling, and civic perspective-taking.

Her educational stance emphasizes developing reflective people rather than merely producing preset outcomes. She favors questions that expose reasoning, long-form interdisciplinary work, expert feedback, and assessment of how students make decisions. She also treats safety as a condition for genuine perspective construction and distinguishes distributed simulation of others from the claim that “mirror neurons” constitute a unique cell type.

## Key Characteristics
- Connects emotion and cognition through bodily regulation, attention, narrative, and meaning.
- Studies complex social and ethical inference with developmental and neuroimaging methods.
- Treats culture and prior experience as active organizers of perception and memory.
- Argues for reflective, inquiry-centered education that makes student thinking visible.
- Emphasizes adolescent agency, curiosity, and meaning-making rather than only compliance or recall.
- Keeps empathy grounded in distributed action-perception systems rather than a special “mirror neuron” category.
