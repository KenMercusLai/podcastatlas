## Overview

孔僖 / Kong Xi enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-755-zhege-chaodai-huangshang-jiu-kaishi-guli-shengwa-le-lj7bdkxkdtjhpo469yxhskjyfdco|Hanji 755]] as the Confucius descendant who answers [[EmperorZhangOfHan|Emperor Zhang]] during the emperor's Qufu sacrifice. He is kept separate from [[KongXi|孔熙]], the Han Xin-side Gaixia commander.

## Current Profile

The source places Kong Xi inside Zhangdi's ritual visit to Qufu. After sacrificing to [[Confucius|Confucius]] and the seventy-two disciples, Zhangdi gathers adult men of the Kong lineage and asks Kong Xi whether the ceremony brings honor to the family.

Kong Xi answers by refusing to make the rite a private family triumph. He says the emperor's visit honors the former teacher and displays sage virtue, while the Kong lineage does not dare claim personal glory. Zhangdi laughs, praises him as worthy of the sage's descendants, and appoints him as langzhong.

The current profile is therefore a ritual-modesty figure. Kong Xi matters because he turns imperial attention away from family vanity and back toward the public meaning of teacher reverence and classical legitimacy.

## Key Characteristics

- Eastern Han Kong-lineage representative in Zhangdi's Qufu sacrifice scene.
- Ritual speaker who redirects imperial praise from family honor to reverence for the former teacher.
- Confucian descendant whose modest answer becomes appointment-worthy.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 755. It does not reconstruct Kong Xi's full career or the whole Kong lineage's Eastern Han status. The page preserves the episode's ritual interpretation and the identity distinction from Kong Xi / 孔熙.
