# 山下有松 / Shanxia Yousong

## Overview

Shanxia Yousong is a Chinese designer bag brand founded in 2019 by Liu Xiaolu in Shandong, represented here as a consumer-brand soft-power case, a domestic light-luxury premiumization case, and a test of whether sales momentum becomes durable loyalty.

## Current Profile

The brand combines a craft-led origin story, tactile design language, rising average prices, cultural content, and high-end offline stores. The earliest source emphasizes its “grandma's handmade bag” story, rough and earthy style, and reported 2025 revenue above RMB 1 billion. The later bag-market source adds average price rising from about RMB 1,600 to RMB 2,200, more than 300% online growth during 2026 618, and about 20 offline stores, while citing a conflicting industry annual-sales estimate of RMB 600-700 million.

The newest evidence supplies source-scoped Double 11 growth, a roughly 42% annual repurchase rate, and high category rankings. It also clarifies that the name's “松” is connected to cofounder Fu Song amid public confusion over whether the brand was Japanese. The stronger profile is now not simply rapid growth but whether active search, full-price sales, retained revenue, repeat purchase, quality, and originality can persist as prices rise.

## Key Characteristics

- Chinese designer bag brand founded in Shandong in 2019.
- Craft, material texture, and a handmade origin story replace logo-heavy luxury signaling.
- Low-logo commuter products, rising prices, and high-end stores support its premium position.
- Short films, humanities conversations, travel, nature, and reading expand the brand world.
- Reported category growth and roughly 42% annual repurchase suggest momentum but remain methodologically undefined.
- Brand durability depends on quality control, aftersales, originality, price discipline, and resistance to reputational attacks.

## Qualifications

Revenue remains inconsistent across sources: above RMB 1 billion in one account versus an industry estimate of RMB 600-700 million in another. The 42% repurchase figure lacks cohort, period, channel, and return methodology. Ranking scope may differ between sources and periods. Higher prices also expose hardware, stitching, aftersales, name confusion, copying, and narrative homogenization risks.
