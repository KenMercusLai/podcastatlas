# 惠子 / Keiko (boxer)

惠子 is the central figure in [[88-bie-renshu-huizi-zai-ting-budao-hecai-de-shijie-li-huiquan-708510803]], where [[MihuanChishu|蜜獾吃书]] presents her as Japan's first hearing-impaired female professional boxer. The episode says she was born in 1979, was diagnosed early with serious hearing impairment, and grew up between ordinary-school inclusion that often failed her and deaf-school communication that finally felt usable.

The source does not turn her into a clean inspirational hero. 惠子 steals once under peer pressure, rejects classes, fights teachers, clashes with her mother, abandons an art-school dream, drifts through work and training, and repeatedly considers quitting. Those details matter because the episode's point is not that disability produces virtue, but that repeated misrecognition can turn into anger until language, art, sport, and trustworthy adults create other routes.

Boxing becomes her strongest route through [[BoxingAsEmbodiedAgency]]. She cannot wear hearing aids in the ring, so she learns to read facial expression, gaze, and movement directly. Her first professional win is important less as a victory record than as permission finally becoming real: after many refusals, she gets to stand on the ring under ordinary standards.

## Key Claims
- 惠子's story connects [[DeafEducationBelonging]] to [[DisabilityAccessInCombatSports]]: both school and sport can include someone formally while still failing if communication and assessment are not adapted.
- Her teenage anger is source-scoped as a response to accumulated isolation and shame, not as an excuse for harm.
- [[WadaSachiko|和田幸子]] gives her a low-pressure refuge before she can repair school relationships.
- [[ZhendouBoxingGym|真斗拳馆]] and [[KobayashiRyoichi|小林亮一]] matter because they combine permission with serious standards.
- Her later work with children and disabled people turns personal access into support for others.
