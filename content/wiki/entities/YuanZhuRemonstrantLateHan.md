## Overview

袁著 / Yuan Zhu is the nineteen-year-old langzhong in Hanji 846 whose memorial warns [[LiangJiLateHan|梁冀]] to reduce power before disaster. Liang interprets the warning as a threat, hunts Yuan through disguise and false-death tactics, and has him beaten to death.

## Current Profile

Hanji 846 presents Yuan Zhu as a young court official who uses direct warning rather than armed resistance. He submits a memorial at the palace gate advising Liang to be cautious at the height of power and to reduce authority in order to preserve the family. The warning is protective in form, but it still attacks the premise of Liang's unchecked position.

Liang's response turns remonstrance into a hunt. Yuan changes name and pretends to have died; his family stages a funeral with a grass dummy. Liang sees through the ruse, continues the pursuit, captures Yuan, and has him flogged to death. The episode therefore treats Yuan as a speech victim whose advice exposes how closed Liang's correction channel has become.

## Key Characteristics

- Young langzhong who warns Liang Ji at the height of Liang power.
- Remonstrant whose advice is framed as family-preserving withdrawal rather than rebellion.
- Fugitive who uses name change and staged death to avoid capture.
- Victim beaten to death after Liang sees through the concealment.
- Source case where corrective speech becomes personal offense to a powerholder.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 846 and does not reconstruct Yuan Zhu's wider family or official career. His memorial is treated as the episode's source-scoped account of protective warning, not as proof that Liang ever had a viable peaceful exit.
