# Archimedes / 阿基米德

## Overview

Archimedes / 阿基米德 is the Syracusan mathematician and engineer credited in the episode with designing artillery and mechanical defenses that initially repel Roman attacks on [[Syracuse]].

## Current Profile

The episode places Archimedes inside Hieron II's prosperous Syracuse, where theoretical brilliance and practical engineering meet state defense. Catapults and smaller projectile weapons cover different ranges, while mechanical claws are said to lift or overturn attacking ships, creating fear as well as physical damage.

These devices alter the opening siege but do not decide the campaign. [[MarcusClaudiusMarcellus]] abandons direct confidence in assault, yet Roman forces eventually enter the city by surprise and continue the conquest over months. Archimedes is killed during the sack despite Marcellus's reported wish to spare him.

The profile separates contemporary-style siege engineering from later wonder stories. The episode treats the solar-mirror death ray as probably unhistorical, even if later elaboration drew credibility from Archimedes' real work on optics and machines.

## Key Characteristics

- Joins abstract mathematical reputation to applied mechanical engineering.
- Designs layered defenses for different ranges and attack modes.
- Produces tactical delay and psychological deterrence rather than strategic invulnerability.
- Becomes the subject of later legends that exceed the episode's historical confidence.
- Dies during the Roman sack despite a reported order that he be protected.

## Evidence

- Intellectual and civic setting: [[640-romes-greatest-enemy-carthage-at-the-gates-part-1-glt9223169972]] places Archimedes in Hieron's wealthy, fortified Syracuse.
- Defensive engineering: [[640-romes-greatest-enemy-carthage-at-the-gates-part-1-glt9223169972]] attributes catapults, scorpions, and ship-grappling devices to his preparation.
- Operational limit: [[640-romes-greatest-enemy-carthage-at-the-gates-part-1-glt9223169972]] shows the machines checking initial assaults but not preventing eventual entry and conquest.
- Death and legend: [[640-romes-greatest-enemy-carthage-at-the-gates-part-1-glt9223169972]] recounts his death and explicitly doubts the mirror death ray.

## Qualifications

The source is a modern narrative drawing on ancient and later traditions. Exact machine designs, effects, attribution, Roman fear, Marcellus's order, Archimedes' final actions, and the identity or motive of his killer remain source-scoped. The mirror weapon is retained only as a rejected or highly doubtful later claim.

## What Changed

- Created a bounded profile separating documented-style siege machines from the later death-ray legend.
- Framed engineering as delay and deterrence rather than a guarantee against capture.
