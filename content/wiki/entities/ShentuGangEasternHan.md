## Overview

申屠刚 / Shentu Gang is synthesized as a recurring Han remonstrant figure whose source roles are separated by chronology but share a pattern: he gives politically inconvenient advice to a powerholder controlling a fragile ruler or regime. In [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-610-1-jiemi-gudai-huangdi-niangjiaren-de-quanli-douzheng-lplz8f3wg1p0m5kmlh-w32jwnlzq|Hanji 610-1]], he answers [[WangMang|王莽]]'s call for direct speech by urging that [[HanPingdi|汉平帝]] be allowed contact with his mother-side and Feng/Wei relatives. In [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-698-wangchao-xingshuai-qishilu-weiao-de-yihan-yu-jinchao-zhi-jing-lqim2hbjtdzb0wwmsaxcyzj7u8ff|Hanji 698]], he is the [[WeiXiaoEasternHan|隗嚣]]-side adviser who urges submission to [[LiuXiu|光武帝刘秀]] against [[WangYuanWeiXiaoRetainer|王元]]'s holdout strategy.

## Current Profile

Hanji 610-1 adds the earlier court-remonstrance layer. Shentu Gang uses the Zhougong regency precedent to argue that even a virtuous regent can become the object of jealousy and rumor, so a child emperor should not be isolated from family protectors. His proposal to summon the Zhongshan Empress Dowager and install Feng and Wei relatives in idle posts challenges Wang Mang's separation of Pingdi from outer relatives. Wang Mang responds by having [[WangZhengjun|王政君]] issue an edict dismissing him, making Shentu Gang a case of [[ScriptedCandorTrap|直言作秀陷阱]]: an advertised opening for honest speech punishes the person who takes it literally.

Hanji 698 gives the later Longyou-adviser layer. Shentu Gang's advice is framed through mandate and public alignment. He says that when human support gathers, Heaven grants rule, and when human support departs, Heaven removes it. In the source's reading, Liu Xiu's repeated edicts and promises show a ruler offering trust and shared wealth rather than immediate destruction.

The later remonstrance asks Wei Xiao to identify the real objects of his fear and desire. That wording matters because it makes continued hesitation look less like prudent statecraft and more like a failure to read the changed political field. Wei Xiao does not accept the advice, so Shentu Gang again functions as an ignored warning rather than as a successful policy mover.

## Key Characteristics

- Direct remonstrant whose early source role challenges Wang Mang's isolation of Han Pingdi from outer relatives.
- Uses Zhougong precedent to turn regency virtue into a reason for safeguards rather than unchecked control.
- Punished speaker in a scripted candor setting where invited honesty is recoded as ideological offense.
- Wei Xiao-side adviser whose later source role is pro-submission remonstrance.
- Uses mandate and human-support language to describe Liu Xiu's advantage.
- Challenges Wei Xiao's fear and desire as the motives behind continued hesitation.
- Negative contrast to Wang Yuan's terrain-backed holdout strategy.

## Qualifications

This page preserves the established `ShentuGangEasternHan` route for continuity even though Hanji 610-1 broadens the title beyond an Eastern Han-only role. The sources support a shared named figure and repeated remonstrance pattern, but they do not reconstruct Shentu Gang's full biography, intervening offices, exact memorial wording, or whether every detail of the early and later career should be treated as continuous outside these episode summaries. Hanji 610-1 supports the outer-relative safeguard argument but leaves Zhongshan Empress Dowager and the Feng/Wei relatives source-scoped. Hanji 698 supports his internal remonstrance to Wei Xiao, not proof that submission carried no danger.

## What Changed

- Broadened the page from an Eastern Han-only adviser role to include Hanji 610-1's earlier Wang Mang-era remonstrance.
- Added the scripted-candor branch where Shentu Gang's invited direct speech is punished after challenging Pingdi's isolation from outer relatives.
- Preserved Hanji 698's later Wei Xiao-side submission warning as the Longyou branch.
