## Overview
McMurdo Station appears in [[the-continent-nobody-owns-everyone-benefits-from-summer-school]] as the main human-settlement case inside the episode's broader [[Antarctica]] economics story.

## Current Profile
The source presents McMurdo as a U.S.-operated scientific base and temporary company-town-like settlement. Its importance is not only scientific; the station makes ordinary economic questions visible in Antarctica, including worker services, stores, entertainment scarcity, fuel, waste, dormitories, and logistics.

## Key Characteristics
- McMurdo is described as a U.S.-operated Antarctic scientific base with about 2,000 people moving through during the year.
- It combines scientific purpose with settlement infrastructure such as mess halls, a post office, a store, an infirmary, and social spaces.
- It shows that even where essentials are provided, people still seek novelty, shopping, and diversion.
- Later environmental rules around fuel and waste make the station a small case of operational governance inside a protected continent.

## Evidence
- Settlement profile: [[the-continent-nobody-owns-everyone-benefits-from-summer-school]] describes McMurdo as a noisy, industrial company-town-like base with ordinary services.
- Retail and social life: [[the-continent-nobody-owns-everyone-benefits-from-summer-school]] uses [[LizWalters]]'s store work to show demand for novelty and entertainment.
- Environmental operations: [[the-continent-nobody-owns-everyone-benefits-from-summer-school]] says McMurdo later gained stricter rules for fuel and waste along with better heated dormitories.

## Qualifications
The source uses McMurdo as an illustrative economics case, not as a complete operational history of the station or the U.S. Antarctic Program.
