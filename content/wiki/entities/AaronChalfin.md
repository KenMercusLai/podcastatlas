# Aaron Chalfin

## Overview

Aaron Chalfin is a criminologist who supplies the episode's account of New York's post-Stop-and-Frisk homicide trend and its movement toward more focused violence enforcement.

## Current Profile

Chalfin reports that New York homicides fell by about half between 2010 and 2019 even as Stop and Frisk declined sharply and national homicide levels were relatively flat. The episode uses this sequence to reject a claim that mass stops were necessary for the city's safety gains.

He also describes the NYPD as shifting toward hotspot deployment, targeted investigations, and attention to the small number of people driving shootings. The source reports a large homicide reduction after major gang takedowns near public housing, but its summary does not establish the research design or separate enforcement effects from concurrent changes.

## Key Characteristics

- Interprets longitudinal New York homicide and policing trends.
- Contrasts mass stops with hotspot and network-focused enforcement.
- Supplies episode-reported program effects that require primary-study verification.
