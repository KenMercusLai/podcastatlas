# Are You Lonesome Tonight?

## Overview

“Are You Lonesome Tonight?” is the song at the center of the episode's [[Parapraxis]] case because [[ElvisPresley]] repeatedly performs its sung sections accurately while disrupting its emotionally exposed spoken bridge.

## Current Profile

Elvis recorded the song in Nashville in 1960 after military service, reportedly at the suggestion of his manager's wife. He preferred a live room with the musicians and asked for the lights to be turned off. The released performance contrasts a sung opening made mostly of questions to an absent lover with a spoken bridge in which the narrator admits deception, changed behavior, abandonment, and surrounding emptiness.

That formal reversal matters to the episode's interpretation. The opening lets the singer appear composed and concerned about the other person; the bridge forces first-person exposure while its speech must remain aligned with a waltz rhythm. Across ten reported live recordings, errors clustered in the bridge rather than the sung sections, making the song a joint case of musical difficulty, repeated memory disruption, and possible emotional pressure.

## Key Characteristics

- Combines sung questions with a spoken bridge that reverses attention toward the narrator's own loneliness.
- Places speech against a waltz pulse, making the bridge rhythmically unusual and demanding.
- Was recorded by Elvis in darkness with musicians performing together in the room.
- Produced repeated live variants involving altered words, jokes, laughter, and loss of place.
- Concentrates the episode's reported error pattern around betrayal, rejection, and emptiness.
