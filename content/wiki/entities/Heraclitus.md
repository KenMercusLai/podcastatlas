# Heraclitus / 赫拉克利特

## Overview

Heraclitus is represented through the river-change motif: the world and the observer are continually changing, so a person cannot encounter exactly the same river twice. One source uses the motif directly to frame identity through change; another turns it into a workplace-satire joke about repeated resignation and rehiring.

## Current Profile

In [[introducing-revisionist-history-season-two]], [[MarkCohen]] uses Heraclitus' river image as the opening step toward the [[ShipOfTheseus|Ship of Theseus]]. The river changes continuously while retaining a recognizable name, preparing the question of whether an object remains itself when every material component is gradually replaced.

In [[92-bailatu-shangbanji-yong-zhexue-gaoxiao-zhichang-716822350]], Heraclitus appears at his own farewell party after repeatedly resigning and being rehired. The satire combines the river-change motif with [[FriedrichNietzsche|Nietzsche]]'s eternal-return frame: a farewell, gifts, resignation, and rehiring can become an exploitative routine that repeats without producing meaningful change.

## Key Characteristics

- Associated in both sources with continual change rather than static identity.
- The river image distinguishes recognizable continuity from identical material state.
- Provides the conceptual bridge to the Ship of Theseus persistence problem.
- Is repurposed in workplace satire to expose repetition disguised as change.
