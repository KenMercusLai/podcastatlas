# Martin Shkreli

## Overview
Martin Shkreli is a biotech entrepreneur and former hedge-fund manager who appears in an [[AllIn|All-In]] interview about quantum computing, alternative AI hardware, pharmaceutical asset selection, and the psychological lessons he draws from prison.

## Current Profile
The source presents Shkreli as a contrarian investor-operator whose strongest claimed edge is not short-term trading but finding scientific and commercial possibilities in overlooked drug molecules. He applies the same distinction between technology and investability to quantum computing: a field can matter scientifically while current companies lack compelling workloads, economics, or timing.

His closing self-assessment qualifies the persona. Shkreli says attachment to being seen as an exceptional trader prevented him from accepting that entrepreneurship suited him better, and he treats a broader identity grounded in science, family, and ordinary relationships as a corrective.

## Key Characteristics
- Separates scientific possibility from present commercial utility and investment price.
- Describes molecule selection, indication choice, paper and patent review, and relationship building as core biotech work.
- Is bullish on long-run demand for AI while skeptical that present systems bypass drug-development validation.
- Prefers compute-architecture improvement, including photonics, to assuming indefinite replication of today's infrastructure.
- Uses his own trading losses and imprisonment as evidence about timing error, ego, and identity overconcentration.
