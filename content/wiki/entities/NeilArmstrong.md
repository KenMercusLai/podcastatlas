# Neil Armstrong

## Overview
Neil Armstrong was the commander of [[Apollo11]] and the first person to step onto the [[Moon]].

## Current Profile
The episode presents Armstrong through trained judgment under uncertainty. His public first step dominates memory, but [[438-the-moonwalkers-with-tom-hanks-glt1422432857]] places it after a landing in which terrain, depth perception, fuel, dust, and visual cues required active command, and before an equally necessary ascent and return chain.

## Key Characteristics
- Commanded the first crewed lunar landing.
- Took control when the planned landing area appeared unsafe.
- Used shadow and dust cues in an environment without atmospheric depth cues.
- Became the public voice of the first human step on the Moon.
- Shared a mission dependent on Aldrin, Collins, spacecraft systems, and ground support.

## Evidence
### Landing judgment
- [[438-the-moonwalkers-with-tom-hanks-glt1422432857]] describes Armstrong responding to rocky terrain and unfamiliar lunar visibility during Eagle's descent.

### First step and crew role
- [[438-the-moonwalkers-with-tom-hanks-glt1422432857]] defends the remembered form of his first-step line and links exit order to command and hatch design.

### Scientific work
- [[438-the-moonwalkers-with-tom-hanks-glt1422432857]] reports that rocks gathered during the short mission contributed substantially to lunar geology.

## Qualifications
- The source offers a celebratory conversation, not a complete biography or technical reconstruction.
- Exact dialogue, motives, landing-detail emphasis, and geology claims remain episode-attributed.
- Individual courage should not obscure the mission's collective systems and personnel.
