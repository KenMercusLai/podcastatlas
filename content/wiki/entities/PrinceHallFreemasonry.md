# Prince Hall Freemasonry

## Overview

Prince Hall Freemasonry is the Black American Masonic tradition formed after African Americans were excluded from white lodges.

## Current Profile

The episode presents Prince Hall Freemasonry as both evidence of American Masonry's racial division and an example of excluded people adapting the lodge form for durable civic capacity. Its networks supported mutual recognition and organization across abolition, Civil War recruitment, civil-rights work, NAACP funding, and the public lives of members such as Thurgood Marshall and Medgar Evers.

## Key Characteristics

- It emerged from racial exclusion within American [[Freemasonry]].
- It reused lodge ritual, fraternity, rank, and network structure under Black leadership.
- It is associated in the episode with anti-slavery activity and Civil War recruitment.
- It later supported civil-rights organization and funding.

## Evidence

### Exclusion and institutional formation

- [[335-the-freemasons-historys-greatest-conspiracy-theory-glt4122245847]] connects the tradition's foundation to exclusion from white lodges after the American Republic's founding.

### Civic and civil-rights capacity

- [[335-the-freemasons-historys-greatest-conspiracy-theory-glt4122245847]] links Prince Hall networks to abolition, recruitment, NAACP support, Thurgood Marshall, and Medgar Evers.

## Qualifications

The episode gives a compressed survey rather than an institutional history. It does not establish the relative contribution of Prince Hall organizations to each named campaign or figure, and it should not turn diverse Black Masonic bodies into one uniform political actor.
