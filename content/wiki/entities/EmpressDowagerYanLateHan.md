## Overview

阎太后 / Empress Dowager Yan is a Shun-restoration dowager whose power first appears as active succession control and later as defeated formal status. Hanji 803 shows her concealing [[LiuYiBeixiangHouLateHan|北乡侯刘裔]]'s death, closing palace gates, summoning royal descendants, and trying to use [[YanXianLateHan|阎显]]'s side to recover control; Hanji 805 then shows [[EmperorShunOfHan|汉顺帝]]'s camp deciding whether humiliating or isolating her will strengthen the new regime or damage its legitimacy.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-803-han-shundi-ruhe-zai-xuefengxueyu-zhong-dengshang-huangwei-lqvno7t-gcgxtbs3phuflr4ybe2s|Hanji 803]] supplies Yan Taihou's active crisis role. When Liu Yi dies on 125-10-27, she fears disorder, conceals the death, sends edicts to summon royal descendants, closes palace gates, stations troops, and increases security. After [[SunChengLateHan|孙程]]'s group enthrones Liu Bao, she promises large marquisate rewards for capturing Liu Bao or [[LiRunLateHan|李润]]. The restoration side then cuts her off from outside communication, forces her to surrender the imperial seal, and moves her to Changle Palace.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-805-tongtou-renxing-de-nvren-jing-neng-yuce-jixiong-ll3bzoyrkbtnvt-aqu9nqelqonew|Hanji 805]] places Yan Taihou at the center of Shun's immediate post-accession settlement. After the Yan family is destroyed and Liu Bao is restored as Emperor Shun through Sun Cheng's eunuch group, Yilang Chen Chan argues that Yan Taihou has no blood tie or mother-son affection with Shun and should be moved to a separate palace where they never meet.

The counterargument comes through [[ZhouJuLateHan|周举]], who advises Li He to memorialize Shun. Zhou Ju invokes filial repair stories around Shun, Zheng Zhuang Gong, and Qin Shi Huang, then makes the political risk explicit: if Yan Xian's brothers have just been executed and Yan Taihou is confined before dying unexpectedly, Shun will struggle to command the empire. Li He submits the advice; Shun accepts it, visits the East Palace on 126-01-01, performs full rites, and keeps the public account that Yan Taihou is settled. She dies on 126-01-19, with the official explanation given as illness and the host's suspicion kept source-scoped.

## Key Characteristics

- Defeated dowager whose kin network has been destroyed but whose title still shapes Shun's public legitimacy.
- Active succession controller who conceals Liu Yi's death and tries to complete a new selection before opponents move.
- Gate-and-seal loser whose communication is cut and whose seal surrender marks restoration victory.
- Formal mother-like figure whom Chen Chan wants separated because personal affection and blood ties are absent.
- Political-risk figure whose confinement would make any sudden death look like regime violence.
- Recipient of a ritual repair strategy: visible audience, full rites, continued support, and official language of calm.
- Source-bounded death case where illness is the official account and further suspicion remains unproven.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 803 and Hanji 805 and does not reconstruct Yan Taihou's earlier regency, family network, or private palace conditions beyond the restoration crisis. Hanji 803 supports active succession control, concealed death, seal surrender, and transfer; Hanji 805 supports Shun's later legitimacy management. The current judgment does not prove the true cause of Yan Taihou's death. Minor actors in the debate, including Chen Chan and Li He, remain source-scoped until additional notes make them recurring canonical subjects.
