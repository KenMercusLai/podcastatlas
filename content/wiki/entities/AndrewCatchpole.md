# Andrew Catchpole

## Overview

Andrew Catchpole is identified in the episode as the leader of a controlled COVID-19 human challenge study involving young unvaccinated volunteers.

## Current Profile

The study deliberately exposed 36 people aged 18 to 29 to SARS-CoV-2 under hospital monitoring to investigate the amount of exposure required for infection. Catchpole describes roughly 27,000 applicants as people seeking a concrete way to help during the pandemic.

His account supplies the contemporary side of the episode's ethical comparison. Large volunteer interest is evidence of altruistic motivation worth investigating, but it does not by itself establish comprehension, proportional risk, scientific necessity, or ethical validity.

## Key Characteristics

- Led the COVID challenge study as represented in the episode.
- Used controlled exposure and hospital monitoring to study early infection.
- Interpreted high application volume as a desire to contribute during crisis.
- Represents the researcher case for taking volunteer altruism seriously.
- Does not eliminate residual unknown-risk and selection questions.

## Evidence

### Study design

- [[the-rise-of-the-guinea-pigs]] describes 36 unvaccinated volunteers aged 18 to 29 receiving controlled nasal exposure with monitoring and treatments available.

### Volunteer response

- [[the-rise-of-the-guinea-pigs]] attributes to Catchpole the interpretation that approximately 27,000 applicants wanted to do their part during the pandemic.

### Ethical comparison

- [[the-rise-of-the-guinea-pigs]] compares the study with Minnesota to test whether informed adults may accept uncertain harm for scientific benefit.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to a structured podcast summary, not the study protocol, publication, consent materials, adverse-event data, institutional review, or Catchpole's wider work. The episode reports no death or serious illness, but that does not establish absence of all short- or long-term harm.
