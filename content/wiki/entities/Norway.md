# Norway

## Overview

Norway appears in the wiki as a Scandinavian state whose wartime defense of sovereignty and later governance of oil and export markets both depend on institutions converting geographic or resource exposure into durable national capacity.

## Current Profile

The wartime branch begins with vulnerability. [[621-the-nazis-at-war-blitzkrieg-part-2-glt8711886512]] connects Norway's strategic importance to Swedish iron ore shipped through the ice-free port of Narvik and to rival German and Allied plans. The country was poorly prepared, but Oscarsborg's sinking of the Blücher delayed Oslo's capture, [[HaakonVII|Haakon VII]] and the government escaped, and volunteers sustained resistance for roughly two months. Allied intervention then failed and withdrew, leaving German occupation despite meaningful Norwegian resistance.

The later resource-governance branch shows a stronger anticipatory state. [[how-to-beat-the-resource-curse-in-norway-summer-school]] presents Norway as rich and high-trust but not frictionless: [[HildeBjornland]] notes debates over entrepreneurship, productivity, technology, AI readiness, wealth taxes, and public spending. Norway prepared early for oil, regulated companies, limited license blocks, taxed heavily, reinvested in offshore technical capacity, and saved later revenues through the [[NorwegianOilFund]].

The salmon branch turns Norway from resource manager into market creator. With excess supply and cultural resistance to raw salmon in [[Japan]], Norwegian officials and [[BjornAdekOlsen]] used [[ExportMarketCoordination]], [[NationalExportBranding]], and a deal with [[Nishire]] to help normalize salmon sushi.

## Key Characteristics

- Strategically exposed Scandinavian state whose coast and Narvik ore route attracted rival wartime plans.
- Poorly prepared but capable of consequential military, civic, and constitutional resistance in 1940.
- Resource-rich democracy that used prior state capacity and social trust to govern oil wealth.
- Builder of offshore knowledge and fiscal savings rather than only an extractor and spender.
- Public-private export coordinator that helped create a Japanese market for Norwegian salmon.

## Qualifications

The bounded sources cover one wartime campaign and one political-economy survey, not a complete national history. Campaign duration, force and loss totals, the effect of resistance, Allied and German motives, oil-fund value, per-capita comparisons, institutional causality, and salmon-market origin claims remain source-scoped. Later institutional success should not be projected backward to erase weak 1940 preparation, and 1940 defeat should not be projected forward as a stable national characteristic.
