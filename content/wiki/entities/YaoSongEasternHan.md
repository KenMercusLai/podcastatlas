## Overview

姚松 / Yao Song is an Eastern Han court official whose current wiki profile is a single vivid scene of speech under imperial anger. The page is disambiguated from the modern [[YaoSong|Yao Song / 姚颂]] entity.

## Current Profile

Yao Song appears when Mingdi becomes angry enough to move against him physically. Yao Song hides under a bed and quotes a ritual formula, "天子穆穆，诸侯皇皇", reframing the scene around the dignified bearing expected of rulers and nobles. The move defuses the emperor's anger without a frontal rebuke.

That narrow episode matters because it exposes a practical language for survival in a severe court. Yao Song does not reverse a policy, but he shows how ritualized speech can give an angry superior a face-saving path away from violence.

## Key Characteristics

- Court official remembered through a crisis of imperial anger rather than through a policy portfolio.
- Uses ritual language as indirect correction, making restraint available without explicit confrontation.
- Illustrates hierarchy-aware speech under a ruler known in the same episode for harshness and surveillance.
- Requires identity disambiguation from the modern Yao Song already present in the wiki.

## Qualifications

The evidence is single-scene and single-source. Yao Song should be treated as an example of ritualized speech under pressure, not yet as a general remonstrance theorist or major Eastern Han administrator.
