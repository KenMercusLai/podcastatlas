# Cesar Millan

## Overview
Cesar Millan is the dog-behavior expert interviewed by [[AndrewHuberman]] in [[raising-a-dog-mastering-calm-assertive-energy-cesar-millan-scim6380580289]]. In this source, he presents dog training as a human self-regulation and leadership practice built around calm presence, movement, structure, trust, respect, and correctly timed affection.

## Current Profile
Millan's source profile is practical and interpretive rather than laboratory-driven. He argues that dogs respond before words to human energy, body language, intention, and action; a handler therefore has to create safety and clarity before asking for obedience or giving affection. His dog-care order is body, mind, then heart: exercise and structured rules come before affection, because affection given during anxiety, excitement, or confusion can reinforce the very state the owner wants to change.

The episode also makes Millan a bridge between companion-animal care and human leadership. He uses greeting, walking, feeding, leash handling, puppy selection, barking, bed access, and end-of-life care as recurring tests of whether humans can stay calm, patient, consistent, and context-aware.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames dog behavior as a mirror of the human handler's emotional state, body language, and follow-through.
- Treats dogs first as animals with species-specific needs rather than as names, breeds, children, or human fantasies.
- Organizes practical dog care around exercise, discipline, and affection, with safety, peace, trust, and respect preceding love.
- Uses structured walks, calm greetings, feeding rules, and context-specific touch as repeatable household rituals.
- Extends dog handling into a broader source-scoped account of leadership, relationship roles, and self-regulation.
