# Diego Bohórquez

## Overview
Diego Bohórquez is presented in [[the-science-of-your-gut-sense-the-gut-brain-axis-dr-diego-bohorquez-scim8970398714]] as a Duke University researcher trained across gastrointestinal physiology, nutrition, and neuroscience whose laboratory studies rapid sensory communication from the gut to the brain.

## Current Profile
Bohórquez's work reframes the intestinal lining as a sensory interface. His laboratory used fluorescent labeling, cell culture, viral tracing, sequencing, organoids, and gut-adapted optogenetics to investigate enteroendocrine cells that form neural connections. He coined “neuropod” for these process-bearing cells and argues that their fast vagal communication complements slower hormone diffusion.

The interview also presents a broader intellectual style: mechanistic experiments are connected to food preference, bariatric surgery, appetite, Amazonian plant knowledge, shared meals, bodily awareness, and intuition. The strongest profile is therefore a cross-disciplinary investigator who moves between cellular mechanism and lived experience while acknowledging that several wider interpretations remain emerging or speculative.

## Key Characteristics
- Studies gut sensory epithelial cells and their neural connections to the vagus nerve and brainstem.
- Combines gastrointestinal physiology, nutrition, neuroscience, and modern circuit-tracing tools.
- Uses sugar preference and optogenetic manipulation to test whether gut sensory cells influence behavior.
- Treats indigenous food and plant knowledge as a structured knowledge system rather than mere folklore.
- Distinguishes well-supported nutrient-sensing mechanisms from emerging ideas about intuition, rhythms, and shared experience.
