# Flight to Varennes

## Overview

The Flight to Varennes was the failed June 1791 escape attempt by [[LouisXVI|Louis XVI]] and [[MarieAntoinette|Marie Antoinette]] that transformed distrust of the royal couple into a crisis of monarchy itself.

## Current Profile

The source presents Varennes as a decisive evidentiary break. The royal family tried to reach the eastern frontier, where loyal forces and possible foreign support could enable resistance to the Revolution, but they were intercepted and returned to Paris. Louis's accompanying declaration rejected the revolutionary settlement, making earlier professions of cooperation look deceptive and helping turn republicanism from a marginal preference into a plausible institutional demand.

## Key Characteristics

- Failed royal escape toward the eastern frontier.
- Plan connected to loyal troops and possible armed action against Paris.
- Public return marked by hostility and conspicuous withdrawal of royal deference.
- Written rejection of the Revolution that contradicted Louis's prior acceptance.
- Trigger for republican petitioning and destruction of royal symbols.
- Evidence that made broader conspiracy narratives harder to dismiss.
- Immediate precondition of the Champ de Mars political crisis.

## Qualifications

- The source's reconstruction of intended troop movements and private royal calculations remains source-scoped.
- Real royal plotting did not prove every later accusation against nobles, priests, strangers, or political moderates.
- Varennes made republicanism plausible but did not itself abolish monarchy.

## What Changed

- Adds the trust-breaking event that made the 1791 constitutional settlement and later wartime legitimacy crisis intelligible.
