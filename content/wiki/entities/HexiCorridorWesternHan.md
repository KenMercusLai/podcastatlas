## Overview

The Western Han Hexi Corridor is presented as a long northwestern zone whose strategic transfer unfolds through battlefield disruption, [[HunyeKingWesternHan|浑邪王]]'s later surrender, settlement, and administrative development rather than one isolated act. The [[SecondHexiCampaignWesternHan|第二次河西之战]] weakens the western Xiongnu field around Qilian and Yanzhi, while the surrender-and-settlement sequence supports more durable Han use of the corridor as a base toward the Western Regions. Its strategic importance is clearer in the current evidence than the exact dates on which Jiuquan, Wuwei, Zhangye, and Dunhuang became commanderies.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-315-1-hanwudi-gao-qiangchai-yiju-shoufu-xiongnu-wang-llyk4i76izmxe_el_ewpbbayqq5i|Hanji 315-1]] links the surrender and resettlement of Hunye King's former followers to reduced Xiongnu activity from Jincheng and Hexi toward the Qilian Mountains and Yanze. The episode says fewer beacon alarms allow [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] to halve garrison forces in Longxi, Beidi, and Shang, while the corridor becomes a forward base for Han movement toward the Western Regions.

The earlier battlefield rupture makes corridor transfer a process rather than a single surrender event. After [[GongsunAoWesternHan|公孙敖]] misses the planned Juyan reunion, [[HuoQubing|霍去病]] continues into the opposing rear; the source reports surrenders, defeated groups, advance to the Qilian area, and the Xiongnu lament over losing Qilian and Yanzhi. This supports a major weakening of the western Xiongnu position, but the claim that the northwest thereafter had no Xiongnu presence is too absolute to reconcile with the episode's own closing raids and the later surrender sequence. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-309-1-huoqubing-duohui-hexi-zoulang-zhongguo-bixu-tongyi-llcwkkdgjpx2zjeoyvhdjln3nqik|Hanji 309-1]]

The administrative chronology remains contested. The episode attributes one sequence to the *Hanshu* annals, another to its geographical treatise, and two further reconciliations to Zhang Weihua and Li Bingquan. The current profile therefore treats the corridor's strategic consolidation and the four commanderies' exact establishment dates as separate questions.

## Key Characteristics

- Northwestern corridor whose transfer from Xiongnu control is presented as a sequence of battlefield disruption, surrender, supervised settlement, and later administration.
- Forward base connecting Han frontier control with later movement toward the Western Regions.
- Region whose security is linked in the episode to lower beacon activity and reduced garrison demand in several inner frontier commanderies.
- Administrative space associated with Jiuquan, Wuwei, Zhangye, and Dunhuang, but with disputed establishment dates and sequence.

## Qualifications

The page is bounded to two compact podcast summaries and does not reconstruct the corridor's full geography, prior populations, military colonization, roads, irrigation, administration, or later history. Hanji 309-1's route, Lesser Yuezhi framing, group identities, distance, losses, captive ranks, and claims of fatal or permanent Xiongnu displacement remain source-scoped. Hanji 315-1's 900-kilometre advance and 150,000-square-kilometre area are not independently verified here. Their causal language may compress the combined effects of military campaigns, surrender, resettlement, commandery formation, and later diplomacy. Most importantly, strategic possession of the corridor does not by itself settle when all four commanderies were formally established or how their boundaries changed.

## What Changed

- Added the second-Hexi battlefield layer before Hunye King's surrender and settlement.
- Reframed corridor transfer as a multi-stage process rather than the result of one victory or one submission.
- Preserved the strategic-base synthesis while keeping commandery chronology and permanent-clearance claims unresolved.
