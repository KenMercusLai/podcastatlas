# Khartoum

## Overview

Khartoum is the capital of [[Sudan]], represented across the current sources through the 1884-1885 [[SiegeOfKhartoum|Mahdist siege]] and the devastation left by the 2020s war between the [[SudaneseArmedForces|SAF]] and [[RapidSupportForces|RSF]].

## Current Profile

The historical source makes Khartoum the place where an ambiguous British-Egyptian mission became a siege, military defeat, and imperial martyr story. [[CharlesGeorgeGordon|General Gordon]] arrived in February 1884, performed reforming gestures, began civilian evacuation, organized defenses, and refused to leave. Mahdist forces captured the city on 26 January 1885; Gordon was killed, and British steamers arrived two days later.

The contemporary source resists a second victory narrative. After the SAF retook Khartoum from the RSF, [[TomGardner]] described a quiet, dark, looted, and devastated capital rather than a recovered city. Across both periods, capture of the capital has high symbolic value but does not by itself resolve the political order, humanitarian crisis, or wider war.

## Key Characteristics

- National capital whose control carries exceptional political and symbolic weight.
- Site of the 1884-1885 siege and the death of General Gordon.
- Central object in British imperial martyr and revenge memory.
- Major battlefield in the SAF-RSF war and severely damaged after recapture.
- Recurring example of why taking a capital should not be equated with stable national recovery.

## Qualifications

The two sources cover distinct wars roughly 140 years apart and should not be collapsed into one continuous conflict. Both are largely organized through British or international reporting rather than Khartoum residents' own historical record. Casualties, siege decisions, the circumstances of Gordon's death, current territorial control, and contemporary damage estimates remain source-scoped or time-sensitive.
