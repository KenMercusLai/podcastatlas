# 知乎 / Zhihu

Zhihu / 知乎 is one of the four PC-era Chinese community cases in [[no-212-cengjing-de-jingshen-jiaoluo-douban-zhihu-tieba-hupu-zhongguo-hulianwang-gushi-25-1003510980]]. The episode presents it as a knowledge Q&A platform founded by [[ZhouYuan|周源]] after his journalism and startup attempts, with early product inspiration from serious technology-blog comment and question culture.

The source emphasizes invitation as both product mechanism and status signal. Early Zhihu accumulated professional answerers and familiar internet-industry figures, making "谢邀" a sign of being asked by someone inside the room before it became a joke about Zhihu style. Opening registration, launching Zhihu Daily, and adding columns helped growth, but also made the community less intimate and more difficult to govern.

Zhihu matters in the wiki as a case of [[KnowledgeCommunityCreatorIncentives]]. It wanted to become a market for knowledge through Live, books, paid consulting, membership, and courses, but the source argues that creators received weaker emotional and economic feedback as the room scaled. The Wukong Q&A subsidy fight led by [[ByteDance]] exposed this weakness: subsidies could attract answerers, but they did not automatically build [[ChinesePCInternetCommunities|community]].

After its U.S. and Hong Kong listings, Zhihu's paid-content business moved toward stories, web-fiction-like consumption, IP, and YanYan Stories. The source treats that turn as commercially pragmatic but also as a move away from the platform's early self-image as an elite knowledge community.
