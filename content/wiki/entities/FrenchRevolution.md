# French Revolution / 法国大革命

## Overview

The French Revolution was the political and social upheaval that overthrew the Bourbon monarchy, created a republic, executed [[LouisXVI|Louis XVI]], and by 1793 entered a crisis in which war, scarcity, revolt, factional purge, and emergency institutions converged into the Reign of Terror.

## Current Profile

The sources frame the Revolution as a pressure system rather than a simple contest between liberty and reaction. Fiscal and monarchical breakdown opened the sequence, and Louis's trial converted the Republic's unresolved relation to sacred kingship into public judgment, de-royalization, and execution. By 1793 inflation, food scarcity, multi-front war, conscription, the [[VendeeWar|Vendée]], Dumouriez's defection, Girondin-Montagnard conflict, and federalist revolt made coercive centralization appear urgent. The Terror's machinery was assembled through emergency responses, while political death repeatedly generated rival martyr and enemy narratives.

## Key Characteristics

- Its opening crisis joined absolutist centralization, fiscal failure, bad harvests, war debt, Enlightenment ideas, and collapsing royal legitimacy.
- Louis's trial and execution joined legal punishment to the symbolic destruction of sacred kingship and an asserted republican foundation.
- Republican institutions remained vulnerable to armed Parisian pressure and factional definitions of "the people."
- Material deprivation made claims of sabotage and betrayal politically powerful.
- Foreign war and internal rebellion blurred the distinction between political opposition and security threat.
- Emergency bodies accumulated into a terror apparatus without requiring a single original blueprint.
- Execution, burial, art, funeral ritual, Roman precedent, and Christian forms shaped revolutionary legitimacy and memory.

## Qualifications

- The sources focus on Louis's execution and the later Marat-Corday crisis and therefore do not provide a comprehensive social or imperial history of the Revolution.
- The episode-level Vendée death estimate and modern comparisons to populism remain source-scoped.
- The claim that the Terror was improvised does not mean it was accidental or that participants lacked agency once the institutions existed.
- The interpretation of regicide as the Republic's founding sacrifice and the “one vote” sentence margin remain source-scoped rather than settled constitutional or historiographic judgments.

## What Changed

- The profile now connects the January 1793 execution of Louis to the later crisis and Terror branch.
- De-royalization and regicide are distinguished from the later machinery of emergency coercion.
- Rival martyr memories now link Louis's execution to Marat's later political afterlife without equating the two deaths.
