# French Revolution / 法国大革命

## Overview

The French Revolution was the political and social upheaval that overthrew the Bourbon monarchy, created a republic, and by 1793 entered a crisis in which war, scarcity, revolt, factional purge, and emergency institutions converged into the Reign of Terror.

## Current Profile

The sources frame the Revolution as a pressure system rather than a simple contest between liberty and reaction. Fiscal and monarchical breakdown opened the revolutionary sequence; by 1793 inflation, food scarcity, multi-front war, conscription, the [[VendeeWar|Vendée]], Dumouriez's defection, Girondin-Montagnard conflict, and federalist revolt made coercive centralization appear urgent. The Terror's machinery was assembled through emergency responses, while Marat's assassination fused institutional escalation with martyr politics and fear of conspiracy.

## Key Characteristics

- Its opening crisis joined absolutist centralization, fiscal failure, bad harvests, war debt, Enlightenment ideas, and collapsing royal legitimacy.
- Republican institutions remained vulnerable to armed Parisian pressure and factional definitions of "the people."
- Material deprivation made claims of sabotage and betrayal politically powerful.
- Foreign war and internal rebellion blurred the distinction between political opposition and security threat.
- Emergency bodies accumulated into a terror apparatus without requiring a single original blueprint.
- Art, funeral ritual, Roman precedent, and Christian forms shaped revolutionary legitimacy and memory.

## Evidence

### Structural origins and factional language

- [[125-mala-zhisi-beibi-de-ansha-huo-zhengyi-de-chujue-783972516]] traces fiscal, monarchical, intellectual, and urban pressures and the hardening conflict among Girondins, Jacobins, the Mountain, and the Plain.

### The 1793 crisis system

- [[707-the-terror-an-assassination-in-paris-part-1-glt6580043397]] joins inflation, shortages, European war, Saint-Domingue, Vendée revolt, military betrayal, federalist resistance, and Girondin purge.

### Institutional and symbolic radicalization

- [[707-the-terror-an-assassination-in-paris-part-1-glt6580043397]] identifies the tribunal, surveillance committees, deputies on mission, and public-safety committee as accumulating machinery, then shows Marat's cult intensifying the moral case for terror.
- [[125-mala-zhisi-beibi-de-ansha-huo-zhengyi-de-chujue-783972516]] supplies the longer art, virtue, assassination, and historical-memory frame.

## Qualifications

- Both sources focus on Marat and Corday and therefore do not provide a comprehensive social or imperial history of the Revolution.
- The episode-level Vendée death estimate and modern comparisons to populism remain source-scoped.
- The claim that the Terror was improvised does not mean it was accidental or that participants lacked agency once the institutions existed.

## What Changed

- The Revolution's profile now includes a detailed 1793 transition from overlapping crisis to organized terror.
- The Girondin purge and provincial federalist resistance make the Paris-versus-provinces dimension explicit.
- Emergency institutional formation is separated from later retrospective ideas of a unified Terror plan.
