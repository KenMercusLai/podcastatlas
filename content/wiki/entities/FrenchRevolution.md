# French Revolution / 法国大革命

## Overview

The French Revolution was the political and social upheaval that overthrew the Bourbon monarchy, created a republic under foreign-war pressure, executed [[LouisXVI|Louis XVI]], and by 1793 entered a crisis in which scarcity, revolt, factional purge, and emergency institutions converged into the Reign of Terror.

## Current Profile

The sources frame the Revolution as a pressure system rather than a simple contest between liberty and reaction. Fiscal and monarchical breakdown opened the sequence; abolition and [[BattleOfValmy|Valmy]] then gave an institutionally unsettled Republic political and military existence. Its universal claims were bounded from the start by [[GenderedRepublicanCitizenship|gendered citizenship]]: [[Marianne]] could embody Liberty while actual women remained outside suffrage and direct deliberation. Victory encouraged [[RevolutionaryIdeologicalWar|universalizing war]], while factional conflict made moderation vulnerable to betrayal claims. Louis's trial converted the unresolved relation to sacred kingship into judgment and execution; by 1793 scarcity, war, revolt, betrayal, and purge made coercive centralization appear urgent.

## Key Characteristics

- Its opening crisis joined absolutist centralization, fiscal failure, bad harvests, war debt, Enlightenment ideas, and collapsing royal legitimacy.
- Abolition created a Republic before its institutions, mandate, founding symbolism, or meaning of universal citizenship were fully settled.
- Valmy transformed limited battlefield survival into republican confidence and enabled a shift toward ideological expansion.
- Girondin-Montagnard conflict made political position unstable as radicals could be outflanked and moderation recoded as betrayal.
- Louis's trial and execution joined legal punishment to the symbolic destruction of sacred kingship and an asserted republican foundation.
- Republican institutions remained vulnerable to armed Parisian pressure and factional definitions of “the people,” while women participated in revolutionary action without equal formal power.
- Material deprivation, foreign war, and internal rebellion made sabotage claims powerful and blurred opposition with security threat.

## Qualifications

- The sources focus on Louis's execution and the later Marat-Corday crisis and therefore do not provide a comprehensive social or imperial history of the Revolution.
- The women's-rights episode is selective rather than a comprehensive social history; “first feminist,” participation levels, personal influence, and reasons women accepted exclusion remain source-scoped.
- Valmy's “victory” and the absence of one clear republican proclamation are qualified interpretations rather than uncontested classifications.
- The episode-level Vendée death estimate and modern comparisons to populism remain source-scoped.
- The claim that the Terror was improvised does not mean it was accidental or that participants lacked agency once the institutions existed.
- The interpretation of regicide as the Republic's founding sacrifice and the “one vote” sentence margin remain source-scoped rather than settled constitutional or historiographic judgments.

## What Changed

- The profile now treats women's exclusion as an internal limit on revolutionary universalism, not a separate social-history footnote.
- Marianne's female embodiment and de Gouges's declaration expose the divide between republican symbolism and citizenship.
- Women's crowd, salon, club, and military activity now qualifies any account that treats revolutionary politics as exclusively male action.

- [[LouisXVI]] - deposed king whose trial and execution became a test of republican legitimacy.
- [[NationalConvention]] - assembly that combined judgment, representation, and revolutionary symbolism.
- [[Marianne]] - female personification of a Republic that denied women equal citizenship.
- [[OlympeDeGouges]] - writer who applied revolutionary universal rights to women.
- [[GenderedRepublicanCitizenship]] - boundary between male public rights and female domestic patriotic duty.
- [[BattleOfValmy]] - limited military success with transformative republican consequences.
- [[Montagnards]] - radical Convention camp opposed to the Girondins.
- [[RepublicanFoundingWithoutSettlement]] - gap between abolition and a fully settled new order.
- [[RevolutionaryIdeologicalWar]] - outward universalization of revolutionary reform through war.
- [[RevolutionaryFactionalOutflanking]] - mechanism by which factional competition narrowed moderation.
- [[RegicideAsRepublicanFounding]] - interpretation of the king's death as a constitutive republican act.
- [[VendeeWar]] - civil-war front that intensified emergency politics.
- [[EmergencyInstitutionsToTerror]] - institutional pathway from crisis response to coercive system.
- [[CrisisValidatedConspiracyPolitics]] - threat-perception mechanism sharpened by real betrayal.
