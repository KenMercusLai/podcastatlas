# Jen Easterly

## Overview
Jen Easterly is the security and resilience leader in the source who reframes cybersecurity as a software-quality and incentive problem rather than only an attacker-detection problem.

## Current Profile
Drawing on military, intelligence, government cyber-defense, and private-sector experience as described by the episode, Easterly argues that security should be embedded from the beginning. She characterizes secure software as difficult for customers to evaluate, which lets speed, cost, contract terms, patch obligations, and weak liability rules displace quality. She expects AI to advantage attackers in the short term but believes automated repair and legacy-code refactoring could improve defender advantage over a longer horizon.

## Key Characteristics
- Reframes cybersecurity as a software-quality problem.
- Critiques security added after development through an aftermarket of compensating products.
- Connects poor security incentives to information asymmetry, liability, and regulation.
- Highlights industrial-scale reliance on artisanal-scale open-source maintenance.
- Holds a near-term attacker-advantage and long-term defender-opportunity view of AI.
