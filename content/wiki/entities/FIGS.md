## Overview
FIGS is a healthcare-apparel company co-founded by [[HeatherHasson]] and [[TrinaSpear]] and launched in 2013. [[figs-trina-spear-and-heather-hasson-the-2-5-billion-scrubs-brand-that-investors-said-would-never-work-vmp6448511514]] presents it as a design-led, direct-to-consumer challenge to distributor-centered medical uniforms.

## Current Profile
The company's profile rests on a linked product and channel thesis. [[TechnicalComfort]] reframed scrubs as performance apparel for healthcare work, while [[EndUserPurchasedWorkwear]] explains why FIGS addressed clinicians directly even though the clothing was used inside institutions. Early direct selling generated product feedback; later online distribution, workplace visibility, stronger production spending, and word of mouth supported scale.

## Key Characteristics
- FIGS treats healthcare workers as a distinct professional community and direct customer group.
- Its product position joins stretch, softness, fit, pockets, tool storage, layering, and deliberate colors.
- Its direct model redirects distributor and licensing economics toward product quality and customer relationships.
- Its founding team divided product and manufacturing leadership from operations and fundraising.
- Its history includes concentrated founder governance, a costly legal defense, pandemic supply response, and a 2021 IPO.

## Evidence
- **User-led product development:** [[figs-trina-spear-and-heather-hasson-the-2-5-billion-scrubs-brand-that-investors-said-would-never-work-vmp6448511514]] describes hospital observation, tailoring requests, sidewalk sales, and clinician feedback on fit and function.
- **Direct channel logic:** [[figs-trina-spear-and-heather-hasson-the-2-5-billion-scrubs-brand-that-investors-said-would-never-work-vmp6448511514]] says institutional selling stalled because clinicians commonly purchased their own uniforms, while workplace word of mouth amplified online acquisition.
- **Product economics:** [[figs-trina-spear-and-heather-hasson-the-2-5-billion-scrubs-brand-that-investors-said-would-never-work-vmp6448511514]] reports that avoiding distributors and licensing fees supported materially higher production spending with a smaller retail-price premium.
- **Governance and capital:** [[figs-trina-spear-and-heather-hasson-the-2-5-billion-scrubs-brand-that-investors-said-would-never-work-vmp6448511514]] describes staged fundraising, profitability by 2017, and a three-seat board with two founder seats after Thomas Tull's investment.
- **Stress tests and public transition:** [[figs-trina-spear-and-heather-hasson-the-2-5-billion-scrubs-brand-that-investors-said-would-never-work-vmp6448511514]] covers the Strategic Partners lawsuit, COVID-19 protective-equipment response, and May 2021 public listing.
