# Jason / 伊阿宋

## Overview

Jason is the dispossessed Greek hero who leads the Argonauts to Colchis for the [[GoldenFleece|Golden Fleece]], succeeds through [[MedeaCharacter|Medea]]'s magic and counsel, and later becomes the self-justifying husband whose betrayal drives [[Euripides]]' [[MedeaPlay|Medea]].

## Current Profile

[[605-greek-myths-jason-the-quest-for-the-golden-fleece-part-4-glt3067620137]] reconstructs the quest before the tragedy. Raised away from the throne after Pelias displaced his father Aeson, Jason fulfills the warning about a one-sandaled man and is sent to recover the fleece from Colchis. In [[ApolloniusOfRhodes|Apollonius]]' [[Argonautica]], his success is collaborative rather than autonomous: Medea protects him from fire-breathing bulls, tells him how to defeat the earth-born warriors, and helps make the fleece obtainable.

[[67-meidiya-gu-xila-qinxianglian-de-fuchou-ji-qi-xiandaixing-662400556]] follows the relationship into tragedy. Jason accepts a Corinthian royal marriage and presents it as prudent provision for the children, but the source reads secrecy, abandonment, and denial of his debt to Medea as morally central. The heroic voyage and domestic catastrophe therefore qualify one another: the adventure depends on assistance that the later hero tries to minimize.

## Key Characteristics

- Dispossessed royal heir sent on a near-impossible recovery quest.
- Leader whose status depends on a collective expedition and Medea's indispensable help.
- Hero situated inside a layered tradition rather than one fixed canonical biography.
- Husband whose polished prudential language conceals betrayal and unequal sacrifice.
- Figure through whom heroic prestige is first constructed and then deconstructed.

## Evidence

- Quest and succession: [[605-greek-myths-jason-the-quest-for-the-golden-fleece-part-4-glt3067620137]] connects Aeson, Pelias, the one-sandal oracle, Hera, and the demand for the fleece.
- Dependent success: [[605-greek-myths-jason-the-quest-for-the-golden-fleece-part-4-glt3067620137]] gives Medea's ointment and dragon-teeth strategy a decisive role in Jason's trials.
- Heroic debt: [[67-meidiya-gu-xila-qinxianglian-de-fuchou-ji-qi-xiandaixing-662400556]] makes Medea's earlier exile, magic, and violence the background to Jason's later abandonment.
- Tragic self-justification: [[67-meidiya-gu-xila-qinxianglian-de-fuchou-ji-qi-xiandaixing-662400556]] reads Jason's new marriage and family-benefit defense as rhetorically controlled but morally hollow.

## Qualifications

Jason's story survives in changing literary versions rather than one authoritative plot. The sources are podcast interpretations of Apollonius and Euripides, not a complete account of every ancient variant. The hero's motives, the political logic of the quest, and the degree to which later audiences read him as weak, prudent, or treacherous remain work- and source-specific.

## What Changed

- Added the quest narrative and made Medea's practical help central to Jason's heroic success.
- Reframed the Euripidean betrayal as the later collapse of an already dependent heroic identity.
