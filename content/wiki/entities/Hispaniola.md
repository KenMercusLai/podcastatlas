# Hispaniola

## Overview

Hispaniola is the Caribbean island where Columbus's first settlement at La Navidad was destroyed and his second-voyage colony at [[LaIsabela]] became a center of Spanish settlement, conflict, tribute, captivity, and royal investigation.

## Current Profile

[[308-columbus-death-in-the-caribbean-part-3-glt3250512968]] presents Hispaniola not as a discovered blank space but as a populated [[TainoPeople|Taíno]] homeland transformed into an unstable colonial laboratory. The deaths of the men at La Navidad followed local abuse and Spanish internal conflict; the succeeding settlement at La Isabela was badly sited and afflicted by shortages, disease, weak administration, and factional division.

As expected gold failed to appear, Columbus and his associates intensified kidnapping, captive export, tribute, and labor extraction. Imported animals also damaged local subsistence systems. The island therefore becomes an early junction of [[SpanishCaribbeanExpansion]], [[EncomiendaColonialLaborSystem|encomienda precursors]], and [[ColumbianExchange|ecological exchange]].

## Key Characteristics

- Taíno homeland rather than an empty arena for European action.
- Site of the destroyed La Navidad outpost and the failed La Isabela settlement.
- Early center of Spanish gold seeking, captive export, tribute, and compelled labor.
- Setting for resistance, colonial violence, internal Spanish rivalry, and royal intervention.
- Ecological contact zone altered by transported animals, crops, and later disease.

## Evidence

- Settlement failure: [[308-columbus-death-in-the-caribbean-part-3-glt3250512968]] links La Navidad's destruction and La Isabela's poor site to abuse, conflict, shortages, disease, and weak government.
- Coercive extraction: [[308-columbus-death-in-the-caribbean-part-3-glt3250512968]] describes mass seizure, shipment to Castile, gold and cotton tribute, and labor demands.
- Political intervention: [[308-columbus-death-in-the-caribbean-part-3-glt3250512968]] makes reports from Hispaniola the basis for royal investigation and the weakening of Columbus's monopoly.
- Ecological disruption: [[308-columbus-death-in-the-caribbean-part-3-glt3250512968]] connects horses, sheep, rats, and transferred foods to changing subsistence systems.

## Qualifications

This episode offers a Columbus-centered account of one early phase and does not provide a complete environmental, Taíno, Dominican, Haitian, or later colonial history of the island. Settlement chronology, population loss, disease prevalence, reported motives, and the causes of every conflict remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Established Hispaniola as a populated colonial contact zone rather than voyage scenery.
- Connected settlement failure to coercive extraction, ecological change, and royal oversight.

## Relationships

- [[TainoPeople]] - Indigenous peoples whose homeland and subsistence were transformed by colonization.
- [[LaIsabela]] - failed second-voyage settlement on the island.
- [[ChristopherColumbus]] - governor whose authority and extraction program unraveled there.
- [[SpanishCaribbeanExpansion]] - process for which the island supplied the earliest settlement base.
- [[EncomiendaColonialLaborSystem]] - later institution anticipated by island tribute and labor demands.
- [[ColumbianExchange]] - biological and ecological transformation visible in the island setting.
