# Hispaniola

## Overview

Hispaniola is the Caribbean island where Columbus praised Taíno society, misidentified the land as part of Asia, founded the improvised La Navidad outpost, and later pursued settlement, gold, tribute, captivity, and labor extraction.

## Current Profile

[[307-columbus-a-new-world-part-2-glt7219741357]] presents Hispaniola before sustained colonization as a populated [[TainoPeople|Taíno]] homeland of substantial villages, canoes, hammocks, tobacco, agriculture, ball courts, stonework, and possible gold. [[ChristopherColumbus]] calls it La Española, praises its people and landscape, and tries to fit it into an Asian geography. The Santa María wreck then turns the island from a visited place into a continuing European foothold through [[LaNavidad]].

[[308-columbus-death-in-the-caribbean-part-3-glt3250512968]] follows the outpost's destruction after Spanish abuse and internal conflict and the failure of the succeeding [[LaIsabela]] settlement. As expected gold fails to appear, Columbus and his associates intensify kidnapping, captive export, tribute, and labor extraction. Imported animals damage local subsistence systems, making the island an early junction of [[SpanishCaribbeanExpansion]], [[EncomiendaColonialLaborSystem|encomienda precursors]], and [[ColumbianExchange|ecological exchange]].

## Key Characteristics

- Taíno homeland with settled communities, regional exchange, and material culture before Spanish settlement.
- Island repeatedly misread through Columbus's Asian geography and commercial expectations.
- Site where shipwreck created La Navidad as an accidental first foothold.
- Site of the destroyed La Navidad outpost and failed La Isabela settlement.
- Early center of Spanish gold seeking, captive export, tribute, and compelled labor.
- Contact zone transformed by resistance, colonial violence, ecological change, and royal intervention.

## Evidence

- First-voyage society and interpretation: [[307-columbus-a-new-world-part-2-glt7219741357]] describes villages, material culture, exchange, gold reports, praise, and Columbus's identification of the island with Japan.
- Accidental foothold: [[307-columbus-a-new-world-part-2-glt7219741357]] links La Navidad to the Santa María wreck and insufficient return capacity.
- Settlement failure: [[308-columbus-death-in-the-caribbean-part-3-glt3250512968]] links La Navidad's destruction and La Isabela's poor site to abuse, conflict, shortages, disease, and weak government.
- Coercive extraction and ecological disruption: [[308-columbus-death-in-the-caribbean-part-3-glt3250512968]] describes mass seizure, shipment, tribute, labor demands, livestock, rats, and transferred foods.
- Political intervention: [[308-columbus-death-in-the-caribbean-part-3-glt3250512968]] makes reports from Hispaniola the basis for royal investigation and the weakening of Columbus's monopoly.

## Qualifications

These episodes offer a Columbus-centered account of early contact and colonization, not a complete environmental, Taíno, Dominican, Haitian, or later colonial history of the island. Asian identifications, settlement chronology, population loss, disease prevalence, reported motives, Indigenous communication, and the causes of individual conflicts remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Extended the island profile to its first-voyage Taíno society and Columbus's Asian misidentification.
- Reframed La Navidad as an accidental shipwreck response that created a continuing colonial foothold.
- Connected early praise and exchange directly to the later escalation of settlement and extraction.

## Relationships

- [[TainoPeople]] - Indigenous peoples whose homeland and subsistence were transformed by colonization.
- [[LaNavidad]] - improvised first-voyage outpost created by shipwreck and limited return capacity.
- [[LaIsabela]] - failed second-voyage settlement on the island.
- [[ChristopherColumbus]] - navigator-governor whose geographical and extractive expectations shaped the encounter.
- [[MartinPinzon]] - mariner who searched independently around the island for valuable goods.
- [[SpanishCaribbeanExpansion]] - process for which the island supplied the earliest settlement base.
- [[EncomiendaColonialLaborSystem]] - later institution anticipated by island tribute and labor demands.
- [[ColumbianExchange]] - biological and ecological transformation visible in the island setting.
