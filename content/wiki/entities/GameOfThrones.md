# 《权力的游戏》 / Game of Thrones

## Overview

Game of Thrones is the screen-facing route into the historically layered world of [[ASongOfIceAndFire|A Song of Ice and Fire]] and a major modern vehicle for dragons as political and destructive power.

## Current Profile

[[31-bing-yu-huo-zhi-ge-suiran-cong-bu-zicheng-lishi-594509848]] emphasizes that the television present sits inside a much older invented world. Westeros, Essos, Valyria, the First Men, the Andals, and the Targaryens create depth because their institutions and memories have layered histories, not because the series secretly reproduces one real medieval society.

[[461-dragons-glt6416738853]] places the series in the modern afterlife of the [[WesternDragonCulturalSynthesis|Western dragon]]. Its medieval atmosphere makes dragons feel historically fitting, while their role in Martin's world shifts emphasis from Tolkien's hoarded wealth toward political domination, apocalypse, and weapon-like destructive capacity.

## Key Characteristics

- Television adaptation and popular entry point into Martin's invented world.
- Screen present supported by deep fictional prehistory and unreliable memory.
- Recombination of medieval, classical, religious, and mythic source textures.
- Major modern representation of dragons as political and military force.
- Adaptation whose visual choices require separate evidence from the literary source.

## Evidence

- Historical depth: [[31-bing-yu-huo-zhi-ge-suiran-cong-bu-zicheng-lishi-594509848]] locates the visible conflict within accumulated conquest, migration, religion, geography, and invented record.
- Adaptation boundary: [[31-bing-yu-huo-zhi-ge-suiran-cong-bu-zicheng-lishi-594509848]] uses Qarth to show that production constraints and filming choices are weaker evidence for Martin's historical models than repeated textual patterns.
- Dragon afterlife: [[461-dragons-glt6416738853]] links the series to the modern mainstreaming of dragons and to their interpretation as concentrated political power.

## Qualifications

The sources do not provide a complete adaptation history or independent production evidence. Visual resemblance should not be treated as proof of a literary source, and medieval analogies should not be converted into one-to-one identifications. The nuclear and apocalyptic dragon readings are interpretive frames rather than exhaustive meanings.
