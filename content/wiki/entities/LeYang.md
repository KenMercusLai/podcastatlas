## Overview

乐羊 / Le Yang is a [[WeiState|魏国]] general associated with the three-year conquest of [[ZhongshanState|中山国]]. His wiki significance lies in the full command-trust cycle: recommendation, endurance under court criticism, refusal of hostage coercion, military victory, post-victory hierarchy repair, and eventual reward without continuing trust.

## Current Profile

Le Yang's Zhongshan campaign is repeatedly used to show that military competence cannot operate without political backing. [[DiHuang|翟黄]] recommends him, and [[WeiWenhou|魏文侯]] sustains the long campaign against hostile memorials. After victory, the ruler shows Le Yang those memorials to curb his pride and make the shared production of success visible.

Zhouji 05 part 2 adds a harsher trust problem inside the same campaign. When Zhongshan captures and kills Le Yang's son, the commander refuses to retreat and consumes broth made from the son's flesh before continuing the war. The episode reads this as extraordinary commitment but also as a signal of missing restraint. Wei Wenhou therefore rewards the conquest while ceasing to trust the commander, making Le Yang an example of both ruler-supported perseverance and [[ExtremeLoyaltyThreatParadox|extreme loyalty reversing into threat]].

## Key Characteristics

- Military recommendee used by Di Huang as evidence of personnel judgment.
- Commander whose Zhongshan campaign takes three years and attracts repeated opposition.
- Beneficiary of ruler trust that keeps a delayed operation politically viable.
- Officer who refuses to let the capture and killing of his son end the campaign.
- High-merit subordinate whose pride is corrected and whose extreme sacrifice later makes him frightening to the ruler.
- Lineage predecessor used to connect early Wei service with [[LeYi|乐毅]]'s later career.

## Evidence

- Referral role: [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-06-weiwenhou-xuan-shui-dang-zaixiang-ne-1-lk-6ejgmjhvukqgtlz2lowu8qx-v|Zhouji 06 part 1]] says Di Huang recommended Le Yang for the Zhongshan campaign.
- Trust during delay: [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-57-gudai-fa-dushi-haiyou-zhe-zuoyong-lj6wi61osaytowgmdp8dtkrwb-si|Zhouji 57]] uses the three-year campaign and box of hostile letters as [[GanMao|甘茂]]'s warning that court rumor can destroy a commander without ruler commitment.
- Hostage resistance and trust reversal: [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-05-guangcai-duomu-de-weiwenhou-2-lrpjtxkpfccebs-0phxiozjsv5rn|Zhouji 05 part 2]] says Le Yang continues after his son's killing and the broth episode, then receives reward but loses Wei Wenhou's trust.
- Post-victory correction: [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-05-guangcai-duomu-de-weiwenhou-3-lqbr7i9s82pi1jptizs18gidsipi|Zhouji 05 part 3]] frames the memorial box as Wei Wenhou's response to Le Yang's pride and says Le Yang returns credit upward.
- Lineage afterlife: [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-52-chenzhuang-panqin-duanqi-wan-chifan-fangxia-wan-ma-niang-ls13ixmbhflepjgpelxgu6idjqa2|Zhouji 52]] identifies Le Yi as Le Yang's descendant within Yan's talent-recruitment branch.

## Qualifications

The bounded sources use Le Yang mainly as a personnel, loyalty, and trust example rather than as a complete military biography. His son's identity, the literal and textual status of the meat-broth episode, Le Yang's inner motive, the exact number and content of memorials, the boundaries of the Lingshou reward, the full campaign chronology, and the lineage details connecting Le Yang to Le Yi remain episode-attributed. The material supports a distinction between achievement and trust; it does not prove that Wei Wenhou's later distrust was fair or caused by only one act.

## What Changed

- Added the hostage, son's killing, and broth episode to the campaign synthesis.
- Distinguished military reward from later relational trust.
- Connected the campaign's earlier rumor problem to the post-victory extreme-loyalty threat reversal.

- [[DiHuang|翟黄]] - adviser who claims credit for recommending Le Yang.
