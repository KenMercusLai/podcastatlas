# Marine Le Pen

## Overview

Marine Le Pen is the [[NationalRally]] leader whose rebranding of an older far-right inheritance, restored legal eligibility, stable polling base, second-round viability, and law-and-order positioning define the wiki's current French presidential-election branch.

## Current Profile

The historical source interprets Le Pen's expulsion of [[JeanMarieLePen|her father]], party renaming, and softer public presentation as attempts to clean up a movement linked by the hosts to Vichy, Action Française, anti-republican nationalism, and the Algerian conflict. Her protectionist and social policies complicate a simple left-right classification.

The later election sources move from restored practical eligibility to unusually stable first-round support and tested runoff leads amid [[CentristFragmentationRunoffRisk]]. The unrest sources add a conditional campaign advantage: government loss of street control can move public attention toward the law-and-order ground on which she campaigns, and [[NationalRally]] can convert riot footage into a direct electoral message. That advantage remains a forecast, not proof that violence determines the election or that she can govern effectively.

## Key Characteristics

- Leads National Rally after a deliberate effort to reduce its inherited reputational ceiling.
- Distanced the party from her father without erasing its older ideological lineage.
- Combines hard-right immigration and law-and-order politics with protectionist and social-policy appeals.
- Benefits from a stable base, opposition fragmentation, and crises that foreground state control.
- Remains subject to legal, polling, coalition, transfer, and governing-capacity uncertainty.

## Qualifications

The historical lineage is the hosts' interpretive framing, not proof that every current voter, policy, or party institution directly continues older movements. Legal decisions, polling, unrest, campaign agendas, candidate declarations, coordination, transfers, and governing capacity are time-bounded. The sources forecast electoral benefit from disorder and report campaign use of the images, but do not measure a vote effect or attribute the violence to Le Pen.

## What Changed

- Added public order as a crisis-sensitive extension of her established electoral appeal.
- Added direct campaign use of riot footage while retaining the distinction between agenda advantage and observed vote effect.
