# John the Fearless

## Overview

John the Fearless is the Burgundian duke whose pressure on the Armagnacs aided English expansion and whose murder at Montereau transformed the factional balance.

## Current Profile

John maneuvered between negotiation with [[HenryVOfEngland|Henry V]] and reconciliation with the Dauphin. His forces captured towns and Paris, indirectly giving Henry strategic room in [[Normandy]] while intensifying French civil violence.

At Montereau, John was killed by the Dauphin's servants during a controlled meeting. Regardless of whether misunderstanding or treachery caused the attack, blame attached to the Dauphin and moved John's son [[PhilipTheGood|Philip the Good]] toward England.

## Key Characteristics

- Burgundian faction leader competing with the Armagnacs for control of France.
- Conditional interlocutor whose posture reduced pressure on Henry's invasion.
- Captor of Paris through Burgundian political and military action.
- Would-be reconciler killed during negotiations with the Dauphin.
- Posthumous cause of a stronger Anglo-Burgundian alignment.

## Qualifications

The source does not settle whether John's killing resulted from a planned betrayal or a confrontation triggered by his movement toward his sword. His relationship with England was tactical and shifting rather than a simple permanent alliance.

## What Changed

- Created John as the factional broker whose death converted civil conflict into decisive English diplomatic leverage.
