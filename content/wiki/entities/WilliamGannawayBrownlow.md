# William Gannaway Brownlow

## Overview

William Gannaway “Parson” Brownlow was Tennessee's Reconstruction-era governor, presented by the source as a forceful Unionist defender of Republican government and Black voting rights against former Confederate resistance and the emerging first Ku Klux Klan.

## Current Profile

The bounded source places Brownlow at the center of Tennessee's postwar legitimacy conflict. He supported Black political participation, threatened to use federal troops, pressed for a loyal state militia, and eventually gained stronger authority against secret organizations disturbing the peace. Those measures aimed to close a protection gap in which local officials often tolerated racial terror, but they also intensified ex-Confederate claims that Reconstruction government was coercive.

Brownlow therefore illustrates a central Reconstruction dilemma: rights without enforceable protection were exposed to organized violence, while efforts to create that protection became propaganda and recruitment material for the movement threatening those rights.

## Key Characteristics

- Tennessee Unionist and Republican governor during early Reconstruction.
- Advocate of Black voting rights and resistance to restored ex-Confederate control.
- Supporter of federal or state coercive capacity against organized political violence.
- Political target whose rhetoric and militia plans were used by former Confederates to justify resistance.
- Example of the difficulty of protecting citizenship when local courts and law enforcement are unreliable.
