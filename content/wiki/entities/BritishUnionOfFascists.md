# British Union of Fascists

## Overview

The British Union of Fascists was [[OswaldMosley|Oswald Mosley]]'s Blackshirt organization, represented in the episode as a weakened national movement that rebuilt localized support through economic grievance, antisemitic East End campaigning, and later ultra-appeasement.

## Current Profile

The BUF entered decline after its stewards' violence at Olympia cost press support and Mosley's forecast of democratic-capitalist collapse failed. It then attacked big business and chain stores, appealed to small traders threatened by retail consolidation, and increasingly fused those complaints with antisemitism. In late 1935 and 1936 it competed with communists and other political groups through street meetings in poor East London neighborhoods.

The [[BattleOfCableStreet|Cable Street]] blockade stopped its planned march but did not immediately destroy the organization. The episode reports continued meetings, increased estimated London membership, and anti-Jewish violence in Stepney soon afterward. By the late 1930s the BUF presented itself as the anti-war alternative to both major parties, opposed war over Poland, and defended further appeasement of Hitler. Wartime detention of Mosley and hundreds of fascists under Regulation 18B then broke its momentum.

## Key Characteristics

- Uniformed Blackshirt movement organized around Mosley's leadership.
- Movement weakened by public violence and the resilience of British democratic politics.
- Local organizer of small-trader resentment and explicit antisemitic scapegoating.
- Provocative street movement whose march produced the Cable Street blockade and subsequent backlash.
- Ultra-appeasement movement that used peace and anti-establishment rhetoric to seek late-1930s growth.
- Organization whose momentum ended under wartime emergency detention rather than at Cable Street alone.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to one episode and does not provide a complete organizational history, membership series, financing account, regional comparison, or study of rank-and-file motives. The reported rise from roughly 6,000 to 30,000 members, Special Branch estimates, and causal interpretation of campaign appeals remain source-scoped. Peace advocacy alone is not fascism; here it belongs to a wider organization defined by authoritarianism, uniforms, violence, antisemitism, and accommodation of Nazi aggression.
