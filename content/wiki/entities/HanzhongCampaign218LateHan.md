## Overview

The [[HanzhongCampaign218LateHan|218–219 CE Hanzhong campaign]] is presented as a multi-stage contest in which a Fa Zheng-backed opportunity probe survives early local defeats through Yi-region mobilization, breaks the Cao-side field position at [[DingjunMountainBattleLateHan|Dingjun Mountain]], then shifts into a patient confrontation that ends with Cao Cao's withdrawal and Liu Bei's control of Hanzhong.

## Current Profile

The campaign opens when [[FaZheng|法正]] reads [[CaoCao|曹操]]'s departure from Hanzhong and failure to attack Yi as an opportunity, and [[LiuBei|刘备]] authorizes a probe. Separate deployments by [[ZhangFei|张飞]], [[MaChao|马超]], and [[WuLanLateHan|吴兰]] let [[CaoHong|曹洪]] concentrate against the weaker Wu Lan wing. [[CaoXiuLateHan|曹休]] then interprets Zhang Fei's public rear-cutting threat as a feint.

Liu Bei's renewed offensive against commanders including [[XiahouYuan|夏侯渊]], [[ZhangHeLateHan|张郃]], and [[XuHuangCaoGeneral|徐晃]] initially stalls. Road-cutting and fortress attacks fail, making troops, grain, roads, and rear administration decisive; [[YangHongLateHan|杨洪]] persuades [[ZhugeLiang|诸葛亮]] to mobilize Yi-region support.

Hanji 1163 supplies the campaign hinge. Liu Bei shifts from the Yangping standoff to Dingjun Mountain, pressures Zhang He's sector, and uses fire and repeated attack to induce Xiahou Yuan to divide his force. When Xiahou Yuan moves with a small group to fight the fire and repair defenses, [[FaZheng|法正]] identifies the opening and [[HuangZhong|黄忠]] attacks, killing the commander. [[DuXiLateHan|杜袭]] and [[GuoHuaiLateHan|郭淮]] then support Zhang He as emergency commander and keep the defeated army from immediately collapsing.

Hanji 1164 supplies the concluding phase. After Xiahou Yuan's death, Cao Cao advances cautiously, while Liu Bei declines decisive battle and lets distance, rear pressure, time, and morale work against the relief army. [[ZhaoYun|赵云]]'s open-camp bluff reportedly turns Cao-side caution into a disorderly withdrawal after a grain-convoy operation by [[HuangZhong|黄忠]]. Prolonged stalemate then erodes Cao-side morale, and Cao Cao abandons Hanzhong for Chang'an. [[ZhangJiCaoOfficialLateHan|张既]] subsequently proposes eastward civilian relocation to reduce the supplies available along a future route toward Guanzhong.

## Key Characteristics

- Multi-stage campaign moving from opportunity probe through setbacks, reinforcement, attrition, and withdrawal.
- Early Cao-side exploitation of separated Liu-side forces and a weaker wing.
- Liu-side tactical failures answered by rear-area mobilization rather than abandonment.
- Tactical dislocation at Dingjun Mountain turns pressure on one sector into force division and command exposure.
- Emergency succession preserves a reduced Cao-side defense after the senior commander's death.
- Shift from direct assault to patient denial of a decisive battle.
- Final territorial change followed by population-and-supply measures intended to protect Guanzhong.

## Qualifications

The four sources are structured popular-history summaries rather than transcripts or critical military histories. Dates, route names, troop totals, precise command relations, the Dingjun fire and repair sequence, Xiahou Yuan's death, emergency appointment, Cao Cao's motives, Liu Bei's psychological assessment, the Zhao Yun maneuver, casualties, morale change, and the “鸡肋” anecdote require comparison with the underlying chronicles. The episode's claim that taking Hanzhong basically formed the tripartite order compresses later political and military developments. The reported voluntary character and strategic effect of Zhang Ji's relocation proposal remain unverified.

## What Changed

- Filled the central campaign gap between early failed assaults and Cao Cao's later relief attempt.
- Added force redistribution and commander exposure as the Dingjun Mountain breakthrough mechanism.
- Added emergency succession as the reason a command loss did not become immediate army collapse.
