# Dorothy Thompson

## Overview

Dorothy Thompson is presented as an American foreign correspondent whose suffrage work, European reporting, Jewish family connection, and relationships among outsiders informed an unusually clear early diagnosis of fascism.

## Current Profile

In [[hitler-s-olympics-part-3-mustache-to-mustache-hitler-s-olympics-part-3-mustache-to-mustache|Hitler’s Olympics, Part 3]], Thompson is the interpretive counterweight to [[CharlesHitchcockSherrill]]. Where his social position encouraged deference to successful men, her work among suffragists, artists, radicals, and European outsiders made exclusion and demagoguery more visible.

She read [[AdolfHitler]] as angry, insecure, vicious, and potentially hysterical rather than as an impressive leader. The episode also applies her later “Mr. B” personality sketch to Sherrill: a conventional elite figure may reject a marginal extremist movement yet adapt readily once it becomes successful enough to carry status and authority.

## Key Characteristics

- Foreign correspondent with sustained experience studying European fascism.
- Former suffrage organizer whose public formation occurred outside established male elites.
- Observer attentive to oppressed groups, outsiders, and the moral meaning of power.
- Early critic who interpreted Hitler as a dangerous demagogue rather than a leader deserving deference.
- Analyst of how class codes and attraction to success can make respectable people adaptable to authoritarianism.
