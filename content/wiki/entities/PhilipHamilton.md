# Philip Hamilton

## Overview

Philip Hamilton appears in the episode as [[AlexanderHamilton|Alexander Hamilton]]'s son, whose fatal 1801 duel foreshadows and morally burdens the [[HamiltonBurrDuel|Hamilton-Burr duel]].

## Current Profile

The episode uses Philip less as a full biographical subject than as a tragic precedent. Hamilton had advised Philip to throw away his shot, and Philip died after a duel near the same Weehawken ground later used by Hamilton and Burr. That parallel makes Hamilton's 1804 decision look more irrational and shows how [[HonorDuelCulture|honor duel culture]] could repeat harm across a family.

## Key Characteristics

- Son of Alexander and [[ElizaHamilton|Eliza Hamilton]].
- Died in an 1801 duel before Hamilton's own fatal encounter.
- Associated with Hamilton's advice to throw away a shot.
- Memory precedent that intensifies the episode's critique of honor pressure.

## Evidence

- Earlier duel death: [[685-hamilton-duel-to-the-death-part-3-glt9231431046]] says Philip died in a duel in 1801.
- Advice pattern: [[685-hamilton-duel-to-the-death-part-3-glt9231431046]] says Hamilton had advised Philip to throw away his shot, then later stated a similar intention for his own duel.
- Weehawken echo: [[685-hamilton-duel-to-the-death-part-3-glt9231431046]] says Hamilton and Burr met near the place where Philip had been killed.
