# Southampton Plot

## Overview

The Southampton Plot is the conspiracy exposed immediately before [[HenryVOfEngland|Henry V's]] 1415 fleet sailed, demonstrating that the invasion of France unfolded under continuing domestic legitimacy risk.

## Current Profile

The source says Edmund Mortimer revealed a conspiracy involving Richard of Conisburgh, Earl of Cambridge, Henry Scrope, and others. Henry responded with trials and executions while the expedition was assembling at Southampton.

Its importance in the episode is less the plot's operational viability than its timing. The conspiracy made visible the continuing alternatives to Lancastrian rule and raised the domestic cost of failure in France.

## Key Characteristics

- Conspiracy discovered during final preparations for the 1415 invasion.
- Plot exposed by Edmund Mortimer, whose dynastic position mattered to its design.
- Crisis answered by rapid prosecution and execution.
- Evidence that Henry's authority was formidable but not politically risk-free.

## Evidence

- Discovery and participants: [[487-hundred-years-war-henry-v-s-invasion-of-france-part-1-glt7554933021]] says Mortimer informed Henry of the conspiracy involving Cambridge and Scrope.
- Royal response: [[487-hundred-years-war-henry-v-s-invasion-of-france-part-1-glt7554933021]] places executions immediately before embarkation.
- Political meaning: [[487-hundred-years-war-henry-v-s-invasion-of-france-part-1-glt7554933021]] uses the plot to show why military failure could destabilize the Lancastrian regime.

## Qualifications

The source does not fully reconstruct the plot's legal record, every participant's motive, or its likelihood of success. Its strongest evidential role here is as a sign of domestic vulnerability, not proof that Henry's overthrow was imminent.

## What Changed

- Added the conspiracy as the immediate domestic-risk boundary around Henry's invasion.
