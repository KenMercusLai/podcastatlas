## Overview

庄参 / Zhuang Can is the Western Han official who refuses [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s order to lead two thousand people into [[Nanyue|南越]]. His reported distinction between a small diplomatic mission and an adequately sized coercive force makes him the cautious counterpoint to [[HanQianqiuWesternHan|韩千秋]]'s volunteer confidence.

## Current Profile

Hanji 352-1 says Wudi chooses Zhuang Can after learning that Lü Jia resists the royal party and the earlier envoys have failed to act. Zhuang Can argues that friendly persuasion needs only a few people, whereas coercion cannot be accomplished with two thousand. He refuses the assignment and is dismissed rather than executed.

The next volunteer proposal makes his judgment consequential. Han Qianqiu claims three hundred brave men can kill Lü Jia because the Nanyue king and queen dowager will respond from inside. A two-thousand-person mission is sent and ultimately fails, but this sequence does not prove that Zhuang Can knew the exact outcome or that every larger force would have succeeded.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han official selected for a two-thousand-person Nanyue mission.
- Analyst who separates diplomatic staffing from coercive force sufficiency.
- Refuser dismissed by Wudi after declining the assignment.
- Cautious counterpoint to Han Qianqiu's small-force confidence.

## Qualifications

This profile rests on one short popular-history summary. It does not supply Zhuang Can's office, earlier or later career, full memorial, private motive, or whether refusal reflected military expertise, self-preservation, or both. The later defeat makes his caution look prescient, but hindsight does not establish that his complete strategic assessment was correct.
