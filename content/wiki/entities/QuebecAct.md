# Quebec Act

## Overview

The Quebec Act was a British imperial settlement that protected Catholic religious practice in Quebec and expanded the province's boundaries.

## Current Profile

The episode treats the act as successful imperial accommodation in Quebec but inflammatory policy in the thirteen colonies. Colonists folded it into their list of Intolerable Acts, where anti-Catholicism, land anxiety, and older English fears of arbitrary rule shaped its reception. The contrast matters: the same measure that helped keep Quebec loyal made British government look more threatening to many Protestant colonists.

## Key Characteristics

- Protected Catholic religious practice in Quebec.
- Expanded Quebec's territorial boundaries southward.
- Helped secure Quebec's loyalty to Britain.
- Provoked anti-Catholic and constitutional fears in the thirteen colonies.
- Demonstrates uneven imperial effects across neighboring colonies.
