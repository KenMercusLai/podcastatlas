# Kate (AHA Creator)

## Overview
Kate is the [[AHACreator|AHA Creator]] founder interviewed in [[146-dui-mai-liang-he-pu-huo-shuo-bye-bye-ai-ruhe-qudong-chuhai-pinpai-zengzhang-feng-tou-quan-1-146-1]] and [[no-226-ai-yueqiang-chuangyezhe-yueyao-huidao-yonghu-xianchang-gkwrijionp-cayktqgs6szvt]]. The sources identify her by first name and use her operating experience to explain why overseas creator marketing is difficult to scale manually and why AI became useful after large models improved.

## Current Profile
The current profile is narrow and source-bounded. Kate says she was born in 2002, went to the United States for school around age 16, dropped out of university around age 18, and joined an early startup that first made a website-building product for designers and later an AI prompt-to-website product. She says she began working on overseas growth around 2021, discovered that creator marketing could outperform other channels for user registration cost, and then encountered the heavy manual work of creator discovery, authenticity judgment, email outreach, negotiation, payment, and multilingual follow-up.

After GPT-era large models matured, she framed these tasks as suitable for AI because they require reading messy creator context and maintaining many semi-structured conversations. Her strategic position is pragmatic: AI should reduce execution burden, but humans still define strategy, briefs, budgets, creator profiles, trust relationships, creative direction, and final review standards. She also argues that application-layer companies need business-model and workflow depth because marketplace supply, demand, contracts, disputes, and pricing norms do not appear automatically when models get smarter.

## Key Characteristics
- Overseas growth operator background: Kate grounds the company story in first-hand channel testing and manual creator-marketing work beginning around 2021.
- AI adoption thesis: she argues that large models are useful because creator marketing requires context understanding, communication, and matching rather than only structured database filtering.
- Human-boundary clarity: she repeatedly keeps strategy, problem definition, customer trust, and final standards with people rather than describing creator marketing as fully automated ads.
- Marketplace-builder perspective: she treats AHA as a two-sided platform that must build creator supply, brand demand, workflow rules, and delivery trust.
- Operational transparency stance: she emphasizes small-budget testing, unused-balance refunds, fake-creator refunds, and anti-fraud optimization as ways to reduce customer distrust.
- User-site product thesis: the 三五环 title and conversation frame her as arguing that stronger AI makes founder field exposure and concrete workflow understanding more important, not less.
