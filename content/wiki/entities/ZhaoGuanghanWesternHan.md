## Overview

赵广汉 / Zhao Guanghan enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-426-lianghan-diyi-qingguan-zhaoguanghan-weihe-bushi-haoguan-lsu5ijajbkz2nkxvpc85kf-tsd3f|Hanji 426]] as the Western Han official used to break the simple equation between "clean official" and "good official." The source preserves his real administrative force: as Yingchuan governor, he attacks corrupt officials, local strongmen, and powerful families, and gains public affection through hard anti-corruption action. It then follows his collapse after private anger and office power merge in the Su Xian, Rong Xu, and Wei Xiang cases.

## Current Profile

Zhao's profile is intentionally double-sided. He is not introduced as a mere villain. His early reputation rests on clean conduct, courage against haoqiang networks, and a reporting system that lets ordinary people expose corrupt or violent actors. That is why the later case matters: the episode treats his fall as a warning about the insufficiency of reputation, not a denial that he once governed effectively.

The turning point is a private-retainer dispute. After Zhao's retainer is expelled from the market for illegal private wine selling and suspects Su Xian, Zhao has Chang'an commandant Shi Yu arrest Su Xian under a military-absence pretext. When Su's father appeals, [[EmperorXuanOfHan|汉宣帝]] investigates; Shi Yu is executed, and Zhao admits fault but is only demoted because of amnesty. Zhao then uses his information network to identify Rong Xu inside the chancellor's office and kills him on suspicion. Once [[WeiXiangWesternHan|魏相]] investigates, Zhao attempts to turn an old household death case against Wei Xiang, even calling Wei's wife through legal process. The episode reads that move as retaliatory counter-investigation rather than neutral enforcement.

Zhao's public support becomes part of the danger. Tens of thousands of Chang'an residents and minor officials reportedly plead and weep for him, but the source says this makes his death more certain: a local official with mass affection can look like someone able to mobilize public sentiment against imperial judgment. Zhao therefore stands beside [[HanYanshouWesternHan|韩延寿]] and [[YangYunWesternHan|杨恽]] in the broader Xuan-era field of capable or admired officials executed under politically charged conditions, while remaining more culpable than a simple loyal-victim story would allow.

## Key Characteristics

- Clean and forceful Western Han local official whose early reputation rests on anti-corruption and anti-haoqiang action.
- Builder or user of anonymous-reporting infrastructure that strengthens official information gathering.
- Example of a clean official whose private anger turns legal authority into retaliation.
- Escalates from wrongful arrest to lethal abuse when suspicion against Rong Xu becomes execution.
- Politically dangerous because public affection and petitioning make his punishment look like a ruler-versus-popular-official confrontation.
- Used by the episode to separate integrity, competence, and popular support from lawful and humane office-holding.

## Qualifications

This page is source-scoped to Hanji 426's retelling of the Zhao Guanghan case. It does not reconstruct Zhao's full career, exact offices, the complete original legal record, or the full biographies of Su Xian, Rong Xu, Shi Yu, and Zhao's retainer. The source's judgment is not that clean government is worthless; it is that clean reputation cannot excuse abuse of law, private retaliation, or killing on suspicion. Zhao is therefore a more culpable Xuan-era execution case than pages such as [[HanYanshouWesternHan|韩延寿]] or [[GaiKuanraoWesternHan|盖宽饶]], even though all remain connected to the broader problem of admired officials dying under [[EmperorXuanOfHan|汉宣帝]].
