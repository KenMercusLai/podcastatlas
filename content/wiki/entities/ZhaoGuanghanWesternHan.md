## Overview

赵广汉 / Zhao Guanghan enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-426-lianghan-diyi-qingguan-zhaoguanghan-weihe-bushi-haoguan-lsu5ijajbkz2nkxvpc85kf-tsd3f|Hanji 426]] as the Western Han official used to break the simple equation between "clean official" and "good official." [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-415-xihan-zhaoguanghan-ruhe-daji-xiangba-cunba-heie-shili-ltnvaopr2hv0jx-v0oii3l9rjaur|Hanji 415]] now backfills the admired early side of that profile: Zhao's [[YingchuanCommandery|颍川郡]] anti-haoqiang campaign, anonymous reporting, elite-splitting tactics, subordinate management, and Jingzhao information network.

The combined profile preserves his real administrative force while making the boundary problem sharper. Zhao can suppress local strongmen and frighten potential criminals out of Chang'an, but the same intelligence access, coercive reach, and public affection later become dangerous when private anger merges with official power in the Su Xian, Rong Xu, and Wei Xiang cases.

## Current Profile

Zhao's profile is intentionally double-sided. He is not introduced as a mere villain. Hanji 415 shows why people admired him: in Yingchuan he uses sealed anonymous-reporting tubes, rumor pressure among powerful families, and targeted raids on the Yuan and Chu households to break a network of intermarried strongmen and colluding officials. After promotion to Jingzhao Yin, the episode credits him with rewarding subordinates, knowing their abilities, and maintaining a fast information network that can interrupt a kidnapping plot or track an official's small unreported greeting.

That early reputation rests on clean conduct, courage against haoqiang networks, and a reporting system that lets ordinary people expose corrupt or violent actors. That is why the later case matters: Hanji 426 treats his fall as a warning about the insufficiency of reputation, not a denial that he once governed effectively.

The turning point is a private-retainer dispute. After Zhao's retainer is expelled from the market for illegal private wine selling and suspects Su Xian, Zhao has Chang'an commandant Shi Yu arrest Su Xian under a military-absence pretext. When Su's father appeals, [[EmperorXuanOfHan|汉宣帝]] investigates; Shi Yu is executed, and Zhao admits fault but is only demoted because of amnesty. Zhao then uses his information network to identify Rong Xu inside the chancellor's office and kills him on suspicion. Once [[WeiXiangWesternHan|魏相]] investigates, Zhao attempts to turn an old household death case against Wei Xiang, even calling Wei's wife through legal process. The episode reads that move as retaliatory counter-investigation rather than neutral enforcement.

Zhao's public support becomes part of the danger. Tens of thousands of Chang'an residents and minor officials reportedly plead and weep for him, but the source says this makes his death more certain: a local official with mass affection can look like someone able to mobilize public sentiment against imperial judgment. Zhao therefore stands beside [[HanYanshouWesternHan|韩延寿]] and [[YangYunWesternHan|杨恽]] in the broader Xuan-era field of capable or admired officials executed under politically charged conditions, while remaining more culpable than a simple loyal-victim story would allow.

## Key Characteristics

- Clean and forceful Western Han local official whose early reputation rests on anti-corruption, anti-haoqiang action, and public-order success.
- Builder or user of anonymous-reporting and informant infrastructure that strengthens official information gathering.
- Capable team manager whose rewards, personnel knowledge, and case preparation improve local enforcement.
- Example of a clean official whose private anger turns legal authority into retaliation.
- Escalates from wrongful arrest to lethal abuse when suspicion against Rong Xu becomes execution.
- Politically dangerous because public affection and petitioning make his punishment look like a ruler-versus-popular-official confrontation.
- Used by the episode to separate integrity, competence, and popular support from lawful and humane office-holding.

## Qualifications

This page is source-scoped to Hanji 415 and Hanji 426's retelling of the Zhao Guanghan case. It does not reconstruct Zhao's full career, exact offices, the complete original legal record, or the full biographies of Su Xian, Rong Xu, Shi Yu, Zhao's retainer, the Yuan and Chu Yingchuan families, or the small officials in the anecdotes. The sources' combined judgment is not that clean government or strong enforcement is worthless; it is that clean reputation, real enforcement success, and public affection cannot excuse abuse of law, private retaliation, or killing on suspicion. Zhao is therefore a more culpable Xuan-era execution case than pages such as [[HanYanshouWesternHan|韩延寿]] or [[GaiKuanraoWesternHan|盖宽饶]], even though all remain connected to the broader problem of admired officials dying under [[EmperorXuanOfHan|汉宣帝]].

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 415's earlier favorable layer: Yingchuan anti-haoqiang action, anonymous reporting, subordinate management, and Jingzhao information-network success.
- Refined Zhao's profile so later abuse appears as the corruption of real governing capacity rather than proof he never had capacity.
