# Abhijit Banerjee

## Overview
Abhijit Banerjee is a development economist represented here through Nobel context and a biographical account of his shift from grand theory toward randomized field experiments.

## Current Profile
The Planet Money source places Banerjee alongside [[MichaelKremer]] and [[EstherDuflo]] in the mainstreaming of [[RandomizedControlledTrials]] within [[DevelopmentEconomics]]. The [[HiddenBrain]] source makes his methodological posture more specific: childhood contact with capable children living in severe poverty made one-dimensional explanations unconvincing, while repeated experimental surprises turned “How do you know that's true?” into a research program.

His account is neither theory-free nor uncritical of experiments. Teacher, vaccination, cash-transfer, and bed-net results are used for [[InterventionMechanismLearning]], but Banerjee also warns that researchers can frame the wrong possibilities and that Nobel prestige can invite opinions beyond the evidence. His profile therefore joins practical experimentation with [[ExpertUncertaintyCommunication]] and self-skepticism.

## Key Characteristics
- Challenges ideological and intuitive poverty stories by asking for concrete mechanisms and evidence.
- Uses randomized field experiments to test specific interventions rather than relying only on broad models.
- Treats failed interventions as evidence that can revise theory, not merely as discarded policies.
- Emphasizes implementation detail through a “plumbing” orientation toward how systems actually work.
- Acknowledges framing limits, uncertainty, and the temptation created by expert authority.

## Evidence
- Research-movement context - [[piles-of-cash-and-a-town-of-solutions-in-kenya-nigeria-summer-school]] names Banerjee among the economists recognized for randomized approaches to poverty alleviation.
- Biographical skepticism - [[putting-our-assumptions-to-the-test]] connects his Kolkata childhood, political debates, and dissatisfaction with simplistic poverty accounts to his evidence-first posture.
- Experimental practice - [[putting-our-assumptions-to-the-test]] uses teacher, vaccination, cash-transfer, and bed-net cases to show his movement from testing effects to revising mechanisms.
- Authority boundary - [[putting-our-assumptions-to-the-test]] records his concern that experimental framing can be incomplete and Nobel deference can encourage overstatement.

## Qualifications
The direct profile comes from a structured public-radio episode summary rather than Banerjee's publications, trial protocols, datasets, or a complete intellectual biography. The causal role of childhood experience, the interpretation of individual studies, the reported effect sizes, and the wording of methodological reflections remain source-scoped. Nobel recognition marks influence but does not independently validate every claim or intervention associated with him.
