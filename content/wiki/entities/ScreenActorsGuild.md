# Screen Actors Guild

## Overview

The Screen Actors Guild is the Hollywood performers' union through which [[RonaldReagan|Ronald Reagan]] developed labor-political experience before electoral office.

## Current Profile

[[310-ronald-reagan-and-the-american-dream-part-1-glt3254036306]] presents the guild as a contested institution between actors, studios, rival labor organizations, anti-communist pressure, strikes, and congressional investigation. Reagan served as a film-set representative from the late 1930s and became guild president in March 1947. The episode depicts him as an effective advocate whom many actors trusted, while also recording his cooperation with HUAC and the FBI, refusal to defend the Hollywood Ten, and effort to distinguish union protection from communist-led action.

## Key Characteristics

- A performers' labor organization operating inside the studio system rather than outside Hollywood production.
- A training ground in negotiation, representation, public argument, and coalition management for Reagan.
- An institution pressured simultaneously by studio power, strikes, rival unions, anti-communism, and congressional scrutiny.
- A setting in which legitimate labor advocacy and political loyalty policing became entangled.

## Qualifications

This profile is limited to one biographical podcast episode and primarily reflects the guild's role in Reagan's development, not a complete institutional history. The episode distinguishes declining to name names in public testimony from broader cooperation with anti-communist investigation; neither fact erases the other. Its account of threats, communist influence, strike leadership, actor opinion, and Reagan's private calculations remains source-scoped.
