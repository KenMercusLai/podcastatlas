# Stanley Kubrick / 斯坦利·库布里克

## Overview
Stanley Kubrick enters the wiki through [[dianying-li-de-ai-qingren-juying-he-ducai-zhe-gkwriuen9djgazhkswsh-d-b]], where [[DianyingJubian|电影巨辩]] treats his long-unfinished [[AIArtificialIntelligenceFilm|《人工智能》]] project as a bridge between science-fiction cinema, Pinocchio myth, artificial love, and human self-destruction.

## Current Profile
The source presents Kubrick less as a cold stylist than as the long-term architect of a dark modern fairy tale. He acquired the source story in 1970, worked for decades with writers including Ian Watson, and left behind key elements often credited to [[StevenSpielberg|斯皮尔伯格]]'s sentimentality: the robot teddy bear, the family opening, the Flesh Fair, the Blue Fairy search, and the final one-day return of Monica.

Kubrick's role is therefore conceptual as much as biographical. The episode links him to questions about whether programmed love can become real, whether humanity behaves like an infant seeking endless gratification, and whether technological creation repeats the [[FrankensteinComplexAI|Frankenstein complex]] under climate-collapse and consumer-desire pressure.

## Key Characteristics
- He functions here as the originating creative force behind the modern-Pinocchio structure of [[AIArtificialIntelligenceFilm|《人工智能》]].
- His version of the project is associated with dark fairy-tale logic rather than simple warmth.
- The source treats his AI interests as philosophical and psychological: love, imprinting, consciousness transfer, self-destruction, and creation responsibility.
- His collaboration with [[StevenSpielberg|斯皮尔伯格]] is framed as a collision of styles that makes the film both tender and cruel.
- His unrealized projects, including Napoleon and The Aryan Papers, contextualize [[AIArtificialIntelligenceFilm|《人工智能》]] as part of a broader pattern of ambitious unfinished work.

## Evidence
- Project origin: [[dianying-li-de-ai-qingren-juying-he-ducai-zhe-gkwriuen9djgazhkswsh-d-b]] says Kubrick bought the story rights in 1970 and kept developing the project until his death in 1999.
- Modern Pinocchio: [[dianying-li-de-ai-qingren-juying-he-ducai-zhe-gkwriuen9djgazhkswsh-d-b]] presents his project as an expansion of the robot-child story into a modern Pinocchio myth.
- Dark-sentimental mixture: [[dianying-li-de-ai-qingren-juying-he-ducai-zhe-gkwriuen9djgazhkswsh-d-b]] argues that elements commonly read as Spielbergian warmth were already in Kubrick's treatment, complicating the cold-Kubrick/warm-Spielberg binary.
- Philosophical AI influence: [[dianying-li-de-ai-qingren-juying-he-ducai-zhe-gkwriuen9djgazhkswsh-d-b]] connects the project to mechanistic emotion, artistic failure, and consciousness-transfer ideas from AI thinkers Kubrick consulted or read.
