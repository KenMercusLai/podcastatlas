# Spain

## Overview

Spain appears across the bounded sources as an imperial and confessional power, a colonial state in [[Morocco]], a modern European border and policy setting, and a sports, labor, health-law, and climate comparison case.

## Current Profile

The early-modern sources present a global Catholic monarchy whose silver, Portuguese succession, Low Countries war, diplomacy, and oceanic monopoly made it formidable but vulnerable to English privateering and religious conflict. The [[RifWar]] source adds a later empire seeking recovery after 1898: mining interests, royal backing, crusading language, and Africanist prestige drew Spain into a war its conscript army was institutionally unprepared to fight.

Annual deepened political crisis and helped strengthen military authoritarianism. Spain rebuilt around the [[ArmyOfAfrica]], the Foreign Legion, Moroccan regulares, chemical weapons, and combined operations, creating an officer world that later mattered in the [[SpanishCivilWar]]. Modern sources then show a democratic Spain facing distinct questions around Ceuta migration and Moroccan diplomacy, assisted death, football development, paid leave, wildfire risk, and World Cup hosting.

## Key Characteristics

- Early-modern Catholic and maritime empire whose power depended on silver, dynastic reach, continental war, and vulnerable oceanic systems.
- Colonial power in northern Morocco whose post-imperial ambitions produced military catastrophe, escalation, and authoritarian political effects.
- Institutional home of the Army of Africa and officers whose Moroccan careers shaped later domestic rebellion.
- Modern EU border state managing Ceuta, migration, Western Sahara diplomacy, and relations with Morocco and Algeria.
- Dense football-development and lower-tier club environment that can offer sporting depth without eliminating ownership risk.
- Comparative setting for assisted-death ethics, paid leave, World Cup hosting, and wildfire exposure.

## Qualifications

These branches span centuries and do not support one continuous theory of Spain. The Rif episode's casualty estimates, weapon details, reported dialogue, and causal line from Morocco to the Civil War remain source-scoped; colonial military inheritance is a contributing condition, not a complete explanation. Modern migration, labor, health, sports, and wildfire evidence comes from separate sources and should not be merged into the imperial branch.
