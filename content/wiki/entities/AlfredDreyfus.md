# Alfred Dreyfus

## Overview

Alfred Dreyfus appears in the wiki as the Jewish French artillery officer from Alsace whose wrongful treason conviction, public degradation, imprisonment, pardon, and eventual exoneration became the human center of the [[DreyfusAffair|Dreyfus Affair]].

## Current Profile

[[12-days-death-of-edward-the-confessor-and-the-dreyfus-affair-glt1738062149]] presents Dreyfus as an officer selected as a plausible suspect after French intelligence recovered the bordereau from the German embassy. His Alsatian origin, reserved manner, limited social network, and above all the [[RacialAntisemitism|antisemitism]] of 1890s [[France]] made him vulnerable within a military institution already anxious about Germany after the Franco-Prussian War.

After conviction, he was publicly stripped of rank and had his sword broken at the École Militaire on 5 January 1895. The episode emphasizes his insistence on innocence and composure during the ceremony, then follows his removal to isolated imprisonment on Devil's Island.

Evidence pointing to Ferdinand Walsin Esterhazy did not immediately free him because senior officers protected the army's honor and used forged evidence. Dreyfus was eventually retried and again convicted before accepting a presidential pardon reluctantly; the pardon secured freedom without conceding innocence. He was fully exonerated in 1906 and later served in the French army during the [[FirstWorldWar|First World War]].

## Key Characteristics

- Jewish French artillery officer made vulnerable by antisemitism and post-1871 suspicion of Germany.
- Wrongly convicted defendant whose personal reserve was treated as suspicious rather than probative.
- Publicly degraded officer who continued to assert innocence under ritual humiliation.
- Isolated prisoner on Devil's Island whose case became larger than his individual biography.
- Reluctant recipient of a pardon that restored liberty before the state restored innocence.
- Exonerated officer who later returned to military service during the First World War.
