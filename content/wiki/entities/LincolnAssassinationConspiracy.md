# Lincoln Assassination Conspiracy

## Overview

The Lincoln assassination conspiracy was the April 1865 multi-target plot in which [[JohnWilkesBooth|John Wilkes Booth]] killed [[AbrahamLincoln|Abraham Lincoln]], Lewis Powell attacked William Seward, and George Atzerodt failed to attack [[AndrewJohnson|Andrew Johnson]].

## Current Profile

[[590-the-assassination-of-abraham-lincoln-death-at-the-theatre-part-1-glt2286421954]] gives the plot a prehistory in abduction. Booth first developed a scheme to seize Lincoln near his summer cottage and exchange him for Confederate prisoners, with John Surratt and Mary Surratt's boarding house connecting him to a support network. The plan never materialized, and its exact reach remains unclear. Confederate collapse, Lee's surrender, and Lincoln's April 11 endorsement of limited Black suffrage frame Booth's shift from kidnapping toward murder.

[[591-the-assassination-of-abraham-lincoln-manhunt-for-the-killer-part-2-glt7618197031]] treats the resulting conspiracy as a plan to decapitate the United States government at the moment of Confederate defeat. Booth succeeded at Ford's Theatre; Powell entered Seward's home under a medicine-delivery pretext and wounded several people without killing Seward; Atzerodt went to Johnson's hotel but abandoned his assignment.

The plot extended into an escape and assistance network. David Herold joined Booth, Mary Surratt's tavern held weapons and binoculars, Samuel Mudd treated Booth's fractured leg, Thomas Jones supplied and transported the fugitives, and other Confederate sympathizers helped them move toward Virginia. That support establishes a wider conspiracy without proving the episode's more expansive possibility that Jefferson Davis or the Confederate high command authorized it.

The manhunt ended at Richard Garrett's farm. Herold surrendered, Booth remained inside the burning tobacco barn, and [[BostonCorbett|Boston Corbett]] shot him through the wall. Atzerodt, Powell, Herold, and Mary Surratt were later convicted and hanged; the episode preserves questions about what individual helpers knew and how far formal responsibility extended.

## Key Characteristics

- Plot that developed from an unrealized presidential abduction scheme into a coordinated attempt to kill the president, vice president, and secretary of state.
- Uneven execution in which one target died, one survived a violent attack, and one assailant abandoned his assignment.
- Escape route sustained by weapons caches, medical help, guides, food, boats, and Confederate-sympathizer contacts.
- Manhunt shaped by Booth's immediate identification, military controls, large rewards, and movement toward Virginia.
- Network whose existence is well defined while its outer command boundary remains unresolved.

## Evidence

### Multi-target design

- [[590-the-assassination-of-abraham-lincoln-death-at-the-theatre-part-1-glt2286421954]] traces the kidnapping scheme, Surratt connection, political-racial escalation, and Booth's April 14 contact with Atzerodt, Herold, and Powell.
- [[591-the-assassination-of-abraham-lincoln-manhunt-for-the-killer-part-2-glt7618197031]] links Booth, Powell, and Atzerodt to simultaneous assignments against Lincoln, Seward, and Johnson.

### Flight and assistance

- [[591-the-assassination-of-abraham-lincoln-manhunt-for-the-killer-part-2-glt7618197031]] follows Booth and Herold through Surratt's tavern, Mudd's treatment, Jones's pine-thicket support, the Potomac crossing, Confederate soldiers, and the Garrett farm.

### Capture and punishment

- [[591-the-assassination-of-abraham-lincoln-manhunt-for-the-killer-part-2-glt7618197031]] describes Herold's surrender, Corbett's shot, Booth's death, and the later hanging of Atzerodt, Powell, Herold, and Surratt.

## Qualifications

The sources are structured popular-history summaries rather than transcripts, trial records, or a complete documentary reconstruction. They support an earlier kidnapping scheme, a real coordinated murder plot, and a wider assistance network, but not a settled account of the abduction plan's full membership or proof that Davis or Confederate high command ordered either phase. The moment and mechanism of escalation, intentions and prior knowledge of individual helpers, legal fairness of the trials, precise route details, dialogue, and responsibility at the network's edge require broader evidence.

## What Changed

- Added the unrealized kidnapping scheme and distinguished its unclear scope from the later documented multi-target murder plot.
- Connected escalation to Confederate collapse and Lincoln's limited Black-suffrage speech without treating that chronology as proof of senior Confederate authorization.
