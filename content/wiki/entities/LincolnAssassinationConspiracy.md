# Lincoln Assassination Conspiracy

## Overview

The Lincoln assassination conspiracy was the April 1865 multi-target plot in which [[JohnWilkesBooth|John Wilkes Booth]] killed [[AbrahamLincoln|Abraham Lincoln]], Lewis Powell attacked William Seward, and George Atzerodt failed to attack [[AndrewJohnson|Andrew Johnson]].

## Current Profile

[[591-the-assassination-of-abraham-lincoln-manhunt-for-the-killer-part-2-glt7618197031]] treats the conspiracy as a plan to decapitate the United States government at the moment of Confederate defeat. Booth succeeded at Ford's Theatre; Powell entered Seward's home under a medicine-delivery pretext and wounded several people without killing Seward; Atzerodt went to Johnson's hotel but abandoned his assignment.

The plot extended into an escape and assistance network. David Herold joined Booth, Mary Surratt's tavern held weapons and binoculars, Samuel Mudd treated Booth's fractured leg, Thomas Jones supplied and transported the fugitives, and other Confederate sympathizers helped them move toward Virginia. That support establishes a wider conspiracy without proving the episode's more expansive possibility that Jefferson Davis or the Confederate high command authorized it.

The manhunt ended at Richard Garrett's farm. Herold surrendered, Booth remained inside the burning tobacco barn, and [[BostonCorbett|Boston Corbett]] shot him through the wall. Atzerodt, Powell, Herold, and Mary Surratt were later convicted and hanged; the episode preserves questions about what individual helpers knew and how far formal responsibility extended.

## Key Characteristics

- Coordinated attempt to kill the president, vice president, and secretary of state.
- Uneven execution in which one target died, one survived a violent attack, and one assailant abandoned his assignment.
- Escape route sustained by weapons caches, medical help, guides, food, boats, and Confederate-sympathizer contacts.
- Manhunt shaped by Booth's immediate identification, military controls, large rewards, and movement toward Virginia.
- Network whose existence is well defined while its outer command boundary remains unresolved.

## Evidence

### Multi-target design

- [[591-the-assassination-of-abraham-lincoln-manhunt-for-the-killer-part-2-glt7618197031]] links Booth, Powell, and Atzerodt to simultaneous assignments against Lincoln, Seward, and Johnson.

### Flight and assistance

- [[591-the-assassination-of-abraham-lincoln-manhunt-for-the-killer-part-2-glt7618197031]] follows Booth and Herold through Surratt's tavern, Mudd's treatment, Jones's pine-thicket support, the Potomac crossing, Confederate soldiers, and the Garrett farm.

### Capture and punishment

- [[591-the-assassination-of-abraham-lincoln-manhunt-for-the-killer-part-2-glt7618197031]] describes Herold's surrender, Corbett's shot, Booth's death, and the later hanging of Atzerodt, Powell, Herold, and Surratt.

## Qualifications

The source is a structured popular-history summary rather than a transcript, trial record, or complete documentary reconstruction. It supports a real coordinated plot and wider assistance network, but not a settled claim that Davis or Confederate high command ordered it. The intentions and prior knowledge of individual helpers, legal fairness of the trials, precise route details, dialogue, and responsibility at the network's edge require broader evidence.

## What Changed

- Created a bounded event profile separating the established multi-target plot and escape network from unproved senior Confederate authorization.
