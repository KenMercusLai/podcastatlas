# Vans

Vans is the California-born footwear brand analyzed in [[vans-kuangwei-fengguang-buzai-jingdian-fanbuxie-weishenme-maibudongle-1006742982]] as a former skate and street-culture symbol now facing slower demand. The episode traces the brand to a 1966 front-shop/back-factory shoe store opened by the Van Doren brothers, then to 1970s skateboarders who valued the shoes' durable canvas uppers and waffle soles.

The source frames Vans as both a classic [[SubcultureLedMarketing]] success and a current [[ClassicCanvasShoeDecline]] case. Era, Old Skool, Sk8-Hi, and the checkerboard Slip-On became recognizable products, but the episode argues that current buyers increasingly compare them against more comfortable running, outdoor, clog, and sandal-like alternatives.

[[VFCorporation|VF Corporation / 威富集团]] acquired Vans in 2004. The source says Vans later became an important growth engine, with 2019 fiscal-year global revenue growth of 24%, but then saw annual revenue shrink by more than 40% over three years. It attributes the downturn to [[ComfortLedFootwearDemand|comfort-led footwear demand]], [[SubcultureMainstreamingDilution]], and [[HeritageProductInnovationDebt]].

## Key Claims
- Vans' skate credibility came from a real use case: board feel, durability, grip, and South California youth scenes.
- The brand's strongest old assets are also current constraints when consumers demand cushioning, arch support, and everyday comfort.
- Heavy dependence on several 1970s-born models makes product renewal harder than simple nostalgia marketing suggests.
- [[SunChoe]]'s turnaround work, including product, store, marketing, and [[VansWarpedTour|Vans Warped Tour]] adjustments, is presented as an early attempt to reconnect product innovation with culture.
