# Old Sarum

## Overview

Old Sarum is a former settlement and parliamentary borough used in the source as the clearest emblem of unreformed British representation.

## Current Profile

The episode says Old Sarum retained the right to return two MPs from 1295 even after the settlement had declined to almost no resident population. In 1802, five electors reportedly met in a cornfield booth beneath a tree, while the landowner who controlled the site also controlled the electoral interest.

Its importance is comparative rather than merely eccentric. Old Sarum's two seats alongside Manchester's lack of representation shows how inherited territorial privilege could survive demographic and economic transformation, turning parliamentary representation into a saleable property-like asset.

## Key Characteristics

- It retained two parliamentary seats despite having virtually no inhabitants.
- Its tiny electorate made control through landownership unusually direct.
- The 1802 election scene became a visual symbol of a borough surviving after its social basis disappeared.
- Its representation contrasted sharply with the absence of MPs for industrial Manchester.
- It serves as the episode's strongest case of institutional persistence outlasting practical justification.

## Evidence

- Demographic-representational mismatch: [[463-mad-elections-part-1-glt4220133865]] says Old Sarum kept two MPs from 1295 despite later depopulation.
- Controlled electoral interest: [[463-mad-elections-part-1-glt4220133865]] describes five electors and ownership by the Earl of Caledon in the 1802 election.
- Comparative inequality: [[463-mad-elections-part-1-glt4220133865]] contrasts Old Sarum's seats with unrepresented Manchester.

## Qualifications

The purchase price and modern monetary equivalent are reported from the podcast and not independently audited here. Old Sarum is an unusually vivid case, so it should illustrate rather than substitute for the wider variety of county, borough, corporation, scot-and-lot, and potwalloper franchises.
