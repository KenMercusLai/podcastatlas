# Princesse de Lamballe

## Overview

The Princesse de Lamballe was a Savoyard princess, widowed courtier, and loyal friend of [[MarieAntoinette|Marie Antoinette]] who was killed at La Force during the [[SeptemberMassacres]].

## Current Profile

The episode places Lamballe at the intersection of personal loyalty, royalist hatred, and gendered political pornography. Her documented death was brutal, but the most sensational stories about her remains drew on an older pamphlet culture that had already sexualized her relationship with the queen; distinguishing evidence from propaganda is therefore part of understanding both her killing and its memory.

## Key Characteristics

- She remained closely loyal to Marie Antoinette after the monarchy's collapse.
- Revolutionary pamphlets portrayed the two women through sexualized and lesbian court-conspiracy claims.
- A La Force tribunal condemned her after she refused to swear hatred of the king and queen.
- Her body was decapitated and later recovered for private burial by family servants.
- Her death became a vehicle for counter-revolutionary atrocity stories whose factual reliability varies sharply.

### Tribunal and death

- [[544-the-french-revolution-the-september-massacres-part-1-glt8301042051]] places her before the La Force tribunal on 3 September and treats her decapitated body's delivery to a section as firmly known.

### Atrocity narrative

- [[544-the-french-revolution-the-september-massacres-part-1-glt8301042051]] distinguishes the plausible but uncertain pike procession from later unsupported stories of sexual mutilation and cannibalism.

## Qualifications

- The episode is not a full biography of Lamballe or a complete reconstruction of her relationship with Marie Antoinette.
- Refusal of the oath, the procession route, the display of her head, and details of her remains depend on uneven contemporary and later accounts.
- Rejecting sensational embellishment does not minimize the established fact that she was killed and decapitated.

## What Changed

- The wiki now separates Lamballe's documented death from the sexualized atrocity mythology built around it.
