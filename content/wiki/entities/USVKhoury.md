# U.S. v. Khoury

## Overview

U.S. v. Khoury was the federal prosecution of [[AymanKhoury]] arising from a payment to Georgetown tennis coach [[GordonErnst]] connected to [[CatherineKhoury]]'s admission.

## Current Profile

The prosecution initially appeared strong through [[TimothyDonovan]]'s account of a $200,000 agreement and $180,000 cash transfer. [[BrendaSmithGeorgetown]]'s emails then weakened Georgetown's claim that fundraising and admissions were separate, before Catherine's testimony and contemporaneous messages supplied a direct alternative explanation for why she did not play tennis. The jury acquitted Ayman Khoury on every count.

## Key Characteristics

- Criminal case joining college admission, athletic recruitment, private payment, and disputed intent.
- Government theory relied in part on immunized intermediary [[TimothyDonovan]]'s account of an agreed payment and planned injury excuse.
- Defense theory used Catherine's family rupture and messages to challenge the alleged plan.
- Georgetown fundraising emails and donor-capacity research complicated the institutional narrative, although the later Salesforce document was not admitted.
- Jury acquitted Khoury on every count without resolving every ethical question about the payment.

## Qualifications

The bounded sources are defense-centered structured episode summaries, not the indictment, transcript, jury instructions, exhibits, or appellate record. They disagree over Catherine's ability and original intent, and Part 1 uses Amon/Katherine where Part 2 uses Ayman/Catherine. An acquittal means the charged offenses were not proved beyond a reasonable doubt; it does not independently determine every ethical or institutional question surrounding the admitted payment.

## What Changed

- Made the conflict over Catherine's ability and intent explicit.
- Preserved the separation among institutional inconsistency, reasonable doubt, and ethical approval.
