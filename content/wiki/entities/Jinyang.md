# 晋阳 / Jinyang

## Overview
Jinyang is a northern strategic center whose wiki profile spans its deliberate preparation as Zhao's pre-Qin refuge and its later use as a Han campaign and frontier-administration base.

## Current Profile
In the earliest source, [[ZhaoJianzi|Zhao Jianzi]] chooses to have [[YinDuo|Yin Duo]] preserve Jinyang as a fallback instead of maximizing extraction. Reduced burdens and repaired defenses make the city the instructed destination for [[ZhaoXiangzi|Zhao Wuxu]] if Jin politics collapse, setting up its role in the [[BattleOfJinyang|Battle of Jinyang]]. Centuries later, [[HanXin|Han Xin]] takes Jinyang after defeating Dai forces and helps convert the northern campaign into Taiyuan commandery. Early Han then uses Jinyang as [[HanWangXin|Han Wang Xin]]'s initial frontier capital, although he asks to move closer to the Xiongnu-facing edge at [[Mayi|Mayi]].

## Key Characteristics
- Deliberately preserved as a refuge through lower extraction and stronger defenses.
- Links social resilience with fortress value in the Zhao crisis.
- Serves as an administrative hinge between conquest and Taiyuan commandery formation.
- Functions as a frontier capital whose location can still be judged too distant from the active border.

## Evidence
- Prepared refuge: Zhouji 02 has Zhao Jianzi choose refuge over extraction, Yin Duo reduce burdens and strengthen defenses, and Zhao Wuxu receive explicit instructions to seek Jinyang in crisis. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-02-jiangyu-baizhi-bi-gufuzhi-lglk2_eu1uvegdac8sj6bfuwv1dw|Zhouji 02]]
- Conquest and administration: Hanji 155-2 says Han Xin takes Jinyang after defeating Dai forces and establishes Taiyuan commandery. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-155-2-zhuan-gei-ni-piqi-da-de-pengyou-weiwang-panhan-jing-shi-liubang-ai-maren-ls5nvunmhakvefd2oxn4zdyoob4l|Hanji 155-2]]
- Frontier-capital role: Hanji 174 makes Jinyang Han Wang Xin's initial capital before his requested move to Mayi. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-174-liubang-weihe-dafeng-zijide-chouren-1-lnzzvpieqtgatmvgogz3msyhs60u|Hanji 174]]

## Qualifications
The sources come from one podcast series across different historical periods. Exact administrative measures, dialogue, and causal claims about public loyalty remain source-scoped. The Jinyang siege itself is synthesized on [[BattleOfJinyang]] from the direct battle episode; this place page focuses on the base's preparation and later strategic reuse.
