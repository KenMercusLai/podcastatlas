# Kurt von Schuschnigg

## Overview

Kurt von Schuschnigg was Austria's Catholic-authoritarian chancellor during the final crisis before the [[Anschluss]], when [[AdolfHitler]] used negotiation, Austrian Nazi pressure, and invasion threats to destroy Austrian independence.

## Current Profile

Schuschnigg succeeded [[EngelbertDollfuss]] after the failed 1934 Nazi coup. In February 1938 he entered the Berghof expecting a summit but faced an orchestrated ultimatum requiring concessions to Austrian Nazis, the appointment of [[ArthurSeyssInquart]], and closer economic alignment with Germany.

His March referendum was an attempt to create a public mandate for a free and independent Austria, but its loaded wording, short timetable, and age restriction also made it a managed political gamble. Hitler escalated rapidly; without external protection and under threat of invasion, Schuschnigg cancelled the vote and resigned. Nazi Germany imprisoned him after the takeover, though he survived the war.

## Key Characteristics

- Catholic-authoritarian defender of Austrian state independence rather than a democratic liberal.
- Successor to Dollfuss after Austrian Nazis murdered the previous chancellor.
- Target of Hitler's personal intimidation and escalating demands.
- Political gambler who tried to use a managed referendum against Nazi pressure.
- Isolated leader forced to resign under invasion threat.

## Qualifications

Opposing Hitler did not make Schuschnigg's regime democratic: the source identifies it as Catholic and authoritarian. The referendum's likely result, Hitler's reported dialogue, the legality of cabinet decisions, and the precise alternatives available under military and diplomatic pressure remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Established Schuschnigg as both an authoritarian ruler and a defender of Austrian independence.
- Framed the referendum as resistance constrained by manipulation and severe miscalculation.
