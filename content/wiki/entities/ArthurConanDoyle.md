# Arthur Conan Doyle / 柯南道尔

## Overview

Arthur Conan Doyle was a doctor, writer, sportsman, and public campaigner whose [[SherlockHolmes|Sherlock Holmes / 福尔摩斯]] stories established his durable fame despite his preference for historical fiction. The wiki uses him across detective method, literary afterlife, justice advocacy, spiritualist belief, and the suspect history of [[PiltdownMan|Piltdown Man]].

## Current Profile

Doyle's profile is built around productive contradictions. His Edinburgh medical training under [[JosephBell]] helped him turn diagnostic observation into a detective method, and his fiction makes rational explanation culturally vivid, especially when [[TheHoundOfTheBaskervilles|The Hound of the Baskervilles / 《巴斯克维尔的猎犬》]] turns supernatural atmosphere back toward human causes. Yet his defense of spiritualism, the Cottingley fairy photographs, seances, and messages from the dead makes him a central case in [[OccultPseudoscienceLiteraryModernity]].

The Holmes episode broadens him beyond authorship. Doyle wanted greater recognition for historical novels, took sport seriously, entered Liberal Unionist politics, and used his public standing to challenge the wrongful convictions of George Edalji and Oscar Slater. Those justice campaigns resemble a real-world extension of detective work, while also showing that Doyle's evidential attention could be forceful in one domain and credulous in another. Later writers and readers detach Holmes from Doyle and reuse the character through illustration, fandom, global pastiche, and adaptation.

The Piltdown episode adds a narrow historical qualification. Doyle lived near the site, knew people in the Piltdown circle, and had written *The Lost World*, so he became a colorful suspect. The source rejects him as unlikely and treats the theory as an association-driven alternative to the much stronger case against [[CharlesDawson|Charles Dawson]].

## Key Characteristics

- His medical underemployment helped produce Sherlock Holmes, the character that eclipsed his desired identity as a historical novelist.
- [[JosephBell]]'s diagnostic observation supplied an acknowledged influence that Doyle transformed into fictional detection.
- He joined gothic atmosphere to rational solution and became central to the modern detective-fiction lineage and [[SherlockHolmesVictorianCulturalMyth]].
- His spiritualism and support for dubious supernatural evidence complicate the rational aura associated with Holmes.
- His Edalji and Slater campaigns used literary fame in public challenges to miscarriage of justice.
- Holmes became a collaborative and reusable cultural figure shaped by serial publishing, [[SidneyPaget|illustration]], translation, parody, and pastiche.
- His Piltdown connection is biographically suggestive but evidentially weak and should not be treated as guilt.

## Qualifications

The literary-gossip source is explicitly anecdotal, and the Holmes episode is a conversational survey rather than a definitive biography. Doyle's spiritualism does not erase the evidential structure of his fiction, and Holmes's rationality does not prove Doyle personally applied identical standards in every domain. The Piltdown theory rests on association and thematic coincidence rather than the means-opportunity pattern attributed to Dawson. Sporting details, political motives, the exact scope of Bell's influence, and claims about Doyle's justice work require specialist corroboration beyond these notes.

## What Changed

- Added Bell's medical influence, Doyle's sporting and political identity, and his Edalji and Slater justice campaigns.
- Sharpened the contrast between domain-specific evidential advocacy and spiritualist credulity without reducing Doyle to either side.
- Extended Holmes's afterlife through mass publishing, visual co-production, fandom, and Victorian cultural myth.
