# 大食代 / Food Republic

Food Republic, known in Chinese as 大食代, is the central food-court operator in [[vol-270-da-shi-dai-liu-zai-le-ta-de-shi-dai-1006063835]]. The episode describes it as a commercialized version of [[HawkerCentre|Singapore hawker-centre]] logic: take a large mall area, subdivide it into food stalls, provide shared seating and operating services, and use unified cashiering to collect a sales commission.

The source's main claim is that Food Republic was useful because early Chinese shopping centers did not yet have strong restaurant leasing capability. 大食代 could lease basement or top-floor space, solve contracts, sanitation, fire, licences, and cash control for dozens of small vendors, and give malls a stable tenant while small restaurants gained cheaper mall access.

[[kafeidou-chuantong-meishi-guangchang-jielian-bidian-dashidaimen-yudao-naxie-fazhan-zuai-1007530222]] adds a shorter listener-question version of the same transition. It repeats the lease-expiry explanation for the Beijing closure but treats the larger issue as market-fit loss: chain restaurants can now enter malls directly, food-delivery commissions make stall economics tighter, and customers can satisfy cheap-workday-meal demand through more branded options. The source also names [[SkylandFoodCourt|食通天 Skyland]] and [[CityMart]] as successor operators, while keeping enterprise and education canteen cooperation as an exploratory Food Republic direction.

The same source treats the model as period-specific. As chain restaurants, landlord-operated food streets, supermarkets, and platform-led foodservice spaces improved, Food Republic's [[FoodCourtSecondLandlord|second-landlord]] value fell. The episode frames 大食代 Elite and enterprise or education canteen cooperation as attempts to reuse the company's centralized foodservice capabilities outside the old mall-food-court peak.
