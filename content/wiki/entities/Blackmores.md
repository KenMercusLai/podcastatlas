# Blackmores / 澳佳宝

## Overview
Blackmores is the Australian supplement brand the source uses to show how a naturopathic practice became a mainstream consumer business, and one of the two origin-country brands Chinese consumers met through daigou and bonded warehouses.

## Current Profile
The source describes the business as starting from naturopathic consultation rather than manufacturing, with one of Australia's earliest health-food stores attached to it, and only turning into a supplement brand under the second generation of the owning family. That transition is presented as a translation problem: the founders' naturopathy was treated as too mystical and non-standard for mainstream acceptance, so the company worked with research institutions and universities to give familiar habits - the source's example is a daily spoon of fish oil - a standardised product form and a more scientific-looking story.

Distribution stayed anchored to the naturopathy and health-food store network even as the product line modernised, which the hosts compare to the modernisation of Chinese medicine: a self-contained traditional system merged with industrial production and development. On the China side, the source puts Blackmores' own estimate of average supplement tariffs around 20%, higher than the 3-10% range it attributes to Australian official statistics, and says the free trade agreement scheduled that tariff to fall to zero within four years. The episode adds that many Chinese consumers first encountered Blackmores and Swisse during the bonded-warehouse era rather than through Australian pharmacy visits.

## Key Characteristics
- Naturopathic clinical origin rather than pharmaceutical or retail origin.
- Family-business transition from practitioner consultation to a branded product company.
- Deliberate mainstreaming of alternative medicine through research collaborations and standardised dosage forms.
- Retail base in naturopathy and health-food stores, which persisted after the brand mainstreamed.
- Used in the source as the clearest bridge between Australian self-care culture and industrial supplement production.
- Tariff exposure treated as material to the China channel, with the episode's average-rate figure attributed to the company itself.

## Qualifications
The company's research collaborations, tariff estimate, and historical sequence are relayed by a podcast and are not independently verified here, and the transcript renders the brand as "澳家宝 / Black Moss". The source gives no ownership, financial, or product-level evidence, and it does not discuss any regulatory action against the brand.
