# Achaemenid Empire / Persian Empire

## Overview

The Achaemenid Empire / Persian Empire appears in the wiki through the Marathon episode as the imperial power that sends a punitive expedition against Athens and Eretria after the Ionian revolt.

## Current Profile

The source presents Persia as a large empire using expeditionary force, naval mobility, cavalry, local intelligence, and exiled political clients to punish rebellious or hostile cities. [[DariusI]] wants revenge on [[Athens]] because Athens supported the Ionian revolt; Eretria is destroyed and its inhabitants enslaved before the fleet turns to Marathon.

The empire is not treated as merely a cartoon of despotism. Its commanders choose Marathon because the site gives them a beach, a plain for cavalry, and strategic flexibility. The episode also stresses that the Persian view of Marathon may not have matched the Greek one: the expedition failed to take Athens, but it returned with Eretrian captives, and a great empire could understand the battle as a peripheral setback rather than a civilizational defeat.

## Key Characteristics

- The empire acts through punitive expedition, naval transport, cavalry capability, and hostage or exile politics.
- Persian strategy at Marathon depends on terrain, cavalry room, and possible internal betrayal in Athens.
- [[HippiasAthenianTyrant]] represents Persia's use of an exiled local ruler as a political instrument.
- The episode treats Persian defeat at Marathon as militarily real but not necessarily empire-defining from the Persian viewpoint.
- The absence of comparable Persian narrative evidence makes the empire's inner view harder to reconstruct than the Greek memory.

## Evidence

- Revenge motive: [[669-greece-vs-persia-the-battle-of-marathon-part-2-glt2696648260]] says Darius sought revenge for Athenian support of the Ionian revolt.
- Punitive sequence: [[669-greece-vs-persia-the-battle-of-marathon-part-2-glt2696648260]] describes Eretria's destruction and enslavement before the fleet moves to Marathon.
- Strategic landing: [[669-greece-vs-persia-the-battle-of-marathon-part-2-glt2696648260]] says Marathon offered a beach, plain, and cavalry space.
- Political warfare: [[669-greece-vs-persia-the-battle-of-marathon-part-2-glt2696648260]] presents Hippias and possible betrayal as part of the Persian threat.
- Imperial perspective: [[669-greece-vs-persia-the-battle-of-marathon-part-2-glt2696648260]] notes that Marathon may have seemed peripheral to Persia and that no Persian account comparable to Herodotus survives.

## Qualifications

This profile is limited to the Marathon Part 2 source. It does not synthesize the whole Achaemenid state, court, economy, religion, administration, or later Xerxes invasion. The Persian perspective is inferred from the episode's framing because the source emphasizes the absence of a comparable Persian narrative tradition.
