# Bronislaw Malinowski / 马林诺夫斯基

Bronislaw Malinowski appears in [[35-tianzhende-renleixuejia-zuo-tianye-hui-shangyin-de-603418624]] as the classic fieldwork ancestor whose diary complicated anthropology's heroic self-image. The episode says his diary upset later readers because it recorded anger, loneliness, dislike of informants, sexual frustration, and ordinary human resentment rather than a purified scientific posture.

The source uses Malinowski to frame [[NigelBarley|Nigel Barley]] and [[TheInnocentAnthropologist|《天真的人类学家》]]. If traditional anthropology turned fieldwork into a rite of professional legitimacy, Malinowski's diary and Barley's comic narrative reveal the hidden human underside of that rite.

## Key Claims
- Fieldwork prestige can become a disciplinary mythology around courage, authenticity, and qualification.
- Diaries and comic ethnography expose the gap between clean monographs and the bodily, emotional, and social labor that produced them.
- Malinowski's role in this source is methodological: he helps define [[FieldworkRomanceDeflation]] and [[EthnographicEverydayFriction]].
