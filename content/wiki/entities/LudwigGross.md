# Ludwig Gross

## Overview
Ludwig Gross is the Polish-born cancer researcher presented as a core member of the episode's “obscure virus club” for his work on virus-caused mouse leukemia.

## Current Profile
At the Veterans Administration in the Bronx, Gross pursued evidence that mouse leukemia could be caused by a virus transmitted from mother to offspring. The episode treats the work as a bridge between [[PeytonRous]]'s chicken-sarcoma result and the mouse leukemia virus in which [[DavidBaltimore]] later detected reverse transcriptase. His long-delayed recognition supports the episode's account of research whose significance can emerge well after initial rejection.

## Key Characteristics
- Investigated viral causation and transmission in mouse leukemia.
- Continued despite explanations based on genetics, contamination, or poor technique.
- Led cancer research at the Veterans Administration in the Bronx.
- Is portrayed as motivated by scientific work rather than wealth or recognition.
- Received the Lasker Award in 1974, late in his career.

## Evidence
- Mouse-leukemia experiments: [[the-obscure-virus-club-the-obscure-virus-club]] reports Gross's evidence for a maternally transmitted leukemia virus.
- Scientific reception: [[the-obscure-virus-club-the-obscure-virus-club]] records contamination, genetics, and technique as rival explanations raised by critics.
- Downstream mechanism: [[the-obscure-virus-club-the-obscure-virus-club]] says Baltimore later found reverse transcriptase in a mouse leukemia virus connected to Gross's research line.
- Recognition and character: [[the-obscure-virus-club-the-obscure-virus-club]] reports his 1974 Lasker Award and describes his devotion to research.

## Qualifications
