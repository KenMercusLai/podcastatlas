# ICANN

## Overview
ICANN is the internet-governance organization presented in the source as setting and administering the process for adding new generic top-level domains.

## Current Profile
The episode makes ICANN the gatekeeper for a scarce, root-level namespace. Its process must evaluate applicants with enough financial and technical capacity to operate registries, resolve competing applications, recognize some community claims, and manage the boundary between public registration, private brand use, and speculative control. That makes domain expansion a governance allocation problem as well as a technical one.

## Key Characteristics
- Coordinates the application process for new generic top-level domains.
- Requires substantial application funding and registry-operating capability.
- Uses evaluation, priority rules, and auctions when multiple applicants seek the same string.
- Can give community applicants a distinct route within a process otherwise favoring well-resourced organizations.
- Shapes who may control commercially, culturally, or politically meaningful internet identifiers.

## Qualifications
The source is a short journalistic overview rather than a full account of ICANN's multistakeholder structure, applicant guidebook, objection procedures, evaluation criteria, contractual enforcement, or accountability disputes. Application does not imply approval, delegation, public availability, or commercial success.

## What Changed
- Established ICANN as the wiki's canonical organization for generic top-level-domain allocation.
- Added the distinction among application, auction victory, delegation, public launch, and commercial success.
