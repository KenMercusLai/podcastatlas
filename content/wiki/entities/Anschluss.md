# Anschluss

## Overview

The Anschluss was [[NaziGermany|Nazi Germany]]'s March 1938 coercion, invasion, and complete absorption of [[Austria]], followed immediately by institutional takeover, plunder, mass repression, and anti-Jewish violence.

## Current Profile

The episode presents the Anschluss as a sequence rather than a single border crossing. [[AdolfHitler]] first benefited from weakened military restraint and [[BenitoMussolini]]'s retreat from defending Austria, then intimidated [[KurtVonSchuschnigg]] into concessions. Schuschnigg's proposed independence referendum provoked escalating ultimatums, his resignation, and the installation of [[ArthurSeyssInquart]] before German troops crossed on 12 March.

The conquest combined coercion with visible Austrian enthusiasm. Large crowds welcomed Hitler, especially in Linz and Vienna, and local Nazis had already seized buildings and attacked Jewish property. Yet organized attendance, intimidation, political isolation, and the erasure of Austrian choice prevent official plebiscite totals or crowd scenes from functioning as free measures of unanimous consent.

Annexation removed Austria from the map, subordinated its army and institutions, and transferred financial reserves, companies, infrastructure, art, and raw materials to Germany. The rapid detention and persecution that followed made foreign expansion inseparable from racial and political repression.

## Key Characteristics

- Coercive annexation prepared through diplomatic isolation, ultimatum, and threatened invasion.
- Event in which genuine public enthusiasm coexisted with intimidation and the destruction of political choice.
- Complete absorption rather than an equal union of two German-speaking states.
- Immediate institutional, fiscal, economic, military, and cultural plunder.
- Trigger for mass arrest, anti-Jewish street violence, dispossession, and forced-emigration administration.
- Strategic success that increased Hitler's prestige and appetite for further expansion.

- Coercive sequence: [[407-the-nazis-in-power-the-conquest-of-austria-part-4-glt5380362144]] follows the Berghof demands, referendum crisis, ultimatums, resignation, Seyss-Inquart appointment, and invasion.
- Mixed reception: [[407-the-nazis-in-power-the-conquest-of-austria-part-4-glt5380362144]] records large cheering crowds while preserving organized attendance, fear, opportunism, and motive uncertainty.
- Absorption and plunder: [[407-the-nazis-in-power-the-conquest-of-austria-part-4-glt5380362144]] describes the loss of Austrian statehood and German control of its army, banks, reserves, infrastructure, art, and resources.
- Persecutory aftermath: [[407-the-nazis-in-power-the-conquest-of-austria-part-4-glt5380362144]] connects annexation to mass detention, Jewish-business seizure, public humiliation, flight pressure, and Eichmann's Vienna apparatus.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to one narrative podcast episode. It does not treat the official 99-percent plebiscite as a free opinion poll, nor does it infer uniform resistance from Germany's coercion. Private motives, popular-attitude proportions, crowd organization, reported dialogue, numerical estimates, and the causal weight of individual military and diplomatic actors remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Established the event as a coercion-to-annexation sequence rather than only a military entry.
- Joined strategic conquest to institutional plunder and immediate racial persecution.
- Preserved the coexistence of Austrian victimization and Austrian participation.
