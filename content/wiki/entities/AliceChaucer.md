# Alice Chaucer

## Overview

Alice Chaucer was Geoffrey Chaucer's granddaughter, presented in the episode as an unusually successful late-medieval heiress and dynastic actor.

## Current Profile

Twice widowed by age 24, Alice accumulated inherited wealth and married William de la Pole, later Duke of Suffolk. The episode says she retained his lands after his execution and that their descendants approached the succession closely enough for John de la Pole to be named Richard III's heir in 1484.

Her role in the episode is interpretive: the distance between her grandfather's wine-trade ancestry and her descendants' proximity to the crown illustrates the scale, but not the typicality, of [[PostPlagueSocialMobility]].

## Key Characteristics

- Heiress whose early widowhood brought substantial property.
- Wife of William de la Pole and later Duchess of Suffolk.
- Durable property holder after her husband's execution.
- Dynastic bridge between the Chaucer family and late-medieval royal politics.
- Exceptional rather than representative example of female and family mobility.

## Qualifications

This profile is based on a brief podcast segment rather than a full study of Alice's property, patronage, marriages, or political conduct. Her extraordinary ascent should not be generalized to medieval women or post-plague society as a whole.
