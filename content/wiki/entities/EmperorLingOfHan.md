# 汉灵帝 / Emperor Ling of Han

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-912-mimi-daodi-gai-bugai-shuo-chukou-lhsgipxwc17pahc-r6jl7xdyrs2a|Hanji 912]] adds the confidential-remonstrance prelude before Cai Yong's Hanji 913 punishment. Ling asks senior officials about palace omens, receives [[YangCiLateHan|杨赐]] and [[CaiYong|蔡邕]]'s diagnoses of harem, eunuch, [[HongduMenxue|鸿都门学]], personnel, and extravagance disorder, and then fails to protect Cai Yong's confidential memorial from [[CaoJieLateHan|曹节]]'s access. The episode makes Ling's feedback problem one of channel protection: he can hear corrective speech, but cannot keep the speaker safe enough for truth to remain governable.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-913-kan-dong-saiweng-shima-wu-tou-rensheng-fuhuo-lkfbrwtgsjma85bzugqonzk3hy4g|Hanji 913]] adds the 178 CE court layer before the Hongdu and office-sale material. Ling commutes [[CaiYong|蔡邕]]'s death sentence after [[LyuQiangLateHan|吕强]] intercedes, but still exiles him without pardon; he then lets [[WangFuLateHan|王甫]]'s wugu accusation destroy [[EmpressSongLateHan|宋皇后]] and ignores [[LuZhiLateHan|卢植]]'s memorial on party prisoners, burial, tenure stability, and appointment standards. The episode uses this mix of mercy, palace accusation, personal indulgence, omens, and ignored correction to deepen Ling's [[CourtFeedbackCollapse|君臣反馈失灵]] profile.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-914-xinren-de-ren-weihe-zong-shang-ni-zui-shen-lvwit-rzrkscn5a3h6wblntxppso|Hanji 914]] adds the 178 CE prelude before the Hanji 915 prosecution layer. [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]] favors [[HongduMenxue|鸿都门学]] as a literary and artistic channel against the士人 world he resents after the [[ChenFanLateHan|陈蕃]] and [[DouWuLateHan|窦武]] anti-eunuch crisis, then ignores [[YangQiuLateHan|阳球]]'s memorial attacking that channel as shallow, favor-driven, and appointment-distorting.

The same source adds Ling's earliest office-sale layer in this wiki. He opens the Xidi sale-of-office system, prices posts by rank and commandery value, allows delayed double payment, and puts the proceeds into his West Garden private store. That ties him to [[OfficeSaleLegitimacyDamage|买官名节损耗]], [[ImperialPrivateTreasuryExtraction|帝王私库进奉盘剥]], and [[CourtOfficeCapture|朝廷开府任官失序]] before the later 181-185 private-treasury, fee, and bought-situ branches. The episode also shows his desire for comparison-validation when [[YangQiLateHan|杨琦]] answers the Emperor Huan question too directly and Ling mocks him through [[YangZhenLateHan|杨震]] family reputation.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-915-shishang-meiyou-zhenzheng-de-kouwu-zhehua-dui-ma-lgxcodjxnaozvyegxqurgzji5gus|Hanji 915]] adds the immediate 179 CE prosecution layer before the eunuch-regrouping branch. After earthquake, sikong replacement, and eclipse notices, [[YangQiuLateHan|阳球]] accuses [[WangFuLateHan|王甫]], [[DuanJiongLateHan|段熲]], and Wang Fu's family network; Ling authorizes the arrests that destroy Wang Fu and push Duan to suicide.

The source makes Ling's role double-edged. He can approve a strike against one eunuch-linked network, but the host frames [[CaoJieLateHan|曹节]]'s access relationship with him as stronger than Yang Qiu understands, setting up the Hanji 916 transfer and later Hanji 918 plot leak.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-916-ming-hao-he-yun-hao-nage-geng-zhongyao-lmzudjq5zfkfsukmhdeiumesm9qu|Hanji 916]] adds a 179 CE eunuch-regrouping and memorial-shelving layer before Lyu Qiang's Hanji 917 remonstrance. After [[CaoJieLateHan|曹节]] and other palace eunuchs complain about [[YangQiuLateHan|阳球]], Ling transfers Yang from sili xiaowei to weiwei, weakening the anti-eunuch prosecution channel. Cao Jie's later shangshu ling access is presented through [[ShangshuTaiImperialAccess|尚书台近侍权力]]: a nominally modest document office becomes powerful because it sits near the ruler.

The same source shows Ling receiving but not acting on [[ShenZhongLateHan|沈忠]]'s memorial against [[ZhuYuLateHan|朱瑀]] and broader eunuch corruption. The episode therefore extends Ling's profile as a ruler who can hear severe corrective speech yet let [[CourtFeedbackCollapse|君臣反馈失灵]] persist.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-917-ruhe-xunsu-zhaodao-shengzhi-jiaxin-de-pojudian-lk2zt5c3onvui9a4ppbowshy66tg|Hanji 917]] adds a 179 CE remonstrance and repair layer before the anti-[[CaoJieLateHan|曹节]] plot. Ling offers [[LyuQiangLateHan|吕强]] a marquisate, hears his refusal and broad criticism of eunuch enfeoffment, palace consumption, peasant burden, [[CaiYong|蔡邕]]'s punishment, and [[DuanJiongLateHan|段熲]]'s family exile; the source says Ling recognizes the loyalty but does not adopt the advice. The same source shows a limited repair when he accepts [[HeHaiLateHan|何海]]'s petition narrowing party-prohibition kinship liability, and a harsh legality response when [[ZhangXiuProtectorOfXiongnu|张脩]] is punished after killing [[HuzhengChanyu|呼征]] and installing [[QiangquChanyu|羌渠]] without authorization.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-918-shiqing-bu-shunli-shi-weishenme-yao-bizui-lh-tbohgo96qw8bjqu3x1yp6czv5|Hanji 918]] adds a 179-180 CE pre-garden layer to Emperor Ling's reign. After [[LiuHeSituLateHan|刘郃]], [[ChenQiuLateHan|陈球]], [[LiuNaLateHan|刘纳]], and [[YangQiuLateHan|阳球]] are accused by [[CaoJieLateHan|曹节]] of plotting with [[WangFuLateHan|王甫]], Ling orders their arrest and deaths; the episode reads this as an early case of court balance and eunuch-preserving interpretation before the later [[CourtFeedbackCollapse|feedback-collapse]] branches. The same source places the executions beside [[BandunMan|板楯蛮]], [[YizhouLateHan|益州]], [[Xianbei|鲜卑]], disaster, omen, and [[EmpressDowagerHe|Empress He]]'s elevation, showing the reign's disorder before the 181 CE private-treasury sequence.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-919-xiang-huode-hao-xian-tikai-shanliang-de-ziji-lrqxfzir4bqxt0v3ccvfef8faade|Hanji 919]] adds a garden-remonstrance layer before the private-treasury and office-selling episodes. Emperor Ling orders new imperial gardens, and [[YangCiLateHan|Yang Ci]] warns that the plan will destroy fertile suburban fields, displace residents, and ignore already sufficient pleasure grounds; Ling hesitates, but attendants invoke [[ZhouWenwang|King Wen of Zhou]] and [[QiXuanWang|King Xuan of Qi]] through [[MenciusText|Mencius]] to make the project sound like legitimate "shared pleasure." The case extends [[UntimelyExtravagance]] by showing private imperial enjoyment consuming productive land rather than only treasury money.

The same source gives Emperor Ling a reactive justice role in the [[YangXuanLateHan|Yang Xuan]] case: he pardons Yang and punishes [[ZhaoKaiLateHan|Zhao Kai]] after Yang's blood-written appeal reaches the palace. That reversal does not make the court healthy; it shows that a false provincial report can nearly destroy a successful local official until an extreme [[BloodLetterVindication|blood-letter vindication]] bypasses captured channels.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-920-mingwu-zhege-daoli-caineng-zhuan-daqian-lqmabg3bpyz6ct2v4arx-iun57ws|Hanji 920]] adds the first 181 CE market-and-responsibility layer before the private-treasury branch in Hanji 921. Emperor Ling's court creates an office for requisitioned grain and war horses, while local strongmen capture the demand signal and raise horse prices, making [[ProcurementSignalPriceCornering|征发信号价格垄断]] part of the reign's fiscal disorder.

The same source makes Ling's palace market play a direct role failure. He builds shops in the harem, has palace women trade, quarrel, fight, and steal, then wanders through a miniature market of treasures, tea houses, wine shops, music, and acrobatics. The episode places this [[PalaceMarketTheater|宫市游乐式权力错位]] beside [[JiaozhiCommandery|交趾郡]] rebellion, [[Xianbei|鲜卑]] pressure, hail, eclipse, palace fire, and office churn, so his amusement becomes an early form of [[UntimelyExtravagance|时绌举赢]] before the explicit [[ImperialPrivateTreasuryExtraction|帝王私库进奉盘剥]] critique.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-921-jinqian-ru-shui-que-ze-ke-si-tan-ze-yan-si-lj1dk4qdgcago9elr7ctuvre3zic|Hanji 921]] adds the 181 CE private-treasury layer before the later Yellow Turban warnings and palace-construction fee branches. Emperor Ling hoards private wealth and asks commanderies and kingdoms to send extra fine goods into his private-property office, turning imperial appetite into [[ImperialPrivateTreasuryExtraction|帝王私库进奉盘剥]].

The same source makes the damage explicit through [[LyuQiangLateHan|吕强]]. Lyu Qiang tells Ling that dividing public and private imperial wealth produces extra local requisitions, carriage and horse burdens, fees, and conscript labor, while appointments routed through Shangshu or direct edicts weaken the Three Excellencies' responsibility for selecting and testing officials. Ling ignores the memorial and remains framed by [[UntimelyExtravagance|时绌举赢]], palace pleasure, and eunuch intimacy, including [[CaoJieLateHan|曹节]]'s death, [[ZhaoZhongLateHan|赵忠]]'s succession, and the birth-family setup around Liu Xie / [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-922-ruhe-zai-fanchen-zhong-xiulian-qingshang-lsxiqneyyeod6aeeqxuhs517pn1x|Hanji 922]] adds the 182-183 CE pre-Yellow-Turban layer. Emperor Ling hears popular criticism and orders a review of harmful provincial and commandery officials, then partially corrects the false report after [[ChenDanLateHan|陈耽]] exposes [[XuXuTaiweiLateHan|许訏]] and [[ZhangJiSikongLateHan|张济]]'s protection of eunuch-linked corrupt officials. The repair remains limited because the filtering officials receive only a scolding.

The same source places Ling's display politics beside crisis: epidemic, drought, palace fire, Yellow River flooding, landslide, and the [[BandunMan|板楯蛮]] rebellion surround his 400-chi tower, hunting trips, and title grant to [[WuyangJun|武阳君]]. It also shows the court able to settle the Bandun case when [[ChenBaoLateHan|陈鲍]] diagnoses official extraction and [[CaoQianLateHan|曹谦]] is sent with amnesty, while [[ZhangJiaoLateHan|张角]]'s [[TaipingDao|太平道]] continues growing under local nonintervention.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-923-chengnianrende-dingji-henjin-guzi-lide-juejue-lifzh-gohpqnmdznnfv72tndqf8h|Hanji 923]] adds the ignored-prewarning layer before the open 184 CE emergency. [[YangCiLateHan|杨赐]] warns that [[ZhangJiaoLateHan|张角]] is using [[TaipingDao|太平道]] to gather followers and recommends dispersing the base before attacking the head, but the memorial is left without effective action after Yang Ci leaves office. [[LiuTaoLateHan|刘陶 / 刘桃]] then repeats the warning, while Emperor Ling redirects him to textual work rather than acting on the threat.

The source therefore makes Emperor Ling's Yellow Turban failure visible before Hanji 924's emergency correction. His profile is not only late reaction; it is a ruler whose court receives concrete warning about organization, palace risk, and timing but cannot convert it into prevention until [[TangZhouLateHan|唐周]]'s disclosure forces a crisis response.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-925-tuoxie-bushi-rensong-xuehui-tuoxie-lu-geng-changliang-limafqskgblrwetestyh39w-hzm4|Hanji 925]] adds an immediate 184 CE warning-and-office layer. Emperor Ling asks [[YangCiLateHan|杨赐]] about the [[YellowTurbansLateHan|Yellow Turban]] uprising, dislikes a too-direct answer, and dismisses Yang Ci in April; later, older memorials by Yang Ci and [[LiuTaoLateHan|刘陶 / 刘桃]] about [[ZhangJiaoLateHan|张角]] are recognized as accurate and rewarded.

The same source also records senior-office churn after the warnings prove true: Deng Sheng becomes taiwei, Zhang Ji is removed as sikong, and Zhang Wen becomes sikong. Emperor Ling's profile here is a ruler who can retroactively reward accurate advice without repairing the information and punishment filters that made the warning costly in the first place.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-927-zuo-shengyi-de-zuigao-jingjie-shi-shenme-lngfbjn6xui9k-9lp-ihdqkhpbpr|Hanji 927]] adds the first 184 CE internal-root warning on this page. [[FuXie|傅燮]] tells Emperor Ling that [[YellowTurbansLateHan|Yellow Turban]] disorder cannot be solved only as local rebellion because the deeper cause lies in eunuch power and the ruler's failure to use loyal ministers.

The episode makes Ling's feedback problem visible before the inspection and palace-finance branches. The memorial reaches court, but [[ZhaoZhongLateHan|赵忠]] sees it, resents Fu Xie, and later helps block his reward. Accurate diagnosis therefore enters the throne room without repairing the information system around the emperor.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-928-zhichang-buke-mingshuo-song-dui-li-banshi-geng-shunli-lgup21lsa-u1ykaiyabcko2vhp0o|Hanji 928]] adds a 184 CE command-trust layer before the palace-finance and later succession branches. While [[LuZhiLateHan|卢植]] is close to victory against [[ZhangJiaoLateHan|张角]] at Guangzong, Emperor Ling worries about a front-line general holding a large force and sends [[ZuoFengLateHan|左丰]] to inspect the camp.

The episode's imperial profile is one of captured interpretation. Zuo Feng's report presents Lu Zhi's siege posture as idle resting; Emperor Ling believes it, recalls Lu Zhi to [[LuoyangLateHan|洛阳]] in a prison cart, and appoints [[DongZhuo|董卓]] to replace him. This makes Emperor Ling's early Yellow Turban crisis a case of [[InspectionGiftGatekeeping|巡查送礼关口]], [[PalaceAccessMonopoly|宫廷接触垄断]], and [[CourtFeedbackCollapse|君臣反馈失灵]] before the later Zhang Rang and Zhao Zhong slander branches.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-931-rensheng-jiannan-chu-bianshi-xiuxin-shi-lpzmvxop8lmlwvxhoojzj5ok9qd6|Hanji 931]] adds a 184-185 CE trust-and-disaster layer before the palace-finance branch. [[WangYunLateHan|王允]] submits letters tying [[ZhangRangLateHan|张让]]'s retainers to the Yellow Turban side, and Emperor Ling angrily scolds Zhang Rang but does not investigate deeply enough to prevent Zhang Rang's retaliation.

The same source closes with 185 CE epidemic and palace fires in [[LuoyangLateHan|洛阳]], linking disease, hunger, war, refugee movement, and revolt to accelerating late-Han decline. Ling's profile here is not only extravagant or succession-negligent; it is also a ruler whose personal trust and weakened state environment make accurate warning hard to convert into durable action.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-932-weishenme-ren-yue-hao-yue-nan-dang-daguan-lvwzel782wcxs1lpt3rg2vmkdy29|Hanji 932]] adds a 184 CE palace-finance layer before the later office-sale, remonstrance, and succession branches. Emperor Ling accepts [[ZhangRangLateHan|张让]] and [[ZhaoZhongLateHan|赵忠]]'s proposal for added palace-repair and bronze-statue funding, while material requisitions and office-entry fees push the cost from the court through officials onto local people.

The same source gives Emperor Ling one partial response to feedback. [[LuKangLateHan|陆康]]'s protest is recoded as disrespect and punished, but [[SimaZhiLateHan|司马直]]'s death memorial later moves Ling to stop the palace-construction fee temporarily. The episode therefore frames Ling as capable of momentary moral shock inside a deeper [[PalaceConstructionFeeExtraction|修宫钱转嫁盘剥]] and [[CourtFeedbackCollapse|君臣反馈失灵]] environment.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-933-weiguan-zhidao-yousuo-wei-yousuo-buwei-lopulnufsagoqyx7nngvcqi2nhrj|Hanji 933]] adds a 185 CE incorporation-and-office-sale layer. Emperor Ling accepts [[ZhangYanLateHan|张燕]]'s surrender because suppression capacity is weak, granting office and delegated order over the northern Yellow River mountain zone. The same source shows senior offices as priced through West Garden and palace access when [[CuiLieLateHan|崔烈]] becomes situ after a five-million-cash payment through [[ChengFurenLateHan|程夫人]], while Ling privately regrets not extracting ten million.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-934-sha-yiren-ze-tianxia-an-ni-sha-busha-ltar8hvdkqct8oizm3k22z38b1v8|Hanji 934]] adds the Liangzhou policy and Huangfu Song demotion layer. Emperor Ling is first tempted by [[CuiLieLateHan|崔烈]]'s proposal to abandon [[LiangzhouLateHan|凉州]], then accepts [[FuXie|傅燮]]'s rebuttal that the province is a western shield whose loss would create a larger future threat.

The same episode also shows Ling's dependence on eunuch favorites. He enfeoffs [[ZhangRangLateHan|张让]] and other eunuchs as liehou, then believes Zhang Rang and [[ZhaoZhongLateHan|赵忠]] when they accuse [[HuangfuSongLateHan|皇甫嵩]], even though Huangfu Song had been recalled because the court still needed his military capacity.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-935-ren-huo-yi-kou-qi-nan-cheng-de-shi-ziji-lmy8tyrttv-gjvke7sdi6dx-oopw|Hanji 935]] adds the western-crisis and remonstrance layer from the same late 185 field. Emperor Ling appoints [[ZhangWenLateHan|张温]], [[DongZhuo|董卓]], and [[ZhouShenLateHan|周慎]] against the western rebels, but then accepts eunuch accusations against [[LiuTaoLateHan|刘陶 / 刘桃]] after Liu Tao warns that the court is endangered by successive rebellions.

This episode's imperial profile is one of captured interpretation. A warning about [[ZhangJiaoLateHan|张角]]'s aftermath, [[BianZhangLateHan|边章]], [[HanSui|韩遂]], and pressure near [[LuoyangLateHan|洛阳]] reaches the throne, but palace intermediaries make the warning look like rebel collusion and dark exaggeration, sending Liu Tao to prison and death.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-936-luanshi-xian-sha-shengmu-xin-zhehua-dui-ma-lml-kuyc7yys2ijh2kt46pwnugar|Hanji 936]] adds an earlier 185-186 CE corruption-and-display layer. Emperor Ling builds a private treasury around West Garden's Wanjin Hall, stores money and silk in eunuch households, buys land and builds an estate in his old fief, and orders [[SongDianLateHan|宋典]] and [[BiLanLateHan|毕岚]] into palace construction and spectacle.

The episode uses this material as [[UntimelyExtravagance|时绌举赢]] under late-Han conditions. Palace wealth, mechanical devices, and road-watering displays are set against [[ZhaoCiLateHan|赵慈]]'s revolt, eunuch retaliation against [[ZhangYanTaiweiLateHan|张延]], and frontier pressure, making Emperor Ling's profile one of private accumulation during public breakdown.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-937-zhanshen-zhisi-haoren-zongshi-duozai-duonan-lp7przsmag9ookq7eoisvsmnm5aw|Hanji 937]] gives Emperor Ling an earlier 187 CE crisis layer. He issues a general amnesty, rewards [[HeMiaoLateHan|何苗]] after the Yingyang thief suppression, and still cannot prevent the Liangzhou collapse that kills [[GengBiLateHan|耿鄙]] and [[FuXie|傅燮]]. The episode's imperial profile is one of central incapacity: local loyalty can still exist, but the court cannot protect or coordinate it.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-938-lishi-de-jingxing-ruanruo-zhihui-dailai-quru-lnlnrketgfbadhfjbzdjjtrmqfsh|Hanji 938]] shows Emperor Ling amid 187 CE multi-front crisis before Hanji 939's amnesty and provincial-shepherd response. He faces Liangzhou rebellion, the [[ZhangJuLateHan|张举]] abdication demand, [[QuXingLateHan|曲星]]'s Changsha rising, and fiscal status monetization through the sale of Guannei marquis rank. The episode's imperial profile is reactive: appointments and title sales continue, but they do not prevent military and symbolic humiliation.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-939-donghan-luanju-de-fengkuang-sanguo-xuqu-lthmvw8ihk96fuez9vamlryzcngy|Hanji 939]] adds the crisis-management layer before Emperor Ling's Wang Fen and West Garden branch. He issues an amnesty in early 188, responds to a comet in the Zi Gong star region through ritual apology, and accepts the first [[ProvincialShepherdAutonomy|州牧集权与地方割据]] appointments while [[BaiboArmy|白波军]], [[BingzhouLateHan|并州]], and the [[SouthernXiongnu|南匈奴]] all remain unstable.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-941-shenchu-guanchang-ta-jing-xiang-ganfan-huangdi-liu-fqiz9pi7u6cltrn-cjmjz-r|Hanji 941]] adds the 188 CE pre-succession layer of Emperor Ling's late reign. A northern red qi omen causes him to cancel a trip that [[WangFenLateHan|王芬]] planned to exploit for an imperial seizure and replacement with [[HefeiMarquisLateHan|合肥侯]].

The same episode shows Emperor Ling responding to capital-war warnings through military display and command restructuring. He establishes the [[WestGardenEightColonels|西园八校尉]], trusts [[JianShuoLateHan|蹇硕]] with the top command channel, and reviews troops at Pingle Guan while calling himself "无上将军"; [[GaiXunLateHan|盖勋]] rebukes the move as misplaced martial boasting before the next episode develops Gai Xun's anti-eunuch posture.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-942-yue-youxiu-de-ren-huode-yue-lei-heli-ma-lkwlrcgnt-kxxc2qqz6z6wkg7e5z|Hanji 942]] adds a pre-death layer to Emperor Ling's late reign. [[GaiXunLateHan|盖勋]] reads him as a ruler whose judgment is blocked by attendants rather than as simply unreachable, while [[JianShuoLateHan|蹇硕]] can still use imperial access to move Gai Xun out and later try to send [[HeJin|何进]] west.

The episode therefore makes Emperor Ling's court disorder visible before the non-designated succession crisis. The problem is not only what he fails to settle at death, but also how palace access already shapes appointments and campaign assignments while he is alive.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-943-quanshi-kaoyan-renxing-beihou-cangzhe-juda-xiongxian-lg7ia5xok-swdmhml35yv9qqkvt|Hanji 943]] makes Emperor Ling the immediate source of a late-Han [[SuccessionNonDesignationRisk|未定继承人风险]] case. He dies in 189 CE without formally naming a crown prince, while [[LiuBianHongnongWang|刘辩 / 弘农王]] and Liu Xie, later [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]], already have different maternal and foster support lines.

The source says Emperor Ling privately prefers Liu Xie because he finds Liu Bian frivolous and lacking majesty, but he never turns that preference into a settled succession order. His deathbed entrustment of Liu Xie to [[JianShuoLateHan|蹇硕]] therefore creates an unstable substitute for public designation: Jian Shuo treats the trust as authority to strike [[HeJin|何进]], while the He side can still enthrone Liu Bian after the palace attempt fails.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-949-lishishang-de-dongzhuo-daodi-duo-canbao-lk6idogwmc54nk1sdfc5wonyvgbb|Hanji 949]] later uses Emperor Ling as the death-timing marker that leaves [[YufuluoChanyu|于扶罗]] without an effective Han court judgment during the [[SouthernXiongnu|南匈奴]] succession crisis. The source says Yufuluo comes to [[LuoyangLateHan|洛阳]] to complain, but the emperor's death and the ensuing disorder make the court unable to settle the matter.

The same source also names Emperor Ling as one of the authorities whose summons [[ShentuPanLateHan|申屠蟠]] had refused before [[DongZhuo|董卓]] tried to recruit him. The page is source-scoped: Hanji 949 uses Emperor Ling mainly to mark the late-Han court breakdown before Dong Zhuo's more direct coercive rule.
