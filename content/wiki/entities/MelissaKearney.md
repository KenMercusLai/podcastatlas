# Melissa Kearney

## Overview
Melissa Kearney is the economist across two [[FreakonomicsRadio]] episodes who interprets U.S. fertility decline and examines parenting expectations, financial policy, historical mortgage access, and family-suitable housing.

## Current Profile
Kearney argues that the post-2007 decline cannot be adequately explained by the standard economic variables considered in the episode. She treats shifting priorities, delayed marriage and parenthood, and [[IntensiveParentingFertilityConstraint|intensive parenting norms]] as plausible contributors, while judging incremental cash, leave, and childcare measures unlikely to produce large sustained fertility changes.

Her housing work with Lisa Dettling is presented as evidence that earlier access to affordable, stable homeownership can advance family formation. [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-dde046734f50fc7c6a18ae9c19ada137-55355169-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-dde046734f50fc7c6a18ae9c19ada137-55355169]] adds a three-channel account of housing costs, housing wealth, and access to mortgages and family-appropriate homes. Kearney does not treat rent alone as clearly correlated with fertility and keeps housing form and culturally shaped childrearing expectations in view.

## Key Characteristics
- Reads recent fertility decline as more than temporary birth postponement.
- Separates stable stated ideals from changing marriage and parenthood behavior.
- Treats flexible work and less demanding parenting norms as potentially important.
- Uses historical mortgage policy to examine housing's relationship to family timing.
- Separates general housing cost from wealth, credit access, and family-suitable form.
