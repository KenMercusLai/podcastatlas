## Overview

谷永 / Gu Yong (Western Han) enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-552-tongtou-renxing-de-ren-zhichang-pingbuqingyun-lnnhr8e-67qdqwhzi1h0qav-valw|Hanji 552]] as a Chengdi-era official whose career is read through patronage stigma and ruler distrust. The source says he had been recommended through the Wang-family side, repeatedly memorialized against [[HanChengdi|汉成帝]] and his inner-palace conduct, then was allowed to retire during illness and died a few months later. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-505-xihan-de-huicheng-yaoyan-da-le-shui-de-lian-ljuwccxx_aexasafkpbl-y2rvos5|Hanji 505]] backfills an earlier alignment layer: Gu Yong and [[DuQinWesternHan|杜钦]] interpret Chengdi-era eclipse and earthquake signs as harem favoritism and heir-risk problems, shifting pressure away from Wang-family outer-relative power. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-506-hanhuangdi-weihe-zhongqing-yihun-funv-lqlj6msoz2nk_bodf8b5ljv1ny8q|Hanji 506]] sharpens that alignment: Gu Yong answers a White Tiger Hall anomaly inquiry by arguing that Heaven's warning concerns harem fertility, then asks Chengdi to show the memorial to trusted ministers, which the host reads as making [[WangFengWesternHan|王凤]] the real audience and beneficiary. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-507-ta-zhansha-xiongnu-chanyu-weihe-fanbei-xiayu-lhfkej7pqtikx5st73sls-kwo_ze|Hanji 507]] adds a proportional-merit defense layer: Gu Yong argues that [[ChenTangWesternHan|陈汤]] should not be executed for a Kangju hostage-report offense because his killing of [[ZhizhiChanyu|郅支单于]] had deterred frontier powers. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-510-hanchengdi-de-zhedao-shengzhi-weihe-dachen-gan-yangfengyinwei-ljxebrollq4yydnmg8otocckmth4|Hanji 510]] adds a diplomatic-restraint layer: Gu Yong and Du Qin advise refusing [[YiYanMoyan|一言莫衍]]'s defection because accepting one Xiongnu minister could damage trust with a submitted chanyu or fall into a test. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-543-citou-yuangong-tai-nan-guan-yonghao-zhezhao-qingsong-rang-ta-furuan-lm9_0r8mnwcrpqukmsmoxwndzwy0|Hanji 543]] adds an earlier anti-fangshu layer: Gu Yong warns Chengdi that claims about immortals, elixirs, and gold-making are deceptive and costly when ritual anxiety is rising. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-538-ma-hanchengdi-weihe-mei-bei-zhulian-lmdphggod_t9uk7uu5z_53rjkj7u|Hanji 538]] adds the harsher Yongshi 2 remonstrance layer: Gu Yong uses Chengdi's omen inquiry to attack the ruler's women, drinking, fiscal burden, private outings, and indulgence, then survives because Wang-family backing and Chengdi's cooling anger keep the arrest order bounded. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-545-xihan-huangzu-jingtian-dagua-lvdplebjdtlav7kywaxvevmbtx6x|Hanji 545]] adds a legal-procedure layer: Gu Yong defends [[LiuLiWesternHan|梁王刘立]] against a "禽兽行" charge by questioning case expansion, private evidence, emotional plausibility, and the damage done to royal-house trust by rash prosecution. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-546-haoyou-bianwei-choudi-neng-you-duo-kepa-lrtypxhdqvzkme_rgesxwoiv1asv|Hanji 546]] adds a later Yuan延 1 disaster-warning layer, where Gu Yong ties repeated anomalies to dynastic danger, inner-court disorder, rebellion risk, and the need for Chengdi to restrain favorites, reduce burden, and settle the people.

## Current Profile

Hanji 552 uses Gu Yong as the opening example before the Kong Guang succession case. In the episode's workplace language, relying on a visible backer is unsafe if the real decision-maker distrusts that patronage line. Gu Yong's link to the Wang family and his unwelcome memorials make Chengdi disinclined to trust him, even if the office form remains ordinary.

The source also stresses reputation memory. It says later historians judge Gu Yong harshly because his earlier path upward involved attaching himself to [[WangFengWesternHan|王凤]] and reversing moral language for advancement. Gu Yong therefore functions less as a full biography here than as a caution about how early court alignment shapes later credibility.

Hanji 505 and Hanji 506 supply that early alignment in action. In Hanji 505's reading, disaster signs could have been understood as pressure on Wang-family outer-relative power, but Gu Yong and Du Qin redirect the interpretation toward harem favoritism, jealousy, and the lack of an heir. Hanji 506 makes the court-benefit clearer: when many frank speakers blame Wang Feng directly, Gu Yong rejects that explanation, treats harem fertility as the true crisis, and gives Wang Feng a way to defend the presentation of a previously married woman to Chengdi. The source reads this as Gu Yong choosing Wang Feng's side after the Xu family appears to be declining.

Hanji 507 shows Gu Yong as a defender of proportional punishment rather than only an omen interpreter. Chen Tang's Kangju case is not framed as innocence: the episode accepts property-taking and false reporting as serious faults. Gu Yong's memorial instead argues that the state should remember a commander's extraordinary frontier service and avoid killing someone whose earlier victory had intimidated Xiongnu and other powers. Chengdi's outcome, release without restored title, makes Gu Yong's advice a limited success.

Hanji 510 adds that Gu Yong's caution can also be diplomatic. When Yi Yan Moyan asks to defect, Gu Yong and Du Qin oppose accepting him because the chanyu is now submitted to Han. Their argument weighs the symbolic gain of one defector against frontier trust, moral bad faith, and the possibility that the request is a deliberate test. Chengdi's refusal makes this advice a successful restraint case.

Hanji 543 shows Gu Yong in a different advisory register. After Chengdi restores major sacrifices following an eclipse and violent wind at Ganquan, fangshu petitioners increase around the emperor's childlessness. Gu Yong's memorial rejects the authority of those who promise immortals, deathlessness, and transmutation, placing him beside [[FangshiFraudAndAuthority|方士骗术与权威]] as a court skeptic rather than only a patronage-stigma example.

Hanji 538 shows why later memories of his repeated Chengdi criticism could be politically fraught. As Liangzhou刺史, Gu Yong is asked to interpret repeated natural phenomena and answers with unusually direct blame. The episode treats the boldness as sponsored speech: [[WangZhengjun|王政君]] and imperial uncles want Chengdi corrected, while [[WangShangWesternHan|王商]] warns Gu Yong to flee once Chengdi orders him arrested. Gu Yong's safety therefore depends on both the content of remonstrance and the backing structure around it.

Hanji 545 shows Gu Yong in a procedural rather than omen-only register. After the Liang king case expands from speech against Chengdi's outer-relative family into an incest allegation involving [[LiuYuanziWesternHan|刘原子]], Gu Yong argues that private royal-house accusations require careful review. His defense moves from ritual privacy to evidentiary plausibility: the original charge was unproved, Liu Li denies the sexual accusation, the age and incentive structure seem odd, and Liu Yuanzi would have reason not to expose such disgrace. The episode therefore adds Gu Yong as a defender of bounded procedure when scandal could become a capital case.

Hanji 546 shows Gu Yong still working inside the disaster-remonstrance register several years later. The memorial links ruler virtue with Heaven's response, then converts the unusually dense sequence of anomalies into a warning about inner ministers, dangerous consorts, popular rebellion, unsafe imperial conduct, extravagance, and burdens on commoners. This adds policy direction to the earlier rebuke: Gu Yong does not only condemn Chengdi's private conduct, but also urges hierarchical discipline, consort restraint, caution in travel, frugality, grace to the people, and pacification of disorder.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han official under [[HanChengdi|汉成帝]].
- Recommended through Wang-family influence, making him suspect to Chengdi.
- Memorializes repeatedly against Chengdi and palace conduct, using disaster and omen warnings both to redirect pressure toward harem favoritism and to connect Chengdi's consort favoritism, revelry, spending, inner-court danger, rebellion risk, public burden, and dynastic survival.
- Defends Chen Tang against execution by weighing extraordinary frontier merit against a serious but non-treason reporting offense, and later helps refuse a Xiongnu envoy's defection to preserve wider frontier trust.
- Defends Liu Li by turning a royal incest scandal back into questions of evidence, plausibility, privacy, and proper review.
- Warns Chengdi against fangshi claims about immortals, elixirs, and gold-making after sacrifice restoration increases ritual and specialist petitions.
- Allowed to retire during illness and dies soon after, while the episodes also use him as a patronage and reputation case: backing can protect dangerous speech in the moment, but earlier attachment to power can make later criticism easier to dismiss.

## Qualifications

This page is source-scoped to Hanji 552, Hanji 543, Hanji 538, Hanji 545, Hanji 546, Hanji 505, Hanji 507, Hanji 510, and Hanji 506 and does not reconstruct Gu Yong's full biography, offices, memorial texts, or standard-history evaluation. The source transcripts write the name as "古勇" or "谷勇"; this page normalizes to 谷永 because the Chengdi-era official, Wang Feng association, anti-fangshu Chengdi memorial, Liang king defense, Chen Tang defense, Xiongnu defection advice, and disaster remonstrance fit the known Western Han figure. The existing [[GuYongLateHan|Gu Yong (late Han)]] page is a separate person and should not be merged. Hanji 506's factional-benefit reading is a host interpretation of memorial target and effect; it does not independently prove Gu Yong's inner motive.
