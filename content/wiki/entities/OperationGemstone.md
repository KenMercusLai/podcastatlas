# Operation Gemstone

## Overview

Operation Gemstone was the umbrella for dirty-tricks proposals developed by [[GordonLiddy|G. Gordon Liddy]] and [[HowardHunt|Howard Hunt]] for Nixon's 1972 reelection campaign.

## Current Profile

The episode depicts Gemstone as a mixture of theatrical absurdity and institutional danger. Proposals included disruption, surveillance, sexual entrapment, and other covert schemes. Even where senior campaign officials rejected the most extravagant versions, continued reliance on the planners preserved the path toward bugging Democratic National Committee offices in the Watergate building.

## Key Characteristics

- Collection of covert campaign proposals rather than one isolated tactic.
- Included surveillance, disruption, and entrapment ideas.
- Developed by Liddy and Hunt for the reelection campaign.
- Partly rejected as too expensive or extreme.
- Demonstrated that senior filtering reduced proposals without ending the underlying illegal-operational culture.
- Culminated in the plan to bug Democratic National Committee offices.

## Evidence

### Proposal range and institutional reception

- [[106-watergate-part-1-glt6675580126]] describes schemes involving false food orders, a houseboat, prostitutes, surveillance, and the later DNC target.

## Qualifications

The episode's colorful examples are retrospective narrative claims and do not establish which proposals reached formal approval, how seriously every participant treated them, or the complete budget and authorization sequence.

## What Changed

- Established Gemstone as the program-level bridge between fantastic dirty tricks and the actionable Watergate burglary.
