# Scotland

## Overview

Scotland appears as a gradually consolidated medieval kingdom, an Anglo-French strategic actor, and a modern constituent nation whose sporting identity does not map neatly onto British state symbols.

## Current Profile

[[104-macbeth-glt3313769671]] pushes the profile back before a settled modern Scotland. Pictish, Gaelic, Brittonic, Northumbrian, and Viking power interact as Alba emerges, while Moray remains a major northern center and [[MedievalScottishSuccession|royal-kin competition]] makes kingship unstable. The episode also argues that English pressure and later dynastic needs helped consolidate both political identity and a usable national backstory.

By the Hundred Years' War branch, Scotland is a recognized ally of [[France]] and a northern strategic pressure point against [[England]]. In the modern anthem branch, the same distinct identity appears through separate football and rugby representation and [[FlowerOfScotland|Flower of Scotland]], while Culloden memory warns against flattening internal Scottish division into a timeless England-versus-Scotland story.

## Key Characteristics

- Medieval polity formed through consolidation among multiple peoples, regions, and external pressures.
- Kingdom in which Alba and Moray initially represented uneven centers of authority.
- Historical-memory case where later national genealogy simplifies earlier political fragmentation.
- Medieval ally of France and northern pressure point against England.
- Constituent nation inside the United Kingdom with distinct football and rugby identity.
- Setting where national symbols, monarchy, Culloden, and sporting feeling do not align neatly.
