# Christopher Avery

## Overview

Christopher Avery is the economist whose research with [[CarolineHoxby]] is used in [[carlos-doesn-t-remember-carlos-doesn-t-remember|Carlos Doesn’t Remember]] to examine why many high-achieving low-income students do not apply to selective colleges.

## Current Profile

The episode presents Avery and Hoxby as testing an institutional assumption. Selective colleges believed recruitment, counseling networks, campus visits, and aid had already reached most plausible low-income high achievers, yet linked test and contextual data reportedly revealed a much larger population whose application choices did not match their academic records.

The research gives [[CollegeApplicationUndermatch]] a national empirical frame. It does not, on the episode's own argument, measure children whose ability was weakened or whose schooling ended before SAT or ACT participation.

## Key Characteristics

- Economist associated with national research on low-income high achievers.
- Tests whether selective-college outreach reaches the full plausible applicant pool.
- Helps distinguish application behavior from an underlying absence of talent.
- Supplies a measured lower bound that the episode argues omits earlier pipeline loss.

## Evidence

### Institutional question

- [[carlos-doesn-t-remember-carlos-doesn-t-remember]] frames Avery and Hoxby's work as a response to selective colleges' weak low-income enrollment gains.

### Reported result

- [[carlos-doesn-t-remember-carlos-doesn-t-remember]] reports their estimate of roughly 35,000 high-scoring low-income students annually and widespread nonapplication to selective colleges.
