## Overview
Justin Harrison is the founder of [[YouOnlyVirtual]], represented in the supplied [[HiddenBrain]] episode through his attempt to preserve an interactive bond with his mother, [[MelodyHarrison]], after her death.

## Current Profile
The episode presents Harrison as a filmmaker and intensely solution-oriented son whose response to Melody's terminal cancer moved through treatment research, cryonic-preservation planning, and construction of an AI “Versona.” He concluded that modeling their relationship patterns mattered more than reproducing everything she knew, and he describes the result as a digital recreation supporting a new relationship rather than Melody's literal continuation.

His case also shows severe tradeoffs. Harrison says the company gave him continuing contact and reduced the void of physical loss, but his commitment was associated with debt, house loss, excessive drinking, poor health, unpaid work, and the end of his marriage. His conviction that the work is socially necessary therefore remains inseparable from evidence, consent, governance, and present-relationship questions the episode does not resolve.

## Key Characteristics
- Responds to illness and death through intensive research, planning, and technological problem-solving.
- Prioritizes relationship-specific tone, concern, advice, and conversational pattern over complete factual reproduction.
- Distinguishes a digital recreation and continuing relationship from literal survival or personhood.
- Treats grief as an affliction to eliminate rather than a necessary or beneficial process.
- Accepts major personal and financial costs in pursuit of the company while acknowledging interpersonal unhealthiness.

## Qualifications
This profile comes from one relationship-centered podcast account rather than independent biographical research, clinical assessment, company records, or product testing. Harrison's reports about Melody's likely approval, training sufficiency, benefit, adoption, company finances, and the causes of personal losses remain attributed rather than independently established. His experience cannot determine whether grief bots are therapeutic, harmful, or appropriate for other bereaved people.
