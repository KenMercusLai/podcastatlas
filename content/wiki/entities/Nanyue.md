## Overview

南越 / Nanyue is the southern polity built by [[ZhaoTuo|赵佗]] from former Qin Lingnan commanderies. Its current profile runs from an outside refuge and recognition-backed submission under [[LiuBang|刘邦]], through a locally rooted power-sharing structure, to resistance under [[LyuJiaNanyue|吕嘉]] and the multi-route conquest campaign of [[HanWudi|汉武帝]].

## Current Profile

Hanji 170 first uses Nanyue as an outside destination that a pressured former enemy might strengthen. Hanji 184 and 185 then make it a direct policy subject: Zhao Tuo uses [[RenXiao|任嚣]]'s handoff, Lingnan terrain, and Qin's collapse to consolidate [[NanhaiCommandery|南海郡]], [[GuilinCommandery|桂林郡]], and [[XiangCommandery|象郡]]. Liu Bang recognizes him as Nanyue king, and [[LuJia|陆贾]] combines identity pressure, Han victory, family risk, and formal title recognition to make submission preferable to war.

That settlement is incorporation without direct occupation. Zhao Tuo accepts Han hierarchy and external obligations while retaining a local base. Hanji 191 qualifies the arrangement with a South Sea king notice that may be a nominal carve-out rather than effective removal of Nanhai from his control.

Hanji 353-1 adds an internal structure absent from the earlier profile. It says Zhao Tuo secured support by aligning his royal house with the locally influential Lü family: Zhao held the kingship, Lü Jia held the chancellorship, and marriage joined the families. On this account, whether Nanyue accepted closer Han incorporation depended not only on the king but also on locally rooted power.

Hanji 352-1 identifies the political rupture before the campaign. [[ZhaoXingNanyue|赵兴]] and [[QueenDowagerJiuNanyue|樛太后]] favor closer Han incorporation, but their banquet confrontation does not remove Lü Jia. When a two-thousand-person Han mission enters, Lü Jia mobilizes against the royal party, kills the king, queen dowager, and Han envoys, and installs [[ZhaoJiandeNanyue|赵建德]]. The sequence shows that Nanyue's final war begins as an internal legitimacy and power struggle, not only an external invasion.

Hanji 352-2 and 353-1 then show the military rupture. [[HanQianqiuWesternHan|韩千秋]]'s force is annihilated near Panyu, after which Wudi mobilizes a reported ten-wan-person army. [[LuBodeWesternHan|路博德]] and [[YangPuWesternHan|杨仆]] lead principal routes, three surrendered Nanyue figures lead others, and Yang Pu's [[TowerShipWaterborneWarfare|tower-ship force]] adds a waterborne branch to the convergence plan.

Hanji 354 supplies the outcome. Yang Pu attacks Panyu from the southeast while Lu Bode receives prisoners and amplifies surrender through credentials and recruited intermediaries. Zhao Jiande and Lü Jia flee by sea and are captured; local rulers and officials then submit before the supporting routes arrive. Nanyue's destruction is followed by nine-commandery reorganization and the reported establishment of [[ZhuyaCommandery|珠崖郡]] and [[DanerCommandery|儋耳郡]] on Hainan.

## Key Characteristics

- Southern polity formed from former Qin commandery territory and difficult Lingnan routes.
- Early Han frontier order incorporated through recognition and deterrent diplomacy rather than immediate occupation.
- Locally autonomous regime whose political durability is tied to both Zhao royal authority and the Lü family's local influence, and whose final rupture becomes a coup over Han incorporation.
- Political field whose nominal borders remain uncertain in the later South Sea kingship notice.
- Resistance theater where an initial Han force is destroyed and a much larger multi-route campaign follows.
- Strategic environment requiring land routes, surrendered local participants, and specialized waterborne forces.
- Conquered polity whose collapse is followed by differentiated surrender rewards and commandery administration.

## Qualifications

The sources describe different moments and should not be collapsed into one static relationship. Liu Bang-era recognition does not prove permanent Han control, while the later rebellion frame does not erase the early settlement's temporary reality. The South Sea kingship remains source-scoped and possibly nominal. Hanji 352-1's banquet counterfactual and Lü Jia's sexual, treasure, and enslavement allegations are interpretive or partisan rather than settled fact. Hanji 353-1's Zhao-Lü settlement and Hanji 354's conquest sequence are compact Han-centered retellings rather than a full Nanyue political history. Force totals, routes, battle details, surrender motives, nine-commandery chronology, and local perspectives remain incomplete or source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Added the internal coup that converts a divided incorporation policy into replacement kingship and open war.
- Distinguished partisan anti-royal mobilization from neutral evidence about Queen Dowager Jiu.
