## Overview

陈汤 / Chen Tang enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-541-ta-shi-minzu-yingxiong-zuihou-weihe-yin-tan-ruyu-llcktocpmxxib1pjckboldlzgxdu|Hanji 541]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-570-xinyang-buke-queshi-yingxiong-burong-dihui-lrrcbw5rrurytgjxasbp12buf82l|Hanji 570]] as a Western Han frontier commander whose later Changling punishment has to be held beside his earlier campaign merit. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-489-weizao-shengzhi-shadi-hanjiang-chentang-you-duo-kuang-ligjvbvujg4nujinr1k39aiv28lz|Hanji 489]] supplies the decision prequel: Chen Tang reads [[ZhizhiChanyu|郅支单于]]'s Kangju-based threat as urgent, fails to persuade [[GanYanshouWesternHan|甘延寿]] to act without authorization, then falsely transmits command and coerces Gan into accepting the already-started mobilization. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-490-dahan-zhanlang-chentang-weizao-shengzhi-li-qigong-lnmkvvmwppn6w3-prhfi6nyoqina|Hanji 490]] supplies the operational continuation: Chen Tang's childhood poverty, rough survival, study, eloquence, boldness, six-route Western Regions advance, Kangju intelligence work, and final assault against Zhizhi Chanyu. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-491-zhongguo-waijiao-zui-qiangying-de-shike-jing-shi-zai-xihan-lpgajp_vky9dx7jvnoahaxdfjflk|Hanji 491]] supplies the victory-publication layer: after killing Zhizhi Chanyu, Chen Tang and Gan Yanshou send the head to Chang'an, frame the result through a hardline deterrence memorial, and receive first public celebration and titles under [[HanYuandi|汉元帝]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-496-qiong-pa-le-jiemi-tanguan-zouxiang-tanfu-de-xinli-youyin-lnskbcqvjhtpfi4ikq44kpbbfnol|Hanji 496]] then supplies the immediate pre-reward accusation layer: Chen Tang is still exposed by false-order mobilization, carried valuables, and court claims that his fault should block reward. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-497-huangniba-diao-kudang-xihan-mingjiang-chentang-dangguan-you-duo-nan-liz2ssrmijhowebl5hhx8-61ybmp|Hanji 497]] supplies the original Yuan-era pardon-and-reward outcome after [[LiuXiang|刘向]]'s defense. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-507-ta-zhansha-xiongnu-chanyu-weihe-fanbei-xiayu-lhfkej7pqtikx5st73sls-kwo_ze|Hanji 507]] then supplies the Chengdi-era legal case: [[KuangHengWesternHan|匡衡]] impeaches Chen Tang for taking confiscated [[Kangju|康居]] property, Chen later faces death after a false report about a Kangju hostage, and [[GuYongWesternHan|谷永]] persuades [[HanChengdi|汉成帝]] to release him while still stripping his title. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-526-hanchao-qipa-zhidu-huangdi-si-le-jiu-neng-qianhukou-lkht_jo0z7je4gbyk10ckuxv4cub|Hanji 526]] supplies the pre-Changling policy and household-interest layer. Hanji 541 gives the later Changling punishment mechanics, and Hanji 570 later gives [[GengYuWesternHan|耿育]]'s memorial defending Chen Tang and Gan Yanshou for the same distant campaign.

## Current Profile

The sources present Chen Tang as a person whose merit and fault must be held together. Hanji 489 gives the launch version before the army moves: he sees a narrow opportunity against a feared but vulnerable Zhizhi Chanyu, treats Western Regions distance as a reason for emergency action, and pushes beyond legality by forging command and threatening his superior. Hanji 490 gives the campaign version before the court debate: he is hungry for office after a deprived childhood, capable of reading strategic opportunity, and willing to march before permission can arrive. The same source shows that the military success was not accidental. Chen Tang keeps discipline in Kangju territory, uses local aristocratic contacts and Kaimou's guidance, times the assault, and combines crossbows, shields, fire attack, and encirclement to kill Zhizhi Chanyu despite Kangju support nearby.

Hanji 491 gives the public-victory version: he is the commander whose success lets Han advertise that even a faraway defiant chanyu can be punished, but the same episode already names greed and captured wealth as the flaw that will make later attack possible. Hanji 496 gives the accusation-stage version: Chen Tang has real military achievement, but his forged-order mobilization and handling of captured valuables give opponents legal and moral ammunition before reward is settled. Hanji 497 gives the initial high-reward version: Chen Tang's forged-order campaign is treated as an immense legal and political risk, but Liu Xiang and Yuan weigh its result as extraordinary enough to justify pardon and honors. Hanji 507 then gives the later sanction version: Chen Tang's reputation from killing Zhizhi Chanyu makes Gu Yong argue against execution, but the episode still treats Kangju-property taking, loose money conduct, and false reporting as real reasons his political position collapses. Chengdi's compromise preserves the difference between sparing a useful hero and fully exonerating him.

Hanji 526 shows why his later Changling proposal is not reducible to either civic reform or private corruption. Under strict household registration, his children could live in Chang'an while he held office but still lack permanent capital status; a new mausoleum county would give his family residence, land, and identity security. The same memorial also points to real public problems: land concentration, fiscal weakness, and powerful Guandong households outside easy central control.

Hanji 541 shows how that policy becomes dangerous after the fact: the formal charge concerns reporting and disrespectful words toward Chengdi, but the host reads the deeper cause as Changling relocation politics. Chen Tang had proposed moving powerful Guandong households to Changling, a policy read as [[StrongTrunkWeakBranchCentralization|strong-trunk weak-branch]] centralization. That proposal threatened local elites and their court relationships, while Chen Tang's loose handling of money made corruption suspicion politically usable even though the episode does not prove a specific illicit gain.

His great service remains the victory over Zhizhi Chanyu, framed in Hanji 570 as a restoration of Han prestige and deterrence after long conflict with [[Xiongnu|匈奴]]. Geng Yu's memorial argues that the court had once celebrated this achievement but failed to reward it proportionately, leaving Chen Tang and Gan Yanshou treated as lesser men after an extraordinary act.

The sources also refuse a clean heroic varnish. Chen Tang's later Changling role, his proposal to relocate powerful Guandong households, the enemies this created, and his corruption problem all remain part of the profile. The combined judgment is not that merit abolishes fault, but that public charges, hidden interest conflict, later fault, and factional hostility should not be collapsed into either pure guilt or pure victimhood.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han commander whose wiki role is now split between the Zhizhi campaign operation, victory-publication scene, Yuan-era reward dispute, Kangju-related misconduct, Changling punishment, and later hero-memory dispute.
- Paired with Gan Yanshou as the military actor whose distant victory becomes a court-memory dispute.
- Presented as a morally mixed striver whose poverty, study, boldness, greed, and office hunger help explain both initiative and vulnerability.
- Treats frontier timing as capable of overriding ordinary authorization, making forged command both the origin of his triumph and the legal fault later used against him.
- Uses a Changlingyi proposal to join public centralization arguments with private household-status benefits for his family.
- Treated by Geng Yu as a rare talent still useful to a state that should remember danger while secure.
- Has a qualified record: the sources acknowledge Kangju-property misconduct, false reporting, Changling-related political offense, elite backlash, and corruption suspicion rather than portraying him as flawless.

## Qualifications

This page is source-bounded. Hanji 489 gives the campaign-decision and false-command setup, but its account of Gu Ji's envoy case, Zhizhi Chanyu's Kangju violence, troop mobilization, and Chen Tang's psychology remains podcast-summary-bounded. Hanji 490 gives the operational and character-origin version of Chen Tang, but its route, title, troop-count, casualty, and psychology details remain podcast-summary-bounded. Hanji 491 gives the victory-publication and character-vulnerability layer but not the full campaign chronology, exact memorial text, or complete legal inventory. Hanji 496 gives the accusation-stage version of Chen Tang's return but not the full inventory, legal record, or complete campaign chronology. Hanji 497 gives Chen Tang's original pardon-and-reward case but not the full campaign chronology, legal mechanics of forged orders, or Liu Xiang's complete memorial. Hanji 507 gives Chen Tang's Kangju-property and hostage-report case but not the full legal record, the precise property inventory, or the hostage's independent identity. Hanji 526 gives Chen Tang's Changlingyi proposal and household-status motive, but not his full family record or the complete administrative law of capital registration. Hanji 541 gives the Changling punishment and interest-conflict frame, but not the full case file, exact legal guilt, or corruption evidence. Hanji 570 gives Chen Tang's merit-vindication frame, not a complete biography, full military record, or independent legal assessment. The combined value is proportional judgment under political memory and interest-conflict pressure.
