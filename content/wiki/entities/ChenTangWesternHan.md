## Overview

陈汤 / Chen Tang enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-541-ta-shi-minzu-yingxiong-zuihou-weihe-yin-tan-ruyu-llcktocpmxxib1pjckboldlzgxdu|Hanji 541]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-570-xinyang-buke-queshi-yingxiong-burong-dihui-lrrcbw5rrurytgjxasbp12buf82l|Hanji 570]] as a Western Han frontier commander whose later Changling punishment has to be held beside his earlier campaign merit. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-507-ta-zhansha-xiongnu-chanyu-weihe-fanbei-xiayu-lhfkej7pqtikx5st73sls-kwo_ze|Hanji 507]] now supplies the earlier Chengdi-era legal case: [[KuangHengWesternHan|匡衡]] impeaches Chen Tang for taking confiscated [[Kangju|康居]] property, Chen later faces death after a false report about a Kangju hostage, and [[GuYongWesternHan|谷永]] persuades [[HanChengdi|汉成帝]] to release him while still stripping his title. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-526-hanchao-qipa-zhidu-huangdi-si-le-jiu-neng-qianhukou-lkht_jo0z7je4gbyk10ckuxv4cub|Hanji 526]] supplies the pre-Changling policy and household-interest layer: Chen Tang supports reviving [[MausoleumCountyEliteRelocation|陵县式豪强迁徙]] at Changling partly to strengthen the capital and check Guandong magnates, but also to secure a durable capital-region household future for his wife and children. Hanji 541 gives the later Changling punishment mechanics: [[WangShangWesternHan|王商]] impeaches him, Chengdi spares his life because of old merit, but reduces him to commoner and moves him toward the frontier. Hanji 570 later gives [[GengYuWesternHan|耿育]]'s memorial defending Chen Tang and [[GanYanshouWesternHan|甘延寿]] for the distant campaign that killed [[ZhizhiChanyu|郅支单于]].

## Current Profile

The sources present Chen Tang as a person whose merit and fault must be held together. Hanji 507 gives the clearest early version: Chen Tang's reputation from killing Zhizhi Chanyu makes Gu Yong argue against execution, but the episode still treats Kangju-property taking, loose money conduct, and false reporting as real reasons his political position collapses. Chengdi's compromise preserves the difference between sparing a useful hero and fully exonerating him.

Hanji 526 shows why his later Changling proposal is not reducible to either civic reform or private corruption. Under strict household registration, his children could live in Chang'an while he held office but still lack permanent capital status; a new mausoleum county would give his family residence, land, and identity security. The same memorial also points to real public problems: land concentration, fiscal weakness, and powerful Guandong households outside easy central control.

Hanji 541 shows how that policy becomes dangerous after the fact: the formal charge concerns reporting and disrespectful words toward Chengdi, but the host reads the deeper cause as Changling relocation politics. Chen Tang had proposed moving powerful Guandong households to Changling, a policy read as [[StrongTrunkWeakBranchCentralization|strong-trunk weak-branch]] centralization. That proposal threatened local elites and their court relationships, while Chen Tang's loose handling of money made corruption suspicion politically usable even though the episode does not prove a specific illicit gain.

His great service remains the victory over Zhizhi Chanyu, framed in Hanji 570 as a restoration of Han prestige and deterrence after long conflict with [[Xiongnu|匈奴]]. Geng Yu's memorial argues that the court had once celebrated this achievement but failed to reward it proportionately, leaving Chen Tang and Gan Yanshou treated as lesser men after an extraordinary act.

The sources also refuse a clean heroic varnish. Chen Tang's later Changling role, his proposal to relocate powerful Guandong households, the enemies this created, and his corruption problem all remain part of the profile. The combined judgment is not that merit abolishes fault, but that public charges, hidden interest conflict, later fault, and factional hostility should not be collapsed into either pure guilt or pure victimhood.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han commander whose wiki role is now split between Kangju-related misconduct, Changling punishment, and Zhizhi Chanyu campaign memory.
- Paired with Gan Yanshou as the military actor whose distant victory becomes a court-memory dispute.
- Uses a Changlingyi proposal to join public centralization arguments with private household-status benefits for his family.
- Treated by Geng Yu as a rare talent still useful to a state that should remember danger while secure.
- Has a qualified record: the sources acknowledge Kangju-property misconduct, false reporting, Changling-related political offense, elite backlash, and corruption suspicion rather than portraying him as flawless.
- Becomes an example of how disproportionate punishment or slander against a great contributor can chill future service.

## Qualifications

This page is source-bounded. Hanji 507 gives Chen Tang's Kangju-property and hostage-report case but not the full legal record, the precise property inventory, or the hostage's independent identity. Hanji 526 gives Chen Tang's Changlingyi proposal and household-status motive, but not his full family record or the complete administrative law of capital registration. Hanji 541 gives the Changling punishment and interest-conflict frame, but not the full case file, exact legal guilt, or corruption evidence. Hanji 570 gives Chen Tang's merit-vindication frame, not a complete biography, full military record, or independent legal assessment. The combined value is proportional judgment under political memory and interest-conflict pressure.
