## Overview

魏尚 / Wei Shang is the Yunzhong governor whose troop provisioning and punishment for a six-head reporting discrepancy let [[FengTangWesternHan|冯唐]] test whether the Western Han court makes successful frontier command usable or legally precarious.

## Current Profile

In [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-225-1-fengtang-xiuru-wendi-liuheng-weihe-bu-nu-fan-xi-linynx-dnz0rrptlafz__fch4cgd|Hanji 225-1]], Wei Shang directs collected revenue and part of his own salary toward food and rewards for Yunzhong troops. The episode credits that support with stronger morale and with deterring [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] pressure around the commandery.

His downfall supplies the episode's incentive problem. A report overstates the number of enemy heads by six, and the court removes his rank and imprisons him. Feng Tang does not argue that reporting accuracy is irrelevant; he argues that the punishment is grossly out of proportion to the commander's accumulated frontier value and reveals a system that rewards too lightly while punishing too heavily.

Liu Heng accepts the criticism, sends Feng Tang with a tally to announce a pardon, and restores Wei Shang. Wei Shang's wiki role is therefore narrower than a complete general's biography: he is the concrete personnel case showing how central audit and field effectiveness can become destructive when minor error erases major service.

## Key Characteristics

- Yunzhong frontier governor responsible for troops facing Xiongnu pressure.
- Commander who converts local revenue and personal salary into soldier food and reward.
- Strong-performance official exposed by a small but real military-reporting discrepancy.
- Test case for proportional discipline, command trust, and retention of frontier capacity.

## Qualifications

This profile is bounded to one popular-history episode. The revenue source, salary contribution, provisioning cadence, deterrence claim, six-head discrepancy, legal basis, imprisonment, pardon procedure, restoration, and later career require comparison with transmitted records. The current synthesis does not excuse inaccurate reporting; it identifies proportionality and the preservation of effective command as the episode's institutional issue.
