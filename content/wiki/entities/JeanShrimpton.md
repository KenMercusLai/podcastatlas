# Jean Shrimpton

## Overview

Jean Shrimpton is the British model whose 1965 Melbourne Derby Day appearance anchors the episode's account of changing fashion and respectability codes.

## Current Profile

Shrimpton appears as a symbolic carrier of the mid-1960s youth-fashion break. Her short white shift dress, worn without the expected hat, gloves, or stockings, generated press outrage in conservative Melbourne and helped internationalize the image of rising hemlines. The episode is careful that she wore a dress, not literally a miniskirt, and treats the event's importance as media symbolism rather than sole causation.

## Key Characteristics

- British model associated with the global visibility of 1960s youth fashion.
- Triggered a public-respectability scandal at Melbourne's 1965 Derby Day.
- Became associated with the miniskirt moment despite wearing a shift dress.
- Illustrates how press attention can turn one appearance into a historical fashion marker.

## Evidence

- Derby Day appearance: [[364-sixties-fashion-swinging-london-part-2-glt8438824218]] describes the short white dress and absence of hat, gloves, and stockings.
- Media effect: [[364-sixties-fashion-swinging-london-part-2-glt8438824218]] says the incident made front pages in Australia and Britain and became linked to the miniskirt's rise.
- Interpretive limit: [[364-sixties-fashion-swinging-london-part-2-glt8438824218]] distinguishes the actual dress from the wider miniskirt symbolism attached to it.

## Qualifications

This profile is limited to one episode's use of the Melbourne appearance. It does not establish Shrimpton as the inventor or sole cause of shorter hemlines, nor does it cover her full modeling career.
