## Overview

太一 / Taiyi, written 太乙 in the Hanji 350-2 source summary, is a supreme celestial figure connected in the current sources to imperial ritual under both Qin and Western Han. Qinji 121 infers Taiyi as the likely target of the heaven-centered Ji Miao sacrifice; the Han sources describe [[MiuJiWesternHan|缪忌]]'s rank-and-precedent argument, a Ganquan altar with subordinate Five Emperors altars, Han Wudi's winter-solstice worship, and later regularization.

## Current Profile

The Qin and Han evidence differs in strength and function. Qinji 121 uses cosmology and spatial layout to infer that [[QinJimiao|极庙]] centered Taiyi within [[QinCosmicCapitalPlanning|秦宇宙秩序式都城规划]]. Hanji 259 part 2 gives the advocacy mechanism: Miu Ji ranks Taiyi above the Five Emperors and invokes an ancient emperor-led rite; Wudi first delegates worship, then builds at [[GanquanPalace|甘泉宫]] and officiates at winter solstice. Hanji 350-2 reports the mature altar hierarchy, sun-and-moon greeting, luminous-sign reports, and triennial observance. Together they show specialist argument becoming court institution, while overlap between the Han accounts and uncertainty about Qin-Han continuity prevent treating them as independent proof of one unchanged theology.

## Key Characteristics

- Supreme celestial figure used to connect imperial rule with a cosmic center rather than only an ancestral lineage.
- Inferred sacrificial target of Qin Ji Miao in Qinji 121 rather than an explicit claim of the base chronicle as presented by the source.
- Explicit altar recipient in Hanji 350-2's account of Han Wudi's Ganquan winter-solstice rite.
- Placed above Five Emperors altars in the Han ritual layout, expressing a hierarchy among celestial powers.
- Legitimated in the Hanji 259 account through claimed ancient precedent and exclusive imperial sponsorship.

## Evidence

- **Qin cosmic center:** [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-121-yingzheng-zao-shijie-zuizao-gaosu-gonglu-2200nian-huangcao-busheng-ls1u8dmuvs5bbrzadf2dtd00js-p|Qinji 121]] links Taiyi to Ji Miao, the heavenly center, Wei River/Milky Way analogy, and palace-road ordering around Xianyang.
- **Han altar and hierarchy:** [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-350-2-weishenme-buneng-re-dongshizhang-mishu-lmxrw9cg4lsqzzbx_628vwqtwfgu|Hanji 350-2]] says Wudi establishes a Taiyi altar at Ganquan with Five Emperors altars arranged below it.
- **Recurring imperial rite:** [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-350-2-weishenme-buneng-re-dongshizhang-mishu-lmxrw9cg4lsqzzbx_628vwqtwfgu|Hanji 350-2]] describes winter-solstice worship and Wudi's acceptance of a proposal attributed to [[SimaTanWesternHan|司马谈]] and Kuanshu for observance every three years.
- **Advocacy and institutional transition:** [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-259-hanwudi-wo-ba-ni-dang-dashi-ni-ba-wo-dang-shazi-2-lo4brmkuzpoozgszshrqz4hbgrgt|Hanji 259 part 2]] attributes Taiyi's superiority and ancient seasonal sacrifice to Miu Ji and follows Wudi from delegated worship to the Ganquan altar and personal officiation.

## Qualifications

Qinji 121's Taiyi identification is explicitly inferential. The two Han episodes overlap and should not be counted as independent confirmation of every altar detail. Hanji 350-2's light and yellow-vapor reports are evidence of court reporting and reception, not verified supernatural phenomena. Hanji 259 part 2 does not verify Miu Ji's ancient precedent, exact memorial, the 112 BCE chronology, or continuity through Emperor Ai. The supplied notes do not establish whether 太一 and 太乙 reflect a simple orthographic variant in every context, nor whether the Qin and Han institutions had direct organizational continuity.

## What Changed

- Added Miu Ji's hierarchy-and-antiquity argument as the proposal stage before the Ganquan rite.
- Distinguished delegated worship, personal imperial officiation, and later triennial regularization.
- Kept ancient precedent, chronology, luminous signs, and long institutional continuity source-scoped.
