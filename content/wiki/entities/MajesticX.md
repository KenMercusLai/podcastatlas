# Majestic X

## Overview

The Majestic X is the seized oil tanker used by the episode to illustrate the cost and due-process tension in disposing of captured maritime property.

## Current Profile

The Justice Department sought to sell the tanker and its oil soon after seizure, but a judge required notice to alleged owners and the Iranian National Oil Company before a rapid private sale. Victims of Iranian terrorism later sought proceeds and requested a jury trial. The episode reports that custody, maintenance, and repairs cost $42 million within roughly six weeks even though the vessel itself was valued below $3 million.

## Key Characteristics

- Central modern case for the gap between asset value and custody cost.
- Subject to competing ownership, victim-compensation, notice, and jury-trial claims.
- Still unresolved after nearly seven months at the source's recording date.
- Practical catalyst for interest in faster prize procedure, without itself proving that prize jurisdiction applies.

## Qualifications

All ownership, valuation, cost, sanctions, and claimant details are episode-reported. Delay is costly, but the case also demonstrates why notice and competing-property claims cannot be treated as mere administrative friction.

## What Changed

- Created the profile as the episode's concrete test case for seizure cost and procedural protection.
