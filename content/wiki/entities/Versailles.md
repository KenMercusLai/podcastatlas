# Versailles

## Overview

Versailles was the royal palace and court system that concentrated French aristocratic life around the monarchy while making status, ceremony, surveillance, and gossip politically consequential.

## Current Profile

The source presents Versailles as both the physical expression of Bourbon absolutism and a social mechanism inherited from Louis XIV. Drawing nobles into court made them orbit royal power, but it also placed the royal family inside a dense factional environment. For the fourteen-year-old [[MarieAntoinette|Marie Antoinette]], strict hierarchy, monitored correspondence, ceremonial dress, and hostile courtiers made private preference difficult to separate from public role.

## Key Characteristics

- Monumental palace representing French monarchical power.
- Court system that concentrated nobles around the crown.
- Rigid hierarchy in which dress, speech, access, and acknowledgment carried political meaning.
- Information environment where gossip about the monarchy often originated inside the court.
- Setting whose formality made Marie Antoinette's search for privacy politically legible.
