# A Wonderful Country

## Overview

*A Wonderful Country* is an Israeli political-satire television program presented in [[the-satire-paradox-the-satire-paradox|The Satire Paradox]] as a model of comedy that makes its accusation clear and lets laughter give way to political pain.

## Current Profile

The episode highlights two sketches. A quiz-show parody changes “one versus 100” to “one versus 119,” with wrong answers by the prime minister making representations of soldiers killed in the 2006 Lebanon War disappear. A kindergarten sketch has children repeat adult arguments against peace negotiations, a two-state solution, and international criticism until inherited hopelessness becomes the emotional center.

Executive producer Muli Segev describes the second sketch's shift from laughter to sadness as intentional. Gladwell treats that transition as evidence of satirical courage: comedy draws the audience in, but clarity about political responsibility and human consequence prevents amusement from becoming a complete release.

## Key Characteristics

- Overtly political Israeli television satire associated in the episode with creators on the political left.
- Connects government decisions to deaths in war through a familiar quiz-show structure.
- Uses children repeating adult fatalism to expose the inheritance of political hopelessness.
- Designs a delayed emotional movement from laughter toward sadness and discomfort.
- Serves as Gladwell's contrast to satire that becomes ambiguous, affectionate, or mutually promotional.
