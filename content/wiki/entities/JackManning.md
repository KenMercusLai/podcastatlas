## Overview
Jack Manning is the researcher interviewed by [[MarketplaceTech]] about participant experiences with AI “generative ghosts” built from limited information about deceased loved ones.

## Current Profile
Manning's contribution is an interaction-design and safeguarding account of post-mortem AI. His study compared a third-person representation with a first-person reincarnation, then examined what made the conversations feel useful or inauthentic. He reports mostly positive, informative, or cathartic reactions in the supervised setting, while emphasizing that direct commercial deployment needs substantially more protection against unpredictable output, dependence, manipulation, and harm.

## Key Characteristics
- Studies user experience with conversational simulations of deceased people.
- Distinguishes archival representation from first-person reincarnation as materially different designs.
- Highlights relational communication style rather than treating factual accuracy as the only authenticity criterion.
- Treats family tradition and intergenerational knowledge as possible uses beyond acute grief.
- Maintains a strong boundary between researcher-mediated trials and unsupervised commercial products.

## Evidence
