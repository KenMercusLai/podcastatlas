## Overview

阴识 / Yin Shi is synthesized as a Guangwu-era maternal relative whose political value depends on public restraint, not simply family proximity. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-683-fangniuwa-liupenzi-buxiang-dang-huangdi-dan-meiyudi-lt7hmrorydsazpkuwby1ntd7t1eh|Hanji 683]] now supplies the earliest current layer: as [[YinLihua|阴丽华]]'s brother, Yin Shi refuses added fief after merit because the optics would make an outer relative look overrewarded among war heroes. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-721-ta-sha-jianu-ma-gongzhu-weihe-fanbei-shang-lryzcejdnrm4-92esnddfvyjkqli|Hanji 721]] then presents him as a careful, loyal model around Crown Prince Liu Zhuang; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-728-weihe-mingchen-nan-shanzhong-beihou-de-yuanyin-ni-gai-dong-ltjl2bgpyyz1bmwe-4mfcyd9e6cf|Hanji 728]] later makes his tutor candidacy the case that triggers [[CrownPrinceTutorPublicInterest|太子师傅公器化]].

## Current Profile

Hanji 683 places Yin Shi inside Liu Xiu's early merit-reward settlement. He is Yin Lihua's elder brother and already has merit that could justify added fief. He refuses, however, because the realm has only recently begun stabilizing and many battlefield commanders are visibly meritorious. If an imperial in-law accepts more land in that setting, he fears he cannot face the heroes of the realm.

That refusal makes Yin Shi useful as an outer-relative restraint case rather than a generic modesty anecdote. His point is not that he has no service; it is that kinship changes how reward is read by others. Refusal protects both his household reputation and Liu Xiu's reward order from the appearance that family access outranks public merit.

Hanji 721 presents Yin Shi before the later tutor-selection debate. He is Crown Prince Liu Zhuang's maternal uncle, is made acting zhijinwu, and is described as loyal, upright, and careful with official information. Liu Xiu uses him as an example for royal relatives and attendants because his conduct keeps public office distinct from careless private talk.

Hanji 728 reuses that same status in a sharper selection scene. When Liu Xiu asks who can serve as the crown prince's teacher, the officials and emperor converge on Yin Shi. His candidacy is plausible inside kinship and rank: he is the crown prince's maternal uncle, holds the zhijinwu office, and is Yuanlu Marquis.

The point is not that Yin Shi is personally unworthy. [[ZhangYiEasternHan|张义]] asks whether the crown prince is being established for the Yin family or for the realm. If for the Yin family, Yin Shi can be teacher; if for the realm, the court should choose worthy talent from the realm. Liu Xiu accepts the correction and appoints Zhang Yi instead.

## Key Characteristics

- Yin-family maternal relative whose reward choices are judged against public merit and outer-relative optics.
- Merit holder who refuses added fief because recent stabilization and visible war heroes make family-linked enlargement politically risky.
- Crown Prince Liu Zhuang's maternal uncle in the Guangwu court.
- Acting or holding zhijinwu office and Yuanlu Marquis status across the bounded sources.
- Loyal and upright official whose private speech is disciplined around public affairs.
- Plausible family-and-rank candidate for crown-prince tutor before a public-interest objection changes the decision.
- Not condemned personally by the tutor-selection source; the issue is the appointment criterion.

## Qualifications

This page is not a complete biography or a negative judgment on Yin Shi's competence. Hanji 683 says he has enough merit for added fief, so the refusal should be read as public-risk management rather than denial of all service. Hanji 721 makes him a positive maternal-kin model, while Hanji 728 says even a plausible relative needs a public-role justification for heir education. The transcript form 殷实 in Hanji 721 and Hanji 728 is normalized to 阴识 because the maternal-uncle, zhijinwu, and crown-prince context matches the existing route.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 683 as Yin Shi's early reward-restraint case around outer-relative optics.
- Reframed Yin Shi's public value as consistent across reward refusal, careful office conduct, and tutor-selection limits.
- Preserved Hanji 721 as the later positive profile: acting zhijinwu, disciplined private speech, and royal-relative model.
- Preserved Hanji 728's objection as a limit on selection criteria rather than a condemnation of Yin Shi.
