# Herodotus

## Overview

Herodotus is the Greek historian whose narrative underpins both the liberation-and-performance frame for early Athenian democracy and the recoverable account of Marathon, while also creating source-asymmetry and interpretation problems.

## Current Profile

The democracy episode opens from Herodotus' claim that Athenians became more effective after escaping tyranny. It uses that observation to frame 506 BC resistance and the relation among freedom, collective mobilization, and military performance, but then complicates any simple modern reading by reconstructing the sacred and exclusionary meaning of the demos.

The Marathon episode treats Herodotus as indispensable but not transparent. Tom Holland says Marathon is the first battle in European history that can be reconstructed in detail mainly because Herodotus describes it. That makes Herodotus the central evidence channel for Miltiades' decision, the Athenian attack, the fighting at the ships, and the later memory of the Persian Wars.

At the same time, the episode uses Herodotus dependence as a caution. There is no comparable surviving Persian account, and later traditions about Pheidippides, Pan, panic, and the victory announcement show how quickly military narrative becomes religious and civic memory. Herodotus therefore anchors the story while forcing the wiki to keep Greek narrative dominance explicit.

## Key Characteristics

- Herodotus is the main recoverable narrative source for the Marathon episode's battle reconstruction.
- His liberation claim gives the democracy episode its opening connection between political order and Athenian effectiveness.
- His account makes Marathon unusually detailed compared with many ancient battles.
- The source's dependence on Herodotus creates asymmetry because Persian voices are not comparably preserved.
- Marathon memory mixes battle narrative, civic identity, religious story, and later political interpretation.
- The episode treats Herodotus as evidence to use carefully rather than as a guarantee of settled certainty.
