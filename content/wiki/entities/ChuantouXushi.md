# 《穿透叙事》

## Overview
《穿透叙事》 is presented in the wiki as [[ZouPeixuan|邹佩轩]]'s book that completes a trilogy from financial reports to valuation to narrative. The [[Mianji|面基]] episode frames it as the work where valuation is finally reduced to quantified terminal narrative.

## Current Profile
The book's source-scoped argument is that narrative is not a vague story. It is a logical, falsifiable, and quantifiable market consensus about a company's terminal performance and the discount rate applied to that future.

Within the episode, the book sharpens several existing investing questions: why fast growth can disappoint, why good reported earnings may not lift a stock, why long bull-market winners often require repeated narrative upgrades, and why A-share liquidity can price optimistic expectations strongly when short selling is constrained.

## Key Characteristics
- It treats narrative as the final output of financial-report reading and valuation work.
- It defines useful narrative through logic, falsifiability, and quantification.
- It interprets DCF as a formal narrative about future cash flow and discount rate.
- It emphasizes terminal space over near-term speed of growth.
- It explains A-share movements through both fundamental narrative and auction-like liquidity mechanics.
- It treats case review as a way to validate and refine a unified investment framework.

## Qualifications
- This page reflects the podcast's presentation of the book, not a direct full-text reading.
- The source names only selected examples and does not reproduce the book's complete case evidence.
- The book's framework is strongest as an investment-research lens; it is not a mechanical trading signal.
- The source's A-share claims depend on a stylized view of market participation and may vary by stock, cycle, liquidity, and policy setting.
