# Simon de Montfort

## Overview

Simon de Montfort is the devout and exceptionally violent crusading commander who turned the opening of the [[AlbigensianCrusade]] into a prolonged war of occupation and territorial rule.

## Current Profile

After Béziers and Carcassonne, Simon became lord of both conquered cities. When most pilgrims completed their forty-day service and left, he held the occupation with a small core and recurring seasonal reinforcements. The episode pairs military skill and religious conviction with burnings, destruction, mutilation, and uncompromising conquest rather than treating devotion as a restraint on violence.

His victories expanded the war beyond an initial punitive expedition, but his regime remained vulnerable. The king of Aragon died opposing him at Muret, yet Toulouse resisted, and Simon was killed outside the city in 1218 by a siege-engine stone said to have been launched by women and girls. His son Amaury could not hold the conquests, creating the opening for direct French royal intervention.

## Key Characteristics

- Crusading commander and lord of conquered Béziers and Carcassonne.
- Presented as devout, tactically gifted, and extremely brutal.
- Sustained occupation through a small permanent force and recurring crusader arrivals.
- Oversaw a war of sieges, burned settlements, devastated land, and mass executions.
- Defeated Aragonese intervention but died during the siege of Toulouse.
- Left conquests his heir could not consolidate without royal power.

## Evidence

- Territorial command: [[303-the-bloodiest-crusade-glt7887097153]] places Simon in control after the capture of Carcassonne.
- Repeated violence: [[303-the-bloodiest-crusade-glt7887097153]] links his campaigns to burnings at Castres, Minerve, Lavaur, and other strongholds.
- Limits of rule: [[303-the-bloodiest-crusade-glt7887097153]] describes his 1218 death and Amaury's failure to preserve the inheritance.
