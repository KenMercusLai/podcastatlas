# 官渡之战 / Guandu Campaign

## Overview
官渡之战 / Guandu Campaign is currently represented in the wiki as a looming [[CaoCao|曹操]]-[[YuanShao|袁绍]] confrontation rather than as a fully narrated battle. The available sources map the preconditions: Cao Cao's eastern settlement after Lü Bu, southern constraint against [[LiuBiao|刘表]], Yuan Shao's northern-rear settlement after [[GongsunZan|公孙瓒]], Cao's [[ShequanLateHan|射犬]] operation, Yuan Shao's flawed war-council process, Cao-side analysis of Yuan's organization, forward deployment through [[LiyangYellowRiverLateHan|黎阳]], Zhang Xiu's renewed submission, Yang Fu's Cao/Yuan comparison, and Wei Ji's Guanzhong rear-governance plan.

## Current Profile
The campaign's current synthesis is that Guandu is prepared before it is fought. Cao Cao's position improves through territorial consolidation, selective incorporation of defeated and defected forces, pressure on potential Yuan Shao allies, practical attention to grain and population control, and conversion of analysis into deployment. Yuan Shao remains the larger northern rival and emerges from Gongsun Zan's fall with Qing, You, Bing, and Ji advantages, but he still needs Wuhuan and Youzhou rear settlement. The sources increasingly frame Cao Cao's advantage as organizational: decisive command, talent use, adviser compatibility, disciplined law, and capacity to turn surrender, salt revenue, refugees, and field placement into usable military and logistical strength.

## Key Characteristics
- Strategic horizon for Cao Cao's transition from regional survival to direct contest with Yuan Shao.
- Multi-front preparation problem involving Xuzhou/Qingzhou, Jingzhou, Zhang Xiu's Nanyang force, Guanzhong, the Yellow River line, Youzhou-Wuhuan rear security, and Guandu.
- Northern rear and border-contact problem after Gongsun Zan's fall, where Yuan Shao's Wuhuan settlement and Cao Cao's Shequan operation make the confrontation more immediate.
- Adviser-system contrast in which Yuan Shao has capable names but Cao Cao and Xun Yu produce clearer shared judgment.
- Defector-incorporation arena where former enemies can become manpower, legitimacy signals, and rear-security assets.
- Logistics-and-governance problem in which refugee settlement, agriculture, salt revenue, grain supply, and postwar exhaustion matter before battlefield contact.
- Deployment threshold where Cao secures Qingzhou through Zang Ba, places Yu Jin on the Yellow River bank, returns to Xudu, and arrays troops at Guandu.

## Evidence
- Eastern settlement: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1026-jiemi-sanguo-di-yi-mengjiang-lvbu-de-luomu-lhjf-agujuliglqchumtff3tftxg1|Hanji 1026]] links the fall of Lü Bu, the incorporation of Zhang Liao and local-force actors, and Qingzhou/Yuan Tan pressure to Cao Cao's pre-Guandu capacity.
- Southern constraint: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1027-jingzhou-zhizhu-liubiao-weihe-nan-cheng-bazhu-ll-ebguqllsf-5ycvpdogihzn3lj|Hanji 1027]] treats Liu Biao's Jingzhou position and the Zhang Xian-Huan Jie breakaway as a southern variable that can reduce Cao Cao's exposure to Yuan Shao.
- Northern rear and first border contact: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1032-duidai-pantu-caocao-weihe-renci-lmrr3c2f2dayectlwndt4p-katuy|Hanji 1032]] shows the post-Gongsun-Zan north splitting into Yuan Shao's Wuhuan settlement through [[TadunLateHan|蹋顿]] and [[YanRou|阎柔]], Tian Yu's advice that [[XianyuFu|鲜于辅]] submit to Cao Cao's court-backed order, and Cao Cao's Shequan operation that kills Sui Gu, captures Wei Zhong, and leaves Cao-Yuan territory directly touching.
- Yuan-camp deliberation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1034-yuanshao-de-baiju-zhi-yin-buken-ting-jushou-de-hua-log-wy2zvpdjopbdyexb0ijqmph4|Hanji 1034]] contrasts Ju Shou's recovery-and-harassment plan with Guo Tu and Shen Pei's flattering immediate-war advice, showing how Yuan's adviser abundance becomes factional weakness.
- Cao-side assessment and deployment: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1034-yuanshao-de-baiju-zhi-yin-buken-ting-jushou-de-hua-log-wy2zvpdjopbdyexb0ijqmph4|Hanji 1034]] has Cao Cao and Xun Yu diagnose Yuan's ruler, advisers, and generals before Cao moves through Liyang, Qingzhou, the Yellow River bank, Xudu, and Guandu.
- Zhang Xiu incorporation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1035-zhansha-caocao-qinzi-he-aijiang-ta-jing-zaidu-guijiang-caocao-lh10wwkwcqw5hnap3bodw6oxpt7v|Hanji 1035]] presents Zhang Xiu's renewed submission as both the removal of a potential spoiler and the gain of a strong force before Guandu.
- Guanzhong rear base: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1035-zhansha-caocao-qinzi-he-aijiang-ta-jing-zaidu-guijiang-caocao-lh10wwkwcqw5hnap3bodw6oxpt7v|Hanji 1035]] connects Yang Fu's Cao/Yuan comparison and Wei Ji's salt-funded agricultural recovery to rear order and grain support.

## Qualifications
The battle itself has not yet been narrated by the current bounded sources, so tactical battlefield claims should remain out of this page. Cao Cao and Xun Yu's assessment of Yuan Shao is source-bounded and should not be treated as a complete neutral audit. Hanji 1034 also qualifies the simple "Yuan ignored Ju Shou" explanation by making advice framing and factional meeting dynamics part of the causal field. Hanji 1032's transcript phrase "称帝后的袁绍" is kept source-scoped because adjacent sources treat Yuan Shu, not Yuan Shao, as the self-declared emperor.

## What Changed
- Added Hanji 1032 as the missing bridge between Gongsun Zan's fall and the later Yuan Shu / Yuan Shao pre-Guandu episodes.
- Added Yuan Shao's Wuhuan-Youzhou rear settlement as a campaign precondition.
- Added Cao Cao's Shequan operation and Wei Zhong pardon as Cao-side border contact and talent incorporation before Guandu.
