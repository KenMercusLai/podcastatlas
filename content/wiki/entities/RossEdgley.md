# Ross Edgley

## Overview
Ross Edgley is a British ultra-endurance athlete presented in [[scim9489463983-scim9489463983]] through long-distance swims around Great Britain and Iceland, a nonstop Yukon River swim, and other deliberately difficult projects.

## Current Profile
Edgley's account combines unusual tolerance for repetitive effort with an increasingly explicit safety and purpose filter. He describes early difficulty-seeking and competition, then a shift from records and prestige toward conservation, family, service, and meaningful teamwork. His strongest practical contribution is not limitless toughness: it is the recognition that motives expire, judgment degrades under exhaustion, and trusted teammates must sometimes override the person in the water.

The profile remains source-scoped. His endurance stories illustrate [[FlexibleMotivationalPortfolio]], [[DelegatedEnduranceStopRules]], and [[StrengthReserveForEndurance]], but they do not establish universal psychology, neuroscience, training doses, or medical safety.

## Key Characteristics
- Seeks difficult endurance projects and treats struggle itself as a source of meaning.
- Switches among commitment, competition, love, affiliation, gratitude, enjoyment, and service as motivational conditions change.
- Describes expedition success as a collective achievement dependent on crew, specialists, medical judgment, and family support.
- Uses mythology, Stoicism, and third-person self-talk to organize effort and create distance from immediate suffering.
- Acknowledges that refusal to stop can be a defect and delegates removal authority when his own judgment becomes unsafe.
- Continues maximal-strength training to reduce the relative cost of repetitive swimming strokes.
- Reports fatherhood as raising the threshold for accepting risk and increasing the need for a purpose beyond records.
