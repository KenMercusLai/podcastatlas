# 阜陵王刘延 / Liu Yan (Fuling King, Eastern Han)

## Overview

阜陵王刘延 / Liu Yan appears in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-762-lishi-gaosu-ni-nanren-youquan-neng-you-duo-huangtang-limdo2hfd8epcfkwwsrmzz9no0dj|Hanji 762]] as an Eastern Han imperial prince whose long career is repeatedly shadowed by prophecy and rebellion-linked accusations. He is kept distinct from [[LiuYanDongCommandery|Liu Yan of Dong Commandery]] and [[LiuYanLateHan|Liu Yan (late Han warlord)]].

## Current Profile

Liu Yan is presented as a son of [[LiuXiu|Emperor Guangwu]] and Consort Guo who is first enfeoffed as King of Huaiyang. Under [[EmperorMingOfHan|Emperor Ming]], he becomes implicated in prophecy and rebellion-associated writings. Emperor Ming spares his life but moves him from Huaiyang to Fuling, showing punishment without dynastic execution.

The pattern repeats under [[EmperorZhangOfHan|Emperor Zhang]] when Liu Yan and his son Liu Fang are accused again. Emperor Zhang again spares them, demotes Liu Fang, restricts retainers, and later restores Liu Yan's title and territory. Liu Yan dies after fifty-one years as king and receives the posthumous name Zhi.

## Key Characteristics

- Same-surname Eastern Han prince whose career spans repeated imperial reigns.
- Repeatedly associated with prophecy, forbidden writings, and rebellion suspicion.
- Receives limited penalties rather than execution under both Emperor Ming and Emperor Zhang.
- Moves from Huaiyang to Fuling as part of a managed royal-house punishment.
- Serves as an example of royal-house discipline short of extermination.
