## Overview

赵合德 / Zhao Hede enters this wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-573-zhaofeiyan-jiemei-shi-hongyanhuoshui-haishi-tizuigaoyang-lkacfndvi8t1opep67xmquyzr_ww|Hanji 573]] as [[HanChengdi|汉成帝]]'s favored consort and the central named target in accusations that Chengdi and Hede repeatedly killed imperial sons. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-536-1-renxing-huanghou-zhaofeiyan-ruhe-mingmu-zhangdan-gaowaiyu-llxo0my5ngp-mnbvjbl92yod1x2n|Hanji 536-1]] adds the earlier favor-and-protection layer: Zhao Hede receives the lavish Zhaoyang Palace, defends [[ZhaoFeiyanWesternHan|赵飞燕]] against adultery reports, and shares the childlessness insecurity that makes other pregnancies politically dangerous. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-571-hanchengdi-qinshou-shazi-shi-zhen-shi-jia-lhjrwpg-t6izp__tshwzem1t0dyzh|Hanji 571]] supplies the [[CaoGongWesternHan|曹宫]] case narrative, while [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-572-lishishang-zhe-nanren-zhen-gou-hen-lkd0wugllcr6ewmmz6lyximyzl6c|Hanji 572]] supplies the [[XuMeirenWesternHan|徐美人]] sealed-box case. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-561-cancancan-jiemi-chi-cuiqingyao-si-de-huangdi-no-1-ljwoku7zvutdfitvtb3n0-9bvneo|Hanji 561]] adds the immediate death-room and investigation context: Zhao Hede is beside Chengdi when he suddenly collapses, then kills herself after Wang Zhengjun orders a formal inquiry. The received suspicion is historically important but remains evidentially unstable in the wiki.

## Current Profile

Hanji 536-1 makes Zhao Hede active before the later missing-child cases. The source says Chengdi gives her the unusually ornate Zhaoyang Palace and favors her more than Zhao Feiyan. When Chengdi hints that he has heard reports about Zhao Feiyan's hidden men, Zhao Hede cries, frames the reports as slander, and wins Chengdi's promise not to believe them; afterward, informers are reportedly executed. The episode then says Zhao Feiyan rewards Zhao Hede with a male favorite and that the sisters' shared childlessness drives fear of rival pregnancies.

Hanji 571 makes the later accusation field concrete. In [[XieGuangWesternHan|谢光]]'s report, Zhao Zhaoyi's driver asks whether [[CaoGongWesternHan|曹宫]]'s newborn boy has died, conveys anger that the child is still alive, later delivers pills that kill Cao Gong, and the six attendants say Zhao Zhaoyi knows they are innocent but will not let them live. The episode identifies the immediate post-death rumor around Zhao Hede and her suicide, but it stops before fully adjudicating the title question.

Hanji 572 adds a second concrete branch. Zhao Hede confronts Chengdi over Xu Meiren's child, framing the birth as betrayal and as a possible Xu-family revival. The report describes Zhao Hede's self-harm, crying, hunger strike, and demand to return home as pressure on Chengdi, followed by the letter, heavy box, private handling, and sealed burial chain. [[WuQiuzunWesternHan|武秋尊]]'s later claim that Yeting officials were Zhao Hede's people expands the accusation into a broader palace-control picture, though the host flags the disclosure to [[JiWuWesternHan|纪武]] as puzzling.

Hanji 573 then treats that received narrative critically rather than erasing it. It focuses on how Zhao Hede becomes the figure onto whom Chengdi's sudden death, repeated child loss, and late Western Han succession anxiety can be concentrated. The source acknowledges that the received texts make her suspicious, then presses against the case by asking whether the evidence is too testimony-dependent and whether [[WangZhengjun|王政君]]'s political interests make unchecked heir-killing implausible.

Hanji 561 sharpens why the post-death blame could harden so quickly. Zhao Hede is the person physically present at Chengdi's sudden morning collapse, and the court investigation asks about Chengdi's daily conduct and death circumstances. The episode also recounts the aphrodisiac-pill tradition around Chengdi's death, but marks those details as rumor or non-standard-history material. Its political reading is clearer than its medical one: a court needing a public explanation for a shameful sudden death can make the politically weak favorite bear the burden.

The profile therefore remains qualified. Zhao Hede is neither exonerated nor treated as the sole cause of Chengdi's death or no-heir outcome. She is a case where palace intimacy, accusation, missing evidence, political explanation, and later "red-beauty disaster" narration overlap.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han favored consort whose lavish palace and practical favor exceed Zhao Feiyan's formal-empress advantage.
- Active shield for Zhao Feiyan in Hanji 536-1, turning adultery reports into slander in Chengdi's eyes.
- Source-scoped suspect in the `Han Shu` and `Zizhi Tongjian` tradition, not a fully adjudicated actor in this wiki.
- Present at Chengdi's sudden morning collapse and then exposed as the immediate target of post-death rumor and investigation.
- Accused through reported palace chains involving Cao Gong's missing infant and Xu Meiren's sealed box.
- Convenient blame target after Chengdi's sudden death and Han Aidi's anti-Zhao-family incentives.
- Helps expose how palace accusations can combine favor, testimony, political need, and moralized storytelling.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 571, Hanji 572, Hanji 573, Hanji 561, and Hanji 536-1. Hanji 536-1 supplies an early scandal-and-favor layer; Hanji 571-572 supply grave accusation narratives; Hanji 561 supplies the death-room and political-blame context; and Hanji 573 warns against treating the combined narrative as fully settled. The page does not settle Zhao Hede's guilt, the full legal record, Chengdi's exact death cause, or the medical explanation for the deaths of Chengdi's children. Hanji 536-1's fertility-drug and reproductive-interference claims, Hanji 561's aphrodisiac-pill tradition, and Hanji 573's family-health argument all remain source-scoped rather than proof.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 536-1's earlier practical-favor and sister-protection layer: Zhao Hede's palace luxury, emotional defense of Zhao Feiyan, and shared childlessness now precede the later accusation field.
- Preserved Hanji 573's caution against letting scandal narration become a complete explanation for Chengdi's heir failure.
