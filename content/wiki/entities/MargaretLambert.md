# Margaret Lambert

## Overview

Margaret Lambert, born Gretel Bergmann in 1914, was a German Jewish high jumper whose coerced candidacy and exclusion from Berlin 1936 expose the gap between promised Olympic equality and politically controlled access to training and selection.

## Current Profile

[[hitler-s-olympics-part-5-the-amateur-s-hour-hitler-s-olympics-part-5-the-amateur-s-hour|Hitler’s Olympics, Part 5]] follows Lambert from athletic prominence through expulsion from her club after Hitler came to power. Nazi authorities later pressured her to train and threatened her family because her visible candidacy could answer foreign criticism that Jewish athletes were barred from the Games.

Her participation was conditional and instrumental rather than equal. She trained without the facilities and competition available to non-Jewish athletes, yet equaled the German high-jump record shortly before the Games. Authorities then excluded her on a false claim of poor recent performance. Lambert concluded that her candidacy had always been a propaganda device, and she sought a visa to leave Germany as the Games opened.

## Key Characteristics

- Elite German high jumper expelled from organized sport because she was Jewish.
- Pressured to pursue Olympic selection under threat to her family.
- Denied preparation and competition equivalent to non-Jewish athletes.
- Equaled the German high-jump record shortly before Berlin 1936.
- Excluded through a false performance rationale after serving the regime's international image.
- Used her experience to identify nominal inclusion as controlled propaganda.
