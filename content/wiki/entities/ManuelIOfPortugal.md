# Manuel I of Portugal

## Overview

Manuel I of Portugal was the monarch who commissioned [[VascoDaGama|Vasco da Gama's]] voyage to India and gave Portuguese expansion an expansive commercial, royal, and Christian mission.

## Current Profile

The source presents Manuel as the ruler under whom Portuguese ambition widened from route-finding to claims of global lordship. He sought spices, a blow against Islamic trading power, advantage over Venice, and the spread of Christianity, then used da Gama's return to advertise Portugal's success against Spain's western gamble.

His court turned empire into spectacle through titles, exotic animals, architecture, religious foundations, and Lisbon's growth as a global entrepôt. These symbols of [[PortugueseMaritimeExpansion|maritime achievement]] existed alongside the coercion and massacres used to secure the network.

## Key Characteristics

- Monarch who selected da Gama for the first Portuguese voyage to India.
- Ruler joining spice commerce to anti-Muslim war and Christian universalism.
- Dynast who expanded royal titles to claim authority over distant conquest and trade.
- Patron associated with Manueline monuments and Lisbon's imperial display.
- Court ruler who used returning goods and animals to stage global prestige.

## Evidence

- Strategic commission: [[228-portugal-the-golden-age-of-discovery-part-2-glt7949934800]] connects Manuel's choice of da Gama to spices, India, Christian mission, and competition with Venice and Islam.
- Royal claims: [[228-portugal-the-golden-age-of-discovery-part-2-glt7949934800]] says Manuel enlarged his titles after the voyage and wrote to the Pope about spreading Christianity and defeating Islam.
- Imperial display: [[228-portugal-the-golden-age-of-discovery-part-2-glt7949934800]] links his Lisbon court to the elephant procession, Belém Tower, the Jerónimos Monastery, and imported goods.

## Qualifications

The source emphasizes militant ambition and spectacle rather than offering a complete reign. Exact wording of titles and papal correspondence, personal motive, court decision-making, and the relationship between royal policy and commanders' violence remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Established Manuel as the royal organizer and public impresario of the India-route empire.
