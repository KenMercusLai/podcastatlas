# Olympe de Gouges

## Overview

Olympe de Gouges was a French playwright, abolitionist, and revolutionary writer whose [[DeclarationOfTheRightsOfWoman|Declaration of the Rights of Woman and of the Female Citizen]] challenged women's exclusion from supposedly universal rights.

## Current Profile

The episode presents de Gouges as an intellectually independent revolutionary shaped by outsider status, literary self-making, abolitionism, and unusually broad compassion. She supported the Revolution but resisted its simplest factional loyalties: she opposed slavery, defended women's political equality, pitied the poor and animals, and retained sympathy for [[LouisXVI|Louis XVI]]. Her declaration converted the Revolution's own rights language into an indictment of male-only citizenship.

## Key Characteristics

- She came from Languedoc to Paris and entered the Palais-Royal intellectual and political world.
- She became a novelist and playwright despite limited early literacy and speaking Occitan before French.
- Her play *L'Esclavage des Noirs* attacked slavery and met organized hostility associated with slave-trade interests.
- She combined revolutionary commitment with political independence and compassion across factional boundaries.
- Her declaration demanded that universal rights include women as citizens.
- Dedicating the declaration to Marie Antoinette made the intervention especially risky.

## Qualifications

- “First feminist” is the episode's framing and should not be treated as a settled claim of historical priority.
- The source is a narrative podcast and does not reconstruct the complete publication history, reception, or wording of every work.
- Her compassion and radicalism are interpretive characterizations, not evidence that all of her positions fit one later ideological program.
