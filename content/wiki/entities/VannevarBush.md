# Vannevar Bush

## Overview
Vannevar Bush appears in [[episode-224-bushi-bushi-bushi-bushi-8213451338-252768|Episode 224: 不是不识不时不适]] through the essay "As We May Think" and the [[Memex]] idea, which the host uses to connect literacy, knowledge overload, association, hypertext, and AI.

## Current Profile
The source presents Bush as a hinge between print-era knowledge organization and machine-supported association. After literacy trains abstraction and classification, Bush's problem is that books, indexes, and libraries struggle under knowledge explosion; his proposed response is an information system that follows associative trails closer to human memory.

## Key Characteristics
- Represents the episode's transition from text-shaped cognition to machine-supported knowledge connection.
- Frames knowledge overload as a retrieval and relation problem, not only an accumulation problem.
- Grounds [[Memex]] as an ancestor of hypertext, knowledge-work systems, and the web in the host's account.
- Provides the pre-AI contrast: Bush's machine stores and displays human-made associations, while AI now generates many associations itself.

## Evidence
- Knowledge explosion: [[episode-224-bushi-bushi-bushi-bushi-8213451338-252768]] presents Bush as asking how postwar knowledge can be reconnected and made usable.
- Associative trails: [[episode-224-bushi-bushi-bushi-bushi-8213451338-252768]] describes Bush's view that human memory works by association rather than alphabetic or rigid catalog order.
- AI contrast: [[episode-224-bushi-bushi-bushi-bushi-8213451338-252768]] contrasts Bush's human-led association with recommendation systems and LLMs that increasingly propose links for users.

## Qualifications
The source treats Bush through the episode's media-theory lineage. It does not independently verify the full history from Memex to hypertext, Engelbart systems, and the web; those remain source-scoped connections.
