# British Legion

## Overview

The British Legion is the veterans' organization founded in 1921 that the episode connects to ex-servicemen's welfare and the first mass British remembrance-poppy appeal.

## Current Profile

[[118-end-of-the-first-world-war-remembrance-glt2743125909]] places the Legion at the junction of symbol and material need. Its adoption of the poppy transformed a poetic and transnational commemorative idea into mass public participation, while sales supported veterans in a postwar society marked by injury, unemployment, poverty, and disappointment over promised reconstruction.

The organization therefore shows that remembrance was not only ceremonial. Public commemoration and practical obligations to survivors developed together, even when ritual attention could not solve the scale of postwar hardship.

## Key Characteristics

- Veterans' organization founded in 1921.
- Associated in the episode with Douglas Haig and postwar fundraising.
- Adopted and scaled the British poppy appeal.
- Linked commemoration to support for injured and impoverished ex-servicemen.

## Evidence

- Formation and welfare: [[118-end-of-the-first-world-war-remembrance-glt2743125909]] connects the Legion's founding to fundraising for injured servicemen.
- Poppy appeal: [[118-end-of-the-first-world-war-remembrance-glt2743125909]] says Anna Guerin brought the idea to the Legion and that its first sale reached mass scale.

## Qualifications

This profile is limited to the episode's 1921 remembrance context. It does not cover the organization's full merger history, royal charter, later programs, finances, policy work, membership, or present institutional form.
