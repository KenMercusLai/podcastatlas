## Overview

梁孝王刘武 / Liu Wu is a Western Han vassal king, son of Empress Dowager Dou and younger full brother of [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]]. The current evidence presents his Rebellion-of-the-Seven-States service as both valuable political credit and the precondition for later overreach: military contribution increases prestige, while emperor-like protocol turns brotherly favor into a sovereign-subject crisis that [[HanAnguoWesternHan|韩安国]] must mediate.

## Current Profile

Both supplied sources credit Liu Wu's Liang forces with important resistance during the Rebellion of the Seven States. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-338-1-mei-tianli-shasi-shangbairen-ta-jing-bu-bei-pan-si-lggp0q9ka9jhb9pej9z7w1ue5lod|Hanji 338-1]] says Liang holds out for more than three months and buys time for the central counterattack. The episode then shows that remembered service outlives him: when officials seek execution of his son [[LiuPengliJidongKingWesternHan|刘彭离]] for more than one hundred known killings, the host treats Liu Wu's contribution, together with royal kinship, as part of [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s choice of deposition and exile instead.

The same prestige also creates immediate political danger. After the rebellion, Liu Wu reportedly becomes proud and uses travel or court ceremony comparable to the emperor's. Jingdi's anger and refusal to receive the Liang envoy show that conduct presented as family-favor display can also be read as a challenge to hierarchy and loyalty. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-266-sihui-furan-yu-yi-pao-niao-de-gushi-1-lrdhzy9cykdnjgoi90h95atugnxw|Hanji 266]] supplies this crisis layer.

Han Anguo's intervention preserves the distinction between explanation and excuse. Through [[LiuPiaoWesternHan|馆陶长公主刘嫖]], he presents Liu Wu's behavior as excessive boasting about imperial and dowager affection rather than non-subject ambition. Jingdi accepts the less threatening reading, but Han still warns Liu Wu that continued dependence on sibling feeling can lead to destruction. Liu Wu's current profile therefore combines military credit, royal intimacy, protocol overreach, and the continuing need for [[KinshipProtocolBoundary|亲情名分边界]].

## Key Characteristics

- Son of Empress Dowager Dou and younger full brother of Han Jingdi.
- Western Han ruler of Liang credited with prolonged resistance during the Rebellion of the Seven States.
- Prince whose wartime contribution produces prestige and durable family political credit.
- Royal actor whose emperor-like protocol is treated as an overreach capable of triggering sovereign suspicion.
- Beneficiary of Han Anguo's motive-reframing mediation and target of his warning about legal and rank limits.
- Father of Liu Pengli, whose later punishment is presented as mitigated partly by Liu Wu's remembered service.

## Evidence

Rebellion-era service:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-338-1-mei-tianli-shasi-shangbairen-ta-jing-bu-bei-pan-si-lggp0q9ka9jhb9pej9z7w1ue5lod|Hanji 338-1]] credits Liang's resistance with supporting suppression of the Rebellion of the Seven States.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-266-sihui-furan-yu-yi-pao-niao-de-gushi-1-lrdhzy9cykdnjgoi90h95atugnxw|Hanji 266]] likewise makes Liu Wu's rebellion contribution the background to his later prestige.

Court overreach and mediation:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-266-sihui-furan-yu-yi-pao-niao-de-gushi-1-lrdhzy9cykdnjgoi90h95atugnxw|Hanji 266]] says Liu Wu adopts emperor-like protocol, angers Jingdi, benefits from Han Anguo's indirect mediation, and receives a warning that affection cannot indefinitely protect improper conduct.

Inherited political credit:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-338-1-mei-tianli-shasi-shangbairen-ta-jing-bu-bei-pan-si-lggp0q9ka9jhb9pej9z7w1ue5lod|Hanji 338-1]] invokes Liu Wu's service when explaining why Han Wudi does not execute Liu Pengli despite officials' request.

## Qualifications

This remains a bounded profile rather than a full biography. The precise siege, duration, military contribution, succession position, Liang administration, and wider relationship with Jingdi require broader evidence. Hanji 266's court protocol, audience sequence, words, motives, rewards, and successful-causation story remain episode-attributed; Han Anguo's defense is a strategic interpretation, not independent proof that Liu Wu had no succession ambition. Hanji 338-1's causal link between the father's merit and the son's clemency is an interpretation of Wudi's refusal to execute, not a surviving formal judgment quoted in full.

## What Changed

- Added the post-rebellion transition from military credit to protocol overreach and sovereign suspicion.
- Added Han Anguo's mediation while preserving the distinction between reduced threat perception and continued fault.
- Connected Liu Wu's immediate benefit from royal affection with the later inherited clemency attributed to his service.

## Relationships

- [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]] - elder full brother and emperor whose throne Liu Wu's resistance supports and whose hierarchy his later display strains.
- [[HanAnguoWesternHan|韩安国]] - Liang adviser who mediates the crisis and warns Liu Wu about the limit of family protection.
- [[LiuPiaoWesternHan|馆陶长公主刘嫖]] - imperial-family intermediary through whom Han's explanation reaches Jingdi and the dowager.
- [[LiuPengliJidongKingWesternHan|济东王刘彭离]] - son whose requested execution is mitigated in part through the father's remembered service.
- [[RoyalKinshipPenaltyMitigation|宗室亲缘减刑]] - pattern through which Liu Wu's merit is presented as benefiting his son.
- [[KinshipProtocolBoundary|亲情名分边界]] - principle showing why family favor can soften conflict without canceling princely duty.
