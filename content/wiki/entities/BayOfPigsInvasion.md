# Bay of Pigs Invasion

## Overview

The Bay of Pigs invasion was the failed April 1961 landing in Cuba by anti-Castro exiles backed by the CIA and inherited by [[JohnFKennedy|John F. Kennedy]]'s new administration.

## Current Profile

The episode treats the operation as both an early foreign-policy failure and a concrete source of hostility toward Kennedy. Planners expected an exile landing to trigger a wider anti-Castro uprising, but the operation collapsed within roughly three days. Kennedy refused greater U.S. air escalation once failure was clear, leaving invaders killed or captured and giving exile communities a specific reason to regard him as having abandoned them.

That grievance matters to the series' assassination inquiry, but the source keeps motive separate from proof. Kennedy continued anti-Castro activity, including Operation Mongoose, and the later episodes reject the leap from Cuban-exile anger to an established assassination conspiracy.

## Key Characteristics

- CIA-supported exile invasion inherited by the Kennedy administration.
- Operation premised on a broader Cuban uprising that did not materialize.
- Rapid military failure followed by Kennedy's refusal to escalate U.S. air involvement.
- Source of lasting anti-Kennedy resentment among some anti-Castro exiles.
- Motive-bearing historical context that does not by itself establish conspiracy.

## Qualifications

This page rests on one retrospective podcast episode and is not an operational history of invasion planning, command decisions, casualty totals, Cuban responses, or later covert action. The source establishes a plausible grievance, not participation by any exile group in Kennedy's assassination. Precise responsibility within the Eisenhower administration, Kennedy administration, CIA, exile leadership, and military remains outside the bounded evidence.
