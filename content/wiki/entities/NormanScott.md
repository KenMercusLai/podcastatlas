# Norman Scott

## Overview

Norman Scott, earlier known as Norman Joseph, is the former stable worker whose relationship with [[JeremyThorpe|Jeremy Thorpe]], retained correspondence, persistent requests for help, and survival of a failed shooting became central to the Thorpe scandal.

## Current Profile

[[65-a-very-british-scandal-glt1741780940]] presents Scott as emotionally distressed, economically insecure, and frequently dismissed as chaotic or unreliable. He met Thorpe around 1961 at Kingham stables, sought money and help obtaining a replacement national insurance card, and attached much greater emotional significance to their relationship than the episode believes Thorpe did.

Scott's retained letters gave his account potential corroboration, but stigma and his public presentation made his allegations easy for powerful people to discount. That changed when [[AndrewNewton|Andrew Newton]] drove him onto Exmoor, killed his Great Dane Rinka, pointed the gun at Scott, and failed to fire because it jammed. Scott's survival transformed an apparently implausible story into evidence demanding investigation, even though later newspaper arrangements complicated witness credibility at trial.

## Key Characteristics

- Former stable worker who met Thorpe around 1961 and later sought financial and administrative help from him.
- Retained letters and notes that could support his account of their relationship.
- Was treated as unstable and unreliable in ways that weakened attention to his claims.
- Persisted in making allegations as Thorpe's political prominence increased.
- Survived Newton's failed shooting after witnessing the killing of Rinka.

## Evidence

- Relationship and dependence: [[65-a-very-british-scandal-glt1741780940]] describes the Kingham meeting, Scott's national-insurance-card problem, small payments, promises, and growing sense of betrayal.
- Documentary leverage: [[65-a-very-british-scandal-glt1741780940]] says Scott retained letters and notes whose political danger increased with Thorpe's rise.
- Failed attack: [[65-a-very-british-scandal-glt1741780940]] recounts Newton killing Rinka, putting the gun to Scott's head, and abandoning him when the weapon failed.
- Credibility boundary: [[65-a-very-british-scandal-glt1741780940]] says the attack made Scott harder to dismiss while later press payments weakened the prosecution's presentation.

## Qualifications

This profile comes from one retrospective podcast narrative, not Scott's own complete testimony, medical history, correspondence, or the full trial record. Descriptions of him as damaged, chaotic, fixated, or emotionally dependent are the hosts' characterizations and should not be treated as diagnoses or as reasons to discount his victimization. The source does not settle every detail of the relationship or every request he made of Thorpe.
