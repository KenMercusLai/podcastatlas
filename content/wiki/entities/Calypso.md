# Calypso

## Overview

Calypso is the island nymph who holds [[Odysseus]] on Ogygia in [[TheOdyssey]], as summarized in [[688-the-odyssey-return-of-the-king-part-2-glt2882541084]].

## Current Profile

In this episode, Calypso marks the stalled-homecoming problem. Odysseus has lost his ships and men and has spent seven years on her island, a place described as outside ordinary geography and time. When Zeus sends Hermes, Calypso must let him go, but she first offers immortality if he will stay.

## Key Characteristics

- Calypso's island is a suspension point where Odysseus remains alive but outside home, time, and social recognition.
- Her offer of immortality makes Odysseus' commitment to mortal homecoming explicit.
- Her release of Odysseus depends on divine command rather than voluntary surrender.
- She provides practical departure support by allowing Odysseus to build a boat and by giving provisions.

## Evidence

- Stalled return: [[688-the-odyssey-return-of-the-king-part-2-glt2882541084]] says Calypso has held Odysseus for seven years while he longs for Penelope.
- Immortality contrast: [[688-the-odyssey-return-of-the-king-part-2-glt2882541084]] frames Calypso's offer as a rejected alternative to mortal marriage and home.
- Compelled release: [[688-the-odyssey-return-of-the-king-part-2-glt2882541084]] has Zeus send Hermes to order Calypso to release Odysseus.
- Departure assistance: [[688-the-odyssey-return-of-the-king-part-2-glt2882541084]] says she permits boat building and gives provisions after accepting the command.

## Qualifications

This page records Calypso's role in the episode's homecoming structure. It does not settle broader mythological traditions about Calypso beyond this retelling.
