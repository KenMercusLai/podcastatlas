# HMS Endeavour

## Overview

HMS Endeavour was the sturdy, armed but non-warship vessel commanded by [[JamesCook]] on the voyage that observed the transit of Venus at Tahiti, charted New Zealand, reached eastern Australia, and returned to Britain in 1771.

## Current Profile

From departure, the ship functions as mobile observatory, collecting platform, provisioned workplace, armed authority, trading community, and vector of disease risk. It carries a multinational crew, Banks's scientific party and equipment, instruments, animals, food, guns, and sealed imperial instructions. During contact, it also brings nails, muskets, sexual demand, punishment, and internal theft. The first-contact frame depends on this dual character: the Endeavour brings observation and exchange in a form backed by superior firepower and a distant Crown.

Its near-loss on the Great Barrier Reef also keeps contingency visible. British claims and later historical consequences depended on a vessel that could easily have been wrecked before completing the voyage.

## Key Characteristics

- Mobile platform for astronomy, natural history, cartography, language study, and collecting.
- Crowded logistical system carrying crew, scientific personnel, instruments, provisions, animals, weapons, and expensive private equipment.
- Hierarchical shipboard society governed through Cook's strict discipline.
- Armed contact environment whose muskets and Marines shape negotiation.
- Trading system in which iron and nails become high-value exchange goods and sources of theft.
- Vulnerable wooden vessel nearly destroyed on the Great Barrier Reef and later hit by severe disease losses.
