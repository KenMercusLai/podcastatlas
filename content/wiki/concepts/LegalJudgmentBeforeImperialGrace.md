# Legal Judgment Before Imperial Grace / 先法度后恩宥

## Definition

Legal judgment before imperial grace / 先法度后恩宥 is the governance pattern where officials first process a protected person under public law, then leave room for the ruler to soften the final outcome through clemency after the legal boundary has been established.

## Current Synthesis

The concept enters through [[ShiBiLateHan|史弼]]'s confidential memorial about [[LiuKuiBohaiKing|刘亏]] in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-864-mingchen-zhihou-weihe-cansi-yu-diwang-zhishou-lqwexx6nchx10roxsrvh8c0ktkfq|Hanji 864]]. Liu Kui is Huan's brother and a Bohai king, so ordinary officials are reluctant to report or correct him. Shi Bi's proposal avoids two simpler failures: ignoring royal misconduct because of kinship, or demanding irreversible punishment that would force the emperor to appear heartless toward his brother.

The proposed sequence separates institutional roles. Ministers and legal officials make the offense public and adjudicate it; only afterward does the emperor use pardon or special grace to preserve familial mercy and the kingdom. The source treats this as politically skillful because it lets law, imperial benevolence, and dynastic kinship coexist without making any one of them erase the others.

## Key Claims

- Kinship-protected misconduct becomes harder to correct when officials believe legal reporting itself is disrespectful to the ruler's family.
- A law-first sequence lets the state mark a boundary before the ruler performs mercy.
- Clemency is more institutionally legible after judgment than before judgment, because it does not deny that an offense existed.
- The approach protects the ruler's image by separating ministerial enforcement from sovereign grace.
- The pattern remains only a proposed repair if the ruler ignores the memorial.

## Evidence

- Royal-protection problem: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-864-mingchen-zhihou-weihe-cansi-yu-diwang-zhishou-lqwexx6nchx10roxsrvh8c0ktkfq|Hanji 864]] says Liu Kui relies on being Huan's younger brother, behaves arrogantly and unlawfully, and is not effectively corrected by ordinary local or kingdom officials.
- Public-law request: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-864-mingchen-zhihou-weihe-cansi-yu-diwang-zhishou-lqwexx6nchx10roxsrvh8c0ktkfq|Hanji 864]] says Shi Bi asks Huan to allow the memorial to become public and to handle Liu Kui under law.
- Grace-after-judgment sequence: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-864-mingchen-zhihou-weihe-cansi-yu-diwang-zhishou-lqwexx6nchx10roxsrvh8c0ktkfq|Hanji 864]] says Shi Bi proposes that after judgment Huan can issue a special pardon to preserve law, imperial favor, and Bohai.
- Missed repair: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-864-mingchen-zhihou-weihe-cansi-yu-diwang-zhishou-lqwexx6nchx10roxsrvh8c0ktkfq|Hanji 864]] says Huan does not take the memorial seriously, and the source treats this as prelude to later Liu Kui trouble.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Hanji 864 presents this as Shi Bi's proposal, not as a completed institutional success. The source does not show whether a trial would have established every claim about Liu Kui or whether clemency would have stabilized Bohai. The concept should therefore be used as a procedural design pattern and missed repair path, not as proof that late-Han law reliably constrained imperial kin.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Shi Bi's Hanji 864 proposal to separate legal process from later imperial pardon in the Liu Kui case.
