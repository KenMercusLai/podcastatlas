## Definition

Household and occupation control / 户籍与职业管控 is the state practice of fixing people by residence, identity, and work category so they become taxable, monitorable, mobilizable, and punishable administrative units.

## Current Synthesis

The early reform version appears through [[GuanZhong|管仲]]'s Qi reforms: population is divided by status and work category, urban and rural residence are separated, and people are prevented from freely changing residence or occupation. This is harsh but state-building because fixed households make taxation, discipline, and mobilization easier.

The Western Han consequence layer is personal as well as administrative. [[ChenTangWesternHan|陈汤]] can serve in Chang'an and keep family there, but his children still lack secure capital-region household status; if he loses office or dies, they risk return to origin registration. In this setting, [[MausoleumCountyEliteRelocation|陵县式豪强迁徙]] is attractive because relocation can convert proximity to the capital into durable household identity, land, and status.

Hanji 400-2 adds a Ming intensification through [[ZhuYuanzhang|朱元璋]]. In the episode's telling, [[MingDynasty|明朝]] household registration fixes people from birth, updates records periodically, ties adult work to inherited occupation, restricts movement, and allows officials to return displaced people to origin registration for punishment. The concept therefore now spans both state legibility and class reproduction: administrative categories can harden into a rule that makes poverty, farming, commerce, and residence difficult to escape.

## Key Claims

- Household registration turns residence into a political and legal resource, not just an address.
- Occupational and residential fixing increases state capacity through taxation, supervision, labor allocation, and military mobilization.
- The mechanism has personal consequences: elite or official families can be near the center without having secure capital-region status.
- Mobility control creates incentives to seek institutional exceptions, such as mausoleum-county relocation.
- Ming-style hereditary occupation turns household control into a class-fixation mechanism, not only a tax and mobilization device.
- Source claims about earliest hukou-like origins, exact Han legal mechanics, and Ming enforcement practice remain bounded to their episodes.

## Evidence

- Guan Zhong reform mechanism: [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-75-2-qiongshe-jichi-de-zushiye-guanzhong-lo8uei2u1qwj9tszftel1b4lswoa|Zhouji 75-2]] says people are divided by residence and work category and prevented from freely changing either.
- State-capacity effect: [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-75-2-qiongshe-jichi-de-zushiye-guanzhong-lo8uei2u1qwj9tszftel1b4lswoa|Zhouji 75-2]] says the system makes population, labor, and neighborhoods usable for administration and mobilization.
- Western Han household consequence: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-526-hanchao-qipa-zhidu-huangdi-si-le-jiu-neng-qianhukou-lkht_jo0z7je4gbyk10ckuxv4cub|Hanji 526]] says Chen Tang's family could live in Chang'an but still lack permanent capital registration.
- Institutional exception: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-526-hanchao-qipa-zhidu-huangdi-si-le-jiu-neng-qianhukou-lkht_jo0z7je4gbyk10ckuxv4cub|Hanji 526]] says reviving a mausoleum county would give relocated families residence, land, and status advantages.
- Ming hereditary occupation and migration control: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-400-2-yidai-qiong-shidai-qiong-nandao-qiongren-jiu-gai-daidai-qiong-lluvrlqzbzx4bb_aatfmtyqibdym|Hanji 400-2]] says Ming records fixed people by origin and adult occupation, prevented unauthorized movement or job change, and could return disaster migrants to origin registration.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The page does not claim a continuous institution from Guan Zhong to Western Han to Ming. Zhouji 75-2 supplies a source-scoped early hukou-like mechanism; Hanji 526 supplies a later household-status problem inside Western Han official life; Hanji 400-2 supplies a Ming popular-history account of hereditary occupation and migration restriction. Precise legal pathways and enforcement practice remain source-scoped.
