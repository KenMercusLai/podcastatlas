## Definition
Arendtian friendship is [[RogerBerkowitz|Roger Berkowitz]]'s reconstruction of friendship in [[HannahArendt|Hannah Arendt]] as a durable shared world created through conversation, time, repeated presence, and respect while differences between persons remain intact.

## Current Synthesis
The concept is stronger than liberal toleration and narrower than unconditional solidarity. Friends need not share politics, religion, taste, or judgment, but they must be able to speak openly, show up over time, respect one another's character, and risk honest disagreement without making ideological conformity the price of continued association. For an exile, such relationships can become a homeland; in public life, they model how plurality can remain relational rather than collapsing into distance, performance, or enforced sameness.

## Key Claims
- Friendship is constituted by conversation, time, repeated presence, and respect rather than agreement alone.
- Respect gives each person room to occupy a distinct standpoint inside a shared world.
- Character and reliability in moments of need can matter more than similarity of opinion or taste.
- Honest accusation can remain compatible with friendship when it is direct and relationship-preserving.
- Privacy and civic persona protect friendship from becoming either networking performance or an audit of pure motives.
- Friendship across disagreement humanizes another person more fully than passive toleration at a distance.

### Durable relational practice
- [[zuozhe-duitan-pengyou-shifou-bi-zhenli-gengjia-zhongyao-roger-berkowitz-ln7i59hzhpigtixkj-dvwmkqrrsf]] identifies conversation, time, and respect as the elements that crystallize into friendship, with repeated presence giving those elements durability.

### Difference without dissolution
- [[zuozhe-duitan-pengyou-shifou-bi-zhenli-gengjia-zhongyao-roger-berkowitz-ln7i59hzhpigtixkj-dvwmkqrrsf]] uses Arendt's relationships and correspondence to argue that respect for a person and their character can survive severe disagreement.

### Private and political form
- [[zuozhe-duitan-pengyou-shifou-bi-zhenli-gengjia-zhongyao-roger-berkowitz-ln7i59hzhpigtixkj-dvwmkqrrsf]] distinguishes intimate friendship from civic friendship while treating both as ways of sustaining a world among different persons.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- Friendship does not require mildness: the episode explicitly preserves forceful argument and the risk of rupture.
- The model does not establish an obligation to maintain relationships involving abuse, direct danger, organized dehumanization, or deliberate falsehood.
- Berkowitz reconstructs the concept from dispersed evidence; Arendt did not publish a systematic theory of friendship.
- Extreme exile conditions shaped Arendt's expectations of loyalty, so her demanding examples should not become literal universal timelines or sacrifice tests.

## What Changed
- Added friendship as a distinct organizing thread in the wiki's Arendt branch.
- Distinguished friendship from toleration, similarity, networking, civility, and unconditional loyalty.
- Connected private presence and respect to political plurality without treating intimate and civic friendship as identical.
