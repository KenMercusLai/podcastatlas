## Definition

外交成本风险核算 / diplomatic cost-risk accounting is the statecraft pattern where a court evaluates a diplomatic expense against the larger security, trust, and war-risk costs that may follow if the expense is cut. The concept enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-589-jiemi-wunao-shengqian-de-kepa-houguo-ln7zzxy9dydebce_b3bomkcr872d|Hanji 589]], where [[YangXiong|扬雄]] argues that refusing a [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] chanyu's visit to save money and grain could damage a fragile frontier settlement.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 589 defines the concept by opposing two frames. The ministers around [[HanAidi|汉哀帝]] see the Xiongnu court visit as an avoidable reception cost, while Yang Xiong treats it as a credibility and frontier-security investment. The same act, paying to receive the chanyu, is therefore either waste or insurance depending on whether the decision maker counts downstream risk.

The source's reasoning is historical. Yang Xiong recalls Qin wall-building, Liu Bang's Baideng danger, Han Wendi's border alarms, Han Wudi's expensive northern offensives, and Han Xuandi's later mobilization to show that Xiongnu conflict had repeatedly imposed high costs. Against that background, gifts, audience ritual, heqin, and guest treatment look less like ceremonial extravagance and more like maintenance costs for a hard-won peace.

The concept is narrower than generic generosity. It applies when refusing an expense can signal contempt, withdraw recognition, or create suspicion in a counterpart that still has coercive capacity. In that situation, the cheap option may be only locally cheap: it saves the visible outlay while increasing the probability of a much larger military or diplomatic loss.

## Key Claims

- Diplomatic expenses should be compared with the plausible cost of damaged trust, renewed hostility, and lost credibility, not only with the treasury line item.
- A low-cost refusal can become strategically expensive when the counterpart interprets it as rejection or demotion.
- The value of reception, gifts, heqin, or ritual respect depends on the threat history and current leverage of the other side.
- Historical memory can make cost accounting more accurate by revealing what renewed conflict has cost before.
- The pattern does not prove every diplomatic expense is justified; it requires a concrete risk channel from saving to instability.

## Evidence

- Short-term saving frame: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-589-jiemi-wunao-shengqian-de-kepa-houguo-ln7zzxy9dydebce_b3bomkcr872d|Hanji 589]] says Aidi's ministers advise refusing the chanyu's visit because it would consume money and grain.
- Credibility warning: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-589-jiemi-wunao-shengqian-de-kepa-houguo-ln7zzxy9dydebce_b3bomkcr872d|Hanji 589]] says Yang Xiong warns that rejecting the chanyu's request would create more suspicion and contradiction between Han and Xiongnu.
- Threat-history accounting: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-589-jiemi-wunao-shengqian-de-kepa-houguo-ln7zzxy9dydebce_b3bomkcr872d|Hanji 589]] reviews Qin, early Han, Han Wendi, Han Wudi, and Han Xuandi precedents to show that Xiongnu problems had repeatedly demanded large military outlays.
- Maintenance-cost logic: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-589-jiemi-wunao-shengqian-de-kepa-houguo-ln7zzxy9dydebce_b3bomkcr872d|Hanji 589]] presents heqin, equal guest treatment, and continued gifts after submission as part of keeping Xiongnu from becoming an enemy again.
- Source boundary: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-589-jiemi-wunao-shengqian-de-kepa-houguo-ln7zzxy9dydebce_b3bomkcr872d|Hanji 589]] explicitly leaves the fuller explanation of why Xiongnu was uniquely dangerous to Han for the next episode.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept does not reject thrift or public-spending restraint. Hanji 589 argues against "mindless saving," not against all budget discipline. The evidence does not quantify the reception cost or prove exactly how the chanyu would have reacted if refused. It also uses Yang Xiong's remonstrance as mediated by one podcast episode, so the current claim remains source-scoped: in a high-risk frontier relationship, some visible expenses may be cheaper than the instability created by refusing them.
