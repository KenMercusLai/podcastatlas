## Definition
Mission-economics alignment is the design principle that a company's social or environmental commitments become more durable when they also create customer value, demand, savings, access, or operating advantage.

## Current Synthesis
The episode rejects a simple choice between purpose and growth. [[NickGreen]] points to [[ThriveMarket]] programs where access initiatives still support purchasing and packaging reduction can lower environmental impact and cost. Applied to [[PeakStateCoffee]], the principle requires separating genuine non-negotiables from inherited practices, explaining why higher standards matter to customers, and choosing capital whose expectations do not force the mission away from the business model.

## Key Claims
- A mission is more defensible when customers can experience its product or service value.
- Cost reduction and environmental improvement can sometimes reinforce one another.
- Founders should identify which values are non-negotiable instead of treating every current practice as equally essential.
- Aligned equity can share risk and add advice, while debt preserves ownership but increases repayment risk.
- Organic growth can protect control and impose discipline, though it may slow expansion.

## Evidence
- Company examples - [[advice-line-with-nick-green-of-thrive-market-7764e494-e791-48db-b95a-2d18994139a0]] cites Thrive's free memberships, food-stamp acceptance, and packaging reduction as mission-and-economics cases.
- Founder application - [[advice-line-with-nick-green-of-thrive-market-7764e494-e791-48db-b95a-2d18994139a0]] applies the principle to Peak State's organic sourcing, conservation giving, B Corp status, pricing, and financing question.
- Capital boundary - [[advice-line-with-nick-green-of-thrive-market-7764e494-e791-48db-b95a-2d18994139a0]] distinguishes debt, aligned equity, and bootstrapping by dilution, risk sharing, advice, and discipline.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Not every socially valuable commitment produces near-term financial benefit, and forcing all mission work to justify itself through profit can erase public or moral value. The Thrive examples are company-reported, and the source does not quantify their net economics or long-term impact.

## What Changed
- Established a focused mission design principle linking customer value, operating economics, and financing fit.
