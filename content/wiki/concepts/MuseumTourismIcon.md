# Museum Tourism Icon

## Definition
A museum tourism icon is an object whose fame organizes visitor behavior so strongly that seeing it becomes a primary reason to enter, navigate, crowd, photograph, or judge a museum.

## Current Synthesis
[[667-the-mystery-of-the-mona-lisa-glt1735270133]] presents [[MonaLisa|the Mona Lisa]] as the extreme case. Its fame draws visitors to the [[Louvre]] specifically for that object, making the painting both artwork and crowd-management problem. The planned dedicated underground gallery for 2031, as reported by the episode, shows institutional space adapting to iconic demand.

## Key Claims
- A tourism icon can dominate visitor intention beyond its physical scale.
- Museum display becomes part of the object's meaning when crowds gather around it.
- Theft, absence, and return can create destination value as well as narrative value.
- Dedicated infrastructure can become necessary when fame exceeds ordinary display conditions.
- Tourism-icon status can coexist with viewer disappointment or aesthetic numbness.

## Evidence
- Visitor intention - [[667-the-mystery-of-the-mona-lisa-glt1735270133]] says many Louvre visitors go specifically to see the Mona Lisa.
- Crowd history - [[667-the-mystery-of-the-mona-lisa-glt1735270133]] says crowds visited the empty frame after the 1911 theft.
- Infrastructure - [[667-the-mystery-of-the-mona-lisa-glt1735270133]] says a planned underground gallery dedicated to the painting is scheduled for 2031.
- Ambivalent encounter - [[667-the-mystery-of-the-mona-lisa-glt1735270133]] notes that ubiquity can make viewers numb to the painting's beauty.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The 2031 gallery claim is source-scoped. The concept should not assume that crowd size equals artistic quality; the episode explicitly separates fame, story, and aesthetic response.

## What Changed
- Created a museum-tourism concept grounded in the Mona Lisa's Louvre status.
