# Imperial Local-God Conflict / 皇权与地方神冲突

Imperial local-god conflict / 皇权与地方神冲突 is the pattern added by [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-122-3-qinshihuang-jing-he-shenxian-si-qilai-le-ljnw5estg6dvgfllm3kfcq6ulhxu]] for moments when an empire's touring ruler meets a local sacred order that does not smoothly become imperial property. The source contrasts two modes: Qin Shi Huang can worship the [[QiEightSpirits|齐地八神]] in former Qi territory, but at [[XiangshanShrine|湘山祠]] he reads obstruction by [[XiangJun|湘君]] as a personal and political affront.

The concept matters because it turns geography into a contested authority field. A storm or blocked crossing is not only weather inside the episode's logic; it becomes the sign of a place-based god refusing imperial passage. Qin Shi Huang's order to strip Xiangshan's trees then makes the conflict visible through forced labor, environmental damage, and humiliation of a Chu-linked sacred site.

## Key Claims
- Incorporating conquered sacred geography and punishing it are different imperial strategies.
- A local deity can become politically dangerous when the ruler interprets natural resistance as symbolic defiance.
- The Xiangshan episode extends [[ImperialTourPoliticalTheater|帝国巡游政治剧场]] by showing that a tour can produce resentment when spectacle becomes desecration.
- The pattern should be kept source-scoped until additional sources show how later actors remembered or politicized the incident.
