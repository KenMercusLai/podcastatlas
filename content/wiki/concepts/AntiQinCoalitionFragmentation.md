# Anti-Qin Coalition Fragmentation / 反秦阵营诸侯化分裂

## Definition
Anti-Qin coalition fragmentation describes how a shared enemy did not create a unified rebel command: local forces restored old states, defended separate titles and territory, and sometimes fought one another while [[QinState|Qin]] remained militarily dangerous.

## Current Synthesis
The fracture begins with Zhao's decision to strengthen its own territory instead of following Zhang Chu west, then spreads across revived Qi, Yan and Wei and within Chen Sheng's own army. Royal succession, command legitimacy, personal security and border bargaining shape cooperation, defection or attachment. Chu's restored kingship and the Huaiwang reward rule attempt to coordinate the field, but Song Yi's command crisis and the watching lords at Julu show their limits. Xiang Yu's victory produces temporary deference to a victorious commander, not evidence that conflicting interests disappeared.

## Key Claims
- **C1 — Territorial restoration competes with common strategy:** New Zhao and other revived states turn anti-Qin mobilization into separate state-building and bargaining.
- **C2 — Command fractures from within:** Claimed or disputed authorization, killing or desertion of commanders, and Chen Sheng's weakened personal authority undermine nominally unified rebel armies.
- **C3 — Title and succession disputes obstruct cooperation:** Local defections, rival Chu claimants and Qi's succession conditions convert status conflicts into failed joint operations; attachment can also consolidate forces without every leader claiming kingship.
- **C4 — Royal legitimacy and reward rules are partial repairs:** The restored Chu royal line and Huaiwang's Guanzhong promise seek to coordinate armies, but appointment does not settle timing, command or the obligation to rescue Zhao.
- **C5 — Victory recenters, rather than abolishes, fragmentation:** Xiang Yu's intervention at Julu draws submission from previously passive lords, shifting coordination toward his military authority.

## Evidence
- **C1 — Territorial restoration competes with common strategy:** After [[ZhouWenRebel|Zhou Wen]] retreats and [[ChenSheng|Chen Sheng]] reportedly kills returning commanders on slander, [[ZhangEr|Zhang Er]] and [[ChenYu|Chen Yu]] urge [[WuChen|Wu Chen]] to become Zhao king. [[CaiCi|Cai Ci]] persuades Chen Sheng not to kill Wu Chen's family while Qin still threatens; Chen Sheng recognizes Zhao and requests a westward attack, but the Zhao advisers distrust recognition that a victorious [[ZhangChuRegime|Zhang Chu]] could revoke. Wu Chen expands toward Yan, Dai, Henei, Changshan and Shangdang instead. This is the divergence from [[SixStateRestorationAntiQinStrategy|restoration as anti-Qin multiplication]] to territorial self-strengthening. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-128-5-wo-you-zhengju-zanmen-buneng-shuo-qinershi-sha-ye-bushi-ljuome-xea6sjv4hhzmrr0b0-1hg]]
- **C1 — Territorial restoration competes with common strategy:** [[TianDanQinRebel|Tian Dan]] claims Qi kingship; Wu Chen's envoy [[HanGuang|Han Guang]] accepts Yan kingship despite family-hostage concerns; [[ZhouShiRebel|Zhou Shi]] refuses the Wei crown and instead secures the old royal heir [[WeiJiu|Wei Jiu]]. The new [[ZhaoState|Zhao]]–[[YanState|Yan]] border produces a hostage and territorial bargaining crisis when Yan captures Wu Chen; a low-status [[ZhaoServantEnvoy|Zhao camp servant]] secures his release by warning that killing him would give Zhao commanders a pretext for war. Restoration multiplies political centers, but the servant's negotiation also shows that separate regimes can bargain short of battle. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-128-8-yi-ming-zhaoguo-chuishibing-nixi-chengwei-waijiao-dachen-lhfwk-hjge5iuqet6astmbclhyri]]
- **C2 — Command fractures from within:** At [[YingyangWarringStates|Yingyang]], where [[LiYou|Li You]] holds the city and Qin relief approaches, [[TianZang|Tian Zang]] and other commanders kill [[WuGuang|Wu Guang]] under a purported Chen Sheng order; Chen Sheng does not punish them and promotes Tian Zang. His knowledge or prior approval is the host's inference, not established fact. Tian Zang then leaves officers at the siege, attacks west, and is killed by [[ZhangHanQin|Zhang Han]], who also defeats the force left at Yingyang: decapitating a commander did not repair operational weakness. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-129-1-jiemi-wuguang-bei-miekou-de-lishi-zhenxiang-lk1bkhqkwf9upbbj08pjkn8cstyp]] [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-129-2-lishishang-hejiu-neng-wu-duo-da-shi-zhuan-gei-jiugui-laogong-kan-zhe-ji-lvzxpoxclwdrr66bwejd-bga8dp]]
- **C2 — Command fractures from within:** [[WangLiQin|Wang Li]]'s recruitment overture to Zhao general [[LiLiangZhaoGeneral|Li Liang]] found him unable to take his assigned route; [[ZhaoWuchenSister|Wu Chen's sister]] then failed to return his salute after a banquet, a perceived status insult his followers used to argue Zhao would not protect them. Li Liang kills her, attacks Handan and kills Wu Chen. [[ChenYu|Chen Yu]]'s local network warns [[ZhangEr|Zhang Er]] and enables escape. The source frames defection as an interaction of military pressure and perceived status, not a proven single-trigger reflex. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-129-2-lishishang-hejiu-neng-wu-duo-da-shi-zhuan-gei-jiugui-laogong-kan-zhe-ji-lvzxpoxclwdrr66bwejd-bga8dp]] [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-129-3-chensheng-zhisi-weishenme-dele-minxin-hanle-renxin-lnx4zs-vsi3zeoixnqskkpexmr3]]
- **C2 — Command fractures from within:** [[QinJiaRebel|Qin Jia]] invokes Chen Sheng's name to kill his monitor [[WuPingJunPan|Wu Ping Jun Pan]], while harsh inspectors, executions of retreating officers and alienation of old associates weaken trust at the Zhang Chu center. Chen Sheng's own driver ultimately kills him under Qin pressure. The account's [[PunitiveInspectionLoyaltyTrap|punitive-loyalty]] mechanism explains how a founder's command can lose willing adherence even while his rebellion remains historically consequential. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-129-3-chensheng-zhisi-weishenme-dele-minxin-hanle-renxin-lnx4zs-vsi3zeoixnqskkpexmr3]]
- **C3 — Title and succession disputes obstruct cooperation:** After Chen Sheng's death, Zhang Er and Chen Yu install [[ZhaoXie|Zhao Xie]] in Zhao; Qin Jia backs [[JingJu|Jing Ju]] as Chu king, but Tian Dan kills envoy [[GongsunQing|Gongsun Qing]] in a dispute over reciprocal recognition. [[YongChi|Yong Chi]] gives [[FengCounty|Feng]] to Wei-side Zhou Shi rather than remaining loyal to [[LiuBang|Liu Bang]]. These cases differentiate rival royal authorization, a failed diplomatic approach and a local garrison defection. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-129-4-jiuquangui-zhangliang-ruhe-zai-liubang-shouxia-anshen-lp6qwg3ma5hdy15g4box7pe23jav]]
- **C3 — Title and succession disputes obstruct cooperation:** [[ZhaoPingRebel|Zhao Ping]] falsely claims dead Chen Sheng authorized [[XiangLiang|Xiang Liang]] to lead Chu west, reusing a fallen center's legitimacy. [[ChenYing|Chen Ying]], encouraged by his mother, refuses kingship and attaches his local army to Xiang Liang; [[YingBu|Ying Bu]] also joins him. Xiang Liang defeats Qin Jia, absorbs his surrendered troops, and dislodges Jing Ju while presenting the war against fellow rebels as punishment for disloyalty to Chen Sheng: [[MoralizedWarPretext|a loyalty rationale]] helped consolidate troops, but incorporation after defeat differs from voluntary attachment and should not be mistaken for disinterested unity. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-130-1-guailaotou-yiqiao-san-shua-zhangliang-chuanshou-shenshu-lqwjo13zzfgtndpajzpuvrb2h4l2]] [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-130-2-lishi-jilu-shouci-tucheng-lm4kuzdpnqn-hicyuvvb4p2wdudm]]
- **C3 — Title and succession disputes obstruct cooperation:** After Tian Dan dies fighting Qin, [[TianRong|Tian Rong]] rejects [[TianJia|Tian Jia]]'s emergency Qi kingship, installs Tian Dan's son and demands that Chu kill the fugitive Tian Jia and Zhao kill [[TianJiao|Tian Jiao]] and [[TianJianQiGeneral|Tian Jian]] before Qi helps Xiang Liang fight Zhang Han. Chu and Zhao refuse; the demanded succession settlement costs the proposed joint operation. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-130-3-weihe-li-mutong-wei-chuwang-limian-dayou-wenzhang-lmcuvrxer-cou6gmlppqwhb3n4vz]]
- **C4 — Royal legitimacy and reward rules are partial repairs:** Fan Zeng argues for an old Chu royal descendant; Xiang Liang installs shepherd [[MiXin|Mi Xin]] as Chu Huai Wang to gather support under a recognizable royal name. Following Xiang Liang's death and Zhang Han's northward turn, Mi Xin reorganizes commands and makes [[HuaiwangAgreement|the promise]] that the first to enter [[GuanzhongRegion|Guanzhong]] will rule there. [[ZhaoXie|Zhao Xie]] and Zhang Er retreat into [[Julu|Julu]] under Wang Li's siege, making Zhao's rescue a test of whether the states avoid being defeated separately. Mi Xin sends Liu Bang west and appoints Song Yi, Xiang Yu and Fan Zeng to the relief force. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-130-3-weihe-li-mutong-wei-chuwang-limian-dayou-wenzhang-lmcuvrxer-cou6gmlppqwhb3n4vz]] [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-132-5wan-wanbao-40wan-xiangyu-cizhan-fengshen-lk87nb3fskg5zh6fcgptypixhzuw]]
- **C4 — Royal legitimacy and reward rules are partial repairs:** The appointed [[SongYi|Song Yi]] stops at [[AnyangQinRebellion|Anyang]] for forty-six days, proposing to let Qin and Zhao exhaust each other; [[XiangYu|Xiang Yu]] wants an immediate two-sided attack. The episode contrasts cold, hungry soldiers with Song Yi's trip to [[WuyanCounty|Wuyan]] to celebrate his son's Qi appointment, and describes a discipline order read as aimed at Xiang Yu. Xiang Yu kills Song Yi and takes practical command: [[DelayedRescueStatecraft|delayed rescue]] and command morale are source explanations for a violent rupture, not proof that the wait could never have worked. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-133-songyi-zhan-xiangyu-weihe-bei-fansha-lms7noyrm5omedv77gqfhjeu53zx]]
- **C5 — Victory recenters, rather than abolishes, fragmentation:** At [[JuluBattle|Julu]], Chen Yu withholds his force while small units sent at Zhang Er's insistence are destroyed, and nearby lords watch from their camps. Xiang Yu first has Ying Bu and Pu General cut Qin's supply corridor to Wang Li, then crosses the Zhang River with a [[BreakingCauldronsAndSinkingBoats|no-retreat morale measure]]. After repeated engagements break Wang Li's army, the watching lords accept Xiang Yu as coalition commander. It is [[MilitaryPersonalization|battlefield authority]], not a durable shared constitution, that temporarily recenters the coalition. ## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- Chen Ying's [[ClaimRestraintThroughAttachment|restraint through attachment]], Ying Bu's attachment and Mi Xin's allocations show alternatives to independent kingship. They did not prove the anti-Qin forces had permanently solved coordination or that all restored states had identical interests.
- [[TextualOmissionAttributionShift|A textual omission matters]]: the source contrasts 《史记》's account that Zhang Er and Chen Yu resented Chen Sheng's failure to adopt their proposal with 《资治通鉴》's omission. That affects where blame falls for Zhao's split; recognition of Zhao and subsequent refusal to march west are distinct reported events, but the advisers' complete motives remain unsettled. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-128-5-wo-you-zhengju-zanmen-buneng-shuo-qinershi-sha-ye-bushi-ljuome-xea6sjv4hhzmrr0b0-1hg]]
- Chen Sheng's promotion of Tian Zang does not prove prior consent to Wu Guang's killing. Likewise the host's [[RebelFounderAlienation|founder-alienation]] reading is a contextual explanation, not an independent test of each rebel's motives. Some transcripts truncate discussions and render names inconsistently. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-129-1-jiemi-wuguang-bei-miekou-de-lishi-zhenxiang-lk1bkhqkwf9upbbj08pjkn8cstyp]] [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-129-3-chensheng-zhisi-weishenme-dele-minxin-hanle-renxin-lnx4zs-vsi3zeoixnqskkpexmr3]]
- Xiang Yu's killing of an appointed general was itself a rupture, not an unqualified organizational repair; the source reports Song Yi's and Xiang Yu's rival operational arguments but does not establish a controlled counterfactual. Qinji 132 advertises Julu in its title yet narrates mainly the setup; the battle and the distinction between the initial Yellow River crossing and later Zhang River no-retreat crossing belong to Qinji 134. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-133-songyi-zhan-xiangyu-weihe-bei-fansha-lms7noyrm5omedv77gqfhjeu53zx]] [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-134-ni-buzhidao-de-pofu-chenzhou-banben-ltnry1fva2hzplw4ft8nrfpoiolw]]

## What Changed
- Reorganizes the rebel episodes into territorial separation, internal command, status rivalry, partial institutional repair and victory-based recentering.
- Treats attachment and royal coordination as real countervailing responses, not proof that fragmentation was inevitable or permanent.
- Distinguishes reported events from host inference, disputed motives and Julu setup versus battle evidence.
