# Confucian Low-Office Reclusion / 儒家低职隐居

Confucian low-office reclusion / 儒家低职隐居 is the episode's explanation for why [[HouYing|侯嬴]] can be treated as a recluse in [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-96-maosui-zijian-5-lp6yfcxoysxgcnmg8v1uljfyptof]] even though he is not living in mountains or refusing all work. He is poor, elderly, and employed as a gatekeeper at [[Daliang|大梁]]'s Yi Gate, yet the source reads that position as compatible with a restrained Confucian refusal of rank-seeking.

The mechanism is subsistence without ambition. The episode uses the "抱关击柝" frame to argue that a worthy person may accept a low, humble office to support basic life while refusing to chase high salary, court favor, or participation in a disordered political order. Reclusion here is therefore not total withdrawal; it is a refusal to convert ability into compromised advancement.

This concept matters for [[TalentAttractionThroughRespect|礼贤下士式人才吸附]] because it changes the recruitment problem. If a person like Hou Ying is genuinely withholding himself from ordinary patronage channels, a lord cannot simply send gifts and wait for compliance. The patron must lower status and make recognition credible enough for the recluse to answer.

## Key Claims
- Reclusion does not always mean geographic isolation or refusal of all livelihood.
- A low office can be morally different from rank-seeking when it supports subsistence without becoming a route to wealth and power.
- The concept keeps Hou Ying's poverty and old age from being treated as evidence against his worth.
- Recruitment of such a figure requires visible humility from the patron, not only payment.
