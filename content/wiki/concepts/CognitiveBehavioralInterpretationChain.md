# Cognitive-Behavioral Interpretation Chain / 认知行为解释链

## Definition
The cognitive-behavioral interpretation chain is the episode's model that an event is interpreted before it shapes feeling and action, so therapy can examine assumptions, recover omitted possibilities, and practice responses that fit present evidence more closely.

## Current Synthesis
EP67 explains the chain through a friend who does not greet someone. Interpreting the event as rejection can produce hurt or anger and avoidant or retaliatory behavior; interpreting it as distraction or urgency can produce a different emotional and behavioral response. The event remains the same, but the meaning assigned to it changes what follows.

The episode extends this logic to trauma cues. A past danger can train a rapid fear response that continues after the original threat is gone, while therapy can help a person notice the trigger and evaluate whether the present situation is actually dangerous. The claim is not that distress is imaginary or that positive thinking cures illness, but that interpretation is one modifiable part of a larger clinical and social context.

## Key Claims
- Events, interpretations, feelings, and actions are related but not interchangeable.
- Automatic interpretations can omit plausible explanations and make one threatening meaning feel certain.
- Therapy can help identify recurring interpretation errors and practice more evidence-aligned alternatives.
- Trauma cues can produce real bodily and emotional reactions even when the original danger has ended.
- Reappraisal is not a substitute for diagnosis, medication, safety planning, environmental change, or social support when those are needed.

- Trauma application - [[ep67-bie-kangju-xinli-zixun-keneng-bing-bushi-ni-xiang-de-nayang-wkglefzxkovwczzbbbvx2la4e4c899]] describes past danger being reactivated by present cues and therapy strengthening present-safety evaluation.
- Clinical boundary - [[ep67-bie-kangju-xinli-zixun-keneng-bing-bushi-ni-xiang-de-nayang-wkglefzxkovwczzbbbvx2la4e4c899]] places cognitive work beside psychiatric medication, practical support, relationships, and environmental context.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source supplies a public-facing simplification rather than a full CBT protocol or outcome study. Alternative interpretation should not become forced optimism, victim blaming, denial of real danger, or a claim that all psychiatric symptoms arise from faulty thoughts. Acute risk, psychosis, severe impairment, trauma exposure, and material constraints can require other interventions.
