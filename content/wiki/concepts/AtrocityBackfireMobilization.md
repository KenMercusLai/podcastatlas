# Atrocity Backfire Mobilization / 暴行反噬式动员

Atrocity backfire mobilization / 暴行反噬式动员 is the mechanism [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-79-shishang-yong-gengniu-da-shengzhang-diyi-ren-lsrl0posblloqwugox160xsicp2p]] uses to explain how [[TianDan|田单]] turns [[QiJie|骑劫]]'s harshness into [[QiState|齐国]] resistance. Tian Dan spreads rumors that Qi most fears nose-cut prisoners being displayed and ancestral graves being dug up; Qi Jie does both, believing the acts will frighten the defenders.

The result is the opposite. Mutilated prisoners make surrender look worse than continued resistance, while grave desecration turns the siege into a family and ancestral violation. The people inside [[JimoWarringStates|即墨]] become angry enough to demand battle, and the already captured Qi cities become harder for [[YanState|燕国]] to hold.

The concept is the negative mirror of [[OccupationPacificationStrategy|occupation pacification strategy]]. [[LeYi|乐毅]] tries to make conquered Qi tolerable; Qi Jie gives Tian Dan visible proof that Yan rule is humiliating and unsafe.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-148-2-peng-le-wangling-muqin-xiangyu-sharen-zhuxin-ljyv8bow4rj1l5ths3znx2evgvtm|Hanji 148-2]] adds a narrower elite-network version through [[XiangYu|项羽]] and [[WangLingMother|王陵母亲]]. Xiang Yu's attempt to use her as a hostage already fails when she kills herself; cooking the corpse then turns failed coercion into a cruelty signal that, in the episode's reading, drives [[WangLingHanMinister|王陵]] decisively toward [[LiuBang|刘邦]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-149-xichu-bawang-xiangyu-weihe-shijun-2-lpohbb3prsgvokpjipfd7i1ntiwn|Hanji 149 part 2]] adds a broader occupation-scale case in Qi. After [[TianRong|田荣]] is killed by local people who appear ready to placate Xiang Yu, Xiang Yu continues killing surrendered troops, burning cities, and plundering civilians. The episode reads this as a major strategic error because violence removes the path from defeat to submission and forces Qi people to resist again.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-998-baima-jiangjun-gongsunzan-cong-sanqian-baima-dao-kunshou-gucheng-lp-1l2gvbsbfnyznutdghxwwvqna|Hanji 998]] adds a late-Han regional version through [[GongsunZan|公孙瓒]]'s killing of [[LiuYuLateHan|刘虞]] and Liu Yu's family. The killing does not settle [[YouzhouLateHan|幽州]]; it gives [[XianyuFu|鲜于辅]], [[LiuHeLateHan|刘和]], [[Wuhuan|乌桓]], [[Xianbei|鲜卑]], and [[YuanShao|袁绍]]-aligned forces a revenge and legitimacy axis against him.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-972-sanguo-liuyu-zhisi-taisha-taitianzhen-lvawrcandbh628rckukosgarh7v2|Hanji 972]] supplies the immediate mechanism behind that Hanji 998 backlash. [[DuanXunLateHan|段迅]] arrives with court edicts, but Gongsun Zan turns the scene into an accusation that Liu Yu colluded with Yuan Shao and sought the imperial title, then executes Liu Yu and his family in the marketplace. The public killing converts battlefield victory into a durable grievance field.

## Key Claims
- Violence meant to frighten a population can instead clarify why surrender is intolerable.
- Public humiliation can remove the defender's psychological exit path.
- Desecration of ancestors and bodies can convert a military siege into a shared moral injury.
- Occupation policy can lose in one visible act what battlefield success previously gained.
- The same mechanism can work at elite-network scale: bodily desecration of a hostage can harden one wavering ally and damage the captor's reputation beyond the immediate family.
- Hanji 149 part 2 adds that atrocity backfire can follow victory: killing and plunder after a rebel leader's death can prevent pacification that was already becoming possible.
- Hanji 998 adds that killing a locally respected rival can create a revenge coalition rather than secure the region.
- Hanji 972 adds that coerced legal theater around a local rival's execution can intensify the backfire by making the act visibly unjust.
