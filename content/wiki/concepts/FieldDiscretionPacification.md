## Definition

Field Discretion Pacification is a frontier-governance pattern where an official outside the capital exceeds or reshapes formal authorization to secure surrender or settlement under urgent conditions, then submits the action to later court judgment.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 726 grounds the pattern in [[ZongJunEasternHan|Zong Jun]]'s handling of the unfinished [[WulingManLateHan|Wuling Man]] campaign after [[MaYuanEasternHan|Ma Yuan]] dies. With epidemic losses killing more than half the Han troops and the opposing side hungry, Zong Jun falsely issues an imperial surrender order, sends an acting official into the enemy camp, follows with troops, accepts surrender, disperses fighters, appoints local officials, and self-impeaches before reaching the capital. [[LiuXiu|Liu Xiu]] rewards rather than punishes him, making the action a bounded case of successful discretionary pacification rather than a general permission to fake edicts.

## Key Claims

- The pattern depends on urgency: epidemic loss, enemy hunger, and ongoing frontier war create a decision environment where waiting for routine authorization may worsen the state position.
- Discretion is still politically dangerous because falsely invoking imperial authority can be a capital offense.
- Pacification succeeds when the unauthorized act produces a verifiable settlement, not merely when the actor claims good intentions.
- Self-impeachment matters because it returns the unauthorized act to imperial judgment after the field crisis passes.
- Ruler acceptance is contingent: Liu Xiu's reward validates this case, but the concept does not excuse every breach of command procedure.

- Settlement mechanics: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-726-ma-ge-guo-shi-de-ma-yuan-weihe-mengyuan-buneng-shuo-de-mimi-lpxrkrx2uffdzpyctiwwcaanfzbu|Hanji 726]] says the rebels kill their leaders, submit, return to their commanderies, and receive newly appointed local officials.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source does not make discretionary command a stable doctrine. It presents one successful case where conditions, outcome, and post-action accountability align. The action remains legally hazardous; the concept should not be generalized into blank authorization for commanders to fabricate imperial orders.

## What Changed

- Created this concept from Hanji 726 to capture Zong Jun's unauthorized but successful Wuling surrender settlement.
