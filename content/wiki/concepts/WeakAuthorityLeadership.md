## Definition
Weak Authority Leadership is the condition in which a nominal superior lacks the resolve, knowledge, or reward-punishment credibility needed to turn rank or private agreement into effective command.

## Current Synthesis
Hanji 768 frames [[EmperorHeOfHan|Emperor He]] as a ruler who could identify self-interested obstruction yet failed to enforce a correct direction. The clearest case is [[CaoBaoEasternHan|Cao Bao's]] Han-rites project: Emperor He had used the rites and recognized that opposition by [[ZhangPuEasternHan|Zhang Pu]] and Zhang Min was not neutral, but he still abandoned the project. Hanji 569 adds a personnel-veto mechanism under [[HanAidi|汉哀帝]]: when [[FuTaihouWesternHan|傅太后]] makes him reverse [[FuQianWesternHan|傅迁]]'s dismissal, formal rank remains with the emperor while credible control shifts to the patron who can veto him.

Hanji 519 adds the most dangerous subordinate-facing version. [[HanChengdi|汉成帝]] privately accepts [[WangZhangWesternHan|王章]]'s warning about [[WangFengWesternHan|王凤]], asks for a replacement, and appears to authorize action. After the plan leaks, [[WangZhengjun|王政君]] applies family pressure and Chengdi reverses course, prosecutes the adviser, and leaves Wang Zhang's household exposed. Weak authority therefore does not merely stall good work or confuse allegiance. It can make a leader's approval actively hazardous because the subordinate commits before learning that the approval will not survive conflict.

Together the sources show weak authority as an enforcement and reliance problem: observers follow the power center whose decisions hold, while advisers who trust nominal authority may bear the cost of reversal.

## Key Claims
- Formal hierarchy does not produce authority unless the superior can back judgment with action.
- Knowing that a subordinate is right is insufficient if the leader cannot defend the subordinate against organized pressure.
- Reward and punishment are not decorative; they are the mechanism by which institutional expectations become credible.
- Weak leaders often allow skilled subordinates to be isolated while self-interested critics define the operational result.
- Reversed personnel decisions reveal a split between nominal hierarchy and effective authority, prompting observers to realign with the actor whose veto is credible.
- Private approval can increase subordinate exposure when the leader cannot protect the decision after it becomes contested.
- Leniency can be prudent in high-risk royal-house cases, but repeated under-enforcement can also reveal weak command.

## Evidence
- Cao Bao's abandoned work: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-768-lishi-gaosu-ni-jidu-shi-shaxin-lgk8qoxvs5ax8oikgecrxqvispna|Hanji 768]] says Emperor He stopped using Cao Bao's rites after repeated Zhang Pu and Zhang Min objections, despite recognizing self-interest in the opposition.
- Management rule: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-768-lishi-gaosu-ni-jidu-shi-shaxin-lgk8qoxvs5ax8oikgecrxqvispna|Hanji 768]] states the leadership standard that good work should be rewarded and errors punished.
- Royal-house leniency: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-768-lishi-gaosu-ni-jidu-shi-shaxin-lgk8qoxvs5ax8oikgecrxqvispna|Hanji 768]] shows Emperor He choosing limited punishment for Liu Chang, which can be read as containment but also fits his broader cautious governing style.
- Personnel-veto case: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-569-nianhui-buneng-ting-doushi-jiade-zhichang-zhandui-caishi-zhende-lvjg3qcsgkq4ltai8mqt6p26smk6|Hanji 569]] says Aidi dismisses Fu Qian but retains him after Fu Taihou intervenes, while Kong Guang and Shi Dan warn that the contradictory edicts damage command credibility.
- Adviser-exposure case: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-519-aiguozhe-wangzhang-de-si-yuanyugen-cuo-le-zhuzi-lt5hzlh9t-gryds1lpn0ieqw3cvn|Hanji 519]] says Chengdi accepts Wang Zhang's diagnosis and asks for a replacement for Wang Feng, then reverses course after Wang Zhengjun intervenes and orders Wang Zhang's impeachment.
- Downstream cost: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-519-aiguozhe-wangzhang-de-si-yuanyugen-cuo-le-zhuzi-lt5hzlh9t-gryds1lpn0ieqw3cvn|Hanji 519]] says Wang Zhang dies in prison and his wife and children are exiled.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Hanji 768 distinguishes weak enforcement from all forms of leniency. Emperor He's limited punishment of Liu Chang may have been prudent because royal-house usurpation accusations were explosive, while the Cao Bao case more directly displays failure to defend a work product the ruler had reason to value. Hanji 569's workplace analogy is interpretive and does not prove Fu Qian's private attitude or every court official's allegiance; its strongest evidence is the observable reversal and the ministers' explicit credibility warning. Hanji 519's interpretation strongly blames Chengdi's weakness, but the outcome also depends on Wang Feng's entrenched power, Wang Zhengjun's position, the leak, and the allegations used against Wang Zhang. Weak authority is therefore one mechanism in a larger court-power field, not a complete causal explanation.

## What Changed
- Added Hanji 519's reliance-risk mechanism: a leader's private approval can expose an adviser when it does not survive family and political pressure.
- Preserved Hanji 569's personnel-veto mechanism and Hanji 768's weak-implementation branch.
- Distinguished weak implementation from strategic restraint in politically dangerous cases.
