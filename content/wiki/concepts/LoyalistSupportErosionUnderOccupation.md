# Loyalist Support Erosion under Occupation

## Definition

Loyalist support erosion under occupation is the process by which an occupying force consumes its own political base when extraction, disorder, coercion, and institutional failure impose greater costs on allied civilians than the protection or stability they expected.

## Current Synthesis

[[BritishOccupationOfStatenIsland]] supplies the current case. Many [[StatenIsland|Staten Islanders]] initially preferred Crown rule because Royal Navy trade was profitable and rebellion threatened disruption. They welcomed British forces, entered royal service, and provided labor, food, transport, fuel, and defensive support.

The occupation then generated the disorder its supporters had hoped to avoid. Theft, fence removal, crop damage, inflation, church destruction, commandeered homes, failed courts, and retaliation against complaints weakened practical allegiance. By January 1780, [[ChristopherBillopp]] could not persuade his militia to defend Richmond; later residents provided intelligence, ambushed patrols, and celebrated evacuation.

The mechanism does not turn every alienated Loyalist into a committed Patriot. It separates withdrawal, evasion, local self-defense, opportunistic intelligence, active resistance, and ideological conversion. It also preserves harm by the opposing side: Patriot troops and civilians looted the island, and the source reports Black residents being abducted and sold into slavery.

## Key Claims

- Political allegiance can rest on expected order, trade, property security, and local autonomy as well as ideology.
- Occupiers depend on civilian supplies, information, labor, institutions, and willingness to serve.
- Repeated extraction and unredressed abuse can make an allied occupation indistinguishable from predation in daily life.
- Support erosion appears in stages, from complaint and service refusal to intelligence sharing and armed resistance.
- A commander's personal loyalty can persist after the social base needed for mobilization has collapsed.
- Alienation from an occupier does not prove trust in, or moral superiority of, the opposing army.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The current framework rests on one local source and does not measure how many residents changed beliefs, acted under coercion, avoided both sides, or remained Loyalist. Refusal to serve may reflect fatigue, risk, household protection, or distrust rather than ideological conversion. The episode's causal interpretation is plausible but does not independently establish the relative weight of occupation abuse, Patriot pressure, war duration, prices, personal loss, or changing expectations of victory.

## What Changed

- Established a staged mechanism connecting allied occupation behavior to loss of local support.
- Distinguished service withdrawal and resistance from complete ideological conversion.
- Added opposing-side predation as a necessary qualification to allegiance change.
