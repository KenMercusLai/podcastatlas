# Cinema Audience Behavioral Contract

## Definition
A cinema audience behavioral contract is the explicit set of venue rules, staff practices, and commercial choices used to protect concentrated collective viewing, including limits on talking and phones, choreographed food service, and control over pre-show interruption.

## Current Synthesis
[[AlamoDrafthouseCinema]] treats audience attention as part of the ticketed product rather than as an accidental outcome. Its contract asks customers to accept stricter conduct rules while requiring the venue to reduce its own disruptions: servers wear dark clothing and move low, service is timed around the film, ordering and payment are simplified, and ordinary pre-show advertising is rejected.

The contract therefore runs in both directions. Customers give up some behavioral freedom, while the theater forgoes some advertising revenue and bears training and enforcement costs. Publicly using an expelled phone user's angry voicemail as a pre-show message made that tradeoff legible and helped turn exclusion of incompatible behavior into a brand promise.

## Key Claims
- Focus can be part of a paid cinema service, not merely an individual act of self-control.
- Audience restrictions are more credible when the venue also reduces service and advertising interruptions.
- Strict enforcement can segment customers by desired behavior and strengthen loyalty among those who value the rule.
- Food service and immersion remain in tension; training and choreography manage rather than eliminate the conflict.
- A memorable enforcement incident can communicate norms, but virality does not prove universal approval or operational consistency.

## Evidence
- Reciprocal attention design: [[alamo-drafthouse-cinema-tim-and-karrie-league-2023-7bc6885b-e29f-4ae8-a587-2e17b1d8bea3]] describes no-talking and no-phone rules alongside dark clothing, crouched service, film-aware timing, reservations, and simplified payment.
- Commercial commitment: [[alamo-drafthouse-cinema-tim-and-karrie-league-2023-7bc6885b-e29f-4ae8-a587-2e17b1d8bea3]] says the founders refused ordinary pre-show commercials despite forgone revenue.
- Norm communication: [[alamo-drafthouse-cinema-tim-and-karrie-league-2023-7bc6885b-e29f-4ae8-a587-2e17b1d8bea3]] recounts the use of an expelled phone user's complaint as a widely viewed pre-show spot.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source concedes that in-theater food delivery still disrupts viewing. It does not measure complaint rates, enforcement disparities, customer retention, accessibility effects, or whether practices are consistent across locations. A strict contract can protect immersion while still alienating customers or being applied poorly.

## What Changed
- Created a reciprocal framework joining audience conduct, staff choreography, and the theater's own revenue choices.
- Distinguished protected collective attention from screen-size or luxury-format premiumization.
