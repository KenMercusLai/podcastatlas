# Conquest Discipline as Legitimacy

## Definition

Conquest discipline as legitimacy is the use of enforced limits on an army's violence and plunder to present invasion as rightful rule rather than unrestricted predation.

## Current Synthesis

[[HenryVOfEngland|Henry V's]] claim to be the rightful king of France made army conduct part of his political case. His ordinances protected churches, clergy, women, crops, and buildings; restricted dealings with local people; required identifying crosses; and punished breach harshly. The source therefore treats discipline as religious obligation, command control, and communication to prospective subjects at once.

The claim remains sharply qualified. Henry's army bombarded [[Harfleur]], imposed coercive surrender terms, imprisoned its garrison, expelled residents, and publicly hanged a soldier for church theft. Restraint did not make conquest nonviolent; it selected, centralized, and politically framed violence while also limiting the army's ability to feed itself through plunder.

## Key Claims

- A ruler claiming lawful sovereignty has stronger incentives to distinguish conquest from uncontrolled pillage.
- Anti-plunder ordinances can serve religious, disciplinary, logistical, and propagandistic purposes simultaneously.
- Visible punishment makes command rules credible but can itself be coercive and exemplary violence.
- Civilian protection rules coexist with siege destruction, imprisonment, expulsion, and battlefield force.
- Restricting forage and pillage increases dependence on planned provisions and therefore creates logistical tradeoffs.

## Evidence

- Political-religious purpose: [[488-hundred-years-war-the-road-to-agincourt-part-2-glt2110427983]] links Henry's conduct rules to his French kingship claim and religious duty.
- Enforcement: [[488-hundred-years-war-the-road-to-agincourt-part-2-glt2110427983]] records restricted contact, identifying crosses, and a public hanging for church theft.
- Coercive boundary: [[488-hundred-years-war-the-road-to-agincourt-part-2-glt2110427983]] places anti-plunder policy beside Harfleur's bombardment, imprisonment, and expulsions.
- Logistical tradeoff: [[488-hundred-years-war-the-road-to-agincourt-part-2-glt2110427983]] connects limited plunder to an eight-day food plan that became inadequate after rerouting.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The ordinances demonstrate announced policy and reported enforcement, not universal compliance or civilian safety. French chroniclers' favorable comparison with French looting is relevant testimony but does not erase English coercion, and the source does not independently audit every punishment or protected category.

## What Changed

- Established the dual role of campaign discipline as a sovereignty claim and a logistical constraint.
