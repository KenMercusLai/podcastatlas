# Embarrassment Trust Signal

## Definition
Embarrassment trust signal is the source's claim that visible embarrassment can make a person seem more trustworthy because it signals awareness of group norms and concern for social belonging.

## Current Synthesis
The source uses embarrassment to show Keltner's broader social-emotion approach. Embarrassment is not only personal discomfort; it has communicative value. Blushing, gaze aversion, and face hiding can tell others that a person recognizes a breach, cares about the group, and is not shamelessly rejecting shared norms.

## Key Claims
- Embarrassment includes visible signals such as blushing, gaze aversion, and face hiding.
- Observers may respond with liking, trust, or support because embarrassment implies norm awareness.
- The emotion can repair social connection after small breaches.
- Embarrassment becomes prosocial only when the social setting allows reinclusion rather than humiliation.
- The source presents embarrassment as a social signal, not as a universal guarantee of honesty.

## Evidence
- Signal description: [[cultivating-awe-emotional-connection-in-daily-life-dr-dacher-keltner-scim6985403733]] says embarrassment includes blushing, gaze aversion, and face hiding.
- Trust claim: [[cultivating-awe-emotional-connection-in-daily-life-dr-dacher-keltner-scim6985403733]] says seeing someone embarrassed can increase liking, trust, and support because it signals commitment to group norms.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Embarrassment signals can be misread, culturally shaped, performed, or exploited. The source does not claim that embarrassed people are always trustworthy or that shame-based environments are healthy.
