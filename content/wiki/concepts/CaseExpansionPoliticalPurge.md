## Definition

案件扩大化政治清洗 / Case-expansion political purge is the pattern where a bounded political or criminal case is deliberately widened through kinship, friendship, marriage, office, teacher-student, factional, harboring, legal-interpretation, or private-scandal links until it becomes a tool for removing rivals and intimidating a wider elite field.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-617-wangmang-dusha-gongzhu-lishi-shimo-lqvzejfm1sd8k0amporgi_mqhsrd|Hanji 617]] creates the concept from the aftermath of the [[DogBloodCase|狗血案]]. The original case begins with [[WangYuXinPrince|王宇]], [[WuZhangWesternHan|吴章]], and [[LuKuanDogBloodCase|吕宽]], but [[WangMang|王莽]] uses the case to reach far beyond the immediate actors. The episode stresses that Western Han high society was small, intermarried, and relationship-dense; once investigators treat association as evidence, nearly any elite connection can become a purge path.

Hanji 402-1 adds an earlier Huo Guang-era legal-classification variant. [[SangHongyangWesternHan|桑弘羊]] is destroyed after the [[LiuDanYanKingWesternHan|燕王刘旦]] rebellion, but the later case expands through [[SangQianWesternHan|桑迁]]'s flight, [[HouShiWuWesternHan|侯史吴]]'s sheltering, and [[WangPingWesternHan|王平]] and [[XuRenWesternHan|徐仁]]'s initial decision to apply the amnesty. [[HuoGuangWesternHan|霍光]]'s review recasts Sang Qian as rebellion-equivalent and Hou Shiwu as an unpardonable harborer, making lenient judicial interpretation itself punishable as indulgence of rebellion.

An earlier Chengdi-era version appears when [[ChunyuZhangWesternHan|淳于长]] is first only dismissed, but his gift-backed attempt to regain office through [[WangLiWesternHan|王立]] reopens the matter. The inquiry widens from private intercession to [[EmpressXuWesternHan|废后许氏]] scandal, Wang Rong's suicide, Wang Li's removal, and [[ZhaiFangjinWesternHan|翟方进]]'s impeachment of party friends, close associates, and personal opponents.

The concept is not just guilt by association. It includes legal-political packaging: Wang Mang first presents his own son's punishment through a Zhougong analogy, then receives an edict in [[WangZhengjun|王政君]]'s name praising him for placing state hierarchy over kinship. That moral frame authorizes harsher action against other royal, Wang-family, and ministerial figures.

The [[JingwuPrincessWesternHan|靖武公主]] branch shows the pattern's intimate edge. Her alleged link to the case is remote, while the direct charge concerns private sexual conduct; Wang Mang then sends poison under Wang Zhengjun's name and hides the truth from Wang Zhengjun. The purge therefore combines network expansion, private-life leverage, and information control.

## Key Claims

- A bounded case can become a purge when association networks are treated as evidence rather than context.
- Elite marriage, friendship, household, and office ties make case expansion especially easy in a small court society.
- Legal classification can widen a case when sheltering, failure to prevent, or amnesty interpretation is recast as rebellion-adjacent conduct.
- Moral language about placing public order above kinship can legitimate violence that actually settles broader political accounts.
- Private scandal can be repurposed as a purge charge when it helps isolate the target and avoid open political accusation.
- Case expansion produces general fear because even indirect ties become dangerous.
- A failed restoration effort can give investigators the transactional hook needed to widen an otherwise bounded dismissal.

- Court-society density: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-617-wangmang-dusha-gongzhu-lishi-shimo-lqvzejfm1sd8k0amporgi_mqhsrd|Hanji 617]] says royal kin, Wang-family members, marquises, and senior officials were tied by acquaintance and marriage, making implication easy.
- Moral authorization: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-617-wangmang-dusha-gongzhu-lishi-shimo-lqvzejfm1sd8k0amporgi_mqhsrd|Hanji 617]] says Wang Zhengjun's edict praises Wang Mang for not letting family feeling harm state hierarchy.
- Expanded victims: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-617-wangmang-dusha-gongzhu-lishi-shimo-lqvzejfm1sd8k0amporgi_mqhsrd|Hanji 617]] names Jingwu Princess, Liang King Liu Li/Liu Li, Wang Li, Wang Ren, He Wu, Wang An, Xin Qingji's sons, and Bao Xuan among those drawn into the widened case.
- Social terror: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-617-wangmang-dusha-gongzhu-lishi-shimo-lqvzejfm1sd8k0amporgi_mqhsrd|Hanji 617]] describes more than a year of killings and says elite society was shaken, with Feng Meng fleeing to Liaodong.
- Private-scandal leverage and concealment: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-617-wangmang-dusha-gongzhu-lishi-shimo-lqvzejfm1sd8k0amporgi_mqhsrd|Hanji 617]] says Jingwu Princess is forced to drink poison over an alleged sexual scandal and that Wang Mang later tells Wang Zhengjun she died from illness.
- Rebellion-to-harboring expansion: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-402-1-sanghongyang-ceng-bang-hanwudi-zhifu-weihe-zuihou-canzao-miezu-ltpcos20d5mafcoywmfcb6iifwsv|Hanji 402-1]] says Sang Hongyang's Liu Dan-linked destruction is followed by Sang Qian's flight and Hou Shiwu's sheltering case.
- Amnesty reinterpretation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-402-1-sanghongyang-ceng-bang-hanwudi-zhifu-weihe-zuihou-canzao-miezu-ltpcos20d5mafcoywmfcb6iifwsv|Hanji 402-1]] says Wang Ping and Xu Ren first treat Hou Shiwu as pardonable, but Huo Guang's review recasts Sang Qian and Hou Shiwu as rebellion-adjacent and accuses the officials of indulging rebels.
- Chancellor exposure: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-402-1-sanghongyang-ceng-bang-hanwudi-zhifu-weihe-zuihou-canzao-miezu-ltpcos20d5mafcoywmfcb6iifwsv|Hanji 402-1]] says Tian Qianqiu tries to protect Xu Ren, his son-in-law, but the court follows Huo Guang's line and Du Yannian must argue against implicating Tian too far.
- Reopened dismissal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-557-wangmang-pk-chunyuchang-nanrenjian-de-jingxin-jiaoliang-lnvmnizx0mhrln-g1p1a8u_9kgnx|Hanji 557]] says Chunyu Zhang's attempt to recover office through Wang Li leads Chengdi to investigate further.
- Wider cleanup: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-557-wangmang-pk-chunyuchang-nanrenjian-de-jingxin-jiaoliang-lnvmnizx0mhrln-g1p1a8u_9kgnx|Hanji 557]] says Zhai Fangjin later impeaches Wang Li's network, Chunyu Zhang's close officials, and Du Ye, resulting in more than twenty senior removals.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The current concept is grounded in three Western Han source notes. It should not be used as a universal explanation for every purge or every collateral punishment case. Some named victims may have had independent political exposure not fully preserved in the episode summaries, and the exact legal categories, death counts, and evidentiary chains remain source-scoped. Hanji 402-1 supports a legal-classification expansion from rebellion to harboring and official liability, but does not prove the full legal merits of Wang Ping and Xu Ren's original judgment. Hanji 557 supports Zhai Fangjin's widening behavior, but not the private motive behind every impeachment. The concept overlaps with [[CollateralPunishmentAndGuiltByAssociation|collateral punishment and guilt by association]], but its distinctive focus is the process by which investigators and powerholders widen a specific case into a broader political cleanup.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 402-1's legal-classification variant: Sang Hongyang's rebellion-linked purge widens through Sang Qian, Hou Shiwu, amnesty interpretation, Wang Ping, Xu Ren, and Tian Qianqiu's exposed kinship tie.
- Preserved Hanji 557 as a case-expansion example where Chunyu Zhang's failed restoration attempt widens into palace scandal, Wang Li's removal, and Zhai Fangjin's broader impeachment wave.
