# Speech As Moral Diagnostic / 言语作为德行信号

Speech as moral diagnostic / 言语作为德行信号 is the ethical reading [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-119-2-zanmen-dou-yuanwang-le-beifu-2000-duonian-heiguo-de-yegong-haolong-lh-h-gezpqtvnyq40zlqac8s-5e]] draws from [[BaiGongSheng|白公胜]] and [[YeGong|叶公]]. The episode rejects the excuse that harmful speech can be dismissed as "刀子嘴豆腐心." In its view, a person's language is evidence of intention, and intention can become action when circumstances open a path.

The concept is grounded in Bai Gong Sheng's threat while sharpening a sword. He says plainly that he is preparing to kill [[ZiXiChu|子西]], and the later [[BaiGongShengRebellion|白公胜之乱]] makes that speech look diagnostic rather than merely rude. The host extends the point through body, speech, and intention: good conduct requires disciplined action, measured words, and benevolent thought.

This concept does not say every harsh sentence proves future violence. It says repeated or explicit cruel speech should be treated as morally and politically meaningful because it reveals what the speaker permits in thought before acting.

## Key Claims
- Speech is not only expression; it can reveal permission structures inside the speaker's intent.
- "Bluntness" is not a sufficient excuse when words carry contempt, threat, or cruelty.
- Moral cultivation links intention, speech, and action, so a person should train all three rather than treating words as harmless.
- Personnel judgment should attend to how someone speaks when frustrated, not only to talent, bravery, or outward promise-keeping.
