# Family Protection Insurance Planning

Family protection insurance planning is the household-responsibility approach to life and accident coverage. In [[ep18-dou-shi-huang-quan-yu-yue-ke-baoxian-mai-dui-xin-an-le-lly-wx9zecfmov5cbmufmynlosw]], [[Xiaodai]] argues that the first question is not which policy is popular, but who creates income, who depends on that income, what debts remain, and how long those responsibilities last.

[[you-bet-your-life-insurance]] adds a later-life exit boundary through [[FrankLifeSettlementSeller|Frank]]. A policy bought as family protection can become a [[LifeSettlement|life settlement]] candidate if coverage needs, health history, premium burden, other insurance, and household risk capacity change enough that present cash competes with the future death benefit.

## Key Claims
- The person who earns more and supports more dependents usually has the larger insurance need.
- Term life insurance is a responsibility-window tool: it can cover parents, children, mortgages, and other obligations until those obligations shrink.
- Whole life insurance serves a different role because it is more naturally tied to inheritance, beneficiary designation, estate planning, or permanent death-benefit needs.
- Large death-benefit policies for children are often misaligned because a child's death does not usually create the same income-interruption problem as a parent's death.
- High income, strong health habits, or confidence at work do not remove risk when a household also has mortgages, young children, and one main earner.
- A life insurance policy can later become a sellable asset, but selling it should be judged against the family protection it replaces, not only against the headline cash offer.
