## Definition

The Vera Cruz legal coup was [[HernanCortes|Hernan Cortes]]' use of a newly founded town and its council to replace his authority from Cuba with offices purportedly held in direct service to the Spanish crown.

## Current Synthesis

Cortes faced a jurisdictional problem because [[DiegoVelazquezDeCuellar|Diego Velazquez de Cuellar]] had not authorized conquest and had tried to recall him. The episode says Cortes founded Villa Rica de la Vera Cruz, resigned his expedition command, and accepted appointment by the town council as chief justice and captain general. Sending treasure to Spain rather than Cuba then supported an appeal for royal recognition.

The maneuver did not settle legality by itself. It converted open defiance into a legal claim and bought a path toward later confirmation, showing that political improvisation was part of conquest infrastructure. Beaching the ships complemented the maneuver materially by limiting retreat and committing the company to the inland gamble.

## Key Claims

- Cortes lacked delegated authority from Velazquez to conquer Mexico.
- Founding a municipality created an alternative body able to appoint him to new offices.
- Formal resignation and reappointment reframed rebellion as direct royal service.
- Sending treasure to Spain appealed over the governor's head for recognition.
- Beaching rather than burning the ships made legal commitment and material commitment reinforce each other.

- Royal appeal and commitment: [[385-the-fall-of-the-aztecs-the-woman-who-changed-the-world-part-2-glt5124604457]] connects the treasure shipment and beached ships to the break with Cuba and inland advance.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Calling the maneuver a legal coup describes its political function, not a settled judgment that the new offices were valid when created. The episode supplies the broad sequence but not a complete legal record, the views of every participant, or the later crown proceedings.

## What Changed

- Established municipal incorporation as a causal step between unauthorized exploration and inland conquest.
- Corrected the ship-burning legend: the expedition beached its ships and reused some timber.
