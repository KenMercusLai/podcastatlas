# Existentialism / 存在主义

## Definition
Existentialism / 存在主义 is the wiki's current label for source material about selfhood, freedom, meaning, judgment, and responsibility under conditions where inherited certainty or social approval cannot settle how a person should live.

## Current Synthesis
[[135-xianliao-weida-zuojiamen-de-bagua-di-san-dan-823746670]] introduces existentialism through [[JeanPaulSartre|Jean-Paul Sartre / 萨特]]'s wartime and postwar influence. The episode says existentialism helped young people in post-liberation France find personal meaning after despair, which explains part of Sartre's national-hero status. The same source deflates that image by placing Sartre's philosophy beside his occupation-era career success, limited resistance activity, left politics, open relationship, and late-life reconsideration of despair.

A Hesse-adjacent branch comes from [[20-demian-gaiyin-de-etou-shang-na-shenmi-de-yinji-567302734]] rather than from a formal existentialist doctrine page. The episode places [[DemianNovel|《德米安》]] near [[FriedrichNietzsche|Nietzsche]] and existential questions because [[EmilSinclair|辛克莱尔]] has to choose a self under war, taboo, and inherited moral pressure. This makes meaning a lived symbolic crisis, not only a postwar French philosophical language.

A compact Sartre-theater branch comes through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-395-2-sanren-beihou-de-renxing-gushi-lnxghfd6esj7jbir-m6qflth2ckp]] and [[NoExit|No Exit / 《禁闭》]]. In that source, "他人就是地狱" is not a rejection of all other people; it names the hellish quality of damaged relations and overdependence on others' judgment. Existentialist freedom therefore appears as a practical demand to change behavior and resist confinement by hostile evaluation.

## Key Claims
- Philosophical language can become socially powerful when it gives people a way to name meaning after crisis.
- A public philosophy should not be used to launder the author's private or political conduct.
- Meaning and selfhood can appear as lived crisis before they appear as formal doctrine.
- Sartre's "other people are hell" line is best treated as a relational and judgment-dependence claim, not as proof that all human contact is inherently degrading.
- Freedom requires changed conduct as well as insight into the social pressures that confine the self.

## Evidence
- Postwar meaning language: [[135-xianliao-weida-zuojiamen-de-bagua-di-san-dan-823746670]] says Sartre-associated existentialism gave young people a way to find meaning after despair.
- Author-image qualification: [[135-xianliao-weida-zuojiamen-de-bagua-di-san-dan-823746670]] places Sartre's influence beside political, wartime, and intimate-life criticisms.
- Lived symbolic crisis: [[20-demian-gaiyin-de-etou-shang-na-shenmi-de-yinji-567302734]] connects [[DemianNovel|《德米安》]] to self-choice under war, taboo, and inherited moral pressure.
- Relational judgment and freedom: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-395-2-sanren-beihou-de-renxing-gushi-lnxghfd6esj7jbir-m6qflth2ckp]] explains [[OtherAsHell|他人即地狱]] through damaged relations, dependence on judgment, and the need to change behavior.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The page is a synthesis of podcast interpretations, not a full taxonomy of existentialist philosophy. Hesse is adjacent rather than a formal Sartrean doctrinal source. Sartre's biographical criticisms and the short Hanji 395-2 reading of [[NoExit|《禁闭》]] remain source-scoped.
