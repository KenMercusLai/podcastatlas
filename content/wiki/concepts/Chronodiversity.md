# Chronodiversity

## Definition
Chronodiversity is the recognition that living systems and environments make and register time in multiple variable ways, and that these temporalities deserve consideration alongside uniform human clocks and calendars.

## Current Synthesis
[[02026-heggli-keats-csank-podcast-v2]] presents [[JonathanKeats]]'s chronodiversity as an ecological and civic challenge to standardized time. A bristlecone's growth, a river's flow, or a changing meander does not merely occur inside an external calendar; it can set the clock's rate. The artistic value lies in making environmental feedback legible in everyday temporal terms and widening whose experience has authority in collective decisions.

## Key Claims
- Standardized time supports coordination but can hide ecological variation and feedback.
- Living and environmental processes can serve as legitimate temporal standards without being uniform.
- A clock that speeds up or slows down with ecological change makes that change part of public timekeeping.
- Temporal plurality can support multispecies attention by treating nonhuman response as information rather than deviation.
- Chronodiversity is primarily an interpretive and civic frame in this source, not a replacement metrology standard.

## Evidence
### Bristlecone Calendar
- [[02026-heggli-keats-csank-podcast-v2]] describes dated markers around a sapling whose future contact depends on the tree's variable growth.

### Municipal Clock
- [[02026-heggli-keats-csank-podcast-v2]] says Keats developed a public clock whose pendulum rate is tied to bristlecone growth, carrying the trees' time from a remote mountain into Reno.

### Other Environmental Clocks
- [[02026-heggli-keats-csank-podcast-v2]] connects the proposal to related projects calibrated by river flow and changing river meanders.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Ecological clocks can be ambiguous, fragile, site-specific, and difficult to compare. Standardized time remains necessary for many forms of coordination, measurement, and accountability. The source provides an artistic-philosophical argument for plural temporal authority, not evidence that variable living clocks can replace conventional timekeeping in technical systems.

## What Changed
- Established chronodiversity as the explicit normative frame behind Keats's living and environmental clocks.
- Distinguished ecological feedback and civic attention from precision timekeeping.
