# Helicobacter pylori Household Management / 幽门螺杆菌家庭管理

## Definition
Helicobacter pylori household management is the source's framework for interpreting a positive infection test within shared household exposure, gastric-disease risk, treatment, and possible reinfection rather than as an isolated food myth or automatic cancer diagnosis.

## Current Synthesis
The episode links H. pylori to elevated gastric-cancer risk while preserving the distinction between infection and cancer. Because close contact and shared eating practices may contribute to transmission, it encourages household members to discuss testing and coordinated treatment with clinicians and presents serving utensils as a practical exposure-reduction measure.

The useful principle is coordination, not automatic identical treatment. A positive breath test establishes infection in the tested person; symptoms, age, prior treatment, contraindications, local resistance, and confirmation of eradication still require qualified interpretation. VOL.53 adds an adherence and retesting layer: adult eradication is described as a complete clinician-prescribed multidrug course, while a later positive breath test should be interpreted with medicine washout, false-positive possibility, and repeat or alternative assessment rather than triggering automatic retreatment.

## Key Claims
- H. pylori infection is a gastric-cancer risk factor, not proof that cancer is present or inevitable.
- Household exposure and possible reinfection can make one person's result relevant to close family members.
- Family-aware testing and treatment discussion may be more useful than repeatedly treating one person without considering the household context.
- Serving utensils and reduced saliva-sharing are practical hygiene measures described by the source.
- A positive breath test, regimen choice, uninterrupted completion, and eradication confirmation belong inside clinician-guided care.
- A later positive breath test is not self-interpreting because medicines, timing, and test limitations can affect the result.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
These podcast sources are not current H. pylori guidelines. They do not establish that every household member is infected, that communal eating explains any particular case, that all family members should receive the same regimen, or that every adult or child follows one testing and treatment rule. Prevalence, reinfection estimates, test accuracy, medicine washout, indications, antibiotic resistance, contraindications, timing, eradication confirmation, and cancer surveillance are person- and region-specific.

## What Changed
- Added a household-aware infection-management concept separating coordinated prevention from automatic family-wide diagnosis or treatment.
- Added treatment-completion and retesting safeguards without turning the episode's regimen or timing examples into a universal protocol.
