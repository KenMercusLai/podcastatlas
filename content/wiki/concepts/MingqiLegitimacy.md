# Mingqi Legitimacy / 名器合法性

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-901-jixingzi-de-ren-hui-shi-shenme-xiachang-lh1yfyjzqptxvtnv9taiqms9yjud|Hanji 901]] adds an imperial-burial branch before the later title, seal, and captured-court cases. [[EmpressDowagerDouMiao|窦皇太后]]'s place beside [[EmperorHuanOfHan|汉桓帝]], her continued sacrificial recognition, and the posthumous title Huan Si Empress all function as `名器`: they decide whether a deceased dowager remains inside the official role order.

The episode shows why symbolic status is politically active. [[CaoJieLateHan|曹节]] and [[WangFuLateHan|王甫]] resist the burial because recognizing Dou would also pressure the memory of [[DouWuLateHan|窦武]] and [[ChenFanLateHan|陈蕃]], while [[LiXianTaiweiLateHan|李贤]] and [[ChenQiuLateHan|陈球 / 陈求]] argue that later officials should not casually undo a settled mother-son and ruler-minister status field.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-931-rensheng-jiannan-chu-bianshi-xiuxin-shi-lpzmvxop8lmlwvxhoojzj5ok9qd6|Hanji 931]] adds an official-death procedure layer before the later seal, edict, and court-title crises. In prison, [[WangYunLateHan|王允]] refuses poison because a minister who has offended the ruler should be executed through formal court judgment and public announcement.

This source makes `名器` broader than objects alone. The office-holder's body and death also belong to a public role order: private suicide may end suffering, but it would let a captured court environment erase the visible meaning of judgment and office responsibility.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-938-lishi-de-jingxing-ruanruo-zhihui-dailai-quru-lnlnrketgfbadhfjbzdjjtrmqfsh|Hanji 938]] adds a pre-Dong-Zhuo rebel-title warning before the missing-seal and forced-deposition layers. [[ZhangJuLateHan|张举]] declares himself emperor and demands that [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]] abdicate, while the court also sells Guannei marquis rank for cash. The case shows title order already under pressure before the later capital seizure: names and ranks still matter, but their public exclusivity is weakening.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-946-zhichang-douzheng-shi-yourouguaduan-you-duo-zhiming-lpyyweaajonzg7kxuhwm9z7ae7p5|Hanji 946]] adds the pre-Dong-Zhuo damage layer before Hanji 947's recovered five seals and missing [[HeirloomImperialSeal|传国玉玺]]. After [[HeJin|何进]] is killed, [[LiuBianHongnongWang|刘辩 / 弘农王]] and Liu Xie / [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]] are taken out of the palace by [[ZhangRangLateHan|张让]] and [[DuanGuiLateHan|段珪]] without the six imperial seals or a normal escort.

This shows `名器` as material absence during flight, not just symbolic procedure after return. The imperial title remains worth fighting over, but the objects, guard formation, and stable command channels that should carry it are already scattered before Dong Zhuo begins using the court's symbolic order.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-947-dongzhuo-shoufu-lvbu-kaiqi-chengba-zhilu-lvr-24l6j8f6zvpfcmqgknmgywa1|Hanji 947]] adds the missing-seal precondition before Hanji 948's seal-transfer ceremony and Hanji 955's recovered-seal branch. After [[LiuBianHongnongWang|刘辩 / 弘农王]] returns to the palace, the source says five imperial seals are recovered while the [[HeirloomImperialSeal|传国玉玺]] is not found.

This episode shows `名器` first as loss, not use. The imperial office still matters, but the visible objects that should stabilize authority are already exposed to disorder before Dong Zhuo formalizes his takeover.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-948-dongzhuo-feidi-quanchen-ruhe-gaibian-huangquan-youxi-ltekg1a-5b5u37aeekjm3dm2m6kk|Hanji 948]] adds the internal replacement case before the forced-relocation and alternative-sovereign branches. [[DongZhuo|董卓]] can intimidate the court, but he still needs edict language, [[YuanWeiLateHan|袁隗]]'s seal-transfer role, and a visible ceremony to move title from [[LiuBianHongnongWang|刘辩 / 弘农王]] to [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]].

This source sharpens `名器` by showing why a coercive actor preserves symbolic procedure. The old title order is weak enough to be captured, but strong enough that Dong Zhuo wants the court to perform the replacement rather than abandoning the office vocabulary altogether.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-951-tianjiang-hengcai-jiujing-shi-haoshi-haishi-huaishi-lovwgng2wrtfno-s9ttpybbhksvh|Hanji 951]] adds the forced-relocation precondition before the alternative-sovereign debate. [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]] is moved from [[LuoyangLateHan|洛阳]] to [[ChangAnHanCapital|长安]] under [[DongZhuo|董卓]]'s coercion, while officials still argue through the court's ancestral and title order.

The source also adds the inhibited-action side of `名器`. [[LiangYanLateHan|梁衍]] proposes that [[HuangfuSongLateHan|皇甫嵩]] use his troops to welcome the emperor and attack Dong Zhuo, but Huangfu Song refuses partly because Dong Zhuo still stands inside the formal court structure. The emperor's recognized title can therefore enable a rescue plan while also making action against the title's current handler politically dangerous.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-954-renxingbenshan-haishi-renxingbene-lkurz9dbb25jrjyl5c-rrahda39h|Hanji 954]] adds the alternative-sovereign side of late-Han title legitimacy before the seal and Xudu branches. [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]] is controlled by [[DongZhuo|董卓]], but his title remains recognized enough that [[CaoCao|曹操]] rejects replacing him and [[LiuYuLateHan|刘虞]] refuses the imperial title offered by [[YuanShao|袁绍]] and [[HanFuLateHan|韩馥]].

This source sharpens the concept by showing that `名器` can constrain action even when the holder is weak. A captured emperor creates a practical command problem, but installing a new emperor before the old ruler has forfeited recognition would turn the anti-Dong coalition's public reason into a legitimacy liability.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-955-guanyu-zhan-huaxiong-qishi-shi-wei-lishi-lndkrj-fnepye8iu-ifgyh-vzysf|Hanji 955]] adds the discovery side of the [[HeirloomImperialSeal|传国玉玺]] before Yuan Shu's later title ambition. [[SunJianLateHan|孙坚]] enters damaged [[LuoyangLateHan|洛阳]], cleans the imperial temples, performs sacrifice, and finds the seal in a palace well. The object is powerful enough to matter later, but in this source its force begins as ritual recovery inside a ruined capital rather than as a sufficient claim to rule.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-966-meiyou-yexin-budong-quanmou-jiu-bie-dang-lingdao-lldn3hi1gl5v3axvklwakgf6vx3u|Hanji 966]] adds a captured-summons version before the later office and flight branches. After [[LiJueLateHan|李傕]] and [[GuoSiLateHan|郭汜]] take [[ChangAnHanCapital|长安]], [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]]'s court can still issue amnesty, grant offices, and summon officials, but those forms now operate inside remnant military coercion. [[SongYiLateHan|宋翼]] treats the summons as binding; [[WangHongLateHan|王宏]] reads it as a trap. The episode therefore shows `名器` as still meaningful but dangerous when its command channel is captured.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-967-ta-canyu-mousha-dongzhuo-jingneng-taoguo-sishen-weilie-lv24biyswj9ljdld5htynhauhph3|Hanji 967]] adds an earlier captured-court title layer. After [[LiJueLateHan|李傕]] and [[GuoSiLateHan|郭汜]] take [[ChangAnHanCapital|长安]], the court still appoints [[LiuBiao|刘表]] as Zhengnan general, Jingzhou governor, and Chengwu marquis, while removing [[HuangfuSongLateHan|皇甫嵩]] as taiwei. The case sharpens the concept because formal office still orders public recognition even when coercive control over the capital belongs to armed remnant commanders.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-969-yuanshi-xiongdi-neidou-shui-neng-jianlou-lsgj6sewo35jpsgfm-koivijtr6r|Hanji 969]] adds an earlier late-Han use before Yuan Shu's self-coronation. [[LiJueLateHan|李傕]] can grant [[YuanShu|袁术]] left-general, Yangzhai-marquis, and `假节` recognition from a captured court, and Yuan Shu still benefits because the title package gives public language to his [[Shouchun|寿春]] position. The case sharpens the concept: `名器` can remain useful even when everyone can see that the issuing court is materially and militarily hollow.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-979-zaoyu-tongliao-beici-ta-ruhe-wei-caocao-liwan-kuanglan-luu58a2ww11g-radh-5g4l7fcis|Hanji 979]] adds the office-capture version of late-Han `名器`. [[GuoSiLateHan|郭汜]], [[FanChouLateHan|樊稠]], and later [[YangDingLateHan|杨定]] receive titles and kaifu-style privileges, while [[LiJueLateHan|李傕]] already holds comparable privilege. The titles still matter, but their effect is to move appointment authority toward armed actors rather than to restore a coherent court hierarchy.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-982-ba-yi-ba-liubei-ru-xu-beihou-de-liyi-zhi-zheng-ljajdc95xtylhkn5teefz26yyyz1|Hanji 982]] adds the seized-tally version before the later Yuan Shu self-coronation branch. [[MaRidiLateHan|马日磾]] arrives at [[Shouchun|寿春]] with imperial authority, but [[YuanShu|袁术]] takes the tally, uses it to force appointments, and tries to keep Ma Ridi as an adviser. The source shows a symbolic object still matters enough to seize, while also showing that its authority can be redirected by armed coercion.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-985-fanmu-chengchou-liguo-zhiluan-de-xijie-tanjiu-lt-t2un0zcudkige9ucl1txa2hhk|Hanji 985]] adds the household-office version before later title grants and road ceremonies. [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]] appoints [[FuShouEmpress|伏寿]] as empress and [[FuWanLateHan|伏完]] as zhijinwu while [[LiJueLateHan|李傕]] and [[GuoSiLateHan|郭汜]] control the emperor and ministers. The appointment has real name-order meaning, but the same source shows its weakened operating base: court rank cannot prevent guarded doors, hunger, rotten bones, or arrows reaching the emperor's tent area.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-986-tianyu-qiwang-bixian-lingqi-kuang-lvlvu057-tnz3hal6oodhydvzvbd|Hanji 986]] adds the coerced-office version before the departure sequence. [[LiJueLateHan|李傕]] is appointed 大司马 while holding [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]], so the title remains formally powerful but is generated under hostage pressure. The episode also shows [[HuangfuLiLateHan|皇甫郦]] still using the emperor's edict vocabulary to judge Li Jue, even though Li Jue can refuse the edict and threaten the envoy.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-988-shoujin-lingru-ta-shi-hanmo-beican-de-huangdi-lsdkpakvk5u-omegcri-wstup-zl|Hanji 988]] adds the visible-body version before the route-control crisis of Hanji 989. [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]] can be stopped by soldiers at the bridge out of Chang'an, meaning the title alone no longer guarantees passage. Yet once [[LiuAiLateHan|刘艾]] and the lifted curtain make the emperor publicly visible, his rebuke still makes [[GuoSiLateHan|郭汜]]'s soldiers stand aside; Mingqi has not disappeared, but it works only in a narrow, exposed moment.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-989-ningwei-taipingquan-buzuo-luanshi-ren-lkoycygmjnltuvpq-6wjv-ls61s6|Hanji 989]] adds the title-grant side before the court's material collapse becomes even more visible. [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]] can still grant recognized offices to [[ZhangJiLateHan|张济]], [[GuoSiLateHan|郭汜]], [[YangDingLateHan|杨定]], [[YangFengLateHan|杨奉]], and [[DongChengLateHan|董承]], but those titles do not settle who controls the emperor's route, food, or camp access. Mingqi therefore remains valuable while its command force is badly weakened.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-991-zhengduo-xiandi-zhizhan-shui-neng-zuizhong-qusheng-lkbsl5uzqxouzm6mlewrnuns0x6p|Hanji 991]] adds the battlefield-loss side of Mingqi legitimacy. During [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]]'s flight, imperial objects, tallies, books, and archives are lost amid the pursuit by [[LiJueLateHan|李傕]], [[GuoSiLateHan|郭汜]], and [[ZhangJiLateHan|张济]]. The title remains politically valuable enough for armies to fight over the emperor, but the visible objects and records that normally carry court authority are scattered or destroyed.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-992-luanshi-you-duo-kepa-jiemi-changan-de-xuese-mengyan-ltuxzrb-1x5y2ctwo4drggxn5stz|Hanji 992]] backfills the damaged-court side of Mingqi legitimacy. At [[Anyi|安邑]], [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]] can still reward officials, grant ranks, and make appointments, but the court lacks proper food, buildings, and even enough seals, so emergency wooden seals have to stand in for normal official objects. The source shows that `名器` can keep political force after material capacity collapses, but only in a fragile and improvised form.

The Hanji 1015-1016 late-Han branch later develops the explicit title-and-symbol vocabulary. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1015-sanguo-luanshi-weishenme-yuanshu-gan-chengdi-lim0jemkwyjcesj2nvsr3jndhxfk|Hanji 1015]] supplies the initiating case: [[YuanShu|袁术]] declares himself emperor at [[Shouchun|寿春]], names the regime "仲家", renames the [[JiujiangCommandery|九江郡]] office into a [[HuainanRegion|淮南]] capital-style post, and performs imperial ritual. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1016-sanguo-luanshi-li-chengui-jiazu-de-mingyun-jueze-ltuckdcxpyxk-xmzp8rt-2gjfzcs|Hanji 1016]] then opens from the principle that "名器" should not be lightly accepted. "名" covers titles, rank, offices, and named political roles; "器" covers the physical objects that make power visible, such as seals, tallies, robes, vehicles, and insignia.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1001-weishenme-quan-ni-buyao-fanzhongnu-lolurkcjpie14nnczk0tu7iqerke|Hanji 1001]] adds the material prelude before the public title claim. Yuan Shu combines the "代汉者当涂高" prophecy, [[YinYangFivePhasesPoliticalTheory|five-phases]] reasoning, and the [[HeirloomImperialSeal|传国玉玺]] as support for imperial ambition, but [[YanXiangLateHan|阎象]] and [[ZhangChengLateHan|张承]] show that symbolic objects and omen readings cannot compensate for missing virtue, accepted name order, and social recognition.

The concept is not about symbols replacing force. Its point is that force becomes more or less usable depending on whether the symbolic order around it is accepted. Yuan Shu claims an imperial name without enough backing reality, so the title becomes a liability. [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]]'s court, by contrast, still lets [[CaoCao|曹操]] describe the campaign against Yuan Shu as punishment of an illegitimate actor rather than as ordinary warlord rivalry.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1002-yuanshu-chengdi-hou-sunce-chengwei-zui-da-ying-llya3c2-pefujwxdxefvqxwxjizjo|Hanji 1002]] adds the earlier two-sided use of the same symbolic field. [[SunCe|孙策]] treats Emperor Xian's still-recognized title as a reason Yuan Shu cannot claim succession from Han, while [[XunYu|荀彧]] treats that title as a strategic opening for Cao Cao. The same weak sovereign title therefore blocks one claimant and enables another actor's [[LegitimacyWindowSeizure|合法性窗口抢占]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1004-caocao-yingfeng-hanxiandi-shi-shui-de-zhuyi-lrrga-flmx5ajnhu1c42x7kooe94|Hanji 1004]] adds the earlier court-site condition for that vocabulary. Once [[CaoCao|曹操]] moves [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]] from ruined [[LuoyangLateHan|洛阳]] to [[Xudu|许县 / 许都]], the emperor can still appoint, reward, and legitimate, but the effective setting has shifted toward Cao Cao's control.

Hanji 1015 adds the coalition-pressure side of the same title problem. Yuan Shu's name claim forces other actors to protect their own public standing: Cao Cao's Han-court role, [[LiuBei|刘备]]'s仁义 persona, and the opportunistic calculations of [[SunCe|孙策]], [[YuanShao|袁绍]], and [[LyuBu|吕布]] all make non-response costly. This becomes [[AntiUsurpationCoalitionPressure|讨逆同盟压力]] rather than only personal overreach.

Hanji 1016 also shows the personal risk of mishandled名器. [[JinShang|金尚]] refuses Yuan Shu's taiwei title and is killed, while [[ChenGui|陈珪]] refuses Yuan Shu's appeal and then steers [[LyuBu|吕布]] away from a marriage alliance. Accepting or rejecting titles is therefore not ornamental; it changes legitimacy, danger, and alliance exposure.

## Key Claims
- Hanji 901 adds burial and co-sacrifice as symbolic infrastructure: tomb placement and posthumous title can recognize or erase a deceased political role.
- Hanji 931 adds that formal judgment and public execution can be part of role legitimacy: an official's death is not only biological but procedural.
- Hanji 938 adds that unsupported imperial self-coronation and cash sale of rank can weaken title exclusivity even before the capital court is physically captured.
- Hanji 946 adds that symbolic legitimacy can be damaged before formal takeover: the emperor can survive while seals, escort, and command channels are absent during flight.
- Hanji 947 adds that symbolic legitimacy can first appear as loss: missing seals show the court's material authority exposed before later actors use the objects.
- Hanji 948 adds that even a forced deposition needs seal, edict, and office procedure because the captured symbolic order remains politically useful.
- Hanji 951 adds that the emperor's title can make a rescue plan imaginable while also making attack on a court-controlling powerholder legally and socially dangerous.
- Hanji 955 adds that symbolic objects can enter political circulation through ritual recovery from institutional ruin before becoming later title-claim evidence.
- Hanji 966 adds that summonses, amnesty, and offices can remain formally authoritative while the command channel is captured, making obedience itself a judgment problem.
- Hanji 967 adds that appointment and removal can keep ordering the public title field immediately after military capture of the capital.
- Hanji 979 adds that title legitimacy can be hollowed through office multiplication: the court keeps naming roles while armed men occupy the appointment machinery behind those names.
- Hanji 982 adds that a court tally can remain valuable while being physically seized and repurposed by a regional warlord.
- Hanji 985 adds that imperial household and office appointments can continue inside captivity, but their command force is exposed by bodily deprivation and armed access.
- Hanji 986 adds that offices can still be issued while the sovereign is coerced, making title recognition real but politically contaminated.
- Hanji 988 adds that a ruler's visible body can still activate title awe even after armed men have already blocked his route.
- Hanji 989 adds that title grants can still placate and classify armed actors even when those titles no longer command their route choices.
- Hanji 991 adds that imperial symbols can be physically lost while the sovereign title remains valuable enough to trigger pursuit and battle.
- Hanji 992 adds that recognized titles can keep functioning in an improvised court, but the visible degradation of seals, housing, and food reveals the gap between symbolic authority and state capacity.
- Hanji 1001 adds that even a powerful object like the Heirloom Imperial Seal can become false confidence when it is not backed by virtue, adviser acceptance, and public recognition.
- Hanji 1015 adds that taking the imperial title can create coalition pressure before a full military collapse has occurred.
- Hanji 1002 adds that the old title can be simultaneously a shield against unsupported usurpation and an opportunity for the actor who first protects the court.
- Titles and ritual objects can act as political infrastructure, not merely decoration.
- A title that outruns military capacity and social recognition can intensify backlash.
- A weak but still recognized court can give stronger actors a legitimacy vocabulary.
- Accepting office from an unsupported claimant can compromise the recipient, while refusing it can create immediate danger.
- The concept links personal survival to public symbolic order: an actor must judge both what the title says and who can enforce or punish it.
