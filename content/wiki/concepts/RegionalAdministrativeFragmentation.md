# Regional Administrative Fragmentation

Regional administrative fragmentation is the development risk created when an economic region is split across multiple local governments that may duplicate infrastructure, compete for projects, and weaken shared planning. In [[no-205-chaoshan-wangshi-qiaopi-jingji-tequ-he-meiyou-denglaide-shenzhen-qiji-989721046]], the 1991 division of the old Shantou region into [[Shantou]], [[Chaozhou]], and [[Jieyang]] is presented as one reason [[Chaoshan]] had trouble preserving scale.

The concept is not a claim that administrative separation is always bad. The episode acknowledges that separate cities can gain autonomy, but it emphasizes the tradeoff: when a region already lacks [[Shenzhen]]'s large special-zone base and close [[HongKong]] adjacency, fragmented planning can make [[MerchantToIndustrialCityUpgrade]] harder.

## Key Claims
- Regional economies and administrative boundaries do not always align.
- Splitting a region can improve local autonomy while reducing shared infrastructure and industrial coordination.
- Competition among nearby jurisdictions can produce duplicated facilities and "small but complete" industrial structures.
- Fragmentation can interact with [[ChinaSpecialEconomicZoneAsymmetry]] and [[ExportTaxFraudCreditCrisis]] rather than operating alone.
