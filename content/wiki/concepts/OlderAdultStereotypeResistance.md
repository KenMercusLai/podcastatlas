# Older Adult Stereotype Resistance / 老年人刻板印象抵抗

## Definition
Older adult stereotype resistance / 老年人刻板印象抵抗 is the refusal to treat older people as a uniform category defined by frailty, confusion, dependency, danger, or sentimental wisdom rather than as specific persons with varied capacities and contradictions.

## Current Synthesis
The episode's aging frame is that "old people" do not exist as a useful moral type. 《前浪2》 uses cases that are deliberately hard to summarize: a 95-year-old learner driver can be inspiring and stubborn; a bereaved older adult can be funny on the surface and grieving underneath; a companion can be loving and legally unprotected; a parent can be cared for and resented; a caregiver can be both needed and suspect.

The current synthesis is that anti-ageist representation should not replace negative stereotypes with positive stereotypes. It should preserve specificity, including desire, incapacity, humor, fear, selfishness, courage, pain, and ordinary preference, because [[PatientDignityInDailyCare]] and [[SubjectLedCare]] both depend on seeing the person before the category.

## Key Claims
- Treating older adults as automatically confused, dangerous, selfless, weak, or inspirational is a form of category violence.
- Specificity matters more than typicality: aging cases can reveal social problems without making one person stand for all elders.
- Rights and autonomy should not disappear because an action appears age-inappropriate to younger observers.
- Old-age dignity includes unflattering traits as well as admirable ones; complexity is part of personhood.
- Representation can support [[ElderCareStateCapacity]] by showing concrete needs instead of vague respect-for-elders slogans.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Anti-stereotype work does not remove real risk around driving, cognitive decline, fraud, dependency, or medical capacity. The source's point is that these risks need concrete assessment rather than categorical denial of older adults' rights or personhood.
