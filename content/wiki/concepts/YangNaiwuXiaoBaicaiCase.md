## Definition

The 杨乃武与小白菜案 / Yang Naiwu and Xiao Baicai case was a late-Qing wrongful-conviction case in which the sudden death of Xiao Baicai's husband led local officials to accuse [[XiaoBaicai|Xiao Baicai]] and [[YangNaiwu|Yang Naiwu]] of adultery and poisoning, obtain confessions through torture, and repeatedly affirm the resulting judgment before a capital review exposed the error.

## Current Synthesis

[[29-dongxifang-de-yuanan-deleifusi-yangnaiwu-he-xiaobaicai-lmmhqfl-idiuetsvufgrmpso2l-8|episode 29]] presents the case as a cumulative institutional failure. Rumor about the defendants' relationship and the magistrate's prior hostility toward Yang narrowed suspicion early; torture then supplied confessions without reliable proof of poison purchase, transfer, or use. Successive levels tended to approve the inherited finding rather than re-investigate it, and the defendants were repeatedly exposed to coercion.

Correction came only after the case escaped that local review chain. Yang's family petitioned in Beijing, [[HuXueyan|Hu Xueyan]] supplied money and connections, sympathetic metropolitan officials drew attention to defects, and [[ShenBao|《申报》]] sustained public scrutiny. A capital retrial and post-mortem examination eventually overturned the murder accusation, but Yang's rank, health, and livelihood and Xiao Baicai's reputation and life course were not fully restored.

## Key Claims

- Social rumor and official hostility created a presumption of guilt before reliable evidence was established.
- Torture converted that presumption into confessions while basic poison evidence remained unverified.
- Hierarchical approval reproduced the original error because reviewing officials had weak incentives to reopen facts or expose colleagues.
- Family persistence, elite support, metropolitan review, and newspaper publicity formed a correction coalition outside the original local chain.
- Acquittal removed the capital accusation without restoring all status, health, reputation, or lost years.
- Later opera, fiction, film, and television romanticized the relationship, making public memory diverge from the episode's evidentiary account.

- Administrative persistence: [[29-dongxifang-de-yuanan-deleifusi-yangnaiwu-he-xiaobaicai-lmmhqfl-idiuetsvufgrmpso2l-8|episode 29]] describes repeated county, prefectural, provincial, and court handling that largely preserved the initial conclusion.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This synthesis rests on one podcast source rather than Qing case files, a complete run of newspaper reports, or specialist legal history. The cause and timing of death, official titles and procedure, autopsy findings, number and content of *Shen Bao* reports, degree of Hu Xueyan's involvement, factional explanations, punishments, later biographies, and the defendants' precise relationship require independent verification. The episode treats anti-Xiang-Army and Zhejiang-faction accounts as overbroad, but does not establish that high politics played no role.
