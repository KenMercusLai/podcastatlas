# Dairy Choice and Tolerance Boundary / 奶制品选择与耐受边界

## Definition
Dairy choice and tolerance boundary is the distinction between a dairy product's nutrient profile, a person's lactose or protein tolerance, the complete formulation, alternatives, price, and any separate medical condition affecting use.

## Current Synthesis
Milk is a useful food for many people, not a mandatory or universally interchangeable one. Protein, calcium, fat, lactose, vitamins, taste, and price vary across products, while a prominent “high protein,” “high calcium,” skimmed, buffalo, goat, or premium claim describes only part of the choice. The practical comparison begins with ordinary intake and tolerance rather than the most impressive front-of-pack number.

Lactose intolerance is not solved merely by switching animal species because animal milks generally still contain lactose. Tolerance can vary by amount and food context, and some people may do better with yogurt, genuine cheese, or lactose-free milk. Plant drinks can satisfy taste or convenience but should not be assumed nutritionally identical to milk. Disease-specific restrictions and infant feeding require separate qualified guidance rather than extrapolation from ordinary adult food choice.

## Key Claims
- Milk protein, calcium, fat, lactose, micronutrients, taste, price, and total diet all belong to product comparison.
- A higher protein or calcium headline does not by itself establish better value or personal fit.
- Lactose intolerance, milk-protein response, and preference are different questions and should not be collapsed.
- Buffalo or goat milk generally does not remove lactose exposure simply because the animal source changes.
- Yogurt, genuine cheese, lactose-free milk, or non-dairy foods can provide different practical routes depending on the person's goal and tolerance.
- Plant drinks may replace the sensory role of milk without matching its protein, calcium, or complete nutrient profile.
- Infant formula, metabolic disease, and persistent symptoms sit outside ordinary consumer comparison and require qualified advice.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source provides general food education, not diagnosis. Its numerical composition comparisons, claims about original versus added calcium, A1/A2 beta-casein, adaptation to lactose, dairy and insulin or androgen response, vitamin D dosing, and disease-specific restrictions are not independently substantiated in the supplied note. Allergy, persistent gastrointestinal symptoms, infant feeding, pregnancy, polycystic ovary syndrome, insulin resistance, and other medical contexts require current individualized guidance.

## What Changed
- Established a dairy comparison that separates nutrient profile, tolerance, formulation, price, and medical context.
- Clarified that changing animal species does not automatically resolve lactose intolerance.
