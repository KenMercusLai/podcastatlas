# Culinary Grammar

Culinary grammar is the idea, foregrounded in [[66-yuchi-yu-huajiao-liao-chi-de-wo-ke-jiu-bu-kun-le-659957536]], that a food system has rules, expectations, sensory categories, and social contexts that outsiders have to learn before they can judge it well. The episode uses [[FuchsiaDunlop|胡霞 / Fuchsia Dunlop]]'s [[SharkFinAndSichuanPepper|《鱼翅与花椒》]] to show this through [[SichuanCuisine|川菜]], Western food served to Chinese friends, and mutual puzzlement around portions, blandness, rawness, table manners, and texture.

The concept does not require liking every food. It asks for a delay between first disgust and final judgment, because repeated exposure can reveal why a community values a mouthfeel, aroma, preparation method, or eating scene that initially seems strange.

[[sanlian-chuantai-shen-ci-gei-ren-putao-shijie-weixun-le-585125156]] extends culinary grammar from cuisine into [[Wine|wine / 葡萄酒]], tea, [[Coffee|coffee]], perfume, and food writing. [[Heimai|黑麦]] treats taste as a system that can be learned by comparison and vocabulary, but he keeps the endpoint personal: [[TasteTraining]] should help people recognize [[FlavorAsSelfKnowledge|what they actually like]] rather than perform status.

[[ep245-yinian-yidu-xunwei-zhilv-jinnian-qu-chi-chuangyi-cai-li9wtavemokcckialbbj7unpmqsz]] adds a restaurant-innovation version. [[CreativeChineseCuisine|创意中餐]] works when it changes dish form, technique, drink pairing, or restaurant scene while keeping enough grammar for diners to recognize [[GuizhouCuisineFlavorTransfer|Guizhou sour-spicy systems]], [[YunnanCuisineMenuNarrative|Yunnan route logic]], [[SichuanCuisine|Sichuan internal renewal]], or [[GuangxiCuisineLegibility|Guangxi memory points]].

[[ep260-yikou-feimei-zhifang-cangzhe-zhongguo-yangzhi-de-xin-gushi-linjq2fkzhqvzjcgrmhudq12vs4j]] adds a production-to-table version. Learning the grammar of [[RainbowTrout|虹鳟]], [[Eel|鳗鱼]], and [[FoieGras|鹅肝]] means knowing species labels, water origin, seedling risk, force-feeding discomfort, processing labor, sauce ratio, and whether a prepared format should be treated as convenience, craft, or opacity.

## Key Claims
- Food judgment often fails when people treat their own culinary grammar as neutral and another cuisine's grammar as irrational.
- Learning a food system includes taste, smell, [[FoodTextureAesthetics|口感]], freshness standards, meal structure, and social etiquette.
- [[FoodCulturalBias]] can soften when diners understand that the unfamiliar food is not ruleless but organized by different priorities.
- Culinary grammar has ethical limits: understanding why a practice exists does not require endorsing waste, coercion, or animal suffering.
- Beverage grammars can work like food grammars: acidity, tannin, aroma, body, bitterness, sweetness, and aftertaste need context and comparison before they become legible.
- Creative cuisine depends on grammar rather than escaping it: without recognizable regional logic, innovation becomes generic novelty.
- New high-value food categories need production grammar as well as flavor grammar; otherwise diners cannot judge what a menu or package is actually selling.
