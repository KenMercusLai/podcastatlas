# Nasal Mucosa Protection and Bleeding Triage / 鼻黏膜保护与鼻出血分流

## Definition
Nasal mucosa protection and bleeding triage is the distinction between preserving the nose's filtering and mucosal barriers, using low-trauma first aid for a minor anterior bleed, and recognizing bleeding or infection patterns that require prompt professional care.

## Current Synthesis
[[vol-50-erbiyanhouke-ni-bei-biyan-kunrao-duojiu-le-shishi-zhe-jizhao-huanjie-banfa-kou-bishi-zhi-bixue-de-wuqu-lsz_dxnzyvxtli_qc0i2goxn1yyk]] joins everyday grooming and first aid through one mechanism: dryness, inflammation, picking, deep trimming, and forceful cleaning can injure vascular mucosa. Nasal hair and ordinary secretions are protective rather than dirt to eliminate completely, so exposed hair may be trimmed conservatively and crusts softened before gentle clearance instead of shaving, plucking, or digging inside the cavity.

For an ordinary anterior nosebleed, the episode favors forward positioning and direct compression of the soft nasal wing rather than tilting the head back, which can send blood toward the throat and airway. This is not a universal home-treatment rule. Heavy, persistent, posterior, arterial, hypertension-associated, tumor-related, or post-radiotherapy bleeding, or bleeding with other concerning symptoms, moves beyond routine self-care. Infected lesions in the facial “danger triangle” likewise should not be squeezed casually because local infection can rarely spread through valveless facial venous pathways.

## Key Claims
- Nasal hair and mucosal secretions form part of a filtering and humidifying barrier and should not be removed indiscriminately.
- Repeated picking, deep trimming, shaving, or forceful cleaning can cause erosion, dryness, infection, and bleeding.
- A minor anterior nosebleed is managed by forward positioning and direct nasal-wing pressure rather than head tilting.
- Heavy, persistent, posterior, arterial, recurrent high-risk, tumor-related, or post-radiotherapy bleeding requires prompt assessment.
- An infected facial lesion should not be squeezed into a larger wound; suspected abscess or spreading infection belongs in qualified care.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a podcast summary, not a first-aid guideline or individualized examination. It does not specify compression duration, packing materials, medication effects, bleeding-disorder assessment, trauma protocols, pediatric rules, or emergency-service thresholds. The suggestion to insert sterile cotton and several anatomical or causal explanations remain source-scoped; substantial, recurrent, unexplained, or hard-to-stop bleeding requires qualified advice rather than improvised packing.

## What Changed

- Separated ordinary grooming and anterior bleeding from infection and high-risk hemorrhage contexts.
