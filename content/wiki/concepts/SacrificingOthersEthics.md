# Sacrificing Others Ethics

## Definition

Sacrificing others ethics is the moral problem of deciding that another person must bear severe or lethal cost for a larger cause, especially when the decision-maker controls the framing, does not share the same risk, or treats necessity as already proven.

## Current Synthesis

The bounded sources distinguish voluntary self-sacrifice from assigning sacrifice to someone else. In [[Andor|《安多》 / Andor]], [[CassianAndor]] and [[MaarvaAndor]] choose risks for themselves, while [[LuthenRael]] and [[SawGerrera]] decide whether another rebel group may be lost to protect an intelligence source. The resistance goal may be legitimate, but it does not make other people's deaths emotionally clean or automatically heroic.

[[VeilOfIgnorance]] supplies a rule-level test. Collective-calm or social-welfare arguments look different when the chooser may become the scapegoat, defendant, abused spouse, or child burdened by another person's record. A rule that appears efficient from above must remain defensible from the position of the person made expendable.

[[KunlunShrineGhostBlowsOutTheLight|《昆仑神宫》]] makes the distinction interpersonal and immediate. 王胖子 proposes the older and injured 明叔; Shirley 杨 prepares to volunteer herself; 明叔 claims that raising 阿香 entitles him to demand her death; 胡八一 refuses and turns to a lottery. The episode treats equal odds as procedurally preferable to unilateral selection only under genuine necessity and consent. A lottery distributes a burden but cannot establish that the burden must exist, and urgency does not excuse failure to test whether the sacrificial rule has been misread.

## Key Claims

- A noble collective goal does not automatically purify the decision to sacrifice another person.
- Self-sacrifice and sacrificing others differ because authority over one's own life does not transfer into authority over someone else's death.
- Moral risk increases when decision-makers are insulated from the cost or can define another person as older, weaker, indebted, marginal, or otherwise expendable.
- Necessity must be tested rather than assumed; deceptive framing, incomplete information, and time pressure can manufacture the appearance that someone must be sacrificed.
- A fair procedure can reduce arbitrary selection without making the underlying act just; lotteries require necessity, informed consent, and rules participants can genuinely refuse.
- Serious judgment should preserve moral residue and ask how the situation reached the point where sacrificing others appears unavoidable.

## Evidence

### Self-risk versus assigned risk

- [[42-anduo-fengqi-yu-qingping-zhi-mo-616391638]] distinguishes Cassian and Maarva's chosen risks from Luthen and Saw's willingness to let other rebels die for strategic protection.
- [[109-quhua-gui-chuideng-zhi-kunlun-shengong-p3-xishengzhe-shui-jitan-liangnan-lpd-4m9kuh2ghomoxlfpdckefnb9]] contrasts Shirley 杨's attempted volunteering with proposals to kill 明叔 or 阿香.

### Weak-position and expendability tests

- [[100-fa-lv-de-bei-lun-xie-tian-xie-di-xing-hao-wo-men-hai-you-luo-xiang-733477302]] uses the veil of ignorance to test whether social-priority rules remain acceptable when the chooser may be their scapegoat.
- [[109-quhua-gui-chuideng-zhi-kunlun-shengong-p3-xishengzhe-shui-jitan-liangnan-lpd-4m9kuh2ghomoxlfpdckefnb9]] rejects age, injury, expected lifespan, or parental care as sufficient authority to select another person's death.

### Necessity and procedure

- [[109-quhua-gui-chuideng-zhi-kunlun-shengong-p3-xishengzhe-shui-jitan-liangnan-lpd-4m9kuh2ghomoxlfpdckefnb9]] questions the mural interpretation and treats the five-person lottery as conditionally fair distribution, not proof that sacrifice is necessary or justified.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The sources do not claim that every collective-risk decision is impermissible. Resistance, law, and survival can create genuine conflicts in which inaction also kills. Their narrower shared claim is that necessity, authority, consent, and distribution of risk require separate examination. The 《昆仑神宫》 case is fictional and later reveals an alternative ritual interpretation, so it clarifies the moral structure of perceived necessity rather than proving how a real emergency should be resolved. The Andor and podcast judgments are interpretive, while the veil of ignorance is a philosophical test rather than an empirical decision algorithm.

## What Changed

- Added autonomy as the decisive distinction between volunteering oneself and assigning another person's death.
- Added expendability markers such as age, injury, dependency, and claimed familial debt.
- Added the limit of procedural fairness: equal odds cannot establish necessity or erase coercion.
- Added a duty to test the framing before accepting that sacrifice is unavoidable.
