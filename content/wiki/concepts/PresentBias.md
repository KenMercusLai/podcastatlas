# Present Bias

## Definition

Present bias is the tendency to give disproportionate weight to immediate rewards and costs relative to consequences borne by a future self.

## Current Synthesis

[[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-2fc443663e259ff389aeeb81792c6086-50338835-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-2fc443663e259ff389aeeb81792c6086-50338835|You, But Better]] uses snacks, purchases, sleep, workload, health, and retirement saving to make the conflict concrete. Knowing the long-term benefit is insufficient when the immediate option is more salient or enjoyable. The episode therefore treats advance design—defaults, automation, deadlines, and [[UlyssesContract|commitment devices]]—as a way for a reflective present self to protect later interests before temptation peaks.

## Key Claims

- Immediate gratification can outweigh larger delayed benefits even when the person understands the tradeoff.
- Costs imposed on a future self can feel less salient than benefits received now.
- Information and sincere intention alone do not remove predictable preference reversal.
- Advance changes to friction, defaults, timing, or consequences can reduce exposure to the biased choice point.
- Future-self protection remains legitimate only when constraints are proportionate, revisable where possible, and safe.

## Evidence

- Everyday conflict - [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-2fc443663e259ff389aeeb81792c6086-50338835-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-2fc443663e259ff389aeeb81792c6086-50338835]] uses eating, spending, sleep, workload, health, and saving examples.
- Advance design - [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-2fc443663e259ff389aeeb81792c6086-50338835-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-2fc443663e259ff389aeeb81792c6086-50338835]] connects deadlines, retirement defaults, future contribution commitments, and cash commitment devices to future-self protection.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Choosing an immediate benefit is not automatically irrational: urgent needs, uncertainty, poverty, illness, caregiving, or changing circumstances can make present consumption reasonable. The source summary does not establish one discount rate, mechanism, or intervention effect across populations. Commitment tools can also overconstrain later selves or become coercive when another party controls them.

## What Changed

- Established present bias as the episode's core explanation for conflict between present and future selves.
