# Multi-Archive Conflict History

## Definition

Multi-archive conflict history reconstructs a transnational conflict from records produced by several belligerents and affected groups so that no single state's moral or causal narrative controls the evidence.

## Current Synthesis

[[XiangLanxin|Xiang Lanxin]] uses the Boxer War to show the method's value. Foreign records can expose diplomatic arrogance, political insensitivity, and provocation, while Chinese and missionary evidence can preserve Boxer superstition, coercion, violence, and the killing of Chinese Christians. Holding both together resists the binary of “civilization versus barbarism” and a purely heroic anti-imperialist story.

The method also has a contemporary purpose: reconstructing how each side experienced the other's behavior can reveal escalation mechanisms hidden by retrospective certainty. Archive plurality does not automatically remove bias, however; survival, access, language, institutional production, and researcher selection still shape the record.

## Key Claims

- Conflicts involving many states require evidence beyond a bilateral or national archive.
- Rival archives can reveal incompatible perceptions of the same signal or action.
- Correcting an imperial narrative does not require sanitizing violence by anti-imperial actors.
- Understanding perceived provocation can explain escalation without excusing harm.
- Archive plurality reduces narrative closure but does not eliminate source bias.
