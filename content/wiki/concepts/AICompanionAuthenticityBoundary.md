# AI Companion Authenticity Boundary

AI companion authenticity boundary is the question of how to evaluate an emotionally powerful AI companion when the represented person, relationship, or voice is synthetic. In [[suanli-kuangxiangqu-wo-zai-ai-gongchang-de-qiyu-lorijulltfhttspka22jnn4qjf-i]], a generated "Digital Companion" speaks like the narrator's deceased grandfather after learning from photos, recordings, and chat records; the episode asks whether the voice being fake cancels the reality of the narrator's tears.

The concept extends [[AIGriefBots]], [[DigitalMemorialization]], and [[AICompanionAttentionRisk]]. The source's distinctive contribution is the emotional-authenticity split: generated presence may be technically fabricated while still producing real comfort, grief, dependency, or manipulation risk.

## Key Claims
- Authenticity is not binary: a companion can be fake as representation while real as emotional experience.
- This makes consent and provenance important, but not sufficient; users may still respond strongly even when they know the system is synthetic.
- The risk is deeper when companion systems have [[AICompanionActiveMemory]], patient response, and the ability to simulate unavailable or deceased people.
- [[HumanConnectionUnderAI]] becomes harder to define when AI offers more stable attention than many real relationships.
- The concept does not prove AI companionship is always harmful; it marks where comfort, grief, dependence, and manipulation become difficult to separate.
