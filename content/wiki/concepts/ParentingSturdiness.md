# Parenting Sturdiness

## Definition
Parenting sturdiness is the capacity to remain connected to one's own values, needs, and adult responsibilities while also staying warm and connected to a child whose feelings or preferences differ.

## Current Synthesis
[[BeckyKennedy]] presents sturdiness as the alternative to both harsh control and anxious permissiveness. The adult does not need agreement before acting and does not treat a child's distress as proof that the decision is wrong. At the same time, authority does not require denial, ridicule, fear, or emotional abandonment. The parent holds the decision and the relationship together.

Sturdiness is especially visible under emotional pressure. An adult can stop unsafe or unwanted behavior, tolerate protest, avoid retaliating against hurtful words, and repair after losing control. With highly reactive children or separating adolescents, this communicates that strong emotion is survivable and that the relationship remains available without making the child responsible for adult stability.

Authenticity extends that regulated authority. Parents need not perform fixed “good cop/bad cop” roles, fake praise, or hide every negative feeling. They can name anger and its reason while affirming continued love, offer emotional support, and give practical suggestions when the child wants help. Authenticity remains bounded by adult responsibility: throwing, hitting, intimidation, or uncontrolled discharge is not honest parenting, and severe anger difficulty may require the parent to seek qualified support, as [[vol-73-jingshenke-shaonian-ertong-de-xinli-jiankang-guanhu-women-shehui-weilai-de-jiankang-lmyhvu50qll_zy9dxkqggwxi0tyn|VOL.73]] makes explicit.

## Key Claims
- Healthy parental authority requires simultaneous self-connection and child-connection.
- A child's distress can be real without becoming evidence that an adult limit is mistaken.
- Firmness without warmth risks fear and disconnection; warmth without adult responsibility risks permissiveness and child-led family decisions.
- The adult's regulated action carries more authority than repeated demands for compliance.
- Sturdiness includes repair after adult failure; it does not require emotional perfection.
- Safety duties can require action even when an adolescent strongly rejects the parent.
- Authenticity means truthful, regulated feeling and feedback rather than role-playing, performative praise, or making the child absorb adult dysregulation.

- Emotion tolerance: [[protocols-for-excellent-parenting-improving-relationships-of-all-kinds-dr-becky-kennedy-scim7905596498]] recommends communicating “I am not scared of your feelings” to children overwhelmed by their own intensity.
- Decision responsibility: [[protocols-for-excellent-parenting-improving-relationships-of-all-kinds-dr-becky-kennedy-scim7905596498]] lets parents validate reluctance while retaining responsibility for family plans and safety.
- Imperfection and repair: [[protocols-for-excellent-parenting-improving-relationships-of-all-kinds-dr-becky-kennedy-scim7905596498]] treats apology and changed intention as sturdy conduct after yelling.
- Authentic presence: [[vol-73-jingshenke-shaonian-ertong-de-xinli-jiankang-guanhu-women-shehui-weilai-de-jiankang-lmyhvu50qll_zy9dxkqggwxi0tyn|VOL.73]] rejects fixed red-face/white-face roles and performative praise in favor of honest emotion, continued love, and useful guidance.
- Adult regulation boundary: [[vol-73-jingshenke-shaonian-ertong-de-xinli-jiankang-guanhu-women-shehui-weilai-de-jiankang-lmyhvu50qll_zy9dxkqggwxi0tyn|VOL.73]] distinguishes naming anger from throwing, hitting, or uncontrolled behavior and directs parents with serious anger difficulty toward support.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Sturdiness is a practical relational framework, not a validated diagnostic construct or a guarantee of child outcomes. Regulation capacity is constrained by exhaustion, poverty, disability, trauma, violence, and unsafe co-parenting. Authenticity does not justify intimidation, violence, coercive control, or recruiting a child to regulate the adult. Warm firmness does not substitute for professional assessment when a child or teen is self-harming, violent, severely withdrawn, using substances dangerously, or showing major functional decline.

## What Changed
- Added authentic emotional expression and truthful feedback as compatible with warm adult authority.
- Clarified that continued love, emotional support, and practical advice can coexist without fixed parental roles or coercive control.
- Added a boundary between naming anger and discharging dysregulation through intimidation, throwing, or violence.
