## Definition

Advantage-first combat doctrine is the strategy pattern where a commander first changes information, timing, morale, position, or political commitment enough to make victory likely, then fights from that constructed advantage instead of treating battle as the place where advantage will somehow appear.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-742-banchao-chushi-xiyu-37-ren-da-sha-si-fang-ltutsinxtciar0tmpxkge0mnsnp1|Hanji 742]] creates the concept through the host's reading of [[SunziBingfa|《孙子兵法》]]: "胜兵先胜而后求战，败兵先战而后求胜." The episode applies that frame to [[BanChaoEasternHan|Ban Chao]] at [[ShanshanKingdomLateHan|Shanshan]] and [[KhotanKingdomLateHan|Khotan]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-705-3-gudai-tezhongbing-banchao-yige-ganyu-danqiangpima-shenru-dibei-de-chuanqi-lgmwbhpwxqqniawf-n6xuctqgqkc|Hanji 705-3]] later reinforces the same reading by placing the Shanshan and Khotan actions inside Ban Chao's broader small-contingent opening arc.

At Shanshan, Ban Chao does not begin with open negotiation or a set-piece fight. He reads the host kingdom's cooling attitude as evidence of hidden [[NorthernXiongnuLateHan|Northern Xiongnu]] influence, bluffs a local intermediary into confirming the envoy camp, keeps Guo Xun outside the plan to reduce leakage risk, and uses fire, drums, shouting, and ambush at night. The fight matters, but only after information, secrecy, timing, wind, and surprise have been arranged.

At Khotan, the same doctrine becomes political shock rather than camp destruction. Ban Chao lets the shaman's horse demand bring the hostile religious intermediary to him, kills him, punishes Si Laibi, and sends the head to King Guangde. Khotan's submission follows because the prior Shanshan story and the immediate shaman killing alter the perceived balance before wider fighting begins.

## Key Claims

- The doctrine treats battle as the endpoint of advantage creation, not as a gamble that produces advantage by itself.
- Information testing comes first: changed reception, hidden envoys, and hostile intermediaries must be diagnosed before action.
- Secrecy can matter more than procedural inclusion when a fearful insider might leak the decisive plan.
- Shock action can create political commitment when a small force cannot win by sustained occupation.
- The doctrine differs from simple bravery because it pairs danger with timing, surprise, and opponent psychology.
- In this source, the doctrine is also a critique of overlarge ambition: plans fail when desire for scale or speed exceeds actual capacity.

- Capacity critique: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-742-banchao-chushi-xiyu-37-ren-da-sha-si-fang-ltutsinxtciar0tmpxkge0mnsnp1|Hanji 742]] has the host criticize "志小而谋大" cases where ambition, resources, and capability do not match.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept begins with Hanji 742's reading of Ban Chao and Sunzi, with Hanji 705-3 acting as corroborating recap rather than a new independent doctrine. It does not prove that small teams can reliably defeat large forces, nor does it make secrecy universally superior to consultation. Ban Chao's success depends on a narrow political window, opponent uncertainty, a host kingdom not yet committed, and a rival envoy party that can be isolated. Later Ban Chao pages must still be read for proxy strategy, recall crisis, accusation resilience, supply-line judgment, and harsh punitive limits.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Hanji 742's explicit Sunzi frame.
- Defined Ban Chao's Shanshan and Khotan actions as constructed advantage rather than simple courage or battlefield technique.
- Added Hanji 705-3 as corroborating recap tying Shanshan and Khotan into Ban Chao's broader small-contingent opening arc.
