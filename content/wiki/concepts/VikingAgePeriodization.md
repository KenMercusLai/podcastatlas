# Viking Age Periodization

## Definition

Viking Age periodization is the choice among event-based dates such as Lindisfarne in 793 and Stamford Bridge in 1066 and broader, regionally uneven chronologies based on social transformation.

## Current Synthesis

[[358-viking-sorcery-glt1200485419]] prefers a flexible opening around 720-750 and an uneven ending around the mid-11th century because regions changed at different speeds. This complements the symbolic endpoint in [[BattleOfStamfordBridge]]: 1066 remains narratively useful without functioning as a universal switch from “Viking” to “post-Viking.”

## Key Claims

- Famous raids and battles provide memorable boundaries but privilege event history.
- Social, religious, political, and regional transformations do not begin or end simultaneously.
- An opening around 720-750 can capture processes preceding the Lindisfarne raid.
- A mid-11th-century transition can coexist with continued Scandinavian warfare after 1066.
- Period labels are interpretive tools rather than facts with one uncontested cutoff.

## Evidence

- Broader chronology: [[358-viking-sorcery-glt1200485419]] explicitly shifts from 793-1066 toward circa 720-750 through the mid-11th century.
- Regional unevenness: [[358-viking-sorcery-glt1200485419]] says different areas changed at different speeds.
- Symbolic endpoint: [[358-viking-sorcery-glt1200485419]] uses 1066 as the conventional boundary that its broader chronology qualifies, aligning with [[BattleOfStamfordBridge]]'s distinction between Hardrada's defeat and a literal end to Scandinavian warfare.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The broader dates are Price's preferred analytical frame as presented by one episode. Event-based conventions remain useful for communication, and different research questions may justify different boundaries.
