# 神骨相法 / Spirit-Bone Physiognomy

神骨相法 / spirit-bone physiognomy is the method frame [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-135-fanwaipian-3-jiao-ni-xiangmian-kanren-xiaojiqiao-ruhe-yi-mao-kantou-mingyun-lpupsu8fduwdwbdg8xq8gjxegztu|Qinji 135 番外篇3]] draws from the attributed [[Bingjian|《冰鉴》]] tradition. The source says "神" is concentrated in the eyes, while "骨" is concentrated in the face, making eye quality and facial bone structure the two main entrances into person-reading.

The source presents this as more than private fortune-telling. In its strongest formulation, external cues become a rough ancient system for reading temperament, ability, will, and role fit. The episode moves from eyes to head and face bones, then to facial features, bearing, affect, and speech, eventually treating language order as the most practical version of the method.

The concept is deliberately source-scoped. This wiki records the structure of the traditional method without accepting that eye shape, bones, nose, mouth, or skin texture reliably determine morality, career, or fate. Its boundary is held by [[PhysiognomyMoralAppearanceFallacy|以貌定德谬误]], while its more useful personnel-judgment neighbors are [[WenWangGuanrenFa|文王官人法]], [[SituationalCharacterTests|五观识人法]], and [[SpeechAsMoralDiagnostic|言语作为德行信号]].

## Key Claims
- The source treats eyes as the most visible sign of inner spirit because speech and facial performance can be manipulated more easily.
- Bone structure is read as a deeper root of disposition and fate, especially through head and face categories.
- Affect and bearing can modify or compensate for what the source describes as weaknesses in bones or spirit.
- The episode's practical endpoint is not bone taxonomy but communicative evidence: confused speech, disordered thought, and self-promoting talk become role-fit warnings.
- The method should be recorded as traditional personnel lore and story logic, not as modern empirical assessment.
