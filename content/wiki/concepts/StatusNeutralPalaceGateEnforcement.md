## Definition

宫门礼制的身份中立执行 / status-neutral palace gate enforcement is the practice of applying an access or ritual rule to high-status royal figures before any personal exception is granted, so that rank does not silently erase procedure.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-214-tiemian-zhangshizhi-dui-huangdi-lan-taizi-1-lqlbul2drksmqgmligaelawflsvq8|Hanji 214 part 1]] defines the pattern through the Sima Gate incident. Crown Prince [[HanJingdi|刘启]] and [[LiuWuLiangKingWesternHan|梁王刘武]] reportedly ride through without dismounting; [[ZhangShizhiWesternHan|张释之]] stops them, denies entry, and formally accuses them of disrespect.

The important sequence is rule, accountability, and then pardon. [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]] acknowledges failed instruction, a dowager sends a formal pardon, and only then do the two royals enter. Because Liu Heng subsequently promotes Zhang, the episode presents constraint on the ruler's family as public service rather than personal hostility. Status neutrality here does not mean that sovereign mercy disappears; it means that mercy is made visible as a separate authorized act instead of an unspoken exemption.

## Key Claims

- Royal status does not automatically cancel a palace access or ritual rule in the episode's account.
- Enforcement becomes institutionally legible when the violation is named and recorded rather than waived informally.
- Formal pardon and initial legal judgment can coexist if they remain distinguishable acts.
- Ruler support is necessary when an official enforces a rule against the ruler's own heir or close kin.
- Promotion after constraint signals that public-duty enforcement is valued rather than treated as disloyalty.

## Evidence

Status-neutral stop:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-214-tiemian-zhangshizhi-dui-huangdi-lan-taizi-1-lqlbul2drksmqgmligaelawflsvq8|Hanji 214 part 1]] says Zhang stops Liu Qi and Liu Wu after they fail to dismount at Sima Gate and refuses immediate entry.

Recorded accountability:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-214-tiemian-zhangshizhi-dui-huangdi-lan-taizi-1-lqlbul2drksmqgmligaelawflsvq8|Hanji 214 part 1]] says Zhang files an accusation of disrespect instead of resolving the violation through private deference.

Separate grace step:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-214-tiemian-zhangshizhi-dui-huangdi-lan-taizi-1-lqlbul2drksmqgmligaelawflsvq8|Hanji 214 part 1]] says Liu Heng apologizes for poor instruction and a dowager-issued pardon precedes the royals' admission.

Institutional backing:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-214-tiemian-zhangshizhi-dui-huangdi-lan-taizi-1-lqlbul2drksmqgmligaelawflsvq8|Hanji 214 part 1]] says Liu Heng promotes Zhang after the incident.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept rests on the episode's retelling and does not establish the complete Sima Gate code, the exact legal force of “不敬,” the normal handling of comparable violations, or whether enforcement was consistent beyond this incident. The pardon also shows that the case is not modern equality before law: royal authority remains able to resolve the violation. The narrower evidence is that protocol is applied and formally released rather than silently ignored.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from the Sima Gate sequence of stop, accusation, formal pardon, entry, and promotion.
- Distinguished status-neutral initial enforcement from the separate exercise of sovereign or dowager grace.
