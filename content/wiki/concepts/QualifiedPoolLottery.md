# Qualified-Pool Lottery

## Definition

A qualified-pool lottery is a two-stage selection design that first identifies options meeting a defensible minimum standard and then chooses randomly among that acceptable set.

## Current Synthesis

The design separates threshold judgment from unsupported rank precision. Expert or community review remains useful for excluding clearly unsuitable options, but once several candidates are plausibly capable and prediction becomes weak, a lottery distributes opportunity without pretending that small score or presentation differences forecast outcomes. The episode applies the idea to student leadership, NIH grants, and college admissions; transfer across those domains remains a proposal rather than demonstrated equivalence.

## Key Claims

- Threshold decisions can be more defensible than precise rankings among closely matched options.
- Random selection can make residual uncertainty explicit rather than hiding it inside scores or performances.
- The model retains standards and therefore differs from unrestricted chance.
- Pool construction controls the result: biased eligibility or review can survive into the lottery.
- The design can broaden opportunity only if entry and qualification do not reproduce the same barriers it seeks to reduce.
- Accountability after selection remains necessary because fair allocation does not guarantee good performance.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source does not compare qualified lotteries with validated assessment systems or show effects on long-term performance, diversity, applicant effort, public legitimacy, or strategic behavior. Thresholds can be arbitrary; anonymous work can still reflect unequal preparation; and citation impact is not the sole measure of research value. High-stakes settings may also require differentiated matching rather than one common qualification boundary.

## What Changed

- Established the threshold-then-randomize design across leadership, grants, and admissions.
- Made pool construction and post-selection accountability explicit failure points.
