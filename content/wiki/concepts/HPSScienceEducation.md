# HPS Science Education / 科学史哲社会科学教育

HPS science education / 科学史哲社会科学教育 is the episode's term for putting science back into history, philosophy, and society. In [[ep262-gaokao-guohou-zhishi-guiling-women-xuyao-zenyang-de-kexue-jiaoyu-ls8ecqcdpe9d5ioytyca3jqdf-3r]], [[WeiQianSanlian|魏茜]] uses HPS to argue that students should not encounter science only as settled textbook results.

The source's examples include Newton, Leibniz, Galileo, scientific paradigms, technical invention histories, and the older distinction between "月上天" and "月下天." These stories show students that science develops through problems, concepts, instruments, disputes, and social conditions rather than appearing fully formed.

HPS also keeps culturally loaded science questions from becoming slogans. The source cites the question of why modern science did not emerge in China as a topic that should be understood through different ways of knowing, not as a simple civilizational ranking.

## Key Claims
- Science education is stronger when students see how concepts were made, disputed, revised, and socially embedded.
- History and philosophy can make abstract theories legible without replacing evidence with storytelling.
- HPS helps students understand science as a way of knowing, not only a stock of facts.
- Scientific values such as openness, evidence, criticism, and self-correction become easier to teach when students see their historical conflicts.
- The approach complements [[BigConceptScienceTeaching]] by showing where big concepts came from and why they mattered.
