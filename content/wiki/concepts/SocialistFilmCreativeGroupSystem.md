## Definition
The socialist film creative-group system is a production arrangement in which relatively stable teams of directors, writers, cinematographers, and other creators worked inside state studios around shared aesthetic or project orientations.

## Current Synthesis
The system sat between centralized planning and individual authorship. Studios held production infrastructure and annual output obligations, while creative groups could concentrate collaborators, develop scripts, mentor younger filmmakers, and create a limited zone of aesthetic continuity. That autonomy was conditional: politically sensitive support could bring censorship, shelving, loss of work, or dissolution of the group.

## Key Claims
- Stable collaboration can improve production efficiency and aesthetic continuity.
- A group can create bounded autonomy inside a planned studio without becoming institutionally independent.
- Leadership includes script development, project selection, and protection of younger creators.
- Political control remains capable of overriding group judgment and ending the arrangement.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
One Polish case does not establish uniform practice across socialist countries, studios, or decades. Group stability may also reproduce hierarchy or gatekeeping, and production efficiency does not establish political freedom. The episode notes Chinese investigation of the model in the 1980s but says it was not comprehensively implemented.

## What Changed
- Added a bounded-autonomy model for creative collaboration inside state film production.
