# Sudden Hearing Change Escalation

## Definition
Sudden hearing change escalation is the episode's care-seeking threshold for abrupt hearing loss, one-sided ear fullness, tinnitus, dizziness, asymmetry between ears, distorted sound quality, or a clear sense that sound has become abnormal.

## Current Synthesis
The source treats sudden hearing change as a low-delay medical signal. [[LvDoctorENT|耳鼻喉吕博士]] says obvious sudden inability to hear should not be watched passively, and he broadens the warning list because sudden hearing loss can present indirectly. A person may first notice one-sided ear fullness, dizziness, tinnitus, altered sound quality, or asymmetric ear sensation rather than a simple statement that hearing is gone.

This concept sits beside the wiki's broader clinical-risk pages. It does not tell listeners what diagnosis they have. It tells them which symptom pattern should move from casual explanation, headphone adjustment, or waiting into qualified ear evaluation.

## Key Claims
- Obvious sudden hearing loss should prompt immediate medical attention rather than waiting to see if it resolves.
- Sudden hearing loss can be hidden by subtler symptoms such as one-sided ear fullness, dizziness, tinnitus, or changed sound quality.
- A new asymmetry between the two ears is more concerning than a vague bilateral or long-familiar sensation.
- Distorted hearing, strange timbre, or sound that feels unlike normal listening can be an escalation signal.
- Escalation is symptom-based and does not depend on proving that headphones, noise, infection, or stress caused the change.
- The episode keeps warning signs practical but does not replace diagnosis, hearing testing, or treatment decisions by clinicians.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This is not a diagnostic checklist and does not decide whether a symptom is sudden sensorineural hearing loss, middle-ear disease, external-canal blockage, pressure change, vestibular disorder, infection, or another condition. The source's core point is that sudden, unilateral, asymmetric, dizzying, tinnitus-linked, or distorted hearing changes should not be managed only by self-observation.
