# Textual Chronology Origin Testing

## Definition

Textual chronology origin testing evaluates a proposed origin by asking when each supposedly inherited motif first appears, whether it exists in the earliest recoverable text, and whether a plausible transmission route connects the proposed source to that text.

## Current Synthesis

[[346-the-mystery-of-the-holy-grail-part-2-glt9518077570]] uses the method to test explanations of the [[HolyGrail]]. Fertility theories depend heavily on a supernatural bond between wounded king and wasteland, but the episode argues that Chrétien's earliest recoverable landscape is devastated through failed defense rather than magical sterility. Welsh-cauldron theories face an object mismatch and an uncertain route into French romance. Universal-archetype readings may describe psychological resonance without explaining the dated literary sequence.

The method does not forbid comparison. It ranks claims: direct early attestation and plausible transmission support historical origin; later resemblance supports reception or analogy; missing chronology and route require explicit uncertainty.

## Key Claims

- A motif that appears only in later versions cannot automatically explain an earlier text.
- Object form matters: platter, cup, stone, and cauldron should not be treated as interchangeable evidence.
- Cross-cultural resemblance needs a route of transmission before it becomes a genealogy.
- A theory that explains almost any symbol may have psychological value but weak historical discrimination.
- The earliest recoverable text is a boundary, not proof that no lost influence existed.
- Origin, later development, and modern reception require separate claims.

## Evidence

- Wasteland chronology: [[346-the-mystery-of-the-holy-grail-part-2-glt9518077570]] distinguishes Chrétien's defenseless land from a later supernatural fertility bond.
- Object mismatch: [[346-the-mystery-of-the-holy-grail-part-2-glt9518077570]] contrasts the early serving vessel with Welsh cauldron comparison and Wolfram's later stone.
- Transmission problem: [[346-the-mystery-of-the-holy-grail-part-2-glt9518077570]] notes the absence of a demonstrated medieval French route for the Bran material it reviews.
- Explanatory reach: [[346-the-mystery-of-the-holy-grail-part-2-glt9518077570]] treats Jungian or Campbell-style universality as potentially meaningful but historically under-discriminating.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Survival of manuscripts is incomplete, oral transmission is difficult to reconstruct, and absence from an extant early text is not proof of absolute nonexistence. The episode's dismissal of particular Welsh, Iranian, pagan, or psychological theories is a source-scoped judgment; the method requires examination of the underlying texts and scholarship before becoming a final verdict.

## What Changed

- Created a source-critical framework for separating historical origin claims from analogy, reception, and later accretion.
