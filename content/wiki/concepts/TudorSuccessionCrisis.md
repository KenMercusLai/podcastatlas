# Tudor Succession Crisis

[[692-elizabeth-i-vs-the-catholics-a-massacre-in-paris-part-2-glt4665799025]] adds the threat-imagination layer before named invasion plots. The Pope's excommunication of [[ElizabethI|Elizabeth I]], [[MaryQueenOfScots|Mary, Queen of Scots]]' Catholic claim, and English Protestant memory of the [[StBartholomewsDayMassacre]] make succession danger part of ordinary security reasoning before [[ThrockmortonPlot|Throckmorton]] or [[BabingtonPlot|Babington]] evidence appears.

[[694-elizabeth-i-vs-the-catholics-a-treacherous-conspiracy-part-4-glt1627515087]] adds the succession crisis before the [[BabingtonPlot]]. In the [[ThrockmortonPlot]], [[MaryQueenOfScots|Mary, Queen of Scots]] is the replacement claimant who makes foreign invasion and domestic Catholic rising politically coherent, even while she remains imprisoned.

The episode also adds the [[BondOfAssociation]] as a pre-Act security response. [[WilliamCecil|Burleigh]] and [[FrancisWalsingham]] try to make any person responsible for Elizabeth's death ineligible to succeed her, directly targeting the succession mechanism that made Mary dangerous.

Tudor succession crisis is the episode's frame for why [[MaryQueenOfScots|Mary, Queen of Scots]] matters beyond ordinary imprisonment in [[696-elizabeth-i-vs-the-catholics-killing-the-queen-of-scots-part-6-glt2334035496]]. As [[ElizabethI|Elizabeth I]]'s cousin and an obvious Catholic heir if Elizabeth died, Mary could convert assassination, claimant politics, foreign invasion, and domestic religious fracture into one crisis.

The concept connects personal dynastic status to state security. Mary's body and correspondence become strategic because Protestant ministers believe her survival keeps a replacement regime available to English Catholics, Scottish interests, and continental powers.
