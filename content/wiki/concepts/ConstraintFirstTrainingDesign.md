# Constraint-First Training Design

## Definition
Constraint-first training design is a planning method that defines the desired adaptation and the most likely reason it will fail before selecting exercises, volume, intensity, or a weekly split.

## Current Synthesis
The framework begins with assessment and a specific, measurable, realistic goal, then works backward through intermediate phases. It calls the likely blocker a defender: injury history, travel, work, limited equipment, low interest, incompatible goals, or the same condition that ended earlier attempts. Those constraints are not afterthoughts; they determine which program can actually be completed.

Only after the goal, blocker, calendar, realistic session frequency, and true available duration are known does the method select exercises and order them by priority. Intensity, volume, rest, progression, and deloading then follow the target adaptation. The final step is [[TrainingChaosManagement]]: predict where execution will break and specify adjustments before motivation, fatigue, or schedule pressure forces an improvised decision.

## Key Claims
- Program design starts with a specific goal and a diagnosis of what has prevented or is most likely to prevent it.
- Long-term goals become more actionable when worked backward into intermediate phases with measurable proxies.
- Calendar, recovery, equipment, injury history, interest, and true session duration constrain the feasible program before exercise selection begins.
- Exercise choice, order, intensity, volume, and rest should follow the intended adaptation rather than habit or fashion.
- The highest-priority work belongs early in the session and on the most reliable, high-energy training day.
- A plan is incomplete until it specifies how to respond to predictable disruption.

- Adaptation matching: [[guest-series-dr-andy-galpin-optimize-your-training-program-for-fitness-longevity-scim2066998732]] makes exercise selection, order, intensity, volume, rest, and progression depend on the priority adaptation.
- Failure planning: [[guest-series-dr-andy-galpin-optimize-your-training-program-for-fitness-longevity-scim2066998732]] closes program construction by reviewing where the plan will probably fail.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The ten steps are a coaching framework, not evidence that one sequence is uniquely optimal. Goals can change, multiple constraints can interact, and some blockers require clinical assessment, rehabilitation, accessibility support, or environmental change rather than better exercise programming. The source's numerical progression and recovery examples are heuristics without enough methods in the supplied note to generalize.
