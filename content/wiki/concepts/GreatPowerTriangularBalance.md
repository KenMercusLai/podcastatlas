# Great-Power Triangular Balance

## Definition

Great-power triangular balance is a realist strategy that seeks a managed equilibrium among the [[UnitedStates]], [[China]], and [[Russia]] so no single rivalry hardens into uncontrolled bloc confrontation or major-power war.

## Current Synthesis

[[XiangLanxin|Xiang Lanxin]] presents the triangle as an extension of [[HenryKissinger|Kissinger's]] balance-of-power thinking. In this account, American strategic contraction, avoidance of major war, and protection of important sea lanes matter more than regime-change ideology or identical political systems.

Balance does not mean harmony or equal power. The three states continue to compete, and the framework depends on contested judgments about Russia's capacity, China's rise, U.S. endurance, and whether India can function as an autonomous pole. It is best retained as Xiang's organizing model rather than an established description of policy.

## Key Claims

- Equilibrium can be a peace mechanism even among ideologically different states.
- Strategic contraction may concentrate power rather than prove national decline.
- Major sea-lane continuity is treated as a core interest inside the balance.
- The triangle requires Russia to retain enough autonomy and capability to matter.
- Balance-of-power logic can reduce regime-change pressure without ending economic or technological competition.

- Pole qualification: [[508-xiang-lanxin-tan-guoji-guanxi-shiye-xia-de-zhongmeiou-jinxi-cong-sitanfo-xiaojituan-dao-junshi-kaiensizhuyi-lqrjnxctet30isfmgt-wyavdwffu]] records Xiang's judgment that India cannot replace Russia in the triangle.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source does not establish that Trump actually follows a coherent triangular strategy or that Russia has the durable capacity the model requires. It may underweight smaller states, alliances, domestic politics, ideology, and the agency of countries treated as objects of balance.
