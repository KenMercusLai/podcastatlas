# Small Nucleic Acid Drugs

Small nucleic acid drugs are the RNA-layer therapeutic modality discussed in [[156-shengwu-yiyao-de-2026-dang-shichang-bu-zai-wei-bd-zaodong-zhongguo-yaoqi-de-xingchen-dahai-cai-ganggang-zhankai-lil-ugrzq8uvzviq3f8i-wm9ilup]]. [[XiaoPTeacher|小P老师]] explains the route by moving from DNA transcription to RNA translation: instead of only inhibiting a protein after it is made, a small nucleic acid drug can reduce or silence RNA upstream.

The source highlights sequence specificity and delivery constraints. Because the intervention is sequence-based, target and off-target risk can be evaluated through genomic information, but practical delivery still shapes disease choice. The episode says many current applications focus on liver-expressed targets and metabolic disease areas such as lipid metabolism.

## Key Claims
- RNA-layer intervention can reduce protein production at the source rather than only blocking protein function.
- Sequence specificity is an advantage, but delivery remains a bottleneck.
- Liver delivery is comparatively advanced, which shapes the early commercial and BD focus.
- The source treats 2024-2025 small nucleic acid BD as active, but still subject to clinical and commercialization proof.
