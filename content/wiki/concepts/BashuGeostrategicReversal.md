## Definition
Ba-Shu Geostrategic Reversal is the episode's explanation that Western Wei's acquisition of Hanzhong and Ba-Shu changed both its resource base and the routes available against southern rivals.

## Current Synthesis
Western Wei and Eastern Wei–Northern Qi repeatedly found offense across the Tong Pass–Yellow River–Luoyang zone difficult to sustain. The more durable shift came south. Hou Jing's rebellion killed Emperor Wu of Liang, fragmented central authority, and enabled Western Wei to exploit rival claimants through alliances, territorial demands, and force. [[BaShu|Ba-Shu]] supplied resources to a damaged [[GuanzhongRegion|Guanzhong]] base and an upper-Yangtze line of advance, while its loss exposed southern regimes to pressure from both the river corridor and northern fronts.

## Key Claims
- Eastern stalemate reflected geography and supply limits on both sides, not merely commander quality.
- Southern Liang's prior stability had constrained Western Wei expansion toward Hanzhong.
- Hou Jing's rebellion and Liang dynastic conflict opened the decisive territorial opportunity.
- Ba-Shu strengthened the conqueror through production and an upper-Yangtze route.
- Losing Ba-Shu weakened southern defense by removing resources and adding another direction of attack.
- Territorial-resource change offers a more durable explanation than one decisive battle.

## Evidence
- Eastern limits: [[ltxaost4pgqhjdirwas15vizersj-ltxaost4pgqhjdirwas15vizersj]] describes repeated stalemate around passes, river lines, and Luoyang.
- Southern opening: [[ltxaost4pgqhjdirwas15vizersj-ltxaost4pgqhjdirwas15vizersj]] connects Hou Jing's rebellion and Liang civil war to Western Wei intervention.
- Resource gain: [[ltxaost4pgqhjdirwas15vizersj-ltxaost4pgqhjdirwas15vizersj]] contrasts productive Ba-Shu with damaged Guanzhong.
- Route gain: [[ltxaost4pgqhjdirwas15vizersj-ltxaost4pgqhjdirwas15vizersj]] emphasizes movement down the Yangtze and dual pressure on southern regimes.
- Comparative pattern: [[ltxaost4pgqhjdirwas15vizersj-ltxaost4pgqhjdirwas15vizersj]] compares the structure with Qin unification and Western Jin's conquest of Wu.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The comparison across Qin, Western Jin, and Northern Zhou is structural analogy, not proof of a timeless geographic law. Ba-Shu mattered alongside institutional integration, rival fragmentation, leadership, and military outcomes, and the source does not quantify their relative contributions.

## What Changed
- Established Ba-Shu acquisition as a joined resource-and-route explanation for Western Wei–Northern Zhou growth.
