# Mate Value and Mutual Choice

## Definition
Mate value is a context-dependent judgment of a person's desirability as a romantic or sexual partner, while mutual choice emphasizes that courtship depends on reciprocal selection rather than a one-sided ranking.

## Current Synthesis
The source treats mate value as multidimensional and relational. Health, dependability, intelligence, kindness, attraction, age, status, resources, personality, family circumstances, and social attention can all contribute, but no validated single measure captures the full construct. Some traits are broadly valued; others matter intensely to a particular person.

Self-esteem may partly track acceptance, rejection, status, and perceived desirability, yet self-assessment is neither fixed nor purely private. Partners, rivals, social media attention, mate-choice copying, and changes in circumstance can alter perceived value. Because choice is mutual, a person's preferences must be considered alongside what they offer and whether the other person reciprocates. The Essentials edit repeats the multidimensional and person-specific account without independently validating a mate-value measure.

## Key Claims
- Mate value is multidimensional and cannot be reduced to one universal score.
- Some traits attract broad agreement while others are specific to a particular chooser.
- Status and social attention can raise perceived desirability through comparative cues.
- Self-esteem may monitor some acceptance, rejection, and status signals without becoming a complete measure of worth.
- Children, age, health, resources, personality, and relationship context can affect assessments, but their effects vary.
- Mutual choice limits any model that treats one side as shopper and the other as ranked inventory.

- Family context: [[how-humans-select-keep-romantic-partners-in-short-long-term-dr-david-buss-scim4159413839]] says children from prior relationships can be perceived as costs while noting compatible exceptions.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
"Mate value" can invite dehumanizing or falsely precise rankings if detached from consent, reciprocity, safety, and pair-specific meaning. [[IdiosyncraticAttraction]] shows that broad consensus can weaken with familiarity, and [[CharacterBasedRomanticCompatibility]] centers demonstrated conduct and direction rather than a market score. Neither editorial version offers a comprehensive measure, and rejection should not be interpreted as an objective verdict on personal worth.
