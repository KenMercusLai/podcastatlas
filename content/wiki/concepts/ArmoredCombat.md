# Armored Combat / 盔甲格斗

Armored combat / 盔甲格斗 is the historical close-combat sport explained by [[XuePeng|薛鹏]] in [[ep238-2025-nian-ni-wan-guo-zenyang-de-xiaozhong-yundong-lpaw-kela5vk6avl-s11h-yexwlg]]. Participants wear historically styled armor and use blunt steel weapons in one-on-one or team fights, with five-on-five presented as a standard international format.

The concept matters because it makes [[UrbanNicheSports|城市小众运动]]'s threshold problem unusually clear. Armored combat is not only a combat sport; it requires knowledge of armor culture, safety norms, historical authenticity, hand-made or custom equipment, and enough anaerobic fitness to move and grapple under heavy protection. It is adjacent to [[SportsAsHistoricalReadingFrame]], but unlike a reading analogy, the body and equipment carry the historical imagination directly.

## Key Claims
- The source describes modern standardized armored combat as reviving internationally around the 1990s, with Russia named as a particularly strong scene.
- In China, the episode places participation around 2016-2017 onward, with teams or clubs in several cities.
- Five-on-five combat is decided when all members of one side are down; fallen fighters cannot continue participating.
- The sport's role differentiation rewards different body types, from frontal power to mobile flanking and opportunistic attacks.
- Historical authenticity rules and armor fit make equipment a core training issue rather than decoration.
- The sport may be easier to enjoy as spectacle than to practice long-term, which is why the episode raises [[UFC]] as a possible but difficult commercialization comparison.
