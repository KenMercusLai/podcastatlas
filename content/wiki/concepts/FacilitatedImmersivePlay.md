# Facilitated Immersive Play / 引导式沉浸游戏

## Definition
Facilitated immersive play is a group-experience model in which text or scenario quality, participant-role fit, facilitator guidance, and sustained player commitment jointly determine whether a fictional situation becomes coherent and affecting.

## Current Synthesis
EP194 rejects immersion as a property of scenery alone. Costume, rooms, lighting, and music can support a session, but strong writing, suitable allocation, timely DM guidance, and attentive co-players can create immersion even online or in visually simple settings. EP165 extends the model from 剧本杀 to escape rooms, where mechanisms and spatial production matter more but still cannot compensate for incoherent story, unreliable operation, weak live performance, or disengaged players. Because participants mediate one another's access to the story, experience quality is multiplicative: one component cannot reliably substitute for the absence of another.

Role fit is also plural. Some players project personal experience into a character, some understand the character's internal logic without self-identification, and some maintain distance. Good facilitation therefore does not require one authorized form of emotional absorption; it protects comprehension, pacing, information flow, consent, and enough shared attention for different modes of participation to coexist.

Facilitation may be distributed between a formal DM and performers embedded in the fiction. Escape-room NPCs must improvise around jokes, silence, unexpected questions, and divergent player behavior while preserving character and plot. Players also shape the result: they may need time to cross an initial embarrassment threshold, but excessive scene-taking can crowd out other participants just as absence or divided attention can break a distributed-information game.

## Key Claims
- Immersion is jointly produced by scenario quality, role fit, facilitation, and participant attention.
- Physical production supports but does not guarantee immersion, while text and interaction can sometimes sustain it with minimal scenery.
- Role allocation improves when facilitators discuss disposition, reading tolerance, and desired intensity before assigning characters.
- Players can enter roles through self-projection, internal understanding, or more distanced interpretation.
- The facilitator manages pacing, prompts, music, clarification, and relationship comprehension without replacing player agency.
- Divided attention and absence are especially damaging when information is distributed across participants.
- Live performers can make repeat sessions meaningfully variable when improvisation and branching reveal new relationships or narrative information.

## Evidence
- Quality system: [[ep194-zai-jubensha-li-chenjin-baoku-shi-yizhong-zenyang-de-tiyan-ckwriw4fquvtabaaaadtzwuv]] repeatedly identifies script, DM, role-player match, and co-player investment as interdependent experience factors.
- Multiple entry modes: [[ep194-zai-jubensha-li-chenjin-baoku-shi-yizhong-zenyang-de-tiyan-ckwriw4fquvtabaaaadtzwuv]] contrasts personal projection, understanding a character's logic, and remaining at a distance.
- Production boundary: [[ep194-zai-jubensha-li-chenjin-baoku-shi-yizhong-zenyang-de-tiyan-ckwriw4fquvtabaaaadtzwuv]] argues that decoration and costume are optional supports while strong text and serious participation are more fundamental.
- Remote facilitation: [[ep194-zai-jubensha-li-chenjin-baoku-shi-yizhong-zenyang-de-tiyan-ckwriw4fquvtabaaaadtzwuv]] describes online and remote-DM sessions in which prompting, music, pacing, and interpretation remain possible if players protect the time.
- Embedded facilitation: [[ep165-mishi-taotuo-baofa-qi-banyan-bieren-jiujing-zai-xunzhao-yizhong-zenyang-de-ciji-ckwriueennadabaaaacxz2y3]] shows NPCs sustaining character, redirecting confused players, and adapting to unscripted behavior inside an escape-room story.
- Replay boundary: [[ep165-mishi-taotuo-baofa-qi-banyan-bieren-jiujing-zai-xunzhao-yizhong-zenyang-de-ciji-ckwriueennadabaaaacxz2y3]] treats hidden endings and changing live interaction as valuable when they provide new story content rather than repetition alone.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The model is derived from experienced-player testimony about 剧本杀 and Beijing escape rooms, not controlled comparison across games, theatre, education, or therapy. Emotional intensity is not an automatic measure of artistic quality, and crying, fear, expensive production, or repeat attendance does not establish insight, safety, or durable benefit. Role matching can improve fit but may also stereotype participants or narrow experimentation; facilitators need to preserve consent and allow players to disengage safely even though abrupt withdrawal can disrupt the group.

## What Changed
- Extended the model from 剧本杀 to escape rooms with built mechanisms and performers embedded inside the fiction.
- Added live improvisation, embarrassment thresholds, scene-sharing, and narrative replay as group-experience variables.
- Qualified expensive production and novelty as supports rather than guarantees of immersion.
