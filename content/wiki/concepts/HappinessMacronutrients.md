# Happiness Macronutrients

## Definition
Happiness macronutrients is [[ArthurBrooks]]' frame for happiness as a combination of enjoyment, satisfaction, and meaning rather than a single feeling or the absence of unhappiness.

## Current Synthesis
In [[the-art-of-true-happiness-dr-arthur-brooks-scim2290485637]], Brooks uses the nutrition analogy to make happiness inspectable. Enjoyment is not bare pleasure; satisfaction is accomplishment after struggle; meaning is the larger answer to coherence, purpose, significance, love, and transcendence. The strongest synthesis is that happiness is compatible with difficulty because each macronutrient can contain frustration or suffering.

This framework connects personal wellbeing to moral and social design. Leisure, work, marriage, faith, teaching, and ambition are evaluated by whether they deepen love, service, practice, and long-term values rather than merely increasing comfort or public status.

## Key Claims
- Happiness can be broken into enjoyment, satisfaction, and meaning.
- Unhappiness, frustration, and suffering can exist inside a happy life.
- Enjoyment requires more than pleasure because it includes people, memory, intention, and moral aspiration.
- Satisfaction depends on struggle, progress, or accomplishment rather than reward alone.
- Meaning asks why life matters and whether action coheres with deeper beliefs and values.
- The framework is practical because it turns vague happiness seeking into inspectable life domains.

## Evidence
- Three-part model - [[the-art-of-true-happiness-dr-arthur-brooks-scim2290485637]] defines enjoyment, satisfaction, and meaning as the three macronutrients of happiness.
- Difficulty inside happiness - [[the-art-of-true-happiness-dr-arthur-brooks-scim2290485637]] says suffering and frustration can be embedded within the components of happiness.
- Life-domain testing - [[the-art-of-true-happiness-dr-arthur-brooks-scim2290485637]] applies the model to leisure, work, relationships, retirement, faith, ambition, and pilgrimage.
- Practical self-assessment - [[the-art-of-true-happiness-dr-arthur-brooks-scim2290485637]] closes with questions about coherence, purpose, belief, significance, and whether one is choosing status over happiness.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source presents Brooks' integrated philosophical and religious account, not a settled measurement model for happiness. Claims about faith, transcendence, right-hemisphere meaning, and life-stage theory remain source-scoped.
