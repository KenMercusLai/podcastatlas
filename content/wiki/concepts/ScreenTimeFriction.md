# Screen Time Friction

Screen time friction is the deliberate insertion of pauses, effort, distance, or inconvenience before a person uses a phone or app. [[tech-20260102-0102-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260102-0102-mp-tech-pod-128]] adds the concept through [[Clearspace]] prompts, physical app-blocking devices, lockboxes, and [[BenGoldhersh]]'s heavy anti-scrolling experiments.

The design pattern reverses the usual product goal of making use effortless. A breath prompt, short exercise, dashboard check, app-blocking tap, locked case, or six-pound phone case creates enough interruption for the user to notice the impulse and choose again.

[[154-sishisui-ganyan-bu-zuo-nazhi-wenshuili-de-qingwa-lg-qj3p2lq-8sk-rn2vk209tq9x]] adds a self-measurement trigger. [[DavidWeng|大卫翁]] checks phone use and finds heavy daily screen time plus frequent pickups, treating those numbers as evidence that autonomy is leaking through small automatic openings rather than through one dramatic addiction story.

[[ep258-women-ruhe-chongshi-shuiqian-dushu-lobyzxqk2oaov0v51cm355u8noix]] adds a bedtime-reading contrast. The episode's opening asks listeners to put phones down for ninety seconds, and its larger argument implies that a bedside book or body practice can create friction against default pre-sleep phone use.

[[ep265-dang-qing3xiu13-chongshang-resou-women-ruhe-gei-rensheng-fang-yige-changjia-lsloczlnkmcrrfpn9coh8rrqo-wb]] adds travel as situational friction. Time zones, unfamiliar environments, walking routes, ferries, forests, and local social rhythm can make automatic phone and work-message checking less smooth, but the source still treats personal and workplace boundaries as necessary.

## Key Claims
- Friction can be cognitive, behavioral, social, or physical.
- The point is not to remove the phone entirely; it is to make automatic opening less automatic.
- [[Clearspace]] uses prompts and pauses before selected apps.
- Physical blockers and lockboxes externalize self-control by making app access depend on a device or object.
- [[BenGoldhersh]]'s Staff of Destiny and [[MatterNeuroscience]]'s proposed heavy case make inconvenience the product.
- Friction can help, but the source leaves open whether these tools produce durable behavior change over time.
- Episode 154 adds that usage dashboards and pickup counts can make invisible impulse loops visible enough to become a self-governance question.
- EP258 adds that a reading or sleep ritual can be a low-tech friction pattern before bed, especially when phones pull attention into feeds.
- EP265 adds that travel can create environmental friction against the phone, but it works only when the workplace does not keep reopening the channel.
