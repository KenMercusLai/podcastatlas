# Screen Time Friction

## Definition
Screen time friction is the deliberate insertion of pauses, effort, distance, alternative routines, or social boundaries before a person uses a phone or app automatically.

## Current Synthesis
Friction reverses the normal product goal of effortless use. It can be built into software through prompts and pauses, embodied in app-blocking objects and heavy cases, created by putting a device out of sight, or produced by rules that remove phones from shared spaces. Self-measurement can expose automatic pickups, while books, body practices, pets, friends, travel, and bedtime routines provide an activity to move toward rather than only a prohibition to obey.

The sources support friction as an interruption mechanism, not a stand-alone cure. [[PaulaMejia]] reduced screen time by changing her morning cue and accepting social invitations, but use rebounded within days. Travel and retreats can alter the environment, yet work messages can reopen the channel. Durable change therefore depends on repeated routines and social or [[WorkRestBoundary|workplace boundaries]] as well as momentary inconvenience.

## Key Claims
- Friction can be cognitive, software-based, physical, environmental, behavioral, or social.
- Its immediate purpose is to make an automatic opening visible enough for another choice.
- Substituting reading, movement, companionship, or social contact gives the interruption a positive destination.
- Usage dashboards and pickup counts can reveal hidden impulse loops, although measurement itself can become another engagement mechanic.
- Phone-free spaces and out-of-sight storage can reduce cues without requiring complete disconnection.
- Environmental changes help only while work and social expectations do not continually reopen access.
- Short-term reduction does not establish durability; rebound is a central qualification.

## Evidence
- Software and physical counter-design: [[tech-20260102-0102-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260102-0102-mp-tech-pod-128]] describes [[Clearspace]] prompts, app-blocking devices, lockboxes, and [[BenGoldhersh]]'s deliberately inconvenient phone experiments.
- Cue awareness: [[154-sishisui-ganyan-bu-zuo-nazhi-wenshuili-de-qingwa-lg-qj3p2lq-8sk-rn2vk209tq9x]] uses daily screen time and pickup counts to make repeated automatic checking visible as an autonomy problem.
- Positive substitution: [[ep258-women-ruhe-chongshi-shuiqian-dushu-lobyzxqk2oaov0v51cm355u8noix]] frames bedtime books and body practices as alternatives to default feed use, while [[tech-20260922-0922-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260922-0922-mp-tech-pod-128]] adds morning reading, time with a dog, and meeting friends.
- Environmental friction: [[ep265-dang-qing3xiu13-chongshang-resou-women-ruhe-gei-rensheng-fang-yige-changjia-lsloczlnkmcrrfpn9coh8rrqo-wb]] uses travel, time zones, walking, and unfamiliar routines; [[tech-20260922-0922-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260922-0922-mp-tech-pod-128]] adds [[GoldenDoor]]'s phone-free common areas and device pouch.
- Durability boundary: [[tech-20260922-0922-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260922-0922-mp-tech-pod-128]] reports lower screen time during Mejia's experiment and a rebound within days; [[ep265-dang-qing3xiu13-chongshang-resou-women-ruhe-gei-rensheng-fang-yige-changjia-lsloczlnkmcrrfpn9coh8rrqo-wb]] says workplace contact can defeat vacation distance.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not provide controlled comparisons, effect sizes, or long-term follow-up. Friction can add inconvenience without changing the underlying need, reward, or work obligation, and commercial tools can reproduce dashboards or streaks that keep the user inside a measurement loop. Device reading can also be meaningful reading, so the relevant distinction is intentional versus automatic use rather than screen versus paper in every case.

## What Changed
- Expanded friction from product features and physical barriers into cue substitution, shared-space rules, and social commitments.
- Added direct evidence of rapid rebound after a short reduction in screen time.
- Strengthened the judgment that durable friction requires supportive workplace and social boundaries.
