# Character-Reading Delegation Trust / 识人授权信任

Character-reading delegation trust / 识人授权信任 names the leadership pattern [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1029-guoshi-wushuang-taishici-yinuo-qianjin-yi-dangxian-lvckwlhz5g-oappy89thye9oyrto|Hanji 1029]] draws from [[SunCe|孙策]] and [[TaishiCi|太史慈]]. A leader delegates despite visible defection risk because the decision rests on a judgment about the person's character, obligations, and need for real recognition.

The case is concrete. After [[LiuYaoLateHan|刘繇]] dies, Sun Ce sends Taishi Ci to calm Liu Yao's remaining followers and inspect [[YuzhangCommandery|豫章郡]]. Others warn that Taishi Ci may never return, but Sun Ce believes Taishi Ci values promises and has no better worthy lord to follow. Taishi Ci returns inside the agreed sixty-day window and brings back information about [[HuaXin|华歆]], [[TongZhiLateHan|僮芝]], and [[PanyangLocalForces|番阳民帅]].

The concept is adjacent to [[RetainerReciprocityEthic|士为知己者死]], but it is less death-centered: the repayment here is reliable service and intelligence, not martyrdom. It also differs from [[OathBoundCommandTrust|盟誓约束下的统帅信任]], because no public oath protects the decision. Sun Ce's confidence comes from reading Taishi Ci's character and prior relationship, making the episode a positive extension of [[SituationalCharacterTests|五观识人法]] and [[TalentAsStateTreasure|以贤臣为宝]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1028-xiaobawang-sunce-liwei-chengba-jiangdong-lgur-3fmpr55wy3aji-0rm38y7x|Hanji 1028]] supplies the immediate precondition for that trust. Sun Ce first captures Taishi Ci, releases him, and appoints him as `门下督` after reading his courage, loyalty, and need for a worthy lord. The later Yuzhang mission in Hanji 1029 is therefore not a sudden blind gamble; it follows an earlier act of [[CapturedOpponentTalentIncorporation|降敌任用式立威]].

## Key Claims
- Delegation can be rational even when escape is physically possible if the leader correctly reads the person's honor logic and recognition needs.
- Character reading is only valuable when it turns into real authorization; otherwise talent remains admired but unused.
- Promise-keeping becomes strategic when the returned subordinate brings information or capacity the center could not obtain by force alone.
- The pattern is risky: a wrong character read would convert trust into defection, so the source keeps Sun Ce's judgment as a concrete case rather than a universal rule.
- The concept should not be collapsed into blind trust, because Sun Ce's decision follows a specific reading of Taishi Ci's values and available alternatives.
- Hanji 1028 adds that trust delegation may be preceded by victorious incorporation: the leader first establishes control, then tests whether a former opponent can become authorized talent.
