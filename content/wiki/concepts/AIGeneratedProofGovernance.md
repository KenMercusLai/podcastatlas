# AI-Generated Proof Governance

## Definition
AI-generated proof governance is the problem of deciding when a mathematical result produced or accelerated by AI should be accepted, credited, explained, and integrated into the human mathematical record.

## Current Synthesis
The sources turn claimed [[OpenAI]] mathematics milestones into a governance problem. The issue is not only whether a proof is correct; it is also whether the route is inspectable, nearby human work is handled fairly, priority can be judged, and large volumes of cumbersome but valid output can be absorbed. If proof production becomes abundant, institutions may need to reward verification, explanation, problem choice, and usefulness without pretending those roles are equivalent to authorship of the original result.

## Key Claims
- Mathematical acceptance depends on explanation and traceability, not only a final answer.
- AI-agent proof production can blur authorship when many agents, tools, messages, and prior public or private work interact.
- Priority disputes become harder when a lab can move quickly after hearing that human mathematicians are near a result.
- AI math milestones may shift human mathematical value toward interpretation, verification, taste, and conceptual synthesis.
- Prize and publication norms may need adjustment when the winning path is less human-readable than traditional proof work.
- High-volume result generation can overwhelm peer review and make triage, replication, and conceptual synthesis institutional bottlenecks.

## Evidence
- Proof explanation - [[out-numbered-ais-contentious-maths-milestone-6aa3ce622e8bb8424c920cfb]] says mathematicians are uneasy because the OpenAI paper does not show the usual level of detail about how the result was reached.
- Priority and overlap - [[out-numbered-ais-contentious-maths-milestone-6aa3ce622e8bb8424c920cfb]] says [[TristanBuckmaster]] and [[LeVanElgindi]] had related unfinished work and published early before OpenAI's announcement.
- Agent-swarm production - [[out-numbered-ais-contentious-maths-milestone-6aa3ce622e8bb8424c920cfb]] describes large teams of AI agents reading, coding, and messaging one another in the path to the claimed result.
- Incentive redesign - [[schrodingers-chat-can-ai-and-scientists-co-exist-cc0f4a5a1edb896ae869b3d9c711bc4f]] asks how departments should organize credit when machines generate results and leading researchers direct, verify, explain, or select among them.
- Elegance and absorption - [[schrodingers-chat-can-ai-and-scientists-co-exist-cc0f4a5a1edb896ae869b3d9c711bc4f]] says reported outputs can favor cumbersome counterexamples or solutions over the harmonious proofs human mathematicians value.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Neither source validates or invalidates the reported proofs, and both cover a fast-moving controversy rather than a completed review process. The page therefore treats the cases as governance signals, not settled mathematical facts. Institutional redesign proposals also remain prospective: abundant generated output does not by itself show which credit system will produce better science.

## What Changed
- Extended proof governance from a single priority dispute to high-volume review, incentive, and interpretation capacity.
