# China Long-Term Care Insurance / 中国长期护理险

## Definition
China long-term care insurance / 中国长期护理险 is the public insurance and assessment framework discussed in the source for paying long-term care services for selected people with disability,失能, or high-care needs.

## Current Synthesis
The episode treats long-term care insurance as a necessary but incomplete answer to dementia and disability care. Tang Yong describes pilot expansion from cities such as Qingdao and Shanghai toward broader rollout, while emphasizing that access depends on assessment and that benefits mainly pay for care services rather than unrestricted cash.

The concept's source-scoped judgment is that long-term-care insurance can reduce family-only burden only when it is paired with trained workers, available service providers, dementia-specific programs, and culturally acceptable care options. Insurance without service supply leaves families with money constraints; service supply without payment leaves access unequal.

## Key Claims
- China long-term-care insurance is presented as moving from city pilots toward broader public coverage, but implementation remains staged.
- Eligibility is assessment-based, so not every older adult or every dementia stage is covered.
- The benefit is framed mainly as service payment rather than a cash subsidy.
- The policy can support care socialization only if professional services, institutions, and home/community care capacity are available.
- Dementia care exposes a gap between formal coverage categories and the messy realities of cognitive, behavioral, family, and housing needs.

## Evidence
- Pilot timeline - [[manchang-de-gaobie-shizhizheng-zhaohuzhe-yu-zhongjiang-laoqu-de-women-lscotoglqyoznytqatlefdwv3jez]] says long-term-care insurance had pilot cities around 2016 and had expanded to many cities by 2025.
- Policy direction - [[manchang-de-gaobie-shizhizheng-zhaohuzhe-yu-zhongjiang-laoqu-de-women-lscotoglqyoznytqatlefdwv3jez]] records 2026 as an important policy node with discussion of broader coverage over the next two to three years.
- Eligibility and benefit form - [[manchang-de-gaobie-shizhizheng-zhaohuzhe-yu-zhongjiang-laoqu-de-women-lscotoglqyoznytqatlefdwv3jez]] says benefits require unified assessment, focus on key groups, and pay mainly for nursing/care services rather than cash.
- Family burden context - [[manchang-de-gaobie-shizhizheng-zhaohuzhe-yu-zhongjiang-laoqu-de-women-lscotoglqyoznytqatlefdwv3jez]] links self-payment, family taboo around institutions, high care costs, and changing generational attitudes toward institutional care.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source reports policy discussion at podcast-summary level. It does not verify city-by-city benefit rules, national legislation, actuarial sustainability, provider reimbursement levels, or the exact 2026 implementation timetable.
