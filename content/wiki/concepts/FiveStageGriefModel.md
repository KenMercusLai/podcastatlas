# Five-Stage Grief Model

## Definition
The five-stage grief model names denial, anger, bargaining, depression, and acceptance; in this source it is treated as a historically influential descriptive framework that became misleading when popular culture turned it into a required sequence for bereavement.

## Current Synthesis
The episode locates the model in Elisabeth Kübler-Ross's work with terminally ill patients, then distinguishes that context from the later expectation that bereaved people should progress through five emotions in order. Its appeal is legibility: a tidy sequence gives practitioners, families, and mourners language for an untidy experience. Its risk is prescription, including the inference that an absent stage, a recurring feeling, or a moment of pleasure signals defective grief.

The wiki's bounded grief sources instead support non-linear adaptation. [[DualProcessBereavementOscillation]] allows movement between loss and restoration, while [[GriefResponseAgency]] distinguishes involuntary reactions from choices without imposing a timetable. The five labels may remain recognizable descriptions, but this source does not support using them as a diagnostic checklist, prognosis, or universal path.

## Key Claims
- The model's original terminal-illness context differs from its popular use as a bereavement sequence.
- Denial, anger, bargaining, depression, and acceptance are not shown here to occur universally or in a fixed order.
- A simple framework can provide language while still distorting individual variation.
- Prescriptive stage expectations can remove hope or make normal non-linearity appear pathological.
- Oscillation and response agency offer complementary alternatives without defining another mandatory route.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a public podcast summary, not a historical review of Kübler-Ross's full work or a systematic review of stage research. Rejecting a mandatory sequence does not mean that the five named experiences never occur or that stage language cannot help some people describe part of their experience. The episode supports qualification of universal and prescriptive use, not erasure of the model's historical influence.

## What Changed
- Established the model as historically influential but non-universal and non-prescriptive.
