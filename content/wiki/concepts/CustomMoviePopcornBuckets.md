# Custom Movie Popcorn Buckets / 电影定制爆米花桶

Custom movie popcorn buckets are limited theatrical merchandise objects that still perform a concession function but are mainly bought as film memorabilia, social-media props, and fan identity signals. In [[aodesai-ba-baomihua-zhuangjin-teluoyi-muma-haolaiwu-dapian-weihe-zhadui-dingzhi-baomihuatong-1004599628]], the episode treats the shift from printed buckets to Trojan horses, IMAX cameras, sandworms, helmets, handbags, and cars as a move from packaging toward collectible IP goods.

The concept extends [[ThemeParkAsMediaPlatform]]. Theme parks such as [[Disneyland]] and [[TokyoDisneyResort]] proved that food containers can hold memory and character attachment as well as snacks. Cinemas borrow that logic, but compress it into the film-release window: the bucket has to sell while the movie is culturally present.

The business chain combines rights holders, design vendors, theaters, fans, and resale markets. [[ZincPopcornBuckets|Zinc]], [[SnapCreative]], and [[GoldenLink]] sit in the design-and-licensing layer; [[AMCTheatres|AMC Theatres]] and [[IMAX]] show how theater brands and premium formats can use the object as a revenue and marketing surface.

## Key Claims
- The bucket can carry [[EntertainmentIPFlywheel|entertainment IP]] after the screening ends, turning a temporary viewing experience into a displayed object.
- It raises [[ProductLedWillingnessToPay]] when the buyer values the prop, character, joke, scarcity, or format association more than the popcorn.
- Its package function matters because the container's shape, opening, grip, and visibility make [[PackagingAsProductExperience]] part of the media object.
- Social sharing turns design weirdness into marketing, especially when fans photograph, joke about, or compare the object online.
- Limited release windows can produce [[CollectibleDropScarcity]], fandom signaling, and secondary-market premiums.
- The same release-window dependence creates [[RetailInventoryVelocity]] pressure because theaters must order before they know box office, word of mouth, and character popularity.
- Failed demand forecasts can turn high-margin buckets into [[InventoryWriteDownRisk]].
- China-side examples in the source show the format transferring across markets, but supply management and fan-demand forecasting remain local operating problems.
