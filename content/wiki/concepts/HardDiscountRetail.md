# Hard-Discount Retail / 硬折扣零售

Hard-discount retail is the supermarket and grocery format where the merchant tries to make low price structurally credible through narrower assortment, direct sourcing, private-label development, factory cooperation, simpler operations, or lower supply-chain cost. [[zhizhuxia-xinpian-naxia-jinban-guonei-piaofang-ai-moxing-baofa-jiagezhan-1004403588]] adds the concept through [[Aldi|奥乐齐]]'s Hangzhou expansion and the broader Chinese retailer interest in hard discount.

The source's caution is that hard discount is not automatically the same as a strong private-label business. If many retailers rely on the same contract factories and lack product-development capability, low prices may be easy to copy while trust, taste, packaging, and category judgment remain weak.

## Key Claims
- Hard discount turns price into an operating-system claim, not only a promotion.
- Direct sourcing or factory cooperation can reduce markups, but only if quality control and product definition are strong enough.
- The format can pressure conventional supermarkets when consumers become more price sensitive.
- Private labels strengthen the model only when customers trust the retailer's selection, quality, and consistency.
- City rollout still depends on [[RetailSiteSelection]] because low price cannot compensate for poor store access or weak neighborhood fit.
