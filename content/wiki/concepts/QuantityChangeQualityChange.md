# Quantity Change to Quality Change / 量变引发质变

Quantity change to quality change is [[152-guanyu-2026-nian-de-si-ge-caixiang-nhx-c5xjwaovpaqjpua24diohsaz]]'s umbrella for threshold-style forecasting. [[DavidWeng|大卫翁]] argues that 2026's most important surprises may not be new phenomena, but old accumulated pressures crossing visibility, legitimacy, or financing thresholds.

The source applies the frame to four areas: [[AIBacklashPolitics]] when AI's distribution and infrastructure costs become political; [[PrivateMarketBubbleOpacity]] when private assets hide leverage and valuation risk; [[ChinaExcessSavingsReallocation]] when low-yield deposits and broken real-estate expectations push households toward other assets; and [[WesternChinaMisreading]] when stale narratives about China fail to explain asset prices, industrial loans, or company globalization.

## Key Claims
- A threshold event can look sudden because the measuring system did not observe the buildup well.
- The concept is useful for source-dated predictions because it shifts attention from exact timing to accumulated pressure, transmission paths, and triggers.
- A threshold frame should not become inevitability. The source treats 2026 as a watch window, not a guaranteed break date.
- The same pattern can apply across society, finance, household behavior, and geopolitical perception.
