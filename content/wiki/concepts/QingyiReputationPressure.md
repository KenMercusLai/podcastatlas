## Definition

Qingyi reputation pressure / 清议声誉压力 is the late-Han mechanism in which scholar-student praise and blame becomes a public force that ranks officials, disciplines conduct, and later exposes the same reputation network to hostile party framing.

## Current Synthesis

The current evidence starts with Hanji 871's pre-prohibition public-opinion field. Taixue students number in the tens of thousands, [[GuoTaiLateHan|郭泰]] and [[JiaBiaoLateHan|贾彪]] stand at the front, and public phrases elevate [[LiYingLateHan|李膺]], [[ChenFanLateHan|陈蕃]], and [[WangChangLateHan|王畅]] as models. The point is not only admiration. Officials fear criticism enough to visit and seek approval, so reputation becomes a non-office disciplinary channel.

The same mechanism becomes dangerous once hostile interpreters control court language. Hanji 874 shows association, student reception, mutual praise, and party formation recoded as disorder before Emperor Huan. Hanji 892 formalizes fame into lists, while Hanji 893 turns list visibility into a persecution target. Qingyi therefore has two sides: it can restrain officials when formal institutions are weak, but it also makes independent moral authority legible to an insecure court.

## Key Claims

- Qingyi works by making praise and blame public enough that officials care about reputation costs.
- Taixue and scholar networks supply a social infrastructure for distributed judgment.
- Public labels can discipline high officials even without formal command authority.
- The same visibility that gives moral criticism force can expose named figures to repression.
- Party accusations become more plausible when independent reputation networks already look organized.
- Qingyi is not identical to treason framing; it is the social-evaluation field that later accusers recode.

## Evidence

- Taixue scale: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-871-cheng-e-yang-shan-weihe-fanbei-wuxian-ruyu-lujkihgvh-cafkh9bfak6-vttpwt|Hanji 871]] says Taixue students number in the tens of thousands, giving scholar opinion a large public base.
- Reputation leadership: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-871-cheng-e-yang-shan-weihe-fanbei-wuxian-ruyu-lujkihgvh-cafkh9bfak6-vttpwt|Hanji 871]] says Guo Tai and Jia Biao are leading student-scholars and mutually praise Li Ying, Chen Fan, and Wang Chang.
- Official pressure: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-871-cheng-e-yang-shan-weihe-fanbei-wuxian-ruyu-lujkihgvh-cafkh9bfak6-vttpwt|Hanji 871]] says officials from the highest ranks downward fear public criticism and seek approval.
- Disorder recoding: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-874-zhenjing-muzi-xiangcan-tianli-nanrong-lpr3eglbnwbve1clrttapw6k5zdq|Hanji 874]] says Lao Xiu's accusation treats student reception, mutual praise, party formation, court slander, and disordering customs as punishable danger.
- List formalization: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-892-xiu-zhiyu-guren-youshenme-jiejing-lnobbkp6mj4xyb9jeuqsiuqogct|Hanji 892]] catalogs士人 honor categories before the later crackdown and warns that public fame can become exposure.
- Target conversion: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-893-rongyu-bang-ruhe-biancheng-siwang-mingdan-lmxzqoauf79-swwnk-0cevav0gq|Hanji 893]] says reputation-list visibility becomes a death-facing target field once Cao Jie explains party association as throne danger.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept should not be collapsed into a modern public-opinion theory or into every late-Han reputation practice. The source evidence concerns scholar-official and Taixue prestige in a specific Huan-Ling party-prohibition sequence. Qingyi can discipline corrupt power, but the same episode sequence shows that visibility without institutional protection can become targetability.

## What Changed

- Created this concept to separate the social-evaluation mechanism from later treason framing and reputation-list targeting.
