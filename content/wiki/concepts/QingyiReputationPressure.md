## Definition

Qingyi reputation pressure / 清议声誉压力 is the late-Han mechanism in which scholar-official and student praise and blame becomes a public force that ranks officials, disciplines conduct, turns personal recognition into status, and later exposes the same reputation network to hostile party framing.

## Current Synthesis

The current evidence now starts before the Taixue formula. Hanji 869 shows [[LiYingLateHan|李膺]] earning visible prestige after his pardon and restoration as Sili xiaowei. His execution of [[ZhangShuoLateHan|张硕]], successful defense before [[EmperorHuanOfHan|汉桓帝]], and ability to make palace eunuchs afraid turn him into more than a famous name. The episode says士人 who were received or accommodated by him were described as having "登龙门," so access to a moral figure becomes a public credential.

Hanji 871 expands that prestige into a wider pre-prohibition public-opinion field. [[Taixue|太学]] students number in the tens of thousands, [[GuoTaiLateHan|郭泰]] and [[JiaBiaoLateHan|贾彪]] stand at the front, and public phrases elevate Li Ying, [[ChenFanLateHan|陈蕃]], and [[WangChangShumaoLateHan|王畅]] as models. The point is not only admiration. Officials fear criticism enough to visit and seek approval, so reputation becomes a non-office disciplinary channel.

The same mechanism becomes dangerous once hostile interpreters control court language. Hanji 874 shows association, student reception, mutual praise, and party formation recoded as disorder before Huan. Hanji 892 formalizes fame into lists, while Hanji 893 turns list visibility into a persecution target. Qingyi therefore has two sides: it can restrain officials when formal institutions are weak, but it also makes independent moral authority legible to an insecure court.

## Key Claims

- Qingyi works by making praise and blame public enough that officials care about reputation costs.
- Personal recognition by a high-prestige figure can become a status credential before formal honor lists appear.
- Taixue and scholar networks supply a social infrastructure for distributed judgment.
- Public labels can discipline high officials even without formal command authority.
- The same visibility that gives moral criticism force can expose named figures to repression.
- Party accusations become more plausible when independent reputation networks already look organized.
- Qingyi is not identical to treason framing; it is the social-evaluation field that later accusers recode.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept should not be collapsed into a modern public-opinion theory or into every late-Han reputation practice. The source evidence concerns scholar-official and Taixue prestige in a specific Huan-Ling party-prohibition sequence. Qingyi can discipline corrupt power, but the same episode sequence shows that visibility without institutional protection can become targetability. Hanji 869 adds a positive credential case before the larger danger appears, but it does not prove that Li Ying's recognition could protect people once the court later recoded reputation as faction.

## What Changed

- Hanji 869 moves the concept earlier by adding Li Ying's "登龙门" recognition power before Hanji 871's broader Taixue praise formula.
- The current judgment now treats personal access to a moral exemplar as one form of reputation pressure, alongside public lists and student praise.
