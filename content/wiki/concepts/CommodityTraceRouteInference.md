## Definition

商品踪迹式路线推断 / Commodity-Trace Route Inference is the use of an object's observed location and reported origin to hypothesize otherwise unknown trade connections, distances, and travel routes.

## Current Synthesis

Two episodes now supply parallel cases. In Hanji 269-1, [[TangMengWesternHan|唐蒙]] finds Shu sauce in [[Nanyue|南越]], asks about its immediate source, and checks the explanation with Shu merchants. Their testimony points through [[YelangStateWesternHan|夜郎]] and a navigable river corridor, which Tang Meng converts into a proposed military and administrative approach to Nanyue. In [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-306-2-hanchao-diyiwei-zouxiang-shijie-de-zhongguoren-lhm5be2xdxbj0v5zmkbqdoguf1z|Hanji 306-2]], [[ZhangQianWesternHan|张骞]] sees bamboo staffs and fine cloth associated with Shu in [[DaxiaWesternHan|大夏]], asks where they were obtained, and converts the answer into a hypothesis that a southeastern market and perhaps a route toward [[ShenduWesternHan|身毒]] lie comparatively near Shu.

Together the cases separate inference from execution. Goods can move through intermediaries across a network that states cannot traverse or control directly. Tang Meng supplements the clue with merchant testimony, navigability claims, political assessment, gifts, and a large logistical mission; Zhang Qian's proposed search still fails because geographic information, local relationships, and security capacity are insufficient. Commodity traces can choose a direction and expose a strategic possibility, but only reconnaissance and local bargaining test whether the inferred corridor is usable.

## Key Claims

- A commodity's displacement can reveal connectivity even when the route and intermediaries are unknown.
- Reported origin and market information can turn an object into geographic and diplomatic intelligence.
- Indirect circulation does not establish direct bilateral trade or a safe, continuous route.
- Cross-checking testimony and assessing transport capacity, political control, and local consent are separate steps after the initial clue.
- Route hypotheses must be tested against terrain, political fragmentation, access control, and violence.
- Failed reconnaissance can still generate useful regional knowledge and redirect strategy.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept currently rests on two structured podcast summaries rather than the underlying primary texts, archaeology, or reconstructed trade networks. The presence of goods may reflect multiple exchanges, copying, relabeling, or long intermediary chains; it cannot by itself establish provenance, chronology, route direction, transport continuity, political control, or merchant identity. Hanji 269-1's reported river width, route, influence relations, troop availability, and conditional agreement remain unverified, while Hanji 306-2's reported lack of bandits on the proposed Shu route is a prior judgment contradicted by the subsequent account of obstruction and robbery.

## What Changed

- Added Tang Meng's sauce case as a second commodity-to-route inference.
- Extended the framework from direction selection to merchant cross-checking, navigability, political assessment, and local negotiation.
- Preserved the distinction between circulation evidence, operational access, and durable state control.
