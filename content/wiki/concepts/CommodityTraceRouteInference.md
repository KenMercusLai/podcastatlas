## Definition

商品踪迹式路线推断 / Commodity-Trace Route Inference is the use of an object's observed location and reported origin to hypothesize otherwise unknown trade connections, distances, and travel routes.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-306-2-hanchao-diyiwei-zouxiang-shijie-de-zhongguoren-lhm5be2xdxbj0v5zmkbqdoguf1z|Hanji 306-2]] supplies a compact historical case. [[ZhangQianWesternHan|张骞]] sees bamboo staffs and fine cloth associated with Shu in [[DaxiaWesternHan|大夏]], asks where they were obtained, and converts the answer into a hypothesis that a southeastern market and perhaps a route toward [[ShenduWesternHan|身毒]] lie comparatively near Shu.

The resulting expedition also shows the inference's limit. Goods can move through intermediaries across a network that states cannot traverse directly. Han parties can use the trace to choose a search direction, yet still fail because their geographic information, local relationships, and security capacity are insufficient. The failed mission nevertheless produces secondary intelligence about [[DianKingdomWesternHan|滇国]] and the Southwestern Yi.

## Key Claims

- A commodity's displacement can reveal connectivity even when the route and intermediaries are unknown.
- Reported origin and market information can turn an object into geographic and diplomatic intelligence.
- Indirect circulation does not establish direct bilateral trade or a safe, continuous route.
- Route hypotheses must be tested against terrain, political fragmentation, access control, and violence.
- Failed reconnaissance can still generate useful regional knowledge and redirect strategy.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept currently rests on one structured podcast summary rather than the underlying primary text, archaeology, or a reconstructed trade network. The presence of goods may reflect multiple exchanges, copying, relabeling, or long intermediary chains; it cannot by itself establish provenance, chronology, route direction, transport continuity, political control, or merchant identity. The episode's reported lack of bandits on the proposed Shu route is especially a prior judgment contradicted by the subsequent account of obstruction and robbery.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Zhang Qian's conversion of commodity observations into a route hypothesis.
- Distinguished evidence of circulation from proof of a direct, safe, or state-controlled road.
- Added failed reconnaissance as a source of secondary intelligence rather than treating it as information-free failure.
