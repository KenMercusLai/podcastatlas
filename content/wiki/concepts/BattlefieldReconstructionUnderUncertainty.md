# Battlefield Reconstruction Under Uncertainty

## Definition

Battlefield reconstruction under uncertainty is the practice of combining terrain, archaeology, material traces, eyewitness testimony, later recollection, and institutional records while keeping the gaps and conflicts among those evidence types explicit.

## Current Synthesis

The [[BattleOfLittleBighorn]] is an extreme case because no U.S. soldier who stayed with [[GeorgeArmstrongCuster]] survived. Native testimony supplies indispensable perspectives but varies and was often recorded later through translation. U.S. recollections describe only adjacent positions or later discovery. Topography constrains possible routes, while bodies, cartridge cases, weapon evidence, and archaeology after a 1983 fire reveal fighting across several locations.

The method does not produce one cinematic sequence. It narrows possibilities: Custer likely moved along the eastern bluffs, fighting occurred around Calhoun Hill and ridge positions, and the final collapse was dispersed rather than a single immaculate stand. Claims about motive, exact timing, dialogue, surrender, suicide, or individual deaths remain less secure than claims grounded in location and material pattern.

## Key Claims

- Missing survivor testimony raises the value of independent material and Native evidence.
- Each evidence type answers different questions and carries different biases.
- Terrain and artifacts can constrain movement without revealing intention.
- Later recollection must be weighed against delay, translation, reputation, and commercial incentives.
- A responsible synthesis ranks confidence rather than filling every narrative gap.
- Contradictory accounts can bound possibilities without being forced into one timeline.

## Evidence

- Testimonial asymmetry: [[453-custers-last-stand-the-final-showdown-part-8-glt8261745977]] emphasizes the absence of survivors with Custer and the necessity of Native accounts.
- Material record: [[453-custers-last-stand-the-final-showdown-part-8-glt8261745977]] uses cartridge cases, body locations, weapons, and archaeology exposed after the 1983 fire.
- Terrain constraint: [[453-custers-last-stand-the-final-showdown-part-8-glt8261745977]] uses bluffs, ravines, river approaches, and ridge positions to test possible movements.
- Recollection risk: [[453-custers-last-stand-the-final-showdown-part-8-glt8261745977]] treats Crow scout claims and Peter Thompson's story as possible but not secure.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

- Material evidence can be disturbed, incompletely recovered, or compatible with more than one sequence.
- Testimony recorded later is not worthless; discounting it wholesale would reproduce the source asymmetry the method is meant to correct.
- Agreement among evidence types raises confidence but does not make internal states or exact chronology directly observable.
- The concept is grounded here in one popular-history treatment rather than a technical archaeological methodology.

## What Changed

- Established a confidence-ranked framework for reconstructing battles without complete survivor testimony.
- Separated constraints on movement from claims about motive and exact sequence.
- Made Native testimony and archaeology complementary rather than interchangeable evidence.
