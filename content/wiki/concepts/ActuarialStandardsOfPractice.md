# Actuarial Standards of Practice

Actuarial standards of practice are the professional expectations that guide credentialed actuaries when they use data, build models, and communicate results. [[data-risk-and-actuarial-science-in-insurance]] highlights ASOP 23 on data quality, ASOP 41 on actuarial communications, and ASOP 56 on modeling as especially relevant to actuaries and to non-actuaries who work with actuarial data.

The source treats these standards as practical discipline rather than paperwork. [[MaryPatCampbell]] says the data-quality standard does not require a full audit of every dataset, but it does require actuaries to understand how data can go wrong and to make reasonability checks before relying on it.

## Key Claims
- Standards make actuarial work accountable when data, models, and business decisions are uncertain.
- ASOP 23 turns data quality into an explicit professional responsibility.
- ASOP 41 makes communication part of the work because model limits and data caveats must be explained.
- ASOP 56 links actuarial modeling to assumptions, controls, and model-use judgment.
- Non-actuary collaborators can work better with actuaries when they understand these expectations.
- Standards do not eliminate judgment; they structure the checks, disclosures, and reasonability tests that judgment must cover.
