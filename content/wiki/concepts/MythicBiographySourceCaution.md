# 神话化身世的史料边界 / Mythic Biography Source Caution

## Definition
神话化身世的史料边界 / mythic biography source caution is the practice of preserving legendary birth, omen, romance, and encounter material as reception evidence while refusing to treat it as verified biography.

## Current Synthesis
[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-601-2-fenghuo-xi-zhuhou-yixiao-tianxia-wang-baosi-shi-hefang-yaonie-lkirbrvonyiyaousoblcd|Hanji 601-2]] provides a compact case through [[BaoSi|褒姒]]. The episode retells an elaborate supernatural chain from dragon saliva to a sealed box, black lizard, miraculous pregnancy, abandonment, and adoption, but then says the account is myth-like and "当不得真."

The concept's value is to keep two kinds of evidence apart: the legend matters because it shaped Bao Si's later image, but it does not by itself prove her origin, agency, or responsibility for Western Zhou's fall.

[[266-south-korea-the-riddle-of-hwang-jini-glt1910695360]] extends the rule from supernatural birth myth to a famous artist's romance-and-wit biography. Stories about [[HwangJini|Hwang Jini]] can show how later audiences imagined female beauty, literary skill, sexuality, and agency within the [[Gisaeng|gisaeng institution]], even when an immovable coffin, a journey with a patron, a monk encounter, a consent riddle, or the details of her death cannot be securely established.

## Key Claims
- Legendary biography can be historically important as evidence of reception, fear, and moral imagination.
- Supernatural origin stories need to be quarantined from factual profile claims unless corroborated by other evidence.
- A narrator's explicit caution is itself part of the source record and should travel with the claim.
- Mythic biography often pairs with blame narratives by making later events feel fated or ominous.
- Non-supernatural anecdotes can require the same caution when a famous life is reconstructed largely through romance, riddles, encounters, and later adaptation.

## Evidence

- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-601-2-fenghuo-xi-zhuhou-yixiao-tianxia-wang-baosi-shi-hefang-yaonie-lkirbrvonyiyaousoblcd]] spends most of the episode on the dragon-saliva and black-lizard story because it explains Bao Si's later uncanny image.

Boundary from factual profile:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-601-2-fenghuo-xi-zhuhou-yixiao-tianxia-wang-baosi-shi-hefang-yaonie-lkirbrvonyiyaousoblcd]] tells listeners that the preceding material should not be taken literally.

Narrator caution:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-601-2-fenghuo-xi-zhuhou-yixiao-tianxia-wang-baosi-shi-hefang-yaonie-lkirbrvonyiyaousoblcd]] closes by calling Bao Si complex, possibly innocent or guilty, and historically obscure.

Fated-blame pairing:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-601-2-fenghuo-xi-zhuhou-yixiao-tianxia-wang-baosi-shi-hefang-yaonie-lkirbrvonyiyaousoblcd]] links the supernatural biography to the later "妖姬" and "一笑亡国" reception frame.

Legendary artist biography:
- [[266-south-korea-the-riddle-of-hwang-jini-glt1910695360]] repeatedly acknowledges uncertainty while retelling stories that construct Hwang as beautiful, witty, sexually self-directed, and culturally exceptional.

Secure-core separation:
- [[266-south-korea-the-riddle-of-hwang-jini-glt1910695360]] more securely supports Hwang's literary reputation and modern cultural afterlife than the specific events of her legend-heavy life.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This is a handling rule rather than a claim that mythic materials are worthless. Both sources show that legends can carry cultural and political meaning even when they are not reliable as literal event reports. The two podcasts are popular retellings rather than independent specialist verification of the biographies they discuss.

## What Changed
- Extended the rule from supernatural origin stories to romance-and-encounter traditions surrounding a famous woman poet.
