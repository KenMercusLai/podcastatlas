# 乳房自我觉察边界 / Breast Self-Awareness Boundary

## Definition
Breast self-awareness is familiarity with one's ordinary breast appearance and feel so that a meaningful change can be reported; its boundary is that routine self-palpation is not a diagnostic test or a substitute for risk-appropriate screening and clinical assessment.

## Current Synthesis
[[vol-98-ruxianwaike-sheide-ruxian-bushi-ruxian-ne-ruxian-jibing-shi-bei-qi-chulai-de-ma-ruxian-zicha-zhinan-lginwlcqmjiaxozgtogn7sqlgqlt|VOL.98]] demonstrates visual observation and orderly palpation as a way to notice lumps, hard areas, shape or skin changes, nipple inversion, and unexpected discharge. Its most defensible synthesis is change awareness: know what is ordinary for the individual, notice a persistent or new difference, and report it instead of trying to settle the diagnosis by feel.

That framing narrows the episode's “self-check” language. For average-risk people, repetitive systematic self-examination has not been established as a mortality-reducing screening protocol and can produce false alarms or repetitive checking. A noticed change is a prompt for care, not proof of cancer; an unremarkable self-check is not proof that screening or assessment is unnecessary.

## Key Claims
- Familiarity with ordinary appearance and feel can help a person notice a new change.
- Visual change, a persistent lump, spontaneous nipple discharge, or recurrent skin and nipple abnormality should be reported clinically.
- Self-palpation cannot determine pathology or replace appropriate imaging and follow-up.
- Repeated pressing of a noticed abnormality can intensify anxiety without adding diagnostic certainty.
- A normal self-check does not cancel age-, risk-, symptom-, or clinician-directed care.

## Evidence
- Observation and palpation: [[vol-98-ruxianwaike-sheide-ruxian-bushi-ruxian-ne-ruxian-jibing-shi-bei-qi-chulai-de-ma-ruxian-zicha-zhinan-lginwlcqmjiaxozgtogn7sqlgqlt]] describes mirror observation and systematic touch in standing and lying positions.
- Change signals: [[vol-98-ruxianwaike-sheide-ruxian-bushi-ruxian-ne-ruxian-jibing-shi-bei-qi-chulai-de-ma-ruxian-zicha-zhinan-lginwlcqmjiaxozgtogn7sqlgqlt]] identifies persistent lumps, skin or shape changes, nipple changes, and spontaneous discharge as reasons for assessment.
- Manipulation boundary: [[vol-98-ruxianwaike-sheide-ruxian-bushi-ruxian-ne-ruxian-jibing-shi-bei-qi-chulai-de-ma-ruxian-zicha-zhinan-lginwlcqmjiaxozgtogn7sqlgqlt]] advises against repeatedly pressing a discovered lump and rejects massage as treatment.
- Professional-care boundary: [[vol-98-ruxianwaike-sheide-ruxian-bushi-ruxian-ne-ruxian-jibing-shi-bei-qi-chulai-de-ma-ruxian-zicha-zhinan-lginwlcqmjiaxozgtogn7sqlgqlt]] repeatedly routes uncertain or persistent change toward examination and imaging.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source presents a stepwise self-check, whereas contemporary guidance for average-risk people commonly emphasizes self-awareness rather than scheduled systematic breast self-examination. This page therefore preserves the episode's practical change-recognition content but does not claim that a monthly technique reduces mortality. People with symptoms or elevated risk need individualized clinical advice regardless of self-check findings.

## What Changed
- Created a change-awareness framework that explicitly separates self-observation from screening and diagnosis.
