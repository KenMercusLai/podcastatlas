# Fortune-Misfortune Reversal / 塞翁失马式福祸反转

Fortune-misfortune reversal / 塞翁失马式福祸反转 is the Hanji 913 frame for cases where an apparent loss removes someone from a worse exposure. The episode applies it to [[CaiYong|蔡邕]]: exile to Shuofang looks ruinous, but the source argues that staying near [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]] and the capital's eunuch faction might have been more dangerous.

The concept is intentionally bounded. It does not make suffering intrinsically good or advise passivity; it names a retrospective interpretation problem in which visible punishment can also create distance, time, or warning channels that were unavailable at the center.

## Key Claims
- Apparent misfortune can sometimes reduce exposure to a more dangerous center of power.
- The same event should be interpreted through position, timing, and surrounding incentives, not only immediate pain.
- The frame is retrospective and source-scoped; it should not be used to justify avoidable harm or moralize victims into acceptance.
- In Hanji 913, Cai Yong's survival depends on both distance from court and concrete people who choose not to kill him.
