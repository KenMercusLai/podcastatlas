# Mencian Idealism Limits / 孟子理想主义的限度

Mencian idealism limits is [[30-mengzi-zhanguo-fenqing-de-chizi-zhixin-592494138]]'s caution that [[Mengzi|孟子 / Mencius]]'s political force also has failure modes. The episode admires Mencius's courage, but it does not make him infallible: the Qi campaign against Yan shows how a belief in benevolent conquest can fail when army behavior, interstate balancing, and institutional safeguards do not match the moral premise.

This concept links Mencius to the wiki's broader [[GoodIntentionsPoliticalLimits]] and [[MachiavellianRealism]] branches. The source's point is not that renyi is useless; it is that moral confidence must be tested against incentives, coercive instruments, implementation capacity, and the question of who gets to define "the benevolent ruler."

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-49-mengzi-yue-wo-jiao-ni-zhiguo-ni-que-ba-wo-dang-huaping-lhy14pbf9thcfdiaulcmf8dwcef8]] strengthens the concept from inside the [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] sequence. Mencius gives [[QiXuanWang|齐宣王]] a coherent repair plan for [[YanState|燕国]], but the king does not accept the loss of immediate gain that repair requires. The limit is therefore not only Mencius's idealism; it is also the ruler's refusal to translate moral counsel into costly administrative action.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-99-4-shui-shi-kongzi-hou-zui-weidade-sixiangjia-lr4phoq9chc4tz0yb8y95gy21qdd]] extends the limit into war memory. The host cites Mencius's disbelief toward the "血流漂杵" description of [[ZhouWuwang|周武王]]'s conquest as a sign that Confucian moral imagination can resist acknowledging how violent even a righteous punitive war may be. This becomes a parallel to [[Xunzi|荀子]]'s "有诛而无战" ideal inside [[ConfucianMilitaryIdealismLimits|儒家军事理想的限度]].

## Key Claims
- Mencian politics can be morally bracing and institutionally under-specified at the same time.
- War justified as benevolent unification needs more than the ruler's intention.
- The failure in Yan exposes a gap between moral language and military administration.
- The source uses this limit to keep Mencius human rather than sanctified.
- Zhouji 49 adds that even a clear benevolent-policy prescription can fail when a ruler wants Mencius as moral cover rather than as a governing adviser.
- Qinji 99 part 4 adds that idealized punitive war can become selective memory about violence, not only a policy-implementation problem.
