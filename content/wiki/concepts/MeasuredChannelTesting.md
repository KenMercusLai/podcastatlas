# Measured Channel Testing

Measured channel testing is the practice of choosing a small number of plausible marketing channels, running bounded experiments, and increasing spend only when customer behavior can be traced well enough to justify the next step. In [[advice-line-with-curt-richardson-of-otterbox-e94bf774-de11-4434-843b-a551309a6773]], [[Everloop]] faces this problem because baby-registry purchases can lag discovery by three to six months.

[[CurtRichardson]] advises [[VinceGiudice]] to research and test four or five channels instead of spreading money across ten or more, and to avoid spending where the company cannot measure the result. [[GuyRaz]] adds that customer calls can reveal why buyers chose [[Everloop]] and whether the 20% buyback promise is the real hook. The concept overlaps with [[ChannelFocusExperiments]], but it emphasizes measurement and attribution before scaling spend.

[[advice-line-with-chris-riccobono-of-untuckit-c087247f-1f85-4c15-b4aa-4ffea0368c4c]] adds [[SnugSafety]] and [[AeroShorts]] as two more consumer cases. [[ChrisRiccobono]] recommends testing radio or audio reads separately for Snug before scaling spend, while AeroShorts is advised to direct money toward product and focused marketing channels instead of assuming paid social will work like it did for older DTC brands.

[[advice-line-with-bobbi-brown-of-jones-road-beauty-september-2025-427590c2-11bc-43fa-9c92-d1eccf05842a]] adds three early consumer-product tests. [[CabbageLabs]] is advised to replace full-size giveaways with smaller [[TrialSizeProduct]] samples and targeted PR; [[AbbyRose]] is advised to test new live-selling faces and community formats before committing to wholesale or paid ads; and [[TannenOralCare]] is advised to test broader oral-care positioning through one dentist office, gym, or wellness community before scaling the repositioning.

## Key Claims
- Channel tests should be small enough that failed learning does not threaten the company.
- A founder should account for channel vendors' incentives because agencies often recommend the channel they sell best.
- Attribution lag can make good channels look weak and weak channels look good unless the company talks directly to customers.
- Third-party credibility can matter when customers trust outside voices more than company advertising.
- Measured channel testing should connect spend to customer interviews, purchases, registry behavior, repeat visits, or other observable actions.
- Channel tests should match the buyer's trust behavior; a senior-safety product, youth apparel product, and hockey visor may require different evidence carriers even when all are "marketing" problems.
- A founder-dependent channel should be tested for transferability before the company assumes it can scale beyond the founder.
