# 永明时代 / Yongming Era

## Definition

The Yongming era is the reign period of [[XiaoZeSouthernQi|Xiao Ze]] in [[SouthernQi|Southern Qi]], treated in the episode as a short interval of relative political stability, central state reconstruction, aristocratic cultural production, and prosodic innovation.

## Current Synthesis

Yongming's significance comes from the interaction of government and culture rather than either domain alone. Fiscal accumulation, registration inspection, relief, and tighter control over princes and regional commands strengthened the center after Liu Song disorder, but policy targets and intermediary rent-seeking shifted costs onto households and contributed to rebellion. Relative stability therefore describes comparison with surrounding upheaval, not an absence of coercion or conflict.

In literature, writers around Xiao Ziliang and other patrons developed tonal analysis, shared formal rules, and networks later associated with Liang leaders such as Xiao Yan, Shen Yue, and Fan Yun. The era functions as a bridge: politically it attempted reconstruction; culturally it transformed inherited Wei-Jin practices into personnel and forms that continued after the Jingling circle broke apart.

## Key Claims

- Yongming joined central state rebuilding to a relatively stable political interval.
- Fiscal capacity and household control grew together, producing both administrative leverage and distributional harm.
- Court and princely networks made literary production inseparable from succession and patronage politics.
- Tonal formalization was one part of a wider culture of rule, rank, and patterned expression.
- The period's personnel and techniques continued into Liang even after its political coalition dissolved.

## Evidence

- Reconstruction and stability - [[lvyohbnakaa_iop9nirokkjmhqs-lvyohbnakaa_iop9nirokkjmhqs|Episode 513]] describes stronger treasuries, reduced major regional revolt, and tighter central authority.
- Social cost - [[lvyohbnakaa_iop9nirokkjmhqs-lvyohbnakaa_iop9nirokkjmhqs|Episode 513]] connects registration quotas, official extraction, privilege loss, and Tang Yuzhi's rebellion.
- Literary networks - [[lvyohbnakaa_iop9nirokkjmhqs-lvyohbnakaa_iop9nirokkjmhqs|Episode 513]] places Wang Rong, Xie Tiao, Shen Yue, Fan Yun, Xiao Ziliang, and Xiao Yan in the era's cultural field.
- Long afterlife - [[lvyohbnakaa_iop9nirokkjmhqs-lvyohbnakaa_iop9nirokkjmhqs|Episode 513]] treats Liang culture and regulated verse as later developments with formative Yongming roots.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

“Stable,” “joyful,” and “cultural mother” are comparative or metaphorical judgments. The episode also records rebellion, fiscal pressure, corruption, court conflict, and executions. It does not isolate Yongming from longer phonological, literary, Buddhist, and institutional developments, and it does not prove that all Liang cultural strength originated in this reign.

## What Changed

- Established Yongming as a joined state-reconstruction and literary-transition concept.
- Made relative stability compatible with coercion, corruption, and rebellion.
- Distinguished continuity into Liang from survival of one unified literary faction.
