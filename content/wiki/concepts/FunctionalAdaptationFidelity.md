## Definition

Functional adaptation fidelity is fidelity to a source work's character logic, narrative effects, growth, and thematic function rather than to literal event order, dialogue, or exhaustive plot coverage.

## Current Synthesis

The bounded [[LegendOfTheCondorHeroes|《射雕英雄传》]] discussion treats adaptation as reconstruction under a new medium's constraints. Novels can explain motive through narration and interiority, while screen stories must make motive perceptible through speech, acting, staging, editing, and action. A changed scene can therefore be more functionally faithful than a literal one if it lets viewers understand the same concealed knowledge, relationship development, or decision logic.

This standard does not license arbitrary rewriting. Changes still have to produce coherent conflict and resolution, preserve the character tensions that drive action, and sustain the work's larger arc. The episode's criticism of “魔改” is thus directed less at deviation itself than at deviations that flatten motives, lose causal support, or replace earned wuxia development with generic fantasy spectacle.

## Key Claims

- Literal similarity and functional fidelity are separate judgments.
- A successful change preserves or rebuilds character causality in a form viewers can perceive.
- Compression requires selection, but selection should retain the relationships and turning points that make growth intelligible.
- Casting resemblance cannot substitute for performance of a character's internal contrasts.
- Screen conflict must be set up and resolved as drama rather than relying on the novel's explanatory authority.
- Formal invention is justified by achieved function, not by novelty alone.
- Thematic fidelity must be earned through the adapted plot rather than asserted as a slogan.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept is derived from one informal, multi-host comparison whose judgments vary by performer, version, nostalgia, and personal taste. “Character core” can itself become a vague defense of preferred adaptations unless tied to observable setup, motive, choice, and consequence. Functional success also does not erase the value of language, pacing, structure, or cultural texture that may be lost even when plot purpose survives.

## What Changed

- Established a distinction between literal reproduction and preservation of narrative function.
- Made character causality, perceivable motive, and earned thematic development the main fidelity tests.
- Added a boundary against treating any formally inventive rewrite as automatically faithful.
