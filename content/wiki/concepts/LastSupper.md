# Last Supper

## Definition

The Last Supper is the final meal of [[Jesus]] and his disciples before his arrest, treated in the source as a Gospel narrative, an early Christian tradition, and the ritual foundation of [[Eucharist|the Eucharist]].

## Current Synthesis

The meal concentrates several meanings without making them identical: humble service through foot washing, embodied remembrance through bread and wine, foreknowledge through the prediction of betrayal and denial, and communal ethics through the command to love. Its dramatic force comes from knowing that the community will fracture almost immediately: [[JudasIscariot|Judas]] betrays, [[ApostlePeter|Peter]] denies, and Jesus himself later prays to avoid suffering. The source reads that vulnerability as a source of human and literary power while withholding a judgment on supernatural claims or the historicity of every scene.

## Key Claims

- The meal is situated by the source in a volatile Passover and Jerusalem political context.
- Foot washing frames leadership through service and relational connection.
- Bread and wine turn impending death into repeated ritual remembrance.
- Betrayal and denial make communal failure part of, rather than external to, the narrative.
- The command to love supplies the episode's primary ethical bridge from the meal to later life.

## Evidence

- Political and narrative setting: [[24-dongxifang-de-fanju-zuihou-de-wancan-yu-lanting-yaji-lraekm-bgowhg1wxysfshcwoz5xp]] places the meal between temple conflict, Passover crowds, arrest, and crucifixion.
- Early attestation: [[288-jesus-christ-the-history-part-2-glt4776258433]] notes that Paul already transmits the Last Supper tradition before the written Gospels.
- Service and ritual: [[24-dongxifang-de-fanju-zuihou-de-wancan-yu-lanting-yaji-lraekm-bgowhg1wxysfshcwoz5xp]] joins foot washing, bread, wine, remembrance, and the command to love.
- Human vulnerability: [[24-dongxifang-de-fanju-zuihou-de-wancan-yu-lanting-yaji-lraekm-bgowhg1wxysfshcwoz5xp]] connects Judas's betrayal, Peter's denial, and Gethsemane to fear, agency, and repentance.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The page is bounded to two podcast treatments rather than a full historical or theological survey. Gospel variation, historical confidence in individual scenes and sayings, denominational doctrine, and the supernatural meaning of the Passion require separate treatment. Early Pauline attestation supports an early tradition but does not authenticate every later narrative detail.

## What Changed

- Created a synthesis separating ritual, ethics, literary vulnerability, and historical confidence.
