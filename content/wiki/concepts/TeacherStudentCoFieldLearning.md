# Teacher-Student Co-Field Learning / 师生共同在场学习

## Definition
Teacher-student co-field learning / 师生共同在场学习 is EP280's pattern in which teachers and students enter the same uncertain field situation and learn from it together. The teacher still guides, but authority comes partly from experience, attention, and accompaniment rather than from delivering fixed knowledge.

## Current Synthesis
In the Guangzhou art-school reform, teachers cannot fully script the field. They prepare students with methods, simulations, interview language, and discussion, but they also face moments where they do not know what to do. That shared uncertainty changes the relationship: students see teachers as people with judgment and limits, while teachers learn more about students' fears, family stories, work histories, and blocked capacities. The result resembles a modernized apprenticeship without becoming a closed master-disciple hierarchy.

## Key Claims
- Shared field presence can soften classroom hierarchy because the teacher is no longer only the person who knows the answer.
- Teacher guidance remains necessary because students need scaffolding for approaching strangers, taking notes, and revising assumptions.
- Long post-field conversations can reveal student experiences that ordinary classroom interaction misses.
- Teacher authority shifts toward modeled judgment, accompaniment, and interpretation under uncertainty.
- The pattern becomes more important when information access is cheap but experience, attention, and trust remain scarce.

## Evidence
- Shared uncertainty: [[ep280-guangzhou-meiyuan-fangwenji-dang-meishusheng-kaishi-zuo-tianye-diaocha-lr4-jmpdo-oo7w8sdmcgcw-chqc9]] says teachers also encounter moments of not knowing what to do when they enter the field with students.
- Scaffolding: [[ep280-guangzhou-meiyuan-fangwenji-dang-meishusheng-kaishi-zuo-tianye-diaocha-lr4-jmpdo-oo7w8sdmcgcw-chqc9]] describes teachers designing simulations, opening scripts, specific wording, and Cantonese help for students afraid to talk to strangers.
- Hidden student experience: [[ep280-guangzhou-meiyuan-fangwenji-dang-meishusheng-kaishi-zuo-tianye-diaocha-lr4-jmpdo-oo7w8sdmcgcw-chqc9]] recounts long conversations where a quiet student disclosed pre-university factory work and later folded that experience into coursework.
- Authority shift: [[ep280-guangzhou-meiyuan-fangwenji-dang-meishusheng-kaishi-zuo-tianye-diaocha-lr4-jmpdo-oo7w8sdmcgcw-chqc9]] cites Xiang Biao's description of a relation similar to but different from apprenticeship, where teacher and student learn in the same scene.
- Information-age relevance: [[ep280-guangzhou-meiyuan-fangwenji-dang-meishusheng-kaishi-zuo-tianye-diaocha-lr4-jmpdo-oo7w8sdmcgcw-chqc9]] connects teacher value to experience sharing, accompaniment, and facing real situations after knowledge itself becomes easier to access.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Co-field learning does not make teacher authority disappear, and it does not solve every institutional pressure. The source keeps professional coursework, grading, and employment constraints outside what shared field presence alone can fix.
