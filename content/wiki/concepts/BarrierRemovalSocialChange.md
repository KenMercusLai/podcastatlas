# Barrier-Removal Social Change

Barrier-removal social change is the philanthropic and policy frame [[CharlesKoch|Charles Koch]] and [[ChaseKoch|Chase Koch]] describe in [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-charles-chase-koch-on-how-they-quietly-built-a-150b-empire-41270895]]. The core claim is that people flourish when institutions remove barriers that prevent them from discovering gifts, building capability, and helping others succeed.

The source connects this frame to [[StandTogether|Stand Together]], education reform, addiction recovery, occupational licensing, immigration, tariffs, and AI access. Chase says Stand Together tries to change paradigms by showing results rather than telling people what to believe, and that people closest to problems often have the best solutions.

The concept overlaps with [[HumanFlourishingProfit]] and [[UsefulWorkCareerCompounding]], but it is more policy-and-institution focused. Its main test is whether social programs, schools, markets, and rules expand people's agency and contribution rather than trapping them in credential, regulatory, or dependency systems.

## Key Claims
- Social change should look for barriers that block agency, learning, contribution, and economic mobility.
- People closest to a problem may have better solution knowledge than distant institutions.
- Demonstrated results can change minds more effectively than ideological persuasion.
- Education reform is central because the source argues that many people never discover their gifts inside conventional schooling.
- The source's capitalism defense depends on reducing barriers and cronyism rather than defending every existing market outcome.
