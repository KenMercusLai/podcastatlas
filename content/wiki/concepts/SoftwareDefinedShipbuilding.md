# Software-Defined Shipbuilding

Software-defined shipbuilding is the pattern of designing ships, components, supply chains, and yards around software-controlled subsystems rather than treating software as a late add-on. [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-saronic-founders-autonomous-warships-chinas-230x-advantage-swarms-of-robot-ships-42334230]] adds the concept through [[VibhAltakar]]'s description of [[Saronic]] digitizing historically analog vessel components.

The concept matters because autonomous vessels need more than an autopilot. The source describes engine-room tasks, electronics, sensors, compute, component APIs, remote access, and selective in-house hardware as part of one architecture. [[PortAlpha|Port Alpha]] extends that logic from the vessel into the yard: Saronic wants to co-design the ship for the shipyard and the shipyard for the ship.

## Key Claims
- Components become easier to control, monitor, and update when they expose software APIs.
- Vertical integration can be useful when commercial off-the-shelf parts do not meet autonomy, sensing, compute, or defense needs.
- Software-defined shipbuilding connects design, production, maintenance, and fleet operations rather than isolating each step.
- The source presents ship cost reduction as a product-process-yard problem, not only a purchasing problem.
