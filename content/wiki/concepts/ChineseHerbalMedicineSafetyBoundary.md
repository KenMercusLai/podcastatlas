# Chinese Herbal Medicine Safety Boundary / 中药安全边界

## Definition
Chinese herbal medicine safety boundary rejects naturalness, culinary familiarity, tradition, product naming, or dosage form as sufficient evidence that an herb, formula, proprietary product, or food therapy is safe and suitable.

## Current Synthesis
The source correctly makes safety relational rather than categorical: substance identity, dose, preparation, duration, patient age and physiology, pregnancy, disease, allergy, current medicines, product composition, and monitoring all matter. A familiar food can still be unsuitable in a given context, while an herb can have both intended and harmful effects. “Medicine-food homology” therefore does not create a standing recommendation.

Product identity also matters. A Chinese-sounding name, pill, syrup, granule, or traditional ingredient does not show that a product contains only herbal ingredients. Convenient granules or concentrates can change preparation and use, but this source does not establish product equivalence, bioavailability, interaction behavior, or comparative effect. Pregnancy, childhood, hypertension, organ disease, acute illness, and adverse reactions require qualified review rather than a general “can use” or “cannot use” rule.

## Key Claims
- “Natural,” “traditional,” “food-derived,” and “used before” do not prove safety or personal suitability.
- Substance, dose, preparation, duration, patient context, interactions, and monitoring jointly determine risk.
- Pregnancy and childhood change the decision but do not support blanket permission or blanket prohibition from this source.
- Product names and formats do not reliably reveal whether a medicine is purely herbal or contains conventional active ingredients.
- Food therapy should change with actual intake, tolerance, symptoms, and health state rather than become a permanent universal regimen.
- Granules, decoctions, concentrates, and proprietary products should not be assumed clinically interchangeable without product-specific evidence.
- Acute or worsening illness and suspected adverse effects require escalation rather than additional self-prescribing.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source offers no ingredient certificates, contaminant testing, pharmacokinetic data, dose tables, interaction review, pregnancy or pediatric safety dataset, comparative dosage-form study, or adverse-event surveillance. Its examples do not authorize use of ginseng, ephedra, asarum, aconite, patent remedies, bloodletting, or any named formula. Current product labeling, regulation, pharmacist or clinician review, and urgent-care guidance take priority.

## What Changed
- Established a unified safety boundary for herbs, proprietary medicines, food therapy, life-stage use, and dosage forms.
- Made product-name ambiguity and non-equivalence between preparations explicit.
