# Paramilitary Spoiler Violence

Paramilitary spoiler violence is the pattern where armed groups use attacks to prevent or destroy a political settlement. In [[697-the-troubles-murder-in-belfast-part-1-glt4877520736]], the early source case is the 1966 loyalist violence tied to [[GustySpence]] and the [[UlsterVolunteerForce|UVF]], including attacks that killed [[MatildaGould]], [[JohnPatrickScullion]], and [[PeterWard]].

In [[698-the-troubles-the-brink-of-civil-war-part-2-glt2308408988]], the key source case becomes the 1969 loyalist bombing campaign against infrastructure, publicly blamed on the [[IrishRepublicanArmy|IRA]] but attributed by the episode to the UVF and Paisley-linked [[UlsterProtestantVolunteers]]. The episode says Gusty Spence later described the plan as a way to expose [[TerenceONeill|Terence O'Neill]]'s weakness and bring him down. The target was therefore not only infrastructure but moderate unionist authority and the possibility of reform.

## Key Claims
- Spoiler violence can work by changing how responsibility is perceived, not only by causing physical damage.
- False attribution can turn violence into political evidence against a moderate government.
- Armed actors can attack their own side's institutional leadership when moderation threatens their strategy.
- Preemptive violence can begin before a full armed campaign when a paramilitary treats imagined enemies as already legitimate targets.
