# Urban-Edge Nature / 城市边缘自然

Urban-edge nature is the mixed habitat pattern added by [[85-chedundun-yeshiji-zou-a-bandao-xiangxia-zuan-xiaoshuliner-qu-700935571]] through [[YinQiCheDundun|尹琪]]'s life around [[ChedunTown|车墩镇]]. It names nature at the border of city, suburb, village, transport, field, and unmanaged woods: not deep wilderness, but not sealed urban interior either.

The source makes the edge visible through mismatched details. Bats rest near balcony pipes, swallows nest above storefronts, zhang/deerlike animals may pass from a release base, pheasants hide in fields, insects are displaced by landscaping or bikes, raccoon dogs use building gaps and garbage, and fireflies depend on whether community lighting stays dark enough. At the same time, the small woods hold trash, discarded objects, informal sex, trapping, toilets, and safety risks.

## Key Claims
- Urban-edge nature is ecological and social at once: animals, residents, shops, property management, traffic, lighting, waste, and informal behavior all shape the same place.
- The category resists both pastoral fantasy and city-nature denial; the habitat is real precisely because it is messy.
- Repeated local observation can reveal more ecological texture than occasional scenic travel.
- The observer's body and identity matter: fear of dogs, gendered safety planning, and willingness to ask neighbors all decide what can be seen.
- Urban-edge nature can lead back to people. The source's movement from watching animals to "looking at people again" makes coexistence a judgment practice, not only a species list.
