# 破釜沉舟 / Breaking Cauldrons and Sinking Boats

破釜沉舟 / breaking cauldrons and sinking boats enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-134-ni-buzhidao-de-pofu-chenzhou-banben-ltnry1fva2hzplw4ft8nrfpoiolw|Qinji 134]] as [[XiangYu|项羽]]'s battlefield commitment device during the [[JuluBattle|巨鹿之战]]. The episode treats it as more precise than a generic motivational slogan: it comes after Chu has already attacked Qin's supply corridor and just before the decisive close-range fight against [[WangLiQin|王离]].

In the source's sequence, Xiang Yu crosses the [[ZhangRiver|漳水]] near [[Julu|巨鹿]], then orders the boats sunk, the cooking vessels destroyed, and the camp burned. Each soldier carries only three days of food. The point is to make retreat, delay, and ordinary camp life unavailable, forcing the army to experience victory as the only viable path.

The concept extends [[MilitaryMoraleThroughSharedHardship|同甘共苦式士气]] but is harsher than ordinary shared hardship. It does not only show that a commander shares risk with troops; it deliberately removes fallback options from the whole force. In the episode, that extremity works because [[YingBu|英布]] and [[PuGeneral|蒲将军]] have already weakened Qin's logistics through [[TransportCorridorSeverance|交通线切割]].

The source also corrects the common simplified geography. The crossing tied to the idiom is the Zhang River crossing near Julu, not the earlier Yellow River crossing from Pingyuanjin. This keeps the idiom attached to plausible marching distance and immediate battle tempo.

## Key Claims
- The idiom is a death-ground morale device, not a substitute for operational preparation.
- Removing retreat can intensify commitment, but it is only coherent when the commander has already improved the battlefield conditions.
- Three days of rations make sense in the episode because the decisive crossing is near Julu.
- The action converts Xiang Yu's personal command authority into army-wide irreversible commitment.
- The idiom's later inspirational meaning should not erase the supply-corridor and geography details in the source story.
