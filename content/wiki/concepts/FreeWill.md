# Free Will / 自由意志

## Definition
Free will is the wiki's concept for meaningful agency and responsibility under causal, biological, social, experiential, and temporal constraint.

## Current Synthesis
[[ep256-ai-shidai-ziyou-yizhi-hai-cunzai-ma-lk9aci8oqnwerk26jy683nbdddcx]] traces pressure on intuitive self-sovereignty through [[CausalDeterminism]], religion, mechanics, evolution, neuroscience, unequal environments, biology, and AI. [[scim5791514102-scim5791514102]] supplies Sapolsky's categorical version: behavior has no remainder outside recent context, hormones, learning, development, culture, genes, evolution, and brain construction. Yet both sources resist fatalism. Living systems change when circumstances, information, and learning become new causal inputs, so denial of an uncaused author does not imply behavioral immobility.

The fixed-time branch uses [[StoryOfYourLife|《你一生的故事》]] to ask whether love, parenthood, and experience can still be chosen when loss is already known. The first-person branch challenges authorship through [[SamHarris]]'s argument that candidate thoughts and decisions simply appear in consciousness, yet preserves distinctions among voluntary action, accident, negligence, intention, and coercion. The current synthesis therefore rejects both an uncaused inner author and the inference that deliberation, change, or accountability is meaningless.

## Key Claims
- Free will becomes trivial if it is reduced to random button pressing; the harder problem is meaningful action under causal pressure.
- Ancient atomism, causal chains, religious omnipotence, Newtonian mechanics, [[LaplacesDemon|Laplace's demon]], evolution, and neuroscience each narrow intuitive self-sovereignty in different ways.
- Biological life supplies a form of agency that cannot be captured by the image of passive matter alone.
- Thoughts and choices may arise without a separately observable author, while deliberation and differences among intention, accident, negligence, and coercion remain practically consequential.
- Knowledge and circumstance can change behavior without becoming exceptions to causality; they operate as additional causes.
- Foreknowledge or determinism does not automatically empty chosen love, commitment, or experience of meaning.
- Less absolute freedom does not imply no responsibility; it changes how responsibility, punishment, prevention, education, and social support should be designed.

## Evidence
- Causal and social constraints: [[ep256-ai-shidai-ziyou-yizhi-hai-cunzai-ma-lk9aci8oqnwerk26jy683nbdddcx]] maps determinist pressure and argues that unequal starting conditions should inform responsibility and policy.
- Biological agency: [[ep256-ai-shidai-ziyou-yizhi-hai-cunzai-ma-lk9aci8oqnwerk26jy683nbdddcx]] distinguishes living goal-directed action from passive matter through [[BiologicalAgency]] and [[MeaningAsEvolvedFunction]].
- Predestined choice: [[104-ni-yisheng-de-gushi-zhongdian-yijing-zhuding-haiyao-chufa-ma-739384680]] asks whether known loss invalidates a decision to love, parent, and experience life.
- Arising decisions: [[using-meditation-to-focus-view-consciousness-expand-your-mind-dr-sam-harris-scim3261454307]] uses spontaneous city naming, memory retrieval, and drink choice to challenge the felt authorship of thoughts.
- Preserved distinctions: [[using-meditation-to-focus-view-consciousness-expand-your-mind-dr-sam-harris-scim3261454307]] explicitly retains practical differences among deliberate conduct, accident, negligence, harmful intent, and involuntary action.
- Causal change without fatalism: [[scim5791514102-scim5791514102]] argues that learning, neuroplasticity, inspiring or discouraging information, and altered circumstance can change an organism without requiring free will.
- AI boundary: [[ep256-ai-shidai-ziyou-yizhi-hai-cunzai-ma-lk9aci8oqnwerk26jy683nbdddcx]] treats future systems with their own goals, meaning, embodiment, and action channels as a different governance problem from present language prediction.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not establish a single scientific resolution. The literary source is a philosophical thought experiment; the Harris source argues from introspection; the Sapolsky interview states a strong mechanistic position rather than demonstrating that no alternative account can survive; and EP256 treats button-press experiments as too narrow for morally significant choice. Biological goal-directedness does not by itself prove metaphysical freedom, while denial of a separate author does not settle the correct legal or ethical allocation of responsibility. AI free-will risk remains conditional and speculative rather than a claim about present chatbots.

## What Changed
- Added Sapolsky's explicit causal account and clarified why change through knowledge or circumstance is compatible with determinism rather than evidence of an uncaused will.
- Strengthened the distinction between denying authorship and endorsing fatalism.
