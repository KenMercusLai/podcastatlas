# Free Will / 自由意志

Free will is the wiki's concept for meaningful agency under constraint. [[ep256-ai-shidai-ziyou-yizhi-hai-cunzai-ma-lk9aci8oqnwerk26jy683nbdddcx]] gives the first direct explainer: [[TuMotuo|土摩托]] traces the problem through ancient [[CausalDeterminism|determinism]], religion, modern science, neuroscience, biology, and AI. The episode does not settle on a simple yes/no answer. It argues that the valuable question is not whether a person can make causeless random movements, but whether meaningful choice, moral responsibility, social policy, and life-directed action remain intelligible.

[[104-ni-yisheng-de-gushi-zhongdian-yijing-zhuding-haiyao-chufa-ma-739384680]] adds the literary fixed-time version through [[StoryOfYourLife|《你一生的故事》]]. There the problem is not causal determinism from brain or society, but whether a person can choose love and parenthood when the future is already known. That branch becomes [[PredestinedChoice]].

The EP256 contribution is to pull free will toward biology. Living beings are not stones; they initiate action, pursue food, avoid harm, and sustain themselves. [[BiologicalAgency]] and [[MeaningAsEvolvedFunction]] therefore become the source's middle path between pure mechanism and mystical will.

## Key Claims
- Free will becomes trivial if it is reduced to random button pressing; the harder problem is meaningful action under causal pressure.
- Ancient atomism, causal chains, religious omnipotence, Newtonian mechanics, [[LaplacesDemon|Laplace's demon]], evolution, and neuroscience each narrow intuitive self-sovereignty in different ways.
- Less free will does not imply no responsibility. It changes how responsibility, punishment, prevention, education, and social support should be designed.
- Biological life supplies a form of agency that cannot be captured by the image of passive matter alone.
- AI makes the concept practical again because a system with goals, meaning, and action freedom would create different risks from a tool that only predicts language.
