# Free Will / 自由意志

## Definition
Free will is the wiki's concept for meaningful agency and responsibility under causal, biological, social, experiential, and temporal constraint.

## Current Synthesis
[[ep256-ai-shidai-ziyou-yizhi-hai-cunzai-ma-lk9aci8oqnwerk26jy683nbdddcx]] traces pressure on intuitive self-sovereignty through [[CausalDeterminism]], religion, mechanics, evolution, neuroscience, unequal environments, biology, and AI. Its middle path is that random button pressing cannot settle meaningful agency: living systems initiate goal-directed action, interpret meaning, and remain embedded in bodies and social conditions. Less absolute freedom should change how responsibility, punishment, prevention, education, and support are designed rather than erase those institutions.

The fixed-time branch uses [[StoryOfYourLife|《你一生的故事》]] to ask whether love, parenthood, and experience can still be chosen when loss is already known. The first-person branch challenges authorship through [[SamHarris]]'s argument that candidate thoughts and decisions simply appear in consciousness, yet preserves distinctions among voluntary action, accident, negligence, intention, and coercion. The current synthesis therefore rejects both an uncaused inner author and the inference that deliberation or accountability is meaningless.

## Key Claims
- Free will becomes trivial if it is reduced to random button pressing; the harder problem is meaningful action under causal pressure.
- Ancient atomism, causal chains, religious omnipotence, Newtonian mechanics, [[LaplacesDemon|Laplace's demon]], evolution, and neuroscience each narrow intuitive self-sovereignty in different ways.
- Biological life supplies a form of agency that cannot be captured by the image of passive matter alone.
- Thoughts and choices may arise without a separately observable author, while deliberation and differences among intention, accident, negligence, and coercion remain practically consequential.
- Foreknowledge or determinism does not automatically empty chosen love, commitment, or experience of meaning.
- Less absolute freedom does not imply no responsibility; it changes how responsibility, punishment, prevention, education, and social support should be designed.
- AI makes the concept practical again because a system with goals, meaning, and action freedom would create different risks from a tool that only predicts language.

## Evidence
- Causal and social constraints: [[ep256-ai-shidai-ziyou-yizhi-hai-cunzai-ma-lk9aci8oqnwerk26jy683nbdddcx]] maps determinist pressure and argues that unequal starting conditions should inform responsibility and policy.
- Biological agency: [[ep256-ai-shidai-ziyou-yizhi-hai-cunzai-ma-lk9aci8oqnwerk26jy683nbdddcx]] distinguishes living goal-directed action from passive matter through [[BiologicalAgency]] and [[MeaningAsEvolvedFunction]].
- Predestined choice: [[104-ni-yisheng-de-gushi-zhongdian-yijing-zhuding-haiyao-chufa-ma-739384680]] asks whether known loss invalidates a decision to love, parent, and experience life.
- Arising decisions: [[using-meditation-to-focus-view-consciousness-expand-your-mind-dr-sam-harris-scim3261454307]] uses spontaneous city naming, memory retrieval, and drink choice to challenge the felt authorship of thoughts.
- Preserved distinctions: [[using-meditation-to-focus-view-consciousness-expand-your-mind-dr-sam-harris-scim3261454307]] explicitly retains practical differences among deliberate conduct, accident, negligence, harmful intent, and involuntary action.
- AI boundary: [[ep256-ai-shidai-ziyou-yizhi-hai-cunzai-ma-lk9aci8oqnwerk26jy683nbdddcx]] treats future systems with their own goals, meaning, embodiment, and action channels as a different governance problem from present language prediction.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not establish a single scientific resolution. The literary source is a philosophical thought experiment; the Harris source argues from introspection; and the EP256 source treats button-press experiments as too narrow for morally significant choice. Biological goal-directedness does not by itself prove metaphysical freedom, while denial of a separate author does not settle the correct legal or ethical allocation of responsibility. AI free-will risk remains conditional and speculative rather than a claim about present chatbots.
