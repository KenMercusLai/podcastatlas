# Negotiation Reality Testing

## Definition
Negotiation reality testing is the disciplined verification of whether a deal exists, claimed leverage is real, identity and trust are credible, threats are specific, and promised implementation is plausible before urgency drives commitment.

## Current Synthesis
[[how-to-succeed-at-hard-conversations-chris-voss-scim5142864264]] places verification before agreement. [[ChrisVoss]] recommends distinguishing a good deal, bad deal, and no deal; checking whether a scammer controls the account, information, money, or person they claim to control; and asking questions that force an implementation picture. Urgency is treated as a reason to verify faster and more carefully, not as evidence that the demand is true.

## Key Claims
- Early discovery of no deal or a bad deal can be a successful outcome.
- Claimed leverage should be demonstrated rather than inferred from urgency or fear.
- Introducing a false shared detail can test whether a suspicious sender has the identity or history they claim.
- Threat specificity across who, what, when, and where changes risk assessment.
- Words, tone, expression, behavior, and implementation detail should be assessed for alignment rather than reduced to one universal deception cue.
- A gut signal can motivate testing, but it is not proof by itself.

## Evidence
- Deal diagnosis - [[how-to-succeed-at-hard-conversations-chris-voss-scim5142864264]] prioritizes determining whether there is a viable deal and whether the counterpart is trustworthy.
- Scam verification - [[how-to-succeed-at-hard-conversations-chris-voss-scim5142864264]] describes planting false shared information in a suspicious text exchange to test identity.
- Threat assessment - [[how-to-succeed-at-hard-conversations-chris-voss-scim5142864264]] contrasts a vague threat with a specific next-day death threat in kidnapping work.
- Implementation testing - [[how-to-succeed-at-hard-conversations-chris-voss-scim5142864264]] asks a promising party to explain how follow-through will occur.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Behavioral alignment and intuition are fallible, culturally variable, and vulnerable to bias. The source offers practitioner heuristics rather than validated lie detection. High-risk scams, extortion, kidnapping, stalking, abuse, and credible threats require appropriate authorities, platform controls, legal advice, or emergency services rather than solo experimentation.

## What Changed
- Created a verification framework linking deal diagnosis, leverage proof, threat specificity, and implementation credibility.
