# Clear Customer Definition

Clear customer definition is the product discipline of knowing who the product is for, what problem they have, and why the first version matters to them. In [[we-almost-had-a-smartphone-in-the-90s-why-did-it-fail]], [[DavidEpstein]] and [[TonyFadell]] use [[GeneralMagic]] as the negative case: the company imagined a broad "Joe Sixpack" user without a precise need strong enough to force tradeoffs.

The contrast case is [[IPod]]. The source says [[Apple]] focused on a concrete customer desire around carrying digital music everywhere, which made feature choice, component borrowing, and launch sequencing easier than General Magic's all-purpose mobile future.

## Key Claims
- A vague target customer cannot reliably tell a team what not to build.
- Customer clarity turns technical possibility into priority order.
- Lack of customer definition makes [[FeatureCreep]] and [[PrematureEcosystemBuild]] more likely.
- Customer clarity is related to [[CustomerEvidenceStrategy]] and [[CustomerPull]], but the source focuses on product framing rather than sales evidence.
