## Definition

Organized institutional science is the production of cumulative knowledge through specialized roles, shared facilities, coordinated experiments, durable records, funding, and research agendas rather than through isolated individual discovery alone.

## Current Synthesis

[[SalomonsHouse|Salomon's House]] makes the organizational layer explicit. Knowledge collectors, craft observers, readers, experiment designers, operators, analysts, theorists, and inventors perform different tasks inside a continuing institution. Caves, towers, farms, lakes, pharmacies, health rooms, and optical and acoustic laboratories let inquiry exceed the time, equipment, and cognitive limits of a single investigator.

This arrangement expands discovery but also creates governance questions. Resource allocation shapes what can be known, specialization makes outsiders dependent on internal translation, and institutional continuity permits both cumulative learning and durable secrecy. Modern drugs, chips, particle accelerators, and large AI models strengthen the analogy at the level of scale and coordination, not as proof that all research institutions share one political form.

## Key Claims

- Large scientific advances can depend on coordinated specialization rather than solitary genius.
- Facilities, records, funding, and organizational continuity are part of knowledge production, not merely administrative support.
- Research agendas allocate scarce attention and thereby influence which questions become answerable.
- Specialization improves depth while increasing dependence on translators, managers, and institutional judgment.
- Cumulative collective capacity can become a public good and a source of concentrated power at the same time.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept does not deny individual creativity or imply that bigger organizations are always more innovative. Bureaucracy, agenda lock-in, funding concentration, secrecy, and disciplinary silos can impede discovery. Salomon's House is a fictional design, and the episode does not establish a direct causal line from Bacon to every modern research organization.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Bacon's division-of-labor model for cumulative experimental inquiry.
- Added facilities, agenda setting, records, and institutional continuity as constitutive parts of scientific capacity.
