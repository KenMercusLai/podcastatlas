# Battle of Monte Cassino

## Definition

The Battle of Monte Cassino was the prolonged 1944 Allied struggle to break the German Gustav Line in Italy, culminating in the capture of the ruined [[AbbeyOfMonteCassino|Monte Cassino monastery]] by the Polish Second Corps on 18 May.

## Current Synthesis

[[533-wojtek-the-bear-who-beat-the-nazis-glt8956421015]] enters the battle through [[AndersArmy|Anders' Army]] and its 22nd Artillery Supply Company. Earlier Allied assaults had failed and the monastery had been reduced to rubble before [[WladyslawAnders|Władysław Anders]] accepted a further high-casualty attack. Polish troops renewed their assault on 17 May and raised their flag over the ruins the next day.

The battle joins operational breakthrough, sacred-site destruction, severe loss, and national memory. The source reports 1,150 Polish dead and 3,050 wounded and emphasizes the supply effort required to keep artillery functioning. [[Wojtek]]'s reported shell carrying and the company's bear-and-shell badge compress that larger system into a memorable image, but the episode's own uncertainty cautions against letting the mascot story replace the battle's human cost.

## Key Claims

- Monte Cassino was a key defensive position on the Gustav Line and had resisted repeated Allied assaults.
- The monastery's destruction made a sacred ruin part of both the battlefield and its later memory.
- Anders accepted the Polish assault despite expecting severe casualties.
- The Polish Second Corps captured the monastery ruins on 18 May 1944 after renewing its attack the previous day.
- Artillery supply was central to the operation, and the 22nd Company's shell-carrying-bear badge linked that logistical work to Wojtek.
- Polish commemoration of the victory remains inseparable from invasion, deportation, exile, and an uncertain postwar return.

## Evidence

- Defensive setting and assault: [[533-wojtek-the-bear-who-beat-the-nazis-glt8956421015]] describes the Gustav Line, previous attacks, Anders's decision, and the Polish flag over the ruins.
- Cost and logistics: [[533-wojtek-the-bear-who-beat-the-nazis-glt8956421015]] gives Polish casualty figures and emphasizes ammunition, fuel, and food supplied by the 22nd Company.
- Symbolic memory: [[533-wojtek-the-bear-who-beat-the-nazis-glt8956421015]] connects Wojtek's reported assistance and the company badge to the soldiers' wider exile story.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This source centers the Polish culmination rather than all phases, national contingents, command decisions, German defenses, civilian effects, or the disputed military value of bombing the monastery. Casualty and supply totals remain episode-attributed. Wojtek's precise actions are not secure enough to bear the operational explanation of victory.
