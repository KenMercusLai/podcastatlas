# Women's Distance-Running Exclusion

## Definition

Women's distance-running exclusion is the institutional restriction of women's participation through claims of physical incapacity, sensational reporting, missing team and entry pathways, and enforcement practices that turn social prejudice into sporting eligibility.

## Current Synthesis

[[pushkin-goes-to-the-olympics-pushkin-goes-to-the-olympics]] joins an Olympic example to the Boston Marathon. Reports about the 1928 women's 800 meters emphasized collapse and medical danger even though the episode says all nine runners finished and the first six beat the prior world record. The event's removal for three decades shows how inaccurate or selective representation can become an institutional rule.

The [[KathrineSwitzer]] and [[BobbiGibb]] cases show another layer: participation can precede official recognition. Switzer found no explicit rule barring women and entered with a numbered bib, while an official's physical intervention exposed the operative exclusion. Gibb's earlier and faster participation prevents the visible confrontation from erasing less formally recognized predecessors.

## Key Claims

- Medical-sounding claims can translate gender hierarchy into apparently neutral eligibility judgments.
- Sensational coverage can outweigh actual completion and performance evidence in institutional decisions.
- Written silence does not guarantee practical access when teams, entry customs, and officials still enforce exclusion.
- Official recognition, first participation, and public visibility are distinct historical milestones.
- Correcting a famous milestone should expand the chronology rather than diminish the athlete's documented resistance.

## Evidence

- Olympic restriction: [[pushkin-goes-to-the-olympics-pushkin-goes-to-the-olympics]] contrasts reported collapse with nine finishers and subsequent removal of the women's 800 meters.
- Training-pathway gap: [[pushkin-goes-to-the-olympics-pushkin-goes-to-the-olympics]] describes Syracuse's lack of women's teams and Switzer's training with men.
- Rule and enforcement gap: [[pushkin-goes-to-the-olympics-pushkin-goes-to-the-olympics]] reports no explicit entry ban alongside an official's attempt to remove Switzer during the race.
- Recognition chronology: [[pushkin-goes-to-the-olympics-pushkin-goes-to-the-olympics]] places [[BobbiGibb]] before and ahead of Switzer while preserving the importance of Switzer's numbered entry and photographed confrontation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode summary does not provide the 1928 newspapers, governing-body minutes, full race results, Boston rules, registration records, or a complete global history of women's running. It supports an institutional-prejudice interpretation but does not isolate the causal weight of journalism, medical belief, administrators, or later advocacy. One famous race cannot represent every woman's access or every jurisdiction's timeline.

## What Changed

- Added a framework joining media representation, medicalized claims, eligibility, and enforcement.
- Distinguished participation, official entry, and public visibility as separate milestones.
