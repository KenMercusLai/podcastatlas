# Japanese Industrial-Policy Path Dependence / 日本产业政策路径依赖

Japanese industrial-policy path dependence is [[vol-125-riben-daodi-hai-xing-bu-xing-chuantai-dongya-guancha-ju-lsilax772olwi9xvvvfz6xcelfqw]]'s explanation for why a historically successful development state can struggle in newer innovation cycles. [[FanYiru|樊一如]] invokes postwar bureaucratic coordination, MITI-style industry organization, and large-firm alliances as a model that worked for textiles, cars, and manufacturing catch-up.

The source's concern is that the same system can become rigid. High-level coordination, consensus among established firms, and experience-based planning may help in known industrial domains, but internet, software, AI, and frontier entrepreneurship often reward small teams, fast iteration, tolerance for failure, and disruption of incumbents.

## Key Claims
- Bureaucratic coordination can accelerate catch-up when the target industry and technology route are knowable.
- The same coordination can protect incumbents and slow experimentation when the frontier is uncertain.
- Postwar success can become an institutional memory that makes later reform harder.
- Japan's large-organization prestige and young people's risk aversion reinforce the path-dependence problem.
