## Definition

政绩述职造假 / administrative performance fraud is the governance failure in which officials exaggerate or fabricate reported achievements because promotion, reward, and reputation depend on central assessment of performance claims.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 419 grounds the concept in the Western Han Wang Cheng case. The source first notes that Qin-Han official evaluation already used 考课 and 上计; officials could rise, fall, be rewarded, or be punished according to reported results. That structure makes written performance reports valuable, and therefore vulnerable to fabrication.

[[WangChengWesternHan|王成]] supplies the compact failure mode. As Jiaodong xiang, he is praised by [[EmperorXuanOfHan|汉宣帝]] for supposedly attracting more than 80,000 displaced people back to the locality and receives Guannei Hou rank and stipend. A later review finds that the achievement was inflated to deceive the ruler. The episode's institutional lesson is that false metrics do not merely misreward one official; once the ruler publicly endorses the false model, other officials learn that reputation packaging can beat ground truth.

## Key Claims

- Performance evaluation creates fraud risk when advancement depends on reported achievements that the center cannot directly observe.
- False local-achievement claims can win real rank, stipend, and reputation before verification catches up.
- Premature public endorsement by a ruler can suppress corrective feedback because exposing the fraud also embarrasses the superior who praised it.
- Fabricated merit harms bureaucratic culture by teaching mediocre officials that image management can substitute for governance.
- Post-hoc review can expose false reporting, but delayed correction may not fully repair the reward signal already sent.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept is currently grounded in one source and one compact Western Han case. Hanji 419 does not prove that all Qin-Han evaluation was unreliable, nor does it provide the original audit files, local population records, or a full map of who knew the truth before the later review. The host's connection to modern workplace performance packaging is useful as analogy, but the concept's source-backed core remains the historical incentive and delayed-verification problem.
