# Causal Determinism / 因果决定论

Causal determinism is the challenge to [[FreeWill|free will]] that every event, including a human act, may be the result of prior causes. In [[ep256-ai-shidai-ziyou-yizhi-hai-cunzai-ma-lk9aci8oqnwerk26jy683nbdddcx]], [[TuMotuo|土摩托]] introduces this pressure through [[Democritus|Democritus / 德谟克利特]] and atomism, then through [[Chrysippus|Chrysippus / 克律西波斯]] and causal chains.

The episode extends the same pattern into modern science. [[IsaacNewton|Newtonian mechanics]] and [[LaplacesDemon|Laplace's demon]] make the universe look predictable in principle; [[CharlesDarwin|Darwinian evolution]], addiction, genes, and neural activity make ordinary choices look less self-originating than they feel.

## Key Claims
- Determinism turns "I chose" into a question about where the causal chain began.
- The challenge is stronger when causes include body, genes, upbringing, poverty, nutrition, addiction, and institutions rather than only abstract physics.
- Causal explanation can reduce blame without eliminating the need for responsibility, prevention, and repair.
- The episode resists a flat conclusion: understanding causes shrinks naive freedom, but does not automatically erase [[BiologicalAgency]] or [[MoralResponsibilityUnderDeterminism]].
