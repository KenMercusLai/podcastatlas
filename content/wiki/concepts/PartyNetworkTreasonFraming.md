# Party Network Treason Framing / 朋党网络谋反化

## Definition

Party network treason framing / 朋党网络谋反化 is the pattern where a reputation, recommendation, or association network is explained to a ruler as a rebellion or throne-security threat.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-893-rongyu-bang-ruhe-biancheng-siwang-mingdan-lmxzqoauf79-swwnk-0cevav0gq|Hanji 893]] gives the current source case through [[CaoJieLateHan|曹节]]. When officials report that party figures are mutually implicated, the fourteen-year-old [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]] asks what the wording means. Cao Jie explains that party people recommend one another, form a faction, and may do improper things; he then escalates the explanation into possible overthrow of the court and emperor.

The framing is powerful because it translates social visibility into ruler fear. A士人 network may begin as mutual praise, teacher-student ties, and reputation politics, but once an inner-court interpreter controls the explanation, those ties can be made to look like a coordinated security threat. That moves the case from moral conflict into arrest authorization.

## Key Claims

- Ambiguous "party" language becomes more lethal when an interpreter defines it for an inexperienced ruler.
- Mutual recommendation and reputation ties can be recoded as factional preparation rather than social esteem.
- The treason frame does not need to prove actual rebellion if it succeeds in activating ruler self-protection.
- Palace access matters because the person explaining the accusation can decide whether the ruler sees honor, faction, or rebellion.
- This pattern links private accusations against named people to broader political persecution.

## Evidence

- Interpreter role: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-893-rongyu-bang-ruhe-biancheng-siwang-mingdan-lmxzqoauf79-swwnk-0cevav0gq|Hanji 893]] says Emperor Ling asks Cao Jie what the party accusation means.
- Association-to-threat move: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-893-rongyu-bang-ruhe-biancheng-siwang-mingdan-lmxzqoauf79-swwnk-0cevav0gq|Hanji 893]] says Cao Jie explains mutual recommendation and party formation as preparation for improper action.
- Rebellion escalation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-893-rongyu-bang-ruhe-biancheng-siwang-mingdan-lmxzqoauf79-swwnk-0cevav0gq|Hanji 893]] says Cao Jie further tells the emperor that the party figures may overthrow the court and emperor.
- Arrest authorization: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-893-rongyu-bang-ruhe-biancheng-siwang-mingdan-lmxzqoauf79-swwnk-0cevav0gq|Hanji 893]] says Emperor Ling soon approves the report after hearing Cao Jie's explanation.
- Local-to-court bridge: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-893-rongyu-bang-ruhe-biancheng-siwang-mingdan-lmxzqoauf79-swwnk-0cevav0gq|Hanji 893]] also shows Zhu Bi's accusation against Zhang Jian and others becoming part of the court arrest sequence.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept names the framing mechanism, not proof that every named party figure was innocent of all political ambition. Hanji 893's evidence supports a source-scoped judgment that Cao Jie converts an association network into a fear-inducing rebellion story for a young emperor.

## What Changed

- Hanji 893 creates the concept to capture the specific interpretive move from party association to treason-like ruler threat.
