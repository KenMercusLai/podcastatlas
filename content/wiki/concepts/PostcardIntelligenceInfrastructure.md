# Postcard Intelligence Infrastructure

Postcard intelligence infrastructure is the episode's case for everyday analog media becoming military information. In [[socialist-mobility-a-telling-michigan-vote-6a74600ec3e1347e8b5bf216]], [[CatherineNixie|Catherine Nixey]] says [[JohnGodfrey]] appealed on the BBC in March 1942 for photos and postcards of European beaches and landing grounds, expecting 10,000 items and receiving 10 million.

The concept shows that intelligence does not always begin as secret data. Public images, tourist habits, postal circulation, sorting labor, and military questions can convert ordinary objects into actionable knowledge for [[DDay|D-Day]] planning.

## Key Claims
- Everyday media can become intelligence when collected at scale and organized around operational questions.
- Public participation can supply geographic and visual detail unavailable to formal agencies.
- The usefulness of postcards came from their image content, location specificity, and mass circulation.
- The intelligence value depended on sorting and interpretation, not only on the existence of the objects.
