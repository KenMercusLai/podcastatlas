# Executive War Powers Breakdown

## Definition
Executive war powers breakdown is the source's claim that Congress abandons constitutional responsibility when it passively accepts presidential control over war initiation, tariffs, and emergency action.

## Current Synthesis
Shapiro's institutional critique is not only that Trump made bad decisions. It is that congressional leaders, especially Speaker Johnson in his telling, became a rubber stamp and surrendered powers the constitutional system expected legislators to defend. The concept links war powers, tariff authority, executive orders, and corruption norms into one checks-and-balances failure.

## Key Claims
- Presidential overreach depends on legislative deference as well as executive ambition.
- War powers and tariff authority are treated as constitutional responsibilities Congress should actively guard.
- Institutional design cannot work if officeholders refuse to defend their own branch's authority.
- Corruption, pardons, self-dealing, and gifts worsen the breakdown by weakening norms beyond formal legality.
- Voter demand for ethical norms is presented as part of restoring checks and balances.
