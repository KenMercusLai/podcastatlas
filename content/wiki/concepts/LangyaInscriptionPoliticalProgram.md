# Langya Inscription Political Program / 琅琊刻石政治纲领

Langya inscription political program / 琅琊刻石政治纲领 is the source-scoped pattern from [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-122-2-ribenren-daodi-shi-bushi-xufu-de-houdai-lhgbbhg3zw8rlx1jjb7lpb5ihihh|Qinji 122-2]] where Qin's stone inscription at [[LangyaMountain|琅琊山]] turns the emperor's travel into public doctrine. The inscription is described as praising [[QinShiHuang|秦始皇]] and Qin, promoting agriculture over commerce, stressing unified writing and measures, urging people to learn and obey law, and presenting unification as the end of war.

The concept sits between [[ImperialSymbolicStandardization|帝国符号标准化]] and [[QinAdministrativeStandardization|秦国行政标准化]]. It is symbolic because it broadcasts Qin's theory of rule in durable public text; it is administrative because the message centers on standards, law, farming, and governed subjects.

## Key Claims
- Public inscriptions can carry an empire's operating doctrine into newly conquered space.
- The Langya inscription links peace after conquest to obedience under law rather than to negotiated pluralism among former states.
- "重农抑商" is presented as part of the moral economy of Qin rule, not only as an economic preference.
- Standard writing and measurement become claims about universal order when carved into a conquered landscape.
