## Definition
Crisis leader signal amplification is the pattern in which a leader's words, gestures, emotional state, interactions, and silence produce intensified effects on followers when danger makes cues from authority unusually consequential.

## Current Synthesis
The episode's three cases make communication and emotional display part of operational leadership. [[FrancescoSchettino]]'s delay, blame, and departure signal self-protection; [[EkkapolChantawong]]'s routine and hopeful language preserve direction; and [[TammyJoShults]]'s short explanation turns a terrifying descent into a controlled route toward Philadelphia. Amplification does not mean calm alone solves a crisis: the signal is credible when joined to competent action and visible concern for the group.

## Key Claims
- Followers treat leader signals as especially informative under ambiguity and threat.
- Emotional state can spread through a group, with authority increasing its impact.
- Concise explanation can convert frightening motion into a comprehensible plan.
- Visible calm is most credible when paired with technical competence and protective action.
- Silence, delay, blame, and abandonment also communicate priorities and can amplify disorder.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept is supported here by an expert's synthesis of vivid cases rather than direct comparison across crises. Emotional contagion does not prove that every follower responds alike, and calm presentation can become misleading if it suppresses material risk or blocks help-seeking. Technical competence, resources, team action, and external rescue remain indispensable.

## What Changed
- Established a crisis-specific signal-amplification concept across maritime, cave, and aviation cases.
