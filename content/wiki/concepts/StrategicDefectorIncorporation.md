# 纳叛安抚式整合 / Strategic Defector Incorporation

## Definition
纳叛安抚式整合 / strategic defector incorporation is the settlement pattern in which a ruler selectively incorporates former enemies, defectors, or those who protected defectors because pardon and office can stabilize territory, advertise surrender survivability, and convert dangerous armed capacity into usable strength.

## Current Synthesis
The concept now covers Cao Cao's pre-Guandu and in-Guandu incorporation branches. After Lü Bu's collapse at Xiapi, Cao Cao executes actors judged too dangerous while incorporating Zhang Liao, Zhang Ba, Xu Xi, Mao Hui, and other local or former-defector figures. Wei Zhong is the narrower personal-betrayal case: Cao had recommended him, felt anger when he fled during the Yanzhou crisis, but still pardoned and appointed him after Shequan because he valued his talent. Zhang Xiu is the higher-cost former-enemy case: he had caused the deaths of Cao Cao's son, nephew, and guard, yet Jia Xu judges that Cao Cao will still accept him because Cao needs his force, holds emperor-backed legitimacy, and can turn forgiveness into a public signal of magnanimity.

Hanji 1049 extends the pattern into active battlefield collapse. After the [[WuchaoRaidLateHan|Wuchao Raid]], [[ZhangHeLateHan|Zhang He]] and [[GaoLanLateHan|Gao Lan]] surrender because [[YuanShao|Yuan Shao]] rejected Zhang He's correct advice and [[GuoTu|Guo Tu]]'s slander made them unsafe. [[XunYou|Xun You]] helps [[CaoHong|Cao Hong]] accept the surrender by identifying the internal-camp cause. The pattern is therefore selective, strategic, and reputationally expensive, but it can also be opportunistic when the enemy's command system breaks.

## Key Claims
- Incorporation is selective: some defeated actors remain too trust-destroying to spare, while others can be made useful under a new hierarchy.
- Pardon advertises that surrender can remain survivable even after prior hostility, reducing resistance among local armed groups.
- Talent can override private anger when the actor fills an administrative or local-stabilization need.
- Local brokers and advisers matter because figures like Zhang Ba and Jia Xu can make risky incorporations credible or strategically legible.
- The victor needs enough legitimacy and power for pardon to look like state policy rather than weakness.
- Pre-campaign pressure raises the value of incorporation: before Guandu, manpower, rear security, and surrender signaling are unusually valuable.
- Battlefield collapse can create a fast incorporation window when correct but endangered enemy commanders have immediate reasons to defect.

- Former-defector pardon: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1026-jiemi-sanguo-di-yi-mengjiang-lvbu-de-luomu-lhjf-agujuliglqchumtff3tftxg1|Hanji 1026]] treats Zhang Ba's protection of Xu Xi and Mao Hui as a case where Cao Cao rewards credible local protection rather than demanding revenge.
- Talent over private revenge: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1032-duidai-pantu-caocao-weihe-renci-lmrr3c2f2dayectlwndt4p-katuy|Hanji 1032]] shows Cao Cao sparing [[WeiZhongLateHan|魏种]] after prior flight because he still judges Wei Zhong talented, personally unbinding him and appointing him commandery governor after [[ShequanLateHan|射犬]].
- High-cost former enemy: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1035-zhansha-caocao-qinzi-he-aijiang-ta-jing-zaidu-guijiang-caocao-lh10wwkwcqw5hnap3bodw6oxpt7v|Hanji 1035]] makes Zhang Xiu's renewed submission a more reputationally expensive case because Wancheng had killed Cao Ang, Cao Anmin, and Dian Wei.
- Legitimacy and magnanimity logic: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1035-zhansha-caocao-qinzi-he-aijiang-ta-jing-zaidu-guijiang-caocao-lh10wwkwcqw5hnap3bodw6oxpt7v|Hanji 1035]] records Jia Xu's argument that Cao Cao's emperor-backed name, weaker position, and ambition to display grace make acceptance likely.
- Guandu horizon: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1026-jiemi-sanguo-di-yi-mengjiang-lvbu-de-luomu-lhjf-agujuliglqchumtff3tftxg1|Hanji 1026]], [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1032-duidai-pantu-caocao-weihe-renci-lmrr3c2f2dayectlwndt4p-katuy|Hanji 1032]], and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1035-zhansha-caocao-qinzi-he-aijiang-ta-jing-zaidu-guijiang-caocao-lh10wwkwcqw5hnap3bodw6oxpt7v|Hanji 1035]] all tie incorporation to the expanding field before Cao Cao's confrontation with Yuan Shao.
- Battlefield defection after Wuchao: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1049-guandu-zhizhan-jieju-jiemu-caocao-zhongyu-yi-ruo-sheng-qiang-lvtutwyhrix4rhiypa9dknz3da1m|Hanji 1049]] has Zhang He and Gao Lan surrender after Yuan Shao rejects Zhang He's Wuchao advice and Guo Tu slanders him, while Xun You reads that cause as making the surrender credible.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Lü Bu's execution shows that the pattern is not universal mercy. Wei Zhong's later performance is not detailed by the current source, so the case demonstrates Cao Cao's stated talent logic more than a proven administrative outcome. Zhang Xiu's prior attack makes him a higher trust risk than many defeated officers, and the current source does not independently document the full later record of his service. Zhang He and Gao Lan differ from the earlier examples because their surrender happens during campaign collapse, not after a settled conquest; the case depends on Xun You's reading that Yuan-camp rejection and slander make the defection credible. The concept also remains distinct from [[CapturedOpponentTalentIncorporation|降敌任用式立威]], where the focus is captured opponent talent after hierarchy is visibly established; strategic defector incorporation puts more weight on prior betrayal, local brokerage, internal-camp danger, and the reputational cost of pardon.

## What Changed
- Added Hanji 1049's Zhang He and Gao Lan battlefield-defection case.
- Extended the concept from pre-Guandu settlement into active campaign collapse after Wuchao.
- Added surrender-screening as a credibility mechanism through Xun You's reading of Yuan-camp politics.
