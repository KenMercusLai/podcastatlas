# 纳叛安抚式整合 / Strategic Defector Incorporation

## Definition
纳叛安抚式整合 / strategic defector incorporation is the settlement pattern in which a ruler selectively incorporates former enemies, defectors, rebels, or those who protected defectors because pardon and office can stabilize territory, advertise surrender survivability, and convert dangerous armed capacity into usable strength.

## Current Synthesis
The concept now covers Cao Cao's pre-Guandu, in-Guandu, and post-Guandu incorporation branches. After Lü Bu's collapse at Xiapi, Cao Cao executes actors judged too dangerous while incorporating Zhang Liao, Zhang Ba, Xu Xi, Mao Hui, and other local or former-defector figures. Wei Zhong is the narrower personal-betrayal case: Cao had recommended him, felt anger when he fled during the Yanzhou crisis, but still pardoned and appointed him after Shequan because he valued his talent. Zhang Xiu is the higher-cost former-enemy case: he had caused the deaths of Cao Cao's son, nephew, and guard, yet Jia Xu judges that Cao Cao will still accept him because Cao needs his force, holds emperor-backed legitimacy, and can turn forgiveness into a public signal of magnanimity.

Hanji 1049 extends the pattern into active battlefield collapse. After the [[WuchaoRaidLateHan|Wuchao Raid]], [[ZhangHeLateHan|Zhang He]] and [[GaoLanLateHan|Gao Lan]] surrender because [[YuanShao|Yuan Shao]] rejected Zhang He's correct advice and [[GuoTu|Guo Tu]]'s slander made them unsafe. [[XunYou|Xun You]] helps [[CaoHong|Cao Hong]] accept the surrender by identifying the internal-camp cause.

Hanji 1054 adds a negotiated local-rebel variant through [[ChangXiLateHan|昌豨]]. [[ZhangLiao|张辽]] persuades Chang Xi to surrender under siege, and [[CaoCao|曹操]] appoints him Donghai taishou. The later renewed rebellion and killing by [[YuJin|于禁]] make this a qualified case: incorporation can solve the immediate armed problem without permanently transforming the actor.

## Key Claims
- Incorporation is selective: some defeated actors remain too trust-destroying to spare, while others can be made useful under a new hierarchy.
- Pardon advertises that surrender can remain survivable even after prior hostility, reducing resistance among local armed groups.
- Talent, local control, or administrative usefulness can override private anger when the actor fills a stabilization need.
- Local brokers, advisers, and negotiators matter because figures like Zhang Ba, Jia Xu, Xun You, and Zhang Liao make risky incorporations credible or strategically legible.
- The victor needs enough legitimacy and power for pardon to look like state policy rather than weakness.
- Incorporation windows can come from conquest settlement, pre-campaign need, battlefield collapse, or siege negotiation.
- The pattern's success must be judged after settlement; Chang Xi shows immediate surrender and office can still end in renewed rebellion.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Lü Bu's execution shows that the pattern is not universal mercy. Wei Zhong's later performance is not detailed by the current source, so the case demonstrates Cao Cao's stated talent logic more than a proven administrative outcome. Zhang Xiu's prior attack makes him a higher trust risk than many defeated officers, and the current sources do not independently document the full later record of his service. Zhang He and Gao Lan differ from the earlier examples because their surrender happens during campaign collapse, not after a settled conquest; the case depends on Xun You's reading that Yuan-camp rejection and slander make the defection credible. Chang Xi adds an even sharper qualification: he can be persuaded and appointed, but later rebellion means incorporation remained temporary. The concept also remains distinct from [[CapturedOpponentTalentIncorporation|降敌任用式立威]], where the focus is captured opponent talent after hierarchy is visibly established; strategic defector incorporation puts more weight on prior betrayal, local brokerage, internal-camp danger, and the reputational cost of pardon.

## What Changed
- Added Hanji 1054's Chang Xi case as a negotiated local-rebel incorporation branch.
- Extended the concept beyond post-Xiapi settlement, pre-Guandu clemency, and Wuchao battlefield surrender into post-Guandu siege negotiation.
- Added a stronger durability qualification: office after surrender can solve immediate danger without preventing later rebellion.
