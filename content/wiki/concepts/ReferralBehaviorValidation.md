## Definition
Referral behavior validation is the use of customers' unsolicited sharing, recommendation, and friend conversion as evidence that a product is memorable and valuable enough to travel beyond the founder's direct selling effort.

## Current Synthesis
For Romanticize Collective, the useful sequence is not simply asking whether subscribers like a box. The company should learn whether members complete the experiences, talk about them, invite others into them, and cause new subscriptions. Missing referrals are diagnostic rather than dispositive: founders should investigate whether the obstacle is weak value, private use, unclear explanation, poor timing, price, friction, or a format that should evolve.

## Key Claims
- Referral evidence is stronger when it follows actual use rather than stated enthusiasm before use.
- Recommendation and friend conversion test both product value and the customer's ability to explain that value.
- Absence of referral should trigger causal inquiry, not an automatic pivot or an assumption that the mission is wrong.
- Products designed around shared or offline experience may reveal value through participation and invitations as well as purchases.
- Referral is one product-market-fit signal and should be read alongside retention, completion, margin, and acquisition data.

## Evidence
### Experience completion and sharing
- [[advice-line-with-tara-bosch-of-smartsweets-8aab80a1-b0c5-4103-b875-f5d4823d07ce]] recommends asking Romanticize Collective members whether they complete experiences, tell friends and family, and inspire those people to subscribe.

### Format learning
- [[advice-line-with-tara-bosch-of-smartsweets-8aab80a1-b0c5-4103-b875-f5d4823d07ce]] links this diagnostic to tests of gatherings, local collaboration, and partnerships rather than assuming the monthly box is the final product.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- Some valuable products are private, sensitive, infrequent, hard to explain, or used in contexts where referrals are naturally weak.
- Incentivized referrals can measure reward-seeking rather than product conviction.
- A small member base produces noisy conversion data, so qualitative interviews and cohort retention remain important.

## What Changed
- Established referral behavior as a diagnostic bridge between experience quality, communicability, acquisition, and product-format decisions.
