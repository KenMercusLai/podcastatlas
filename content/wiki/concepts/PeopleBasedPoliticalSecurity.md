# People-Based Political Security / 民心型政治安全

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-927-zuo-shengyi-de-zuigao-jingjie-shi-shenme-lngfbjn6xui9k-9lp-ihdqkhpbpr|Hanji 927]] adds a Jiaozhi administrative-repair case before the later Liangzhou, Yizhou, and Youzhou branches. Earlier inspectors in [[JiaozhiCommandery|交趾郡]] extract wealth and block complaints, so local people and lower officials no longer experience Han rule as protection.

[[JiaCongLateHan|贾从]] rebuilds that security environment by investigating causes, reassuring people, recalling refugees and hungry households, exempting corvee labor, removing major violent leaders, and appointing clean county officials. The source's praise for officials not taking even a meal from locals makes people-support a result of practical administrative conduct, not only moral reputation.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-937-zhanshen-zhisi-haoren-zongshi-duozai-duonan-lp7przsmag9ookq7eoisvsmnm5aw|Hanji 937]] adds a late-Han personal-safety version before Hanji 938's paired frontier and moral-authority cases. [[FuXie|傅燮]] has enough reputation among Beidi Hu cavalry that even when they are attacking [[HanyangCommanderyLateHan|汉阳郡]] with the rebels, they kneel and offer to escort him home. People-support can create a survival channel for the person, but it cannot by itself save the commandery once [[FrontierCommandCollapse|边郡军政崩盘]] has already occurred.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-938-lishi-de-jingxing-ruanruo-zhihui-dailai-quru-lnlnrketgfbadhfjbzdjjtrmqfsh|Hanji 938]] adds a paired late-Han prelude before Hanji 940's Yizhou case and Hanji 942's Youzhou pacification. Negatively, [[GengBiLateHan|耿鄙]]'s Liangzhou field shows how corrupt frontier governance leaves Qiang-Hu groups and ordinary locals unable to live, making rebellion socially intelligible. Positively, [[ChenShiLateHan|陈寔]] shows local moral authority preserving dignity and repairing a theft case without coercive exposure.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-940-de-minxin-zhe-de-tianxia-ni-xin-bu-xin-loipf7b0p5a-yh0rj2njnsml8a1j|Hanji 940]] adds a late-Han Yizhou case through the contrast between [[XiJianLateHan|郗俭]], [[MaXiangLateHan|马相]], [[JiaLongLateHan|贾龙]], and [[LiuYanLateHan|刘焉]]. Xi Jian's extraction leaves him undefended, Ma Xiang can ride resentment but cannot build durable rule, Jia Long restores order through remaining officials and commoners, and Liu Yan's [[YizhouPacificationByLeniency|益州宽惠安抚]] turns people-support into a provincial entry strategy.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-942-yue-youxiu-de-ren-huode-yue-lei-heli-ma-lkwlrcgnt-kxxc2qqz6z6wkg7e5z|Hanji 942]] adds a positive late-Han frontier case through [[LiuYuLateHan|刘虞]]'s entry into [[YouzhouLateHan|幽州]]. Instead of treating [[ZhangJuLateHan|张举]], [[ZhangChunLateHan|张纯]], [[QiuLijuLateHan|丘力居]], [[Wuhuan|乌桓]], and [[Xianbei|鲜卑]] as a problem solvable only by force, Liu Yu uses reputation, envoys, and reward signals to induce submission and dispersal.

The source also marks the limit of people-based security as military structure. Liu Yu can stabilize Youzhou, but he still leaves [[GongsunZan|公孙瓒]] with cavalry at [[YoubeipingCommandery|右北平]], preserving the armed actor whose suppression-first posture later turns into the Hanji 971-972 rupture.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-953-luanshi-zhizhong-yaobuyao-jiangjiu-renyi-lrzfimgqatjxyw9zx9ry2kiwxkon|Hanji 953]] adds a late-Han positive case through [[LiuBiao|刘表]]'s entry into [[Jingzhou|荆州]]. [[KuaiLiang|蒯良]] argues that officials fail because they do not love the people, so Liu Biao can turn "仁义" into practical attachment. The source does not make people-support stand alone: it works because [[KuaiYue|蒯越]] supplies the parallel force-absorption plan and Liu Biao turns both into a base at [[XiangyangLateHan|襄阳]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-969-yuanshi-xiongdi-neidou-shui-neng-jianlou-lsgj6sewo35jpsgfm-koivijtr6r|Hanji 969]] adds a late-Han negative case through [[QingzhouLateHan|青州]]. The source does not present Qingzhou civilians as a loyal base for either claimant; it shows the opposite endpoint, where competing appointees and food exhaustion make ordinary people the victims of military survival. People-based security is absent: armies remain in the field, but the population loses protection and food access.

People-based political security / 民心型政治安全 is the practical security lesson [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-25-zhaoliang-dui-shangyang-shuo-ni-bupei-lnqmwjbrc9qomwrdt5brourf-78p]] draws from [[ZhaoLiang|赵良]]'s warning to [[ShangYang|公孙鞅 / 商鞅]]. Zhao Liang's line "得人者兴，失人者崩" is not treated only as moral advice. It explains why one minister can move without guards while another cannot leave home unless surrounded by armed protection.

The source's contrast case is [[BailiXi|百里奚]]. Baili Xi is remembered as high-achieving but plain, trusted, and mourned by Qin society; Shang Yang is remembered as effective but feared and resented. In this reading, public regard and elite acceptance are a form of political infrastructure. They reduce security costs, lower revenge risk, and give a minister more room to survive a ruler's death or a change in factional weather.

The concept sits between [[RenhePoliticalPower|人和政治力量]] and [[MencianPeopleBasedLegitimacy|民本合法性]], but it is narrower than both. It does not claim that popular support alone creates rightful rule. It says that a powerful actor who loses enough social support may become operationally unsafe even while holding office, because protection depends too much on coercion and a single patron.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-971-luanshi-miyun-shui-sha-le-caocao-laodie-ljh3hdhssigmv78bueo-hprfqwku|Hanji 971]] adds [[Xuzhou|徐州]] as the negative civilian-security case before the Liu Yu/Gongsun Zan branch. [[CaoCao|曹操]]'s revenge for [[CaoSongMurder|曹嵩遇害]] may answer family injury, but the reported [[XuzhouMassacreLateHan|徐州屠杀]] destroys the people-support environment that later Hanji 987 says makes Xuzhou a poor target for Cao Cao while [[YanzhouLateHan|兖州]] remains unsecured.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-45-yi-shi-wei-jian-junwang-daodi-gai-bu-gai-sharen-chengxing-lo3cok7cfwzrxesp-i8miz1fc1db]] extends the same logic from ministerial safety to rulership through [[SongJunYan|宋康王 / 宋君偃]]. Song Kang Wang's violence wins territory but leaves him without people willing to defend the state when Qi, Chu, and Wei attack. The episode links that collapse to [[NonKillingUnification|不爱杀人者能一之]]: coercion can create fear, but lost people-support becomes a security failure.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-49-mengzi-yue-wo-jiao-ni-zhiguo-ni-que-ba-wo-dang-huaping-lhy14pbf9thcfdiaulcmf8dwcef8]] adds the conquered-population version through [[YanState|燕国]]. [[QiState|齐国]] can occupy Yan quickly, but if Yan's people experience Qi as a new source of killing, imprisonment, and plunder, they will rebel or seek another rescuer. The source makes popular support a security condition for post-conquest rule, not only a moral sign.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-68-jingtan-songkangwang-huangyinwudu-de-gouxue-lishi-2-lkn073gbtpmg11hcrcm8h6uvawzt]] sharpens the Song version. [[SongJunYan|宋康王 / 宋君偃]] is not portrayed as weak; he expands Song and maintains alliances. The security failure comes when fear and disgust replace defendable loyalty, allowing [[QiMinWang|齐湣王]] to turn cruelty stories into [[MoralizedWarPretext|道德化战争借口]] and leaving the capital without people willing to protect the ruler.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-70-xihuan-huayang-zuosi-de-qiguo-junzhu-luuyasp5-nhnikdcozzaueosb-md]] applies the same mechanism back onto Qi Min Wang. After [[LeYi|乐毅]] defeats Qi and takes [[Linzi|临淄]], Qi Min Wang's flight shows how lost people-support becomes practical exposure: hosts in Wey, Zou, and Lu are unwilling or unable to protect him, and [[ZhuoChi|卓齿]] can turn from rescuer into killer. The episode therefore treats popular and elite abandonment as part of the security environment around a ruler's body.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-72-shenme-yang-de-ren-cai-shi-zhongzhen-zhishi-logxegnqexgc7z-lodw6tr6-8f28]] adds the post-conquest repair side. [[LeYi|乐毅]]'s forces try to make captured [[QiState|齐国]] governable by disciplining troops, forbidding plunder, honoring recluses, reducing taxes, and restoring older good policies. At the same time, [[WangZhu|王蠋]] and [[TianFazhang|田法章]] show that security remains broken for people tied to the old order: a worthy former official is coerced into a lethal choice, and the dead ruler's son survives only by hiding.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-73-lishishang-ziyou-lianai-de-kaichuangzhe-shi-shui-lkumfsfialqgdl6qpb3c-hnz7h9u]] adds the restoration side of the same security problem. [[TianFazhang|田法章]] can survive only by hiding in [[JuWarringStates|莒城]], but [[WangSunGu|王孙古]]'s revenge mobilization and the ministers' search for the royal heir show that Qi still has enough local loyalty to punish [[ZhuoChi|淖齿 / 卓齿]] and install [[QiXiangWang|齐襄王]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-78-qiguo-mingyun-jing-bei-chengguan-gei-jiule-lpt3reyr6ok8g2lhcxrnd5qctyug]] adds the final-holdout and commander-trust version. [[LeYi|乐毅]] treats [[JuWarringStates|莒城]] and [[JimoWarringStates|即墨]] as political as well as military problems: a destructive assault could frighten already occupied Qi cities and undo the security he has been building. [[TianDan|田单]] then reverses the security balance by exploiting [[YanHuiwang|燕惠王]]'s suspicion and damaging Yan army morale through [[BattlefieldCommanderReplacementRisk|commander replacement]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-79-shishang-yong-gengniu-da-shengzhang-diyi-ren-lsrl0posblloqwugox160xsicp2p]] adds the popular-anger version of the same mechanism. [[QiJie|骑劫]] tries to frighten Jimo by mutilating prisoners and digging graves, but the acts make surrender look unbearable and turn private family violation into collective resolve. Tian Dan's later [[FireOxNightRaid|fire-ox night raid]] works because Yan has lost the people-support environment that Le Yi was trying to preserve.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-81-lishi-zaoqi-pua-jishi-lu-1-ljxbn4gznuohegzyee0-tudi9z-y]] adds the danger of people-based security for the minister who possesses it. [[TianDan|田单]]'s care for a freezing old man makes sense as a humane reflex from a commander who lived with [[JimoWarringStates|即墨]]'s people through siege, but [[QiXiangWang|齐襄王]] reads that same public affection as rival legitimacy. The episode therefore shows popular support as a double-edged asset: it protects a state and can endanger the actor who is seen as owning it.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-74-huanyuan-wanbi-guizhao-zhenxiang-lg1irvbbc1wp8oph2kmpatfoxebk]] adds Xunzi's people-support criterion as a compact theoretical statement. In the episode's closing evaluation, winning the people makes a state secure, while ritual-righteousness and administrative competence rank even higher and wealth extraction ranks lower. The source therefore gives people-based security an explicit place inside a broader [[ConfucianIdealGovernance|Confucian]] and [[XunzianWangbaThreePaths|Xunzian]] hierarchy.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-75-3-gudai-de-jingji-zhanzheng-neng-you-duo-kepa-liybgoz8lspypax6dhuke2js0pxf]] adds an economic-migration version of the same security logic. The episode says [[LuState|鲁国]], [[LiangState|梁国]], and [[ChuState|楚国]] lose people after market distortion and food scarcity make survival easier in [[QiState|齐国]]. The point is not only that people like benevolent rulers; grain prices and trade access can make population loyalty operationally mobile.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-99-3-xunzi-bingfa-li-cangzhe-renxing-de-mimi-lgfcp4hkruyhhrjguqhjbhllvwp43]] adds Xunzi's military-intelligence version. In the source, a renyi ruler's people become extended ears and eyes: they reveal a tyrant's plots, leak hidden information, and make deceptive tactics fail. People-support is therefore not only post-conquest stability or ruler safety; it directly affects battlefield secrecy, reconnaissance, and the reliability of command.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-99-4-shui-shi-kongzi-hou-zui-weidade-sixiangjia-lr4phoq9chc4tz0yb8y95gy21qdd]] adds Xunzi's civilian-protection version. A [[XunzianMilitaryLegitimacy|renyi army]] should not kill the old and weak, trample crops, punish those who retreat without fighting, or treat voluntary submitters as ordinary captives. The source's point is that people-support is not only something a ruler already has; it is also something an army preserves or destroys through conduct.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-139-ta-zaici-yingxiang-hanchao-lp1zujuq0o3ohxc79xqi1phpb5zo|Hanji 139]] gives the early Han contrast in practical form. [[LiuBang|刘邦]] protects [[ZiyingQin|子婴]], returns to [[Bashang|霸上]], announces [[YuefaSanzhang|约法三章]], refuses local gifts, and tells Guanzhong people that harsh Qin law is the target rather than the population itself. The same source contrasts this with [[XiangYu|项羽]]'s [[XinanMassacre|新安坑降]], where surrendered Qin troops are treated as a security threat and eliminated, turning people-based security into a dividing line between governable occupation and fear-based control.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-993-shentingling-zhizhan-sunce-dazhan-taishici-lrivpk7col2l7k-s4dqzxxp2kqp1|Hanji 993]] adds a late-Han Jiangdong version through [[SunCe|孙策]]. His army's strict discipline makes frightened people return with food and wine, and his later Qu'a settlement avoids forced enlistment while offering amnesty and household tax relief. The source treats those choices as part of Sun Ce's ability to turn conquest into local support and military growth, not merely as personal generosity.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-142-zhechang-juhui-xiangyu-shudiao-le-yiqie-ln7dv2jxrk6lt1c4szylsrb41k3r|Hanji 142]] extends the Xiang Yu side of that contrast after [[HongmenYan|鸿门宴]]. Xiang Yu's killing of Ziying, burning of [[Xianyang|咸阳]], plunder, and execution of [[HanSheng|韩生]] after criticism all damage the social and adviser support needed to turn conquest into security. The source makes Liu Bang's earlier restraint more than personal caution: it is the kind of conduct Xiang Yu fails to sustain when he controls Qin's center.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-168-bawangbieji-qian-fashengle-shenme-1-lowxwc8oq8as1trjo-gjimmuhnr3|Hanji 168 part 1]] adds the final Xiang Yu version. After [[FourSidedChuSongs|四面楚歌]] and [[YuJi|虞姬]]'s death, Xiang Yu's breakout is undone partly by a nameless farmer who deliberately points him the wrong way near Yinling. The host reads this not as random trivia but as the concrete shape of lost民心: when Xiang Yu says Heaven is destroying him, the source answers that "Heaven" is appearing through people who no longer protect him.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-168-bawangbieji-qian-fashengle-shenme-2-liqgict85wh-bf4fn2bytygmk-ca|Hanji 168 part 2]] keeps the same "天亡我" language but shifts the emphasis to self-diagnosis. [[XiangYu|项羽]] repeatedly treats failure as Heaven's decision, while the episode argues that his real losses are strategic, political, and psychological: he cannot convert popular abandonment, morale collapse, or the [[WujiangPavilionChief|乌江亭长]]'s retreat option into corrected action. People-based insecurity has therefore become internal as well as external.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1020-sanguo-baijiazi-yuanshu-ruhe-ba-hao-pai-da-de-xilan-lhwr9hfmanr-zktleufy3kvl38u0|Hanji 1020]] adds a late-Han court and local-protection version. [[HeKui|何夔]] says [[YuanShu|袁术]] cannot gain popular support because he lacks trust, while [[XuChu|许褚]] shows the micro-level opposite: in a violent countryside, local households attach to someone who can actually protect them. The [[YangBiao|杨彪]] case adds a court version, because [[ManChong|满宠]] warns [[CaoCao|曹操]] that punishing a famous old minister without proof would lose public confidence.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1019-jiemi-chenminwang-liuchong-de-chuanqi-yisheng-ll2fma7wupp5gczudysfett5lhio|Hanji 1019]] adds a polity-scale late-Han version through [[ChenStateLateHan|陈国]]. [[LiuChongChenKing|刘宠]]'s military protection and [[LuoJun|骆俊]]'s civil administration make Chen attractive to displaced people after disorder; popular movement toward Chen is therefore evidence of protection capacity. The same case also shows the limit: people-based security can make a local order valuable, but it does not by itself protect the leadership core from [[YuanShu|袁术]] and [[ZhangKaiAssassin|张闿]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1001-weishenme-quan-ni-buyao-fanzhongnu-lolurkcjpie14nnczk0tu7iqerke|Hanji 1001]] adds the pre-usurpation version. [[ZhangChengLateHan|张承]] answers [[YuanShu|袁术]] that rule lies in virtue, not strength, and invokes the pattern that near people should be pleased and distant people should come. Yuan Shu's generals' silence and [[YanXiangLateHan|阎象]]'s warning show the support failure before formal opposition forms.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-998-baima-jiangjun-gongsunzan-cong-sanqian-baima-dao-kunshou-gucheng-lp-1l2gvbsbfnyznutdghxwwvqna|Hanji 998]] adds a northern late-Han version through [[GongsunZan|公孙瓒]]. After killing [[LiuYuLateHan|刘虞]], abusing local elites, and empowering disliked dependents, Gongsun Zan loses the social base needed to hold [[YouzhouLateHan|幽州]]. The later coalition around [[XianyuFu|鲜于辅]], [[YanRou|阎柔]], [[Wuhuan|乌桓]], [[Xianbei|鲜卑]], [[LiuHeLateHan|刘和]], and [[YuanShao|袁绍]] shows people-based insecurity at regional scale: officials are killed, commanderies defect, and [[YijingFortress|易京]] cannot make up for the lost support.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-972-sanguo-liuyu-zhisi-taisha-taitianzhen-lvawrcandbh628rckukosgarh7v2|Hanji 972]] supplies the immediate prehistory for that regional collapse. [[LiuYuLateHan|刘虞]] has enough local moral authority that his killing becomes dangerous after his defeat, while [[TianChou|田筹]] turns loyalty to Liu Yu into a functioning mountain settlement in [[XuWuMountain|徐无山]]. The source therefore separates people-support from battle success: Liu Yu loses militarily, but Gongsun Zan still inherits a legitimacy wound.

## Key Claims
- Hanji 937 adds that people-support can protect an official's body through enemy-side mercy or rescue offers, but may still be insufficient to protect the office or territory.
- Hanji 938 adds that people-based security can appear as both a negative frontier-governance failure and a positive local moral-authority repair within the same year.
- Hanji 940 adds that people-support can decide whether a provincial official survives revolt, whether a rebel can stabilize success, and whether a new governor can enter through pacification.
- Hanji 942 adds that reputation, envoy diplomacy, and credible rewards can make frontier pacification cheaper than repeated military suppression, but unresolved armed command can keep the settlement fragile.
- Hanji 953 adds that people-support can help found a regional base when it is paired with coercive capacity and local elite management.
- Hanji 993 adds that strict military discipline and lenient recruitment policy can turn a newly entered region's fear into food support, enlistment, and governability.
- Hanji 998 adds that local resentment and elite alienation can turn a conquered province into a coalition field against the conqueror.
- Hanji 972 adds that a defeated authority's people-support can survive the battle as posthumous legitimacy, and that a loyal follower can convert it into local order.
