# People-Based Political Security / 民心型政治安全

People-based political security / 民心型政治安全 is the practical security lesson [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-25-zhaoliang-dui-shangyang-shuo-ni-bupei-lnqmwjbrc9qomwrdt5brourf-78p]] draws from [[ZhaoLiang|赵良]]'s warning to [[ShangYang|公孙鞅 / 商鞅]]. Zhao Liang's line "得人者兴，失人者崩" is not treated only as moral advice. It explains why one minister can move without guards while another cannot leave home unless surrounded by armed protection.

The source's contrast case is [[BailiXi|百里奚]]. Baili Xi is remembered as high-achieving but plain, trusted, and mourned by Qin society; Shang Yang is remembered as effective but feared and resented. In this reading, public regard and elite acceptance are a form of political infrastructure. They reduce security costs, lower revenge risk, and give a minister more room to survive a ruler's death or a change in factional weather.

The concept sits between [[RenhePoliticalPower|人和政治力量]] and [[MencianPeopleBasedLegitimacy|民本合法性]], but it is narrower than both. It does not claim that popular support alone creates rightful rule. It says that a powerful actor who loses enough social support may become operationally unsafe even while holding office, because protection depends too much on coercion and a single patron.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-45-yi-shi-wei-jian-junwang-daodi-gai-bu-gai-sharen-chengxing-lo3cok7cfwzrxesp-i8miz1fc1db]] extends the same logic from ministerial safety to rulership through [[SongJunYan|宋康王 / 宋君偃]]. Song Kang Wang's violence wins territory but leaves him without people willing to defend the state when Qi, Chu, and Wei attack. The episode links that collapse to [[NonKillingUnification|不爱杀人者能一之]]: coercion can create fear, but lost people-support becomes a security failure.

## Key Claims
- Social support can be a security asset, not only a legitimacy ornament.
- Heavy personal security can reveal political weakness by showing how much resentment surrounds the officeholder.
- A ruler's backing can hide insecurity without resolving it.
- Reformers who break privileges and punish broadly need an exit plan that includes social repair, not only formal resignation.
- In Shang Yang's case, the episode suggests that repair may have come too late even if he had accepted the Shiji retirement plan.
