# Homeric Nostos

## Definition

Homeric nostos is the homecoming drive in [[TheOdyssey]]: the desire to return to home, kin, household, and recognizability after war and wandering.

## Current Synthesis

In [[687-the-odyssey-hero-of-the-trojan-horse-part-1-glt2393125423]], nostos is not passive homesickness. It is the pressure that organizes the entire first-half voyage, because each episode tests whether [[Odysseus]] and his men can keep wanting [[Ithaca]], [[Penelope]], and [[Telemachus]] more than booty, comfort, intoxication, erotic delay, hunger, safety, or immortality-like suspension.

## Key Claims

- Nostos gives the wandering episodes a fixed direction even when the route is geographically and narratively unstable.
- The desire for home can be lost through pleasure and forgetfulness before any enemy defeats the hero.
- Odysseus' homeward goal is repeatedly endangered by his crew's appetite and distrust as well as by monsters and gods.
- Delay scenes such as Circe's island and Calypso's island test whether survival without return still counts as success.
- Nostos remains tied to social recognition, so the first-half voyage prepares the second-half return to household, name, bed, and community.

## Evidence

- Organizing term: [[687-the-odyssey-hero-of-the-trojan-horse-part-1-glt2393125423]] defines nostos as Odysseus' desperate yearning to return home.
- Forgetfulness threat: [[687-the-odyssey-hero-of-the-trojan-horse-part-1-glt2393125423]] says the Lotus Eaters make scouts lose the desire to return.
- Appetite and distrust: [[687-the-odyssey-hero-of-the-trojan-horse-part-1-glt2393125423]] shows Ismarus feasting, Aeolus' opened bag, and Helios' cattle all obstructing homecoming.
- Delay without death: [[687-the-odyssey-hero-of-the-trojan-horse-part-1-glt2393125423]] makes Circe's year and Calypso's later confinement homecoming problems rather than simple rest.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept is grounded in this source's retelling and should not be treated as a complete philological account of all Greek nostos traditions. It also does not make Odysseus' homeward desire ethically clean; the same return is entangled with raiding, deception, crew losses, and later revenge.

## What Changed

- Created Homeric Nostos as the first-half Odyssey concept for homeward desire under repeated pressure.
