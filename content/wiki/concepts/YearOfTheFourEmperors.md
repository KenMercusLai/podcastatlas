# Year of the Four Emperors

## Definition

The Year of the Four Emperors was the AD 69 Roman civil war in which [[Galba]], Otho, [[Vitellius]], and [[Vespasian]] successively claimed imperial power after [[Nero]]'s fall.

## Current Synthesis

[[12-days-neros-succession-and-the-fall-of-the-byzantine-empire-glt6148714296]] uses the Rhine mutiny to show that the crisis was not simply a rapid list of emperors. New Year sacrifice, augury, statues, oaths, the Senate and people, aristocratic pedigree, provincial governorship, army grievance, and battlefield success all offered different forms of legitimacy, but no one form could secure the throne by itself.

The Rhine legions' rejection of Galba converted dissatisfaction into a search for an imperially credible commander. Vitellius supplied rank and ancestry, but his victory over Otho did not settle the empire; Vespasian's supporters defeated him and founded a new dynasty. Provincial armies therefore emerged as kingmakers, while Vespasian's eventual Flavian settlement had to convert civil-war victory into a broader claim of restored order.

## Key Claims

- Nero's fall exposed a succession system without a universally binding transfer rule.
- New Year oaths made military loyalty visible but could not manufacture allegiance where grievance already existed.
- The Upper German oath to the Senate and people was a provisional rejection of Galba, not a completed constitutional settlement.
- Rank, age, and pedigree made Vitellius more usable as an imperial candidate than the junior commanders driving the mutiny.
- Successive defeats of Galba, Otho, and Vitellius made armed coalition and provincial command decisive tests of authority.
- Vespasian's victory ended the year's sequence by establishing the Flavian dynasty, not by restoring the old Julio-Claudian succession.

## Evidence

- Ritual and mutiny - [[12-days-neros-succession-and-the-fall-of-the-byzantine-empire-glt6148714296]] links January 1 loyalty rites to the Rhine armies' rejection of Galba.
- Candidate construction - [[12-days-neros-succession-and-the-fall-of-the-byzantine-empire-glt6148714296]] explains why commanders moved from a Senate-and-people oath toward Vitellius.
- Violent sequence - [[12-days-neros-succession-and-the-fall-of-the-byzantine-empire-glt6148714296]] summarizes Galba's murder, Otho's defeat, Vitellius's death, and Vespasian's accession.
- Dynastic outcome - [[12-days-neros-succession-and-the-fall-of-the-byzantine-empire-glt6148714296]] identifies Vespasian's victory as the foundation of Flavian rule.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source concentrates on the Rhine opening and compresses the wider civil war. It does not reconstruct the Praetorian role in Galba's death, Otho's coalition, the battles in Italy, Vespasian's eastern base, or the political positions of every province. Reports of Vitellius's reluctance and prophecy remain ancient narrative traditions rather than transparent access to motive.

## What Changed

- Created a synthesis of AD 69 as a competition among ritual, institutional, dynastic, and military forms of legitimacy.
