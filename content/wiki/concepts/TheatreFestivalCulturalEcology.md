# Theatre-Festival Cultural Ecology / 戏剧节文化生态

## Definition
Theatre-festival cultural ecology is the dense, recurring system through which productions, creators, audiences, venues, curation, technical capacity, discussion, and informal social life reinforce one another around live performance.

## Current Synthesis
[[MengJinghui|孟京辉]] compares isolated theatrical activity to small trees and a festival to a forest. In the [[WuzhenTheatreFestival|乌镇戏剧节]] case, density matters: invited domestic and international work, youth competition, public performance, talks, readings, multiple venues, and life in one compact place increase the chance of discovery, comparison, collaboration, and audience conversion.

The ecology is not automatically healthy because it is crowded. Check-in visitors, commercial socializing, competition for attention, adult audience attrition, and insufficiently distinctive new work can coexist with sold-out productions and international visibility. The concept therefore describes enabling conditions and tensions rather than treating scale as cultural success by itself.

## Key Claims
- A festival can do more than aggregate shows when programming, place, and duration create repeated contact among creators and audiences.
- International selection broadens aesthetic comparison only when venues and technical systems can actually host the work.
- Youth programs can support both new creators and new spectators, which are separate needs in a sustainable theatre ecology.
- Informal social life can deepen cultural participation while also shifting attention toward networking, consumption, or check-in performance.
- Attendance scale does not by itself establish artistic renewal, long-term audience retention, or creator distinctiveness.

## Evidence
- Forest model: [[xiju-daoyan-mengjinghui-luoyonghao-cong-ai-reshi-de-fanpanzhe-dao-xiju-wutuobang-de-jiangouzhe-lib-ndrl-9td9h0kql7paz834qep]] records Meng's comparison between small theatrical clusters and a festival-scale forest.
- Program density: [[xiju-daoyan-mengjinghui-luoyonghao-cong-ai-reshi-de-fanpanzhe-dao-xiju-wutuobang-de-jiangouzhe-lib-ndrl-9td9h0kql7paz834qep]] describes multiple activity types and venues operating over an extended period in Wuzhen.
- International capacity: [[xiju-daoyan-mengjinghui-luoyonghao-cong-ai-reshi-de-fanpanzhe-dao-xiju-wutuobang-de-jiangouzhe-lib-ndrl-9td9h0kql7paz834qep]] uses large visiting productions and overseas scouting to connect curation to logistics and audience readiness.
- Ecology tensions: [[xiju-daoyan-mengjinghui-luoyonghao-cong-ai-reshi-de-fanpanzhe-dao-xiju-wutuobang-de-jiangouzhe-lib-ndrl-9td9h0kql7paz834qep]] acknowledges check-in culture, recent audience uncertainty, adult attrition, and concern about domestic creative energy.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence comes from one festival artistic director and does not compare institutions systematically. Reported attendance, conversion, international recognition, and creator impact are not independently measured. A concentrated event can create temporary intensity without producing year-round employment, geographic access, or durable audience habits.

## What Changed
- Established a festival-ecology model that separates cultural density from attendance scale alone.
