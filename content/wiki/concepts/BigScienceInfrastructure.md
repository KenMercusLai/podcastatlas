# Big Science Infrastructure / 大科学基础设施

Big science infrastructure is the source's frame for research that cannot be done by a lone scientist or a small laboratory alone. In [[ep248-zai-gaoyuan-yu-dixia-tanxun-zhongguo-da-kexue-xiangmu-lidn7xxkcw4-66fw-y50fqqacwo]], [[MiaoQian|苗千]] defines contemporary big science through large facilities, long timelines, multi-institution teams, extreme environments, and frontiers of physics where ordinary instruments cannot isolate the relevant evidence.

The episode distinguishes this from a pure national-mobilization story. Manhattan Project-style mobilization, "两弹一星," and the space race are historical reference points, but the cases here are basic-science projects whose value includes open knowledge, [[BigScienceInternationalCollaboration|international collaboration]], and shared scientific competition.

The concept gathers four project cases: [[AliPrimordialGravitationalWaveObservatory|阿里原初引力波探测站]] for cosmic microwave background polarization, [[JiangmenUndergroundNeutrinoObservatory|江门中微子实验]] for neutrino physics, [[ChinaSpallationNeutronSource|中国散裂中子源]] for neutron-scattering and materials research, and [[FAST|贵州天眼]] for radio astronomy and public encounter.

## Key Claims
- Big science becomes necessary when the signal is too weak, noisy, rare, or environment-dependent for ordinary instruments.
- Infrastructure includes geography, civil engineering, detector technology, computing, management, budgets, and training pipelines.
- The "big" in big science is not only size; it is also organizational scale, uncertainty scale, data scale, and verification time.
- The source treats basic-science infrastructure as both knowledge production and state-capacity expression, while avoiding a simple prestige-only reading.
