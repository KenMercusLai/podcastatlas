# Commodity Time-Horizon Framework

Commodity time-horizon framework is [[155-ruhe-lijie-huangjin-de-shishiji-bodong-lp3lcda5zskiv-dcezcugf2q93vi]]'s method for keeping commodity analysis aligned with the investor's holding period. [[DavidWeng|大卫翁]] maps short-term moves to liquidity and emotion, medium-term moves to market narrative, long-term moves to supply and demand, and gold's extra-long layer to world order, inflation, and monetary-system confidence.

The framework is meant to prevent horizon mixing. A short-term gold trader cannot rely only on the ultra-long [[GoldMonetaryAnchor]] thesis when ETF flows, momentum reversal, and stop-loss selling dominate the next few days. A strategic allocator should not automatically abandon the thesis because one crowded move has reversed.

## Key Claims
- The same commodity can be a trade, a six-to-twelve-month narrative position, a three-to-five-year allocation, or a strategic monetary-order hedge.
- Short-term analysis emphasizes liquidity, ETF flows, market emotion, momentum, and forced selling.
- Medium-term analysis asks whether the dominant narrative has changed, such as gold shifting from classic safe haven to U.S. or dollar distrust vote.
- Long-term commodity analysis returns to supply and demand; for gold, stable mine supply makes central-bank and private demand more important.
- Gold has an additional ultra-long layer because it can be priced through [[CurrencyAnchorTransition]], global reserve behavior, and confidence in [[USTreasury|U.S. Treasuries]].
- The framework complements [[PortfolioSuitability]] because the correct evidence set depends on how long the investor can and intends to hold.
