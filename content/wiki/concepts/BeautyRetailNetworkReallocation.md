# Beauty Retail Network Reallocation / 美妆零售网络再分配

## Definition
Beauty retail network reallocation is the channel strategy of closing some symbolic or high-visibility stores while expanding into more stable cities, malls, and online channels.

## Current Synthesis
The source uses [[AesopBeauty|Aesop / 伊索]] to show that store closures do not automatically mean market retreat. Airport and landmark stores can be useful for early visibility, but a maturing China strategy may shift resources toward broader city coverage, recurring shopping environments, and online channels such as [[Tmall]].

## Key Claims
- Symbolic stores can build early brand awareness but may not remain the best long-term retail nodes.
- Store closures need to be read alongside new-market entry and planned openings.
- Online channels can reduce dependence on a few highly visible physical locations.
- Parent-company ownership can accelerate network expansion while still requiring store-level optimization.

## Evidence
- Closure evidence: [[yibao-chunjingshui-fene-xiahua-anta-qixia-yujiafu-pinpai-kaishe-jianshenfang-1012409578]] says Aesop closed its Shanghai Hongqiao airport T2 store and earlier symbolic mainland locations.
- Expansion evidence: [[yibao-chunjingshui-fene-xiahua-anta-qixia-yujiafu-pinpai-kaishe-jianshenfang-1012409578]] says Aesop entered Chongqing and Wuhan and was preparing multiple stores.
- Channel evidence: [[yibao-chunjingshui-fene-xiahua-anta-qixia-yujiafu-pinpai-kaishe-jianshenfang-1012409578]] says Aesop entered Tmall and had about 30 mainland stores after L'Oreal's acquisition accelerated expansion.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not prove lease expiry was the only reason for closures or that new stores have better unit economics. The concept should be used as a qualified reading pattern, not as an automatic defense of every closure.

## What Changed
- Created the concept from Aesop's mainland China store closures and continued expansion.
