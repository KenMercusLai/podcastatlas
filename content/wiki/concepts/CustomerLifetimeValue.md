# Customer Lifetime Value

Customer lifetime value is the business value of a customer relationship over repeated transactions rather than a single purchase. [[140-jiudian-jituan-weishenme-dou-rezhongyu-dazao-huiyuan-he-jifen-tixi-chuantai-yuanxingzhe-yu-suibingjiang-lj-xa3wxzjog6-8zm9nfakh6ulew]] uses hotel membership to make the idea concrete: if a guest joins a loyalty program, books directly, returns more often, and buys across brands, the hotel group can justify benefits that would look expensive on one stay.

The source cites a claim attributed to [[InterContinentalHotelsGroup]] that loyalty members spend more after joining. The important wiki-level point is the mechanism, not the exact percentage: rewards, status, remembered preferences, and reduced booking friction can convert an anonymous buyer into a higher-value repeat user.

## Key Claims
- Lifetime value shifts analysis from transaction margin to retention, frequency, cross-sell, and direct relationship.
- In hotels, CLV connects [[HotelLoyaltyPrograms]] to [[HotelDirectBookingChannels]] and [[HotelBrandPortfolio]].
- CLV can justify benefits only when the program changes repeat behavior enough to cover points, breakfast, upgrades, IT, marketing, and operations.
- The concept also creates risk: companies may over-invest in loyalty mechanics if users chase benefits without becoming durable customers.
