# Beaver Fur Trade

## Definition

The beaver fur trade was the commodity system linking Indigenous hunting and exchange, inland waterways, European traders and chartered companies, Atlantic shipping, felt-hat demand, imperial rivalry, and ecological depletion.

## Current Synthesis

The episode distinguishes long-standing Indigenous beaver use from the export-oriented system that followed European arrival. Once pelts became valuable in Atlantic fashion markets and useful as a medium of exchange, traders moved farther inland, companies converted trade access into territorial claims, and French, Dutch, and English competition interacted with Indigenous strategy and conflict. Lightweight traps and industrial demand increased extraction until beaver numbers collapsed, revealing how a commodity chain could reorganize both political geography and ecosystems.

## Key Claims

- Indigenous communities used the whole animal within subsistence, material, and ceremonial systems before European export demand intensified.
- Felt-hat fashion made beaver pelts unusually valuable, portable, and currency-like.
- River corridors and Indigenous exchange networks initially enabled European access to inland pelts.
- Trade competition became entangled with the [[BeaverWars|Beaver Wars]] and wider imperial rivalry.
- Chartered companies joined commerce to mapping, monopoly, and territorial claims.
- Industrial trapping converted a renewable animal resource into severe ecological depletion.

## Evidence

- Indigenous use and exchange: [[285-canada-beaver-wars-glt6084067740]] lists food, fur, sinew, jawbone, claw, and archaeological uses while distinguishing them from later near-extermination.
- Atlantic demand: [[285-canada-beaver-wars-glt6084067740]] explains how barbed beaver hair produced stiff, water-resistant felt for several hat styles.
- Conflict and empire: [[285-canada-beaver-wars-glt6084067740]] links trade rivalry to the Beaver Wars, the Hudson's Bay charter, the Seven Years' War, and British control.
- Industrial scale and collapse: [[285-canada-beaver-wars-glt6084067740]] connects new traps, bait, and wide commercial reach to population collapse and later conservation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source's distinction between Indigenous use and European industrial extraction is analytically important but broad. Indigenous nations were active political and commercial actors, patterns varied across time and place, and neither the Beaver Wars nor European empire can be reduced to fur demand alone. Population estimates, exchange values, and technology effects remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Established a commodity-chain account joining animal material, fashion demand, Indigenous exchange, corporate power, empire, and ecological collapse.
