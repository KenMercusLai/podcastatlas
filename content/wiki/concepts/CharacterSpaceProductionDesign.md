# Character-Space Production Design / 人物空间美术设计

## Definition
Character-space production design is the use of room allocation, furniture position, sightlines, temperature and color, clutter, mismatch, privacy, and functional compromise to express a character's relationships, resources, responsibilities, and present dramatic pressure.

## Current Synthesis
VOL.88 distinguishes character design from interior decoration. Lin Yi's small household gives its only bedroom to the child, places the medically vulnerable Lin Hai in the living room's center, and locates Lin Yi on the balcony where he can monitor his brother. The arrangement makes limited space, care, interrupted privacy, and sensitivity to death part of the character before dialogue explains them.

The Fang and Zhou homes work comparatively. Fang Xiaoran's home is larger and warmer, while Zhou Xiaofeng's is colder and more impersonal. Two comfortable seats in Fang's living room fit the established mother-daughter unit but leave no settled place for Zhou when he returns. Such choices need not form a harmonious decorating scheme: objects accumulated for use can conflict aesthetically while remaining biographically and dramatically coherent.

## Key Claims
- Who receives a bedroom, central room, private zone, or line of sight can encode care and family hierarchy.
- Furniture count and placement can represent belonging or exclusion without explicit dialogue.
- Warmth, coldness, order, and clutter gain meaning through contrast among characters rather than as universal style codes.
- Functional mismatch and accumulated objects can strengthen realism when they reflect household history and use.
- Visual harmony should be rejected when it contradicts character resources, relationships, or narrative pressure.
- Present dramatic clarity can require simplification, but unresolved backstory and floor-plan questions should remain acknowledged.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source acknowledges that Lin Yi's one-bedroom home does not completely answer how a larger earlier family configuration would have worked. Production-design intention is not proof that audiences infer the intended character meaning, and warm or cold palettes, clutter, room size, or furniture arrangements should not be treated as universal psychological diagnostics outside the narrative context.

## What Changed
- Established a character-centered production-design concept based on spatial allocation and lived functional compromise.
- Preserved the source's unresolved floor-plan and backstory qualification.
