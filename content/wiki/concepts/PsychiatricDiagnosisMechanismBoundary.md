# Psychiatric Diagnosis-Mechanism Boundary

## Definition
The psychiatric diagnosis-mechanism boundary distinguishes labels that describe recurring symptom clusters from explanations of the biological, psychological, or social causes producing those symptoms in a particular person.

## Current Synthesis
The episode argues that labels such as schizophrenia, bipolar disorder, depression, or autism can organize symptoms and care without closing causal inquiry. Palmer uses metabolism and mitochondria to connect trauma, inflammation, infection, hormones, nutrition, stress, and other factors, but this integration is a research and clinical hypothesis rather than a single-cause replacement for psychiatric diagnosis.

This distinction is especially important in the episode's vaccine and autism discussion. Inflammation can impair mitochondrial function, and responses to infection or vaccination can vary, but a plausible pathway or rare adverse response does not establish a blanket causal claim. Associations involving parental obesity or diabetes likewise identify risk patterns rather than deterministic explanations for an individual child.

The optogenetics crossover adds a complementary decomposition. Anxiety, fear, depression, or compulsion can bundle subjective feeling, physiology, avoidance, freezing, appetite, reward, and other behaviors even when experiments can manipulate some components without moving all of them together. Circuit-level causality can therefore refine a broad diagnosis without proving that one pathway is the essence of the diagnosis or the cause in every patient.

## Key Claims
- Diagnostic labels can describe symptom patterns without identifying final causes.
- Biological, psychological, and social factors can contribute to the same diagnostic category.
- A plausible inflammatory or mitochondrial mechanism is not equivalent to demonstrated population-level causation.
- Risk associations do not determine an individual's diagnosis or outcome.
- Mechanism-focused inquiry should widen investigation without erasing the practical value of diagnosis.
- Broad psychiatric labels may contain separable behavioral, physiological, subjective, and circuit components.
- A treatment response or experimentally induced behavior does not by itself prove the root cause of a whole disorder.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not invalidate DSM diagnosis, establish mitochondrial dysfunction or one neural circuit as the cause of all psychiatric illness, or independently prove their reported vaccine, autism, inflammation, parental-risk, neurotransmitter, or animal-model claims. Diagnostic utility, component decomposition, mechanism, association, treatment response, and causation remain distinct questions.

## What Changed
- Extended the boundary from metabolic mechanisms to circuit-level decomposition and treatment-response inference.
