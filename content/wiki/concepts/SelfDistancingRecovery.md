# Self-Distancing Recovery

## Definition
Self-distancing recovery is a perspective practice for viewing a distressing encounter as an observer or from a longer time horizon so that emotional capture decreases and attention can return to effective action.

## Current Synthesis
In the episode's nursing example, the person imagines the rude scene as if watching a movie and asks how much it will matter in five years. The intended shift is not denial but reduced immersion: greater objectivity can help identify a useful response and return attention to patients or other high-stakes tasks.

The practice belongs inside a wider recovery system that may include exercise, sleep, nutrition, volunteering, community, coaching, positive relationships, and meaningful activity. It restores agency over one's next move while leaving prevention, boundaries, reporting, and institutional accountability intact.

## Key Claims
- Observer perspective can reduce emotional immersion in a remembered encounter.
- A longer time horizon can recalibrate the event's immediate psychological dominance.
- Lower immersion can improve objectivity and response selection.
- Faster attentional recovery matters when the next task affects other people's safety.
- Personal recovery complements rather than replaces prevention and accountability.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The supplied summary does not provide study design, effect size, duration, or comparisons with other interventions. Distance can become minimization or avoidance if it suppresses needed action, documentation, boundary setting, or support. Asking how much an event will matter later is inappropriate when the encounter signals ongoing abuse, discrimination, retaliation, or safety risk.
