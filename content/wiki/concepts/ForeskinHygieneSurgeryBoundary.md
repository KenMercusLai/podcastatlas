# Foreskin Hygiene Surgery Boundary / 包皮清洁与手术边界

## Definition
The foreskin hygiene surgery boundary separates a retractable long foreskin that can be kept clean from phimosis, recurrent inflammation, urinary or anatomic problems, and acute [[ParaphimosisEmergency|paraphimosis]] that change the threshold for intervention.

## Current Synthesis
VOL.31 and VOL.78 reject foreskin length alone as a universal surgical indication. A long foreskin may still retract fully and remain manageable through cleaning, while repeated smegma accumulation and inflammation can create health problems. VOL.78 sharpens the anatomical distinction: phimosis involves inability to retract enough to expose the glans fully, and a tight foreskin trapped after retraction is a separate emergency rather than an elective hygiene question.

Intervention is therefore condition- and person-specific. Recurrent inflammation, phimosis, urinary difficulty, hidden lesions, acquired narrowing associated with diabetes, or anatomy that impairs cleaning can move the balance toward treatment. Childhood physiologic non-retractability, concealed or buried anatomy, cooperation and anesthesia, spontaneous change, and the risk of removing too much skin argue against a single age or visual rule. Qualified examination must assess both the cost of non-treatment and the harms of unnecessary or excessive surgery.

## Key Claims
- A retractable long foreskin is not automatically a disease or mandatory surgery indication when cleaning is feasible and inflammation does not recur.
- Retractability is a key distinction: inability to expose the glans fully falls within the source's phimosis boundary.
- Repeated smegma accumulation and inflammation can contribute to narrowing, urinary problems, and a stronger treatment indication.
- A retracted foreskin trapped behind the glans is [[ParaphimosisEmergency|paraphimosis]] and needs prompt escalation rather than routine observation.
- Pediatric decisions depend on development, symptoms, anatomy, spontaneous change, cooperation, anesthesia, and specialist assessment; forceful early retraction is discouraged.
- Concealed or buried anatomy and over-resection risk can make apparently simple circumcision harmful, so tissue sufficiency and future function matter.
- Diabetes-related skin fragility and recurrent inflammation can produce acquired narrowing in older adults while also complicating healing.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
These sources do not establish a universal age, technique, or indication for circumcision. VOL.78's preferred 10-11-year window, sexual-function reassurance, cancer and HIV associations, lichen-sclerosus discussion, stapler description, and diabetes-related surgery judgment are source-scoped public education. Current examination and guidance may differ by development, anatomy, symptoms, infection, urinary function, metabolic control, procedure, and jurisdiction. This page does not prescribe retraction, manual reduction, surgery, anesthesia, or postoperative care.

## What Changed
- Added retractability as the practical distinction between uncomplicated long foreskin and phimosis.
- Separated acute paraphimosis from elective hygiene and circumcision decisions.
- Added pediatric development, concealed anatomy, excessive-resection, and diabetes-related qualifications.
