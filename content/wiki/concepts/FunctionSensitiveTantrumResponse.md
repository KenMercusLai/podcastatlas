# Function-Sensitive Tantrum Response

## Definition
Function-sensitive tantrum response is the practice of matching an adult response to both the emotional phase of an outburst and the consequence the behavior is currently producing for the child.

## Current Synthesis
The episode presents tantrums as overlapping anger and distress rather than undifferentiated misbehavior. Distress may persist while anger spikes and then subsides, so the child's sounds and actions can indicate whether the episode is escalating or becoming available for comfort.

Behavioral function adds a second layer. Withholding attention may reduce an outburst used to obtain attention or reverse a denial, but it can reward avoidance when the tantrum delays a required task. The same surface behavior therefore does not justify one universal technique.

## Key Claims
- Anger and distress can coexist and follow different time courses within one tantrum.
- Reasoning or questioning during peak anger may prolong escalation.
- Attention can reinforce behavior even when the attention is negative.
- Ignoring can reward avoidance when it postpones a required task.
- Response choice should depend on function, phase, safety, and the child's needs.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This is an episode-level framework, not individualized behavioral or clinical guidance. “Planned ignoring” should not override safety, pain, disability, communication difficulty, sensory overload, trauma, or the need for comfort and assessment. Inferring function from one episode can be wrong, and completing a request should not mean coercive escalation.
