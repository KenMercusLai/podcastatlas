## Definition

敏感人事建议边界 / Sensitive Personnel Recommendation Boundary is the authority boundary around unsolicited advice on high-level appointments, especially when the proposed office touches military power, a ruler's trusted relatives, or a superior's unstated timing.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 455 creates the concept through [[HuangBaWesternHan|黄霸]]'s recommendation that [[ShiGaoWesternHan|史高]] become taiwei. The host's point is not that all personnel advice is forbidden. The sharper boundary is that Huang speaks without being asked on a matter Xuan treats as reserved: a top appointment with military implications involving someone the emperor already knows well.

The case combines ancient court politics with a workplace translation. In the court setting, Huang's act can be read as overstepping chancellor duties, trying to connect with a powerful outer relative, or disturbing imperial timing. In the workplace translation, a subordinate who volunteers strong opinions about sensitive personnel moves can be read as not understanding the leader's authority lane. The survival lesson is to answer carefully when consulted, but to avoid initiating sensitive appointment proposals unless the role, mandate, and timing are explicit.

## Key Claims

- High personnel appointments are authority signals, not merely neutral optimization decisions.
- Military-linked or command-linked offices raise the risk because recommendation can look like intervention in coercive power.
- Recommending a superior's trusted relative or intimate can look like factional courting even when framed as flattery.
- A recommendation can be substantively plausible and still politically wrong if it ignores decision ownership and timing.
- A superior's delayed decision can itself be a discipline mechanism, forcing the overstepping subordinate to feel uncertainty.
- The modern workplace lesson is conditional: advice is safer when asked for, bounded by role, and phrased as information rather than as an appointment push.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept does not mean officials or employees should never offer personnel information. It is scoped to sensitive, unsolicited intervention in high appointments where the recommender lacks mandate and the superior has not opened the decision. The episode's workplace lesson is interpretive; the historical case remains a Western Han court incident involving chancellor scope, taiwei military meaning, and Shi Gao's outer-relative proximity.
