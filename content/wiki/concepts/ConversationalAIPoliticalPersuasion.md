# Conversational AI Political Persuasion

## Definition
Conversational AI political persuasion is the use or effect of interactive AI dialogue to change political attitudes, candidate preferences, or voting intentions through individualized questions, responses, and information exchange.

## Current Synthesis
Unlike a fixed political advertisement, a chatbot can respond to a person's questions and supply a large amount of tailored policy information within one exchange. [[DavidRand]] reports that a purpose-built system tested before the 2024 U.S. presidential election produced meaningful preference movement after an average conversation of about six minutes, with movement toward both [[KamalaHarris]] and [[DonaldTrump]].

The episode presents the measured effect as roughly three to four times that of a standard political ad, making interactivity a potentially important persuasion channel. That comparison remains bounded, however: the source does not show whether the change lasted, altered votes, generalized beyond the study sample, or resulted from personalization, information quantity, conversational form, or some combination. A research chatbot also cannot be assumed equivalent to ordinary commercial assistants.

## Key Claims
- Interactive dialogue can adapt to a user's questions in ways a fixed advertisement cannot.
- A short conversation may deliver enough policy information to move stated candidate preferences in an experimental setting.
- Bidirectional movement shows persuasive capacity without by itself establishing partisan neutrality, balance, or safety.
- Measured attitude change should not be equated with durable behavior or an election outcome.
- System purpose, training, prompting, information selection, and sycophancy can shape both the direction and legitimacy of persuasion.

## Evidence
- Experimental result - [[tech-20261019-1019-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261019-1019-mp-tech-pod-128]] attributes to [[DavidRand]] meaningful preference shifts after roughly six-minute chatbot conversations.
- Direction of movement - [[tech-20261019-1019-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261019-1019-mp-tech-pod-128]] says participants moved toward both Harris and Trump.
- Comparative magnitude - [[tech-20261019-1019-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261019-1019-mp-tech-pod-128]] reports Rand's estimate that the effect was about three to four times a standard political advertisement's effect.
- Proposed mechanism - [[tech-20261019-1019-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261019-1019-mp-tech-pod-128]] suggests that rapidly supplying extensive policy information may help explain why people respond to the format.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode does not provide the underlying study design, sample, control condition, confidence intervals, treatment content, follow-up period, or voting-behavior measure. The result therefore supports a research agenda, not a universal effect size. Preference movement in both directions does not prove absence of bias, and a purpose-built experimental chatbot may behave differently from [[ChatGPT]], [[Claude]], or [[Gemini]].

## What Changed
- Created a bounded concept separating reported experimental attitude change from durable electoral impact.
