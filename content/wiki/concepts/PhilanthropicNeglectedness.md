# Philanthropic Neglectedness

## Definition
Philanthropic neglectedness is the principle that a funding target's value depends not only on importance but also on how much attention, money, talent, and workable capacity it already receives, including how much the next gift can change a specific recipient.

## Current Synthesis
The sources treat neglectedness as dynamic [[MarginalAnalysis|marginal analysis]] at two levels. Across causes, early AI safety or longevity work could be attractive because a contribution might change a thin field; once a cause becomes crowded, the next dollar may matter less, while global public health can become neglected again as political support declines. Within one sector, [[HankRowan]]'s gift to the institution now called [[RowanUniversity]] illustrates how recipient baseline matters: the same nominal gift can create capacity at a resource-constrained public college while producing a smaller visible institutional change at an already wealthy university.

Money is not automatically useful merely because a target has less of it. Resource scarcity must be separated from method failure, absorptive capacity, execution quality, and mission fit. [[WeakLinkStrongLinkSystems]] adds a structural test: fund the floor when interdependence makes bottlenecks decisive, but consider concentration when exceptional capacity can control outcomes or is genuinely missing.

## Key Claims
- Importance alone does not determine a donation's marginal value.
- Cause and recipient priority change with funding, talent, politics, institutional capacity, and baseline resources.
- A thin field can become crowded, while a mature field can become neglected again.
- Resource shortage must be distinguished from methodological failure.
- Equal-sized gifts can have sharply unequal effects at different institutions.
- Donors need monitoring rather than permanent loyalty to one issue, method, or institution.

## Evidence
- **Changing balance:** [[e257-duihua-vitalik-ai-anquan-beihoude-zhexue-sixiang-youxiao-lita-zhuyi-ruhe-yingxiang-guigu-7a1e61d3-f02c-498b-ac84-cf68f59b4ca9]] attributes to [[VitalikButerin|Vitalik Buterin]] the judgment that AI safety is less neglected while public-health gaps have re-emerged.
- **Institutional drift:** [[e257-duihua-vitalik-ai-anquan-beihoude-zhexue-sixiang-youxiao-lita-zhuyi-ruhe-yingxiang-guigu-7a1e61d3-f02c-498b-ac84-cf68f59b4ca9]] uses his disagreement with the [[FutureOfLifeInstitute|Future of Life Institute]] to show why donors reassess direction.
- **Method boundary:** [[e257-duihua-vitalik-ai-anquan-beihoude-zhexue-sixiang-youxiao-lita-zhuyi-ruhe-yingxiang-guigu-7a1e61d3-f02c-498b-ac84-cf68f59b4ca9]] distinguishes problems needing resources from problems using the wrong method.
- **Recipient baseline:** [[my-little-hundred-million-my-little-hundred-million]] contrasts [[HankRowan]]'s institution-changing engineering gift with mega-gifts to already wealthy universities.
- **System structure:** [[my-little-hundred-million-my-little-hundred-million]] uses [[WeakLinkStrongLinkSystems]] to argue that an interdependent society may gain more by strengthening accessible educational capacity than by adding resources to its strongest institutions.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Neither source provides a complete comparative impact model. Attention, spending, tractability, talent, legitimacy, useful capacity, execution, donor restrictions, and spillovers differ. Donor disagreement does not prove misuse or low impact, and a visibly transformed institution does not by itself establish greater lifetime social return. The higher-education episode gives less attention to elite research spillovers and selection effects, while its own strong-link exception prevents a blanket rule against concentrated excellence.

## What Changed

- Extended neglectedness from cause selection to recipient selection within a sector.
- Added baseline resources and absorptive capacity to the marginal-value test.
- Qualified floor-raising with a system-specific strong-link exception.
