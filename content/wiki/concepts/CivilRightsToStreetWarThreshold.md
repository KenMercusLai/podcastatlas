# Civil Rights To Street War Threshold

Civil rights to street war threshold is the episode's hinge: the moment when protest, counter-protest, policing, and communal defense cross into sustained urban violence. In [[698-the-troubles-the-brink-of-civil-war-part-2-glt2308408988]], that threshold is reached across [[Derry]] and [[Belfast]] in 1969 after Caledon, the banned Derry march, [[BurntolletBridgeAttack|Burntollet]], [[TerenceONeill|O'Neill]]'s failed election gamble, loyalist bombings, and the [[BattleOfTheBogside]].

The concept is not a claim that civil rights protest naturally causes violence. The source's argument is the opposite: discrimination, unionist fear, radical publicity strategy, police partiality, loyalist sabotage, republican preparation, British ignorance, and Irish-state rhetoric interacted until return to moderation became difficult.

## Key Claims
- Escalation thresholds are built from sequences, not single incidents.
- Violence becomes self-reinforcing when each side treats the last event as proof that its worst fears were justified.
- Military intervention can become unavoidable before the political problem has been defined clearly.
