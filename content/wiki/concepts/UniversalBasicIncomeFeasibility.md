# Universal Basic Income Feasibility / 全民基本收入可行性

## Definition
Universal basic income feasibility is the question of whether a government can sustainably provide a recurring unconditional cash floor while resolving funding, benefit interaction, regional costs, labor incentives, and political durability.

## Current Synthesis
The episode treats pandemic checks as a useful analogy but not a sufficient test of universal basic income. Temporary payments during a shutdown have a different purpose, duration, fiscal baseline, and political coalition from a permanent entitlement. UBI may simplify access and reduce eligibility bureaucracy, but its evaluation must include what it replaces, who loses targeted support, how it is financed, and whether one national amount fits sharply different local costs.

## Key Claims
- Universality can reduce qualification errors, stigma, and administrative burden.
- A cash floor can protect dignity and stabilize consumption during income disruption.
- Permanent funding is harder than temporary emergency deficit spending.
- Replacing targeted programs may simplify administration while reducing support for people with higher needs.
- Labor-supply effects depend on benefit level, taxes, wages, care obligations, and local opportunity.
- Small resource-dividend cases do not automatically scale to a large, diverse national economy.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode does not review UBI trials or estimate fiscal and behavioral effects. Alaska's resource dividend is treated as a limited analogy because its population, funding base, and payment design differ from a full national income floor.
