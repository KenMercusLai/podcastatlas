## Definition
Court women historiography is the pattern in which elite women inside the imperial household shape dynastic memory through court records, editing, teaching, continuation, or control over what becomes authoritative history.

## Current Synthesis
[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-750-jiemi-donghan-wanmei-nvxing-beihou-manshi-xisheng-lu-96lbdh7r0tsmiopbqqrj0q1gt|Hanji 750]] gives [[EmpressMaMingdeEasternHan|Mingde Empress Ma]] as an early Eastern Han case. The episode says she was learned, participated in editing `显宗起居注`, and can be treated as China's first female historian in this source's framing. It explicitly compares her record work with [[BanZhaoLateHan|Ban Zhao]]'s later continuation of the `Han Shu`.

The concept emphasizes a court route into historiography. Women in this pattern need not hold a compiler's office in the same way as male historians; their position inside the palace gives them proximity to imperial memory, household evaluation, and access to ruler-centered materials. The key synthesis is that court women can affect what later becomes the official or semi-official account even when their political legitimacy is expressed through family and virtue language.

## Key Claims
- Court women can influence history through access to palace records and ruler-centered memory.
- Historical authority in this pattern is tied to household position as well as textual skill.
- Empress Ma's `起居注` work links maternal governance to record production.
- Ban Zhao provides a later comparison for female participation in Eastern Han historiography.
- The source's "first female historian" judgment is significant but needs corroboration before becoming a wiki-wide universal claim.

- Female historian framing: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-750-jiemi-donghan-wanmei-nvxing-beihou-manshi-xisheng-lu-96lbdh7r0tsmiopbqqrj0q1gt|Hanji 750]] presents Empress Ma as a candidate for China's first female historian.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The current evidence is a podcast interpretation, not a dedicated historiographic source. The exact authorship, editorial scope, and genre history of `显宗起居注` need further source support before the wiki treats the "first female historian" label as settled.
