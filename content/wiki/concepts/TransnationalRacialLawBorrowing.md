# Transnational Racial-Law Borrowing

## Definition

Transnational racial-law borrowing is the selective study and adaptation of another jurisdiction's discriminatory categories, restrictions, enforcement practices, and legitimacy strategies for a new racial project.

## Current Synthesis

[[hitler-s-olympics-part-8-vater-it-is-to-be-fayetteville-hitler-s-olympics-part-8-vater-it-is-to-be-fayetteville|Hitler’s Olympics, Part 8]] presents the early Nazi encounter with American law as selective learning rather than simple imitation. Hitler invoked U.S. immigration restriction and national sovereignty, while [[HeinrichKrieger]] catalogued segregation, anti-miscegenation statutes, criminal law, state constitutions, racial definitions, reputation evidence, and judicial discretion.

What traveled was not a complete code. American rules varied by state, and German jurists evaluated them according to their own aims; some reportedly considered the one-drop rule too severe. The useful precedent was both technical and reputational: the United States showed how a respected state could make a racial group second-class, celebrate selected Black achievements, and retain international standing.

The strongest current judgment is therefore bounded. American law supplied examples, vocabulary, and administrative possibilities within an independently racist Nazi project. Influence does not mean identity, sole causation, or moral equivalence between the two systems.

## Key Claims

- Oppressive policy can circulate through comparative legal research as well as propaganda or diplomatic alliance.
- Borrowers select, reject, and recombine foreign practices rather than copying an entire system.
- Variation and discretion can be attractive when purported biological categories cannot be made objective.
- International legitimacy can function as a transferable policy resource alongside specific statutes.
- Cross-system influence should be traced without collapsing distinct regimes, histories, and outcomes.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source is a narrative episode summary rather than a full comparative legal archive. It establishes German attention to American examples but not a monocausal derivation of the [[NurembergLaws]]. Nazi antisemitism, German political institutions, European racial thought, party radicalization, and later genocidal development retain independent explanatory weight. Comparison identifies transmission and evaluation; it does not make Jim Crow and Nazi rule equivalent.

## What Changed

- Established selective legal borrowing as a mechanism connecting American racial governance to Nazi deliberation.
- Separated technical and reputational influence from complete copying or sole causation.
