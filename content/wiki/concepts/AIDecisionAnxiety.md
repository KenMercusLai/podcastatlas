# AI-Era Decision Anxiety / AI 时代决策焦虑

## Definition
AI-era decision anxiety is the pressure created when answers and possible actions become cheap and abundant while choosing the relevant problem, judging conditional fit, and committing finite time remain the user's responsibility.

## Current Synthesis
AI can reduce search friction without resolving uncertainty about what deserves pursuit. A model may return several plausible answers whose usefulness depends on unstated goals, domain conditions, and tradeoffs. The user therefore faces a higher-order task: formulate a concrete problem, recognize the knowledge needed to evaluate output, and accept that selecting one path closes or delays others.

The episode's practical answer is neither maximal tool adoption nor total withdrawal. Small experiments turn imagined possibilities into evidence; domain experience makes outputs more judgeable; and commitment converts information into consequences from which a person can learn. Observation remains legitimate, especially during rapid technical change, but endless option preservation can itself become an unchosen life path.

## Key Claims
- More answers can increase choice and verification burden even as retrieval becomes easier.
- Conditional correctness is not personal relevance; users need goals and domain context to judge fit.
- Tool learning can become avoidance when no concrete problem or desired outcome has been chosen.
- Practical trials reduce anxiety by replacing imagined possibilities with evidence about fit and limits.
- Commitment has opportunity cost, but refusing commitment also consumes irreversible time.
- AI literacy includes problem selection, evaluation, and responsibility for consequences—not only tool fluency.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The framework is a philosophical and practitioner interpretation from one conversation, not a clinical diagnosis or comparative study of anxiety. More options do not affect every person equally, and delayed commitment can preserve flexibility under genuinely immature technology. Domain expertise can improve evaluation without eliminating hallucination, hidden assumptions, or the need for external verification. Concrete action should not be romanticized when errors are costly or irreversible.
