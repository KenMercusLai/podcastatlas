# Variable Interest Entity / VIE 架构

A variable interest entity, or VIE, is the cross-border structure the source describes for many Chinese internet companies seeking overseas capital or listings. [[qiquan-zhe-zhang-bing-weishenme-yuelaiyue-nanchi-le-1]] explains a simplified chain: an offshore listing company, often in the Cayman Islands, connects through Hong Kong and a domestic foreign-owned enterprise to a Chinese operating company controlled by contracts rather than direct equity.

The source frames VIE as a legal and trust arrangement. It exists because some domestic internet licenses or businesses require Chinese ownership while overseas investors and future public shareholders need exposure to the economics of the operating company. That makes the contracts and practical relationship central to [[StartupGovernance]], not a footnote.

## Key Claims
- VIE separates formal equity ownership from practical control and economic benefit.
- The structure can let an offshore listing vehicle represent a domestic internet business to foreign investors.
- VIE credibility depends on the parties consistently treating the offshore and domestic entities as economically connected.
- A company that denies that connection in one context may weaken confidence in the structure in another context.
- The employee-equity version appears when [[EmployeeStockOptions]] are issued by the offshore vehicle while labor contracts sit with a domestic employer.
