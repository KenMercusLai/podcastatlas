# Police Killing Rate Divergence

Police killing rate divergence is the pattern in [[kyiv-brawl-ukraines-top-general-fired-6a609aec4fac21e67f1be165]] where police killings rise even as murder rates fall. [[DanielKnowles]] uses [[MappingPoliceViolence]] figures and the [[Albuquerque]] case to challenge the assumption that lower violent crime automatically means fewer deadly police encounters.

The source treats the divergence as institutional rather than purely criminal. Police killings can be shaped by crisis routing, tactical choices, weapons prevalence, training, accountability, and local culture, so they may not move in parallel with murder rates.

## Key Claims
- Falling murder rates do not guarantee falling police killings.
- Fatal police encounters can be driven by mental-health calls, overdose pressure, and low-level threat escalation as well as violent crime.
- Local departments can be outliers even when national crime trends improve.
- Data sources such as [[MappingPoliceViolence]] matter because official categories may not make the pattern obvious.
- The divergence creates public-trust risk when official accounts conflict with witnesses or body-camera evidence.
