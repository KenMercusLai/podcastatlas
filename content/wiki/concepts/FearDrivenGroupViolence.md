# Fear-Driven Group Violence / 恐惧驱动的群体暴力

## Definition
Fear-driven group violence / 恐惧驱动的群体暴力 is the episode's pattern for how uncertain threat, ritual, excitement, and collective identification can turn a group toward attack.

## Current Synthesis
In the Lord of the Flies reading, the "beast" is powerful because it remains uncertain. Jack's faction gives fear a ritual outlet through hunting, masks, chant, and sacrifice, while Simon's discovery of the dead parachutist cannot survive reentry into the group's storm-charged dance. The concept therefore treats group violence as produced by fear plus social form, not just by one bad leader or one bad child.

## Key Claims
- Ambiguous threat can become more socially useful than verified danger.
- Ritual can stabilize fear while also intensifying aggression.
- A crowd can convert the person carrying truth into the object to eliminate.
- Violence becomes easier when masks, chant, weather, and group movement blur individual responsibility.
- The pattern is literary evidence for civil-order fragility, not a complete social-psychology model.

## Evidence
- Ambiguous threat: [[vol-351-yingwang-21shiji-de-huangdao-gushi-haineng-zenme-kai-naodong-1983-nuojiang-dezhu-gkwriueodap1a3taaattkgol]] says the boys fear a beast no one has truly confirmed.
- Ritual outlet: [[vol-351-yingwang-21shiji-de-huangdao-gushi-haineng-zenme-kai-naodong-1983-nuojiang-dezhu-gkwriueodap1a3taaattkgol]] describes the pig-head offering, dance, chant, and Jack's hunting camp.
- Truth reversal: [[vol-351-yingwang-21shiji-de-huangdao-gushi-haineng-zenme-kai-naodong-1983-nuojiang-dezhu-gkwriueodap1a3taaattkgol]] says Simon returns with the beast's truth and is killed as if he were the beast.
- Responsibility blur: [[vol-351-yingwang-21shiji-de-huangdao-gushi-haineng-zenme-kai-naodong-1983-nuojiang-dezhu-gkwriueodap1a3taaattkgol]] foregrounds storm, circle, chant, and collective frenzy in the death scene.
- Source boundary: [[vol-351-yingwang-21shiji-de-huangdao-gushi-haineng-zenme-kai-naodong-1983-nuojiang-dezhu-gkwriueodap1a3taaattkgol]] keeps the discussion tied to the novel's symbols and plot.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode does not claim fear explains all violence in the book. Roger's direct cruelty, Jack's power competition, hunger for meat and pleasure, and broader rule failure all remain distinct elements.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to capture the Simon death and beast-rumor branch of the episode.
