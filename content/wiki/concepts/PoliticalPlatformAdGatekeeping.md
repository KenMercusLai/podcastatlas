## Definition
Political platform ad gatekeeping is the control that large digital platforms exercise when review systems classify, reject, restrict, or approve paid promotion connected to candidates, public figures, or broader social and political issues.

## Current Synthesis
Political-ad review reaches beyond campaign advertising when platforms classify documentary promotion or issue-based material as political. That breadth can serve transparency and abuse prevention, but it also gives a small number of companies practical power over media visibility. Automated or human review mistakes become especially consequential when platforms dominate distribution and have reasons to maintain favorable government relationships.

The source's documentary case does not establish political interference. Its stronger finding is epistemic: opaque review followed by reversal makes ordinary error difficult to distinguish from pressure, selective enforcement, or overbroad policy.

## Key Claims
- Political-content definitions can cover social issues and public figures beyond election advertisements.
- Initial rejection can materially affect reach even when a platform later reverses the decision.
- Concentrated distribution turns moderation workflow into a media-access decision.
- Opaque review systems make error, policy application, and political pressure difficult for outsiders to distinguish.
- Public reporting on rejection can create a Streisand effect that increases attention to the restricted work.

## Evidence
### Rejection and reversal
- [[tech-20261002-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261002-mp-tech-pod-128]] reports that Meta and YouTube initially rejected, then accepted, ads for Alex Gibney's Elon Musk documentary.

### Classification breadth
- [[tech-20261002-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261002-mp-tech-pod-128]] says platform definitions of political advertising can extend to social and political issues rather than only candidate campaigns.

### Visibility effect
- [[tech-20261002-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261002-mp-tech-pod-128]] interprets the controversy as potentially generating extra publicity for a documentary about concentrated power.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The platforms attributed the rejections to mistakes, and the source provides no internal review logs or evidence of government direction. A reversal may demonstrate a functioning appeal or correction process rather than censorship. One documentary case cannot establish systematic political bias across platform advertising systems.

## What Changed
- Established documentary promotion as a political-ad gatekeeping case.
- Added opacity and reversibility as separate governance dimensions.
- Preserved system error as a live explanation alongside political-pressure suspicion.
