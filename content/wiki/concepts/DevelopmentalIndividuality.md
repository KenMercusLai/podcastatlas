# Developmental Individuality

## Definition
Developmental individuality is the framework that a person's traits arise from inherited variation interacting with broadly defined experience through partly random development, rather than from separable or opposing forces called nature and nurture.

## Current Synthesis
The source replaces “nature versus nurture” with three interacting terms. Heritability describes how variation is distributed in a particular population and environment; experience includes fetal conditions, nutrition, illness, microbiome, light, temperature, training, peers, and culture; and stochastic development captures variation that appears even among genetically similar organisms in similar settings.

Examples span sensory receptors, myopia, sweat-gland innervation, vole fur density, perfect pitch, height, personality, brain wiring, adolescent behavior, schizophrenia concordance, and stable differences among genetically similar mice. Together they support a non-deterministic account: genes matter, environments calibrate development, people increasingly select environments that fit their dispositions, and none of these processes fully specifies the resulting individual. The same trait can have a different heritability estimate when environmental variation changes.

## Key Claims
- Heritability is a population-and-environment statistic, not the percentage of an individual's trait caused by genes.
- Experience includes biological and developmental conditions well beyond parenting or conscious learning.
- Genetic effects can depend on environment, as illustrated by diet-dependent phenylketonuria risk.
- Early environmental signals can calibrate physiology within one lifetime without requiring genetic evolution.
- Genetically similar animals and identical siblings can diverge through stochastic development.
- Shared family environment is not equivalent to all non-genetic influence, especially in personality research.
- Gene-environment correlation can strengthen across development as people select contexts compatible with their emerging traits.

## Evidence
- Population dependence - [[scim1807559844-scim1807559844]] contrasts highly heritable earwax type, non-heritable accent, and environment-sensitive height estimates.
- Developmental calibration - [[scim1807559844-scim1807559844]] uses outdoor light and myopia, childhood heat exposure and sweat-gland innervation, and maternal day-length signals in voles.
- Gene-experience interaction - [[scim1807559844-scim1807559844]] uses perfect pitch and phenylketonuria to reject purely inherited or purely learned accounts.
- Stochastic divergence - [[scim1807559844-scim1807559844]] cites identical armadillo quadruplets and inbred mice that differ in anatomy and behavior.
- Adolescent and adult divergence - [[how-genes-shape-your-risk-taking-morals-dr-kathryn-paige-harden-scim9105075356]] uses identical-twin schizophrenia discordance, stable personality differences in inbred mice, recombination, and increasing environmental selection to reject destiny.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources' examples differ in species, evidence type, and causal strength. Twin-study residual variance does not automatically measure developmental randomness, and “shared environment” in behavioral genetics is narrower than all shared family influence. Population heritability cannot determine an individual's capacity to change. The episode-attributed age trajectories for cognition and personality heritability, and the quoted schizophrenia concordance, require study-level context before generalization.

## What Changed
- Added adolescence, recombination, twin discordance, and active environment selection as routes from inherited similarity to divergent lives.
