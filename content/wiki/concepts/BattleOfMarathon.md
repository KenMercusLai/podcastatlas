# Battle of Marathon

## Definition

The Battle of Marathon is the 490 BC battle in which an Athenian and Plataean hoplite force attacked a Persian expeditionary army on the plain of Marathon after the [[IonianRevolt]], won the field, and then marched back to secure [[Athens]] against the Persian fleet.

## Current Synthesis

The paired Marathon sources make the battle the endpoint of a longer imperial and Ionian chain. [[CyrusTheGreat]]'s conquests bring Ionians under Persian rule; [[DariusI]] consolidates the [[AchaemenidEmpire]] through administration, terror, and imperial order ideology; [[Aristagoras]] turns a failed Naxos gamble into revolt; Athens and Eretria help burn [[Sardis]]; [[Miletus]] is destroyed; and Darius sends a punitive expedition that takes Naxos and Eretria before choosing Marathon for its bay, beach, and cavalry-friendly plain.

The battle itself is presented as a sequence of strategic choices rather than a simple heroic charge. The Athenians, guided by [[Miltiades]], march out to block the road to Athens and initially avoid open battle because they lack cavalry. The decisive opportunity comes when the Persian cavalry leaves and Ionian conscripts reportedly warn the Athenians that the Persian force is divided.

The battle plan is a high-risk use of hoplite strengths. Miltiades thins the center to match the Persian frontage, strengthens the wings, and orders an attack at dawn. The Persian center presses the weakened Athenian center, but the Greek wings break the Persian flanks and then turn inward. The outcome remains incomplete until the Athenians fight at the ships, lose senior leaders, and rapidly return to Athens in time to deter the Persian fleet at Phalerum.

Marathon's historical meaning is therefore double. It preserves Athens at a moment when defeat could have meant destruction, enslavement, exile, or restored tyranny, making later [[AthenianDemocracy]] and cultural influence possible. Yet the episode also treats the later "freedom versus despotism" reading as a memory construction that needs qualification by [[Herodotus]] dependence, Persian imperial perspective, and Athens's later imperial behavior.

## Key Claims

- Marathon is best understood as the result of the Ionian Revolt, Sardis, Miletus, and Darius's retaliation rather than as an isolated invasion.
- Persian choice of Marathon reflects naval access, cavalry terrain, punishment strategy, and possible Athenian internal fracture.
- The Athenian response begins as containment of the Persian landing force, not as immediate eagerness for open battle.
- The absence of Persian cavalry creates the tactical window for the Athenian attack.
- The Athenian line's strong wings and thinned center explain both early danger and eventual victory.
- Fighting at the ships and the march back to Athens are part of the battle's strategic completion.
- The battle is decisive for Athens's survival but does not end the Persian threat, and later memory simplifies the conflict.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The page is source-scoped to podcast reconstructions that depend heavily on Greek literary tradition, especially [[Herodotus]]. Darius's motives, Aristagoras's motives, Persian cavalry movements, Ionian intelligence, the signal to Athens, casualty figures, and counterfactual claims about Athens's destruction remain qualified. The sources also note that from a Persian imperial perspective the expedition may have looked like a peripheral setback rather than an existential disaster.
