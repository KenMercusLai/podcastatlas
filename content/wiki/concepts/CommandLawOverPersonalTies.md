# Command Law Over Personal Ties / 军令高于私交

## Definition
Command law over personal ties is the principle that a military officer applies a publicly stated command rule even when friendship, sympathy, or personal history creates a strong reason to make an exception.

## Current Synthesis
Hanji 1076 supplies a hard case through [[YuJin|于禁]] and [[ChangXiLateHan|昌豨]]. Chang Xi waits until encirclement and then surrenders specifically to an old acquaintance, apparently expecting their relationship to make submission survivable. Yu Jin instead invokes the rule that surrender after encirclement is not pardoned, rejects transfer to [[CaoCao|曹操]] as an escape from his duty, and executes Chang Xi after a personal farewell.

The case shows both the institutional value and the moral cost of impersonal enforcement. Predictable rules prevent private relationships from becoming privileged channels, but consistency can eliminate discretion even when surrender has already ended immediate resistance.

## Key Claims
- A rule is most institutionally revealing when it binds the enforcer against personal preference.
- Old acquaintance can create a surrender channel, but it does not necessarily create authority to waive the governing command.
- Referring a hard case upward may preserve mercy, yet an officer can interpret that move as evasion when the standing order is explicit.
- Visible consistency limits favoritism and makes military expectations more predictable across different actors.
- The same consistency can carry a moral and strategic cost by narrowing the incentive for late surrender.

## Evidence
- Personal conflict: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1076-ta-fanpan-caocao-sanci-zuizhong-jing-si-yu-jiuyou-shouzhong-lrulyifphhp2vao79ac5hky7srzv|Hanji 1076]] says Chang Xi seeks surrender from Yu Jin because they are old acquaintances and Yu Jin weeps at their farewell.
- Rule invocation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1076-ta-fanpan-caocao-sanci-zuizhong-jing-si-yu-jiuyou-shouzhong-lrulyifphhp2vao79ac5hky7srzv|Hanji 1076]] has Yu Jin cite the non-pardon rule for surrender after encirclement and reject the proposal to send Chang Xi to Cao Cao.
- Enforcement outcome: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1076-ta-fanpan-caocao-sanci-zuizhong-jing-si-yu-jiuyou-shouzhong-lrulyifphhp2vao79ac5hky7srzv|Hanji 1076]] records Chang Xi's execution despite the prior relationship and completed surrender.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This concept currently rests on one podcast's presentation of one episode. It should not imply that every command admits only mechanical enforcement, that Yu Jin lacked lawful discretion, or that execution necessarily produced the best long-run surrender incentives. The exact wording of the command, farewell scene, and Cao Cao's response remain source-scoped pending comparison with received texts.

## What Changed
- Created the concept from Hanji 1076's conflict between a standing surrender rule and Yu Jin's friendship with Chang Xi.
