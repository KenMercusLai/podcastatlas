## Definition

多方利益嵌入式政治经纪 / interest-embedded political brokerage is a source-attributed strategy in which a broker advances a client's request by placing it inside a sequence that also serves the ruler, intermediary, and likely opponents, so the desired concession appears to solve their problems rather than only reward the client.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-203-taihuangtaihou-lvzhi-sansha-zhaowang-lqblvnengllzwnfc1yjyy8b9v71g|Hanji 203]] presents [[TianZichunWesternHan|田子春]] as using two linked proposals. First, he gives a palace intermediary a personal incentive to surface [[LuZhi|吕雉]]'s reported wish to make [[LuLuWesternHan|吕禄]] a king through a ministerial petition. Second, after that grant produces resentment, he recommends making [[LiuZeLangyeKingWesternHan|刘泽]] king as symbolic reassurance to the Liu house and ministers.

The mechanism is therefore more than flattery or a single reframed request. Tian reportedly diagnoses several actors' incentives, sequences one grant so that it creates the need for another, declines an immediate share of the intermediary's reward, and moves Liu Ze beyond easy recall before Lu Zhi reconsiders. Success depends on access, timing, an available title and territory, and the ruler's willingness to treat symbolic balance as useful.

## Key Claims

- A broker can lower resistance by translating a client's private objective into a remedy for the decision-maker's existing political problem.
- An intermediary becomes active when institutional access is joined to a credible personal reward or threat.
- Sequential design matters: the first concession can create a legitimacy problem that the client's desired concession then appears to solve.
- Symbolic balance may be politically useful even when the compensating grant does not reverse the original redistribution of power.
- Timing and execution can matter as much as assent when a ruler may reconsider an already issued grant.
- Apparent multi-party benefit does not prove equal benefit, durable consent, or lawful procedure.

## Evidence

Ruler and intermediary incentives:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-203-taihuangtaihou-lvzhi-sansha-zhaowang-lqblvnengllzwnfc1yjyy8b9v71g|Hanji 203]] says Tian frames Lü Lu's kingship as relief for Lu Zhi's unspoken family objective and as a route to reward for the palace intermediary.

Compensating grant and execution:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-203-taihuangtaihou-lvzhi-sansha-zhaowang-lqblvnengllzwnfc1yjyy8b9v71g|Hanji 203]] says Tian answers resistance to Lü-family kingship by proposing Liu Ze's enfeoffment, then urges Liu Ze to leave Chang'an before the decree can be recalled.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept currently rests on one structured popular-history summary, not independent comparison with transmitted texts. The account attributes hidden wishes, ambition, foresight, regret, and causal effectiveness to the actors without establishing each from direct evidence. The supposed balance is also asymmetric: Lü Lu receives Zhao while Liu Ze's grant is presented as reassurance, and Langye is reportedly removed from Qi. A brokered package can redistribute grievance rather than eliminate it.

## What Changed

- Created the concept to distinguish a sequenced multi-party brokerage design from a single act of interest-reframing persuasion.
