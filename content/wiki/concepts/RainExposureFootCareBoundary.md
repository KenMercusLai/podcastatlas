# Rain Exposure and Foot-Care Boundary / 雨后蹚水足部照护边界

## Definition
The rain-exposure and foot-care boundary separates simple post-wading hygiene—leaving contaminated water, rinsing, drying, and changing wet footwear—from [[MaoZheTCMDoctor|毛哲]]'s source-scoped claims that rain introduces cold-damp or damp-heat and should be treated through warming, soaking, sweating, food, ginger, or 藿香正气.

## Current Synthesis
The episode supports a narrow, practical routine after commuting through rain or standing water: reduce exposure time, rinse dirt or contaminated water from the feet, dry skin and footwear, and do not remain in a warm, wet, occlusive shoe environment. This routine does not require diagnosing a TCM pattern and is compatible with the source's own emphasis on exposure duration, place, personal condition, and moderation.

The broader treatment layer remains uncertain. Warm soaking may feel comfortable, but hot water, impaired circulation, reduced sensation, broken skin, or vascular disease can change risk. The transcript does not show that direct fan air causes systemic illness, that sweating removes rain-related “dampness,” or that ginger, brown sugar, lamb, porridge, or 藿香正气 treats the consequences of wading. Symptoms, comorbidities, contamination, skin injury, and impairment therefore outrank a universal warming routine.

## Key Claims
- Prompt exit from contaminated water, rinsing, drying, and changing wet footwear form the episode's most transferable post-exposure routine.
- Prolonged moisture, warmth, occlusion, and dirty footwear can create local skin problems even when the shoe itself drains quickly.
- Foot soaking is an optional comfort practice, not a universal treatment; water temperature and vascular, sensory, skin, or metabolic risk matter.
- Cold-damp, damp-heat, fan-entry, induced-sweating, ginger, food-therapy, and patent-medicine claims are source-scoped TCM interpretations rather than established consequences or remedies of rain exposure.
- Persistent skin change, open wounds, fever, severe gastrointestinal symptoms, substantial pain, or worsening systemic illness should move the problem from routine self-care to qualified assessment.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source supplies no clinical comparison of drying methods, shoe materials, soaking, fan use, induced sweating, ginger, food therapy, or 藿香正气. It does not define safe soak temperatures, vascular contraindications, contamination severity, wound care, infection signs, diabetes-related foot risk, medicine interactions, or impairment duration. Its regional cold-damp and damp-heat account organizes the guest's TCM advice but cannot establish the cause of skin, gastrointestinal, vascular, or systemic symptoms after rain exposure.

- Separated local moisture and contamination concerns from source-scoped systemic cold-damp explanations.
- Added vascular, skin, systemic-symptom, medicine, and driving-safety qualifications.
