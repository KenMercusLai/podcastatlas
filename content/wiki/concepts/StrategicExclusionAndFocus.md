## Definition

Strategic exclusion and focus / 战略排除与聚焦 is the discipline of defining what an actor will not pursue so scarce time, force, capital, and attention can be concentrated on a coherent path.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 1083 introduces the concept through its reading of [[LiuBei|刘备]] before and after [[ZhugeLiang|诸葛亮]]. Strong operators and repeated activity do not by themselves create a stable position when resources move among disconnected targets and patrons. The episode therefore treats the [[LongzhongPlan|隆中对]] as valuable partly because it excludes premature confrontation with [[CaoCao|曹操]], rejects treating [[SunQuan|孙权]] as an immediate acquisition target, and concentrates territorial effort on [[Jingzhou|荆州]] and [[YizhouLateHan|益州]].

Exclusion is not permanent blindness. The same source says strategy is a dynamic guide, so the stable layer is a prioritized direction and resource logic, while routes and sequence must change when opponents, capacity, or timing change. This keeps focus distinct from rigidity.

## Key Claims

- A strategy becomes operational when it rules out plausible but resource-diluting actions.
- Execution strength cannot rescue a coalition whose actions do not accumulate toward a durable position.
- Exclusion should concentrate scarce resources on a base, partner structure, and long-horizon objective that reinforce one another.
- Stable direction can coexist with changes in operational sequence as force and timing change.
- Focus becomes dangerous when “do not” rules survive after their underlying conditions have expired.

- Execution-strategy gap: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1083-weihe-liubei-zaoqi-kong-you-dingji-zhanli-que-wu-lizu-zhi-di-lkrforfxvqjw-dl1ejvfkjivz3f3|Hanji 1083]] contrasts strong commanders with repeated dependence and lack of a durable base.
- Explicit exclusions: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1083-weihe-liubei-zaoqi-kong-you-dingji-zhanli-que-wu-lizu-zhi-di-lkrforfxvqjw-dl1ejvfkjivz3f3|Hanji 1083]] frames Cao Cao as an opponent not to confront directly and Sun Quan as a partner not to absorb.
- Concentrated path: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1083-weihe-liubei-zaoqi-kong-you-dingji-zhanli-que-wu-lizu-zhi-di-lkrforfxvqjw-dl1ejvfkjivz3f3|Hanji 1083]] links those exclusions to the Jingzhou-Yizhou base and conditional long-term restoration objective.
- Adaptation limit: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1083-weihe-liubei-zaoqi-kong-you-dingji-zhanli-que-wu-lizu-zhi-di-lkrforfxvqjw-dl1ejvfkjivz3f3|Hanji 1083]] explicitly describes strategy as adjustable rather than fixed doctrine.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept currently rests on one popular-history episode using modern business and personal-development analogies. Its claim that Liu Bei previously lacked strategy compresses earlier evidence of coalition building, portable political capital, and Xinye accumulation. The concept therefore distinguishes an integrated, exclusion-bearing program from the absence of all prior planning. It also does not prove that narrower scope is always superior: focus must still be tested against changing capacity, adversaries, geography, and timing.

## What Changed

- Established exclusion as a first-class strategic act rather than an implicit by-product of choosing goals.
- Separated adaptive focus from rigid persistence.
