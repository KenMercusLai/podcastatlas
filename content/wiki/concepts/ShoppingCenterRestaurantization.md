# Shopping-Center Restaurantization / 购物中心餐饮化

Shopping-center restaurantization is the shift described in [[vol-270-da-shi-dai-liu-zai-le-ta-de-shi-dai-1006063835]] where Chinese malls increased the role of restaurants after retail traffic and department-store logic weakened. The source says basements, underground levels, and edge spaces became better traffic positions as metro access, parking, and escalators improved, making foodservice a more central tenant strategy.

This shift undermines [[FoodRepublic|大食代]] because a landlord that understands restaurant brands, movement routes, licences, and tenant mix no longer needs an external [[FoodCourtSecondLandlord|food-court second landlord]] as much. A mall can lease directly to branded chains or run a food-court-like area itself, often earning more than it would from a whole-area sublease.

## Key Claims
- Restaurants can become traffic anchors rather than minor amenities.
- Underground or edge space can become valuable when transport and circulation patterns change.
- Landlord capability matters: foodservice leasing is operationally harder than retail leasing, but once learned it weakens intermediaries.
- Restaurantization does not kill food courts; it changes who organizes them and how much design or brand selection is expected.
