# Public-Figure Workplace Advice Satire

Public-figure workplace advice satire is the method used by the [[BartlebyColumn]] segment in [[settler-bet-a-west-bank-flashpoint-6a842bb539e73ea8f3c13a2d]]. [[AndrewPalmer]] introduces mock reader letters from thinly disguised public figures and institutions, and Bartleby answers them as if they were ordinary office problems.

The technique works by shrinking headline controversy into familiar management patterns: failing to hold pre-meetings, having two bosses with conflicting priorities, needing recovery time after a major role, insulting superiors while claiming exceptional competence, and losing thinking time to constant meetings. The source extends [[UpwardManagement]] and [[WorkplacePacing]] through satire rather than direct career advice.

## Key Claims
- A public controversy can become more legible when translated into an everyday management problem.
- Satire can preserve ambiguity by using disguised names, which is why the wiki should keep the allusions source-scoped.
- The advice format works because powerful institutions still contain ordinary coordination failures: unclear authority, bad meeting design, impatience, flattery, and time scarcity.
