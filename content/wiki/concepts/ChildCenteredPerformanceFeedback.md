# Child-Centered Performance Feedback / 儿童中心的表现反馈

## Definition

Child-centered performance feedback describes a child's observable choices, effort, ideas, and change over time without turning one answer, artwork, score, win, or loss into a global judgment of worth or ability.

## Current Synthesis

[[ep94-meiyou-nage-haizi-tiansheng-jiushi-xionghaizi-liaoliao-zhongmei-de-zaoqi-jiaoyu-xia-wkgmbv3qpbud6lata67q4kiompu513|EP94]] links feedback language to evaluation design. Specific observations about color, shape, composition, effort, or a child's idea give usable recognition while leaving room for the child to disagree with an adult suggestion. In classroom work, acknowledging an attempted answer before offering a prompt keeps error from becoming a reason to stop participating.

The same logic applies to comparison. Private records can help a child compare current performance with past performance, while public scores and rankings amplify shame and status pressure. Where competition remains, adults can acknowledge disappointment, offer a calming practice, frame a single result as temporary, encourage learning from the winner, and teach winners to respond without humiliation.

## Key Claims

- Specific descriptive feedback provides more information than global praise or dismissal.
- Recognition of an attempt can preserve participation after error.
- A child's work should remain the child's expression rather than a parent-produced status object.
- Private self-comparison can support growth without the shame effects of public ranking.
- Winning and losing require emotional teaching, not denial of disappointment or permanent identity labels.
- Adult suggestions should not automatically override a child's non-safety-related creative choice.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode provides practical examples rather than controlled evidence about praise, ranking, creativity, or competition across ages and cultures. Descriptive feedback can still become intrusive or manipulative if adults narrate every action, and private records can still create pressure when tied to rigid targets. Competition is not inherently harmful, while removing public rankings does not by itself remove scarce opportunities or wider status comparison.

## What Changed

- Created a feedback frame joining descriptive recognition, child authorship, error tolerance, self-comparison, and emotional support around competition.
