# Evolutionary Mating Strategies

## Definition
Evolutionary mating strategies are context-dependent patterns of mate choice, competition, courtship, commitment, short-term sex, retention, and switching interpreted through sexual selection and recurrent reproductive problems.

## Current Synthesis
[[DavidBuss]] presents human mating as mutual choice rather than one sex choosing while the other merely competes. Both women and men have preferences, both compete for desirable partners, and strategies change across long-term bonding, short-term mating, affairs, breakups, and re-pairing.

The source reports shared long-term preferences for intelligence, kindness, mutual attraction, love, health, dependability, and emotional stability, alongside average sex differences in status-resource cues versus youth-attractiveness cues. These are distributions, not personal destinies. Culture, life stage, relationship context, individual priorities, and pair-specific histories can change or outweigh an average tendency.

## Key Claims
- Human sexual selection combines mutual preferential choice with competition within each sex.
- Long-term and short-term mating contexts change which traits receive weight and what risks each person accepts.
- Shared long-term preferences coexist with average sex differences rather than eliminating overlap.
- Status, attention, and mate-choice copying can make desirability socially comparative.
- Deception often targets the traits a prospective partner is thought to value.
- Modern apps and pornography can amplify or redirect older motives in novel environments.

## Evidence
- Core model: [[how-humans-select-keep-romantic-partners-in-short-long-term-dr-david-buss-scim4159413839]] distinguishes intrasexual competition from preferential mate choice and says both operate for women and men.
- Long-term preferences: [[how-humans-select-keep-romantic-partners-in-short-long-term-dr-david-buss-scim4159413839]] reports shared preferences plus average differences around resources, status, ambition, attractiveness, and age.
- Context shift: [[how-humans-select-keep-romantic-partners-in-short-long-term-dr-david-buss-scim4159413839]] says short-term contexts can raise the salience of appearance, confidence, risk-taking, and sexual variety.
- Social information: [[how-humans-select-keep-romantic-partners-in-short-long-term-dr-david-buss-scim4159413839]] discusses attention structure and mate-choice copying as signals of status and desirability.
- Environmental mismatch: [[how-humans-select-keep-romantic-partners-in-short-long-term-dr-david-buss-scim4159413839]] argues that online dating and pornography create cues and opportunities unlike small-group ancestral settings.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode note does not provide the methods or effect sizes needed to judge each reported difference. [[IdiosyncraticAttraction]] qualifies broad rankings by showing how repeated contact can produce pair-specific preference, while [[PerceivedSimilarityInRelationships]] distinguishes felt fit from abstract trait matching. Evolutionary interpretations also compete with cultural, developmental, economic, and situational explanations and should not be used to stereotype an individual.

## What Changed
- Created a mutual-choice model separating long-term, short-term, retention, and switching contexts.
- Added social-comparison and modern-environment qualifications to average preference claims.
