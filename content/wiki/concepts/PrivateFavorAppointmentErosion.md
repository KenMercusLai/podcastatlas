## Definition

私恩任官侵蚀 / Private-Favor Appointment Erosion is the institutional failure in which titles, offices, and recommendation channels are used to repay intimacy, kinship, palace service, or near-attendant influence, weakening office as a public trust.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-814-dangguan-buwei-min-zuozhu-buru-huijia-mai-hongshu-lqlrfpuenzhr1tbrdd3urtolfw2p|Hanji 814]] grounds the concept through [[LiGuLateHan|李固]]'s 133 CE disaster-policy answer to [[EmperorShunOfHan|汉顺帝]]. Li Gu does not deny the ruler's ability to show private gratitude: he explicitly treats money gifts as less dangerous than public appointment. His target is the conversion of personal favor into public office, because even minor offices can harm common people when they are granted without merit or institutional limit.

The episode's examples form a chain. [[SongELateHan|宋娥]] and the Liang empress's relatives represent wet-nurse and outer-relative privilege; attendants' and Shangshu-adjacent children represent near-body and document-channel pressure; the Mingdi and Guantao princess precedent shows a ruler choosing cash gift over office appointment. Together, the concept links [[PalaceProximityPower|宫廷近身权力]] to [[AppointmentQualityAsStateCapacity|任官适任性国家能力]]: private favor becomes dangerous when it bypasses the fitness tests that let office serve the public.

## Key Claims

- Public office differs from private reward because officeholders can directly affect households, local administration, and legal order.
- Personal gratitude is institutionally safer when expressed as bounded gifts rather than as titles, salaries, or governing authority.
- Empress-kin, wet-nurse, eunuch, attendant, and Shangshu-family channels can pressure local recommendation systems even when no explicit order is issued.
- Small offices still matter because a poorly chosen holder can create public harm below the court's visibility.
- Appointment erosion is cumulative: one exception can become a precedent that weakens standards for the next favor request.
- The concept is a precondition for broader [[CourtOfficeCapture|court office capture]] rather than identical with full armed or monetary capture of offices.

## Evidence

- Gift-office distinction: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-814-dangguan-buwei-min-zuozhu-buru-huijia-mai-hongshu-lqlrfpuenzhr1tbrdd3urtolfw2p|Hanji 814]] says Li Gu allows that Shun may reward favored intimates with money while warning against granting titles and offices outside proper limits.
- Wet-nurse and Liang-kin privilege: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-814-dangguan-buwei-min-zuozhu-buru-huijia-mai-hongshu-lqlrfpuenzhr1tbrdd3urtolfw2p|Hanji 814]] identifies Song E and the Liang empress's relatives as examples of private closeness turning into public power.
- Near-attendant children: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-814-dangguan-buwei-min-zuozhu-buru-huijia-mai-hongshu-lqlrfpuenzhr1tbrdd3urtolfw2p|Hanji 814]] has Li Gu argue that children of attendants, Shangshu, and palace officials should not directly become officials or be pushed through Xiaolian recommendations.
- Precedent logic: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-814-dangguan-buwei-min-zuozhu-buru-huijia-mai-hongshu-lqlrfpuenzhr1tbrdd3urtolfw2p|Hanji 814]] uses Mingdi's refusal to appoint Guantao princess's son as proof that office is more institutionally sensitive than money.
- Cumulative erosion: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-814-dangguan-buwei-min-zuozhu-buru-huijia-mai-hongshu-lqlrfpuenzhr1tbrdd3urtolfw2p|Hanji 814]] says that apparently small appointment mistakes can gradually break the court's rules.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept is grounded in one source note and should remain narrower than a general theory of corruption. Hanji 814 preserves Li Gu's own boundary: not every private favor is equally damaging, and money gifts are less dangerous than office grants. The episode also does not independently prove the later performance records of Wuxuan, Yang Di, or every Liang-family appointee. Its claim is institutional rather than biographical: the public meaning of office changes when appointment follows private closeness rather than tested fit.

## What Changed

- Established a narrow Shun-era concept for Li Gu's distinction between private reward and public office.
- Separated personal favor as gift from personal favor as appointment authority.
- Linked wet-nurse, outer-relative, eunuch, attendant, and Shangshu-family privilege to appointment-quality failure.
- Kept Wuxuan, Yang Di, and detailed Liang-family names source-scoped rather than creating weak entity pages.
