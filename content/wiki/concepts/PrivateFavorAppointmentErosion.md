## Definition

私恩任官侵蚀 / Private-Favor Appointment Erosion is the institutional failure in which titles, offices, and recommendation channels are used to repay intimacy, kinship, palace service, or near-attendant influence, weakening office and title as public trusts.

## Current Synthesis

The earliest current Emperor An layer shows private-favor appointment erosion as indirect procedural pressure rather than only a completed appointment. In [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-799-donghan-mingchen-yangzhen-ruhe-jujue-zou-houmen-lmufb7lvb-kzsvjtn6iu-zrpiptf|Hanji 799]], [[YangZhenLateHan|杨震]] refuses [[GengBaoGrandGeneralLateHan|耿宝]]'s request to recommend [[LiRunLateHan|李润]]'s elder brother because no imperial edict authorizes the appointment, then also refuses [[YanXianLateHan|阎显]]'s request for a close person. The pressure is indirect but institutionally serious: palace trust, outer-relative status, and guessed imperial intent are being used as substitutes for formal procedure.

The same source also connects appointment erosion to wet-nurse privilege and favorite solicitation. [[EmperorAnOfHan|汉安帝]] enfeoffs [[WangShengPalaceActorLateHan|王圣]] and orders a mansion for her; Yang objects that disasters, poverty, frontier war, and empty treasury make the construction improper. He then criticizes [[FanFengEunuchLateHan|樊丰]], [[ZhouGuangLateHan|周广]], [[XieYunLateHan|谢恽]], and other favored actors for partying for private benefit, soliciting from local governments, accepting bribes, and letting barred corrupt officials occupy important posts.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-813-gudai-de-gaoguan-jing-ye-hui-bei-ouda-ln-dw3xohnmpavkpu-bdvasvietv|Hanji 813]] gives the concept a Shun-era title-boundary layer. [[ZuoXiongLateHan|左雄]] argues that [[SongELateHan|宋娥]] and [[LiangJiLateHan|梁冀]] should not receive marquisate through wet-nurse service or Liang-family intimacy. His boundary is not anti-gratitude: material support for Song E is acceptable, but landed title changes public authority and violates the meaning of Han title rules.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-814-dangguan-buwei-min-zuozhu-buru-huijia-mai-hongshu-lqlrfpuenzhr1tbrdd3urtolfw2p|Hanji 814]] broadens the concept through [[LiGuLateHan|李固]]'s 133 CE disaster-policy answer to [[EmperorShunOfHan|汉顺帝]]. Li Gu likewise distinguishes money gifts from public appointment, then extends the warning from wet nurses and Liang relatives to attendants' children, Shangshu-adjacent families, and other near-court actors. Even a small office can harm the people when its holder enters through favor rather than tested fit.

Together, the sources make the concept a boundary between private gratitude and public authority. [[PalaceProximityPower|宫廷近身权力]], wet-nurse service, outer-relative pressure, eunuch trust, and document-channel access become dangerous when they bypass the fitness tests that let office and title serve the public.

## Key Claims

- Public office and public title differ from private reward because they change land, authority, households, local administration, and legal order.
- Private-favor appointment pressure can be soft and deniable, using guessed imperial intent, kinship prestige, or palace trust without a written order.
- Personal gratitude is institutionally safer when expressed as bounded gifts rather than as titles, salaries, or governing authority.
- Empress-kin, wet-nurse, eunuch, attendant, and Shangshu-family channels can pressure title and appointment systems even when no explicit order is issued.
- Small offices and narrow title exceptions still matter because precedent weakens standards for the next favor request.
- Appointment erosion is cumulative: one exception can become a rule-like precedent that makes later correction harder.
- The concept is a precondition for broader [[CourtOfficeCapture|court office capture]] rather than identical with full armed or monetary capture of offices.

## Evidence

- Procedural refusal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-799-donghan-mingchen-yangzhen-ruhe-jujue-zou-houmen-lmufb7lvb-kzsvjtn6iu-zrpiptf|Hanji 799]] says Yang Zhen refuses Geng Bao's request for Li Run's elder brother and Yan Xian's request because the requests lack imperial-edict authority.
- Favorite solicitation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-799-donghan-mingchen-yangzhen-ruhe-jujue-zou-houmen-lmufb7lvb-kzsvjtn6iu-zrpiptf|Hanji 799]] says Yang criticizes Fan Feng, Zhou Guang, Xie Yun, and other favored actors for private-benefit partying, bribery, and placing barred corrupt officials into key posts.
- Wet-nurse title and construction: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-799-donghan-mingchen-yangzhen-ruhe-jujue-zou-houmen-lmufb7lvb-kzsvjtn6iu-zrpiptf|Hanji 799]] says Emperor An enfeoffs Wang Sheng as Yewang Jun and orders a mansion for her despite Yang's criticism.
- Title-boundary objection: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-813-gudai-de-gaoguan-jing-ye-hui-bei-ouda-ln-dw3xohnmpavkpu-bdvasvietv|Hanji 813]] says Zuo Xiong opposes enfeoffing Song E and Liang Shang's son because unmerited marquisates violate Han public-title rules.
- Gift-title distinction: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-813-gudai-de-gaoguan-jing-ye-hui-bei-ouda-ln-dw3xohnmpavkpu-bdvasvietv|Hanji 813]] says Zuo permits money support for Song E while warning against the more damaging grant of land and title.
- Gift-office distinction: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-814-dangguan-buwei-min-zuozhu-buru-huijia-mai-hongshu-lqlrfpuenzhr1tbrdd3urtolfw2p|Hanji 814]] says Li Gu allows that Shun may reward favored intimates with money while warning against granting titles and offices outside proper limits.
- Near-attendant children: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-814-dangguan-buwei-min-zuozhu-buru-huijia-mai-hongshu-lqlrfpuenzhr1tbrdd3urtolfw2p|Hanji 814]] has Li Gu argue that children of attendants, Shangshu, and palace officials should not directly become officials or be pushed through Xiaolian recommendations.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept should remain narrower than a general theory of corruption. Hanji 799, Hanji 813, and Hanji 814 all preserve the same boundary: not every private favor is equally damaging, and bounded support or gifts are less dangerous than title or office grants. Hanji 799 does not prove that Li Run personally initiated the request for his elder brother, nor does it settle Wang Sheng's own decision-making. The claim is institutional rather than biographical: the public meaning of title and office changes when grants follow private closeness rather than merit, edict procedure, or tested fit.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 799 as an earlier Emperor An layer with Yang Zhen refusing appointment requests from Geng Bao and Yan Xian.
- Added Wang Sheng's wet-nurse title and mansion as a pre-Shun example of private favor entering title and expenditure.
- Added Fan Feng, Zhou Guang, and Xie Yun's solicitation and barred-official placement to the concept's appointment-abuse evidence.
- Preserved Zuo Xiong's Hanji 813 title-boundary argument and Li Gu's Hanji 814 broader appointment warning.
