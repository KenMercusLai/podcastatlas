## Definition
Historical image narrative construction is the process by which selection, omission, editing, music, captioning, institutional authority, and repetition turn recorded material into an apparently coherent account of public history.

## Current Synthesis
Images do not simply preserve an event; institutions arrange them into claims about what happened, who deserves recognition, and what should disappear. The bounded source uses *Man of Marble* to contrast exhausted workers and difficult conditions in raw material with their removal from polished propaganda, and *Katyń* to show documentary imagery and political discourse reallocating responsibility for mass killing. Counter-cinema can reveal construction by placing finished images beside excluded material and by making investigation itself part of the story.

## Key Claims
- Recorded footage becomes evidence only through a framing and authorization process.
- Omission and sequencing can be as politically consequential as fabrication.
- Documentary form can manufacture state memory as well as challenge it.
- Exposing the production chain helps audiences distinguish an image from the historical claim built around it.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept does not imply that all edited historical imagery is propaganda or that raw footage provides interpretation-free truth. Film readings should be checked against the works themselves, and conclusions about state campaigns require archival evidence beyond a podcast summary. The Katyń casualty figure and timeline remain source-scoped here.
