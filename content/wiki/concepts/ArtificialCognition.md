# Artificial Cognition

Artificial cognition is [[SteveShaw]]'s term in [[tech-20260408-0408-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260408-0408-mp-tech-pod-128]] for AI reasoning that enters a human decision process. Shaw frames it as "system three": a third layer beside fast intuitive thinking and slower deliberative thinking.

The concept is useful because it separates external model reasoning from the user's own cognition. [[ChatGPT]] can supply an answer, explanation, or recommendation, but once that output appears inside a choice, the human decision is no longer only a fast-versus-slow psychology problem. It also includes whether the user treats AI output as evidence, suggestion, shortcut, or authority.

Artificial cognition is not automatically bad. The Marketplace Tech episode says Shaw uses AI daily and allows students to use it. The risk appears when artificial cognition becomes the first or only reasoning path, producing [[CognitiveSurrender]] instead of an inspected aid to [[HumanJudgmentUnderAI]].

## Key Claims
- AI adds an external cognitive layer to decision-making rather than only speeding up search or drafting.
- "System three" is source terminology for that AI-mediated layer, not a general claim that AI has humanlike consciousness.
- Artificial cognition can help structure work when the user has already formed a goal, hypothesis, or draft.
- It can undermine judgment when users accept AI conclusions without enough independent reasoning or review.
- In learning contexts, artificial cognition should usually follow [[FirstDraftThinking]] rather than replace the learner's first attempt.
- In autonomous workflows, artificial cognition needs review gates because AI may act over tasks before a person has challenged its reasoning.
