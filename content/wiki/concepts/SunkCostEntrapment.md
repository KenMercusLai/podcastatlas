## Definition

Sunk cost entrapment is the pattern in which prior investment of money, time, status, intimacy, identity, effort, or battlefield progress makes continued commitment feel preferable even though the past cost cannot be recovered by the present decision.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 556 supplies the extractive-relationship version: isolation and hope make repeated payments to a false restoration channel harder to stop because exit would force recognition that earlier spending and humiliation produced nothing. The [[XiaoAoJiangHu|《笑傲江湖》]] source extends the mechanism to identity-protective loyalty, where disconfirming evidence threatens a person's account of a life choice as well as the relationship.

EP95 adds trading loss chasing. A prior loss does not create a claim on future market gains, but the goal of “earning it back” can turn new income, borrowed funds, or a moved stop into fresh exposure. The vol.58 career case shows that even a successful path can become sunk-cost pressure when the next decade is used to prove the earlier choice was correct.

The military version appears when [[GuanYu|关羽]] learns of a threat to his rear but does not immediately abandon the Fancheng objective. Proximity to a valued result may raise the psychological cost of retreat, but the case is explicitly qualified: he also reportedly trusts the defenses at Jiangling and Gong'an, and the forward objective may still appear strategically valuable. Across all domains, the corrective rule is forward-looking judgment about present prospects, future downside, and exit conditions, not automatic abandonment.

## Key Claims

- Past cost is irreversible and should not by itself determine the next commitment.
- Sunk cost can defend identity, status, hope, and strategic narrative as well as money.
- Loss chasing or doubling down creates a new risk; it does not logically reverse the prior loss.
- Near-success can intensify entrapment because withdrawal feels like forfeiting an almost-achieved result.
- Power imbalance, coercion, incomplete information, and genuinely positive forward prospects can coexist with sunk-cost pressure.
- Precommitted stopping conditions and current-information review protect later decisions from escalation driven by identity or consistency.

## Evidence

### Extractive promise under isolation
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-556-lishi-hanjian-shui-yao-gei-chengdi-dai-lvmaozi-licvtkyvoh-p3ucobowldj6g-id|Hanji 556]] says Empress Xu repeatedly gives Chunyu Zhang valuables while waiting for a restoration promise the host treats as empty.

### Identity-protective relationship investment
- [[lkmtkpbafg5n5lcknxvjl8g6hfg2-lkmtkpbafg5n5lcknxvjl8g6hfg2]] interprets Yue Lingshan's continued defense of Lin Pingzhi through care, marriage, incomplete knowledge, and the cost of admitting that a life-defining choice may have failed.

### Trading loss chasing
- [[ep95-fx-zhanshi-jiuliumei-mama-kuidiao-de-qian-wo-yao-qinshou-zhuan-huilai-lgdurd85j7_cbimza4cegor-jfly|EP95]] treats the need to win back a loss, reinvest income, or move a stop as ways past loss can create new exposure.

### Successful-path continuation
- [[vol-58-youwenbida-cong-1-dao-0-ruhe-fangqi-que-bu-fouding-ziji-ljf7qgb2ckoql1xo5bkcqq6pgcin|vol.58]] says an established career can remain valuable without obligating the next fifteen years and redirects judgment toward future cost, value, and preset exits.

### Near-success under changing battlefield risk
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1169-2026-gai-zenme-shou-zhu-zishen-de-fuqi-lu-3dpvj0nbqhise2aqx-a3zi3kp|Hanji 1169]] interprets Guan Yu's continued Fancheng commitment after learning of Sun Quan's move as possible sunk-cost pressure, while also reporting his confidence in rear defenses.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Delayed exit is not automatically irrational. A person may face danger, incomplete information, genuine attachment, illiquidity, transaction cost, family responsibility, retraining cost, an adversary's uncertain intent, or a still-positive forward prospect. Empress Xu's motives remain source-scoped; Yue Lingshan's knowledge and exits are disputed; a losing trade may recover for reasons independent of prior loss; a mature career may still offer the best future value; and Guan Yu's decision may reflect strategic belief rather than psychological entrapment. Sunk cost is an interpretive mechanism, not a clinical diagnosis or proof that continued commitment is mistaken.

## What Changed

- Extended the concept to near-success under changing military risk.
- Added a sharper boundary between sunk-cost pressure and a still-positive forward judgment.
- Preserved precommitted stopping conditions and present-information review as the corrective rule.
