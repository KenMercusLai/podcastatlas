# Indigenous Alliances in Spanish Conquest

## Definition

Indigenous alliances in Spanish conquest were strategic coalitions in which local rulers and communities supplied troops, intelligence, logistics, legitimacy, and political access to Spanish-led campaigns for reasons rooted in their own rivalries and survival calculations.

## Current Synthesis

[[649-the-fall-of-the-incas-the-last-emperor-part-6-glt4505534064]] makes Indigenous participation causally central in Peru. Paullu Inca changes allegiance after Las Salinas, receives property and heraldic recognition, and assists Gonzalo Pizarro with thousands of warriors; Huanca and Cañari groups also appear as Spanish allies, targets of [[MancoInca|Manco's]] retaliation, or participants in colonial violence.

This evidence defeats a Spaniards-alone story without turning alliance into free or stable consent. Communities operated amid Inca imperial legacies, civil war, Spanish coercion, rival claims, immediate danger, and opportunities for status or survival. Their agency shaped conquest, but Spanish victory later embedded a colonial hierarchy that could dispossess and exploit allies as well as enemies.

## Key Claims

- Spanish forces depended materially on Indigenous troops and local political networks.
- Indigenous actors changed sides and negotiated rewards rather than belonging to one unified bloc.
- Alliance choices reflected rivalries, survival, status, coercion, and strategic calculation.
- Manco's retaliation against Spanish-aligned communities made conquest an Indigenous civil conflict as well as an invasion.
- Recognizing Indigenous agency does not make the resulting colonial order equal or consensual.
- Alliance evidence is necessary for comparing conquest in Peru with conquest in Mexico.

## Evidence

- Paullu's alignment: [[649-the-fall-of-the-incas-the-last-emperor-part-6-glt4505534064]] describes his rapid switch after Las Salinas and subsequent Spanish-style rewards.
- Military dependence: [[649-the-fall-of-the-incas-the-last-emperor-part-6-glt4505534064]] reports Paullu and thousands of Indigenous warriors supporting Gonzalo Pizarro at Cochabamba and on the Vilcabamba expedition.
- Conflict among Indigenous groups: [[649-the-fall-of-the-incas-the-last-emperor-part-6-glt4505534064]] identifies Huanca targets and Cañari participation in Spanish operations and violence.
- Comparative mechanism: [[649-the-fall-of-the-incas-the-last-emperor-part-6-glt4505534064]] repeatedly places small Spanish forces inside much larger Indigenous coalition systems.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode names only some allied groups and gives limited access to their own records, internal divisions, aims, or later outcomes. Numerical totals and named loyalties remain source-scoped. “Alliance” can conceal coercion and asymmetric bargaining, while “collaboration” can impose a later moral judgment on actors navigating several competing powers.

## What Changed

- Established Indigenous coalition power as a necessary explanation of conquest in Peru.
- Qualified alliance as strategic agency under unequal and coercive conditions.
