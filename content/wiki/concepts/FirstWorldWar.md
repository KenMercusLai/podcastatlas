# First World War

## Definition

The First World War is the 1914-1918 conflict that the Gallipoli episodes enter through the [[GallipoliCampaign|Gallipoli campaign]] and its failed attempt to break strategic deadlock by attacking the Dardanelles.

## Current Synthesis

The wiki's current First World War page is intentionally narrow. [[675-the-first-world-war-slaughter-at-gallipoli-part-5-glt4394132715]] adds the war's coalition and imperial opening: Ottoman alignment with Germany, Russian pressure after Sarikamish, British fear about imperial prestige, and a peripheral operation meant to avoid Western Front attrition. [[676-the-first-world-war-churchills-calamity-part-6-glt1275431911]] then shows how that offensive problem traveled beyond the Western Front: trenches, machine guns, barbed wire, and defensive firepower defeated confidence in speed, dash, and breakthrough.

It also adds a political and memory layer. First World War campaigning in this source reshapes careers and national identities: [[WinstonChurchill]] is damaged, [[MustafaKemalAtaturk|Mustafa Kemal / Ataturk]] rises, and [[AnzacSpirit]] becomes a civic memory in Australia and New Zealand.

## Key Claims

- First World War offensive failure was not confined to the Western Front.
- Ottoman entry and Russia's Caucasus crisis helped make the Dardanelles look strategically useful to Allied leaders.
- Gallipoli reproduced trench, machine-gun, barbed-wire, and attrition dynamics in a different theater.
- Coalition politics and press scandal could shape campaign continuation as much as battlefield analysis.
- Disease, sanitation, weather, and terrain formed part of the war's human cost.
- Defeat could later be remembered as identity formation, especially through Anzac memory.

## Evidence

- Coalition and imperial context: [[675-the-first-world-war-slaughter-at-gallipoli-part-5-glt4394132715]] connects Ottoman entry, Russian diversion requests, British imperial prestige, and Balkan-neutral calculations to the Dardanelles decision.
- Offensive problem: [[675-the-first-world-war-slaughter-at-gallipoli-part-5-glt4394132715]] shows the April landings immediately meeting modern defensive firepower, while [[676-the-first-world-war-churchills-calamity-part-6-glt1275431911]] compares Gallipoli to the Western and Italian fronts through trenches, machine guns, barbed wire, and failed offensive assumptions.
- Political consequence: [[676-the-first-world-war-churchills-calamity-part-6-glt1275431911]] links the Dardanelles to Churchill's removal, coalition crisis, and delayed response to Hamilton.
- Memory consequence: [[676-the-first-world-war-churchills-calamity-part-6-glt1275431911]] connects Gallipoli to Ataturk's later reputation and Anzac public memory.
