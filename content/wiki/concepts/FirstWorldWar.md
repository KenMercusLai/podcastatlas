# First World War

## Definition

The First World War is the 1914-1918 conflict that the wiki currently enters through [[EdithCavell|Edith Cavell]]'s occupied-Belgium case and the [[GallipoliCampaign|Gallipoli campaign]].

## Current Synthesis

The wiki's current First World War page is still selective rather than general. [[674-the-first-world-war-the-spy-who-took-on-the-germans-part-4-glt1889262052]] adds the war behind the front line: occupied [[Belgium]], German reprisals and labor coercion, escape networks, military law, and the propaganda afterlife of a nurse's execution. [[675-the-first-world-war-slaughter-at-gallipoli-part-5-glt4394132715]] adds the war's coalition and imperial opening: Ottoman alignment with Germany, Russian pressure after Sarikamish, British fear about imperial prestige, and a peripheral operation meant to avoid Western Front attrition. [[676-the-first-world-war-churchills-calamity-part-6-glt1275431911]] then shows how that offensive problem traveled beyond the Western Front: trenches, machine guns, barbed wire, and defensive firepower defeated confidence in speed, dash, and breakthrough.

It also adds a political and memory layer. First World War campaigning and occupation reshape reputations and public symbols: Cavell becomes a martyr-propaganda figure, [[WinstonChurchill]] is damaged, [[MustafaKemalAtaturk|Mustafa Kemal / Ataturk]] rises, and [[AnzacSpirit]] becomes a civic memory in Australia and New Zealand.

## Key Claims

- First World War offensive failure was not confined to the Western Front.
- Occupation and resistance made non-frontline civilian and medical spaces part of the war's moral and legal battlefield.
- Ottoman entry and Russia's Caucasus crisis helped make the Dardanelles look strategically useful to Allied leaders.
- Gallipoli reproduced trench, machine-gun, barbed-wire, and attrition dynamics in a different theater.
- Coalition politics and press scandal could shape campaign continuation as much as battlefield analysis.
- Public memory and propaganda could convert battlefield or occupation events into national symbols.

## Evidence

- Occupation and resistance: [[674-the-first-world-war-the-spy-who-took-on-the-germans-part-4-glt1889262052]] connects Belgium's occupation, escape routes to the Netherlands, Cavell's nursing clinic, German military law, and Allied propaganda into one occupation-war case.
- Coalition and imperial context: [[675-the-first-world-war-slaughter-at-gallipoli-part-5-glt4394132715]] connects Ottoman entry, Russian diversion requests, British imperial prestige, and Balkan-neutral calculations to the Dardanelles decision.
- Offensive problem: [[675-the-first-world-war-slaughter-at-gallipoli-part-5-glt4394132715]] shows the April landings immediately meeting modern defensive firepower, while [[676-the-first-world-war-churchills-calamity-part-6-glt1275431911]] compares Gallipoli to the Western and Italian fronts through trenches, machine guns, barbed wire, and failed offensive assumptions.
- Political consequence: [[676-the-first-world-war-churchills-calamity-part-6-glt1275431911]] links the Dardanelles to Churchill's removal, coalition crisis, and delayed response to Hamilton.
- Memory consequence: [[674-the-first-world-war-the-spy-who-took-on-the-germans-part-4-glt1889262052]] follows Cavell from execution into Allied propaganda and memorial culture, while [[676-the-first-world-war-churchills-calamity-part-6-glt1275431911]] connects Gallipoli to Ataturk's later reputation and Anzac public memory.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This page is a seed page, not a general history of the First World War. The Cavell source also qualifies simple atrocity-propaganda readings: the execution was politically disastrous and symbolically potent, but the underlying escape assistance and German military-law argument are treated as real source facts rather than invented propaganda.
