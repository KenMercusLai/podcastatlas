# Pragmatic Religious Toleration

## Definition

Pragmatic religious toleration is a settlement in which a state allows multiple religious communities to function for civic, commercial, or political reasons without granting full equal public status to every faith.

## Current Synthesis

The Dutch anthem episode presents toleration as part of the Dutch Republic's modernity but keeps it short of modern liberal equality. [[WilliamOfOrange|William of Orange]] is said to have wanted freedom of religion for both Reformed and Roman Catholic worship, while the later [[DutchRepublic]] made Calvinism the public religion and tolerated other communities through legal fictions and official blind eyes. [[Amsterdam]]'s later Jewish life and [[BaruchSpinoza|Spinoza]] appear as examples of the space such a settlement could create.

This toleration is politically pragmatic because the [[LowCountries|Low Countries]] were already religiously and institutionally fragmented. It reduces the civic cost of confessional conflict without erasing public hierarchy, suspicion, or the limits placed on disfavored groups.

## Key Claims

- Toleration can be a stability strategy in a region too plural and fragmented for simple confessional uniformity.
- A public or established religion can coexist with tolerated minority practice through legal and administrative workarounds.
- Religious moderation may serve civic harmony without becoming full rights equality.
- Urban commerce and migration can make practical toleration more valuable to a state.
- The settlement remains qualified because tolerated groups can still depend on discretion, silence, or legal fiction.

## Evidence

- William's stance: [[680-the-netherlands-the-revolt-that-made-the-modern-world-part-4-glt4209356543]] says William wanted freedom of religion for Reformed and Roman Catholic worship, in public or private.
- Public church with tolerance: [[680-the-netherlands-the-revolt-that-made-the-modern-world-part-4-glt4209356543]] says the Dutch Republic later made Calvinism public while tolerating others through legal fictions and blind eyes.
- Urban pluralism: [[680-the-netherlands-the-revolt-that-made-the-modern-world-part-4-glt4209356543]] names Amsterdam's Jewish life and Spinoza as downstream examples.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Pragmatic toleration is not the same as religious neutrality. The source explicitly says Calvinism became the public religion, so the model should be read as managed pluralism under hierarchy rather than equal citizenship across confessions.

- Created the concept to capture the Dutch Republic's legal-fiction and civic-harmony model of toleration.
