## Definition

Three Excellencies blame without authority / 三公无权背责 is the Hanji 798 pattern where nominally senior ministers carry disaster-accountability and dismissal risk even after practical decision power has shifted to Shangshu and inner-court channels.

## Current Synthesis

The concept comes from [[ChenZhongLateHan|陈忠]]'s second memorial in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-798-donghan-shiqi-de-baixing-you-duo-ku-loolveumdtv4eo-omubfi5z2p2r5|Hanji 798]]. The source says the Three Excellencies have high rank, but confidential affairs, selection, recommendation, punishment, reward, and legal decision are handled by Shangshu offices trusted more than the formal senior ministers.

The failure is not only that power moves inward. It is that responsibility does not move with it. When disasters are interpreted as the result of political fault, the court still reprimands or removes the Three Excellencies. Chen Zhong therefore identifies a bureaucratic accountability sink: the visible office absorbs blame while the practical decision channel remains protected.

## Key Claims

- Formal rank can survive after practical decision power has shifted elsewhere.
- Disaster politics can assign blame to visible officeholders even when they lack control over the relevant decisions.
- Shangshu control of documents, selection, punishments, rewards, and confidential affairs can make senior titles hollow.
- Accountability detached from authority protects the real decision channel and weakens institutional correction.
- Chen Zhong's diagnosis helps explain later An-era feedback failure before Yang Zhen's death.

## Evidence

- Hollow senior office: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-798-donghan-shiqi-de-baixing-you-duo-ku-loolveumdtv4eo-omubfi5z2p2r5|Hanji 798]] says the Three Excellencies have high title but no practical authority.
- Shangshu decision control: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-798-donghan-shiqi-de-baixing-you-duo-ku-loolveumdtv4eo-omubfi5z2p2r5|Hanji 798]] says selection, recommendation, punishment, reward, confidential affairs, and legal decisions are controlled through Shangshu.
- Disaster blame: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-798-donghan-shiqi-de-baixing-you-duo-ku-loolveumdtv4eo-omubfi5z2p2r5|Hanji 798]] says disasters still lead the court to blame or remove the Three Excellencies.
- Non-conversion: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-798-donghan-shiqi-de-baixing-you-duo-ku-loolveumdtv4eo-omubfi5z2p2r5|Hanji 798]] says Emperor An does not adopt Chen Zhong's warnings, leaving the mismatch unresolved.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept is bounded to Chen Zhong's memorial as summarized in Hanji 798. It should not be read as a complete institutional history of the Three Excellencies or Shangshu. Later sources may show moments when senior ministers still act effectively; the present claim is that this source identifies a specific An-era mismatch between authority and blame.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Chen Zhong's diagnosis of Three Excellencies responsibility after practical power had shifted to Shangshu channels.
