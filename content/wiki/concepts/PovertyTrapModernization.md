# Poverty-Trap Modernization / 贫困陷阱与现代化

Poverty-trap modernization is the source's combined question: what does it mean for a country to escape agricultural stagnation, and how did [[China]] do so without first matching the formal institutions of already-modern economies? In [[150-zhongguo-ruhe-tiaochu-pinkun-xianjing-xiandaihua-zhi-lu-you-hui-zouxiang-hefang-lgpvp04rngagkpubte2ac6i5ji3g]], [[LiLu|李璐]] supplies the civilizational frame while [[YuenYuenAng|洪源远]] supplies the institutional-development mechanism.

The concept links two scales. At the macro scale, modernization means moving out of the [[MalthusianTrap]] into [[ModernizationAsCompoundGrowth]]. At the institutional scale, escaping poverty can begin through [[WeakInstitutionsMarketBuilding]], [[DirectedImprovisation]], and [[LocalGovernmentPolicyExperimentation]] before formal governance catches up.

## Key Claims
- Poverty traps are not only income levels; they are systems where institutions, incentives, markets, technology, and expectations keep reinforcing low productivity.
- Modernization requires sustained compounding growth, not only a one-time income jump.
- China's escape cannot be explained by one cause such as cheap labor, strong government, foreign trade, or culture.
- The source's synthesis joins [[ModernizationAsCompoundGrowth]] with [[DevelopmentAsVariationSystem]].
