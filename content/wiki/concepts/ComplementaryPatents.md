# Complementary Patents

Complementary patents are patents that work together as parts of a larger invention rather than competing as substitutes. In [[the-invention-invention]], this distinction decides whether a [[PatentPool]] looks useful or suspicious: pooling complementary claims can make production possible, while pooling substitutes can reduce competition.

The source explains the idea through examples such as different technologies needed for one coffee-making process or different parts of a sewing machine. The [[MPEGPatentPool]] applies the same principle by trying to include patents necessary to the standard rather than optional alternatives.

## Key Claims
- Complementary patents solve different pieces of one product or standard.
- Pooling complements can reduce transaction costs and unblock production.
- Pooling substitutes risks cartel behavior because competing alternatives are being coordinated.
- The complement-versus-substitute distinction is central to [[CollaborationVsCollusion]].
