# California Post-Fire Rebuilding Delay

California post-fire rebuilding delay is the recovery bottleneck highlighted by [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-adam-carolla-on-californias-collapse-fires-failed-leadership-and-gyno-fascism-39710360]]. [[AdamCarolla|Adam Carolla]] argues that after the Palisades fire, approval processes, the [[CaliforniaCoastalCommission|California Coastal Commission]], appeals, engineering requirements, insurance friction, and owner exhaustion can prevent [[Malibu]] and [[PacificPalisades|Pacific Palisades]] homes from being rebuilt on any humane timeline.

[[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-spencer-pratt-on-fixing-la-wildfires-homelessness-corruption-the-fight-to-take-it-back-41236810]] adds the pre-rebuilding accountability layer through [[SpencerPratt|Spencer Pratt]]. Pratt's campaign account argues that recovery delay starts with the fire itself: warnings, water readiness, asset deployment, lawsuits, audits, and mayoral accountability all affect whether residents trust the rebuilding process after the flames are gone.

The concept complements [[tech-20260112-0112-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260112-0112-mp-tech-pod-128]], which asks how destroyed L.A. homes might be rebuilt with [[FireResilientConstruction]]. Taken together, the pages separate the material question from the state-capacity question: knowing how to build more fire-resilient homes is not enough if permission, financing, labor, and leadership do not move at recovery speed.

## Key Claims
- Rebuilding delay can become permanent displacement when older or less-liquid owners cannot wait years.
- Permitting and appeal risk can convert a disaster from a temporary loss into a community-exit decision.
- Fire-resilient construction does not solve recovery if homeowners cannot get plans approved, financed, insured, and built.
- Skilled-trades supply matters because recovery requires electricians, plumbers, framers, roofers, drywallers, inspectors, and contractors.
- The source frames delay as a legitimacy problem: residents judge government by whether destroyed homes visibly return.
- Pratt's source adds that failed warnings and water readiness can make post-fire delay feel like a continuation of the same capacity failure, not a separate permitting problem.
