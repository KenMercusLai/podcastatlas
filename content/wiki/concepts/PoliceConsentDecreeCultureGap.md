# Police Consent Decree Culture Gap

Police consent decree culture gap is the difference between formal reform compliance and actual changes in police behavior. In [[kyiv-brawl-ukraines-top-general-fired-6a609aec4fac21e67f1be165]], the [[AlbuquerquePoliceDepartment]] is described as spending 11 years under a federal consent decree while police shootings remained a major concern.

The source does not dismiss policy reform. It says rules about drawing guns, tasers, body cameras, and federal oversight can matter. The gap appears when those rules do not sufficiently change tactical patience, supervision, accountability, risk perception, or the culture officers use in ambiguous encounters.

## Key Claims
- A consent decree can produce policy, training, equipment, and compliance metrics without producing enough fatal-force reduction.
- Culture matters when officers interpret danger, decide whether to wait, and decide whether alternatives to force are real options.
- Body cameras and tasers can change evidence and tactics, but they do not automatically prevent escalation.
- In a heavily armed society, some shootings may be hard to judge in the moment, but that does not eliminate the need to study avoidable deaths.
- Reform can make officers more risk-averse in ways that still fail civilians if mental-health and low-threat calls are handled as enforcement problems.
