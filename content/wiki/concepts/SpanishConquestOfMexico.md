## Definition

The Spanish conquest of Mexico is the military, political, legal, demographic, economic, religious, and cultural transformation conventionally centered on the fall of Tenochtitlan in 1521 but treated here as a longer and uneven process.

## Current Synthesis

The episode's strongest corrective is temporal and regional. Battlefield victory over the [[MexicaEmpire|Mexica Empire]] did not equal immediate control of Mesoamerica. Spanish power expanded through Indigenous alliances, royal confirmation, urban rebuilding, encomienda labor, Christian institutions, biological exchange, and continued campaigns; some allies gained privileges, some elites adapted, and remote regions retained older practices.

That complexity does not sanitize conquest. Torture, execution, sexual coercion, dispossession, forced hierarchy, and catastrophic disease remain central. The best current account therefore combines coalition politics and Indigenous agency with the unequal power and suffering of the colonial result.

## Key Claims

- Tenochtitlan's fall was a decisive event but not a complete or simultaneous conquest of Mexico.
- Indigenous political division and alliance were causal, not incidental, especially in Tlaxcala's role and later privileges.
- Spanish authority depended on law, labor, urban space, religion, and crown recognition as well as arms.
- Conquest outcomes varied by location, class, community, and relationship to the invading coalition.
- Disease and the [[ColumbianExchange|Columbian Exchange]] changed the balance more deeply than many individual tactical episodes.
- Later heroic and Black Legend narratives each risk obscuring the joined reality of contingency, agency, exploitation, and violence.

- Law and Indigenous strategy: [[391-the-fall-of-the-aztecs-the-last-emperor-part-8-glt2984940365]] links Spanish legitimation to Tlaxcalan and elite legal claims.
- Regional duration: [[391-the-fall-of-the-aztecs-the-last-emperor-part-8-glt2984940365]] says some places experienced devastation while others remained outside effective rule much longer.
- Global consequence: [[391-the-fall-of-the-aztecs-the-last-emperor-part-8-glt2984940365]] presents Mexico as a template for Spanish and later European overseas expansion.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This synthesis comes from the final episode of a narrative series and cannot settle the whole military campaign, Indigenous perspectives, or demographic history. The hosts' claim that sustained European colonization was probably unavoidable is a counterfactual judgment, not a demonstrated law. Black Legend polemic can distort comparative interpretation, but identifying that polemic does not rebut evidence of Spanish atrocities.

## What Changed

- Established conquest as a prolonged, uneven system rather than a single 1521 event.
- Integrated coalition politics, law, disease, labor, religion, and memory with military defeat.
