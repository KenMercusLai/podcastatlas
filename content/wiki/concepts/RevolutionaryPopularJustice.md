# Revolutionary Popular Justice

## Definition

Revolutionary popular justice is the claim that ordinary citizens may improvise judgment and punishment outside lawful courts when invasion, conspiracy, or institutional failure makes delay appear existentially dangerous.

## Current Synthesis

The [[SeptemberMassacres]] show this logic in an unusually stark form. Local teams created tribunals, heard accusations and answers, acquitted some prisoners, and executed others in silence; selectivity and procedure made the violence look like justice to participants and supporters, even though guilt standards, authority, appeal, and protection were replaced by revolutionary suspicion and killing.

## Key Claims

- Improvised procedure can make extra-legal violence feel lawful without supplying due process.
- Selective acquittal may strengthen rather than weaken perpetrators' belief that killing is reasoned judgment.
- Emergency rumors can expand “traitor” from political enemies to criminals, forgers, children, sex workers, and other marginal groups.
- Public approval and elite non-intervention can legitimate local violence without a centralized command.
- Ritual solemnity and claims of necessity can be more politically durable than an image of uncontrolled frenzy.

## Evidence

### Improvised tribunals and selection

- [[544-the-french-revolution-the-september-massacres-part-1-glt8301042051]] describes questioning, acquittals, executions, and survival by more than half of detainees in the targeted prisons.

### Expanding enemy categories

- [[544-the-french-revolution-the-september-massacres-part-1-glt8301042051]] records the killing of priests, petty criminals, forgers, young detainees, and sex workers under widening counter-revolutionary suspicion.

### Political legitimation

- [[544-the-french-revolution-the-september-massacres-part-1-glt8301042051]] connects radical incitement, official inaction, local section organization, and contemporary descriptions of the killings as duty or necessity.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

- “Popular justice” names the actors' and defenders' legitimating logic; it does not endorse the tribunals as lawful or factually reliable.
- The foreign invasion and fall of frontier fortresses were real, but rumors of coordinated prisoner conspiracy did not establish individual guilt.
- Distributed local coordination does not mean the violence was wholly spontaneous or that national leaders bore no responsibility.

## What Changed

- The wiki now distinguishes selective quasi-judicial massacre from both ordinary lawful judgment and indiscriminate crowd frenzy.
