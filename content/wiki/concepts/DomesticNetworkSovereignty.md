# Domestic Network Sovereignty

Domestic network sovereignty is the pattern where a state can preserve approved internal digital services while cutting or heavily filtering connectivity to the global internet. [[tech-20260402-0402-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260402-0402-mp-tech-pod-128]] adds the [[Iran]] case through [[AmirRashidi]]'s explanation of the [[NationalInformationNetwork|National Information Network]].

The concept matters because blackout and connectivity are not binary. A regime can keep domestic delivery apps, email, search, and other local services running while preventing access to outside news, social media, search, and alerting tools. That makes the network both infrastructure and political boundary.

## Key Claims
- Domestic network sovereignty can make a shutdown look less like total collapse and more like selective partitioning.
- The state gains more control when infrastructure, local apps, and search all sit inside a domestic regime-approved environment.
- Local service continuity can reduce daily disruption while still isolating people from independent information.
- Workarounds such as VPNs, satellite internet, or outside alerting tools may help some users but may not scale to an entire population under active blocking.
