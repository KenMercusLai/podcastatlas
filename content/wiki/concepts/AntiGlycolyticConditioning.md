# Anti-Glycolytic Conditioning

## Definition
Anti-glycolytic conditioning is a source-scoped training frame that uses brief submaximal efforts and generous recovery to postpone local fatigue while preserving the target activity's fibers and contraction-relaxation pattern.

## Current Synthesis
[[the-correct-way-to-build-strength-endurance-flexibility-at-any-age-pavel-tsatsouline-scim3681411661]] attributes the frame to Yuri Verkhoshansky and contrasts it with sessions organized mainly around tolerating severe glycolytic discomfort. One example uses about 70% of one-repetition maximum for only three repetitions, repeated many times with enough rest to maintain quality. Kettlebell swings, snatches, strength circuits, and stair efforts illustrate the broader work-rest idea, but the exact prescription remains dependent on the target activity.

The durable principle is specificity: useful endurance is not one generic capacity, and conditioning should match recruited fibers, intensity, and contraction-relaxation rhythm. That principle does not establish that glycolytic training is harmful or unnecessary; hard longer efforts can serve different goals.

## Key Claims
- Conditioning can target delayed fatigue without making maximal discomfort the organizing goal.
- Brief submaximal efforts with sufficient rest can accumulate specific work while limiting output decay.
- Endurance transfers best when the trained fibers and contraction-relaxation pattern resemble the target activity.
- Repeat training and interval training should be distinguished by purpose and recovery rather than grouped under a vague HIIT label.
- Hard glycolytic work remains relevant for goals that actually require sustained high-intensity tolerance.

## Evidence
- Method claim - [[the-correct-way-to-build-strength-endurance-flexibility-at-any-age-pavel-tsatsouline-scim3681411661]] describes repeated low-repetition work around 70% of one-repetition maximum with generous recovery.
- Specificity claim - [[the-correct-way-to-build-strength-endurance-flexibility-at-any-age-pavel-tsatsouline-scim3681411661]] states that marathon, combat-sport, kettlebell, and strength-endurance demands recruit different patterns and should not be treated as interchangeable.
- Practical examples - [[the-correct-way-to-build-strength-endurance-flexibility-at-any-age-pavel-tsatsouline-scim3681411661]] uses kettlebell work, strength circuits, and stair climbing to illustrate effort-recovery structures while also distinguishing 30-second power repeats with much longer rest.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The supplied summary gives no controlled comparison, standardized definition, participant data, or outcome measures for the named method. The term can obscure ordinary interval-programming principles unless work duration, intensity, rest, volume, and target adaptation are specified. Cardiovascular disease, blood-pressure risk, injury, medications, heat, and exercise inexperience can materially change safe intensity and recovery.

## What Changed
- Established the concept with a specificity-first definition and an explicit boundary against treating glycolytic work as universally undesirable.
