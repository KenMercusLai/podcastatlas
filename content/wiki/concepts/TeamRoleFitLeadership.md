# Team Role-Fit Leadership

## Definition
Team role-fit leadership is the practice of identifying how people most reliably contribute—such as executing, creating, communicating, or supporting—and matching responsibility to those demonstrated strengths while still developing missing capability.

## Current Synthesis
The episode rejects the idea that success in one demanding environment proves universal leadership or occupational fit. Passing SEAL training demonstrates a specific accomplishment; it does not guarantee success after military transition, in business, or in every team role. Willink instead asks leaders to observe what people naturally and repeatedly do well, then place them where those tendencies help the group.

Role fit here is neither a permanent personality label nor an excuse to stop learning. It is a working allocation judgment. Teams need different forms of contribution, transitions can remove the externally supplied next step, and leaders remain responsible for clarifying purpose, authority, support, and development rather than merely sorting people into boxes.

## Key Claims
- Achievement in one domain does not prove fit or competence in every later domain.
- Teams need differentiated contributions, including execution, creation, communication, and support.
- Leaders should use observed behavior and demonstrated ability when assigning responsibility.
- Structured environments can hide transition difficulty by supplying the next task and success criteria.
- Role fit should guide placement and development without becoming a fixed identity judgment.

## Evidence
- Domain boundary - [[how-to-become-resilient-forge-your-identity-lead-others-jocko-willink-scim5755591173]] says being a SEAL proves completion of basic SEAL training, not success in every domain.
- Transition difficulty - [[how-to-become-resilient-forge-your-identity-lead-others-jocko-willink-scim5755591173]] describes some people struggling after structured environments stop supplying the next box to check.
- Contribution types - [[how-to-become-resilient-forge-your-identity-lead-others-jocko-willink-scim5755591173]] asks leaders to distinguish people who execute, create, communicate, or support and place them accordingly.
- Performance and autonomy - [[how-to-become-resilient-forge-your-identity-lead-others-jocko-willink-scim5755591173]] links competent task performance to trust, recognition, and greater control over one's work.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source offers a leadership heuristic, not a validated psychometric taxonomy. People can contribute across categories, change with training and context, or be misread because of bias, opportunity, disability, culture, or weak management. Fit should not replace fair access, clear expectations, feedback, reasonable accommodation, or a chance to grow.
