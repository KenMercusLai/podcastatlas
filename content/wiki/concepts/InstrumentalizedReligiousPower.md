# 工具化宗教权力 / Instrumentalized Religious Power

## Definition
Instrumentalized Religious Power / 工具化宗教权力 is the pattern where religious symbols, ritual, healing, charity, or belonging become political infrastructure: they organize people, resources, exemptions, obedience, and legitimacy claims in ways that can support relief, rebellion, predation, or local rule.

## Current Synthesis
The concept is not a claim that religion itself is false or violent. It tracks what happens when religious practice becomes a durable organizing medium in a collapsing political order. Hanji 923 shows the relief and mobilization side through [[ZhangJiaoLateHan|张角]] and [[TaipingDao|太平道]]: epidemic pressure, poverty, healing, and practical help create trust before the movement develops military-political infrastructure. Hanji 995 shows the predatory-cover side through [[ZeRong|笮融]], whose Buddhist display at [[Xiapi|下邳]] sits beside plunder and repeated killing of hosts.

Hanji 1055 adds the most administrative version through [[ZhangLuFivePecks|张鲁]] and [[WudoumiDao|五斗米道]] in [[Hanzhong|汉中]]. Zhang Lu's order uses confession, prayer, healing claims, local "jijiu" officers, discipline, refugee reception, and nominal court accommodation. That case broadens the concept from "religion as mobilization or cover" to "religion as local governance infrastructure."

## Key Claims
- Religious practice can create trust and belonging before it becomes openly political or military.
- Ritual, charity, exemption, and office-like roles can organize people and resources as effectively as formal administration.
- A moral vocabulary is not reliable proof of moral constraint; Ze Rong's case makes doctrine and conduct diverge sharply.
- Religious organization can stabilize a local order as well as threaten or prey on one.
- Comparator cases must be bounded: Taiping Dao, Wudoumi Dao, and Ze Rong's Buddhist display show different uses of religious infrastructure.
- The pattern connects to [[InstrumentalBeliefCritique]] when belief becomes a usable cover rather than a binding commitment.

## Evidence
- Relief-to-rebellion pathway: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-923-chengnianrende-dingji-henjin-guzi-lide-juejue-lifzh-gohpqnmdznnfv72tndqf8h|Hanji 923]] describes Zhang Jiao's healing work, thirty-six `fang`, "甲子" marking, and Ma Yuanyi's palace-contact plan as practical care becoming rebellion infrastructure.
- Predatory religious cover: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-995-ta-zuishang-cibei-weihe-duoci-cansha-enren-llicukoezccd5iycin4uq7qun51k|Hanji 995]] presents Ze Rong's Buddhist temples, sutra recitation, exemptions, and bathing-Buddha feasts beside his later murders of [[ZhaoYuLateHan|赵昱]], [[XueLiLateHan|薛礼]], and [[ZhuHaoLateHan|朱皓]].
- Governance infrastructure: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1055-ta-wei-biaozhongxin-yuan-he-yong-ziji-erzi-ao-de-routang-lmr6owlwljlfibuktsyquqtfi7-o|Hanji 1055]] describes Zhang Lu's Hanzhong order through confession, prayer, healing claims, local "jijiu" officers, discipline, and nominal Han court accommodation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept must not flatten every religious movement into Ze Rong-style predation. Zhang Jiao's branch begins with relief and then becomes rebellion infrastructure; Ze Rong's branch exposes violent divergence between pious display and conduct; Zhang Lu's branch shows religious administration that can stabilize a territory. The durable claim is organizational, not a blanket judgment about doctrine.
