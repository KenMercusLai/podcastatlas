# Terrorism Pressure and Wrongful Conviction

## Definition
Terrorism pressure and wrongful conviction describes the risk that public fear, investigative urgency, coercive interrogation, and institutional commitment produce convictions of innocent people after mass-casualty attacks.

## Current Synthesis
[[420-britain-in-1974-thatcher-enters-the-ring-part-4-glt2799978515]] grounds the concept in the Guildford and Birmingham pub bombings of 1974. The attacks killed civilians and generated immense pressure for results, but the Guildford Four and Birmingham Six were wrongly convicted; the episode states that the Birmingham confessions were beaten out of the accused. The cases therefore join immediate terrorist harm with a second, long-lived failure of justice.

## Key Claims
- Severe terrorist violence creates legitimate urgency but also raises the cost of premature certainty.
- Confession evidence becomes especially dangerous when coercion, fear, or investigative tunnel vision shapes it.
- Wrongful convictions harm the accused while leaving the true perpetrators and institutional failures obscured.
- Later exoneration does not restore years lost in prison or erase the original public narrative.
- Security response and procedural legitimacy must be evaluated together rather than treated as opposing concerns.

## Evidence
- Guildford case: [[420-britain-in-1974-thatcher-enters-the-ring-part-4-glt2799978515]] says four innocent people were imprisoned for 15 years while the likely perpetrators were members of a Provisional IRA unit.
- Birmingham case: [[420-britain-in-1974-thatcher-enters-the-ring-part-4-glt2799978515]] says the Birmingham Six were wrongfully convicted after coerced confessions and had their convictions overturned in 1991.
- Dual harm: [[420-britain-in-1974-thatcher-enters-the-ring-part-4-glt2799978515]] narrates the civilian deaths and injuries alongside the later miscarriages of justice rather than allowing either to cancel the other.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a podcast summary rather than a full legal history of either case. Precise investigative conduct, forensic disputes, appeals, responsibility, and identification of the actual bombers require dedicated primary and judicial sources.
