# Military Keynesianism / 军事凯恩斯主义

## Definition

Military Keynesianism is the use of defense expenditure as demand support and industrial policy, with military budgets funding research, manufacturing capacity, skilled employment, and exportable weapons alongside security objectives.

## Current Synthesis

The episode applies the term to expanding French and German defense investment after energy and geopolitical shocks weakened parts of Europe's industrial model. Military expenditure can create research and manufacturing demand while helping states compete in profitable arms markets.

That mechanism is analytically distinct from the stronger claim that governments want war to continue so industry has time to retool. The first is a political-economic channel; the second is an attributed motive requiring much stronger evidence than the interview supplies.

## Key Claims

- Defense budgets can function as industrial stimulus as well as security spending.
- Military research can support technological and manufacturing capability.
- Arms exports can make defense buildup part of international industrial competition.
- Energy shocks and weak civilian industry can increase the attraction of defense-led demand.
- Industrial benefit does not by itself establish a motive to prolong war.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode supplies no budget, employment, multiplier, procurement, export, crowding-out, or counterfactual data. Defense spending can strain budgets, divert civilian investment, create dependency on conflict demand, or fail to produce competitive spillovers. Claims about war-prolongation motives remain source-scoped.
