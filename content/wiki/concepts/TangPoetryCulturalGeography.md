# 唐诗文化地理 / Tang Poetry Cultural Geography

唐诗文化地理 / Tang poetry cultural geography is the source's method for reading poems through place, movement, institutions, and material evidence. In [[ep264-tafang-tangshi-zhilu-shengtang-qixiang-shaonianxin-loo2dfuxffizpbl4si5nwcixnngi]], the method links [[ChangAnTang|唐代长安]], [[Wangchuan|辋川]], the [[ZhedongTangPoetryRoad|浙东唐诗之路]], and western-region sites such as [[BeitingAncientCity|北庭故城]] and [[JiaoheAncientCity|交河故城]].

The concept sits between literary reading and [[FieldworkAsKnowledgeMethod|fieldwork]]. It treats a poem's images as historically situated without flattening poetry into maps: court ritual, Buddhist monasteries, mountain roads, Daoist recommendation networks, military offices, climate, ruins, inscriptions, and excavated documents all become possible context, but only when the source can show how they are connected.

## Key Claims
- Literary geography is strongest when it combines texts, sites, social institutions, and uncertainty labels.
- Capital, frontier, and southern landscape routes produce different poetic pressures: fame and social writing, career risk and border shock, or cultural pilgrimage and reclusion.
- Site visits can make poems vivid, but missing relics and disputed itineraries should remain visible.
- Archaeological and documentary evidence can strengthen poem-site claims when names, dates, offices, and routes align.
