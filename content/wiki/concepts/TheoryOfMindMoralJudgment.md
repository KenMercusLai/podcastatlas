# Theory of Mind in Moral Judgment

## Definition
Theory of mind is the capacity to represent another person's beliefs, desires, knowledge, and intentions as mental states that can differ from reality and from one's own. In moral judgment, it helps distinguish accidental harm from attempted harm and informs blame, trust, avoidance, and punishment.

## Current Synthesis
The bounded episode evidence presents theory of mind as an everyday coordination system rather than a specialist calculation. Conversation, humor, stories, audience adjustment, and danger assessment all require tracking what different people know or intend. Explicit false-belief performance is described as becoming more reliable between roughly ages three and five, with substantial individual and contextual variation.

Moral evaluation combines intent with outcome rather than applying either mechanically. Innocent intent often mitigates accidental harm, while malicious intent can support blame when an attempt fails. Severe outcomes and taboo violations can reduce that mitigating effect. Reported right temporoparietal-junction imaging and transcranial-stimulation results support a neural contribution from intention processing, but they do not reduce moral judgment to one region or remove cultural and situational influence.

## Key Claims
- Representing false beliefs lets an observer predict action from what another person thinks rather than from what is actually true.
- Intention information separates accidents from attempts and changes judgments of blame and threat.
- Outcome severity and taboo can overpower or narrow the weight assigned to innocent intent.
- Moral judgment draws on neural, developmental, cultural, environmental, and unconscious influences rather than one isolated mechanism.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The supplied document summarizes experiments without methods, effect sizes, replication history, or full diagnostic and developmental context. Approximate childhood timing does not define every child or make performance a simple empathy measure. A neural association or temporary stimulation effect does not establish that one brain region independently produces moral judgment. Innocent intent also does not erase causal responsibility or the consequences of severe harm.

## What Changed
- Established a combined developmental, moral, and neural synthesis of intention-sensitive judgment.
