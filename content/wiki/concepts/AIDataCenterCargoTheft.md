# AI Data Center Cargo Theft

## Definition
AI data center cargo theft is the diversion or seizure of chips, servers, copper, cooling equipment, cabling, batteries, and related high-value infrastructure while it moves through freight networks before installation.

## Current Synthesis
[[tech-20260821-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260821-mp-tech-pod-128]] establishes that AI-infrastructure security begins before the facility perimeter: one truck can hold millions of dollars of equipment, and paperwork manipulation, escorts, routes, loading docks, and ports can all be attacked. [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-b8f7b496cd7337e614f9c50ae34981a8-27045356-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-b8f7b496cd7337e614f9c50ae34981a8-27045356]] places that risk inside the wider [[StrategicCargoTheft]] economy and reports $100 million in data-center freight stolen over four months.

## Key Claims
- High component value makes a single data-center load an unusually attractive target.
- The target set extends beyond chips to servers, copper, cooling parts, network and fiber cabling, batteries, and mining equipment.
- Document fraud and suspected port export indicate organized resale or smuggling rather than casual disposal.
- Export controls and restricted legal supply can increase black-market incentives for some technology.
- Theft can delay deployment and repair even when financing, power, construction, and chip allocation are available.
- Vehicle escorts and tracking reduce some risks but remain defeatable layers.

## Evidence
- Value and component breadth - [[tech-20260821-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260821-mp-tech-pod-128]] reports that one semi can carry millions of dollars across a broad equipment set.
- Diversion and overseas movement - [[tech-20260821-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260821-mp-tech-pod-128]] describes doctored paperwork, shipments tracked toward ports, and suspected export.
- General crime-system fit - [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-b8f7b496cd7337e614f9c50ae34981a8-27045356-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-b8f7b496cd7337e614f9c50ae34981a8-27045356]] connects data-center freight to identity compromise, organized logistics crime, escort defeat, and layered security limits.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The $100 million figure, shipment destinations, and overseas movement are source-reported and not independently documented in the supplied notes. Neither source establishes how much stolen equipment was specifically AI-bound, recovered, exported, or linked to export-control evasion. The category should not collapse ordinary construction theft into proven international technology smuggling.

## What Changed
- Data-center freight is now situated within a general strategic cargo-theft model rather than treated as an isolated AI-sector anomaly.
- Escort defeat and the reported four-month loss total sharpen the operational risk while remaining source-scoped.
- The page now follows the synthesis-first schema across both bounded sources.
