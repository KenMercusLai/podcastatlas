# China Innovation Transparency Gap / 中国创新透明度缺口

China innovation transparency gap is [[165-nianbaoji-zhong-de-zhenshi-zhongguo-2026-lpredevu-gakn92dwutmulytmslo|episode 165]]'s late-stage concern that China's technical and corporate capabilities can become harder for outsiders to see. [[DavidWeng|大卫翁]] says annual reports contain less direct managerial thinking than before, while overseas observers may also receive less information about Chinese research environments, company strategy, and technical work.

The concept does not argue for unlimited openness. The source explicitly recognizes that great-power competition changes disclosure boundaries. Its narrower claim is that [[LocalizedGlobalCompany|localized global companies]], global R&D, biotech clinical development, and talent return all need enough credible communication for foreign researchers, engineers, investors, customers, and regulators to understand why joining or trusting a Chinese platform is rational.

## Key Claims
- Less candid annual-report language reduces the signal available to [[AnnualReportMacroReading]].
- External opacity can weaken talent attraction and return flows because researchers cannot evaluate China's labs, companies, or innovation scenes from abroad.
- Transparency matters most where Chinese firms need global trust: clinical data, R&D teams, product quality, after-sales networks, and capital-market credibility.
- The source treats transparency as an operating constraint, not only a media or public-relations problem.
- The tension is source-scoped: disclosure must balance security and competition, but excessive opacity can make Chinese innovation less legible.
