# Human Taste Creative Boundary / 人类审美的创造边界

## Definition
The human-taste creative boundary is the provisional line between creative production that AI can standardize or automate and the human judgment that selects, evaluates, invents, or legitimizes new styles and expressions.

## Current Synthesis
The source separates design skill from design judgment. [[ChenMian|陈冕]] expects AI systems such as [[Lovart]] to absorb much standardized, repetitive, and homogeneous production, especially when a client can state the need directly. He nevertheless treats frontier taste, original direction, and the creation of new styles or emotions as temporarily more dependent on humans.

This is a moving boundary rather than a permanent reservation for elite creators. Models learn from established styles, skilled users can produce more differentiated output than casual prompting, and future capability may narrow the remaining human role. Copyright and style protection, authorship, compensation, evidence of originality, and the social authority to decide what is good remain unresolved governance questions rather than technical details.

## Key Claims
- Automating execution does not automatically automate the judgment that defines a desirable result.
- Standardized and homogeneous creative tasks are more exposed than work that establishes a new direction, style, or emotional vocabulary.
- Better tools may increase the leverage and price of a smaller group of high-judgment creators while displacing routine work.
- AI-generated differentiation depends partly on the user's expertise, context, and ability to direct and evaluate the model.
- Style imitation and creator compensation remain unresolved even when technical generation is easy.

## Evidence
- Task displacement and retained judgment: [[lh8a4swn9y9z0tw8avcmlgljbegx-lh8a4swn9y9z0tw8avcmlgljbegx]] attributes to Chen a large forecast for design-work automation while preserving taste and top-level creativity as near-term differentiators.
- User expertise and output variety: [[lh8a4swn9y9z0tw8avcmlgljbegx-lh8a4swn9y9z0tw8avcmlgljbegx]] distinguishes simple prompting from professional users or agents that work more deeply with models.
- Style and governance boundary: [[lh8a4swn9y9z0tw8avcmlgljbegx-lh8a4swn9y9z0tw8avcmlgljbegx]] records the speakers' concern about imitation of mature artistic styles and their lack of a settled protection mechanism.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source offers practitioner forecasts rather than labor-market measurement, so the “more than 80% within five years” prediction is not adopted as a wiki judgment. Taste is difficult to define and can be institutional, collaborative, market-shaped, or learned by models rather than residing only in exceptional individuals. Photography's effect on portrait painters is an analogy, not proof that AI design will follow the same transition. Longer-run AGI capability could invalidate the proposed boundary.

## What Changed
- Created a provisional framework separating creative execution, evaluation, direction, and style invention.
- Preserved labor, governance, and long-run capability uncertainty instead of treating taste as a permanent human moat.
