# Path Dependence

[[PathDependence]] is the idea that historical starting points and earlier institutional choices shape later options. [[sand-heists-and-property-rights-in-the-caribbean-summer-school]] uses [[Barbuda]] to make the concept concrete: slavery, the Codrington lease, weak colonial institutions, abolition, and the absence of a private-title transition all shaped the island's later [[CommunalLandTenure]].

The episode's point is not that history determines everything. It is that policy proposals such as post-[[HurricaneIrma]] title conversion do not begin from neutral ground; they collide with accumulated expectations, legal claims, and community memory.

## Key Claims
- Institutions can persist because changing them reallocates power, not only because people forget to update rules.
- A system that looks inefficient from outside may be carrying historical protection, identity, or bargaining value for insiders.
- Path dependence explains why two places facing similar economic incentives may not converge on the same property system.
