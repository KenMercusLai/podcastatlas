# STP Marketing Framework / STP营销框架

## Definition
The STP marketing framework is the segmentation, targeting, and positioning bridge between strategy and 4P execution. It identifies a useful market variable, makes the target customer concrete, and decides which belief must change for the desired behavior to happen.

## Current Synthesis
The episode argues that STP is not demographic labeling. Segmentation should find a variable that matters to consumers, fits [[CoreCompetenceStrategy]], and leaves a large enough market. Targeting should turn the segment into a vivid customer muse with life scenes, behavior, and adjacent consumption. Positioning should then locate the current belief behind current behavior and design a proposition that can shift belief and action.

## Key Claims
- Useful segmentation is not the smallest possible demographic slice; it is the market variable where the company can spend effort effectively.
- Behavior, attitude, and aspiration can explain demand better than age, gender, income, city, or identity labels alone.
- Targeting requires a concrete customer picture, not just a segment name.
- Positioning works by changing the belief behind behavior, often by attaching to something already present in the customer's mind.
- The X/Y-axis method separates category "entry ticket" variables from differentiating "killer" variables.

## Evidence
- Segmentation purpose: [[ep85-yingxiao-guanli-gkwrimaoomq-bvejtas8o50a]] says segmentation should direct marketing effort while preserving a large enough potential market.
- Variable choice: [[ep85-yingxiao-guanli-gkwrimaoomq-bvejtas8o50a]] prefers behavior, attitude, and aspiration variables over demographic stereotypes when those variables better explain demand.
- Muse method: [[ep85-yingxiao-guanli-gkwrimaoomq-bvejtas8o50a]] uses a fictional [[PopMart]] customer and [[Lululemon]]'s Ocean and Duke personas to show how targeting becomes concrete.
- Positioning mechanics: [[ep85-yingxiao-guanli-gkwrimaoomq-bvejtas8o50a]] explains current behavior, current belief, consumer proposition, desired belief, and desired behavior through a five-box method.
- Piggyback cognition: [[ep85-yingxiao-guanli-gkwrimaoomq-bvejtas8o50a]] treats "non-cola," "we are second," [[Apple]] versus PC, and [[Samsung]] versus [[Apple]] as examples of entering customer cognition through familiar reference points.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source emphasizes conceptual clarity more than research methods. It does not specify survey design, statistical clustering, media testing, or full positioning validation methods.
