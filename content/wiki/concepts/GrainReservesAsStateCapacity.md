## Definition

Grain reserves as state capacity / 粮食储备即国家能力 is the principle that stored staple food gives households and governments usable time and options during harvest failure, war, relief demand, and political disorder.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-212-jiayi-lun-jizhu-shu-yu-zhongnong-zhuzhang-2-lpdh1bl4etefwusbbl2q4ytmrda1|Hanji 212]] introduces the concept through [[JiaYi|贾谊]]'s [[LunJiZhuShu|《论积贮疏》]]. Reserves are not described as wealth at rest: they preserve household survival, finance relief, feed armies, support defense and attack, and reduce the chance that drought or invasion becomes robbery, forced family separation, local armed power, and rebellion.

The concept therefore separates capacity from nominal authority. A court may issue orders across a wide territory, but without material stores it cannot reliably sustain emergency action or make its commands effective in distant places. The episode's agrarian prescription is only one proposed production route; later tools such as public procurement, grain-for-rank delivery, transport, and price stabilization can contribute to reserves without treating every nonfarm livelihood as dispensable.

## Key Claims

- Grain reserves convert prior production into response time during famine, war, and supply interruption.
- Household reserves protect survival and family integrity, while public reserves support relief, armies, and frontier policy.
- Material scarcity can turn environmental or military shocks into public-order and regime-security crises.
- Formal jurisdiction without food and logistical depth is weak state capacity, especially in distant regions.
- Building reserves requires production, collection, storage, transport, and preservation rather than agrarian status rhetoric alone.

## Evidence

Household and relief resilience:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-212-jiayi-lun-jizhu-shu-yu-zhongnong-zhuzhang-2-lpdh1bl4etefwusbbl2q4ytmrda1|Hanji 212]] links drought and absent savings to panic and the sale or exchange of children for survival.

Military and frontier capacity:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-212-jiayi-lun-jizhu-shu-yu-zhongnong-zhuzhang-2-lpdh1bl4etefwusbbl2q4ytmrda1|Hanji 212]] asks how a state without stores can provision a large defensive force when the frontier is threatened.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-212-jiayi-lun-jizhu-shu-yu-zhongnong-zhuzhang-2-lpdh1bl4etefwusbbl2q4ytmrda1|Hanji 212]] says surplus grain and resources support attack, defense, and relations with nearby and distant opponents.

Political-order capacity:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-212-jiayi-lun-jizhu-shu-yu-zhongnong-zhuzhang-2-lpdh1bl4etefwusbbl2q4ytmrda1|Hanji 212]] presents combined drought and war as a setting for robbery, extreme violence, and rebellion beyond the effective reach of central orders.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The current concept is grounded in one source's presentation of a normative memorial. It establishes a causal model, not measured reserve levels, response thresholds, or proof that every larger stockpile produces effective relief. Grain can spoil, be captured, distributed unequally, or fail to reach people; procurement can burden cultivators; and storage institutions can be corrupt or expensive. The episode's preference for transferring merchants and craftspeople into farming also understates the nonfarm labor needed for tools, storage, transport, and exchange.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from the memorial's integrated household, relief, military, and regime-security argument.
