# Promotion Ambition Gap

Promotion ambition gap is the source's term-level pattern for diverging desire for advancement between men and women. In [[burning-questions-a-more-fire-prone-world-6a687ea64207b2a60119df8b]], [[VingeruMakandewiri]] says survey data show women wanting promotions at far lower rates than men, reversing a trend that McKinsey and Lean In had tracked for about a decade.

The concept matters because ambition is not treated as an isolated personality trait. The source connects it to [[WomenWorkplaceProgressStall]], [[ChildcareInfrastructureConstraint]], gender pay gaps, promotion systems, leadership examples at [[GoldmanSachs]] and [[JPMorganChase]], and the difficulty of balancing senior careers with family life.

## Key Claims
- Promotion ambition can be an institutional signal when one group sees less credible payoff or higher total cost from advancement.
- Entry-level ambition gaps matter because they affect future leadership pipelines.
- A flat or falling desire for promotion can coexist with high education and strong labor-market capability.
- Survey results need to be read beside childcare, pay, culture, and DEI policy rather than as standalone preferences.
