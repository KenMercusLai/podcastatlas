## Definition

Gift refusal as authority / 拒礼立威 is the governance pattern in which an official refuses interested gifts so later judgment, command, or negotiation is not compromised by private obligation.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 841 introduces the pattern through Zhang Huan's refusal of gifts from Qiang leaders while fighting them. The episode reads the refusal as more than personal uprightness: Zhang does not take small profit because doing so would blur his position, weaken credibility, and reduce the room to negotiate or command from above the immediate exchange.

The concept therefore joins moral and strategic reasoning. Refusing a gift can signal clean boundaries, protect public authority, and keep future bargaining from being framed as repayment. The host's maxim that a person who does not seek small profit must have a larger plan points to long-term authority, not only self-denial.

## Key Claims

- Gifts from interested counterparts can create obligation even when they look like small private benefits.
- Refusal preserves distance, credibility, and later bargaining authority.
- Public integrity can be strategically useful because it keeps an official from being read as bought.
- Long-range plans may require leaving immediate gain on the table.
- Gift refusal is strongest when paired with a clear public role rather than performed as empty moral display.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept currently rests on one Zhang Huan source note and should not imply that every gift refusal is effective or sincere. Other wiki patterns show that reward and gift systems can also be gatekeeping tools, patronage channels, or public-service signals depending on context. The concept should stay tied to interested gifts that would compromise authority.
