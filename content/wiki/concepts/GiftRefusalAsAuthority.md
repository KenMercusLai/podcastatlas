## Definition

Gift refusal as authority / 拒礼立威 is the governance pattern in which an official refuses interested gifts so later judgment, command, recommendation, or negotiation is not compromised by private obligation.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 783 adds an earlier clean-office version through [[YangZhenLateHan|Yang Zhen]] and [[WangMiLateHan|Wang Mi]]. Wang is a former maocai recommended by Yang and now Changyi magistrate; his nighttime offer of ten jin of gold looks like gratitude and relationship maintenance, but it would also bind a public recommender to a private subordinate. Yang's Four Knows answer makes secrecy irrelevant: the refusal protects judgment because the giver and receiver already know the transaction.

Hanji 840 supplies the frontier-command source case through [[ZhangHuanLateHan|Zhang Huan]]'s refusal of gifts from Qiang leaders after a 155 CE frontier victory. The refusal matters because the preceding context is political, not merely personal: eight earlier commandants are described as corrupt, Qiang groups have just been separated from a Southern Xiongnu alignment, and Zhang needs command authority to become trusted order rather than another extraction channel.

Hanji 841 then restates the pattern through the host's maxim that someone who does not seek small profit must have a larger plan. Across the two notes, the refusal is more than uprightness. Zhang does not take small profit because doing so would blur his position, weaken credibility, and reduce the room to negotiate or command from above the immediate exchange.

The concept therefore joins moral and strategic reasoning. Refusing a gift can signal clean boundaries, protect public authority, and keep future bargaining from being framed as repayment. The host's maxim that a person who does not seek small profit must have a larger plan points to long-term authority, not only self-denial.

## Key Claims

- Gifts from interested counterparts can create obligation even when they look like gratitude, courtesy, or small private benefits.
- Refusal preserves distance, credibility, and later bargaining authority.
- Public integrity can be strategically useful because it keeps an official from being read as bought or as another extractor.
- Gift refusal can repair trust when predecessors have made office look like a predatory channel.
- Refusal also protects recommendation and appointment relationships, where earlier help can be converted into later private obligation.
- Long-range plans may require leaving immediate gain on the table.
- Gift refusal is strongest when paired with a clear public role rather than performed as empty moral display.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept now rests on Yang Zhen's recommender-subordinate refusal and two adjacent Zhang Huan frontier source notes. It should not imply that every gift refusal is effective or sincere. Other wiki patterns show that reward and gift systems can also be gatekeeping tools, patronage channels, or public-service signals depending on context. The concept should stay tied to interested gifts that would compromise authority or trust.
