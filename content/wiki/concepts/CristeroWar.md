# Cristero War

## Definition

The Cristero War was an armed uprising against the post-revolutionary Mexican state's anti-clerical program, especially associated in this episode with rural Catholic resistance to [[PlutarcoEliasCalles|Plutarco Elias Calles]].

## Current Synthesis

The episode uses the war as the coercive background to a cultural transformation. Restrictions on clerical education, political activity, institutional property, and public authority did not merely secularize administration; they produced armed resistance under the slogan *Viva Cristo Rey* and sharply reduced the Church's public capacity. The later promotion of Indigenous-facing national ritual therefore occurred after violent conflict, not through neutral folkloric evolution alone.

## Key Claims

- The conflict emerged from aggressive enforcement of anti-clerical state policy.
- Rural Catholic resistance gave the secularization struggle a mass armed form.
- The war weakened the institutional Church while revealing durable popular religious loyalty.
- Its aftermath shaped the conditions in which Catholic customs could be recoded as national folklore.
- Cultural reinvention and political coercion must be analyzed together without treating them as the same process.

## Evidence

- Political cause: [[264-mexico-day-of-the-dead-glt9022174156]] connects Calles's education and political restrictions to Christian revolt.
- Popular mobilization: [[264-mexico-day-of-the-dead-glt9022174156]] identifies *Viva Cristo Rey* as the insurgent slogan and locates support in rural Mexico.
- Institutional damage: [[264-mexico-day-of-the-dead-glt9022174156]] reports a steep fall in priests and severe restrictions on the Church by 1940.
- Cultural aftermath: [[264-mexico-day-of-the-dead-glt9022174156]] places later state promotion of Dia de Muertos within this weakened-clerical environment.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode treats the war briefly and does not reconstruct regional variation, state and rebel violence, negotiations, chronology, or the Church hierarchy's changing position. Its death toll and priest-count figures remain source-scoped. Cultural promotion after the war should not be reduced to a direct administrative consequence of the conflict.

## What Changed

- Created a bounded account of the church-state war as political context for festival recoding.
