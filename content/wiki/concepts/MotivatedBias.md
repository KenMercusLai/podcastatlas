## Definition

Motivated bias / 动机偏差 is the distortion of evidence, intent, risk, or alternatives toward an interpretation a person wants to be true because it protects hope, identity, status, group belonging, or a preferred outcome.

## Current Synthesis

The bounded sources show motivated bias at three scales. In negotiation and social perception, a person can read kindness as weakness, error as malice, or supportive evidence as stronger than disconfirming evidence while experiencing the judgment as objective. Under identity pressure, [[CognitiveInertia|cognitive inertia]], [[TribalTruth|tribal truth]], and group belonging make assimilation into the preferred story cheaper than genuine updating.

Historical cases make the decision cost concrete. [[GongziAng|公子卬 / 公子昂]] accepts [[ShangYang|公孙鞅 / 商鞅]]'s friendly peace proposal because old ties and a bloodless success fit what he wants, without testing why an invading Qin army would withdraw empty-handed. [[LiuAn|淮南王刘安]] reportedly rewards news that [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] lacks an heir or governs badly and becomes angry at contrary reports. One actor accepts a convenient external promise; the other reshapes an internal reporting environment so preferred intelligence is easier to deliver.

The sources support active correction rather than a command to “be objective.” Ask what the other side's incentives imply, separate interests from attribution stories, seek disconfirming evidence, and deliberately consider the opposite interpretation. These checks reduce bias only when information channels remain usable; punishing unwelcome reports makes individual correction an institutional problem.

## Key Claims

- Motivated bias changes the weight given to evidence, not only the final opinion stated.
- Desired peace, status, certainty, or group loyalty can make a strategically improbable story feel self-evident.
- Bias becomes organizational when leaders reward confirming reports and punish disconfirming ones.
- Overconfidence and self-serving attribution can narrow perceived bargaining space by turning assumptions about intent into facts.
- Information exchange, incentive testing, and consider-the-opposite review are more concrete corrections than generic demands for objectivity.
- Correction is limited when retaliation, identity threat, or captured reporting channels make contrary evidence costly to provide.

## Evidence

Negotiation and attribution:
- [[106-bie-zai-haipa-chongtu-kuai-lai-xuexi-tanpan-shu-742539645|Episode 106]] links motivated bias to overconfidence, optimism, superiority beliefs, and mistaken readings of counterpart intent, while recommending information exchange and interest-based questioning.

Social perception and belief maintenance:
- [[123-renxing-shiyan-dangxin-ni-ziji-779479569|Episode 123]] uses partisan evidence evaluation and hostile-media perception to show preferred interpretation being experienced as objectivity; it offers [[ConsiderTheOppositeBiasCorrection|consider-the-opposite correction]] as a stronger check than “be objective.”
- [[132-zhenglun-yu-shuofu-women-weishenme-chaojia-zenme-cai-suan-ying-807583318|Episode 132]] connects belief persistence to low-cost assimilation, identity, group belonging, information cocoons, and the pressure of cognitive dissonance.

Strategic cases:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-22-gongsunyang-zha-wei-gongzi-ang-lss1l7lojfwgwdtcje7m5ijodpxi|Zhouji 22]] says Gongzi Ang accepts a peace parley that conflicts with Qin's invasion incentives because friendship and bloodless success fit his hopes.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-302-1-xihan-shuzhi-neidou-jieguo-kengdie-ye-kengye-ln6rfk8nzoyeqxduik_edlqqiyug|Hanji 302-1]] says Liu An welcomes reports favorable to his imperial ambition and becomes angry when envoys report an heir or competent government.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The sources do not establish that every mistaken judgment is motivated, that a preferred conclusion must be false, or that disagreement proves bad faith. Perception can differ because people have different information and experience, and negotiation errors can also come from uncertainty or poor communication. The historical cases are podcast interpretations with unverified private motives and dialogue: Gongzi Ang's trust and Liu An's anger fit motivated bias, but they are not controlled demonstrations of internal psychology. Hanji 302-1 also shows that forceful counterargument temporarily changes Liu An's course, so preferred belief is not presented as permanently immune to evidence.
