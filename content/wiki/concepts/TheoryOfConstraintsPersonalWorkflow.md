# Theory of Constraints for Personal Workflow

## Definition
Theory of constraints for personal workflow is the use of bottleneck-first diagnosis to improve total output by finding the one limiting step, exploiting it, subordinating other work to it, elevating it, and then repeating the cycle.

## Current Synthesis
The episode translates the Theory of Constraints from factory throughput into team and individual work. The Herbie hiking example becomes a general rule: the slowest constraint determines the system's pace, so speeding up non-bottlenecks can create inventory, rework, or frustration without raising total throughput.

In personal work, bottlenecks can be technical, cognitive, emotional, or organizational. The host uses writing workflows, sleep, mind dumps, reporting ability, boss decisions, and reading-note digestion to show that the constraint is often not the most visible task but the step where unfinished work keeps accumulating.

## Key Claims
- System throughput is set by the bottleneck, not by the average speed of all steps.
- Non-bottlenecks should adapt to the bottleneck instead of creating more upstream work.
- Bottlenecks can be decision access, legal review, engineering capacity, emotional recovery, sleep, or expression skill.
- Career growth can stall on one narrow constraint even when general professional competence is high.
- DBR logic turns bottleneck pace, buffer, and upstream release control into a coordination system.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The bottleneck metaphor can oversimplify work with multiple interacting limits. The page should not infer that every life or career problem has exactly one stable constraint; the episode's method is best treated as a recurring diagnostic pass.
