# Estado Novo (Portugal)

## Definition

Portugal's Estado Novo was the conservative authoritarian order dominated by [[AntonioDeOliveiraSalazar|Salazar]], inherited by [[MarceloCaetano]], and overthrown in April 1974.

## Current Synthesis

The episode explains the regime's late collapse as accumulated rigidity rather than one isolated failure. Political repression, slow modernization, colonial commitment, fiscal burden, blocked liberalization, and military professional resentment interacted until a relatively small officer movement could remove the government with little armed resistance.

## Key Claims

- The regime joined censorship and secret policing to conservative Catholic social order.
- Salazar's removal by illness changed leadership without dismantling institutions or imperial commitments.
- Caetano's limited liberalization was insufficient to create legitimate renewal.
- Colonial war consumed resources and politicized the officers required to sustain empire.
- The state's apparent durability concealed shallow military willingness to defend it in April 1974.

## Evidence

- Institutional rigidity: [[273-portugal-the-carnation-revolution-glt3297148398]] describes a poor and repressive late dictatorship whose controlled reforms stalled.
- Imperial burden: [[273-portugal-the-carnation-revolution-glt3297148398]] ties the regime's crisis to expensive wars in Angola and Mozambique.
- Collapse: [[273-portugal-the-carnation-revolution-glt3297148398]] says most government forces joined the rebels and Caetano surrendered within a day.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source is a narrative overview, not a full social history of the Estado Novo. It gives limited attention to regime constituencies, economic change, colonial perspectives, opposition parties, the secret police's institutional history, or why authoritarian rule endured for decades.
