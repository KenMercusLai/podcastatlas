# Academic Translation Reliability / 学术翻译可靠性

## Definition
Academic translation reliability is the degree to which a translation lets readers recover an author's argument, conceptual distinctions, ambiguity, and textual context without the translator silently replacing them with stylistic preferences or a favored interpretation.

## Current Synthesis
[[307-weishenme-guonei-henduo-xueshu-fanyi-zhiliang-zheme-cha-lkbjmojuqcokbee64h9hqvrzaf4r]] treats reliability as a hierarchy of obligations. Accuracy, fidelity, completeness, and terminology consistency come before elegance; readability matters, but it cannot justify changing a concept merely to avoid repetition or make a difficult thinker sound naturally Chinese.

Reliability is also corpus-level and institutional. A translator may need an author's wider works, research tradition, correspondence, people, places, editions, and existing terminology before deciding how one sentence works. Notes and reasons make judgment inspectable, while scholarly distance prevents admiration, political purpose, or discomfort from silently editing the source.

## Key Claims
- Scholarly translation should preserve argument and conceptual relations before optimizing literary elegance.
- Key terms need deliberate consistency unless context supplies a documented reason to vary them.
- Whole-author and historical context can be necessary to translate a local passage reliably.
- Ambiguity in the source should remain visible when evidence does not license one definitive interpretation.
- Annotation and explicit justification make departures from established translations reviewable.
- Critical distance requires presenting objectionable or inconvenient material rather than promoting or sanitizing the author.

## Evidence
- Reader consequence: [[307-weishenme-guonei-henduo-xueshu-fanyi-zhiliang-zheme-cha-lkbjmojuqcokbee64h9hqvrzaf4r]] uses difficult Chinese editions of Marcuse and Weber to show how unreliable prose can make novice readers blame themselves.
- Terminology: [[307-weishenme-guonei-henduo-xueshu-fanyi-zhiliang-zheme-cha-lkbjmojuqcokbee64h9hqvrzaf4r]] contrasts virtue with morality and examines amour-propre and amour de soi as system-level distinctions.
- Context and apparatus: [[307-weishenme-guonei-henduo-xueshu-fanyi-zhiliang-zheme-cha-lkbjmojuqcokbee64h9hqvrzaf4r]] describes using Rousseau's wider corpus, letters, relationships, places, and explanatory notes to test renderings.
- Translator restraint: [[307-weishenme-guonei-henduo-xueshu-fanyi-zhiliang-zheme-cha-lkbjmojuqcokbee64h9hqvrzaf4r]] criticizes translations that rank political forms, beautify authors, omit uncomfortable material, or resolve openness through the translator's politics.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source supplies a normative account and examples rather than a validated quality metric or systematic comparison of editions. Literal consistency can itself mislead when context changes, and popular editions may reasonably use less apparatus than specialist ones. Reliability therefore requires reasoned consistency and accountable variation, not mechanical one-word substitution or maximal annotation in every publication.

## What Changed
- Established reliability as a hierarchy in which fidelity and conceptual stability constrain style.
- Added whole-corpus research, preserved ambiguity, annotation, and critical distance as reviewable practices.
- Identified reader self-blame as a downstream harm of unreliable translation.
