# Advice Seeking for Improvement

## Definition
Advice seeking for improvement is the practice of asking what to change next time, shifting critique from global evaluation of the person toward specific future action.

## Current Synthesis
[[AdamGrant]] argues that a generic request for feedback often elicits cheerleading or criticism. Asking for advice instead can make the task prospective: the responder searches for a useful change, while the recipient has less reason to hear a fixed verdict about identity.

The method works best with task-specific questions and several independent reviewers. Repeated concerns are more informative than one person's taste, and the recipient's "second score" - how well criticism is received and used - keeps an initially poor evaluation from becoming the final judgment. Advice giving can also build agency in the giver, including children, when their contribution is later remembered and reused.

## Key Claims
- Future-oriented wording can elicit more actionable guidance than a broad request for evaluation.
- Task-focused critique is less identity-threatening than person-focused judgment.
- Several independent reviewers help distinguish recurring quality problems from individual taste.
- Receiving criticism is itself a learnable performance that can be assessed after the first result.
- Asking someone for advice can communicate that their judgment matters and may strengthen their confidence.

## Evidence
- Advice framing - [[how-to-unlock-your-potential-motivation-unique-abilities-dr-adam-grant-scim2670765142]] contrasts advice for next time with generic feedback that becomes praise or criticism.
- Multiple evaluators - [[how-to-unlock-your-potential-motivation-unique-abilities-dr-adam-grant-scim2670765142]] describes Grant asking five to eight trusted people to score early creative work.
- Second score - [[how-to-unlock-your-potential-motivation-unique-abilities-dr-adam-grant-scim2670765142]] uses Sheila Heen's label for evaluating how criticism is received.
- Advice-giver agency - [[how-to-unlock-your-potential-motivation-unique-abilities-dr-adam-grant-scim2670765142]] describes Grant asking his daughter for speaking advice and later helping her reuse it.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Advice is not automatically accurate, kind, independent, or relevant. Reviewer selection can reproduce shared blind spots, numeric scores can hide reasons, and power differences can make requests feel performative. The episode summarizes feedback and advice-giving research without full methods, so the wording advantage and confidence effects remain source-scoped rather than universal.

## What Changed
- Established a future-oriented critique method with reviewer-diversity and advice-giver-agency boundaries.
