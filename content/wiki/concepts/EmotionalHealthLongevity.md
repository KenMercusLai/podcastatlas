# Emotional Health Longevity

## Definition
Emotional health longevity is the source's claim that longer life has limited value unless connection, purpose, presence, emotional regulation, relationship repair, and humane self-talk are preserved.

## Current Synthesis
The episode's emotional-health section deliberately widens longevity beyond biomarkers and exercise. [[PeterAttia]] says emotional health may be the most important healthspan component because endless lifespan while miserable would be meaningless. He defines it through connection, healthy relationships, purpose, fulfillment, emotional regulation, satisfaction, and presence.

The practical branch is repair-oriented. Attia describes treatment in 2017 and 2020, journaling, affirmations, therapy, DBT, noticing damage in relationships, apologizing quickly, and changing harsh self-talk by speaking to himself as he would to a close friend. The concept therefore links longevity to emotional acceptance and relational skill rather than perfect emotional control.

## Key Claims
- Emotional health is treated as a core healthspan domain, not an optional supplement to physical longevity.
- Connection, relationships, purpose, fulfillment, satisfaction, emotional regulation, and presence are named components.
- Mortality awareness can redirect attention toward agency, relationship quality, and eulogy virtues.
- Relationship repair matters more than never causing friction.
- Conflict with loved ones requires asking whether the relationship or the outcome is the priority.
- Therapy and DBT are presented as tools for emotional acceptance, anger interpretation, and self-talk change.
- Harsh self-criticism can be retrained through repeated, emotionally salient practice in the source's account.

- Repair practice: [[improve-vitality-emotional-physical-health-lifespan-dr-peter-attia-scim5907126956]] describes quickly noticing and repairing relational damage, including apologizing after yelling while still maintaining a boundary.
- Therapy branch: [[improve-vitality-emotional-physical-health-lifespan-dr-peter-attia-scim5907126956]] describes treatment, phone-free intensive therapy, DBT, emotional acceptance, anger as a cover for hurt or shame, and repeated self-talk exercises.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source gives Attia's personal experience and public healthspan framing, not a psychiatric treatment plan. Severe depression, trauma, suicidal ideation, addiction, family violence, and other mental-health crises require qualified care and cannot be addressed by self-talk or relationship repair alone.
