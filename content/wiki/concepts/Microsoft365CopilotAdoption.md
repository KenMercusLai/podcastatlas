# Microsoft 365 Copilot Adoption

Microsoft 365 Copilot adoption is [[ep-48-from-pilots-to-productivity-what-it-actually-takes-to-make-ai-work-in-the-enterprise]]'s practical rollout frame for moving [[MicrosoftCopilot|Microsoft 365 Copilot]] from license assignment into durable work change. [[JimSpignardo]] argues that organizations go wrong when they treat Copilot like another Office application and expect usage to keep rising without business alignment, training, governance, and measurement.

The source's 90-day sequence starts with readiness assessment, stakeholder investment, an AI usage policy, guardrails, and a small enthusiastic pilot group. It then moves into role-based pain-point discovery, use-case inventory, business-leader input, high-value/low-effort prioritization, [[AIAdoptionBaselineMeasurement]], and outcome tracking.

## Key Claims
- Copilot rollout should begin with business readiness and stakeholder commitment, not only license procurement.
- Early pilot groups should be small enough to coach and enthusiastic enough to surface practical workflow lessons.
- Use cases should be role-based and pain-point-specific; generic training creates initial curiosity but often plateaus.
- The strongest candidate use cases are high-value and low-effort enough to produce measurable adoption momentum.
- Baselines matter because organizations cannot measure Copilot's effect if they never measured the current workflow.
- Governance and guardrails can speed adoption by making approved uses, data boundaries, and escalation expectations clear.
