## Definition

Tiger tally command system / 虎符调兵制度 is a split-credential mechanism for authenticating military orders: a field commander holds one part and the ruler or central authority controls the matching part needed to validate a command.

## Current Synthesis

The two current sources show opposite limits of credential-based authority. In the [[StealingTigerTalliesToRescueZhao|窃符救赵]] story, [[Xinlingjun|信陵君]] obtains the ruler's half and can present a formally valid signal even though the ruler has not authorized the rescue. The credential is genuine while the represented intent is false, and [[JinBi|晋鄙]]'s suspicion shows that matching objects cannot eliminate field judgment.

Hanji 252 reverses the configuration. [[YanZhuWesternHan|严助]] carries the emperor's actual purpose to Kuaiji but lacks the tally, so the local authority initially refuses to mobilize. The episode says coercion and direct assertion of imperial command overcome that refusal. Taken together, the cases show why command needs both verifiable form and credible institutional context: possession can be detached from intent, while intent without the credential can be indistinguishable from unauthorized demand.

## Key Claims

- A split tally turns command authorization into a physically verifiable credential.
- A stolen or improperly obtained matching half can make an unauthorized order appear formally valid.
- Genuine sovereign intent without the expected tally can properly trigger refusal at the local level.
- Formal credentials do not eliminate field discretion when an order is implausible, strategically dangerous, or procedurally irregular.
- When authentication and asserted intent diverge, command transfer may depend on coercion rather than institutional trust.

## Evidence

Valid-looking credential without valid intent:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-96-maosui-zijian-6-lteqe-0ykjtv01cmxv1fhx6z0vdi|Zhouji 96 part 6]] explains the split tally, Ru Ji's access to the ruler's half, Jin Bi's suspicion, and Zhu Hai's killing of Jin Bi when he resists the handover.

Valid intent without the credential:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-252-yiqun-chigua-qunzhong-ruhe-xiansha-hanwudi-lndyhnpi9ygvvrh-h1vnxocs3oap|Hanji 252]] says Kuaiji initially refuses Yan Zhu's troop demand because he lacks a tiger tally, after which Yan kills an opposing official and asserts the emperor's command.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Both sources are popular-history summaries rather than institutional or material-history studies. The precise construction, period-specific rules, distribution of tally halves, legal exceptions, identity of the Kuaiji official, and formal basis for Yan Zhu's action need primary-text and specialist comparison. The cases demonstrate narrative failure modes of credential systems; they do not prove that the institution generally failed or that resistance was always lawful.

## What Changed

- Added the inverse authentication case in which genuine imperial intent arrives without the expected credential.
- Reframed the mechanism around divergence among credential, intent, context, and coercive enforcement.
