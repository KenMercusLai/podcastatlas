# Entertainment as Problem-Setting / 娱乐产品出题

## Definition
Entertainment as problem-setting is [[LiangChenqi|梁琛奇]]'s distinction between tools that solve an already recognized problem and entertainment products that propose a new activity, feeling, interaction, or world for users to care about.

## Current Synthesis
The concept explains why stronger models or cheaper generation do not mechanically reveal a winning entertainment product. The search space is broad, so teams need imagination to formulate an experience and disciplined lightweight tests to learn whether it can become novel, frequent, long-lived, and resistant to fatigue.

## Key Claims
- Tool demand often begins from visible friction such as cost, slowness, or error, while entertainment demand may not exist until a product makes a new experience legible.
- Model capability can expand the design space without selecting the experience users will repeatedly value.
- A good entertainment hypothesis must survive after the short-term surprise of “AI can do this” fades.
- Lightweight validation should precede deep technical investment when the central uncertainty is whether the proposed activity is enjoyable.
- Product taste and human understanding remain important because entertainment quality is partly subjective and culturally variable.

## Evidence
- **Wide search space** - [[duihua-liangchenqi-douyin-maoxiang-chuangye-tamen-dou-gao-shengchanli-wo-xiang-yong-ai-chuangzao-kaixin-1-182-1]] contrasts productivity's clearer problem set with entertainment's need to invent new worlds, interactions, and feelings.
- **Validation discipline** - [[duihua-liangchenqi-douyin-maoxiang-chuangye-tamen-dou-gao-shengchanli-wo-xiang-yong-ai-chuangzao-kaixin-1-182-1]] uses Liang's failed real-time social incubation project to show how stacking attractive but unverified assumptions reduces overall success probability.
- **Demand standard** - [[duihua-liangchenqi-douyin-maoxiang-chuangye-tamen-dou-gao-shengchanli-wo-xiang-yong-ai-chuangzao-kaixin-1-182-1]] records Liang's proposed novelty, longevity, frequency, and low-fatigue tests and his argument that technical amazement should be removed when judging durable demand.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- Existing entertainment genres, communities, and content formats can provide validated starting points; problem-setting does not require invention from nothing.
- The criteria are a founder's heuristic rather than a predictive formula, and the source provides no controlled comparison of successful and failed products.
- New experiences can require time and ecosystem development before early retention accurately reflects their eventual value.

## What Changed
- Added a vocabulary for entertainment innovation as demand creation rather than task fulfillment.
- Connected imaginative product choice to low-cost validation and model-independent demand testing.
