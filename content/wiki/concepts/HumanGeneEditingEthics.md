# Human Gene-Editing Ethics

## Definition
Human gene-editing ethics is the episode's boundary for evaluating embryo editing and sequencing through technical precision, causal certainty, disease prevention, enhancement, consent, inequality, and the meaning of normal variation.

## Current Synthesis
The source presents disease prevention as the strongest aspiration for gene editing while refusing to treat the path from variant to desirable intervention as simple. CRISPR has had off-target risks, complex traits are not single-gene switches, and an observed genetic correlation may not be a causal target. Embryo changes also affect a future person who cannot consent and may enter the germline.

Embryo sequencing raises related issues even when no sequence is edited. Selecting among embryos using uncertain risk correlations can turn probabilistic data into high-stakes choices, and high costs may distribute benefits and harms unequally. The central judgment is that technical improvement does not by itself settle the boundary among treating disease, selecting normal variation, and enhancing traits.

## Key Claims
- Preventing serious disease is ethically distinct from optimizing preferred traits, even when the boundary is difficult to draw.
- Off-target effects and incomplete causal knowledge remain core technical constraints.
- Complex traits make correlation-based embryo prediction especially uncertain.
- Germline intervention can affect descendants beyond the consenting decision-makers.
- Embryo sequencing can create consequential selection pressure without directly editing DNA.
- Unequal access can turn genetic intervention into a mechanism of social stratification.

## Evidence
- Precision limit: [[behaviors-that-alter-your-genes-to-improve-your-health-performance-dr-melissa-ilardo-scim2045300705]] characterizes CRISPR as historically blunt and highlights off-target effects while noting improvement.
- Treatment-enhancement boundary: [[behaviors-that-alter-your-genes-to-improve-your-health-performance-dr-melissa-ilardo-scim2045300705]] contrasts disease prevention with correction, normal variation, and enhancement.
- Embryo prediction: [[behaviors-that-alter-your-genes-to-improve-your-health-performance-dr-melissa-ilardo-scim2045300705]] treats IVF embryo sequencing as expensive and often dependent on correlations rather than certain causal outcomes.
- Human precedent: [[behaviors-that-alter-your-genes-to-improve-your-health-performance-dr-melissa-ilardo-scim2045300705]] discusses the genetically modified babies in China as a warning case for embryo intervention.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source provides a high-level ethics discussion rather than a current technical, legal, or clinical review. CRISPR precision, approved therapies, embryo-screening methods, and jurisdictional rules can change quickly. The page therefore records the episode's decision framework, not a claim that all somatic gene therapy, embryo testing, or disease-focused research has the same risk profile.

## What Changed
- Created an ethics framework joining technical uncertainty, treatment-enhancement boundaries, germline consequences, and unequal access.
