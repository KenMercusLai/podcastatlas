## Definition

Inherited enmity revision is the process by which a received duty to hate or avenge is re-evaluated through causal uncertainty, firsthand encounter, and evidence of the alleged enemy's actual character.

## Current Synthesis

[[HuFei|胡斐]] and [[MiaoRenfeng|苗人凤]] supply the bounded case. Hu Fei inherits a plausible grievance because Miao's duel belongs to the chain ending in 胡一刀's death, yet he does not know the complete causal truth. Direct experience then supplies evidence that the inherited label cannot contain: Miao trusts him while blinded, faces danger without evasion, exercises restraint, teaches him, and appears willing to share mortal risk.

Revision does not require declaring that no harm occurred. It separates causal participation from murderous intent, inherited narrative from observed conduct, and moral inquiry from automatic execution of a revenge role. Respect can therefore interrupt vengeance before every fact is known without becoming proof of innocence.

The concept contrasts with clear redress elsewhere in the same source. Hu Fei can identify [[FengTiannan|凤天南]]'s ongoing abuse and victims directly, whereas his grievance against Miao arrives through lineage, partial knowledge, and a death whose agency is disputed. “恩仇难明” names a demand for better judgment, not indifference to wrongdoing.

## Key Claims

- A received enemy label may preserve a real grievance while oversimplifying intent and causation.
- Firsthand evidence of character can legitimately revise an inherited duty before supplying total certainty.
- Respect for an alleged enemy does not by itself prove innocence or erase loss.
- Causal participation in a death must be distinguished from deliberate killing.
- Moral revision is strongest when it changes conduct despite lineage pressure and incomplete information.
- Clear victim-centered redress and ambiguous inherited vengeance require different evidentiary standards.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept comes from one literary discussion, not a general legal or psychological model. Charisma, admiration, or intimacy can also distort judgment, so firsthand experience should not automatically defeat documentary or victim evidence. The episode summary does not independently settle the complete circumstances of Hu Yidao's death, and revision of vengeance is not equivalent to acquittal.

## What Changed

- Established the concept from Hu Fei's changing judgment of Miao Renfeng.
- Distinguished moral revision from forgiveness, acquittal, and denial of loss.
