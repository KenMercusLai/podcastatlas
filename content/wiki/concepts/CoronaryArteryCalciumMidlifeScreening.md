# Coronary Artery Calcium Midlife Screening

## Definition
Coronary artery calcium midlife screening is the source-scoped prevention frame that a coronary artery calcium score can help midlife women decide how aggressively to focus on cardiometabolic risk.

## Current Synthesis
The Gottfried episode gives coronary artery calcium scoring unusually high practical priority. Gottfried says that if women take one action from the episode, it should be getting a coronary artery calcium score by age 45, or earlier when premature heart-disease risk is present. In the source, the test is not presented as treatment; it is risk stratification that can make cardiometabolic prevention more concrete.

The concept links perimenopause care to cardiovascular and brain-health prevention. Hot flashes, night sweats, insulin, glucose, thyroid markers, and cardiometabolic markers are treated as part of the same midlife risk conversation, while conventional care may not routinely order the scan.

The full interview confirms that this is Gottfried's unusually strong practical recommendation rather than an independently established universal age threshold. The resulting synthesis is therefore to raise coronary-risk stratification with a clinician when personal or family risk makes it relevant, not to turn age 45 into an automatic scan for every woman.

## Key Claims
- The source presents coronary artery calcium scoring as a high-priority action for women by about age 45.
- Earlier screening is framed as relevant when premature heart-disease risk is present.
- The score is described as a CT scan of the chest that helps stratify cardiometabolic focus.
- The episode links calcium scoring to a broader prevention bundle that includes insulin, glucose, thyroid, hormone, symptom, and menopause-transition context.
- Conventional clinicians may not routinely order the test, making patient-clinician discussion part of the screening pathway.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This page records Gottfried's recommendation in two edits of one interview but does not turn it into a universal screening mandate or independent corroboration. Radiation exposure, age, baseline and family risk, likely decision impact, cost, interpretation, and follow-up should be discussed with a qualified clinician.

- Clarified that the full and Essentials episodes are one underlying recommendation and narrowed the age threshold to clinician-guided risk discussion.
