# Zhou-Qin Hybrid Order / 周秦混合制

Zhou-Qin hybrid order / 周秦混合制 enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-143-xiangyu-shi-ruhe-feijin-xinji-nongsi-chuhuaiwang-mixin-lstfzfvbd2h9i39f7y5dxrbxxwv2|Hanji 143]] as the source's interpretation of [[XiangYu|项羽]]'s post-Qin settlement. The episode argues that Xiang Yu is not simply restoring ancient Zhou feudalism after Qin's centralized empire. He is operating in a political field already shaped by [[HuaiwangAgreement|怀王之约]], surrendered Qin commanders, restored old-state rulers, and the limited military capacity of his own Chu army.

The hybrid part has two sides. The Zhou-like side is that [[MiXin|芈心 / 楚怀王]]'s promise already imagines kingship as territorial reward, and Xiang Yu's settlement creates many hereditary-looking kingdoms with their own laws, officials, armies, people, and land. The Qin-like side is that Xiang Yu uses recent military performance as the sorting principle and keeps the new kings under a hegemonic obligation to appear at court, obey the West-Chu overlord, and fight when summoned.

The concept therefore connects [[MeritBasedRewardPunishment|因功赏罚]] to postwar territorial order. Xiang Yu claims that those who wore armor and fought for three years, not a figurehead ruler with no campaign merit, should decide the distribution. This makes the system morally legible to soldiers and generals while also undermining the public promise that had made [[LiuBang|刘邦]]'s first entry into [[GuanzhongRegion|关中]] politically meaningful.

Hanji 143 also uses the concept to qualify the contrast with [[CommanderyCountyAntiEnfeoffment|郡县制反分封定局]]. Qin Shi Huang could reject enfeoffment because Qin had already conquered and administratively absorbed the world; Xiang Yu, with limited force and a coalition full of armed partners, could not immediately impose the same centralization. The source's criticism is therefore not that he failed to become Qin Shi Huang overnight, but that his hybrid order still left too many legitimacy, route-control, and reward-balance fractures.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-144-xiangyu-jituan-fumie-de-yuanyin-1-lr0ft8-jupxgvutn6c89mfs8r4wb|Hanji 144]] deepens the reward-balance fracture. The episode says Xiang Yu's design divides the field into a smaller resentful "倒项派" and a larger land-rich "拥项派," but the split does not stabilize the order because demoted kings, excluded rivals, and even Xiang Yu's own under-rewarded commanders can experience the partition as unfair. This makes [[RewardAllocationBacklash|分配反噬]] part of the hybrid order's failure, not merely an aftereffect.

## Key Claims
- Xiang Yu's settlement starts from a Zhou-like promise field but reorders it through recent military merit.
- Independent-looking kingdoms remain tied to Xiang Yu through hegemonic obligations, making the order less loose than a simple feudal reset.
- The system can be read as a constrained statecraft choice rather than only as nostalgic incompetence.
- The same hybrid quality also makes it unstable: it preserves local sovereignty while concentrating final settlement prestige in one military overlord.
- The concept explains why [[ThreeQinContainment|三秦 containment]] can be both a reward for surrendered Qin generals and a strategic lock placed around Liu Bang.
- Hanji 144 adds that the hybrid order's reward logic can produce social comparison and resentment even when the favored camp controls more territory.
