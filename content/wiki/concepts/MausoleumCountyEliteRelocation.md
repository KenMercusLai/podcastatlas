## Definition

陵县式豪强迁徙 / mausoleum-county elite relocation is the Western Han practice of moving wealthy Guandong households and senior officials' families to imperial mausoleum settlements near the capital, turning royal burial geography into population concentration, elite supervision, and status redistribution.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-526-hanchao-qipa-zhidu-huangdi-si-le-jiu-neng-qianhukou-lkht_jo0z7je4gbyk10ckuxv4cub|Hanji 526]] presents mausoleum counties as a branch of [[StrongTrunkWeakBranchCentralization|强干弱枝式集权]]. By moving rich and influential households from the Guandong region into the capital orbit, the state strengthens [[GuanzhongRegion|关中]] and weakens regional social bases that might otherwise keep wealth, followers, and local authority outside central supervision.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-281-hanwudi-jing-qiangzhi-heishehui-wei-ziji-shoumu-ljqf4fjov2gjoqwnhc8tblfvki9x|Hanji 281]] supplies an earlier Maoling case under [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]. [[ZhuFuYan|主父偃]] proposes relocating wealthy and influential local households both to populate the settlement and remove regional threats. [[GuoJieWesternHan|郭解]] is reportedly included despite falling below the three-million asset line, while [[WeiQingHanGeneral|卫青]]'s ability to intercede for him becomes evidence that political reach can override a nominal wealth screen.

The mechanism is also a status machine. Because mausoleum counties gather wealthy families, officials, land, and capital-region access, they become attractive to smaller families, new rich households, and officials whose families lack secure [[HouseholdOccupationControl|household registration]] near Chang'an. This explains why [[ChenTangWesternHan|陈汤]]'s Changlingyi memorial can combine public anti-elite policy with private family advantage.

## Key Claims

- Mausoleum counties convert imperial burial zones into administrative and demographic instruments.
- The policy strengthens the capital region while reducing the independent base of Guandong magnates.
- Nominal asset thresholds can coexist with discretionary targeting when social influence, followers, or elite access make a household politically salient.
- Relocation can be desirable to some upwardly mobile families even when old wealthy families resist it.
- Chen Tang's Changlingyi proposal is a mixed-motive case: public centralization and fiscal rationale coexist with private family household-status benefit.
- The policy's legitimacy problem is real because coerced households may lose ancestral, property, and local-network continuity while influence-based exceptions weaken predictable administration.

## Evidence

- Institutional origin: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-526-hanchao-qipa-zhidu-huangdi-si-le-jiu-neng-qianhukou-lkht_jo0z7je4gbyk10ckuxv4cub|Hanji 526]] says Western Han emperors moved Guandong rich households and high officials' families around mausoleums, gradually forming prosperous mausoleum counties.
- Maoling control case: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-281-hanwudi-jing-qiangzhi-heishehui-wei-ziji-shoumu-ljqf4fjov2gjoqwnhc8tblfvki9x|Hanji 281]] says Zhufu Yan proposes using relocation to populate Maoling and weaken local forces; Guo Jie's inclusion below the reported asset threshold makes high-level influence a practical selection signal.
- Status attraction: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-526-hanchao-qipa-zhidu-huangdi-si-le-jiu-neng-qianhukou-lkht_jo0z7je4gbyk10ckuxv4cub|Hanji 526]] says small families, new rich households, and minor officials could find mausoleum-county residence attractive because it conferred identity and resource advantages.
- Yuan-era repeal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-526-hanchao-qipa-zhidu-huangdi-si-le-jiu-neng-qianhukou-lkht_jo0z7je4gbyk10ckuxv4cub|Hanji 526]] says ministers object that relocation separates people from ancestral graves, breaks property and livelihood, violates classical norms, and unsettles powerful families.
- Changlingyi revival: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-526-hanchao-qipa-zhidu-huangdi-si-le-jiu-neng-qianhukou-lkht_jo0z7je4gbyk10ckuxv4cub|Hanji 526]] says Chen Tang proposes moving five thousand wealthy households with assets above five million into Changlingyi.
- Mixed motive: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-526-hanchao-qipa-zhidu-huangdi-si-le-jiu-neng-qianhukou-lkht_jo0z7je4gbyk10ckuxv4cub|Hanji 526]] says Chen Tang wants a permanent capital-region future for his family while also invoking land concentration, fiscal weakness, and powerful-household control.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The two episodes do not settle the full history of every Western Han mausoleum county, exact population totals around Chang'an, or the legal details of relocation and household registration. Hanji 281 does not independently establish the three-million threshold, Guo Jie's actual wealth, or whether influence was a formal criterion, discretionary exception, or retrospective explanation. Hanji 526 keeps the policy morally mixed: it is neither only corrupt self-dealing nor only public-minded statecraft, and Hanji 281 adds the risk that an anti-elite rationale can widen administrative discretion.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Hanji 526 to separate the mausoleum-county mechanism from the broader Guanzhong relocation and strong-trunk centralization pages.
- Added the Maoling case, distinguishing nominal wealth qualification from discretionary selection based on social and political reach.
