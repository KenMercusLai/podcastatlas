# Revolutionary Romanism

## Definition

Revolutionary Romanism is the French revolutionary use of early [[RomanRepublic|Roman republican]] people, objects, virtues, and civic settings as a language of liberty, sacrifice, anti-tyranny, and political belonging.

## Current Synthesis

The revolutionaries' claim to begin a new world coexisted with deep reliance on an ancient model. They preferred the early Republic after the expulsion of kings over imperial or Christian Rome, using Brutus, Horatius, Cicero, the Campus Martius, the pileus, fasces, and austere civic virtue to make republican politics visible. Before the later festival and dress culture, Brissot already used Rome to cast citizenship as sacrifice and war as a regenerative test of a free people. This vocabulary could replace weakening monarchical and clerical symbols, but it also mixed elite classical learning with street clothing, consumer objects, festivals, and armed mobilization.

## Key Claims

- Revolutionary novelty and classical imitation reinforced rather than simply contradicted one another.
- The admired Rome was selectively republican, anti-king, civic, and pre-Christian.
- Roman examples made sacrifice, citizenship, public virtue, and resistance to tyranny emotionally legible.
- Roman republican war memory helped turn military sacrifice into proof of citizenship and national regeneration.
- Objects and performances carried the symbolism beyond elite speeches and paintings.
- Roman reference fused with working-class and military identities rather than remaining an antiquarian style.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Romanism was selective historical construction, not institutional restoration. The sources emphasize early republican virtue and anti-tyranny while giving less attention to Roman hierarchy, slavery, empire, or exclusion. Roman precedent could support both civic sacrifice and aggressive war, so its moral force did not determine the legitimacy of policy. Roman symbols also changed meaning when combined with anti-clericalism, popular dress, and modern nationalism.

## What Changed

- Added the prewar rhetorical layer in which Roman citizenship and sacrifice helped justify regenerative war.
- Romanism is now qualified as a vocabulary usable for both civic virtue and armed expansion.
