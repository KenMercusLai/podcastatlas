# Secrecy-Driven Historical Myth

## Definition

Secrecy-driven historical myth is the feedback loop in which conspicuous concealment signals importance, invites explanation, and lets later storytellers turn sparse evidence into a durable identity or plot.

## Current Synthesis

The [[ManInTheIronMask]] case shows why secrecy is not merely the absence of information. [[SaintMars]]'s extraordinary precautions made observers infer that the prisoner must be important; incomplete records then left enough space for [[Voltaire]], [[AlexandreDumas]], and later theorists to supply royal, diplomatic, and sensational explanations. The process can preserve a real coercive core while distorting material details, rank, and motive. Historical correction therefore requires separating existence, identification, and causation instead of treating one strong finding as proof of all three.

## Key Claims

- Visible secrecy acts as a signal that can increase rather than suppress attention.
- Sparse archives encourage narrative substitution, especially when monarchy, legitimacy, sex, and hidden diplomacy offer compelling plots.
- Repetition by prestigious writers can make a late theory feel older and better evidenced than it is.
- A legend may retain a real institutional truth even when its literal details and proposed identity are false.
- Strong identification of a person does not automatically explain the reason for concealment.

## Evidence

### Security as publicity

- [[411-the-man-in-the-iron-mask-glt3787137343]] reports that Saint-Mars's guarded transfers made observers try to guess the prisoner's identity.

### Literary completion of archival gaps

- [[411-the-man-in-the-iron-mask-glt3787137343]] traces iron-mask mechanics and royal birth claims through Voltaire and Dumas rather than contemporary prison evidence.

### Layered historical inference

- [[411-the-man-in-the-iron-mask-glt3787137343]] supports the prisoner's existence and Degé identification more strongly than any account of his offense.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

- Secrecy does not mean every later story is false, only that narrative attraction is not evidence.
- Myth does not erase the reality of arbitrary imprisonment under Louis XIV.
- Missing correspondence could change the causal explanation even if the leading identity remains stable.
- The concept is grounded here in one case and should not be generalized to every classified or poorly documented event without independent evidence.

## What Changed

- Establishes a distinction among documented existence, probable identity, unknown cause, and culturally dominant explanation.
