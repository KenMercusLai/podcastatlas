# Colonial Survey Dispossession

## Definition

Colonial survey dispossession is the use of mapping, measurement, classification, and administrative evidence to convert conquered territory into land that can be reassigned, taxed, settled, or exploited by a ruling power.

## Current Synthesis

[[WilliamPetty]]'s Irish survey shows that evidence-based state knowledge can be technically innovative and politically violent at the same time. Mapping did not merely describe land after conquest; it helped make redistribution operational following killings, forced movement, and mass dispossession.

The concept therefore separates epistemic quality from moral neutrality. Accurate measurement can increase administrative capacity while embedding the survey inside a coercive property settlement whose categories and outputs serve the conqueror.

## Key Claims

- Surveys make territory administratively legible for transfer and extraction.
- Measurement can participate in violence without being physically violent itself.
- Scientific innovation and colonial coercion can develop together.
- Administrative categories often preserve the victor's property assumptions.
- Technical accuracy does not settle the legitimacy of the political purpose served.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode compresses the survey's institutional chronology, methods, personnel, and later land settlement. The concept identifies an enabling relationship, not a claim that mapping alone caused conquest or that every survey is colonial dispossession.

- Created a mechanism linking technical land knowledge to coerced property transfer.
