# Wearable Optical Display Friction / 可穿戴光学显示摩擦

## Definition

Wearable optical display friction is the combined visual, ergonomic, thermal, power, interaction, and social cost that determines whether display glasses feel like ordinary eyewear or intrusive equipment.

## Current Synthesis

The bounded sources show two sides of the same constraint. The RayNeo interview defines the low-friction target: transparent optics, uniform transmission, low ghosting and rainbow artifacts, low weight, stable connection, low latency, and modest display ambition. The Specs episode demonstrates what happens when a product pursues fuller spatial computing: even a roughly 40% weight reduction can leave the device at about 132-136 grams, with limited field of view, four hours of stated battery, heat and compute tradeoffs, gesture delay, and a price that narrows tolerance for compromise.

Display friction is therefore a system property rather than an optical-spec problem. A wider or richer display can demand more battery, compute, thermal capacity, frame volume, and interaction precision. The acceptable point also varies by wearing duration: short repair or training sessions can tolerate more than all-day navigation, reminders, or conversation.

## Key Claims

- Transparency, uniformity, ghosting, rainbow artifacts, and field of view shape whether overlays feel natural.
- Weight, nose and ear pressure, heat, battery, compute, latency, and connection design are part of display friction.
- More capable spatial display can worsen the very conditions required for daily wear.
- Large percentage improvements do not prove that an absolute consumer threshold has been crossed.
- Short-duration task use can tolerate higher friction than all-day assistant use.
- Display value must exceed the simpler alternative of camera sensing plus audio output.

## Evidence

- **Low-friction target:** [[no-221-leiniao-ceo-xinjishu-yuelaiyueduo-women-weishenme-hai-xuyao-yifu-zhineng-yanjing-gkwridon6uwmaoosszqset9vd]] names transparency, transmission uniformity, ghosting, rainbow effects, weight, reliability, and latency.
- **Absolute wearability threshold:** [[snap-zuole-shinian-yanjing-zhongyu-dengdao-ta-de-shidai-le-ma-s10e30-f98c4370-6653-4af3-9a43-386bf0ea0547]] reports Specs falling from 226 grams to roughly 132-136 grams while remaining too heavy for all-day wear in the speakers' judgment.
- **Display and interaction limits:** [[snap-zuole-shinian-yanjing-zhongyu-dengdao-ta-de-shidai-le-ma-s10e30-f98c4370-6653-4af3-9a43-386bf0ea0547]] reports a 51-degree field of view, gesture latency, unstable virtual objects under load, battery uncertainty, and comfort pressure.
- **Duration sensitivity:** [[snap-zuole-shinian-yanjing-zhongyu-dengdao-ta-de-shidai-le-ma-s10e30-f98c4370-6653-4af3-9a43-386bf0ea0547]] finds task-specific assembly, repair, or training more plausible than continuous use and notes that weight can undermine even a meditation application.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

- The Specs assessment is based on limited hands-on exposure rather than long-duration controlled testing.
- Reported specifications and ordinary-glasses weight comparisons are source-scoped.
- Some users or enterprise jobs may accept heavy hardware when the task benefit is large.
- Lower display friction alone does not create demand without a compelling application and acceptable privacy.

## What Changed

- The concept now includes system-level compute, heat, battery, field-of-view, and interaction tradeoffs.
- Specs adds an absolute-threshold warning: a large percentage improvement can still miss daily-wear acceptability.
- Wearing duration is now explicit in the product-fit judgment.
