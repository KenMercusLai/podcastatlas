# Nonviolent Symbolic Power

Nonviolent symbolic power is the political force a figure can retain through legitimacy, memory, and mass recognition rather than through armed command. [[the-case-of-the-missing-totem-aung-san-suu-kyi-6a560fc4f96471dde4821d2f]] adds it through [[AungSanSuuKyi]], whom the [[MyanmarMilitaryJunta]] may still fear despite armed resistance now existing across [[Myanmar]].

The concept helps explain why release or verified access can feel risky to a regime. The episode says some analysts think the military may fear Aung San Suu Kyi's nonviolent symbolic power more than armed resistance, because her name still organizes loyalty and could complicate domestic politics or international normalization.

## Key Claims
- Symbolic power can survive detention when supporters continue to take risks in a person's name.
- Nonviolent legitimacy can threaten a military regime even when armed groups are the visible battlefield opponents.
- Releasing a symbolic figure can divide opposition movements if the figure's older nonviolence conflicts with newer armed strategies.
- International actors may overestimate or underestimate symbolic power depending on whether they focus on diplomacy, battlefield dynamics, or public memory.
