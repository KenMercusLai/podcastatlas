# Political Survival First / 安全第一政治生存

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-902-kongzhi-qingxu-shi-rensheng-bixiuke-ljynylvdlsdtjf4es9jmilav863b|Hanji 902]] adds a late-Han humbled-survival version through [[ZhangHuanLateHan|张奂]]. After [[WangYuLateHan|王寓]]'s accusation and [[DuanJiongLateHan|段熲]]'s threat to send Zhang toward a dangerous exile route, Zhang writes to Duan in a lowered posture and survives. The source treats this not as moral beauty but as preserved agency: Zhang returns home, withdraws from office, teaches, writes, and dies old.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-918-shiqing-bu-shunli-shi-weishenme-yao-bizui-lh-tbohgo96qw8bjqu3x1yp6czv5|Hanji 918]] adds a negative court-conspiracy version. [[LiuHeSituLateHan|刘郃]] sees that [[CaoJieLateHan|曹节]] and the eunuch side have many eyes and ears, but he still accepts [[ChenQiuLateHan|陈球]] and [[LiuNaLateHan|刘纳]]'s moral pressure and joins a plan that cannot protect itself from [[YangQiuLateHan|阳球]]'s household leak. Survival-first politics here is the discipline Liu He recognizes too late: without secrecy and timing, righteous action may only create a death route.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-925-tuoxie-bushi-rensong-xuehui-tuoxie-lu-geng-changliang-limafqskgblrwetestyh39w-hzm4|Hanji 925]] adds a compromise-as-survival branch through [[GongsunHong|公孙弘]]. The episode treats Gongsun Hong's advice to [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] as a way to keep future influence alive: he does not directly deny the ruler's wishes, but narrows attention between the [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] front and southwest development.

This source is adjacent to survival-first politics because the goal is not just to avoid punishment. It becomes [[StrategicCompromiseAsAgency|策略性妥协保全机会]]: protected access and continued agency are the political asset being preserved.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-954-renxingbenshan-haishi-renxingbene-lkurz9dbb25jrjyl5c-rrahda39h|Hanji 954]] adds a title-refusal survival case through [[LiuYuLateHan|刘虞]]. Yuan Shao and [[HanFuLateHan|韩馥]] offer Liu Yu the imperial title, then a fallback role supervising memorials and appointments, but Liu Yu refuses both and considers fleeing to [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] rather than becoming the coalition's figurehead.

The source treats this as more than abstract loyalty. Liu Yu's son [[LiuHeLateHan|刘和]] is in Chang'an under [[DongZhuo|董卓]]'s reach, and the Yuan-family massacre makes household retaliation credible. Survival-first politics here means avoiding a title that would transfer other actors' ambition and risk onto Liu Yu's family while also preserving loyalty to [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-956-yongren-he-zhizhe-jiujing-chaju-zai-nali-lphee8cj-7gcmg-cynpiphjvauuf|Hanji 956]] supplies the pre-collapse warning that survival language can become a trap when separated from real conditions. [[HanFuLateHan|韩馥]] yields [[JizhouLateHan|冀州]] because it appears to preserve safety and reputation, but the source shows subordinates arguing that his troops and grain still make resistance safer than surrender. This makes Han Fu a negative survival-first case before Hanji 957 shows the resulting post-power safety collapse.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-957-ta-ceng-xiongba-yifang-weihe-ruonuo-dao-zai-cesuo-zijin-lnkhah144822mc4aa9bnezklgvoo|Hanji 957]] adds a late-Han contrast between failed and usable survival. [[HanFuLateHan|韩馥]] loses [[JizhouLateHan|冀州]], loses protective value, faces [[ZhuHanLateHan|朱瀚]]'s revenge, and then kills himself after misreading a private exchange at [[ZhangMiaoLateHan|张邈]]'s [[Chenliu|陈留]] office as a murder signal.

The same episode shows more functional survival logic elsewhere. [[BaoXin|鲍信]] advises [[CaoCao|曹操]] to take [[DongCommandery|东郡]] as a safer strategic base before [[YuanShao|袁绍]]'s new Jizhou position becomes overwhelming, while [[ZhangYangLateHan|张杨]] survives through changing attachments among Yuan Shao, the [[SouthernXiongnu|南匈奴]], Puyang, and [[DongZhuo|董卓]]'s Henei appointment. Survival-first politics is therefore not panic; it is the ability to preserve agency by choosing routes, patrons, and bases before danger closes.

Political survival first / 安全第一政治生存 is the rule [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-150-chenping-weihe-bei-cheng-zhiwei-chenggong-zhengzhijia-yangban-3-lhfbm3twyxhyvokphf8hc9ssblqj|Hanji 150 part 3]] extracts from [[ChenPing|陈平]]'s career. The source treats early Han court politics as a field where a wrong stand can mean clan destruction, so survival is not a secondary concern after policy; it is the condition that lets any policy actor keep operating.

The concept is not simple cowardice. In the episode's reading, Chen Ping survives because he keeps room to maneuver, reads human danger, avoids irreversible commitments when the power field is unclear, and shifts only when the dominant structure has actually changed. His conduct under [[LuZhi|吕雉]] is therefore kept source-scoped as tactical ambiguity rather than settled proof that he belongs to the Lu faction.

The concept also explains why "good" and "effective" diverge in this source. Chen Ping can look slippery, morally compromised, and cold, yet the same discretion lets him remain alive long enough to help [[ZhouBo|周勃]] and the Liu-family restoration after Lu Zhi's death. Survival-first politics preserves agency by limiting exposure.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-965-bangguo-bieren-de-mang-weishenme-yao-shao-ti-ltjd-cv59hnm-n8ar9jxdogp9q0q|Hanji 965]] adds a late-Han group-survival version before Jia Xu's advice is carried out in Hanji 966. Dong Zhuo's old commanders fear that if they disband, rumors, Wang Yun's refusal of amnesty, and examples of punished Dong Zhuo associates will leave them without protection. Jia Xu's proposal to keep troops together and move on Chang'an is therefore framed as survival-first politics at the group level, while the later outcome keeps the moral risk visible through [[ExecutorCharacterAdviceRisk]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-967-ta-canyu-mousha-dongzhuo-jingneng-taoguo-sishen-weilie-lv24biyswj9ljdld5htynhauhph3|Hanji 967]] adds two late-Han forms. [[ShiSunRuiLateHan|士孙瑞]] survives the anti-[[DongZhuo|董卓]] aftermath because he does not publicize or demand credit, making hidden merit a protection for the clan. [[JiaXu|贾诩]] survives by advising [[LiJueLateHan|李傕]] and [[GuoSiLateHan|郭汜]] against disbanding, refusing reward afterward, and later lowering his own visibility; the source keeps the effective survival result together with the moral risk of releasing violent executors into [[ChangAnHanCapital|长安]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-985-fanmu-chengchou-liguo-zhiluan-de-xijie-tanjiu-lt-t2un0zcudkige9ucl1txa2hhk|Hanji 985]] adds a captivity-restraint version before Hanji 988's exit sequence. [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]] is angry after [[LiJueLateHan|李傕]] sends rotten bones instead of usable food for starving attendants, but [[YangQiLateHan|杨琦]] tells him not to speak out because Li Jue may retaliate or move him farther away. Survival-first politics here is not strategic advantage; it is suppressing justified anger so the captive ruler's body and remaining attendants are not put at greater risk.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-988-shoujin-lingru-ta-shi-hanmo-beican-de-huangdi-lsdkpakvk5u-omegcri-wstup-zl|Hanji 988]] adds a bodily humiliation and food version immediately before Hanji 989. [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]] survives because threats are handled piecemeal: [[JiaXu|贾诩]] removes the Qiang-Hu harassment through promised rewards, [[LiuAiLateHan|刘艾]] helps make the emperor visible to blocking soldiers, and [[ZhangJiLateHan|张济]] later distributes inadequate food at Baling. Survival-first politics here is not strategy from strength; it is the improvised preservation of the ruler's body when normal court logistics have collapsed.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-989-ningwei-taipingquan-buzuo-luanshi-ren-lkoycygmjnltuvpq-6wjv-ls61s6|Hanji 989]] adds a hunger, fire, and supply version through [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]] and [[DuanWeiLateHan|段煨]]. The emperor's refusal to eat is a desperate survival signal when [[GuoSiLateHan|郭汜]] blocks his route, while Duan Wei's supplies at [[HuayinLateHan|华阴]] show that survival can depend on accepting food without surrendering camp control. The episode's title frames late-Han disorder as a world where even the ruler's safety turns on immediate bodily needs and rumor management.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-990-lijue-guosi-shengsi-yuanjia-weihe-turan-lianshou-lmop5mcrsjuq-ixuv0sfho5mvc-f|Hanji 990]] adds a local strongman version through [[DuanWeiLateHan|段煨]]. Duan Wei resists [[YangDingLateHan|杨定]]'s attack while continuing to feed [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]] and officials, then the episode uses his later submission to [[CaoCao|曹操]] and death in office as a source-scoped contrast with Yang Ding's failed gamble. Survival-first politics here is not escape from danger, but refusing to turn a defensible base into a doomed bid for imperial control.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-991-zhengduo-xiandi-zhizhan-shui-neng-zuizhong-qusheng-lkbsl5uzqxouzm6mlewrnuns0x6p|Hanji 991]] adds a crisis-flight version through [[YangBiao|杨彪]], [[DongChengLateHan|董承]], [[FuShouEmpress|伏皇后]], and [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]]. Yang Biao rejects a dangerous river route proposed under pressure and arranges a secret night crossing, while Dong Cheng's soldiers use quilts as an improvised shield after pursuers notice the escape. This is survival-first politics at its most physical: preserving the ruler's body and a few officials requires abandoning normal ritual order, and many followers are left exposed.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-995-ta-zuishang-cibei-weihe-duoci-cansha-enren-llicukoezccd5iycin4uq7qun51k|Hanji 995]] adds a route-and-contact version through [[LiuYaoLateHan|刘繇]] and [[XuShao|许劭]]. Xu Shao's advice favors [[YuzhangCommandery|豫章郡]] over [[KuaijiCommandery|会稽郡]] because survival depends on fallback routes, official legitimacy, and the chance of help from [[CaoCao|曹操]] or [[LiuBiao|刘表]]. The same episode then shows the limit of geographic prudence: sending [[ZeRong|笮融]] to assist [[ZhuHaoLateHan|朱皓]] fails because Ze Rong is a no-reciprocity actor.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1000-hao-meng-panluan-jingbi-lvbu-fanqiang-taopao-lluthd9hv-0nqbot4fuctjcuka68|Hanji 1000]] adds a factional non-prosecution version through [[LyuBu|吕布]]. After [[CaoXingLateHan|曹性]] names [[ChenGongLateHan|陈宫]] in [[HaoMeng|郝萌]]'s revolt, Lü Bu does not punish Chen Gong because the source reads Chen Gong as a necessary Yanzhou士族 counterweight to the Bingzhou military core around [[GaoShun|高顺]] and [[ZhangLiao|张辽]]. Survival-first politics here means tolerating unresolved guilt to avoid breaking the coalition needed to keep governing.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1004-caocao-yingfeng-hanxiandi-shi-shui-de-zhuyi-lrrga-flmx5ajnhu1c42x7kooe94|Hanji 1004]] adds a court-site version through [[DongZhaoLateHan|董昭]]'s advice to [[CaoCao|曹操]]. Staying in [[LuoyangLateHan|洛阳]] would leave Cao Cao exposed to competing generals, damaged infrastructure, and supply failure, while moving [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]] to [[Xudu|许县 / 许都]] protects the court and Cao Cao's new position through geography and food.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1005-sunce-zhuding-zaoshi-shi-xingge-jueding-mingyun-lpqnere2cwcrngggyezd-bsqahx4|Hanji 1005]] adds a ruler-body version through [[SunCe|孙策]]. [[YuFan|虞翻]] warns that Sun Ce's rank and mission make solo hunting different from ordinary recreation: if guards cannot prepare and followers cannot keep up, personal pleasure turns into political exposure. This branch is captured more narrowly as [[LeaderPersonalExposure|领导者轻身涉险]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-998-baima-jiangjun-gongsunzan-cong-sanqian-baima-dao-kunshou-gucheng-lp-1l2gvbsbfnyznutdghxwwvqna|Hanji 998]] adds a negative bodily-safety version through [[GongsunZan|公孙瓒]]. His move into [[YijingFortress|易京]] lowers immediate exposure but also eliminates adviser access, field initiative, and the ability to repair [[YouzhouLateHan|幽州]] support. The episode therefore separates survival discipline from mere hiding: safety that destroys future action becomes [[FortressSecurityIllusion|筑城避世安全幻觉]], not a useful survival-first strategy.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1030-shouwo-hao-pai-gongsunzan-weihe-da-de-xilan-lpkntyov4viqyzgxgcujn36qhv8|Hanji 1030]] sharpens the same negative case with the source's "消极自保" language. Gongsun Zan's fortress posture does not preserve future action because the rescue relationships needed for action have already been broken. Once [[YuanShao|袁绍]] reaches Yijing, leaving with elite cavalry may look like abandonment, while staying preserves only enclosure.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-972-sanguo-liuyu-zhisi-taisha-taitianzhen-lvawrcandbh628rckukosgarh7v2|Hanji 972]] adds the positive loyal-envoy version through [[TianChou|田筹]]. Tian Chou avoids official banners, selects only twenty riders, takes a frontier route through [[JuyongPass|居庸关]], and refuses office from [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]] because the court is displaced. After [[LiuYuLateHan|刘虞]] is killed, his survival discipline does not erase loyalty; it preserves enough agency to rebuke [[GongsunZan|公孙瓒]] and later build order in [[XuWuMountain|徐无山]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-996-luanshi-zanghong-jiangyiqi-de-ren-xiachang-zui-biequ-lgpa-m6h6bvhyintnuj6zztopui76|Hanji 996]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-997-zanghong-zhisi-shi-yibaoyuntian-haishi-qingjie-nandu-lln2liblq-kyjrsvitnbh7cspbax|Hanji 997]] add a negative loyalty-rigidity version through [[ZangHong|臧洪]]. Hanji 996 shows him choosing [[DongCommandery|东郡]] resistance after [[YuanShao|袁绍]] refuses to rescue [[ZhangChaoLateHan|张超]], while Hanji 997 shows him still refusing submission after capture. The sources respect the courage but treat the refusal as a failure to preserve future agency: [[EmotionalAttachmentPoliticalDistortion|情执政治判断失衡]] closes paths before and after the siege.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-150-chenping-weihe-bei-cheng-zhiwei-chenggong-zhengzhijia-yangban-4-lhhprrkjcuzijgndi2uuazaalern|Hanji 150 part 4]] makes the discipline more concrete. Chen Ping avoids visible factional power under Liu Bang, uses [[TacticalDelayRiskTransfer|延时转责式政治化解]] in the [[FanKuai|樊哙]] affair, performs loyalty before [[LuZhi|吕雉]] after Liu Bang's death, and later hides his tie to Zhou Bo until the anti-Lu opportunity exists. The source's survival claim is therefore not passive endurance; it is controlled exposure until timing makes action possible.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-182-weishenme-shuo-chengye-xiaohe-baiye-xiaohe-3-lq9fif0ow90zsyrmovhwxb7-qicr|Hanji 182 part 3]] adds Han Xin as a negative contrast. The episode says Han Xin could read battle but not politics: he did not sufficiently study history, motive, or how people change when class position and power incentives change. In this light, [[PoliticalSurvivalFirst|安全第一政治生存]] is not praised because it is morally clean; it is necessary because brilliance without motive-reading leaves even a victorious commander exposed.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1006-guojia-buman-yuanshao-weihe-que-neng-guishun-caocao-lup-p-inz1sdlhpnaggqjp0eavar|Hanji 1006]] adds a late-Han adviser-style version through [[XunYou|荀攸]]. The source presents him as outwardly plain and inwardly intelligent, useful precisely because he keeps secrets, avoids overexposure, and knows how to remain in the political system after high-stakes counsel. The contrast with [[GuoJia|郭嘉]] shows that survival-first discipline can coexist with strategic ability rather than replacing it.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1007-cong-shentong-dao-fuwang-kongrong-zhenshi-mianmu-puguang-lkiljj4yvl5fhslrc9gcxudxxklt|Hanji 1007]] adds a local-governor negative case through [[KongRong|孔融]]. The source presents Kong Rong as isolated in [[BeihaiCommandery|北海郡]] while short of troops and grain, then shows him rejecting [[ZuoChengzu|左承祖]]'s advice to rely on a stronger power. [[LiuYixun|刘义逊]]'s departure after Zuo's killing is the survival-first response on the adviser side: when honest advice is unsafe, leaving preserves agency.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1017-sanguo-yincang-yingxiong-chendeng-de-chuanqi-wangshi-lhcvtm8fobuzjfyo9uirdcgx2plq|Hanji 1017]] adds a late-Han family version through [[ChenGui|陈珪]] and [[ChenDeng|陈登]]. The Chen family does not simply pick the strongest visible actor at once. The episode reads its survival as staged: use [[LyuBu|吕布]] while he balances immediate danger, build capacity through offices such as [[GuanglingCommandery|广陵]], and keep a long-term path to [[CaoCao|曹操]] for the post-Lü-Bu order.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1016-sanguo-luanshi-li-chengui-jiazu-de-mingyun-jueze-ltuckdcxpyxk-xmzp8rt-2gjfzcs|Hanji 1016]] supplies the prior decision point for that family version. Chen Gui refuses [[YuanShu|袁术]] despite old friendship and pressure through Chen Deng, then blocks the Yuan Shu-[[LyuBu|吕布]] marriage because it would expose [[Xuzhou|徐州]] and tie the family to an illegitimate claim. Survival-first politics here is not flight from principle; it is the choice of a legitimacy field and war-risk path that preserves future room.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1011-miheng-zhisi-zuiju-beiju-secai-de-jiedao-sharen-ltrzpchwdfcyp4ntmum9giu-5mva|Hanji 1011]] adds a negative late-Han example through [[MiHengLateHan|祢衡]]. Mi Heng has talent and moral independence, but the source argues that unmanaged pride and speech turn him into material for [[BorrowedKnifeKilling|借刀杀人]] by [[CaoCao|曹操]], [[LiuBiao|刘表]], and [[HuangZu|黄祖]]. The host's comparison to [[JiaXu|贾诩]] makes survival-first politics a discipline of hiding sharpness when direct expression only supplies stronger actors with a route to remove you.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1010-sanguo-liubiao-jiujing-shibushi-piaoliang-de-caobao-lo58hzfo-qv0soauogtgfrkourrd|Hanji 1010]] makes that Jia Xu contrast concrete. Jia Xu leaves [[DuanWeiLateHan|段煨]] despite good treatment because he reads Duan Wei's suspicion and Jia Xu's own prestige as a future risk, then moves to [[ZhangXiu|张绣]] because Zhang Xiu needs the advice more. Survival-first judgment here means evaluating host incentives, not only current hospitality.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-977-yi-zheng-yi-xie-luanshi-dushi-de-guijue-fengyun-lgtpgxl5anxial8ym2nt8dmuc3ee|Hanji 977]] adds a generalized adviser version. ## Key Claims
- Hanji 902 adds that lowering one's posture can preserve life and future non-office work when a captured court makes dignity itself dangerous.
- Hanji 918 adds that political survival can fail when moral urgency overrides an actor's own accurate diagnosis of surveillance and leakage risk.
- Hanji 925 adds that compromise can be a survival-first move when it preserves access and agency under a powerful ruler.
- Hanji 956 adds that a choice presented as survival can be self-defeating if it gives up the troops, grain, office, and adviser environment that made survival possible.
- Hanji 957 adds a late-Han contrast: Han Fu's panic shows failed post-power survival, while Bao Xin's base advice and Zhang Yang's attachment changes show survival logic that preserves agency.
- Hanji 965 adds that survival-first politics can operate at group level when disbanding removes the only credible protection an exposed military faction still has.
- Hanji 967 adds that survival-first politics can mean refusing visible credit after a dangerous deed, or refusing reward after effective advice whose consequences are morally contaminated.
- Hanji 977 adds that survival-first politics can describe an adviser's managed visibility: the adviser survives by knowing when to expose or hide the dangerous part of his usefulness.
- Hanji 985 adds that survival-first politics can require suppressing justified anger under captivity when the coercive host controls food, doors, and relocation.
- Hanji 988 adds that survival-first politics can shrink to bodily protection, public identification, and emergency food when normal imperial logistics collapse.
- Hanji 989 adds that survival-first politics can be as immediate as hunger protest, fire escape, and cautious use of a supplier's camp.
- Hanji 990 adds that survival-first politics can mean holding a local base, sustaining useful supply, and later submitting at the right time instead of grabbing the emperor-control prize.
- Hanji 991 adds that survival-first politics can require bodily extraction under disorder, where saving the central person means accepting ritual collapse and collateral abandonment.
- Hanji 995 adds that survival-first politics must assess both routes and people: a safer geography can still fail if the entrusted actor has no reciprocal floor.
- Hanji 996 and Hanji 997 add that principled refusal can become a survival failure when it makes no distinction between moral memory, follower safety, and future agency.
- Hanji 1005 adds that survival discipline can apply directly to a leader's body, movement, and guard boundary, not only to factional alignment.
- Hanji 998 adds that bodily safety can become self-defeating when it severs initiative, counsel, and political repair.
- Hanji 1030 adds that passive self-protection fails when earlier non-rescue has destroyed the reciprocal command trust needed for future action.
- Hanji 972 adds that survival-first discipline can coexist with loyalty when it preserves the actor's ability to deliver a mission, speak at the tomb, and build later order.
- Hanji 1004 adds that survival discipline can apply to the court's physical location when the symbolic capital is too ruined and factionalized to secure.
- Hanji 1006 adds that a strategist can preserve influence by controlling visibility, secrecy, and self-exposure rather than seeking dramatic credit.
- Hanji 1007 adds that refusing dependence can be a survival failure when local capacity is insufficient, and that advisers may preserve themselves by leaving once correction becomes punishable.
- Hanji 1011 adds that talent without self-protection can be actively weaponized by stronger actors through indirect danger routing.
- Hanji 1010 adds that good treatment can still be unsafe when the host has reason to fear a guest's prestige.
- Hanji 182 part 3 adds that the absence of survival-first judgment can make military genius politically unusable after the victory order changes.
- Hanji 1016 adds that family survival can depend on refusing a dangerous title or alliance even when the refusal creates immediate hostage and retaliation risk.
- Hanji 1017 adds that survival-first politics can operate at family scale, using staged alignment rather than a single irreversible camp choice.
- Hanji 1000 adds that survival-first politics can appear as factional non-prosecution when punishing one suspect would damage the alliance structure a warlord needs.
- In a lethal court, self-preservation can become a political operating discipline rather than a private weakness.
- Survival-first actors avoid closing every path before succession, regency, or factional outcomes are known.
- The strategy can preserve useful agency, but it also damages moral legibility because outsiders may read prudence as opportunism.
- Chen Ping's case links personal safety, hidden work, factional timing, and later restoration politics into one survival pattern.
- Survival can require visible compliance, feigned distance from allies, and delay of irreversible orders until the authority field changes.
