# Social Reporting Burnout / 社会报道消耗

Social reporting burnout / 社会报道消耗 is the cost side of [[JournalisticPublicness|journalistic publicness]] in [[ep244-jizhe-yanzhong-de-hao-gongzuo-shenme-yang-lk8iz9cllzx2jou5-sw9yt2ogpyb]]. [[WeiQianSanlian|魏茜]], [[WuShubin|吴淑彬]], and [[LiXiaojie|李小杰]] describe social news as meaningful because it reaches public problems and unfamiliar worlds, but also as draining because the scene often does not welcome reporters.

The episode ties burnout to repeated confrontation, cognitive overload, online suspicion, personal life changes, and family care responsibilities. It presents journalism as a role that may fit unattached youth more easily than people whose own lives have heavy illness, caregiving, or emotional demands.

## Key Claims
- Social reporting concentrates both the highest public meaning and the highest emotional cost of journalism.
- Reporters can be treated as troublemakers by institutions, subjects, and online audiences at the same time.
- Family illness or caregiving can reduce the spare curiosity and emotional capacity that reporting demands.
- Burnout pushes some reporters toward longer-cycle, less confrontational topics or toward seeing journalism more plainly as a job.
