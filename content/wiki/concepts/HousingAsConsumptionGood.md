# Housing As Consumption Good / 房子消费品化

Housing as consumption good is [[131-wo-zai-riben-mai-le-yi-tao-zizhu-fang-llofkgaj-hyajksrpkoylakuocxz]]'s frame for why many Japanese homes should be judged by use, maintenance, and remaining life rather than by automatic appreciation. The episode says buildings often lose value with age, while scarce central land, durable location demand, management quality, and repair discipline carry more investment significance.

The concept extends [[HousingExperienceInvestmentSplit]]. A self-use home can be rational because it improves commute, comfort, daily services, and independence. But once a buyer treats the property as an investment, the relevant questions change to liquidity, depreciation, tax, repair reserve, exit demand, rental demand, and whether the land can keep attracting users.

## Key Claims
- A home can create real lived value even when the building is a depreciating asset.
- New-build units can lose value quickly in early years, then flatten as age effects slow and land or management quality becomes more important.
- Condo value depends partly on collective management and repair, not only private interior quality.
- Detached-house value can migrate toward land value as the structure ages.
- Treating every home as an investment can hide carrying costs and the risk of [[NegativeRealEstate]].
