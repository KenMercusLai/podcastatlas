# Congressional War-Powers Constraint

## Definition

Congressional war-powers constraint is the practical limitation on unilateral presidential military action created by legislation, funding refusal, oversight, and the loss of political authorization.

## Current Synthesis

The episode uses Vietnam after Watergate to show that a foreign-policy promise is only as credible as the domestic authority behind it. The Case-Church Amendment prohibited renewed U.S. military intervention in Indochina without explicit congressional approval, while the War Powers Resolution narrowed unilateral presidential action more broadly. Congress then refused Ford's requests for additional aid as public support for the war had collapsed.

This constraint did not itself cause South Vietnam's military weakness. It changed the strategic environment by making Nixon's earlier retaliation assurance noncredible and leaving Ford unable or unwilling to restore the scale of support on which South Vietnam had depended.

## Key Claims

- Domestic constitutional authority shapes whether international commitments can be enforced.
- Scandal can weaken foreign-policy deterrence by reducing presidential political power.
- Funding refusal can constrain policy even when executive officials still favor aid or force.
- Legislative limits and public opinion can reinforce one another without being identical.
- Constraint explains narrowed U.S. options, not the whole cause of allied collapse.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

- The concept does not establish that Congress caused South Vietnam's defeat or that requested funds and bombing would have produced survival.
- The episode compresses legal detail; the exact scope, timing, and constitutional interpretation of each measure remain source-scoped here.
- Presidential choice, military feasibility, public opinion, and legislative authority should not be collapsed into one cause.

## What Changed

- Established the link between domestic constitutional authority and the credibility of an external security promise.
- Distinguished a constraint on intervention from a complete explanation of allied collapse.
