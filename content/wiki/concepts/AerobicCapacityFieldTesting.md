# Aerobic Capacity Field Testing / 有氧能力场地测试

## Definition
Aerobic capacity field testing is the episode's low-cost route to an estimated VO2 max when laboratory gas collection is unavailable, using a 12-minute run test or a submaximal one-mile walk test fed into a calculator.

## Current Synthesis
Galpin names the laboratory VO2 max test with a mask and gas collection as the gold standard, then offers two substitutes. The 12-minute Cooper test asks the person to run as far as possible and enter the distance into an online calculator, while the one-mile walk test is submaximal and needs a stopwatch or ideally a heart-rate monitor, after which time and ending heart rate are entered into a calculator.

The reference values he gives are a minimum of about 35 milliliters per kilogram per minute for men and 30 for women, with a preference for men above 50 and women above 50, and he says he is not really satisfied below 55 for men. The one-mile walk test is recommended for people who cannot or do not want to run hard, including those with knee or back pain, which is what keeps maximal aerobic capacity testable outside a lab.

## Key Claims
- Laboratory VO2 max testing with a mask and gas collection is the gold standard and follows a specific protocol for a true maximum test.
- The 12-minute Cooper test and the submaximal one-mile walk test give estimated VO2 max values without lab access when distance, time, and heart rate are entered into a calculator.
- Reference values are a minimum of about 35 for men and 30 for women, with 50-plus as the target range and men below 55 described as not satisfying.
- The one-mile walk test exists for people who cannot or do not want to run hard, including those with knee or back pain.
- VO2 max is positioned as a central health marker alongside strength and muscle mass rather than as a performance-only metric.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Estimated VO2 max from field tests carries meaningful error and depends on pacing, motivation, terrain, and the prediction equation used. Reference values are population benchmarks that shift with age and baseline health, and a maximal run test is inappropriate without appropriate screening for people with cardiovascular disease, uncontrolled blood pressure, or musculoskeletal pain, which is why the episode offers the submaximal walk version as a substitute rather than a lesser option.
