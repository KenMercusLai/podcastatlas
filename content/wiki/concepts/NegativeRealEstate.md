# Negative Real Estate / 负动产

Negative real estate is [[131-wo-zai-riben-mai-le-yi-tao-zizhu-fang-llofkgaj-hyajksrpkoylakuocxz]]'s post-bubble warning that property can become an obligation rather than a desirable asset. The episode uses depopulating rural areas, weak-demand resort regions, and hard-to-sell older property as examples where ownership may bring tax, repair, management, inheritance, and disposal burdens without reliable buyers.

The concept extends the wiki's [[JapaneseLostDecades]] branch. The source treats Japan's real-estate bust not only as price decline but as a mental and institutional change: after the bubble, people could no longer assume land and buildings would always be liquid, scarce, and wealth-preserving.

## Key Claims
- Real estate value depends on population, jobs, transport, services, maintenance, and future users, not only on ownership rights.
- A very low or zero purchase price can hide future carrying and disposal costs.
- Depreciating structures and weak land demand make remote or poorly connected property especially vulnerable.
- The concept pushes against the belief that permanent property rights automatically mean permanent investment value.
