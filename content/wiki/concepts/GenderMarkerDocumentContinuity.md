# Gender-Marker Document Continuity / 性别标记与证件连续性

## Definition
Gender-marker document continuity is the problem of keeping identity cards, household registration, education credentials, online verification, employment records, and prior experience usable and privacy-preserving after a legal sex-marker change.

## Current Synthesis
The episode distinguishes changing a primary identity record from repairing an entire documentary history. It describes a relatively defined post-surgery route for household-registration and identity-card changes, but says diplomas and degree certificates are often treated as historically correct when issued and therefore difficult to amend. A school letter may preserve proof of graduation without replacing the original record, while online verification can still reveal mismatched information.

Continuity is therefore more than possession of a new card. A workable system must connect the same person across old and new records while minimizing forced disclosure, fraud suspicion, repeated explanation, and employment disadvantage. The source also identifies an equity gap: tying administrative recognition to surgery leaves people who do not want, cannot access, or are not eligible for surgery with fewer options.

## Key Claims
- Changing one identity document does not automatically align education and employment records.
- Historically accurate issuance rules can conflict with present identity and privacy.
- Explanatory letters preserve proof but can force repeated disclosure.
- Online verification can undermine paper-based workarounds.
- Surgery-gated recognition excludes some transgender people from document alignment.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source gives a December 2024 discussion, not primary statutes, regulations, agency rules, university policies, or case law. Procedures can differ by institution and change over time. The stated post-surgery route and diploma limitations require current legal and administrative verification; the page does not provide legal advice.
