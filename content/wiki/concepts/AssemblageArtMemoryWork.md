# Assemblage Art Memory Work

Assemblage art memory work is the use of found, discarded, or inherited objects to make historical violence, identity, and liberation visible. [[outsider-chance-ending-sudans-proxy-war-6a75b3695d765781a2b1b132]] grounds the concept in [[BetyeSaar]], who treated objects from flea markets, yard sales, and market stalls as materials carrying the energy of previous owners.

The source links Saar's method to slavery and Black history. Rope, ships, chains, birdcages, canoes, pleading hands, branches, and stones are not neutral props in the episode; they become ways to materialize captivity, journey, restricted life, and possible transformation.

[[TheLiberationOfAuntJemima]] gives the concept its sharpest example. By altering a racist commercial figure with weapons and a Black Power fist, Saar changed a stereotype into a confrontational image of agency and resistance.

## Key Claims
- Found objects can carry social and historical meaning because their prior uses remain legible.
- Assemblage can reassign power to objects originally tied to degradation, confinement, or stereotype.
- Memory work becomes material when viewers confront the physical texture of ropes, cages, tools, toys, figures, or discarded domestic goods.
