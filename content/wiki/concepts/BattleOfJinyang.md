# Battle of Jinyang / 晋阳之战

## Definition
The Battle of Jinyang is the siege in which Zhi Yao's coalition nearly destroys Zhao, then collapses when Wei and Han join Zhao, reverse the flood, and destroy the Zhi house; its enabling conditions were created earlier through prepared refuge, coerced territorial concessions, and accumulated resentment.

## Current Synthesis
The two sources present Jinyang as a political-military system prepared before the siege. Zhao Jianzi chooses Jinyang as a fallback and Yin Duo converts lighter burdens plus defenses into future endurance. Zhi Yao's earlier humiliation and land demands lead Han and Wei to concede tactically while waiting for his expansion to create shared enemies. During the siege, hydraulic innovation brings the city close to collapse but reveals how the same technique could threaten the allies; Chi Ci sees the incentive fracture but cannot make counsel effective; and Zhang Mengtan converts shared fear into coordinated defection. Victory comes from the interaction of long-horizon governance, tactical concession, engineering, intelligence, secrecy, and alliance reversal rather than from one heroic act.

## Key Claims
- Defensive capacity begins before the siege because a prepared refuge turns ordinary tax, public-burden, and defense policy into survival capacity before the crisis is visible.
- Coerced allies can concede in the short term while allowing the dominant actor's appetite to manufacture a countercoalition.
- Hydraulic attack can reduce assault costs while creating an example that frightens exposed allies.
- Coalition labels conceal sequential-elimination fears when one member is much stronger than the others.
- Accurate counsel has no corrective value if the principal dismisses it and exposes the warning.
- Zhang Mengtan's persuasion works because it names an existing common interest and provides a secret route to act on it.
- Reversing the dike turns the attacker's infrastructure into the physical mechanism of defeat.

## Evidence
- Social base and endurance: Zhao Wuxu rejects Changzi and Handan, chooses Jinyang for its accumulated public loyalty, and holds through flood and famine. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-03-weiju-hanhu-wujiandao-lkiwf0zdnylmaqjgsix-dktxea0l|Zhouji 03]]
- Pre-siege preparation and enemy-making: Zhao Jianzi commissions Jinyang as a refuge, while Duan Gui and Ren Zhang turn Zhi Yao's land demands into deliberate concessions expected to widen resistance. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-02-jiangyu-baizhi-bi-gufuzhi-lglk2_eu1uvegdac8sj6bfuwv1dw|Zhouji 02]]
- Engineering and threat perception: Zhi Yao floods the city, then makes Wei and Han recognize that Anyi and Pingyang are similarly exposed. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-03-weiju-hanhu-wujiandao-lkiwf0zdnylmaqjgsix-dktxea0l|Zhouji 03]]
- Intelligence and failed feedback: Chi Ci predicts defection from the allies' behavior, but Zhi Yao rejects and leaks the warning. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-03-weiju-hanhu-wujiandao-lkiwf0zdnylmaqjgsix-dktxea0l|Zhouji 03]]
- Coalition reversal and battle outcome: Zhang Mengtan secures agreement, Zhao reverses the water, and Wei-Han flank attacks destroy Zhi's position. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-03-weiju-hanhu-wujiandao-lkiwf0zdnylmaqjgsix-dktxea0l|Zhouji 03]]

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept rests on two consecutive podcast summaries of transmitted sources. Zhouji 03 distinguishes an unspecified siege duration in the Zizhi Tongjian account from a three-year duration in the Zhanguo Ce tradition. Exact dialogue, city size, water height, famine details, personal gestures, motives, and the total destruction or escape details of the Zhi clan remain source-scoped. Zhao's endurance also carried catastrophic civilian costs, while Han and Wei's concessions exposed their own populations and territory; later success is not a complete moral vindication of either choice.

## What Changed
- Added the pre-siege succession, fallback-base, humiliation, and tactical-concession conditions that made the later battle reversal possible.
