# Migrant Family Remittance Obligation / 移民家庭汇款义务

Migrant family remittance obligation is the pressure that turns overseas earning into a continuing duty toward people at home. [[168-yin-yu-xin-yibu-riben-laogong-yimin-de-kuayang-huikuan-shi-lmgfmmgkawoa-6xyucnm1ljebzwu]] makes this visible through Japanese families who petitioned village officials, the foreign ministry, and overseas consulates to locate relatives in [[Hawaii]] or California and urge them to send money back.

The concept keeps agency and coercion together. Families used available state channels to survive illness, poverty, and abandonment, while migrants experienced remittance as moral obligation, bureaucratic pressure, and part of the official image of the dutiful overseas Japanese worker.

## Key Claims
- Remittance is not only a voluntary financial transaction; it can be family duty enforced through social, bureaucratic, and moral channels.
- Left-behind households can exercise agency by using state infrastructure to make claims on absent relatives.
- Standardized forms make family need legible but can compress complex relations into payment status and amounts.
- The same obligation can support households while exposing migrants to state and family surveillance.
