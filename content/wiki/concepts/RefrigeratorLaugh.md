# Refrigerator Laugh

## Definition

A refrigerator laugh is [[CarlReiner]]'s heuristic for a comic moment that returns to a viewer after the film and provokes another laugh during ordinary later recollection.

## Current Synthesis

The heuristic shifts evaluation from immediate audience response to memory. [[SteveMartin]] connects it to odd ideas whose clarity may initially be uncertain but whose surprising visual logic persists. His example is an improvised *Roxanne* scene in which his character reacts to a newspaper headline, buys the paper again, and discards it again without Martin first explaining the planned bit to the director.

Delayed recall does not replace the immediate laugh or prove a film's overall quality. It identifies one form of comic durability and gives creators a reason to preserve some strange, concise ideas that conventional explanation might flatten.

## Key Claims

- A comic moment can have value beyond its first audience response if it returns spontaneously in memory.
- Oddness and uncertain immediate clarity can contribute to memorability rather than merely signal failure.
- Visual logic can carry a joke without extensive verbal explanation.
- Protecting a spontaneous idea long enough to perform it can reveal possibilities that advance explanation might suppress.
- Delayed recall is one criterion of comic durability, not a complete measure of quality.

### Odd visual logic

- [[introducing-so-many-steves-a-new-audiobook-from-steve-martin-and-pushkin-introducing-so-many-steves-a-new-audiobook-f]] connects the heuristic to Martin's unannounced *Roxanne* newspaper sequence and his belief that uncertain odd ideas are often remembered.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source offers a practitioner heuristic, not audience-recall research. Memorable moments can be confusing, offensive, or detached from a film's larger aims, while less conspicuous comedy may contribute more to character or structure. Martin's recollection does not establish how the scene was developed, edited, received, or remembered across audiences, and unannounced improvisation may create production or collaboration costs in other settings.

- Established delayed recollection as a distinct comic-durability test linked to odd visual risk.
