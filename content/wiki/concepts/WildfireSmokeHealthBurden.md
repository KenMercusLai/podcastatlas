# Wildfire Smoke Health Burden

Wildfire smoke health burden is the public-health frame added by [[burning-questions-a-more-fire-prone-world-6a687ea64207b2a60119df8b]]. [[OliverMorton]] says fires kill most people through smoke rather than direct burning, and the source cites estimates of around 250,000 annual deaths from fire-smoke effects.

The concept matters because smoke turns a localized fire into a population-wide exposure problem. The source says around a billion people each year experience at least one day of wildfire-smoke-related indoor air-quality problems, making air quality, ageing populations, housing, filtration, and public communication part of [[ClimateAdaptation]].

## Key Claims
- Smoke can make fire damage regional or continental even when flames are local.
- The health burden may grow as populations age and more people live near or downwind of fire-prone landscapes.
- Indoor exposure matters, so adaptation includes building design, filtration, warnings, and household capacity, not only evacuation.
- Smoke effects complicate any narrow disaster metric focused only on burned structures or direct deaths.
