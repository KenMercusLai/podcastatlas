# Wildfire Smoke Health Burden

## Definition

Wildfire smoke health burden is the population harm caused when particles and gases from landscape fires degrade outdoor and indoor air quality, often far beyond the area directly burned.

## Current Synthesis

[[burning-questions-a-more-fire-prone-world-6a687ea64207b2a60119df8b]] establishes smoke, rather than direct flame, as the dominant human-cost channel in its wildfire account. It turns fire into an air-quality, housing, ageing, filtration, warning, and health-system problem rather than only an evacuation or property-loss event.

[[dunes-and-gloom-mbss-annus-horribilis-0a44ecf1bba6689b3b1e02af9ca13b2c]] makes the transboundary mechanism concrete: Indonesian peat and land-clearing fires affected Malaysia, the Philippines, and Singapore. Mask distribution and air-conditioned haze shelters can reduce exposure for people without adequate home filtration or cooling, but these downstream protections cannot substitute for prevention at the fire source.

## Key Claims

- Smoke can make a localized fire a regional public-health emergency.
- Indoor air quality matters because people may remain exposed even away from flames.
- Age, respiratory vulnerability, housing quality, cooling, and filtration shape unequal harm.
- Masks, warnings, clean-air shelters, and health guidance are distinct layers of adaptation.
- Source prevention remains necessary because exposure protection cannot cover whole populations indefinitely.
- Cross-border transport separates the location of health costs from the jurisdiction controlling ignition.

## Evidence

- **Scale and mortality frame:** [[burning-questions-a-more-fire-prone-world-6a687ea64207b2a60119df8b]] reports that smoke causes more fire deaths than direct burning and cites large annual mortality and indoor-exposure estimates.
- **Regional transport:** [[dunes-and-gloom-mbss-annus-horribilis-0a44ecf1bba6689b3b1e02af9ca13b2c]] reports haze moving from Indonesian fires into neighboring countries.
- **Exposure protection:** [[dunes-and-gloom-mbss-annus-horribilis-0a44ecf1bba6689b3b1e02af9ca13b2c]] describes Singapore distributing masks and opening cooled haze shelters.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The sources do not provide pollutant measurements, exposure-response estimates, clinical outcome data, or a comparison of masks and shelters. Their global mortality, annual exposure, and city pollution claims are episode-reported estimates. Air-conditioned shelter access may reduce heat and smoke exposure, but effectiveness depends on filtration, access, duration, and public communication.
