## Definition

School belonging through care / 以关怀赢得学校归属 is the view that an institution becomes a meaningful alma mater by teaching, protecting, and continuing to value students, especially when they struggle, rather than through prestige, formal enrollment, or credentials alone.

## Current Synthesis

The episode connects fictional longing for a school or lineage with a guest's contrast between two real schools. A high school concentrated resources on three students to pursue a breakthrough result, leaving others feeling abandoned; a later remedial school offered strong teaching, warmth, and support despite the student's weak foundation. Belonging follows the second institution because it treated education as commitment to learners rather than extraction of a success metric.

This does not make warmth sufficient for education. Strong instruction, expectations, peer learning, and institutional continuity still matter. The claim is that these capacities generate legitimate loyalty only when students remain persons the school is responsible to, not expendable inputs sorted by likely contribution to reputation.

## Key Claims

- Formal attendance, graduation, prestige, and credentials do not by themselves create durable school belonging.
- Students are more likely to claim an institution as an alma mater when teaching and support remain available during difficulty.
- Concentrating resources on a few likely high performers can convert educational aspiration into abandonment for everyone else.
- Warmth and care need strong teaching and expectations rather than serving as substitutes for learning.
- Institutional loyalty is ethically stronger when it is earned through non-abandonment rather than demanded through identity or gratitude.
- Fictional attachment to a martial school can illuminate real belonging while remaining an analogy rather than evidence about school outcomes.

## Evidence

- Selective-investment harm: [[076-jiaoshijie-teji-jinyong-yuzhou-shifu-dapandian-li_8lkza7up0nnupptlmnz4gu4y0]] recounts a high school concentrating support on three students and producing estrangement among the rest.
- Supportive contrast: [[076-jiaoshijie-teji-jinyong-yuzhou-shifu-dapandian-li_8lkza7up0nnupptlmnz4gu4y0]] describes a remedial school whose teaching, warmth, and support made it feel more like an alma mater.
- Metric boundary: [[076-jiaoshijie-teji-jinyong-yuzhou-shifu-dapandian-li_8lkza7up0nnupptlmnz4gu4y0]] criticizes removing or abandoning lower-performing students to protect progression statistics.
- Fictional analogy: [[076-jiaoshijie-teji-jinyong-yuzhou-shifu-dapandian-li_8lkza7up0nnupptlmnz4gu4y0]] links Linghu Chong's attachment to Huashan, Peach Blossom Island disciples' wish to return, and Xuzhu's attachment to Shaolin with alma-mater feeling.
- Teaching-and-care combination: [[076-jiaoshijie-teji-jinyong-yuzhou-shifu-dapandian-li_8lkza7up0nnupptlmnz4gu4y0]] treats educational quality, warm treatment, and durable support as mutually reinforcing rather than interchangeable.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The real-school comparison is one participant's retrospective account and does not establish the schools' full resource constraints, selection policies, outcomes, or other students' experiences. Institutions sometimes triage scarce resources, and equal care does not require identical support. The concept sets an ethical test: resource decisions should not make struggling students disposable or ask them to feel loyalty to an institution that excluded them from its educational concern.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from the episode's contrast between formal school attendance and emotionally earned alma-mater identity.
- Joined instructional quality, warmth, non-abandonment, and metric restraint in one belonging standard.
