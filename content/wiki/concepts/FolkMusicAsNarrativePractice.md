# Folk Music as Narrative Practice

## Definition
Folk music as narrative practice treats folk as a way of carrying lived experience, local speech, characters, memory, and shared improvisation rather than as a fixed acoustic-guitar sound or lifestyle image.

## Current Synthesis
[[ZhangWeiweiMusician|张玮玮]] grounds folk in narrative. Everyday detail can become poetic without abandoning ordinary life, and the musical carrier can include oral variation, prison-song forms, accordion, rock inheritance, electronic timbre, spatial production, or other techniques. Genre identity therefore comes less from instrument policing than from what and how the music remembers, tells, and circulates.

## Key Claims
- Folk's durable core can be narrative even when instrumentation and production change.
- Ordinary life and marginal position are legitimate sources of poetic language rather than lesser substitutes for elevated subject matter.
- Oral repetition, modular lyrics, improvisation, and community transmission challenge the idea of a single fixed work.
- Electronic timbre and spatial design can strengthen historical or personal storytelling instead of negating folk identity.

- Everyday poetry: [[yinyueren-zhangweiwei-luoyonghao-women-doushi-nage-hunluan-you-weida-de-90-niandai-de-xingcunzhe-llhey1qkmnmxesjlxgledjntaov]] has Zhang identify Zhang Chu's small-person perspective as permission to write nearby life into songs.
- Oral and communal form: [[yinyueren-zhangweiwei-luoyonghao-women-doushi-nage-hunluan-you-weida-de-90-niandai-de-xingcunzhe-llhey1qkmnmxesjlxgledjntaov]] describes Northwest prison-song forms with stable openings and endings around replaceable or improvised middle verses.
- Technological flexibility: [[yinyueren-zhangweiwei-luoyonghao-women-doushi-nage-hunluan-you-weida-de-90-niandai-de-xingcunzhe-llhey1qkmnmxesjlxgledjntaov]] presents electronic sound as a way to build image, time, history, and narrative space.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source offers a practitioner's expansive definition, not a comprehensive musicological taxonomy. Its prison-song history and claims about a more “original” folk state are source-scoped and should not erase other folk traditions, authored forms, or the importance some listeners place on acoustic convention.

## What Changed
- Established narrative, locality, oral variation, and lived experience as the concept's organizing features.
- Separated folk identity from mandatory acoustic instrumentation.
