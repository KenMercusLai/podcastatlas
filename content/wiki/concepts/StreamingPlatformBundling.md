# Streaming Platform Bundling

Streaming platform bundling is the media-distribution pattern added by [[tech-20260731-0731-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260731-0731-mp-tech-pod-128]], where a content owner's streaming library becomes available inside a larger platform subscription rather than only through a separate app or discount bundle. The source's case is [[NBCUniversal]] placing [[PeacockStreaming|Peacock]] content inside [[YouTubePremium|YouTube Premium]].

The concept specializes [[StreamingConsolidation]]. Consolidation asks whether ownership, catalogs, or subscriptions combine; platform bundling asks where the viewer actually watches. In the source, that location matters because the viewer remains inside [[YouTube]], a platform that already combines creator video, subscriptions, YouTube TV, search/discovery, and advertising infrastructure.

## Key Claims
- Platform-native bundles can reduce app switching more than external discount bundles.
- The arrangement can strengthen the host platform's subscriber value while giving legacy media a route to audiences whose attention has moved toward YouTube.
- Sports rights make platform bundling more important because live games can help justify subscription retention and recurring attention.
- A legacy streamer gains distribution but may also deepen [[PlatformDependencyRisk]] if the viewer relationship, interface, and discovery layer sit inside another platform.
- Platform bundling can make streaming look more like cable aggregation while preserving easier cancellation and digital interface control.

## Connections
