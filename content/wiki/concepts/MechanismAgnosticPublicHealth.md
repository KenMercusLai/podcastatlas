# Mechanism-Agnostic Public Health

## Definition

Mechanism-agnostic public health is the use of observed outcome improvements to justify preventive action before the underlying biological mechanism is fully or correctly understood.

## Current Synthesis

The episode places eighteenth-century empiricism and nineteenth-century sanitation between religious or miasmatic explanation and modern microbiology. Ventilation, washing clothes, clean water, swamp drainage, sewage removal, and vaccination could reduce illness because they changed exposure, even when practitioners lacked germ theory or held incorrect causal models.

The lesson is bounded rather than anti-theoretical. Useful outcomes can precede correct explanation, but a partially successful practice does not validate every claim in the theory attached to it. Better mechanism should refine targeting, reveal harms, and distinguish interventions that work from those merely correlated with improvement.

## Key Claims

- Repeated outcome evidence can justify proportionate preventive action before mechanism is settled.
- Incorrect theories may still direct attention toward real environmental hazards.
- Practical success does not make the surrounding causal explanation correct.
- Clean water, sanitation, ventilation, and vector reduction can work across competing historical theories.
- Mechanistic knowledge remains valuable for precision, transfer, safety, and causal confidence.

## Evidence

- Empirical turn - [[147-disease-the-new-world-and-modern-pandemics-glt1023018457]] uses Edward Jenner's 1798 vaccination work as an example of case evidence preceding full mechanism.
- Environmental action - [[147-disease-the-new-world-and-modern-pandemics-glt1023018457]] names laundry, ventilation, clean water, and swamp drainage as practices that could reduce disease under incomplete theories.
- Sanitation reform - [[147-disease-the-new-world-and-modern-pandemics-glt1023018457]] explains how miasma thinking could still motivate sewage and urban-cleanliness improvements despite missing microbial agents.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept does not excuse harmful treatment, weak evidence, or refusal to revise theory. Historical co-occurrence does not establish efficacy, and interventions that appear beneficial can have hidden costs or depend on context. The source gives illustrative examples rather than controlled comparative evidence for each practice.

## What Changed

- Created the concept to separate useful empirical prevention from correctness of the theory used to explain it.
