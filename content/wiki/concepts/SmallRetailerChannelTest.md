# Small Retailer Channel Test

## Definition
A small retailer channel test is a deliberately limited retail pilot in local or independent stores that lets a consumer-product company learn shelf behavior, sampling effectiveness, story transfer, pricing, replenishment, and operational burden before pursuing larger retail accounts.

## Current Synthesis
The Daymond John Advice Line episode makes the small retailer test a bridge between farmers-market traction and larger grocery distribution. [[SaborAMexicoSalsas]] already has direct selling, customer feedback, and revenue momentum, but [[DaymondJohn]] warns that big retail can introduce buyer requirements, shipping, pricing pressure, and large-company criteria too early. The recommended move is a small Chicago store pilot with demos and customer conversation.

## Key Claims
- A small local store pilot can test whether farmers-market demand transfers to a shelf environment.
- Independent retailers can tell a local founder story more personally than large stores.
- In-store sampling turns retail into customer research rather than only a distribution milestone.
- The test should be long enough to reveal reorder behavior, pricing pressure, and operational load.
- Big retail should follow evidence from smaller channels, not replace the work of creating that evidence.

## Evidence
- Retail risk: [[advice-line-with-daymond-john-of-fubu-0961848f-0f5a-4fb7-932c-3a04f08bc8d4]] records Daymond's warning that retail can mean more work for less reward because of buyers, vendor requirements, shipping, pricing pressure, and large-company criteria.
- Pilot design: [[advice-line-with-daymond-john-of-fubu-0961848f-0f5a-4fb7-932c-3a04f08bc8d4]] has Guy suggest testing five or six Chicago food stores for six months to a year.
- Story transfer: [[advice-line-with-daymond-john-of-fubu-0961848f-0f5a-4fb7-932c-3a04f08bc8d4]] says mom-and-pop stores can explain a local founder's story directly to customers.
- Sampling loop: [[advice-line-with-daymond-john-of-fubu-0961848f-0f5a-4fb7-932c-3a04f08bc8d4]] recommends sampling and speaking with customers to understand how the salsa works in retail.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Small retailers may not reveal all requirements of regional or national grocery accounts, such as slotting, broker relationships, promotions, insurance, EDI, chargebacks, or distributor margins. The test is a learning gate, not proof that larger retail will scale profitably.
