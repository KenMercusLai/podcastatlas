# Supply Depot Decisive Strike / 粮草命门打击

## Definition
Supply Depot Decisive Strike describes a campaign pattern in which a concentrated attack on an enemy's grain or supply center changes the war faster than frontal attrition. The strike matters because it attacks material endurance, morale, command confidence, and alliance cohesion at once.

## Current Synthesis
Hanji 1049 introduces this concept through [[WuchaoRaidLateHan|the Wuchao Raid]]. [[CaoCao|Cao Cao]] does not merely win a local depot fight; he turns [[XuYouLateHan|Xu You]]'s intelligence into a strike against the point that keeps [[YuanShao|Yuan Shao]]'s larger army functional.

The concept is distinct from ordinary logistics pressure because the attack is concentrated, time-sensitive, and politically contagious. Once Wuchao burns, Yuan Shao loses grain, [[ChunyuQiong|Chunyu Qiong]]'s defeat damages confidence, [[GuoTu|Guo Tu]] shifts blame, [[ZhangHeLateHan|Zhang He]] and [[GaoLanLateHan|Gao Lan]] surrender, and the larger force loses the coherence that numerical superiority alone cannot restore.

## Key Claims
- A decisive depot strike can reverse numerical imbalance when the larger army's endurance depends on a vulnerable supply node.
- Intelligence from a defector or insider can turn a hidden logistical weakness into a targetable battlefield objective.
- Concentration matters: Cao's refusal to divide his small force keeps pressure on the decisive target until Wuchao falls.
- The effect is material and psychological at the same time; grain loss becomes morale collapse once troops see the supply base destroyed.
- The strike can trigger political secondary effects, including blame shifting, elite defection, and leadership flight.
- The pattern depends on timing and risk tolerance, because failed penetration behind enemy lines could destroy the weaker army.

- Vulnerable supply node: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1049-guandu-zhizhan-jieju-jiemu-caocao-zhongyu-yi-ruo-sheng-qiang-lvtutwyhrix4rhiypa9dknz3da1m]] presents Wuchao as Yuan Shao's grain depot and therefore as the point where a raid can break a much larger army.
- Intelligence and risk: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1049-guandu-zhizhan-jieju-jiemu-caocao-zhongyu-yi-ruo-sheng-qiang-lvtutwyhrix4rhiypa9dknz3da1m]] says Cao acts on Xu You's information even though the information might be false and the target lies behind Yuan lines.
- Concentrated attack: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1049-guandu-zhizhan-jieju-jiemu-caocao-zhongyu-yi-ruo-sheng-qiang-lvtutwyhrix4rhiypa9dknz3da1m]] highlights Cao's choice to keep attacking Wuchao rather than splitting forces when Yuan reinforcements approach.
- Secondary collapse: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1049-guandu-zhizhan-jieju-jiemu-caocao-zhongyu-yi-ruo-sheng-qiang-lvtutwyhrix4rhiypa9dknz3da1m]] links Wuchao's burning to Chunyu Qiong's execution, Guo Tu's slander, Zhang He and Gao Lan's surrender, Yuan Shao's flight, and Ju Shou's capture.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The current concept rests on one Guandu episode and should be extended cautiously. A depot strike is not automatically decisive: it requires accurate intelligence, a reachable supply node, enough force concentration to destroy the depot, and an enemy command system unable to compensate quickly. The episode also frames Guandu as a system-wide victory, so Wuchao should be treated as the campaign hinge rather than the only cause.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to capture Hanji 1049's logistics-centered explanation of Cao Cao's weak-over-strong victory.
- Linked supply destruction to morale, blame shifting, elite defection, and command collapse rather than treating it as a purely material event.
