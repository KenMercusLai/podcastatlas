# Health Insurance Death Spiral

## Definition
A health insurance death spiral is the adverse-selection loop in which rising premiums cause healthier people to leave an insurance pool, making the remaining pool sicker and more expensive, which then pushes premiums higher again.

## Current Synthesis
The episode uses the concept to explain the risk facing ACA exchanges after enhanced subsidies expired. When premiums and deductibles rise, healthier exchange customers can decide coverage is no longer worth the price, while sicker customers remain because they need care. The resulting risk pool is more expensive to insure, which can make the next round of pricing worse.

The source does not claim total collapse is inevitable. Original Obamacare subsidies remain, and the episode says the market may shrink sharply rather than disappear. The broader synthesis is that death-spiral risk should be read as a gradient: subsidy levels, mandate strength, health mix, insurer pricing, hospital obligations, and patient behavior decide whether the market stabilizes at a smaller size or keeps deteriorating.

## Key Claims
- Death-spiral pressure begins when price increases make healthier people more likely to exit than sicker people.
- Subsidy expiry can create adverse selection even without repealing the insurance exchange structure.
- A shrinking risk pool affects more than buyers because uninsured people may delay treatment and rely on emergency care.
- Remaining subsidies and regulatory structure can blunt collapse while still allowing large enrollment losses.
- Hospital unpaid-care data can reveal downstream pressure from coverage loss.

## Evidence
- Mechanism - [[stars-in-their-ais-tech-is-changing-hollywood-6a917d0b63de6f7b41319a4b]] explains that insurance becomes more expensive when healthier people leave and sicker customers remain.
- Affordability shock - [[stars-in-their-ais-tech-is-changing-hollywood-6a917d0b63de6f7b41319a4b]] links death-spiral fears to premium increases near 60% and deductible increases near 40%.
- Enrollment loss - [[stars-in-their-ais-tech-is-changing-hollywood-6a917d0b63de6f7b41319a4b]] says exchange enrollment had already fallen from about 22 million to about 19 million.
- Forecast boundary - [[stars-in-their-ais-tech-is-changing-hollywood-6a917d0b63de6f7b41319a4b]] says experts expect 10 million fewer marketplace buyers by 2028 compared with 2025.
- Collapse qualification - [[stars-in-their-ais-tech-is-changing-hollywood-6a917d0b63de6f7b41319a4b]] says original Obamacare subsidies remain even though enhanced subsidies expired.
- Hospital signal - [[stars-in-their-ais-tech-is-changing-hollywood-6a917d0b63de6f7b41319a4b]] says unpaid hospital care was up 16% in May compared with the same time last year.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source gives a concise explanatory model rather than actuarial evidence. It does not prove which enrollees left, how insurers priced the risk pool, how Medicaid or employer coverage absorbed former exchange customers, or whether future policy changes could reverse the trend.
