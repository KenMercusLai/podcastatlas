# 安宁疗护 / Hospice Care

## Definition
Hospice or comfort-focused end-of-life care prioritizes relief, dignity, communication, and the person's remaining life when cure or reversal is no longer realistic, while remaining distinct from euthanasia, assisted suicide, and abandonment.

## Current Synthesis
The wiki treats hospice care as active illness care rather than failure, abandonment, or a euphemism for intentionally ending life. Across late-stage cancer narrative, assisted-death ethics, ICU practice, and bedside nursing, its center is a change in treatment priority: relief, dignity, communication, and quality of remaining life lead when cure or reversal is no longer realistic, while beneficial disease-directed treatment can continue when it still reduces suffering or serves the patient's goals.

This care is dynamic and whole-person. Pain, breathlessness, feeding, elimination, hygiene, consciousness, fear, relationships, belief, familiar surroundings, unfinished work, and goodbye can all matter. [[DoNotResuscitateDecision|DNR]] is one bounded treatment-limit choice, not a complete hospice plan; patient and family preferences may change under clinical or emotional pressure. [[WholePersonEndOfLifeCare|身心社灵照护]] therefore joins symptom control to psychological, social, spiritual, and family support across hospital, community, residential, or home settings. The ICU boundary remains important: burdensome intervention may be reduced in irreversible terminal decline, while intensive care can still buy time for potentially reversible illness.

## Key Claims
- End-of-life comfort is a positive care goal, not proof that the person's illness story or personhood has ended.
- Pain, breathing, feeding, excretion, smell, consciousness, family presence, and goodbye can be discussed without hiding deterioration behind compulsory optimism.
- Continued disease treatment, unfinished work, and hospice preparation can coexist while goals and reversibility remain under review.
- Symptom relief, terminal sedation, treatment withdrawal, assisted suicide, and active euthanasia require distinct clinical, ethical, and legal analysis.
- ICU support should be judged by expected reversibility and benefit rather than equated automatically with dying.
- DNR is a resuscitation-limit decision rather than a withdrawal of all care, and it can require renewed discussion as distress, understanding, or wishes change.
- Whole-person care connects physical comfort with psychological, social, spiritual, family, and setting needs without imposing a fixed checklist.

## Evidence
- Whole-person cancer care: [[184-zhenshi-yu-wo-you-wanjun-zhi-zhong-huanying-xianv-ajiao-990545010]] links possible hospice transition with ninth-line treatment, physical degradation, unfinished manuscripts, public writing, and dignity beyond patient identity.
- Assisted-death boundary: [[119-anlesi-xianchang-zhengyin-shengming-ruci-zhengui-770771087]] distinguishes comfort care and terminal sedation from actions intended to cause death.
- Reversibility and burden: [[vol-110-shanzhong-shi-weile-genghao-de-huozhe-shenglaobingsi-6-wei-yisheng-shijiao-zenme-kan-shijie-dushuri-lklwhndplyk73rpqxuraocs4cii5]] has an ICU clinician describe reducing invasive treatment when terminal decline is irreversible while preserving ICU's role in treatable critical illness.
- Advance discussion: [[vol-110-shanzhong-shi-weile-genghao-de-huozhe-shenglaobingsi-6-wei-yisheng-shijiao-zenme-kan-shijie-dushuri-lklwhndplyk73rpqxuraocs4cii5]] connects earlier family conversation with clearer wishes and less crisis-time conflict.
- Dynamic treatment goals: [[vol-44-xianli-hushijie-yu-xiehe-yiyuan-hushi-duitan-linzhong-guanhuai-zhong-de-shen-xin-she-ling-luernzy5tf_brgkkrkc4nw0fgmog]] distinguishes comfort-focused priority from stopping every treatment and shows a family revisiting DNR under severe respiratory distress.
- Whole-person nursing and access: [[vol-44-xianli-hushijie-yu-xiehe-yiyuan-hushi-duitan-linzhong-guanhuai-zhong-de-shen-xin-she-ling-luernzy5tf_brgkkrkc4nw0fgmog]] joins symptom control with sensory, psychological, relational, spiritual, home-setting, and service-network needs.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
These sources are narrative and public-education accounts, not individualized prognosis or a complete hospice protocol. Reversibility, capacity, pain and symptom control, documentation, legal rules, culture, religion, setting, caregiver burden, service availability, family conflict, and changing patient wishes can alter the appropriate path. Sensory, movement, music, pet, or religious support is not universally suitable, and whole-person language should not conceal inadequate clinical care. Comfort-focused care should not be used as a hidden name for abandonment, assisted death, or denial of potentially beneficial treatment.

## What Changed
- Reframed hospice as active reprioritization that may retain beneficial disease-directed treatment.
- Added DNR as a bounded, revisable decision rather than a complete care plan.
- Expanded the judgment from comfort alone to physical, psychological, social, spiritual, family, and setting needs.
- Added service access and caregiver capacity as practical limits on where and how care occurs.
