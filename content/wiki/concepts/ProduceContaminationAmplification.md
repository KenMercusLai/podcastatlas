# Produce Contamination Amplification

Produce contamination amplification is the risk pattern in [[big-lettuce-meets-small-intestine]] where a small contamination event can become a multi-state outbreak because fresh produce is washed, chopped, mixed, packaged, and distributed through large processors. The episode uses [[TaylorFarms]] and the July 2026 [[Cyclospora]] outbreak to make the mechanism concrete.

The concept is not simply that large processors are careless. The source says food-safety work at Taylor Farms included irrigation-water testing and a dedicated food-safety department. The amplification problem is structural: when raw products from multiple farms enter a shared processing and distribution system, a small upstream failure can reach many restaurants and stores before investigators trace it.

## Key Claims
- Fresh-produce illness rates and outbreak sizes rose as bagged salads and value-added produce expanded, though better detection explains part of the increase.
- Mixing produce from multiple farms can spread contamination across many finished products.
- National distribution makes tracing depend on records from restaurants, stores, processors, and farms.
- The 2006 spinach outbreak exposed how harm can precede trace-back certainty.
- [[FoodSafetyModernizationAct]] and [[FreshProduceSafetyRegulation]] are attempts to prevent rather than only trace these events.
