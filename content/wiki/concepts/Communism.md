# Communism

## Definition

Communism is a political and social tradition seeking a classless order organized around common ownership, equality, and distribution by ability and need; in this source it names both an enduring ideal and the party-states that claimed to implement it.

## Current Synthesis

The episode places modern communism inside a much longer history of communal property and earthly justice, from the early church and Christian radical movements to Thomas More and the [[Diggers]]. Marx and Engels transformed that inheritance through industrial capitalism, class analysis, and claims of scientific history. Lenin then adapted the project to agrarian Russia through the vanguard party, while Stalinist rule joined collectivization and industrialization to mass coercion.

The source distinguishes ideal, doctrine, movement, and regime. Justice and liberation can retain moral appeal even when economic prophecy or party infallibility is rejected. Conversely, states may preserve communist names and ruling parties while allowing market practices or fusing the ideology with nationalism. The resulting concept is historically variable but carries a recurring institutional question: whether abolishing private control and political opposition requires a concentration of power incompatible with pluralism.

## Key Claims

- Communist ideals have pre-Marx Christian, millenarian, and utopian precedents.
- Marx and Engels recast equality and common ownership through industrial class conflict and scientific-historical language.
- Leninist vanguardism changed who could legitimately act in the name of the promised classless future.
- Communist states can combine revolutionary universalism with nationalism, bureaucracy, and regime preservation.
- The ideal of liberation remains separable from claims that one party or economic law guarantees its realization.
- The episode argues, without conclusively proving, that confiscation and anti-pluralism create a persistent tendency toward coercive rule.

## Evidence

- Genealogy and ideal: [[38-communism-glt7587678050]] connects Acts, Pelagius and Augustine, Tabor, Münster, Thomas More, the Diggers, Marx, and Engels to an older dispute over earthly perfection.
- Revolutionary adaptation: [[38-communism-glt7587678050]] contrasts industrial-European expectations with Lenin's vanguard party in agrarian Russia.
- State practice and mutation: [[38-communism-glt7587678050]] connects Stalinism, Soviet conservatism, nationalism, and contemporary China to divergence between emancipatory promise and governing practice.
- Continuing appeal: [[38-communism-glt7587678050]] preserves justice and liberation as morally powerful even while rejecting historical inevitability and one-party implementation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This is one skeptical popular-history episode, not a comparative survey of communist thought, parties, movements, or states. It emphasizes Soviet violence and anti-pluralism while giving less attention to internal Marxist disagreements, democratic socialism, anti-colonial achievements, labor organization, welfare institutions, or differences among China, Cuba, Vietnam, Laos, and other cases. Its claims about inherent totalitarianism and human nature remain argued interpretations.
