# British Coup Paranoia in 1974

## Definition
British Coup Paranoia in 1974 is the episode's label for overlapping fears that economic disorder, strikes, military emergency roles, security-service suspicion, and retired-officer organizing might culminate in an extra-constitutional removal of [[HaroldWilson|Harold Wilson]]'s government.

## Current Synthesis
[[419-britain-in-1974-countdown-to-a-coup-part-3-glt2033018212]] reconstructs a climate in which coup fear was neither pure fantasy nor demonstrated plot. MI5 had monitored Wilson, CIA counterintelligence figures entertained claims that he was a Soviet agent, the army had domestic emergency roles, Sunningdale had just shown organized disruption defeating an executive, [[WalterWalker]] used strongman rhetoric, and [[DavidStirling]] organized GB75. Those fragments explain Wilson's and Benn's anxiety, but the source does not connect them into a single command structure, operational plan, or imminent seizure of government.

## Key Claims
- Security-service suspicion and anti-Wilson gossip made official conspiracy fears intelligible.
- Domestic army intervention and Northern Ireland gave military involvement in civil crisis a real precedent.
- Retired officers supplied rhetoric and organization that could be read as extra-constitutional preparation.
- Wilson's personal exhaustion and Number 10 dysfunction amplified threat perception.
- Available evidence in the episode does not establish a unified or executable coup plan.

## Evidence
- Security suspicion: [[419-britain-in-1974-countdown-to-a-coup-part-3-glt2033018212]] discusses Wilson's MI5 file, Soviet contacts, Angleton's suspicions, and Peter Wright's later reduced allegations.
- Domestic precedent: [[419-britain-in-1974-countdown-to-a-coup-part-3-glt2033018212]] connects army emergency duties and Sunningdale's collapse to state-fragility fears.
- Retired-officer activity: [[419-britain-in-1974-countdown-to-a-coup-part-3-glt2033018212]] presents Walker's rhetoric and Stirling's GB75 as distinct but politically resonant initiatives.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Suspicion, rhetoric, contingency planning, and volunteer organizing are not equivalent to a coordinated coup. Peter Wright's claims weakened under questioning, Frank Kitson's counterinsurgency writing was not a coup blueprint, GB75 publicly described continuity of services, and the source identifies no operational chain linking these elements. "Paranoia" here describes a political atmosphere while allowing that some underlying suspicions and activities were real.

## What Changed
- Created a bounded synthesis separating evidence of crisis-era suspicion and organizing from evidence of an operational coup.
