## Definition

三公监察内廷职权 / Three Excellencies inner-court jurisdiction is the late-Han claim that the highest civil ministers can review, dismiss, or impeach eunuch-linked officeholders when inner-court actors endanger the ruler or state, even if ordinary bureaucratic categories treat eunuchs and their networks as palace insiders.

## Current Synthesis

The 163 CE layer in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-858-weishenme-quan-ni-yiding-yao-huanwei-sikao-lobr-xznqo-ctjpc-ytpstubmahl|Hanji 858]] supplies an administrative-review version of the jurisdiction claim. [[ZhouJingLateHan|周景]] and [[YangBingLateHan|杨秉]] argue that eunuch relatives, guests, and unqualified youths have spread through local officeholding, creating official resentment and popular hardship. Their accepted proposal routes review through the Sili, two-thousand-dan offices, gates, five camps, and northern army, with dismissible people reported to the Three Excellencies. Yang Bing then impeaches more than fifty officials, showing jurisdiction as a personnel-cleanup procedure rather than only a single impeachment dispute.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-865-zhichang-boss-ye-rongyi-bei-zhezhong-ren-beici-ljfhfevljqnx6zceakjfbw5gwfma|Hanji 865]] gives the sharper impeachment case. Yang Bing first impeaches [[HouCanLateHan|侯参]] for Yizhou corruption, then argues that [[HouLanLateHan|侯览]] should not remain near [[EmperorHuanOfHan|汉桓帝]] after his brother's crimes expose the household's danger. The shangshu side challenges the move by asking whether the Three Excellencies have authority over inner-court eunuchs.

The answer matters because it prevents palace status from becoming a jurisdictional shield. Yang Bing's side invokes Spring and Autumn reasoning and Han precedents involving Deng Tong and Shen Tujia to argue that senior ministers have no blind spot when removing danger for the ruler. Emperor Huan's removal of Hou Lan makes this a temporary institutional breakthrough, not merely a moral protest.

The concept is narrow. It does not mean the outer court controls the inner court permanently. Later Huan and Ling-era evidence still shows eunuch complaint channels, intercepted memorials, and palace access power. Hanji 865 instead supplies one successful legal-precedent answer to the question: "Can palace proximity exempt a eunuch from ministerial impeachment?"

## Key Claims

- Eunuch-linked officeholding can be framed as a reviewable public-administration problem, not only as household service.
- Palace insider status can be used as a jurisdictional defense against ordinary review or impeachment.
- Senior ministers can pierce that defense when they frame the issue as protecting the ruler and state from inner danger rather than meddling in household service.
- Classical and dynastic precedent matter because they give the impeachment an institutional vocabulary beyond personal accusation.
- The jurisdiction claim works best when tied to concrete corruption evidence, office qualifications, or public harm rather than abstract anti-eunuch dislike.
- Implementation requires an execution chain: target classes, responsible reporting offices, and a receiving authority.
- A successful impeachment can produce a temporary retreat without permanently dismantling palace access power.

## Evidence

- Administrative-review jurisdiction: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-858-weishenme-quan-ni-yiding-yao-huanwei-sikao-lobr-xznqo-ctjpc-ytpstubmahl|Hanji 858]] says Zhou Jing and Yang Bing ask Huan to review eunuch relatives, guests, and unqualified officeholders through named civil and military channels that report to the Three Excellencies.
- Review implementation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-858-weishenme-quan-ni-yiding-yao-huanwei-sikao-lobr-xznqo-ctjpc-ytpstubmahl|Hanji 858]] says Huan accepts the request and Yang Bing impeaches more than fifty officials, with some killed and others removed.
- Jurisdiction challenge: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-865-zhichang-boss-ye-rongyi-bei-zhezhong-ren-beici-ljfhfevljqnx6zceakjfbw5gwfma|Hanji 865]] says the shangshu side asks Yang Bing's officials why the Three Excellencies can impeach palace eunuchs.
- Precedent defense: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-865-zhichang-boss-ye-rongyi-bei-zhezhong-ren-beici-ljfhfevljqnx6zceakjfbw5gwfma|Hanji 865]] says Yang Bing's side answers through Spring and Autumn reasoning and Han precedents, including Deng Tong and Shen Tujia.
- Concrete evidence base: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-865-zhichang-boss-ye-rongyi-bei-zhezhong-ren-beici-ljfhfevljqnx6zceakjfbw5gwfma|Hanji 865]] ties the jurisdiction fight to Hou Can's corruption, prison-cart transfer, suicide, and seized valuables.
- Temporary outcome: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-865-zhichang-boss-ye-rongyi-bei-zhezhong-ren-beici-ljfhfevljqnx6zceakjfbw5gwfma|Hanji 865]] says Emperor Huan removes Hou Lan and that civil officials then press related eunuch-family and enfeoffment cases.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept is grounded in bounded Huan-era source notes. It should not be read as proof that late-Han Three Excellencies reliably controlled the palace: adjacent Hanji sources show repeated failures of correction once eunuch complaint, document access, and palace proximity recover. Hanji 858 proves a temporary accepted review and cleanup, not durable control over eunuch-linked appointments. Hanji 865 is best treated as a successful bounded precedent, with the later reappearance or continued influence of Hou Lan left as a chronology gap rather than a resolved institutional victory.

## What Changed

- Hanji 858 adds an earlier administrative-review path before Hanji 865's sharper impeachment dispute.
- The definition now includes review and dismissal of eunuch-linked officeholders, not only impeachment of palace eunuchs.
- The current judgment now treats execution chain design as part of the jurisdiction claim.
