## Definition

肉刑废除与可改过性 / mutilating punishment abolition and reformability is the principle that punishment should not irreversibly destroy the body and thereby foreclose repentance, repair, social return, or later service. It also requires testing whether a formally non-mutilating substitute causes equal or greater permanent harm in practice.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-224-tiying-jiufu-14sui-shaonv-ruhe-gandong-hanwendi-lifxjdmrqoao7b8dq43w-xjh_dhy|Hanji 224]] grounds abolition in [[ChunyuTiying|淳于缇萦]]'s attributed petition for [[ChunyuYi|淳于意]]: death cannot be reversed and severed limbs cannot be restored, so permanent mutilation removes a meaningful route to reform. [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]] reportedly turns the individual appeal into a decree replacing named mutilations with labor, flogging, or execution according to offense.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1139-jiemi-gudai-rouxing-yige-bi-yige-xiaren-lgnj-8smphspf8nud-2o3xh_lggk|Hanji 1139]] supplies the restoration objection through [[ChenQun|陈群]] and [[ChenJiLateHan|陈纪]]: if replacement flogging kills people whom earlier mutilation would have left alive, the reform may be humane in name but harsher in outcome. That objection strengthens the implementation test without defeating the abolition principle. A complete comparison must count mortality, permanent disability, pain, stigma, reproductive injury, social exclusion, and the possibility of return; survival alone does not make mutilation benign.

## Key Claims

- Bodily irreversibility matters because punishment can remove the practical capacity for reform and social return.
- Abolishing tattooing, nose-cutting, and foot mutilation is narrower than abolishing all corporal punishment.
- Replacement penalties must be judged by implemented harm rather than a more humane legal label.
- Lethal flogging is a real objection to poorly designed substitution, not automatic proof that mutilation is preferable.
- Comparative severity must include disability, stigma, reproductive harm, and exclusion as well as death.

## Evidence

### Irreversibility and reformability

- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-224-tiying-jiufu-14sui-shaonv-ruhe-gandong-hanwendi-lifxjdmrqoao7b8dq43w-xjh_dhy|Hanji 224]] attributes to Tiying the argument that death and severed limbs cannot be restored, preventing later reform from repairing the consequence.

### Reform substitution and implemented harm

- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-224-tiying-jiufu-14sui-shaonv-ruhe-gandong-hanwendi-lifxjdmrqoao7b8dq43w-xjh_dhy|Hanji 224]] reports labor, high-count flogging, and execution as replacements, showing that abolition of mutilation did not end bodily coercion.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1139-jiemi-gudai-rouxing-yige-bi-yige-xiaren-lgnj-8smphspf8nud-2o3xh_lggk|Hanji 1139]] reports the Chen Ji-Chen Qun claim that flogging could cause more deaths and their proposal to restore selected mutilations.

### Harms beyond mortality

- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1139-jiemi-gudai-rouxing-yige-bi-yige-xiaren-lgnj-8smphspf8nud-2o3xh_lggk|Hanji 1139]] describes permanent marking, disfigurement, disability, reproductive destruction, humiliation, and social stigma across the five-punishment taxonomy.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Both inputs are popular-history summaries rather than statute collections, medical studies, or comparative outcome data. Tiying's narrative role should not be inflated into sole causation, and the claim that flogging killed more people is a reported historical argument, not a verified rate comparison here. Labor can be coercive, flogging can kill, and execution is irreversible; conversely, survival after mutilation can still mean permanent disability and exclusion. The present synthesis therefore supports outcome-sensitive abolition with safer substitutes, not a simple ranking based only on legal names or survival.

## What Changed

- Added the Jian'an restoration argument as counterpressure on abolition by legal label alone.
- Expanded the severity test from mortality and reformability to stigma, disability, reproductive injury, and social return.
- Clarified that bad substitution supports redesign of alternatives without proving mutilation humane.
