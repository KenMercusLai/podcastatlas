# Marketing Ethics / 营销伦理

## Definition
Marketing ethics is the boundary discipline for deciding whether consumer insight, targeting, pricing, channels, and promotion are being used to create real value or to manipulate, exploit, shame, or mislead consumers.

## Current Synthesis
The episode treats ethics as the first marketing topic because marketing works on belief, identity, fear, aspiration, habit, and social meaning. That power can help consumers understand useful value, but it can also convert health, aging, education, status, and belonging anxieties into private responsibility and paid demand. Ethical marketing therefore requires more than legal compliance; it requires respectful product truth, price fairness, channel control, and direct accountability when customers raise problems.

EP145 adds the consumer-side test: marketing can create attention and social meaning without proving that a purchase is needed, affordable, or product-led. Nostalgia, collaboration, local culture, price urgency, identity, and gendered status cues become ethically defensible only when they remain connected to real quality and do not depend on hiding incentives or manufacturing deficiency. Rational consumption is not immunity from persuasion; it is a practice of making the persuasion legible before deciding.

## Key Claims
- Marketing tools are not neutral because they can shape what people think happiness, success, attractiveness, and responsibility mean.
- Vulnerable groups such as older people, children, and patients require stricter caution in targeting and fear-based messaging.
- Short-term goals and incentives increase the risk that shareholder, company, and consumer interests diverge.
- Ethical risk appears across STP and 4P: segmentation, product quality, side effects, price discrimination, channel behavior, and promotion can all harm consumers.
- Corporate apology language and dispute responses are part of marketing ethics because they reveal whether the company respects consumers as counterparties.
- Ethical persuasion keeps cultural memory, identity, urgency, reviews, and social aspiration connected to product truth, disclosed incentives, affordability, and actual need.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources argue for ethical constraints but do not provide a formal regulatory standard or compliance checklist. They treat rational consumption and "Do no evil" as practical norms, not proof that informed consumers cannot be influenced or that long-term businesses will behave ethically. EP145's gender, class, city-tier, and consumer-motive generalizations remain practitioner observations rather than universal behavioral evidence.
