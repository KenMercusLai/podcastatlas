# Merit-Based Immigration Filter

Merit-based immigration filter is the episode's interpretation of how Trump-aligned policy documents may distinguish between types of migrants. In [[vol-113-cong-jiqian-ye-zhiku-wenjian-zhong-goule-telangpu-2-0-zhizheng-jihua-beihou-de-ren-jigou-sixiang-he-mailuo-lihnknc-3om5evexocb6a6zdwueu]], the [[RepublicanParty]] platform uses invasion and mass-deportation language, while the [[AmericaFirstPolicyInstitute]] agenda uses more neutral language about legal immigration, skills, individual merit, chain migration, and visa lotteries.

[[37-zhishang-ceshi-qingwen-ni-shi-zhili-poluomen-ma-608020679]] adds a historical intelligence-filter precursor. The source says American immigration screening used [[IntelligenceTesting|intelligence testing]] to identify people labeled intellectually deficient, while language, exhaustion, schooling, and culture created [[CulturalBiasInTesting|cultural bias in testing]] that could be mistaken for individual unfitness.

[[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-howard-lutnick-how-america-can-hit-6-gdp-growth-in-2026-39668255]] adds [[HowardLutnick|Howard Lutnick]]'s fiscal-contribution version. He criticizes immigration lotteries, says welfare-state costs change the logic of open immigration, frames a million-dollar "Trump Card" contribution as proof of benefit, and distinguishes a high-paid H-1B engineer from a low-paid entry-level import.

The source's inference is that implementation may be less "no immigration" than selective immigration. That selectivity can still be harsh: it treats people through expected economic value, public-service burden, and skill contribution rather than through family unity or humanitarian belonging.

[[the-ice-hiring-boom]] qualifies the filter by showing how selective rhetoric can interact with enforcement scale. The source says [[USDepartmentOfHomelandSecurity|DHS]] presents the crackdown as targeting serious criminals, while the episode reports that many people caught in the crackdown had no criminal convictions, some had legal status, and some U.S. citizens were taken into custody.

[[tech-20251223-1223-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20251223-1223-mp-tech-pod-128]] adds a technology-sector version through H-1B visas. [[SuyashPasi]] says [[DonaldTrump]] has treated the visas as valuable enough to keep, even as protectionist [[MAGA]] factions resist high-skill immigration and technology leaders push for more access.

## Key Claims
- The concept extends [[ImmigrationBacklashCycle]] by showing a value-filter version of restriction rather than only broad exclusion.
- A high-skill opening can coexist with aggressive illegal-immigration enforcement.
- Enforcement expansion can widen the gap between a stated category filter and who is actually exposed to detention or custody.
- The source links immigration to inflation and labor costs, creating an unresolved policy tension: fewer low-wage workers may raise costs even while politicians promise lower prices.
- The McKinley comparison suggests high tariffs and high-skill immigration can be part of the same industrial-policy package.
- The Marketplace Tech source shows that even the high-skill side of the filter can become contested when technology employers and protectionist activists define national interest differently.
- Episode 37 adds that merit filters can become pseudo-scientific when tests are treated as neutral despite language, class, race, and cultural context.
- The Lutnick source makes fiscal contribution explicit: immigration is judged by expected benefit to the country and by whether the welfare state is exposed to net cost.
