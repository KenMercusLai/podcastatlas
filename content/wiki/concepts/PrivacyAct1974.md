# Privacy Act of 1974

## Definition
The Privacy Act of 1974 is the U.S. federal privacy law the episode uses to explain limits on agencies repurposing data collected for one reason without consent.

## Current Synthesis
The source treats the Privacy Act as a response to historical government data-abuse concerns. Its role in the wiki is not a full legal treatise; it is the named purpose-limitation boundary inside [[GovernmentDataSiloCollapse]]. When agencies seek to combine or repurpose data across silos, the Act becomes a reminder that some separation was designed to prevent surveillance misuse, not merely bureaucratic inefficiency.

## Key Claims
- The Act is presented as a federal constraint on repurposing data collected by agencies for one purpose into another use without consent.
- The episode places the Act after historical examples of government data misuse, including wartime census-data use and Nixon-era FBI misuse.
- The Act's relevance increases when agencies describe information silos as obstacles to enforcement or anti-fraud work.
- Purpose limitation is a governance mechanism rather than a technical data-management preference.
- The source uses the Act to frame ICE data access as a civil-liberties issue, not only an operational-efficiency issue.
