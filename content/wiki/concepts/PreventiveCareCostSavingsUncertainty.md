# Preventive Care Cost-Savings Uncertainty / 预防医疗控费不确定性

## Definition
Preventive care cost-savings uncertainty is the gap between showing that prevention can improve health or find disease earlier and proving that it reduces total medical spending.

## Current Synthesis
The Japan evidence in [[177-riben-yibao-jijin-weihe-yao-zhichi-mianfei-de-guomin-tijian-lh06qonrzgjprqdwl8dygn4hoxpk]] cautions against treating screening as automatically self-funding. Repeated guidance may improve some metabolic indicators, but the cited evidence on broader cardiovascular factors and total medical spending is mixed, participation remains incomplete, and the official savings target is described as weakly grounded and unrealized.

The Neko discussion in [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-daniel-ek-life-after-spotify-broken-healthcare-incentives-catching-di]] adds an important but separate incentive point. [[HealthcarePayerHorizonMismatch]] can make insurers reluctant to fund prevention when members may leave before benefits arrive. Fixing that mismatch could increase rational prevention investment, but it would not prove that every broad annual scan improves outcomes or saves money.

The current judgment is therefore two-part: preventive care can have health, equity, work-capacity, and patient-value benefits without lowering aggregate spend; and stronger longitudinal evidence is needed before early detection, behavior change, or favorable unit economics can be translated into system-level savings.

## Key Claims
- Health benefit and fiscal savings are different outcomes and should be measured separately.
- Participation, follow-up, and repeated support can matter more than nominally free access.
- Earlier detection may prevent later harm while also creating near-term diagnostic and treatment spending.
- Short payer tenure can suppress prevention investment even when long-run social value is positive.
- Product-level unit economics or founder-reported detection rates do not establish system-level savings.

## Evidence
- Mixed public-program evidence: [[177-riben-yibao-jijin-weihe-yao-zhichi-mianfei-de-guomin-tijian-lh06qonrzgjprqdwl8dygn4hoxpk]] contrasts some metabolic improvement with limited participation, small effects in another cited study, and weak support for the national savings claim.
- Incentive-horizon evidence: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-daniel-ek-life-after-spotify-broken-healthcare-incentives-catching-di]] argues that employment-linked insurer turnover can separate today's prevention payer from the future beneficiary.
- Early product signals: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-daniel-ek-life-after-spotify-broken-healthcare-incentives-catching-di]] reports Neko detection, behavior-change, and unit-economic claims while acknowledging that long-term budget impact is not yet known.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not show that prevention is generally ineffective or wasteful. Some interventions can be highly valuable and some may save costs in defined populations. The Japanese evidence is program- and study-specific, while the Neko evidence is founder-reported and lacks sufficient methodological detail, comparison groups, false-positive analysis, and downstream-cost accounting. This page is an evidence boundary, not an argument against screening.
