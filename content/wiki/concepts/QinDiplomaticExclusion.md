# Qin Diplomatic Exclusion / 秦国外交排斥

Qin diplomatic exclusion / 秦国外交排斥 is the status problem [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-11-shijie-fengqi-yunyong-2-llzzalowv5vmbvgd6l-jujltkbyf]] attaches to [[QinXiaogong|秦孝公]]'s accession. The episode says [[QinState|秦国]] faced military pressure from [[WeiState|魏国]] and [[ChuState|楚国]], but also a symbolic problem: the eastern states treated Qin as a barbarian or peripheral state and did not invite it into interstate meetings.

The concept matters because it makes Qin Xiao Gong's "布德修政" more than generic good-government language. Repairing rule, caring for vulnerable people, recruiting warriors, and clarifying merit rewards answer a double exclusion: Qin needs internal capacity to survive military pressure, and it needs visible strength to overcome a status order that has not accepted it as an equal.

This differs from [[WeakStateStatusDemotion|weak-state status demotion]]. [[WeyState|卫国]] lowers its titles to avoid pressure from stronger neighbors, while Qin is a rising but stigmatized power. Its problem is not only weakness; it is that outsiders can describe it as outside the civilized diplomatic circle even as its battlefield strength is growing.

## Key Claims
- Qin's pre-reform problem is military and diplomatic at once.
- Cultural stigma can shape interstate access even when a state is already militarily dangerous.
- Qin Xiao Gong's restoration agenda responds to exclusion by building domestic order, military recruitment, and reward credibility.
- Later Zhou recognition of Qin matters because it partially reverses this earlier exclusion.
