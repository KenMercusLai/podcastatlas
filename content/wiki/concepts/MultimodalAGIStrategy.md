# Multimodal AGI Strategy / 多模态 AGI 路线

## Definition

Multimodal AGI strategy is [[YanJunjie|严俊杰]]'s route to general intelligence in which a system must support both multimodal input and multimodal output, while language, speech, video, music, and other modalities may first develop separately before deeper integration.

## Current Synthesis

The strategy is simultaneously technical, organizational, and commercial. It assumes that modalities share enough scaling, data, training, and infrastructure logic to justify parallel capability building, but not that they can be merged immediately or developed without tradeoffs. Early separation allows each modality to reach useful quality; later fusion is expected to create a system whose perception, reasoning, interaction, and generation are less constrained by a text-only interface.

For [[MiniMax]], the strategy also supplies portfolio logic: different modalities create different product surfaces, user groups, cost structures, and revenue paths. This broad scope is defensible only if the organization can preserve foundations across modalities and make explicit resource choices rather than treating “multimodal” as a collection of unrelated features.

## Key Claims

- General intelligence requires more than language-only input and output.
- Language, vision, speech, video, and music share meaningful methodological foundations without being operationally identical.
- Separate modality progress can precede integration when joint training or product readiness is premature.
- Scaling remains central, but compute limits can force local architectural, data, and efficiency innovation.
- Multimodal scope creates strategic option value only when the organization can sustain technical depth and eventual integration.
- Product and revenue evidence may arrive at different speeds across modalities.

## Evidence

- AGI definition - [[lhqbap06a7-s4dxosnv6gdx1zesx-lhqbap06a7-s4dxosnv6gdx1zesx]] attributes the multimodal-input and multimodal-output definition directly to Yan.
- Development sequence - [[lhqbap06a7-s4dxosnv6gdx1zesx-lhqbap06a7-s4dxosnv6gdx1zesx]] says MiniMax let each modality develop first and expected later-generation integration when timing and capability permitted.
- Shared methods and constraints - [[lhqbap06a7-s4dxosnv6gdx1zesx-lhqbap06a7-s4dxosnv6gdx1zesx]] connects modalities through scaling and first-principles engineering while arguing that Chinese compute constraints discourage simple copying of U.S. routes.
- Portfolio evidence - [[lhqbap06a7-s4dxosnv6gdx1zesx-lhqbap06a7-s4dxosnv6gdx1zesx]] describes language, speech, video, music, Talkie, Hailuo, and APIs as related but economically non-identical parts of MiniMax's strategy.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source is a founder account, not a comparative benchmark of multimodal architectures. Parallel modality investment can dilute focus, increase infrastructure and evaluation burden, and delay clear product-market proof. Separate modality leadership does not itself prove successful fusion, AGI, or a durable business, and the source supplies no technical specification for the proposed integrated model.

## What Changed

- Created a bounded strategy concept separating multimodal AGI from generic media-generation breadth.
- Made staged modality development and later integration explicit.
- Added organizational focus, compute constraint, and modality-specific economics as qualifications.
