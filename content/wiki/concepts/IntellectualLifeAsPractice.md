# Intellectual Life As Practice / 智识生活作为实践

Intellectual life as practice is [[ep259-yu-miao-shifu-mantan-xu-guangqi-sibainian-qian-ruhe-rangren-biancongming-lkirczxnx8skbbfpmz5-m-mivs4e]]'s answer to the title question: how did people try to become smarter four hundred years ago? [[MiaoWeiSanlian|苗伟]] uses a term glossed in the source as "制食生活" and summarizes it with "心心归实，跃跃含灵": a life that faces real problems while keeping the mind active.

The concept differs from information accumulation. In the source, [[XuGuangqi|徐光启]] becomes exemplary because he studies difficult geometry, asks about astronomy and the universe, translates terms, works on agriculture, and still cares about famine, defense, and the state. The intellectual life is therefore both contemplative and practical.

The concept also criticizes moralized or decorative learning. The episode admires people who learn hard things before claiming certainty and warns against turning knowledge into obscure language, cultural purity, or empty status. That puts it near [[LearningHowToLearn|learning how to learn]], but with a stronger historical and ethical emphasis.

## Key Claims
- Intellectual life is a habit of curiosity, reasoning, and reality contact.
- Becoming smarter requires difficult tools: mathematics, language, physical knowledge, and disciplined attention.
- Practical statecraft and deep curiosity can reinforce each other instead of being opposites.
- Moral confidence without learning can become an obstacle to understanding.
- The source treats Xu Guangqi as a model because he combines responsibility with genuine intellectual appetite.
