# Scarcity Mindset / 稀缺心态

## Definition
Scarcity mindset / 稀缺心态 is the cognitive state created when a person experiences having less than they need, causing the scarce resource to dominate attention, narrow perception, and reshape decisions.

## Current Synthesis
The EP87 source treats scarcity as broader than poverty. Money, time, food, companionship, safety, status, beauty, ability, and self-worth can all become scarce enough to pull attention into a tunnel. That makes scarcity partly objective and partly subjective: real constraints matter, but the perceived gap between "have" and "need" also determines how strongly the mind is captured.

Scarcity is not only harmful. In short bursts, it can focus attention and make a task feel concrete, which is why deadlines sometimes work. The cost is that the tunnel hides side effects. Once the person loses slack, the mind spends bandwidth on urgent shortage management, makes more mistakes, and can enter a downward scarcity trap.

## Key Claims
- Scarcity is experienced as a gap between available resources and felt needs, not only as an absolute resource level.
- Attention capture is the first mechanism: scarce objects become easier to notice and harder to ignore.
- Scarcity can create a focus dividend when the task is bounded and the cost is contained.
- The same focus becomes harmful when it hides future costs, safety checks, relationships, health, or important non-urgent work.
- Poverty is treated as an especially severe scarcity because money, time, slack, and bandwidth shortages often compound.
- Structural pressures can manufacture scarcity messages, especially when social norms repeatedly tell people they are not enough.

## Evidence
- Definition and scope evidence: [[ep87-xique-ruhe-sheji-yige-rang-ziji-bian-congming-de-xitong-gkwrirwoeedlbme1xqtv2orv]] defines scarcity as having less than one needs and applies it to time, money, food, loneliness, dieting, anxiety, and safety.
- Attention evidence: [[ep87-xique-ruhe-sheji-yige-rang-ziji-bian-congming-de-xitong-gkwrirwoeedlbme1xqtv2orv]] uses hunger, thirst, loneliness, and deadline examples to show scarce objects capturing perception.
- Focus-dividend evidence: [[ep87-xique-ruhe-sheji-yige-rang-ziji-bian-congming-de-xitong-gkwrirwoeedlbme1xqtv2orv]] describes deadline focus and the "angry blueberry" game as cases where scarcity increases precision.
- Trap evidence: [[ep87-xique-ruhe-sheji-yige-rang-ziji-bian-congming-de-xitong-gkwrirwoeedlbme1xqtv2orv]] links missing slack, bandwidth decline, tunneling, short loans, procrastination, and dopamine cascades into a reinforcing scarcity trap.
- Structural-pressure evidence: [[ep87-xique-ruhe-sheji-yige-rang-ziji-bian-congming-de-xitong-gkwrirwoeedlbme1xqtv2orv]] argues that women often receive chronic "not enough" messages around appearance, competence, marriage, fertility, work, family, and the second shift.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept should not imply that subjective reframing can solve every shortage. The source explicitly treats poverty and structural pressure as real constraints, not mere mindset errors. Its experimental examples and specific effect sizes remain source-scoped unless later sources independently corroborate them.
