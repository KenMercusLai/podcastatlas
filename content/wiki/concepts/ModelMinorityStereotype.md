# Model Minority Stereotype / 模范少数族裔刻板印象

## Definition
The model-minority stereotype assigns Asian people generalized success, diligence, educational attainment, or technical competence and presents those traits as group culture rather than unequal and individual histories.

## Current Synthesis
An apparently positive stereotype can still constrain. Cultural praise can erase personal effort, hide people who need support, narrow occupational expectations, and imply that Asian Americans succeed by remaining compliant and apolitical. It also coexists with the [[PerpetualForeignerStereotype]]: the same person may be treated as unusually capable yet unsuitable for leadership, socially unassimilated, or politically suspect. The newer source adds a relational function through [[RacialTriangulation]]: selected Asian outcomes can be used as evidence against structural explanations for Black inequality even when immigration selection, historical exposure, and internal Asian diversity make that comparison unsound.

## Key Claims
- Positive valence does not remove a stereotype's power to flatten individual difference.
- Explaining achievement through group culture can discount effort, institutions, selection effects, class, and unequal opportunity.
- Assumed competence in mathematics or technical work can coexist with assumptions of weak leadership, masculinity, sociability, or creativity.
- Aggregate success imagery can conceal poverty, distress, discrimination, and variation across Asian communities.
- Model-minority praise can be used to discipline other minorities or portray structural inequality as a problem of culture alone.
- Success does not dissolve conditional belonging; achievement and foreignness can be assigned simultaneously.
- Immigration selection and unequal historical starting points limit what cross-group outcome comparisons can establish.

## Evidence
- Cultural attribution: [[ep169-meiguo-shehui-ruhe-zhizao-mofan-shaoshu-he-beihou-de-yinxing-qishi-ckwriaiewlfyabaaaacimxkl]] says educational and economic achievement is often attributed to Asian cultural tradition in ways that deny individual effort.
- Occupational constraint: [[ep169-meiguo-shehui-ruhe-zhizao-mofan-shaoshu-he-beihou-de-yinxing-qishi-ckwriaiewlfyabaaaacimxkl]] pairs mathematical or technical competence with assumptions that Asian men lack masculinity or leadership.
- Belonging contradiction: [[ep169-meiguo-shehui-ruhe-zhizao-mofan-shaoshu-he-beihou-de-yinxing-qishi-ckwriaiewlfyabaaaacimxkl]] argues that praise for success can reinforce cultural difference and therefore the idea that Asian Americans remain foreign.
- Political comparison: [[ep134-cong-zhongzu-geli-de-nanfei-dao-92-nian-de-luoshanji-saoluan-quanshijie-de-pingquan-zouguo-le-duoshao-jiannan-suiyue-wkg5iv9bqfhcsvfcbefeypd8dsq114]] says Asian success is invoked to blame Black Americans while overlooking different histories and the selection of many post-1965 migrants for education or skill.
- Internal variation: [[ep134-cong-zhongzu-geli-de-nanfei-dao-92-nian-de-luoshanji-saoluan-quanshijie-de-pingquan-zouguo-le-duoshao-jiannan-suiyue-wkg5iv9bqfhcsvfcbefeypd8dsq114]] stresses that East, South, and Southeast Asian communities do not share one socioeconomic position.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not deny real educational effort, family strategy, immigration selection, or community resources. They dispute treating averages as an essence or using success stories to infer that every Asian subgroup or individual has the same outcomes and needs. Neither podcast supplies comparative demographic evidence sufficient to quantify subgroup outcomes or causal weights, and the phrase's full political history requires additional evidence.

## What Changed
- Established model-minority praise and perpetual foreignness as mutually compatible rather than opposing stereotypes.
- Added leadership restriction, erased effort, and hidden internal inequality as distinct harms.
- Added racial triangulation, migration selection, and unequal historical baselines to the stereotype's political use.
