# Micro-Rest Learning Replay

## Definition
Micro-rest learning replay is the insertion of brief, wakeful, low-input pauses within practice so recently performed material can be reactivated without continued overt effort.

## Current Synthesis
The episode presents rest as part of learning rather than only recovery after it. In the cited skill-learning work, practice interrupted by roughly ten-second idle periods produced better learning and retention than uninterrupted practice, while hippocampal and cortical activity reportedly replayed the practiced sequence at accelerated speed during the pauses. This fits a broader spacing principle: continuous input can compete with offline organization, whereas short quiet gaps may create repeated opportunities for consolidation.

The evidence supports a modest practice-design experiment, not a universal timing law. The supplied note does not identify the task, sample, effect size, or whether the reported replay directly caused the behavioral improvement. A short pause within practice is also distinct from a later nap, sleep, or [[NonSleepDeepRestRecovery]], even though the episode proposes that these recovery scales may complement one another.

## Key Claims
- Brief wakeful pauses can function as part of a practice bout rather than as lost training time.
- The episode reports improved learning and retention when ten-second idle periods interrupt continuous skill practice.
- Accelerated hippocampal and cortical replay during pauses is a proposed mechanism for the improvement.
- Micro-rests instantiate the broader spacing principle by alternating input with low-interference intervals.
- Within-practice pauses and post-learning recovery operate at different timescales and should not be treated as interchangeable evidence.

## Evidence
- Interrupted-practice result - [[scim1865244910-scim1865244910]] reports a Cell Reports skill-learning study in which ten-second idle rests improved learning and retention relative to continuous practice.
- Replay mechanism - [[scim1865244910-scim1865244910]] describes hippocampal and cortical replay of the practiced sequence at roughly twenty times ordinary speed during the rests.
- Multi-timescale rest - [[scim1865244910-scim1865244910]] connects micro-rests to spacing and separately proposes a short nap or decompressed period after learning.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The supplied source summary does not provide the study population, task, sample size, effect magnitude, replication status, or full recording method. The reported replay association does not by itself prove that replay caused the learning benefit, and a ten-second interval should not be treated as optimal across cognitive study, motor practice, rehabilitation, or every learner. The episode explicitly says that combining micro-rests with post-learning rest had not been directly tested.

## What Changed
- Created a bounded account of short within-practice pauses, accelerated replay, and their distinction from later recovery.
