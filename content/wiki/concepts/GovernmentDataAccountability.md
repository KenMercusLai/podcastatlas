# Government Data Accountability

Government data accountability is the expectation that agencies should answer for how they collect, use, share, and misuse personal data. In [[tech-20260409-0409-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260409-0409-mp-tech-pod-128]], [[ElizabethLaird]] says Americans across demographic groups supported congressional oversight and accountability when agencies may be misusing people's data.

The concept is the governance counterpart to [[FederalDataPracticeTrust]]. Trust cannot be restored only by asking people to accept data collection; it needs visible process, oversight, and consequences when data use exceeds the purpose people expected.

## Key Claims
- Accountability matters most when people believe the government has extensive data and little individual recourse exists.
- Oversight can turn generalized distrust into a concrete policy agenda.
- Data accountability must cover sharing and secondary use, not only breaches or collection.
- Congress can matter as an institutional check when agency data practices become a public-trust issue.
