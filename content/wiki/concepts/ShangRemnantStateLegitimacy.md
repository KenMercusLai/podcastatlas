# Shang-Remnant State Legitimacy / 商裔封国合法性

Shang-remnant state legitimacy / 商裔封国合法性 is the episode's explanation of why [[SongState|宋国]] was special among pre-Qin states. In [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-07-liangqi-cisha-2-lhngst3xsnyjrmzh1cvheocvvsqm]], Song is presented as the preserved lineage of the conquered Shang house through [[WeiziQi|微子启]], honored by Zhou rather than eliminated.

The concept is a legitimacy mechanism distinct from simple conquest. By allowing the prior dynasty's survivors to continue ancestor worship and by granting Song high status, the new order can display ritual magnanimity and continuity. The episode then connects that status to later cultural memory by tracing [[Confucius|孔子]]'s ancestry through [[KongfuJia|孔父嘉]] and Song nobility.

## Key Claims
- Conquered-lineage preservation can help stabilize a new order's legitimacy.
- Ritual honor can turn a defeated house into a guest within the new political system.
- Ancestor worship and lineage continuity are political as well as familial.
- Genealogy can connect later intellectual authority back into older aristocratic orders.
