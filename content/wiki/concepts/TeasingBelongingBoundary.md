# Teasing Belonging Boundary

## Definition
Teasing belonging boundary is the source's distinction between healthy teasing that keeps people inside a group and harmful teasing that humiliates, bullies, or excludes them.

## Current Synthesis
The concept turns teasing into a social-boundary test. Keltner's fraternity research and Huberman's comments on male friendship both frame teasing as potentially affiliative when loyalty and face-to-face play remain intact. The boundary is whether the target remains a valued member of the group or is pushed into shame, status loss, or exclusion.

## Key Claims
- Healthy teasing can strengthen belonging when it communicates inclusion and shared norms.
- Embarrassment can increase closeness when the social context remains supportive.
- Harmful teasing becomes bullying or harassment when it humiliates, excludes, or strips dignity.
- Male friendship is discussed as one setting where teasing may coexist with behind-the-back loyalty.
- The distinction depends on relationship context, power, and whether the target is protected or abandoned.

## Evidence
- Keltner research example: [[cultivating-awe-emotional-connection-in-daily-life-dr-dacher-keltner-scim6985403733]] says he studied fraternity groups teasing one another with nicknames and found embarrassment could increase liking and closeness.
- Boundary claim: [[cultivating-awe-emotional-connection-in-daily-life-dr-dacher-keltner-scim6985403733]] distinguishes healthy teasing that keeps people in the group from harmful teasing that humiliates or excludes.
- Friendship example: [[cultivating-awe-emotional-connection-in-daily-life-dr-dacher-keltner-scim6985403733]] has Huberman connect male friendship to face-to-face teasing plus loyalty behind someone's back.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source's examples do not erase power differences, gender differences, cultural variation, or the possibility that teasing framed as affectionate is experienced as harm. The boundary requires attention to consent, context, repetition, and exclusion.
