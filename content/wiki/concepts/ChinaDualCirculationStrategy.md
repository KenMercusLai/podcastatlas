# China Dual Circulation Strategy / 中国双循环战略

## Definition
China's dual circulation strategy is a long-run effort to strengthen domestic demand and domestic production capability while retaining trade and investment links with the world.

## Current Synthesis
The episode presents dual circulation as a response to worsening external conditions and the limits of export-led growth in a very large, increasingly higher-income economy. It is not equated with autarky. Domestic circulation requires household purchasing power and lower internal development gaps, while international circulation still supplies markets, technology, inputs, and competitive pressure. Supply-side upgrading and demand expansion are therefore complementary rather than interchangeable.

## Key Claims
- Dual circulation responds to geopolitical and external-demand risk rather than only to the pandemic.
- Domestic circulation requires real household consumption capacity, not merely production targets.
- Subsidies aimed at lower-tier markets can activate demand but do not substitute for income growth and social support.
- Supply-side reform should move resources away from low-value, high-energy production toward higher-productivity sectors.
- Complete supply chains can slow relocation even when simple labor-intensive stages move abroad.
- International trade remains part of the strategy; resilience is not the same as isolation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source offers an early explanatory framework rather than evidence of later implementation or success. It does not quantify domestic-demand rebalancing, productivity gains, relocation, technology dependence, or household-income effects.

## What Changed
- Added dual circulation as a linked demand, resilience, trade, and industrial-upgrading strategy rather than a synonym for self-sufficiency.
