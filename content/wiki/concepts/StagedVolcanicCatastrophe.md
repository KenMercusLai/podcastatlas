# Staged Volcanic Catastrophe

## Definition

Staged volcanic catastrophe is a framework for understanding an eruption as a changing sequence of hazards whose timing, direction, and survivability differ across nearby places.

## Current Synthesis

The Vesuvius episode shows why a disaster should not be compressed into one moment. Earthquakes and drying springs preceded the eruption; column growth and darkness changed perception; wind concentrated pumice over Pompeii; streets and roofs became hazards; pumice blocked sea escape; a surge destroyed Herculaneum; a temporary easing enabled renewed movement; and final column collapse overwhelmed Pompeii. Relative safety in one phase could become fatal exposure in the next.

## Key Claims

- Eruption risk changes through time as warning, fall, accumulation, surge, flow, ash, gas, and secondary collapse become dominant.
- Nearby settlements can face different sequences because wind, distance, terrain, coast access, and infrastructure redistribute exposure.
- Shelter and evacuation are phase-dependent choices: a building may protect against falling material yet fail under roof loading or surge.
- Temporary easing can create a genuine escape window without meaning the catastrophe is over.
- Retrospective narratives should preserve uncertainty about exact timing while still distinguishing materially different hazard stages.

## Evidence

- Precursor and onset sequence: [[355-roman-apocalypse-pompeii-79-ad-glt6664993493]] moves from earthquakes and dry springs to column growth, darkness, and pumice.
- Spatial divergence: [[355-roman-apocalypse-pompeii-79-ad-glt6664993493]] contrasts the wind-driven Pompeii fall with Herculaneum's lighter early exposure.
- Reversal of safety: [[355-roman-apocalypse-pompeii-79-ad-glt6664993493]] shows Herculaneum spared heavy pumice before an unsurvivable surge.
- Escape window and closure: [[355-roman-apocalypse-pompeii-79-ad-glt6664993493]] describes Pompeian movement near dawn before final column collapse.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode's reconstructed chronology is not a universal eruption template. Precise timing, flow terminology, speeds, and individual decisions require site-specific geological and archaeological evidence, and modern evacuation planning cannot be derived directly from an ancient case.

## What Changed

- Created a time- and place-sensitive model of volcanic disaster from the Pompeii-Herculaneum contrast.
