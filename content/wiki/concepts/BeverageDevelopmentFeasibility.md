# Beverage Development Feasibility / 饮品研发可落地性

## Definition

Beverage-development feasibility is the translation of a creative drink idea into a repeatable product that fits flavor, brand, economics, workflow, safety, supply, and intended production scale.

## Current Synthesis

A recipe is commercially incomplete until it can be manufactured or served reliably. Hospitality development works backward from brand position, occasion, and intended experience while testing cost, ingredient count, preparation, staff execution, safety, and supply. Packaged products add aroma construction, formulation stability, and manufacturing minimums. Chain fast food adds another scale test: a drink must survive thousands of stores, purchasing systems, training, equipment, and throughput limits. Constraints do not merely obstruct creativity; they determine which concept becomes a repeatable product. Feasibility still does not prove durable demand.

## Key Claims

- Brand position, intended occasion, and flavor logic define the solution space before recipe optimization.
- Cost, ingredient count, preparation steps, equipment, and production minimums determine whether an idea leaves the laboratory or test kitchen.
- Safety, formulation stability, reliable supply, and staff repeatability are product properties rather than downstream details.
- Aroma, mouthfeel, function, packaging, and service conditions must work as one system.
- Chain-scale feasibility requires purchasing and store workflows to survive wide variation in staff and demand.
- A feasible prototype or rollout is not proof of product-market fit or durable traffic.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The sources describe practitioner methods, reported company visits, and secondary industry examples rather than comparative operating data. Exact thresholds depend on format, jurisdiction, channel, and store system. The Starbucks and McDonald's figures are episode-attributed; the supplied summary does not document testing criteria, rollout completion, unit economics, or a full sensory, safety, regulatory, and commercial evaluation.

## What Changed

- Extended feasibility from hospitality and packaged drinks into thousands-of-stores fast-food execution.
- Added staff, equipment, purchasing, and throughput as chain-scale development gates.
