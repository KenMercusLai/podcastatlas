# Desegregation and Educator Displacement

## Definition

Desegregation and educator displacement describe an implementation pattern in which legal integration moves Black students into white-controlled schools while closing Black schools, dismissing Black teachers and administrators, and failing to redistribute institutional authority.

## Current Synthesis

The bounded episode distinguishes the constitutional end of enforced segregation from the organizational choices used to implement it. In Moberly, officials closed Lincoln School, transferred its Black students, and dismissed all eleven Black teachers. In Topeka, [[DarlaBuchanan]] was terminated because officials assumed white families would reject a Black teacher.

Subjective evaluation made exclusion portable into the formally integrated system. Moberly officials invoked personality, character, disposition, and adaptability, even describing highly qualified Mary Ella Timoney as superior and resentful of authority. The episode reports a much wider purge—roughly half of about 82,000 Black Southern teachers dismissed within a decade—and treats the continuing underrepresentation of Black teachers as an institutional deficit, not a temporary side effect.

## Key Claims

- Student desegregation and integration of professional authority are distinct institutional outcomes.
- Closing Black schools often made Black educators, rather than white incumbents, absorb consolidation costs.
- Subjective professional criteria could preserve racial exclusion without an explicit segregation rule.
- White parental prejudice became an administrative reason for excluding Black teachers.
- Educator displacement removed employment, authority, community knowledge, and representative access at the same time.
- [[CelestinePorter]]'s reciprocal teacher-integration proposal identifies an alternative that would distribute adult authority across schools.

- [[revisited-ms-buchanan-s-period-of-adjustment-revisited-ms-buchanan-s-period-of-adjustment]] describes officials' reliance on intangible traits to reject Black teachers, including a highly qualified applicant.

### Parental prejudice and unequal burden

- [[revisited-ms-buchanan-s-period-of-adjustment-revisited-ms-buchanan-s-period-of-adjustment]] links Buchanan's termination to anticipated white resistance and reports national-scale job losses and humiliation among retained Black staff.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The supplied files are substantially overlapping versions of the same episode and are not independent corroboration. They supply a corrective history, not a complete national implementation study. Their teacher totals, timeline, legal outcomes, and present representation claim require verification against personnel datasets, court records, and local histories. Districts varied, and the pattern does not negate the necessity or gains of ending state-enforced school segregation; it shows that enrollment integration alone was insufficient to protect employment or redistribute authority.

## What Changed

- Established a distinction between integrated enrollment and integrated professional authority.
- Identified school closure, subjective evaluation, and accommodation of parental prejudice as displacement mechanisms.
- Connected historical dismissal to a persistent teacher-representation deficit while keeping the national figures source-scoped.
- Added the original episode as overlapping provenance without increasing the apparent independence of the evidence.
