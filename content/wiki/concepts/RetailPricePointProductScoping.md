# Retail Price-Point Product Scoping

Retail price-point product scoping is the pattern where a target shelf price determines what the product can physically include. In [[who-decides-what-big-box-sells-our-game-got-us-answers]], buyer questions about whether [[SellMeASasquatch]] belongs near a $9.99 card-game price or closer to a $20 game become product-design questions, not only pricing questions.

The episode says [[ExplodingKittens]] ultimately gets agreement to a suggested retail price of $19.99. That decision lets the team put more value into higher-quality cards, a better box, and Pantone purple rather than treating the price only as margin.

## Key Claims
- Retail price can define component scope, packaging quality, and perceived product category.
- A higher price point has to make value visible through [[PackagingAsProductExperience]], not only through internal cost.
- Buyer price questions can be a positive signal when they imply the buyer is thinking about how to sell the product.
- Price scoping connects [[ProductLedWillingnessToPay]] to manufacturing choices because materials, box quality, and shelf signal must justify the chosen price.
