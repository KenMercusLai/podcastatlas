# ETF Holding-Horizon Classification / ETF 持有期限分类

## Definition
ETF holding-horizon classification separates funds that may serve as long-duration exposure from funds whose returns mainly depend on volatility, cycles, concentration, or tactical timing. The classification begins with the economic behavior of the underlying assets and index rules, not with the ETF label or the investor's greater emotional tolerance for a basket.

## Current Synthesis
The [[xin-yidai-touzizhe-sihu-bu-ai-mai-gupiao-le-zhujian-bei-etf-zhudao-de-shichang-he-weilai-lk1lxwniotregvs9euhepkm9nruo|面基 episode]] observes that investors often use broad ETFs for recurring accumulation but thematic ETFs for aggressive bets. It proposes a first split: assets supported by durable earnings growth, dividends, or buybacks may qualify for long-horizon ownership, while cyclical or high-volatility themes need an explicit [[TrendFollowing|trend]] or range-based trading framework. This does not make every broad index safe or every theme tactical; concentration, valuation, methodology, fees, liquidity, and personal capacity still determine [[PortfolioSuitability|suitability]]. The key discipline is to classify the exposure before buying so that a losing trade is not renamed a long-term investment after the fact.

## Key Claims
- ETF structure does not determine holding period; underlying cash flows, cyclicality, concentration, leverage, and index rules do.
- Durable earnings, dividends, or buybacks can support long-horizon exposure without guaranteeing a good entry price.
- High-volatility or cyclical themes require a predetermined trading method, position size, and exit rule.
- Greater willingness to tolerate an ETF loss than a single-stock loss can improve discipline, but it can also hide weak analysis behind the comfort of a basket.
- Broad and thematic labels are insufficient: investors should inspect constituents, weights, rebalancing rules, fees, scale, and liquidity.
- A tactical loss should not be converted into indefinite holding merely because the ETF wrapper makes the position feel diversified.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode offers a decision heuristic rather than a backtested taxonomy. Earnings-backed indexes can still be overvalued, dividend strategies can suffer payout cuts, and apparently tactical themes can become durable industries. Trend and range methods introduce whipsaw, model, tax, and execution costs. Product-level details such as leverage, daily reset, derivatives, currency exposure, replication, and market hours can override the broad category. Suitability also depends on horizon, goals, liquidity needs, tax, and drawdown capacity, so this concept is not individualized investment advice.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to distinguish ETF wrapper, underlying economics, and intended holding period.
- Added the pre-commitment rule against turning an unsuccessful trade into an unplanned long-term holding.
- Preserved valuation, product mechanics, and personal suitability as checks on the long-horizon category.
