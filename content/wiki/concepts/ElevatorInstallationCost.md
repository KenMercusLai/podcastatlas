# Elevator Installation Cost

## Definition
Elevator installation cost is the combined price and spatial burden of adding an elevator, shaped in the source by labor practices, assembly rules, cabin dimensions, and code requirements.

## Current Synthesis
The episode reports a striking United States–Europe gap: €20,000–€30,000 for a compact three-story Italian installation versus roughly $120,000–$150,000 for an American counterpart. It attributes part of the gap to a small, specialized, highly paid, and highly unionized workforce and to practices that can require factory-assembled equipment to be taken apart and rebuilt on site.

Its larger claim concerns physical standards. American and Canadian cabins for small apartment buildings may be roughly twice the size of common European examples. A larger cabin can provide better wheelchair maneuvering and stretcher access, but it also requires more shaft area and project spending, making installation harder in existing or affordable small buildings.

## Key Claims
- American elevator installation is reported to cost several times more than a compact European counterpart.
- Specialized labor and on-site reassembly can add cost and inefficiency.
- Large mandatory cabins increase both equipment cost and the floor area consumed by the shaft.
- Cost and footprint influence whether a small project installs any elevator, not merely which model it buys.
- The source does not isolate the percentage of the price gap caused by labor, cabin size, regulation, or other market conditions.

## Evidence
### Comparative price and form
- [[the-great-american-elevator-tragedy-the-mistakes-series-the-great-american-elevator-tragedy-the-mistakes-series]] reports the Italian and American estimates and says compact European elevators can fit buildings that current American rules would exclude.

### Labor mechanism
- [[the-great-american-elevator-tragedy-the-mistakes-series-the-great-american-elevator-tragedy-the-mistakes-series]] describes specialized pay, factory assembly in Europe, and American on-site disassembly and rebuilding practices.

### Cabin-size mechanism
- [[the-great-american-elevator-tragedy-the-mistakes-series-the-great-american-elevator-tragedy-the-mistakes-series]] reports that a typical European cabin in the comparison is about half the size of an American or Canadian one.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not normalize estimates for currency, date, capacity, speed, safety equipment, maintenance, taxes, building type, retrofit difficulty, or local labor market. It gives no full cost decomposition or response from unions and manufacturers. The reported gap is a strong episode-level signal, not a controlled international estimate.

## What Changed
- Created a cost concept separating labor and physical-standard mechanisms from the broader accessibility judgment.
