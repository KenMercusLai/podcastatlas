# Pesticide Pollinator Risk / 农药传粉昆虫风险

Pesticide pollinator risk is the agricultural risk created when chemicals meant to control crop pests also injure bees and other insects that pollinate crops and wild plants. In [[ep261-mifeng-weiji-ruguo-shiqu-mifeng-shijie-jianghui-zenyang-lqp-cwcdxx1ziixb35ijmmvd6cv]], the problem appears through [[DuanNongyu|段农裕]]'s beekeeper reporting and [[LiuChang|刘畅]]'s [[Xishuangbanna|西双版纳]] field material: drone spraying, high-toxicity pesticides, and poorly coordinated orchard management can kill or weaken pollinators outside the narrow target pest.

The risk is practical before it becomes abstract. If a managed colony is heavily poisoned, a beekeeper may need one or two months to rebuild the colony and can miss the next flower season. For researchers, one nearby spraying event can invalidate field experiments. For crop growers, pesticide damage can undercut the pollination service that would otherwise raise yields.

## Key Claims
- Pesticide harm depends on toxicity, timing, exposure path, and whether growers coordinate with beekeepers.
- Drone spraying can enlarge the coordination problem because chemicals are applied quickly and across larger areas.
- Low-toxicity choices may reduce harm, but the episode presents high-toxicity pesticide exposure as capable of causing large colony deaths.
- Pesticide risk can be priced into [[PollinationServiceMarket|pollination services]], but pricing does not restore lost flower seasons or wild-insect diversity.
- The issue links beekeeper income, crop yield, field science, and [[WildPollinatorCrisis|wild-pollinator decline]] into one operating problem.
