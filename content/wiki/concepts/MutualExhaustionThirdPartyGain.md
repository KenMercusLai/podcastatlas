# 两败俱伤、旁人得利 / Mutual Exhaustion Third-Party Gain

## Definition
Mutual exhaustion third-party gain is the pattern where two rivals spend enough force, legitimacy, attention, or trust fighting each other that an outside actor can profit from their depletion.

## Current Synthesis
Hanji 1059 gives the concept through [[LiuBiao|刘表]]'s use of the Hanlu dog and Dongguo hare parable while warning [[YuanTan|袁谭]] and [[YuanShang|袁尚]] to stop fighting. In the story, the pursuing hound and fleeing hare both die from exhaustion, and an uninvolved farmer receives the gain. Liu Biao maps this onto Yuan-family conflict: if the brothers continue to fight, [[CaoCao|曹操]] can benefit without bearing the full cost of defeating a unified Yuan bloc.

The episode then complicates the pattern through Cao Cao's decision room. Some officials want to attack Liu Biao and let the Yuan brothers exhaust each other, but [[XunYou|荀攸]] argues that passivity can expire as a strategy. If one brother absorbs the other and reunites Yuan Shao's four-province base, the third party loses the easy opening. The pattern therefore names both the opportunity created by internal conflict and the timing risk of waiting too long.

Hanji 1060 follows that qualification into action. [[XinPiLateHan|辛毗]] argues that [[YuanTan|袁谭]]'s request and [[YuanShang|袁尚]]'s inability to finish him prove that mutual exhaustion has already reached a usable threshold. Cao Cao should not simply watch while turning toward wealthy [[Jingzhou|荆州]]; he should support Yuan Tan and attack into Hebei before harvest recovery or reconciliation closes the opening.

## Key Claims
- Rival conflict can transfer value to an outside actor even when neither rival intends to help that actor.
- Exhaustion is not only troop loss; it can include legitimacy damage, family trust collapse, alliance fatigue, and loss of coordinated timing.
- Warnings against mutual exhaustion often use moral language, but the warning speaker may also have a self-preserving strategic interest.
- A third party can benefit by waiting, mediating, or intervening, depending on whether the rivals are still fragmenting or already reconsolidating.
- The opportunity is time-limited: if one side wins the internal conflict and absorbs the other, the outside actor may face a stronger unified opponent.
- Once mutual exhaustion is visible in both elite conflict and material collapse, the third party may need to support one side to keep the rivalry operationally useful.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
These sources provide one late-Han application and one inherited parable, not a universal law of conflict. Mutual exhaustion may help a bystander only if the bystander has enough capacity to act, avoid being drawn into the cost, or choose the right intervention point. The same internal conflict that creates an opening can also end in reconsolidation if the outsider waits too long, and Hanji 1060's easy-victory forecast still remains source-scoped until later campaign results are integrated.

## What Changed
- Created from Hanji 1059 to capture the Hanlu-Dongguo parable as a reusable strategic pattern.
- Added Xun You's qualification that waiting for rivals to exhaust each other can become dangerous if one side absorbs the other.
- Added Hanji 1060 as the move from bystander gain to active support for one exhausted rival.
