# Physical-Constraint Forecasting

## Definition
Physical-constraint forecasting estimates what a technology can do, where it can operate, and when it can scale by tracing measurable material, capacity, cost, and deployment limits.

## Current Synthesis
The current source calls this method "follow the physics." A useful forecast need not name the exact next invention if it can bound energy production, data-center and network capacity, cloud and edge placement, procurement cycles, infrastructure build-out, hardware economics, product life, and scaling speed. The method turns technology forecasting from a narrative of possibility into a conditional deployment map, but its outputs remain estimates that depend on disclosed assumptions and can be changed by policy, behavior, substitution, or unforeseen engineering progress.

## Key Claims
- Physical and economic capacity can bound plausible adoption even when the winning invention is unknown.
- Energy, compute, networks, manufacturing, infrastructure, procurement, and replacement cycles operate on different clocks.
- A technically functional prototype is not evidence that a product can scale through the intended environment or channel.
- Five-year forecasts are more defensible when major capacity additions and enterprise adoption cycles are observable.
- Digital systems remain dependent on materials, energy, facilities, and logistics rather than existing in an immaterial layer.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source supplies a method and examples, not a scored forecast record or quantitative model. Constraints are not fixed laws: regulation, war, capital allocation, demand changes, component substitution, and technical breakthroughs can alter them. The episode's AI, freight, manufacturing, healthcare, and space forecasts therefore remain source-scoped rather than validated outputs of the method.

## What Changed
- Established a forecasting method that treats deployment capacity and physical economics as primary evidence.
- Added a boundary between estimating possibility and predicting the exact invention or market winner.
