## Definition
Human-machine voice agency is a control model in which a person remains the author of communicative intention and emotional expression while a machine assists with vocal sound, identity, amplification, conversion, or delivery.

## Current Synthesis
The source avoids a binary choice between untouched biological voice and autonomous synthetic speech. In Zieliński's described system, he manually controls emotional expression while the device helps produce a recognizable vocal sound. This makes agency depend on the locus and legibility of control: assistance can extend a person's capacity when it follows their timing and affect, but can obscure authorship when the model invents emotion, changes language, delays entry, or speaks beyond the user's intent.

The distinction is practical rather than metaphysical. A person can call himself a cyborg and remain human because the tool is integrated into action without becoming the source of purpose. The same standard suggests that voice systems should expose override, constrain unexpected behavior, and preserve evidence that a person—not an autonomous agent—authorized the utterance.

## Key Claims
- Generated vocal sound does not by itself eliminate human authorship.
- Intention, emotion, timing, and initiation are distinct control dimensions from timbre and acoustic rendering.
- Assistive systems should preserve user override and prevent autonomous language or affect changes.
- Low latency matters to agency because delayed output weakens control over conversational timing.
- Personal vocal identity can be machine-mediated while remaining subject to consent and portability.
- Authentication should distinguish a human using augmentation from an autonomous system imitating a human.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source supplies one founder-user's architecture and philosophy rather than a general measure of agency. Manual control can create cognitive or physical load, and other users may prefer more automation. The episode does not specify how intention is captured, how errors are reversed, or how responsibility should be assigned when the generated voice diverges from the user's intended utterance.

## What Changed
- Defined voice agency as an allocation of control across intention, emotion, timing, and acoustic rendering.
