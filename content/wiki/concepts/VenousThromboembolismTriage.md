# Venous Thromboembolism Triage / 静脉血栓栓塞分诊

## Definition
Venous thromboembolism triage / 静脉血栓栓塞分诊 is the public-facing escalation boundary for suspected lower-limb thrombosis or pulmonary embolism risk: recognizing concerning leg-swelling patterns, limiting risky movement, and routing the person to urgent clinical evaluation.

## Current Synthesis
The VOL.204 source makes triage practical by asking what the leg looks and feels like rather than asking laypeople to diagnose vascular anatomy. Continuous swelling below a vascular level, sudden unilateral swelling, pain, calf hardness, muscle-tension change, or marked whole-limb swelling shifts the situation away from ordinary travel discomfort and toward urgent evaluation.

The distinctive contribution is transport logic. If thrombosis is genuinely suspected, the episode says the first problem is not which outpatient department to register for but how to get to care without provoking embolization risk: avoid self-walking where possible, use a wheelchair or stretcher, and let emergency workflows assess pulmonary embolism danger.

VOL.111 adds the wider clinical frame. Leg swelling can reflect thrombosis, venous narrowing or compression, reflux, varicose veins, or lymphatic obstruction, while breathlessness can signal pulmonary embolism among other causes. It also distinguishes acute, less organized clot from older clot and connects treatment choice to timing and mechanism, without turning those descriptions into a home rule for judging clot stability.

VOL.67 contributes a retrospective pulmonary-embolism case rather than a general symptom algorithm. After fracture-related immobility, [[LiangGeAnesthesiologist|亮哥]] reports sudden breathlessness, oxygen saturation in the 70s to 80s, blood pressure around 70/40, emergency transport, CTA evidence of extensive embolism, thrombolysis, and ICU observation. His medical knowledge shortened his own route to a plausible emergency diagnosis, but the transferable lesson is prompt emergency assessment for severe cardiopulmonary deterioration—not self-confirmation of the cause or self-directed thrombolysis.

## Key Claims
- Sudden unilateral leg swelling is more concerning for thrombosis than symmetric dependent swelling that improves after rest.
- Continuous swelling below a level, rather than patchy local swelling, is a more suspicious pattern.
- Pain, calf hardness, and increased muscle tension add concern, including in possible calf intermuscular venous thrombosis.
- Newer, larger, or more central thrombi carry more dangerous embolic implications; sudden severe breathlessness, hypoxemia, hypotension, or collapse in an immobility-risk context requires emergency assessment for possible pulmonary embolism rather than outpatient self-triage.
- Suspected high-risk thrombosis should be routed to emergency evaluation with reduced walking and reduced leg manipulation.
- Massage, forceful movement, or casual post-travel recovery behavior is acceptable only when thrombosis is not suspected.
- Leg swelling remains a differential-diagnosis problem because venous obstruction, reflux, varicose veins, lymphatic disease, and systemic causes can overlap thrombosis.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept is not a diagnostic rule. Bilateral swelling, pitting edema, posture-related swelling, venous reflux or obstruction, heart, kidney, liver, medication, lymphatic, injury, infection, and other causes may overlap; breathlessness and low oxygen also have multiple causes, so emergency or vascular clinicians must make the diagnosis. The seven-day and two-week descriptions of clot behavior and the survivor's measurements and treatment course are source-scoped, not safe waiting periods, treatment criteria, or transport rules.
