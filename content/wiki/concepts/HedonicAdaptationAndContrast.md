## Definition
Hedonic adaptation and contrast is the tendency for emotional responses to repeated or newly achieved conditions to weaken, together with the role comparison, interruption, imagined loss, and finitude can play in renewing appreciation.

## Current Synthesis
Achievement, possessions, improved circumstances, and severe changes often have less permanent emotional force than people forecast. This is not a claim that all gains and harms cancel or that everyone returns to one fixed baseline. Contrast can change appraisal: interruption, a different comparison point, or brief recognition that a valued condition is finite may make an existing good perceptible again.

The pattern applies to both pursuit and possession. [[BrianChesky]] describes [[Airbnb]]'s IPO and extraordinary recognition as an intense but short emotional high, after which ordinary work and isolation returned. The resulting reorientation did not reject ambition; it reduced the demand that valuation and applause provide durable wellbeing and shifted attention toward friendship, vulnerability, service, and enjoyment of creation. Adaptation can therefore expose neglected needs rather than merely invite renewed savoring.

Briefly imagining a valued person, activity, job, or possession absent, or recognizing that an ordinary activity will eventually happen for the last time, can interrupt familiarity and make the present good perceptible again. Adverse comparison may similarly change appraisal, but these practices are not guarantees and must not become demands to deny hardship simply because worse suffering exists.

## Key Claims
- Repeated positive and negative conditions can lose emotional intensity through adaptation.
- Anticipated arrival often overpromises durable happiness because the achieved state becomes ordinary.
- Comparison points influence appraisal and can make the same outcome feel different.
- Interruption, negative visualization, and awareness of finitude may restore attention to existing goods.
- Process orientation can retain meaning during pursuit instead of reserving value for one arrival state.
- When achievement fades into emptiness, the response may require relationships, purpose, or life change rather than another status goal.
- Adaptation varies and does not imply equal recovery from pleasure, trauma, disability, grief, or deprivation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The Santos note summarizes research without full methods, replication history, or effect sizes; the Chesky case is a personal retrospective; and Irvine's practices are philosophical guidance supported here by anecdotes rather than comparative trials. Return toward baseline is neither complete nor uniform. The familiar lottery-versus-paraplegia shorthand must not erase persistent pain, disability, trauma, inequality, or material need. Negative visualization, mortality reflection, and worse-case comparison can worsen anxiety, grief, shame, obsessive thought, or trauma responses and are not universally appropriate. Comparison must not invalidate hardship, while social and purpose changes may help some people without replacing clinical care or structural improvement.

## What Changed
- Added bounded negative visualization and last-time reflection as interruption practices for familiar goods.
- Added adverse comparison while clarifying that a worse reference point must not invalidate present hardship.
- Strengthened the anxiety, grief, trauma, and evidence boundaries around appreciation exercises.
