# Intracranial Circuit Mapping

## Definition
Intracranial circuit mapping is the source's translational method for using implanted electrodes, stereo-EEG, awake procedures, provoked symptoms, synchronized behavior, and machine learning to identify brain signals linked to psychiatric or craving states.

## Current Synthesis
[[essentials-compulsive-behaviors-deep-brain-stimulation-dr-casey-halpern-scim9347675622]] presents invasive recording as a bridge between severe human symptoms and future scalable treatment. [[CaseyHalpern]] describes how movement-disorder surgery can convert tremor-related cells into audible signals, then asks what the equivalent signal would be for craving, appetite, binge eating, or obsession. Awake procedures and early human trials become ways to establish where electrodes should go before a treatment can be made more precise.

The full [[biology-treatments-for-compulsive-behaviors-binge-eating-dr-casey-halpern-scim4911670131]] interview specifies the method: personally rated food cues, fasting where appropriate, single-unit activity, local field potentials, intraoperative CT, connectomics, mood provocation, synchronized video, and eye tracking. These measurements aim to distinguish anatomy from timing by identifying a pre-craving or pre-binge state that could trigger [[ResponsiveDeepBrainStimulation]].

The larger synthesis is that invasive discovery may inform TMS, ultrasound, wearables, warning systems, or physiological monitoring, but only after the disease-state signal is credibly identified. Millimeter-scale targeting sensitivity, small samples, provocation validity, and animal-to-human translation remain central limits.

## Key Claims
- Human intracranial recordings can reveal disease-state signals that animal models may miss.
- Stereo-EEG, already common in epilepsy, is discussed as a possible research path for OCD and depression.
- Awake procedures can help determine symptom-specific electrode targets in early trials.
- Laboratory provocation, synchronized video, eye tracking, and implanted-device data can characterize pre-binge or craving states.
- Machine learning may eventually anticipate highly impulsive states from brain or physiological signals.
- The method is a discovery bridge, not proof that wearables or non-invasive tools already solve severe psychiatric risk.
- Anatomical precision and state-timing precision are separate requirements that must both be validated.

## Evidence
- Tremor analogy - [[essentials-compulsive-behaviors-deep-brain-stimulation-dr-casey-halpern-scim9347675622]] compares audible tremor-cell activity to the search for craving-related cells.
- Stereo-EEG bridge - [[essentials-compulsive-behaviors-deep-brain-stimulation-dr-casey-halpern-scim9347675622]] describes invasive epilepsy recordings and interest in applying them to OCD or depression research.
- Lab-state measurement - [[essentials-compulsive-behaviors-deep-brain-stimulation-dr-casey-halpern-scim9347675622]] describes provoking cravings and recording synchronized behavior before binge episodes.
- Prediction direction - [[essentials-compulsive-behaviors-deep-brain-stimulation-dr-casey-halpern-scim9347675622]] says machine learning may anticipate impulsive states, while noting Halpern is not the engineer building those systems.
- Trial instrumentation - [[biology-treatments-for-compulsive-behaviors-binge-eating-dr-casey-halpern-scim4911670131]] adds single-unit recordings, local field potentials, intraoperative CT, connectomics, personally relevant cues, and brief responsive stimulation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not establish a validated biomarker for craving, OCD, suicidality, obesity, depression, or addiction. Intracranial methods are invasive and research-heavy; provoked laboratory states may not reproduce everyday episodes, and future wearable or non-invasive translation remains speculative.
