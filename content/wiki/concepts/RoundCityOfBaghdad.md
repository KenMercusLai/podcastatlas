# Round City of Baghdad

## Definition

The Round City of Baghdad was al-Mansur's circular, fortified capital plan, designed to concentrate palace, mosque, administration, military access, and routes of exchange around a visibly hierarchical center.

## Current Synthesis

The plan joined practical and symbolic goals. A site between the Tigris and Euphrates offered access to regional and long-distance trade, while walls, moat, four gates, radial avenues, and Khorasani settlement supported defense and control. At the center, the mosque and Palace of the Golden Gate placed religious and caliphal authority beside one another; restrictions on who could ride there sharpened the hierarchy.

The city also expressed cultural reorientation. Its enduring name was Persian, its designers were probably Persian, circular Sasanian precedents qualified later claims of uniqueness, and architectural references linked the complex both to Medina and older Persian royal forms. Yet the planned core was not the whole metropolis: bazaars moved to Karkh, migrants settled beyond the walls, and commercial growth created the larger [[Baghdad]] described in later episodes.

## Key Claims

- Site selection joined river access, trade routes, security, and proximity to the Abbasids' eastern support.
- Circular walls and radial gates organized movement around a controlled dynastic center.
- Palace and mosque expressed the conjunction of caliphal government and sacred legitimacy.
- Persian designers, names, precedents, and regional history shaped the capital's form.
- Khorasani settlement tied urban geography to the revolution's military constituency.
- Moving markets outside the core separated commercial crowds from the central enclosure.
- Rapid extramural growth shows how metropolitan life exceeded the founder's ideal geometry.

## Evidence

- **Site and construction:** [[377-baghdad-crossroads-of-the-universe-part-2-glt9127243683]] connects the Mesopotamian site to trade, eastern power, astrologically timed foundation, and rapid construction.
- **Hierarchy and defense:** [[377-baghdad-crossroads-of-the-universe-part-2-glt9127243683]] describes the moat, walls, gates, central palace and mosque, mounted-access restriction, and Khorasani land grants.
- **Cultural synthesis:** [[377-baghdad-crossroads-of-the-universe-part-2-glt9127243683]] links Persian designers and circular precedents to Medina, Persepolis, Ctesiphon, and other older urban traditions.
- **Growth beyond the plan:** [[377-baghdad-crossroads-of-the-universe-part-2-glt9127243683]] describes the relocation of bazaars to Karkh and immigration that carried Baghdad beyond its original core.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode is a synthetic popular-history account, and archaeological survival from the original city is limited. The exact foundation date, role of astrologers, identity of designers, architectural genealogy, gate details, Byzantine-emissary story, and intended symbolism require specialist corroboration. Formal geometry should not be mistaken for a complete account of how residents used or transformed the city.

## What Changed

- Established the Round City as a combined defensive, commercial, dynastic, and sacred plan.
- Qualified claims of uniqueness through Persian and Sasanian precedents.
- Distinguished the controlled circular core from the larger metropolis that grew around it.
