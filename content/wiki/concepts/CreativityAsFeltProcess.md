# Creativity as Felt Process

## Definition
Creativity as felt process is [[RickRubin]]'s frame for art-making as attention to excitement, curiosity, enthusiasm, interest, and other sensed responses before the work can be fully explained in language.

## Current Synthesis
[[essentials-how-to-access-your-creativity-rick-rubin-scim7816635332]] presents creative work as closer to dreams, clouds, fragments, and bodily response than to a preplanned intellectual system. The artist preserves and tests material because meaning may appear later. Rules, models, imitation, and skill can help, but they become dangerous when they replace direct felt judgment about what the work is doing.

## Key Claims
- Creative material can arrive before it is explainable, so preserving fragments matters.
- Felt response is a legitimate creative signal, not merely a post-hoc preference.
- Childhood creativity is linked to openness and fewer inherited assumptions.
- Skill and imitation can build capacity, but mature creativity often requires loosening those same rules.
- Commercial prediction is weaker than the creator's owned response when deciding what belongs in the work.

## Evidence
- Felt signal - [[essentials-how-to-access-your-creativity-rick-rubin-scim7816635332]] records Rubin saying artists look for excitement, enthusiasm, curiosity, or interest and that creativity is not primarily intellectual.
- Dreams and fragments - [[essentials-how-to-access-your-creativity-rick-rubin-scim7816635332]] compares the art-making process to dreams whose pieces may not make sense until they are written down or preserved.
- Openness and rules - [[essentials-how-to-access-your-creativity-rick-rubin-scim7816635332]] says children are more creative because they are open and have fewer assumptions about how things are supposed to work.
- Taste ownership - [[essentials-how-to-access-your-creativity-rick-rubin-scim7816635332]] says a key artistic skill is knowing how you feel and owning that feeling without letting commercial ideas undermine it.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not reject craft, skill, or form. It treats rules as useful scaffolding when they build capacity, while warning that they can later block direct creative response. The episode also does not present a measurable neuroscience model of creativity.
