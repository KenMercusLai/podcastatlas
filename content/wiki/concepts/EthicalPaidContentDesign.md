# Ethical Paid-Content Design / 内容付费伦理设计

## Definition
Ethical paid-content design is a creator-payment principle that charges for genuine additional value without manufacturing anxiety, withholding essential public-interest context, or breaking a complete account solely to force purchase.

## Current Synthesis
The source separates the legitimacy of charging from the method used to secure payment. Creators should not treat compensation as shameful, especially when professional research and production support a team. But a valid price does not justify every conversion tactic. In public-event storytelling, the free layer should remain coherent enough that a listener is not misled by a strategically missing fact; paid material can then add detail, interpretation, craft, or depth.

This is not a rule that all information must be free or that paid episodes cannot use suspense. It is a proportionality boundary: the stronger the public-interest and factual stakes, the weaker the justification for exploiting an artificial information deficit. Sustainable payment also requires resisting the opposite failure, where creator unworthiness turns every paid release into an unsustainable multiplication of labor.

## Key Claims
- Charging for researched creator work is legitimate and can be necessary for quality and team continuity.
- A public-interest free account should not be made materially incomplete merely to create a purchase trigger.
- Paid content can ethically offer additional detail, structure, analysis, performance, or convenience.
- Anxiety, artificial information gaps, and sunk-cost pressure are ethically distinct from clear value exchange.
- Payment unworthiness can harm sustainability by making the creator promise far more labor than the price or team can support.
- Format and stakes matter; entertainment cliffhangers and consequential factual reporting do not carry identical disclosure duties.

## Evidence
- Complete-free-layer boundary - [[vol-56-duitan-mizi-rensheng-shi-renling-ziji-yu-queli-shiming-de-guocheng-lgrbpfubc8s6y59jb6tsd6glz-c]] says [[MiziCrimePodcaster|咪仔]] kept the Epstein series' free public-event account complete and placed additional richness behind payment.
- Anti-manipulation boundary - [[vol-56-duitan-mizi-rensheng-shi-renling-ziji-yu-queli-shiming-de-guocheng-lgrbpfubc8s6y59jb6tsd6glz-c]] rejects using anxiety, suspense, or information asymmetry merely to control the user into paying.
- Compensation legitimacy - [[vol-56-duitan-mizi-rensheng-shi-renling-ziji-yu-queli-shiming-de-guocheng-lgrbpfubc8s6y59jb6tsd6glz-c]] has both speakers argue that strong work may charge directly even when the Chinese content market has weak payment habits.
- Overproduction risk - [[vol-56-duitan-mizi-rensheng-shi-renling-ziji-yu-queli-shiming-de-guocheng-lgrbpfubc8s6y59jb6tsd6glz-c]] links charging-related unworthiness to escalating time and effort that can undermine the business.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This concept is grounded in one creator conversation rather than consumer research or a complete media-ethics framework. The source does not define a universal minimum free layer, prove that the featured series was complete, or settle pricing, refund, subscription, advertising, or intellectual-property policy. Some paid journalism can ethically place the entire work behind a paywall when access terms are clear and no deceptive partial account is presented as complete.

## What Changed
- Created a boundary between legitimate creator compensation and manipulative conversion design.
- Added creator overproduction and unworthiness as sustainability risks on the opposite side of underpayment.
