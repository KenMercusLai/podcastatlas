## Definition

The Argentine Dirty War is the episode's name for the military dictatorship's campaign of state terror after the March 1976 coup, situated at the end of an earlier escalation involving guerrilla violence, right-wing death squads, economic crisis, and institutional breakdown.

## Current Synthesis

The episode does not present the 1976 dictatorship as emerging from nowhere. It traces a sequence from Peronist proscription and radicalization through the movement's violent left-right split, the AAA under the political environment associated with [[JoseLopezRega|José López Rega]], instability during [[IsabelPeron|Isabel Perón]]'s presidency, and the military takeover. The dictatorship's killing remains state terror; the prior violence is context, not exoneration.

## Key Claims

- The Dirty War followed a prolonged escalation of political violence and institutional instability.
- Peronism's armed factional split preceded the military dictatorship.
- Right-wing death-squad violence blurred party, household, and state power before the coup.
- The 1976 coup removed Isabel Perón and opened a more systematic regime of state terror.
- Numerical estimates and causal compression require source caution.

## Evidence

- Pre-coup escalation: [[498-evita-the-mystery-of-the-missing-body-part-5-glt5587823308]] connects the Montoneros, Ezeiza violence, the AAA, inflation, and Isabel Perón's weakened government.
- Regime change: [[498-evita-the-mystery-of-the-missing-body-part-5-glt5587823308]] dates Isabel's overthrow to March 1976 and describes her subsequent detention.
- State terror: [[498-evita-the-mystery-of-the-missing-body-part-5-glt5587823308]] says the military dictatorship killed between 20,000 and 30,000 people.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This page reflects a brief closing segment rather than a comprehensive history of the dictatorship, disappearances, resistance, international involvement, or transitional justice. The casualty range is retained as the hosts' figure, not independently adjudicated here. Prior insurgent or factional violence does not make state terror inevitable or morally equivalent.
