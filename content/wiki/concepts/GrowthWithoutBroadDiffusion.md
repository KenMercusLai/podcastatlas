# Growth Without Broad Diffusion / 增长收益扩散失灵

## Definition
Growth without broad diffusion describes expansion in output, exports, profit, or capital investment whose employment, service demand, local spending, and household-income effects reach a much narrower population than the headline performance suggests.

## Current Synthesis
The episode contrasts older labor-intensive export and property cycles with technology-intensive exports and AI infrastructure. Shoes, garments, housing, and local projects could recruit large workforces and circulate money through sales, services, advertising, materials, and nearby consumption. Advanced manufacturing, automated export operations, compute, and data centers can generate valuable output with fewer workers and weaker ties to local commercial networks.

This does not make high-productivity growth false or harmful. It means firm efficiency, export value, aggregate output, employment breadth, labor income, and lived prosperity are different variables. A K-shaped or disconnected recovery becomes plausible when the expanding branch has few transmission channels to households facing weak assets, insecure work, or falling bargaining power.

## Key Claims
- Equal increments of export value can have very different employment and local-spending multipliers.
- Automation, translation tools, organizational efficiency, and capital intensity can reduce the sales and service labor attached to growth.
- Compute and technical investment may circulate through global suppliers and specialized labor more than through local consumer networks.
- Aggregate success can coexist with weak household sentiment when jobs, wages, and asset values do not share the same trajectory.
- Diffusion should be evaluated through transmission channels rather than inferred from output or profit alone.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode does not provide a complete input-output study, audited employment comparison, or causal estimate. Advanced exports can support supplier employment, tax revenue, cheaper products, productivity gains, and future industries; property-led circulation can also create leverage, misallocation, and instability. The concept identifies a transmission risk and a measurement distinction, not a claim that labor-intensive or property growth is always superior.
