## Definition

性格中介的机缘 / character-mediated fortune is the literary mechanism in which chance supplies an encounter but relatively stable dispositions determine the action taken within it, converting contingency into a consequence that cannot be described as either pure luck or fully earned reward.

## Current Synthesis

The bounded [[Xuzhu|虚竹]] discussion repeats one structure across the 珍珑棋局, rescue of [[TianshanTonglao|天山童姥]], and Western Xia bride selection. Xuzhu does not design any of these opportunities. He is brought to the chess gathering by others, mistakes Tonglao for an endangered child, and travels to Western Xia for his sworn brothers rather than to seek marriage. The outcomes are therefore contingent.

His responses are nevertheless patterned. He acts to relieve immediate suffering, does not calculate status returns, and answers truthfully when a competitive answer would be more obvious. Those actions open paths to the Xiaoyao inheritance, Lingjiu Palace, and reunion with 梦姑. Character mediates fortune because the same chance situation would not have the same result for every participant.

The concept does not turn benevolence into a cosmic reward system. Many of Xuzhu's gains are unwanted, carry loss, or depend on other people's coercion. It instead separates three layers: arrival of the situation, the character-shaped response, and consequences whose scale and value exceed the actor's control.

## Key Claims

- Chance explains why an opportunity appears, not why a person responds in one way rather than another.
- Repeated conduct across unrelated situations provides stronger character evidence than a single lucky result.
- Compassion can initiate action without a strategic plan or knowledge of the beneficiary's power.
- Honesty can change a competitive situation when the actor refuses to optimize for the expected prize.
- A beneficial external result can still involve unwanted identity loss, coercion, grief, or burdensome responsibility.
- Character-mediated fortune is probabilistic and narrative, not a guarantee that virtue receives reward.

## Evidence

### Compassion inside accidental situations

- [[065-tianlongbabu-zhi-xuzhu-pian-hongyan-tanzhi-lao-chana-fanghua-lvs6-y-_lurbgf_r1he5q11b-z]] links Xuzhu's attempt to save 段延庆 to his first chess move and his attempt to save an unknown child to his relationship with Tonglao.

### Truthfulness without prize-seeking

- [[065-tianlongbabu-zhi-xuzhu-pian-hongyan-tanzhi-lao-chana-fanghua-lvs6-y-_lurbgf_r1he5q11b-z]] contrasts the strategic answers at the Western Xia selection with Xuzhu's literal account of his happiest place and unseen beloved.

### Loss inside apparent fortune

- [[065-tianlongbabu-zhi-xuzhu-pian-hongyan-tanzhi-lao-chana-fanghua-lvs6-y-_lurbgf_r1he5q11b-z]] shows that power, office, and marriage arrive alongside loss of Shaolin identity, broken vows, parental death, and duties Xuzhu did not choose.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept is derived from one literary interpretation, not empirical evidence that personality determines life outcomes or that goodness attracts reward. Xuzhu receives unusually large benefits because the plot concentrates rare teachers, inheritances, and recognition devices around him. 段延庆's chess guidance, Tonglao's power and coercion, 梦姑's search, edition changes, and coincidence remain independent causal inputs. The concept should therefore preserve luck, structure, other agents, and the actor's own valuation of the result.

## What Changed

- Created the concept to distinguish contingent opportunity, patterned response, and uncontrolled consequence.
- Added unwanted gain as a limit on judging fortune only by status, wealth, martial power, or marriage.
- Excluded providential and deterministic readings of virtue and reward.
