# Exercise Assistive Gear Boundary / 运动护具辅助边界

## Definition
Exercise assistive gear boundary / 运动护具辅助边界 is the distinction between using braces, supports, kinesiology tape, or rehabilitation devices as temporary aids and relying on them as substitutes for muscle strength, technique, recovery, or professional assessment.

## Current Synthesis
[[vol-212-pingshi-jiuzuo-zhoumo-wanming-yundong-yundong-yixueke-yisheng-gei-dagongrende-fang-lian-fei-zhinan-lqtwr4m-9npye9giib1-om-tylmy]] develops this boundary around knee braces, wrist guards, lumbar supports, kinesiology tape, and exoskeleton devices. The episode accepts that external supports can add stability during activity that exceeds a person's normal load, but it warns that routine overuse can reduce the body's own muscular contribution.

The source treats expertise as part of the boundary. Kinesiology tape is not just colorful adhesive; it depends on anatomy, muscle direction, damaged tissue, and joint tracking. Exoskeleton equipment is framed mainly as rehabilitation support for postoperative or function-impaired patients, not a universal prevention device for healthy exercisers.

## Key Claims
- Braces and supports can provide stability, especially during temporary high-intensity or above-usual activity.
- Routine reliance on supports can reduce the need for one's own muscles to work, weakening the very capacity that protects joints.
- Ordinary people who can move normally and face no special risk are not advised to default to braces before training strength and control.
- Kinesiology tape requires professional anatomical and injury-specific knowledge; poor placement can alter joint tracking and aggravate problems.
- Exoskeleton devices are mainly discussed as disease- or rehabilitation-specific tools rather than general consumer prevention equipment.
- External support should be time-limited and context-specific, with removal and strength rebuilding after the risky activity or rehabilitation need passes.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept is not anti-gear. The source allows supports when load, instability, competition, rehabilitation, or medical need makes them appropriate. The warning is against vague overprevention and unskilled use, especially when pain, injury, or disease should be evaluated rather than covered up.
