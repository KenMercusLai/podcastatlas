## Definition

Bandit mutual-reporting pacification / 群盗互纠式平乱 is the Guangwu-era anti-bandit pattern where the state uses pardons, peer reporting, official-incentive repair, sheltering penalties, and resettlement to break mobile bandit groups without relying only on pursuit by troops.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 718 presents the pattern after repeated failed suppression. Bandits in Qingzhou, Xuzhou, Youzhou, Yizhou, and other commanderies disperse when troops arrive and reassemble after troops leave, making ordinary campaign pursuit ineffective. [[LiuXiu|刘秀]]'s new policy changes the incentive structure around the bandit field.

The policy has three linked parts. First, bandits are allowed to inform on each other, and groups of five who jointly kill one bandit can be pardoned and sent home. Second, local officials are not punished simply for delay, fear, or lax pursuit; their performance is judged by captured numbers, while people who shelter bandits are punished. Third, bandit leaders who surrender or are captured are moved to other commanderies and given land and grain for their families.

The current synthesis is that the policy works by making bandit cohesion expensive and return to ordinary life plausible. It also removes a perverse bureaucratic pressure: if local officials fear punishment for every delay, they may falsify, avoid action, or worsen conflict. The result, in the source's summary, is rapid dissolution of bandit groups and enough restored order that cattle and horses no longer need night enclosure and cities no longer need guards.

## Key Claims

- Mobile bandit groups can defeat ordinary pursuit by dispersing and reassembling.
- Peer-reporting incentives attack bandit cohesion from inside the group.
- Pardon for collective anti-bandit action turns marginal participants into enforcement resources.
- Official accountability has to reward concrete capture rather than punish every delay or fear response.
- Punishing sheltering bandits targets the civilian support channel that keeps groups alive.
- Resettling leaders with land and grain makes post-suppression control and livelihood part of the same policy.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source presents the policy as successful, but it does not provide bandit names, local casualty figures, coercion details, or evidence from the resettled families. The concept also should not be treated as pure leniency: it includes killing bandits, punishing shelterers, moving leaders away from their original bases, and making official performance measurable.

## What Changed

- Created a concept for Liu Xiu's 40 CE anti-bandit incentive design.
- Distinguished it from ordinary military suppression by emphasizing peer reporting, official accountability repair, and resettlement.
