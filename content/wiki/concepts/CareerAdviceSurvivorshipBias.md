# Career Advice Survivorship Bias

Career advice survivorship bias is the risk that unusually successful people convert their own rare path into general guidance. [[brave-new-whirl-turkeys-opposition-overhaul-6a69c7766043035f89eb097f]] applies the critique to [[BillGurley]]'s *Running Down a Dream* and [[JodiKantor]]'s *How to Start*.

The source does not dismiss all advice from successful people. It says the books contain useful points, but their examples can overstate the value of passion, dream pursuit, and atypical success stories for ordinary graduates facing [[AIGraduateCareerUncertainty]].

## Key Claims
- Successful outcomes can hide the many similar attempts that did not work.
- Advice based on a rare career path may understate timing, talent, social capital, and luck.
- Passion-centered guidance can be especially vulnerable when the passion maps poorly onto available jobs.
- Better advice starts from skills, labor-market fit, usefulness, and patient exploration.
