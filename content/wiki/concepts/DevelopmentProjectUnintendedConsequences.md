# Development Project Unintended Consequences / 发展项目的意外后果

Development project unintended consequences is the source's pattern for well-meaning interventions that solve one visible problem while creating new pressure elsewhere. In [[35-tianzhende-renleixuejia-zuo-tianye-hui-shangyin-de-603418624]], the clearest cases are a German expert's cotton-promotion success that reduces food planting, a vegetable-garden campaign whose apparent market success may come from [[NigelBarley|Nigel Barley]]'s own hires, and a water project that could improve health while moving water away from [[DowayoPeople|Dowayo]] territory and weakening rain-ritual authority.

The concept does not reject development. It asks who gains, who pays, which local meanings are disrupted, and whether technical improvement is being mistaken for full social understanding.

## Key Claims
- A project can meet its technical target and still damage food security, trust, resource control, or local legitimacy.
- Development experts and anthropologists can each see harms the other misses.
- Health benefits, such as cleaner water, still need accounting for ownership, belief, and extraction effects.
- Comic feedback loops are evidence: when a policy seems to work because of the observer's own intervention, evaluation has already been contaminated.
