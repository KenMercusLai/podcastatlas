# Military AI Oversight Compression

## Definition
Military AI Oversight Compression is the degradation of meaningful human review when battlefield speed, volume, automation, and institutional pressure reduce humans from independent decision-makers to rapid approvers of AI recommendations.

## Current Synthesis
The bounded sources identify two forms of compression. [[an-interview-with-yuval-noah-harari-6a872b94c71d02aaba56ca7c]] describes operational compression, where battlefield tempo reduces humans to rapid approval of machine recommendations. [[ep-35-who-actually-controls-ai-the-governance-gap-explained]] adds upstream institutional compression, where private contract negotiations can settle military-AI boundaries before legislators, publics, or independent experts deliberate. Meaningful human control therefore depends on both decision-time review and legitimate predeployment authorization.

## Key Claims
- A human signature is not the same as meaningful human judgment.
- AI targeting and battlefield recommendation systems can create speed pressure that collapses review time.
- Oversight compression can occur even when formal policy says humans remain involved.
- Wartime institutions may drift from independent human review toward approval of machine-ranked options.
- The concept applies to targeting, escalation, procurement, and other military decisions shaped by AI recommendations.
- Oversight can be compressed before deployment when procurement channels substitute for public and legislative authorization.

## Evidence
- Human-in/on-the-loop distinction: [[an-interview-with-yuval-noah-harari-6a872b94c71d02aaba56ca7c]] records Harari contrasting meaningful human involvement with weaker monitoring or approval.
- Speed pressure: [[an-interview-with-yuval-noah-harari-6a872b94c71d02aaba56ca7c]] links AI battlefield use to the risk that humans rapidly approve machine recommendations.
- Procedural control problem: [[an-interview-with-yuval-noah-harari-6a872b94c71d02aaba56ca7c]] warns that formal oversight can become hollow if the human cannot realistically understand or challenge the system.
- Governance relevance: [[an-interview-with-yuval-noah-harari-6a872b94c71d02aaba56ca7c]] treats military AI as one of the concrete policy domains democracies must address.
- Upstream authorization: [[ep-35-who-actually-controls-ai-the-governance-gap-explained]] argues that autonomous-weapons boundaries should not be settled through a private contract negotiation among company executives and defense officials.
- Missing scrutiny: [[ep-35-who-actually-controls-ai-the-governance-gap-explained]] identifies public deliberation, congressional debate, and independent technical review as necessary checks in high-stakes military AI.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not claim every military AI tool eliminates human judgment. Some systems may improve review or reduce civilian harm if designed and governed well, and confidential procurement can be legitimate where security requires it. The episode summary also does not provide the underlying defense contracts or deliberation record. The concept identifies the failure mode where battlefield tempo, machine authority, or closed procurement make human and democratic review nominal.

## What Changed
- Expanded the concept from battlefield approval into predeployment authorization and procurement.
- Added public, legislative, and independent technical scrutiny as distinct oversight layers.
