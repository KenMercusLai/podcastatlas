## Definition

贞节荣誉的性别化强制 / Gendered Chastity Honor Coercion is the conversion of women's widowhood, bodily suffering, self-denial, or death into publicly praised evidence of sexual and moral fidelity, so that an honor category can normalize extreme personal cost.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-347-1-gudai-shi-ruhe-yong-lijiao-pua-zhenjie-lienv-de-lt39fogfivnipellq6y2xl6ctrzq|Hanji 347-1]] introduces the concept through the host's move from Zhao Yu's extreme consistency to 贞妇 and 烈女. The episode lists suicide after a husband's death, bodily binding or injury, disfigurement, and lifelong widowhood, then contrasts those living costs with the “dead” reward of a chastity arch.

The durable claim is narrower than the title's full promise: exemplary honor can make suffering appear morally desirable and can place a distinctly gendered burden on women. The supplied transcript does not yet show who enforced the norm, how common each practice was, whether particular women acted voluntarily, or how the mechanism varied across periods and communities.

## Key Claims

- Public moral honor can obscure the bodily and lifetime costs borne by the honored person.
- The episode's chastity examples impose a specifically gendered standard of fidelity and self-denial.
- Extreme consistency becomes dangerous when suffering itself is treated as proof of virtue.
- A symbolic reward such as a chastity arch does not compensate for death, injury, disfigurement, or a life constrained by the norm.
- Mechanism, prevalence, and individual agency must be demonstrated rather than inferred from the moral label alone.
