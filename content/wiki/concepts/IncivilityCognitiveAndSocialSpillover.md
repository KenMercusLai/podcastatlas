# Incivility Cognitive and Social Spillover

## Definition
Incivility cognitive and social spillover is the process by which a disrespectful encounter captures attention and degrades performance beyond the original interaction, affecting targets, witnesses, teams, customers, patients, and later contacts.

## Current Synthesis
The mechanism begins with emotional and threat-related capture: people replay the slight, focus on self-protection, and lose capacity for the task in front of them. The episode reports slower problem-solving and physical response, more memory and calculation errors, missed visual information, narrower creativity, and unusually aggressive ideas after brief rude exposure.

The same disruption can alter a social system. Reduced trust makes people less willing to contribute ideas, report errors, share information, or request help. Witnesses may then carry the negative state into unrelated interactions, so an incident can cross team, customer, patient, and household boundaries without deliberate imitation.

## Key Claims
- Incivility consumes limited attention through threat arousal, self-focus, and rumination.
- Targets and witnesses can show performance impairment even when they do not recognize the decline.
- Creativity can narrow toward less flexible and more aggressive responses after rude exposure.
- Lower trust reduces speaking up, error detection, information sharing, and help-seeking.
- Social transmission can carry the effects beyond the initial target and setting.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The supplied episode does not provide the underlying studies, samples, replications, or boundary conditions, so exact effect sizes and generalizability remain source-scoped. Perceived disrespect varies across cultures, relationships, media, and power contexts; direct criticism or urgent correction is not automatically incivility. The framework identifies a plausible transmission chain but does not establish that every later error, withdrawal, or rude act was caused by the original encounter.

## What Changed
- Established a combined account of cognitive capture, witness impairment, trust loss, and onward social transmission.
