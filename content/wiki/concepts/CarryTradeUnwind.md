# Carry Trade Unwind

## Definition

A carry trade unwind is the closing of positions funded or justified by an interest-rate differential when currency movement, funding cost, collateral pressure, or falling target assets overwhelms the expected carry.

## Current Synthesis

EP38 presents the global market-structure version. When the yen strengthens and Japanese rates rise, yen-funded positions become less profitable or loss-making, prompting traders to sell risk assets and buy yen for repayment. Shared funding can make otherwise unrelated assets fall together.

EP95 adds a retail AUD/JPY and 2008 crisis example. A high-yield currency can fall far more than the interest collected, and collective repurchase of yen can reinforce yen appreciation. The unwind is therefore not merely a changed opinion; leverage, margin, stops, and repayment can make it mechanical.

## Key Claims

- Interest differential is conditional return, not protection against exchange-rate loss.
- Closing a funded position can require selling the target asset and buying back the funding currency.
- Shared funding can correlate asset sales across markets.
- Margin, stop rules, and funding-cost changes can make an unwind mechanical.
- Funding-currency appreciation can reinforce itself as more positions close.
- Derivatives and crowded leverage can turn repricing into [[DerivativeAmplifiedVolatility]].

### Global yen-funded unwind

### Retail AUD/JPY crisis example
- [[ep95-fx-zhanshi-jiuliumei-mama-kuidiao-de-qian-wo-yao-qinshou-zhuan-huilai-lgdurd85j7_cbimza4cegor-jfly|EP95]] says 2008 selling of Australian dollars and repurchase of yen overwhelmed carry income and intensified currency movement.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The sources do not quantify what share of either market move was caused by carry unwinding rather than growth expectations, valuation, liquidity, or other positioning. Not every yen appreciation is a carry unwind, and not every carry position is leveraged or forced to close. The AUD/JPY history is episode-attributed and not independently reconstructed here.

## What Changed

- Added the retail AUD/JPY case and made exchange-rate loss versus interest carry explicit.
- Extended the mechanism from global asset sales to account-level margin and repayment pressure.
