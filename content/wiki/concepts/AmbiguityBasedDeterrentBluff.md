## Definition

疑兵式心理威慑 / ambiguity-based deterrent bluff is a battlefield tactic in which a visibly weak force behaves in a way that is hard to reconcile with vulnerability, inducing the opponent to infer hidden support, an ambush, or another unobserved danger.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-243-bairen-xiatui-sanqian-dijun-liguang-ruhe-zuodao-de-lp6a-9rzznikouo5xbknz8ywaz1r|Hanji 243]] supplies a compact case through [[LiGuangHanGeneral|李广]]. With roughly one hundred riders facing several thousand [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] cavalry and the Han camp too distant for safe retreat, Li Guang treats flight as a confirming signal of weakness. He instead keeps the group in place, advances closer, orders everyone to dismount, and sustains the pose until night. The enemy reportedly withdraws because such calm exposure makes the small force look like bait for a larger concealed formation.

The mechanism is not generic confidence. It requires a plausible hidden threat, control over the weak side's visible behavior, and an opponent unwilling to pay the cost of testing the ambiguity. The episode reports success but does not establish how often this tactic failed elsewhere or whether the enemy withdrew solely because of the inferred ambush.

## Key Claims

- Counterintuitive calm can make a weak force look connected to an unseen stronger force when ordinary self-preservation would predict flight.
- The bluff works by raising uncertainty and expected attack cost, not by changing the force's actual combat capacity.
- Discipline and commander credibility are prerequisites because panic or premature retreat would collapse the signal.
- Success is contingent on terrain, timing, enemy risk tolerance, and the plausibility of nearby support.

## Evidence

Signal reversal and inferred ambush:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-243-bairen-xiatui-sanqian-dijun-liguang-ruhe-zuodao-de-lp6a-9rzznikouo5xbknz8ywaz1r|Hanji 243]] says Li Guang rejects flight, orders his small force to dismount near the opposing cavalry, and causes the enemy to suspect concealed Han support.

Discipline and time:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-243-bairen-xiatui-sanqian-dijun-liguang-ruhe-zuodao-de-lp6a-9rzznikouo5xbknz8ywaz1r|Hanji 243]] says the pose is maintained until night, when the Xiongnu force withdraws without testing it through a full attack.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept currently rests on one popular-history source and one successful anecdote. Force totals, distance from camp, sequence, exact orders, enemy deliberation, and withdrawal cause remain episode-attributed. A force that advances or dismounts without a plausible ambush context could instead make itself easier to destroy; the tactic is therefore a high-variance response to a constrained situation, not a general substitute for reconnaissance, reserves, or physical strength.

## What Changed

- Created a bounded concept separating Li Guang's inferred-ambush signal from generic courage or ordinary battlefield deception.
