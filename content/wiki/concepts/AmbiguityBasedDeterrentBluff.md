## Definition

疑兵式心理威慑 / ambiguity-based deterrent bluff is a battlefield tactic in which a visibly weak force behaves in a way that is hard to reconcile with vulnerability, inducing the opponent to infer hidden support, an ambush, or another unobserved danger.

## Current Synthesis

The current evidence supplies two variations on the same mechanism. In Hanji 243, [[LiGuangHanGeneral|李广]] keeps roughly one hundred riders exposed near a much larger [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] force, advances, dismounts, and sustains the pose until night. Because flight would be the expected response of an isolated weak force, calm exposure makes the group look like bait for concealed support.

In Hanji 1164, [[ZhaoYun|赵云]] returns to camp ahead of pursuing Cao-side troops and leaves the gate open rather than visibly sealing the position. The anomalous lack of defense reportedly makes [[CaoCao|曹操]], already alert to deception, suspect an ambush and order withdrawal; arrows and pursuit then turn the retreat into disorder.

Across both cases, the bluff changes the opponent's estimate of hidden force without changing actual combat capacity. Its effect depends on disciplined visible behavior, a plausible unseen threat, the opponent's prior beliefs and risk tolerance, and the ability to exploit hesitation or withdrawal.

## Key Claims

- Counterintuitive calm or exposure can make a weak force appear connected to an unseen stronger force.
- The bluff raises uncertainty and expected attack cost without changing actual force size.
- Opponent priors matter: existing suspicion can make an ambiguous posture more persuasive.
- Discipline and commander credibility are prerequisites because panic or premature closure collapses the signal.
- A successful deterrent bluff may create only hesitation unless the weak side can also exploit withdrawal.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Both cases come from structured popular-history summaries of successful anecdotes. Force totals, locations, exact orders, enemy deliberation, withdrawal causes, casualties, and dialogue remain episode-attributed. A force that exposes itself without a plausible ambush context can become easier to destroy. The tactic is therefore a high-variance response to a constrained situation, not a general substitute for reconnaissance, reserves, physical defense, or withdrawal planning. The second case also repeats material already summarized by Hanji 1147, so it adds corroborating episode provenance and a clearer opponent-prior interpretation rather than independent underlying evidence.

## What Changed

- Added Zhao Yun's open-camp maneuver as a second form of anomalous exposure.
- Added opponent prior belief and post-withdrawal exploitation to the mechanism.
- Qualified the second episode as repeated rather than fully independent provenance.
