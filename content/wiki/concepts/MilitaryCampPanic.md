# Military Camp Panic / 军营夜惊群体恐慌

Military camp panic / 军营夜惊群体恐慌 enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-965-bangguo-bieren-de-mang-weishenme-yao-shao-ti-ltjd-cv59hnm-n8ar9jxdogp9q0q|Hanji 965]]. It names the episode's explanation of ancient `夜惊`: soldiers under prolonged stress, poor sleep, rumor, superstition, crowding, toxins, and contagious fear may wake suddenly, seize weapons, and spread panic across the camp.

The source case is [[NiuFuLateHan|牛辅]]. Even after defeating [[LiSuLateHan|李肃]], Niu Fu cannot stabilize his army or himself; a night-fright episode leads him to flee and be killed by close followers. The lesson is that battlefield victory does not guarantee command stability when the camp's psychological environment is already broken.

The episode also uses cross-historical examples, including Roman, Song, Han-slip, Qi Jiguang, and Qing cases, to treat camp panic as a management problem rather than a supernatural one. Those examples remain source-scoped, but the operating pattern is clear: military order depends on morale, space, ritual, guard rhythm, sleep, and command response as much as on battle outcomes.

## Key Claims

- Camp panic is a collective stability problem, not just an individual fear episode.
- Victory can coexist with severe insecurity when soldiers and commanders believe political death is still likely.
- Rumor, cramped conditions, exhaustion, illness, poisoning, and superstition can amplify each other.
- Camp design and command routines can reduce or accelerate fear transmission.
- A commander's panic can be more dangerous than a soldier's panic because it signals that the force has lost its center.
