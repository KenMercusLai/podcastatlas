# Flavor As Self-Knowledge

Flavor as self-knowledge is the source's claim that tasting food and drink can help people discover what they actually like, need, remember, and are ready to change. In [[sanlian-chuantai-shen-ci-gei-ren-putao-shijie-weixun-le-585125156]], [[Heimai|黑麦]] rejects the idea that people have to deserve good [[Wine|wine]] and argues that trying wines, foods, fruits, tea, or [[Coffee|coffee]] is ultimately a search for one's own preferred flavor.

The concept is sharpened through [[SidewaysFilm|《杯酒人生》]]. The episode reads the film's wine fixation as self-projection: liking Pinot Noir, disliking Merlot, and finally opening a long-stored bottle become ways to speak about sensitivity, resentment, release, and self-reconciliation. In ordinary life, the same logic appears when someone leaves a formal meal wanting noodles, McDonald's, staple food, or another familiar taste.

## Key Claims
- Sensory preference is not trivial; it can reveal habit, comfort, memory, identity, and change.
- Expertise should make preference more articulate, not turn food and drink into a deservingness test.
- Personal descriptions of taste can be odd but meaningful because they draw on local memory and individual comparison.
- The concept complements [[TasteTraining]]: training gives more vocabulary, while self-knowledge decides what matters to the taster.
