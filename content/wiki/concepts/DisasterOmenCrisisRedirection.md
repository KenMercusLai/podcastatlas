## Definition

Disaster Omen Crisis Redirection / 灾异压力转移 is a court-governance pattern where disaster interpreted as Heaven's warning against the ruler is redirected into a narrower administrative fault, symbolic correction, personnel punishment, or scapegoat sacrifice that preserves central legitimacy while acknowledging the omen frame.

## Current Synthesis

The concept now has constructive administrative, negative symbolic, and fatal scapegoat forms. In the constructive form, ministers use disaster rhetoric against Empress Dowager Deng's regency and imply that Heaven is warning the court to return power to the young emperor. Deng does not answer by denying the omen system. Instead, she reviews prisoners in Luoyang and palace prisons, identifies a wronged prisoner, arrests the Luoyang magistrate, and lets the subsequent rain validate the administrative correction.

The symbolic form appears through [[WangMang|王莽]]. After violent wind damages Wanglu Hall, Wang Mang accepts the disaster frame but redirects the crisis toward crown-prince naming: [[WangLingXinPrince|王陵]]'s apparent heir status is declared improper, and [[WangAnXinPrince|王安]] is restored as Xin Qian Wang. Unlike Deng's prison-review case, the redirection does not repair flood, famine, construction, or rebellion stress; it converts natural and social disorder into title correction.

The fatal scapegoat form appears through [[HanChengdi|汉成帝]] and [[ZhaiFangjinWesternHan|翟方进]]. After 荧惑守心 is read as a direct warning to the emperor, Chengdi lets the responsibility move toward the chancellor. Zhai's harsh enforcement record and many enemies make the transfer easy to legitimate, but the outcome is not administrative repair: Chengdi's document and gifts force Zhai's suicide, and posthumous honors cover the coercion.

The concept is a political maneuver that may or may not have administrative content. It can produce real redress for a wronged person and discipline for a local official, but it can also shift blame into symbolic succession, title, ritual correction, or a person's death. The key move is not skepticism toward omens; it is accepting disaster language while changing its object from existential legitimacy failure into a narrower fixable-looking problem or removable target.

## Key Claims

- Disaster omens can function as coded political pressure against vulnerable rulers or regents.
- A ruler can defuse the pressure by accepting the omen language while changing its object from central legitimacy to local administrative harm.
- The redirected object can be substantive, as in legal review, or symbolic, as in succession-name correction.
- In its most dangerous form, redirection can become scapegoat sacrifice rather than correction.
- Visible investigation and punishment are central because they give the redirection public proof.
- The maneuver can coexist with real relief or legal correction; it is not automatically empty theater.
- Redirection is especially useful under regency because calls for moral correction can also be calls for transfer of power.

- Legitimacy repair: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-781-yushan-cai-shi-ren-dui-ji-zui-da-de-e-luvaw93hnnxcbgfnwgsjb7x0hlqz|Hanji 781]] says rain follows and Deng frames abnormal sky/weather as Heaven warning that officials are harming the people.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept now has one constructive regency case, one negative late-Xin succession case, and one fatal Western Han scapegoat case, so it should remain bounded rather than treated as a universal doctrine. Hanji 781 gives a strong interpretive reading of Deng's political skill; it does not independently prove that the prisoner review caused rain, that all ministers accepted the redirection sincerely, or that local-official punishment solved the broader disaster field. Hanji 657 shows Wang Mang's redirection, but the source does not prove his inner belief or that title correction had any practical effect on the named disasters. Hanji 559 shows the political logic of Zhai Fangjin's coerced death, but it does not settle the full justice of every accusation against him.

- Added Hanji 559 as a fatal scapegoat variant where Chengdi turns a celestial omen from imperial responsibility into Zhai Fangjin's coerced suicide.
