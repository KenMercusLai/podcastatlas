## Definition

Disaster Omen Crisis Redirection / 灾异压力转移 is a court-governance pattern where disaster interpreted as Heaven's warning against the ruler is redirected into a visible administrative fault, investigation, punishment, or relief action that preserves central legitimacy while acknowledging the omen frame.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 781 gives the initial case through Empress Dowager Deng's drought crisis. Ministers use disaster rhetoric against female regency and imply that Heaven is warning the court to return power to the young emperor. Deng does not answer by denying the omen system. Instead, she reviews prisoners in Luoyang and palace prisons, identifies a wronged prisoner, arrests the Luoyang magistrate, and lets the subsequent rain validate the administrative correction.

The concept is a political maneuver with administrative content. It can produce real redress for a wronged person and discipline for a local official, but it also shifts the target of blame away from the regency itself. The key move is not skepticism toward omens; it is converting an existential legitimacy accusation into a fixable governance problem.

## Key Claims

- Disaster omens can function as coded political pressure against vulnerable rulers or regents.
- A ruler can defuse the pressure by accepting the omen language while changing its object from central legitimacy to local administrative harm.
- Visible investigation and punishment are central because they give the redirection public proof.
- The maneuver can coexist with real relief or legal correction; it is not automatically empty theater.
- Redirection is especially useful under regency because calls for moral correction can also be calls for transfer of power.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The current concept has one source note, so it should remain a provisional pattern rather than a mature doctrine. The source gives a strong interpretive reading of Deng's political skill; it does not independently prove that the prisoner review caused rain, that all ministers accepted the redirection sincerely, or that local-official punishment solved the broader disaster field.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Hanji 781 to separate disaster-as-omen pressure from the narrower maneuver of redirecting that pressure toward fixable administration.
- Added a regency-specific case in which legal review, punishment, and rain narrative protect central authority under drought pressure.
