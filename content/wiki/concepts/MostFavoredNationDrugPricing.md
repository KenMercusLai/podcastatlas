# Most-Favored-Nation Drug Pricing

Most-favored-nation drug pricing is the source's pharmaceutical policy frame in [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-howard-lutnick-how-america-can-hit-6-gdp-growth-in-2026-39668255]]. [[HowardLutnick|Howard Lutnick]] says Americans pay far more for some drugs than Europeans and that [[DonaldTrump]] wrote to major pharmaceutical companies demanding most-favored-nation pricing.

[[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-supercharging-a-new-fda-marty-makary-on-science-power-patients-39750050]] adds [[MartyMakary|Marty Makary]]'s FDA-side version. Makary calls high U.S. prices "the great American ripoff," gives a [[GLP1Agonists|GLP-1]] example of a very high U.S. price versus a much lower London price, and says companies agreed to pricing changes including a lower first-three-month price for one GLP-1 example. This qualifies the Lutnick source by making the exact product, payer, and duration of the $149 claim source-specific.

In the episode, [[USDepartmentOfHealthAndHumanServices|HHS]] leads drug negotiations while [[USDepartmentOfCommerce|Commerce]] uses [[Section232TariffAuthority]] as a threat. Lutnick says companies were told to provide MFN prices and reshore production or face high tariffs, and he names [[Ozempic]] and [[Mounjaro]] as drugs that would be available through [[Medicaid]] and [[Medicare]] at $149.

## Key Claims
- The source frames high U.S. drug prices as America subsidizing lower prices in other wealthy countries.
- The tariff threat is used to join price negotiation with domestic production policy.
- Lutnick claims the deals save $25 billion to $35 billion a year; the wiki keeps that as a source claim.
- The concept links consumer healthcare costs to [[TradeReciprocityProtectionism]] because foreign pricing and production location are both treated as bargaining targets.
- The Makary source adds that similar pricing rhetoric can be framed through patient affordability and FDA/public-health legitimacy, not only Commerce tariff leverage.
