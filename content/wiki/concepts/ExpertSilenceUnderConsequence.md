## Definition

Expert silence under consequence is the withdrawal, anonymization, or hedging of informed judgment when a public conclusion creates legal, financial, professional, or reputational exposure.

## Current Synthesis

The disputed Van Gogh investigation in [[return-to-the-404-return-to-the-404|Return to the 404]] shows expertise becoming least available when its consequences are greatest. Curators, journalists, lawyers, and specialists stop responding, avoid firm conclusions, or speak only in the background because declaring a valuable painting fake can trigger conflict and liability.

Silence is itself evidence about incentives, but not evidence that the painting is false. The inconclusive computer-vision result—initially split between Van Gogh and non-Van Gogh classifications, then tentatively distinguishing flowers from background—shows why reluctance may reflect both external risk and genuine uncertainty. Reporting should therefore preserve three separate observations: experts face asymmetric consequences, access to attributable judgment narrows, and the underlying authenticity question remains unresolved.

## Key Claims

- Consequential expert judgments can carry asymmetric downside with little comparable reward for speaking publicly.
- Background-only participation preserves some information while weakening transparency and accountable evaluation.
- Widespread reluctance can reveal institutional incentives without proving which substantive conclusion is correct.
- Ambiguous technical analysis may intensify silence because it raises stakes without resolving uncertainty.
- A reporting dead end can still produce knowledge about the system governing access to expertise.

## Evidence

### Withdrawal and attribution limits

- [[return-to-the-404-return-to-the-404]] describes repeated outreach ending in nonresponse, avoidance, or background-only discussion.

### Consequence structure

- [[return-to-the-404-return-to-the-404]] records Van Gogh expert David Brooks describing authenticity disputes as unusually dangerous because fake judgments invite legal and reputational conflict.

### Unresolved technical evidence

- [[return-to-the-404-return-to-the-404]] reports Erik Postma's initially divided computer-vision result and later suggestion that flowers and background might classify differently.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Nonresponse can reflect workload, confidentiality, incomplete evidence, distrust of the reporter, or responsible caution rather than fear alone. Anonymous or background expertise may improve reporting even when it cannot be shown to readers. The computer-vision system, training set, sampling procedure, and error rates are not supplied, so its result cannot authenticate the painting or establish multiple authorship.

## What Changed

- Created a reporting-incentive concept that keeps expert withdrawal separate from the truth of the disputed claim.
