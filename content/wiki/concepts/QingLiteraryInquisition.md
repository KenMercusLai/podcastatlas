# 清代文字狱 / Qing Literary Inquisition

清代文字狱 / Qing literary inquisition is the source's frame for how writings, editions, diaries, students, and family networks could become political evidence in [[11-yongzheng-wangchao-zhi-dayi-juemi-shijingqian-bi-xia-de-qiangu-qi-an-547265513]]. The [[ZengJing|曾静]] case began with [[ZhangXi|张熙]]'s letter, but it expanded when [[YongzhengEmperor|雍正]] identified [[LuLiuliang|吕留良]]'s anti-Qing writing as the deeper cause of disloyalty.

The concept is not just censorship after publication. In the episode, [[LiWeiQingOfficial|李卫]]'s search of the Lu household turns book-buying, stored manuscripts, diaries, descendants, and scholarly reputation into an actionable criminal field. Because Lu was dead, punishment shifted across text, family, students, and symbolic memory.

## Key Claims
- Writing could be treated as political contagion even when direct conspiracy evidence was weak.
- Book circulation connected private reading, regional literati networks, and imperial suspicion.
- Punishing texts allowed the court to relocate guilt from a confused commoner to a more legible ideological source.
- Literary inquisition made [[HanManchuLegitimacyPolitics]] enforceable through archives, print, and family punishment.
