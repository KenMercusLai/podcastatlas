# Investment Master Narrative / 投资大师叙事

Investment master narrative is [[134-touzi-dashi-xilie-xiandao-pian-tamen-bu-zhi-zhuan-le-henduo-qian-geng-chuangzao-le-lijie-shijie-de-fangfa-lqkjpfqc0ymgvnboek5jmjycwtyg]]'s method for reading famous investors. Instead of treating [[RayDalio]], [[JimSimons]], [[HowardMarks]], [[PeterLynch]], [[StanleyDruckenmiller]], or [[PaulTudorJones]] as heroic money-making machines, the episode asks what each person reveals about market structure, tools, cycles, temperament, work intensity, and historical timing.

The concept is useful when biography becomes a bridge between market history and [[InvestmentWorldviewFit]]. It becomes harmful when a listener compresses a complicated life into a single slogan, such as copying [[PeterLynch]] without doing institutional-level company research or copying a macro master without the same capital and risk path.

## Key Claims
- A master's return record should be interpreted with era, asset class, institution, and market structure attached.
- Biography can make abstract investing methods easier to remember, but it can also create [[InvestorIdolRisk]] when narrative clarity is mistaken for repeatability.
- The point of studying masters is to broaden judgment, not to outsource judgment.
- Failure cases, family costs, wrong calls, and unsuitable methods belong inside the narrative because they reveal the real price of a strategy.
