# Teacher Evaluation and Care Burden / 教师评价与照护负担

## Definition
Teacher evaluation and care burden is the mismatch between what schools can readily measure—especially class scores, rankings, and improvement—and the wider safety, relational, developmental, and family-facing responsibilities teachers must still carry.

## Current Synthesis
[[ep188-jiaoshijie-duihua-zuotijia-yangchengshi-dangdai-zuotijie-xianzhuang-dayi-ckwriasfcgevabaaaadf6hi3|EP188]] describes score analysis as a major part of subject-teacher evaluation while showing that homeroom work includes student safety, psychological-risk recognition, discipline, seating fairness, parent communication, and conflict documentation. The concept is not an argument against academic accountability. It identifies a governance problem: the most visible metric can dominate attention even when the most consequential duties are difficult to quantify.

## Key Claims
- Scores, rankings, and improvement measures make teaching performance legible but incomplete.
- Homeroom teachers carry safety and care responsibilities that may outweigh academic metrics during a crisis.
- Parent communication and class-rule fairness are substantive work, not incidental customer service.
- Narrow metrics can crowd out individual difference and long-term developmental outcomes.
- Sustainable evaluation must distinguish teacher influence from student, family, cohort, and institutional conditions.

## Evidence
- Score visibility: [[ep188-jiaoshijie-duihua-zuotijia-yangchengshi-dangdai-zuotijie-xianzhuang-dayi-ckwriasfcgevabaaaadf6hi3|EP188]] describes stage-by-stage quality analysis comparing class results, rank, progress, and performance before and after a teacher takes a class.
- Care responsibility: [[ep188-jiaoshijie-duihua-zuotijia-yangchengshi-dangdai-zuotijie-xianzhuang-dayi-ckwriasfcgevabaaaadf6hi3|EP188]] says homeroom teachers must prioritize safety and respond to serious psychological or injury risks in addition to academic management.
- Family and fairness work: [[ep188-jiaoshijie-duihua-zuotijia-yangchengshi-dangdai-zuotijie-xianzhuang-dayi-ckwriasfcgevabaaaadf6hi3|EP188]] uses seating requests and written communication to show how teachers explain constraints, protect fairness, and manage disputed expectations.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source offers one teacher's estimate that scores may constitute roughly sixty to seventy percent of an instructor's goals; it does not provide the school's formal rubric or representative data. Academic outcomes can reveal real instructional quality, and broader evaluation can itself become subjective or administratively costly. The point is incompleteness, not that scores should never matter.

## What Changed
- Created the concept from EP188's contrast between visible score accountability and less measurable care work.
