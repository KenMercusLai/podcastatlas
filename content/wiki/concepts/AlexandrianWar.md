# Alexandrian War

## Definition

The Alexandrian War was the 48–47 BC conflict in which [[JuliusCaesar|Julius Caesar]]'s attempt to arbitrate the Ptolemaic succession turned a palace occupation in [[Alexandria]] into war with forces supporting [[PtolemyXIII|Ptolemy XIII]] and [[ArsinoeIV|Arsinoe IV]].

## Current Synthesis

[[196-julius-caesar-cleopatra-part-2-glt3216275988]] treats the war as a collision between Roman civil war and Ptolemaic dynastic crisis. Pompey's murder did not purchase security for Ptolemy's court; Caesar arrived with too few troops, occupied the palace quarter, summoned the rival monarchs, and backed Cleopatra after her covert return.

The resulting coalition was unstable on both sides. Ptolemy, Cleopatra, Arsinoe, Pothinus, and Caesar were initially confined within one political space, while Arsinoe's escape and Ptolemy's later release expanded resistance outside it. Caesar executed Pothinus, burned ships to prevent their capture, and survived until reinforcements under Mithridates of Pergamon helped defeat the royal army. Ptolemy's death then enabled Cleopatra's restoration with Ptolemy XIV.

## Key Claims

- A foreign arbitration attempt became urban war because Caesar's small force could not impose an uncontested succession settlement.
- Pompey's murder intensified rather than resolved the Egyptian court's exposure to Roman civil war.
- Cleopatra's alliance with Caesar converted covert access and personal negotiation into military-backed restoration.
- Arsinoe's escape shows that anti-Caesarian resistance contained competing Ptolemaic claims rather than one unified royal command.
- Caesar's harbor fire was a defensive military act that damaged stored books but does not establish destruction of the whole [[LibraryOfAlexandria|Library of Alexandria]].
- Relief forces and Ptolemy XIII's death, not palace negotiation alone, secured Cleopatra's throne.

### Fragmented dynastic opposition

- [[196-julius-caesar-cleopatra-part-2-glt3216275988]] follows Arsinoe's escape, Ptolemy's release, and the rival court factions outside Caesar's control.

### Military outcome

- [[196-julius-caesar-cleopatra-part-2-glt3216275988]] records the fleet fire, relief under Mithridates of Pergamon, Ptolemy's drowning, and Cleopatra's restoration.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode is a narrative synthesis rather than a complete military history. Force sizes, the precise sequence of palace decisions, the extent of urban support, the fire's damage, Caesar's motives in releasing Ptolemy, and the details of the final battle remain source-scoped. Cleopatra's restoration was real, but the source does not make it evidence of broad Egyptian consent.

## What Changed

- Established the war as the hinge joining Roman civil war, Ptolemaic succession, and Cleopatra's restored rule.
- Separated limited harbor-book damage from the later total-library destruction myth.
