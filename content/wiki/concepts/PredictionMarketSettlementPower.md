# Prediction Market Settlement Power

## Definition
Prediction market settlement power is the authority created when a platform's event wording and resolution process decide which messy real-world facts count as Yes, No, or unresolved for a tradable contract.

## Current Synthesis
The [[DuanwenNewsPodcast|端闻]] Polymarket episode adds this concept by contrasting news reality with contract settlement. [[Polymarket]] can make information legible as price, but that price depends on binary definitions: whether a ceasefire happened, whether the [[StraitOfHormuz]] reopened, whether [[VolodymyrZelensky]] wore a suit, or when a U.S. invasion of [[Venezuela]] would count.

The episode's core warning is that settlement rules are not neutral summaries of reality. They can privilege official statements, platform wording, or narrow verification standards over lived experience, continued violence, ambiguity, and human consequences. This does not mean all prediction markets are useless; it means that the social power of the market includes definition, not only aggregation.

## Key Claims
- Binary contracts can hide the interpretive work needed to turn events into tradable facts.
- Official declarations and platform settlement can diverge from field reality, especially around war, ceasefires, and political action.
- Clear wording can reduce ambiguity but cannot remove all judgment when reality is continuous, contested, or symbolic.
- Settlement power connects prediction markets to journalism because both decide which facts matter, but markets often compress context more aggressively.
- Platform definition risk becomes more important when market prices are treated as news or public probability signals.

## Evidence
- Ceasefire and passage ambiguity: [[dang-xinwen-chengwei-dupan-jizhe-keyi-da-zhuan-yibi-ma-f8800ef95c64ad18f08802b15cd20b79]] contrasts announced temporary ceasefires with continuing missiles and casualties, and distinguishes Hormuz passage from broader peace.
- Symbolic-definition disputes: [[dang-xinwen-chengwei-dupan-jizhe-keyi-da-zhuan-yibi-ma-f8800ef95c64ad18f08802b15cd20b79]] uses Zelenskyy's NATO dinner clothing to show that a word like suit can carry disputed criteria.
- State-action thresholds: [[dang-xinwen-chengwei-dupan-jizhe-keyi-da-zhuan-yibi-ma-f8800ef95c64ad18f08802b15cd20b79]] uses the "when would the U.S. invade Venezuela" market to show how official definitions and civilian perceptions can diverge.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- Some markets can be written with objective, externally verifiable criteria, which reduces but does not eliminate settlement power.
- The source focuses on journalistic and ethical interpretation; it does not audit Polymarket's actual dispute-resolution procedure or compare it against other platforms' rules.
- The episode's examples show risk patterns, not proof that every binary contract misrepresents reality.

## What Changed
- New concept created to name the definition and resolution authority exposed by the Polymarket episode.
