# Oral-Care Product Treatment Boundary / 口腔护理产品治疗边界

## Definition
The oral-care product treatment boundary separates supportive hygiene, cosmetic, sensitivity, or mineral-delivery functions from claims that a consumer product diagnoses, cures, regenerates, permanently repairs, or substitutes for cause-directed clinical care.

## Current Synthesis
[[vol-132-bao-zhu-ni-qian-bao-bi-lei-na-xie-keng-ren-de-kou-qiang-hu-li-chan-pin-he-wei-ke-pu-lmjeye3oacbawtn3omsskuqeubeh]] uses toothpaste, repair liquids, rinses, and whitening products to show how plausible ingredients or laboratory findings can be stretched into treatment promises. Toothpaste can help mechanically remove soft deposits and may support sensitivity relief, mineral balance, or stain control, but this does not prove tooth regrowth, repair of an established cavity, eradication of gastric H. pylori infection, or cure of gum inflammation while its cause remains.

## Key Claims
- Laboratory, animal, and early research findings do not by themselves establish routine human clinical effectiveness.
- Mechanical plaque disruption remains the primary daily function of brushing; ingredients do not compensate for ineffective technique.
- Reduced gum bleeding is not equivalent to removal of plaque, calculus, inflammation, or other underlying causes.
- Toothpaste is not a treatment for confirmed H. pylori infection, whose assessment and care belong in an appropriate medical pathway.
- Daily cosmetic or breath-freshening rinses differ from medicated rinses such as chlorhexidine products, which should not be treated as indefinite routine care.
- Claims such as tooth regrowth, rapid enamel reconstruction, “permanent repair,” or treatment-level effects require scrutiny beyond promotional language.

## Evidence
- Regrowth and repair claims: [[vol-132-bao-zhu-ni-qian-bao-bi-lei-na-xie-keng-ren-de-kou-qiang-hu-li-chan-pin-he-wei-ke-pu-lmjeye3oacbawtn3omsskuqeubeh]] explicitly rejects advertised tooth growth and 48-hour structural repair.
- Plaque and bleeding: [[vol-132-bao-zhu-ni-qian-bao-bi-lei-na-xie-keng-ren-de-kou-qiang-hu-li-chan-pin-he-wei-ke-pu-lmjeye3oacbawtn3omsskuqeubeh]] places brushing, cleaning, and professional removal of irritants ahead of symptom-suppressing toothpaste.
- Infection boundary: [[vol-132-bao-zhu-ni-qian-bao-bi-lei-na-xie-keng-ren-de-kou-qiang-hu-li-chan-pin-he-wei-ke-pu-lmjeye3oacbawtn3omsskuqeubeh]] routes confirmed H. pylori infection to gastroenterology rather than an “anti-H. pylori” toothpaste.
- Rinse boundary: [[vol-132-bao-zhu-ni-qian-bao-bi-lei-na-xie-keng-ren-de-kou-qiang-hu-li-chan-pin-he-wei-ke-pu-lmjeye3oacbawtn3omsskuqeubeh]] distinguishes treatment-type mouthwash from ordinary care products and cautions against long-term unsupervised chlorhexidine use.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The boundary does not imply that every functional ingredient or oral-care product is useless. Desensitizing, fluoride, mineral-support, whitening, antimicrobial, and prescription products can have legitimate indications, formulations, and evidence. The source does not provide brand-level testing, ingredient concentrations, comparative trials, or regulatory adjudication of a named claim.

## What Changed
- Created a product-versus-treatment test covering toothpaste, repair liquids, rinses, and infection or bleeding claims.
- Made research stage, lesion stage, underlying cause, and clinical indication explicit decision points.
