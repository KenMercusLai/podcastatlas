# Early Confucian Resistance Politics / 早期儒家的反抗政治

Early Confucian resistance politics is [[30-mengzi-zhanguo-fenqing-de-chizi-zhixin-592494138]]'s corrective to the idea that Confucianism is only obedience, hierarchy, and spiritual pressure. The episode argues that [[Mengzi|孟子 / Mencius]] preserves a hard-edged early Confucian language of dignity, remonstrance, moral courage, and refusal to recognize an unrighteous ruler as truly legitimate.

The concept matters because it separates Mencius from later imperial domestications of Confucianism. In the source, [[Confucius|孔子]] and Mencius are not used to sanctify arbitrary rule; they become resources for saying that ministers are not slaves, the people matter more than the ruler, and a tyrant can lose the name of ruler.

## Key Claims
- Early Confucian hierarchy is not identical with unconditional submission.
- Mencian remonstrance depends on the speaker's moral dignity, not on courtly flattery.
- "暴君非君" turns moral failure into a legitimacy failure.
- The source uses this frame to challenge the idea that universal values can only be imported from Western political vocabulary.
