# Ostarbeiter Forced Labor / 东方劳工强制劳动

Ostarbeiter forced labor / 东方劳工强制劳动 is the World War II forced-labor system introduced in [[05-ta-laizi-maliwuboer-ruguo-ni-kanjian-guo-wo-ceng-jiandao-de-533375925]]. The episode describes Eastern workers as people taken, recruited, deceived, or coerced from Eastern Europe into [[NaziGermany|Nazi Germany]] to work in factories, farms, companies, and private households.

The source's focus is not only wartime labor extraction. It emphasizes the afterlife of the category: many workers feared repatriation to the [[SovietUnion]] because they could be treated as traitors, punished, or socially erased after the war. [[YevgeniaSheCameFromMariupol|叶弗根尼亚]]'s postwar hiding and later collapse make forced labor part of a longer chain of exile, shame, and [[DisasterSilenceAndShame]].

## Key Claims
- Forced labor can continue to shape life after formal liberation through stigma, documentation fear, displacement, and repatriation risk.
- The category is historically underremembered compared with better-known Nazi crimes, which makes testimony and family recovery important.
- Labor extraction is part of [[RationalizedStateViolence]] when it is organized through state categories, camps, factories, and bureaucratic control.
- Family memory can preserve the consequences of forced labor even when public memory is thin.
