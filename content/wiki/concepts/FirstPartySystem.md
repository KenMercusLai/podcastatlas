# First Party System

## Definition
The First Party System is the episode's founding-era conflict between [[ThomasJefferson|Thomas Jefferson]]'s [[DemocraticRepublicanParty|Democratic-Republicans]] and the [[FederalistParty|Federalists]] associated with [[AlexanderHamilton|Alexander Hamilton]] and John Adams.

## Current Synthesis
The episode uses party conflict to show Jefferson as an operator, not only a thinker. His disputes with Hamilton over federal power, banks, debt, Britain, France, farmers, and tyranny became organized party politics. The system produced bitter personal and ideological attacks, but the 1800 election also became a crucial democratic test because defeat by one party did not prevent the transfer of presidential power to another.

## Key Claims
- Early U.S. party conflict emerged around competing visions of federal power, finance, foreign alignment, and republican liberty.
- Jefferson's philosopher image depended on practical party organization, especially the work attributed to James Madison.
- The 1796 election exposed an unstable constitutional design because the runner-up became vice president.
- The 1800 Jefferson-Burr tie showed how party machinery could fail inside the Electoral College.
- The peaceful resolution of the 1800 crisis made party competition compatible with democratic continuity.

## Evidence
- Ideological split: [[686-jefferson-the-betrayal-of-liberty-part-4-glt6535571769]] lists federal power, banks, debt, Britain, France, farmers, and tyranny as the main Jefferson-Hamilton disputes.
- Party roles: [[686-jefferson-the-betrayal-of-liberty-part-4-glt6535571769]] presents Jefferson as Democratic-Republican standard-bearer and James Madison as party organizer.
- Constitutional awkwardness: [[686-jefferson-the-betrayal-of-liberty-part-4-glt6535571769]] says Jefferson became Adams's vice president after losing the 1796 election.
- Electoral crisis: [[686-jefferson-the-betrayal-of-liberty-part-4-glt6535571769]] says Jefferson and Aaron Burr tied at 73 electoral votes and the House chose Jefferson on the 36th ballot.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode's first-party-system account is designed to support a Jefferson biography. It does not provide a full account of local party organization, congressional blocs, press networks, or the later constitutional repair of the Electoral College.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to connect Jefferson's political skill, party rivalry, and the 1800 transfer.
