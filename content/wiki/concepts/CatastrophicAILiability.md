# Catastrophic AI Liability

Catastrophic AI liability is the accountability problem raised when AI systems are alleged to contribute to severe harms such as cyberattacks on critical infrastructure or bioweapon creation. [[tech-20260417-0417-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260417-0417-mp-tech-pod-128]] adds the concept through [[MariaCurie|Maria Currie]]'s critique of treating AI liability like ordinary social media platform liability.

The concept matters because model developers, users, and lawmakers may all point to different causal layers. A user can initiate misuse, but a model provider may shape model capability, access, safeguards, monitoring, and post-release response. A state law that limits liability therefore has to decide how much responsibility remains with the developer when the harm is unusually severe.

## Key Claims
- Catastrophic harms make AI liability different from ordinary content-hosting disputes.
- Intentional or reckless developer conduct is only one possible liability threshold; negligence, safety protocol adequacy, access control, and monitoring may also matter.
- Safety protocols can reduce risk, but they need grounding in real model behavior and plausible misuse pathways.
- State AI liability shields may be politically unstable when constituents demand regulation of severe AI harms.
- The concept connects model-release governance with civil liability, not only internal safety policy.
