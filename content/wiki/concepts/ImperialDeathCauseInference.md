## Definition

帝王死因史料推断 / Imperial Death-Cause Inference is the practice of reading sparse death notices together with symptom records, reported exposures, behavior changes, workload evidence, chronology, and source comparison while keeping diagnosis bounded by what the sources actually establish.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-732-donghan-guangwudi-liuxiu-jiabeng-bingsi-de-lnsu1le2x-cj75cjslysvbndeuvr|Hanji 732]] grounds one form of the problem through [[LiuXiu|光武帝刘秀]]. [[ZizhiTongjian|《资治通鉴》]] records his death in terse court terms: place, age, and date. The episode then uses [[DongguanHanji|《东观汉记》]] notices of earlier dizziness, immobility, cold/wind illness, and jaundice-like symptoms to ask whether Liu Xiu's death followed a longer health decline.

The [[LuZhi|吕雉]] case is more diagnostically specific but still underdetermined. A dog-like apparition, armpit pain, an estimated four-month interval, and later reports of mental disturbance are linked to rabies, demonstrating why specificity raises the evidentiary burden: without a confirmed bite or saliva exposure, infected animal, wound, complete symptom course, or contemporaneous diagnosis, compatibility with selected features cannot establish causation. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-205-nvhuangdi-lvzhi-jingran-yin-gou-er-si-1-loyaahdx4svudlgwwq5ww_fcj8f9|Hanji 205 part 1]]

The synthesis is methodological rather than diagnostic. A ruler's late health can be historically legible without a named disease, and a later medical hypothesis can be plausible without becoming proven. Symptom selection, retrospective narrative fit, supernatural framing, missing negative evidence, and uncertain transmission all have to be separated from directly observed exposure and clinical course.

## Key Claims

- Terse imperial death notices often identify the event but leave cause, course, and clinical context underdetermined.
- Supplementary source traditions can add symptom history, but symptom history is not the same thing as diagnosis.
- A proposed exposure must itself be established; an apparition or ambiguous encounter cannot substitute for evidence of a bite, wound, or infectious contact.
- Ruler workload and self-description can explain plausible health pressure while still remaining indirect evidence.
- Responsible inference separates "this pattern is suggestive" from "this disease caused the death."
- In Liu Xiu's case, the strongest current claim is long-term health vulnerability, not a definitive cause of death.
- In Lu Zhi's case, rabies is a source-attributed retrospective hypothesis whose exposure and clinical chain remain incomplete.

## Evidence

- Sparse death notice: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-732-donghan-guangwudi-liuxiu-jiabeng-bingsi-de-lnsu1le2x-cj75cjslysvbndeuvr|Hanji 732]] says Zizhi Tongjian gives Liu Xiu's death at Nangong in a short date/place/age form.
- Supplementary symptoms: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-732-donghan-guangwudi-liuxiu-jiabeng-bingsi-de-lnsu1le2x-cj75cjslysvbndeuvr|Hanji 732]] uses Dongguan Hanji notices from 41 CE and 44 CE to add earlier illness clues.
- Workload pressure: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-732-donghan-guangwudi-liuxiu-jiabeng-bingsi-de-lnsu1le2x-cj75cjslysvbndeuvr|Hanji 732]] describes Liu Xiu's long court days, late-night work, and refusal to treat governing as tiring.
- Inference boundary: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-732-donghan-guangwudi-liuxiu-jiabeng-bingsi-de-lnsu1le2x-cj75cjslysvbndeuvr|Hanji 732]] keeps the death-cause discussion indirect rather than naming a settled disease.
- Specific-diagnosis test: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-205-nvhuangdi-lvzhi-jingran-yin-gou-er-si-1-loyaahdx4svudlgwwq5ww_fcj8f9|Hanji 205 part 1]] proposes rabies from season, a dog-like encounter, pain, elapsed time, and mental disturbance, but supplies no confirmed bite, infected animal, wound, complete syndrome, or contemporaneous diagnosis.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Neither source gives an autopsy or a clinically complete final illness narrative. Hanji 732 does not directly connect Liu Xiu's earlier symptoms to his death in 57 CE. Hanji 205 part 1 selects features compatible with rabies but does not establish exposure, characteristic symptom progression, or rule out other explanations; its spirit account also belongs to a different explanatory register from medical evidence. Modern disease names should not be retrofitted as conclusions without stronger source support. The concept also differs from cases where a source directly reports concealed death, poison, murder, or a clear acute event.

## What Changed

- Expanded the method from one nonspecific decline case to contrast it with a named retrospective diagnosis.
- Added exposure verification, complete clinical sequence, and narrative selection as explicit tests before accepting a disease-specific cause.
