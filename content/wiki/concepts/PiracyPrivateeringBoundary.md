# Piracy-Privateering Boundary

## Definition

The piracy-privateering boundary separates unauthorized maritime raiding from capture conducted under state commission and subject to prize adjudication.

## Current Synthesis

Authorization changes the legal position of maritime violence without making the violence harmless. [[FrancisDrake]]'s career shows the unstable edge: raids without a written commission looked like piracy even when [[ElizabethI|Elizabeth I]] benefited from ambiguity. Historical privateers, by contrast, could carry commissions, post financial guarantees, submit captures to prize courts, and face penalties for improper taking.

The modern revival discussed by [[PlanetMoney]] concerns [[MaritimePrizeLaw|prize adjudication]] for regular naval captures, not restored privateering. A court process for state forces does not create a civilian license to raid, and physical seizure still does not establish lawful prize.

## Key Claims

- Explicit state commission is a central legal distinction between privateering and piracy.
- Deniable or unwritten authorization can benefit a ruler while leaving a captain exposed.
- Prize adjudication disciplines authorized capture by testing enemy connection, evidence, neutrality, and entitlement.
- State authorization changes legal classification but does not erase violence, exploitation, or diplomatic consequences.
- Reviving prize courts for regular naval forces is not equivalent to reviving privateers.

## Evidence

### Ambiguous authorization

- Drake's post-[[SanJuanDeUlua]] raids and 1577 expedition lacked a clear written commission in the episode's account; [[ThomasDoughty]] exposed that vulnerability by demanding to see one. [[693-elizabeth-i-vs-the-catholics-englands-greatest-hero-part-3-glt5069845329]]

### Formal privateering structure

- The Planet Money episode says privateers required official commissions and financial guarantees and could be penalized for seizing neutral ships or stealing property improperly. ### Modern distinction

- Officials are described as considering prize-court machinery for regular Navy captures while rejecting private mercenaries or a civilian “bonus navy.” [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-d94256e09d5ac8ce0f836a06ea427926-26299318-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-d94256e09d5ac8ce0f836a06ea427926-26299318]]

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The sources cover different periods and institutional settings. Drake's deniable Elizabethan raiding does not establish modern U.S. law, while formal historical commissions do not prove every capture was lawful or nonviolent. The proposed procedure remains untested, and the separate [[PrizeLawWarThreshold|war threshold]] may prevent its use regardless of who conducts the seizure.

## What Changed

- Distinguished revival of prize courts from revival of civilian privateering.
- Added formal commission, financial guarantee, and prize review to the earlier deniable-authorization case.
