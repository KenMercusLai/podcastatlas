# Regime Basic-Base Stabilization / 政权基本盘稳定

Regime basic-base stabilization / 政权基本盘稳定 is [[LiuYiSanlian|刘仪]]'s reading of how postwar money would likely be used by [[Iran]]'s existing system. In [[ep251-yilang-kunju-sishinian-zhanlue-xuanze-de-daijia-yu-xianshi-lp8vyonyugtd2ozymgof-mim9zkd]], he is skeptical that compensation, security guarantees, or fiscal relief would become a genuine reform-and-opening package; he expects it would first stabilize people and organizations dependent on the state machine.

The concept extends [[IranPostwarEconomicRelief]] by asking where relief goes inside a damaged regime. The episode estimates a large labor force share dependent on public employment, religious institutions, the [[IslamicRevolutionaryGuardCorps]], foundations, and public services. In that structure, fiscal inflow can preserve loyalty and administrative continuity without creating broad private-sector growth.

The source also limits the simple "sanctions lifted, development follows" story. Even if restrictions ended quickly, foreign capital would likely prefer energy and adjacent sectors, which cannot absorb enough labor; manufacturing would face competition from India and Southeast Asia, and services would compete with Gulf hubs already organized around safety and confidence.

## Key Claims
- Relief can stabilize a regime without opening reform space.
- State-dependent employment and patronage turn fiscal inflow into political maintenance.
- Energy investment may restore revenue without creating broad labor absorption.
- Reform-and-opening analogies need time, labor structure, market access, and institutional change; sanctions removal alone is not enough.
