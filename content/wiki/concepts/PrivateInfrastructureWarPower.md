# Private Infrastructure War Power

## Definition
Private infrastructure war power is the practical authority a company acquires when access rules, technical thresholds, or service continuity in its commercial system materially affect military and civilian capability during war.

## Current Synthesis
The first source establishes the access-control problem through [[Starlink]] in [[Ukraine]]. [[ElonMusk]] says [[SpaceX]] and Ukraine used approved-terminal whitelists to deny Russian access obtained through smuggling or Ukrainian channels, while also cutting off some innocent users. A defensible security control therefore created collateral exclusion as a direct operational consequence.

A reported performance-threshold case broadens the problem. Ukrainian officials believed terminals had been limited to operation below roughly 100 miles per hour, leaving ground communication intact while impairing fast-moving drones. SpaceX and Starlink had not confirmed that the restriction was deliberate. The broader synthesis does not depend on that allegation being true: when military capability relies on private infrastructure, even an unexplained technical change can alter strategy and expose uncertainty over who sets, communicates, reviews, and can appeal operational rules.

## Key Claims
- Commercial infrastructure can become strategically necessary before public governance catches up.
- Whitelists can deny adversary use while also excluding legitimate users.
- Technical thresholds can affect military platforms differently even when general service continues.
- A company can exercise de facto wartime gatekeeping without publicly describing a decision as military strategy.
- Transparency, review, state coordination, and recovery paths matter because unexplained changes can themselves disrupt planning.

## Evidence
- Wartime access control: [[an-interview-with-elon-musk-6a6212214fac21e67f9b8c8c]] records Musk's claim that SpaceX and Ukraine used terminal whitelists against Russian access and also disconnected some innocent users.
- Reported performance restriction: [[kicking-kyiv-trumps-doubtful-deals-64ee795d3a366335cf4a682ed4b0f0d5]] says Ukrainian officials believed a speed threshold threatened deep-strike and battlefield drones while leaving ground links functional.
- Confirmation boundary: [[kicking-kyiv-trumps-doubtful-deals-64ee795d3a366335cf4a682ed4b0f0d5]] explicitly says SpaceX and Starlink had not confirmed deliberate restriction.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The first account is Musk's own explanation and does not independently audit terminal sourcing, whitelist design, or collateral effects. The second account reports Ukrainian belief rather than a confirmed company policy; technical failure, product behavior, or another cause may explain the observation. The page therefore identifies a governance dependency and risk, not proof that SpaceX intentionally constrained Ukrainian operations.

## What Changed
- Added technical performance thresholds as a second form of private wartime access power.
- Made non-confirmation and alternative technical explanations explicit.
- Added the planning risk created by opaque or unexplained infrastructure changes.
