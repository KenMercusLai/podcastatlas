# Private Infrastructure War Power

Private infrastructure war power is the governance problem created when privately owned infrastructure becomes operationally important in war. [[an-interview-with-elon-musk-6a6212214fac21e67f9b8c8c]] adds the concept through [[Starlink]] in [[Ukraine]]: [[ElonMusk]] says [[SpaceX]] never sold terminals to Russians, but that Russian users obtained terminals through Ukraine and smuggling, leading SpaceX and Ukraine to work on approved-terminal whitelists.

The episode makes [[Starlink]] more than a connectivity product. It is a battlefield access-control system where private technical decisions can affect soldiers, civilians, occupied areas, innocent users, and strategic expectations, while state authority and company authority overlap imperfectly.

## Key Claims
- Private infrastructure can become strategically necessary before public law and procurement catch up.
- Access control can help deny adversaries capability while also cutting off legitimate or innocent users.
- A company can become a de facto wartime gatekeeper even when it frames itself as providing commercial service rather than choosing military strategy.
- The issue extends [[StrategicAIInfrastructureDependence]] into war: critical capability can sit inside a private stack controlled by a small number of executives.
