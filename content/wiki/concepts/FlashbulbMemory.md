# Flashbulb Memory

## Definition
Flashbulb memory is vivid, highly confident recollection associated with learning about or experiencing a dramatic public event; vividness and confidence can persist even when reported details change over time.

## Current Synthesis
[[free-brian-williams-free-brian-williams|Free Brian Williams]] presents longitudinal September 11 research in which participants answered questions shortly after the event and again across ten years. The episode reports substantial consistency decline alongside extremely high confidence, showing that emotional importance can preserve the feeling of certainty more reliably than exact detail.

The concept therefore sharpens [[ReconstructiveMemory]] rather than creating a special class of photographic memory. Retrieval, later information, source confusion, and time-slice errors can alter who was present, what was said, or when a nearby event occurred while leaving the recollection coherent and sincere.

## Key Claims
- Dramatic public events can produce memories that feel unusually vivid and certain.
- Confidence and emotional importance do not guarantee stable detail across repeated reports.
- Early records are especially valuable because later confidence can exceed later consistency.
- A time-slice error can move a real conversation or action into the wrong point in a remembered sequence.
- General flashbulb-memory fallibility cannot establish the cause or truth of a particular disputed account.

## Evidence
- Longitudinal drift - [[free-brian-williams-free-brian-williams]] reports repeated September 11 surveys across one, two, and ten years with substantial consistency decline.
- Confidence dissociation - [[free-brian-williams-free-brian-williams]] says participants remained highly confident and sometimes rejected their own earlier written accounts.
- Time-slice example - [[free-brian-williams-free-brian-williams]] contrasts Deedee Gordon's September 11 recollection with records placing Malcolm Gladwell's Montreal flight a week later.
- Applied case - [[free-brian-williams-free-brian-williams]] uses the research to make sincere reconstruction of Brian Williams's Iraq story plausible without proving that explanation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The supplied source is a structured episode summary rather than the underlying study or a systematic literature review. Its sample size, reported average consistency decline, confidence pattern, and characterization of expert agreement require verification against the original papers. A general mechanism does not show that every vivid discrepancy is innocent, nor does it remove verification duties when a claim affects reputation, justice, or public trust.
