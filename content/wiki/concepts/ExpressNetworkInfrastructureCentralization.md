# 快递网络基础设施收权

## Definition
Express network infrastructure centralization is the shift from locally controlled or improvised transport and sorting toward headquarters-controlled hubs, scheduled trunk routes, routing systems, automation, and settlement.

## Current Synthesis
Franchise outlets could create coverage, but stable national service required headquarters control over the bottlenecks between them. Scheduled vehicles, acquired hubs, prepaid waybills, direct routes, drop-and-pull transport, automated sorting, and electronic waybills made time commitments more credible and prevented local operators from taking key network nodes away.

## Key Claims
- Scheduled routes can create demand by making delivery promises credible.
- Hub ownership converts a loose federation into a controllable network.
- Automation raises peak capacity and lowers unit cost.
- Data standards connect platform orders to physical routing.

## Evidence
- Route and hub control: [[no-219-kuaidi-jianghu-sanshinian-tonglubang-taobao-pinduoduo-yu-jiage-zhan-1015471748]] describes 中通's trunk vehicles and later headquarters acquisition of transfer centers.
- System upgrade: [[no-219-kuaidi-jianghu-sanshinian-tonglubang-taobao-pinduoduo-yu-jiage-zhan-1015471748]] connects the 2010 peak failure to direct routing, drop-and-pull transport, sorting equipment, and [[Cainiao|菜鸟]] electronic waybills.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Infrastructure control does not eliminate last-mile fragmentation or service variation. Cost and efficiency claims are carrier- and period-specific.
