# 杯酒释兵权 / Cup of Wine Disarms Generals

Cup of Wine Disarms Generals / 杯酒释兵权 is the famous shorthand that [[169-songtaizu-zhisi-zhuying-fusheng-yinmoulun-zhong-de-renxing-mituan-shang-957052909]] treats as a compressed and romanticized version of a broader political process. The episode says [[ZhaoKuangyin|赵匡胤]] did not simply solve military risk in one banquet; he gradually removed command from禁军 leaders and later from regional military governors.

The source emphasizes the logic behind the story. [[ZhaoPu|赵普]] warns that even loyal commanders can be endangered by soldiers who want reward, making army loyalty a structural threat. Zhao Kuangyin's answer is not mass killing but a form of elite buyout: wealth, land, honorable retirement, and marriage ties replace direct military command.

For the wiki, the concept is the hinge between the founding coup and [[SongMilitaryCentralization|宋初军事集权]]. The same military relationships that made [[ChenQiaoMutiny|陈桥兵变]] possible had to be neutralized once Zhao Kuangyin became emperor.

## Key Claims
- The banquet story preserves a real political pattern but simplifies its duration and bargaining.
- Disarmament targets not only betrayal but commanders' inability to control their own troops.
- Wealth, land, status, and marriage can be tools of centralization.
- Buyout is less bloody than purge, but it can still institutionalize suspicion of generals.
- The event links founding violence to later civil-over-military governance.
