# Pro Contractor Retail Strategy

## Definition

Pro contractor retail strategy is the [[home-depot-1]] pattern where a home-improvement retailer deepens its business with professional trades through credit, pro desks, bulk pricing, job-site delivery, specialized distribution, and category-specific logistics.

## Current Synthesis

Home Depot's pro strategy grows out of the same warehouse base as DIY retail but has different needs. Contractors value speed, availability, credit, bulk handling, and delivery to the job site. The episode treats the modern SRS and HD Supply direction as more focused than earlier adjacency expansion because it stays close to professional home-improvement demand.

## Key Claims
- Pro customers make logistics and availability more valuable than showroom experience alone.
- Credit, bulk pricing, and job-site delivery can turn a retail store network into a professional supply platform.
- Adjacent distribution can fit when it strengthens the core customer system.
- Timing and execution determine whether pro expansion helps or distracts.

## Evidence
- [[home-depot-1]] says Home Depot expanded pro tools with business credit, pro desks, bulk pricing, and job-site delivery in the 1990s.
- [[home-depot-1]] says a little over half of modern revenue comes from professional contractors.
- [[home-depot-1]] says Home Depot reacquired valuable HD Supply assets and bought [[SRSDistribution]] to serve roofers, landscapers, pool contractors, and other trades.
- [[home-depot-1]] distinguishes the later pro-distribution push from [[BobNardelli]]'s broader adjacency strategy.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept is source-scoped to Home Depot. It does not yet compare independent contractor supply houses, Lowe's pro strategy, or post-SRS integration results.
