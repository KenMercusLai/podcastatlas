# Dynamic Pricing Fairness

## Definition
Dynamic pricing fairness is the tension between prices that adjust to demand, supply, route, or timing and the allocation rules buyers experience as legitimate.

## Current Synthesis
Dynamic pricing can attract supply or spread demand, but fairness depends on what changes, why it changes, and whether buyers can see a defensible rule. Rideshare, groceries, online retail, air travel, and market-based rail fares show that peak demand does not mechanically raise every price: supplier response, customer mix, route capacity, timing, and discount inventory can produce both surcharges and discounts.

## Key Claims
- Variable prices can reduce shortages or rebalance demand when supply and timing can respond.
- Consumer discomfort can reflect disagreement about allocation rules rather than ignorance of economics.
- Route-level customer mix can make a holiday fare fall even while broader travel demand rises.
- Transparent variables and alternatives improve legitimacy but do not eliminate distributional concerns.

## Evidence
- Allocation tradeoff: [[live-anthropic-co-founder-on-ai-and-jobs]] contrasts rideshare surge, groceries, Amazon, wait lists, and envy-free allocation.
- Transport differentiation: [[jinghu-gaotie-zhongqiujie-qian-chuxian-jiangjia-xinbailun-qisu-dikanong-qinquan-1017386449]] reports increases on some long-distance routes but discounts on the business-heavy Beijing-Shanghai high-speed railway during Mid-Autumn Festival.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The railway example is source-reported and does not provide fare distributions, seat inventory, purchase dates, or comparable route controls. A lower observed fare on one corridor does not establish that the pricing system is fair, only that peak-demand effects are heterogeneous.

## What Changed
- Added route, timing, and customer-mix heterogeneity from holiday rail pricing.
- Rejected a simple peak-demand-equals-universal-price-rise rule.
