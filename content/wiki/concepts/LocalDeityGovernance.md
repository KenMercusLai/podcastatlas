# Local Deity Governance

Local deity governance is the way folk religion imagines gods as territorial administrators, neighborhood protectors, and moral record-keepers. [[136-chunri-mingmei-liaoliao-guishen-827911486]] develops it through [[Chenghuang|城隍]] and [[Tudigong|土地]], whose authority is closer to a city magistrate, local gentry figure, or community elder than to a distant cosmic god.

[[ep249-minnan-gucheng-youshenji-chuantong-yishi-beihou-de-xiangtu-wangluo-lpk1vb9-zmgmuous1nyzefgapgww]] adds a living institutional case through [[YongningAncientCity|永宁古城]]. The source shows local deity governance not only as an imagined office, but as temple committees, incense money, heritage status, procession routes, [[JitongSpiritMedium|鸡童/乩童]] mediumship, and conflict arbitration inside [[MinnanRitualNetwork|闽南仪式网络]].

The concept is the local layer of [[UnderworldBureaucracy]]. The city god handles a jurisdiction, can be associated with historical worthies, and may be appointed through examination stories. The land god is lower-ranking but intimate, handling day-to-day territory and even death registration. Together they show how the afterlife becomes accountable to place, not only to universal judgment.

[[06-soushenji-ershisi-xiao-zhe-kou-guo-ganbao-bu-bei-536821044]] adds [[JiangZiwen|蒋子文]] as a zhiguai local-cult case. The episode presents him as a dead figure whose demand for worship, threat of disaster, temple recognition, offended-daughter stories, and later underworld association make local deity governance narrative rather than only doctrinal.

## Key Claims
- Folk religion often maps divine authority onto familiar territorial offices.
- Local gods make cosmic order usable by households, villages, cities, and neighborhoods.
- Historical figures can become local deities when moral reputation and civic memory attach to place.
- Low rank does not mean low importance; intimate gods may matter most in ordinary ritual life.
- A local cult can become legible through stories of posthumous demand, disaster pressure, temple recognition, and offended ritual authority.
- EP249 adds that a temple can be a public-governance object: sacred authority, money, committee seats, routes, and lineage competition can be bound together.
