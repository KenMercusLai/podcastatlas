# Battery Supplier Diversification / 动力电池供应商多元化

## Definition
Battery supplier diversification is the automaker strategy of shifting some orders, equity support, or cooperation toward non-dominant battery suppliers to reduce cost, increase negotiating leverage, and lower dependence on one incumbent.

## Current Synthesis
The source frames Chinese automakers' move beyond [[CATL|宁德时代]] as both a cost and bargaining response. [[LiAuto|理想汽车]] invests in [[SunwodaPower|欣旺达动力]], while [[Xiaomi]] cooperates with Sunwoda Power and [[CALB|中创新航]]. The tradeoff is that lower prices may come with weaker consistency, brand effect, or quality confidence compared with CATL.

## Key Claims
- Supplier diversification can be a bargaining tool even when the dominant supplier remains technically strong.
- Automakers under margin pressure have stronger incentives to test second-line suppliers.
- Cost differences at the battery-pack level can be material for vehicle pricing.
- The strategy is constrained by quality consistency, safety, brand trust, and production reliability.

## Evidence
- Li Auto evidence - [[tulasi-zishengtang-qisu-pingjia-shangchao-aoleqi-alo-qianyue-wangyibo-1014854463]] says Li Auto invested in Sunwoda Power and became its second-largest shareholder.
- Xiaomi evidence - [[tulasi-zishengtang-qisu-pingjia-shangchao-aoleqi-alo-qianyue-wangyibo-1014854463]] says Xiaomi announced cooperation with Sunwoda Power and CALB.
- Incumbent context - [[tulasi-zishengtang-qisu-pingjia-shangchao-aoleqi-alo-qianyue-wangyibo-1014854463]] says CATL led global installed power-battery volume in the first half of the year with roughly 40% market share and more than RMB40 billion net profit.
- Tradeoff evidence - [[tulasi-zishengtang-qisu-pingjia-shangchao-aoleqi-alo-qianyue-wangyibo-1014854463]] cites industry views that second-line batteries may be about 5% cheaper but still face consistency and brand gaps.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not show whether the new suppliers can meet automaker quality thresholds at scale. It also does not imply CATL's dominance has ended; diversification can strengthen buyer leverage without displacing the incumbent.
