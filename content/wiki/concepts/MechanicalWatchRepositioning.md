# Mechanical Watch Repositioning

Mechanical watch repositioning is the shift from selling wristwatches mainly as practical timekeeping tools to selling them as durable symbols of taste, achievement, engineering, craft, and identity. In [[rolex-1]], the quartz crisis makes the distinction concrete: [[Seiko]] and later low-cost Asian manufacturing made accurate timekeeping cheap, so [[Rolex]] could not win by pretending mechanical watches were functionally superior.

The source argues that Rolex survived because it helped mechanical watches become a different category. Accuracy, waterproofing, and automatic winding still mattered, but increasingly as proof of permanence and quality rather than as the main reason to own a watch.

## Key Claims
- A category can survive functional obsolescence if customers find a new job for the product.
- The old wristwatch market was about telling time; the new high-end mechanical market is about meaning, status, reliability, and continuity.
- Functional proof remains useful when it supports symbolic value instead of competing directly with cheaper utility.
- The [[AppleWatch|Apple Watch]] and phones make the functional alibi even weaker, which pushes mechanical watches closer to jewelry and identity goods.
