# Distributed Volunteer Governance

## Definition
Distributed volunteer governance is the development and enforcement of shared rules by geographically dispersed contributors who supply monitoring, correction, dispute resolution, and institutional legitimacy without ordinary managerial command.

## Current Synthesis
Wikipedia's early scarcity made governance endogenous. With little money for staff moderators, volunteers repaired vandalism across time zones, discussed blocking and acceptable content, and developed principles such as neutral point of view. Visible failures such as the false John Seigenthaler biography then became inputs to stronger sourcing rules. The case shows that communities can scale oversight, but only through accumulated norms, permissions, technical tools, and credible procedures for disagreement.

## Key Claims
- Geographic distribution can turn continuous exposure into continuous oversight rather than only coordination cost.
- Volunteer moderators and editors are operating infrastructure, not an optional audience layer.
- Rules gain legitimacy when contributors can discuss and revise them, though deliberation can also reproduce power imbalances.
- Public failures can improve governance when they produce specific procedural changes rather than reputation management alone.
- Volunteer governance requires tools for reversion, blocking, sourcing, discussion, and escalation; goodwill is insufficient.

## Evidence
- Continuous repair - [[vmp5702291377-vmp5702291377]] says volunteers in other time zones often detected vandalism, blocked offenders, and repaired pages before Wales intervened.
- Rule formation - [[vmp5702291377-vmp5702291377]] describes community development of moderation practices, content boundaries, and neutral point of view.
- Failure-driven policy - [[vmp5702291377-vmp5702291377]] links the Seigenthaler false biography to stronger sourcing standards for living people.
- Leadership boundary - [[vmp5702291377-vmp5702291377]] has Wales describe persuasion and respectful relationships as necessary when contributors cannot be commanded.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
One founder account cannot establish that volunteer governance distributes voice or workload fairly, resolves every dispute, or scales without professional staff and infrastructure. Global coverage may improve response time while leaving topic, language, demographic, and power asymmetries. The Seigenthaler case shows that correction after publication can still impose serious harm.

## What Changed
- Established a governance concept separating volunteer scale from automatic fairness or reliability.
