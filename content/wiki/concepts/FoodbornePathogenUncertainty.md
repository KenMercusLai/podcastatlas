# Foodborne Pathogen Uncertainty

Foodborne pathogen uncertainty is the scientific limit on prevention described in [[big-lettuce-meets-small-intestine]]. The episode argues that even with farm rules, inspections, and traceback work, scientists may not fully know how a pathogen moved into a crop, where it persisted, or which test would have caught it early enough.

The source gives two examples. A salmonella outbreak involving peaches was traced to a California farm near a poultry house, but investigators did not prove the exact route into the peaches. The current [[Cyclospora]] case is also presented as uncertain because the parasite's spread, hiding places, and best testing methods remain poorly understood.

## Key Claims
- Unknown transmission routes limit how precisely regulators can write prevention rules.
- Testing limits mean a pathogen may be hard to detect before raw produce reaches consumers.
- Scientific uncertainty does not remove responsibility, but it makes zero-risk promises unrealistic.
- The concept explains why [[FreshProduceSafetyRegulation]] still leaves residual risk even after the [[FoodSafetyModernizationAct]].
