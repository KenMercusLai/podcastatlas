# Principled Non-Killing Under Risk / 风险中的不杀原则

## Definition

Principled non-killing under risk is a refusal to kill that remains binding when lethal force would be advantageous, thereby making restraint morally substantive while shifting danger and responsibility onto other available means of protection.

## Current Synthesis

The [[GreatDesertGuLong|《大沙漠》]] discussion treats [[ChuLiuxiang|楚留香]]'s non-killing rule as meaningful precisely because it survives encounters with enemies such as [[ShiGuanyin|石观音]]. A rule retained only when nothing important is at stake would be posture rather than principle.

The same test exposes a responsibility problem. A hero's chosen restraint is not borne by that hero alone when [[HuTiehua|胡铁花]], [[JiBingyan|姬冰雁]], hostages, or future victims remain exposed. Non-killing therefore requires more than personal purity: containment, incapacitation, rescue, lawful judgment, escape, or some other credible means must address continuing danger. When the story instead relies on another character or sudden intervention to remove the threat, ethical consistency can become narrative cost-shifting.

## Key Claims

- A non-killing rule becomes a principle only when it constrains action under genuine pressure.
- Personal restraint and protection of others are distinct obligations that can conflict.
- Refusal to kill does not by itself resolve containment, rescue, or future-harm duties.
- Alternative success conditions can preserve restraint without pretending danger has disappeared.
- Plot convenience becomes visible when an outside actor repeatedly bears the lethal or incapacitating work the hero refuses.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept is derived from a fictional case, not a general doctrine for policing, war, self-defense, or emergency ethics. The episode does not provide a complete account of available containment options, and fictional plot design can exaggerate both the clarity of lethal choices and the reliability of alternatives. Non-killing should not be equated with passivity, nor should protective responsibility automatically make killing necessary.

## What Changed

- Created the concept by separating the integrity of retained restraint from responsibility for danger transferred to others.
