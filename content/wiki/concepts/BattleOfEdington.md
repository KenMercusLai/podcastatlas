# Battle of Edington

## Definition

The Battle of Edington was [[AlfredTheGreat|Alfred the Great]]'s 878 victory over [[Guthrum]]'s Danish army, followed by a siege at Chippenham and negotiated withdrawal and baptism.

## Current Synthesis

Edington matters in the episode because it converts Alfred's survival at Athelney into recoverable political capacity. Alfred summoned supporters to Egbert's Stone, caught the Danish army near Edington, defeated it, and then maintained pressure through a siege rather than treating battlefield success as sufficient.

The result did not end Viking power. It created terms under which Guthrum withdrew and accepted baptism, while other forces and later settlements left substantial territory under Danish law. Edington is therefore a hinge between emergency warfare and the institutional rebuilding summarized by [[AngloSaxonStateFormation]].

## Key Claims

- Edington reversed the immediate threat created by the Chippenham attack and Alfred's flight.
- Alfred's mobilization and pursuit show that political recovery preceded the battlefield victory.
- The post-battle siege converted tactical success into enforceable negotiated terms.
- Guthrum's baptism connected military defeat to a new form of Christian political legitimacy.
- The victory bought room for rebuilding but did not itself create a united England or eliminate Viking rule.

## Evidence

- Recovery and mobilization - [[251-alfred-the-great-return-of-the-king-part-2-glt9621369023]] follows Alfred from Athelney to the summons at Egbert's Stone.
- Battle and siege - [[251-alfred-the-great-return-of-the-king-part-2-glt9621369023]] describes victory near Edington and roughly a fortnight of pressure at Chippenham.
- Negotiated outcome - [[251-alfred-the-great-return-of-the-king-part-2-glt9621369023]] links the settlement to withdrawal and Guthrum's baptism.
- Institutional consequence - [[251-alfred-the-great-return-of-the-king-part-2-glt9621369023]] treats the following respite as the opening for fortified and administrative reform.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source offers a narrative overview rather than a military reconstruction. Exact force sizes, tactics, locations, terms, and the relationship between battlefield compulsion and genuine religious change are not established. Later English unification should not be projected backward into the battle's immediate outcome.

## What Changed

- Established Edington as a bridge from military recovery to negotiated coexistence and state rebuilding.
