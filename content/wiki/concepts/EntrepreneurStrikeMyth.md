# Entrepreneur Strike Myth / 企业家罢工神话

Entrepreneur Strike Myth / 企业家罢工神话 is the political-economic fable that [[09-atelasi-songsongjian-anlande-zhishi-shuangwen-zuojia-ma-543277898]] draws from [[AtlasShrugged|Atlas Shrugged / 《阿特拉斯耸耸肩》]]. The source says the novel's original "strike" idea is inverted: the people withdrawing are not workers but capitalists, entrepreneurs, scientists, and other creators who decide that a society hostile to creation no longer deserves their productive effort.

The myth is not presented as a literal policy forecast. It is a sharp image for a recurring anxiety: when institutions moralize against builders, seize their gains, punish competence, or force them to serve people who despise them, productive capacity can leave, hide, or stop renewing itself. In the episode, John Galt's valley is the pure allegorical endpoint of that withdrawal.

The source keeps the image morally bounded. It does not treat all rich people as creators or all businesspeople as admirable; it distinguishes value creation from collusion, privilege, and power dependency. That limit ties the concept to [[CreatorParasiteMoralFrame]] rather than to a blanket celebration of wealth.

## Key Claims
- The myth expresses the social cost of treating creators and entrepreneurs only as exploitable resources.
- It is most useful as a literary image for institutional incentives and moral language, not as a complete account of real economies.
- The episode uses the myth to push back against automatic shame around money and lawful wealth creation.
- The same myth becomes dangerous when detached from social interdependence, luck, public goods, and ordinary vulnerability.
