# Model as Product / 模型即产品

## Definition

Model as product is the view that in an AI-native company the model's capability, behavior, cost, and tradeoffs constitute the primary product, while applications and interfaces mainly deliver that capability to users and supply feedback about where it is useful.

## Current Synthesis

The concept does not make application design irrelevant. It changes the dependency: when model intelligence improves rapidly, product success may depend more directly on absorbing new capability, choosing the right model tradeoffs, and constructing effective user access than on preserving a fixed interface. Consumer apps, APIs, coding agents, voice tools, and video products become different delivery and learning surfaces for an evolving capability core.

Because inference cost and optimization objectives remain finite, one model is unlikely to dominate every scenario equally. Dialogue, code, image, video, latency, price, and safety impose different choices. Model-as-product therefore implies a portfolio and evaluation discipline rather than a single universal-model claim.

## Key Claims

- Core model behavior can be the primary unit of product value in an AI-native company.
- Applications remain important as delivery, feedback, distribution, and monetization surfaces.
- Rapid model progress makes product roadmaps unusually dependent on capability absorption.
- Cost, latency, modality, and scenario needs prevent every model from maximizing every quality at once.
- Product differentiation can come from capability selection and workflow fit, not only interface novelty.
- Paying use remains a stronger commercialization test than attention alone.

## Evidence

- Product hierarchy - [[lhqbap06a7-s4dxosnv6gdx1zesx-lhqbap06a7-s4dxosnv6gdx1zesx]] attributes to Yan the view that the model is the true product and conventional products are channels through which users reach it.
- Non-universality - [[lhqbap06a7-s4dxosnv6gdx1zesx-lhqbap06a7-s4dxosnv6gdx1zesx]] says models make different choices across dialogue, code, image, video, cost, and capability, resisting a simple one-model-takes-all conclusion.
- Delivery and payment - [[lhqbap06a7-s4dxosnv6gdx1zesx-lhqbap06a7-s4dxosnv6gdx1zesx]] contrasts Talkie usage with video/API revenue and treats genuine payment, first visible overseas, as a central test.
- Organizational effect - [[lhqbap06a7-s4dxosnv6gdx1zesx-lhqbap06a7-s4dxosnv6gdx1zesx]] links model progress to internally grown product managers, technical fluency across functions, and the blurring of product, development, and algorithm work.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The framing can understate distribution, trust, workflow context, safety, service reliability, community, and switching costs. A technically stronger model does not automatically create a better product, and user demand may attach to an application or relationship rather than to the underlying model. The evidence is one founder's account of an unusually model-centric company.

## What Changed

- Created a concept separating model capability as product core from applications as access and learning surfaces.
- Added inference tradeoffs and willingness to pay as limits on a universal-model interpretation.
- Preserved product design, distribution, trust, and workflow context as explicit qualifications.
