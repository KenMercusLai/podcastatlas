# 交通枢纽郡控制 / Transit-Hub Commandery Control

## Definition
交通枢纽郡控制 / transit-hub commandery control is the statecraft problem where a commandery matters not only for its own territory but because routes, local elites, nearby rebels, and frontier-facing military movement make its governor selection strategically consequential.

## Current Synthesis
Hanji 1070 creates the concept through Cao Cao's concern over [[HedongCommandery|河东郡]]. After Cao Cao's northern victories, the western field is still unstable: [[GaoGanLateHan|高干]] rebels again, [[ZhangShengLateHan|张盛]] gathers forces around Xiaoshan and Mianchi, [[ZhangYanHedongLateHan|张衍]] responds in Hongnong, and [[WeiGuLateHan|卫固]] plus [[FanXianLateHan|范先]] are suspected of using [[WangYiHedongLateHan|王邑]]'s retention request to protect hidden alignment with Gao Gan. Cao Cao therefore treats Hedong as a control problem rather than a routine office vacancy.

The appointment of [[DuJiLateHan|杜畿]] makes the concept administrative. [[XunYu|荀彧]] recommends Du Ji because the role requires judgment suited to a complex local field. [[ZhongYao|钟繇]]'s failed effort to make Wang Yi hand over cleanly shows that the governor choice has to overcome existing local interests before governance can even begin.

## Key Claims
- Some commanderies are strategic because route control and neighboring disturbances amplify local office decisions.
- Local elite preference for an incumbent can be a warning sign when it aligns with nearby rebel pressure.
- Appointment quality becomes state capacity when the officeholder must manage collusion, handoff resistance, and regional military movement.
- A post-victory ruler still needs administrative control over hubs that connect newly conquered and still-unstable regions.
- The concept differs from ordinary local governance because the commandery's transport position raises the cost of a bad appointment.

- Local elite risk: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1070-jiaochu-shaxue-weimeng-quan-buxia-panjiang-caocao-lqvyfglj3qefhyxyebfvvda8om5o|Hanji 1070]] links Wei Gu and Fan Xian's Wang Yi retention request to hidden coordination with Gao Gan.
- Disturbance environment: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1070-jiaochu-shaxue-weimeng-quan-buxia-panjiang-caocao-lqvyfglj3qefhyxyebfvvda8om5o|Hanji 1070]] groups Gao Gan, Zhang Sheng, and Zhang Yan into a wider western instability chain.
- Fitted appointment: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1070-jiaochu-shaxue-weimeng-quan-buxia-panjiang-caocao-lqvyfglj3qefhyxyebfvvda8om5o|Hanji 1070]] says Xun Yu recommends Du Ji and Cao Cao appoints him Hedong administrator.
- Handoff friction: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1070-jiaochu-shaxue-weimeng-quan-buxia-panjiang-caocao-lqvyfglj3qefhyxyebfvvda8om5o|Hanji 1070]] says Wang Yi resists handoff and takes the seal to Xudu.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept currently has one source case. Hanji 1070 sets up Du Ji's appointment but does not yet narrate whether his later Hedong governance succeeds, so the page should be revised when follow-up episodes describe outcomes. The source also treats Wei Gu and Fan Xian's collusion as an interpretive warning, so the concept should not turn every local retention request into proof of rebellion.
