## Definition
Happiness-based de-escalation / 幸福者退让原则 is Xiao Kuai's source-scoped frame for yielding in conflict because the person with more to preserve can keep their own happiness by refusing escalation.

## Current Synthesis
The source makes de-escalation morally and psychologically ambiguous. Xiao Kuai presents restraint during road conflict and after a drunk-driving crash as a way to avoid becoming worse off than he already is. He treats retreat as an active choice: if the other person becomes angrier or more degrading, he can keep his own state by not joining the descent.

The panel repeatedly challenges this interpretation. Others ask whether the same behavior is really courage, kindness, calculation, or fear rationalized as happiness. That challenge is part of the concept: happiness-based de-escalation is not automatically noble. It is a self-protective explanation whose value depends on actual power, risk, cost, and whether retreat protects dignity or erodes legitimate boundary-setting.

## Key Claims
- A person may retreat from conflict to preserve their own life state rather than to concede moral ground.
- De-escalation can be rational when escalation would add risk without repairing the original harm.
- Observers may read the same restraint as cowardice, kindness, superiority, or self-protection.
- Material and emotional asymmetry matter: the "happier" or safer party may be able to absorb a loss that another person could not.
- The concept should remain contested because retreat can protect peace or excuse avoidant boundary failure.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source gives personal stories, not legal or safety advice. In some conflicts, retreat can preserve safety; in others, it can leave harm unaddressed or shift costs to victims and bystanders. The concept should not be used to pressure people to forgive, underclaim, or stay silent after real harm.
