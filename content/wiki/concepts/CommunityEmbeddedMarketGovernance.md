## Definition

Community-embedded market governance is the long-term coordination of managers, vendors, residents, and local services so a food market remains useful to its actual neighborhood rather than functioning only as rental property, a renovated facade, or a visitor destination.

## Current Synthesis

The bounded source argues that market renewal is operational and relational. Managers need to understand what nearby households buy, retain vendors with product and customer knowledge, adapt services to local age and household structure, and solve succession, branding, sanitation, access, and public-use problems with the community.

Destination design and mixed services can help, but they are tools rather than universal templates. A dining hall may matter in an older neighborhood; a library, fitness room, food class, or family activity may matter elsewhere. The governing test is whether changes support everyday provisioning and broader participation without displacing affordable goods or capable vendors.

## Key Claims

- Market management shapes the food system through tenant choice, rent, service design, sanitation, access, and vendor support.
- Skilled vendors are institutional assets because they carry sourcing, product, preparation, and customer knowledge.
- Community consultation should precede copying a standardized renovation or service package.
- All-age use can broaden continuity through food education, family activity, cooking, and ordinary shopping.
- Destination branding is compatible with community function only when it does not displace resident provisioning or affordability.

## Evidence

- Management shift: [[486d1fea-dd57-4ff1-8de9-cfea1f9de16a-486d1fea-dd57-4ff1-8de9-cfea1f9de16a]] uses Suzhou's Caixiang Market to argue that managers must move beyond rent collection and treat capable local-goods vendors as core assets.
- Community-specific services: [[486d1fea-dd57-4ff1-8de9-cfea1f9de16a-486d1fea-dd57-4ff1-8de9-cfea1f9de16a]] contrasts landmark-style renovation with neighborhood centers and warns that older and younger communities need different service mixes.
- Continuity and participation: [[486d1fea-dd57-4ff1-8de9-cfea1f9de16a-486d1fea-dd57-4ff1-8de9-cfea1f9de16a]] describes consultation, trademark help, apprenticeships, family learning, food education, cooking, and youth activities as possible extensions of market use.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source does not provide comparative budgets, rents, vendor turnover, footfall, resident surveys, or long-run outcomes for the renovation cases. Public, state-owned, private, cooperative, and informal markets face different authority and financing constraints. Added services can also raise costs, accelerate touristification, or crowd out affordable food retail if governance does not protect everyday use.

## What Changed

- Established a co-governance model centered on neighborhood fit, vendor capability, and everyday provisioning.
- Qualified all-age programming and destination design as context-dependent tools rather than goals in themselves.
