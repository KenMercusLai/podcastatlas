## Definition

四川战乱人口毁伤 / Sichuan war devastation is Hanji 388-4's frame for using repeated Sichuan catastrophes to show how war, famine, epidemic, and social collapse can destroy a regional population far beyond the court-level story of dynastic transition.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 388-4 makes [[Sichuan|四川]] the episode's main case for "离乱人不如太平犬." It first cites Song-Yuan warfare as a moment when the population of Shu was said to have been devastated almost beyond survival. It then turns to the late-Ming and early-Qing transition, where the host links [[ZhangXianzhong|张献忠]], other armies, famine, epidemic, and tiger disaster to the claim that roughly nine-tenths of the people died.

The concept is useful because it keeps dynastic war from being read only through rulers, generals, and legitimacy. In this source, the evidence that matters is the ruined landscape: Li Qiande's reported scene of brambles, empty habitation, wild animals, and mutilated survivors becomes a moral image of social collapse.

## Key Claims

- Regional war devastation should include indirect mortality from famine, epidemic, ecological disruption, and social breakdown, not only battlefield deaths.
- Sichuan is used as a repeated-catastrophe case across Song-Yuan and Ming-Qing transitions.
- The episode treats Zhang Xianzhong as important but not as a single-cause explanation for Sichuan's late-Ming and early-Qing collapse.
- Descriptions of ruined landscapes and injured survivors function as evidence of social-system failure, not just dramatic detail.
- Exact demographic percentages remain source-scoped and require stronger corroboration before becoming a settled wiki-wide statistic.

- Late-Ming and early-Qing catastrophe: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-388-4-jiemi-gudai-zuican-de-sheng-ta-ceng-si-le-90-baixing-luivvup1twdt6t6dgow9xnfwirbu|Hanji 388-4]] attributes the late devastation to Zhang Xianzhong, other troops, famine, epidemic, and tigers.
- Landscape evidence: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-388-4-jiemi-gudai-zuican-de-sheng-ta-ceng-si-le-90-baixing-luivvup1twdt6t6dgow9xnfwirbu|Hanji 388-4]] cites Li Qiande's account of a depopulated Sichuan with brambles, wild animals, and visibly injured survivors.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source is a short popular-history episode and does not adjudicate demographic data, archival basis, or scholarly disputes over responsibility. The concept should therefore preserve the source's multi-cause structure while keeping the 90% figure and specific descriptions source-scoped.
