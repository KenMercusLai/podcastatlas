# Creative Fatigue

## Definition
Creative fatigue is the decline in an advertisement's response as an audience repeatedly encounters the same or closely similar asset.

## Current Synthesis
The current source treats fatigue as the operating reason advertisers need a renewable supply of creative material. New variants can restore novelty and create experiments, but volume is not itself value: useful variation must preserve brand identity, be reviewed, reach enough audience for interpretable evidence, and connect to outcomes appropriate to the advertiser's maturity. Generating more material can also worsen information overload and shift attention toward activity rather than meaning.

## Key Claims
- Repeated exposure can weaken response, making creative supply a recurring campaign constraint.
- Variant generation supports experimentation only when volume, audience, time, and metrics are sufficient for interpretation.
- Relevant measures change by business stage, from awareness and click-through toward conversion and return on investment.
- AI can lower production friction by generating assets or recombining existing brand libraries.
- Novelty can regain attention while also contributing to the broader content surplus that produced the scarcity of attention.
- Solving fatigue without brand guardrails can optimize short-term response at the cost of coherent identity.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source gives no underlying campaign data, causal design, or cross-customer comparison. Its jewelry-volume and evaluation-window examples are founder-reported and should not be generalized across industries. A response decline may also reflect targeting, offer, distribution, seasonality, competition, or measurement error rather than the asset alone.

## What Changed
- Established creative fatigue as a measurable but multi-causal advertising constraint rather than a simple demand for more content.
- Added the qualification that novelty supply can worsen attention overload and brand drift.
