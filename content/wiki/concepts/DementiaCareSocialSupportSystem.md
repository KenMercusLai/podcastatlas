# Dementia Care Social Support System / 失智照护社会支持系统

## Definition
Dementia care social support system / 失智照护社会支持系统 is the coordinated support layer that joins family care, trained workers, community services, medical knowledge, institutions, insurance, and dignity-centered environments for people with dementia.

## Current Synthesis
The sources treat dementia care as too complex for either family sacrifice or medical treatment alone. Dementia can change memory, orientation, behavior, movement, recognition, and daily function over years, so support must work across the person with dementia, the family caregiver, the community, and the payment system.

The current judgment is that China-facing dementia care needs shared infrastructure before families can stop functioning as isolated crisis managers. The fieldwork source emphasizes trained workers, targeted community services, non-drug intervention spaces, acceptable institutions, and financing. VOL.133 adds an earlier and lighter-touch layer: informed neighbors, public-facing staff, online peers, family mobilization, communication changes, and safer homes can support people before or alongside formal services.

## Key Claims
- Dementia care should be designed as a public support system, not only a household morality test.
- Family caregivers remain important, but they need knowledge, respite, professional backup, and financing.
- Specialized dementia services differ from ordinary active-elder activities because they require trained staff, safe places, and non-drug interventions.
- Public awareness can turn banks, shops, streets, and neighborhoods into practical inclusion points without turning bystanders into diagnosticians.
- Care environments should preserve life routines and resident identity instead of reducing the person to a managed patient.
- Insurance expansion helps only when assessment rules, service supply, labor quality, and affordability line up.

## Evidence
- System map - [[manchang-de-gaobie-shizhizheng-zhaohuzhe-yu-zhongjiang-laoqu-de-women-lscotoglqyoznytqatlefdwv3jez]] has Tang Yong list individual brain training, family caregivers, age-friendly communities, medical systems, insurance, and care systems as parts of the response.
- Shenzhen service gap - [[manchang-de-gaobie-shizhizheng-zhaohuzhe-yu-zhongjiang-laoqu-de-women-lscotoglqyoznytqatlefdwv3jez]] says Shenzhen has facilities and young demographics but relatively few targeted dementia services, trained workers, and affordable specialized places.
- Environment model - [[manchang-de-gaobie-shizhizheng-zhaohuzhe-yu-zhongjiang-laoqu-de-women-lscotoglqyoznytqatlefdwv3jez]] uses [[HogeweykDementiaVillage|Hogeweyk]] to frame care as embedded daily life.
- Financing boundary - [[manchang-de-gaobie-shizhizheng-zhaohuzhe-yu-zhongjiang-laoqu-de-women-lscotoglqyoznytqatlefdwv3jez]] describes long-term-care insurance as assessment-based and service-oriented rather than a universal cash answer.
- Community inclusion - [[vol-133-nin-haijide-wo-shi-shui-ma-qing-tingzhi-zheyang-de-tiwen-nineng-zuo-de-haiyou-henduo-lmnn6rq16uvpkpmnimxfd5xqbr4a]] uses street, supermarket, bank, and neighborhood situations to show how informed public help can reduce exclusion.
- Family and peer support - [[vol-133-nin-haijide-wo-shi-shui-ma-qing-tingzhi-zheyang-de-tiwen-nineng-zuo-de-haiyou-henduo-lmnn6rq16uvpkpmnimxfd5xqbr4a]] urges families to mobilize wider networks and describes online peer support for relatives.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence consists of two podcast summaries: one centered on Tang Yong's fieldwork and Shenzhen examples, the other on clinician and advocate public education. It does not independently measure nationwide service capacity, the reach or effectiveness of public-awareness programs, regional insurance implementation, or the cost-effectiveness of particular care models.

## What Changed
- Added public-awareness, customer-service, neighborhood, online-peer, and family-mobilization layers to the formal care-system account.
