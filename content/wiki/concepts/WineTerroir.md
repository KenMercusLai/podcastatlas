# Wine Terroir / 葡萄酒风土

Wine terroir / 葡萄酒风土 is the source's frame for how [[Wine|wine]] carries place. In [[sanlian-chuantai-shen-ci-gei-ren-putao-shijie-weixun-le-585125156]], terroir includes soil, geography, weather, ripeness, grape variety, and surrounding smells, but the conversation keeps expanding it into "风土人情": people, habits, succession, and winemaking choices are part of what a wine becomes.

This matters because the episode treats terroir as both learnable and unstable. [[SOMDocumentary|SOM]] illustrates how blind tasting can infer region and variety from structure, aroma, acidity, tannin, and maturity. [[DropsOfGod|《神之水滴》]] shows how terroir becomes story. But the source also notes that wines from the same estate or region can change across decades because climate, technique, and people change.

## Key Claims
- Terroir is not only natural background; human practice can be part of the tasted place.
- Blind tasting works best with typical regions and varieties, and becomes harder when styles converge or winemakers break expectations.
- A region's reputation can lag behind changes in farming, climate, market preference, or generational handoff.
- Terroir language is useful when it points back to material conditions, not when it becomes status jargon.
