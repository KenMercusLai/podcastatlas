# European Integration As War Prevention

## Definition

European integration as war prevention is the bounded claim that shared institutions, political commitments, supply chains, and industrial production can reduce incentives for conflict among participating European states, without making trade alone a universal guarantee of peace.

## Current Synthesis

The strongest positive case is internal and institutional. [[139-cong-zuo-ri-de-shi-jie-dao-ru-jin-de-ou-zhou-mei-hao-nian-dai-belle-epoque-ta-hai-neng-hui-lai-ma-ltvsgov8ht9-l7yp9qamdgjmyrf]] uses [[Airbus]] to show production distributed across countries so that economic value is jointly made rather than nationally enclosed. [[SchengenArea|Schengen]], shared mobility, and the [[EuropeanUnion]] deepen that political community, though access and identification with it remain unequal.

The limiting case is external interdependence without shared security assumptions. Both sources point to Europe's energy relationship with [[Russia]]: dependence did not prevent the invasion of [[Ukraine]], and sanctions or supply rupture quickly turned trade ties into liabilities. [[290-2022-a-history-glt4625765030]] sharpens the distinction by showing that unequal energy exposure can constrain allies differently.

Integration can therefore lower internal conflict risk while leaving Europe vulnerable to external coercion, energy shock, and reliance on [[NATO]] and the [[UnitedStates]]. Institutions, political trust, and security alignment do more explanatory work than commerce by itself.

## Key Claims

- Industrial interdependence is most plausibly peace-supporting when embedded in shared institutions and political commitments.
- Distributed production can create mutual stakes in continuity rather than nationally enclosed gain.
- Trade or energy dependence without shared security assumptions does not reliably prevent war.
- Integration can reduce conflict among members while leaving them exposed to external coercion and supply disruption.
- Unequal dependence can divide partners because the economic cost of common policy is not evenly distributed.

## Evidence

- Institutional production: [[139-cong-zuo-ri-de-shi-jie-dao-ru-jin-de-ou-zhou-mei-hao-nian-dai-belle-epoque-ta-hai-neng-hui-lai-ma-ltvsgov8ht9-l7yp9qamdgjmyrf]] uses Airbus and European supply chains as the main positive example.
- Unequal integration: [[139-cong-zuo-ri-de-shi-jie-dao-ru-jin-de-ou-zhou-mei-hao-nian-dai-belle-epoque-ta-hai-neng-hui-lai-ma-ltvsgov8ht9-l7yp9qamdgjmyrf]] shows mobility and European identity varying with passport, class, language, and location.
- External limit: both [[139-cong-zuo-ri-de-shi-jie-dao-ru-jin-de-ou-zhou-mei-hao-nian-dai-belle-epoque-ta-hai-neng-hui-lai-ma-ltvsgov8ht9-l7yp9qamdgjmyrf]] and [[290-2022-a-history-glt4625765030]] use Russian energy dependence and the Ukraine war to reject a universal commercial-peace claim.
- Asymmetric costs: [[290-2022-a-history-glt4625765030]] contrasts Europe's Russian-energy exposure with the United States' lower dependence and gas-export capacity.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The sources do not establish that Airbus, Schengen, trade, or EU institutions independently caused peace, nor do they compare integration against NATO deterrence, democratization, historical memory, or American security guarantees. “Europe” contains large national and class differences. Russia's invasion does not disprove all interdependence arguments, but it shows that commerce without shared institutions and security expectations is an insufficient peace mechanism.
