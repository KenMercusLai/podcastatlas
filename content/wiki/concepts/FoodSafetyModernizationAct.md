# Food Safety Modernization Act

The Food Safety Modernization Act is the 2010 U.S. food-safety law highlighted in [[big-lettuce-meets-small-intestine]]. The episode presents it as the major statutory response to produce outbreaks, especially the 2006 spinach E. coli outbreak that showed the [[FoodAndDrugAdministration|FDA]] had rules for processors but not official farm-safety rules for fruit and vegetable growers.

The law matters because it shifted the FDA from a mostly reactive posture toward farm-level prevention. In the source, that means authority to inspect farms and enforce standards around worker hygiene, clean water, compost, manure, and animal feces in fields.

## Key Claims
- The episode describes the law as the biggest dramatic change for the FDA in nearly a century.
- It gave the FDA authority to inspect farms and enforce produce-safety standards.
- Rulemaking was slow: routine farm-safety inspections began only in 2019.
- Some water standards were finalized only recently in the source's account, and some farms do not have to comply until 2027.
- The law's practical effect depends on [[FarmSafetyInspectionCapacity]], [[ImportedProduceVerification]], and scientific knowledge about pathogens.
