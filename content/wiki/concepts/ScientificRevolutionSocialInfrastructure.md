# Scientific Revolution Social Infrastructure

[[ep259-yu-miao-shifu-mantan-xu-guangqi-sibainian-qian-ruhe-rangren-biancongming-lkirczxnx8skbbfpmz5-m-mivs4e]] extends the concept into early modern China through [[LateMingWesternLearning|晚明西学东渐]]. [[XuGuangqi|徐光启]]'s encounter with [[MatteoRicci|利玛窦]] shows that a scientific revolution is not just a better idea arriving: it requires translation partnerships, mathematical vocabulary, maps, teachers, calendar offices, military and agricultural use cases, and enough institutional continuity for methods to survive.

[[ep248-zai-gaoyuan-yu-dixia-tanxun-zhongguo-da-kexue-xiangmu-lidn7xxkcw4-66fw-y50fqqacwo]] extends the concept from early modern science into contemporary [[BigScienceInfrastructure|大科学基础设施]]. [[MiaoQian|苗千]] explicitly challenges the lone-genius story by showing that modern frontier physics depends on shared instruments, extreme sites, cleanrooms, long data accumulation, international teams, and chief scientists who also act as [[ScientistAsProjectOrganizer|project organizers]].

Scientific revolution social infrastructure is the source's frame for why [[IsaacNewton|Isaac Newton / 牛顿]] should not be read only as isolated genius. In [[26-niudun-yu-weibi-zhizaozhe-keneng-shi-sineipu-de-yuanxing-580065317]], Newton's breakthroughs depend on accumulated navigation data, astronomy, geometry, mechanical philosophy, instrument-making, correspondence, [[RoyalSociety|Royal Society]] networks, and people such as [[EdmondHalley|Edmond Halley]], [[JohnLocke|John Locke]], [[RobertHooke|Robert Hooke]], [[RobertBoyle|Robert Boyle]], and [[ChristiaanHuygens|Christiaan Huygens]].

The concept does not reduce Newton's originality. It says originality becomes historically possible and legible through shared problems, measurement systems, publication channels, instruments, and trusted evaluators. This keeps the wiki's science-history branch away from both master worship and simple anti-genius leveling.
