# Small Error Systemic Failure / 细小错误系统性放大

Small error systemic failure / 细小错误系统性放大 is the Hanji 924 frame that a small flaw can become total failure when the wider system is already under stress. In [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-924-1-de-cuowu-100-de-shibai-lmeo7vufm6ib5lni6p49rponazut]], the host's "1%的错误=100%的失败" line is not treated as a literal metric; it is the episode's way of reading how palace corruption, bad information filters, and delayed correction interact during the [[YellowTurbansLateHan|Yellow Turban]] emergency.

The mechanism is cumulative. [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]] can appoint [[HeJin|何进]], release banned party figures, and send [[LuZhiLateHan|卢植]], [[HuangfuSongLateHan|皇甫嵩]], and [[ZhuJunLateHan|朱儁]] to fight, but the same court lets the [[TenAttendantsLateHan|十常侍]] protect themselves, recode warnings, and kill or imprison corrective speakers. A minor-seeming access advantage therefore becomes a state-capacity failure.

## Key Claims
- A small error becomes systemic when it is embedded in a stressed institution rather than isolated to one actor.
- In Hanji 924, the dangerous "small error" is not one mistaken order but a repeated tolerance for palace access capture.
- Corrective actions can be real yet still insufficient if the institution keeps punishing the people who name the deeper problem.
- The concept overlaps with [[CourtFeedbackCollapse|君臣反馈失灵]] and [[PalaceAccessMonopoly|宫廷接触垄断]], but it emphasizes amplification under crisis pressure.
