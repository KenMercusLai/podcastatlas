## Definition

Macrohistorical institutional analysis reads individual lives and small events inside long-run systems of finance, land, military supply, administration, information, organization, and technical execution.

## Current Synthesis

The episode attributes this method to [[RayHuang|黄仁宇]] and [[WanliFifteenYear|《万历十五年》]]. It does not eliminate biography; it changes what biography is for. Six contrasting lives become tests of whether personal will, forceful reform, accommodation, integrity, technical competence, or dissent can produce durable capacity inside the [[MingDynasty|明朝]].

The method gains explanatory power by linking action to system, but it risks making selected cases illustrate a predetermined arc. Good use therefore requires a visible boundary among documented event, causal inference, analogy, and historical judgment.

## Key Claims

- Long time horizons can reveal constraints hidden by event-by-event political narrative.
- Fiscal, logistical, informational, and administrative technique are central historical evidence.
- Biography can test how different strategies interact with one institutional order.
- Durable change requires more than exceptional individuals or locally successful action.
- Strong structural interpretation remains revisable and must confront counterexamples and alternative explanations.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

A long-run institutional frame can underweight contingency, regional variation, popular agency, ideology on its own terms, and real short-run success. Six selected figures cannot represent a whole dynasty, and defining 1587 as a year of failure may make the evidence appear more unified than it was.

## What Changed

- Established a historiographical method linking narrative biography to institutional capacity and evidence criticism.
