# Decisive Resource Commitment / 决断型资源投入

## Definition

Decisive resource commitment / 决断型资源投入 is the decision pattern where a leader deliberately allocates enough resources at the beginning of a high-risk problem to avoid repeated mobilization, prolonged cost, damaged credibility, and wider contagion.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 481 creates the concept through [[FengFengshiWesternHan|冯奉世]]'s argument during the [[XianjieQiangWesternHan|Xianjie Qiang]] rebellion. Feng does not treat "decisive" as reckless speed. He asks first how large the opponent is, what weapons and morale conditions look like, how long the campaign should last, and what happens if a smaller force fails. Hanji 482 adds the result side: once Han forces assemble in [[LongxiCommandery|陇西郡]] and advance together, the rebellion is broken, later emergency levies can be stood down, and only defensive/tuntian arrangements remain.

The concept's core distinction is between visible first cost and hidden total cost. [[HanYuandi|汉元帝]] and ministers have reason to fear autumn conscription during hunger, but Feng argues that an underpowered expedition may lose, be unable to protect local people, signal weakness, and require a larger later mobilization. Hanji 481 makes the half-measure risk concrete through early defeat and reinforcement; Hanji 482 uses the subsequent victory to turn the military case into a management lesson against [[BuridanIndecisionEffect|Buridan-style indecision]].

Hanji 374-1 and 374-2 add an earlier and morally harder test through [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s second campaign against [[DayuanWesternHan|大宛]]. The first source exposes the objective dispute before mobilization: ministers want to stop after [[ZhaoPonuWesternHan|赵破奴]]'s capture and concentrate on Xiongnu, while Wudi treats Dayuan as a Western Regions flank problem and a test of credibility after public commitment. The second shows the court then committing troops, animals, grain, engineers, corridor protection, and horse specialists at a scale that lets [[LiGuangliWesternHan|李广利]] intimidate intermediate states, sustain a siege, and accept a settlement once the central political and horse objectives become available. The campaign supports the claim that repeated underpowered attempts can be costlier than one sufficient commitment, but it also sharpens the boundary: a clear objective and victory do not prove proportionality when alternative threats, coercive recruitment, massacre, civilian harm, animal loss, and opportunity cost are not fully counted.

Hanji 375-2 makes the boundary concrete. The return brings prestige, tribute, hostages, route infrastructure, and rewards, but the episode attributes much of the troop loss to commanders' greed and abuse rather than enemy action or inadequate provisioning. Sufficient state resources can solve an external objective while weak command accountability destroys people internally; decisive commitment therefore requires governance of the force, not only adequate force size.

## Key Claims

- Decisive commitment should follow assessment of enemy scale, material conditions, timing, and desired duration.
- A smaller initial allocation can be more expensive when it causes defeat, repetition, or wider escalation.
- Deterrence is part of resource calculation: visible insufficiency can invite other actors to test the state.
- The concept differs from overcommitment because it asks for enough resources to finish a bounded problem, not unlimited expansion.
- Humanitarian and logistical costs matter together: a defensive stalemate may fail to relieve affected people while still consuming supplies.
- Decisive commitment requires acting before complete clarity arrives, but the action should still be anchored in goals, available chips, and information triage.
- A successful large commitment still requires an exit rule, full cost accounting, and command accountability; resource sufficiency cannot justify leaders wasting or abusing the personnel committed to them.

- Half-measure risk: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-481-wei-feng-xihan-fengfengshi-yiju-mie-diguo-libgqzvquegllma9soju57k_jxue|Hanji 481]] says Feng warns that ten thousand troops would not intimidate the rebels and would either lose in attack or fail to protect the people in defense.
- Escalation proof: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-481-wei-feng-xihan-fengfengshi-yiju-mie-diguo-libgqzvquegllma9soju57k_jxue|Hanji 481]] says Han Chang's forward detachment is defeated and Yuan later sends a much larger reinforcement.
- Result proof: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-482-renqing-bulidan-xiaoying-zhihao-nide-xuanze-kunnan-zheng-ljuimsm-o4mglmsntyp8w5ru-dt8|Hanji 482]] says the assembled Han force advances together, defeats the rebels, and allows the court to suspend the new levy except for border defense and tuntian.
- Management translation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-482-renqing-bulidan-xiaoying-zhihao-nide-xuanze-kunnan-zheng-ljuimsm-o4mglmsntyp8w5ru-dt8|Hanji 482]] frames Feng's decisiveness as a lesson for managers who must act under incomplete information without falling into Buridan-style paralysis.
- Contested objective: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-374-1-hanwudi-weihe-ningyuan-zisun-baqian-ye-buyuan-fangguo-dayuanguo-lqlfjifmkxgmlit1mmgxjg4oornh|Hanji 374-1]] says ministers prefer concentrating on Xiongnu after Zhao Ponu's capture, while Wudi and the host justify continuing against Dayuan through credibility and Western Regions flank strategy.
- State-scale reinforcement: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-374-2-hanwudi-qingjin-quanli-da-dayuan-zhi-bu-zhi-lmfzkllejz3m0vanmnu7gyxtwbul|Hanji 374-2]] says the second Dayuan expedition combines force, transport animals, provisions, engineering, corridor defense, and post-victory horse specialists rather than treating troop numbers alone as commitment.
- Bounded settlement: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-374-2-hanwudi-qingjin-quanli-da-dayuan-zhi-bu-zhi-lmfzkllejz3m0vanmnu7gyxtwbul|Hanji 374-2]] says Li Guangli stops after Wugua's death, horse-and-grain concessions, and intelligence showing that total conquest would remain costly.
- Accountability failure after success: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-375-2-shuwan-hanjiang-kugu-chengjiu-liguangli-fenghou-zhi-wei-ltfhrxkyc-w0pxw92bbyxx0blxwl|Hanji 375-2]] says much of the troop loss follows commanders' greed and abuse, yet the victorious command receives offices, pardons, and cash rewards.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source set does not prove that Feng's requested forty thousand was the objectively optimal number. Ministers' objections also have real force: hunger and autumn harvest work make large troop levies socially costly. Hanji 482's management analogy favors decisiveness more than process design, so the concept should be read as a disciplined anti-half-measure pattern, not a rule that bigger allocations or faster choices are always better. Hanji 374-1 shows that the objective itself can be contested when resources could answer a more immediate threat; its flank-and-credibility rationale remains the host's interpretation. Hanji 374-2 and 375-2 make the warning still sharper: strategic success coexists with coerced manpower, massacre, abusive command, severe troop loss, animal loss, civilian suffering, and rewards that may suppress accountability.

## What Changed

- Added the pre-commitment Dayuan dispute: decisive allocation requires choosing among competing threats, not merely scaling resources after an objective is declared.
- Preserved the aftermath warning that sufficient resources and a bounded exit do not prevent internal command abuse or accountability failure.
