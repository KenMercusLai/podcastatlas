# Wartime Command Decapitation / 战时统帅斩首

Wartime command decapitation / 战时统帅斩首 is the crisis mechanism [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-129-1-jiemi-wuguang-bei-miekou-de-lishi-zhenxiang-lk1bkhqkwf9upbbj08pjkn8cstyp|Qinji 129-1]] builds from [[TianZang|田臧]]'s killing of [[WuGuang|吴广]]. A subordinate or peer command group turns strategic emergency into permission to eliminate a commander, then seeks legitimacy by invoking or obtaining recognition from the higher authority.

The source case has three layers. First, the military argument is plausible: Wu Guang cannot take [[YingyangWarringStates|荥阳]], [[ZhouWenRebel|周文 / 周章]] has been defeated, and [[ZhangHanQin|章邯]] may soon create a two-front trap. Second, the personal judgment is hostile: Tian Zang's group says Wu Guang is arrogant and does not understand warfare. Third, the authority claim is unstable: they kill Wu Guang by falsely claiming [[ChenSheng|陈胜]]'s order, then bring the head to Chen Sheng.

This differs from [[BattlefieldCommanderReplacementRisk|阵前换将风险]]. Replacement risk focuses on removing or swapping a commander and the resulting strategic discontinuity. Wartime command decapitation adds lethal intra-coalition violence, forged or ambiguous authorization, and after-the-fact legitimation through promotion or non-punishment.

## Key Claims
- Military crisis can make an otherwise treasonous act look like urgent operational correction.
- Killing a commander can be justified publicly through competence language while still serving factional or political interests.
- False authorization is useful because it gives subordinates a claim of obedience before the superior authority has openly committed.
- Non-punishment and promotion after the killing can become evidence for tacit approval, but the inference remains source-scoped unless the source gives direct authorization.
- The mechanism can deepen rebellion-side fragmentation even when all actors still oppose the same external enemy.
