# Belle Epoque / 美好年代

Belle Epoque / 美好年代 is the episode's name for the late nineteenth-century to pre-World War I European period remembered as peaceful, prosperous, technologically confident, and culturally rich. In [[139-cong-zuo-ri-de-shi-jie-dao-ru-jin-de-ou-zhou-mei-hao-nian-dai-belle-epoque-ta-hai-neng-hui-lai-ma-ltvsgov8ht9-l7yp9qamdgjmyrf]], that memory is entered through [[StefanZweig|Stefan Zweig / 茨威格]] and [[TheWorldOfYesterday|《昨日的世界》]].

The source's point is not simple nostalgia. It argues that the Belle Epoque was never evenly distributed: it was far more available to wealthy, educated, multilingual cultural elites than to miners, textile workers, migrants, or people outside imperial and class privilege. The contemporary parallel is that today's "beautiful age" may still exist for mobile professionals with strong passports, money, and global industries, while others experience borders, rent pressure, war, and weak bargaining power.

## Key Claims
- A golden age can be historically real for some groups and structurally inaccessible to others.
- Cultural abundance and free movement need to be read together with class, citizenship, language, and empire.
- The question "can it return" is therefore partly a distribution question, not only a Europe question.
