# Dictionary Public-Good Governance / 词典公共品治理

## Definition

Dictionary public-good governance is the problem of funding and controlling a shared linguistic reference whose benefits extend across a community but whose production requires costly, sustained editorial labor.

## Current Synthesis

The episode rejects a simple state-versus-market answer. State-supported lexicography may preserve continuity and treat the dictionary as public infrastructure, but it can also make editors answerable to official ideology. A private publisher can preserve expertise and institutional independence, yet weak sales and corporate survival pressures can redirect revisions toward publicity, fashionable additions, and marketable editions.

The relevant design question is therefore not who is intrinsically neutral, but how editorial independence, transparent evidence, durable training, public accessibility, and financial continuity can coexist. The decline of print revenue and formal apprenticeship sharpens the problem because an online dictionary can look infinitely available while the human craft behind it becomes thinner.

## Key Claims

- Dictionaries produce shared educational and coordination value beyond the benefit captured by individual buyers.
- Government sponsorship risks direct ideological influence over definitions, labels, and historical framing.
- Commercial ownership risks subordinating entry selection and revision timing to sales, marketing, and corporate survival.
- Long revision cycles and apprenticeship make professional lexicography a capability that can decay when revenue weakens.
- Online access reduces distribution scarcity but does not finance evidence gathering, editorial review, or professional training by itself.
- Governance quality depends on institutional safeguards and transparent practice rather than a categorical preference for state or market control.

## Evidence

- State–market comparison: [[306-cidian-you-shenme-yong-lj-xsj-bvpmo0imc3qnv8njlgzso|306-词典有什么用？]] contrasts Chinese state-linked dictionary production with the privately owned American tradition.
- Commercial continuity: [[306-cidian-you-shenme-yong-lj-xsj-bvpmo0imc3qnv8njlgzso|306-词典有什么用？]] says Webster’s project nearly failed commercially before the Merriam brothers sustained it as a business.
- Capability erosion: [[306-cidian-you-shenme-yong-lj-xsj-bvpmo0imc3qnv8njlgzso|306-词典有什么用？]] describes long training, multi-decade revision cycles, shrinking recruitment, and a shift toward online operations.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode identifies a governance dilemma but does not compare nonprofit, university, foundation, cooperative, subscription, open-data, or mixed-funding models. It does not provide budgets, staffing data, editorial policies, sales figures, or a systematic comparison of Chinese and American dictionaries. State and market influence vary by institution, and neither funding source proves that any particular definition is biased.

## What Changed

- Created a public-good framework that treats editorial independence, professional capacity, access, and financial continuity as joint governance requirements.
- Distinguished free digital distribution from sustainable lexicographical production.
