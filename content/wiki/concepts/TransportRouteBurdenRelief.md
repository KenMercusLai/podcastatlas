# Transport Route Burden Relief / 贡道改线减负

## Definition

Transport route burden relief is the administrative practice of reducing public cost, danger, and labor pressure by redesigning tribute or supply routes and exempting tribute when conditions make extraction harmful. In Hanji 753, [[ZhengHongEasternHan|Zheng Hong]] uses this logic first for Jiaozhi transport and later for tribute reduction during drought, frontier alarm, and food shortage.

## Current Synthesis

The Jiaozhi route case treats logistics as a moral and fiscal problem. The old Dongye sea route is described as dangerous and expensive, while the Lingling and Guiyang mountain-road alternative reduces deaths and saves large state expenditure.

The later tribute-relief proposal extends the same principle from route engineering to demand management. When households face drought, frontier alarm, and food shortage, reducing tribute is a way to reduce corvee and transport burdens rather than simply insisting on scheduled extraction.

## Key Claims

- Route choice is governance: a dangerous route imposes avoidable human and fiscal costs.
- Administrative reform can be burden relief when it changes the physical path by which obligations move.
- Tribute exemptions can protect state capacity by reducing labor pressure during crisis.
- Fiscal savings and public care are not opposed in this case; safer routing saves both lives and money.
- Logistics decisions need local geographic knowledge, including sea-risk and mountain-road alternatives.

## Evidence

- Dangerous old route: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-753-banchao-beigao-heizhuang-lt-sksxrz30sawit81eqbeonhzv|Hanji 753]] describes Jiaozhi tribute using a hazardous sea route through Dongye.
- Safer replacement route: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-753-banchao-beigao-heizhuang-lt-sksxrz30sawit81eqbeonhzv|Hanji 753]] reports Zheng Hong opening mountain-road access through Lingling and Guiyang, saving cost and reducing danger.
- Tribute demand relief: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-753-banchao-beigao-heizhuang-lt-sksxrz30sawit81eqbeonhzv|Hanji 753]] has Zheng Hong propose exempting some tribute during drought, border alarms, and food shortage to reduce public labor and expense.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

- The current evidence does not measure long-term maintenance costs for the new route.
- Route relief is not the same as abolishing tribute; it changes the burden profile of state demand.
- Lingling, Guiyang, and Dongye remain source-scoped places here unless later sources justify standalone pages.
