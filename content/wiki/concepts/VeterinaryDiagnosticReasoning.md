# Veterinary Diagnostic Reasoning / 兽医诊断推理

## Definition
Veterinary diagnostic reasoning is the process of combining caregiver observations, systematic physical examination, species-specific tests, imaging, clinician experience, and change over time into revisable explanations for an animal's condition.

## Current Synthesis
The source describes diagnosis as a puzzle rather than a verdict delivered by one laboratory number or image. Because animals cannot describe pain, timing, or subjective change, the clinician must extract more information from behavior, caregiver history, whole-body examination, stress context, and patterns across tests. Uncharged examination work can therefore be as important as visible imaging or laboratory line items.

Species boundaries are central. Human medical knowledge may help formulate questions, but the canine pancreatitis example shows why a familiar human marker cannot simply be assigned the same diagnostic role in a dog. Owners can decline a piece of the workup, yet that choice changes how complete the evidence picture can become and should be discussed as an information tradeoff rather than as a guarantee of the same certainty at lower cost.

## Key Claims
- Diagnosis integrates history, caregiver observations, physical examination, laboratory evidence, imaging, response, and time.
- Animals' inability to narrate symptoms increases the information value of systematic examination and clinician sensitivity.
- A single abnormal value rarely establishes a complex disease, and a normal result can still narrow the differential diagnosis.
- Human medical concepts require species-specific validation before they guide veterinary interpretation.
- Fear, restraint, technique, equipment, and local capability can alter the quality or meaning of measurements.
- Declining a test is a legitimate caregiver choice that also leaves a defined information gap and possible missed-diagnosis risk.
- Capability limits should be stated honestly and followed by referral rather than concealed as certainty.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This is a reasoning framework, not a diagnostic algorithm. Appropriate tests, thresholds, referral routes, and interpretation vary by species, breed, age, clinical state, equipment, local expertise, and disease prevalence. The source's pancreatitis, blood-pressure, imaging, and tumor cases are illustrative clinician accounts rather than validated universal rules.

## What Changed
- Created a veterinary-specific reasoning model centered on nonverbal patients and whole-body examination.
- Added species translation and caregiver-selected information gaps as explicit uncertainty sources.
- Distinguished capability-limited referral from unwilling refusal of care.
