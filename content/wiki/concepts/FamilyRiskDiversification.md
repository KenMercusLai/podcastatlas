## Definition
Family risk diversification is the strategy or retrospective pattern in which a family, household, or individual avoids dependence on one power channel by spreading members, careers, assets, or loyalties across different paths so that one collapse does not destroy the whole group.

## Current Synthesis
The current evidence comes from [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-792-dengkang-renu-taihou-dagou-weishenme-yao-kan-zhuren-lh3xkjneqfd9sqijqo3rgzsa5vwn|Hanji 792]]'s treatment of [[DengKangLateHan|Deng Kang]]. Deng Kang is a member of the [[DengFamilyAnEraLateHan|Deng family]] who warns against excessive outer-relative power, angers [[EmpressDowagerDengLateHan|Empress Dowager Deng]], and is pushed away from the court power core before the family's later purge.

The source's durable insight is not that Deng Kang certainly planned his removal as strategy. It is that separation from a dominant family bloc can look like punishment in the short term and risk reduction in retrospect. The host extends that logic to broader family and career behavior: concentrating every path in one direction increases collapse risk, while spreading effort across more than one direction can preserve options when a political or social environment shifts.

## Key Claims
- A family power bloc can become dangerous to its own members when public authority begins to read the household as a threat.
- Internal dissent may function as warning, distancing, or mixed self-protection, even when it fails to change the dominant actor's behavior.
- Short-term disgrace can become retrospective protection if it removes a person from a later purge field.
- Diversification is not automatic virtue; it can be deliberate strategy, accidental separation, or an interpretive label applied after outcomes are known.
- Modern career analogies in the source are useful as heuristics but remain separate from the historical proof about Deng Kang.

## Evidence
- Internal family warning: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-792-dengkang-renu-taihou-dagou-weishenme-yao-kan-zhuren-lh3xkjneqfd9sqijqo3rgzsa5vwn|Hanji 792]] says Deng Kang repeatedly urges stronger court authority and weaker Deng outer-relative power while still belonging to the Deng family.
- Separation from the core: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-792-dengkang-renu-taihou-dagou-weishenme-yao-kan-zhuren-lh3xkjneqfd9sqijqo3rgzsa5vwn|Hanji 792]] says Deng is angered, removes Deng Kang from office, sends him back to his fief, and cancels his registration/status connection.
- Retrospective safety logic: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-792-dengkang-renu-taihou-dagou-weishenme-yao-kan-zhuren-lh3xkjneqfd9sqijqo3rgzsa5vwn|Hanji 792]] reads that removal as possible "因祸得福" because Deng Kang may avoid worse consequences when the Deng family is later purged.
- Broader heuristic: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-792-dengkang-renu-taihou-dagou-weishenme-yao-kan-zhuren-lh3xkjneqfd9sqijqo3rgzsa5vwn|Hanji 792]] generalizes from Deng Kang to large-family risk spreading and modern multi-path career behavior.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence does not prove Deng Kang consciously engineered a safe exit from the Deng power core. His punishment follows feigned illness and a palace-status insult, so the immediate cause is still offense and dowager anger. The concept should be used as a risk-structure reading, not as proof of deliberate strategy by every actor who ends up outside a later purge.

## What Changed
- Created the concept from Hanji 792's reading of Deng Kang's punishment as possible short-term loss but long-term risk separation.
