## Definition
Founding commander suspicion management is the pattern in which a high-merit or locally powerful military actor makes loyalty credible to a new ruler before personal networks, family troops, or prior affiliations are interpreted as rebellion risk.

## Current Synthesis
[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-733-2-gengyan-youzhizhe-shijingcheng-jiangmen-huzi-yongguan-sanjun-lnlfumtdzrigby9ox4v6pzbeogeh|Hanji 733-2]] shows the pattern through [[GengYanEasternHan|耿弇]] and the Geng family during the [[PengChongEasternHan|彭宠]] rebellion. The family has the local military resources [[LiuXiu|刘秀 / 光武帝]] needs, but those same resources become politically dangerous when Peng Chong, an old connection, tries to draw them into rebellion. Geng Yan's request to return to Luoyang and [[GengKuangEasternHan|耿况]]'s decision to send [[GengGuoEasternHan|耿国]] to serve at court make loyalty visible enough for Liu Xiu to keep using the family.

The concept is not generic humility. It is a coordination device: the commander and family reduce ambiguity around their intentions so military capacity can remain deployable. The episode's "hostage-like" reading of Geng Guo's court service is therefore best treated as a political signal, not as proof of a formal hostage institution.

## Key Claims
- Founding regimes need powerful local commanders, but those same commanders can become threats once authority centralizes.
- Suspicion becomes acute when military households have prior relationships with rebels or regional claimants.
- Credible loyalty can be signaled through physical presence at court, family service, returned command, or separation from a suspect network.
- The ruler's trust is not purely personal sentiment; it is maintained by visible arrangements that lower coordination risk.
- The Geng family case shows suspicion management enabling continued military action rather than removing the commander from service.

## Evidence
- Local military power as both asset and risk: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-733-2-gengyan-youzhizhe-shijingcheng-jiangmen-huzi-yongguan-sanjun-lnlfumtdzrigby9ox4v6pzbeogeh|Hanji 733-2]] links Geng-family Youzhou power to Liu Xiu's cavalry needs and to Peng Chong's attempted recruitment.
- Court-service signal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-733-2-gengyan-youzhizhe-shijingcheng-jiangmen-huzi-yongguan-sanjun-lnlfumtdzrigby9ox4v6pzbeogeh|Hanji 733-2]] says Geng Kuang sends Geng Guo to Luoyang while Geng Yan seeks return to court, which the episode interprets as reducing Liu Xiu's suspicion.
- Continued deployment: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-733-2-gengyan-youzhizhe-shijingcheng-jiangmen-huzi-yongguan-sanjun-lnlfumtdzrigby9ox4v6pzbeogeh|Hanji 733-2]] shows Geng Yan continuing as a useful commander after the loyalty problem is addressed.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This concept currently rests on one episode and should not be generalized to all founding commanders without more cases. The episode's comparison to hostage logic is interpretive; the source does not establish that Geng Guo entered court under a formal hostage category. Trust management here also differs from later purges of meritorious ministers: the outcome is continued command, not immediate removal.

## What Changed
- Created a concept page for loyalty signaling by powerful founding commanders.
- Anchored the concept in the Geng family's response to Peng Chong's rebellion.
- Distinguished political credibility signaling from simple moral humility or formal hostage law.
