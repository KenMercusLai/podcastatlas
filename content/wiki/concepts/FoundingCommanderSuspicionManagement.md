## Definition
Founding commander suspicion management is the pattern in which a high-merit or locally powerful military actor makes loyalty credible to a new ruler before personal networks, family troops, or prior affiliations are interpreted as rebellion risk.

## Current Synthesis
[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-685-donghan-dachen-songhong-weihe-buken-zuo-guangwudi-jiefu-ljafg8giyhg5wmumv-g-09lywcu2|Hanji 685]] now gives the negative and triggering side of the pattern through [[PengChongEasternHan|彭宠]]. Peng had real founding contribution and a powerful Yuyang base, but his reward grievance, conflict with [[ZhuFuEasternHan|朱浮]], stored grain, troops, Liu Xiu's warning leak, and summons all turn local capacity into suspicion. Instead of making loyalty credible, Peng remains inside a wife-and-retainer advice environment that reinforces staying put and rebelling.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-733-2-gengyan-youzhizhe-shijingcheng-jiangmen-huzi-yongguan-sanjun-lnlfumtdzrigby9ox4v6pzbeogeh|Hanji 733-2]] shows the positive signaling side through [[GengYanEasternHan|耿弇]] and the Geng family during the same Peng Chong rebellion. The family has the local military resources [[LiuXiu|刘秀 / 光武帝]] needs, but those same resources become politically dangerous when Peng Chong, an old connection, tries to draw them into rebellion. Geng Yan's request to return to Luoyang and [[GengKuangEasternHan|耿况]]'s decision to send [[GengGuoEasternHan|耿国]] to serve at court make loyalty visible enough for Liu Xiu to keep using the family.

The concept is not generic humility. It is a coordination device: the commander or family reduces ambiguity around intentions so military capacity can remain deployable. Hanji 685 shows what happens when ambiguity is amplified instead, while Hanji 733-2 shows how visible separation from a rebel network keeps a local military family usable. The episode's "hostage-like" reading of Geng Guo's court service is therefore best treated as a political signal, not as proof of a formal hostage institution.

## Key Claims
- Founding regimes need powerful local commanders, but those same commanders can become threats once authority centralizes.
- Suspicion becomes acute when military households have prior relationships with rebels or regional claimants.
- Reward grievance and independent supply can make local military capacity look like pending rebellion even before a rebel title is declared.
- Credible loyalty can be signaled through physical presence at court, family service, returned command, or separation from a suspect network.
- The ruler's trust is not purely personal sentiment; it is maintained by visible arrangements that lower coordination risk.
- The Peng and Geng cases show opposite outcomes: unmanaged suspicion hardens into rebellion, while visible signaling enables continued military use.

## Evidence
- Suspicion trigger and negative case: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-685-donghan-dachen-songhong-weihe-buken-zuo-guangwudi-jiefu-ljafg8giyhg5wmumv-g-09lywcu2|Hanji 685]] says Peng Chong's reward grievance, Yuyang resources, troop and grain accumulation, Zhu Fu's reports, Liu Xiu's leaked warning, and summons push him toward rebellion.
- Advice environment: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-685-donghan-dachen-songhong-weihe-buken-zuo-guangwudi-jiefu-ljafg8giyhg5wmumv-g-09lywcu2|Hanji 685]] says Peng Chong's wife and retainers oppose going to Luoyang, reinforcing the refusal to make loyalty visible.
- Direct Geng refusal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-685-donghan-dachen-songhong-weihe-buken-zuo-guangwudi-jiefu-ljafg8giyhg5wmumv-g-09lywcu2|Hanji 685]] says Peng repeatedly asks Geng Kuang to join, but Geng Kuang refuses and kills the envoys.
- Local military power as both asset and risk: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-733-2-gengyan-youzhizhe-shijingcheng-jiangmen-huzi-yongguan-sanjun-lnlfumtdzrigby9ox4v6pzbeogeh|Hanji 733-2]] links Geng-family Youzhou power to Liu Xiu's cavalry needs and to Peng Chong's attempted recruitment.
- Court-service signal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-733-2-gengyan-youzhizhe-shijingcheng-jiangmen-huzi-yongguan-sanjun-lnlfumtdzrigby9ox4v6pzbeogeh|Hanji 733-2]] says Geng Kuang sends Geng Guo to Luoyang while Geng Yan seeks return to court, which the episode interprets as reducing Liu Xiu's suspicion.
- Continued deployment: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-733-2-gengyan-youzhizhe-shijingcheng-jiangmen-huzi-yongguan-sanjun-lnlfumtdzrigby9ox4v6pzbeogeh|Hanji 733-2]] shows Geng Yan continuing as a useful commander after the loyalty problem is addressed.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This concept currently rests on two Peng/Geng-centered episodes and should not be generalized to all founding commanders without more cases. Hanji 685 does not prove that Peng Chong was doomed to rebel as soon as he was under-rewarded; it shows a cumulative suspicion chain involving reward, resources, Zhu Fu, Liu Xiu, family advice, and old ties. Hanji 733-2's comparison to hostage logic is interpretive; the source does not establish that Geng Guo entered court under a formal hostage category. Trust management here also differs from later purges of meritorious ministers: the positive Geng outcome is continued command, not immediate removal.

## What Changed
- Added Hanji 685 as the negative Peng Chong suspicion-chain and direct Geng Kuang refusal layer.
- Reframed the concept around paired outcomes: suspicion amplified into rebellion versus suspicion reduced through visible loyalty signals.
- Preserved Hanji 733-2 as the main Geng Yan and Geng-family court-service evidence.
