# Post-Practice Motor Consolidation

## Definition
Post-practice motor consolidation is the proposed low-interference window after movement training in which quiet wakefulness and later sleep may help replay, stabilize, and select successful motor sequences.

## Current Synthesis
The source makes the minutes after practice part of the session. It recommends briefly sitting quietly, often with eyes closed, instead of immediately switching to a phone, another learning task, or performance analysis. This is presented as a way to leave space for neural replay of successful sequences and weakening of unsuccessful ones.

The claim fits the wiki's broader consolidation account but is narrower: it concerns procedural movement traces after physical practice. Sleep remains the larger recovery and consolidation layer, while the immediate quiet period is a plausible, low-cost practice whose exact duration and incremental benefit remain unsettled.

## Key Claims
- The end of physical practice does not necessarily end the learning process.
- Brief quiet wakefulness may reduce interference while successful motor sequences are replayed.
- Immediate phone use, new learning, or intense analysis may compete with this low-interference period.
- Even a short pause may be useful, while the episode commonly suggests five to ten minutes.
- Subsequent sleep remains a central support for consolidation and recovery.

## Evidence
- Quiet-wakefulness protocol - [[essentials-how-to-learn-skills-faster-scim2224786015]] recommends doing little or nothing for a short period after motor practice.
- Replay mechanism - [[essentials-how-to-learn-skills-faster-scim2224786015]] describes replay of correct sequences and elimination of incorrect ones after training.
- Sleep layer - [[essentials-how-to-learn-skills-faster-scim2224786015]] places sleep after repetitions, errors, and post-training idle time in the complete practice sequence.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode does not provide full citations or quantify how much quiet wakefulness adds beyond sleep, ordinary rest, expectancy, or reduced distraction. The five-to-ten-minute duration is a source recommendation rather than a universal threshold, and rehabilitation or clinical motor-learning plans may require individualized guidance.

## What Changed
- Created a motor-specific consolidation concept for the immediate low-interference period after practice.
- Connected quiet wakefulness to sleep without treating the two as interchangeable.
