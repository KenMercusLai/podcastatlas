## Definition
Ritual-agrarian frontier integration / 礼俗农事式边疆整合 is a governance pattern in which a frontier population is incorporated through instruction in ritual norms, marriage customs, agriculture, and everyday administrative practice after or alongside formal allegiance.

## Current Synthesis
Hanji 699 creates the current bounded form of the concept through the southern branch of early Guangwu consolidation. [[CenPengEasternHan|Cen Peng]] and Qu Chong help make allegiance visible by letters, proclamations, tribute, and enfeoffment; [[XiGuangEasternHan|Xi Guang]] and [[RenYanEasternHan|Ren Yan]] then represent the slower local work of teaching customs and agriculture in Jiaozhi and Jiuzhen.

The concept therefore separates frontier integration from a pure recognition or campaign model. Submission and rewards bring local elites into the Han order, but the episode's durable claim is about everyday social and productive practice: officials teach how people marry, farm, and recognize ritual authority.

This pattern also stays qualified. Hanji 699 gives the court-facing integration narrative, while later Jiaozhi sources show revolt, extraction, and repair problems. The concept should be used as a claim about one integration method, not as proof that southern incorporation was peaceful, complete, or uncontested.

## Key Claims
- Formal allegiance is only the first layer of frontier integration; durable control also depends on everyday norms and livelihood practice.
- Ritual instruction and marriage-custom reform make imperial order socially legible rather than merely administrative.
- Agricultural instruction links frontier governance to production, household stability, and local material life.
- Southern integration can be narrated through paired officials whose roles are instructional rather than military.
- The pattern needs later qualification because the same southern region later appears as a revolt and extraction theater.

## Evidence
- Allegiance prelude: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-699-gan-yu-liuxiu-zhengxiong-de-ta-weihe-candan-shouchang-lth-8mndudj3khf9cnsouj86c1-z|Hanji 699]] says Cen Peng's friendship letter and Qu Chong's proclamations help southern and Jiangnan actors send tribute and receive enfeoffment.
- Ritual and social instruction: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-699-gan-yu-liuxiu-zhengxiong-de-ta-weihe-candan-shouchang-lth-8mndudj3khf9cnsouj86c1-z|Hanji 699]] says Xi Guang teaches ritual and customs in Jiaozhi.
- Agricultural instruction: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-699-gan-yu-liuxiu-zhengxiong-de-ta-weihe-candan-shouchang-lth-8mndudj3khf9cnsouj86c1-z|Hanji 699]] says Ren Yan teaches agriculture in Jiuzhen.
- Lingnan adoption claim: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-699-gan-yu-liuxiu-zhengxiong-de-ta-weihe-candan-shouchang-lth-8mndudj3khf9cnsouj86c1-z|Hanji 699]] frames Xi Guang and Ren Yan as starting points for Lingnan's adoption of central-plains customs.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The current evidence is one episode's retrospective integration narrative. It does not show local reception, coercion level, non-Han perspectives, or the later costs of cultural change. Existing Jiaozhi material already warns against overgeneralization: the region later produces rebellion, extraction problems, logistics debates, and root-cause pacification. This concept should therefore describe a method available to Han officials, not a settled outcome.

## What Changed
- Created the concept from Hanji 699's paired Xi Guang and Ren Yan southern-frontier branch.
- Distinguished ritual and agricultural instruction from campaign force, tribute submission, and title recognition.
- Added a built-in qualification that later Jiaozhi unrest can coexist with an earlier instruction narrative.
