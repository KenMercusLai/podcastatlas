## Definition

Mythic ancestor historical legitimation / 神话祖先的历史化正统建构 is the use of historical selection and narrative reshaping to turn a sacred or mythic ancestor into a human ruler whose conduct can anchor a dynasty's account of legitimate sovereignty.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-360-1-simaqian-taishigong-chenghao-jing-you-ci-er-lai-lq6ulwhl_jscf6ipkjxev6zvwbrf|Hanji 360-1]] presents [[Huangdi|黄帝]] as the key case. Earlier traditions place him amid gods, immortals, five phases, five colors, omens, and chenwei-style cosmology. The episode says [[SimaQian|司马迁]] compares inherited materials, rejects much of the overtly marvelous content, and narrates Huangdi and the Five Emperors as human rulers closer to the Confucian sage-king model of Yao and Shun.

That humanization does political work. A human sage ancestor supplies a past against which imperial authority and conduct can be explained, while [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s sacrifice at Huangdi's tomb makes the ancestral narrative ritually present. Historical writing and imperial practice therefore operate in the same legitimacy field, although the source does not prove that legitimation was Sima Qian's sole intention or that his reconstruction establishes Huangdi's historicity.

## Key Claims

- Mythic materials can be selected and reorganized into a historical genealogy of rulership.
- Humanizing a sacred ancestor does not remove political meaning; it changes divine marvel into exemplary kingship.
- Confucian sage-ruler norms provide the episode's model for recasting Huangdi and the Five Emperors.
- Historical authorship and imperial ritual can reinforce the same claim of dynastic continuity and legitimate authority.
- Narrative usefulness does not prove the historical existence, exact deeds, or authorial intent assigned to the ancestor.

## Evidence

- Myth-to-ruler transformation - [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-360-1-simaqian-taishigong-chenghao-jing-you-ci-er-lai-lq6ulwhl_jscf6ipkjxev6zvwbrf|Hanji 360-1]] contrasts a marvelous Huangdi embedded in five-phase and chenwei traditions with Sima Qian's humanized ruler.
- Confucian narrative selection - [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-360-1-simaqian-taishigong-chenghao-jing-you-ci-er-lai-lq6ulwhl_jscf6ipkjxev6zvwbrf|Hanji 360-1]] says Sima Qian uses classical materials and a Confucian model to shape the Five Emperors as sage kings.
- Ritual-historical convergence - [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-360-1-simaqian-taishigong-chenghao-jing-you-ci-er-lai-lq6ulwhl_jscf6ipkjxev6zvwbrf|Hanji 360-1]] moves from Han Wudi's sacrifice at Huangdi's Qiaoshan tomb to the political problem of Han imperial legitimacy.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The current framework is grounded in one short podcast episode, not a systematic comparison of Shiji with every pre-Han Huangdi tradition. “神仙的黄帝消失” is the host's compressed interpretation, not evidence that all marvelous material vanished or that later readers accepted only one image. The concept records narrative and political function; it does not establish Huangdi's historicity, prove that Sima Qian deliberately fabricated a ruler, or reduce Shiji to dynastic propaganda.

## What Changed

- Created a source-bounded framework linking Huangdi's humanization, Confucian sage-kingship, and Han imperial legitimacy.
