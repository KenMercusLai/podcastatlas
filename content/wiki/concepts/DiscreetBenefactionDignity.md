# Discreet Benefaction Dignity / 沉默施恩的尊严保护

Discreet benefaction dignity / 沉默施恩的尊严保护 enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-965-bangguo-bieren-de-mang-weishenme-yao-shao-ti-ltjd-cv59hnm-n8ar9jxdogp9q0q|Hanji 965]]. The episode argues that help is not only a material transfer; it also changes status, memory, and the recipient's sense of exposure. When the giver publicly repeats the deed, the help can become a reminder of vulnerability and a pressure to display gratitude.

The source uses [[Daodejing|《道德经》]], Lu Kun's `施恩不求报` warning, [[JeanPaulSartre|Jean-Paul Sartre / 萨特]], and Dunhuang donor imagery as interpretive frames rather than as independent historical claims. The common point is that a gift is ethically complete only when it does not trap the recipient inside shame, indebtedness, or the giver's public self-image.

This differs from [[DignityThroughSmallGifts|小礼物中的尊严]], which emphasizes practical objects that preserve ordinary self-respect under scarcity. Discreet benefaction dignity focuses on what happens after help is given: silence, restraint, and non-display can be part of the care itself.

## Key Claims

- Help can become domination when the giver repeatedly publicizes the recipient's dependence.
- Gratitude should be allowed to emerge freely rather than extracted as reputation payment.
- Protecting dignity may require hiding the giver's role after the practical problem has been solved.
- A good deed can fail relationally even when it succeeds materially, if it fixes the recipient as weak.
- The concept is source-scoped as an ethical and psychological interpretation, not a universal rule that all public philanthropy is harmful.
