# Sectional Party Realignment

## Definition

Sectional party realignment is the collapse of a cross-sectional party system and the rise of a Northern antislavery party capable of winning national power through the Electoral College.

## Current Synthesis

The episode traces the Whig Party's collapse after 1852 and the [[RepublicanParty|Republican Party]]'s rise amid the Kansas-Nebraska crisis. Because the new party had almost no Southern support, it appeared not merely oppositional but regionally hostile to the South.

The Electoral College converted concentrated Northern strength into national viability. [[AbrahamLincoln|Abraham Lincoln]] won in 1860 with less than 40 percent of the popular vote but a decisive electoral majority. The victory did not create slavery conflict, but it demonstrated that a president could be elected without Southern political support, triggering secession by states organized around protection of slavery.

## Key Claims

- The Whig collapse removed a major cross-sectional container for slavery conflict.
- Kansas-Nebraska helped organize a Northern antislavery Republican coalition.
- Minimal Southern support made Republican national victory appear sectional.
- Electoral College geography mattered more than a national popular-vote majority.
- Lincoln's victory transformed long-running conflict into an immediate secession crisis.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept does not treat electoral rules as a substitute for slavery causation or claim that every party member held the same racial or constitutional position. Vote shares, party labels, regional support, and the causal weight of the Electoral College remain episode-level summaries rather than a complete electoral analysis.
