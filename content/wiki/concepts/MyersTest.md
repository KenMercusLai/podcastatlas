# Myers Test

The Myers Test is the religious-legibility framework [[JohnRapp]] uses in [[a-trip-to-the-magic-mushroom-megachurch]] when advising [[PsychedelicChurches]]. The episode says relevant signals include a founder or spiritual leader, regular services, ceremonies, rituals, developed theology, sacred texts, religious clothing, codes of ethics, and holidays.

In the source, the test matters less as abstract doctrine than as institution-building pressure. Rapp helps groups translate visions into canons, bylaws, scriptures, rituals, and calendars so that religious claims can be understood by courts and agencies considering [[ReligiousControlledSubstanceExemption]].

## Key Claims
- Courts and agencies need observable criteria when a group claims religious status around controlled substances.
- The criteria can encourage real institutional discipline, but they can also make religion partly a legal-performance problem.
- Sacred texts, leaders, rituals, and holidays become evidence as well as internal community practices.
- A group can look more legally legible without resolving whether outsiders will see its commercial or access model as sincere.
