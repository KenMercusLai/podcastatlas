# Genre Literary Legitimacy / 类型文学正名

Genre literary legitimacy is the wiki frame for sources that resist treating genre labels as evidence of lower literary value. [[117-zuzhoutu-guaitan-wenxue-ye-yao-shangzhuo-chifan-766574341]] makes the claim directly through [[GuaitanLiterature|怪谈文学]] and [[CursedBunny|《诅咒兔》]]: horror, folklore, curse objects, and grotesque bodies can carry serious feeling precisely because they bypass polite abstraction.

The concept also connects back to [[112-xianliao-jinyong-diyidan-mingjiao-jingran-jintian-hai-cunzai-754593095]], where [[JinYong|金庸]] is defended as culturally dense popular literature rather than only entertainment. In both cases, the point is not that genre is secretly "pure literature." The stronger claim is that form, audience, motif, pacing, and inherited conventions can be literary tools in their own right.

[[fanwai-15-banlatte-jiang-jinpingmei-la-erqie-haishi-luse-de-991508433]] adds a scandal-reputation case through [[JinPingMei|《金瓶梅》]]. [[Banlatte|半拿铁]] argues that the novel's erotic reputation is a weak test of literary seriousness because [[UrbanEverydayFiction]], [[HouseholdAsSocialWeb]], character network, social history, and later influence on [[HongLouMeng|《红楼梦》]] remain central even in a [[CleanEditionClassicReading|绿色版]] retelling.

[[130-tuokaerqiuke-guaidan-gushiji-xunzhao-he-gaobie-803543569]] extends the frame through [[OlgaTokarczuk|托卡尔丘克]]'s [[BizarreStories|《怪诞故事集》]]. Here legitimacy is not about defending a low-status genre, but about trusting [[EverydayWeirdness]] and speculative devices to carry old age, grief, AI intimacy, and farewell without requiring a realist default.

[[10-wang-erde-qiyi-gushiji-yong-zui-hui-sahuang-de-zuichun-qinwen-tianshi-545332195]] adds a boundary discussion around [[WildeStrangeStories|《王尔德奇异故事集》]], ghost stories, black comedy, and the later comparison between science fiction and serious literature. The source's point is that genre rank is a weak reading test: a ghost story like [[TheCantervilleGhost|《坎特维尔的幽灵》]] or a speculative novel can matter when form makes humor, tenderness, cultural conflict, or contemporary anxiety available.

## Key Claims
- A genre label describes a form's toolkit; it does not decide in advance whether the work is serious.
- Directness, suspense, horror, martial arts, mystery rules, or folk motifs can expose pressures that prestige realism may soften.
- Genre legitimacy does not require denying pleasure or readability; pleasure can be part of how difficult material travels.
- The useful question is what the form makes thinkable, feelable, or memorable, not whether it resembles a high-literary default.
- "Weird" and speculative premises can be legitimate when they sharpen emotional and ethical recognition rather than replacing it.
- Prestige-literature status is not a substitute for testing what a genre form actually does with language, feeling, social pressure, or speculation.
- Scandalous reputation can be as flattening as a low-status genre label when it prevents attention to structure, social texture, and influence.
