# Confidence Profile Team Management

Confidence profile team management is [[ep-3-demystifying-the-imposter-syndrome]]'s workplace takeaway from contrasting [[ImpostorSyndrome|imposter syndrome]] with the [[DunningKrugerEffect|Dunning-Kruger effect]]. [[SamDataScienceWithSam|Sam]] argues that managers should identify people who underestimate themselves and people who overestimate themselves so both groups can be managed efficiently.

The source treats confidence profiles as actionable tendencies, not permanent labels. Cautious people may bring care, humility, and risk awareness; overconfident people may pull the team toward action or ambition. The managerial task is to pair those strengths with feedback, evidence, and role design so neither paralysis nor reckless certainty dominates.

The concept links individual psychology to team design. Confidence is not only a private feeling; it changes who volunteers, who speaks, who overrules others, and how risk is distributed across colleagues.

## Key Claims
- Managers should notice both underconfidence and overconfidence because each changes team behavior.
- Different confidence profiles can have useful strengths when their failure modes are managed.
- Team confidence should be calibrated through evidence, feedback, and role boundaries.
- [[LearnableEmotionalIntelligence]] means people can update these patterns rather than remain fixed in them.
