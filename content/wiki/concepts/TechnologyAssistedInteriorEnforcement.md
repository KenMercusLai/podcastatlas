# Technology-Assisted Interior Enforcement

## Definition
Technology-assisted interior enforcement is the use of data tools, target-listing software, and expanded funding to make immigration enforcement inside a country more systematic even when visible raids become less theatrical.

## Current Synthesis
The concept now has two linked inputs. [[AaronBraun]]'s account of [[DonaldTrump]]'s second-term deportation drive describes quieter but still aggressive enforcement after backlash around highly visible federal operations, with Congress appropriating $70 billion more and court testimony describing a [[Palantir]]-created app that can show potential immigrant targets in an area. The Palantir-focused Planet Money source adds the data-integration mechanism: ICE is described as accessing 30 to 40 datasets, seeking taxpayer information through an IRS memorandum, and using Palantir-style [[DataOperationalization]] to build richer files faster.

## Key Claims
- Interior enforcement becomes more personally visible than border enforcement because arrests occur on streets, in homes, and near long-settled communities.
- Funding and software can increase enforcement capacity while reducing the need for spectacular public operations.
- Target-listing tools change deportation work from reactive case handling toward data-aided area search and prioritization.
- Technology-assisted enforcement can broaden political backlash when public promises about criminals and gang members meet broader removal effects.
- The source ties technology to courts and legal-status stripping, not only field arrests.
- Cross-agency data access can extend target selection beyond field observation by combining tax, administrative, public-record, and enforcement datasets.

## Evidence
- Funding and software - [[ire-and-ice-the-toll-of-americas-deportations-6a993426809ae7c2f4293df9]] says Congress appropriated $70 billion more for immigration enforcement and court testimony described an ICE agent using a Palantir-created map-like app to identify potential targets.
- Interior-arrest shift - [[ire-and-ice-the-toll-of-americas-deportations-6a993426809ae7c2f4293df9]] contrasts a quiet border with enforcement focused inside the United States, including long-term residents and people whose legal status is being stripped.
- Political backlash - [[ire-and-ice-the-toll-of-americas-deportations-6a993426809ae7c2f4293df9]] links broad enforcement effects to underwater immigration approval, criticism of ICE force, and Hispanic-voter discontent.
- Multi-dataset targeting - [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-8eb9e254581eaa220d9428df389214d5-27844515-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-8eb9e254581eaa220d9428df389214d5-27844515]] says ICE can access 30 to 40 datasets and that Palantir software can help enrich separate datasets into more detailed files about individuals and networks.
- Silo-collapse mechanism - [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-8eb9e254581eaa220d9428df389214d5-27844515-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-8eb9e254581eaa220d9428df389214d5-27844515]] ties the enforcement branch to an anti-silo executive order, the [[PrivacyAct1974|Privacy Act of 1974]], and an ICE-IRS data-sharing memorandum.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not independently verify the app's full capabilities, the administration's removal totals, exact database composition, or ICE's final use of tax information. The wiki should keep specific functionality, deportation counts, dataset counts, database scale, and political polling figures source-scoped unless later evidence confirms them.
