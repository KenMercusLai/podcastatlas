# Portuguese Maritime Expansion / 葡萄牙海上扩张

## Definition
Portuguese maritime expansion is the source's frame for Portugal's shift from mainland Iberian consolidation toward North Africa, Atlantic islands, African coasts, and eventually Indian Ocean routes.

## Current Synthesis
The Ceuta episode treats the 1415 capture of [[Ceuta]] as the conventional first station in Portugal's overseas expansion story, but it keeps that claim qualified. Ceuta clearly marks a turn from mainland Reconquista to sea-facing conquest and trade imagination; it does not prove that Portuguese leaders already had a fully formed global empire plan in 1415.

## Key Claims
- Ceuta matters as a narrative opening because it redirected Portuguese ambition across the strait and toward maritime routes.
- Expansion followed from both opportunity and constraint: Portugal's mainland frontier had stabilized while North Africa and Atlantic islands remained available targets.
- The 1415 victory revealed wealth and commercial networks, but occupation also damaged the port economy it had sought to control.
- Later memory can make the expansion look more planned and linear than the source says it was at the time.

- Campaign evidence: [[496-yiminchao-beihou-de-xiuda-cong-gudian-shidai-manzu-duhai-yibiliya-shoufu-shidi-yundong-dao-putaoya-dahanghai-lu8pawnkiselmmrgubrey4pq-4fw]] describes the 1415 fleet, storm, target discipline, and one-day capture of Ceuta.
- Commercial evidence: [[496-yiminchao-beihou-de-xiuda-cong-gudian-shidai-manzu-duhai-yibiliya-shoufu-shidi-yundong-dao-putaoya-dahanghai-lu8pawnkiselmmrgubrey4pq-4fw]] says Portuguese forces were struck by Ceuta's shops, luxury goods, and wealth.
- Qualification evidence: [[496-yiminchao-beihou-de-xiuda-cong-gudian-shidai-manzu-duhai-yibiliya-shoufu-shidi-yundong-dao-putaoya-dahanghai-lu8pawnkiselmmrgubrey4pq-4fw]] says the hosts reserve judgment on whether 1415 already contained a conscious global conquest design.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept is not a complete history of Portuguese exploration. The source's strongest claim is about Ceuta as a conventional and symbolic starting point, not as proof of a fully designed global expansion strategy.
