# Frontier Model Verified Access

## Definition
Frontier model verified access is the governance approach of allowing more capable model use for identified, accountable, and risk-reviewed users instead of applying broad anonymous-user restrictions or silent downgrades to everyone.

## Current Synthesis
The source presents verified access as a middle path between unrestricted frontier-model use and blanket refusal behavior. Chamath and Sacks argue that if Anthropic is worried about cyber, biology, or weapons misuse, it can verify users, know who is doing sensitive work, and place accountability on high-risk access rather than broadly degrading model capability or pushing for a new approval agency.

The concept matters because it changes where governance happens. Instead of treating model intelligence itself as the only control surface, verified access combines identity, customer type, contractual terms, monitoring, and downstream safeguards. It can reduce over-refusal for legitimate enterprise and research users, but it also risks becoming [[HyperscalerAIGatekeeping]] if only a few cloud or model providers can operate the identity and audit layer.

## Key Claims
- Verified access is a narrower control than a general frontier-model licensing agency.
- Identity and customer due diligence can make full-capability access more accountable for legitimate enterprise, scientific, or defensive users.
- The approach is most relevant when broad safety filters block useful biology, cybersecurity, or research workflows.
- Verified access can reduce [[AIRegulatoryCaptureRisk]] if it substitutes for broad compliance gates, but it can increase gatekeeping if implemented only through large cloud platforms.
- User verification does not eliminate the need for downstream controls when dangerous outputs require physical-world execution.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Verified access is not a full safety solution. It depends on reliable identity, organizational controls, audit design, privacy boundaries, and enforcement against account sharing or proxies. It may also exclude independent researchers or smaller firms if verification becomes expensive or discretionary.
