## Definition

君意即法 / ruler will as law is the official-survival pattern where legal judgment is treated as the execution of the ruler's current intent rather than fidelity to stable written rules, procedures, or an autonomous legal standard.

## Current Synthesis

The pattern appears in both explicit doctrine and operational calibration. When accused of unfair judging, [[DuZhouWesternHan|杜周]] answers that the ruler's intent is law and that an official need not cling to fixed rules if he follows the ruler's will. [[ZhangTangWesternHan|张汤]] is instead said to condemn whom [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] wants condemned and release or mitigate for whom Wudi wants mercy. His command of legal terminology lets current preference travel through procedural language, while [[JiAnWesternHan|汲黯]] can challenge the public harm but cannot defeat him clause by clause. The joined evidence makes usefulness depend on locating the real legal center in imperial preference, whether the official states that principle openly or embeds it in case technique. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-402-2-shudiwushu-de-kuli-duzhou-weihe-zuizhong-deyi-shanzhong-lqrunv51se0w7_2fi2ifs_f7zub4|Hanji 402-2]] [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-288-shen-laoshu-de-huai-haizi-ruhe-chengwei-hanchao-diyi-kuli-ltglqpdnj-9u2igdtuccds5z8lpn|Hanji 288]]

The concept is narrower than all [[LegalistRulerTechnique|法家君术]]. Legalist technique can include incentives, standards, appointments, and ruler control; ruler will as law names the dangerous collapse of law into immediate sovereign desire. It helps explain why a hated cruel official can survive while still offending nearly everyone: if the official reliably tracks the power center and later moderates when the environment shifts, enemies in the bureaucracy may not be enough to destroy him.

## Key Claims

- The pattern separates legal effectiveness from procedural justice.
- It makes the ruler's intent the real source of legal action, even when written rules appear to provide the form.
- Officials who master this pattern can become useful instruments of harsh centralized power.
- The same pattern creates broad resentment because colleagues cannot rely on stable rules against ruler-directed enforcement.
- Specialist legal vocabulary can conceal or normalize the pattern by making ruler-responsive outcomes appear to arise from autonomous rules.
- Survival depends on reading changes in the power environment; pure harshness without later adjustment may become dangerous.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept should not be flattened into a claim that all Han law was merely arbitrary ruler desire. The two episodes give pointed accounts of two cruel officials' operating logic; they do not reconstruct the full Western Han legal system or document every judgment. Hanji 288 does not identify each case in which Zhang allegedly followed Wudi's preferred outcome, and Ji An's hostile assessment is evidence of a court conflict rather than neutral proof of all motives. The connection to Legalist technique is conceptual and adjacent, while the joined cases specifically concern the collapse of rule, ruler intent, technical interpretation, and official self-preservation.

## What Changed

- Added Zhang Tang's operational case, in which punishment and mercy allegedly track Wudi's desired outcomes without an explicit doctrinal statement.
- Added technical legal vocabulary as the mechanism that can make ruler-responsive adjudication appear rule-grounded.
