## Definition

Wugu Political Panic / 巫蛊政治恐慌 is the pattern in which alleged hidden curse practice becomes politically actionable evidence, allowing illness, resentment, palace rivalry, succession anxiety, interrogation, torture, and denunciation to turn invisible ritual suspicion into state punishment.

## Current Synthesis

The Western Han background source defines wugu as curse practice using figures, needles, burial, and hidden ritual action, then shows how court belief in invisible harm can expand through digging, accusation, torture, and denunciation. The core danger is institutional: when rulers and investigators treat hidden ritual action as provable political attack, the search for evidence can produce the case it claims to uncover.

Hanji 773 moves the pattern into Emperor He's palace. [[EmpressYinHeLateHan|Empress Yin]] loses favor while [[EmpressDowagerDengLateHan|Deng Sui]] rises; after Yin reportedly threatens the Deng family, Yin and her maternal grandmother Deng Zhu are accused of wugu. The case leads to investigation, family deaths under torture, exile of relatives, Yin Gang's suicide, and Yin's 102-06-22 deposition. The source does not prove the curse accusation true; it shows how wugu language can convert harem danger into formal deposition and kin punishment.

Hanji 913 supplies a later late-Han parallel. [[WangFuLateHan|Wang Fu]] fears [[EmpressSongLateHan|Empress Song]]'s resentment and accuses her of wugu, after which [[EmperorLingOfHan|Emperor Ling]] removes her seal, sends her to prison, and lets the case destroy her natal family. The episode treats the accusation as a palace-political weapon rather than reliable evidence of curse practice.

The current synthesis is therefore not a claim that every recorded wugu case is fabricated. It is a narrower governance warning: when invisible harm is admissible as political evidence, vulnerable palace actors can be destroyed through accusation systems that reward preemptive fear, coerced testimony, and ruler anxiety more than observable proof.

## Key Claims

- Wugu panic depends on institutions treating hidden ritual action as politically actionable evidence.
- Palace rivalry and succession anxiety make wugu accusations useful even when the underlying truth is unclear.
- Torture and denunciation can expand a case far beyond the original claim.
- The strongest warning is that superstition becomes most dangerous when joined to state violence.
- Hanji 773 adds an Emperor-He-era harem-deposition branch before the later Ling-era Empress Song case.
- Hanji 913 adds that wugu accusation can be used as a preemptive palace weapon by an actor who fears future resentment.

## Evidence

- Western Han mechanism: [[87-guonian-zui-shihe-liao-xuanxue-tantao-shenxian-fangshu-fazhanshi-wei-wan-daixu-704051997]] defines wugu through hidden curse practice and presents the political danger of invisible harm being pursued through digging, torture, accusation, and denunciation.
- Emperor-He harem deposition: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-773-donghan-meinv-zhengzhijia-dengsui-ruhe-nixi-cheng-huanghou-lsmm5ngthj368guumm2xbwc3vea5|Hanji 773]] reports that Empress Yin and Deng Zhu are accused of wugu, investigated by Zhang Shen and Chen Bao, and punished through deposition, family deaths, exile, and suicide in the Yin kin network.
- Ling-era palace weaponization: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-913-kan-dong-saiweng-shima-wu-tou-rensheng-fuhuo-lkfbrwtgsjma85bzugqonzk3hy4g|Hanji 913]] reports Wang Fu's wugu accusation against Empress Song after he fears her future resentment, followed by seal removal, imprisonment, and natal-family destruction.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept should not flatten all ritual practice into fraud or assume every accusation was knowingly fabricated. The bounded evidence supports a political-mechanism claim, not a full religious history of wugu belief. Hanji 773 is especially useful for political consequence but does not independently prove whether Yin or Deng Zhu performed curse practices. Hanji 913 strongly frames Wang Fu's accusation as strategic, but that source also remains a podcast interpretation rather than a complete evidentiary dossier.
