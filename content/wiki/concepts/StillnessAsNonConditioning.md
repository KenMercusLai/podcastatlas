# Stillness as Non-Conditioning

## Definition
Stillness as non-conditioning is [[ShyalpaRinpoche]]'s account of stillness as freedom from chasing, rejecting, or solidifying thoughts and feelings.

## Current Synthesis
The source distinguishes stillness from withdrawal. Stillness does not mean abandoning family, work, thoughts, or life; it means not being trapped by the need to make something out of every thought. Shyalpa Rinpoche compares stillness to a mirror that reflects beautiful and ugly things without judging, chasing, or rejecting them.

## Key Claims
- Thoughts and feelings do not have an inherent permanent place unless people condition them.
- Stillness is not the absence of thought or responsibility.
- The mirror metaphor explains receptive awareness without judgment, pursuit, or rejection.
- Stillness is freedom that does not trap or distract.
- The episode connects conditioned thought to borders, self-versus-other dualism, war, and narrow attachment.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept is grounded in one contemplative interview. Its political extension is philosophical and does not replace material explanations of war, resources, states, or borders.
