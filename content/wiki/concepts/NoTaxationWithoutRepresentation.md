# No Taxation Without Representation

## Definition

No taxation without representation is the colonial constitutional claim that Parliament could not legitimately tax people whose interests and circumstances were not meaningfully represented in its lawmaking.

## Current Synthesis

The episode roots the slogan in rival understandings of British liberty rather than treating it as a ready-made demand for independence. Parliament defended forms of virtual representation also used inside Britain, but geographical distance and legislators' limited colonial knowledge made that claim especially weak for Americans. The Tea Act sharpened the issue because a commercially attractive price could still function as a test of parliamentary sovereignty.

## Key Claims

- Material tax burden and constitutional legitimacy are distinct questions.
- A lower-priced commodity can remain politically unacceptable when accepting it concedes disputed authority.
- Virtual representation weakens as distance, information gaps, and lack of accountability grow.
- Revolutionary claims can begin as arguments about inherited rights inside an empire rather than demands to leave it.
- Monopoly privilege can fuse commercial grievance with constitutional resistance.

## Evidence

- Constitutional inheritance: [[348-the-boston-tea-party-part-2-glt5189620267]] presents the slogan as grounded in colonists' understanding of English liberties.
- Knowledge boundary: [[348-the-boston-tea-party-part-2-glt5189620267]] attributes to Adam I. P. Smith the point that almost nobody in the Commons had direct colonial knowledge.
- Tea Act case: [[348-the-boston-tea-party-part-2-glt5189620267]] says direct East India Company exports undercut smuggled tea while preserving the sovereignty dispute.
- Radical support: [[348-the-boston-tea-party-part-2-glt5189620267]] notes British sympathizers who treated colonial claims as continuous with an English republican tradition.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source supports the colonial case but does not show that direct representation was then universal within Britain or that all resistance was disinterested. Smuggling, property, land, commercial position, and elite ambition mattered alongside constitutional principle. Nor did the slogan initially imply a republican break with the monarchy.

## What Changed

- Created the concept to distinguish the amount of a tax from the legitimacy of the authority imposing it.
