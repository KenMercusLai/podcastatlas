# Model Building Code Diffusion

## Definition
Model building code diffusion is the process by which a rule developed through a private model-code organization acquires broad public force when many jurisdictions adopt the resulting code.

## Current Synthesis
The source describes the [[InternationalCodeCouncil]]'s International Building Code as the foundation for most local American building codes. This structure can reduce the need for every jurisdiction to design technical rules independently, but it also concentrates leverage: a specialized amendment can spread well beyond the location and problem that motivated it.

The seven-foot stretcher amendment illustrates the mechanism. A technical committee rejected an open-ended local-equipment rule, but [[GregoryAVictor|Gregory A. Victor]] returned with a fixed dimension and won the broader membership vote. The proposal reported no cost impact, while the episode argues that larger shafts, omitted elevators, and forgone housing are indirect costs that conventional review may miss.

## Key Claims
- Widely adopted model codes turn private standard-setting into a major public-governance mechanism.
- Technical-committee and membership decisions can apply different evidentiary and persuasive filters.
- Standardization can solve local variation while imposing one requirement across unlike buildings and markets.
- Cost review can miss indirect effects such as design changes, omitted features, or projects that never proceed.
- The elevator case does not establish that model-code diffusion is generally harmful; it identifies a scale and accountability problem.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not compare the model-code system with fragmented local drafting, quantify benefits from consistency, or establish the amendment's current adoption and causal impact. The claim that industry representatives were absent during the vote is explicitly relayed information rather than independently established evidence in the supplied summary.

## What Changed
