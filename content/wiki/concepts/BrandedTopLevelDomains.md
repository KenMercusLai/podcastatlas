# Branded Top-Level Domains

## Definition
Branded top-level domains are internet extensions controlled by a company, organization, or public figure for proprietary identity, internal use, customer or partner portals, marketing, or defensive control.

## Current Synthesis
Owning the string to the right of the dot gives a brand more control than registering one name under `.com`. A company can keep the namespace private, create structured customer or partner addresses, signal authenticity, or prevent another party from controlling a matching extension. The strategic benefit remains conditional because users already understand `.com`, browsers and search mediate discovery, and a proprietary extension still requires costly operation and adoption work.

## Key Claims
- A branded extension can create a controlled namespace rather than one isolated website address.
- Defensive value can matter even when the company has no strong public-registration plan.
- Private operation and public sale are different strategies; `.google` illustrates the private route.
- Structured addresses could support host, customer, product, or partner portals, but examples such as `Samantha.Airbnb` remain hypothetical until deployed.
- Brand control does not automatically create user recognition, security, traffic, or economic return.

## Evidence
- Prior and current applications - [[tech-20261119-1119-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261119-1119-mp-tech-pod-128]] names Google, Amazon, Banana Republic, Airbnb, and Taylor Swift's management company while reporting about 300 brand applications in the current round.
- Private namespace - [[tech-20261119-1119-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261119-1119-mp-tech-pod-128]] says Google uses `.google` without offering public registrations.
- Defensive and portal uses - [[tech-20261119-1119-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261119-1119-mp-tech-pod-128]] presents defensive acquisition and individualized Airbnb host portals as possible motives rather than confirmed outcomes.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode does not measure traffic, trust, fraud reduction, customer adoption, operating cost, or return on branded extensions. A defensive application may be rational without producing visible innovation, while a memorable proprietary string can still confuse users trained to expect `.com`.

## What Changed
- Added root-level namespace control as a distinct form of brand strategy.
- Separated private operation, structured portals, marketing, and defensive ownership.
