# Libet Experiment Interpretation / 利比特实验解释

Libet experiment interpretation is the caution [[ep256-ai-shidai-ziyou-yizhi-hai-cunzai-ma-lk9aci8oqnwerk26jy683nbdddcx]] attaches to [[BenjaminLibet|Benjamin Libet]]'s famous button-press task. In the experiment, brain activity appears before the participant reports conscious intention, which has often been read as evidence against [[FreeWill|free will]].

The episode treats the data as important but rejects overextension. A participant's arbitrary decision to press a button is not the same as choosing a career, resisting addiction, parenting, committing a crime, or deciding how to live. For [[TuMotuo|土摩托]], the experiment narrows naive self-report but cannot settle [[MoralResponsibilityUnderDeterminism]] or [[BiologicalAgency]].

## Key Claims
- Neural readiness before conscious report challenges the feeling that conscious intention always begins action.
- The task's narrowness matters: random finger movement is a weak proxy for meaningful choice.
- Neuroscience can pressure free-will intuitions without automatically deciding legal, ethical, or existential questions.
- The experiment belongs near [[ConsciousnessMeasurement]] because it infers inner timing from measurable brain activity.
