# Native American Oral Literature / 美洲原住民口头文学

Native American oral literature / 美洲原住民口头文学 is the transmission frame for [[32-yindianren-jiang-le-yi-bai-wan-ci-de-gushi-shi-maerkesi-de-laoshi-596386714]]. The source uses [[StoriesToldAMillionTimes|《讲了一百万次的故事》]] to treat the tales as oral, variant-rich, and regionally embedded rather than as polished literary plots.

The episode repeatedly emphasizes that oral stories can feel abrupt because they are doing work other than modern novelistic smoothness. A tale may explain why the moon has spots, why stars exist, why salmon move upriver, why fire is hidden in wood, why animals have markings, why a plant matters, or why a rule around hunting and food exists. Collection, translation, missionary writing, and colonial contact can add more layers, so the page sits close to [[StoryMotifTransmission]] and [[MythicSourceLayering]].

## Key Claims
- Oral stories preserve local explanation, performance memory, and practical life rules alongside wonder.
- Variants are not noise; they are part of how oral literature survives across communities and retellings.
- Later recording can mix Indigenous, European, missionary, and colonial materials inside one story.
- The source's strongest comparative claim is [[OralMythMagicalRealismSource]], not a claim that every motif directly influenced one later writer.
