# Listener-Supported Media

## Definition

Listener-supported media is a funding model in which audiences sustain journalism and programming through one-time gifts, recurring donations, or paid membership products while core public access remains available.

## Current Synthesis

The earlier NPR appeal presents recurring listener gifts as a response to lost government funding. The NPR+ episode adds product mechanics: sponsor-free feeds, bonus and archive material, early access, and community benefits can give supporters private value while their payments help sustain a wider free public service.

Support remains a conversion and retention problem. The episode says fewer than 2% of NPR podcast listeners support NPR+, versus fewer than 10% of broadcast listeners donating to stations. Direct enrollment can create a stronger relationship, but failed payments, platform fees, benefit quality, and listener feedback all affect whether support persists.

## Key Claims

- Recurring audience payments can diversify funding when government or advertising revenue is constrained.
- Supporter benefits can coexist with free public access when payments finance a broader system.
- A network may pool supporter revenue rather than assign it only to the program that generated enrollment.
- Direct contact and membership benefits can deepen a donor relationship beyond one audio feed.
- Low participation and involuntary churn limit how much audience support can contribute.

## Evidence

- Donation evidence - [[congress-has-voted-to-eliminate-government-funding-for-public-media|Congress has voted to eliminate government funding for public media]] asks listeners for recurring gifts to protect local journalism, emergency access, and free service.
- Participation evidence - [[the-economics-of-podcasting-and-npr-plus|The economics of podcasting and NPR+]] reports sub-2% support among podcast listeners and a sub-10% broadcast donation rate.
- Product evidence - [[the-economics-of-podcasting-and-npr-plus|The economics of podcasting and NPR+]] describes sponsor-free listening, bonus material, archives, early access, events, and a shop discount.
- System-benefit evidence - [[the-economics-of-podcasting-and-npr-plus|The economics of podcasting and NPR+]] says subscription value returns to NPR and public-radio stations rather than only an originating show.
- Retention evidence - [[the-economics-of-podcasting-and-npr-plus|The economics of podcasting and NPR+]] separates voluntary cancellation from failed-payment churn.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The evidence comes from NPR's own appeal and product explanation. It does not establish optimal pricing, causal effects on retention, station distribution, donor substitution, audience equity, or whether paid benefits meaningfully increase total support. Benefits intended to reward supporters can also create administrative cost or a two-tier experience.
