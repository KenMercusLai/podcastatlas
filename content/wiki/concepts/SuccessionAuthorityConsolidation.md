# 继位权威整合 / Succession Authority Consolidation

## Definition
Succession authority consolidation is the pattern in which a new ruler turns inherited title, kinship, or appointment into practical control by pairing personnel recognition, advice channels, and selective coercion. The concept is narrower than succession itself: it focuses on the first operating tests after succession, when commanders, relatives, appointees, and outside patrons learn whether the new authority is real.

## Current Synthesis
Hanji 1053 supplies the current source case through [[SunQuan|孙权]]. Sun Quan's succession after [[SunCe|孙策]] begins with senior support from Zhang Zhao and Zhou Yu in earlier evidence, but this episode shows the next layer: he inspects commanders, rewards [[LyuMeng|吕蒙]] after seeing disciplined troops, accepts [[LuoTong|骆统]]'s talent-observation advice, punishes [[SunFuLateHan|孙辅]] for secretly inviting [[CaoCao|曹操]], and destroys [[LiShuLateHan|李术]] after cutting off Cao-side rescue.

The concept is not a simple praise of severity. Sun Quan's actions show real consolidation because recognition and discipline move together, but the Li Shu campaign also includes civilian slaughter and forced relocation. The source therefore makes consolidation a state-building mechanism with moral and social cost, not a pure leadership virtue.

## Key Claims
- Inherited title is unstable until inherited people and forces are tested again under the successor.
- Personnel recognition can consolidate authority when the successor observes actual performance rather than relying only on senior opinions or inherited status.
- Advice channels matter because a new ruler needs repeated contact with worthies before they become usable capacity.
- Internal defectors and semi-autonomous appointees become urgent when they can invite an outside patron to turn a domestic succession problem into a wider war.
- Coercive consolidation may produce real authority while also imposing costs that must remain visible in the synthesis.

## Evidence
- Personnel recognition: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1053-genzhe-sunquan-xuexi-ruhe-zhengdun-jituan-gaoguan-lq9hy25xonjzhj1sfisvauu3bwmi|Hanji 1053]] shows Sun Quan inspecting military units and increasing Lyu Meng's troops after seeing their discipline.
- Advice-channel formation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1053-genzhe-sunquan-xuexi-ruhe-zhengdun-jituan-gaoguan-lq9hy25xonjzhj1sfisvauu3bwmi|Hanji 1053]] has Luo Tong advise Sun Quan to honor worthies, meet them, ask about their situations, and observe their speech.
- Internal threat suppression: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1053-genzhe-sunquan-xuexi-ruhe-zhengdun-jituan-gaoguan-lq9hy25xonjzhj1sfisvauu3bwmi|Hanji 1053]] records Sun Fu's secret message to Cao Cao and Sun Quan's punishment of his intimates, troop breakup, and eastward relocation.
- Outside rescue cutoff and coercive result: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1053-genzhe-sunquan-xuexi-ruhe-zhengdun-jituan-gaoguan-lq9hy25xonjzhj1sfisvauu3bwmi|Hanji 1053]] reports Sun Quan's letter to Cao Cao before attacking Li Shu, followed by Wan City's capture, Li Shu's death, civilian slaughter, and forced relocation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source's management language is interpretive and should not be turned into a universal organization rule. The current evidence is also Sun Quan-centered; Liu Zhang's Yizhou failure in the same episode is a contrast case, not yet a second fully developed positive example. Finally, coercive success does not erase civilian harm: the Li Shu campaign is evidence for effective consolidation and for the costs of making authority visible through force.
