# 继位权威整合 / Succession Authority Consolidation

## Definition
Succession authority consolidation is the pattern in which a new ruler turns inherited title, kinship, or appointment into practical control by pairing external breathing room, visible support structures, autonomy choices, personnel recognition, advice channels, selective coercion, and local pacification. The concept is narrower than succession itself: it focuses on the first operating tests after succession, when commanders, relatives, appointees, local communities, and outside patrons learn whether the new authority is real.

## Current Synthesis
The current evidence makes this a four-stage [[SunQuan|孙权]] case. Hanji 1052 gives the protection and legibility stage. [[ZhangHongLateHan|张纮]] restrains [[CaoCao|曹操]] from attacking during Sun Quan's mourning and redirects the moment toward formal recognition. [[LadyWuSunJian|吴夫人]] entrusts Zhang Hong and [[ZhangZhaoLateHan|张昭]] with assisting the under-19 successor, while [[DongXiLateHan|董袭]] reassures her that geography, Sun Ce's favor, Sun Quan's inheritance, and civil-military alignment can hold Jiangdong together.

Hanji 1058 adds the autonomy test. Cao Cao demands that Sun Quan send a younger brother or son to court, and [[ZhouYuLateHan|周瑜]] argues that doing so would bind Sun Quan to Cao Cao's summons. Because Jiangdong still has commanderies, soldiers, grain, and stable local support, Zhou Yu advises refusal. Lady Wu endorses Zhou Yu and tells Sun Quan to treat him like an elder brother, so the decision becomes both a strategy judgment and a household-authorized succession act.

Hanji 1053 supplies the next operating tests. Sun Quan inspects commanders, rewards [[LyuMeng|吕蒙]] after seeing disciplined troops, accepts [[LuoTong|骆统]]'s talent-observation advice, punishes [[SunFuLateHan|孙辅]] for secretly inviting Cao Cao, and destroys [[LiShuLateHan|李术]] after cutting off Cao-side rescue. The synthesis is therefore not simple praise of severity. Recognition, autonomy, and discipline make consolidation real, but the Li Shu campaign also includes civilian slaughter and forced relocation.

Hanji 1061 adds the local pacification stage. Sun Quan's attack on [[HuangZu|黄祖]] stalls when [[Shanyue|山越]] rebellion erupts behind him, forcing him to turn from external pressure to county-level control. He assigns officers into vulnerable counties and sends [[HeQiLateHan|贺齐]] to suppress major county rebellions, rebuild seats, and select troops. The new layer makes consolidation practical and territorial: inherited authority must be made governable inside local armed communities.

## Key Claims
- Inherited title is unstable until external attack pressure is managed and inherited people can see a support structure around the successor.
- Senior advisers and family elders make succession legible before the successor can prove independent command capacity.
- A new ruler's autonomy is tested by whether he can accept recognition without handing over family leverage to a stronger patron.
- Personnel recognition can consolidate authority when the successor observes actual performance rather than relying only on senior opinions or inherited status.
- Advice channels matter because a new ruler needs repeated contact with worthies before they become usable capacity.
- Internal defectors, semi-autonomous appointees, and local armed communities become urgent when they can turn succession weakness into outside dependency or rear disruption.
- Coercive and administrative consolidation may produce real authority while also imposing costs that must remain visible in the synthesis.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source's management language is interpretive and should not be turned into a universal organization rule. The current evidence is still Sun Quan-centered; Liu Zhang's Yizhou failure in Hanji 1053 is a contrast case, not a second fully developed positive example. Hanji 1058's hostage-refusal layer depends on Zhou Yu and Lady Wu as much as on Sun Quan alone. Hanji 1061 proves local capacity only within the source's Shanyue and county-rebellion frame. Finally, coercive success does not erase civilian harm: the Li Shu campaign is evidence for effective consolidation and for the costs of making authority visible through force.

## What Changed
- Added Hanji 1058's refusal of Cao Cao's hostage demand as an autonomy test inside succession consolidation.
- Inserted Zhou Yu and Lady Wu as the adviser and household-authority channels that make the refusal credible.
- Preserved Hanji 1052 as the external-restraint stage and Hanji 1053 as the personnel/coercion operating-test stage.
- Added Hanji 1061's local pacification stage: Shanyue disruption, county officer placement, He Qi's rebellion suppression, and troop extraction.
