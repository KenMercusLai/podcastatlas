# Everyday Humanitarianism In War Film / 战争片世俗人道主义

## Definition
Everyday humanitarianism in war film / 战争片世俗人道主义 is the move of elevating food, family, children, survival, and ordinary labor into humane values that resist war without necessarily analyzing the war's political causes and responsibilities.

## Current Synthesis
The concept comes from the source's reading of [[WelcomeToLailongRestaurant|《欢迎来龙餐馆》]]. The film's "eat well, live well, raise children" value is treated as sincere and emotionally powerful: it protects a child from being swallowed by war and turns restaurant labor into a moral refuge. This gives the film a strong ethical baseline and ties it to [[StrangerHospitalityEthics]].

The limitation is scale. Everyday humanitarianism can say what should be preserved from violence, but it cannot by itself distinguish invasion from anti-invasion, resistance from terrorism, ordinary soldier innocence from state responsibility, or sectarian and local political conflicts. The source's core critique is that the film sometimes presents the outsider's "just live" position as if it had surpassed politics, when it may actually mark the point where the film stops doing political analysis.

## Key Claims
- Food, children, family, and survival can create a real humane baseline against war.
- Ordinary care becomes cinematic ideology when daily life is made sacred and visually heightened.
- Protecting one child is narratively and emotionally meaningful but not automatically a superior political answer.
- "Life is more important than war" is ethically valid but politically incomplete.
- Outsider neutrality can be mistaken for wisdom when the story does not enter local responsibility and legitimacy disputes.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept should not dismiss ordinary-care ethics. The source explicitly treats food, children, and survival as sincere values; the criticism is only that they cannot bear the whole burden of war analysis.
