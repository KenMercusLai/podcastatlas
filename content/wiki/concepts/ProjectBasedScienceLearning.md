# Project-Based Science Learning / 项目式科学学习

Project-based science learning / 项目式科学学习 is the inquiry mode developed in [[ep262-gaokao-guohou-zhishi-guiling-women-xuyao-zenyang-de-kexue-jiaoyu-ls8ecqcdpe9d5ioytyca3jqdf-3r]]. The source presents projects as a way to put concepts into action, not as a substitute for learning concepts.

The cotton-candy-machine case is the clearest classroom example. Students encounter current meters, batteries, magnets, coils, electric motors, sugar melting, and alcohol-lamp safety as pieces of a working project, while the teacher does not expect complete mastery of electromagnetism in one lesson. The project gives students a scaffold for later, more formal learning.

The episode also pushes project learning outside expensive equipment. A rural or suburban school can use local stones, campus plants, nearby mountains, birds, subway systems, or a village sports-field problem as scientific objects. What matters is organizing observation, question-asking, evidence, and explanation around a real situation.

## Key Claims
- Projects are useful when they convert abstract science into observable, manipulable, and revisable situations.
- A good project gives students a first conceptual foothold, then later instruction makes the underlying science clearer.
- Project-based science learning should not be "pure play"; it needs evidence, concepts, reflection, and teacher guidance.
- Local materials can be powerful scientific resources when teachers connect them to geology, ecology, systems, or design problems.
- AI and maker tools can help projects move faster, but they can also become shallow display if success narratives outrun inquiry.
