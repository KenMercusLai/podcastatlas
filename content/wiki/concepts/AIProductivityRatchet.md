# AI Productivity Ratchet / AI 生产率棘轮

## Definition
AI productivity ratchet is the workplace pattern in which faster AI-assisted output becomes a new baseline for workload, availability, scope, or review responsibility instead of creating proportional recovery time or reward.

## Current Synthesis
The two sources show the pattern from both worker-incentive and operating evidence. [[ep275-token-tongzhang-shidai-shui-hai-neng-bu-ke-tidai-renzai-zhongliu-tebie-cehua-01-lgnagvell4jyvv7eejrcgno2xjsr]] argues that capable AI users can be punished with more tasks while prompting, checking, context building, and accountability remain hidden. [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-debt-spiral-or-new-golden-age-super-bowl-insider-trading-booming-token-budgets-ferraris-new-ev-40104725]] adds a source-attributed workplace study in which AI users worked faster and more broadly but also extended work into more hours.

The current judgment is conditional: AI can increase leverage and even demand for skilled workers, but measured output gains do not establish better work unless organizations redesign scope, rewards, pacing, and review capacity.

## Key Claims
- Faster AI-assisted production can increase task assignment rather than reduce working time.
- Prompting, review, correction, and responsibility are often less visible than output speed.
- Broader task coverage can indicate worker leverage while also increasing cognitive load and workday length.
- Adoption incentives weaken when employees expect every gain to become a higher permanent target.
- Sustainable deployment requires explicit choices about scope, reward, quality, pacing, and error ownership.

## Evidence
- Worker-incentive mechanism: [[ep275-token-tongzhang-shidai-shui-hai-neng-bu-ke-tidai-renzai-zhongliu-tebie-cehua-01-lgnagvell4jyvv7eejrcgno2xjsr]] argues that strong AI users can receive more work without matching reward or redesign.
- Operating pattern: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-debt-spiral-or-new-golden-age-super-bowl-insider-trading-booming-token-budgets-ferraris-new-ev-40104725]] reports faster work, wider task coverage, and longer hours in the same company study.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The All-In source summarizes rather than reproduces the cited study, so the wiki does not independently establish causality, effect size, or generalizability. Longer hours may reflect temporary experimentation, employee choice, management pressure, or task demand. Increased knowledge-worker demand can coexist with worse workload design for individual workers.
