## Definition

知足知止式退场 / Timely Retirement Risk Avoidance is the decision to leave office after rank, reputation, money, and mission have reached sufficiency, before continued visibility turns success into humiliation, danger, or dependency.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 431 creates the concept through [[ShuGuangShuShouWesternHan|疏广与疏受]]. In the episode, Shu Guang tells Shu Shou that satisfaction avoids humiliation and knowing when to stop avoids danger. The two are crown-prince teachers, have reached two-thousand-shi rank, and possess enough honor and wealth. They therefore ask to retire together by citing illness, and their departure is publicly praised.

The concept is narrower than the broader [[PowerExitTrap|权力退场困境]]. It marks a successful, low-conflict exit before the trap fully forms. The actors do not wait for accusation, ruler suspicion, or factional reversal; they read sufficiency as an active signal. Hanji 431 also links the exit to wealth handling: retirement becomes durable because reward money is not converted into an excessive family accumulation project.

## Key Claims

- Sufficiency can be an action signal, not only a feeling.
- Leaving before danger becomes visible can preserve reputation better than maximizing office tenure.
- Retirement is easier when honor, money, and public recognition have already reached a socially legible enough point.
- A paired or mutually recognized exit can reduce the shame or suspicion attached to withdrawal.
- Timely retirement is strengthened when wealth and descendants are handled with restraint.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode does not prove that staying would have destroyed Shu Guang or Shu Shou. The concept is therefore a risk-avoidance pattern, not a deterministic prediction. It applies most clearly when a role has reached visible completion and additional tenure mainly increases exposure.
