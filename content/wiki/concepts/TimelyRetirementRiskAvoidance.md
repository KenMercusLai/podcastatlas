## Definition

知足知止式退场 / Timely Retirement Risk Avoidance is the decision to leave office after rank, reputation, money, and mission have reached sufficiency, before continued visibility turns success into humiliation, danger, or dependency.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 431 creates the concept through [[ShuGuangShuShouWesternHan|疏广与疏受]]. In the episode, Shu Guang tells Shu Shou that satisfaction avoids humiliation and knowing when to stop avoids danger. The two are crown-prince teachers, have reached two-thousand-shi rank, and possess enough honor and wealth. They therefore ask to retire together by citing illness, and their departure is publicly praised.

The concept is narrower than the broader [[PowerExitTrap|权力退场困境]]. It marks a successful, low-conflict exit before the trap fully forms. The actors do not wait for accusation, ruler suspicion, or factional reversal; they read sufficiency as an active signal. Hanji 431 also links the exit to wealth handling: retirement becomes durable because reward money is not converted into an excessive family accumulation project.

A qualified near-case comes from [[ZhaoYuWesternHan|赵禹]], who reportedly becomes gentler in old age, is demoted and later dismissed, returns home, and dies there. Unlike Shu Guang and Shu Shou, he does not initiate a timely retirement at visible sufficiency. His case therefore broadens the risk lesson without changing the concept's core: reducing harshness and surviving removal can still preserve a domestic end, but involuntary exit is not the same achievement as choosing the moment to leave. ## Key Claims

- Sufficiency can be an action signal, not only a feeling.
- Leaving before danger becomes visible can preserve reputation better than maximizing office tenure.
- Retirement is easier when honor, money, and public recognition have already reached a socially legible enough point.
- A paired or mutually recognized exit can reduce the shame or suspicion attached to withdrawal.
- Timely retirement is strengthened when wealth and descendants are handled with restraint.
- Late softening and acceptance of reduced office may preserve survival even when the exit is imposed, though this remains distinct from voluntary retirement.

- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-431-zhichang-bidu-kuli-zhizi-zhanganshi-de-zhichang-zhihui-lmxey8uoliwynimxcnh1khjefenl|Hanji 431]] says Xuan approves and grants gold, the crown prince also grants gold, and officials and commoners praise them as worthy people.

- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-348-1-guren-fancuo-houde-fenshi-qishilu-ljglue4ppujp0auyai0noltvcrwm|Hanji 348-1]] says Zhao Yu softens in old age, survives demotion and dismissal, and dies at home.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Hanji 431 does not prove that staying would have destroyed Shu Guang or Shu Shou. Hanji 348-1 does not establish that Zhao Yu's reported softening caused his survival, and his dismissal was not a voluntary timing choice. The concept is therefore a risk-avoidance pattern, not a deterministic prediction. It applies most clearly when a role has reached visible completion and additional tenure mainly increases exposure.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Hanji 431's Shu Guang and Shu Shou retirement case.
- Added Zhao Yu as a qualified involuntary-exit contrast rather than treating every safe ending as timely retirement.
