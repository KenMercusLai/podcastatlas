# Furniture Affordability Price Reset

## Definition
Furniture affordability price reset is a retailer's deliberate reduction of visible home-furnishing prices to restore value perception under cost-of-living pressure, usually funded by supply-chain or operating-cost changes.

## Current Synthesis
[[daisen-jinru-diandong-yashua-lingyu-chuantong-jindian-jiameng-chixu-shousuo-1010976825]] grounds the concept in [[IKEA]]'s European price-cut program. The source says IKEA committed EUR 1.2 billion to lower prices across hundreds of products, with average reductions of 15%-25%, and links the move to consumer pressure as well as packaging and automation savings. The concept is geographically bounded in the source because IKEA China has not yet followed the European cuts.

## Key Claims
- Furniture retailers can use broad price resets to rebuild affordability signals.
- Cost-of-living pressure makes visible basket prices strategically important.
- Supply-chain improvements can fund lower prices without relying only on margin sacrifice.
- Geographic differences matter because affordability pressure and competitive response vary by market.
- Price resets can strengthen value perception when they are tied to operating discipline.

## Evidence
- Investment evidence: [[daisen-jinru-diandong-yashua-lingyu-chuantong-jindian-jiameng-chixu-shousuo-1010976825]] says IKEA announced a EUR 1.2 billion European price-cut investment.
- Scope evidence: [[daisen-jinru-diandong-yashua-lingyu-chuantong-jindian-jiameng-chixu-shousuo-1010976825]] says hundreds of products would receive average reductions of 15%-25%.
- Cost-base evidence: [[daisen-jinru-diandong-yashua-lingyu-chuantong-jindian-jiameng-chixu-shousuo-1010976825]] links lower prices to packaging and automation savings.
- Market-boundary evidence: [[daisen-jinru-diandong-yashua-lingyu-chuantong-jindian-jiameng-chixu-shousuo-1010976825]] says IKEA China had not yet followed the European price cuts.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not show post-cut sales lift, consumer-response data, or margin effects. A price reset can be strategic without proving that all furniture retailers should follow the same timing or geography.

## What Changed
