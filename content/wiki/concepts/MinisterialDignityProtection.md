## Definition

九卿体面保护 / Ministerial Dignity Protection is the institutional principle that senior offices require procedural and ritual respect even when the officeholder is being summoned, questioned, or disciplined. The point is not personal privilege alone: humiliating a high office damages the court order that the office represents.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-813-gudai-de-gaoguan-jing-ye-hui-bei-ouda-ln-dw3xohnmpavkpu-bdvasvietv|Hanji 813]] grounds the concept through [[LiuJuLateHan|刘矩]], then dasinong. Liu Ju is summoned urgently to Shangshu Tai, rushed, shouted at, and beaten. [[ZuoXiongLateHan|左雄]] answers with an office-status argument: the Nine Ministers stand below the Three Excellencies but still carry a ritual dignity that should not be reduced to physical humiliation.

The repair is concrete. [[EmperorShunOfHan|汉顺帝]] accepts Zuo Xiong's point, and the source says that nine ministers were no longer beaten afterward. The concept therefore marks a bounded institutional repair inside an otherwise uneven Shun-era court: authority can still question or punish, but high office must not be made contemptible through procedural abuse.

## Key Claims

- Senior office dignity is a public asset, not only a personal comfort for the officeholder.
- Physical humiliation of high officials damages ritual hierarchy and court credibility.
- Procedural urgency can become institutional abuse when summons, rebuke, and beating collapse into one coercive act.
- A ruler can repair dignity norms without resolving every surrounding palace or appointment problem.
- The concept is narrower than immunity from investigation: it protects the form of handling, not blanket exemption from accountability.

## Evidence

- Triggering case: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-813-gudai-de-gaoguan-jing-ye-hui-bei-ouda-ln-dw3xohnmpavkpu-bdvasvietv|Hanji 813]] says Liu Ju is summoned to Shangshu Tai, rushed by officials, rebuked, and beaten.
- Dignity argument: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-813-gudai-de-gaoguan-jing-ye-hui-bei-ouda-ln-dw3xohnmpavkpu-bdvasvietv|Hanji 813]] says Zuo Xiong argues that the Nine Ministers are next below the Three Excellencies and should be handled with ritual respect.
- Accepted repair: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-813-gudai-de-gaoguan-jing-ye-hui-bei-ouda-ln-dw3xohnmpavkpu-bdvasvietv|Hanji 813]] says Shun accepts the memorial and the practice of beating nine ministers stops.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept is currently bounded to one Shun-era source note. It does not prove that all senior officials were protected in practice, that lower officials received similar protection, or that ministerial dignity prevented investigation. The same episode shows Shun keeping Song E's title despite criticism, so this repair should be treated as a narrow procedural correction rather than broad court reform.

## What Changed

- Created a narrow concept for the Liu Ju and Zuo Xiong case in Hanji 813.
- Separated office dignity from immunity by defining the issue as ritual and procedural handling.
- Linked ministerial humiliation to wider Shun-era problems of private favor, palace influence, and uneven responsiveness.
