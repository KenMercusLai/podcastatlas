# Demographic Standardization of Crime Rates

## Definition

Demographic standardization of crime rates adjusts group comparisons for differences in population composition—especially age and sex—so that a crude count-to-population ratio does not attribute demographic structure to nationality or another group label.

## Current Synthesis

The episode shows that both the denominator and its composition can materially change a politically charged crime statistic. Its disputed 22-fold comparison used a small-sample Afghan population estimate. Reconstructing annual population counts from census, visa, humanitarian-arrival, asylum, naturalization, and departure data changed the denominator; standardizing for age and sex then addressed the younger, more male risk profile of the compared population. The resulting estimate was roughly seven-fold.

That correction improves comparability but does not produce a causal nationality effect. The numerator counts convictions rather than unique offenders, nationality fields are self-reported and inconsistent, biometric records may affect detection, reported offences are a small subset of victimization, and social conditions can differ sharply between migrant and host populations.

## Key Claims

- An unstable population denominator can dominate a rate comparison, especially for a relatively small migrant group.
- Age and sex composition must be separated from nationality when offence risk is highly concentrated in particular demographic groups.
- Standardization can substantially reduce an inflated comparison without making the residual disparity irrelevant.
- Conviction counts, distinct offenders, recorded crime, and victimization prevalence are different measures.
- A standardized association remains non-causal when detection, repeat offending, reporting, migration history, and social conditions differ.

## Evidence

- Denominator reconstruction - [[c46ed825801a2b1bd8e16069674e07e0-c46ed825801a2b1bd8e16069674e07e0mp3aid6aec4fff51ac9ecbba3b7818chidd556eb54-6160-4]] says the 22-fold claim relied on a population estimate drawn from fewer than 60 survey respondents and describes a replacement built from multiple administrative sources.
- Age-sex adjustment - [[c46ed825801a2b1bd8e16069674e07e0-c46ed825801a2b1bd8e16069674e07e0mp3aid6aec4fff51ac9ecbba3b7818chidd556eb54-6160-4]] reports that three-quarters of sexual offences are committed by men aged 21–59 and that the Afghan population is about ten years younger, with standardization reducing the estimate to about seven-fold.
- Measurement limits - [[c46ed825801a2b1bd8e16069674e07e0-c46ed825801a2b1bd8e16069674e07e0mp3aid6aec4fff51ac9ecbba3b7818chidd556eb54-6160-4]] distinguishes convictions from unique offenders and victimization and notes inconsistent nationality data, possible identification effects, and underreporting.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Standardization is not a device for explaining away a disparity; it states more accurately what is being compared. The episode does not provide its full cleaned dataset, model specification, uncertainty interval, sensitivity analysis, or reproducible code, so the seven-fold estimate remains source-scoped. Nationality is also not ethnicity, and neither category alone identifies a mechanism.
