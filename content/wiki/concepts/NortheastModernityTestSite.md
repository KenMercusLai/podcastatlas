## Definition
Northeast modernity test site is the episode's frame for reading late Qing and early Republican Northeast China as a frontier where migration, land, religion, treaty power, empire, and global modernity interacted.

## Current Synthesis
The concept pushes against a Northeast story limited to later industrial decline, rice, farms, or "共和国长子" memory. In [[100-nian-qian-dongbei-nvhai-xie-gei-bali-de-xin-duitan-lishixuezhe-liji-putongren-ruhe-chuanguo-da-lishi-3d36f181-94ad-4883-8db3-5f040b5ed45b]], the region appears as a space newly opened after Qing restrictions, filled by migrants seeking land, missionaries seeking converts, and international forces working through treaty-era institutions. That makes villages such as [[SantaiZiCatholicVillage|三台子]] part of modern China's formation rather than a peripheral footnote.

## Key Claims
- The Northeast should not be read only through twentieth-century industrial nostalgia or decline.
- Large-scale migration after earlier Qing restrictions created villages with weaker old lineage structures than many southeastern settlements.
- Catholic mission villages became part of the region's local society formation, not only foreign religious outposts.
- Treaty power and imperial expansion are structural background conditions, even when individual actors live locally and ordinarily.
- The region's modernity is produced through overlap: farmers, land, religion, state weakness, maps, archives, and global networks.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
