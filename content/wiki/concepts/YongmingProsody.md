# 永明声律 / Yongming Prosody

## Definition

Yongming prosody is the Southern Qi literary movement associated with clearer recognition of the four tones and deliberate tonal arrangement in verse, treated as an important precursor to later regulated poetry.

## Current Synthesis

The episode places Zhou Yong and Yongming writers at a point where tonal categories became more explicit and poets used them more consciously in composition. Possible contact with Buddhist scripture and Sanskrit phonological knowledge is presented as one influence, not a fully demonstrated single origin.

Its historical importance lies in formalization. Later regulated verse fixed line patterns, parallel positions, and tonal sequences more completely, so Yongming prosody should be understood as a developmental source rather than a finished Tang system. The episode further argues that this formal turn belonged to a wider aristocratic culture of rank, ritual, and appropriate form, while the poetic rules survived long after that social order weakened.

## Key Claims

- Explicit four-tone awareness enabled more deliberate control of poetic sound.
- Yongming tonal practice is a precursor to, not an identity with, mature regulated verse.
- Buddhist and Sanskrit phonological knowledge may have contributed to tonal analysis.
- Formal rules create a learnable literary community while also setting entry standards.
- Prosodic forms can persist after the institutions and status culture that shaped their development decline.

## Evidence

- Tonal awareness - [[lvyohbnakaa_iop9nirokkjmhqs-lvyohbnakaa_iop9nirokkjmhqs|Episode 513]] attributes clearer four-tone analysis and deliberate arrangement to Zhou Yong and Yongming writers.
- Regulated-verse lineage - [[lvyohbnakaa_iop9nirokkjmhqs-lvyohbnakaa_iop9nirokkjmhqs|Episode 513]] presents Yongming tonal technique as an important source for later level-oblique patterning.
- Social form - [[lvyohbnakaa_iop9nirokkjmhqs-lvyohbnakaa_iop9nirokkjmhqs|Episode 513]] connects poetic regulation to aristocratic expectations of rank, ritual, and appropriate behavior.
- Persistence - [[lvyohbnakaa_iop9nirokkjmhqs-lvyohbnakaa_iop9nirokkjmhqs|Episode 513]] uses the “golden fruit” metaphor for prosody outlasting aristocratic society.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source does not provide phonological treatises, poem-level scansion, or a documented transmission chain into Tang rules. Sanskrit influence is presented as possible, and the aristocratic-culture explanation is interpretive rather than exclusive. Chinese prosody developed across longer linguistic, musical, textual, and institutional histories, and later rules changed through practice.

## What Changed

- Established Yongming prosody as a developmental bridge between tone analysis and regulated verse.
- Kept Sanskrit influence and aristocratic causation qualified rather than settled.
- Distinguished formal survival from social continuity.
