# Autism Cure-Scam Risk

## Definition
Autism cure-scam risk is the danger that promises to cure, erase, or repair autism exploit family fear and redirect attention away from support, accommodation, communication, and evidence-based intervention.

## Current Synthesis
The earlier autism source rejects cure promises as a market built on parental panic. The 不合时宜 episode extends that warning from obvious scams to more prestigious medical and scientific forms: a treatment can sound advanced, genetic, or institutionally backed and still be ethically dangerous if it treats autism-like behavior or developmental disability as a defect worth extreme risk.

The concept now distinguishes scam risk from intervention itself. The wiki's current position is not anti-treatment; it is anti-miracle, anti-erasure, and anti-risk-taking driven by stigma. Useful intervention should be evidence-based, proportionate, disclosed, and oriented toward the autistic person's communication and life.

## Key Claims
- "Cure" framing can turn autistic difference and family fear into a market.
- Scientific language can intensify the risk when families equate novelty with proof, safety, or necessity.
- Helpful intervention does not validate promises to make a person non-autistic.
- Anti-scam boundaries should preserve hope by redirecting it toward support, evidence, and capability building.
- Cure-seeking becomes more dangerous when joined to [[AbleistCurePressure]] and weak medical-risk disclosure.

- Diagnosis vulnerability - [[46-zhe-shijie-weiyi-de-ni-qing-xiangxin-na-duo-manman-zhanfang-de-hua-623717720]] shows how uncertainty after diagnosis makes families vulnerable to confident claims.

- Scientific-risk boundary - [[cong-jiyin-bianji-nvtong-zhisi-shuoqi-zibizheng-bushi-xuyao-bei-xiufu-de-cuowu-lg7dofyeqv-7noq9w74oh6hsyk4b]] links the gene-editing case to immature evidence, prior animal harm signals, and incomplete risk disclosure claims.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept should not be used to reject speech therapy, occupational therapy, behavioral support, medical treatment for co-occurring conditions, or interventions requested by autistic people themselves. Its target is cure rhetoric that bypasses evidence, consent, proportionality, and respect for personhood.
