## Definition
Block and dissent hygiene / 拉黑与异见卫生 is the practice of managing an online environment by blocking, deleting, ignoring, or actively seeking disagreement while watching the tradeoff between safety, comfort, and informational narrowing.

## Current Synthesis
The source refuses a single rule for blocking. Blocking can be an effective boundary and a quick way to reduce noise, but the panel also describes its addictive or world-controlling feel. Xiao Kuai's position is the clearest anti-cocoon warning: if he deletes or blocks followers too readily, he worries that he will only encounter agreement and become narrower.

Luo Yonghao offers a mixed strategy. He is willing to delete and block, but also searches his own name to make sure he still sees criticism and disagreement. The resulting synthesis treats online hygiene as a designed exposure system: clean enough to function, porous enough to avoid mistaking a curated feed for reality.

## Key Claims
- Blocking can protect attention and reduce abusive or low-value contact.
- Blocking can also create addictive control over the visible world.
- Refusing to block can preserve exposure to dissent, but may impose unnecessary emotional cost.
- A mixed strategy can combine boundary-setting with deliberate criticism search.
- The core risk is confusing a managed audience surface with the broader public.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This source is about public figures and comedy-panel experience, not a universal moderation policy. Severe harassment, doxxing, threats, stalking, or professional risk may justify much stronger blocking and reporting than the panel's debate implies.
