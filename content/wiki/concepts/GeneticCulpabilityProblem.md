# Genetic Culpability Problem / 遗传归责问题

Genetic culpability problem / 遗传归责问题 is the question of whether inherited traits, family violence, neurological findings, or psychological risk should change how law judges a person's responsibility. [[24-hanwei-yagebu-xiangxin-ni-shi-wo-weiyi-de-zhinian-576218441]] develops the problem through [[DefendingJacob|《捍卫雅各布》 / Defending Jacob]], where [[JacobBarber|Jacob Barber]] is evaluated against the violent history of [[BillyBarber|Billy Barber]] and the Barber family.

The source's legal stance is cautious. Risk traits may matter for fear, family self-understanding, mitigation arguments, or treatment questions, but they cannot by themselves become guilt. The hosts connect this to free will, self-fulfilling prophecy, and the danger of punishing a person for who they are rather than what they have done.

## Key Claims
- Genetic or psychological risk can explain concern without proving a criminal act.
- Punishing identity rather than conduct would undermine [[CriminalLawAsPowerLimitation]].
- If bad traits are treated as destiny, law risks becoming preventive detention for disliked or feared people.
- The concept is different from denying biology. The source asks how law can acknowledge risk without making risk equal guilt.
- [[ActionDefinesIdentity]] is the safer legal and ethical premise: behavior under conditions matters more than trait labels alone.
