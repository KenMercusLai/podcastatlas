## Definition

绣衣使者皇权监察 / Xiuyi Envoy Imperial Surveillance is the wiki's frame for [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]-era emperor-direct investigative authority: special envoys selected by the ruler, dispatched for particular cases or elite oversight, and feared because they can bypass normal hierarchy, report directly upward, and punish severe offenses.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-382-1-bazhan-shumu-hanwudi-zhizi-liudan-you-duo-huangtang-loi-6-s1z2okmskqvbfwryz-qjto|Hanji 382-1]] presents绣衣 authority through Jiang Chong's rise. After Jiang accuses [[LiuDanZhaoPrinceWesternHan|赵太子刘丹]] and wins Wudi's favor, he is made a Zhizhi Xiuyi envoy and later assigned to inspect royal kin, close ministers, and powerful households for excessive ritual display and illegality. The source shows the mechanism's useful and dangerous faces together: direct reporting can discipline elite overreach, but it also rewards denunciation and lets a favored inspector build personal enemies.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-388-2-yin-chanyan-cuosha-taizi-hanwudi-wannian-zuidade-huihen-lm9e_lrbbcc7a6kf22hbid04_u73|Hanji 388-2]] presents绣衣使者 as a high-pressure imperial tool. Their power is not only legal; it is reputational. The failed [[GongsunYongWesternHan|公孙勇]] and Hu Qian rebellion attempt depends on local fear that a supposed绣衣 figure might legitimately command arrest, escort, and deference. The episode therefore treats绣衣 authority as a coercive brand that can be impersonated by rebels as well as used by the ruler.

The same source links the mechanism to the [[WuguIncidentWesternHan|巫蛊之祸]] through [[JiangChongWesternHan|江充]]. Jiang Chong's status as a famous绣衣 figure helps explain why his investigation could create extreme pressure around [[LiuJuCrownPrinceWesternHan|卫太子刘据]]. The concept therefore sits between administrative supervision and succession violence: emperor-direct investigation can expose local disorder, but it can also magnify accusation panic when attached to court faction and ruler suspicion.

## Key Claims

- 绣衣使者 are presented as emperor-direct special investigators rather than ordinary local officials.
- Their power comes from bypassing the regular chain of command and reporting directly to the ruler.
- Hanji 382-1 shows the office applied to elite and royal oversight, especially through Jiang Chong's strict reporting against powerful households.
- Their reputation is strong enough that impostors can try to weaponize local fear of them.
- Effective local verification can interrupt that prestige panic, as in the Gongsun Yong branch.
- The Jiang Chong/Wugu branch shows that the same special-authority mechanism can become lethal inside succession politics.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept is bounded to Hanji 388-2 and Hanji 382-1. It does not reconstruct the full institutional history of绣衣使者, distinguish every title variant, or settle the exact legal boundary between inspector, censor, envoy, and later secret-police analogies. Hanji 382-1 supplies Jiang Chong's appointment and elite-policing profile, not the full office record. The comparison to later锦衣卫 is recorded as an explanatory analogy, not as institutional identity.
