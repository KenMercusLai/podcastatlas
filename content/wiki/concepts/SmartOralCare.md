# Smart Oral Care

## Definition
Smart oral care is the use of sensors, cameras, app feedback, and integrated cleaning functions to turn brushing and flossing into an instrumented consumer-hardware category.

## Current Synthesis
[[daisen-jinru-diandong-yashua-lingyu-chuantong-jindian-jiameng-chixu-shousuo-1010976825]] makes smart oral care visible through [[Dyson]]'s [[CameraJet]], a USD 499 device that combines electric brushing, water flossing, camera inspection, and live app images. The concept is commercially meaningful because it tests whether consumers will pay a premium for more observable hygiene routines, but the source leaves adoption unresolved because oral care is habitual, privacy-sensitive, and already served by cheaper devices.

## Key Claims
- Smart oral care reframes toothbrushes from simple cleaning tools into sensor-rich feedback devices.
- CameraJet makes the category more visual by showing tooth gaps and brushing views through an app.
- Integrated brushing and water flossing can justify a higher functional claim than a standalone toothbrush.
- Premium pricing makes usefulness, trust, and habit formation decisive adoption constraints.
- The category sits near broader consumer-AI and consumer-hardware product-fit problems.

## Evidence
- Product evidence: [[daisen-jinru-diandong-yashua-lingyu-chuantong-jindian-jiameng-chixu-shousuo-1010976825]] reports Dyson's 2026-09-01 CameraJet launch.
- Feature evidence: [[daisen-jinru-diandong-yashua-lingyu-chuantong-jindian-jiameng-chixu-shousuo-1010976825]] says CameraJet combines brushing, water flossing, camera-assisted gap finding, and app-based live images.
- Price evidence: [[daisen-jinru-diandong-yashua-lingyu-chuantong-jindian-jiameng-chixu-shousuo-1010976825]] reports a USD 499 price point.
- Adoption evidence: [[daisen-jinru-diandong-yashua-lingyu-chuantong-jindian-jiameng-chixu-shousuo-1010976825]] explicitly treats mainstream acceptance of a camera-equipped toothbrush as uncertain.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source provides no clinical outcomes, repeat-use data, privacy analysis, or sales evidence. A single premium launch does not prove that smart oral care will become a large category rather than a niche personal-care experiment.
