# Bounded Social Contagion

## Definition

Bounded social contagion is the source-scoped proposition that behaviors and beliefs can spread intensely through imitation, influential actors, organization, and local norms while remaining sharply limited by community, professional, institutional, or geographic boundaries.

## Current Synthesis

[[the-tipping-point-revisited-an-excerpt|The Tipping Point Revisited: An Excerpt]] joins three contrasts. Los Angeles robbery methods evolved and scaled through visible examples and a few organizers, yet New York did not develop the same outbreak despite Willie Sutton's fame. [[JohnWennberg]] found large local differences in medical practice, and low vaccination rates clustered around particular Waldorf schools rather than spreading evenly across California.

The useful synthesis is influence plus setting plus boundary. Exceptional actors can accelerate transmission, opportunity can make a behavior feasible, and group norms can stabilize it, but none alone explains why diffusion stops. The shared epidemic language is therefore a hypothesis-generating comparison rather than proof that crime, clinical judgment, and vaccine skepticism have one mechanism.

## Key Claims

- A small number of highly active organizers can contribute disproportionately to the scale and form of a social outbreak.
- Demonstration and imitation can modify a practice as it passes among participants rather than reproduce it exactly.
- Opportunity is enabling but insufficient: more bank branches did not make every city resemble Los Angeles.
- Community and professional boundaries can produce sharp local differences despite similar training, information, or neighboring populations.
- Geographic clustering does not by itself establish person-to-person transmission or identify the relevant boundary mechanism.

## Evidence

### Influential actors and organizational replication

- [[the-tipping-point-revisited-an-excerpt]] moves from the Yankee Bandit and takeover crews to [[RobertSheldonBrown]] and [[DonzellThompson]], whose organization reportedly separated planning from execution and scaled recruitment.

### Local receptivity and failed diffusion

- [[the-tipping-point-revisited-an-excerpt]] contrasts Los Angeles with New York, where Willie Sutton's fame did not produce a comparable robbery epidemic.

### Community-bounded norms

- [[the-tipping-point-revisited-an-excerpt]] uses [[JohnWennberg]]'s medical maps and Waldorf-school vaccination clusters to illustrate locally concentrated practice and belief.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source does not identify one tested causal model across the three domains. Crime trends can reflect policing, security technology, opportunity, markets, incarceration, demographics, and reporting; medical variation can reflect capacity, referral patterns, patient mix, incentives, and uncertainty; school vaccination clusters can reflect selection into institutions, policy, access, and shared ideology. Arrest timing, geographic contrast, and clustering support investigation but do not by themselves prove contagion or the identity of a “super spreader.”

## What Changed

- Added community boundaries as a necessary complement to influential-actor and opportunity explanations.
- Distinguished organizational replication from simple individual imitation.
- Preserved the cross-domain comparison as a qualified hypothesis rather than a unified causal finding.
