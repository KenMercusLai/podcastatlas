# Bounded Social Contagion

## Definition

Bounded social contagion is the source-scoped proposition that behaviors and beliefs can spread intensely through imitation, influential actors, organization, and local norms while remaining sharply limited by community, professional, institutional, or geographic boundaries.

## Current Synthesis

[[the-tipping-point-revisited-an-excerpt|The Tipping Point Revisited: An Excerpt]] joins three contrasts. Los Angeles robbery methods evolved and scaled through visible examples and a few organizers, yet New York did not develop the same outbreak despite Willie Sutton's fame. [[JohnWennberg]] found large local differences in medical practice, and low vaccination rates clustered around particular Waldorf schools rather than spreading evenly across California.

An earlier and more institutionally constrained case comes from [[acting-out]]. Moreno and Jennings interpreted 14 escapes over 14 days at the [[NewYorkStateTrainingSchoolForGirls|New York State Training School for Girls]] as an impulse moving through mapped relationships and reported fewer runaways after reorganizing cottages. The sequence is consistent with network transmission, but the same institution subjected residents to confinement, segregation, racialized labor, punishment, and abuse. Those conditions can create a common motive to escape even when ties influence timing or opportunity.

The useful synthesis is influence plus setting plus boundary plus institutional cause. Exceptional actors or connected peers can accelerate transmission, opportunity can make a behavior feasible, and group norms can stabilize it, but network topology alone does not explain why a practice becomes attractive or why diffusion stops. Epidemic language remains a hypothesis-generating comparison rather than proof that crime, clinical judgment, vaccine skepticism, and escape share one mechanism.

## Key Claims

- A small number of highly active organizers can contribute disproportionately to the scale and form of a social outbreak.
- Demonstration and imitation can modify a practice as it passes among participants rather than reproduce it exactly.
- Opportunity is enabling but insufficient: more bank branches did not make every city resemble Los Angeles.
- Community and professional boundaries can produce sharp local differences despite similar training, information, or neighboring populations.
- Network ties may shape the timing or pathway of conduct while institutional conditions shape its motive and desirability.
- Geographic clustering does not by itself establish person-to-person transmission or identify the relevant boundary mechanism.

## Evidence

### Influential actors and organizational replication

- [[the-tipping-point-revisited-an-excerpt]] moves from the Yankee Bandit and takeover crews to [[RobertSheldonBrown]] and [[DonzellThompson]], whose organization reportedly separated planning from execution and scaled recruitment.

### Local receptivity and failed diffusion

- [[the-tipping-point-revisited-an-excerpt]] contrasts Los Angeles with New York, where Willie Sutton's fame did not produce a comparable robbery epidemic.

### Community-bounded norms

- [[the-tipping-point-revisited-an-excerpt]] uses [[JohnWennberg]]'s medical maps and Waldorf-school vaccination clusters to illustrate locally concentrated practice and belief.

### Network pathways inside a coercive institution

- [[acting-out]] reports mapped ties among the first two escapees and later runaways, followed by a reported decline after cottage reorganization, while also documenting segregation, forced labor, punishment, and abuse as plausible non-network causes of escape.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The sources do not identify one tested causal model across their domains. Crime trends can reflect policing, security technology, opportunity, markets, incarceration, demographics, and reporting; medical variation can reflect capacity, referral patterns, patient mix, incentives, and uncertainty; school vaccination clusters can reflect selection into institutions, policy, access, and shared ideology. At Hudson, mapped ties and a before-and-after institutional claim do not isolate interpersonal transmission from shared coercion, reassignment effects, punishment, policy, seasonality, or reporting. Arrest timing, geographic contrast, clustering, and network paths support investigation but do not by themselves prove contagion or identify its causal weight.

## What Changed

- Added institutional cause as distinct from the network pathway through which conduct may spread.
- Extended the synthesis to a mapped escape cluster while keeping its intervention evidence qualified.
- Preserved cross-domain contagion as a hypothesis family rather than a unified causal mechanism.
