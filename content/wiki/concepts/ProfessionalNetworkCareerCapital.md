# Professional-Network Career Capital / 职业人脉资本

## Definition
Professional-network career capital is the durable job-market value accumulated through maintained relationships, visible competence, occupational reputation, and repeated evidence of growth beyond a one-time résumé or application.

## Current Synthesis
The Maimai interview treats network investment as countercyclical. When employers compete for workers and applications quickly produce several offers, maintaining weak professional ties may feel optional. When applications receive fewer responses, former colleagues, classmates, alumni, industry contacts, and a visible record of useful work can improve discovery, trust, information access, and referral paths.

This is not a claim that networking replaces skill or guarantees mobility. The stronger version joins relationships with demonstrated competence: people need reasons to remember, trust, and recommend someone. A professional network is therefore a distribution and reputation layer around career capital, not a substitute for it.

## Key Claims
- The return to professional networking rises when open applications become less effective and job opportunities become scarcer.
- Weak ties can preserve access to information, introductions, referrals, and occupational context over long periods.
- Visible growth and concrete achievements give a network evidence to circulate; relationship count alone is weak capital.
- Career reputation accumulates through repeated conduct and contribution rather than only through episodic job search.
- Professional-network platforms compete with general social tools and content platforms because users can maintain ties or demonstrate ability through several channels.

- Reputation practice - [[no-213-duitan-maimai-linfan-dang-zhaogongzuo-tairongyi-de-shidai-jieshu-zhihou-zhichang-zhengzai-fasheng-shenme-bianhua-gkwridonh85zagrq-qrmh58i]] links networking with showing ability, progress, and results rather than requesting help only at the moment of need.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a founder account from a company that benefits when professional networking appears more necessary. Network advantage can reproduce class, school, geography, employer, and status inequality, and it may be inaccessible to workers with limited time or platform visibility. Relationships cannot compensate for discrimination, sector contraction, weak demand, or missing qualifications, and the episode provides no independent causal estimate of networking's effect on hiring outcomes.

## What Changed
- Established labor-market tightness as a condition that can raise the value of maintained professional ties.
- Distinguished relationship count from reputation backed by visible competence and repeated contribution.
