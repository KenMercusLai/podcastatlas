# Menstrual Public Infrastructure

## Definition
Menstrual public infrastructure is the material and social system that makes menstrual needs manageable outside the home, including products, toilets, disposal, emergency access, standards, affordability, education, and permission to state the need without shame.

## Current Synthesis
The current sources converge on the claim that private market availability is not enough. Product materials, wrappers, retail channels, school education, transit stocking, safety rules, taxes, and disposal determine real access, while stigma can prevent institutions from recognizing demand or users from requesting help. The 2020 conversational source adds an early pandemic-era example of women publicly naming sanitary-pad needs and concern that male-dominated decision and reporting structures can overlook ordinary bodily requirements.

## Key Claims
- Menstruation requires emergency access because timing cannot always be planned around private purchasing.
- Infrastructure includes products, toilets, disposal, stocking, standards, affordability, and usable information.
- A product can exist in the market yet remain inaccessible in transit, schools, workplaces, emergencies, or low-income settings.
- Speech norms are operational: shame can suppress requests, stocking decisions, education, and policy attention.
- Product format and adoption vary, so infrastructure should not assume one universal menstrual practice.
- Treating menstrual needs as a private exception transfers design and planning costs to users.

## Evidence
### Material systems and public access
- [[175-nvxing-weisheng-yongpin-de-shehuishi-bi-xiangxiang-zhong-geng-jingcai-969467044]] traces pads, tampons, cups, packaging, education, toilets, transport retail, tax, standards, and affordability as one public-access system.

### Speakability and institutional attention
- [[175-nvxing-weisheng-yongpin-de-shehuishi-bi-xiangxiang-zhong-geng-jingcai-969467044]] shows how taboo and hidden purchasing shape adoption and access; [[ep115-yige-nuhaier-shi-ruhe-zhangda-wkgo216r_s7z_i1ba6abesgukow003]] describes women using online action to make sanitary-pad needs publicly discussable.

### Decision-making blind spots
- [[ep115-yige-nuhaier-shi-ruhe-zhangda-wkgo216r_s7z_i1ba6abesgukow003]] connects overlooked supplies for women medical workers to male-dominated decision and reporting structures, while also noting price, import, qualification, and cultural constraints around tampons.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The historical source centers Japan and gives only a partial Chinese history; the 2020 episode supplies conversational claims rather than procurement records, product-market data, or a pandemic logistics study. Better visibility does not by itself solve cost, quality, toxic-shock risk, environmental impact, school access, transport access, or disposal. Different bodies, products, health needs, and cultural contexts require plural rather than one-size-fits-all infrastructure.
