# First Indochina War

## Definition

The First Indochina War was the 1946–1954 conflict between French colonial forces and the Viet Minh that ended with France's defeat at Dien Bien Phu and preceded the Cold War division of Vietnam.

## Current Synthesis

The war joined decolonization to the emerging Cold War. France attempted to restore authority after the Second World War, while the United States increasingly supported France because it feared communism and needed French cooperation in Europe. Earlier Viet Minh–OSS cooperation therefore gave way to opposed alignments rather than developing into a durable anti-colonial partnership.

Dien Bien Phu ended French military predominance without resolving Vietnamese sovereignty. The subsequent division and American backing of a non-communist South created the institutional setting for later escalation, but Vietnamese actors retained projects and agency that cannot be reduced to external sponsorship.

## Key Claims

- The conflict grew from attempted French recolonization after the Second World War.
- U.S. support for France linked European alliance priorities to Asian decolonization.
- Hardening anti-communism narrowed space for cooperation with Vietnamese nationalists.
- Dien Bien Phu defeated French colonial restoration but did not produce a settled unified state.
- The war's outcome created the divided setting inherited by later American intervention.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source provides a compressed chronology rather than a full military, diplomatic, Vietnamese, Cambodian, or Laotian history. It does not resolve the exact balance among nationalism, communism, colonialism, superpower strategy, and local political conflict, nor does it establish that a different U.S. policy could have produced a durable alternative outcome.
