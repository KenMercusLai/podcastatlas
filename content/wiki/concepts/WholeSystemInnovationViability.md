# Whole-System Innovation Viability

## Definition
Whole-system innovation viability is the requirement that an applied invention align customer value with physical feasibility, economics, timing, manufacturing, lifespan, infrastructure, distribution, and a legible product story.

## Current Synthesis
The source argues that desirability research is necessary but incomplete. Features, products, platforms, infrastructure, and technology platforms create value through different assets and investment logics, so each must be tested as an inputs-processes-outputs system. Generating many strong alternatives and selecting aggressively can improve clarity, but even a lovable prototype fails if it is too early, too expensive, hard to manufacture, incompatible with its sales channel, or unable to communicate why a buyer should care.

## Key Claims
- Customer observation does not establish manufacturing, distribution, timing, or business-model viability.
- Feature, product, platform, and infrastructure decisions require different value and investment logic.
- Inputs, transformation processes, and outputs reveal what a company must change to create a new kind of value.
- High-fidelity alternatives make tradeoffs and the customer story more explicit before selection.
- Channel fit includes how quickly a product communicates its value to both users and purchasers.
- Being ahead of the market often means surrounding costs, infrastructure, or procurement are not yet ready.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The corporate examples are Brill's interpretations and are not supported by primary design records, commercial data, or counterfactual analysis in the supplied document. A complete viability checklist reduces avoidable error but cannot remove competition, luck, external shocks, or uncertainty. The critique narrows the scope of customer-centered methods rather than showing that they are ineffective.

## What Changed
- Established innovation viability as alignment across desirability, physics, economics, timing, production, and distribution.
- Qualified design thinking as one component rather than a complete new-product system.
