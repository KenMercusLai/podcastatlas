# Rescue Reciprocity Command Trust

## Definition
救援互惠式统帅信任 names the command expectation that leaders and subordinates will share danger enough to rescue one another when the position can still be saved. It is not sentimental loyalty; it is an operating condition for making dispersed forces believe that holding out, taking risk, and coordinating under pressure are rational.

## Current Synthesis
The concept emerges from the Yijing endgame. Gongsun Zan refuses to rescue isolated commanders because he wants them to rely on themselves, but that policy teaches the force that danger is individualized and that the center may not reciprocate. Hanji 1031 shows the terminal consequence: when outside help does approach, the command network depends on a secret fire signal rather than durable trust, Yuan Shao captures the message, and the failed sortie confirms that late rescue cannot easily repair earlier non-rescue.

## Key Claims
- Rescue reciprocity is a form of command infrastructure: soldiers and subordinate officers fight differently when they believe risk will be shared.
- Refusing rescue can be tactically defensible once, but repeated non-rescue becomes a signal about what the center values.
- Gongsun Zan's Yijing policy converts self-reliance rhetoric into isolation, morale decay, and reluctance to believe in common fate.
- The final fire-signal plan shows that external relief still needs trustworthy channels, timing, and morale inside the fortress.
- Guan Jing's advice and death show the tragic edge of the concept: leadership movement can look like abandonment once trust is already damaged.

## Evidence
- Non-rescue as command signal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1030-shouwo-hao-pai-gongsunzan-weihe-da-de-xilan-lpkntyov4viqyzgxgcujn36qhv8|Hanji 1030]] shows Gongsun Zan refusing to rescue threatened commanders and watching defections, flight, refusal, and surrenders spread through the southern defensive belt.
- Abandonment fear inside Yijing: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1030-shouwo-hao-pai-gongsunzan-weihe-da-de-xilan-lpkntyov4viqyzgxgcujn36qhv8|Hanji 1030]] records Guan Jing's warning that if Gongsun Zan leaves, remaining defenders may think the leader has abandoned them.
- Failed late rescue: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1031-gongsunzan-zhisi-cong-bianjiang-zhanshen-dao-kunshou-zifen-lmqoechccfwmfpmbdx8-tjmnm3jz|Hanji 1031]] records the intercepted fire-signal plan, failed sortie, Yuan Shao's siege breakthrough, Gongsun Zan's death, and Guan Jing's loyalist end.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Not every rescue is wise, possible, or worth the loss of the whole force. The concept applies when repeated refusal to share danger changes the expected relationship between the center and its subcommands. In Yijing, the issue is not a single missed rescue but a pattern that makes later coordination brittle.

## What Changed
- Hanji 1031 shifts the concept from warning signs to terminal outcome.
- The current synthesis now includes failed outside relief as a second-order consequence of earlier non-rescue.
- Guan Jing's role now illustrates both rational abandonment concern and tragic retrospective doubt.
