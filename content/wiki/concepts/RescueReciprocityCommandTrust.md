# Rescue Reciprocity Command Trust / 救援互惠式统帅信任

救援互惠式统帅信任 names the command relationship in which subordinates believe that shared risk will be reciprocated. In [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1030-shouwo-hao-pai-gongsunzan-weihe-da-de-xilan-lpkntyov4viqyzgxgcujn36qhv8|Hanji 1030]], [[GongsunZan|公孙瓒]] refuses to rescue surrounded generals because he wants each unit to fight hard without expecting help; when [[YuanShao|袁绍]] later attacks, those commanders do not rescue him either, and some surrender or flee.

The concept captures a specific failure mode of coercive leadership. Withholding rescue may look like discipline in the short run, but it teaches commanders that the center will not share danger with them. Once the center itself is threatened, the same lesson returns as non-cooperation, forcing Gongsun Zan to depend on [[YijingFortress|易京]], [[GongsunXuLateHan|公孙旭]]'s mission to the [[HeishanArmy|黑山军]], and [[GuanJingLateHan|关静]]'s warning that even an outward sortie may be read as abandonment.

## Key Claims
- Rescue is not only a battlefield tactic; it is a signal that the command center accepts reciprocal obligation.
- A policy of refusing rescue can harden local units temporarily while corroding trust in shared risk.
- When a leader later needs support, previous non-rescue can return as surrender, flight, or passivity.
- Fortress security can preserve material resources while exposing that the command network no longer trusts enough to move together.

## Connections
