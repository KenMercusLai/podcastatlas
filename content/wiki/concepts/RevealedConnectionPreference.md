# Revealed Connection Preference

## Definition

Revealed connection preference is the use of whom a person chooses to contact, ignore, or accept from a recommendation set as behavioral evidence about the relationships and opportunities they value.

## Current Synthesis

[[ArticulerAI|Articuler.ai]] distinguishes static identity data from relationship-choice data. Profiles may describe occupation, background, and stated goals, but selecting eight people from ten recommendations reveals a comparative preference in a particular context; reciprocal acceptance adds a second signal that the proposed relationship may matter to both sides.

The value is not the interaction count alone. A useful learning loop must retain the recommendation set, context, user action, reciprocal response, and later outcome while avoiding the mistake of treating every click or acceptance as equivalent to a valuable connection. The episode's decision not to rely heavily on fragmented message, email, video, and offline conversation data reinforces this boundary: partial observation can create more noise than understanding.

## Key Claims

- Choice among displayed alternatives reveals more than an isolated profile view because it records comparative preference.
- Reciprocal acceptance can strengthen a signal by adding evidence from both sides of a proposed relationship.
- Recommendation context matters; the same person may seek different relationships at an event, during fundraising, or while hiring.
- Fragmentary communication data can mislead when important interactions move across email, video calls, platform messages, and offline meetings.
- Behavioral feedback can improve future matching only if the system distinguishes exposure, selection, response, and meaningful outcome.
- A connection-data advantage is contingent on consent, coverage, representativeness, and demonstrated improvement rather than collection volume alone.

## Evidence

### Comparative and reciprocal choice
- [[lnvlrnkk-mvyhiy31eq-gw4qopje]] uses the example of a user selecting eight of ten recommendations and treats acceptance by the other party as a stronger preference signal.

### Context and incomplete observation
- [[lnvlrnkk-mvyhiy31eq-gw4qopje]] says the company explored broader communication data but found that conversations spread across several online and offline channels, making partial capture noisy.

### Matching loop
- [[lnvlrnkk-mvyhiy31eq-gw4qopje]] argues that observed connection behavior can refine later understanding of both parties' backgrounds, needs, and preferences.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Connection behavior is not ground truth for relationship value. Users may choose familiar, prestigious, demographically similar, or immediately useful people; non-response may reflect overload or timing rather than disinterest; acceptance may be polite rather than consequential. Logging these actions can also create privacy and surveillance risk, and optimizing past choices can reinforce unequal visibility. The source offers a company thesis, not audited evidence that the loop improves match quality.

## What Changed

- Created a synthesis separating static profile data, comparative connection choice, reciprocal response, and verified relationship outcome.
