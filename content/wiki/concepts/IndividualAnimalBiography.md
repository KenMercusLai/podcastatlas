# Individual-Animal Biography / 动物个体传记

## Definition

Individual-animal biography is a longitudinal record organized around one recognizable nonhuman individual—through bands, scars, movement history, repeated encounters, and eventual absence—rather than an interchangeable image of its species.

## Current Synthesis

Episode 230 uses individual biography to reverse the field-guide abstraction from concrete animal to standard type. Once a bird can be recognized across time, images with backlight, obstruction, distance, or imperfect focus may gain value as episodes in a life. The sequence can show migration, injury, seasonal return, habitat use, and changing relation to a place.

The form also has an unavoidable endpoint. Death, disappearance, or failure to return can turn an empty landscape into the final image. Biography therefore combines photographic duration with epistemic restraint: absence is meaningful, but it does not always disclose its cause.

## Key Claims

- Stable marks or tags can shift attention from species category to a particular life.
- Chronological sequence can make technically imperfect images evidentially and narratively valuable.
- Repeated observation joins portrait, behavior, place, and ecological time.
- Non-return or an empty scene can become part of the biography without proving death or cause.
- Individual focus adds thickness but cannot by itself represent population-level condition.

## Evidence

### Recognizable life across time
- [[episode-230-ziran-sheying-meiyou-weilai-8206247965-646769]] proposes rings, scars, and migration experience as anchors for following one animal through imperfect but cumulative photographs.

### Absence as qualified ending
- [[episode-230-ziran-sheying-meiyou-weilai-8206247965-646769]] describes death or non-return as a possible endpoint in which an empty landscape becomes the last portrait.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Recognition can be mistaken, tracking can disturb the subject, and a compelling individual story can obscure population patterns. Non-return is ambiguous: the animal may have died, moved, changed route, or simply escaped observation. The concept is a photographic and observational method, not a substitute for ecological study.
