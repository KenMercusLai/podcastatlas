# Narrative Authority and Identity / 叙事权与身份建构

## Definition
Narrative authority and identity describes the struggle over who can produce, circulate, and preserve the account through which a person becomes publicly or privately knowable.

## Current Synthesis
[[TheBlindAssassinNovel|《盲刺客》]] distributes identity-making power among family expectation, marriage, war, newspapers, attributed authorship, and retrospective memoir. [[IrisChase|Iris]] appears differently as daughter, sister, wife, lover, author, public figure, and elderly narrator. None of these positions alone exhausts her, but some have greater power to circulate and define consequences.

Self-narration can contest imposed versions without recovering a pure, final self. Iris writes after memory has changed, Laura can no longer answer, and damage cannot be undone. Narrative agency is therefore real but bounded: it can disclose hidden experience, accept responsibility, and leave another version for descendants, while remaining selective and dependent on whether anyone reads it.

## Key Claims
- Identity is shaped by accounts that circulate through family, institutions, media, and art.
- Attribution controls not only ownership but how a person's desire, agency, and suffering are interpreted.
- Self-narration can resist an imposed identity without becoming a complete or neutral final truth.
- Narrative authority depends on access, safety, credibility, preservation, and audience attention.
- Retrospective telling can acknowledge responsibility even when repair or forgiveness is unavailable.
- Abundant external traces increase the material for identity reconstruction but do not eliminate the need for first-person account.

### Late-life manuscript
- [[vol-360-mangcike-ai-de-xilihutu-dan-zan-tong-de-mingmingbaibai-a-gkwridoox2n2a5pykqt4iowi]] treats Iris's final account as testimony toward Sabrina and as confirmation of her own lived existence.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Narrative control is not total control over identity. Other people have their own experiences, records may constrain self-serving revision, and an audience may reject or ignore a manuscript. The social-media extension is host analogy rather than a general empirical finding. The principal evidence is fictional and cannot establish a universal theory of selfhood.

## What Changed
- Created a framework linking attribution, circulation, retrospective responsibility, and bounded self-definition.
