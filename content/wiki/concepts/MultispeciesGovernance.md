# Multispecies Governance

## Definition
Multispecies governance is the design of institutions that recognize, represent, and negotiate the interests of humans, animals, plants, ecosystems, and differently embodied agents.

## Current Synthesis
The episode's speculative workplaces and constitutional convention show that recognition does not make a biosphere cooperative. Species differ in sensory access, body scale, lifespan, resource use, ecological role, vulnerability, and dependence on consuming other life. Representation therefore needs explicit choices about constituency, voting units, consent, accommodation, safety, labor, and conflicts that no common language can erase.

## Key Claims
- Communication expands the set of politically visible claims.
- Equal standing does not determine the correct unit of representation.
- Body and sensory differences require accommodation beyond verbal translation.
- Ecological relations create conflicts that consent alone cannot solve.
- Involuntary uplift creates claims for remedy as well as representation.
- Institutions must preserve disagreement rather than presume universal cooperation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source offers speculative theater rather than a workable constitution. Ecosystems cannot necessarily speak with one voice, representatives may project human interests, and extending legal standing does not settle enforcement or tradeoffs. Existing environmental and animal-welfare institutions provide partial analogues but are not evaluated here.

## What Changed
- Established multispecies representation as a conflict-bearing institutional design problem rather than automatic harmony.
