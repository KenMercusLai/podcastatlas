# Hong Kong Article 23 Crisis / 香港二十三條危機

## Definition
Hong Kong Article 23 Crisis is the source's frame for the 2003 Basic Law Article 23 national-security legislation push, the July 1 mass march, and the collapse of [[TungCheeHwa|董建華]]'s governing authority.

## Current Synthesis
In the source, Article 23 is the final trigger rather than an isolated legal dispute. Earlier economic and policy controversies had already weakened Tung's authority; the 2003 legislation attempt then produced a mass street response and became the moment when both public legitimacy and Beijing confidence broke.

## Key Claims
- Article 23 condensed wider Tung-era governance dissatisfaction into one visible confrontation.
- The July 1 march is presented as destroying the government's governing credibility.
- The crisis helped lead to Beijing losing confidence in Tung and to his early resignation in 2005.

## Evidence
- Crisis sequence: [[dong-jianhua-shishi-aiguozhe-yuanlao-de-xianggang-rensheng-7d743a8515cf65334add1a55cd20148e]] says the government pushed Article 23 in 2003 and triggered a July 1 march of 500,000 people.
- Governing effect: [[dong-jianhua-shishi-aiguozhe-yuanlao-de-xianggang-rensheng-7d743a8515cf65334add1a55cd20148e]] describes the march as breaking Tung's governing prestige and Beijing's confidence.
- Historical contrast: [[dong-jianhua-shishi-aiguozhe-yuanlao-de-xianggang-rensheng-7d743a8515cf65334add1a55cd20148e]] contrasts that public-pressure environment with today's changed Hong Kong atmosphere.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source gives the protest figure and political interpretation but does not reconstruct the full legal text, civil-society coalition, legislative process, or later national-security law changes.
