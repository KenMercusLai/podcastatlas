# Content Moderation Occupational Harm / 内容审核职业伤害

## Definition
[[ContentModerationOccupationalHarm]] is the physical, psychological, and organizational burden created when workers repeatedly inspect disturbing material under production targets without adequate protection or recovery.

## Current Synthesis
The Yonghe source treats safety review as both AI data work and occupational exposure. About 50 workers reportedly participated between roughly 2022 and 2024, sometimes viewing thousands of sexual, violent, or graphic images per person per day for one or two months.

Workers skipped images, handed them to less-affected colleagues, moved temporarily to other projects, or relied on peer reassurance. These practices show local coping but are not substitutes for workload design, rotation, professional support, and monitoring of lasting effects.

## Key Claims
- Safety classification can expose workers to concentrated sexual, violent, and graphic content.
- Piece-rate volume and long project runs may reduce time to recover between exposures.
- Informal coping redistributes harm among colleagues rather than eliminating it.
- Scarce alternative employment can lead workers to normalize or minimize injury.
- Physical strain from prolonged screen work compounds psychological exposure.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode does not provide clinical assessment, longitudinal follow-up, exposure logs, comparison groups, or a complete employer policy. It cannot establish diagnoses, prevalence across the industry, or the long-term effect on each worker. The harms remain reported experiences that warrant protection, not population-level clinical estimates.
