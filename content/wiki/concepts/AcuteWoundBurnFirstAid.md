# Acute Wound and Burn First Aid / 急性伤口与烫伤急救

## Definition
Acute wound and burn first aid / 急性伤口与烫伤急救 is the household trauma boundary for separating superficial self-care from wounds, punctures, tendon signs, bleeding, contamination, and burns that need sustained cooling, protection, and medical evaluation.

## Current Synthesis
The two sources treat wound and burn care as simple but not casual. Minor superficial abrasions may fit basic cleaning and dressing, but deep wounds, persistent bleeding, visible fat or tendon, contaminated punctures, movement loss, foreign material, or burns with stuck clothing require more cautious routing.

The burn branch rejects folk applications such as toothpaste, soy sauce, and oil. Both sources prioritize sustained cool running water and avoiding forcible removal of adhered clothing. VOL.115 adds the public sequence “cool, remove, soak, cover, send,” with clean covering and transfer after heat removal, while placing first aid inside prevention of falls, furniture tip-over, unsafe heat sources, and traffic injury.

## Key Claims
- Superficial abrasions that stop bleeding quickly may be handled with simple cleaning and dressing.
- Wounds that are deep, gaping, visibly involve fat or tendon, or do not close naturally may need suturing and infection-risk management.
- Bleeding that does not stop after several minutes of pressure can signal deeper injury.
- Deep narrow punctures from nails, rusted objects, soil-contaminated objects, or similar sources raise tetanus-risk concern and need qualified care.
- Finger or toe movement loss after a small-looking wound can indicate tendon injury.
- Burns should be cooled long enough to remove heat; both sources use a source-scoped 15-30-minute range.
- Clothing stuck to burns should not be forcibly torn away; clean covering and timely transfer follow initial cooling and careful clothing management.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The page records podcast first-aid education, not a wound-care, burn-care, tetanus, surgery, or infection-management protocol. Water temperature and duration, burn size, depth, location, age, contamination, vaccination status, immune state, function loss, inhalation injury, and associated trauma determine clinical urgency.
