## Definition

Wrongful conviction persistence and correction is the comparative pattern in which prejudice, coercive or closed procedure, hierarchical deference, and institutional self-protection harden a weak accusation, while correction requires an externalized coalition able to reopen evidence and make refusal to reconsider politically costly.

## Current Synthesis

[[29-dongxifang-de-yuanan-deleifusi-yangnaiwu-he-xiaobaicai-lmmhqfl-idiuetsvufgrmpso2l-8|episode 29]] compares the [[DreyfusAffair|Dreyfus Affair]] and [[YangNaiwuXiaoBaicaiCase|杨乃武与小白菜案]] without treating French military justice and late-Qing administration as equivalent. In France, antisemitism, national-security anxiety, secret material, forgery, and military honor protected a wrongful espionage conviction. In Zhejiang, rumor, magistrate hostility, torture, untested poison claims, and routine approval across administrative levels protected a wrongful murder judgment.

In both cases, contrary facts were necessary but insufficient. Correction depended on family persistence, insiders or reviewers willing to challenge the inherited record, patrons or politicians able to move the case into another forum, press amplification, and a political setting in which continued defense of the judgment became costly. The endpoint also matters: pardon or acquittal can stop punishment while leaving innocence, rank, health, reputation, and lost time only partly repaired.

## Key Claims

- Identity prejudice or social rumor can make circumstantial suspicion feel evidentially stronger than it is.
- Closed procedure and torture block adversarial testing and can manufacture evidence that confirms the original theory.
- Once a verdict implicates organizational honor or many officials, correction threatens careers and legitimacy as well as the judgment itself.
- Hierarchical review does not guarantee independence when each level inherits facts and incentives from the level below.
- Successful correction usually combines evidence with family advocacy, institutional dissent, elite access, media attention, and favorable political timing.
- Release, pardon, acquittal, exoneration, and restoration are distinct remedies with different effects on liberty, legal innocence, status, and lived damage.

- Institutional hardening: [[29-dongxifang-de-yuanan-deleifusi-yangnaiwu-he-xiaobaicai-lmmhqfl-idiuetsvufgrmpso2l-8|episode 29]] links secret evidence, forgery, torture, repeated affirmance, and organizational reputation to prolonged error.
- Correction coalition: [[29-dongxifang-de-yuanan-deleifusi-yangnaiwu-he-xiaobaicai-lmmhqfl-idiuetsvufgrmpso2l-8|episode 29]] identifies relatives, Picquart, Zola, newspapers, Hu Xueyan, Beijing officials, and changing political pressure as distinct reopening channels.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The comparison identifies a mechanism rather than institutional or moral equivalence. The cases involved different states, courts, evidence systems, prejudices, punishments, media environments, and paths to review. The source is a comparative podcast summary, so it cannot determine the relative causal weight of every actor or establish that its pattern generalizes to all miscarriages of justice. Public attention can expose error, but other wiki cases also show that popular pressure may compromise judicial independence or favor punishment rather than correction.

## What Changed

- Created a comparative model separating fact discovery, institutional reopening, formal legal relief, and fuller rehabilitation.
