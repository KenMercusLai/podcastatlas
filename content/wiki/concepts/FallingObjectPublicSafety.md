# Falling Object Public Safety

Falling object public safety is the hazard pattern described by [[LeoMirani]] in [[taken-littorally-spains-sudden-crisis-in-ceuta-6a70712c034f16a52ebfaed7]]: people in [[India]] are repeatedly killed by billboards, tanker trucks, trash mountains, trees, airport canopies, school roofs, molten metal, building parts, and other objects that should have been managed.

The source's point is not that falling objects are a quirky category. It treats them as a visible symptom of [[InfrastructureAccountabilityGap]]: weather exposure, old structures, rapid construction, weak enforcement, corruption, small fines, and poor maintenance turn ordinary objects into lethal infrastructure risk.

## Key Claims
- Weather explains some incidents, especially tree and branch falls during wet seasons, but it does not explain the full pattern.
- Aging buildings and infrastructure need maintenance regimes, not only reactive repairs after deaths.
- Construction booms increase risk when materials, machinery, scaffolding, and site rules are weakly supervised.
- India has many relevant safety rules, but the source says they are not enforced consistently enough to change incentives.
- The absence of a national annual count for falling-object deaths is itself a data gap.
