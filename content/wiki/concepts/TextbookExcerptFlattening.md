# Textbook Excerpt Flattening / 教材删节造成的文本扁平化

Textbook excerpt flattening is the risk that a school-selected passage or deletion changes the emotional and intellectual shape of a work. In [[164-guwen-de-liliang-qing-shou-xia-zhe-fen-qiannian-wei-bian-de-shenqing-dongjian-he-yongqi-940236576]], QinZong uses [[XiangJiXuanZhi|《项脊轩志》]] as the key example: removing allusions to a widow honored by Qin and to Zhuge Liang's secluded rise can make [[GuiYouguang|归有光]] look only mournful, when the full text also contains youthful ambition.

The concept does not say excerpts are always illegitimate. It says readers should ask what the excerpt has made easy to remember and what it has made hard to see.

## Key Claims
- A shortened school text can shift the perceived center of a work.
- Memorization can preserve phrases while losing the life problem around them.
- Restoring omitted material is a way to reopen [[ClassicReadingComplexity]] rather than a mere philological correction.
