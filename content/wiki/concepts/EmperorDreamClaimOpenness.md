## Definition

皇帝梦与帝位开放想象 / emperor dream claim openness is Hanji 388-4's idea that Chinese political culture could imagine the imperial throne as reachable by people outside an inherited aristocratic line, making regime-change ambition socially broader.

## Current Synthesis

The episode uses folk and rebel language to explain why dynastic struggle could spread widely. "皇帝轮流坐，明年到我家" and Chen Sheng's "王侯将相，宁有种乎" are presented as signs that beggars, refugees, soldiers, powerful ministers, and non-Han groups could all imagine entering the competition for imperial rule.

The concept is not a claim that imperial accession was easy or democratic. It names the source's political-imagination mechanism: when the highest office is imagined as open to talent, force, luck, and mandate rather than fixed noble blood alone, many more actors can participate in the struggle, and regime-change violence can expand.

## Key Claims

- The imperial throne could be imagined as open to non-aristocratic contenders, even when actual success remained rare and violent.
- Rebel slogans matter because they express a political imagination in which rank does not permanently determine rule.
- Broad claimant imagination can widen dynastic struggle beyond courtly succession.
- The source contrasts this with European kingship, where royal and noble birth are presented as stronger preconditions for kingship.
- The concept explains ambition and mobilization possibilities, not moral legitimacy or actual governing capacity.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode's Europe-China comparison is broad and source-scoped. The concept should not deny aristocratic power in China, non-noble military usurpation in Europe, or the many institutional constraints on imperial accession.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Hanji 388-4 to distinguish throne-claim imagination from the separate question of war scale.
