# Wild Release Human Avoidance / 野放前人类回避训练

Wild release human avoidance is the source's practical version of "teaching an animal to forget the zoo." In [[163-jiao-yi-zhi-sheli-wangji-dongwuyuan-xiye-de-gushi-bi-fengkuang-dongwucheng-2-geng-jingcai-939887179]], [[XiningWildlifePark|西宁野生动物园]] treats [[TianxianBaobaoLynx|天线宝宝]] while also trying to keep the young [[Lynx|lynx]] from becoming comfortable with visitors, keepers, glass viewing, or human food cues.

The concept is adjacent to [[CaptiveAnimalCareLabor]], but its goal is different from ordinary long-term captive welfare. If the animal may return to the wild, care has to protect health while preserving distance, fear, hunting, and nondependence. The source's striking details - covered glass, no visitor display, loud keeper behavior, and live-prey testing - make affection and release preparation pull against each other.

## Key Claims
- A releasable wild animal can be harmed by comfort around humans even when that comfort comes from good care.
- Anti-habituation work may look emotionally harsh, but it can reduce later risks of begging, approaching villages, livestock conflict, or being harmed by people.
- Release preparation needs species behavior and individual testing, not only medical recovery.
- Public display can conflict with release goals because the animal learns from watching humans and being watched.
- Human avoidance is not an excuse for cruelty; it must remain bounded by health care, nutrition, stress control, and release viability.
