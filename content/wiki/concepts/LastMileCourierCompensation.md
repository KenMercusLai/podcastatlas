# 快递末端派费与劳动压力

## Definition
Last-mile courier compensation is the per-parcel and workload system through which network pricing pressure reaches local outlets and delivery workers.

## Current Synthesis
Early franchise networks assumed outlets could earn from pickups and deliver incoming parcels without compensation. Ecommerce's geographic imbalance made that unsustainable, leading to paid delivery fees and regional settlement tiers. Later price wars compressed those fees again, leaving couriers to absorb high parcel counts, communication costs, and low unit pay until policy began requiring reasonable terminal fees and labor standards.

## Key Claims
- Geographic flow imbalance made zero-fee delivery untenable.
- Inter-outlet settlement was necessary for national network stability.
- Price competition can be transmitted from headquarters and merchants to workers.
- Per-piece pay can hide communication cost, workload, and income volatility.

## Evidence
- Settlement reform: [[no-219-kuaidi-jianghu-sanshinian-tonglubang-taobao-pinduoduo-yu-jiage-zhan-1015471748]] describes 中通's move from zero compensation to tiered delivery fees.
- Labor pressure: [[no-219-kuaidi-jianghu-sanshinian-tonglubang-taobao-pinduoduo-yu-jiage-zhan-1015471748]] reports very low net per-piece fees and a 2021 income survey.
- Policy response: [[no-219-kuaidi-jianghu-sanshinian-tonglubang-taobao-pinduoduo-yu-jiage-zhan-1015471748]] cites seven-department guidance and later Zhejiang rules on reasonable terminal fees.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Pay systems differ by company, region, employment status, and period. The cited fee and income figures are snapshots from source-reported surveys, not current universal rates.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to connect network settlement with courier labor outcomes.
