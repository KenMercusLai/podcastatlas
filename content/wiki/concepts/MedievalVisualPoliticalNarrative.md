# Medieval Visual Political Narrative

## Definition
Medieval visual political narrative is the use of images, sequence, symbols, bodily detail, and material craft to make claims about rule, legitimacy, conflict, and memory without relying primarily on prose argument.

## Current Synthesis
The episode defines the concept through the [[BayeuxTapestry|Bayeux Tapestry]]. Its legibility comes from visual sequence, character markers, battle detail, Latin, marginal fables, and a missing ending that keeps the political story open.

## Key Claims
- Visual sequence can turn a succession dispute into a claim about rightful rule.
- Hair, moustaches, clothing, posture, animals, arrows, bodies, and margins can work as political evidence or cues.
- Graphic immediacy is not modern by default; medieval objects can remain readable because they organize action spatially and sequentially.
- Missing evidence can strengthen interpretation by making viewers ask what story has been cut off.
- Monumental public narrative can be produced through intimate labor by unnamed makers.

## Evidence
- Graphic comparison - [[ire-and-ice-the-toll-of-americas-deportations-6a993426809ae7c2f4293df9]] has [[MichaelLewisCurator|Michael Lewis]] compare the tapestry's characters to cartoon books or graphic novels.
- Political sequence - [[ire-and-ice-the-toll-of-americas-deportations-6a993426809ae7c2f4293df9]] traces Edward, Harold, William, oath, comet, battle, death, and victory as a legible story.
- Margins and details - [[ire-and-ice-the-toll-of-americas-deportations-6a993426809ae7c2f4293df9]] notes Latin phrases, Aesop fables, prominent genitalia, animals, and people as part of the object's visual world.
- Interpretive gaps - [[ire-and-ice-the-toll-of-americas-deportations-6a993426809ae7c2f4293df9]] uses [[NickCullinan]]'s comments to frame the missing ending as an invitation to interpretation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not make a universal claim about all medieval art. It uses the Bayeux Tapestry as a highly unusual surviving object whose scale, content, and afterlife make the visual-political function especially visible.
