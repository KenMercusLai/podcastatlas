# Frontier Defensive Patience / 边境防御耐心

Frontier defensive patience / 边境防御耐心 is the military pattern [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-105-zhanzheng-huayang-zhaoguo-limu-zui-hui-shua-ljtqdg46a8o13zzsjv-p-fpol5oh]] extracts from [[LiMu|李牧]]'s command against [[Xiongnu|匈奴]]. The pattern begins by refusing the enemy the kind of fight it wants: when raiders enter, people, livestock, and stores withdraw into fortified positions, and unauthorized pursuit is punished.

The method looks weak in the short term because it produces no visible victories and lets critics call the commander timid. The source treats that reputation as part of the strategic cost. Li Mu preserves population, animals, supplies, and soldiers while training troops, rewarding them, gathering intelligence, and learning the enemy's habits.

The patience becomes useful only when it converts into timing. Once Li Mu has troops eager to fight and the Xiongnu believe he will not attack, he opens the frontier enough to create bait, feigns retreat, lets a small party be captured, and draws the main force into a place where Zhao can fix, flank, and cut off retreat.

This concept is related to [[WarCostTempoDiscipline|war cost tempo discipline]], but it operates at the frontier-defense level. It is not a generic preference for defense; it is a way to lower the cost of repeated raids until a commander can choose a decisive battle on better terms.

## Key Claims
- Refusing battle can be active strategy when the enemy's strength is mobility and opportunism.
- Defensive patience requires discipline over one's own troops, because unauthorized courage gives raiders contact, captives, and morale.
- Reputation damage can become usable if the enemy converts the commander's apparent timidity into overconfidence.
- The final attack must be prepared before the bait is offered; patience without a planned strike becomes mere passivity.
- Frontier defensive patience depends on local autonomy, supply control, intelligence, and soldier morale, not only on walls or forts.
