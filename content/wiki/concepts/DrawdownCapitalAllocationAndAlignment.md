# Drawdown Capital Allocation and Alignment

## Definition
Drawdown capital allocation and alignment is a crisis response in which a cash-generative public company combines share repurchases with performance-linked employee equity to exploit perceived undervaluation while keeping key staff exposed to a recovery.

## Current Synthesis
The AppLovin case joins two responses to a roughly 92% share-price drawdown. Management redirected capital toward repurchases when outside investors would not buy, while performance stock plans gave key employees a renewed reason to remain through public-market stress. The combination can concentrate future upside and signal conviction, but only if the operating thesis is sound, liquidity remains adequate, repurchases are genuinely accretive, and incentives do not reward excessive risk or retrospective storytelling.

## Key Claims
- A severe drawdown can create a capital-allocation opportunity when cash generation remains strong and management's valuation judgment is correct.
- Retiring shares can increase each remaining holder's claim on future value, but only relative to the price paid and alternative uses of cash.
- Performance equity can convert market volatility from a retention threat into potential recovery participation for employees.
- Buybacks and employee grants must be assessed together because repurchase accretion can be offset by dilution or poorly designed targets.
- A later recovery does not prove that the original decision was low-risk or repeatable.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
All figures and causal explanations come from the CEO interview and are not independently audited in the episode. Repurchases can destroy value when management is wrong, weaken resilience, favor short-term price support, or coincide with offsetting stock compensation. Performance equity can also encourage aggressive targets. The ex post share-price recovery creates survivorship and outcome bias, so the case should not be generalized without contemporaneous cash-flow, balance-sheet, dilution, and valuation analysis.

## What Changed
- Created the concept from AppLovin's combined buyback and performance-equity response.
- Added opportunity-cost, dilution, incentive-design, and survivorship qualifications.
- Separated operating recovery from investor re-engagement as potentially reinforcing but distinct mechanisms.
