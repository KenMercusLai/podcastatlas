## Definition

Righteousness over profit / 义利之辨 is the ethical and political ordering that judges benefit, wealth, rank, and status through rightness first. It does not deny practical benefit; it rejects making private gain the first question when governing, serving, or accepting reward.

## Current Synthesis

The earlier Mencius sources define the concept through [[Mengzi|孟子 / Mencius]]'s answer to [[LiangHuiWang|梁惠王]]: if the ruler opens with profit, ministers and commoners learn to pursue their own advantage downward, weakening the polity. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-26-mengzi-laile-ln4twwp2frmr-xpumu0rqg-2gpyn|Zhouji 26]] refines this by stressing that Mencius is not against benefit itself; [[RenyiBenefitOrdering|仁义优先的利益秩序]] can produce public benefit precisely because it keeps private advantage from becoming the standard.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-882-fengguang-taisheng-zhuding-bude-haosi-lphagu5ljuxxjwx-uvnbs229wjii|Hanji 882]] extends the concept from Warring States ruler discourse into late-Han title politics. The host invokes [[Analects|《论语》]] to argue that wealth and rank are acceptable only when gained by the right path. [[ChenFanLateHan|陈蕃]]'s repeated refusal of [[EmpressDowagerDouMiao|窦妙 / 窦皇太后]]'s favor-based marquisate becomes the practical case: dignity and public legitimacy matter more than a valuable but improperly grounded reward.

## Key Claims

- Benefit is not rejected, but it must be subordinated to public rightness and legitimate ordering.
- When a ruler makes private gain the first question, self-interested imitation spreads through the political hierarchy.
- Renyi can be practical statecraft because it preserves the trust conditions that make durable benefit possible.
- Wealth and rank are morally unstable when obtained outside the proper path, even if they bring immediate advantage.
- Refusing improperly grounded reward is an active political signal, not merely personal modesty.
- Late-Han title cases show that the concept applies to office, marquisates, food revenue, and reputation as well as abstract moral theory.

## Evidence

- Mencian political diagnosis: [[30-mengzi-zhanguo-fenqing-de-chizi-zhixin-592494138|Mengzi episode 30]] frames Liang Huiwang's profit-first question as a state-order problem because every rank can imitate the same pursuit of advantage.
- Benefit after rightness: [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-26-mengzi-laile-ln4twwp2frmr-xpumu0rqg-2gpyn|Zhouji 26]] distinguishes public benefit produced through renyi from ruler language that places profit first.
- Late-Han rank and title application: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-882-fengguang-taisheng-zhuding-bude-haosi-lphagu5ljuxxjwx-uvnbs229wjii|Hanji 882]] uses the Analects wealth-and-rank frame to interpret Chen Fan's refusal of a favor-based marquisate.
- Reward legitimacy as reputational safety: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-882-fengguang-taisheng-zhuding-bude-haosi-lphagu5ljuxxjwx-uvnbs229wjii|Hanji 882]] also has [[LuZhiLateHan|卢植]] warn [[DouWuLateHan|窦武]] that excessive accession reward can damage reputation when it exceeds personal merit.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

- The sources do not present benefit, wealth, or rank as inherently corrupt. The distinction is between rightful benefit and advantage pursued or accepted outside the proper path.
- Hanji 882 applies Analects language to late-Han politics through the host's moral interpretation; the page keeps that as a source-grounded extension rather than claiming that every title dispute in late Han belongs to this concept.
- The Mencian statecraft frame and Chen Fan's personal refusal are related but not identical: one diagnoses political language from the ruler downward, while the other shows a minister refusing a private-benefit channel.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 882 as the late-Han wealth/rank application of the concept.
- Expanded the concept from Mencian ruler discourse into title legitimacy and refusal of improperly grounded reward.
- Connected righteousness-over-profit to Chen Fan's marquisate refusal and Lu Zhi's warning against excessive accession honors.
