# Revolutionary Purge Security Dilemma

## Definition

A revolutionary purge security dilemma arises when eliminating successive factional rivals makes the survivors less secure, giving otherwise divided insiders a shared incentive to remove the purging center before they become its next targets.

## Current Synthesis

The episode shows the mechanism after the central government destroyed both the radical [[Hebertists]] and moderate [[Indulgents]]. Each purge removed a visible challenge and strengthened the committees in the short term, but together they erased any stable boundary of acceptable disagreement. Deputies and committee members could no longer infer safety from revolutionary history, ideological position, or personal prominence, so immediate supremacy produced a broader coalition of anticipatory fear.

## Key Claims

- Removing one rival can increase immediate control while reducing credible limits on future punishment.
- Purging opposite factional wings makes ideological conformity an unreliable guarantee of safety.
- Politically assembled conspiracies allow past association to become retrospective evidence against almost anyone.
- Procedural manipulation signals that formal institutions cannot reliably protect insiders.
- Shared fear can align actors who otherwise disagree about policy or ideology.
- A ruler or committee may become most vulnerable immediately after appearing unchallengeable.

## Evidence

### Destruction of both flanks

- [[710-the-terror-death-at-the-guillotine-part-4-glt5038102951]] follows the Hébertist executions with the arrest and execution of Danton, Desmoulins, and their associates.

### Collapse of reliable safety signals

- [[710-the-terror-death-at-the-guillotine-part-4-glt5038102951]] emphasizes that deputies saw radical and moderate revolutionaries alike condemned through conspiracy accusations and compressed trial procedure.

### Anticipatory coalition pressure

- [[710-the-terror-death-at-the-guillotine-part-4-glt5038102951]] links committee secrecy and internal suspicion after Danton's death to a growing incentive to act before becoming the next target.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

- The episode identifies conditions for later backlash but does not by itself provide a complete account of Robespierre's fall.
- Fear was not the only source of disagreement inside the Convention or committees; ideology, policy, administration, rivalry, and personal grievance also mattered.
- The mechanism does not imply that all purges automatically unite survivors or that the purging center lacks real support.
- Reported private fears and Danton's prediction of Robespierre's fall remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- A canonical mechanism now links successful elimination of both factional wings to the governing center's later political isolation.
