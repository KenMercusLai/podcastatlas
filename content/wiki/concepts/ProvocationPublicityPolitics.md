# Provocation Publicity Politics

Provocation publicity politics is the source's pattern where actors seek or accept confrontation because visible conflict can move opinion, force attention, or expose opponents. In [[698-the-troubles-the-brink-of-civil-war-part-2-glt2308408988]], some radicals are disappointed by a peaceful first civil-rights march because it does not generate the publicity that confrontation might.

The pattern recurs when [[PeoplesDemocracyNorthernIreland|People's Democracy]] marches from Belfast to [[Derry]] despite warnings from [[TerenceONeill|Terence O'Neill]] and other civil-rights figures. The episode argues that loyalist attackers at [[BurntolletBridgeAttack|Burntollet]] also made avoidable choices, making publicity politics inseparable from coercive counter-mobilization.

## Key Claims
- Confrontation can reveal state violence, but it can also harden opponent narratives.
- A movement's radical edge can force attention while making moderate reform harder.
- Counter-protest violence can turn a provocation into proof of the grievance it was meant to expose.
