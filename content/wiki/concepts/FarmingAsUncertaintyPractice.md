# Farming as Uncertainty Practice

## Definition

Farming as uncertainty practice is the episode's view that cultivation requires preparation, care, and optimism while leaving weather, markets, biology, and final outcomes partly beyond control.

## Current Synthesis

[[board-game-season-board-game-season]] moves from agricultural history and business survival to a broader claim about action under chance. Farmers prepare soil, plant seeds, maintain animals and equipment, manage money, and learn local conditions, but none of those actions guarantees a harvest or a viable price. Optimism is therefore not a forecast of certain success; it is a condition of acting responsibly when results remain open.

The episode's final move adds partnership. [[GeorgeRohrbacher]] and [[AnneRohrbacher]] bring different tolerances for risk, and [[TheFarmingGame]] turns private exposure into shared play. Care and companionship cannot erase loss, but they can preserve meaning, relationship, and the willingness to continue through good and bad seasons.

## Key Claims

- Preparation changes odds and consequences without producing control over every outcome.
- Planting requires action before the result is knowable.
- Care remains worthwhile even when weather, markets, or chance defeat the intended result.
- Complementary risk temperaments can strengthen decisions when neither becomes absolute.
- Shared experience can preserve meaning when conventional measures register loss.

## Evidence

### Limits of control

- [[board-game-season-board-game-season]] contrasts farming knowledge and preparation with drought, harvest variability, interest rates, breakdowns, and market shocks.

### Optimism as action

- [[board-game-season-board-game-season]] attributes to George the view that farmers must be optimists or they would never plant seeds.

### Partnership and shared chance

- [[board-game-season-board-game-season]] uses George and Anne's contrasting styles and family play to argue that uncertain games are best played with others.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This is an interpretive philosophy drawn from one family's retrospective story, not evidence that optimism improves every farm outcome or that partnership resolves structural disadvantage. Emphasizing chance must not obscure policy, lending, land access, race, labor, environmental change, or preventable management failures. Care can be meaningful without making continued exposure financially or personally sustainable.

## What Changed

- Established a bounded account of farming as prepared action without guaranteed control.
