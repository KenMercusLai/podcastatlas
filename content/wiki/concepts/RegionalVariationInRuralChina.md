# Regional Variation in Rural China / 中国农村的区域差异

## Definition

Regional variation in rural China is the requirement to explain village life through locally specific land relations, kinship, commercialization, ecology, credit, industry, and market exposure rather than treating “the Chinese countryside” as one social structure.

## Current Synthesis

The episode uses Jiangnan sericulture to show why apparently irrational behavior can be locally intelligible. Households in a commercialized silk economy might borrow to buy mulberry leaves and bear market risk, while lineage organization or land relations were more decisive elsewhere. Literature and social theory built from one region can therefore misdescribe another even when the local evidence is valid.

Regionalization does not mean every place is incomparable or that national and global forces disappear. World prices, war, state projects, and ideological debates crossed regions, but they entered different household economies and institutions. Comparison should preserve both shared pressure and locally variable mechanisms.

## Key Claims

- Rural risk behavior depends on the commercial, ecological, and institutional setting in which households act.
- A valid regional case cannot become a national model without explicit comparison.
- Literary schools can conceal social-geographic difference by grouping unlike rural worlds under one aesthetic label.
- National crisis and world markets produce uneven local effects rather than uniform rural outcomes.
- Policy disputes may each contain regional truth while overreaching when generalized.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

One episode's regional contrasts are illustrative rather than a systematic typology. Regions also contain internal class, gender, village, and temporal variation. Emphasizing difference must not deny nationwide political structures or global shocks, and it should not be used to avoid testing whether a mechanism travels beyond its first case.

## What Changed

- Created a methodological concept making regional scope an explicit condition of rural generalization.
- Linked literary classification, social theory, and policy transfer to the same geographic evidence problem.
