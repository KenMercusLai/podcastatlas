# AI Creative Floor-Ceiling Gap

## Definition
The AI creative floor-ceiling gap is the distinction between lowering the minimum effort needed to produce an acceptable artifact and raising the maximum level of originality, craft, or significance humans can achieve.

## Current Synthesis
Generative AI lowers entry and execution costs across code, design, motion, video, and drafting. In creator workflows, that compression can be granular: subtitles, insert selection, noise removal, color work, research, and some historical visualization may become faster before systems reliably replace emotional timing, dramatic structure, taste, or distinctive judgment. That access and efficiency gain does not prove that the best attainable work has improved; the ceiling depends on how creators direct, verify, reshape, and combine the tools.

## Key Claims
- AI lowers the production floor by reducing skill, time, and blank-page barriers.
- Adequate output becomes more abundant without automatically making original output more common.
- A creative ceiling depends on human ambition, judgment, care, and iterative refinement rather than model availability alone.
- Generated material is most useful when creators can reshape it instead of accepting model defaults.
- Lower access costs can expand agency, but passive dependence can weaken the thinking that ambitious work requires.
- Task substitution can proceed unevenly inside one production pipeline, compressing routine execution before the judgment-heavy whole.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This is a conceptual distinction, not evidence that AI can never raise creative ceilings. New tools may enable forms that were previously infeasible. Field's account concerns design software, while Keshwala's task boundary is a creator forecast rather than measured displacement data and may change quickly as models improve. The bounded evidence offers practitioner judgment rather than longitudinal measurement of originality, quality, employment, or task automation.

## What Changed
- Added uneven task compression inside video production workflows.
- Distinguished routine execution from emotion, structure, and judgment without treating that boundary as permanent.
