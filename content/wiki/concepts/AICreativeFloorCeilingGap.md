# AI Creative Floor-Ceiling Gap

## Definition
The AI creative floor-ceiling gap is the distinction between lowering the minimum effort needed to produce an acceptable artifact and raising the maximum level of originality, craft, or significance humans can achieve.

## Current Synthesis
Generative AI reliably widens participation by making code, design, motion, video, and drafting easier to begin. That access gain does not prove that the best attainable work has improved: exceptional creation still requires intention, sustained effort, judgment, refinement, and a willingness to depart from familiar patterns. AI can support those activities, but the ceiling rises only through what creators do with the tool.

## Key Claims
- AI lowers the production floor by reducing skill, time, and blank-page barriers.
- Adequate output becomes more abundant without automatically making original output more common.
- A creative ceiling depends on human ambition, judgment, care, and iterative refinement rather than model availability alone.
- Generated material is most useful when creators can reshape it instead of accepting model defaults.
- Lower access costs can expand agency, but passive dependence can weaken the thinking that ambitious work requires.

## Evidence
### Access and average output
- [[audio-200-dylan-field-int-1-audio-200-dylan-field-int-1]] has [[DylanField]] apply the lower-floor argument across code, motion, video, design, and other digital production.

### Ceiling and exceptional work
- [[audio-200-dylan-field-int-1-audio-200-dylan-field-int-1]] records Field revising his earlier belief that AI would itself raise the ceiling and locating exceptional results in time, boldness, care, and unprecedented attempts.

### Shaping generated material
- [[audio-200-dylan-field-int-1-audio-200-dylan-field-int-1]] describes [[Figma]] workflows that alternate generation with direct manipulation and treats model output as material to be molded.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This is a conceptual distinction, not evidence that AI can never raise creative ceilings. New tools may enable forms that were previously infeasible, and the source itself compares AI with cameras, layers, and filters that eventually supported new practices. The bounded evidence offers practitioner judgment rather than longitudinal measurement of originality or creative quality.

## What Changed
- Established the floor-versus-ceiling distinction as a canonical concept.
- Separated broader participation from stronger originality.
- Added direct manipulation and human agency as conditions for higher-end creative value.
