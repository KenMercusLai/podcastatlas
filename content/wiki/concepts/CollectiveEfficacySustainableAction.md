# Collective Efficacy for Sustainable Action

## Definition
Collective efficacy for sustainable action is the belief and practice that people can contribute to difficult shared goals through coordinated effort while remaining limited, interdependent, and permitted to rest.

## Current Synthesis
[[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-b88be0f610dc041ad6ff6e655ae4dc57-64189981-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-b88be0f610dc041ad6ff6e655ae4dc57-64189981|Wellness 2.0: When It's All Too Much]] contrasts collective efficacy with both despair and solitary saviorhood. Its choir analogy makes the design principle concrete: one participant can pause because the group continues carrying the song. Action therefore becomes more durable when responsibility, knowledge, labor, and emotional load are distributed rather than converted into a test of individual purity.

Pleasure and rest are internal to this model. Food, music, dancing, gardening, beauty, and companionship are among the goods collective action aims to preserve and also resources that make continued participation possible. A limited act may matter because it contributes care or improvement now, even when no one can promise that it will scale into decisive change.

## Key Claims
- Shared efficacy differs from optimism because it concerns capacity to act together rather than confidence that outcomes will be good.
- Distributed participation permits rest without defining a pause as abandonment.
- Bounded contribution is more sustainable than an impossible demand to personally compensate for systemic harm.
- Joy, beauty, and pleasure can support endurance and represent values being protected rather than evidence of insufficient commitment.
- Small actions can possess inherent ethical or relational value without guaranteeing transformation.
- Releasing savior fantasies reduces perfectionism while preserving responsibility and participation.

## Evidence
- Collective-action mechanism - [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-b88be0f610dc041ad6ff6e655ae4dc57-64189981-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-b88be0f610dc041ad6ff6e655ae4dc57-64189981]] uses a choir to illustrate continuity through individual pauses and reports that collective rather than merely individual action is important to climate-anxiety relief.
- Rest and pleasure - [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-b88be0f610dc041ad6ff6e655ae4dc57-64189981-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-b88be0f610dc041ad6ff6e655ae4dc57-64189981]] rejects martyrdom and treats ordinary enjoyment as both a protected good and an emotional resource.
- Limited action - [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-b88be0f610dc041ad6ff6e655ae4dc57-64189981-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-b88be0f610dc041ad6ff6e655ae4dc57-64189981]] argues that gardening and other modest contributions need not solve the whole crisis to be worthwhile.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Collective efficacy does not establish that every group is effective, inclusive, safe, or strategically competent. Collective action may fail, reproduce inequity, demand inaccessible time and money, or leave anxiety unchanged. Rest and small actions can sustain participation but can also be used rhetorically to avoid structural change; adequacy of scale, policy design, and measurable outcomes remain separate questions.

## What Changed
- Created a nonheroic collective-action model integrating shared capacity, bounded contribution, pleasure, and rest.
