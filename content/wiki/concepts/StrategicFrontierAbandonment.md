## Definition

弃边失屏 / Strategic Frontier Abandonment is the strategic mistake of treating a costly frontier, corridor, or outer buffer as removable budget pressure while ignoring its shield, depth, alliance, and enemy-resource functions.

## Current Synthesis

The earlier Western Regions case shows the abandonment error before the later Liangzhou debate. In [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-799-donghan-mingchen-yangzhen-ruhe-jujue-zou-houmen-lmufb7lvb-kzsvjtn6iu-zrpiptf|Hanji 799]], Northern Xiongnu and Cheshi pressure Hexi, and some at court propose closing Yumen Pass and Yangguan. [[ZhangDangLateHan|张珰]] answers from Dunhuang that the [[HexiFourCommanderiesLateHan|河西四郡]] cannot stand if the Western Regions are abandoned, and [[ChenZhongLateHan|陈忠]] argues that leaving Shanshan unsupported would push the Western Regions toward Northern Xiongnu, increasing Xiongnu resources, courage, and possible connection with Qiang pressure.

[[EmperorAnOfHan|Emperor An]] accepts Chen Zhong's anti-abandonment argument and sends [[BanYongLateHan|班勇]] to Liuzhong. The concept here is not maximal expansion. Zhang Dang's three plans include attack, limited garrisoning, and fallback relocation; the accepted policy is a constrained retention move that tries to keep the outer buffer without pretending costs do not exist.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-934-sha-yiren-ze-tianxia-an-ni-sha-busha-ltar8hvdkqct8oizm3k22z38b1v8|Hanji 934]] later gives the Liangzhou version. [[CuiLieLateHan|崔烈]] proposes abandoning [[LiangzhouLateHan|凉州]] because rebellion and taxation have made it expensive, and [[FuXie|傅燮]] answers that the proposal would endanger the state. Liangzhou is described as a western gate and major route. If the court withdraws, mounted non-Han forces could take the region, gain horses and space, and return with a larger threat.

Together, the sources define the concept as a frontier-accounting error. Withdrawal may look like savings from the capital, but the abandoned space can become an enemy base, dissolve allied confidence, expose core corridors, and destroy the very strategic depth that made the frontier worth holding.

## Key Claims

- A frontier region can be expensive and still be strategically cheaper to hold than to abandon.
- Tax burden, rebellion, and pass-defense costs are real constraints, but they do not by themselves settle whether withdrawal reduces danger or compounds it.
- Outer-buffer polities matter because abandonment can shift their alignment and resources to the enemy.
- Frontier geography can convert a current local problem into a future core-region threat if enemy forces inherit the corridor, horses, alliance network, and depth.
- The concept supports constrained retention, graded alternatives, and strategic depth analysis rather than a blanket rule that every border must always be held.

## Evidence

- Western Regions-Hexi dependency: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-799-donghan-mingchen-yangzhen-ruhe-jujue-zou-houmen-lmufb7lvb-kzsvjtn6iu-zrpiptf|Hanji 799]] says Zhang Dang argues that if the Western Regions are abandoned, Hexi cannot stand alone.
- Alignment and resource warning: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-799-donghan-mingchen-yangzhen-ruhe-jujue-zou-houmen-lmufb7lvb-kzsvjtn6iu-zrpiptf|Hanji 799]] says Chen Zhong warns that abandoned Western Regions states would attach to Northern Xiongnu, increasing Xiongnu wealth and courage.
- Constrained retention: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-799-donghan-mingchen-yangzhen-ruhe-jujue-zou-houmen-lmufb7lvb-kzsvjtn6iu-zrpiptf|Hanji 799]] says Emperor An accepts Chen Zhong's advice and sends Ban Yong with five hundred troops to Liuzhong.
- Liangzhou abandonment proposal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-934-sha-yiren-ze-tianxia-an-ni-sha-busha-ltar8hvdkqct8oizm3k22z38b1v8|Hanji 934]] says Cui Lie proposes abandoning Liangzhou because rebellion and taxation make it costly.
- Shield and corridor argument: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-934-sha-yiren-ze-tianxia-an-ni-sha-busha-ltar8hvdkqct8oizm3k22z38b1v8|Hanji 934]] says Fu Xie answers that Liangzhou is a western gate and route whose loss would give hostile mounted forces horses, space, and a route back toward the core.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept should not become an automatic anti-withdrawal rule. Hanji 799 itself preserves fallback options and limited garrisoning rather than unlimited commitment, while Hanji 934 occurs in a later fiscal and rebellion context with different capacity constraints. The shared claim is narrower: before abandoning a frontier, the court must price the buffer, corridor, alliance, and enemy-resource effects, not only the immediate tax and deployment burden.
