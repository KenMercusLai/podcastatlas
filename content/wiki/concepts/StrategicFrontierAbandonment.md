# 弃边失屏 / Strategic Frontier Abandonment

弃边失屏 / strategic frontier abandonment enters through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-934-sha-yiren-ze-tianxia-an-ni-sha-busha-ltar8hvdkqct8oizm3k22z38b1v8|Hanji 934]], where [[CuiLieLateHan|崔烈]] proposes abandoning [[LiangzhouLateHan|凉州]] because rebellion and taxation have made it expensive, and [[FuXie|傅燮]] answers that the proposal would endanger the state. The concept names the strategic mistake of treating a costly frontier only as a budget drain while ignoring its function as a shield, corridor, and depth layer.

Fu Xie's argument is geographic and historical rather than only moral. Liangzhou is described as a western gate and major route. If the court withdraws, mounted non-Han forces could take the region, gain horses and space, and return with a larger threat. Fu Xie also invokes earlier Han policy, especially [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s Hexi expansion, to argue that the province's value had already been tested as part of a long anti-Xiongnu security system.

The concept is adjacent to [[StrategicDepthDefense|战略纵深防御]] but points in the opposite direction. Strategic depth defense explains how space can buy survival after defeat; strategic frontier abandonment explains how giving up a buffer can destroy that space and move the danger closer to the core.

## Key Claims

- A frontier province can be expensive and still be strategically cheaper to hold than to abandon.
- Tax burden and rebellion are real costs, but they do not by themselves settle whether withdrawal reduces danger or compounds it.
- Frontier geography can convert a current local problem into a future core-region threat if enemy forces inherit the corridor, horses, and depth.
- Historical frontier policy matters because earlier investment may define why a region is a shield rather than ordinary marginal land.
- The concept requires source grounding; it should not become a blanket claim that every border must always be held regardless of capacity.
