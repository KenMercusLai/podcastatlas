## Definition
Battlefield feint and lure is the use of false targets, visible retreats, controlled exits, provocation, or misinformation to move an enemy into a weaker position before the decisive blow.

## Current Synthesis
[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-733-2-gengyan-youzhizhe-shijingcheng-jiangmen-huzi-yongguan-sanjun-lnlfumtdzrigby9ox4v6pzbeogeh|Hanji 733-2]] develops this tactic through [[GengYanEasternHan|耿弇]]'s Qinghe and Qi-region campaigns. The episode stresses that he does not merely overpower opponents: he leaves an escape route to keep encircled troops from fighting to the death, announces one target while attacking another, refuses to let imperial arrival dictate tempo, feigns retreat to draw pursuit, and prepares ambushes on retreat paths.

The Chengdu campaign in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-712-liuxiu-weihe-bei-yuwei-zui-hui-da-zhang-de-huangdi-lt7rx7tblodjfngqfdq33ikrcmyn|Hanji 712]] shows deception used by both sides after earlier command risk. [[WuHanEasternHan|Wu Han]] uses flags, fires, and secret night movement to escape encirclement and rejoin [[LiuShangEasternHan|Liu Shang]]. [[YanCenEasternHan|Yan Cen]] later uses a visible bridge/market challenge to fix Wu Han's attention while cavalry circles behind him. [[ZhangKanEasternHan|Zhang Kan]] then recommends showing weakness rather than retreating, treating enemy exhaustion as a condition for a lure.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-697-yi-kou-qi-kan-dong-donghan-mingjiang-gengyan-de-chuanqi-rensheng-lpbhbmedezq7pjuz5l3q-s1unh7c|Hanji 697]] reinforces the Geng Yan pattern as a broader tactical repertoire: psychological warfare, strongpoint attacks, encirclement-relief, false-target strikes, avoid-strong/strike-weak choices, and calm morale maintenance after a thigh wound. It also ties the tactic to finish-work because Zhang Bu's surrender is followed by troop and wagon dispersal rather than left as a battlefield result alone.

The current synthesis is that battlefield deception works when it controls the enemy's expectation of safety, urgency, or prestige. In the Geng Yan story, the best move is often not to maximize immediate pressure, but to shape what the opponent thinks is possible next and then convert that movement into surrender, dispersal, or territorial consolidation.

## Key Claims
- Feints work by changing enemy movement, not only by hiding one's own movement.
- Leaving an apparent exit can be more effective than total encirclement when panic resistance would be costly.
- Public statements about intended targets can become tactical instruments when the enemy treats them as reliable information.
- Feigned weakness or retreat is useful only if the pursuer's route and timing have already been prepared.
- Geng Yan's campaign narrative links tactical deception to political consolidation because battlefield movement is followed by surrender handling and force dispersal.
- Deceptive signals can preserve a force after overextension by masking concentration or withdrawal timing.
- A frontal display can become a trap when it fixes attention while mobile force strikes from the rear.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The page currently synthesizes three source notes and should not be treated as a complete theory of ancient Chinese deception tactics. Hanji 712 ends before the final outcome of Zhang Kan's show-weakness advice, so that item supports the intended tactical logic rather than a completed result in this source. Hanji 697's "战神", "兵仙", and "every battle victorious" language is treated as source-scoped evaluation, not as an independent tactical proof. Some minor battlefield names in the transcripts may need textual verification, so the concept records the pattern of action rather than over-committing to every named locality or subordinate.

## What Changed
- Added Hanji 697's compact Geng Yan layer, connecting tactical repertoire to surrendered-force dispersal after Zhang Bu's submission.
- Added Hanji 712's Chengdu examples: Wu Han's concealed night concentration, Yan Cen's frontal-display/rear-cavalry strike, and Zhang Kan's show-weakness advice.
- Preserved Hanji 733-2 as the Geng Yan campaign base for controlled exits, false targets, feigned retreat, and ambush.
- Kept feint-and-lure logic distinct from broader battlefield information control.
