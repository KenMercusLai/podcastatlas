## Definition
Battlefield feint and lure is the use of false targets, visible retreats, controlled exits, provocation, or misinformation to move an enemy into a weaker position before the decisive blow.

## Current Synthesis
[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-733-2-gengyan-youzhizhe-shijingcheng-jiangmen-huzi-yongguan-sanjun-lnlfumtdzrigby9ox4v6pzbeogeh|Hanji 733-2]] develops this tactic through [[GengYanEasternHan|耿弇]]'s Qinghe and Qi-region campaigns. The episode stresses that he does not merely overpower opponents: he leaves an escape route to keep encircled troops from fighting to the death, announces one target while attacking another, refuses to let imperial arrival dictate tempo, feigns retreat to draw pursuit, and prepares ambushes on retreat paths.

The current synthesis is that battlefield deception works when it controls the enemy's expectation of safety, urgency, or prestige. In the Geng Yan story, the best move is often not to maximize immediate pressure, but to shape what the opponent thinks is possible next.

## Key Claims
- Feints work by changing enemy movement, not only by hiding one's own movement.
- Leaving an apparent exit can be more effective than total encirclement when panic resistance would be costly.
- Public statements about intended targets can become tactical instruments when the enemy treats them as reliable information.
- Feigned weakness or retreat is useful only if the pursuer's route and timing have already been prepared.
- Geng Yan's campaign narrative links tactical deception to political consolidation because battlefield tempo accelerates surrender.

- Announced target and real target: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-733-2-gengyan-youzhizhe-shijingcheng-jiangmen-huzi-yongguan-sanjun-lnlfumtdzrigby9ox4v6pzbeogeh|Hanji 733-2]] says Geng Yan talks about one target while striking at Linzi.
- Lure and ambush: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-733-2-gengyan-youzhizhe-shijingcheng-jiangmen-huzi-yongguan-sanjun-lnlfumtdzrigby9ox4v6pzbeogeh|Hanji 733-2]] presents feigned retreat, provoked pursuit, and retreat-path ambush as recurring moves in the Zhang Bu-linked campaign.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The page currently synthesizes one episode and should not be treated as a complete theory of ancient Chinese deception tactics. Some minor battlefield names in the transcript may need textual verification, so the concept records the pattern of action rather than over-committing to every named locality or subordinate.
