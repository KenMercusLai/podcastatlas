# Purpose Identity Lock-In / 目标身份锁定

## Definition
Purpose identity lock-in is the condition in which pursuing one target becomes necessary to justify injury, sacrifice, and self-worth, so stopping feels like erasing the life already spent and succeeding threatens the identity built around pursuit.

## Current Synthesis
[[CaptainAhab|Ahab]] supplies the founding case. In the episode's reading, the white whale is not only an enemy but the answer to “why me?” and the promised proof that forty years of danger, absence, aging, and limited reward led somewhere. The target therefore repairs a threatened life story while narrowing the future to one outcome.

The concept has two traps. Failure preserves the chase by making completion still necessary; success can create emptiness by removing the role, routine, and explanation that organized the self. Authority magnifies the damage when a locked-in individual transfers private existential need into collective obligation.

## Key Claims
- A target becomes identity-locking when it explains both past suffering and future worth.
- Stopping can feel more threatening than continued loss because it exposes sacrifice as possibly unrecoverable.
- Success may destabilize the pursuer when no identity or relation exists beyond the goal.
- Injury can intensify lock-in when the target is imagined as a meaningful agent rather than an accident.
- Formal authority lets private purpose externalize risk onto other people.
- Purpose identity lock-in is interpretive, not a clinical diagnosis.

## Evidence
- Injury meaning - [[vol-356-baijing-yongchang-de-shenghuo-yishougou-qing-dahai-daizou-wode-aichou-gkwriw4oms-ma1lixgtjj3um]] reads Ahab's lost leg as an accident transformed into cosmic grievance.
- Life justification - [[vol-356-baijing-yongchang-de-shenghuo-yishougou-qing-dahai-daizou-wode-aichou-gkwriw4oms-ma1lixgtjj3um]] connects the hunt to decades of danger, separation, fatigue, aging, and inadequate material return.
- Success trap - [[vol-356-baijing-yongchang-de-shenghuo-yishougou-qing-dahai-daizou-wode-aichou-gkwriw4oms-ma1lixgtjj3um]] imagines that victory could leave Ahab without a sustaining structure or recognizable self.
- Collective cost - [[vol-356-baijing-yongchang-de-shenghuo-yishougou-qing-dahai-daizou-wode-aichou-gkwriw4oms-ma1lixgtjj3um]] asks whether one person's purpose gives him the right to expose an entire crew to destruction.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Long commitment is not itself pathological or unjustified. Difficult goals often require endurance, and apparent failure can precede real achievement. Lock-in applies when protecting the identity function of a target overrides new evidence, viable alternatives, relationships, and the autonomy or safety of others. The current evidence is literary and does not establish prevalence outside the novel.

## What Changed
- Created the concept from Ahab's attempt to make injury and a sacrificed working life meaningful through one terminal goal.
