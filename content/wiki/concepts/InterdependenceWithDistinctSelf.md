# Interdependence with a Distinct Self

## Definition
Interdependence with a distinct self is cooperation among participants who remain differentiated enough to hold boundaries, contribute different capacities, and stand up for their own needs.

## Current Synthesis
The source rejects both isolated individualism and total merger. Relationship requires an “I” and a “you” capable of forming a “we”; if participants become identical or one absorbs the other, division of labor and reciprocity lose meaning. Distinct selfhood therefore makes interdependence possible rather than obstructing it. The same boundary also protects against domination, coercive unity, and collective systems that demand participation without voice or exit.

## Key Claims
- Meaningful relationship requires distinct participants rather than an undifferentiated whole.
- Difference creates the possibility of complementary contribution and division of labor.
- Cooperation should preserve enough agency for participants to refuse, negotiate, and represent themselves.
- Belonging to a wider system does not erase responsibility at the level of the person or group.
- Union becomes harmful when it absorbs identity, hides coercion, or prevents accountability.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode offers a normative framework rather than criteria for measuring when identity or exit is sufficiently protected. Some relationships legitimately constrain unilateral choice, and some collective goods require obligations that cannot be abandoned at will. Distinctness should not become a new excuse for denying material dependence or shared responsibility.
