## Definition

封爵继承公共责任 / title succession public duty is the governance ethic that inheriting a state-conferred title or fief can be a public responsibility, not a private asset that the heir may freely refuse for moral display, illness claim, or family preference.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 738 grounds the concept through [[DingHongEasternHan|Ding Hong]] and [[BaoJunEasternHan|Bao Jun]]. After Ding's father dies, Ding should inherit the marquisate, but he reports illness and asks to yield the title to his younger brother. When the court refuses, he leaves quietly after the burial.

Bao Jun's correction changes the meaning of the act. He does not simply condemn humility, and he does not deny that old yielding stories can be honorable in some settings. Instead, he argues that examples such as Bo Yi and Ji Zha belong to disorder or special circumstances. In an ordered state, a title granted through public authority carries obligation; personal affairs cannot cancel it.

The result is practical rather than only moral. Ding returns, inherits, and is later recommended for classics learning and conduct before being appointed shizhong. The concept therefore treats title succession as a channel where family inheritance, reputation, and state duty overlap.

## Key Claims

- A state-conferred title can impose responsibility on an heir rather than functioning only as privilege.
- Yielding can be misapplied when a disorder-era moral example is copied into an ordered institutional setting.
- Personal illness claims, family preference, or self-effacing reputation do not automatically override public assignment.
- Correcting the heir's duty can convert private retreat into useful court service.
- The concept is about title succession and public obligation, not about forcing every inheritance or office in all circumstances.

- Institutional outcome: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-738-ta-pinsi-baoquan-dahan-qijie-weihe-canzao-mengyuan-lrvc1f6fv2h66q0plecihmfmdvxu|Hanji 738]] says Ding returns to inherit and is later recommended and appointed shizhong.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept does not prove that all yielding is empty performance or that all inherited titles should always be accepted. It is source-bounded to a case where the court has already refused the transfer and where Bao Jun frames the title as a public responsibility inside an ordered state.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Bao Jun's correction of Ding Hong's title-yielding attempt in Hanji 738.
