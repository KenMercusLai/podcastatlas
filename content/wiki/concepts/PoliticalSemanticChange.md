# Political Semantic Change / 政治性语义变迁

## Definition

Political semantic change is the alteration of a word’s public meaning, emotional charge, or field of application through ideological systems, campaigns, movements, and strategic rhetoric.

## Current Synthesis

The episode distinguishes ordinary language change from deliberate political participation in meaning. Political actors do not merely argue about already stable terms; they can attach stigma, virtue, backwardness, oppression, or threat to a label until the new association shapes what audiences perceive before substantive argument begins.

“封建,” “death tax,” “爹味,” and “woke” are used as different examples of this mechanism. Their histories and political directions differ, so the concept does not imply that every semantic shift is centrally planned or illegitimate. It instead asks analysts and dictionary editors to separate current usage, historical meaning, strategic origin, audience uptake, and normative evaluation.

## Key Claims

- Political conflict can change connotation as well as denotation.
- A strategically chosen label can pre-load judgment before a policy or social pattern is examined.
- Repetition and uptake can make an initially partisan framing part of ordinary language.
- Dictionary inclusion can document that uptake without endorsing the political project that helped produce it.
- Historical etymology does not automatically invalidate a newer meaning, but it can expose what the new usage hides or compresses.
- Competing movements can use the same mechanism; political semantic change is not exclusive to one ideology.

## Evidence

- Ideological recoding: [[306-cidian-you-shenme-yong-lj-xsj-bvpmo0imc3qnv8njlgzso|306-词典有什么用？]] argues that “封建” moved from a political-institutional term toward a broad pejorative through a Marxist historical frame applied to China.
- Strategic labeling: [[306-cidian-you-shenme-yong-lj-xsj-bvpmo0imc3qnv8njlgzso|306-词典有什么用？]] uses “death tax” as an example of naming designed to stigmatize a tax.
- Movement and backlash terms: [[306-cidian-you-shenme-yong-lj-xsj-bvpmo0imc3qnv8njlgzso|306-词典有什么用？]] uses “爹味” and “woke” to show that social movements and opponents can both reshape evaluative meaning.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The examples are interpretive and do not supply corpus frequencies, reception studies, complete etymologies, or proof of a single causal author for each shift. Ordinary metaphor, humor, group identity, translation, and decentralized imitation can change meanings without coordinated manipulation. Earlier definitions are not automatically more correct, and a politically introduced term can still identify a real pattern.

## What Changed

- Created a framework separating current usage from strategic origin, historical meaning, and normative endorsement.
- Clarified that dictionaries can document politicized uptake without treating its political framing as neutral truth.
