# Omnipotent Self-Blame / 全能自恋式归因

## Definition
Omnipotent self-blame / 全能自恋式归因 is the pattern where a person treats external hardship, relational disappointment, or system volatility as if it must have been fully caused by their own defect, failure, or insufficient effort.

## Current Synthesis
In the source, Steve uses "全能自恋" less as grandiosity than as an anxious form of false omnipotence. The person cannot tolerate uncertainty, dependence, or uncontrollable context, so they preserve an illusion of control by saying the bad outcome happened because they were not good enough.

This pattern appears across study-abroad loneliness, creator traffic anxiety, interpersonal hurt, and emotional flashbacks. Its signal is asymmetrical attribution: negative events are immediately internalized, while success, persistence, and good outcomes are discounted or treated as luck. The practical correction is to restore mixed causality: situation, system, other people, body state, history, and self-action all have to be considered before the self becomes the only defendant.

## Key Claims
- Self-blame can feel like control because it makes a painful world seem causally simple.
- The pattern is strongest when negative events become personal defects but positive events are not allowed to count as one's own contribution.
- Difficult situations such as study abroad, platform work, or social ambiguity should be evaluated as contexts, not only as tests of character.
- Omnipotent self-blame overlaps with shame because the conclusion usually attacks the whole self rather than a specific action.
- Trauma-linked alarm and insecurity can intensify the pattern by making old danger feel present and personally deserved.
- Correction requires multiple-cause thinking, self-kindness, and action experiments rather than another round of self-attack.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept does not deny agency, accountability, or the need to learn from mistakes. Its boundary is attribution discipline: responsibility should be specific, proportional, and evidence-based rather than a total verdict on the self. The source is public psychoeducation and does not make omnipotent self-blame a clinical diagnosis.
