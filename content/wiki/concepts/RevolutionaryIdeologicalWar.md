# Revolutionary Ideological War

## Definition

Revolutionary ideological war is armed conflict justified not only by defence or territorial interest but by a claim to transform the political and social order of other peoples.

## Current Synthesis

After [[BattleOfValmy|Valmy]] and French victories in the Rhineland, Savoy, Nice, and Belgium, the Convention debated whether to stop at defensible borders or assist peoples seeking liberty. Its promise of aid and later commitment to anti-feudal, anti-clerical, and republican reform made French war universal in aspiration, while the use of armies to define liberation left consent and conquest in unresolved tension.

## Key Claims

- Military survival can shift revolutionary war from defence to expansion.
- Universal political principles make borders appear morally provisional.
- Liberation policy can join aid to institutional redesign imposed by an occupying army.
- Abolition of feudal and church privilege turned war aims into social transformation.
- Patriotic victory and ideological confidence can reinforce one another.
- A declared wish to free peoples does not settle whether those peoples consent to the liberator's program.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

- The episode poses but does not resolve the difference between liberation, strategic expansion, and conquest.
- “The people” of another territory cannot be assumed to speak with one voice or to welcome the same reforms.
- The source covers the declaration and early advances, not the full later record of French occupation.

## What Changed

- A canonical concept now captures the Revolution's move from defensive war to universalizing armed reform.
