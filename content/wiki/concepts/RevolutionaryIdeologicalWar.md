# Revolutionary Ideological War

## Definition

Revolutionary ideological war is armed conflict justified not only by defence or territorial interest but by a claim to transform the political and social order of other peoples.

## Current Synthesis

The ideological program preceded French military success. [[JacquesPierreBrissot|Brissot]] described war as sacred, regenerative, equalizing, financially restorative, and capable of liberating humanity, while [[MaximilienRobespierre|Robespierre]] warned that foreign peoples would reject armed missionaries and that expansion could create a military dictator. [[CharlesFrancoisDumouriez|Dumouriez]] then treated a Belgian rising as the premise of an easy campaign. After [[BattleOfValmy|Valmy]] and French victories, the Convention promised aid and anti-feudal, anti-clerical, and republican reform to peoples seeking liberty.

[[NapoleonBonaparte|Napoleon]] supplies the later personal and operational bridge. Revolutionary universalism helped him reinterpret movement from Corsica into French service, while his plans for an army through Italy toward Vienna joined strategic concentration, national war, personal advancement, and the outward movement of the Revolution. Across these phases, universal aspiration left identity, consent, strategic interest, conquest, and military dictatorship in unresolved tension.

## Key Claims

- Military survival can shift revolutionary war from defence to expansion, while ideological war can begin before combat when leaders treat national regeneration and foreign liberation as one project.
- Universal political principles make borders appear morally provisional.
- Liberation policy can join aid to institutional redesign imposed by an occupying army.
- Abolition of feudal and church privilege turned war aims into social transformation.
- Patriotic victory and ideological confidence can reinforce one another.
- Universalist war can offer outsiders a new identity while attaching their advancement to national expansion.
- A declared wish to free peoples does not settle whether those peoples consent to the liberator's program.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

- The episode poses but does not resolve the difference between liberation, strategic expansion, and conquest.
- The episode's retrospective link from Robespierre's warning to Napoleon is suggestive, not proof of inevitability.
- “The people” of another territory cannot be assumed to speak with one voice or to welcome the same reforms.
- The source covers the declaration and early advances, not the full later record of French occupation.
- Napoleon's identity transition and Italian planning do not prove that universalism alone caused his allegiance or that later empire was inevitable.

## What Changed

- Added Napoleon as the personal bridge from revolutionary universalism to French military expansion.
- Added identity formation and career opportunity to the existing consent, aggression, and dictatorship risks.
