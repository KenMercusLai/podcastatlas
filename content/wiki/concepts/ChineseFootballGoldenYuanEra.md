## Definition
中国足球金元时代 / Chinese Football Golden-Yuan Era is the Chinese professional-football period when multiple clubs escalated transfer spending, foreign-player recruitment, and domestic-player salaries beyond what ordinary club economics could support.

## Current Synthesis
The source argues against blaming the whole era on [[Evergrande]]'s 2010 entry alone. Evergrande spent heavily and changed competitive expectations, but a true arms race emerged only when other clubs such as Hebei China Fortune, Quanjian, Suning, and others joined high-priced recruitment around 2016. The era therefore reflects league-wide competitive imitation: one rich club is distortionary, but many clubs bidding against each other reset wages, transfer prices, and player expectations.

## Key Claims
- The golden-yuan era is better dated to the multi-club 2016 arms race than to Evergrande's first high-spending years alone.
- Foreign-player prices became a visible signal through 50-million-euro-class names such as Alex Teixeira, Oscar, and Hulk in the source's account.
- Domestic-player salaries and transfer expectations also changed, especially after other clubs began poaching or threatening to poach Guangzhou players.
- High domestic wages weakened incentives for some Chinese players to seek lower-paid overseas development paths.
- The spending boom created achievement and damage at the same time: stronger club rosters and Asian success coexisted with fragile economics and professional-norm erosion.

## Evidence
- Timing claim: [[vol-225-hengda-de-zuqiu-yichan-ft-panweili-gkwrijeozrpkafcpbatn-ivb]] says a single Evergrande spender was not enough to define the era, while 2016 brought a wider arms race involving multiple clubs.
- Foreign-player escalation: [[vol-225-hengda-de-zuqiu-yichan-ft-panweili-gkwrijeozrpkafcpbatn-ivb]] cites Teixeira, Oscar, and Hulk as examples of the high-price winter window.
- Domestic wage reset: [[vol-225-hengda-de-zuqiu-yichan-ft-panweili-gkwrijeozrpkafcpbatn-ivb]] says Guangzhou players' renewals rose after outside clubs began applying poaching pressure.
- Overseas-development effect: [[vol-225-hengda-de-zuqiu-yichan-ft-panweili-gkwrijeozrpkafcpbatn-ivb]] discusses high domestic pay as one reason players such as Zhang Linpeng and Wei Shihao did not take or sustain overseas paths.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not give a formal league-wide salary dataset, so the era boundary is interpretive. Earlier clubs and markets showed gold-rush symptoms before 2016, and Evergrande still played a catalytic role even if it was not the only cause.
