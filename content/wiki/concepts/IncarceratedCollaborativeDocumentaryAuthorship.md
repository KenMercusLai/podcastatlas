# Incarcerated Collaborative Documentary Authorship

## Definition

Incarcerated collaborative documentary authorship is a production relationship in which imprisoned participants materially shape a documentary's evidence, access, narrative, and representation rather than appearing only as filmed subjects.

## Current Synthesis

In [[TheAlabamaSolution|The Alabama Solution]], incarcerated people had already recorded years of prison conditions and organized protests before the outside filmmakers arrived. Their prohibited phones supplied images, live calls, and continuing communication after official access ended. The resulting authority is distributed: [[AndrewJarecki]] and [[CharlotteKaufman]] edit and publish the film, while participants create much of the primary record and negotiate how their organizing appears.

Collaboration also changes responsibility. Showing participants the completed film, maintaining dialogue, organizing legal support, and continuing wellness checks recognize that publication can expose contributors to retaliation. Shared authorship is therefore not equal control in every decision, but it requires more than extracting testimony.

## Key Claims

- Participants can become evidence producers when institutional access excludes outside observers.
- Preexisting organizing and recording should not be redescribed as a filmmaker's discovery alone.
- Editorial consultation can redistribute narrative authority without erasing the director's final power.
- Continuing retaliation risk turns release into a new phase of duty rather than the end of the relationship.
- Collaboration claims should remain specific about control, consent, credit, security, and material support.

## Evidence

### Evidence and narrative contribution

- [[behind-the-scenes-with-andrew-jarecki-behind-the-scenes-with-andrew-jarecki]] says participants supplied years of footage, approximately 30 percent of the finished film's video calls, and the organizing activity the film depicts.

### Continuing responsibility

- [[behind-the-scenes-with-andrew-jarecki-behind-the-scenes-with-andrew-jarecki]] describes phone screenings, representation dialogue, a whistleblower defense committee, and legal wellness checks.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode does not specify contracts, compensation, ownership of footage, veto rights, credit allocation, security failures, or disagreements over the final cut. Calling participants collaborators does not by itself prove equal power or eliminate the risks of extraction.

## What Changed

- Established the concept through participant-generated evidence, organizing, editorial dialogue, and post-release protection.
