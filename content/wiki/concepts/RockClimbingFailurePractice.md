# Rock Climbing Failure Practice / 攀岩失败练习

Rock climbing failure practice / 攀岩失败练习 is [[ep250-zoujin-shanye-shunliu-ershang-xunzhao-huwai-yundong-zhong-de-xinliu-shike-lpv4egumbxcjrxk3jypehyxcqfuo]]'s account of climbing as a sport that makes fear, frustration, breath, and self-judgment visible. [[Amon|阿蒙]] describes hard moves where breathing must stay steady, outdoor routes where wind and rain make the scene unforgettable, and projects that may require many attempts across months or years.

The concept extends [[FailureDesensitization]] but is not only about embarrassment. Climbing failure is physical, emotional, technical, and environmental at once: a person may fall, freeze, blame the route, cry, feel guilt, or discover resilience. That makes failure a training surface for [[EmbodiedJudgment]], not a generic motivational slogan.

## Key Claims
- Climbing exposes mental state because fear and breathing immediately affect movement.
- Failure does not erase flow; an unfinished route can still become one of the most immersive experiences.
- Repeated attempts build resilience when the climber treats failure as information rather than identity.
- Emotional reactions at the wall can reveal patterns usually hidden in ordinary life.
- Controlled protection and realistic judgment are what make failure practice different from recklessness.
