# Rural Industry-Led Improvement / 乡村工业改良

## Definition

Rural industry-led improvement is a development strategy that uses sideline production, technical upgrading, processing, and farmer organization to raise rural income while trying to return industrial gains to producing households.

## Current Synthesis

In the Jiangnan silk case, the strategy responds to limited land and falling rural income by improving silkworm varieties, production methods, processing, cooperation, and market access. Its defining question is distribution: similar technology can serve a private firm's profit and raw-material control or a reformer's effort to let farmers share the gain.

The strategy is neither a denial of rural crisis nor a universal alternative to redistribution or revolution. Silk households remained exposed to synthetic substitutes, Japanese mechanized competition, collapsing international demand, war, credit, and unequal local capacity. Technical improvement works only within institutions that govern risk, bargaining power, ownership, and benefit allocation.

## Key Claims

- Rural industry can supplement land income and connect technical progress to household livelihood.
- Technology alone does not determine who captures productivity gains.
- Cooperative organization and benefit return distinguish farmer-centered reform from upstream control by firms.
- World-market exposure can overwhelm local efficiency gains.
- Capital access and successful participation may increase household differentiation as well as income.
- A sector-specific regional success cannot automatically become a national rural program.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode does not provide audited farmer incomes, productivity series, ownership records, or comparative program outcomes. Industrial participation can exclude poorly capitalized households or deepen differentiation. External demand, ecology, and local skills constrain transfer, while redistribution and political power remain analytically separate from production growth.

## What Changed

- Created a distribution-aware account of rural technical and industrial reform.
- Distinguished shared methods from divergent enterprise and farmer-benefit objectives.
- Added world-market, inequality, and transfer limits to the improvement case.
