# Chinese Woodblock Printing Economics / 中国雕版印刷经济性

## Definition
Chinese woodblock printing economics is the source's frame for why woodblock printing could remain practically dominant in China: production cost, labor process, typeface, edition scale, paper, and reuse mattered more than a simple technology-progress story.

## Current Synthesis
The current sources challenge a movable-type-centered progress story through practical production incentives. Woodblocks could be efficient for actual publishing conditions, straighter typefaces made carving faster, and cheaper paper signaled commercial scale as well as preservation risk. The writing-traditions episode adds that Chinese movable type required a large inventory, duplicates of common characters, literate compositors, and careful alignment, while reusable carved blocks supported repeat printing with more consistent pages.

## Key Claims
- Movable type was not automatically more economical or suitable than woodblock printing in Chinese publishing practice.
- Typeface design affected labor cost because straighter Song-style forms were easier for artisans to carve quickly.
- Material choices such as bamboo paper can reveal commercialization as well as later preservation fragility.
- Printing technology history should be explained through workflow and cost, not only invention priority.
- Print quality, character inventory, skilled labor, expected reuse, and intended durability jointly determine the practical choice of method.

## Evidence
- Woodblock dominance - [[246-hangzhou-bowuguan-yiwei-hangzhi-haojiu-mei-kan-guo-zheme-tuijian-de-zhanlan-le-8217911423-973793]] says the hosts emphasize China's long reliance on woodblock printing because it remained cost- and efficiency-competitive.
- Typeface and labor - [[246-hangzhou-bowuguan-yiwei-hangzhi-haojiu-mei-kan-guo-zheme-tuijian-de-zhanlan-le-8217911423-973793]] links Song typeface forms to faster carving and lower cost.
- Bamboo paper - [[246-hangzhou-bowuguan-yiwei-hangzhi-haojiu-mei-kan-guo-zheme-tuijian-de-zhanlan-le-8217911423-973793]] treats late-Ming bamboo paper as both fragile and evidence of publishing commercialization.
- Movable-type overhead - [[244-shuxie-gei-guoqu-weilai-de-ren-yu-shenming-zhongguo-wenming-zhong-de-shuxie-chuantong-yu-changqi-zhuyi-8225426843-437457]] emphasizes character inventory, duplicate sorts, literacy, spacing, alignment, and inconsistent page appearance.
- Block reuse - [[244-shuxie-gei-guoqu-weilai-de-ren-yu-shenming-zhongguo-wenming-zhong-de-shuxie-chuantong-yu-changqi-zhuyi-8225426843-437457]] notes that finished blocks could be stored and repeatedly printed, aligning cost with expected future use.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources offer podcast-level syntheses rather than a full economic model. They support a corrective against simplistic movable-type triumphalism, but exact cost comparisons and claims about which genres used which method should remain source-scoped.
