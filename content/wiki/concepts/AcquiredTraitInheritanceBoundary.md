# Acquired-Trait Inheritance Boundary

## Definition
Acquired-trait inheritance boundary is the source's distinction between traits biologically transmitted through germ cells and traits or memories acquired during life that usually remain in the body or brain of the individual.

## Current Synthesis
The episode treats the boundary as the default starting point for any discussion of inherited memories. Eye color and other ordinary inherited traits make sense because sperm and egg carry genetic information into the next generation. Learned architecture, synaptic patterns, and gym-built muscle do not automatically enter germ cells, so they are not expected to appear biologically in children.

The boundary is not absolute in every organism or mechanism. The source's worm examples show that acquired molecular responses can cross generations under specific conditions, especially through small RNAs. The current judgment is therefore conditional: acquired-trait inheritance is real in some C. elegans experiments, but that evidence does not establish inherited human memories, learned skills, trauma responses, or acquired physical adaptations.

## Key Claims
- Genetic inheritance and acquired experience are different evidence categories.
- Somatic changes such as learning or muscle growth normally do not become germline information.
- The [[WeismannBarrier]] and [[EpigeneticReprogrammingBoundary]] explain why acquired states usually stop within the individual.
- Narrow molecular exceptions can exist, especially in [[CElegansModelOrganism]] small-RNA systems.
- Worm evidence should not be generalized into claims that human memories or learned skills are biologically inherited.

## Evidence
- Ordinary boundary - [[essentials-genes-inheritance-memories-across-generations-dr-oded-rechavi-scim1624813352]] contrasts eye color inheritance with learned architecture and gym-built muscle.
- Somatic memory location - [[essentials-genes-inheritance-memories-across-generations-dr-oded-rechavi-scim1624813352]] places learned information in synapses and neural circuits rather than sperm or eggs.
- Barrier mechanisms - [[essentials-genes-inheritance-memories-across-generations-dr-oded-rechavi-scim1624813352]] names soma-germline separation and epigenetic reprogramming as two major obstacles.
- Narrow exception - [[essentials-genes-inheritance-memories-across-generations-dr-oded-rechavi-scim1624813352]] presents C. elegans small-RNA inheritance as a real acquired-response mechanism.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not deny every form of non-genetic inheritance. Its caution is about scope: the strongest positive evidence is in C. elegans, while mammals and humans remain much less settled. The page should not be used as proof that human memories, skills, trauma responses, or parental exercise adaptations are inherited biologically.

## What Changed
- Created a boundary concept for distinguishing inherited genetic information from acquired memories or bodily changes.
