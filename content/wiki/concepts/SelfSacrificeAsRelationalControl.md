# Self-Sacrifice as Relational Control / 以自我牺牲替他人做主

## Definition

Self-sacrifice as relational control is the pattern in which a person accepts pain or loss for a supposedly noble end while also withholding information, narrowing another person's choice, or deciding what outcome is best for them.

## Current Synthesis

The bounded case is [[LiXunhuan|李寻欢]]'s effort to repay [[LongXiaoyunElderGuLong|龙啸云]] by pushing [[LinShiyin|林诗音]] toward marriage through performed betrayal. His own cost is real: he loses home, relationship, direction, and happiness. That cost does not make the plan consensual because Lin must decide under false beliefs and is treated as part of a debt settlement between men.

The concept therefore separates generosity from authority. A person may choose what they will surrender, but willingness to suffer does not automatically grant the right to organize another person's intimate life. Noble intention can intensify the blind spot when it makes candid conversation feel less morally impressive than unilateral sacrifice.

## Key Claims

- Personal cost is evidence of commitment, not proof that a decision respects everyone affected.
- A sacrifice becomes controlling when its success depends on another person's ignorance or constrained agency.
- Gratitude and life debt do not turn a third person's love, marriage, labor, or future into a transferable asset.
- Refusing candid communication can protect the sacrificer's moral identity while moving risk onto others.
- Harm can persist even when every participant retains some agency and no one intends the final outcome.
- Repair requires truth and restored choice, not only continued suffering by the person who initiated the plan.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept is drawn from a fictional relationship and does not make every sacrifice manipulative. Emergency decisions, asymmetric information, caregiving, or voluntary gifts can be legitimate when authority, necessity, consent, and review are proportionate. The problem is not choosing personal cost; it is using that cost to justify deception or control over another person's consequential choices.

## What Changed

- Created the concept by separating the moral weight of personal sacrifice from authority over another person's intimate future.
