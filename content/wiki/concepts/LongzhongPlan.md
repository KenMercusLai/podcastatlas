# Longzhong Plan / 隆中对

## Definition
The Longzhong Plan / 隆中对 is the strategic framework attributed to [[ZhugeLiang|诸葛亮]] for turning [[LiuBei|刘备]]'s mobile coalition into a territorial power while maintaining cooperation with [[SunQuan|孙权]].

## Current Synthesis
Hanji 1098 presents the plan as the point where Liu Bei's broad restoration ambition becomes operational. The episode compresses it into three components: build a territorial base around [[Jingzhou|荆州]] and [[YizhouLateHan|益州]], use restoration of the Han as the highest legitimating objective, and cooperate with [[SunQuan|孙权]] against [[CaoCao|曹操]] within an emerging tripartite field.

Hanji 1083 makes the plan's negative boundaries explicit. It reads the framework as saying not to confront Cao Cao directly and not to treat Sun Quan as an immediate conquest target, so limited resources can concentrate on a territorial base and coalition path. This adds [[StrategicExclusionAndFocus|战略排除与聚焦]] to the plan without treating every narrated phrase as a verified quotation.

Hanji 1099 develops the alliance-sequencing component. It says cooperation with Sun Quan was already part of Zhuge Liang's plan, but the post-Changban crisis changes the order: instead of first controlling Jingzhou and then forming the alliance, Liu Bei accepts alliance before securing the territory.

The episode treats the revision as beneficial because early possession of contested Jingzhou could expose a still-weak Liu Bei to pressure from both [[CaoCao|曹操]] and Sun Quan. In this reading, a strategy can preserve its directional goal while changing the operational sequence when the actor's capacity collapses.

## Key Claims
- Cooperation with Sun Quan is presented as an existing component rather than an improvised abandonment of the plan.
- Post-Changban weakness moves alliance formation ahead of territorial acquisition.
- Sequence adaptation can preserve a strategy's purpose when original prerequisites no longer hold.
- Taking strategically valuable territory too early can create simultaneous pressure from stronger neighboring powers.
- A strategic program adds value by translating aspiration into territorial, legitimacy, and alliance components.
- Explicit non-targets prevent strong execution from being dispersed across incompatible fronts.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This page currently rests on three popular-history episodes and does not reconstruct the full Longzhong text, chronology, territorial program, later execution, or historiography. Hanji 1098's three-line compression, Hanji 1083's modern “strategy means what not to do” formula, the exact original ordering, and Hanji 1099's claim that alliance-first was unequivocally better remain source-scoped interpretations. Hanji 1083's contrast with a supposedly strategy-free earlier Liu Bei is also too absolute given evidence of prior coalition building and capacity accumulation.

## What Changed
- Expanded the plan from alliance sequencing to its source-presented territorial, legitimacy, and coalition components.
- Connected the plan to Liu Bei's shift from general ambition toward operational direction.
- Made exclusion and resource concentration explicit while retaining the plan's adaptive-sequencing qualification.
