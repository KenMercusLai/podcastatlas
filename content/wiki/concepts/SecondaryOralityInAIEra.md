# Secondary Orality In AI Era / AI 时代的次生口语文化

## Definition
Secondary orality in the AI era is the episode's extension of [[WalterOng]]'s secondary-orality idea to a media environment shaped by short video, recommendation feeds, and large language models.

## Current Synthesis
The sources use secondary orality cautiously. Episode 224 asks whether text-shaped cognition is weakening as short video and AI dialogue grow, while The Intelligence supplies a political and rhetorical branch: podcasts, clips, rallies, repetition, epithets, and concrete symbols can make speech-like persuasion newly scalable. Together they describe a post-literate media mix rather than a return to pre-literate society.

## Key Claims
- Secondary orality names a media condition after literacy, not a simple absence of literacy.
- Short video and AI dialogue can feel oral or conversational while depending on technical and textual infrastructures.
- The concept helps connect reading decline to new forms of attention, speech, and machine mediation.
- AI may strengthen concept circulation while weakening people's incentive to perform slow textual association themselves.
- The source's preferred response is dual capacity: retain literacy while renewing embodied experience.
- Oral-style media can change political persuasion by rewarding live delivery, repetition, memorable labels, and concrete symbols.

## Evidence
- Ong frame: [[episode-224-bushi-bushi-bushi-bushi-8213451338-252768]] explicitly invokes secondary orality after discussing Ong's orality-literacy distinction.
- Short-video context: [[episode-224-bushi-bushi-bushi-bushi-8213451338-252768]] connects reading decline and long-text avoidance to contemporary feed media.
- AI context: [[episode-224-bushi-bushi-bushi-bushi-8213451338-252768]] applies the term to large language models and machine-mediated association.
- Closing qualification: [[episode-224-bushi-bushi-bushi-bushi-8213451338-252768]] rejects anti-technology nostalgia and argues for holding abstraction and embodied experience together.
- Political-rhetoric branch: [[the-end-of-the-world-is-ai-an-existential-threat-6aaa68a64cef87591d2b659b]] applies Ong's oral-form distinction to podcasts, public debate, political epithets, and symbolic construction projects.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources use the framework interpretively rather than proving that media form causes political success or cognitive decline. They also note that print and text-based platforms have spread rage and falsehood, so orality is not uniquely irrational.
