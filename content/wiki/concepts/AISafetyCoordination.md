## Definition
AI safety coordination is the practice or proposal that AI labs, governments, regulators, experts, or international institutions should share information, review risks, and align safeguards around frontier AI development before failures become unmanageable.

## Current Synthesis
The bounded evidence describes a ladder rather than one coordination mechanism. It begins with recurring lab contact and competitor peer review, moves through worker appeals and mandatory incident investigation, and reaches government diplomacy and global compute monitoring. Track-two expert dialogue, technical standards work, private vulnerability remediation, and shared international evaluations may remain feasible when a comprehensive agreement to slow frontier development is not.

The central obstacle is incentive compatibility. Firms and states may benefit from others' restraint while continuing themselves, capability diffuses beyond a few actors, and U.S.-China security competition turns mutual fear into acceleration pressure. The Asilomar precedent adds a hybrid route: experts can define a narrow safety agreement, but durable coordination still needs liability or control over a resource participants cannot easily replace. A San Francisco picnic adds an informal community layer: convening can expose shared concern and map disagreement before formal negotiation, but it creates neither authority nor compliance. Coordination is therefore most credible when it joins bounded objectives, evidence access, repeat interaction, clear authority, practical engineering work, and an enforcement lever rather than relying on goodwill alone.

## Key Claims
- Coordination can share threat information and identify technical failures faster than ordinary lawmaking.
- Rivalry does not eliminate common interests, but it makes broad restraint vulnerable to defection and strategic mistrust.
- Peer review, incident investigation, and expert dialogue address different layers and should not be treated as interchangeable.
- Government pace-setting and international diplomacy become relevant when voluntary company coordination is too weak.
- Narrow technical cooperation can reduce cross-border risk without requiring agreement on political systems or overall AI strategy.
- Coordination remains incomplete without evidence access, durable channels, enforcement, a response path when warnings are ignored, and voice for people bearing concentrated costs; informal convening can surface competing remedies but cannot substitute for those mechanisms.
- Safety institutes and summits can build shared technical capacity, but they do not become governance authority merely by producing evaluations or recommendations.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Coordination can become symbolic when participants share little, lack authority, or use safety channels strategically. The sources disagree on institutional ambition: informal picnics, recurring lab calls, common evaluations, mandatory investigations, compute monitoring, funding conditions, and private vulnerability disclosure require different trust and enforcement. The reported AI incident remains contested and source-scoped, while the WeChat example comes from cybersecurity and does not prove that frontier-model restraint can be verified. Summit participation, community convening, and safety-institute capacity do not establish compliance. Nuclear, aviation, arms-control, and recombinant-DNA analogies are suggestive but imperfect because AI capability is more commercially funded, internationally distributed, and reproducible. Asilomar also had representation failures and concentrated career costs.

## What Changed
- Added shared international evaluations and safety-institute capacity to the coordination ladder.
- Clarified that summits and technical competence remain advisory unless linked to authority and response mechanisms.
- Added Asilomar's expert-rule-plus-funder-enforcement model and its representation boundary.
- Added informal community convening as a pre-negotiation layer that surfaces disagreement without creating authority or compliance.
