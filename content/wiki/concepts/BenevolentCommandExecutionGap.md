# Benevolent Command Execution Gap / 仁厚指挥执行缺口

Benevolent command execution gap / 仁厚指挥执行缺口 is the command failure named from [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-972-sanguo-liuyu-zhisi-taisha-taitianzhen-lvawrcandbh628rckukosgarh7v2|Hanji 972]]. [[LiuYuLateHan|刘虞]] attacks [[GongsunZan|公孙瓒]] with a large force while ordering his soldiers not to burn the city and to harm no one except Gongsun Zan. The order preserves a humane boundary, but the source says Liu Yu's troops are loose and undisciplined, so the moral rule is not matched by an executable military design.

The gap is not that mercy is bad. It is that civilian protection, targeted coercion, and battlefield victory require command structure, training, route control, and tactical means. Liu Yu's restraint narrows the permitted action while leaving the army without a credible way to take the target, letting Gongsun Zan counterattack with a smaller disciplined group.

The concept sits between [[GoodIntentionsPoliticalLimits]] and [[ConfucianMilitaryIdealismLimits|儒家军事理想的限度]]. It accepts the moral value of restraint but treats command as the mechanism that must make restraint usable. In that sense it is also a late-Han practical counterpart to [[XunzianCommandRestraint|五权三制]]: humane force is only real when orders, incentives, and troops can actually carry it out.

## Key Claims
- A humane operational constraint can fail if it is stated as a wish rather than built into command design.
- Liu Yu's "kill only Gongsun Zan" order narrows violence but also turns siege warfare into an almost impossible targeting problem for undisciplined troops.
- The failure should not be read as proof that civilian protection is naive; it is evidence that restraint needs training, discipline, and feasible tactics.
- Gongsun Zan's counterattack shows the asymmetry between a large force with unclear execution and a small force with decisive action.
- The case explains why Liu Yu can retain [[PeopleBasedPoliticalSecurity|people-based political security]] while still losing the battle and his life.
