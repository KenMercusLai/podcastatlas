# Permissionless AI Innovation

Permissionless AI innovation is [[DavidSacks|David Sacks]]'s regulatory frame in [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-inside-americas-ai-strategy-infrastructure-regulation-and-global-competition-39846955]]. He argues that Silicon Valley works because founders do not need Washington's approval before starting companies, and that AI competitiveness depends on preserving that operating surface while avoiding a state-by-state compliance maze.

The concept sits between [[FederalAIPreemption]] and [[StateAIRegulationPatchwork]]. It supports a lighter national framework, but it does not eliminate every state role: the source still leaves room for child safety, data-center permitting, and similar local concerns. Its sharper contrast is with a precautionary regulatory model associated in the source with the [[EuropeanUnionAIAct|EU AI Act]].

## Key Claims
- Innovation policy can affect global AI competition as directly as chips or model quality.
- Startups are especially exposed to fragmented state compliance because they have less legal and regulatory staff than large incumbents.
- A federal framework can protect national scale while still leaving specific state and local domains unresolved.
- Permissionless innovation is a competitive claim, not a complete governance system; it still needs boundaries around safety, procurement, speech, infrastructure, and ratepayer costs.
