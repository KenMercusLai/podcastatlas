# Partition Minority Trap

Partition minority trap is the source's pattern where partition avoids one civil-war risk by creating a new state with an embedded hostile-minority problem. In [[697-the-troubles-murder-in-belfast-part-1-glt4877520736]], [[NorthernIreland]] is designed around a two-thirds Protestant majority, but that design also leaves roughly one-third of the population Catholic inside a state many Catholics experience as unsafe or closed.

The trap works in both directions. Many Catholics read the state through early violence, job expulsions, housing exclusion, policing powers, and [[InstitutionalSectarianDiscrimination]]. Many Protestants read the same settlement through [[UnionistSiegeMentality]], seeing themselves as a local majority but an island-wide minority threatened by [[Ireland]], Catholic birth rates, and internal fifth-column fears.

## Key Claims
- Partition can settle a constitutional dispute at one scale while preserving a legitimacy dispute at another.
- A built-in minority becomes more dangerous when administrative systems, security institutions, and political boundaries repeatedly signal exclusion.
- Majority security inside the new unit does not erase minority fear at the larger regional scale.
