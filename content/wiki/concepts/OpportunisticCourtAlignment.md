## Definition

[[OpportunisticCourtAlignment|投机型朝堂站队 / opportunistic court alignment]] is the pattern where a person changes praise, accusation, advice, or factional attachment according to perceived advantage rather than principle, evidence, or role boundaries. Hanji 478 creates the concept through Yang Xing, Zhuge Feng, Zheng Peng, and Gong Yu around Han Yuandi's court; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-468-yan-duo-bi-shi-xiao-wangzhi-ruhe-yin-zui-chi-da-kui-losjg52ruc7ov6k3uvmxvhxlzvjw|Hanji 468]] backfills the earlier attachment stage in which Zheng Peng uses anti-Shi-Gao accusations to seek entry into Xiao Wangzhi's circle.

## Current Synthesis

The concept differs from ordinary political disagreement. Its core marker is not that a person takes a side, but that the side-taking reveals unstable judgment: Yang Xing over-reads imperial intention and attacks Zhou Kan; Zhuge Feng reverses from praise to denunciation after his own demotion; Zheng Peng first uses accusations as an entry ticket to Xiao Wangzhi's side, then turns rejection into factional accusation; Gong Yu gives safe thrift advice while avoiding the main problem of slanderers and favorites.

Hanji 478 also makes the superior part of the system. The host argues that Han Yuandi's own lack of firm principle creates the environment in which subordinates chase signals, hedge, and attack rivals. Opportunistic alignment is therefore both a character problem and an organizational ecology problem.

## Key Claims

- Opportunistic alignment appears when people read power signals before they read evidence or principle.
- Sudden reversal from praise to denunciation damages credibility even if some criticism later sounds plausible.
- Private resentment becomes especially dangerous when a faction can turn it into formal accusation.
- The first opportunistic move can be attractive to principled actors when it seems to attack a shared enemy.
- Harmless correct advice can still be evasive if it avoids the decisive institutional problem.
- Superiors who lack stable standards encourage signal-chasing and factional gambling below them.
- The workplace translation is bounded: it warns against unprincipled advancement behavior, not against all political judgment or all alliances.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept is source-scoped to moralized podcast readings of Western Han court episodes. A changed judgment is not automatically opportunism; new evidence can justify reversal. Likewise, tactical silence may be necessary under coercive conditions. The warning is strongest when a person repeatedly subordinates evidence, role boundaries, or the decisive problem to personal advancement, and when patrons reward the move because it temporarily serves their faction.
