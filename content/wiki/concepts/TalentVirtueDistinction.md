# Talent-Virtue Distinction / 才德之分

Talent-virtue distinction / 才德之分 is the personnel-judgment frame [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-04-yurang-wei-zhiyao-fuchou-ln34tydha6iqwlrgmw7iugqfgfik]] draws from [[SimaGuang|司马光]]'s explanation of [[ZhiYao|智瑶]]. The source divides people into four types: saintly people with both talent and virtue, foolish people with neither, gentlemen whose virtue exceeds talent, and lesser people whose talent exceeds virtue.

The episode's practical rule is severe: if a ruler cannot find a saint or gentleman, Sima Guang would rather use a fool than a talented immoral person. The source's reasoning is that incapacity limits damage, while talent gives bad character more tools.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-06-weiwenhou-xuan-shui-dang-zaixiang-ne-1-lk-6ejgmjhvukqgtlz2lowu8qx-v]] tests that severe rule through [[WuQi|吴起]]. The host contrasts what [[SimaGuang|司马光]] might say under the talent-virtue frame with [[LiKe|李克]]'s advice to [[WeiWenhou|魏文侯]]: Wu Qi is morally suspect, yet his military ability may still be worth using under a role-specific [[UseStrengthsOverFaults]] standard.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-52-chenzhuang-panqin-duanqi-wan-chifan-fangxia-wan-ma-niang-ls13ixmbhflepjgpelxgu6idjqa2]] applies the distinction inside Yan's ruler sequence. [[YanWangKuai|燕王哙]] mistakes [[ZiZhi|子之]] for a worthy person and helps ruin [[YanState|燕国]], while [[YanZhaowang|燕昭王]]'s later use of [[LeYi|乐毅]] and [[JuXin|剧辛]] shows how better personnel judgment can support recovery after defeat.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-47-yanwangkuai-de-lixiangzhuyi-lhgl6rqkv9nonzxc9xqquzzlz5pr]] gives the failed judgment before the aftermath. Yan Wang Kuai is personally decent but politically naive; Zi Zhi is close enough to power to look like a worthy minister but uses [[SuDai|苏代]] and [[LuMaoshou|鹿毛寿]]'s persuasion chain to capture appointment authority. The source therefore separates a ruler's personal virtue from the practical capacity to judge another person's virtue and role-fitness.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-56-qiaoqiao-liangqian-duo-nianqian-sha-shi-da-zhangfu-ltokbutqgsnrno-y29xmzwa3da41]] extends the distinction from officeholding into persuasive talent. [[YangXiong|扬雄]]'s [[Fayan|《法言》]] critique does not deny that [[ZhangYiStrategist|张仪]] and [[SuQin|苏秦]] are rare talents; it denies that talent, novelty, or effectiveness makes their [[ZonghengjiaDiplomacy|纵横家外交]] morally acceptable. The episode's contrast with [[Zigong|子贡 / 端木赐]] turns this into [[MotiveBasedTalentEvaluation|动机导向的才干评价]]: the question is what and whom the talent serves.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-63-degao-wangzhong-mengchangjun-shi-weijunzi-lmd0owe3rq-faponbwzhkaigprin]] applies a related distinction to patronage quality. [[Mengchangjun|孟尝君 / 田文]]'s retainers are capable enough to save him through theft and rooster imitation, but [[WangAnshi|王安石]]'s critique asks whether such ability counts as the kind of talent a ruler or minister should seek. The source therefore adds [[JimingGoudaoTalentCritique|鸡鸣狗盗式人才批评]]: ability must be ranked by governing level and public consequence, not just by usefulness in a crisis.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-154-diangu-chenping-daosao-lingyou-yinqing-li-c6ezwfpejcvbrpzojg-xv2ge8|Hanji 154]] adds a wartime reversal through [[WeiWuzhi|魏无知]] defending [[ChenPing|陈平]] to [[LiuBang|刘邦]]. Wei Wuzhi does not say virtue is irrelevant in all offices; he says Liu Bang's immediate question is whether Chen Ping's talent can help win the Chu-Han struggle. The episode therefore qualifies the older severe rule with a role-and-crisis lens: personal virtue concerns remain visible, but they cannot automatically overrule scarce useful ability.

This concept sits beside the wiki's existing talent-use branch. [[UseStrengthsOverFaults]] asks rulers not to waste useful ability over lesser blemishes, while talent-virtue distinction asks when a flaw is not a blemish but a moral direction that can turn ability into danger.

## Key Claims
- Talent is an amplifier, not an independent good.
- Moral direction matters before capability when the role controls people, military force, or state resources.
- The attractive quality of talent can make rulers overlook virtue because ability is easier to like than moral seriousness is to respect.
- The distinction is source-scoped and normative; it records the episode's Sima Guang reading rather than a neutral modern hiring model.
- Zhouji 52 adds that ruler error can be a false-positive talent judgment: treating the wrong person as worthy may damage the state before better recruitment can repair it.
- Zhouji 47 adds that even a personally benevolent ruler can fail the talent-virtue test if he cannot evaluate whether trust, office, and succession authority belong together.
- Zhouji 56 adds a motive test: a person can be talented, persistent, and historically consequential while still failing moral approval if the skill serves wealth, rank, or deceptive private advantage.
- Zhouji 63 adds a level test: a skill can be useful and still too low-level to count as statecraft talent.
- Hanji 154 adds a crisis-use test: in wartime, a recommender may defend talent by asking whether the ability changes the strategic situation, not whether the person satisfies an ideal virtue profile.
