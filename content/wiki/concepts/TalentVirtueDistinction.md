# Talent-Virtue Distinction / 才德之分

Talent-virtue distinction / 才德之分 is the personnel-judgment frame [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-04-yurang-wei-zhiyao-fuchou-ln34tydha6iqwlrgmw7iugqfgfik]] draws from [[SimaGuang|司马光]]'s explanation of [[ZhiYao|智瑶]]. The source divides people into four types: saintly people with both talent and virtue, foolish people with neither, gentlemen whose virtue exceeds talent, and lesser people whose talent exceeds virtue.

The episode's practical rule is severe: if a ruler cannot find a saint or gentleman, Sima Guang would rather use a fool than a talented immoral person. The source's reasoning is that incapacity limits damage, while talent gives bad character more tools.

This concept sits beside the wiki's existing talent-use branch. [[UseStrengthsOverFaults]] asks rulers not to waste useful ability over lesser blemishes, while talent-virtue distinction asks when a flaw is not a blemish but a moral direction that can turn ability into danger.

## Key Claims
- Talent is an amplifier, not an independent good.
- Moral direction matters before capability when the role controls people, military force, or state resources.
- The attractive quality of talent can make rulers overlook virtue because ability is easier to like than moral seriousness is to respect.
- The distinction is source-scoped and normative; it records the episode's Sima Guang reading rather than a neutral modern hiring model.
