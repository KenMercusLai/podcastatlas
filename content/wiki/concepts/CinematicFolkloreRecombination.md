## Definition

Cinematic folklore recombination is the construction of a coherent screen world by selecting motifs from folk belief, ritual, literature, costume, performance, and prior films, then changing or inventing rules for dramatic use.

## Current Synthesis

The concept separates cultural source material from ethnographic accuracy. In [[MrVampire1985|《僵尸先生》]], geomancy, incense, talismans, ink line, blood, rice, peach wood, breath, costume, and corpse lore do not reproduce one complete historical system. They become a modular screen grammar whose credibility arises from setup, visible consequence, repetition, and character confidence.

Recombination does not make every motif arbitrary. Earlier stories, practices, and symbols can supply recognition and resonance, while film craft determines which meanings become operational. Evidence must therefore track at least three levels separately: attested cultural antecedent, production or author claim, and the rule actually dramatized on screen.

## Key Claims

- A convincing fictional ritual system can draw from several incompatible or incomplete traditions.
- Cultural antecedence does not prove faithful representation or sole origin.
- Visible function and repeated consequence make screen rules feel systematic.
- Production invention can later be remembered as inherited folklore.
- Costume conventions can become “traditional” through repetition across films.
- Historical, religious, literary, production, and reception evidence should not be collapsed into one origin story.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The bounded episode does not independently date the cited folk motifs or identify a complete textual transmission chain. Recombination should not be used to deny real ritual continuity merely because a film transforms it; nor should a plausible antecedent be treated as proof of production influence without direct evidence.
