# Passport Mobility Inequality

Passport mobility inequality is the difference between experiencing borders as minor formalities and experiencing them as paperwork, permission, uncertainty, cost, and surveillance. [[139-cong-zuo-ri-de-shi-jie-dao-ru-jin-de-ou-zhou-mei-hao-nian-dai-belle-epoque-ta-hai-neng-hui-lai-ma-ltvsgov8ht9-l7yp9qamdgjmyrf]] develops the concept through [[TheWorldOfYesterday|《昨日的世界》]]'s permit complaints and [[FeiQizhulou|费]]'s contemporary experience with [[SchengenArea|Schengen]] visas.

The source makes mobility identity concrete. European passport holders may feel Europe as an everyday travel space, while non-European travelers can experience the same space through consulates, police offices, residence permits, visa duration, and proof requirements. The difference shapes who can inhabit [[EliteCosmopolitanMobility]] rather than merely admire it.

## Key Claims
- Mobility freedom is not only distance and money; it is document status.
- A long visa can feel like an achievement because it temporarily converts bureaucracy into practical freedom.
- World-citizen language hides large differences between citizens, residents, tourists, migrants, and refugees.
