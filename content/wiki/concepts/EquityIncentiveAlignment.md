## Definition
Equity incentive alignment is the use of ownership-linked upside to bring a worker's long-term incentives closer to a company's results. Alignment depends on what the worker can influence, understand, and eventually receive, not merely on issuing a grant.

## Current Synthesis
A core contributor doing hard-to-measure work may benefit from a long-term equity contract, but broad rank-and-file programs show that ownership need not be restricted to core startup hires. Visibility and trust matter: a surprise payment transfers value after the fact, whereas an intelligible stake can inform work before an exit. Dilution, vesting, liquidity, legal entity, and governance rights determine whether the supposed common interest survives; alternatives such as profit sharing may be safer before a founder delegates control.

## Key Claims
- Equity fits long-term risk sharing better than an opaque substitute for cash pay, particularly when work is difficult to measure directly.
- Broad worker ownership may reach rank-and-file employees, but communication and empathetic leadership condition whether it motivates rather than merely pays.
- Economic participation does not imply voting control, permanent eligibility, or an equal claim to the entire exit value.
- An uncertain exit, recapturable options, or disputed grantor relationship can turn a promised incentive into mistrust.
- When an operating partner is untested, role milestones or profit-linked rewards can offer upside without immediately transferring founder control.

## Evidence
- Contract fit: [[79-gewei-lingdao-danfan-zan-xuedian-boyilun-690004262]] uses [[ContractTheory]], [[MixedIncentiveContracts]], and the [[Alibaba]] example to discuss hard-to-measure work, willingness to bear risk, and why equity must not disguise low wages; its comparison to [[CEOPayLuckSkimming]] cautions against treating luck as effort.
- Communication and scope: [[a-pro-worker-experiment-in-private-equity]] contrasts [[PeteStavros]] and [[KKR]]'s [[CapitalSafety]] surprise worker payout with [[GSI]]'s advance notice and payments; the latter also had no voting rights and tied some money to staying. These cases extend [[WorkplaceIncentiveDesign]] beyond senior employees.
- Credibility of the grant: [[qiquan-zhe-zhang-bing-weishenme-yuelaiyue-nanchi-le-1]] describes [[EmployeeStockOptions]], [[EmployeeStockOptionLiquidityRisk]], delayed exit, option-pool recapture, and a particular [[VIEEmployeeEquityMismatch|domestic employment/offshore grant dispute]].
- Control-preserving alternatives: [[advice-line-strategy-sessions-4962a0d6-36bc-4797-8002-3bd8b748a89a]] records [[TonyDeRosa]] seeking partners for [[HearsayBrewingAndTheater]]; [[JeffreyHollender]] and [[GuyRaz]] suggest testing accountable operators and considering profit sharing or ownership-like incentives before giving away control, not reporting an implemented program.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The contract-theory episode's preference for core contributors is a contextual fit test, not a universal eligibility rule: KKR's program deliberately included ordinary workers. GSI's reported retention and payments are case evidence, not proof that issuing shares mechanically improves performance. Hearsay received advice rather than implementing a verified plan. The option-dispute account is a podcast's account of a contested case and should not be generalized into VIE law.

## What Changed
- Reconciled the core-contributor framework with the rank-and-file ownership counterexample.
- Separated incentive awareness, economic rights, and voting/control rights.
- Made enforceability and control-preserving alternatives explicit conditions of alignment.
