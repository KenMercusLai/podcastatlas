# Occupation Pacification Strategy / 占领区怀柔治理

Occupation pacification strategy / 占领区怀柔治理 is the post-conquest approach the wiki first saw in [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-72-shenme-yang-de-ren-cai-shi-zhongzhen-zhishi-logxegnqexgc7z-lodw6tr6-8f28]] through [[LeYi|乐毅]]'s discipline, tax relief, respect for worthies, and restoration of older policies in conquered [[QiState|齐国]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-78-qiguo-mingyun-jing-bei-chengguan-gei-jiule-lpt3reyr6ok8g2lhcxrnd5qctyug]] makes the military logic more explicit. Le Yi does not simply storm [[JuWarringStates|莒城]] and [[JimoWarringStates|即墨]], because forcing the last cities may create heavy casualties, resentment, and fear across the already occupied seventy-plus Qi cities. He instead retreats the camp, allows civilians to leave, assists the hungry, and lets people return to old livelihoods.

The strategy belongs under [[PeopleBasedPoliticalSecurity|people-based political security]] and [[MencianPeopleBasedLegitimacy|Mencian people-based legitimacy]] without becoming a pure moral approval. The same occupation still contains coercion, shown earlier by [[WangZhu|王蠋]]'s lethal refusal. The concept names the governing calculation: conquest becomes durable only if the conquered population can imagine the occupier as tolerable.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-79-shishang-yong-gengniu-da-shengzhang-diyi-ren-lsrl0posblloqwugox160xsicp2p]] gives the collapse case after that strategy is interrupted. [[QiJie|骑劫]] follows Tian Dan's bait by cutting prisoners' noses and digging graves, creating [[AtrocityBackfireMobilization|atrocity backfire mobilization]] rather than submission. The point is not only that Qi Jie loses a battle; he makes Yan rule feel intolerable, so captured cities are ready to defect after the [[FireOxNightRaid|fire-ox night raid]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-99-2-mingming-yilian-baixiang-de-caize-ping-shenme-baixiang-lhfyb6hzcqkpbzdrwsxajb7hhmhe]] adds a cultural-governance version through [[Chunshenjun|黄歇 / 春申君]]'s appointment of [[Xunzi|荀子]] to [[LanlingWarringStates|兰陵 / Lanling]]. The source frames [[LuState|鲁国]]'s old territory as militarily conquerable but culturally persistent. Putting a major Confucian scholar in charge of a large, strategic county gives [[ChuState|楚国]] a way to make rule feel more legitimate to local elites than occupation by force alone.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-99-4-shui-shi-kongzi-hou-zui-weidade-sixiangjia-lr4phoq9chc4tz0yb8y95gy21qdd]] returns to the same cultural-governance version after Lu's sharper collapse. Chu occupies Lu and exiles its ruler, while Xunzi's second Lanling tenure is credited with prosperity through scholar-administration. The source therefore makes occupation pacification span both active conquest and the quieter problem of making absorbed territory governable after the old state's formal survival becomes nominal.

## Key Claims
- A conquered territory can be militarily taken but politically unsecured.
- Harsh assault on the last holdouts can destabilize already captured places.
- Relief, non-plunder, tax reduction, and livelihood restoration are tools of control as well as humane policy.
- Pacification strategy depends heavily on commander continuity and ruler trust.
- Pacification can be undone quickly when a successor commander substitutes humiliation and terror for tolerable control.
- Cultural authority can be a pacification tool when newly occupied territory has a strong inherited scholarly or ritual identity.
- Qinji 99 part 4 adds that this cultural pacification can continue after the conquered state's formal survival has become only nominal.
