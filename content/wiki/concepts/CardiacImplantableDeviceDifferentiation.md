# Cardiac Implantable Device Differentiation / 心脏植入装置功能区分

## Definition
Cardiac implantable device differentiation / 心脏植入装置功能区分 is the practice of matching a cardiac problem to the function of a pacemaker, defibrillator, resynchronization system, monitor, or pump-support device instead of treating all implanted heart technology as interchangeable.

## Current Synthesis
[[vol-152-nazha-wo-ming-you-wo-bu-you-tian-xianshi-zhong-de-gaiming-shenqi-ni-zhidao-ma-zhen-neng-yufang-cusi-ma-lvt24r7x6bppx7e5gqhas1yn-jru]] uses a house analogy to separate vascular “pipes,” valves as “doors,” myocardium as structure, and electrical activity as wiring. The analogy supports a functional map: pacemakers address selected slow-rate or conduction problems; [[ImplantableCardioverterDefibrillator|ICDs]] monitor and treat selected malignant ventricular tachyarrhythmias; CRT coordinates ventricular contraction in selected heart-failure patterns; implantable monitors observe without providing the same therapy; and ventricular-assist devices support failing pump output.

The episode also distinguishes implanted high-risk protection from [[CPRAEDResponseBoundary|AED-supported public rescue]]. These systems may overlap in electrical concepts, but their users, timing, invasiveness, and functions differ.

VOL.66 reinforces the electrical-versus-pump distinction from a cardiac-surgery perspective. A pacemaker supplies timing or conduction support through implanted leads and a subcutaneous generator, while a ventricular-assist device adds mechanical pumping to a failing native heart and a total artificial heart replaces more of the pump function. External power or controller needs, pulse characteristics, daily restrictions, complication burden, and expected role differ accordingly.

## Key Claims
- Device choice should start with the failed physiological function, not with the generic idea of “helping the heart.”
- Conventional pacemakers principally support rates or conduction that are too slow or unreliable.
- ICDs add detection and treatment of selected dangerous fast ventricular rhythms and may also provide pacing.
- CRT and CRT-D add resynchronization for selected heart-failure patients; CRT-D also incorporates defibrillation capability.
- Implantable cardiac monitors provide long-duration observation but are not equivalent to pacemakers or defibrillators.
- Ventricular-assist or “artificial heart” support addresses advanced pump failure rather than merely resetting an electrical rhythm; it may serve as a transplant bridge and can produce flow characteristics unlike a normal palpable pulse.
- Leadless, transvenous, subcutaneous, and extravascular designs trade off vascular access, pacing capability, anatomy, and therapeutic scope.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The simplified map supports public understanding but does not capture every device type, combination, indication, contraindication, sensing or pacing mode, implantation route, or complication. “Artificial heart” is used colloquially in the sources and should not erase distinctions among ventricular-assist devices, total artificial hearts, transplant bridging, and destination therapy. Survival, pulse, battery, electromagnetic-interference, air-travel, and external-controller statements are device- and patient-specific rather than universal rules.
