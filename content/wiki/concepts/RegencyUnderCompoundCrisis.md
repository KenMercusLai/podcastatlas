## Definition

Regency Under Compound Crisis / 复合危机下的摄政 is a governance pattern where a regent is judged not by a single succession or family-power question, but by simultaneous child accession, education, justice, appointment fit, coup risk, disaster frequency, omen pressure, frontier abandonment, conscription backlash, war, fiscal stress, rebellion, court legitimacy, family power, institutional accountability mismatch, and power-transfer pressure.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 779 adds the missing 106-107 CE administrative layer at the front of the Deng-regency picture. [[FanZhunLateHan|Fan Zhun]] pushes scholar recruitment and imperial education repair; [[LuGongLateHan|Lu Gong]] corrects judicial timing so farming and capital-case review are not damaged by rigid procedure; and [[RenShangLateHan|Ren Shang]]'s harsh Western Regions governance shows how a wrong appointment can turn inherited frontier leverage into rebellion and withdrawal. This means the compound crisis begins not only with disaster and succession, but with whether the court can choose the right people for education, justice, and frontier offices.

Hanji 780 then broadens the pre-succession environment. The court opens under an Eastern Han disaster-frequency frame, gives up the Western Regions after [[BanChaoEasternHan|Ban Chao]]'s earlier achievement, faces Qiang unrest produced by official abuse and distant-service fear, and answers with amnesty, dismissals, and austerity. The same source makes the institutional environment explicit through [[ZhongchangTongLateHan|Zhongchang Tong]]: a regent operates inside a court where real power can move through Shangshu and inner-court channels while formal senior offices carry public blame.

Hanji 781 establishes the succession-security layer. The court moves from Emperor He's death to [[EmperorShangOfHan|Emperor Shang]]'s infant accession, then to [[EmperorAnOfHan|Emperor An]]'s prepared installation after Emperor Shang dies; immediately afterward, [[ZhouZhangLateHan|Zhou Zhang]]'s failed coup tests the regency's coercive boundary. The same layer already contains refugees, earthquakes, floods, drought, early Qiang fighting, Xianbei incorporation, Fan Zhun's relief memorial, and disaster-omen redirection.

Hanji 782-786 make the crisis fiscal, logistical, and local. Dianling's Qiang coalition, Ren Shang's Pingxiang defeat, Deng Zhi's recall but continued favor, Pang Can's retrenchment memorial, extreme grain prices, Luoyang famine, rank sale, salary cuts, Zhang Bailu's rebellion, Southern Xiongnu/Wuhuan/Xianbei pressure, Fa Xiong's amnesty timing, Ban Zhao's mourning advice, forced relocation, fresh-food restraint, Hanyang disorder, and frontier official abuse all coexist. The point is not that every decision is bad; it is that each repair occurs while other systems are deteriorating.

Hanji 788-793 add the mature pressure field. Deng appoints Yu Xu to Wudu and accepts tactical advice, but Qiang war cost, forced relocation, Deng-family reward politics, Western Regions underimplementation, Deng Kang's internal warning, and Deng's death leave her legacy unstable. The concept therefore evaluates regency as an operating environment: the same ruler can be austere, competent, and damaging when crisis layers interact faster than institutions can absorb them.

## Key Claims

- Compound-crisis regency is multi-axis: education, justice, appointment fit, inherited disaster pressure, child succession, coup risk, frontier abandonment, conscription backlash, war, fiscal depletion, family power, omen pressure, institutional authority mismatch, and transfer timing interact rather than appearing in sequence.
- The earliest layer now includes personnel fit: Fan Zhun and Lu Gong show repair through the right adviser, while Ren Shang shows frontier collapse through a misfit appointment.
- Succession security remains central because Deng's preparation of Liu Hu, Zhou Zhang's failed coup, and later transfer-power pressure precede the more visible fiscal and frontier layers.
- Fiscal emergency remains central because rank sale, salary pressure, grain transport, and war logistics appear before later moral and institutional judgments.
- Austerity and ritual restraint can signal seriousness but cannot by themselves solve revenue collapse, frontier military adaptation, or civilian relocation damage.
- Capable appointments such as Fan Zhun, Lu Gong, Yu Xu, and selective acceptance of Pang Can or Ban Yong show real state capacity inside the crisis, not simple collapse.
- Kin reliance and inner-court access are double-edged: trusted actors supply capacity, but they also intensify legitimacy risk when authority, patronage, and accountability separate.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept should not become a blanket condemnation of regency. Hanji 779-793 preserve meaningful competence: education repair, judicial timing reform, austerity, amnesty, succession preparation, relief, seasonal restraint, appointments, tactical adaptation, prison review, refugee reassurance, and consultation all matter. The stronger claim is narrower: compound crisis makes each successful repair insufficient unless disaster, succession, fiscal, frontier, family-power, omen, authority-accountability, and transfer constraints are also managed.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 779 as the 106-107 CE administrative and appointment-fit layer before the broader Hanji 780 disaster-frontier prelude.
- Added Fan Zhun, Lu Gong, Ren Shang, Liang Jin, Duan Xi, and Qiuci to the concept's evidence.
- Reframed compound crisis as beginning with personnel fit in education, justice, and frontier governance, not only with succession and disaster pressure.
