## Definition

Regency under compound crisis is a governance pattern in which a non-ruling emperor's regent must be judged through simultaneous disaster, frontier war, fiscal strain, public legitimacy pressure, internal kin risk, court information control, and eventual transfer of power.

## Current Synthesis

The current evidence centers on [[EmpressDowagerDengLateHan|Empress Dowager Deng]] across the 116-121 CE transition. Hanji 789 now makes the crisis visible earlier than the prior 118-121 frame: the regency faces Qiang war, forced relocation backfire, Dingxi failure, Xianbei pressure, Yizhou unrest, Yuan Chang's death after a court-secret leak, and Liu Kai's mourning-policy intervention. This layer shows the regency making both coercive frontier choices and institutional ritual choices under stress.

Hanji 790 adds multi-front pressure, large Qiang-war expenditure, Deng-family military reward through [[DengZunLateHan|Deng Zun]], [[RenShangLateHan|Ren Shang]]'s fatal credit dispute, Deng-family education policy, and Tang Tan's refusal to present an auspicious omen while outer relatives are strong and the ruler is weak. Hanji 792 then brings the internal family warning: [[DengKangLateHan|Deng Kang]] argues that court prestige should rise and outer-relative power should weaken, but Deng removes him instead of treating the warning as transfer-risk repair.

Hanji 793 makes the retrospective judgment mixed rather than binary. Deng is credited with austerity, relief, frugality, education, ritual repair, pardons, tax and labor reduction, old palace women's release, and Jiangnan development measures. The same source treats the Western Regions and Qiang problem as the major qualification because long war, office sales, treasury exhaustion, lost territory, and later repair by Ban Yong show frontier failure inside otherwise serious domestic crisis governance.

## Key Claims

- A regency's legitimacy is judged by crisis performance as well as formal authority, gender, or kinship position.
- Frontier, fiscal, disaster, ritual, and personnel pressures compound each other rather than remaining separate policy boxes.
- Personal austerity has political weight only when connected to relief, fiscal restraint, and administrative measures.
- Internal warning from the regent's own family can reveal transfer risk before the regency formally ends.
- A regency can be domestically serious and still suffer major legitimacy loss from frontier failure, coerced population policy, and delayed power transfer.

## Evidence

- Early compound stress: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-789-shanliang-meiyou-dixian-ni-jiang-yiwen-buzhi-ljv5hgmd0vxoa-teckj2zkigvcoi|Hanji 789]] combines Qiang war, forced relocation, Xianbei raids, Yizhou unrest, Yuan Chang's death, and Liu Kai's mourning policy in the 116-117 CE regency field.
- Frontier and family pressure: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-790-lishi-gaosu-ni-ruhe-he-lingdao-da-jiaodao-lulf0r1sdkod3luzwru0-kkrijxk|Hanji 790]] combines disasters, Qiang-war cost, Deng Zun's reward, Ren Shang's execution, Deng-family education, and Tang Tan's omen refusal.
- Internal kin warning: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-792-dengkang-renu-taihou-dagou-weishenme-yao-kan-zhuren-lh3xkjneqfd9sqijqo3rgzsa5vwn|Hanji 792]] has Deng Kang warn against outer-relative overreach before Deng punishes him.
- Death and retrospective evaluation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-793-lishi-zhuming-nvhuang-dengsui-zenme-si-de-lszfgj7waiuusrddzjmv5mbrp1i|Hanji 793]] gives Deng's illness and death dates and evaluates her regency through relief measures, austerity, public criticism, and frontier failure.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept should not reduce Deng's regency to failure. The sources preserve real relief, austerity, education, ritual, and administrative work. The qualification is that crisis governance must also be priced through coercive frontier implementation, family-power concentration, transfer risk, and the information channels that make criticism or warnings actionable.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 789 as the earlier 116-117 CE layer of Deng-regency compound crisis.
- Expanded the concept beyond disaster and frontier pressure to include coercive relocation, court-secret leakage, and mourning-policy repair.
