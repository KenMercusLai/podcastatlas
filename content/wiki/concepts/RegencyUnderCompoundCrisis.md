## Definition

Regency under compound crisis is a governance pattern in which a non-ruling emperor's regent must be judged through simultaneous disaster, frontier war, fiscal strain, official-pay incentives, family-power management, public legitimacy pressure, court information control, local rebellion, and eventual transfer of power.

## Current Synthesis

The current evidence centers on [[EmpressDowagerDengLateHan|Empress Dowager Deng]] across the pre-121 CE transition, with Hanji 783 now extending the earliest layer. In that source, the regency field already contains [[ZhangBailuLateHan|Zhang Bailu / Zhang Bolu]]'s surrender-and-release rebellion cycle, Southern Xiongnu, Wuhuan, and Xianbei pressure, earthquakes, celestial anomalies, floods, severe famine, court ritual austerity, salary cuts, Ren Shang's burdensome campaign withdrawal, new commandant posts near Chang'an and Yong, and the setup for the Liangzhou abandonment debate. It therefore makes [[OfficialSalaryExtractionRisk|official salary extraction risk]] part of the crisis field rather than a separate management aside.

Hanji 784 then continues that earliest layer through a court debate over abandoning [[LiangzhouLateHan|Liangzhou]], [[YuXuEasternHan|Yu Xu]]'s warning that Sanfu, Chang'an, and imperial tombs would lose their shield, a hostage-like elite-control proposal for Liangzhou sons, a dangerous [[ZhaogeCountyLateHan|Zhaoge]] rebellion assignment, an early [[SouthernXiongnu|Southern Xiongnu]] surrender, and renewed [[XianlingQiang|Xianling Qiang]] pressure at Baozhong.

Hanji 785 continues the early layer in 110-111 CE: the regency faces fires, earthquakes, floods, locusts, [[ZhangBailuLateHan|Zhang Bailu]]'s coastal rebellion, Xianling Qiang pressure, destructive forced relocation, and the death of Deng's mother while [[DengFamilyAnEraLateHan|Deng-family]] officeholding must be publicly managed. [[BanZhaoLateHan|Ban Zhao]] advises accepting the Deng brothers' mourning withdrawal as a non-monopoly signal, while Deng later preserves consultation through fengchaoqing. [[FaXiongLateHan|Fa Xiong]] adds a local pacification branch, showing that amnesty timing and command synchronization also belong inside the regency's crisis field.

Hanji 786 then extends the early layer through 110-114 CE: pirates are eventually suppressed in Donglai, Hanyang/Shanggui rebellion connects to Qiang forces, Qiang-war cost accumulates, locusts, drought, landslide, and office turnover continue, and a 112 CE edict restrains fire-warmed and dug-sprouting off-season fresh foods for tomb, temple, and palace offerings. This layer makes [[SeasonalRitualConsumptionRestraint|seasonal ritual consumption restraint]] part of crisis governance rather than a side anecdote.

Hanji 788 then makes the crisis visible through appointment and honor management before the better-developed forced-relocation and transfer-risk layers: Deng recognizes Yu Xu as a commander and sends him to [[WuduCommanderyLateHan|Wudu]], the court accepts his light-cavalry advice through [[RenShangLateHan|Ren Shang]], and the same source records earthquakes, Wuling unrest, office turnover, and restrained handling of [[DengHongLateHan|Deng Hong]]'s posthumous honors.

Hanji 789 shows the same regency under sharper Qiang pressure: forced relocation backfires, Dingxi operations falter, Xianbei and Yizhou add pressure, Yuan Chang dies after a court-secret leak, and Liu Kai wins a mourning-policy repair. Hanji 790 adds multi-front pressure, large Qiang-war expenditure, Deng-family military reward through [[DengZunLateHan|Deng Zun]], Ren Shang's fatal credit dispute, Deng-family education policy, and Tang Tan's refusal to present an auspicious omen while outer relatives are strong and the ruler is weak.

Hanji 792 brings the internal family warning: [[DengKangLateHan|Deng Kang]] argues that court prestige should rise and outer-relative power should weaken, but Deng removes him instead of treating the warning as transfer-risk repair. Hanji 793 makes the retrospective judgment mixed rather than binary. Deng is credited with austerity, relief, frugality, education, ritual repair, pardons, tax and labor reduction, old palace women's release, and Jiangnan development measures, while Western Regions and Qiang failures remain the major qualification.

## Key Claims

- A regency's legitimacy is judged by crisis performance as well as formal authority, gender, or kinship position.
- Frontier, fiscal, salary-incentive, disaster, rebellion, ritual-consumption, personnel, mourning, family-honor, and local-pacification pressures compound each other rather than remaining separate policy boxes.
- Personal austerity and restraint matter only when priced beside policy outcomes, including relief, appointments, frontier costs, and family-power concentration.
- Capable appointments, amnesty pacing, frontier-retention advice, ritual restraint, and visible austerity can coexist with policy failure; Yu Xu's Zhaoge and Wudu successes do not erase salary-cut incentive damage, Qiang-war cost, forced-relocation backfire, or Western Regions underimplementation.
- Internal warning from the regent's own family can reveal transfer risk before the regency formally ends.
- A regency can be domestically serious and still suffer major legitimacy loss from frontier failure, coerced population policy, and delayed power transfer.

- Seasonal and continued crisis layer: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-786-guren-weishenme-paichi-fanji-shuiguo-lmcwqwdxwe0ehuusfeoaqk82msm7|Hanji 786]] combines the seasonal fresh-food edict, locusts, drought, landslide, Liu Kai's office appointment, Hanyang/Shanggui rebellion, Anding Qiang operations, Donglai suppression, and Han-Qiang war cost.

- Early compound stress: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-789-shanliang-meiyou-dixian-ni-jiang-yiwen-buzhi-ljv5hgmd0vxoa-teckj2zkigvcoi|Hanji 789]] combines Qiang war, forced relocation, Xianbei raids, Yizhou unrest, Yuan Chang's death, and Liu Kai's mourning policy in the 116-117 CE regency field.
- Frontier and family pressure: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-790-lishi-gaosu-ni-ruhe-he-lingdao-da-jiaodao-lulf0r1sdkod3luzwru0-kkrijxk|Hanji 790]] combines disasters, Qiang-war cost, Deng Zun's reward, Ren Shang's execution, Deng-family education, and Tang Tan's omen refusal.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept should not reduce Deng's regency to failure. The sources preserve real relief, austerity, education, ritual restraint, appointment, amnesty-pacing judgment, local pacification success, and administrative work, and Hanji 788 adds a clear tactical success through Yu Xu. The qualification is that crisis governance must also be priced through salary-cut incentives, coercive frontier implementation, war cost, family-power concentration, command synchronization failure, transfer risk, and the information channels that make criticism or warnings actionable. Hanji 783-784's precise campaign dating and some minor actor names remain source-scoped pending follow-up evidence.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 783 as the earliest current layer for Zhang Bailu's initial rebellion cycle, multi-front pressure, disasters, court austerity, salary cuts, Ren Shang's campaign drawdown, and the Liangzhou abandonment prelude.
- Repositioned Hanji 784 as the continuation layer for Liangzhou abandonment debate, Yu Xu's elite-control proposal, Zhaoge pacification, Southern Xiongnu surrender, and Baozhong Qiang pressure.
- Repositioned Hanji 785 and Hanji 786 as continuation layers rather than the start of the current Deng-regency evidence.
- Expanded the concept to include strategic frontier-retention debate and local information-led pacification inside compound crisis governance.
- Preserved Hanji 789's coercive relocation and Hanji 792's rejected internal warning as major constraints on the positive Deng-regency record.
