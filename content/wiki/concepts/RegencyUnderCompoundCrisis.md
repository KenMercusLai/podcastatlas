## Definition
Regency under compound crisis is a governance pattern in which a non-ruling emperor's regent must be judged through simultaneous disaster, frontier war, fiscal strain, public legitimacy pressure, and the eventual risk of transferring power.

## Current Synthesis
The current evidence comes from [[EmpressDowagerDengLateHan|Empress Dowager Deng]] across the 120-121 CE transition. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-792-dengkang-renu-taihou-dagou-weishenme-yao-kan-zhuren-lh3xkjneqfd9sqijqo3rgzsa5vwn|Hanji 792]] shows the regency before death through Qiang-front pressure, flood and earthquake notices, office turnover, Liu Bao's crown-prince appointment, and [[DengKangLateHan|Deng Kang]]'s internal warning against Deng outer-relative overreach. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-793-lishi-zhuming-nvhuang-dengsui-zenme-si-de-lszfgj7waiuusrddzjmv5mbrp1i|Hanji 793]] then does not give a specific medical cause for Deng's death, but turns her death into a retrospective assessment of regency under pressure: floods, drought, frontier attacks, banditry, fiscal depletion, and gendered criticism make her record harder to reduce to either virtue or usurpation.

The synthesis is mixed. Deng is credited with austerity, relief, frugality, education and ritual repair, pardons, reduced labor and taxes, release of old palace women, and Jiangnan development measures. The same source treats the Western Regions and Qiang problem as the largest qualification because long war, office sales, treasury exhaustion, lost territory, and later repair by [[BanYongLateHan|Ban Yong]] show that crisis governance can succeed domestically while failing badly at the frontier.

## Key Claims
- A regency's legitimacy is judged by crisis performance as well as formal authority, gender, or kinship position.
- Personal austerity has political weight only when it is connected to relief, fiscal restraint, and administrative measures.
- Long-running disasters and frontier pressure amplify public criticism and make delayed transfer of power more dangerous.
- Internal warning from a regent's own family can reveal transfer risk before the regency formally ends.
- Frontier and fiscal failure can qualify a regent's otherwise successful domestic crisis record.
- A regent's death converts crisis-performance judgment into succession and purge risk if transfer arrangements remain unsettled.

## Evidence
- Pre-death crisis field: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-792-dengkang-renu-taihou-dagou-weishenme-yao-kan-zhuren-lh3xkjneqfd9sqijqo3rgzsa5vwn|Hanji 792]] places Deng's court amid Qiang-front fighting, floods, earthquakes, a solar eclipse, office turnover, and Liu Bao's crown-prince appointment before her death.
- Internal transfer warning: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-792-dengkang-renu-taihou-dagou-weishenme-yao-kan-zhuren-lh3xkjneqfd9sqijqo3rgzsa5vwn|Hanji 792]] says Deng Kang urges stronger court authority and weaker Deng outer-relative power, but Deng rejects him and later expels him.
- Compound pressure field: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-793-lishi-zhuming-nvhuang-dengsui-zenme-si-de-lszfgj7waiuusrddzjmv5mbrp1i|Hanji 793]] describes disasters, frontier war, banditry, fiscal strain, and criticism of a female ruler during Deng's long regency.
- Austerity and relief: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-793-lishi-zhuming-nvhuang-dengsui-zenme-si-de-lszfgj7waiuusrddzjmv5mbrp1i|Hanji 793]] says Deng reduced personal consumption, music, offerings, labor, taxes, and palace burdens while redirecting attention to people in distress.
- Institution and development claims: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-793-lishi-zhuming-nvhuang-dengsui-zenme-si-de-lszfgj7waiuusrddzjmv5mbrp1i|Hanji 793]] credits Deng with school repair, pardons, women released from palace service, and agricultural and migration measures in Jiangnan.
- Frontier qualification: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-793-lishi-zhuming-nvhuang-dengsui-zenme-si-de-lszfgj7waiuusrddzjmv5mbrp1i|Hanji 793]] identifies the Western Regions and Qiang war as costly enough to empty the treasury, force office sales, lose territory, and require later repair.
- Transfer-risk after death: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-793-lishi-zhuming-nvhuang-dengsui-zenme-si-de-lszfgj7waiuusrddzjmv5mbrp1i|Hanji 793]] has Emperor An restore return-power remonstrants after Deng's death, while the following Hanji 794 layer turns replacement fear into anti-Deng purge politics.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This concept is currently grounded in one regency across two adjacent episodes. The source's positive evaluation of Deng is a host synthesis rather than an exhaustive institutional audit, and several specifics, including Jiangnan-development causality, exact disaster counts, Deng Kang's private motive, and the 120 CE disaster/tribute notices, remain source-scoped. The page also does not infer a precise cause of Deng's death beyond illness followed by death in 121 CE.

## What Changed
- Created the concept from Hanji 793's retrospective evaluation of Empress Dowager Deng's regency.
- Added Hanji 792 as the pre-death layer showing Deng-regency crisis pressure and internal family warning before the transfer rupture.
