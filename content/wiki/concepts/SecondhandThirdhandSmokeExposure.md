# Secondhand and Thirdhand Smoke Exposure / 二手烟与三手烟暴露

## Definition
Secondhand exposure occurs when other people inhale smoke or aerosol during use; thirdhand exposure concerns residues that remain on clothing, skin, furnishings, and indoor surfaces after active smoking has stopped.

## Current Synthesis
The current source expands tobacco control beyond "do not smoke directly beside someone." Electronic-cigarette aerosol can still affect shared air, outdoor smoke can reach people downwind, and residues can persist in homes, clothing, hotels, entertainment venues, and other enclosed spaces. Household behavior therefore matters even when a smoker moves to a balcony or waits until a child is absent.

## Key Claims
- Electronic-cigarette exhalate should not be assumed harmless to nearby people.
- Outdoor exposure depends on proximity, airflow, wind direction, and how long smoke remains around later passersby.
- Moving smoking to a balcony may reduce immediate exposure without eliminating residue carried back on clothing or surfaces.
- Smoke-free rooms and designated, ventilated smoking areas address different parts of the exposure pathway.
- Family exposure can reproduce smoking norms as well as physical exposure across generations.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not quantify dose, duration, residue chemistry, ventilation performance, or relative risk across environments. The distinction between secondhand aerosol and combustible smoke should not be flattened into identical composition or magnitude. Exposure and health effects remain context-dependent.

## What Changed
- Created a combined environmental-exposure frame spanning active smoke/aerosol and persistent residues.
- Added household and public-space behavior without asserting equivalent doses across settings.
