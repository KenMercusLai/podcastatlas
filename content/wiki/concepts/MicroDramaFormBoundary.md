## Definition
The micro-drama form boundary is the set of storytelling affordances and limits created by episodes measured in minutes: rapid information, immediate hooks, and dense payoff become central, while long preparation and thematic accumulation become harder.

## Current Synthesis
The episode treats micro-drama as an immature form rather than a temporary mistake. Early financialization, repeated dominance and revenge tropes, and weak professional self-respect can produce crude work, yet technical distribution change is irreversible and more capable creators may develop a richer grammar.

Unit length matters. Song compares micro-drama to a highlight reel and feature film to watching a complete match. The analogy captures a real difference in pacing and buildup without proving that short works cannot become aesthetically serious or that their audiences are simply failed film audiences.

## Key Claims
- Short units reward immediate legibility, strong hooks, and frequent payoff.
- The same density makes slow buildup and heavy thematic development more difficult.
- Early genre conventions should not be mistaken for permanent formal limits.
- Regulation and professional entry can change production quality without guaranteeing mature work.
- Copyright or revenue diversion from recaps is distinct from direct audience substitution.

## Evidence
- Unit-time constraint: [[lt24pcobvqnyxjeven2dwhuhvmfu-lt24pcobvqnyxjeven2dwhuhvmfu]] records Song's argument that one- or few-minute episodes cannot use the same preparation rhythm as films or long television.
- Immature grammar: [[lt24pcobvqnyxjeven2dwhuhvmfu-lt24pcobvqnyxjeven2dwhuhvmfu]] describes micro-drama as not yet having a mature syntax or system while citing more ambitious examples.
- Audience qualification: [[lt24pcobvqnyxjeven2dwhuhvmfu-lt24pcobvqnyxjeven2dwhuhvmfu]] separates short-form viewers, film viewers, and the economic harm of derivative recaps rather than assuming one-for-one substitution.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source offers practitioner judgment rather than reception research or a representative genre sample. Short duration does not by itself prevent thematic depth, and serialized accumulation can offset per-episode limits. Claims about market size, regulation, audience overlap, and long-term maturation remain source-scoped.
