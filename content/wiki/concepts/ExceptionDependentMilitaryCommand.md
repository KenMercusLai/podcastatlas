## Definition

例外才能依赖式治军 / Exception-dependent military command is a command pattern in which safety and performance rely heavily on one leader's unusual perception, judgment, reputation, or loyalty network rather than on routines that ordinary successors can execute. A method can work for the exceptional leader while remaining unsafe as a general rule.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-257-handai-dagangjing-jiujing-you-duogang-2-lnqd8g2sh79s1uywcbyn56n_vmmv|Hanji 257]] develops the pattern through [[LiGuangHanGeneral|李广]] and [[ChengBushiWesternHan|程不识]]. Li Guang minimizes formation control, camp construction, night signals, and paperwork while observing the surroundings closely and building unusually strong soldier loyalty. Cheng maintains ordered movement, camps, watches, and administration at a much higher burden. Both return without attack.

The concept does not say that flexible or person-centered command is false. Li Guang's reported outcome and loyalty are real within the source. The issue is transferability: if success depends on perception and charisma that cannot be specified, taught, audited, or inherited, copying the visible looseness without the hidden capability increases variance. Cheng's procedures are valuable as a floor rather than a ceiling. They reduce dependence on rare talent but may create fatigue, bureaucracy, and lower soldier preference.

## Key Claims

- A successful exceptional case does not automatically establish a safe general method.
- Visible simplicity may conceal a heavy dependence on tacit perception, judgment, and reputation.
- Procedures can lower outcome variance by carrying readiness across ordinary commanders and successors.
- Procedure-backed reliability can impose real morale, rest, and administrative costs.
- Leader popularity is operationally valuable but does not replace surprise readiness or succession design.
- Evaluating a command system requires separating its performance under the originator from its imitability and inheritance risk.

## Evidence

Exceptional personal control:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-257-handai-dagangjing-jiujing-you-duogang-2-lnqd8g2sh79s1uywcbyn56n_vmmv|Hanji 257]] says Li Guang relaxes ordinary formation, camp, signaling, and paperwork requirements while relying on close observation and retaining strong troop loyalty.

Procedure-backed floor:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-257-handai-dagangjing-jiujing-you-duogang-2-lnqd8g2sh79s1uywcbyn56n_vmmv|Hanji 257]] says Cheng Bushi uses ordered marches, camps, watches, and documents and is treated by Sima Guang as the safer model to imitate even without conspicuous achievement.

Transfer and spillover risk:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-257-handai-dagangjing-jiujing-you-duogang-2-lnqd8g2sh79s1uywcbyn56n_vmmv|Hanji 257]] argues that successors and peer commanders face difficulty when soldiers compare ordinary discipline with Li Guang's exceptional leniency.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The reported deployments do not expose either army to attack, so the source cannot directly demonstrate Cheng's superiority under surprise or Li Guang's collapse. The concept should not be used to equate all flexibility with negligence, all documentation with readiness, or all charismatic leadership with fragility. Procedures can become ceremonial workload, while experienced commanders may need discretion that rigid rules cannot supply. The strongest supported conclusion is narrower: methods dependent on rare tacit ability require a transferability test before they are promoted as doctrine.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Sima Guang's distinction between what Li Guang can personally do and what others may safely imitate.
- Added successor, peer-command, morale, and administrative-cost boundaries to the replication judgment.
- Preserved the absence of a direct enemy attack as the main outcome limitation.
