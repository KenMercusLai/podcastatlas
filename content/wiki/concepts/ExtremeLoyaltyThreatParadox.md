## Definition

极端忠诚的威胁反转 / extreme loyalty threat paradox is the pattern in which a subordinate's willingness to pay an unlimited personal or moral cost proves task commitment but also makes the superior infer that the subordinate lacks restraints and may therefore be dangerous.

## Current Synthesis

[[LeYang|乐羊]] supplies the founding case. When [[ZhongshanState|中山国]] captures and kills his son, he refuses to abandon [[WeiWenhou|魏文侯]]'s campaign and consumes the broth sent from the son's flesh before continuing the war. The act removes hostage leverage and demonstrates that command duty overrides private affection, yet the same proof of loyalty produces a darker inference: a person who crosses this boundary for the ruler may be capable of crossing other boundaries against the ruler or anyone else.

The episode therefore separates performance recognition from relational trust. Wei Wenhou can reward the successful conquest while refusing deeper future confidence. In organizational terms, sacrifice is not a monotonic trust signal: beyond a threshold, devotion stops reading as dependability and begins reading as unbounded capacity for harm.

## Key Claims

- Extreme sacrifice can prove mission commitment without proving moral reliability.
- A signal becomes threatening when it demonstrates that the actor has few visible restraints outside the assigned objective.
- Leaders can separate reward for completed performance from trust in future discretion.
- Hostage resistance can strengthen operational credibility while damaging relational credibility.
- The paradox is strongest when a subordinate treats family, law, or ordinary moral limits as expendable on behalf of a patron.

## Evidence

Mission commitment and hostage resistance:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-05-guangcai-duomu-de-weiwenhou-2-lrpjtxkpfccebs-0phxiozjsv5rn|Zhouji 05 part 2]] says Le Yang refuses to retreat when Zhongshan captures his son and continues the three-year campaign after consuming the broth sent by the enemy.

Reward separated from trust:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-05-guangcai-duomu-de-weiwenhou-2-lrpjtxkpfccebs-0phxiozjsv5rn|Zhouji 05 part 2]] says Wei Wenhou rewards Le Yang's achievement but ceases to trust him after an adviser reframes the act as evidence of dangerous ruthlessness.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode does not establish Le Yang's inner motive, whether consuming the broth was literal, performative, coerced by the strategic situation, or elaborated in transmission, or whether Wei Wenhou's distrust had additional political causes. Refusal to yield to hostage coercion does not always imply moral indifference, and large sacrifice does not automatically make a subordinate dangerous. The concept applies narrowly when the same observed act is explicitly interpreted both as commitment and as evidence of missing restraints.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from the episode's Le Yang reward-without-trust reversal.
