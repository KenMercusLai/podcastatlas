## Definition
Missionary map knowledge production is the process by which mission maps record geography while also making particular churches, villages, routes, and believer communities appear important.

## Current Synthesis
The episode's map case is [[SantaiZiCatholicVillage|三台子]]. A Catholic map of the Northeast made the village prominent because it was important within the mission network, even if ordinary administrative or local geography would not mark it the same way. The concept is not that missionary maps are false; it is that every map is built from a purpose, archive, and audience, so it creates a different imagined region.

## Key Claims
- Maps do not only reflect space; they also rank and organize space for a viewer.
- A mission map can make religious density and church infrastructure more visible than administrative hierarchy.
- Foreign readers may imagine a region differently when their primary source is a church-produced map.
- Map interpretation requires asking who made the map, for what audience, and with what categories of importance.
- Missionary mapping connects to broader archive power because the map can later guide what historians notice.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not claim that missionary maps are uniquely biased or useless. The point is comparative: all spatial knowledge has selection rules, and the church map's selection rules are especially visible.
