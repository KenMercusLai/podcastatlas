# Listener Community Co-Production / 听众社群共创

## Definition
Listener community co-production is the bounded participation through which a podcast audience contributes topic signals, norms, professional skills, artifacts, rituals, and offline relationships without taking over editorial or specialist authority.

## Current Synthesis
The anniversary source presents a listener community as more than a metric or comment stream. Requests can reveal unanswered health questions; a group nickname such as “群内没医生” can reinforce the boundary against informal diagnosis; listeners can contribute music, legal review, letters, food, gatherings, or a collectively rewritten theme song. These acts make the show partly reciprocal while preserving distinct roles.

Co-production is therefore layered. Topic requests influence relevance, skilled contributions support production, community reminders protect norms, and meetups turn repeated listening into embodied social contact. None of those contributions establishes medical evidence, authorizes clinical advice, or guarantees that the community represents the wider audience.

## Key Claims
- Listener requests can reveal demand and lived confusion without deciding editorial conclusions or evidentiary status.
- Community-created reminders can reinforce safety boundaries, including that a listener group is not a clinic.
- Listeners may contribute professional skill, music, legal support, feedback, or event labor as well as attention.
- Shared songs, meals, letters, and gifts can turn one-way familiarity into limited reciprocity and collective memory.
- Co-production works best when editorial, clinical, legal, and community roles remain explicit.

## Evidence
- Topic and boundary participation: [[vol-143-zhe-liang-nian-zhe-xie-buzhengjing-de-yisheng-zuozhe-zhengjing-de-kepu-kaibo-2-zhounian-teji-ljufnnoli6cgs_jkugf-f9wlkk6c|VOL.143]] describes listener requests for leukemia, rare-disease, and everyday-anxiety topics alongside the “群内没医生” reminder.
- Skilled contribution: [[vol-143-zhe-liang-nian-zhe-xie-buzhengjing-de-yisheng-zuozhe-zhengjing-de-kepu-kaibo-2-zhounian-teji-ljufnnoli6cgs_jkugf-f9wlkk6c|VOL.143]] credits listeners with a guitar arrangement and ongoing legal review of collaborations and contracts.
- Ritual and reciprocity: [[vol-143-zhe-liang-nian-zhe-xie-buzhengjing-de-yisheng-zuozhe-zhengjing-de-kepu-kaibo-2-zhounian-teji-ljufnnoli6cgs_jkugf-f9wlkk6c|VOL.143]] describes seven meetups, shared meals, a birthday performance, letters, gifts, and later reuse of the listener-created song.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence is one show's retrospective account, not a representative study of podcast communities. Highly engaged participants may differ from quiet listeners, gifts and volunteer skill can create obligation or unequal visibility, and informal moderation cannot replace professional clinical, legal, safeguarding, or event responsibility. A warm community can still transmit misinformation unless authority and verification remain explicit.

## What Changed
- Created a bounded model separating listener contribution from editorial and professional authority.
- Connected topic feedback, community norms, skilled help, and offline ritual in one co-production framework.
