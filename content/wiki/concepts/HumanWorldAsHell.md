# Human World As Hell / 人间如地狱

Human world as hell is the reading frame where hell imagery names worldly social reality rather than only afterlife punishment. [[17-diyubian-zhongsheng-jie-ku-yi-nian-ru-mo-561780565]] adds the concept through [[HellScreen|《地狱变》]], where the painting's [[TenKingsOfHell|十殿阎罗]], fire, torment, and bodies are not distant religious spectacle; they mirror lordly impunity, coerced service, sexualized power, and obedient narration.

This differs from [[AfterlifeMoralAccounting]]. In afterlife moral accounting, hell restores some imagined order by punishing wrongdoing. In [[HellScreen|《地狱变》]] as read by the episode, hell is already here, and the social order protects the people who make it.

## Key Claims
- Religious hell imagery can become social diagnosis when earthly institutions reproduce torment.
- A story can use supernatural or Buddhist vocabulary without making the afterlife its main concern.
- When official praise surrounds violence, the social world itself becomes infernal.
- The frame explains why a painting of hell can frighten the powerful: it shows them their own world.
