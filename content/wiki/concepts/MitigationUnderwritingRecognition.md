# Mitigation Underwriting Recognition

## Definition
Mitigation underwriting recognition is the process by which verified property- or community-level risk reduction changes an insurer's assessment, price, eligibility, or coverage decision.

## Current Synthesis
[[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-043ffe0bc2c7516bac64132229755e93-43026861-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-043ffe0bc2c7516bac64132229755e93-43026861]] shows that mitigation can exist physically without being visible in underwriting. Stale aerial data, broad geographic categories, and weak certification translation produced cancellations, small discounts, and high prices even for hardened homes. Direct inspection corrected some errors and enabled limited coverage, but recognition does not remove catastrophe correlation or guarantee scale.

## Key Claims
- Risk reduction creates insurance value only when underwriting systems can observe, verify, and use it.
- Remote property data can be stale or too coarse for recent household changes.
- Certification improves legibility but does not automatically bind insurers to offer coverage or a particular discount.
- Site-level evidence can correct broad classifications when it enters an accountable decision process.
- Predictable recognition can strengthen incentives to invest in costly mitigation.
- Recognition is distinct from affordability and portfolio capacity: an accurately assessed property may still be expensive or declined.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- The source does not reveal the carriers' models, inspection criteria, portfolio thresholds, or complete policy differences.
- A lower quoted premium after a visit does not isolate the effect of mitigation from regulation, competition, or other underwriting changes.
- More granular recognition can reward adaptation but may also intensify surveillance and property-level exclusion.

## What Changed
- Created an information-and-incentive layer between physical adaptation and insurance-market outcomes.
- Distinguished corrected property assessment from broader questions of portfolio capacity and affordability.
