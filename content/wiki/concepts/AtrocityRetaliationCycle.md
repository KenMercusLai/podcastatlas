# Atrocity Retaliation Cycle

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-902-kongzhi-qingxu-shi-rensheng-bixiuke-ljynylvdlsdtjf4es9jmilav863b|Hanji 902]] supplies the first turn in the late-Han private-feud branch. [[LiHaoLateHan|李昊]] kills [[SuQianLateHan|苏谦]], [[SuBuweiLateHan|苏不韦]] delays full burial and plans revenge, then the first hidden attack misses Li but kills Li's concubine and young son. The cycle begins before public corpse insult: private injury is converted into patient retaliatory capacity.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-903-weishenme-quan-ni-yuanyuan-xiangbao-heshi-le-lokpt93muc8kasvn44vgsjki8bom|Hanji 903]] continues the same late-Han private-feud version. Su Buwei's revenge against Li Hao escalates from failed killing to grave violation and corpse display, then [[DuanJiongLateHan|段熲]] answers through the extermination of Su's household. The cycle here is not sectarian recruitment or battlefield reprisal; it is the way private injury, patron ties, and coercive retainers turn reciprocal resentment into mass collateral violence.

Atrocity retaliation cycle is the episode's pattern where killings create fear, revenge expectations, and new recruitment for opposing armed groups. In [[699-the-troubles-return-of-the-ira-part-3-glt9519811642]], the killings of [[RobertCurtis]] and three [[RoyalHighlandFusiliers]] make the [[BritishArmy]] feel directly targeted, while IRA and loyalist bombings push Catholic and Protestant civilians into daily fear.

The late-1971 sequence makes the cycle explicit: attacks on Protestant venues, the [[UlsterVolunteerForce|UVF]] bombing of [[McGurksBarBombing|McGurk's Bar]], and later IRA bombing are presented as mutually reinforcing atrocities. The concept is related to [[SectarianSecurityDilemma]], but its emphasis is not only anticipation of attack; it is the way real harm becomes justification for further harm.

## Key Claims
- Hanji 903 adds that a revenge cycle can move through family honor, corpse humiliation, and patron retaliation without needing formal war or organized insurgency.
- Retaliatory violence can recruit by trauma even when it alienates outsiders.
- Civilian-targeted attacks harden communal threat perception faster than political speeches do.
- Security-force revenge impulses become part of the cycle when soldiers start to read restraint as impossible.
