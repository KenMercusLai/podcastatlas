# 浙东唐诗之路 / Eastern Zhejiang Tang Poetry Road

浙东唐诗之路 / Eastern Zhejiang Tang Poetry Road is the southern route branch in [[ep264-tafang-tangshi-zhilu-shengtang-qixiang-shaonianxin-loo2dfuxffizpbl4si5nwcixnngi]]. [[BoJingSanlianCulture|薄静]] describes it as a route after crossing the Qiantang River, moving through places such as Shaoxing, Shengzhou, Xinchang, Tiantai, Linhai, and toward Wenzhou.

The route attracts Tang poets because it combines landscape, waterways, Buddhist and Daoist sites, Six Dynasties cultural prestige, Wang Xizhi and Xie Lingyun memory, social visits, and possible recommendation networks. The source uses [[MengHaoran|孟浩然]] as the main case while clearly marking that his exact route and dating remain disputed.

## Key Claims
- Zhejiang's route value comes from both terrain and inherited cultural prestige.
- Tang travel in the region can mix sightseeing, pilgrimage, reclusion performance, friendship, and official ambition.
- Modern field reporting can recover some route feeling through roads, streams, temples, villages, and mountain paths even where Tang waterways no longer function.
- The route should remain evidence-bounded because poet itineraries are often reconstructed from partial texts and later scholarship.
