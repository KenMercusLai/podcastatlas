# Long-Horizon Knowledge Stewardship / 长时段知识托管

## Definition

Long-horizon knowledge stewardship is the continuing work of selecting, recording, organizing, copying, explaining, storing, opening, and transferring valued knowledge so that future people can still use and reassess it.

## Current Synthesis

[[244-shuxie-gei-guoqu-weilai-de-ren-yu-shenming-zhongguo-wenming-zhong-de-shuxie-chuantong-yu-changqi-zhuyi-8225426843-437457]] reframes long-termism from a slogan about patience into an institutional chain. [[OracleBoneDivination|Oracle bones]] record uncertainty, [[BronzeInscriptionPosterity|bronzes]] name descendants, [[ChineseClassicalInterpretationContinuity|classical interpretation]] keeps inherited texts active, and state compilations and archives reorganize older knowledge for later use.

The cases also show failure modes. Costly material and imperial scale can protect a record, but [[YongleDadian|《永乐大典》]] demonstrates that restricted readership and weak distributed custody make even a monumental project vulnerable. [[ImperialVeritableRecords|Official records]] can survive while embedding political edits. Stewardship therefore requires access, criticism, recopying, and renewed use as well as storage.

## Key Claims

- Preservation is a chain of active practices, not a one-time act of collection or construction.
- Intended audience shapes format: gods, rulers, descendants, scholars, and future publics produce different records.
- Recopying and continued use distribute custody and improve the odds of textual survival.
- Classification and indexing determine what later users can retrieve from a large body of knowledge.
- Official preservation can carry political selection and narrative bias even when it safeguards valuable evidence.
- Long-term value is partly realized when later users can reinterpret a record beyond its creator's original purpose.

## Evidence

- Audiences across time - [[244-shuxie-gei-guoqu-weilai-de-ren-yu-shenming-zhongguo-wenming-zhong-de-shuxie-chuantong-yu-changqi-zhuyi-8225426843-437457]] contrasts divinatory, descendant-facing, canonical, imperial, and public uses of writing.
- Monumental fragility - [[244-shuxie-gei-guoqu-weilai-de-ren-yu-shenming-zhongguo-wenming-zhong-de-shuxie-chuantong-yu-changqi-zhuyi-8225426843-437457]] presents *Yongle Dadian* as a huge knowledge store with few readers, an incomplete backup history, and major dispersal.
- Productive reuse - [[244-shuxie-gei-guoqu-weilai-de-ren-yu-shenming-zhongguo-wenming-zhong-de-shuxie-chuantong-yu-changqi-zhuyi-8225426843-437457]] shows Qing compilers extracting lost texts and modern historians using politically shaped official records critically.
- Material and workflow - [[244-shuxie-gei-guoqu-weilai-de-ren-yu-shenming-zhongguo-wenming-zhong-de-shuxie-chuantong-yu-changqi-zhuyi-8225426843-437457]] connects bone preparation, bronze casting, carving, movable type, handwriting, storage, and cabinets to different survival possibilities.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Time does not automatically select what is most valuable. Power, cost, accidents, institutions, conquest, private custody, and later demand shape survival. Nor does wider access alone solve preservation: handling, copying error, censorship, commercial incentives, and technical decay introduce new risks.

## What Changed

- Created an institutional definition of long-termism centered on active custody, access, interpretation, and transfer.
- Added restricted readership and politically edited survival as distinct preservation failure modes.
