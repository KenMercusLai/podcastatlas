# Famine Entitlement Failure

Famine entitlement failure is the episode's frame for why hunger can occur when food has not vanished completely. In [[65-longwang-zhinu-1931-nian-de-changjiang-hongshui-658100922]], flood destroys harvests, lowers wages, raises grain prices, kills livestock, disrupts credit, and strips people of the production and exchange rights that let them obtain food.

The source uses the 1931 flood to separate starvation from simple food absence. Some people still had access to markets, loans, transport, or stores; others were pushed into eating husks, roots, bark, leaves, lotus products, or worse because their claims on food had collapsed.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-155-1-lishi-jizai-shenme-qingkuang-hui-fasheng-ren-chi-ren-canju-lrwz-prnxd8hzchagyrbywkux7lh|Hanji 155-1]] adds a Chu-Han scarcity case where [[GuanzhongRegion|关中]] grain prices reportedly reach ten thousand coins per unit and people resort to cannibalism. The episode pairs aggregate production collapse with unequal access: [[RenShiQinMerchant|任氏]] can profit from stored grain while ordinary households are pushed toward hunger and migration, making the famine also a case for [[PowerBackedGrainHoarding|权力保护下的乱世囤粮]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-984-liangzhouxi-junfa-neidou-ta-weihe-di-yige-daoxia-lurb2qfzsa7e-owh-dl-m-sccze5|Hanji 984]] adds a pre-flight Sanfu case. The source presents famine together with armed plunder: households may still exist as a population count, but soldiers' extraction and loss of protection break ordinary claims on food and safety. The reported cannibalism therefore belongs to an entitlement failure as well as simple shortage.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-992-luanshi-you-duo-kepa-jiemi-changan-de-xuese-mengyan-ltuxzrb-1x5y2ctwo4drggxn5stz|Hanji 992]] adds a late-Han capital-collapse case. After [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]] leaves [[ChangAnHanCapital|长安]], the city is unmanaged, strong residents flee, weak residents remain, and the source reports cannibalism across the wider [[GuanzhongRegion|关中]] disaster. Here the access failure is political and physical as much as market-based: people without strength, mobility, protection, or supply channels cannot reach food.

## Key Claims
- Hunger can follow from lost access, wages, credit, transport, or exchange power, not only from aggregate food shortage.
- Livestock loss matters because animals are savings, labor, transport, fertilizer, and recovery capacity.
- Relief rules that require labor or compliance can misread people whose immediate problem is starvation.
- Debt can help production restart while also trapping households after the flood.
- Hanji 155-1 adds that famine can coexist with concentrated stores when protection, position, or prior official access lets some actors hold grain through crisis.
- Hanji 984 adds that armed plunder can break household claims on food and safety before the later capital-flight collapse.
- Hanji 992 adds that entitlement collapse can follow from lost protection and mobility when a capital's order disappears.
