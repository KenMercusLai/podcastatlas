## Definition

帝王尊师礼制 / imperial teacher-honor ritual is the pattern in which a ruler publicly performs student-like deference toward a teacher so that learning, hierarchy, and classical authority become visible court order rather than private gratitude alone.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-734-huanrong-cheng-daru-yinwei-mingdi-shige-hao-penggen-lhwbxrqocf102or9k0nb6z93gzx9|Hanji 734]] grounds the concept through [[EmperorMingOfHan|汉明帝]] and [[HuanRongEasternHan|桓荣]]. Mingdi had studied `Shangshu` with Huan Rong as crown prince. After becoming emperor, he keeps that teacher-student relation publicly legible through Biyong elder-care ritual, Taichang-office lectures, scholar audiences, sickbed visits, mourning, and burial grants.

An earlier Western Han variant does not require a prior personal teacher-student relationship between emperor and scholar. After [[ZhaoWanWesternHan|赵绾]] and [[WangZangWesternHan|王臧]] recommend their teacher [[ShenGongWesternHan|申公]], Wudi sends gifts and a specially cushioned four-horse carriage to bring the elderly scholar from Lu, then questions and appoints him. Here the ruler honors a minister's teacher to authorize a wider court learning program. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-249-dongzhongshu-wo-bushi-rujia-de-zuiren-livm-uxnbsm0mkbrm-lvnozqnail|Hanji 249]]

The concept differs from generic respect for elders or talent attraction. The key mechanism is role performance by the ruler. Mingdi's seating choices, hand-held text, refusal to answer in the teacher's presence, walking from the street to Huan Rong's sickbed, and funeral conduct all tell officials and students how to recognize classical authority. Imperial deference therefore increases the teacher's standing while also strengthening the ruler's Confucian legitimacy.

The current synthesis keeps the host's "supporting actor" interpretation qualified. Huan Rong's learning and long accumulation matter, but the source argues that public recognition by imperial ritual gives that learning a much larger audience and makes it socially enforceable.

## Key Claims

- Teacher respect becomes politically powerful when the ruler performs it in front of officials, students, and scholars.
- Ritual seating, movement, text handling, and refusal to speak can signal that learning has authority even over the emperor's public posture.
- Imperial honor can convert private teacher-student memory into state-backed classical legitimacy.
- The ruler need not be the teacher's former pupil: honoring officials' teacher can also legitimate a court program and scholarly lineage.
- The same performance elevates both teacher and ruler: the teacher gains public authority, while the ruler gains a Confucian role-order image.
- Teacher-honor ritual is adjacent to elder-care ceremony but narrower because the teacher-student relation is the operative hierarchy.
- Public example can create conduct norms for others, as shown when officials copy Mingdi's walking approach to Huan Rong's home.

## Evidence

- Biyong elder-care setting: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-734-huanrong-cheng-daru-yinwei-mingdi-shige-hao-penggen-lhwbxrqocf102or9k0nb6z93gzx9|Hanji 734]] says Mingdi appoints Huan Rong as `wugeng`, assigns ritual seating and objects, and personally performs food-and-wine respect.
- Public scholarship scene: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-734-huanrong-cheng-daru-yinwei-mingdi-shige-hao-penggen-lhwbxrqocf102or9k0nb6z93gzx9|Hanji 734]] says Mingdi summons Huan Rong and his students, discusses classics, and creates a large audience of officials and scholars.
- Teacher priority in interpretation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-734-huanrong-cheng-daru-yinwei-mingdi-shige-hao-penggen-lhwbxrqocf102or9k0nb6z93gzx9|Hanji 734]] has Mingdi decline to explain a question because his teacher is present.
- Sickbed ritual example: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-734-huanrong-cheng-daru-yinwei-mingdi-shige-hao-penggen-lhwbxrqocf102or9k0nb6z93gzx9|Hanji 734]] says Mingdi leaves his carriage at the street entrance, carries the text, holds Huan Rong's hand, and weeps.
- Norm diffusion: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-734-huanrong-cheng-daru-yinwei-mingdi-shige-hao-penggen-lhwbxrqocf102or9k0nb6z93gzx9|Hanji 734]] says later visitors do not dare ride directly to Huan Rong's door after seeing Mingdi's example.
- Posthumous honor: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-734-huanrong-cheng-daru-yinwei-mingdi-shige-hao-penggen-lhwbxrqocf102or9k0nb6z93gzx9|Hanji 734]] records mourning dress, funeral attendance, burial land, and family title settlement.
- Western Han summons: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-249-dongzhongshu-wo-bushi-rujia-de-zuiren-livm-uxnbsm0mkbrm-lvnozqnail|Hanji 249]] uses gifts, wheel cushioning, a four-horse carriage, audience, and appointment to make Shen Gong's age and teacher status politically visible.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Hanji 734 does not prove that every question at Biyong was spontaneous; the host suggests the public questions may have been arranged. That qualification strengthens rather than weakens the concept because the pattern concerns staged public legitimacy as much as private affection. Hanji 249 likewise does not prove that the careful summons gave Shen Gong decisive policy influence: the episode says Wudi disliked his plain answer even while appointing him. The concept therefore separates visible honor from substantive adoption and does not replace [[MoralMentorshipTalentCultivation|moral mentorship as talent cultivation]].

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Mingdi's public honoring of Huan Rong in Hanji 734.
- Distinguished teacher-honor ritual from broader elder respect, talent attraction, and moral mentorship.
- Added Wudi's summons of Shen Gong as an earlier form that honors officials' teacher rather than the ruler's own former teacher.
