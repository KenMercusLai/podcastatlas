# Postwar Fashion Consumer Revolution

## Definition

The postwar fashion consumer revolution is the shift from rationed, durable, adult-oriented clothing toward abundant, rapidly changing, media-amplified styles increasingly organized around young consumers.

## Current Synthesis

The episode makes wartime scarcity the necessary baseline: coupons, utility rules, expensive garments, cold and dirty environments, and “make do and mend” favored longevity. Dior's New Look first rejected that restraint through elite glamour, while Britain's later recovery, full employment, youth earnings, boutiques, magazines, television, colour printing, and international retail converted change itself into a mass-market value. Disposable fashion was therefore not merely a new aesthetic; it joined economic capacity, distribution, media, and youth identity.

## Key Claims

- Rationing and utility regulation made clothing durability a material necessity.
- Dior's New Look offered an early luxury rejection of wartime restraint.
- Recovery and youth income widened the market for novelty and self-differentiation.
- Boutiques lowered some retail barriers and made experimentation more informal.
- Magazines, television, photography, and colour printing accelerated visual diffusion.
- The ideal of “young” helped normalize short fashion cycles and disposable clothing.

## Evidence

- Austerity baseline: [[363-sixties-fashion-the-teenage-revolution-part-1-glt5487814001]] describes rationing, utility clothing, limited wardrobes, and long garment life.
- Luxury break: [[363-sixties-fashion-the-teenage-revolution-part-1-glt5487814001]] positions Dior's 1947 New Look against wartime restraint.
- Consumer capacity: [[363-sixties-fashion-the-teenage-revolution-part-1-glt5487814001]] links full employment, rising living standards, youth work, and pocket money to fashion spending.
- Retail and media system: [[363-sixties-fashion-the-teenage-revolution-part-1-glt5487814001]] connects boutiques, teenage magazines, television, colour supplements, photography, and international distribution.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The transition was uneven: early boutique clothing could remain expensive, adult anxiety accompanied youth spending, and iconic London images did not represent everyday dress throughout Britain. “Revolution” names a structural direction rather than a simultaneous change for every class, region, age, or gender.

## What Changed

- Created the concept to join wartime scarcity, postwar recovery, youth demand, and media distribution into one transition.
