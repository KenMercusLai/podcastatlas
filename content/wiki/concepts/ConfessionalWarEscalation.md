# Confessional War Escalation

[[694-elizabeth-i-vs-the-catholics-a-treacherous-conspiracy-part-4-glt1627515087]] adds the prewar conspiracy and diplomacy layer. [[FrancisDrake]]'s return turns privateering into a Spanish diplomatic crisis, [[FrancoisDukeOfAnjou|Anjou]]'s failure removes a French solution in the [[LowCountries|Low Countries]], the [[ThrockmortonPlot]] ties invasion to [[MaryQueenOfScots|Mary, Queen of Scots]], and [[BernardinoDeMendoza]]'s expulsion breaks normal Anglo-Spanish diplomacy.

[[695-elizabeth-i-vs-the-catholics-the-shadow-war-part-5-glt5637968455]] adds the Low Countries prelude to confessional war escalation. [[PhilipII|Philip II]]'s pressure on the [[DutchRevolt]], [[AlexanderFarnese|Alexander Farnese / Duke of Parma]]'s recovery of the southern [[LowCountries|Low Countries]], [[Antwerp]]'s fall, English trade pressure, and [[FrancisDrake]]'s raids turn Protestant solidarity, invasion fear, and interstate rivalry into the setting for open Anglo-Spanish conflict.

Confessional war escalation is the pattern where religious identity, dynastic legitimacy, and interstate rivalry make local security decisions feed wider conflict. [[696-elizabeth-i-vs-the-catholics-killing-the-queen-of-scots-part-6-glt2334035496]] grounds the concept in the movement from the [[BabingtonPlot]] to [[MaryQueenOfScots|Mary, Queen of Scots]]' execution and then toward the [[SpanishArmada]].

The Elizabethan episodes do not reduce the Armada to a single-cause reaction to Mary. Instead, they treat Mary's death as removing one succession threat while intensifying the Catholic-Protestant and Anglo-Spanish struggle already surrounding [[England]], [[Spain]], [[ElizabethI|Elizabeth I]], and [[PhilipII|Philip II]].
