# Delegated Endurance Stop Rules

## Definition
Delegated endurance stop rules are advance agreements that give trusted crew or medical decision-makers authority to end an effort when objective danger or cognitive impairment makes the athlete's own determination an unreliable safety signal.

## Current Synthesis
Extreme persistence creates a governance paradox: the trait that keeps an athlete moving can also prevent appropriate stopping. Edgley's “outsourcing common sense” resolves part of that conflict by separating the suffering participant from the people monitoring navigation, weather, injury, consciousness, and other evidence. Authority is agreed before crisis, when the athlete is better able to recognize the limits of future judgment.

The principle is broader than obedience to a team. It requires competent observers, agreed roles, meaningful indicators, and an actual power to stop the attempt. It also distinguishes completion from success: survival, recovery, return, and family responsibility remain part of the outcome. The episode supplies a vivid case, not a validated universal checklist.

## Key Claims
- Determination and pain tolerance can become hazards when they delay adjustment to severe injury or physiological decline.
- Stop authority should be assigned before exhaustion, hypothermia, pain, and sleep loss degrade judgment.
- Expedition safety depends on distributed expertise in medicine, navigation, tides, weather, wildlife, and local conditions.
- Objective evidence should outrank an athlete's identity, sunk cost, public promise, or immediate desire to continue.
- A team needs clear authority, not merely advice, if the participant is likely to reject stopping in crisis.
- Completion is not the only success criterion; survival, recovery, obligations to others, and acceptable residual harm matter.

## Evidence
- Prior failure and precommitment - [[scim9489463983-scim9489463983]] describes hospitalization after previous swims and Edgley's instruction that trusted team members remove him when evidence made continuation unsafe.
- Judgment impairment - [[scim9489463983-scim9489463983]] links sleep loss, near-continuous daylight, injury, cold, and exhaustion to unreliable thoughts and postponed decisions.
- Distributed expertise - [[scim9489463983-scim9489463983]] attributes safe progress to navigation crews, medical judgment, tide prediction, and local wildlife or environmental knowledge.
- Responsibility threshold - [[scim9489463983-scim9489463983]] presents fatherhood as increasing the importance of survival and requiring a stronger reason to accept expedition risk.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not define validated thresholds, medical protocols, legal authority, crew competence standards, or rescue plans. Delegation can fail through unclear roles, poor monitoring, group pressure, conflicts of interest, or delayed intervention. This concept is not a substitute for medical care, qualified coaching, event rules, environmental expertise, or emergency services.

## What Changed
- Established precommitted stop authority as a counterweight to pathological persistence.
- Reframed extreme endurance as distributed safety governance rather than solo willpower.
- Added sleep loss and pain tolerance as reasons to distrust in-the-moment self-assessment.
