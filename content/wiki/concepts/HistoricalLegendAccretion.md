# Historical Legend Accretion / 历史传说的层累形成

## Definition

Historical legend accretion is the process by which a remembered event, person, ritual argument, or sparse narrative acquires new actions, technologies, motives, moral roles, places, and symbolic meanings as later texts, performances, and communities retell it.

## Current Synthesis

[[17-dongxifang-de-changcheng-lvlaqbfmfxbvytpsimiknt0km-xp]] provides two compact cases. The story later known as “烽火戏诸侯” grows from a Western Zhou succession and invasion setting into a repeated false alarm first told with drums and later with beacon fires. The [[MengJiangnu|Meng Jiangnü]] tradition grows from Qiliang's widow defending ritual propriety into lament, a death at a wall, Qin conscription, a body built into the Great Wall, wall collapse, bone recognition, and instruction in marital fidelity.

The framework does not reduce every later element to fabrication. It asks when a feature becomes visible, what genre and institution transmit it, which contemporary technology or moral concern it reflects, and what cultural work it performs. A story's durable symbolic force and its value as reception history can therefore coexist with uncertainty about event history.

A named-place attachment can also grow from a personal epithet. The current Pang Tong case distinguishes death during the attack on 雒城 from “落凤坡,” a place name the episode says appears only in later Tang-Song memory and fits [[PangTong|庞统]]'s “凤雏” title too neatly to be accepted as direct event evidence. It likewise treats the familiar “卧龙、凤雏” maxim as literary reception rather than a verbatim line from [[Sanguozhi|《三国志》]]. ## Key Claims

- Familiar legends often combine layers that entered the record at different times.
- Changes in technology and institutions can update narrative mechanics, as drums become frontier beacon fires.
- Relocation to a famous monument or reign can make a story more memorable while weakening direct biographical inference.
- Histories, biographies, ritual texts, fiction, drama, tourism, and oral transmission can preserve different and sometimes incompatible layers.
- Later moral systems can redirect a story's center, as ritual protest becomes marital fidelity or a succession crisis becomes a woman's smile causing dynastic collapse.
- Source criticism should date individual motifs and identify genre without treating the whole tradition as either verified fact or worthless fiction.

## Evidence

- **Alarm-technology substitution:** [[17-dongxifang-de-changcheng-lvlaqbfmfxbvytpsimiknt0km-xp]] distinguishes an earlier drum-alarm version from the beacon-fire form familiar through later historiography.
- **Monument attachment:** [[17-dongxifang-de-changcheng-lvlaqbfmfxbvytpsimiknt0km-xp]] traces how Qiliang's widow becomes [[MengJiangnu|Meng Jiangnü]] and is attached to Qin Great Wall construction.
- **Moral recentering:** [[17-dongxifang-de-changcheng-lvlaqbfmfxbvytpsimiknt0km-xp]] shows ritual propriety, grief, forced labor, romantic devotion, and wifely fidelity becoming different centers of the evolving narrative.
- **Material reinforcement:** [[17-dongxifang-de-changcheng-lvlaqbfmfxbvytpsimiknt0km-xp]] notes that later stories can become associated with named remains and visitor sites, giving narrative layers an apparently physical anchor.
- **Title-driven place attachment:** [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1144-fengchu-pangtong-shi-zenme-si-de-lpc9kyfhupts3zfjwjsv4-ugjxox|Hanji 1144]] separates Pang Tong's recorded campaign death from the later “落凤坡” name and its fit with the “凤雏” epithet.
- **Literary formula stabilization:** [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1144-fengchu-pangtong-shi-zenme-si-de-lpc9kyfhupts3zfjwjsv4-ugjxox|Hanji 1144]] treats the familiar “卧龙、凤雏” wording as later literary memory rather than direct historical-record language.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Surviving texts provide earliest recoverable attestations, not guaranteed dates of invention; oral forms may be older. A changed motif does not by itself disprove the underlying succession conflict, invasion, death, mourning, labor experience, or Pang Tong's death in the Yizhou campaign. Neither source supplies a complete textual stemma, so the first appearance, transmission path, dating, and relationship among oral, geographical, dramatic, and novelistic forms remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Added Pang Tong's “落凤坡” as a title-driven place-attachment case.
- Distinguished a later “卧龙、凤雏” literary formula from direct historical-record wording.
- Extended the framework from monuments and moral recentering to personal epithets and named death sites.
