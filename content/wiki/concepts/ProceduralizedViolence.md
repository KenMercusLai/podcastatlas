# Proceduralized Violence / 程序化暴力

Proceduralized violence is the paradox [[78-xifang-juedou-shi-chujian-ba-weile-rongyu-687731000]] makes visible through dueling rules. A duel remains violence, but the practice tries to separate authorized combat from murder by adding written challenges, seconds, witnesses, time limits, weapon choice, medical objections, and end conditions such as first blood.

The concept sits between [[PowerViolenceDistinction]] and [[PrivateRevengeModernLaw]]. It shows that violence can borrow institutional form without becoming stable public justice. Procedure can reduce cheating, regulate escalation, and make reputation legible, yet still fail to protect life or replace law.

## Key Claims
- Procedure can make violence socially legible without making it morally or legally safe.
- Seconds and witnesses matter because reputation is judged by a public or semi-public audience.
- Weapon and disability rules show an attempt to preserve fairness, not just lethality.
- A first-blood rule can transform combat from killing contest into symbolic proof, but accidental or intentional death remains structurally possible.
- Cheating or using an unauthorized weapon converts the duel's claimed honor into assassination-like violence.
- Proceduralized violence is fragile because the procedure can itself create new insults, as in [[RichardBrinsleySheridan|Sheridan]]'s second duel.
