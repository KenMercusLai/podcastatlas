# Archaic Human Interbreeding

## Definition

Archaic human interbreeding is the genetic and demographic process by which Homo sapiens, [[Neanderthals]], Denisovans, and possibly other recent human lineages produced descendants across periods of contact.

## Current Synthesis

In [[127-neanderthals-glt5202048433]], surviving Neanderthal ancestry in most populations outside Africa and surviving Denisovan ancestry in billions of people replace a clean branching tree with a partially reticulated human history. Neanderthals became physically extinct as a recognizable population around 40,000 years ago, yet some of their genetic inheritance persists within living humans.

Interbreeding also changes the extinction question. Gene flow may have provided immune adaptations to incoming Homo sapiens and may have absorbed some Neanderthal individuals or groups into expanding modern-human populations. But present evidence does not reconstruct the social character of every encounter, its sex balance, its reproductive success in both directions, or whether contact was voluntary, coercive, adoptive, episodic, or mixed.

## Key Claims

- Repeated gene flow makes strict total-replacement accounts inadequate.
- Physical lineage extinction can coexist with partial genetic survival.
- Present-day ancestry demonstrates successful reproduction but not the social conditions of contact.
- Gene flow may have transferred locally useful immune variants while also carrying later health tradeoffs.
- Asymmetry in sampled genomes may reflect demography, selection, sampling, or unequal reproductive success rather than one settled mechanism.

## Evidence

### Genetic persistence

- [[127-neanderthals-glt5202048433]] reports that most people outside Africa carry roughly a small percentage of Neanderthal ancestry and that Denisovan ancestry also persists widely.

### Contact and absorption

- [[127-neanderthals-glt5202048433]] describes early modern humans with recent Neanderthal ancestry, long geographic overlap, and absorption as one possible contributor to Neanderthal demographic decline.

### Biological consequences

- [[127-neanderthals-glt5202048433]] presents rapid immune adaptation, present-day infection response, and autoimmune tradeoffs as plausible consequences requiring gene-specific evidence.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

An ancestry percentage is a population summary, not a measure of individual cultural identity. The episode's approximate figures and directional hypotheses should not be universalized across all populations. Lack of detected modern-human ancestry in sampled late Neanderthals does not prove it never occurred. Friendly exchange, sexual violence, capture, adoption, sex-biased mating, hybrid fertility, and immune effects cannot be inferred from gene flow alone.

- Added the demographic-absorption implication while preserving uncertainty about encounter conditions and directionality.
