## Definition

Shangdi in early imperial ritual is a bounded interpretive concept for reading “上帝” inside transmitted Chinese sacrificial, dynastic, and ruler-cult contexts rather than importing its most familiar modern religious meaning automatically.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-371-2-shangdi-shuyu-zhongguo-zhexia-shichui-le-ltllclatwddfxlawrdpgziqlp3xq|Hanji 371-2]] uses [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s 104 BCE winter-solstice sacrifice at the Mingtang to distinguish a celestial-sovereign reading from the host's further claim that “上帝” can also designate Shun. The episode places the term beside Gaozu-era sacrifice to celestial and directional powers, Wendi's Five Emperors temple, Wudi's repeated Five Emperors rites, Confucian political culture, and the [[YaoShunYuLegend|Yao-Shun-Yu tradition]].

The useful synthesis is contextual rather than etymological: one word can belong to a historical ritual vocabulary whose referent depends on period, text, and cult setting. The source does not by itself establish that every Western Han occurrence has two simultaneous meanings, that Shun was generally identical with a supreme celestial deity, or that ancient and modern uses are interchangeable.

## Key Claims

- “上帝” should be interpreted from its specific historical and ritual context rather than from modern usage alone.
- Hanji 371-2 reads Han Wudi's Mingtang sacrifice inside a longer Western Han sequence of celestial and Five Emperors worship.
- The host proposes both a celestial-sovereign referent and a Shun referent for the term.
- The surrounding political meaning joins sacrifice, exemplary-ruler memory, Confucian legitimation, and imperial performance.
- The dual reading remains a source-scoped interpretation, not a settled dictionary definition for all early imperial texts.

## Evidence

- Context-dependent ritual language - [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-371-2-shangdi-shuyu-zhongguo-zhexia-shichui-le-ltllclatwddfxlawrdpgziqlp3xq|Hanji 371-2]] places Wudi's Mingtang sacrifice beside earlier Han sacrifices and Five Emperors institutions.
- Celestial and exemplary-ruler readings - [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-371-2-shangdi-shuyu-zhongguo-zhexia-shichui-le-ltllclatwddfxlawrdpgziqlp3xq|Hanji 371-2]] explicitly supplies the two meanings while connecting Shun to the moral ancestry claimed for Confucian rule.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The only current source is a short popular-history episode represented by a structured summary rather than a full primary-text comparison. It does not reproduce the relevant passages from the *Shiji*, *Hanshu*, or ritual classics, distinguish every named deity or directional cult, or establish how widespread the proposed Shun identification was. The concept therefore records an interpretive warning and a source claim, not a comprehensive history of Shangdi theology.

## What Changed

- Created a bounded concept separating early imperial ritual usage from automatic projection of modern meanings.
- Preserved the host's celestial-sovereign and Shun readings while marking the second as requiring primary-text and specialist verification.
