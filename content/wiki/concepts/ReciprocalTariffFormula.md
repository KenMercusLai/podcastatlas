# Reciprocal Tariff Formula

Reciprocal tariff formula is the mechanism [[vol-123-telangpu-de-duideng-guanshui-an-buzhi-shi-yi-chang-daxing-fucongxing-ceshi-lm6bkiqgyyefwompxon7lo6cnqp6]] uses to separate [[DonaldTrump]]'s "reciprocal" tariff rhetoric from traditional tariff reciprocity. The episode says the calculation does not start from the actual tariff or non-tariff barriers another country places on U.S. goods. Instead, it uses a goods-trade imbalance: the trading partner's surplus with the [[UnitedStates]] divided by that partner's exports to the United States, then divided by two.

That makes the formula hard to resolve through ordinary tariff negotiation. If the problem is defined as a trade deficit, removing a listed barrier may not be enough; the counterparty may have to change production location, purchasing, investment, currency, or broader strategic alignment. In the episode's reading, the formula therefore becomes the technical face of [[TariffComplianceTest]] and [[SupplyChainSovereignty]], not just a rate schedule.

## Key Claims
- The formula makes the "reciprocal" label politically legible while detaching it from direct tariff matching.
- [[Vietnam]] is the episode's concrete example: a high bilateral surplus can produce a high implied rate even if the dispute is not about one formal tariff line.
- A deficit-based formula shifts bargaining from trade barriers to trade balances, supply-chain routing, and geopolitical dependence.
- The concept extends [[TradeReciprocityProtectionism]] by showing how a fairness claim can become a blanket pressure tool.
- It also intensifies [[EffectiveTariffRateShock]] because the computed rate can be much higher than ordinary postwar U.S. tariff levels.
