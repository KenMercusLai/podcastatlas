## Definition

新朝汉臣拒仕谱系 / Xin loyalist refusal spectrum names the range of ways former Han scholars and officials in Hanji 644 avoid validating [[WangMang|王莽]]'s [[XinDynasty|新朝]]: fatal martyrdom, allusive reclusion, illness-based retirement, gift refusal, and cautious officeholding.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 644 makes refusal plural rather than singular. [[GongShengXinScholar|龚胜]] stands at the most costly end: he receives the summons with ritual seriousness, states that Han favor prevents him from serving two houses, and dies by refusing food and water. [[XueFangXinRecluse|薛方]] refuses more safely by invoking Xu You and Mount Ji, using Wang Mang's own archaizing culture to make nonservice honorable rather than insulting.

[[GuoQinXinOfficial|郭钦]] and [[JiangXuXinOfficial|蒋诩]] show lower-visibility withdrawal. Both had official status and upright reputations, but after Wang Mang takes power they claim illness, return home, and remain secluded until death. Huan Xiang's son refuses funeral gifts according to his father's instructions, shifting the same refusal logic into posthumous household conduct. [[TangZunXinOfficial|唐尊]] supplies a qualification: not every famous classicist refuses; some serve and are rewarded, while their conduct may still be judged as performative.

The spectrum matters because it prevents the wiki from flattening Xin-era moral response into one heroic form. The common thread is unwillingness to let reputation become easy regime capital; the differences are cost, publicity, rhetoric, and survivability.

## Key Claims

- Refusal under usurpation can range from death to quiet withdrawal without losing its moral logic.
- Ritual respect and political nonrecognition can coexist, as Gong Sheng receives the edict properly while refusing office.
- Classical allusion can make refusal survivable by giving the ruler a face-saving interpretation.
- Illness claims can serve as low-visibility exits when open confrontation would be dangerous or useless.
- Household and funerary conduct can continue refusal after the official actor dies.
- The presence of serving scholars such as Tang Zun qualifies the pattern: elite response to Xin was divided, not uniform.
- The shared political problem is whether respected Han-era names become usable legitimacy for Wang Mang.

## Evidence

- Fatal refusal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-644-shui-gan-he-huangdi-shizhe-taojiahuanjia-lkgn0idimbu6twz-symr5012f8sn|Hanji 644]] says Gong Sheng refuses师友祭酒, cites Han favor and the rule against serving two houses, then dies after fourteen days without food or water.
- Ritual-respectful nonrecognition: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-644-shui-gan-he-huangdi-shizhe-taojiahuanjia-lkgn0idimbu6twz-symr5012f8sn|Hanji 644]] says Gong Sheng receives the edict in formal sickbed posture before refusing the office.
- Allusive reclusion: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-644-shui-gan-he-huangdi-shizhe-taojiahuanjia-lkgn0idimbu6twz-symr5012f8sn|Hanji 644]] says Xue Fang invokes the Xu You/Mount Ji model and Wang Mang stops forcing the summons.
- Illness-based withdrawal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-644-shui-gan-he-huangdi-shizhe-taojiahuanjia-lkgn0idimbu6twz-symr5012f8sn|Hanji 644]] says Guo Qin and Jiang Xu claim illness, return home, and close their gates until death.
- Gift refusal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-644-shui-gan-he-huangdi-shizhe-taojiahuanjia-lkgn0idimbu6twz-symr5012f8sn|Hanji 644]] says Huan Xiang's son refuses crown-prince funeral gifts because Huan Xiang had instructed against accepting them.
- Serving qualification: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-644-shui-gan-he-huangdi-shizhe-taojiahuanjia-lkgn0idimbu6twz-symr5012f8sn|Hanji 644]] says Ji Qun, Tang Ling, and [[TangZunXinOfficial|唐尊]] serve under Xin and receive titles or respect.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode supplies a moral spectrum but not a full prosopography of all Xin-era scholars. Some figures who serve may have mixed motives, sincere classicist commitments, or tactical reasons not established by this source. The concept should therefore describe Hanji 644's refusal field rather than claim a complete rule for all former Han officials.

## What Changed

- Created from Hanji 644 to group Gong Sheng, Xue Fang, Guo Qin, Jiang Xu, Huan Xiang's family, and Tang Zun into a differentiated Xin-era response pattern.
