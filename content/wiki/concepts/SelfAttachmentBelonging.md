# Self-Attachment and Belonging / 自我依恋与归属

## Definition
Self-attachment and belonging is the practice of becoming a stable inner witness, caregiver, and integrator of one's experience, so belonging is not wholly outsourced to a place, partner, audience, or social platform.

## Current Synthesis
Episode 477 presents external relationships and places as real but partial sources of belonging. They provide experiences, feedback, memories, and care, while the person who continues sensing, reflecting, and organizing those fragments becomes their enduring point of continuity.

The episode uses the image of becoming one's own sufficiently good parent. This does not mean radical self-sufficiency. It means learning to offer oneself steadiness, curiosity, protection, and permission to explore while still receiving healthy dependence, feedback, and care from others.

## Key Claims
- Belonging can be supported externally without being permanently guaranteed by any one relationship or location.
- Self-attachment grows through reflection, recorded experience, realistic feedback, experimentation, goodbye, and repair.
- Mature self-care includes protection and encouragement as well as acceptance of need, grief, and uncertainty.
- External and internal attachment are complementary: healthy relationships can help build capacities later carried within the self.
- Unstable attention platforms are poor substitutes for dependable attachment because their incentives and feedback are volatile.

## Evidence
- Internal integration - [[shi-di-fu-shuo-477-zixunshi-huixin-lijie-shenme-shi-e-caineng-zhudong-xuanze-weishan-lj-c37-abvr5oa0a7vbhvl93pvia]] describes locations, relationships, and experiences as fragments ultimately integrated by the person who lives through them.
- Ideal-parent image - [[shi-di-fu-shuo-477-zixunshi-huixin-lijie-shenme-shi-e-caineng-zhudong-xuanze-weishan-lj-c37-abvr5oa0a7vbhvl93pvia]] defines maturity partly as being able to offer a child secure belonging and to form a similarly stable relationship with oneself.
- Practice - [[shi-di-fu-shuo-477-zixunshi-huixin-lijie-shenme-shi-e-caineng-zhudong-xuanze-weishan-lj-c37-abvr5oa0a7vbhvl93pvia]] recommends trying, communicating, recording, meeting, and separating as ways to build a more coherent self-system rather than seeking a single final external home.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Self-attachment should not become a demand to meet every need alone or a way to blame isolated people for lacking support. Belonging also depends on material safety, accessible community, reciprocal relationships, and social conditions. The ideal-parent metaphor is reflective rather than a clinical protocol.
