# Live-Theatre Co-Presence / 戏剧现场共同在场

## Definition
Live-theatre co-presence is the condition in which performers, audience, space, timing, and production elements share one unfolding event whose feedback and variation cannot be fully reduced to a fixed recording.

## Current Synthesis
[[MengJinghui|孟京辉]] treats a stage work as something regenerated rather than merely reproduced. A new cast requires the director to rehearse again because actors' energy, judgment, and relation to the current moment enter the work; tonight's weak performance can be changed tomorrow. Audience attention, phone use, room scale, light, sound, bodies, and atmosphere also alter what the event becomes.

This does not make recording pointless. High-quality image and sound archives can preserve otherwise lost casts, expand later access, and support study. The source-backed distinction is between preservation and equivalence: an archive can retain much of a production without recreating reciprocal presence in the same room.

## Key Claims
- A live production is renewed through each cast, rehearsal process, venue, audience, and performance date.
- Performer and audience states can modify timing, energy, attention, and interpretation during the event.
- Theatre's revisability lets a production learn across performances instead of ending at one fixed capture.
- Director presence means active aesthetic and relational work, not only an original set of instructions.
- High-quality recording is valuable for preservation and access without becoming a complete substitute for attendance.

## Evidence
- Regeneration through casting: [[xiju-daoyan-mengjinghui-luoyonghao-cong-ai-reshi-de-fanpanzhe-dao-xiju-wutuobang-de-jiangouzhe-lib-ndrl-9td9h0kql7paz834qep]] has Meng say a recurring work must be rehearsed anew because director and actors jointly live on the stage.
- Revisability: [[xiju-daoyan-mengjinghui-luoyonghao-cong-ai-reshi-de-fanpanzhe-dao-xiju-wutuobang-de-jiangouzhe-lib-ndrl-9td9h0kql7paz834qep]] contrasts theatre's ability to change tomorrow with film's fixed capture.
- Audience and attention: [[xiju-daoyan-mengjinghui-luoyonghao-cong-ai-reshi-de-fanpanzhe-dao-xiju-wutuobang-de-jiangouzhe-lib-ndrl-9td9h0kql7paz834qep]] says visible phone use affects actors and weakens the time an audience gives to a performance.
- Archive boundary: [[xiju-daoyan-mengjinghui-luoyonghao-cong-ai-reshi-de-fanpanzhe-dao-xiju-wutuobang-de-jiangouzhe-lib-ndrl-9td9h0kql7paz834qep]] supports high-specification preservation while retaining the claim that mediated viewing is incomplete.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not measure what proportion of theatrical experience a recording preserves or prove that every audience changes every performance. Co-presence can coexist with boredom, distraction, poor access, uneven sightlines, or weak work; liveness is a condition of possibility rather than a guarantee of quality.

## What Changed
- Established a distinction between live regeneration and archival preservation.
