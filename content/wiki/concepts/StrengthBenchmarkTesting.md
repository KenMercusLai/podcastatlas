# Strength Benchmark Testing / 力量基准测试

## Definition
Strength benchmark testing is the episode's set of accessible proxies for maximum force: hand-grip dynamometry, a dead hang, a leg extension at body weight, and a half-body-weight goblet- or front-squat hold, each with a minimum and a good range.

## Current Synthesis
Galpin starts from the simplest proxy, grip strength, and gives concrete thresholds rather than a single number: a minimum of about 40 kilograms for men and 35 kilograms for women, ideally above 60 kilograms, with less than 10% variation between hands. He notes that the non-dominant hand is often stronger, so the comparison is between hands rather than against handedness.

For the lower body he prefers a leg extension over a back squat for average people because it is standardized and not technically demanding, with a target of body weight that drops about 10% per decade after age 40, and with repetition-conversion equations that can estimate a one-rep max when fewer than about five reps are used. A front squat or goblet squat hold is the alternative: about half body weight held at the bottom position for roughly 45 seconds, with beginners starting at one-third of body weight for 30 seconds. Two caveats matter more than the numbers: do not test to maximum without comfort in the technique, and standardize the warm-up so results remain comparable across [[FitnessAssessmentTestingWeek|testing weeks]].

## Key Claims
- Grip strength is the cheapest strength proxy, with roughly 40 kg and 35 kg as minimums for men and women, above 60 kg as an ideal, and less than 10% between-hand variation.
- A dead hang of at least 30 seconds is treated as a minimum, 30 to 50 seconds as good, and above 60 seconds as generally satisfying, while the standard scales imperfectly with body weight.
- A leg extension at body weight is preferred over a back squat for average people because it is standardized and technically undemanding, with about a 10% per-decade decline after age 40.
- A half-body-weight goblet- or front-squat hold for about 45 seconds tests strength together with position, core strength, and low back stability.
- Technique comfort and a standardized warm-up are preconditions for meaningful or safe maximal strength testing.

- Grip thresholds: [[essentials-how-to-assess-improve-all-aspects-of-your-fitness-dr-andy-galpin-scim5641095924]] describes a hand grip dynamometer costing roughly $20 to $100 and gives minimums of about 40 kilograms for men and 35 kilograms for women, ideally above 60 kilograms with under 10% between-hand variation.
- Dead hang: [[essentials-how-to-assess-improve-all-aspects-of-your-fitness-dr-andy-galpin-scim5641095924]] sets a 30-second minimum, 30 to 50 seconds as good, and above 60 seconds as generally satisfying while noting the standard scales imperfectly for very heavy or very light people.
- Leg extension preference: [[essentials-how-to-assess-improve-all-aspects-of-your-fitness-dr-andy-galpin-scim5641095924]] prefers a leg extension over a back squat for average people because it is standardized and not technically demanding, with a body-weight target declining about 10% per decade after 40.
- Squat hold: [[essentials-how-to-assess-improve-all-aspects-of-your-fitness-dr-andy-galpin-scim5641095924]] suggests holding about half body weight at the bottom for roughly 45 seconds and one-third of body weight for 30 seconds for beginners.
- Caveats: [[essentials-how-to-assess-improve-all-aspects-of-your-fitness-dr-andy-galpin-scim5641095924]] warns against testing to maximum without technique comfort and requires a standardized warm-up for retesting.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
These are public benchmark heuristics, not clinical norms, and the episode itself flags several of them as body-size dependent or technique dependent. Grip and dead-hang standards are distorted by body weight and hand size, repetition-conversion equations are estimates, and goblet-hold performance depends on mobility, limb proportions, and experience as much as on strength, so results are best read against a person's own prior tests rather than as universal cutoffs.
