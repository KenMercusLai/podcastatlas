## Definition
中国足球金字塔设计 / Chinese football pyramid design is the problem of connecting city leagues, the [[ChineseChampionsLeague]], [[ChineseLeagueTwo]], [[ChineseLeagueOne]], and [[ChineseSuperLeague]] into a stable pathway with enough matches, club identity, and promotion-relegation continuity.

## Current Synthesis
The source rejects a simple European-copy model because China's base, club culture, and lower-tier economics are different. It treats city leagues as a possible Chinese-style base, but says lower-tier clubs need home fields, more annual matches, and realistic operating loops if they are to become more than short-lived promotion vehicles.

## Key Claims
- China's football pyramid is incomplete, so top-flight expansion depends on strengthening the tiers below it.
- City leagues can act as a practical local base, but they are a different product from professional leagues.
- [[ChineseChampionsLeague]] needs more match supply to reduce randomness and keep clubs active across the year.
- [[ChineseLeagueTwo]] should remain part of a three-tier professional system but has format and fairness risks.
- Home-ground continuity matters because clubs cannot build supporter habits if they remain detached from their city.

## Evidence
- Hybrid base: [[vol-226-zhongchao-kuojun-yu-kuanianzhi-liansai-xiayibu-gai-zenme-zou-ft-chenyong-gkwrimaoef2yadmp9qtvpacu]] says China cannot mechanically copy a European pyramid and should treat city leagues as part of the base.
- Match scarcity: [[vol-226-zhongchao-kuojun-yu-kuanianzhi-liansai-xiayibu-gai-zenme-zou-ft-chenyong-gkwrimaoef2yadmp9qtvpacu]] criticizes the Chinese Champions League for too few games and high randomness.
- Third-tier constraints: [[vol-226-zhongchao-kuojun-yu-kuanianzhi-liansai-xiayibu-gai-zenme-zou-ft-chenyong-gkwrimaoef2yadmp9qtvpacu]] says China League Two should exist but that regional and grouped formats can distort fairness.
- Home connection: [[vol-226-zhongchao-kuojun-yu-kuanianzhi-liansai-xiayibu-gai-zenme-zou-ft-chenyong-gkwrimaoef2yadmp9qtvpacu]] argues that stable home grounds are necessary for clubs to connect with cities and supporters.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source offers structural diagnosis rather than an official pyramid blueprint. It does not settle how city leagues should connect to professional admissions, how many China League Two clubs are financially viable, or whether B teams should be allowed into higher tiers.

## What Changed
- Added a Chinese-specific football-pyramid concept centered on city leagues, lower-tier match supply, and home-ground continuity.
