# Automated Deterrence Interaction Risk

## Definition

Automated deterrence interaction risk is the danger that separately authorized military, allied, and commercial systems respond to one another in ways that widen a crisis beyond the signals and adversaries their planners intended.

## Current Synthesis

[[a-serious-game-a-serious-game|A Serious Game]] derives the risk from a future East Asia simulation. U.S. and Japanese missile defenses are placed on automatic response to signal resolve toward [[China]], but an independently acting [[NorthKorea|North Korea]] fires at Japan and triggers retaliation outside that planning frame. A commercial shipping AI continues serving its market during a limited blockade, while Chinese and American participants interpret sparse machine behavior without shared context.

Later, ambiguity about whether autonomous anti-submarine systems intend to track or attack leads to destruction of an uncrewed aircraft, the sinking of a crewed submarine, and retaliatory missile fire. Automation does not remove human intent or accountability; it changes tempo, creates cross-system coupling, and gives people consequential machine actions whose meaning may remain unclear.

## Key Claims

- Automation authorized for one deterrence relationship can react to an unplanned third actor.
- Independent allied behavior can redirect an automatic response outside the intended signaling frame.
- Commercial optimization can continue during conflict under assumptions that military actors do not share.
- Sparse or surprising machine behavior can increase uncertainty about capability and intent.
- Ambiguous tracking and targeting functions can create escalatory interpretations before human casualties occur.
- Advance human authorization does not eliminate interaction risk among multiple systems and institutions.

## Evidence

### Third-actor trigger and commercial coupling

- [[a-serious-game-a-serious-game|A Serious Game]] describes North Korea triggering an automated U.S.-Japanese response and a commercial shipping AI continuing into a Chinese blockade until a cargo ship is sunk.

### Ambiguity and escalation

- [[a-serious-game-a-serious-game|A Serious Game]] connects uncertain anti-submarine intent to the destruction of an autonomous aircraft, a crewed submarine loss, demands for retaliation, and missile fire.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This is a scenario-derived risk, not evidence that deployed systems have the represented rules or would produce the played sequence. Defensive automation can also reduce response time and intercept attacks, and human review does not guarantee de-escalation. The source does not specify technical architectures, rules of engagement, communication channels, fail-safes, model behavior, cyber compromise, or escalation-control procedures, so it identifies a governance question rather than measuring likelihood.

## What Changed

- Established cross-system and third-actor interaction as a distinct risk beyond individual model accuracy.
- Qualified human-authorization frameworks by separating accountable approval from predictable joint behavior.
