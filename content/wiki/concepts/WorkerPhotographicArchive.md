# Worker Photographic Archive / 工人摄影档案

Worker photographic archive is [[ruci-dagong-30-nian-liushuixian-shang-de-miankong-yu-shijian-lodsuktwtcfxnhmzwum5iesbzbme]]'s method for turning everyday factory life into evidence. [[ZhanTeacher|占老师]] carries a camera through work, streets, factories, commutes, dorms, canteens, and industrial districts, then stores the material in theme folders so future questions can reorganize the archive.

The concept differs from outside documentary extraction. The worker making the archive belongs to the world being photographed, and the repeated act of shooting becomes self-directed labor rather than line-managed repetition. That links the source to [[FieldworkAsKnowledgeMethod]], [[PersonalArchiveAsHistory]], and [[ObservationBeforeInference]]: the archive's value comes from long contact, disciplined accumulation, and later re-reading.

## Key Claims
- A worker archive can preserve labor life that ordinary company records, media stories, and policy summaries miss.
- Repetition is not automatically alienating; repeated photography can build personal knowledge when the worker controls purpose, selection, and later use.
- The archive is strongest as a system rather than as isolated images: themes, dates, places, people, documents, and later captions all matter.
- Blind shooting and everyday observation reduce performance for the camera while raising ethical and interpretive demands.
