# Deep-Time Mental Prosthesis

## Definition
Deep-time mental prosthesis is the source's idea that long-duration artworks and rituals can extend human temporal imagination beyond ordinary biological and social timescales.

## Current Synthesis
In [[salt-020260914-archive-keats-podcast]], [[JonathanKeats]] argues that humans are not naturally equipped to think across thousands of years even though their technologies can affect climate, cities, and environments at that scale. Century cameras, millennium cameras, and [[CenturiesOfTheBristlecone]] matter before their final outputs exist because imagining their future viewers or living timekeeper can already change present attention.

## Key Claims
- Human cognition struggles with millennial-scale consequence.
- Long-duration objects can make future accountability psychologically present.
- The imagined future image or future calendar event can influence choices before any final result is visible.
- Deep-time art works best when it creates a handoff between generations or between human institutions and living systems.

## Evidence
### Future Viewers
- [[salt-020260914-archive-keats-podcast]] describes 100-year cameras hidden in Berlin and San Francisco so future generations can recover the exposure and see urban change compressed into one image.

### Millennial Imagination
- [[salt-020260914-archive-keats-podcast]] says the 1,000-year camera is no longer a direct generational handoff but a way to look into deep time.

### Ecological Calendar
- [[salt-020260914-archive-keats-podcast]] presents [[CenturiesOfTheBristlecone]] as a 5,000-year calendar that uses bristlecone growth to make ecological time legible.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not prove that such artifacts reliably change policy or behavior. Their force is symbolic, participatory, and anticipatory, and their physical survival depends on unresolved material and institutional conditions.

## What Changed
- Added a Long Now concept for speculative artifacts that help people think across centuries and millennia.
- Connected Keats's cameras and bristlecone calendar to future accountability rather than only art history.
