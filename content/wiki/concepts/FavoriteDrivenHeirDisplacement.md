## Definition

Favorite-driven heir displacement / 宠幸驱动的废嫡立庶 is the attempted or completed replacement of an established crown prince with the child of a ruler's favored partner. It is narrower than [[AffectionDrivenSuccessionRisk]] because the consequential step is pressure against an already designated heir.

## Current Synthesis

The bounded cases now show three outcomes rather than a single pattern. [[ZhaoDaoxiangwang|赵悼襄王]] completes the replacement of [[ZhaoJia|赵嘉]] with [[ZhaoYoumiuWang|赵迁]], leaving the displaced branch able to reappear in [[DaiState|代地]] after Zhao's defeat. [[LiuBang|刘邦]] repeatedly considers replacing [[LiuYing|刘盈]] with [[QiJi|戚姬]]'s son [[LiuRuyi|刘如意]], but ministerial resistance and Liu Ying's visible support prevent formal displacement while leaving the favored branch exposed.

Hanji 305-1 adds a vassal-household formation stage. [[XuLaiHengshanQueen|徐来]] wants her son 刘广 to replace [[LiuShuangHengshanCrownPrince|刘爽]], but she does not yet control the settlement. She instead exploits the separate grievances of [[LiuXiaoHengshanPrince|刘孝]] and [[LiuWucaiHengshanPrincess|刘无采]]. The case shows that favorite-driven displacement can first appear as a tactical anti-heir coalition whose members agree on removal but not succession.

## Key Claims

- A ruler's or queen's favored branch can turn private preference into pressure against a publicly designated heir.
- Completed displacement can leave a rival lineage able to mobilize later, as Zhao Jia's Dai remnant shows.
- Ministerial resistance and visible heir support can block replacement even when the ruler repeatedly prefers another son.
- Failed replacement can endanger the favored alternative branch if it becomes visible without gaining durable institutional protection.
- A later queen can recruit sibling resentment and ambition against an heir before she can secure her own preferred successor.
- Shared opposition to an heir does not imply agreement over the post-removal settlement.

## Evidence

Completed replacement and political afterlife:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-111-1-ruoda-de-zhaoguo-jing-wang-yu-yige-jinv-zhi-shou-lhrqb8-swlkuyoydgjyn8ldnlkzc|Qinji 111-1]] reports Zhao Jia's removal in favor of Zhao Qian, whose mother the ruler favored.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-114-1-jintian-liaoliao-yingzheng-dou-you-naxie-canren-shouduan-lmqky6g3qi9nklpdnye4lk5rrbz6|Qinji 114-1]] describes Zhao Jia gathering kin and officials in Dai after Zhao Qian's capture; the captive-ruler constraint on his title remains the host's interpretation.

Resisted replacement and exposed alternative branch:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-181-jiemi-liubang-feili-taizi-de-zhenshi-yuanyin-lgnbvnnfwxe4weupqgz-rh9cbp2s|Hanji 181]] recounts Zhou Chang's refusal and argues that his later Zhao appointment gives Liu Ruyi only thin protection.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-189-liubang-siqian-zuihou-yibo-lllydj2xyk4cr7b9d2qx0hvfw-dz|Hanji 189]] records Zhang Liang's noncooperation, Shusun Tong's remonstrance, and the Shangshan elders' visible support for Liu Ying.

Coalition formation before displacement:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-305-1-yin-chugui-lihun-de-ta-weihe-yao-mouhai-qinge-ljbwegjf-hve9we3gq0mrbipjvnp|Hanji 305-1]] says Xu Lai wants her own son to replace Liu Shuang and recruits Liu Xiao and Liu Wucai into repeated denunciation of the crown prince.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-305-1-yin-chugui-lihun-de-ta-weihe-yao-mouhai-qinge-ljbwegjf-hve9we3gq0mrbipjvnp|Hanji 305-1]] also distinguishes their aims: Liu Xiao wants the heirship, while Liu Wucai seeks freedom from her brother's control.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Only the Zhao case records completed formal replacement in these bounded inputs. The Liu Bang and Hengshan cases are attempts or formation stages. Zhao's fall also involved war, command decisions, and [[QinEastwardPressure|Qin pressure]], so the Qinji title's blame of one woman is stigmatizing single-cause rhetoric rather than this page's conclusion. Hanji 305-1 stops before the coalition acts on the title's fratricide promise and contains transcript identity errors normalized on the linked entity pages. Five notes from one interpretive podcast series do not provide independent corroboration or a general causal estimate.

## What Changed

- Added coalition formation as a pre-displacement stage, not merely completed or resisted replacement.
- Added the Hengshan distinction between shared anti-heir action and conflicting preferred successors.
- Preserved the three outcomes: completed Zhao replacement, resisted Liu Bang attempt, and unresolved Hengshan pressure.
- Tightened source boundaries around gendered blame, transcript errors, and causal overreach.
