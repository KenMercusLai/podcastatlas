## Definition

Favorite-driven heir displacement / 宠幸驱动的废嫡立庶 is the attempted or completed replacement of an established crown prince with the child of a ruler's favored partner. It is narrower than [[AffectionDrivenSuccessionRisk]] because the consequential step is pressure against an already designated heir.

## Current Synthesis

The bounded cases show three distinct outcomes. [[ZhaoDaoxiangwang|赵悼襄王]] completes the replacement of [[ZhaoJia|赵嘉]] with [[ZhaoYoumiuWang|赵迁]], leaving the displaced branch able to reappear in [[DaiState|代地]] after Zhao's defeat. [[LiuBang|刘邦]] repeatedly considers replacing [[LiuYing|刘盈]] with [[QiJi|戚姬]]'s son [[LiuRuyi|刘如意]], but ministerial resistance and Liu Ying's visible support prevent formal displacement while leaving the favored branch exposed.

The Hengshan sequence supplies a destructive non-settlement. [[XuLaiHengshanQueen|徐来]] recruits [[LiuXiaoHengshanPrince|刘孝]] and [[LiuWucaiHengshanPrincess|刘无采]] against [[LiuShuangHengshanCrownPrince|刘爽]] while preferring her own son Liu Guang. Hanji 305-2 makes the incompatibility operational: Xu Lai compromises Liu Xiao, [[LiuCiHengshanKingWesternHan|刘赐]] petitions to promote him, and reciprocal denunciations expose an alleged conspiracy. No alternative heir becomes secure; the ruling principals die and the [[HengshanKingdomWesternHan|kingdom]] is abolished.

## Key Claims

- A ruler's or queen's favored branch can turn private preference into pressure against a publicly designated heir.
- Completed displacement can leave a rival lineage able to mobilize later, as Zhao Jia's Dai remnant shows.
- Ministerial resistance and visible heir support can block replacement even when the ruler repeatedly prefers another son.
- Failed replacement can endanger both the favored alternative branch and the established heir when neither gains durable institutional protection.
- A later queen can recruit sibling resentment and ambition against an heir without accepting an ally's preferred successor.
- Shared opposition can collapse into reciprocal exposure, producing no succession settlement and inviting intervention by superior authority.

## Evidence

Completed replacement and political afterlife:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-111-1-ruoda-de-zhaoguo-jing-wang-yu-yige-jinv-zhi-shou-lhrqb8-swlkuyoydgjyn8ldnlkzc|Qinji 111-1]] reports Zhao Jia's removal in favor of Zhao Qian, whose mother the ruler favored.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-114-1-jintian-liaoliao-yingzheng-dou-you-naxie-canren-shouduan-lmqky6g3qi9nklpdnye4lk5rrbz6|Qinji 114-1]] describes Zhao Jia gathering kin and officials in Dai after Zhao Qian's capture; the captive-ruler constraint on his title remains the host's interpretation.

Resisted replacement and exposed alternative branch:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-181-jiemi-liubang-feili-taizi-de-zhenshi-yuanyin-lgnbvnnfwxe4weupqgz-rh9cbp2s|Hanji 181]] recounts Zhou Chang's refusal and argues that his later Zhao appointment gives Liu Ruyi only thin protection.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-189-liubang-siqian-zuihou-yibo-lllydj2xyk4cr7b9d2qx0hvfw-dz|Hanji 189]] records Zhang Liang's noncooperation, Shusun Tong's remonstrance, and the Shangshan elders' visible support for Liu Ying.

Coalition formation and destructive non-settlement:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-305-1-yin-chugui-lihun-de-ta-weihe-yao-mouhai-qinge-ljbwegjf-hve9we3gq0mrbipjvnp|Hanji 305-1]] says Xu Lai wants Liu Guang to replace Liu Shuang and recruits Liu Xiao and Liu Wucai into repeated denunciation despite their divergent aims.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-305-2-hengshanwang-liuci-jing-yin-erzi-gouyin-xiaoma-wuguo-lkkfwvjh-kfxcr1rhroigrpbdmh2q|Hanji 305-2]] says Xu Lai compromises Liu Xiao, Liu Ci seeks to promote him, and the resulting denunciations end in investigation, deaths, and abolition rather than stable replacement.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Only the Zhao case records completed formal replacement in these bounded inputs. Liu Bang's attempt is blocked, while the Hengshan case destroys every principal succession branch before a durable alternative is installed. Zhao's fall also involves war, command decisions, and [[QinEastwardPressure|Qin pressure]], so the Qinji title's blame of one woman is stigmatizing single-cause rhetoric rather than this page's conclusion. The Hengshan notes are consecutive parts of one popular retelling, not independent corroboration; their sexual allegations, exact motives, conspiracy details, punishment sequence, and claim that the kingdom became 衡山郡 remain source-scoped. Six notes from one interpretive podcast series do not provide a general causal estimate.

## What Changed

- Reclassified Hengshan from an unresolved coalition-formation stage to a destructive non-settlement.
- Added reciprocal exposure as a possible outcome when anti-heir allies disagree over the replacement.
- Clarified that failed displacement can destroy the established heir, rival candidates, and the governing house together.
