# Intelligence Testing / 智力测试

Intelligence testing / 智力测试 is the broad measurement practice traced in [[37-zhishang-ceshi-qingwen-ni-shi-zhili-poluomen-ma-608020679]]. The episode follows it from [[FrancisGalton|Francis Galton / 弗朗西斯·高尔顿]]'s measurement culture and hereditarian ambitions to [[AlfredBinet|Alfred Binet / 阿尔弗雷德·宾内]] and [[TheodoreSimon|Theodore Simon / 西奥多·西蒙]]'s child-assessment work, then into American schools, immigration, the army, courts, and state policy.

The concept is not treated as pure fraud. The source accepts that people differ in cognitive strengths and development. Its concern is institutional use: a test that can help an individual understand ability becomes harmful when the score is used for [[PsychometricSocialSorting|psychometric social sorting]], [[EducationalTrackingByTesting|educational tracking]], immigration exclusion, or [[ForcedSterilizationByEugenics|forced sterilization]].

## Key Claims
- Intelligence testing becomes politically important when the score affects school resources, entry, employment, citizenship, reproduction, or dignity.
- Early school-support uses differ morally from later rank-and-exclusion uses, even when the measurement language looks similar.
- IQ comparisons across class, country, race, or culture are especially vulnerable to [[CulturalBiasInTesting]].
- The source's practical boundary is self-knowledge without contempt: a test can guide personal direction, but cannot assign human worth.
