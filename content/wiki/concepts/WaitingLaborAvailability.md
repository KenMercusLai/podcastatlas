# Waiting Labor as Availability / 作为待命的等待劳动

## Definition
Waiting labor as availability is paid or unpaid time in which a worker must remain ready at a workplace or within reach even when no transaction or visible task is occurring.

## Current Synthesis
Hospitality output is easy to count through drinks, meals, or sales, but the service promise also depends on someone being present before demand is known. In the episode's Spanish bars, opening from morning until after midnight made quiet time part of the operating model. Workers could not reliably convert that interval into language study, rest, social life, or another job because attention, location, and response remained constrained.

Waiting labor is not identical to inactivity. Cleaning, stocking, monitoring, and customer preparation may fill part of it, while the remaining readiness still has opportunity cost. Analysis should therefore distinguish active tasks, constrained standby, genuinely free breaks, ownership returns, and employee compensation.

## Key Claims
- Customer-facing businesses produce availability as well as completed transactions.
- No-customer time remains labor when the worker's location and response are constrained.
- Very long opening hours can make low-intensity intervals cumulatively exhausting.
- Standby time restricts language learning, education, leisure, and alternative employment.
- Owners and employees may bear waiting differently because ownership returns and control are unequal.
- Measuring only visible transactions systematically understates the time required to operate the service.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not provide time-use logs or distinguish every active task, break, owner choice, and employee obligation. Quiet time can sometimes permit conversation or light personal activity, and not all standby has equal intensity. The claim is about constrained time and opportunity cost, not continuous maximum exertion.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to make availability and opportunity cost visible in long-hours hospitality work.
