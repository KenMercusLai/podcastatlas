# People-First Economic Statecraft / 富民优先的经济治国

People-first economic statecraft / 富民优先的经济治国 is the constructive side of Guan Zhong's economics in [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-75-3-gudai-de-jingji-zhanzheng-neng-you-duo-kepa-liybgoz8lspypax6dhuke2js0pxf]]. The episode summarizes Guan Zhong's answer to [[QiHuanGong|齐桓公]] as a claim that a strong state must first make the people materially secure.

The source treats this as more than moral rhetoric. Guan Zhong is presented as favoring production, circulation, trade, reasonable consumption, and reduced pressure on agriculture because farming is necessary but hard to expand quickly and vulnerable to weather. In that frame, the state should not confuse treasury extraction with national strength: a rich government with poor people is politically brittle.

This concept also explains why Guan Zhong can fight economic wars. The same sensitivity to food, work, trade, and movement that supports Qi's people lets [[QiState|齐国]] detect and exploit the fragility of rival states that neglect grain production.

## Key Claims
- Livelihood is the basis of state power: food and income precede stable order.
- Economic statecraft includes production, trade, circulation, and consumption, not only taxation.
- Agricultural taxes should be handled carefully because farming is essential but vulnerable and slow to scale.
- A state that lets people become poor risks flight, disorder, and lost political security.
- Guan Zhong's offensive economic warfare depends on the same insight: people follow survivable prices and food access.
