## Definition
Intergenerational responsibility transition is the asymmetric family shift in which adults retain obligations to support older parents while increasingly treating children as independent people who should not be required to fund or organize the adults' own old age.

## Current Synthesis
[[zanmen-80hou-yige-daiji-yu-zhongguo-zhuigan-de-40nian-lkwrwuj1jcfxwte6dbvt1elvgl6e]] presents many 80后 households as a hinge between family models. They may send money, goods, time, and emergency care upward while spending heavily on children and granting those children more autonomy than they themselves received. Urban migration, smaller families, longer lives, and low fertility make this ethic harder to deliver: responsibility persists even when relatives live far apart, while the expectation of reciprocal support from the next generation weakens.

## Key Claims
- Traditional upward duty can coexist with modern downward respect for independence.
- Family consumption often hides care labor because spending for parents and children is recorded as household consumption rather than personal sacrifice.
- Migration can improve opportunity while increasing the time, travel, and emotional cost of elder care.
- Smaller families and longer lives make informal care expectations harder to sustain without savings, services, technology, or public support.
- Refusing to treat children as retirement instruments does not remove the need for a credible later-life care plan.

## Evidence
- **Asymmetric expectation:** [[zanmen-80hou-yige-daiji-yu-zhongguo-zhuigan-de-40nian-lkwrwuj1jcfxwte6dbvt1elvgl6e]] describes speakers who accept responsibility for parents but do not expect fixed financial or care support from their children.
- **Responsibility-led consumption:** [[zanmen-80hou-yige-daiji-yu-zhongguo-zhuigan-de-40nian-lkwrwuj1jcfxwte6dbvt1elvgl6e]] reports frequent purchases for family members alongside restrained personal spending.
- **Migration distance:** [[zanmen-80hou-yige-daiji-yu-zhongguo-zhuigan-de-40nian-lkwrwuj1jcfxwte6dbvt1elvgl6e]] describes economic support and phone contact as ordinary long-distance care, with travel home concentrated around serious illness.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept describes an emerging family ethic, not a universal Chinese household pattern. Expectations vary by region, class, gender, sibling structure, health, housing, and access to formal care. The episode's optimism about care robots concerns possible efficiency gains and does not establish affordability, quality, emotional adequacy, or equal access.

## What Changed
- Created an asymmetric responsibility model linking elder support, child autonomy, and self-funded aging.
- Added migration distance and family-size change as constraints on informal care.
