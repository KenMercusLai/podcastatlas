# Claim Restraint Through Attachment / 有所归属式名位节制

Claim restraint through attachment / 有所归属式名位节制 is the pattern [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-130-1-guailaotou-yiqiao-san-shua-zhangliang-chuanshou-shenshu-lqwjo13zzfgtndpajzpuvrb2h4l2|Qinji 130-1]] draws from [[ChenYing|陈英 / 陈婴]] and his mother. In a moment when many anti-[[QinState|秦国]] actors can claim old titles, Chen Ying is advised not to become king but to attach himself to a stronger leader.

The logic is asymmetric risk. If the attached side succeeds, Chen Ying can still receive reward; if it fails, he is less likely to be singled out as the principal rebel. The source frames this as knowing "degree" rather than simply lacking ambition.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-130-2-lishi-jilu-shouci-tucheng-lm4kuzdpnqn-hicyuvvb4p2wdudm|Qinji 130-2]] makes the reward side explicit. Chen Ying does not only avoid immediate danger; after the later Chu-Han outcome, he surrenders to [[LiuBang|刘邦]], receives a marquisate, serves in the Han-linked Chu kingdom, and has descendants tied to the royal house by marriage. The pattern is therefore not pure retreat. It is a way to keep optionality when independent kingship would make the person too visible.

This differs from [[TitleRealityRestraint|名实相符式名号节制]]. Title-reality restraint refuses a title because the title lacks substance; claim restraint through attachment refuses top status because the claimant lacks enough lineage, backing, or risk capacity to survive being named as the center.

It also differs from [[PowerExitTrap|权力退场困境]]. Power-exit cases often concern leaving after authority has accumulated. This pattern concerns not entering the highest-risk position in the first place.

## Key Claims
- In a fragmented rebellion, taking the highest title can create more exposure than power.
- Attachment to a stronger claimant can preserve reward upside while reducing blame concentration.
- Qinji 130-2 adds that attachment can become long-term elite integration if the attached side or a later successor regime wins.
- Family reputation and ancestral standing can matter as political risk signals, not only as status decoration.
- The source uses Chen Ying's restraint to echo [[ZhangLiang|张良]]'s broader lesson of measured action.
