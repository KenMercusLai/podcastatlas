# Investment Adviser Fiduciary Duty / 投资顾问信义义务

## Definition

Investment adviser fiduciary duty is the principle that a financial adviser or asset manager entrusted with client decisions must put client interests ahead of conflicting personal or institutional revenue opportunities, while disclosing, controlling, or avoiding conflicts.

## Current Synthesis

Episode 145 uses the U.S. Investment Advisers Act, registration, disclosure, supervision, and a larger advisory ecosystem to explain why client-interest obligations matter for trust. It also warns that legal language does not eliminate misconduct and that China's fund-advisory system still faces thin profiling, opaque recommendation conflicts, fee skepticism, and weak post-purchase service.

Episode 187 broadens the concept from advisory law to financial professional ethics. Client-first conduct includes refusing unauthorized products, avoiding insider or occupational conflicts, understanding the purpose of expert management, and building institutional culture and controls that make ethical duties operational rather than personal promises. Fiduciary duty is therefore a governance system, not a claim that credentials or morality make products safe.

## Key Claims

- **Client interest must govern entrusted judgment.** Advice should not become a disguised product-distribution channel.
- **Conflicts require more than disclosure language.** Compensation, related products, occupational information, and unauthorized sales need supervision, avoidance, or enforceable controls.
- **Legal duty and institutional culture are complementary.** Rules, registration, monitoring, and professional norms make client-first conduct more durable.
- **Fiduciary status is not a guarantee of performance or honesty.** Due diligence, suitability, and enforcement remain necessary.
- **Expert management carries a purpose obligation.** The institution and individual should understand that customer welfare, not transaction completion, is the role's endpoint.
- **Crisis conduct tests trust.** Refusals, transparent communication, and disciplined handling of loss matter alongside portfolio selection.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The U.S. comparison is source-scoped and not proof that one jurisdiction or credential eliminates misconduct. Fiduciary rules differ by service, license, contract, and jurisdiction. Client-first duties also do not remove investment risk or guarantee returns; they improve the decision process and accountability around advice.

## What Changed

- Expanded the concept from adviser regulation to institutional culture and individual professional ethics.
- Added unauthorized selling, occupational information, and insider conflicts to the control boundary.
- Clarified that fiduciary duty structures accountability but does not authenticate every product or outcome.
