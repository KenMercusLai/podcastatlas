# Bias Blind Spot

## Definition
The bias blind spot is the tendency to recognize distorted judgment in other people more readily than in oneself.

## Current Synthesis
[[AdamGrant]] argues that strong cognitive ability does not automatically protect against this problem. Repeated praise for being a good thinker may instead make a person more skilled at defending an initial view and more confident that bias belongs elsewhere.

Correction therefore requires evidence that does not depend solely on introspection. Reflected-best-self stories can reveal overlooked strengths, while reasoned disagreement, advice from several observers, and [[ScientistModeThinking]] can expose weaknesses or assumptions that self-inspection misses.

## Key Claims
- People can identify bias generally while exempting their own reasoning from the same scrutiny.
- Cognitive skill can improve rationalization as well as error detection.
- Multiple outside perspectives can reveal strengths and weaknesses hidden from first-person view.
- Treating beliefs as revisable hypotheses creates a practical route around self-certainty.

## Evidence
- Self-exemption - [[how-to-unlock-your-potential-motivation-unique-abilities-dr-adam-grant-scim2670765142]] defines the bias blind spot as believing oneself less biased than others.
- Ability qualification - [[how-to-unlock-your-potential-motivation-unique-abilities-dr-adam-grant-scim2670765142]] says highly capable thinkers may be especially vulnerable after reinforcement for being right.
- Outside-view practices - [[how-to-unlock-your-potential-motivation-unique-abilities-dr-adam-grant-scim2670765142]] connects reflected-best-self stories and principled disagreement to discovery of hidden qualities and assumptions.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Outside observers also carry biases, shared incentives, limited context, and relationship dynamics. Agreement among several people is stronger evidence than one opinion but not proof. The source provides a practical interview-level account rather than full measurement criteria or evidence that any single exercise reliably removes bias.

## What Changed
- Created a bounded account of self-exemption and outside-view correction.
