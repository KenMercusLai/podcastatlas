## Definition

Claimant-impostor crisis handling is the court-politics pattern in which a person claiming royal identity creates immediate legitimacy danger before the court can fully verify truth, forcing officials to decide whether to prioritize identity investigation, public order, dynastic continuity, or legal containment.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-396-2-hanwudi-zhizi-liuju-wugu-zhi-an-hou-fuhuo-le-lhj6dzjsikkmbbcjlfgnz1bbok-1|Hanji 396-2]] provides the current case. A man appears at [[WeiyangPalace|未央宫]] claiming to be [[LiuJuCrownPrinceWesternHan|卫太子刘据]], whose death after the [[WuguIncidentWesternHan|巫蛊之祸]] remains politically charged. Senior officials hesitate because the claimant resembles Liu Ju, crowds gather, and the young [[EmperorZhaoOfHan|汉昭帝刘弗陵]]'s reign still depends on stable recognition.

[[JunBuyiWesternHan|隽不疑]] resolves the problem by changing the decision frame. He does not wait for perfect recognition; he arrests the claimant and argues that even a true surviving Liu Ju would still be legally and politically unusable after offending the former emperor and fleeing. The later confession that the man is [[ChengFangsuiWesternHan|程方遂]] proves the fraud, but the mechanism matters before the confession: when resemblance and rumor threaten succession order, the court can contain the claimant first and verify later.

## Key Claims

- A royal-identity claim can become politically dangerous before its truth is known when it touches a recent succession trauma.
- Resemblance can paralyze officials because denying a possible royal survivor carries personal and institutional risk.
- Public crowd formation turns an identity dispute into a palace-security and legitimacy problem.
- Crisis handling may require reframing the question from "is he real?" to "what status could this claimant legally hold even if real?"
- Former-ruler authority and current dynastic continuity can be used to block a claimant from becoming politically usable.
- Verification still matters, but in the source it comes after containment rather than before action.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The current evidence is a single Western Han case. The source's reasoning depends on the specific Wugu-affair background and on Jun Buyi's claim that even a true surviving Liu Ju would be politically and legally disabled. The concept should not be generalized into a rule that every claimant can be arrested without inquiry; it names a crisis pattern where waiting for perfect verification can itself create disorder.
