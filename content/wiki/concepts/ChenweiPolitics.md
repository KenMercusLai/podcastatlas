## Definition

Chenwei politics / 谶纬政治 is the use of prophetic, omen, and apocryphal interpretation as political evidence. It can legitimate a ruler, justify a policy, make a claimant's ambition seem fated, or manufacture public belief; its force comes less from proving supernatural truth than from making uncertain political action appear cosmically authorized.

## Current Synthesis

The Wang Mang and fangshu sources define the core environment. [[126-wangmang-zhisi-shensheng-diguo-de-huixuanbiao-he-huixuanbiao-de-huixuanbiao-786255780]] treats [[WangMang|王莽]] as a product of late Western Han sacred legitimacy, where [[MandateOfHeavenLegitimacy|天命]], [[AuspiciousOmenPolitics|祥瑞]], moral reputation, and ritual architecture make dynastic replacement imaginable. [[87-guonian-zui-shihe-liao-xuanxue-tantao-shenxian-fangshu-fazhanshi-jie-shangji-704051550]] places chenwei inside a wider Han field of star signs, disasters, rainmaking, yin-yang/five phases, fangshu, and state ideology, using [[LiuXiu|刘秀]] to show prophecy can support restoration and then be recentralized once private interpretation becomes dangerous.

The Qin source notes are precursors rather than mature Han chenwei. In [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-123-1-shen-zhi-yuyan-wangqin-zhe-hu-jing-shi-zhi-lg7m3vfdbcweb4joxe4jvhxjx6y8|Qinji 123-1]], "亡秦者胡" arrives through Lu Sheng's fangshi channel and becomes a policy-triggering warning aimed at the northern Xiongnu in Qin Shi Huang's moment, while later readers can reinterpret "Hu" through Hu Hai. In [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-128-2-qilai-bei-yapo-de-renmen-lpx9-iicwog4nj-nudaj-i4ajb25|Qinji 128-2]], Chen Sheng and Wu Guang manufacture signs through fish-belly writing and fox cries, showing a bottom-up rebel version of mandate-looking sign production.

Later cases show the same political grammar without requiring one stable doctrine. [[169-songtaizu-zhisi-zhuying-fusheng-yinmoulun-zhong-de-renxing-mituan-shang-957052909]] treats the "点检做天子" rumor before Chenqiao as a prophecy-like signal that makes command change and dynastic transfer thinkable. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-950-budong-quanli-beihou-de-renxing-bi-bai-wuyi-lu8-u78yqvvwwarsz8dlevvzchxr|Hanji 950]] shows [[DongZhuo|董卓]] using apocryphal prophecy to dress a security-driven Luoyang-to-Chang'an relocation as Heaven-backed necessity. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1001-weishenme-quan-ni-buyao-fanzhongnu-lolurkcjpie14nnczk0tu7iqerke|Hanji 1001]] shows [[YuanShu|袁术]] reading "代汉者当涂高," five-phases ancestry, and the imperial seal as self-flattering authorization that his advisers do not accept.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-730-zhongguo-baijiaxing-juran-you-diwu-xing-lsm3xean8fzidhhhxi6-ppphexxh|Hanji 730]] makes the Liu Xiu branch more concrete. Liu Xiu reads the `Hetu Huichang Fu` line "赤刘之九，会命岱宗," orders officials to collect related river-map and Luo-writing passages, and accepts Taishan fengshan after thirty-six references are found. The episode therefore shows chenwei moving from legitimacy atmosphere into direct ritual state action: prophecy does not merely praise the dynasty; it helps decide that the emperor should travel, sacrifice, seal jade documents, and announce a new era.

Across the current evidence, chenwei politics is strongest when a sign or text links cosmic order to an actionable institutional setting: accession, relocation, campaign, provincial destination, rebellion, purge, or ritual. It is also unstable. The same sign can be reinterpreted after events, fabricated from below, resisted by advisers, or used to hide more material motives such as security, prestige, or coalition weakness.

## Key Claims

- Prophecy and omen interpretation can function as political technology by turning uncertain action into Heaven-linked necessity.
- Han sacred politics made chenwei especially useful because legitimacy, moral reputation, auspicious signs, and ritual order already interacted inside court life.
- Qin precursor cases show the same grammar before mature chenwei: warning texts can trigger policy, and fabricated signs can make rebellion look mandate-backed.
- Prophecy-like signals can pressure military appointment, capital relocation, and dynastic transfer even when participants may understand the manipulation.
- Claimants can bend vague prophecy toward themselves, but the argument still depends on adviser, elite, and public acceptance.
- Hanji 730 shows chenwei can trigger ritual state action, not only accession claims or speech-risk disputes.
- The system remains contestable because signs can be fabricated, reinterpreted in hindsight, or used to package practical motives as cosmic command.

## Evidence

- Han sacred-legitimacy setting: [[126-wangmang-zhisi-shensheng-diguo-de-huixuanbiao-he-huixuanbiao-de-huixuanbiao-786255780]] uses Wang Mang to show how auspicious signs, petitions, moral image, and Confucian-sacred politics make dynastic transfer thinkable.
- Fangshu and restoration setting: [[87-guonian-zui-shihe-liao-xuanxue-tantao-shenxian-fangshu-fazhanshi-jie-shangji-704051550]] places chenwei inside Han star-omen, disaster, fangshu, and Confucian-imperial vocabulary, and uses Liu Xiu as both beneficiary and later controller of prophecy.
- Qin warning-text precursor: [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-123-1-shen-zhi-yuyan-wangqin-zhe-hu-jing-shi-zhi-lg7m3vfdbcweb4joxe4jvhxjx6y8|Qinji 123-1]] says Lu Sheng's "亡秦者胡" report helps Qin Shi Huang frame northern action while leaving room for later Hu Hai reinterpretation.
- Rebel sign manufacture: [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-128-2-qilai-bei-yapo-de-renmen-lpx9-iicwog4nj-nudaj-i4ajb25|Qinji 128-2]] says Chen Sheng and Wu Guang use fish-belly writing and fox cries to make a desperate uprising appear fated.
- Military-rumor founding signal: [[169-songtaizu-zhisi-zhuying-fusheng-yinmoulun-zhong-de-renxing-mituan-shang-957052909]] treats "点检做天子" as a prophecy-like signal in the Later Zhou-to-Song command transition.
- Relocation packaging: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-950-budong-quanli-beihou-de-renxing-bi-bai-wuyi-lu8-u78yqvvwwarsz8dlevvzchxr|Hanji 950]] says Dong Zhuo invokes apocryphal prophecy to make a security-driven relocation to Chang'an sound cosmically and popularly required.
- Claimant self-authorization: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1001-weishenme-quan-ni-buyao-fanzhongnu-lolurkcjpie14nnczk0tu7iqerke|Hanji 1001]] says Yuan Shu reads "代汉者当涂高," five-phases ancestry, and the imperial seal as support for imperial ambition, while advisers resist the leap.
- Ritual policy trigger: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-730-zhongguo-baijiaxing-juran-you-diwu-xing-lsm3xean8fzidhhhxi6-ppphexxh|Hanji 730]] says Liu Xiu's reading of chenwei and the discovered thirty-six passages lead him to approve and perform Taishan fengshan.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The Qin examples are precursors and should not be flattened into the mature Han chenwei system described by later sources. None of the source notes requires treating prophecy as literally true; their shared claim is political efficacy, not supernatural proof. Hanji 950 and Hanji 1001 show that prophecy can package motives already present in security, ambition, prestige, or institutional weakness. Qinji 128-2 shows sign production can be deliberately fabricated, while Qinji 123-1 shows the same phrase can carry different meanings in the moment and in hindsight. Hanji 730 makes Liu Xiu's fengshan a chenwei-triggered action, but it also preserves older ritual precedent, court proposals, and the emperor's frugality as part of the decision field.
