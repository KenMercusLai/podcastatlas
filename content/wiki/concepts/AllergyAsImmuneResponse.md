# Allergy As Immune Response

## Definition
Allergy as immune response is the medical-literacy frame that allergic disease comes from the body's response to environmental substances, not from moral fragility, weak character, or ordinary irritability.

## Current Synthesis
The wiki now has three complementary but unequal allergy frames. [[97-huafen-zheng-yu-renlei-cong-niandeteren-aqiu-dao-kongqi-jinghuaqi-728113059]] treats hay fever historically, showing how pollen allergy became legible through plants, class, medicine, urbanization, domestic technology, and public-environment management. [[vol-201-weishenme-jinjinian-guomin-de-ren-yuelaiyue-duo-guomin-tongshike-lgmmw7ejv8ulj0y5gokkv7wieu0z]] makes the clinical version explicit: allergy is not strong immunity, but [[AllergyImmuneImbalance|immune imbalance]] and failed tolerance toward otherwise harmless substances. [[vol-154-chuntian-yang-dao-naoxin-poju-guomin-jie-de-shengcun-zhinan-lnxaxys733qzi9rkcwvgqt6uzfur]] offers an earlier TCM-oriented public explanation of over-sensitive protection, seasonal exposure, and individual variation.

Together, the sources argue against both dismissal and simple eradication. Allergy can require symptom relief, exposure management, environmental source control, and chronic medical management, but the response must respect immune-system tradeoffs, ecological tradeoffs, individual triggers, medication boundaries, evidence quality, and quality-of-life burden. TCM pattern language can be recorded as the guest's interpretive framework without being promoted to an established immunological mechanism.

## Key Claims
- Allergy should not be reduced to weakness, moral fragility, "矫情", or simple overreaction.
- Harmless external substances can become harmful through an individual's immune response and loss of tolerance.
- Historical hay-fever recognition required linking symptoms, seasonality, pollen, exposure, and immune interpretation.
- Clinical management must distinguish immune imbalance, environmental exposure, medication indication, and chronic disease burden.
- Source-control and avoidance strategies matter, but they can fail or backfire when ecology, household exposure, or new triggers are ignored.
- Attempts to eliminate all allergic disease need caution because immune responses also have protective and regulatory functions.
- Public self-care advice should preserve emergency escalation and distinguish established care from source-scoped traditional or experiential claims.

## Evidence
- Historical immune interpretation: [[97-huafen-zheng-yu-renlei-cong-niandeteren-aqiu-dao-kongqi-jinghuaqi-728113059]] shows hay fever moving from vague seasonal suffering and class-coded illness toward pollen exposure and immune response.
- Clinical immune-imbalance correction: [[vol-201-weishenme-jinjinian-guomin-de-ren-yuelaiyue-duo-guomin-tongshike-lgmmw7ejv8ulj0y5gokkv7wieu0z]] has [[WangHuiyingAllergyDoctor|汪惠英]] explain that allergy is not immune strength but loss of immune balance and tolerance.
- Management implications: [[97-huafen-zheng-yu-renlei-cong-niandeteren-aqiu-dao-kongqi-jinghuaqi-728113059]] emphasizes environmental management, air purification, and ecological caution, while [[vol-201-weishenme-jinjinian-guomin-de-ren-yuelaiyue-duo-guomin-tongshike-lgmmw7ejv8ulj0y5gokkv7wieu0z]] adds medication, pregnancy, pets, dust mites, asthma, dermatitis, stress, sleep, and long-term care.
- Seasonal self-care boundary: [[vol-154-chuntian-yang-dao-naoxin-poju-guomin-jie-de-shengcun-zhinan-lnxaxys733qzi9rkcwvgqt6uzfur]] adds pollen, dust-mite, mold, cleaning, mask, testing, medicine-sedation, and breathing-difficulty claims while explicitly locating diet, tea, herbs, acupressure, and TCM patterns in a source-scoped framework.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The three sources are public educational podcast notes, not full clinical guidelines. They do not specify diagnostic criteria, dosing, immunotherapy protocols, biologic indications, a complete anaphylaxis protocol, or quantified exposure-control effects. Claims about hygiene, COVID-era infection, hormones, stress, lifestyle, “发物,” TCM patterns, teas, herbs, aloe, acupressure, and tap-water nasal rinsing remain source-scoped and differ substantially in evidentiary status.

## What Changed
- Added the earlier VOL.154 TCM-oriented explanation without equating its pattern language or remedies with the later clinical-immunology model.
- Added emergency escalation and evidence quality to the self-care boundary.
