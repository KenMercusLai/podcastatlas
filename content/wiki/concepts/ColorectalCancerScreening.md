# Colorectal Cancer Screening

## Definition
Colorectal cancer screening is the structured use of stool testing, blood or risk tools, colonoscopy, and polyp removal to detect colorectal cancer or precancerous lesions early.

## Current Synthesis
The sources frame colorectal screening as neither a symptom checklist nor a tumor-marker shortcut. Early tumors and polyps can be silent, so risk-based screening should not wait for obvious bleeding or bowel change. At the same time, new blood, narrowed stool, pain, or a sustained change from one's own bowel baseline can create a diagnostic route that is more urgent than routine screening.

The test hierarchy remains clear: tumor markers cannot rule cancer out, fecal occult-blood testing is a useful low-friction screen that can route positive results onward, emerging blood and AI tools may concentrate high-risk groups, and colonoscopy remains the gold standard when direct diagnosis or polyp removal is needed. The prevention logic is especially important because identifying and removing precancerous polyps can interrupt progression. Family history and prior polyps can alter the timing and intensity of evaluation, while a technically poor bowel preparation weakens the reassurance a colonoscopy can provide.

## Key Claims
- Colorectal-cancer screening matters because early disease and polyps can be silent for years.
- Screening of people without symptoms and evaluation of warning symptoms are complementary but different pathways.
- Tumor markers are insufficient as stand-alone early-cancer screening or reassurance.
- Stool testing can detect bleeding or inflammation clues and can help route people toward further evaluation.
- Colonoscopy remains the source's gold-standard diagnostic test for colorectal cancer.
- China-facing source discussions emphasize midlife screening, but their stated age thresholds differ; family history, prior polyps, pathology, and examination quality can alter the route.
- Screening is meant to find cancer and precancerous polyps, not only explain current symptoms.

## Evidence
- Silent progression: [[vol-203-kao-bingmeishi-tongbian-yi-jinzhang-jiu-fuxie-nide-cuipi-changdao-xuyao-zhefen-shiyongshu-lvtxc2z3hn5ukh3va9rzijremcih]] notes that polyps can remain asymptomatic for years and that tumor symptoms may worsen stepwise.
- Tumor-marker limit: [[vol-203-kao-bingmeishi-tongbian-yi-jinzhang-jiu-fuxie-nide-cuipi-changdao-xuyao-zhefen-shiyongshu-lvtxc2z3hn5ukh3va9rzijremcih]] says tumor markers can be normal even in early, middle, or advanced disease and cannot prove absence of tumor.
- Colonoscopy and stool tests: [[vol-203-kao-bingmeishi-tongbian-yi-jinzhang-jiu-fuxie-nide-cuipi-changdao-xuyao-zhefen-shiyongshu-lvtxc2z3hn5ukh3va9rzijremcih]] treats stool testing as useful and colonoscopy as the gold standard.
- Age and prevention: [[vol-203-kao-bingmeishi-tongbian-yi-jinzhang-jiu-fuxie-nide-cuipi-changdao-xuyao-zhefen-shiyongshu-lvtxc2z3hn5ukh3va9rzijremcih]] says China recommends attention to screening after 45 and explains that removing polyps can block precancerous progression.
- Symptom-screening distinction: [[vol-93-xiaohuaneike-you-pi-bu-fang-sunhai-wuzang-zhe-jige-huai-xiguan-ni-zhan-jige-bianmi-he-fuxie-shi-zheyang-zuo-lgrsyf6ozplphgjjpcrh7dspc_do]] says early tumors may be asymptomatic while blood mixed with stool, narrowing stool, pain, or sustained bowel change can justify diagnostic evaluation.
- Risk context and prevention: [[vol-93-xiaohuaneike-you-pi-bu-fang-sunhai-wuzang-zhe-jige-huai-xiguan-ni-zhan-jige-bianmi-he-fuxie-shi-zheyang-zuo-lgrsyf6ozplphgjjpcrh7dspc_do]] connects family history, long-term dietary pattern, movement, constipation, endoscopy, and polyp detection to prevention without claiming one factor determines cancer.
- Positive-screen routing and test limits: [[vol-52-xiaohuaneijingke-ni-weichang-gongneng-zenmeyang-ni-jingli-guo-weichangjing-jiancha-ma-cai-guo-zhexie-wuqu-ma-lgvjd8plhexcbqjhck_u0kaem_z5]] describes fecal occult-blood testing as an initial screen whose positive results warrant endoscopic evaluation and rejects tumor markers or genetic testing as stand-alone proof of presence or absence.
- Family history and examination quality: [[vol-52-xiaohuaneijingke-ni-weichang-gongneng-zenmeyang-ni-jingli-guo-weichangjing-jiancha-ma-cai-guo-zhexie-wuqu-ma-lgvjd8plhexcbqjhck_u0kaem_z5]] connects earlier assessment to family history and explains that inadequate bowel cleansing can conceal small lesions, while prior polyp findings and subsequent results shape surveillance.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
These podcast sources are not screening guidelines. Their stated midlife thresholds are not identical, and the diet-risk mechanisms and “three increases, two pains” symptom shorthand are not diagnostic rules. Exact age, interval, modality, urgency, bowel-prep suitability, anticoagulant handling, family-history threshold, and post-polyp surveillance should be individualized by qualified clinicians and current local standards.

## What Changed
- Added positive fecal-test routing and a clearer boundary against tumor-marker or genetic-test substitution.
- Added bowel-preparation quality, prior polyps, and source disagreement on age thresholds to the surveillance context.
