# Media Technology and Ghost Culture

## Definition

Media technology and ghost culture describes the reciprocal process by which communication and image technologies supply new forms for haunting while ghost stories teach audiences how to imagine those technologies.

## Current Synthesis

[[66-ghosts-glt2309926019]] links newspapers, magic lanterns, photography, X-rays, cinema, radio, horror film, Zoom, text messages, and autocorrect to changing ghost narratives. Technologies that preserve voice or moving images, reveal invisible interiors, or transmit messages without physical presence can appear uncanny when new; mass media then standardize and distribute those impressions.

Technology also changes evidence and incentives. Cameras and instruments promise objective capture, but cropping, staging, publicity, and interpretation can manufacture persuasive artifacts. Media therefore widen both the repertoire of apparitions and the need for source criticism.

## Key Claims

- New media create culturally plausible channels through which ghosts can appear.
- Recording and transmission blur presence and absence by letting dead or distant people continue to move, speak, or message.
- Mass circulation can transform local disturbance into public spectacle.
- Investigative technology can increase credibility without guaranteeing evidentiary integrity.
- Horror films feed motifs back into later reports and expectations.
- Digital haunting continues older adaptation patterns rather than marking the disappearance of belief.

## Evidence

- Visual modernity - [[66-ghosts-glt2309926019]] pairs late-nineteenth-century X-rays and cinema as uncanny technologies and connects photography to continuing images of the dead.
- Mass publicity - [[66-ghosts-glt2309926019]] uses the [[CockLaneGhost]] to show newspapers and crowds expanding a domestic claim.
- Instrument and fraud - [[66-ghosts-glt2309926019]] uses [[HarryPrice]] and [[BorleyRectory]] to join technical investigation with publicity and the manipulated floating-brick photograph.
- Narrative feedback - [[66-ghosts-glt2309926019]] connects *The Exorcist*, *The Amityville Horror*, and Japanese technology horror to later haunting expectations.
- Digital adaptation - [[66-ghosts-glt2309926019]] records Zoom apparitions, messages from the dead, and haunted autocorrect as twenty-first-century motifs.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Temporal association does not prove that a technology caused a belief, and the episode does not provide a systematic reception history for every medium named. A fraudulent photograph does not show that all visual evidence is fabricated, while a technically unexplained artifact does not prove a ghost.
