# County Lines Drug Trafficking

## Definition
County lines drug trafficking is the movement of vulnerable children and other couriers through networks that supply drug markets outside the traditional urban-gang core.

## Current Synthesis
The source uses county lines to explain why British knife-crime geography can change even when large cities become safer. The important shift is not simply that urban gangs export crime to rural areas; it is that drug distribution has become more localized inside counties as suppliers compete on speed, availability, and customer service.

## Key Claims
- County lines make rural and county-level violence part of the same national drug-market system as urban gang violence.
- Vulnerable children are central to the harm model because they can be recruited or coerced into moving drugs across places.
- The phenomenon complicates prevention because risk may appear in dispersed communities that lack dense city prevention infrastructure.
- Localized drug lines inside counties mean policy cannot rely only on transport-route interdiction from cities to towns.
- Knife-crime prevention needs county-level intelligence about children, schools, families, and local drug markets.

## Evidence
- Geography shift: [[bargaining-chips-nvidia-is-the-bank-of-ai-6a9a86ffe7a9fe2478c4fc41]] presents county lines as a reason knife violence can rise in counties while London and other large cities improve.
- Vulnerable-courier mechanism: [[bargaining-chips-nvidia-is-the-bank-of-ai-6a9a86ffe7a9fe2478c4fc41]] says vulnerable children are used to move drugs from urban gangs toward rural users.
- Localization: [[bargaining-chips-nvidia-is-the-bank-of-ai-6a9a86ffe7a9fe2478c4fc41]] says the number of county lines has grown sharply, and that internal lines within counties have expanded as rural markets demand faster service.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source reports a structural explanation, not a complete causal proof for every local rise in knife violence. County lines should be treated as one mechanism inside a wider mix of policing, deprivation, youth vulnerability, drug demand, and data-quality issues.
