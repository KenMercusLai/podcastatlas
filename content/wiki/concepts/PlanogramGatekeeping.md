# Planogram Gatekeeping

Planogram gatekeeping is the retail constraint where a buyer must fit products into a shelf blueprint and defend the productivity of each slot. In [[who-decides-what-big-box-sells-our-game-got-us-answers]], [[MattAdelman]] explains that big-box retailers use planograms and that buyers mentally test where a candidate product would go before pitching the plan to senior leaders.

The concept extends [[RetailShelfPlacement]] by moving one layer upstream. A founder or publisher may think about the final shopper, but the buyer must first decide what the product replaces, how the shelf will look, and whether senior leaders will accept the tradeoff.

## Key Claims
- Shelf space is scarce, so product approval requires a replacement logic, not only enthusiasm for a new item.
- Planograms make [[RetailShelfAppeal]] accountable to layout, visibility, category fit, and predicted productivity.
- Internal buyer pitches can filter products even after an external line-review meeting goes well.
- [[SalesVelocity]] and [[RetailIncrementality]] matter because they give the buyer evidence to defend the shelf-space decision.
