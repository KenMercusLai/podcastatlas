# Participant-Observer Entanglement / 参与观察者纠缠

Participant-observer entanglement is the problem that an anthropologist cannot stand outside a field site like an invisible recorder. [[35-tianzhende-renleixuejia-zuo-tianye-hui-shangyin-de-603418624]] makes this concrete through [[NigelBarley|Nigel Barley]]: he needs help, gives help, hires people, accepts beer, builds a house, asks questions through translators, relies on missionaries, and is locally classified as a strange white outsider.

The concept extends [[FieldworkAsKnowledgeMethod]] by adding the cost of presence. The fieldworker's body, money, medicines, race, language, and social mistakes all become part of the field situation. That does not make knowledge impossible, but it makes the fantasy of untouched observation impossible.

## Key Claims
- Observation in human settings changes the setting being observed.
- Refusing all intervention can be ethically and practically impossible when people ask for medicine, money, transport, or help.
- Local categories assigned to the researcher shape what they are allowed to see and how others speak around them.
- A good account should show the observer's position rather than pretending the observer is absent.
