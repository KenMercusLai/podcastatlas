## Definition

Moral stress is the strain that arises when a person acts against an important moral intuition or must choose among values that each feel binding.

## Current Synthesis

[[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-8ba2ac9ba457581786d577922ac762fc-50949639-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-8ba2ac9ba457581786d577922ac762fc-50949639|When Doing Right Feels Wrong]] introduces the term through [[AdamWaytz]]'s account of lying about alcohol at a high-school party and then returning to correct the statement. His distress signaled conflict with honesty even before any external punishment.

[[DarwinRocher]] provides the more severe boundary case. Reporting a planned robbery served justice and harm prevention, but covertly cooperating against trusted colleagues violated personal loyalty and required prolonged deception. His later hypervigilance, panic attacks, and isolation cannot be reduced to moral stress because danger and retaliation were also present, yet the case shows why believing a decision is right does not make it emotionally painless.

The concept is therefore diagnostic rather than self-validating. Distress may identify a value conflict, but it does not prove which choice is correct; judgment still requires evidence, stakes, duties, alternatives, and consequences.

## Key Claims

- Distress can arise from violating honesty, loyalty, justice, care, or another important intuition.
- Corrective action may follow when a person treats distress as information about a value conflict.
- Choosing the more justified value can still produce grief, guilt, or strain over the value sacrificed.
- Severe cases also involve danger, trauma, coercion, and retaliation that moral conflict alone cannot explain.
- Moral stress is evidence of internal conflict, not proof that a feeling or decision is morally correct.

## Evidence

- Immediate correction - [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-8ba2ac9ba457581786d577922ac762fc-50949639-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-8ba2ac9ba457581786d577922ac762fc-50949639]] recounts Waytz returning to correct a lie after feeling distressed by it.
- Competing duties - [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-8ba2ac9ba457581786d577922ac762fc-50949639-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-8ba2ac9ba457581786d577922ac762fc-50949639]] describes Rocher's gut-wrenching choice between trusted colleagues and prevention of robbery or murder.
- Severe outcome boundary - [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-8ba2ac9ba457581786d577922ac762fc-50949639-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-8ba2ac9ba457581786d577922ac762fc-50949639]] reports hypervigilance, panic attacks, threats, and isolation during and after the investigation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source does not provide a clinical definition, measurement instrument, differential diagnosis, or evidence that moral stress is distinct from guilt, anxiety, trauma, moral injury, or ordinary cognitive dissonance. Rocher's symptoms occurred amid credible danger and retaliation, so they should not be attributed to value conflict alone. Absence of distress also does not establish moral innocence.

## What Changed

- Created a bounded account separating value-conflict strain from moral correctness and clinical diagnosis.
