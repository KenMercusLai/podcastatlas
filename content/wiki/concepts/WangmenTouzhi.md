# 望门投止 / Wangmen Touzhi

## Definition

Wangmen touzhi / 望门投止 is the idiom Hanji 895 ties to Zhang Jian's flight: a desperate fugitive sees a household gate and seeks immediate shelter. Hanji 894 supplies more of the route logic behind the phrase by showing Zhang Jian repeatedly relying on households and local discretion to stay alive.

## Current Synthesis

The idiom preserves the pressure of political pursuit, but the current evidence refuses to leave it as only a survival phrase. [[ZhangJianLateHan|张俭]]'s need to live is real: Hanji 894 shows him hungry, desperate for shelter, aided by [[LiDuLateHan|李笃]], spared by [[MaoQinLateHan|毛钦]], and routed through [[XiziRanLateHan|戏子然]]. Yet the same sheltering chain exposes hosts and kin to lethal punishment, and Hanji 895 makes that exposure vivid through [[KongRong|孔融]], [[KongBaoLateHan|孔褒]], and [[KongRongMother|孔融母]]. The phrase therefore belongs with [[FugitiveShelterCollateralRisk|收留逃亡者的牵连风险]] as much as with courage or refuge.

## Key Claims

- The idiom foregrounds urgency: survival pressure can make any available household look like a temporary refuge.
- Hanji 894 adds the repeated-route version: multiple households and officials become part of the escape path before the idiom is later compressed.
- Its Hanji 895 use keeps the host side visible because Zhang Jian's refuge implicates the Kong family after he escapes.
- The phrase can romanticize desperate trust if separated from Xia Fu's criticism of collateral harm.
- Tan Sitong's later allusion keeps the idiom available for broader political-persecution memory.

## Evidence

- Route pressure: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-894-yi-chang-taowang-yinfa-de-da-tusha-lhulp9hqu07uayyfl3eg-xwrtkeu|Hanji 894]] shows Zhang Jian moving from one refuge possibility to another under lethal pursuit, including Li Du's home and Xizi Ran's home.
- Origin scene: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-895-xuexing-tudao-xia-de-gurou-qingshen-llsplyyrjxhqkpi2aiuqzulgm6w3|Hanji 895]] explains the phrase through Zhang Jian's flight and urgent search for shelter.
- Host exposure: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-895-xuexing-tudao-xia-de-gurou-qingshen-llsplyyrjxhqkpi2aiuqzulgm6w3|Hanji 895]] places the idiom after the Kong-family imprisonment and competing responsibility claims.
- Later memory: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-895-xuexing-tudao-xia-de-gurou-qingshen-llsplyyrjxhqkpi2aiuqzulgm6w3|Hanji 895]] cites Tan Sitong's prison poem as a later reuse of the Zhang Jian and Du Gen contrast.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This page records how Hanji 894 and Hanji 895 use Zhang Jian's flight to frame the idiom. It does not yet evaluate wider literary history, variants of the phrase, or whether every later use carries the same moral balance between refuge and collateral harm.

## What Changed

- Hanji 894 adds the route-level desperation and helper-punishment background behind Hanji 895's explicit idiom explanation.
