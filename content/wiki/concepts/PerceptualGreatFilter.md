# Perceptual Great Filter

## Definition
The perceptual great filter is the possibility that unfamiliar life or intelligence remains undetected because human senses, concepts, instruments, and search categories are tuned to familiar terrestrial forms.

## Current Synthesis
Conventional great-filter arguments ask which evolutionary or civilizational steps are rare. The episode adds a detection problem: observers may search successfully only for what resembles known organisms or technologies. Overcoming that filter requires evidence of life-like causal history without assuming Earth-like appearance, chemistry, behavior, or embodiment in advance.

## Key Claims
- Absence of recognized life is not identical to absence of life.
- Human categories can constrain instruments and interpretation.
- Familiar appearance is neither necessary nor sufficient for alien lineage.
- Detection needs general signatures while avoiding empty generality.
- Independent causal history is proposed as a stronger criterion than strangeness alone.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept is a hypothesis about search bias, not evidence that alien life exists or has already been missed. More general detection criteria can increase false positives, and the episode does not specify validated instruments or thresholds.
