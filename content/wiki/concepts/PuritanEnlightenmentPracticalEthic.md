# Puritan-Enlightenment Practical Ethic

## Definition

Puritan-Enlightenment practical ethic is the Franklin episode's synthesis of religiously inherited discipline, thrift, moral improvement, journalistic wit, and experimental usefulness.

## Current Synthesis

The source presents Franklin as a bridge between older Puritan habits and Enlightenment curiosity. Boston family discipline, Bible reading, work, thrift, and self-improvement shape his public moralism, but he loosens strict doctrine into practical religion and useful experiment. That combination makes Franklin's genius social rather than solitary: he prints, writes, jokes, invents, tests, measures, and turns knowledge into civic reputation.

## Key Claims

- Franklin's self-made identity depends on family and religious discipline as well as individual talent.
- Industry and frugality function as moral habits before they become wealth advice.
- Franklin's public writing moralizes, but wit and anecdote keep it socially usable.
- The source treats Franklin's religion as morally Christian but less doctrinally strict.
- Practical science and invention extend the same ethic from conduct into experiment.
- The ethic helped Franklin become a credible symbol of American possibility in Europe.

## Evidence

- Puritan inheritance: [[684-franklin-revenge-of-the-american-genius-part-2-glt6013835340]] describes Josiah and Abiah Franklin's Puritan background and Benjamin Franklin's disciplined Boston household.
- Wealth ethic: [[684-franklin-revenge-of-the-american-genius-part-2-glt6013835340]] says Franklin's recipe for wealth centered on industry and frugality.
- Moral journalism: [[684-franklin-revenge-of-the-american-genius-part-2-glt6013835340]] describes Franklin's style as moralizing softened by wit, satire, parable, and folksy anecdote.
- Practical religion: [[684-franklin-revenge-of-the-american-genius-part-2-glt6013835340]] uses Thomas Kidd's interpretation to describe Franklin as retaining Christian moral assumptions while shedding strict doctrinal emphasis.
- Experimental usefulness: [[684-franklin-revenge-of-the-american-genius-part-2-glt6013835340]] lists electricity, lightning rods, swimming flippers, weather forecasting, bifocals, the Gulf Stream, and surface tension as evidence of Franklin's curiosity.
- European reputation: [[684-franklin-revenge-of-the-american-genius-part-2-glt6013835340]] says Franklin's scientific reputation helped brand America in Europe as experimental and useful.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept is not a general theory of America or Protestant capitalism. It is grounded in the episode's Franklin portrait. The same practical ethic coexists with contradiction, especially Franklin's slaveholding and 1770 pro-slavery defense.
