# Circadian Travel Adaptation

## Definition
Circadian travel adaptation is a flexible approach to shifting toward destination time through coordinated light, wake time, movement, social activity, meals, caffeine, and evening darkness.

## Current Synthesis
The AMA offers two compatible paths. When preparation is practical, wake time can move earlier over several days before eastward travel, with bright light after the estimated temperature minimum and avoidance of bright light before it. When preparation is not practical, the traveler can adopt local wake time after arrival and use morning light, movement, caffeine, meals, and social activity as daytime cues while keeping nighttime light dim.

The larger principle is coordination without rigidity. Local meals can supply timing information, and a short late-morning nap may sometimes bridge acute sleepiness, but travel routines should preserve safety, social connection, and enjoyment rather than become an all-or-nothing optimization exercise.

## Key Claims
- Gradual pre-travel wake-time shifts can reduce the size of the required adjustment after arrival.
- Light timing around the estimated temperature minimum can advance or delay the circadian clock in different directions.
- Morning light, movement, caffeine, meals, and social activity can reinforce local daytime.
- Dim nighttime light helps avoid sending an opposing timing signal.
- Meal timing can support local alignment but does not require one universal breakfast routine.
- Travel adaptation should remain flexible enough to protect safety, social life, and sustainable behavior.

## Evidence
- Prepared shift - [[ama-16-sleep-vertigo-tbi-ocd-tips-for-travelers-gut-brain-axis-more-scim8512997965]] recommends moving wake time earlier before a three-hour eastward trip when feasible.
- Arrival cues - [[ama-16-sleep-vertigo-tbi-ocd-tips-for-travelers-gut-brain-axis-more-scim8512997965]] coordinates local light, exercise, caffeine, meals, and social activity after arrival.
- Night boundary - [[ama-16-sleep-vertigo-tbi-ocd-tips-for-travelers-gut-brain-axis-more-scim8512997965]] advises dim light before the temperature minimum and during nighttime waking.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not provide a complete phase-response curve, individualized nap protocol, trial evidence, or guidance for shift workers, children, pregnancy, medication use, serious sleep disorders, or safety-critical work. Temperature-minimum estimates and cue timing can be wrong, and severe sleepiness should not be managed by driving or other hazardous activity.

## What Changed
- Created a travel-specific branch that joins pre-adjustment and after-arrival strategies.
- Made flexibility and safety explicit limits on circadian optimization.
