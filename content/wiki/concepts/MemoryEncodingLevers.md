# Memory Encoding Levers

## Definition
Memory encoding levers are four features that can make experience more memorable: novelty captures attention, repetition strengthens a trace, association connects new material to existing knowledge, and emotional resonance recruits salience around an event.

## Current Synthesis
The [[WendySuzuki]] episode presents the four levers as complementary routes rather than a guaranteed formula. Novelty can make material noticeable, repetition gives the system repeated opportunities to encode it, association supplies retrieval structure, and emotion can make a single experience unusually durable. The framework links attention at the moment of learning with [[HippocampalAssociativeMemory]] and later consolidation, while also explaining why threatening or personally meaningful events may be remembered after one exposure.

The practical inference is modest: learning can be designed to combine attention, repeated retrieval or exposure, meaningful links, and appropriate personal relevance. Strong emotion is not required, and deliberately creating distress is neither necessary nor justified by the source.

## Key Claims
- Novelty supports encoding by drawing selective attention to an event or item.
- Repetition creates multiple opportunities for a memory representation to strengthen.
- Association embeds new material within existing knowledge and contextual networks.
- Emotional resonance can make one-trial learning durable through salience-related hippocampal and amygdala involvement.
- The four levers can complement one another but do not guarantee accurate or permanent memory.

## Evidence
- Four-part framework - [[boost-attention-memory-with-science-based-tools-dr-wendy-suzuki-scim2980742096]] explicitly names novelty, repetition, association, and emotional resonance as memorability levers.
- One-trial learning - [[boost-attention-memory-with-science-based-tools-dr-wendy-suzuki-scim2980742096]] uses threatening and personally salient events to explain how emotion may substitute for repeated exposure.
- Network integration - [[boost-attention-memory-with-science-based-tools-dr-wendy-suzuki-scim2980742096]] links association to existing memory networks and hippocampal context building.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode offers a useful teaching framework, not a complete taxonomy or a comparative trial of learning methods. Attention can be captured without producing durable or accurate memory, repetition can reinforce errors, association can mislead, and high emotional arousal can distort recall or become clinically harmful. The source does not specify optimal doses, effect sizes, developmental differences, or conditions under which one lever should dominate another.

## What Changed
- Created a bounded four-lever model that separates encoding supports from guarantees of retention or accuracy.
