# Hacker House Startup Infrastructure

## Definition
Hacker house startup infrastructure is the use of shared live-work housing as part of the startup formation system: residence, peer density, meals, events, affordability, selection, and network access become mechanisms for helping founders enter or advance inside a startup ecosystem.

## Current Synthesis
The wiki now treats hacker houses as a spectrum rather than a single model. At one end, [[StartupEmbassy]] shows a mission-driven and crowded gateway for startup dreamers entering the [[SanFranciscoBayArea]] from places such as Istanbul and Seville. At the other end, [[TheResidency]], [[HF0]], and [[AGIHouse]] show more formal AI-era or equity-driven houses that blend housing with selection, investor access, and value capture.

The durable pattern is residential compression: founders, inventors, and operators place daily life inside the startup network so that peer observation, motivation, introductions, and identity reinforcement happen continuously. The new [[HF0]] participant account adds execution compression: extensive support removes daily-life interruptions, one company-specific metric narrows attention, and weekly public review turns proximity into accountability. That can reduce access and coordination friction, but it also makes the model operationally and politically fragile when crowding, neighborhood complaints, investor over-presence, revenue pressure, public comparison, or mission purity overwhelm the community.

## Key Claims
- The category ranges from informal and mission-driven houses to rent-driven branded communities and equity-driven startup programs.
- Physical proximity matters because founders can observe work habits, exchange help, absorb peer pressure, and build trust without scheduling every interaction.
- Housing affordability and daily-life support can become startup access infrastructure when a city is expensive but still valuable for networks, investors, and technical peers.
- The same density that creates opportunity can create civic friction when many residents occupy houses designed for ordinary neighborhood life.
- Investor access and equity capture can move value earlier than accelerators, but only if the house preserves founder trust and does not turn residential community into a permanent pitch room.
- Mission purity can create a strong resident identity, yet it can also weaken financial resilience if the house rejects easier revenue sources that do not fit its founder-community purpose.
- Residential support can function as an attention-management system when it is joined to one bounded objective and a recurring review cadence.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Hacker houses are not inherently successful startup infrastructure. The evidence is source-scoped to documentary, field-visit, and participant accounts, and it weighs community benefits against crowding, business fragility, neighborhood complaints, selection risk, investor over-presence, uncertain equity returns, psychological pressure, and exclusion of founders whose responsibilities or needs do not fit residential intensity.

## What Changed
- Added HF0's participant-described operating cadence and company-specific metric.
- Reframed residential support as both community infrastructure and an attention-management mechanism.
- Added public-comparison pressure and transferability as explicit limits.
