# Climate Property Insurance Availability

## Definition
Climate property insurance availability is the capacity of households in hazard-exposed places to obtain sufficiently comprehensive and affordable coverage as expected losses, correlated events, regulation, underwriting data, and insurer portfolio limits change.

## Current Synthesis
[[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-043ffe0bc2c7516bac64132229755e93-43026861-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-043ffe0bc2c7516bac64132229755e93-43026861]] treats insurability as a market and community condition, not a binary property label. Higher risk can produce higher prices, nonrenewals, residual-market dependence, or withdrawal; stronger codes and mitigation can preserve some coverage, but the resulting price may still obstruct mortgages, rebuilding, transactions, population return, and local commerce.

## Key Claims
- Climate-amplified catastrophe losses can reduce both affordability and the amount of private insurance capacity offered in exposed areas.
- Rate suppression can delay price shocks while increasing incentives for insurers to restrict or withdraw coverage.
- Risk-reflective pricing can support market capacity without making coverage affordable to every household.
- Residual-market coverage prevents complete exclusion but may be narrower and require expensive supplements.
- Insurance availability is infrastructure for housing finance and community recovery, not merely an individual financial product.
- Physical adaptation helps but must be recognized by underwriting and combined with portfolio and regulatory changes.

### Conditional market return
- [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-043ffe0bc2c7516bac64132229755e93-43026861-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-043ffe0bc2c7516bac64132229755e93-43026861]] describes catastrophe-model reform, regional writing obligations, and a limited return of private policies.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- The episode does not establish the relative causal weight of climate change, regulation, reinsurance, construction cost, portfolio concentration, data quality, or insurer strategy.
- Higher prices may signal recognized risk and still be economically exclusionary.
- Paradise's limited market return does not establish a scalable national solution.

## What Changed
- Created a market-access frame separating physical insurability, private capacity, coverage breadth, and household affordability.
- Connected insurance availability to community reconstruction and local economic recovery.
