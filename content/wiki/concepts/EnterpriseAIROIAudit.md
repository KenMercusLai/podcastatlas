# Enterprise AI ROI Audit

[[ep-48-from-pilots-to-productivity-what-it-actually-takes-to-make-ai-work-in-the-enterprise]] adds the pre-measurement side through [[AIAdoptionBaselineMeasurement]]. [[JimSpignardo]] says organizations often fail to measure current workflows before rollout, so later ROI claims around [[MicrosoftCopilot|Copilot]] have no baseline.

Enterprise AI ROI audit is the discipline of testing AI spending against measurable productivity, revenue, cost reduction, and earnings effects. In [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-more-trillion-dollar-ipos-anthropic-3t-zucks-price-war-china-ends-open-source-trump-accounts-42041390]], [[ChamathPalihapitiya|Chamath Palihapitiya]] says token costs can grow much faster than observable productivity, so CFOs will eventually ask whether AI spend beats the risk-free rate or appears in EPS.

The concept connects [[AIInferenceCostStructure]] to [[AIRevenueLegibility]]. If companies cannot distinguish experimentation, real labor substitution, revenue growth, and cost avoidance, then model-company revenue can look strong before customer economics are proven. [[ForwardDeployedEngineer]] work and [[AgenticWorkflow]] design are presented as possible bridges from model usage to business value.

[[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-mark-cuban-on-the-ai-bubble-who-actually-gets-wiped-out-42155640]] adds the implementation-cost side through [[MarkCuban|Mark Cuban]]. Cuban argues that CEOs often do not understand what enterprise AI can and cannot do, and that the need for FDEs, systems thinking, and [[AgentMaintenanceBurden|agent maintenance]] shows AI is not yet plug-and-play. In this version, ROI audit has to count the people required to turn brittle agents into accepted work.

## Key Claims
- AI spend should be tested by token cost, workflow adoption, avoided labor, increased output, revenue creation, and verification overhead.
- Reported usage is not enough if it does not change accepted work, customer outcomes, or financial statements.
- Companies may need to report AI-attributable EPS or margin effects for investors to believe the adoption story.
- Frontier labs may prefer public-market timing before customers fully audit whether usage converts into economic return.
- FDE teams and agentic pods can improve ROI only if they choose workflows with clear acceptance criteria and accountable owners.
- Agent maintenance, model drift, and unusable generated workflows should be counted as costs when evaluating whether AI has actually improved productivity.
- EP48 adds that ROI audit should begin before rollout: if current workflow duration, handoff cost, and quality are not measured, the organization cannot tell whether AI improved anything.
