# Survival Cannibalism and Power Inequality / 生存性食人的权力不平等

## Definition

Survival cannibalism and power inequality describes how an emergency framed as common necessity can allocate mortal risk through preexisting differences in rank, race, gender, age, health, physical strength, and command authority.

## Current Synthesis

[[18-dongxifang-de-ren-xiangshi-lt7saup5pmf5u5fco0mb4dohldmp|Weird History episode 18]] distinguishes reliance on people who have already died from the deliberate killing of a selected person. The *Mignonette* case makes that boundary legal: the need to preserve several lives does not itself authorize murder of a weaker companion. The commutation of sentence and inconsistent treatment across jurisdictions show that legal condemnation did not settle every moral or historical response.

The episode's shipwreck and siege examples challenge the image of an equal lottery among interchangeable survivors. A procedure can be fabricated, avoided by leaders, or imposed only after status and force have already narrowed the victim pool. Extreme circumstances therefore do not dissolve social structure. They may reveal who can define necessity, administer the draw, refuse exposure, or make another person's body available.

## Key Claims

- Using the already dead and killing a person for food create different legal and moral problems.
- Necessity is not self-interpreting; someone decides whether alternatives remain and whose death appears permissible.
- A lottery is fair only if participation, odds, information, and enforcement are genuinely equal.
- Existing hierarchy can persist or intensify in crisis, making marginalized or weaker people more available for sacrifice.
- Legal refusal to recognize necessity as a murder defense protects against retrospective justification by survivors with greater power.

## Evidence

- Death-versus-killing distinction: [[18-dongxifang-de-ren-xiangshi-lt7saup5pmf5u5fco0mb4dohldmp]] contrasts the Donner and Andes survival accounts with the killing of Richard Parker aboard the *Mignonette*.
- Legal boundary: [[18-dongxifang-de-ren-xiangshi-lt7saup5pmf5u5fco0mb4dohldmp]] presents the 1884 judgment as rejecting necessity as a defense to deliberate murder, while noting later sentence reduction.
- Procedural inequality: [[18-dongxifang-de-ren-xiangshi-lt7saup5pmf5u5fco0mb4dohldmp]] uses real and alleged lotteries to show how leaders, stronger participants, or fraudulent procedures can shift exposure.
- Unequal victimization: [[18-dongxifang-de-ren-xiangshi-lt7saup5pmf5u5fco0mb4dohldmp]] identifies racialized, enslaved, young, sick, female, dependent, and lower-status people as recurrently vulnerable in the selected examples.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode's cases do not establish a universal distribution of victims, and some incident details or interpretations require specialist legal and historical verification. Genuine consent, equal lotteries, deaths from natural causes, active killing, and later consumption must not be collapsed. Different jurisdictions and periods also define necessity, homicide, jurisdiction, and maritime custom differently.

## What Changed

- Defined survival selection as a power-allocation problem rather than a scarcity problem alone.
- Distinguished procedural equality on paper from the social conditions required for a fair lottery.
