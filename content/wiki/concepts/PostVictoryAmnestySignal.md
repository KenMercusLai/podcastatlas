## Definition
胜后既往不咎信号 / post-victory amnesty signal is the leadership pattern in which a victor deliberately refuses to investigate exposed wavering or hidden contact after a major win, making clemency visible enough to reduce fear, destroy the evidentiary basis for future purges, and convert uncertain insiders back into the winning order.

## Current Synthesis
The concept now has two direct case anchors. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-672-shenchu-zhichang-hui-zhuang-cai-shi-gaoji-zhihui-ls35j8plj-cqnblgufc4plbzpnby|Hanji 672]] supplies the [[LiuXiu|刘秀]] case: after Handan falls and [[WangLangHebeiClaimant|王郎]] is killed, Liu Xiu finds letters from his own officials and acquaintances to Wang Lang, burns them in public, and makes non-pursuit explicit so "反侧" insiders can settle. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1050-cong-caocao-fenxin-xuexi-baogui-de-lingdao-zhihui-lsqg-qaxozjpaxyukrvxbtf-mcse|Hanji 1050]] supplies the [[CaoCao|曹操]] case: after defeating [[YuanShao|袁绍]] at [[GuanduCampaign|Guandu]], Cao Cao burns letters from his own officials and generals to Yuan Shao and explains that Yuan's prior strength made wavering understandable.

The pattern is narrower than general pardon. It applies when a victor has enough power to punish, when the exposed misconduct is widespread or fear-driven enough that investigation would destabilize the organization, and when the leader can make the refusal public enough to settle expectations. Hanji 672's [[KangxiEmperor|Kangxi]] comparison broadens the pattern as a later analogy, while Hanji 1050 keeps a counter-pattern visible: Yuan Shao cannot absorb being proved wrong by [[TianFengLateHan|田丰]], so defeat produces punishment of correct advice rather than trust repair.

## Key Claims
- Visible refusal to investigate can be more stabilizing than private mercy because it publicly destroys the evidentiary basis for later revenge.
- The signal works best after a credible victory or power shift; without that shift, non-punishment may look like weakness or inability to enforce.
- The pattern treats wavering under overwhelming enemy pressure as fear to be managed, not automatically as hardened betrayal.
- Amnesty can preserve usable people and reduce internal panic at the moment when a victorious side still needs cohesion.
- The public script matters: Liu Xiu makes non-pursuit visible through burning, while Cao Cao explains others' fear through his own prior vulnerability.
- Historical analogy can make the pattern portable, but each case still depends on timing, evidence type, and the actors' continuing danger.
- The concept is not blanket tolerance; it distinguishes fear-driven contact from active hostile organization.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This concept should not become a rule that all betrayal evidence must be ignored. Amnesty can backfire if the actors remain actively hostile, if the contact was an ongoing conspiracy rather than fear-driven hedging, or if non-punishment undermines command boundaries. Hanji 672's Kangxi branch is currently a source-scoped analogy rather than a complete Qing political source note. The concept therefore sits between [[PardonAsStatePower]] and [[StrategicDefectorIncorporation|纳叛安抚式整合]], but it focuses specifically on post-victory insiders whose hidden wavering could trigger a purge.
