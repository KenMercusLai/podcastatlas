# 胜后既往不咎信号 / Post-Victory Amnesty Signal

## Definition
胜后既往不咎信号 / post-victory amnesty signal is the leadership pattern in which a victor deliberately refuses to investigate exposed wavering or hidden contact after a major win, making clemency visible enough to reduce fear, prevent internal purges, and convert uncertain insiders back into the winning order.

## Current Synthesis
The current concept is grounded in Cao Cao's post-Guandu letter-burning scene. After defeating [[YuanShao|袁绍]], [[CaoCao|曹操]] finds letters from his own officials and generals to Yuan Shao. Instead of identifying and punishing each writer, he burns the letters publicly and explains that when Yuan Shao was strong even he could barely protect himself, let alone everyone else. The episode reads the act as more than personal generosity: it removes evidence that could fuel reciprocal fear, tells wavering insiders that survival under the new field is possible, and keeps the organization focused on the next contest rather than consuming itself through score-settling.

The concept is adjacent to but narrower than general pardon. It applies when the victor has enough power to punish, when the exposed misconduct is widespread or fear-driven enough that full investigation would destabilize the group, and when the signal is made public enough to settle expectations. It also has a counter-pattern inside the same source: Yuan Shao cannot absorb being proved wrong by [[TianFengLateHan|田丰]], so defeat produces punishment of correct advice rather than trust repair.

## Key Claims
- Visible refusal to investigate can be more stabilizing than private mercy because it destroys the evidentiary basis for later revenge.
- The signal works only after a credible power shift; without victory, non-punishment may look like weakness or inability to enforce.
- The pattern treats wavering under overwhelming enemy pressure as fear to be managed, not automatically as hardened betrayal.
- Amnesty can preserve usable people and reduce internal panic at the moment when a victorious side still needs cohesion.
- The leader's explanation matters: Cao Cao frames others' fear through his own vulnerability, making clemency legible rather than arbitrary.
- The concept is not blanket tolerance; it depends on distinguishing fear-driven contact from actors who remain too dangerous to retain.

- Fear reframing: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1050-cong-caocao-fenxin-xuexi-baogui-de-lingdao-zhihui-lsqg-qaxozjpaxyukrvxbtf-mcse|Hanji 1050]] records Cao Cao explaining that Yuan Shao's strength had made even Cao unsure of self-preservation, so others' wavering was understandable.

- Counter-pattern inside Yuan's camp: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1050-cong-caocao-fenxin-xuexi-baogui-de-lingdao-zhihui-lsqg-qaxozjpaxyukrvxbtf-mcse|Hanji 1050]] contrasts Cao Cao's refusal to purge wavering insiders with Yuan Shao killing Tian Feng after defeat and allowing captured-family suspicion around Shen Pei to spread.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This concept is currently grounded in one source note and should remain narrow until more cases are added. The source's Liu Xiu comparison is a useful analogy but is not developed enough here to rewrite Liu Xiu's broader page. Amnesty can backfire if the underlying actors remain actively hostile, if the offense was not fear-driven, or if non-punishment undermines clear command boundaries. The pattern therefore sits between [[PardonAsStatePower]] and [[StrategicDefectorIncorporation|纳叛安抚式整合]], but it focuses specifically on post-victory insiders whose hidden wavering could trigger a purge.

## What Changed
- Created the concept from Hanji 1050's Cao Cao letter-burning scene after Guandu.
- Distinguished post-victory insider amnesty from broader enemy or defector incorporation.
- Added Yuan Shao's post-defeat handling of Tian Feng and Shen Pei as an internal counter-pattern.
