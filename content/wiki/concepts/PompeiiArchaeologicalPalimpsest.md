# Pompeii as an Archaeological Palimpsest

## Definition

Pompeii as an archaeological palimpsest is the framework that treats the city as accumulated urban, social, political, and interpretive layers rather than as a complete, static snapshot frozen by the [[EruptionOfVesuvius79|79 CE eruption]].

## Current Synthesis

The destruction horizon preserves an extraordinary concentration of evidence, but it does not flatten Pompeii's history. Oscan and Greek connections, Roman colonization, Sulla's siege, rebuilding, house modification, work, worship, slavery, commerce, waste, and food all preceded the eruption, while Bourbon collecting, nationalist and fascist politics, conservation, tourism, scanning, and new excavation shaped what later audiences could see and believe.

The framework therefore joins stratigraphy to interpretive humility. Bodies invite stories but scans can overturn assigned identities; a charcoal date can challenge August without supplying its own year; one worn coin can lose apparent decisiveness; and inscriptions scattered across a city can gradually reconstruct [[GaiusAlleiusNigidiusMaius]] without yielding a complete biography. Conservation and contextual finds may add more knowledge than searching for another spectacular object.

## Key Claims

- A catastrophe can preserve one destruction horizon without making the preceding city historically static.
- Excavated areas, open buildings, tourist routes, and famous objects are a managed sample rather than the whole urban record.
- Later excavation, display, politics, conservation, and technology become part of the site's interpretive history.
- Everyday systems such as food service, drainage, waste, rental, labor, and house alteration can be more historically informative than isolated treasures.
- Humanizing archaeological remains does not justify certainty about identity, relationship, motive, religion, or emotion.
- Strong interpretation combines dispersed evidence while preserving what each trace cannot establish.

## Evidence

- Long urban chronology: [[16-pompeii-glt3349011345]] places pre-Roman connections, Roman colonization, siege marks, and later rebuilding beneath the destruction horizon.
- Managed visibility: [[16-pompeii-glt3349011345]] describes an unexcavated third, rotating building access, conservation closures, and narrow visitor itineraries.
- Revisable identity: [[16-pompeii-glt3349011345]] uses plaster-cast scans and changing interpretations to challenge projected biographies.
- Cumulative reconstruction: [[16-pompeii-glt3349011345]] follows rental, games, and tomb inscriptions associated with Gaius Alleius Nigidius Maius.
- Everyday urban evidence: [[16-pompeii-glt3349011345]] uses a bar, food residue, sewers, soakaways, waste, graffiti, and political notices to reconstruct city life.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Calling Pompeii a palimpsest should not minimize the exceptional value of its destruction horizon or imply that every layer is equally visible. The framework derives from one public-history conversation rather than a full archaeological synthesis. Site chronology, population, excavation extent, artifact interpretation, identity claims, religious presence, food distribution, and political motives require specialist and context-specific verification.

- Established a framework separating Pompeii's preserved catastrophe from its long urban development and changing archaeological interpretation.
