# Frontier Auxiliary Supply Failure / 边兵粮饷供应失灵

Frontier auxiliary supply failure / 边兵粮饷供应失灵 enters through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-938-lishi-de-jingxing-ruanruo-zhihui-dailai-quru-lnlnrketgfbadhfjbzdjjtrmqfsh|Hanji 938]]. [[ZhangWenLateHan|张温]] recruits three thousand [[Wuhuan|乌桓]] cavalry for a Liangzhou campaign, rejects [[ZhangChunLateHan|张纯]]'s request to command them, and assigns [[GongsunZan|公孙瓒]] instead. When grain and pay fail to arrive around Ji, most of the cavalry abandon the campaign and return to their tribes.

The failure is logistical and political at once. The center tries to convert frontier mobility into campaign capacity, but cannot supply the force or manage the disappointed local actor who wanted command. [[ZhangChunLateHan|张纯]]'s resentment then joins with [[ZhangJuLateHan|张举]] and [[QiuLijuLateHan|丘力居]], helping turn a failed auxiliary mobilization into Youzhou rebellion infrastructure.

This concept differs from [[FrontierConscriptionBackfire|边疆征发反噬]]. Frontier conscription backfire names a requisition that destabilizes an allied frontier group's internal legitimacy. Frontier auxiliary supply failure names the narrower operational path where an unfed or unpaid auxiliary detachment deserts and leaves behind resentment, military credibility loss, and rebellion opportunity.

## Key Claims
- Frontier auxiliaries are not usable capacity unless food, pay, command legitimacy, and local expectations are maintained together.
- A failed supply channel can turn an auxiliary force from a campaign resource into a desertion and rebellion risk.
- Command appointment decisions can compound logistics failure when rejected local actors treat exclusion as humiliation or lost opportunity.
- The Wuhuan case in Hanji 938 is a prelude to Hanji 942's later Youzhou pacification problem.
