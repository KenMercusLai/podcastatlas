# Campaign Logistics and Tempo

## Definition

Campaign logistics and tempo is the interaction among supply, route consumption, transport, treasury, bases, fleet cost, movement speed, and operational timing that determines where an army can go and how long it can remain effective.

## Current Synthesis

Alexander's speed helps secure cities, treasuries, and coastal positions before [[DariusIII|Darius III]] can fully exploit imperial scale. Yet speed does not eliminate logistics: a force of tens of thousands consumes grain and water like a mobile city, cannot remain stationary indefinitely, and may be unable to retreat over a stripped route. Captured tribute eases Alexander's finances, while the expense and weakness of his fleet contribute to its controversial reduction.

The Persian system shows the other side of scale. Roads, staged depots, regional resources, and supply organization make a huge royal host possible, but numbers also increase ceremonial train, consumption, and coordination demands. The source therefore treats logistics as a combat condition rather than background administration.

## Key Claims

- Operational speed can pre-empt opposition and convert captured treasuries into continued campaigning capacity.
- Armies consume routes and cannot assume that stationary supply or backward movement remains available.
- Naval strength depends on crews, bases, and continuing expense, not ship counts alone.
- Large imperial armies require roads, depots, administration, and political cooperation across regions.
- Greater numbers increase supply and coordination burdens as well as battlefield strength.
- Strategic decisions can rationally accept one vulnerability, such as naval weakness, to preserve resources for another mode of war.

## Evidence

- Macedonian movement and finance: [[dchha74-mania-for-subjugation-iv-dchha74-mania-for-subjugation-iv]] links continuous marching, divided forces, city surrender, treasury capture, and easing debt.
- Fleet tradeoff: [[dchha74-mania-for-subjugation-iv-dchha74-mania-for-subjugation-iv]] presents expense, inferior quality, rear risk, and later naval restoration around the disbandment decision.
- Persian scale: [[dchha74-mania-for-subjugation-iv-dchha74-mania-for-subjugation-iv]] connects roads, depots, entourage, grain, water, and force-size uncertainty.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode rejects ancient totals but cannot establish exact army sizes or consumption. Strategic rationality does not prove that disbanding most of the fleet was optimal; subsequent Persian operations and Macedonian naval rebuilding show that the accepted risk was real.

## What Changed

- Created a mechanism joining speed, supply, finance, fleet tradeoffs, and route exhaustion across the campaign.
