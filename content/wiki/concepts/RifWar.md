# Rif War

## Definition

The Rif War was the early-20th-century colonial conflict in northern [[Morocco]] in which [[MohammedAbdelKrim]]'s Rif resistance defeated an overextended Spanish army, attempted state formation, and was ultimately crushed by combined Spanish and French power.

## Current Synthesis

The episode places the war between historical eras. Spain entered through protectorate claims, mining interests, wounded imperial prestige, and crusading rhetoric, yet fought with a corrupt and badly supplied conscript army. Annual turned those weaknesses into catastrophe and opened space for the [[RepublicOfTheRif]].

The imperial response looked increasingly modern: professional colonial troops, chemical weapons, civilian air raids, combined Franco-Spanish command, and the amphibious Alhucemas landing. The conflict therefore joins anti-colonial resistance and state-building to the formation of the [[ArmyOfAfrica]] and the coercive officer culture later active in Spain.

## Key Claims

- Extractive interest and imperial humiliation helped drive Spanish escalation in the Rif.
- Annual was an institutional failure of command, supply, deployment, and colonial contempt, not merely a lost battle.
- Abdel Krim combined military resistance with limited state-building and Islam-inflected political legitimacy.
- French intervention changed a Spanish colonial crisis into a coordinated two-power war.
- Chemical weapons, air power, and combined amphibious operations made the war a laboratory for modern coercion.
- The Army of Africa carried personnel, practices, prestige, and mythology from Morocco into later Spanish politics.

## Evidence

- Spanish commitment and Annual: [[282-morocco-the-rif-war-glt7389759975]] links mines, the Africanist lobby, protectorate expansion, blockhouse dispersal, failed relief, retreat, and mass death.
- Resistance and state formation: [[282-morocco-the-rif-war-glt7389759975]] follows Abdel Krim's coalition building and the Republic of the Rif's administrative efforts.
- Escalation and innovation: [[282-morocco-the-rif-war-glt7389759975]] describes chemical agents, foreign volunteer bombing, French intervention, and the Alhucemas landing.
- Spanish afterlife: [[282-morocco-the-rif-war-glt7389759975]] connects Franco, Mola, Sanjurjo, the Foreign Legion, and the Army of Africa to the Civil War.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode's “last colonial war” and “first modern war” formulations are interpretive rather than exclusive classifications. Rif society was not politically uniform, and Moroccan voices are mostly mediated through the hosts. Casualty totals, weapon details, operational-first claims, and direct causal links to later wars require specialist corroboration.

## What Changed

- Added the Rif War as both an anti-colonial conflict and a transition point in modern military violence.
- Centered institutional failure and learning rather than reducing the war to individual commanders.
