# Team-Based Service Delivery

## Definition

Team-based service delivery is an operating model in which an outcome depends on several specialized actors sharing information, timing, authority, and responsibility rather than one visible professional controlling the entire intervention.

## Current Synthesis

The episode connects two very different fields through the same organizational change. Minimally invasive bypass compresses the working space and makes coordinated control of cameras, instruments, anesthesia, and surgical tasks essential. Focused deterrence makes police one component of a wider network that can include outreach workers, educators, coaches, community figures, housing staff, and family services.

Part One makes the public-safety branch more concrete: a weekly Western District meeting combines police, clergy, counselors, outreach workers, and political representatives who recognize different people and relationships. The model therefore requires more than adding participants to a meeting. It needs stable roles, common judgment, information boundaries, retained expertise, accountable coordination, and resources that let each part act. Shared delivery can reduce dependence on individual heroics, but it can also diffuse responsibility unless decision rights and follow-through remain explicit.

## Key Claims

- Complex outcomes may require interdependent specialized work that no single profession can deliver alone.
- Team adoption changes authority and identity as well as workflow.
- Coordination quality, role clarity, retention, and shared timing can become outcome-critical capabilities.
- A nominal coalition is insufficient when members lack resources, information, or authority to act.
- Shared responsibility needs explicit accountability to prevent diffusion, delay, and incomplete implementation.
- Complementary relationship knowledge can be as operationally important as formal authority when a crisis requires a trusted intermediary.

## Evidence

### Surgical coordination

- [[wolfgang-s-law-part-two]] uses [[JohnPuskas]]'s contrast between singles tennis and soccer to describe minimally invasive bypass as coordinated team performance through a small operative field.

### Violence-prevention coordination

- [[wolfgang-s-law-part-two]] describes more than a dozen Baltimore actors coordinating around a young man assessed as at risk, illustrating the practical reach and resource burden of [[FocusedDeterrence]].
- [[wolfgang-s-law-part-one]] shows weekly case review drawing on police, clergy, counseling, outreach, and political knowledge to map retaliation risk and identify a credible contact.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The cross-domain analogy identifies an organizational pattern but does not make surgery and violence prevention equivalent. Clinical teams operate under different evidence, consent, authority, and accountability structures from public-safety networks. More participants can also increase delay, privacy exposure, conflict, and responsibility diffusion.

## What Changed

- Established a cross-domain synthesis of teamwork as shared authority, specialized coordination, and accountable execution.
- Added relationship recognition and trusted community access as distinct operational capabilities in violence prevention.
