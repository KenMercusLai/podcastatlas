# Cat Domestication History

Cat domestication history is the natural-history layer of [[34-maomi-mishi-he-yuchun-you-keai-de-renlei-601590607]]. The episode says the ancestors of [[Cat|猫]] were Libyan or African wildcats, and it ties human-cat proximity to agriculture: stored grain attracts rodents, rodents threaten food and disease control, and cats become tolerated or welcomed around human settlements because they hunt efficiently.

The source frames early cat domestication as a commensal relationship rather than a clean human conquest. Humans may leave scraps for useful cats, but cats remain visibly independent, which later lets the episode connect their biological and behavioral traits to [[CatCulturalAmbivalence]].

## Key Claims
- Cat domestication begins with stored food and rodent pressure more than with deliberate pet breeding.
- Early usefulness does not require immediate intimacy; cats can become part of human settlement before becoming household companions.
- Cat independence is not incidental to the source's cultural reading. It helps explain later admiration, resentment, and philosophical projection.
- The concept complements [[HorseDomesticationHistory]] by showing a different domestication path: pest-control cohabitation rather than transport, burden, milk, meat, or riding.
