## Definition

Translation as political mediation is the principle that interpreters and translated documents do not merely transmit language: they select categories, status relations, implications, and risks that can alter political action.

## Current Synthesis

The episode's two cases show mediation at different scales. In the [[SpanishConquestOfMexico|Spanish conquest of Mexico]], [[Malinche]] and [[GeronimoDeAguilar|Geronimo de Aguilar]] made communication possible through a relay chain. That chain opened access to local grievances, alliance choices, warnings, and court speech. It also left later historians unable to recover exactly how Nahuatl diplomatic convention became Spanish claims of submission.

In the [[MacartneyEmbassy|Macartney Embassy]], mediation occurred through people and document genres. Friendship could become deference, a diplomatic gift could become tribute, and an indirect request to depart could be understood as an invitation to remain. Such changes were not always random errors: they could lower political risk, fit a message into an institution's recognized form, or tell patrons what they were prepared to hear.

The concept therefore distinguishes access from agreement. Better translation can reveal a genuine conflict rather than solve it, as when [[QianlongEmperor|乾隆]] rejected British requests presented in comparatively clear Chinese. Translation can create a relationship, stabilize ceremony, and expose disagreement, but it cannot by itself reconcile incompatible political orders.

## Key Claims

- Relay interpretation expands communicative reach while multiplying points where meaning can shift.
- Political translation carries status, genre, implication, and institutional assumptions in addition to lexical meaning.
- Interpreters' identity, coercive exposure, patron incentives, and domain knowledge shape the messages they can produce.
- Strategic ambiguity and softening can preserve short-term interaction while accumulating false beliefs about consent.
- Material objects and scientific instruments require linguistic framing; they do not communicate political or epistemic meaning by themselves.
- Accurate translation can sharpen rather than remove conflict when interests and institutions are incompatible.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept should not make interpreters omnipotent or treat every disagreement as a translation problem. Military force, alliance choice, trade interests, law, disease, institutional authority, and security concerns independently shape outcomes. The episode's reconstructed word choices and motives require verification against the full multilingual documentary record. Adaptation to a recipient's genre may be necessary communication as well as distortion, and exact equivalence may be impossible when political categories do not align.

## What Changed

- Established a cross-case framework joining colonial interpretation and diplomatic document translation.
- Distinguished productive mediation, strategic softening, accidental error, and substantive disagreement.
- Added technical explanation and material gifts to the scope of political translation.
