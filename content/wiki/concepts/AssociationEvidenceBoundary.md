## Definition
The association evidence boundary is the rule that contact with a wrongdoer, concealment or minimization of that contact, facilitation, knowledge, and participation in crime are distinct propositions requiring progressively different evidence.

## Current Synthesis
The All-In episode applies this boundary to Epstein-related records. Contact can justify questions; an inaccurate public account can support a credibility judgment; and assistance or knowing enablement would require additional proof. None of those steps alone proves participation in sexual crimes. This layered model protects both accountability and due process because it permits scrutiny without converting proximity into guilt.

## Key Claims
- Association is evidence of contact, not automatic evidence of shared conduct or knowledge.
- Misleading disclosure can be independently important even when the underlying association is not criminal.
- Claims of knowing facilitation require evidence about awareness, action, and causal contribution.
- Criminal participation requires offense-specific proof rather than reputational proximity.
- Political consistency requires applying the same boundary to allies, opponents, donors, and public figures.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Association can be probative when frequency, timing, secrecy, benefits, or surrounding conduct make innocent explanations less plausible; the boundary is not a command to ignore patterns. Conversely, an allegedly false account requires proof of falsity and intent, not merely a different count or interpretation. The source does not independently validate the records used in its Hoffman or Andrew discussions.
