# Short-Term Conservation Volunteering / 短期动物保护志愿服务的边界

## Definition
Short-term conservation volunteering is temporary, usually non-specialist participation in wildlife or habitat work. Its ethical boundary lies in separating documented conservation benefit from participant experience, emotional attachment, tourism, and later awareness-raising.

## Current Synthesis
The current source presents a fourteen-day wildlife placement whose clearest effects fell on the volunteer. Feeding, supervised animal contact, difficult living conditions, and temporary digital disconnection produced learning, attachment, and reflection, but the guest explicitly denied that limited time and expertise justified a large protection claim.

This does not make the placement meaningless. It changes the test of value. A credible program should match tasks to competence, protect people and animals, explain how captivity and prior intervention shape present choices, and connect vivid experience to grounded follow-through. Personal transformation and public communication can be real outcomes, but neither should be counted automatically as ecological impact.

## Key Claims
- Short duration and limited expertise constrain claims of direct conservation benefit.
- Close or affectionate animal contact does not remove species-specific behavior or safety risk.
- Existing captivity and habituation make intervention choices path-dependent; simple appeals to either noninterference or control are insufficient.
- Participant learning is a legitimate outcome only when it is named separately from benefits to animals, habitats, or institutions.
- Modest follow-through, such as refusing wildlife products and sharing grounded information, is more credible than moral grandiosity or rescue fantasy.

## Evidence
- Impact boundary - [[ep48-aishang-feifei-de-shisitian-wkglcvwnuqbyica2a-cefishi5m963]] records the guest's judgment that two weeks of non-specialist participation changed her more clearly than it changed the conservation cause.
- Proximity and safety - [[ep48-aishang-feifei-de-shisitian-wkglcvwnuqbyica2a-cefishi5m963]] describes retained leopard hunting behavior, baboon grabbing, a food-related bite, supervised access, barriers, and emergency-distance concerns.
- Path-dependent intervention - [[ep48-aishang-feifei-de-shisitian-wkglcvwnuqbyica2a-cefishi5m963]] opposes deliberate taming in principle while acknowledging that prior human intervention already structures the available choices.
- Participant outcome - [[ep48-aishang-feifei-de-shisitian-wkglcvwnuqbyica2a-cefishi5m963]] links the stay to psychological distance from work, digital disconnection, reading, group life, and a stronger desire to reconsider the guest's ordinary routine.
- Follow-through - [[ep48-aishang-feifei-de-shisitian-wkglcvwnuqbyica2a-cefishi5m963]] presents opposition to ivory purchasing and humble awareness-sharing as more defensible actions than claiming to have changed the world.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence is one guest's recollection as represented in a structured episode summary, not a transcript, program audit, welfare assessment, or conservation-outcome study. The facility is not identified, so its staffing, finances, animal origins, release goals, handling rules, and ecological results cannot be evaluated. Supervised walking and close contact may raise animal-welfare or voluntourism concerns, but the source does not provide enough evidence to adjudicate them. The guest's later consumption of zebra and antelope meat shows ethical inconsistency without proving that all earlier concern or all short-term volunteering is worthless.

## What Changed
- Created a framework separating participant transformation from demonstrated conservation impact.
- Made competence, safety, prior intervention, and post-placement follow-through explicit evaluation tests.
- Preserved ethical inconsistency as a qualification rather than turning volunteers into moral exemplars.
