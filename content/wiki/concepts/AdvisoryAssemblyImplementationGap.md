## Definition

The advisory assembly implementation gap is the institutional distance between a deliberative body's informed recommendations and the binding decisions, budgets, laws, or administrative actions needed to carry them out.

## Current Synthesis

An assembly can improve representation, learning, and proposal quality without acquiring authority over publication, legislative agenda, drafting, or enactment. The Connecticut case shows that this gap begins before a legislative vote: recommendation wording, media timing, official sponsorship, spokesperson control, and electoral calculations can all shape whether the public receives the assembly's work as promised. A credible design therefore needs a response contract, transparent drafting, publication independence, and an identified implementation route.

## Key Claims

- Advisory status preserves elected authority but exposes citizen recommendations to institutional veto and delay.
- Implementation risk begins with drafting and publication rather than only with final legislative rejection.
- Political sponsors can enable an assembly while also becoming gatekeepers of its output.
- Electoral timing can reintroduce the risk aversion the assembly was created to bypass.
- Centralized communications can protect a collective message while moving participants toward conventional political tactics.
- A mandatory response is distinct from mandatory adoption and can improve accountability without transferring full lawmaking power.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

- The report was ultimately released before the election, so attempted delay did not become permanent suppression.
- Concern about partisan weaponization or weak reception can be sincere even when delay violates participant expectations.
- Direct citizen lawmaking creates separate legitimacy, rights, competence, and accountability questions that this source does not resolve.
- The episode does not report subsequent legislative action, so long-run implementation remains unknown.

## What Changed

- Added the pre-legislative stages of wording, publication, sponsorship, and communication to the implementation gap.
- Distinguished guaranteed institutional response from guaranteed policy adoption.
- Preserved the sponsor's enabling and gatekeeping roles simultaneously.
