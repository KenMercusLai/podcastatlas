# Family-Sized Housing Supply

## Definition
Family-sized housing supply is the availability of affordable homes with enough bedrooms, stability, and usable space for households that want children, rather than housing supply measured only by total unit count.

## Current Synthesis
[[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-dde046734f50fc7c6a18ae9c19ada137-55355169-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-dde046734f50fc7c6a18ae9c19ada137-55355169]] distinguishes general affordability from fit. Studios and one-bedroom apartments can address real housing needs and lower aggregate rent pressure, yet may do less for prospective parents who expect multiple bedrooms, a townhouse, a yard, or another child-compatible form.

The synthesis is not that only detached houses support families. Austin's smaller three-bedroom infill homes illustrate how density and family suitability can coexist. The policy measurement problem is therefore to track bedroom mix, location, price, tenure stability, and construction volume together.

## Key Claims
- Total unit production can overstate the supply available to households seeking space for children.
- Bedroom count and housing form may affect family formation separately from rent level.
- Dense housing and family suitability are compatible when design and unit mix support them.
- Strong price growth for larger homes can signal unmet demand but does not quantify fertility effects.
- Housing fit removes only one constraint among childcare, work, relationships, preferences, and biological timing.

## Evidence
- Unit-mix distinction: [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-dde046734f50fc7c6a18ae9c19ada137-55355169-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-dde046734f50fc7c6a18ae9c19ada137-55355169]] contrasts New York's high completion count with its concentration in studios and one-bedroom apartments.
- Fertility association: [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-dde046734f50fc7c6a18ae9c19ada137-55355169-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-dde046734f50fc7c6a18ae9c19ada137-55355169]] attributes to [[LymanStone]] low birth rates among studio and one-bedroom residents and demand for three-bedroom homes.
- Dense family form: [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-dde046734f50fc7c6a18ae9c19ada137-55355169-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-dde046734f50fc7c6a18ae9c19ada137-55355169]] describes smaller Austin homes that retain three bedrooms after lot and density reform.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Household selection is substantial: people without children may choose smaller homes, while prospective parents may move before births. Cultural expectations about bedrooms, yards, and tenure differ across places, and larger homes can consume more land or cost more to produce. The episode does not establish a causal fertility effect from bedroom count or a universal family-housing standard.

## What Changed
- Created a unit-mix qualification for housing-supply and fertility policy.
