# Cross-Category Brand Trust / 跨品类品牌信任

## Definition
Cross-category brand trust is a customer's willingness to treat good experience with one product as useful evidence when considering a different product from the same company.

## Current Synthesis
[[Laifen|Laifen's]] stated moat thesis is that small-appliance technology can be copied, while repeated delivery of useful, attractive, reasonably priced, and well-supported products can make trust travel from hair dryers into other personal-care and household categories. That transfer is conditional rather than automatic. A category-leading first product supplies attention and a prior, but later products still need a distinct user benefit, reliable delivery, and coherent brand meaning. Launching many technically interesting devices without strong reasons to choose them can dilute rather than compound trust.

## Key Claims
- Product-level satisfaction can reduce uncertainty for a later purchase from the same brand.
- Trust transfer is stronger when categories share users, use contexts, technical capabilities, service expectations, or design language.
- A famous first product is an entry asset, not proof that every adjacent product deserves adoption.
- Repeated design, experience, pricing, quality, and after-sales delivery can become harder to copy than one technical feature.
- Multi-category ambition raises the required product-success rate and increases organizational dependence on distributed judgment.

## Evidence
- Moat thesis: [[ll4ekwtvjnpxit2gdkvcfphf0eli-ll4ekwtvjnpxit2gdkvcfphf0eli]] reports Ye's view that small-appliance technical advantages are rarely permanent and brand trust must be earned repeatedly.
- Category adjacency: [[ll4ekwtvjnpxit2gdkvcfphf0eli-ll4ekwtvjnpxit2gdkvcfphf0eli]] links hair dryers and toothbrushes through personal care, overlapping users, and motor capability.
- Transfer challenge: [[ll4ekwtvjnpxit2gdkvcfphf0eli-ll4ekwtvjnpxit2gdkvcfphf0eli]] records Luo's view that some later products remained good but lacked the first hair dryer's strong must-have reason.
- Portfolio ambition: [[ll4ekwtvjnpxit2gdkvcfphf0eli-ll4ekwtvjnpxit2gdkvcfphf0eli]] describes more than ten concurrent R&D projects and a long-run goal of twenty to thirty categories.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source offers a strategy and founder belief, not direct evidence that Laifen trust already transfers across categories. Buyers may associate a brand narrowly with its first product, compare each new category against a different incumbent, or interpret low price as the main reason for purchase. Overextension can increase quality, inventory, service, and attention risk faster than trust compounds.
