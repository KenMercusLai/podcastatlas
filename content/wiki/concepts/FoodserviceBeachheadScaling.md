# Food-Service Beachhead Scaling

## Definition

Food-service beachhead scaling is a bottom-up route into restaurant distribution: start with small operators that already understand the category, prove service performance and repeat use, build chef advocacy and case studies, and approach national chains only after supply and quality systems are ready.

## Current Synthesis

[[BoostCoos]] illustrates why product appeal is only half the sale. A young CPG company also has to show that the product cooks quickly, holds well, works in a serving line, arrives reliably, and can be supported without breaking the organization. Small restaurants reduce buyer education and make operational learning cheaper; recognized chefs can extend that proof without replacing it.

## Key Claims
- Small restaurants can provide faster and more forgiving operational learning than national chains.
- Choosing operators already familiar with the category reduces simultaneous product and category education.
- Restaurant trials should test cook time, holding stability, serving-line fit, waste, and reorder behavior.
- Chef adoption can create credibility and new use cases, but it should support rather than substitute for operating evidence.
- National-chain readiness requires supply reliability, quality assurance, purchasing compliance, staff, and equipment.
- A company's suitability for a channel matters as much as the product's suitability for the customer.

## Evidence
- Beachhead selection: [[advice-line-with-danny-meyer-of-shake-shack-c7199e93-5dba-43ab-8fb0-91403917c3ac]] recommends one- or two-location restaurants that already serve couscous.
- Operational learning: [[advice-line-with-danny-meyer-of-shake-shack-c7199e93-5dba-43ab-8fb0-91403917c3ac]] identifies cooking speed, holding stability, and high-volume line performance as restaurant tests.
- Advocacy: [[advice-line-with-danny-meyer-of-shake-shack-c7199e93-5dba-43ab-8fb0-91403917c3ac]] recommends six to twelve recognized chefs who already use couscous as proof points.
- Scale gate: [[advice-line-with-danny-meyer-of-shake-shack-c7199e93-5dba-43ab-8fb0-91403917c3ac]] contrasts early-company capacity with national chains' supply, quality, and purchasing requirements.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Success with independent restaurants may not generalize to national chains whose procurement, price, volume, preparation, and consistency requirements differ. Chef enthusiasm can overrepresent novelty, and the episode reports no reorder, waste, margin, or throughput results.

## What Changed
- Added a staged food-service entry model that combines buyer education, operational proof, advocacy, and organizational readiness.
