## Definition
Oral history memory analysis is the method of treating interview testimony as both evidence about past events and evidence about how people remember, organize, revise, and present the past.

## Current Synthesis
Li Ji's episode treats oral history as disciplined listening, not extraction. The historian may arrive with archive questions, but must sit and listen before forcing the material into a prior frame. At the same time, memory can be mistaken, unconscious, shaped by desired self-image, or organized by later family and religious identity. Oral material therefore gains strength when read alongside documents, places, genealogy, and historical context.

## Key Claims
- Listening matters before interrogation because older interviewees may carry contexts not present in the researcher's initial question.
- Memory error is not only noise; it can show how people want to see themselves, family, religion, or local history.
- Oral testimony should be analyzed rather than accepted as direct fact restoration.
- Cross-checking with archives, genealogy, and field sites makes oral history stronger.
- The method connects living descendants to archival subjects without pretending descendant memory is identical to the original event.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not make oral history superior to written archives. It argues for triangulation and analysis because both memory and archives are partial.
