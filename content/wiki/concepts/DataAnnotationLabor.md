# Data Annotation Labor / 数据标注劳动

## Definition
[[DataAnnotationLabor]] is the human work of labeling, transcribing, classifying, checking, or describing data so machine-learning and automated systems can be trained, evaluated, or operated.

## Current Synthesis
The Yonghe case makes AI's hidden labor chain visible. Workers boxed vehicles and products, transcribed text, checked results, categorized unsafe material, collected dialect speech, and later attempted more complex historical or 3D tasks. Low entry requirements made early projects accessible, but this accessibility also made basic work easy to standardize, underprice, and partially automate.

The durable unit of analysis is therefore not “the annotation market” alone. Task complexity, customer integration, required expertise, local recruitment capacity, management regime, and whether workers gain portable skills determine who benefits as the market changes.

## Key Claims
- Human labeling supports products that appear automated to end users.
- Basic annotation can create accessible employment where formal local alternatives are scarce.
- Standardization enables scale but also piece-rate control, price compression, and automation.
- Annotation includes both routine perception tasks and high-risk safety review.
- Market growth can coincide with geographic and worker-level displacement when demand moves to specialized tasks or in-house teams.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
One county cannot establish the national composition or trajectory of annotation labor. Continued market growth may create other jobs and higher-skill roles, while the episode does not compare wages, retention, training, or automation across firms and regions. The claimed female share and market projections remain source-scoped.

## What Changed
- Added annotation as a labor process rather than only a technical data-preparation step.
- Added the distinction between aggregate market growth and retention of specific workers, tasks, and locations.
