# Standing Genetic Variation

## Definition
Standing genetic variation is inherited diversity already present within a population before an environmental change makes one or more variants advantageous.

## Current Synthesis
The episode uses standing variation to explain why adaptation need not wait for a new beneficial mutation. A variant may remain neutral or inconsequential under one set of conditions and become valuable after a population moves to high altitude, adopts a demanding subsistence practice, or encounters a different diet or disease environment.

This model also clarifies the meaning of evolutionary fitness. Selection favors variants that improve fit to a particular environment and reproductive context, not traits that make a person or population universally superior. Culture can help create the recurring environment in which a variant becomes advantageous.

## Key Claims
- Useful evolutionary variation can predate the environment that makes it useful.
- Environmental change can shift the reproductive value of an existing variant.
- Adaptation from standing variation can occur faster than waiting for a new mutation.
- Fitness means context-specific fit rather than progress toward perfection.
- Cultural practices can maintain selection pressures that interact with existing inherited diversity.

- Core mechanism: [[behaviors-that-alter-your-genes-to-improve-your-health-performance-dr-melissa-ilardo-scim2045300705]] defines standing variation as diversity already present when conditions change.

- Diving example: [[behaviors-that-alter-your-genes-to-improve-your-health-performance-dr-melissa-ilardo-scim2045300705]] asks whether sustained breath-hold diving selected variants related to spleen size, thyroid signaling, and oxygen availability.
- Fitness boundary: [[behaviors-that-alter-your-genes-to-improve-your-health-performance-dr-melissa-ilardo-scim2045300705]] replaces a ladder-of-progress picture with "best fit" to environment.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The presence of a population association does not by itself establish when selection occurred, which variant is causal, or how much individual performance it explains. Standing variation can support faster adaptation, but drift, migration, population structure, developmental plasticity, and cultural transmission can produce or complicate similar patterns.
