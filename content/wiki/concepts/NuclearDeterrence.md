# Nuclear Deterrence

## Definition

Nuclear deterrence is the attempt to prevent attack by making credible the prospect that nuclear aggression will bring unacceptable retaliation, including mutual national destruction.

## Current Synthesis

The combined record presents the strongest case and the central qualification together. Fear of retaliation may have prevented deliberate nuclear launch and a conventional Third World War, including during [[AbleArcher83|Able Archer 83]], but deterrence did not eliminate danger. Its operation depended on leaders perceiving signals accurately, command systems functioning, forces remaining controlled, and crises not converting ambiguity into panic. [[92-nuclear-weapons-glt2236644203]]

Eighty years without wartime nuclear use is historically exceptional, but non-use cannot be separated from the institutional achievement of limiting proliferation. Deterrence is therefore a conditional restraint system inside a wider [[NuclearNonproliferationOrder]], not proof of safe or fully rational nuclear governance. [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-graham-allison-on-the-global-realignment-iran-china-israel-greenland-40373425]]

## Key Claims

- Credible retaliatory destruction can make initiating nuclear war appear suicidal.
- Deterrence may inhibit both nuclear attack and conventional conflict among nuclear powers.
- Retaliatory capability also requires readiness, which can shorten decision time during a false warning or misread exercise.
- Stable deterrence depends on communication, restraint, legible limits, technical reliability, and some degree of luck.
- More nuclear actors and flashpoints may multiply pathways for misunderstanding without making escalation inevitable.
- The post-1945 record of nuclear non-use is an achievement to explain and maintain, not evidence that use has become impossible.

## Evidence

### Restraint through unacceptable retaliation

- [[92-nuclear-weapons-glt2236644203]] argues that Soviet leaders did not launch during the 1983 scare because retaliation would have amounted to national suicide.

### Conditional and fragile stability

- [[92-nuclear-weapons-glt2236644203]] pairs deterrence with Able Archer misperception, aircraft accidents, damaged weapons, false alarms, and crisis panic.

### Exceptional non-use within a limited nuclear order

- [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-graham-allison-on-the-global-realignment-iran-china-israel-greenland-40373425]] uses the second "80" in "80-80-9" for years without wartime nuclear use and the "9" for the number of nuclear-armed states.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The absence of nuclear war does not by itself prove deterrence caused peace; diplomacy, conventional conditions, arms control, nonproliferation, institutional restraint, contingency, and luck may also matter. Both sources are broad conversations rather than systematic comparisons of deterrence theories. Allison's figures for latent nuclear capability and North Korea's arsenal remain episode-attributed, and the multipolar-risk discussion is cautionary rather than a prediction that a particular dispute will cross the threshold.

## What Changed

- Established deterrence as a conditional restraint model whose claimed success must be evaluated alongside misperception and command-system fragility.
- Added eighty years of wartime non-use as an outcome requiring explanation rather than proof of permanent safety.
- Distinguished deterrence from the related nonproliferation system that limits the number of deterrence relationships.
