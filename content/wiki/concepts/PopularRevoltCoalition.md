# Popular Revolt Coalition

## Definition

A popular revolt coalition is a temporary alignment of groups whose overlapping targets permit joint action despite different social positions, motives, ideologies, and preferred outcomes.

## Current Synthesis

The [[PeasantsRevolt1381|1381 revolt]] began as more than an undifferentiated peasant crowd. Commercially connected and upwardly mobile communities joined poorer laborers, horse-borne organizers linked villages, and the Bocking oath articulated a shared political commitment before Kentish and Essex forces converged on London. There the movement widened to townspeople, released prisoners, religious radicals, commercial rivals, and political opportunists.

Shared enemies included servitude, wage controls, poll taxation, corrupt officials, manorial and ecclesiastical power, monopolies, foreign merchants, and figures tied to [[JohnOfGaunt|John of Gaunt]]. Yet agreement on targets did not amount to a unified program.

Coalition breadth explains both capacity and ambiguity. Local knowledge may have enabled attacks on figures such as Richard Lyons, while help or persuasion may explain access to London and the Tower. The same openness that made rapid expansion possible also admitted elite score-settling, xenophobic murder, sanctuary violations, and demands extending from free trade to radical redistribution of church power.

The framework resists assigning the entire revolt a modern left-right identity. Its components could be egalitarian, anticlerical, royalist, anti-tax, anti-regulatory, commercially motivated, or violently exclusionary at the same time.

## Key Claims

- Common targets can coordinate actors who do not share one ideology or social class.
- Transport, oath-taking, and inter-village organizers can turn local grievances into a regional movement.
- Urban knowledge, insider help, and elite rivalry can amplify a movement originating outside the city.
- Coalition breadth increases tactical capacity while weakening control over violence and political meaning.
- A revolt’s later ideological memory often selects one strand and suppresses incompatible elements.
- Modern left-right labels cannot adequately describe the combination of egalitarianism, royalism, market demands, religious radicalism, and xenophobia.

## Evidence

- Pre-London organization: [[413-the-peasants-revolt-england-erupts-part-1-glt8198769595]] traces horse-borne coordinators, village networks, the Bocking oath, and parallel escalation in Essex and Kent.
- Social base: [[413-the-peasants-revolt-england-erupts-part-1-glt8198769595]] emphasizes assertive and upwardly mobile participants alongside laborers subject to taxation and servile controls.
- Social breadth: [[414-the-peasants-revolt-londons-burning-part-2-glt6726049231]] connects Kent and Essex rebels with Londoners, prisoners, wealthy rivals, preachers, and opportunists.
- Mixed program: [[414-the-peasants-revolt-londons-burning-part-2-glt6726049231]] combines abolition of servitude and manorial courts with attacks on tolls, monopolies, church hierarchy, and supposed traitors.
- Coalition harms: [[414-the-peasants-revolt-londons-burning-part-2-glt6726049231]] records anti-Flemish killings, sanctuary violations, summary executions, and commercial score-settling.
- Later interpretation: [[414-the-peasants-revolt-londons-burning-part-2-glt6726049231]] compares radical, Protestant, American, free-market, and Thatcherite readings while emphasizing ambiguity.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Coalition language should not erase leadership, coordination, or genuine shared commitments. Nor does ideological diversity excuse violence or make every interpretation equally strong. Exact social proportions and the degree of advance planning remain uncertain; the sources suggest but cannot prove insider assistance at several sites, and their long-run ideological comparisons are interpretive rather than evidence of direct transmission.

## What Changed

- Added the pre-London mechanisms of regional coordination: mobile organizers, village networks, and oath-taking.
- Qualified the “peasant” label with evidence for ambitious and commercially connected participants.
