# Fortified Attrition Patience / 坚壁耗敌式耐心

Fortified attrition patience / 坚壁耗敌式耐心 is the conquest-side waiting strategy [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-118-1-wangjian-lv-liushiwan-dajun-duizhan-xiangyan-haoxi-kaichang-le-lsf2puinwg0m3bgyi25znof7aznu]] attaches to [[WangJianQin|王翦]]'s campaign against [[ChuState|楚国]]. After [[QinShiHuang|嬴政 / 秦始皇]] grants the 600,000 troops Wang Jian demanded, Wang Jian still refuses immediate decision against [[XiangYan|项燕]].

The patience is active rather than timid. Wang Jian builds and holds fortifications, declines Chu challenges, lets [[QinState|秦国]]'s stronger logistics and food base become a weapon, and watches whether his own soldiers remain physically ready and eager to fight. The source's detail about stone-throwing contests matters because it gives Wang Jian a camp-level signal that the army's energy has not decayed into passivity.

This concept differs from [[FrontierDefensivePatience|边境防御耐心]]. Li Mu uses patience to protect a frontier from mobile raiders until he can bait them into a prepared field. Wang Jian uses patience while invading a major state: he lets the defender's mobilization become expensive, waits until its morale and supply fall, and attacks when withdrawal makes the enemy's formation psychologically and operationally loose.

## Key Claims
- Refusing battle can be a positive command choice when the commander's logistics are stronger than the enemy's.
- Fortified waiting needs discipline, supply, and morale monitoring; otherwise it becomes passivity.
- A large army does not remove the timing problem. Wang Jian's 600,000 troops matter because he can sustain waiting, not because numbers alone make immediate attack wise.
- Attrition can target morale and food before it targets bodies.
- The attack window appears when the opponent stops expecting battle, begins to withdraw, or otherwise reveals that the will to force decision has weakened.
