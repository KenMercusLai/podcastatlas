# Fortified Attrition Patience / 坚壁耗敌式耐心

## Definition
Fortified attrition patience / 坚壁耗敌式耐心 is a command pattern in which defended positions, supply, morale discipline, and enemy fatigue become active resources. The point is not avoiding battle indefinitely; it is waiting when time harms the opponent more than oneself and then acting when exhaustion or movement creates the better window.

## Current Synthesis
The current concept spans short field retreats, siege relief, conquest warfare, and pre-Guandu strategy. In Hanji 925, [[HuangfuSongLateHan|皇甫嵩]] retreats into [[ChangsheBattleLateHan|长社]], lets [[BoCaiLateHan|波才]]'s [[YellowTurbansLateHan|Yellow Turban]] camp tire, and then uses night fire and pursuit. In Hanji 942, Huangfu Song refuses [[DongZhuo|董卓]]'s immediate-relief impulse because [[ChenCang|陈仓]] can hold while [[WangGuoLateHan|王国]]'s besiegers pay the higher cost. In Qinji 118-1, [[WangJianQin|王翦]] uses fortified refusal against [[XiangYan|项燕]] because Qin can feed and discipline a long pause better than Chu can sustain total mobilization.

Hanji 1042 adds a late-Han grand-strategy variant before [[GuanduCampaign|官渡之战]]. [[TianFengLateHan|田丰]] advises [[YuanShao|袁绍]] to hold defensible terrain, cultivate local support and agriculture, prepare arms, connect with outside heroes, and send elite cavalry to harass [[CaoCao|曹操]] south of the Yellow River. The plan makes patience offensive through pressure, but Yuan Shao rejects it because he wants a more direct decision.

## Key Claims
- Patience is active only when defended positions, logistics, morale, and enemy cost trends are deliberately managed.
- The tactic can appear as local defensive retreat, delayed siege relief, large-scale conquest posture, or theater-level harassment strategy.
- Stronger resources can justify waiting when they make the opponent's mobilization, food, or movement costs compound faster.
- Waiting needs a switch mechanism: fire attack, pursuit, cavalry raids, or other pressure must turn accumulated enemy fatigue into result.
- The failure mode is political as well as tactical; leaders may reject patience because direct battle better satisfies pride, status, or visible decisiveness.

## Evidence
- Field-retreat version: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-925-tuoxie-bushi-rensong-xuehui-tuoxie-lu-geng-changliang-limafqskgblrwetestyh39w-hzm4|Hanji 925]] links Huangfu Song's Changshe retreat to enemy fatigue, night fire, and pursuit.
- Siege-relief version: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-942-yue-youxiu-de-ren-huode-yue-lei-heli-ma-lkwlrcgnt-kxxc2qqz6z6wkg7e5z|Hanji 942]] has Huangfu Song delay relief while Chen Cang holds, then pursue Wang Guo's withdrawing and exhausted army.
- Conquest-side version: [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-118-1-wangjian-lv-liushiwan-dajun-duizhan-xiangyan-haoxi-kaichang-le-lsf2puinwg0m3bgyi25znof7aznu|Qinji 118-1]] shows Wang Jian fortifying, refusing Xiang Yan's challenges, monitoring Qin morale, and attacking only when Chu begins to withdraw.
- Pre-Guandu strategic version: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1042-congming-haishi-wangu-tianfeng-yin-dingzhuang-yuanshao-ruyu-lk5x6if2rouarmgichtynzhl3cgs|Hanji 1042]] presents Tian Feng's advice to Yuan Shao as a defended-terrain, agriculture, preparation, alliance, and cavalry-harassment plan against Cao Cao.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Fortified attrition patience is not a universal preference for delay. It depends on the defended side's supply, cohesion, morale, and ability to recognize or create the later action window. Hanji 1042 is especially counterfactual: the episode argues that Tian Feng's plan was workable, but the wiki should not treat that as proof that Yuan Shao would certainly have defeated Cao Cao.
