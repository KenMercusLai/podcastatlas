# Workstation Posture Adjustment / 工位姿势调整

## Definition
Workstation posture adjustment is the source's practice of fitting screen position, viewing distance, chair and lumbar support, desk proximity, keyboard and elbow placement, and task layout to the worker while pairing those changes with regular movement and symptom-sensitive capacity building.

## Current Synthesis
VOL.89 treats the workstation as one modifiable part of a larger movement system. Raising a low screen, moving closer to the desk, supporting the lumbar region, and resting the forearms near the keyboard may reduce repeated forward reaching or neck extension. Multi-screen or asymmetric work also matters when it repeatedly directs the head or trunk to one side.

These adjustments are not a cure or a demand for rigid posture. Chair design, cushions, lumbar supports, and screen placement can redistribute load, but they do not remove the effects of prolonged stillness or diagnose persistent pain. The durable approach combines an individually usable setup with position changes, walking, wrist or trunk movement, gradual mobility and strength work, and assessment when symptoms persist or radiate.

## Key Claims
- Screen height and viewing distance should reduce avoidable forward-head positioning without forcing one identical setup on every worker.
- Desk proximity and forearm support can reduce unnecessary reaching and sustained shoulder loading.
- Lumbar supports and shaped cushions can redistribute pressure but do not make uninterrupted sitting harmless.
- Repeatedly facing one side screen or performing an asymmetric occupation can create a different exposure from centered desk work.
- A tolerable workstation should support movement variability rather than lock the worker into a single “correct” pose.
- Persistent, severe, radiating, numb, or function-limiting symptoms require assessment rather than further equipment experimentation alone.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a public-education conversation, not a comparative ergonomics trial, and it supplies no universal measurements for screen height, desk distance, chair angle, cushion shape, break interval, or exercise dose. Comfort and reduced reaching do not prove that a setup prevents disc disease or corrects structural alignment. Vision needs, disability, pain diagnosis, body dimensions, job constraints, and equipment availability can change the appropriate configuration.

## What Changed
- Established workstation fit as a conditional layer within movement variability and individualized rehabilitation.
