# 自辩式保权 / Power-Retention Self-Justification

## Definition

自辩式保权 / power-retention self-justification is a rhetorical and political pattern in which an actor acknowledges suspicion created by concentrated power, offers a bounded concession, and argues that retaining the core coercive capacity is necessary for personal safety and public order.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 1115 grounds the concept in [[SelfClarificationEdict|《自明本志令》]]. [[CaoCao|曹操]] explains his power through a career narrative and a counterfactual claim that his presence prevents further fragmentation. He is willing to return part of his fief, which signals that not every benefit is indispensable, but he refuses to surrender military command because loss of force would expose himself and his descendants.

The pattern differs from a clean retirement claim. It makes the actor's continued power simultaneously the object of public suspicion and the proposed remedy for instability. The argument may describe a genuine security bind, strategic reputation management, or both; the source cannot independently settle the balance between them.

## Key Claims

- Accumulated merit is used to explain why extraordinary power arose and why it should not be read only as private ambition.
- The actor concedes the legitimacy of public suspicion without conceding that the core power must be surrendered.
- A partial concession separates expendable rewards from non-negotiable coercive capacity.
- Personal, family, and regime security are rhetorically joined so that disarmament appears dangerous to more than the officeholder.
- The justification is strongest as evidence of public positioning, not as transparent proof of private motive.

## Evidence

- Merit and counterfactual order: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1115-caocao-jian-tongquetai-zhen-jiu-weile-suo-erqiao-lhqombf5uxbwvg_pz8atvk9l-izs|Hanji 1115]] presents Cao Cao's campaign record and his claim that more actors would have declared kingship or empire without him.
- Suspicion and bounded concession: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1115-caocao-jian-tongquetai-zhen-jiu-weile-suo-erqiao-lhqombf5uxbwvg_pz8atvk9l-izs|Hanji 1115]] has Cao acknowledge doubts about his intentions and offer to return part of his fief.
- Security boundary: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1115-caocao-jian-tongquetai-zhen-jiu-weile-suo-erqiao-lhqombf5uxbwvg_pz8atvk9l-izs|Hanji 1115]] makes military command the line he will not cross because disarmament would endanger him and his descendants.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept currently rests on one podcast's summary of one political text. A public document can mix candor, persuasion, legal positioning, and strategic concealment, so stated necessity should not be treated as independent proof that retaining power was the only route to order. Returning part of a fief may reduce criticism without materially reducing command, making the concession meaningful but asymmetric.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from the edict's combination of retrospective merit, acknowledged suspicion, partial concession, and command retention.
