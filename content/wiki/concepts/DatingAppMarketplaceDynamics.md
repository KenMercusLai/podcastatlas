# Dating-App Marketplace Dynamics

## Definition
Dating-app marketplace dynamics are the attention and selection patterns that appear when romantic interest is routed through profiles, first impressions, swipes, filters, and visible desirability cues rather than repeated in-person acquaintance.

## Current Synthesis
The source treats dating apps as the setting where marketplace models of attraction have the most explanatory force. Apps emphasize first impressions, trait filters, photos, quick written style, and asymmetric swipe behavior, so attention can concentrate around a small group of highly desired profiles.

That app logic is not presented as the whole dating world. Eastwick contrasts it with ordinary acquaintanceship, where repeated contact, friends, group activities, responsiveness, and shared narratives can create [[IdiosyncraticAttraction]] that profile ranking would miss.

## Key Claims
- Dating apps amplify first-impression selection because profiles foreground visible traits before shared context exists.
- Unequal attention can become self-reinforcing when users infer desirability from perceived demand or peer interest.
- App filters can help with genuine deal-breakers but can overstate how well abstract preferences predict face-to-face attraction.
- Text-based dating may reward written wit, cadence matching, and hyperverbal fluency while obscuring embodied behavior.
- The app context can be useful for some people, including some same-gender daters, but it remains a partial dating environment rather than a complete compatibility test.

## Evidence
- First-impression fit: [[science-of-attraction-compatibility-romance-dr-paul-eastwick-scim9984287085]] says marketplace ideas can describe initial attraction settings where people meet for the first time.
- Swipe asymmetry: [[science-of-attraction-compatibility-romance-dr-paul-eastwick-scim9984287085]] reports source-scoped claims that women swipe yes on about 5 percent of men they see while men swipe yes about half the time.
- Filter boundary: [[science-of-attraction-compatibility-romance-dr-paul-eastwick-scim9984287085]] says online filters may help with deal-breakers such as wanting children, but abstract preferences often fail after face-to-face meetings.
- Texting and cadence: [[science-of-attraction-compatibility-romance-dr-paul-eastwick-scim9984287085]] discusses how text communication can help anxious people and advantage people who match style, cadence, and wit.
- Same-gender dating qualification: [[science-of-attraction-compatibility-romance-dr-paul-eastwick-scim9984287085]] says apps have helped some gay and lesbian people find partners where local networks were limited or unsafe.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source says dating-app companies generally do not share data with researchers, so the app evidence remains incomplete. Apps can solve real discovery problems and should not be treated only as harmful. The concept also should not be generalized to all attraction because the same source argues that repeated contact can sharply change what people want or perceive.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to separate app-based attention markets from broader relationship formation.
- Qualified dating-app pessimism by preserving both the unequal-attention risk and the discovery value for some users.
