# Heart-Rate Variability

## Definition
Heart-rate variability, or HRV, is variation in the time interval between successive heartbeats, used in the bounded source as one signal of autonomic regulation, recovery, and stress relative to an individual's baseline.

## Current Synthesis
HRV is not the same as average heart rate. WHOOP uses repeated HRV measurements within recovery and stress interpretation, with personal trajectory treated as more informative than comparison with another person's number. The source describes higher HRV as generally associated with healthier sympathetic-parasympathetic regulation and notes that HRV often declines with age.

Those broad associations do not make one reading a diagnosis or a direct measure of longevity. HRV varies with measurement conditions, sleep, illness, training, stress, age, and individual physiology; a device score can support pattern recognition only when its acquisition and interpretation are sufficiently reliable.

## Key Claims
- HRV measures beat-to-beat interval variation rather than average beats per minute.
- Repeated within-person trends are generally more interpretable than cross-person comparison.
- HRV can contribute to recovery and stress estimates alongside other signals and context.
- Age, illness, training, sleep, stress, and measurement conditions can change HRV.
- HRV alone does not identify a disease, prove readiness, or demonstrate longevity benefit.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not provide acquisition protocol, validation study, artifact handling, effect sizes, diagnostic thresholds, age norms, or outcome evidence. Its longevity interpretation and autonomic explanation are condensed public education, not an individualized clinical assessment or proof that raising a wearable HRV score improves health.
