# 指鹿为马 / Pointing Deer As Horse

指鹿为马 / pointing deer as horse is the public obedience test [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-137-shendu-jiedu-zhiluweima-zhong-de-quanli-boyi-lgkjmddvrcelw2ltdwt92e4poguj|Qinji 137]] draws from [[ZhaoGao|赵高]]'s court politics after [[LiSi|李斯]] is dead and Zhao Gao has become chancellor. Zhao Gao brings a deer before [[QinErshi|胡亥 / 秦二世]], calls it a horse, and watches how officials answer when the emperor asks whether it is a deer or a horse.

The episode's interpretation is that the test is not about zoology or confusion. Zhao Gao already knows the claim is false; the point is to separate those willing to repeat his falsehood from those still willing to state the visible fact. The absurdity makes the loyalty signal stronger because everyone understands the cost of truth before speaking.

The later purge completes the mechanism. Officials who say "deer" are later trapped through legal accusation, and the rest of the court learns that visible reality cannot protect them if Zhao Gao controls punishment. The result is a harsher form of [[CourtFeedbackCollapse|君臣反馈失灵]]: speech no longer fails only because advice is ignored, but because factual correction itself marks a person for elimination.

## Key Claims
- An obvious falsehood can be useful to power because it tests obedience more clearly than a plausible lie.
- The test maps the court before the purge: answers become a list of who can be pressured, spared, or destroyed.
- Public compliance with absurdity changes the court's epistemic norm; silence and repetition become safer than correction.
- The mechanism links false speech to procedure because Zhao Gao later uses law to punish those who answered truthfully.
- The episode keeps the idiom political: "指鹿为马" is a purge technology before it is a moral anecdote about lying.
