# Campaign Coordination Without Communication

## Definition

Campaign coordination without communication is the failure mode in which separated forces are expected to converge or support one another even though they cannot exchange timely status, revise shared assumptions, or reliably know whether the other parts of the plan remain active.

## Current Synthesis

[[PhilipSheridan]]'s 1876 campaign assigned [[AlfredTerry]], [[JohnGibbon]], and [[GeorgeCrook]] different approaches toward a mobile Lakota and Cheyenne coalition. The geometry looked coordinated, but the columns lacked reliable communications, their commanders did not fully trust one another, and the target could move faster than information passed among them.

The [[BattleOfRosebud]] made the defect concrete. Crook stopped advancing after a six-hour engagement but did not notify Terry. When Terry released [[GeorgeArmstrongCuster]], he still operated on the assumption that the southern column was converging. Discretionary orders then pushed adaptation down to an ambitious field commander whose own information about village size and location was incomplete.

## Key Claims

- A converging map is not a coordinated operation unless state changes can propagate among participants.
- Mobile opponents magnify the cost of stale assumptions.
- Mutual distrust makes ambiguous silence harder to interpret.
- A missing column can leave the rest of a force acting on a plan that no longer exists.
- Broad discretion may compensate for communication limits while increasing divergence and accountability ambiguity.

## Evidence

- Structural design: [[451-custers-last-stand-the-charge-of-the-7th-cavalry-part-6-glt4301705603]] describes three columns approaching from different directions without reliable communication.
- Trust limits: [[451-custers-last-stand-the-charge-of-the-7th-cavalry-part-6-glt4301705603]] notes dislike and distrust among commanders.
- Silent state change: [[451-custers-last-stand-the-charge-of-the-7th-cavalry-part-6-glt4301705603]] says Crook's Rosebud check and halted advance were unknown to Terry's officers.
- Delegated adaptation: [[451-custers-last-stand-the-charge-of-the-7th-cavalry-part-6-glt4301705603]] presents Terry's orders as leaving Custer room to attack before full convergence.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

- Nineteenth-century campaigning often required delegated judgment; communication limits alone do not make convergence irrational.
- Custer's later decisions cannot be reduced to the absent Crook column, because reconnaissance, fatigue, logistics, assumptions, and personal incentives also mattered.
- The concept identifies structural vulnerability, not proof that real-time communication would have produced victory.
- The current synthesis is grounded in one popular-history episode rather than comparative campaign research.

## What Changed

- Established the concept from the 1876 three-column campaign.
