# Synthetic Performers

## Definition
Synthetic performers are AI-generated actor-like figures used in scenes, demonstrations, or entertainment products without being conventional human actors.

## Current Synthesis
[[TillyNorwood]] makes the category concrete for the wiki. The episode describes an arrest scene in which the performer, crew, and production setup do not exist outside generated media, then contrasts the four-minute generation process with a much slower and more expensive live-action setup. Synthetic performers therefore sit between visual effect, actor, character, and model output.

The current judgment is that synthetic performers are not yet substitutes for star labor, but they are strong enough to unsettle casting, cost, likeness, and provenance assumptions. They attract producers because they compress production time, but they trigger resistance because they can appear to bypass actors, crews, contracts, authorship, and audience expectations about human presence.

## Key Claims
- A synthetic performer can demonstrate production compression before it becomes a credible replacement for human stars.
- The category unsettles the boundary between character, actor, visual effect, and generated asset.
- Synthetic performers are most controversial when they appear to substitute for paid creative labor or human likeness.
- Quality remains a constraint because generated scenes may need repeated takes and refinement before they feel film-like.
- Disclosure matters because viewers and workers evaluate the same image differently when they learn no human performer or crew was present.

## Evidence
- Concrete case - [[stars-in-their-ais-tech-is-changing-hollywood-6a917d0b63de6f7b41319a4b]] identifies Tilly Norwood as an AI-generated actor.
- Production compression - [[stars-in-their-ais-tech-is-changing-hollywood-6a917d0b63de6f7b41319a4b]] says the Tilly Norwood London scene took about four minutes to create.
- Quality boundary - [[stars-in-their-ais-tech-is-changing-hollywood-6a917d0b63de6f7b41319a4b]] describes the generated clip as imperfect and still needing more takes for film-like quality.
- Labor comparison - [[stars-in-their-ais-tech-is-changing-hollywood-6a917d0b63de6f7b41319a4b]] contrasts the AI scene with a live-action London shoot that would take about half a day and cost more than GBP5,000.
- Replacement boundary - [[stars-in-their-ais-tech-is-changing-hollywood-6a917d0b63de6f7b41319a4b]] says AI is not currently good or creative enough to replace human actors on the red carpet.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not establish how Tilly Norwood was trained, licensed, represented, or used commercially. It also does not prove that audiences would accept synthetic performers in long-form work or that studios can resolve guild, likeness, and credit questions around them.
