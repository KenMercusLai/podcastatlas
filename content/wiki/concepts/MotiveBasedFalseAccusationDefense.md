## Definition

Motive-Based False-Accusation Defense / 动机反证式辩诬 is the response pattern where a person facing a hard-to-disprove accusation avoids being trapped in exhaustive self-proof and instead argues from motive, expected benefit, values, and behavioral plausibility.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 531 defines the concept through [[BanJieyuWesternHan|班婕妤]]'s answer to a charge that she used wugu-style cursing against [[HanChengdi|汉成帝]]. The host emphasizes that false accusations can force the target into proving a negative, which is often impossible without decisive external evidence. Ban Jieyu's response shifts the question: if spirits exist, they would not accept a disloyal appeal; if they do not, cursing is useless; and if righteous conduct does not guarantee fortune, unrighteous conduct offers even less hope.

The episode turns that historical defense into a practical rule. When direct evidence exists, produce it. When direct evidence is unavailable because the accusation is invented or hidden by design, the answer can move to absence of motive, incompatibility with one's values, supporting circumstances, authoritative endorsement, and legal recourse. The method is not passive tolerance; it combines disciplined rebuttal with refusal to let a malicious accuser dictate the whole field of explanation.

## Key Claims

- Invented accusations create an asymmetry because proving that something never happened is much harder than asserting that it did.
- A strong reply can challenge the accusation's motive structure instead of trying to reconstruct every nonexistent detail.
- Values and stable behavior matter as evidence of plausibility, especially when the alleged act would not benefit the accused.
- Evidence remains preferable when available; motive-based rebuttal is a supplement for claims designed to evade direct disproof.
- Reputation defense may require outside authority, public boundary-setting, or legal action when social or online slander creates lasting harm.
- The method works best when it avoids both helpless self-explanation and uncontrolled retaliation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept is based on one source and one interpreted court episode. It should not be generalized into a rule that all accusations are false or that motive analysis can replace factual investigation. The modern examples in the episode are commentary about slander dynamics rather than complete legal or evidentiary records.
