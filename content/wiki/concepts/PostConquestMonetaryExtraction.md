# Post-Conquest Monetary Extraction / 战后货币财政汲取

## Definition
Post-conquest monetary extraction is the rapid reconstruction of state purchasing power by defining a high-denomination currency, controlling where key goods can be exchanged, and using the new money for official procurement after military victory has depleted the treasury.

## Current Synthesis
Hanji 1146 supplies a single case through [[LiuBa|刘巴]]'s reported advice to [[LiuBei|刘备]] after the capture of Chengdu. The policy is described as a bundle: issue “值百钱,” require grain, salt, or other important goods to use the new currency, establish an official market, and purchase supplies for the government. In the episode's interpretation, low minting cost and compulsory market acceptance allow the regime to acquire real goods quickly.

The same mechanism distributes costs. Holders of old money lose relative purchasing power, while suppliers exchange goods for a state-defined claim. The current evidence supports the mechanism as an episode-level interpretation, not a measured conclusion about inflation, incidence, enforcement, or long-run success.

## Key Claims
- Military reward promises can convert victory into an immediate fiscal shortage.
- Coin denomination creates usable state purchasing power only when institutions and market rules support acceptance.
- Official procurement converts monetary authority into grain, cloth, and other real resources.
- Rapid treasury replenishment can shift losses toward old-money holders and civilian suppliers.
- Short-run fiscal success does not establish price stability, voluntary acceptance, or durable monetary legitimacy.

## Evidence
- Post-conquest shortage: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1146-liubei-qu-guafu-beihou-de-suanji-taishenle-lv4if2rvouqruziv8pt-klrcasfp|Hanji 1146]] links promised plunder of Chengdu government property to a depleted treasury.
- Policy bundle: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1146-liubei-qu-guafu-beihou-de-suanji-taishenle-lv4if2rvouqruziv8pt-klrcasfp|Hanji 1146]] combines high denomination, required use for important goods, an official market, and state purchasing.
- Distributional interpretation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1146-liubei-qu-guafu-beihou-de-suanji-taishenle-lv4if2rvouqruziv8pt-klrcasfp|Hanji 1146]] explicitly interprets old-money depreciation and cheap acquisition of goods as costs shifted to the population.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a structured popular-history summary, not a numismatic or price-history study. It does not provide coin weight and metal content, issue size, geographic reach, enforcement records, price series, tax rules, counterfeiting, later reforms, or household-level incidence. Calling the policy “hidden plunder” is the host's normative interpretation; the current page preserves the distributional question without treating that label as a settled historical verdict.

## What Changed
- Established a bounded concept linking post-conquest fiscal shortage, monetary authority, market control, procurement, and distributional cost.
