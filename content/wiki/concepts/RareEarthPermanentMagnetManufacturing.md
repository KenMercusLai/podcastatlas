# Rare Earth Permanent Magnet Manufacturing / 稀土永磁材料制造

## Definition

Rare earth permanent magnet manufacturing / 稀土永磁材料制造 is the industrial process of producing strong permanent magnets, especially sintered NdFeB, whose performance depends on composition, grain structure, heat behavior, machining, coating, and quality consistency.

## Current Synthesis

The Ningbo evidence in [[no-217-ningbo-wangshi-cong-fangtai-dao-shenzhou-cong-youyanji-dao-zhongguo-zui-da-fuzhuang-daigong-jutou-1010906666]] centers a manufacturing-process view of rare-earth magnets through [[NingboYunsheng|宁波韵生]]. The episode explains permanent magnets through electric motors: stronger magnets can make motors smaller, lighter, or more efficient, which connects NdFeB to electric vehicles, consumer electronics, industrial equipment, and robots.

The source also makes the manufacturing problem concrete. Sintered NdFeB involves alloying, strip casting, hydrogen decrepitation, jet milling, magnetic-field orientation pressing, vacuum sintering, cutting, grinding, high-temperature resistance processes, and plating. Small variation in formulation, temperature, grain, dimension, or machining can change magnetic performance.

## Key Claims

- Rare-earth magnet value is not only access to rare-earth elements; process control and consistency matter.
- NdFeB performance is strategic because motor efficiency, size, and weight affect many downstream products.
- Sintered magnet production is precision manufacturing, not commodity "magnet" work.
- Independent R&D can matter when imported technology is tied to market restrictions.
- The Ningbo case links magnetic materials to local foundations in machining, molds, motors, appliances, auto parts, and precision manufacturing.
