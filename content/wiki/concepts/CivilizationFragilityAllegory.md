# Civilization Fragility Allegory / 文明脆弱性寓言

## Definition
Civilization fragility allegory / 文明脆弱性寓言 is the episode's reading of [[LordOfTheFlies|《蝇王》]] as a story where social order fails when adults, institutions, routine, and technical support disappear.

## Current Synthesis
The concept names a literary pattern rather than a general law. In the episode, the island strips the boys down to improvised rule-making: conch, assemblies, shelters, signal fire, and role assignment. The collapse of those devices shows that civilization is not simply a possession; it must be continuously performed, enforced, and believed. The rescue ending intensifies the allegory because the adult world outside the island is also at war.

## Key Claims
- Civilization appears in the novel as fragile practice rather than automatic moral inheritance.
- Child characters make adult political violence visible by removing ordinary adult institutions.
- Rules fail when symbolic legitimacy cannot produce sustained participation.
- The island's violence mirrors the adult wartime world instead of opposing it.
- The frame should be kept literary and source-scoped, not converted into a universal proof of innate evil.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode itself complicates a simple civilized-good versus savage-evil split: Ralph is imperfect, hunting and pleasure are not inherently evil, and the destructive fire unexpectedly brings rescue. The concept is therefore a close-reading frame, not a deterministic claim about all children, disasters, or human beings.
