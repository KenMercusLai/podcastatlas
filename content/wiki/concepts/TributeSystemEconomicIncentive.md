## Definition

朝贡体系经济激励 / Tribute System Economic Incentive is the pattern where tribute participation is encouraged by material returns: travel money, rewards,回赐, above-market purchase prices, hosted reception, or controlled trade access.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 422-2 argues that tribute should not be read only as fear, admiration, or cultural submission. The episode treats the system as a political and cultural order with a real economic engine. A court seeking "万国来朝" prestige may overpay for tribute goods, host envoys, and return gifts in order to make recognition visible.

The concept's current value is corrective. Tribute can still signal hierarchy, cultural order, and diplomacy, but the source insists that outsiders may enter the system because the exchange is profitable. That means tribute missions can also generate fraud, competition, fiscal strain, and even violence when access or prices are disputed.

## Key Claims

- Tribute participation can be materially rational for smaller polities, merchants, or frontier powers.
- Prestige-seeking courts may subsidize recognition by paying far more than the practical value of tribute goods.
-回赐, purchase prices, envoy reception, and trade access turn ritual hierarchy into an economic opportunity.
- Once tribute becomes profitable, fake envoys and rival claimants can exploit the system.
- Broken or blocked tribute-trade expectations can transform diplomatic ritual into security conflict.
- The source supports an incentive concept, not a claim that all tribute missions were only profit-seeking.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source is a short explanatory episode and does not provide a full comparative history of tribute institutions. It compresses Han, Ming, Mongol, Japanese, Siamese, and Qing examples into one interpretive arc. The concept should therefore remain a bounded incentive lens: it qualifies prestige narratives without reducing all tribute to cash arbitrage.
