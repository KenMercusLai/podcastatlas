# Appeasement Logic / 绥靖逻辑

## Definition
Appeasement logic is the risk that avoiding an immediate conflict with an expansionist or persecuting power can be described as prudence or peace while transferring costs to others and narrowing one's future options. The label is an interpretation of incentives and outcomes, not a synonym for every negotiated compromise or cautious neutrality.

## Current Synthesis
The sources offer different scales rather than one identical historical mechanism. In the IBM episode, 'trade brings peace' is criticized as a moral and business rationale for continued cooperation with Nazi Germany; the source alleges that tailored data systems materially assisted persecution. In the Qi episodes, Jun Wang Hou's distant, low-posture diplomacy is credited with decades of peace but may have mistaken Qin's temporary reassurance for lasting security as neighboring states fell. Qi Wang Jian's final surrender exposes the strategic endpoint and a humanitarian tension: lost sovereignty and an unhonored promise coexist with a reported low-casualty conquest. Modern EU–US tariff bargaining belongs to a separate security-dependence analogy, not evidence of classical appeasement.

## Key Claims
- **C1 — Peace rhetoric can legitimate harmful cooperation:** A commercial actor can frame continued ties with a violent regime as stabilizing trade while contributing tools or legitimacy to its coercive capacity.
- **C2 — Short-run peace and strategic erosion can coexist:** Neutrality toward a distant expansionist power can spare a state immediate war even as that power removes neighboring buffers and changes later bargaining terms.
- **C3 — Surrender can spare lives without redeeming the earlier strategy:** Qi's terminal bargain reportedly avoided a major final battle, yet ended its independence and did not deliver the promised land.

## Evidence
- **C1 — Peace rhetoric can legitimate harmful cooperation:** The hosts criticize [[ThomasJWatson]]'s trade-and-peace defense of [[IBM]]'s German business and describe Dehomag's locally adapted punched-card machinery, cards, maintenance and expertise in [[NaziGermany|Nazi administration]]. The episode links administrative identification, deportation and labor control to [[TheHolocaust|persecution and the Holocaust]], while contrasting collaborator Lentz with resistant technician Carmille. Its warning is that repeated accommodation can teach an aggressor that threats yield concessions and make further demands attractive; this is a conditional incentive argument, not a universal law or an independent allocation of corporate or individual legal culpability. [[133-ibm-yu-nacui-weishenme-putongren-bu-ying-zanmei-jizeizhuyi-815684110]]
- **C2 — Short-run peace and strategic erosion can coexist:** Regent [[JunWangHou]]'s [[QiState|Qi]] policy toward Qin is described as cautious and non-aligned, preserving more than forty years of relative peace. In the host's [[YuanjiaoJingongStrategy|distant-friend/near-enemy]] reading, Qin had its own reason to reassure Qi while conquering neighboring states. Her breaking of the linked jade rings answers a diplomatic test without humiliation: restraint was not simple submission, yet distance could still hide the loss of buffers. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-119-3-lishishang-di-yi-wei-zhenzheng-yiyishang-chuilian-tingzheng-taihou-liq9d3itmbar00ij43zzed-mulul]]
- **C3 — Surrender can spare lives without redeeming the earlier strategy:** In the episode's account, [[WangBenQin]] advances into Qi from the north after the other states' defeat. Qin promises [[QiWangJian]] five hundred li of land for surrender but does not honor the promise; the host nevertheless credits Qi's long peace and a final campaign without large-scale killing. [[CasualtySparingSurrender]] names that bounded humanitarian result, not a vindication of lost sovereignty. ## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- Jun Wang Hou's diplomatic boundary and Qi's decades of peace matter independently of the host's Munich analogy. [[MachiavellianRealism]] can acknowledge the actual balance of force without endorsing every concession, and [[GoodIntentionsPoliticalLimits]] cautions that good motives do not settle consequences; these are adjacent interpretive frames, not direct evidence of what alternatives Qi had.
- IBM's particular degree of knowledge, control and culpability requires more evidence than this podcast note for a definitive historical finding. The episode contrasts [[CleverOpportunismAsMoralRisk|opportunistic cleverness]] with [[OrdinaryPeopleResistance|ordinary resistance]] and [[PoliticalTruthTelling|truth-telling]]; these are ethical counterresponses, not proof that any one ordinary act would have prevented persecution.
- The modern source reports that the [[EuropeanUnion]]'s market size does not automatically translate into bargaining power because [[NATO]] and U.S. security support shape the tariff negotiation context. Its semi-appeasement/Chamberlain language is rhetorical and source-dated; EU–US bargaining is not equated with fascist conquest. [[139-cong-zuo-ri-de-shi-jie-dao-ru-jin-de-ou-zhou-mei-hao-nian-dai-belle-epoque-ta-hai-neng-hui-lai-ma-ltvsgov8ht9-l7yp9qamdgjmyrf]]
- Neither Qi's strategic loss nor the IBM episode demonstrates a universal law that every concession produces aggression. Corporate service, interstate neutrality, terminal surrender and an ally's tariff concession have different agency and consequences; [[SacrificingOthersEthics|sacrificing third parties]] is an ethical concern where costs are borne by the weak, not an interchangeable causal diagnosis of every case.

## What Changed
- Separates the corporate cooperation case from Qi's interstate neutrality and final surrender rather than forcing one historical mechanism.
- Keeps Qi's long peace and low-casualty surrender as substantive qualifications to a strategic-loss judgment.
- Restricts the EU–US/NATO material to a bargaining-dependence analogy, not direct evidence of appeasement.
