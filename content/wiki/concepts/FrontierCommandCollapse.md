# Frontier Command Collapse / 边郡军政崩盘

Frontier command collapse / 边郡军政崩盘 enters through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-937-zhanshen-zhisi-haoren-zongshi-duozai-duonan-lp7przsmag9ookq7eoisvsmnm5aw|Hanji 937]], where [[GengBiLateHan|耿鄙]]'s campaign against [[HanSui|韩遂]] fails before it can become a normal battle. The source traces the collapse to corrupted staff, weak local trust, a newly assembled six-commandery army, and rejected advice from [[FuXie|傅燮]].

[[ChengQiu|程球]] is the local ignition point. Because Geng Bi trusts a figure already resented for bribery and self-enrichment, the army carries political resentment into the campaign. When it reaches [[DiDaoLateHan|狄道]], mutineers kill Cheng Qiu and Geng Bi, and the rebel side then surrounds [[HanyangCommanderyLateHan|汉阳郡]].

The concept differs from ordinary battlefield defeat. The force collapses from governance failure, personnel choice, timing, and morale before enemy pressure has to decide the matter. It also interacts with [[InterestStructuredAllianceFragility|利益结构式联盟脆弱性]] on the rebel side: Han Sui's coalition may be unstable, but Geng Bi loses the chance to exploit that instability.

## Key Claims
- A frontier campaign can fail internally before battle if corrupt staff destroys trust in the command.
- Freshly assembled troops need discipline, rewards, punishments, and credible leadership before being pushed into a hard campaign.
- Waiting can be active strategy when the enemy coalition has internal power struggles that may reopen.
- The collapse of field command can immediately expose civilian and administrative centers, as Hanyang shows after Di Dao.
