# Service-Oriented Local Government / 服务型地方政府

Service-oriented local government is the source's next-stage governance problem for [[China]]. In [[150-zhongguo-ruhe-tiaochu-pinkun-xianjing-xiandaihua-zhi-lu-you-hui-zouxiang-hefang-lgpvp04rngagkpubte2ac6i5ji3g]], local governments were powerful early development actors through招商引资, fiscal incentives, and [[LocalGovernmentPolicyExperimentation]], but later modernization requires a shift toward services, welfare, care, business environment, and quality-of-life governance.

The difficulty is measurement. The source says later cadre-evaluation systems became more complex and less objectively measurable than early growth targets. When goals multiply and become fuzzy, officials may fall back to the clearest metrics: GDP, fiscal income, and visible projects.

## Key Claims
- Growth-oriented local government can help early industrialization but become insufficient after markets mature.
- Service quality, welfare, trust, and human development are harder to measure than investment or fiscal revenue.
- The source links this transition to the limits of [[DirectedImprovisation]] and [[EngineeringState]] when the task shifts from building things to governing lived systems.
- A service turn needs feedback from citizens, firms, researchers, and front-line social systems.
