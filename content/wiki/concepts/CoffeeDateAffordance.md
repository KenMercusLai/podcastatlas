# Coffee Date Affordance / 咖啡馆约会可退可进性

Coffee date affordance / 咖啡馆约会可退可进性 is the source's explanation for why coffee shops are common first-meeting venues. In [[ep246-rang-nvsheng-anxin-de-yuehui-xiaojiuguan-shenme-yang-lpd5zw9ryd3kplmnaywsoqokvakm]], [[ZhangXingyun|张星云]] says a coffee date can end after a short drink if the interaction is poor, or extend into a walk, meal, or second location if the conversation works.

The source shows that the affordance is spatial, not only beverage-based. A useful first-date coffee shop has enough other people and sound to create safety, but not so much noise that conversation fails. Seating matters too: side-by-side or right-angle seating can feel less confrontational than facing each other across a table, while an overly exposed or cramped shop can turn the date into public performance.

## Key Claims
- Coffee shops work for early dates because they make commitment reversible.
- A first-date venue needs a balance of public visibility, conversational privacy, and easy exit.
- Seating angle and distance can change a date from conversation into interview-like evaluation.
- Coffee shops and bistros serve different relationship stages: coffee often suits first contact, while a familiar bistro can fold someone into an existing social world.
