# Buridan Indecision Effect / 布利丹效应

## Definition

Buridan Indecision Effect / 布利丹效应 is the decision-failure pattern where a person or organization freezes between options because goals, available resources, or choice criteria are unclear, causing delay to become its own cost.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 482 introduces the concept through the traditional "Buridan's donkey" story, then applies it to management. The episode does not treat indecision as a harmless personality quirk. It argues that when a manager cannot identify the goal or the chips in hand, the organization may spend scarce resources while competitors act, employees carry the risk, and the moment for action passes.

The source's most useful contribution is its opportunity-information tradeoff. Waiting for perfect clarity may feel responsible, but in competitive settings clear information is often public information. The leader therefore needs enough judgment to act before every variable is settled, while still distinguishing speed from blind impulse.

## Key Claims

- Indecision is often caused by unclear goals and unclear assessment of available chips.
- Organizational indecision externalizes risk to teams, employees, and families rather than staying inside the leader's private psychology.
- Waiting for complete information can destroy opportunity because rivals receive the same clarity.
- Too many voices and too many plans can paralyze decision systems when authority and criteria are weak.
- Good decision-making requires information triage: separating true from false, necessary from useless, and signal from noise.
- Historical cases can train judgment by exposing recurring patterns of timing, commitment, and hesitation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source strongly favors decisiveness, but it gives less detail on decision process, risk reduction, or conditions where waiting is prudent. The concept should therefore not be flattened into "always choose quickly." Its stronger reading is that leaders need explicit criteria, resource awareness, and information triage so that delay is a chosen strategy rather than passive paralysis.

The AI-assisted decision point is only a brief direction-of-travel claim. Hanji 482 does not specify a model, workflow, accountability boundary, or evidence standard for AI decision support.
