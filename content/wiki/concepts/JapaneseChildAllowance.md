# Japanese Child Allowance / 日本儿童手当

Japanese child allowance is the cash-transfer branch of [[JapaneseFertilitySupportSystem|Japan's fertility support system]] in [[137-qinli-riben-shengyu-butie-guojia-bang-wo-yangwa-guilai-reng-shi-tunjinshou-lowze7haomxbmqrrajpl51dvvxe7]]. The episode traces the policy from a 1972 third-child-and-after benefit of 3,000 yen per month into a broader post-1990 low-fertility response after the "1.57 shock."

The episode emphasizes policy drift and expansion. Over decades, Japan repeatedly adjusted eligibility, age limits, income restrictions, payment size, and funding shares. The October 2024 expansion is treated as a major universalizing step: payments extended closer to high-school age, income limits were removed, third-child-and-after payments increased, and payment frequency shifted toward every two months.

## Key Claims
- Child allowance is visible cash, but it is only one layer of family support.
- The policy's history shows gradual escalation after fertility decline became undeniable.
- Payment frequency matters because low-income households may need cash-flow support rather than a few large annual transfers.
- Removing income limits may improve universality, but it raises harder questions about fiscal cost and whether high-income households are constrained by cash at all.
