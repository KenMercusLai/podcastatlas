# Orbital Compute Governance

Orbital compute governance is the rule, coordination, and enforcement problem that appears when [[SpaceBasedAIInfrastructure]] becomes large enough to affect orbital traffic, data jurisdiction, and state power. [[e239-spacex-yao-rang-taikong-suanli-cong-kehuan-zouxiang-xianshi-dan-ta-huasuan-ma-259291f5-2715-4dde-bcfe-b5beb4df5793]] starts with engineering questions but treats scaled orbital data centers as a governance problem as soon as many satellites, collision-avoidance maneuvers, and valuable data flows are involved.

The episode separates low-earth-orbit and higher-orbit risk. Low-orbit failures may decay and burn up faster, while high-orbit debris can remain dangerous much longer. [[LouisHong]] emphasizes [[Starlink]]'s large collision-avoidance data base, while [[LiuBinyan]] warns that stronger cooperation may arrive only after a serious incident forces the issue.

The same concept covers data sovereignty. If computation and storage happen in orbit, the source asks which country governs the data, who can inspect or seize it, and whether states can extend enforcement power once they have practical space capabilities. The episode's answer is not that orbit is lawless, but that law and sovereignty follow enforcement capacity.

## Key Claims
- Orbital data centers increase the importance of traffic coordination, safe spacing, debris mitigation, and avoidance data.
- Orbital compute turns data-center jurisdiction into a space-law and enforcement question, not only a cloud-contract question.
- Technical capacity and governance capacity co-evolve: rules matter more when states or firms can actually monitor, maneuver, and enforce.
- The same infrastructure that makes space compute valuable can create shared risk, because collisions and debris affect other orbital users.
