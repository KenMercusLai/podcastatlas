# Instrumental Truce Breach / 工具化议和毁约

Instrumental truce breach / 工具化议和毁约 is the pattern [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-166-jiran-xiangyu-bujiang-wude-kan-liubang-ruhe-fuchou-ltwwbn9rzeqzzimmxxfihb9-drj1|Hanji 166]] draws from [[LiuBang|刘邦]]'s conduct after the [[HonggouSettlement|鸿沟议和]]. In the host's interpretation, Liu Bang seeks peace to recover [[LiuTaigong|刘太公]] and [[LuZhi|吕雉]], not because he intends to preserve a stable Chu-Han division.

The breach is politically staged. Once [[XiangYu|项羽]] returns the hostages and withdraws, [[ZhangLiang|张良]] and [[ChenPing|陈平]] argue that Chu is weak, grain is exhausted, and failing to pursue would leave a future danger. Liu Bang accepts that counsel and attacks. The episode reads the adviser speech as giving Liu Bang a public route from agreement to pursuit without making the reversal appear only as personal bad faith.

The concept is therefore not a general celebration of breaking treaties. It records a source-scoped political mechanism: an agreement can solve an immediate hostage or timing problem, then be abandoned when strategic conditions make enforcement less valuable than decisive victory. That places it near [[NonMoralPoliticalAnalysis]] and [[CovertPoliticalTechnology|阴谋式政治技术]], while keeping the ethical tension visible.

## Key Claims
- A truce can be used as an instrument for a narrower goal, such as hostage recovery, rather than as a durable peace design.
- The moment of breach matters: Liu Bang waits until the hostage problem is solved and Xiang Yu has begun withdrawal.
- Adviser counsel can function as political cover, shifting visible responsibility from a ruler's private intent to an apparently strategic consensus.
- The host's reading depends on inferred motive, so the concept should be applied cautiously outside this source.
