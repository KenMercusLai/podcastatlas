# Oral Contraceptive Informed Consent

## Definition
Oral contraceptive informed consent is the source-scoped requirement that the benefits of oral contraceptives be discussed together with hormone, micronutrient, microbiome, inflammatory, sexual-function, and alternative-care tradeoffs.

## Current Synthesis
The Gottfried episode does not reject oral contraceptives outright. It treats reproductive choice as essential and emphasizes ovarian-cancer risk reduction. The sharper claim is that consent is incomplete when women are told only about cycle control, pregnancy prevention, or convenience while side effects on micronutrients, inflammation, microbiome, thyroid function, HPA-axis signaling, SHBG, free testosterone, libido, vaginal dryness, muscle response, confidence, agency, and sexual anatomy are not discussed.

The practical synthesis is risk-benefit and context. Oral contraceptives may create real freedom, and painful periods may still need treatment. But the episode pushes the wiki's health-literacy branch to ask what alternatives exist, what symptoms are being masked, what side effects are known or uncertain, and whether the patient is receiving enough information to choose.

## Key Claims
- Oral contraceptives are framed as reproductive-choice tools that can reduce ovarian cancer risk by reducing ovulation.
- The episode distinguishes synthetic progestins from the body's normal progesterone.
- Potential tradeoffs include micronutrient depletion, microbiome change, inflammation, thyroid effects, HPA-axis effects, increased SHBG, and reduced free testosterone.
- Sexual side effects are treated as an informed-consent issue, including libido, vaginal dryness, muscle response, confidence, agency, risk-taking, and the source's clitoral-size claim.
- Alternatives for painful periods may include addressing inflammation and considering non-hormonal options under clinical guidance.

## Evidence
- Benefit side - [[essentials-how-to-optimize-female-hormone-health-for-vitality-longevity-dr-sara-gottfried-scim5131677929]] says oral contraceptives support reproductive choice and can reduce ovarian cancer risk by reducing ovulation.
- Hormone composition - [[essentials-how-to-optimize-female-hormone-health-for-vitality-longevity-dr-sara-gottfried-scim5131677929]] describes oral contraceptives as ethinyl estradiol plus a synthetic progestin rather than normal progesterone.
- Tradeoff inventory - [[essentials-how-to-optimize-female-hormone-health-for-vitality-longevity-dr-sara-gottfried-scim5131677929]] lists effects on micronutrients, microbiome, inflammation, HPA axis, thyroid function, SHBG, and free testosterone.
- Sexual function boundary - [[essentials-how-to-optimize-female-hormone-health-for-vitality-longevity-dr-sara-gottfried-scim5131677929]] includes libido, vaginal dryness, muscle response, confidence, agency, risk-taking, and limited reversibility data for the clitoral-size claim.
- Painful-period alternatives - [[essentials-how-to-optimize-female-hormone-health-for-vitality-longevity-dr-sara-gottfried-scim5131677929]] mentions inflammatory tone, fish oil, SPMs, and possibly aspirin during the period as alternatives to discuss.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The page preserves the episode's pro-choice framing: contraception can provide real freedom, and avoiding medication is not automatically safer. Several side-effect claims are source-scoped and would need patient-specific risk review, stronger evidence grading, and clinician discussion before action.

## What Changed
- Created an informed-consent concept that holds oral-contraceptive benefits and side effects together rather than treating either side as complete.
