# 辣条产业标准化 / Latiao Industry Standardization

## Definition

辣条产业标准化 is the transition from dispersed, low-price workshop production toward cleaner facilities, specified product identity, controlled additives, testing, packaging, and national regulatory consistency.

## Current Synthesis

[[no-221-zhongguo-latiao-jianghu-1020016798]] presents standardization as both safety reform and industrial restructuring. The same low entry barriers that let [[Pingjiang|平江]] workshops spread a new product also supported intense price competition and underinvestment in sanitation. Provincial standards beginning in 2007 raised equipment and inspection requirements, while repeated scandals, the 2018 additive dispute, the 2019 CCTV investigation, national classification, and the 2022 industry standard accelerated a broader clean-up.

The result is not costless. Firms faced factory, equipment, recipe, licensing, supply-chain, shelf-life, and testing burdens; weaker workshops exited; and prices rose. The episode nevertheless reports much lower later failure rates, making standardization a qualified case where consumer safety, category legitimacy, and market concentration advanced together.

## Key Claims

- Product standardization followed rapid diffusion rather than preceding it.
- Hygiene, testing, mechanical production, packaging, and transport requirements raised the minimum viable scale of production.
- Provincial standards improved local control but also created cross-border inconsistency when classifications differed.
- Public scandals and forced shutdowns acted as enforcement shocks that accelerated capital investment and exit.
- National classification made the product's legal identity and permitted additives more consistent.
- Compliance costs contributed to higher prices and shorter shelf-life constraints while reported pass rates improved.

## Evidence

- **Local standard formation:** [[no-221-zhongguo-latiao-jianghu-1020016798]] says Pingjiang firms funded research supporting a 2007 Hunan standard, while Henan developed its own standard.
- **Capital and exit:** [[no-221-zhongguo-latiao-jianghu-1020016798]] describes clean-room, machinery, laboratory, formula, inspection, and packaging costs that many workshops could not absorb.
- **National convergence:** [[no-221-zhongguo-latiao-jianghu-1020016798]] traces Henan's 2019 withdrawal of its local standard, national treatment as convenience food, and the 2022 seasoned-flour-products industry standard.
- **Outcome and cost:** [[no-221-zhongguo-latiao-jianghu-1020016798]] reports a later failure rate below 1.3% alongside higher unit prices and recurring annual compliance costs.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

- A lower reported failure rate does not prove that every producer or risk is controlled.
- Additive count alone is not evidence of illegality; classification, substance, dose, and production hygiene matter separately.
- Firm counts, costs, prices, and failure rates use different years and scopes and remain source-scoped.
- Standardization can protect consumers while also favoring firms with greater access to capital.
