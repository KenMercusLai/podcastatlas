# Cell-Free Enzyme Engineering

## Definition
Cell-free enzyme engineering is the design and operation of enzyme pathways outside living cells so a targeted biochemical reaction can be controlled without relying on a whole organism as the production factory.

## Current Synthesis
The bounded source presents cell-free engineering as a control tradeoff. Living cells provide evolved machinery but can change, become unhealthy, resist production burdens, or create unwanted byproducts. Removing the cell can simplify the reaction environment and make individual pathway steps easier to tune, but the enzymes must then be redesigned to remain stable and productive outside their native context.

For [[Exozyme]], the intended payoff is access to natural molecules too scarce for economical extraction. The platform claim remains incomplete until a product demonstrates reproducible yield, purity, cost, scale, safety, and regulatory fit.

## Key Claims
- Removing living cells can reduce biological variability, competing activity, and unwanted reaction products.
- Cell-free operation creates a new engineering burden because enzymes evolved to function inside cells.
- Stepwise pathway control can make it easier to target one molecule and exclude unwanted products.
- Scarce natural molecules are a plausible application when direct extraction cannot meet useful quantity or cost.
- Platform credibility depends on manufacturing and product evidence, not only successful laboratory reactions.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode provides no yield, purity, stability, throughput, cost, life-cycle, safety, or scale comparison against cell-based fermentation or extraction. Greater control in principle does not establish commercial superiority. Claims about target molecules and exclusion of unwanted cannabinoid products remain source-scoped.

## What Changed
- Created the concept around the control tradeoff between whole cells and isolated engineered pathways.
- Added product proof, scale, purity, cost, and regulation as the commercialization boundary.
