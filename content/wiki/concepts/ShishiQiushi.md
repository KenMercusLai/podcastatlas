## Definition

实事求是 / Seeking Truth from Facts is presented here as a disciplined willingness to acknowledge what one does not know, establish the particulars of a matter, and seek the most supportable judgment. In this source, the phrase is tied specifically to ancient-text recovery and collation rather than treated only as a modern political slogan.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 267 builds the idea in two layers. The epistemic layer uses “知之为知之，不知为不知” to oppose forcing every problem into an existing conceptual tool. The historical layer reads the [[HanShu|《汉书》]] phrase “修学好古，实事求是” through [[LiuDeHejianKingWesternHan|河间献王刘德]]'s reported practice: search across regions, negotiate access to privately held books, copy without dispossessing owners, convene multiple scholars, compare and edit texts, and deposit results at court. On that account, seeking truth is an institutional workflow as well as a personal attitude.

The origin claim remains bounded. The episode alternates between describing the words as Ban Gu's praise and saying that Liu De proposed them as a collation rule. Without primary-text comparison, the evidence supports association of the phrase with Liu De's scholarly reputation more securely than it supports authorship by Liu De or a fully specified method used under that name.

## Key Claims

- Epistemic humility is a precondition: inquiry begins by distinguishing knowledge from ignorance rather than concealing uncertainty.
- Existing concepts are tools, not universal templates; applying one familiar frame to every problem can narrow perception.
- Fact-seeking becomes more reliable when embodied in repeatable practices such as access, copying, comparison, collective review, and preservation.
- Distributed custody and non-confiscatory copying can protect knowledge better than concentrating the only exemplar in one place.
- The phrase's textual association with Liu De is stronger than the episode's unresolved claim that he personally coined or formally announced it.

## Evidence

- Epistemic posture: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-267-shishiqiushi-shui-diyige-ti-chulaide-lgabh7x_akbgdbyrjupmsfaz4qzm|Hanji 267]] joins the hammer-and-nail metaphor to “知之为知之，不知为不知,” making acknowledged uncertainty an alternative to rigid prior knowledge.
- Scholarly workflow: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-267-shishiqiushi-shui-diyige-ti-chulaide-lgabh7x_akbgdbyrjupmsfaz4qzm|Hanji 267]] reports that Liu De sought private books, paid or pleaded for access, made copies while preserving owner custody, recruited scholars, collated texts, and presented edited copies.
- Phrase and attribution: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-267-shishiqiushi-shui-diyige-ti-chulaide-lgabh7x_akbgdbyrjupmsfaz4qzm|Hanji 267]] cites the Han Shu appraisal “修学好古，实事求是” and glosses it as “实其事，求其是.”

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This page represents one podcast's account, not a complete lexical history of the phrase or an independent reconstruction of Western Han philology. The source does not resolve whether the words describe Liu De retrospectively or reproduce a method he formulated. It also does not provide manuscript-level evidence that each reported acquisition, editorial act, or presented text followed one explicit rule called “实事求是.” The phrase's later intellectual and political meanings are outside this source's bounded evidence.
