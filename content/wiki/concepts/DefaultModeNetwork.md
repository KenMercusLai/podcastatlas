# Default Mode Network

## Definition
The default mode network is a set of interacting brain systems associated in these sources with internally oriented processing, self-modeling, narrative, memory, social inference, and meaning-making rather than simply inactivity.

## Current Synthesis
The sources correct two opposing simplifications. The network is not merely an idle system that shuts off whenever cognition becomes meaningful, and it is not only an engine of ego, rumination, or pathology. [[MaryHelenImmordinoYang]] describes its recruitment when people infer grief, virtue, courage, or other complex meanings from stories and connect those meanings to ethical and self-relevant reflection.

[[BryanJohnson]] uses the same network more speculatively to interpret psychedelic ego dissolution, reduced defensiveness, and possible psychological flexibility. That account may fit the network's role in self-modeling, but his immediate 5-MeO-DMT report and broader rejuvenation hypothesis do not establish that disrupting the network resets a person, improves health, or produces durable benefit. The current synthesis is functional plurality: internally oriented self, social, and narrative processing can support reflection or repetitive distress depending on task, state, and context.

## Key Claims
- Default-mode activity should not be equated with doing nothing or an absence of demanding cognition.
- The network can contribute to narrative, social, ethical, autobiographical, and self-relevant inference.
- Internally oriented processing can support constructive reflection as well as rumination and defensive loops.
- Adolescent engagement with meaningful stories may recruit default-mode systems while producing broader ethical reflection.
- Psychedelic alteration of self-experience may involve default-mode changes, but subjective disruption is not proof of therapeutic reset, rejuvenation, or lasting plasticity.

## Evidence
- Complex meaning: [[how-emotions-social-factors-impact-learning-dr-immordino-yang-scim3156934002]] reports default-mode recruitment when participants inferred stories behind grief, virtue, and socially meaningful events rather than responding only to direct physical scenes.
- Adolescent reflection: [[how-emotions-social-factors-impact-learning-dr-immordino-yang-scim3156934002]] describes teenagers pausing and returning to narratives with ethical and self-relevant conclusions.
- Rest-history qualification: [[how-emotions-social-factors-impact-learning-dr-immordino-yang-scim3156934002]] explains that the network was first identified through rest-related activity and task-related decoupling but should not be reduced to passive rest.
- Psychedelic self-model account: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-bryan-johnson-i-just-took-the-most-powerful-dose-of-dmt-in-the-world-heres-what-it-was-like-40626165]] links psilocybin and 5-MeO-DMT experiences to altered ego, rumination, internal monologue, and psychological rigidity.
- Evidence boundary: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-bryan-johnson-i-just-took-the-most-powerful-dose-of-dmt-in-the-world-heres-what-it-was-like-40626165]] says objective 5-MeO-DMT results were not yet available, leaving its strongest claims subjective and single-person.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The supplied notes do not provide network definitions, imaging methods, connectivity analyses, sample sizes, or effect magnitudes sufficient to settle which subsystem performs which function. Network activation is not a direct readout of moral insight, learning, ego, or health. Psychedelic experiences can change self-perception and values, but benefits, harms, durability, and identity consequences depend on substance, dose, person, supervision, and context.

## What Changed
- Replaced the earlier psychedelic-only account with a synthesis that includes narrative and social meaning-making.
- Qualified the portrayal of the network as solely an ego, rumination, or defensive-pattern engine.
- Preserved psychedelic disruption as a source-scoped hypothesis rather than a demonstrated reset mechanism.
