# Act of Union 1801

## Definition

The Act of Union 1801 abolished the separate Irish Parliament, incorporated Ireland into a United Kingdom governed from Westminster, and retained Dublin Castle as the main executive machinery in Ireland.

## Current Synthesis

The source presents Union as a security response to the [[UnitedIrishmen]] and the 1798 rebellion. British leaders sought to remove a potentially unstable Dublin legislature and absorb Ireland into what they described as a modern unitary state after revolutionary organization and attempted French intervention exposed the existing settlement's weakness.

Passage itself revealed limited legitimacy: the first Irish parliamentary vote failed even amid bribery, patronage, and corruption. More importantly, Catholic emancipation was associated with the Union settlement but did not immediately follow. The broken expectation left the new constitutional order with a major legitimacy deficit that the following episode traces through [[DanielOConnell]] and nineteenth-century mass politics.

## Key Claims

- Union was shaped by the security shock of the 1798 rebellion and attempted French intervention.
- It removed legislative autonomy from Dublin and centralized sovereignty at Westminster.
- Dublin Castle remained the practical machinery of Irish administration.
- Patronage and corruption were central to securing passage after an initial defeat.
- Failure to deliver Catholic emancipation damaged the Union's legitimacy from its beginning.

## Evidence

- Security rationale: [[336-ireland-celts-conquest-and-cromwell-part-1-glt6280042699]] presents the Union as Britain's institutional response to 1798.
- Constitutional design: [[336-ireland-celts-conquest-and-cromwell-part-1-glt6280042699]] describes removal of the Dublin Parliament and government from Westminster through Dublin Castle.
- Passage and broken promise: [[336-ireland-celts-conquest-and-cromwell-part-1-glt6280042699]] notes the failed first vote, patronage, and non-delivery of Catholic emancipation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source summarizes a complex legislative process and uses “promise” for the relationship between Union and Catholic emancipation. It does not fully distinguish personal assurances, ministerial expectations, statutory terms, or George III's opposition. Claims about corruption, motives, and whether an alternative settlement could have survived remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Established the Union as both a centralizing security settlement and a legitimacy failure rooted in coerced passage and delayed emancipation.
