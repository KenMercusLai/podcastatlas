# Travel Price Parity Enforcement

Travel price parity enforcement is the platform practice of requiring or pressuring merchants to keep a booking channel's price no higher than competing channels, then monitoring and correcting deviations. [[ep91-dingfang-dingpiao-dingjiangshan-xiecheng-51-yi-wei-aoman-maidan-lovhfkz4rklv1ik-uqyeswrdf3uw]] describes [[Ctrip]]'s alleged "金牌" arrangement as a full-network-lowest-price rule backed by cross-platform price monitoring.

The episode makes the mechanism concrete by saying Ctrip compared final effective prices after coupons and could use tools such as "调价助手" or "挂牌通" to lower the Ctrip-side price when another channel was cheaper. That turns price parity from a simple sales promise into [[HotelPlatformPricingPower]] and a [[PlatformDataRegulation]] problem.

## Key Claims
- Price-parity enforcement can protect a platform's user promise while weakening merchant pricing autonomy.
- Cross-platform monitoring creates a travel-specific version of [[SurveillancePricing]] because the relevant price is the final effective checkout price, not only the posted room rate.
- Automatic repricing can make a merchant's formal price decision less meaningful if the platform can adjust display or channel price after detection.
- The line between legitimate price consistency and abusive parity depends on market share, ranking leverage, penalties, and auditability.
