# Moral Face Under Power Competition / 权力竞争中的要脸

Moral face under power competition is the episode's worry that repeated success by shameless actors can push a society toward thicker cynicism. In [[164-guwen-de-liliang-qing-shou-xia-zhe-fen-qiannian-wei-bian-de-shenqing-dongjian-he-yongqi-940236576]], QinZong says [[XiangYu|项羽]] might have won if he had been more ruthless at [[HongmenYan|《鸿门宴》]], but she resists turning that into a simple recommendation to become shameless.

The concept is the moral counterweight to [[MachiavellianRealism]]. It accepts that power competition punishes hesitation, but asks what is lost when "要脸容易输" becomes a social lesson. [[SimaQian|司马迁]]'s treatment of Xiang Yu is read as preserving historical respect for force and residual moral bearing rather than reducing history to winner worship.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-140-xiangyu-yu-liubang-shi-zhen-youqing-haishi-xianghu-liyong-1-lmmhoq2wo3bs6b1johpc0ixuf662|Hanji 140 part 1]] adds the pre-banquet version of the same tension. The host says [[XiangYu|项羽]] is an aristocrat rather than a bandit, so even if [[FanZeng|范增]] is right that [[LiuBang|刘邦]] is dangerous, the attack still needs a nameable, upright-looking basis. Here "face" is not mercy; it is the political constraint that a noble commander must be seen as acting with public reason.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-140-xiangyu-yu-liubang-shi-zhen-youqing-haishi-xianghu-liyong-2-lvdgirxmwxcckh2loh-yk2zzer2e|Hanji 140 part 2]] shows that constraint working against the attack order. [[XiangBo|项伯]] gives Xiang Yu a face-preserving reason to pause: Liu Bang entered Guanzhong first and had made later arrival possible, so killing him immediately would look "不义." The point is not that Xiang Yu becomes gentle, but that he can still be moved by an argument that protects his moral self-image as a Chu aristocratic commander.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-141-huanyuan-lishi-zhong-de-hongmenyan-lpmb-bcoocngtqjvtnpj0elm-siy|Hanji 141]] adds the banquet-form version. The host argues that Xiang Yu's silence before [[FanZeng|范增]]'s jade-ring signals is not only dullness; his warrior pride makes covert killing during a feast feel incompatible with the public image he wants. This face constraint protects [[LiuBang|刘邦]] in the moment, but it also leaves the underlying rivalry unresolved.

## Key Claims
- Political realism can diagnose why restraint loses without making shamelessness admirable.
- Residual moral face matters because it keeps victory from becoming the only source of legitimacy.
- Historiography can preserve this tension by honoring historical force without erasing brutality.
- Hanji 140 part 1 adds that face can require legitimate procedure before violence, not only restraint during a banquet.
- Hanji 140 part 2 adds that face can also block a strategically tempting attack when the opposing side supplies a plausible义 narrative through a trusted elder.
- Hanji 141 adds that face can attach to method: open attack and covert banquet assassination do not carry the same reputational meaning for an aristocratic military leader.
