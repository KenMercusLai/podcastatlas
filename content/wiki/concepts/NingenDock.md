# Ningen Dock / 人间 Dock

Ningen dock is the Japanese comprehensive-checkup tradition described in [[177-riben-yibao-jijin-weihe-yao-zhichi-mianfei-de-guomin-tijian-lh06qonrzgjprqdwl8dygn4hoxpk]]. The episode says the phrase borrows the image of a ship entering dock for maintenance and applies it to the body: a person enters a hospital or checkup center for systematic inspection before obvious illness.

The source treats ningen dock as a historical root rather than the same thing as free public checkups. Early versions in the 1950s were intensive, expensive, and hospital-centered; later versions became shorter and more automated as employers, health-insurance societies, and checkup centers normalized health screening. By contrast, [[JapaneseSpecificHealthCheckups]] are narrower, standardized, and focused on metabolic syndrome and lifestyle-disease risk for 40-to-74-year-old insured people.

## Key Claims
- Ningen dock began as a high-end systematic examination before becoming part of Japan's broader checkup culture.
- Automation, shorter stays, and health-insurance involvement helped move checkups from elite service toward mass practice.
- The concept should not be confused with public specific health checkups, which are more targeted and policy-governed.
- The source uses [[HinoharaShigeaki|日野原重明]] to connect ningen dock with Japan's wider preventive-medicine narrative.
