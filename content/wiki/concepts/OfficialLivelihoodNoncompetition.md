## Definition

官吏不与民争生计 / official noncompetition with popular livelihoods is the ethical boundary that people already maintained by public stipends should not combine office, status, and private production or commerce in ways that displace ordinary households from their means of support.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-248-dongzhongshu-ruhe-gaibian-zhonghua-minzu-de-mingyun-2-lrjy7ozds7peyu6x3iwufety2zvt|Hanji 248(2)]] links compensation to restraint: officials receive substantial support because their role is public service and moral instruction, so they should not also exploit their power and resources to compete for every smaller source of income. The 公仪休 story makes the boundary domestic as well as formal; household weaving and garden production are rejected because a chancellor's household can crowd out textile workers and vegetable growers.

The claim is not a general objection to all wealth or exchange. It is a conflict-of-role argument about actors whose public office gives them income, authority, information, and influence unavailable to ordinary producers. The episode then shifts to a modern claim that wealthy consumption promotes economic development, but that extrapolation is analytically separate: refraining from production under official advantage does not by itself prove that more luxury consumption produces broad welfare.

## Key Claims

- Public stipends create an ethical expectation that officeholders prioritize public duties over private accumulation.
- Political authority and elite resources can make nominally private competition structurally unequal.
- Official self-restraint protects livelihoods as well as the perceived legitimacy of public office.
- The 公仪休 household story extends the boundary from an officeholder's direct conduct to production organized inside the official household.
- A prohibition on livelihood competition does not establish the episode's separate modern claim that increased wealthy consumption necessarily benefits ordinary people.

## Evidence

Stipend and role separation:

Household-level restraint:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-248-dongzhongshu-ruhe-gaibian-zhonghua-minzu-de-mingyun-2-lrjy7ozds7peyu6x3iwufety2zvt|Hanji 248(2)]] uses 公仪休's rejection of household weaving and garden vegetables to dramatize how elite self-provision can be framed as taking work from producers.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept currently rests on one structured episode summary and does not independently collate the 公仪休 story, the quoted classics, or the economic institutions of the period. Its ethical intuition does not specify a workable boundary between household use, ordinary property, corruption, market participation, and coercive advantage. The host's consumption-led-growth analogy is preserved as commentary, not treated as historical evidence or as a sufficient modern economic model.

## What Changed

- Established the concept as a stipend-and-role boundary against converting public advantage into private livelihood competition.
- Separated the ancient official-ethics claim from the host's modern consumption argument.
