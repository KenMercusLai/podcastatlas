# Meditation as Refocusing Practice

## Definition
Meditation as refocusing practice treats noticing attention drift and deliberately returning to a chosen object as the central training repetition rather than evidence that the session failed.

## Current Synthesis
[[how-meditation-works-science-based-effective-meditations-scim5642770846]] frames meditation as repeated focus, drift, detection, and return. The object may be breath, a point near the forehead, bodily sensation, or something external; the common operation is recovering the intended placement of attention. Experience may therefore appear as faster or more consistent return rather than perfectly uninterrupted concentration.

The source favors consistency over one rigid session length and cites short daily practices as potentially useful. Those examples support repeatability, not a universal dose, guaranteed outcome, or claim that all meditation styles reduce stress, improve sleep, or serve the same aim.

## Key Claims
- Mind wandering creates the opportunity for a refocusing repetition rather than automatically invalidating practice.
- Progress can mean earlier detection and more reliable return, not thought suppression or unbroken focus.
- The chosen object and inward or outward mode can vary while the return operation remains common.
- Short practice can be worthwhile when it is repeatable, but duration should match context and tolerance.
- Refocusing meditation is distinct from practices aimed mainly at defocusing, sleep transition, targeted hypnosis, or non-dual insight.

## Evidence
- Training operation - [[how-meditation-works-science-based-effective-meditations-scim5642770846]] explicitly defines meditation as focusing and refocusing after the mind wanders.
- Experience interpretation - [[how-meditation-works-science-based-effective-meditations-scim5642770846]] describes experienced practice as faster and more consistent return rather than permanent absorption.
- Dose examples - [[how-meditation-works-science-based-effective-meditations-scim5642770846]] discusses three-, five-, and thirteen-minute daily practices and prioritizes consistency over fixed duration.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source summary does not provide enough study detail to establish an optimal duration, effect size, generalization across meditation traditions, or treatment efficacy for attention, sleep, depression, trauma, or anxiety disorders. Mind wandering can support planning and creativity, and some meditation practices intentionally use open monitoring or investigate the structure of awareness rather than repeatedly narrowing onto one object.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to make the return after attentional drift the unit of practice.
- Separated repeatable short sessions from claims about a universal minimum or optimal dose.
