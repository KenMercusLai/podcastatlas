# Platform Community Governance

## Definition
Platform community governance is the operating problem of managing a participatory platform whose users, moderators, norms, abuse patterns, commercialization choices, and data-access policies determine whether the product remains healthy and legitimate.

## Current Synthesis
The concept now spans both crisis response and institution formation. [[Wikipedia]] shows a participatory system developing reversion, blocking, neutrality, sourcing, and dispute norms because open contribution and scarce staff made community oversight unavoidable. [[Reddit]]'s 2015 crisis and Chinese PC and mobile cases show the reverse pressure: moderators, users, commercialization choices, and unexplained intervention can destabilize mature communities. AI-era evidence adds two linked boundaries: platforms govern who may extract value from user labor, and identity controls intended to stop bots can damage pseudonymous access, vulnerable-user safety, and the willingness to contribute.

## Key Claims
- Moderators are not external helpers; they can become core infrastructure whose trust affects platform availability.
- Concentrated abuse or unexplained decisions can shape the whole platform and escalate into leadership crises.
- Commercialization decisions can become governance failures when users experience them as seizure of a relationship space.
- Keyword, Q&A, taste, sports, mobile, anonymity, bullet-comment, creator-income, and lifestyle-search communities create different abuse and legitimacy surfaces.
- AI-era data access makes governance a value-extraction question: platforms must decide how API pricing, data licensing, and chatbot use affect moderators and users who create the underlying corpus.
- Access and identity controls should be judged by proportionality: anti-bot or traffic goals do not erase public-access, privacy, and participation costs.
- Open contribution requires technical and social correction capacity; low entry barriers alone do not produce trustworthy community knowledge.

## Evidence
- Reddit crisis - [[socialradarspod-stevehuffman-final]] says Reddit's 2015 crisis involved press problems, team disarray, abusive users, a fired moderator liaison, moderators taking communities private, and [[SteveHuffman]] returning as a founder-credibility repair move.
- Chinese PC communities - [[no-212-cengjing-de-jingshen-jiaoluo-douban-zhihu-tieba-hupu-zhongguo-hulianwang-gushi-25-1003510980]] shows [[BaiduTieba|百度贴吧]], [[Douban|豆瓣]], [[Zhihu|知乎]], and [[Hupu|虎扑]] facing governance around keyword openness, old-post trust, fan behavior, creator incentives, private groups, and commercial intervention.
- Chinese mobile communities - [[no-214-xunzhao-tonglei-xiaohongshu-bilibili-yiji-wuhuabamen-de-naxie-shequ-zhongguo-hulianwang-gushi-26-1005977305]] adds authenticity, soft-ad, illegal-content, bullet-comment, membership, ad-format, and anonymity governance surfaces.
- API and AI data access - [[ire-and-ice-the-toll-of-americas-deportations-6a993426809ae7c2f4293df9]] says Reddit's 2023 volunteer moderator strike followed API charging plans, and later data deals with Google and OpenAI turned community data into a licensed AI asset.
- Login and identity controls - [[tech-20261111-1111-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261111-1111-mp-tech-pod-128]] says Reddit's login wall may reduce learning and expert contribution, while stronger verification may deter bots but exclude users who need pseudonymity.
- Volunteer institution formation - [[vmp5702291377-vmp5702291377]] says Wikipedia volunteers repaired vandalism across time zones and developed blocking, content, neutrality, sourcing, and living-person rules as the project scaled.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept should not assume every commercialization, login, or verification decision is a governance failure, or that volunteer rulemaking is automatically representative and fair. Legitimacy depends on implementation, communication, purpose, data handling, appeal, category, workload distribution, and whether the community still sees the platform as stewarding rather than seizing shared value. The sources do not compare verification technologies, measure the net effect of Reddit's proposed access policy, or independently evaluate Wikipedia's governance outcomes across languages and contributor groups.

## What Changed
- Added login access as a contributor-incentive and public-benefit question.
- Added bot resistance versus pseudonymous participation as a distinct governance tradeoff.
- Added Wikipedia as a positive institution-formation case in which volunteer oversight and policy grew alongside open contribution.
