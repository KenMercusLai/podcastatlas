# Learnable Emotional Intelligence

Learnable emotional intelligence is [[ep-3-demystifying-the-imposter-syndrome]]'s claim that confidence habits and interpersonal self-awareness can change. [[StephenMathis|Stephen Mathis]] says people should not be treated as fixed in their current mindset; personality may exist, but people can modify how it expresses itself and how they interact with others.

In the source, understanding [[ImpostorSyndrome|imposter syndrome]] and the [[DunningKrugerEffect|Dunning-Kruger effect]] is part of emotional intelligence. A person can learn to notice their confidence profile, accept useful feedback, question distorted self-judgment, and communicate with others more effectively over time.

The concept matters for management because [[ConfidenceProfileTeamManagement]] depends on change being possible. If underconfidence and overconfidence were fixed traits, managers could only route around them; if they are learnable patterns, feedback and practice can improve the team.

## Key Claims
- Emotional intelligence includes noticing how confidence, doubt, and overconfidence shape behavior.
- Personality does not fully determine how someone interacts with feedback or uncertainty.
- Feedback, effort, and time can change confidence habits.
- Management should treat confidence profiles as adjustable rather than permanent labels.
