# Eurozone Internal Imbalance

Eurozone internal imbalance is the source's use of Europe as a trade and currency comparison case. In [[139-cong-zuo-ri-de-shi-jie-dao-ru-jin-de-ou-zhou-mei-hao-nian-dai-belle-epoque-ta-hai-neng-hui-lai-ma-ltvsgov8ht9-l7yp9qamdgjmyrf]], the hosts note that European countries can share a currency and low internal tariffs while still developing north-south grievances around manufacturing strength, services, fiscal transfers, tax burden, and rescue costs.

The concept matters because it removes some usual explanations for trade imbalance. If exchange-rate manipulation and high tariffs are less available as explanations, then industrial structure, comparative advantage, fiscal design, and lived distribution become more visible. The same arrangement can look efficient in macro terms and unfair in local political terms.

## Key Claims
- Shared currency does not erase regional specialization, resentment, or balance-of-payments pressure.
- Manufacturing centers and service/tourism/residency economies can experience the same integration differently.
- A policy can be economically legible and still politically felt as exploitation by multiple sides.
