## Definition

分香卖履 / Fenxiang Mailu is the deathbed-household frame attached to [[CaoCao|曹操]]'s distribution of useful goods and instructions that surviving women support themselves through textile and shoe-making work rather than convert household resources into burial display.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 1014 establishes the practical-settlement reading: incense is divided rather than buried, handiwork becomes a fallback livelihood, and remarriage is presented as permissible rather than subordinated to death-bound loyalty. Hanji 1105 reinforces the work-and-survival element and places it beside Cao Cao's guilt over [[CaoAng|曹昂]] and [[LadyDingCaoCao|丁夫人]], making the final instructions part of a wider household-memory portrait.

The durable synthesis is pragmatic care under anticipated household decline. The women are not promised that rank or family wealth will remain secure; they are told to preserve useful goods and retain productive ability. The episode's modern language of economic and spiritual independence is a suggestive interpretation, not proof that the instruction established full autonomy by modern standards.

## Key Claims

- Deathbed distribution can preserve household utility instead of serving burial prestige.
- Productive skill is framed as a fallback against elite-household decline.
- Hanji 1014 presents remarriage as permissible, qualifying coercive widow-loyalty expectations.
- Hanji 1105 connects livelihood planning with Cao Cao's broader final concern for surviving family members.
- Practical provision and patriarchal household authority can coexist; the arrangement should not be equated automatically with modern emancipation.

## Evidence

- Useful-goods and remarriage reading: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1014-caocao-de-15-ge-laopo-dou-you-shui-lv-hyvprg73k8w8jspxjosqwmc69|Hanji 1014]] says Cao Cao divides incense, rejects burial use, directs women toward handiwork, and permits remarriage.
- Livelihood-risk emphasis: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1105-guanyu-zhisi-maizang-le-caocao-zenyang-de-mengxiang-lgmk_dlknjpzz7fvzasno6tu7fs3|Hanji 1105]] highlights weaving, clothing, and shoe-making as protection if the Cao household declines.
- Household-memory context: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1105-guanyu-zhisi-maizang-le-caocao-zenyang-de-mengxiang-lgmk_dlknjpzz7fvzasno6tu7fs3|Hanji 1105]] places the arrangement after Cao Cao's reported regret over Cao Ang and Lady Ding.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The sources are podcast interpretations of a famous final-instruction tradition, not a complete social history of late-Han women or the Cao household. Economic survival capacity is narrower than legal, social, or spiritual independence. The exact goods, wording, scope of the remarriage permission, and later lives of the women require direct textual and historical verification.
