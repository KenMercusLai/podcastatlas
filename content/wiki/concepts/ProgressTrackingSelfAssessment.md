# Progress Tracking Self-Assessment

Progress tracking self-assessment is the episode's answer to vague self-judgment: record plans, actions, completions, and year-over-year change so the person has evidence beyond mood. In [[ep-3-demystifying-the-imposter-syndrome]], [[StephenMathis|Stephen Mathis]] describes applying this to writing by tracking whether he sat down to write, finished pieces, and submitted work.

[[SamDataScienceWithSam|Sam]] adds a roadmap version: create yearly goals and ask whether life, learning, or work is improving over time. This turns comparison away from strangers and toward a person's own trajectory.

The concept supports [[ConcreteSelfPraise]] because completed actions have to be noticed before they can become self-trust. It also supports [[CapabilityGapSelfDiagnosis]] because records can show whether a problem is effort, skill, time, emotional capacity, or an unrealistic standard.

## Key Claims
- Self-assessment is less biased when goals and completed actions are written down.
- Longitudinal review can make progress visible even when unfinished work still feels large.
- Tracking should include process actions, not only final outcomes.
- Small celebrations make records emotionally usable instead of only administrative.
