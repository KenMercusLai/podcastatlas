# Maker Community

Maker community is [[Albert]]'s product/community direction in [[dang-ruanjian-rongyi-bei-chuangzuo-xin-shidai-de-chanpin-chang-shenme-yang-duitan-albert-ltgalbpl0p41d33vvsbaczqrcbdi]]. The target is not everyone who uses AI coding at work, but people who like creating software itself and want other creators, users, or respected peers to notice and respond.

The source's value formula is response, echo, and return: creators need to be answered, recognized by people they care about, and sometimes paid. That makes maker community an AI-era distribution and motivation layer, not only a gallery of tools.

[[ep242-duli-youxi-shi-yitiao-gaoqian-hao-saidao-ma-lrqbzrceumbuggzuero5qb4jv3zu]] adds a pre-AI game-maker version. [[WuJunIndieGameCreator|巫君]] learned from [[RPGMaker]], [[SixtySixRPG|66RPG]], and [[ProjectOneForum|Project One论坛]], showing that maker communities can create confidence, examples, feedback, and later commercial pathways even before production tools become dramatically easier.

## Key Claims
- Lower creation cost increases the need for connection because more people can make things than can easily find relevant users or peers.
- The community's early value is not only monetization; recognition and useful feedback can be more important for many makers.
- A maker platform needs discovery, creator profiles, collaboration paths, and infrastructure such as deployment or backend services.
- The community is meant for people with taste and creation drive, including designers and nontraditional programmers empowered by [[CodingDemocratization]].
- The source keeps a narrow target: it does not claim all AI-coding workers will want to publicly share small products.
- Maker community can become a distribution surface for [[SoftwareAsCulturalWork]], where the user must understand the creator's aesthetic or use context.
- Forum-era maker communities can become a training and peer-review base for later [[IndieGameCommercialization|indie commercialization]].
