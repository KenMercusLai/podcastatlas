# Early Opportunity Intervention

## Definition

Early opportunity intervention is sustained support that identifies and develops ability before late-stage admissions, while addressing the material, relational, and navigational barriers that can remove a child from the educational pipeline.

## Current Synthesis

The episode contrasts the college gate with the much longer path leading to it. Free tuition, recruitment mail, and campus visits can help students who reach age seventeen with achievement and confidence intact. They cannot recover children whose schooling, safety, family responsibilities, or self-belief has already broken down.

[[EricEisner]] and the [[YESProgram|YES program]] supply the practical model: identify students around elementary-school age, tutor them, translate unfamiliar institutions, use relationships to secure placements, notice material barriers, and remain available when life interrupts. This is more intensive than recruitment and more fragile than a guaranteed public system because it depends on individualized advocacy.

## Key Claims

- College access is the endpoint of a developmental pipeline, not a single admissions transaction.
- Intervention timing matters because confidence, preparation, safety, and school attachment can deteriorate before late adolescence.
- Effective support may need to combine instruction, material aid, placement, navigation, and continuing relationships.
- A scholarship removes price but not family duty, housing instability, trauma, transport, or institutional unfamiliarity.
- Advocate-led programs can demonstrate a mechanism without proving that philanthropy can provide it equitably at scale.

## Evidence

### Early identification

- [[carlos-doesn-t-remember-carlos-doesn-t-remember]] describes YES identifying Carlos in fifth grade and Eisner arguing for support from around fourth grade in Lennox.

### Multi-domain support

- [[carlos-doesn-t-remember-carlos-doesn-t-remember]] combines tutoring and school placement with shoes, travel, family context, foster disruption, and later re-placement.

### Limit of late outreach

- [[carlos-doesn-t-remember-carlos-doesn-t-remember]] reports that many high-achieving low-income students did not apply to selective colleges despite generous aid and recruitment efforts.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source does not compare intervention ages or program models, estimate costs, or measure long-term effects. Early selection can overlook late bloomers, misclassify children, intensify tracking, or direct scarce support toward those already easiest to recognize. The durable claim is that upstream barriers matter, not that one private placement model should be universal.

## What Changed

- Established a pipeline model separating early preservation from late admissions outreach.
- Added material, family, and relational support to the meaning of educational access.
- Preserved scalability and selection risks around advocate-led intervention.
