# Office-Duty Martyrdom / 守官赴死

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-931-rensheng-jiannan-chu-bianshi-xiuxin-shi-lpzmvxop8lmlwvxhoojzj5ok9qd6|Hanji 931]] adds a prison-and-procedure version through [[WangYunLateHan|王允]]. After [[ZhangRangLateHan|张让]]'s retaliation puts him back in jail, Wang Yun refuses poison from grieving subordinates because a minister who has offended the ruler should await formal court execution and public announcement.

This is not martyrdom at a besieged post like Fu Xie's later case. Wang Yun's stance preserves official meaning inside captivity: he refuses to let suffering, humiliation, or private pity convert a public office case into quiet self-disposal.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-937-zhanshen-zhisi-haoren-zongshi-duozai-duonan-lp7przsmag9ookq7eoisvsmnm5aw|Hanji 937]] develops the besieged-post version, where [[FuXie|傅燮]] refuses to leave [[HanyangCommanderyLateHan|汉阳郡]] after [[GengBiLateHan|耿鄙]]'s campaign collapses. The source frames his choice as an official's duty to die at post rather than as confidence that the city can still be saved.

The concept is not simple self-destruction. Fu Xie distinguishes his own obligation from [[FuGan|傅干]]'s survival by entrusting his son to [[YangHuiLateHan|杨晖]]. He also rejects [[HuangYanLateHan|黄衍]]'s surrender proposal because Huang Yan had held Han office and now speaks for rebels, making official identity the moral boundary.

The source keeps the cost visible. Fu Xie's death does not restore Liangzhou; it marks the point at which the Han court has effectively lost the province. The value of the act lies in a moral and reputational boundary under collapse, not in immediate strategic success.

## Key Claims
- Hanji 931 adds a prison-procedure version: an official may refuse private suicide because formal judgment and public notice still matter even when the case is unjust.
- The source treats office as a duty-bearing role even when the ruler is bad and the local military situation is unwinnable.
- Martyrdom is paired with family-preservation planning: Fu Xie's son must live even though Fu Xie chooses to die.
- A surrender offer can become a moral test when it asks an appointed official to legitimize rebellion.
- The act can preserve reputation and moral meaning while still failing to preserve territory or institutional control.
