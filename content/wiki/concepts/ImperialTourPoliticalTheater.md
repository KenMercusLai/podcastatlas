## Definition
帝国巡游政治剧场 is the use of a ruler's journey as political action: public presence, ritual claims, inscriptions or elite summons can operate alongside inspection, exposure to danger and personal motives.

## Current Synthesis
The 219 BCE [[QinState]] eastern tour made newly conquered territory a stage for recognition and intimidation; Langya and the return route brought incorporation and conflict with local sacred places. Subsequent tours persisted despite attack and carried announcements, while death omens changed the likely purpose of the final journey. [[LiuBang]]'s feigned Yunmeng trip is a related but distinct protocol trap, not a standard explanation of Qin touring. All seven notes are from one history-podcast series.

## Key Claims
- Ruler presence across former rival lands can address local audiences differently: spectacle for subjects and pressure on old elites.
- Stays, inscriptions, relocations and local worship turn a route into territorial claim-making, without proving genuine local consent.
- Sacred encounters can resist easy appropriation in the narrative, prompting frustrated or punitive actions.
- Repeated movement exposes a ruler to attack and may also carry policy orders across territories.
- A death omen may recast a final journey as attempted fate-changing, but psychological intent is inferred.
- Tour protocol can be used tactically to summon and isolate a vassal, separate from public legitimacy theater.

## Evidence
- Eastern claim: [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-122-1-jiemi-qinshihuang-fengchan-weishenme-xuan-taishan-lkcyayuqrtsgy-k4bhdvy-l-lapo]] describes [[QinShiHuang]]'s 219 BCE trip through formerly six-state lands, prepared roads and inscriptions; [[Taishan]] [[FengshanRitualLegitimacy]] claims heavenly recognition, but more than 70 invited Qi-Lu scholars disagreed on its form. Prepared routes supported the ceremony; [[QinChidao]] is a related road-system cross-reference, not a claim of this episode’s documented tour route, while [[ImperialHomogenization]] remains a separate administrative goal.
- Coastal incorporation: [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-122-2-ribenren-daodi-shi-bushi-xufu-de-houdai-lhgbbhg3zw8rlx1jjb7lpb5ihihh]] reports a three-month [[LangyaMountain]] stay, migration in former Qi territory and [[QiEightSpirits]] worship and the [[LangyaInscriptionPoliticalProgram]] proclaiming Qin law and standards. [[XuFu]]'s sea-immortal proposals arose in this court setting but belong to [[ImmortalityQuestPolitics]], not proven results of the ceremony.
- Limits of sacred control: [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-122-3-qinshihuang-jing-he-shenxian-si-qilai-le-ljnw5estg6dvgfllm3kfcq6ulhxu]] recounts the failed [[Jiuding]] search near [[Xuzhou]]/Pengcheng and storm at [[XiangshanShrine]], read as [[XiangJun]]'s resistance. The reported felling of trees by convict labor is a punitive response to that interpretation, not evidence a deity caused weather. The proposed [[Huangdi]] route imitation is speculative; [[ZhangLiang]]'s destroyed-Han family revenge is the episode's later anti-Qin setup.
- Travel and exposure: [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-123-1-shen-zhi-yuyan-wangqin-zhe-hu-jing-shi-zhi-lg7m3vfdbcweb4joxe4jvhxjx6y8]] has Zhang Liang's [[Bolangsha]] attack strike a second carriage rather than the emperor. Qin continued through Zhifu/Langya and [[Jieshi]], using inscriptions and orders against large walls and levees, the host's [[StrongTrunkWeakBranchCentralization]] reading; [[LuSheng]]'s “亡秦者胡” warning also ties a later northern campaign to the tour sequence without making the warning its only cause.
- Last journey: [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-126-qinshihuang-siyin-zhimi-1-lgtlhau6ixqglk1ict1fs0jexjwe]] introduces 211 BCE meteor writing, the returned jade bi and “今年祖龙死”; plans for “仙真人诗” on tours concern a sacred-imperial message, but this part mainly sets up the journey. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-126-qinshihuang-siyin-zhimi-2-ln15p5qb7lc20goni1ibcpmn3wca]] supplies divination “游徙吉,” 30,000 households sent toward 北河/榆中 and [[OmenCountermeasureMobility]] on the final tour. [[ZhaoZhengShu]] is used in the host's fate-changing interpretation; 《资治通鉴》河北 versus 《史记》北河 remains a source-critical discrepancy.
- Tactical inversion: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-172-liubang-yangyou-yunmengze-zhiqin-hanxin-lrpkye8y6ibqdqs9pz7artk-6chv]] says [[ChenPing]] advised a feigned [[Yunmengze]] tour after an accusation against [[HanXin]] in 201 BCE. [[ChenPing]] advised against open battle given Han Xin’s command ability and arranged a vassal summons at [[ChenCountyChuHan]]. [[ZhongliMo]] warned that surrendering him would strip Han Xin of a deterrent, then died; Han Xin presented his head but was arrested anyway, later spared and reduced from Chu king to marquis at [[Huaiyin]]. This is [[RitualTourSummonsEntrapment]], not a Qin-style legitimacy ritual.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Presence is not administration: former-Qi worship and Xiangshan punishment show differing treatment of local traditions, while audience responses are inferred. The storm and omen are transmitted stories, not supernatural proof. The final tour's private motive cannot be directly observed and Qinji 126 part 1 ends before the proposed countermeasure. Han Xin's arrest is one elite security maneuver, not evidence all tours concealed arrests. [[SouthernTourPoliticalTheater]] is a later comparison, not the same institutional setting.

## What Changed
- Organized tours by public display, local incorporation/conflict, bodily exposure, omen response and elite summons.
- Preserved Qin-versus-Han mechanism distinctions and uncertain motives.
