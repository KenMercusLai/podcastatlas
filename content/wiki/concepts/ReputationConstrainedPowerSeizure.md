# Reputation-Constrained Power Seizure / 声望约束下的夺权节奏

## Definition

Reputation-constrained power seizure is the timing problem in which an actor wants control but delays an immediately available coercive move because the method would damage the trust, recruitment, or legitimacy needed to govern afterward.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 1125 frames [[LiuBei|刘备]]'s refusal to capture [[LiuZhang|刘璋]] at Fu as a combination of incomplete force and accumulated reputation. Liu Bei has accepted the objective of taking [[YizhouLateHan|益州]], but he has only recently arrived, has not established local恩信, and faces Liu Zhang's large escort. A bloodless seizure at the meeting could therefore be tactically fast while making later elite cooperation, civilian acceptance, and claims of humane rule harder to sustain.

The mechanism does not reduce reputation to hypocrisy. A norm can be sincerely held, strategically valuable, or both; once an actor has used it to attract followers, the norm also constrains which shortcuts remain affordable. Delay becomes a way to align military capacity, local support, and public justification before open seizure.

## Key Claims

- Reputational capital can constrain its holder as well as attract allies and followers.
- A tactically cheap capture may impose higher post-seizure costs if it makes the new ruler look predictably treacherous.
- Local trust and elite cooperation matter most when a conqueror lacks an established administrative base.
- Delay can combine sincere restraint, military caution, and strategic image protection without the source being able to separate private motives.
- Public justification becomes more valuable when the actor expects to recruit from the defeated polity rather than merely destroy it.

## Evidence

- Accepted objective, rejected method: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1125-bing-bu-xueren-zhuo-liuzhang-liubei-weihe-bu-yuanyi-lsaakh0scmfvorjhhtjv5j4m0kcb|Hanji 1125]] says Liu Bei enters Yizhou after accepting adviser arguments but refuses the proposed capture of Liu Zhang during their meeting.
- Reputation constraint: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1125-bing-bu-xueren-zhuo-liuzhang-liubei-weihe-bu-yuanyi-lsaakh0scmfvorjhhtjv5j4m0kcb|Hanji 1125]] connects the refusal to Liu Bei's reliance on generosity, loyalty, and humane reputation against Cao Cao.
- Local-capacity gap: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1125-bing-bu-xueren-zhuo-liuzhang-liubei-weihe-bu-yuanyi-lsaakh0scmfvorjhhtjv5j4m0kcb|Hanji 1125]] says Liu Bei has not yet established恩信 in Yizhou and Liu Zhang is not militarily undefended.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode does not establish Liu Bei's inner motive or show that reputation was the decisive factor. Immediate capture may also have failed militarily, divided Liu Bei's own coalition, or provoked local resistance. Later conflict does not prove that the earlier restraint was insincere, and later success does not prove that delay was the only viable strategy.

## What Changed

- Established the distinction between accepting a power-seizure objective and rejecting a reputationally costly method or moment.
- Preserved moral commitment, military caution, and strategic image management as overlapping explanations.
