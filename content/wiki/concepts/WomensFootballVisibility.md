## Definition

Women's football visibility / 女足主体性可见度 is the condition in which women footballers and women's teams are remembered, watched, supported, and criticized as sporting subjects rather than treated as novelties or symbols used mainly for another debate.

## Current Synthesis

Public attention is distinct from durable subjectivity. Victory can create recognition and opportunity, yet women athletes are still reduced when their success serves mainly to criticize men's football. Local fans, girls who choose the sport, live matches, and athlete-centered media create a more stable form of visibility grounded in the women's game itself.

An institutional-memory layer extends that distinction. Women's football had an earlier tradition before exclusion from men's grounds and formal restriction interrupted it. Present revival should therefore be understood not as the invention of a new game but as the rebuilding of participation, spectatorship, memory, and institutional access after suppression.

## Key Claims

- Attention after victory can expand recognition while making visibility dependent on exceptional results.
- Comparison with men's football can erase subjectivity when women become instruments in another argument.
- Local fan communities make women's football more durable because they watch teams rather than only viral national moments.
- Girls' participation needs visible women players and ordinary football settings that make entry imaginable.
- Athlete-controlled media can preserve complexity beyond beauty, toughness, or national-pride labels.
- Historical exclusion interrupted an existing women's football tradition, so revival includes recovery of memory and institutional access.

## Evidence

- Comparison problem: [[zhao-lina-zaichang-bi-ying-zhongyao-menjiang-de-xinxin-zhijue-yu-reai-229fde7d-d39d-4f7c-a33d-607f5370fc60]] says women's wins can draw praise while reducing players to a comparison with men's football.
- Local fandom and girls' choice: [[zhao-lina-zaichang-bi-ying-zhongyao-menjiang-de-xinxin-zhijue-yu-reai-229fde7d-d39d-4f7c-a33d-607f5370fc60]] links Shanghai fan growth, live atmosphere, participation, and athlete narration.
- Interrupted tradition: [[33-the-beautiful-game-glt5860295083]] identifies Lily Parr and describes women's football as important before bans and ground exclusion, followed by later recovery.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Comparison with men's football can still bring awareness or funding pressure, and historical recovery does not by itself secure present audiences, facilities, pay, or player agency. The English chronology in the new source is partly garbled, so the exact sequence and motives of institutional restriction remain source-scoped pending stronger evidence. The current evidence base is limited to one China-centered athlete conversation and one broad British football-history discussion.

## What Changed

- Added the interrupted-tradition frame to the existing distinction between attention and athlete subjectivity.
- Expanded durable visibility from media recognition to historical memory and institutional access.
