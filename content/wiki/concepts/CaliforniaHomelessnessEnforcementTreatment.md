# California Homelessness Enforcement Treatment

## Definition
California homelessness enforcement treatment is Hilton's source-scoped approach that combines encampment removal, addiction or mental-health treatment, sober-service requirements, and jail as the fallback for noncompliance.

## Current Synthesis
The concept extends the wiki's existing homelessness branch by adding a statewide gubernatorial campaign version. Hilton argues that local governments already have authority to remove encampments, especially after Grants Pass v. Oregon, and that a governor should escalate state enforcement if localities do not act.

The policy frame is not only punitive in the source: Hilton argues that money should move from permanent supportive housing toward recovery and mental-health treatment, including larger facilities enabled by an IMD waiver. But the source's causal emphasis differs from housing-supply pages because addiction, severe mental illness, enforcement, and sobriety rules are treated as the center of the problem.

## Key Claims
- Encampments are treated as already illegal and removable under existing authority.
- State law enforcement is presented as a backstop if local governments do not remove encampments.
- Addiction and severe mental illness are treated as the dominant homelessness drivers in the source.
- The proposed service model requires sobriety and redirects money toward recovery and mental-health infrastructure.
- Jail is presented as the fallback if treatment is refused or public-order rules are not followed.
