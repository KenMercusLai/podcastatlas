## Definition
Touristification of everyday markets is the shift in a resident-serving market's products, rules, hours, and spatial priorities toward visitor consumption and shareable experience.

## Current Synthesis
The source's Xiaoheyan case shows a feedback loop: short-video attention brings visitors; ready-to-eat foods become more profitable and legible than routine produce; vendors switch categories; managers extend experience-oriented supply; and residents move to a nearby market better suited to buying meat and vegetables. Tourism can add income and preserve visibility, but if replication removes ordinary provisioning and local diversity, the market may lose the lived foundation that made it attractive.

## Key Claims
- Visitor attention changes tenant mix because portable, photogenic food competes differently from vegetables, meat, and household goods.
- A market can remain crowded while becoming less useful to residents.
- Year-round presentation can detach seasonal foods from their earlier calendar without making the new practice inherently false.
- Management choices about stall curation, vehicle access, and opening hours shape whether visitor and resident functions can coexist.
- Homogenization occurs when visible success is copied faster than local variety can be protected.

## Evidence
- Category displacement: [[ep287-yiqi-guang-zaoshi-xunzhao-beifang-zui-xianhuo-de-zaochen-lpuox4b-b02k-awt-hsjuph-froi]] reports more cooked-food stalls and some produce vendors switching into food service at Xiaoheyan.
- Resident substitution: [[ep287-yiqi-guang-zaoshi-xunzhao-beifang-zui-xianhuo-de-zaochen-lpuox4b-b02k-awt-hsjuph-froi]] says local shoppers increasingly use nearby Binhe for meat and vegetables, while electric-vehicle restrictions further discourage larger purchases at Xiaoheyan.
- Calendar change: [[ep287-yiqi-guang-zaoshi-xunzhao-beifang-zui-xianhuo-de-zaochen-lpuox4b-b02k-awt-hsjuph-froi]] notes that pork-and-pickled-cabbage dishes once associated more strongly with the pre-Spring-Festival period are sold year-round for visitors.
- Management boundary: [[ep287-yiqi-guang-zaoshi-xunzhao-beifang-zui-xianhuo-de-zaochen-lpuox4b-b02k-awt-hsjuph-froi]] argues for curation that prevents repeated trending stalls from erasing diversity.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Tourism is not presented as pure damage: it can create sales, maintain attention, and introduce visitors to regional food. Resident and visitor markets may differentiate spatially rather than one simply destroying the other. The account is based mainly on one prominent Shenyang market and does not measure vendor income, resident welfare, or long-run survival.

## What Changed
- Established the concept from the Xiaoheyan-Binhe differentiation described by the source.
- Separated crowd size from resident usefulness as measures of market health.
- Framed curation and transport rules as part of the authenticity tradeoff.
