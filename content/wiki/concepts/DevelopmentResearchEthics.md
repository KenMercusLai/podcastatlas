# Development Research Ethics

Development research ethics is the tension [[piles-of-cash-and-a-town-of-solutions-in-kenya-nigeria-summer-school]] raises around experiments in places such as [[BusiaKenya|Busia]]. [[TavneetSuri]] says some Kenyans may feel proud that knowledge was generated locally, while others may feel uneasy about being part of experiments or ask whether research benefits everyone.

The concept does not reject [[RandomizedControlledTrials]]. Instead, it asks whether global knowledge, Nobel recognition, and academic careers are matched by local capacity, policy uptake, and practical benefits. [[CarolNkesa]]'s career shows one positive route: local staff can become research leaders and institution builders.

## Key Claims
- Field experiments can create useful knowledge while still raising questions about consent, benefit, and ownership.
- Policy translation matters ethically because study communities should not be treated only as data sources.
- Local research capacity can reduce extractive dynamics when people such as [[CarolNkesa]] help set questions and build organizations.
- [[EvidenceBasedDevelopmentPolicy]] should be judged by implementation and local benefit, not only publication or citation.
