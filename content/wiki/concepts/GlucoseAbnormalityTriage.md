# Glucose Abnormality Triage / 血糖异常分诊

## Definition
Glucose abnormality triage is a clinical-escalation frame for interpreting symptoms, screening values, low readings, and related reproductive or skin signals in context before inferring insulin resistance or diabetes.

## Current Synthesis
The episode separates ordinary variation from repeated or concerning patterns. Seeing food and feeling hungry can be a sensory response rather than evidence of insulin resistance, and occasional post-meal sleepiness may warrant observation rather than immediate diagnosis. Recurrent marked sleepiness after most meals, abnormal fasting glucose at screening, or low readings accompanied by palpitations, sweating, or tremor create a stronger reason to review measurements and seek qualified assessment.

One value or device curve remains incomplete. Glucose elevation can have causes other than diabetes, an asymptomatic overnight low on a consumer sensor may not establish disease, and menstrual irregularity, persistent acne or folliculitis, excess hair growth, or pigmentation may justify endocrine, gynecologic, and dermatologic review without proving one syndrome. Selected clinical evaluation may include an oral glucose tolerance test, C-peptide, or insulin-release testing, but test choice belongs to the clinical context.

## Key Claims
- Repeated patterns, associated symptoms, and baseline change are more informative than one nonspecific sensation or isolated device value.
- Recurrent marked post-meal somnolence or abnormal screening glucose can justify measurement review and clinical follow-up.
- A low glucose reading is interpreted with symptoms, medication, timing, and confirmation rather than by the number alone.
- Menstrual and persistent skin changes can be part of a wider endocrine-metabolic presentation, but they are not diagnostic by appearance.
- Oral glucose tolerance, C-peptide, and insulin-release tests are selected clinical tools rather than universal self-tests.
- Online menus, AI advice, and generic fasting plans cannot replace assessment that accounts for disease, medication, age, body size, and routine.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode provides public medical education, not diagnostic criteria or an individualized care pathway. Its fasting, two-hour, and low-glucose numbers are episode-reported snapshots whose interpretation may differ by specimen, symptoms, pregnancy, medication, diabetes status, guideline, and jurisdiction. “PMOS” nomenclature and the relationship among insulin resistance, PCOS-related presentations, fertility, acne, pigmentation, and body size require current clinical criteria and differential diagnosis.

## What Changed
- Established a symptom-to-assessment frame that resists diagnosis from one sensation, skin sign, or wearable reading.
- Kept numeric thresholds and named tests inside source and clinical context.
