## Definition

器物式政治最后通牒 / Object-mediated political ultimatum is a coercive message conveyed through a deliberately selected object whose meaning depends on rank, prior relations, material dependence, and immediate political context rather than explicit words.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 1130 supplies one disputed but analytically useful case: [[CaoCao|曹操]] reportedly sends [[XunYu|荀彧]] an empty food box after Xun Yu opposes Cao Cao's elevation as Duke of Wei and recipient of the [[JiuXiRitualPromotion|九锡礼遇]]. The episode interprets emptiness as a compressed message that Xun Yu's food, office, political relationship, or life has reached its end.

The mechanism is not a universal symbolic dictionary. The same object could be banal outside this relationship. Its force comes from asymmetric power, Xun Yu's dependence on the political order Cao Cao controls, the recent public rupture, and the danger of asking for clarification. Ambiguity can protect the sender from an explicit order while shifting interpretation and action onto the recipient.

## Key Claims

- An ordinary object can become a political command when context sharply narrows its plausible meanings.
- Material absence can signal withdrawal of livelihood, office, favor, protection, or permission to remain.
- Ambiguity can intensify coercion because the weaker party must infer the safe response without demanding clarification.
- The signal's effectiveness depends on shared history and power asymmetry, not on a fixed meaning inherent in the object.
- A vivid object story is memorable evidence but not self-authenticating proof of the sender's intent or the recipient's cause of death.

## Evidence

- Empty-box interpretation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1130-xunyu-weihe-zai-caocao-dianfeng-shi-bei-bi-zijin-li10bxmmfjp0z2u8t17x2lekwyfh|Hanji 1130]] says Cao Cao sends Xun Yu a food container with nothing inside after their political break and interprets it as a terminal message.
- Contextual coercion: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1130-xunyu-weihe-zai-caocao-dianfeng-shi-bei-bi-zijin-li10bxmmfjp0z2u8t17x2lekwyfh|Hanji 1130]] links the box to Xun Yu's removal from the central court, opposition to Cao Cao's elevation, and subsequent death.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source is a commentary episode, not direct proof of the event or Cao Cao's private intent. Different records give different accounts of Xun Yu's death, and even an authentic empty box would not establish one compulsory interpretation. The concept therefore describes how such a signal could work inside an asymmetric relationship; it does not treat the box as a verified execution order.

## What Changed

- Established the concept from the Xun Yu empty-box account while preserving the dispute over event, meaning, intent, and causation.
