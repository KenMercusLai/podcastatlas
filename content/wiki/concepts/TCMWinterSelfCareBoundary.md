# TCM Winter Self-Care Boundary / 中医冬季养生边界

## Definition
TCM winter self-care boundary separates adaptable, low-complexity winter routines from [[MaoZheTCMDoctor|毛哲]]'s source-scoped “winter storage,” pathogenic-cold, yang, qi, blood-stasis, food-therapy, herbal, patent-medicine, foot-soak, and syndrome-treatment claims.

## Current Synthesis
Across the two winter episodes, the strongest practical layer is moderation and scene matching. Activity need not become forced heavy sweating; indoor heating need not make the room uncomfortably hot; removable layers can accommodate a warm room, cold commute, wind, and different activity levels; and tolerable meals may suit some people without becoming universal prescriptions. The repeated insistence that clothing, food, tea, exercise, foot soaking, and “tonifying” practices depend on the person is more durable than any specific remedy.

The newer episode usefully distinguishes dry cough from difficult sputum before discussing pear, orange, or radish, and rejects treating 板蓝根 as a universal cold medicine. That is symptom awareness, not a validated home diagnostic or prescribing algorithm. The clinical boundary matters whenever the discussion moves from comfort into influenza, mycoplasma infection, cardiovascular disease, anemia, elevated glucose, painful or discolored extremities, chronic throat symptoms, pregnancy, persistent digestive problems, or medication selection. “Wind-cold,” “wind-heat,” external-cold/internal-heat, yang deficiency, qi deficiency, blood stasis, yin-deficiency fire, and dampness organize the guest's TCM account but do not identify causes, choose medicines, or exclude serious disease.

## Key Claims
- Winter routines should adapt to actual temperature, indoor-outdoor transition, travel mode, activity, symptoms, tolerance, age, and medical context rather than follow one universal seasonal rule.
- Moderate heating, removable weather-appropriate clothing, comfortable activity, regular sleep, daylight, and tolerable meals form a lower-complexity wellbeing layer without becoming infection prevention or treatment.
- Heavy sweating, cold food, tea, lamb, radish, pear, salted orange, Chinese yam, soup, tremella, and foot soaking cannot be assigned one preventive or therapeutic effect for every person from these sources.
- The episodes' “winter storage,” pathogenic-cold, open-pore, yang, qi, blood-stasis, yin-deficiency, dampness, organ-nourishing, regional, and food-therapy mechanisms remain source-scoped TCM interpretations rather than validated diagnoses.
- Fever, sore throat, anemia, glucose abnormalities, pain, purple lips, pregnancy-related chills, cardiovascular risk, or worsening illness override casual seasonal experimentation and need appropriate care.
- Herbal teas, 荆防颗粒, antiviral products, 板蓝根, 三九贴, and other medicines or physical practices require indication, dosing, age- and pregnancy-specific safety, contraindication, interaction, and evidence checks absent from the episodes.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources are structured podcast summaries, not guidelines, diagnostic examinations, controlled trials, medication reviews, or individualized nutrition assessments. They do not establish that sweating depletes yang, cold food causes clinically important digestive energy loss, tea generally depletes qi, thermal transitions cause infection, or northern and southern geography selects a safe medicine sequence. Pear, salted orange, radish, lamb, herbs, patent products, sunlight, forehead rubbing, and 三九贴 are not shown here to prevent or treat infection, vascular disease, anemia, glucose dysregulation, chronic pharyngitis, or pregnancy-related symptoms. Moderate warmth and personally comfortable food or movement can support comfort, but urgent or persistent symptoms require appropriate medical evaluation.

## What Changed
- Added scene-matched layering, indoor-outdoor transitions, and commute context to the lower-complexity winter routine.
- Added dry-cough versus difficult-sputum awareness without promoting food remedies into diagnosis or treatment.
- Expanded the medication boundary to herbal teas, patent products, antiviral products, 板蓝根, pediatric infection, and regional or stage-based prescribing claims.
