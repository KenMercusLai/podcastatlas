# 1973 Chilean Coup

## Definition
The 1973 Chilean Coup was the September 11 military overthrow of [[SalvadorAllende]]'s elected government and the opening rupture that enabled [[AugustoPinochet]]'s dictatorship.

## Current Synthesis
[[371-the-1973-chilean-coup-general-pinochet-seizes-power-part-2-glt3740766328]] explains the coup through interacting causes rather than a single mastermind: class polarization, inflation and shortage, disputed constitutional authority, anti-Marxist fear, U.S. interference and encouragement, internal military plotting, and the removal of constitutionalist commander [[CarlosPrats]]. The United States helped make a coup more likely, but Chilean officers chose, organized, and executed it. The coup's moral assessment also cannot end with the government's fall because rapid mass detention and later institutionalized terror formed part of the regime it created.

## Key Claims
- Domestic social conflict and institutional deadlock were causal forces, not scenery around a purely foreign operation.
- U.S. interference and CIA contacts mattered without proving operational authorship of the September 11 plan.
- The failed Tanquetazo served as both warning and rehearsal for later plotters.
- Prats's resignation and Pinochet's appointment removed a key command-level restraint.
- Allende's refusal of exile preserved democratic legitimacy but could not stop military force.
- The coup's consequence was not only a change of government but the creation of conditions for personal dictatorship and state terror.

## Evidence
- Polarization and foreign pressure: [[371-the-1973-chilean-coup-general-pinochet-seizes-power-part-2-glt3740766328]] combines reform support, economic distress, constitutional conflict, U.S. hostility, and officer agency.
- Command succession: [[371-the-1973-chilean-coup-general-pinochet-seizes-power-part-2-glt3740766328]] links the Tanquetazo, Prats's fall, and Pinochet's appointment.
- September 11 operation: [[371-the-1973-chilean-coup-general-pinochet-seizes-power-part-2-glt3740766328]] traces the seizure of Valparaiso, isolation and attack of La Moneda, and Allende's refusal to leave.
- Regime consequence: [[371-the-1973-chilean-coup-general-pinochet-seizes-power-part-2-glt3740766328]] follows early killings and detention into the Caravan of Death, DINA, and Pinochet's personal rule.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode disputes the simplified claim that Washington masterminded the operation, not the well-supported claim that the United States interfered and welcomed Allende's removal. Allende's possible referendum plan and the timing of Pinochet's commitment remain uncertain. Explanations for support among Chileans do not establish legal or moral equivalence between political-economic crisis and military repression.

## What Changed
- Created a multi-causal account joining domestic conflict, foreign pressure, military agency, and command succession.
- Extended the event's scope from the seizure of power into the repressive regime it enabled.
