# Frontier Model Commercial Half-Life

## Definition

Frontier model commercial half-life is the period during which a model generation remains a material source of competitive customer demand before newer capability, lower prices, routing, or substitutes reduce its revenue contribution.

## Current Synthesis

The source portrays frontier-model revenue as unusually perishable: it reports that about 80% of Anthropic revenue came from models released in the preceding six months. If directionally correct, a frontier lab cannot treat a successful model as a durable installed product; it must repeatedly fund research, training, deployment, migration, and distribution merely to defend current demand.

This does not prove that every model becomes worthless after six months. Older generations may persist where cost, latency, stability, local control, or workflow certification matter more than maximum capability. The durable judgment is that model-company valuation must reflect both rapid adoption and the replacement cost of continually refreshing what customers buy.

## Key Claims

- Revenue concentration in recent model vintages makes release cadence and migration quality part of recurring economics.
- A short commercial half-life can turn technical leadership into a renewable expense rather than a permanent moat.
- Older models can retain segmented demand when their price, speed, stability, or deployment boundary fits the task.
- Workflow, distribution, data, trust, and switching costs can lengthen commercial life even when benchmark leadership changes.
- Valuation should compare growth with the continuing compute and R&D cost required to replace aging revenue.

## Evidence

- **Recent-vintage revenue:** [[03e51cc1-020d-4c30-8c07-8f8614b46e7f-03e51cc1-020d-4c30-8c07-8f8614b46e7f]] reports that roughly 80% of Anthropic revenue came from models released during the previous six months.
- **Possible segmentation:** [[03e51cc1-020d-4c30-8c07-8f8614b46e7f-03e51cc1-020d-4c30-8c07-8f8614b46e7f]] suggests that model generations could eventually coexist like chip process nodes if capability progress slows and different price-performance tiers remain useful.
- **Competitive renewal:** [[03e51cc1-020d-4c30-8c07-8f8614b46e7f-03e51cc1-020d-4c30-8c07-8f8614b46e7f]] connects short model life to continual talent, compute, workflow feedback, and product investment rather than treating one release as a lasting lead.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The 80% figure is episode-attributed and lacks the underlying prospectus definition, cohort table, discounts, routing behavior, and revenue-recognition detail. Rapid recent-model revenue can partly reflect fast total growth rather than collapse of older demand. One company's high-growth period cannot establish a stable industry half-life, and future slowing of capability gains could extend model longevity.

## What Changed

- Created a reusable distinction between rapid model adoption and the durability of each revenue-bearing generation.
- Added the possibility that older and newer models coexist in price-performance tiers rather than following total replacement.
