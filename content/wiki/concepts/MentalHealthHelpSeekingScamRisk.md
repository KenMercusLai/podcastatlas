# Mental-Health Help-Seeking Scam Risk / 心理求助被骗风险

## Definition
Mental-health help-seeking scam risk / 心理求助被骗风险 is the vulnerability that appears when people in psychiatric or psychological distress urgently seek relief and pay unqualified or exploitative providers who market themselves as healers, therapists, or guides.

## Current Synthesis
The concept adds a consumer-protection branch to the wiki's mental-health access discussion. [[JiangTaoPsychiatrist|姜涛]] warns that patients and families should spend money on regular treatment, and gives examples of people spending very large sums on online "healers" or informal helpers whose qualifications and accountability are weak.

The risk is intensified by distress, shame, access gaps, and hope for speed. When regular psychiatric appointments, reliable psychotherapists, family cooperation, and social supports feel inadequate, a seller's confident language can look like care. The concept therefore does not dismiss all nonclinical support; it marks the point where unverified services become financially and clinically dangerous substitutes for care.

## Key Claims
- Distress and urgency can make patients and families vulnerable to expensive, unqualified mental-health services.
- Informal healing language can imitate therapy while lacking diagnosis, supervision, referral, and complaint channels.
- Access scarcity and stigma increase the demand for quick, private, or comforting alternatives.
- Financial loss is not the only harm; delayed regular treatment and false confidence can worsen risk.
- The safer boundary is to prioritize regular hospitals, qualified clinicians, and accountable services when symptoms are serious.

- Regular-care advice - [[shi-di-fu-shuo-466-jiangtao-5wan-jingshenke-yisheng-dui-1yi-jingshen-jibing-huanzhe-lpdsk96akpwtccxyesjdgtw8skbn]] records Jiang Tao's advice to seek treatment at regular hospitals and spend money where it matters.
- Exploitative examples - [[shi-di-fu-shuo-466-jiangtao-5wan-jingshenke-yisheng-dui-1yi-jingshen-jibing-huanzhe-lpdsk96akpwtccxyesjdgtw8skbn]] describes patients spending large sums on online healers or similar informal services.
- Qualification concern - [[shi-di-fu-shuo-466-jiangtao-5wan-jingshenke-yisheng-dui-1yi-jingshen-jibing-huanzhe-lpdsk96akpwtccxyesjdgtw8skbn]] says some helpers may themselves be recovered patients rather than qualified professionals.
- Marketing vulnerability - [[shi-di-fu-shuo-466-jiangtao-5wan-jingshenke-yisheng-dui-1yi-jingshen-jibing-huanzhe-lpdsk96akpwtccxyesjdgtw8skbn]] links these services to opportunism, rhetoric, and online marketing.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not prove that every informal support service is fraudulent or that only hospitals can help with every form of distress. The concept applies where unqualified services replace needed care, conceal risk, extract large payments, or make claims beyond their competence.
