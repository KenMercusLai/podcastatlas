# Forced Scam Labor

## Definition
Forced scam labor is the coercion of recruited or trafficked people into performing online fraud through document confiscation, confinement, threats, violence, or other restrictions on leaving.

## Current Synthesis
The source shows that a scam operation can victimize people on both sides of the contact. Targets abroad lose money through fraudulent cryptocurrency platforms, while some workers are recruited with false salary promises, deprived of passports, and forced to carry out the approaches. This makes worker rescue, safe return, and action against traffickers necessary parts of enforcement rather than incidental humanitarian concerns.

## Key Claims
- Deceptive recruitment can turn an apparent overseas job into coerced criminal labor.
- Passport confiscation, threats, and violence restrict a worker's ability to leave or refuse tasks.
- A person performing a scam may simultaneously be an agent of victimization and a trafficking victim.
- Closing a compound does not by itself provide safe return, accountability, or protection against re-trafficking.
- Effective enforcement must address recruiters, controllers, financial infrastructure, and protected leadership as well as physical sites.

### Enforcement boundary
- [[tech-20261103-1103-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261103-1103-mp-tech-pod-128]] warns that operations can move into smaller sites or other countries after large compounds close.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source says many workers are victims but does not establish that every worker in every compound is coerced. It gives no worker testimony, prevalence estimate, recruitment-chain detail, or post-closure outcome data in the supplied summary. Worker victimhood also does not erase the financial harm experienced by scam targets.

## What Changed
- Added a dedicated framework for the worker-victim side of industrialized online fraud.
- Distinguished physical-site closure from rescue, safe return, and dismantling of coercive labor networks.
