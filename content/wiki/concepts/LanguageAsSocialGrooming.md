# Language As Social Grooming / 语言作为社会性梳毛

Language as social grooming is [[83-shumao-bagua-ji-yuyan-de-jinhua-renji-guanxi-dengbashu-zan-bu-fu-bu-xing-698132861]]'s central explanation for why ordinary conversation matters. Drawing on [[GroomingGossipAndEvolutionOfLanguage|《梳毛、八卦及语言的进化》]], the episode says language can replace part of one-to-one grooming because a speaker can include several people in the same talk event.

This makes language social infrastructure before it is only an information channel. The source uses small-group conversation, gossip, emoji, office chat, art, religion, and podcasting to show how language creates felt presence, affiliation, and social updating even when little "serious" content is exchanged.

## Key Claims
- Conversation can maintain relationships at lower time cost than one-to-one grooming.
- Human small-group talk often stays bounded; when too many people join, conversation tends to split into smaller groups.
- Language transmits social position, emotional stance, and affiliation as well as factual content.
- Digital text can feel weaker as social grooming, so users add emoji, memes, voice, video, or stronger emotional signals.
- This frame complements [[LanguageUserInterface]]: language can operate tools, but it also binds people.
