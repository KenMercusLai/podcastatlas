# Party-Membership Leadership Selection

## Definition

Party-membership leadership selection is a system in which enrolled activists choose a party leader, including a potential head of government, rather than leaving the decision only to elected legislators or the general electorate.

## Current Synthesis

[[246-the-fall-of-liz-truss-glt3484252092]] treats membership ballots as a participation-legitimacy tradeoff. They widen choice beyond MPs, but a small, motivated membership may differ sharply from the parliamentary party, ordinary party voters, and the national electorate. When a governing party changes leader between general elections, that mismatch can install a prime minister whose activist mandate does not translate into parliamentary discipline or governing capacity.

The Truss case is not proof that member ballots always fail. It warns that internal democratic legitimacy and public governing legitimacy are different tests, especially when members reward ideological clarity or perceived authenticity over coalition maintenance and policy feasibility.

## Key Claims

- Enlarging participation inside a party does not necessarily make the selector representative of the wider electorate.
- Party activists may reward ideological or authenticity signals that legislators and general-election voters assess differently.
- A membership mandate cannot substitute for parliamentary confidence, administrative feasibility, or economic credibility.
- Governing parties create unusually high public stakes when an internal contest selects the prime minister between elections.

## Evidence

- Truss selection: [[246-the-fall-of-liz-truss-glt3484252092]] says Conservative members chose Truss despite substantial divergence from MPs and broader public opinion.
- Cross-party comparison: [[246-the-fall-of-liz-truss-glt3484252092]] compares Conservative rule changes with Labour's member-driven selection of Jeremy Corbyn.
- Competence-authenticity tension: [[246-the-fall-of-liz-truss-glt3484252092]] suggests activists may have read Truss's lack of polish as authenticity while penalizing Sunak's fluency.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode supplies two British examples and an argument by the hosts, not a comparative study of leadership-selection systems. Member ballots can broaden participation and challenge closed parliamentary elites. The Truss collapse cannot be attributed to selection rules alone because policy design, external shocks, party faction, communication, and individual judgment also mattered.
