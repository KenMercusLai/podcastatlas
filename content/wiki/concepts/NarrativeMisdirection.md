# Narrative Misdirection / 叙事误导

Narrative misdirection / 叙事误导 is the story practice foregrounded in [[27-xiqukeke-yizai-nin-zhuoxia-anzhi-zhadan-582370829]]: the audience is led toward one likely center, culprit, motive, or genre expectation, then forced to reinterpret earlier details after a shift or reveal.

The source's strongest examples are [[PsychoFilm|《惊魂记》 / Psycho]], where the theft plot yields to the motel and mother mystery, and the rocket-launch story, where suspicions move from sabotage by a jealous scientist to food logistics and finally to the implied presence of a hidden wife in the spacecraft. The technique is not arbitrary trickery when earlier details keep supporting the later reading.

## Key Claims
- Misdirection works by making a provisional explanation feel plausible, not by withholding all logic.
- A [[MacGuffin|MacGuffin]] can anchor misdirection by giving the audience an object or question to follow until the real pressure appears.
- Effective reversal changes how earlier evidence reads, rather than only adding a surprise ending.
- The device differs from fair-play deduction: it may overlap with mystery, but its main effect is emotional and attentional redirection.
