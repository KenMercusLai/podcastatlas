## Definition

忠告不转化 / Corrective advice nonconversion is the pattern where accurate, concrete, potentially self-saving advice reaches a powerholder but does not become changed action. The failure can come from arrogance, self-image protection, private-interest dependence, or inability to see restraint as preservation rather than humiliation.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-834-lishi-gaosu-ni-xiaoren-weihe-chang-qiqi-lj8z2vfu6lpggumcakwejoi6z9hc|Hanji 834]] supplies the founding case through [[ZhuMuLateHan|朱穆]] and [[LiangJiLateHan|梁冀]]. Zhu Mu does not only scold Liang in moral abstractions. He names public hardship, local official abuse, expense pressure, redirected resentment, and the risk that all blame will attach to Liang's name; he also gives executable remedies such as dismissing unfit officials, reducing luxury, refusing gifts, and strengthening impartial inspection.

The advice fails at conversion. Liang treats the warning as a personal indictment and replies sarcastically rather than using it as a path to preserve position and reputation. The episode then shows the external consequences of failed advice: [[ChenFanLateHan|陈蕃]], [[YanDuLateHan|延笃]], and [[ZhangLingLateHan|张陵]] each create visible checks against Liang or his agents, but those checks are riskier and less systemic than voluntary self-restraint would have been.

The ruler-facing version comes through [[DuQiaoLateHan|杜乔]] and [[EmperorHuanOfHan|汉桓帝]] in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-829-junzi-weishenme-doubuguo-xiaoren-lthvnstkfu8n9thx1sz6yezl4yqq|Hanji 829]]. Du Qiao's memorial names appointment quality, fair reward, punishment of wrongdoing, and the danger of unmerited honors to [[LiangJiLateHan|梁冀]]'s family and eunuchs. Huan gives no answer, while Liang Ji and eunuch-side actors can later convert Du Qiao's institutional stance into personal resentment and political danger.

## Key Claims

- Corrective advice is most valuable before collapse, but that is also when the powerholder may least want to hear it.
- Advice can fail even when it contains concrete remedies rather than only moral accusation.
- A recipient's self-image can recode a survival warning as insult, making nonconversion psychological as well as political.
- When advice does not convert into self-restraint, later correction often arrives through coercion, public humiliation, or risky enforcement by others.
- The adviser can be a genuine "benefactor" only if the recipient recognizes the advice as help rather than attack.
- Nonconversion becomes sharper when the same power field can turn unaccepted advice into later accusation or resentment.

## Evidence

- Ruler-facing nonresponse: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-829-junzi-weishenme-doubuguo-xiaoren-lthvnstkfu8n9thx1sz6yezl4yqq|Hanji 829]] says Du Qiao warns Huan that worthy appointment and fair reward and punishment are core state tasks, but Huan gives no answer.
- Interest collision after advice: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-829-junzi-weishenme-doubuguo-xiaoren-lthvnstkfu8n9thx1sz6yezl4yqq|Hanji 829]] says Du Qiao criticizes unmerited Liang and eunuch rewards, then later blocks Liang Ji's rite and recommendation requests.
- Resentment conversion: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-829-junzi-weishenme-doubuguo-xiaoren-lthvnstkfu8n9thx1sz6yezl4yqq|Hanji 829]] says Tang Heng and Zuo Guan turn Du Qiao and Li Gu's earlier accession stance into Huan's resentment.
- Concrete warning: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-834-lishi-gaosu-ni-xiaoren-weihe-chang-qiqi-lj8z2vfu6lpggumcakwejoi6z9hc|Hanji 834]] says Zhu Mu warns Liang Ji that local extraction, official violence, fiscal exhaustion, and unrest will turn the general-in-chief into the object of public resentment.
- Executable remedies: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-834-lishi-gaosu-ni-xiaoren-weihe-chang-qiqi-lj8z2vfu6lpggumcakwejoi6z9hc|Hanji 834]] says Zhu proposes dismissing unfit officials, reducing mansions and gardens, refusing commandery and kingdom gifts, and making inspectors enforce law fairly.
- Nonconversion: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-834-lishi-gaosu-ni-xiaoren-weihe-chang-qiqi-lj8z2vfu6lpggumcakwejoi6z9hc|Hanji 834]] says Liang Ji does not adopt the advice and answers with sarcasm.
- External checks after failed advice: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-834-lishi-gaosu-ni-xiaoren-weihe-chang-qiqi-lj8z2vfu6lpggumcakwejoi6z9hc|Hanji 834]] places Chen Fan's envoy punishment, Yan Du's killing of Liang's retainer, and Zhang Ling's court sword-removal scene after Liang ignores self-limiting counsel.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept currently rests on two late-Han source notes and should not be treated as a universal model of all failed advice. Hanji 829 and Hanji 834 do not prove that different wording would have succeeded; the problem may be the recipient's interests, power ecology, and dependence on the networks being criticized. Hanji 834 also says Liang Ji still respects Zhu Mu enough not to punish him directly, so nonconversion is not the same as immediate retaliation. The Hai Rui analogy and closing moral claims are useful source framing but are not independent evidence for the concept.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 829 as the ruler-facing version where Du Qiao's accurate memorial reaches Huan but does not convert into changed reward or appointment policy.
- Created the concept from Hanji 834's Zhu Mu-to-Liang Ji warning sequence.
- Defined failed advice conversion as distinct from advice quality or courage to speak.
- Linked failed self-restraint to later higher-cost enforcement signals against Liang and his agents.
