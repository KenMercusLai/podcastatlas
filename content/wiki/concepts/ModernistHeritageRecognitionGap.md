# Modernist Heritage Recognition Gap / 现代主义遗产识别落差

## Definition
Modernist heritage recognition gap is the preservation problem where modernist buildings look too plain, recent, or unornamented for the public to recognize their historical and architectural value.

## Current Synthesis
The episode argues that modernist heritage can be endangered because people associate old-building value with carved ornament, columns, age, and visible historicity. Modern forms may be easier to dismiss even when they embody important design history or collective memory. The demolished Garden Hotel swimming pool and Shanghai diving pool discussion give this gap a concrete memory and preservation dimension.

## Key Claims
- Visual simplicity can make modernist heritage seem less valuable than older-looking architecture.
- Lack of obvious ornament can weaken public support before demolition decisions.
- Sports and leisure structures can hold memory value even when they are not monumental.
- Replacement quality complicates preservation judgment when a new building improves public cultural space.
- Archive-backed interpretation and public writing can reduce the recognition gap.

## Evidence
- Simplicity evidence: [[498-xunzhao-laian-wei-jindai-shanghai-wutongqu-chuangzao-fengmao-de-faguo-jianzhushi-lskf8bdkhs71lbwjomt3gu1ko8be]] says modernist buildings can be demolished because they look simple and lack carved flowers or columns.
- Pool evidence: [[498-xunzhao-laian-wei-jindai-shanghai-wutongqu-chuangzao-fengmao-de-faguo-jianzhushi-lskf8bdkhs71lbwjomt3gu1ko8be]] discusses an Olympic-standard pool at Garden Hotel that was later demolished despite its historical and design interest.
- Diving-pool evidence: [[498-xunzhao-laian-wei-jindai-shanghai-wutongqu-chuangzao-fengmao-de-faguo-jianzhushi-lskf8bdkhs71lbwjomt3gu1ko8be]] describes the 1960s Shanghai diving pool as a major west-Shanghai memory site whose demolition was contested.
- Interpretation evidence: [[498-xunzhao-laian-wei-jindai-shanghai-wutongqu-chuangzao-fengmao-de-faguo-jianzhushi-lskf8bdkhs71lbwjomt3gu1ko8be]] connects books, plaques, and research to protecting the meaning of such sites.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not imply every modernist building should be preserved unchanged. It explicitly leaves room for cases where a new building, such as Shanghai Symphony Hall, may be judged better than the structure it replaced.
