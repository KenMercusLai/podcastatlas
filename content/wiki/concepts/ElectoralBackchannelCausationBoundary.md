# Electoral Backchannel Causation Boundary

## Definition

The electoral backchannel causation boundary separates evidence of contact or improper signaling from proof that a campaign caused a foreign actor to change policy and thereby altered an election.

## Current Synthesis

The Chennault controversy shows why four questions must remain distinct: whether contact occurred, what authority and message the intermediary carried, whether the recipient changed course because of it, and whether that change affected the election. The episode accepts the first and treats questionable signals as plausible, but denies that the available account establishes decisive causation because South Vietnam had independent objections to the talks and already preferred Nixon.

## Key Claims

- Documented contact is not by itself proof of an authorized offer or successful intervention.
- Impropriety and decisive causal effect are separate judgments.
- Foreign governments and leaders retain agency rather than functioning as passive instruments of a U.S. campaign.
- Prior preferences and independent policy objections can make a backchannel redundant rather than decisive.
- Counterfactual claims about peace and election outcomes require a higher evidentiary threshold than evidence of communication.

## Evidence

- Contact and suspicion: [[513-america-in-68-nixons-great-comeback-part-6-glt3853956463]] accepts Chennault-campaign contact and Johnson's belief that Nixon was encouraging non-cooperation.
- Claimed mechanism: [[513-america-in-68-nixons-great-comeback-part-6-glt3853956463]] states the theory that South Vietnam was promised a better deal after a Nixon victory.
- Recipient agency: [[513-america-in-68-nixons-great-comeback-part-6-glt3853956463]] emphasizes Thieu's objection to Viet Cong participation, pre-existing Nixon preference, and likely rejection of Johnson's proposal.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode's rejection of decisive sabotage does not establish that the backchannel was harmless, lawful, or irrelevant. Missing records, disputed authority, Chennault's self-presentation, Johnson's surveillance-derived beliefs, and an unknowable peace-talk counterfactual prevent a definitive causal conclusion from this source alone.

## What Changed

- Added a reusable test that keeps contact, authorization, recipient response, policy effect, and electoral effect analytically separate.
