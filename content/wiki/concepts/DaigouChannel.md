# Daigou Channel / 代购渠道

## Definition
The daigou channel is informal overseas purchasing by students, migrants, and resellers that carries foreign consumer goods into China outside official import channels and builds category awareness before formal distribution exists.

## Current Synthesis
The source's account is specific: [[Swisse|Swisse]]'s expensive U.S. expansion underperformed, and back in Australia the company found that pharmacies in Chinese-Australian districts sold roughly ten times what ordinary stores sold. Students and migrants were buying in bulk to take home or resell online, and supplements - like infant formula - suited the model because unit values were high and purchases repeated. The hosts stress that early daigou was neither industrial nor fully open: it was grey, relationship-dependent work that later lost that character when large e-commerce platforms ran purchasing operations themselves.

The wiki treats this as a demand-discovery mechanism rather than only a workaround. Daigou proved mainland willingness to pay for a category that had no official presence, moved goods without standard import registration, and produced something more durable than the sales themselves - consumer familiarity with Australian supplement brands. When the platformised cross-border channel arrived, that familiarity was already in place, which is why the source can date the "Australian supplements are good" belief to the overlap of informal purchasing and the [[BondedWarehouseCrossBorderImport|2015 policy window]].

## Key Claims
- Daigou surfaced demand that neither the brand nor official channels had identified, which is why the discovery surprised the company itself.
- Diaspora concentration made the effect visible: a handful of Chinese-district pharmacies showed roughly tenfold volume against comparable ordinary stores.
- Product fit matters: high unit value plus repeat purchase makes supplements and infant formula natural daigou categories.
- Because goods travelled outside standard import procedures, the channel functioned as an unregistered route into the market while it lasted.
- Platform entry professionalised the grey market, converting person-to-person purchasing into cross-border e-commerce rather than ending it.
- The channel's most durable output was category awareness, which later formal channels could monetise.

## Evidence
- Demand discovery: [[vol-275-aozhou-baojianpin-shi-zenme-huoqilai-de-1015773861]] says Swisse noticed Chinese-district pharmacies selling at roughly ten times ordinary store levels after an underwhelming U.S. push.
- Buyer behaviour: [[vol-275-aozhou-baojianpin-shi-zenme-huoqilai-de-1015773861]] describes students and migrants buying in bulk to carry home or resell online.
- Category fit: [[vol-275-aozhou-baojianpin-shi-zenme-huoqilai-de-1015773861]] pairs supplements with infant formula as early overseas-purchasing staples with substantial value and repeat frequency.
- Gray-to-platform transition: [[vol-275-aozhou-baojianpin-shi-zenme-huoqilai-de-1015773861]] notes that daigou stopped being a relationship-based grey activity once large platforms ran purchasing themselves.
- Awareness legacy: [[vol-275-aozhou-baojianpin-shi-zenme-huoqilai-de-1015773861]] treats daigou as the step that introduced the category and its consumer impression before platform-scale distribution arrived.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The tenfold volume claim is anecdotal, and the episode gives no sales totals, channel split, or timeline, so daigou should be read as a demand signal rather than a measured market. The source also does not address the channel's compliance, tax, authenticity, or consumer-protection problems, and its account of platform substitution is one brand's perspective. The pattern's applicability to other categories should be checked case by case, since daigou economics depend on price gaps, trust in origin markets, and product portability.

## What Changed
- Created the informal-channel concept that precedes formalised cross-border retail in the supplement story.
