# Movement Participation Threshold

## Definition

A movement participation threshold is a descriptive association between the share of a population actively mobilized in a campaign and the observed likelihood of campaign success.

## Current Synthesis

The episode reports that none of 323 revolutionary or maximalist campaigns in one analysis appeared to fail after actively mobilizing roughly 3.5 percent of the population. The claim concerns active participants rather than passive approval and can represent millions of people in a large country.

The threshold is best treated as a memorable empirical pattern, not a mechanical victory condition. Campaigns capable of reaching it may already possess much broader social support, institutional access, tactical competence, or favorable conditions. Measurement and coding also matter, and campaigns below the threshold may succeed.

## Key Claims

- Active participation is narrower and more demanding than sympathy or survey support.
- A small population percentage can still require enormous absolute mobilization.
- Threshold attainment may proxy for coalition breadth and institutional penetration.
- The reported 3.5 percent pattern applies to a bounded set of maximalist campaigns.
- The pattern is a rule of thumb rather than an iron law or organizing prescription.

- [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-5212bd1a2369bdd5087bc9f33d3a033a-49704134-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-5212bd1a2369bdd5087bc9f33d3a033a-49704134]] records Chenoweth's warning that the threshold may reflect much wider underlying support and should not be treated as universal law.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source does not provide the dataset, uncertainty interval, denominator rules, participation measurement, missing-data treatment, or later recoding. The pattern does not guarantee success, prescribe unsafe mobilization, or apply automatically to reform campaigns, elections, decentralized movements, or durable post-victory governance.
