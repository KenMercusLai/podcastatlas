# Conservative Leadership Election 1975

## Definition
Conservative Leadership Election 1975 refers to the February 1975 contest in which [[MargaretThatcher|Margaret Thatcher]] defeated [[EdwardHeath|Ted Heath]] and then [[WillieWhitelaw]], becoming leader of [[ConservativePartyUK|the Conservative Party]].

## Current Synthesis
The two sources treat the election as a contingent response to cumulative failure with structural meaning. [[420-britain-in-1974-thatcher-enters-the-ring-part-4-glt2799978515]] supplies the immediate prehistory: Heath lost the [[October1974UKGeneralElection|October election]] but would not resign, and [[KeithJoseph]] withdrew after a damaging Birmingham speech, prompting Thatcher to stand. [[662-britain-in-the-70s-the-rise-of-thatcher-part-1-glt1785737730]] explains how [[AireyNeave]] converted anti-Heath sentiment into first-ballot votes and how Thatcher's eventual victory signalled movement toward a more combative, middle-class, free-market Conservatism.

## Key Claims
- Heath's losses in February and October 1974 made a leadership challenge increasingly difficult to avoid.
- Heath's refusal to resign turned electoral weakness into an internal party contest.
- Joseph's withdrawal mattered because he had been the expected ideological challenger.
- Thatcher's initially unlikely candidacy could collect protest votes before becoming a credible bid.
- Neave's parliamentary tactics converted diffuse dissatisfaction into a first-ballot defeat for Heath.
- The result revealed a party-class and ideological shift as well as a personal succession.

## Evidence
- Electoral prehistory: [[420-britain-in-1974-thatcher-enters-the-ring-part-4-glt2799978515]] says Heath lost three of four elections, including both in 1974, and still refused to resign.
- Joseph contingency: both [[420-britain-in-1974-thatcher-enters-the-ring-part-4-glt2799978515]] and [[662-britain-in-the-70s-the-rise-of-thatcher-part-1-glt1785737730]] connect Joseph's Birmingham speech and withdrawal to Thatcher's decision to stand.
- Campaign tactics: [[662-britain-in-the-70s-the-rise-of-thatcher-part-1-glt1785737730]] credits Neave's teas and vote-lending strategy with helping Thatcher's first-ballot surge.
- Ballot results: [[662-britain-in-the-70s-the-rise-of-thatcher-part-1-glt1785737730]] reports Thatcher beating Heath 130 to 119 on 4 February and Whitelaw 146 to 79 on 11 February.
- Structural reading: [[662-britain-in-the-70s-the-rise-of-thatcher-part-1-glt1785737730]] says Thatcher's support skewed more southern, grammar-school, middle-class, and right-wing than Heath's.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources resist inevitability. Different election results, timing, or a different Joseph speech might have produced another contest, and Thatcher's early leadership was weak rather than immediately dominant. The party-realignment interpretation is episode-level synthesis rather than a complete study of MP motives.

## What Changed
- Added the October 1974 defeat and Joseph's November withdrawal as the direct prehistory of the contest.
- Clarified the sequence from electoral failure to leadership opening to party realignment.
