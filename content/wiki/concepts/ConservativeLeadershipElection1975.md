# Conservative Leadership Election 1975

## Definition
Conservative Leadership Election 1975 refers to the February 1975 contest in which [[MargaretThatcher|Margaret Thatcher]] defeated [[EdwardHeath|Ted Heath]] and then [[WillieWhitelaw]], becoming leader of [[ConservativePartyUK|the Conservative Party]].

## Current Synthesis
[[662-britain-in-the-70s-the-rise-of-thatcher-part-1-glt1785737730]] treats the election as both accident and structural signal. Thatcher could run because [[KeithJoseph]] had damaged himself and Heath had lost two 1974 elections, and she could win because [[AireyNeave]] turned resentment into first-ballot votes. Yet the result also exposed deeper movement inside the party: MPs were increasingly receptive to a more combative, southern, grammar-school, middle-class, free-market Conservatism.

## Key Claims
- The contest opened because Heath was weakened but unwilling to leave after two 1974 defeats.
- Joseph's collapse mattered because he had been the expected ideological challenger.
- Thatcher's candidacy initially looked hopeless, which made her useful as a protest vehicle.
- Neave's parliamentary tactics helped convert diffuse discontent into the first-ballot shock.
- The Whitelaw second ballot showed that the establishment alternative could not restore Heathite control.
- The outcome was a party realignment signal as well as a personal victory.

## Evidence
- Heath weakness: [[662-britain-in-the-70s-the-rise-of-thatcher-part-1-glt1785737730]] says Heath lost two 1974 elections but resisted resigning.
- Joseph contingency: [[662-britain-in-the-70s-the-rise-of-thatcher-part-1-glt1785737730]] says Joseph's Birmingham speech took him out of contention.
- Campaign tactics: [[662-britain-in-the-70s-the-rise-of-thatcher-part-1-glt1785737730]] credits Neave's teas and vote-lending strategy with helping Thatcher's first-ballot surge.
- Ballot results: [[662-britain-in-the-70s-the-rise-of-thatcher-part-1-glt1785737730]] reports Thatcher beating Heath 130 to 119 on 4 February and beating Whitelaw 146 to 79 on 11 February.
- Structural reading: [[662-britain-in-the-70s-the-rise-of-thatcher-part-1-glt1785737730]] says Thatcher's supporters skewed more southern, grammar-school, middle-class, and right-wing than Heath's.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode resists a destiny narrative. Different timing or a different Joseph speech might have changed the contest, and Thatcher's early leadership was weak rather than immediately dominant.
