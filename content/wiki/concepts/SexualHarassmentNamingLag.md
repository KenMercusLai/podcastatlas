# Sexual Harassment Naming Lag

## Definition
Sexual harassment naming lag is the interval in which workers recognize unwanted sexualized workplace conduct as harmful, but the shared vocabulary for naming, reporting, or legally framing it is still weak or not yet widely used.

## Current Synthesis
The episode grounds the concept through [[NineToFiveFilm|9 to 5]]. The film depicts harassment and boss abuse, but the phrase "sexual harassment" is absent because the episode says the term was not yet common in that workplace context. The absence matters: harm can be visible in story before it is stable in policy vocabulary.

The concept links language to organizing. [[NineToFiveOrganization|9to5]] could collect humiliating boss behavior and make it public through humor, contests, television, and film; those forms created recognition even when formal terminology lagged. Once the vocabulary strengthens, the same behavior can be connected more directly to rights, policy, and workplace accountability.

## Key Claims
- Workplace harms can be socially recognizable before they are named in common policy language.
- Missing vocabulary raises the cost of converting individual discomfort into formal complaints.
- Comedy, storytelling, and public contests can surface patterns while legal or HR categories are still forming.
- Naming matters because it changes whether behavior is treated as personal awkwardness, boss eccentricity, discrimination, or abuse.
- Naming is not sufficient by itself; workers still need enforcement, bargaining power, and institutional protection.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The page describes a naming dynamic from one episode and should not be read as a complete history of the term, its legal development, or every worker's experience. The episode's evidence is strongest for how a 1980 film and the 9to5 movement made recognizable behavior public before the phrase was normalized.
