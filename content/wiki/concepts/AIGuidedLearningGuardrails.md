# AI Guided Learning Guardrails / AI引导式学习护栏

## Definition
AI guided learning guardrails are product and classroom constraints that preserve a learner's first attempt, reasoning process, and feedback loop before AI supplies hints, explanations, or answers.

## Current Synthesis
The source set distinguishes answer substitution from guided support. Students should first articulate their thinking, then receive hints, stepwise prompts, or feedback that returns them to the reasoning loop. Product and school-system rules can protect that process, but the newest evaluation adds an enforcement gap: a study mode may claim to guide while remaining easy to override into direct answer delivery. Effective guardrails therefore require outcome testing, not only product labels or stated behavior.

## Key Claims
- Guardrails should protect the learner's first attempt before explanations, hints, or final answers.
- A useful AI tutor diagnoses stuck points and returns students to reasoning rather than completing the task for them.
- Direct answer access can improve practice completion while weakening transfer if students do not internalize the steps.
- Product-level guardrails are fragile when competing chatbots can win users by removing learning friction.
- A guided-study label is not evidence of preserved learning if ordinary prompting can recover direct answers.
- Institution-level rules can preserve classroom responsibility around student work and grading, but bans alone do not create AI literacy.

## Evidence
- First-attempt evidence: [[yong-ai-rang-women-bianbenle-ma-s10e25-a6b360da-07f7-4d9e-ab1b-64a7130e9254]] presents the good tutoring pattern as asking students to state their thinking before AI gives hints or stepwise guidance.
- Transfer evidence: [[yong-ai-rang-women-bianbenle-ma-s10e25-a6b360da-07f7-4d9e-ab1b-64a7130e9254]] contrasts direct AI access with guardrailed tutoring in a math-learning experiment, where practice gains did not necessarily translate to stronger closed-book performance.
- Product-competition evidence: [[tech-20260821-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260821-mp-tech-pod-128]] says OpenAI's teen ChatGPT mode tries to avoid simply giving answers, while Paresh Dave warns students may move to more permissive chatbots.
- Product-enforcement evidence: [[tech-20261105-1105-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261105-1105-mp-tech-pod-128]] reports that evaluators could easily override the teen study mode and often obtain answers directly.
- School-policy evidence: [[tech-20260904-0904-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260904-0904-mp-tech-pod-128]] says New York City public schools announced restrictions on AI and some screens through middle school, including no AI grading and no companion chatbots.
- Literacy evidence: [[tech-20260904-0904-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260904-0904-mp-tech-pod-128]] records the concern that students still need AI education even when classroom use is restricted.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Guardrails and bans can backfire if they only remove tools while leaving students unprepared for AI use outside school. The sources do not prove that any single product or district policy can maintain boundaries against home-device access, peer workarounds, or competing tools that provide direct answers. The newest result is an attributed independent assessment disputed by OpenAI, and the supplied source does not expose its complete prompt corpus or methodology.

## What Changed
- Added enforcement reliability as a distinct requirement beyond announcing a guided-study mode.
- Added independent findings that the evaluated teen study safeguards were easy to override.
- Preserved the methodological dispute and the need for AI literacy alongside restrictions.
