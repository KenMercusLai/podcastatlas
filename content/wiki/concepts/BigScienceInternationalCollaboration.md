# Big Science International Collaboration / 大科学国际合作

Big science international collaboration is the source's claim that contemporary large-scale physics is often more open and cross-border than older war-driven science-mobilization examples. In [[ep248-zai-gaoyuan-yu-dixia-tanxun-zhongguo-da-kexue-xiangmu-lidn7xxkcw4-66fw-y50fqqacwo]], [[MiaoQian|苗千]] says several China-led projects include substantial foreign scientific participation and operate through competition and cooperation together.

The concept does not deny national capacity or strategic pride. It argues that in basic physics, the value of discovering a neutrino property or a cosmological signal is hard to privatize as a secret advantage, because the knowledge matters to the shared scientific picture of nature. This makes [[BigScienceInfrastructure|大科学基础设施]] different from purely military or commercial projects.

The source's examples include mutual problem-solving among gravitational-wave observatories and experience exchange around liquid-scintillator neutrino detectors. It also keeps a boundary: some space-related projects may require review before public discussion when they have not launched or have technical sensitivities.

## Key Claims
- Contemporary big science often mixes national leadership, international participation, competition, and shared verification.
- Openness is more plausible when the object is fundamental knowledge rather than directly deployable military capability.
- Collaboration does not remove rivalry; it changes the rivalry into priority, method, instrument, and interpretation competition within a shared evidence regime.
- Public reporting must still distinguish open basic-science projects from projects with launch, space, or security review constraints.
