# Urban Field Interviewing / 城市田野访谈

## Definition
Urban field interviewing is a qualitative method that asks residents about concrete choices—work, housing, children, education, hukou, and staying or leaving—to connect lived decisions with a city's industrial, demographic, and institutional conditions.

## Current Synthesis
In [[chengshi-jiushi-zheyang-22-zhongguo-de-zhongweishu-chengshi-shi-nazuo-1021674577|城市就是这样22]], [[DavidFishman|David Fishman]] uses cities as sampling frames for people. Data and rankings choose a place; conversations with taxi drivers, shop workers, owners, and tourism employees then test what the city-level category conceals. The method is strongest when questions begin with observable decisions and respondents' own tradeoffs, and when the researcher states that a handful of conversations can reveal mechanisms or counterexamples without representing all residents.

## Key Claims
- Concrete behavioral questions are more informative than asking respondents to summarize an entire city or country.
- Housing connects income, costs, family strategy, and expectations about local value.
- Questions about children and education reveal judgments about the city's future and mobility options.
- Staying-versus-leaving decisions connect individual preference to local jobs, family care, wages, and migration systems.
- Interview evidence should correct or complicate quantitative categories without being promoted into population estimates.

## Evidence
- Linfen interviews: [[chengshi-jiushi-zheyang-22-zhongguo-de-zhongweishu-chengshi-shi-nazuo-1021674577|城市就是这样22]] reports roughly eight to ten conversations that exposed differences between the old urban core and outer localities.
- Zixing mechanism: [[chengshi-jiushi-zheyang-22-zhongguo-de-zhongweishu-chengshi-shi-nazuo-1021674577|城市就是这样22]] uses one tourism worker's preference for lower-paid local work near parents over factory migration to show how an industry can create a viable option to stay.
- Shishi specialization: [[chengshi-jiushi-zheyang-22-zhongguo-de-zhongweishu-chengshi-shi-nazuo-1021674577|城市就是这样22]] uses a noodle-shop conversation linking the worker, relatives, and acquaintances to clothing work to make the city's industrial concentration legible through a household network.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Convenience interviews favor people who are accessible and willing to speak; a foreign interviewer who attracts curiosity changes the encounter itself. Respondents' accounts may not match administrative or economic data, and striking stories may receive disproportionate narrative weight. The method should therefore generate bounded explanations and follow-up questions, not claims about how all residents think.

## What Changed
- Established choice-centered interviewing as the qualitative half of the episode's mixed city-research method.
- Added housing, children, and mobility decisions as bridges between household experience and urban structure.
- Made sample limits and observer effects explicit rather than treating access as representativeness.
