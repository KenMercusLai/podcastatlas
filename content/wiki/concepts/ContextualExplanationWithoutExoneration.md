# Contextual Explanation Without Exoneration

## Definition

Contextual explanation without exoneration is a historical method that reconstructs the norms, institutions, incentives, beliefs, and constraints shaping an actor while preserving agency, contemporary dissent, and moral accountability.

## Current Synthesis

[[309-columbus-villain-or-hero-part-4-glt6751575003]] applies the method to [[ChristopherColumbus]]. A violent, slaveholding, crusading, commercially competitive Mediterranean and Atlantic world helps explain his religious imagination, status ambitions, coerced labor practices, and willingness to enslave. That context is causally important, but it is not an acquittal: [[BartolomeDeLasCasas]] and other critics show that opposition existed within the wider historical world, while the episode's accounts of pillage, forced removal, and family separation preserve the harms being explained.

The method also separates capability from virtue. Columbus's navigation can be historically impressive without functioning as moral compensation for failed government and coercion. This differs from [[HeroVillainHistoricalReduction]] because it does not require one label to absorb every dimension of a life.

## Key Claims

- Historical context explains available beliefs and incentives but does not mechanically determine action.
- Contemporary dissent is evidence against treating harmful conduct as morally invisible in its own period.
- Achievement in one domain does not cancel responsibility in another.
- Intent, mechanism, and consequence should be distinguished rather than fused into one accusation or defense.
- Moral judgment becomes stronger when it follows accurate causal reconstruction rather than replacing it.

## Evidence

- Formative world: [[309-columbus-villain-or-hero-part-4-glt6751575003]] situates Columbus within slavery, crusading Christianity, dynastic competition, maritime trade, and imperial rivalry.
- Contemporary dissent: [[309-columbus-villain-or-hero-part-4-glt6751575003]] uses Las Casas and royal opposition to some enslavement as evidence that criticism existed within the period.
- Capability and culpability: [[309-columbus-villain-or-hero-part-4-glt6751575003]] credits seamanship and courage while condemning pillage, enslavement, coercion, and misrule.
- Intent and consequence: [[309-columbus-villain-or-hero-part-4-glt6751575003]] distinguishes a disputed genocide-intent claim from catastrophic Indigenous outcomes produced by disease, forced labor, hunger, violence, and social destruction.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Context can become excuse if background conditions are allowed to erase choices, victims, or dissent. Conversely, present judgment can become causally thin if it treats historical actors as detached from institutions and available concepts. The source itself is a modern popular-history interpretation and does not independently settle the genocide classification, Columbus's private motives, or the precise causal share of each mechanism in Indigenous population collapse.

## What Changed

- Established a reusable distinction between historical explanation and moral acquittal.
- Added contemporary dissent as a key test of claims about period norms.
- Separated cross-domain achievement from moral compensation.
