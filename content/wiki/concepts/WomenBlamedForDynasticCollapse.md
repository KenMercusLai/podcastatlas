# 女性亡国归咎叙事 / Women Blamed for Dynastic Collapse

## Definition
女性亡国归咎叙事 / women blamed for dynastic collapse is the retrospective pattern that condenses ruler failure, court disorder, and regime collapse into the moral image of a dangerous woman.

## Current Synthesis
[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-601-2-fenghuo-xi-zhuhou-yixiao-tianxia-wang-baosi-shi-hefang-yaonie-lkirbrvonyiyaousoblcd|Hanji 601-2]] introduces the pattern through [[BaoSi|褒姒]] and the so-called "四大妖姬" grouping of Mei Xi, Da Ji, Bao Si, and Li Ji. The episode repeats the traditional vocabulary because it is central to Bao Si's reception, but its closing caution resists turning the label into a complete historical explanation. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-536-4-jiemi-zhaofeiyan-jiemei-wuming-yiyun-lo557jbq9ni-xissplybtcuraelm|Hanji 536-4]] adds [[ZhaoFeiyanWesternHan|赵飞燕]], [[ZhaoHedeWesternHan|赵合德]], and [[XiaJiSpringAutumn|夏姬]] as cases where sexualized female reputation can absorb responsibility for ruler desire, ministerial behavior, and later writerly condemnation.

The useful wiki claim is not that these women caused dynastic collapse. It is that later political memory often makes a vivid female figure carry the explanatory and emotional weight of a broader institutional failure.

## Key Claims
- The pattern compresses complex political collapse into a memorable moralized person-story.
- Bao Si's "一笑亡国" memory shows how a single image can become more durable than the underlying institutional sequence.
- The "妖姬," "荡妇," and "红颜祸水" labels are reception artifacts and need separation from evidence of actual agency.
- Xia Ji and the Zhao sisters show that blame can fall on women even when the source narrative itself contains consequential male choices and political incentives.
- Source-critical qualification is part of the concept because the episode itself marks Bao Si's supernatural biography as myth-like.

## Evidence
Compression into person-story:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-601-2-fenghuo-xi-zhuhou-yixiao-tianxia-wang-baosi-shi-hefang-yaonie-lkirbrvonyiyaousoblcd]] opens with the "倾国倾城" image and uses Bao Si's smile as the famous shorthand for Western Zhou collapse.

Durable image:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-601-2-fenghuo-xi-zhuhou-yixiao-tianxia-wang-baosi-shi-hefang-yaonie-lkirbrvonyiyaousoblcd]] says the beacon-fire story binds Bao Si to Zhou Youwang's loss of vassal trust and the final attack.

Reception artifact:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-601-2-fenghuo-xi-zhuhou-yixiao-tianxia-wang-baosi-shi-hefang-yaonie-lkirbrvonyiyaousoblcd]] groups Bao Si with three other "妖姬" figures, then concludes that Bao Si may be innocent or guilty, pitiable or hateful, and remains historically obscure.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-536-4-jiemi-zhaofeiyan-jiemei-wuming-yiyun-lo557jbq9ni-xissplybtcuraelm]] groups Zhao Feiyan, Zhao Hede, Mei Xi, Da Ji, Bao Si, and Xia Ji as examples of how later memory moralizes women near rulers.

Male-agency displacement:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-536-4-jiemi-zhaofeiyan-jiemei-wuming-yiyun-lo557jbq9ni-xissplybtcuraelm]] uses Xia Ji to argue that Chen Ling Gong, his ministers, Hei Yao, and Wu Chen made choices, while later reputation turned Xia Ji into the memorable scandal center.

Source-critical qualification:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-601-2-fenghuo-xi-zhuhou-yixiao-tianxia-wang-baosi-shi-hefang-yaonie-lkirbrvonyiyaousoblcd]] explicitly warns that the dragon-saliva birth narrative is closer to myth than reliable history.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept is grounded in short popular-history episodes and should not be generalized as a universal rule without more source work. The Bao Si episode preserves the traditional label but does not independently test alternative historical accounts of Western Zhou's fall. Hanji 536-4 broadens the pattern but likewise works as interpretive commentary rather than full textual criticism of every case it invokes.

## What Changed
- Broadened the concept from Bao Si's "妖姬" and "一笑亡国" reception to include Zhao-sister and Xia Ji reputation-making.
