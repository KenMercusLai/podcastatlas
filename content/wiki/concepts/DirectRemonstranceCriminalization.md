## Definition

Direct remonstrance criminalization / 直谏入罪 is the court-politics pattern in which open corrective speech is recoded as disrespect, deception, factional pressure, resentment, slander, or disloyalty, then punished through execution, prison death, forced suicide, dismissal, fief reduction, family pressure, or sanctions against defenders.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 850 defines the Huan-era ruler-facing branch through [[LiYunLateHan|Li Yun]] and [[DuZhongLateHan|Du Zhong]]. Li Yun's unsealed memorial criticizes overreward and then challenges [[EmperorHuanOfHan|Emperor Huan]]'s willingness to hear truth. Huan answers by arresting and interrogating him, then widening punishment to Du Zhong's shared-fate memorial, [[ChenFanLateHan|Chen Fan]]'s clemency plea, other petitioners, and [[GuanBaLateHan|Guan Ba]]'s final mitigation attempt. The result is a lesson to the court that both loyal criticism and rescue speech can become offenses.

Hanji 800 supplies the earlier Emperor An death edge. [[ZhaoTengLateHan|Zhao Teng]] submits blunt criticism of court gains and losses; [[EmperorAnOfHan|Emperor An]] treats it as grave disloyalty and executes him despite [[YangZhenLateHan|Yang Zhen]]'s mitigation plea. Yang's own disaster-remonstrance and evidence gathering against [[FanFengEunuchLateHan|Fan Feng]], [[ZhouGuangLateHan|Zhou Guang]], and [[XieYunLateHan|Xie Yun]] are later recoded as resentment after Fan Feng's side uses [[GengBaoGrandGeneralLateHan|Geng Bao]] as formal accuser, leading to Yang's seal removal, forced return, suicide, and funeral humiliation.

Hanji 801 gives a less lethal but still coercive An-era variant. [[LaiLiLateHan|Lai Li]], [[HuanYanLateHan|Huan Yan]], [[ZhangHaoRemonstratorLateHan|Zhang Hao]], [[ShiYanLateHan|Shi Yan]], [[ZhuChangLateHan|Zhu Chang]], and others defend young [[EmperorShunOfHan|Liu Bao]] against deposition at Hongdu Gate. An's edict recodes the remonstrance as noisy hidden motive and threatens punishment; Lai's persistence leads to dismissal for him and his brothers, fief-revenue reduction, and pressure on his mother.

Hanji 761 adds a favored-insider branch before those ruler-facing cases. [[ZhiShouEasternHan|Zhi Shou]] refuses [[DouXianEasternHan|Dou Xian]]'s private request, publishes the letter, imprisons the messenger, invokes [[WangMang|Wang Mang]], and attacks Dou's Northern Xiongnu expedition and mansion-building. Dou converts that speech into a public-land and slander accusation, and [[EmpressDowagerDouHeLateHan|Empress Dowager Dou]]'s favoritism lets it become a death sentence. [[HeChangEasternHan|He Chang]] mitigates death to exile by arguing that correction is Zhi's duty, but Zhi's suicide shows that criminalized remonstrance can remain lethal after formal mitigation.

## Key Claims

- Direct remonstrance becomes most dangerous when it forces a ruler or favored insider to absorb criticism in public.
- Public form, documentary evidence, or open distribution can amplify risk by making private pressure or policy failure hard to hide.
- A captured court can recode corrective speech as resentment, deception, slander, faction, or disloyalty when the target is powerful.
- Criminalization can widen from the original speaker to solidarity actors, defenders, clemency advocates, relatives, and funeral memory.
- Mitigation can reduce formal sentence while failing to restore safety when the political threat remains.
- The pattern ranges from execution and prison death to forced suicide, dismissal, fief reduction, and family pressure.
- The outcome strengthens feedback collapse by teaching officials that truthful correction can become punishable.

## Evidence

- Ruler-focused criticism and public form: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-850-huandi-weihe-zhiyi-sha-zhongchen-lokxtceq7vv-giec6iufwwk9fwk7|Hanji 850]] says Li Yun criticizes overlarge rewards, questions Huan's truth-hearing capacity, submits an unsealed memorial, and sends copies to the Three Excellencies.
- Solidarity and failed rescue: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-850-huandi-weihe-zhiyi-sha-zhongchen-lokxtceq7vv-giec6iufwwk9fwk7|Hanji 850]] says Du Zhong asks to share Li Yun's fate, Chen Fan and other officials plead for clemency, Guan Ba seeks mitigation, and Li Yun plus Du Zhong still die in prison.
- Zhao Teng's execution: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-800-yangzhen-zhisi-yi-chang-jianchen-dui-qingguan-de-jiaosha-lk9qcfs4xl-rjd-zpwdtsblybxa8|Hanji 800]] says Zhao Teng criticizes court gains and losses, is treated as deceiving the ruler and committing grave disloyalty, and is executed despite Yang Zhen's plea.
- Yang Zhen recoded as resentful critic: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-800-yangzhen-zhisi-yi-chang-jianchen-dui-qingguan-de-jiaosha-lk9qcfs4xl-rjd-zpwdtsblybxa8|Hanji 800]] says Yang investigates forged orders by Fan Feng's group, then is accused through omen and resentment language, stripped of the taiwei seal, sent home, and driven to suicide.
- Hongdu Gate sanctions: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-801-han-andi-weishenme-henxin-fei-taizi-lgnhbsqnp6vdigsmqfkxiqmx2vdy|Hanji 801]] says Emperor An threatens Liu Bao's defenders and later punishes Lai Li through office removal, fief reduction, and family pressure.
- Partial tolerance contrast: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-852-dang-meinv-ruyun-shi-fu-haishi-huo-luml30ytbc-awpvk-7-23ehzbqpx|Hanji 852]] shows that blunt correction is not automatically criminalized when Huan partially accepts Chen Fan's harem-cost warning and initially tolerates Yuan Hong's middle-ruler diagnosis.
- Punished but not killed contrast: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-873-ta-ruhe-liyong-tianxiang-tongma-diwang-lqebkodcbcrsvyf80qwxnobydbhc|Hanji 873]] shows Xiang Kai punished after religious and omen-coded criticism, qualifying the pattern's range of penalty.
- Zhi Shou's documentary refusal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-761-cong-sixingfan-dao-shachang-yingxiong-ta-shi-ruhe-zuodao-de-lmcllzgpj19dstnzfztstrkawuex|Hanji 761]] says Zhi refuses Dou Xian's private letter, publicizes it at court, imprisons the messenger, and repeatedly attacks Dou's policy and luxury.
- Slander conversion and mitigation limit: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-761-cong-sixingfan-dao-shachang-yingxiong-ta-shi-ruhe-zuodao-de-lmcllzgpj19dstnzfztstrkawuex|Hanji 761]] says Dou accuses Zhi of public-land misconduct and slander, He Chang changes death to exile, and Zhi kills himself before departure.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Directness is a risk amplifier under certain authority, face, evidence, and faction conditions, not a sufficient cause by itself. Hanji 852 and Hanji 873 show that some blunt or religiously coded criticism can be partly accepted or punished short of death. Hanji 761 also shows partial institutional resilience: He Chang's argument changes the formal sentence. The concept should therefore track how speech is recoded and punished, not assume that all direct advice inevitably dies.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 761 as the favored-insider branch where Zhi Shou's public refusal of Dou Xian's private request becomes a slander case.
- Expanded the pattern from ruler-only face threat to powerful-insider protection under dowager favor.
- Added mitigation failure as a subtype: formal death reduction still leaves Zhi Shou politically unsafe.
- Preserved Hanji 852 and Hanji 873 as penalty-range qualifications.
