## Definition

祥瑞怀疑式进谏 / omen skepticism remonstrance is the court-speech pattern where an official warns that unusual signs praised as auspicious may also signal misgovernment or danger and should not be converted automatically into legitimacy celebration.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 757 creates the concept through [[HeChangEasternHan|He Chang]] at the end of Zhangdi's reign. Zhangdi changes the era name to Zhanghe because auspicious signs have appeared repeatedly around the capital and the realm. Many people treat the volume of signs as a good political event.

He Chang refuses the automatic reading. He argues that auspicious signs follow virtue while disasters arise from bad governance, so strange birds entering palace halls and unusual grasses growing in palace courtyards should make the court cautious. [[SongYouEasternHan|Song You]] and [[YuanAnEasternHan|Yuan An]] fear the implication and do not respond, showing that skepticism toward favorable signs can itself be politically risky.

## Key Claims

- Auspicious-sign reporting can become a legitimacy celebration before officials have tested its meaning.
- Omen skepticism does not deny symbolic politics; it asks whether the sign points to virtue, disorder, or a need for caution.
- Palace-centered anomalies are especially sensitive because they touch the ruler's virtue and court condition directly.
- Remonstrance through omen interpretation can be risky even when it uses accepted moral-cosmological language.
- Silence by senior officials can be part of the political effect when favorable-omen narratives become hard to challenge.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept is bounded to Hanji 757 and should not be used as a universal anti-omen claim. The source does not adjudicate whether the reported signs were fabricated, natural, or symbolically meaningful. It only records the political move of refusing automatic praise and asking the court to read anomalies cautiously.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Hanji 757's He Chang warning about Zhangdi-era auspicious signs.
