# Exercise Fat-Oxidation Sequencing

## Definition
Exercise fat-oxidation sequencing is the episode's proposal that session duration, fed or fasted state, and ordering intense work before lower-intensity cardio can change when exercise shifts from glycogen toward greater use of stored fat.

## Current Synthesis
The source distinguishes sprint intervals, high-intensity intervals, and moderate continuous exercise, then argues that fed versus fasted status matters little for shorter sessions but may affect fat oxidation after roughly ninety minutes of moderate work. It proposes that twenty to sixty minutes of intense exercise before zone-two cardio can move this substrate-use transition earlier, especially while fasted, through glycogen use, lower insulin, and greater epinephrine release.

This is a substrate-selection hypothesis, not a general training hierarchy. The episode explicitly frames it for fat loss rather than peak performance. The practical judgment must still account for session quality, total energy balance, recovery, glucose regulation, medication, heat, hydration, training history, and whether fasting makes the person less able to train safely or consistently.

## Key Claims
- Sprint intervals, high-intensity intervals, and moderate continuous exercise differ in intensity and work-rest structure.
- Fed versus fasted status is presented as having little fat-oxidation effect during shorter sessions.
- The source proposes a later divergence during prolonged moderate exercise, with more fat oxidation after roughly ninety minutes when fasted.
- Intense work before zone-two cardio is proposed to bring the shift toward stored fuel earlier.
- Acute fat oxidation, athletic performance, and long-term fat loss are separate outcomes.

## Evidence
- Exercise categories - [[essentials-lose-fat-with-science-based-tools-scim9321862300]] distinguishes sprint intervals above 100% of VO2 max, HIIT at roughly 80-100%, and moderate continuous work at lower percentages.
- Fasted-duration claim - [[essentials-lose-fat-with-science-based-tools-scim9321862300]] says pre-exercise feeding matters little for shorter work but describes a divergence around ninety minutes of moderate exercise.
- Sequencing claim - [[essentials-lose-fat-with-science-based-tools-scim9321862300]] proposes twenty to sixty minutes of weights, sprints, kettlebell work, squats, deadlifts, or other intense exercise before zone-two cardio, particularly while fasted.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode summary does not provide the underlying trials, effect sizes, participant training status, sex distribution, dietary control, or evidence that the acute substrate shift improves long-term fat loss. Ninety minutes is not a universal metabolic switch. Fasted or prolonged training can be inappropriate for people with diabetes, hypoglycemia risk, pregnancy, eating disorders, relevant medications, illness, or poor recovery, and intense-first sequencing may reduce endurance quality or increase injury risk for some trainees.

## What Changed
- Added a source-scoped hypothesis connecting fasted duration and exercise order to substrate use.
- Separated acute fat oxidation from performance and durable body-fat outcomes.
