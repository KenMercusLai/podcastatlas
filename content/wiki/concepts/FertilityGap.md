# Fertility Gap

## Definition
The fertility gap is the difference between the number of children people say they want and the number they ultimately have.

## Current Synthesis
[[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-58f17fc3725ef3102277ae962324f7dd-59875813-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-58f17fc3725ef3102277ae962324f7dd-59875813]] uses the gap to move fertility policy away from a universal duty to reproduce or a fixed replacement-rate target. The relevant welfare question is whether people who want children can realize those intentions.

The episode's U.S. account joins biological timing to social and material timing. Later marriage, unstable early-career earnings, reduced in-person socializing, intensive parenting expectations, inflexible work, and delayed access to suitable housing may all narrow the feasible path from preference to birth. Stated ideals remain near two children in the source's account even as marriage and parenthood are delayed or forgone.

## Key Claims
- Desired and completed family size are distinct demographic measures.
- A preference-realization frame can support families without treating non-parenthood as failure.
- Delay can become non-recoverable because biological and relationship timing impose limits.
- The gap is multi-causal and should not be reduced to income or a single policy lever.
- Emotional costs of unrealized goals and fiscal effects of population aging are separate reasons to study the gap.

## Evidence
- Definition and voluntary boundary: [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-58f17fc3725ef3102277ae962324f7dd-59875813-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-58f17fc3725ef3102277ae962324f7dd-59875813]] attributes the preference-realization framing to [[LymanStone]].
- Cohort timing: [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-58f17fc3725ef3102277ae962324f7dd-59875813-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-58f17fc3725ef3102277ae962324f7dd-59875813]] attributes to [[MelissaKearney]] the judgment that later births are unlikely to fully compensate for declining childbearing in the twenties and early thirties.
- Constraint bundle: [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-58f17fc3725ef3102277ae962324f7dd-59875813-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-58f17fc3725ef3102277ae962324f7dd-59875813]] connects the gap to marriage, work, parenting norms, socializing, infertility, and [[HousingAccessFamilyFormation|housing]].

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Stated preferences can change and survey answers do not prove that every observed difference is involuntary. The source does not establish the relative causal weight of each proposed constraint, and international desired-family-size comparisons remain disputed and source-scoped.
