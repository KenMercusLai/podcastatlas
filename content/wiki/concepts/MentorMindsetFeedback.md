# Mentor Mindset Feedback

## Definition
Mentor mindset feedback pairs high standards and candid criticism with explicit confidence in a person's capacity to improve and concrete support for meeting the standard.

## Current Synthesis
The mentor's dilemma is that honest criticism can improve performance while also being heard as rejection or a verdict on ability. [[DavidYeager]] presents a combined signal as the solution: the work does not yet meet the standard, the standard remains meaningful, and the recipient is believed capable of reaching it with feedback and support.

This stance differs from both protection and enforcement. A protector lowers demands to avoid discomfort; an enforcer preserves demands without supplying trust or help. Mentor mindset keeps productive strain while making the relationship and route to improvement credible.

## Key Claims
- Useful feedback communicates standards, present gaps, belief in potential, and a practical route forward together.
- Lowering standards to protect someone can remove a growth opportunity and signal low confidence.
- Maintaining standards without support can turn criticism into threat, shame, or disengagement.
- Trust built before difficult correction can make technical feedback easier to receive and use.
- The framework applies across teaching, parenting, coaching, management, and scientific critique.

## Evidence
- Mentor's dilemma - [[how-to-master-growth-mindset-to-improve-performance-dr-david-yeager-scim6606381639]] frames criticism as simultaneously necessary for growth and capable of crushing motivation.
- Combined signal - [[how-to-master-growth-mindset-to-improve-performance-dr-david-yeager-scim6606381639]] describes high standards plus high support and confidence as the mentor-mindset response.
- Boundary cases - [[how-to-master-growth-mindset-to-improve-performance-dr-david-yeager-scim6606381639]] contrasts mentor mindset with protective standard-lowering and unsupported enforcement.
- Applied examples - [[how-to-master-growth-mindset-to-improve-performance-dr-david-yeager-scim6606381639]] uses coaching, workplace leadership, admissions, and scientific criticism to illustrate demanding support.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Supportive wording cannot make an unfair standard, unsafe relationship, biased evaluation, or unattainable demand legitimate. Trust and power differ across classrooms, families, labs, teams, and workplaces, so the same script may not be credible everywhere. The episode's examples illustrate a design principle rather than proving that one feedback formula produces uniform outcomes.

## What Changed
- Created the concept around the mentor's dilemma and the joint high-standards/high-support signal.
- Distinguished mentor mindset from both protective under-demanding and unsupported enforcement.
