# 江湖虚拟血缘 / Jianghu Virtual Kinship

## Definition
Jianghu virtual kinship is the pattern where unrelated people build group belonging and hierarchy by adopting family-like roles: sworn brothers, masters and disciples, godfathers and godchildren, in-laws, leaders, and followers.

## Current Synthesis
[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-483-3-lishi-jiemi-hun-zhichang-de-jianghu-guiju-lg6vr0avzvtqireys3hhp7trwrsl]] frames Jianghu not as pure violence but as social organization under weak individual security. Virtual kinship gives strangers a usable grammar for trust, obligation, rank, and protection, but the same grammar can hide dependency, private interest, opportunism, and coercive leader-follower relations.

The concept is therefore morally mixed. It borrows the warmth and legitimacy of family language, yet the episode stresses that Jianghu "小义" serves one's own circle rather than a universal public order. Brotherhood can generate support and identity; it can also make exit, dissent, and equal standing difficult.

## Key Claims
- Virtual kinship converts strangers into legible insiders by naming them brothers, disciples, godchildren, in-laws, leaders, or followers.
- Family language supplies emotional legitimacy for a non-family organization, especially when formal protection is weak.
- Jianghu "义" is narrower than public righteousness because it primarily protects one's own circle.
- The same structure can support belonging, mutual aid, patronage, opportunism, and coercion.
- Workplace readings of Jianghu rules often inherit this ambiguity: relationship fluency may help survival while also normalizing dependency.

## Evidence
### Kinship Roles As Organization
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-483-3-lishi-jiemi-hun-zhichang-de-jianghu-guiju-lg6vr0avzvtqireys3hhp7trwrsl]] names vertical master-father relations and horizontal same-sect brother relations as Jianghu's "虚拟血缘制."

### Narrow Righteousness
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-483-3-lishi-jiemi-hun-zhichang-de-jianghu-guiju-lg6vr0avzvtqireys3hhp7trwrsl]] distinguishes Confucian "大义" from Jianghu "小义," where obligation is directed toward one's own brothers and leader.

### Workplace Extension
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-483-3-lishi-jiemi-hun-zhichang-de-jianghu-guiju-lg6vr0avzvtqireys3hhp7trwrsl]] uses "混职场" framing to move the Jianghu grammar from historical/social circles into modern relationship navigation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode is thematic commentary rather than close source criticism, so the concept should not be treated as a complete history of Chinese kinship, guilds, gangs, or workplace networks. It also compresses Confucian, Mohist, Buddhist, Daoist, and folk sources into a broad cultural account without separating periods or institutional settings.

## What Changed
- Created the concept from Hanji 483-3 to preserve the episode's specific "virtual blood relation" account of Jianghu order.
