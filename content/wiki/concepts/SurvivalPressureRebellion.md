## Definition

[[SurvivalPressureRebellion|生存压力型起义 / survival pressure rebellion]] is the pattern where people turn to collective violence because official pressure, coercive labor, hunger, displacement, and blocked peaceful options make ordinary survival feel impossible.

## Current Synthesis

The concept now has two Western Han variants. The livelihood-collapse variant is visible in [[GuanghanZhengGongUprising|郑公's Guanghan uprising]], where the revolt is not presented as a bid to rule all under Heaven. Instead, the causal emphasis is "官逼民反": court elites fight for private interests, eunuchs and officials abuse power, burdens become unbearable, granaries are empty, people are hungry, and many die or wander on the roads.

The coercive-production variant is visible in the [[YingchuanIronOfficeUprising|颍川铁官徒起义]]. Here the pressure is not only tax or famine but forced labor inside a state production site: iron-office convicts and corvee laborers face harsh work, poor subsistence, punishment, and little freedom, then seize weapons and attack officials.

The concept therefore names a cause field rather than excusing every rebel action. Zheng Gong's force attacks offices, frees prisoners, and seizes weapons; Shentu Sheng's group kills officials after taking arsenal weapons. The violence is real. But the sources' interpretive center is that ordinary people are more likely to rebel when governance or production systems have made peaceful life unavailable.

## Key Claims

- Survival pressure can turn local grievance into armed collective action without a broad dynastic program.
- Official oppression and livelihood collapse are mutually reinforcing: abuse matters most when people already lack food, security, freedom, or tolerable work conditions.
- Coercive state production sites can become rebellion nodes when laborers have both unbearable pressure and access to weapons.
- Prison release, arsenal seizure, and official killing show that rebellion can rapidly convert desperation into organized coercive capacity.
- State suppression can defeat the rebel organization without proving that the underlying livelihood or labor causes were repaired.
- The concept should be used only where a source explicitly ties rebellion to subsistence collapse, coercive labor, or unbearable official pressure.

- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-532-xihan-zuida-de-nongmin-qiyi-lingdaozhe-shi-ta-lsjb4yu-36ef9lolcbml5ztypr_z|Hanji 532]] says Zheng Gong's followers attack government offices, free prisoners, and seize weapons.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-532-xihan-zuida-de-nongmin-qiyi-lingdaozhe-shi-ta-lsjb4yu-36ef9lolcbml5ztypr_z|Hanji 532]] says the force later reaches four counties and nearly ten thousand people before a large state campaign and amnesty split it.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept currently rests on two Western Han source notes and should not be generalized to every peasant uprising or every local revolt. Hanji 523 also complicates the concept by stressing that survival pressure may explain rebellion without supplying the organization, discipline, legitimacy, strategic vision, or civilian-governance capacity needed for success. Hanji 532 gives a moral and social interpretation, not a full economic dataset or rebel-side testimony. Comparable concepts such as [[FiscalExtractionRebellionSpiral|财政压榨叛乱螺旋]] and [[FrontierOfficialAbuseRebellionSpiral|边吏侵害引发反叛螺旋]] cover different mechanisms.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 523's iron-office coercive-labor variant, distinguishing state-production pressure from Hanji 532's broader livelihood-collapse uprising.
- Added a qualification that survival pressure explains rebellion formation but not successful rebel governance or durable military organization.
