## Definition

[[SurvivalPressureRebellion|生存压力型起义 / survival pressure rebellion]] is the pattern where people turn to collective violence because official pressure, hunger, displacement, and blocked peaceful options make ordinary survival feel impossible.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 532 defines the concept through [[GuanghanZhengGongUprising|郑公's Guanghan uprising]]. The source does not present the revolt as a bid to rule all under Heaven. Instead, it emphasizes "官逼民反": court elites fight for private interests, eunuchs and officials abuse power, burdens become unbearable, granaries are empty, people are hungry, and many die or wander on the roads.

The concept therefore names a cause field rather than excusing every rebel action. Zheng Gong's force attacks offices, frees prisoners, seizes weapons, and later expands into four counties; the violence is real. But the episode's interpretive center is that ordinary people usually prefer peaceful life, so armed action becomes legible when governance has made peaceful life unavailable.

## Key Claims

- Survival pressure can turn local grievance into armed collective action without a broad dynastic program.
- Official oppression and livelihood collapse are mutually reinforcing: abuse matters most when people already lack food and security.
- Prison release and arsenal seizure show that rebellion can rapidly convert desperation into organized coercive capacity.
- State suppression can defeat the rebel organization without proving that the underlying livelihood causes were repaired.
- The concept should be used only where a source explicitly ties rebellion to subsistence collapse or unbearable official pressure.

## Evidence

- Explicit causal frame: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-532-xihan-zuida-de-nongmin-qiyi-lingdaozhe-shi-ta-lsjb4yu-36ef9lolcbml5ztypr_z|Hanji 532]] says the Guanghan revolt should be understood as "官逼民反."
- Livelihood collapse: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-532-xihan-zuida-de-nongmin-qiyi-lingdaozhe-shi-ta-lsjb4yu-36ef9lolcbml5ztypr_z|Hanji 532]] mentions excessive burdens, empty granaries, hunger, displacement, and deaths in the tens of thousands.
- Violence and capacity: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-532-xihan-zuida-de-nongmin-qiyi-lingdaozhe-shi-ta-lsjb4yu-36ef9lolcbml5ztypr_z|Hanji 532]] says Zheng Gong's followers attack government offices, free prisoners, and seize weapons.
- Expansion and suppression: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-532-xihan-zuida-de-nongmin-qiyi-lingdaozhe-shi-ta-lsjb4yu-36ef9lolcbml5ztypr_z|Hanji 532]] says the force later reaches four counties and nearly ten thousand people before a large state campaign and amnesty split it.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept currently rests on one Western Han source note and should not be generalized to every peasant uprising or every local revolt. Hanji 532 gives a moral and social interpretation, not a full economic dataset or rebel-side testimony. It also does not show whether the court later corrected the burdens that helped produce the revolt. Comparable concepts such as [[FiscalExtractionRebellionSpiral|财政压榨叛乱螺旋]] and [[FrontierOfficialAbuseRebellionSpiral|边吏侵害引发反叛螺旋]] cover different mechanisms.

## What Changed

- Created this concept from Hanji 532 to capture the episode's "官逼民反" interpretation without folding it into later Xin fiscal extraction or frontier official-abuse patterns.
