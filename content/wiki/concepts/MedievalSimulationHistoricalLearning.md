# Medieval Simulation as Historical Learning / 中世纪模拟式历史学习

## Definition

Medieval simulation as historical learning is the use of interacting bodily, material, occupational, and social rules to help a player understand historical dependence through action and consequence rather than exposition alone.

## Current Synthesis

In the source's reading of [[KingdomComeDeliverance|《天国：拯救》]], historical learning comes from friction. Hunger, fatigue, hygiene, food decay, literacy, clothing, noise, reputation, crime, combat, and craft force the player to notice dependencies that scenic reconstruction can hide. NPC routines and local reputations make society part of the simulation rather than a static backdrop.

The learning becomes stronger when compared with place. Visits to Trosky Castle, Bohemian rock landscapes, [[KutnaHora|Kutná Hora]], churches, and mines let the player compare ruins and preserved evidence with a game world's selective reconstruction. Simulation is therefore an interpretive bridge, not a substitute for historical sources or material remains.

## Key Claims

- Rules and consequences can teach historical dependence more effectively than decorative period imagery alone.
- Bodily maintenance makes survival and time allocation part of historical understanding.
- Reputation, dress, noise, literacy, crime, and NPC routine model social legibility and hierarchy.
- Procedural combat and craft turn competence into learned action rather than automatic character power.
- Travel and surviving material evidence can test, deepen, or qualify what a game reconstruction makes imaginable.

- Social systems: [[no-234-jieke-youji-shuyu-chengbao-yinkuang-yu-jiuguan-de-boximiya-ditu-gkwrijiovueba7xf6gt0j48r]] connects reputation, clothing, noise, theft, occupation, and daily routine to player interaction.
- Procedural and spatial learning: [[no-234-jieke-youji-shuyu-chengbao-yinkuang-yu-jiuguan-de-boximiya-ditu-gkwrijiovueba7xf6gt0j48r]] links swordsmanship, forging, alchemy, historical glossaries, Trosky Castle, and Kutná Hora to a cross-check between play and place.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Difficulty does not automatically produce accuracy, and a coherent system can still compress, omit, or invent history. The source reflects one player's experience and does not compare learning outcomes, game versions, accessibility, archival evidence, or specialist assessments. Simulation should be treated as a question-generating model rather than an authoritative reconstruction.

## What Changed

- Established friction and consequence as the mechanism connecting game systems to historical understanding.
- Added travel and material remains as an external comparison layer that limits simulation's authority.
