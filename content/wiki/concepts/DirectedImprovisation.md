# Directed Improvisation / 引导创变

Directed improvisation is [[150-zhongguo-ruhe-tiaochu-pinkun-xianjing-xiandaihua-zhi-lu-you-hui-zouxiang-hefang-lgpvp04rngagkpubte2ac6i5ji3g]]'s name, through [[YuenYuenAng|洪源远]], for a center-local reform style where central reformers set boundaries, signals, and incentives while local governments improvise inside those constraints. The source uses the "蜂窝煤" metaphor to show that reform transmission must leave channels open between central structure and local airflow.

The concept explains why Chinese reform was neither pure decentralization nor pure command. Local officials and departments could use local relationships, fiscal incentives, and policy wording to solve practical problems, but their room to act remained structured by central authorization, red lines, and career or fiscal incentives.

## Key Claims
- Central direction and local improvisation can coexist.
- Policy wording matters because small central-language shifts can open or close local action space.
- Local experimentation is bounded; it is not unlimited bottom-up market autonomy.
- The model can generate fast adaptation but also uneven outcomes and incentives to chase measurable growth.
