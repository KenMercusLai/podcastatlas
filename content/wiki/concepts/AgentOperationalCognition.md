# Agent Operational Cognition

## Definition
Agent operational cognition is an agent's situated understanding of how an organization actually performs work: its rules, goals, workflows, exceptions, approvals, task history, and reasons for acting.

## Current Synthesis
The source separates operational cognition from interface execution with a “brain” and “hand” analogy. A system may click, type, browse, and manipulate software competently while still misunderstanding what the business task means or which local rule governs it. Reliable enterprise agency therefore requires computer use to be joined with accumulated organizational context, feedback, memory, and judgment.

## Key Claims
- Interface control and business understanding are distinct capabilities.
- Software execution is necessary because digital work ends in applications, websites, files, or APIs.
- Organizational rules and exceptions determine what action is appropriate, not only whether the action can be executed.
- Repeated work and feedback can build situated competence that a generic model does not possess initially.
- Stronger base models may simplify execution scaffolds without eliminating local cognition or verification needs.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- The claim that computer-use execution is nearly solved conflicts with unresolved reliability, speed, permission, privacy, rollback, and verification evidence elsewhere in the wiki.
- Organizational cognition can become stale or encode harmful local practice; learning requires provenance, correction, access control, and deletion paths.
- Better task success does not reveal whether the agent formed transferable understanding or merely exploited familiar patterns.
- The source provides operator judgment rather than comparative benchmark evidence for the brain-hand split.

## What Changed
- Established operational cognition as the organizational-knowledge layer distinct from interface manipulation.
