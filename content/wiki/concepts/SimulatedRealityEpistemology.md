# Simulated Reality Epistemology / 模拟现实认识论

## Definition
Simulated reality epistemology is the problem of how a person can know what is real when technical systems, media environments, or AI-created worlds can generate a complete experienced reality.

## Current Synthesis
[[dianying-li-de-ai-qingren-juying-he-ducai-zhe-gkwriuen9djgazhkswsh-d-b]] uses [[TheMatrix|《黑客帝国》]] to move AI cinema from physical rebellion into epistemology. The point is not only that machines imprison bodies; it is that a simulated world can make reality itself hard to identify.

The episode connects the film to Plato's cave, Schopenhauer, Kant's thing-in-itself, and Mou Zongsan's infinite mind. Its strongest qualification is that leaving one simulation may not guarantee access to absolute reality; a person may move from one layer of appearance to another.

## Key Claims
- AI-created simulation changes the AI-film question from survival to knowledge.
- A convincing simulated world can make experience internally coherent while still concealing its conditions.
- Escape from one mediated layer does not prove direct access to ultimate reality.
- The concept connects film simulation to broader algorithmic and media projection problems.
- The source treats simulation as a philosophical device, not as evidence that our world is literally simulated.

## Evidence
- Matrix reading: [[dianying-li-de-ai-qingren-juying-he-ducai-zhe-gkwriuen9djgazhkswsh-d-b]] classifies [[TheMatrix|《黑客帝国》]] as an AI film where AI creates simulated reality.
- Philosophical frame: [[dianying-li-de-ai-qingren-juying-he-ducai-zhe-gkwriuen9djgazhkswsh-d-b]] invokes Plato, Schopenhauer, Kant, and Mou Zongsan to ask whether humans can know the real world.
- Layer qualification: [[dianying-li-de-ai-qingren-juying-he-ducai-zhe-gkwriuen9djgazhkswsh-d-b]] suggests that leaving the Matrix may still mean entering another phenomenon layer rather than absolute truth.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source uses simulation as an interpretive frame for film and AI culture. It does not make an empirical claim that human reality is currently simulated by AI.
