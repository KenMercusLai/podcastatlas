# Chinese Modernist Architects in Shanghai / 上海华人现代建筑师

## Definition
Chinese modernist architects in Shanghai are the returned or locally active Chinese architects whom the episode treats as important contributors to Shanghai's modern built environment alongside foreign architects.

## Current Synthesis
The source rejects the idea that foreign architects alone defined modern Shanghai. Wu Feipeng says early access to good projects could be harder for Chinese designers, but the 1925-after real-estate boom created enough work for capable architects. Figures such as Zhuang Jun, Dong Dayou, Li Jingpei, Fan Wenzhao, Lu Qianshou, and Xi Fuquan show different paths through public buildings, cinemas, banks, apartments, factories, new-capital planning, and later socialist or overseas-aid construction.

## Key Claims
- Chinese architects were structurally disadvantaged at first but not absent from important commissions.
- Real-estate expansion after 1925 opened room for strong Chinese architectural practices.
- Returned architects came to Shanghai with explicit modernist design ambitions.
- Different education systems shaped different routes, including Beaux-Arts, Pennsylvania-system, German, Bauhaus, and public-building practices.
- Chinese architects' careers often extended beyond Republican Shanghai into New China-era factories and public projects.
