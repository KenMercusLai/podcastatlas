# Elder Technology Exclusion / 老年技术排除

Elder technology exclusion is the source's frame for how fast digital-service migration can make ordinary life harder for older people. [[165-yinfa-chuanliu-yong-youmo-miandui-laonian-zanmen-lai-xie-dayoushi-944213646]] names online shopping, tax queries, train-ticket buying, smartphone use, and voice assistants as examples of daily tasks that can become barriers when service design assumes fluent mobile use.

The concept sits between [[AgeFriendlyInfrastructure]] and [[ElderCareStateCapacity]]. Infrastructure is not only stairs, elevators, roads, and clinics; it is also account login, app navigation, identity verification, payment flow, ticketing, and customer support. When those systems move faster than elders can adapt, family members, scammers, platform staff, and public-service offices all become part of the care environment.

The episode's [[Siri]] joke shows the ambivalence. A patient digital assistant can help when it answers repeated questions without irritation, but a voice interface does not by itself solve exclusion if the surrounding services remain difficult, fragmented, or unforgiving.

## Key Claims
- Digital convenience for younger users can become access friction for elders.
- Technology barriers are care problems, not only product-usability problems.
- Fraud vulnerability can grow when official systems are hard to use but unofficial sellers offer attention, patience, and conversation.
- Age-friendly design needs slower, redundant, and human-supported routes for public services and daily commerce.
