# External Feedback Self-Calibration

External feedback self-calibration is the episode's idea that trusted outside observers can correct distorted self-assessment. In [[ep-3-demystifying-the-imposter-syndrome]], [[SamDataScienceWithSam|Sam]] and [[StephenMathis|Stephen Mathis]] describe mentors, supervisors, managers, family, parents, and friends as people who may see abilities that the person experiencing [[ImpostorSyndrome|imposter syndrome]] cannot recognize internally.

The concept does not treat all praise as automatically true. It asks people to give sincere, reasoned recognition a fair hearing instead of dismissing it reflexively. That makes it a bridge between [[ConcreteSelfPraise]] and outside evidence: the person records and accepts specific feedback only when it is connected to actual work, behavior, or effort.

The source also uses the idea that feelings can be valid without being accurate. External calibration helps separate "I feel inadequate" from "I lack the ability required for this task."

## Key Claims
- A trusted observer can sometimes see competence more clearly than the person experiencing self-doubt.
- Feedback is most useful when it names concrete work, effort, or behavior rather than vague reassurance.
- Accepting praise is a self-assessment practice, not a claim of superiority.
- Outside feedback should support [[CapabilityGapSelfDiagnosis]], not replace judgment entirely.
