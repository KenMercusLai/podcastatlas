# Cover-Up Escalation

## Definition

Cover-up escalation is the process by which attempts to conceal an initiating offense create new acts, dependencies, evidence conflicts, and institutional confrontations that become more consequential than the original event.

## Current Synthesis

The [[WatergateScandal]] supplies the bounded case. The burglary initially survived as a contained criminal matter while money, false explanations, and participant silence held. Each effort to preserve that containment increased exposure: insiders acquired incentives to cooperate, recorded conversations became discoverable, refusal to produce evidence created a constitutional conflict, and the attempted dismissal of a prosecutor made obstruction publicly visible. The mechanism is cumulative rather than automatic; escalation occurs when concealment creates fresh liabilities and forces increasingly costly choices.

## Key Claims

- A cover-up depends on continued coordination among participants whose incentives change as prosecution risk rises.
- Payments, perjury, witness pressure, and false institutional explanations generate liabilities beyond the initiating offense.
- Objective records can transform a credibility dispute into a falsifiable evidentiary conflict.
- Resistance to lawful investigation can become an independent abuse rather than merely a defense against the original allegation.
- Escalation narrows exit options because every new concealment decision raises the cost of disclosure.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept does not imply that every denial proves an underlying offense or that lawful assertions of privilege are themselves obstruction. The bounded source concerns a case where recorded evidence established presidential participation and where resistance unfolded alongside payments, perjury, and efforts to impede investigation. Motives for preserving the tapes and the exact counterfactual effect of destroying them remain source-scoped.
