# Consequence-Sensitive Chivalry / 后果敏感的侠义

## Definition

Consequence-sensitive chivalry is the principle that benevolent intent and martial capacity become ethically meaningful only when joined to factual judgment, appropriate method, attention to others' interests, and responsibility for foreseeable harm.

## Current Synthesis

The bounded source develops the concept through [[GuoFurong|郭芙蓉]]'s failed heroism. She sincerely wants to oppose wrongdoing, but reads ordinary situations through a preselected heroic script and treats force as evidence that she is righteous. The injured groom, doctor, and ferryman show why self-understood goodness cannot settle the moral status of an intervention.

The correction is not passivity. 佟湘玉 emphasizes concern for others and method, while [[BaiZhantang|白展堂]] argues that martial ability should answer actual injustice rather than the desire to compete. Debt, repair, work, and restraint then make responsibility concrete. Chivalry is therefore preserved but displaced from glamorous action toward disciplined perception and accountable conduct.

## Key Claims

- Good intention is necessary but insufficient for righteous intervention.
- Force cannot validate the story an actor tells about a situation.
- Method and likely consequence belong inside moral judgment, not after it.
- Responsibility includes repair when mistaken action imposes costs on others.
- Restraint can express martial ethics rather than cowardice or lack of capacity.
- Everyday care and accountable participation can carry more ethical weight than public heroic display.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept is derived from a comic fictional story and a podcast interpretation, not a complete history of 游侠 or a general legal test for intervention. The hosts themselves qualify the episode's character-based etymology of “侠” and note that traditional accounts may emphasize promise, courage, sacrifice, or bearing responsibility differently. Attention to consequences must also not become a demand for perfect foresight or a reason never to intervene.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from the contrast between Guo Furong's self-image and the consequences of her interventions.
