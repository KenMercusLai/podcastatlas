# Culinary Tradition Reconstruction / 饮食传统复原

## Definition

Culinary tradition reconstruction is the recovery or re-creation of a discontinuous dish, banquet, or technique from old recipes, oral memory, surviving craft, available ingredients, and modern culinary judgment.

## Current Synthesis

The episode treats reconstruction as normal historical work rather than proof of fraud. Some Suzhou banquet dishes and Nanjing eel preparations described as old cuisine were restored or standardized by twentieth-century cooks using textual records and contemporary imagination. The result may preserve real culinary knowledge while still differing from an uninterrupted lineage.

Credible reconstruction therefore needs two kinds of honesty: practical skill sufficient to make a coherent dish, and historical disclosure about what survives, what was inferred, what ingredients changed, and what the modern cook supplied. A recipe text alone cannot reproduce apprenticeship, timing, texture, service, or ecological context.

## Key Claims

- Written recipes are incomplete instructions because tacit skill, ingredient condition, tools, timing, and service context also shape a dish.
- Oral memory, family practice, chef lineage, archival records, and comparative cooking can each contribute different evidence.
- Reconstruction necessarily includes modern judgment when a technique, ingredient, or continuous teaching chain has been lost.
- A reconstructed dish can become a meaningful tradition without being misrepresented as unchanged survival.
- Ecological and supply changes may make exact restoration impossible even when the method is documented.

- Recipe evidence: [[512-yu-wangkai-manhua-jiangnan-shishi-cong-wenren-xieyan-dao-xiangye-tucai-lh0w1a0jwafuydxpdghqppuwvbzy]] warns that dishes advertised as Song survivals may be modern restorations based on old texts plus contemporary imagination.
- Nanjing evidence: [[512-yu-wangkai-manhua-jiangnan-shishi-cong-wenren-xieyan-dao-xiangye-tucai-lh0w1a0jwafuydxpdghqppuwvbzy]] describes some eel dishes as recreated or standardized in the 1980s from earlier records rather than continuously transmitted in fixed form.
- Material-limit evidence: [[512-yu-wangkai-manhua-jiangnan-shishi-cong-wenren-xieyan-dao-xiangye-tucai-lh0w1a0jwafuydxpdghqppuwvbzy]] notes unavailable river species, substitute fish, abandoned blood-based preparations, and degraded canned water shield.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode does not document each recipe genealogy in full, and its historical claims require archival and culinary corroboration. Reconstruction ranges from careful experimental recovery to loose branding; the concept should not confer authenticity merely because a restaurant cites an old book.

## What Changed

- Created the concept to distinguish disclosed culinary recovery from claims of uninterrupted antiquity.
