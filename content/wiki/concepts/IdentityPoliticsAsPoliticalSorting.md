# Identity Politics As Political Sorting

## Definition
Identity politics as political sorting is the pattern in which race, class, region, religion, occupation, national belonging, and perceived deservingness organize how people interpret policy facts.

## Current Synthesis
The general U.S. frame distinguishes acquired identities from inherited or origin identities, while also treating identity politics as present on both left and right. Welfare, health care, immigration, crime, campus speech, [[BlackLivesMatter]], and [[MAGA]] are all treated as policy arguments that can be sorted through group identity.

A comparative migration case sharpens the concept. White South Africans can be treated as favored refugees or sympathetic workers inside a restrictionist U.S. immigration environment because their story fits a [[WhiteVictimhoodNarrative]]. The same workers also show identity sorting's limits: in local Mississippi, shared whiteness does not erase foreignness, class, accent, employer dependence, or job-competition anxiety.

## Key Claims
- Identity politics is not only a left-wing phenomenon; [[MAGA]] can turn white identity and national belonging into a political boundary.
- Welfare, health care, immigration, crime, campus politics, and refugee deservingness can be argued as policy questions while being sorted through group identity.
- Fiscal or legal language can carry racialized assumptions about who deserves benefits, protection, or suspicion.
- Ordinary voters should not be reduced to prejudice alone; contact patterns, media exposure, local economic decline, and social dignity shape identity alignment.
- [[WhiteVictimhoodNarrative]] shows how perceived loss of status can be recoded as discrimination and exported across national contexts.
- Identity sorting can fail to produce full belonging when national, class, occupational, and local-culture boundaries remain strong.

## Evidence
- General U.S. identity sorting: [[sp-05-liuqing-linyao-qiangsha-yibao-jutou-an-beihou-de-sikao-xia-fenlie-de-meiguo-hui-you-xianzheng-weiji-ma-790241978]] says welfare, health-care, immigration, movement, and campus-speech disputes are often filtered through race, party, media, and national belonging.
- Welfare beneficiary shift: [[sp-05-liuqing-linyao-qiangsha-yibao-jutou-an-beihou-de-sikao-xia-fenlie-de-meiguo-hui-you-xianzheng-weiji-ma-790241978]] uses [[ThedaSkocpol|Theda Skocpol]]'s Tea Party research to argue that support can shift when imagined beneficiaries move from white mothers to Black, Asian, or Latino recipients.
- Right-side identity politics: [[sp-05-liuqing-linyao-qiangsha-yibao-jutou-an-beihou-de-sikao-xia-fenlie-de-meiguo-hui-you-xianzheng-weiji-ma-790241978]] says [[MAGA]] can carry white-national belonging language while also being shaped by economic and media conditions.
- White South African exception: [[398-yi-chang-meiguo-guancha-mantan-shijiebei-hongpai-nanfei-bairen-laogong-b28f7eaa-c801-4d5f-a6e5-35326d335df7]] presents white South Africans as a migrant group favored by a restrictionist politics because they fit a white-threat story.
- Belonging limit: [[398-yi-chang-meiguo-guancha-mantan-shijiebei-hongpai-nanfei-bairen-laogong-b28f7eaa-c801-4d5f-a6e5-35326d335df7]] reports that local white residents do not fully absorb white South African workers into local whiteness, showing that identity sorting is layered rather than one-dimensional.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept names a sorting mechanism, not a total explanation. Economic interests, institutions, media infrastructure, legal design, and local relationships still matter. The Mississippi source also warns against flattening white South African workers into symbols: individual labor hardship can be real even when the group is politically instrumentalized.
