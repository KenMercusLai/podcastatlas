# Cross-Border Disaster Early Warning

## Definition
Cross-border disaster early warning is the monitoring, information-sharing, and public-alert chain needed when a hazard forms in one jurisdiction but threatens downstream communities in another.

## Current Synthesis
The Nepal flood episode shows that a functioning domestic alert cannot recover time lost upstream. Nepalese authorities reportedly received information only after the surge had entered the country and then issued phone warnings, leaving about sixteen minutes while upstream destruction was already under way. Because Himalayan rivers and ice-rock hazards cross borders, monitoring data and notification protocols operate as shared safety infrastructure rather than diplomatic extras.

## Key Claims
- Warning time depends on the entire upstream-to-household chain, not only the speed of the final text message.
- Hazards that cross borders require shared monitoring, thresholds, contact points, and practiced notification procedures before an emergency.
- An unimplemented recommendation is not preparedness; institutional follow-through determines whether technical knowledge becomes evacuation time.
- Warning systems cannot eliminate exposure, but they can change the time available for evacuation, shutdown, and rescue staging.

## Evidence
- Compressed alert window: [[niboer-teda-hongzai-xianchang-yichang-meiyou-yiti-de-zangli-cd38365e015f056efaa8b0506c123504]] says Rasuwa authorities learned of the flood about sixteen minutes after it entered Nepal and then sent mobile warnings.
- Prior institutional knowledge: [[niboer-teda-hongzai-xianchang-yichang-meiyou-yiti-de-zangli-cd38365e015f056efaa8b0506c123504]] says Nepal had proposed a Nepal-China warning and river-risk system after an earlier cross-border flood, but the proposals remained on paper.
- Regional exposure: [[niboer-teda-hongzai-xianchang-yichang-meiyou-yiti-de-zangli-cd38365e015f056efaa8b0506c123504]] cites research identifying dozens of potential Himalayan danger sites affecting millions of people.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode does not provide the Chinese-side monitoring record, a technical incident timeline, or evidence that a particular warning interval would have prevented specified losses. It supports a governance-gap finding, not a complete allocation of responsibility for the disaster.
