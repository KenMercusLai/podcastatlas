# Sports Analytics Stakeholder Communication

Sports analytics stakeholder communication is the practice of translating technical analysis into language and formats that coaches, players, scouts, executives, and other non-technical stakeholders can use. [[ep-12-insightful-conversation-with-a-football-analytics-professional]] grounds the concept through [[AnnaDSouza|Anna D'Souza]], who argues that analytics skill is insufficient if the analyst cannot explain the insight.

The source treats football knowledge as the sports equivalent of business acumen in data science. A technically strong report can still fail if it foregrounds Tableau, Python, or model details when a coach needs a high-level decision cue, a player needs an actionable adjustment, or a team needs a game-model fit assessment.

Anna's coaching experience matters here. She says coaching children helped her learn how to simplify and communicate ideas, making the analyst's job partly pedagogical: understand the audience, remove unnecessary abstraction, and present insight in a way that respects the football environment.

[[ep-13-soccer-analytics-through-the-lens-of-coaching]] adds a coach-player trust version through [[BrunoSoccerCoach|Bruno]]. He says data can help a coach ask players to trust the evidence, but only if the coach can explain what the numbers mean and how they change practice, tactics, or player behavior.

## Key Claims
- The audience for sports analytics often cares more about action than tool choice.
- Analysts need enough domain knowledge to know which questions coaches, players, and scouts are actually trying to answer.
- Communication should preserve the decision-relevant idea while reducing technical friction.
- Coaching, playing, or close operational experience can make communication more credible.
- The concept generalizes the wiki's [[DomainExpertAlignment]] theme into a sports setting.
- In coaching contexts, communication also has to protect trust, confidence, and fast in-game decision-making.
