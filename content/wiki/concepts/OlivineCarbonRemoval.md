# Olivine Carbon Removal

Olivine carbon removal is the mineral-specific [[OceanAlkalinityEnhancement]] pathway in [[tech-20260728-0728-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260728-0728-mp-tech-pod-128]]. The episode says [[Vesta]] placed more than 8,000 metric tons of olivine on the sea floor near Duck, North Carolina, to test whether dissolving olivine can change seawater chemistry and store more CO2 as bicarbonate.

[[JacquelineSentiner]] explains olivine as a natural silicate mineral, while [[AdamSubhas]] describes alkaline materials as an ocean "antacid." The source's main boundary is verification: the mineral pathway has to prove actual net removal, not only plausible chemistry, and it has to do so without unacceptable effects on marine life.

## Key Claims
- Olivine can dissolve in seawater and help convert CO2 into bicarbonate, according to the source's explanation.
- The project is monitored through ocean-chemistry sampling at different offshore locations.
- [[Vesta]] estimates about 5,000 tons of net CO2 removal for the Duck project after project emissions.
- The source does not independently prove the estimate; it treats the figure as a company estimate needing monitoring and broader research.
- Olivine deployment could connect [[CarbonRemoval]] to coastal-restoration work if ecological impact remains low.
