# 守陵孝行进身凭证 / Filial Mourning Access Credential

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-907-suoyou-de-bing-doushi-ni-de-xinbing-ltqzyohcauduwuanlvqph9l81d79|Hanji 907]] supplies the event before Cai Yong's corrective reading. Several dozen commoners go to Emperor Huan's Xuanling and call themselves "Xuanling filial sons"; [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]] is pleased and gives them taizi sheren appointments. The source uses the scene to show performed filiality becoming an office and heir-proximity credential before the next episode records the critique.

Filial mourning access credential / 守陵孝行进身凭证 is the appointment-risk pattern [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-908-nijing-chu-qiangzhe-xialu-chu-yongzhe-gudu-chu-zhizhe-lqih1dradhzi0mipxsaiedd52dlj|Hanji 908]] draws from [[CaiYong|蔡邕]]'s warning to [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]]. The emperor had placed "Xuanling filial sons" near the crown prince as taizi sheren, but Cai Yong argues that many had no real kinship obligation or deep imperial favor to justify extraordinary mourning status.

The problem is not filial piety itself. Cai Yong cites mourning-restraint precedent to show that even sincere grief must remain under制度, while performed mourning is more dangerous because it can convert visible virtue into [[PalaceAccessMonopoly|宫廷接触垄断]] and proximity to a vulnerable succession environment.

The source gives the risk a concrete criminal edge through a fugitive who allegedly posed as a filial mourner during Empress Dowager Dou's funeral and entered the procession before being arrested. That example makes the concept more than moral skepticism: a credential built from public piety can become an access-control failure when the court does not verify identity, motive, and role.

## Key Claims

- Hanji 907 supplies the initial appointment event: Xuanling filial performance is rewarded with taizi sheren status.
- Moral performance can become politically valuable when it grants access to office, ritual space, or heir proximity.
- Filial devotion is not rejected; the source argues that it needs institutional boundaries and verification.
- The Xuanling case differs from modern elder-care law in [[FilialPietyLaws]] because it concerns court appointment and succession access, not family support enforcement.
- Emperor Ling's partial correction moves the Xuanling group away from the crown prince, but does not by itself solve the broader [[OfficialSelectionChannelCompetition|选官通道竞争]] problem.
