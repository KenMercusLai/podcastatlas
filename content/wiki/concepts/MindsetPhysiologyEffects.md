# Mindset Effects on Physiology

## Definition
Mindset effects on physiology are changes in experience, behavior, biological response, or health-related outcomes associated with the interpretive frame through which a person understands food, activity, stress, feedback, symptoms, or treatment.

## Current Synthesis
The sources distinguish mindset from generic optimism and from a narrow inert-placebo model. A mindset is a domain-specific assumption that organizes what a person expects, notices, explains, and tries to do. Identical milkshakes labeled as indulgent or sensible were associated with different ghrelin responses, while hotel workers told that their labor met exercise guidelines later showed changes in several health metrics.

The newer [[AliCrum]] interview widens the framework to stress, sleep feedback, side effects, and active treatment. Its practical proposal is not to decide whether a belief is perfectly true before acting, but to ask what effect the belief has and whether a more useful interpretation is credible. The bounded synthesis remains interactive: objective inputs, social context, behavior, expectation, and physiological capacity jointly shape outcomes.

## Key Claims
- Mindsets are domain-specific default assumptions that organize expectation, attention, explanation, motivation, and behavior.
- Informational framing can change responses to a similar physical input, including food and occupational activity in the reported studies.
- Feedback about sleep, fitness, symptoms, or insufficiency can prime expectations that may change performance or felt experience.
- Stress mindset can influence whether arousal is treated as a reason to disengage or as a resource for a viable valued action.
- Treatment framing can interact with an active intervention by changing anxiety, symptom interpretation, and possibly physiological pathways.
- Material nutrition, activity, sleep, disease, treatment, and social conditions remain causally important even when mindset contributes.

## Evidence
### Milkshake framing
- [[how-placebo-effects-work-to-change-our-biology-psychology-scim2208611274]] reports steeper ghrelin reduction and different satiety after an identical drink was described as indulgent rather than sensible.
- [[science-of-mindsets-for-health-performance-dr-alia-crum-scim9502231820]] reports the same contrast and uses it to caution against a chronic deprivation interpretation of healthy eating.

### Occupational exercise framing
- [[how-placebo-effects-work-to-change-our-biology-psychology-scim2208611274]] reports reduced blood pressure, resting heart rate, and body weight after hotel workers were told their daily work qualified as exercise.
- [[science-of-mindsets-for-health-performance-dr-alia-crum-scim9502231820]] adds the public-health implication that activity guidance can motivate more movement while also helping people recognize activity they already perform.

### Feedback, stress, and treatment
- [[science-of-mindsets-for-health-performance-dr-alia-crum-scim9502231820]] reports cognitive deficits after false poor-sleep feedback, improved workplace outcomes after stress-enhancing information, and improved allergy-treatment outcomes after adaptive side-effect framing.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not resolve whether attention, later behavior, reporting, anxiety, statistical variation, or other mediators explain each outcome. Full protocols, sample sizes, effect sizes, replications, and durability are absent. Mortality associations do not establish that perceived inactivity caused death. These findings do not justify deceptive labeling, withholding information, blaming patients, or treating belief as a substitute for nutrition, exercise, sleep, safety, or medical care.

## What Changed
- Expanded the synthesis from food and activity to stress, sleep feedback, symptoms, and active treatment.
- Defined deliberate mindset change as effect-oriented and credibility-bounded rather than generic positive thinking.
- Added explicit limits around causal inference, deceptive framing, patient blame, and material inputs.
