# Operational Digital Twin

## Definition
An operational digital twin is an interoperable, continuously updated data representation of a physical system used for situational awareness, pattern detection, forecasting, and decisions rather than necessarily being a complete visual or behavioral replica.

## Current Synthesis
The source broadens digital twins across bodies, hospitals, airplanes, air-traffic control, sports, household systems, and a reef tank. The common value is operational: sensors turn physical change into a longitudinal view that can reveal trends or deviations humans may miss. A twin is therefore useful when the selected data supports a real decision, not when it merely creates a more elaborate dashboard.

This general frame connects specialized twins without collapsing their safety requirements. A movement model, factory simulation, workplace representation, hospital system, and household aquarium differ in stakes, consent, model validity, and acceptable error even when each uses live data to guide action.

## Key Claims
- Continuous insight and decision support matter more than visual resemblance to the physical system.
- Interoperability determines whether separate sensor streams can form a useful operating picture.
- A twin is selective by design and should include the variables relevant to the intended decision.
- Early anomaly detection is valuable only when measurements, thresholds, and responses are trustworthy.
- Human, medical, and workplace twins require stronger consent, privacy, validation, and accountability than low-stakes equipment monitoring.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source offers examples rather than comparative validation of digital-twin accuracy or outcomes. More data does not guarantee a faithful model, causal understanding, or better decisions, and interoperable personal data can expand surveillance and security risk. Clinical and safety-critical twins require domain-specific evidence and accountable human review.
