# Ballot Harvesting Trust Risk

## Definition
Ballot harvesting trust risk is the legitimacy problem that arises when third-party ballot collection, universal mailed ballots, loose voter-roll maintenance, weak voter ID, or unclear chain of custody makes voters doubt whether legal ballot collection reflects individual voter intent.

## Current Synthesis
The source distinguishes legality, suspicious appearance, and proof. The hosts argue that California's election rules can permit organized ballot collection that looks manipulable even if it is legal, while Jason repeatedly asks whether there is current evidence of fraud and what proof would be needed. The reusable concept is that access-expanding election rules can lower trust if the public cannot see how identity, custody, signature verification, and tabulation are controlled.

The concept does not assert that ballot collection is inherently fraudulent. It captures a procedural-risk claim: when late ballots strongly differ from election-day ballots, and when parties or campaigns can collect ballots at scale, institutions need explanations and auditability good enough to survive partisan suspicion.

## Key Claims
- Legal ballot collection can still reduce legitimacy if voters believe organized intermediaries can shape turnout or custody invisibly.
- Universal mailed ballots increase the importance of voter-roll maintenance, signature verification, custody records, and timely tabulation.
- Late-count vote shifts require cohort explanations before they are treated as fraud evidence.
- Voter ID and tighter ballot controls can be trust repairs even when they do not prove that a specific prior result was fraudulent.
- Public confidence erodes when procedural questions are dismissed as partisan taboo rather than answered with evidence and audits.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This concept is based on a debate episode, not an official election audit. Suspicious timing, legal ballot collection, or partisan turnout operations do not by themselves prove illegal voting or changed outcomes. Existing wiki material on [[ElectionAdministrationTrust]] also preserves Nate Silver's warning that mail-voting cohorts can create predictable shifts without fraud.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to separate ballot-collection trust risk from settled fraud claims.
