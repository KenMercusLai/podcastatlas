# Ballot Harvesting Trust Risk

## Definition
Ballot harvesting trust risk is the legitimacy problem that arises when third-party ballot collection, universal mailed ballots, loose voter-roll maintenance, weak voter ID, or unclear chain of custody makes voters doubt whether legal ballot collection reflects individual voter intent.

## Current Synthesis
The sources distinguish legal organizing, suspicious appearance, and proof. The June discussion argues that California's rules permit organized ballot collection that can look manipulable even when legal, while Jason asks what evidence would establish fraud. In the September follow-up, Pratt distinguishes ballot harvesting from allegedly fake ballots and concedes that he cannot claim fraud caused his loss without evidence.

The reusable concept is therefore procedural rather than accusatory. When late ballots differ from election-day ballots and campaigns collect votes at scale, institutions need cohort explanations, identity and custody controls, and audits legible enough to survive partisan suspicion. A recount can verify tabulation of accepted ballots without independently proving that every accepted ballot was validly cast.

## Key Claims
- Legal ballot collection can still reduce legitimacy if voters believe organized intermediaries can shape turnout or custody invisibly.
- Universal mailed ballots increase the importance of voter-roll maintenance, signature verification, custody records, and timely tabulation.
- Late-count vote shifts require cohort explanations before they are treated as fraud evidence.
- Ballot harvesting of legally cast votes, allegedly fake ballots, and tabulation errors are distinct claims requiring different evidence.
- Voter ID, roll maintenance, tighter custody, and clearer audit procedures can be trust repairs without proving a prior result fraudulent.
- A candidate's suspicion or inability to trace individual ballots after envelope separation does not itself establish invalid votes or a changed outcome.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
These sources are political discussions, not official election audits. Suspicious timing, legal ballot collection, envelope separation after verification, or partisan turnout operations do not by themselves prove illegal voting or changed outcomes. The later source strengthens rather than removes this boundary because Pratt expressly acknowledges the lack of proof. Existing wiki material on [[ElectionAdministrationTrust]] also preserves Nate Silver's warning that mail-voting cohorts can create predictable shifts without fraud.

## What Changed
- Added Pratt's explicit concession that he cannot attribute his loss to fraud without evidence.
- Distinguished legal ballot harvesting, fake-ballot allegations, and recount or audit limits.
- Clarified that traceability concerns create a trust problem but do not establish an outcome-changing crime.
