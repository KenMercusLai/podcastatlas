# Sexual Script and Performance Pressure / 性脚本与表现压力

## Definition
Sexual script and performance pressure is the anxiety produced when narrow bodily or social conventions define complete, successful, or appropriately meaningful sex—from penetration and orgasm to the demand that a casual encounter appear intoxicated, spontaneous, and emotionally empty.

## Current Synthesis
The sources identify two connected layers. Absent formal sex education, pornography and popular culture can become default teachers, foregrounding visible intensity and a male-centered sequence rather than the slower, negotiated work of safety, arousal, touch, feedback, and mutual pleasure. On a college campus, peers and party institutions can add a meaning script: alcohol, desirable partners, apparent spontaneity, and distance afterward classify sex as acceptably casual.

Once this script becomes an achievement standard, sex can resemble a task. Men may treat erection as the central proof of competence, while any partner may interpret interrupted desire or absent orgasm as evidence of unattractiveness or failure. A wider script includes embracing, touching, external stimulation, pacing, hygiene, communication, products, and stopping without defining every variation as deficiency.

The same widening applies to meaning. Casual sex need not require callousness, and care need not prove a promise of relationship. Directly negotiated preference can replace both a fixed bodily sequence and the competitive display of caring less.

## Key Claims
- Penetration-to-ejaculation is one possible sequence, not the universal definition of complete sex.
- Pornographic visibility and intensity are poor substitutes for reciprocal real-world education.
- Performance metrics can turn pleasure and curiosity into tests of competence or attractiveness.
- Good sexual interaction depends on feedback, pacing, safety, and partner-specific learning.
- Social scripts can also dictate what sex is allowed to mean by rewarding intoxication, spontaneity, status, and emotional distance.
- Broadening the script does not remove the need to assess persistent pain, distress, or function concerns.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Pornography exposure does not determine behavior, and preferred sexual activities and relationship forms vary. Broadening the script should not minimize pain, unwanted contact, persistent dysfunction, infection risk, pregnancy risk, or the need for qualified care. The Talk三联 source offers journalistic and counseling observations rather than prevalence estimates; the Hidden Brain source summarizes Wade's research without supplying the underlying studies, samples, or full interview record. A dominant script is not a universal description of every sexual encounter or campus.

## What Changed
- Extended the concept from bodily completion and adequacy tests to social rules governing whether sex may appear caring, repeated, sober, or emotionally meaningful.
- Added peer status and unequal access as limits on treating one script as freely chosen by everyone.
