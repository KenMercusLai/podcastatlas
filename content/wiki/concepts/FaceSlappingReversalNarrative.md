# Face-Slapping Reversal Narrative / 打脸反杀叙事

Face-slapping reversal narrative / 打脸反杀叙事 is the modern story label [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-117-1-papa-dalian-lishishang-shuangwen-de-kaishan-bizu-lr0h-kfeguvatattnj-mz8fgvnc]] applies to the [[TangJuEnvoy|唐雎]] and [[AnlingJun|安陵君]] story. A powerful ruler threatens a tiny polity; a weak envoy refuses submission; the confrontation ends with the strong party losing face and the weak party gaining moral and emotional victory.

The concept is related to the Su Qin "爽文" branch already recorded in the wiki. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-29-suqin-lizhi-shuangwen-zhishi-gaibian-mingyun-lrf5cqvoriyep2lkbozju-lyjaxv]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-33-lishi-diyi-shuangwen-nanzhujiao-suqin-lvebjyhvzctjgaevwwoddpvg9nr0]] use Su Qin's failure, study, return, and [[QianjuHougongReversal|前倨后恭]] homecoming as a status-reversal template. Qinji 117-1 adds a more confrontational version: the reversal happens in direct speech under mortal threat.

The episode does not reduce the Tang Ju story to entertainment. Its point is double: the scene has strong narrative payoff, and that payoff is exactly why historical readers should keep literary force separate from strict evidence.

## Key Claims
- The structure requires an initial status imbalance, public humiliation or threat, and a reversal that gives the weak side visible moral advantage.
- Historical anecdotes can become "爽文" templates when later readers recognize the emotional grammar of humiliation answered by public reversal.
- The same emotional force can make a story easier to remember and easier to overbelieve.
