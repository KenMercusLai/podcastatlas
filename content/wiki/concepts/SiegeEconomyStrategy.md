# Siege Economy Strategy

## Definition
Siege economy strategy is the episode's label for a crisis response built around controls, protectionism, nationalisation, and pressure on allies rather than acceptance of IMF-backed spending cuts.

## Current Synthesis
In [[665-britain-in-the-70s-the-bailout-from-hell-part-4-glt9598017819]], the strategy is associated with [[TonyBenn]] and Anthony Crosland's anti-cuts arguments during [[Britain1976IMFCrisis|Britain's 1976 IMF crisis]]. It appears as both an economic program and a bargaining posture: Britain might impose import and capital controls, nationalise banks, raise taxes on the rich, leave the Common Market, reduce defense commitments, and dare the IMF, Americans, and Germans to accept the consequences.

## Key Claims
- The strategy uses economic controls as a sovereignty defense against external financial discipline.
- Its political appeal comes from refusing national humiliation and keeping faith with Labour-left anti-capitalist arguments.
- Its bargaining logic depends on allies valuing Britain's strategic role enough to soften financial terms.
- The episode presents the strategy as unrealistic under 1976 conditions, especially as a substitute for restoring sterling credibility.

## Evidence
- Program content: [[665-britain-in-the-70s-the-bailout-from-hell-part-4-glt9598017819]] lists import controls, capital controls, nationalisation, higher taxes, and Common Market exit as Benn's alternative economic strategy.
- Bargaining posture: [[665-britain-in-the-70s-the-bailout-from-hell-part-4-glt9598017819]] says Crosland argued Britain could threaten defense reductions and siege-economy measures to make allies back down.
- Rejection: [[665-britain-in-the-70s-the-bailout-from-hell-part-4-glt9598017819]] shows Callaghan, Healey, and loyalist ministers defeating the argument in cabinet.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The page records how this source frames the strategy; it does not independently adjudicate whether a different set of controls could have worked under other conditions.

## What Changed
- Initial concept created to capture the rejected Labour-left response to the IMF crisis.
