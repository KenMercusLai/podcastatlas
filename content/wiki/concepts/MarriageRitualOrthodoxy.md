# Marriage Ritual Orthodoxy / 婚姻礼法正当性

Marriage ritual orthodoxy / 婚姻礼法正当性 is the family-and-ritual standard [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-79-shishang-yong-gengniu-da-shengzhang-diyi-ren-lsrl0posblloqwugox160xsicp2p]] uses to reinterpret the earlier [[CrisisAutonomousMarriage|crisis autonomous marriage]] of [[TianFazhang|田法章]] and [[JunWangHou|君王后]]. In [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-73-lishishang-ziyou-lianai-de-kaichuangzhe-shi-shui-lkumfsfialqgdl6qpb3c-hnz7h9u]], the relationship is framed as private recognition during collapse; episode 79 adds the ritual backlash.

The source cites [[MenciusText|《孟子》]] to emphasize parental command and matchmaker mediation. From that frame, [[TaishiJiao|太史敫 / 太史角]] refuses to see his daughter because she entered a relationship without the expected family procedure, even though she later becomes queen.

The concept does not erase the earlier autonomy. It names the competing norm: a crisis bond may be emotionally and politically vindicated after restoration, while still violating the family-ritual order recognized by the father and by the text the episode invokes.

## Key Claims
- A marriage can become politically legitimate without satisfying every family-ritual expectation.
- Crisis can create relationships outside ordinary procedure, but later restoration re-exposes the ritual breach.
- The episode presents Taishi Jiao as severe but principled within his own norm, not merely resentful.
- Jun Wang Hou's later filial respect matters because it prevents the conflict from becoming simple rebellion against parental authority.
