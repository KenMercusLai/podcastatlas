## Definition

Marriage ritual orthodoxy / 婚姻礼法正当性 is the standard that judges marriage through legitimate family, mediation, consent, life-status, and ceremonial procedure rather than through affection, later rank, or a powerful patron's wish alone.

## Current Synthesis

The Qi restoration case shows that political and emotional vindication do not erase procedural breach. In [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-73-lishishang-ziyou-lianai-de-kaichuangzhe-shi-shui-lkumfsfialqgdl6qpb3c-hnz7h9u|Zhouji 73]], [[TianFazhang|田法章]] and [[JunWangHou|君王后]] form a private relationship while he hides during Qi's collapse; his later restoration makes her queen. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-79-shishang-yong-gengniu-da-shengzhang-diyi-ren-lsrl0posblloqwugox160xsicp2p|Zhouji 79]] adds the ritual backlash: [[TaishiJiao|太史敫 / 太史角]] refuses to see his daughter because parental command and matchmaker procedure were bypassed, even though the relationship has become politically successful.

Hanji 1111 widens the boundary from an irregular living marriage to a proposed posthumous one. After [[CaoChongLateHan|曹冲]] and [[BingYuanLateHan|邴原]]'s daughter have both died, [[CaoCao|曹操]] asks that they be married and buried together. Bing Yuan refuses because death and grief do not authorize him to invent a marriage outside the ritual order. Cao Cao's acceptance shows that political power can withdraw an exceptional request without denying the sincerity of the grief that produced it.

The combined synthesis separates outcome from legitimacy. A relationship may be loving, restorative, or politically successful yet remain procedurally disputed; conversely, refusal of an emotionally sympathetic union can be principled rather than callous when it protects the rule that gives marriage public and family meaning.

## Key Claims

- Political success or later royal status does not retroactively satisfy every family-ritual requirement of a marriage.
- Crisis can make ordinary procedure impossible, but restoration reopens the question of whether the bypass was legitimate.
- Parental command and mediation function as public-family authorization rather than decorative ceremony in the source frame.
- Death does not automatically create permission for survivors to assign marriage to deceased children.
- A powerful ruler's grief can motivate an exceptional request without making that request ritually valid.
- Maintaining respect across disagreement can keep ritual conflict from collapsing into a simple hero-villain story.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept records standards asserted within the source corpus rather than a complete legal history of ancient Chinese marriage or posthumous marriage. Zhouji 73's “free love” language is a modern podcast frame, and Zhouji 79's account preserves both Taishi Jiao's severity and Jun Wang Hou's continuing filial respect. Hanji 1111 does not establish the daughter's identity, the detailed rite proposed, or the broader prevalence and legal status of posthumous marriage. The synthesis therefore distinguishes source-backed ritual objection from a universal claim about every period or region.
