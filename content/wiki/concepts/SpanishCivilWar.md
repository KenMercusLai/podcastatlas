# Spanish Civil War

## Definition

The Spanish Civil War appears in the current wiki evidence as the later domestic conflict whose nationalist military leadership, colonial troops, and coercive repertoire were partly formed in the [[RifWar]].

## Current Synthesis

The episode is a prehistory rather than a full account of the Civil War. It argues that the [[ArmyOfAfrica]] supplied the rebellion with experienced troops and that Africanist officers including [[FranciscoFranco]], Emilio Mola, and José Sanjurjo carried prestige, networks, distrust of parliamentary politics, and acceptance of extreme violence from Morocco into Spain.

That lineage is important but bounded. The Rif War did not by itself produce the Civil War, and this source does not cover the later conflict's social coalitions, republican politics, class struggle, regional questions, international intervention, battlefield course, or aftermath.

## Key Claims

- The Army of Africa formed a major military base for the nationalist rebellion.
- Franco's Moroccan command experience and mythology strengthened his later authority.
- Mola, Sanjurjo, and Franco belonged to an Africanist officer world shaped by colonial war.
- Colonial violence supplied a repertoire that could be redirected into domestic conflict.
- Morocco was an enabling institutional prehistory, not a sufficient cause of civil war.

## Evidence

- Personnel and forces: [[282-morocco-the-rif-war-glt7389759975]] links Franco, Mola, Sanjurjo, regulares, and the Foreign Legion to the Moroccan campaign and later rebellion.
- Political culture: [[282-morocco-the-rif-war-glt7389759975]] describes humiliation, distrust of politicians, reactionary identity, and belief in extreme violence among Africanist officers.
- Leadership outcome: [[282-morocco-the-rif-war-glt7389759975]] says Franco became the rebellion's central face after other senior figures died.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This source does not provide a comprehensive history of the Civil War, and its Morocco-to-Spain line must not displace independent political, economic, regional, ideological, and international causes. The concept will require substantial revision when direct Civil War sources are ingested.
