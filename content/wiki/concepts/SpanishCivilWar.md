# Spanish Civil War

## Definition

The Spanish Civil War was the conflict produced when the July 1936 military coup failed to take all of Spain, dividing the country between Nationalist and Republican zones; the current evidence also traces part of the Nationalist military leadership, colonial forces, and coercive repertoire back to the [[RifWar]].

## Current Synthesis

The current synthesis combines a detailed colonial prehistory with a brief direct overview. [[282-morocco-the-rif-war-glt7389759975]] argues that the [[ArmyOfAfrica]] supplied the rebellion with experienced troops and that Africanist officers including [[FranciscoFranco]], Emilio Mola, and José Sanjurjo carried prestige, networks, distrust of parliamentary politics, and acceptance of extreme violence from Morocco into Spain.

The bonus clip in [[216-pigeons-glt2770080110]] adds that Spain was already divided by political turbulence, social inequality, rural traditional structures, and industrialized regions; the coup's incomplete success created competing zones. It identifies strong Italian and German support for the Nationalists and later Soviet aid and foreign volunteers for the Republicans, while emphasizing atrocities and mass graves. These points broaden the page but remain an outline rather than a full account of the conflict's coalitions, regional questions, battlefield course, or aftermath.

## Key Claims

- The Army of Africa formed a major military base for the nationalist rebellion.
- Franco's Moroccan command experience and mythology strengthened his later authority.
- Mola, Sanjurjo, and Franco belonged to an Africanist officer world shaped by colonial war.
- Colonial violence supplied a repertoire that could be redirected into domestic conflict.
- Morocco was an enabling institutional prehistory, not a sufficient cause of civil war.
- The coup's failure to capture the whole country created the territorial and political division of civil war.
- Foreign state support and international volunteers made the conflict international as well as domestic.

## Evidence

- Personnel and forces: [[282-morocco-the-rif-war-glt7389759975]] links Franco, Mola, Sanjurjo, regulares, and the Foreign Legion to the Moroccan campaign and later rebellion.
- Political culture: [[282-morocco-the-rif-war-glt7389759975]] describes humiliation, distrust of politicians, reactionary identity, and belief in extreme violence among Africanist officers.
- Leadership outcome: [[282-morocco-the-rif-war-glt7389759975]] says Franco became the rebellion's central face after other senior figures died.
- Conflict outline: the bonus clip in [[216-pigeons-glt2770080110]] links the failed coup to Nationalist-Republican division, foreign backing, volunteers, massacres, and mass graves.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Neither source provides a comprehensive history of the Civil War. The Morocco-to-Spain line must not displace independent political, economic, regional, ideological, and international causes, while the bonus clip is an appended introduction framed through Hemingway rather than a sustained treatment. The balance, timing, motives, and effect of foreign intervention and the scale and responsibility for violence remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Added a bounded direct outline of the failed coup, divided zones, international support, and mass violence.
- Preserved the Rif and Army of Africa account as an important prehistory rather than a single-cause explanation.
