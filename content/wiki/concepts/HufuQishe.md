# 胡服骑射 / Hu Fu Qi She

胡服骑射 / Hu Fu Qi She is the reform [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-58-zhaowulingwang-ruhe-dazao-qiangguo-lq9mgpib0whh0w-jkv0kxcafbnnz]] attaches to [[ZhaoWulingwang|赵武灵王]]'s plan to strengthen [[ZhaoState|赵国]]. The episode treats it as more than a costume change: Zhao Wuling Wang wants to take [[ZhongshanState|中山国]] and northern Hu lands, so he has to change the way Zhao soldiers move and fight.

The military logic is direct. Wide robes and chariot-centered fighting are slow, costly, and awkward in the terrain and tactical environment Zhao faces. Hu clothing means shorter garments and narrower sleeves; riding and shooting mean soldiers can move on horseback, fight at range with bows, and close with shorter weapons when needed.

The political logic is just as important. Zhao Wuling Wang first wears the Hu clothing himself, then faces resistance from [[ZhaoChengWarringStates|赵成]] and the aristocracy. In this source, military modernization depends on symbolic compliance: adopting frontier practice threatens elite identity, but refusing it leaves Zhao with an older and less mobile battlefield form.

## Key Claims
- The reform links technology, clothing, and tactics rather than treating dress as a standalone cultural gesture.
- Zhao Wuling Wang frames ridicule as the cost of seeing what others miss.
- The target is strategic expansion: northern Hu land and Zhongshan, not generic fashion imitation.
- Elite resistance arises because the reform crosses the Hua-Hu cultural boundary and public ritual norms.
- The ruler's family becomes the first enforcement surface because kin compliance can model aristocratic compliance.
