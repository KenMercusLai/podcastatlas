# Anti-Altruism as Moral-Coercion Critique / 反利他主义的道德胁迫批判

Anti-Altruism as Moral-Coercion Critique / 反利他主义的道德胁迫批判 names the specific [[AynRand|Ayn Rand / 安·兰德]] argument highlighted by [[09-atelasi-songsongjian-anlande-zhishi-shuangwen-zuojia-ma-543277898]]: "selfishness" should not automatically mean immorality, and "altruism" should not automatically mean morality. The source says Rand tries to make moral judgment depend on rational and objective evaluation rather than on whether an act performs sacrifice.

The episode's strongest version of the critique is about coercion. When altruism becomes a social rule, institutional demand, or moral badge, it can be used to require other people to surrender work, value, time, taste, or selfhood while the demander claims virtue. In [[TheFountainhead|The Fountainhead / 《源泉》]], this appears through Ellsworth Toohey's language of humility and service; in [[AtlasShrugged|Atlas Shrugged / 《阿特拉斯耸耸肩》]], it becomes a society-wide demand that creators keep producing for those who shame them.

The source also marks the risk. A critique of coerced altruism can protect self-possession, but it can slide into contempt, social Darwinist feeling, or indifference to real dependency when it becomes a total doctrine. That danger connects the concept to [[Objectivism]], [[PureRationalityTrap]], and [[IdeologicalTotalizationRisk]].

## Key Claims
- The source separates voluntary care from moralized demands for sacrifice.
- Rand's "selfishness" is presented as a challenge to inherited moral language rather than simple permission to exploit others.
- The critique is useful when it exposes people who use virtue language to gain power over creators.
- The critique becomes dangerous when it erases vulnerability, interdependence, and the practical need for mutual care.
