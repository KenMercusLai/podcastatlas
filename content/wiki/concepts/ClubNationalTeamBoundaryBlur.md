## Definition
Club-national team boundary blur is the governance risk that a professional club's resources, signings, coach appointments, or player-management decisions become subordinated to national-team or political goals.

## Current Synthesis
The Evergrande case gives a Chinese football version of sports institutional blur. The source accepts that [[MarcelloLippi]]'s national-team tenure and "为国养士" investments may have produced short-term sporting benefits, but it treats the process as damaging because club, association, and national-team functions became difficult to separate. The core problem is not patriotism or national-team importance; it is whether professional league actors can retain independent rules, contracts, incentives, and accountability.

## Key Claims
- A strong club can become a national-team instrument when administrators treat its roster, coach, and budget as available public resources.
- Short-term national-team performance can coexist with long-term institutional damage.
- Naturalized-player spending and national-team-oriented signings are hard to evaluate through ordinary club ROI.
- Professional leagues need boundary clarity between club competition and national-team policy.
- Boundary blur can make a club's football project depend on political demand rather than sustainable sporting economics.

## Evidence
- Lippi transition: [[vol-225-hengda-de-zuqiu-yichan-ft-panweili-gkwrijeozrpkafcpbatn-ivb]] says the transfer of the Evergrande model into the national team began around Lippi's late-2016 national-team role.
- Mixed performance judgment: [[vol-225-hengda-de-zuqiu-yichan-ft-panweili-gkwrijeozrpkafcpbatn-ivb]] acknowledges national-team effects while criticizing the institutional procedure.
- Naturalization spending: [[vol-225-hengda-de-zuqiu-yichan-ft-panweili-gkwrijeozrpkafcpbatn-ivb]] discusses Alan, Luo Guofu, Fernandinho, Elkeson, Xiao Taotao, and reported Brazil-player naturalization costs above 800 million yuan.
- Separation principle: [[vol-225-hengda-de-zuqiu-yichan-ft-panweili-gkwrijeozrpkafcpbatn-ivb]] states that professional league football and national-team football should be separated.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not deny that national teams are socially and politically important. It also does not prove every national-team-linked investment was ineffective; the qualification is institutional, not purely sporting.
