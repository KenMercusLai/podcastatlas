## Definition
Administrative information conformity is the distortion that occurs when official communication is rewarded for safety, polish, and alignment more than for exposing operational problems.

## Current Synthesis
The Qing memorial system in the source illustrates a real tradeoff. Direct and secret channels can reduce public factional performance, yet restricted speaking rights, fear of private-motive accusations, and concentrated review can make officials avoid responsibility. “圆美” then becomes an information pathology: smooth documents circulate while difficult facts fail to travel upward.

Hanji 379-1 adds a sharper coercive version. When local officials face death if they fail to discover bandits or cannot capture every member, truthful reporting becomes an admission of potentially fatal failure. Commandery and county officials therefore conceal the disorder, giving the center cleaner reports precisely as public order worsens. Across the two cases, the mechanism is not a timeless cultural preference for euphemism; it is incentive-sensitive information filtering under blame and punishment.

## Key Claims
- Channel secrecy can reduce one political distortion while creating another.
- The right to report is also a responsibility structure; narrow access makes intermediaries more cautious.
- Blame-sensitive review encourages agreement, rejection without ownership, or polished ambiguity.
- Decision quality declines when local implementation facts cannot safely reach the center.
- Impossible performance liability can turn non-reporting into bureaucratic self-preservation and leave the center governing from false calm.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The Qing source does not show that publicity is always better: open memorials could become reputational performance and factional weapons. Hanji 379-1 does not quantify the prevalence of concealment or reconstruct the exact law. The concept is therefore an incentive-and-channel diagnosis, not a simple argument for full openness or immunity from accountability.

## What Changed
- Added a bounded model of how secrecy, restricted access, blame, and polished prose can degrade administrative truthfulness.
- Added Hanji 379-1's punitive variant, where impossible capture liability converts truthful incident reporting into a mortal risk.
