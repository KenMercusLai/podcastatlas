## Definition

Administrative information conformity is the distortion that occurs when official communication is rewarded for safety, polish, and alignment more than for exposing operational problems.

## Current Synthesis

The three sources show different routes to the same information failure. Qing direct and secret memorial channels can reduce public factional performance, yet restricted speaking rights, concentrated review, and private-motive accusations reward smooth “圆美” prose. Under Han Wudi, impossible death liability for incomplete bandit suppression makes truthful reporting an admission of potentially fatal failure, so local officials conceal worsening disorder. Under Qin Er Shi, the ruler directly imprisons bearers of unwelcome rebellion news, after which later officials downgrade a large revolt to scattered banditry already under control.

The common mechanism is incentive-sensitive filtering rather than a timeless preference for euphemism. When accurate bad news creates personal danger, the center receives cleaner reports as reality deteriorates. Channel design, performance liability, and ruler retaliation differ, but each breaks the link between local observation and central decision.

## Key Claims

- Information channels can reduce one distortion while creating another when access and responsibility are narrowly concentrated.
- Blame-sensitive review encourages agreement, rejection without ownership, polished ambiguity, or silence.
- Impossible performance liability turns non-reporting into bureaucratic self-preservation.
- Direct punishment of bad-news messengers rapidly teaches later officials to report the ruler's preferred reality.
- Decision quality declines when local implementation or crisis facts cannot safely reach the center.

## Evidence

### Channel and language incentives

- [[501-cong-hongliangji-gongzizhen-dao-weiyuan-qingzhongye-de-ren-ruhe-kandai-ziji-guojia-weilai-lgu0h9kaxmfwzoe1byxeyq7png9o|忽左忽右 501]] compares public Ming memorial practice with Qing direct and secret memorials, and defines “圆美” as safe, polished language that avoids engaging difficult problems.

### Punitive liability and concealment

- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-379-1-hanwudi-zhifa-yanke-weihe-faner-shi-daozei-yuelaiyueduo-lpseslnnocr4ex2yudx1bvcy5r0f|Hanji 379-1]] says officials threatened with death for incomplete bandit suppression conceal activity they cannot eliminate.

### Ruler retaliation and false reassurance

- [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-128-4-ta-jingran-shi-zhugeliang-de-zuxian-lkof-myhhubvksvl67i5myj1v3he|Qinji 128-4]] says Hu Hai imprisons officials who accurately report the eastern rebellion, after which later reporters call it minor banditry already under control.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The Qing source does not show that publicity is always better: open memorials can become reputational performance and factional weapons. Hanji 379-1 does not quantify concealment or independently establish the exact law. Qinji 128-4 gives a compact narrative sequence rather than a complete reconstruction of Qin reporting institutions or the timing of central response. The concept is therefore an incentive-and-channel diagnosis, not an argument for costless reporting, universal openness, or immunity from accountability.

## What Changed

- Added Qin Er Shi's direct bad-news punishment as a fast, ruler-driven route to official minimization.
- Distinguished retaliation against truthful messengers from the earlier channel-design and impossible-liability variants.
- Strengthened the cross-case judgment that false calm can be produced by several different institutional incentives.
