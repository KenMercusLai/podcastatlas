# Sichuan Cuisine / 川菜

Sichuan cuisine / 川菜 is the food world through which [[66-yuchi-yu-huajiao-liao-chi-de-wo-ke-jiu-bu-kun-le-659957536]] reads [[FuchsiaDunlop|胡霞 / Fuchsia Dunlop]]'s [[SharkFinAndSichuanPepper|《鱼翅与花椒》]]. The episode centers [[Chengdu|成都]] dan dan noodles, huajiao, mapo tofu affection, rabbit heads, brain, offal, market buying, and cook relationships, presenting 川菜 as an everyday knowledge system rather than a dish list.

The source's 川菜 frame is also a learning problem. To appreciate it, Dunlop has to adjust to heat, numbing sensation, textures, animal parts, freshness norms, and social eating expectations. That makes [[SichuanCuisine|川菜]] a concrete case of [[CulinaryGrammar]].

[[ep245-yinian-yidu-xunwei-zhilv-jinnian-qu-chi-chuangyi-cai-li9wtavemokcckialbbj7unpmqsz]] adds a contemporary innovation layer. [[Heimai|黑麦]] argues that young Sichuan chefs often renew the cuisine from inside the tradition: pork-trotter soup can borrow bak kut teh structure, fish-fragrant chicken can use brining and yogurt marinade, thick-cut tongue can combine low-temperature treatment with Sichuan condiments, and precise extraction can make pepper aroma more portable.

## Key Claims
- 川菜 in the episode is sensory but also social: vendors, cooks, markets, and repeated meals create knowledge.
- Huajiao is treated as a turning point in Dunlop's appetite, not just as a seasoning.
- Unfamiliar ingredients such as brain, rabbit head, offal, duck intestine, and preserved egg reveal the gap between disgust and learned [[FoodTextureAesthetics|口感]].
- The source resists reducing 川菜 to national pride; it uses it to ask how taste becomes identity and how criticism can coexist with love.
- EP245 shows that a strong cuisine can innovate through technique and internal recombination rather than needing to adopt an external "fusion" identity.
