## Definition

Wuzhu coin restoration / 五铢钱恢复 is the Guangwu-era monetary-policy act in which [[LiuXiu|刘秀]] restores the five-zhu coin after [[MaYuanEasternHan|马援]] recommends it as a familiar and convenient currency for common use.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 718 gives the current wiki's first direct Eastern Han five-zhu restoration layer. Ma Yuan submits a memorial arguing that five-zhu coins are convenient for the people, and Liu Xiu adopts the recommendation. The note sits beside bandit pacification, Jiaozhi revolt, land-and-grain fraud punishment, and frontier diplomacy, so monetary restoration appears as one part of wider postwar normalization.

The concept's current claim is intentionally narrow. It does not yet reconstruct Han currency history, minting volume, metallurgical standard, or market effects. It records a governance judgment: after disruptive regimes and frontier disorder, restoring a familiar coinage is presented as a way to make exchange more ordinary and administratively legible.

## Key Claims

- Monetary restoration is treated as practical stabilization, not only fiscal symbolism.
- Ma Yuan appears as a policy adviser, not only a frontier general, in this episode.
- Liu Xiu's adoption of the recommendation fits the broader 40 CE normalization sequence.
- The source grounds the decision in convenience for common use.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source gives a policy notice rather than an economic assessment. It does not establish how quickly the restored coins circulated, whether counterfeiting or debasement changed, how the decision related to Wang Mang's currency reforms, or what regional variation followed. Those claims remain outside the current evidence.
