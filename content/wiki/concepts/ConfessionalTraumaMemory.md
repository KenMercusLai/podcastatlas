# Confessional Trauma Memory

Confessional trauma memory is the pattern where religious violence becomes a usable political memory for people outside the original event. [[692-elizabeth-i-vs-the-catholics-a-massacre-in-paris-part-2-glt4665799025]] grounds the concept in English Protestant readings of the [[StBartholomewsDayMassacre]].

The episode does not treat Paris only as French violence. [[ChristopherMarlowe]]'s later theatrical memory, [[FrancisWalsingham]]'s direct experience as ambassador, [[RobertBeale]]'s warning, papal celebration, and [[WilliamCecil|William Cecil / Lord Burleigh]]'s conspiracy reading all make the massacre a warning about what might happen in [[England]].

This concept sits upstream of [[ConfessionalWarEscalation]]. It explains why policy makers may treat religious opponents as future perpetrators before an equivalent local event has happened. In the source, the memory of Paris helps convert Catholic nonconformity, papal authority, [[MaryQueenOfScots|Mary, Queen of Scots]]' claim, and priest missions into a security imagination.
