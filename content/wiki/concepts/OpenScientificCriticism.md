# Open Scientific Criticism

## Definition
Open scientific criticism is a publication and evaluation model that exposes claims, methods, data, code, or working artifacts to broader and faster scrutiny while retaining explicit standards for evidence, correction, and accountability.

## Current Synthesis
[[LexFridman]] and [[AndrewHuberman]] criticize conventional peer review for slow turnaround, unpaid labor, narrow reviewer perspectives, and friction between fast public communication and formal publication. They favor a more continuous form of criticism, especially in engineering and software, where other researchers or users can test an artifact directly. The useful proposal is not review-free science but a shift from one pre-publication gate toward visible post-publication challenge and revision.

The model's strength depends on what can actually be inspected. Code, data, methods, preregistration, error reports, and version history make criticism more actionable; popularity, speed, or a large comment volume do not by themselves establish reliability. Fields involving patient privacy, dangerous methods, long follow-up, scarce instruments, or subtle causal inference may need different mixtures of closed review, open materials, replication, and moderated criticism.

## Key Claims
- Slow and unpaid review can constrain both speed and diversity of scientific criticism.
- Preprints and public discussion can surface errors earlier than journal publication alone.
- Engineering artifacts can sometimes be evaluated through direct use, testing, and failure reports.
- Open criticism complements rather than automatically replaces expert review and replication.
- Inspectable materials and accountable revision matter more than comment volume or popularity.

## Evidence
- Review-incentive critique: [[navigating-conflict-finding-purpose-maintaining-drive-dr-lex-fridman-scim7125421200]] identifies unpaid reviewing, limited perspectives, and delay as weaknesses of conventional review.
- Crowdsourced alternative: [[navigating-conflict-finding-purpose-maintaining-drive-dr-lex-fridman-scim7125421200]] records Fridman's preference for faster, broader criticism.
- Engineering distinction: [[navigating-conflict-finding-purpose-maintaining-drive-dr-lex-fridman-scim7125421200]] argues that tools and systems can sometimes be tested through use in ways unavailable to less directly executable claims.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode does not provide comparative outcome evidence for open versus conventional review. Public criticism can reward visibility, speed, status, harassment, or ideological alignment rather than accuracy. Some work cannot safely expose data or methods, and post-publication correction may come after clinical, policy, financial, or reputational harm. The Watson-Crick publication anecdote is historical context, not proof that peer review is generally unnecessary.

## What Changed
- Created a bounded model of continuous criticism that retains expert review, replication, and field-specific safeguards.
- Distinguished direct artifact testing from evaluation of non-executable or high-risk claims.
