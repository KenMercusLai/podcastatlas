# Intervention Mechanism Learning

## Definition
Intervention mechanism learning is the use of successful, null, or counterintuitive intervention results to revise an explanation of how a problem is produced and what change can affect it.

## Current Synthesis
The episode distinguishes asking whether a program works from asking what its result teaches about the problem. A second teacher or smaller class can fail when both reproduce syllabus-driven instruction that has already lost many pupils. Reliable vaccination supply can improve uptake without solving postponement, while a small immediate incentive can reveal the importance of attention and acting today. Free bed nets and cash transfers can challenge stories that payment creates value or assistance destroys effort.

This is disciplined theory revision, not automatic mechanism proof. An outcome can eliminate or weaken one account while leaving several alternatives compatible with the data. Mechanism learning becomes stronger when experiments measure intermediate behavior, compare rival designs, test replication and transfer, and separate implementation failure from theory failure.

## Key Claims
- Null results can expose a mismatch between added resources and the operating model using them.
- Small design changes can reveal attention, timing, convenience, or incentive mechanisms hidden by moralized explanations.
- Counterintuitive outcomes can improve theory rather than merely select a winning program.
- A trial result usually narrows causal stories more reliably than it proves a single mechanism.
- Mechanism claims require intermediate measures, rival tests, and transfer checks beyond the headline effect.

## Evidence
- Instructional mismatch - [[putting-our-assumptions-to-the-test]] links failed teacher and class-size additions to continued syllabus teaching above many pupils' current level.
- Attention and timing - [[putting-our-assumptions-to-the-test]] uses predictable vaccination access and a lentil incentive to distinguish supply from immediate motivation and postponement.
- Value and work assumptions - [[putting-our-assumptions-to-the-test]] reports that bed-net use did not depend on price and cash transfers did not reduce work.
- Framing boundary - [[putting-our-assumptions-to-the-test]] explicitly warns that researchers may test the wrong or incomplete set of possibilities.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source summarizes each study without enough methodological detail to adjudicate all rival mechanisms. A null result can arise from low power, weak implementation, contamination, poor measurement, or a genuinely ineffective theory. A successful incentive may alter behavior without identifying the only reason for it, and a mechanism found in one setting may not transfer to another.

## What Changed
- Created a focused concept separating policy-effect estimation from theory revision.
- Preserved experimental framing and alternative-mechanism limits as part of the method.
