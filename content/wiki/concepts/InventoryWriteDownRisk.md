# Inventory Write-Down Risk

## Definition
Inventory write-down risk is the possibility that recorded stock will not recover its carrying value because demand, usability, permission to sell, freshness or verification fails. Discounting and slow turnover are warning signs, not necessarily a booked impairment.

## Current Synthesis
Accounting scrutiny begins with inventory age, salability and cash conversion. Adidas supplies a concrete brand-shock and discounting case, but the registered note does not establish a booked Yeezy impairment provision; cinema merchandise, bespoke shared bikes and grocery illustrate different prospective pathways. Receivables owed by a failed buyer are not themselves inventory.

## Key Claims
- Reported stock value must be tested against demand, verifiability and the prospect of cash recovery.
- Fashion lead times and a terminated licensed collaboration can turn shortage into stranded, discounted goods.
- Event-specific licensed merchandise faces a narrow release window even when scarcity occasionally creates resale premiums.
- Bespoke shared-bike hardware can lose alternative-use value, separately from suppliers' unpaid receivables.
- Fresh-grocery and apparel expansion expose spoilage, shrinkage and unsold-SKU risk beneath GMV growth.

## Evidence
- The financial-statement lesson joins balance-sheet inventory to profit and operating cash; [[BestBuy]] is used for electronics obsolescence and [[Zhangzidao]] for difficult-to-verify sea-product stock. Those are warning examples rather than a fresh audit of either company's impairment charge. [[ep86-mianzi-dizi-rizi-caibao-zhi-jiang-zhe-san-jian-shi-lukmnlkkuxvrvv12frosd9teg7bj]]
- [[Adidas]]' 8–9-month outsourced-production lead time and pandemic shipping shifts preceded excess stock and 2022 discounting. Ending [[KanyeWest|Kanye West]]'s [[Yeezy]] partnership changed the sale prospects of committed goods; the registered note describes inventory pressure, batch sales, repaired dealer channels and [[BjornGulden|Gulden]]'s course correction, not a quantified or confirmed accounting provision or instant full recovery. [[vol-271-a-di-da-si-ruhe-chudi-fantan-1007333155]]
- [[CustomMoviePopcornBuckets|Licensed movie buckets]] for [[TheOdyssey|The Odyssey]] include Trojan-horse and [[IMAX]]-camera forms. Studios' approvals and theater ordering months before release make a wrong film/character forecast expensive; the reported [[AMCTheatres|AMC]] R2-D2 and Dune successes do not remove unsold-bucket risk. [[aodesai-ba-baomihua-zhuangjin-teluoyi-muma-haolaiwu-dapian-weihe-zhadui-dingzhi-baomihuatong-1004599628]]
- [[Mobike]]'s reinforced frame, solid tire and fixed electronic lock made platform bikes harder to repurpose; [[Ofo|OFO]] and Mobike's order surge also exposed manufacturers to unpaid bills. The latter is [[ReceivablesRisk|receivables]], not proof of an inventory write-down. [[no-199-zixingche-200-nian-973489465]]
- [[Vancl]]'s growing apparel SKUs and [[Missfresh]]'s perishables appear in a wider ecommerce failure history. [[Vipshop]]'s focused tail-stock clearing is a counterexample to treating all inventory-heavy channels as failures; cold chain, shrinkage and working capital still matter for grocery. ## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Inventory pressure, a promotional discount, accounting impairment and a complete write-off are distinct. The popcorn-bucket forecast is a single episode's industry account; no case-wide ledger of losses is provided. The shared-bike episode also includes receivables and hardware reuse, which must not be booked as the same risk. Profit and stock figures in these shows are reported rather than independently checked against issuer filings.

## What Changed
- Distinguished Adidas's reported stock pressure from any unverified booked impairment and from prospective exposure in other cases.
- Separated resale value of bespoke bikes from supplier credit losses.
