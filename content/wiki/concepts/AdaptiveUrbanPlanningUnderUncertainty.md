# Adaptive Urban Planning Under Uncertainty / 不确定性下的弹性城市规划

## Definition

Adaptive urban planning under uncertainty keeps collective direction and infrastructure coordination while limiting dependence on precise long-range forecasts and preserving room for revision, mixed use, and unplanned activity.

## Current Synthesis

The sources do not argue against planning. EP128 explains why fifteen-year master plans remain necessary to translate economic and social direction into centers, transport, land use, and public facilities, while shorter plans and annual work implement them. The later Beijing route shows the forecast problem in practice: construction cycles are long enough for pandemics, AI, demographics, and economic conditions to invalidate confident assumptions, and visible gains in greenery, heritage repair, and river access coexist with storefront displacement and standardized streetscapes.

The resulting standard is direction plus revision capacity. Set public goals, protect ecological and historical assets, and coordinate infrastructure, but use staged implementation, mixed use, time-sharing, distributed centers, and reversible interventions where possible. Data can improve observation without making prediction infallible.

## Key Claims

- Long construction cycles expose city plans to shocks that cannot be forecast reliably.
- Planning remains necessary for infrastructure, public goods, and coordination despite forecast limits.
- Master plans require shorter implementation cycles and observation that can revise assumptions.
- Reversible or adaptable interventions reduce the cost of mistaken assumptions.
- Informal commercial and cultural activity supplies information that top-down plans may miss.
- Ecological, mixed-use, and flexible public-space improvements can be robust under several futures, though they still carry opportunity costs.

## Evidence

- Planning horizon: [[406-cong-jingshan-qi-dao-sanlitun-zai-jietou-xunzhao-beijing-de-shinian-henji-shizhounian-tebie-jiemu-2f9cb979]] contrasts decade-scale construction with unforeseen pandemic, AI, population, and economic changes.
- Planning hierarchy: [[ep128-shiwunianyigao-de-chengshi-guihua-jiujing-zai-guihua-shenme-cmcocqgc36nfb4cpjgba98wr|EP128]] describes approximately fifteen-year master plans implemented through five-year and annual plans.
- Mixed outcomes: the same route records valued road greening and Liangma River repair alongside commercial displacement and standardized street fronts. [[406-cong-jingshan-qi-dao-sanlitun-zai-jietou-xunzhao-beijing-de-shinian-henji-shizhounian-tebie-jiemu-2f9cb979]]
- Direction with revision: the episode links many changes to the 2016-era capital plan but notes later reflection and adjustment without reversal of the overall direction. [[406-cong-jingshan-qi-dao-sanlitun-zai-jietou-xunzhao-beijing-de-shinian-henji-shizhounian-tebie-jiemu-2f9cb979]]
- Flexible response: EP128 connects remote work and public-health shocks to changing occupancy, time-sharing, crowd management, and rapid conversion of ordinary spaces. [[ep128-shiwunianyigao-de-chengshi-guihua-jiujing-zai-guihua-shenme-cmcocqgc36nfb4cpjgba98wr]]

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

“Flexibility” can become an excuse for weak commitments or under-provision if it is not paired with clear public goals. One source provides an interpretive Beijing route and the other a broad 2020 practitioner conversation; neither compares the fiscal, distributional, accessibility, or climate performance of alternative planning systems.

## What Changed

- Added the hierarchy from long-range direction to shorter implementation and revision.
- Extended adaptability from informal urban life to mixed use, time-sharing, and emergency conversion.
