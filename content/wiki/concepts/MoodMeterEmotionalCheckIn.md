# Mood Meter Emotional Check-In

## Definition
Mood Meter emotional check-in is [[MarcBrackett]]'s source-scoped practice of locating a present emotional state on two dimensions, pleasantness and energy, before selecting a more precise label or regulation strategy.

## Current Synthesis
The Mood Meter divides experience into four color quadrants: yellow for pleasant high energy, green for pleasant low energy, blue for unpleasant low energy, and red for unpleasant high energy. The map is descriptive rather than moral. It makes unpleasant emotion observable without assuming that the state is bad, pathological, or immediately in need of fixing.

Its practical value lies in sequence. Broad coordinates help a person notice where they are; [[EmotionalGranularity]] then differentiates the state; [[RULERFramework]] adds cause, expression, and regulation judgment. Repeated check-ins can also reveal patterns by person, place, activity, and time, but the source does not establish a validated self-treatment protocol.

The earlier Huberman solo episode uses the same energy-and-pleasantness map as a practical entry point and places it within a broader [[EmotionStateCoordinateModel]] that also asks whether attention is directed inward or outward. This strengthens the distinction between describing a state and explaining it: a quadrant can locate felt energy and valence, while attentional direction, context, bodily condition, and precise labeling remain additional questions.

## Key Claims
- Pleasantness and energy provide a simple first map of emotional state.
- The four quadrants describe experience without ranking pleasant emotion as good and unpleasant emotion as bad.
- A check-in creates space between feeling and automatic action.
- Broad mapping should lead toward more precise labeling rather than replace it.
- Not every unpleasant state needs to be changed; the relevant question is whether the state fits the task, context, and goal.

## Evidence
- Two-axis map - [[how-to-increase-your-emotional-intelligence-dr-marc-brackett-scim6059113217]] describes the Mood Meter through pleasantness and energy.
- Quadrant meanings - [[how-to-increase-your-emotional-intelligence-dr-marc-brackett-scim6059113217]] assigns yellow, green, blue, and red to the four pleasantness-energy combinations.
- Nonjudgment boundary - [[how-to-increase-your-emotional-intelligence-dr-marc-brackett-scim6059113217]] stresses that unpleasant emotions are not bad emotions and do not always need fixing.
- Pattern tracking - [[how-to-increase-your-emotional-intelligence-dr-marc-brackett-scim6059113217]] describes the How We Feel app as tracking emotion by people, place, and activity while offering strategies.
- Earlier dimensional use - [[the-science-of-emotions-relationships-scim6339543648]] presents the Mood Meter as an energy-and-pleasantness check-in and adds inward-versus-outward attention as a separate dimension.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source explains the tool in a public interview rather than presenting validation data, measurement reliability, or outcome effects. A quadrant cannot by itself identify causes, diagnose a condition, or determine the right intervention, and repeated tracking may be unhelpful if it becomes rumination or compulsive monitoring.

## What Changed
- Added the earlier solo-episode use of the two-axis map and separated it from the additional attention-direction dimension.
