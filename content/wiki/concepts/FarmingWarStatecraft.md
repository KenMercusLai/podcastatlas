# Farming-War Statecraft / 耕战逻辑

Farming-war statecraft / 耕战逻辑 is the Qin reform principle that state strength and personal advancement should be routed through agriculture and war. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-13-gongsunyang-keke-bianfa-re-shashen-1-lgmjapc2eecefbdshnpenzfcv6ga]] supplies the institutional base: [[ShangYangReforms|商鞅变法]] rewards agricultural and textile production while making battlefield achievement convertible into status through the [[MilitaryMeritRankSystem|军功爵制]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-22-gongsunyang-zha-wei-gongzi-ang-lss1l7lojfwgwdtcje7m5ijodpxi]] names the logic more directly. The episode asks why [[ShangYang|公孙鞅 / 商鞅]], already a central minister under [[QinXiaogong|秦孝公]], would personally command against [[WeiState|魏国]]. Its answer is that farming has a lower ceiling for visible personal merit, while war can generate large reward, rank, and political capital in [[QinState|秦国]].

The concept therefore links domestic reform to external expansion. Qin does not merely fight because it has an army; the reform system teaches soldiers and officials that battle success is the highest-status contribution. That helps explain why Shang Yang's proposed peaceful withdrawal to [[GongziAng|公子卬 / 公子昂]] should have looked improbable: it would contradict the very incentive architecture he built.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-23-weihuiwang-hui-buyong-gongshu-zhi-yan-lveaqklo7oncmqudgmdk7l03qhsf]] shows the reward side. The victory after Gongzi Ang's capture earns Shang Yang the [[Shangyu|商於]] fief and 商君 title, making the logic concrete as a conversion of war outcome into place, status, and political capital.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-93-zai-mengli-cangzhe-toutian-gaiming-2-lrlulw0b6v0fb2xpr2i5ifem1h4n]] adds an outside assessment of the same Qin capacity. [[ZhaoBao|赵豹 / 平阳君]]'s fuller warning against accepting [[Shangdang|上党]] says Qin's cattle-assisted farming improves agricultural output while transport and reward systems lower campaign friction and raise morale. The episode therefore shows farming-war statecraft as something rival advisers must price before provoking Qin.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-95-changping-zhi-zhan-hou-qinguo-weishenme-meiyou-mie-le-zhaoguo-2-lutsvwvnwzsezoorhe-vu7yjthyx]] adds a post-Changping political reading through [[KongBin|孔斌]]. Kong Bin warns [[WeiState|魏国]] that Qin's victory over [[ZhaoState|赵国]] will not satisfy Qin, because a state organized around post-[[ShangYangReforms|商鞅变法]] war incentives must keep generating campaigns, rewards, and territorial gain. The concept therefore explains not only Qin's military efficiency but why neighboring states should not expect Qin to stop after one neighbor falls.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-115-1-jingke-ciqin-sihu-you-dianer-caoshuai-le-lgz1adsn1t8jjiaobmtfedpr-ixn]] uses the same logic to limit the strategic value of [[JingKe|荆轲]]'s assassination. The episode argues that even killing [[QinShiHuang|嬴政 / 秦始皇]] would not easily reverse unification because Qin's expansion was already supported by institutions and incentives created after [[ShangYang|商鞅]]. Farming-war statecraft thus becomes a reason a weak state's ruler-killing shortcut may buy time without stopping the underlying war machine.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-123-2-gudai-shiqi-de-daochamen-daodi-you-duo-weixian-lgcknwubwm3uzqmzgbgdlxlvcwxy]] carries that machine past unification. The episode argues that once the six states become Qin commanderies, the old enemy field disappears but the social expectation of war rewards remains. Qin's southern and northern campaigns therefore become [[PostUnificationWarInertia|统一后战争惯性]]: the farming side is still slow and low-ceiling for ambitious subjects, while frontier war can absorb risk-seeking soldiers and marginalized groups under [[MarginalStatusConscription|边缘身份征发]].

## Key Claims
- Agriculture and war are treated as the privileged contributions to state capacity.
- Farming feeds and stabilizes the state, but war has the higher personal reward ceiling.
- [[MilitaryMeritRankSystem|军功爵制]] turns battlefield performance into rank, office access, and political capital.
- A reformer operating inside this system faces pressure to produce visible military results, not only administrative reforms.
- The same incentive logic makes empty-handed peace suspicious when Qin is the invading side.
- Successful war can become land, title, and reputation for the commander, not only territorial gain for the state.
- Qin's agricultural and military incentive base can appear inside another state's risk calculation even when the immediate issue is a diplomatic gift.
- A farming-war state can create continuing political demand for campaigns, making "Qin will stop after this victory" a weak assumption for neighboring states.
- Qinji 115-1 extends that point to assassination counterfactuals: replacing or killing one ruler does not automatically erase the incentive system that keeps a conquest state moving.
- Qinji 123-2 extends the logic after unification: once former enemies become internal subjects, frontier campaigns can function as outlets for a war-reward society.
