# Farming-War Statecraft / 耕战逻辑

Farming-war statecraft / 耕战逻辑 is the Qin reform principle that state strength and personal advancement should be routed through agriculture and war. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-13-gongsunyang-keke-bianfa-re-shashen-1-lgmjapc2eecefbdshnpenzfcv6ga]] supplies the institutional base: [[ShangYangReforms|商鞅变法]] rewards agricultural and textile production while making battlefield achievement convertible into status through the [[MilitaryMeritRankSystem|军功爵制]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-22-gongsunyang-zha-wei-gongzi-ang-lss1l7lojfwgwdtcje7m5ijodpxi]] names the logic more directly. The episode asks why [[ShangYang|公孙鞅 / 商鞅]], already a central minister under [[QinXiaogong|秦孝公]], would personally command against [[WeiState|魏国]]. Its answer is that farming has a lower ceiling for visible personal merit, while war can generate large reward, rank, and political capital in [[QinState|秦国]].

The concept therefore links domestic reform to external expansion. Qin does not merely fight because it has an army; the reform system teaches soldiers and officials that battle success is the highest-status contribution. That helps explain why Shang Yang's proposed peaceful withdrawal to [[GongziAng|公子卬 / 公子昂]] should have looked improbable: it would contradict the very incentive architecture he built.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-23-weihuiwang-hui-buyong-gongshu-zhi-yan-lveaqklo7oncmqudgmdk7l03qhsf]] shows the reward side. The victory after Gongzi Ang's capture earns Shang Yang the [[Shangyu|商於]] fief and 商君 title, making the logic concrete as a conversion of war outcome into place, status, and political capital.

## Key Claims
- Agriculture and war are treated as the privileged contributions to state capacity.
- Farming feeds and stabilizes the state, but war has the higher personal reward ceiling.
- [[MilitaryMeritRankSystem|军功爵制]] turns battlefield performance into rank, office access, and political capital.
- A reformer operating inside this system faces pressure to produce visible military results, not only administrative reforms.
- The same incentive logic makes empty-handed peace suspicious when Qin is the invading side.
- Successful war can become land, title, and reputation for the commander, not only territorial gain for the state.
