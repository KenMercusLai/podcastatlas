# Skimpflation

Skimpflation is the pattern in [[reeses-heir-vs-chocolate-skimpflation]] where a company maintains a familiar product surface while quietly reducing the quality, amount, service level, or ingredient standard underneath. In the episode, the candidate case is some [[Reeses|Reese's]] products moving from milk chocolate and peanut butter language toward [[ChocolateCompound|chocolate compound]], chocolate candy, or peanut butter cream.

The concept sits near shrinkflation but is not identical. Shrinking a package changes quantity; skimpflation changes the product's composition or experience. That can make it harder for consumers to detect because the brand, shape, and package color may still suggest continuity while [[ChocolateLabelStandards]] reveal a different formula.

## Key Claims
- Skimpflation is a quality-change response to cost pressure, not only a visible price increase.
- It can appear in food as reformulation, ingredient substitution, service downgrading, or reduced standard compliance.
- Legal labels matter because they can expose a quality shift that packaging and brand memory might otherwise hide.
- Skimpflation creates [[ConsumerBrandMoat]] risk when customers feel a trusted product no longer matches the promise they remember.
- [[IngredientReformulationStrategy]] may be defensible for engineering reasons, but it becomes skimpflation when consumers experience it as lower quality without clear disclosure.
