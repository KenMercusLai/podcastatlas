## Definition

Tribute access gatekeeping / 朝见通道垄断 is the use of an intermediary ruler's geographic or political position to control whether smaller neighboring polities can petition, visit, or form a direct relationship with an imperial center.

## Current Synthesis

The current case begins with a negotiated frontier hierarchy. [[WimanJoseonFounder|卫满]] receives Han recognition as an outer vassal and is expected to protect the border without preventing nearby leaders from reaching the emperor. Recognition therefore gives the intermediary authority but also reserves the center's right to receive other participants directly.

Under [[UgeoWimanJoseon|卫右渠]], the episode says that the channel closes: Ugeo does not attend court himself and blocks neighboring small states from sending petitions or appearing before [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]. This is more than missing ceremony. It lets an intermediary monopolize information, status, and external alignment while denying the imperial center direct confirmation of subordinate relationships. The Han response, however, shows the danger of treating access control as a simple obedience test: [[SheHeWesternHan|涉何]]'s violence and reward convert a hierarchical dispute into reciprocal killing and war.

## Key Claims

- Frontier recognition can delegate protection while explicitly preserving third parties' access to the center.
- An intermediary can increase autonomy by controlling routes, petitions, audiences, and information between smaller polities and the center.
- Blocking access can be read by the center as a challenge to hierarchy even when no direct invasion has yet occurred.
- Restoring access through coercive envoy behavior can worsen the dispute by collapsing diplomacy into retaliation.

## Evidence

- Recognition with an access condition: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-364-2-hanwudi-da-chaoxian-de-lishi-wenti-lrid25t03ell_bs6siq6yvrj3lp9|Hanji 364-2]] says Wiman's outer-vassal agreement includes not obstructing neighboring leaders who wish to reach the Han emperor.
- Gatekeeping under Ugeo: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-364-2-hanwudi-da-chaoxian-de-lishi-wenti-lrid25t03ell_bs6siq6yvrj3lp9|Hanji 364-2]] says Ugeo blocks nearby small states from petitioning or attending the Han court.
- Escalation risk: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-364-2-hanwudi-da-chaoxian-de-lishi-wenti-lrid25t03ell_bs6siq6yvrj3lp9|Hanji 364-2]] links the failed demand to She He's killing of an escort officer, his frontier appointment, Joseon's retaliation, and the move toward war.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This is currently a one-source concept. The episode does not identify every blocked polity, reproduce the agreement, establish how often court access had previously occurred, or separate ritual attendance from trade, intelligence, and alliance functions. It supports an access-control mechanism in this case, not a claim that every tribute dispute is gatekeeping or that the blockade alone caused the war. Han court ambition, Wiman Joseon's growing power, envoy violence, frontier retaliation, and later campaign decisions remain separate contributing layers.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from the Wiman-to-Ugeo shift between delegated frontier intermediation and monopolized court access.
- Preserved coercive restoration as a failure mode rather than treating central access claims as self-validating.
