# Real-Time Livestream Labor / 实时数据直播劳动

Real-time livestream labor / 实时数据直播劳动 is the work of performing, operating, and deciding while platform feedback arrives during the session. In [[baiwan-ge-jingguan-shehui-tingshuo-ni-ye-xiang-dang-zhubo-lgd0yapclwvsessg7xnhaarpyiwy]], [[TianFeng|田峰]] says early streamers might receive broad data a week later, while current operators can watch second-level live data alongside the video feed.

That timing changes the labor itself. A host or operator can decide whether to repeat a dance, switch songs, give a member more expression time, adjust pacing, or investigate a compliance warning while the audience is still present. The anchor must also process dance movement, camera position, public chat, data cues, fan IDs, and social response at once.

## Key Claims
- Real-time metrics move platform evaluation from after-the-fact reporting into live performance.
- Operators and hosts become part of the performance because their decisions shape what the audience sees next.
- Anchors do cognitive labor while doing bodily labor: movement, rhythm, fan recognition, camera awareness, comment reading, and persona response are simultaneous.
- Second-level feedback creates faster iteration but also stronger metric pressure.
- Post-session review extends the live feedback loop into training, peer critique, new choreography, and SOP revision.
