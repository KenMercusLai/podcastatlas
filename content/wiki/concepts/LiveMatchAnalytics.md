# Live Match Analytics

Live match analytics is the use of video, match software, staff observers, and real-time data to support decisions during a soccer match. [[ep-13-soccer-analytics-through-the-lens-of-coaching]] frames it through [[BrunoSoccerCoach|Bruno]]'s contrast between older high-angle coaching observation and newer live video/data workflows.

The source treats live analytics as an extension of coaching vision, not a replacement for it. Staff can identify passing patterns, opponent overloads, build-up weaknesses, or crossing zones, but the coach still has to decide whether the finding is reliable enough to change halftime instructions, substitutions, pressing triggers, or positional responsibilities.

[[RubenAmorim]] and [[SportingLisbon]] appear as the source's clearest example: Bruno cites a coach and assistant using live information during matches to notice what is or is not working. The concept therefore sits between [[FootballEventTrackingData]], [[SportsAnalyticsStakeholderCommunication]], and [[CoachingIntegratedSoccerAnalytics]].

## Key Claims
- High-angle observation and video remain important because many tactical patterns are spatial.
- Live data can help a staff find overloaded zones, repeated passing patterns, and opponent tendencies.
- The analytic output has to arrive fast enough to affect halftime or in-game decisions.
- Coaches still need judgment about whether a live pattern is meaningful or just game noise.
- Live analytics works best as a staff process, not as one person watching a dashboard.
