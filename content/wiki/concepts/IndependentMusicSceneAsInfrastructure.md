# Independent Music Scene as Infrastructure

## Definition
An independent music scene functions as infrastructure when its rehearsal spaces, venues, survival jobs, mentors, collaborators, informal care, and repeated encounters make sustained creative practice possible.

## Current Synthesis
The Zhang Weiwei and Wutiaoren accounts show that a scene is not only an audience or aesthetic label. [[WildChildrenBand|野孩子]]'s basement rehearsal supplied method, its members supplied care and instruction, bar work supplied stage repetition and income, and 合酒吧 joined rehearsal to gathering space. [[RenkeMusician|仁科]] and [[AMaoWutiaoren|阿茂]] add a less venue-centered Guangzhou route in which street book and cutout-record trade, record stores, informal revenue sharing, MIDI work, friends, and technically constrained shows supplied livelihood, repertoire, contacts, and practice. Together the sources show both enabling density and fragility: death, regulation, epidemic disruption, neighborhood change, venue loss, and dispersal can reconfigure the infrastructure on which individual creativity depends.

## Key Claims
- Repeated proximity to working artists can turn an admired vocation into a visible, learnable practice.
- Informal care and survival support can be as consequential as formal instruction.
- Commercial or humiliating performance work may still build stage competence and keep an artist economically present.
- Venues can combine rehearsal, livelihood, discovery, collaboration, and community memory.
- Informal cultural trade can function as scene infrastructure by circulating recordings, books, knowledge, contacts, and small amounts of income.
- Scenes are fragile systems whose loss can produce artistic disorientation even when individual talent remains.

## Evidence
- Practice and mentorship: [[yinyueren-zhangweiwei-luoyonghao-women-doushi-nage-hunluan-you-weida-de-90-niandai-de-xingcunzhe-llhey1qkmnmxesjlxgledjntaov]] connects basement rehearsal, metronome discipline, instruction from 小索 and 张佺, and Zhang's accordion entry into [[WildChildrenBand|野孩子]].
- Survival and stage training: [[yinyueren-zhangweiwei-luoyonghao-women-doushi-nage-hunluan-you-weida-de-90-niandai-de-xingcunzhe-llhey1qkmnmxesjlxgledjntaov]] shows busking, bar singing, restaurant performance, and supporting work as both compromise and practical training.
- Venue and network: [[yinyueren-zhangweiwei-luoyonghao-women-doushi-nage-hunluan-you-weida-de-90-niandai-de-xingcunzhe-llhey1qkmnmxesjlxgledjntaov]] describes 合酒吧 as rehearsal base, workplace, original-music venue, and meeting point before urban and epidemic change weakened that ecology.
- Street trade and low-resource performance: [[lml-euxpi9ekjvk-ihnkfzwp4ysq-lml-euxpi9ekjvk-ihnkfzwp4ysq]] connects [[WutiaorenBand|五条人]]'s formation to book and cutout-record sales, record-store activity, MIDI production, friends, small shows, and shared or poorly understood equipment.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Both sources are participant memories centered on particular Beijing, Guangzhou, and Haifeng-linked networks. They do not establish a complete history of Chinese independent music or prove that precarity, illegality, equipment failure, and humiliation are necessary for creativity. Stable institutions, digital networks, and newer local scenes may provide different infrastructure.

## What Changed
- Added informal book and record trade as circulation, livelihood, and relationship infrastructure.
- Extended the model from a Beijing rehearsal-and-venue ecology to a Guangzhou street-trade and low-resource-performance route.
