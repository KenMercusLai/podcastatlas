# Migrant-City Belonging / 移民城市归属

## Definition

Migrant-city belonging is an urban identity formed through residence, participation, shared institutions, everyday memory, and contribution to a city whose population is largely composed of arrivals, rather than through birthplace or ancestry alone.

## Current Synthesis

[[ep138-laile-jiushi-shenzhenren-cmcoosydpdsgbanyaqbwgizp|EP138]] presents [[Shenzhen]] as a city where people can retain a family hometown, accent, cuisine, and regional background while also calling themselves Shenzhen people. The identity is cumulative: work, school uniforms, neighborhoods, urban villages, climate, leisure, and participation in a still-changing city make membership lived rather than genealogical.

This openness is structural as well as rhetorical. When most residents or their parents arrived from elsewhere, newcomers encounter fewer entrenched claims that only one lineage, dialect, or inherited local culture defines legitimate membership. Yet symbolic inclusion is not the same as durable settlement. Housing, work intensity, education, healthcare, culture, and family life decide whether an accessible identity becomes a sustainable home.

## Key Claims

- Urban belonging can be additive: a person may keep an origin identity while acquiring a Shenzhen identity.
- Shared participation and ordinary memory can substitute partly for ancestry as the basis of city membership.
- A population made largely through migration can lower the social entry threshold for later arrivals.
- Common institutions and artifacts can create local identity even when the city's modern history is comparatively short.
- Symbolic welcome remains incomplete when material conditions prevent residents from staying, forming families, or using city services.

## Evidence

- Additive identity: [[ep138-laile-jiushi-shenzhenren-cmcoosydpdsgbanyaqbwgizp|EP138]] describes speakers who still name family origins while also recognizing themselves as Shenzhen people.
- Participation and memory: [[ep138-laile-jiushi-shenzhenren-cmcoosydpdsgbanyaqbwgizp|EP138]] connects belonging to growing up, working, crossing the former special-zone boundary, wearing the common school uniform, and watching the city change.
- Low entry threshold: [[ep138-laile-jiushi-shenzhenren-cmcoosydpdsgbanyaqbwgizp|EP138]] presents widespread newcomer status and mixed regional backgrounds as reasons local ancestry and dialect carry less exclusionary force.
- Material limit: [[ep138-laile-jiushi-shenzhenren-cmcoosydpdsgbanyaqbwgizp|EP138]] contrasts “来了就是深圳人” with housing, schooling, healthcare, work-pressure, and family-settlement barriers.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept is grounded in three personal trajectories rather than a representative study of Shenzhen residents. Low perceived regional discrimination does not mean all migrants, classes, dialect groups, or service workers enter city life equally. Belonging can also be temporary, contested, or recognized only after departure. The episode's comparison with [[Guangzhou]] is a conversational contrast, not proof that one city is uniformly open and the other closed.

## What Changed

- Established an additive model of city identity grounded in participation rather than ancestry.
- Separated symbolic welcome from the material ability to remain.
