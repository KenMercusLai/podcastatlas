# Pseudo-Consensus Team Failure / 团队伪共识

## Definition
Pseudo-consensus team failure occurs when members publicly signal indifference or agreement while privately holding incompatible preferences, leaving the coordinator without the information needed to allocate scarce resources or resolve conflict.

## Current Synthesis
EP387 grounds the concept in the camper-van dispute from [[FlowerAndYouthSeason2|《花儿与少年》第二季]]. Seven travelers need to divide between a camper and a tent, but nobody clearly says where they want to sleep. “I don’t mind” sounds cooperative while concealing factional avoidance, safety concerns shared only with part of the group, and a desire not to bear responsibility for the final arrangement.

Pseudo-consensus is therefore not peace. It moves conflict from preference negotiation into mind-reading, resentment, coordinator overload, and later blame. Scarcity makes the failure sharper, but a small increase in resources would not solve the informational problem in settings where people still hide constraints and expect the organizer to infer them.

## Key Claims
- Public flexibility can conceal private constraints and incompatible preferences.
- Coordinators cannot make legitimate allocations when members withhold decision-relevant information.
- Fear of responsibility encourages people to say “anything is fine” and criticize the eventual choice afterward.
- Factions make disclosure asymmetric when safety or preference information circulates only inside a subgroup.
- Resource scarcity intensifies pseudo-consensus by making every hidden preference consequential.
- Repair begins with explicit wants, constraints, tradeoffs, and a named decision rule.

## Evidence
- Hidden preference: [[ep387-neiyu-mingzhu-huashao-2-youyouyou-shangresou-jiujing-shui-caishi-zhongji-langren-gkwrirwofzopajzxyatybpqr|EP387]] says all seven participants tell Jing Boran they are flexible while the two small groups do not want to sleep together.
- Withheld constraint: [[ep387-neiyu-mingzhu-huashao-2-youyouyou-shangresou-jiujing-shui-caishi-zhongji-langren-gkwrirwofzopajzxyatybpqr|EP387]] reports that a camper-van safety concern was shared inside one subgroup rather than with everyone.
- Coordinator overload: [[ep387-neiyu-mingzhu-huashao-2-youyouyou-shangresou-jiujing-shui-caishi-zhongji-langren-gkwrirwofzopajzxyatybpqr|EP387]] describes Jing Boran as unable to infer the real sleeping preferences and therefore postponing the conflict.
- Scarcity multiplier: [[ep387-neiyu-mingzhu-huashao-2-youyouyou-shangresou-jiujing-shui-caishi-zhongji-langren-gkwrirwofzopajzxyatybpqr|EP387]] notes that another tent or camper would have reduced the immediate stakes.
- Related backup failure: [[ep387-neiyu-mingzhu-huashao-2-youyouyou-shangresou-jiujing-shui-caishi-zhongji-langren-gkwrirwofzopajzxyatybpqr|EP387]] applies the same responsibility-avoidance lens to the search for Yang Yang.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept is currently grounded in a retrospective interpretation of one edited reality-TV case. Participants may have expressed preferences in unaired footage, and fatigue or production pressure may explain apparent passivity. Pseudo-consensus should not be inferred merely because a group later disagrees; evidence of withheld or falsely minimized preferences is required.

## What Changed
- Created the concept from EP387’s camper-van dispute analysis.
- Distinguished apparent flexibility from usable consent and preference disclosure.
- Added scarcity, factional information, and responsibility avoidance as interacting mechanisms.
