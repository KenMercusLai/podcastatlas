# Peak-End Task Memory

## Definition
Peak-end task memory is the application of the peak-end rule to repeated difficult activities: remembered experience is shaped disproportionately by an emotional peak and the ending, which can affect willingness to return.

## Current Synthesis
[[how-to-shape-your-identity-goals-dr-maya-shankar-scim1420437673]] proposes making the final minutes of an unpleasant but worthwhile task somewhat less unpleasant. The aim is not to deny the full experience or maximize every moment; it is to leave a more tolerable remembered ending that lowers resistance to the next session.

The relevant ending is subjective. A slower workout finish, a preferred cool-down, or another personally tolerable close may help one person and annoy another. The tool therefore belongs inside adaptable task design rather than as a universal protocol.

## Key Claims
- Remembered experience can differ from the average of moment-to-moment experience.
- Emotional peaks and endings may carry disproportionate weight in later recall.
- A somewhat better ending can make future repetition feel more approachable.
- The intervention is most relevant to worthwhile tasks that recur, not to making harmful conditions easier to tolerate.
- Endings should be tailored to the person's actual subjective experience.

## Evidence
- Rule and application - [[how-to-shape-your-identity-goals-dr-maya-shankar-scim1420437673]] explains the peak-end rule and applies it to workouts and other difficult recurring activities.
- Subjective boundary - [[how-to-shape-your-identity-goals-dr-maya-shankar-scim1420437673]] notes that the most favorable ending depends on the individual.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The supplied note does not identify the studies, populations, effect sizes, or duration of the memory effect. Improving an ending cannot compensate for unsafe training, abusive work, excessive load, untreated pain, or a fundamentally misaligned goal. A pleasant ending may also fail when the most intense experience dominates recall.
