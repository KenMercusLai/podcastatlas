# Forced Sterilization By Eugenics / 优生学强制绝育

## Definition

Forced sterilization by eugenics / 优生学强制绝育 is state-backed denial of reproductive autonomy to people labeled unfit in the name of heredity, public welfare, demographic duty, or racial health.

## Current Synthesis

Across the American and Nazi cases, sexual or social judgment, institutional interest, weak legal protection, and diagnostic labels made irreversible bodily coercion appear lawful. [[CarrieBuck|Carrie Buck / 卡丽·巴克]] shows how gender, class, victimization, and intelligence claims could converge on one person; later evidence undermining the labels applied to Buck and her daughter exposes the confidence and prejudice embedded in the decision.

Nazi hereditary-health law expanded the mechanism into a national administrative system. Broad medical categories and health courts staffed by a judge and doctors framed compulsory sterilization as scientific prevention rather than punishment. The sequence also shows escalation risk: once racial health displaced individual rights, the regime moved from preventing reproduction toward the [[NaziInvoluntaryEuthanasiaProgram|involuntary killing]] of people classified as genetically diseased.

## Key Claims

- Forced sterilization turns diagnostic and hereditary labels into irreversible bodily control.
- Legal and medical procedure can legitimate coercion without making the underlying classification reliable or just.
- Gender, class, disability, sexuality, and institutional self-interest shape who is labeled unfit.
- Reproductive control can form part of a wider escalation from classification to segregation and killing.
- Individual autonomy is the central boundary obscured by public-welfare and racial-health rhetoric.

## Evidence

- U.S. case mechanism: [[37-zhishang-ceshi-qingwen-ni-shi-zhili-poluomen-ma-608020679]] connects Carrie Buck's sterilization to institutional interest, gendered blame, courts, and disputed intelligence labels.
- Nazi legal system: [[408-the-nazis-in-power-hitlers-dream-part-5-glt6100029744]] describes the 1933 law, broad hereditary-disease categories, and health-court process.
- Escalation boundary: [[408-the-nazis-in-power-hitlers-dream-part-5-glt6100029744]] follows racial-health policy from compulsory sterilization toward involuntary killing.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept concerns nonconsensual or coercive sterilization, not voluntary contraception or clinically indicated procedures chosen with informed consent. The two sources cover different legal systems and historical contexts; comparison identifies mechanisms without claiming equivalence in scale, ideology, or outcome. Exact Nazi sterilization totals remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Extended the concept from the Carrie Buck case to Nazi hereditary-health law and medical courts.
- Added the escalation boundary between reproductive coercion and involuntary killing.
