# Idiom Origin Skepticism / 典故来源辨伪

Idiom origin skepticism / 典故来源辨伪 is the source-critical pattern [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-46-ma-ni-shi-ge-erbaiwu-de-lishi-laiyuan-lkz5hiamaojtvanllbunecy3g3ff]] uses for the popular story that "二百五" comes from [[SuQin|苏秦]]'s assassination. The story says a Qi ruler offered a thousand-gold reward, four people claimed responsibility, and each received "二百五."

The episode rejects that derivation. Its reasoning is historical rather than merely aesthetic: in the source's view, a pre-Qin aristocratic setting with weak monetary circulation and status-bound great families makes it implausible that murderers of Su Qin would expose themselves for a cash-like split reward. The explanation is treated as a memorable later tale, not a reliable origin.

This concept extends [[HistoricalDetectiveReasoning]] and [[EvidenceBoundFolkloreInquiry]]. A vivid origin story should be checked against period economy, status incentives, source proximity, and narrative convenience. It also sits beside [[FolkCharacterEtymologyRisk]]: a clever explanation can travel because it is easy to remember, even when the evidence does not support it.

## Key Claims
- A memorable idiom origin is not evidence by itself.
- The social and economic world assumed by an origin story has to fit the period being explained.
- Reward, punishment, class status, and exposure risk are part of source criticism when a story turns on someone self-identifying as guilty.
- Rejecting one origin story does not require solving the true origin; it can be enough to mark a weak derivation as unreliable.
- Popular etymology can still be culturally useful as reception history while failing as event history.
