# Robot Supply-Chain Certification / 机器人供应链认证

## Definition
Robot supply-chain certification is the regulatory problem created when market authorization depends not only on a robot's brand or final assembly site, but also on production location, communications functions, and the origin share of critical components.

## Current Synthesis
The bounded source presents new [[FederalCommunicationsCommission|FCC]] authorization restrictions as a supply-chain constraint on some foreign-produced advanced robots, including source-described humanoid, quadruped, and mobile connected devices. Existing certified models may retain a short-term path to market if they avoid major changes, while new products face greater uncertainty.

The deeper implication is that final assembly alone may not establish compliant domestic production. Robot vendors may need traceable component origins, redesignable bills of materials, local supplier capacity, and stable certification plans. Moving a dense robotics supply chain is slow, so compliance becomes an industrial-capability problem as well as a legal one.

## Key Claims
- Communications and connected-control functions can make robot market access depend on FCC authorization.
- Product origin may be evaluated through both final production location and critical-component provenance.
- Previously authorized models can face a different short-term risk profile from materially changed or newly introduced models.
- Final assembly in the United States may be insufficient when restricted-origin critical components remain above the applicable threshold.
- Long-term compliance can require supplier migration, traceability, local manufacturing, and product redesign rather than paperwork alone.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This page relies on a podcast summary rather than the underlying FCC text, covered-equipment list, legal analysis, or company filings. The source-reported 65% threshold, July cutoff, product categories, grandfathering conditions, and treatment of major modifications must remain unverified here. The rules may differ by device, component, entity, and authorization pathway, so this concept is not legal advice or a determination about any specific robot model.
