# Prestige-Constrained Political Prosecution / 名望约束下的政治追诉

Prestige-constrained political prosecution / 名望约束下的政治追诉 is the pattern [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1020-sanguo-baijiazi-yuanshu-ruhe-ba-hao-pai-da-de-xilan-lhwr9hfmanr-zktleufy3kvl38u0|Hanji 1020]] draws from the [[YangBiao|杨彪]] case. [[CaoCao|曹操]] treats Yang Biao as politically dangerous because of old-Han prestige and a marriage tie to [[YuanShu|袁术]], but the case cannot be safely closed by punishment if the evidence remains weak.

The constraint comes from reputation and public trust. [[KongRong|孔融]] argues that Yuan Shu's guilt cannot simply be transferred to Yang Biao, and [[ManChong|满宠]] later tells Cao Cao that convicting a famous man without firm proof would lose popular support. In the source's reading, release is not pure leniency; it is political risk management under a visible evidence gap.

The concept sits between [[PoliticalShowTrial]] and [[LegalTruthEvidenceGap|法律真相与证据差距]]. Unlike a show trial, the preselected punishment does not succeed. Unlike a neutral evidence problem, the case is shaped by court power, elite prestige, and the ruler's need not to burn public confidence around [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]]'s court.

## Key Claims
- Political prosecutions can be constrained by the target's reputation when punishment would damage the punisher's legitimacy.
- Weak evidence is especially costly when the accused person represents an older elite order or public moral capital.
- A release can be a controlled exit from political risk rather than proof that the initial suspicion was innocent or apolitical.
- Procedural severity does not automatically produce credible guilt; in this source, failed extraction of proof becomes the reason to release.
