# Domestic-Care Labor Matching / 家政照护劳动匹配

## Definition
Domestic-care labor matching is the continuing process of discovering a household's real needs, finding and preparing suitable care workers, negotiating scope and pay, and supporting adaptation or replacement after work begins.

## Current Synthesis
The source shows why domestic-care matching is more than resume distribution. Employers often begin with proxy requirements such as age, appearance, English, education, or a long task list, while their underlying need may concern trust, teaching ability, emotional fit, household workload, or alignment among several family decision-makers. Workers bring their own pay expectations, task preferences, family obligations, and judgments about the household.

The consultant therefore creates value by converting vague or contradictory preferences into a feasible role, screening both sides, sequencing sensitive conversations, and remaining accountable after placement. Formal skills narrow the candidate pool, but durable fit emerges only when work scope, personality, communication, boundaries, price, and household practice cohere.

## Key Claims
- Matching is bilateral because domestic workers can compare and reject households as employers compare and reject candidates.
- Stated requirements are often imperfect proxies for the actual care, education, workload, and relationship need.
- Care skills are partly trainable, while personality, habits, boundaries, and interpersonal fit remain difficult to standardize.
- A consultant's value includes requirement discovery, communication translation, negotiation, adaptation support, and replacement, not only access to resumes.
- Reputation and referral matter because strong workers leave the open market quickly and successful placements generate repeated trust.
- Long task lists fail when they treat workers as frictionless machines rather than people with fatigue, emotion, and judgment.

## Evidence
- Three-sided market - [[vol-276-zuo-yuesao-yuersao-de-jiejie-dou-you-gushi-1017890906]] describes employers, workers, and agencies as the industry's core participants, with both employers and workers using several intermediaries.
- Hidden-needs discovery - [[vol-276-zuo-yuesao-yuersao-de-jiejie-dou-you-gushi-1017890906]] follows a family that requested a young bilingual educator-housekeeper but ultimately hired an older worker with the right education and live-in experience after cleaning and companionship were separated.
- Bilateral choice - [[vol-276-zuo-yuesao-yuersao-de-jiejie-dou-you-gushi-1017890906]] reports workers choosing child-only roles over jobs that combine childcare and housework, even when an employer is ready to hire.
- Post-placement value - [[vol-276-zuo-yuesao-yuersao-de-jiejie-dou-you-gushi-1017890906]] argues that adaptation, conflict, replacement, and long-term follow-up test the consultant more than obtaining a signature.
- Referral economics - [[vol-276-zuo-yuesao-yuersao-de-jiejie-dou-you-gushi-1017890906]] says good workers circulate through direct referrals and that experienced consultants increasingly depend on former clients and word of mouth.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a practitioner's qualitative account from Shanghai and a relatively high-end segment. It does not compare agency outcomes with direct hiring, measure whether consultant intervention improves retention or safety, or establish which screening practices predict fit. Fast recommendations and referrals can indicate competence, but they can also reflect network size or market position.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to separate continuing care-labor mediation from simple candidate search.
