# Emergency Dispatch Triage

## Definition

Emergency dispatch triage is the structured classification of calls by expected time sensitivity so that response mode, warning-device use, and clinical resources are matched to likely need.

## Current Synthesis

In [[running-hot|Running Hot]], triage is the mechanism that makes [[LightsAndSirensRiskCalibration]] operational. Berrien County's former rules reportedly labeled about half of calls priority one, sometimes because broad screening questions elevated complaints such as dental pain. Revised coding preserved priority-one response for cardiac arrest, choking, and respiratory arrest while downgrading cases where a short delay was unlikely to change outcomes.

The source also describes graded scripted systems from life-threatening “echo” calls to low-risk “omega” calls. Their accuracy is imperfect but usable; the policy task is not certainty at dispatch but calibrated thresholds, feedback from downstream care, and explicit monitoring for under-triage.

## Key Claims

- Dispatch classification should distinguish emergency contact from time-critical physiology.
- Overbroad questions can make a nominal priority category too common to guide risk well.
- Selective response requires both protected high-urgency categories and deliberate downgrading rules.
- Hospital reclassification can provide feedback but is not a complete patient-outcome measure.
- Scripted determinant systems are imperfect and should be evaluated rather than treated as infallible.
- Public communication matters because quieter response can be mistaken for abandonment or delay.

## Evidence

### Berrien County redesign

- [[running-hot]] describes the old priority-one share, overbroad screening, protected critical conditions, and elimination of about half of warning-device responses.

### Feedback and under-triage check

- [[running-hot]] reports no increase in lower-priority cases later classified priority one after the reform.

### National determinant context

- [[running-hot]] describes graded dispatch determinants and the eye-problem example, in which all analyzed calls received lights and sirens despite a 0.67-percent potentially lifesaving-intervention rate.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Dispatchers work with incomplete caller reports, atypical symptoms, language differences, and changing conditions. A low intervention rate does not establish that a category is safe to downgrade universally, while a hospital priority comparison can miss deterioration, pain, delay burden, or rare catastrophic errors. Thresholds therefore remain geography-, protocol-, and population-dependent.

## What Changed

- Established triage as the control layer behind selective siren use.
- Preserved under-triage, incomplete-information, and outcome-measure limits.
