# Lights and Sirens Risk Calibration

## Definition

Lights and sirens risk calibration is the principle that emergency warning devices should be treated as a high-risk intervention whose expected time-sensitive benefit must outweigh collision, patient, responder, and community harms.

## Current Synthesis

[[running-hot|Running Hot]] argues that warning devices remain valuable for a narrow set of emergencies such as cardiac arrest, choking, and respiratory arrest, but should not be the default for calls where seconds or a few minutes are unlikely to change the outcome. Studies summarized in the episode report more than a 50 percent increase in crash risk and average time savings from 42 seconds to 3 minutes 48 seconds.

The strongest operational evidence in the source comes from selective-use reforms rather than an anti-siren absolute. [[JonathanBayer]] reportedly removed about half of Berrien County's warning-device responses without increasing lower-to-priority-one reclassification, while [[JeffJarvisEMS|Jeff Jarvis]] reduced use by about one-third with a six-second median delay.

## Key Claims

- Warning devices are justified by marginal clinical benefit, not by the mere existence of an emergency call.
- Crash exposure and transport risk belong in the same decision as response-time savings.
- Cardiac arrest and other narrow time-critical conditions preserve a strong case for rapid warning-device response.
- Large utilization-to-intervention gaps indicate overuse but do not prove that every non-intervention call was nonurgent.
- Selective use can reduce risk with little median delay when dispatch criteria and monitoring are adequate.
- Community noise, hearing damage, burnout, and warning desensitization are real externalities of routine use.

## Evidence

### Benefit-risk comparison

- [[running-hot]] reports increased crash risk, modest average time savings, and Bayer's medical-therapy analogy.

### Selective-use implementation

- [[running-hot]] reports the Berrien County and Jarvis reductions together with their triage and response-time checks.

### Utilization gap and wider harms

- [[running-hot]] reports Jarvis's 86-percent utilization versus 6.9-percent potentially lifesaving-intervention comparison and connects overuse to noise, hearing damage, collisions, and burnout.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source does not support eliminating lights and sirens. Minutes can matter in selected conditions, a delivered intervention is not the only measure of urgency, median delay can conceal individual tails, and hospital priority does not capture every adverse outcome. The episode summary does not provide the cited studies, dataset definitions, confidence intervals, geography-specific protocols, or subgroup results.

## What Changed

- Established a selective benefit-risk framework for emergency warning devices.
- Preserved genuine time-critical exceptions and measurement limits.
