## Definition

Operational tempo breakthrough is a military pattern where a force collapses an elongated or geography-dependent defense by moving faster than the defender can reorient. In the current wiki evidence, it is grounded by Cen Peng and Zang Gong's Hanji 711 Shu campaign: speed, route surprise, local shock, and psychological pressure turn mountains, rivers, and distance from defensive assets into liabilities.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-711-ta-zhu-liuxiu-da-xia-banbi-jiangshan-weihe-si-fa-biequ-liib38zub0-tgrbtycmlqvohv-w|Hanji 711]] supplies the current bounded case. [[CenPengEasternHan|Cen Peng]] breaks the Shu line not by grinding down every position but by defeating Hou Dan, detouring through difficult distance, seizing Wuyang, and sending light cavalry toward Guangdu fast enough that Chengdu is shocked before [[GongsunShuEasternHan|Gongsun Shu]] can adapt.

[[ZangGongEasternHan|Zang Gong]] provides the supporting branch. His troops are unstable and his counties are hesitant, but he uses supplies, urgent authority, day-night marching, flags, and drums to pressure [[YanCenEasternHan|Yan Cen]] into defeat. The shared pattern is that tempo converts enemy uncertainty into collapse before the defensive system can settle.

The concept is narrower than generic speed. It requires a defender whose confidence depends on long defensive depth or terrain, an attacker able to concentrate movement through an unexpected path or rhythm, and a political result that arrives before normal tactical attrition would have finished the campaign.

## Key Claims

- Tempo matters most when the defender's confidence rests on static depth, river/mountain barriers, or assumptions about how quickly an attacker can arrive.
- A breakthrough can be operational rather than merely tactical when it changes the defender's whole map of risk before the capital or core position is reached.
- Speed has to be coupled to psychological and logistical pressure; Zang Gong's branch works because resources, night movement, flags, and drums convert a shaky force into credible threat.
- The pattern exposes overreliance on geographic safety: once Cen Peng reaches Wuyang and Guangdu, Chengdu's defensive depth becomes an argument for panic, not security.
- The concept can produce political outcomes before final conquest, including surrender letters, court shock, assassination attempts, and replacement-command decisions.

## Evidence

- Breaking the long line: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-711-ta-zhu-liuxiu-da-xia-banbi-jiangshan-weihe-si-fa-biequ-liib38zub0-tgrbtycmlqvohv-w|Hanji 711]] says Cen Peng defeats Hou Dan, marches day and night, captures Wuyang, and sends light cavalry toward Guangdu near Chengdu.
- Terrain reliance exposed: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-711-ta-zhu-liuxiu-da-xia-banbi-jiangshan-weihe-si-fa-biequ-liib38zub0-tgrbtycmlqvohv-w|Hanji 711]] frames Gongsun Shu as overreliant on Shu geography and says the breakthrough shocks Chengdu.
- Supporting psychological pressure: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-711-ta-zhu-liuxiu-da-xia-banbi-jiangshan-weihe-si-fa-biequ-liib38zub0-tgrbtycmlqvohv-w|Hanji 711]] says Zang Gong uses supplies, day-night marching, flags, and drums to defeat Yan Cen despite unstable surrendered troops.
- Political follow-through: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-711-ta-zhu-liuxiu-da-xia-banbi-jiangshan-weihe-si-fa-biequ-liib38zub0-tgrbtycmlqvohv-w|Hanji 711]] ties the breakthrough to Liu Xiu's surrender letter, Gongsun Shu's refusal, Cen Peng's assassination, and Wu Han's later appointment.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept is currently grounded in one source note and should not be generalized into all fast campaigns. It also does not erase the later Hanji 712-713 evidence that Chengdu still had major forces, that Wu Han could create new risks by overadvancing, and that final conquest required further fighting and post-surrender governance. The durable claim is that tempo can break a defensive system's confidence before it ends every military problem.

## What Changed

- Created this concept from Hanji 711 to capture the Cen Peng/Zang Gong pattern of speed, surprise, psychological pressure, and long-line collapse in the Shu campaign.
