## Definition

Operational tempo breakthrough is a military pattern where a force collapses an elongated or geography-dependent defense by moving faster than the defender can reorient. In the current wiki evidence, it is grounded by [[CenPengEasternHan|Cen Peng]] and [[ZangGongEasternHan|Zang Gong]] in the Shu campaign: water-command preparation, bridge breaking, no-plunder discipline, split routes, speed, route surprise, local shock, and psychological pressure turn mountains, rivers, and distance from defensive assets into liabilities.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-710-shui-si-le-jiemi-donghan-zui-zhuming-de-ansha-shijian-lok1aczap1u-tmtyutx-upokaplz|Hanji 710]] supplies the setup layer. When [[WuHanEasternHan|Wu Han]] wants to dismiss water troops to save grain, Cen Peng argues that Shu remains strong and that the river force should not be dispersed. [[LiuXiu|Liu Xiu]] backs Cen Peng's specialist judgment, making Jingmen affairs follow Cen Peng because water warfare is not Wu Han's strongest domain.

The same source shows tempo being built before the famous Wuyang/Guangdu dash. Cen Peng recruits volunteers for the floating-bridge attack, promises high rewards, and uses [[LuQi|Lu Qi]]'s ship assault plus wind-driven fire to break the bridge works. After Ren Man is killed and Cheng Fan captured, Cen Peng orders no plunder, wins local surrender, takes Pingqu grain, bypasses difficult Jiangzhou, and sets up split movement: Zang Gong toward the Fu River and Cen Peng toward the Min River and [[Chengdu]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-711-ta-zhu-liuxiu-da-xia-banbi-jiangshan-weihe-si-fa-biequ-liib38zub0-tgrbtycmlqvohv-w|Hanji 711]] supplies the completion layer. Cen Peng breaks the Shu line not by grinding down every position but by defeating Hou Dan, detouring through difficult distance, seizing Wuyang, and sending light cavalry toward Guangdu fast enough that Chengdu is shocked before [[GongsunShuEasternHan|Gongsun Shu]] can adapt.

Zang Gong provides the supporting branch. His troops are unstable and his counties are hesitant, but he uses supplies, urgent authority, day-night marching, flags, and drums to pressure [[YanCenEasternHan|Yan Cen]] into defeat. The shared pattern is that tempo converts enemy uncertainty into collapse before the defensive system can settle.

The concept is narrower than generic speed. It requires a defender whose confidence depends on long defensive depth or terrain, an attacker able to concentrate movement through an unexpected path or rhythm, and a political result that arrives before normal tactical attrition would have finished the campaign.

## Key Claims

- Tempo matters most when the defender's confidence rests on static depth, river/mountain barriers, or assumptions about how quickly an attacker can arrive.
- Operational tempo needs an enabling command decision before the visible dash; in this case, Liu Xiu's backing keeps Cen Peng's water-force option alive.
- Bridge-breaking and no-plunder discipline are part of tempo because they open the route while making local surrender and supply capture easier.
- A breakthrough can be operational rather than merely tactical when it changes the defender's whole map of risk before the capital or core position is reached.
- Speed has to be coupled to psychological and logistical pressure; Zang Gong's branch works because resources, night movement, flags, and drums convert a shaky force into credible threat.
- The pattern exposes overreliance on geographic safety: once Cen Peng reaches Wuyang and Guangdu, Chengdu's defensive depth becomes an argument for panic, not security.
- The concept can produce political outcomes before final conquest, including surrender letters, court shock, assassination attempts, and replacement-command decisions.

- Bridge breaking: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-710-shui-si-le-jiemi-donghan-zui-zhuming-de-ansha-shijian-lok1aczap1u-tmtyutx-upokaplz|Hanji 710]] says Cen Peng uses reward incentives, Lu Qi's ship assault, and wind-driven fire to burn the floating-bridge defense, killing Ren Man and capturing Cheng Fan.
- No-plunder and split route: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-710-shui-si-le-jiemi-donghan-zui-zhuming-de-ansha-shijian-lok1aczap1u-tmtyutx-upokaplz|Hanji 710]] says Cen Peng forbids looting, receives local surrender, captures Pingqu grain, bypasses Jiangzhou, and divides the advance toward the Fu River and Min River lines.
- Breaking the long line: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-711-ta-zhu-liuxiu-da-xia-banbi-jiangshan-weihe-si-fa-biequ-liib38zub0-tgrbtycmlqvohv-w|Hanji 711]] says Cen Peng defeats Hou Dan, marches day and night, captures Wuyang, and sends light cavalry toward Guangdu near Chengdu.
- Terrain reliance exposed: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-711-ta-zhu-liuxiu-da-xia-banbi-jiangshan-weihe-si-fa-biequ-liib38zub0-tgrbtycmlqvohv-w|Hanji 711]] frames Gongsun Shu as overreliant on Shu geography and says the breakthrough shocks Chengdu.
- Supporting psychological pressure: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-711-ta-zhu-liuxiu-da-xia-banbi-jiangshan-weihe-si-fa-biequ-liib38zub0-tgrbtycmlqvohv-w|Hanji 711]] says Zang Gong uses supplies, day-night marching, flags, and drums to defeat Yan Cen despite unstable surrendered troops.
- Political follow-through: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-711-ta-zhu-liuxiu-da-xia-banbi-jiangshan-weihe-si-fa-biequ-liib38zub0-tgrbtycmlqvohv-w|Hanji 711]] ties the breakthrough to Liu Xiu's surrender letter, Gongsun Shu's refusal, Cen Peng's assassination, and Wu Han's later appointment.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept is currently grounded in two adjacent source notes and should not be generalized into all fast campaigns. Hanji 710 sets up the route, water-force authority, bridge attack, local surrender, and split movement, but it does not complete the Wuyang/Guangdu breakthrough. Hanji 711 remains the stronger evidence for Chengdu shock and the assassination response. The concept also does not erase the later Hanji 712-713 evidence that Chengdu still had major forces, that Wu Han could create new risks by overadvancing, and that final conquest required further fighting and post-surrender governance. The durable claim is that tempo can break a defensive system's confidence before it ends every military problem.

## What Changed

- Created this concept from Hanji 711 to capture the Cen Peng/Zang Gong pattern of speed, surprise, psychological pressure, and long-line collapse in the Shu campaign.
- Added Hanji 710 as the setup layer: Liu Xiu-backed water command, floating-bridge destruction, no-plunder discipline, Pingqu grain, and split-route design.
- Reframed tempo as a preparation-to-breakthrough pattern rather than only the final Wuyang/Guangdu dash.
