# Public Corpse Humiliation / 公开尸体羞辱

Public corpse humiliation / 公开尸体羞辱 is the after-death violence pattern [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-963-shishang-bei-diantian-deng-de-diyiren-shi-ta-lkpkhlpkv3xpw-hur4b7sh-3druk|Hanji 963]] draws from [[DongZhuo|董卓]]'s fall. The episode does not stop at [[LyuBu|吕布]] killing Dong Zhuo under [[WangYunLateHan|王允]]'s plan; it dwells on the public treatment of Dong Zhuo's body in [[ChangAnHanCapital|长安]], where the corpse becomes a spectacle of vengeance and moral reversal.

The concept is distinct from execution or assassination. A corpse can no longer be deterred or neutralized, so the violence works on witnesses and memory: the dead ruler's body is made to answer for prior cruelty, and the public is invited to see his collapse as complete. In this source, that spectacle sits beside popular celebration, killing of Dong Zhuo's household, and confiscation of [[MeiwuFortress|郿坞]] wealth.

The episode also shows the limit of this symbolic settlement. Public humiliation can make a hated ruler look defeated, but it does not build a stable order by itself. The follow-on Hanji 964-965 branch shows [[WangYunLateHan|王允]] and Lü Bu immediately facing reputation anxiety, merit conflict, and the unresolved danger of Dong Zhuo's remnant soldiers.

## Key Claims
- Posthumous treatment of a body can be political communication, not only revenge.
- Corpse humiliation broadcasts a reversal of fear: the person who dominated others becomes exposed to collective contempt.
- The same spectacle can blur lawful punishment, popular catharsis, and uncontrolled vengeance.
- Symbolic domination of a dead enemy does not solve the live problems of remnant troops, offices, food, titles, or court legitimacy.
