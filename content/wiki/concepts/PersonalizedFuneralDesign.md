# Personalized Funeral Design / 个性化送别设计

## Definition
Personalized funeral design is the practice of building a farewell from the deceased's biography, preferences, relationships, culture, and the needs of survivors rather than applying one fixed ceremonial package.

## Current Synthesis
The source treats personalization as inquiry before aesthetics. Planners ask who the person was, who knew them best, which relationships matter, what the deceased wanted hidden or shown, and what the ceremony must help participants do. Clothing, music, favorite food, photographs, objects, colors, speech, silence, humor, and traditional forms become design materials only after that purpose is understood.

Personalization does not guarantee reconciliation. A funeral can recognize a person, reduce avoidable regret, and provide separate emotional spaces, but it cannot erase betrayal, family hierarchy, neglect, or conflict accumulated during life. The design task is therefore both creative and political: information is incomplete, the deceased cannot correct it, relatives disagree, and the payer may not be the person who knew them best.

## Key Claims
- Good funeral design begins with person, relationship, and function rather than a standard sequence.
- Traditional forms and individualized elements can coexist when each has a clear care or meaning function.
- Design details can make a deceased person recognizable and reduce preventable survivor regret.
- Information authority should track knowledge of the deceased, not only kinship rank, payment, or emotional display.
- Ceremony can hold mixed emotions without forcing everyone into one approved grief performance.
- Personalization is constrained by family power, incomplete evidence, time, money, law, and the deceased's silence.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence is founder testimony and selected cases, not comparative outcome research. Simple, private, secular, ecological, or low-cost funerals may be deeply appropriate when they reflect the deceased and survivors; complexity is not quality by itself. Cultural practice, law, time, finances, body condition, and family safety can legitimately limit personalization.
