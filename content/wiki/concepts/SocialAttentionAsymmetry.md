# Social Attention Asymmetry

## Definition
Social attention asymmetry is the mismatch between direct access to one's own thoughts, feelings, flaws, and acts of observing and limited access to what other people notice, conceal, remember, or continue thinking about.

## Current Synthesis
[[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-1dfa7e5aca191bd78c9a8028baf43cb1-50295125-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-1dfa7e5aca191bd78c9a8028baf43cb1-50295125|Mind Reading 2.0]] presents several apparently opposite errors as products of this shared information imbalance. During interaction, a person may overestimate how visible their low energy, embarrassment, or flawed delivery is because those details dominate private attention. Outside direct interaction, the same person may underestimate being watched because other observers deflect their gaze and hide attention. Afterward, each party may underestimate how much the other continues thinking about advice, praise, conflict, or reconciliation.

The synthesis is not simply that people receive less attention than they imagine. Attention changes with setting, gaze is strategically concealed, and internal experience is only partly expressed. The corrective move is to distinguish private salience from public evidence and, where appropriate, seek perspective or communicate rather than infer a single rule about scrutiny.

In close relationships, the transparency problem extends beyond public embarrassment. A feeling can seem obvious from the inside while remaining unexpressed to a partner; fear of rejection can then suppress the very disclosure needed for correction. The resulting distance should not automatically be treated as evidence that the other person failed to care or understand [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-ea5bbec6d5f8dceb54214fa00e91a356-48702281-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-ea5bbec6d5f8dceb54214fa00e91a356-48702281|What Makes Relationships Thrive]].

## Key Claims
- Internal states usually feel more externally visible than they are.
- Self-conscious attention magnifies an embarrassing feature that observers encounter as one part of a wider scene.
- People can feel like the observer rather than the observed because others often look when direct gaze is unavailable.
- Lack of visible attention does not prove lack of notice, memory, or later thought.
- Overestimated scrutiny during interaction and underestimated observation outside interaction can coexist because context and access differ.
- Calibration requires external evidence rather than projecting private salience onto another mind.
- In close relationships, assumed transparency and protective concealment can reinforce one another by withholding corrective information.

## Evidence
- Perceived transparency - [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-1dfa7e5aca191bd78c9a8028baf43cb1-50295125-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-1dfa7e5aca191bd78c9a8028baf43cb1-50295125]] contrasts Boothby's felt decline in a second lecture with nearly identical ratings from both classes.
- Embarrassment and broad impressions - [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-1dfa7e5aca191bd78c9a8028baf43cb1-50295125-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-1dfa7e5aca191bd78c9a8028baf43cb1-50295125]] reports that embarrassing-shirt wearers overestimated how much another person noticed or considered the shirt.
- Hidden observation - [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-1dfa7e5aca191bd78c9a8028baf43cb1-50295125-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-1dfa7e5aca191bd78c9a8028baf43cb1-50295125]] uses cafeteria and waiting-room studies plus gaze deflection to explain why each person can feel unusually unseen.
- Continuing thought - [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-1dfa7e5aca191bd78c9a8028baf43cb1-50295125-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-1dfa7e5aca191bd78c9a8028baf43cb1-50295125]] reports underestimation of how long one's advice, stories, arguments, or conversations remain in another person's mind.
- Relational transparency - [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-ea5bbec6d5f8dceb54214fa00e91a356-48702281-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-ea5bbec6d5f8dceb54214fa00e91a356-48702281]] connects expectations that a partner should know unexpressed feelings with concealment, distance, and blame.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This is a synthesis across findings presented in two structured episode summaries, not a claim that the named experimental literatures are identical or governed by one proven causal mechanism. Real scrutiny, surveillance, stigma, prejudice, audience evaluation, and reputational harm can be substantial, especially under unequal power. Disclosure is not always safe or owed. The episodes do not provide methods, effect sizes, cultural comparisons, replications, or clinical thresholds, and their examples do not establish how a particular observer or partner thinks.

## What Changed
- Extended the illusion-of-transparency branch into close relationships, where unexpressed feeling and fear-driven concealment can create a self-reinforcing information gap.
