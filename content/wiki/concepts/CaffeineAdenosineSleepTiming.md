# Caffeine-Adenosine Sleep Timing

## Definition
Caffeine-adenosine sleep timing is the source's frame for using caffeine cautiously because it blocks adenosine-related sleep pressure and can either support daytime alertness or interfere with later sleep depending on timing and tolerance.

## Current Synthesis
The episode treats caffeine as optional rather than foundational. Huberman describes caffeine as an adenosine antagonist and argues that some people feel better when they delay caffeine until 90-120 minutes after waking, which may reduce the afternoon crash and the perceived need for later caffeine.

The sleep boundary matters more than the stimulant itself. Late-day caffeine can push against the night's sleep pressure, so the source recommends limiting caffeine after 4 p.m. and ideally keeping it below a low amount when sleep quality is the priority. This refines [[SleepAsDailyHealthAccount]] by making caffeine one adjustable input rather than a moral rule or universal ban.

## Key Claims
- Caffeine is optional and strongly dependent on individual tolerance.
- The source frames caffeine as an adenosine antagonist that can reduce sleepiness.
- Delaying caffeine 90-120 minutes after waking may reduce afternoon caffeine need for many people.
- Late-day caffeine can impair sleep quality even when a person can still fall asleep.
- The source recommends limiting caffeine after 4 p.m., with a low late-day ceiling when sleep is the goal.
- Caffeine timing belongs inside the whole sleep-wake timing system, alongside light, temperature, movement, food, and consistency.
