# Human-Centered AI Education

## Definition
Human-centered AI education is the design of school and university AI learning around human agency, teacher judgment, ethics, domain context, privacy, bias, and student reasoning rather than around tool access or answer generation alone.

## Current Synthesis
The bounded sources give this concept a K-12, university, public-AI, and school-design layer. The early ChatGPT education source argues that schools should move past bans and detectors toward teacher literacy, computer-science access, and human-driven classroom use. The USC source expands the frame into higher education: AI should be central to curriculum, but humans should remain central to AI through ethics, interdisciplinary research, and agency-aware design. The Fei-Fei Li episode adds the broadest civic version: denying students AI access can be harmful, but tools should not take away motivation to learn, and teachers and parents need direct support instead of being left behind by policymakers, technologists, and investors. The Alpha School episode adds an operational version: AI can handle right-level academic practice only when humans retain responsibility for motivation, identity, standards, belonging, and developmental judgment.

## Key Claims
- AI education should preserve student reasoning and motivation rather than turn tools into answer machines.
- Teachers need AI literacy and practical support because classroom judgment and human relationships remain central.
- Human-centered AI education belongs outside computer science alone; non-STEM fields also need AI fluency, ethics, and domain judgment.
- Ethics, privacy, bias, agency, and social impact should be built into curriculum and research rather than deferred to later compliance.
- Bans and cheating panic are incomplete responses because students already encounter AI and need guided, responsible use.
- Prompting can become a learning skill when it helps students ask better questions instead of bypassing understanding.
- School-level AI education should redesign roles and schedules rather than insert chatbots into unchanged classrooms.

## Evidence
- K-12 classroom evidence: [[ep-9-chatgpt-and-education-systems]] presents [[ChatGPT]] as both an academic-integrity risk and a tool for [[TeacherAIAugmentation]], arguing for teacher literacy and human-driven AI rather than bans alone.
- University evidence: [[tech-20260730-0730-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260730-0730-mp-tech-pod-128]] uses [[UniversityOfSouthernCalifornia|USC]]'s AI school, AI major, and non-STEM minors to argue that AI education should include ethics, privacy, bias, human agency, and interdisciplinary research.
- Teacher and parent support: [[using-ai-to-increase-your-intelligence-enrich-humanity-dr-fei-fei-li-scim5701398040]] says teachers, parents, and students are often neglected in AI policy and investment discussions and need direct exposure and support.
- Student agency evidence: [[using-ai-to-increase-your-intelligence-enrich-humanity-dr-fei-fei-li-scim5701398040]] says the worst outcome would be AI taking away young people's agency and motivation, while denying students access because of cheating concerns would also be harmful.
- Prompting and learning evidence: [[using-ai-to-increase-your-intelligence-enrich-humanity-dr-fei-fei-li-scim5701398040]] links good prompting to the Socratic method and uses organic chemistry as an example of a motivated student asking deeper questions with an AI companion.
- Equity evidence: [[ep-9-chatgpt-and-education-systems]] connects AI and computer-science access to future opportunity gaps across communities.
- School-design evidence: [[how-to-accelerate-learning-improve-education-joe-liemandt-scim7393383815]] frames [[AlphaSchool]] as a model where AI supports mastery and prerequisite repair while human guides handle [[EducationMotivationArchitecture]].

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not claim that unrestricted AI use is harmless. They preserve academic-integrity, shortcut, privacy, bias, developmental, and teacher-capacity risks. Human-centered education requires assignment design, literacy, verification, domain expertise, classroom norms, and human motivation support; access alone does not create learning. The Alpha School evidence is source-scoped and should not be treated as independent proof that its model generalizes.

## What Changed
- Added Alpha School's school-design version, where AI handles mastery practice only inside human motivation and developmental support.
- Clarified that human-centered AI education is a role redesign problem, not just an access or ban policy.
