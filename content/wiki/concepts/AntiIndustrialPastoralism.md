# Anti-Industrial Pastoralism

## Definition

Anti-industrial pastoralism is a literary-political critique that treats machinery, pollution, centralized control, and the destruction of trees or local life as mutually reinforcing forms of domination, while imagining the cultivated rural home as something worth defending and repairing.

## Current Synthesis

[[226-the-lord-of-the-rings-glt1788342017]] grounds the concept in [[JRRTolkien|Tolkien]]'s Shire and its Scouring. The returning hobbits do not find an untouched refuge: trees have been felled, the landscape polluted, inns closed, rules multiplied, surveillance normalized, and ordinary hobbits recruited into petty collaboration. Industrial damage is therefore ecological, political, and social at once.

The episode explains how a reactionary dislike of modernity could later resonate with environmental and countercultural readers. That overlap does not make Tolkien straightforwardly progressive; it shows how defense of place, limits, and living landscapes can travel across political traditions.

## Key Claims

- Industrial destruction can join ecological damage to bureaucracy, surveillance, and social conformity.
- Homecoming after war is incomplete when distant conflict has transformed the home itself.
- Trees and cultivated landscapes can function as moral and political subjects rather than decorative settings.
- Reactionary localism and later environmentalism may overlap without becoming identical ideologies.
- Repair requires local agency and renewed stewardship, not only victory over a distant enemy.

## Evidence

- Scouring cluster: [[226-the-lord-of-the-rings-glt1788342017]] links felled trees, pollution, machinery, rationing, officials, surveillance, closed inns, and collaboration.
- Political reception: [[226-the-lord-of-the-rings-glt1788342017]] places Tolkien's conservative and anti-democratic instincts beside his 1960s countercultural and environmental reception.
- Narrative function: [[226-the-lord-of-the-rings-glt1788342017]] argues that the ruined Shire blocks a simple final-victory ending.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Pastoral defense can idealize hierarchy, obscure rural labor, or treat social change as decline. Tolkien's reactionary politics should not be converted into a modern environmental platform, and later readers' ecological use of the work does not settle authorial intention. The concept describes this source's literary synthesis rather than a complete political program.

## What Changed

- Defined the Scouring as a combined ecological, bureaucratic, and postwar-homecoming critique.
- Distinguished overlap between reactionary localism and environmental reception from ideological equivalence.
