# Strategic Patron Exit Judgment / 主公前途与安全退出判断

## Definition
Strategic patron exit judgment / 主公前途与安全退出判断 is the decision pattern in which a dependent actor leaves a powerful patron before obvious collapse because the patron's judgment, personnel treatment, retaliation risk, and future path make staying more dangerous or less useful than a controlled exit.

## Current Synthesis
Hanji 1047 defines the pattern through [[LiuBei|刘备]]'s departure from [[YuanShao|袁绍]] before [[GuanduCampaign|官渡之战]] has been decided. The episode rejects a simple betrayal explanation: Yuan Shao is not yet desperate, and [[LiuBiao|刘表]] is not framed as a Cao Cao ally. Liu Bei's calculation is instead read through Yuan's character, Yuan's treatment of advisers such as [[TianFengLateHan|田丰]] and [[JuShou|沮授]], the possible resentment created by [[GuanYu|关羽]] killing [[YanLiangLateHan|颜良]], and the broader question of whether Yuan can turn power into victory.

The safe-exit part matters as much as the judgment. Liu Bei does not merely flee; he proposes an alliance with Liu Biao, receives Yuan Shao's permission, and returns to Runan where he joins [[GongDuLateHan|龚都]]. The move turns a dangerous patron relationship into a new local foothold while still appearing useful to Yuan's anti-Cao strategy.

Hanji 1096 broadens the pattern from a mobile warlord to scholars around Liu Biao. [[HeKui|何夔]] and [[PeiQianLateHan|裴潜]] create geographic distance despite favorable treatment, while [[LiuWangzhiLateHan|刘望之]] resigns but remains within reach and is killed. [[HanJiLateHan|韩暨]] shows the limit of the pattern: attempted withdrawal can fail when a patron has enough coercive reach to force service. Safe exit therefore requires not only correct diagnosis but actual distance, a viable destination, and a ruler unable or unwilling to reverse the move.

## Key Claims
- A patron can be unsafe even while still materially strong if command judgment and personnel treatment make future survival doubtful.
- Exit decisions often depend on character evidence, adviser treatment, and resentment risk, not only battlefield forecasts.
- A controlled departure needs a plausible service frame; Liu Bei's Liu Biao proposal gives his exit a Yuan-compatible purpose.
- Leaving a patron can be a strategic repositioning when the actor moves into a local base rather than simple flight.
- Resignation is not an effective exit if the former patron retains practical reach over the person.
- Geographic distance, a viable receiving environment, and coercive capacity determine whether withdrawal succeeds.
- The pattern sits between loyalty judgment and self-preservation, so motive claims should remain source-scoped unless multiple sources converge.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources' motive accounts are interpretive and transcript-sensitive. They do not prove Liu Bei's or the Jingzhou scholars' internal reasoning directly. Nor is exit always feasible or desirable: Han Ji's case shows coercion defeating withdrawal, while later Cao-side service shows that leaving one patron need not mean permanent reclusion.

## What Changed
- Created this concept from Hanji 1047 to capture Liu Bei's Yuan Shao exit as strategic survival judgment rather than simple opportunism.
- Added Hanji 1096's successful, failed, and coerced Jingzhou exit cases, making practical distance a central condition.
