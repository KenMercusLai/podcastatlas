# Illness Outcome Moralization Boundary / 疾病结果道德化边界

## Definition
Illness outcome moralization boundary is the distinction between supporting treatment participation and wrongly treating survival, recovery, fear, or rationality under severe illness as direct measures of courage, effort, character, or deservingness.

## Current Synthesis
[[YeTan|叶檀]]'s cancer account argues that treatment response contains biological uncertainty and luck beyond personal control. People who recover and can narrate the experience are a selected group; their courage, discipline, or meaning-making cannot explain the outcomes of people who followed treatment but did not survive or could not leave a public story.

The boundary does not make action irrelevant. Patients still make consequential choices about treatment, information, symptoms, support, and daily care. It rejects the extra moral claim that better outcomes prove superior will or that fear, desperation, hope, changing one's mind, or apparently irrational choices under pain and mortality prove failure. Support should preserve agency and clinical care without requiring a heroic performance.

## Key Claims
- Treatment response and survival include biological variation, uncertainty, and luck that effort cannot fully govern.
- Survivor testimony is valuable but systematically excludes many people who did not live or could not speak publicly.
- Encouragement becomes moral pressure when a patient is told that courage or persistence should determine the outcome.
- Fear, desperation, and non-ideal reasoning under severe pain or mortality are human responses rather than character verdicts.
- Respecting patient agency includes allowing vulnerability, changing preferences, and refusal of a public inspirational role.
- Nonjudgmental presence complements rather than replaces evidence-based treatment, symptom control, and qualified support.

## Evidence
### Outcome uncertainty and survivorship
- [[e249-duihua-yetan-wo-buzai-shi-rensheng-de-guokeren-li8lfk6ayg48zk63bpnh4qtkmn9x]] has Ye Tan attribute part of her cancer response to uncontrollable drug response and luck, while noting that public survival stories omit many people who died.

### Permission not to perform bravery
- [[e249-duihua-yetan-wo-buzai-shi-rensheng-de-guokeren-li8lfk6ayg48zk63bpnh4qtkmn9x]] rejects demands that patients be more courageous or rational and asks supporters to replace blame and repeated instruction with listening, companionship, and respect for decisions.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is one survivor's retrospective account, not a study of treatment adherence, prognosis, coping, or survivorship bias. Psychological support and treatment participation can affect experience and sometimes outcomes, but that does not justify reducing outcome differences to virtue or blame. Patient agency is bounded by decision capacity, urgent risk, clinical indication, public-health duties, law, and the rights and burdens of caregivers; nonjudgment does not require endorsing misinformation or unsafe treatment.
