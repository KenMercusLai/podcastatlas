## Definition

Lineage rules and elite durability / 家规型士族延续 is the pattern where a family or clan is interpreted as sustaining long-run status through internal instructions, admonitions, discipline, and reputation management rather than through one founder, one office, or one generation of success.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-723-bi-kongjia-gengniu-de-jiazu-pingsha-shi-peishi-lqti3stvdcw7utgm6uzwv5giazhj|Hanji 723]] introduces the pattern through [[HedongPeiClan|河东裴氏]]. The episode uses the Pei clan's claimed official scale, long chronological arc, and Wenxi/Pei Bai place memory to ask why an elite family could remain visible from Qin-Han origins through Sui-Tang peak and later traces.

The concept's current claim is deliberately narrow. The source treats family rules as the key explanation, naming twelve instructions and ten admonitions, but it does not list them. The durable synthesis is therefore that lineage durability can be analyzed as a family-governance problem; the actual Pei rule content remains unverified by this note.

## Key Claims

- Elite family durability can be framed as an internal governance problem, not only as inherited bloodline or accumulated luck.
- Family instructions and admonitions can serve as reputation and conduct infrastructure when many descendants seek office over time.
- The Pei case links local place memory, official counts, and moral explanation into one lineage-success story.
- A long-running family can peak and decline without disappearing from political or examination memory.
- Source-scoped headline counts are useful as scale signals but should not be treated as verified genealogy without stronger evidence.
- The content of family rules matters; a source that names rules without enumerating them can support the concept but not a detailed code.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept currently rests on one source note. It does not prove that family rules caused Pei success, nor does it identify which rules mattered most, because the transcript only introduces the rules. Later evidence should test whether the family-rule explanation survives comparison with geography, marriage networks, education, state structure, office pipelines, and retrospective genealogical exaggeration.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Hanji 723's Pei-clan opening.
- Kept the causal claim cautious because the family instructions and admonitions are named but not enumerated.
- Positioned the concept beside family-strategy and lineage-capital pages without merging those distinct mechanisms.
