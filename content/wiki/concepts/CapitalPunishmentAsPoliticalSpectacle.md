## Definition

Capital punishment as political spectacle is the use of an execution's place, audience, duration, bodily treatment, ritual, and aftermath to communicate authority and terror beyond the condemned person's death.

## Current Synthesis

The comparative mechanism joins authority, body, setting, and witnesses. Market execution, roadside crosses, suspended cages, exposed heads, broken bodies, and cannon execution make punishment legible to audiences of subjects, soldiers, kin, and potential rebels. Pain, speed, humiliation, bodily integrity, and duration are separable design choices; rapid death can remain spectacular when machinery, destruction, or aftermath is made exemplary. [[21-dongxifang-de-sixing-cong-shizijia-dao-paojue-lpxzwqy8b9naizslmotzcmz4liso|Weird History episode 21]]

The Chinese taxonomy places corpse exposure, 醢, dismemberment, decapitation, and waist-cutting within the broad 大辟 category. [[PengYue|彭越]]'s reported posthumous distribution to other lords makes the audience especially clear: once the condemned is dead, bodily treatment can no longer deter that person, so its operative audience is the living political field. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1139-jiemi-gudai-rouxing-yige-bi-yige-xiaren-lgnj-8smphspf8nud-2o3xh_lggk|Hanji 1139]]

## Key Claims

- Execution can be designed for an audience rather than only for the condemned person.
- Pain, humiliation, bodily destruction, exposure, speed, and duration are separable spectacle tools.
- Corpse display or destruction communicates power after the condemned can no longer be deterred.
- Public warning can extend punishment toward kin, followers, military units, and rival elites.
- Rapid death can remain spectacular when bodily treatment or execution machinery is made exemplary.
- Similar bodily practices across regions do not establish identical laws, meanings, or institutions.

- [[21-dongxifang-de-sixing-cong-shizijia-dao-paojue-lpxzwqy8b9naizslmotzcmz4liso|Weird History episode 21]] compares public and posthumous display across multiple regions as communication to witnesses.

### Political distribution of bodily warning

- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1139-jiemi-gudai-rouxing-yige-bi-yige-xiaren-lgnj-8smphspf8nud-2o3xh_lggk|Hanji 1139]] reports that Peng Yue's remains were distributed among lords as a warning against rebellion.

### Speed and spectacle

- [[21-dongxifang-de-sixing-cong-shizijia-dao-paojue-lpxzwqy8b9naizslmotzcmz4liso|Weird History episode 21]] contrasts prolonged crucifixion and confinement with rapid beheading, firing squads, and cannon execution while retaining audience and authority as the common mechanism.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1139-jiemi-gudai-rouxing-yige-bi-yige-xiaren-lgnj-8smphspf8nud-2o3xh_lggk|Hanji 1139]] places killing, corpse destruction, and display within the broad category of 大辟 rather than treating it as one method.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The two sources are structured popular-history summaries that combine better- and worse-attested examples. Exact mechanics, dates, motives, audience effects, and transmitted stories require primary or specialist verification. Political spectacle is a mechanism, not a complete explanation of any execution; retribution, incapacitation, legal custom, religion, status, and administrative convenience may also matter. Cross-regional resemblance must not flatten institutional differences.

## What Changed

- Added the 大辟 taxonomy as a Chinese source-scoped extension.
- Added Peng Yue's reported posthumous distribution as a clear elite-audience mechanism.
- Reinforced the distinction between death itself and political communication through the corpse.
