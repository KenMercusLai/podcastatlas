# War Cost Tempo Discipline / 战争成本与速决纪律

War cost tempo discipline / 战争成本与速决纪律 is the strategic lesson [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-94-shashen-baiqi-changping-zhi-zhan-zhong-de-caobao-mingjiang-3-ljwz8ebk0iyfleivkefvvoonrqb9]] draws from [[SunziBingfa|《孙子兵法》]] after the [[ChangpingBattle|长平之战]]. The host treats "兵贵神速" less as a speed fetish than as a cost argument: war consumes food, manpower, morale, and state capacity, so victory should be pursued by nonmilitary means when possible and finished quickly when fighting cannot be avoided.

The episode applies that frame to Qin's own victory. [[QinState|秦国]] destroys Zhao's main force, but [[BaiQi|白起]] later resists replacing [[WangLing|王陵]] against [[Handan|邯郸]] because Qin is already "国虚民饥." In this reading, a decisive tactical or operational win does not erase exhaustion; the winner may still need time for population recovery, food recovery, and campaign sequencing before the next strategic move.

The concept also disciplines late-Warring-States expansion narratives. Qin can accelerate unification through Changping, but the source says immediate overextension remains dangerous. War cost tempo discipline therefore sits between [[WarringStatesMilitaryTransformation|战国军事形态转变]] and [[YuanjiaoJingongStrategy|远交近攻]]: larger state armies and better strategy make conquest possible, but they do not abolish logistics or demographic limits.

## Key Claims
- The reason to prefer quick victory is that prolonged war is expensive, not that speed is always tactically correct.
- Nonmilitary victory is strategically superior when it can preserve resources and still secure the objective.
- A state can win a battle and still be too depleted for an immediate hard follow-on campaign.
- Population and food recovery are strategic constraints, not merely background conditions.
