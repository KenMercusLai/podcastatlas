# Speech Neuroprosthetics

## Definition
Speech neuroprosthetics are brain-machine-interface systems that attempt to restore communication by decoding speech-related neural activity into words, text, or embodied speech output.

## Current Synthesis
The two edits of the interview present speech neuroprosthetics as restoration technology for people whose cognition and language intent remain but whose voluntary movement or speech output is blocked. [[EddieChang]] describes the [[BRAVOTrial]] as a concrete case: implanted electrodes over speech-motor cortex record attempted movements for articulators such as the larynx, lips, tongue, and jaw, then machine-learning systems and context-based word modeling translate those patterns into on-screen words or sentences.

The current synthesis is bounded optimism. The technology can be clinically meaningful because it targets a devastating communication barrier in locked-in syndrome or severe paralysis. It is still not general mind reading: the early system used constrained vocabulary, required weeks of training, made errors, depended on embodied attempts to speak, and used language-model-style correction to improve imperfect decoding. The wired skull port adds infection risk and motivates wireless development. Avatar work extends the goal from text output toward feedback and social presence, but remains source-scoped.

## Key Claims
- Speech neuroprosthetics restore attempted speech output rather than decode arbitrary thoughts.
- Invasive cortical electrodes can capture speech-motor signals closer to the articulator-control system than external behavioral interfaces.
- Machine-learning decoding and contextual language correction can combine neural signals with word-sequence expectations.
- Communication restoration should be evaluated through agency, speed, accuracy, embodiment, and usability, not only technical novelty.
- Avatar output may improve social presence and user feedback by making decoded speech feel more embodied.
- Enhancement beyond medical restoration raises separate ethical and social questions, especially when surgery is involved.
- Present interfaces remain far below the bandwidth of natural neural communication and should be evaluated as evolving clinical systems.

## Evidence
- Clinical target - both [[essentials-the-science-of-learning-speaking-languages-dr-eddie-chang-scim7185728420]] and [[the-science-of-learning-speaking-languages-dr-eddie-chang-scim2227065926]] describe locked-in syndrome as intact cognition and awareness with no reliable voluntary movement or speech channel.
- Signal and decoding - [[essentials-the-science-of-learning-speaking-languages-dr-eddie-chang-scim7185728420]] and [[the-science-of-learning-speaking-languages-dr-eddie-chang-scim2227065926]] describe cortical articulator signals, machine-learning algorithms, weeks of training, and context-based sentence modeling similar to autocorrect.
- Current limits - both sources report an early 50-word vocabulary, imperfect accuracy, and giggling-related interference; [[the-science-of-learning-speaking-languages-dr-eddie-chang-scim2227065926]] adds the wired-port infection risk and need for wireless systems.
- Embodied output - [[essentials-the-science-of-learning-speaking-languages-dr-eddie-chang-scim7185728420]] and [[the-science-of-learning-speaking-languages-dr-eddie-chang-scim2227065926]] describe future avatar communication using speech movements and facial expressions rather than text alone.
- Bandwidth and augmentation - [[the-science-of-learning-speaking-languages-dr-eddie-chang-scim2227065926]] contrasts current interface capacity with the much larger bandwidth of evolved biological communication and separates restoration from speculative enhancement.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources are two edits of one public conversation, not independent evidence, a full clinical-trial report, or a device-performance benchmark. They do not establish broad safety, general availability, long-term implant risk, comparative effectiveness, open-vocabulary accuracy, or non-medical enhancement acceptability.
