# Local Elite Security Panic / 地方士族安全恐慌

Local elite security panic / 地方士族安全恐慌 enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-974-caocao-xiang-ta-tuo-qi-xian-zi-weihe-zhuanshen-ai-daozi-lhfabab7xv2vfsgnhr9ozwqhmhd|Hanji 974]] as the source's explanation for why the Yanzhou betrayal becomes politically plausible. The concept names a regime-risk pattern: when a ruler's purge of a visible elite figure signals that criticism, lineage, office, or local standing no longer protects anyone, surrounding elites may treat rebellion as preemptive self-defense.

The source case is [[BianRangLateHan|边让]]. After Bian Rang humiliates [[CaoCao|曹操]] and challenges his legitimacy, Cao Cao's harsh retaliation shocks people tied to [[YanzhouLateHan|兖州]] local society. [[ChenGongLateHan|陈宫]] and [[ZhangMiaoLateHan|张邈]] are then read as moving from discomfort to fear: if Bian Rang can be destroyed, their own safety under Cao Cao may also be unstable.

This differs from [[LocalEliteProxySelection|地方豪强代理人选择]]. Local elite proxy selection explains why elites may choose a weaker governor to preserve local interests. Local elite security panic explains why elites may invite an armed outsider such as [[LyuBu|吕布]] when the current ruler's violence makes continued cooperation feel unsafe.

## Key Claims
- Elite cooperation depends partly on predictable security, not only on appointments, etiquette, or private friendship.
- A purge of one prominent critic can become a public signal about the danger facing a whole local network.
- Fear can combine with ambition: Zhang Miao and Chen Gong are not presented as only self-defensive or only opportunistic.
- The mechanism should remain source-scoped where Hanji 974 ties Bian Rang's opposition to early [[TuntianSystem|屯田制]] or land-clearance pressure.
