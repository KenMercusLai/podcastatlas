# Plantation Slavery Control Regime

## Definition

The Plantation Slavery Control Regime is the linked apparatus of surveillance, mobility restriction, exemplary punishment, sale, and terror used to compel labor and suppress escape or revolt in slaveholding societies.

## Current Synthesis

Plantation labor depended on more than legal ownership. Patrols, passes, curfews, overseers, bodily punishment, family separation, and public examples of suffering made coercion routine and communicated the cost of resistance. Where enslaved people were numerous, slaveholders often treated internal revolt like a standing security threat.

The regime also reshaped the ruling society. Continuous dependence on domination trained children and adults in arbitrary authority, while fear of uprising encouraged further monitoring and violence. The result was a feedback loop: forced labor produced wealth, wealth required coercive maintenance, resistance anxiety widened control, and wider control normalized brutality.

## Key Claims

- Legal ownership required a practical infrastructure of surveillance and force.
- Mobility controls such as patrols, passes, and curfews treated enslaved movement as a security problem.
- Bodily punishment compelled work and served as a warning to others.
- Sale and family separation were control mechanisms as well as market transactions.
- Fear of revolt turned slaveholding communities toward standing internal-defense practices.
- Unchecked domination corrupted slaveholders and reproduced authoritarian behavior across generations.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode ranges across different colonies, cities, plantations, and centuries, so no single control pattern should be assumed universal. Testimony about extreme violence establishes institutional possibility and lived suffering but not the frequency of every practice. Enslaved agency, mutual aid, negotiation, flight, maroonage, sabotage, and revolt complicate any picture of total control.

## What Changed

- Established control as an infrastructure rather than a series of isolated punishments.
- Added fear of revolt and slaveholder moral corruption as mutually reinforcing consequences.
- Included market sale and family separation inside the control regime.
