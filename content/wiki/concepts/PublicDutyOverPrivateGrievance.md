# Public Duty Over Private Grievance / 对事不对人式公义

## Definition

Public duty over private grievance is the leadership discipline of refusing to let personal resentment decide public action. It does not erase hostility or injury; it subordinates them to law, office duty, collective order, or the state's legitimate purpose.

## Current Synthesis

The concept now has two complementary cases. In [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-929-duishi-buduiren-ni-xin-le-ma-lgaxj0jzopddmt3c5hibnexqlhb3|Hanji 929]], [[GaiXunLateHan|盖勋]] dislikes Su Zhenghe but still stops an illegal attempt to murder him, rejects Su's gratitude as factional misunderstanding, and later risks himself to rescue Zuo Chang and Xin Zeng despite being injured by their corruption.

In [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-753-banchao-beigao-heizhuang-lt-sksxrz30sawit81eqbeonhzv|Hanji 753]], [[LiYiEasternHan|李毅]] slanders [[BanChaoEasternHan|班超]] to [[EmperorZhangOfHan|汉章帝]], yet Ban Chao sends Li Yi back with the Wusun hostage prince rather than keep him for private satisfaction. His explanation makes the principle explicit: because Li Yi had slandered him, using public command to indulge revenge would not be loyal minister conduct.

Hanji 1157 adds a personnel-selection variant. [[JiangQinEasternWu|蒋钦]] has reason to resent [[XuShengEasternWu|徐盛]] after Xu Sheng detains his subordinate and seeks the man's execution, yet Jiang Qin later recommends Xu Sheng's loyalty, diligence, courage, and command capacity to [[SunQuan|孙权]]. Here duty does not only block retaliation; it requires giving useful ability a fair public evaluation.

## Key Claims

- Public action remains legitimate only when it is governed by duty rather than by the actor's private likes and resentments.
- The principle can require protecting someone one dislikes when unlawful harm or public disorder is the alternative.
- The principle can also require sending away an enemy when keeping him would only satisfy personal anger.
- In personnel decisions, the principle can require recommending a capable former adversary rather than silently withholding recognition.
- Refusing private retaliation is not reconciliation; it is disciplined boundary-setting between office and emotion.
- The pattern depends on self-knowledge: the actor must notice when a public power could be serving a private appetite.
- The concept is strongest when the injured or offended party has enough power to retaliate but chooses not to use it that way.

## Evidence

- Protecting a disliked person from unlawful killing: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-929-duishi-buduiren-ni-xin-le-ma-lgaxj0jzopddmt3c5hibnexqlhb3|Hanji 929]] shows Gai Xun stopping planned revenge against Su Zhenghe even though he personally detests Su.
- Refusing gratitude as factional capture: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-929-duishi-buduiren-ni-xin-le-ma-lgaxj0jzopddmt3c5hibnexqlhb3|Hanji 929]] has Gai Xun reject Su's thanks and explain that he acted for policy and justice, not friendship.
- Rescuing corrupt colleagues despite injury: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-929-duishi-buduiren-ni-xin-le-ma-lgaxj0jzopddmt3c5hibnexqlhb3|Hanji 929]] records Gai Xun risking himself to rescue Zuo Chang and Xin Zeng while still condemning their misrule.
- Refusing revenge against a slanderer: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-753-banchao-beigao-heizhuang-lt-sksxrz30sawit81eqbeonhzv|Hanji 753]] records Ban Chao sending Li Yi back because Li Yi had slandered him, not despite that fact.
- Ministerial-duty boundary: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-753-banchao-beigao-heizhuang-lt-sksxrz30sawit81eqbeonhzv|Hanji 753]] makes Ban Chao's standard explicit: keeping Li Yi to satisfy himself would not be loyal minister conduct.
- Grievance-separated recommendation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1157-duanzan-de-suncao-lianmeng-yaobai-de-sunquan-lpngfqsoz-zjr2iytaaox89df1zcj|Hanji 1157]] says Jiang Qin recommends Xu Sheng despite their earlier subordinate-related conflict.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

- Public duty over private grievance is not softness. Both Gai Xun and Ban Chao operate within coercive political and military systems; the point is disciplined use of power, not refusal of force altogether.
- The principle does not prove that the protected person is innocent or worthy. Su Zhenghe, Zuo Chang, Xin Zeng, and Li Yi remain morally or politically compromised within their episodes.
- The concept can fail if "public duty" is only a mask for private advantage; the evidence here is strongest because the actors act against their own immediate resentment.
- Emotional clarity matters: Gai Xun's rejection of gratitude and Ban Chao's explanation to Xu Gan both prevent others from misreading restraint as personal alliance.
- Jiang Qin's recommendation does not prove that Xu Sheng's earlier action was lawful or that the grievance disappeared; it shows only that the episode separates the dispute from a later capacity judgment.

## What Changed

- Added Jiang Qin's recommendation of Xu Sheng as a personnel-selection variant.
- Extended the concept from refusing retaliation to affirmatively recognizing useful ability across a grievance.
- Preserved the distinction between fair evaluation, reconciliation, and approval of the earlier conduct.
