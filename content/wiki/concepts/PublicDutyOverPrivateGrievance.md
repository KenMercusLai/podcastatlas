# Public Duty Over Private Grievance / 对事不对人式公义

Public duty over private grievance / 对事不对人式公义 is the ethical pattern [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-929-duishi-buduiren-ni-xin-le-ma-lgaxj0jzopddmt3c5hibnexqlhb3|Hanji 929]] builds through [[GaiXunLateHan|盖勋]]. The concept does not require liking an adversary or pretending past injury did not occur. It requires that public judgment, office duty, and law not be decided by private hatred.

The first source case is [[SuZhengheLateHan|苏正和]]. Gai Xun dislikes him, but when [[LiangHuLateHan|梁鹄]] considers killing Su to avoid implication, Gai Xun stops the plan because it would make Liang Hu disloyal and inhumane. Gai later refuses Su's thanks, making the distinction sharper: the rescue was not private reconciliation, but public responsibility.

The second source case is [[ZuoChangLateHan|左昌]]. Zuo Chang steals grain, resents Gai Xun's warning, and tries to expose him to danger, but when rebels besiege Zuo at Ji County, Gai still pressures [[XinZengLateHan|辛曾]] to support a rescue. The source therefore separates the truth of private grievance from the duties created by office and military crisis.

This concept sits beside [[EmotionalAttachmentPoliticalDistortion|情执政治判断失衡]], but points in the opposite direction. Emotional attachment political distortion names a failure where feeling becomes the whole decision. Public duty over private grievance names the discipline of keeping feeling visible but bounded.

## Key Claims

- Private dislike can remain morally and psychologically real while still being irrelevant to a public-duty decision.
- Protecting a disliked person can be justified by protecting law, office, and the larger order rather than by forgiving the person.
- Refusing personal thanks can clarify that an action was not private favor or renewed friendship.
- Public duty is tested most sharply when the beneficiary has harmed or tried to harm the actor.
- In a military setting, resentment toward a superior cannot by itself suspend rescue or command obligations.
- The concept is narrower than general kindness: it is not sentimentality but judgment discipline under office constraints.
