# Dongmen Yellow Dog / 东门黄犬

Dongmen Yellow Dog / 东门黄犬 is the regret idiom [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-131-1-qinxiang-lisi-yisheng-jingming-que-zao-yaozhan-liupvalcghtas2ibfyjrtdz6ctg7|Qinji 131-1]] draws from [[LiSi|李斯]] before execution. As he is taken to the marketplace in [[Xianyang|咸阳]], Li Si tells his son that they can no longer leave [[Shangcai|上蔡]]'s east gate for ordinary hunting pleasure.

The source uses the image to close Li Si's earlier [[MousePhilosophy|老鼠哲学]] arc. As a young clerk, Li Si reads environment as destiny and leaves a small Chu post for study, Qin service, and imperial power. At the end, the remembered value is not chancellorship, standardization, or court triumph, but a modest pleasure from before the climb.

The idiom therefore connects personal regret to [[PowerExitTrap|权力退场困境]]. It does not simply say that office is bad. It marks the point at which success has trapped the successful person so deeply that ordinary life can only return as memory. In this episode's reading, Li Si's failure to withdraw after success, his weak support network, and his earlier elimination of rivals all make the regret sharper.

## Key Claims
- The idiom contrasts spectacular office with small, ordinary pleasure.
- It turns Li Si's downfall into a retrospective judgment on ambition, not only a record of punishment.
- The source uses the scene to show how late regret can reveal what power had made invisible.
- The image is linked to failed exit: by the time Li Si recognizes what he misses, his political and family position is no longer recoverable.
- Qinji 131-1 makes the idiom a companion to [[MousePhilosophy|老鼠哲学]]: the move to the "granary" creates achievement, but it also exposes the actor to palace politics and irreversible ruin.
