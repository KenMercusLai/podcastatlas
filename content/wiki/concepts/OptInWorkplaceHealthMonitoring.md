# Opt-In Workplace Health Monitoring

## Definition
Opt-in workplace health monitoring is an employee-initiated model for sharing wearable or behavioral health data in which participation, data use, and personal benefit are disclosed rather than imposed.

## Current Synthesis
Formal consent is necessary but not sufficient in an employment relationship. A credible program needs understandable purpose, voluntary entry and exit, data minimization, separation from employment decisions, limited retention, and a concrete employee benefit so that workplace power does not turn an apparent option into surveillance pressure.

## Key Claims
- Employees should initiate participation rather than be enrolled through a workplace mandate.
- Employers must explain whether data supports the employee's health journey or enables intrusive oversight.
- Programs should be framed and operated as benefits, with transparent use of generated insights.
- Wearable data should remain separated from performance management, discipline, and employment eligibility decisions.

## Evidence
### Agency and transparency
- [[ep-19-navigating-the-future-of-workplace-health-and-benefits-with-ai]] recommends employee opt-in, clear motivation, transparent insight use, and benefit rather than mandate framing.

### Surveillance concern
- [[ep-19-navigating-the-future-of-workplace-health-and-benefits-with-ai]] explicitly raises employee hesitation and the risk that continuous monitoring creates a nanny-state workplace culture.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- The episode does not specify withdrawal procedures, retention periods, secondary-use controls, employment-firewall rules, vendor contracts, or remedies for misuse.
- Economic or cultural pressure can weaken voluntariness even when a program is formally opt-in.

## What Changed
- Created a consent and power-boundary concept for workplace wearable programs.
