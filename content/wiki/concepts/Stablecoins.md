# Stablecoins

## Definition
Stablecoins are blockchain-based tokens designed to track a reference asset, usually a fiat currency, and function as settlement, payment, trading-liquidity, or dollar-access infrastructure.

## Current Synthesis
Across the bounded sources, stablecoins have the wiki's strongest crypto use case outside speculative trading: cross-border settlement, B2B payments, remittances, invoices, on-chain markets, agent payments, and dollar access. Regulation and reserve design can make these rails more legible to institutions, while Treasury-backed reserves can also turn issuers into material buyers of short-term U.S. debt.

The same properties are dual-use. Fast global transfer and relative price stability can serve lawful users, sanctioned actors, scam networks, and politically connected issuers. The current judgment is therefore neither blanket endorsement nor rejection: stablecoin value depends on reserve quality, redemption, licensing, identity controls, consumer recourse, governance, and whether the underlying payment problem is better than existing rails.

## Key Claims
- Payment and settlement demand gives stablecoins a more concrete adoption base than purely speculative crypto assets.
- Reserve-backed issuers can extend dollar reach and Treasury demand while creating private monetary-governance and concentration questions.
- Regulated frameworks can support bank and enterprise adoption, but issuer rules, platform rewards, and deposit competition remain contested.
- Stablecoins are dual-use infrastructure: lawful financial access and sanctions or AML evasion can rely on the same speed and global reach.
- Agent and B2B payments are plausible growth areas, but irreversibility and automation increase the need for spend controls, escrow, mediation, and recourse.
- Political branding or office-linked ownership adds conflict-of-interest risk beyond ordinary peg, reserve, and compliance analysis.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not establish that stablecoins are always cheaper, safer, or preferable to bank, card, or real-time-payment systems. Reserve composition, redemption rights, operational resilience, smart-contract risk, issuer concentration, transaction screening, privacy, reversibility, and jurisdiction matter. USDC and Tether claims are participant-attributed, and reported illicit-use totals or political-profit estimates remain source-scoped.

## What Changed
- Elevated B2B cross-border settlement as the clearest new growth claim.
- Added the fully reserved regulatory model and bank-versus-platform distribution conflict.
- Preserved sanctions, AML, irreversibility, and political-conflict risks alongside the adoption case.
- Compressed the prior source-led accumulation into a single current judgment.
