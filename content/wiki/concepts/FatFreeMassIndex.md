# Fat-Free Mass Index / 去脂体重指数

## Definition
Fat-free mass index (FFMI) is the episode's height-adjusted measure of how much muscle a person carries, calculated from total body weight, body fat percentage, and height, and used as a health floor rather than an aesthetic score.

## Current Synthesis
Galpin splits hypertrophy into two questions. The aesthetic portion of muscle size is entirely a personal choice, while the health-relevant question is whether the person carries a sufficient amount of muscle, which is assessed through body composition testing and described as best done with a DEXA scan.

The number to watch is FFMI. Targets are roughly 20 or higher for men and 18 for women, above 24 to 25 for a man is described as a lot of muscle mass assuming reasonable leanness of roughly sub-30% body fat for men and sub-35% for women, and men below 17 or women below 15 are described as being in an area of pretty severe physiological detriment from insufficient muscle. Because FFMI can be estimated from total body weight, body-fat percentage, and height, standard online calculators make the health floor checkable without a lab.

## Key Claims
- Hypertrophy splits into an aesthetic choice and a health-relevant minimum amount of muscle.
- Body composition testing, ideally a DEXA scan, is the measurement route, with FFMI as the height-adjusted number to watch.
- Roughly 20 or higher for men and 18 for women is the target zone, and above 24 to 25 for a man is a lot of muscle when leanness is reasonable.
- Men below 17 and women below 15 are described as being in an area of severe physiological detriment from insufficient muscle.
- FFMI is calculable outside a lab from total body weight, body-fat percentage, and height using standard calculators.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The cutoffs are the episode's practical framing rather than consensus clinical thresholds, and they assume reasonable leanness, which makes FFMI misleading for very lean or very heavy people. FFMI is also a population-level stat rather than an individualized diagnosis, and body-composition estimates vary by method, so a score near a boundary is best treated as a prompt to look at function, strength, and trends rather than as a verdict.
