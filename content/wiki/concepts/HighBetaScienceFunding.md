# High-Beta Science Funding

## Definition
High-beta science funding is the argument that public science should deliberately fund some low-probability, high-upside people and ideas because rare breakthroughs can justify many failed attempts.

## Current Synthesis
The All-In Weinstein interview sharpens the wiki's existing portfolio-funding branch by making it more people-centered and more tolerant of failure. Weinstein argues that markets and ordinary philanthropy cannot reliably fund deep basic science, while grant committees often prefer safe deliverables. The resulting funding logic is not "anything goes"; it is a call for credible experts to back blocked but serious researchers when the upside is civilizationally large.

## Key Claims
- Basic science can have enormous public value before it has a clear commercial endpoint, so markets often underfund it.
- Grant systems that require specific near-term objectives can suppress strange, high-upside work.
- A small probability of an enormous scientific gain may justify funding even when most projects fail.
- People selection matters because some valuable scientists may be blocked by senior gatekeepers who would still not bet against them.
- Public funding still needs judgment, review, and accountability because high variance can otherwise become a cover for weak work.

## Evidence
- Market-failure basis: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-eric-weinstein-the-state-of-american-science-breakthrough-coverups-and-the-danger-of-physics-42568645]] says conservatives often overestimate markets' ability to fund basic science and uses homotopy theory as an example of knowledge without an obvious industrial endpoint.
- Upside logic: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-eric-weinstein-the-state-of-american-science-breakthrough-coverups-and-the-danger-of-physics-42568645]] argues that a very small chance of curing all cancer could justify investment because the payoff is so large.
- Grant-culture critique: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-eric-weinstein-the-state-of-american-science-breakthrough-coverups-and-the-danger-of-physics-42568645]] contrasts risky discovery with departments choosing high-probability, modest outcomes and exaggerating their importance.
- People-first selection: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-eric-weinstein-the-state-of-american-science-breakthrough-coverups-and-the-danger-of-physics-42568645]] suggests funding people whom established leaders may block but would not confidently short.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
High-upside rhetoric can hide poor evidence, bad incentives, or favoritism if the selection process lacks strong judgment. The source does not provide a complete grant-design mechanism, so the concept should remain paired with [[ScienceFundingPortfolioLogic]], outcome tracking, and evidence-based review.
