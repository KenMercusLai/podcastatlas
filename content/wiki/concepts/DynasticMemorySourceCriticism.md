## Definition
Source Criticism of Dynastic Memory is the practice of testing later official accounts of politically useful ancestors against contemporary rank, offices, campaign roles, textual absence, and independent inscriptions.

## Current Synthesis
[[LiHu|Li Hu]] supplies a two-directional example. Tang ancestral veneration plausibly elevated his rank and converted participation in campaigns into independent command, so official dynastic memory cannot be read transparently. Yet skepticism can also overreach: a Sui epitaph reportedly confirms that some high titles existed before Tang, correcting the thesis that all were retrospective fabrication. Good criticism therefore compares provenance and date, distinguishes rank from fame and participation from command, and remains revisable when new evidence appears.

## Key Claims
- Later ruling houses have incentives to raise the status of dynastic ancestors.
- Biography absence, inconsistent ordering, office history, and campaign role can expose retrospective reshaping.
- Participation in an operation should not automatically become independent command credit.
- Official histories remain evidence even when their narrative has political incentives.
- Inscriptions can provide pre-dynastic checks on later titles and ranks.
- New evidence may qualify both celebratory tradition and modern debunking.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
An official source's political incentive does not make every statement false, and one newly found inscription may itself require contextual and textual criticism. The episode summary does not reproduce the relevant passages or epitaph, so the precise title sequence remains source-scoped.
