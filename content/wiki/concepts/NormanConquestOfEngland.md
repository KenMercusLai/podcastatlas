# Norman Conquest of England

## Definition
The Norman Conquest of England is the 1066 invasion and succession conflict that the source presents through the visual narrative of the [[BayeuxTapestry|Bayeux Tapestry]].

## Current Synthesis
The episode does not give a full military history. It uses the tapestry's surviving sequence to show conquest as political storytelling: [[EdwardTheConfessor]] sends [[HaroldGodwinson]] to Normandy, Harold appears to swear that [[WilliamTheConqueror]] should receive the crown, Harold takes the throne, Halley's Comet appears as an omen, and William wins in battle.

## Key Claims
- The conquest is remembered in this source through textile narrative rather than a prose chronicle.
- Succession legitimacy is central: oath, throne-taking, omen, invasion, and victory are arranged into a political argument.
- The tapestry makes Norman and English identity visually legible through hair and moustache conventions.
- The missing ending keeps the conquest narrative interpretively open because the presumed coronation endpoint is not preserved.
- The event becomes a durable art-memory case because violent victory is stitched by unnamed makers and reinterpreted across centuries.

## Evidence
- Narrative sequence - [[ire-and-ice-the-toll-of-americas-deportations-6a993426809ae7c2f4293df9]] summarizes the Edward-Harold-William sequence, Halley's Comet, battle scenes, Harold's death, English flight, and William's victory.
- Visual identity - [[ire-and-ice-the-toll-of-americas-deportations-6a993426809ae7c2f4293df9]] says Normans and English are visually distinguished by hair and moustaches.
- Missing ending - [[ire-and-ice-the-toll-of-americas-deportations-6a993426809ae7c2f4293df9]] says the ending may have shown William's coronation, but this remains uncertain.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This concept page is bounded to the episode's Bayeux Tapestry framing. It does not adjudicate broader historiographical debates about Norman legitimacy, battle details, or the tapestry's patron.
