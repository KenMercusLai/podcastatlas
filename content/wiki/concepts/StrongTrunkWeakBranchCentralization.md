# Strong-Trunk Weak-Branch Centralization / 强干弱枝式集权

Strong-trunk weak-branch centralization / 强干弱枝式集权 is the control logic [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-123-1-shen-zhi-yuyan-wangqin-zhe-hu-jing-shi-zhi-lg7m3vfdbcweb4joxe4jvhxjx6y8]] uses to interpret [[QinShiHuang|嬴政 / 秦始皇]]'s order to remove large city walls and river levees after the [[Jieshi|碣石]] tour. The point is not only construction policy. In the episode's reading, walls and defensive hydraulic works can preserve local autonomy, letting powerful families or regional forces resist central command.

The source connects this logic to [[Confucius|孔子]]'s earlier "堕三都" precedent in [[LuState|鲁国]]: a ruler who wants effective central authority must reduce local power bases that can become semi-independent. Qin's version is much larger and harsher because it acts across a newly unified empire rather than inside one old aristocratic state.

This concept extends [[ImperialHomogenization|帝国整齐划一]]. Standards, commandery-county rule, elite relocation, and weapon confiscation change administration, population, and arms; strong-trunk weak-branch centralization adds the built environment as another object of post-conquest control.

## Key Claims
- Local defensive infrastructure can be treated as political capacity, not only as public works.
- Removing large walls and levees reduces the ability of local actors to hold territory against the center.
- The episode does not claim every wall or embankment disappears; it says the policy mainly targets large sites that could support separatist defense.
- The concept sits beside [[PostConquestDisarmament|统一后收兵器]] and [[EliteRelocationControl|豪杰迁徙控制]] as another way Qin weakens rebellion capacity after conquest.
