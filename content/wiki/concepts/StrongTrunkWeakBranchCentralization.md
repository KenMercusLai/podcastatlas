## Definition

Strong-trunk weak-branch centralization / 强干弱枝式集权 is the imperial governance logic of concentrating practical capacity at the center while reducing the military, demographic, infrastructural, or elite-network capacity of regions that could resist central command.

## Current Synthesis

The Qin version appears through [[QinShiHuang|嬴政 / 秦始皇]]'s order to remove large city walls and river levees after the [[Jieshi|碣石]] tour. The point is not only construction policy: walls and defensive hydraulic works can preserve local autonomy and make regional actors harder to command.

The Western Han version works through [[GuanzhongEliteRelocation|关中豪强迁徙]] and [[MausoleumCountyEliteRelocation|陵县式豪强迁徙]]. Instead of dismantling physical defenses, the state moves powerful households and senior officials' families toward the imperial capital and mausoleum zone, making the center stronger while thinning regional power bases.

## Key Claims

- Regional capacity can reside in walls, levees, armed resources, population, elite networks, and household registration, not only formal offices.
- Qin's version weakens local defensive infrastructure after conquest.
- Western Han's version strengthens Guanzhong and mausoleum settlements by moving wealthy and powerful households toward the capital region.
- The same centralization logic can look like security policy, population policy, ritual-mausoleum policy, or elite supervision depending on the institutional surface.
- Strong-trunk policy creates legitimacy and consent costs when local families lose ancestral, property, or livelihood continuity.

## Evidence

- Built-environment control: [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-123-1-shen-zhi-yuyan-wangqin-zhe-hu-jing-shi-zhi-lg7m3vfdbcweb4joxe4jvhxjx6y8|Qinji 123-1]] reads Qin's removal of large walls and levees as reducing local ability to resist the center.
- Precedent logic: [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-123-1-shen-zhi-yuyan-wangqin-zhe-hu-jing-shi-zhi-lg7m3vfdbcweb4joxe4jvhxjx6y8|Qinji 123-1]] links the logic to [[Confucius|孔子]]'s "堕三都" precedent in [[LuState|鲁国]].
- Han relocation logic: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-526-hanchao-qipa-zhidu-huangdi-si-le-jiu-neng-qianhukou-lkht_jo0z7je4gbyk10ckuxv4cub|Hanji 526]] says moving Guandong wealthy households and senior officials' families into mausoleum counties strengthens the capital and weakens local magnates.
- Policy revival: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-526-hanchao-qipa-zhidu-huangdi-si-le-jiu-neng-qianhukou-lkht_jo0z7je4gbyk10ckuxv4cub|Hanji 526]] says Chen Tang argues for reviving Changlingyi under Chengdi to address powerful-household growth and fiscal strain.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept should not be flattened into one institution. Qinji 123-1 concerns walls, levees, and post-conquest local capacity; Hanji 526 concerns capital-region population concentration and household relocation. Hanji 526 also records moral and political resistance to forced migration, especially ancestral-grave separation, livelihood disruption, and elite unease.
