# Asymmetric Energy Ceasefire

## Definition
An asymmetric energy ceasefire is a formally reciprocal agreement to stop attacks on energy infrastructure in which the assets protected, pressure surrendered, enforcement exposure, and side benefits differ substantially between the parties.

## Current Synthesis
The source applies this pattern to a reported proposal under which [[Ukraine]] would stop attacks on Russian refineries while [[Russia]] refrained from strikes on Ukrainian refineries. Formal symmetry obscures the different baseline: Russia has many economically important refineries, while the source says Ukraine has one refinery that Russia has already struck. Ukraine would therefore surrender a larger portfolio of available pressure targets.

The surrounding diesel arrangement could compound the imbalance. The source doubts that the promised Russian exports are large or feasible enough to lower world prices materially, while sanctions relief could still create Russian revenue, trade legitimacy, and propaganda value. The reusable test is not whether both sides promise the same category of restraint, but whether they protect comparable value and receive comparable economic, military, and political benefits.

## Key Claims
- Nominally reciprocal restraint can be materially unequal when target portfolios differ.
- Prior damage changes the value of a promise not to attack an asset again.
- A ceasefire can transfer leverage by removing attacks that make war costs visible to the adversary's public.
- Sanctions relief and export legitimacy can matter even when added supply is too small to transform global prices.
- Enforcement, counterfactual capacity, and side payments must be counted alongside the written exchange.

## Evidence
- Target imbalance: [[kicking-kyiv-trumps-doubtful-deals-64ee795d3a366335cf4a682ed4b0f0d5]] contrasts Russia's many valuable refineries with Ukraine's single already-struck refinery.
- Supply qualification: [[kicking-kyiv-trumps-doubtful-deals-64ee795d3a366335cf4a682ed4b0f0d5]] reports a possible maximum below 1% of global demand and doubts about damaged Russian production capacity.
- Political and revenue effects: [[kicking-kyiv-trumps-doubtful-deals-64ee795d3a366335cf4a682ed4b0f0d5]] argues that sanctions relief could benefit Moscow even without a large retail-price effect.
- Visibility leverage: [[kicking-kyiv-trumps-doubtful-deals-64ee795d3a366335cf4a682ed4b0f0d5]] says refinery strikes brought the war closer to ordinary Russians and created political discomfort for [[VladimirPutin]].

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The arrangement was reported rather than supplied as a binding public text, and Russian attacks reportedly continued. Export volumes, refinery capacity, sanctions treatment, price response, political motives, and electoral effects are analyst judgments or source-reported figures. A temporary pause could still have humanitarian or tactical value not captured by comparing refineries alone; this page records the source's structural critique rather than a complete negotiation record.

## What Changed
- Created a bargaining test that compares protected value, surrendered leverage, and side benefits rather than labels alone.
