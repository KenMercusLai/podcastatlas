## Definition

Known-misconduct non-punishment is the deterrence failure that occurs when a ruler knows a protected actor committed serious wrongdoing but does not establish a credible legal consequence.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-752-shiqu-kaoshan-de-ren-gai-ruhe-chushi-lt0qb7alyh0ipf6mk8hvz92cs297|Hanji 752]] defines the pattern through [[DouXianEasternHan|窦宪]]'s seizure of [[QinshuiPrincessEasternHan|沁水公主]]'s estate and attempted truth suppression. [[EmperorZhangOfHan|汉章帝]] rebukes Dou, orders restitution, and withholds major responsibility, but does not legally punish him. The immediate loss is repaired while the public boundary remains weak.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-335-dangzhe-wudi-de-mian-huoqubing-weihe-gan-sha-liguang-zhi-zi-lupxhu64fi0d0xl223zq9pwenmy1|Hanji 335]] supplies a more severe Western Han form. [[HuoQubing|霍去病]] kills [[LiGanWesternHan|李敢]] during an imperial hunt, and [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] is said not merely to omit punishment but to protect Huo with a deer-accident account. The concept therefore ranges from incomplete sanction to active concealment, provided the misconduct is known and protected status prevents a credible legal response.

## Key Claims

- Discovery does not deter misconduct when consequences stop before a credible legal boundary is established.
- Restitution or private disapproval may repair part of the harm while leaving protected status intact.
- Kinship, palace access, military indispensability, or personal favor can make uneven enforcement especially visible.
- Active concealment is a stronger form of the same failure because authority protects the offender and distorts the public record.
- The pattern differs from mercy after judgment because accountability is never established first.

## Evidence

Incomplete sanction:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-752-shiqu-kaoshan-de-ren-gai-ruhe-chushi-lt0qb7alyh0ipf6mk8hvz92cs297|Hanji 752]] says Dou Xian returns the estate and loses major future trust under Zhangdi but receives no legal punishment.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-752-shiqu-kaoshan-de-ren-gai-ruhe-chushi-lt0qb7alyh0ipf6mk8hvz92cs297|Hanji 752]] presents Sima Guang's warning that known evil left unremoved makes the wicked less afraid.

Protected killing and concealment:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-335-dangzhe-wudi-de-mian-huoqubing-weihe-gan-sha-liguang-zhi-zi-lupxhu64fi0d0xl223zq9pwenmy1|Hanji 335]] says Huo Qubing shoots Li Gan, receives no punishment because of Wudi's favor, and is protected by an official deer-collision explanation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept should not be used for all leniency. It requires known wrongdoing, a protected actor, and a response that fails to make accountability credible. Hanji 752 records real partial consequences, so that case is not complete inaction. Hanji 335 gives a compressed popular-history account; Huo's motive, the mechanics of imperial concealment, and Wudi's full deliberation remain source-scoped even though the episode clearly presents non-punishment.

## What Changed

- Added Huo Qubing's protected killing of Li Gan as a more severe active-concealment form.
- Broadened the concept from failed sanction after restitution to deliberate protection of a favored offender.
