# Need-Led AI Product Design

## Definition

Need-led AI product design begins with a recognizable user problem and packages model capability into an understandable outcome, workflow, vocabulary, and control surface rather than asking users to discover value from a general-purpose interface.

## Current Synthesis

The [[MarketplaceTech]] source separates technical possibility from consumer product fit. A model may write software or operate tools, yet most people do not begin with a desire to design and maintain an application. An empty chat box, unfamiliar labels, and a long list of integrations therefore shift problem definition, workflow design, and product education onto the user.

Need-led design reduces that burden. [[MusePersonalAgent|Muse]] is used as an example because reviewing an inbox, summarizing required actions, and performing approved tasks expresses an outcome before it expresses the underlying technology. Task Lit supplies a second source-scoped example in which smoother setup and simpler language may justify an application-layer premium even without unique model capability.

## Key Claims

- Model capability is not consumer value until it maps to a problem users recognize and want solved.
- Blank prompting surfaces impose discovery and specification work that many users cannot or do not want to perform.
- Easier software creation does not remove the motivation, maintenance, judgment, and conceptual work required to decide what to build.
- Workflow, onboarding, vocabulary, setup, and approval design can differentiate products built on similar models.
- Outcome-first assistance can broaden access by hiding unnecessary technical structure while preserving consequential control points.
- Product success should be tested through voluntary return behavior and real-life improvement rather than feature exposure alone.

## Evidence

### Problem recognition and interface burden
- [[tech-20261014-1014-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261014-1014-mp-tech-pod-128]] records Moran's argument that an empty chat field hands people a technology and asks them to invent their own need.
- [[tech-20261014-1014-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261014-1014-mp-tech-pod-128]] reports user difficulty with connectors, integrations, skills, plugins, and distinctions among related AI products.

### Outcome packaging and setup
- [[tech-20261014-1014-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261014-1014-mp-tech-pod-128]] uses Muse's inbox review and approved follow-through as a concrete assistance proposition.
- [[tech-20261014-1014-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261014-1014-mp-tech-pod-128]] presents Task Lit as reducing specialist knowledge and setup friction while charging above underlying model usage.

### Adoption test
- [[tech-20261014-1014-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261014-1014-mp-tech-pod-128]] distinguishes unavoidable or mandatory exposure from voluntary repeated use motivated by meaningful improvement.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

General-purpose interfaces remain valuable for expert users, open-ended exploration, and needs a product team did not anticipate. Hiding complexity can also obscure model limits, data flows, human fallback, or consequential actions. The source gives qualitative examples rather than controlled comparisons, so it supports a design principle without proving that Muse, Task Lit, or any particular interface achieves durable retention or better outcomes.

- Created a product-design synthesis linking concrete needs, interface legibility, setup burden, and voluntary adoption.
