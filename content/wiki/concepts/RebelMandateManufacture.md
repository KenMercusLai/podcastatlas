## Definition

Rebel Mandate Manufacture / 起义天命制造 is the use of fabricated or sacralized signs, names, rumors, prophecies, and bodily anomalies to make a revolt or claimant bid appear destined rather than merely opportunistic.

## Current Synthesis

Qinji 128-2 grounds the concept in [[ChenSheng|Chen Sheng]] and [[WuGuang|Wu Guang]]. After the [[DazexiangUprising|Dazexiang uprising]] becomes necessary for survival, the leaders still need to make rebellion feel larger than a desperate mutiny. They borrow the names of [[FuSu|Fusu]] and [[XiangYan|Xiang Yan]], then create sign-like evidence through fish-belly writing and fox cries, turning "Chen Sheng shall be king" into a message that appears to come from beyond ordinary politics.

Qinji 128-3 adds that the borrowed Fusu name is not random ornament. The host reads Chen Sheng's later [[ZhangChuRegime|Zhang Chu]] choice as an argument that unification should be inherited by a figure or line less violent than Hu Hai and more acceptable to Chu-region followers. The fabricated signs coordinate belief, while the names supply political direction.

Qinji 128-7 adds a less deliberately fabricated Liu Bang version. The white-snake and red-emperor story around [[MangdangMountain|Mangdang Mountain]] is not presented as a planned mobilization trick like Chen Sheng's fish-belly writing, but it performs a similar political function: sacred rumor makes an outlaw leader look destined and encourages local youths to follow.

Hanji 684 broadens the concept into an Eastern Han royal-kin setting through [[LiuYangZhendingKingEasternHan|Liu Yang, Zhending King]]. Liu Yang manufactures a chen text around an unusual neck growth and presents himself as chosen for rule. Unlike Dazexiang, this is not a mass survival revolt; it is a claimant disturbance inside a restoration polity. The mechanism is still mandate manufacture because bodily anomaly and prophetic wording are used to disturb public allegiance.

## Key Claims

- Survival pressure, military ambition, or royal-kin opportunity can start a challenge, but mandate stories help it scale beyond ordinary self-interest.
- Borrowed names matter when they connect a challenge to existing grievance, regional memory, or dynastic identity.
- Fabricated sacred signs can work politically because they coordinate rumor, expectation, and follower confidence.
- The sources present these signs as political devices, not as proof that the supernatural claim is true.
- Sacred rumor can be deliberately staged, as with Chen Sheng and Liu Yang, or retrospectively attached, as with Liu Bang's white-snake story.
- Hanji 684 shows that mandate manufacture can threaten a new restoration order before open military rebellion occurs.

- Borrowed legitimacy names: [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-128-2-qilai-bei-yapo-de-renmen-lpx9-iicwog4nj-nudaj-i4ajb25|Qinji 128-2]] says they invoke Fusu and Xiang Yan to attach rebellion to Qin grievance and Chu memory.
- Programmatic Fusu reading: [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-128-3-chensheng-wuguang-zudui-chenggong-locnaq-fo1fztyud5nghwkteams|Qinji 128-3]] reads the Fusu claim and Zhang Chu creation as a succession argument rather than only anti-unification nostalgia.
- Sacred-rumor extension: [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-128-7-liubang-he-xiangyu-youhe-butong-llwcqatndck4zita2piufwhjpxp1|Qinji 128-7]] treats Liu Bang's white-snake story as sacred coloring that helps make fugitive survival look destined.
- Royal-kin chen claim: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-684-zhending-wang-liuyang-weishenme-beipan-guangwudi-liuxiu-lhtdqtfxihyua1nj8ofsdxl8il8d|Hanji 684]] says Liu Yang manufactures a chen text around his unusual neck growth and implies he is chosen to rule.
- Pre-rebellion risk: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-684-zhending-wang-liuyang-weishenme-beipan-guangwudi-liuxiu-lhtdqtfxihyua1nj8ofsdxl8il8d|Hanji 684]] says Liu Yang's case is later treated as short of actual rebellion, showing that the mandate device itself can still trigger lethal state action.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept should not collapse every omen, prophecy, or rumor into deliberate fabrication. Qinji 128-7 is explicitly weaker on intentional staging than Qinji 128-2, and Hanji 684 gives Liu Yang's device but not his full organizational plan. The current synthesis therefore distinguishes planned sign-making, retrospective sacral rumor, and claimant self-legitimation while grouping them by political function.
