# Wildfire Climate Feedback

Wildfire climate feedback is the source's frame for fires that affect climate systems after they burn. In [[burning-questions-a-more-fire-prone-world-6a687ea64207b2a60119df8b]], [[OliverMorton]] says fires release CO2 that heats the atmosphere for centuries unless carbon removal changes the balance, while smoke can also produce cooling or ecological effects researchers are still studying.

The episode uses the 2019-20 [[Australia]] fires as a striking example. Their smoke is presented as possibly contributing to a multi-year La Nina state and fertilizing Pacific waters enough to produce a large phytoplankton bloom. The concept therefore sits between [[ExtremeWildfire]], [[ClimateAdaptation]], and carbon-removal discussions: fire is not only damage on land but also atmospheric and ocean-system forcing.

## Key Claims
- Fire emissions can create long-lived warming unless offset or removed.
- Smoke can have cooling effects, so the climate effect is not reducible to one direction or one timescale.
- Large fires can affect oceans and atmospheric circulation far from where vegetation burned.
- The uncertainty itself is policy-relevant because prevention, suppression, and adaptation decisions are being made before all feedbacks are fully understood.
