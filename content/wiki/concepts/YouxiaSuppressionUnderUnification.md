## Definition

Youxia suppression under unification / 大一统下的游侠压制 is the political-order pattern in which independent armed loyalty, private retaliation, and aid organized outside official hierarchy become security and legitimacy threats under centralized rule.

## Current Synthesis

Qinji 115-2 anchors the concept in the transition from the Warring States to Qin unification. [[JingKe|荆轲]] can still be imagined through retainer loyalty and weak-state rescue, but his failed assassination is followed by military retaliation, literary mourning, and a political order that increasingly monitors people willing to place personal义 above official command.

Qinji 128-6 shows that suppression does not erase the social style. [[LiuBang|刘邦]]'s friendship, generosity, boldness, and local loyalty survive under Qin as county-level social capital. Once rebellion opens, those ties combine with a [[GrassrootsOfficialNetwork|基层官吏网络政治资本]] and become politically convertible. The concept therefore distinguishes delegitimization by the state from disappearance in society.

Hanji 284 part 1 adds a historiographical and ethical dispute. [[XunYue|荀悦]] is presented as treating rule-defying youxia as a symptom of disorder, while [[SimaQian|司马迁]] values the willingness to aid people abandoned by status-conscious officials. This does not settle private action as virtuous; it shows why centralized authority and vulnerable people can evaluate the same extra-institutional capacity differently.

## Key Claims

- Unification changes private armed loyalty from a usable retainer virtue into a security risk.
- Political suppression can narrow legitimacy without eliminating youxia habits, local trust, or their later mobilization value.
- Literary and historical memory preserve admiration for figures whose conduct the governing order cannot safely authorize.
- The state-order case against youxia emphasizes law, office, hierarchy, and predictable public authority.
- The sympathetic case emphasizes practical aid when morally credentialed officials or institutions fail people in crisis.
- Neither frame makes all private violence just or all official suppression legitimate; the conflict must be judged through conduct, consequence, and institutional context.

## Evidence

Political transition and memory:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-115-2-qinwang-danan-busi-zhihou-ganle-jian-ni-buzhidao-de-shi-lidfjb6-nggubw8vpz5fvnfacrqu|Qinji 115-2]] links Jing Ke's failure, Qin retaliation, later poetry, and the shrinking political space for people who value personal义 above official order.

Social survival under suppression:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-128-6-dingji-tianshilun-touziren-lvgong-lhqqja27zxdiypgf6wuv1abgkw3j|Qinji 128-6]] presents Liu Bang's youxia-style sociability as local capital that survives Qin rule and later joins anti-Qin mobilization.

Normative and historiographical conflict:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-284-daoxing-nishi-de-zhufuyan-jiexiao-tancai-de-zhenxiang-1-loeyzfhhdr9wdjhmdpfhmkvsm5va|Hanji 284 part 1]] contrasts Xun Yue's order-centered criticism with Sima Qian's sympathy for aid outside official hierarchy.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The sources do not provide a complete legal history of Qin or Han policy toward youxia, a precise chronology of suppression, or a stable definition covering every侠士, retainer, assassin, local notable, or bandit. Qinji 115-2's claim about the post-unification climate is a broad closing interpretation, and its Qin Wuyang counterfactual does not establish an alternative outcome. Qinji 128-6 shows survival and conversion of youxia style but does not prove that every local loyalty network opposed Qin. Hanji 284 part 1 compresses Xun Yue and Sima Qian into contrasting positions without quoting their full texts; Sima Qian's punishment may illuminate the host's reading but does not prove the cause of his historiographical sympathy. Extra-institutional aid can resist abusive power, but it can also become coercion, faction, retaliation, or unaccountable violence.

## What Changed

- Added the explicit normative conflict between Xun Yue's public-order criticism and Sima Qian's sympathy for aid outside official hierarchy.
- Distinguished state suppression from the continued social survival of youxia habits.
- Made the ethical boundary explicit: institutional failure can explain admiration without legitimizing every private violent act.
