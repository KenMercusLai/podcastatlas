# Ritual Hope Consumption / 仪式性希望消费

Ritual hope consumption / 仪式性希望消费 is the emotion-economy pattern where people buy signs, rituals, objects, or symbolic actions that make uncertainty feel briefly handleable. In [[ep236-lao-wu-kuai-qian-xinlihua-fufei-de-peiban-shi-zhende-ma-ltvuoeluoeu4-a3j3lamhc3buudx]], examples include virtual "brains" for exams or intelligence, manifestation notebooks, blessed objects, bracelets, and prayer-linked souvenirs.

The episode treats these goods less as literal causal tools than as emotional participation. Buying or using them can create a feeling of agency, hope, and ritual action when the underlying problem remains uncertain.

## Key Claims
- Ritual goods can convert anxiety into a visible, repeatable action.
- The emotional value may come from participation, symbolism, and temporary hope rather than belief in physical efficacy.
- Ritual hope consumption belongs to [[EmotionEconomy]] because it changes how uncertainty feels, not because it solves the underlying problem.
- The pattern can remain harmless when buyers treat it as comfort, but it becomes risky when symbolic action replaces necessary medical, legal, educational, or financial action.
- Older divination and ritual traditions help explain why uncertainty repeatedly generates technical, spiritual, and commercial forms.
