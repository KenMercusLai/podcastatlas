# Mitochondrial Mental Health Model

## Definition
The mitochondrial mental health model is Palmer's proposal that cellular energy regulation can connect neurotransmission, plasticity, hormones, inflammation, stress response, epigenetics, aging, and psychiatric symptoms.

## Current Synthesis
The two sources present mitochondria as dynamic energy, signaling, and resource-allocation systems rather than static cellular powerhouses. They move, fuse, change shape, manage ATP production, influence reactive oxygen species, and participate in neurotransmitter handling, steroid-hormone synthesis, inflammatory state, cortisol biology, and gene regulation. This breadth makes mitochondria a plausible integration layer for diverse psychiatric risk factors.

The model remains a framework, not a demonstrated universal cause. Ketogenic diet, exercise, fasting-like states, selected nutrients, supplements, and neurostimulation may affect mitochondrial processes through different routes, but the sources do not isolate mitochondrial change as the causal mediator of psychiatric benefit. No single available blood test is presented as a complete measure of mitochondrial health.

## Key Claims
- Mitochondria regulate energy and signaling functions relevant to brain activity, not only ATP production.
- Neurotransmitters, steroid hormones, inflammation, stress responses, epigenetics, and plasticity can intersect with mitochondrial state.
- Mitochondrial dynamics include movement, fusion, shape change, mitophagy, and biogenesis.
- Aging, excessive stimulation, alcohol exposure, nutritional state, and exercise can alter mitochondrial burden or adaptation.
- Interventions that affect mitochondria are heterogeneous and should not be treated as interchangeable cures.
- Biomarker panels are exploratory because no single comprehensive mitochondrial blood test is available in the sources.

## Evidence
- Neurotransmitter and hormone roles: [[essentials-diet-nutrition-for-mental-health-dr-chris-palmer-scim2465421786]] connects mitochondria with transmitter regulation, stress hormones, steroid hormones, and inflammation.
- Dynamic-cellular account: [[transform-your-mental-health-with-diet-lifestyle-dr-chris-palmer-scim2561346508]] describes mitochondrial movement, fusion, morphology, ATP production, epigenetics, cortisol regulation, and cellular coordination.
- Adaptation and stress: [[transform-your-mental-health-with-diet-lifestyle-dr-chris-palmer-scim2561346508]] links exercise, aging, stimulants, alcohol, mitophagy, and reactive oxygen species with mitochondrial state.
- Intervention mechanisms: [[essentials-diet-nutrition-for-mental-health-dr-chris-palmer-scim2465421786]] and [[transform-your-mental-health-with-diet-lifestyle-dr-chris-palmer-scim2561346508]] connect ketosis with mitophagy and biogenesis, while the full interview adds fasting-like states, creatine, methylene blue, urolithin A, nutrient sufficiency, and TMS as qualified branches.
- Measurement gap: [[transform-your-mental-health-with-diet-lifestyle-dr-chris-palmer-scim2561346508]] says no single available blood test measures overall mitochondrial health.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Mechanistic coherence does not prove that mitochondrial dysfunction causes every psychiatric disorder or that changing a mitochondrial marker will improve symptoms. The sources do not provide completed trials isolating mitochondria as mediator, and some intervention evidence comes from small trials, animal work, non-psychiatric outcomes, or early biomarker research.

## What Changed
- Expanded the model from ketosis-related mechanisms into mitochondrial dynamics, aging, lifestyle, substances, nutrients, supplements, and neurostimulation.
- Added the distinction between a unifying mechanism and proof of universal causation.
- Added the current measurement gap and exploratory biomarker-panel direction.
