# Autism Early Intervention / 自闭症早期干预

## Definition
Autism early intervention is the source's evidence-oriented support frame for helping autistic or possibly autistic children build communication, social attention, daily participation, and family interaction without treating autism as an error to erase.

## Current Synthesis
The episode places early intervention between two mistakes: passive waiting and cure fantasy. Xu Yue says signs such as language delay, weak eye contact, unusual play, repetitive movement, sensory overload, and limited social gestures should prompt attention, and she names early childhood as an especially valuable intervention window. At the same time, the episode rejects the idea that intervention should mean risky repair, miracle pills, or making the person non-autistic.

The practical center is family-involved and behavioral: parents need to put down distractions, sit with the child, play, take turns, use face-to-face interaction, and follow professional guidance. Adults can also benefit from social-skill support, so the concept is early-weighted but not age-exclusive.

One earlier practitioner anecdote illustrates the assessment-and-support sequence without proving a treatment effect. A roughly four-year-old child who did not respond to their name, interacted little, and used screaming, crying, or running when limited reportedly received a professional autism diagnosis and showed more response, conversation, explanation tolerance, and basic interaction after several months of intervention. Persistent signs can merit assessment and support, but a summarized before-and-after case cannot identify the active intervention, measure effect, or establish prognosis. ## Key Claims
- Early attention is useful when children show language delay, weak social gaze, unusual play, repetitive behavior, sensory overload, or missing gestures.
- The source names roughly one-and-a-half to five or six years old as a valuable intervention period, while saying intervention at other ages can still help.
- Evidence-based intervention in the episode includes behavioral approaches, pivotal response training, joint-attention training, and applied-behavior-analysis-related methods.
- Family participation is not optional: progress depends on repeated everyday interaction, not only clinic sessions.
- Intervention should expand communication and participation while staying aligned with [[AutismAsHumanDifference]].
- Individual improvement anecdotes can motivate timely assessment but do not establish a universal developmental trajectory or prove that autism has been removed.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This is a source-scoped public-education synthesis, not a clinical guideline. The sources do not settle which intervention model fits which child, how to evaluate provider quality, or how to handle controversies around specific behavior-analysis practices. EP93's historical “mild autism” label, diagnosis, intervention, and improvement are reported through a structured episode summary without records, methods, outcome measures, or follow-up.

## What Changed
- Created the early-intervention concept for the episode's practical autism-support guidance.
- Added a bounded practitioner case while separating observed skill change from cure or universal prognosis.
