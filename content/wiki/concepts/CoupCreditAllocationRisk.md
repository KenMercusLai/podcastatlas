## Definition

Coup Credit Allocation Risk / 宫变功劳分配风险 is the pattern where a successful coup or restoration turns shared danger into a ranked reward problem. The list of contributors, certificates, titles, and household grants does not merely record merit; it creates hierarchy, exposes hidden hesitation, and gives insiders leverage over one another.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-804-gei-ni-tang-de-ren-bu-yiding-shi-guiren-lqongdqg-hgetoegpylv-os86buh|Hanji 804]] grounds the concept in the 125 CE restoration of [[EmperorShunOfHan|汉顺帝]]. [[SunChengLateHan|Sun Cheng]] and the nineteen eunuchs win by committing to the coup, including the Zhangtai Gate killing of Yan-side generals. After victory, Sun Cheng receives the top reward, while [[WangKangLateHan|Wang Kang]] and [[WangGuoEunuchLateHan|Wang Guo]] receive the next level.

The risk appears when Sun Cheng delegates the merit list to Wang Kang. Wang Kang quietly includes [[MiaoGuangLateHan|Miao Guang]], whose hesitation at the gate made his commitment doubtful. When Miao Guang fails to receive a certificate or tally, he confesses the manipulation. The episode reads Sun Cheng's non-punishment as political management: reward allocation can be used to bind a rival, signal generosity, balance another high-rank actor, and keep a private handle.

## Key Claims

- A coup's success does not settle internal hierarchy; the reward list becomes a second political battlefield.
- Point-of-no-return actions determine who can credibly claim full merit.
- Certificates, tallies, titles, and household grants make hidden contribution quality visible after victory.
- Delegating list-making can transfer risk and reveal another actor's private network.
- Leniency toward a flawed participant can be strategic when it creates gratitude, reputation, balance, and leverage.

## Evidence

- Commitment threshold: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-804-gei-ni-tang-de-ren-bu-yiding-shi-guiren-lqongdqg-hgetoegpylv-os86buh|Hanji 804]] treats the Zhangtai Gate killing of Liu An and Chen Da as a point-of-no-return act for Sun Cheng's group.
- Reward hierarchy: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-804-gei-ni-tang-de-ren-bu-yiding-shi-guiren-lqongdqg-hgetoegpylv-os86buh|Hanji 804]] says Sun Cheng receives the leading reward and Wang Kang and Wang Guo receive the next level.
- List manipulation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-804-gei-ni-tang-de-ren-bu-yiding-shi-guiren-lqongdqg-hgetoegpylv-os86buh|Hanji 804]] says Wang Kang quietly includes Miao Guang, whose earlier hesitation made his claim doubtful.
- Document-triggered exposure: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-804-gei-ni-tang-de-ren-bu-yiding-shi-guiren-lqongdqg-hgetoegpylv-os86buh|Hanji 804]] says Miao Guang panics when no certificate arrives and reports both his hesitation and Wang Kang's manipulation.
- Strategic tolerance: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-804-gei-ni-tang-de-ren-bu-yiding-shi-guiren-lqongdqg-hgetoegpylv-os86buh|Hanji 804]] reads Sun Cheng's non-punishment as a way to win Wang Kang, reassure others, balance Wang Guo, and retain leverage.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept currently rests on one source note and should not be generalized to every coup reward settlement. Hanji 804 gives a strong internal-alliance case, but it does not settle later outcomes for Wang Kang, Wang Guo, Miao Guang, or the full nineteen-eunuch group. The strategic-tolerance reading is the host's interpretation of Sun Cheng's behavior, not direct proof of private intent.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Hanji 804's Sun Cheng, Wang Kang, Miao Guang, and Wang Guo reward-list sequence.
