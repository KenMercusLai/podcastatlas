## Definition

A wet-market food system is the flexible network through which small or irregular producers, specialized intermediaries, vendors, and knowledgeable consumers move local, seasonal, perishable, and non-standard food into urban households.

## Current Synthesis

The bounded source explains market persistence through complementarity rather than retail nostalgia. Fragmented farms and small-scale fishing produce variable quantities and varieties; dock workers, agents, transporters, ice sellers, and vendors sort that supply; vendors decide what can sell and how to preserve or divide it; consumers recognize and cook it. Trust and repeated relationships carry information and sometimes credit through the network.

The same flexibility creates limits. Knowledge can be unequal, informal ties can exclude outsiders, hygiene and transparency can vary, and valuable urban land can weaken policy protection. The system's distinctive capability is therefore not universal superiority but its fit with goods and household needs that standardized, scale-oriented channels handle poorly.

## Key Claims

- Market persistence depends on an interacting producer-vendor-consumer system, not on stalls alone.
- Flexible intermediaries can aggregate, sort, preserve, explain, and divide irregular supply without requiring one national product standard.
- Relationships and reputation carry commercially important information where catch, quality, price, and availability change daily.
- Consumer recognition and cooking knowledge convert unusual ingredients into demand; without that competence, variety can lose commercial value.
- Small-quantity sale serves households whose needs do not match standardized packs.
- Policy, land use, alternative retail, and management determine whether this network can remain viable in a specific city.

## Evidence

- Seafood network and trust: [[486d1fea-dd57-4ff1-8de9-cfea1f9de16a-486d1fea-dd57-4ff1-8de9-cfea1f9de16a]] describes fishers, dock agents, secondary buyers, transporters, ice sellers, and vendors coordinating variable Hainan catch through judgment, reputation, and sometimes deferred payment.
- Non-standard supply and demand: [[486d1fea-dd57-4ff1-8de9-cfea1f9de16a-486d1fea-dd57-4ff1-8de9-cfea1f9de16a]] links local varieties and seasonal foods to vendors able to accept them and consumers able to identify and cook them.
- Household and urban fit: [[486d1fea-dd57-4ff1-8de9-cfea1f9de16a-486d1fea-dd57-4ff1-8de9-cfea1f9de16a]] contrasts flexible quantities and neighborhood relationships with standardized packs, land pressure, community stores, and online delivery.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

One anthropological episode cannot establish the relative price, safety, waste, emissions, farmer income, or consumer welfare of wet markets and alternative channels. Informal trust can coexist with information asymmetry, variable hygiene, gatekeeping, or opportunism. Supermarkets and platforms can also sell local and seasonal goods, while some wet-market supply is already regional or industrial rather than direct and local.

## What Changed

- Established a system-level explanation for wet-market persistence around complementary capabilities and non-standard supply.
- Kept trust, flexibility, and local variety together with transparency, safety, and exclusion limits.
