# National Director Emotion Outlet / 国民导演情绪出口

## Definition
National director emotion outlet / 国民导演情绪出口 is the cultural role of a mainstream filmmaker who does more than sell tickets: they give a period's public feelings a recognizable narrative form.

## Current Synthesis
The source uses this concept to explain why [[FengXiaogang|冯小刚]] mattered beyond comedy and box office. In the hosts' account, a national director must touch collective feelings that an audience already carries but cannot easily formulate. Feng's strongest period runs from 《集结号》 through 《芳华》 because these works process fears of being forgotten, denied recognition, abandoned by institutions, or discarded by history.

The same frame also explains the episode's pessimism about [[ZhuaTewu|《抓特务》]]. If a film must turn pain into clean reconciliation and national-progress reassurance, it may lose the ability to serve as an honest public-emotion outlet. The source does not say national feeling disappears; it says a particular mainstream route becomes harder when the work cannot fully acknowledge pain before resolving it.

## Key Claims
- A national director is measured by public-emotion representation, not only by box office or popularity.
- The role depends on articulating shared injury, nostalgia, fear, resentment, or desire for recognition.
- Feng Xiaogang's serious peak is read as a period when he could convert abandonment into mainstream emotional form.
- The role becomes unstable when censorship, mainstream genre rules, or market expectations demand clear reconciliation.
- Over-neat historical closure weakens the director's ability to carry unresolved collective feeling.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept is a critical interpretation from one episode, not a settled industry category. It should be tested against more sources on Chinese directors, audiences, censorship, and market change.
