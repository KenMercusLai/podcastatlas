# Enterprise AI Deployment Bottlenecks

## Definition
Enterprise AI deployment bottlenecks are the context, security, governance, economic, and strategic-control constraints that prevent capable models from becoming reliable organizational systems.

## Current Synthesis
The source groups four barriers. AI needs access to meetings, decisions, documents, responsibilities, communications, and operating data; that access expands insider-threat and cyber risk; agentic token consumption can grow faster than business value; and dependence on one provider can weaken control of company data, intelligence, intellectual property, and switching. Older and regulated organizations face more legacy software, security processes, and organizational inertia, so adoption speed depends on institutional redesign as much as model progress.

## Key Claims
- Model capability cannot substitute for missing company context.
- Wider agent access increases permission, insider-threat, and external-attack exposure.
- Token usage must be tied to workflow value because consumption can scale without corresponding revenue or productivity.
- Strategic control includes data and IP ownership, model choice, portability, and avoidance of excessive vendor dependence.
- Legacy systems, regulation, missing digital records, and organizational inertia make diffusion uneven across firms.
- Deployment bottlenecks can keep macroeconomic effects slow even when model intelligence improves quickly.

## Evidence
- Four-barrier framework: [[audio-99-alighodsi-databricks-audio-99-alighodsi-databricks]] names context, security/governance, economics, and strategic control.
- Organizational example: [[audio-99-alighodsi-databricks-audio-99-alighodsi-databricks]] contrasts Databricks' company ontology with a bank unwilling to record meetings.
- Cost example: [[audio-99-alighodsi-databricks-audio-99-alighodsi-databricks]] describes routing simpler work to cheaper or open models rather than maximizing model spend.
- Diffusion claim: [[audio-99-alighodsi-databricks-audio-99-alighodsi-databricks]] estimates that closing the context gap may take 10–15 years.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The four-part grouping is Ghodsi's operator framework, not a complete causal model or comparative adoption study. Labor incentives, workflow selection, model reliability, evaluation quality, procurement, regulation, and change management may cut across or extend the categories. Databricks also has a commercial interest in defining the deployment problem around enterprise data and governance infrastructure.

## What Changed
- Established a unified deployment framework linking context, permission, cost, and strategic ownership.
- Added institutional diffusion time as a boundary on capability-driven economic forecasts.
