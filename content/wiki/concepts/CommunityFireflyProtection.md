# Community Firefly Protection / 社区萤火虫保护

Community firefly protection is the source's small-scale version of urban conservation. In [[85-chedundun-yeshiji-zou-a-bandao-xiangxia-zuan-xiaoshuliner-qu-700935571]], [[YinQiCheDundun|尹琪]] argues that fireflies can live in city or suburban communities if dark habitat remains available for light-based communication and breeding.

The practical conflict is ordinary infrastructure. Her community added low path lights, after which firefly numbers fell in the observed area. 尹琪 communicated with the community, and some corner lights were turned off during the breeding season; in a parent-child night observation, a father covered a light with his jacket and fireflies soon appeared in the grass.

## Key Claims
- Firefly protection can depend on micro-decisions such as low lights, seasonal darkness, corners left unmanaged, and resident cooperation.
- The point is not only species preservation; community memory also matters when children and parents learn that fireflies once lived in their own neighborhood.
- Public participation can turn local ecology from private hobby into [[CitizenScience]] and informal stewardship.
- Small interventions should stay grounded in observation: where lights are, when insects flash, and how residents use paths all affect what is feasible.
- The concept adds an insect-and-darkness case to [[UrbanEcology]] and [[ConservationIntervention]].
