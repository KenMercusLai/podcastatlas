## Definition

策问式选才 / imperial policy-questioning talent selection is a recruitment pattern in which recommendation opens access and a ruler's questions about government test whether learned candidates can turn reputation and classical knowledge into usable policy argument.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-248-dongzhongshu-ruhe-gaibian-zhonghua-minzu-de-mingyun-1-lnbdqi1evkkfbajdmhh_l5m70n0z|Hanji 248]] presents early [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] recruitment as a sequence: announce demand for 贤良方正 and 直言极谏, gather recommended scholars, question them about rule, and identify people for later service. The institutional value in this account is not only rank allocation. The questions give the ruler access to competing diagnoses while giving scholars such as [[DongZhongshu|董仲舒]] a path from learning into the state policy arena.

The same channel also shapes political speech. The first installment says historical examples let a candidate advise indirectly when blunt criticism would be dangerous, so policy questioning tests both substantive judgment and the ability to make counsel receivable. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-248-dongzhongshu-ruhe-gaibian-zhonghua-minzu-de-mingyun-2-lrjy7ozds7peyu6x3iwufety2zvt|Hanji 248(2)]] adds the institutional content of Dong's answer: recommendation should carry sponsor liability, examination should test ability, conduct should inform placement, and tenure should not substitute for fitness.

Together the two installments separate four stages that a modern “civil-service examination” analogy can blur: finding candidates, giving them access, testing policy judgment, and holding nominators responsible for selection quality. Skilled presentation can still open access without proving that an answer is correct, that a nominee will perform well, or that the ruler will implement the advice.

## Key Claims

- Recommendation and questioning perform different functions: the first opens the candidate pool, while the second tests policy reasoning before the ruler.
- A policy answer can convert scholarly reputation into political access without guaranteeing appointment, implementation, or long-term influence.
- Historical analogy can make remonstrance more receivable, but rhetorical tact is not the same as evidentiary or policy correctness.
- The episode uses Dong Zhongshu to show how a selection channel can become an ideological access point when ruler demand and scholarly ambition align.
- Recommendation accountability, examination of ability, and conduct-based placement are complementary checks rather than a single test score.
- “Civil-service examination” is a modern explanatory analogy here, not evidence that the event was institutionally identical to later 科举.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The current concept rests on two short installments from one popular-history show and does not reconstruct the relevant edict or policy answers independently. The first episode's date, age, candidate total, interview format, ranking, participant grouping, and “first national civil-service examination” label remain source-scoped. The sequel supplies substantive selection proposals but does not independently establish its nomination quota, salary threshold, exact examination sequence, adoption, or relationship to later 察举 institutions. Together they identify an access-and-evaluation mechanism rather than proving a direct causal path to later Confucian predominance.

## What Changed

- Added the sequel's distinction among nomination, sponsor accountability, ability testing, conduct review, and placement.
- Narrowed the modern examination analogy further by separating candidate discovery from testing and later performance.
