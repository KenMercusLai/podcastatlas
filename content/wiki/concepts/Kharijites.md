# Kharijites

## Definition

The Kharijites are presented as an early Islamic opposition current that rejected inherited or kin-based political privilege, made piety the basis of rightful leadership, and repeatedly joined uncompromising religious judgment to armed revolt.

## Current Synthesis

The movement challenged both Ali and the Umayyads. Its piety-centered standard could sound radically egalitarian because no sacred bloodline guaranteed rule, but the episode also emphasizes violent exclusion and rebellion.

Ali defeated Kharijite forces before a Kharijite murdered him in [[Kufa]]. Renewed uprisings in the 740s and the proclamation of a rival caliph in 745 helped turn Iraq into a major front in the terminal crisis of the [[UmayyadCaliphate]].

## Key Claims

- Rightful rule was tied to piety rather than blood or inherited status.
- The movement rejected Ali despite his kinship with Muhammad.
- Egalitarian religious principle coexisted with uncompromising and violent militancy.
- Kharijite revolt remained politically significant after Ali's defeat of an early force.
- A Kharijite caliphate claim in 745 exposed weak Umayyad control in Iraq.

## Evidence

- **Piety standard:** [[376-baghdad-the-forging-of-islam-part-1-glt9529602805]] contrasts Kharijite piety with aristocracy based on lineage.
- **Conflict with Ali:** [[376-baghdad-the-forging-of-islam-part-1-glt9529602805]] describes military defeat followed by Ali's assassination.
- **Late Umayyad rebellion:** [[376-baghdad-the-forging-of-islam-part-1-glt9529602805]] links renewed 740s uprisings and a rival caliph to Iraq's instability.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode uses strongly negative language and compresses diverse Kharijite groups, doctrines, and later histories into a short political narrative. Piety, egalitarianism, violence, and attitudes toward other Muslims should not be assumed to have taken one uniform form.

## What Changed

- Established Kharijite opposition as both a rival legitimacy principle and a military cause of late Umayyad instability.
