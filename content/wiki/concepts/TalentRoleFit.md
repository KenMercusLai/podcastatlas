## Definition

Talent-role fit is the judgment that performance depends partly on whether a person's capability scale and task pattern match the assigned role, while preserving accountability for actual neglect or harm.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 1149 develops the concept through [[JiangWan|蒋琬]]. His failure as Guangdu magistrate is real in the episode, but [[ZhugeLiang|诸葛亮]] argues that county routine is a poor test of a person suited to broader state responsibility. Later service and succession provide the evidence that turns an initial defense into a durable reassessment.

The concept therefore requires two moves at once: do not infer total incapacity from one role, and do not infer hidden greatness merely from poor performance. A fit hypothesis becomes credible only through better assignment, observed later work, and bounded tolerance rather than indefinite exemption.

## Key Claims

- One assignment samples only part of a person's capability.
- Task scale, work type, and decision horizon can change performance.
- Role mismatch can coexist with genuine misconduct or neglect.
- Reassignment is an evaluative test, not a declaration of greatness.
- Later evidence must confirm the fit judgment.

## Evidence

- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1149-jiemi-sanguo-dingji-moushi-xunyou-de-guanchang-shengcunshu-ljv1fwephe86-qczfy0o1n2yh7um|Hanji 1149]] pairs Jiang Wan's failed county service with Zhuge Liang's state-scale judgment and Jiang Wan's later senior Shu-Han offices.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source does not prove a general management rule from one historical portrait. Role-fit language can become a retrospective excuse for favoritism, neglect, or lack of basic competence. The stronger claim depends on later performance and should not erase the costs of the original failure.

## What Changed

- Established role fit as a testable reassignment judgment rather than a blanket excuse for underperformance.
