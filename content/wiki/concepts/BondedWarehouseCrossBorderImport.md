# Bonded Warehouse Cross-Border Import / 保税仓跨境电商

## Definition
Bonded-warehouse cross-border import is the retail model in which platforms buy foreign goods in bulk into domestic bonded warehouses, ship them on consumer order, and sell them as personal-use items that bypass ordinary import registration.

## Current Synthesis
The source dates the opening to 2015: Hangzhou's cross-border e-commerce comprehensive pilot zone was approved in March, and the China-Australia free trade agreement was signed in June and took effect in December. The two changes worked differently. The free trade agreement mainly affected general trade - agents importing in bulk - where the episode puts vitamin and supplement tariffs at 3-10% by Australian official statistics and around 20% on average by [[Blackmores|Blackmores]]' own estimate, scheduled to fall to zero within four years. The bonded warehouse changed retail economics instead.

Platforms bought overseas goods in bulk, stored them domestically, and shipped on order. Consumers paid a small tax but received goods much faster and cheaper than individual overseas purchasing, and the platforms obtained bulk discounts. The decisive detail is classification: goods sold this way counted as personal-use items, so supplements avoided the ordinary import and registration or record-filing requirements that domestic sales would trigger. The hosts say many Chinese consumers first met [[Swisse|Swisse]] or Blackmores in this era, which makes 2015 the point where daigou-level familiarity became platform-scale distribution.

## Key Claims
- Two 2015 policy moves aligned: a cross-border e-commerce pilot zone and a bilateral free trade agreement.
- The agreement's tariff relief mattered mainly for bulk general trade, while the pilot zone mattered for platform retail.
- Bonded warehousing changed the consumer experience through shorter delivery times, lower logistics cost, and bulk purchasing discounts.
- Personal-use classification was the structural advantage, because it let supplements skip standard import registration and filing.
- The model converted awareness built by informal purchasing into mass distribution, which is why the episode treats 2015 as the category's inflection point.
- The same mechanism explains why origin-country regulation could substitute for destination-country registration in consumer perception.

## Evidence
- Policy timing: [[vol-275-aozhou-baojianpin-shi-zenme-huoqilai-de-1015773861]] dates the Hangzhou pilot to March 2015 and the China-Australia agreement to June signature and December effect.
- Tariff layer: [[vol-275-aozhou-baojianpin-shi-zenme-huoqilai-de-1015773861]] reports the 3-10% official range, Blackmores' roughly 20% average estimate, and the four-year plan to zero.
- Warehouse mechanics: [[vol-275-aozhou-baojianpin-shi-zenme-huoqilai-de-1015773861]] describes bulk purchase into domestic warehouses, on-order shipping, a small tax, faster and cheaper delivery, and platform discounts.
- Regulatory shortcut: [[vol-275-aozhou-baojianpin-shi-zenme-huoqilai-de-1015773861]] says goods were treated as personal-use items, so supplements did not need the ordinary import process or registration and filing.
- Consumer adoption: [[vol-275-aozhou-baojianpin-shi-zenme-huoqilai-de-1015773861]] says many consumers met Swisse and Blackmores during the bonded-warehouse era.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The tariff range, the average figure, and the four-year phase-down are episode claims that should be verified against customs schedules and the agreement text. The source says nothing about the later tightening of cross-border retail rules, customs thresholds, or the authenticity and quality-control problems that bonded channels can create, and it describes the model mainly through one category and one corridor. Bonded warehousing is therefore recorded as a policy window that shaped supplement distribution, not as a general claim about how all cross-border trade works.

## What Changed
- Created the policy-and-logistics concept that formalised the cross-border supplement channel.
