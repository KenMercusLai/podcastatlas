# Strategic Pass Control / 兵家必争通道控制

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-953-luanshi-zhizhong-yaobuyao-jiangjiu-renyi-lrzfimgqatjxyw9zx9ry2kiwxkon|Hanji 953]] gives an early late-Han pass-control design in [[CaoCao|曹操]]'s critique of the [[SuanzaoCamp|Suanzao]] coalition. Cao Cao argues that after [[DongZhuo|董卓]] moves west, the coalition should hold [[HeneiCommandery|Henei]]/Mengjin, [[Chenggao|Chenggao]], and [[Aocang|Aocang]], while blocking Huanyuan, Taigu, Danshui, Xi, and [[Wuguan|Wuguan]] approaches. The episode treats the plan as strategically coherent but institutionally unusable because the coalition lacks unified command.

Strategic pass control / 兵家必争通道控制 enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-959-ruguo-ni-chuanyue-dao-sanguo-gai-ruhe-cunhuo-lroaihmpnrelmfcugym9zx2h2dhq|Hanji 959]] as the episode's explanation for why [[XieguRoadLateHan|斜谷道]] matters. The host defines a "兵家必争之地" through three linked qualities: strategic position, useful resources, and transportation convenience.

In the source, pass control is political as well as military. After [[ZhangLuFivePecks|张鲁]] and [[ZhangXiuFivePecks|张修]] take Hanzhong for [[LiuYanLateHan|刘焉]], blocking the Baoxie / Xiegu route makes it harder for the Chang'an court to reach [[YizhouLateHan|益州]]. Liu Yan can then describe the road as cut by "rice-bandits," turning a logistical fact into a public explanation for autonomy.

## Key Claims

- A good pass-control plan can fail politically even when it is militarily coherent.
- Strategic terrain matters when it controls movement, communication, and resource access together.
- A blocked pass can become a political instrument by making separation from a weak center look externally forced.
- Route control helps convert a provincial office into practical autonomy when the central court lacks coercive reach.
