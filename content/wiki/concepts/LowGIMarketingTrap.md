# Low-GI Marketing Trap / 低GI营销陷阱

## Definition
The low-GI marketing trap is the source's warning that glycemic-index claims can make a product look safe while hiding total sugar load, fructose metabolism, serving size, or weak certification context.

## Current Synthesis
The episode uses "low-GI honey" as the clearest example. GI focuses on blood glucose response, so a product with substantial fructose can appear less concerning on that metric while still contributing calories and liver-metabolism burden. The source therefore brings in GL and total amount: speed of glucose rise is useful, but it cannot replace quantity, food form, and metabolic context.

The mooncake source extends the warning from honey to seasonal reformulation. As traditional high-oil, high-sugar mooncakes face weaker demand, low-sugar and low-GI become selling points. The concept is not anti-metric: nutrition metrics become misleading when marketers turn one number into a broad health promise. Consumers still need to ask what sugar source, fat content, dose, serving size, and food matrix they are actually eating.

## Key Claims
- GI is useful but incomplete because it focuses on blood glucose response rather than every sugar-related metabolic burden.
- Low-GI honey can look safer because fructose contributes less to immediate blood glucose while still requiring liver metabolism.
- GL and total sugar amount matter alongside glycemic speed.
- Certification and clean-label claims need regulatory and evidentiary context rather than blind trust in front-package language.
- The practical consumer response is to read labels and serving context instead of treating one health metric as permission.
- A seasonal food can respond to health concerns through low-sugar or low-GI positioning while remaining energy-dense or high in fat and fructose.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not reject glycemic-index information as useless. Their objection is to broad safety claims built from one metric, especially when the product still contains meaningful sugar, fat, or calories or when the consumer lacks dose and certification context. The mooncake-market discussion is a short news item rather than individualized nutrition guidance.

## What Changed
- Extended the concept from honey to health-positioned seasonal foods.
- Added fat and whole-product composition to the low-GI qualification.
