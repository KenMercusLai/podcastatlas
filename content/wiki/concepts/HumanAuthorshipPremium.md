# Human Authorship Premium

Human authorship premium is the added trust, authenticity, or willingness to pay that can attach to work explicitly described as made, designed, written, or led by people in an AI-saturated market. [[tech-20260413-0413-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260413-0413-mp-tech-pod-128]] develops the idea through [[StephanieHughes]]'s [[GenuineFred]] example and [[ColleenKirk]]'s consumer-research comments.

The concept does not mean consumers reject every AI tool. Its core distinction is authorship control: consumers penalize work more when AI appears to be the primary creator and humans merely edit, but can accept AI involvement when a human develops the work and AI assists. The premium is strongest where buyers expect emotion, identity, care, or self-expression rather than pure utility.

## Key Claims
- "Human made" can function as a value signal even when machine manufacturing is still part of production.
- Consumers may infer emotional investment, care, and self-expression from human authorship.
- Identity-linked and emotionally meaningful products are more sensitive to AI involvement than utilitarian products.
- AI disclosure can reduce trust when it implies AI authorship rather than human-led tool use.
- The premium connects product value to perceived process, not only to the finished object's objective features.
- Human imperfections can become part of perceived authenticity for some buyers.
- Younger consumers may not be automatically more accepting of AI-generated advertising; the source says Gen Z appears especially skeptical.
