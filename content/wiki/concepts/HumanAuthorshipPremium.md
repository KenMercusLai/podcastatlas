# Human Authorship Premium

## Definition
Human authorship premium is the added trust, authenticity, emotional investment, attention, or willingness to pay that can attach to work understood as made, designed, written, or intentionally led by people in an AI-saturated environment.

## Current Synthesis
The current synthesis joins consumer-market, literary-audience, process-verification, and premium-game versions. People may value human-led design and communication more when they expect emotion, identity, care, self-expression, or a durable relationship with a creator and brand, and may penalize work when AI appears to be the primary author. Human-led work can still use AI; the key distinction is who supplies intention and authorship control.

Through [[ChetanBhagat]], the literary case argues that memorable art transfers lived emotion from a human creator to a human audience, while AI remains useful for research, proofreading, routine agreements, and formulaic correspondence. [[AndresMorales|Andres Morales]] adds a process-verification version: clients may ask for layers, work-in-progress snippets, or other evidence that a person actually made the work, while human-made and live experiences gain value as synthetic output becomes common.

The premium-game version comes through [[LiangQiwei|梁其伟]]. A single-player game sells not only functional entertainment but also trust in a known team, world, style, and accumulated relationship. Technical AI use in logic, testing, or optimization can remain compatible with that premium when artistic authorship stays legibly human-led. The premium therefore concerns both relationship and verifiable process: audiences may care about the person, labor, intention, history, brand continuity, and accountable chain behind the artifact, not only its surface quality.

## Key Claims
- Human-made or human-led status can function as a value signal even when machines assist production.
- The premium is stronger for identity-linked, emotional, expressive, or relational work than for purely utilitarian output.
- Authorship control matters: human intention with AI assistance differs from AI authorship followed by human editing.
- Consumers and audiences can infer care, lived experience, or emotional investment from a human creator.
- Imperfection can strengthen authenticity when it carries character, voice, or evidence of a person rather than mere production error.
- Surface competence alone does not settle whether audiences will form the same relationship with synthetic work; proof of process can therefore become part of the premium.
- In premium games, a known team, coherent world, and history of player expectation can make authorship part of the product rather than only a production label.

## Evidence
- Consumer trust and willingness to pay - [[tech-20260413-0413-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260413-0413-mp-tech-pod-128]] reports lower trust, authenticity, purchase intent, and positive word of mouth when AI is framed as primary creator, especially for emotionally meaningful goods.
- Human-led assistance boundary - [[tech-20260413-0413-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260413-0413-mp-tech-pod-128]] reports a smaller penalty when a person develops the work and AI assists than when AI develops it and a person edits.
- Lived emotion and artistic intent - [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-fe4d0dc018bd4482039d4a6798e610cf-124292559-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-fe4d0dc018bd4482039d4a6798e610cf-124292559]] presents Bhagat's claim that fiction and art communicate human pain, history, purpose, and moral stance.
- Audience interest in creators - [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-fe4d0dc018bd4482039d4a6798e610cf-124292559-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-fe4d0dc018bd4482039d4a6798e610cf-124292559]] argues that people remain interested in the human experiences behind books, songs, interviews, and other creative work even when AI can assist execution.
- Process evidence and live experience - [[ep-27-ai-and-the-creative-arts-innovation-or-appropriation]] reports client requests for work-in-progress evidence and predicts more interest in human-made objects, personable encounters, and live experiences whose origin feels real.
- Premium-game brand and team relationship - [[156-xia-dui-ying-zhi-ren-ling-zhizuoren-liangqiwei-6-xiaoshi-fangtan-lvnhry4glcc3ut-6nmjn7qrmd-dan]] argues that players value the accountable creators, world, brand, and accumulated expectation behind a single-player game, while allowing AI in subordinate technical tasks.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The Marketplace findings are source-reported consumer research, while Bhagat's, Morales's, and Liang's accounts are practitioner interpretations rather than proof of a permanent boundary. Preferences vary by audience, disclosure, medium, quality, price, utility, and changing norms. AI-generated work may become more compelling, and a human label, process artifact, known brand, or “no AI” claim does not guarantee care, originality, truth, fair labor, or artistic value; it can also become superficial marketing. The Phantom Blade account is a pre-release producer claim and does not establish how buyers will value the final game.

## What Changed
- Extended the premium from labels and audience relationship to verifiable creative process, live experience, and accountable origin.
- Added premium games as a case where creator, team, world, brand, and accumulated player trust are part of perceived value.
- Distinguished subordinate technical AI assistance from artistic authorship without treating the boundary as permanent or self-verifying.
