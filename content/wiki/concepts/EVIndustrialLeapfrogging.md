# EV Industrial Leapfrogging / 电动车产业跨越式追赶

## Definition

EV industrial leapfrogging is the strategy of using a technological transition from internal combustion to electrification to reduce the value of incumbents' accumulated engine and transmission advantages while building strength in batteries, electric drive, electronics, software, suppliers, and manufacturing.

## Current Synthesis

The source treats electrification as a changed competitive map, not a shortcut around industrial competence. China could enter a field where leading countries held less inherited advantage in engines and transmissions, but a reliable vehicle still required the [[PowerBatteryIndustryChain]], motors, electronic controls, chassis, crashworthiness, thermal management, electronics, manufacturing quality, service, and infrastructure. Policy support and concentrated investment could create demand and learning, while subsidy fraud and unsafe low-quality products could destroy trust.

The catch-up mechanism is therefore systemic: stimulated demand creates scale; domestic suppliers and anchor firms accumulate process knowledge; foreign localization such as [[Tesla]] can deepen supplier capability and competition; and repeated financing, production, delivery, safety, and consumer tests remove weak firms. The source is a 2020 interpretation, so it establishes a mechanism and historical viewpoint rather than proving every later outcome.

## Key Claims

- A technology transition can reduce the relative value of incumbent-specific accumulated knowledge without erasing the need for deep engineering.
- Batteries, motors, electronic control, suppliers, factories, infrastructure, and product quality form the new capability stack.
- Policy-created demand can accelerate learning but can also reward subsidy gaming and superficial compliance.
- Foreign flagship localization can strengthen domestic suppliers through orders, standards, talent movement, and competitive pressure.
- Industrial catch-up becomes durable only when firms survive financing, production, delivery, safety, and user-experience tests.

## Evidence

- Changed bottlenecks: [[ep142-shizhijinri-ni-hai-kanbuqi-guochan-xinnengyuanche-ma-cmcoosidyoetcpz-zabeibq]] contrasts inherited engine and transmission gaps with China's emerging battery, electric-drive, and supplier base.
- System capability: [[ep142-shizhijinri-ni-hai-kanbuqi-guochan-xinnengyuanche-ma-cmcoosidyoetcpz-zabeibq]] says lower assembly barriers did not make safe, mature, attractive vehicles easy to build.
- Demand and discipline: [[ep142-shizhijinri-ni-hai-kanbuqi-guochan-xinnengyuanche-ma-cmcoosidyoetcpz-zabeibq]] describes subsidy abuse followed by enforcement, financing, mass-production, and delivery filters.
- Localization spillover: [[ep142-shizhijinri-ni-hai-kanbuqi-guochan-xinnengyuanche-ma-cmcoosidyoetcpz-zabeibq]] compares Tesla's supplier effect with foreign-led consumer-electronics manufacturing spillovers.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode does not provide comparative productivity, patent, cost, safety, export, market-share, or lifecycle-emissions data. Replacing engine and transmission bottlenecks can create new dependencies in minerals, battery chemistry, semiconductors, software, charging, standards, and cybersecurity. The claim is a dated practitioner interpretation and should not be read as proof that every supported firm, localization project, or national policy produced net benefit.
