## Definition

Police network spillover is the proposed influence of officers' professional ties—partners, teams, trainers, and supervisors—on their likelihood of aggressive conduct or use-of-force complaints.

## Current Synthesis

The episode rejects a purely isolated-offender model. Its Chicago research example says officers connected to several aggressive colleagues had a higher chance of receiving a use-of-force complaint, while Jason Van Dyke and the officers around Laquan McDonald's killing were already located in a high-risk group. Seniority and training authority can make the mechanism more consequential because harmful practice may be learned, normalized, protected, or left unchallenged.

Network association is not automatically causal influence. Officers may cluster because of assignment, selection, neighborhood exposure, supervision, or shared reporting conditions. The concept is therefore strongest as a prompt for network-aware supervision and investigation, not as proof of guilt by association.

## Key Claims

- Harmful police conduct can be relational rather than confined to isolated individuals.
- Partners, trainers, and work groups can normalize tactics and intervention failures.
- A small high-risk cluster can influence officers who have fewer prior warning signs.
- Network information may improve supervision when combined with incident evidence and exposure controls.
- Association alone cannot establish peer causation or individual misconduct.
- Network-based intervention requires safeguards against guilt by association and biased data.

## Evidence

### Chicago network association

- [[memorial-day-2020-part-2-memorial-day-2020-part-2]] reports research finding a 26 percent higher chance of a use-of-force complaint among officers connected to even a modest number of aggressive colleagues.

### High-risk group case

- [[memorial-day-2020-part-2-memorial-day-2020-part-2]] reports Andrew Papachristos's account that Jason Van Dyke and involved colleagues were already among Chicago policing's highest-risk group before Van Dyke shot Laquan McDonald.

### Training authority

- [[memorial-day-2020-part-2-memorial-day-2020-part-2]] treats [[DerekChauvin]]'s former training role and [[ThomasLane]]'s deference as a concrete route through which senior officers can shape junior conduct.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode summary does not provide the study's sample, model, confidence interval, network definition, exposure adjustment, or causal identification strategy. The reported association could partly reflect common assignment, selection, supervision, incident exposure, or complaint practices. Network analysis should guide inquiry and support, not replace incident-specific evidence or due process.

## What Changed

- Created a bounded peer-influence concept with an explicit non-causal-association boundary.
