# Correlated Catastrophe Insurance Risk

## Definition
Correlated catastrophe insurance risk arises when one event can damage many insured properties at once, making geographic concentration and shared neighborhood vulnerability matter in addition to each property's individual construction quality.

## Current Synthesis
[[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-9de180c7bff8bba31ae5f11fce51f1e1-34185790-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-9de180c7bff8bba31ae5f11fce51f1e1-34185790]] uses wildfire to show the limit of house-level underwriting. A fortified home can still ignite from a neighboring structure, and an insurer can still reduce coverage if too much of its portfolio could burn in the same event. Structural mitigation lowers expected damage but does not automatically restore diversification, affordability, or market capacity.

## Key Claims
- Catastrophe risk is portfolio-level because one hurricane, wildfire, or hail event can trigger many simultaneous claims.
- Individual resilience is valuable but cannot be evaluated independently of neighboring properties and community conditions.
- Insurers may withdraw or reprice coverage even for mitigated homes when geographic accumulation remains too high.
- Climate-driven increases in severe losses can pressure the viability of pooled property insurance without making every region or event equally risky.
- Engineering, pricing, coverage design, public mitigation, and portfolio diversification address different layers of the problem.

## Evidence
### Neighborhood dependence
- [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-9de180c7bff8bba31ae5f11fce51f1e1-34185790-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-9de180c7bff8bba31ae5f11fce51f1e1-34185790]] explains that an unfortified neighboring home or wooden fence can transmit wildfire risk to a fortified property.

### Insurer concentration
- [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-9de180c7bff8bba31ae5f11fce51f1e1-34185790-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-9de180c7bff8bba31ae5f11fce51f1e1-34185790]] says an insurer may limit coverage when it holds too many policies in an area that could suffer one simultaneous disaster.

### Mitigation-market gap
- [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-9de180c7bff8bba31ae5f11fce51f1e1-34185790-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-9de180c7bff8bba31ae5f11fce51f1e1-34185790]] reports that some [[California]] communities following mitigation standards still faced sharp premium increases or loss of coverage.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- The episode does not provide insurer portfolio data, reinsurance terms, capital models, regulatory rules, or a causal decomposition of premium increases and exits.
- Reported premium increases approaching 1,000% are community examples, not a population-wide estimate.
- Better construction can materially reduce losses even when it cannot by itself guarantee insurance availability.

## What Changed
- Added a portfolio and neighborhood-level explanation for the gap between safer homes and available insurance.
- Separated physical mitigation from insurer diversification and market-capacity constraints.
