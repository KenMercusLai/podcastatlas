# Reality-Oriented Social Science Research / 现实问题导向的社会科学研究

Reality-oriented social science research is [[150-zhongguo-ruhe-tiaochu-pinkun-xianjing-xiandaihua-zhi-lu-you-hui-zouxiang-hefang-lgpvp04rngagkpubte2ac6i5ji3g]]'s final example of a Chinese modernization gap. [[DavidWeng|大卫翁]] cites [[SongZheng|宋铮]] on what kind of economic research [[China]] needs, arguing that technical sophistication is not enough if research drifts away from urgent institutional, economic, and social questions.

The concept belongs to the episode's broader [[ChineseModernizationCulturalGap]] branch. Engineering and manufacturing can answer some problems through buildout and iteration, but governance, welfare, labor, demography, education, media, and markets need social science that remains grounded in lived and policy reality.

## Key Claims
- Technical methods can become sterile when they are detached from real institutional questions.
- Modernization needs social-science feedback because many next-stage problems are not engineering problems.
- Research awards, incentives, and academic prestige can pull scholars away from public relevance.
- The source connects grounded research to better service governance and cultural self-understanding.
