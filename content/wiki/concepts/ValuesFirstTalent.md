# Values-First Talent

Values-first talent is the hiring and leadership principle [[CharlesKoch|Charles Koch]] and [[ChaseKoch|Chase Koch]] describe in [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-charles-chase-koch-on-how-they-quietly-built-a-150b-empire-41270895]]. Charles says one major Koch failure came from hiring first for talent rather than values, while Chase summarizes the desired ordering as values first, skills second, credentials last.

The concept is a management-side complement to [[ValuesAsOperationalAsset]]. It argues that very capable people can destroy a culture if their motivation is power, control, status, or hiding failure. In the source, this matters because a principle-based operating system depends on local judgment and empowerment; destructive leaders can misuse that autonomy.

Values-first talent also qualifies [[DegreeAsTrustCredential]] and [[EducationSignalInflation]]. The source does not deny skill or education, but it treats credentials as weak evidence compared with contribution, values, and demonstrated capability. Chase's example of CIO Jared Benson, who began by striping parking-lot lines and lacked a college degree, is used to make this point.

## Key Claims
- Talent without values can increase organizational risk because capable leaders have more power to hide failures or distort incentives.
- Credentials can help screen, but they should not outrank evidence of values, contribution, and capability.
- Values-first hiring has to be paired with accountability; otherwise values language becomes performative.
- The source connects values-first hiring to [[CapabilityBoundedGrowth]] because expansion requires trusted people to judge unfamiliar businesses.
