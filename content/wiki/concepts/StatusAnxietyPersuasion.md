# Status Anxiety Persuasion / 位阶焦虑式说服

Status anxiety persuasion / 位阶焦虑式说服 is the diplomatic move [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-95-changping-zhi-zhan-hou-qinguo-weishenme-meiyou-mie-le-zhaoguo-1-lq4wvkd8qx3q7zji6fnnkxi3qcl1]] attributes to [[SuDai|苏代]] when he lobbies [[FanJu|范雎]] after the [[ChangpingBattle|长平之战]]. Instead of only arguing that [[QinState|秦国]] should avoid overextension, Su Dai asks whether [[BaiQi|白起]]'s capture of [[Handan|邯郸]] and destruction of [[ZhaoState|赵国]] would make Bai Qi's merit too great for Fan Ju's comfort.

The persuasion works by changing the addressee's frame of calculation. The public question is whether Qin should finish Zhao; the private question becomes whether Fan Ju wants to empower a military rival whose future title and prestige could exceed his own. That makes the argument a specialized branch of [[InterestReframingPersuasion|利益重构式说服]] inside [[ZonghengjiaDiplomacy|纵横家外交]].

The source does not say Fan Ju was irrational. Su Dai combines the status argument with a strategic one: destroying Zhao might scatter Zhao's population into neighboring states, leaving Qin with land but not people. Status anxiety persuasion is most effective when it can attach the personal fear to a plausible state-interest objection.

## Key Claims
- A persuader can alter policy by turning a state benefit into a minister's personal threat.
- The anxiety does not have to be preexisting and explicit; the persuader can create a pain point by naming a future rank comparison.
- The method works best when personal risk and state risk point to the same recommended action.
- It can redirect a campaign without openly opposing the stronger state's ambition.
