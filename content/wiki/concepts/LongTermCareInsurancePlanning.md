# Long-Term Care Insurance Planning

## Definition
Long-term care insurance planning is the household and public-finance frame for paying ongoing care after disability,失能, cognitive decline, or high-care aging creates needs that ordinary medical reimbursement or critical-illness payout may not cover.

## Current Synthesis
The bounded evidence separates three layers. The private-planning layer asks whether a household can cover ongoing care, equipment, rehabilitation, home help, and institutional care after an acute diagnosis or hospital bill. The service-system layer asks whether money can actually buy trained and dignified care. The public-policy layer asks whether social insurance can finance care services for assessed need rather than leaving families to self-pay.

The current judgment is that long-term-care planning is not just an insurance-product question. It only works when care financing, professional labor, community services, discharge coordination, home support, institutional options, and subject-led dignity are present together. The dementia-care episode adds a China-facing public long-term-care-insurance branch while keeping implementation rules and coverage boundaries source-scoped.

## Key Claims
- Long-term care costs are a separate risk from diagnosis risk, hospital-bill risk, and death-benefit risk.
- Planning must account for care duration, providers, equipment, home adaptation, rehabilitation, institutional options, and cash flow.
- Service quality matters as much as funding because underpaid, overloaded, or poorly trained workers can transfer system failure to vulnerable people.
- Community and hospital systems are connected: weak care channels can produce social hospitalization or family crisis management.
- China-facing long-term-care insurance is a public service-payment mechanism, but it remains assessment-based and incomplete in the source.
- Dementia care shows that cognitive, behavioral, emotional, and family burdens can exceed simple physical-disability benefit categories.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The private-insurance evidence includes sponsored content and should not be treated as product endorsement. The public-policy evidence is a podcast-level account of China long-term-care insurance and does not settle regional benefit rules, eligibility thresholds, fiscal sustainability, provider quality, or implementation timelines.
