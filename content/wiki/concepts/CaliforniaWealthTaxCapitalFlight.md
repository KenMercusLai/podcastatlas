# California Wealth-Tax Capital Flight

California wealth-tax capital flight is the All-In prediction-round frame that a proposed [[California]] wealth tax could push founders, investors, and high-net-worth technology workers to move assets, residence, or company formation elsewhere before the tax even passes. In [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-all-ins-2026-predictions-39681260]], the hosts argue that taxing illiquid or super-voting founder shares can feel like asset seizure when the taxpayer cannot easily sell enough stock to pay.

The concept extends [[WealthTaxLegitimacy]] by making mobility and liquidity the central constraint. The episode does not settle the justice case for taxing billionaires; it asks whether a state that depends on mobile technology wealth can raise revenue without causing the taxable base to leave.

[[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-adam-carolla-on-californias-collapse-fires-failed-leadership-and-gyno-fascism-39710360]] adds a less technical, more populist version of the same warning. [[AdamCarolla|Adam Carolla]] says wealth taxes and higher taxes accelerate migration because politicians treat productive people as a fiscal reserve rather than reducing spending.

## Key Claims
- A wealth-tax campaign can affect behavior before voters decide, because founders and investors may move while the policy is still a ballot risk.
- Illiquid founder stakes make an annual wealth tax different from taxing realized cash income.
- Capital flight can weaken future state budgets if high-income taxpayers, company formations, and investment networks leave.
- Ballot qualification costs and signature-gathering become part of the fiscal story because the political process itself can create uncertainty.
- The source frames [[Texas]] as the obvious destination contrast because Sacks says he has moved there and set up an Austin office.
- The Carolla source broadens the mechanism from founder-share liquidity to a general "productive people leave" claim tied to spending, pensions, and state services.
