# Commitment Bias Public Promise

## Definition
Commitment bias public promise is Brooks' use of public vows or declarations as a behavioral support for staying aligned with long-term values during future difficulty.

## Current Synthesis
[[the-art-of-true-happiness-dr-arthur-brooks-scim2290485637]] presents public commitment as a tool rather than mere ceremony. Wedding vows are the central example: people promise fidelity through sickness, poverty, and hardship because a meaningful relationship is expected to include unhappiness, not avoid it entirely.

The concept sits between self-control and relationship ethics. Public promises make values socially visible, create accountability, and help the future self remember what the present self judged worth suffering for.

## Key Claims
- Publicly declaring an aspiration can create pressure to remain consistent with it.
- Wedding vows illustrate a promise that openly anticipates future difficulty.
- Meaningful commitment does not require constant peace, comfort, or positive emotion.
- Public promises can help long-term values survive short-term impulse, conflict, or discouragement.

## Evidence
- Public declaration - [[the-art-of-true-happiness-dr-arthur-brooks-scim2290485637]] says declaring moral aspirations can create commitment bias.
- Marriage example - [[the-art-of-true-happiness-dr-arthur-brooks-scim2290485637]] uses wedding vows as public promises to stay bonded through sickness, poverty, and difficulty.
- Suffering-with branch - [[the-art-of-true-happiness-dr-arthur-brooks-scim2290485637]] emphasizes staying with another person through difficulty rather than expecting constant ease.
- Practice sequence - [[the-art-of-true-happiness-dr-arthur-brooks-scim2290485637]] links commitment to repeated practice when feeling is absent.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Public commitment can support good values, but it can also trap people in harmful roles if used without judgment, safety, or boundaries. This source treats vows and promises as supports for chosen love and service, not as unconditional commands.
