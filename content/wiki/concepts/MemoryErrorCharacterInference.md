# Memory Error as Character Inference

## Definition
Memory error as character inference is the move from establishing that a recollection is false, inconsistent, or embellished to concluding that the speaker is vain, cowardly, disloyal, or deliberately dishonest.

## Current Synthesis
[[a-polite-word-for-liar-a-polite-word-for-liar|A Polite Word for Liar]] argues that the first finding does not reliably establish the second. [[LarryAdler]]'s glider error and admitted anecdotal borrowing show that desire and narrative improvement can reshape recall, but his anti-McCarthy sacrifice makes a global diagnosis of weak principle or self-serving dishonesty too simple. [[JohnWeitz]]'s secrecy and later revised Munich account likewise admit several interpretations: duty, trauma, friendship, correction, accommodation, or reconstruction.

The practical rule is claim-specific. Contradictory accounts should be compared with early records, independent witnesses, and archives; known falsehoods should be corrected. Moral judgment requires additional evidence about intention, incentives, conduct, and pattern. Narrative style is especially weak evidence: chatter is not proof of lying, silence is not proof of accuracy, and a less dramatic account is not automatically truer.

## Key Claims
- Factual inaccuracy and deliberate deception are separate findings.
- A wish-shaped or improved memory can be sincerely experienced without becoming historically accurate.
- Conduct in other high-cost settings can complicate a global character diagnosis without proving a disputed memory true.
- Silence, secrecy, expansiveness, and humor are memory-management styles, not transparent lie detectors.
- A later merged account can reflect correction, loyalty, accommodation, or reconstruction and therefore needs independent corroboration.
- Claim-level correction should remain firm even when motive and character remain uncertain.

## Evidence
- Admitted reconstruction - [[a-polite-word-for-liar-a-polite-word-for-liar]] reports Adler's glider error and his explanation that a desired version became fact in his mind.
- Countervailing conduct - [[a-polite-word-for-liar-a-polite-word-for-liar]] places Adler's story improvement beside career-ending refusal to name others during the McCarthy era.
- Conflicting styles - [[a-polite-word-for-liar-a-polite-word-for-liar]] contrasts Adler's chatter and whimsy with Weitz's secrecy and retreat.
- Version instability - [[a-polite-word-for-liar-a-polite-word-for-liar]] preserves violent, anticlimactic, and later partially merged accounts of the Munich raid.
- Forensic limit - [[a-polite-word-for-liar-a-polite-word-for-liar]] explicitly cannot establish exactly what occurred in Munich.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Memory fallibility must not become a blanket excuse for fraud or a reason to avoid consequences for consequential false claims. Repeated strategic falsehood, concealed contrary records, direct evidence of intent, or material incentives can support character and deception judgments. Conversely, courage in one setting does not make a person accurate or unselfish everywhere. The episode provides a narrative case study, not a validated diagnostic test or complete historical adjudication.

## What Changed
- Created a rule separating correction of memory content from diagnosis of the rememberer's character.
- Added narrative style and high-cost countervailing conduct as limits on motive inference.
- Preserved corroboration and accountability even when intent remains unresolved.
