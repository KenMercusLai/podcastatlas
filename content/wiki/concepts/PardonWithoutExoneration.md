# Pardon Without Exoneration / 赦免不等于昭雪

## Definition
Pardon Without Exoneration is the gap between release from punishment and proof of innocence. It describes cases where an amnesty may end confinement or penalty but still leaves a public stain of guilt unless the underlying accusation is confronted.

## Current Synthesis
[[ZhangPanLateHan|Zhang Pan]]'s prison refusal in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-867-wugu-bei-po-zangshui-ta-xuanze-baqi-huiji-lqenvoy7x8ic-qn3-bf93qj8tjvk|Hanji 867]] is the concept's core case. After [[DuShangLateHan|Du Shang]] falsely accuses him, Zhang Pan is detained in the Tingwei prison. A general amnesty arrives before the case is finalized, but Zhang Pan refuses to leave because release by grace would not erase the false charge.

The concept separates practical mercy from reputational truth. Zhang Pan's insistence on a face-to-face confrontation shows that a lawful or court-approved exit can still be inadequate if it leaves the factual record unresolved. [[FrankSmith]] extends the concept into modern criminal justice: his death sentence was commuted after doubts arose, but the episode says he did not receive the second trial he wanted, leaving survival from execution disconnected from public exoneration.

## Key Claims
- Amnesty can end punishment while leaving implied guilt intact.
- Officials whose legitimacy depends on public trust may rationally prefer continued confinement to a release that does not clear the accusation.
- Direct confrontation can function as an evidentiary repair when the written memorial record is false.
- Commutation can reduce punishment severity while refusing to reopen the factual record.
- The concept is strongest when the accused person can force the court to reopen the truth question, not merely when a pardon is granted.

## Evidence
- Refusal of release: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-867-wugu-bei-po-zangshui-ta-xuanze-baqi-huiji-lqenvoy7x8ic-qn3-bf93qj8tjvk|Hanji 867]] reports that Zhang Pan remains in restraints after a general amnesty rather than accept a release that would leave the accusation standing.
- Reputation and office legitimacy: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-867-wugu-bei-po-zangshui-ta-xuanze-baqi-huiji-lqenvoy7x8ic-qn3-bf93qj8tjvk|Hanji 867]] has Zhang Pan argue from the position of a provincial governor falsely accused and tortured.
- Evidentiary repair: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-867-wugu-bei-po-zangshui-ta-xuanze-baqi-huiji-lqenvoy7x8ic-qn3-bf93qj8tjvk|Hanji 867]] resolves the case only when Du Shang is summoned and cannot defeat Zhang Pan's account.
- Commutation without a second trial: [[bargaining-chips-nvidia-is-the-bank-of-ai-6a9a86ffe7a9fe2478c4fc41]] says Smith's death sentence was commuted in 1954 after doubts emerged, but he never received the new trial he sought.
- Life after unresolved mercy: [[bargaining-chips-nvidia-is-the-bank-of-ai-6a9a86ffe7a9fe2478c4fc41]] presents Smith's later imprisonment and release as shaped by a conviction that was softened by clemency but not undone by exoneration.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not prove that pardons or commutations generally lack legal effect. They show that legal mercy can be inadequate for factual and reputational needs. Zhang Pan's success depends on officials reporting the refusal and the emperor allowing confrontation, while Smith's case shows the opposite limit: mercy may save life without creating a forum for proof.
