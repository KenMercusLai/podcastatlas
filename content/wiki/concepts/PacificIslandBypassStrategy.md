# Pacific Island Bypass Strategy

## Definition

Pacific island bypass strategy is the selective seizure of positions needed for air, naval, and logistical reach while isolating stronger enemy bases whose supply lines can be cut without a direct assault.

## Current Synthesis

[[dchha66-supernova-in-the-east-v-dchha66-supernova-in-the-east-v|Supernova in the East V]] describes Allied leapfrogging as a way to make Japanese fortification lose strategic value. Instead of reducing every defended position, Allied forces moved through weaker points, established or captured bases, and used air power, submarines, and naval control to sever the positions left behind.

Rabaul is the episode's clearest case: elaborate defenses and expectations of a final battle did not force the Allies to supply that battle. The bypass saved the attacker from some direct costs but transferred prolonged deprivation onto isolated Japanese troops. It was therefore an operational economy, not a humane end to fighting for those trapped inside the abandoned perimeter.

## Key Claims

- A fortified base has strategic value only while it can affect movement, supply, air operations, or command beyond its perimeter.
- Airfields, anchorages, and shipping lanes can matter more than possession of every island.
- Air-sea control can turn a bypassed garrison into a containment problem rather than an assault objective.
- Selective attack reduces some attacker casualties and time while imposing starvation, disease, and strategic irrelevance on isolated defenders.
- Bypass requires sufficient reach, intelligence, engineering, transport, and defensive capacity to keep the isolated base from disrupting the new route.

## Evidence

- Rabaul case: [[dchha66-supernova-in-the-east-v-dchha66-supernova-in-the-east-v]] says Japanese defenders prepared major fortifications but were neutralized and bypassed rather than given the expected decisive battle.
- Weak-point logic: [[dchha66-supernova-in-the-east-v-dchha66-supernova-in-the-east-v]] reports a Japanese intelligence officer's comparison of American movement to water entering a ship through its weakest point.
- Isolation mechanism: [[dchha66-supernova-in-the-east-v-dchha66-supernova-in-the-east-v]] connects submarines, air attacks, shipping loss, and bypass operations to more than 100,000 Japanese troops isolated in New Guinea.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Bypass is not universally available. A base may still control a required route, threaten nearby operations, shelter mobile forces, or retain political importance. The episode does not provide a full comparison of assaulted and bypassed objectives, and its claims about avoided costs and isolated troop totals remain source-scoped. Isolation can reduce attacker exposure while creating an extended humanitarian catastrophe among defenders.

## What Changed

- Established selective seizure and supply-line isolation as a linked operational strategy rather than a simple refusal to attack.
