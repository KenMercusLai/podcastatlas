# Predictive World-Model Anxiety / 预测世界模型焦虑

## Definition
Predictive world-model anxiety is E46's claim that the same simulation capacity that lets the brain plan, remember, and imagine can also replay possible bad futures as present distress. The mind does not simply copy the world; it generates a model and uses sensory input to correct it.

## Current Synthesis
The concept sits at the third layer of [[EvolutionaryMisrecognition]]. Mammalian simulation creates a practical advantage because an organism can observe, model, and rehearse before acting. The cost is that the model can keep producing scenarios that are not happening now. Meng Yan connects this to meditation and breath attention: returning to immediate sensation can interrupt the simulator's output without pretending the model is useless.

## Key Claims
- The brain's world model is generative and predictive, not a neutral recording.
- Simulation enables planning, memory, counterfactuals, and regret.
- Anxiety can be understood as repeated internal playback of possible future harm.
- Sensory attention can temporarily rebalance the relation between model output and present input.
- The mechanism helps explain why a person can treat an imagined betrayal as emotionally real.

## Evidence
- Simulation layer: [[e46-women-congwei-kanjian-bici-lmliksaiwxakmjbn-p2voaaoenxh]] locates the third intelligence breakthrough in mammalian world modeling and pre-action simulation.
- Anxiety explanation: [[e46-women-congwei-kanjian-bici-lmliksaiwxakmjbn-p2voaaoenxh]] explicitly describes anxiety as bad events replaying inside the brain's simulator before they occur.
- Film bridge: [[e46-women-congwei-kanjian-bici-lmliksaiwxakmjbn-p2voaaoenxh]] applies this model-making to Hyun-tae's generated picture of In-chul stealing the Walkman and fleeing.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept does not claim that all anxiety is only imagination or that meditation replaces clinical care. It names one source-framed cognitive mechanism: simulation can be adaptive and distressing at the same time.
