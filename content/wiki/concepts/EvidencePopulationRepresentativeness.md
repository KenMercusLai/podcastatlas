# Evidence Population Representativeness

## Definition

Evidence population representativeness is the degree to which a study population contains the biological, demographic, clinical, and social variation needed for its findings to support the intended use population.

## Current Synthesis

Recruiting any controlled cohort can be difficult, and a narrow baseline study may still answer a bounded question. The problem arises when evidence from white adult men is treated as automatically actionable for women, pregnant people, different ages or racial groups, or patients with distinct conditions and exposures.

Inclusion is not merely a counting target. Researchers must anticipate effect modification, measure relevant differences, recruit without repeating historical coercion, and report where subgroup evidence is too weak for confident inference. Expanding representation can improve relevance while also introducing fertility, pregnancy, burden, trust, and consent considerations that require protection rather than exclusion by default.

## Key Claims

- A study can be internally informative while remaining externally weak for groups it did not adequately include.
- Sample adequacy depends on the target decision, not on demographic variety in the abstract.
- Sex, pregnancy, age, race, health status, exposure timing, and social conditions may change both benefit and harm.
- Recruitment difficulty explains some narrow evidence bases but does not justify silent universalization.
- Historical mistreatment can reduce trust and make meaningful recruitment harder, requiring stronger relationships and safeguards.
- Subgroup inclusion without sufficient size, measurement, or analysis can create an appearance of representativeness without usable evidence.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

- The episode does not identify the heart-attack studies, quantify underrepresentation, define racial categories, or provide subgroup effect estimates.
- Representation alone does not establish causal validity, measurement quality, adequate power, or clinical usefulness.
- Biological difference should not be inferred from demographic labels without evidence, while social and environmental mechanisms should not be mislabeled as innate biology.

## What Changed

- Created a medical-evidence framework separating a useful bounded baseline from unjustified population-wide generalization.
