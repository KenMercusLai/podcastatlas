# Guizhou Cuisine Flavor Transfer / 贵州酸辣风味迁移

Guizhou cuisine flavor transfer / 贵州酸辣风味迁移 is the episode's account of why [[Guizhou|贵州]] flavors can enter contemporary restaurant cooking as modular, recognizable components. In [[ep245-yinian-yidu-xunwei-zhilv-jinnian-qu-chi-chuangyi-cai-li9wtavemokcckialbbj7unpmqsz]], [[QiuLian|邱连]] explains that Guizhou was long summarized as "川菜黔味," but its own sour, chili, and fermentation systems give chefs many practical handles.

The source emphasizes fermented acid and chili variety: red sour soup, 虾酸, 臭酸, 胡辣, 糟辣椒, oil chili, and 木姜子. These can cut fat, brighten meat, or become aromatic markers in dishes and drinks, making Guizhou a strong case for [[RegionalFlavorTransfer|地方风味迁移]] and [[ChineseBistroLocalization|中式小酒馆本土化]].

## Key Claims
- Guizhou's acid-spicy system is not one flavor; it is a set of fermented, aromatic, and heat-based tools.
- 木姜子 works as a portable regional signal because its fresh citrus-lemongrass-like aroma can balance fattier roasted dishes.
- Red sour soup becomes more transferable when it moves from sour-soup fish or beef into wontons, meatballs, and bistro dishes.
- A cuisine can become visible first through transferable elements before diners understand its full regional grammar.
