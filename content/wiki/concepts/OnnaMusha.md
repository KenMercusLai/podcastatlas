# Onna-musha

## Definition
Onna-musha are female warriors in Japanese samurai culture, introduced in [[661-dawn-of-the-samurai-the-shogun-triumphant-part-4-glt7837412375]] through the figure of [[TomoeGozen]].

## Current Synthesis
The episode uses Tomoe to move from one famous warrior woman to a broader historical category. Girls from samurai families could be taught riding, saddle skills, and archery, and female warriors were recognizable enough in Japanese culture for the naginata to become especially associated with them in literature.

The source's cautious synthesis is that Tomoe's exact life is uncertain but the broader category of female samurai participation is not simply fantasy. The cited 2022 excavation of a 1580 battle site, where 35 of 105 warrior remains were identified as women, supports the argument that female battlefield presence has material grounding.

## Key Claims
- Tomoe's fame should not be reduced to a purely fictional exception.
- Female warriors were a recognized cultural category in Japanese samurai memory.
- Martial training for girls from samurai families makes female combat competence historically plausible.
- The naginata's literary association with women gives the category a durable visual and narrative form.
- Archaeological evidence broadens the question beyond Tomoe's individual historicity.

## Evidence
- Tomoe case: [[661-dawn-of-the-samurai-the-shogun-triumphant-part-4-glt7837412375]] says most scholars of the period appear to regard Tomoe as real but amplified into legend.
- Training and weaponry: [[661-dawn-of-the-samurai-the-shogun-triumphant-part-4-glt7837412375]] describes riding, saddle, archery, and naginata associations.
- Material evidence: [[661-dawn-of-the-samurai-the-shogun-triumphant-part-4-glt7837412375]] cites a 2022 excavation of a 1580 battle site identifying 35 of 105 warrior remains as women.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source cautions that specific later claims about Tomoe, including command at Kurikara Pass, lack contemporary evidence. The excavation evidence also comes from a later battle site, so it supports broader plausibility rather than proving Tomoe's biography.
