# Selective Sports Neutrality

## Definition

Selective sports neutrality is an institutional posture that treats event continuity as outside politics while overlooking political control of athlete access, training, selection, expression, or safety.

## Current Synthesis

[[hitler-s-olympics-part-5-the-amateur-s-hour-hitler-s-olympics-part-5-the-amateur-s-hour|Hitler’s Olympics, Part 5]] makes neutrality testable at the level of conditions rather than declarations. [[AveryBrundage]] said Olympic participation should be free of class, creed, and racial discrimination, yet his German inspection relied on Nazi interpreters, questioned Jewish representatives in front of regime officials, discounted reports of exclusion and assault, and accepted promises of formal eligibility without equal access to preparation.

[[MargaretLambert]] shows the failure of that posture. Her coerced candidacy could be displayed as inclusion while the regime controlled facilities, competition, family pressure, and final selection. A neutrality doctrine therefore becomes selective when it protects the institution from boycott pressure but does not protect athletes from the political conditions determining who can qualify or speak safely.

## Key Claims

- Political neutrality cannot be assessed solely from formal eligibility promises.
- Training access, selection authority, interpreter control, and safe testimony are part of sporting fairness.
- Event continuity is itself politically consequential when participation supplies legitimacy to a host regime.
- Institutions can punish visible protest while tolerating less visible political control over athletes.
- Coerced token inclusion can conceal exclusion rather than correct it.
- Neutrality is most credible when it is applied symmetrically to hosts, officials, and athletes.

## Evidence

- Managed inquiry: [[hitler-s-olympics-part-5-the-amateur-s-hour-hitler-s-olympics-part-5-the-amateur-s-hour]] describes Brundage's reliance on Nazi interpreters and questioning in officials' presence.
- Unequal conditions: [[hitler-s-olympics-part-5-the-amateur-s-hour-hitler-s-olympics-part-5-the-amateur-s-hour]] reports closed facilities, disrupted events, assaults, and inadequate preparation for Jewish athletes.
- Token inclusion: [[hitler-s-olympics-part-5-the-amateur-s-hour-hitler-s-olympics-part-5-the-amateur-s-hour]] connects Lambert's coerced candidacy to international propaganda.
- Selection control: [[hitler-s-olympics-part-5-the-amateur-s-hour-hitler-s-olympics-part-5-the-amateur-s-hour]] reports Lambert's exclusion after she equaled the German record.
- Institutional consequence: [[hitler-s-olympics-part-5-the-amateur-s-hour-hitler-s-olympics-part-5-the-amateur-s-hour]] links accepted assurances to the American decision to participate and Brundage's later IOC entry.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source does not establish that every neutrality claim is cynical, that participation always produces more harm than refusal, or that Brundage alone controlled American and international Olympic decisions. It finds no explicit appointment bargain and presents some motive analysis as inference. The concept should therefore diagnose asymmetric standards and ignored conditions, not substitute for case-specific evidence about authority, intent, or boycott effects.

## What Changed

- Established a framework distinguishing formal political separation from equal material conditions for participation.
- Added coerced token inclusion and controlled testimony as tests of neutrality claims.
