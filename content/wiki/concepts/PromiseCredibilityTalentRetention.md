# Promise Credibility and Talent Retention / 承诺信用与人才流失

Promise credibility and talent retention / 承诺信用与人才流失 is the leadership pattern [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-983-zhi-hui-pianren-de-laoban-yongyuan-bushi-hao-laoban-lknr2zydwjytjo7od-rt3zi-4k-s|Hanji 983]] extracts from [[YuanShu|袁术]]'s treatment of [[SunCe|孙策]]. Yuan Shu can command offices and use Sun Ce's military ability, but he repeatedly promises commandery rewards and gives them to others after Sun Ce has relied on the promise.

The episode's concrete chain runs through [[JiujiangCommandery|九江郡]] and [[LujiangCommandery|庐江郡]]. Yuan Shu first promises Sun Ce Jiujiang and appoints [[ChenJiLateHan|陈纪]] instead. He then promises Lujiang if Sun Ce defeats [[LuKangLateHan|陆康]], but appoints [[LiuXunLateHan|刘勋]] after the victory. The repeated reversal makes Sun Ce's later exit from Yuan Shu intelligible as a trust failure, not only as ambition.

This concept is narrower than [[SmallTrustBuildsLargeTrust|小信诚则大信立]]. Small trust builds large trust treats credibility as a statecraft condition across public promises and law. Promise credibility and talent retention focuses on a leader-subordinate relationship: talent may keep working after one disappointment, but repeated non-delivery teaches capable people that staying is unsafe or unrewarding.

The concept also differs from [[TalentAttractionThroughRespect|礼贤下士式人才吸附]]. Respect can bring talent into a camp; credible promises help keep that talent once it has produced value. Yuan Shu's failure is that he wants Sun Ce's service while preserving full discretionary control over rewards, and that combination eventually makes his control weaker rather than stronger.

## Key Claims
- A leader can lose useful talent by repeatedly changing promised rewards after the subordinate has relied on them.
- Broken promises are more damaging when they concern offices, troops, territory, or career security rather than only etiquette.
- Short-term discretion over appointments can produce long-term loss of trust and autonomous exit.
- Talent retention requires credible follow-through after value is delivered, not only initial respect or verbal encouragement.
- A broken-promise pattern can make later separation appear legitimate to the departing actor and to observers.
