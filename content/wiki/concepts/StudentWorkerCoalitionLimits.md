# Student-Worker Coalition Limits

## Definition

Student-Worker Coalition Limits distinguish simultaneous or mutually reinforcing protest by students and workers from a coalition with shared demands, organization, strategy, and preferred settlement.

## Current Synthesis

In [[353-paris-1968-the-students-revolt-part-1-glt6124707164]], student unrest helped open a national crisis, but the worker strike wave did not simply join the students' project. Students challenged university hierarchy, sexual rules, paternal authority, and everyday life; workers emphasized wages, hours, conditions, and recognition; unions negotiated; and the Communist Party initially distrusted the student revolt as bourgeois disorder.

The distinction matters because aggregate scale can conceal political heterogeneity. Millions striking at the same time as student occupations created leverage against the state, yet different groups could reject or accept concessions for different reasons and could not be assumed to support one revolutionary outcome.

## Key Claims

- Temporal overlap and a common opponent do not establish a unified movement.
- Students and workers may differ in grievance, organization, class position, tactics, and desired settlement.
- Union and party intermediaries can mobilize workers while distrusting student leadership or culture.
- Material concessions test coalition unity because they address some demands while leaving broader anti-authoritarian aims untouched.
- Coalition analysis should identify who can negotiate, who can end action, and whose goals remain outside the settlement.

## Evidence

- Different demands: [[353-paris-1968-the-students-revolt-part-1-glt6124707164]] contrasts student demands for transformed life with worker demands over wages, hours, conditions, and recognition.
- Organizational distrust: [[353-paris-1968-the-students-revolt-part-1-glt6124707164]] says the Communist Party initially treated the student revolt as bourgeois disorder.
- Settlement divergence: [[353-paris-1968-the-students-revolt-part-1-glt6124707164]] reports large government concessions and their rejection at Renault Billancourt.
- Retrospective qualification: [[353-paris-1968-the-students-revolt-part-1-glt6124707164]] attributes to [[DanielCohnBendit]] the judgment that students and workers were never one force.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Difference does not mean separation. Student street conflict helped widen the crisis, worker strikes transformed its scale, and each could strengthen the other's leverage. The source gives a high-level narrative rather than workplace-by-workplace or faction-by-faction evidence, so the degree of cooperation and disagreement remains uneven and source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Created a coalition test that separates shared timing and leverage from shared program and authority.
