# Science Education As Civic Literacy / 作为公民素养的科学教育

Science education as civic literacy is the main frame added by [[ep262-gaokao-guohou-zhishi-guiling-women-xuyao-zenyang-de-kexue-jiaoyu-ls8ecqcdpe9d5ioytyca3jqdf-3r]]. The episode argues that science education is not only pre-professional training for future scientists; it is ordinary infrastructure for judging health, technology, parenting, environmental claims, and [[AIDefaultLearningEnvironment|AI-era information]].

The concept turns "scientific literacy" into a life-practice skill. A person may forget formulas after the gaokao, but still needs habits for finding evidence, separating observation from inference, weighing uncertainty, avoiding casual medication, deciding when to trust expertise, and modeling everyday tradeoffs such as breakfast choices across health, cost, and convenience.

This is why the episode connects school science to public life. Vaccines, rabies, infectious disease, drug use, AI answers, environmental change, and children's projects all become tests of whether science education has become usable judgment rather than a set of exam facts.

## Key Claims
- Science education should serve ordinary citizens as well as future specialists.
- Scientific literacy is stronger when it combines knowledge, evidence habits, inquiry, responsibility, and everyday decision-making.
- "Knowledge reset" after exams signals that facts were not integrated into durable concepts or methods.
- AI raises the stakes because students and adults need evidence-seeking and [[HumanJudgmentUnderAI|human judgment]] when fluent systems supply plausible answers.
- Everyday life can be modeled scientifically when people identify variables, constraints, weights, and revised assumptions.
