## Definition
Retail channel complementarity is the condition in which physical retail and direct-to-consumer sales serve different purchase occasions, customers, or product bundles, allowing one channel to add demand rather than merely transfer revenue from the other.

## Current Synthesis
XO Marshmallow supplies an impulse-oriented case: customers may want immediate access to one indulgent item without shipping cost or delay, while DTC can retain planned purchases, larger baskets, assortment depth, gifting, and owned customer relationships. Complementarity is a hypothesis, not a default; the brand must test whether grocery adds customers and occasions after accounting for wholesale margins, fees, inventory, production, and possible online cannibalization.

## Key Claims
- Channel analysis should begin with the job and timing of the purchase rather than a generic DTC-versus-wholesale preference.
- Immediate, low-ticket impulse products may gain incrementality from physical availability when shipping friction suppresses online demand.
- DTC can remain valuable for assortment, planned replenishment, bundles, gifting, customer data, and story depth after retail launch.
- Retail expansion should be staged so that sell-through and production reliability are learned before mass commitments.
- Incremental revenue must be evaluated against wholesale economics and any decline in higher-margin direct orders.

## Evidence
### Distinct purchase occasions
- [[advice-line-with-tara-bosch-of-smartsweets-8aab80a1-b0c5-4103-b875-f5d4823d07ce]] records XO Marshmallow customers objecting to shipping cost and wait time and Bosch's view that grocery accessibility serves a different occasion from online purchasing.

### Channel mix and staged learning
- [[advice-line-with-tara-bosch-of-smartsweets-8aab80a1-b0c5-4103-b875-f5d4823d07ce]] uses SmartSweets' reported wholesale-heavy mix and recommends natural or regional retail before mass expansion.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- Grocery can cannibalize DTC, compress margin, increase trade spending, create returns or chargebacks, and make customer attribution harder.
- Physical access does not guarantee retail velocity; shelf position, price, formulation, packaging, retailer fit, and repeat purchase still decide performance.
- The episode provides advice and reported experience, not a controlled comparison of XO Marshmallow channel cohorts.

## What Changed
- Established channel complementarity as a purchase-occasion hypothesis that requires explicit incrementality and economics tests.
