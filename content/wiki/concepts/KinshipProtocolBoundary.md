## Definition

亲情名分边界 / kinship protocol boundary is the court-governance principle that royal affection remains politically safe only when it stays inside public hierarchy, ritual form, fiscal limits, and territorial distance.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 758 creates this concept through [[EmperorZhangOfHan|Emperor Zhang of Han]]'s late-reign treatment of royal kin. The episode does not condemn kinship affection or generosity as such. It argues that affection becomes politically dangerous when relatives in the capital receive treatment that weakens the visible distinction between family intimacy and the emperor-subject order.

The source gives two complementary objections. [[HeChangEasternHan|He Chang]] makes the fiscal objection: disasters, Qiang pressure, exhausted local finance, and depleted reserves mean that lavish rewards and extended royal stays consume public capacity at the wrong time. [[SongYiEasternHan|Song Yi]] makes the protocol objection: uncles and brothers may be kin, but in the court setting they are still subjects, so palace entry, seating, bowing, food, gifts, residence, and fief return have to preserve rank.

The concept therefore marks a boundary between private emotion and public form. A ruler can remain humane and loyal while still needing rules that tell kin when to approach, when to withdraw, and when their status must be treated as official rather than familial.

## Key Claims

- Royal kinship does not cancel sovereign-subject hierarchy inside the court.
- Affection becomes a governance risk when it changes access, seating, meals, gifts, residence, or fief boundaries.
- Fiscal circumstance is part of protocol: generosity is harder to justify when disasters, war, or public exhaustion define the moment.
- Ritual form is not empty ceremony; it helps keep private intimacy from becoming public privilege.
- The concept is a proportion test, not an anti-family rule.

## Evidence

- Royal-kin privilege: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-758-suoyou-gaoqingshang-beihou-doushi-fencungan-lr-6tzffmwdtzidkf3y8ule35qf7|Hanji 758]] says Zhangdi gives visiting royal relatives unusually high treatment, keeps them in Luoyang, and lets their court privileges exceed regular distance.
- Fiscal objection: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-758-suoyou-gaoqingshang-beihou-doushi-fencungan-lr-6tzffmwdtzidkf3y8ule35qf7|Hanji 758]] has He Chang argue that floods, droughts, Qiang harm, public-private exhaustion, and depleted reserves make excessive gifts improper.
- Protocol objection: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-758-suoyou-gaoqingshang-beihou-doushi-fencungan-lr-6tzffmwdtzidkf3y8ule35qf7|Hanji 758]] has Song Yi argue that royal uncles and brothers are still subjects and should not use kinship to bypass hierarchical form.
- Territorial-distance remedy: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-758-suoyou-gaoqingshang-beihou-doushi-fencungan-lr-6tzffmwdtzidkf3y8ule35qf7|Hanji 758]] says Song Yi asks that adult princes with households be sent back to their fiefs rather than remain in the capital.
- Qualification inside praise: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-758-suoyou-gaoqingshang-beihou-doushi-fencungan-lr-6tzffmwdtzidkf3y8ule35qf7|Hanji 758]] also presents Zhangdi as loyal, humane, filial, frugal in his final edict, and oriented toward rites and music, showing that the problem is lost proportion rather than bad intent alone.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept should not be flattened into hostility toward relatives or court ritualism for its own sake. Hanji 758 praises Zhangdi's humane temperament and simple deathbed order, and it presents kinship feeling as good before it becomes excessive. The concept applies when affection alters public resources, capital residence, or visible hierarchy enough that other officials have to restore distance.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Hanji 758's paired He Chang and Song Yi remonstrance against Zhangdi's overgenerous royal-kin treatment.
