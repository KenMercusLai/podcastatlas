# Enlightenment

## Definition

The Enlightenment is a contested label for overlapping later-seventeenth- and eighteenth-century European projects that elevated inquiry, criticism, sociability, reform, and universal claims while remaining shaped by particular Christian, political, commercial, and imperial settings.

## Current Synthesis

The source rejects a single story in which reason suddenly defeated superstition. It treats the Enlightenment as internally plural: British, Scottish, Dutch, French, Protestant, Catholic, courtly, university, scientific, deist, religious, and atheist variants overlapped without becoming one doctrine. Its self-image of light after darkness and reform after corruption partly repeated older Christian patterns even when thinkers attacked Churches or revelation.

The strongest synthesis joins achievement to qualification. Enlightenment inquiry, science, constitutional admiration, public discussion, and universal rights helped reorganize modern politics and knowledge. The same confidence in classification and civilization also accompanied slavery, colonial wealth, racial hierarchy, and universal claims that obscured their own European provenance.

## Key Claims

- The period's dates and geography are approximate; the later seventeenth century through the French Revolution and Napoleon is a useful but porous frame.
- Enlightenment thought was produced through institutions, media, commerce, correspondence, and sociability as well as through canonical individuals.
- Religion remained active inside the period: revival, civil religion, deism, Church conflict, and atheism coexisted.
- Scientific inquiry encouraged mechanical and law-governed models of nature without making every thinker a simple rationalist or opponent of faith.
- Universal reason, liberty, and civilization were historically generative but also vulnerable to colonial and racial hierarchy.
- The Enlightenment is best treated as a family of contested transformations rather than either pure emancipation or a single corrupt project.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

- The episode is an interpretive overview and cannot establish one causal genealogy for every Enlightenment thinker, institution, or later ideology.
- Christian inheritance does not erase classical, Islamic, scientific, commercial, or other intellectual inputs, nor does it make anti-clerical conflict unreal.
- Racist or colonial claims by particular writers should not be generalized to all Enlightenment figures; anti-colonial criticism and recognition of non-European sophistication also existed.

## What Changed

- Established an internally plural account joining religious inheritance, social infrastructure, scientific inquiry, universal ambition, and colonial-racial limits.
