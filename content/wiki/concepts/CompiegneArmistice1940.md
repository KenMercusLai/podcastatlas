# Compiègne Armistice (1940)

## Definition

The Compiègne armistice was the June 1940 agreement ending the main campaign between Germany and France and dividing metropolitan France between German occupation and a southern regime led from Vichy.

## Current Synthesis

The episode treats the armistice as both a strategic settlement and a performance of historical revenge. [[AdolfHitler]] ordered the ceremony held in the same railway carriage and clearing where Germany had accepted the 1918 armistice, sat in Marshal Foch's former place, heard the preamble, and then departed. The setting turned control of territory into a symbolic reversal of Germany's earlier humiliation.

The political precondition was French division over whether military defeat required surrender. [[PaulReynaud]] and [[CharlesDeGaulle]] favored continued resistance from abroad, while [[PhilippePetain]] and [[MaximeWeygand]] prioritized ending the metropolitan battle and preserving order. The agreement therefore joined battlefield collapse, cabinet choice, occupation, and the creation of [[VichyFrance]].

## Key Claims

- The armistice followed both military defeat and a contested French political decision.
- Compiègne's 1918 site and railway carriage were deliberately reused.
- Hitler staged the ceremony as symbolic reversal and humiliation.
- The settlement divided metropolitan France into occupied and Vichy-governed zones.
- The armistice ended the principal campaign without ending French resistance as a political claim.

- Territorial result: [[622-the-nazis-at-war-the-fall-of-france-part-3-glt6447940829]] describes German occupation and Pétain's southern government at Vichy.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode supplies a high-level narrative rather than the full articles, negotiations, legal consequences, imperial implications, prisoner arrangements, occupation administration, or later changes to the zones. Territorial descriptions, motives, dialogue, and the ceremony's psychological meaning remain source-scoped.
