# Assisted Driving Trust Formation / 辅助驾驶信任形成

## Definition

Assisted driving trust formation is the process by which drivers move from unease or distrust toward calibrated routine use of advanced driver-assistance features. It depends on clear responsibility boundaries, direct experience, visible safety behavior, predictable motion, low-risk demonstrations, peer reassurance, and repeated success across complete journeys.

## Current Synthesis

Trust is a lived adoption problem rather than a feature-list problem. Owners change behavior after concrete successes such as automatic parking or fatigue reduction, but the newer source adds that difficult-scene completion is not enough: steering oscillation, abrupt motion, hesitation, or behavior outside passenger expectations can prompt takeover even when the car eventually succeeds.

The current judgment is cautious and calibrated. Trust should grow from stable performance, restrained claims, clear responsibility, and predictable behavior over full journeys. It is slow to build, easy to destroy, and should not be confused with a transfer of legal responsibility.

## Key Claims

- Trust in assisted driving forms through repeated successful scenes more than through specifications alone.
- Initial surprise, such as unexpected steering movement, can delay adoption even when the feature works.
- Low-risk demonstrations such as automatic parking can lower the psychological threshold for users and observers.
- Active safety and broad road coverage can support trust, but they do not remove the need for human supervision under current assisted-driving boundaries.
- Family, peers, and professional driving experience can accelerate trust when they connect the system to real safety or fatigue reduction.
- Natural, stable, and predictable behavior over long drives matters more than isolated spectacular maneuvers.
- Marketing that exceeds the product's responsibility boundary can destroy trust when user expectations and system behavior diverge.

## Evidence

- **Embodied adoption:** [[ep279-dang-fangxiangpan-manman-songkai-women-ruhe-yu-che-xiangchu-lq2elbnjy6xrhixxqjs-y7erhlnv]] describes an M9 owner who avoided assisted driving for thousands of kilometers after a frightening steering experience, then began using it routinely after a mountain-road return showed clear fatigue reduction.
- **Demonstration effect:** [[ep279-dang-fangxiangpan-manman-songkai-women-ruhe-yu-che-xiangchu-lq2elbnjy6xrhixxqjs-y7erhlnv]] says automatic side parking convinced friends because they could directly see the car do better than their own driving.
- **Safety framing:** [[ep279-dang-fangxiangpan-manman-songkai-women-ruhe-yu-che-xiangchu-lq2elbnjy6xrhixxqjs-y7erhlnv]] has Peng Lei connect consumer acceptance to safety restraint, active safety, 2023 mass delivery, and a no-map route designed for real roads.
- **Family and professional experience:** [[ep279-dang-fangxiangpan-manman-songkai-women-ruhe-yu-che-xiangchu-lq2elbnjy6xrhixxqjs-y7erhlnv]] includes a professional driver who expected assisted driving because of a relative's impaired night vision and an older father who moved from skepticism to sharing the experience.
- **Predictable journey behavior:** [[tefan-cong-hui-kaiche-dao-hui-zuoshi-ai-ruhe-jinru-zhenshi-shijie-gkwrijeopcilaoo4hwtn2dns]] distinguishes scene completion from calm, human-like performance and says unstable steering or expectation-breaking action can trigger takeover despite technical success.
- **Claim calibration:** [[tefan-cong-hui-kaiche-dao-hui-zuoshi-ai-ruhe-jinru-zhenshi-shijie-gkwrijeopcilaoo4hwtn2dns]] argues that clearer responsibility rules and restrained marketing help align user expectations with current L2 supervision duties.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

- The episode is based on product-leader and owner stories, not independent safety evaluation.
- Positive trust stories can create overconfidence if users forget the current [[AutonomousDrivingResponsibilityBoundary|responsibility boundary]].
- The source does not quantify disengagements, edge-case failures, accident rates, or misuse risk.
- Trust formation differs by road type, weather, driver skill, regulation, and interface design.
- “Human-like” behavior is not an independent safety metric and can conceal rare-event failure if judged only by comfort.

## What Changed

- Added long-duration stability and predictable motion as trust mechanisms beyond feature coverage.
- Added expectation calibration: expansive marketing can destroy trust even when some functions work well.
