# Interpersonal Synchrony and Rapport

## Definition
Interpersonal synchrony is temporal coordination across posture, movement, gaze, breathing, heart activity, or brain activity that can accompany rapport and shared task engagement.

## Current Synthesis
The Platt episode presents synchrony as a measurable correlate of social closeness, communication, trust, teamwork, and group decision performance. It can emerge through affiliative touch, mirroring, shared identity, or structured deep conversation. Schore adds a clinical and developmental interpretation in which shared emotional timing helps one person recognize and regulate another through face, voice, gesture, and bodily state.

Across both sources, synchrony is most useful as one signal or process of coordination, not a standalone test of trustworthiness, attachment security, therapeutic effectiveness, or relationship quality.

## Key Claims
- Rapport can involve coordination across several behavioral and physiological channels.
- Synchrony is associated with communication, trust, teamwork, and relationship quality.
- Structured deep conversation may align strangers more than superficial exchange.
- Shared team cues can redirect attention toward common identity and coordination.
- Synchrony should be combined with outcome and context measures before making decisions about people or teams.
- Emotional synchrony may support attunement and co-regulation, but mismatch and repair remain part of healthy relationships.

## Evidence
- Multichannel coordination - [[how-to-make-better-decisions-dr-michael-platt-scim9606820714]] lists movement, gaze, breathing, heartbeat, and brain activity as synchrony channels.
- Group decisions - [[how-to-make-better-decisions-dr-michael-platt-scim9606820714]] reports greater heart-rhythm synchrony in committees more likely to solve a difficult problem correctly.
- Deep conversation - [[how-to-make-better-decisions-dr-michael-platt-scim9606820714]] discusses Fast Friends-style questions and alignment of strangers' brain activity.
- Affective synchrony - [[how-relationships-shape-your-brain-dr-allan-schore-scim1947263719]] connects shared emotional timing, face, voice, gesture, hyperscanning, and therapeutic regulation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Association does not establish that synchrony causes accuracy, trust, attachment security, therapeutic change, or healthy relationships. Shared stress, imitation, compliance, measurement choices, or task structure may also create coordination. The sources do not validate synchrony as a hiring, compatibility, diagnosis, treatment-selection, or deception-detection tool, and Schore's right-hemisphere mechanism remains source-scoped.

## What Changed
- Extended behavioral and physiological synchrony into affective attunement and co-regulation.
- Preserved mismatch, repair, and causal uncertainty as boundaries on synchrony claims.
