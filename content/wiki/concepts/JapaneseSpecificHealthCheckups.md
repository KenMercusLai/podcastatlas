# Japanese Specific Health Checkups / 日本特定健诊

Japanese specific health checkups are the post-2008 screening system described in [[177-riben-yibao-jijin-weihe-yao-zhichi-mianfei-de-guomin-tijian-lh06qonrzgjprqdwl8dygn4hoxpk]]. The episode says insurers are required to provide standardized checks for many 40-to-74-year-old insured people, with the [[MinistryOfHealthLabourAndWelfare|厚生劳动省]] setting the basic items and implementation standards.

The system is narrower than [[NingenDock|ningen dock]]. Its target is not a luxury whole-body exam but [[LifestyleDiseasePrevention|lifestyle-disease prevention]], especially metabolic syndrome and visceral-fat-related risk. Measures such as waist circumference, BMI, blood pressure, blood tests, urine tests, and lifestyle questionnaires become a way to classify risk and route people into follow-up guidance.

## Key Claims
- The checkup is an insurer-governed prevention workflow rather than only a medical-service benefit.
- The policy works through four steps in the source: planning, examination, screening and stratifying people for guidance, and delivering health guidance over several months.
- Its practical value depends on whether the screened person accepts follow-up and changes behavior, not only whether the exam is free.
- Participation remains a weakness: the source cites 2022 implementation rates of 58.1% for checkups and 26.5% for specific health guidance.
- Evidence is stronger for modest weight and waist improvements than for broad cardiovascular-risk or total-medical-cost reduction.
