# Workplace Behavior Training Data

Workplace behavior training data is employee activity captured as training material for AI systems, especially agents that must operate software interfaces. [[tech-20260424-0424-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260424-0424-mp-tech-pod-128]] adds the concept through [[Reuters]] reporting that [[Meta]] is installing software on U.S. employee computers to capture mouse movements, clicks, and keystrokes for AI training.

The source makes this distinct from ordinary productivity monitoring. Meta says the data will not be used for performance reviews and managers will not be able to access it, but [[AnitaRamaswamy]] emphasizes the labor-trust problem: employees can feel they are training AI replacements, especially when layoffs are expected nearby.

## Key Claims
- Computer-use agents need demonstrations of how people actually operate software, not only text instructions or final outputs.
- Capturing mouse, click, and keystroke traces can create valuable [[AgentData]] while also expanding [[AIWorkforceMonitoring]].
- Company assurances about non-performance use still need trust, transparency, data boundaries, and enforceable governance.
- Workforce anxiety rises when behavior capture coincides with layoff plans or broader pressure to adopt AI at work.
- The practice could spread if large technology companies normalize it or if smaller employers find a market for employee activity data.
