# Community Wildfire Mitigation

## Definition
Community wildfire mitigation is coordinated risk reduction across neighboring properties and public space because vegetation, embers, fences, and burning structures can transmit fire beyond any one homeowner's boundary.

## Current Synthesis
[[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-043ffe0bc2c7516bac64132229755e93-43026861-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-043ffe0bc2c7516bac64132229755e93-43026861]] presents home hardening as necessary but incomplete. Paradise residents organized Firewise groups to clear brush, trim trees, and reduce shared ignition pathways, yet small and inconsistent premium discounts weakened the private incentive for expensive collective work. The stronger case is therefore joint: mitigation can reduce physical loss and improve underwriting evidence, but neither benefit is automatic.

## Key Claims
- Wildfire exposure has neighborhood spillovers that individual construction cannot fully control.
- Collective vegetation and ignition management complements fire-resistant building assemblies.
- Visible standards and coordinated programs can make community risk reduction more legible to insurers.
- Weak, unpredictable discounts create an incentive gap between the social value and household cost of mitigation.
- Insurer site visits can reveal risk reduction that broad geographic models fail to capture.
- Community mitigation reduces risk without eliminating severe-event, affordability, or portfolio-concentration constraints.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- The episode does not measure how much the community programs reduced expected loss or caused later policy offers.
- Reported discounts and expenses are examples rather than representative estimates.
- Collective programs face participation, enforcement, equity, maintenance, and residual-risk problems not resolved by recognition alone.

## What Changed
- Created a collective-action account of wildfire adaptation beyond the individual house.
- Made the mitigation incentive and underwriting-recognition gaps explicit.
