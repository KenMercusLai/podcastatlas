# Iranian Constitutional Revolution

## Definition

The Iranian Constitutional Revolution was the early twentieth-century movement that established constitutional and parliamentary government in Iran, presented by the source as an Iranian reform project strongly influenced by British institutional models.

## Current Synthesis

[[259-iran-england-the-little-satan-glt8347276204]] places the 1906 revolution at the high point of Iranian reformist admiration for British law and parliamentary government. Some constitutionalists called it Britain's “spiritual child,” yet Britain's increasing reluctance to antagonize Russia denied reformers the support they expected and made constitutionalism a central example of disappointed political trust.

## Key Claims

- Iranian constitutionalists borrowed selectively from the British parliamentary model.
- British institutional prestige helped make law, consultation, education, and representative government appear compatible with national strengthening.
- Freemasonry supplied some reformers, including religious figures, with anti-superstition and constitutional networks in the episode's account.
- British strategic accommodation with Russia qualified or undermined the influence created by British ideals.

## Evidence

Institutional borrowing:
- [[259-iran-england-the-little-satan-glt8347276204]] describes the revolution as strongly modeled on British parliamentary government.

Reform networks:
- [[259-iran-england-the-little-satan-glt8347276204]] connects nineteenth-century Iranian Freemasonry with iconoclasm and new political ideas.

Failed expectations:
- [[259-iran-england-the-little-satan-glt8347276204]] says constitutionalists appealed for British backing as Britain became less willing to oppose Russia.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

British influence does not make the revolution a British creation. The source's “spiritual child” phrase is attributed to some leaders, and a full account would require Iranian social, religious, economic, and regional evidence beyond this bilateral frame.
