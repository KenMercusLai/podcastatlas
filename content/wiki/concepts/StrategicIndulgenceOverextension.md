# Strategic Indulgence for Overextension / 姑辅式诱导过度扩张

## Definition
Strategic indulgence for overextension is temporary compliance with an aggressor's demand intended to increase the aggressor's appetite, confidence, carelessness, and number of enemies until coordinated resistance becomes more feasible.

## Current Synthesis
The source develops the pattern through parallel advice. [[DuanGuiWarringStates|Duan Gui]] tells [[HanKangzi|Han Hu]] that refusing [[ZhiYao|Zhi Yao]] means immediate isolated attack, while conceding land encourages him to demand from another house. [[RenZhangWarringStates|Ren Zhang]] then tells [[WeiJu|Wei Ju]] that an unjustified gain will make Zhi Yao more arrogant and the pressured houses more united. The later [[BattleOfJinyang|Battle of Jinyang]] supplies the payoff when Wei and Han reverse against Zhi, but the strategy remains a gamble: early concessions also increase the aggressor's real resources.

## Key Claims
- Tactical compliance can postpone an unfavorable isolated confrontation.
- Easy gains can alter the aggressor's behavior by rewarding escalation and weakening caution.
- Repeated coercion can create a common interest among previously separate targets.
- The strategy requires survival, communication, and a later coordination opportunity; overconfidence alone does not produce defeat.
- Material concession strengthens the aggressor in the short term, so later success should not be mistaken for a universally safe rule.

## Evidence
- Han branch: Duan Gui argues that surrendering one city will redirect Zhi Yao's demand toward others and provoke wider conflict. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-02-jiangyu-baizhi-bi-gufuzhi-lglk2_eu1uvegdac8sj6bfuwv1dw|Zhouji 02]]
- Wei branch: Ren Zhang argues that unjustified gain will increase Zhi Yao's arrogance and unite those he coerces. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-02-jiangyu-baizhi-bi-gufuzhi-lglk2_eu1uvegdac8sj6bfuwv1dw|Zhouji 02]]
- Adversary competence: the same source says Zhi Yao previously used a gift and induced road construction to open Qiu You to conquest, showing that the eventual target also understands give-first manipulation. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-02-jiangyu-baizhi-bi-gufuzhi-lglk2_eu1uvegdac8sj6bfuwv1dw|Zhouji 02]]

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The account is a podcast retelling of transmitted narrative, and the exact advice, city sizes, motives, and Qiu You episode remain source-scoped. Concession may appease rather than overextend an aggressor, leave victims weaker, or fail to create trust among them. This differs from [[IndulgenceAsPoliticalElimination|indulgence as political elimination]]: there, a superior allegedly tolerates a subordinate until punishment becomes legitimate; here, weaker targets feed an external aggressor's escalation so resistance can broaden.

## What Changed
- Created from the paired Han and Wei responses to Zhi Yao's territorial demands.
