# Worldly Political Cultivation / 入世政治修行

Worldly political cultivation / 入世政治修行 is the frame [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-150-chenping-weihe-bei-cheng-zhiwei-chenggong-zhengzhijia-yangban-4-lhhprrkjcuzijgndi2uuazaalern|Hanji 150 part 4]] builds from [[ChenPing|陈平]]'s career. The source argues that Chen Ping's highest achievement is not retreating from dirty politics, but remaining inside the dangerous court, preserving judgment, and acting when action can actually matter.

The concept is deliberately uncomfortable. The host contrasts Chen Ping with figures such as [[FanLi|范蠡]], [[TaoYuanming|陶渊明]], and [[Confucius|孔子]] to argue that withdrawal, literary purity, or scholarly authority do not automatically equal successful politics. In this reading, Chen Ping's "道场" is the court itself: he practices through danger, ambiguity, compromise, concealment, and timing.

This does not turn Chen Ping into a moral saint. The concept sits beside [[CovertPoliticalTechnology|阴谋式政治技术]] and [[PoliticalSurvivalFirst|安全第一政治生存]] because the same worldly practice includes morally gray means and self-protection. Its point is narrower: for this source, worldly politics becomes a discipline only when survival is used to keep later action possible, not merely to protect private comfort.

## Key Claims
- Political cultivation can occur inside compromised institutions rather than only through withdrawal from them.
- A clean public image may be less useful than preserved agency when the field is violent and timing-dependent.
- Entering the world is not the opposite of detachment in this source; serious participation comes before the final letting-go language at the episode's close.
- The frame depends on distinguishing tactical concealment from permanent surrender.
