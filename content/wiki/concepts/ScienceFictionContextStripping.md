## Definition
Science-fiction context stripping is the translation error that occurs when a speculative work's appealing device, interface, or future image is extracted as a product goal while its surrounding critique of dependence, subjectivity, hierarchy, capitalism, colonialism, or social order is discarded.

## Current Synthesis
The source calls the broader phenomenon “错意”: not necessarily malicious misreading, but a loss of meaning when cultural material moves into product, investment, and market language. Science fiction often makes technology the site of a question. Product culture can reverse that structure by treating the technology inside the question as the answer.

The distinction is not between correct literary scholars and illegitimate ordinary readers. Multiple interpretations are normal. The stakes change when a leader can materialize a private interpretation through a widely used product. [[HerFilm|《她》]] is the clearest case: extracting a warm, responsive voice leaves behind Samantha's own development, multiple relationships, refusal of possession, and departure from Theodore's world.

## Key Claims
- Context stripping removes a speculative device from the social and ethical problem that made it meaningful.
- A partial reading can be ordinary at the individual level but consequential when embedded in a mass product.
- Satire and warning are especially vulnerable because their memorable technologies can outlive the criticism around them.
- Restoring context requires asking whose agency, labor, vulnerability, and power disappear when fiction becomes a roadmap.
- The same pattern can reproduce older colonial or salvation narratives even in stories set in technologically advanced futures.

### Historical power carried into futurism
- [[zong-di-074-qi-tamen-ceng-jianguo-weilai-yushi-chuangzaole-xianzai-cmvkvb75u00q001xaevgt4t42]] uses a benevolent higher-dimensional civilization thought experiment to show how technical superiority can preserve colonial assumptions about rescue and civilization.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a podcast's cultural interpretation of secondary writing, not a causal study of product design or an authoritative reading of every cited work. Readers can legitimately emphasize different aspects of the same story, and resemblance between fiction and a product does not prove direct influence. The concept should identify lost context, not prohibit inspiration or presume bad faith.

## What Changed
- Established a framework for distinguishing ordinary selective reading from consequential product translation.
- Made AI subjectivity and colonial-salvation carryover explicit tests for what a product reading leaves out.
