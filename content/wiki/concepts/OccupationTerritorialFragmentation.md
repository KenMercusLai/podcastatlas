# Occupation Territorial Fragmentation

## Definition
Occupation territorial fragmentation is the use of settlements, roads, barriers, administrative zones, permits, and resource control to divide a nominally connected territory into spaces with unequal access and authority.

## Current Synthesis
The source applies the concept to the West Bank, arguing that limited Palestinian self-administration does not equal territorial sovereignty. Israeli settlements and infrastructure can join contiguous Israeli movement while leaving Palestinian communities separated, dependent on checkpoints or permits, and constrained in land and water access.

## Key Claims
- Administrative autonomy can coexist with overriding external territorial control.
- Settlement and road networks alter practical sovereignty before formal borders change.
- Movement restrictions connect spatial control to labor, residence, and economic dependence.
- The bantustan analogy highlights fragmented autonomy but does not establish historical identity between cases.

## Evidence
- Spatial mechanism: [[ep177-ba-yi-chongtu-women-gai-ruhe-lijie-yaoyuan-de-kusheng-ckwriaiepeo1abaaaac8efxz]] describes divided West Bank zones, settlement roads, barriers, and restricted Palestinian mobility.
- Political-economic effect: [[ep177-ba-yi-chongtu-women-gai-ruhe-lijie-yaoyuan-de-kusheng-ckwriaiepeo1abaaaac8efxz]] connects land and water control with a labor market lacking equivalent Israeli protections.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The account partly rests on guest travel observations and a broad South African comparison. It does not map every jurisdiction, road rule, security rationale, or change over time.
