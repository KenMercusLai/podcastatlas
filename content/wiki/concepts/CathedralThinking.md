# Cathedral Thinking

## Definition
Cathedral thinking is the willingness to build, preserve, or model something whose benefits may arrive after the actor's lifetime and without lasting personal credit.

## Current Synthesis
[[create-your-ideal-future-using-science-based-protocols-ari-wallach-scim9541341305]] develops cathedral thinking through a story of planting a carob tree for later people. [[AriWallach]] contrasts this orientation with egoic legacy: a name on a building matters less than behavior, institutions, knowledge, culture, or infrastructure that others can carry forward.

The concept joins the moral imagination of [[TransgenerationalEmpathy]] to the action orientation of [[LongPathFramework]]. It includes large projects, but the episode also locates legacy in ordinary interactions and modeled values, preventing the idea from applying only to monuments or elites.

## Key Claims
- Some worthwhile projects mature beyond the initiator's lifetime.
- Lack of future credit does not eliminate present responsibility or meaning.
- Behavioral and cultural transmission can matter more than named memorials.
- Large institutions and everyday interactions can both carry long-term effects.
- Long-duration work should be judged by beneficiaries and consequences, not scale alone.

## Evidence
- Carob-tree story - [[create-your-ideal-future-using-science-based-protocols-ari-wallach-scim9541341305]] uses planting for another generation as its core image.
- Credit distinction - [[create-your-ideal-future-using-science-based-protocols-ari-wallach-scim9541341305]] contrasts egoic naming with behavioral legacy.
- Everyday transmission - [[create-your-ideal-future-using-science-based-protocols-ari-wallach-scim9541341305]] places intergenerational influence in daily treatment of children, partners, colleagues, and strangers.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Long duration does not make a project beneficial. Cathedral thinking still requires accountability to present people, revisability as knowledge changes, and scrutiny of who bears costs while future beneficiaries are invoked.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to distinguish beneficiary-centered legacy from personal credit.
