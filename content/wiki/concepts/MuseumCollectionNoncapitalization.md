# Museum Collection Noncapitalization

## Definition

Museum collection noncapitalization is the accounting treatment under which collection objects are not assigned financial value on an institution's statement of financial position like cash, investments, receivables, buildings, or inventory.

## Current Synthesis

[[dragon-psychology-101-dragon-psychology-101|Dragon Psychology 101]] presents the treatment through a 1991 accounting dispute and the [[MetropolitanMuseumOfArt|Metropolitan Museum of Art]]'s later financial statements. The Financial Accounting Standards Board considered requiring museums to capitalize art and historical treasures; the Met opposed the proposal, and American art museums were not forced to report collection value in that way.

The episode interprets noncapitalization as more than technical presentation. When no balance-sheet value is attached to a collection, operating assets and cultural holdings occupy different decision languages. That can protect mission assets from simplistic liquidation logic, but it can also make storage cost, opportunity cost, comparative value, and disposal harder to discuss through ordinary financial measures.

## Key Claims

- Noncapitalization keeps collection value outside the main statement of financial position.
- The treatment distinguishes cultural holdings from ordinary assets available for operating use or sale.
- Absence of a reported value does not mean a collection lacks economic, cultural, research, or replacement value.
- Accounting separation can protect public-trust purposes while reducing visibility into resource allocation and opportunity cost.
- Collection governance still requires mission-based review even when financial statements do not capitalize holdings.

## Evidence

### Accounting dispute

- [[dragon-psychology-101-dragon-psychology-101]] describes the 1991 proposal, C. Douglas Dillon's opposition for the Met, and the accountants' argument for asset recognition.

### Financial-statement treatment

- [[dragon-psychology-101-dragon-psychology-101]] reports that the Met's 2019 statements listed ordinary financial assets while excluding collection value from the statement of financial position.

### Governance implication

- [[dragon-psychology-101-dragon-psychology-101]] connects the exclusion to institutional resistance to valuation, sale, and comparative allocation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source does not provide the full accounting standard, its recognition criteria, museum-sector implementation, audit guidance, valuation costs, tax rules, donor restrictions, or international comparison. Balance-sheet recognition would not automatically make a collection liquid or available for operations, and noncapitalization does not prove irrational retention. The episode's claim that the treatment expresses a hoarder's worldview is an interpretation, not an accounting finding.
