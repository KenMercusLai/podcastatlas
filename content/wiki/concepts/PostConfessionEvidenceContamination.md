# Post-Confession Evidence Contamination

## Definition
Post-confession evidence contamination is the process by which learning that a suspect confessed changes how witnesses, alibi providers, informants, investigators, jurors, or forensic examiners remember and interpret the rest of the case.

## Current Synthesis
[[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-7b40091055d1394cc6a3435ffecc95c8-53960014-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-7b40091055d1394cc6a3435ffecc95c8-53960014|Did I Really Do That?]] argues that a confession does not merely enter a fixed evidentiary record. Because people regard self-incrimination as unusually diagnostic, the confession can become an organizing story that weakens contradictory testimony, changes recollection, redirects forensic interpretation, and makes later evidence appear mutually corroborating.

This creates a dependence problem: evidence collected or reinterpreted after a confession may no longer be independent confirmation. Reliability therefore depends on chronology, information flow, preserved original statements, blind or insulated review where feasible, and continued testing of alternative explanations.

## Key Claims
- Confession evidence can reorganize how later facts are perceived rather than simply adding one independent item of proof.
- Witnesses may revise memory or confidence after being told that another person confessed.
- Alibi, informant, and forensic evidence can shift in meaning after investigators adopt a guilt narrative.
- Multiple post-confession errors may look like corroboration even when they share the confession as a common cause.
- DNA exclusion and other contradictory evidence may receive too little weight once a confession anchors judgment.
- Evidence chronology and information-flow records are necessary for judging independence.
- Investigators should continue testing alternative suspects and explanations after obtaining a confession.

## Evidence
- Witness experiment - [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-7b40091055d1394cc6a3435ffecc95c8-53960014-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-7b40091055d1394cc6a3435ffecc95c8-53960014]] reports that many witnesses stopped confirming another person's continuous presence after being told that person had confessed to leaving and stealing money.
- Wrongful-conviction pattern - [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-7b40091055d1394cc6a3435ffecc95c8-53960014-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-7b40091055d1394cc6a3435ffecc95c8-53960014]] reports that most cited Innocence Project false-confession cases also contained at least one additional evidentiary error arising after the confession.
- Central Park case - [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-7b40091055d1394cc6a3435ffecc95c8-53960014-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-7b40091055d1394cc6a3435ffecc95c8-53960014]] shows confession evidence retaining force despite contradictions, unsupported details, and DNA exclusion before another perpetrator's confession and matching DNA changed the case.
- Public-safety consequence - [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-7b40091055d1394cc6a3435ffecc95c8-53960014-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-7b40091055d1394cc6a3435ffecc95c8-53960014]] connects premature closure to failure to pursue actual perpetrators.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not show that every evidentiary change after a confession is contamination or that every convergent item lacks independent value. The experimental effect, reported case percentage, forensic mechanisms, and case chronology require confirmation from the underlying studies and records. Independence must be assessed item by item, and lawful procedures differ across jurisdictions.

## What Changed
- Established confession evidence as a possible common cause of multiple later errors.
- Added chronology, information flow, and alternative-hypothesis testing as independence safeguards.
