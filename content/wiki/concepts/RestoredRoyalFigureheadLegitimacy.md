## Definition

Restored royal figurehead legitimacy / 复国王室名义合法性 is the political mechanism in which a military or rebel coalition elevates a descendant of a remembered royal house because the old lineage carries public legitimacy that the armed organizers do not possess by themselves. The figurehead may begin with little practical command, but the title can mobilize followers, allocate offices, regulate claims, or later become dangerous because others can still rally around it.

## Current Synthesis

The concept began with the anti-Qin Chu restoration. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-130-3-weihe-li-mutong-wei-chuwang-limian-dayou-wenzhang-lmcuvrxer-cou6gmlppqwhb3n4vz|Qinji 130-3]] shows [[FanZeng|范增]] persuading [[XiangLiang|项梁]] to install [[MiXin|芈心 / 楚怀王]] because old Chu royal memory and the grievance around the earlier [[ChuHuaiwang|楚怀王]] can legitimize rebellion more effectively than Xiang Liang's own name. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-132-5wan-wanbao-40wan-xiangyu-cizhan-fengshen-lk87nb3fskg5zh6fcgptypixhzuw|Qinji 132]] adds that this figurehead can become an allocator after the military patron dies: Mi Xin merges armies, grants offices, recognizes Wei Bao, and issues the [[HuaiwangAgreement|怀王之约]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-149-xichu-bawang-xiangyu-weihe-shijun-1-lsqxs2hzu0ngnrpmzsklkqhtyuvw|Hanji 149]] then shows the dangerous afterlife of the same mechanism, because [[XiangYu|项羽]] kills Mi Xin partly to prevent [[LiuBang|刘邦]] or other lords from using him as a political banner.

The Han-restoration variant through [[LiuXuanGengshiEmperor|更始帝刘玄]] makes control part of the mechanism from the start. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-663-gengshidi-liuxuan-shi-tan-fu-bushangqiang-de-lanni-lott3pdjjunwlv3wgksn4r5tiqyz|Hanji 663]] says anti-[[WangMang|王莽]] commanders need a Liu-family royal descendant to align with public sentiment, but New Market and Pinglin commanders prefer Liu Xuan over [[LiuYanLiuXiuBrother|刘縯]] because Liu Yan is more capable and harder to command. This makes the concept explicitly double-edged from the start: a symbolic royal claimant can unify a coalition while also reflecting the coalition's fear of strong leadership.

## Key Claims

- Old royal descent can supply legitimacy when a rebellion wants to present itself as restoration rather than raw seizure.
- The figurehead may be useful precisely because symbolic value and direct command capacity are separated.
- A figurehead can become an allocator when surrounding command hierarchy is disrupted, as Mi Xin does after Xiang Liang's death.
- Residual symbolic authority can become lethal risk after practical power is stripped away, because rivals may reuse the figurehead as a banner.
- Hanji 663 adds that controllability can shape figurehead selection from the beginning, not only after the figurehead loses power.
- The mechanism is distinct from omen fabrication: it depends on remembered lineage, title, and grievance rather than invented signs alone.

## Evidence

- Restoration through old royal memory: [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-130-3-weihe-li-mutong-wei-chuwang-limian-dayou-wenzhang-lmcuvrxer-cou6gmlppqwhb3n4vz|Qinji 130-3]] says Fan Zeng argues that Chen Sheng failed by making himself king instead of restoring a Chu royal descendant, and that Xiang Liang installs Mi Xin as Chu Huai Wang to use old Chu memory.
- Figurehead as active allocator: [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-132-5wan-wanbao-40wan-xiangyu-cizhan-fengshen-lk87nb3fskg5zh6fcgptypixhzuw|Qinji 132]] says Mi Xin moves to Pengcheng, merges armies, grants offices, recognizes Wei Bao, and issues the Guanzhong reward rule.
- Residual banner danger: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-149-xichu-bawang-xiangyu-weihe-shijun-1-lsqxs2hzu0ngnrpmzsklkqhtyuvw|Hanji 149]] says Xiang Yu removes and kills Mi Xin partly because another actor could use him as a political banner.
- Controllable Liu-lineage claimant: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-663-gengshidi-liuxuan-shi-tan-fu-bushangqiang-de-lanni-lott3pdjjunwlv3wgksn4r5tiqyz|Hanji 663]] says anti-Xin commanders seek a Liu-family royal descendant and that New Market and Pinglin commanders choose Liu Xuan over Liu Yan because Liu Xuan is weaker and easier to control.
- Premature-title risk: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-663-gengshidi-liuxuan-shi-tan-fu-bushangqiang-de-lanni-lott3pdjjunwlv3wgksn4r5tiqyz|Hanji 663]] says Liu Yan warns that emperor title before defeating Wang Mang and Chimei could create internal suspicion and weaken the restoration.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept should not imply that every restored claimant is powerless. Qinji 132 shows Mi Xin gaining real allocation authority after Xiang Liang's death. It should also not imply that every figurehead is chosen only for weakness: Qinji 130-3 emphasizes Chu memory and legitimacy more than controllability, while Hanji 663 foregrounds controllability because the Gengshi coalition is already divided over Liu Yan's stronger profile. The sources support a mechanism, not a universal law that restored royal claimants must fail.

This concept also remains distinct from [[RebelMandateManufacture|起义天命制造]]. Chen Sheng, Wang Lang, and other claimants may use rumor, omen, or false identity; restored royal figurehead legitimacy works through an old house whose name still carries public meaning. The two mechanisms can overlap in a rebellion, but they are not the same claim.
