# Farmers Market Urbanism

Farmers market urbanism is the way a recurring public market can reshape food access, neighborhood life, regional agriculture, and restaurant sourcing at once. In [[a-firm-andy-what-are-new-british-pms-plans-6a5deeca76f8ee25bb4bbf9c]], [[Greenmarket]] and [[UnionSquare]] are the source case.

The episode presents the market as more than a shopping amenity. [[BarryBenepe]] and [[BobLewis]] designed a direct link between [[NewYorkCity|New York City]] residents and regional farmers; [[RonBenaghi]]'s family farm shows how city demand can change what farms grow; [[GrowNYC]] turns the network into an operating system with farm vetting, neighborhood expansion, and food-benefit payment infrastructure.

## Key Claims
- A farmers market can be public-space infrastructure when it reliably brings diverse groups into a shared urban routine.
- Direct producer access can help farms survive suburban-sprawl and fuel-cost pressure by creating a city customer base.
- Market governance matters: vendor vetting, benefit payment systems, and neighborhood selection determine who can participate.
- Chef-farmer relationships can turn market access into [[RestaurantSupplyChainLocalization]] and seasonal menu design.
