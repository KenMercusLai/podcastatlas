# Renal Finding Triage / 肾脏发现分层处理

## Definition
Renal finding triage is the assessment boundary for kidney stones, renal masses, ultrasound findings, CT clarification, obstruction, infection, kidney-function impact, and observation versus intervention.

## Current Synthesis
[[vol-31-miniao-waike-guanyu-niaopin-niaoye-huang-jieshi-nanxing-qianliexian-de-yaoyan-ji-wuqu-lpn3fhozej2v-3oijpakvkdx-r-y]] treats kidney findings as neither trivial nor automatically surgical. Kidney stones are influenced by urinary calcium, genetics, hydration, obstruction, infection, and function rather than by a simplistic "calcium supplement equals stone" rule. Small stones that do not obstruct the urinary pathway or harm kidney function may be observed, while stones causing obstruction, infection, pain, urinary symptoms, or functional impact need active care.

The same threshold logic applies to renal masses found during checkups. Ultrasound can discover possible lesions but may not be clear enough; CT can clarify. Malignant-leaning findings usually move toward surgery, while small benign-leaning cysts or angiomyolipoma-like findings can sometimes be monitored when location and size do not affect function.

## Key Claims
- Routine calcium supplementation is not equated with kidney stones, though excessive supplementation can raise risk in some contexts.
- Stone formation depends more on urinary calcium and individual risk than on assuming dietary calcium directly becomes a stone.
- Asymptomatic, small, non-obstructing stones that do not affect kidney function may be observed rather than immediately treated.
- Stones that cause obstruction, infection, urinary pain, frequency, urgency, or kidney-function impact should be handled more actively.
- Ultrasound-detected renal masses may need CT because routine abdominal ultrasound can be insufficiently clear.
- Malignant imaging concern generally changes the threshold toward surgery, while small benign-leaning findings may be dynamically observed.
- Surgical judgment is framed around patient benefit, function, location, size, imaging, and risk rather than a blanket "found it, cut it" rule.

## Evidence
- Kidney-stone risk: [[vol-31-miniao-waike-guanyu-niaopin-niaoye-huang-jieshi-nanxing-qianliexian-de-yaoyan-ji-wuqu-lpn3fhozej2v-3oijpakvkdx-r-y]] explains calcium supplementation, urinary calcium, genetic influence, excessive supplementation, and routine recommended calcium as separate considerations.
- Stone treatment threshold: [[vol-31-miniao-waike-guanyu-niaopin-niaoye-huang-jieshi-nanxing-qianliexian-de-yaoyan-ji-wuqu-lpn3fhozej2v-3oijpakvkdx-r-y]] separates small non-obstructing asymptomatic stones from stones that obstruct, impair kidney function, or trigger infection and urinary symptoms.
- Renal-mass follow-up: [[vol-31-miniao-waike-guanyu-niaopin-niaoye-huang-jieshi-nanxing-qianliexian-de-yaoyan-ji-wuqu-lpn3fhozej2v-3oijpakvkdx-r-y]] describes ultrasound, CT, malignant versus benign leaning, cysts, angiomyolipoma-like examples, dynamic observation, and surgery when clinically favored.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This concept does not determine whether a given stone, cyst, angiomyolipoma, tumor, renal mass, ultrasound result, or CT result should be observed or treated. It is a source-grounded triage pattern, not a nephrology, urology, radiology, oncology, or surgical guideline.
