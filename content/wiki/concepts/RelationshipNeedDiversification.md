# Relationship Need Diversification / 关系需求分散

Relationship need diversification is the episode's practical answer to the false choice between a partner who is exactly similar and a partner who completes every missing part. In [[chuantai-ni-chi-xiangcai-ma-nvshengban-ni-yuanyi-he-xingzhuan-zhihou-de-ziji-zai-yiqi-ma-555771723]], [[ZhiZaoLaoYe|纸造/老爷]] argues that eating, films, sports, conversation, and other needs can be met by different friends rather than all loaded onto a romantic partner.

The source's closing advice, "多谈恋爱，多交朋友", fits this frame. It treats relationships as experiential learning rather than a purely imagined compatibility test, and it keeps romance inside a wider social ecology instead of making one partner responsible for all sameness, novelty, stability, and care.

[[ep236-lao-wu-kuai-qian-xinlihua-fufei-de-peiban-shi-zhende-ma-ltvuoeluoeu4-a3j3lamhc3buudx]] adds a mental-health support version. The episode suggests that many people buy [[PaidListeningServices|paid listening]] because they lack enough "随时可以联系的人" or fear burdening friends with negative emotion. That does not make paid listening wrong, but it shows why a durable [[EmotionRegulationToolkit|emotion-regulation toolkit]] should include several trusted people, embodied practices, and clear boundaries rather than one paid listener, partner, or AI companion.

## Key Claims

- A romantic partner does not need to satisfy every interest, habit, and social need.
- Friendship can reduce pressure on intimacy by distributing companionship and activity across multiple relationships.
- Diversification makes both [[SelfSimilarityInIntimacy]] and [[ComplementaryRelationshipSupport]] less absolute: a partner can be similar on core values and different elsewhere.
- More real relationships provide better data than abstract standards alone.
- Need diversification supports [[RelationshipOptionality]] because a person can choose intimacy without treating singleness or friendship as failure.
- Support diversification prevents emotional overload: friends, family, professionals, paid services, self-regulation practices, and emergency help have different appropriate roles.
