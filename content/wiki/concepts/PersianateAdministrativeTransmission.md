# Persianate Administrative Transmission

## Definition

Persianate administrative transmission is the episode's account of Persian bureaucratic language, habits, and models passing through the [[MughalEmpire]] into British rule in India and then influencing later civil-service practice.

## Current Synthesis

[[41-persia-glt6414068538]] argues that British administrators did not build Indian government on an empty institutional field. They inherited Mughal structures in which Persian was an administrative language and Persianate court-bureaucratic practice supplied established routines.

The source uses Persian study by East India Company and Indian Civil Service personnel, reportedly lasting until 1857, as evidence for institutional transmission. The strongest conclusion is layered inheritance: British administration adapted predecessor systems, not that every feature of the modern civil service was invented in Persia.

## Key Claims

- Mughal administration used Persian language and Persianate governing models.
- British Indian government inherited and adapted existing administrative structures.
- Persian training among British officials is evidence of practical continuity across regime change.
- Administrative vocabulary and habits can outlive the dynasty or empire that developed them.
- The modern-civil-service claim is best framed as partial transmission rather than exclusive origin.

## Evidence

Mughal inheritance:
- [[41-persia-glt6414068538]] says British rule took over administrative machinery from a Mughal system grounded in Persian models and language.

British adaptation:
- [[41-persia-glt6414068538]] reports that officials learned Persian into the nineteenth century and connects inherited attitudes to the Indian Civil Service.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode compresses a long institutional history. It does not separate Mughal, East India Company, Crown, Indian, Islamic, regional, and British contributions in detail, and it does not establish Persia as the sole origin of modern civil service.

## What Changed

- Created a layered-transmission concept connecting Persianate, Mughal, and British Indian administration.
