## Definition
Football squad resilience is a team's capacity to preserve essential functions, tactical options, physical output, and competitive confidence when injuries, suspensions, ageing, opponent adaptation, or fixture pressure remove preferred players and plans.

## Current Synthesis
The [[ChengduRongcheng|成都蓉城]] case distinguishes nominal depth from functional cover. Adding forwards does not replace [[FelipeChengduRongcheng|Felipe]] if none can reproduce his reference-point role; having another winger does not restore [[WeiShihao]]'s left-sided threat; and a deep roster on paper does not help if substitutes and young players lack trusted competitive minutes.

Resilience also depends on tactical and developmental redundancy. When opponents learn the main pattern, a squad must support credible alternatives without abandoning its strengths. An ageing core and disconnected B-team or academy pipeline turn temporary absences into a wider continuity problem.

## Key Claims
- Functional redundancy matters more than having multiple players listed in the same broad position.
- Injury and suspension risk should shape recruitment before decisive matches expose the dependency.
- Tactical resilience requires alternatives that the available squad can actually execute.
- Competitive minutes for substitutes and young players are part of depth creation, not merely emergency use.
- Age concentration and weak academy linkage compound short-term availability problems into succession risk.

## Evidence
- Attacking functions: [[vol-231-chengdu-diuguan-zhushude-yazi-zenme-feile-ft-qinyun-gkwrijioygv7adhjrwt48vcx]] says Felipe's focal role and Wei Shihao's left-side breakthrough were not reproduced when they became unavailable.
- Tactical adaptation: [[vol-231-chengdu-diuguan-zhushude-yazi-zenme-feile-ft-qinyun-gkwrijioygv7adhjrwt48vcx]] reports that opponents increasingly limited a stable playing model while Chengdu's wing progression and midfield control weakened.
- Succession depth: [[vol-231-chengdu-diuguan-zhushude-yazi-zenme-feile-ft-qinyun-gkwrijioygv7adhjrwt48vcx]] connects an ageing first team with B-team relegation and too few trusted young players.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source offers no comparative injury model, roster-cost data, minutes analysis, or tactical event data. Injuries and suspensions can expose weak design without proving that all such losses are preventable. More rotation may also reduce short-term performance, and no reserve structure can guarantee equivalent replacement for a distinctive core player.

## What Changed
- Established functional cover, tactical variation, competitive minutes, and succession as one squad-resilience problem.
- Distinguished roster headcount from the ability to preserve specific match functions.
