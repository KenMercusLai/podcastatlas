# Tenant Screening Eviction Backfire

## Definition
Tenant screening eviction backfire is the claim that strict limits on landlord screening, rent collection, renovation incentives, or eviction can produce second-order harms for tenants through higher upfront rents, weaker maintenance, vacant units, or unmanaged disruption.

## Current Synthesis
The initial source frames the issue through New York housing politics under [[ZohranMamdani|Zohran Mamdani]]. The hosts argue that tenant-protection rules can shift risk from delinquent or disruptive tenants to landlords and other renters; landlords may then respond by raising starting rents, demanding prepayment, reducing upgrades, or leaving units empty. The concept records that argued mechanism without treating the episode as proof of the empirical size of the effects.

## Key Claims
- Screening limits can cause landlords to price risk upfront through higher rents or prepayment requirements.
- Eviction limits can affect other tenants when nonpayment, noise, property damage, or misuse of common areas cannot be resolved.
- Rent freezes and costly renovation rules can reduce incentives to maintain or upgrade housing stock.
- Vacancy can become rational under strict rules if renovation and tenant-risk exposure make renting unattractive.
- Anti-eviction moral framing can hide distributional conflict among renters, not only between tenants and landlords.

## Evidence
- New York policy frame: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-the-fight-over-open-source-ai-anthropics-15b-payout-nyc-socialists-evictions-violence-42209480]] says the discussed policy limits application fees, restricts combined credit and income screening, recognizes tenant unions, and freezes rent for a year.
- Other-tenant harm: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-the-fight-over-open-source-ai-anthropics-15b-payout-nyc-socialists-evictions-violence-42209480]] records Sacks's argument that limiting evictions can harm modest-income tenants affected by nonpayment, noise, poor maintenance, or disorder.
- Landlord response: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-the-fight-over-open-source-ai-anthropics-15b-payout-nyc-socialists-evictions-violence-42209480]] says stricter rules could lead landlords to raise starting rents and require multi-month prepayment.
- Vacancy and renovation: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-the-fight-over-open-source-ai-anthropics-15b-payout-nyc-socialists-evictions-violence-42209480]] cites reports of 50,000 New York ghost apartments and attributes the mechanism to costly renovation rules and low incentives.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a political-economy argument by the hosts, not a housing-economics study. Screening limits, eviction procedures, rent stabilization, tenant unions, maintenance rules, and vacancy taxes may have different effects depending on local enforcement, market tightness, subsidy design, and tenant protections. This concept should track claimed backfire mechanisms alongside empirical housing-supply evidence.
