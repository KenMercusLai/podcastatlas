# Digital Protégé

## Definition
A digital protégé is the source's term for an AI interface designed to represent a named human expert's knowledge, perspective, and decision process while asking for context and escalating cases beyond its learned boundary back to that expert.

## Current Synthesis
The concept combines expert provenance, contextual dialogue, and continued human involvement. Unlike a generic assistant, the user is choosing whose judgment to consult; unlike an autonomous agent, the protégé's represented purpose is advice and interpretation rather than independent task execution. The expert supplies interview answers and materials, remains available for edge cases, and can convert an unfamiliar case into future coverage.

This pattern can widen access to specialized judgment and preserve some institutional knowledge, but it does not eliminate expert selection, disagreement, or accountability. Users still need to assess reputation, specialization, fit, and conflicts. The model also creates incentive and capability risks: professionals may fear replacement or withhold premium knowledge, organizations may mistake elicited material for complete tacit expertise, and learners may consume answers without developing the judgment needed to evaluate them.

## Key Claims
- Named provenance differentiates represented expert judgment from anonymous general-purpose output.
- Contextual questions matter most when the user does not know which facts a professional would consider relevant.
- Uncertainty disclosure and expert escalation are core design requirements, not optional failure handling.
- Continued learning can broaden coverage, but prior resolution of one edge case does not prove general reliability.
- Marketplace access and enterprise retention create different incentives, security needs, and accountability boundaries.
- Multiple expert protégés preserve professional disagreement better when routed as distinct perspectives than when merged into one authoritative voice.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept currently rests on one company's interview claims rather than independent comparison with general-purpose models, live experts, retrieval systems, or conventional training. The source does not define a validated fidelity measure or establish that an hour-long interview plus documents captures tacit judgment. Human escalation narrows but does not remove hallucination, privacy, bias, professional-regulation, liability, impersonation, or response-latency risk. Cheaper access may democratize advice while also commoditizing parts of professional work or weakening entry-level practice.

## What Changed
- Created a bounded concept separating named expert representation from autonomous task execution.
- Made contextual questioning, uncertainty disclosure, and human escalation part of the core definition.
- Preserved disagreement, incentives, tacit-knowledge limits, and accountability as unresolved boundaries.
