# Injury Recovery Adherence

## Definition
Injury recovery adherence is the disciplined alignment of activity, rest, rehabilitation, and return-to-sport progression with qualified clinical guidance rather than with identity, impatience, pain tolerance, or previous success at pushing through injury.

## Current Synthesis
[[harnessing-passion-drive-persistence-for-lifelong-success-tony-hawk-scim8571002701]] supplies a cautionary contrast from [[TonyHawk]]’s broken femur. During the first recovery, Hawk relied on an athlete’s familiar push-through model, resumed skating despite professional advice, and later learned that the fracture had not united and had been forced farther apart. After specialist assessment and a second surgery, he followed a two-month movement restriction, prioritized sleep, diet, schedule, and physical therapy, and then rebuilt skating gradually.

The case reframes discipline. Athletic drive can support rehabilitation only after it is redirected toward the recovery plan; the same persistence becomes harmful when it treats pain tolerance or desire to return as evidence of tissue readiness. Successful return can also be selective: Hawk recovered the personally meaningful 540 while accepting that some other tricks and grabs no longer worked as before.

## Key Claims
- Prior success at pushing through minor injuries does not establish safety after a major fracture or surgery.
- Pain tolerance, motivation, and athletic identity are not substitutes for evidence of tissue healing.
- Adherence can require immobility or reduced activity even when the athlete strongly wants to train.
- Sleep, nutrition, schedule, rehabilitation, and staged relearning can become expressions of discipline rather than interruptions to it.
- Return to sport should be progressive and responsive to changed capacity.
- Recovering a meaningful ability does not require recovering every former movement.

## Evidence
- Failed first recovery - [[harnessing-passion-drive-persistence-for-lifelong-success-tony-hawk-scim8571002701]] records Hawk ignoring advice, feeling the bone move, remaining in denial, and learning that skating had widened a non-union fracture.
- Adherence correction - [[harnessing-passion-drive-persistence-for-lifelong-success-tony-hawk-scim8571002701]] contrasts the second surgery with two months of obeyed movement restriction and greater attention to sleep, diet, and schedule.
- Rehabilitation - [[harnessing-passion-drive-persistence-for-lifelong-success-tony-hawk-scim8571002701]] describes physical-therapy support for difficult post-injury movements and session-by-session relearning.
- Selective return - [[harnessing-passion-drive-persistence-for-lifelong-success-tony-hawk-scim8571002701]] joins Hawk’s return to the 540 with his acceptance that some tricks and grabs had changed permanently.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This is a single retrospective case, not a fracture-healing or return-to-sport protocol. The source does not provide imaging, operative reports, rehabilitation criteria, timelines suitable for others, or the treating clinicians’ accounts. Immobilization, loading, nutrition, physical therapy, and return decisions depend on injury type, surgery, healing, comorbidities, and specialist assessment. The concept should not be used to infer that strict rest is always preferable to active rehabilitation.

## What Changed
- Created an adherence concept that distinguishes recovery discipline from identity-driven premature return to sport.
