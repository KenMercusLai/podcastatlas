# Domain Know-How Moat

## Definition
Domain know-how moat is durable professional advantage built from tacit constraints, system interfaces, standards, failure modes, regulation, company-specific logic, communication, and judgment that a general AI system cannot infer reliably from a task prompt alone.

## Current Synthesis
The bounded sources connect manufacturing, finance, and small-business agent deployment through the same mechanism. Semiconductor and battery work depends on process effects, interfaces, tooling, yield, materials, and cross-team judgment; traditional financial systems depend on regulation, legacy architecture, business rules, enterprise history, and data boundaries; production agents depend on proprietary data, undocumented practices, workflow exceptions, and integration with existing systems. In each case AI can accelerate bounded work without automatically knowing what problem is safe, valuable, or valid.

The current judgment is that the moat is not merely possession of facts. It comes from connecting technical breadth to the upstream and downstream system, making tacit constraints legible, and turning them into tools, workflows, verification criteria, discovery inputs, or training data. HummingAgent's “wiring” claim adds a product version: base models may become substitutable while the company-specific integration layer remains difficult to reproduce. That can make expertise more valuable under AI, but undocumented employer-specific knowledge can also become lock-in rather than portable capital.

## Key Claims
- AI is strongest when the task boundary and success criterion are already clear.
- Undefined engineering or business problems still require people who know interfaces, failure modes, standards, regulation, and tradeoffs.
- Career resilience comes from linking technical skill with domain knowledge, communication, and accountable judgment.
- Domain experts can move from operating tools toward designing, training, and governing domain-specific AI systems.
- Knowledge becomes more portable when firm-specific experience is generalized into commercial, legal, regulatory, and organizational understanding.
- Proprietary context becomes a product moat only when it is encoded into usable workflows, controls, and verification rather than merely accumulated.

## Evidence
- Manufacturing constraints: [[zhenzheng-gaibian-shijie-de-jishu-weishenme-yikaishi-dou-bu-bei-kanhao-s10e16-8c95b3dc-d75a-4bdd-84d4-2c06fd2d85b1]] uses semiconductor and battery examples where small parameters, process behavior, tooling, and cross-team interfaces determine whether a design works.
- Traditional-enterprise constraints: [[8228694742-944685]] says legacy financial systems embody industry standards, technical specifications, business logic, company-specific logic, and regulatory limits that are difficult to provide fully to a general model.
- Career-conversion claim: [[8228694742-944685]] argues that domain professionals should help train and design narrow AI systems rather than remain only routine operators.
- Production-agent product layer: [[ep-51-building-ai-agents-that-actually-work-in-production]] argues that company rules, proprietary data, legacy software, and workflow “wiring” are more defensible than a generic model connector.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Domain knowledge does not make a role or product permanently safe. Enterprises and competitors can document, standardize, retrieve, and train on local knowledge, and some apparently tacit work may become automatable once evaluation improves. The sources are practitioner discussions, not comparative labor or product-moat studies. Broad reading, career breadth, or proprietary data may strengthen judgment and differentiation, but none guarantees portability, employment security, or durable commercial advantage.

## What Changed
- Extended the concept into production-agent workflows where proprietary context and legacy integration form the application layer around replaceable models.
- Added the boundary that accumulated knowledge becomes defensible only when encoded into workflows, controls, and verification.
