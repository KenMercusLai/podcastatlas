# Nonhuman Moral Agency In Strange Tales

Nonhuman moral agency in strange tales is the pattern [[28-liaozhai-zhiyi-buwei-junwang-chang-zange-pianxiang-cangsheng-shuo-guihua-587843930]] draws from [[LiaozhaiZhiyi|《聊斋志异》]]. The source treats snakes, foxes, ghosts, and other beings not as simple monsters, but as agents who can repay kindness, refuse cruelty, seek remembrance, punish greed, protect households, or reveal human moral failure.

The concept is visible across the episode's examples. 二青 the snake recognizes attachment and eventually leaves the performance world; the fox revenge in 《九山王》 works because the human already carries violence and ambition; 青凤 and 红玉 make fox women sources of intelligence, affection, rescue, and chivalric help; 王六郎 the drowned ghost refuses to harm a mother and child and becomes worthy of local-god status.

## Key Claims
- A nonhuman figure can be ethically clearer than a human official, scholar, husband, or opportunist.
- Repayment, mercy, revenge, friendship, and remembrance can move across human and nonhuman worlds.
- The source does not make every fox or ghost benign; moral agency includes danger, punishment, desire, and manipulation.
- Nonhuman agency often reveals a human lack: the person, family, court, or examination system fails to protect or recognize what a ghost or fox can still see.
- The pattern extends strange-tale moral plurality without turning the stories into modern equality parables.
