# Missionary Religion Conversion Dynamics

## Definition

Missionary religion conversion dynamics is the source's model for how a translocal religion of universal salvation can displace rooted local religions by converting rulers, offering new forms of authority and afterlife, building institutions, and selectively adapting older ritual forms.

## Current Synthesis

[[114-stonehenge-ancient-ritual-and-the-origins-of-paganism-glt2588049137]] explains Christianization as more than theological persuasion or simple destruction. Missionary religions arrive with a general message, an active conversion strategy, and political advantages for rulers, while local traditions may lack institutions designed to resist universal conversion.

Victory does not require cultural blankness. Christianity could suppress pagan institutions while reusing basilican form, hangings, incense, altars, images, sacred sites, seasonal observance, sacrifice language, and patron-like holy figures. This is adaptation and redirection, not automatic proof that each Christian form is a disguised continuous pagan cult.

## Key Claims

- Universal salvation and active mission give conversion religions a different expansion logic from locally embedded traditions.
- Ruler conversion can join personal belief, dynastic alliance, enhanced authority, and wider social transformation.
- Indigenous religions may be institutionally vulnerable because they were not organized around resisting conversion.
- Successful Christianization could combine displacement of institutions with absorption and reinterpretation of selected forms.
- Cultural inheritance should be traced case by case rather than inferred from resemblance alone.

## Evidence

- Missionary structure: [[114-stonehenge-ancient-ritual-and-the-origins-of-paganism-glt2588049137]] places Christianity among world religions with a general salvation message.
- Elite conversion: [[114-stonehenge-ancient-ritual-and-the-origins-of-paganism-glt2588049137]] emphasizes ruler conversion, authority, afterlife, and the Lithuanian marriage-alliance case.
- Institutional asymmetry: [[114-stonehenge-ancient-ritual-and-the-origins-of-paganism-glt2588049137]] argues that local religions often lacked established defenses against missionary competition.
- Adaptation: [[114-stonehenge-ancient-ritual-and-the-origins-of-paganism-glt2588049137]] identifies architectural, sensory, seasonal, sacrificial, and patronal forms that Christianity could redirect.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The model is a broad explanation from one podcast and cannot account by itself for coercion, popular agency, theology, law, economic incentives, institutional capacity, military conquest, regional chronology, or conversion from below. Rulers did not always control religious change, and similarity between pagan and Christian practice does not establish direct borrowing. Harran and Lithuania show unusually late survival, not one universal transition sequence.

## What Changed

- Established a combined political, missionary, and adaptive model of Christianization while preserving case-specific causal limits.
