## Definition

Public Reason / Private Motive Mismatch / 公开理由与私下动机错位 is the pattern where a ruler or organization gives a formal reason for reward or punishment, while the effective cause lies in a separate gratitude debt, factional need, interest conflict, or face-saving calculation.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 541 defines the pattern through the Changling reversal. [[ChenTangWesternHan|陈汤]] is formally attacked for disrespectful speech and suspicious conduct, but the host reads the deeper pressure as the backlash from his proposal to move Guandong powerful households to Changling. The public charge matters, especially because Chen Tang has a reputation for greed, but it does not exhaust why he becomes removable.

The positive reward side is even clearer. [[HanChengdi|汉成帝]] publicly rewards Wang Hong and [[ChunyuZhangWesternHan|淳于长]] for proposing to stop Changling palace-city works, while the host argues that Chunyu's real advantage is his earlier help in making [[ZhaoFeiyanWesternHan|赵飞燕]] empress. [[PingDangWesternHan|平当]] is punished because he evaluates the formal merit standard and misses the private gratitude signal.

## Key Claims

- Formal reasons are still politically useful even when they are not the whole cause.
- A visible charge works better when the target already has a plausible weakness, as Chen Tang's greed reputation does in Hanji 541.
- Rewards can be attached to public merit while actually repaying earlier private service.
- Misreading the private motive can be dangerous for officials who answer only the formal standard.
- The pattern requires evidence of mismatch; it should not turn every official explanation into a conspiracy by default.

## Evidence

- Punishment mismatch: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-541-ta-shi-minzu-yingxiong-zuihou-weihe-yin-tan-ruyu-llcktocpmxxib1pjckboldlzgxdu|Hanji 541]] says Wang Shang impeaches Chen Tang for words and reporting, but the host connects Chen's fall to Changling relocation interests.
- Plausible weakness: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-541-ta-shi-minzu-yingxiong-zuihou-weihe-yin-tan-ruyu-llcktocpmxxib1pjckboldlzgxdu|Hanji 541]] says Chen Tang's loose handling of money makes corruption suspicion easier to use, while the host explicitly does not prove a specific illicit gain.
- Reward mismatch: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-541-ta-shi-minzu-yingxiong-zuihou-weihe-yin-tan-ruyu-llcktocpmxxib1pjckboldlzgxdu|Hanji 541]] says Chengdi uses the Changling-cancellation proposal to justify rewarding Chunyu Zhang.
- Hidden gratitude: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-541-ta-shi-minzu-yingxiong-zuihou-weihe-yin-tan-ruyu-llcktocpmxxib1pjckboldlzgxdu|Hanji 541]] says the host treats Chunyu Zhang's help with Zhao Feiyan's elevation as the real reward cause.
- Misread standard: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-541-ta-shi-minzu-yingxiong-zuihou-weihe-yin-tan-ruyu-llcktocpmxxib1pjckboldlzgxdu|Hanji 541]] says Ping Dang objects from the formal enfeoffment standard and is quickly demoted.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept should stay evidentiary. Hanji 541 gives a host interpretation of hidden causes, not direct access to every actor's inner motive. It also keeps Chen Tang's corruption question qualified: the episode says his habits made accusation easier but does not establish the exact profit or proof. Public reasons can be partial rather than false; the point is mismatch between stated justification and operative political cause.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Hanji 541's repeated contrast between formal reward/punishment reasons and underlying interest or gratitude motives.
