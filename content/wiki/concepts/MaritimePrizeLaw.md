# Maritime Prize Law

## Definition

Maritime prize law is the wartime legal framework for deciding whether captured ships and cargo should be condemned and sold, returned, or otherwise disposed of.

## Current Synthesis

Historically, prize law converted maritime capture into adjudicated property disposition. Commissioned privateers and regular naval forces brought vessels, papers, testimony, and other evidence before courts; lawful prizes could be auctioned and proceeds distributed, while improper capture could be penalized. The modern proposal discussed by [[PlanetMoney]] concerns prize courts for regular military seizures, not renewed civilian privateering.

The appeal is speed and lower custody cost, especially for oil cargo. Yet the mechanism is not ready-made: procedures have had to be reconstructed, claimant protections and evidence rules remain consequential, and [[PrizeLawWarThreshold|the existence of legally sufficient war]] remains unresolved.

## Key Claims

- Prize law adjudicates maritime capture rather than making seizure itself proof of lawful ownership.
- Historical procedure used ship papers, crew questioning, and standardized judicial inquiry to classify a claimed prize.
- State commissions distinguished privateering from unauthorized piracy and exposed captors to penalties for improper seizure.
- Modern officials seek the court mechanism for regular Navy captures, not a civilian “bonus navy.”
- Early sale of crude oil may reduce custody and hazard costs without necessarily resolving final entitlement to proceeds.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Historical speed does not prove that a modern prize case will be fast, lawful, or less violent. Lost institutional custom, complex ownership, sanctions, victim claims, due process, appellate review, and dispute over the statutory meaning of war can recreate delay. Historical prize commerce also included enslaved people, so administrative efficiency cannot be separated from the institution's coercive record.

## What Changed

- Established a modern synthesis separating prize adjudication from both piracy and renewed privateering.
- Added early cargo sale as a cost-control mechanism whose proceeds can remain contested.
