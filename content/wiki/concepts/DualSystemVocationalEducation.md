# Dual-System Vocational Education / 双元制职业教育

Dual-system vocational education / 双元制职业教育 is the deeper [[SchoolEnterpriseCooperation|校企合作]] model in which school and enterprise both become formal training sites. In [[ep241-xiaoqi-hezuo-shi-xinyidai-de-tiefanwan-ma-lnmpsbvoqbexeardxyx88hywufig]], [[Bosch|博世]] represents the model through a China training program that splits student time between school and an enterprise training center.

The episode stresses the model's cost and management burden. It requires curriculum coordination, credit transfer, education-department filing, student status rules, workplace discipline, equipment, trainers, and exit paths for students who leave the track. That makes it much heavier than ordinary lectures, internships, or a branded class.

## Key Claims
- Students occupy two roles: school students and enterprise apprentices or quasi-employees.
- Enterprise training can teach process discipline, workplace cleanliness, materials handling, and production standards that classrooms struggle to simulate.
- Dual-system programs can generate transferable skills even when the enterprise curriculum starts from one company's standards.
- High fixed costs and long payback make the model fragile if the company changes ownership, the industry cools, or the school shifts priorities.
- The model is not a guarantee of employment unless student interest, enterprise demand, and retention mechanisms all hold.
