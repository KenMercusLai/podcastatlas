# Castrati

## Definition

Castrati were male singers castrated before puberty to preserve a high vocal range, produced by the interaction of musical demand, family decisions, surgical harm, intensive training, church and court institutions, and highly unequal career outcomes.

## Current Synthesis

[[45-top-ten-eunuchs-glt5221212176]] uses [[Farinelli]] to show both the attraction and distortion of celebrity evidence. A successful castrato could become an international star, command remarkable fees, and perform for leading European courts. Those visible rewards encouraged poor families to treat surgery as an investment in a child's voice.

The labor market was far broader than its stars. The episode claims that thousands of boys were castrated annually in eighteenth-century Italy, while only a small share could become Farinellis. The institution's decline into the recording era and papal prohibition show changing musical and moral boundaries, but exact chronology and “last castrato” labels require careful sourcing.

## Key Claims

- The castrato voice was institutionally produced through childhood surgery rather than merely discovered as natural talent.
- Family poverty and expected earnings could turn bodily harm into a speculative investment decision made for children.
- Celebrity rewards were highly concentrated, making famous careers misleading evidence about typical outcomes.
- Courts, opera, and church music sustained demand even where formal or moral disapproval existed.
- The institution's disappearance reflects changing ethical and musical systems, not simply loss of vocal taste.

## Evidence

- Star outcome - [[45-top-ten-eunuchs-glt5221212176]] traces Farinelli's fame, European travel, court performances, and high earnings.
- Family and labor-market logic - [[45-top-ten-eunuchs-glt5221212176]] describes poor families authorizing surgery in hope of a lucrative singing career.
- Institutional ending - [[45-top-ten-eunuchs-glt5221212176]] places castrati into the recording era and associates papal prohibition with the practice's final decline.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode's annual totals, income claims, surgery conditions, papal date, and identification of the last castrato remain source-scoped. Family economic pressure did not produce uniform motives or consent, and Farinelli's career cannot supply a representative survival or success rate.

## What Changed

- Created a labor-system synthesis that places Farinelli's success within childhood harm and unequal selection.
