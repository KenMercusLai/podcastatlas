# 宫廷后门自救 / Court Backchannel Survival

Court backchannel survival / 宫廷后门自救 is the survival pattern [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-53-qinhuiwang-yong-zhangyi-huan-dipi-lvmo2cwbafcg2gnxliuo18fewimk]] draws from [[ZhangYiStrategist|张仪]]'s entry into hostile [[ChuState|楚国]]. Zhang Yi does not rely first on formal debate before [[ChuHuaiwang|楚怀王]]; he relies on an already cultivated court relationship that can reach [[ZhengXiu|郑袖]].

The pattern works by converting one person's state danger into another person's private risk. A Chu favorite warns Zheng Xiu that [[QinState|秦国]] may redeem Zhang Yi with [[Shangyong|上庸]]'s six counties and a beautiful woman. Zheng Xiu's fear for her own position then becomes a repeated appeal to Chu Huaiwang to spare Zhang Yi.

This is not the same as healthy counsel. It can save a life and avert envoy-killing risk, but it does so through status anxiety, palace access, and selective persuasion rather than open deliberation. The concept therefore sits next to [[CourtFeedbackCollapse|君臣反馈失灵]]: informal influence can bypass bad formal advice channels, but it can also be manipulated for private reasons.

## Key Claims
- Survival in a hostile court may depend on relationships built before the crisis, not only on the public speech given during it.
- Backchannels work best when they translate geopolitical danger into the immediate incentives of someone close to the ruler.
- Palace influence can produce a strategically useful outcome without becoming principled or reliable feedback.
- Zhang Yi's case shows that zonghengjia craft includes social infrastructure, not just eloquence.
