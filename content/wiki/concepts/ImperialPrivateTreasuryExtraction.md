# 帝王私库进奉盘剥 / Imperial Private Treasury Extraction

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-914-xinren-de-ren-weihe-zong-shang-ni-zui-shen-lvwit-rzrkscn5a3h6wblntxppso|Hanji 914]] adds an earlier Xidi office-sale branch before the 181 CE private-store remonstrance. [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]] sells offices through Xidi and stores the proceeds in the West Garden private treasury, so the imperial private store is funded by monetizing public appointment power rather than only by demanding tribute goods.

This branch sharpens the public/private problem. The revenue is private to the ruler, but the costs are public: buyers who pay cash or accept double repayment pressure have reason to extract from the people after taking office. The source therefore connects private treasury extraction to [[OfficeSaleLegitimacyDamage|买官名节损耗]] and [[CourtOfficeCapture|朝廷开府任官失序]] before later sources show more direct tribute and palace-fee demands.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-920-mingwu-zhege-daoli-caineng-zhuan-daqian-lqmabg3bpyz6ct2v4arx-iun57ws|Hanji 920]] supplies the concept's prelude. The source closes by explaining the formal separation between the public state treasury and the emperor's private "small treasury", then warns that emperors repeatedly try to move public wealth into private stores. [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]]'s palace shops and [[PalaceMarketTheater|宫市游乐式权力错位]] therefore set up the sharper private-store extraction branch in the next episode.

The detailed late-Han case enters through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-921-jinqian-ru-shui-que-ze-ke-si-tan-ze-yan-si-lj1dk4qdgcago9elr7ctuvre3zic|Hanji 921]] as a pattern in which Emperor Ling treats the empire as a source of private palace wealth. The episode says he accumulates personal stores and asks commanderies and kingdoms to send additional fine goods to the office managing imperial private property.

[[LyuQiangLateHan|吕强]] supplies the concept's clearest diagnosis. If all wealth in the realm already belongs to the emperor in public terms, then dividing imperial wealth into public and private stores does not create real legitimacy; it creates an extra extraction channel. Local officials then search for treasures, requisition carriages and horses, add charges, and impose labor burdens that fall on common households.

The concept differs from [[PalaceConstructionFeeExtraction|修宫钱转嫁盘剥]]. Hanji 932 focuses on palace-repair, statue, and office-entry charges; Hanji 921 shows an earlier private-store demand where the immediate driver is imperial possession itself. Both patterns connect private appetite to public impoverishment, but this page marks the public/private treasury logic specifically.

## Key Claims
- Public rulership language can be hollowed out when the ruler treats public resources as a separate private store to be replenished.
- Hanji 914 adds that private-store extraction can run through appointment monetization: public offices are sold, private cash is collected, and local people face the recoupment burden.
- Extra tribute demands rarely stay at the palace level; they become local requisitions, carriage and horse burdens, fees, and labor pressure.
- A remonstrance can identify the extraction clearly without changing policy when the ruler's desire and palace channel remain intact.
- Private accumulation worsens [[UntimelyExtravagance|时绌举赢]] when it appears beside social hardship and institutional weakening.
- Hanji 920 adds that the private-treasury problem begins as role confusion before formal requisition: the ruler who should govern public order plays at commerce and anticipates diverting public wealth into private stores.
