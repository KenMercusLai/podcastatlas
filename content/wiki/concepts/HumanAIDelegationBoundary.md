# Human–AI Delegation Boundary

## Definition
The human–AI delegation boundary separates work that may be safely assigned to AI from judgment, responsibility, creativity, care, and relationship-defining activity that people deliberately retain.

## Current Synthesis
The source proposes a values-based task inventory: identify administrative and repetitive work that does not deepen human capability or connection, then distinguish responsibilities that should not be surrendered. AI can organize information, automate follow-up, compare factors, and support strategy, while people retain quality assurance, leadership, final decisions, speeches or events they regard as authored work, and intimate relationships.

The boundary is not fixed by technical capability alone. A system may be able to simulate conversation or recommend an option without becoming the appropriate bearer of love, consequence, or accountability. Saved time counts as successful delegation only when it is reinvested in meaningful work, family, creativity, or rest rather than converted automatically into more low-value activity.

## Key Claims
- Delegation should begin with the purpose and stakes of a task, not with AI capability alone.
- Repetitive administration and information organization are stronger delegation candidates than intimate or irreversible decisions.
- Human review and quality assurance remain necessary for agent-assisted output.
- Leadership, communication, and coordination may become more valuable as people manage both humans and agents.
- Simulated intimacy is not equivalent to reciprocal human relationship.
- Reclaimed time is an outcome measure alongside revenue or throughput.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source offers a normative framework, not a universal task taxonomy. Accessibility needs, cultural expectations, risk tolerance, and the quality of human alternatives can move particular boundaries. Human retention does not guarantee competent or ethical judgment, while AI assistance can sometimes increase access to care or communication; the relevant question is whether assistance supports human relationship and responsibility or substitutes for them without informed consent.

## What Changed
- Established a purpose- and stakes-based boundary between useful delegation and human substitution.
- Added reclaimed time and preserved relationship quality as outcomes of successful automation.
