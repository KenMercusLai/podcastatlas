# Contrast Media Safety and Consent / 造影剂安全与知情同意

## Definition
Contrast media safety and consent is the framework for explaining why an imaging contrast agent is used, distinguishing it from radioactive tracers, screening relevant risk, recognizing adverse reactions, and pairing informed agreement with active clinical preparedness.

## Current Synthesis
[[vol-01-yingxiangke-yixue-yingxiangke-li-fushe-chulai-de-naxie-shier-lgxelt6usztrpyoamymmk-v46-v8u|VOL.01]] describes enhanced CT as adding an injected agent so clinicians can observe structures and patterns of blood supply more clearly. [[vol-225-qu-yiyuan-paipianzi-meiren-gaosu-ni-de-jijianshi-yingxiangke-gei-ni-zhuge-jiangmingbai-lhhurs_v1d-vp6rr4eycunr0zmhs|VOL.225]] generalizes the purpose: non-contrast imaging relies on existing tissue differences, while an enhancement agent can make selected normal and abnormal structures more distinguishable. Ordinary CT and MRI contrast are not PET radiotracers, and the distinction prevents a common misunderstanding that every injection used in imaging makes the patient radioactive.

The sources also separate routine intravenous enhancement from catheter angiography. Both can use injected agents to create diagnostically useful differences, but route, invasiveness, equipment, staffing, and procedure risk are not interchangeable. Adverse-reaction risk remains real but not reducible to a simple skin-test result. The safety system includes appropriate indication, history and risk screening, explanation, informed consent, monitoring, and capacity to respond if a reaction occurs. Consent documents uncertainty and participation; it does not transfer the clinical team's duty to prevent, recognize, and manage harm.

## Key Claims
- Contrast enhancement is used to answer a clinical question that non-contrast imaging may not answer as clearly.
- Ordinary CT contrast and PET radiotracers differ in mechanism, radiation implications, and post-examination precautions.
- A negative or omitted skin test is not a universal guarantee that an adverse reaction cannot occur.
- Reaction risk should be managed through screening, agent and protocol choice, monitoring, and response readiness.
- Informed consent does not waive professional responsibility or mean that the patient will be abandoned if a complication develops.
- Routine intravenous enhancement and catheter angiography may share contrast-making goals while remaining different procedures with different routes and risk profiles.

## Evidence
- Imaging purpose: [[vol-01-yingxiangke-yixue-yingxiangke-li-fushe-chulai-de-naxie-shier-lgxelt6usztrpyoamymmk-v46-v8u|VOL.01]] explains enhanced CT through the added visibility of internal structure and contrast flow.
- Tracer distinction: [[vol-01-yingxiangke-yixue-yingxiangke-li-fushe-chulai-de-naxie-shier-lgxelt6usztrpyoamymmk-v46-v8u|VOL.01]] distinguishes CT contrast from a PET-CT radiopharmaceutical that temporarily makes the body a radiation source.
- Testing limitation: [[vol-01-yingxiangke-yixue-yingxiangke-li-fushe-chulai-de-naxie-shier-lgxelt6usztrpyoamymmk-v46-v8u|VOL.01]] says routine skin testing was not used in the described workflow because the test did not reliably reproduce the actual injection exposure.
- Consent and response: [[vol-01-yingxiangke-yixue-yingxiangke-li-fushe-chulai-de-naxie-shier-lgxelt6usztrpyoamymmk-v46-v8u|VOL.01]] pairs disclosure of uncommon serious reactions with departmental readiness to intervene.
- Enhancement purpose and procedure boundary: [[vol-225-qu-yiyuan-paipianzi-meiren-gaosu-ni-de-jijianshi-yingxiangke-gei-ni-zhuge-jiangmingbai-lhhurs_v1d-vp6rr4eycunr0zmhs|VOL.225]] distinguishes native tissue contrast, injected enhancement, routine intravenous studies, catheter angiography, and nuclear-medicine radiopharmaceuticals.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources' statements about skin testing, agent type, impurity, reaction frequency, clearance timing, and post-procedure management are public-education accounts, not universal or current protocols. Contrast choice and precautions depend on the modality, agent, kidney function, prior reactions, other medical history, emergency context, and local guidance. Reported 6–8-hour concentration change or 24–48-hour clearance language should not be used as an individual prediction. This page does not supply individual premedication, testing, hydration, or emergency instructions.

## What Changed
- Expanded the framework from enhanced CT to the shared purpose and distinct procedures of CT enhancement, MRI enhancement, and catheter angiography.
- Added an explicit boundary against treating conversational clearance timing as an individual metabolic prediction.
