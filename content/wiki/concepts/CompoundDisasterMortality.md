# Compound Disaster Mortality

## Definition

Compound disaster mortality is the cumulative loss produced when an initial hazard interacts with secondary hazards, exposure, food insecurity, disease, damaged shelter, and response failures across time.

## Current Synthesis

The Huaxian-Lisbon comparison shows why a single death total can hide different causal chains. Huaxian joins collapse to winter exposure, drought, hunger, aftershocks, and epidemic; Lisbon joins shaking to tsunami, fire, dense urban occupancy, and emergency control. The Japan account extends the framework into current preparedness: stress, disrupted care, poor food, heat, and prolonged evacuation can kill after the initial event, with older residents especially exposed. Mortality totals therefore need a time boundary, geographic boundary, and distinction between direct, secondary, and later deaths.

## Key Claims

- Hazard magnitude alone does not determine mortality.
- Housing, settlement, season, time of day, and population concentration shape immediate exposure.
- Fire, tsunami, cold, hunger, disease, and homelessness extend mortality beyond the first shock.
- Administrative speed, information quality, relief usability, and public-health measures can interrupt or deepen the chain.
- Famous historical totals should not be treated as self-explanatory measurements.
- Age, chronic illness, care continuity, shelter food, cooling, and evacuation duration shape post-disaster mortality.

- Lisbon chain: [[43-dongxifang-de-dizhen-1556nian-jiajing-dadizhen-vs-1755nian-lisiben-dadizhen-lqz5aznr86rd4a8xawybzotkkaps]] combines earthquake, tsunami, candles, fire, waterfront movement, and urban density.
- Counting boundary: [[43-dongxifang-de-dizhen-1556nian-jiajing-dadizhen-vs-1755nian-lisiben-dadizhen-lqz5aznr86rd4a8xawybzotkkaps]] presents Huaxian's 830,000 as a debated cumulative figure rather than a secure immediate count.
- Contemporary indirect deaths: [[in-the-life-of-prime-ais-latest-maths-triumph-0543079e679f77fa9c07ce929a73b21f]] says prolonged stress, interrupted health, poor food, heat, and evacuation conditions increasingly account for Japanese disaster deaths and disproportionately affect older people.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Compound explanation does not license assigning an exact share to each cause. The sources supply comparative and institutional frameworks, not validated demographic decompositions; their totals, ranges, historical counts, recent mortality shares, and causal weights remain source-scoped. The Japanese category of “disaster-related deaths” may also depend on administrative recognition and counting rules that the episode summary does not provide.

## What Changed

- Extended the concept from historical earthquake chains to contemporary post-evacuation mortality in Japan.
- Added ageing, chronic-care continuity, food, cooling, and shelter duration as explicit vulnerability channels.
