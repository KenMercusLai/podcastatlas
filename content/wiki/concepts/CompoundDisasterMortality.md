# Compound Disaster Mortality

## Definition

Compound disaster mortality is the cumulative loss produced when an initial hazard interacts with secondary hazards, exposure, food insecurity, disease, damaged shelter, and response failures across time.

## Current Synthesis

The Huaxian-Lisbon comparison shows why a single death total can hide different causal chains. Huaxian joins collapse to winter exposure, drought, hunger, aftershocks, and epidemic; Lisbon joins shaking to tsunami, fire, dense urban occupancy, and emergency control. Historical totals therefore need a time boundary, geographic boundary, and distinction between direct, secondary, and later deaths.

## Key Claims

- Hazard magnitude alone does not determine mortality.
- Housing, settlement, season, time of day, and population concentration shape immediate exposure.
- Fire, tsunami, cold, hunger, disease, and homelessness extend mortality beyond the first shock.
- Administrative speed, information quality, relief usability, and public-health measures can interrupt or deepen the chain.
- Famous historical totals should not be treated as self-explanatory measurements.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Compound explanation does not license assigning an exact share to each cause. The episode supplies a comparative framework, not a validated demographic decomposition; its totals, ranges, and causal weights remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Added the concept through two historical earthquake mortality chains.
- Made time, geography, and direct-versus-secondary death boundaries explicit.
