# Conflict Reporting Access Bias / 冲突报道准入偏差

## Definition
Conflict reporting access bias is the systematic distortion created when language, class, politics, intermediaries, surveillance, visas, transport, and personal safety determine whom a reporter can encounter and who is willing to speak.

## Current Synthesis
The source rejects both remote omniscience and on-scene omniscience. Reporting only in English in Iran can overrepresent educated, reform-minded, or outward-facing interviewees, while translators and fixers add their own networks and judgments. Yet local-language fluency does not remove class, faction, or social-circle boundaries.

Field presence can broaden the sample through streets, shops, neighborhoods, and public space. It therefore improves the opportunity to observe and encounter; it does not by itself convert a selected sample into a complete or objective account.

## Key Claims
- Language affects nuance and determines which groups are easiest to interview.
- Translators, fixers, and local contacts expand access while also filtering it.
- Class, political identity, social network, and surveillance remain selection mechanisms even when language barriers are low.
- Physical presence can widen the sample through ordinary environments and unplanned encounters.
- Reporters should describe access conditions and evidence limits rather than present a partial sample as the whole society.

## Evidence
- Language and sample - [[lkuvt7driaody6cy5pq-bj517ord-lkuvt7driaody6cy5pq-bj517ord]] says English-only interviews in Iran tend to reach educated, liberal, reformist, or foreigner-friendly participants.
- Intermediated access - [[lkuvt7driaody6cy5pq-bj517ord-lkuvt7driaody6cy5pq-bj517ord]] emphasizes translators, fixers, and local relationships when reporters cannot master every relevant language.
- Persistent selection - [[lkuvt7driaody6cy5pq-bj517ord-lkuvt7driaody6cy5pq-bj517ord]] notes that fluency still leaves reporters bounded by class, politics, and social circles, while streets and public spaces can broaden observation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Remote reporting can reach local experts, creators, and witnesses that a short field visit misses, while a reporter on the ground may be tightly monitored or guided. The concept therefore supports explicit sampling humility, not a universal hierarchy in which field reporting is always superior.
