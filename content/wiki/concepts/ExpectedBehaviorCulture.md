# Expected-Behavior Culture

## Definition

Expected-behavior culture is [[DannyMeyer]]'s model of culture as the conduct an organization wants, celebrates, and holds people responsible for, reduced by the unwanted conduct management tolerates.

## Current Synthesis

The model makes culture operational by replacing family language and abstract values with observable expectations. It also assigns managers responsibility for enforcement and working conditions: customers can often see culture indirectly in whether employees perform well, enjoy one another, and sustain opening-day care after novelty fades.

## Key Claims
- Every organization has a culture whether leaders define it intentionally or allow it to emerge.
- A business-as-family metaphor can blur accountability because dismissal or correction feels like personal rejection.
- Expected behaviors are useful only when they are understood, celebrated, and enforced consistently.
- Tolerated unwanted behavior is part of the real culture even when written values reject it.
- Customer-facing warmth depends partly on management creating a healthy employee environment.
- A day-one mentality can make mature teams re-examine normalized declines in pride and detail.

## Evidence
- Definition and accountability: [[advice-line-with-danny-meyer-of-shake-shack-c7199e93-5dba-43ab-8fb0-91403917c3ac]] gives Meyer's "how we do things around here" definition and wanted-minus-tolerated behavior formulation.
- Family-metaphor boundary: [[advice-line-with-danny-meyer-of-shake-shack-c7199e93-5dba-43ab-8fb0-91403917c3ac]] explains why Meyer moved away from "family values" language.
- Customer-visible culture: [[advice-line-with-danny-meyer-of-shake-shack-c7199e93-5dba-43ab-8fb0-91403917c3ac]] says competence without human warmth is insufficient and makes management primarily responsible for the climate.
- Renewal practice: [[advice-line-with-danny-meyer-of-shake-shack-c7199e93-5dba-43ab-8fb0-91403917c3ac]] describes the day-one mentality used by Union Square Hospitality Group.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The framework is a leadership model, not independent evidence that stated behavior systems reliably change outcomes. Clear expectations can also become coercive or cosmetic if leaders apply them inconsistently, ignore power differences, or celebrate warmth while under-resourcing the work.

## What Changed
- Added a culture model that links written expectations, management tolerance, accountability, and customer-visible employee behavior.
