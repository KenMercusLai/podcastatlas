# Post-Success Arrogance / 得势后的骄傲

## Definition
Post-success arrogance / 得势后的骄傲 is the failure pattern where victory, office, inherited status, or visible resource advantage makes an actor less able to hear correction, preserve proportion, protect relationships, or read changed conditions.

## Current Synthesis
The concept began in the Dong Zhuo aftermath and now extends into the Yuan Shu/Yuan Shao pre-Guandu arc. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-963-shishang-bei-diantian-deng-de-diyiren-shi-ta-lkpkhlpkv3xpw-hur4b7sh-3druk|Hanji 963]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-964-bie-jiaoao-bie-jiaoao-bie-jiaoao-lo2-edzffu0v9ixoabc06rx-oqoq|Hanji 964]] show [[WangYunLateHan|王允]] turning anti-[[DongZhuo|董卓]] success into reputation anxiety and refusal to spare [[CaiYong|蔡邕]]. Hanji 964 also shows [[LiuDaiLateHan|刘岱]] letting status and face override [[BaoXin|鲍信]]'s material advice against the [[QingzhouYellowTurbans|青州黄巾军]], while [[LyuBu|吕布]] turns real merit into public boasting. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-965-bangguo-bieren-de-mang-weishenme-yao-shao-ti-ltjd-cv59hnm-n8ar9jxdogp9q0q|Hanji 965]] then shows Wang Yun's post-victory posture damaging safety design for the [[LiangzhouWarlordClique|凉州系军阀]] remnants.

At larger political scale, the same pattern becomes final-regime collapse and prewar overconfidence in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1033-geju-junfa-yuanshu-zuizhong-tuxue-er-wang-lt0ibs-en0oeoqkkcxl8sevjng4|Hanji 1033]]. [[YuanShu|袁术]]'s imperial performance and luxury separate him from hungry soldiers and exhausted resources until old retainers and soldiers no longer hold him up. [[YuanShao|袁绍]], after defeating [[GongsunZan|公孙瓒]], also becomes less responsive to court obligations and warning: he is tempted by [[GengBaoLateHan|耿包]]'s imperial-title proposal and then discounts [[JuShou|沮授]] and [[CuiYanLateHan|崔琰]] because force, grain, and family prestige make immediate victory over [[CaoCao|曹操]] seem obvious.

## Key Claims
- Success is a character and information test because it changes how correction feels to the successful actor.
- Reputation management can become destructive when a new victor fears criticism more than institutional repair.
- Status confidence can hide material weakness, including food limits, exhausted populations, and supply pressure.
- Merit or force becomes corrosive when converted into entitlement, boasting, title ambition, or refusal to hear constraint.
- Post-success arrogance is not only personal vanity; it can break refuge networks, alliance design, adviser use, and command unity.
- Hanji 1033 extends the concept from court aftermath and personal status to final regime collapse and prewar strategic misjudgment.

## Evidence
- Reputation panic after victory: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-963-shishang-bei-diantian-deng-de-diyiren-shi-ta-lkpkhlpkv3xpw-hur4b7sh-3druk|Hanji 963]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-964-bie-jiaoao-bie-jiaoao-bie-jiaoao-lo2-edzffu0v9ixoabc06rx-oqoq|Hanji 964]] tie Wang Yun's refusal to spare Cai Yong to fear of historical judgment after the anti-Dong-Zhuo success.
- Status over material analysis: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-964-bie-jiaoao-bie-jiaoao-bie-jiaoao-lo2-edzffu0v9ixoabc06rx-oqoq|Hanji 964]] has Liu Dai reject Bao Xin's defensive advice against a hungry raiding force, and it frames Lü Bu's repeated merit display as socially corrosive.
- Safety-design failure: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-965-bangguo-bieren-de-mang-weishenme-yao-shao-ti-ltjd-cv59hnm-n8ar9jxdogp9q0q|Hanji 965]] shows Wang Yun canceling amnesty, rejecting Huangfu Song's reassurance path, and leaving remnant commanders without a credible safety route.
- Regime and prewar scale: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1033-geju-junfa-yuanshu-zuizhong-tuxue-er-wang-lt0ibs-en0oeoqkkcxl8sevjng4|Hanji 1033]] shows Yuan Shu's luxury and unsupported imperial claim ending in abandonment, while Yuan Shao's post-Gongsun-Zan confidence makes him vulnerable to title temptation and ignored strategic warnings.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept should not flatten every failure into personality. The sources also foreground material conditions: food, exhausted soldiers, captured title systems, refugee pressure, factional advice channels, and rival incentives. Post-success arrogance names the actor-side distortion that makes those conditions harder to see or manage.

## What Changed
- Hanji 1033 broadens the concept from post-Dong-Zhuo court behavior to Yuan Shu's final collapse and Yuan Shao's pre-Guandu overconfidence.
- The page is migrated to synthesis-v1 while preserving the prior source order.
