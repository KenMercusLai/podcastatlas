# Rehabilitation Goal Differentiation / 康复目标分层

## Definition
Rehabilitation goal differentiation / 康复目标分层 is the practice of defining recovery against the person's required activities, risk tolerance, timing, and performance demands rather than treating pain relief, daily function, tissue healing, and return to elite competition as interchangeable endpoints.

## Current Synthesis
[[vol-127-zhe-xiang-keji-tigao-le-aoyun-chengji-jianshao-le-yundong-henhuo-shanghai-zan-putongren-ye-neng-yong-lai-zhuli-kangfu-ln2tg_vwgjzmmss49rn6nagis5p0]] contrasts ordinary and professional-athlete recovery without claiming that one group deserves better care. Ordinary patients may reasonably prioritize reduced pain and daily independence, while an athlete may need precise control, sport-specific loading, confidence, and timely return to competition.

That distinction changes measurement and decision requirements. A simple symptom or circumference check may answer one question, while repeated strength, gait, movement-quality, workload, and sport-specific assessment may be needed for another. The episode also shows the danger of importing elite-sport choices into ordinary care: a short competitive window can produce different tradeoffs around surgery, materials, and risk.

## Key Claims
- Rehabilitation endpoints should be defined before treatment and revisited as function, symptoms, and goals change.
- Pain reduction, daily function, tissue healing, strength symmetry, movement control, and return to competition are related but non-identical outcomes.
- Elite athletes may accept different timelines or risks because career and event windows matter, but those choices are not default recommendations for ordinary patients.
- Measurement intensity should match the decision: more data are useful only when they change a meaningful rehabilitation choice.
- Return-to-sport planning should include the demands of the actual sport rather than stopping at a generic “recovered” label.

## Evidence
- Goal contrast: [[vol-127-zhe-xiang-keji-tigao-le-aoyun-chengji-jianshao-le-yundong-henhuo-shanghai-zan-putongren-ye-neng-yong-lai-zhuli-kangfu-ln2tg_vwgjzmmss49rn6nagis5p0]] contrasts ordinary goals of pain relief and daily function with athletes' need to return to competition and recover fine control.
- Measurement implications: [[vol-127-zhe-xiang-keji-tigao-le-aoyun-chengji-jianshao-le-yundong-henhuo-shanghai-zan-putongren-ye-neng-yong-lai-zhuli-kangfu-ln2tg_vwgjzmmss49rn6nagis5p0]] compares simple thigh-circumference follow-up with proposed dynamic gait and quadriceps assessment.
- Risk and timing: [[vol-127-zhe-xiang-keji-tigao-le-aoyun-chengji-jianshao-le-yundong-henhuo-shanghai-zan-putongren-ye-neng-yong-lai-zhuli-kangfu-ln2tg_vwgjzmmss49rn6nagis5p0]] uses ligament reconstruction and an athlete's pre-Olympic finger injury to illustrate career-window tradeoffs.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The ordinary-patient versus athlete contrast is a heuristic, not a rigid classification. Non-professional patients may have demanding work, caregiving, disability, or sport goals, and athletes also need long-term health. The episode's examples do not establish the best graft, test, timeline, or acceptable risk for any individual.

## What Changed
- Added a goal-first framework separating symptom relief, daily function, and return-to-sport performance.
- Added career timing and risk tolerance as explicit rehabilitation variables.
- Qualified the athlete-versus-ordinary-patient contrast so it does not become a hierarchy of care.
