# Insect Bite Dermatitis

## Definition
Insect bite dermatitis is the source's broad dermatology frame for inflammatory or allergic skin reactions caused by mosquitoes, midges, caterpillars, rove beetles, mites, ticks, or similar bite and contact exposures.

## Current Synthesis
The concept matters because "bug bite" is too blunt. The same summer exposure label can cover ordinary mosquito saliva reactions, stronger midge-like allergy, caterpillar hairs left in the skin, rove-beetle toxin injury, tick attachment, and mite or chigger bites that raise infection-risk questions.

The current source keeps mechanism and severity together. Many reactions are local immune or inflammatory responses, but severe blistering, pain, broken skin, persistent swelling, retained arthropod parts, fever, or eschar changes the practical problem from casual itch relief into clinical assessment.

## Key Claims
- Insect bite dermatitis is heterogeneous; mosquito, midge, caterpillar, rove-beetle, tick, mite, and chigger exposures can produce different skin patterns.
- Ordinary bite swelling is often an allergic or inflammatory response to saliva, venom, hairs, or irritant fluids rather than a primary infection.
- Caterpillar dermatitis may involve residual hairs or foreign material that should be removed gently before washing and anti-itch treatment.
- Rove-beetle dermatitis can follow toxin contact rather than a remembered bite, producing redness, pain, blisters, crusting, or erosions with prolonged marks.
- Individual immune response affects severity, so the same exposure can create minor itch in one person and a larger, longer reaction in another.
- The dermatitis frame must stay linked to escalation because some arthropod exposures also carry infection or retained-body-part risks.

## Evidence
- Heterogeneous exposure set: [[vol-213-wenzi-weishenme-pianai-yao-ni-pifuke-yisheng-chaichuan-quwen-zhishangshui-fu-kuaisu-zhiyang-xuanyao-zhinan-lifkugb27d79ut30lwfmu5vojegec]] explicitly expands from mosquitoes to midges, caterpillars, rove beetles, ticks, mites, and chiggers.
- Allergy and inflammation mechanism: [[vol-213-wenzi-weishenme-pianai-yao-ni-pifuke-yisheng-chaichuan-quwen-zhishangshui-fu-kuaisu-zhiyang-xuanyao-zhinan-lifkugb27d79ut30lwfmu5vojegec]] says ordinary mosquito bumps are mostly allergic reactions and uses antihistamines and steroids to explain itch, redness, edema, and broader inflammation.
- Caterpillar pattern: [[vol-213-wenzi-weishenme-pianai-yao-ni-pifuke-yisheng-chaichuan-quwen-zhishangshui-fu-kuaisu-zhiyang-xuanyao-zhinan-lifkugb27d79ut30lwfmu5vojegec]] describes exposed-area caterpillar dermatitis, possible retained hairs, tape removal, washing, wet compresses, calamine, and mild topical steroid use.
- Rove-beetle pattern: [[vol-213-wenzi-weishenme-pianai-yao-ni-pifuke-yisheng-chaichuan-quwen-zhishangshui-fu-kuaisu-zhiyang-xuanyao-zhinan-lifkugb27d79ut30lwfmu5vojegec]] describes acidic toxin exposure that can cause redness, stinging, itch, blisters, crusting, erosions, and persistent skin traces.
- Severity variation and escalation: [[vol-213-wenzi-weishenme-pianai-yao-ni-pifuke-yisheng-chaichuan-quwen-zhishangshui-fu-kuaisu-zhiyang-xuanyao-zhinan-lifkugb27d79ut30lwfmu5vojegec]] uses a severe case and later mite/tick distinctions to show why reaction strength and infection-risk signs change the response.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not turn insect bite dermatitis into one diagnosis with one treatment. It keeps organism, exposure route, severity, skin breakage, fever, eschar, tick attachment, and patient context as reasons to seek clinical judgment instead of relying on a general anti-itch routine.

## What Changed
- VOL.213 adds an insect-bite dermatitis concept that distinguishes ordinary mosquito reactions from caterpillar, rove-beetle, mite, tick, and chigger patterns.
