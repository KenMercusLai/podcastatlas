## Definition
Trust-dense distribution is a growth strategy that concentrates on people and communities already close to the problem, where credibility, relevance, and referral context can outperform broad paid reach.

## Current Synthesis
The source supplies two versions. [[ThriveMarket]] reportedly identified influencers who were already customers and offered partnership or equity, making authentic use part of the referral. [[Voiceback]] faces a rarer and more sensitive need, so clinicians, speech pathologists, support groups, caregivers, patient forums, advisers, and advocacy organizations can place the product near diagnosis and communication decisions. In both cases, a smaller credible network may be more valuable than a larger unqualified audience.

## Key Claims
- Existing users with relevant audiences can be stronger advocates than paid promoters with no product attachment.
- Referral value depends on proximity to the customer's moment of need, not audience size alone.
- Professional credibility matters when products touch health, disability, safety, or identity.
- Personalized founder outreach and lived experience can open trust-bearing relationships.
- Trusted distribution still requires product, pricing, retention, access, and safety validation.

## Evidence
- Customer-advocate model - [[advice-line-with-nick-green-of-thrive-market-7764e494-e791-48db-b95a-2d18994139a0]] describes Thrive searching customer records for influencers who had already adopted the service.
- Clinical and community model - [[advice-line-with-nick-green-of-thrive-market-7764e494-e791-48db-b95a-2d18994139a0]] recommends clinicians, ALS clinics, speech pathologists, support groups, caregivers, forums, and advocacy organizations for Voiceback.
- Early credibility - [[advice-line-with-nick-green-of-thrive-market-7764e494-e791-48db-b95a-2d18994139a0]] records Voiceback's medical adviser and user advisory group as evidence that targeted relationships can produce feedback and attention.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Trusted intermediaries can be slow, conflicted, inaccessible, or already committed to incumbent tools. Equity-based advocacy needs transparent disclosure, while clinical referral requires stronger privacy, safety, evidence, and governance than ordinary consumer influence. The source does not report controlled channel comparisons.

## What Changed
- Established a distribution concept spanning authentic customer advocates and clinical or patient referral networks.
