# Dance Participation–Professionalism Boundary / 舞蹈参与与专业创作边界

## Definition
The dance participation–professionalism boundary holds together two claims: everyone can move, dance, and gain bodily awareness, while making dance as an artistic work requires learned form, sustained bodily training, choreographic judgment, and responsibility for how action becomes legible.

## Current Synthesis
EP286 rejects both exclusion and flattening. It invites ordinary people to dance and recommends that everyone experience a class or workshop, but it does not equate spontaneous club movement with a finished artwork. Even standing still or making a small gesture can require extensive discipline when it must carry choreographic meaning. Public education is therefore not a claim that expertise disappears; it is a way to understand what trained artists do and to deepen participation, attention, and respect.

## Key Claims
- The human body gives everyone a legitimate entry into movement and dance experience.
- Personal expression and artistic production are overlapping but non-identical activities.
- Professional dancers maintain a trained bodily instrument through repeated practice.
- Minimal or ordinary-looking actions can demand substantial technique and compositional context.
- Workshops can widen access by making artistic demands experientially understandable.
- Contemporary dance's use of walking or imperfection does not eliminate craft; selection, relation, timing, and framing remain compositional work.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The boundary should not become gatekeeping that denies amateur, community, vernacular, social, or culturally transmitted dance their artistic value. The source offers a professional curator's and artists' framing rather than a universal definition of art. Training can take institutional, communal, or self-directed forms, and expertise does not guarantee that a work will communicate to every audience.
