# Creative Aperture Shift

## Definition
Creative aperture shift is the practice of changing the width or focus of attention so a creative problem can be seen from a different perceptual position.

## Current Synthesis
[[essentials-how-to-access-your-creativity-rick-rubin-scim7816635332]] treats creative insight as partly an attentional shift. [[AndrewHuberman]] introduces narrow and broad visual focus, and [[RickRubin]] generalizes the point: creativity may require either widening or narrowing attention, depending on which habitual aperture has trapped the work. The source's comedy example makes novelty secondary to perspective; an outrageous line works when a shifted view reveals truth inside it.

## Key Claims
- Creativity can involve broadening attention when the current frame is too narrow.
- It can also involve narrowing attention when diffuse awareness lacks usable shape.
- The key move is not a permanently broad or narrow state, but departure from the habitual aperture.
- A perspective shift can reveal truth or interest inside material that first appears strange.
- Attention change is practical because it can be tested directly in the work rather than explained abstractly.

## Evidence
- Visual-focus prompt - [[essentials-how-to-access-your-creativity-rick-rubin-scim7816635332]] records Huberman connecting creative strategy to narrow and broad visual focus.
- Broad-or-narrow answer - [[essentials-how-to-access-your-creativity-rick-rubin-scim7816635332]] records Rubin saying creativity can involve broadening or narrowing attention, with the key being a change in the usual aperture.
- Comedy analogy - [[essentials-how-to-access-your-creativity-rick-rubin-scim7816635332]] compares creative shifts to comedy, where something outrageous feels funny because there is truth in it.
- Experiment priority - [[essentials-how-to-access-your-creativity-rick-rubin-scim7816635332]] says actionable suggestions matter most in settings where creators can try changes immediately.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not claim that visual focus alone causes creativity. It uses broad and narrow focus as a practical analogy for changing the perceptual frame around a problem.
