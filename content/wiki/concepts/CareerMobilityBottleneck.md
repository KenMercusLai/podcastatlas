# Career Mobility Bottleneck

Career mobility bottleneck is the blocked-advancement pattern in [[older-workers-arent-retiring-should-they-be-forced-to]] where younger or mid-career workers see senior incumbents staying in place and conclude there is no path upward. The concept is narrower than a general shortage of jobs: it concerns scarce promotions, tenure-like posts, supervisory seats, and high-status roles.

[[RyanHendrickson]] and [[BJSwami]] make the bottleneck visible. Both eventually left their organizations after concluding that waiting would not create a path. The episode uses their cases to test whether [[MandatoryRetirementPolicy]], [[PhasedRetirementSuccession]], or ordinary job switching is the better answer.

## Key Claims
- A mobility bottleneck can exist even if the economy as a whole is creating jobs.
- The bottleneck is most plausible where senior roles are scarce, prestigious, high-paying, or protected by tenure-like structures.
- Workers can experience the problem as unfair even when the incumbent is qualified.
- The source keeps the emotional complaint separate from the stronger policy claim: frustration does not by itself prove that forced retirement is the right remedy.
- Leaving for another organization can solve an individual bottleneck while leaving the institutional design problem unresolved.
