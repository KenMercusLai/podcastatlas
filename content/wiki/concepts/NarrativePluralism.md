# Narrative Pluralism / 叙事多样性

## Definition
Narrative pluralism / 叙事多样性 is the position that cultures and works need multiple legitimate story shapes rather than one compulsory model of heroic conflict, linear progress, origin, victory, or anti-grand-narrative refusal.

## Current Synthesis
The episode's final claim is not that scale, grandeur, or macro narrative is inherently bad. Its target is narrative monopoly: when power allows only one organizing story, other forms of relation, coexistence, care, gathering, and digression are treated as weak or illegitimate.

## Key Claims
- Rejecting all grand narrative can become as reductive as turning everything into grand narrative.
- The problem is not macro scale itself but the loss of multiple forms for making meaning.
- Carrier-bag, spear-like, ecological, anecdotal, lyrical, and atmospheric structures can all be legitimate when their internal standards are clear.
- Narrative pluralism requires judging works by their own organizing relations rather than by one conflict-first rule.
- The concept has political force because single-story authority can erase marginal, feminist, ecological, or more-than-human perspectives.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Pluralism does not mean every narrative arrangement works. The source distinguishes relational carrier-bag form from loose accumulation, so plurality still requires standards of coherence, relation, and meaningful selection.

## What Changed

- Added a distinction between criticizing narrative monopoly and rejecting large-scale narrative altogether.
- Added the political connection among form, power, marginality, feminism, and ecology.
