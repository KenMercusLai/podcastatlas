# Chocolate Compound

Chocolate compound is the chocolate-like coating discussed in [[reeses-heir-vs-chocolate-skimpflation]]. The episode says it can use some chocolate ingredients while still failing the legal standard for milk chocolate, especially when vegetable oil replaces cocoa butter as the relevant fat.

The concept matters because consumers may read "chocolate candy" or similar language as close enough to chocolate, while [[ChocolateLabelStandards]] treat milk chocolate as a regulated category. In the source, [[BradReese]] turns compound chocolate into evidence for [[Skimpflation]], while [[Hershey]] says recipe differences can support product shapes, sizes, and consumer preferences.

## Key Claims
- Chocolate compound can resemble chocolate in a finished candy while not qualifying as milk chocolate.
- Replacing cocoa butter with vegetable oil changes the legal label and can lower exposure to expensive cocoa-butter inputs.
- Compound coating may make some shapes or formats easier to manufacture, but it can also weaken [[ProductLedWillingnessToPay]] if consumers notice a taste or trust difference.
- A compound-chocolate label is a signal for [[PackagingAsProductExperience]] because the package is where consumers can detect the formula change.
