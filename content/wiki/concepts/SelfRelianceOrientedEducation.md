# Self-Reliance-Oriented Education / 自立导向教育

## Definition

Self-reliance-oriented education is an educational approach that prepares a young person to support themselves, make and revise choices, seek help, accept responsibility, and continue developing without treating credentials, placement, or class mobility as guaranteed outcomes.

## Current Synthesis

The Food School case places self-reliance beneath generic employability skills. Communication, collaboration, time management, and execution matter, but they become durable only when a student understands why a task or job is worth attempting and accepts that teachers cannot permanently arrange opportunities or absorb consequences.

Self-reliance here is not solitary independence. Students work inside rules, relationships, and a continuing community; they may ask for help and can fail without being abandoned. The educational task is therefore to combine real responsibility with enough safety for experimentation, making the first job a supported beginning rather than either a final identity or proof that the institution succeeded.

## Key Claims

- Educational success can include livelihood, dignity, vitality, and continued growth even without a new credential or immediate status gain.
- Agency requires a reasoned choice and willingness to face its consequences, not merely compliance with a teacher's recommendation.
- Consequential tasks build confidence when other people depend on a student's coordination, reliability, service, or delivery.
- Help-seeking is part of self-reliance because difficult transitions exceed any individual's knowledge and capacity.
- Rules and a non-abandoning community can make failure instructive without turning school into permanent shelter from work.
- A first job should be evaluated as a career starting point whose fit can be revised through later experience.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

- Self-reliance language can obscure structural limits if it turns unemployment, low pay, discrimination, illness, or weak labor demand into individual failure.
- The source offers selected cases, not comparative evidence that this model outperforms longer schooling, formal vocational education, or other transition programs.
- Continued community support makes the model relational rather than proof that individuals can or should manage alone.
- Economic independence is a meaningful threshold but does not by itself establish safe work, adequate income, occupational progression, or well-being.

## What Changed

- Established self-reliance as a relational education-to-work goal rather than isolated independence.
- Separated supported career beginnings from guaranteed placement, credentials, or class mobility.
