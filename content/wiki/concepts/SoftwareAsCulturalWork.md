# Software As Cultural Work

Software as cultural work is the source's claim that AI-built software can carry taste, mood, identity, and interaction in ways that resemble creative media rather than only productivity tools. In [[dang-ruanjian-rongyi-bei-chuangzuo-xin-shidai-de-chanpin-chang-shenme-yang-duitan-albert-ltgalbpl0p41d33vvsbaczqrcbdi]], [[Albert]] uses examples such as vertical-writing notes, sleep-related maps, walk videos, and poetry tied to geography to argue that small software may be valuable because of how it feels, not only because it saves time.

This extends [[TokenDrivenSoftware]] and [[AIInteractiveEntertainment]] beyond games or generated interfaces. When software becomes easier to make, the scarce layer can move toward aesthetic judgment, emotional fit, social meaning, and the creator's recognizable taste.

## Key Claims
- Use value is not the only reason people make or consume software; mood, play, identity, and meaning can matter.
- Low-cost creation lets personal tools remain worthwhile even when they cannot support venture-scale revenue.
- Small software works still need a [[ProductContainer]] that makes them discoverable, runnable, and shareable.
- The "impact model" in the source treats influence, resonance, and recognized taste as a value path alongside ordinary monetization.
- Software as cultural work can coexist with [[ProductLedWillingnessToPay]], but it should not be judged only by SaaS-style recurring utility.
- The risk is that most works still follow a harsh attention distribution: many can be made, but only a few may find repeated use or social resonance.
