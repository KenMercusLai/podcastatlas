# Nuclear Safety Propaganda

Nuclear safety propaganda is the risk-communication pattern added by [[04-qieernuobeili-de-wuye-woyao-jin-jianyu-le-530594843]]. In the episode's reading of [[MidnightInChernobyl|《切尔诺贝利的午夜》]], official claims that nuclear power was "absolutely safe" did not merely reassure the public; they shaped what managers and operators could believe after the [[ChernobylNuclearPowerPlant]] explosion.

The source treats propaganda as a safety factor. If a system is repeatedly described as unable to fail catastrophically, evidence of catastrophe becomes harder to accept, and the first institutional reflex may be denial, minimization, or a search for a narrower explanation.

The episode's comparison to the [[ThreeMileIslandAccident]] shows the same communication problem from another angle. Criticizing another country's nuclear accident too loudly could make one's own nuclear safety story less stable.

## Key Claims
- Public safety narratives can feed back into operator and manager expectations.
- "Absolute safety" language weakens readiness for catastrophic exceptions.
- Information control after an accident can preserve political image while worsening public trust and exposure.
- Propaganda can turn technical uncertainty into administrative certainty before the facts are understood.
