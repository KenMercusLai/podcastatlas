# Weapon-Category Policy Framing

## Definition

Weapon-category policy framing is the use of firearm labels, operating mechanisms, appearance, and platform distinctions to define a policy target, sometimes at the cost of obscuring broader risk from weapon availability and use.

## Current Synthesis

The sources argue that semi-automatic and automatic firearms are substantively different, while some assault-weapon bans regulate combinations of grips, stocks, magazines, and other features without removing the underlying AR-15 platform or functionally comparable rifles. AR-15 modularity makes configuration legally salient even when many components primarily affect handling, fit, or customization. Accurate classification matters, but classification alone does not settle policy: focusing on a narrow subset can imply that other widely circulating guns are comparatively unimportant.

The mass-shooting autopsy example adds a use-context qualification. In the selected 23-event sample, handgun victims reportedly had the highest likelihood of death because attackers were closer, struck the head or torso more often, and fired repeated shots. That does not make handguns intrinsically more lethal or rifles benign; it shows that distance, shot placement, repeated hits, concealability, and treatment can alter category-level outcomes. The durable synthesis is therefore two-level: avoid technically false equivalence, then ask whether the legal category captures the actual distribution and mechanisms of harm.

## Key Claims

- Automatic and semi-automatic operation should not be treated as interchangeable.
- Appearance-based feature rules can leave the underlying weapon platform accessible.
- Modularity lets law distinguish configurations even when listed components do not define the firing mechanism.
- Correct taxonomy is necessary but does not establish that the chosen category captures most firearm harm.
- Practical use, distance, shot placement, repeated hits, and treatment can change observed lethality beyond headline ballistic properties.
- Narrow category debates can distract from the aggregate risk of widespread firearm circulation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The supplied summaries do not provide the full statutes, proposals, exemption lists, demonstrations, firearm specifications, advocacy publications, or autopsy paper. A rule's incomplete coverage, a fast reload demonstration, or weapon substitution does not show that feature or magazine restrictions have no effect, and category-specific regulation can coexist with broader measures. The 23-event autopsy sample does not establish intrinsic handgun superiority or generalize to all mass shootings, interpersonal violence, suicide, or accidental injury. Legal precision can also improve a narrow rule without answering whether the rule's benefit, enforceability, substitution effects, and opportunity costs justify it.
