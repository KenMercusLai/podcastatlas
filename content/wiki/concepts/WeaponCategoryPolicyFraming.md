# Weapon-Category Policy Framing

## Definition

Weapon-category policy framing is the use of firearm labels, operating mechanisms, appearance, and platform distinctions to define a policy target, sometimes at the cost of obscuring broader risk from weapon availability and use.

## Current Synthesis

The episode argues that semi-automatic and automatic firearms are substantively different, while some assault-rifle bans select cosmetic or menacing-looking features without removing the underlying AR-15 platform. Accurate classification matters, but classification alone does not settle policy: focusing on a narrow subset can imply that other widely circulating guns are comparatively unimportant.

The emergency-medicine example adds a use-context qualification. A handgun may be highly lethal in a mass shooting depending on handling, setting, ammunition, access, and victim treatment. The durable synthesis is therefore two-level: avoid technically false equivalence, then ask whether the category captures the actual distribution of harm.

## Key Claims

- Automatic and semi-automatic operation should not be treated as interchangeable.
- Appearance-based feature rules can leave the underlying weapon platform accessible.
- Correct taxonomy is necessary but does not establish that the chosen category captures most firearm harm.
- Practical use and setting can change relative lethality beyond a weapon's headline ballistic properties.
- Narrow category debates can distract from the aggregate risk of widespread firearm circulation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The supplied summary does not identify the statutes, regulated features, firearm models, ammunition, shooting scenarios, casualty data, or physician's underlying evidence. A rule's incomplete coverage does not show it has no effect, and category-specific regulation can coexist with broader measures. Relative lethality is context-dependent and should not be generalized from one clinician's episode-reported assessment.

## What Changed

- Created a two-level synthesis separating technical firearm classification from category coverage and real-world harm.
