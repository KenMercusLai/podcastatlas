# Japanese Drug Pricing Reform / 日本药品定价改革

Japanese drug pricing reform is the pharma-policy branch in [[vol-120-riben-yiliao-tixi-de-bengkui-yu-zhongsheng-yiyuan-yaopin-yibao-sanjiao-kunju-de-yizhong-jiefa-lp-p5p1cybbldphe0qajteggsitt]]. The episode says Japan first used broad low-price procurement and price cuts to save insurance spending, but then faced lower drug quality, generic-drug distrust, weaker profits, and innovation decline.

The post-2003 reform, as summarized by the source, tried to stop treating all drug spending as waste. First-in-class drugs could receive higher full-cycle cost-based pricing; me-better drugs could receive added-value premiums; me-too drugs stayed closer to lower anchors. The point was to protect innovation and quality without losing the insurance-cost discipline of [[JapaneseUniversalHealthCoverage]].

## Key Claims
- Drug cost control can backfire if it pushes prices below sustainable quality and R&D levels.
- Paying more for valuable innovation can support total system efficiency if better drugs reduce downstream treatment burden.
- Pricing reform needed industry consolidation and [[GenericDrugTrustRebuilding]], not just a new reimbursement formula.
