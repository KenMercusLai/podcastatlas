# Immigrant Industry Clustering

## Definition
Immigrant industry clustering is the concentration of members of an immigrant community in a particular line of business, sustained by network-based transfer of employment, training, finance, suppliers, operating knowledge, and trust.

## Current Synthesis
The episode treats clustering as business infrastructure rather than simple cultural preference. An initial opening may be contingent, but family and community ties lower the cost of entering and learning the field; specialization can then reproduce itself and reportedly improve earnings relative to working alone elsewhere. The advantage is bounded: it can coexist with long hours, low margins, physical strain, limited bank access, concentrated risk, and obligations the episode does not fully examine.

## Key Claims
- A relative or community member can turn migration into an immediate employment and training pathway.
- Informal finance can substitute for missing credit history or conventional lending access.
- Repeated social contact circulates tacit knowledge about suppliers, regulations, products, leases, and customers.
- Smaller groups with stronger internal ties may concentrate more because dense networks make specialization easier to reproduce.
- Participation in a community self-employment cluster may bring an earnings advantage without guaranteeing good working conditions.
- Clusters can begin through historical contingency and persist through cumulative network effects.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source supplies associations and illustrative cases, not the underlying statistical evidence needed to establish causality. Marriage is an incomplete proxy for network strength. Selection, discrimination, licensing, geography, market access, prior skills, and capital constraints may also shape concentration. Earnings advantages do not reveal their distribution within families or communities and do not cancel overwork, saturation, or business risk.

## What Changed
- Added a bounded mechanism linking social ties to employment, training, finance, and tacit knowledge.
- Added reported earnings advantage while separating it from job-quality claims.
- Added historical contingency and later network reproduction as distinct stages.
- Added second-generation recombination into an adjacent industry.
