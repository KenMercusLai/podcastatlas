# Death Normalization Practice / 死亡日常化练习

## Definition
Death normalization practice is the deliberate effort to make death discussable through ordinary language, wills, last wishes, funerals, social-account plans, burial preferences, and everyday farewell rather than treating death as a taboo or euphemism.

## Current Synthesis
In [[caikangyong-luoyonghao-zai-canku-de-shijie-xunzhao-zizai-yu-hejie-baochi-cibei-yu-zhenshi-lqbw_yofwhs8rpt3w0xse_g9ajj1]], [[CaiKangyong|蔡康永]] argues that talking about life while avoiding death is false optimism. His version of death normalization is practical rather than grand: distinguish legal wills from last wishes, decide what should happen to social accounts, discuss burial or announcement preferences, and keep the word "死" usable.

## Key Claims
- Death avoidance can masquerade as optimism while leaving practical and emotional work undone.
- Legal wills and personal last wishes should be distinguished because not every preference has the same enforceable status.
- Digital memorial choices, funeral participation, burial style, and public announcement can be planned as ordinary life administration.
- Direct language matters; euphemism can turn death into a forbidden word rather than a real human fact.
- Humor around tombstones or funerals can reduce taboo without trivializing loss.
- Practicing smaller farewells helps make final farewell less foreign.

- Digital and burial planning: [[caikangyong-luoyonghao-zai-canku-de-shijie-xunzhao-zizai-yu-hejie-baochi-cibei-yu-zhenshi-lqbw_yofwhs8rpt3w0xse_g9ajj1]] discusses closing social platforms after death and deciding burial-related preferences.
- Direct language: [[caikangyong-luoyonghao-zai-canku-de-shijie-xunzhao-zizai-yu-hejie-baochi-cibei-yu-zhenshi-lqbw_yofwhs8rpt3w0xse_g9ajj1]] records Cai's dislike of avoiding the word "死."
- Humor and ritual: [[caikangyong-luoyonghao-zai-canku-de-shijie-xunzhao-zizai-yu-hejie-baochi-cibei-yu-zhenshi-lqbw_yofwhs8rpt3w0xse_g9ajj1]] includes tombstone jokes, funeral examples, and discussion of daily-life death customs.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a personal and cultural conversation, not legal or medical advice. It does not settle how different families, religions, jurisdictions, or clinical situations should handle wills, funerals, euthanasia, grief, or digital assets.
