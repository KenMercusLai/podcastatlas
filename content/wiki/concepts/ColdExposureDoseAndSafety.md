# Cold Exposure Dose and Safety

## Definition
Cold exposure dose and safety is a minimum-effective-dose framework that scales water temperature and duration to individual tolerance while treating immersion, cardiovascular stress, hypothermia, and breath manipulation as material hazards.

## Current Synthesis
The AMA does not identify one scientifically settled temperature-duration formula. It instead uses a subjective dose: the exposure should feel cold enough that the person wants to leave, yet remain safe enough for a brief additional stay. Beginners may therefore use warmer water or very short exposure, while colder plunges demand more caution, shorter duration, and another person nearby.

The key boundary is that discomfort is not proof of safety. Very cold water can trigger an involuntary gasp, cardiovascular stress, and hypothermia. Hyperventilation or breathwork before entering water can blunt warning responses and increase blackout risk, so it should not be paired with immersion. [[tools-to-enhance-working-memory-attention-scim1948560111]] adds a proposed use 30-60 minutes before demanding cognitive work, but explicitly says that specific working-memory timing studies have not been done. Alertness or motivation hypotheses therefore remain secondary to safety.

## Key Claims
- No universal cold-water temperature and duration is established by the source.
- Subjective discomfort can guide dose only inside a separate safety boundary.
- Short exposures can be sufficient when initiation is the main barrier.
- Colder water increases the need for caution, supervision, and conservative duration.
- Breathwork or hyperventilation should not precede water immersion.
- Alertness and motivation claims do not justify escalating exposure beyond safe tolerance.
- Catecholamine changes do not establish that pre-task cold reliably improves working memory.

## Evidence
- Dose heuristic: [[ama-18-cold-therapy-advice-skin-health-tips-motivation-learning-strategies-more-scim1100391235]] recommends water that creates a desire to exit while remaining safe enough for a brief stay.
- Minimum effective exposure: [[ama-18-cold-therapy-advice-skin-health-tips-motivation-learning-strategies-more-scim1100391235]] allows very short sessions when a longer plunge would prevent initiation.
- Risk boundary: [[ama-18-cold-therapy-advice-skin-health-tips-motivation-learning-strategies-more-scim1100391235]] names hypothermia, cardiovascular shock, blackout, and drowning as reasons for conservative practice.
- Breathwork boundary: [[ama-18-cold-therapy-advice-skin-health-tips-motivation-learning-strategies-more-scim1100391235]] warns against hyperventilation or breathwork before entering water.
- Cognitive-timing extrapolation: [[tools-to-enhance-working-memory-attention-scim1948560111]] proposes cold 30-60 minutes before focused work based on catecholamine effects while acknowledging that the specific working-memory studies are absent.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources explicitly leave temperature, duration, time of day, and cognitive benefit unresolved as one protocol. Example temperatures, long-duration laboratory immersion, catecholamine changes, alertness effects, dopamine framing, and sauna comparison are source-scoped, not medical clearance. Cardiovascular disease, fainting history, pregnancy, medications, cold injury, neurologic conditions, impaired sensation, open water, and unsupervised immersion can materially change risk.
