# Civil War Slavery Causation

## Definition

Civil War slavery causation is the episode's mechanism-level explanation of how slavery produced war through constitutional compromise, economic expansion, territorial conflict, federal enforcement, ideological polarization, party breakdown, secession, and armed escalation.

## Current Synthesis

[[200-american-civil-war-the-causes-part-1-glt1124511401]] argues that “slavery caused the war” is correct but incomplete without a sequence. The founding Constitution protected slavery enough to secure union while leaving some Americans expecting gradual decline. Cotton profitability and western land defeated that expectation; each territorial acquisition reopened where slave property could exist; federal fugitive enforcement made slavery present in free states; and Southern defense hardened as Northern resistance grew.

The result was not mechanical inevitability. The exact war remained contingent, but the available compromises became less stable as Kansas violence, [[JohnBrown|John Brown]], [[DredScott|Dred Scott]], sectional party competition, Lincoln's election, slavery-protecting secession, and [[FortSumter|Fort Sumter]] aligned.

## Key Claims

- Founding compromise preserved the Union by postponing slavery's constitutional conflict.
- Cotton profitability and territorial growth turned anticipated decline into expansion.
- Federal enforcement exposed free-state residents to slave law and changed Northern political incentives.
- Proslavery ideology and racial caste broadened Southern commitment beyond direct slaveholders.
- Antislavery mobilization remained compatible with substantial Northern racism and exclusion.
- Party and judicial institutions transmitted rather than resolved the sectional struggle.
- Lincoln's election triggered slavery-centered secession, while Fort Sumter converted rupture into war.

## Evidence

### Founding, economy, and territory

- [[200-american-civil-war-the-causes-part-1-glt1124511401]] joins constitutional protection, cotton demand, the cotton gin, and Mexican War territorial acquisition into the long causal sequence.

### Law and political experience

- [[200-american-civil-war-the-causes-part-1-glt1124511401]] uses fugitive-slave enforcement and [[AnthonyBurns]] to show how federal power made slavery visible in free states.

### Escalation and rupture

- [[200-american-civil-war-the-causes-part-1-glt1124511401]] connects Kansas, Brown, congressional violence, Dred Scott, Republican victory, secession, and Fort Sumter without claiming the exact outcome was predetermined.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept does not reduce every participant's motive to one belief, make all Northerners racial egalitarians, or deny disputes over union, federalism, territory, party, and constitutional power. It argues that those disputes became war-producing through slavery. Alternative timelines remained imaginable; economic figures, ideological periodization, individual motives, and rankings of the most important trigger remain source-scoped.
