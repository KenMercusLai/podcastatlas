# Stand-up Competition Material Strategy / 脱口秀竞赛素材配置

## Definition
Stand-up competition material strategy is the allocation of premises, drafts, revision time, collaborators, and performance confidence across elimination rounds when the supply of strong tested material is limited.

## Current Synthesis
In [[heguangzhi-luoyonghao-heguangzhi-wo-dao-changan-le-lkz6amse3ftpktl8vkozpfxj2kwt]], [[HeGuangzhiStandup|何广智]] describes a championship season with little stored material. The semifinal became the highest-risk point because he had to write something new while refusing to spend two ideas he believed were strong enough for the final. Apparent confidence helped him perform, but did not remove the underlying inventory problem.

The final also shows that material inventory is not a shelf of finished scripts. One routine preserved a form across several years—first a proposed love letter, then an annual review, and finally a new literary-film analogy. Competition strategy therefore sits inside [[StandupComedyIteration]]: allocation determines when an idea is exposed, while drafting, live judgment, writer discussion, and performance revision determine what that idea becomes.

## Key Claims
- Elimination formats create an inter-round allocation problem because using the strongest available piece now can weaken later options.
- Material inventory includes premises, forms, partial drafts, tested routines, and performance knowledge rather than only finished scripts.
- Visible confidence can support delivery while coexisting with scarce reserves and genuine uncertainty.
- Preserving an idea is useful only if later incubation and revision improve its fit for a decisive round.
- Collaboration can increase revision capacity without transferring primary authorship or guaranteeing audience response.
- Technical construction can secure a round while still feeling artistically weaker than material the performer genuinely wants to express.
- Audience votes provide competition evidence, but they do not settle long-term artistic value or readiness for a coherent special.

## Evidence
- Scarce reserve and round allocation - [[heguangzhi-luoyonghao-heguangzhi-wo-dao-changan-le-lkz6amse3ftpktl8vkozpfxj2kwt]] records few stored scripts, a newly written semifinal, and two protected final ideas.
- Long incubation - [[heguangzhi-luoyonghao-heguangzhi-wo-dao-changan-le-lkz6amse3ftpktl8vkozpfxj2kwt]] traces the final routine from a multi-year love-letter form through an annual-review structure to its final analogy.
- Revision support - [[heguangzhi-luoyonghao-heguangzhi-wo-dao-changan-le-lkz6amse3ftpktl8vkozpfxj2kwt]] describes He as primarily writing alone while using writers and trusted listeners during competition.
- Technique and expression - [[heguangzhi-luoyonghao-heguangzhi-wo-dao-changan-le-lkz6amse3ftpktl8vkozpfxj2kwt]] contrasts a technically assembled early-round routine with later material he valued more strongly.
- Competition versus body of work - [[heguangzhi-luoyonghao-heguangzhi-wo-dao-changan-le-lkz6amse3ftpktl8vkozpfxj2kwt]] closes the title arc while leaving a coherent special as the next artistic objective.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept is derived from one winner's retrospective account and does not establish an optimal tournament strategy. Judging systems, episode order, editing, deadlines, team support, opponent quality, and audience composition can alter incentives. Saving a strong idea can cause earlier elimination, while using it early can generate momentum; the source does not compare those counterfactuals.

## What Changed
- Created a round-allocation model that distinguishes premise inventory, finished material, collaborative revision, and visible confidence.
- Added a boundary between success within a competition and the artistic coherence of a longer special.
