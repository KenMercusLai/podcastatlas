## Definition

Han commercial wealth aspiration / 汉代商业财富愿望 is the episode's frame for open, visible, high-ceiling desires to get rich in the Han world, especially when those desires appear on display objects or auspicious inscriptions.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 410-2 uses the [[RiliBaqianwanBeltHook|“日利八千万” belt hook]] to argue that Han-era wealth aspiration could be direct, public, and materially displayed. The point is not that every person could actually earn such sums; it is that the phrase works as a social imagination of wealth, similar to other auspicious phrases such as “日入千金” or “日入八千万.”

The source pushes against a flat picture of ancient commerce as merely despised. It says belt hooks could signal rank and wealth, five-zhu money gave the number a concrete economic vocabulary, and the昭宣-era background made the wish feel culturally plausible. The concept remains source-scoped because the episode gives one artifact-centered interpretation, not a general survey of all Han commercial classes.

## Key Claims

- Visible objects can preserve wealth desire, not only rank, taste, or ritual identity.
- The “日利八千万” inscription is read as unusually blunt wealth aspiration.
- Auspicious income phrases point to a social language in which profit could be wished for openly.
- The episode distinguishes a cultural ceiling of aspiration from verified daily earnings.
- The frame complicates but does not overturn the broader [[HeavyAgricultureLightCommercePolicy|heavy-agriculture, light-commerce]] political background.

- Broader phrase field: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-410-2-rilibaqianwan-gudai-shangren-zhuanqian-you-duoganxiang-lskrjfgimcqzdztlo42pnp6vgh5|Hanji 410-2]] mentions auspicious seals or phrases such as “日入千金” and “日入八千万.”
- Economic plausibility frame: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-410-2-rilibaqianwan-gudai-shangren-zhuanqian-you-duoganxiang-lskrjfgimcqzdztlo42pnp6vgh5|Hanji 410-2]] situates the aspiration in a relatively stable and economically active昭宣 context.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source does not show how common such inscriptions were, whether this specific object belonged to a merchant, or whether “八千万” should be read literally. The concept records a cultural signal of aspiration rather than a population-wide economic conclusion.
