# Adaptation As Moral Reframing / 改编作为道德重心重置

## Definition

[[AdaptationAsMoralReframing|Adaptation as moral reframing]] is the practice of changing both a story's medium-specific construction and the moral center that guides how an audience judges its characters, relationships, and outcomes.

## Current Synthesis

The two [[FeixingjiaFilm|《飞行家》]] conversations show that adaptation can alter plot, visibility, and ethical attention at once. [[ShuangXuetao|双雪涛]] describes the original story as more interested in failure and able to keep Li Mingqi elusive, assembling him through search and other people's accounts. The film instead moves toward ordinary personhood and must sustain a visible protagonist for two hours. It gives him technical grounding, actions, relationships, and a fuller life, making his flight something built and shared rather than only imagined or reported.

That formal externalization also changes judgment. The leap is held inside [[FamilySupportedAspiration|family support]], responsibility, friendship, women's household agency, and historical change. Adaptation fidelity is therefore not exhausted by preserved incidents or endings: a medium change can require invention and compression, while a moral change can reposition dignity, failure, fantasy, and self-realization.

## Key Claims

- Adaptation can change the moral object of attention without discarding the source premise.
- Moving from reported literary absence to visible screen action requires new capacities, scenes, and relationships.
- Formal externalization can turn a private or ambiguous dream into a materially built, socially interpreted action.
- A shift from failure to ordinary personhood changes how aspiration, responsibility, and dignity are judged.
- Fidelity debates are too narrow when they ignore medium capacity and moral emphasis.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Both sources are conversations with participants in or around the film, not a scene-by-scene comparison of the published fiction, screenplay, and final cut. Their interpretation may reflect retrospective promotional framing. The concept does not imply that every major alteration preserves function, that screen characters must always be made more concrete, or that ordinary-person framing is inherently more ethical than a sharper failure narrative.

## What Changed

- Added medium-specific externalization to the existing moral-center account.
- Distinguished a character assembled by testimony from one established through visible action.
- Added technical capacity and feature-length dramatic sufficiency as reasons for invention.
- Connected ordinary-person reframing to audience recognition of impractical pursuits.
