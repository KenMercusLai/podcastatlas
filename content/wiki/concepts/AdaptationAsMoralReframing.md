# Adaptation As Moral Reframing / 改编作为道德重心重置

[[AdaptationAsMoralReframing|Adaptation as moral reframing]] is the pattern where an adaptation keeps a work's recognizable premise while changing the moral center that tells the audience what kind of life is being judged. [[ep243-jiang-qiming-x-li-xueqin-x-shuang-xuetao-xiang-zai-dongbei-shangtian-zhe-shi-er-haishi-tai-fuza-le-lkqbo0zg5y7p1fo4x90n8yq394t6]] adds the concept through [[ShuangXuetao|双雪涛]]'s account of [[FeixingjiaFilm|《飞行家》]].

In the source, the important change is not only plot mechanics or ending fidelity. Shuang says the original story came from a period when he was interested in "failure," while the film moves toward "ordinary person." That shift changes how Li Mingqi's flight reads: the leap is no longer just defeat, delusion, or triumph, but a moment where [[FamilySupportedAspiration|aspiration]], responsibility, and a whole life's ethical pressure meet.

## Key Claims
- Adaptation can change the moral object of attention without discarding the source premise.
- Moving a story from "failure" to "ordinary person" changes whether the audience judges an act as fantasy, completion, responsibility, or self-realization.
- Fidelity debates are too narrow when the adaptation's main work is shifting character dignity, relational meaning, and ethical emphasis.
- The concept sits beside [[AdaptationEndingEthics]] and [[AdaptationOriginalTextConfusion]], but focuses on moral center rather than only ending change or memory of the original.
