## Definition

Scorched-earth supply denial / 坚壁清野式断粮 is a military logistics pattern that denies an advancing enemy local food, shelter, transport, and usable infrastructure. It ranges from protective removal and fortification ahead of a plundering force to destructive withdrawal that burns settlements, breaks bridges, poisons wells, and eliminates allied bases, trading territory and civilian welfare for enemy attrition.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-675-luanshi-fenzheng-zhizhong-liuxiu-ruhe-jueqi-lrvxu1q4-ysg4kfxkf25hswygfyo|Hanji 675]] grounds the concept through [[ChenJunEasternHan|陈俊]] after [[LiuXiu|刘秀]] recovers from the Shunshui defeat. Liu Xiu has already resumed pursuit after the enemy withdraws at night, but Chen Jun reframes the problem: because the enemy plunders along the route, a light-cavalry screen can move ahead and make every settlement deny food.

The strategy works because it turns the enemy's operating method against itself. A force that expects to live off local extraction loses momentum when defended settlements hold out and supplies are cleared. The Hanji case emphasizes civilian protection inside fortified positions and victory without frontal collision.

[[565-the-great-northern-war-revenge-of-the-cossacks-part-2-glt1223770243|Great Northern War Part 2]] supplies a harsher state-scale variant. Russian and auxiliary forces destroy routes and resources before [[CharlesXII|Charles XII]], while [[PeterTheGreat|Peter the Great]] plans a broad belt of destruction and later eliminates the Swedish-Cossack base at [[Baturin]]. Denial helps avoid battle until Swedish distance, delay, weather, and the lost relief column make the army vulnerable, but the mechanism transfers severe costs to local populations and landscapes.

## Key Claims

- Supply denial becomes decisive when the enemy depends on plunder or local food during movement.
- The tactic requires action ahead of the enemy, whether through mobile screens, evacuation, fortification, destruction, or control of key bases.
- Denial can protect civilians and stores, as in the Hanji case, or deliberately sacrifice settlements and infrastructure, as in the Russian campaign.
- It becomes stronger when combined with distance, weather, delayed relief, manpower asymmetry, and avoidance of the enemy's preferred decisive battle.
- The military benefit and civilian cost must be evaluated separately; effective denial is not automatically restrained or humane.

## Evidence

- Enemy dependence on plunder: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-675-luanshi-fenzheng-zhizhong-liuxiu-ruhe-jueqi-lrvxu1q4-ysg4kfxkf25hswygfyo|Hanji 675]] says the retreating enemy plunders along the route toward Yuyang.
- Forward screen: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-675-luanshi-fenzheng-zhizhong-liuxiu-ruhe-jueqi-lrvxu1q4-ysg4kfxkf25hswygfyo|Hanji 675]] has Chen Jun propose sending light cavalry ahead of the enemy.
- Fortify and clear: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-675-luanshi-fenzheng-zhizhong-liuxiu-ruhe-jueqi-lrvxu1q4-ysg4kfxkf25hswygfyo|Hanji 675]] says local people should strengthen walls and clear resources so the enemy gains no food.
- Collapse mechanism: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-675-luanshi-fenzheng-zhizhong-liuxiu-ruhe-jueqi-lrvxu1q4-ysg4kfxkf25hswygfyo|Hanji 675]] says the enemy scatters after failing to obtain supplies.
- Accepted advice: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-675-luanshi-fenzheng-zhizhong-liuxiu-ruhe-jueqi-lrvxu1q4-ysg4kfxkf25hswygfyo|Hanji 675]] records Liu Xiu adopting and later praising Chen Jun's plan.
- Destructive belt: [[565-the-great-northern-war-revenge-of-the-cossacks-part-2-glt1223770243]] describes burned settlements, broken bridges, poisoned wells, removed food, and a planned wide denial zone along Swedish routes.
- Combined attrition: [[565-the-great-northern-war-revenge-of-the-cossacks-part-2-glt1223770243]] links denial to Swedish distance, weather, manpower limits, and the loss of Lewenhaupt's relief column.
- Allied-base denial: [[565-the-great-northern-war-revenge-of-the-cossacks-part-2-glt1223770243]] presents Baturin's destruction as both removal of supplies and coercive deterrence against wider Cossack defection.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The two cases differ morally and operationally. Hanji 675 emphasizes defended communities and cleared stores; the Russian campaign includes destruction of villages, wells, bridges, countryside, and Baturin, with heavy civilian exposure. Neither source gives a complete local implementation record, and the Great Northern War episode's numbers and motives remain source-scoped. The concept is narrower than all attrition or siege starvation: it concerns denying an advancing force access to the local resource system on which movement depends.

## What Changed

- Expanded the concept from a civilian-protective Hanji case to include destructive, state-scale denial during Charles XII's Russian campaign.
- Added an explicit distinction between military effectiveness and civilian or landscape cost.
- Connected route denial, relief-column isolation, weather, and destruction of an allied base into one attrition system.
