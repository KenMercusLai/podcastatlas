# Listener-Constructed Musical Meaning

## Definition
Listener-constructed musical meaning is the view that experienced emotion, imagery, movement, and significance emerge from an interaction among acoustic structure, learned expectation, personal association, embodiment, and cultural exposure.

## Current Synthesis
The source rejects a simple choice between meaning located entirely in notes and meaning freely projected by isolated listeners. People learn statistical regularities in the music around them and the situations in which musical features occur. Those regularities create expectations that composers can satisfy or violate, while accumulated contexts attach scenes, emotions, identities, and social uses to a style.

This learning makes response patterned without making it universal. Listeners can independently imagine similar scenes from unfamiliar excerpts, yet different cultural groups may converge on different stories for the same music. Repetition also changes the perceiver: another hearing can expose form, detail, or expression that was unavailable before, even when the recording itself is unchanged.

## Key Claims
- Musical experience is jointly produced by sound and a listener's learned predictive and associative history.
- Statistical exposure creates expectations whose fulfillment or violation can carry expression.
- Shared cultural learning can make imagery converge across listeners without placing one literal story inside the notes.
- Cross-cultural difference limits claims that a musical device has a fixed universal meaning.
- Repetition changes attention toward larger structures and finer details rather than merely duplicating an experience.
- Beat and phrasing can recruit simulated movement, making listening partly embodied even when the body remains still.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The summary does not provide stimuli, coding rules, samples, effect sizes, cultural sampling, replication, or alternative explanations for shared imagery. Film conventions, genre exposure, instrumentation, tempo, demand characteristics, and translation may contribute to convergence. Cross-cultural difference does not mean cultures are internally uniform, and statistical learning does not reduce music to passive conditioning or eliminate formal, historical, compositional, or individual interpretation.

## What Changed
- Created an interaction model joining musical structure to learned expectation, cultural association, embodiment, and repetition.
