# Marketplace Live Commerce

Marketplace live commerce is the platform pattern where many sellers or creators sell through real-time video while the marketplace supplies discovery, checkout, seller tools, fulfillment support, and trust. [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-gamestop-ceo-ryan-cohens-56b-plan-to-take-over-ebay-41752370]] adds the concept through [[RyanCohen]]'s claim that [[EBay|eBay]] has the users, brands, and marketplace base for live commerce but weak front-end and back-end execution.

The source distinguishes marketplace live commerce from narrower [[FounderLedLiveSelling]]. Cohen is not arguing that one founder should sell directly forever; he is arguing that eBay should make live selling work for many sellers, with [[GameStop]] stores potentially acting as studios, authentication sites, and fulfillment support. The core risk is operational: seller onboarding, inventory handling, content production, checkout, and customer trust all have to work before live demand becomes marketplace growth.

## Key Claims
- Live commerce can add entertainment and urgency to marketplace transactions, but the platform still has to solve seller tooling and fulfillment.
- Approval delays and weak back-end tooling can block supply even if user demand exists.
- A physical store network can lower creator friction by providing places to film, list, authenticate, and ship products.
- Platformized live commerce needs marketplace trust and operations, not only audience attention.
