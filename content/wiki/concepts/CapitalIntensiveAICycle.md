# Capital-Intensive AI Cycle

Capital-intensive AI cycle is [[DanDreyfus|Dan Dreyfus]]'s frame in [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-dan-dreyfus-americas-critical-minerals-crisis-is-here-41594225]] for the shift from software-led, capital-light growth toward physical infrastructure bottlenecks. The source argues that the next technology cycle depends on mines, grids, data centers, power generation, semiconductor fabs, aerospace production, defense capacity, and skilled labor.

The concept extends [[DataCenterPowerBottleneck]] and [[CriticalMineralsGeopolitics]]. AI growth is not treated as only a model, chip, or software market; it becomes a materials-and-construction market where copper, silver, rare earths, energy infrastructure, transmission equipment, and [[CraftLaborBottleneck|craft labor]] determine how much useful compute can actually be deployed.

## Key Claims
- AI and reindustrialization can move the bottleneck from code and capital formation to permitting, construction, power, materials, and labor.
- Multiple sectors can create simultaneous demand for the same inputs: data centers, aerospace, defense, EVs, grid upgrades, semiconductor fabs, and renewable power.
- A capital-intensive cycle can make slow physical lead times more important than fast software iteration.
- The source treats supply-chain resilience as an investment and national-security question, not only as an operating cost.
