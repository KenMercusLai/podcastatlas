# FDA Review Modernization

FDA review modernization is the central regulatory reform frame in [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-supercharging-a-new-fda-marty-makary-on-science-power-patients-39750050]]. [[MartyMakary|Marty Makary]] argues that the [[FoodAndDrugAdministration|FDA]] should move faster without making safety performative: public rejection letters, priority reviews, one well-designed pivotal trial where appropriate, digital workflows, and less siloed agency structure are presented as tools for speed and transparency.

The concept sits beside [[ClinicalDevelopmentCapability]]. A company still needs good trial design, endpoints, data, manufacturing, and evidence, but the regulator can either add friction through paperwork and silos or make evidence review more continuous and legible.

## Key Claims
- The source frames approval delay as an active patient risk, not only as protection from unsafe drugs.
- Makary says the FDA should compete on speed and efficiency because global biotech competition makes slow domestic trial starts costly.
- Modernization includes internal culture, review structure, public transparency, and clinical-trial design rather than only faster deadlines.
- The strongest version of the claim depends on [[PostMarketDrugSurveillance]]: faster decisions are easier to justify if monitoring continues after approval.
