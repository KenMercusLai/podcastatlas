# Creator Trust Advertising / 创作者信任广告

Creator Trust Advertising describes the advertising tension the source surfaces through [[Bilibili]]. In [[no-214-xunzhao-tonglei-xiaohongshu-bilibili-yiji-wuhuabamen-de-naxie-shequ-zhongguo-hulianwang-gushi-26-1005977305]], the no-forced-pre-roll debate turns on whether the platform should sell standardized attention inventory or preserve the more flexible trust between UP creators and viewers.

The source does not claim creator-read ads are frictionless. It explicitly notes that sponsorship income can concentrate among head creators and leave mid-tail creators with unstable income. The concept is therefore a tradeoff: platform-level ads can distribute revenue more systematically, while creator-native ads can preserve more audience control and community tone.

## Key Claims
- Advertising format can signal who owns the viewing experience: platform, creator, advertiser, or viewer.
- Forced pre-roll may be economically simple but can damage a community's browsing rhythm and trust identity.
- Creator sponsorship can feel more native and skippable, but it can also produce winner-take-most income distribution.
- The concept belongs to [[OnlineCommunityCommercializationFriction]] because revenue design becomes community-governance design.
