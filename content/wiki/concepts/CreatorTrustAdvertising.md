# Creator Trust Advertising / 创作者信任广告

## Definition
Creator trust advertising is advertising that relies on the credibility, tone, and audience relationship of creators rather than only platform-controlled interruptive inventory.

## Current Synthesis
The concept began with [[Bilibili]]'s no-forced-pre-roll debate. The earlier source frames the issue as a community-governance question: a platform can sell standardized attention, but intrusive formats may damage the creator-viewer relationship and browsing rhythm that make a community distinctive.

The new Bilibili earnings segment adds a stronger commercial test. Advertising became Bilibili's largest revenue segment, and management linked growth to young users' willingness to buy from platform ads and UP creator recommendations. That means creator trust is no longer only a defensive argument against bad ad formats; it can become an asset inside [[PlatformAdvertisingMonetization]]. The constraint remains that trust can be depleted if advertising density, targeting, or sponsor fit outruns audience permission.

## Key Claims
- Advertising format signals who controls the viewing experience: platform, creator, advertiser, or viewer.
- Forced pre-roll may be economically simple but can damage a community's browsing rhythm and trust identity.
- Creator-native sponsorship can feel more acceptable because it is embedded in creator voice and remains easier for audiences to interpret or skip.
- Creator trust can become a monetization asset when users are willing to buy through creator recommendations.
- Trust-based ads can still produce head-creator concentration, sponsor-mismatch risk, and eventual audience fatigue.

## Evidence
- No-forced-pre-roll tradeoff: [[no-214-xunzhao-tonglei-xiaohongshu-bilibili-yiji-wuhuabamen-de-naxie-shequ-zhongguo-hulianwang-gushi-26-1005977305]] uses Bilibili to contrast standardized ad inventory with creator-viewer trust and community browsing rhythm.
- Creator-income qualification: [[no-214-xunzhao-tonglei-xiaohongshu-bilibili-yiji-wuhuabamen-de-naxie-shequ-zhongguo-hulianwang-gushi-26-1005977305]] notes that creator sponsorship can concentrate income among head creators and leave mid-tail creators less stable.
- Commercial asset signal: [[kaile-shi-zai-ouzhou-kaidian-guanggao-shouci-chengwei-b-zhan-diyi-da-yewu-1010144081]] reports that Bilibili users are willing to buy from platform ads and UP creator recommendations.
- Revenue-scale pressure: [[kaile-shi-zai-ouzhou-kaidian-guanggao-shouci-chengwei-b-zhan-diyi-da-yewu-1010144081]] says advertising became Bilibili's largest revenue segment, making trust preservation a larger business constraint.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Creator trust does not make advertising frictionless. It may concentrate revenue among the most visible creators, create sponsor-fit conflicts, and become less trusted if audiences feel recommendations are too commercial. The latest source does not prove that Bilibili's ad growth is caused mainly by creator trust; it also cites platform demographics, AI content, and advertiser demand.
