# Animal Testing Substitution

Animal testing substitution is the episode's regulatory and ethical argument for replacing some animal studies with better human-relevant models. In [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-supercharging-a-new-fda-marty-makary-on-science-power-patients-39750050]], [[MartyMakary|Marty Makary]] says the [[FoodAndDrugAdministration|FDA]] is reducing animal testing requirements, including no longer requiring chimpanzee studies for monoclonal antibodies.

Makary's claim is not simply animal-welfare preference. He argues that animal studies can be misleading because many drugs that pass animal studies still fail human safety or efficacy tests, so computational modeling and organ-on-chip technology can sometimes improve both ethics and evidence quality.

## Key Claims
- The source frames animal-testing reform as a competitiveness issue, a scientific-validity issue, and an ethical issue.
- Substitution is strongest where animal models poorly predict human response or where modern alternatives capture relevant biology better.
- The concept still requires validation: organ-on-chip and computational models must themselves prove relevance before replacing legacy studies.
- The claim fits inside [[FDAReviewModernization]] because changing evidence requirements can shorten development without merely lowering standards.
