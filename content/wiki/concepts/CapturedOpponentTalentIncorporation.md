## Definition

降敌任用式立威 / captured opponent talent incorporation is the pattern in which a victor establishes hierarchy, then converts defeated-side people into usable capacity and public evidence of restraint, judgment, and an open talent market.

## Current Synthesis

The concept now covers four variants. [[SunCe|孙策]] captures [[ZuLangLateHan|祖郎]] and [[TaishiCi|太史慈]], releases and appoints them, then displays their incorporation to the army. After Xiapi, [[CaoCao|曹操]] executes actors he judges unusable or unsafe while absorbing [[ZhangLiao|张辽]], elite families, local-force brokers, and former defectors. The [[ChenLinLateHan|陈琳]] case broadens the mechanism from military strength to hostile literary talent.

Hanji 1095 adds peaceful provincial surrender and retained local command. Cao Cao appoints several Liu Biao-era officials after [[LiuCongLateHan|刘琮]] submits [[Jingzhou|荆州]], then preserves [[WenPing|文聘]]'s troops and gives him Jiangxia after treating grief for the old regime as evidence of character. Incorporation therefore need not begin with personal capture; it can follow coercive strategic victory and use local legitimacy, knowledge, and command continuity as the asset being converted.

## Key Claims
- Clear victory or hierarchy makes clemency persuasive rather than merely weak accommodation.
- Appointment can advertise that useful capacity outweighs private grievance.
- Incorporated leaders may bring followers, local knowledge, elite trust, or reputational power.
- The concept includes military, administrative, and literary talent.
- Preserving an existing command can reduce transition costs when old loyalty is judged compatible with new service.
- Selection remains bounded by trust and control; not every defeated actor is reusable.

## Evidence
- Captured commanders: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1028-xiaobawang-sunce-liwei-chengba-jiangdong-lgur-3fmpr55wy3aji-0rm38y7x|Hanji 1028]] says Sun Ce releases and appoints Zu Lang and Taishi Ci after capture.
- Xiapi settlement: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1026-jiemi-sanguo-di-yi-mengjiang-lvbu-de-luomu-lhjf-agujuliglqchumtff3tftxg1|Hanji 1026]] contrasts executions with incorporation of Zhang Liao, Chen-family elites, Zhang Ba, and former defectors.
- Hostile literary talent: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1069-chenlin-taocao-xiwen-mingzhen-tianxia-weihe-zuizhong-touxiang-caocao-lh9uzrzkgcaz45h-winquox-7jxf|Hanji 1069]] says Cao Cao pardons Chen Lin and keeps using his writing skill.
- Provincial personnel transfer: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1095-liubei-shujin-yiqie-hou-ping-shenme-jianxin-neng-fanshen-lmflbj9kshnmgsztdghnqj2foav3|Hanji 1095]] lists appointments for Han Song, Kuai Yue, Liu Xian, Deng Xi, and Cai Mao after Jingzhou submits.
- Retained local command: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1095-liubei-shujin-yiqie-hou-ping-shenme-jianxin-neng-fanshen-lmflbj9kshnmgsztdghnqj2foav3|Hanji 1095]] says Wen Ping keeps his troops and receives Jiangxia.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Hanji 1026 keeps Lü Bu, Chen Gong, and Gao Shun outside the incorporation set, showing that utility, trust, and political risk constrain pardon. Hanji 1095's claim that Wen Ping's old loyalty predicts new reliability is an interpretation, not a universal rule. The concept also differs from office granted before control is secure and from [[FormerEnemyLoyaltyReframing|旧敌忠义重释]], which centers moral reclassification rather than capacity conversion.

## What Changed
- Added Hanji 1095's peaceful provincial-transfer and retained-command variants.
- Clarified that incorporation can follow strategic surrender rather than literal personal capture.
- Added local legitimacy and command continuity to the assets a victor may preserve.
