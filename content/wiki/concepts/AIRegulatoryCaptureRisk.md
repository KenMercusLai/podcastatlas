# AI Regulatory Capture Risk

## Definition
AI regulatory capture risk is the chance that safety rules, audits, release gates, compute limits, or compliance procedures are shaped by leading AI firms in ways that protect incumbents while appearing to serve public safety.

## Current Synthesis
The concept remains narrower than opposition to AI regulation. The durable concern is that a rule can address a real cyber, biological, national-security, or catastrophic-risk problem while still hardening the position of companies with the largest compliance teams, closed APIs, lobbying access, cloud partnerships, and model-control infrastructure.

The newest bounded input sharpens the practical test. Capture risk rises when a lab pairs broad model restrictions or agency-style approval proposals with weak use of narrower tools such as verified access, downstream physical-world screening, voluntary standards, or public auditability. It falls when rules are technically narrow, externally contestable, focused on true frontier risks, and designed so open-source or startup developers are not forced into a closed-lab operating model.

## Key Claims
- Capture risk is highest when firms that benefit from barriers also define risk thresholds, audit methods, model-release rules, or certification practices.
- Sincere safety concern and anti-competitive effect can coexist; motive alone does not settle the governance question.
- Open-source and smaller labs are exposed when rules require centralized monitoring, rollback, insurance, or review practices that closed API providers can support more easily.
- Frontier slow-down rhetoric can function as a liability shield or moat if leading labs continue racing while asking others to accept tighter gates.
- Voluntary or self-regulatory bodies reduce capture risk only when representation is broad, scope is narrow, and the process substitutes for rather than adds to a new approval bureaucracy.
- Narrower controls such as [[FrontierModelVerifiedAccess]] and [[SyntheticBiologyScreeningSafeguards]] are important capture tests because they target misuse without general licensing of model capability.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Some frontier risks may require coordination that small teams cannot credibly provide alone. Treating every safety proposal as capture would flatten real cyber, biological, national-security, and catastrophic-risk concerns.

The sources are debate episodes with strong host opinions. Anthropic motives, state-lobbying strategy, the mechanics of Fable 5 downgrades, and the commercial implications of any standards body remain source-scoped unless corroborated by primary filings, policy text, or independent technical evidence.
