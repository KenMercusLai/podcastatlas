# Psychological Realism / 心理现实主义

Psychological realism is the literary method made explicit by [[21-hong-yu-hei-ta-si-yu-zhencheng-569042001]] through [[Stendhal|Stendhal]]'s [[RedAndBlackNovel|《红与黑》]]. The episode argues that the novel's event chain is powered by inner movement: pride, shame, ambition, love, jealousy, calculation, religious performance, and sudden sincerity.

This concept explains why plot summary can mislead. [[JulienSorel|Julien Sorel]] may look in outline like a social-climbing seducer, but the source says the novel's real achievement is showing how each gesture is overdetermined by class humiliation, self-respect, desire, fear, and fantasy.

Psychological realism also makes the women characters harder to flatten. [[MadameDeRenal|Madame de Renal]] and [[MathildeDeLaMole|Mathilde]] matter because their changing feelings, conscience, pride, and dramatic imagination shape the plot rather than merely receiving Julien's actions.

## Key Claims
- Inner motive can be a plot engine rather than decorative explanation.
- Psychological detail prevents simple moral labels from replacing interpretation.
- A character's public mask can be understood only by tracing private shame, desire, and self-defense.
- Psychological realism supports [[ClassicReadingComplexity]] because a familiar plot can keep yielding new meaning when the reader attends to emotional sequence.
