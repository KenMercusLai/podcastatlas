## Definition

实地复核式情报判断 / field-verified intelligence is the practice of testing inherited reports, surrender testimony, maps, or expectations against terrain, supply, defensive works, and observable operational conditions before treating them as a decision basis.

## Current Synthesis

The current case is Cao Cao's Yangping approach. Surrendering men reportedly say the position is difficult to hold, but direct inspection shows steep terrain and extended defenses. The mismatch matters because attack costs and food pressure are already rising. Verification does not produce immediate victory; it corrects the model enough for Cao Cao to order withdrawal before an unrelated battlefield accident changes the opportunity set.

The concept therefore separates better knowledge from guaranteed success. Field verification can reveal that optimistic intelligence is wrong and justify stopping, while later events may still create a new decision. The discipline is to update twice: first when observation disproves the report, and again when verified new conditions replace the earlier plan.

## Key Claims

- Information from defectors or surrendering actors may be useful but is shaped by limited knowledge, incentives, and local perspective.
- Terrain, fortification depth, casualties, and supply are evidence, not background details.
- Verification can rationally support withdrawal even when the campaign's final result is victory.
- A corrected assessment must remain revisable when genuinely new battlefield conditions appear.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

One compressed narrative cannot establish how formal, personal, or complete Cao Cao's inspection was, or whether the surrender reports were false, obsolete, misunderstood, or simply narrower than the later defensive situation. Direct observation can also be incomplete. The concept supports triangulation and revision, not a claim that commanders always see ground truth by visiting the field.

## What Changed

- Created a verification concept distinguishing report intake from terrain- and logistics-tested judgment.
