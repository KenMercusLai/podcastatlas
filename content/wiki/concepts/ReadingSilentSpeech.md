# Reading Silent Speech

## Definition
Reading silent speech is the episode's frame for reading as visual input that recruits speech pathways, including silent or subvocal speech activity, rather than remaining purely visual processing.

## Current Synthesis
Jarvis explains reading as a route from visual signals on the page through the eyes and visual cortex into speech pathways. People may silently speak what they read even without visible muscle movement, and laryngeal EMG can detect activity during silent speech attempts. Writing is even more distributed because it uses visual, speech-production, speech-perception, and hand-motor pathways together.

The concept matters because it links literacy to the speech-motor and auditory systems without reducing all reading to mouth movement. Reading and writing become coordinated multimodal acts: visual marks, internal speech, sound knowledge, and hand control interact.

## Key Claims
- Reading begins with visual signals but can recruit speech pathways.
- Silent reading can involve covert speech activity even when no visible speech occurs.
- Laryngeal muscle activity can appear during reading or silent speech attempts.
- Writing coordinates visual, speech-production, speech-perception, and hand-motor circuits.
- Literacy therefore sits between visual cognition, speech circuits, auditory knowledge, and motor control.

- Writing circuits: [[the-neuroscience-of-speech-language-music-dr-erich-jarvis-scim2182961996]] says writing uses visual, speech-production, speech-perception, and hand-motor pathways, as does the Essentials edit.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The full interview and Essentials edit are overlapping evidence. They do not provide a full reading-science account, dyslexia theory, literacy pedagogy, sign-language reading comparison, or evidence that every reader subvocalizes in the same way. The proposed matching of internal-speech and writing speed is source-scoped. Silent speech is one pathway in a broader reading system.
