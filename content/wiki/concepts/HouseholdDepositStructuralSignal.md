# Household Deposit Structural Signal / 居民存款结构信号

## Definition

Household deposit structural signal is the use of deposit ownership and maturity composition—not the household total alone—to infer risk preference, opportunity scarcity, and how smoothly funds circulate between households and firms.

## Current Synthesis

The source warns that bank deposits are largely created through credit, so aggregate household deposits are not simply the sum of money people chose not to spend. A household saving more can shift deposits away from firms without directly increasing system-wide deposits. The more informative observations are therefore household deposits relative to corporate deposits and term deposits relative to demand deposits.

Faster household than corporate deposit growth can indicate that money is not returning readily to enterprise activity. Faster term than demand deposit growth can indicate lower risk appetite, fewer attractive investments, or a preference for safety and delayed use. These are diagnostic signals rather than complete causal explanations.

## Key Claims

- Bank credit creation matters for interpreting total deposit quantities.
- Household saving can change sectoral ownership without changing aggregate deposits one-for-one.
- Household-versus-corporate deposit growth can reveal circulation frictions.
- Term-versus-demand deposit growth can reveal lower risk appetite or opportunity scarcity.
- Deposit structure should be combined with credit, income, investment, and spending evidence.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Deposit shifts can also reflect regulation, product pricing, payment technology, corporate financing choices, seasonal flows, wealth transfers, or classification changes. The source supplies no time series or causal decomposition, so the signals should not be read as stand-alone proof of weak confidence.
