# Fragrance Wardrobe / 香水衣橱

## Definition
Fragrance wardrobe / 香水衣橱 is the consumption pattern of owning and rotating multiple perfumes or scent products for different moods, seasons, occasions, identities, or layering effects instead of relying on one signature scent.

## Current Synthesis
[[meizhuang-xiaoyang-bianshen-duli-shangpin-pinpai-weihe-dou-zai-fenfen-buju-mini-chanpin-1011859226]] uses fragrance wardrobe to explain why small perfume bottles can become paid products. If consumers want several scents rather than one long-term signature, 10-30 ml bottles lower the total cost of building a collection and make rotation easier.

The concept links [[FragranceProductStorytelling]] to [[MiniBeautyStandaloneProduct]]. A scent wardrobe still needs stories, names, occasions, and emotional cues because buyers must know when and why each scent belongs in the set. But the source keeps the trend bounded: it does not prove that every small-bottle launch converts into high-margin repeat purchase.

## Key Claims
- Fragrance wardrobe shifts perfume demand from single-signature loyalty toward rotation and collection.
- Small bottles fit the pattern because they reduce the cash needed to own multiple scents.
- Mood, season, occasion, and layering become purchase logic, not just marketing copy.
- The pattern increases the importance of scent naming, story, and use-scene explanation.
- It can support standalone mini fragrance products even when consumers do not intend to buy the full-size version.

## Evidence
- Rotation evidence: [[meizhuang-xiaoyang-bianshen-duli-shangpin-pinpai-weihe-dou-zai-fenfen-buju-mini-chanpin-1011859226]] says more consumers change fragrance by mood, season, and occasion or layer multiple scents.
- Historical-language evidence: [[meizhuang-xiaoyang-bianshen-duli-shangpin-pinpai-weihe-dou-zai-fenfen-buju-mini-chanpin-1011859226]] notes that the wardrobe metaphor existed in the perfume industry as early as the 1920s.
- Product-format evidence: [[meizhuang-xiaoyang-bianshen-duli-shangpin-pinpai-weihe-dou-zai-fenfen-buju-mini-chanpin-1011859226]] says brands are offering 10-30 ml bottles that consumers can buy separately to build collections at lower total price.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source establishes a trend frame, not a complete fragrance-market forecast. It does not settle long-term repeat rates, brand loyalty, margin structure, or whether wardrobe behavior expands total demand rather than shifting spending among more small bottles.
