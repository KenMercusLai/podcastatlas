# Internet Blackout Public-Safety Risk

Internet blackout public-safety risk is the civilian harm that appears when a network shutdown blocks medical access, emergency communication, public alerts, police checks, and basic coordination. [[tech-20260402-0402-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260402-0402-mp-tech-pod-128]] adds the [[Iran]] case through [[AmirRashidi]]'s examples of people unable to reach doctors and police unable to check a reported license plate because they lacked internet access.

The concept turns censorship into an infrastructure problem. When a state blocks global connectivity during war, the loss is not limited to social media or news consumption; it can also degrade public services and civilian warning systems, especially when approved domestic networks cannot carry sensitive war information.

## Key Claims
- Emergency and medical services can depend on internet access even when they are not usually thought of as internet products.
- Domestic connectivity does not automatically preserve public safety if sensitive alerting, reporting, or outside communication is blocked.
- Workarounds such as VPNs, satellite internet, or local communication tools may be useful but can fail at population scale.
- Public-safety risk increases when censorship removes war-related information from the same domestic services people are expected to use.
