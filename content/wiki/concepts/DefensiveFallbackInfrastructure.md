## Definition

防御性退路基础设施 / defensive fallback infrastructure is a prepared position that preserves force and decision time when the preferred battle plan fails, withdrawal is disrupted, or transport cannot be reached safely.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1128-meiyou-heikeji-guren-ruhe-diaocha-nimingxin-lqdm57jzw9wqo_umlasz2j3vlzqh|Hanji 1128]] grounds the concept in [[LyuMeng|吕蒙]]'s proposal for [[RuxuFortressLateHan|濡须坞]]. Other commanders assume troops can land, fight, and return to their ships, so a fortified position looks like avoidable expense. Lyu Meng instead designs for the losing branch: an enemy may pursue too quickly for orderly re-embarkation, leaving soldiers exposed between battlefield and fleet.

[[SunQuan|孙权]]'s authorization converts that contingency into infrastructure. The fort's value lies not in guaranteeing victory but in preventing one unfavorable engagement from becoming irreversible collapse. It joins terrain, preparation, retreat timing, and force preservation into the campaign design before the emergency arrives.

The later campaign environment shows why preserved decision time can matter. Cao Cao's renewed pressure meets a sustained river defense, and the expected value of continuing falls as water rises and Jiangdong's naval advantage grows. This does not prove that the fort itself decides the campaign; it strengthens the narrower judgment that preparation can preserve resistance until external conditions change. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1133-sunquan-rensheng-zui-xiaosa-de-zhanyi-ruxukou-zhi-zhan-lrkz2p2fvh2fpw5owjdj7xg_dc8g|Hanji 1133]]

Hanji 1143 adds the offensive mirror at [[WanChengLujiang|皖城]]. [[LyuMeng|吕蒙]] argues that rising water enables shipborne arrival but falling water can make delayed withdrawal dangerous. The case does not add a fortification; it clarifies why access to a fallback or transport route must be evaluated across the whole campaign window rather than only at entry.

## Key Claims

- Plans should include the losing branch rather than assume successful execution and orderly withdrawal.
- A transport asset is not a fallback if troops cannot reach or board it under pressure.
- Fortification can buy time and preserve options even when it does not win the initial engagement.
- Redundancy may look wasteful in the favorable scenario but become decisive in the adverse one.
- Leaders institutionalize risk judgment when they fund preparedness before its need is visible.
- Preserved time can matter strategically because terrain, weather, supply, and relative capability continue to change during a standoff.
- A route that is available at campaign entry may cease to function as a fallback when water, weather, or enemy pressure changes.

## Evidence

- Winning-plan assumption: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1128-meiyou-heikeji-guren-ruhe-diaocha-nimingxin-lqdm57jzw9wqo_umlasz2j3vlzqh|Hanji 1128]] says commanders believe troops can simply return to ships after fighting ashore.
- Adverse-branch analysis: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1128-meiyou-heikeji-guren-ruhe-diaocha-nimingxin-lqdm57jzw9wqo_umlasz2j3vlzqh|Hanji 1128]] says Lyu Meng points to battlefield loss and fast pursuit as reasons re-embarkation may fail.
- Institutional response: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1128-meiyou-heikeji-guren-ruhe-diaocha-nimingxin-lqdm57jzw9wqo_umlasz2j3vlzqh|Hanji 1128]] says Sun Quan funds construction of the Ruxu position.
- Campaign payoff: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1133-sunquan-rensheng-zui-xiaosa-de-zhanyi-ruxukou-zhi-zhan-lrkz2p2fvh2fpw5owjdj7xg_dc8g|Hanji 1133]] describes sustained defense in the Ruxu environment until rising water and naval asymmetry make Cao Cao's withdrawal rational.
- Time-varying transport access: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1143-guren-lihun-neng-you-duo-qipa-lutfgjyzwfwwh4ovzuevene8xupy|Hanji 1143]] says Lyu Meng rejects a long siege partly because falling water could endanger return to the ships.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Fallback infrastructure has costs: labor, money, maintenance, fixed-position exposure, and possible overconfidence. The sources do not compare alternatives, establish how often the fort was used successfully, or isolate its effect from river geography, naval forces, weather, supply, command, and diplomacy. Hanji 1143's water timing and withdrawal account also remain source-scoped. A retreat position or transport route is valuable only if forces can reach, supply, command, and defend it under the actual threat and changing conditions.

## What Changed

- Extended the concept from pre-battle contingency design to the later Ruxu campaign's time-and-conditions payoff.
- Preserved the boundary that Hanji 1133 does not prove the fort alone caused withdrawal.
- Added Wan City as an offensive case showing that fallback access can decay during the campaign window.
