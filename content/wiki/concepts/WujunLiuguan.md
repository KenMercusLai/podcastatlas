## Definition

五均六管 / Five Equalizations and Six Controls is the late-[[XinDynasty|新朝]] economic program that [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-653-ba-shehuizhuyi-daihui-gudai-wangmang-youduo-shen-lo7fqi-zlujpd3cymg7uxsujy4yk|Hanji 653]] presents as [[WangMang|王莽]]'s 17 CE attempt to regulate prices, provide official credit, and put key resources and commodities under state control.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 653 treats 五均六管 as the clearest fiscal and market-control layer inside [[WangMangReforms|王莽改制]]. Its language is explicitly retro-classical: Liu Xin names the program through ancient texts, and Wang Mang's court wraps economic intervention in the authority of earlier ritual and administrative models.

The five equalizations are presented as price regulation for mountains-and-marshes resources, salt, iron, money, and cloth. The state sets official prices, intervenes when market prices rise above them, allows private exchange below the official level, and offers loans to people with transaction difficulties. The six controls extend the same logic into state management of mountains-and-marshes resources, salt, iron, money, cloth, and alcohol.

The source's synthesis is not that state price control or anti-usury policy is inherently impossible. Its claim is narrower and more institutional: a system meant to restrain wealthy merchants and protect farmers is executed largely through wealthy merchants and local officials, creating direct capture risk. Once operators collude, falsify accounts, and extract private gains, the reform fails fiscally and socially at the same time.

The episode therefore makes 五均六管 a bridge between policy design and revolt. The policy's proclaimed livelihood aims collide with harsh prohibitions, slave taxes, corvee, disaster, legal delay, and property seizure; later armed groups become intelligible not as isolated criminality but as survival responses inside overbearing and predatory governance.

## Key Claims

- 五均六管 joins price regulation, official credit, resource monopoly, commodity monopoly, and tax control into one state-led economic program.
- Its legitimacy depends on [[TuoguReformPolitics|托古改制]]: ancient textual naming makes a new intervention look like restoration of proper order.
- The stated goal is livelihood protection against merchant extraction and high-interest lending.
- Implementation is captured because wealthy merchants and local officials administer the system they are supposed to restrain.
- Fiscal failure and social failure reinforce each other: the treasury is not strengthened, while commoners face more predation, legal risk, taxes, and survival pressure.
- The program extends [[IdeologicalOvercontrol|意识形态过度控制]] into markets because morally attractive order is imposed without matching feedback, monitoring, or administrative capacity.

## Evidence

- Program creation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-653-ba-shehuizhuyi-daihui-gudai-wangmang-youduo-shen-lo7fqi-zlujpd3cymg7uxsujy4yk|Hanji 653]] says Wang Mang establishes officials in 17 CE to implement 五均 and 六管.
- Classical packaging: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-653-ba-shehuizhuyi-daihui-gudai-wangmang-youduo-shen-lo7fqi-zlujpd3cymg7uxsujy4yk|Hanji 653]] says Liu Xin derives the name from classical texts, fitting Xin's broader retro-reform atmosphere.
- Livelihood rhetoric: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-653-ba-shehuizhuyi-daihui-gudai-wangmang-youduo-shen-lo7fqi-zlujpd3cymg7uxsujy4yk|Hanji 653]] says the official purpose is to restrict merchant exploitation of farmers and suppress usury.
- Price and credit mechanism: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-653-ba-shehuizhuyi-daihui-gudai-wangmang-youduo-shen-lo7fqi-zlujpd3cymg7uxsujy4yk|Hanji 653]] describes official price setting, intervention above official prices, freer exchange below them, and state loans for transaction difficulty.
- Monopoly scope: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-653-ba-shehuizhuyi-daihui-gudai-wangmang-youduo-shen-lo7fqi-zlujpd3cymg7uxsujy4yk|Hanji 653]] says the six controls cover mountains-and-marshes resources, salt, iron, money, cloth, and alcohol.
- Capture and collusion: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-653-ba-shehuizhuyi-daihui-gudai-wangmang-youduo-shen-lo7fqi-zlujpd3cymg7uxsujy4yk|Hanji 653]] says many implementing officials are wealthy merchants who use courier travel, false accounts, and county-commandery collusion for private gain.
- Social breakdown: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-653-ba-shehuizhuyi-daihui-gudai-wangmang-youduo-shen-lo7fqi-zlujpd3cymg7uxsujy4yk|Hanji 653]] links the policy environment to harsh laws, slave taxes, corvee, drought, locusts, unresolved litigation, official property seizure, and rising banditry.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source does not provide a technical institutional history of every office, rate, product category, or regional implementation channel. It also does not prove that all later rebellion derived only from 五均六管; later wiki pages add private-coinage punishment, famine relief corruption, official-army abuse, and command failure as separate layers. The "socialism" framing remains a modern analogy used by the episode title, not the wiki's analytical category.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Hanji 653 as the focused policy mechanism inside Wang Mang's broader reform program.
- Clarified that the episode's key contribution is implementation capture: anti-merchant policy administered through merchants and local officials.
- Linked the policy to later late-Xin social breakdown without making it the sole cause of rebellion.
