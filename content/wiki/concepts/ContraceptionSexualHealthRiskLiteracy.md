# Contraception and Sexual-Health Risk Literacy / 避孕与性健康风险素养

## Definition
Contraception and sexual-health risk literacy is the ability to distinguish pregnancy prevention, infection-risk reduction, comfort, emergency response, and qualified clinical care rather than treating every product or after-the-fact intervention as interchangeable protection.

## Current Synthesis
VOL.84 organizes practical sexual-health knowledge around layered purposes. Condoms can reduce pregnancy and sexually transmitted infection risk; correct fit and use matter; lubrication can support comfort and reduce friction; and material sensitivity may require alternative products. None of these functions should be inferred from marketing labels alone.

The episode treats withdrawal as unreliable and emergency contraception as time-sensitive backup rather than routine contraception. If pregnancy occurs, decision-making and care should move to a qualified institution rather than an advertising-led provider whose low entry price may conceal charges or inadequate treatment. Persistent pain, discharge, itching, or infection concern similarly requires cause-specific assessment.

## Key Claims
- Pregnancy prevention, STI-risk reduction, lubrication, and pain reduction are related but distinct goals.
- Condoms serve both contraception and infection-risk reduction, while correct use and material compatibility matter.
- Withdrawal should not be treated as reliable contraception.
- Emergency contraception is a backup whose timing, method, side effects, and individual context matter.
- Unintended pregnancy and persistent sexual-health symptoms should be handled through qualified clinical care.
- Advertising language such as low-price or painless care is not evidence of provider quality, total cost, or safety.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is broad podcast education and does not establish a complete contraception, STI, pregnancy, abortion, vaccination, or sexual-pain protocol. Product compatibility, method effectiveness, emergency-contraception choice, pregnancy timing, pain causes, infection testing, IUD decisions, vasectomy, and HPV questions require current local guidance and individualized qualified care. The episode's proposed edible-lubricant heuristic should not replace ingredient, compatibility, and manufacturer information.
