# Disarmament Withdrawal Sequencing

Disarmament withdrawal sequencing is the implementation dilemma in [[in-arms-way-gaza-deal-sticking-points-6a71b49647235c354d3c9b74]]: [[Hamas]] wants [[Israel]] to withdraw first and then begin disarming, while Israel says it will not leave areas it controls until Hamas has completely disarmed. Each side is being asked to give up leverage before trusting the other side's next move.

The source adds two important details. Hamas argues that any arms it gives up should remain in [[Gaza]] and be stored by a Palestinian administration, while Israel wants complete disarmament before withdrawal. That turns the dispute from a generic peace-process problem into a practical question of custody, verification, timing, and enforceability.

## Key Claims
- Sequencing disputes are trust disputes expressed as calendars.
- A side that moves first may lose its main bargaining leverage before the other side has performed.
- Arms custody matters because "disarmament" can mean destruction, removal, storage, monitoring, or transfer to another authority.
- Verification procedures are not a technical afterthought; they determine whether the withdrawal condition can be judged.
