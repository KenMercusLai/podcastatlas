# China Special Economic Zone Asymmetry

China special economic zone asymmetry is the source's point that first-batch special economic zones did not start with equal area, function, strategic priority, or external adjacency. In [[no-205-chaoshan-wangshi-qiaopi-jingji-tequ-he-meiyou-denglaide-shenzhen-qiji-989721046]], [[Shenzhen]] is described as a large comprehensive special zone, while [[ShantouSpecialEconomicZone]], [[Zhuhai]], and [[Xiamen]] are framed closer to export-processing or externally oriented zones with narrower initial scope.

The concept matters because it prevents a false comparison: saying both Shantou and Shenzhen were special economic zones hides differences in land capacity, Hong Kong proximity, industrial transfer, policy ambition, and institutional room to experiment. Those differences shape whether [[DiasporaCapitalReturnLimits]] become binding and whether a city can complete [[MerchantToIndustrialCityUpgrade]].

## Key Claims
- Policy labels can conceal large differences in actual policy capacity.
- Initial area and functional scope matter because they shape what kind of firms, infrastructure, and urban system can be built.
- External adjacency to [[HongKong]] gave [[Shenzhen]] a different capital and industrial-transfer channel from [[Shantou]].
- Special-zone status did not protect Shantou from later [[RegionalAdministrativeFragmentation]] or [[ExportTaxFraudCreditCrisis]].
