## Definition

Embodied visual photography uses the camera not merely to reproduce an external scene but to extend, translate, and communicate how a particular body perceives it.

## Current Synthesis

For [[QilinPhotographer|麒麟]], visual impairment changes photography without ending it. Night blindness first made the camera a way to see stars and scenes his unaided eyes could not resolve. Manual focus later allowed changes in his sight to enter the image directly, so blur could register his bodily experience rather than count only as technical failure.

The camera also mediates relationships: making and giving a portrait can create an approach to strangers and communities. Embodied photography therefore joins assistive perception, self-expression, and social contact, while remaining subject to the ordinary limits of framing, consent, editing, and viewer interpretation.

## Key Claims

- Optical equipment can extend perception without restoring a neutral or universal view.
- Focus, blur, and exposure can communicate bodily experience as well as technical control.
- Disability may reorganize artistic method and identity rather than simply terminate practice.
- Photography can operate as a relational tool for approaching and reciprocating with strangers.
- The resulting image remains mediated; personal authenticity does not guarantee factual completeness.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

One photographer's method should not stand for all visually impaired people or all disability art. Blur is not self-interpreting, and viewers may romanticize impairment unless process and context are supplied. Camera mediation can facilitate contact without resolving consent, unequal power, or the ethics of later circulation.
