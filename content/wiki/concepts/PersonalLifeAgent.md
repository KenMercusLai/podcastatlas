# Personal Life Agent / 个人生活智能体

## Definition
A personal life agent is a consumer AI agent organized around one continuing person that turns daily-life or work intent into planning, service discovery, booking, purchasing, payment, communication, and follow-through over time.

## Current Synthesis
The Ant source establishes the service-graph version: payment accounts, order history, bills, mini-programs, and providers can give an agent real execution paths. The comparison of Town, Instinct, Grokbot, and Muse broadens the category across email, messaging, role-based bots, and social distribution. Today adds the relationship-and-delegation version: one named front agent maintains continuity while memory and initiative reduce how much context the user must repeatedly supply.

The combined evidence shows that neither context nor execution is sufficient alone. Transaction and service platforms can finish tasks but may lack the user's unfulfilled intent; content and communication platforms may see desire but lack fulfillment rights; memory can retain facts yet still assign them to the wrong person or time. [[AlexaPlus|Alexa+]] adds a household-and-hardware route, while [[DotsPersonalAgent|Dots]] adds a paid cloud-computer route inside ChatGPT. The product challenge is to join a [[PersonalAgentUnderstandingLayer|relevant understanding layer]] to reliable service execution while keeping [[AgentDelegationFriction|delegation cost]] below direct action and earning enough trust for accounts, communication, and money.

## Key Claims
- Personal life agents must complete services and follow-through, not only converse or recommend.
- The organizing unit is the person across time, so work and life boundaries can blur without making the agent merely an office tool.
- Service graphs, payment rails, messaging, email, and attention platforms contribute different parts of context and execution.
- Long-term memory adds value only when stale or noisy information is filtered into current intent.
- Progressive delegation and recoverable permissions are essential when tasks touch money, accounts, family, health, or travel.
- One continuous front agent can hide specialist routing and reduce the user's context-management burden.
- Broad personal context still needs deep recurring workflows and must pass four coupled thresholds: operational reliability, ecosystem access, continuing personalization, and security.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources are strategic discussions and company interviews rather than adoption studies. They do not show that users will grant sufficient data, payment authority, or cross-platform access, nor that current products can complete long chains reliably. Office, household, and personal agents are not cleanly separated: one continuing assistant may span all three while shared devices complicate identity and permission. Platform scale, hardware reach, downloads, and memory volume do not guarantee useful understanding when data is siloed, stale, misattributed, behaviorally noisy, or shared among household members. Subscription, advertising, and transaction models can also conflict with the assistant's duty to the user. Product-market fit, willingness to pay, reliability, and business model remain unsettled.

## What Changed
- Added Dots's paid cloud-computer route alongside consumer, messaging, service-graph, and household-hardware strategies.
- Consolidated maturity around operational reliability, ecosystem access, continuing personalization, and security.
- Added advertising and compute availability to the unresolved business-model boundary.
