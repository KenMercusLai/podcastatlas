## Definition
Antarctic climate services are the climate-regulating benefits attributed to [[Antarctica]] and the [[SouthernOcean]], especially reflective ice, ocean carbon absorption, and stored land ice that helps limit sea-level-rise damage.

## Current Synthesis
The source treats these services as the largest part of Antarctica's economic value. Tourism and fisheries are concrete and market-like, but climate regulation is the durable public benefit that makes non-development economically meaningful. The concept therefore links environmental protection to avoided global damage rather than to scenery alone.

## Key Claims
- Reflective Antarctic ice cools the planet by sending sunlight back rather than absorbing it as heat.
- The [[SouthernOcean]] contributes value by absorbing carbon dioxide and helping regulate climate.
- Land ice storage matters economically because ice loss would raise sea levels and damage people, infrastructure, and agriculture elsewhere.
- These services are hard to sell directly, making [[NonMarketEnvironmentalValuation]] necessary if they are to enter economic decision-making.
- The services create a commons problem because benefits are global while damage may be caused by many dispersed actors.

## Evidence
- Ice albedo: [[the-continent-nobody-owns-everyone-benefits-from-summer-school]] says Antarctica's bright white ice cap reflects sunlight and helps keep the planet cool.
- Ocean carbon: [[the-continent-nobody-owns-everyone-benefits-from-summer-school]] says the Southern Ocean absorbs large amounts of carbon dioxide.
- Avoided damage: [[the-continent-nobody-owns-everyone-benefits-from-summer-school]] links lost regulating services to unstable climate, reduced agriculture, extreme events, sea-level rise, and major damages.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode reports an economic valuation but does not supply a full climate model, discount-rate analysis, or sensitivity range. The concept should remain tied to source-level evidence until more climate-science and valuation sources are added.
