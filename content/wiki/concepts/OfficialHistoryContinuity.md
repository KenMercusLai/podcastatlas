## Definition
Official history continuity is the claim that dynastic history writing preserves institutional memory and shared historical values across regime change, even when the political rulers themselves change.

## Current Synthesis
Hanji 591-2 presents official-history continuity as one reason Chinese historical writing matters for reading Zizhi Tongjian. The episode treats the practice of one dynasty compiling the history of a predecessor as more than record keeping: it becomes a civilizational habit in which power, legitimacy, lessons, and inherited values are organized into a continuous archive. This is an interpretive claim from the source, not a full theory of Chinese historiography.

## Key Claims
- Dynastic history writing is presented as a governance and memory practice, not only as retrospective scholarship.
- Successive official histories create a continuity frame across regime replacement.
- The continuity claim helps explain why Zizhi Tongjian can draw lessons from many dynasties for later rulers.
- Official continuity does not remove source criticism; each text still has form, selection, and evidentiary limits.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source's continuity language is an interpretive frame and should not erase discontinuity, factional selection, textual loss, court ideology, or later editorial politics. The wiki keeps the claim as source-scoped unless supported by more specialized historiography pages.
