# Self-Abandonment Boundary

## Definition
The self-abandonment boundary is the relationship limit at which love, empathy, pleasing, or care begins to require overriding one's own perception, truth, needs, or agency in order to regulate another person.

## Current Synthesis
The source applies [[IntegrityAsInnerCoherence]] to romantic and family relationships. Beck distinguishes love that wants freedom for the beloved from codependent patterns in which one person assumes responsibility for the other's happiness, consumes the other's energy, or treats distress as proof that a boundary is cruel. The practical diagnostic is to find the first moment one knew that staying agreeable required leaving oneself.

Changing the pattern can trigger resistance. The episode uses “extinction burst” for the temporary escalation that may occur when expected overgiving stops. The response is not indifference: [[EmpathyBoundaries]] preserve self-other distinction and emotional regulation so compassion can remain present without requiring matched suffering or false compliance.

## Key Claims
- Love does not require surrendering one's own truth, agency, or boundaries.
- Repeated responsibility for another person's happiness can turn care into codependent regulation.
- Early bodily unease may reveal a boundary breach before the relationship story fully explains it.
- Empathy remains healthier when self and other stay distinguishable and emotion remains regulatable.
- Pushback can intensify when overgiving stops, so resistance alone does not prove the new boundary is wrong.
- Honest refusal may be kinder than continuing to provide a false or resentful version of oneself.

## Evidence
- Love and self-abandonment - [[access-your-best-self-with-mind-body-practices-belief-testing-imagination-dr-martha-beck-scim3796933031]] responds to Huberman's relationship pattern by distinguishing freeing love from self-erasure.
- First breach - [[access-your-best-self-with-mind-body-practices-belief-testing-imagination-dr-martha-beck-scim3796933031]] recommends locating the earliest moment a person knew they were overriding an important boundary.
- Happiness responsibility - [[access-your-best-self-with-mind-body-practices-belief-testing-imagination-dr-martha-beck-scim3796933031]] argues that one person cannot make another person happy and uses parenting as a case for preserving agency.
- Boundary pushback - [[access-your-best-self-with-mind-body-practices-belief-testing-imagination-dr-martha-beck-scim3796933031]] uses the extinction-burst analogy for escalation after a familiar pattern of giving stops.
- Regulated empathy - [[access-your-best-self-with-mind-body-practices-belief-testing-imagination-dr-martha-beck-scim3796933031]] distinguishes compassion from automatically taking on another person's suffering.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The extinction-burst analogy comes from behavioral language and should not be used to label every disagreement or dismiss legitimate harm caused by a boundary's timing, content, or delivery. Dependence is not automatically codependence, and caregiving duties vary across illness, disability, parenting, culture, and material circumstance. Safety planning and professional support may be necessary in coercive or violent relationships.
