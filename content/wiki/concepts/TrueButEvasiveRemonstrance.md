## Definition

[[TrueButEvasiveRemonstrance|真实但避重就轻的进谏 / true but evasive remonstrance]] is corrective speech that says factually true things while avoiding the urgent, difficult, or structurally decisive problem that public duty requires the speaker to name.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-467-guanchang-li-jiang-zhenhua-shuo-shihua-you-duo-nan-ls_zlyqolvpfttspah8wery4e3fs|Hanji 467]] defines the pattern through [[GongYuWesternHan|贡禹]]'s early memorial to [[HanYuandi|汉元帝]]. Gong Yu's advice to reduce palace women, horses, rare animals, construction, and waste is not false, and Yuan partially accepts it. The criticism is about priority: [[SimaGuang|司马光]] says a loyal minister should first help the ruler resolve difficult and urgent problems.

In this source, Yuan's urgent danger is not only excess spending. It is the ruler's weak judgment under奸佞, slanderers, and corrupt favorites. Gong Yu's speech therefore protects his image as an adviser while leaving the main political hazard untouched. The episode contrasts that with [[WeiZhengTang|魏征]] and [[FanZhongyan|范仲淹]], where direct warning becomes part of official responsibility rather than optional personal bravery.

## Key Claims

- Truthfulness at the sentence level does not guarantee truthful public duty if the speaker deliberately avoids the decisive problem.
- Evasive remonstrance can be harder to identify than flattery because its surface claims are reasonable and may produce partial policy gains.
- The ethical failure lies in priority and omission: the adviser chooses a safer true topic while leaving the urgent danger unnamed.
- Ruler receptiveness to mild correction can coexist with deeper feedback failure when advisers do not test the hard issue.
- Direct truth-telling depends on political environment, but the source still treats "knowing and not speaking" as an official responsibility failure.
- The pattern is adjacent to opportunism but narrower: it concerns truthful advice that functions as self-protection.

## Evidence

Priority failure:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-467-guanchang-li-jiang-zhenhua-shuo-shihua-you-duo-nan-ls_zlyqolvpfttspah8wery4e3fs|Hanji 467]] says Sima Guang criticizes Gong Yu because loyal ministers should handle hard and urgent problems before easier or less urgent ones.

Truthful surface:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-467-guanchang-li-jiang-zhenhua-shuo-shihua-you-duo-nan-ls_zlyqolvpfttspah8wery4e3fs|Hanji 467]] says Gong Yu's thrift advice about palace women, horses, animals, construction, and public burden is true enough for Yuan to adopt parts of it.

Avoided danger:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-467-guanchang-li-jiang-zhenhua-shuo-shihua-you-duo-nan-ls_zlyqolvpfttspah8wery4e3fs|Hanji 467]] says Yuan's deeper problem is weak decisiveness and the influence of奸佞 and corrupt favorites, which Gong Yu does not address.

Public-duty contrast:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-467-guanchang-li-jiang-zhenhua-shuo-shihua-you-duo-nan-ls_zlyqolvpfttspah8wery4e3fs|Hanji 467]] uses Wei Zheng and Fan Zhongyan as contrast cases for officials who treat dangerous direct speech as part of public responsibility.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source does not prove Gong Yu's private motive; it presents a Sima Guang-centered and host-interpreted moral judgment. Direct criticism of favorites can be risky, and a safer true memorial may still produce real public benefit. The concept should therefore mark a tension rather than erase the positive thrift layer: Gong Yu's advice can be beneficial and still evasive relative to the court's central danger.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Hanji 467's distinction between factually true advice and responsibility to name the urgent problem.
