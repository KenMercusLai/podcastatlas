# Emotional Contagion / 情绪感染

## Definition
Emotional contagion / 情绪感染 is the rapid transmission or mirroring of affect through expression, voice, attention, group tone, and interaction before reflective judgment fully catches up.

## Current Synthesis
The social-psychology sources show emotion traveling without requiring explicit agreement. Political anger, fear, charisma, disgust, ridicule, and contempt can enter a viewer's bodily response before identity or reasoning reclassifies the experience. This makes media literacy partly a practice of noticing felt intensity rather than evaluating propositions alone.

In live comedy, contagion becomes reciprocal. [[TomSegura]] describes performers inheriting a room made loose by laughter or tense by failure, then affecting it in turn through play, anxiety, vulnerability, voice, facial cues, and responsiveness. [[PerformerAudienceStateCoupling]] therefore extends contagion from one-way exposure into a feedback loop: the audience changes the performer while the performer changes the audience.

## Key Claims
- Emotion can travel faster than explicit belief or agreement.
- Anger, fear, disgust, charisma, ridicule, and shared group tone can shape judgment before reflection.
- Political or group identity can reinterpret an initial response after it occurs.
- Contempt can spread through visible disgust and social distance, not only argument.
- In live performance, affective transmission is reciprocal rather than simply broadcast.
- Media and performance literacy must notice felt intensity, room state, and group feedback as well as content.

## Evidence
- Political affect - [[123-renxing-shiyan-dangxin-ni-ziji-779479569]] discusses political-speech research in which participants imitate expression and anger or fear can spread despite reflective resistance.
- Contempt and disgust - [[145-jidu-yu-bishi-wo-gai-ruhe-duobi-zhe-shuangxiang-de-dujian-882378647]] connects visible disgust, ridicule, comparison, and shared group tone to environmental contempt.
- Performance feedback - [[the-science-art-of-comedy-creativity-tom-segura-scim7538555033]] has Segura describe a laughing or failed room as a state the next performer inherits and must either use or rebuild.
- Transmission channels - [[the-science-art-of-comedy-creativity-tom-segura-scim7538555033]] discusses sound, faces, anxiety, vulnerability, and broad group energy as channels between stage and audience.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not establish one universal contagion mechanism or quantify effect sizes. Similar expressions can reflect imitation, conformity, shared appraisal, venue acoustics, selection effects, or deliberate performance. A unified crowd is a lived description, not evidence that every member has the same internal state, and emotion transmission does not settle whether the underlying belief is true.
