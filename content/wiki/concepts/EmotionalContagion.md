# Emotional Contagion / 情绪感染

## Definition
Emotional contagion / 情绪感染 is the rapid transmission or mirroring of affect through expression, voice, attention, group tone, and interaction before reflective judgment fully catches up.

## Current Synthesis
The social-psychology sources show emotion traveling without requiring explicit agreement. Political anger, fear, charisma, disgust, ridicule, and contempt can enter a viewer's bodily response before identity or reasoning reclassifies the experience. This makes media literacy partly a practice of noticing felt intensity rather than evaluating propositions alone.

In live comedy, contagion becomes reciprocal. [[TomSegura]] describes performers inheriting a room made loose by laughter or tense by failure, then affecting it in turn through play, anxiety, vulnerability, voice, facial cues, and responsiveness. [[PerformerAudienceStateCoupling]] therefore extends contagion from one-way exposure into a feedback loop: the audience changes the performer while the performer changes the audience.

Conflict settings add another transmission case: fear or anger can recruit friends, professionals, media audiences, or political groups as “negative advocates” before they know the whole situation. In this branch, emotional transmission is not evidence that the originating account is false; it is a reason to separate felt urgency from independent fact-finding.

Kross adds uncertainty and response quality. Emotion can spread within seconds when people look to others to interpret an ambiguous situation, and digital environments can accelerate expression while removing delay and face-or-voice feedback. His proposed group response—validation, curiosity, humility, perspective taking, and orientation toward social good—aims to make the emotional state discussable without treating intensity as sufficient evidence.

## Key Claims
- Emotion can travel faster than explicit belief or agreement.
- Anger, fear, disgust, charisma, ridicule, and shared group tone can shape judgment before reflection.
- Political or group identity can reinterpret an initial response after it occurs.
- Contempt can spread through visible disgust and social distance, not only argument.
- In live performance, affective transmission is reciprocal rather than simply broadcast.
- Conflict emotion can recruit allies whose certainty exceeds their information.
- Media and performance literacy must notice felt intensity, uncertainty, low-feedback digital channels, room state, and group feedback as well as content.

## Evidence
- Political affect - [[123-renxing-shiyan-dangxin-ni-ziji-779479569]] discusses political-speech research in which participants imitate expression and anger or fear can spread despite reflective resistance.
- Contempt and disgust - [[145-jidu-yu-bishi-wo-gai-ruhe-duobi-zhe-shuangxiang-de-dujian-882378647]] connects visible disgust, ridicule, comparison, and shared group tone to environmental contempt.
- Performance feedback - [[the-science-art-of-comedy-creativity-tom-segura-scim7538555033]] has Segura describe a laughing or failed room as a state the next performer inherits and must either use or rebuild.
- Transmission channels - [[the-science-art-of-comedy-creativity-tom-segura-scim7538555033]] discusses sound, faces, anxiety, vulnerability, and broad group energy as channels between stage and audience.
- Conflict recruitment - [[how-to-deal-with-high-conflict-people-bill-eddy-scim8877999828]] describes “negative advocates” and professionals becoming emotionally hooked by fear or anger before seeing the full dispute.
- Uncertainty and wise dialogue - [[how-to-control-your-inner-voice-increase-your-resilience-dr-ethan-kross-scim5809773417]] links rapid contagion to uncertainty and recommends validation, humility, curiosity, perspective taking, and social-good orientation in difficult groups.
- Digital acceleration - [[how-to-control-your-inner-voice-increase-your-resilience-dr-ethan-kross-scim5809773417]] contrasts slower embodied sharing with social media's reduced delay and interpersonal feedback.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not establish one universal contagion mechanism or quantify effect sizes. Similar expressions can reflect imitation, conformity, shared appraisal, uncertainty reduction, venue acoustics, selection effects, deliberate performance, or accurate response to real danger. A unified crowd is a lived description, not evidence that every member has the same internal state. Emotion transmission proves neither that an underlying belief is true nor that it is false, so de-escalation must not become automatic disbelief of a person reporting harm. Validation and curiosity are dialogue conditions, not substitutes for safety action or independent fact-finding.

## What Changed
- Added conflict-driven advocate recruitment as a third transmission setting alongside media and live performance.
- Clarified that emotional intensity warrants independent fact-finding rather than automatic belief or disbelief.
- Added uncertainty, low-feedback digital expression, and wise-dialogue practices.
