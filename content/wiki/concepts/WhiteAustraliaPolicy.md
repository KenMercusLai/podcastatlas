# White Australia Policy

## Definition

The White Australia policy was a long-running Australian system of racially restrictive immigration law and administration, exemplified by the Immigration Restriction Act and discretionary language tests.

## Current Synthesis

The episode places the policy at federation's core rather than outside Australia's progressive self-image. Early voting, education, pension, and wage reforms could coexist with an immigration regime designed to exclude non-European entrants. The Menzies era begins the policy's gradual dismantling, showing change within rather than an immediate clean break from exclusion.

## Key Claims

- Racial restriction was embedded in early federal nation-building.
- Language testing provided an administrative mechanism for selective exclusion.
- Egalitarian welfare for insiders did not amount to universal equality.
- Strategic anxiety about Asia overlapped with domestic racial politics.
- Dismantling was gradual and had begun under Menzies before later abolition.
