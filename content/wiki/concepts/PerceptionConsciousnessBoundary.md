# Perception-Consciousness Boundary

## Definition
The perception-consciousness boundary separates an organism's capacity to detect, value, and respond to environmental conditions from evidence that there is a subjective experience associated with those capacities.

## Current Synthesis
The source uses plants to challenge the assumption that sophisticated sensing and problem-solving require a human-like nervous system. Roots following gradients, responses to sound, kin-sensitive growth, self-versus-other discrimination, and disrupted behavior under anesthetic gases make plant agency scientifically interesting, but they do not demonstrate that it feels like something to be a plant.

The boundary also prevents the opposite error: lack of language, cortex, or a human-style self should not be treated as proof that an organism lacks consciousness. Consciousness may be one implementation of adaptive sensitivity rather than the only one, and self-awareness may be a separable layer rather than a prerequisite for all experience.

## Key Claims
- Environmental sensing, valence, learning-like adaptation, and problem-solving are not by themselves proof of subjective experience.
- Plant behavior broadens the study of agency without settling plant consciousness.
- A human cortical standard risks excluding unfamiliar forms of experience by definition.
- Consciousness and self-consciousness should be separated; experience may persist when self-centered organization weakens.
- Animal consciousness becomes more plausible under embodied theories, but neural anatomy alone still does not provide direct access to experience.

## Evidence
- Plant sensing - [[what-humans-animals-and-plants-tell-us-about-consciousness-michael-pollan-scim4063268713]] describes roots tracking nutrients, responses to sound, kin-sensitive behavior, and self-versus-other shade discrimination.
- Anesthesia boundary - [[what-humans-animals-and-plants-tell-us-about-consciousness-michael-pollan-scim4063268713]] reports that anesthetic gases interrupt Venus flytrap behavior while noting that anesthesia does not itself prove consciousness.
- Self distinction - [[what-humans-animals-and-plants-tell-us-about-consciousness-michael-pollan-scim4063268713]] uses meditation, psychedelic states, and waking transitions to separate experience from a fully organized sense of self.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The supplied note does not provide experimental methods, replication status, effect sizes, or competing interpretations for the plant studies. Goal-directed language can be a useful evolutionary shorthand without implying foresight or intention. Anesthetic sensitivity, complex behavior, and biological self/non-self discrimination are correlates or functional capacities, not direct reports of phenomenal experience.

## What Changed
- Added a bounded distinction between plant agency and demonstrated consciousness.
- Added self-awareness as a separable layer rather than a universal consciousness criterion.
- Preserved uncertainty across plants, animals, and unfamiliar biological systems.
