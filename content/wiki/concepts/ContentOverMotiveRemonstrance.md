# Content-Over-Motive Remonstrance / 只问对错不问动机的纳谏

Content-over-motive remonstrance / 只问对错不问动机的纳谏 is the advice-taking principle [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-42-gei-mengchangjun-ti-yijian-de-houguo-lr1jp0tpiaboaca3j5-yvyhyn4vf]] draws from [[Mengchangjun|孟尝君 / 田文]]. The principle is that a ruler or patron should test a corrective claim by whether it identifies a real fault and prevents a real mistake, not only by whether the speaker's motives are pure.

The episode's case is [[GongsunXu|公孙须]]. He advises Mengchang Jun to refuse the Chu king's ivory bed after [[DengTuzhi|邓图直]] privately asks him for help and offers a reward. Mengchang Jun accepts the advice, learns about the private bargain, and still posts a notice encouraging anyone who can promote his reputation and stop his errors to speak up.

This differs from [[AllusiveRemonstrance|隐语进谏]]. Gongsun Xu does not mainly hide a criticism inside coded speech; he gives a practical reputation-and-precedent argument. It also differs from ordinary [[CourtFeedbackCollapse|court feedback collapse]] repair because the barrier is not fear alone but moral screening: if leaders require spotless motives before hearing correction, they may reject usable information.

## Key Claims
- Advice quality and adviser purity are related but separable.
- A mixed motive can still carry a correct diagnosis.
- Screening advice only by the speaker's private interest can reproduce feedback failure.
- The listener still has to judge content; motive-blind listening is not automatic acceptance.
- In this source, Mengchang Jun's strength lies in extracting use from flawed people, which partly offsets but does not erase [[SimaGuang|司马光]]'s criticism of indiscriminate retainer patronage.
