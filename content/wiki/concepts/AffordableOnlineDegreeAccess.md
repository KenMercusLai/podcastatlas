## Definition

Affordable online degree access is the use of lower-cost remote delivery to expand entry into a credentialed academic program while claiming continuity in faculty, course content, and academic standards.

## Current Synthesis

The bounded case is [[GeorgiaTech|Georgia Tech]]'s online master's in computer science. [[CharlesIsbell]] presents it as a large-scale access intervention: the program reportedly grew to roughly 11,000 students in under six years, cost about $6,500 rather than approximately $46,000 on campus, and sought talent beyond conventional geographic and financial channels, including in sub-Saharan Africa.

Price and reach are necessary but incomplete measures of access. Shared professors, materials, and standards support the program's claim to academic continuity, yet the episode does not supply completion, learning, support, labor-market, or distributional outcomes. The durable synthesis is therefore that online delivery can widen the feasible applicant pool without making format equivalence or equitable results automatic.

## Key Claims

- A sharply lower price and remote delivery can remove geographic and financial barriers to graduate education.
- Shared faculty, materials, and standards are stronger evidence of continuity than credential branding alone.
- Rapid enrollment growth demonstrates demand and reach, not by itself learning quality, completion, or career value.
- International recruitment can recognize talent excluded by conventional campus geography while creating new support and infrastructure questions.
- Access should be evaluated across admission, participation, support, completion, and outcomes rather than enrollment alone.

## Evidence

### Lower-cost scale

- [[return-to-the-404-return-to-the-404]] reports Isbell's comparison between the online and campus price and the online program's growth to roughly 11,000 students.

### Academic continuity

- [[return-to-the-404-return-to-the-404]] attributes the same-professor, same-material, and same-standard claim to Isbell.

### Wider talent pool

- [[return-to-the-404-return-to-the-404]] describes Georgia Tech's effort to identify and educate computing talent in sub-Saharan Africa.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source does not provide audited cost, admissions, retention, completion, learning, debt, employment, or demographic outcome data. It also does not examine internet access, time zones, student services, assessment integrity, employer perceptions, or whether the campus and online experiences are equivalent beyond faculty, materials, and stated standards. The program is one institutional case, not a universal result for online education.

## What Changed

- Created a layered access model separating price and enrollment reach from academic and career outcomes.
