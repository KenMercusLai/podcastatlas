# Older-Adult Healthcare Avoidance / 老年人就医回避

## Definition
Older-adult healthcare avoidance is delayed screening, examination, follow-up or treatment when fear, cost, information, logistics, family roles, low symptom visibility and threatened control make non-attendance feel safer than care.

## Current Synthesis
The sources resist treating avoidance as a simple knowledge deficit. A parent may want health while fearing pain, invasive procedures, a serious diagnosis, expense, unfamiliar hospital workflows, dependence on a child or evidence of aging. Old information and peer anecdotes can reinforce delay, especially when disease is asymptomatic and the burden of treatment is immediate while the benefit is probabilistic or future-facing.

VOL.75 makes the pattern concrete in oral care. A person may normalize never having had professional cleaning, missing teeth, poor denture fit, bleeding, looseness or smoking-related staining because change has been slow or because tooth loss is treated as inevitable with age. Yet low pain does not establish low risk, and restoration does not remove the need for maintenance.

The practical implication is diagnostic: before repeating a medical conclusion, the family needs to identify what is actually being avoided. Cost opacity, navigation, fear of procedures, bad news and loss of agency require different responses. Delay can be consequential in progressive disease, while specific treatment windows and outcomes remain source-scoped.

## Key Claims
- Healthcare avoidance can coexist with a strong wish to remain healthy.
- Fear may attach to diagnosis, pain, procedures, expense, workflow, dependence or loss of control.
- Old information, fragmented media and peer anecdotes can outweigh current advice when they better fit hopes or fears.
- Asymptomatic and slowly progressive conditions are vulnerable to delay because present care burdens are more visible than future harm.
- Dental loss, bleeding, looseness, poor prosthesis fit or lack of prior cleaning can become normalized as aging rather than treated as assessable problems.
- Digital, spatial and procedural complexity can turn an intention to seek care into non-attendance.

- Maintenance delay: [[vol-75-kouqiangke-bama-xiya-ma-fanxiang-zhuanfa-jiazuqun-rang-tamen-zhonghui-nianqing-you-juezhao-loerbxipaqszz4ggchblzsnvtxo9|VOL.75]] identifies food trapping, bleeding, looseness and bite discomfort as review signals after restoration.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources are public-education discussions, not representative studies of older adults. Avoidance may also reflect prior medical harm, disability, transport limits, inaccessible services, poverty, caregiving duties, cultural context or reasoned refusal after informed discussion. Family concern does not cancel the older person's agency, and urgency, screening choice and treatment decisions require qualified clinical context.

## What Changed
- Added oral care as a case where slow change and age stereotypes can normalize treatable loss or maintenance problems.
- Clarified that low pain and long familiarity do not by themselves establish low risk.
