# Wartime Coalition Accountability

## Definition

Wartime Coalition Accountability names the way military failure, press scandal, ministerial rivalry, and coalition formation can reassign responsibility while a campaign is still unfolding.

## Current Synthesis

The Gallipoli episode shows accountability under wartime strain as both necessary and distorting. [[WinstonChurchill]] is removed from the Admiralty after Fisher's resignation, the shell-shortage scandal, criticism from colleagues, and Asquith's coalition formation, but the campaign itself continues.

The concept captures a split outcome: political systems can punish or sideline a visible sponsor without immediately fixing the operational problem that created the crisis.

## Key Claims

- Wartime accountability can be triggered by overlapping battlefield failure, press exposure, and elite conflict.
- Coalition formation can stabilize government while obscuring or delaying campaign decisions.
- Removing one minister may satisfy political pressure without producing a clear strategic choice.
- Public blame can simplify responsibility even when many actors approved or prolonged a campaign.
- Delayed political response can materially extend soldiers' exposure to battlefield conditions.

## Evidence

- Cabinet and press crisis: [[676-the-first-world-war-churchills-calamity-part-6-glt1275431911]] links Fisher's anti-Churchill pressure, the Times shell story, Lloyd George criticism, and naval-officer hostility.
- Personnel consequence: [[676-the-first-world-war-churchills-calamity-part-6-glt1275431911]] says Asquith formed a coalition and removed Churchill from the Admiralty.
- Operational delay: [[676-the-first-world-war-churchills-calamity-part-6-glt1275431911]] says Ian Hamilton's reinforcement warning went unanswered for weeks because the War Council was consumed by political crisis.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept is currently anchored in one British 1915 case. It should not be treated as a full theory of coalition government or civil-military accountability.

## What Changed

- Added a Gallipoli-derived concept for wartime blame, coalition repair, and delayed operational decision-making.
