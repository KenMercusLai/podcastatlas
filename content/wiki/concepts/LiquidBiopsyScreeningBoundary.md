# Liquid Biopsy Screening Boundary

## Definition
Liquid biopsy screening boundary is the distinction between promising blood, stool, biomarker, and algorithmic cancer-screening tools and their current limits as replacements for validated diagnostic pathways.

## Current Synthesis
The Ding Kefeng episode is optimistic about future blood tests, stool tests, multi-indicator panels, and AI algorithms that could identify high-risk people and reduce unnecessary colonoscopy. It is also explicit that current tumor markers and immature commercial tests should not be oversold. The boundary is therefore a "screen and concentrate risk" frame: new tools may improve routing, but colonoscopy and validated clinical follow-up still anchor colorectal-cancer diagnosis.

## Key Claims
- Single tumor markers cannot reliably rule out cancer.
- Future liquid-biopsy-style tools may work as multi-indicator risk filters rather than stand-alone answers.
- AI algorithms may help identify high-risk groups for further testing.
- Commercial or immature screening products need evidence and approval before routine trust.
- Emerging tests should reduce unnecessary procedures only when they preserve detection quality and follow-up clarity.

- Maturity boundary: [[vol-203-kao-bingmeishi-tongbian-yi-jinzhang-jiu-fuxie-nide-cuipi-changdao-xuyao-zhefen-shiyongshu-lvtxc2z3hn5ukh3va9rzijremcih]] says current company-promoted tests are not mature enough for uncritical routine use.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not reject research or future adoption. It only warns that promising screening technologies need evidence, regulatory review, clinical workflows, and clear follow-up before replacing established tests.
