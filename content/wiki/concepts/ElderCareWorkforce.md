# Elder-Care Workforce / 养老照护人才

Elder-care workforce / 养老照护人才 is the trained labor force needed to support older people in institutions, communities, and homes. [[ep241-xiaoqi-hezuo-shi-xinyidai-de-tiefanwan-ma-lnmpsbvoqbexeardxyx88hywufig]] develops it through [[LiXiaojie|李小杰]]'s reporting on [[MinzhengVocationalUniversity|民政职业大学]], [[TaikangHome|太康之家]], and elder-care institutions that want students to encounter real older people before final-year internships.

The source stresses that elder care is not only a labor-shortage category. Students must learn how to help people who are partially disabled, cognitively impaired, emotionally distressed, or fully dependent; they must also decide whether they can accept intimate frontline care, stigma, pay, and long-run progression.

## Key Claims
- Real care scenes matter because mannequins and classmates cannot fully simulate frailty, dementia, refusal, emotion, toileting, eating, and family context.
- High demand does not automatically solve retention if students view frontline care as low-status or too bodily demanding.
- Higher-quality institutions may improve wages and training, but sector-wide dignity and progression remain central.
- [[AIAndRoboticElderCareLimits|AI and robotics]] can monitor, assist, or redesign equipment, but they do not remove the need for human contact and professional care judgment.
- Elder-care workforce development connects vocational education to social-policy capacity, not only company hiring.
