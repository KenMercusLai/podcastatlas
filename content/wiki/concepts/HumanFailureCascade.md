# Human Failure Cascade

## Definition

A human failure cascade is a sequence in which an initial personal or social breakdown is amplified by later failures of judgment, justice, care, or correction, producing consequences far beyond the first event.

## Current Synthesis

Part 1 of [[TheAlabamaMurders]] borrows the cascade model from infrastructure failure: one defect does not mechanically determine catastrophe, but it changes the conditions under which later safeguards must work. The series applies that model to [[CharlesSennett]]'s concealed affairs, abuse, debt, mental-health crisis, and religious-social pressure, then signals that later failures of character, justice, and compassion will compound the harm.

The model is useful only if it preserves agency and distinct responsibility. A background pressure is not the same as a violent choice, a disputed conviction is not the same as a sentencing rule, and an execution protocol is not the same as the originating crime. “Cascade” names dependency and amplification, not inevitability or equal blame.

## Key Claims

- A serious outcome can emerge from multiple linked failures rather than one sufficient cause.
- Early concealment or unresolved crisis can narrow later options and increase the burden on safeguards.
- Character, institutional judgment, and compassion can fail at different stages and through different actors.
- Later systems may amplify the original harm when they treat prior decisions as settled inputs.
- Cascade analysis should preserve counterfactual intervention points rather than imply inevitability.
- Causal connection does not make every actor, decision, or harm morally equivalent.

## Evidence

### Initial condition and amplification

- [[the-alabama-murders-part-1-the-true-church]] compares the case to cascading technical failures and locates the initial human crisis in Sennett's concealed private collapse.

### Distinct failure domains

- [[the-alabama-murders-part-1-the-true-church]] frames the seven-part series around failures of character, justice, and compassion rather than one mechanical defect.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept currently rests on one episode's organizing analogy, not a formal causal model or comparative study. Infrastructure metaphors can overstate sequence, understate choice, or make later events seem inevitable. The source does not establish the relative causal weight of religious culture, mental illness, debt, abuse, affairs, criminal decisions, legal procedure, or punishment institutions. Each link requires its own evidence and responsibility analysis.

## What Changed

- Created a framework for compounding human and institutional failure while preserving agency and unequal responsibility.
