# Resistance Axis Backfire / 抵抗之弧反噬

Resistance axis backfire / 抵抗之弧反噬 is the episode's diagnosis that [[Iran]]'s regional proxy and allied network became less like a disciplined strategic asset and more like an expensive, semi-autonomous, transactional system. In [[ep251-yilang-kunju-sishinian-zhanlue-xuanze-de-daijia-yu-xianshi-lp8vyonyugtd2ozymgof-mim9zkd]], [[LiuYiSanlian|刘仪]] argues that Iran repeatedly found tactical leverage through such networks, but the long-run result was strategic exposure.

The concept extends [[ProxyConflictSpoilerRisk]] from a diplomacy problem into an originating-state problem. Proxies and allies can complicate an enemy's plans, but they can also make Iran responsible for conflicts it cannot fully control, invite attacks, create sanctions exposure, and turn revolutionary influence into patronage, smuggling, and survival business.

The source's Syria example is kept source-scoped: Liu Yi describes a border zone near Homs where Hezbollah and IRGC-linked influence allegedly became tied to villas, smuggling, and cross-border trade before the Assad regime's collapse. In the episode's logic, that case shows how "exporting revolution" can decay into network maintenance and private benefit.

## Key Claims
- Proxy networks can begin as deterrence and depth but later become fiscal, reputational, and command liabilities.
- Tactical success does not equal strategic coherence if each tool creates new exposure.
- Revolutionary influence can decay into local business, smuggling, and patronage when ideological attraction weakens.
- The more autonomous the network becomes, the harder it is for formal diplomacy to bind actual battlefield behavior.
