## Definition

Urban wildlife coexistence / 城市野生动物共存 is the practical condition in which wild animals live, feed, move, reproduce, or rest inside human-built city systems while residents, tourists, managers, engineers, and media repeatedly respond to them.

## Current Synthesis

The current evidence frames coexistence as imperfect rather than sentimental. [[ep282-bu-wanmei-de-gongcun-dang-yesheng-dongwu-chengwei-chengshi-xin-jumin-lg31-8lajmed4k4mhg5iz7hh8r1a]] shows animals becoming city “new residents” through habitat change, tourism, leftover food, cat feeding, greenbelts, missing predators, and public attention. The same animal can be a source of delight, nuisance, injury, disease anxiety, conservation concern, or management cost depending on where and how people encounter it.

[[TorontoRaccoons]] make infrastructure adaptation part of the same synthesis. Compost bins designed to resist raccoons become part of the animals' environment and an iterative contest between engineering and exploratory persistence. The case also cautions against reading urban success as a simple intelligence score: physical manipulation and uninhibited persistence can be ecologically effective even when standard cognition tests produce weak results.

Coexistence therefore sits between [[UrbanEcology]] and [[ConservationIntervention]]. It asks how to preserve enough room for wild animals while preventing feeding, waste, media-driven closeness, and poorly designed infrastructure from turning ordinary overlap into conflict. City wildlife is a governance and design problem before it becomes a crisis-response problem.

## Key Claims

- Urban wildlife is produced by human systems as much as by animal adaptation: parks, buildings, garbage, cat food, compost bins, tourism, and landscaping all change behavior.
- Public affection is unstable because the same animal can be read as cute, dirty, frightening, ingenious, invasive, or worthy of protection.
- Coexistence requires early norm-setting around feeding, distance, waste, habitat, reporting, and the design of animal-accessible infrastructure.
- Conflict management has to protect both sides because injuries, disease risk, and property damage can push protected animals toward harsher control.
- Urban effectiveness should be interpreted through species-specific behavior and infrastructure fit rather than treated as a direct measure of general intelligence.

## Evidence

### Human systems create encounters

- [[ep282-bu-wanmei-de-gongcun-dang-yesheng-dongwu-chengwei-chengshi-xin-jumin-lg31-8lajmed4k4mhg5iz7hh8r1a]] describes macaques using tourist food and greenbelts, raccoon dogs using old residential gaps and cat food, and birds responding to urban trees, noise, and nesting materials.
- [[rat-vs-raccoon-rat-vs-raccoon|Rat vs. Raccoon]] describes raccoons using compost bins, homes, attics, stores, refrigerators, transit, and roads.

### Public interpretation is ambivalent

- [[ep282-bu-wanmei-de-gongcun-dang-yesheng-dongwu-chengwei-chengshi-xin-jumin-lg31-8lajmed4k4mhg5iz7hh8r1a]] contrasts balcony doves, squirrels, raccoon dogs, bats, snakes, and cartoon monkey signage to show how cuteness and fear change conduct.
- [[rat-vs-raccoon-rat-vs-raccoon|Rat vs. Raccoon]] turns raccoons from nuisance to intelligence symbol and then qualifies both readings through modern observation.

### Design and norms shape conflict

- [[ep282-bu-wanmei-de-gongcun-dang-yesheng-dongwu-chengwei-chengshi-xin-jumin-lg31-8lajmed4k4mhg5iz7hh8r1a]] emphasizes feeding bans, volunteer persuasion, controlled food access, tree choice, and movement corridors.
- [[rat-vs-raccoon-rat-vs-raccoon|Rat vs. Raccoon]] reports that costly raccoon-resistant bins were defeated, showing that engineered barriers require realistic behavioral assumptions and adaptation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Both sources are reported public conversations rather than complete policy evaluations. They do not establish exact population counts, carrying capacities, disease pathways, comparative intervention effects, or the independent performance of Toronto's bin system. Macaques, boars, raccoon dogs, birds, and raccoons differ in biology and risk; one city's coexistence design should not be generalized without local evidence. Reported barrier failure also does not establish that all bins failed or that exclusion engineering is futile.

## What Changed

- Added engineered waste infrastructure as part of the urban habitat animals learn to exploit.
- Qualified the tendency to infer general intelligence directly from successful city adaptation.
