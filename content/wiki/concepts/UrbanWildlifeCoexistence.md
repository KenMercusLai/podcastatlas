## Definition
Urban wildlife coexistence / 城市野生动物共存 is the practical condition in which wild animals live, feed, move, reproduce, or rest inside human-built city systems while residents, tourists, managers, and media repeatedly respond to them.

## Current Synthesis
The current evidence frames coexistence as imperfect rather than sentimental. [[ep282-bu-wanmei-de-gongcun-dang-yesheng-dongwu-chengwei-chengshi-xin-jumin-lg31-8lajmed4k4mhg5iz7hh8r1a]] shows animals becoming city "new residents" through a mix of habitat change, tourism, leftover food, cat feeding, greenbelts, missing predators, and public attention. The same animal can be a source of delight, nuisance, injury, disease anxiety, conservation concern, or management cost depending on where and how people encounter it.

Coexistence therefore sits between [[UrbanEcology]] and [[ConservationIntervention]]. It asks how to preserve enough room for wild animals while preventing human feeding, media-driven closeness, and poorly designed space from turning ordinary overlap into conflict. The episode's strongest contribution is to make city wildlife a governance problem before it becomes a crisis response problem.

## Key Claims
- Urban wildlife is produced by human systems as much as by animal adaptation: parks, old residential buildings, garbage, cat food, greenbelts, tourism, and landscaping all change animal behavior.
- Public affection is unstable because the same animal can be read as cute, dirty, frightening, invasive, spiritually charged, or worthy of protection.
- Coexistence is not passive tolerance; it requires early norm-setting around feeding, distance, waste, habitat, and reporting.
- Conflict management has to protect both sides because injuries, disease risk, and property damage can push protected animals toward harsher control.
- Planning choices such as tree species, ecological passages, low-cost infrastructure retrofits, and food-access design can reduce conflict without removing animals from the city.

## Evidence
- Human systems create the encounter surface: [[ep282-bu-wanmei-de-gongcun-dang-yesheng-dongwu-chengwei-chengshi-xin-jumin-lg31-8lajmed4k4mhg5iz7hh8r1a]] describes macaques using tourist food and greenbelts, raccoon dogs using old residential gaps and cat food, and birds responding to urban trees, noise, and nesting materials.
- Public affection is ambivalent: [[ep282-bu-wanmei-de-gongcun-dang-yesheng-dongwu-chengwei-chengshi-xin-jumin-lg31-8lajmed4k4mhg5iz7hh8r1a]] contrasts balcony doves, squirrels, raccoon dogs, bats, snakes, and cartoon monkey signage to show how cuteness and fear change human conduct.
- Coexistence requires norms before crisis: [[ep282-bu-wanmei-de-gongcun-dang-yesheng-dongwu-chengwei-chengshi-xin-jumin-lg31-8lajmed4k4mhg5iz7hh8r1a]] emphasizes feeding bans, volunteer persuasion, public education, and controlled food access as attempts to alter behavior before injuries or complaints dominate.
- Planning can reshape conflict: [[ep282-bu-wanmei-de-gongcun-dang-yesheng-dongwu-chengwei-chengshi-xin-jumin-lg31-8lajmed4k4mhg5iz7hh8r1a]] links bird droppings to tree fruit, boar movement to fragmented habitat, and raccoon dog feeding to cat-food placement.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode is a reported public conversation, not a complete policy evaluation. It presents several interventions as possibilities or partial examples, while leaving exact population counts, carrying-capacity estimates, disease pathways, and the scientific basis for some culling thresholds source-scoped. It also warns that urban residents and farmers may experience the same species very differently.

## What Changed
- Added city wildlife coexistence as a distinct branch of urban ecology and conservation governance.
- Reframed "animals entering cities" as a feedback problem involving human food, space, media, and regulation rather than only animal behavior.
