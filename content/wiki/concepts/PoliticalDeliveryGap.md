# Political Delivery Gap

[[sp-05-liuqing-linyao-qiangsha-yibao-jutou-an-beihou-de-sikao-xia-fenlie-de-meiguo-hui-you-xianzheng-weiji-ma-790241978]] adds a U.S. version of the gap. The source argues that [[USHealthInsuranceDenialPolitics]], inflation frustration, and blocked reform channels can make voters feel that the [[DemocraticParty]] is describing indicators rather than the lived problem, while [[MAGA]] and [[RightWingMediaInfrastructure]] convert non-delivery into identity and grievance politics.

Political delivery gap is the mismatch between a government's promises and the visible outcomes voters or party insiders can observe. In [[a-keir-death-experience-britains-pm-clings-on-698b0dc3337b2a3b2ef0aa07]], [[SashaNauta]] argues that [[KeirStarmer]] is vulnerable not only because of the [[PeterMandelson]] scandal, but because Labour has shown limited visible delivery on small boats, NHS waiting times, and a clear governing mission.

The concept is useful because it explains why scandals do not land evenly. A scandal that might be survivable for a trusted government can become a leadership crisis when it reinforces doubts about competence, direction, and follow-through.

[[starmergeddon-british-pm-resigns-6a390b674a8189f2c3b4e6f1]] shows the delivery gap hardening into exit. The episode says Starmer's premiership was politically weak from the start, damaged by unpopular decisions, poor optics, and the sense that he was behind events; it then broadens the diagnosis to Britain as badly governed rather than ungovernable.

[[a-firm-andy-what-are-new-british-pms-plans-6a5deeca76f8ee25bb4bbf9c]] shows the same gap surviving leadership change. [[AndyBurnham]] arrives with a popularity and unity advantage, but the source says voters are more focused on seeing a doctor or dentist, sluggish growth, high borrowing, and high taxes than on slogans about [[DevolutionAsGoverningProject|devolution]], AI, or foreign policy.

## Key Claims
- Delivery gaps are political facts even when policy work is underway, because voters and MPs respond to visible outcomes.
- A judgment scandal is more dangerous when the government has already lost the benefit of the doubt.
- A large majority does not by itself solve delivery legitimacy.
- Delivery failure can become a succession problem when party insiders conclude that changing leader is easier than restoring authority under the incumbent.
- In the U.S. case, delivery gaps can persist even when a party has policy arguments, because voters also judge acknowledgement, institutional obstacles, and trusted media channels.
- Replacing the leader can reset party mood while leaving the public-service and fiscal tests unchanged.
