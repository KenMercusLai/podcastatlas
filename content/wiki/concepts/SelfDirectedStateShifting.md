# Self-Directed State Shifting

## Definition
Self-directed state shifting is the deliberate use of scheduled or in-the-moment practices to alter arousal, attention, bodily tension, or emotional response without relying on the addictive substance or behavior that previously performed that function.

## Current Synthesis
The episode organizes state-shifting tools by timing. Proactive practices—therapy, peer connection, exercise, meditation, yoga nidra or NSDR, breathwork, prayer, and selected forms of cold exposure—are scheduled before crisis. Reactive tools are brief enough to use when distress is already present, including intentional breaths, naming bodily sensations, contacting someone, or drawing on rehearsed attention and relaxation skills.

The synthesis is functional rather than tool-centric: the practice matters because it expands choice and supports [[RecoveryDistressTolerance]], not because one technique is universally superior. Scheduling also matters because strong arousal can temporarily narrow access to reflection. Tools should therefore be rehearsed when a person is relatively stable and matched to medical condition, trauma history, preference, and professional guidance.

## Key Claims
- Recovery benefits from alternative ways to change state without the addictive object.
- Proactive practices and reactive tools serve different timing needs.
- Scheduled rehearsal can make a regulation skill more accessible during stress.
- Body awareness can add information that thought labels alone may miss.
- Very brief practices can preserve continuity when longer sessions are unavailable.
- Tool choice requires safety, fit, and evidence boundaries rather than universal prescriptions.

## Evidence
- Timing distinction - [[tools-for-overcoming-substance-behavioral-addictions-ryan-soave-scim5476071523]] separates proactive daily practices from reactive in-the-moment tools.
- Yoga nidra and NSDR - [[tools-for-overcoming-substance-behavioral-addictions-ryan-soave-scim5476071523]] discusses guided relaxation, long exhalation, stillness, and body awareness as scheduled practices.
- Brief continuity - [[tools-for-overcoming-substance-behavioral-addictions-ryan-soave-scim5476071523]] recommends a small number of intentional breaths when a longer session is impractical.
- Body labeling - [[tools-for-overcoming-substance-behavioral-addictions-ryan-soave-scim5476071523]] emphasizes naming bodily sensations associated with anger, fear, anxiety, and stimulant-linked activation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode does not establish a universal protocol, comparative effectiveness hierarchy, or safe dose for every practice. Cold exposure, breath manipulation, exercise, meditation, and trauma-focused introspection can be inappropriate for some people or situations; none replaces medical detoxification, emergency care, or condition-specific treatment.
