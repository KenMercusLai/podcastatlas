# Migrant-Family Debt–Care Tradeoff / 移民家庭债务—照护权衡

## Definition
The migrant-family debt–care tradeoff is the recurring allocation conflict in which borrowing, remittance goals, business survival, housing, and long work hours compete with parental presence, childcare, education support, health attention, and reunion.

## Current Synthesis
The tradeoff is not a clean choice between money and love. Migration can expand income, legal mobility, assets, and future options while debt service and low-margin work make daily care harder to provide. In the episode, bringing a child to Europe implied larger housing and care costs plus fewer working hours; leaving the child in China protected earning and sometimes education continuity but transferred care to relatives and prolonged attachment loss.

The same mechanism continues after reunion. A heavily financed family bar makes every unpaid family hour appear economically necessary, so the strategy undertaken “for the child” can demand the child's labor and still leave little time for relationship repair. The relevant judgment therefore includes who carries debt risk, who supplies care, who loses education or rest, and whether economic gains become usable autonomy for the child.

## Key Claims
- Migration debt converts future earnings into an immediate family obligation.
- Child reunion raises housing, supervision, education, and opportunity costs while potentially reducing paid work.
- Leaving children with relatives can preserve earning or schooling but transfers care and attachment costs.
- Business borrowing can turn kinship into an expectation of unpaid or always-available labor.
- Asset accumulation does not by itself compensate for missed childhood or create belonging.
- A complete evaluation tracks money, care, health, education, autonomy, and intergenerational repetition together.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source cannot determine the counterfactual outcome had the parents stayed, reunited earlier, or chosen another education route. Debt, income, housing, business capital, and legal-status details are not independently verified. The concept should not imply that separated parents lack care or that reunion is always preferable; it identifies constrained allocation and transferred costs.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to connect migration finance with care allocation before and after reunification.
