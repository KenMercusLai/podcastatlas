## Definition

赦令不能替代治理 / Amnesty Cannot Substitute for Governance is the pattern where a court uses pardon to reset legal exposure or project mercy, even though pardon neither repairs the causes of disorder nor reverses punishment already imposed without sufficient care.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-480-keben-bugan-ti-jiekai-zaobitouguang-de-heian-houxu-lhn89rqrj1dtntixgt5a7vd8p6ao|Hanji 480]] creates the concept through [[KuangHengWesternHan|匡衡]]'s answer to [[HanYuandi|汉元帝]] after eclipse and earthquake pressure. Kuang Heng does not reject mercy as such: he acknowledges the ruler's wish to let offenders start over. His criticism is that repeated amnesty becomes ineffective when people leave prison and quickly offend again because the court has not corrected the social and political causes of misconduct.

The source's governance logic runs from pardon to pedagogy. Crime is not treated only as individual badness to be punished or forgiven; it is also a symptom of poor elite example, profit-seeking customs, factional conduct, and weak instruction. Amnesty can suspend punishment, but it cannot supply the moral and institutional environment that would make law work.

A second failure mode concerns legitimacy after irreversible punishment. A general amnesty placed immediately after the Huainan and Hengshan cases is criticized because later mercy cannot substitute for caution before killing. Here the problem is not recidivism after pardon: a broad merciful signal cannot retrospectively establish that the preceding case expansion was discriminating or just. This judgment comes from [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-306-1-lvchabiao-bizu-baijian-hanwudi-lnruo3m6ahmj5z8k7uu0vlxlapje|Hanji 306-1]] and remains bounded by that source's incomplete case evidence.

## Key Claims

- Amnesty can be humane without being sufficient.
- Repeated pardon creates little order if people return to the same incentives and examples.
- Legal leniency must be paired with instruction, officeholder conduct, and public norms.
- The source distinguishes giving people a chance from mistaking pardon for root-cause repair.
- Amnesty after mass punishment cannot reverse death or substitute for careful adjudication before punishment.
- The pattern is broader than a single legal device: it asks whether mercy, punishment, and governance are aligned.

## Evidence

- Amnesty failure: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-480-keben-bugan-ti-jiekai-zaobitouguang-de-heian-houxu-lhn89rqrj1dtntixgt5a7vd8p6ao|Hanji 480]] says Kuang Heng notes that crime does not decrease after repeated amnesty, with some people reoffending soon after release.
- Root-cause diagnosis: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-480-keben-bugan-ti-jiekai-zaobitouguang-de-heian-houxu-lhn89rqrj1dtntixgt5a7vd8p6ao|Hanji 480]] says the problem lies in failed instruction, bad customs, and court example rather than in insufficient pardons.
- Legal-governance boundary: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-480-keben-bugan-ti-jiekai-zaobitouguang-de-heian-houxu-lhn89rqrj1dtntixgt5a7vd8p6ao|Hanji 480]] says Kuang Heng argues that punishment cannot function if social teaching and elite modeling are not repaired.
- Post-punishment legitimacy: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-306-1-lvchabiao-bizu-baijian-hanwudi-lnruo3m6ahmj5z8k7uu0vlxlapje|Hanji 306-1]] criticizes a general amnesty after the Huainan and Hengshan cases because mercy announced after large-scale killing cannot repair insufficient care before irreversible punishment.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept does not claim all amnesties are bad. Hanji 480 itself preserves the idea that pardon can give offenders another chance, and the episode later quotes [[XunYue|荀悦]] to distinguish extraordinary political contexts from normal law. Hanji 306-1 supplies the host's moral interpretation rather than a case-by-case review proving that every person punished in the Huainan and Hengshan cases was innocent. The concept names two failure modes: pardon can leave the causes of disorder untouched, and it can be used as a mercy signal without repairing irreversible earlier punishment.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Hanji 480's Kuang Heng memorial on repeated amnesty, reoffending, and root-cause governance.
- Added Hanji 306-1's distinction between post-killing mercy and pre-punishment care, while keeping its casualty and innocence judgments source-scoped.
