## Definition

赦令不能替代治理 / Amnesty Cannot Substitute for Governance is the pattern where a court uses pardon to reset legal exposure, while a remonstrant argues that crime and disorder will continue unless the underlying instruction, example, and administrative causes are repaired.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-480-keben-bugan-ti-jiekai-zaobitouguang-de-heian-houxu-lhn89rqrj1dtntixgt5a7vd8p6ao|Hanji 480]] creates the concept through [[KuangHengWesternHan|匡衡]]'s answer to [[HanYuandi|汉元帝]] after eclipse and earthquake pressure. Kuang Heng does not reject mercy as such: he acknowledges the ruler's wish to let offenders start over. His criticism is that repeated amnesty becomes ineffective when people leave prison and quickly offend again because the court has not corrected the social and political causes of misconduct.

The source's governance logic runs from pardon to pedagogy. Crime is not treated only as individual badness to be punished or forgiven; it is also a symptom of poor elite example, profit-seeking customs, factional conduct, and weak instruction. Amnesty can suspend punishment, but it cannot supply the moral and institutional environment that would make law work.

## Key Claims

- Amnesty can be humane without being sufficient.
- Repeated pardon creates little order if people return to the same incentives and examples.
- Legal leniency must be paired with instruction, officeholder conduct, and public norms.
- The source distinguishes giving people a chance from mistaking pardon for root-cause repair.
- The pattern is broader than a single legal device: it asks whether mercy, punishment, and governance are aligned.

## Evidence

- Amnesty failure: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-480-keben-bugan-ti-jiekai-zaobitouguang-de-heian-houxu-lhn89rqrj1dtntixgt5a7vd8p6ao|Hanji 480]] says Kuang Heng notes that crime does not decrease after repeated amnesty, with some people reoffending soon after release.
- Root-cause diagnosis: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-480-keben-bugan-ti-jiekai-zaobitouguang-de-heian-houxu-lhn89rqrj1dtntixgt5a7vd8p6ao|Hanji 480]] says the problem lies in failed instruction, bad customs, and court example rather than in insufficient pardons.
- Legal-governance boundary: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-480-keben-bugan-ti-jiekai-zaobitouguang-de-heian-houxu-lhn89rqrj1dtntixgt5a7vd8p6ao|Hanji 480]] says Kuang Heng argues that punishment cannot function if social teaching and elite modeling are not repaired.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept does not claim all amnesties are bad. Hanji 480 itself preserves the idea that pardon can give offenders another chance, and the episode later quotes [[XunYue|荀悦]] to distinguish extraordinary political contexts from normal law. The concept names a failure mode: amnesty becomes a substitute for governance when it is repeated without changing the causes that reproduce disorder.
