# Dynastic Cycle Perception / 王朝周期感知

## Definition
Dynastic cycle perception is how historical actors locate their own society within trajectories such as flourishing order, decline, disorder, reform, or revival.

## Current Synthesis
The concept treats perception as part of causation. People in the same period can draw different forecasts from their positions, texts, experiences, and historical analogies. Those forecasts shape whether they simplify institutions, prepare personnel, wait for pressure, predict collapse, or retain hope of revival.

## Key Claims
- Quiet middle periods can contain decisive diagnoses before open crisis.
- Administrative location and access to records shape what a thinker can see.
- Intellectual resources influence both diagnosis and proposed remedy.
- Disagreement within one period is evidence, not noise to be flattened by hindsight.
- Historical feeling becomes action when it guides reform, recruitment, withdrawal, or revolution.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Later events can make fulfilled pessimistic predictions look uniquely insightful, but the source warns that one era contains multiple judgments and that later readers define which elements count. Perception should not be confused with accurate prediction.
