# Social Rank Flexibility

## Definition
Social rank flexibility is the episode's distinction between fixed linear dominance and context-dependent leadership that can change with competence, task, group composition, and experience.

## Current Synthesis
Rank is difficult to isolate because a neural signal may encode a particular individual rather than that individual's position. Tye's lab addresses this by reorganizing mouse groups so the same animal can rise, fall, or retain rank. Competition data also suggest that dominant and subordinate animals attend differently: subordinates may monitor dominants closely, while dominants need less reciprocal tracking. The human extension is explicitly speculative but useful—groups may work better when leadership shifts toward the person competent for the current task instead of remaining locked to a single hierarchy.

## Key Claims
- Social rank is not always a stable, linear hierarchy.
- Identity and rank must be experimentally separated before neural activity can be interpreted.
- Rehousing provides a way to observe the same animal moving up, down, or remaining stable.
- Prefrontal activity can contain information about dominance and upcoming competition outcomes.
- Subordinates may devote more attention to higher-ranked individuals than the reverse.
- Task-dependent leadership may distribute status more adaptively than fixed dominance.

## Evidence
- Rank manipulation - [[the-biology-of-social-interactions-and-emotions-dr-kay-tye-scim4105255786]] describes regrouping former alpha, intermediate, and subordinate mice into new hierarchies.
- Competition decoding - [[the-biology-of-social-interactions-and-emotions-dr-kay-tye-scim4105255786]] reports prefrontal decoding of dominance and above-chance prediction of the next reward winner.
- Attention asymmetry - [[the-biology-of-social-interactions-and-emotions-dr-kay-tye-scim4105255786]] reports different timing of dominant and subordinate decoding and offers monitoring as a hypothesis.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Developmental rank effects, intermediate-rank reorganization, attention asymmetry, and healthier competence-based hierarchy are preliminary, animal-model, or speculative claims. They do not establish a direct human leadership prescription or a single neural rank code.

## What Changed
- Added identity-controlled rank change, competitive-state decoding, and task-dependent leadership as one qualified synthesis.
