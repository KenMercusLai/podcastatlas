# Neanderthal Extinction Causal Pluralism

## Definition

Neanderthal extinction causal pluralism is the view that the disappearance of [[Neanderthals]] as a distinct population likely resulted from interacting demographic, climatic, ecological, competitive, pathogenic, and absorptive processes rather than one decisive cause.

## Current Synthesis

[[127-neanderthals-glt5202048433]] places Neanderthals under pressure before sustained modern-human expansion: rapidly shifting European climates, small and dispersed groups, and low genetic diversity reduced resilience. Repeated Homo sapiens incursions then added competition for food, caves, and breeding partners, possible pathogen exchange, and [[ArchaicHumanInterbreeding]] that could absorb individuals into larger incoming populations.

This framework does not deny violence or behavioral differences. It rejects the stronger claim that organized extermination, one epidemic, one climatic event, or simple cognitive superiority has been demonstrated as the master cause. The archaeological record instead suggests uneven contact over many millennia, with local outcomes that may have differed.

## Key Claims

- Low population size and genetic diversity can make a capable lineage vulnerable to ordinary shocks.
- Rapid climate oscillation can fragment habitat and repeatedly stress already sparse groups.
- Competition may operate without planned warfare through food, shelter, territory, and partner access.
- Interbreeding can preserve ancestry while reducing the continuity of a distinct population.
- Pathogen exchange is plausible but sparse settlement makes epidemic-only extinction uncertain.
- Long, uneven overlap supports variable regional processes rather than a single invasion event.

## Evidence

### Pre-contact vulnerability

- [[127-neanderthals-glt5202048433]] links repeated warm-to-cold shifts, sparse populations, low variation, and some inbreeding to reduced demographic resilience.

### Contact pressures

- [[127-neanderthals-glt5202048433]] describes repeated Homo sapiens appearances, competition for resources and breeding partners, interbreeding, and possible two-way pathogen exchange.

### Limits of conquest narratives

- [[127-neanderthals-glt5202048433]] notes at least about 20,000 years of coexistence and finds no secure evidence that ambiguous wounds or close contact establish organized interspecies war.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The framework organizes plausible contributors rather than estimating their relative weights. Dates, population structure, regional overlap, reproductive asymmetry, disease ecology, and cultural advantages remain incomplete. Absence of secure warfare evidence is not evidence that violence never occurred, while interbreeding is not evidence that relations were peaceful. “Extinction” also needs qualification because Neanderthal ancestry persists genetically after population-level disappearance.

## What Changed

- Created a multicausal extinction framework centered on interaction among prior vulnerability, contact pressure, and partial absorption.
- Separated ordinary ecological competition from unproven organized extermination.
