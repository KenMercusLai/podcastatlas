# State Policing Legitimacy Crisis

State policing legitimacy crisis is the pattern where police structure and behavior become evidence that the state itself is partisan. In [[697-the-troubles-murder-in-belfast-part-1-glt4877520736]], the [[RoyalUlsterConstabulary|RUC]] and [[BSpecials|B Specials]] are overwhelmingly Protestant, hold wide coercive powers, and sit inside a wider [[InstitutionalSectarianDiscrimination|sectarian institutional structure]].

In [[698-the-troubles-the-brink-of-civil-war-part-2-glt2308408988]], the RUC removal of [[AustinCurrie]] at Caledon, the filmed baton and water-cannon attack in [[Derry]], the police inaction at [[BurntolletBridgeAttack|Burntollet]], and armoured-car fire in [[Belfast]] all deepen Catholic distrust. The episodes do not present policing as a neutral technical failure. The existence of the B Specials, allegations of loyalist sympathy, and the later Hunt Report recommendation that the [[BritishArmy]] take over military policing make police legitimacy part of the political structure of [[TheTroubles]].

In [[699-the-troubles-return-of-the-ira-part-3-glt9519811642]], the legitimacy crisis moves from street policing into intelligence and detention. [[OperationDemetrius]] relies on outdated [[RoyalUlsterConstabulary|RUC]] files, arrests Catholics only, and is followed by detention-abuse allegations around the [[FiveTechniques]], making [[IntelligenceFailureMassArrest]] and [[CounterinsurgencyLegitimacyTrap]] the next stage of the same institutional problem.

## Key Claims
- Security institutions can become conflict accelerators when one community reads them as another community's force.
- Publicly visible police violence can internationalize local grievances.
- Replacing police with troops may reduce immediate violence while creating a new legitimacy problem.
- Coercive powers are especially delegitimizing when their staffing and associational ties already map onto one community.
- Intelligence files become politically dangerous when they preserve an old organizational picture and produce visibly one-sided arrests.
