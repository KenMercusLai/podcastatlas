## Definition

Awaiting-punishment performance / 待刑谢罪式政治表演 is the tactic of responding to a superior's threatened punishment with exaggerated submission, visible guilt, and near-execution imagery so that the superior must either escalate into real severity or accept the performance as repentance.

## Current Synthesis

The current evidence comes from [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-527-1-hanchengdi-shajiuji-zhuangkelian-suiran-kechi-danshi-youyong-lioh59ybajvtcm3ayzplfoywswsn|Hanji 527-1]]. After [[HanChengdi|汉成帝]] sees Wang-family luxury crossing palace and city boundaries, he invokes the precedent of Emperor Wen forcing his uncle Bo Zhao toward suicide. The Wang relatives then answer not by contesting the charge directly, but by making themselves look already punishable: crying to [[WangZhengjun|王政君]], threatening facial mutilation, sitting on mats associated with execution, and carrying chopping implements.

The maneuver works because it changes the audience and the cost structure. A ruler can rebuke arrogant uncles for luxury and symbolic usurpation, but once they publicly perform total submission before the dowager-maternal field, killing them becomes harder to frame as necessary discipline rather than excessive cruelty. The episode therefore treats "装可怜" as shameful but effective: humiliation becomes leverage.

## Key Claims

- The tactic is strongest when the offender is powerful enough that ordinary punishment is politically costly.
- Extreme submissive posture can convert a legal or status transgression into a family-emotion scene.
- Execution imagery pressures the superior to choose between accepting repentance and proving that the threat was literal.
- The performance does not erase the original offense; it changes whether the superior can punish without looking excessive.
- The case remains source-scoped because Chengdi's true intent is left unresolved.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept does not prove that such performance always succeeds. Its force in Hanji 527-1 depends on the offenders being imperial maternal uncles, Wang Zhengjun's emotional-political position, and Chengdi's ambiguous intent. The episode also does not provide the full legal record of the punishment process, so the wiki keeps the inference bounded to this narrative rather than treating it as a general Han legal institution.
