# 咽喉疼痛升级就医 / Sore-Throat Escalation

## Definition
Sore-throat escalation is the separation of common, often self-limited throat discomfort from symptom patterns suggesting laryngeal obstruction, deep infection, foreign-body injury, or another condition needing prompt in-person assessment.

## Current Synthesis
The episode's core triage principle is location and function rather than pain alone. Pain centered near the larynx, a marked foreign-body sensation, muffled speech, difficulty breathing, or rapid worsening can point beyond routine pharyngitis. Peritonsillar infection adds a different severe pathway: it may spread through neck spaces and toward the chest even without the same immediate epiglottic obstruction.

Common throat symptoms remain heterogeneous. Acute infection, allergy, reflux, voice overuse, dryness, irritants, and chronic inflammation can overlap. A headline, visible redness, or a self-applied “pharyngitis” label cannot establish which pathway is present; symptom pattern, examination, course, and sometimes laryngeal visualization or imaging guide escalation.

## Key Claims
- Severity, location, speech, swallowing, breathing, and change over time matter more than the generic label “sore throat.”
- Muffled speech, pronounced foreign-body sensation, laryngeal pain, or breathing difficulty lowers the threshold for urgent assessment.
- Deep tonsillar or neck infection can become serious through spread even without immediate airway closure.
- Common causes such as infection, allergy, reflux, overuse, and irritation can overlap and require contextual interpretation.
- Online fear and casual reassurance are both poor substitutes for warning-sign assessment.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This page does not provide a remote diagnostic rule, and absence of one listed feature cannot exclude serious illness. Age, immune status, allergy exposure, foreign-body history, fever, secretion handling, neck swelling, dehydration, and overall decline can alter urgency. The source's cases and treatment descriptions remain educational examples.

## What Changed
- Created a location-, function-, and trajectory-based escalation frame for throat symptoms.
- Added deep infection spread as a serious pathway distinct from immediate epiglottic obstruction.
