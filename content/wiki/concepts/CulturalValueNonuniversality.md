## Definition

Cultural value nonuniversality is the principle that an object, practice, canon, or belief can be deeply valuable within one cultural frame without automatically carrying the same meaning or desirability in another.

## Current Synthesis

[[lriy1oybgigotwswtzkfbrdrhtl7|纸醉金迷FM第021期]] develops the idea through the 高昌 treasure in [[WhiteHorseNeighsInWesternWind|《白马啸西风》]]. The sought-after hoard proves to consist of paintings, statues, and other cultural objects brought from the Central Plains. Their value is real to those formed by that tradition, but local people do not therefore experience them as treasure.

The episode connects this reversal to the novel's statement about love: something can be “very good” without becoming the thing a particular person wants. This does not make all value arbitrary or prohibit cultural exchange. It limits the inference from “we prize this” to “everyone should prize this,” especially when admiration is backed by coercion, hierarchy, or a civilizing claim.

## Key Claims

- Cultural objects do not carry identical value independently of memory, practice, use, and interpretation.
- Sincere admiration does not establish universal desirability.
- Calling something treasure describes a relationship between people and objects, not only an intrinsic property.
- Cross-cultural offering differs from imposition because recipients retain the authority to interpret, adapt, refuse, or ignore.
- Nonuniversality does not require moral relativism about concrete harm.

## Evidence

### Treasure as situated value

- [[lriy1oybgigotwswtzkfbrdrhtl7]] presents the 高昌 hoard as Han paintings and statues that motivate pursuit but do not function as treasure for local people.
- [[lriy1oybgigotwswtzkfbrdrhtl7]] links this reversal to “很好很好，可是我偏偏不喜欢,” moving from romantic preference to cultural valuation.

### Boundary against imposition

- [[lriy1oybgigotwswtzkfbrdrhtl7]] interprets the attempt to universalize one's own valued goods as possible cultural arrogance even when intention is benevolent.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The current evidence is a literary interpretation of a fictional treasure, not an anthropological demonstration that cultures are internally uniform or mutually incommensurable. Objects can acquire new meanings through trade, translation, migration, education, and voluntary exchange. The concept does not make every value claim equally sound and does not excuse violence, exploitation, or denial of individual rights under the label of cultural difference.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from the 高昌 treasure reversal.
- Distinguished situated value from both universal imposition and total moral relativism.
