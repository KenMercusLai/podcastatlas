# Women Workplace Progress Stall

Women workplace progress stall is the pattern described by [[VingeruMakandewiri]] in [[burning-questions-a-more-fire-prone-world-6a687ea64207b2a60119df8b]]. The episode argues that several indicators have begun moving in the wrong direction after decades of improvement: fewer new female executives, falling female board-seat gains, weaker senior leadership pipelines, lower promotion appetite, lower full-time participation, and widening gender pay gaps.

The concept is not simply that women are leaving work. The source links labor-force participation, [[PromotionAmbitionGap]], [[ChildcareInfrastructureConstraint]], corporate diversity-policy rollback, and workplace-family balance into one stalled-progress story. That makes it a gendered extension of [[GoodWorkNewStandard]]: the quality of work is measured by whether advancement remains compatible with family life, dignity, and credible payoff.

## Key Claims
- Senior representation can stall even when entry-level education and participation have improved.
- Falling promotion ambition may reflect institutional strain rather than lower capability.
- Pay-gap widening in several countries suggests the reversal is broader than one firm or one promotion cycle.
- Childcare quality and availability can constrain career advancement as strongly as childcare cost.
- DEI rollback can matter if it removes formal attention from pipeline, promotion, and board-seat decisions.
