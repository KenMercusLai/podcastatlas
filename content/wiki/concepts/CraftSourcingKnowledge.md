# Craft Sourcing Knowledge

## Definition

Craft sourcing knowledge is the practical ability to identify place-specific materials, antiques, makers, sellers, and fabricators, and to know who can create, restore, alter, or combine objects for a particular design purpose.

## Current Synthesis

The source distinguishes access to products from knowledge of production relationships. [[NateBerkus]] learned through his mother that distinctive interiors depend on objects with history and on people capable of making or transforming them. Travel extends that knowledge by showing which places specialize in which materials and crafts.

Online search can widen discovery, but it does not automatically supply tacit knowledge about provenance, quality, fabrication capacity, restoration, or collaboration. Sourcing therefore sits between aesthetic judgment and execution: it converts an intention into a feasible material network.

## Key Claims

- Objects can carry history, place, and craft knowledge as well as surface appearance.
- Effective sourcing includes makers, sellers, restorers, and fabricators, not only finished products.
- Place-specific exploration helps designers learn where particular materials and skills are concentrated.
- Tacit knowledge about who can alter or combine objects can be as important as identifying the objects themselves.
- Digital access expands the search field but does not eliminate the need to assess provenance, capability, and relationships.

### Place, makers, and fabrication

- [[revisionist-history-live-with-nate-berkus-revisionist-history-live-with-nate-berkus]] says designers need to know what particular places make and which craftspeople can create, transform, or combine distinctive objects.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source is practitioner testimony rather than a supply-chain, labor, provenance, or sustainability audit. Historical character does not guarantee authenticity, ethical acquisition, quality, affordability, or functional fit. Online marketplaces may sometimes provide expertise and access that local fieldwork cannot.

## What Changed

- Distinguished sourcing knowledge from generic product availability.
- Connected design execution to place-specific craft and fabrication networks.
