# Open Web Social Contract Erosion

Open web social contract erosion is the breakdown of informal norms that once made crawling, indexing, archiving, and linking feel mutually tolerable. [[tech-20260421-0421-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260421-0421-mp-tech-pod-128]] adds the concept through [[AndrewDeck]]'s claim that the [[InternetArchive]] is facing a watershed moment because the explosion of AI crawlers has weakened the older honor-system model.

The episode distinguishes this from a simple technical failure. The problem is institutional trust: publishers may share the [[InternetArchive]]'s values around transparency and documentation, but they now worry that open access can be routed into commercial model training without permission or compensation.

## Key Claims
- The open web depended partly on informal restraint around crawling, indexing, linking, and archival reuse.
- AI crawler demand can make those norms feel inadequate because text archives now have clear training-data value.
- Once trust erodes, even public-interest crawlers may be blocked because they resemble or enable commercial extraction.
- The result can be less open-web memory, weaker search/discovery ecosystems, and more pressure for contractual or technical access controls.
- Social-contract erosion links publisher economics to public-record fragility rather than only to copyright litigation.
