# Abbasid Revolution

## Definition

The Abbasid Revolution was the Khorasan-centered coalition and military campaign that overthrew the Umayyad caliphate, installed the Abbasid dynasty, and shifted the Islamic imperial center toward Iraq and Persian-influenced eastern networks.

## Current Synthesis

The episode presents the revolution as more than a dynastic coup. Late Umayyad defeats, regional distrust, sectarian claims, juristic criticism, and the alienation of Marwan created openings, but [[AbuMuslim]] made Khorasan decisive by joining Abbasid sacred genealogy to black-banner symbolism, Persian memory, and local expectations of just rule.

Victory exposed the coalition's limits. As-Saffah's proclamation and the defeat of Marwan were followed by the killing of surviving Umayyads and desecration of their tombs. Promises of justice and Quranic order did not prevent coercive dynastic rule. Under [[AlMansur]], the revolution's durable institutional result became a consolidated [[AbbasidCaliphate]] and a new capital at [[Baghdad]], not the abolition of imperial hierarchy.

## Key Claims

- Umayyad military, regional, sectarian, and legitimacy crises created a revolutionary opening.
- Khorasan supplied distance from the center, military resources, mixed traditions, and a receptive coalition.
- Abbasid family descent and sacred expectation gave dynastic politics a religious language.
- Persian memories were redirected toward Abbasid victory without constituting a simple Sasanian restoration.
- Military success moved from Merv across Iran to Kufa and the defeat of Marwan.
- Destruction of Umayyad people and memory made violence constitutive of the new order.
- Justice promises were qualified by continuity in coercive imperial and dynastic rule.

## Evidence

- **Coalition conditions:** [[377-baghdad-crossroads-of-the-universe-part-2-glt9127243683]] links late Umayyad strain to Khorasan's regional and religious mixture.
- **Mobilization and victory:** [[377-baghdad-crossroads-of-the-universe-part-2-glt9127243683]] follows Abu Muslim's banners and armies from Merv through Iran and Iraq to the proclamation of as-Saffah and Marwan's defeat.
- **Violence and disappointed expectations:** [[377-baghdad-crossroads-of-the-universe-part-2-glt9127243683]] describes the destruction of Umayyads and the rapid disillusionment of supporters who had expected justice.
- **Institutional outcome:** [[377-baghdad-crossroads-of-the-universe-part-2-glt9127243683]] connects al-Mansur's consolidation and foundation of Baghdad to the revolution's eastward political reorientation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

One narrative episode cannot resolve the revolution's full social composition, chronology, ideological diversity, or balance of Arab, Iranian, religious, fiscal, and military causes. Behiferid's relationship to Abbasid mobilization and the meaning of Persian symbols are interpretive; shared imagery does not show that participants held one program or sought Sasanian restoration.

## What Changed

- Established a coalition model linking Umayyad crisis, Khorasani mobilization, sacred legitimacy, Persian memory, and military conquest.
- Made dynastic violence and disappointed justice claims central qualifications of the revolutionary frame.
- Connected the revolution's durable outcome to Abbasid consolidation and Baghdad's foundation.

## Related Concepts

- [[AbbasidCaliphate]] - dynastic polity created by the revolution.
- [[AlMansur]] - consolidator who converted victory into durable rule and a new capital.
- [[Baghdad]] - city that embodied the revolution's eastward political reorientation.
- [[RoundCityOfBaghdad]] - planned center that materialized post-revolutionary legitimacy.
