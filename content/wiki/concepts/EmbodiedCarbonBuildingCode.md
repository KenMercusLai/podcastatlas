# Embodied Carbon Building Code

Embodied carbon building code is the policy move described in [[tech-20260420-0420-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260420-0420-mp-tech-pod-128]], where [[California]] requires large non-residential buildings to account for carbon emitted during construction. The concept shifts building-climate policy from only reducing [[OperationalCarbon]] after occupancy toward measuring material and construction emissions before the building is used.

The episode's point is not that the rule makes every project radically different. Through [[MalibuHighSchool]], [[VerticalGroup]], [[BNBuilders]], and [[EmilyEnglish]], it frames the code as a compliance and documentation system that makes builders inspect materials, choose among approved routes, and pay attention to [[LowCarbonConstructionMaterials]].

## Key Claims
- Embodied carbon covers emissions created while making and assembling the building, not energy used later in operation.
- California is presented as the first state to regulate embodied carbon in its building code.
- The episode says large non-residential buildings over 50,000 square feet must follow the code.
- The policy makes lower-carbon materials and documentation part of ordinary project review.
- Compliance can happen through reuse, whole-project carbon reduction, or material-level global-warming thresholds.
- The rule can work as a market signal because suppliers see demand when code compliance requires lower-carbon options.
