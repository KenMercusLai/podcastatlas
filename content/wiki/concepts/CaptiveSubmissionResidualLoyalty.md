# Captive Submission with Residual Loyalty / 被俘归顺与旧主忠义

## Definition
Captive submission with residual loyalty is the pattern in which a defeated or captured actor accepts temporary service under a new power because refusal may mean death or imprisonment, while openly preserving an older loyalty claim and framing later departure as repayment rather than betrayal.

## Current Synthesis
The concept is defined through [[GuanYu|关羽]] after [[CaoCao|曹操]] captures him at [[Xiapi|下邳]]. The episodes distinguish this from the novelized "关公约三事": the historical-source frame does not require a formal surrender contract to preserve Guan Yu's loyalty. Instead, the tension sits in the sequence of capture, generous treatment, useful service, repayment, gift refusal, patron restraint, and eventual return to [[LiuBei|刘备]]. Hanji 1043 clarifies the repayment act itself by placing Guan Yu's killing of [[YanLiangLateHan|颜良]] at [[BaimaReliefLateHan|White Horse]], while Hanji 1044 supplies the gift-sealing departure endpoint.

## Key Claims
- Captivity changes the moral and practical meaning of submission because refusal may lead to execution or confinement.
- Temporary service can coexist with residual loyalty when the actor marks the prior bond as non-transferable.
- Repayment of a captor's favor can become the bridge between accepting treatment and leaving without framing the move as treachery.
- Patron restraint after the departure can help preserve the moral boundary between accepted repayment and coerced retention.
- Literary memory may add formal conditions to stabilize a hero's image even when the source record gives a looser loyalty-and-repayment structure.

## Evidence
- Coercive context: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1041-jiemi-lishishang-zhenshi-de-guanyu-jiangcao-luisp0ndk1lfrc87byygn6r8myhx|Hanji 1041]] compares Guan Yu's captive submission with the harsher outcomes for figures who do not submit, such as [[GaoShun|高顺]] and [[ChenGongLateHan|陈宫]].
- Temporary incorporation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1041-jiemi-lishishang-zhenshi-de-guanyu-jiangcao-luisp0ndk1lfrc87byygn6r8myhx|Hanji 1041]] says Cao Cao appoints Guan Yu 偏将军 and treats him generously after capture.
- Residual loyalty: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1041-jiemi-lishishang-zhenshi-de-guanyu-jiangcao-luisp0ndk1lfrc87byygn6r8myhx|Hanji 1041]] reports Guan Yu's statement to [[ZhangLiao|张辽]] that Liu Bei's恩义 prevents betrayal.
- Repayment act: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1043-yuanshao-tongma-caocao-jing-zhihaole-tade-piantouteng-ltibjj6xizsrynpgr9oyf-q3ydna|Hanji 1043]] has Guan Yu kill Yan Liang during the White Horse relief, supplying the concrete merit that fulfills the repayment promise.
- Exit form: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1044-guanyu-qianli-zou-danqi-xu-ganxie-caocao-busha-enqing-lpptlnc-e4zu4lcjr49bquxdw0pm|Hanji 1044]] has Guan Yu seal Cao Cao's rewards, leave a letter, and return toward Liu Bei while Cao prevents pursuit.
- Fiction boundary: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1041-jiemi-lishishang-zhenshi-de-guanyu-jiangcao-luisp0ndk1lfrc87byygn6r8myhx|Hanji 1041]] treats "关公约三事" as a [[RomanceOfTheThreeKingdoms|《三国演义》]] construction rather than a recorded condition set.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This concept should not become a blanket excuse for every surrender. It applies most cleanly when the source shows coercive capture, a stated prior loyalty, and an explicit repayment/departure logic. It differs from civilian-protection surrender, strategic defection, and opportunistic camp-switching because the central issue is the actor's remaining obligation to a prior bond. The exact interior motives of Cao Cao and Guan Yu remain source-bounded; the public sequence is stronger evidence than inferred emotion.

## What Changed
- Added Hanji 1043 as the concrete repayment act between Hanji 1041's promise and Hanji 1044's departure.
- Clarified the sequence as capture and service, White Horse merit, then gift-sealing exit.
