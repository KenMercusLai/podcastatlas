# New Golden Age Science Agenda

## Definition
The New Golden Age science agenda is the Trump administration science-policy frame described by [[MichaelKratsios|Michael Kratsios]] in the All-In interview. It defines support for science as renewing public trust, changing funding incentives, backing scientists, setting national missions, and measuring whether federal research dollars produce breakthroughs.

## Current Synthesis
The agenda's distinctive move is to reject a budget-total definition of pro-science policy. It argues that the U.S. can spend large sums on research and still stagnate if incentives, politicized grant criteria, short time horizons, and institutional channel-locking crowd out risky or high-upside work. Weinstein's interview gives the agenda an outside heterodox ally while also pushing it beyond Kratsios's administrative reform frame: science reform must protect dissenting scientists, fund higher-variance work, and confront secrecy or dangerous-knowledge problems. Its counter-risk is that reform language can justify disruptive cuts, politicized selection, or unaccountable discretion unless continuity and evidence standards remain explicit.

## Key Claims
- Federal science policy should apply the scientific method to itself: test funding models, measure outcomes, and stop treating bigger budgets as the only success metric.
- The agenda favors funding scientists and early-stage discovery over preserving institutional channels by default.
- Discrete national missions in space, quantum computing, fusion, nuclear power, bio, and AI-enabled science are used to restore a more ambitious public science posture.
- Public trust is treated as part of scientific capacity because COVID, DEI, and climate-emergency disputes are said to have politicized the authority of science.
- Competition with China turns research productivity, talent formation, critical minerals, and global scientific leadership into national-security issues.
- The Weinstein source adds a coalition claim: scientists should work with the current Trump science team on reform even if they dislike the surrounding politics.
- The agenda is now bounded by a stronger caution that high-variance science and dangerous knowledge need credible governance, not only deregulation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The agenda is still described largely from aligned or sympathetic interviews. The bounded sources do not prove that funding disruptions will improve output, that DEI-related grants were correctly classified, that mission timelines for quantum, fusion, space, and AI-enabled science are achievable, or that heterodox science can be funded without weakening evidence standards.

## What Changed
- Added Weinstein's outside heterodox support for the Trump science-policy team.
- Added scientific precarity, high-beta funding, and dangerous-knowledge governance as stronger boundaries around the agenda.
