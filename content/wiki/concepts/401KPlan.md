# 401(k) Plan

401(k) plan is the U.S. retirement-account example used in [[vol-126-gongmu-jijin-hai-zhide-mai-ma-lojrz1qctaqzkc2rg0lqokos-o6v]] to explain why mutual-fund ecosystems need long-term capital. The episode treats 401(k)-style retirement savings as a structural demand source that can support [[PublicMutualFundEcosystem|mutual funds]], target-date products, advisory services, and ordinary household wealth accumulation.

The page exists mainly as a comparison point for [[PersonalPensionAccount|中国个人养老金账户]]. The source argues that China's personal pension system is not yet a full 401(k)-like force because tax benefits, product breadth, contribution habits, and investment-advisory support remain limited.

[[145-jijin-tougu-zhide-xinren-ma-lrckug0zjqolcczni8ajikb0k5mi]] adds the [[BuySideInvestmentAdvisory|buy-side advisory]] version. The episode treats 401(k) and IRA-style retirement assets as one reason U.S. investment advisory became more common: workers need to choose allocations, target-date products, or advisor-supported portfolios inside long-duration accounts.

## Key Claims
- Retirement accounts can lengthen fundholder time horizons by tying product choice to retirement income rather than short-term market timing.
- Long-term contribution systems can make [[TargetDateFund|target-date funds]], low-fee index funds, and managed allocation products more useful.
- Tax preference helps only when the product menu and advisory layer make the account holdable.
- A retirement wrapper can improve [[FundLiabilityMatching]] for the fund industry, but only if investors trust the system enough to keep contributing through drawdowns.
- Episode 145 adds that retirement accounts can create demand for advice, because long horizons make [[GoalBasedClientProfiling]], rebalancing, and [[InvestmentBehaviorCoaching]] practically valuable.
