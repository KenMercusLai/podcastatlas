# Payment for Order Flow

## Definition
Payment for order flow is a brokerage revenue arrangement in which a market maker pays to receive customer orders for execution, allowing the broker to reduce or remove visible commissions while earning from order routing.

## Current Synthesis
The source uses [[Robinhood]] and [[CitadelSecurities|Citadel Securities]] to show that “zero commission” changes where trading costs and conflicts appear rather than eliminating them. Retail orders can be valuable because they are small and comparatively insensitive to tiny execution differences. The relevant user question therefore expands from the posted commission to routing incentives, spread, execution quality, disclosure, and whether the interface encourages profitable activity for the platform rather than good outcomes for the investor.

## Key Claims
- A zero headline commission does not mean brokerage and execution services have no economic cost.
- Market makers may pay brokers because retail order flow is commercially valuable.
- Small execution differences can matter even when each user sees only a minor amount.
- Order-routing revenue can create a conflict between broker monetization and best execution.
- Lower visible fees can broaden access while still encouraging excess trading or obscuring the platform's take rate.

## Evidence
- **Revenue mechanism:** [[ep159-gamestop-gupiao-touzi-yulehua-weishenme-jiazhi-touzi-bu-chixiangle-ckwrirwd-dwbabaaaachv6pg]] says Robinhood routed customer orders to market makers such as Citadel and received revenue in return.
- **Retail-order value:** [[ep159-gamestop-gupiao-touzi-yulehua-weishenme-jiazhi-touzi-bu-chixiangle-ckwrirwd-dwbabaaaachv6pg]] argues that small retail traders are often less sensitive to small price differences, making their flow attractive to market makers.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source explains the incentive mechanism but does not measure Robinhood users' execution quality against alternatives or establish that every routed order harmed the customer. Lower commissions can create real consumer benefit. A full judgment requires execution data, routing disclosures, regulation, and comparison with explicit-commission models.

## What Changed
- Created an incentive model separating visible commission from execution and routing economics.
