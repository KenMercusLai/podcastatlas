# Karma as Cause and Effect

## Definition
Karma as cause and effect is [[ShyalpaRinpoche]]'s explanation in [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-97450677c47f502770248abb02f35793-163628914-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-97450677c47f502770248abb02f35793-163628914]] that actions produce consequences no one can simply escape.

## Current Synthesis
The episode frames karma as both metaphysical and practical. All beings are said to have Buddha nature, but as long as people live with a physical body in the world, they are bound by karma unless they find complete liberation. At the same time, Shyalpa Rinpoche rejects fatalism: belief in karma does not mean leaving everything to karma, and compassionate action can still matter.

## Key Claims
- Karma is explained as cause and effect rather than arbitrary reward or punishment.
- Embodied beings remain bound by karma until complete liberation.
- Belief in karma is not passive fatalism; it does not excuse inaction.
- Compassionate action is treated as causally meaningful, especially in the fish-release story around the guest's parents' illness.
- Karma links biography, ethics, and spiritual practice by making consequences inseparable from intention and action.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source presents karma from Shyalpa Rinpoche's Buddhist perspective. The wiki should not generalize this as an empirical medical claim or as a complete cross-tradition definition of karma.
