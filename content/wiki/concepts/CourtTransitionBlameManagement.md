## Definition

新君清算中的责任安排 / court transition blame management is the pattern in which a new ruler limits, redirects, or suppresses accountability claims after a predecessor's power structure collapses, because the same accusation can threaten court stability, predecessor dignity, senior-office continuity, and the supply of ministers who can absorb future responsibility.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 502 creates the concept through the first-year Chengdi cleanup after [[ShiXianWesternHan|石显]]'s fall. [[WangZunWesternHan|王尊]] attacks [[KuangHengWesternHan|匡衡]] and [[ZhangTanWesternHan|张谭]] for not reporting Shi Xian while he was powerful and for later wording that makes officials appear to have feared Shi Xian more than [[HanYuandi|汉元帝]]. On the merits, the accusation is not absurd: the episode treats post-amnesty speech and predecessor-blame as real dangers.

Yet [[HanChengdi|汉成帝]] does not remove Kuang Heng. The source reads this as new-ruler calculation rather than simple moral exoneration. Removing the chancellor and Yushi Dafu at the start of the reign would unsettle a court whose old order has already collapsed. Protecting Kuang Heng also creates an obligation: the retained chancellor must bow, apologize, request retirement when disasters occur, and absorb some public responsibility that might otherwise climb to the emperor.

The pattern therefore differs from ordinary [[BureaucraticImpeachmentReview|官员弹劾公开复核]]. The problem is not only whether an accusation is true. It is whether pressing the accusation now would create a worse instability, dishonor the predecessor, or remove useful responsibility-bearing posts before the new ruler has consolidated authority.

## Key Claims

- Transition cleanup creates real accountability opportunities, but also office vacancies and incentive competition.
- Accusing old-regime accommodators can be politically reasonable while still destabilizing if it targets too many senior posts at once.
- Blame directed at a fallen favorite can rebound onto the deceased predecessor who empowered or failed to restrain that favorite.
- A new ruler may protect a compromised minister because stability and usable office continuity matter more than immediate moral sorting.
- Retention can create reciprocal obligation: the spared senior minister must visibly submit and later absorb disaster or omen responsibility.
- Demoting the accuser can mean "stop here" rather than "the accusation has no point."

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept currently rests on one compact podcast source. It records the host's transition-politics reading, not Chengdi's private deliberation or the complete legal file against Kuang Heng and Zhang Tan. It should not be used to excuse all post-transition impunity: the source's point is that timing, office continuity, and predecessor dignity can alter the political cost of accountability, not that accountability is irrelevant.
