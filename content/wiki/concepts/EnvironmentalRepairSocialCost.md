# Environmental Repair Social Cost

Environmental repair social cost is the livelihood damage that can accompany fast ecological intervention. In [[socialist-mobility-a-telling-michigan-vote-6a74600ec3e1347e8b5bf216]], the [[YangtzeRiver|Yangtze]] fishing ban helps fish biomass recover, but the government says 230,000 fishermen were affected and [[GabrielCrossley]] reports local claims that compensation was uneven.

The concept does not argue against environmental repair. It extends [[EnvironmentalTradeoffAccounting]] by asking who pays during repair, how compensation is distributed, and whether retraining or replacement livelihoods actually work.

## Key Claims
- A policy can be ecologically necessary and still socially damaging.
- Compensation claims should be evaluated against local reports of who actually received support.
- Retraining and aquaculture can fail if they do not match skills, markets, capital, or local conditions.
- Migration after a ban is part of the policy outcome, not an unrelated side effect.
- Environmental emergency can justify sweeping action, but that does not remove the need to count livelihood loss.
