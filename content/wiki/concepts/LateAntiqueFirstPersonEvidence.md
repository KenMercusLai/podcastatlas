# Late Antique First-Person Evidence

## Definition

Late antique first-person evidence is testimony written by a historical participant whose proximity gives rare access to a poorly documented world while purpose, genre, memory, and self-presentation constrain what biography can be recovered from it.

## Current Synthesis

[[SaintPatrick|Saint Patrick]] is the episode's central case. His *Confessio* and letter to Coroticus illuminate clerical family background, Roman civic status, enslavement, Irish language contact, Christian conversion, episcopal authority, mission, and slave raiding across the Irish Sea. Their survival matters especially because comparable fifth-century writing from Britain is so scarce.

First person does not mean transparent. The *Confessio* is saturated with scripture and organized around Patrick's mission and relation to Christ, not modern chronological biography. Its voices, miracles, numbers, silences, and self-defense therefore remain evidence that must be interpreted rather than copied into a complete life story.

## Key Claims

- Scarcity can make a small first-person corpus historically disproportionate in value.
- Authorial proximity supplies access but does not remove genre or self-presentation.
- Theological and biblical organization can preserve biography while reshaping its form.
- Uncertainty should be retained when a text does not settle chronology, location, motive, or literal event structure.
- Later legend must be dated independently rather than used to fill gaps in the first-person record.

## Evidence

- Documentary value: [[164-saint-patrick-glt2665917893]] uses Patrick's two writings to illuminate Roman Britain, Irish slavery, mission, conversion, and Christian authority.
- Interpretive limits: [[164-saint-patrick-glt2665917893]] notes extensive biblical allusion, unexplained episodes, inconsistent durations, uncertain locations, and disputed readings.
- Later-source boundary: [[164-saint-patrick-glt2665917893]] separates Patrick's writings from medieval lives and much later snakes and shamrock traditions.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept does not imply that first-person writing is inherently more honest than external evidence or that theological narrative is historically useless. Nor does uncertainty make all reconstructions equally plausible. External chronicles, archaeology, language, social structure, and later textual criticism can corroborate, qualify, or challenge a participant's account.

## What Changed

- Created a historical-method concept for preserving both the exceptional value and the limits of Patrick's surviving testimony.
