# Research Integrity Incentives

## Definition
Research integrity incentives are the rules, rewards, infrastructure, and inspection odds that shape whether researchers make work reproducible, report limits, share materials, and cooperate with correction.

## Current Synthesis
The bounded sources move from individual good practice to an institutional reward system. [[PaulinaNemkova|Paulina Nemkova]] presents replication before extension as ordinary research discipline. [[AbelBrodeur]] adds credible inspection: transparent commitments matter more when authors expect code, data, and robustness to be checked. Bhattacharya adds positive career incentives through funded replication, searchable negative results, data sharing, and cooperation with replicators. Integrity therefore depends on both audit risk and a labor market in which careful checking is worth doing.

## Key Claims
- Researchers respond to publication rewards, journal requirements, career pressure, and the likelihood of being audited.
- [[PublicationBias]] and [[StatisticalSignificanceThreshold|significance thresholds]] can reward selective or tuned results.
- [[ReplicationPackage|Replication packages]] and [[Preregistration]] create visible commitments, but incentives change more when those commitments are actually inspected.
- Neutral author contact and response windows help keep correction from becoming automatic accusation.
- The strongest system would catch errors before publication, citation, and public spread rather than after.
- Replication can be a constructive part of new research, not only a post-publication policing mechanism.
- Grants, publication venues, searchable negative findings, and credit for data sharing can make integrity-producing work a viable career activity.

## Evidence
- Replication before extension: [[ep-6-data-science-ai-talk]] has Nemkova describe reproducing related Stanford EEG work before attempting improvement and links statistical caution to research integrity.
- Credible inspection: [[dont-hate-the-replicator-hate-the-game]] presents the Institute for Replication and Replication Games as mechanisms that raise the expected chance of scrutiny.
- Procedural fairness: [[dont-hate-the-replicator-hate-the-game]] describes author response windows and distinguishes objective reruns from judgment-dependent robustness choices.
- Positive incentives: [[scim5646582763-scim5646582763]] records Bhattacharya's proposal to reward data sharing, cooperation with replication, participation in replication work, and publication of negative findings.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
More auditing can create defensive bureaucracy, conflict, or metric gaming if inspection quality and procedural fairness are weak. Positive scores for cooperation can also become performative. The proposed NIH journal, grants, and prosocial measures are policy intentions in an interview, not demonstrated outcomes.
