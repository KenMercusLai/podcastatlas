## Definition

Memory-group personal continuity is [[XunMengNiKuangNovel|《寻梦》]]'s fictional proposal that an organized bundle of memories, rather than a separable immortal soul, can persist after death and be projected into another person's brain.

## Current Synthesis

The model translates reincarnation into an information problem. Recurring first-person dreams, unfamiliar dialect, bodily correspondences, place recognition, and overlapping testimony act as signs that a prior memory organization is operating in present people. Because Yang Liqun and Liu Liling receive opposed viewpoints from the same killing, the story treats memory not as a neutral archive but as perspective-bound identity carrying attachment, fear, guilt, and resentment.

The episode's cloud-storage, shared-database, AI, and consciousness-upload comparisons make the 1980 premise legible in newer technical language, but they remain analogies. The account supplies no storage medium, transmission rule, selection process, capacity limit, or reason that one memory group enters one recipient. It therefore works as speculative literature about personal identity and repetition, not as evidence that memory survives biological death.

## Key Claims

- The model locates continuity after death in organized memory rather than a traditional soul.
- First-person perspective matters because the same event can persist as incompatible experiential identities.
- Recovered memory can transmit emotion and behavioral pressure, not only propositional information.
- Multiple converging clues increase narrative plausibility without establishing a real transfer mechanism.
- Cloud storage and consciousness upload are explanatory analogies, not demonstrated equivalences.
- The model leaves storage, routing, embodiment, error, and recipient selection unresolved.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept is derived from one podcast's account of a novel, not neuroscience, psychology, or independently verified reincarnation research. Dreams, birthmarks, pain, dialect, recognition, and historical correspondence are plot evidence inside the retelling. Even in the story, they can strengthen an interpretation without logically excluding memory distortion, suggestion, coincidence, psychological mechanisms, or other unknown explanations. Treating memory as sufficient for identity also leaves embodiment, character, agency, and moral responsibility unresolved.

## What Changed

- Created a bounded literary concept separating transferable organized memory from a traditional immortal soul.
- Made the information-system analogy explicit while preserving the absence of a scientific mechanism.
