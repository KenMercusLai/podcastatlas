# Longitudinal Personal Health Baselines

## Definition
Longitudinal personal health baselines are repeated measurements collected while a person is relatively healthy so that later changes can be judged against that person's prior range, trajectory, and context.

## Current Synthesis
The episode treats within-person change as a complement to population thresholds. Repeated blood, urine, microbiome, omics, imaging, and wearable data may expose deviations earlier than episodic symptom-driven care, while baseline imaging may help distinguish stable findings from changing ones. The method becomes useful only when measurements are reliable, clinically interpretable, proportionate to risk, and connected to actions that improve outcomes; more data alone does not guarantee prevention.

## Key Claims
- A person's prior trajectory can reveal change even when a current value remains within a broad reference range.
- Multiple modalities can connect physiological change with sleep, activity, diet, illness, medication, and environment.
- Baseline imaging can aid comparison, but screening can also uncover incidental findings and downstream uncertainty.
- AI can help organize dense longitudinal records but cannot convert weak correlations into validated clinical recommendations.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not provide denominators, false-positive rates, downstream harms, cost effectiveness, or randomized outcome evidence for the broad screening program. Repeated testing can create incidental findings, anxiety, unnecessary procedures, and misleading correlations. Measurement cadence and clinical action thresholds must be tailored to risk and evidence.
