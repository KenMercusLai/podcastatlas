## Definition
Agricultural practice as textual entry / 农业实践作为文本入场券 is the idea that doing farm work can change how a reader approaches agricultural books, classical literature, food history, and older writers.

## Current Synthesis
The source turns practice into a reading credential without making it the only valid route to knowledge. [[DuanZhiqiang|段志强]] argues that because he plants, reads, and writes, he can face agricultural texts and figures such as [[XuGuangqi|徐光启]] less as distant authorities and more as people working on partly comparable problems of land, season, food, and expression.

## Key Claims
- Bodily farming experience can make old agricultural and literary texts more concrete.
- Practice changes the questions a reader asks, such as what a named herb was eaten as rather than only how it appears in materia medica.
- Textual learning remains necessary because lived experience triggers further checking rather than replacing research.
- The method can make present rural life into future historical evidence.
- The idea is strongest when it stays bounded by the practitioner's actual experience and does not claim total peasant representation.

## Evidence
- Entry-ticket claim: [[yu-duan-zhiqiang-tan-chongming-xiangye-bowuzhi-wo-zhu-changjiang-wei-8212963740-775078]] records Duan's statement that farming is his subjective entry point into agricultural books and older authors.
- Herb and food inquiry: [[yu-duan-zhiqiang-tan-chongming-xiangye-bowuzhi-wo-zhu-changjiang-wei-8212963740-775078]] describes his inquiry into Henan edible jingjie through eating practice, botany, materia medica, and historical timing.
- Labor-to-reading loop: [[yu-duan-zhiqiang-tan-chongming-xiangye-bowuzhi-wo-zhu-changjiang-wei-8212963740-775078]] says repetitive farm work lets ideas arise, after which Duan connects them to books and checks more material.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Practice is an entry point, not a guarantee of accuracy. The episode explicitly separates Duan's real first-person experiences from historical sections that require research, inference, and interpretation.
