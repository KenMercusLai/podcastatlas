# Black Comic Moral Inversion

Black comic moral inversion is the source's pattern for stories where serious moral categories are rearranged through comic logic. In [[10-wang-erde-qiyi-gushiji-yong-zui-hui-sahuang-de-zuichun-qinwen-tianshi-545332195]], [[LordArthurSavilesCrime|《阿瑟·萨维尔勋爵的罪行》]] makes a future murder feel like a duty to be responsibly completed before marriage, while [[TheCantervilleGhost|《坎特维尔的幽灵》]] turns haunting into a practical household-management problem before returning to pity.

The concept captures why [[WildeStrangeStories|《王尔德奇异故事集》]] is not only sad, beautiful, or epigrammatic. [[OscarWilde|Wilde]] uses manners, social duty, ghost-story convention, and emotional excess to make moral life absurd without simply declaring morality meaningless.

## Key Claims
- Black comedy can make morality sharper by making respectable behavior appear absurd.
- A story can laugh at moral language without abandoning moral attention.
- Genre convention can be inverted: murder becomes duty, haunting becomes inconvenience, and comedy can still end with tenderness.
- The pattern works best when read through [[ArtAgainstMoralInstruction]] rather than as a direct moral lesson.
