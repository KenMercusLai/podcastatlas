# Solo Death Preparedness / 独居死亡准备

Solo death preparedness is the episode's late-life and end-of-life extension of [[SoloAgingPlanning]]. In [[vol-118-danshen-renshi-yanglao-zhinan-bushi-gudu-de-daimingci-shi-gengzao-zhangwo-rensheng-de-zhudongquan-lhvkuveznxd4citlwf5marzaj-wt]], the speakers cite Japanese advice for older people that treats death as something each person ultimately experiences alone, even if others are present.

The concept does not romanticize dying alone. It asks how a solo elder can reduce avoidable fear, delayed discovery, legal confusion, family conflict, and social shame around "孤独死" before death becomes an emergency managed entirely by others.

[[kyiv-brawl-ukraines-top-general-fired-6a609aec4fac21e67f1be165]] adds a housing-market consequence through [[JikobukkenIncidentProperty|jiko bukken]]. [[MoekaIida]] visits an apartment where an isolated elderly woman died and went unnoticed for days; the delayed discovery becomes part of the home's social meaning and may produce a [[HousingStigmaDiscount]] even after the death itself has passed.

## Key Claims
- A solo elder needs arrangements so an accident or death can be discovered reasonably early.
- Death surrounded by family is not automatically better if the dying person's own wishes are overridden.
- Social shame around solitary death can create unnecessary fear and dependence on unsuitable family arrangements.
- Delayed discovery can also affect later residents, landlords, and property value when the death becomes part of a home's disclosed history.
- Advance medical, legal, and household plans can protect autonomy and reduce confusion for others.
- The source cautions against magical health regimens because fear of death can make people vulnerable to bad advice.
