# 纵横家外交 / Zonghengjia Diplomacy

纵横家外交 / Zonghengjia diplomacy is the coming Warring States mode that [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-28-hanzhaohou-de-kuaile-ni-xiangxiang-budao-ln2tbxniz4kvwogbwp32exmdokz8]] says [[ZizhiTongjian|《资治通鉴》]] is preparing through the arrival of [[SuQin|苏秦]] and [[ZhangYiStrategist|张仪]]. The concept names a shift in narrative weight: persuasion, alliance design, threat framing, and status rhetoric start to matter as much as terse succession and battle notices.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-29-suqin-lizhi-shuangwen-zhishi-gaibian-mingyun-lrf5cqvoriyep2lkbozju-lyjaxv]] develops the threshold while keeping the chronology doubtful. In the chronicle's placement, Su Qin appears as leader of [[HezongAlliance|合纵]] and as "six-state minister"; the host questions whether such coordination could happen suddenly in the source's 333 BCE setting. The concept therefore includes both the diplomatic phenomenon and the need to separate later legendary drama from exact annalistic placement.

The same episode begins the Su Qin / Zhang Yi pairing. Su Qin and Zhang Yi are presented as [[Guiguzi|鬼谷子]] students who later stand on opposed lines: Su Qin through hezong, Zhang Yi through the rival path associated with Qin-facing persuasion. Their story shows that zonghengjia diplomacy is not only policy theory; it is a competitive profession built from rhetoric, humiliation, status desire, and court access.

## Key Claims
- Diplomatic rhetoric becomes a direct instrument of power in the Warring States field.
- The chronicle's narrative allocation matters: brief annals give way to fuller persuader stories.
- Zonghengjia activity grows out of a world where title status, coalition pressure, and multi-state fear already shape decisions.
- Hezong-style alliance depends on cross-state coordination; the Su Qin legend should not be read as proof that such coordination appeared instantly.
- Persuader rivalry can be mutually enabling: one strategist's success creates the conditions or provocation for another's rise.
