## Definition

边疆册封信用权衡 / frontier recognition credibility tradeoff is the statecraft problem where recognizing a frontier claimant may stabilize one surrendered group while damaging earlier commitments, allied-group trust, neighboring frontier relations, and public finances.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-765-donghan-fengyun-nengchen-nengbuneng-gandiao-bahu-waiqi-ltnvdqnyofuelaklssbquguwivan|Hanji 765]] defines the concept through [[YuanAnEasternHan|Yuan An]] and [[RenKuiEasternHan|Ren Kui]] opposing [[DouXianEasternHan|Dou Xian]]'s proposal to recognize [[YuChuNorthernXiongnu|Yu Chu / 于除]] as a new Northern Xiongnu chanyu. Dou treats recognition as an extension of the [[SouthernXiongnu|Southern Xiongnu]] precedent; Yuan treats it as a breach of the older Southern Xiongnu commitment structure.

The core tradeoff is cumulative. A new title could win a visible submission, but it might also signal that Han sacrifices long-serving frontier partners once their military purpose is complete, provoke [[Wuhuan|Wuhuan]] and [[Xianbei|Xianbei]] because of their prior conflict with the Northern Xiongnu line, and create provisioning costs beyond existing Southern Xiongnu and Western Regions burdens. In this source, Yu Chu's later rebellion confirms the downside risk of choosing surface incorporation over credibility discipline.

## Key Claims

- Frontier recognition must be judged against existing commitments, not only the newest claimant's request.
- A title granted to one frontier actor can damage trust with older allies who supplied prior military cooperation.
- Neighboring frontier groups matter because recognizing one claimant can reopen grievances with actors who fought that claimant's lineage.
- Fiscal capacity is part of diplomatic credibility when a recognition package implies long-term provisioning and supervision.
- A powerful court patron can make a risky recognition plan prevail even when ministers identify the tradeoff.
- Later defection or rebellion can turn a recognition win into evidence that the original credibility objections were material.

## Evidence

- Existing-commitment problem: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-765-donghan-fengyun-nengchen-nengbuneng-gandiao-bahu-waiqi-ltnvdqnyofuelaklssbquguwivan|Hanji 765]] says Yuan An and Ren Kui opposed using the Southern Xiongnu precedent to install Yu Chu because the older Southern Xiongnu line had already served Han for more than forty years.
- Trust signal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-765-donghan-fengyun-nengchen-nengbuneng-gandiao-bahu-waiqi-ltnvdqnyofuelaklssbquguwivan|Hanji 765]] has Yuan An cite fidelity language and warn that breaking faith would make other frontier groups doubt Han commitments.
- Neighbor reaction: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-765-donghan-fengyun-nengchen-nengbuneng-gandiao-bahu-waiqi-ltnvdqnyofuelaklssbquguwivan|Hanji 765]] says Wuhuan and Xianbei had killed Youliu chanyu, so recognizing his brother Yu Chu risked angering them.
- Fiscal load: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-765-donghan-fengyun-nengchen-nengbuneng-gandiao-bahu-waiqi-ltnvdqnyofuelaklssbquguwivan|Hanji 765]] quotes high annual costs for Southern Xiongnu and Western Regions support and argues a more distant Northern Xiongnu arrangement would cost still more.
- Failed outcome: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-765-donghan-fengyun-nengchen-nengbuneng-gandiao-bahu-waiqi-ltnvdqnyofuelaklssbquguwivan|Hanji 765]] records that Emperor He followed Dou Xian's proposal and that Yu Chu later rebelled.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept is currently source-bounded to Hanji 765. The source gives Yuan An's objections and the later rebellion, but it does not prove that every frontier-recognition package will fail or that Dou Xian's proposal had no short-term rationale. The concept should be used when recognition changes an existing frontier trust structure, not for every routine investiture.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Hanji 765's dispute over whether Han should recognize Yu Chu as a Northern Xiongnu chanyu.
