## Definition

Mausoleum frugality remonstrance / 陵墓节俭式进谏 is advice that challenges extravagant royal burial by linking tomb construction to moral virtue, public burden, precedent, and the logical futility or danger of lavish burial.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 214 part 1 now supplies the earlier Baling case through [[ZhangShizhiWesternHan|张释之]]'s advice to [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]]. When Liu Heng imagines a stone outer coffin sealed so tightly that it cannot be opened, Zhang shifts the problem from barrier strength to theft incentive: coveted contents can motivate people to break even a mountain-like defense, while a tomb without desirable goods needs little sealing. Restraint is therefore both frugality and root-cause security.

Hanji 536-1 shows that warning becoming precedent through [[LiuXiang|刘向]]'s advice to [[HanChengdi|汉成帝]] about the Changling works. The episode says Changling is large, luxurious, and long unfinished, making burial construction a visible sign of Chengdi's broader imbalance between pleasure, ritual display, and responsible rule. Liu Xiang invokes the Wendi-era Zhang warning, then presses Chengdi with a two-sided test: if the dead have awareness, disturbing others' graves during construction creates danger; if the dead lack awareness, lavish construction has no benefit. Across the two cases, burial restraint joins security incentives, public burden, precedent, virtue, and afterlife logic.

## Key Claims

- Tomb construction can become a governance issue when it consumes national resources and displays ruler desire.
- Reducing desirable grave goods can address looting risk more directly than continually strengthening physical barriers.
- Frugality arguments gain force when they combine historical precedent with present fiscal burden.
- A two-sided afterlife argument can attack thick burial whether the ruler assumes postmortem awareness or not.
- Burial restraint is presented as a sign of virtue and wisdom, not merely a budget preference.

## Evidence

- Baling root-cause argument: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-214-tiemian-zhangshizhi-dui-huangdi-lan-taizi-1-lqlbul2drksmqgmligaelawflsvq8|Hanji 214 part 1]] says Zhang Shizhi answers Liu Heng's stone-sealing proposal by contrasting a desirable tomb that people will break open with an austere tomb that gives them little reason to enter.
- Changling burden: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-536-1-renxing-huanghou-zhaofeiyan-ruhe-mingmu-zhangdan-gaowaiyu-llxo0my5ngp-mnbvjbl92yod1x2n|Hanji 536-1]] describes Chengdi's Changling works as large, luxurious, nationally burdensome, and still unfinished after a long time.
- Precedent use: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-536-1-renxing-huanghou-zhaofeiyan-ruhe-mingmu-zhangdan-gaowaiyu-llxo0my5ngp-mnbvjbl92yod1x2n|Hanji 536-1]] says Liu Xiang cites the Han Wendi-era Zhang Shizhi warning about burial scale.
- Virtue contrast: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-536-1-renxing-huanghou-zhaofeiyan-ruhe-mingmu-zhangdan-gaowaiyu-llxo0my5ngp-mnbvjbl92yod1x2n|Hanji 536-1]] says Liu Xiang contrasts wise, virtuous rulers buried simply with unwise rulers who build high and lavish tombs.
- Afterlife dilemma: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-536-1-renxing-huanghou-zhaofeiyan-ruhe-mingmu-zhangdan-gaowaiyu-llxo0my5ngp-mnbvjbl92yod1x2n|Hanji 536-1]] reports Liu Xiang's argument that if the dead are aware, disturbed graves create danger, and if they are unaware, lavish burial is useless.
- Ruler response: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-536-1-renxing-huanghou-zhaofeiyan-ruhe-mingmu-zhangdan-gaowaiyu-llxo0my5ngp-mnbvjbl92yod1x2n|Hanji 536-1]] says Chengdi is moved by the memorial even though the source does not show complete policy conversion.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Hanji 214 part 1 does not establish Baling's final design, grave inventory, actual resistance to theft, or the full administrative effect of Zhang's advice. Hanji 536-1 does not reconstruct the full Changling budget, labor system, legal process, or final outcome. Neither episode creates a complete theory of burial ritual. The concept is limited to a remonstrance form that uses theft incentives, frugality, precedent, afterlife logic, virtue, and public burden to challenge excessive royal burial.

## What Changed

- Added Zhang Shizhi's earlier Baling argument as the root precedent later reused by Liu Xiang.
- Extended the concept from fiscal and moral criticism to incentive-based tomb security: fewer coveted goods reduce the motive for forced entry.
