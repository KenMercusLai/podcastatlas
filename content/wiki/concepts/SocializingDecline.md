# Socializing Decline

Socializing decline is the trend used in [[burning-questions-a-more-fire-prone-world-6a687ea64207b2a60119df8b]] to explain why nightlife officials are no longer only mediating noise complaints. The episode says young Americans spent more than a third less time socializing in 2023 than in 2003, and a nightlife consultant describes the industry as being in a "war against the couch."

The concept connects [[NighttimeEconomyGovernance]] to the wiki's existing [[FriendshipRecession]] branch. Bars and clubs are not the only answer to loneliness, but the source argues that city officials may need to think about how phones, home entertainment, cost, weak community routines, and licensing systems affect whether people go out at all.

## Key Claims
- Declining social time can become an urban economic problem when venues lose customers.
- It is also a civic problem because social habits, loneliness, and local public life interact.
- Nightlife policy has to consider people who are not already going out.
- The problem may require more than bars and clubs: events, third places, transport, safety, affordability, and community-building can all matter.
