# By-Name Homelessness Coordination

## Definition

By-name homelessness coordination is a person-level workflow that combines outreach, named records, vulnerability and duration information, and recurring case review to match people experiencing homelessness with housing and support.

## Current Synthesis

In Jacksonville, [[ChangingHomelessness]] uses street outreach to supplement shelter and service data, represents each person as a human figure rather than an anonymous dot, and reviews individual barriers each week. The method converts an aggregate problem into a changing operational queue without reducing a person to the queue position.

Its strength is actionable specificity; its limit is resource dependence. A list can reveal who needs help, support prioritization, and coordinate staff, but it cannot manufacture apartments at the required rent or prove that the prioritization rules and data coverage are fair.

## Key Claims

- Named records can restore identity and accountability that aggregate counts obscure.
- Outreach beyond shelters and service systems can reduce omission of people who rarely seek assistance.
- Vulnerability and time without housing can support prioritization when their definitions and use are transparent.
- Recurring multidisciplinary review turns a static count into an operational coordination process.
- List quality depends on coverage, consent, data protection, updating, and fair correction.
- Identification is necessary for individualized action but insufficient when affordable housing supply is absent.

## Evidence

### Outreach and person-level visibility

- [[a-memorial-for-the-living-a-memorial-for-the-living]] describes volunteers searching streets, bridges, cars, and abandoned buildings and then entering named individuals into the list.

### Prioritization and case review

- [[a-memorial-for-the-living-a-memorial-for-the-living]] describes a vulnerability-duration scatterplot and Tuesday discussions of each person's barriers and possible resources.

### Housing supply boundary

- [[a-memorial-for-the-living-a-memorial-for-the-living]] reports slow net progress because new entries continued while affordable apartments remained scarce.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source does not provide the list's definitions, privacy safeguards, consent model, missingness, error correction, racial or disability effects, outcome rates, or comparative evaluation. Prioritization can make scarcity more orderly without making it just, and a person-shaped icon does not itself guarantee dignity or effective help.

## What Changed

- Created a bounded operational model separating person-level coordination from the affordable-housing capacity required for exits.
