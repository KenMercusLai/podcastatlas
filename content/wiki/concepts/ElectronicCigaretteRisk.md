# Electronic Cigarette Risk / 电子烟风险

## Definition
Electronic-cigarette risk is the health and dependence risk created by inhaling heated aerosol containing nicotine and other constituents, even when the product does not burn tobacco or contain conventional-cigarette tar.

## Current Synthesis
The current source rejects a binary choice between "safe vaping" and "dangerous smoking." Electronic cigarettes and combustible cigarettes have different exposure profiles, but neither is treated as harmless. Flavor, attractive packaging, and the absence of smoke smell can reduce perceived risk while nicotine, heated-liquid byproducts, metals, airway irritation, cardiovascular effects, dependence, and habitual hand-to-mouth behavior remain relevant.

## Key Claims
- Absence of tar does not establish absence of harm.
- Nicotine can preserve dependence, while ritual and repeated hand-to-mouth action can reinforce habitual use.
- Heated liquid may generate aldehydes, and flavoring or device constituents may add respiratory irritants or metals.
- Individual sensitivity changes when symptoms appear but does not determine whether the product is safe at population level.
- Vaping should not be used to make combustible smoking look safe, nor should combustible risk be used to make vaping look harmless.

## Evidence
- Product and airway risk: [[vol-164-shijie-wuyanri-dianzishi-yande-weihai-yuan-chao-xiangxiang-yisheng-qinshu-nianqing-huanzhe-qidao-bi-laoyanmin-gengzao-ljg7oqxs6d_n1-u7fzcyl7xdbxn]] links nicotine, aldehydes, flavor additives, metals, cough, chest tightness, and airway-mucosal injury in its public-education account.
- Risk-perception mismatch: [[vol-164-shijie-wuyanri-dianzishi-yande-weihai-yuan-chao-xiangxiang-yisheng-qinshu-nianqing-huanzhe-qidao-bi-laoyanmin-gengzao-ljg7oqxs6d_n1-u7fzcyl7xdbxn]] connects fruit and dessert flavors, novelty packaging, "fresh breath" claims, and indoor use to reduced public caution.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source acknowledges that direct head-to-head cancer-risk data are limited and does not quantify relative risk between vaping and combustible tobacco. Its single young-patient case cannot establish incidence or causation by itself. The "popcorn lung" analogy, exact chemical exposure, individual susceptibility, and product-specific risk require stronger product and clinical evidence.
