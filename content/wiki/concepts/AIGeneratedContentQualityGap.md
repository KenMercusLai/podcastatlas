# AI-Generated Content Quality Gap

[[tech-20260813-0813-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260813-0813-mp-tech-pod-128]] adds a taste-centered explanation for the same gap. [[SophieHagney]] argues that AI can be trained on more curated material and still tend toward imitation or averages, so the gap is not only model fidelity but whether the output carries [[EmbodiedTaste]], discovery, timing, and judgment.

AI-generated content quality gap is the source's explanation for why lower creation cost does not automatically produce a successful entertainment product. In [[youhua-shenglv-erfei-peilv-ba-yi-jian-shi-zuodao-lilun-shang-gaiyou-de-yangzi-duitan-lianxu-chuangyezhe-albert-lu0vamaawctwva3qblnsf99esar2]], [[Albert]] and the host describe a gap where AI can make an "80 point" experience, but users still spend attention on "90 point" games, videos, or creator work.

The concept matters most for [[AIInteractiveContentPlatforms]]. Interactive and immersive experiences ask more from the user than passive video, so the content quality bar can be higher, not lower. More generated supply may simply flow into existing [[Douyin]], [[TikTok]], game, or creator platforms unless the new product also owns a stronger [[ProductContainer]] and distribution loop.

## Key Claims
- Content markets are scarcity markets for attention, not only supply markets for generated material.
- The higher the immersion and participation cost, the more the product competes with polished games and high-quality feeds.
- AI can lower production barriers while increasing the amount of mediocre content users ignore.
- A tool-side improvement can still be valuable, but it does not by itself create a consumer platform.
- The gap can narrow when [[ModelCapabilityPackaging]], stronger models, or new interaction containers make the result feel genuinely better.
- The gap can also narrow through human taste curation, but the Marketplace Tech source keeps that separate from AI having taste of its own.
