# Missionary Fieldwork Infrastructure / 传教士田野基础设施

Missionary fieldwork infrastructure is the practical support system that [[35-tianzhende-renleixuejia-zuo-tianye-hui-shangyin-de-603418624]] identifies beneath anthropology's formal anti-missionary posture. The episode contrasts [[BronislawMalinowski|Malinowski]]-style professional suspicion of missionaries with [[NigelBarley|Nigel Barley]]'s discovery that missionaries in [[Cameroon]] could provide vehicles, medicine, loans, translation help, language records, and local cultural knowledge.

The concept is deliberately ambivalent. Missionaries may carry religious and colonial pressures into local life, but anthropologists may still depend on their infrastructure to survive, move, communicate, and understand enough to work.

## Key Claims
- Fieldwork purity can hide dependence on older or parallel institutions.
- Missionary infrastructure can support research while also shaping the cultural and political environment being researched.
- The researcher should name dependencies rather than treating them as accidental background.
- Practical help does not erase ethical caution about conversion, authority, or colonial history.
