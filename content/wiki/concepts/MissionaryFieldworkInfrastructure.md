## Definition
Missionary fieldwork infrastructure is the practical support and knowledge system created by missionaries through mobility, local relationships, translation, records, medicine, maps, letters, buildings, and social access.

## Current Synthesis
The concept remains ambivalent. In the anthropology source, missionaries provide the vehicles, medicine, loans, translation, and local knowledge that make fieldwork possible while also carrying religious and colonial pressure. The Li Ji source adds a historical archive version: missionaries in the Northeast surveyed villages, mapped Catholic settlements, recorded families and believers, and preserved letters and drawings that later let historians reconstruct ordinary lives. The infrastructure is useful evidence, but it also shapes what becomes visible.

## Key Claims
- Fieldwork purity can hide dependence on older or parallel missionary institutions.
- Missionary infrastructure can help researchers survive, move, communicate, and interpret local settings.
- The same infrastructure can carry conversion pressure, colonial entanglement, treaty power, and institutional hierarchy.
- Mission records do not merely preserve facts; they select villages, believers, routes, and categories of importance.
- Later historians should use missionary sources while naming how their institutional purpose shaped the archive.

## Evidence
- Practical support claim: [[35-tianzhende-renleixuejia-zuo-tianye-hui-shangyin-de-603418624]] describes missionaries in Cameroon providing vehicles, medicine, loans, translation help, language records, and local cultural knowledge for anthropology fieldwork.
- Ethical ambiguity claim: [[35-tianzhende-renleixuejia-zuo-tianye-hui-shangyin-de-603418624]] contrasts missionary help with anti-missionary professional suspicion and colonial or conversion pressure.
- Historical archive claim: [[100-nian-qian-dongbei-nvhai-xie-gei-bali-de-xin-duitan-lishixuezhe-liji-putongren-ruhe-chuanguo-da-lishi-3d36f181-94ad-4883-8db3-5f040b5ed45b]] describes [[ParisForeignMissionsSociety|巴黎外方传教会]] village surveys, Catholic Northeast maps, family records, and long-preserved letters.
- Selection effect claim: [[100-nian-qian-dongbei-nvhai-xie-gei-bali-de-xin-duitan-lishixuezhe-liji-putongren-ruhe-chuanguo-da-lishi-3d36f181-94ad-4883-8db3-5f040b5ed45b]] shows [[SantaiZiCatholicVillage|三台子]] appearing important on a mission map because Catholic infrastructure made it important to that knowledge system.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Missionary infrastructure should not be treated as either neutral data service or pure domination. The sources jointly require a double reading: use the evidence, but keep conversion aims, treaty protection, colonial pressure, and archive selection visible.
