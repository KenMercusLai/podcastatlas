# Platform Definition Convergence

Platform definition convergence is the regulatory problem that services become harder to classify when their product formats grow alike. [[tech-20260724-0724-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260724-0724-mp-tech-pod-128]] adds the concept through [[WillOremus]] asking whether [[YouTube]] counts as social media and noting that TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, and Netflix-style vertical video increasingly resemble one another.

The concept matters for [[SocialMediaAgeBans]] because categorical laws depend on category boundaries. If a ban covers TikTok and Instagram but not YouTube, and if YouTube Shorts or vertical Netflix content looks behaviorally similar, then enforcement turns on definitions that may lag product design.

## Key Claims
- Product categories become unstable when platforms copy each other's format, feed, creator, recommendation, and short-video mechanics.
- Regulation written for "social media" can miss services with similar user behavior but different legal or product labels.
- Narrow exclusions can create loopholes or inconsistent treatment across platforms.
- Category ambiguity can shift policy attention from names to features: communication, feeds, recommendations, user-generated content, creator tools, and age-sensitive interaction.
