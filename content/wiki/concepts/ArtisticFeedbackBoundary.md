# Artistic Feedback Boundary

## Definition
Artistic feedback boundary is [[RickRubin]]'s distinction between using external response as information and letting it sever the creator's felt relationship to the work.

## Current Synthesis
[[essentials-how-to-access-your-creativity-rick-rubin-scim7816635332]] does not treat feedback from audiences or platforms as inherently good or bad. Rubin's rule is relational: outside response may help if it clarifies the work, but it becomes damaging when the creator starts obeying likes, dislikes, commercial prediction, or other people's reasons at the expense of the work's felt truth. This differs from confidence calibration because the goal is not accurate self-esteem alone; it is keeping artistic judgment connected to the piece.

## Key Claims
- Feedback is information, not command authority.
- Audiences may like or dislike a work for reasons unrelated to the creator's reasons.
- The creator controls only their relationship to the work, not audience interpretation.
- Commercial ideas can undermine taste when they replace owned response.
- Feedback is most useful when it can be tested concretely in the work.

## Evidence
- Platform-feedback question - [[essentials-how-to-access-your-creativity-rick-rubin-scim7816635332]] records Huberman asking whether rapid online feedback from Twitter or Instagram can harm creativity.
- Information boundary - [[essentials-how-to-access-your-creativity-rick-rubin-scim7816635332]] records Rubin saying feedback is neither inherently right nor wrong; it is information that can be used or not used.
- Relationship-to-work test - [[essentials-how-to-access-your-creativity-rick-rubin-scim7816635332]] says external information is probably bad for the art when it weakens the creator's connection to the work.
- Direct testing - [[essentials-how-to-access-your-creativity-rick-rubin-scim7816635332]] says suggestions are more useful in a setting where creators can try them immediately.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept does not reject critique, editing, collaboration, or audience learning. It rejects only the transfer of final artistic authority from the creator's judgment to external reaction.
