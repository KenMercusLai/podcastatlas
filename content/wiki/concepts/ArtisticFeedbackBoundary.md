# Artistic Feedback Boundary

## Definition
Artistic feedback boundary is [[RickRubin]]'s distinction between using external response as information and letting approval, criticism, or commercial prediction sever the creator's felt relationship to the work.

## Current Synthesis
[[essentials-how-to-access-your-creativity-rick-rubin-scim7816635332]] establishes the basic rule: feedback is neither command nor contamination but information to test against the work. [[protocols-to-access-creative-energy-and-process-rick-rubin-scim5595688842]] adds the motivational failure case. A creator who tries to please critics can lose the audience and felt truth that made the work compelling, while a diary-like frame can keep outside judgment from claiming final authority. The boundary remains medium-sensitive: live performance may make audience response part of the work rather than merely commentary on it.

## Key Claims
- Feedback is information rather than final command authority.
- The creator controls their relationship to the work more directly than audience interpretation.
- Critic-pleasing and business-first prediction can weaken distinctive judgment.
- A diary-like honesty frame reduces anticipated judgment without rejecting revision.
- Feedback is most useful when it can be tested concretely against the work and its medium.
- In live or collaborative forms, external response may be constitutive rather than optional.

## Evidence
- Information boundary - [[essentials-how-to-access-your-creativity-rick-rubin-scim7816635332]] records Rubin treating feedback as information that may or may not be used.
- Relationship-to-work test - [[essentials-how-to-access-your-creativity-rick-rubin-scim7816635332]] says outside response is damaging when it weakens the creator's connection to the piece.
- Diary frame - [[protocols-to-access-creative-energy-and-process-rick-rubin-scim5595688842]] reframes work as an honest personal entry that outsiders cannot invalidate for the maker.
- Critic-pleasing failure - [[protocols-to-access-creative-energy-and-process-rick-rubin-scim5595688842]] uses Andrew Dice Clay as an example of work losing charge when aimed at critics rather than its owned voice and audience.
- Direct testing - [[essentials-how-to-access-your-creativity-rick-rubin-scim7816635332]] favors suggestions that creators can try immediately rather than accept abstractly.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept does not reject critique, editing, collaboration, audience learning, or commercial responsibility. It rejects transferring final artistic authority to untested reaction. In stand-up and other live arts, [[ComedyAsInvoluntaryJudgment|audience response]] can be evidence internal to the medium, so the boundary must distinguish governing by approval from learning what the work actually does.

## What Changed
- Added diary-like honesty and critic-pleasing failure as mechanisms explaining why the feedback boundary matters.
- Clarified that live audience response can qualify the boundary without abolishing it.
