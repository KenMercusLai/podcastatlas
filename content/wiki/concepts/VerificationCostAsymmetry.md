# Verification Cost Asymmetry / 信息核查成本不对称

## Definition
Verification cost asymmetry is the gap between the low effort needed to repeat an appealing claim and the greater time, skill, access, or language work needed to determine whether it is accurate.

## Current Synthesis
EP113 argues that vivid stories spread because they satisfy an existing desire for heroes, villains, certainty, or identity confirmation. Sharing may require one click, while checking original material, locating foreign reporting, comparing accounts, and following later corrections can demand sustained work. Attention systems then add a timing disadvantage: the attractive version can saturate discussion before verification is complete.

The asymmetry explains persistence without assuming that audiences never care about truth. People face limited time and uneven access, and corrections are often less novel than the original claim. A practical response therefore needs provenance, accessible correction, and friction at the point of sharing rather than relying only on individual vigilance after distribution.

## Key Claims
- Claim repetition is often much cheaper than primary-source verification.
- Narrative vividness and identity fit can reward a claim independently of accuracy.
- Corrections arrive later and usually attract less attention than the original story.
- Information abundance can multiply selectable “truths” when users choose the version they already want.
- Individual cross-checking helps but cannot by itself remove platform, language, access, and attention constraints.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode's examples are conversational and not reconstructed from primary documents. The concept does not establish that all popular claims are false, that corrections never spread, or that more information necessarily reduces knowledge. Verification costs vary by subject, institution, access, and the quality of available evidence.

## What Changed
- Created a framework linking cheap repetition, costly checking, narrative reward, correction delay, and bounded individual response.
