# Social Jetlag / 社会时差

## Definition
Social jetlag is the mismatch between biological time and social time, where work, school, light environments, meals, screens, exercise, and weekend catch-up sleep make ordinary life feel like repeated small time-zone shifts.

## Current Synthesis
The Talk三联 source uses social jetlag to move sleep trouble away from a narrow "not enough hours" frame. A person can sleep seven or eight hours and still feel misaligned if bedtime, wake time, light exposure, meals, bowel routines, and high-arousal evening work all point in different directions.

The strongest mechanism in the source is light. Modern environments can create "continuous twilight": indoor days are much dimmer than outdoor daylight, while evenings are kept bright by phones, screens, offices, hospitals, and schoolwork. This weakens the day-night contrast that helps the circadian system interpret when to be alert and when to wind down.

The social layer matters because the body is not fully free to follow its signals. Teenagers may naturally drift later while school forces early starts; students may skip morning bowel windows because the school day is tightly scheduled; workers may only exercise late at night after overtime. The practical answer is therefore not just discipline, but reducing avoidable conflict between schedules and body rhythms.

## Key Claims
- Social jetlag frames sleep disruption as a recurring mismatch between biological clocks and imposed social schedules.
- Sleep duration alone can hide circadian misalignment when the person's best waking hours, work hours, meals, and rest periods are shifted.
- Light contrast is central: dim indoor days and bright screen-lit nights make the body less certain about day and night.
- School schedules can push adolescents into social jetlag because puberty shifts sleep timing later while institutions still demand early starts.
- Meals and bowel routines are timing cues, so late-night eating or suppressed morning defecation can extend rhythm conflict beyond sleep.
- Over-management through trackers, strict diets, or late-night exercise can worsen body mismatch when metrics override felt state.
- Practical repair starts with stronger morning light, dimmer evenings, earlier food cutoff, caffeine caution, gentler late routines, and respect for chronotype variation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is public health reporting, not a clinical circadian-disorder protocol. It does not settle individual treatment for insomnia, delayed sleep phase, shift work, depression, gastrointestinal disease, adolescent school policy, or exercise safety. Chronotype flexibility is preserved: the goal is stable and sustainable rhythm, not forcing everyone into early rising.
