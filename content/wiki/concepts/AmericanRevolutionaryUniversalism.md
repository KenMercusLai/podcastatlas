# American Revolutionary Universalism

## Definition

American revolutionary universalism is the framing of colonial independence as a cause carrying general claims about equality, liberty, consent, and republican government rather than only a local separation from Britain.

## Current Synthesis

The episode traces this frame from [[ThomasPaine|Paine]]'s claim that America represented all humanity to the [[DeclarationOfIndependence|Declaration]]'s equality language. Universalism helped unify colonists, attract foreign sympathy, and give later reformers a standard with which to criticize the United States. Its power is inseparable from its contradiction: the language was articulated by an elite revolution that included slaveholders and did not apply equality consistently.

## Key Claims

- Universal language enlarged a colonial dispute into a moral and political cause.
- Republican and natural-rights vocabularies allowed different audiences to attach their own meanings to independence.
- The Declaration converted universal claims into an official justification for a new polity.
- Foreign sympathy and diplomacy were strengthened when the rebellion appeared to represent more than local interest.
- Later reformers could use the founding promise against exclusions the founders preserved.
- Slavery makes universalism a contested standard rather than proof of consistent founding practice.

## Evidence

- Paine's frame: [[349-the-birth-of-the-united-states-part-3-glt9804967639]] emphasizes the claim that America's cause belonged to all humanity and attracted hopes from radicals outside America.
- Ideological mixture: [[349-the-birth-of-the-united-states-part-3-glt9804967639]] connects republican language to Roman precedent, Enlightenment thought, biblical traditions, and Lockean social contract.
- Declaration and afterlife: [[349-the-birth-of-the-united-states-part-3-glt9804967639]] presents equality as central to later appeals by Abraham Lincoln and Martin Luther King Jr.
- Founding contradiction: [[349-the-birth-of-the-united-states-part-3-glt9804967639]] pairs universal equality with Jefferson's slaveholding and removal of an anti-slavery grievance from the draft.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source does not show that all revolutionaries shared one universal program or that universal language determined equal practice. The same claims could unify diverse constituencies while leaving slavery, political exclusion, and elite leadership intact.

## What Changed

- Created the concept to hold together universal founding language, international appeal, later reform use, and the slavery contradiction.
