# Seasonal Burning Agriculture / 季节性烧山农业

Seasonal burning agriculture / 季节性烧山农业 is the dry-season farming practice discussed in [[ep267-tafang-meigonghe-yuji-hunfeiyi-yu-shuili-diguo-lkq2vxaatn4wxue7y5q1fc3-8yfk]]. [[XingHaiyang|邢海洋]] describes March and April burning in parts of [[MainlandSoutheastAsia|中南半岛]] as preparation for rainy-season cultivation: vegetation is cleared and ash becomes fertilizer before the monsoon arrives.

The source keeps the practice double-edged. Burning can be a local agricultural technique, but it also produces severe haze, PM2.5 exposure, and landscape change, especially when connected to broader clearing and dry-season weather. The concept therefore belongs with [[AgriculturalSystemsReality]] and [[EnvironmentalTradeoffAccounting]] rather than a simple moral label.

## Key Claims
- Burning is tied to seasonal timing, not random disorder.
- The same practice can support low-input agriculture and create serious health and ecological costs.
- Dry-season haze should be read through farm calendars, land pressure, wind, enforcement, and alternatives.
- Climate and agriculture become visible together when smoke changes the traveler's first impression of a tropical region.
