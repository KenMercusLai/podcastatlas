# Step-Count Health Dose Response

## Definition
Step-count health dose response is the graded association between daily walking volume, walking intensity, and health outcomes, with meaningful gains below 10,000 steps and diminishing marginal benefit at higher counts.

## Current Synthesis
The episode replaces a binary 10,000-step goal with a curve. Moving from very low activity toward 4,000 and then about 7,000 daily steps is associated with substantial benefit, while additional steps above roughly 7,000 still help but add less. Cadence supplies a second dimension: brisk intervals may increase benefit without requiring the highest daily volume. The practical message is progress from one's current level, not a universal pass-fail threshold.

## Key Claims
- The 10,000-step target originated in Japanese marketing rather than a medically established cutoff.
- Any increase from a low baseline can matter; benefit does not begin only after reaching 10,000.
- A 2025 review cited by the episode places much of the observed risk reduction around 7,000 daily steps.
- Marginal gains taper as step count rises, so 7,000 to 10,000 adds less than 2,000 to 4,000 in the cited evidence.
- Faster cadence can partly compensate for lower volume, making count and intensity complementary.
- Population associations should guide attainable activity goals rather than rigid individual prescriptions.

## Evidence
- Origin of the target: [[effective-non-altruism-elon-musks-foundation-6ae30277305baddee189d087]] traces 10,000 steps to advertising before the 1964 Tokyo Olympics and the visual appeal of the Japanese character for 10,000.
- Lower-count gains: [[effective-non-altruism-elon-musks-foundation-6ae30277305baddee189d087]] reports a 2025 review associating 7,000 versus roughly 2,000 steps with lower mortality, cardiovascular-disease, and diabetes risk.
- Diminishing returns: [[effective-non-altruism-elon-musks-foundation-6ae30277305baddee189d087]] says mortality benefit increased only slightly from 7,000 to 10,000 in that review.
- Cadence: [[effective-non-altruism-elon-musks-foundation-6ae30277305baddee189d087]] summarizes a roughly 65,000-person [[UKBiobank]] analysis linking faster walking with better outcomes and identifying brisk intervals near 100 steps per minute as useful.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode summarizes review and cohort associations without supplying full study methods, outcome definitions, confounder adjustment, uncertainty intervals, or subgroup results. Reverse causation and baseline health can influence observed step counts. “7,000” and “100 steps per minute” are population summaries, not personalized medical thresholds, and mobility limits, disability, cardiovascular risk, medication, pain, pregnancy, age, environment, and injury can alter safe activity.

## What Changed
- Created a dose-response model replacing 10,000 steps as an all-or-nothing benchmark.
- Added cadence as a complementary dimension to volume.
- Preserved observational and individualization limits around the numerical targets.
