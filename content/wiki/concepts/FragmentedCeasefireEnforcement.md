# Fragmented Ceasefire Enforcement

Fragmented ceasefire enforcement is the problem of implementing a ceasefire when the nominal parties are coalitions rather than tightly controlled organizations. [[outsider-chance-ending-sudans-proxy-war-6a75b3695d765781a2b1b132]] applies the concept to [[Sudan]], where [[TomGardner]] says both the [[SudaneseArmedForces|SAF]] and [[RapidSupportForces|RSF]] have become fragmented armed camps.

The source's point is that outside agreement is not enough. Even if the [[UnitedStates]], [[Egypt]], [[SaudiArabia]], and the [[UnitedArabEmirates]] agree on a political future and pressure local partners, actual enforcement still depends on commanders, subgroups, militias, and field incentives inside Sudan.

## Key Claims
- A ceasefire can fail because signatories do not control every unit fighting in their name.
- Fragmented coalitions make verification harder because violations may be denied, localized, or blamed on undisciplined partners.
- The problem compounds [[ForeignBackedCivilWar]]: foreign patrons may influence senior leaders without being able to discipline every armed component.
