# Medical Dogma Trust Repair

## Definition
Medical dogma trust repair is the wiki's source-scoped frame for rebuilding confidence after public-health institutions overstate certainty, resist updating guidance, or treat legitimate questions as illegitimate dissent.

## Current Synthesis
The concept now has a health-agency version, a broader science-policy version, and a dissent-protection version. Makary focuses on COVID-era school closures, vaccine mandates, natural immunity, toddler masking, nutrition guidance, and FDA/CDC transparency. Kratsios extends the same trust pattern by using Fauci, school masking, protest and hospital-visit restrictions, and anti-science labeling as examples of scientific authority losing credibility. Weinstein adds that scientists must be free to investigate COVID origins and claims about Fauci without either predetermined condemnation or predetermined exoneration.

## Key Claims
- Public-health authority depends on humility, update discipline, and evidence strength as much as credentials.
- Trust is damaged when recommendations are presented as equally certain after evidence changes or when risk groups differ.
- COVID-era disputes function in the wiki as trust-repair evidence, not as settled adjudication of every intervention.
- The Kratsios source broadens the concept by arguing that people who asked questions were too readily labeled anti-science.
- The Weinstein source adds that trust repair also requires protecting inquiry into sensitive origin, funding, and institutional-responsibility questions.
- Repair requires distinguishing empirical claims, policy tradeoffs, political values, and uncertainty.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The bounded sources are critical of COVID-era institutions and sympathetic to Trump administration health or science reformers. The concept therefore stores a trust mechanism, not a final verdict on pandemic policy, vaccine policy, COVID origins, the Great Barrington Declaration, or Fauci's overall record.

## What Changed
- Added Weinstein's inquiry-protection version of medical dogma trust repair.
- Added Jay Bhattacharya and the Great Barrington Declaration as source-scoped dissent examples.
- Expanded the unresolved boundary to include COVID-origin and institutional-responsibility claims.
