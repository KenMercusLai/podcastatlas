# Medical Dogma Trust Repair

## Definition
Medical dogma trust repair is the wiki's source-scoped frame for rebuilding confidence after public-health institutions overstate certainty, resist updating guidance, or treat legitimate questions as illegitimate dissent.

## Current Synthesis
The concept now has a health-agency version and a broader science-policy version. Makary focuses on COVID-era school closures, vaccine mandates, natural immunity, toddler masking, nutrition guidance, and FDA/CDC transparency. Kratsios extends the same trust pattern by using Fauci, school masking, protest and hospital-visit restrictions, and anti-science labeling as examples of scientific authority losing credibility.

## Key Claims
- Public-health authority depends on humility, update discipline, and evidence strength as much as credentials.
- Trust is damaged when recommendations are presented as equally certain after evidence changes or when risk groups differ.
- COVID-era disputes function in the wiki as trust-repair evidence, not as settled adjudication of every intervention.
- The Kratsios source broadens the concept by arguing that people who asked questions were too readily labeled anti-science.
- Repair requires distinguishing empirical claims, policy tradeoffs, political values, and uncertainty.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Both bounded sources are critical of COVID-era institutions and sympathetic to Trump administration health or science reformers. The concept therefore stores a trust mechanism, not a final verdict on pandemic policy, vaccine policy, or Fauci's overall record.
