# Channel Native Internal Communication

## Definition
Channel native internal communication is the practice of sending employee messages through the channels workers already use, while preserving governance, analytics, and coordination for communicators. It treats email, SMS, chat, intranet, and collaboration tools as a managed distribution system rather than as disconnected destinations.

## Current Synthesis
The Workshop case suggests that internal communication products need to respect existing employee habits instead of forcing every message into a new portal. The operating challenge is to help communicators author once, adapt by channel, govern access, and measure reach without losing the native context of email, chat, SMS, or intranet surfaces.

## Key Claims
- Employees are fragmented across channels, so internal communications tools must meet them where they already work.
- Channel-native delivery does not eliminate the need for central governance; it increases the value of permissions, templates, analytics, and brand controls.
- Email can remain central while still participating in a broader internal communications stack.
- Multi-channel communication is strongest when the sender can coordinate message quality and measurement across channels from one workflow.
- The pattern is buyer-specific: communications teams, leaders, departments, and executives need different controls than ordinary chat users.

## Evidence
- Multi-channel scope: [[rick-knudtson-workshop-the-email-signal-he-ignored-for-9-months]] describes Workshop distributing internal messages across email, SMS, Slack, Microsoft Teams, SharePoint, and related channels.
- Governance and measurement: [[rick-knudtson-workshop-the-email-signal-he-ignored-for-9-months]] links internal communications work to analytics, permissions, brand consistency, and control over who sends messages.
- Email's continued role: [[rick-knudtson-workshop-the-email-signal-he-ignored-for-9-months]] says customers repeatedly identified email as the main employee communication channel even while other channels existed.
- Product positioning: [[rick-knudtson-workshop-the-email-signal-he-ignored-for-9-months]] presents Workshop as a communications-team product, not just another messaging app.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Channel-native communication can fragment attention if organizations add destinations without clear governance. The Workshop source also centers more strongly on email than on independent evidence for each supported channel, so the broader channel-native claim should remain qualified until more source material covers usage outside email.

## What Changed
- Added a concept for internal communications products that coordinate delivery across existing employee channels.
- Linked Workshop's multi-channel thesis to governance, analytics, and email-centered product-market fit.
