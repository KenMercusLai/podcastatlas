# Corporate Complicity With Authoritarianism / 企业对威权的共谋

## Definition

Corporate complicity with authoritarianism is business participation in abusive state capacity or dispossession while that participation is framed as trade, neutrality, legal compliance, ordinary acquisition, technical service, or distance through subsidiaries.

## Current Synthesis

The IBM and Dehomag case shows complicity through ongoing operational capacity. Punched-card systems required ownership arrangements, card designs, customization, maintenance, training, expertise, and political access rather than a single generic product sale. Corporate control and benefit therefore persisted through a local subsidiary even when public rhetoric emphasized world trade or distance.

The Kristallnacht episode adds complicity through [[EconomicAryanization|economic Aryanization]]. Forced sales, license removal, fines, intimidation, and confiscation created acquisition opportunities for banks, industrial firms, professionals, and local competitors. A transaction can preserve legal form while racial law and violence destroy meaningful consent; benefiting from such transfers embeds firms in persecution even when they do not design the entire regime.

Across both cases, the strongest assessment asks what capacity or benefit the firm supplied, what control and knowledge it retained, how coercion shaped the transaction, and what alternatives were available. Legal permission and commercial paperwork do not settle moral responsibility when law itself organizes exclusion and violence.

## Key Claims

- Subsidiaries and local partners can create reputational distance without eliminating control, service, or profit.
- Ongoing customization and maintenance create deeper responsibility than a one-time generic sale.
- Racially coerced asset transfers are not ordinary market transactions merely because they use contracts and payment.
- Material benefit can recruit firms into persecution and give them incentives to normalize it.
- Idealistic trade language and legal-compliance defenses require testing against retained knowledge, capacity, and benefit.
- Corporate responsibility varies by agency and evidence but does not vanish because the state is the primary criminal actor.

## Evidence

- Operational-capacity case: [[133-ibm-yu-nacui-weishenme-putongren-bu-ying-zanmei-jizeizhuyi-815684110]] describes IBM, Dehomag, customized punched cards, maintenance, ownership, elite access, and administrative use.
- Comparative technical agency: [[133-ibm-yu-nacui-weishenme-putongren-bu-ying-zanmei-jizeizhuyi-815684110]] contrasts Jacobus Lentz's optimization with Rene Carmille's technical resistance.
- Dispossession case: [[410-the-nazis-in-power-the-night-of-broken-glass-part-7-glt5771265943]] describes forced sales, discriminatory fiscal pressure, violence, and named corporate beneficiaries.
- Post-pogrom acceleration: [[410-the-nazis-in-power-the-night-of-broken-glass-part-7-glt5771265943]] connects collective punishment and exclusion law to the transfer of remaining Jewish businesses.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The IBM source is a strongly argued podcast account rather than a complete corporate archive, while the Kristallnacht source names beneficiaries without giving a firm-by-firm evidentiary record. Knowledge, control, coercion, transaction terms, and capacity to refuse may differ across cases. Technology and organizational expertise are not inherently criminal, and state responsibility remains primary, but those qualifications do not justify treating customized enablement or coerced acquisitions as neutral commerce.

## What Changed

- Added racially coerced asset acquisition to the existing operational-capacity model of corporate complicity.
- Distinguished legal transaction form from meaningful consent under persecution.
- Added material benefit as a mechanism connecting firms to distributed authoritarian participation.
