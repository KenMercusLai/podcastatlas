# Advance News Trading Experiment

## Definition
Advance news trading experiment names cases where unusually good or early access to news is tested against actual trading outcomes. The concept asks whether knowing the headline is enough, or whether expectation reading, timing, sizing, and instrument choice decide the result.

## Current Synthesis
The original evidence comes from [[cockroaches-1-modi-0-indias-remarkable-protests-6a6729a33e2f0b6f92d26ab8]], where participants saw historical next-day newspaper front pages and then traded stocks and bonds. Ordinary participants and AI models struggled because markets move against expectations, not headlines alone, and because poor [[PositionSizing]] turned partial insight into loss.

The Duanwen Polymarket episode adds a less privileged but more journalistic version. [[XiaoweiDuanwenReporter|小薇]] does not know tomorrow's news, but she begins with daily news training and close attention to official statements. Her simulated loss on [[Polymarket]] reinforces the same result: information quality is only one part of trading edge, and it can be defeated by prior market pricing, late entry, settlement wording, and emotional attention changes.

## Key Claims
- Better news access or stronger news habits do not automatically create investable edge.
- The [[MarketExpectationGap]] matters because prices react to surprise relative to expectations, not to headline importance alone.
- Sizing and abstention can matter more than directional correctness.
- Prediction-market trading adds settlement wording as another obstacle: the trade pays on contract resolution, not on the full social meaning of the event.
- Trading exposure can change news attention, making public events feel like portfolio threats.

## Evidence
- Advance-headline experiment: [[cockroaches-1-modi-0-indias-remarkable-protests-6a6729a33e2f0b6f92d26ab8]] describes 120 participants trading after seeing historical future front pages; only about half finished above the starting amount and one in six went bust.
- Expert-trader contrast: [[cockroaches-1-modi-0-indias-remarkable-protests-6a6729a33e2f0b6f92d26ab8]] says five expert macro traders all made money by sizing sharply and sometimes refusing to trade.
- Reporter simulation: [[dang-xinwen-chengwei-dupan-jizhe-keyi-da-zhuan-yibi-ma-f8800ef95c64ad18f08802b15cd20b79]] describes Xiaowei's one-month simulated Polymarket test, including the Hormuz reversal and final virtual loss.
- Attention cost: [[dang-xinwen-chengwei-dupan-jizhe-keyi-da-zhuan-yibi-ma-f8800ef95c64ad18f08802b15cd20b79]] says breaking-news alerts began to trigger worry about trading losses rather than concern about the event itself.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- The two source cases are not identical: one uses historical next-day front pages, while the other uses contemporaneous public news and a simulation tool.
- The Duanwen experiment lasted only one month and used virtual funds, so it cannot establish a durable return profile.
- Experts can sometimes convert information into edge, but the evidence stresses calibration, sizing, and abstention rather than information alone.
