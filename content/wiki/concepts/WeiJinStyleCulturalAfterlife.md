# Wei-Jin Style Cultural Afterlife / 魏晋风度文化后效

Wei-Jin style cultural afterlife / 魏晋风度文化后效 is the cultural bridge [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1013-dianwei-zhisi-jingshi-caocao-haose-haileta-lhe6jrpjp3cfbdhvjtdiezijfnoh|Hanji 1013]] opens through [[HeYan|何晏]]. The episode uses He Yan's place in [[CaoCao|曹操]]'s household, his beauty, his xuanxue role, and his reputed five-stone-powder use to point forward from late-Han family politics to later Wei-Jin elite style.

The source's point is not that He Yan alone explains Wei-Jin culture. It treats him as an early visible carrier of practices and tastes that later become symbolic of literati style: medicine or drug use, fragrance, bodily presentation, aesthetic self-display, and qingtan. The host's evaluation remains ambivalent, crediting cultural influence while criticizing the aristocratic indulgence and moral consequences attached to that style.

## Key Claims
- A household incorporation in late-Han politics can have cultural afterlife when the child grows into a visible elite style-maker.
- The episode frames Wei-Jin style as partly embodied practice, not only abstract philosophy.
- He Yan is kept as a complex source-scoped figure: talented and influential, but not sanitized.
- The concept is a bridge marker for future Wei-Jin material rather than a complete account of xuanxue or Wei-Jin culture.
