## Definition
Football club custodial governance is an interim or recurring operating arrangement in which a club lacks one clear controlling parent company and instead relies on coordinated public oversight, multiple supporters, professional management, and tightly reviewed finances.

## Current Synthesis
The [[TianjinJinmenTiger]] case presents custodial governance as a possible post-spending-boom survival model. Multi-party support and strict review can prioritize timely wages, bonuses, and financial stability, while lean staffing and close supporter contact can make the club feel more civic than corporate.

The arrangement does not by itself create durable institutions. Its funding base, next cycle, authority boundaries, and succession system must remain credible. When one manager-coach supplies most of the recruitment judgment, tactical direction, player trust, and public legitimacy, apparently stable custody may still contain a concentrated continuity risk.

## Key Claims
- Custody can preserve club operations when no single parent company or controlling shareholder provides a conventional ownership structure.
- Multi-party support and public-sector oversight can improve payment reliability and constrain undisciplined spending.
- Lean operation can strengthen local proximity, but it may also leave limited organizational redundancy.
- Matchday attendance, merchandise, and supporter access can make the club function as a civic asset as well as a team.
- Stability during one custody cycle does not resolve the next-cycle funding, control, or succession problem.
- Concentrating management and coaching in one trusted figure can improve alignment while weakening institutional transferability.

## Evidence
- Financial and control structure: [[vol-229-fafen-zhihou-tianjin-baojilu-ft-shenwei-gkwrijeosmquazu-rgttvpfk]] describes multi-party support, strict financial review, sports-authority supervision, and regular wage and bonus payment.
- Operating capacity: [[vol-229-fafen-zhihou-tianjin-baojilu-ft-shenwei-gkwrijeosmquazu-rgttvpfk]] describes a small staff whose members cover multiple functions.
- Civic character: [[vol-229-fafen-zhihou-tianjin-baojilu-ft-shenwei-gkwrijeosmquazu-rgttvpfk]] connects large home crowds, merchandise demand, and closer player-supporter relations to a possible "citizens' club" identity.
- Continuity risk: [[vol-229-fafen-zhihou-tianjin-baojilu-ft-shenwei-gkwrijeosmquazu-rgttvpfk]] says the next custody arrangement was unclear and depicts [[YuGenwei]] as unusually difficult to replace.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source provides no legal map of the custody contracts, audited income data, complete contributor list, or comparison with other Chinese clubs. "Citizens' club" is an analogy, not evidence of supporter ownership or formal civic control. Payment stability and sporting success during the observed period do not establish long-run replicability.

## What Changed
- Established custodial football-club governance as a distinct operating model.
- Separated current payment stability from unresolved ownership, funding, and succession durability.
