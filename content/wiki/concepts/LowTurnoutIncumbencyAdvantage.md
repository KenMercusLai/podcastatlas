# Low-Turnout Incumbency Advantage

## Definition
Low-turnout incumbency advantage is the electoral mechanism by which danger, cynicism, weak alternatives, or participation costs keep dissatisfied citizens home while an incumbent's organization and loyal constituencies continue to mobilize.

## Current Synthesis
The Nigeria segment separates popularity from victory. Economic pain and insecurity can make an incumbent unpopular without producing defeat when party machinery, funding, visibility, identity loyalty, selective service credit, and opposition weakness shape who knows the candidates and who reaches the ballot box.

In Borno, voting can also carry physical risk. The source reports turnout below 20% there and 27% nationwide in 2023, making nonparticipation an active part of the electoral system rather than merely a passive measure of dissatisfaction.

## Key Claims
- Candidate popularity and electoral victory can diverge sharply under low participation.
- Incumbents possess organizational, funding, visibility, and service-credit advantages.
- Religious and ethnic affiliation can stabilize support despite performance dissatisfaction.
- Insecurity raises the direct cost of voting and can selectively demobilize opponents.
- Cynicism and unfamiliarity with alternatives allow organized loyalists to carry greater weight.

## Evidence
- Incumbent machinery and identity: [[songs-of-disenchantment-the-sorrow-of-nigeria-6275426999307c5f9d5c7073a5b16e0d]] describes ruling-party visibility, funding, religious solidarity in the north, and Yoruba affiliation in the southwest.
- Participation cost: [[songs-of-disenchantment-the-sorrow-of-nigeria-6275426999307c5f9d5c7073a5b16e0d]] says travel to vote in Borno can expose residents to militants and terrorists.
- Turnout mechanism: [[songs-of-disenchantment-the-sorrow-of-nigeria-6275426999307c5f9d5c7073a5b16e0d]] reports sub-20% Borno turnout and 27% national turnout in 2023 and links abstention to incumbent advantage.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
One election and one correspondent's interpretation do not establish a universal causal rule. Low turnout can hurt incumbents, identity loyalties can change, and election administration, coercion, opposition coordination, local candidates, and regional variation may matter independently. The episode does not provide voter-level causal evidence.

## What Changed
- Created a mechanism-level explanation for how severe dissatisfaction may fail to defeat an organized incumbent.
