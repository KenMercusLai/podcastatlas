# Maryland v. King

## Definition
Maryland v. King is the Supreme Court DNA-collection precedent used in [[tech-20260831-0831-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260831-0831-mp-tech-pod-128]] as a contrast case for immigration-detainee DNA collection.

## Current Synthesis
The episode presents Maryland v. King as legally important because it involved DNA collection from people criminally arrested on probable cause for serious crimes. [[StevieGleiberson]] argues that DHS and ICE detainee DNA collection is different because the trigger can be immigration or federal detention rather than criminal arrest, and because the episode does not identify comparable judicial review or removal safeguards.

## Key Claims
- Maryland v. King supplies the episode's baseline for constitutionally permitted criminal-arrestee DNA collection.
- The precedent does not automatically resolve DHS collection from immigration detainees because the detention context is different.
- Probable cause, criminal-law purpose, and process safeguards are the decisive distinctions in the episode's legal critique.

## Evidence
- Contrast with DHS collection - [[tech-20260831-0831-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260831-0831-mp-tech-pod-128]] says Gleiberson distinguishes Maryland v. King from DNA collection based only on detention.
- Serious-crime arrest boundary - [[tech-20260831-0831-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260831-0831-mp-tech-pod-128]] describes the precedent as involving criminal arrests on probable cause for serious crimes.
- Fourth Amendment significance - [[tech-20260831-0831-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260831-0831-mp-tech-pod-128]] uses the distinction to support [[CompelledDNASamplingFourthAmendment]] rather than treating DNA collection as settled across contexts.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source summarizes the precedent through Georgetown's critique and does not provide a full doctrinal analysis. Later legal sources may refine how courts apply Maryland v. King to civil detention, immigration enforcement, or CODIS retention.
