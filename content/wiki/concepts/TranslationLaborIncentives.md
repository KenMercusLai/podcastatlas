# Translation Labor Incentives / 翻译劳动激励

## Definition
Translation labor incentives are the payment, recognition, demand, editorial support, and career signals that determine how much research and revision translators can rationally invest in a publication.

## Current Synthesis
[[307-weishenme-guonei-henduo-xueshu-fanyi-zhiliang-zheme-cha-lkbjmojuqcokbee64h9hqvrzaf4r]] argues that rigorous intellectual translation is poorly represented by a simple character count. Terminology research, comparison across an author's works, checking people and places, annotation, and revision all consume time that may not increase a translator's fee or academic standing.

Low pay does not prove that any particular translation is bad, and love of an author can motivate valuable work. The institutional claim is narrower: when careful work brings little additional compensation, recognition, or sales, a publishing system cannot reliably depend on exceptional devotion to produce authoritative editions over generations.

## Key Claims
- Per-character compensation can hide large differences in research and verification effort.
- Weak academic credit reduces the career return to producing careful translations and annotations.
- Limited sales make publishers and translators cautious about investing in specialist editions.
- Personal commitment can sustain individual projects but is not a stable system for quality assurance.
- Authoritative translations require cumulative scholarly and editorial investment across editions and generations.

## Evidence
- Compensation: [[307-weishenme-guonei-henduo-xueshu-fanyi-zhiliang-zheme-cha-lkbjmojuqcokbee64h9hqvrzaf4r]] reports low per-thousand-character rates while describing research burdens not captured by length.
- Recognition and demand: [[307-weishenme-guonei-henduo-xueshu-fanyi-zhiliang-zheme-cha-lkbjmojuqcokbee64h9hqvrzaf4r]] links weak academic evaluation and slow sales to difficulty sustaining high-effort scholarly translation.
- Hidden labor: [[307-weishenme-guonei-henduo-xueshu-fanyi-zhiliang-zheme-cha-lkbjmojuqcokbee64h9hqvrzaf4r]] describes terminology comparison, contextual research, and long notes as necessary work beyond drafting sentences.
- Cumulative quality: [[307-weishenme-guonei-henduo-xueshu-fanyi-zhiliang-zheme-cha-lkbjmojuqcokbee64h9hqvrzaf4r]] presents reliable editions as products of repeated scholarly correction rather than one translator's isolated fluency.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The fee figures, sales anecdote, and evaluation claims come from one episode and are not representative industry statistics. High payment does not guarantee expertise, low payment does not erase conscientious work, and translation quality also depends on selection, training, editing, review, rights, schedules, and access to sources. Incentives are an explanatory condition rather than a complete causal account.

## What Changed
- Established a labor-and-institutions explanation for why research-intensive translation quality is difficult to reproduce consistently.
- Distinguished exceptional personal commitment from a sustainable quality system.
