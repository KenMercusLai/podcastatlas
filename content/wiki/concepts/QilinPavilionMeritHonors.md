## Definition

Qilin Pavilion merit honors / 麒麟阁功臣荣典 are a ruler-sponsored political-memory practice in which meritorious ministers are portrayed, titled, and named in a palace setting so that frontier, administrative, and succession achievements become a visible honor order.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-463-caicaikan-zai-hanxuandi-yanzhong-shui-shi-zuidade-gongchen-lqblzdg7sdkzln4vxhtnnye92292|Hanji 463]] grounds the concept in [[EmperorXuanOfHan|汉宣帝]]'s Ganlu 3 order to paint eleven meritorious ministers in the Qilin Pavilion. The episode emphasizes that the list follows Xuan's successes in the Western Regions, Western Qiang, and Xiongnu fields. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-432-kai-jinian-gongchen-zhi-xianhe-jiemi-qilinge-shiyi-gongchen-lj65dvmawrppq2iomuzarg1ah9ak|Hanji 432]] backfills the same honor roll from the perspective of [[ZhangAnshiWesternHan|张安世]], treating the pavilion as a precedent for commemorating ministers whose service made restoration durable. The portraits do not merely praise individuals; they convert the reign's restored order into court memory.

The practice is narrower than general reward. It is retrospective, selective, visual, and tied to public historical ranking. Hanji 432 adds that the list can also be read biographically: Zhang Anshi's inclusion is not only a title but a judgment that cautious competence, careful power positioning, and long service count as restoration merit. The episodes compare it with Tang Taizong's Lingyan Pavilion honors, but the wiki keeps this page centered on the Western Han case: Xuan's honor roll marks a late-reign statement that "middle restoration" depended on a ministerial coalition as well as imperial judgment.

## Key Claims

- The honor roll is political memory, not only personal reward.
- Portrait, office title, noble title, and name turn service into a durable palace record.
- The list links frontier achievement, administrative repair, and succession service into one restored-reign narrative.
- Huo Guang's special labeling by office and surname rather than direct personal name shows that honor can also preserve hierarchy and sensitivity.
- The Qilin Pavilion case makes ministerial merit part of imperial self-definition: Xuan's restoration is narrated through the people who made it workable.
- Zhang Anshi's placement shows that the honor roll can recognize quiet survival and stabilizing service, not only battlefield spectacle.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept is based on compact podcast accounts. It does not settle the original physical arrangement of the Qilin Pavilion images, exact court ceremony, how later historical memory transmitted the list, or each minister's individual claim to merit. Hanji 432's Zhang Anshi explanation is a representative biographical reading, not a complete prosopography of all eleven ministers. The Tang Lingyan Pavilion comparison is useful as a listening bridge, not evidence that the two practices were institutionally identical.
