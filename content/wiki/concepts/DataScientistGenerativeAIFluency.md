# Data Scientist Generative AI Fluency

## Definition
Data scientist generative AI fluency is the practical ability to select, prototype, evaluate, govern, and communicate LLM-enabled workflows while retaining the statistical, machine-learning, data, software, and domain foundations needed to judge them.

## Current Synthesis
Across EP15, EP16, and EP24, generative-AI fluency expands the data scientist role rather than replacing it. Prompting, APIs, lightweight demos, embeddings, retrieval, and AI-assisted coding matter, but they become credible only when joined to [[DomainExpertAlignment]], [[AIDataReadiness]], model choice, statistical experiment design, software-engineering judgment, privacy and bias controls, and [[AIVerification]]. The role is increasingly translational: clarify the business target, choose among LLMs and simpler methods, build a usable workflow, measure behavior across representative cases, trace failures, and communicate results to the people who remain accountable.

## Key Claims
- Generative-AI fluency starts from mathematics, statistics, machine learning, deep learning, data work, and software engineering rather than superseding them.
- Prompt quality depends on task and domain understanding; vocabulary and context matter more than isolated wording tricks.
- API, prototype, retrieval, and lightweight application skills help turn model capability into testable business workflows.
- Data scientists should choose a model for the problem, comparing LLMs with discriminative ML, rules, and other architectures.
- Evaluation requires datasets, metrics, experiment management, and safeguards against fitting prompts to a few preferred examples.
- Responsible implementation includes traceability, hallucination checks, bias review, privacy boundaries, source validation, and human ownership.
- Career titles are unstable, so practitioners should evaluate the actual responsibilities and collaboration boundaries of each role.

### Auditability and statistical evaluation
- [[ep-24-redefining-data-science-in-the-generative-ai-era]] adds problem-led model choice, domain-language prompting, embeddings, application traceability, cautious treatment of model self-explanations, and experiment discipline against prompt overfitting.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- The three sources are practitioner interviews rather than labor-market studies, curriculum evaluations, or controlled comparisons of skill portfolios.
- The right balance of prompting, coding, statistics, engineering, and domain expertise varies by role and organization.
- Easier parsing of mixed files in some LLM workflows does not eliminate preparation, retrieval-quality, privacy, or evaluation work.
- AI-generated code can reduce manual drafting while increasing the importance of architecture, testing, security, and review.

## What Changed
- Added auditability and statistical experiment management as core generative-AI data-science skills.
- Clarified that domain vocabulary strengthens prompts because it carries task structure, not because jargon is inherently better.
- Added job-description-first career judgment under unstable AI and data-science titles.
