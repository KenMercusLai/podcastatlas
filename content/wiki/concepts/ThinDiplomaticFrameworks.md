# Thin Diplomatic Frameworks

Thin diplomatic frameworks are agreements that create the appearance of settlement while leaving the decisive implementation questions unresolved. [[omission-accomplished-why-the-iran-war-cycle-spins-on-6a6c7475aa1642a30f17f1cb]] adds the concept through [[GregCarlstrom]]'s critique of the June U.S.-[[Iran]] MOU: the text says the war is ending, but disputes over [[StraitOfHormuz]] control, frozen funds, sanctions relief, the nuclear file, and regional conflicts remain live.

The concept extends the wiki's earlier [[ChokepointShippingConfidence]] branch. A phrase such as safe passage can let each side claim victory while shipowners, insurers, and military actors still face incompatible procedures. Carlstrom also generalizes the problem to [[Houthis]] and [[SaudiArabia]], [[Gaza]] and [[Hamas]], plus [[Lebanon]] and [[Hezbollah]].

[[in-arms-way-gaza-deal-sticking-points-6a71b49647235c354d3c9b74]] turns the Gaza example into a fuller case. The source says the plan has a timetable and named bodies, but unresolved [[DisarmamentWithdrawalSequencing]], verification rules, territorial control, [[GazaTechnocraticAdministration]], and Israeli election incentives still determine whether the deal changes facts on the ground.

## Key Claims
- A diplomatic text can lower public pressure briefly while postponing the concrete issues that determine whether conflict stops.
- Thin frameworks are especially fragile when they cover multiple domains: money, sanctions, shipping, arms, withdrawal, proxy groups, and political recognition.
- Ambiguity can be useful for signature but costly for implementation if each side interprets the same clause differently.
- Vague deals can become narrative assets for leaders while leaving operational actors with no stable rules.
- A plan can gain institutional names before those institutions have defined powers, staffing, procedures, or control over territory.
