# Coerced Voluntary Labor / 强制性自愿劳动

## Definition

Coerced voluntary labor is work formally described as optional but made difficult to refuse through evaluation, promotion, belonging, scheduling, or livelihood consequences.

## Current Synthesis

The Watami case shows why instruction language alone cannot identify work. Reading founder materials, writing reports, volunteering, training, arriving early, and waiting for transport were separated from scheduled labor, yet some were evaluated, necessary for performance, or created by the employer's operating conditions. The relevant test is practical control: whether a reasonable worker seeking to keep the job or compete for advancement could decline without penalty, and whether the employer structured the burden.

This mechanism joins direct organizational design with [[InternalizedOverwork]]. Workers may appear to choose extra effort, but the choice can be manufactured through scarce advancement, moral praise, and fear of being judged uncommitted.

EP157 widens the mechanism from scored off-shift activity to competitive norms. A worker may actively choose extreme hours because targets, rapid feedback, peer comparison, options, manager presence, and future reward make the choice attractive or hard to refuse. The episode adds two tests for meaningful voluntariness: whether the worker understands the health and career costs, and whether the choice raises the competitive burden borne by others.

## Key Claims

- Formal optionality does not establish meaningful freedom to refuse.
- Performance scores and promotion competition can turn participation into an implicit requirement.
- Employer-created scheduling and transport conditions can produce controlled waiting and lost recovery even without a direct task order.
- Moral labels such as growth, service, or volunteering can obscure who receives the benefit and who bears the cost.
- Practical control and predictable penalty are stronger indicators than the word “voluntary.”
- Information about health and career cost, plus spillover onto other workers, helps distinguish informed choice from a system-wide pressure mechanism.

## Evidence

- **Evaluation pressure:** [[2026-qiuji-pian-fanwai-jinru-dachang-lianggeyue-ta-xuanzele-zisha-lmtfkbce-sjbl05lcdcvs8vhtnmc]] says philosophy study, reports, and volunteering affected performance assessment and advancement.
- **Operational necessity:** [[2026-qiuji-pian-fanwai-jinru-dachang-lianggeyue-ta-xuanzele-zisha-lmtfkbce-sjbl05lcdcvs8vhtnmc]] describes early arrival to compensate for inadequate training and overnight waiting caused by late closing and unavailable transport.
- **Accountability test:** [[2026-qiuji-pian-fanwai-jinru-dachang-lianggeyue-ta-xuanzele-zisha-lmtfkbce-sjbl05lcdcvs8vhtnmc]] reports that review reconsidered activities labeled non-work and ultimately recognized the death as work-related.
- **Competitive voluntariness:** [[ep157-kun-zai-pinduoduo-de-xitong-li-weida-de-gongsi-bu-yinggai-rang-ren-gandao-haipa-ckwriued61wwabaaaacbzp2q]] argues that targets, peer norms, immediate performance rewards, future options, and incomplete cost information can make self-chosen overtime collectively coercive.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Not every optional workplace activity is coercive, and not every transport delay or competitive effort is legally compensable work. The concept requires contextual evidence about control, benefit, penalty, information, spillover, frequency, and reasonable refusal. The sources' Japanese and Chinese legal discussion is source-scoped and should not be generalized across jurisdictions.

## What Changed

- Established practical control and refusal cost as the boundary between genuine choice and nominally voluntary labor.
- Added informed-cost and competitive-spillover tests for apparently self-chosen overwork.
