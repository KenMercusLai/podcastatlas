# Public-Safety-First Urban Governance

Public-safety-first urban governance is [[SpencerPratt|Spencer Pratt]]'s source-scoped claim that [[LosAngeles|Los Angeles]] cannot rebuild, attract investment, revive production, support transit, or retain families until people believe streets and public spaces are safe. In [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-spencer-pratt-on-fixing-la-wildfires-homelessness-corruption-the-fight-to-take-it-back-41236810]], he proposes warning people for several weeks, then enforcing existing laws against public drug use, theft, disorder, animal abuse, and visible street danger.

The concept extends the wiki's [[BusinessLedGovernmentManagement]] and [[BureaucraticRiskAvoidance]] branches into city basics. It is not a full policing theory; it captures one candidate's argument that order is prerequisite infrastructure for housing, small business, film production, transit use, and fire recovery.

## Key Claims
- A city can lose economic recovery capacity if residents, crews, officers, and investors assume public spaces are uncontrolled.
- Pratt argues that non-enforcement teaches people not to call 911 and makes official crime statistics less informative.
- The source ties enforcement to treatment and shelter policy rather than presenting street sweeps as a standalone fix.
- Public safety becomes an operating dependency for [[LosAngelesMetro|Los Angeles Metro]], small businesses, schools, and independent production.
