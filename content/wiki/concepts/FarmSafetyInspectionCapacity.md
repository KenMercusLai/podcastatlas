# Farm Safety Inspection Capacity

Farm safety inspection capacity is the state-capacity constraint described in [[big-lettuce-meets-small-intestine]]. The episode argues that the [[FoodSafetyModernizationAct]] gave the [[FoodAndDrugAdministration|FDA]] new authority, but authority does not automatically produce frequent farm inspections, fully implemented water standards, or enough people to monitor a large and seasonal produce system.

The source says routine farm-safety inspections began only in 2019, years after the 2010 law passed. It also says some water standards were finalized only recently, and some farms do not have to comply until 2027.

## Key Claims
- The episode identifies limited money and limited inspector staffing as core constraints.
- A former FDA rule writer says inspecting farms once every three years would be ideal, but the agency is not meeting even that level in the source account.
- Inspection capacity matters because farm practices, water, compost, manure, and animal feces can all affect raw produce safety.
- The concept connects legal authority to implementation reality: a statute can be strong on paper while prevention remains incomplete.
