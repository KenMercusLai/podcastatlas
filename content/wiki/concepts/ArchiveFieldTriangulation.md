## Definition
Archive-field triangulation is the practice of testing records from different producers against maps, images, testimony, and direct observation of historical space.

## Current Synthesis
More material does not automatically produce stronger history. The method begins with a question, compares what institutions recorded or concealed, and asks how each source's purpose shaped its account. In the Xita research described here, Liaoning and Qing archives can expose selective statements, while Japanese publications, photographs, and maps reveal colonial ways of defining and displaying space.

Field presence adds evidence that documents often flatten. Standing at a bridge or following a route can clarify distance, visibility, movement, and the relation among sites. It does not override records; it creates another line of evidence that can reveal inconsistency or suggest better questions.

## Key Claims
- Archives made by different institutions should be compared at claim level rather than merged into one neutral record.
- Omissions, evasions, and selective descriptions can reveal historical actors' incentives.
- Maps and photographs are arguments about space as well as representations of it.
- On-site observation can test spatial relationships that prose alone leaves abstract.
- A clear research question is necessary to turn heterogeneous traces into an evidence chain.

## Evidence
- Cross-archive comparison: [[cong-dongbei-dao-dongya-lishi-shi-yige-guocheng-ren-yeshi-lgolueqq4aiwj4bg5jk-im4qhzc5]] describes reading Liaoning records alongside Qing central archives to identify concealment and motive.
- Colonial representation: [[cong-dongbei-dao-dongya-lishi-shi-yige-guocheng-ren-yeshi-lgolueqq4aiwj4bg5jk-im4qhzc5]] uses Japanese publications, photographs, and maps to examine how colonizers made space legible.
- Spatial test: [[cong-dongbei-dao-dongya-lishi-shi-yige-guocheng-ren-yeshi-lgolueqq4aiwj4bg5jk-im4qhzc5]] treats presence at sites such as Sandong Bridge as knowledge unavailable from documents alone.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Triangulation does not guarantee truth when sources share incentives, copy one another, or preserve the same exclusions. Present landscapes have also changed, so current spatial experience cannot be projected backward without reconstruction and uncertainty.
