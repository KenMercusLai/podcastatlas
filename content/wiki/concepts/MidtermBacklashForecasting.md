# Midterm Backlash Forecasting

## Definition
Midterm backlash forecasting estimates congressional and state control by combining presidential approval, economic mood, special-election evidence, map structure, shocks, and candidate quality, then comparing those expectations with later outcomes.

## Current Synthesis
The June source separated a strongly Democratic House environment from a harder Senate path requiring red-state wins. The November source reports a broader result than that base case: Democrats won both chambers, several governorships, and races across traditionally Republican states. The forecast correctly identified presidential backlash, economic anxiety, candidate selection, and the possibility that a Senate win would imply a wider sweep, while underweighting or at least not resolving the eventual scale of Senate and state-level movement.

The case supports probabilistic rather than binary evaluation. A forecast can identify chamber asymmetry and the right causal variables while still missing the upper tail of a wave.

## Key Claims
- Presidential approval, economic anxiety, and special-election signals can align into a strong opposition-party environment.
- Senate geography can make one chamber harder to win without making it immune to a sufficiently broad wave.
- Candidate quality and visible prices shape how national conditions translate into individual contests.
- A Senate win in a difficult map can signal an even wider House and gubernatorial sweep.
- Forecast quality should be evaluated against both direction and magnitude, not only whether one chamber changed hands.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The earlier probabilities are a June snapshot and the later file is an immediate interpretive summary rather than official results or a formal forecast scorecard. A lower-probability Senate win is not a failed probabilistic forecast by itself; evaluating calibration requires many comparable forecasts, not one outcome.
