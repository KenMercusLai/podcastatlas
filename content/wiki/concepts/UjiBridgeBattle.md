# Uji Bridge Battle

## Definition
The Uji Bridge Battle is the failed anti-Taira stand where [[PrinceMochihito]] and [[MinamotoNoYorimasa]] are defeated while trying to turn imperial and Minamoto opposition into open revolt.

## Current Synthesis
[[659-dawn-of-the-samurai-bloodbath-at-the-bridge-part-2-glt3420393571]] treats Uji Bridge as both a military defeat and a memory-forming scene. The plot against [[TairaNoKiyomori]] is discovered early, forcing Mochihito and Yorimasa to flee Kyoto, gather limited support from Miidera monks, and move toward Nara in hopes of building a larger anti-Taira force.

At Uji, Yorimasa orders bridge planks removed to slow the Taira. The defenders briefly gain a dramatic defensive setting, but the Taira cross in force, overwhelm them, and kill Mochihito. Yorimasa's death then becomes the episode's clearest early seppuku memory scene.

## Key Claims
- Uji Bridge is an opening rupture in the anti-Taira conflict, not a successful battle.
- The revolt fails because the coalition lacks enough monastic and military support before Taira forces arrive.
- Bridge sabotage shows tactical adaptation but cannot compensate for Taira numerical superiority.
- The battle's cultural importance comes from Heike-style heroism and Yorimasa's death more than from military success.
- News of the defeat helps embolden Yoritomo to raise the Minamoto banner.

## Evidence
- Coalition limits: [[659-dawn-of-the-samurai-bloodbath-at-the-bridge-part-2-glt3420393571]] says Miidera supports Mochihito but other nearby monasteries do not join in time.
- Defensive tactic: [[659-dawn-of-the-samurai-bloodbath-at-the-bridge-part-2-glt3420393571]] describes removed bridge planks and defenders fighting from the broken bridge.
- Memory formation: [[659-dawn-of-the-samurai-bloodbath-at-the-bridge-part-2-glt3420393571]] presents Jomyo's heroic combat and Yorimasa's seppuku as Heike-style scenes.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode relies on dramatic material from [[TheTaleOfTheHeike]] for some combat details, so individual feats, speeches, and ritualized death elements should remain source-scoped.
