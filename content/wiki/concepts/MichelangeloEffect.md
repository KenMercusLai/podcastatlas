# Michelangelo Effect

## Definition
The Michelangelo effect is the source's account of partners helping one another move from their current selves toward valued ideal selves through affirmation, opportunity, encouragement, and behavior that fits those aspirations.

## Current Synthesis
The sculpture metaphor treats a desired possible self as something a responsive partner can help reveal rather than impose. Growth depends on knowing what the other person actually values and offering support compatible with that direction; substituting one's own ideal for the partner's aspiration would be control rather than affirmation.

The effect helps explain the promise and cost of high-expectation marriage. Mutual growth can deepen connection and psychological fulfillment, but it requires attention, compatible aspirations, and enough relational resources. It therefore complements [[RelationshipSelfExpansion]] while remaining narrower: self-expansion concerns enlarged identity and capability broadly, whereas this effect concerns movement toward a particular valued self.

## Key Claims
- Partners can influence whether valued possible selves become easier to enact.
- Effective affirmation begins with the supported person's own aspirations rather than the partner's preferred redesign.
- Encouragement, opportunity, feedback, and everyday interaction can align behavior with an ideal self.
- Mutual support can make personal growth a source of relational fulfillment.
- Incompatible aspirations, low investment, or controlling support can block or distort the process.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The supplied summary does not identify the underlying studies, measures, samples, effect sizes, or direction of causality. The concept should not justify reshaping a partner against their values, making one person responsible for another's development, or treating growth as proof of relationship health. Independent capacities, friendships, and structural opportunity still matter.

## What Changed
- Established the concept and distinguished partner affirmation from imposed change and from broader relationship self-expansion.
