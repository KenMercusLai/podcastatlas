# Identified Motivation Persistence

## Definition
Identified motivation persistence is continued effort based on personally endorsing an activity's importance and value, even when the activity itself is no longer interesting or enjoyable.

## Current Synthesis
[[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-db5f9d45fb650a533486e6eb38780ebf-49695184-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-db5f9d45fb650a533486e6eb38780ebf-49695184|Wellness 2.0: Who Do You Want To Be?]] separates three motivational states that are often collapsed. Intrinsic motivation comes from the activity's present interest or enjoyment. External control relies on reward, punishment, approval, or loss avoidance. Identified motivation persists because the person regards the action as meaningful, important, or expressive of chosen values.

The distinction explains how a self-concordant path can remain difficult. In the episode's Pacific Crest Trail example, enjoyment reportedly declined as hiking became repetitive and arduous, yet personally endorsed meaning could still sustain the journey. This makes identified motivation a bridge between fit and endurance without turning hardship itself into proof that a goal is worthwhile.

## Key Claims
- Intrinsic enjoyment and personally endorsed importance are distinct reasons for acting.
- Enjoyment can decline during a worthwhile long-term project without invalidating the project.
- Value endorsement can sustain effort when novelty, pleasure, or flow weakens.
- Identified motivation differs from compliance driven mainly by reward, punishment, approval, or fear of loss.
- Persistence remains conditional on safety, feasibility, opportunity cost, and continuing goal fit.
- Difficulty is not evidence by itself that an activity is meaningful or should continue.

## Evidence
- Motivation distinction - [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-db5f9d45fb650a533486e6eb38780ebf-49695184-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-db5f9d45fb650a533486e6eb38780ebf-49695184]] separates intrinsic, extrinsic, and identified reasons for action.
- Endurance example - [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-db5f9d45fb650a533486e6eb38780ebf-49695184-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-db5f9d45fb650a533486e6eb38780ebf-49695184]] reports declining intrinsic motivation among Pacific Crest Trail hikers while value-based importance remained a possible source of persistence.
- Goal-fit connection - [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-db5f9d45fb650a533486e6eb38780ebf-49695184-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-db5f9d45fb650a533486e6eb38780ebf-49695184]] places identified motivation downstream of choosing goals that express values and deeper inclinations.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode does not provide the hiking study's design, sample, effect sizes, or comparison with people who stopped. People can sincerely endorse harmful, infeasible, or socially imposed goals, and identity language can conceal coercion or sunk-cost escalation. Value-based persistence therefore does not supersede injury, exhaustion, financial limits, caregiving duties, changing evidence, or the need to disengage and choose again.

## What Changed
- Established identified motivation as distinct from both enjoyment and external control.
- Added a persistence model that permits declining pleasure without treating endurance as unlimited.
