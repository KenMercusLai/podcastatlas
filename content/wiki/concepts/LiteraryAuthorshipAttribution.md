# Literary Authorship Attribution

## Definition

Literary authorship attribution is the inference of who wrote an anonymous or disputed text by combining documentary provenance, chronology, transmission history, comparative corpora, stylistic features, and evidence capable of disconfirming a favored candidate.

## Current Synthesis

[[twas-the-night-before-christmas-twas-the-night-before-christmas]] shows why a persuasive attribution cannot rest on resemblance or expert reputation alone. [[DonFoster]] used meter, vocabulary, punctuation, attitudes, and computer-assisted patterns to argue for [[HenryLivingstonJr]], while Livingston family recollections supplied an explanatory narrative.

The rebuttal exposes the necessary checks. Candidate corpora must be representative; quotation context and family chronology must survive verification; missing manuscripts cannot be treated as available proof; and supposedly incompatible writing by the conventional author must be actively sought. In this case, relevant verse by [[ClementClarkeMoore]] changes the judgment because it directly defeats the claim that he did not write in the required mode.

## Key Claims

- Stylistic resemblance is probabilistic evidence, not an authorship certificate.
- Corpus selection determines which habits appear characteristic or exceptional.
- Documentary provenance and chronology constrain what stylistic comparison can plausibly show.
- Family memory can generate leads but requires contemporaneous corroboration.
- Candidate exclusion is weak unless the full relevant range of that candidate's writing has been examined.
- Prior expert successes and failures affect warranted confidence but do not decide a case independently.

## Evidence

- Multi-feature comparison: [[twas-the-night-before-christmas-twas-the-night-before-christmas]] describes Foster's use of meter, word choice, punctuation, attitudes, and statistical patterns.
- Provenance gap: [[twas-the-night-before-christmas-twas-the-night-before-christmas]] distinguishes descendants' recollections and a reported destroyed manuscript from inspectable contemporary evidence.
- Disconfirming search: [[twas-the-night-before-christmas-twas-the-night-before-christmas]] reports Moore's anapestic St. Nicholas letter and child-oriented winter verse, directly challenging an exclusion premise.
- Reliability calibration: [[twas-the-night-before-christmas-twas-the-night-before-christmas]] uses Foster's mixed record to qualify authority while resting the substantive judgment on case-specific evidence.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Stylometry and close reading can identify real authorship patterns, and the episode includes Foster's successful *Primary Colors* attribution. Documentary survival is uneven, so the absence of a Livingston manuscript is not proof of non-authorship. Conversely, Moore's stylistic compatibility is not by itself conclusive. The current judgment favors Moore because several evidence types converge, not because any single test is decisive.

## What Changed

- Established a framework that subordinates stylistic resemblance and expert authority to converging, testable evidence.
- Added active search for candidate-disconfirming texts as a core attribution requirement.
