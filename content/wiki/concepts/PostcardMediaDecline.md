# Postcard Media Decline

Postcard media decline is the "post-postcard era" frame in [[socialist-mobility-a-telling-michigan-vote-6a74600ec3e1347e8b5bf216]]. [[CatherineNixie|Catherine Nixey]] treats postcards as once cheap, visual, global, and culturally powerful, while the source says [[RoyalMail]] letter volumes and [[EnglishHeritage]] postcard-use data show how far physical correspondence has fallen.

The concept is not only nostalgia. It asks what disappears when a medium that carried travel proof, place imagery, jokes, military usefulness, and small personal gestures becomes rare.

## Key Claims
- Postcards were cheaper and lighter than letters in envelopes, which helped them scale as a mass medium.
- The source credits [[TheodoreHook]] with sending the first postcard in 1840.
- Postcards helped standardize how places were seen through saturated travel imagery, sunsets, palm trees, and comic or vulgar local styles.
- Postal decline changes social practice: a quick physical message becomes an exceptional act rather than a default travel gesture.
- Preservation should include the medium's social function, not only individual card images.
