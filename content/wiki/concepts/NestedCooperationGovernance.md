# Nested Cooperation Governance

## Definition
Nested cooperation governance evaluates cooperation and competition at multiple levels, asking whether coordination within one group supports or damages the larger systems on which that group depends.

## Current Synthesis
The source refuses a simple cooperation-good, competition-bad rule. Immune cells may compete while sustaining an organism; cancer cells may cooperate effectively while killing the host; firms may coordinate to provide public value or collude as a cartel. Governance therefore needs an explicit system boundary, collectively chosen definitions of health and excess, feedback across levels, and accountability when a local coalition externalizes harm. The relevant unit is a network of relationships rather than the apparent success of one participant or partnership.

## Key Claims
- Cooperation can be harmful when it strengthens a coalition at the expense of its host or public.
- Competition can be useful when it supports adaptation, discovery, or system-level resilience.
- Evaluation depends on the level and time horizon at which outcomes are measured.
- Governance must define health, well-being, unacceptable excess, and accountability collectively.
- No single optimizer adequately represents a living or social network with plural purposes.

## Evidence
### Biological Levels
- [[02026-thurston-aguera-y-arcas-podcast-v4]] contrasts competing immune cells that serve an organism with cooperating cancer cells that can kill it.

### Economic Coordination
- [[02026-thurston-aguera-y-arcas-podcast-v4]] uses collusion, cartels, beneficial competition, and races to the bottom to show that either coordination mode can help or harm.

### Public Standards
- [[02026-thurston-aguera-y-arcas-podcast-v4]] argues for collectively chosen definitions of health, well-being, excess, and systems of accountability.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The framework does not itself choose the proper system boundary, resolve conflicts among scales, or supply measurable thresholds. Appeals to a larger whole can justify suppressing minorities, while narrow participant rights can permit large externalities. Collective definition also raises representation and enforcement problems that the source identifies but does not institutionally design.

## What Changed
- Established a multilevel test for judging cooperation, competition, and technological collaboration.
