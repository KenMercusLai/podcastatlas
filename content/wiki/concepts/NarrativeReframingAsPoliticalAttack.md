## Definition

叙事改写式政治攻击 / narrative reframing as political attack is the tactic of changing how a ruler understands a dispute so that the same facts no longer appear as ordinary conflict, mutual accusation, or defense, but as disloyalty, obstruction, or threat to public order.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-451-ruzhe-xiaowangzhi-shui-youxiu-wo-jiu-nongsi-shui-lo20q2_kdbmsw2zfhehoanceo2oa|Hanji 451]] grounds the pattern through [[XiaoWangzhiWesternHan|萧望之]]'s handling of [[HanYanshouWesternHan|韩延寿]]. Han Yanshou responds to an investigation by accusing Xiao of earlier misuse of funds. Xiao does not only deny the accusation; he tells [[EmperorXuanOfHan|汉宣帝]] that, as the official responsible for documents and law, he is being resisted by Han. The host treats this as the critical move that shifts the emperor's anger and makes Han's defense look like attack.

The concept therefore focuses on framing power rather than evidence alone. Han's own old-account violations matter, and his counteraccusation fails on evidence, but the political danger rises when Xiao turns the case from "officials investigating each other" into "a subordinate obstructing lawful central authority." That reframing changes the moral and procedural terrain before the final legal decision.

## Key Claims

- Political accusation can work by changing the category of the event before all evidence is settled.
- A counterattack becomes especially risky when a superior can recast it as resistance to lawful office rather than self-defense.
- Narrative control matters because the ruler's anger and attention shape how later facts are interpreted.
- Reframing does not require inventing every fact; it can combine real faults with a more lethal interpretation.
- The tactic is distinct from ordinary slander because it targets the meaning of the dispute, not only the target's character.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept should not flatten every accusation into mere spin. Hanji 451 says Han Yanshou had real old-account exposure, and the final sentence also rests on a failed counteraccusation. The reframing claim is narrower: Xiao's decisive political move is to define Han's response as obstruction by a subordinate, thereby changing the ruler's perception of the case. The broader comparison to Zhao Guanghan and Wei Xiang remains source-scoped in Hanji 451 until integrated through direct source notes.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Hanji 451's account of Xiao Wangzhi reframing Han Yanshou's counteraccusation as resistance to lawful supervision.
