## Definition

Controlled capability visibility is the deliberate management of how competence, information, credit, and ambition appear to a power center so that value remains legible without creating avoidable threat.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 1149 develops the concept through [[XunYou|荀攸]]. He is presented as a high-value strategist who keeps plans secret, avoids broadcasting credit, and prevents relatives from turning privileged knowledge into social display. This limits both information leakage and the political meaning attached to his success.

The concept is not simple self-erasure. If competence becomes invisible, it cannot be trusted or used; if it becomes a rival prestige center, it can trigger suspicion. Controlled visibility therefore balances evidence of value, secrecy duties, credit restraint, and clarity about whose authority converts advice into action.

## Key Claims

- Secret-bearing roles require control over both content and social signaling.
- Credit can create political threat when it implies an independent following or competing center.
- Low visibility differs from low contribution.
- The strategy depends on context and can preserve hierarchy rather than justice.
- Excessive concealment can reduce accountability, learning, and rightful recognition.

## Evidence

- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1149-jiemi-sanguo-dingji-moushi-xunyou-de-guanchang-shengcunshu-ljv1fwephe86-qczfy0o1n2yh7um|Hanji 1149]] reports Xun You's secret counsel, refusal to publicize credit, and shutdown of a relative's inquiry into a plan.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source interprets Xun You's survival through low-profile conduct but does not prove that visibility alone determines survival or that every concentrated-power environment rewards the same strategy. Secrecy may protect operations while also preventing scrutiny, attribution, and institutional learning.

## What Changed

- Established controlled visibility as a balance among usefulness, secrecy, credit, and perceived threat.
