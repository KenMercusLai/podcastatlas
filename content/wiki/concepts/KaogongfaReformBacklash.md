## Definition

考功法改革反噬 / Kaogongfa reform backlash is the risk pattern where an official-performance assessment reform threatens enough incumbents that the reformer becomes politically exposed before the proposal can gain institutional protection.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-486-jiemi-yixue-dashi-jingfang-de-guanchang-chuanqi-lk_gg7uparsjgxlt4wquibcm5fpr|Hanji 486]], [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-487-xiang-bubugao-sheng-bixu-dongdian-zhichang-boyilun-llyn3gebws-gkmy8bavltcugrbet|Hanji 487]], and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-488-yixue-dashi-jingfang-ruhe-shenxian-bisi-ju-llf_4yvk8urtkgidnj2qhvca3vah|Hanji 488]] ground the concept through [[JingFang|京房]]. Jing Fang wants [[HanYuandi|汉元帝]] to use a kaogong method to evaluate officials and, if necessary, remove figures such as [[WeiXuanchengWesternHan|韦玄成]] and [[ShiXianWesternHan|石显]]. The host's reading is that this makes Jing Fang's danger structural: the proposal threatens officials who prefer vague reputation, patronage, or non-measured survival over explicit assessment.

Hanji 486 adds the origin mechanism: the reform is first justified as a response to disasters and disorder. Jing Fang argues that ancient rulers selected the worthy by achievement, while declining rulers appointed through praise, blame, and reputation. Yuan authorizes Jing Fang to draft and debate the law, but ministers and provincial inspectors immediately describe it as too intricate and hard to implement. The backlash therefore begins not only with Shi Xian's later factional move, but with broad bureaucratic resistance to being measured.

Hanji 487 shows the first backlash mechanism before formal accusation. Jing Fang tries to keep himself in the capital while students test the method locally, because reform depends on protected communication with Yuan. Shi Xian's counter is to make the reformer geographically distant: the Wei Commandery appointment looks like office but functions as isolation from the ruler, and Yuan's later cancellation of return reporting removes the channel Jing Fang knew he needed.

Hanji 488 then shows why the backlash is sharper because Jing Fang lacks the platform needed for a system-wide reform. The episode contrasts him with Zhang Juzheng's later kaocheng reform: Zhang had senior authority and political backing yet still faced posthumous retaliation, while Jing Fang is a lower-positioned court figure acting through private conversation, princely petition routing, and ruler favor.

The concept therefore names a reformer's exposure when measurement is politically correct but institutionally premature. The problem is not that official assessment is wrong; it is that assessment creates concentrated fear before a durable coalition, procedural legitimacy, and enforcement channel exist.

## Key Claims

- Performance assessment threatens officials more broadly than ordinary criticism because it can turn nonperformance into evidence.
- Assessment reform can be triggered by legitimate crisis diagnosis while still failing at the implementation-design and acceptance stage.
- A reformer without senior authority or coalition backing may become isolated even if the proposed standard is normatively sound.
- Assessment reform can trigger both factional attack and bureaucratic self-defense, starting with isolating the reformer from protected access.
- Reform danger rises when the proposal names personnel consequences rather than remaining abstract.
- Later success stories cannot be copied without matching power base, timing, and protection.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept is currently grounded in three adjacent episodes' interpretation of Jing Fang's case. It should not be used to imply that every performance-assessment reform is doomed or that opponents had no legal basis for action. Hanji 486 shows real implementation objections before factional attack; Hanji 487 frames Jing Fang's transfer as a political access cut; Hanji 488 explicitly says Jing Fang's death is tragic but not simply a clean wrongful case, because the legal-political accusation attached to real private coordination and a proposal with major personnel consequences.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 487's setup layer: kaogong begins as a pilot-reform and communication-channel problem before Hanji 488's formal death case.
- Added Hanji 486's earlier setup: Jing Fang's disaster-response justification, Yuan-authorized law debate, and broad complaints that the assessment system was too detailed to implement.
