## Definition

考功法改革反噬 / Kaogongfa reform backlash is the risk pattern where an official-performance assessment reform threatens enough incumbents that the reformer becomes politically exposed before the proposal can gain institutional protection.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-488-yixue-dashi-jingfang-ruhe-shenxian-bisi-ju-llf_4yvk8urtkgidnj2qhvca3vah|Hanji 488]] grounds the concept through [[JingFang|京房]]. Jing Fang wants [[HanYuandi|汉元帝]] to use a kaogong method to evaluate officials and, if necessary, remove figures such as [[WeiXuanchengWesternHan|韦玄成]] and [[ShiXianWesternHan|石显]]. The host's reading is that this makes Jing Fang's danger structural: the proposal threatens officials who prefer vague reputation, patronage, or non-measured survival over explicit assessment.

The backlash is sharper because Jing Fang lacks the platform needed for a system-wide reform. The episode contrasts him with Zhang Juzheng's later kaocheng reform: Zhang had senior authority and political backing yet still faced posthumous retaliation, while Jing Fang is a lower-positioned court figure acting through private conversation, princely petition routing, and ruler favor.

The concept therefore names a reformer's exposure when measurement is politically correct but institutionally premature. The problem is not that official assessment is wrong; it is that assessment creates concentrated fear before a durable coalition, procedural legitimacy, and enforcement channel exist.

## Key Claims

- Performance assessment threatens officials more broadly than ordinary criticism because it can turn nonperformance into evidence.
- A reformer without senior authority or coalition backing may become isolated even if the proposed standard is normatively sound.
- Assessment reform can trigger both factional attack and bureaucratic self-defense.
- Reform danger rises when the proposal names personnel consequences rather than remaining abstract.
- Later success stories cannot be copied without matching power base, timing, and protection.

## Evidence

- Reform target: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-488-yixue-dashi-jingfang-ruhe-shenxian-bisi-ju-llf_4yvk8urtkgidnj2qhvca3vah|Hanji 488]] says Jing Fang believes Shi Xian, Wulu Chongzong, and Wei Xuancheng dislike kaogong because it touches their interests.
- Structural threat: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-488-yixue-dashi-jingfang-ruhe-shenxian-bisi-ju-llf_4yvk8urtkgidnj2qhvca3vah|Hanji 488]] says officials want capable government in theory but do not want to be evaluated in practice.
- Weak platform: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-488-yixue-dashi-jingfang-ruhe-shenxian-bisi-ju-llf_4yvk8urtkgidnj2qhvca3vah|Hanji 488]] contrasts Jing Fang's low position with Zhang Juzheng's later senior authority and backing.
- Personnel consequence: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-488-yixue-dashi-jingfang-ruhe-shenxian-bisi-ju-llf_4yvk8urtkgidnj2qhvca3vah|Hanji 488]] says Jing Fang proposes that if kaogong cannot immediately proceed, Liu Qing could at least recommend removing Wei Xuancheng and Shi Xian and using Zheng Hong and Xu Li.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept is currently grounded in one episode's interpretation of Jing Fang's case. It should not be used to imply that every performance-assessment reform is doomed or that opponents had no legal basis for action. Hanji 488 explicitly says Jing Fang's death is tragic but not simply a clean wrongful case, because the legal-political accusation attached to real private coordination and a proposal with major personnel consequences.
