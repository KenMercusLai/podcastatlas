# Dietary Constraint Product Insight

## Definition
Dietary constraint product insight is the conversion of a health, allergy, nutrition, or institutional eating restriction into product requirements that can serve a broader market.

## Current Synthesis
[[CatalinaCrunch]], [[MadeGood]], and [[SweetLorens]] show three origins for the same discovery pattern. A founder can begin from a personally managed condition, a school rule affecting children and parents, or repeated customer messages after a product is already on shelves. In every case, the constraint becomes commercially useful only when it specifies ingredients and use occasions while the product still succeeds on taste, convenience, trust, and repeat buying.

The constraint is therefore a discovery lens, not proof of demand. It can reveal overlooked requirements and household exclusion costs, but customer payment, retailer response, sell-through, manufacturing control, and broader sensory appeal determine whether the insight becomes a durable category position.

## Key Claims
- Lived or closely observed dietary constraints can reveal product requirements that ordinary category research misses.
- Constraint origin may be individual, institutional, or customer-led rather than always coming from the founder's own diagnosis.
- A restriction becomes market evidence only when customers buy, request, reorder, or prefer the resulting product.
- Multi-person settings can enlarge the opportunity because one person's restriction changes what a school or household can share.
- Taste and familiar use remain adoption thresholds; dietary compatibility should not imply a compromised experience.
- Ingredient claims require manufacturing, cross-contact, packaging, and trust systems before they become credible at scale.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Personal symptoms, customer messages, and founder testimony do not establish medical causality or universal safety. The sources provide company cases rather than comparative nutrition studies, allergy audits, or representative market research. Constraint-led positioning can also remain too narrow, taste can fail, and compliant manufacturing can be more capital- and process-intensive than early demand signals reveal.

## What Changed
- Expanded the concept from founder-lived and school-based constraints to post-launch customer-led discovery.
- Added the shared-household inclusion mechanism shown by Sweet Loren's.
- Made taste and manufacturing credibility explicit gates between insight and durable positioning.
