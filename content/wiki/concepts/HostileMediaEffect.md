# Hostile Media Effect / 敌意媒体效应

## Definition

The hostile media effect is the tendency for strongly identified groups to interpret the same or nominally neutral coverage as biased against their own side.

## Current Synthesis

The social-psychology source establishes the audience-side pattern: partisan identity changes what counts as balance, so sincere observers can experience the same report as hostile in opposite directions. Knowledge and confidence do not automatically remove the effect because the disputed object is interpreted through group stakes.

An adjacent but distinct producer-side mechanism appears in the [[SeenOnRadio]] case. [[EthanJordan]] describes coverage as persistently anti-Trump and pro-Harris, while [[PartisanFramingAcrossExposure]] shows that outlets can also take shared material and genuinely package it into opposed moral narratives. Perceived hostility may therefore arise from motivated reception, real framing differences, or both; the concept cannot decide which is operating in a particular case without content evidence.

## Key Claims

- Group identity can change what counts as neutral or fair coverage.
- Opposing groups can sincerely perceive the same media object as biased against them.
- Political knowledge and confidence do not guarantee immunity from identity-protective interpretation.
- Real editorial framing differences can coexist with audience-side hostile perception.
- Bias diagnosis requires comparison of claims, selection, language, and omitted context rather than felt unfairness alone.

## Evidence

- Shared-object experiments: [[123-renxing-shiyan-dangxin-ni-ziji-779479569]] uses sports and Arab-Israeli news examples to show opposed perceptions of the same material.
- Field case: [[introducing-the-news-from-scene-on-radio-introducing-the-news-from-scene-on-radio]] records Jordan's asymmetric frustration with perceived Trump and Harris coverage.
- Framing boundary: [[introducing-the-news-from-scene-on-radio-introducing-the-news-from-scene-on-radio]] describes partisan interpretations of shared Ketanji Brown Jackson confirmation exchanges.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The effect does not prove that all bias complaints are mistaken or that nominally shared coverage is substantively neutral. The new source provides one participant and a summarized example rather than a content analysis. [[ConsiderTheOppositeBiasCorrection]] can test perception but cannot replace verification of selection, accuracy, emphasis, ownership, or editorial incentives.
