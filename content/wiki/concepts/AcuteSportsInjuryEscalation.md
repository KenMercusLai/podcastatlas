# Acute Sports Injury Escalation / 急性运动损伤升级

## Definition
Acute sports injury escalation / 急性运动损伤升级 is the boundary between ordinary rest-and-observe soreness and sport or fall injuries that need timely clinical assessment because swelling, bruising, deformity, function loss, locking, neurologic symptoms, or delayed treatment can worsen outcome.

## Current Synthesis
[[vol-222-aoye-xintiao-kuangbiao-shi-cusi-qianzhao-aed-hui-dian-cuo-huoren-dai-ni-bikai-zhiming-changshi-shijie-jijiu-ri-teji-lnemogq8gb9o3e2835jvtfx3emiy]] extends the wiki's exercise-safety branch from prevention into acute orthopedic routing. The source emphasizes that compensation-style exercise, inadequate warmup, fatigue, and "five more minutes" decisions can produce real tendon, ligament, meniscus, fracture, or spine-related injury.

The strongest practical claim is that function matters. Some serious injuries, including Achilles rupture, may not make walking impossible, but loss of running, jumping, push-off, or normal movement can still require early medical judgment.

The combined response frame adds an immediate sequence: prevent secondary injury, stop repeated movement or amateur stress-testing, immobilize the affected area with an appropriate support when possible, then use ice and arrange medical evaluation or rescue. This conservative public-response principle is grounded by [[vol-90-yundong-yixue-pengyou-ni-huaxue-ma-bugai-yao-chenzao-kemu-san-jinji-renqun-lvfameb1ztoxm5yx5e-ylsrekbsq]]; it is not a license to manipulate, reduce, or diagnose an injured joint at the scene.

The earlier [[vol-18-jizhen-weizhongbingke-tufa-yundong-sunshang-de-yuanqian-jijiu-he-cpr-liucheng-lsdfg_wubb1qdmi_boh8lvulyr-1|VOL.18]] episode strengthens that no-manipulation boundary. Severe pain, inability to walk, suspected fracture, cervical concern, deformity, or substantial bleeding should shift the goal from proving the diagnosis to stopping activity, minimizing movement, using simple support only when safe, and reaching qualified care without amateur reduction.

## Key Claims
- Sudden heavy or fatigue-state exercise can create acute injury even without collision.
- Rapid swelling, broad bruising, inability to bear weight, marked pain with movement, or loss of force should prompt clinical assessment.
- Visible deformity, restricted activity, locked knees, inability to straighten, catching, or painful popping can indicate deeper injury than a simple sprain.
- Older-adult falls with back or leg pain, radiating pain, numbness, or neurological symptoms can involve spine, spinal cord, or nerve risk.
- Achilles rupture can still allow walking, so preserved walking should not be treated as proof that the tendon is intact.
- Delayed treatment of some tendon injuries can increase surgical difficulty or infection risk.
- Suspected acute joint, limb, or cervical injury should be protected from repeated testing, forceful straightening, or amateur reduction; safe support and help take priority over proving what structure is damaged.

## Evidence
- Overload context: [[vol-222-aoye-xintiao-kuangbiao-shi-cusi-qianzhao-aed-hui-dian-cuo-huoren-dai-ni-bikai-zhiming-changshi-shijie-jijiu-ri-teji-lnemogq8gb9o3e2835jvtfx3emiy]] describes young people who exercise little, then play basketball or football beyond their usual load, especially when tired near the end.
- Red flags: [[vol-222-aoye-xintiao-kuangbiao-shi-cusi-qianzhao-aed-hui-dian-cuo-huoren-dai-ni-bikai-zhiming-changshi-shijie-jijiu-ri-teji-lnemogq8gb9o3e2835jvtfx3emiy]] names swelling, bruising, inability to step, loss of force, deformity, locking, pain, and abnormal movement as reasons to seek evaluation.
- Achilles and delayed care: [[vol-222-aoye-xintiao-kuangbiao-shi-cusi-qianzhao-aed-hui-dian-cuo-huoren-dai-ni-bikai-zhiming-changshi-shijie-jijiu-ri-teji-lnemogq8gb9o3e2835jvtfx3emiy]] says an Achilles rupture can leave a person able to walk while losing running or jumping, and that delay can make later treatment harder.
- Secondary-injury prevention: [[vol-90-yundong-yixue-pengyou-ni-huaxue-ma-bugai-yao-chenzao-kemu-san-jinji-renqun-lvfameb1ztoxm5yx5e-ylsrekbsq]] prioritizes stopping activity, avoiding repeated movement checks, using a support for immobilization when feasible, and then coordinating ice and care.
- No-amateur-reduction boundary: [[vol-18-jizhen-weizhongbingke-tufa-yundong-sunshang-de-yuanqian-jijiu-he-cpr-liucheng-lsdfg_wubb1qdmi_boh8lvulyr-1]] warns against moving a suspected cervical injury or forcefully straightening a painful, deformed, or possibly fractured limb, and favors clean covering, safe support, and reduced movement during transfer.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources are not injury diagnoses, reduction instructions, or treatment protocols. Scene safety, training history, age, anatomy, symptoms, imaging, examination, available first-aid training, and local medical access determine the proper route; cooling schedules and return-to-loading advice are context-sensitive, and immobilization should not create additional pain, circulation compromise, or delay emergency help.

## What Changed
- VOL.222 creates an acute sports-injury escalation concept that complements prevention-side [[ExerciseLoadManagement]] and symptom-boundary [[JointSymptomEscalation]].
- VOL.90 adds prevention of secondary injury, minimal manipulation, immobilization before repeated testing, and task-sharing for help.
- The current judgment now explicitly rejects amateur reduction and extends movement protection to suspected cervical injury, marked deformity, and bleeding-associated trauma.
