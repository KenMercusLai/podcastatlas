# Mental Imagery Spectrum

## Definition
The mental imagery spectrum describes stable variation in the vividness and availability of internally generated sensory imagery, from aphantasia through ordinary imagery to hyperphantasia.

## Current Synthesis
The source treats visual imagery and inner speech as dimensions rather than universal mental features. Eagleman's self-described aphantasia and the reported presence of aphantasia among successful animation professionals separate vivid internal pictures from visual skill, creativity, or the ability to build external representations. Strategy may adapt to the available form of thought rather than one imagery style determining capability.

## Key Claims
- People differ substantially in the vividness of voluntary visual imagery.
- Aphantasia is absence or near-absence of conscious visual imagery, while hyperphantasia involves unusually vivid imagery.
- Inner speech also varies and should not be assumed universal.
- Weak visual imagery does not imply weak visual creativity or design performance.
- External drawing, modeling, or iteration may compensate for or complement limited internal visualization.

## Evidence
- Spectrum account - [[science-tools-of-learning-memory-dr-david-eagleman-scim5564931565]] distinguishes aphantasia, ordinary imagery, hyperphantasia, and inner-voice variation.
- First-person case - [[science-tools-of-learning-memory-dr-david-eagleman-scim5564931565]] records [[DavidEagleman]] identifying himself as aphantasic.
- Creativity qualification - [[science-tools-of-learning-memory-dr-david-eagleman-scim5564931565]] reports Ed Catmull's observation that several strong Pixar directors and animators described aphantasia.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source provides interviews and a reported professional observation, not prevalence estimates, diagnostic criteria, causal evidence, or a complete account of nonvisual imagery. The suggestion that aphantasia causes stronger external design practice remains an inference.

## What Changed
- Established the imagery-variation concept and separated vividness from creative competence.
