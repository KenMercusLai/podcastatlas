# Post-Arab Spring Islamist Decline

Post-Arab Spring Islamist decline is the pattern [[spring-then-fall-a-weakened-muslim-brotherhood-6a7aef1b616564bf00d9b354]] adds through the [[MuslimBrotherhood]]. The movement briefly seemed positioned to benefit from regional uprisings, especially when [[MohamedMorsi]] won Egypt's presidency, but the source says the later pattern is repression, exile, leadership loss, fragmentation, and absence from new political settlements.

The concept is not a claim that Islamist politics has disappeared. [[GarethBrown]] says economic stagnation, regression, dignity politics, and anger around [[Gaza]] and [[Israel]] could revive demand for opposition movements. The narrower claim is that the Brotherhood no longer reliably captures that demand after [[AbdelFattahAlSisi]]'s coup, regional pressure, Western suspicion, and internal bickering.
