# Context-Dependent Performance

## Definition

Context-dependent performance is the principle that visible individual ability partly reflects the teammates, routines, coaching, tools, culture, and role in which a person works, so observed success or failure may not transfer intact to another setting.

## Current Synthesis

The episode connects basketball, cardiac surgery, financial services, and aviation through a common transfer problem. A person can improve after entering a better-fitting system, decline after leaving a strong coach or familiar collaborators, or perform worse with an unfamiliar team despite retaining the same nominal expertise. Organizations therefore risk misattributing system output to isolated stars.

The principle does not imply that talent is unreal or that intact teams should never change. Familiarity can build tacit coordination, but too much shared experience can produce rigidity. Fit can also be normatively double-edged: a cultural misfit may contribute a missing orientation, while a highly capable person can execute a misguided mission more effectively.

## Key Claims

- Individual output can change materially when coaching, teammates, routines, or organizational culture changes.
- Repeated work with a particular team can create tacit coordination that does not fully transfer with one expert.
- Hiring or rewarding a visible star alone can separate the person from contributors who helped produce the result.
- Team familiarity can outperform individual freshness in coordination-sensitive work.
- Excessive stability can harden routines, so team preservation needs renewal and review.
- Fit is not equivalent to virtue: it can unlock useful capability or improve execution of a harmful goal.

## Evidence

- Player-system interaction - [[malcolm-gladwell-debates-adam-grant-malcolm-gladwell-debates-adam-grant]] uses NBA improvement and decline after team or coach changes to challenge isolated talent judgments.
- Team-specific expertise - [[malcolm-gladwell-debates-adam-grant-malcolm-gladwell-debates-adam-grant]] reports that cardiac-surgery experience transferred incompletely across hospitals because established teams knew one another's routines and limits.
- Familiarity and rigidity - [[malcolm-gladwell-debates-adam-grant-malcolm-gladwell-debates-adam-grant]] contrasts experienced flight crews with unfamiliar rested crews, then warns that excessive shared experience can reduce adaptability.
- Normative fit boundary - [[malcolm-gladwell-debates-adam-grant-malcolm-gladwell-debates-adam-grant]] contrasts culture-complementing leadership with competent execution of a mission Gladwell considers misguided.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The supplied source summarizes examples and research claims without methods, effect sizes, samples, or competing evidence. Selection, role change, health, incentives, competition, and regression to the mean may also explain performance shifts. Team-level evaluation can conceal unequal contribution, free riding, discrimination, or individual accountability. Preserving a team can impede mobility and renewal, while breaking one up can be necessary for safety, fairness, or changed goals.
