# Symptom–Function Dissociation

## Definition
Symptom–function dissociation is the clinically important possibility that perceived discomfort or wellbeing changes while the underlying physiological function or disease process does not change in parallel.

## Current Synthesis
The episode grounds this distinction with asthma and cancer. Placebo could reduce breathing discomfort without improving measured breathing patterns, and expectation or supportive care could reduce pain, nausea, stress, or treatment burden without shrinking tumors. Subjective outcomes remain real and worth treating, but they cannot automatically serve as proxies for airway function, tumor response, or other disease markers.

## Key Claims
- Symptom relief can be meaningful even when objective function is unchanged.
- Objective improvement and subjective improvement are distinct outcomes that should both be measured.
- Placebo response is especially easy to overinterpret when comfort is treated as proof of disease modification.
- Active asthma medication in the cited example improved both breathing and discomfort, unlike placebo.
- Supportive mind-body practices can complement treatment without becoming direct tumor therapies.

## Evidence
### Asthma
- [[how-placebo-effects-work-to-change-our-biology-psychology-scim2208611274]] reports reduced perceived breathing discomfort under placebo without changed breathing patterns, while active medication improved both.

### Cancer care
- [[how-placebo-effects-work-to-change-our-biology-psychology-scim2208611274]] distinguishes relief of pain, nausea, and treatment discomfort from any claim that placebo reduces or eliminates tumors.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept does not make subjective outcomes unimportant or imply that every subjective change lacks physiological mediation. It instead requires outcome-specific evidence. The episode note does not supply complete measurement methods, effect sizes, or long-term follow-up.

## What Changed
- Created an explicit evidence boundary between experience, functional measurement, and disease modification.
