# Indie Game Commercialization / 独立游戏商业化

Indie game commercialization is the path in [[ep242-duli-youxi-shi-yitiao-gaoqian-hao-saidao-ma-lrqbzrceumbuggzuero5qb4jv3zu]] where a hobbyist or small team turns game-making into saleable work, usually through [[Steam]] and a buyout-style PC game model. The source keeps the definition loose: independent games can come from individuals, small teams, or micro-companies as long as outside capital is not deeply steering the work.

The concept's main value is its cost realism. [[DigitalGameDistribution]] can lower publishing friction, but it does not remove development time, art and music cost, localization, platform share, tax, publisher splits, QA, competition, or the need to fund the next game. The source therefore treats indie games as a possible low-cost creative business for skilled niche teams, not as a general high-return side hustle.

## Key Claims
- Steam access can turn a hobby project into a global saleable product, but storefront access is not demand.
- Niche knowledge and early community access can matter more than generic "game market" enthusiasm.
- Gross sales can overstate creator income because many layers sit between player payment and personal take-home pay.
- Multiple releases and long-tail revenue can make the model more viable than a single launch.
- AI tools can help production, translation, and code, but [[AIGameIndustrialization]] still requires domain judgment and quality control.
