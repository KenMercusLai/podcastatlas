# Chemical-Weapons Scientist Responsibility

## Definition
Chemical-weapons scientist responsibility is the layered responsibility of researchers, laboratories, commanders, and states for creating, controlling, declaring, identifying, and remediating toxic capabilities designed for coercion or killing.

## Current Synthesis
[[VladimirUglev]]'s source profile resists a simple conversion story. Technical authorship helped create durable harm; later identification, disclosure, and stockpile warnings supplied socially useful knowledge but did not undo that contribution. Secrecy also distributes responsibility unevenly: individual scientists may face coercion, compartmentalization, and occupational danger, while states control objectives, deployment, declaration, and archives. Ethical assessment therefore needs both individual agency and institutional command, plus attention to what a knowledgeable participant does when later able to disclose or reduce risk.

## Key Claims
- Technical authorship creates responsibility even when a state controls deployment.
- State secrecy can constrain individual knowledge and choice without eliminating agency.
- Later disclosure can improve attribution and risk reduction without erasing earlier contribution.
- Laboratory accidents and unintended civilian exposure are part of a weapon programme's harm, not external anomalies.
- Declaration, stockpile destruction, medical response, and evidence preservation are distinct remedial duties.
