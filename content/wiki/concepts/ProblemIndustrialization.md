# Problem Industrialization

Problem industrialization is the pattern where an unresolved problem becomes the basis for services, products, fees, careers, media value, and institutional legitimacy. [[23-feima-pai-qiche-anquan-shi-di-yi-wei-de-572699943]] adds the concept through [[FeimaPaiQiche|《飞马牌汽车》]]: once the trapped bus is normalized, the surrounding system invents delivery, waste handling, remote birth support, family visits, entertainment, hotels, research devices, and charges for air, lighting, food, medicine, and bodily needs.

The source's satirical point is not that services are always bad. In emergencies, services matter. The problem begins when managing the harm becomes more institutionally rewarding than removing the harm, and when adaptation is then used to prove that the original failure has become productive.

## Key Claims
- A problem can become sticky once jobs, revenue, promotion, prestige, and technical projects depend on its continuation.
- Industrialization can make suffering look like innovation because every workaround becomes a product or achievement.
- Users may adapt to the degraded condition and begin treating former normal life as optional or old-fashioned.
- The pattern explains why solving the root problem may later appear expensive, disruptive, or anti-growth.
