# 盐利农具式复耕安置 / Salt-Revenue Agricultural Recovery

## Definition
盐利农具式复耕安置 / salt-revenue agricultural recovery is the late-Han policy pattern in which state-controlled salt revenue is used to buy farming tools and oxen for returning refugees, turning displaced people back into agricultural producers before local generals can convert them into private troops.

## Current Synthesis
The current evidence comes from Wei Ji's Guanzhong proposal before Guandu. The policy treats salt administration, refugee settlement, agriculture, and military security as one system: the state recovers a revenue channel, converts that revenue into productive inputs, restores farming households, increases grain supply, and weakens the manpower base of semi-autonomous local commanders. It is close to [[TuntianSystem|屯田制]] and [[AgriculturalReliefStatecraft|农业救济式国家能力]], but its distinct mechanism is not a formal farm colony; it is fiscal recycling from salt monopoly into refugee resettlement and household agriculture.

## Key Claims
- Fiscal control can be converted into productive recovery when monopoly revenue funds concrete farming inputs rather than only court or military consumption.
- Refugee return is a security problem as well as a relief problem, because displaced people can be absorbed by local generals if the state cannot settle them.
- Agricultural recovery supports military strategy indirectly by rebuilding grain supply and rear order before a decisive campaign.
- Institutional placement matters: keeping administrative authority in Guanzhong helps strengthen officials and commoners while limiting independent generals.
- The pattern differs from generic resource monopoly because the revenue is tied to a specific resettlement and production objective.

## Evidence
- Fiscal mechanism: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1035-zhansha-caocao-qinzi-he-aijiang-ta-jing-zaidu-guijiang-caocao-lh10wwkwcqw5hnap3bodw6oxpt7v|Hanji 1035]] records Wei Ji's proposal to restore salt offices, sell salt, and use the proceeds to buy farming tools and oxen.
- Refugee-security link: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1035-zhansha-caocao-qinzi-he-aijiang-ta-jing-zaidu-guijiang-caocao-lh10wwkwcqw5hnap3bodw6oxpt7v|Hanji 1035]] says local generals were absorbing returning refugees into their armies, creating a future threat if left unchecked.
- Agricultural and grain effect: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1035-zhansha-caocao-qinzi-he-aijiang-ta-jing-zaidu-guijiang-caocao-lh10wwkwcqw5hnap3bodw6oxpt7v|Hanji 1035]] frames tools, oxen, farming recovery, accumulated grain, and stable social order as the material base for war.
- Institutional control: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1035-zhansha-caocao-qinzi-he-aijiang-ta-jing-zaidu-guijiang-caocao-lh10wwkwcqw5hnap3bodw6oxpt7v|Hanji 1035]] links the plan to keeping Sili Xiaowei authority in Guanzhong and placing salt/fiscal supervision under court appointment.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The wiki currently has one source for this specific pattern, so implementation durability and measured output remain unresolved. The policy should not be conflated with all salt monopolies or all agricultural relief; its distinctive feature is the targeted conversion of salt revenue into refugee farming capacity in Guanzhong before Guandu. It also does not prove that local generals were permanently neutralized, only that Wei Ji diagnosed their manpower absorption as a risk and Cao Cao adopted a countermeasure.

## What Changed
- Established a new Guanzhong policy concept for Wei Ji's salt-revenue-to-farming proposal.
- Distinguished the pattern from broader resource monopoly and from the tuntian system.
- Connected refugee settlement to war logistics and local-general containment before Guandu.
