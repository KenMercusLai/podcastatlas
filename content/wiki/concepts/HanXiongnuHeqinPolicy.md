## Definition

Han-Xiongnu heqin policy / 汉匈和亲政策 is the use or attempted restoration of ruling-house marriage and kinship diplomacy to manage Han-Xiongnu frontier pressure when direct coercion is too costly, incomplete, or politically damaged. The current wiki evidence begins after the [[BaidengEncirclement|白登之围]], includes [[WangZhaojun|王昭君]]'s favorable-moment marriage to [[HuhanyeChanyuWesternHan|呼韩邪单于]], includes a Xin attempt compromised by hostage execution, and extends to an Eastern Han attempt to restore old relations while raiding continues.

## Current Synthesis

The Western Han layer begins with [[LouJing|刘敬 / 楼敬]] after [[LiuBang|刘邦]] sees that immediate war cannot easily solve the [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] problem. Liu Jing proposes marriage rather than renewed direct confrontation, imagining that a legitimate imperial daughter married into [[MaoDunChanyu|冒顿单于]]'s line could produce future kinship leverage if a grandson became chanyu. That plan is strategic rather than romantic: kinship is meant to reduce raids, buy time, and manage frontier asymmetry after a failed campaign.

The implementation evidence also shows the policy's costs and compromises. [[LuZhi|吕雉]] blocks using [[LuYuanPrincess|鲁元公主]], and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-180-xihan-diyi-yinghan-guangao-de-gushi-1-li5u-dmsmb1lp4ny3rk02kaiyu4k|Hanji 180]] says Liu Bang instead sends another woman under a legitimate-princess identity. That preserves the policy form while compromising the bride-authenticity principle that Liu Jing had emphasized.

A later Western Han version in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-492-fuhao-xihuan-shenme-nvren-qiaoqiao-wangzhaojun-ltxliplj2l-kvmey3imgklzgm1jt|Hanji 492]] is not framed as desperate post-defeat accommodation. Huhanye comes to court after the Han-Xiongnu balance has shifted, asks to become a Han son-in-law, and Wang Zhaojun's voluntary request is interpreted as situation judgment: the same marriage policy can be a state arrangement and a constrained woman's chosen opportunity when the frontier relationship, partner status, and timing look favorable.

The Eastern Han recurrence makes the repertoire portable but still bounded. While [[LuFangEasternHan|Lu Fang]] and Xiongnu raids continue, [[LiuXiu|Liu Xiu]] sends Guide Hou Liu Sa to seek restored old good relations with Xiongnu, described in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-704-weixiao-wo-da-ni-shi-bu-dui-dan-ni-ping-sha-hai-shou-lgi-cjyledgzldrie6kwpvejwsls|Hanji 704]] as heqin. The Chanyu sends an envoy to Luoyang but continues raiding, so this source supports heqin as a reusable diplomatic repertoire under pressure, not as proof that a completed marriage settlement or lasting peace was achieved.

The Xin variant adds damaged-trust heqin. In [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-647-shisanchao-gudu-luoyang-weihe-zhiguanzhongyao-ljjcpxlri4dq2zoa9oonyauhcwfv|Hanji 647]], a new Xiongnu chanyu asks to restore heqin contact, and Xin envoys bring congratulations and gifts, but Wang Mang has already executed the chanyu's hostage son Luanti Deng. When asked about the son, the envoys conceal the death. This makes heqin a diplomacy of temporary form without repaired trust.

## Key Claims

- Heqin appears as a policy response to military constraint after Baideng, not as a first-choice alliance gesture.
- Marriage diplomacy can be imagined as multi-generational leverage when a future Xiongnu ruler might have kinship obligations to Han.
- Implementation can preserve the policy form while compromising the bride-authenticity principle that justified the original design.
- Wang Zhaojun's episode shows heqin under a more favorable power balance, where state diplomacy also creates an individual exit from palace constraint.
- The policy is bounded: it may buy time or reopen contact, but it does not by itself end Xiongnu raiding.
- Hanji 704 shows the heqin repertoire recurring in Eastern Han frontier diplomacy when Xiongnu and Lu Fang pressure continues.
- Hanji 647 shows heqin becoming fragile when a hostage killing leaves envoys preserving diplomatic form through concealment.

## Evidence

- Post-Baideng policy trigger: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-178-maodun-weihe-yao-fangguo-liubang-zhe-cai-shi-zhenxiang-1-lumszkbr-klnlodxmxdf-n8p5wym|Hanji 178]] introduces Liu Jing's proposal after the Baideng crisis makes immediate northern conquest impractical.
- Return-route confirmation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-178-maodun-weihe-yao-fangguo-liubang-zhe-cai-shi-zhenxiang-2-loluh53iyrzo4ucf3ttzmn3-otkv|Hanji 178 part 2]] says Liu Bang's Xiongnu approach has moved from conquest toward reluctant accommodation by the time of the post-Baideng return-route material.
- Household and authenticity crisis: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-179-lishishang-heqin-zenme-laide-lslks-ovbomneornk4j-q6-ns0x1|Hanji 179]] says Liu Jing insists on a real legitimate princess and that Lu Zhi blocks using Lu Yuan Princess.
- Compromise implementation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-180-xihan-diyi-yinghan-guangao-de-gushi-1-li5u-dmsmb1lp4ny3rk02kaiyu4k|Hanji 180]] says Liu Bang sends another woman as a legitimate elder princess and has Liu Jing escort the mission.
- Bounded effectiveness: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-180-xihan-diyi-yinghan-guangao-de-gushi-1-li5u-dmsmb1lp4ny3rk02kaiyu4k|Hanji 180]] says early Han-Xiongnu heqin does not end Xiongnu incursions and later gives way to open war by [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s reign.
- Favorable-moment marriage: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-492-fuhao-xihuan-shenme-nvren-qiaoqiao-wangzhaojun-ltxliplj2l-kvmey3imgklzgm1jt|Hanji 492]] says Huhanye asks to become a Han son-in-law when Han is strong and Han-Xiongnu relations are in a relatively favorable moment.
- Individual agency inside policy: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-492-fuhao-xihuan-shenme-nvren-qiaoqiao-wangzhaojun-ltxliplj2l-kvmey3imgklzgm1jt|Hanji 492]] says Wang Zhaojun voluntarily asks to go, and the host interprets that as judging a better opportunity than aging unseen in the palace.
- Eastern Han recurrence: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-704-weixiao-wo-da-ni-shi-bu-dui-dan-ni-ping-sha-hai-shou-lgi-cjyledgzldrie6kwpvejwsls|Hanji 704]] says Liu Xiu sends Liu Sa to restore old good relations, described as heqin, while Xiongnu and Lu Fang raiding continues.
- Xin rupture variant: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-647-shisanchao-gudu-luoyang-weihe-zhiguanzhongyao-ljjcpxlri4dq2zoa9oonyauhcwfv|Hanji 647]] says a new chanyu asks for heqin, but Xin envoys hide that Wang Mang has executed the chanyu's hostage son.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Hanji 492 centers Wang Zhaojun's agency and the host's modern interpretation, so it should not be used alone to settle full state policy mechanics, bride selection practice, or Huhanye's broader strategic motive. Hanji 647 does not show a successful marriage settlement; it shows a request, mission, gifts, and deception under damaged trust. Hanji 704 does not detail a new marriage, hostage exchange, tribute package, or durable settlement, so it should be read as evidence for attempted restoration of heqin relations rather than a full implemented Eastern Han marriage alliance. Hanji 179's Tang, Qing, Song, and Ming comparisons remain host framing rather than independently developed cases in this page's source inventory. The policy differs from [[WarringStatesMarriageDiplomacy|战国联姻外交]] by being set inside an agrarian-steppe frontier asymmetry after failed coercion, and it differs from [[IntimateFrontierPacification|亲密关系式边患安抚]] because the evidence here concerns state diplomacy rather than a private relationship absorbing a frontier polity.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 492's Wang Zhaojun case as a favorable-balance and individual-agency layer inside Western Han heqin.
- Preserved the prior synthesis that heqin is a reusable but bounded frontier-diplomacy repertoire, not a guarantee of peace.
