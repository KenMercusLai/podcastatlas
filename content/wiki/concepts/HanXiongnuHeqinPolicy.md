# Han-Xiongnu Heqin Policy / 汉匈和亲政策

Han-Xiongnu heqin policy / 汉匈和亲政策 enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-178-maodun-weihe-yao-fangguo-liubang-zhe-cai-shi-zhenxiang-1-lumszkbr-klnlodxmxdf-n8p5wym|Hanji 178]] as [[LouJing|刘敬 / 楼敬]]'s proposed answer after the [[BaidengEncirclement|白登之围]]. Once [[LiuBang|刘邦]] has seen that immediate war cannot easily solve the [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] problem, Liu Jing suggests political marriage rather than renewed direct confrontation.

The proposal is strategic rather than romantic. Liu Jing's version would marry the emperor and empress's legitimate daughter into [[MaoDunChanyu|冒顿单于]]'s line, hoping that a future child could turn kinship into restraint: a grandson who becomes chanyu would be less likely, in the proposal's logic, to attack his Han grandfather.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-178-maodun-weihe-yao-fangguo-liubang-zhe-cai-shi-zhenxiang-1-lumszkbr-klnlodxmxdf-n8p5wym|Hanji 178]] leaves the conflict unresolved. The proposed bride is tied to [[LuZhi|吕雉]] and likely to [[LuYuanPrincess|鲁元公主]], so heqin immediately becomes an imperial-household problem as well as a frontier policy.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-178-maodun-weihe-yao-fangguo-liubang-zhe-cai-shi-zhenxiang-2-loluh53iyrzo4ucf3ttzmn3-otkv|Hanji 178 part 2]] reaffirms the pivot after Liu Bang's return route and palace-settlement material: by the time Liu Bang goes to Luoyang in the summer after Baideng, the episode describes his attitude toward Xiongnu as having shifted from conquest toward compromise. Heqin is therefore not a detached marriage idea but the policy answer that follows a failed northern campaign, Chen Ping's emergency rescue, and Liu Jing's vindicated warning.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-179-lishishang-heqin-zenme-laide-lslks-ovbomneornk4j-q6-ns0x1|Hanji 179]] supplies the first concrete implementation crisis. Liu Jing insists that the bride must be the real legitimate princess, not a substituted palace woman or distant clan woman, because discovery could make [[MaoDunChanyu|冒顿单于]] dangerous. [[LiuBang|刘邦]] initially accepts sending [[LuYuanPrincess|鲁元公主]], even though the episode says she is already married to [[ZhangAo|张敖]], but [[LuZhi|吕雉]] blocks the plan by pleading that she has only this one daughter.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-180-xihan-diyi-yinghan-guangao-de-gushi-1-li5u-dmsmb1lp4ny3rk02kaiyu4k|Hanji 180]] turns the blocked plan into a compromise implementation. Liu Bang selects another woman, calls her legitimate elder princess, sends her to Modu, and has Liu Jing escort the mission. This qualifies Hanji 179's rank-authenticity warning: the policy proceeds, but by using a substitute identity that the previous episode itself had framed as risky.

The same source broadens and bounds the concept. It defines heqin as marriage between a Chinese imperial woman and a foreign ruler, praises later Western Han, Tang, and Qing kinship networks as frontier-stabilizing in the host's interpretation, but also says the early Han-Xiongnu policy did not end Xiongnu incursions. By [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s reign, the episode says the long heqin phase gives way to open war.

This differs from [[WarringStatesMarriageDiplomacy|战国联姻外交]] by setting marriage inside an agrarian-steppe frontier asymmetry after a failed campaign. It also differs from [[IntimateFrontierPacification|亲密关系式边患安抚]] because the source has not yet shown a private relationship pacifying and then absorbing a frontier polity; it is a proposed kinship instrument to buy time, reduce raids, and manage fear after defeat.

## Key Claims
- Heqin appears here as a policy response to military constraint after Baideng, not as a first-choice alliance gesture.
- Marriage diplomacy can be imagined as multi-generational leverage, especially when a future heir might have kinship obligations to both sides.
- The same policy that may reduce frontier risk can impose a direct cost on the imperial household.
- Hanji 179 resolves the initial family-resistance question: Liu Bang accepts Liu Jing's plan in principle, but Lu Zhi blocks using Lu Yuan Princess.
- Hanji 180 adds that implementation can preserve the policy form while compromising the bride-authenticity principle that Liu Jing had emphasized.
- Hanji 178 part 2 confirms the policy direction after the return-route episodes: Liu Bang's Xiongnu approach has moved from conquest to reluctant accommodation.
- The policy is explicitly bounded: it may reduce pressure and buy time, but it does not by itself end Xiongnu raiding.
- The Tang/Qing/Song/Ming comparison in Hanji 179 is useful as the host's interpretive frame, but the episode does not independently develop those dynastic cases.
