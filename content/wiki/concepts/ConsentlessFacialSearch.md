# Consentless Facial Search

## Definition
Consentless facial search is the privacy problem where photos or videos taken in public, at events, or by other people become searchable by face even though the person did not understand themselves as consenting to later identification.

## Current Synthesis
The episode adds this concept through Zach's reaction to the dating-event profile. The profile correctly identifies parts of his work and political history and surfaces photos from a protest and a concert that he did not know had been taken. His response separates ordinary public presence from searchable biometric retrievability: being visible somewhere is not the same as consenting to being findable by face across contexts.

## Key Claims
- Public appearance does not equal meaningful consent to later biometric search.
- Facial search can connect contexts a person experienced separately, such as work, travel, protest, concerts, and campaign activity.
- The privacy harm can occur even when facts are accurate because the person loses control over context and audience.
- Protest and political-activity photos are especially sensitive because later search can chill association or speech.
- Consentless facial search amplifies [[CrossDatasetPrivacyLinkage]] by turning images into lookup keys.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not specify the exact facial-recognition vendor, matching threshold, photo sources, or legal status of each image. The concept should therefore stay focused on the consent and context problem rather than making a product-specific legal claim.

## What Changed
