# State-Mediated Remittance System / 国家中介汇款体系

A state-mediated remittance system is the source's pattern where migrant money is routed through official or semi-official institutions rather than only through private trust networks. In [[168-yin-yu-xin-yibu-riben-laogong-yimin-de-kuayang-huikuan-shi-lmgfmmgkawoa-6xyucnm1ljebzwu]], Japanese families, village officials, the foreign ministry, overseas consulates, and [[YokohamaSpecieBank|Yokohama Specie Bank]] all help make [[JapaneseLaborMigrationRemittances]] traceable and governable.

The concept contrasts with [[QiaopiRemittanceNetworks]]. The Chinese qiaopi and silver-letter systems described in the episode relied more on water couriers, associations, merchant networks, clan ties, and hometown trust, while the Japanese case became more visible in centralized state and bank records.

## Key Claims
- State mediation can lower coordination problems by giving migrant families, consulates, and banks a shared process.
- The same process can become paternalistic when the state defines the good migrant as thrifty, obedient, remittance-sending, and reputationally useful.
- Centralized mediation produces richer archives, but those archives reflect what officials and banks cared to record.
- Comparing state-backed and private-trust systems avoids treating remittance as one universal institution.
