# Opposition Legitimacy Gap

Opposition legitimacy gap is the transition risk in [[caracas-under-pressure-democracy-in-venezuela-6a7d8bbe7d85cbdf96e00822]] where negotiations include some opposition parties but exclude the figure or movement with the strongest popular mandate. In [[Venezuela]], the source identifies [[MariaCorinaMachado]] as the most popular opposition leader and says her absence is the main weakness in the process.

The gap does not mean talks are useless. The episode says Machado will not obstruct the talks and may support them if they produce a fairer [[VenezuelanElectoralCouncil]], a real [[VenezuelanSupremeCourt]], and a path to elections. The risk is that a process can become easier to start precisely because it excludes the actor whose participation would make the result more legitimate.

## Key Claims
- Transition talks can gain tactical feasibility by excluding a polarizing or threatening opposition leader.
- The same exclusion can weaken public trust in the process and make later endorsement necessary.
- Institutional outputs can partially close the gap if they create credible election conditions.
- Outside powers may prefer a manageable negotiating table, but legitimacy still has to be earned with voters and opposition supporters.
