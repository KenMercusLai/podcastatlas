# 长期IP组合孵化 / Long-Horizon IP Portfolio Building

## Definition
Long-horizon IP portfolio building is a strategy for managing unpredictable character success by sourcing multiple creators, testing characters through products and distribution, concentrating behind observed demand, and limiting short-term exploitation when it could damage long-term attachment.

## Current Synthesis
The source separates licensed-IP operations from original-IP creation. International licenses bring existing recognition and teach product design, approval, and retail execution; original characters may create more control and upside but face a much higher discovery problem. [[Miniso|名创优品]] proposes to use artist agreements, product launches, store data, and a global network as a portfolio-testing system.

The central tension is exploration versus extraction. The company wants enough parallel experiments to encounter rare hits, yet Ye Guofu argues that a character can be harmed by maximizing every available short-term sale. Patience therefore means both accepting failed tests and deliberately pacing successful ones. This is a strategic claim, not evidence that the portfolio will produce a durable global franchise.

## Key Claims
- Blockbuster characters contain substantial luck and social-amplification effects, so one hit cannot be reproduced mechanically.
- A portfolio of artists and product tests creates more learning opportunities than betting everything on one internally planned character.
- Existing licenses can build operating capability without solving original-IP discovery.
- Store placement and sales data can reveal product demand, but observed sales do not fully measure narrative or cultural durability.
- Overdevelopment can exhaust a character even when near-term demand would support more revenue.
- Long-horizon investment requires tolerance for failed experiments, disciplined concentration, and explicit stopping rules.

## Evidence
- Hit uncertainty and portfolio response: [[ltdplf6zr1agmhhbopqptutvr-ng-ltdplf6zr1agmhhbopqptutvr-ng]] describes celebrity and social amplification around blockbuster IP and Miniso's plan to sign many artists, test products, and promote stronger results.
- Capability path: [[ltdplf6zr1agmhhbopqptutvr-ng-ltdplf6zr1agmhhbopqptutvr-ng]] traces Miniso from international licenses and a growing design team toward exclusive artist agreements and original characters.
- Restraint and risk budget: [[ltdplf6zr1agmhhbopqptutvr-ng-ltdplf6zr1agmhhbopqptutvr-ng]] reports Ye's example of deliberately capping one character's sales and his willingness to tolerate substantial creative experimentation costs.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence is a founder's forward-looking strategy account. Product sales can favor transient trends, distribution power, or discounting rather than durable character attachment. A large portfolio can also dilute attention, raise inventory and creator-management costs, and rationalize undisciplined spending. Claimed budgets, character economics, ownership mix targets, and the ability to identify future hits remain source-scoped.

## What Changed
- Created a portfolio-and-patience model that keeps original-IP ambition separate from proven licensed-IP execution.
