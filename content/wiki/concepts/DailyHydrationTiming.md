# Daily Hydration Timing

## Definition
Daily hydration timing is a practical framework for distributing fluid across waking hours while adapting to thirst, activity, heat, diet, health status, and the sleep cost of late-evening intake.

## Current Synthesis
[[how-to-optimize-your-water-quality-intake-for-health-scim6169879512]] proposes about 8 ounces or 240 milliliters per waking hour during the first 10 hours after waking, then less fluid later as kidney filtration becomes less efficient and nighttime urination becomes more likely. It also reports that modest dehydration can impair cognition and physical performance, while thirst may lag behind need.

The useful synthesis is timing-aware rather than quota-driven: hydrate steadily earlier in the waking period, adapt intake to heat, exercise, sweat, food, caffeine, and personal response, and sip rather than gulp when late fluid is needed. The numerical rule and circadian-kidney explanation remain source-scoped and do not override medical restrictions or the risks of excessive water intake.

## Key Claims
- Earlier, steady intake may reduce the need for large late-evening fluid loads.
- The reported 240-milliliter-per-hour rule is an orientation heuristic, not a universal requirement.
- Exercise, heat, sauna, sweating, caffeine, food, and baseline health can materially change fluid needs.
- Thirst is useful feedback but may not perfectly track developing dehydration in every setting.
- Reducing or sipping late fluid can be tested against nighttime urination without deliberately becoming dehydrated.

## Evidence
- Baseline and timing heuristic: [[how-to-optimize-your-water-quality-intake-for-health-scim6169879512]] recommends roughly 8 ounces or 240 milliliters per waking hour for the first 10 hours.
- Dehydration and adaptation: [[how-to-optimize-your-water-quality-intake-for-health-scim6169879512]] links slight dehydration to poorer physical and cognitive performance and names exercise, heat, sauna, sweat, and caffeine as modifiers.
- Evening boundary: [[how-to-optimize-your-water-quality-intake-for-health-scim6169879512]] connects later kidney timing with nighttime urination and suggests reduced volume plus sipping after about 10 waking hours.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source note does not provide the full evidence behind the 2% dehydration threshold, hourly intake rule, caffeine ratio, kidney-timing claim, or sleep effect. Body size, climate, diet, pregnancy, age, kidney or heart disease, medications, illness, and exercise load can alter safe intake, and excessive water consumption can be dangerous. This is public education rather than an individualized hydration prescription.
