# Investor Suitability Friction

Investor suitability friction is deliberate slowdown before a user buys, trades, borrows, or otherwise takes financial risk. In [[e44-li-xiaobo-duihua-mengyan-zheci-jiu-zheyang-ba-lpcrvfgnseaed-eambk9ofnvuq2]], [[YouzhiYouxing]] requires users to answer questions before buying funds. [[MengYan]] compares the quiz to a brake or road-readiness test: it reduces conversion but tests whether the user understands enough to proceed.

The source also shows the design risk inside friction. [[LiXiaobo]] argues that if a quiz lacks an "I don't know" option, it may teach users to answer dishonestly, and if terms such as public funds, Alpha, and Beta are too dense, the quiz may become gatekeeping rather than education.

[[145-jijin-tougu-zhide-xinren-ma-lrckug0zjqolcczni8ajikb0k5mi]] adds the fund-advisory version. The episode criticizes shallow risk questionnaires and product shelves because they can look like suitability work while failing to learn the client's goal, family context, tax situation, time horizon, and real drawdown tolerance.

## Key Claims
- Friction can serve users when it interrupts impulsive buying, FOMO, and weak product understanding.
- Friction can fail when it becomes performative compliance, confusing language, or a hurdle users learn to game.
- In finance, a conversion loss can be an ethical product feature when the alternative is unsuitable purchase.
- Good friction belongs with [[InvestorEducation]], [[InvestmentRiskManagement]], and [[BehavioralInvestingBiases]], not only legal disclosure.
- [[FinancialPlatformIncentives]] decide whether a platform can afford to make users pause.
- Episode 145 adds that suitability friction should feed [[GoalBasedClientProfiling]] and post-purchase service, not only produce a risk-label output.
