# Psychological Distance Self-Control

## Definition
Psychological distance self-control is the strategy of creating mental, physical, temporal, or social distance from an immediate temptation so higher-order goals and meanings become easier to act on.

## Current Synthesis
In [[master-self-control-overcome-procrastination-dr-kentaro-fujita-scim8156556751]], [[KentaroFujita]] presents self-control as distance-dependent. Immediate states such as tiredness, alcohol, anger, or strong temptation pull attention toward concrete details and short-term relief. Distance makes the broader purpose more available: why the goal matters, what a role model would do, what the future self needs, or what a third-person view reveals.

The strategy is not just abstract reflection. The episode treats physical distance from temptation, third-person self-talk, role-model simulation, writing, and shared reality with another person as ways to make a goal more external, stable, or socially real. It therefore links attention management with identity and meaning rather than treating self-control as silent internal force.

## Key Claims
- Immediate temptation narrows attention toward concrete "how" details and near-term relief.
- Higher-order "why" thinking can improve self-control by making broader goals salient.
- Physical distance, attentional distance, and third-person self-talk can reduce impulsive capture.
- Role models can supply borrowed identity and decision rules.
- Writing or talking with someone who understands can make a goal feel more real.
- Distance tools must fit the person and context; they are not guaranteed scripts.

## Evidence
- Why-thinking effect - [[master-self-control-overcome-procrastination-dr-kentaro-fujita-scim8156556751]] reports Fujita's lab finding that thinking about why one pursues goals improved self-control on an unrelated conflict compared with thinking about how.
- State vulnerability - [[master-self-control-overcome-procrastination-dr-kentaro-fujita-scim8156556751]] says alcohol, anger, tiredness, and similar states bias people toward immediate concrete thinking.
- Third-person and role-model tools - [[master-self-control-overcome-procrastination-dr-kentaro-fujita-scim8156556751]] discusses third-person self-reference and children using chosen characters as self-control prompts.
- Shared reality - [[master-self-control-overcome-procrastination-dr-kentaro-fujita-scim8156556751]] says writing and responsive social exchange can externalize thoughts and make them stronger.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Distance can become avoidance if it replaces needed action or honest problem assessment. The episode also does not present role-model simulation, writing, or shared reality as universal treatments for compulsive behavior, severe anxiety, addiction, or crisis states.
