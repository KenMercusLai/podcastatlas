# Internet Liability Spillover

## Definition
Internet liability spillover is the possibility that legal theories applied to one online service's design, delivery or speech reach other interactive products. It is a forecast about litigation pathways, not a settled uniform rule.

## Current Synthesis
The source set follows one social-media case from allegation to jury verdict and prospective appeal, then compares it with proposed AI liability legislation and chatbot-generated harmful images. The product-design/content distinction, causation, Section 230 and the origin of generated output are different legal questions; analogy does not decide any of them.

## Key Claims
- A child-harm design theory aimed at social media could be pleaded against games or AI services, but plaintiffs still need legally sufficient causation.
- Jury acceptance of social-media design claims does not settle their fate under Section 230 or First Amendment appeal.
- When a provider's own chatbot generates harmful material, the origin of the content creates a distinct liability question from hosting users' speech.
- A proposed shield for model developers invokes platform-law analogies but must be assessed against different possible catastrophic harms and its actual legislative status.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
All four notes are [[MarketplaceTech]] coverage; the two Goldman episodes are stages of related litigation, not replications. [[SocialMediaProductLiability]] and [[ChatbotGeneratedContentLiability]] have different factual origins. Litigation can alter youth safeguards or communication, but the old page's claim that resilient incumbents gain market share and users pay more remains Goldman-style speculation, not an evidenced outcome. Catastrophic AI harms are hypothetical liability scenarios, not demonstrated consequences of this bill.

## What Changed

- Withdrew an unsupported market-share prediction from the core claims.
