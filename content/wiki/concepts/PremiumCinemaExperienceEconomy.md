# Premium Cinema Experience Economy / 影院体验经济

## Definition
Premium cinema experience economy is the shift from moviegoing as low-friction content access toward moviegoing as a paid, format-sensitive, venue-specific event involving screen, sound, aspect ratio, quality control, social atmosphere, and participation.

## Current Synthesis
The sources treat moviegoing as a choice that must justify itself against convenient home viewing. One account describes a 2026 market recovery in which box office rises while attendance remains below pre-pandemic levels, implying greater revenue per committed visit rather than a full return of habitual mass attendance. The Alamo case adds a different premium path: first-run films become a designed night out through food, alcohol, protected attention, themed programming, and local community.

Premium value can therefore come from technical format, quality control, social participation, or behavioral and service design. These layers can reinforce one another, but they also add cost, operational complexity, and decision burden. Alamo's model further shows that food service creates an immersion tradeoff and that distinctive customer experience does not by itself guarantee healthy workplace culture or resilient expansion economics.

## Key Claims
- Box-office recovery can coexist with lower attendance when the audience pays more per trip.
- Premium formats turn the ticket into a bundle of image, sound, aspect ratio, seat, calibration, and immersion.
- Experience value can include quality control, not only bigger screens or luxury branding.
- Participatory screenings create value through being present with other viewers, not only through the film's text.
- Protected attention, hospitality, and local programming can premiumize moviegoing without relying primarily on proprietary projection technology.
- The same experience economy can make ordinary screenings feel downgraded while making moviegoing more expensive, operationally complex, and cognitively demanding.

## Evidence
- Recovery with attendance gap: [[mianfei-de-quanwei-fufei-de-tiyan-cmtwkgbmq01wt01tp3mgj78e4]] cites a report saying 2026 global box office rose while attendance remained more than 20 percent below pre-pandemic levels.
- Format complexity: [[mianfei-de-quanwei-fufei-de-tiyan-cmtwkgbmq01wt01tp3mgj78e4]] discusses IMAX, Cinity, Chinese Giant Screen, Dolby, multiple film prints, and format choice as meaningful to the viewing result.
- Quality-control value: [[mianfei-de-quanwei-fufei-de-tiyan-cmtwkgbmq01wt01tp3mgj78e4]] treats IMAX monitoring and calibration as a claimed part of the premium experience.
- Participatory event value: [[mianfei-de-quanwei-fufei-de-tiyan-cmtwkgbmq01wt01tp3mgj78e4]] describes theater events and audience-made materials around a domestic sleeper hit as part of the draw.
- Service and attention value: [[alamo-drafthouse-cinema-tim-and-karrie-league-2023-7bc6885b-e29f-4ae8-a587-2e17b1d8bea3]] describes [[AlamoDrafthouseCinema]] combining food, alcohol, themed events, no-advertising policy, and a strict [[CinemaAudienceBehavioralContract]].
- Community-brand value: [[alamo-drafthouse-cinema-tim-and-karrie-league-2023-7bc6885b-e29f-4ae8-a587-2e17b1d8bea3]] presents roadshows and special screenings as long-horizon brand investment even when their direct margins were thin.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not independently verify every box-office figure, technical claim, location result, or operating outcome. They do not argue that story no longer matters; rather, story is no longer the only reason some viewers pay to enter a theater. Alamo also demonstrates that premium hospitality can interrupt the film, that strict rules may exclude some customers, and that customer-facing distinctiveness can coexist with workplace and financial weakness.

## What Changed
- Expanded premium value beyond format choice to include hospitality, protected attention, local programming, and community ritual.
- Added operating, workplace, and balance-sheet qualifications to the experience-economy account.
