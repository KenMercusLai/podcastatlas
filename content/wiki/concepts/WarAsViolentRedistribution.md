# War as Violent Redistribution / 战争的暴力再分配

## Definition
War as Violent Redistribution / 战争的暴力再分配 is the source-scoped frame that war forcibly reallocates survival space: territory, population, ships, productive systems, families, food, authority, and future security.

## Current Synthesis
Hanji 1038 introduces the frame through [[SunCe|孙策]]'s Lujiang operation. Victory transfers the practical base of survival after [[LiuXunLateHan|刘勋]] is lured away: [[WanChengLujiang|皖城]], people, ships, family captives, and appointment power move into Sun Ce's order.

Hanji 1085 strengthens the frame through [[JiangxiaCommanderyLateHan|江夏郡]]. After the river barrier is broken and [[HuangZu|黄祖]] is killed, the episode treats captured population, shipbuilding capacity, trained naval manpower, and access along the Yangtze-Han corridor as the deeper result. Across both cases, conquest matters because it removes an opponent's capacity while adding people, infrastructure, and movement options to the victor.

## Key Claims
- War reallocates survival conditions through coercion, not only through battlefield casualties.
- Bases, people, supplies, ships, productive systems, and family captives can matter as much as the named victory.
- A campaign can combine positional breakthrough with post-victory absorption of resources and authority.
- Material transfer is double-sided: the winner gains usable capacity while the defeated order loses future options.
- The concept is strongest when tied to concrete transfers rather than used as a loose metaphor for all competition.

## Evidence
- Lujiang transfer: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1038-sanshiliu-ji-zhi-diaohu-lishan-sunce-zhiqu-wancheng-lhomk8hfdidl61degwbqtj2xkj9z|Hanji 1038]] records Sun Ce taking family members, population, ships, Wan City, and appointment control after Liu Xun is drawn away.
- Follow-on pressure: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1038-sanshiliu-ji-zhi-diaohu-lishan-sunce-zhiqu-wancheng-lhomk8hfdidl61degwbqtj2xkj9z|Hanji 1038]] follows the Lujiang transfer with Liu Xun's failed reliance on Huang Zu and further capture of ships and family members.
- Jiangxia transfer: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1085-dongwu-naxia-jiangxia-dengyu-wan-le-liubiao-de-xin-lrnhjns-qu0d6tsit3j_re96zk2u|Hanji 1085]] reports mass capture and interprets Jiangxia's population, shipyards, naval training, and corridor position as capacity absorbed by Wu.
- Opponent-side loss: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1085-dongwu-naxia-jiangxia-dengyu-wan-le-liubiao-de-xin-lrnhjns-qu0d6tsit3j_re96zk2u|Hanji 1085]] describes the same transfer as removal of Liu Biao's naval heart, making redistribution a change in both sides' future options.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Both sources are short popular-history retellings, and their broad war-economy language remains interpretive. Exact captive totals, the degree of forced incorporation, shipyard ownership and scale, naval-training continuity, and whether all captured capacity became immediately usable require primary-source verification. The concept should not be promoted into a universal law or used to minimize killing, displacement, coercion, and civilian loss behind the word “redistribution.”

## What Changed
- Added Jiangxia as a second late-Han case beyond the Lujiang campaign.
- Extended the material inventory from people and ships to productive and training systems.
- Made the winner-gain and defeated-side capacity-loss relationship explicit.
- Strengthened the civilian-harm qualification around the redistribution label.
