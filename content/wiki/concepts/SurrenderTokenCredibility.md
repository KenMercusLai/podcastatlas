# Surrender-Token Credibility / 降服凭证式可信度

## Definition
Surrender-token credibility / 降服凭证式可信度 is the use of a politically scarce object, office symbol, hostage, or other costly credential to make capitulation more believable than words alone.

## Current Synthesis
Hanji 1092 presents the pattern through [[LiuCongLateHan|刘琮]]'s delivery of an imperial tally when [[CaoCao|曹操]] reaches the Jingzhou field. Cao-side officers suspect a false surrender, but [[LouGuiLateHan|娄圭]] argues that rival powers in a fractured political order compete to possess symbols of legitimate command. Voluntarily surrendering such a token therefore reduces Liu Cong's ability to continue presenting independent authority and makes deception less likely.

The concept is narrower than a guarantee. A costly signal can improve a receiver's estimate of commitment without proving that every subordinate, garrison, or local elite will obey. Its force depends on the token's recognized scarcity and on what political capacity the sender gives up by transferring it.

## Key Claims
- Verbal surrender is less credible when the receiver fears ambush or delay.
- A scarce symbol of lawful command can function as a costly signal because rivals normally seek to retain it.
- Voluntary transfer raises credibility when it weakens the sender's ability to claim independent authority.
- Credibility concerns the sender's commitment, not automatic control over every subordinate or territory.
- The mechanism depends on shared recognition of the token's political meaning.

- Costly-symbol interpretation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1092-fu-ke-di-guo-de-lougui-yin-yiju-gankai-bei-caocao-cisi-lutwkvmtn_7cv4r5l-mwknmgyjhs|Hanji 1092]] has Lou Gui argue that the voluntary delivery of the imperial tally is inconsistent with continued independent resistance.
- Decision effect: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1092-fu-ke-di-guo-de-lougui-yin-yiju-gankai-bei-caocao-cisi-lutwkvmtn_7cv4r5l-mwknmgyjhs|Hanji 1092]] says Cao Cao accepts the surrender after Lou Gui's analysis.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode does not independently verify the exact tally, dialogue, or whether this judgment alone determined Cao Cao's acceptance. A token can be stolen, duplicated, surrendered without universal compliance, or used inside deception; the current source supports a credibility update, not certainty. The reported acquisition of seven commanderies is also an outcome claim that requires primary-source checking.
