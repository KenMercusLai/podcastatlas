# Research Institutions As Strategic Capacity

## Definition
Research institutions as strategic capacity is the view that universities, national labs, public research agencies, life sciences, advisory councils, and talent pipelines are part of national power rather than only education, health, or discretionary spending.

## Current Synthesis
The wiki's current judgment is that research capacity depends on institutions and people together. Emanuel and the PCAST source emphasize that NIH, NSF, DARPA, universities, and national labs are strategic assets in competition with China. Kratsios qualifies that institution-first view: the U.S. distributed system is a feature, but public money should fund strong scientists and needed discovery wherever they are, including universities, focused research organizations, and independent researchers.

## Key Claims
- Public research institutions are part of American power because they concentrate talent, knowledge, infrastructure, and scientific credibility.
- China competition makes research capacity strategic in life sciences, AI, quantum, nuclear power, semiconductors, and basic science.
- The U.S. advantage is plural rather than centralized: venture capital, private industry, universities, agencies, national labs, and global talent all contribute.
- Research capacity can be weakened by indiscriminate funding cuts, but it can also be weakened by channel preservation that ignores output or additionality.
- The current synthesis favors institution-neutral scientist support over either anti-institution cuts or automatic protection of incumbents.

- Strategic institution defense: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-rahm-emanuel-trumps-foreign-policy-china-europes-decline-immigration-dsa-vs-democrats-42416310]] treats NIH, NSF, DARPA, universities, national labs, and life sciences as U.S. strategic advantages.
- Distributed-system qualification: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-michael-kratsios-trumps-science-agenda-anti-science-claims-faucis-damage-dei-china-42543770]] says U.S. science agencies are distributed by design and that mission-adjacent R&D can work better when embedded near mission needs.
- Scientist-centered support: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-michael-kratsios-trumps-science-agenda-anti-science-claims-faucis-damage-dei-china-42543770]] says the administration wants to fund strong scientists in universities, focused research organizations, or independent settings if they pass merit review.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept should not become a blanket defense of every institution or grant. Kratsios's source warns that old channels may not fit a world where private R&D supplies much more of the total, while Emanuel's source warns that cutting research capacity can damage U.S. strategy. The unresolved question is which reforms preserve capacity while improving output.

## What Changed
- Migrated the concept to `synthesis-v1`.
- Added Kratsios's qualification that the target should be scientists and additional discovery, not institutions by default.
- Made the U.S. distributed research system a feature of the concept rather than a defect to be consolidated away.
