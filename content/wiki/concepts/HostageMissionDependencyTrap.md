# Hostage Mission Dependency Trap

## Definition

Hostage mission dependency trap names a crisis pattern in which the people sent to rescue, negotiate for, or support hostages become additional leverage because the detaining ruler can convert each new mission into a new dependency.

## Current Synthesis

The Abyssinian hostage crisis in [[705-queen-victorias-revenge-the-mad-emperor-of-abyssinia-part-1-glt8608697296]] shows the trap in stages. Captain Cameron and missionaries are detained while Teodros waits for a British answer; [[HormuzdRassam]] is sent to repair the relationship and briefly succeeds; then Rassam, Blanc, and Prideaux are also seized, while Reverend Martin Flad is sent to London with demands for technicians and equipment, leaving his family behind in Abyssinia.

## Key Claims

- A rescue envoy can become part of the hostage set if the detaining ruler sees continued possession as the only reliable way to command attention.
- Each new negotiator or technician request can widen the hostage field rather than narrowing it.
- Humanitarian caution can prolong the trap when the outside government fears that force will trigger hostage killing.
- The trap ends diplomatic ambiguity by making the outside power choose between continued bargaining and coercive rescue.

## Evidence

- Envoy capture: [[705-queen-victorias-revenge-the-mad-emperor-of-abyssinia-part-1-glt8608697296]] says Rassam, Blanc, and Prideaux are seized after Rassam's apparently successful audience with [[TeodrosII]].
- Dependency demand: [[705-queen-victorias-revenge-the-mad-emperor-of-abyssinia-part-1-glt8608697296]] says Teodros keeps envoys and others while demanding blacksmiths, gunsmiths, artillery specialists, and equipment.
- Bargaining limit: [[705-queen-victorias-revenge-the-mad-emperor-of-abyssinia-part-1-glt8608697296]] records Flad's warning that troops could cause Teodros to slaughter hostages, followed by hostage messages saying Teodros is unlikely to release anyone.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source leaves Teodros's exact motive unresolved: deception, hatred of missionaries, emotional dependency on Rassam, and strategic leverage are all presented as possible explanations. The concept should therefore be used for the structural trap, not as a settled psychological diagnosis.
