## Definition

主动陵墓发掘的保存风险 / preventive tomb excavation conservation risk is the danger that deliberately opening a sealed burial exposes objects and contextual information faster than they can be documented, stabilized, conserved, and securely managed.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-311-wenxuejia-guomoruo-ruhe-niangjiu-kaogu-dabeiju-lpq9bozhrdxqn7tiotyx3j-epm9l|Hanji 311]] supplies a single cautionary case through [[MingDingling|明定陵]]. The episode says opening the underground palace abruptly introduced oxygen into a long-isolated environment and associates that shift with deterioration of paintings, calligraphy, and textiles. It also alleges breakage and loss during or after the excavation.

The useful synthesis is a readiness test rather than a claim that excavation has no value. The same episode reports more than three thousand recovered objects. Recovery and loss can coexist: opening creates access to artifacts and historical context while also starting irreversible physical and administrative risks. A responsible judgment therefore depends on conservation methods, recording, storage, custody, staffing, and long-term care being ready before exposure.

## Key Claims

- A sealed tomb's environment is part of the preservation problem, not merely an obstacle to access.
- Excavation can begin deterioration by changing oxygen, humidity, temperature, light, or microbial conditions.
- Recovery counts alone cannot measure success when objects or contextual information are damaged or lost.
- Conservation, documentation, storage, and custody capacity must be assessed before deliberate opening.
- A cautionary case does not by itself establish one advocate's sole responsibility or a universal ban on excavation.

## Evidence

Recovery and loss together:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-311-wenxuejia-guomoruo-ruhe-niangjiu-kaogu-dabeiju-lpq9bozhrdxqn7tiotyx3j-epm9l|Hanji 311]] reports more than three thousand finds while also alleging deterioration, breakage, and loss.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The current concept rests on one short popular-history summary. It does not provide measured environmental data, conservation records, an itemized loss register, comparative unexcavated controls, or the institutional decision chain. Oxygen may be one part of a more complex deterioration process, and “instantaneous” destruction is the episode's rhetoric rather than a verified timing measure here. The case supports a readiness framework, not a conclusion that all archaeological excavation is destructive or that leaving every tomb sealed is risk-free.

## What Changed

- Created a readiness-centered concept that keeps artifact recovery and excavation-caused loss visible at the same time.
