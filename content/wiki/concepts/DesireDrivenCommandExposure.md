# Desire-Driven Command Exposure / 私欲驱动的统帅暴露

Desire-driven command exposure / 私欲驱动的统帅暴露 is the Wancheng pattern [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1013-dianwei-zhisi-jingshi-caocao-haose-haileta-lhe6jrpjp3cfbdhvjtdiezijfnoh|Hanji 1013]] draws from [[CaoCao|曹操]]'s conduct after [[ZhangXiu|张绣]] surrenders. The source does not make desire work alone. Cao Cao's taking of [[LadyZhouWancheng|周氏]] creates status humiliation, his gifts to [[HuCheEr|胡车儿]] create threat perception, and his leaked plan to kill Zhang Xiu makes waiting dangerous.

The exposure becomes military because private conduct changes security posture. [[JiaXu|贾诩]] turns Zhang Xiu's resentment into a pre-emptive camp-entry tactic, Hu Che'er removes [[DianWei|典韦]]'s guard function through drink and weapon theft, and Cao Cao's camp is caught unready at night. The result is a commander's escape at the cost of a loyal guard, a son, and a nephew.

The concept sits beside [[AlcoholAsPowerCatalyst|酒作为权力催化剂]] and [[StatusInsultDefectionCascade|名分羞辱触发叛变]] but is narrower. Alcohol helps disable the guard, and status insult helps motivate rebellion, yet the source's specific mechanism is that a commander's private desire alters how surrendered enemies, household access, personal guards, and camp discipline line up.

## Key Claims
- A leader's private indulgence can become a command problem when it humiliates a newly surrendered actor.
- Concubine-taking, rewards to another man's subordinate, and secret assassination planning can be read together as status insult plus threat.
- Guard vulnerability matters because personal-security failure can quickly become camp-wide battlefield exposure.
- Alcohol is an enabling tactic in this source, not the full cause; the deeper failure is the command environment that makes drinking, disarming, and surprise possible.
- The cost of the exposure falls on subordinates and family members as well as on the exposed leader.
