## Definition

Reflective desire autonomy is the capacity to examine, evaluate, revise, or refuse one's desires and to redirect life accordingly, rather than treating freedom as the mere availability or satisfaction of more options.

## Current Synthesis

[[FaustusMarlowe|Faustus]] gains travel, objects, spectacle, and command that ordinary people lack, yet becomes less able to reconsider what he wants or act on his knowledge of the bargain. His external choice set grows while entertainment, vanity, fear, and the contract increasingly govern his direction.

The concept therefore separates capability from self-direction. A technology may remove friction and satisfy preferences while leaving preference formation untouched or making reflection harder. Freedom remains incomplete unless a person retains attention, time, exit capacity, and practical ability to change course.

## Key Claims

- More available actions do not necessarily produce greater freedom.
- Preference satisfaction is different from reflective endorsement of a preference.
- Attention is part of autonomy because sustained reflection is required to reconsider direction.
- Self-command does not require suppressing every desire; it requires the ability to rank, revise, or refuse desires.
- Technology can expand external capability while weakening the conditions for long-term self-direction.
- A worthwhile purpose cannot be inferred from the efficiency with which a desire can be fulfilled.

## Evidence

- Options without direction: [[372-dang-keji-nenggou-manzu-yiqie-yuanwang-women-hai-zhidao-ziji-xiangyao-shenme-ma-maluo-fushide-boshi-de-beiju-ltehzo-lehi9vpgy5ukexbcrtcpr]] gives Faustus unusual mobility and command while his life narrows toward vanity, entertainment, and control.
- Disrupted reflection: [[372-dang-keji-nenggou-manzu-yiqie-yuanwang-women-hai-zhidao-ziji-xiangyao-shenme-ma-maluo-fushide-boshi-de-beiju-ltehzo-lehi9vpgy5ukexbcrtcpr]] shows repeated awareness of consequences displaced by immediately available diversion.
- Technology analogy: [[372-dang-keji-nenggou-manzu-yiqie-yuanwang-women-hai-zhidao-ziji-xiangyao-shenme-ma-maluo-fushide-boshi-de-beiju-ltehzo-lehi9vpgy5ukexbcrtcpr]] argues that AI, biotechnology, and automation can increase means without deciding what desires or lives are worth pursuing.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

External options, resources, and reduced friction can be genuine conditions of freedom; the concept does not romanticize deprivation or treat desire as inherently suspect. The current evidence is one literary interpretation, not an empirical measure of autonomy or proof that a particular technology weakens it.

## What Changed

- Created a freedom standard that distinguishes additional options from the capacity to reflect on and redirect desire.
