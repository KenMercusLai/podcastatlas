## Definition
Psychological flexibility under performance pressure / 表现压力下的心理灵活性 is the capacity to notice anxiety, fatigue, disappointment, intrusive thought, or imperfect readiness without requiring it to disappear before returning to a valued, controllable action.

## Current Synthesis
The episode treats pressure as partly unavoidable in elite competition. Trying to suppress every worry can add a second struggle and keep attention attached to medals, reputation, other people's expectations, or a previous error. A more flexible sequence identifies the present feeling and its source, separates personally relevant concerns from projected public meaning, distinguishes controllable from uncontrollable factors, and selects the next technical or tactical action.

Acceptance here is not passivity or indifference to winning. The athlete can retain a result goal while placing moment-to-moment attention on the current shot, movement, cue, or plan. The same logic extends beyond sport: imperfect conditions need not authorize reckless action, but they also need not become a permanent entry barrier when a safe, feedback-producing next step is available.

## Key Claims
- Anxiety and unwanted thoughts need not disappear before skilled action can continue.
- Naming the feeling and its source helps separate technical concerns, social evaluation, and uncontrollable uncertainty.
- Result goals can coexist with process attention, but only the latter directly organizes the next movement.
- A previous success or error should not become a demand to recreate or repair the past on the current attempt.
- Practiced attentional cues and regulation routines make flexibility more available under consequence.
- Imperfect action remains bounded by safety, capacity, and feedback rather than celebrating persistence at any cost.

## Evidence
- Acceptance sequence - [[yundongyuan-zui-da-de-duishou-shi-ziji-de-danao-lposnam7-4sk0b8p_lou4gnl4xae]] describes noticing emotion and thought without criticism, asking what concern communicates, and reinvesting effort in partially controllable factors.
- Current-action focus - [[yundongyuan-zui-da-de-duishou-shi-ziji-de-danao-lposnam7-4sk0b8p_lou4gnl4xae]] uses shooting to distinguish wanting a medal from attending to the current shot and “forgetting” each completed one.
- Imperfect-state action - [[yundongyuan-zui-da-de-duishou-shi-ziji-de-danao-lposnam7-4sk0b8p_lou4gnl4xae]] argues that mature athletes adapt their method to current state rather than wait for ideal readiness.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source offers practitioner explanation rather than a comparative trial of acceptance-based methods. Some concerns correctly signal injury, illness, coercion, unsafe workload, or the need to stop, disclose, and seek care. “Focus on what you control” must not relocate institutional failures onto the athlete or imply that every outcome is psychologically manageable. Acceptance cannot substitute for technical preparation, recovery, medical judgment, fair competition, or protection from abuse and harassment.

## What Changed
- Established acceptance plus controllable action as an alternative to emotion elimination under pressure.
- Preserved stopping, care, and institutional responsibility as boundaries on process focus.
