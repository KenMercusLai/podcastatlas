# Mobile Sportsbook Customer Selection

## Definition

Mobile sportsbook customer selection is the use of individual wager and behavior data to distinguish customers an operator wants to retain, reward, steer, restrict, or remove according to their expected long-run value or risk.

## Current Synthesis

[[gambling-with-michael-lewis-gambling-with-michael-lewis]] argues that mobile sportsbooks are not merely casino betting windows moved onto phones. Continuous accounts let operators observe bet type, timing, response to promotions, losses, and whether prices move in a customer's favor after a wager. That makes each bettor both a participant in the market and an object of platform prediction.

The episode's sharpest evidence is [[RufusPeabody]]'s account: [[DraftKings]] reportedly lowered his golf limits after a losing week because his longer-run pattern and favorable closing-line movement suggested a durable advantage. Lewis contrasts this with [[RoxyRoxborough]]'s description of older bookmakers accepting informed bets, changing prices, and redistributing risk. The synthesis is therefore about selective access: the platform can seek volume from likely losers while reducing exposure to users whose decisions improve price discovery.

## Key Claims

- Persistent account data lets a sportsbook evaluate customers separately rather than pricing only the event market.
- Favorable line movement after a wager can signal that a bettor found information the market later absorbed.
- Short-term customer losses do not necessarily reassure an operator if the wager process predicts long-run advantage.
- Limits and removal can reduce the role of informed bettors in price discovery while preserving access for structurally profitable customers.
- VIP treatment and restriction can become inverse forms of selection when likely losses are rewarded and likely skill is constrained.
- The same granular data that supports integrity monitoring can also support commercial customer selection, but the purposes are distinct.

## Evidence

### Advantage inferred from market movement

- [[gambling-with-michael-lewis-gambling-with-michael-lewis]] says operators can examine whether odds repeatedly move in a customer's favor after a wager.

### Limits despite short-term loss

- [[gambling-with-michael-lewis-gambling-with-michael-lewis]] reports that DraftKings reduced Peabody's golf limits after he lost about $30,000 in one week because his longer-run pattern remained threatening.

### Historical contrast

- [[gambling-with-michael-lewis-gambling-with-michael-lewis]] uses Roxborough's account of accepting sharp action and moving or laying off wagers as the older market-making baseline.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source supplies no operator policy, customer-level dataset, prevalence estimate, regulatory record, or participating sportsbook response. Sportsbooks may cite exposure control, integrity, fraud, legal duties, correlated wagers, or other reasons for limits, and the episode does not test those explanations systematically. Favorable line movement is evidence about market timing, not automatic proof of a profitable model or unfair treatment.

## What Changed

- Added a concept separating account-level customer selection from event-level odds setting and integrity monitoring.
