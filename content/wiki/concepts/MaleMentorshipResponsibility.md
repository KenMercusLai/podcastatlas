# Male Mentorship Responsibility

## Definition
Male mentorship responsibility is the source-scoped obligation adult men have to provide boys and younger men with ordinary presence, guidance, invitations, attention, and role modeling when fathers or male role models are absent or weakened.

## Current Synthesis
The source closes by turning masculinity away from image and toward presence. Galloway says men are not stepping up enough for boys affected by death, divorce, or abandonment. The proposed help is deliberately ordinary: ask basic questions, include boys in activities, help single mothers' sons, model provision and protection, and choose service over attention. The concept makes mentorship a distributed community responsibility rather than a heroic program.

## Key Claims
- Boys can lose male role models through death, divorce, abandonment, or ordinary adult absence.
- Adult men can add value through low-drama presence, questions, invitations, and shared activities.
- Mentorship operationalizes positive masculinity by turning protection and service toward the next generation.
- Male mentorship should support, not replace or undermine, mothers and existing caregivers.
- The source treats mentorship as especially important when online role models, manosphere narratives, or public status figures become substitutes for local guidance.
- Simple, repeatable contact can matter even without formal institutional programming.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not define screening, safeguarding, boundaries, trauma-informed care, or formal youth-program design. Mentorship involving minors requires appropriate caregiver consent, institutional safeguards where relevant, and attention to power boundaries.
