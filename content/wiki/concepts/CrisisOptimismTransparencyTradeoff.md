## Definition

危机乐观与透明度权衡 / Crisis optimism transparency tradeoff is the leadership dilemma in which a failing or near-failing organization may need morale and focused delivery, while employees also have a legitimate interest in knowing risk early enough to protect themselves.

## Current Synthesis

The source stages the problem as a company that may fail unless it completes a key delivery soon. [[KuaigeStandup|快哥]] argues for telling employees the truth so they can help solve the problem and prepare alternatives. [[LuoYonghao|罗永浩]] counters that if employees learn that cash flow may last only three months, morale could collapse before the decisive work is finished.

The concept's useful boundary is that neither full concealment nor unmanaged disclosure is automatically virtuous. Leaders owe employees respect and exit time, but they also shape the collective conditions under which a rescue plan might still work. Kuaige's later formulation, telling both bad news and good plan, becomes the source's most practical partial answer.

## Key Claims

- Employees have a legitimate interest in knowing severe organizational risk because jobs, income, and exit timing are at stake.
- Crisis disclosure can harm the organization if it triggers panic, talent flight, or delivery failure before a real rescue attempt.
- Leader optimism can be strategic communication rather than simple lying, but only if tied to a plausible path.
- Transparency becomes more defensible when paired with a concrete plan rather than dumped as unmanaged bad news.
- The source leaves the tradeoff unresolved because different cases depend on cash runway, employee exposure, and rescue probability.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source does not provide a legal, fiduciary, or HR-compliance account of required disclosures. It should be read as a practical ethics and leadership dilemma, not as employment-law guidance.
