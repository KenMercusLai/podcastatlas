# Daily Movement Exposure

## Definition
Daily movement exposure is the practice of distributing varied positions and movement patterns through ordinary life so mobility is maintained by frequent use rather than depending entirely on a separate corrective workout.

## Current Synthesis
[[how-to-improve-your-mobility-posture-flexibility-dr-kelly-starrett-scim3685343544]] describes modern movement as a narrowing language: sitting in chairs, standing, slow walking, and repetitive exercise can leave many joint positions and movement options unused. The proposed response is not a single stretch protocol but recurring exposure to floor sitting, side sitting, 90-90 positions, squatting, getting down and up, changing work positions, walking, jumping, sprinting, and play according to capacity.

The durable judgment is behavioral. A movement practice is easier to sustain when embedded in television time, work, warm-ups, family activity, or recreation. Frequency does not remove progression or symptom boundaries; an unfamiliar floor position, sprint, jump, or deep range still has to match current strength, balance, tolerance, and medical context.

## Key Claims
- Repeatedly using only a narrow set of positions can reduce familiarity, control, and confidence in other ranges.
- Floor sitting and regular transitions to and from the ground can turn mobility practice into environmental exposure rather than an extra appointment.
- Variation across sitting, standing, walking, squatting, rotation, jumping, and faster movement preserves a broader repertoire than one posture or exercise mode.
- Warm-ups can provide playful movement novelty while preparing speed, coordination, and attention for the main task.
- Daily exposure must still be scaled to current balance, strength, pain, tissue tolerance, and fall or injury risk.

## Evidence
- Movement-repertoire claim - [[how-to-improve-your-mobility-posture-flexibility-dr-kelly-starrett-scim3685343544]] argues that modern life repeatedly uses only a small movement vocabulary.
- Embedded-practice claim - [[how-to-improve-your-mobility-posture-flexibility-dr-kelly-starrett-scim3685343544]] recommends accumulating floor time in varied positions and regularly practicing ground transitions.
- Variety and play claim - [[how-to-improve-your-mobility-posture-flexibility-dr-kelly-starrett-scim3685343544]] includes dynamic warm-ups, throwing, rope flow, jumping, sprinting, and skill exploration as ways to maintain broader options.
- Capacity boundary - [[how-to-improve-your-mobility-posture-flexibility-dr-kelly-starrett-scim3685343544]] treats the gym and movement practice as places to notice lost range, asymmetry, pain, and control rather than forcing a universal standard.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not establish a universal twenty-to-thirty-minute floor dose or prove that floor-using cultures have lower fall, hip, or back risk because of floor exposure itself. Cultural comparisons are vulnerable to confounding. Floor transitions, deep squats, sprinting, and jumping may be inappropriate without support or progression for people with acute injury, severe pain, impaired balance, neurological symptoms, recent surgery, frailty, or other clinical constraints.
