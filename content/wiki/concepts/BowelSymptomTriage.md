# Bowel Symptom Triage

## Definition
Bowel symptom triage is the practice of separating ordinary, functional, or lifestyle-linked bowel changes from warning signs that require clinical evaluation.

## Current Synthesis
The Ding Kefeng episode treats bowel symptoms as meaningful but non-specific signals. Stress, cold drinks, sitting, diet, gut sensitivity, and gut-function differences can produce diarrhea, constipation, cramping, or bloating, but the source repeatedly resists both panic and dismissal. The practical task is to observe pattern, persistence, progression, stool color and shape, blood, mucus, frequency, and associated anemia or inflammation clues, then use stool tests, colonoscopy, or other evaluation when risk rises.

## Key Claims
- Bowel symptoms are often indirect, so pattern and persistence matter more than a single episode.
- Stress-related diarrhea, cold-drink sensitivity, constipation, and alternating diarrhea or constipation can be functional, but should not be diagnosed casually.
- Bowel-habit change, mucus, dark-red blood, black stool, or unexplained anemia deserves more concern than isolated mild discomfort.
- Fresh dripping blood can fit hemorrhoids, but repeated bleeding or blood mixed with stool still requires evaluation.
- Stool observation and routine stool testing are low-friction ways to notice inflammation, bleeding, or possible tumor signals.
- Triage should reduce both health anxiety and missed disease by matching symptoms to appropriate professional testing.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode is public education, not a symptom checker. Age, family history, medication, pregnancy, infection exposure, inflammatory bowel disease, prior polyps, anemia, weight loss, fever, pain severity, and clinician judgment can all change urgency.
