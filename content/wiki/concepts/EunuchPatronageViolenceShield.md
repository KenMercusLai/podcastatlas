## Definition

Eunuch patronage violence shield / 宦官庇护下的地方暴行 is the late-Han pattern in which a locally punishable offense becomes politically protected because the offender, target, or complaint channel has palace-connected eunuch access, causing enforcement to rebound against the officials who act.

## Current Synthesis

The current synthesis now has three linked branches in the 166 CE Emperor Huan field. Hanji 871 gives the first rebound chain. [[ZhangFanWanLateHan|张汎]] uses harem ties, luxury gifts, and palace access to become locally dominant at Wan; [[ChengJinLateHan|成瑨]], urged by [[CenZhiLateHan|岑晊]] and [[ZhangMuLateHan|张牧]], kills him and a large network after an amnesty. In Taiyuan, [[LiuZhiTaiyuanLateHan|刘质]] sends [[WangYunLateHan|王允]] to arrest and kill [[ZhaoJinLateHan|赵津]], a small yellow-gate eunuch whose conduct harms a county. [[HouLanLateHan|侯览]] then uses Zhang Fan's wife's petition and eunuch complaint channels to help turn both enforcement actions against Cheng Jin and Liu Zhi.

Hanji 872 gives two more branches. In the Hou Lan branch, [[ZhangJianLateHan|张俭]] attacks illegal property and tomb-building, but Hou Lan can intercept memorials before they reach the ruler. In the [[XuXuanLateHan|徐宣]] branch, private sexual coercion and killing receive political protection because Xu Xuan is tied to [[XuHuangLateHan|徐璜]].

The concept is not just "corrupt official commits crime." Its distinctive mechanism is inversion. [[HuangFuDonghaiLateHan|黄浮]] executes the offender, [[ZhaiChaoLateHan|翟超]] and Zhang Jian stand on the enforcement side, and [[ChenFanLateHan|陈蕃]] argues they acted for the people. Yet [[EmperorHuanOfHan|汉桓帝]] punishes the enforcers after eunuch complaint, so the court response teaches officials that law can be more dangerous than complicity when palace access is captured.

## Key Claims

- Local violence becomes harder to punish when the offender, target, or surviving complainant can reach the palace.
- The shield works through both blocked upward reports and privileged complaint channels.
- Enforcement can be inverted into offense when the ruler receives the patron's grievance before the victim-side evidence.
- Subordinates can correctly read the danger of enforcement even when the law-enforcement act is morally and administratively justified.
- Remonstrance after the fact may diagnose the institution, but diagnosis still fails if the ruler preserves the favorite channel.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept is grounded in the Hanji 871-872 source window and should not be generalized to every eunuch relationship or every late-Han local crime. The source sequence also preserves a chronology caution: Hanji 871-872 sit in the 166 CE Emperor Huan field, while later Zhang Jian and Hou Lan pages cover the 169 CE accusation and flight sequence under Emperor Ling. The concept tracks the enforcement-inversion mechanism, not a complete chronology of every subsequent party-prohibition consequence.

## What Changed

- The concept now begins with the Zhang Fan and Zhao Jin enforcement-rebound cases before the Hanji 872 Zhang Jian/Hou Lan and Xu Xuan/Huang Fu branches.
- The definition now includes complaint-channel access, not only direct patronage of the offender.
