# Taliban Hardline Rule

Taliban hardline rule is the governing pattern described in [[afghanistan-five-years-on-our-correspondent-visits-6a7ee547049c692a6b3ee0a9]], where reduced visible violence in [[Afghanistan]] coexists with ideological policing, gender exclusion, fear of speech and centralized religious-political authority. [[TomSass]] presents the country as safer in the narrow sense while still harshly controlled.

The concept is tied to [[HibatullahAkhundzada]] and the [[Taliban]]'s internal balance. The source says hardliners around the amir have consolidated power from Kandahar, controlled revenue sources including mining contracts, built a separate loyal force, and left more moderate figures unable or unwilling to challenge restrictions.

## Key Claims
- A regime can deliver lower street violence while still making daily life less free, poorer and more fearful.
- Hardline consolidation is visible not only in decrees, but in control over revenue, security forces and moral policing.
- Gender restrictions are a governing signal: bans on secondary school, university access and many workplaces define the regime's social project.
- Apparent order can weaken outside pressure by giving some citizens peace while leaving rights and economic recovery unresolved.
