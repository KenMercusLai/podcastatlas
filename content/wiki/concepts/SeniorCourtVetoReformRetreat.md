## Definition

资深宫廷否决下的改革退让 / Reform Retreat Under Senior Court Veto is the pattern in which a formally superior but politically insecure ruler permits or supports institutional change, encounters a stronger senior power's veto, and preserves the throne by abandoning the program and exposing subordinate reformers to punishment.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 251 part 1 supplies the current case. [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] favors Confucian government while [[EmpressDowagerDouWesternHan|窦太后]] favors Huang-Lao ideas, but the decisive conflict is not philosophical. [[ZhaoWanWesternHan|赵绾]] proposes that major decisions stop going to the Eastern Palace, which would remove the senior dowager from an operative review channel. Dowager Dou can order investigation, showing that she retains coercive capacity beyond ideological influence.

Wudi responds by ending the Mingtang work, handing Zhao and [[WangZangWesternHan|王臧]] over for judgment, and accepting a broader personnel retreat. This can preserve the ruler and leave open a later policy return, but it transfers the immediate cost to subordinates. The source calls the episode a political test and “弃卒保帅”; those labels clarify the mechanism without proving prior authorization or complete intent.

## Key Claims

- Formal title does not eliminate senior-family or regency-era veto power.
- Ideological conflict becomes dangerous when reform changes information, review, or access channels.
- A senior veto holder's ability to investigate personnel reveals real coercive capacity.
- Retreat can preserve a ruler's future agency while destroying the officials who made the contested move.
- Subordinates face exceptional risk when private encouragement is not matched by durable public protection.
- Failed reform can expose the actual power boundary even when it produces no immediate institutional change.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source does not prove that Wudi ordered Zhao Wan's proposal, intended a deliberate test, or viewed Zhao and Wang as expendable. Retreat may reflect legal evidence, policy reconsideration, fear, or later narrative simplification as well as strategic patience. Nor does one case establish that reform under a senior veto should always be delayed: delay can preserve capacity, but it can also entrench the veto and normalize abandonment of advisers.

## What Changed

- Created the concept by separating ideological disagreement from control over decision channels and punishment.
- Made transferred subordinate risk part of the reform-retreat judgment.
