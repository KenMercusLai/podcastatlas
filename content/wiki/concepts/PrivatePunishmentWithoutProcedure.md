# Private Punishment Without Procedure / 无程序私刑

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-903-weishenme-quan-ni-yuanyuan-xiangbao-heshi-le-lokpt93muc8kasvn44vgsjki8bom|Hanji 903]] adds a late-Han revenge version before the Warring States and Qin branches. [[DuanJiongLateHan|段熲]] does not use an open adjudication process against [[SuBuweiLateHan|苏不韦]]; he tests Su with a private post, then uses coercion against a subordinate's father to send that subordinate against Su's household. The source also shows Su himself acting outside procedure when he answers the failed attack on [[LiHaoLateHan|李昊]] through grave desecration and public corpse display.

Private punishment without procedure / 无程序私刑 is the legal-political failure [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-88-ni-zenme-kandai-fenghuangnan-1-lpuxkdq-rvnifuoneq212g53w5z5]] draws from [[FanJu|范雎]]'s ordeal in [[WeiState|魏国]]. After [[XuJia|须贾 / 徐谷]] reports that [[QiXiangWang|齐襄王]] privately rewarded Fan Ju, [[WeiQiWarringStates|魏齐]] treats suspicion as proof and uses banquet violence as punishment.

The episode uses the case to separate Warring States Legalist-style "法制" from modern rule of law. A state or court may value law, punishment, order, and deterrence while still lacking investigation, procedure, evidence standards, and protection against an elite official's anger.

The concept is broader than Fan Ju's injuries. It captures how political theater can replace adjudication: punishment is staged before guests, humiliation turns into a warning to others, and the accused person's denial matters less than the senior official's need to display vigilance.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-90-jiemi-xianqin-zui-wanmei-de-fuchou-liu7gbl-bffknkw6dx5udqcq4tcm]] adds the reversal. The earlier victim, [[FanJu|范雎]], now controls Qin's chancellor-level leverage and can force [[XuJia|须贾]] through public humiliation while demanding [[WeiQiWarringStates|魏齐]]'s head. The concept therefore has a political afterlife: a court that substitutes private violence for procedure may export a future enemy who later returns with state power.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-112-3-hanfei-yu-yaojia-lunzhan-qinwang-dang-caipan-ljxxlok4uh-ajjirmolybodlspv9]] adds a prison-court version through [[HanFei|韩非]]. [[QinShiHuang|嬴政 / 秦始皇]] orders Han Fei imprisoned for review after [[LiSi|李斯]]'s loyalty accusation, but Li Si sends poison before the ruler's later pardon can arrive. The failure is not simply harsh punishment; it is the way a rival minister can convert pending procedure into irreversible death.

## Key Claims
- Hanji 903 adds a late-Han form in which both revenge and counter-revenge bypass adjudication and become family-level punishment.
- Severe law and orderly statecraft do not automatically produce reliable legal procedure.
- Suspicion becomes especially dangerous when foreign recognition is interpreted through hierarchy, jealousy, and espionage fear.
- Punishment performed for witnesses can prioritize deterrent display over truth-finding.
- A court that uses private violence against talent may unintentionally export that talent to a rival.
- The retaliatory return can mirror the original failure: Fan Ju does not repeat the same uninvestigated beating, but he does use public humiliation and coercive pressure as punishment.
- Formal detention can still become private punishment if a court actor controls access to the prisoner before review or pardon is complete.
