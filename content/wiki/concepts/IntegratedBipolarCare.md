# Integrated Bipolar Care

## Definition
Integrated bipolar care is the episode's treatment frame that combines qualified medical care and prescription treatment with supportive psychotherapy, social rhythm structure, lifestyle inputs, and carefully bounded supplement discussion.

## Current Synthesis
[[essentials-the-science-treatment-of-bipolar-disorder-scim8821948371]] is explicit that bipolar disorder should not be handled as a lifestyle-only, supplement-only, or talk-therapy-only problem. Prescription drug therapies are presented as central for many patients, and talk therapy is described as most effective when combined with drug therapy. Cognitive behavioral therapy is presented as the best-supported psychotherapy branch in the episode, while interpersonal and social rhythm therapy is framed around relationships, family, school or workplace context, and social patterns.

The supportive-care branch matters, but it stays adjunctive. Sleep, exercise, nutrition, healthy social interaction, daytime sunlight, avoiding bright light at night, inositol, and omega-3 fatty acids are discussed as possible supports or symptom-adjustment tools. The source's treatment boundary is that these supports should not replace psychiatric care, especially given the disorder's intensity and suicide risk.

## Key Claims
- Prescription treatment and qualified medical care are central in the episode's practical recommendations.
- Talk therapy is presented as most useful alongside drug therapy, not as a reliable stand-alone treatment.
- Cognitive behavioral therapy is described as the best-supported talk-therapy branch in the source.
- Interpersonal and social rhythm therapy links bipolar care to relationships, family, school or work context, and daily social patterns.
- Lifestyle supports such as sleep, exercise, nutrition, sunlight, nighttime darkness, and social connection are useful only as supportive care.
- Inositol and omega-3 fatty acids are discussed as supplement-related supports, not replacements for psychiatric treatment.

## Evidence
- Drug-plus-therapy frame: [[essentials-the-science-treatment-of-bipolar-disorder-scim8821948371]] says drug therapies are most effective when combined with talk therapies.
- Talk-therapy boundary: [[essentials-the-science-treatment-of-bipolar-disorder-scim8821948371]] reports that talk therapy alone is rarely, if ever, effective for bipolar disorder according to psychiatrists the speaker consulted.
- Therapy branches: [[essentials-the-science-treatment-of-bipolar-disorder-scim8821948371]] presents cognitive behavioral therapy as best-supported and describes interpersonal/social rhythm therapy as relationship and context focused.
- Lifestyle supports: [[essentials-the-science-treatment-of-bipolar-disorder-scim8821948371]] names sleep, exercise, nutrition, social interaction, daytime sunlight, and avoiding bright light at night as supportive factors.
- Supplement boundary: [[essentials-the-science-treatment-of-bipolar-disorder-scim8821948371]] discusses inositol and omega-3 fatty acids, including a fish-oil study, while warning against purely natural approaches.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode is not an individualized treatment plan. It does not specify medication selection, psychotherapy dosage, relapse plans, emergency protocols, supplement safety, or how to treat comorbid substance use, psychosis, pregnancy, medical illness, or active suicidality.

## What Changed
- Created an integrated-care concept that keeps therapy, lifestyle, and supplements supportive rather than substituting them for psychiatric treatment.
