# ADHD External Structure and Timing

## Definition
ADHD external structure and timing is a support framework that uses scheduling, recurring bodily routines, environmental boundaries, and ADHD-adapted cognitive behavioral tools to reduce executive demands and align daily actions with longer-term goals.

## Current Synthesis
The episode treats external structure as compensation for inconsistent internal organization, not as proof that ADHD is merely a discipline problem. [[JohnKruse]] starts with scheduling and four recurring domains—sleep, eating, movement, and restorative personal time—then adds consolidated task lists, prioritization, workspace design, procrastination reduction, distraction control, and gradual concentration practice.

Timing matters as much as quantity. Regular sleep and meals create dependable anchors, while the episode links ADHD with later chronotype and raises early bright light as a promising but limited evidence branch. Phone removal, app blockers, lock boxes, and agreed reminders make the environment carry some of the inhibitory burden. These measures can complement treatment, but the source does not present them as universal cures or replacements for professional care.

## Key Claims
- External structure can reduce executive load when self-generated structure is unreliable.
- Scheduling connects immediate actions to larger goals and should include sleep, meals, movement, and restorative time.
- Sleep timing and regularity can matter alongside sleep duration, especially when late chronotype and daytime impairment interact.
- One consolidated task list, urgency-importance prioritization, workspace design, and distraction barriers are central ADHD-adapted CBT tools.
- Regular meals and movement can support attention and energy, while optimal exercise type and timing remain uncertain.
- Environmental limits can be more dependable than repeatedly asking willpower to resist phones and social media.

## Evidence
- Scheduling foundation - [[improve-focus-with-behavioral-tools-medication-for-adhd-dr-john-kruse-scim4112569463]] starts behavioral support with scheduling and four basic life domains.
- Sleep and meals - [[improve-focus-with-behavioral-tools-medication-for-adhd-dr-john-kruse-scim4112569463]] emphasizes sleep timing, regularization, phones outside the bedroom, and regular meal timing.
- Movement - [[improve-focus-with-behavioral-tools-medication-for-adhd-dr-john-kruse-scim4112569463]] reports qualified acute and consistent exercise benefits without naming one best protocol.
- CBT structure - [[improve-focus-with-behavioral-tools-medication-for-adhd-dr-john-kruse-scim4112569463]] highlights task consolidation, prioritization, procrastination control, workspace organization, and concentration extension.
- Distraction barriers - [[improve-focus-with-behavioral-tools-medication-for-adhd-dr-john-kruse-scim4112569463]] discusses separate devices, app blockers, lock boxes, and external reminders.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not establish a single best sleep schedule, meal plan, exercise prescription, light dose, breathing protocol, app blocker, or CBT program for every person with ADHD. Early bright-light evidence is described as limited, cyclic sighing is a calming suggestion rather than ADHD treatment evidence, and serious impairment still calls for qualified assessment and care.

## What Changed
- Added one framework joining daily bodily anchors with task and distraction scaffolding.
- Kept light, exercise, and breathing claims provisional rather than universal protocols.
