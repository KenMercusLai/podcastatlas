## Definition

Regent-shadow accession survival is the pattern where a newly enthroned ruler has formal legitimacy but not yet the personnel, military, and court-command capacity to rule independently. In [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-411-hanxuandi-liubingyi-dengji-hou-de-diyichang-huangwei-daweiji-lks_pia3lxwv_qao02pm4mv-xype|Hanji 411]], [[EmperorXuanOfHan|汉宣帝]] survives the first stage after accession by signaling indirectly, honoring the overmighty minister, and staying inside ritual boundaries while [[HuoGuangWesternHan|霍光]] still controls practical authority.

## Current Synthesis

The episode's core case is not a coup but a power imbalance after a lawful accession. Xuan has the imperial title, but Huo Guang holds government, the Huo family fills military and palace-security offices, and the memory of [[LiuHeHaihunHou|刘贺]]'s recent deposition makes open conflict dangerous. Xuan therefore treats survival as a precondition for later authority.

The method has three parts. First, Xuan uses oblique expression: the old-sword edict communicates his wish to make [[XuPingjunWesternHan|许平君]] empress without publicly saying that ministers should reject a Huo-family candidate. Second, he refuses Huo Guang's staged return of authority because taking power before he has people and command would expose him. Third, he handles the戾太子 line through limited ritual recognition, avoiding a move that would deny the adoptive succession chain or threaten [[ShangguanTaihouWesternHan|上官太后]]'s position.

This is distinct from simple weakness. The source reads Xuan's behavior as strategic patience: he preserves affection, ancestry, and future room to govern while not yet challenging the structure that can destroy him.

## Key Claims

- Formal enthronement does not equal practical sovereignty when a regent controls personnel, security, and decision channels.
- Indirect symbolic speech can be safer than direct command when a ruler's preference touches a regent's family interest.
- Refusing returned authority can be an active survival choice if accepting it would create immediate responsibility without usable power.
- Ritual boundaries let a ruler honor biological or emotional claims without destabilizing the legitimacy chain that supports the throne.
- Early survival under a regent can be the precondition for later consolidation rather than evidence of permanent passivity.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept is currently bounded to one early-Xuan episode. The source argues that Xuan's waiting is strategic, but it does not prove every internal motive behind Huo Guang's returned-power gesture, every minister's reading of the old-sword edict, or the full institutional procedure behind the戾太子 ritual settlement. Later sources may distinguish this survival pattern from cases where a weak ruler never gains independent authority.
