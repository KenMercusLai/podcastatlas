# European Deforestation Regulation Supply Chain

European deforestation regulation supply chain is the compliance problem created when forest-linked commodities require traceability before products can be sold into the [[EuropeanUnion|European Union]]. [[our-book-vs-the-global-supply-chain]] adds the concept through [[JuliaDruskin]]'s explanation that paper sourcing affected the [[PlanetMoneyBook]] printer decision because books sold into Europe would need documentation about where and when the paper's wood was harvested.

The source uses the rule not as a full legal explainer but as a publishing logistics case. A sustainability regulation can make one printer country less attractive even if its price looks good, because the compliance burden and risk category can threaten the schedule.

## Key Claims
- Paper makes forest regulation relevant to physical books, not only to raw lumber or agricultural commodities.
- Compliance can require upstream data that a publisher may not normally track at the level regulators require.
- A country or supplier risk category can change printer selection when schedule slack is limited.
- Sustainability rules can create real supply-chain discipline while also adding operational burden for cultural goods.
