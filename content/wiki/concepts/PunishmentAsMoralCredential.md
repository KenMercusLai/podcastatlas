## Definition

Punishment as moral credential is the pattern where being punished by a discredited or captured authority is treated as evidence of moral alignment, while avoiding punishment can feel like exclusion from the honorable community.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 875 gives the current source case through Huangfu Gui. In an ordinary incentive field, a court not arresting an official would be safety. In the first Party Prohibition field, Huangfu Gui reads it differently because the imprisoned people are famous worthy men. Exclusion from punishment threatens to make him look morally outside the group he respects, so he voluntarily states his own ties to party figures.

The concept differs from martyrdom. Huangfu Gui is not killed, and the court does not even pursue him. The important mechanism is symbolic inversion: punishment no longer signals only guilt; under a morally compromised state, it can become a credential of shared名节. This helps explain why later party-prohibition figures can treat exposure, self-reporting, and prison entry as honor-bearing even when practically dangerous.

## Key Claims

- Punishment can be reinterpreted as honor when the punishing authority lacks moral legitimacy.
- Non-punishment can become reputationally uncomfortable when respected peers are punished.
- Voluntary self-reporting can assert belonging to a moral community rather than merely confess wrongdoing.
- The pattern depends on public knowledge that the punished group is regarded as worthy.
- It is distinct from ordinary martyrdom because the credential effect can appear before death or even without successful punishment.
- The pattern is unstable: it can preserve名节 but also makes repression easier when authorities target visible ties.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept does not claim that punishment is inherently honorable or that all punished actors are worthy. It applies when a community already treats the targets as morally credible and the punishing power as corrupt or misdirected. Hanji 875 is short, so the concept is deliberately anchored in Huangfu Gui's self-report rather than a full theory of late-Han Party Prohibition.
