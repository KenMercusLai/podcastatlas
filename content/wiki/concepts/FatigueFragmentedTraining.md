# Fatigue-Fragmented Training

## Definition
Fatigue-fragmented training distributes a workload into small, well-rested exposures so technique, force, speed, or attention remain high while acute fatigue and recovery cost stay bounded.

## Current Synthesis
The main example in [[the-correct-way-to-build-strength-endurance-flexibility-at-any-age-pavel-tsatsouline-scim3681411661]] is "greasing the groove": practice a strength movement frequently with moderately heavy loads, perform roughly half or fewer of the possible repetitions, and rest long enough to repeat without grinding. The source also describes fragmentation more broadly across high-frequency lifting and low-repetition tonic sessions intended to support rather than deplete later performance.

Fragmentation is not automatically superior to concentrated training. It is most coherent when the target is neural skill, crisp output, or frequent exposure and when the schedule, equipment, and movement safety permit repeated bouts. Total workload, tissue stress, monotony, and recovery still accumulate even when each set feels fresh.

## Key Claims
- Spacing practice can preserve movement quality and force better than cramming the same work into one exhausting bout.
- Submaximal sets leave repetitions in reserve so exposures can be repeated while fresh.
- Frequent successful practice may support motor learning and reduce the biological cost of any single session.
- Fragmentation can create a tonic rather than depleting effect when total volume and intensity remain controlled.
- Distributed work still requires tracking cumulative load, joint stress, and life constraints.

## Evidence
- Greasing-the-groove example - [[the-correct-way-to-build-strength-endurance-flexibility-at-any-age-pavel-tsatsouline-scim3681411661]] describes approximately 75-85% loads, half or fewer of available repetitions, long inter-set spacing, and recurring days of practice.
- System comparison - [[the-correct-way-to-build-strength-endurance-flexibility-at-any-age-pavel-tsatsouline-scim3681411661]] contrasts fragmented high-frequency Soviet weightlifting practice with concentrated lower-frequency cycles and invokes different recovery rates across biological systems.
- Tonic-use example - [[the-correct-way-to-build-strength-endurance-flexibility-at-any-age-pavel-tsatsouline-scim3681411661]] describes low-repetition strength work used to leave track athletes activated rather than exhausted.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The supplied summary does not provide controlled comparisons, adherence data, or injury outcomes for the example schedule. Frequent exposure can increase overuse risk or logistical burden, and a nominally fresh set can still add substantial cumulative fatigue. Hypertrophy and some conditioning goals may benefit from concentrated local fatigue that this approach deliberately limits.

## What Changed
- Established fragmentation as a distinct workload-distribution strategy rather than a synonym for low volume.
