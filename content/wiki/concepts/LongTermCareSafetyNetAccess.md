# Long-Term Care Safety-Net Access / 長照安全網可及性

## Definition
Long-term care safety-net access / 長照安全網可及性 is the difference between care resources existing on paper and a person under sustained care pressure being identified, informed, referred, able to pay, willing to seek help, and continuously supported in practice.

## Current Synthesis
The Taiwan cases show that high service coverage does not by itself establish a functioning safety net. Access is a chain: households must recognize a long-term-care condition, understand eligibility and services, cross financial and cultural barriers, trigger accurate assessment, receive a referral, and remain connected while risk changes.

The current judgment is that a failure at any link can return the whole burden to one family member. A missed J-scale alert and a 160-dollar co-payment are not complete explanations for lethal events, but they reveal how small administrative or financial barriers can become consequential after years of sleep loss, illness, isolation, interrupted treatment, and failed family support.

## Key Claims
- Service coverage and effective access are different measures of care-system performance.
- Risk assessment protects people only when thresholds, alerts, referrals, and follow-up work as one operational chain.
- Information access requires knowing eligibility, available tasks, timing, and rights, not merely recognizing a hotline number.
- Small co-payments can become binding when the caregiver has left work, lacks cash, or must ask an unwilling family member.
- Help-seeking is shaped by shame, gender roles, family withdrawal, distrust, and the belief that care is a private duty.
- Serious-incident review should trace cross-system failures rather than search for one final trigger.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence is one journalism episode built around severe cases, expert interpretation, and reported administrative figures. It does not estimate how often each access failure occurs, whether post-incident reforms work, or whether Taiwan's regional services perform uniformly. Co-payments, alert settings, gender, or diagnosis should not be treated as deterministic causes of violence; urgent risk requires qualified local intervention.

## What Changed
- Established the distinction between nominal coverage and completed access.
- Identified assessment, referral, affordability, information, and help-seeking as one failure-sensitive chain.
