# Dunning-Kruger Effect

The Dunning-Kruger effect appears in [[ep-3-demystifying-the-imposter-syndrome]] as the overconfidence counter-pattern to [[ImpostorSyndrome|imposter syndrome]]. [[SamDataScienceWithSam|Sam]] describes it as overestimating one's ability, while [[StephenMathis|Stephen Mathis]] says he would personally rather err on the cautious side than make confident wrong assumptions that can affect other people.

The episode does not treat either pattern as ideal. Impostor feelings can block action and distort self-worth, while Dunning-Kruger-style confidence can create operational damage when unearned certainty drives decisions. The useful target is [[CapabilityGapSelfDiagnosis]] and team-level balance.

In the source, the managerial implication is [[ConfidenceProfileTeamManagement]]: identify who is too cautious, who is too confident, and how each profile can be used or corrected without assuming the person is fixed.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1009-liubei-touben-caocao-xianxie-langru-hukou-llm0ds4krkptjdtb71hbusnpzbdc|Hanji 1009]] adds a source-scoped historical analogy through [[LyuBu|吕布]]. The host uses the "达克效应" label to argue that Lü Bu's military force is not matched by strategic self-diagnosis: he overestimates his own judgment, fails to learn from shifting alliance conditions, and cannot build the personnel or institutional base needed for durable power.

## Key Claims
- Overconfidence can be more externally harmful than private self-doubt when wrong assumptions shape work for others.
- Caution has value, but too much caution can still block action.
- Teams may need both skepticism and forward-pull, provided confidence is checked by feedback and evidence.
- The practical opposite of impostor syndrome is not arrogance; it is calibrated confidence.
- Hanji 1009 adds the historical-commentary use: tactical strength can hide weak self-calibration if the actor reads every problem through immediate force and threat.
