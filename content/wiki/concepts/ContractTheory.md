# Contract Theory / 契约理论

Contract theory is the central frame in [[79-gewei-lingdao-danfan-zan-xuedian-boyilun-690004262]]. The episode uses [[NieHuihua|聂辉华]]'s [[YiQieJieQiyue|《一切皆契约》]] to define contract broadly: not only written agreements, but durable relationships, rules, institutions, work arrangements, and coordination systems under imperfect information.

The source's practical claim is that many problems should be analyzed by asking what the contract makes rational. Employees, bosses, publishers, students, executives, donors, residents, and internal teams may behave badly because the arrangement hides information, misallocates risk, rewards the wrong signal, or cannot be fully enforced.

## Key Claims
- Real-world contracts are often incomplete because market choice, information, and enforceability are imperfect.
- Contract design should bind incentives, risks, signals, and proof mechanisms to the actual behavior the system needs.
- Moral judgment still matters, but moral exhortation is too weak when the contract makes bad behavior rational.
- Firms exist partly because hierarchy can lower transaction costs where market contracting would require constant renegotiation.
