## Definition

赞颂作为政治记忆 / praise as political memory is the rhetorical pattern in which praise does more than please a ruler in the moment: it frames the ruler's words or actions as material worthy of circulation, imitation, and later historical remembrance.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-416-xihan-xiahousheng-ruhe-pai-hanxuandi-de-mapi-lvqbs--20kvjimwdwgfciq-n6wee|Hanji 416]] creates the concept through [[XiahouShengWesternHan|夏侯胜]]'s answer to [[EmperorXuanOfHan|汉宣帝]]. After Xiahou repeats the emperor's words outside the palace and Xuan becomes angry, Xiahou does not deny the disclosure. He reframes it: because the words are good, they should be spread, just as sage-ruler speech can still be heard after centuries.

That makes the praise politically unusual. Xiahou is not presented as a court flatterer with no credibility; the episode stresses his learning, prison study, plain conduct, direct speech, and lack of private motive. Praise becomes safe and effective because it is backed by a reputation for truthfulness. The ruler receives not only emotional reassurance but an image of himself as a possible sage ruler whose words deserve transmission.

## Key Claims

- Praise can change the meaning of a risky act when it frames the act as publicizing good governance rather than leaking private talk.
- The credibility of praise depends on the speaker's broader character; the same words from a purely opportunistic actor would carry different risk.
- Historical memory is a ruler-facing incentive: praise works by making the ruler imagine later judgment, not only present approval.
- Praise and remonstrance can overlap when praise encourages a ruler to inhabit the better image being attributed to him.

## Evidence

Leak reframed as circulation:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-416-xihan-xiahousheng-ruhe-pai-hanxuandi-de-mapi-lvqbs--20kvjimwdwgfciq-n6wee|Hanji 416]] says Xiahou Sheng repeats Xuan's words after an audience and answers the emperor's anger by saying good royal speech should be spread.

Credibility before praise:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-416-xihan-xiahousheng-ruhe-pai-hanxuandi-de-mapi-lvqbs--20kvjimwdwgfciq-n6wee|Hanji 416]] presents Xiahou as upright, plain, learned, and trusted despite informal speech.

Memory incentive:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-416-xihan-xiahousheng-ruhe-pai-hanxuandi-de-mapi-lvqbs--20kvjimwdwgfciq-n6wee|Hanji 416]] compares the spread of Xuan's words to Yao's words being remembered and uses [[Confucius|孔子]] and the Analects tradition as examples of teaching preserved by disciples.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept is not a general defense of flattery. The episode's own contrast is that Xiahou's praise works because it follows visible integrity, study, and directness. Without that credibility, similar language could look like opportunistic ingratiation or unsafe leakage. The source also gives a podcast-level interpretation rather than a full original-source study of court confidentiality rules.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Hanji 416's Xiahou Sheng episode.
- Defined the pattern as memory-framing praise rather than generic compliment or simple self-protection.
