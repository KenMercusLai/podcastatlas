## Definition

Principled office refusal / 原则性拒仕 is the choice by capable scholars or officials to decline office when service would require trust in a court or patronage structure they no longer regard as morally credible.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 713 adds the harshest current variant: principled office refusal under a coercive breakaway regime. [[GongsunShuEasternHan|Gongsun Shu]] tries to force Shu scholars and local officials into service through poison, hostage families, and title inducements. [[LiNieEasternHan|Li Nie]] drinks poison, [[QiaoXuanShuScholar|Qiao Xuan]] is saved by his son's payment, [[WangHaoShuScholar|Wang Hao]] and [[WangJiaShuScholar|Wang Jia]] kill themselves under hostage pressure, Fei Yi, Ren Yong, and Feng Xin use illness or incapacity strategies, and [[WenQiYizhouEasternHan|Wen Qi]] refuses despite family hostages while secretly submitting to Liu Xiu.

The earlier Emperor An case makes timing central to principled office refusal before the later Huan-era recluse cluster. In [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-799-donghan-mingchen-yangzhen-ruhe-jujue-zou-houmen-lmufb7lvb-kzsvjtn6iu-zrpiptf|Hanji 799]], [[ChenZhongLateHan|陈忠]] recommends [[ZhouXieLateHan|周燮]] and [[FengLiangLateHan|冯良]] as learned and upright men, and Emperor An summons them with formal gifts. Zhou Xie answers his clan by saying cultivated people wait for the right time; he and Feng Liang then return to their counties, claim illness, and do not serve. The refusal is not framed as lack of ability but as a timing judgment under a court already marked by appointment abuse and ignored remonstrance.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-849-sushi-ziyou-du-de-fanpangzhuan-jiujing-shuo-de-shui-ltquglqzvezeyp0mqxwgwnlp12lf|Hanji 849]] creates the concept's fuller late-Han contrast. [[FanPangLateHan|范滂]] enters office and tries to clean up corruption as Qingzhao envoy, while [[XuZhiLateHan|徐稚]], [[JiangGongLateHan|姜公]], [[YuanHongLateHan|袁闳]], [[WeiZhuLateHan|韦著]], and [[LiTanLateHan|李昙]] refuse summons despite imperial gifts. The refusal is not presented as inability, laziness, or hatred of all social ties. Xu Zhi farms and mourns carefully; Jiang Gong sustains family solidarity; Yuan Hong cultivates integrity; Wei Zhu teaches texts; Li Tan practices filial service.

The concept therefore narrows reclusion into a political judgment. A court can offer honors and polite recruitment, but if its surrounding order is distrusted, worthy people may preserve moral agency by withholding service. The serving and non-serving branches stay related: Yang Zhen and Fan Pang show what principled office can look like when one accepts duty, while Zhou Xie, Feng Liang, and the later five recluses show why the same moral seriousness may lead others to refuse.

## Key Claims

- Office refusal can be an ethical judgment about institutional credibility, not a sign that the person lacks ability.
- Respectful recruitment and gifts do not guarantee legitimacy when the court's broader conduct is distrusted.
- Coercive summons can make refusal more, not less, ethically legible because accepting office would help launder forced legitimacy.
- Timing matters: a capable person may judge that service before the right political moment will fail or compromise the person.
- Illness claims, feigned incapacity, ransom-mediated survival, and refusal to let one's family decide can be tactics within principled refusal under lethal pressure.
- The concept is distinct from total retreat because recluses can preserve work, teaching, mourning courtesy, filial service, and family duty while withholding court service.
- Serving integrity and principled refusal answer the same corruption problem differently.

## Evidence

- Poison-backed refusal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-713-liuxiu-nu-le-mingjiang-wuhan-jing-xuexi-chengdu-lv6555l99it33gw34uydsz4tkbje|Hanji 713]] says Li Nie drinks poison rather than accept Gongsun Shu's summoned office, while Qiao Xuan is prepared to do the same before his son pays to save him.
- Hostage-backed refusal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-713-liuxiu-nu-le-mingjiang-wuhan-jing-xuexi-chengdu-lv6555l99it33gw34uydsz4tkbje|Hanji 713]] says Wang Hao and Wang Jia kill themselves rather than serve under family-hostage pressure.
- Tactical non-service: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-713-liuxiu-nu-le-mingjiang-wuhan-jing-xuexi-chengdu-lv6555l99it33gw34uydsz4tkbje|Hanji 713]] says Fei Yi uses lacquer-induced illness or madness and Ren Yong and Feng Xin feign illness or blindness to avoid service.
- Loyal refusal with later service: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-713-liuxiu-nu-le-mingjiang-wuhan-jing-xuexi-chengdu-lv6555l99it33gw34uydsz4tkbje|Hanji 713]] says Wen Qi refuses Gongsun Shu despite hostages and title offer, secretly submits to Liu Xiu, and later accepts Liu Xiu's appointment and enfeoffment.
- Early summons refusal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-799-donghan-mingchen-yangzhen-ruhe-jujue-zou-houmen-lmufb7lvb-kzsvjtn6iu-zrpiptf|Hanji 799]] says Chen Zhong recommends Zhou Xie and Feng Liang, Emperor An summons them, and both refuse by returning to their counties and claiming illness.
- Timing explanation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-799-donghan-mingchen-yangzhen-ruhe-jujue-zou-houmen-lmufb7lvb-kzsvjtn6iu-zrpiptf|Hanji 799]] preserves Zhou Xie's argument that cultivated people act only when the time is right.
- Recruitment failure: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-849-sushi-ziyou-du-de-fanpangzhuan-jiujing-shuo-de-shui-ltquglqzvezeyp0mqxwgwnlp12lf|Hanji 849]] says Chen Fan recommends five scholars, Huan sends formal gifts, and all five refuse office.
- Capable non-service: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-849-sushi-ziyou-du-de-fanpangzhuan-jiujing-shuo-de-shui-ltquglqzvezeyp0mqxwgwnlp12lf|Hanji 849]] presents Xu Zhi, Jiang Gong, Yuan Hong, Wei Zhu, and Li Tan as morally respected figures rather than incapable outsiders.
- Selective obligation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-849-sushi-ziyou-du-de-fanpangzhuan-jiujing-shuo-de-shui-ltquglqzvezeyp0mqxwgwnlp12lf|Hanji 849]] shows Xu Zhi farming and mourning, Jiang Gong protecting and forgiving, Wei Zhu teaching, and Li Tan honoring his stepmother.
- Serving contrast: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-849-sushi-ziyou-du-de-fanpangzhuan-jiujing-shuo-de-shui-ltquglqzvezeyp0mqxwgwnlp12lf|Hanji 849]] places Fan Pang's accountable impeachment work before the recluse cluster, making office integrity and office refusal parallel responses.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept should not be generalized into a rule that all officeholding under disorder is compromised. Hanji 799 itself places Yang Zhen's refusal of private appointment requests inside active service, and Hanji 849 gives Fan Pang as a serving integrity case. Hanji 713 reinforces the distinction: Wen Qi refuses Gongsun Shu but accepts Liu Xiu's later appointment, so the principle attaches to the regime and conditions of service rather than to officeholding as such. Ransom-mediated survival and feigned incapacity should also be read as pressure responses, not as weaker morality than martyrdom.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 713 as a coercive Shu variant: poison, hostage pressure, ransom, feigned incapacity, and loyal local refusal under Gongsun Shu.
- Preserved Hanji 799 as an earlier Zhou Xie and Feng Liang office-refusal layer under Emperor An.
- Preserved "timing" as a central reason for refusal, not only generalized distrust.
- Preserved Hanji 849's Fan Pang and five-recluse contrast as the concept's fuller late-Han form.
