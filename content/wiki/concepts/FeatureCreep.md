# Feature Creep

Feature creep is the pattern where a product expands beyond its useful first purpose because good ideas keep being added without enough priority, customer clarity, or deadline pressure. In [[we-almost-had-a-smartphone-in-the-90s-why-did-it-fail]], [[GeneralMagic]] illustrates the problem through games, sound effects, broad device functions, and the [[StevePerlman]] calendar story.

The calendar example is the source's clearest small-scale case. A limited date-range feature expanded back toward the beginning of astronomical time even though the [[SonyMagicLink]] still lacked core product coherence. The lesson is that technical completeness can become a local optimization when the overall product has not decided what matters.

[[stuck-at-50k-arr-for-5-years-now-1-5m-with-ai-agents]] adds a SaaS qualification through [[Happierleads]]. [[GeorgeGeorgiadis]] says building too broad a lead-generation product contributed to a long $50K ARR plateau, but the same integrated scope later became useful once the company had enough distribution and product maturity. That edge case is better captured by [[SaaSProductScopeDebt]] than by treating all scope expansion as waste.

## Key Claims
- Feature creep often comes from talent and enthusiasm, not laziness.
- It becomes dangerous when no constraint tells the team which good ideas are irrelevant to the first customer.
- It can make a product feel impressive to builders while becoming confusing to customers.
- Deadlines, [[ClearCustomerDefinition]], and [[BuildVsBorrowProductStrategy]] are practical counterweights.
- Some scope expansion can later become differentiation, but only if the company survives long enough and can sell, support, and integrate the broader workflow coherently.
