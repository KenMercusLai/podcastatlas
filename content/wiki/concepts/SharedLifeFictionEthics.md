## Definition

Shared-life fiction ethics concerns the responsibilities created when a writer transforms experiences jointly lived with partners, relatives, friends, or other identifiable people into fiction.

## Current Synthesis

[[AlissaEberhardt|Alissa Eberhardt / 阿丽莎·艾伯哈特]] argues that fiction borrows, divides, distorts, combines, and invents rather than copying life. [[RolandBaines|Roland Baines / 罗兰·贝恩斯]] nevertheless experiences her novels as possible public judgments about him. The ethical problem is therefore not solved by choosing either “fiction is memoir” or “invention cancels impact”: artistic transformation can be real while recognition, asymmetrical publication power, privacy, and reputational harm remain real too.

## Key Claims

- Joint experience does not belong exclusively to either the writer or the person later represented.
- Fictional transformation breaks literal identity without guaranteeing practical anonymity.
- Authorial freedom and another person's exposure can both be genuine interests.
- Readers' search for real-life originals can create harms the text neither fully controls nor eliminates.
- Ethical judgment requires specificity about transformation, identifiability, power, and likely effect.

## Evidence

### Transformation rather than transcription

- [[vol-358-gangqinke-wo-yijing-xiangkai-le-xiwang-ni-ye-xiangkai-yidian-gkwrijeotl1aaype0qtvn81u]] gives Alissa's account of splitting and recombining several people and events into fiction rather than writing direct memoir.

### Recognition and reputational fear

- [[vol-358-gangqinke-wo-yijing-xiangkai-le-xiwang-ni-ye-xiangkai-yidian-gkwrijeotl1aaype0qtvn81u]] shows Roland repeatedly searching her books for himself and fearing that a violent fictional husband will be read as accusation.

### Unequal control over public meaning

- [[vol-358-gangqinke-wo-yijing-xiangkai-le-xiwang-ni-ye-xiangkai-yidian-gkwrijeotl1aaype0qtvn81u]] frames their late dispute as a conflict over who may define shared history once one participant has literary authority.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source supplies a fictional conflict rather than a general legal rule. Identifiability, defamation, consent, genre, public interest, vulnerability, and jurisdiction vary across cases. A represented person can misrecognize themselves, while “fiction” can also be used too casually as a shield against foreseeable harm.

## What Changed

- Created a framework that preserves both transformative invention and the residual claims of people implicated by shared experience.
