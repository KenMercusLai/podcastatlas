# Collective Biological Agency

## Definition
Collective biological agency is coordinated problem-solving by cells, organisms, or groups whose local sensing, memory, and interaction produce adaptive behavior at a larger scale.

## Current Synthesis
The episode joins flocking, swarming, slime molds, social insects, xenobots, planarian regeneration, and tadpole remodeling to argue that biological form is not exhausted by a one-way genetic program. DNA supplies important constraints, but morphology, bioelectric state, embodiment, local signaling, and remembered target states can alter what cellular collectives do. Agency here means adaptive coordination toward outcomes, not proof of human-like consciousness.

## Key Claims
- Collective behavior can improve navigation, feeding, defense, and site selection.
- Development can involve error correction rather than fixed execution alone.
- Bioelectric changes may redirect regenerative outcomes without changing DNA.
- Morphology can alter behavior even among cells with the same genome.
- Biological agency and natural selection need not be rival explanations.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Goal language can clarify adaptive organization while also inviting anthropomorphism. The source does not establish consciousness in cells, validate every Lamarckian mechanism, or supply methods and replication context for the cited experiments. Genetic inheritance and natural selection remain essential parts of the account.
