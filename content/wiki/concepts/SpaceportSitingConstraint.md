# Spaceport Siting Constraint

Spaceport siting constraint is the limited set of places where rockets can launch with acceptable safety, geography, logistics, and public costs. In [[tech-20260818-tech-pod-128-tech-20260818-tech-pod-128]], [[DanielAckerman]] lists three central requirements: distance from population centers, access to a deep water port, and favorable geography for eastward launches near the equator.

The concept explains why [[SpaceLaunchCapacityBottleneck]] cannot be solved only by regulatory waiver or private investment. Launches can create debris, noise, air-travel interruption, habitat pressure, and local safety concerns, while [[WendyWhitmanCobb]] says very few U.S. areas still meet the combined requirements.

## Key Claims
- Good launch geography is scarce because safety, population distance, port access, and orbital mechanics must align.
- Eastward launches near the equator can use Earth's rotation to save fuel, making geography economically relevant.
- Launch externalities include noise, debris risk, commercial airspace disruption, and environmental effects.
- New spaceports may move pressure away from crowded hubs, but they can also shift risk toward coastal communities and sensitive habitats.
- Ocean platforms are a possible workaround when land sites are too scarce or politically constrained.
