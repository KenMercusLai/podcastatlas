# Covert Consciousness

## Definition
Covert consciousness is retained awareness or intentional cognitive response that is not apparent from a patient's visible movement, speech, or routine bedside behavior.

## Current Synthesis
The source presents covert consciousness as the clinically urgent form of [[ConsciousnessBehaviorDissociation|consciousness-behavior dissociation]]. Some apparently unresponsive patients may follow instructions by voluntarily changing measurable brain activity, even though motor injury or other damage prevents an outward answer. Perturbational brain-response complexity offers a complementary state measure when command following cannot be demonstrated.

The ethical implication is significant but not mechanical. Evidence of retained awareness can affect prognosis, pain and communication assumptions, rehabilitation, and decisions about life-sustaining treatment. A neural signal must still be interpreted through test validity, repeatability, diagnosis, timing, uncertainty, patient wishes, and clinical context; it is not by itself a complete end-of-life rule.

## Key Claims
- Lack of visible response is not equivalent to lack of conscious experience.
- Command-following brain activity can reveal intentional response without movement or speech.
- Perturbational complexity may provide complementary evidence when active command following is unavailable.
- Detection can change care, prognosis, communication attempts, and ethical deliberation.
- No single result should replace longitudinal clinical assessment or patient-centered decision-making.

## Evidence
- Hidden command following - [[scim8616450810-scim8616450810]] describes behaviorally unresponsive patients who can modulate brain activity in response to instructions.
- State assessment - [[scim8616450810-scim8616450810]] describes transcranial magnetic stimulation, high-density EEG, and response complexity as a way to distinguish conscious from unconscious states in the discussed data.
- Clinical stakes - [[scim8616450810-scim8616450810]] connects detection to intensive-care and life-sustaining-treatment decisions.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The supplied summary does not provide study designs, sensitivity, specificity, prevalence, false-positive rates, timing effects, or outcome data. Brain activation can be difficult to interpret, and absence of a detected signal does not necessarily prove absence of consciousness. Diagnosis, prognosis, decision-making capacity, suffering, prior wishes, family judgment, and treatment proportionality remain distinct questions.

## What Changed
- Created a clinical concept separating hidden awareness from visible responsiveness and from automatic treatment conclusions.
