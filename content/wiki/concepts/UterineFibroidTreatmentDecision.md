# Uterine Fibroid Treatment Decision / 子宫肌瘤治疗决策

## Definition
Uterine fibroid treatment decision is a framework that weighs fibroid location, cavity distortion, symptoms, growth, fertility goals, malignancy concern, uterine-preservation preference, and available procedures rather than using size alone.

## Current Synthesis
Location changes functional impact. A small fibroid projecting into the uterine cavity may affect bleeding or pregnancy more than a larger outward-growing fibroid without pain, torsion, pressure, or cavity distortion. Intramural fibroids occupy an intermediate branch in which size, inward or outward projection, and symptoms jointly shape the threshold for treatment.

The choice is also preference-sensitive. Uterine preservation, future pregnancy, tolerance for recurrence, desire for a definitive procedure, and access to hysteroscopic, laparoscopic, open, or focused-ultrasound approaches can all matter. The source therefore rejects both “large means hysterectomy” and “benign means never intervene.”

## Key Claims
- Fibroid location and cavity distortion can matter more than size alone.
- Symptoms, pressure effects, growth, fertility context, and malignancy concern modify the treatment threshold.
- Uterine preservation is often a treatment goal rather than an obstacle to care.
- Procedure choice should reflect anatomy, reproductive plans, recurrence tolerance, patient preference, and qualified assessment.
- Family history can support awareness and routine care without making disease inevitable.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is public education rather than a procedure-selection guideline. Exact size thresholds, imaging classification, growth significance, fertility effects, malignancy risk, surveillance intervals, and suitability for hysteroscopy, laparoscopy, laparotomy, focused ultrasound, myomectomy, or hysterectomy require current individualized assessment. Food and supplement claims in the episode do not establish causation for fibroid growth.

## What Changed
- Established location, symptoms, fertility goals, uterine preservation, and patient preference as the primary decision dimensions beyond size.
