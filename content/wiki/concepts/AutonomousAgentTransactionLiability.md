# Autonomous Agent Transaction Liability

## Definition
Autonomous agent transaction liability is the allocation of responsibility when an AI agent uses delegated credentials or authority to move money, buy goods, or otherwise bind a user or organization.

## Current Synthesis
Liability turns on more than who clicked a button. The source separates action within a clear user mandate, user circumvention of warnings or service terms, inadequate platform controls, and a product acting outside its represented purpose. A workable transaction stack therefore needs authenticated parties, a preserved mandate, amount and task limits, warnings, human confirmation for material actions, execution records, revocation, and dispute processes.

## Key Claims
- Delegating credentials can make an agent's in-scope action attributable to the user or organization.
- User bypass of explicit controls can shift responsibility toward the user.
- Action outside the agent's represented purpose can create developer or provider product-liability exposure.
- Banks, merchants, agent platforms, software providers, and users need evidence that distinguishes instruction, execution, and fulfillment failures.
- Transaction limits and final confirmation can reduce ambiguity without resolving every legal question.

## Evidence
### Competing responsibility rules
- [[allin-e294-ch-allin-e294-ch]] records disagreement over a bank-credential scenario: one view assigns responsibility according to terms and bypassed safeguards, while another treats delegated agency as presumptively the user's action.

### Product-deviation boundary
- [[allin-e294-ch-allin-e294-ch]] distinguishes authorized execution from an agent departing from its stated purpose, which the speakers treat as a possible product-liability case.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The discussion is not legal advice and does not analyze a specific jurisdiction, contract, bank rule, negligence standard, consumer-protection regime, or adjudicated case. Actual allocation may depend on authorization language, system design, disclosures, foreseeability, technical controls, causation, and statutory protections.

## What Changed
- Established a responsibility map separating user mandate, safeguard bypass, platform control, and product deviation.
