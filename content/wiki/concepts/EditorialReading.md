# Editorial Reading / 编辑式阅读

## Definition

Editorial reading is a work-first practice that reconstructs what a text is trying to do and how its reporting, selection, form, voice, structure, and omissions make it work before deciding whether it is useful, likable, credible, or aligned with the reader.

## Current Synthesis

The source distinguishes editorial reading from both point extraction and preference matching. A reader first asks what kind of work is present, why it takes this form, which people and scenes carry its abstractions, how the lead and ending frame the subject, and what was gained or sacrificed through those choices. Personal use, agreement, pleasure, and suspicion remain valid later questions, but they should not erase the object before it has been understood.

This sequencing also changes the relation between reader and world. If every new text is immediately translated into an existing need or category, external material becomes another patch for the reader's current system. Letting the work set the first question creates a limited opportunity for unfamiliar subjects, forms, and values to revise that system. Because this is slow and attention-intensive, the source presents it as an occasional heavy method rather than a rule for all reading.

## Key Claims

- Understanding a work and deciding its value to oneself are distinct stages, even when they later interact.
- Leads, scenes, sources, characters, comparisons, pacing, structure, and endings are evidence about a work's judgment, not decorative surplus.
- Delaying motive judgments can reveal choices and trade-offs that a premature label such as “advertising” or “propaganda” would hide.
- Work-first attention can expose questions that are absent from the reader's current interests and categories.
- Accepting that all presentation involves selection and loss can weaken the fantasy of a complete, optimally ranked reading list.
- Editorial reading is costly and situational; fast, instrumental, and recreational reading remain legitimate modes.

## Evidence

### Reconstructing the work before judging it

- [[zong-di-075-qi-xiang-zazhi-bianji-yiyang-yuedu-cmw5vho5t00dr01w28ir3f88r]] asks readers to identify genre, subject, lead, interview choices, scenes, organization, and ending before reducing a piece to usefulness or preference.

### Delayed judgment and external surprise

- [[zong-di-075-qi-xiang-zazhi-bianji-yiyang-yuedu-cmw5vho5t00dr01w28ir3f88r]] argues that motive-first dismissal can obscure fresh material and that an initially unserious-seeming article can still force a reader to re-examine the criterion used to reject it.

### Selective use rather than universal slowness

- [[zong-di-075-qi-xiang-zazhi-bianji-yiyang-yuedu-cmw5vho5t00dr01w28ir3f88r]] explicitly exempts instructions, brief news, and entertainment from mandatory heavy reading.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Delayed judgment can become credulity if sponsorship, fabrication, manipulation, or urgent safety signals are ignored. Not every article has coherent intentions or rewards formal analysis, and readers have finite time, access, language ability, and attention. The source offers a reflective practice grounded in one editor's experience, not evidence that it improves comprehension or reduces anxiety for all readers.

## What Changed

- Established editorial reading as a sequence: reconstruct the work, then judge its relation to oneself.
- Kept motive, preference, utility, and skepticism as later judgments rather than eliminating them.
- Added cost and situational use as explicit boundaries.
