# Artwork Value, Provenance, and Attention / 艺术品价值、来源与注意力

## Definition
Artwork value, provenance, and attention is the framework that separates personal or aesthetic response from market price while explaining price through authorship, provenance, institutional recognition, narrative, scarcity, reproduction, publicity, and expected audience attention.

## Current Synthesis
[[ep163-bei-hulianwang-chongji-de-yishu-ckwriaieiyu2abaaaacscgxf|EP163]] rejects both pure formalism and pure price worship. An imitation may resemble an original, yet the market also prices the artist's larger practice, documented custody, exhibition record, institutional backing, historical position, and story. Conceptual works make the distinction especially visible because ownership may attach to a certificate and right of re-performance rather than irreplaceable material. Internet circulation then changes the attention layer: it can expose more people to competing information while also converting controversy, photogenic spectacle, and notoriety into commercial leverage.

## Key Claims
- Personal meaning, critical judgment, cultural importance, and market price are related but non-identical forms of value.
- Provenance reduces uncertainty by connecting an object to custody, exhibitions, collections, and institutional records.
- An artist's broader life and body of work can matter even when a single form is easy to imitate.
- Conceptual ownership may reside in authorization and reproducible instructions rather than fixed material.
- Theft, publicity, parody, reproduction, and museum traffic can accumulate attention value around a work.
- Internet transparency can weaken information monopolies without eliminating hype, fraud, or status markets.

## Evidence
- Original and imitation - [[ep163-bei-hulianwang-chongji-de-yishu-ckwriaieiyu2abaaaacscgxf]] contrasts visual similarity with an original's place in an artist's life and continuing practice.
- Provenance and fraud - [[ep163-bei-hulianwang-chongji-de-yishu-ckwriaieiyu2abaaaacscgxf]] describes authentication through museum, exhibition, collection, and custody history and shows how forged records can corrupt that chain.
- Conceptual ownership - [[ep163-bei-hulianwang-chongji-de-yishu-ckwriaieiyu2abaaaacscgxf]] uses the taped-banana case to distinguish replaceable materials from a certificate-backed right to reproduce the idea.
- Attention accumulation - [[ep163-bei-hulianwang-chongji-de-yishu-ckwriaieiyu2abaaaacscgxf]] connects the [[MonaLisa|Mona Lisa]] to theft, reporting, touring, and popular reuse and treats museum traffic as one possible commercial input.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The framework does not produce an objective formula for artistic worth or verify the episode's estimates, legal claims, forgery details, or institutional examples. Provenance can be incomplete or falsified, attention can be negative or transient, and high price may reflect wealth, speculation, scarcity, tax treatment, or status rather than broad critical agreement. The source does not settle whether every certificate-based or publicity-driven object succeeds artistically.

## What Changed
- Created a bounded framework distinguishing artistic response from the provenance, institution, narrative, and attention mechanisms that affect price.
