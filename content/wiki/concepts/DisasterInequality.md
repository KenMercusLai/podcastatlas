# Disaster Inequality

Disaster inequality is the pattern in [[afghanistan-five-years-on-our-correspondent-visits-6a7ee547049c692a6b3ee0a9]] where a natural hazard exposes unequal geography, infrastructure, health care and political attention. The source applies it to [[Colombia]] after a 7.4-magnitude earthquake, contrasting better-served areas such as Cali and the coffee region with remote Choco and Quibdo.

The concept extends [[DisasterResponseStateCapacity]] and [[NaturalHazardSocialDisaster]]. The earthquake is damaging in itself, but the uneven response depends on roads, landslides, hospital resources, trained rescue capacity, armed-group pressure and whether residents already feel abandoned by the state.

## Key Claims
- Disaster response can look adequate in richer or better-connected regions while failing in poorer, remote places.
- The first rescue window magnifies inequality because delays in roads, machinery, hospitals and trained personnel become irreversible.
- Existing armed-group control and extortion can make relief logistics a security problem as well as a humanitarian one.
- Public anger after disaster often reflects pre-existing abandonment, not only the immediate emergency.
