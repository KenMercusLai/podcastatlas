## Definition

Daoist alchemy / 道教炼丹 is the later systematic immortality-oriented practice that embeds material transformation in cosmology, bodily salvation, secrecy, and textual authority. The current evidence also includes an earlier Western Han court-fangshi precursor through [[LiShaojunWesternHan|李少君]]; that precursor is adjacent to the later Daoist system, not proof that both were institutionally identical.

## Current Synthesis

[[87-guonian-zui-shihe-liao-xuanxue-tantao-shenxian-fangshu-fazhanshi-jie-shangji-704051550|方术发展史（接上集）]] presents the mature intellectual branch through [[WeiBoyang|魏伯阳]], [[ZhouyiCantongqi|《周易参同契》]], [[GeHong|葛洪]], and [[Baopuzi|《抱朴子》]]. Coded mercury-sulfur vocabulary, gold elixirs, ranked formulas, yin-yang and five-phase correspondences, and the goal of becoming immortal make alchemy a religious-material system rather than an isolated chemical curiosity or modern health supplement.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-259-hanwudi-wo-ba-ni-dang-dashi-ni-ba-wo-dang-shazi-1-lqczbezelhdjrtxdrgsfcnmpoklx|Hanji 259 part 1]] supplies an earlier court precursor. Li Shaojun tells [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] that stove sacrifice brings spirits, spirits permit cinnabar to become gold, and gold vessels extend life before contact with [[AnQisheng|安期生]] and fengshan. The source documents patronage and attempted material work, not successful transmutation or medicine. It also shows why the precursor belongs in the alchemy history: changing matter matters because it is placed inside a path toward bodily transcendence.

[[46-guren-ruhe-changsheng-bulao-dongxifang-de-lianjinshu-liandanshu-yu-changshengyao-liporj2ydsu1pwehpgflapgm6zpx|怪东西 episode 46]] clarifies the distinction between external and internal routes. External alchemy uses furnaces, vessels, minerals, metals, and medicines to produce a consumable elixir; internal cultivation treats the body as a small cosmos and uses breath, meditation, diet, and other disciplines to pursue transformation. The furnace is likewise imagined as a small cosmos that accelerates nature's long material changes. Mercury and cinnabar become symbolically compelling through striking color and phase changes, but the same mineral practice creates a toxicity boundary that immortality narratives can conceal.

## Key Claims

- Alchemy in the current sources is an immortality-oriented religious practice before it is a health supplement or chemical curiosity.
- Early court-fangshi transformation claims can precede later Daoist textual systematization without being collapsed into the same institution.
- Material operations gain meaning from a wider chain joining spirits, cosmology, ritual, privileged vessels, and bodily longevity.
- Coded language protects alchemical knowledge while making interpretation dependent on textual and specialist authority.
- Famous later slogans and transformation techniques should remain connected to their elixir and immortality context.
- Furnace cosmology treats technical apparatus as a miniature world capable of accelerating natural transformation.
- Mercury, lead, sulfur, and cinnabar symbolism must be separated from demonstrated safety; an immortality goal can produce toxic injury or death.

## Evidence

- **Later systematic tradition:** [[87-guonian-zui-shihe-liao-xuanxue-tantao-shenxian-fangshu-fazhanshi-jie-shangji-704051550|方术发展史（接上集）]] connects Wei Boyang, 《周易参同契》, Ge Hong, and 《抱朴子》 to coded material vocabulary, gold elixirs, ranked formulas, cosmology, and immortal aspiration.
- **Early court precursor:** [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-259-hanwudi-wo-ba-ni-dang-dashi-ni-ba-wo-dang-shazi-1-lqczbezelhdjrtxdrgsfcnmpoklx|Hanji 259 part 1]] says Li Shaojun links stove sacrifice and spirits to cinnabar-to-gold transformation and longevity through gold vessels, prompting imperial material work.
- **Political uptake:** [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-259-hanwudi-wo-ba-ni-dang-dashi-ni-ba-wo-dang-shazi-1-lqczbezelhdjrtxdrgsfcnmpoklx|Hanji 259 part 1]] places the material promise inside office, expenditure, maritime search, and fengshan rather than treating it as an autonomous experiment.
- **External and internal routes:** [[46-guren-ruhe-changsheng-bulao-dongxifang-de-lianjinshu-liandanshu-yu-changshengyao-liporj2ydsu1pwehpgflapgm6zpx]] distinguishes furnace-made elixirs from body-centered breath, meditation, fasting, and cultivation while recognizing their shared transcendence goal.
- **Furnace model and toxicity:** [[46-guren-ruhe-changsheng-bulao-dongxifang-de-lianjinshu-liandanshu-yu-changshengyao-liporj2ydsu1pwehpgflapgm6zpx]] presents vessels as miniature cosmological systems that accelerate change, then contrasts the symbolic appeal of mercury and cinnabar with imperial injury, death, and ancient poetic criticism of elixir use.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The sources do not establish an unbroken institutional lineage from Li Shaojun to later Daoist alchemy, and the early court-fangshi case should not be retroactively labeled a complete Daoist system. Hanji 259 part 1 does not verify cinnabar-to-gold transmutation, longevity from gold vessels, recipe details, products, safety, or Li's intent. The two broad history episodes are not laboratory or complete textual studies; their claims about materials, coded terms, chronology, internal-alchemy maturation, and religious organization remain source-scoped. The Wei Boyang revival story is religious narrative, not proof that poisoning was safe or reversible. This concept is adjacent to [[AlchemyChemistryContinuity|炼金术与化学连续性]], but the current evidence does not establish a simple linear path from immortality practice to modern chemistry.

## What Changed

- Added a clear external-alchemy versus internal-cultivation distinction.
- Added furnace-as-microcosm and accelerated-natural-transformation as the technical cosmology.
- Added mercury and cinnabar toxicity as a direct qualification of immortality symbolism.
- Added the Wei Boyang legend as evidence about authority and faith, not efficacy.
