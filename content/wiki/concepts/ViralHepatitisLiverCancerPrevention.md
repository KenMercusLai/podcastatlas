# Viral Hepatitis and Liver-Cancer Prevention

## Definition
Viral hepatitis and liver-cancer prevention is the episode's framework for reducing hepatitis-associated liver injury and cancer risk through accurate transmission knowledge, vaccination where applicable, maternal-infant prevention, testing, treatment, and risk-based follow-up.

## Current Synthesis
VOL.22 distinguishes hepatitis A from chronic hepatitis B and C in its liver-cancer discussion and places hepatitis B and C among important risk backgrounds in its Chinese clinical context. Prevention is layered: reject casual-contact stigma, prevent blood and perinatal transmission, use hepatitis B vaccination appropriately, diagnose infection, obtain current treatment advice, and continue qualified liver follow-up when residual risk warrants it.

VOL.03 adds an intimate-relationship and public-literacy layer. It emphasizes telling a long-term partner about hepatitis B, partner vaccination discussion, blood and body-fluid routes, and maternal-infant prevention rather than generalized fear of sharing space or ordinary contact. It also mentions food-associated hepatitis A and public chopsticks, but hepatitis types and routes should not be collapsed: a generic “hepatitis prevention” behavior cannot substitute for virus-specific vaccination, sanitation, testing, treatment, pregnancy management, or follow-up.

## Key Claims
- Sharing an ordinary meal is not presented as a hepatitis B transmission route; prevention should focus on the relevant blood, perinatal, sexual, and other clinically recognized exposures.
- Hepatitis B vaccination and maternal-infant prevention are distinct from treatment of established infection.
- Hepatitis B and C differ in virology, treatment, and post-treatment follow-up, so “positive,” “negative,” “controlled,” and “cured” require qualified interpretation.
- Known chronic viral hepatitis can justify structured liver assessment even when the person feels well.
- Prevention should reduce both disease risk and stigma toward people living with hepatitis.
- Partner communication, vaccination review, and maternal-infant prevention are distinct tools whose use depends on infection status and current clinical guidance.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources' prevalence percentages, age thresholds, geographic comparisons, treatment-cost claims, saliva and shared-meal descriptions, maternal-infant prevention success, and simplified statements about hepatitis A cure, hepatitis B clearance, and hepatitis C cure are not independently verified here. Public chopsticks may have general hygiene value, but the source does not establish them as a specific hepatitis B or C prevention measure. Transmission risk, vaccination, pregnancy management, antiviral indication, response terminology, fibrosis assessment, and post-treatment surveillance depend on current guidance and individual clinical context. This is public education, not infection testing or treatment advice.

## What Changed
- Initial synthesis joins transmission literacy, vaccination, maternal-infant prevention, treatment, and liver-cancer follow-up.
- Stigma reduction is treated as part of usable prevention rather than a separate social issue.
- Added partner communication and clarified that hepatitis prevention must remain virus- and route-specific.
