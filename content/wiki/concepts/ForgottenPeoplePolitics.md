# Forgotten People Politics

## Definition

Forgotten People politics is Robert Menzies's appeal to home-centered middle-class and suburban voters presented as overlooked between organized labor and concentrated wealth.

## Current Synthesis

The episode treats the appeal as the organizing language of Menzies's postwar return. It joined home ownership, family, aspiration, anti-communism, British sentiment, and growing affluence into a durable conservative coalition, while censorship and moral prudery reveal the social discipline within the settlement.

## Key Claims

- The appeal defined a political constituency between capital and organized labor.
- Homes, families, thrift, and suburban aspiration served as coalition symbols.
- Anti-communism gave the coalition a security and ideological boundary.
- Postwar affluence strengthened the appeal's material credibility.
- Social conformity and censorship qualified the era's prosperity.

## Evidence

- Coalition language: [[187-australian-prime-ministers-edmund-barton-robert-menzies-part-1-glt3017584065]] connects Menzies's "Forgotten People" address to ordinary suburban and middle-class households.
- Consolidation and limits: [[187-australian-prime-ministers-edmund-barton-robert-menzies-part-1-glt3017584065]] links anti-communism and the Petrov affair to electoral strength while noting censorship and prudery.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The comparison with later silent-majority politics in Britain and the United States is suggestive rather than proof of direct influence or identical coalitions. The episode does not quantify class voting, home ownership, prosperity, or Petrov's independent electoral effect.

## What Changed

- Added a coalition concept explaining Menzies's durable postwar return.
- Preserved social conformity as a qualification to affluence and stability.
