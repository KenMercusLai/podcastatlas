## Definition

举荐问责式选才 / recommendation-accountability talent selection is a recruitment design in which senior officials nominate candidates and later receive reward or punishment according to the nominees' demonstrated fitness, making sponsorship a monitored judgment rather than a costless favor.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-248-dongzhongshu-ruhe-gaibian-zhonghua-minzu-de-mingyun-2-lrjy7ozds7peyu6x3iwufety2zvt|Hanji 248(2)]] begins from three alleged distortions in Han office access: family background, purchase, and promotion by accumulated tenure. Its answer combines decentralized search with centralized or formal evaluation. Marquises, commandery governors, and high-ranking officials are asked to find capable people in the jurisdictions they know, while examination tests ability and conduct informs placement.

The distinctive mechanism is retrospective responsibility. A recommender benefits when a nominee proves capable and is penalized when the nominee proves unfit. This can make senior officials invest more care in discovery and screening, but it also creates incentives to nominate safe, conforming, or easily monitored people and may punish reasonable judgments because of later outcomes outside the sponsor's control.

Hanji 1167 adds a narrative case rather than another institutional proposal. [[ZhongYao|钟繇]] recommends [[WeiFengLateHan|魏讽]] for talent and influence, then loses office after Wei Feng's conspiracy is exposed. The case shows sponsor liability operating through political trust and office consequence, but it does not establish that Zhong Yao knew the plan, that every later act was predictable at appointment, or that outcome liability always measures initial screening quality fairly.

## Key Claims

- Local and senior officials can possess candidate knowledge that a central ruler or examination process lacks.
- Reward and penalty turn recommendation from ceremonial endorsement into an accountable administrative judgment.
- Examination of ability and assessment of conduct answer different parts of appointment fitness.
- Seniority alone is weak evidence of talent because time served can coexist with poor judgment or low capacity.
- Outcome-linked sponsor liability may improve screening while also encouraging risk avoidance, conformity, and patronage protection.
- Talent and reputation are incomplete screening signals when conduct, incentives, and access risk remain untested.

## Evidence

Nomination and sponsor responsibility:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-248-dongzhongshu-ruhe-gaibian-zhonghua-minzu-de-mingyun-2-lrjy7ozds7peyu6x3iwufety2zvt|Hanji 248(2)]] attributes to Dong Zhongshu an annual recommendation quota for marquises, commandery governors, and senior officials, with reward or punishment tied to nominees' later quality.

Ability, conduct, and placement:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-248-dongzhongshu-ruhe-gaibian-zhonghua-minzu-de-mingyun-2-lrjy7ozds7peyu6x3iwufety2zvt|Hanji 248(2)]] contrasts testing ability and examining conduct with purchasing office, inherited access, and advancement by tenure alone.

Sponsor liability in practice:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1167-budong-shenshi-duoshi-zhihui-ziqu-miewang-llr4vejetnx3dxjngwauadp-oznb|Hanji 1167]] says Zhong Yao's recommendation of Wei Feng is followed by loss of office after the nominee's planned uprising is disclosed.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The institutional proposal and narrative case both come from compact popular-history summaries. They do not independently establish Dong Zhongshu's transmitted wording, nomination quota, salary threshold, administrative adoption, or relationship to later 察举 and 孝廉 practice, nor Zhong Yao's screening process and formal basis for dismissal. Rewarding or punishing recommenders can sharpen incentives, but responsibility can become outcome bias when performance reflects training, office conditions, coercion, factional conflict, or later choices that were not reasonably predictable at nomination.

## What Changed

- Added the Zhong Yao–Wei Feng case as narrative evidence of sponsor liability.
- Extended screening beyond talent and reputation to conduct, incentives, and access risk.
- Added outcome-bias limits when later conduct was not reasonably predictable.
