# Theory of Constructed Emotion

## Definition
Theory of constructed emotion is [[LisaFeldmanBarrett]]'s account in which the brain constructs context-sensitive emotion categories by predicting and interpreting sensory and bodily signals through prior experience, concepts, language, culture, and current action needs.

## Current Synthesis
The source rejects a one-to-one map from emotion words to universal faces, physiological patterns, or dedicated brain locations. The brain receives ambiguous signals, predicts their likely causes, prepares bodily and skeletal action, and categorizes the situation using learned concepts. An episode of anger, fear, uncertainty, or sadness is therefore real without being a fixed package that appears identically in every person and setting.

Construction does not mean arbitrary invention. Predictions remain constrained by the body, sensory input, context, learned regularities, and the actions available. The theory therefore complements [[EmotionsAsFunctionalControlStates]] while changing the unit of explanation: recurring emotion functions need not imply one invariant biological essence, and broad labels can compress many different instances into one usable category.

## Key Claims
- Emotion categories do not require universal facial, physiological, or neural signatures.
- The brain predicts the causes of ambiguous sensory and bodily input using past experience and context.
- Emotion concepts compress many variable instances into action-relevant categories.
- Language and culture help teach category boundaries without making emotion merely verbal or unreal.
- Action preparation and bodily regulation precede and shape conscious experience.
- Similar facial movements or body states can support different emotional interpretations in different contexts.

## Evidence
- Variable signatures - [[how-to-understand-emotions-dr-lisa-feldman-barrett-scim3919749252]] reports Barrett's rejection of fixed universal packages for anger, fear, sadness, and other categories.
- Predictive categorization - [[how-to-understand-emotions-dr-lisa-feldman-barrett-scim3919749252]] describes the brain as guessing signal causes from past experience and constructing possible futures and actions.
- Concept compression - [[how-to-understand-emotions-dr-lisa-feldman-barrett-scim3919749252]] treats words such as anger as low-dimensional summaries for diverse sensory and motor features.
- Learning and culture - [[how-to-understand-emotions-dr-lisa-feldman-barrett-scim3919749252]] connects face interpretation and emotion categories to caregivers, culture, language, and context.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The page records one interview's theoretical account rather than a full adjudication among competing emotion theories. The source does not imply that biology is irrelevant, that emotions are voluntary fictions, or that people can relabel any state without constraint. Claims about universal expression, cultural variation, and developmental learning remain bounded to the episode's summary and cited-review description.
