## Definition

欧亚迁徙连锁 / Trans-Eurasian Migration Cascade is the wiki term for a source-framed pattern in which military pressure on one steppe frontier is interpreted as setting off displacement, secondary migration, and imperial stress far away.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 764 introduces the pattern through [[DouXianEasternHan|Dou Xian]]'s defeat of the [[NorthernXiongnuLateHan|Northern Xiongnu]]. The episode associates Northern Xiongnu westward movement with later European Huns, then links Hunnic pressure to Gothic movement and Roman vulnerability. That chain makes the local Eastern Han frontier victory legible at a Eurasian scale.

The current judgment is deliberately qualified. The source itself treats the Northern-Xiongnu-to-Roman chain as a compelling historical association rather than settled proof. The concept should therefore be used to mark cascading-pressure arguments and their uncertainty, not to state that Dou Xian directly destroyed the [[RomanEmpire|Roman Empire]].

## Key Claims

- Steppe-frontier victories can be narrated as displacement events with consequences beyond the original theater.
- Hanji 764 links Northern Xiongnu westward movement to later Hunnic pressure in Europe.
- The episode presents Hunnic attacks as pushing Gothic groups into Roman space and increasing pressure on Rome.
- [[AttilaTheHun|Attila]] functions as the remembered late stage of this pressure sequence.
- The concept requires an evidentiary caveat because the source does not prove a continuous identity chain from Northern Xiongnu to European Huns.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The current wiki has only one bounded source for this concept. It should not be used as proof of direct ethnogenesis, a simple east-to-west migration line, or a monocausal explanation for Rome's fall. The concept is useful for marking the structure of the claim and its uncertainty: local victory, displacement, secondary pressure, imperial crisis, and evidentiary caveat.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Hanji 764 to preserve the episode's long-range migration argument without overstating its certainty.
