## Definition

声誉修复与安全风险权衡 describes a decision in which a ruler answers public criticism through pardon, rank, wealth, or restoration, but the same concession can strengthen people who retain a grievance against the regime.

## Current Synthesis

The current case is [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]]'s response after [[LiuChangHuainanKing|淮南王刘长]] dies following exile. A popular song frames the event as a ruler unable to tolerate his own brother. Liu Heng later grants marquis titles to Liu Chang's four sons, making the act legible as familial and reputational repair. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-220-minjian-geyao-fengci-wendi-jia-renyi-liuheng-fanying-guguai-1-liuajl1w1byd6m_hjy0obirtmlu8|Hanji 220]]

[[JiaYi|贾谊]]'s warning identifies the double effect. The grant may imply that the court accepts the public account of wrongful punishment, while rank and resources can give aggrieved heirs greater capacity for revenge. The later conflicts of [[LiuAn|刘安]] and [[LiuCiHengshanKingWesternHan|刘赐]] make the warning historically salient, but they do not establish that the grants alone caused those outcomes.

## Key Claims

- A conciliatory grant can change both reputation and the recipient's material capacity.
- Repair can unintentionally validate the accusation it is meant to quiet.
- Kinship grievance may persist after formal compensation, so favor does not automatically produce loyalty.
- Later conflict can support the relevance of an earlier warning without proving a single causal chain.

## Evidence

- Public criticism and repair: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-220-minjian-geyao-fengci-wendi-jia-renyi-liuheng-fanying-guguai-1-liuajl1w1byd6m_hjy0obirtmlu8|Hanji 220]] places the popular song before Liu Heng's grants to Liu Chang's four sons.
- Narrative cost: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-220-minjian-geyao-fengci-wendi-jia-renyi-liuheng-fanying-guguai-1-liuajl1w1byd6m_hjy0obirtmlu8|Hanji 220]] attributes to Jia Yi the warning that rewarding the sons makes the court appear to admit Liu Chang was wronged.
- Capacity cost: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-220-minjian-geyao-fengci-wendi-jia-renyi-liuheng-fanying-guguai-1-liuajl1w1byd6m_hjy0obirtmlu8|Hanji 220]] treats titles, wealth, and possible later royal territory as resources available to people who may remember their father's death.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source does not prove Liu Heng's private motive, the strength of public pressure, or that the grants were necessary or sufficient causes of later rebellion. Reconciliation can also reduce risk, and withholding status can intensify grievance. The concept therefore names a tradeoff to evaluate, not a rule that compensation is inherently dangerous.

## What Changed

- Created from Jia Yi's source-attributed critique of Liu Heng's grants to Liu Chang's sons.
- Separated later evidentiary relevance from a deterministic claim that compensation caused rebellion.
