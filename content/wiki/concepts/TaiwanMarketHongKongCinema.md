# Taiwan Market For Hong Kong Cinema / 港片台湾市场基本盘

## Definition
Taiwan market for Hong Kong cinema / 港片台湾市场基本盘 is the distribution and financing role that Taiwan demand played in making Hong Kong film production commercially scalable in 王晶's account.

## Current Synthesis
The source frames [[Taiwan]] not as a side market but as part of the base economics of Hong Kong film's golden age. Taiwan theaters needed large volumes of films, local production could not fully supply that demand, and Hong Kong films with recognizable stars could be bid up by buyers.

This market base also makes decline easier to explain. If Hong Kong production had been built around Taiwan demand, television, video, and overseas channels, then the collapse or weakening of that market removed more than incremental revenue. Wang therefore treats the Taiwan base as one of the strongest structural reasons the old Hong Kong film model became hard to sustain.

## Key Claims
- Taiwan's demand for film supply helped turn Hong Kong production into a high-volume export industry.
- Star premiums from Taiwan buyers improved the economics of Hong Kong actor-centered films.
- Television, video, and overseas markets compounded the value beyond theatrical Hong Kong box office.
- The Taiwan base's weakening damaged the commercial foundation of Hong Kong film.
- Taiwan-market causality should be kept distinct from explanations based only on language or local culture.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This is a source-scoped structural explanation from Wang Jing. Future sources should test it against box-office records, Taiwan policy changes, piracy data, Hollywood import rules, and other Hong Kong filmmakers' accounts.
