## Definition
A positional imagination boundary is the way a person's institutional role, capital, incentives, and power shape both what futures they can imagine and which parts of those futures they can make real.

## Current Synthesis
The source argues that the important question is not only how a technology leader imagines the future, but where that person stands while imagining it. A capital owner is surrounded by investment, market, infrastructure, and control problems; a powerful executive can turn a private interpretation into product defaults at scale. Position therefore expands causal reach while narrowing attention toward the futures that the position can finance, govern, and monetize.

Success does not dissolve this boundary. The ability to build a future is evidence of resources and coordination, not proof of superior prediction or moral authority. Separating an individual from the social position they occupy makes it possible to recognize genuine capability while still examining whose needs, agency, history, or alternatives the position renders less visible.

## Key Claims
- Social position shapes which futures appear practical, valuable, urgent, or legible.
- Capital and authority enlarge implementation power but do not confer privileged knowledge of the future.
- A private cultural interpretation becomes a public issue when position allows it to become product infrastructure.
- The futures favored by a role often reproduce that role's incentives around ownership, scale, control, and monetization.
- Analysis should distinguish personal talent from the institutional leverage and blind spots attached to a position.

### Power without future authority
- [[zong-di-074-qi-tamen-ceng-jianguo-weilai-yushi-chuangzaole-xianzai-cmvkvb75u00q001xaevgt4t42]] argues that powerful or successful people can shape the future without thereby understanding it better or earning an exclusive right to decide it.

### Institutional viewpoint
- [[zong-di-074-qi-tamen-ceng-jianguo-weilai-yushi-chuangzaole-xianzai-cmvkvb75u00q001xaevgt4t42]] frames capitalists and powerholders as carriers of the positions they occupy, whose relationships and incentives bound their imagination.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Position does not mechanically determine belief, and leaders can reflect critically on their incentives or support projects with weak direct monetization. Outsiders also have positional blind spots and may lack implementation knowledge. The concept is a boundary on claims of authority, not a claim that powerful people have no insight or that every product decision can be reduced to class position.

## What Changed
- Established a distinction between future-shaping power and future-knowing authority.
- Added institutional incentives and public product reach as mechanisms through which position structures imagination.
