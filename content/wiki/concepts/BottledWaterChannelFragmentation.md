# Bottled Water Channel Fragmentation / 包装水渠道分流

## Definition
Bottled water channel fragmentation is the pressure on established beverage distribution when shoppers move from traditional offline terminals into instant retail, discount stores, and other formats that change price visibility and purchase occasions.

## Current Synthesis
The source uses [[Cestbon|怡宝]] to show why pure-water pressure is not only a shelf-price story. A leading brand can remain present in traditional outlets while losing relative share if lower-price competitors become more visible through immediate delivery, near-expiry discount stores, or other alternative channels.

## Key Claims
- Mature beverage channels can lose control even when the underlying product category remains large.
- Instant retail and discount formats make lower prices more visible and easier to access.
- Channel fragmentation intensifies [[PackagedWaterPriceWar|packaged-water price competition]] by weakening the incumbent's traditional terminal advantage.
- Parent-company portfolio weakness can compound channel pressure when the brand lacks another strong beverage growth engine.

## Evidence
- Channel-shift evidence: [[yibao-chunjingshui-fene-xiahua-anta-qixia-yujiafu-pinpai-kaishe-jianshenfang-1012409578]] says C'estbon faces traditional offline terminals being split by instant retail and near-expiry discount stores.
- Price evidence: [[yibao-chunjingshui-fene-xiahua-anta-qixia-yujiafu-pinpai-kaishe-jianshenfang-1012409578]] says low-price pure-water competition from Nongfu Spring and Wahaha pressures C'estbon's two-yuan band.
- Portfolio evidence: [[yibao-chunjingshui-fene-xiahua-anta-qixia-yujiafu-pinpai-kaishe-jianshenfang-1012409578]] says C'estbon also faces a parent beverage business without another strong major brand.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not quantify how much of C'estbon's share decline came from each channel. Weather, brand heat, promotions, local distribution, and rival capacity may also matter.
